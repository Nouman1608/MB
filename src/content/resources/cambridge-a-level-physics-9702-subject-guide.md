Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics (9702) is a broad,
mathematically demanding physics course for students aiming at
university study in physics, engineering, medicine or any other
quantitative field. Its content is divided into 25 topics, eleven at AS
Level and fourteen at A Level, and it is assessed through five papers
that combine multiple choice, structured questions and practical
skills. On the full A Level, the largest single component is Paper 4,
A Level Structured Questions, at 38.5%. This overview covers the
2025-2027 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

### AS Level content (topics 1–11)

The AS topics build the toolkit that everything else depends on.

- **Foundations and mechanics:** physical quantities and units
  (including SI units, errors and uncertainties, and scalars and
  vectors), kinematics, dynamics, forces, density and pressure, and
  work, energy and power.
- **Materials:** deformation of solids, covering stress and strain and
  elastic and plastic behaviour.
- **Waves:** waves (progressive, transverse and longitudinal waves, the
  Doppler effect for sound, the electromagnetic spectrum and
  polarisation) and superposition (stationary waves, diffraction,
  interference and the diffraction grating).
- **Electricity:** electricity (current, potential difference, power,
  resistance and resistivity) and D.C. circuits (practical circuits,
  Kirchhoff's laws and potential dividers).
- **Particle physics:** atoms, nuclei and radiation, and fundamental
  particles.

### A Level content (topics 12–25)

The A Level topics extend AS ideas into fields, thermal physics,
oscillations and modern physics.

- **Circular motion and gravitation:** motion in a circle and
  gravitational fields.
- **Thermal physics:** temperature, ideal gases (including the kinetic
  theory of gases) and thermodynamics.
- **Oscillations:** simple harmonic motion, its energy, and damped and
  forced oscillations with resonance.
- **Fields and electromagnetism:** electric fields, capacitance,
  magnetic fields (including electromagnetic induction) and alternating
  currents.
- **Modern physics:** quantum physics and nuclear physics.
- **Applications:** medical physics (ultrasound, X-rays and PET
  scanning) and astronomy and cosmology (standard candles, stellar
  radii, and Hubble's law and the Big Bang theory).

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 75 minutes | 40 | 15.5% |
| Paper 2 | AS Level Structured Questions | 75 minutes | 60 | 23% |
| Paper 3 | Advanced Practical Skills | 120 minutes | 40 | 11.5% |
| Paper 4 | A Level Structured Questions | 120 minutes | 100 | 38.5% |
| Paper 5 | Planning, Analysis and Evaluation | 75 minutes | 30 | 11.5% |

Papers 1–3 make up the AS Level. The weightings in the table are
percentages of the full A Level. If you take AS Level as a standalone
qualification, Papers 1, 2 and 3 are weighted at 31%, 46% and 23% of
the AS Level instead.

The course is staged. AS Level can be certificated on its own, or a
candidate can continue to Papers 4 and 5 to complete the full A Level.
Paper 3 is a hands-on practical exam; Paper 5 is a written paper that
tests the planning, analysis and evaluation side of experimental work
without requiring you to carry out an experiment in the exam room.

## How the parts fit together

Physics at this level is cumulative, and 9702 makes that very visible.
Several A Level topics are direct extensions of AS work:

- **Motion in a circle** and **gravitational fields** apply the
  dynamics and energy ideas from topics 3 and 5.
- **Electric fields** and **capacitance** build on the AS electricity
  topics, and the treatment of field and potential in electric fields
  closely parallels the one in gravitational fields.
- **Oscillations** draws on kinematics, energy and, through resonance,
  the wave ideas met in topics 7 and 8.
- **Quantum physics** and **nuclear physics** continue the particle
  physics introduced at the end of AS.

Two strands run through the whole course. The first is quantitative
fluency: rearranging equations, working with units and handling
uncertainties, all introduced in topic 1 and used in every paper. The
second is practical skill. Paper 3 and Paper 5 together carry 23% of
the full A Level, and both reward habits built through regular
laboratory work: recording data properly, drawing and reading graphs,
estimating uncertainties and suggesting realistic improvements.

Paper 4 tests A Level content but can draw on AS knowledge as
background, so earlier topics cannot simply be set aside once AS is
over.

## Planning your study

A natural order is to follow the syllabus numbering, since it moves
from foundations to applications. In general terms:

- **First half of the AS year:** physical quantities and units through
  to work, energy and power, with regular practical sessions.
- **Second half of the AS year:** deformation of solids, waves,
  superposition, electricity, D.C. circuits and particle physics.
- **First half of the A Level year:** motion in a circle, gravitational
  fields, the thermal topics, oscillations, electric fields and
  capacitance.
- **Second half of the A Level year:** magnetic fields, alternating
  currents, quantum and nuclear physics, then medical physics and
  astronomy and cosmology.

Start topic-level practice questions from the first weeks, and bring in
past-paper questions for each AS topic as soon as it is taught. Paper 5
planning questions are worth practising long before the final exams,
because the skill develops slowly. Timed, paper-by-paper preparation
belongs to the final months before each series you are entered for.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Physics 9702?**
There are five: Paper 1 Multiple Choice, Paper 2 AS Level Structured Questions, Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills, Paper 4 A Level Structured Questions and Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation.

**Which papers make up AS Level Physics?**
Papers 1, 2 and 3. As a standalone AS Level they are weighted at 31%, 46% and 23% respectively.

**Can I take AS Level first and complete A Level later?**
Yes. The course is staged: AS Level can be certificated on its own, or you can continue to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level.

**How much of the A Level is practical work?**
Paper 3, Advanced Practical Skills, is worth 11.5% and Paper 5, Planning, Analysis and Evaluation, is also worth 11.5% of the full A Level.

**What is the biggest paper in 9702?**
Paper 4, A Level Structured Questions: 120 minutes, 100 marks and 38.5% of the full A Level.
