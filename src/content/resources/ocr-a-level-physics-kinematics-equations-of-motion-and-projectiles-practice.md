These questions cover section 3.1 (Motion) of OCR A Level Physics A (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026): kinematics, linear motion and projectile motion. Take g = 9.81 m s⁻² unless a question gives another value, and ignore air resistance. Learn the content first from the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-kinematics-equations-of-motion-and-projectiles/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-kinematics-equations-of-motion-and-projectiles-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** A swimmer swims two lengths of a 50 m pool and finishes where she started, in 58 s. Calculate her average speed. **[2]** State her average velocity and explain your answer. **[1]**

**2.** A train leaves a station. Its speed rises uniformly from 0 to 18 m s⁻¹ in 30 s, stays constant for 120 s, then falls uniformly to rest in 45 s.

**(a)** Calculate the total distance travelled. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average speed for the whole journey. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the magnitude of the deceleration in the final stage. **[1]**

**3.** A cyclist's speed is recorded every 5.0 s: 0, 4.5, 7.0, 8.2 and 8.8 m s⁻¹ at 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 s. Estimate the distance travelled in the 20 s. **[3]** State, with a reason, whether your estimate is too high or too low. **[1]**

**4.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards from the ground at 21 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum height reached. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time it spends in the air. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate its speed when it is 10 m above the ground. **[2]**

**5.** A stone dropped from a bridge takes 2.6 s to reach the water. Calculate the height of the bridge **[2]** and the speed of the stone on impact. **[1]**

**6.** In an experiment to find g, a steel ball is held by an electromagnet. Switching off the magnet starts a timer, and the ball stops the timer when it opens a trapdoor. The drop height h and time t are:

| h / m | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 1.25 | 1.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| t / s | 0.319 | 0.391 | 0.452 | 0.505 | 0.553 |

**(a)** Calculate t² for each height. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a graph of h against t² should be a straight line and what its gradient represents. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the first and last points to find the gradient and hence g. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest two ways to improve the reliability or accuracy of the experiment. **[2]**

**7.** A card 0.050 m wide is attached to a trolley accelerating down a slope. It blocks the beam of gate A for 0.0500 s and gate B for 0.0250 s. The gates are 0.90 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the trolley at A and at B. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the acceleration. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the speeds in (a) are only approximate. **[1]**

**8.** A car travels at 25 m s⁻¹. The driver's reaction time is 0.72 s and the car decelerates uniformly at 6.4 m s⁻² once the brakes are applied.

**(a)** Calculate the thinking distance, the braking distance and the stopping distance. **[4]**
**(b)** The driver is distracted and the reaction time rises by 0.30 s. Calculate the new stopping distance. **[2]**
**(c)** With the original reaction time, the speed is raised to 30 m s⁻¹. Calculate the stopping distance and the percentage increase on the 25 m s⁻¹ value. **[2]**

**9.** A ball rolls off the edge of a table 1.10 m high at 2.4 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the time to reach the floor. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the horizontal distance from the table edge to the landing point. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed on impact and the angle below the horizontal. **[3]**
**(d)** A second ball is dropped from rest at the table edge at the same moment. State and explain which lands first. **[1]**

**10.** A golf ball is hit from level ground at 38 m s⁻¹ at 28° above the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time of flight. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the range. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum height. **[2]**
**(e)** State the speed at the highest point and explain your answer. **[1]**

**11.** A stone is thrown from the top of a 15 m cliff at 12 m s⁻¹, 40° above the horizontal, and lands in the sea at the foot of the cliff.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time of flight. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the horizontal distance travelled. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the speed on impact. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the angle of the velocity below the horizontal on impact. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Average speed = total distance / time = 100 / 58 [1] = **1.7 m s⁻¹** [1]. Average velocity = **zero**, because her displacement is zero [1].
*Examiner insight:* name displacement in the explanation; "she returned to the start" alone is weaker than "displacement is zero".

**2. (a)** Phase 1: ½ × 30 × 18 = 270 m [1]. Phase 2: 120 × 18 = 2160 m [1]. Phase 3: ½ × 45 × 18 = 405 m, total = **2835 m** [1].
**(b)** Total time = 30 + 120 + 45 = 195 s [1]. Average speed = 2835 / 195 = **14.5 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** a = 18 / 45 = **0.40 m s⁻²** [1].
*Examiner insight:* split the area into shapes and label each; a single unexplained total is hard to give credit to if one slip occurs.

**3.** Use trapezia of width 5.0 s [1]: 5 × (0 + 4.5)/2 + 5 × (4.5 + 7.0)/2 + 5 × (7.0 + 8.2)/2 + 5 × (8.2 + 8.8)/2 = 11.25 + 28.75 + 38.0 + 42.5 [1] = 120.5, so about **120 m** [1]. Too low: the graph is concave (gradient falls), so each chord lies below the curve [1].
*Examiner insight:* the direction of the error needs a reason that links to the shape of the curve.

**4. (a)** v² = u² + 2as with v = 0: s = 21² / (2 × 9.81) [1] = **22.5 m** [1].
**(b)** Time up = 21 / 9.81 = 2.14 s [1]; total = **4.28 s** [1].
**(c)** v² = 21² − 2 × 9.81 × 10 = 244.8 [1], v = **15.6 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* write the sign convention down; mixing up the sign of g is the usual way to lose credit here.

**5.** s = ½gt² = 0.5 × 9.81 × 2.6² [1] = **33 m** (33.2 m) [1]. v = gt = 9.81 × 2.6 = **26 m s⁻¹** (25.5 m s⁻¹) [1].
*Examiner insight:* carry the unrounded height forward and round only the final answer.

**6. (a)** t²: 0.1018, 0.1529, 0.2043, 0.2550, 0.3058 s² [1].
**(b)** With u = 0, h = ½gt², which has the form y = mx with gradient g/2 [1].
**(c)** Gradient = (1.50 − 0.50) / (0.3058 − 0.1018) = 4.90 m s⁻² [1]; g = 2 × 4.90 = **9.80 m s⁻²** [1].
**(d)** Any two: repeat each drop and average [1]; measure h with a set square or plumb line [1]; use a larger range of heights; allow for the ball's release being delayed by residual magnetism.
*Examiner insight:* "do it more carefully" earns nothing; name the technique and what it improves.

**7. (a)** v = d/Δt: at A, 0.050 / 0.0500 = **1.00 m s⁻¹** [1]; at B, 0.050 / 0.0250 = **2.00 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** a = (v² − u²) / 2s = (4.00 − 1.00) / (2 × 0.90) [1] = **1.7 m s⁻²** [1].
**(c)** The card gives the average speed over its width, not the speed at the instant it reaches the gate [1].
*Examiner insight:* name the quantity that is averaged; "it is not accurate" is not enough.

**8. (a)** Thinking = 25 × 0.72 = **18 m** [1]. Braking = 25² / (2 × 6.4) [1] = **48.8 m** [1]. Stopping = 18 + 48.8 = **66.8 m** [1].
**(b)** Extra thinking distance = 25 × 0.30 = 7.5 m [1]; new stopping distance = **74.3 m** [1].
**(c)** Thinking = 30 × 0.72 = 21.6 m; braking = 900 / 12.8 = 70.3 m; stopping = **91.9 m** [1]. Increase = (91.9 − 66.8) / 66.8 = **38%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the separate parts so that one arithmetic slip does not hide correct method.

**9. (a)** 1.10 = ½ × 9.81 × t², t = √(2.20 / 9.81) [1] = **0.474 s** [1].
**(b)** x = 2.4 × 0.474 = **1.14 m** [1].
**(c)** vᵧ = 9.81 × 0.474 = 4.65 m s⁻¹ [1]; speed = √(2.4² + 4.65²) = **5.2 m s⁻¹** [1]; angle = tan⁻¹(4.65 / 2.4) = **63°** [1].
**(d)** They land together, because both start with zero vertical velocity and the horizontal motion does not affect the vertical motion [1].
*Examiner insight:* the reason matters more than the verdict; name independence of the two motions.

**10. (a)** vₓ = 38 cos 28° = **33.6 m s⁻¹** [1]; vᵧ = 38 sin 28° = **17.8 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** T = 2vᵧ / g [1] = 2 × 17.84 / 9.81 = **3.64 s** [1].
**(c)** Range = 33.55 × 3.637 = **122 m** [1].
**(d)** H = vᵧ² / 2g [1] = 17.84² / 19.62 = **16.2 m** [1].
**(e)** **33.6 m s⁻¹**, because vᵧ = 0 there and vₓ stays constant [1].
*Examiner insight:* "the speed is zero at the top" is the common wrong answer; the ball is still moving horizontally.

**11. (a)** vₓ = 12 cos 40° = **9.19 m s⁻¹** [1]; vᵧ = 12 sin 40° = **7.71 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Taking up as positive, s = −15 m: −15 = 7.71t − 4.905t² [1]. Rearranged: 4.905t² − 7.71t − 15 = 0, so t = (7.71 + √(7.71² + 4 × 4.905 × 15)) / (2 × 4.905) [1]; t = **2.70 s** (the negative root is rejected) [1].
**(c)** x = 9.19 × 2.70 = **24.9 m** [1].
**(d)** vᵧ = 7.71 − 9.81 × 2.70 = −18.8 m s⁻¹ [1]; speed = √(9.19² + 18.8²) = **20.9 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(e)** Angle = tan⁻¹(18.8 / 9.19) = **64°** below the horizontal [1].
*Examiner insight:* the negative root must be rejected explicitly, and the sign of s must match the sign convention.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a distance where a displacement or a vector is wanted.
- Mixing signs for g and u without a stated positive direction.
- Reading speeds off a graph without using the area of each shape.
- Using the final vertical velocity alone as the landing speed instead of combining it with the horizontal component.
- Forgetting that the horizontal velocity is non-zero at the top of the flight.
- Plotting h against t when t² is needed, or halving the gradient incorrectly.
- Treating thinking distance as proportional to speed squared.
- Rounding too early.
- Giving no unit, or an unlikely number of significant figures.
