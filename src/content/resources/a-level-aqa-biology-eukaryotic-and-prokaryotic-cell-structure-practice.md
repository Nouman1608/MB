These questions are aligned to the AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) specification, version 1.5, section 3.2.1.2 Structure of prokaryotic cells and of viruses (AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). They cover the size of prokaryotic cells, the four named differences from eukaryotic cells, plasmids, capsules and flagella, and the structure of virus particles. The specification says that structural details of plasmids, capsules and flagella are not required, so those questions ask only about what they are and whether cells have them.

## Questions

**1.** A technician describes three unknown specimens. Specimen P has a cell wall containing murein and a single circular DNA molecule lying free in the cytoplasm. Specimen Q has a nucleus, mitochondria and a cell-surface membrane, but no cell wall. Specimen R has no cytoplasm and consists of nucleic acid inside a protein coat. For each specimen, state what it is and give one feature from the description that supports your answer. **[4]**

**2.** A bacterial cell is 1.8 µm long. A human liver cell in the same sample is 36 µm long.
**(a)** Calculate how many times longer the liver cell is than the bacterial cell. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the length of the bacterial cell in millimetres. **[1]**
**(c)** State the size relationship between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells that the specification gives. **[1]**

**3.** A student lists five structures: mitochondrion, ribosome, plasmid, nucleolus, murein cell wall. State which of these can be found in a bacterial cell, and for each one you choose, say what is distinctive about it compared with the eukaryotic equivalent, or why it is not an organelle. **[3]**

**4.** A micrograph of a rod-shaped bacterium shows a slimy layer outside the cell wall and a long whip-like thread projecting from one end.
**(a)** Name the two structures. **[2]**
**(b)** A second student says that every prokaryotic cell has both structures. Comment on this claim. **[1]**

**5.** A medicine interferes with the synthesis of murein. Explain why it could harm a bacterial infection without damaging the cells of the person taking it. **[3]**

**6.** Some drugs bind to bacterial ribosomes but not to human ribosomes. Suggest, with reference to the specification, why this kind of selectivity is possible. **[2]**

**7.** A virus particle has three main parts: W, a molecule of nucleic acid; X, a protein layer enclosing W; and Y, protein molecules that project from the outside.
**(a)** Name the three parts W, X and Y. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the function of Y. **[1]**

**8.** State **three** structural differences between a bacterial cell and a virus particle. **[3]**

**9.** A biologist finds a cell 2.5 µm long in pond water. It has ribosomes and a cell wall. A colleague says: "A cell wall and ribosomes prove it is a prokaryote." The cell is then shown to have DNA that is circular and free in the cytoplasm, with no organelles surrounded by a membrane. Evaluate the colleague's claim and the evidence that identifies the cell. Eukaryotic cells in the same sample are about 30 µm long. **[6]**

**10.** A virus infects only cells that line the airways. Its attachment protein fits a receptor found on those cells and on no others in the body.
**(a)** Explain why the virus cannot infect cells in the liver. **[2]**
**(b)** A change in the shape of the attachment protein stops the virus infecting airway cells. Suggest why. **[1]**
**(c)** State the role of the capsid. **[1]**
**(d)** State what is meant by describing a virus as acellular. **[1]**

**11.** Copy and complete the table for a bacterial cell, an animal cell and a virus particle. Write present or absent, or fill in the missing term. There are eight blanks (A to H). **[8]**

| Feature | Bacterial cell | Animal cell | Virus particle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | A | present | absent |
| Membrane-bound organelles | absent | B | C |
| Ribosome size | D | larger | E |
| Cell wall | contains F | G | absent |
| Capsid | absent | absent | H |

## Answers

**1.** P is a **prokaryotic cell (bacterium)**, supported by a murein cell wall or circular DNA free in the cytoplasm [1]. Q is a **eukaryotic (animal) cell**, supported by a nucleus or mitochondria [1]. R is a **virus particle**, supported by nucleic acid in a protein coat, with no cytoplasm [1]. A supporting feature is given for each, and each feature is taken from the description [1]. 
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when each identification is tied to a named feature from the stem. A bare list of three names may not show the reasoning the command word asks for.

**2. (a)** 36 ÷ 1.8 = **20 times** [1].
**(b)** 1.8 µm ÷ 1000 = **0.0018 mm** [1].
**(c)** Prokaryotic cells are **much smaller** than eukaryotic cells [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the division and the conversion factor. In unit conversions the usual error is dividing by 100 or multiplying by 1000, so write 1000 µm = 1 mm before you start.

**3.** A bacterial cell can contain a **ribosome**, which is smaller than the eukaryotic ribosome [1]; a **plasmid**, which is an extra circular DNA molecule that many (not all) bacteria have [1]; and a **murein cell wall**, a glycoprotein wall unlike the walls of plants, algae and fungi [1]. Mitochondria and nucleoli are not present, because prokaryotic cytoplasm lacks membrane-bound organelles and there is no nucleus.
*Examiner insight:* A selection question asks you to commit to the right ones. Adding a wrong structure to your list can undo a correct choice, so do not hedge by listing everything.

**4. (a)** The slimy layer is a **capsule** [1]. The whip-like thread is a **flagellum** [1].
**(b)** The claim is wrong: **many** prokaryotic cells have capsules or flagella, so some lack them [1].
*Examiner insight:* The specification says "many", and an answer that matches that wording is safer than "all" or "none". The structural detail of the capsule and flagellum is not required, so naming is enough.

**5.** Bacteria have a **cell wall that contains murein**, so blocking murein synthesis stops the wall being made or repaired properly [1]. This weakens or damages the bacterial cells, so the infection is harmed [1]. Human (animal) cells **have no cell wall and no murein**, so the medicine has no target in them [1]. 
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer has two halves: what the medicine does to the bacterium, and why human cells are unaffected. Writing only "it kills bacteria" does not explain the difference between the two cell types.

**6.** Bacterial ribosomes are **smaller** than the ribosomes of eukaryotic cells [1], so a drug can fit or bind only to the bacterial version, leaving human ribosomes alone [1]. 
*Examiner insight:* "Different" is weaker than "smaller", which is the term the specification uses. Link the size difference to the selectivity, not just state it.

**7. (a)** W is **genetic material (nucleic acid)** [1]. X is the **capsid** [1]. Y is the **attachment protein** [1].
**(b)** The attachment protein **binds to a specific receptor on the host cell** so the virus can attach to it [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the specification's terms "genetic material", "capsid" and "attachment protein". Calling X "the cell wall" or "the membrane" would suggest you are treating a virus as a cell.

**8.** Any three from: the bacterial cell has **cytoplasm**, but the virus particle does not [1]; the bacterium has a **cell-surface membrane**, and the virus has none of its own [1]; the bacterium has **ribosomes**, the virus has none [1]; the bacterium has a **murein cell wall**, the virus has none; the bacterium is **much larger** and is a cell, whereas the virus is acellular. Each difference is given for both sides.
*Examiner insight:* Each difference should be stated for both sides ("has X, whereas the other has not"). A one-sided list such as "no ribosomes" can leave the comparison unclear.

**9.** The colleague's claim is **not supported by those two features alone [1]**, because **all cells have ribosomes**, and **plant, algal and fungal eukaryotic cells also have cell walls [1]**, so neither feature separates the two types. The cell is **much smaller** than the eukaryotic cells, 12 times shorter (30 ÷ 2.5 = 12), which fits a prokaryote but does not prove it [1]. The decisive evidence is **DNA that is circular, free in the cytoplasm and not associated with proteins, so there is no nucleus [1]**. The lack of **membrane-bound organelles** also fits [1]. The cell is therefore a **prokaryote**, but this is based on the DNA and organelle evidence rather than the wall or ribosomes [1]. 
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" asks for a judgement supported by evidence. Answers tend to gain credit both for saying why a feature is not diagnostic and for naming the ones that are, so do both.

**10. (a)** The attachment protein binds only to its **complementary receptor [1]**, and liver cells **do not have that receptor**, so the virus cannot attach to them [1].
**(b)** The **shape no longer fits the receptor**, so the virus cannot attach [1].
**(c)** The capsid is the **protein coat that encloses and protects the genetic material [1]**.
**(d)** Acellular means the virus is **not made of a cell** (it is a non-living particle, not a cell) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In "explain" answers, link the shape of the protein to the receptor. Writing only "the virus cannot get in" repeats the question without a reason.

**11.** A: **absent** [1]. B: **present** [1]. C: **absent** [1]. D: **smaller** [1]. E: **absent** (a virus has no ribosomes) [1]. F: **murein** [1]. G: **absent** [1]. H: **present** [1]. 
*Examiner insight:* In a completion table, use the exact term asked for in each cell. Writing "no" or "yes" where the column asks for "smaller" or "murein" can leave the comparison incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a prokaryote has "no DNA" or "DNA in a nucleus". The specification describes a single circular molecule free in the cytoplasm.
- Treating a cell wall or ribosomes as proof that a cell is a prokaryote, when other cell types also have them.
- Writing "cell wall" without saying it contains murein for a bacterium.
- Describing viruses as tiny cells, or giving them cytoplasm, a membrane or ribosomes.
- Using "cell wall" or "membrane" for the capsid of a virus.
- Claiming all prokaryotic cells have plasmids, capsules and flagella. The specification says many do.
- Giving only one side of a comparison, such as "bacteria have no nucleus" without stating what the other cell has.
- Slips in unit conversion between µm and mm.
