These questions cover topic 1.2 Migration in Theme 1 (Population and settlement) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. All countries, villages and people in the data questions are fictional, and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Migration study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-geography-0460-migration/) and the [Migration revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-migration-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term "international migration". **[2]**

**2.** Explain the difference between voluntary and involuntary migration. **[2]**

**3.** Give two push factors and two pull factors that can cause rural-urban migration in a low-income country. **[4]**

**4.** State what is meant by "remittances". **[1]**

**5.** Give two intervening obstacles that may prevent a person from migrating to another country. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by a "brain drain". **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the number of migrants arriving in the fictional country of Telvar from three countries.

| Country of origin | Arrivals in 2015 | Arrivals in 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Country A | 12,000 | 18,000 |
| Country B | 8,000 | 4,000 |
| Country C | 3,000 | 9,000 |
| Total | 23,000 | 31,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in total arrivals between 2015 and 2025. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the changes in arrivals from the three countries. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest reasons why arrivals from Country C increased. **[3]**

**8.** Read the fictional account.

> Joseph, aged 24, left the village of Nyoro to work in a car parts factory in the capital, 400 km away. Most of the young men in his age group have also left. Joseph sends half his wages home each month. His father, aged 61, now farms the family's plot with help from Joseph's sisters.

Suggest the positive and negative impacts of this migration on the village of Nyoro. **[4]**

**9.** In a Gulf state, there are far more men than women in the 25 to 44 age groups. Explain how international migration has caused this. **[3]**

**10.** For a named international migration you have studied, explain the reasons why people migrated. **[6]**

**11.** For a named example of forced (involuntary) migration, describe the impacts on the migrants and on the area that received them. **[6]**

**12.** "International migration benefits the destination country more than the country of origin." To what extent do you agree? Use examples. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The movement of people from one country to another [1] to live, usually for at least a year (a change of usual residence across a border) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that does not mention crossing a national border could equally describe internal migration.

**2.** Voluntary migrants choose to move, for example for a job or education [1]. Involuntary (forced) migrants have no real choice because of conflict, persecution or a natural disaster [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Push: low or unreliable farm incomes [1]; lack of services such as secondary schools or hospitals [1]. Pull: more jobs and higher wages in factories and services [1]; better access to education and healthcare [1]. Also credit drought or land shortage as push factors and family networks as a pull factor. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Push factors must be features of the rural area and pull factors features of the city; "better jobs" written as a push factor is not credited.

**4.** Money sent by migrants to family members in their home area or country [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The cost of travel or recruitment fees [1]. Immigration laws, such as visa requirements or quotas [1]. Also credit distance, language barriers or family ties. **[2]**

**6.** The emigration of highly educated or skilled people, such as doctors and engineers [1], so the origin country loses the skills it paid to train and faces shortages [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** (31,000 − 23,000) ÷ 23,000 × 100 [1] = 34.8% [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Arrivals from Country A rose from 12,000 to 18,000, an increase of half [1]. Arrivals from Country B halved, from 8,000 to 4,000 [1]. Arrivals from Country C tripled, from 3,000 to 9,000, the fastest growth [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Country C may have suffered conflict, a natural disaster or an economic crisis, increasing push factors [1]. Telvar may have opened jobs or visas to workers from Country C, reducing obstacles [1]. Earlier migrants from Country C may have created networks, helping more people to follow [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" allows any sensible reasons, but each must be linked to the change in the data.

**8.** Positive: remittances raise the family's income and may pay for schooling, housing or farm improvements [1]. Positive: fewer people competing for land and work in the village [1]. Negative: the loss of young men leaves an ageing population, so farms are worked by older people and women [1]. Negative: farm output may fall and services such as schools may close as the population declines [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Gulf states recruit large numbers of foreign workers for construction and other manual jobs [1]. Most of these workers are young adult men [1]. They come on temporary contracts and usually cannot bring their families, so the number of women in those age groups is much lower [1]. **[3]**

**10.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content, for example for South Asian workers moving to the Gulf:

Named origin and destination, for example Nepal or Bangladesh to Qatar or the UAE [1]. Push: low wages and underemployment at home [1]. Push: small farm plots that cannot support a growing family [1]. Pull: wages several times higher in construction and services [1]. Pull: large demand for labour created by oil-funded building projects, with small local populations [1]. Networks and recruitment agents make the move possible and steer people to particular destinations [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Top answers explain how each factor leads to the decision to move, and use specific places rather than "poor countries" and "rich countries".

**11.** Indicative content, for example for Rohingya refugees moving from Myanmar to Bangladesh from 2017:

Named example with scale: more than 700,000 people fled into the Cox's Bazar district [1]. On migrants: safety from violence [1]. On migrants: crowded shelters on steep slopes, at risk of landslides in the monsoon [1]. On migrants: dependence on aid and limited access to formal education and jobs [1]. On the receiving area: hillsides cleared of trees for shelter and firewood [1]. On the receiving area: competition for low-paid work and higher local prices, and heavy costs for the government and aid agencies [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Indicative content:

Destination benefits: labour shortages are filled, for example in construction or healthcare [1]. Destination benefits: migrants pay taxes and spend money, and young workers offset an ageing population [1]. Destination costs: pressure on housing and services, and possible social tension [1]. Origin benefits: remittances fund housing, education and businesses [1]. Origin benefits: less pressure on jobs and land [1]. Origin costs: brain drain and the loss of young workers, leaving an older population [1]. Recognition that the migrants themselves gain higher incomes but may face poor conditions [1]. Judgement: the balance depends on the type of migration and the skills of the migrants, for example remittances can make the origin a major beneficiary, whereas a brain drain of doctors makes it a loser [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement that "it depends" earns credit only if you say what it depends on and support it with examples.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Push factors written as features of the destination.
- Impacts answers that cover the destination when the question asks about the origin or the migrants.
- Percentage change calculated from the new value instead of the original value.
- Describing data without quoting figures for each country.
- Case studies with no named countries, dates or numbers.
