Revise first with the [supply side policy study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-supply-side-policy-ocr-aslevel-economics/) and the [supply side policy revision notes](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-supply-side-policy-ocr-aslevel-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an interventionist supply side policy? **[1]**

A: Selling a state-owned airline to private investors

B: Removing licensing restrictions on taxi firms

C: Government funding for apprenticeships

D: Cutting the top rate of income tax

**2.** A successful supply side policy is most likely to cause which **one** of the following? **[1]**

A: A rise in the price level and a fall in real output

B: A fall in the price level and a rise in real output

C: A rise in both the price level and real output

D: A fall in both the price level and real output

**3.** State **two** ways in which deregulation can increase competition. **[2]**

**4.** Output per worker in Country S rises from 50 to 54 units a day, while daily wages rise by 3%. Calculate the change in unit labour costs. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how competition policy can improve the supply side of the economy. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why spending on infrastructure affects aggregate demand before it affects long-run aggregate supply. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured and extended questions

**7.** An unemployed worker in Country T receives benefits of 140 a week. A job offers 320 a week, on which tax and contributions are 50.

**(a)** Calculate the net weekly gain from taking the job if all benefits are withdrawn, and the share of earnings lost to tax and withdrawn benefits. **[2]**

**(b)** The government changes the rules so that only 70 of the benefit is withdrawn. Calculate the new net gain. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how this reform could affect long-run aggregate supply. **[2]**

**8.** Explain, with the aid of a diagram, how increased spending on education and training could help a government to achieve economic growth without higher inflation. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** possible advantages of privatising a state-owned industry. **[4]**

**10.** Country U relaxes its immigration controls for workers in occupations with skills shortages. Explain the likely effects on the labour market and on long-run aggregate supply. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of improving labour market flexibility. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the effectiveness of supply side policies in reducing unemployment. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Funding for apprenticeships uses state spending to raise skills. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. LRAS shifts right, raising output and lowering the price level. **[1]**

**3.** Any two from: lowering barriers to entry so new firms can enter [1]; removing restrictions on prices or services that protected existing firms [1]; reducing compliance costs that deterred small firms. **[2]**

**4.** Productivity rises by 4 / 50 = 8% [1]. Unit labour costs change by 1.03 / 1.08 - 1 = **-4.6%** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Competition policy prevents mergers that reduce competition and breaks up cartels [1]. Firms facing rivals must cut costs and improve quality to keep customers [1], so productivity and efficiency rise across the economy, shifting LRAS right [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The spending is government expenditure, a component of AD, so AD rises as soon as construction starts [1]; the extra capacity only arrives when the roads or networks are finished and in use, which can take years [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Net gain = 320 - 50 - 140 = **130** a week [1]. Share lost = 190 / 320 = **59.4%** [1].

**(b)** Net gain = 320 - 50 - 70 = **200** a week [1].

**(c)** A larger gain from working increases the incentive to take a job [1], so more people join the employed labour force, increasing productive capacity and shifting LRAS right [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Education and training raise the skills and productivity of the labour force [1]. This increases productive capacity, shifting LRAS right from LRAS1 to LRAS2 [1]. The diagram shows the new equilibrium with AD at higher real output and a lower price level [1]. So growth can be achieved without inflationary pressure, although the effect takes years to appear [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Advantage 1: the profit motive encourages private owners to cut costs [1], raising productive efficiency and lowering prices [1]. Advantage 2: private firms can raise funds from shareholders for investment [1], leading to innovation and better services [1]. Other valid advantages: one-off revenue for the government; exposure to competition if the market is opened up. **[4]**

**10.** More workers with the skills employers need enter the labour market [1], filling vacancies in shortage occupations and easing upward pressure on wages there [1]. Firms can expand output, and the labour force grows [1], so productive capacity rises and LRAS shifts right [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Advantage: wages and hiring adjust quickly to changes in demand [1], so firms are more willing to create jobs and unemployment falls faster after shocks [1]. Disadvantage: workers face less job security and less predictable hours [1], which can reduce their spending and firms' incentive to invest in training [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Award up to 10 marks across knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation.
- Training and education reduce structural unemployment by matching skills to vacancies [1].
- Benefit reform raises the gain from work, encouraging job search [1].
- Labour market flexibility makes firms more willing to hire [1].
- Lower costs from productivity gains improve competitiveness, raising demand for exports and jobs [1].
- But supply side policies do little against cyclical unemployment caused by low AD [1].
- Effects take a long time: training schemes and infrastructure pay off over years [1].
- Benefit cuts may cause hardship without creating jobs if vacancies are scarce [1].
- Policies are costly and may be poorly targeted, an example of government failure [1].
- Effectiveness depends on the type of unemployment: high for structural, low for cyclical [1].
- Judgement: effective for long-run structural unemployment, especially when combined with demand-side policy to keep AD strong, but not a quick solution in a recession [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Identifying the type of unemployment is the key evaluative step; without it, the judgement has no basis.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating supply side policy as tax cuts only.
- Shifting AD instead of LRAS.
- Ignoring time lags.
- Claiming supply side policy cures cyclical unemployment.
- Writing about immigration without linking it to labour supply.
- Forgetting the equity costs of benefit and labour market reforms.
