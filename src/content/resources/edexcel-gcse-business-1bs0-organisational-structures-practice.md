## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following describes a decentralised organisational structure? Select one answer.

A: A structure with many layers of management

B: A structure in which decisions are passed down to branch or department managers

C: A structure in which all decisions are made by head office

D: A structure with few layers of management

**[1]**

**2.** Define the term "flat organisational structure". **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** barriers to effective communication. **[2]**

**4.** Marsh Lane Bakery employs 3 part-time staff who each work 18 hours a week and 2 full-time staff who each work 37.5 hours a week. Calculate the percentage of the bakery's weekly hours worked by part-time staff. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** impact of insufficient communication on the motivation of employees. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of hiring a freelance worker. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Orchard Point Hotels owns five hotels in different towns. Every decision, including how to deal with a guest complaint, is made at head office. Guests wait an average of 6 days for a complaint to be settled. The owners plan to let each hotel manager settle complaints, which they expect to cut the wait to 2 days.

**(a)** State which type of decision making Orchard Point uses at present. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the expected percentage fall in the time taken to settle a complaint. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Outline **one** risk to Orchard Point of letting hotel managers settle complaints. **[2]**

**8.** Staff at Delmont Accountants each receive about 220 internal emails a week. Each email takes an average of 2 minutes to read and deal with. Staff work a 37.5-hour week.

**(a)** Calculate the number of hours each member of staff spends on internal emails each week. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate this as a percentage of the working week. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Outline **one** way Delmont could reduce excessive communication. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** reason why a large business might choose a hierarchical structure. **[3]**

**10.** Tidewell Insurance Brokers rents an office for its 20 staff at £36,000 a year. If staff work from home three days a week, Tidewell can move to a smaller office costing £21,600 a year. Each member of staff would need equipment costing £600, paid once.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage saving in office rent. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the net saving in the first year after paying for equipment. **[2]**

**(c)** Analyse **one** impact on Tidewell's staff of moving to remote working. **[3]**

**11.** Cove Ices runs an ice-cream kiosk on a seafront that is busy only in summer. The owner is considering two options to staff the kiosk.

Option 1: hire 3 temporary staff for 14 summer weeks, each working 25 hours a week at £11.80 an hour.

Option 2: hire 2 permanent part-time staff for the whole year, each working 20 hours a week at £11.80 an hour, and use the quiet months to run online sales.

Justify which **one** of these options the owner should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Ashby Furnishings has grown from one shop to eight shops across a region. Each shop has a manager, but all buying, pricing and staffing decisions are still made by the founder. Shop managers say customers ask for products that sell locally, and staff complain that requests take weeks to be answered. The founder worries that shops will "go their own way" if given control.

Evaluate whether Ashby Furnishings should move to a decentralised structure. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A describes hierarchical, C centralised and D flat. Decentralised is about where decisions are made, not the number of layers.

**2.** A structure with few layers of management [1], so each manager is responsible for a larger number of staff [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: jargon or unclear language [1]; the wrong method of communication [1]; too many layers in the structure; distance between staff or sites; language differences; technology failure. **[2]**

**4.** Part-time hours = 3 × 18 = 54; full-time hours = 2 × 37.5 = 75 [1]. Total = 129 hours [1]. Percentage = 54 ÷ 129 × 100 = **41.9%** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Insufficient communication means employees are not told about changes that affect them [1]. They may feel ignored or undervalued by managers [1], so they put in less effort and may look for another job [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A freelance worker is hired only for a particular job [1]. The business pays for the specialist skill only when it is needed [1], so it avoids the cost of employing a specialist all year [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Centralised [1]

**(b)** Fall = 6 − 2 = 4 days [1]. 4 ÷ 6 × 100 = **66.7%** [1]

**(c)** Hotel managers may settle similar complaints in different ways [1], so a guest compensated generously at one hotel may be treated less well at another and complain to head office, damaging Orchard Point's reputation [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 220 × 2 = 440 minutes [1]. 440 ÷ 60 = **7.3 hours** [1]

**(b)** 7.33 ÷ 37.5 × 100 [1] = **19.6%** [1]

**(c)** Delmont could send emails only to the staff a message concerns [1], cutting the 220 a week so that accountants spend more of their 37.5 hours on client work [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b), use the unrounded 7.33 hours. Using 7.3 gives 19.5%, so keep values unrounded within a calculation.

**9.** A hierarchical structure has many layers, each with managers looking after small groups [1]. This gives close supervision and clear lines of authority [1], which helps a large business keep control over many staff and check that work meets the required standard [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Saving = £36,000 − £21,600 = £14,400 [1]. £14,400 ÷ £36,000 × 100 = **40%** [1]

**(b)** Equipment = 20 × £600 = £12,000 [1]. Net saving = £14,400 − £12,000 = **£2,400** [1]

**(c)** Staff save travel time and cost on three days a week [1], which may make them more motivated and more likely to stay at Tidewell [1]. However, they see colleagues less, so newer staff may find it harder to get help, slowing their work [1]. **[7]**

**11.** Option 1 costs 3 × 14 × 25 × £11.80 = £12,390 [1]. Option 2 costs 2 × 52 × 20 × £11.80 = £24,544 [1], so Option 1 is £12,154 cheaper [1]. Temporary contracts match staff to the 14 busy weeks, so Cove Ices does not pay for quiet months when the kiosk earns little [1]. Option 1 also gives 1,050 staff hours in summer against 560 in the same 14 weeks under Option 2, so customers queue less in the peak [1]. However, temporary staff must be recruited and trained each year and may not return [1]. Option 2 gives permanent staff who build skills and loyalty [1], and online sales could earn revenue in winter, but this depends on demand that is not proven [1]. Judgement: Option 1, because the kiosk's demand is seasonal, it costs about half as much and provides more hours when they are needed; Option 2 is only better if online sales are likely to cover the extra £12,154 [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A Justify answer should use figures for both options. Hours: 3 × 14 × 25 = 1,050; 2 × 14 × 20 = 560.

**12.** Decentralising would let shop managers order products that sell locally [1], so sales could rise because customers find what they ask for [1]. Decisions would be quicker because requests no longer wait weeks for the founder [1], which could improve staff motivation as managers feel trusted [1]. The founder would also have more time for decisions affecting the whole business [1]. However, shops could set different prices or standards [1], which might confuse customers and weaken the brand across the region [1]. Managers may lack buying experience, so stock could be wasted [1]. Judgement: Ashby should decentralise buying and staffing but keep pricing centralised, because eight shops are too many for one person to run and local demand differs, while one price list keeps the business consistent; success depends on training managers first [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* An Evaluate answer needs a supported conclusion. A partial move, giving managers some decisions while keeping others at the centre, is a valid judgement if it is explained.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing layers (hierarchical or flat) with where decisions are made (centralised or decentralised), as in Question 1.
- Rounding part-way through a calculation, as in Question 8.
- Giving a generic advantage in an Outline answer without using the business in the question.
- In Justify, describing only the chosen option and not comparing it with the other.
- In Evaluate, ending without saying what the decision depends on.
