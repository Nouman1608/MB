## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Momentum (p) | Mass × velocity; a vector, in kg m/s |
| Closed system | A system with no external resultant force acting on it |
| Conservation of momentum | In a closed system, total momentum before an event = total momentum after |
| Change in momentum | m Δv: mass × change in velocity |
| Rate of change of momentum | Change in momentum ÷ time taken; equal to the force |
| Collision | An event in which objects hit each other; momentum is conserved |
| Explosion / pushing apart | Objects start together at rest and move apart; total momentum stays zero |

## Equations

| Equation | Recall or given? |
|---|---|
| p = m v | Recall |
| F = m Δv / Δt | Equation sheet |
| F = m a and a = Δv / t | Recall (combine to give the line above) |

Units: p in kg m/s, F in N, m in kg, v in m/s, t in s.

## Signs and direction

- Choose one direction as positive and write it down.
- Velocity, and so momentum, in the other direction is negative.
- A negative answer for a velocity means "in the opposite direction to the one you chose".

*Worked reminder.* A 0.20 kg ball moving at 6.0 m/s to the right: p = +1.2 kg m/s. The same ball at 6.0 m/s
to the left: p = −1.2 kg m/s.

## Conservation: three types of event

| Event | Before | After | Equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two objects stick | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ | (m₁ + m₂) v | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂) v |
| Two objects separate | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ |
| Push apart from rest | 0 | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ | m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂ |

*Worked reminder (stick).* A 1500 kg car at 12 m/s hits a stationary 1000 kg car; they move off together.
1500 × 12 = 2500 v → v = 18 000 / 2500 = **7.2 m/s**.

*Worked reminder (push apart).* A 0.60 kg trolley and a 0.40 kg trolley spring apart from rest. The 0.60 kg
trolley moves at 0.50 m/s. The 0.40 kg trolley moves at 0.60 × 0.50 / 0.40 = **0.75 m/s** the other way.

## Force and change in momentum

```
change in momentum = m × (v − u)
F = change in momentum ÷ time
```

- Same change in momentum, **longer time** → **smaller force**.
- Same change in momentum, **shorter time** → **larger force**.
- If an object bounces back, Δv = speed after + speed before (directions are opposite).

*Worked reminder.* A 0.15 kg ball hits a wall at 8.0 m/s and rebounds at 6.0 m/s in 0.020 s.
Δv = 6.0 − (−8.0) = 14 m/s; Δp = 0.15 × 14 = 2.1 kg m/s; F = 2.1 / 0.020 = **105 N**.

## Safety features

The chain for every answer:

```
feature deforms / stretches / squashes
→ the time taken for the person's momentum to change increases
→ the rate of change of momentum decreases
→ the force on the person decreases → less injury
```

| Feature | What deforms |
|---|---|
| Air bag | Inflated bag squashes as the head moves into it |
| Seat belt | Belt stretches slightly |
| Gym crash mat | Thick foam compresses |
| Cycle helmet | Foam liner crushes |
| Playground surface | Rubber or bark surface compresses |

## Exam traps

- Leaving out the minus sign for an object moving the other way.
- Using the mass of one object after they stick together, instead of the combined mass.
- "The helmet reduces the momentum" (the change is the same; the time is longer).
- "Absorbs the impact" with no mention of time, rate of change of momentum or force.
- Applying conservation of momentum when an external force acts, such as braking.
- Forgetting that Δv for a rebound adds the two speeds.
- Giving momentum in N, or force in kg m/s.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate the momentum of a 70 kg runner moving at 4.0 m/s.
2. Why is momentum a vector quantity?
3. State the principle of conservation of momentum.
4. A 3.0 kg trolley at 2.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they stick. Find their velocity.
5. Two skaters at rest push apart. A 50 kg skater moves at 3.0 m/s. How fast does the 75 kg skater move?
6. A 4.0 kg trolley moving at 1.5 m/s collides with a stationary trolley. Afterwards the 4.0 kg trolley is at
   rest. What is the momentum of the other trolley?
7. State the equation linking force, mass, change in velocity and time.
8. A 0.50 kg ball, initially at rest, is kicked to 12 m/s in 0.080 s. Calculate the force.
9. A 0.10 kg ball hits a wall at 5.0 m/s and bounces back at 5.0 m/s. Calculate the change in momentum.
10. Explain why the same change in momentum gives a smaller force if it takes longer.
11. Name three safety features that work by increasing the time of an impact.
12. Explain why a seat belt that stretches slightly is safer than one that does not stretch at all.

### Answers

1. 70 × 4.0 = **280 kg m/s**.
2. Velocity is a vector, so momentum has a direction too.
3. In a closed system, the total momentum before an event equals the total momentum after it.
4. 3.0 × 2.0 = 4.0 v → v = **1.5 m/s** in the original direction.
5. 50 × 3.0 = 75 v → v = **2.0 m/s**, in the opposite direction.
6. 4.0 × 1.5 = **6.0 kg m/s**, in the original direction.
7. F = m Δv / Δt.
8. 0.50 × 12 / 0.080 = **75 N**.
9. Δv = 5.0 − (−5.0) = 10 m/s; Δp = 0.10 × 10 = **1.0 kg m/s**.
10. Force equals the rate of change of momentum, so dividing the same change by a longer time gives a smaller
    force.
11. Any three: air bags, seat belts, gymnasium crash mats, cycle helmets, cushioned playground surfaces.
12. The stretching increases the time taken for the person to stop, so the rate of change of momentum, and
    so the force on the person, is smaller.
