These questions cover key question 2.5 of Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O
Level 2147, set through its three case studies: Korea, Cuba and
Vietnam. Each follows the Paper 1 pattern of a descriptive (a), an
explanatory (b) and an evaluative (c).

## Paper 1 style: Structured Questions

### Question 1: Korea, 1950–53

**(a)** Describe the involvement of the United Nations in the Korean
War. **[4]**

**(b)** Why did China enter the Korean War in 1950? **[6]**

**(c)** 'The Korean War was a success for the USA's policy of
containment.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four: after North Korea invaded on 25 June 1950 the
Security Council called on members to help South Korea; the USSR could
not veto this because it was boycotting the Council; a UN force was
formed, mostly American but with troops from Britain and other
members; it was commanded by General MacArthur; it landed at Inchon in
September 1950 and pushed north. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: UN forces crossed the 38th parallel and
advanced towards the Yalu River on China's border, so China feared an
American-led army on its frontier; the new communist government of
Mao Zedong (in power since 1949) wanted to protect a fellow communist
state; MacArthur's statements suggested the war might be carried into
China. **[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** the original aim of containment was achieved —
South Korea stayed non-communist; the UN showed collective action
against aggression; the USA demonstrated it would fight to stop
communist expansion. **Disagree:** the attempt to unite Korea under
the South failed after China intervened; the war ended in stalemate
and an armistice, not a peace; it cost about 36,000 American lives and
far more Korean and Chinese; China became a lasting enemy of the USA.
**Judgement:** a limited success — containment, not victory. **[10]**

### Question 2: Cuba, 1959–62

**(a)** Describe the Bay of Pigs invasion of 1961. **[4]**

**(b)** Why did Khrushchev place nuclear missiles in Cuba? **[6]**

**(c)** 'The USA was the winner of the Cuban Missile Crisis.' How far
do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four: in April 1961 about 1,400 Cuban exiles, trained and
equipped by the CIA, landed at the Bay of Pigs; Kennedy had approved
the plan inherited from Eisenhower but refused open US air support;
the expected rising against Castro did not happen; the invaders were
defeated within days and most captured; it humiliated the USA and
drove Castro closer to the USSR. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: to protect Cuba, the USSR's new ally, from
another American invasion after the Bay of Pigs; to close the gap in
the arms race, since the USA had more long-range missiles and had
placed Jupiter missiles in Turkey, close to the USSR; to gain a
bargaining position, for example over Berlin; to test the young
President Kennedy. **[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** the Soviet missiles were removed; Kennedy appeared
firm and in control; Khrushchev's reputation suffered and he was
removed in 1964. **Disagree:** Cuba stayed communist and the USA
promised not to invade it; the USA secretly removed its missiles from
Turkey; both sides had come close to nuclear war, which led to the
hotline and the Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963. **Judgement:** for
example, a public victory for the USA but in reality a compromise.
**[10]**

### Question 3: Vietnam, 1955–75

**(a)** What tactics did the Viet Cong use against the USA? **[4]**

**(b)** Why did the USA become involved in Vietnam after 1954? **[6]**

**(c)** 'The Tet Offensive was the main reason why the USA withdrew
from Vietnam.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four: guerrilla warfare in small groups, avoiding pitched
battles; hit-and-run ambushes; booby traps and mines; underground
tunnel networks such as those at Cu Chi; mixing with the civilian
population; supply by the Ho Chi Minh Trail through Laos and Cambodia.
**[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: the domino theory — the fear that if South
Vietnam fell, the rest of South-East Asia would follow; the Geneva
Accords (1954) left a communist North under Ho Chi Minh, and the USA
backed Diem in the South, who refused the 1956 elections it was feared
Ho would win; containment and American credibility as an ally; the
Gulf of Tonkin incident (August 1964) allowed Johnson to escalate.
**[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** although the Tet Offensive of January 1968 was a
military defeat for the Viet Cong, it showed Americans that the war
was far from won; public support fell; Johnson did not seek
re-election and began peace talks. **Other reasons:** the rising death
toll and cost; the anti-war movement and events such as My Lai; the
failure of US tactics against guerrillas; Nixon's Vietnamisation;
détente with the USSR and China. **Judgement:** Tet was the turning
point in opinion, but withdrawal came through Nixon's policy and the
Paris Peace Accords of 1973. **[10]**

### Question 4: Comparing the case studies

'US containment of communism was more successful in Korea than in
Cuba or Vietnam.' How far do you agree? **[10]**

#### Worked answer

A strong answer judges each case against its aim. **Korea:** South
Korea defended, though not reunited. **Cuba:** Castro's communist
government survived and the Bay of Pigs failed, but the missiles were
removed and the crisis did not spread communism further. **Vietnam:**
the whole country became communist in 1975, along with Laos and
Cambodia — a clear failure. **Judgement:** most answers will agree
that Korea was the greatest success, as its specific aim was met,
while showing that Cuba was a partial success. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Narrating each war instead of judging how effective containment was.
- Treating the Missile Crisis as a simple US victory.
- Missing the dates that frame each case: 1950–53, 1959–62, 1955–75.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is IGCSE History 0470 Paper 1 structured?**
Paper 1 (2 hours, 60 marks, 40%) has Section A on the Core Content (eight questions, answer two) and Section B on the Depth Studies (two on each, answer one). Each question is a three-part structured essay worth 4, 6 and 10 marks.

**Do 0470 and 2147 share the same Core Content?**
Yes. For 2024–2026, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470 and O Level History 2147 have the same Core Content key questions, so these questions suit both; only the papers' weightings and IGCSE's third component differ.

**What is the most common mistake on containment questions?**
Narrating each war rather than judging how effective containment was in Korea, Cuba and Vietnam.
