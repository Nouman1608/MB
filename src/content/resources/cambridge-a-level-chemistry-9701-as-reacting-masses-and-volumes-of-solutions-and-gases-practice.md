## Section B

**6.** Hydrated copper(II) sulfate is made from copper(II) oxide: CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l). The solution is crystallised to give CuSO₄·5H₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the theoretical mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O (formula mass 249.6) from 3.98 g of CuO (formula mass 79.5). **[2]**

**7.** 25.0 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid is titrated with 0.200 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. The mean titre is 18.75 cm³.

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in g dm⁻³. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O, so 3 × 30 = 90 cm³. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** The reactant that is completely used up, so it determines the amount of product formed [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**3.** n(KOH) = 5.61 ÷ 56.1 = 0.100 mol [1]; c = 0.100 ÷ 0.500 = 0.200 mol dm⁻³ [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, so 2 × 2.00 = 4.00 dm³ [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**5.** 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; n(Mg) = 0.486 ÷ 24.3 = 0.0200 mol = n(MgO) [1]; 0.0200 × 40.3 = 0.806 g [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**6. (a)** n(CuO) = 3.98 ÷ 79.5 = 0.0501 mol = n(CuSO₄·5H₂O) [1]; 0.0501 × 249.6 = 12.5 g [1].

**(b)** 10.6 ÷ 12.5 × 100 = 84.8 % [1].

**(c)** Some CuSO₄ stays dissolved in the solution after crystallisation [1]. Some crystals are lost during filtering and transfer, or some CuO does not react [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**7. (a)** H₂SO₄(aq) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l) [1].

**(b)** n(NaOH) = 0.200 × 18.75 ÷ 1000 = 3.75 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(H₂SO₄) = 1.875 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 1.875 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0750 mol dm⁻³ [1].

**(c)** 0.0750 × 98.1 = 7.36 g dm⁻³ [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**8. (a)** n(Mg) = 2.43 ÷ 24.3 = 0.100 mol; n(HCl) = 0.500 × 0.100 = 0.0500 mol [1]. 0.100 mol Mg would need 0.200 mol HCl, so HCl is limiting [1].

**(b)** n(H₂) = 0.0500 ÷ 2 = 0.0250 mol; V = 0.600 dm³ [1].

**(c)** Mg used = 0.0250 mol, so 0.0750 mol left [1]; 0.0750 × 24.3 = 1.82 g [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**9. (a)** CO₂ = 90 − 30 = 60 cm³ [1]; x = 60 ÷ 15 = 4 [1].

**(b)** O₂ used = 120 − 30 = 90 cm³, which is 6 volumes per volume of hydrocarbon [1]. 4 + y/4 = 6, so y = 8 [1].

**(c)** C₄H₈(g) + 6O₂(g) → 4CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l) [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**10. (a)** CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) [1].

**(b)** n(CaCO₃) = 1.00 ÷ 100.1 = 9.99 × 10⁻³ mol = n(CO₂) [1]; V = 9.99 × 10⁻³ × 24 000 = 240 cm³ [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**11. (a)** HCl added = 0.800 × 0.0500 = 0.0400 mol [1]. HCl reacting with NaOH = n(NaOH) = 0.500 × 0.03604 = 0.01802 mol [1].

**(b)** HCl reacting with CaCO₃ = 0.0400 − 0.01802 = 0.02198 mol, so n(CaCO₃) = 0.01099 mol [1]; mass = 0.01099 × 100.1 = 1.10 g [1].

**(c)** 1.10 ÷ 1.25 × 100 = 88.0 % [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**12. (a)** n(Cu) = 0.635 ÷ 63.5 = 0.0100 mol [1]; n(Ag) = 2.16 ÷ 107.9 = 0.0200 mol [1].

**(b)** Cu : Ag = 1 : 2 [1]; Cu(s) + 2Ag⁺(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + 2Ag(s) [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**13. (a)** Three (or four) significant figures [1], because the acid concentration is given to three significant figures and the answer should match the least precise data, or have one more [1].

**(b)** 0.0447 mol dm⁻³ (or 0.04471) [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to divide cm³ by 1000.
- Missing the 2 : 1 ratio with sulfuric acid.
- Choosing the limiting reagent from masses rather than scaled moles.
- In gas-volume problems, forgetting that NaOH removes CO₂ and that the remaining gas is unused oxygen.
- In back titrations, forgetting to subtract the excess acid before using the ratio.
- Copying a long calculator display as the final answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find a hydrocarbon formula from combustion gas volumes?**
The contraction when sodium hydroxide absorbs CO₂ gives the CO₂ volume and so x. The oxygen used, per volume of hydrocarbon, equals x + y/4, which gives y.

**What is a back titration?**
An excess of reagent is added to the sample, and the unused excess is then titrated. Subtracting the excess from the amount added gives the amount that reacted with the sample.
