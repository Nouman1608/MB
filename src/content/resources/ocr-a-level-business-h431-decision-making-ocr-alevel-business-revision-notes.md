Condensed notes on the Decision making content (1.13 on this site) in Business objectives and strategy, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and the Kinloch and Rowan Hill worked examples are in the [decision making study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-decision-making-ocr-alevel-business/); test yourself with the [decision making practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-decision-making-ocr-alevel-business-practice/). The paired overview is the [decision making and decision trees revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-decision-making-and-decision-trees-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

- Nature and purpose of decision making; impact and importance of effective decisions.
- Factors: level of risk, nature of risk, accuracy of forecasts, volatility, potential for bias.
- Relationship between aims and objectives and decision making.
- Short-, medium- and long-term decisions; strategic, tactical and operational decisions.
- Quantitative and qualitative decision-making tools.
- Usefulness of financial and non-financial measures of performance.
- Which course of action to take; the extent to which a business is a success.

## Core recall

### Stages (a standard model)

Set objective → gather information → identify options (including doing nothing) → evaluate → choose and implement → review.

- The objective sets the criteria for every later stage.
- The review checks the decision moved the business towards its objective.

### Importance of effective decisions

- Impact reaches owners (profit), employees (jobs), customers (products and prices), suppliers and lenders (payment), community (jobs).
- Importance rises with size of commitment and difficulty of reversal.

### The five factors

| Factor | Key question |
|---|---|
| Level of risk | How much could be lost, compared with what the business can absorb? |
| Nature of risk | Quantifiable or not? Short-lived or permanent? Insurable? |
| Accuracy of forecasts | Where did the figures come from, and how reliable were past forecasts? |
| Volatility | How fast are conditions changing? Are reversible options better? |
| Potential for bias | Are managers favouring an option for personal reasons? |

### Common biases

- Optimism: sales too high, costs and time too low.
- Confirmation: seeking only supporting evidence.
- Sunk cost thinking: continuing because of money already spent.
- Groupthink: agreeing to avoid conflict.
- Reduce bias with criteria set in advance, independent challenge and honest review.

### Two classifications

- Level: strategic (whole business, senior managers, costly, hard to reverse); tactical (one function or period, middle managers); operational (day to day, supervisors).
- Time: short, medium, long term. The specification sets no exact periods; state yours.
- They often line up but need not: a cash-crisis sale can be short-term and strategic.

### Tools

| Quantitative | Qualitative |
|---|---|
| Investment appraisal, decision trees, break-even, ratios, forecasts | SWOT, expert opinion, staff and customer views, Ansoff's matrix |
| Objective comparison of what can be measured | Captures context, ethics, fit |
| Only as good as the inputs | Subjective |

### Weighted scoring matrix (not named in the specification)

- Weighted score = sum of (weight × score) for each criterion.
- Combines financial and non-financial criteria.
- Weights and scores are judgements; a small change can reverse the ranking.

### Judging success

- Compare results with each objective.
- Use financial and non-financial measures: financial shows the outcome; non-financial often explains it and warns earlier.
- Compare over time and with competitors.
- Take more than one stakeholder's view.
- Conclude "to what extent", not "yes or no".

## Quick worked reminder

Rowan Hill Hotels (invented): revenue up 11% (target 8%); net margin 9.0% (target 12%); occupancy 84% (target 80%); staff turnover 31% (target below 20%); satisfaction 4.6 (target 4.5). A partial success: strong with customers, weak in converting sales to profit; staff turnover is the priority.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Decision making | Choosing between alternatives to achieve objectives |
| Strategic decision | A long-term, whole-business choice made by senior managers |
| Tactical decision | A medium-term choice about how to deliver a strategy in one area |
| Operational decision | A day-to-day choice about routine running |
| Volatility | How quickly and how much conditions change |
| Bias | A systematic tendency to judge unfairly in favour of one option |
| Quantitative tool | A numerical method of comparing options |
| Qualitative tool | A judgement-based method using non-numerical evidence |

## Exam traps

- Level of risk and nature of risk are separate factors.
- Strategic does not always mean long term.
- A scoring matrix looks precise but rests on judgement.
- Do not judge success on profit alone.
- Recommend a course of action with a condition, not "it depends" on its own.

## Self-test

1. State the stages of a structured decision.
2. How does a business's objective affect its decision making?
3. Distinguish level of risk from nature of risk.
4. Explain why volatility favours reversible options.
5. Name and explain one type of bias.
6. Classify "deciding tomorrow's delivery routes" by level and time horizon.
7. Give an example of a short-term strategic decision.
8. Give two quantitative and two qualitative decision-making tools.
9. Weights 0.5, 0.3, 0.2; scores 6, 8, 5. Calculate the weighted score.
10. Give one limitation of a weighted scoring matrix.
11. Revenue £2.5m, net profit £200,000, target margin 10%. Was the target met?
12. Why use non-financial measures when judging success?

### Answers

1. Set objective, gather information, identify options, evaluate, choose and implement, review.
2. It sets the criteria for comparing options, for example cash for survival or market share for growth.
3. Level is how much could be lost relative to what the business can absorb; nature is the kind of risk, such as whether it can be quantified or insured.
4. When conditions change quickly, a decision that can be undone limits the loss if assumptions turn out wrong.
5. For example, sunk cost thinking: continuing a failing project because money has already been spent.
6. Operational and short term.
7. Selling a division in an emergency to survive a cash crisis.
8. Quantitative: any two of investment appraisal, decision trees, break-even, ratio analysis, forecasts. Qualitative: any two of SWOT, expert opinion, staff or customer views, Ansoff's matrix.
9. 3.0 + 2.4 + 1.0 = **6.4**.
10. Weights and scores are subjective, so a small change can reverse the result.
11. 200,000 ÷ 2,500,000 × 100 = **8%**, so no, the target was missed.
12. They explain the causes of financial results, such as staff turnover raising costs, and can warn of problems before profit falls.
