## Questions

**1.** (a) Predict the charges on the simple ions formed by strontium and selenium. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the formulas of ammonium carbonate and aluminium sulfate. **[2]**

**2.** Ammonia reacts with boron trifluoride, BF₃, to form an addition compound.

**(a)** State what is meant by a co-ordinate bond. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw the displayed formula of the product, using an arrow to show the co-ordinate bond. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why boron trifluoride can accept a pair of electrons. **[1]**

**3.** **(a)** Outline how the particles in magnesium metal are held together. **[2]**
**(b)** Account for magnesium melting at a higher temperature than sodium. **[3]**

**4.** Four solids, P to S, were tested. Data are invented for this question.

| Solid | Melting point / °C | Solid conducts? | Molten conducts? | Soluble in water? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | 782 | No | Yes | Yes |
| Q | Above 2500 | No | Not melted | No |
| R | 56 | No | No | No |
| S | 930 | Yes | Yes | No |

State the type of structure of each solid and give one piece of evidence for each. **[4]**

**5.** **(a)** Draw a diagram of one layer of graphite, showing at least three joined rings. **[2]**
**(b)** Graphite conducts electricity, whereas diamond is an insulator. Explain the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the layers of graphite slide over one another easily. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Explain why the temperature of a pure solid stays constant while it melts. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is overcome when solid iodine sublimes and when sodium chloride melts, and why the second needs a much higher temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why ice floats on liquid water. **[2]**

**7.** Boron trifluoride, BF₃, and nitrogen trifluoride, NF₃, are both molecules with three fluorine atoms. Predict the shape of each and explain why their bond angles differ. **[5]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why SF₆ is octahedral, and give its bond angle. **[3]**
**(b)** Xenon has 8 outer electrons. Deduce the shape of XeF₄, explaining why it is not tetrahedral. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** State the shape of PCl₅ and the bond angles in it. **[3]**
**(b)** The ion ICl₂⁻ has a central iodine atom with 7 outer electrons. Deduce its shape and bond angle. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** State what is meant by electronegativity. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the C-Cl bond is polar. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why BF₃ has polar bonds but no permanent dipole, whereas CH₂Cl₂ has a permanent dipole. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** The boiling points of HCl, HBr and HI increase in that order, but HF boils at a much higher temperature than HCl. Explain both observations. **[5]**
**(b)** Ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH, and methoxymethane, CH₃OCH₃, both have relative molecular mass 46.0. Explain why ethanol has the higher boiling point. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Strontium is in Group 2, so **Sr²⁺** [1]. Selenium is in Group 16, so **Se²⁻** [1].
**(b)** Two NH₄⁺ balance one CO₃²⁻: **(NH₄)₂CO₃** [1]. Two Al³⁺ (+6) balance three SO₄²⁻ (-6): **Al₂(SO₄)₃** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Brackets round the compound ion are usually needed to show a multiple, and a formula written without them, such as Al₂SO₄₃, would not be clear.

**2. (a)** A pair of electrons shared between two atoms, **both supplied by the same atom** [1].
**(b)** H₃N→BF₃, drawn with N bonded to three H, B bonded to three F, and the arrow pointing from N to B [1]. All other bonds drawn as lines [1].
**(c)** Boron has only **six electrons** in its outer shell in BF₃ (three bonding pairs), so it has a vacancy for a lone pair from nitrogen [1].
*Examiner insight:* The direction of the arrow is the point being tested. An arrow drawn from boron to nitrogen names the wrong atom as donor.

**3. (a)** Positive **Mg²⁺ ions** arranged in a lattice [1], attracted electrostatically to the **delocalised electrons** around them [1].
**(b)** Magnesium ions have a higher charge and are smaller [1]. Each atom contributes two delocalised electrons, not one [1]. The attraction between ions and delocalised electrons is stronger, so more energy is needed to melt it [1].
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both substances addressed. Stating only that magnesium has strong bonding does not explain the difference from sodium.

**4.** **P ionic**: conducts only when molten, and dissolves in water [1]. **Q macromolecular (giant covalent)**: melting point above 2500 °C and no conduction [1]. **R molecular**: low melting point, no conduction [1]. **S metallic**: conducts as a solid [1].
*Examiner insight:* Naming the structure alone is not enough. A property taken from the table is required to support each choice.

**5. (a)** Hexagons sharing edges [1], with each carbon joined to three others [1].
**(b)** Each carbon in graphite uses three outer electrons in bonding, so one per atom is **delocalised** and free to move between layers [1]. Diamond has all four outer electrons held in localised covalent bonds, so no electrons are free to move [1].
**(c)** Only weak **induced dipole-dipole forces** act between layers [1], so little energy is needed to move them past each other [1].
*Examiner insight:* Saying that the layers are "not bonded" is weaker than naming the force that acts between them.

**6. (a)** The energy supplied is used to overcome the attractions between particles [1] and raises their potential energy but not their mean kinetic energy, so the temperature stays constant [1].
**(b)** Iodine: weak intermolecular forces between I₂ molecules, not the covalent bond in I₂ [1]. Sodium chloride: strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in the lattice, which needs much more energy [1].
**(c)** In ice every molecule forms hydrogen bonds with four neighbours, giving an open lattice with large gaps [1]. In liquid water some of these bonds break and molecules pack closer, so ice is less dense [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Bonds break" is ambiguous for molecular substances. Specify intermolecular forces or covalent bonds.

**7.** BF₃ has three bonding pairs and no lone pair, so it is **trigonal planar**, 120° [1]. The pairs repel equally [1]. NF₃ has four electron pairs: three bonding and one lone pair [1], so it is **trigonal pyramidal** [1]. The lone pair repels more than a bonding pair, which squeezes the bond angle to less than 109.5° [1].
*Examiner insight:* A shape and an angle without a reason about repulsion between pairs earns less than a full explanation.

**8. (a)** Sulfur is surrounded by six bonding pairs and no lone pair [1]. Equal repulsion pushes the pairs to maximum separation [1], giving **octahedral** with all angles **90°** [1].
**(b)** Xenon has 8 + 4 = 12 outer electrons, so six pairs: four bonding and two lone [1]. The two lone pairs sit opposite each other to reduce lone pair-lone pair repulsion [1], so the shape is **square planar** with 90° angles. A tetrahedron has only four pairs [1].
*Examiner insight:* For six pairs with two lone pairs, state where the lone pairs are placed, not only the name of the shape.

**9. (a)** Phosphorus has five bonding pairs and no lone pair [1], giving **trigonal bipyramidal** [1] with angles of **120° and 90°** [1].
**(b)** Iodine has 7 + 2 + 1 = 10 electrons, so five pairs: two bonding and three lone [1]. The lone pairs lie in the trigonal plane, leaving the shape **linear**, 180° [1].
*Examiner insight:* Counting the extra electron from the negative charge is the step that is often missed in questions on ions.

**10. (a)** The ability of an atom to draw the bonding pair of electrons towards itself in a covalent bond [1].
**(b)** Chlorine is more electronegative than carbon, so the bond is C(δ+)-Cl(δ-) [1].
**(c)** B-F bonds are polar [1], but BF₃ is symmetrical (trigonal planar) so the dipoles cancel [1]. CH₂Cl₂ has two different types of outer atom, so its dipoles do not cancel and it has a net dipole [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bond-polarity answer should name the more electronegative atom and use partial-charge labels on the right atoms.

**11. (a)** From HCl to HI the number of electrons increases [1], so the induced dipole-dipole forces strengthen [1], and a greater energy input is needed to separate molecules [1]. HF has hydrogen bonds [1], involving hydrogen on a highly electronegative fluorine and a lone pair on a neighbouring fluorine, which are stronger than the other forces present [1].
**(b)** Ethanol has an O-H bond, so its molecules form hydrogen bonds [1]. Methoxymethane has no hydrogen on oxygen, so only permanent dipole-dipole and induced dipole-dipole forces act [1]. With equal relative molecular mass the induced forces are similar, so the extra hydrogen bonding makes ethanol's boiling point higher [1].
*Examiner insight:* A comparison of two forces should state which is stronger, and extended answers are easier to credit when they follow the order: force, strength, energy needed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out lone pairs when counting electron pairs for an ion.
- Giving a shape name without any reference to repulsion between pairs.
- Referring to "breaking bonds" when a molecular substance changes state.
- Treating polar bonds as meaning a polar molecule.
- Naming hydrogen bonding where the hydrogen is bonded to carbon.
- Failing to make a comparison explicit when asked "why is X higher than Y".
- Drawing a co-ordinate arrow in the wrong direction.
