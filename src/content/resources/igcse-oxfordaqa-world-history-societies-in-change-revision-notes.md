**These notes condense Section A of OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245) Paper 2, the societies in change study.** Because every question asks how people's lives changed, the notes are organised as "groups and experiences" grids for each option, plus a technique sheet for the four questions (3 + 6 + 9 + 12 = 30 marks). Full explanations: [Societies in change study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-world-history-societies-in-change-study-guide/). Practice: [Societies in change practice questions](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-world-history-societies-in-change-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA 9245, VERSION 1.1 -- 3.2.1 SECTION A** -- choose one: 3.2.1.1 Life in Germany, 1919-1949; 3.2.1.2 Life in Russia and the USSR, 1914-1953; 3.2.1.3 Life in the USA, 1920-1968.

## Technique sheet

| Q | Marks | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Three precise facts about the issue -- no explanation needed |
| 2 | 6 | ONE way, fully explained: point, evidence, explanation of the effect |
| 3 | 9 | TWO ways or changes, each with its own developed paragraph |
| 4 | 12 | The given factor + at least one other, explained, then a judgement on which mattered more and why |

## Germany, 1919-1949: groups and experiences

| Group | Weimar (1919-33) | Nazi (1933-45) | Post-war (1945-49) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workers | Wages rose after 1924; unemployment soared after 1929 | Jobs via public works and rearmament; unions banned | Ruined cities, food shortages |
| Farmers | Falling prices, debt | Promised support; mixed results | Shortages; land reforms in Soviet zone |
| Women | Vote, new jobs, "new woman" | Pushed to motherhood; later war work | Rebuilding ("rubble women") in ruined cities |
| Jewish people | Full citizens, prominent in culture | Persecution, Nuremberg Laws, Final Solution | Survivors displaced |
| Elderly and savers | Ruined by 1923 hyperinflation | | Refugees and expellees |
| Big business | Some gained from inflation | Profited from rearmament | Dismantling in Soviet zone |

**Dates:** 1919 Spartacists; 1920 Kapp; 1923 Ruhr, hyperinflation, Munich Putsch; 1924 Dawes; 1929 Young Plan; 1933 Hitler Chancellor, Enabling Act; 1935 Nuremberg Laws; 1938 Kristallnacht; 1942 Wannsee; 1944 bomb plot; 1945 defeat and zones; 1948-49 Berlin Blockade; 1949 FRG and GDR.

## Russia and the USSR, 1914-1953: groups and experiences

| Group | 1914-24 | 1924-41 | 1941-53 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peasants | War losses; seized land in 1917; requisitioning, then NEP | Collectivisation; kulaks deported; Holodomor | Wartime destruction; post-war farm stagnation |
| Industrial workers | Shortages; workers' control; War Communism | Five-Year Plans, discipline, crowded cities | War production; rebuilding |
| Women | New legal rights after 1917 | "New Soviet Woman": factory work plus home | Soldiers, snipers (Pavlichenko), workers |
| Religious and ethnic minorities | Church attacked | Persecution, deportation, Russification | Deportations continue |
| Party and army | Bolshevik dictatorship | Purges of officers and old Bolsheviks | High Stalinism; Beria's NKVD |

**Dates:** 1917 abdication and Bolshevik revolution; 1918-21 Civil War; 1921 Kronstadt, NEP; 1924 Lenin dies; 1928 First Five-Year Plan; 1929 collectivisation; 1932-33 Holodomor; 1936-38 Great Terror; 1941 German invasion; 1945 victory; 1953 Stalin dies.

## USA, 1920-1968: groups and experiences

| Group | 1920s | 1929-45 | 1945-68 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Middle class | Cars, consumer goods, shares | Lost savings; New Deal mortgage help | Suburbs, TV, prosperity |
| Farmers | Overproduction, debt | Dust Bowl; AAA support | Fewer, larger farms |
| African Americans | Jim Crow, KKK; NAACP, UNIA | Limited New Deal help; war jobs and service | Civil rights movement; 1964 and 1968 Acts |
| Women | Vote; flappers; Sheppard-Towner Act | Problems in Depression; war work | Feminist movement; Equal Pay Act 1963 |
| Immigrants and minorities | Quotas 1921, 1924; Sacco and Vanzetti | Japanese American internment | |

**Dates:** 1920 Prohibition, 19th Amendment; 1921 quotas, Sheppard-Towner; 1924 National Origins Act; 1929 Crash; 1932 Roosevelt elected; 1933 banking crisis and Hundred Days; 1941 Lend-Lease; 1954 Brown; 1955-56 Montgomery; 1957 Little Rock; 1961 Freedom Rides; 1963 Equal Pay Act; 1964 Civil Rights Act; 1968 Civil Rights Act.

## Worked example: question 2

*Explain one way in which the Depression changed the lives of German farmers.* **[6]**

"One way was falling incomes. After 1929 food prices collapsed, so many farmers could not repay debts and lost their farms to the banks. This made them desperate for change, which helps explain why many rural Protestant voters turned to the Nazis, who promised to protect farmers and blamed Jewish bankers." One point, specific evidence, explained consequence.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hyperinflation | The collapse of the German mark in 1923, when prices rose so fast that savings became worthless |
| Volksgemeinschaft | The Nazi idea of a "people's community" that put the nation before the individual |
| Kinder, Küche, Kirche | "Children, kitchen, church": the Nazi ideal role for women |
| NEP | Lenin's New Economic Policy (1921), allowing small private trade and ending grain requisitioning |
| Collectivisation | Stalin's merging of peasant farms into state-controlled collective farms from 1929 |
| Kulak | A better-off peasant; kulaks were persecuted and deported under collectivisation |
| New Deal | Roosevelt's programmes of relief, recovery and reform from 1933 |
| Jim Crow laws | State laws enforcing racial segregation in the southern USA |

## Exam traps

- Answering question 2 with two ways (only one is credited).
- Describing events with no named group.
- Forgetting the post-war part (Germany 1945-49; USSR 1945-53).
- In question 4, failing to compare factors in the conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**How specific should I be about groups?**
Name them as the specification does: women, workers, farmers, minorities, the young, and so on.

**Can the same content answer different questions?**
Yes; for example, the Holodomor can support questions on peasants, collectivisation or Ukraine.

## Self-test

1. What was the Rentenmark? *The new currency of November 1923 that ended hyperinflation.*
2. Who was Lyudmila Pavlichenko? *A Soviet sniper used in wartime propaganda.*
3. What did the Sheppard-Towner Act fund? *Maternal and infant health care (1921).*
4. When were the FRG and GDR created? *1949.*
5. What was the UNIA? *Marcus Garvey's Universal Negro Improvement Association.*
