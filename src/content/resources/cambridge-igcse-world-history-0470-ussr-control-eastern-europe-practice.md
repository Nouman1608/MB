These questions cover key question 6 of Core Content Option B: "How secure was the USSR's control over Eastern Europe,
1948-c.1989?"

## Section A

**1.** Give **three** reasons why many Hungarians opposed Rákosi's government before 1956. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** reforms promised by Imre Nagy in October-November 1956. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** reforms of the Prague Spring. **[3]**

**4.** Give **two** ways in which Czechs and Slovaks resisted the invasion of August 1968. **[2]**

**5.** State **three** problems the flow of refugees through Berlin caused for East Germany. **[3]**

**6.** Identify **three** reasons for the growth of Solidarity in 1980-81. **[3]**

**7.** Give **two** ways in which events in Hungary helped bring down the Berlin Wall in 1989. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the events in Hungary between 23 October and 4 November 1956. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the actions of the Polish government against Solidarity in 1981-82. **[4]**

**10.** Why was the Berlin Wall built in 1961? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** "Events in Hungary in 1956 and in Czechoslovakia in 1968 were more different than similar." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**12.** "Gorbachev was personally responsible for the collapse of Soviet control over Eastern Europe." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: the terror of the ÁVH secret police (1); thousands imprisoned or executed (1); low living
standards and food shortages (1); forced collectivisation of farms (1); the presence of Soviet troops (1); attacks on
the Catholic Church, including the imprisonment of Cardinal Mindszenty (1); resentment of Soviet control of Hungarian
national life (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: free elections (1); a multi-party system (1); withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact (1);
neutrality for Hungary (1); the withdrawal of Soviet troops (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: the end of censorship of press, radio and television (1); more freedom to travel abroad
(1); some economic decentralisation and less central planning (1); more rights for Slovakia (1); open debate within
the Communist Party (1); rehabilitation of victims of earlier purges (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: removing or changing street signs to confuse troops (1); talking to and arguing with
soldiers (1); protest demonstrations and strikes (1); self-burning in protest, as by Jan Palach (1); secret radio
broadcasts (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: loss of young people (1); loss of skilled workers such as doctors, engineers and
teachers (1); damage to the economy and labour shortages (1); humiliation, because it showed people preferred the West
(1); West Berlin acted as a shop window for capitalism (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: food price rises and shortages (1); the strikes at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk (1);
the leadership of Lech Wałęsa (1); the Gdańsk Agreement, which made it legal (1); the support of the Catholic Church
and the Polish pope (1); widespread anger at corruption and economic failure (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: Hungary opened its border with Austria (1); thousands of East Germans used it to escape to
the West (1); this showed that the Wall could be bypassed, so the East German government lost control of emigration
(1); Hungary's reforming government showed the USSR would not stop change (1). **[2]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: on 23 October students and workers demonstrated in Budapest (1); Stalin's statue was
pulled down (1); fighting broke out with the secret police and Soviet troops (1); Imre Nagy became prime minister (1);
Soviet troops withdrew from Budapest (1); Nagy announced free elections and on 1 November withdrawal from the Warsaw
Pact (1); on 4 November Soviet tanks entered Budapest (1); the rising was crushed and Kádár installed (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: General Jaruzelski declared martial law in December 1981 (1); Solidarity was suspended
and then banned (1); its leaders, including Wałęsa, were arrested and interned (1); a curfew was imposed and travel
restricted (1); strikes were broken by the army and police (1); telephone lines were cut and censorship tightened (1);
some protesters were killed (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **to stop emigration**, because millions had crossed into West Berlin since 1949, and the number rose sharply
in 1961, so East Germany was being drained; **economic damage**, because those leaving were often young and skilled,
so industry and services were short of workers; **propaganda**, because the contrast between prosperous West Berlin
and the East showed communism in a bad light; **failure of diplomacy**, because Khrushchev's demands that the West
leave Berlin and the Vienna summit had failed, so a wall was a way to solve the problem without war. **[6]**

**11.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Different:** Nagy aimed to leave the Warsaw Pact and end one-party rule, while Dubček wanted reformed communism
within the Pact; Hungarians fought with weapons, while Czechs and Slovaks resisted mainly peacefully; Hungary was
invaded by Soviet forces, Czechoslovakia by several Warsaw Pact states; thousands died in Hungary and Nagy was
executed, while Dubček survived. **Similar:** both were caused by resentment of Soviet-style rule and economic
failure; both were led by reforming communists; both ended in invasion and a hard-line leader; the West condemned but
did not intervene in either; both showed that Soviet control depended on force. **Judgement:** for example, the aims
and methods differed, but the outcome and what it revealed about Soviet control were the same, so they were more
similar than different. **[10]**

**12.** Levels as for Question 11. Indicative content. **Agree:** Gorbachev's glasnost encouraged open criticism; he
abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine, so satellite governments could not rely on Soviet tanks; he announced troop cuts in
1988 and refused to use force in 1989, when the Wall was opened; he encouraged reform in Eastern Europe. **Disagree:**
the Soviet economy was stagnating and could no longer afford its empire or the arms race; the Afghanistan war drained
resources; Solidarity had already shown the strength of popular opposition; Hungarian reformers opened the border;
mass protests in Leipzig, Berlin and Prague forced the pace; Soviet control had always depended on force, not
consent. **Judgement:** for example, Gorbachev did not intend the collapse, but his refusal to use force allowed
long-term weaknesses and popular movements to succeed, so he was essential but not solely responsible. **[10]**
