Condensed recall for subtopic 7.1 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070. For explanations and a worked comparison of strong and weak acids, use the [acids and bases study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-the-characteristic-properties-of-acids-and-bases/); to practise, try the [acids and bases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-the-characteristic-properties-of-acids-and-bases-practice/).

## Definitions

- Aqueous acids contain **H⁺** ions; aqueous alkalis contain **OH⁻** ions.
- **Acid:** proton donor. **Base:** proton acceptor.
- **Bases** are oxides or hydroxides of metals. **Alkalis** are soluble bases.
- **Strong acid:** completely dissociated in aqueous solution. **Weak acid:** partially dissociated.
- **Neutralisation:** reaction between an acid and a base. For acid + alkali: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton | A hydrogen ion, H⁺ |
| Alkali | A soluble base; releases OH⁻ in water |
| Dissociation | Splitting of a molecule into ions in water |
| Spectator ion | Ion unchanged in a reaction; left out of the ionic equation |
| Indicator | Substance whose colour depends on whether a solution is acidic or alkaline |
| pH | Scale of acidity and alkalinity; 7 is neutral |
| Salt | Compound formed when the H⁺ of an acid is replaced by a metal ion or ammonium ion |

## Reactions to know

```
acid + metal      → salt + hydrogen                     e.g. Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂
acid + base       → salt + water                        e.g. CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O
acid + carbonate  → salt + water + carbon dioxide       e.g. CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂
base + ammonium salt → salt + ammonia + water (warm)    e.g. NaOH + NH₄NO₃ → NaNO₃ + NH₃ + H₂O
```

Ionic versions: metal + 2H⁺ → metal ion + H₂; CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂; NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃ + H₂O.

Naming salts: hydrochloric → chloride; sulfuric → sulfate; nitric → nitrate; ethanoic → ethanoate.

## Indicators

| Indicator | Acid | Alkali |
|---|---|---|
| Litmus | Red | Blue |
| Thymolphthalein | Colourless | Blue |
| Methyl orange | Red | Yellow |
| Universal indicator | Red / orange / yellow (pH below 7) | Blue / purple (pH above 7); green at pH 7 |

Lower pH = higher H⁺ concentration = more acidic. Higher pH = more alkaline.

## Comparing solutions with universal indicator paper

1. Dip a clean glass rod into the solution.
2. Touch it onto universal indicator paper.
3. Match the colour to the pH chart at once.
4. Rinse the rod with distilled water before the next solution.

The solution with the lower pH has the higher H⁺ concentration and is more acidic. pH 7 (green) is neutral.

## Observations to quote

| Reaction | What you see |
|---|---|
| Acid + reactive metal | Fizzing; metal gets smaller; gas pops with a lighted splint |
| Acid + carbonate | Fizzing; solid dissolves; gas turns limewater milky |
| Acid + insoluble base | Solid disappears; coloured solution if a transition metal salt forms (e.g. blue copper(II) sulfate) |
| Alkali + acid | No visible change unless an indicator is present; mixture warms |
| Base + ammonium salt, warmed | Pungent gas; damp red litmus turns blue |

## Writing ionic equations

1. Write the balanced full equation with state symbols.
2. Split every aqueous ionic substance into its ions; keep solids, liquids, gases and water as formulae.
3. Cross out ions that are identical on both sides (spectator ions).
4. Check atoms and charges balance.

Example: Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) becomes CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → H₂O(l) + CO₂(g).

## Dissociation equations

| Acid | Type | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric | Strong | HCl(aq) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) |
| Nitric | Strong | HNO₃(aq) → H⁺(aq) + NO₃⁻(aq) |
| Sulfuric | Strong | H₂SO₄(aq) → 2H⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) |
| Ethanoic | Weak | CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq) |

Strong: single arrow. Weak: reversible arrow ⇌.

## Strong versus weak, same concentration

| Property | Strong (e.g. HCl) | Weak (e.g. CH₃COOH) |
|---|---|---|
| Dissociation | Complete | Partial |
| H⁺ concentration | Higher | Lower |
| pH | Lower | Higher (but still below 7) |
| Rate with Mg or CaCO₃ | Faster | Slower |
| Total gas with excess metal, equal amounts of acid | Same | Same |

## Exam traps

- All alkalis are bases, but insoluble bases (CuO, Fe(OH)₃) are not alkalis.
- Acid + carbonate always gives **three** products.
- Copper does not react with dilute acids.
- Ammonia from an ammonium salt needs a **base** and **warming**; test with **damp red** litmus.
- Strong ≠ concentrated; weak ≠ dilute.
- Single arrow for strong acids; ⇌ for weak acids.
- Sulfuric acid releases **two** H⁺ per molecule.
- Thymolphthalein is colourless in acid, not pink.

## Self-test

1. State the ion present in all aqueous acids.
2. Define a base in terms of protons.
3. Explain why copper(II) oxide is a base but not an alkali.
4. Write the ionic equation for neutralisation of an acid by an alkali.
5. Name the products of zinc carbonate and hydrochloric acid.
6. Name the gas formed when ammonium sulfate is warmed with sodium hydroxide, and give its test.
7. State the colour of methyl orange in alkali.
8. Write the equation for the dissociation of nitric acid in water.
9. Write the equation for the dissociation of ethanoic acid in water.
10. Define a weak acid.
11. Two acids of equal concentration have pH 1 and pH 3. Which is the weak acid?
12. Which ions are spectators in NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O?

**Answers**

1. H⁺.
2. A proton acceptor.
3. It is a metal oxide that neutralises acids, but it is insoluble in water.
4. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
5. Zinc chloride, water and carbon dioxide.
6. Ammonia; turns damp red litmus paper blue.
7. Yellow.
8. HNO₃(aq) → H⁺(aq) + NO₃⁻(aq).
9. CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq).
10. An acid that is partially dissociated in aqueous solution.
11. The one with pH 3 (lower H⁺ concentration).
12. Na⁺ and Cl⁻.
