These questions cover section 2.4 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Interpretation of data presented in tabular, pictorial or diagrammatic form". Diagrams are described in words or by their key values. Revise first with the [interpretation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-interpretation-data-tabular-pictorial-diagrammatic/) or the [interpretation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-interpretation-data-tabular-pictorial-diagrammatic-revision-notes/). All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** In a pie chart showing how people voted, the sector for Option A is 90° and represents 45 people.

**(a)** Find the total number of people represented. **[1]**

**(b)** The sector for Option B is 48°. How many people chose Option B? **[1]**

**2.** In a pictogram, one symbol represents 8 visitors. Tuesday's row has 2½ symbols and Wednesday's row has 4¼ symbols. How many more visitors came on Wednesday than on Tuesday? **[2]**

**3.** A sectional bar chart for one week shows three sections, with tops at 25, 40 and 70 on the vertical scale. Find the size of each section. **[2]**

**4.** A percentage bar chart shows that 30% of the members of a club are juniors. There are 54 juniors. How many members does the club have? **[1]**

**5.** Comparative pie charts show the visitors to two parks. The larger chart has radius 5 cm and represents 5000 visitors. The smaller chart has radius 3 cm.

**(a)** Find the number of visitors represented by the smaller chart. **[2]**

**(b)** In the smaller chart, the sector for "under 16" is 72°. How many visitors were under 16? **[1]**

**6.** In a Venn diagram for sets A and B, there are 12 in A only, 5 in both, 9 in B only and 4 in neither.

**(a)** How many are represented altogether? **[1]**

**(b)** How many are in A? **[1]**

**(c)** What fraction of those in B are also in A? **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Box-and-whisker diagrams show the marks in a test for two classes.

| | Minimum | Q1 | Median | Q3 | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class X | 22 | 35 | 48 | 60 | 81 |
| Class Y | 30 | 42 | 50 | 55 | 70 |

**(a)** Find the interquartile range for each class. **[2]**

**(b)** Compare the marks of the two classes. **[3]**

**(c)** Approximately what fraction of Class Y scored more than 55? **[1]**

**8.** The heights, in cm, of 20 plants are shown in a stem-and-leaf diagram.

```
14 | 2 5 8
15 | 0 3 3 6 7 9
16 | 1 1 2 4 8
17 | 0 3 5 9
18 | 4
19 |
20 | 6
Key: 15 | 3 means 153 cm
```

**(a)** Find the median height. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the range. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the interquartile range, using the halves method. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the interquartile range is a better measure of spread than the range for these data. **[1]**

**(e)** What does the empty row for stem 19 show? **[1]**

**9.** Comparative pie charts show a factory's energy use in two years. Year 1 (400 units) has radius 4 cm; Year 2 has radius 5 cm. The renewable sector is 90° in Year 1 and 72° in Year 2. A manager says, "We used less renewable energy in Year 2."

**(a)** Find the total energy use in Year 2. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the renewable energy used in each year. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on the manager's statement. **[1]**

**10.** Two shops record ice-cream sales by flavour.

| | Vanilla | Chocolate | Strawberry | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shop P | 120 | 90 | 40 | 250 |
| Shop Q | 60 | 80 | 60 | 200 |

**(a)** A pupil says, "Chocolate is more popular in Shop P than in Shop Q." Use percentages to comment on this statement. **[3]**

**(b)** State one other conclusion supported by the table. **[1]**

**11.** A table shows the percentage of pupils in a school gaining each grade in a test in two years.

| Year | Number of pupils | Grade A | Grade B | Grade C or below |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 120 | 15% | 45% | 40% |
| 2025 | 150 | 20% | 46% | 34% |

**(a)** Find the number of pupils gaining grade A in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** The head teacher says that a new textbook, introduced in 2025, caused the improvement. Comment on this claim. **[2]**

**12.** A dual bar chart shows the numbers of boys and girls in four sports clubs, read from a scale as follows: football 24 boys and 10 girls; hockey 8 boys and 14 girls; tennis 12 boys and 12 girls; athletics 16 boys and 20 girls.

**(a)** Which club has the greatest difference between boys and girls? **[1]**

**(b)** What percentage of all the girls play hockey? **[2]**

**(c)** A pupil says, "More girls than boys do sport." Is this supported by the chart? Give a reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 90° is a quarter of 360°, so the total is 4 × 45 = 180 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 48/360 × 180 = 24 [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Tuesday 2.5 × 8 = 20, Wednesday 4.25 × 8 = 34 [1]; difference 14 visitors [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 25 and 40 − 25 = 15 [1]; 70 − 40 = 30 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 54 ÷ 0.3 = 180 members [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** 5000 × (3/5)² [1] = 5000 × 0.36 = 1800 visitors [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 72/360 × 1800 = 360 [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 12 + 5 + 9 + 4 = 30 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 12 + 5 = 17 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** n(B) = 14, so 5/14 [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** X: 60 − 35 = 25 [1]; Y: 55 − 42 = 13 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The medians are similar, 48 for X and 50 for Y, so Class Y did slightly better on average [1]. Class Y's marks are more consistent: IQR 13 against 25 (range 40 against 59) [1]. Class X had both the highest and the lowest marks, so its marks are more varied [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** About a quarter [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** 10th and 11th values are 161 and 161, so the median is 161 cm [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 206 − 142 = 64 cm [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Lower half: median of 142 ... 161 is (153 + 153)/2 = 153; upper half: median of 161 ... 206 is (170 + 173)/2 = 171.5 [1]. IQR = 171.5 − 153 = 18.5 cm [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** The value 206 cm is far above the rest, so it makes the range large; the IQR uses only the middle half and is not affected by it [1]. **[1]**

**(e)** No plant had a height from 190 cm to 199 cm [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** 400 × (5/4)² [1] = 400 × 1.5625 = 625 units [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Year 1: 90/360 × 400 = 100 units [1]; Year 2: 72/360 × 625 = 125 units [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The statement is wrong: renewable energy rose from 100 to 125 units; only its share of the total fell, from 25% to 20% [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** Shop P: 90/250 = 36% chocolate [1]; Shop Q: 80/200 = 40% [1]. More chocolate ice-creams were sold in P, but a higher proportion of Shop Q's sales were chocolate, so the statement is true for numbers but not for popularity as a share of sales [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** For example: vanilla is the most popular flavour in Shop P (48% of sales); or strawberry forms a larger share in Shop Q (30% against 16%) [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** 2024: 0.15 × 120 = 18 [1]; 2025: 0.20 × 150 = 30 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The data show the percentage gaining grade A rose from 15% to 20% [1], but they cannot show the textbook caused this; other things, such as different pupils, teachers or a different test, may explain the change [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** Football (24 − 10 = 14) [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Girls total 10 + 14 + 12 + 20 = 56 [1]; 14/56 × 100 = 25% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** No: the chart shows 60 boys and 56 girls in these clubs, and it does not show how many pupils do sport outside them [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using angles from two pie charts as if they had the same total.
- Treating the top of a sectional bar as the size of that section.
- Comparisons with no figures, or figures with no comparison.
- Accepting a claim about cause from data that show only a change.
- Forgetting to give a percentage when groups differ in size.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I set out a "comment on the claim" answer?**
State whether the claim is supported, then give the evidence from the data and the reason any part of it is not justified.

**Do I need both the median and the IQR in every comparison?**
For box-and-whisker questions, comparing one average and one spread is the usual expectation for full credit.
