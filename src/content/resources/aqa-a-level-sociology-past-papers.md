This page is a guide to AQA A Level Sociology (7192) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds copied from AQA's own grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A Level Sociology (7192) specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/sociology/a-level/sociology-7192/specification)
- [AQA A Level Sociology (7192) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/sociology/a-level/sociology-7192/assessment-resources)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

The assessment resources page lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination for each series. Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 7192 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2015 and first assessed in June 2017. It has three written papers, each 2 hours, 80 marks and one third of the A Level, 240 marks in all. Paper 1 (7192/1) is Education with theory and methods. Paper 2 (7192/2) is Topics in Sociology, where candidates answer on one topic from each of two sections. Paper 3 (7192/3) is Crime and deviance with theory and methods. All three series on this page were set on that specification.

## June 2025

We did not find a June 2025 report for Paper 1 on AQA's assessment resources page, so these notes cover Papers 2 and 3 only. Examiners on Paper 2 said evaluation remains the hardest skill, and that candidates who weighed strengths and limitations and reached a judgement were rewarded more than those who recited a learned criticism. They also noted that a 20-mark essay answered last was often short or unfinished. On Paper 3, the short questions went well but evaluation was again the weakest area, and the last question suffered when time ran short.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 2, 10-mark questions: using the prompts in the item as a starting point but then saying little more than that the idea exists; a second reason developed less than the first; answers on the wrong concept, such as roles instead of functions of the family, or a gender question taken for a sexuality one; and writing everything known about a term rather than answering the question asked.
- Paper 2, 20-mark essays: essays that slid into a list of perspectives, an essay on a single theory that spent most of its length on other theories, and an essay that used the item and little else. Candidates who planned briefly and used other views only to evaluate wrote the better responses.
- Paper 2, topics: social groups in secularisation taken as two reasons for secularisation; new age movements confused with new religious movements or sects; industrialisation answered with modernisation and dependency theory but few country examples; postmodernism treated as another word for pluralism; few references to studies of social mobility or to occupational classification.
- Paper 3, short questions: boundary maintenance and similar identification points were strong, but some answers repeated the same idea twice, or wandered from crime into media representation; the criminal justice hooks were harder, with punishment and rehabilitation read as one hook.
- Paper 3, longer answers: a Marxist theory essay that used Merton and Cohen as knowledge points and spent time on interactionism; a primary methods question where answers moved on from validity to access and representativeness instead of keeping to two problems; the last question, on theory and social policy, answered with large parts of the item repeated.

## June 2024

All three reports are summarised here. Paper 1 was judged comparable with earlier years and slightly more accessible, with some pleasing explicit evaluation in the Marxism essay and a hard final question on feminism and research design. Paper 2 repeated the general advice about 10-mark and 20-mark questions. Paper 3 had a consistent entry and evaluation was again the biggest area for development.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, short and 10-mark questions: statements about vocational education that could apply to any education; parental choice answered with a myth of parentocracy but no explanation; girls' experiences answered through boys' experiences; the last sentence of an item, which only restates the question, used as if it were a hook; overt participant observation discussed as two separate ideas instead of together and in relation to pupil subcultures.
- Paper 1, essays: the Marxist essay answered with other perspectives set side by side rather than a critique of Marxism, or with general class differences in achievement that never reached the role of the education system; the feminist research design question was the weakest, with feminism discussed in general terms and no link to specific method choices.
- Paper 2: candidates writing about why birth rates fall when the question asked about the effects; items copied and only briefly developed; functionalist and New Right views discussed without relating them to family diversity; reasons given for consumption choices or identity without analysing their effect.
- Paper 3: punishment barely mentioned when the question asked about it; repeated reasons that could not be credited twice; a statistics question where candidates struggled to keep to two reasons and to name sources of statistics; essays on realism or on science and sociology where evaluation was weakest, even though the item gave hooks for it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A Level Sociology 7192 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 7192 assessment resources page. Grade boundaries are on its grade boundaries page and archive.

**What is a "hook" in the reports?**
In the 10-mark analyse questions the item gives prompts that the answer is expected to develop. The reports say these are needed to reach the application marks, and that the final sentence of the item, which restates the question, is not one.

**Should a 10-mark answer have an introduction?**
The reports say introductions and conclusions are not needed for 10-mark questions and take up time. They recommend clearly separating two developed points rather than giving more than two thinly.

**How do examiners say to improve evaluation?**
They ask for a reasoned judgement that weighs strengths and limitations, rather than a learned criticism or a bare statement that two views differ, and for other views to be used to evaluate the theory the question names.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
