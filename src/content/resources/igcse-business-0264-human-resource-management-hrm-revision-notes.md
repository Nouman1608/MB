## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting suitable people to apply for a vacancy |
| Selection | Choosing the best applicant from those who applied |
| Job description | Lists the job title, duties and responsibilities |
| Person specification | Lists the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities wanted in the post holder |
| Internal recruitment | Filling the vacancy with someone already employed by the business |
| External recruitment | Filling the vacancy with someone from outside the business |
| Employment contract | A legal agreement setting out the terms of the job |
| Training | Teaching employees the skills and knowledge to do their jobs |
| Induction training | An introduction for new employees to the business |

## 2.1.1 Recruiting and selecting

### The recruitment process in order

1. Job description (what the job is).
2. Person specification (what the person is like).
3. Job advertisement (tells people about the vacancy; may use an employment agency or online recruitment).
4. Selection (the methods below).

### Selection methods at a glance

| Method | One strength | One weakness |
|---|---|---|
| CV or resumé | Quick summary of background | Self-written, may exaggerate |
| Application form | Same questions for all, easy to compare | Time-consuming to design and read |
| References | Outside view of reliability | Can be too generous |
| Testing or assessment centres | Shows real skills | Costly and slow |
| Interviews | Judge personality and communication | Open to bias and first impressions |

### Internal against external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Cost and speed | Cheaper and faster | Dearer and slower |
| Knowledge | Employer already knows the person; person knows the business | Employer knows less; needs induction |
| Choice | Small pool | Large pool |
| Ideas | No new ideas arrive | New skills and ideas |
| Motivation | Promotion prospects motivate staff | Existing staff may feel overlooked |
| Side effect | Leaves a gap to fill | Fills the gap fully |

### Method: recommend and justify who to employ

1. Name your choice clearly.
2. Match the candidate to the person specification, quoting evidence from the case.
3. Compare with the rejected candidate on the same criteria.
4. Weigh one risk of your choice and say how it could be reduced.
5. Finish with one sentence of judgement.

## 2.1.2 Contracts and legal controls

### Contents of an employment contract

- Duties and responsibilities
- Working hours
- Holiday entitlement
- Sick pay

### Benefits

| Employer | Employee |
|---|---|
| Clear expectations, fewer disputes | Knows pay, hours and holiday |
| Evidence if there is a dispute | Evidence if the employer breaks terms |

### Four legal controls (describe in general terms)

| Control | Effect on employer | Effect on employee |
|---|---|---|
| Unfair dismissal | Needs a fair reason and proper process | More job security |
| Discrimination | Must hire, pay and promote fairly | Equal opportunity |
| Health and safety | Pays for safety measures; fewer accidents | Safer workplace |
| Legal minimum wage | Raises wage costs | Guaranteed minimum pay |

Always give an effect for both sides. A legal control can be a cost for the employer and a benefit for the employee at the same time. It can also bring a benefit to the employer, such as lower absence from fewer accidents.

## 2.1.3 Training

### Why it matters

| To the business | To employees |
|---|---|
| Higher productivity and quality | New skills and confidence |
| Fewer errors and accidents | Chance of promotion and higher pay |
| Staff can cover different jobs | Greater satisfaction |
| Helps keep staff | Meets wish to develop |

### Three types compared

| Type | Where | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | At the start, in the business | Settles new starters quickly | Uses existing staff time; not deep job training |
| On-the-job | At the workplace while working | Cheap and relevant | Trainer less productive; bad habits can spread |
| Off-the-job | Away from the workplace | Expert teaching, recognised qualifications | Costly; staff away from work |

Training has a cost, and trained staff might leave. Say so in evaluation.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | The difference in one line |
|---|---|
| Recruitment and selection | Recruitment attracts applicants. Selection chooses between them. |
| Job description and person specification | The first describes the job. The second describes the ideal person. |
| Employment agency and online recruitment | An agency finds or shortlists candidates for a fee. Online recruitment is advertising directly, at low cost. |
| Application form and CV | A form asks every applicant the same questions. A CV is written freely by the applicant. |
| Interview and assessment centre | An interview is a conversation. An assessment centre tests skills through tasks and exercises. |
| On-the-job and off-the-job training | The first happens at work while doing the job. The second takes place away from the workplace. |
| Benefit and cost of a legal control | One control can help employees and cost employers at the same time, so say both. |

## Worked reminder: weighing two applicants

A garage needs a receptionist. The person specification asks for "friendly manner", "good record keeping" and "two years in a customer-facing job". Applicant A is an existing mechanic's assistant who keeps neat records but has not dealt with customers. Applicant B is from outside, has three years in a shop, and impressed at interview.

A short model answer: choose B. B meets all three criteria, while A meets only one. The garage would pay for an advertisement and would need to give B induction, but the post is customer-facing and B has the evidence for it. The risk is that A may feel passed over, so the manager should discuss other opportunities with A.

## How a longer answer is built

For an explain or justify question, build a chain: point, because, so. For example: "A written contract helps the employer (point) because the duties and working hours are agreed in advance (because), so there are fewer arguments about what the employee must do (so)." A developed chain is stronger than a list of bare points.

## Quick calculations to know

Hourly rate = weekly pay ÷ hours worked. Compare it with the legal minimum wage in the question.

Worked reminder: a worker is paid 300 dollars for 40 hours. The rate is 300 ÷ 40 = 7.50 dollars an hour. If the minimum is 8.00 dollars, the shortfall is 0.50 dollars an hour, or 40 × 0.50 = 20 dollars a week.

Cost of training = cost per person × number of people, plus any lost output while they are away.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the term job description.
2. State two things a person specification might list.
3. Identify two ways of advertising a vacancy.
4. State one advantage and one disadvantage of internal recruitment.
5. Identify the four contents of an employment contract named in the syllabus.
6. Outline one benefit of an employment contract to an employee.
7. Identify the four employment issues that legal controls cover in the syllabus.
8. A worker earns 300 dollars for 40 hours. The legal minimum is 8.00 dollars an hour. Calculate the weekly shortfall.
9. Eight staff attend an off-the-job course costing 150 dollars each. Calculate the course cost.
10. Outline one difference between induction training and on-the-job training.
11. Explain one way training helps an employee.
12. A worker is entitled to 25 days of holiday and has taken 5. State how many remain.

### Answers

1. A document that lists the job title, the main duties and the responsibilities of a post.
2. Any two from: qualifications, skills, experience, personal qualities.
3. Any two from: employment agency, online recruitment, the business website, a newspaper or notice board, internal notice.
4. Advantage: cheaper and quicker, or the person already knows the business. Disadvantage: a smaller pool of applicants, or no new ideas.
5. Duties and responsibilities, working hours, holiday entitlement, sick pay.
6. For example, they know their pay and hours, or they have written evidence if the employer changes the terms.
7. Unfair dismissal, discrimination, health and safety, legal minimum wage.
8. 300 ÷ 40 = 7.50 dollars an hour, below 8.00, so the shortfall is 0.50 × 40 = 20 dollars a week.
9. 8 × 150 = 1,200 dollars.
10. Induction introduces new employees to the business and its rules. On-the-job training teaches job skills while the person works.
11. For example, better skills improve promotion chances, or give a higher wage, or make the job more satisfying.
12. 25 − 5 = 20 days.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a job description and person specification as if they were the same document.
- Listing selection methods without saying why a business would choose one.
- Saying external recruitment is "better" without any drawback, or the reverse for internal recruitment.
- Writing a recommendation with no evidence from the case, or with a list of facts and no decision.
- Giving the effect of a legal control on the employer only, or on the employee only.
- Writing about a named law you are not sure of. Describe the control in general terms.
- Forgetting the costs of training: the fee, and the output lost while staff are away.
- Treating induction as skills training for the job rather than an introduction to the business.
- Doing a wage calculation without comparing the result with the minimum, so the conclusion is missing.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the main contents of an employment contract?**
An employment contract sets out duties and responsibilities, working hours, holiday entitlement and sick pay.

**What are the four legal controls over employment?**
The syllabus lists unfair dismissal, discrimination, health and safety, and the legal minimum wage. Describe them in general terms and give an effect on both the employer and the employee.

**How do you calculate the cost of training?**
Multiply the cost per person by the number of people trained, then add any output lost while they are away from work.

**What is induction training?**
Induction introduces new employees to the business, its rules, safety and colleagues. It is not skills training for the job itself.
