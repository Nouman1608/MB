These notes condense subtopic 6.5 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417, online booking systems. For full explanations and an annotated model answer, read the [booking systems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-booking-systems/); then test yourself with the [booking systems practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-booking-systems-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

Characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of online booking systems, including:

- the travel industry
- concerts
- cinemas
- sporting events

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Online booking system | Website or app linked to a central database of seats, rooms or tickets |
| Real-time | The database is updated at once, so every user sees current availability |
| Seat map | Interactive plan showing which seats are free and which are taken |
| Hold or lock | Temporary reservation of a selected seat while the customer completes the booking |
| Booking reference | Unique code that identifies a booking |
| E-ticket | Electronic ticket with a barcode or QR code, shown on a phone or printed |
| Double booking | Selling the same seat or room to two customers |
| Booking fee | Extra charge added by the seller for using the service |

## Characteristics checklist

- Real-time database access
- Search by date, time, place, event or price
- Interactive seat or room maps
- Secure (HTTPS) payment
- Instant confirmation by email or text
- E-tickets with barcode or QR code
- Customer accounts storing details and past bookings

## The booking process in eight steps

1. Search
2. System shows availability from the database
3. Select seat or room; it is **held**
4. Enter personal details
5. Pay; bank authorises
6. Database **updated**: seat marked as booked
7. Confirmation and e-ticket sent
8. E-ticket scanned at venue; marked as used

**Double booking is prevented by** steps 3 and 6: the hold removes the seat from other users' choices, and the update after payment makes the booking permanent. An unpaid hold is released after a time limit.

### Seat status in the database

Each seat moves through three states: **available** (shown on the seat map), **held** (selected by one customer, hidden from others for a time limit) and **booked** (paid for). After the e-ticket is scanned, the booking is marked **used**.

## The four uses at a glance

| Use | One detail to mention |
|---|---|
| Travel industry | Compare flights, trains and hotels; add luggage; prices change with demand; boarding pass on phone |
| Concerts | Huge demand at release; online queues; limits on tickets per customer |
| Cinemas | Pick film, time and exact seat; accessible seating |
| Sporting events | Stadium sections; season or membership tickets; tickets linked to a name |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Who | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Book any time, anywhere; choose seats; compare prices and offers; instant confirmation; e-tickets cannot be lost in the post | Needs internet and a device; fraud and stolen data; booking fees; no personal advice; mistakes hard to fix; site crashes at peak times |
| Organisation | Fewer staff; wider market; no double booking; booking data helps planning | Website and security costs; must protect personal data; lost sales if the system fails; fewer box office jobs |

## Online compared with in person or by phone

- **Online:** any time, see the seat map, instant e-ticket; but no human help and a risk of fake websites.
- **In person:** staff advice, pay in cash, ticket in hand; but limited opening hours, travel and queues.
- **By phone:** a person to talk to; but call queues, cannot see the seat map, and the call costs time.

## Exam traps

- "The computer stops double booking" with no mechanism. Mention the hold and the immediate database update.
- "It is cheaper" without saying for whom or why. Customers may find online offers; organisations save on staff.
- Answering only for customers when the question says "the company".
- Ignoring the named context. Add one feature specific to travel, concerts, cinemas or sport.
- Listing "hacking" without consequence. Say what is stolen and what happens.

## Self-test

1. What is meant by real-time in a booking system?
2. Give three characteristics of an online booking system.
3. Describe how a seat is prevented from being sold twice.
4. What happens if a customer selects a seat but does not pay?
5. What is an e-ticket?
6. How is an e-ticket checked at the venue?
7. Give two advantages of online booking for a customer.
8. Give two disadvantages of online booking for a customer.
9. Give two advantages of online booking for a theatre.
10. Give one disadvantage of online booking for a theatre.
11. Give one feature of online booking specific to the travel industry.
12. Why might a concert booking website crash?

### Answers

1. The database is updated immediately, so every user sees current availability.
2. Any three: real-time database; search and filters; seat maps; secure payment; instant confirmation; e-tickets; customer accounts.
3. The seat is held as soon as it is selected, so others cannot choose it; after payment the database marks it as booked.
4. The hold is released after a time limit and the seat becomes available again.
5. An electronic ticket with a barcode or QR code, shown on a phone or printed.
6. The code is scanned and checked against the database; it is then marked as used so it cannot be used again.
7. Any two: book any time; no travel or queues; choose seats; compare prices; instant confirmation.
8. Any two: needs internet; fraud risk; booking fees; no personal advice; mistakes hard to correct.
9. Any two: fewer staff; sales from a wider area; no double booking; booking data for planning.
10. Any one: cost of the website and its security; must protect customer data; lost sales if the system fails.
11. Any one: compare flights and prices; choose seats or add luggage; online check-in; boarding pass on phone.
12. Very large numbers of people try to book at the moment tickets go on sale.
