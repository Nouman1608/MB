> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, sub-topic 6.3.

Sub-topic 6.3 has five outcomes: causes, impacts, strategies, a discussion of strategies, and
opportunities. Learn the named items and one mechanism for each.

## Causes sorted by mechanism

| Mechanism | Named causes | One-line explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Water arrives **faster** | Land relief (steep slopes) | Rain runs off quickly instead of soaking in |
| | Saturated soil | No room left for infiltration |
| | Compacted soil | Pores closed, so infiltration is slow |
| | Deforestation | No interception or root uptake |
| | Agricultural practices | Ploughing downslope, overgrazing, machinery, drained wetlands |
| | Urbanisation | Impermeable surfaces; drains send water straight to rivers |
| Channel holds **less** | Soil erosion | Sediment deposited in the river bed |
| Water **collects** | Land relief (low, flat land) | Floodplains and deltas fill first |
| **More water** | Climate change: extreme weather events | More intense rain and storms |
| **Sea rises** | Climate change: sea-level rise | Low coasts more exposed |
| | Storm surges | Low pressure and wind push sea onshore |
| | Tsunamis | Displaced sea water floods the coast |

## Nine impacts

Damage to buildings and infrastructure · loss of crops · livestock and habitats · evacuation of people and
livestock · landslides · contamination of drinking-water supplies · water-related disease · financial losses ·
human health.

**Chains to learn**
- Floodwater + sewage → contaminated wells → water-related disease.
- Saturated slopes → landslides → blocked roads → slower rescue.
- Flooded fields → lost crops → lost income and food shortages.

## Eight strategies

| Before | During | After |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Evacuation | Rebuilding of damaged areas |
| Land use zoning | Shelters | International aid |
| Structure of buildings | | |
| Disaster preparation (plans, drills, emergency supplies, emergency rescue teams) | | |

Compare with 6.2: **land use zoning is listed for flooding but not for tropical cyclones.**

## Benefits and limitations

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | River floods forecast hours or days ahead | Flash floods and tsunamis give little time |
| Land use zoning | Cheap; keeps people off risky land | Land scarce; existing homes remain |
| Structure of buildings | Raised floors, stilts, waterproofing cut damage | Cost; hard for existing homes |
| Disaster preparation | Fast, organised response | Funding and practice needed |
| Evacuation | Saves lives | Needs warning and transport; people stay behind |
| Shelters | Safe refuge on high ground | Crowding; disease |
| Rebuilding | Can rebuild to resist floods | Slow; may repeat risky locations |
| International aid | Supplies and money | Delays; uneven distribution |

## Two opportunities (6.3.5)

1. **Nutrient-rich silt deposited on farmland**: floodwater slows on the floodplain and drops fine
   sediment, renewing fertility.
2. **Recharge of surface water and ground water stores**: lakes, wetlands and reservoirs refill; water
   infiltrates to refill aquifers.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flood | Water covering land that is normally dry |
| Land relief | The shape and height of the land |
| Infiltration | Water soaking into the soil |
| Surface run-off | Water flowing over the ground |
| Saturated soil | Soil whose pores are full of water |
| Compacted soil | Soil squashed so that pore spaces are reduced |
| Impermeable surface | A surface water cannot pass through, such as concrete |
| Floodplain | Flat land beside a river that floods naturally |
| Storm surge | Rise in sea level caused by low pressure and onshore winds |
| Tsunami | Large waves caused by displacement of sea water |
| Aquifer | Rock that stores ground water |
| Land use zoning | Planning rules that control what land can be used for |

## Exam traps

- "Rain causes floods": explain why the rain could not infiltrate or why the channel overflowed.
- Saturated and compacted soil treated as the same.
- Forgetting coastal causes (storm surges, tsunamis, sea-level rise).
- Strategies listed without their stage (before, during, after).
- One-sided discussions: every strategy has a limitation.
- Percentage points versus percentage change: a rise from 15% to 35% is 20 percentage points.

## Self-test

1. Explain how land relief can increase flood risk.
2. What is the difference between saturated and compacted soil?
3. Give two agricultural practices that increase flood risk.
4. Explain how urbanisation increases flooding.
5. Name two ways climate change increases flood risk.
6. Explain how soil erosion upstream affects flooding downstream.
7. Name the strategies used during a flood.
8. Give one limitation of land use zoning.
9. Describe one way to improve the structure of a house in a flood-risk area.
10. Explain why floods can benefit farmers.
11. What is meant by recharge of ground water stores?
12. *Invented data:* the built-up area of a 50 km² catchment rises from 10 km² to 18 km². Calculate the
    built-up share before and after.

### Answers

1. Steep slopes send rain quickly into rivers; low, flat land such as floodplains is where water collects.
2. Saturated soil is full of water; compacted soil has been squashed so its pores are reduced.
3. Any two: ploughing up and down slopes; overgrazing; heavy machinery compacting soil; draining wetlands.
4. Impermeable surfaces stop infiltration, and drains carry rain quickly to rivers, so they rise higher and
   faster.
5. Rising sea level; more extreme weather events (more intense rainfall and storms).
6. Eroded soil is deposited in the river bed, so the channel holds less water and overflows sooner.
7. Evacuation and shelters.
8. Land is scarce, especially in growing cities, and existing buildings remain at risk.
9. Any one: raised floors; stilts; waterproof materials; electrics placed high on walls.
10. Floodwater deposits nutrient-rich silt on fields, renewing soil fertility.
11. Floodwater infiltrates and refills aquifers that supply wells and springs.
12. Before: 10 / 50 x 100 = 20%. After: 18 / 50 x 100 = 36%.
