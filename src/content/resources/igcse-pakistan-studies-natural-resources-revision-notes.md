## One question, four resources

Water, forests, minerals and fish all answer the **same underlying
question**: how is this resource used, and can that use continue
without running the resource down? Structure every answer around:
what is it used for → what damages it → what would make its use
sustainable.

## (a) Water

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Named dams | Mangla, Tarbela, Warsak (know their locations) |
| Barrages | Name two examples |
| Key treaty | **Indus Waters Treaty** — reasons and consequences |
| Problems | Pollution, supply issues that can lead to **conflict** |

The Indus Waters Treaty links directly to the "conflict" strand of
the sustainability theme and Pakistan's relations with its neighbours
— a favourite angle for testing a resource's wider significance
beyond technical detail.

## (b) Forests

- Types, main locations, and **physical vs human factors** controlling
their distribution/reduction.
- Named example needed: a **plantation area in the Indus Plain**, and
afforestation on mountain slopes/plateaux.
- **Effects of deforestation**: soil erosion, silting, flooding.
- Deforestation directly **accelerates silting of the dams and
barrages named under (a)** — a cross-topic link worth stating
explicitly in answers.

## (c) Mineral resources

| Mineral | Detail to know |
|---|---|
| Limestone, gypsum, rock salt | Main extraction locations and uses |
| Metallic/non-metallic minerals | Quantities extracted/held in reserve, extent of exploitation |
| Imported minerals | Main ones, sources, quantities |

**"Evaluate" questions** on mineral extraction want the environmental
cost weighed explicitly against the development benefit — not simply
listed alongside it.

## (d) Fish

- Marine vs inland fishing methods, including **fish farms**; name
species caught/reared in each.
- **Named fishing ports**: Balochistan and Sindh coasts.
- Improvements in fishing/processing methods.
- Problems facing the industry and sustainable-development
possibilities.

Inland fish farming, like mineral extraction, **depends on the water
supply and quality** covered under (a) — another cross-topic link.

## The four cross-cutting themes (weave through every part)

1. Problems/opportunities of being a developing country.
2. Effects on and conservation of the environment.
3. Promotion of sustainable development.
4. Importance of feasibility studies.

Every resource represents **both** an opportunity for development
**and** a risk if managed poorly — this is the argument Section 2's
structured questions are actually testing, not rote recall of four
separate lists.

## Worked example: connecting two resources in one answer

A question asks: "Explain how deforestation can affect Pakistan's
water resources." A strong answer traces the cross-topic chain
explicitly rather than answering forests and water as separate
topics:

```
Step 1: Deforestation removes tree cover that would otherwise
        intercept rainfall and stabilise soil on slopes.
Step 2: Without that cover, rainfall causes increased soil erosion,
        washing sediment into rivers.
Step 3: That sediment is carried downstream and deposited behind
        dams and barrages (e.g. Tarbela, Mangla) -- a process
        called silting.
Step 4: Silting reduces a dam's storage capacity over time, directly
        undermining the water-supply role covered under resource (a).
```

This four-step chain answer scores far better than two separate,
unconnected paragraphs on deforestation and on dams, because it
demonstrates the exact "resources are interlinked" understanding
Section 2's structured questions are designed to test.

## Exam traps

- Naming a resource issue without a **specific, located example**
(a named dam, plantation area, mineral site, or fishing port) — vague
answers lose marks even with sound underlying explanation.
- Answering "identify" questions with an explanation instead of a
named fact/location, or "evaluate" questions with a list instead of a
weighed judgement.
- Treating water, forests, minerals and fish as four unconnected
topics rather than tracing the links between them (deforestation →
silting; minerals/fish → water dependency).
- Omitting the Indus Waters Treaty's conflict dimension when water
supply issues are the focus of a question.

## Command words: identify vs evaluate

Section 2 mixes factual recall with judgement-based questions, and
mismatching your answer style to the command word is a common source
of lost marks:

- **"Identify"** wants a named fact or location — a dam, a plantation
area, a mineral site, a fishing port. A well-explained but unnamed
answer scores lower than a bare correct name.
- **"Evaluate"** wants a weighed judgement — costs set explicitly
against benefits, not simply listed one after another. A list of
"advantages" followed by a separate list of "disadvantages" with no
comparison between them under-performs a genuinely weighed conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Name the three dams and state what else you must be able to name
for water supply infrastructure.
2. How does deforestation (b) connect to the dams named under water
(a)?
3. What must an "evaluate" answer on mineral extraction weigh against
each other?
4. Which two coasts host Pakistan's named fishing ports?
5. What single question should structure your revision of all four
resources?

**Answers:** 1. Mangla, Tarbela and Warsak; you must also be able to
name two examples of barrages. 2. Deforestation causes soil erosion
that accelerates silting of dams and barrages, reducing their storage
capacity over time. 3. The environmental problems caused by
extraction against the development benefits and sustainability of
that extraction. 4. Balochistan and Sindh. 5. How is this resource
used, and can that use continue without running the resource down —
applied to what it's used for, what damages it, and what would make
its use sustainable.
