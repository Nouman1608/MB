**This practice set covers the biological approach in Cambridge International AS & A Level Psychology 9990 (syllabus for 2024 to 2026).** Section A has short-answer questions on the assumptions and the three core studies. Section B has longer questions that ask you to evaluate and compare. Mark-scheme answers follow.

## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Outline the assumption of the biological approach that concerns similarities and differences between people. **[2]**

**2.** Dement and Kleitman studied ultradian rhythms. Explain what is meant by an *ultradian rhythm*, using an example from the study. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Identify **one** control used in the study by Dement and Kleitman. **[1]**

**3. (b)** Explain why this control was needed. **[2]**

**4.** Dement and Kleitman woke participants five or fifteen minutes into a REM period. Explain why this part of the study used a correlational approach as well as an experimental one. **[3]**

**5.** Hassett et al. coded monkeys' toy interactions with a behavioural checklist. Suggest **two** behavioural categories that could be on the checklist. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why Hassett et al. compared their findings with data on children from another study. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Describe the sample in the study by Hölzel et al. **[2]**

**7. (b)** Explain how the design of Hölzel et al.'s study allows a conclusion about cause and effect. **[3]**

**8.** Outline what the findings of Hölzel et al. suggest about localisation of function. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**9.** Evaluate the study by Dement and Kleitman in terms of **two** strengths and **two** weaknesses. At least **one** point must be about the validity of the dream reports. **[10]**

**10.** Compare the studies by Hassett et al. and Hölzel et al. in terms of **two** similarities or differences in their methodology. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how the studies by Hassett et al. and Hölzel et al. support different sides of the nature versus nurture debate. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss the ethics of the study by Hassett et al. using the guidelines for research with animals. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Up to 2 marks. Similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors (1), and how these biological factors interact with other factors such as the environment (1).

**2.** Up to 2 marks. A biological cycle that repeats more than once in 24 hours (1). Dement and Kleitman found REM periods recurred at regular intervals through each night, showing the sleep cycle (1).

**3. (a)** 1 mark for any one: participants avoided caffeine and alcohol on the day of testing; they were woken by the same doorbell; they reported dreams into a recorder without talking to the experimenter first; they were not told which sleep stage they were in.

**3. (b)** Up to 2 marks, linked to the control in 3(a). For example: caffeine and alcohol affect sleep (1), so avoiding them stopped them acting as an uncontrolled variable on the sleep stages and dream recall measured (1). Or: recording before any contact (1) stopped the experimenter's questions influencing the dream report (1).

**4.** Up to 3 marks. Neither variable was manipulated in this part: the researchers measured how long REM had lasted and the participant's estimate (1). Looking for a relationship between two measured co-variables is correlational (1). The REM versus NREM comparison, where the waking stage was chosen by the researchers, is the experimental part (1).

**5.** 1 mark each for any two operationally defined categories, such as: touching a toy with hands or mouth; carrying a toy; holding a toy; sitting on or beside a toy; approaching to within reach of a toy. Vague categories such as "playing" alone gain no credit.

**6.** Up to 2 marks. To see whether the sex difference in monkeys matches the pattern found in children (1). If the patterns are similar, this supports the idea that children's toy preferences have a biological component and are not only the result of socialisation (1).

**7. (a)** Up to 2 marks. Sixteen participants in the mindfulness (MBSR) group (1) and seventeen in a control group who did not take the programme during the study (1).

**7. (b)** Up to 3 marks. Brain structure was measured before and after the eight weeks with MRI (1), so the change within each person can be measured, rather than comparing different people once (1). The control group, scanned at the same times, shows whether changes would have happened anyway, so a greater increase in the MBSR group can be attributed to the programme (1).

**8.** Up to 2 marks. Increases in grey matter were found in specific regions, such as the left hippocampus (1), which are linked to particular functions such as learning, memory and emotional regulation, supporting the idea that functions are located in particular areas (1).

**9.** Level-based: 9–10 marks for four well-developed points applied to the study, including the dream-report point, with a clear judgement. Indicative content:

- *Strength, objectivity*: the EEG recorded REM and NREM objectively, so the classification of sleep stage did not rely on the participant or the researcher's opinion.
- *Strength, standardisation*: the same waking method and recording procedure for every awakening made the study replicable and the results comparable across participants.
- *Weakness, validity of dream reports*: whether a person had been dreaming relied on their self-report; they may have forgotten a dream in the seconds after waking or added detail, so recall is not a direct measure of dreaming.
- *Weakness, ecological validity*: sleeping with electrodes in a laboratory and being woken repeatedly is unlike normal sleep, so the timing and content of dreams may differ at home.
- *Weakness, sample*: nine adults, most of them men, is a small sample, so it is hard to generalise to all sleepers.
- *Conclusion*: a reliable, objective method that gave clear differences between REM and NREM, but conclusions about dream content and length depend on subjective reports.

**10.** Up to 4 marks: 2 marks for each comparison fully explained. For example: both collected quantitative data (1), Hassett et al. as frequencies and durations of toy interactions and Hölzel et al. as grey-matter concentration from MRI (1). A difference: Hassett et al. observed animals in their normal living group (1), whereas Hölzel et al. tested humans with brain scans before and after an intervention, a longitudinal design (1).

**11.** Up to 4 marks. Hassett et al. found sex-typed toy preferences in monkeys (1), which suggests an inherited or hormonal influence, supporting nature (1). Hölzel et al. found that eight weeks of mindfulness practice changed grey-matter concentration (1), which shows experience (nurture) altering biology, so biological differences are not fixed at birth (1).

**12.** Up to 4 marks. Minimising harm: the monkeys were only offered toys and were not hurt or deprived (1). Housing: they remained in their normal social troop in a large outdoor enclosure, so their living conditions were not changed (1). Procedures: there was no use of reward, deprivation or aversive stimuli to make them play (1). Species and numbers: rhesus monkeys were suitable because they are thought to be less affected by human socialisation, and observing an existing troop used no extra animals (1).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need the exact figures from Dement and Kleitman?**
Figures such as 152 of 191 REM awakenings make a description precise and are worth learning. A clear description of the direction of the results still earns credit.

**Can I use these studies in A Level answers?**
The core studies are not the direct focus of Papers 3 and 4, but the methods and issues they illustrate are assumed knowledge.
