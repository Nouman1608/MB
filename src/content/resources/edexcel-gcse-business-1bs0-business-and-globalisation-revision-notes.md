These notes condense content item 2.1.3 Business and globalisation, part of Topic 2.1 Growing the business in Theme 2 of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [business and globalisation study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-and-globalisation/); to apply them, try the [business and globalisation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-and-globalisation-practice/). Businesses and figures are invented.

## The specification in one view

- **Impact of globalisation:** imports (competition from overseas, buying from overseas); exports (selling to overseas markets); changing business locations; multinationals.
- **Barriers to international trade:** tariffs; trade blocs.
- **Competing internationally:** the internet and e-commerce; changing the marketing mix.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | Growing links between countries' economies, so goods, services, money and businesses cross borders more freely |
| Import | A good or service bought from another country |
| Export | A good or service sold to customers in another country |
| Landed cost | The full cost of an import once freight, insurance and tariffs are added (standard term) |
| Multinational | A business with operations in more than one country |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Trade bloc | A group of countries that reduce or remove trade barriers between members, usually with a common tariff on outsiders |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling over the internet |
| Relocation | Moving production, offices or services to a different place, including abroad |

## Imports: remember both directions

| Effect | Threat or opportunity | Likely business response |
|---|---|---|
| Competition from overseas | Threat: cheaper or better rivals in the home market | Cut costs, differentiate, target a niche that values UK-made |
| Buying from overseas | Opportunity: cheaper or unique inputs | Compare landed cost with home suppliers; allow for longer delivery |

**Quick landed-cost check.** Corrin Glassware can buy a vase abroad for £3.50, with £0.40 freight and an 8% tariff on the £3.50. Tariff = £0.28, so landed cost = £3.50 + £0.40 + £0.28 = **£4.18**. Compare that, not £3.50, with the UK price.

## Exports

**Why:** growth beyond a crowded home market; higher output can lower unit costs; risk spread across several markets.

**Problems:** different tastes, language and laws; transport and paperwork costs; slower payment; tariffs in the customer's country.

**Tariff passed on:** a £60 product facing a 15% tariff costs the overseas buyer £60 × 1.15 = **£69**. The exporter either accepts that sales may fall, or cuts its price and accepts lower profit per unit.

**Export share:** export revenue ÷ total revenue × 100. A business earning £54,000 of £360,000 revenue from exports has a 15% export share.

## Changing business locations

| Push towards moving | Pull towards staying |
|---|---|
| Lower wages, rent or energy costs | Redundancy and closure costs |
| Avoiding a market's tariffs by producing inside it | Harder to control quality at a distance |
| Being close to growing customers | Reputation risk from UK job losses |
| Skills or materials available abroad | Longer, less reliable supply chains |

Moves can be reversed: rising overseas wages or unreliable shipping can bring work back to the UK.

## Multinationals

| | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| To the business | Larger market; lower costs; risk spread | Hard to co-ordinate; one scandal harms the global brand |
| To the host country | Jobs, training, tax, orders for local suppliers | Pressure on local firms; profits may leave; the firm may move on |

## Barriers to trade

- A **tariff** makes imports dearer in the country that charges it. It protects that country's producers and raises government revenue.
- The importer pays the tariff. The exporter feels it through lower sales or a price cut.
- A **trade bloc** helps members (easier, cheaper trade inside) but acts as a barrier to outsiders (a common external tariff).

## Competing internationally

- **Internet and e-commerce:** worldwide reach at low cost; open all hours; but delivery cost, returns and heavy online competition.
- **Changing the marketing mix:** adapt product (sizes, labels, ingredients), price (local incomes, tariffs, freight), promotion (language, culture, local platforms) and place (local distributors, marketplaces, warehouses).

## Exam traps

- Calling a tariff a tax on exports. It is a tax on imports.
- Ignoring freight and tariffs when comparing suppliers.
- Assuming relocation always saves money; one-off and reputation costs count.
- Treating a trade bloc as a barrier to its own members.
- Writing "Explain" answers with a business context. Explain questions in this specification carry no context, so give a general statement with two linked expansions.
- In Analyse answers, describing globalisation in general without saying what happens to this firm's costs, price or sales.

## Self-test

1. Define the term tariff.
2. State the two effects of imports named in the specification.
3. What is the difference between an import and an export?
4. A UK shop buys rugs abroad at £80 each, pays £12 freight per rug and a 10% tariff on the £80. Calculate the landed cost per rug.
5. Give two reasons why a business might start exporting.
6. Explain why a trade bloc can be a barrier for a business outside it.
7. Give one benefit and one drawback of a multinational for the country that hosts it.
8. A business sells £450,000 in total, of which £81,000 is exports. Calculate the export share.
9. A £25 product faces a 20% tariff in the customer's country. What does the importer pay per unit if the price is unchanged?
10. Give one way a business might change its promotion to sell overseas.
11. State one reason a business might move production back to the UK.
12. Outline one drawback of using e-commerce to sell overseas.

### Answers

1. A tax charged on goods imported into a country.
2. Competition from overseas; buying from overseas.
3. An import is bought from another country; an export is sold to customers in another country.
4. Tariff = 10% × £80 = £8. Landed cost = £80 + £12 + £8 = **£100**.
5. Any two: the home market is saturated; to grow sales; to lower unit costs through higher output; to spread risk across markets.
6. Members charge a common tariff on goods from outside, so the outsider's products cost more than those of firms inside the bloc and may lose sales.
7. Benefit: jobs, training, tax revenue or orders for local suppliers. Drawback: local firms face strong competition, profits may be sent abroad, or the firm may leave.
8. £81,000 ÷ £450,000 × 100 = **18%**.
9. £25 + (20% × £25) = **£30**.
10. Translate adverts, use local social media platforms or change images to suit local culture.
11. Overseas wages have risen, shipping is slow or unreliable, quality is hard to control, or customers value UK-made goods.
12. Delivery to overseas customers is expensive and slow, so customers may choose a local seller, which reduces the sales the website brings.
