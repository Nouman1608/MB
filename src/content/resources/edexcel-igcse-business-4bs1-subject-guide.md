Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Business (4BS1) explores what
businesses do, the people who work in them, how they are financed, how
they market their products and how they organise production. It suits
students who are curious about the organisations around them and who
want to learn to analyse real situations and make reasoned
recommendations. The course is linear and assessed through two written
papers, each lasting 1 hour 30 minutes, marked out of 80 and worth 50%.
One paper is set in the context of a small business and the other in
the context of a large one. The current specification is Issue 2
(February 2026).

## How the course is organised

The specification is arranged in five content areas.

1. **Business activity and influences on business** covers business
   objectives, types of organisation, the classification of businesses,
   location decisions, business and the international economy,
   government objectives and policies, external factors, and what makes
   a business successful.
2. **People in business** covers internal and external communication,
   recruitment and selection, training, motivation and rewards, and
   organisation structure and employees.
3. **Business finance** covers sources of finance, cash flow
   forecasting, costs and break-even analysis, financial documents, and
   accounts analysis.
4. **Marketing** covers market research, the market and the marketing
   mix.
5. **Business operations** covers economies and diseconomies of scale,
   production, factors of production, and quality.

The two papers are distinguished in the assessment information by the
kind of business their scenarios describe, so it pays to study every
area with both small and large organisations in mind.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4BS1/01 | Paper 1: Investigating small businesses | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| 4BS1/02 | Paper 2: Investigating large businesses | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Each paper is externally assessed and contains four compulsory
questions worth 20 marks each. Calculators may be used in both papers.
The scenarios on each paper are defined by business size:

- **Paper 1** is based on a small business, with up to 49 employees.
- **Paper 2** is based on a large business, with more than 250
  employees.

The qualification is linear, so both papers are taken in the same
series. The specification offers June and November availability.

## How the parts fit together

Because the papers are distinguished by business size, the same idea
can look quite different depending on which paper it appears in. What
changes is how a topic applies. Sources of finance for a small business with a
handful of employees look very different from those open to a large
company. Recruitment in a small firm may be informal, while a large
organisation has structured processes and a formal organisation chart.
Economies of scale matter most when a business is already large, while
location decisions and cash flow can be pressing for a small firm.

Learning to ask "how would this work for a business of this size?" is
therefore one of the most valuable habits in the course. It turns
general knowledge into the applied answers both papers reward.

The content areas also depend on each other. Marketing decisions need
finance; production depends on people; external factors such as
government policy or the international economy affect every function.
Business finance is particularly central, since cash flow forecasts,
break-even analysis and accounts analysis give the numerical evidence
that supports judgements elsewhere. Four 20-mark questions on each
paper mean that every question is likely to combine several of these
skills, from calculation to explanation to a reasoned conclusion.

## Planning your study

A sensible order starts with context and builds towards the
quantitative and operational areas:

- **Business activity and influences** first, so that you understand
  the types of businesses and the environment they operate in.
- **People in business** and **marketing** next, as they connect
  closely to customer and employee decisions in everyday business life.
- **Business finance**, giving extra time to cash flow forecasting,
  break-even and accounts analysis, which need regular calculation
  practice.
- **Business operations** last, linking production and quality back to
  costs and to the size of the business.

Over a two-year course, a reasonable aim is to finish the first three
areas in the first year and the remaining two in the second, leaving
time to revisit everything with both small and large business examples
in mind. Begin full past papers once all five areas are covered, and
use the exam-preparation page for detailed advice on answering each
paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Edexcel IGCSE Business 4BS1?**
Two. Paper 1, Investigating small businesses, and Paper 2, Investigating large businesses, each last 1 hour 30 minutes, are marked out of 80 and are worth 50%.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
The papers differ by the business in the scenario. Paper 1 is based on a small business with up to 49 employees, and Paper 2 on a large business with more than 250 employees.

**How many questions are on each IGCSE Business paper?**
Each paper has four compulsory questions worth 20 marks each.

**Can I use a calculator in IGCSE Business?**
Yes. Calculators may be used in both papers.

**When can I take Edexcel IGCSE Business?**
The specification offers June and November availability. Both papers are taken in the same series.
