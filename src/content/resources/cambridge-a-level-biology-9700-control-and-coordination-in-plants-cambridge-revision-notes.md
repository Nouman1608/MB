## Core recall

### Two ways plants coordinate

- **Electrical signals**: action potentials passed from cell to cell (Venus fly trap).
- **Plant hormones**: auxin and gibberellin, acting on target cells to change growth or gene expression.
- No neurones, no muscles: plant movements come from changes in cell water content and cell walls.

### Venus fly trap in seven steps

1. Insect bends a sensitive hair on the inner surface of a lobe.
2. Ion channels in cells at the base of the hair open.
3. A receptor potential forms; at threshold, an action potential is generated.
4. A second stimulation within a short time is needed to trigger closure.
5. Action potentials spread across the lobes.
6. H⁺ pumped into walls of outer cells loosens them; water enters by osmosis; cells expand.
7. Lobes flip from convex to concave and snap shut in a fraction of a second; continued stimulation seals the trap and digestion begins.

**Why two touches?** Prevents closure by raindrops or debris; each closure is costly and the trap reopens slowly.

### Auxin: acid growth in six links

1. Auxin made in the growing tip moves to the zone of elongation.
2. Auxin binds to a receptor in the target cell.
3. Proton pumps (H⁺-ATPases) are stimulated; H⁺ pumped from cytoplasm into cell wall (ATP used).
4. Wall pH falls; expansins are activated.
5. Expansins loosen links between cellulose microfibrils.
6. Water enters by osmosis; turgor stretches the loosened wall; the cell elongates.

### Gibberellin in barley germination

| Stage | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Imbibition | Whole grain | Grain absorbs water |
| Hormone release | Embryo | Gibberellin produced |
| Gene activation | Aleurone layer | DELLA proteins broken down; amylase gene transcribed |
| Enzyme action | Endosperm | Amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose |
| Use of sugars | Embryo | Respiration and growth |

### The three responses compared

| | Venus fly trap | Auxin | Gibberellin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal | Action potential | Hormone | Hormone |
| Speed | Fraction of a second | Minutes to hours | Hours to days |
| Key ion or molecule | H⁺ (and other ions), water | H⁺ in cell wall | DELLA protein breakdown |
| Outcome | Trap closes | Cell elongates | Starch mobilised for growth |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sensitive (trigger) hair | Hair on the fly trap lobe that responds to bending |
| Receptor potential | Change in membrane potential in a receptor cell caused by a stimulus |
| Auxin | Plant hormone made in growing tips that stimulates cell elongation |
| Zone of elongation | Region behind the tip where cells increase in length |
| Expansins | Cell wall proteins, active at low pH, that loosen links between microfibrils |
| Gibberellin | Plant hormone that, in barley, triggers amylase production |
| Aleurone layer | Layer of living cells around the endosperm that makes amylase |
| DELLA protein | Repressor protein that inhibits a factor needed for transcription |
| Imbibition | Uptake of water by a dry seed |

## Exam traps

- Protons are pumped **out of the cytoplasm into the wall**, not into the cell.
- Low pH **loosens** the wall; it does not digest it.
- Gibberellin does not hydrolyse starch; **amylase** does.
- Amylase is made in the **aleurone layer**, not the embryo.
- The fly trap needs **two** stimulations; one touch is not enough.
- Do not mention muscles or contraction in plant answers.
- When describing data, calculate percentage changes from the **initial** value.

## Self-test

1. Where are the sensitive hairs of a Venus fly trap found?
2. What happens in the cells at the base of a hair when it is bent?
3. Why does one touch of a hair not close the trap?
4. Describe the change in shape of the lobes as the trap closes.
5. Where is auxin made, and where does it act to cause elongation?
6. Which enzyme-like proteins are activated by low pH in the cell wall?
7. Why does a cell with a loosened wall elongate?
8. A coleoptile segment grows from 12.0 mm to 13.8 mm. Calculate the percentage increase.
9. What triggers gibberellin production in a barley grain?
10. Which layer of the grain responds to gibberellin?
11. What do DELLA proteins normally do?
12. Name the product of starch hydrolysis by amylase.

### Answers

1. On the inner surface of each lobe of the modified leaf.
2. Ion channels open, ions enter and a receptor potential forms.
3. A second stimulation within a short time is needed; this prevents closure by raindrops or debris.
4. The lobes flip from convex to concave.
5. Made in the growing tip; acts on cells in the zone of elongation.
6. Expansins.
7. Water enters by osmosis and turgor pressure stretches the loosened wall.
8. (13.8 − 12.0) ÷ 12.0 × 100 = **15 %**.
9. Absorption of water (imbibition) activates the embryo, which produces gibberellin.
10. The aleurone layer.
11. Inhibit a factor needed for transcription of genes such as the amylase gene.
12. Maltose.
