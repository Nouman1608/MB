Read the extract (original, written for this practice set).

> (1) When the power failed, it failed all at once. One moment the city was humming; the next, every
> window in the street went black together, as if someone had blown out a row of candles. The
> refrigerator shuddered into silence. Somewhere below, a car alarm began to scream and then thought
> better of it.
>
> (2) Farah felt her way along the corridor with one hand on the wall. The darkness was thick and warm
> and smelled of dust. Her little brother's breathing came from the sofa, quick and shallow, and she
> could hear him pretending not to be afraid.
>
> (3) "Come on," she said. "Roof."
>
> (4) The stairwell was a throat of shadows. They climbed it slowly, counting the steps aloud, their
> voices bouncing back at them from the concrete. At the top, the metal door groaned on its hinges, and
> then they were outside, and the night opened over them like a great dark flower.
>
> (5) Without the streetlights the sky had come back. Stars that Farah had never seen above the city
> were scattered across it, thousands of them, sharp and cold and astonishingly close. On every rooftop
> around them, other figures were emerging, silhouettes with torches and mobile phones, calling softly
> to one another across the gaps between the buildings. Someone was laughing. Someone had brought a
> guitar.
>
> (6) Her brother's hand, which had been gripping hers so tightly that it hurt, slowly loosened. "Is it
> always there?" he whispered. "All that?"
>
> (7) Farah looked up. "Always," she said. "We just switched it off."

## Section A

**1.** Identify the simile in paragraph 1 and explain what it suggests about the way the lights went
out. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the effect of saying the car alarm "began to scream and then thought better of it".
**[2]**

**3.** Identify the word in paragraph 1 which suggests that the refrigerator stopped with a violent
jolt, and explain its effect. **[2]**

**4.** How does the writer make the darkness in paragraph 2 seem almost physical? **[2]**

**5.** What does the description of the brother's breathing as "quick and shallow" suggest? **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** "The stairwell was a throat of shadows." Explain the metaphor and its effect. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the image "the night opened over them like a great dark flower" changes the mood of
the extract. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how the writer's language in the first two sentences of paragraph 5 creates a sense of
wonder. **[3]**

**9.** Explain the effect of the two short sentences that end paragraph 5. **[2]**

**10.** What does the phrase "slowly loosened" (paragraph 6) convey? **[1]**

**11.** Explain how Farah's final words, "We just switched it off", carry more than one meaning.
**[2]**

**12.** A candidate answers Question 8 with: "The writer uses lots of descriptive language to create a
vivid image." Explain why this earns no marks. **[1]**

---

## Answers

**1.** "as if someone had blown out a row of candles" [1]. It suggests the lights went out
simultaneously and effortlessly, in one breath, and makes the modern city seem as fragile as candle
flames [1].

**2.** The alarm is personified, as if it starts to cry out in panic and then changes its mind [1];
this makes the moment slightly comic and eerie, and emphasises how quickly even noise gives way to the
silence of the power cut [1].

**3.** "Shuddered" [1]. It suggests a shaking, almost living machine giving a last shiver as it dies,
which makes the silence afterwards more noticeable and slightly unsettling [1].

**4.** Two points [2]: the darkness is "thick", as though it has texture and must be pushed through
[1]; it is "warm" and "smelled of dust", so it is felt and smelled like a substance, not just the
absence of light [1]. Credit also: Farah has to "feel her way", as if the darkness were a barrier.

**5.** He is frightened / anxious, trying to control his fear [1].

**6.** The stairwell is compared to a throat, a narrow, dark passage inside a body [1]; it makes the
climb feel enclosed and threatening, as if the children were being swallowed, which increases the
tension before they reach the roof [1].

**7.** Two points [2]: a flower opening suggests beauty, growth and release after the "throat" of the
stairwell [1]; the mood shifts from fear and confinement to space and wonder, even though the night
is still "dark" [1].

**8.** Three points from [3]:
- "the sky had come back" personifies the sky as something that returns, suggesting a lost natural
  world is restored [1];
- "thousands of them" and "scattered" convey abundance and the overwhelming number of stars [1];
- the list "sharp and cold and astonishingly close", with repeated "and", builds amazement, and
  "astonishingly" shows Farah's own wonder [1];
- "never seen above the city" emphasises how new and surprising this is to her [1].

**9.** Two points [2]: the short, parallel sentences ("Someone was... Someone had...") give quick
glimpses of life on the rooftops, like overheard fragments [1]; they create a relaxed, friendly mood
of community, contrasting with the fear of the earlier paragraphs [1].

**10.** The brother's fear gradually fades as he relaxes in the calm of the rooftop [1].

**11.** Literally, the city's lights "switched off" the stars by hiding them [1]; more deeply, it
suggests that people have shut themselves off from the natural world and its beauty through their
modern way of life, and that it was there all along [1].

**12.** It names no specific words and gives no particular effect; it could be said of any text, so it
shows no understanding of how this writer creates wonder [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 1**: quoting the whole first paragraph instead of the simile.
- **Question 3**: choosing "silence" rather than the verb that shows the violent stop.
- **Question 6**: explaining that a throat is part of the body without saying what the comparison does
  to the scene.
- **Question 8**: one long point about the stars, used to claim all three marks.
- **Question 11**: giving only the literal meaning.
