## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark questions give one mark for each accurate detail, or one for each point and one for
explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a
general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the
question. The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** The Meeqat [1].

**2.** The 9th [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: cutting hair or nails; using perfume; hunting; sexual relations; arguing; disobedience or wrongdoing.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: Hajj happens on set days in Dhul Hijjah, Umrah at any time; Hajj includes Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah, Umrah does not; Hajj is a Pillar of Islam, Umrah is highly recommended (scholars differ on whether it is obligatory); Hajj includes the stoning and the sacrifice, Umrah does not.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: in Tawaf pilgrims walk around the Ka'aba seven times [1]; anticlockwise, with the Ka'aba on their left [1]; they try to touch or point to the Black Stone at the start of each circuit [1]; they pray two rak'ahs near the Station of Ibrahim [1]; in Sa'i they walk seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwa [1]; this remembers Hajar searching for water for Ismail [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **The Ka'aba** [1]: Ibrahim (AS) and Ismail (AS) built it as a house for one God, and pilgrims circle it [1].
- **Sa'i and Zamzam** [1]: they remember Ibrahim's wife Hajar, who trusted Allah in the valley of Makkah [1].
- **The sacrifice** [1]: it remembers Ibrahim's readiness to sacrifice his son in obedience to Allah [1].
- **The stoning** [1]: it remembers how Ibrahim rejected temptation to disobey Allah [1].
- **The call** [1]: Allah told Ibrahim to proclaim Hajj to the people (22:27), so Hajj began with his call [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Same clothing** [1]: men wear plain white sheets, so wealth, status and nationality are hidden [1].
- **Same rites together** [1]: millions perform Tawaf and stand at Arafat at the same time, which shows one community [1].
- **Pilgrims from every country** [1]: people of different races and languages meet and worship side by side [1].
- **Shared hardship** [1]: crowds, heat and simple living treat everyone alike [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "depart from the place from where [all] the people depart" (Qur'an 2:199): all pilgrims move together, whatever their rank [2].
- "The believers are but brothers" (Qur'an 49:10) applied to the pilgrims' shared worship [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the same white clothing removes signs of wealth and race and makes every pilgrim equal before Allah.
- **For the statement:** the rules of Ihram apply to everyone, rich or poor, and show that Hajj is about humility, not rank.
- **Against the statement:** Ihram has other purposes: it marks the start of Hajj, trains self-control, and focuses the mind on Allah.
- **Against the statement:** its rules about conduct, such as no arguing, teach patience and discipline as well as equality.
- **Balanced view:** equality is one of the central lessons, but self-discipline, sincerity and taqwa matter equally.

*Use of the passage*

- "no sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing" shows the self-control that Ihram requires.
- "whatever good you do – Allah knows it" shows that the pilgrim's sincerity matters, not status.
- "the best provision is fear of Allah" shows that what matters is taqwa, not wealth or supplies.
- "well-known months" shows that all Muslims perform Hajj at the same time of year.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is the heart of Hajj** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said "Hajj is Arafat", and Hajj is not complete without it [1].
- **A time of forgiveness** [1]: pilgrims spend the afternoon asking Allah to forgive their sins [1].
- **A reminder of the Day of Judgement** [1]: the huge crowd standing in simple clothes pictures all people before Allah [1].
- **Link to the Prophet's farewell sermon** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) gave his farewell sermon at Arafat, so pilgrims stand in the place of his final teaching [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "ask forgiveness of Allah. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful" (Qur'an 2:199) [2].
- "the best provision is fear of Allah" (Qur'an 2:197): Arafat is a day of taqwa [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the rites teach lessons: equality in Ihram, trust in Allah through Sa'i, obedience through the sacrifice, and rejection of temptation through the stoning.
- **For the statement:** Muslims believe that Hajj without taqwa is only travel; the passage states that "the best provision is fear of Allah".
- **Against the statement:** the rituals are commanded by Allah and shown by the Prophet (pbuh); they must be performed correctly for Hajj to be valid.
- **Against the statement:** the rituals themselves create the experience from which the lessons are learned, so the two cannot be separated.
- **Balanced view:** the rituals are the means and the lessons are the aim; both are needed.

*Use of the passage*

- "no sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing" shows that conduct is part of the rite.
- "the best provision is fear of Allah" supports the statement strongly.
- "whatever good you do – Allah knows it" shows that Allah sees the sincerity behind the actions.
- "well-known months" and the rites performed within them show that the practice is also fixed and required.
