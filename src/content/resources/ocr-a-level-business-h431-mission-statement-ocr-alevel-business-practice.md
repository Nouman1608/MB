## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by the *scope* of a mission statement. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a mission statement and a business objective. **[3]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following is a mission statement rather than an objective?

A: "To cut customer complaints by 20% this year."

B: "To open five new branches by 2028."

C: "To make reliable bicycles that help people in our city get around without a car."

D: "To achieve a 10% operating profit margin." **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** way a mission statement can help a business that operates from many separate sites. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a mission statement may be of little benefit to a sole trader. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Brennock Ales is a brewery with 30 staff. It brews beer only from barley grown within 40 miles, sells mainly to independent pubs in its county and runs brewery tours. The owners describe their aim as "to grow steadily without becoming a factory". They are choosing between two draft mission statements.

Draft 1: "To be the best brewery in Britain."

Draft 2: "To brew honest beer from our county's barley for the pubs and people who care where it comes from."

**(a)** Using the drafts, explain **two** features that make a mission statement useful. **[4]**

**(b)** Recommend and justify a suitable mission statement for Brennock Ales. **[6]**

**7.** Tallis Homecare provides care workers who visit elderly people in their own homes. In three years it has grown from 60 to 220 care workers across four counties. Most care workers work alone and rarely meet a manager. Complaints have risen from 12 to 41 a year, mostly about rushed visits. The directors are considering writing a mission statement for the first time. Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages to Tallis Homecare of having a mission statement. **[9]**

**8.** Corvane Outdoor sells hiking clothing. Its mission statement reads: "Gear that lasts a lifetime, made responsibly." To compete with discount retailers it has launched a cheaper range made from lower-grade fabric, which is expected to wear out within two seasons. Analyse the possible effects on Corvane Outdoor's stakeholders of acting in a way that does not match its mission statement. **[6]**

**9.** Ellerby Toys is a family company that makes wooden toys in one factory in Yorkshire. Its mission has been: "Toys made by hand, to be handed down." A large toy group has bought Ellerby and proposes a new mission: "Affordable fun for every child, in every shop." The group plans to move most production overseas, add plastic ranges and sell through supermarkets. Evaluate the impact of changing the mission statement on Ellerby Toys and its stakeholders. **[12]**

**10.** Calderford Leisure Trust is a social enterprise that runs three swimming pools for a local council. Profits are reinvested in cheaper sessions for low-income families. Explain **two** ways in which its mission statement is likely to differ from that of a public limited company running leisure centres for profit. **[4]**

**11.** Nimbus Ledger is a start-up that sells accounting software to small charities. Its founders propose the mission "To build the simplest accounting app for UK charities."

**(a)** Explain how this wording might restrict the business's future decisions. **[3]**

**(b)** Recommend a change to the wording and justify it. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Scope is the range of products, customers or places the business says it serves [1]; for example "in our county" limits the market, so moves outside it would conflict with the mission [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A mission statement is a broad, qualitative statement of purpose that is open-ended [1]. An objective is a specific, measurable target with a time limit [1]. The objective serves the mission: it turns the purpose into something that can be checked [1]. **[3]**

**3.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Managers cannot supervise every decision at every site [1], so a shared statement of purpose tells staff what the business values, for example quality over speed [1]; decisions at different sites are then more consistent, protecting the brand [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The owner makes the decisions personally and works alongside any staff [1], so the direction is already clear and the time spent writing a statement brings little return [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Specific to the business [1]: Draft 2 mentions county barley and independent pubs, while Draft 1 could be used by any brewery [1]. Rules something out / guides decisions [1]: Draft 2 would make importing cheap barley or selling to a national supermarket chain look inconsistent, while Draft 1 gives no guidance [1]. Also accept: realistic, memorable, matches behaviour, with use of the drafts. **[4]**

**(b)** Recommendation: Draft 2, or a close version of it [1]. Justification: it states the purpose and scope, matching what Brennock already does [1]; it reflects the owners' aim of steady growth, since it does not promise national leadership [1]; the local-barley value can be used in marketing to pubs whose customers value provenance [1]. Weakness of Draft 1: unrealistic and generic, so it would not guide staff [1]. Condition or limitation: "honest beer" is not measurable, so objectives such as sourcing all barley locally must support it; if the owners later want to sell beyond the county, the scope would need widening [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Knowledge: a mission statement sets out the purpose and standards of a business [1].
Application: Tallis care workers work alone and rarely meet a manager [1]; complaints have more than trebled, from 12 to 41, mostly about rushed visits [1].
Analysis (advantages): a statement such as "unhurried care that treats every client with dignity" would tell lone workers what matters when no manager is present [1], and could be used in recruitment to attract carers who share the values, which matters when the workforce has grown by 160 in three years [1].
Analysis (disadvantages): words alone will not fix rushed visits if the rota gives carers too little time between clients [1]; if complaints continue after a public statement about dignity, clients' families may become cynical and the reputation may suffer more [1].
Evaluation: the advantages are likely to outweigh the disadvantages because Tallis has grown fast across four counties and lone working means a shared purpose has real work to do [1]; but only if the directors change rotas and training to match it, otherwise the statement becomes a disadvantage [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers tie the statement to the cause of the complaints rather than assuming that writing it will solve them.

**8.** Customers who bought because of "lasts a lifetime" may feel misled when the cheaper range wears out [1], so trust in the brand falls and they may buy elsewhere, cutting sales of the premium range too [1]. Employees may feel uncomfortable selling a product that contradicts the stated values [1], which could lower motivation or lead to staff leaving [1]. Shareholders may gain short-term revenue from price-sensitive buyers [1], but risk long-term damage to the brand's value, which is what allows Corvane to charge premium prices [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Size of change: this is a change of purpose and scope, from handmade toys to mass-market toys, not just new wording [1].
Impact on the business: the new mission fits the group's strategy of supermarket distribution and could raise sales volume sharply [1]; but it removes Ellerby's distinctive feature, so it will compete on price with larger rivals [1]; costs include new branding, new product development and closing or downsizing the factory [1].
Stakeholders:
Factory employees face redundancy as production moves overseas [1], and the Yorkshire community loses jobs and local spending [1].
Existing customers and specialist toy shops lose the handmade range they valued, and may see the brand as having lost its identity [1].
The group's shareholders gain if higher volume outweighs lower margins [1]; supermarket customers gain cheaper products [1].
Evaluation: the change is likely to raise revenue but at the cost of the heritage that made the brand worth buying [1]. The impact depends on whether a handmade range is kept as a premium line alongside the new ranges [1], and on how the change is communicated to staff and loyal customers; a phased change would reduce the damage [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Weighing the stakeholders, by how much each is affected and how much power each has, is what lifts this from a list to an evaluation.

**10.** The trust's statement is likely to put its social purpose first, such as making swimming affordable for local families [1], because profits are reinvested in the community rather than paid to shareholders [1]. A plc's statement is more likely to refer to customers and returns for shareholders, or to growth across many sites [1], because its owners expect dividends and a rising share price [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** It ties the business to one customer group, UK charities [1], and to one product, an accounting app [1]; if a bigger opportunity arises, such as selling to small businesses or overseas, the move would contradict the mission and might be rejected or confuse staff [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** For example: "To make money management simple for small organisations that do good" [1]. This keeps the core value of simplicity and the focus on charities' needs [1], while allowing new products and customer groups such as social enterprises without rewriting the mission; the trade-off is that broader wording guides decisions less [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a target with a number in it as a recommended mission statement.
- Recommending a statement without using details from the case.
- Assuming a mission statement changes behaviour on its own.
- Listing stakeholders without saying how much each is affected.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement or a condition.
