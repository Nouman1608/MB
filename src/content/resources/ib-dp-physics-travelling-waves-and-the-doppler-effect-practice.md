## Questions

**1.** A wave travels along a rope. Its frequency is 3.2 Hz and its wavelength is 0.75 m. Calculate the period and the speed of the wave. **[3]**

**2.** A loudspeaker emits a note of frequency 2.5 kHz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m s⁻¹ and in water 1480 m s⁻¹. The note travels from air into water. Calculate the wavelength in air and in water, and state what happens to the frequency. **[4]**

**3.** A longitudinal wave travels in the positive x direction through a gas. At one instant the displacement of the particles, taken as positive in the direction of travel, is 0, +2.0 mm, 0, −2.0 mm and 0 at x = 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 m. The wavelength is 0.4 m.

**(a)** Identify the position of the centre of a compression and the centre of a rarefaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why there is no overall movement of the gas along the direction of travel although energy is transferred. **[2]**

**4.** A radio station transmits at 101.3 MHz. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the radio wave. **[2]**
**(b)** Give three ways in which this wave differs from a sound wave. **[3]**

**5.** A transverse wave travels in the positive x direction along a rope at 6.0 m s⁻¹. The wavelength is 1.5 m and the amplitude is 2.0 cm. At t = 0 the displacement is y = 2.0 sin(2πx/1.5) cm.

**(a)** Calculate the period of the wave. **[2]**
**(b)** State the displacement of the rope at x = 0.375 m at t = 0. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the displacement at x = 0.375 m after one quarter of a period, and state whether the rope at that point is moving up or down. **[2]**

**6.** A police car with its siren on drives past you at constant speed. Use wavefronts to explain why you hear a higher pitch as it approaches and a lower pitch after it passes. **[4]**

**7.** The sodium line of rest wavelength 589.0 nm is observed at 593.1 nm in the light from a galaxy. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Calculate the speed of the galaxy along the line of sight and state whether it is moving towards or away from Earth. **[4]**

**8.** In the spectrum of a star, a line of rest wavelength 434.0 nm is observed to move repeatedly between 433.80 nm and 434.20 nm.

**(a)** Suggest an explanation for the repeated shift. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum speed of the star along the line of sight. **[3]**

**9.** Doppler ultrasound is used to examine blood flow and radar is used to check vehicle speeds.

**(a)** Describe how Doppler ultrasound gives information about the flow of blood. **[2]**
**(b)** State what a radar speed gun compares to find the speed of a vehicle. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) A lorry sounds a horn of frequency 900 Hz as it drives at 18 m s⁻¹ along a straight road past a stationary observer. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency heard as the lorry approaches. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency heard as it moves away. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound reaching the observer ahead of the lorry. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) A stationary source emits sound of frequency 660 Hz. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** A cyclist rides towards the source at 12 m s⁻¹. Calculate the frequency heard. **[2]**
**(b)** A second cyclist riding towards the source hears 700 Hz. Calculate the speed of this cyclist. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** T = 1/f = 1/3.2 [1] = 0.313 s [1]. v = fλ = 3.2 × 0.75 = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A unit and a sensible number of significant figures are expected on both values; showing v = fλ with the numbers substituted lets a slip in arithmetic be seen for what it is.

**2.** In air: λ = v/f = 340/2500 [1] = 0.136 m [1]. The frequency stays the same, **2.5 kHz**, because it is set by the source [1]. In water: λ = 1480/2500 = **0.592 m** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kHz to Hz before substituting. Saying that frequency is unchanged, with the reason, is the point that is easiest to leave out.

**3. (a)** Compression centre at **x = 0.2 m**, because particles on either side are displaced towards it [1]. Rarefaction centre at **x = 0 (or 0.4 m)** [1]
**(b)** Each particle vibrates about its own rest position [1]. The disturbance passes along as energy, so the particles return to where they began and there is no overall displacement of the gas [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A compression sits where the displacement is zero, not where it is largest. State the neighbours' displacement directions to justify the position.

**4. (a)** λ = c/f = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 101.3 × 10⁶ [1] = **2.96 m** [1]
**(b)** Any three of these: the radio wave needs no medium while sound does [1]; the radio wave is transverse while sound is longitudinal [1]; the radio wave travels at c in a vacuum while the speed of sound depends on the medium [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each difference has to be stated for both waves. Writing only "radio is faster" does not make a contrast that can be credited.

**5. (a)** T = λ/v = 1.5/6.0 [1] = **0.25 s** [1]
**(b)** y = 2.0 sin(2π × 0.375/1.5) = 2.0 sin(π/2) = **+2.0 cm** [1]
**(c)** A quarter period is 0.0625 s, in which the pattern moves 6.0 × 0.0625 = 0.375 m to the right, so the displacement at x = 0.375 m is the old value at x = 0, which is **0** [1]. The particle has gone from +2.0 cm to zero, so it is **moving down** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how the pattern shifts by comparing with the earlier profile. A direction with no supporting reasoning is a weak answer.

**6.** Each wavefront is emitted from a different point along the car's path, so the wavefronts are not concentric [1]. Ahead of the car they are closer together, so the wavelength is shorter [1]. The speed of sound is unchanged, so f = v/λ is higher as it approaches [1]. After it passes you, the wavefronts reaching you are further apart, so the wavelength is longer and the frequency lower [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer links spacing to wavelength and then to frequency through the unchanged wave speed. Saying the sound speed changes is a common error that loses credit.

**7.** Δλ = 593.1 − 589.0 = 4.1 nm [1]. v = cΔλ/λ [1] = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 4.1/589.0 = **2.09 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** [1]. The observed wavelength is longer (redshift), so the galaxy is **moving away** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the rest wavelength in the denominator. The direction is a separate point and needs the redshift reason, not only the word "away".

**8. (a)** The star is moving in orbit (for example around another star) [1], so it alternately moves towards and away from us, shifting the line to shorter and then longer wavelength [1]
**(b)** Maximum Δλ = 0.20 nm [1]. v = cΔλ/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.20/434.0 [1] = **1.4 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The greatest shift on one side of the rest wavelength gives the greatest speed. Quoting the full range of 0.40 nm would double the answer.

**9. (a)** High-frequency sound is reflected from moving blood [1]. The frequency of the echo differs from that emitted, and the size and sign of the shift give the speed and direction of the flow [1]
**(b)** It compares the frequency of the microwaves it sends out with the frequency of the reflection [1]; a larger shift means a greater speed [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention what is emitted, what is reflected and what is compared. Descriptions that only say "the Doppler effect is used" gain little.

**10. (a)** f′ = f v/(v − us) [1] = 900 × 340/(340 − 18) = 900 × 340/322 = **950 Hz** [1]
**(b)** f′ = 900 × 340/(340 + 18) [1] = 900 × 340/358 = **855 Hz** [1]
**(c)** λ′ = (v − us)/f = 322/900 [1] = **0.358 m** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Choose the sign by checking the outcome: approaching must give a frequency above 900 Hz. A frequency below the source value for approach is a signal to recheck the sign.

**11. (a)** f′ = f(v + uo)/v [1] = 660 × (340 + 12)/340 = **683 Hz** [1]
**(b)** 700 = 660 × (340 + uo)/340 [1]. 340 + uo = 700 × 340/660 = 360.6 [1]. uo = **20.6 m s⁻¹** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before substituting and show the intermediate value. Using the source formula for a moving observer gives a different and incorrect answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving kHz or MHz unconverted in v = fλ.
- Saying the medium is carried forward by the wave rather than vibrating about its rest position.
- Putting a compression at the largest displacement instead of at zero displacement with neighbours displaced towards it.
- Writing that frequency changes at a boundary.
- Explaining the Doppler effect by a change in the speed of sound rather than a change in wavelength or arrival rate.
- Using the observed wavelength instead of the rest wavelength in Δλ/λ.
- Stating "redshift" without saying the source is moving away.
- Choosing the wrong sign in v ± us or v ± uo.
- Leaving out units or giving a speed to too many significant figures.
