These questions cover sub-topic 4(c), **Cycles within ecosystems** (specification points 4.10 and 4.11B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Point 4.10 can be examined on either paper. Questions labelled **(Paper 2 only)** test the bold **B** point 4.11B, the nitrogen cycle. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

## Questions

**1.** Name the process in the carbon cycle described by each statement.

**(a)** Plants take carbon dioxide from the air and make glucose. **[1]**
**(b)** Bacteria and fungi break down a dead leaf and release carbon dioxide. **[1]**
**(c)** Coal is burnt in a power station. **[1]**
**(d)** All living cells release energy from glucose. **[1]**

**2.** The carbon in a molecule of carbon dioxide in the air can end up in a hawk.

Describe the route that the carbon takes from the air to the hawk and back to the air. Use the food chain grass → mouse → hawk. **[5]**

**3.** In a year, the plants in a field take in 1500 units of carbon by photosynthesis and release 600 units by respiration.

**(a)** Calculate the net carbon taken in by the plants. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the carbon taken in that is released by respiration. **[2]**
**(c)** State what happens to the carbon that is not released by respiration. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the percentage of carbon dioxide in the air stays fairly constant over a long time in a natural ecosystem, even though organisms respire all the time. **[3]**

**5.** A student buries two samples of leaves in soil. Sample A is in ordinary soil. Sample B is in soil that has been heated to kill all the bacteria and fungi. After six weeks sample A has almost disappeared and sample B has hardly changed.

**(a)** Explain the difference between the samples. **[3]**
**(b)** State what happens to the carbon in the leaves of sample A. **[1]**

**6.** (Paper 2 only) Plants need nitrogen to make proteins.

**(a)** Name the form of nitrogen that plants absorb through their roots. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why plants cannot use nitrogen gas from the air. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the compounds made from this nitrogen in plants that are used to make proteins. **[1]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) The table shows four groups of microorganisms in the nitrogen cycle.

| Group | Change they bring about |
|---|---|
| W | Nitrogen gas → ammonium compounds |
| X | Dead material and urea → ammonium compounds |
| Y | Ammonium compounds → nitrates |
| Z | Nitrates → nitrogen gas |

**(a)** Name groups W, X, Y and Z. **[4]**
**(b)** State **one** place where group W can be found in a legume. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why group Z is more active in waterlogged soil. **[2]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) A farmer grows clover in a field for one year and then grows wheat in the same field the next year, without adding nitrogen fertiliser. The wheat grows well.

Explain how growing clover in the first year helped the wheat to grow. **[4]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Describe how nitrogen in a protein in a dead animal becomes nitrogen in a protein in a living plant. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Photosynthesis [1]
**(b)** Decomposition [1]
**(c)** Combustion [1]
**(d)** Respiration [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match the word to the process exactly. "Burning" is accepted for combustion, but "breathing" is not accepted for respiration.

**2.** The grass takes in carbon dioxide and uses it for photosynthesis to make glucose [1]; the carbon is built into the compounds of the grass [1]; the mouse eats the grass and the carbon passes into the mouse; the hawk eats the mouse and the carbon passes into the hawk [1]; carbon dioxide returns to the air by respiration of the plant, mouse and hawk [1]; when they die, decomposers break down the bodies and release carbon dioxide by their respiration [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show a complete loop. A route that stops at the hawk without returning to the air misses the last two marks.

**3.** **(a)** 1500 − 600 = **900 units** [1]
**(b)** 600 ÷ 1500 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
**(c)** It is used for growth / it is stored in the plant's tissues (accept: it passes to animals that eat the plants) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide by the carbon taken in (1500) and not by the net uptake. Dividing by 900 gives 66.7%, which is wrong.

**4.** Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the air [1]; respiration, decomposition and combustion add it [1]; over a long period the amounts removed and added are about equal, so the percentage stays about the same [1]
*Examiner insight:* A full answer names both the removing and the adding processes, and says that they balance.

**5.** **(a)** Sample A contains bacteria and fungi (decomposers) [1]; they feed on the leaves and break them down [1]; in B the decomposers were killed, so the leaves are not broken down [1]
**(b)** It is released as carbon dioxide by the respiration of decomposers (accept: it is built into the bodies of decomposers) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The control matters. The only difference between the samples is the presence of decomposers.

**6.** **(a)** Nitrate (ions) [1]
**(b)** The plant cannot convert nitrogen gas into nitrates / the nitrogen gas is unreactive and plants have no way to use it [1]
**(c)** Amino acids [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Nitrogen" alone in (a) is not specific enough. Name the ion.

**7.** **(a)** W: nitrogen-fixing bacteria [1]; X: decomposers [1]; Y: nitrifying bacteria [1]; Z: denitrifying bacteria [1]
**(b)** In root nodules [1]
**(c)** Waterlogged soil has little oxygen [1]; denitrifying bacteria work in these low-oxygen conditions (nitrifying bacteria need oxygen) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Learn the direction of each change. A common error is to swap Y and Z.

**8.** Clover is a legume, with nitrogen-fixing bacteria in its root nodules [1]; the bacteria change nitrogen gas into ammonium compounds [1]; nitrifying bacteria change these into nitrates [1]; the wheat takes up the nitrates through its roots and uses them to make protein, so it grows well [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each step in the chain is a separate mark. Writing only "clover adds nitrogen" scores one.

**9.** Decomposers break down the protein in the dead animal [1]; they release ammonium compounds [1]; nitrifying bacteria convert ammonium compounds into nitrates [1]; the plant absorbs nitrates through its roots [1]; the plant uses the nitrate to make amino acids and then proteins [1]
*Examiner insight:* In extended answers the order matters. Keep to the sequence decomposers, then nitrifying bacteria, then roots, then amino acids.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "nitrogen" when the question needs "nitrate".
- Confusing nitrifying and denitrifying bacteria.
- Missing that plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
- Dividing by the wrong value in a percentage.
- Leaving out decomposers in the return of carbon to the air.
- Describing the carbon route without naming the processes.
