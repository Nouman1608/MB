Condensed recall for subtopic 6.1 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070: "Identify physical and chemical changes, and describe the differences between them". For full explanations and model answers, use the [physical and chemical changes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-physical-and-chemical-changes/); to practise, try the [physical and chemical changes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-physical-and-chemical-changes-practice/).

## The test

**Has a new substance with a different formula been formed?** Yes: chemical. No: physical.

## Differences to describe

- Chemical change: new substance(s) formed; atoms rearranged; bonds within particles broken and made; shown by an equation with different formulae; usually reversed only by another reaction.
- Physical change: no new substance; same particles, only arrangement, spacing or movement changes; shown by state symbols only; usually reversed by cooling, heating or evaporating.
- **Both** conserve mass. **Both** can involve energy changes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical change | Change in state, shape or mixing with no new substance |
| Chemical change | Change in which at least one new substance is formed |
| Change of state | Melting, freezing, boiling, evaporating, condensing, subliming: always physical |
| Dissolving | Solute particles spread through a solvent; physical |
| Sublimation | Solid turning directly to gas, e.g. iodine; physical |
| Decomposition | One substance breaking into two or more new substances; chemical |
| Precipitate | Insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed; evidence of a chemical change |
| Effervescence | Fizzing as a gas is produced; evidence of a reaction when a new gas is identified |
| Mixture | Two or more substances not chemically combined; can be separated physically |
| Compound | Two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions |

## Classification bank

| Change | Type | Key reason |
|---|---|---|
| Iodine subliming on warming | Physical | Vapour condenses back to iodine |
| Molten wax setting | Physical | Same substance, new state |
| Sodium chloride melting | Physical | Still Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; ionic bonds overcome but no new substance |
| Diluting copper(II) sulfate solution | Physical | Fewer ions per dm³; no new substance |
| Filtering, distilling, crystallising | Physical | Separation only |
| Mixing iron filings and sulfur | Physical | Iron still magnetic; separable |
| Heating iron with sulfur | Chemical | Iron(II) sulfide forms; not magnetic |
| Electrolysis of any electrolyte | Chemical | Decomposes a compound into new substances |
| Heating hydrated copper(II) sulfate | Chemical | Blue CuSO₄·5H₂O becomes white CuSO₄; reversible but chemical |
| Burning methane or wax vapour | Chemical | CO₂ and H₂O formed |
| Magnesium in hydrochloric acid | Chemical | Hydrogen and magnesium chloride formed |
| Heating calcium carbonate strongly | Chemical | CaO and CO₂ formed |
| Mixing silver nitrate and sodium chloride solutions | Chemical | White precipitate of silver chloride |
| Iron rusting | Chemical | Hydrated iron(III) oxide formed |
| Neutralising an acid with an alkali | Chemical | Salt and water formed |

## Particle level

- Melting and boiling a molecular substance: **intermolecular forces** overcome; covalent bonds inside molecules untouched.
- Melting an ionic or giant covalent substance: the lattice breaks down, but the particles are unchanged.
- Chemical change: bonds inside particles break, new bonds form, new formulae result.

## Evidence

Supports a chemical change, **if linked to a new substance**:

- a gas that a test identifies (e.g. CO₂ turns limewater milky; H₂ pops with a lighted splint; O₂ relights a glowing splint)
- a precipitate not present before
- a permanent colour change from a new substance
- a temperature change produced by the reaction itself
- loss of a starting property (e.g. iron no longer magnetic)

Can mislead, because physical changes also show it:

- bubbles when a liquid boils
- paler colour on dilution
- temperature change on dissolving some solids (e.g. ammonium nitrate)
- a solid "disappearing" when it dissolves

## Exam traps

- Reversible does **not** mean physical (hydrated copper(II) sulfate).
- Breaking bonds does **not** always mean chemical (melting an ionic compound).
- Gas given off does **not** always mean chemical (boiling).
- Mass is conserved in **both**; an apparent loss means a gas escaped.
- "A reaction took place" is not an explanation: name the new substance.
- A burning candle involves both types of change.
- Dissolving is physical even when the solution is coloured.

## Self-test

1. State the one test for deciding whether a change is chemical.
2. Give **two** differences between a physical and a chemical change.
3. Classify: (a) chocolate melting; (b) bread toasting; (c) salt dissolving; (d) magnesium burning.
4. Why is melting sodium chloride a physical change even though ionic bonds are overcome?
5. Iron filings and sulfur are mixed. Give **one** way to show this is a physical change.
6. The mixture in question 5 is heated and glows. Give **one** observation showing a chemical change has occurred.
7. Heating blue copper(II) sulfate crystals gives a white powder; adding water turns it blue again. Physical or chemical? Why?
8. Water boils and bubbles form. Why is this not evidence of a chemical change?
9. A solid "disappears" in a liquid. Describe how evaporation could show whether it dissolved or reacted.
10. Mass of a flask of acid and marble chips falls during the reaction. Does this mean mass is not conserved?
11. Which steps in preparing a salt from an insoluble base are physical?
12. Name the type of change in electrolysis and give a reason.

**Answers**

1. Whether a new substance, with a different formula, is formed.
2. Any two: a chemical change forms a new substance, a physical change does not; a chemical change rearranges atoms, a physical change keeps the same particles; physical changes are usually easily reversed by physical means, chemical changes usually are not.
3. (a) Physical; (b) chemical; (c) physical; (d) chemical.
4. The molten salt is still made of the same Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; no new substance forms.
5. A magnet removes the iron; each element keeps its own properties.
6. The product is black and is not attracted to a magnet (or the mixture keeps glowing after heating stops).
7. Chemical: CuSO₄·5H₂O and CuSO₄ are different substances with different formulae.
8. Steam is still water, H₂O; no new substance is formed.
9. If evaporating the liquid leaves the original solid, it dissolved; if it leaves a different substance, it reacted.
10. No: carbon dioxide escaped from the open flask; the total mass of all substances is unchanged.
11. Filtering, evaporating and crystallising.
12. Chemical: the compound is decomposed into new substances at the electrodes.
