This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Physics (8463) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published them, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Physics (8463) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/gcse/physics-8463)
- [AQA GCSE Physics (8463) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/gcse/physics-8463/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series and tier
- [AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification at a glance](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/gcse/physics-8463/specification/specification-at-a-glance)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade-boundary archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. Every paper on this page was set on the 8463 specification, first taught in September 2016 and first examined in 2018, so older papers cover the same content. It is a linear, tiered qualification. Foundation and Higher candidates each sit two papers of 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks, worth half of the GCSE each. Paper 1 covers energy, electricity, particle model of matter and atomic structure; Paper 2 covers forces, waves, magnetism and electromagnetism, and space physics. Match the paper to your tier, for example 8463/1F or 8463/2H. This is the separate-science Physics qualification, not Combined Science: Trilogy.

## June 2025

The reports say candidates had the equations sheet, which improved calculation marks at both tiers. They repeat that handwriting is a problem and that answers should use the question numbers printed on the paper.

**Where marks were lost: Foundation**

- Paper 1: units and rearrangement, particularly at grades 4 and 5. Candidates who substituted first and rearranged afterwards scored better than those who did both at once. Some used equations from Paper 2 on Paper 1.
- Graph work lost marks when the horizontal axis was not labelled or points were plotted instead of drawing bars.
- Internal energy, the idea of a renewable source being replenished, and the term environmental impact (answers drifted to cost and reliability).
- Tidal and wind power comparisons needed a stated flow speed, and a multiple-choice item needed more than one box ticked.
- Paper 2: line-of-best-fit work without a ruler, an unlabelled vertical axis, the wrong unit for wavelength, using one velocity rather than the change in velocity for acceleration, and not knowing the word for a natural satellite.
- Only about 40 per cent knew that the Sun will end as a black dwarf, and the most popular wrong answer was a black hole.
- White light was treated as a single thing rather than a mixture of colours, which spoiled the answer about filters.

**Where marks were lost: Higher**

- Paper 1: answers to sustainability questions were too vague, tidal and wind power were compared without flow speed, and the percentage-difference calculation used the wrong top line.
- Specific heat capacity was confused with thermal conductivity and insulation in a fusion question, and the walls of a reactor were described as simply withstanding heat.
- Energy was not converted to joules in about 15 per cent of the attempts at one calculation, and series and parallel circuit reasoning rested on wrong assumptions.
- Paper 2: transverse and longitudinal waves were compared without a similarity, so about half the candidates stayed at the lowest level; incorrect wavelength conversions and misuse of standard form were common.
- Equation triangles confused many candidates, and rounding part-way through a calculation was unhelpful.
- A random error was recognised by about 60 per cent, while about 30 per cent called it systematic.

## June 2024

The reports note that the equations sheet again helped, that many candidates think a squared or cubed unit means the number must be squared or cubed before it is used in an equation, and that blank answers were common on longer explanations.

**Where marks were lost: Foundation**

- Paper 1: the mean of two values taken when a different approach was needed, unit conversion, drawing the right conclusion from practical data and linking a method to an energy transfer.
- Comparing stored energy wasted with energy usefully transferred was hard, and about 10 per cent did not attempt one such part.
- Paper 2: the process that releases energy in stars (only 56 per cent chose fusion), electromagnetic waves all travelling at the same speed in a vacuum, vague answers on equipment to detect infrared, and direct proportion described wrongly.
- Describing an experiment to measure the speed of sound was vague about the distance needed, and a minority said a sound wave's wavelength increases from air to water.

**Where marks were lost: Higher**

- Paper 1: static electricity explanations were confused (the movement of charge was misdescribed), unit conversions for charge were missed, and total resistance was not found before the current.
- A grade 8-9 multi-step calculation was challenging, and comparative energy calculations were misread.
- Paper 2: descriptions of the refraction practical did not say how the angles would be located or what range to use, and many drew a straight line of best fit through a curved trend.
- Balanced-force explanations did not say that the forces act in opposite directions, and some responses mixed up moments and momentum.
- Red-shift answers did not state that galaxies are moving away, distances were not converted to metres, and standard-form division gave wrong powers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA GCSE Physics 8463 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 8463 assessment-resources page, by series and tier. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page and its archive.

**Which papers should I sit for a full practice run?**
Sit both papers for your tier: 8463/1F and 8463/2F for Foundation, or 8463/1H and 8463/2H for Higher. Each is 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks.

**Do the papers come with an equations sheet?**
The 2024 and 2025 reports say candidates had a Physics Equations Sheet and that calculation marks improved because of it. Check the front of each paper and the AQA specification for the current arrangement.

**Are the paper-level grade thresholds official grades?**
No. AQA states that component boundaries are notional and for illustration only. The grade comes from the total mark out of 200 and the subject boundaries for your tier.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
