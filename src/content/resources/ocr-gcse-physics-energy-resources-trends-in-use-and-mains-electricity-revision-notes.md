## Energy sources (P8.2a)

| Resource | Renewable? | Quick comparison point |
|---|---|---|
| Coal, oil, gas | No | Reliable on demand; burning releases carbon dioxide |
| Nuclear fuel | No | Reliable on demand; radioactive waste needs safe storage |
| Biofuel | Yes | Can be regrown; needs land and time |
| Wind | Yes | No fuel; output depends on wind |
| Hydroelectricity | Yes | Output easy to adjust; needs suitable valleys |
| Tides | Yes | Predictable; few suitable sites |
| The Sun | Yes | No fuel; output depends on daylight and cloud |

**Definitions to learn**

- Renewable: replaced as fast as it is used, or never used up.
- Non-renewable: used faster than it forms, so it will run out.

**Compare using three headings:** reliability, cost and set-up, effect on the environment.

**Steam route for fossil, nuclear and some biofuel stations:** fuel energy heats water, steam turns a turbine, the turbine turns a generator.

**Useful relationship:** energy = power × time. For example 9 MW for 24 h gives 216 MWh.

**Worked reminder: comparing two sources.** Wind and gas for electricity.

- Reliability: gas can run whenever demand needs it. Wind output varies with the weather, so other sources must fill the gap when the wind is weak.
- Cost and set-up: wind needs no fuel once built. Gas needs a continuing fuel supply.
- Environment: gas burning releases carbon dioxide. Wind turbines do not, but they change the landscape.
- Conclusion: say which you would choose for a stated purpose and why, for example wind to cut carbon dioxide emissions, gas for output on demand.

**Worked reminder: hydroelectricity.** Water stored at height has gravitational potential energy. It runs down through turbines, which turn generators. Output can be adjusted quickly by changing how much water flows.

## Trends in use (P8.2b)

**Method in steps**

1. Quote start and end values from the table or graph, with units.
2. Work out the change, then the percentage change: (change ÷ starting value) × 100.
3. Work out shares if totals are given: (source ÷ total) × 100.
4. Give a reason linked to the resource: it is non-renewable, it releases carbon dioxide, a cost has fallen, or investment has grown.
5. Say what the data cannot show.

**Must-know distinction:** a source can grow in output while its share falls, if the total grows faster. Percentage change and percentage-point change are different measures.

**Small worked reminder.** Gas generates 150 TWh in a year of 374 TWh in total. Share = 150 ÷ 374 × 100 = 40.1%. If it falls to 110 TWh in a year of 348 TWh, the share is 31.6%.

**Worked reminder: percentage change.** Wind output rises from 10 TWh to 100 TWh. Change = 90 TWh. Percentage change = 90 ÷ 10 × 100 = 900%. Coal falls from 120 TWh to 8 TWh. Change = 112 TWh. Percentage fall = 112 ÷ 120 × 100 = 93.3%.

**Writing a trend answer.** A strong answer follows this pattern: "Coal fell from 120 TWh to 8 TWh between 2005 and 2025, a fall of 93.3%. This is because coal is a non-renewable fossil fuel that releases carbon dioxide when burned, so the country replaced it with wind and solar."

## Mains supply (P8.2g, P8.2h)

| Feature | UK domestic supply |
|---|---|
| Type | Alternating current (a.c.) |
| Frequency | 50 Hz (50 cycles each second) |
| Potential difference | About 230 V |
| Period of one cycle | 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 s |

| | d.c. | a.c. |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of p.d. | One direction | Repeatedly reverses |
| Trace | Flat line | Repeating wave |
| Source | Cell, battery | Mains socket |

**Reading a trace in steps**

1. Find the length of one complete wave in divisions.
2. Multiply that length by the time base setting; the result is the period T.
3. Use f = 1 ÷ T.
4. Check the unit: hertz for frequency, seconds for period.

**Worked reminder: the 50 Hz supply.** At 50 Hz the supply completes 50 cycles every second, so one cycle lasts 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 s, and a minute holds 50 × 60 = 3000 cycles.

**Worked reminder: why a d.c. cell and a.c. mains differ on a trace.** A cell gives the same p.d. at every moment, so the trace is a flat horizontal line. Mains p.d. changes continually, crossing zero as it reverses, so the trace repeats as a wave.

## Live, neutral and earth (P8.2i, P8.2j)

| Wire | Colour | Function | P.d. to earth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live | Brown | Carries the alternating p.d. to the appliance | About 230 V |
| Neutral | Blue | Completes the circuit | About 0 V |
| Earth | Green and yellow | Safety wire joined to the metal case | 0 V |

**P.d. between wires:** live to neutral about 230 V; live to earth about 230 V; neutral to earth about 0 V.

**Dangers**

- Touching the live wire puts a large p.d. across your body, so a current passes through you.
- A switch in the live wire isolates the appliance. A switch in the neutral wire leaves the appliance connected to the live wire when it is off, so the live wire can be dangerous even when a switch is open.
- Insulation around wires and the casing stops you touching live conductors. Damaged or wet insulation raises the risk.
- A connection between live and earth gives a large current, which can overheat wires and cause fire or shock.
- If a live wire touches a metal case that is not earthed, the case becomes live and the next person to touch it gives a path to earth.

**Worked reminder: faulty case.** A live wire comes loose and touches a metal case.

- With an earth wire: the case is joined to earth, so a large current flows through the earth wire, away from the user.
- Without an earth wire: the case is at about 230 V. A person who touches it provides a path to earth and receives a shock.

**Plug wiring in order.** The live wire is brown, the neutral is blue and the earth is green and yellow. The plug practical in the specification is wiring a plug.

## Must-know distinctions

- Renewable is about replacement, not about cost or cleanliness.
- Neutral completes the circuit in normal use. Earth carries no current in normal use.
- Live to neutral is the working p.d. of about 230 V. Neutral to earth is about 0 V.
- Frequency counts cycles each second. Period is how long a single cycle lasts.

## Quick self-test

1. State two renewable and two non-renewable energy resources.
2. Why is biofuel counted as renewable?
3. A source provides 60 TWh in a year when the total is 240 TWh. Find its share.
4. Output of a source rises from 20 TWh to 50 TWh. Find the percentage change.
5. State the frequency and approximate p.d. of the UK domestic supply.
6. One wave on a trace takes 5 divisions and the time base is 4 ms per division. Find the frequency.
7. How many mains cycles occur in 2 s?
8. Which wire is a safety wire connected to the metal case?
9. What is the approximate p.d. between neutral and earth?
10. Explain why a switch should be in the live wire.

### Answers

1. Renewable: any two of biofuel, wind, hydroelectricity, tides, the Sun. Non-renewable: any two of coal, oil, gas, nuclear fuel.
2. It can be regrown, so it is replaced as fast as it is used.
3. 60 ÷ 240 × 100 = **25%**.
4. Change = 30 TWh, so 30 ÷ 20 × 100 = **150%**.
5. **50 Hz and about 230 V**, alternating (a.c.).
6. T = 5 × 4 ms = 20 ms = 0.020 s, so f = 1 ÷ 0.020 = **50 Hz**.
7. 50 × 2 = **100 cycles**.
8. The **earth wire**.
9. **About 0 V**.
10. Opening a switch in the live wire disconnects the appliance from the 230 V supply, so its parts are not live. A switch in the neutral wire would leave them connected to the live wire.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "it will not run out" for renewable without saying it is replaced or never used up.
- Quoting a change in a graph without figures and units.
- Confusing percentage change (divide by the starting value) with share (divide by the total).
- Giving percentage change as 150 when it should be 150%.
- Writing "current" where "potential difference" is meant, for example for the 230 V of the live wire.
- Saying a.c. "has no direction". Its direction changes repeatedly.
- Stating that the neutral wire is safe to touch. Say it is near 0 V in normal use only.
- Not linking the open switch to the live wire still being connected.
- Treating insulation as stopping current in a faulty circuit, rather than stopping contact with the conductor.
