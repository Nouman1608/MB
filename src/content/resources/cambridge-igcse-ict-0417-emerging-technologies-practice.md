## Section A: short answers

**1.** State what is meant by *extended reality*. **[1]**

**2.** A student points a tablet at a page in a science textbook and a 3D model of the heart appears on the page. Identify the technology being used. **[1]**

**3.** Which statement describes virtual reality?

A: digital labels are added to a live camera view

B: the user's view of the real world is replaced by a computer-generated environment

C: a computer answers spoken questions

D: a computer suggests films based on viewing history

**[1]**

**4.** Give **two** tasks that AI can perform which normally need human intelligence. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** items of hardware a user needs to experience virtual reality. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** An online clothing shop introduces a feature that uses a customer's phone camera to show how a pair of glasses would look on their face.

**(a)** Identify the type of extended reality used. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe **two** benefits of this feature to customers. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe **one** benefit to the shop. **[1]**

**7.** A bank uses AI software to check every card payment as it is made.

**(a)** Explain how this helps customers. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** problem this can cause for customers. **[1]**

**8.** A fire service trains new firefighters using virtual reality.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why VR is used instead of training only with real fires. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** health or safety problems trainees may experience with VR headsets. **[2]**

**9.** A company replaces its customer telephone line with an AI chatbot on its website.

**(a)** Give **two** advantages for customers. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **two** disadvantages for customers. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why the information used to train an AI system affects whether its decisions are fair. **[2]**

**11.** Compare virtual reality and augmented reality for a history lesson about an ancient city. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss the impact of artificial intelligence on everyday life. Do not refer to banking in your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Technologies, including virtual and augmented reality, that change how a person sees or experiences their surroundings using computer-generated content. **[1]**

**2.** Augmented reality. **[1]**

**3.** B. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: recognising speech (1); recognising images or faces (1); translating languages (1); answering questions in natural language (1); making predictions or decisions from data (1); learning from data to improve (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any two: headset (1); motion sensors or trackers (1); hand controllers or gloves (1); a computer or console powerful enough to run it (1). **[2]**

**6. (a)** Augmented reality. **[1]**

**(b)** Any two: see how the glasses look before buying, so they are more confident in their choice (1); no need to travel to a shop (1); can try many styles quickly (1); fewer unwanted purchases to return (1). **[2]**

**(c)** Any one: fewer returns, saving costs (1); more customers may buy because they can see the product (1); attracts customers who like new features (1). **[1]**

**7. (a)** The AI spots payments that do not match the customer's usual pattern, which may be fraud (1), so the payment can be stopped or the customer warned quickly, and money is not lost (1). **[2]**

**(b)** A genuine payment may be blocked, for example when the customer is on holiday, causing inconvenience (1). **[1]**

**8. (a)** Any two: trainees practise in dangerous situations without risk of injury (1); scenarios can be repeated many times (1); rare or large-scale situations can be created that would be impossible to set up for real (1); lower cost than setting real fires or damaging buildings (1). **[2]**

**(b)** Any two: motion sickness (1); headaches or eye strain from long use (1); walking into or tripping over real objects because the real surroundings cannot be seen (1). **[2]**

**9. (a)** Any two: available at any time of day (1); no waiting in a phone queue (1); quick answers to common questions (1); can be used while doing other things on the website (1). **[2]**

**(b)** Any two: may not understand unusual or complex questions (1); may give wrong answers (1); no human contact, which some customers prefer (1); hard to use for people without internet access or skills (1). **[2]**

**10.** The system learns patterns from the data it is given (1); if that data is unfair, incomplete or does not represent everyone, its decisions will repeat that bias (1). **[2]**

**11.** Any four, at least one about each: VR could place students inside a fully computer-generated model of the ancient city, so they can explore streets and buildings as if they were there (1); VR blocks out the classroom, which makes it immersive but means students cannot see each other or the teacher (1); VR needs a headset for each student, which is expensive (1); AR could show a 3D model of the city on the desk or over a map, so students still see the classroom and can discuss it together (1); AR can run on tablets the school may already have (1); AR is less immersive than VR (1). **[4]**

**12.** Positive impacts, up to 4: voice assistants let people carry out tasks hands-free, which helps people with limited mobility (1); recommendations help people find relevant products, music or films quickly (1); translation tools help people communicate in other languages (1); AI analysis of medical scans can detect disease earlier (1); learning apps adapt to each student's level (1). Negative impacts, up to 4: routine jobs may be lost, so workers need retraining (1); large amounts of personal data are collected, reducing privacy (1); AI can be biased if trained on unfair data (1); AI can give wrong answers that people trust without checking (1); realistic fake images or text can spread misinformation (1). **[6]** Award a maximum of 6 marks from these points; one-sided answers are limited to 4 marks; references to banking do not score.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I balance a discuss answer on emerging technologies?**
Give positive and negative impacts from more than one area of life, each with its effect on people. The mark schemes on this page cap one-sided answers, so plan both sides before you write.

**Can I use the same example for AI and AR?**
Only if you make the role of each clear. For example, an AR navigation app shows arrows over the street; it is the AR that changes what the user sees.
