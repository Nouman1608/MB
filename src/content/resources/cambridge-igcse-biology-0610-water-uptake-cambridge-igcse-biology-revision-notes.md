## The four statements

1. **Identify** root hair cells in diagrams and images and **state** their functions.
2. **State** that the large surface area of root hairs increases the uptake of water and mineral ions.
3. **Outline** the pathway of water: root hair cells → root cortex cells → xylem → mesophyll cells.
4. **Investigate**, using a suitable stain, the pathway of water through the above-ground parts of a plant.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Root hair cell | cell in the outer layer of a young root with a long, thin extension into the soil |
| Root hair | the extension itself; part of the root hair cell |
| Function of root hair cells | uptake of water and mineral ions |
| Osmosis | movement of water into the root hair cell through its partially permeable membrane |
| Root cortex | layers of cells between the outer layer of the root and the central xylem |
| Mesophyll cells | cells inside the leaf that receive water from the xylem |
| Stain | water-soluble dye used to show the route water takes |

## Root hair cell checklist

- outer layer of a young root, just behind the tip
- long thin extension between soil particles
- large vacuole; nucleus
- **no chloroplasts** (no light underground)
- takes up **water** (by osmosis) and **mineral ions**

## Surface area in one chain

```
many long root hairs → large surface area in contact with soil water
→ more water and mineral ions taken up at once → faster uptake
```

Damage to root hairs (for example when a seedling is pulled up) → smaller surface area → less uptake → plant may wilt.

## Pathway of water

```
soil water
  → root hair cells      (enters by osmosis)
  → root cortex cells    (cell to cell, towards the centre)
  → xylem                (up the root and stem)
  → mesophyll cells      (in the leaf)
  → evaporates and diffuses out as water vapour (transpiration, 8.3)
```

Direction check: **outside to centre** in the root, **up** the stem, **out** into the leaf.

## Stain investigation: method in steps

1. Stand a freshly cut leafy celery stalk, or a white-flowered shoot, in water containing a dye.
2. Leave it in a warm, bright place for a few hours.
3. Cut thin cross-sections of the stalk with a scalpel on a white tile, cutting away from your fingers.
4. Look with a hand lens or microscope.
5. Record where the colour is.

**Result:** coloured spots in a ring (the xylem in the vascular bundles); coloured leaf veins; coloured petals.
**Conclusion:** water travels in the xylem to the leaves and flowers.

**Variables to keep the same if comparing shoots:** type and length of shoot, number of leaves, dye concentration, time.
**Safety:** sharp scalpel; dyes stain skin and clothes.

## Links to other sub-topics

- **Osmosis (topic 3):** water enters root hair cells through the partially permeable cell membrane.
- **Xylem (8.1):** the stain marks the xylem because that is the tissue carrying water up the stem.
- **Photosynthesis (6.1):** water delivered to the mesophyll cells is a raw material.
- **Mineral ions (6.1):** nitrate and magnesium ions enter through the same root hair cells.

## Answer patterns

- **"State the function of root hair cells"**: uptake of water and mineral ions (both needed).
- **"Explain the shape of a root hair cell"**: long extension → large surface area → more uptake.
- **"Outline the pathway of water"**: the four stages in order with syllabus names.
- **"Describe how to show the pathway"**: plant, dye, time, section, observe, conclude.
- **"Suggest why a transplanted plant wilts"**: root hairs damaged → less surface area → less water taken up.

## Exam traps

- A root hair is part of a cell, not a separate cell.
- No chloroplasts in root hair cells.
- Root hairs take up water and mineral ions, not "food" or "nutrients".
- Do not leave out the cortex or the mesophyll in the pathway.
- The stain investigation is on **above-ground** parts.
- Name the xylem in the result; "the stem went blue" does not score.

## Self-test

1. Where on a root are root hair cells found?
2. State **two** substances taken up by root hair cells.
3. By what process does water enter a root hair cell?
4. Why do root hair cells have no chloroplasts?
5. Explain how the shape of a root hair cell helps its function.
6. Put in order: xylem, mesophyll cells, root hair cells, root cortex cells.
7. Name the tissue that carries water up the stem.
8. Why is a white-flowered shoot useful in the stain investigation?
9. Describe where the dye appears in a cross-section of the stem.
10. Give **one** safety precaution for the stain investigation.
11. A seedling is pulled from the soil and replanted. It wilts. Suggest why.
12. What process in the leaf keeps water moving along the pathway?

### Answers

1. In the outer layer of a young root, a little way behind the tip.
2. Water and mineral ions.
3. Osmosis.
4. They are underground, with no light for photosynthesis.
5. The long thin extension gives a large surface area, increasing the uptake of water and mineral ions.
6. Root hair cells, root cortex cells, xylem, mesophyll cells.
7. Xylem.
8. The dye shows up clearly in white petals, so you can see that water reached the flowers.
9. As coloured spots arranged in a ring near the edge: the xylem in the vascular bundles.
10. Cut away from your fingers on a white tile / wear gloves because dyes stain skin.
11. Root hairs were torn off, reducing the surface area for water uptake.
12. Transpiration: water evaporates from mesophyll cells and diffuses out through the stomata.
