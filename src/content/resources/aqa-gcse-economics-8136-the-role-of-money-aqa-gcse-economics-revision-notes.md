## The two content points

1. **Functions of money:** medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, means of deferred payment.
2. **Definition of money:** money is more than the banknotes and coins in circulation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Money | Anything generally accepted in payment for goods and services and in settling debts |
| Barter | Swapping goods or services directly for other goods or services, without money |
| Double coincidence of wants | In barter, each trader must have what the other wants at the same time |
| Medium of exchange | Money is accepted in return for goods and services |
| Unit of account | Money is a common measure of value, so prices can be compared |
| Store of value | Money can be kept and spent later |
| Means of deferred payment | Money allows debts to be fixed now and repaid later |
| Bank deposit | Money held in a bank or building society account, spent by card or transfer |
| Purchasing power | The quantity of goods and services a sum of money can buy |

## Function, problem solved, everyday example

| Function | Barter problem it solves | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| Medium of exchange | Double coincidence of wants | Paying for a bus ticket by contactless card |
| Unit of account | Too many exchange rates between goods | Comparing a £4 sandwich with a £6 salad |
| Store of value | Goods rot or are hard to keep | Keeping £200 in a savings account for a holiday |
| Means of deferred payment | Debts in goods are hard to measure | Paying off a phone contract over 24 months |

**Linking idea:** deferred payment depends on store of value. People only agree to be repaid later if money will still be worth something.

## Why it matters for the economy

- A medium of exchange lets workers **specialise**, which raises productivity and output.
- A unit of account lets firms calculate **costs, revenue and profit**, and governments measure **GDP** and tax.
- A store of value lets households **save**; savings can be lent to firms for investment.
- Deferred payment allows **borrowing** for houses, cars and business investment.

## Qualities that let money perform its functions

Generally acceptable · durable · divisible · portable · limited in supply · hard to forge.

Quick test: fresh fish fails on durability, so it is a poor store of value; a house fails on divisibility and acceptability, so it is a store of value but not money.

## Money is more than notes and coins

- Most money is **bank and building society deposits**.
- Cards, transfers and phone payments move deposits; no cash changes hands.
- Deposits do all four jobs, so they count as money.
- Bank lending creates new deposits when a loan is credited to an account.
- In the UK, notes and coins are only a small share of all money.

**Not money in themselves:** debit cards (they move deposits), credit cards (a way to borrow), shares and houses (stores of value but not generally accepted in payment).

## Inflation and the functions

| Function | Effect of high inflation |
|---|---|
| Store of value | Each pound buys less over time, so savings lose purchasing power |
| Means of deferred payment | Lenders are repaid in money worth less; they may refuse to lend or charge high interest |
| Unit of account | Prices change so often that comparing values becomes hard |
| Medium of exchange | In extreme cases people stop accepting the currency and use another currency or barter |

## Calculation patterns

**Real value of savings.** £800 held for a year with inflation of 4%: 800 ÷ 1.04 = **£769** in last year's prices (to the nearest pound).

**Purchasing power of a loan repayment.** £2,000 repaid after a year of 10% inflation: 2,000 ÷ 1.10 = **£1,818**; the lender has lost about £182 of purchasing power.

**Exchange rates under barter.** For n goods, the number of pairs is n × (n − 1) ÷ 2. For 8 goods: 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = **28**. With money, 8 prices.

## Exam traps

- Store of value = **saving**; deferred payment = **repaying debt later**. Do not swap them.
- "Money is notes and coins" contradicts the specification. Say that it includes deposits.
- A credit card is not money; it gives access to borrowed money.
- Name the function **and** link it to the context in the question for explain marks.
- Inflation affects several functions, not just store of value.
- Dividing by 1.05 is right for 5% inflation; subtracting 5% gives a slightly different, less accurate answer. Use the method the question asks for and show working.

## Quick self-test

1. Define money.
2. What is a double coincidence of wants?
3. Which function of money is used when comparing the prices of two pairs of trainers?
4. Which function is used when a student saves part-time wages for university?
5. Which function is used when a family repays a mortgage over 25 years?
6. Why does the means of deferred payment depend on the store of value?
7. Explain why bank deposits count as money.
8. Is a credit card money? Explain briefly.
9. Give two qualities that make something good money.
10. £1,200 is kept as cash for a year when inflation is 6%. What is it worth in last year's prices, to the nearest pound?
11. How many exchange rates does barter need for 6 goods?
12. Which function of money allows people to specialise?

### Answers

1. Anything generally accepted in payment for goods and services and in settling debts.
2. In barter, each trader must have what the other wants at the same time.
3. Unit of account.
4. Store of value.
5. Means of deferred payment.
6. Lenders will accept repayment later only if money keeps its value over time.
7. They are accepted in payment through cards and transfers, and can be saved, so they perform the functions of money.
8. No. It is a way of borrowing money, which is repaid later.
9. Any two: generally acceptable, durable, divisible, portable, limited in supply, hard to forge.
10. 1,200 ÷ 1.06 = **£1,132**.
11. 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = **15**.
12. Medium of exchange.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four functions of money in AQA GCSE Economics?**
Medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and means of deferred payment.

**Why is money more than cash?**
Most money is held as bank deposits, which are spent by card and transfer and do all the jobs of money.
