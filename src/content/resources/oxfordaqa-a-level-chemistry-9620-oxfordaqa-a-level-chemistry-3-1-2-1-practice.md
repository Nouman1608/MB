These questions cover **3.1.2.1 Relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, drawn on by Units 2 and 5). Use these Ar values unless a question gives others: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, S 32.1, Cl 35.5, K 39.1, Fe 55.8, Cu 63.5, Ba 137.3, Pb 207.2. Revise first with the [relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-1/) or the [revision notes for this subtopic](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-1-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the term relative atomic mass. **[2]**

**2.** State why the term relative formula mass, rather than relative molecular mass, is used for potassium chloride. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the relative formula mass of ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the relative formula mass of lead(II) nitrate, Pb(NO₃)₂. **[1]**

**5.** Propane (C₃H₈), carbon dioxide (CO₂) and dinitrogen oxide (N₂O) all have the same relative molecular mass. Show that this is true. **[2]**

**6.** A metal M forms an oxide with the formula M₂O and relative formula mass 94.2. Identify M. **[2]**

**7.** The relative molecular mass of sulfur trioxide, SO₃, is 80.1. How many times heavier, on average, is one SO₃ molecule than one carbon-12 atom? **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** Hydrated sodium sulfate has the formula Na₂SO₄·xH₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of anhydrous sodium sulfate, Na₂SO₄. **[1]**

**(b)** The relative formula mass of the hydrated salt is 322.1. Calculate x. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of water in the hydrated salt. **[1]**

**9.** An element E forms a hydroxide with the formula E(OH)₂. Its relative formula mass is 171.3.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of E and identify it. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the relative atomic mass of E is not a whole number. **[2]**

**10.** A student heats 2.50 g of hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·xH₂O, until the mass stops changing. The anhydrous CuSO₄ left has a mass of 1.60 g.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of CuSO₄. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of CuSO₄ and of water in the sample. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the value of x. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the student heats the sample until the mass stops changing. **[1]**

**11.** Boron has two isotopes: boron-10 (abundance 19.9%) and boron-11 (abundance 80.1%).

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of boron to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** State the relative isotopic mass of boron-11, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answer to (a) to calculate the relative molecular mass of diborane, B₂H₆. **[1]**

**12.** Iron forms two common oxides, FeO and Fe₂O₃.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of each oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in Fe₂O₃. **[1]**

**(c)** A sample of an iron oxide contains 72.4% iron by mass. Use your answers to show whether it could be FeO. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** The average (weighted mean) mass of an atom of an element [1] relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Compared with carbon" without "one-twelfth" and "carbon-12" loses the second mark.

**2.** Potassium chloride is ionic, a giant lattice of ions with no molecules [1] **[1]**

**3.** 2(14.0) + 8(1.0) + 32.1 + 4(16.0) = **132.1** [1] **[1]**

**4.** 207.2 + 2(14.0) + 6(16.0) = **331.2** [1] **[1]**

**5.** C₃H₈: 3(12.0) + 8(1.0) = 44.0 [1]; CO₂: 12.0 + 2(16.0) = 44.0 and N₂O: 2(14.0) + 16.0 = 44.0 [1] **[2]**

**6.** Ar(M) = (94.2 − 16.0) ÷ 2 = 39.1 [1]; M is **potassium** [1] **[2]**

**7.** 80.1 ÷ 12 = **6.68 times** (accept 6.675) [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* One-twelfth of a carbon-12 atom is the unit, so a whole carbon-12 atom is 12 units.

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** 2(23.0) + 32.1 + 4(16.0) = **142.1** [1]

**(b)** Mass of water = 322.1 − 142.1 = 180.0 [1]; x = 180.0 ÷ 18.0 = **10** [1]

**(c)** 180.0 ÷ 322.1 × 100 = **55.9%** [1] **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** Ar(E) = 171.3 − 2(16.0 + 1.0) = **137.3** [1]; E is **barium** [1]

**(b)** Barium exists as several isotopes [1]; Ar is a weighted mean of their masses, which need not be a whole number [1] **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** 63.5 + 32.1 + 4(16.0) = **159.6** [1]

**(b)** n(CuSO₄) = 1.60 ÷ 159.6 = 0.0100 mol [1]; n(H₂O) = (2.50 − 1.60) ÷ 18.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]

**(c)** Ratio 0.0500 : 0.0100 = 5 : 1, so **x = 5** [1]

**(d)** To make sure all the water of crystallisation has been removed [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the water by difference before dividing by 18.0; dividing the hydrate's mass by 18.0 is a common slip.

**11.**

**(a)** (10 × 19.9 + 11 × 80.1) ÷ 100 [1] = **10.8** [1]

**(b)** **11** [1]

**(c)** 2(10.8) + 6(1.0) = **27.6** [1] **[4]**

**12.**

**(a)** FeO: 55.8 + 16.0 = **71.8** [1]; Fe₂O₃: 2(55.8) + 3(16.0) = **159.6** [1]

**(b)** 111.6 ÷ 159.6 × 100 = **69.9%** [1]

**(c)** Percentage of iron in FeO = 55.8 ÷ 71.8 × 100 = 77.7% [1]; 72.4% matches neither FeO (77.7%) nor Fe₂O₃ (69.9%), so the sample cannot be pure FeO [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing percentages is a reliable way to test a suggested formula; show both values before concluding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions that miss "average", "one-twelfth" or "carbon-12".
- Not multiplying every atom inside a bracket.
- Forgetting the oxygen in water of crystallisation.
- Adding units to Ar or Mr.
