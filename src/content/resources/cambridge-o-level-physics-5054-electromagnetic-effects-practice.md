## Section A

**1.** State **two** factors that affect the magnitude of the e.m.f. induced in a coil when a magnet is moved near it. **[2]**

**2.** State Lenz's law. **[1]**

**3.** Name the rule used to find the direction of the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, and state what the first finger and the second finger represent. **[2]**

**4.** A transformer has 500 turns on its primary coil and 25 turns on its secondary coil. The primary is connected to a 230 V a.c. supply.

**(a)** Calculate the output p.d. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether this is a step-up or a step-down transformer. **[1]**

**5.** A wire carrying a current lies at right angles to a magnetic field and experiences a force. State the effect on the force of

**(a)** reversing the current only **[1]**
**(b)** reversing both the current and the field. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a transformer does not work when its primary coil is connected to a steady d.c. supply. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A coil is connected to a sensitive centre-zero meter.

**(a)** Describe how a bar magnet and the coil can be used to demonstrate electromagnetic induction, stating what is observed. **[4]**
**(b)** State the effect on the meter reading of moving the magnet faster. **[1]**
**(c)** When the N pole of the magnet is pushed into the coil, the end of the coil nearest the magnet becomes an N pole. Explain how this shows Lenz's law. **[2]**

**8.** A simple a.c. generator has a rectangular coil rotating at a steady rate between the poles of a magnet.

**(a)** State the purpose of the slip rings and brushes. **[2]**
**(b)** State the position of the coil, relative to the field, when the e.m.f. is (i) a maximum and (ii) zero, and explain the maximum position. **[3]**
**(c)** The coil is turned twice as fast. State **two** changes to the graph of e.m.f. against time. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Describe the magnetic field produced by a current in a long solenoid, both inside and outside it. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect on the field of reversing the current. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how a relay allows a small current in one circuit to switch on a large current to a motor in another circuit. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain how a loudspeaker produces sound when an alternating current flows in its coil. **[3]**
**(b)** State why a larger current produces a louder sound. **[1]**

**11.** A simple d.c. motor has a coil that rotates between the poles of a magnet.

**(a)** State **three** ways of increasing the turning effect on the coil. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a split-ring commutator is needed. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the coil continues past the position where the turning effect is zero. **[1]**

**12.** Two long parallel wires carry currents in opposite directions.

**(a)** State whether the wires attract or repel. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain this in terms of the magnetic fields in the region between the wires. **[2]**

**13.** A power station sends 200 kW along cables of total resistance 5.0 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the cables when the power is sent at 20 kV. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the power lost in the cables at 20 kV. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the power lost if the same power were sent at 2.0 kV. **[2]**
**(d)** State the advantage of high-voltage transmission shown by your answers. **[1]**

**14.** A step-up transformer at a power station raises the p.d. from 25 kV to 400 kV. Its primary coil has 1000 turns. Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: the **speed of movement** (rate of change of the field, or rate of cutting field lines); the **strength of the magnet**; the **number of turns** on the coil.

**2.** The **current produced by an induced e.m.f. opposes the change producing it** [1].

**3.** **Fleming's left-hand rule** [1]; first finger = **field**, second finger = **current** (both needed) [1].

**4. (a)** Vs = Vp × Ns ÷ Np = 230 × 25 ÷ 500 [1] = **11.5 V** [1].
**(b)** **Step-down** [1].

**5. (a)** The force **reverses direction** [1].
**(b)** The force is in the **same direction as originally** [1].

**6.** A steady current gives a **constant (unchanging) magnetic field** in the core [1], so **no e.m.f. is induced in the secondary**, since induction needs a changing field [1].

**7. (a)** **Push the magnet into the coil**: the meter needle **deflects** [1]. **Hold the magnet still** inside the coil: the reading **returns to zero** [1]. **Pull the magnet out**: the needle **deflects the opposite way** [1]. An e.m.f. is induced **only while the magnet (or coil) is moving**, that is, while the field through the coil is changing [1].
**(b)** A **larger deflection** (larger induced e.m.f. and current) [1].
**(c)** The N pole at the near end of the coil **repels the approaching N pole** [1], so the induced current **opposes the change (the motion) producing it**, as Lenz's law states [1].

**8. (a)** They **connect the rotating coil to the external circuit** [1] **without the wires twisting** as the coil turns [1].
**(b)** (i) Maximum when the plane of the coil is **parallel to the field** [1]; (ii) zero when the plane of the coil is **perpendicular to the field** [1]. At the maximum position the coil sides are **moving at right angles to the field lines, cutting them at the fastest rate** [1].
**(c)** The **peak e.m.f. is larger** [1]; the **frequency doubles** (twice as many cycles in the same time) [1].

**9. (a)** Outside: **like the field of a bar magnet**, with N and S poles at the ends [1]. Inside: **strong, nearly uniform, with parallel field lines** along the axis [1].
**(b)** The field **reverses direction** (the poles swap) [1].
**(c)** The small current flows in the **relay coil, magnetising its soft-iron core** [1]. The core **attracts an iron armature** [1], which **closes the contacts in the motor circuit**, allowing the large current to flow [1].

**10. (a)** The coil sits in the **field of a permanent magnet**, so the current in it experiences a **force** [1]. Because the current **alternates, the force keeps reversing**, so the coil and attached cone **move backwards and forwards** [1]. The vibrating cone **pushes on the air, producing sound waves** at the frequency of the current [1].
**(b)** A larger current gives a **larger force and larger-amplitude vibrations** [1].

**11. (a)** **More turns on the coil** [1]; **a larger current** [1]; **a stronger magnetic field** (stronger magnet) [1].
**(b)** It **reverses the current in the coil every half turn** [1], so the forces on the coil sides **keep turning it in the same direction** instead of turning it back [1].
**(c)** The coil's **momentum (inertia) carries it past** that position [1].

**12. (a)** They **repel** [1].
**(b)** Between the wires the two fields **point in the same direction and add** [1], making a **strong field between the wires that pushes them apart** [1].

**13. (a)** I = P ÷ V = 200 000 ÷ 20 000 = **10 A** [1].
**(b)** P.d. across cables = I R = 10 × 5.0 = 50 V [1]; power lost = I × p.d. = 10 × 50 = **500 W** [1].
**(c)** I = 200 000 ÷ 2000 = 100 A; p.d. across cables = 100 × 5.0 = 500 V [1]; power lost = 100 × 500 = **50 000 W** (50 kW) [1].
**(d)** Transmitting at high voltage gives a **smaller current and much smaller power losses** in the cables (here one-hundredth) [1].

**14.** Ns = Np × Vs ÷ Vp = 1000 × 400 ÷ 25 [1] = **16 000 turns** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying an e.m.f. is induced while the magnet rests inside the coil.
- Stating Lenz's law without naming the induced pole, or naming the wrong one.
- Confusing slip rings (generator) with the split-ring commutator (motor).
- Using the right hand for the direction of the force on a conductor.
- Saying a transformer "changes a.c. to d.c." or works with d.c.
- Writing that high-voltage transmission reduces the power sent, rather than the current and the losses.
