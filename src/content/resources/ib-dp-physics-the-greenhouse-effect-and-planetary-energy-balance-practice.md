## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State what is meant by the albedo of a planet. **[1]**
**(b)** State two reasons why the albedo of Earth is not constant. **[2]**

**2.** A planet of radius 3.4 × 10⁶ m is exposed to a solar constant of 590 W m⁻² and has an albedo of 0.25.
**(a)** Calculate the total power incident on the planet. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the power scattered by the planet. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean absorbed intensity over the whole surface of the planet. **[2]**

**3.** A flat plate of area 0.50 m² is at a temperature of 400 K and radiates a total of 620 W.
**(a)** Calculate the emissivity of the plate. **[3]**
**(b)** The plate is changed to a surface of emissivity 0.85. Calculate the temperature at which it radiates 1000 W m⁻². **[3]**

**4.** Explain why the mean intensity of sunlight arriving at a planet is S/4, where S is the solar constant. **[3]**

**5.** A planet has no atmosphere, a solar constant of 1900 W m⁻² and an albedo of 0.40.
**(a)** Calculate its equilibrium temperature if it radiates as an ideal black surface. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the equilibrium temperature if its emissivity is 0.90. **[2]**

**6.** Name the four main greenhouse gases. For each, state one natural source or one source created by human activity. **[4]**

**7.** Explain, using molecular energy levels, how a carbon dioxide molecule absorbs infrared radiation from the Earth's surface and what happens afterwards. **[5]**

**8.** Describe how the greenhouse effect can be explained using a resonance model. **[3]**

**9.** The table gives energy flows for a model planet, averaged over its whole surface, in W m⁻².

| Flow | Value |
|---|---|
| Solar radiation incident at the top of the atmosphere | 340 |
| Solar radiation reflected to space | 95 |
| Solar radiation absorbed by the surface | 170 |
| Solar radiation absorbed by the atmosphere | 75 |
| Infrared emitted by the surface | 380 |
| Infrared from the surface absorbed by the atmosphere | 350 |
| Infrared from the surface passing straight to space | 30 |
| Energy moved from surface to atmosphere by evaporation and convection | 120 |
| Infrared emitted by the atmosphere towards the surface | 330 |

**(a)** Calculate the albedo of the planet. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the surface is in energy balance. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the infrared emitted by the atmosphere towards space. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that the planet as a whole is in balance. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the surface temperature, assuming it radiates as an ideal black surface. **[1]**

**10.** A planet has a solar constant of 3000 W m⁻² and an albedo of 0.20. Treat it as radiating as an ideal black surface.
**(a)** Show that the planet would have an equilibrium temperature of 321 K with no atmosphere. **[3]**
**(b)** A model atmosphere is added as a single layer. It lets sunlight through, absorbs all the infrared from the surface, and radiates equally upward and downward. Show that the surface intensity σTs⁴ is 1200 W m⁻² and find Ts. **[2]**
**(c)** In a second model the layer absorbs only 0.60 of the surface infrared and radiates with emissivity 0.60, equally up and down. Determine Ts. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the answer to (c) is lower than the answer to (b). **[1]**

**11.** An Earth-like planet absorbs a mean intensity of 238 W m⁻². Its atmosphere is modelled as a single layer that absorbs a fraction a of the surface infrared and radiates with emissivity a, equally up and down. In this model σTs⁴(1 − a/2) is equal to the absorbed intensity.
**(a)** Calculate the surface temperature when a = 0.78. **[2]**
**(b)** Burning fossil fuels raises a to 0.82. Calculate the new surface temperature and the rise. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the increase in a represents the enhanced greenhouse effect. **[3]**
**(d)** While the surface is warming, a net 0.5 W m⁻² enters a 250 m layer of water (mass 2.5 × 10⁵ kg under each square metre, specific heat capacity 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹). Estimate its temperature rise in one year of 3.15 × 10⁷ s. **[2]**

**12.** The Sun has a surface temperature of 5800 K and the Earth's surface is at 288 K.
**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of peak emission for each. **[2]**
**(b)** State the region of the electromagnetic spectrum for each peak. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy of an infrared photon of wavelength 15 μm. **[2]**
**(d)** Use your answers to explain why the atmosphere warms the surface by absorbing outgoing radiation more than incoming radiation. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Albedo is the total scattered power divided by the total incident power. [1]
**(b)** Cloud formations change from day to day. [1] Latitude (surface type such as ice, ocean or forest, and the angle of the rays). [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give two different reasons; "clouds" and "weather" would be the same idea written twice.

**2. (a)** P = Sπr² = 590 × π × (3.4 × 10⁶)². [1] = **2.14 × 10¹⁶ W**. [1]
**(b)** 0.25 × 2.14 × 10¹⁶ = **5.36 × 10¹⁵ W**. [1]
**(c)** Mean absorbed = S(1 − α)/4 = 590 × 0.75 / 4. [1] = **111 W m⁻²**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check by dividing the absorbed power 1.61 × 10¹⁶ W by 4πr²: it gives the same 111 W m⁻².

**3. (a)** σT⁴ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 400⁴ = 1450 W m⁻². [1] P/A = 620 / 0.50 = 1240 W m⁻². [1] e = 1240 / 1450 = **0.85**. [1]
**(b)** T⁴ = 1000 / (0.85 × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸). [1] = 2.07 × 10¹⁰ K⁴. [1] T = **380 K**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution into P/A = eσT⁴ before the answer, so a slip in arithmetic does not hide the method.

**4.** The planet intercepts sunlight over its projected disc of area πR². [1] The energy is shared (and radiated) over the whole surface of area 4πR². [1] Mean intensity = SπR² / 4πR² = **S/4**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name both areas, and say which one receives the sunlight.

**5. (a)** Absorbed = 1900 × 0.60 / 4 = 285 W m⁻². [1] T⁴ = 285 / 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ = 5.03 × 10⁹ K⁴. [1] T = **266 K**. [1]
**(b)** T⁴ = 285 / (0.90 × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸) = 5.58 × 10⁹. [1] T = **273 K**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Round once at the end; a rounded T⁴ can shift the third significant figure after the fourth root.

**6.** Carbon dioxide: burning fossil fuels (or respiration, volcanoes). [1] Methane: cattle farming or rice paddies (or wetlands). [1] Nitrous oxide: fertilisers (or bacteria in soil). [1] Water vapour: evaporation from oceans (natural) or the warming of the air by other gases. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match each source to its gas; a vague source such as "pollution" is unlikely to earn the mark.

**7.** The molecule has discrete vibrational energy levels. [1] An infrared photon is absorbed if its energy hf equals the difference between two levels. [1] Infrared from the cooler surface has photon energies of this size, which sunlight mostly does not. [1] The molecule then falls back to a lower level and emits a photon, or passes energy to other molecules in collisions. [1] The photon goes off in a random direction, so the radiation is re-emitted in all directions and some returns to the surface. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say "re-emitted in all directions"; "sent back to Earth" misses why only part returns.

**8.** The bonds in the molecule behave as oscillators with a natural frequency in the infrared. [1] Radiation at that frequency drives the oscillation to a large amplitude, so energy is absorbed strongly. [1] The oscillating charges then radiate, in all directions. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link resonance to the frequency match; "the molecule vibrates" alone is too vague.

**9. (a)** α = 95 / 340 = **0.28**. [1]
**(b)** In: 170 + 330 = 500 W m⁻². [1] Out: 380 + 120 = 500 W m⁻², so the surface is in balance. [1]
**(c)** Atmosphere in: 75 + 350 + 120 = 545 W m⁻². [1] Out: 330 + X, so X = **215 W m⁻²**. [1]
**(d)** To space: 30 + 215 = 245 W m⁻², equal to the absorbed solar 340 − 95 = 245 W m⁻². [1]
**(e)** T⁴ = 380 / 5.67 × 10⁻⁸, so T = **286 K**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that" balance, write both totals and the comparison; a bare statement that they are equal does not demonstrate it.

**10. (a)** Absorbed = 3000 × 0.80 / 4 = 600 W m⁻². [1] T⁴ = 600 / 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ = 1.06 × 10¹⁰ K⁴. [1] T = **321 K**. [1]
**(b)** Top of atmosphere: σTA⁴ = 600. Surface: 600 + σTA⁴ = σTs⁴, so σTs⁴ = **1200 W m⁻²**. [1] Ts = (1200 / 5.67 × 10⁻⁸)^(1/4) = **381 K**. [1]
**(c)** Atmosphere: 0.60σTs⁴ = 2 × 0.60σTA⁴, so TA⁴ = Ts⁴/2. [1] Top of atmosphere: 0.40σTs⁴ + 0.60σTA⁴ = 600, so 0.70σTs⁴ = 600. [1] Ts⁴ = 1.51 × 10¹⁰, so Ts = **351 K**. [1]
**(d)** Less of the surface infrared is absorbed, so less is returned to the surface and the surface does not need to be as hot. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write one balance equation per layer before substituting.

**11. (a)** Ts⁴ = 238 / [5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × (1 − 0.39)] = 6.88 × 10⁹. [1] Ts = **288 K**. [1]
**(b)** Ts⁴ = 238 / [5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 0.59] = 7.11 × 10⁹, so Ts = **290 K**. [1] Rise = **2.4 K**. [1]
**(c)** Burning fossil fuels raises the concentration of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide. [1] The atmosphere takes in a larger share of the surface infrared and sends more of it down again, so a is larger. [1] The surface warms until the power escaping to space again equals the absorbed solar power. [1]
**(d)** Rate = 0.5 / (2.5 × 10⁵ × 4200) = 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ K s⁻¹. [1] Over one year: **0.015 K**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the difference from unrounded temperatures, not from rounded ones.

**12. (a)** Sun: 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 5800 = **5.00 × 10⁻⁷ m**. [1] Earth: 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 288 = **1.01 × 10⁻⁵ m**. [1]
**(b)** Sun: visible. Earth: infrared. [1]
**(c)** E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) / 15 × 10⁻⁶. [1] = **1.33 × 10⁻²⁰ J**. [1]
**(d)** Sunlight is mostly visible and passes through; surface infrared matches molecular energy level gaps, so it is absorbed and some is returned. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert 15 μm to metres before using E = hc/λ.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using S where S/4 is needed, or leaving out the factor 1 − α.
- Using °C in σT⁴, or forgetting the fourth root.
- Describing greenhouse gases as reflecting or trapping radiation instead of absorbing and re-emitting it.
- Letting the atmosphere radiate only upward.
