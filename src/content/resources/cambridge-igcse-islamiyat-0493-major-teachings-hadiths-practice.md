**How to use this set.** Question 1 of Paper 2 prints four of the twenty set Hadiths in Arabic and
English; you choose two and answer part (a), their teachings about what Muslims believe, worth 4
marks, and part (b), how Muslims can put them into action, worth 4 marks. The Hadiths are numbered as
in the syllabus appendix, and the English below is the appendix translation. In the exam the Arabic is
printed as well.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Hadith 1, "Religion is sincerity": name the **five** to whom the Prophet (pbuh) said sincerity is
owed. **[3]**

**2.** Hadith 5, "Every person's every joint must perform a charity every day": give **four** actions
the Hadith counts as charity. **[2]**

**3.** Hadith 6: describe the three ways a Muslim may respond to an evil action, and state which the
Hadith calls the weakest of faith. **[3]**

**4.** In Hadith 8, why did the Prophet (pbuh) say that if only those killed in battle were martyrs,
"the martyrs of my community will be very few"? **[2]**

**5.** Hadith 12: state whom the Prophet (pbuh) sent to Yaman and explain what his instruction to them
teaches about leadership. **[3]**

**6.** Explain the comparison in Hadith 13 between a student of the Qur'an and the owner of tethered
camels. **[2]**

**7.** Explain the image used in Hadith 16, "The believers are like a single man". **[2]**

## Section B — Question 1-style tasks

For each question, write about **both** Hadiths.

**8.** Hadith 3, "Let him who believes in Allah and the Last Day either speak good or keep silent … be
generous to his neighbour … be generous to his guest", and Hadith 4, the man who asked whether
obligatory prayer, fasting in Ramadan and keeping the lawful and forbidden would bring him to
paradise, and was told "Yes".
**(a)** Describe their teachings about what Muslims believe. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Muslims can put these teachings into action. **[4]**

**9.** Hadith 7, "The believer who strives hard in the way of Allah with his person and his property",
and Hadith 10, "One who manages the affairs of the widow and the poor man is like the one who exerts
himself in the way of Allah, or the one who stands for prayer in the night or fasts in the day".
**(a)** Describe their teachings about what Muslims believe. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Muslims can put these teachings into action. **[4]**

**10.** Hadith 11, "I and the man who brings up an orphan will be in paradise like this", and Hadith
15, "God will not show mercy to him who does not show mercy to others".
**(a)** Describe their teachings about what Muslims believe. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Muslims can put these teachings into action. **[4]**

**11.** Hadith 14, "May God show mercy to a man who is kindly when he sells, when he buys, and when he
demands his money back", and Hadith 17, "Modesty produces nothing but good".
**(a)** Describe their teachings about what Muslims believe. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Muslims can put these teachings into action. **[4]**

**12.** Hadith 18, faith and pride "as much as a grain of mustard seed", and Hadith 1, "Religion is
sincerity".
**(a)** Describe their teachings about what Muslims believe. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Muslims can put these teachings into action. **[4]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Cambridge marks Question 1 by levels, reading the two Hadiths together.
The guide below breaks this into points: in part (a), for each Hadith, the central teaching stated
[1] and developed with a link to belief, the Qur'an or another Hadith [1]; in part (b), for each
Hadith, one practical action explained [2]. Other valid points of the same quality earn the same
credit.

**1.** Allah; His Book; His Messenger; the leaders of the Muslims; their common people. Five correct
[3], three or four [2], one or two [1].

**2.** Any four from: acting justly between two people; helping a man with his mount or lifting his
belongings onto it; a good word; every step taken to prayer; removing a harmful thing from the road.
Four correct [2], two or three [1].

**3.** Change it with the hand, if able [1]; if not, with the tongue, by speaking out [1]; if not,
with the heart, by hating it inwardly, which is the weakest of faith [1].

**4.** His Companions thought only those killed in battle were martyrs [1]; he widened the meaning to
include those who die of natural causes, plague or cholera while in the way of Allah, showing God's
generous reward for all who live and die striving for Him [1].

**5.** Abu Musa and Mu'adh ibn Jabal, each to govern a part of Yaman [1]. He told them to be gentle
and not harsh, and to cause rejoicing and not drive people away [1], which teaches that leaders and
teachers should make religion easy and attractive, winning people by kindness [1].

**6.** Camels that are tied up and cared for stay with their owner, but untied camels wander off [1];
in the same way the Qur'an stays in the memory only if it is recited and revised regularly, and is
lost if neglected [1].

**7.** The community is like one body: if the eye or head is hurt, the whole person feels it [1]; so
when some Muslims suffer, all others should feel their pain and act to help them [1].

**8. (a)** Hadith 3: true belief in Allah and the Last Day shows itself in conduct towards others [1];
controlling speech, and generosity to neighbours and guests, are signs of faith because a believer
knows he will be judged for his words and deeds [1]. Hadith 4: Islam is achievable through its
basic duties [1]; a Muslim who performs the obligatory prayers and fasting and keeps within the
limits of halal and haram is promised paradise, showing God's mercy and that religion is not meant
to be a burden (Sura 2.185, "God intends every facility for you") [1].
**(b)** Hadith 3: avoiding gossip and hurtful comments online and in person; checking on elderly
neighbours, sharing food and welcoming guests warmly [2]. Hadith 4: making the five daily prayers
and the Ramadan fast the first priority, earning and eating only what is lawful, and not making
religion harder for others by adding extra burdens [2].

**9. (a)** Hadith 7: the best believer strives in God's way with both self and wealth [1]; striving
(jihad) includes effort, time and money used for God's cause, not only fighting [1]. Hadith 10:
caring for widows and the poor is equal in reward to striving in God's way or to night prayer and
constant fasting [1]; service to the vulnerable is itself worship, so faith is shown in society as
well as in private devotion [1].
**(b)** Hadith 7: giving time to teach children the Qur'an, volunteering, and spending on good causes
such as hospitals or schools [2]. Hadith 10: helping a widowed neighbour with shopping, paperwork or
bills; supporting charities that feed the poor; making sure zakat reaches those in need [2].

**10. (a)** Hadith 11: caring for an orphan earns closeness to the Prophet (pbuh) in paradise, shown by
his two fingers held together [1]; the Prophet (pbuh) was himself an orphan, and the Qur'an commands
kindness to orphans (Sura 93.9) [1]. Hadith 15: receiving God's mercy depends on showing mercy [1];
mercy to people and animals reflects God's own attribute, al-Rahman, and a hard heart cuts a person
off from His mercy [1].
**(b)** Hadith 11: sponsoring an orphan's food and schooling, fostering or adopting care within Islamic
guidance, and treating orphans in the family with the same kindness as one's own children [2].
Hadith 15: forgiving people who wrong us, being gentle with children and the elderly, and treating
animals well, for example not overloading or starving them [2].

**11. (a)** Hadith 14: God's mercy is on those who are gentle and fair in business [1]; honest,
easy-going dealing in buying, selling and asking for repayment of debt is part of faith, not separate
from it [1]. Hadith 17: modesty (haya) leads only to good [1]; it is a quality of faith that restrains
a person from shameful speech and actions and earns respect [1].
**(b)** Hadith 14: giving fair weight and prices, not overcharging, and giving debtors more time when
they struggle to repay (Sura 2.280) [2]. Hadith 17: dressing and speaking modestly, avoiding showing off
on social media, and being humble about one's achievements [2].

**12. (a)** Hadith 18: even a little faith saves a person from hell, while even a little pride keeps a
person out of paradise [1]; pride means looking down on others and rejecting the truth, the sin of
Iblis (Sura 2.34) [1]. Hadith 1: religion is sincerity in every relationship [1]; it means pure
intention towards God, following His Book and His Messenger, honest advice to leaders and wishing
good for ordinary people [1].
**(b)** Hadith 18: accepting correction, not boasting about wealth or family, and treating people of
every background with respect [2]. Hadith 1: praying for God's sake and not to impress others, reading
and acting on the Qur'an, giving honest advice to those in authority, and dealing honestly with
customers and friends [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- In part (a), paraphrasing the Hadith instead of saying what it teaches Muslims to believe.
- In part (b), giving actions so general that they could fit any Hadith.
- Writing about only one of the two Hadiths chosen.
