## What 1.2 asks for

- Assets, liabilities and owner's equity.
- The accounting equation.
- How to apply the accounting equation.

## The equation in all its forms

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Assets | Liabilities + Owner's equity |
| Owner's equity | Assets - Liabilities (= net assets) |
| Liabilities | Assets - Owner's equity |
| Closing equity | Opening equity + Capital introduced + Profit - Drawings |
| Profit (or loss) | Closing equity - Opening equity - Capital introduced + Drawings |

A negative answer from the last formula is a loss.

## Classification at a glance

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Premises, machinery, motor vehicles, fixtures and fittings, equipment |
| Current assets | Inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank (in credit), cash |
| Non-current liabilities | Loans repayable after more than one year |
| Current liabilities | Trade payables, other payables, bank overdraft |
| Owner's equity | Capital invested, plus profits, less losses and drawings |

**Rule of thumb:** if the business *owns* it or is *owed* it, it is an asset. If the business *owes* it to an outsider, it is a liability. If the business owes it to the owner, it is owner's equity.

## Effects of common transactions

| Transaction | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner pays capital into the bank | + | | + |
| Buys inventory on credit | + | + | |
| Buys equipment for cash | + and - (no change in total) | | |
| Customer pays what they owe | + and - (no change in total) | | |
| Pays a supplier | - | - | |
| Pays an expense such as rent | - | | - |
| Sells goods above cost on credit | + (by the profit) | | + (by the profit) |
| Owner takes cash or goods | - | | - |
| Receives a long-term loan | + | + | |
| Loan used to pay off an overdraft | | + and - (no change in total) | |

**Quick check:** after any transaction, total assets = total liabilities + owner's equity.

## The sale-at-a-profit line

When goods costing 600 are sold on credit for 1,000:

- inventory falls by 600;
- trade receivables rise by 1,000;
- assets rise overall by 400;
- owner's equity rises by 400, the profit.

The selling price alone never goes into equity: only the profit does.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asset | A resource owned by the business that it expects to benefit from |
| Liability | An amount owed by the business to an outsider |
| Owner's equity | The owner's interest in the business; the amount the business owes the owner |
| Net assets | Total assets less total liabilities; equal to owner's equity |
| Capital introduced | Money or assets the owner puts into the business |
| Drawings | Money or goods taken out by the owner for private use |
| Non-current | Kept or due for more than one year |
| Current | Used up, received or due within one year |
| Business entity | The business is treated as separate from its owner |
| Duality | Every transaction has two equal effects |

## Exam traps

- **Bank overdraft** is a current liability, not a negative asset in the asset list.
- **Goods taken by the owner** are drawings at cost; they reduce inventory and equity.
- A **loan from a relative** of the owner is a liability; only the owner's own money is equity.
- **Equipment bought for cash** does not change total assets: bank falls, equipment rises.
- When finding profit from two equity figures, **subtract capital introduced and add back drawings**; forgetting either is the commonest error.
- The owner's private assets are **not** business assets.

## Self-test

**1.** State the accounting equation.

**2.** A business has assets of 74,000 and liabilities of 21,500. Calculate owner's equity.

**3.** Classify a bank overdraft and a five-year bank loan.

**4.** Which of these does **not** change total assets: paying rent, a customer paying, buying goods on credit?

**5.** State the effect on the equation of the owner paying 4,000 of private money into the business bank account.

**6.** Goods costing 450 are sold for 700 cash. State the change in total assets and in owner's equity.

**7.** The owner takes goods costing 150 for private use. Which two items change?

**8.** Opening equity 30,000; closing equity 33,500; no capital introduced; drawings 8,000. Calculate the profit.

**9.** Opening equity 18,000; closing equity 16,200; capital introduced 2,500; drawings 6,000. Calculate the profit or loss.

**10.** Assets are 52,000 and owner's equity is 39,800. Calculate liabilities.

**11.** Why is owner's equity described as a claim against the business?

**12.** A business uses a long-term loan to pay off its overdraft of 3,000. What happens to total liabilities?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity.

**2.** 74,000 - 21,500 = 52,500.

**3.** Bank overdraft: current liability. Five-year loan: non-current liability.

**4.** A customer paying: bank rises and trade receivables fall by the same amount.

**5.** Assets (bank) rise by 4,000; owner's equity rises by 4,000.

**6.** Assets rise by 250 (cash +700, inventory -450); owner's equity rises by 250.

**7.** Inventory falls by 150 and owner's equity falls by 150 (drawings).

**8.** 33,500 - 30,000 + 8,000 = 11,500 profit.

**9.** 16,200 - 18,000 - 2,500 + 6,000 = -300, a loss of 300.

**10.** 52,000 - 39,800 = 12,200.

**11.** The business is treated as separate from its owner (business entity), so the capital is an amount the business owes the owner.

**12.** No change in total: the loan rises by 3,000 and the overdraft falls by 3,000.
