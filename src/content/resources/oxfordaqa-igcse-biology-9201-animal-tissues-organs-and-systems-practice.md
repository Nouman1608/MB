These questions cover section **3.1.3 Animal tissues, organs and systems** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE
Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All
data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 6 and 7.

Learn the content first in the [Animal tissues, organs and systems study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-animal-tissues-organs-and-systems/)
and the [Organisation revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation-revision-notes/).
For mixed questions on the whole of Topic 1, use the [Organisation practice questions](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation-practice/).

## Section A

**1.** Name the tissue that:

**(a)** can contract to bring about movement. **[1]**
**(b)** can produce substances such as enzymes and hormones. **[1]**
**(c)** covers some parts of the body. **[1]**

**2.** The stomach contains three tissues. Give the job of the muscular tissue in the stomach. **[1]**

**3.**

**(a)** Name the organ that produces bile. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the part of the digestive system where the absorption of soluble food occurs. **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** Name the part of the digestive system where water is absorbed from undigested food. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is produced when this has happened. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the stomach is an organ. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** In a model of the large intestine, 1.20 dm³ of fluid enters in a day and 1.08 dm³ of it is absorbed.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water that leaves with the faeces. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the fluid that is absorbed. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the volumes of digestive juices released by four sources in one day.

| Source | Saliva | Stomach juice | Pancreatic juice | Bile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume (dm³) | 1.0 | 1.8 | 1.3 | 0.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the total volume of juice released. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the total that is stomach juice. **[2]**

**8.** A section of the wall of an organ shows three layers. Layer X is a thin layer of cells on the inside surface. Layer Y is made of long cells that can shorten. Layer Z is made of cells that release a fluid into the inside of the organ.

**(a)** Name the tissue in each of the layers X, Y and Z. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why this structure is an organ and not a tissue. **[1]**

**9.** Cells in the pancreas that make digestive enzymes contain many ribosomes and many mitochondria.

**(a)** Name the type of tissue that makes digestive juices. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why these cells contain many ribosomes. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why these cells contain many mitochondria. **[1]**

**10.** Explain the difference between an organ and an organ system, using the digestive system as an example. **[3]**

**11.** A person has a condition in which the muscular tissue in the wall of the stomach no longer contracts properly. Suggest **two** effects this could have on the stomach. **[2]**

**12.** Describe the parts of the human digestive system and what each part does. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Muscular tissue [1]
**(b)** Glandular tissue [1]
**(c)** Epithelial tissue [1]

**2.** It contracts to allow the contents to move through the digestive system [1]

**3. (a)** The liver [1]
**(b)** The small intestine [1]

**4. (a)** The large intestine [1]
**(b)** Faeces [1]

**5.** An organ is made of tissues [1]; the stomach contains more than one tissue: muscular, glandular and epithelial tissue [1]

**6. (a)** 1.20 − 1.08 = **0.12 dm³** [1]
**(b)** 1.08 ÷ 1.20 × 100 [1] = **90%** [1]

**7. (a)** 1.0 + 1.8 + 1.3 + 0.9 = **5.0 dm³** [1]
**(b)** 1.8 ÷ 5.0 × 100 [1] = **36%** [1]

**8. (a)** X is epithelial tissue [1]; Y is muscular tissue [1]; Z is glandular tissue [1]
**(b)** It is made of more than one tissue (three tissues working together) [1]

**9. (a)** Glandular tissue [1]
**(b)** Ribosomes are where protein synthesis occurs, and enzymes are proteins [1]
**(c)** Making and releasing protein needs energy, and most energy is released in respiration in mitochondria [1]

**10.** An organ is made of several tissues, such as the stomach [1]; an organ system is a group of organs that perform a particular function [1]; the digestive system contains organs such as the stomach, small intestine, liver and pancreas that together digest and absorb food (and exchange substances with the environment) [1]

**11.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: the contents would not be moved (or mixed) properly through the digestive system; food would stay in the stomach longer than normal; food would not be mixed well with the digestive juices; digestion in the stomach would be slower

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- glands such as the salivary glands and pancreas produce digestive juices
- the stomach is where digestion occurs
- the small intestine is where digestion occurs
- the small intestine is where the absorption of soluble food occurs
- the liver produces bile
- the large intestine absorbs water from undigested food, producing faeces

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "muscular tissue moves" without saying it contracts.
- Naming only one or two tissues for the stomach.
- Saying the pancreas or the stomach is a tissue.
- Placing absorption of soluble food in the large intestine, or digestion in the large intestine.
- Saying the liver makes digestive enzymes instead of bile.
- Failing to give the unit (dm³) or the percentage sign in calculations.
