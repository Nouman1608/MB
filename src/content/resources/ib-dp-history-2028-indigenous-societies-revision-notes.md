**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. Every question on this study asks about **one Indigenous society**, so pick one (the notes use the Aztec Triple Alliance and the Inca) and learn it well. The fuller [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies/) gives the detail. Practise with the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies-practice/).

## What Paper 3 wants

Paper 3 has 30 marks in total: two essays, each from a different regional study, each worth 15 marks. The skill is **evaluating arguments**. You must show you understand the argument in the question, weigh different perspectives on it, and reach a supported judgement. Historiography is helpful but is not required for the top markband.

## Key terms

- **Tlatoani**: Aztec ruler, "speaker". Chosen from a royal lineage.
- **Triple Alliance**: Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan, formed in 1428.
- **Calpulli**: Aztec kin-based neighbourhood group holding land.
- **Chinampa**: raised field built in shallow lake water.
- **Pochteca**: Aztec long-distance merchants.
- **Sapa Inca**: Inca ruler, linked to the sun god Inti.
- **Tawantinsuyu**: the Inca state, "land of four parts".
- **Mit'a**: Inca labour service owed to the state.
- **Ayllu**: Inca kin group working shared land.
- **Mitmaq**: Inca resettlement of populations.
- **Quipu**: knotted cords used for records.
- **Kuraka**: local Inca lord.
- **Haudenosaunee**: Iroquois Confederacy of five nations under the Great Law of Peace.

## Political authority

- Aztec: hegemonic empire. Conquered cities kept their rulers but paid tribute and sent troops.
- Inca: more centralised. Four quarters, governors, kurakas, roads, tambos, chaskis, quipu.
- Legitimacy: religion and warfare. Aztec sacrifice to feed the sun and gods. Inca ruler as child of the sun, and royal mummies.
- Law: Aztec courts and harsh penalties. Inca state officials and duties of labour.
- Alliances: the Triple Alliance shared tribute. The Haudenosaunee Confederacy shared power by consensus.

## Economic structures

- Aztec agriculture: chinampas, maize, beans, squash. Inca agriculture: terraces, potatoes, maize, llamas.
- Landholding: calpulli (Aztec) and ayllu (Inca), plus noble, temple and state land.
- Tribute: goods for the Aztec (cloth, cacao, feathers); labour for the Inca (mit'a).
- Exchange: Aztec markets (for example Tlatelolco) and pochteca; the Inca relied more on state redistribution and storehouses.
- Limits to sedentary life: mixed and mobile peoples lived at the edges, and neither empire used draught animals.

## Social organisation

- Aztec ranks: nobles, commoners, dependent workers, enslaved people.
- Inca ranks: Sapa Inca and royal kin, provincial lords, ayllu households.
- Women: weaving and household labour supplied tribute. Aztec women could be priestesses, midwives and traders. Haudenosaunee descent was matrilineal and clan mothers named leaders.
- Identity: state languages (Nahuatl, Quechua) spread, but local gods and customs often continued.

## Warfare

- Purposes: tribute, land, captives, prestige, labour.
- Aztec: rank through captives; flower wars with Tlaxcala.
- Inca: conquest paired with integration, roads and resettlement.
- Consequence: subject peoples resented rule. This is vital for the next study, when many allied with Europeans.

## Culture

- Language: Nahuatl, Quechua.
- Religion: Huitzilopochtli and the Templo Mayor; Inti, Viracocha, Pachamama, huacas.
- Arts: featherwork, codices, poetry; textiles, masonry, metalwork.
- Natural world: sacred mountains, water and crops. Three Sisters for the Haudenosaunee.

## Challenges

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Other Indigenous societies | Tlaxcala and the Tarascans against the Aztec; distant frontiers for the Inca |
| Environment | Drought and famine in the Valley of Mexico (1450s); Maya drought |
| Social | Possible overpopulation, debated |
| Political | Succession disputes (Inca civil war, about 1527); tributary revolts |

## Arguments and perspectives

- Sacrifice: religion, political terror, or both? The protein-shortage theory is widely rejected.
- Aztec state: hegemonic, tribute-based empire (Hassig) versus a more directly controlled one.
- Inca economy: reciprocity and redistribution (Murra) versus the older "socialist empire" idea (Baudin) versus compulsion.
- Maya decline: drought, warfare, overpopulation, or a mix. Strong answers rank and link them.
- Sources: Spanish chroniclers (Sahagún, Cieza de León, Garcilaso de la Vega) wrote after conquest, for Spanish readers. Guaman Poma wrote as an Andean author under Spanish rule. Combine written sources with archaeology and codices.

## Essay skeleton

1. Define the argument in the question in one sentence.
2. State a line: "to a large extent, but..." or similar.
3. Give two or three factors. Rank them and use specific evidence.
4. Include a counter-argument and a perspective (a named historian or a type of source).
5. End with a judgement that matches your analysis.

## Common mistakes

- Using two societies without depth in either.
- Discussing the Spanish conquest rather than the society before it.
- Describing without evaluating.
- Writing "Indigenous people" as one group.

## Self-test

1. Which three cities formed the Aztec Triple Alliance, and when?
2. Name two ways the Inca state kept control of a large empire.
3. What was the difference between Aztec and Inca tribute?
4. Give one reason why historians reject the "socialist empire" label for the Inca.
5. Give two possible causes of Maya decline.
6. Why were some Indigenous peoples hostile to the Aztec and Inca?

**Answers:** 1. Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan, in 1428. 2. Any two: roads and runners, resettlement (mitmaq), kurakas tied to the state, quipu records, governors of the four quarters. 3. Aztec tribute was mostly goods; Inca tribute was mostly labour (mit'a). 4. The state was strictly hierarchical and used compulsion, so the label hides inequality and coercion. 5. Any two: drought, warfare, overpopulation, political failure. 6. They had been conquered, taxed and forced to supply troops.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use the Maya?**
Yes, if you know the society in depth. The notes use the Aztec and Inca because their sources and debates are rich.

**Does the exam ask about the Spanish conquest?**
Not in this study. The date range ends in 1500.

**What makes an answer evaluative?**
It weighs different views on the argument and shows why one is more convincing.
