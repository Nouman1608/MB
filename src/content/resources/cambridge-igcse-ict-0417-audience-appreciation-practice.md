## Section A: short answers

**1.** State what is meant by the *purpose* of an ICT solution. **[1]**

**2.** Give **two** methods a designer could use to find out the needs of an audience. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** ways of making an online video accessible to people with hearing impairments. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why alternative text should be added to images on a web page. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** features of a document whose purpose is to give instructions. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Ridgeway Council is producing information about recycling collections. The audience is all households in the area, including many older residents and families whose first language is not English.

**(a)** Explain why the council should analyse the needs of the audience before creating the information. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **three** features that would make the information responsive to the needs of this audience. **[3]**

**7.** A sports club creates two pieces of work for its junior members aged 11 to 14: a poster to persuade them to join a new swimming squad, and a guide to booking sessions on the club's app.

Describe how the two pieces of work should differ because of their different purposes. **[4]**

**8.** A museum is designing an interactive touch-screen display for visitors who use wheelchairs as well as other visitors.

Explain **two** ways the display can be designed to be respectful of the needs of wheelchair users. **[4]**

**9.** A charity's web page uses red text to show urgent appeals and green text for appeals that have been completed.

**(a)** Explain why this design may not meet the needs of all users. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest an improvement. **[1]**

**10.** A software company is writing a help guide for its accounts program. One version is for experienced accountants and another is for small shop owners with no accounting training.

Describe **three** differences between the two guides, giving a reason for each. **[6]**

**11.** A school is making a presentation about its exchange visit to show to visiting families from several different countries.

Explain **two** ways the presentation can be made respectful of the cultural backgrounds of the audience. **[4]**

**12.** A hospital is creating a web page for teenagers about to have an operation. Discuss how the designer should analyse the needs of this audience and plan a solution that is responsive to them. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** What the solution is intended to achieve, such as to inform, instruct, persuade or entertain [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two of: questionnaires [1]; interviews [1]; observing members of the audience [1]; studying existing materials they use [1]; testing a draft with a sample of the audience [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**3.** Any two of: subtitles or captions [1]; a written transcript [1]; sign language interpretation [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**4.** Screen readers read the alternative text aloud to users who cannot see the image [1], so visually impaired users still understand what the image shows / the information it gives [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: numbered steps in the correct order [1]; one action per step [1]; screenshots or diagrams [1]; plain, direct language [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**6. (a)** The audience includes people with different needs, such as older residents and people with limited English [1]; analysis shows what they need, so the information is understood by everyone and collections are not missed [1].

**(b)** Any three features, each linked to a need: large, clear font for older residents with weaker eyesight [1]; simple, short sentences without jargon so that people with limited English understand [1]; pictures and symbols of what goes in each bin, which work without words [1]; translations into the main community languages [1]; printed leaflets as well as a web page, for residents without internet access [1]; high contrast between text and background [1]. Maximum 3. **[5]**

**7.** Any four, with points about both pieces of work: the poster should have a strong headline to attract attention, whereas the guide should have a clear title stating the task [1]; the poster should use eye-catching images of swimmers, whereas the guide should use screenshots of the app [1]; the poster should give benefits and a call to action such as "sign up by Friday", whereas the guide should give numbered steps [1]; the poster should use lively, persuasive language, whereas the guide should use short, direct instructions [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**8.** Any two, each explained [2 each]: place the screen at a height reachable from a seated position [1], so wheelchair users can see and touch it without help [1]; put the main buttons in the lower part of the screen [1], so they are within reach [1]; allow the screen to be tilted or provide a second, lower screen [1], so everyone can use it independently [1]; leave clear floor space in front of it [1], so a wheelchair can get close [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Some users cannot distinguish between red and green [1], so they cannot tell which appeals are urgent and which are completed [1].

**(b)** Add a text label or symbol such as "Urgent" or a tick as well as the colour [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Three differences, each with a reason [2 each], for example:
- The accountants' guide can use accounting terms without explanation, because they already know them [1]; the shop owners' guide should use plain language or a glossary, because they have no training [1].
- The shop owners' guide should give step-by-step instructions with screenshots for each task [1], because they need to learn the basic tasks; the accountants' guide can be a briefer reference [1].
- The accountants' guide can include advanced features such as detailed reports [1], because they will use them; the shop owners' guide should focus on the everyday tasks they need, such as recording sales [1].
Maximum 6. **[6]**

**11.** Any two, each explained [2 each]: choose images and symbols carefully [1], avoiding gestures or symbols that may be offensive in some cultures [1]; use clear, simple language without slang or idioms [1], so that families with English as a second language understand and nobody feels excluded [1]; avoid humour or examples based on one culture [1], which other families may not understand or may find disrespectful [1]; include greetings or key words in the visitors' languages [1], which shows respect for them [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**12.** Up to 6 marks, with points on both analysis and the solution, for example:
Analysis:
- Use questionnaires or interviews with a sample of teenage patients to find out what they want to know and what worries them [1].
- Find out how they will view the page, probably on phones [1].
- Test a draft with a few teenagers and change it from their feedback [1].
Responsive solution:
- A friendly, informal but accurate tone that does not talk down to them [1].
- Short sections answering common questions, such as what happens on the day [1].
- A mobile-friendly layout and possibly short videos with captions [1].
- Respectful content: reassuring rather than frightening images, and information about privacy [1].
Maximum 6; at least one analysis point and one solution point are needed for full marks. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between responsive and respectful?**
Responsive means the solution meets the needs you found. Respectful means it does not exclude, embarrass or offend anyone in the audience, which includes accessibility and cultural awareness.

**Do I always need to mention the purpose?**
When you justify a feature, link it to the audience and, where it helps, to the purpose. A feature that suits the audience but not the purpose is a weaker answer.
