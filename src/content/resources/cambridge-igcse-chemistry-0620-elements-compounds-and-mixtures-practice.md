## Section A

**1.** Define the term *mixture*. **[2]**

**2.** Which of the following is a compound?

A: Ozone, O₃

B: Brass

C: Ammonia, NH₃

D: Air

**[1]**

**3.** Classify each substance as an element, a compound or a mixture: **(a)** helium, **(b)** glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, **(c)** salt solution, **(d)** magnesium oxide. **[4]**

**4.** State whether each formula represents an element or a compound: **(a)** S₈, **(b)** SO₂, **(c)** CO. **[3]**

**5.** Salt can be separated from a mixture of salt and sand by adding water, filtering and evaporating. Explain why these methods cannot separate salt (sodium chloride) into sodium and chlorine. **[2]**

**6.** A box in a particle diagram contains only identical particles. Each particle is made of one atom of one element joined to one atom of a different element. Is the substance an element, a compound or a mixture? **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Four boxes of particles are described below. Identify each as an element, a compound, a mixture of elements, or a mixture of an element and a compound.

**(a)** Box P: identical single atoms, all of the same kind. **[1]**
**(b)** Box Q: some pairs of identical joined atoms, and some single atoms of a different kind. **[1]**
**(c)** Box R: identical particles, each made of three atoms of two different kinds joined together. **[1]**
**(d)** Box S: some particles like those in Box R, and some pairs of identical joined atoms. **[1]**

**8.** Describe **three** differences between a mixture of hydrogen gas and oxygen gas and the compound water. **[3]**

**9.** A student heats some pink-brown copper powder strongly in air. It slowly turns black, and the mass of the solid increases.

**(a)** Name the black substance formed. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence that a compound has formed rather than a mixture. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the mass of the solid increases. **[1]**

**10.** Give **two** reasons why air is classified as a mixture, not a compound. **[2]**

**11.** A student says: "Brass is a compound because it contains two different metals." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**12.** Sample A melts sharply at 63 °C. Sample B melts gradually between 55 °C and 60 °C.

**(a)** Which sample is a mixture? **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Two or more substances (elements or compounds) together [1] that are not chemically combined [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3. (a)** Element [1]. **(b)** Compound [1]. **(c)** Mixture [1]. **(d)** Compound [1].

**4. (a)** Element: only sulfur atoms [1].

**(b)** Compound: sulfur and oxygen [1].

**(c)** Compound: carbon and oxygen [1]. **[3]**

**5.** In salt, sodium and chlorine are chemically combined (bonded) [1]. Physical methods such as dissolving, filtering and evaporating do not break chemical bonds, so a chemical reaction would be needed to separate them [1].

**6.** Compound [1].

**7. (a)** Element [1].

**(b)** Mixture of elements [1].

**(c)** Compound [1].

**(d)** Mixture of an element and a compound [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: in the mixture the gases are not chemically combined, but in water hydrogen and oxygen are bonded; the mixture's proportions can vary, but water always has a fixed ratio of two hydrogen atoms to one oxygen atom; the mixture keeps the properties of its gases, for example it is flammable, while water has new properties and does not burn; the mixture could be separated by physical methods, but water can only be split by a chemical reaction.

First difference [1]; second difference [1]; third difference [1].

**9. (a)** Copper(II) oxide [1].

**(b)** Any two, 1 mark each: a colour change shows a new substance with different properties; energy (strong heating) was needed for a chemical reaction; the mass increased because another element combined with the copper.

First piece of evidence [1]; second piece of evidence [1].

**(c)** Oxygen from the air has chemically combined with the copper, adding the mass of the oxygen atoms [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Any two, 1 mark each: the gases in air are not chemically combined; the composition of air can vary, for example in the amount of water vapour; each gas keeps its own properties, for example oxygen still supports burning; the gases can be separated by a physical method.

First reason [1]; second reason [1].

**11.** Brass is an alloy, a mixture of copper and zinc [1]. The metals are not chemically combined, and the proportions of copper and zinc can vary, so it is not a compound [1].

**12. (a)** Sample B [1].

**(b)** A mixture melts over a range of temperatures, while a pure substance has a sharp melting point [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "one atom" rather than "one type of atom" for an element.
- Classifying O₂, N₂ or S₈ as compounds because they contain several atoms.
- Calling alloys or air compounds.
- Forgetting that a mixture can contain compounds.
- Missing "not chemically combined" from the definition of a mixture.
