AQA A-Level Law (7162) introduces how law is made, how the English
legal system works and how legal rules are applied to real problems.
It suits students who like structured argument, careful reading and
weighing up competing points of view. The qualification is assessed by three written papers, each
lasting 2 hours and worth 100 marks, for a total of 300 marks.

## How the course is organised

The specification has five content areas: a shared core, two
compulsory areas of substantive law, and a pair of options of which you
study one.

- **The nature of law and the English legal system.** This is the
  common thread of the course. It covers the nature of law and its
  relationship with society, morality and justice; the rule of law;
  law making through Parliament, delegated legislation, statutory
  interpretation, judicial precedent, law reform and the European
  Union; and the legal system itself, from the civil and criminal
  courts and lay people to legal personnel, the judiciary, and access
  to justice and funding.
- **Criminal law.** The rules and theory of criminal law, the general
  elements of liability, fatal and non-fatal offences against the
  person, property offences, a preliminary offence, and capacity and
  necessity defences.
- **Tort.** The rules and theory of tort law, liability in negligence
  for physical injury and damage to property and for economic loss and
  psychiatric injury, occupiers' liability, nuisance and the escape of
  dangerous things, vicarious liability, defences and remedies.
- **Law of contract (option).** The rules and theory of contract law,
  the essential requirements of a contract, contract terms (including
  terms implied by statute in consumer contracts and exclusion
  clauses), vitiating factors, discharge and remedies.
- **Human Rights (option).** Rules and theory, human rights in
  international law, the UK position before and after the Human Rights
  Act 1998, the European Convention on Human Rights 1953 with a focus on
  Articles 2, 5, 8, 10 and 11, restrictions, enforcement, the
  relationship with English law, and reform.

Law of contract and Human Rights are alternatives: you study one of
them, depending on what your centre offers.

## How it's assessed

The course is linear and not tiered, so all three papers are sat at the
end of the course. Each paper mixes multiple-choice, short-answer and
extended-writing questions.

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7162/1 | The nature of law and the English legal system (25 marks) and Criminal law (75 marks) | 2 hours | 100 | 33% |
| 7162/2 | The nature of law and the English legal system (25 marks) and Tort (75 marks) | 2 hours | 100 | 33% |
| 7162/3 | The nature of law and the English legal system (25 marks) and either Law of contract or Human rights (75 marks) | 2 hours | 100 | 34% |

AQA presents the three papers as equal thirds of the A-level; the
third is shown here as 34% only so that the weightings add up to
exactly 100. The specification was first taught in September 2017 and
first assessed in June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The most useful thing to understand about 7162 is that every paper has
the same two-part shape: 25 marks on the nature of law and the English
legal system, and 75 marks on a substantive area of law. That means the
legal system core is examined three times, once alongside each of
criminal law, tort and your option. It is never "done" after the first
year; it is the frame that the substantive areas sit inside.

The links run in both directions. Statutory interpretation and judicial
precedent explain how the offences, torts and contract rules you learn
were developed by Parliament and the courts. Ideas about law and
morality, law and justice, and the rule of law give you the material to
evaluate those rules, for example when judging whether a defence or a
remedy produces a fair outcome. Each substantive area also contains its
own "rules" and "theory" sections, so the habit of evaluating law, not
only describing it, is built into every part of the course.

Two skills cut across all three papers. The first is applying legal
rules to a scenario: identifying the relevant law, using authority such
as cases and statutes, and reaching a reasoned conclusion. The second is
evaluation: weighing the strengths and weaknesses of a rule or an
institution and suggesting reform where it is justified.

## Planning your study

Because the papers share a core, a sensible order teaches some legal
system content first and then returns to it repeatedly.

1. **Start with foundations.** Begin with the nature of law, the rule of
   law and how law is made, with particular care over statutory
   interpretation and judicial precedent, since every later topic
   relies on them.
2. **Move into criminal law.** The general elements of liability give a
   clear method for analysing problem scenarios, which then carries
   over to offences against the person, property offences and
   defences.
3. **Add tort.** Negligence builds naturally on the idea of liability
   from criminal law, and the later tort topics extend it to occupiers,
   neighbours and employers.
4. **Study your option.** Contract or Human Rights usually comes later in
   the course, alongside the remaining legal system topics such as the
   courts, legal personnel and access to justice.
5. **Revisit the core throughout.** Keep returning to the legal system
   and the nature of law, linking each to the substantive area you are
   currently studying.

Start answering short-answer and multiple-choice questions on each
topic soon after you learn it, and build up to longer application and
evaluation answers. Full timed papers and the exam preparation page
belong in the final months, once all four of your areas have been covered.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in AQA A-Level Law?**
There are three written papers, each lasting 2 hours and worth 100 marks, giving 300 marks in total. Each paper mixes multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions.

**Do I study both contract law and human rights?**
No. Paper 3 offers either Law of contract or Human rights, and you answer on the one your centre teaches. Both options are worth 75 marks on that paper.

**Why does the English legal system appear on every paper?**
Each paper gives 25 marks to the nature of law and the English legal system and 75 marks to a substantive area. The core is examined three times, so it needs to be revised alongside each area rather than once.

**Is AQA A-Level Law tiered or modular?**
Neither. It is a linear, untiered qualification, so everyone sits the same three papers at the end of the course.

**When was AQA A-Level Law 7162 first examined?**
It was first taught from September 2017 and first assessed in June 2019.
