These questions cover **4.3.1 Changes of state and the particle model** (4.3.1.1 and 4.3.1.2) of AQA GCSE
Physics (8463), assessed on Paper 1 at both tiers. Questions 8 to 10 are based on required practical 5.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write the equation that links density, mass and volume. **[1]**

**2.** A block of wood has a mass of 3.0 kg and a volume of 0.0050 m³. Calculate its density. **[2]**

**3.** Convert a density of 0.70 g/cm³ into kg/m³. **[1]**

**4.** Name the change of state in each case.

**(a)** A puddle dries up on a warm day. **[1]**
**(b)** Solid carbon dioxide turns straight into a gas. **[1]**
**(c)** Water vapour forms droplets on a cold window. **[1]**

**5.** A sealed flask contains 120 g of ice. The ice melts completely. State the mass of the water and explain your answer. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why changes of state are described as physical changes. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Steam is much less dense than liquid water.

**(a)** Explain this difference using the particle model. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how you would draw particle diagrams of liquid water and steam to show this difference. **[2]**

**8.** A student finds the density of a metal cylinder. Its diameter is 2.00 cm and its height is 5.00 cm. Its mass is 140 g.

**(a)** Name a suitable instrument for measuring the diameter. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of the cylinder in cm³. Use volume = π × radius² × height. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the density in kg/m³. **[3]**

**9.** A student finds the density of a small irregular stone using a displacement can.

**(a)** Describe the method. **[4]**
**(b)** The stone has a mass of 54 g and displaces 20 cm³ of water. Calculate its density in kg/m³. **[2]**
**(c)** The student drops the stone into the can and some water splashes out over the side. Explain the effect on her value for density. **[2]**

**10.** A student measures the density of cooking oil.

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Mass of empty measuring cylinder | 95.0 g |
| Mass of cylinder + oil | 141.0 g |
| Volume of oil | 50.0 cm³ |

**(a)** Calculate the density of the oil in kg/m³. **[3]**
**(b)** State how the student should read the volume of oil accurately. **[1]**

**11.** 1.8 kg of water (density 1000 kg/m³) is frozen. The ice has a density of 900 kg/m³.

**(a)** State the mass of the ice. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of the water and of the ice. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a sealed glass bottle full of water can crack when the water freezes. **[2]**

**12.** Compare a physical change, such as melting chocolate, with a chemical change, such as burning paper. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m / V) [1]

**2.** ρ = 3.0 ÷ 0.0050 [1]; ρ = **600 kg/m³** [1]

**3.** **700 kg/m³** [1]

**4. (a)** Evaporating [1]
**(b)** Sublimating [1]
**(c)** Condensing [1]

**5.** **120 g** [1]; mass is conserved in a change of state: the number of particles is the same and none can escape from the sealed flask [1]

**6.** The material recovers its original properties if the change is reversed [1]

**7. (a)** In steam the particles are much further apart than in liquid water [1]; so the same mass takes up a much larger volume, giving a much lower density [1]
**(b)** Liquid: particles of equal size touching, arranged irregularly [1]; steam: the same size particles, few in number and spread far apart, not touching [1]

**8. (a)** Vernier callipers (accept micrometer) [1]
**(b)** radius = 1.00 cm; V = π × 1.00² × 5.00 [1]; V = **15.7 cm³** [1]
**(c)** ρ = 140 ÷ 15.7 [1] = 8.9 g/cm³ [1]; ρ = **8900 kg/m³** to 2 s.f. (accept 8910 to 8920 kg/m³) [1]

**9. (a)** Measure the mass of the stone on a balance [1]; fill the displacement can with water until it overflows from the spout, and wait until it stops dripping [1]; place a measuring cylinder under the spout and lower the stone gently into the can on a thread [1]; read the volume of water collected, which equals the volume of the stone, then calculate ρ = m / V [1]
**(b)** ρ = 54 ÷ 20 = 2.7 g/cm³ [1]; ρ = **2700 kg/m³** [1]
**(c)** Less water is collected in the cylinder, so the measured volume is too small [1]; dividing the mass by a smaller volume gives a density that is too large [1]

**10. (a)** mass of oil = 141.0 − 95.0 = 46.0 g [1]; ρ = 46.0 ÷ 50.0 = 0.920 g/cm³ [1]; ρ = **920 kg/m³** [1]
**(b)** Read the bottom of the meniscus with the eye level with the liquid surface [1]

**11. (a)** **1.8 kg** [1]
**(b)** Water: V = 1.8 ÷ 1000 = 0.0018 m³ [1]; ice: V = 1.8 ÷ 900 = 0.0020 m³ [1]
**(c)** The mass stays the same but the ice has a larger volume than the water [1]; the expanding ice pushes on the glass, which cannot stretch, so it cracks [1]

**12.** Melting is reversible: cooled chocolate becomes solid chocolate again with its original properties [1]; burning produces new substances, and the paper cannot be recovered by reversing the conditions [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by 1000 instead of multiplying when converting g/cm³ to kg/m³.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in a cylinder volume.
- Not waiting for the displacement can to stop dripping before adding the object.
- Describing particle diagrams with different-sized particles.
- Saying mass changes during a change of state.
