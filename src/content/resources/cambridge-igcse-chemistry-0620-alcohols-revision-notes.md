Condensed recall for **11.6 Alcohols**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). The full explanation and worked examples are in the [alcohols study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alcohols/); exam-style questions are in the [alcohols practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alcohols-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcomes 1–3 (two manufacturing methods, combustion, uses) are Core. Outcome 4 (advantages and disadvantages of each method) is Supplement, marked *(Extended)*. Naming alcohols other than ethanol (11.2) is also Extended.

## Core facts

- Functional group: **–OH**. General formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH**.
- Ethanol: **C₂H₅OH**, structural formula **CH₃CH₂OH**.
- Alcohols are **not** hydrocarbons (they contain oxygen).
- Uses of ethanol: **solvent** and **fuel**.

## Two ways to make ethanol

| | Fermentation | Catalytic addition of steam to ethene |
|---|---|---|
| Reactant(s) | **aqueous glucose** | **ethene + steam** |
| Temperature | **25–35 °C** | **300 °C** |
| Pressure | normal | **6000 kPa / 60 atm** |
| Catalyst | **yeast** (its enzymes) | **acid** |
| Other condition | **absence of oxygen** | — |
| Equation | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |
| By-product | carbon dioxide | none: addition gives one product |

### Why the fermentation conditions?

- **Below 25 °C**: enzymes work slowly.
- **Above 35 °C**: enzymes are denatured.
- **Oxygen present**: yeast respires aerobically, giving CO₂ and water, not ethanol.

After fermentation: filter off the yeast, then concentrate the ethanol by **fractional distillation**.

## Combustion

```
C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O
```

Balancing tip for any alcohol: balance C, then H (include the H in –OH), then O, remembering **one O is already in the alcohol**.

| Alcohol | Complete combustion |
|---|---|
| methanol | 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| ethanol | C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O |
| propan-1-ol | 2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O |
| butan-1-ol | C₄H₉OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O |

## Naming *(Extended)*

| Name | Structural formula |
|---|---|
| propan-1-ol | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| propan-2-ol | CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ |
| butan-1-ol | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH |
| butan-2-ol | CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃ |

The number = the carbon carrying –OH.

## Advantages and disadvantages *(Extended)*

- **Fermentation – for:** renewable raw material (crops); low temperature and normal pressure, so low energy use.
- **Fermentation – against:** slow; dilute, impure product that must be distilled; not continuous; needs land for crops.
- **Steam + ethene – for:** fast; pure product; continuous process.
- **Steam + ethene – against:** ethene comes from non-renewable petroleum; high temperature and pressure need a lot of energy.

## Ethanol in the reaction network

```
petroleum --cracking--> ethene --steam, acid catalyst--> ethanol
glucose --yeast, no oxygen--> ethanol
ethanol --oxidation (Extended)--> ethanoic acid
ethanol + ethanoic acid --acid catalyst (Extended)--> ethyl ethanoate + water
```

Questions often link two steps, for example asking where the ethene came from, or what ethanol can be turned into.

## Oxygen: keep it out or let it in?

| Process | Oxygen | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Fermentation | **excluded** | ethanol |
| Combustion | **plentiful** | carbon dioxide + water |
| Vinegar making *(Extended, 11.7)* | **needed** (air) | ethanoic acid |

## Calculation pattern *(Extended)*

Fermentation equation ratio: **1 mol glucose → 2 mol ethanol**. Mᵣ glucose = 180; Mᵣ ethanol = 46.

So 180 g glucose → 92 g ethanol at most; any mass scales in the same ratio (for example, 18 g glucose → 9.2 g ethanol).

## Answer frames

- **Describe fermentation:** "Aqueous glucose is mixed with yeast at 25–35 °C with oxygen excluded; ethanol and carbon dioxide form."
- **Describe the ethene method:** "Ethene reacts with steam at 300 °C and 6000 kPa using an acid catalyst; ethanol is the only product."
- **Explain a fermentation condition:** name the condition, then link it to the yeast enzymes or to aerobic respiration.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fermentation | conversion of glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide by yeast, without oxygen |
| Anaerobic | without oxygen |
| Hydration (addition of steam) | adding H₂O across C=C to form an alcohol |
| Renewable | can be replaced as it is used, for example by growing more crops |
| Continuous process | reactants are fed in and product removed without stopping |

## Exam traps

- Give **25–35 °C**, not "warm" or "room temperature".
- Give **300 °C and 6000 kPa**, not "high temperature and pressure", when the question asks for the conditions.
- Do not swap catalysts: **yeast** for fermentation, **acid** for ethene.
- Fermentation gives **two** products (ethanol and CO₂); the ethene method gives **one**.
- In combustion equations, ethanol needs **3O₂**, not 3½O₂.
- Advantages must be **specific**: "renewable" or "faster", not "cheaper" or "better". *(Extended)*

## Self-test

1. Give the general formula of the alcohols.
2. State the conditions for fermentation.
3. Write the equation for fermentation of glucose.
4. Explain why fermentation is not carried out at 60 °C.
5. Explain why air is kept out of the fermentation vessel.
6. State the conditions for making ethanol from ethene.
7. Write the equation for the complete combustion of ethanol.
8. State two uses of ethanol.
9. Name the method used to concentrate ethanol after fermentation.
10. *(Extended)* Give two advantages of making ethanol from ethene.
11. *(Extended)* Give one disadvantage of the ethene method linked to its raw material.
12. *(Extended)* Name the alcohol CH₃CH(OH)CH₃.

**Answers:**

1. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH.
2. Aqueous glucose, yeast, 25–35 °C, absence of oxygen.
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.
4. The enzymes in the yeast would be denatured.
5. With oxygen the yeast respires aerobically, producing carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol.
6. Steam, 300 °C, 6000 kPa (60 atm), acid catalyst.
7. C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
8. Solvent; fuel.
9. Fractional distillation.
10. Any two: fast; pure product; continuous.
11. Ethene comes from petroleum, which is non-renewable.
12. Propan-2-ol.
