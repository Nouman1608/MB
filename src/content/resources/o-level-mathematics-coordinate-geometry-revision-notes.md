## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cartesian coordinates | A point written (x, y): x across from the origin, y up |
| Gradient | How much y changes for each 1 unit increase in x |
| y-intercept | Where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0) |
| x-intercept | Where the line crosses the x-axis (y = 0) |
| Line segment | The part of a line between two points |
| Midpoint | The point halfway along a line segment |
| Parallel | Same gradient, never meet |
| Perpendicular | Meet at 90°; gradients multiply to give −1 |
| Perpendicular bisector | Cuts a segment in half at 90° |

## Formulas

| Result | Rule |
|---|---|
| Gradient from two points | m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) |
| Length of a segment | √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) |
| Midpoint | ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2) |
| Gradient-intercept form | y = mx + c |
| Line through (x₁, y₁) with gradient m | y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) |
| Parallel lines | m₁ = m₂ |
| Perpendicular lines | m₁ × m₂ = −1, so the perpendicular gradient is −1/m |
| Horizontal line | y = k, gradient 0 |
| Vertical line | x = k, gradient undefined |

## Section by section

### 3.1 Coordinates

- x first, then y. Positive x is right, positive y is up.
- On the x-axis y = 0. On the y-axis x = 0.
- Sketch any problem that gives several points. Shapes such as rectangles and parallelograms then become easy to read.

### 3.2 Drawing linear graphs

Two ways to draw a line:

1. **Table of values** for y = mx + c. Choose three x-values, work out y, plot and join.
2. **Intercepts** for ax + by = c. Set x = 0 to get the y-intercept, set y = 0 to get the x-intercept, then join.

Always extend the line across the whole grid and check with a third point. Use a table when the line passes through the origin.

Reminder: y = 6 − 3x gives the points (0, 6), (1, 3) and (2, 0). It falls as x rises because the coefficient of x is negative.

### 3.3 Gradient

Method in steps:

1. Write the two points: (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).
2. Subtract y-values on top and x-values below, in the same order.
3. Simplify. Keep the sign.

Reminder: through (0, 5) and (4, −3), the gradient is (−3 − 5) ÷ (4 − 0) = −8 ÷ 4 = −2.

On a graph, use a triangle with corners on grid crossings. Rising to the right is positive. Falling to the right is negative.

### 3.4 Length and midpoint

- Length: find the two differences, square, add, square root.
- Midpoint: add the coordinates and halve each.
- Surds: if a question asks for an exact value, give the answer as a surd. Otherwise the syllabus asks for non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise.
- End-point trick: if M is the midpoint of AB, then B = 2M − A, working in x and y separately.

Reminder: for (−1, −2) and (5, 6) the midpoint is (2, 2) and the length is √(36 + 64) = 10.

### 3.5 Equations of linear graphs

Method in steps (given two points, or a point and a gradient):

1. Find m.
2. Substitute one point into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), or into y = mx + c to find c.
3. Simplify to the form asked.
4. Check with the other point.

To read m and c from ax + by = c, rearrange to y = mx + c. For 3x − 4y = 24: −4y = 24 − 3x, so y = (3/4)x − 6, gradient 3/4 and y-intercept −6. The x-intercept is where y = 0: x = 8.

Give a fully simplified equation. Do not leave brackets or a mix of forms unless asked.

### 3.6 Parallel lines

- Rearrange to y = mx + c to read the gradient.
- Keep the gradient, then use the new point to find the new c.

Reminder: parallel to y = 3x + 1 through (2, 5): 5 = 3(2) + c, so c = −1 and the line is y = 3x − 1.

### 3.7 Perpendicular lines

- Flip the fraction and change the sign.
- Whole-number gradients: 4 becomes −1/4; −5 becomes 1/5.
- For a perpendicular bisector: midpoint, then perpendicular gradient, then the equation.
- To prove a right angle, show that the two gradients multiply to −1 and write the conclusion.

## Two combined reminders

**Perpendicular bisector of P(2, −3) and Q(6, 5).**

1. Midpoint = (4, 1).
2. Gradient of PQ = (5 − (−3)) ÷ (6 − 2) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2.
3. Perpendicular gradient = −1/2.
4. y − 1 = −(1/2)(x − 4), so y = −(1/2)x + 3, or x + 2y = 6.
5. Check: the midpoint gives 4 + 2 = 6.

**Show that 2y − 6x = 5 is parallel to y = 3x + 8.** Rearranging the first gives y = 3x + 5/2. Both gradients are 3, so the lines are parallel.

## Must-know distinctions

| These look similar | The difference |
|---|---|
| Parallel and perpendicular | Parallel: equal gradients. Perpendicular: product −1 |
| Negative reciprocal and reciprocal | The perpendicular gradient needs both the flip and the sign change |
| x = 3 and y = 3 | The line x = 3 runs up and down through (3, 0); the line y = 3 runs across through (0, 3) |
| Gradient and y-intercept | Gradient is the number multiplying x. Intercept is the value where x = 0 |
| Midpoint and length | Midpoint gives a point (two numbers). Length gives one number |
| Line segment and line | A segment has two ends. A line goes on for ever |

## Quick self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Find the gradient of the line through (−2, 1) and (4, 10).
2. Find the midpoint of (−1, −2) and (5, 6), and the length of the segment.
3. Find the gradient and y-intercept of 3x − 4y = 24. Where does it cross the x-axis?
4. Write down the equation of the line parallel to y = 3x + 1 that passes through (2, 5).
5. Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to 6x + 3y = 7.
6. Find the length and midpoint of the segment from (3, −2) to (3, 9).
7. M(1, 3) is the midpoint of AB. A is (−4, 7). Find B.
8. A line with gradient −3 passes through (2, 7) and (k, 1). Find k.
9. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = −x/4 + 1 that passes through (0, −4).
10. A line crosses the x-axis at 8 and the y-axis at −6. Find its equation.
11. Write down the equation of the horizontal line through (5, −3).

### Answers

1. (10 − 1) ÷ (4 − (−2)) = 9 ÷ 6 = **3/2**.
2. Midpoint **(2, 2)**. Length = √(6² + 8²) = **10**.
3. y = (3/4)x − 6. Gradient **3/4**, y-intercept **−6**, and the line crosses the x-axis at **(8, 0)**.
4. Gradient 3. 5 = 3(2) + c gives c = −1. **y = 3x − 1**.
5. 3y = 7 − 6x, so y = −2x + 7/3. The gradient is −2, so the perpendicular gradient is **1/2**.
6. Same x-value, so the length is 9 − (−2) = **11**. Midpoint = **(3, 3.5)**.
7. B = (2 × 1 − (−4), 2 × 3 − 7) = **(6, −1)**.
8. (1 − 7) ÷ (k − 2) = −3, so k − 2 = 2 and **k = 4**.
9. The given gradient is −1/4, so the perpendicular gradient is 4. The y-intercept is −4, so **y = 4x − 4**.
10. The points (8, 0) and (0, −6) give gradient 6/8 = 3/4. The y-intercept is −6, so **y = (3/4)x − 6**, or 3x − 4y = 24.
11. **y = −3**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the order of subtraction in only one of the numerator and the denominator, which gives the wrong sign.
- Reading the gradient of 3x − 4y = 24 as 3 or −3 without rearranging.
- Writing the perpendicular gradient with the right sign but not inverted, or inverted with the wrong sign.
- Using the midpoint formula when the question asks for length, or the reverse.
- Mixing up x = k (vertical) and y = k (horizontal).
- Finding c by substituting the point into the wrong place, and not checking with a second point.
- Stopping at a decimal for a gradient that is a fraction, such as 0.333 for 1/3.
- Leaving the final equation unsimplified, or in a different form from the one requested.
- Claiming lines are parallel or perpendicular without stating the gradients and the conclusion.
