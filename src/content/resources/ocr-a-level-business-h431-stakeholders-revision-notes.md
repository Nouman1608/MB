These notes condense the OCR A Level Business (H431) content "Stakeholders" (specification Version 2.1, January 2026; final assessment summer 2027): meaning, internal and external stakeholders, their objectives, reasons for conflict and why it must be managed, the impact of conflict, how to deal with it, stakeholder influence on aims and objectives, decision making, behaviour and performance, and the impact of business decisions on stakeholder groups. Businesses named are invented.

Related: [Stakeholders study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-stakeholders/) · [Stakeholders practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-stakeholders-practice/) · [Objectives and stakeholders combined revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-different-stakeholder-and-business-objectives-and-stakeholders-revision-notes/)

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder | Individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business's activities |
| Internal stakeholder | Inside the organisation: owners, directors, managers, employees |
| External stakeholder | Outside it: customers, suppliers, lenders, government, community, pressure groups, competitors |
| Stakeholder conflict | Objectives of two or more groups cannot all be met by one decision |
| Power | A group's ability to affect what the business does |
| Interest | How much a group cares about a particular decision |
| Power and interest grid | Matrix (often called Mendelow's) for deciding how to treat each group |

## Grey areas to mention

- **Plc shareholders**: owners (internal) but often behave as outside investors.
- **Employee shareholders**: want both pay and dividends.
- **Agency staff and contractors**: inside the workplace, employed elsewhere.
- **Competitors**: affected by decisions but owed no duty.

## Analysis chain

**Group → objective → why → effect on this decision.**

Example: suppliers want regular orders and prompt payment → because their own cash flow depends on it → so a plan to extend payment terms from 30 to 60 days will strain them and may lead them to raise prices or prioritise other customers.

## Five causes of conflict

| Cause | Typical clash | Usual remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Limited surplus | Wages against dividends | Negotiation; profit-sharing |
| Time horizon | Short-term profit against long-term investment | Explain benefits; phase the plan |
| Risk | Lenders against owners | Security; mixed finance |
| Values | Pressure group against profit-making decision | Modify the decision; engage publicly |
| Information | Staff fear the unknown | Consult; communicate early |

## Why manage conflict

Avoid strikes, lost customers, withdrawn finance, planning objections, bad publicity, legal action and wasted management time.

## Evaluating the impact of conflict

- **Severity**: how badly is output or reputation affected?
- **Duration**: short-term disruption or lasting loss of trust?
- **Who**: does the unhappy group control something vital?
- **Upside**: has the conflict exposed a risk and improved the decision?

## Power and interest grid

| | Low interest | High interest |
|---|---|---|
| High power | Keep satisfied | Manage closely |
| Low power | Monitor | Keep informed |

**Sources of power**: finance; scarce inputs (skills, sole supplier); legal authority; numbers and organisation; public and media attention. Power and interest **change with the decision**.

## Influence: four areas named in the specification

| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| Aims and objectives | Shareholders press for dividend targets |
| Decision making | Bank loan conditions; planning refusal |
| Behaviour | Customer and pressure-group demands for ethical sourcing |
| Performance | Employee motivation and productivity; supplier reliability |

**Evaluate influence by**: power in this case, organisation of the group, the business's dependence on it.

## Impact of a decision: method

1. Do any calculation the data allows (saving, payback, percentage of staff affected).
2. List each group affected, with gain or loss.
3. Weigh who gains most and who bears the cost.
4. Consider which losing group has the power to block or reverse the benefit.
5. Judge, and suggest how to reduce the harm.

**Worked figures**: 14 of 50 jobs replaced = 28%; saving of 14 × £30,000 = £420,000 a year against a cost of £420,000 gives payback of one year.

## Recommend and justify: four steps

Groups and cause → their power and interest → one chosen approach (prioritise, compromise, consult, compensate, phase, change the decision) → who still loses and why that is acceptable.

## Exam traps

- "All stakeholders are equally important."
- A list of groups with no link to the decision.
- Conflict described without a cause.
- Power treated as fixed.
- Only one group considered in an "evaluate impact" answer.
- "Satisfy everyone" as a recommendation.

## Self-test

1. Define a stakeholder.
2. Why can plc shareholders be treated as either internal or external?
3. Give one reason an employee who owns shares might be divided over a pay freeze.
4. Name the five causes of conflict used in these notes.
5. Which cause of conflict is best solved by early consultation?
6. A group has high power and low interest. How should it be treated?
7. A group has low power and high interest. How should it be treated?
8. Give three sources of stakeholder power.
9. Give an example of a stakeholder influencing a business's behaviour.
10. A firm replaces 9 of 36 staff. What percentage is this?
11. A cost saving of £60,000 a year needs an investment of £150,000. What is the payback period?
12. What four steps make a "recommend and justify" answer on conflict?

### Answers

1. An individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, the activities of a business.
2. They own the business, but in a large plc most are investors who take no part in running it.
3. A freeze cuts their real pay but may raise profit and therefore their dividends and share value.
4. Limited surplus, time horizon, risk, values, information.
5. Different information (fear of the unknown).
6. Keep satisfied.
7. Keep informed.
8. Any three: control of finance; control of scarce inputs; legal authority; numbers and organisation; public and media attention.
9. For example: customers' demand for less packaging leading a retailer to change its packaging.
10. 9 ÷ 36 = 25%.
11. 150,000 ÷ 60,000 = 2.5 years.
12. Identify the groups and the cause; judge their power and interest; choose one approach; state who still loses and why that is acceptable.
