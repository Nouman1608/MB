> **Syllabus edition note.** This guide follows the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029.

Sub-topic 1.4 is the largest in Topic 1: three fossil fuels to explain, eleven energy resources to
classify, describe and evaluate, and eight factors that drive demand. The
[energy resources, conservation and fracking guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-environmental-management-energy-resources/)
gives a one-row summary of each resource. This page explains the formation of fossil fuels in more detail,
groups the generating methods so they are easier to remember, and practises the data handling and
"discuss" answers this sub-topic produces.

## What the syllabus asks

- **1.4.1** Describe the formation of fossil fuels: coal, petroleum (oil) and natural gas (methane).
- **1.4.2** Classify energy resources as **renewable (non-finite)**: biofuels (bioethanol, biomass, biogas
  and wood), geothermal, hydro-electric, tidal, wave, solar and wind power; or **non-renewable (finite)**:
  fossil fuels and nuclear power using uranium.
- **1.4.3** Describe how each of these resources is used to generate electricity.
- **1.4.4** Discuss the benefits and limitations of each.
- **1.4.5** Describe and explain eight factors affecting demand for energy: transport; personal and
  national wealth; climate; human population size; industry; disruption to supply; unreliable supply;
  scarcity of resources.

## How fossil fuels form

**Coal**

1. Trees and other plants grow in warm, swampy forests.
2. When they die, they fall into waterlogged ground with little oxygen, so they do not fully decay.
3. Layers of partly decayed plant material build up, forming peat.
4. The peat is buried under sediment. Over millions of years the weight above and heat from below squeeze
   out water and gases.
5. The material becomes coal, which is rich in carbon.

**Petroleum (oil) and natural gas (methane)**

1. Tiny marine organisms, such as plankton, die and sink to the sea bed.
2. They are buried in mud where there is too little oxygen for them to decay.
3. Over millions of years the mud becomes rock, and heat and pressure change the remains into oil and gas.
4. Oil and gas are less dense than water, so they move upwards through permeable rock.
5. They collect where an impermeable layer above traps them, forming a reserve that can be drilled.

The key ideas examiners look for are: **remains of living things**, **lack of oxygen**, **burial**,
**heat and pressure** and **millions of years**. That last point is why fossil fuels are finite.

## Renewable or non-renewable?

The test is whether the resource is replaced as fast as it is used.

- **Biofuels** are renewable because crops and trees regrow. Wood counts as renewable only if trees are
  replanted as fast as they are cut.
- **Geothermal, hydro-electric, tidal, wave, solar and wind** use natural flows of energy that are not
  used up.
- **Fossil fuels** take millions of years to form.
- **Nuclear power** is non-renewable because its fuel, uranium, is mined from a finite supply, even though
  no fuel is burned.

## Generating electricity: the main routes

Nearly every method turns a turbine connected to a generator. What differs is what turns the turbine.

| Route | Resources | Chain |
|---|---|---|
| Heat makes steam | Coal, oil, gas, biomass, wood, nuclear, geothermal | Heat boils water → steam turns turbine → generator |
| Burning gas in an engine or gas turbine | Natural gas, biogas | Burning gas drives the turbine or engine directly → generator |
| Moving water | Hydro-electric, tidal, wave | Flowing water (or air pushed by waves) turns turbine → generator |
| Moving air | Wind | Wind turns blades → generator |
| No turbine | Solar photovoltaic | Cells convert light directly into electricity |

Details to learn for each:

- **Nuclear**: uranium nuclei split (fission), releasing heat that boils water.
- **Biogas**: made by bacteria breaking down manure, sewage or food waste without oxygen; the gas, mainly
  methane, is burned. **Bioethanol** is made by fermenting sugar-rich crops; it is mainly used as a
  vehicle fuel but can be burned to generate electricity.
- **Geothermal**: water is pumped down to hot rocks and returns as steam, or natural steam is used.
- **Hydro-electric**: a dam stores water in a reservoir; water released downhill flows through turbines.
- **Tidal**: a barrage across an estuary traps water at high tide; it flows out through turbines as the
  tide falls.
- **Wave**: the rise and fall of waves pushes air or water through a turbine.
- **Wind**: the blades turn a shaft connected to a generator; turbines are grouped in wind farms on land or
  at sea.

## Benefits and limitations: patterns to apply

Rather than memorising a separate list for every resource, use four questions:

| Question | Points in favour | Points against |
|---|---|---|
| Is output reliable? | Fossil fuels, nuclear, geothermal and hydro-electric can run whenever needed; tides are predictable | Solar, wind and wave vary with weather and time of day |
| What does it emit? | Renewables and nuclear release little or no carbon dioxide while generating | Fossil fuels release carbon dioxide; coal and oil also release sulfur dioxide |
| Where can it be used? | Solar works on rooftops and in remote places | Geothermal needs hot rocks near the surface; tidal needs a large tidal range; hydro-electric needs a valley and high rainfall |
| What are the costs and side-effects? | No fuel cost for wind, solar, wave, tidal and hydro-electric | High building costs; dams flood land and displace people; nuclear waste must be stored safely for a very long time; fuel crops can replace food crops |

### Model "discuss" answer

*Question (original):* Discuss the benefits and limitations of geothermal power for a volcanic island
that imports all its oil. [6]

*Model answer:* A volcanic island has hot rocks close to the surface, so geothermal power is practical
there. It produces electricity continuously, unlike solar or wind, so it can meet demand at any time. It
releases very little carbon dioxide, and because the heat comes from the island itself, the island would
spend less on imported oil and be less affected if supplies were disrupted. However, drilling wells and
building the power station is expensive, which may be difficult for a small economy. Volcanic activity
could damage the plant, and some geothermal sites release gases such as hydrogen sulfide. Suitable sites
may be in areas valued for tourism or wildlife. On balance, geothermal power is likely to be a good choice
because the island has the right geology and an expensive, insecure oil supply, but it would need
investment and careful siting.

## Worked example: electricity mix

*Invented data.* A country generates 80 units of electricity a year.

| Resource | Coal | Natural gas | Nuclear | Hydro-electric | Wind | Solar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units | 30 | 18 | 12 | 10 | 6 | 4 |

```
renewable = 10 + 6 + 4 = 20 units
percentage renewable = 20 / 80 x 100 = 25%
non-renewable = 30 + 18 + 12 = 60 units = 75%
```

Nuclear belongs in the non-renewable total, even though it emits little carbon dioxide; leaving it out is
the most common error in this kind of question.

## Factors affecting demand for energy

"Describe and explain" means giving the factor **and** the reason it changes demand.

| Factor | Effect on demand | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Rises | More vehicles, more and longer journeys, more fuel |
| Personal and national wealth | Rises with wealth | More appliances, air conditioning, cars and industry |
| Climate | Rises in very hot or very cold places | Energy for cooling or heating |
| Human population size | Rises with population | More people need more homes, food, transport and power |
| Industry | Rises with industrialisation | Factories, especially heavy industry, use large amounts of energy |
| Disruption to supply | Can fall or shift | Conflict or natural hazards cut supply, so people use less or switch source |
| Unreliable supply | Shifts | People and businesses buy generators or batteries, or use other fuels |
| Scarcity of resources | Can fall or shift | Scarce fuels are expensive, so people use less or change to alternatives |

## Common mistakes

- Saying coal forms from marine organisms or oil from trees.
- Leaving out the lack of oxygen, which is why the remains do not decay.
- Classifying nuclear power as renewable.
- Saying solar panels "turn a turbine".
- Calling a resource "clean" or "cheap" without saying why or for whom.
- Describing demand factors without explaining how they change demand.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** formation: remains, burial without oxygen, heat and pressure, millions of years.
- **Classify**: renewable (non-finite) or non-renewable (finite), with a reason if asked.
- **Describe** generation: what turns the turbine, then the generator.
- **Discuss**: benefits, limitations, and a judgement tied to the place in the question.
- **Calculate**: show the working, and include nuclear in the right group.
