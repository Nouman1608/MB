All figures are invented.

## The circular flow of income (3.2.2.1)

- **National income** measures the value of goods and services produced in a period.
- **Nominal** income is at current prices; **real** income is adjusted for price changes and is the indicator of
  economic performance.
- **Income = output = expenditure.**
- **Withdrawals:** saving (S), taxation (T), imports (M). **Injections:** investment (I), government spending (G),
  exports (X).
- **Equilibrium:** planned injections = planned withdrawals.
- Injections > withdrawals → national income rises; withdrawals > injections → it falls.
- Equilibrium income may be **below full employment income**.

## AD/AS analysis (3.2.2.2)

- Change in the price level → **movement along** AD and AS.
- Other changes → **shifts**.
- **Underlying growth** → rightward shift of LRAS.
- **Demand-side shock:** shifts AD (for example a fall in confidence or a recession abroad).
- **Supply-side shock:** shifts SRAS (for example a sharp rise in commodity prices).
- AD/AS can show changes in the price level, **demand-deficient (cyclical) unemployment** and economic growth.

## Determinants of AD (3.2.2.3)

**AD = C + I + G + (X − M)**

| Component | Rises when… |
|---|---|
| C | disposable income, wealth, confidence or credit rise; interest rates fall |
| I | expected demand, confidence or profits rise; interest rates fall; technology improves |
| G | the government decides to spend more |
| X | incomes abroad rise; the exchange rate falls; domestic goods become more competitive |
| M | domestic incomes rise; the exchange rate rises; imports become more competitive |

- **Saving** = income not consumed. **Investment** = spending on capital goods. Different agents, different
  motives.
- Determinants of saving: interest rates, confidence, wealth, availability of credit, expectations of future
  income.
- **Basic accelerator:** investment depends on the **rate of change** of output, so it is volatile. No calculations
  required.
- **Net exports:** a rise in X − M raises AD and can raise output and employment.

## The multiplier (3.2.2.4)

- **MPC** = change in consumption ÷ change in income.
- **k = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)**. AQA requires calculation only from the MPC.
- **Final change in income = k × initial change in spending.**
- A higher MPC → a larger multiplier.
- Works upwards and downwards; full effect takes time; smaller near full capacity, where prices rise instead.

| MPC | k |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 2 |
| 0.6 | 2.5 |
| 0.75 | 4 |
| 0.8 | 5 |
| 0.9 | 10 |

## Short-run AS (3.2.2.5)

- Main determinants: **the price level and production costs**.
- SRAS shifts with **money wage rates, raw material prices, business taxation and productivity**.
- Higher costs → SRAS shifts left (higher price level, lower output).

## Long-run AS (3.2.2.6)

- Determinants: **technology, productivity, attitudes, enterprise, factor mobility, economic incentives**.
- Vertical LRAS sits at the **normal capacity level of output**.
- **Institutional structure** matters, for example a banking system that provides business investment funds.
- **Keynesian AS:** horizontal (spare capacity), then rising (bottlenecks), then vertical (full capacity).

## Worked reminders (invented)

- MPC 0.75, extra investment 12 billion: k = 4, income rises **48 billion**.
- MPC 0.6, exports fall 5 billion: k = 2.5, income falls **12.5 billion**.
- First three rounds of a 10 billion injection with MPC 0.8: **10, 8, 6.4** billion.

## Must-know distinctions

- Injection versus withdrawal.
- Saving versus investment.
- Equilibrium income versus full employment income.
- Movement along AD versus shift of AD.
- SRAS shift (costs) versus LRAS shift (capacity).
- Vertical LRAS versus Keynesian AS.

## Chains of reasoning to learn

- **Fall in interest rates:** cheaper borrowing → consumption and investment rise → AD shifts right → output and
  employment rise (with spare capacity) → multiplier adds further rounds.
- **Rise in house prices:** households feel wealthier → consumption rises → AD shifts right.
- **Appreciation of the exchange rate:** exports dearer abroad, imports cheaper → net exports fall → AD shifts left.
- **Rise in money wage rates without higher productivity:** unit costs rise → SRAS shifts left → price level rises,
  output falls.
- **Improved education and training:** productivity rises → LRAS shifts right → potential output rises.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 1 ÷ MPC instead of 1 ÷ (1 − MPC).
- Treating a change in the price level as a shift of AD.
- Explaining the multiplier without the successive rounds of spending.
- Ignoring spare capacity when predicting the effect of higher AD.

## Quick self-test

1. State the identity linking income, output and expenditure.
2. Name the three injections and the three withdrawals.
3. What happens to national income when planned withdrawals exceed planned injections?
4. Why may equilibrium income be below full employment income?
5. Give two determinants of consumption.
6. Explain why saving and investment are not the same.
7. Why is investment the most volatile component of AD?
8. The MPC is 0.9. Calculate the multiplier.
9. Government spending rises by 8 billion and the MPC is 0.75. By how much does national income rise?
10. Give two factors that shift SRAS.
11. Give two factors that shift LRAS.
12. What does a rise in AD do to output and prices on the vertical section of the Keynesian AS curve?

### Answers

1. Income = output = expenditure.
2. Injections: investment, government spending, exports. Withdrawals: saving, taxation, imports.
3. It **falls** until withdrawals and injections are equal again.
4. The circular flow can settle wherever planned injections equal planned withdrawals; nothing guarantees that this
   level uses all resources.
5. Any two: disposable income, interest rates, wealth, consumer confidence, availability of credit.
6. Saving is income not spent, mostly by households; investment is spending on capital goods by firms, driven by
   expected profits. A rise in one need not cause a rise in the other.
7. It depends on the **rate of change** of output and on expectations (the accelerator and confidence), which
   swing sharply.
8. k = 1 ÷ 0.1 = **10**.
9. k = 4, so **32 billion**.
10. Any two: money wage rates, raw material prices, business taxation, productivity.
11. Any two: technology, productivity, attitudes, enterprise, factor mobility, economic incentives.
12. Output stays at full capacity; **only the price level rises**.
