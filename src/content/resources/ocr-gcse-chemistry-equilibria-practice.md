The questions below target **C5.3 Equilibria** in the J248 specification for OCR GCSE (9–1) Chemistry A (Gateway Science), Version 4.0, August 2026. Between them they test learning outcomes C5.3a–C5.3c and mathematical learning outcomes CM5.3i–CM5.3iv. Questions 1 to 7 (C5.3a, C5.3b and the maths) suit both tiers. Questions 8 to 12 apply Le Chatelier's principle (C5.3c) and are labelled **Higher Tier only**.

Before you start, read the [Equilibria study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-chemistry-equilibria/) or the [Equilibria revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-chemistry-equilibria-revision-notes/). The [OCR GCSE Chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/gcse/chemistry/) and the [J248 checklist](/checklists/ocr/gcse/chemistry/) show where this sub-topic fits.

**How to use this set.** Give a unit with every rate. For "Explain" questions on shifts, name the direction **and** say what happens to the substance in the question.

## Questions

**1.** Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride paper is used to test for water.

hydrated cobalt(II) chloride ⇌ anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride + water

**(a)** What does the symbol ⇌ show? **[1]**
**(b)** A drop of a colourless liquid turns blue cobalt(II) chloride paper pink. What does this show about the liquid? **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how the pink paper could be turned blue again. **[1]**

**2.** State the type of system needed for a dynamic equilibrium, and **two** features of a reaction at dynamic equilibrium. **[3]**

**3.** A student writes: "At equilibrium, the reactants and products have equal concentrations." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**4.** Gas C changes into gas D in a sealed container: C(g) ⇌ D(g). At the start the concentration of C is 0.50 mol/dm³ and there is no D.

| Time (s) | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [D] (mol/dm³) | 0.00 | 0.21 | 0.31 | 0.36 | 0.39 | 0.40 | 0.40 | 0.40 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of formation of D in the first 5 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate between 5 s and 15 s and explain why it is lower than your answer to (a). **[2]**
**(c)** Use the data to estimate when equilibrium is reached. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the ratio of D to C at equilibrium, and the percentage of C converted to D. **[2]**

**5.** A graph of the data in question 4 is drawn. A tangent to the curve at 10 s passes through (0 s, 0.17 mol/dm³) and (25 s, 0.52 mol/dm³).

**(a)** Calculate the rate of formation of D at 10 s. **[2]**
**(b)** State the gradient of a tangent drawn at 30 s and what it tells you. **[1]**

**6.** Describe how the rates of the forward and reverse reactions in question 4 change from the start until equilibrium is reached. **[3]**

**7.** In question 4, the forward rate is directly proportional to the concentration of C. At the start it is 0.060 mol/dm³/s.

**(a)** Calculate the forward rate at equilibrium. **[2]**
**(b)** State the rate of the reverse reaction at equilibrium. Give a reason. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A U-tube contains this equilibrium: ICl(l) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ ICl₃(s). ICl is a brown liquid and ICl₃ is a yellow solid. The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** More chlorine is passed into the tube. Predict what you would see and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** The U-tube is then put into a beaker of hot water. Predict what you would see and explain why. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) For each reaction, say which way (if at all) the position moves when the pressure is raised.

**(a)** C(s) + CO₂(g) ⇌ 2CO(g). Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** N₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) **[1]**
**(c)** 2E(g) ⇌ F(g) + 3G(g) **[1]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) In a sealed vessel, gas J forms gas K: 2J(g) ⇌ K(g). The percentage of K once equilibrium is reached was measured under four sets of conditions.

| Temperature | Low pressure | High pressure |
|---|---|---|
| 250 °C | 30% K | 55% K |
| 450 °C | 18% K | 38% K |

**(a)** Compare the two pressures. Explain how the results fit the equation. **[2]**
**(b)** What do the results show about the forward reaction's energy change? Explain. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) Hydrogen can be made by reacting methane with steam:

CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g)    forward reaction endothermic

**(a)** Predict the effect of raising the temperature on the amount of hydrogen at equilibrium. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the effect of raising the pressure on the amount of hydrogen at equilibrium. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** other changes that would increase the amount of hydrogen produced. **[2]**
**(d)** After a change, the system settles at a new equilibrium. Compare the forward and reverse rates now. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher Tier only) Iron(III) ions react with thiocyanate ions in solution:

Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq)
yellow       colourless     blood red

Plan an experiment to test the prediction that adding more of either reactant makes the mixture a deeper red. Include the apparatus, how you make it a fair test, what you record, and the result you expect, with a reason. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The reaction is reversible: it can go in both directions [1].
**(b)** The liquid contains water [1].
**(c)** Heat the paper (to drive off the water) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "the liquid is water" is weaker than "contains water"; a pink colour shows water is present, not that the liquid is pure.

**2.** A closed system [1]. Forward rate = reverse rate [1]. No concentration changes any more (each is constant) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Rates are equal" needs both reactions named; "the reaction has the same rate" does not show you know two directions are involved.

**3.** At equilibrium the concentrations are constant, not necessarily equal [1]. They stay the same because the forward and reverse rates are equal, and the mixture can contain more product than reactant or the other way round [1].
*Examiner insight:* The second mark comes from linking constant concentrations to equal rates, not from repeating the word "constant".

**4. (a)** mean rate = (0.21 − 0.00) ÷ 5 [1] = **0.042 mol/dm³/s** [1].
**(b)** (0.36 − 0.21) ÷ 10 = **0.015 mol/dm³/s** [1]. It is lower because there is less C left to react and the reverse reaction is faster as D builds up [1].
**(c)** At about **25 s**, where [D] stops changing [1].
**(d)** [C] = 0.50 − 0.40 = 0.10 mol/dm³, so D : C = **4 : 1** [1]. Percentage converted = 0.40 ÷ 0.50 × 100 = **80%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A rate with no unit, or one missing the "/s", is unlikely to earn the final mark.

**5. (a)** gradient = (0.52 − 0.17) ÷ (25 − 0) [1] = **0.014 mol/dm³/s** [1].
**(b)** Zero: there is no overall change, because the system is at equilibrium [1].
*Examiner insight:* Read tangent points from far apart on the line; points close together make the gradient less accurate.

**6.** The forward rate is highest at the start and falls as C is used up [1]. The reverse rate starts at zero and rises as D forms [1]. The two rates become equal and then stay constant [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a reason as well as a direction; "the forward rate goes down" alone describes without explaining.

**7. (a)** [C] falls from 0.50 to 0.10 mol/dm³, which is one fifth [1]. Forward rate = 0.060 × (0.10 ÷ 0.50) = **0.012 mol/dm³/s** [1].
**(b)** **0.012 mol/dm³/s**, because at equilibrium the forward and reverse rates are equal [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the scale factor; a bare answer to a proportionality question gives the examiner nothing to credit if the arithmetic slips.

**8. (a)** More yellow solid forms (the brown liquid decreases) [1]. Adding a reactant shifts the position of equilibrium to the right to oppose the change [1].
**(b)** The yellow solid turns back into brown liquid [1]. A rise in temperature shifts the position in the endothermic direction, which is the reverse reaction [1].
*Examiner insight:* Observation questions need colours or states; "the equilibrium shifts left" is a reason, not an observation.

**9. (a)** Shifts to the **left** [1]. Only gases count: 1 gas molecule on the left and 2 on the right, so the position moves to the side with fewer gas molecules [1].
**(b)** **No effect**: 2 gas molecules on each side [1].
**(c)** Shifts to the **left**: 2 gas molecules on the left, 4 on the right [1].
*Examiner insight:* Counting carbon as a molecule in (a) gives 2 against 2 and the wrong answer; check state symbols first.

**10. (a)** At both temperatures there is more K at high pressure (30% to 55% at 250 °C; 18% to 38% at 450 °C) [1]. A higher pressure favours the side with fewer gas molecules, and K's side has 1 against 2 for J [1].
**(b)** The forward reaction is **exothermic** [1]: at either pressure the percentage of K falls when the temperature goes up (for example 55% to 38%), so heating favours the reverse reaction, which must be endothermic [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote a pair of values in which only one condition changes; comparing 30% at 250 °C with 38% at 450 °C mixes two variables.

**11. (a)** The amount of hydrogen **increases** [1], because the position shifts in the endothermic direction, which is the forward reaction [1].
**(b)** The amount of hydrogen **decreases** [1], because the left side has 2 gas molecules and the right side has 4, so higher pressure shifts the position to the left [1].
**(c)** Any two: use excess steam (or methane) [1]; remove hydrogen as it forms [1].
**(d)** They are equal again [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the molecule count is the evidence; "pressure shifts it left" without the numbers 2 and 4 is unlikely to get the second mark.

**12.** Mix dilute iron(III) solution and dilute thiocyanate solution to make a red mixture [1]. Pour equal volumes into test tubes using a measuring cylinder [1]. Add a few drops of iron(III) solution to one tube and the same number of drops of thiocyanate solution to another [1]. Keep one tube as a control, adding the same volume of water so all tubes are diluted equally [1]. Compare the colours against a white tile and record them in a table; wear eye protection [1]. Expected result: both tubes with extra reactant turn deeper red, because the position shifts right to oppose the added reactant, making more FeSCN²⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* A plan gains credit for specific steps; "do a fair test" earns nothing unless you say which variables you keep the same.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "equal concentrations" instead of "constant concentrations" at equilibrium.
- Forgetting "closed system" when asked for the conditions for equilibrium.
- Giving a rate without units, or with the wrong ones.
- Using two table values when the question asks for the rate at one time, which needs a tangent.
- Counting solids, liquids or atoms in a pressure question.
- Stating a shift without saying what happens to the named substance.
- Linking a temperature rise to the exothermic direction.
- In plans, leaving out the control or the step that keeps dilution the same.
