## Core recall

### Purpose of market research

- Identify and understand customers.
- Identify market opportunities.
- Gather information about **demand**, **competition** and the **target market**.

Overall aim: reduce the risk of marketing decisions by using evidence.

### The six named methods

| Method | Primary or secondary | Usually gives | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Primary | Quantitative (closed questions) or qualitative (open questions) | Many people need to answer the same questions cheaply |
| Survey | Primary | Mainly quantitative | The business needs "how many" from a large number of people |
| Interview | Primary | Qualitative | Detailed reasons are needed and questions may need explaining |
| Focus group | Primary | Qualitative | Testing reactions to a new product, design or advert |
| Internet research | Secondary | Either | Quick, cheap background on rivals, prices and trends |
| Printed press | Secondary | Either | Trends and competitor news from newspapers or trade magazines |

### Primary against secondary

| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Collected first-hand for the business | Already exists, collected by others |
| Cost and time | Higher | Lower |
| Relevance | Answers the exact question | May not fit the business's needs |
| Up to date | Yes | May be out of date |
| Available to rivals | No | Often yes |

### Qualitative against quantitative

- **Quantitative:** numbers; easy to compare and chart; does not explain why.
- **Qualitative:** opinions and reasons; explains why; hard to summarise and often from few people.

### Choosing a method: four factors

1. Money available.
2. Time available.
3. What the business needs to know: how many, or why.
4. How much is at stake in the decision.

## Formulas and data skills

| Calculation | Method |
|---|---|
| Share of respondents | Number choosing ÷ total asked × 100 |
| Market size | Total sales of all businesses in the market (by value in £ or volume in units) |
| Market share | Business's sales ÷ total market sales × 100 |
| Total market from share | Business's sales ÷ (share ÷ 100) |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |

Learn these by heart rather than relying on being given them.

Quick example: a gym earns £84,000 in a local market worth £560,000. Share = £84,000 ÷ £560,000 × 100 = 15%. If the market grows to £700,000 and the gym's sales stay the same, its share falls to £84,000 ÷ £700,000 × 100 = 12%.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Market research | Collecting and analysing information about customers, competitors and the market |
| Primary research | New information collected first-hand for the business's own purpose |
| Secondary research | Information that already exists, collected by someone else |
| Quantitative research | Numerical data that can be counted and analysed |
| Qualitative research | Information about opinions, feelings and reasons |
| Questionnaire | A set of questions used to collect information |
| Focus group | A small group of people who discuss a product, led by a researcher |
| Market opportunity | A gap in the market where customer needs are unmet |
| Market size | The total sales of all businesses in a market over a period |
| Market share | One business's sales as a percentage of the market's total sales |

## Exam traps

- Primary means new and first-hand; secondary already exists. Do not swap them.
- Focus groups and interviews are mainly qualitative.
- Use value with value and volume with volume in market share.
- Falling market share does not always mean falling sales; check whether the market grew.
- Interpret, do not just repeat: say what the figure means for the business's decision.
- Match the method to the business's budget and question; an expensive method for a tiny start-up is rarely appropriate.
- Ask who was surveyed: existing customers only may not represent the whole market.

## Self-test

1. State three things market research gives a business information about.
2. Define secondary research.
3. Give two primary research methods.
4. Is a focus group mainly qualitative or quantitative? Why?
5. Give one drawback of internet research.
6. Give one benefit of an interview over a questionnaire.
7. A business asks 400 people and 136 say they would buy. What percentage is this?
8. A firm sells £36,000 in a market worth £240,000. Calculate its market share.
9. A firm with £52,000 of sales has a 13% share. What is the market size?
10. A firm's share fell from 20% to 18% while its sales rose. Explain how.
11. Which method would you suggest for a start-up café with very little money that wants to know about local competitors?
12. What is the difference between market size by value and by volume?

### Answers

1. Any three of: customers, demand, competition, the target market, market opportunities.
2. Information that already exists, collected by someone else for another purpose.
3. Any two of: questionnaire, survey, interview, focus group.
4. Qualitative, because it produces opinions, feelings and reasons rather than numbers.
5. For example, it may be out of date, inaccurate or biased.
6. The interviewer can explain questions and follow up answers, giving more detail.
7. 136 ÷ 400 × 100 = **34%**.
8. £36,000 ÷ £240,000 × 100 = **15%**.
9. £52,000 ÷ 0.13 = **£400,000**.
10. The whole market grew faster than the firm's sales, so rivals took more of the growth.
11. Internet research of rivals' websites, menus and online reviews, because it is quick and free; a short questionnaire to passers-by could follow.
12. Value measures total sales revenue in £; volume measures the number of units sold.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which market research methods does AQA GCSE Business (8132) name?**
Questionnaires, surveys, interviews and focus groups (primary), and internet research and printed press (secondary).

**How do I remember the market share formula?**
The business's part goes on top and the whole market underneath: market share = (business's sales ÷ total market sales) × 100.
