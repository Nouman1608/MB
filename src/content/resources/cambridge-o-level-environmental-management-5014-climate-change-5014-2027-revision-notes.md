Condensed notes for sub-topic 4.2 Climate change in the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The [climate change study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-climate-change-5014-2027/) explains each point with worked examples; the [climate change practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-climate-change-5014-2027-practice/) let you apply them.

## The nine statements as a flow

**Define** greenhouse gas (4.2.1) → **state** CO2, H2O, CH4 (4.2.2) → **describe** six causes (4.2.3) → **explain** the enhanced greenhouse effect (4.2.4) → **explain** twelve impacts (4.2.5) → **describe** twelve footprint strategies (4.2.6) → **define** adaptation (4.2.7) → **describe** four adaptation strategies (4.2.8) → **discuss** strategies (4.2.9).

## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Greenhouse gas**: a gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy.
- **Climate change adaptation**: altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the impacts of climate change.
- Useful extra terms: **global warming** = rise in the Earth's average temperature; **climate change** = long-term change in weather patterns caused by it; **carbon footprint** = total greenhouse gases released by a person, organisation, product or country.

## Causes: match the activity to the gas

- **Carbon dioxide**: burning fossil fuels; deforestation (less photosynthesis); cement manufacture (limestone heated to lime); growing population and energy use.
- **Methane**: cattle digestion; manure decomposition; rice fields; wetland drainage.

Memory hook for methane: **C**attle, **M**anure, **R**ice, **W**etlands.

Cement equation in words: calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide.

## The warming chain

More greenhouse gases → more radiation absorbed → enhanced greenhouse effect → global warming → climate change.

## Twelve impacts in four groups

| Group | Impacts |
|---|---|
| Heat and ice | Hotter land surface; hotter ocean surface; melting ice sheets, glaciers and permafrost → sea-level rise |
| Ocean chemistry | Ocean acidification from more dissolved carbon dioxide |
| Wildlife | Disrupted food chains; change in biodiversity; loss of habitat; more pest outbreaks and invasive species |
| People | Forced migration (humans and animals); more frequent and severe extreme weather (floods and land loss, drought, wildfires); changed crop yields; food shortages |

ENSO, El Niño and La Niña are **not** required.

## Discussion points (4.2.9)

| Strategy type | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| International agreements | Large-scale cuts | Hard to agree and enforce |
| Taxation | Changes behaviour; raises money for green energy | Can be unfair to low-income households |
| Individual choices | Cheap, immediate | Small unless many people act |
| Forests | Carbon removal plus habitats and flood control | Slow; competes for farmland; carbon released if burned |
| Sequestration, models, AI | Potentially powerful | Costly; needs data, investment, expertise |
| Adaptation | Protects people now | Does not reduce emissions; costly for poorer countries |

## Calculation patterns

- Emissions = activity × emission factor, for example 5,000 km × 0.15 kg per km = 750 kg.
- Percentage reduction = reduction ÷ original × 100. Cutting 6.0 to 4.5 tonnes is a 25% reduction.
- Per person = total ÷ population.
- Rate of change = change ÷ years, for example a 90 mm sea-level rise over 30 years is 3 mm per year.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Greenhouse gas | Absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal energy |
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Extra warming caused by increased greenhouse gas concentrations |
| Global warming | Rise in the Earth's average temperature |
| Ocean acidification | Sea water becoming more acidic as it absorbs more carbon dioxide |
| Permafrost | Ground that normally stays frozen all year |
| Carbon footprint | Total greenhouse gases released by a person, organisation, product or country |
| Reforestation | Replanting trees where forest has been lost |
| Afforestation | Planting trees where there was no forest |
| Carbon sequestration | Capturing, removing and storing carbon dioxide |
| Adaptation | Altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to cope with climate change impacts |

## Exam traps

- Global warming ≠ climate change: one causes the other.
- Deforestation increases carbon dioxide because **less is removed**, not because trees "give off" gases.
- Wetland drainage releases **methane**, not carbon dioxide, in the syllabus.
- CFCs and the ozone hole belong to sub-topic 4.4, not here.
- Adaptation does not reduce emissions; do not offer it for a "reduce carbon footprint" question.
- Fewer children per woman is a named strategy; explain it through lower future energy demand.

## Self-test

1. Define a greenhouse gas.
2. Give the chemical formulae of the three greenhouse gases named in the syllabus.
3. Name two agricultural sources of methane.
4. Explain how deforestation increases carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
5. What compound is heated to make lime in cement manufacture?
6. Put in order: climate change, more greenhouse gases, global warming, enhanced greenhouse effect.
7. Why does ocean acidification harm corals?
8. Give two impacts of climate change on people.
9. What is the difference between reforestation and afforestation?
10. Define climate change adaptation.
11. Give one benefit and one limitation of carbon taxation.
12. A family's footprint falls from 8.0 to 6.4 tonnes. What is the percentage reduction?

## Answers

1. A gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy.
2. CO2, H2O, CH4.
3. Any two of: digestion by cattle, manure decomposition, rice fields.
4. Fewer trees remove less carbon dioxide by photosynthesis.
5. Calcium carbonate (limestone).
6. More greenhouse gases → enhanced greenhouse effect → global warming → climate change.
7. More acidic water makes it harder for corals to build their skeletons.
8. Any two of: forced migration, flooding and loss of land, drought, wildfires, changed crop yields, food shortages.
9. Reforestation replants where forest was lost; afforestation plants where there was no forest.
10. Altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the impacts of climate change.
11. Benefit: discourages high-emission choices or raises money for alternatives; limitation: can be unfair to low-income households.
12. 1.6 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = 20%.
