**What do these questions practise?** Topic 3.2.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat 9237, the
life and teachings of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), examined in Paper 2 Section A: one compulsory
25-mark question in parts worth 1, 2, 4, 6 and 12 marks. In the real exam the 12-mark part prints a
prescribed passage from Section 6 of the specification; here you are asked to refer to relevant
teachings from the Qur'an or Hadith that you have studied.

## Practice set 1

**1 (a)** Who was the Prophet's (pbuh) wet nurse? **[1]**

**1 (b)** Give **two** features of religion in pre-Islamic Arabia. **[2]**

**1 (c)** Describe what happened in the Cave of Hira when the Prophet (pbuh) received the first
revelation. **[4]**

**1 (d)** Explain **two** reasons why the leaders of Makkah opposed the Prophet's (pbuh) message. **[6]**

**1 (e)** "The Hijrah was the most important event in the Prophet's (pbuh) life." Examine this
statement, referring to Islamic teaching. **[12]**

## Practice set 2

**2 (d)** Explain **two** ways in which the Ansar supported the Muhajirun. **[6]**

## Answers: set 1

**1 (a)** Halima Sa'diya [1].

**1 (b)** Any two: worship of many idols [1]; idols kept at the Ka'ba [1]; belief in a high god alongside
idols [1]; presence of Jewish and Christian communities [1]; a few hanifs following the faith of
Ibrahim (AS) [1].

**1 (c)** Any four: the Prophet (pbuh) was meditating in the cave in Ramadan [1]; the angel Jibril (AS)
appeared and commanded "Read" [1]; he replied that he could not read [1]; Jibril pressed him tightly
three times [1]; then recited the first verses of Sura 96 [1]; he returned home frightened and asked
Khadija to cover him [1].

**1 (d)** Two reasons, each developed for up to 3 marks. *Economic:* Makkah earned money from pilgrims
who came to worship the idols at the Ka'ba [1]; the call to worship one God threatened this trade
[1]; so wealthy leaders feared losing income [1]. *Social and political:* Islam taught that all
people are equal before Allah [1]; this challenged the power of the Quraysh leaders and the tribal
system [1]; and their loyalty to the religion of their ancestors [1].

**1 (e)** Levels-marked. *Arguments for:* the Hijrah saved the Muslim community from persecution
[2]; it allowed the first Muslim state, the Prophet's Mosque and the Charter of Madinah [2]; it marks
the start of the Islamic calendar, showing how central it is [1–2]; it showed trust in Allah, as in
the cave of Thawr (Qur'an 9.40 tells of the two in the cave and Allah's support) [1–2]. *Arguments
against:* the first revelation in Hira was the beginning of prophethood, without which nothing else
would follow [2]; the conquest of Makkah completed the mission and purified the Ka'ba [1–2]; the
Farewell Sermon summarised his teaching for all time [1–2]. A reasoned conclusion that weighs these
views earns the top level.

## Answers: set 2

**2 (a)** Uhud [1].

**2 (b)** Any two: a ten-year truce [1]; Muslims to return the following year for umrah [1]; Makkans
who joined the Muslims would be returned, but not the reverse [1]; tribes could ally with either
side [1].

**2 (c)** Any four: in one night the Prophet (pbuh) was taken from Makka to Jerusalem (Isra) [1]; he
led the earlier prophets in prayer at al-Aqsa [1]; he ascended through the heavens (Mi'raj) [1]; he
met earlier prophets [1]; the five daily prayers were made obligatory [1]; on his return Abu Bakr (RA)
confirmed his account and was called al-Siddiq [1].

**2 (d)** Two ways, each developed for up to 3 marks. *Shelter and property:* the Ansar welcomed the
Muhajirun into their homes [1] because the migrants had left their property in Makka [1]; Abu Ayyub
Ansari (RA) hosted the Prophet (pbuh) himself [1]. *Brotherhood:* the Prophet paired each Muhajir with
an Ansari [1]; they shared land, wealth and work [1]; this built a united community in Madinah [1].

**2 (e)** Levels-marked. *Arguments for:* it summarises key teachings: sanctity of life and property
[1–2]; equality, with no superiority of Arab over non-Arab except by piety, which challenges racism
today [2]; rights of women [1–2]; the end of interest and blood feuds [1–2]; instruction to hold to the
Qur'an [1]. *Arguments against:* the Qur'an itself is the primary guide and the Sermon points to it
[2]; the whole Sunnah, not one speech, guides daily life [1–2]; modern questions need scholarship using
ijma' and qiyas [1–2]. A balanced conclusion, for example that the Sermon is an excellent summary that
directs Muslims back to the Qur'an and Sunnah, reaches the top level.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a 12-mark answer be?**
Around a page: two or three developed arguments on each side,
references to teaching, and a justified conclusion.

**Will a passage be printed?**
Yes. In the exam the 12-mark part prints a prescribed passage from
Section 6 of the specification, and you must use it in your answer.
