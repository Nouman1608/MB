These questions cover section **3.2.5.3 Shapes of complex ions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Shapes of complex ions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-complex-ions/) and the [Shapes of complex ions revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-complex-ions-revision-notes/). In the structure questions, describe drawings in words if you cannot sketch them, naming the positions of the ligands.

## Questions

**1.** State the shape and the bond angle of each complex ion.

**(a)** [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ **[2]**

**(b)** [CuCl₄]²⁻ **[2]**

**(c)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ **[2]**

**2.** Explain why chloride ions form a tetrahedral complex with cobalt(II) whereas water forms an octahedral complex. **[2]**

**3.** The complex [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is square planar.

**(a)** Draw the cis and trans isomers. **[2]**

**(b)** State which isomer is cisplatin. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this type of isomerism is described as a special case of E–Z isomerism. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the complex [CuCl₄]²⁻ does not show cis–trans isomerism. **[2]**

**5.** The complex ion [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is octahedral.

**(a)** Draw or describe the cis and trans isomers. **[2]**

**(b)** State the co-ordination number and the oxidation state of cobalt. **[2]**

**6.** Does [Pt(NH₃)₃Cl]⁺, which is square planar, show cis–trans isomerism? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** The complex ion [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ has optical isomers.

**(a)** State what is meant by optical isomers. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this complex has optical isomers. **[2]**

**(c)** State the co-ordination number of nickel. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why trans-[Co(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₂Cl₂]⁺ does not show optical isomerism, but the cis form does. **[3]**

**9.** The silver ion forms a complex ion in Tollens' reagent.

**(a)** Give the formula of the complex ion. **[1]**

**(b)** State its shape and bond angle, and explain why it has this shape. **[3]**

**10.** State the type of stereoisomerism, if any, shown by each complex.

**(a)** [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ **[1]**

**(b)** [Ni(NH₃)₄Cl₂] (octahedral with NH₃ ligands and two Cl⁻ ligands, neutral overall) **[1]**

**(c)** [FeCl₄]⁻ **[1]**

**11.** A student draws two structures of an octahedral complex [M(en)₃] and claims they are optical isomers, but one is simply the other rotated. Explain how you could check whether two structures are optical isomers. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why cis–trans isomers of a complex have different properties from each other, and suggest one example of why this matters. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Octahedral [1]; 90° [1]

**(b)** Tetrahedral [1]; 109.5° [1]

**(c)** Linear [1]; 180° [1]. **[6]**

**2.** Chloride ions are larger than water molecules [1]; only four chloride ions can fit round the cobalt ion, whereas six water molecules can [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** cis: two Cl ligands adjacent (90° apart) and two NH₃ adjacent [1]; trans: two Cl ligands opposite each other (180° apart) [1]

**(b)** The cis isomer [1]

**(c)** E–Z isomerism arises from different arrangements of groups on the same side or opposite sides, because of restricted rotation [1]; in the complex the ligands are fixed in a plane around the metal, so identical ligands can be on the same side (cis) or opposite sides (trans) [1]. **[5]**

**4.** [CuCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral with four identical ligands [1]; in a tetrahedron all positions are adjacent to each other, so the ligands cannot be arranged cis or trans (and all four are the same) [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** trans: the two Cl⁻ ligands are opposite each other (180°) with four NH₃ in the plane [1]; cis: the two Cl⁻ ligands are adjacent (90°) [1]

**(b)** Co-ordination number 6 [1]; oxidation state +3 (charge +1 = Co + 2 × (–1), so Co = +3) [1]. **[4]**

**6.** No [1]; there are three identical NH₃ ligands and one Cl⁻, so there is only one way to arrange them in the square plane [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other [1]

**(b)** It is octahedral with three bidentate ligands [1]; the ligands can be arranged in two ways that are mirror images and cannot be superimposed (no plane of symmetry) [1]

**(c)** 6 [1]. **[4]**

**8.** The trans form has a plane of symmetry through the two chloride ligands and the metal [1]; its mirror image is identical to itself, so it has no optical isomer [1]; the cis form has no plane of symmetry and its mirror image cannot be superimposed [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ [1]

**(b)** Linear [1]; 180° [1]; two ligands repel each other as far apart as possible [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Optical isomerism [1]

**(b)** cis–trans isomerism [1]

**(c)** None [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Draw the mirror image of one structure [1]; try to superimpose it on the other structure by rotating it; if it cannot be superimposed, the two are optical isomers, but if it can, they are the same molecule [1]. **[2]**

**12.** They have different shapes (different spatial arrangements), so different physical properties such as polarity, solubility and melting point, and different biological behaviour [1]; for example, cisplatin (cis) is used as an anti-cancer drug but the trans isomer is not effective in the same way [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* For optical isomerism always say "non-superimposable mirror images". Describing the isomers as "different arrangements" without the mirror-image idea does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving 109.5° for a square planar complex, or 90° for a tetrahedral one.
- Saying cisplatin is the trans isomer.
- Claiming tetrahedral complexes show cis–trans isomerism.
- Drawing optical isomers that are the same structure rotated.
- Omitting the charge on a complex ion.
