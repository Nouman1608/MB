These notes condense subtopic **5.5 Compound shapes and parts of shapes** from Topic 5 Mensuration of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. For worked examples, read the [compound shapes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes/); to practise, use the [compound shapes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-practice/).

## The statements

1. Carry out calculations and solve problems involving perimeters and areas of **compound shapes** and **parts of shapes**.
2. Carry out calculations and solve problems involving surface areas and volumes of **compound solids** and **parts of solids**.

Answers may be in terms of π. The syllabus example is "the volume of half of a sphere" for Core and "the surface area and volume of a frustum" for Extended.

## Method

```
1. Sketch; name the pieces (e.g. "cuboid + half cylinder").
2. Work out every missing length; write it on the sketch.
3. AREA / VOLUME: add the pieces, or subtract from a bigger shape.
4. PERIMETER: trace the outside edge only.
5. SURFACE AREA: list outside faces; leave out joins.
6. Combine, then round (or simplify in terms of π).
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compound shape | A 2D shape made from two or more simple shapes |
| Compound solid | A solid made from two or more simple solids |
| Segment | Region between a chord and its arc: sector − triangle |
| Annulus | Ring between two circles with the same centre |
| Hemisphere | Half a sphere: volume (2/3)πr³, curved area 2πr² |
| Frustum | A cone or pyramid with its top cut off parallel to the base (Extended) |
| Similar solids | Same shape, all lengths in the same ratio; used to find the small cone in a frustum |

## Parts of shapes and solids

| Part | Area or volume | Perimeter or surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Semicircle | ½πr² | πr + 2r |
| Quarter circle | ¼πr² | ½πr + 2r |
| Annulus | π(R² − r²) | 2πR + 2πr |
| 90° segment | ¼πr² − ½r² | ½πr + chord |
| Solid hemisphere | (2/3)πr³ | 2πr² + πr² = 3πr² |
| Half cylinder (solid) | ½πr²h | πrh + πr² + 2rh |
| Hollow pipe | π(R² − r²) × length | (depends on which surfaces) |

## Frustum (Extended)

```
frustum volume  = big cone volume − small cone volume
small cone      = similar to big cone; scale factor = small height ÷ big height
curved surface  = πRL − πrl   (L, l = slant heights of big and small cones)
total surface   = curved surface + πR² + πr²
```

**Worked reminder.** Big cone r = 6 cm, h = 8 cm (slant 10 cm); top cone of height 4 cm removed. Scale factor ½, so the small cone has r = 3 cm, slant 5 cm.

```
volume = (1/3)π(36)(8) − (1/3)π(9)(4) = 96π − 12π = 84π cm³
curved = π(6)(10) − π(3)(5) = 45π cm²
```

## Compound solids: what counts as surface

| Solid | Faces that count |
|---|---|
| Cylinder with hemisphere on top | base circle, curved cylinder, curved hemisphere |
| Cone on top of cylinder | base circle, curved cylinder, curved cone |
| Hemisphere bowl (open) | curved surface only, 2πr² |

## Exam traps

- Counting internal lines or joins in a perimeter.
- Adding the perimeters of the pieces.
- Using the diameter as the radius for a semicircle.
- Leaving out the flat face of a hemisphere, or the chord of a segment in its perimeter.
- Including hidden join faces in the surface area of a compound solid.
- Halving the small radius of a frustum without checking the height ratio.
- Rounding intermediate lengths.

## Self-test

1. A shape is a square of side 10 cm with a semicircle on one side. Find its area in terms of π.
2. Find the perimeter of the shape in question 1, in terms of π.
3. An L-shape is a 9 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a 3 cm by 2 cm corner removed. Find its area and perimeter.
4. Find the area of a ring between circles of radii 7 cm and 5 cm, in terms of π.
5. Find the area of the segment of a quarter circle of radius 10 cm, to 3 s.f.
6. Find the volume of a solid hemisphere of radius 3 cm, in terms of π.
7. Find the total surface area of the hemisphere in question 6, in terms of π.
8. A cone of radius 4 cm and height 6 cm sits on a cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 10 cm. Find the total volume in terms of π.
9. A pipe is 50 cm long with outer radius 3 cm and inner radius 2 cm. Find the volume of material in terms of π.
10. Find the volume of a half cylinder of radius 6 cm and length 10 cm, in terms of π.
11. (Extended) A cone of height 12 cm and radius 9 cm has a cone of height 4 cm removed from the top. Find the radius of the small cone.
12. (Extended) Find the volume of the frustum in question 11 in terms of π.
