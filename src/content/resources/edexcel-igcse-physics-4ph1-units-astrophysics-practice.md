## Section A

**1.** State the unit of gravitational field strength. **[1]**

**2.** Explain the difference between mass and weight, giving the unit of each. **[2]**

**3.** A rover has a mass of 900 kg. The gravitational field strength on Mars is 3.7 N/kg. Calculate the rover's weight on Mars. **[2]**

**4.** Convert:

**(a)** 4.5 × 10⁴ km into metres **[1]**
**(b)** 2.0 days into seconds. **[1]**

**5.** Show that the unit N/kg is equivalent to m/s². **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A space probe weighs 2400 N on Earth, where g = 10 N/kg.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the probe. **[2]**

**(b)** The probe lands on the Moon, where g = 1.6 N/kg. Calculate its weight on the Moon. **[1]**

**(c)** State the mass of the probe on the Moon, with a reason. **[1]**

**7.** A space station orbits 400 km above Earth's surface. Earth's radius is 6400 km. The station takes 92 minutes to complete one orbit.

**(a)** Calculate the orbital radius in metres. **[1]**

**(b)** Convert the time period into seconds. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the orbital speed of the station. **[2]**

**8.** A moon of a giant planet has an orbital radius of 4.2 × 10⁵ km and a period of 1.8 days. Calculate its orbital speed in m/s. **[3]**

**9.** A rock is dropped from rest close to the surface of the Moon, where g = 1.6 N/kg. Ignore any atmosphere.

**(a)** State the acceleration of the rock, with its unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the rock after 3.0 s. **[2]**

**10.** A spectral line has a reference wavelength of 400 nm. In the light from a galaxy, the line is observed at 402.4 nm. Take the speed of light as 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

**(a)** Explain why the wavelengths do not need to be converted into metres. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the velocity of the galaxy. **[2]**

**11.** A student calculates a satellite's orbital speed using r = 7000 km and T = 1.6 h, and writes "v = 2π × 7000 / 1.6 = 27 489 m/s".

**(a)** Identify the student's error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct orbital speed in m/s. **[3]**

**12.** Venus orbits the Sun at a radius of 1.08 × 10¹¹ m with a period of 1.94 × 10⁷ s.

**(a)** Estimate Venus's orbital speed without a calculator, by taking 2π as 6. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the orbital speed to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** N/kg (newtons per kilogram) [1].

**2.** Mass is the amount of matter in an object, in kilograms (kg), and is the same everywhere [1]; weight is the gravitational force on the object, in newtons (N), and depends on the gravitational field strength [1].

**3.** W = m × g = 900 × 3.7 [1] = 3330 N, so **3300 N** (2 s.f.) [1].

**4. (a)** 4.5 × 10⁴ × 1000 = **4.5 × 10⁷ m** [1].
**(b)** 2.0 × 24 × 3600 = **172 800 s** (1.7 × 10⁵ s) [1].

**5.** From F = m × a, 1 N = 1 kg × 1 m/s² [1]; so 1 N/kg = 1 kg m/s² ÷ 1 kg = 1 m/s² [1].

**6. (a)** m = W / g [1] = 2400 / 10 = **240 kg** [1].
**(b)** W = 240 × 1.6 = **384 N** [1].
**(c)** 240 kg, because mass does not depend on location (only weight does) [1].

**7. (a)** r = 6400 + 400 = 6800 km = **6.8 × 10⁶ m** [1].
**(b)** T = 92 × 60 = **5520 s** [1].
**(c)** v = 2π × 6.8 × 10⁶ / 5520 [1] = **7.7 × 10³ m/s** (about 7740 m/s) [1].

**8.** r = 4.2 × 10⁸ m [1]; T = 1.8 × 86 400 = 155 520 s [1]; v = 2π × 4.2 × 10⁸ / 155 520 = **1.7 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].

**9. (a)** **1.6 m/s²** [1].
**(b)** change in velocity = acceleration × time = 1.6 × 3.0 [1] = **4.8 m/s** [1].

**10. (a)** The equation uses the ratio of two wavelengths, so the unit cancels; both just need to be in the same unit [1].
**(b)** Δλ / λ₀ = 2.4 / 400 = 0.006 [1]; v = 0.006 × 3.0 × 10⁸ = **1.8 × 10⁶ m/s** [1].

**11. (a)** The student used kilometres and hours, so the answer is in km/h, not m/s [1].
**(b)** r = 7.0 × 10⁶ m [1]; T = 1.6 × 3600 = 5760 s [1]; v = 2π × 7.0 × 10⁶ / 5760 = **7.6 × 10³ m/s** (about 7640 m/s) [1].

**12. (a)** 6 × 1.08 × 10¹¹ / 1.94 × 10⁷ ≈ 6.5 × 10¹¹ / 2 × 10⁷ ≈ **3 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].
**(b)** v = 2π × 1.08 × 10¹¹ / 1.94 × 10⁷ = **3.5 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving weight in kilograms (Questions 3 and 6).
- Using the height above the surface instead of the orbital radius (Question 7).
- Substituting km and hours directly (Question 11).
- Converting days into seconds with 60 × 60 only, forgetting the 24 hours.
- Using 402.4 nm as the denominator in the red-shift ratio (Question 10).
