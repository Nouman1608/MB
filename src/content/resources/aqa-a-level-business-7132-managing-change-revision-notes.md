Condensed notes on section **3.10.1 Managing change** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.10 is A-level only. The force field and delayering examples are in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-change/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-change-practice/).

## Specification content

- Causes of and pressures for change; types of change: internal, external, incremental, disruptive; Lewin's force field analysis.
- The value of change.
- The value of a flexible organisation: restructuring, delayering, flexible employment contracts, organic against mechanistic structures, knowledge and information management.
- The value of managing information and knowledge.
- Barriers to change: Kotter and Schlesinger's four reasons for resistance.
- How to overcome barriers: Kotter and Schlesinger's six ways.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal change | Change triggered inside the business |
| External change | Change triggered by the environment |
| Incremental change | Small, gradual adjustments |
| Disruptive change | Sudden, major change breaking with the past |
| Driving forces | Forces pushing towards change |
| Restraining forces | Forces pushing against change |
| Delayering | Removing levels of management |
| Span of control | Number of staff a manager supervises |
| Organic structure | Flat, flexible, team-based |
| Mechanistic structure | Tall, rigid, hierarchical |

## Force field analysis (Lewin)

1. List driving and restraining forces.
2. Score each (for example 1 to 5) and total.
3. If driving exceeds restraining, change is feasible.
4. Act: strengthen driving forces, weaken restraining forces, or both. Reducing restraint is often more effective than pushing harder.

Limits: scores are subjective; a snapshot; forces interact; does not say how to implement.

## Flexible organisation

| Feature | Value | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Restructuring | Fits strategy; may cut cost | Disruption; uncertainty; redundancy |
| Delayering | Lower cost; faster communication; empowerment | Heavier workloads; less support; lost promotion routes |
| Flexible contracts | Cost matches demand; skills on demand | Lower loyalty; turnover; insecurity |
| Organic structure | Adapts quickly; innovation | Less control and accountability |
| Knowledge management | Better decisions; keeps know-how | Cost; overload; hoarding; security |

Mechanistic suits stable markets. Organic suits fast-changing markets.

## Delayering calculation

Managers needed = operatives ÷ span at each level, rounded up, added together.
Example: 100 operatives, span 5: 20 + 4 + 1 = **25**. Span 10: 10 + 1 = **11**. Reduction **14**; at £40,000 each, saving **£560,000**.

## Resistance: Kotter and Schlesinger's four reasons

1. Parochial self-interest.
2. Misunderstanding.
3. Low tolerance for change.
4. Different assessments of the situation.

## Overcoming resistance: the six ways

| Approach | Best when | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Education and communication | Lack of information | Slow |
| Participation and involvement | Others have power or information | Slow; may dilute the change |
| Facilitation and support | Fear of adjustment | Costly; may fail |
| Negotiation and agreement | Losers have power | Expensive; others may ask for deals |
| Manipulation and co-optation | Other methods too costly | Mistrust if discovered |
| Explicit and implicit coercion | Speed essential; managers have power | Resentment; harms trust |

## Choosing an approach

Depends on speed required, strength of resistance, who holds power and the quality of relations. Often start soft (communicate, involve) and use harder methods last.

## Model analysis paragraph

"Using force field analysis, the driving forces total 12 and the restraining forces 9, so the change is feasible. The largest restraining force is staff fear of job losses. If management guarantees no redundancies and offers training, that force falls and the balance improves. This is more effective than pressing harder on the driving forces, which could increase resistance. Education and communication would be the first approach because the resistance comes from fear and misunderstanding."

## Command words

- **Analyse:** apply the model (Lewin or Kotter and Schlesinger) to the case.
- **Evaluate:** weigh the benefits against the cost, disruption and resistance, and judge the best approach.

## Exam traps

- Change is not always good; weigh cost, disruption and risk.
- Name Kotter and Schlesinger's approaches exactly and choose for the case.
- Delayering lowers cost but also support and promotion routes.
- Organic and mechanistic are opposites; match to the environment.
- Incremental and disruptive describe the pace and size of change, not whether it is good.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish internal from external change.
2. Give an example of disruptive change.
3. State Lewin's two types of force.
4. Driving forces score 4, 3 and 2; restraining forces score 5, 3 and 1. Is change likely to be easy?
5. Explain delayering in one sentence.
6. Give one drawback of delayering.
7. A firm has 200 operatives and a span of 5. Calculate the managers needed (all levels).
8. State two of Kotter and Schlesinger's reasons for resistance.
9. When is coercion most appropriate?
10. Give two features of an organic structure.
11. Give one value and one limit of flexible employment contracts.
12. Why is participation slower than education and communication?

### Answers

1. Internal change starts inside the business (such as a new CEO); external change comes from the environment (such as a new law).
2. For example, a new technology that makes the firm's main product obsolete.
3. Driving forces and restraining forces.
4. Driving total 9 and restraining total 9: the forces are balanced, so change will be hard unless restraints are reduced.
5. Removing one or more levels of management, so the hierarchy is flatter.
6. Any one: heavier workloads; less support; lost promotion routes; redundancy costs.
7. 200 ÷ 5 = 40; 40 ÷ 5 = 8; 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6, rounded up to 2; 2 ÷ 5 gives 1. Total = 40 + 8 + 2 + 1 = **51**.
8. Any two: parochial self-interest; misunderstanding; low tolerance for change; different assessments of the situation.
9. When speed is essential and managers hold the power, and other methods have failed or are too slow.
10. Any two: flat structure; decentralised decisions; broad roles; open communication; team-based.
11. Value: costs match demand. Limit: lower loyalty or higher turnover.
12. Because many people must be consulted and their views taken into account, which takes time.
