These questions cover sections 3.6.3 (Using electricity in the home) and 3.6.5 (Transferring electrical energy) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams from May/June 2018. The qualification is untiered. Take the mains pd as 230 V unless a question says otherwise. ## Questions

**1.** An oscilloscope trace of an ac supply shows 4 complete cycles across a time of 20 ms. Calculate the period and the frequency of the supply. **[3]**

**2.** A trace shows a 9.0 V battery and an ac supply on the same screen. The vertical scale is 3.0 V per division. The ac wave reaches 4.0 divisions above the centre line at its highest point.

**(a)** State two differences between the current from the battery and the current from the ac supply. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the peak pd of the ac supply and state how much greater it is than the battery pd. **[2]**

**3.** A toaster has a metal case, a live wire, a neutral wire and an earth wire, with a fuse in the live wire. A fault makes the live wire touch the metal case. Explain how the earth wire and the fuse make the toaster safe to touch. **[4]**

**4.** A hair dryer has a plastic body and has no earth wire.

**(a)** Explain why this is safe. **[2]**
**(b)** The household circuit also has a circuit breaker. Give two advantages of a circuit breaker over a fuse. **[2]**

**5.**

**(a)** State the main energy transfer in a loudspeaker and in an electric kettle. **[2]**
**(b)** A drill transfers 54 000 J of energy in 90 s. Calculate its power. **[3]**

**6.** An electric iron is rated at 920 W and runs from the 230 V mains. Fuses of 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available. Calculate the current in the iron and choose a fuse, giving a reason. **[5]**

**7.**

**(a)** A 9.0 V lamp has 3.0 C of charge pass through it. Calculate the energy transferred. **[3]**
**(b)** A kettle on the 230 V mains transfers 138 000 J. Calculate the charge that passes through it. **[3]**

**8.** A 60 W lamp is left on for 5.0 hours every day for 30 days. Electricity costs 30 p per kWh. Calculate the energy transferred in kWh and the cost, in pounds. **[5]**

**9.** An electricity meter reads 62581 kWh at the start of a month and 62915 kWh at the end. Electricity costs 30.0 p per kWh. Calculate the cost for the month, in pounds. **[4]**

**10.** An electric shower is rated at 8.8 kW on the 230 V mains.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the shower. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a 13 A fuse is not suitable for protecting the shower circuit. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in joules during a shower lasting 8.0 minutes. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the energy in kWh for the same shower and its cost at 30 p per kWh. **[2]**

**11.** A room heater is rated at 2.0 kW. It is used for 3.0 hours each day for 7 days. Electricity costs 28.0 p per kWh. Fuses of 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the heater on the 230 V mains. **[2]**
**(b)** Choose a suitable fuse. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in the week, in kWh. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the cost for the week, in pounds. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Time for one cycle = 20 ms / 4 = 5.0 ms [1]. Period = 5.0 ms = 0.0050 s [1]. Frequency = 1 / T = 1 / 0.0050 = **200 Hz** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert milliseconds to seconds before using f = 1 / T, and divide by the number of cycles before you quote a period.

**2. (a)** The battery current always flows in the same direction (direct current) [1]. The ac current repeatedly changes direction [1].
**(b)** Peak pd = 4.0 × 3.0 = **12 V** [1]. Difference = 12 − 9.0 = **3.0 V** greater than the battery [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both supplies mentioned. Saying only "ac changes direction" leaves the battery behaviour unstated.

**3.** The earth wire joins the metal case to earth [1]. When the live wire touches the case, a large current flows through the earth wire to earth instead of through the person [1]. The large current makes the fuse wire in the live wire overheat and melt [1]. This disconnects the toaster from the live supply, so the case is no longer live [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the sequence in order: fault, large current, fuse melts, supply disconnected. A list of safety words without that chain earns little.

**4. (a)** The plastic body and internal construction are double insulated, so the user cannot touch any live part [1]. Because the metal parts cannot become live, no earth wire is needed [1].
**(b)** It works faster than a fuse [1]. It can be reset instead of being replaced [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Safer" on its own is too vague. Name the specific advantage, such as speed or resetting.

**5. (a)** Loudspeaker: electrical to sound energy [1]. Kettle: electrical to thermal energy [1].
**(b)** P = E / t [1]. P = 54 000 / 90 [1]. P = **600 W** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the energy forms in full. "Electricity to heat" is weaker than electrical to thermal. Include the unit in the final answer.

**6.** I = P / V [1]. I = 920 / 230 [1]. I = **4.0 A** [1]. The **5 A fuse** is suitable [1] because it is the smallest rating above the 4.0 A working current, so it will not melt in normal use but will protect the wiring [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A fuse choice needs a reason that mentions the working current. Naming 5 A alone does not show the link to the calculated 4.0 A.

**7. (a)** E = Q × V [1]. E = 3.0 × 9.0 [1]. E = **27 J** [1].
**(b)** Q = E / V [1]. Q = 138 000 / 230 [1]. Q = **600 C** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before substituting in part (b). Writing the rearranged form shows the method even if the arithmetic slips.

**8.** Power = 60 W = 0.060 kW [1]. Time = 5.0 × 30 = 150 h [1]. Energy = 0.060 × 150 = **9.0 kWh** [1]. Cost = 9.0 × 30 = 270 p [1]. Cost = **£2.70** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion from watts to kilowatts as its own line. A final answer in pence when pounds are requested loses the last step.

**9.** Units used = 62915 − 62581 = **334 kWh** [1]. Cost = 334 × 30.0 [1] = 10 020 p [1]. Cost = **£100.20** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the earlier reading from the later one, then multiply. Giving a number of pence without converting to pounds does not answer the question.

**10. (a)** I = P / V = 8800 / 230 [1]. I = **38 A** (38.3 A) [1].
**(b)** The current of about 38 A is far greater than 13 A, so a 13 A fuse would melt as soon as the shower was switched on [1].
**(c)** Time = 8.0 × 60 = 480 s [1]. E = P × t = 8800 × 480 [1]. E = **4 224 000 J** (about 4.2 MJ) [1].
**(d)** E = 8.8 × (8.0 / 60) = **1.17 kWh** [1]. Cost = 1.173 × 30 = **35 p** (35.2 p) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick the unit system first and stay in it. Use watts with seconds for joules, or kilowatts with hours for kWh, and do not mix them in one line.

**11. (a)** I = P / V = 2000 / 230 [1]. I = **8.7 A** [1].
**(b)** **13 A** fuse, because 8.7 A is above 5 A [1].
**(c)** Time = 3.0 × 7 = 21 h [1]. E = 2.0 × 21 = **42 kWh** [1].
**(d)** Cost = 42 × 28.0 = 1176 p [1]. Cost = **£11.76** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) follows from your part (a) answer. State the working current in your reason so the link to the fuse rating is clear.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using trough-to-peak instead of the zero line for peak pd on an ac trace.
- Leaving the period in milliseconds when using f = 1 / T.
- Describing safety features as "stopping electricity" without the sequence of fault, large current, fuse melts, supply disconnected.
- Placing the fuse in the wrong wire, or forgetting that it goes in the live wire.
- Choosing a fuse that equals the working current, or giving no reason for the choice.
- Treating double insulation as having an earth wire hidden inside.
- Mixing kW with seconds or W with hours in the same calculation.
- Forgetting to multiply the hours by the number of days.
- Giving the cost in pence when the question asks for pounds.
