## The subtopic in five lines

1. Alkanes are fuels; combustion of any organic compound can be **complete** (CO₂ + H₂O) or **incomplete** (CO and/or C + H₂O).
2. Engines emit **NOₓ, CO, carbon and unburned hydrocarbons**.
3. **Catalytic converters** remove the gaseous pollutants by converting them to CO₂, N₂ and H₂O.
4. Burning sulfur-containing fuels gives **SO₂**, an air pollutant.
5. **CaO or CaCO₃** removes SO₂ from flue gases because they are bases and SO₂ is an acidic oxide.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Fuel | Substance burned to release energy |
| Complete combustion | Burning in excess oxygen; carbon becomes CO₂ |
| Incomplete combustion | Burning in limited oxygen; carbon becomes CO or C |
| NOₓ | Nitrogen oxides, NO and NO₂ |
| Catalytic converter | Exhaust device in which a hot catalyst converts CO, NOₓ and hydrocarbons into less harmful gases |
| Flue gas | Waste gas from a power station or factory chimney |
| Acidic oxide | Oxide that reacts with bases (SO₂) |
| Basic oxide | Oxide that reacts with acids (CaO) |

## Balancing combustion: C, then H, then O

| Fuel | Equation |
|---|---|
| Butane, complete | C₄H₁₀ + 6½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O |
| Butane, to CO | C₄H₁₀ + 4½O₂ → 4CO + 5H₂O |
| Butane, to C | C₄H₁₀ + 2½O₂ → 4C + 5H₂O |
| Propan-1-ol, complete | C₃H₇OH + 4½O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| Pentane, complete | C₅H₁₂ + 8O₂ → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O |

For oxygen-containing fuels, subtract the fuel's own O atoms before halving.

**Gas volumes:** at the same temperature and pressure, volumes react in the ratio of the coefficients. After cooling to room temperature, water is liquid and its volume is ignored. NaOH(aq) absorbs CO₂.

## Engine pollutants

| Pollutant | Origin | Converter reaction |
|---|---|---|
| NO, NO₂ | N₂ + O₂ from air at high engine temperature | reduced to N₂ (2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂) |
| CO | incomplete combustion | oxidised to CO₂ |
| Unburned hydrocarbons | fuel that did not burn | oxidised to CO₂ + H₂O (for example by NO) |
| Carbon particles | incomplete combustion | solid; not removed as a gas |

**Converter design:** ceramic honeycomb support with a thin coating of Pt and Rh; large surface area, low cost; heterogeneous catalysis on the hot surface.

**Hydrocarbon + NO equation pattern:** count O atoms needed for CO₂ and H₂O; that is the number of NO; halve it for N₂.

Example: C₇H₁₆ + 22NO → 7CO₂ + 8H₂O + 11N₂.

## Sulfur dioxide

```
S + O₂ → SO₂
CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃
CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂
```

- SO₂ is acidic; CaO is a basic oxide; CaCO₃ is a base (it reacts with acids).
- The SO₂ is neutralised and trapped as solid calcium sulfite.
- Mass calculations: 1 mol S → 1 mol SO₂ → 1 mol CaO or CaCO₃.

## Exam traps

- Subtract the oxygen in the fuel (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones).
- Half-numbers of O₂ are fine; doubling everything is also fine.
- No H₂ in combustion products.
- Nitrogen in NOₓ comes from air, not fuel.
- Converters change pollutants by reaction; they do not filter or store them.
- Converters do not remove CO₂.
- "CaO absorbs SO₂" earns little without "acidic oxide" and "base".
- Ignore the volume of liquid water in gas-volume problems.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Write an equation for the complete combustion of hexane.
2. Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of methane to carbon monoxide and water.
3. Write an equation for the complete combustion of methanol, CH₃OH.
4. Why is carbon monoxide toxic?
5. Explain how NO forms in a car engine.
6. Write the converter equation for the removal of CO and NO together.
7. Balance: C₅H₁₂ + ?NO → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O + ?N₂.
8. Why is the catalyst in a converter spread on a honeycomb?
9. Explain why calcium oxide can remove SO₂ from flue gases.
10. Write the equation for calcium carbonate removing SO₂.
11. 10 cm³ of ethane burns completely. What volume of oxygen is needed, and what volume of CO₂ forms (same conditions)?
12. Calculate the mass of CaO (Mr 56.1) needed to remove 1.00 tonne of SO₂ (Mr 64.1).

### Answers

1. C₆H₁₄ + 9½O₂ → 6CO₂ + 7H₂O.
2. CH₄ + 1½O₂ → CO + 2H₂O.
3. CH₃OH + 1½O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
4. It replaces oxygen co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II) in haemoglobin, so less oxygen is carried in the blood.
5. At the very high temperature in the cylinder, nitrogen and oxygen from the air combine: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO.
6. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
7. O atoms: 10 + 6 = 16, so 16NO and 8N₂.
8. To give a large surface area of catalyst while using little of the expensive metal.
9. SO₂ is an acidic oxide and CaO is a basic oxide, so they react (neutralisation) to form solid CaSO₃.
10. CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂.
11. C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O: 35 cm³ of O₂ used; 20 cm³ of CO₂ formed.
12. 1.00 × 56.1 ÷ 64.1 = 0.875 tonnes.
