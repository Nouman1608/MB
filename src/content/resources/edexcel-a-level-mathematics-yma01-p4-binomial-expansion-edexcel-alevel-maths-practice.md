These questions cover section 4 of Unit P4: Pure Mathematics 4 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): the binomial series for any rational n, expansions of (a + bx)ⁿ with their ranges of validity, and expansions of rational functions using partial fractions. Calculators may be used, but show every coefficient.

Related: [binomial expansion study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p4-binomial-expansion-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [binomial expansion revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p4-binomial-expansion-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Expand (1 − 2x)^(−1/2) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³, and state the values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[4]**

**2.** Find the coefficient of x² in the expansion of (1 + x/3)^(3/2), giving your answer as a simplified fraction. **[2]**

**3.** Find the first three terms, in ascending powers of x, of the expansion of (8 + 3x)^(1/3), and state the range of validity. **[4]**

**4.** Find the expansion of (1 + 2x)/(1 − x)² in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³. **[3]**

**5.** In the expansion of (1 + ax)⁻³ the coefficient of x is 12. Find the value of a and the coefficient of x². **[3]**

**6.** State the range of values of x for which the expansion of (3 − 4x)⁻¹ is valid, and explain whether the expansion could be used with x = 0.8. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Expand √(1 − 4x) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³. **[3]**

**(b)** By substituting x = 0.01 into your expansion, find an approximation to √6, giving your answer to 5 significant figures. Show your working. **[3]**

**8.** f(x) = (4 − x)/((1 + 2x)(1 − x)).

**(a)** Express f(x) in partial fractions. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence find the expansion of f(x) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³, simplifying each coefficient. **[4]**

**(c)** State the range of values of x for which your expansion is valid. **[1]**

**9.** g(x) = (x + 5)/(1 + x)².

**(a)** Express g(x) in the form A/(1 + x) + B/(1 + x)², where A and B are constants. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence, or otherwise, expand g(x) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³. **[3]**

**10.** The expansion of (1 + ax)ⁿ in ascending powers of x begins 1 − 2x + 6x² + cx³, where a, n and c are constants and n is not a positive integer.

**(a)** Find the values of n and a. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the value of c. **[2]**

**(c)** State the range of values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Show that the expansion of (3 + x)/(2 − x)² in ascending powers of x, up to and including the term in x², is 3/4 + x + (13/16)x². **[5]**

**(b)** State the range of values of x for which this expansion is valid. **[1]**

**12.** **(a)** Expand (1 + x)^(1/3) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x³. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your expansion with x = 1/8 to show that the cube root of 9 is approximately 2.08010. **[3]**

**(c)** The cube root of 9 is 2.080084 to 7 significant figures. Find the percentage error in the approximation from part (b), to 1 significant figure. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Using n = −½ with x replaced by −2x: 1 + (−½)(−2x) + [(−½)(−3/2)/2](−2x)² + [(−½)(−3/2)(−5/2)/6](−2x)³ [1].

= **1 + x** [1] **+ (3/2)x² + (5/2)x³** [1].

Valid for |2x| < 1, so **|x| < ½** [1].

**2.** Coefficient = [(3/2)(1/2)/2] × (1/3)² [1] = (3/8)(1/9) = **1/24** [1].

**3.** (8 + 3x)^(1/3) = 8^(1/3)(1 + 3x/8)^(1/3) = 2(1 + 3x/8)^(1/3) [1].

= 2[1 + (1/3)(3x/8) + ((1/3)(−2/3)/2)(3x/8)²] [1] = 2[1 + x/8 − x²/64] = **2 + x/4 − x²/32** [1].

Valid for |3x/8| < 1, so **|x| < 8/3** [1].

**4.** (1 − x)⁻² = 1 + 2x + 3x² + 4x³ + … [1].

(1 + 2x)(1 + 2x + 3x² + 4x³) [1] = **1 + 4x + 7x² + 10x³** [1].

**5.** (1 + ax)⁻³ = 1 − 3ax + 6a²x² + …, so −3a = 12 [1] and **a = −4** [1].

Coefficient of x² = 6 × (−4)² = **96** [1].

**6.** Valid for |4x/3| < 1, so **|x| < 3/4** [1]. Since 0.8 > 0.75, x = 0.8 lies **outside the range of validity**, so the series does not converge there and cannot be used [1].

**7. (a)** (1 − 4x)^(1/2) = 1 + (½)(−4x) + [(½)(−½)/2](−4x)² + [(½)(−½)(−3/2)/6](−4x)³ [1].

= **1 − 2x − 2x² − 4x³** [2] (one mark for any two correct non-constant terms).

**(b)** x = 0.01 gives √0.96 ≈ 1 − 0.02 − 0.0002 − 0.000004 = 0.979796 [1].

√0.96 = √96/10 = 4√6/10, so √6 = 2.5√0.96 [1].

√6 ≈ 2.5 × 0.979796 = 2.44949, so **√6 ≈ 2.4495** [1].

**8. (a)** 4 − x = A(1 − x) + B(1 + 2x) [1].

x = 1: 3 = 3B, so B = 1. x = −½: 4.5 = 1.5A, so A = 3 [1].

**f(x) = 3/(1 + 2x) + 1/(1 − x)** [1].

**(b)** 3(1 + 2x)⁻¹ = 3(1 − 2x + 4x² − 8x³) = 3 − 6x + 12x² − 24x³ [1].

(1 − x)⁻¹ = 1 + x + x² + x³ [1].

Adding: f(x) = **4 − 5x + 13x² − 23x³** [2] (one mark for two correct terms).

**(c)** The first series needs |x| < ½ and the second |x| < 1, so **|x| < ½** [1].

**9. (a)** x + 5 = A(1 + x) + B. Comparing x terms: A = 1 [1]. Constants: 1 + B = 5, so B = 4. **g(x) = 1/(1 + x) + 4/(1 + x)²** [1].

**(b)** (1 + x)⁻¹ = 1 − x + x² − x³ [1].

4(1 + x)⁻² = 4(1 − 2x + 3x² − 4x³) = 4 − 8x + 12x² − 16x³ [1].

g(x) = **5 − 9x + 13x² − 17x³** [1].

**10. (a)** na = −2 [1] and n(n − 1)a²/2 = 6 [1].

Squaring the first gives n²a² = 4. Dividing: (n − 1)/(2n) = 6/4, so 2(n − 1) = 6n and **n = −½** [1].

Then a = −2 ÷ (−½), so **a = 4** [1].

**(b)** c = [n(n − 1)(n − 2)/6]a³ = [(−½)(−3/2)(−5/2)/6] × 64 [1] = (−15/48) × 64 = **−20** [1].

**(c)** |4x| < 1, so **|x| < ¼** [1].

**11. (a)** (2 − x)⁻² = 2⁻²(1 − x/2)⁻² = ¼(1 − x/2)⁻² [1].

(1 − x/2)⁻² = 1 + (−2)(−x/2) + [(−2)(−3)/2](−x/2)² = 1 + x + (3/4)x² [1].

So (2 − x)⁻² = 1/4 + x/4 + (3/16)x² [1].

(3 + x)(1/4 + x/4 + (3/16)x²) = 3/4 + (3/4)x + (9/16)x² + (1/4)x + (1/4)x² [1].

= **3/4 + x + (13/16)x²** as required [1].

**(b)** |x/2| < 1, so **|x| < 2** [1].

**12. (a)** (1 + x)^(1/3) = **1 + x/3 − x²/9 + (5/81)x³** [2] (one mark for the first three terms correct).

**(b)** 1 + 1/8 = 9/8, so (9/8)^(1/3) = (cube root of 9)/2, and the cube root of 9 = 2 × (1.125)^(1/3) [1].

x = 0.125: 1 + 0.04166667 − 0.00173611 + 0.00012056 = 1.04005112 [1].

Cube root of 9 ≈ 2 × 1.04005112 = 2.0801022, which is **2.08010** to 6 significant figures [1].

**(c)** Percentage error = (2.0801022 − 2.080084)/2.080084 × 100 [1] = **0.0009%** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 8(1 + 3x/8)^(1/3) instead of 2(1 + 3x/8)^(1/3), forgetting to apply the power to the factor taken out.
- Dropping the brackets round −2x or −x/2, which flips the sign of the odd-power terms.
- Stating every range of validity as |x| < 1.
- Giving the wider of two validity ranges when series are added.
- In an approximation, not writing down how the estimate links to the number asked for.
- Leaving coefficients as unsimplified products such as (−½)(−3/2)/2.
