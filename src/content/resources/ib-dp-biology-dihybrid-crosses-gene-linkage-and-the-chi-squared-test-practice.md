Critical values for χ² at p = 0.05: 1 degree of freedom 3.84; 2 degrees 5.99; 3 degrees 7.81; 4 degrees 9.49.

## Questions

**1.** A cell of an insect has 2n = 10 chromosomes. Explain how meiosis I produces independent assortment of genes on different chromosomes, and state how many different combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes are possible in the gametes, ignoring crossing over. **[3]**

**2.** A plant has the genotype GgRr. The genes for G and R are on different chromosomes.

**(a)** State the four types of gamete it makes and the proportion of each. **[2]**
**(b)** The plant is crossed with another GgRr plant. State the number of different genotypes among the offspring. **[1]**
**(c)** State the fraction of offspring that are GGRR. **[1]**

**3.** In an invented bird species, a crest (C) is dominant to a plain head (c), and green plumage (Y) is dominant to yellow plumage (y). The genes are on different autosomes. Two birds of genotype CcYy are crossed.

**(a)** Construct a Punnett grid and state the phenotypic ratio of the offspring. **[3]**
**(b)** From 320 offspring, calculate the expected number with a plain head and yellow plumage. **[2]**

**4.** A CcYy bird is crossed with a ccyy bird. The genes are unlinked.

**(a)** State the expected phenotypic ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how this ratio arises. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says that a 1:1:1:1 result proves the two genes are on different chromosomes. Suggest why this is not certain. **[1]**

**5.** A student uses a database to record the loci of four human genes.

| Gene | Polypeptide product | Locus |
|---|---|---|
| HBA1 | alpha-globin | 16p13.3 |
| HBB | beta-globin | 11p15.4 |
| HBD | delta-globin | 11p15.4 |
| CFTR | membrane channel for chloride and bicarbonate ions | 7q31.2 |

**(a)** Identify one pair of genes expected to assort independently and give the reason. **[2]**
**(b)** HBB and HBD are close together on one chromosome. Predict how their alleles will be inherited and explain. **[2]**

**6.** In an invented plant, tall stem (T) is dominant to dwarf (t), and round pollen (R) is dominant to oval (r). The genes are on the same autosome. A plant with alleles TR on one chromosome and tr on the other was crossed with a plant homozygous recessive for both genes. The 600 offspring were: tall round 262, dwarf oval 258, tall oval 41, dwarf round 39.

**(a)** Show the genotype of the heterozygous parent using vertical lines for the chromosomes, and state its four possible gametes. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the recombinant offspring and calculate the recombinant frequency. **[3]**
**(c)** Another heterozygote has the alleles Tr on one chromosome and tR on the other. State the two recombinant phenotypes in its test cross. **[2]**

**7.**
**(a)** Explain how recombinant gametes arise from linked genes. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the recombinant frequency in a test cross cannot be higher than 50%. **[2]**

**8.** Two plants heterozygous for two unlinked genes were crossed. Among 600 offspring the phenotypes were 330, 121, 106 and 43 in the order dominant-dominant, dominant-recessive, recessive-dominant, recessive-recessive. A chi-squared test is used to compare with 9:3:3:1.

**(a)** State the null hypothesis. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the expected numbers. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate χ². **[2]**
**(d)** State the conclusion at p = 0.05. **[2]**

**9.** A double heterozygote with alleles AB on one chromosome and ab on the other was test crossed. The 1000 offspring were: AB 392, ab 371, Ab 118, aB 119. The genes are autosomal.

**(a)** State the expected numbers if the genes assort independently. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate χ². **[2]**
**(c)** State and explain the conclusion at p = 0.05. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the recombinant frequency. **[1]**

**10.**
**(a)** State what is meant by a result being statistically significant at the p = 0.05 level. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the F2 generation of a cross is described as a sample. **[1]**

**11.** Two test crosses were carried out. In each, a double heterozygote with both dominant alleles on one chromosome was crossed with a double homozygous recessive. Cross 1 involves genes P and Q. Cross 2 involves genes R and S.

| Cross | Parental class 1 | Parental class 2 | Other class 1 | Other class 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 121 | 118 | 126 | 115 |
| 2 | 239 | 249 | 46 | 46 |

**(a)** Calculate χ² for each cross against 1:1:1:1. **[4]**
**(b)** Draw a conclusion for each cross at p = 0.05. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the recombinant frequency for cross 2. **[1]**
**(d)** The 1:1:1:1 and 9:3:3:1 ratios are called Mendel's second law. Discuss, using these data, whether it should be called a law. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** In metaphase I the five homologous pairs line up at the equator as bivalents [1]. Each pair faces the poles in either orientation, independent of the others, so alleles on different chromosomes are sorted independently [1]. Combinations = 2⁵ = **32** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit for naming the stage (metaphase I) and for saying the orientation of each pair is independent; "chromosomes mix up" is too vague.

**2. (a)** **GR, Gr, gR, gr** [1], each **¼** [1]
**(b)** **9** [1]
**(c)** ¼ × ¼ = **1/16** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a gamete list, show every allele pairing once; state the proportion of each type as well as listing the types.

**3. (a)** Gametes from each parent: CY, Cy, cY, cy [1]. A 4 × 4 grid with 16 correct cells [1]. Ratio **9 crested green : 3 crested yellow : 3 plain green : 1 plain yellow** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Plain and yellow is 1/16 [1]; 320 ÷ 16 = **20** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Label gamete types around the grid and say which phenotype each combination gives; a bare ratio without a visible grid or reasoning is less secure.

**4. (a)** **1:1:1:1** [1]
**(b)** The ccyy bird makes only cy gametes [1]. The CcYy bird makes CY, Cy, cY, cy in equal numbers, so the four phenotypes appear equally [1]
**(c)** Genes far apart on the same chromosome also give 1:1:1:1 because recombinant frequency reaches 50% [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mention independent assortment or 50% recombination to explain why equal classes occur.

**5. (a)** HBA1 and HBB (or CFTR with any other) [1]; they are on different chromosomes (16 and 11), so homologous pairs orient independently in metaphase I [1]
**(b)** They are likely to be inherited together [1]; the closer two loci are on a chromosome, the less likely a chiasma forms between them [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the chromosome numbers from the table when reasoning.

**6. (a)** **TR | tr** [1]. Gametes **TR, tr, Tr, tR** [1]
**(b)** Recombinants have a combination not on either parental chromosome: tall oval and dwarf round [1]. 41 + 39 = 80 [1]. 80/600 × 100 = **13.3%** [1]
**(c)** Parental gametes are now Tr and tR, so the recombinants are **tall round (TR) and dwarf oval (tr)** [2]
*Examiner insight:* State the parental arrangement first. Do not assume the two largest classes are always parental.

**7. (a)** In prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair as bivalents [1]. Non-sister chromatids exchange segments at a chiasma between the two loci [1]. The chromatids now carry new allele combinations, which pass into gametes [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Even if a chiasma forms between the loci in every meiosis, only two of the four chromatids are recombinant [1], so at most half the gametes, 50%, are recombinant [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "non-sister chromatids" and "between the two loci"; crossing over elsewhere on the chromosome does not create a recombinant for these genes.

**8. (a)** There is no significant difference between the observed and expected numbers; any difference is due to chance [1]
**(b)** 600 × 9/16, 3/16, 3/16, 1/16 = **337.5, 112.5, 112.5, 37.5** [1]
**(c)** χ² = 56.25/337.5 + 72.25/112.5 + 42.25/112.5 + 30.25/37.5 [1] = 0.167 + 0.642 + 0.376 + 0.807 = **1.99** [1]
**(d)** 3 degrees of freedom, critical value 7.81; 1.99 < 7.81 [1]. Do not reject the null hypothesis: the results fit 9:3:3:1, consistent with independent assortment [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the expected numbers and the degrees of freedom; a conclusion in words about the genes, not only "not significant", completes the answer.

**9. (a)** 1000 ÷ 4 = **250** in each class [1]
**(b)** χ² = (392 − 250)²/250 + (371 − 250)²/250 + (118 − 250)²/250 + (119 − 250)²/250 [1] = 80.66 + 58.56 + 69.70 + 68.64 = **277.6** [1]
**(c)** 277.6 is much greater than the critical value 7.81 (3 degrees of freedom) [1]. Reject the null hypothesis: the difference is significant, so the genes are not assorting independently and are probably linked [1]
**(d)** (118 + 119)/1000 × 100 = **23.7%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the critical value you compare with, and avoid saying linkage is "proved": the test shows chance alone is an unlikely explanation.

**10. (a)** If the null hypothesis were true, results at least this different from those expected would occur by chance less than 5% of the time [1]. So the difference is unlikely to be due to chance alone, and the null hypothesis is rejected [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The F2 offspring are a limited group that represents all the offspring the cross could produce, the population [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link p = 0.05 to the probability of chance; saying "95% certain the hypothesis is true" is a common misstatement.

**11. (a)** Cross 1: expected 480 ÷ 4 = 120. χ² = (1 + 4 + 36 + 25)/120 [1] = **0.55** [1]. Cross 2: expected 580 ÷ 4 = 145. χ² = (94² + 104² + 99² + 99²)/145 [1] = 39 254/145 = **270.7** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Cross 1: 0.55 < 7.81, so not significant; consistent with independent assortment [1]. Cross 2: 270.7 > 7.81, so significant; the genes are linked [1] **[2]**
**(c)** (46 + 46)/580 × 100 = **15.9%** [1]
**(d)** Cross 1 follows the ratio, but cross 2 does not [1]. The ratios hold only for genes on different chromosomes or far apart, so there are exceptions and "law" should be applied with that condition [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In a discuss question, use both data sets and state the condition under which the second law holds.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing only two gametes for a dihybrid.
- Calling the largest classes recombinant when the parental arrangement is the opposite one.
- Giving recombinant frequency as a fraction of the recombinants instead of the total offspring.
- Using the wrong degrees of freedom, such as the number of classes.
- Concluding "the results are significant" without a comparison with a critical value.
- Stating the null hypothesis as the explanation, such as "the genes are linked".
- Saying the null hypothesis is "accepted", or that linkage is "proved".
