## Section A

**1.** Define the term *urbanisation*. **[1]**

**2.** State the meaning of *rural depopulation*. **[1]**

**3.** Give **one** physical, **one** economic and **one** social push factor
that may cause people to leave rural areas. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why natural increase is often high in rapidly growing cities in
developing countries. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** positive impacts of rural–urban migration on the rural
areas that migrants leave. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows **illustrative data** for two cities in developing
countries.

| City | Population in 2000 (millions) | Population in 2020 (millions) |
|---|---|---|
| City P | 4.0 | 7.0 |
| City Q | 6.0 | 9.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in population of City P between
2000 and 2020. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Which city grew by the greater percentage? Support your answer with
figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons why a city may grow rapidly. **[2]**

**7.** A photograph shows homes made of corrugated iron and wood built on a
steep hillside above a city, with narrow paths, overhead wires and rubbish in
a drainage channel.

**(a)** Describe **three** characteristics of this squatter settlement shown
in the description. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest **two** hazards faced by people living on this hillside.
**[2]**
**(c)** Explain why people choose to live in settlements like this. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **three** impacts of rapid urban growth on the natural
environment. **[6]**

**9.** Compare a site-and-service scheme with an in-situ upgrading scheme as
ways of improving housing in a developing city. **[4]**

**10.** For a named rapidly growing urban area in a developing country, describe
the causes of migration to it and evaluate strategies used to reduce the
negative impacts of its growth. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** An increase in the proportion of a country's population living in
urban areas [1].

**2.** A decrease in the number of people living in rural areas, mainly as a
result of out-migration [1].

**3.** Physical: drought, desertification, floods, soil erosion or crop
failure [1]. Economic: low or unreliable farm incomes, mechanisation reducing
farm jobs, or small landholdings [1]. Social: lack of schools, clinics or
electricity, or conflict [1].

**4.** Most migrants are young adults of childbearing age [1], so birth rates
are high while death rates are relatively low because of the young age
structure and better access to health care in cities [1].

**5.** Any two of: remittances sent home increase family incomes [1]; less
pressure on land and food supply [1]; returning migrants bring new skills and
ideas [1]; reduced rural unemployment [1].

**6. (a)** Increase = 7.0 − 4.0 = 3.0 million [1]; 3.0 ÷ 4.0 × 100 = **75%**
[1].
**(b)** City P [1]: it grew by 75%, whereas City Q grew by 3.0 ÷ 6.0 × 100 =
50%, even though both added 3 million people [1].
**(c)** Any two of: high rural–urban migration due to push and pull factors
[1]; high natural increase because of the young population [1]; the city
offers jobs in industry and services, attracting workers [1]; physical
hazards or conflict in rural areas drive people out [1].

**7. (a)** Any three of: homes built from scrap or low-cost materials such as
corrugated iron and wood [1]; built on a steep hillside, which is marginal
land [1]; narrow paths rather than paved roads [1]; informal electricity
connections [1]; poor waste disposal, with rubbish in drains [1]; high
density of housing [1].
**(b)** Any two of: landslides, especially after heavy rain [1]; flash
flooding or blocked drains overflowing [1]; fire spreading between closely
packed homes [1]; disease from contaminated water and rubbish [1];
electrocution from informal wiring [1].
**(c)** Migrants arriving in the city cannot afford formal housing [1], so they
build on land that is free or not in use [1]; the hillside may be close to
jobs in the city centre, so travel costs are low, and relatives or people from
the same region may already live there [1].

**8.** Three explained impacts, 2 marks each: **air pollution**, as more
vehicles and factories release exhaust and smoke, harming health [2]; **water
pollution**, as untreated sewage and industrial waste enter rivers and the
sea, killing aquatic life [2]; **loss of farmland, forest or wetland** as the
city sprawls outwards, destroying habitats [2]; **solid waste** dumped on open
land or burned, contaminating soil and air [2]; **increased flooding**,
because concrete and tarmac reduce infiltration so runoff is faster [2];
**landslides** where slopes are cleared and built on [2].

**9.** Up to 4 marks for valid comparison, which must refer to both schemes:
- Site-and-service provides **new plots** with water, sewerage and roads for
  residents to build on, whereas upgrading improves an **existing**
  settlement where people already live [1].
- Upgrading keeps communities and job links intact, whereas site-and-service
  plots may be on the city edge, far from work [1].
- Both rely on residents building or improving homes themselves, which keeps
  costs low [1].
- Upgrading can be expensive in dense, hillside settlements, while
  site-and-service plots may still cost too much for the poorest migrants [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking:
- **Level 3 (6–7 marks):** a named city with place-specific detail; causes of
  migration explained, including both push and pull factors; at least two
  strategies described and evaluated with a judgement.
- **Level 2 (3–5 marks):** causes and strategies described with some detail,
  but limited evaluation or limited reference to the named place.
- **Level 1 (1–2 marks):** generic statements about migration or slums with
  little or no place detail.

Example of Level 3 content (Karachi): migrants pulled by port, industrial and
service jobs, and pushed from rural areas by low farm incomes, small
landholdings, floods and poor services; growth of large katchi abadis such as
Orangi Town; the Orangi Pilot Project (from 1980) helped residents build
low-cost sewers lane by lane, which improved sanitation at low cost but relies
on community organisation; regularising land tenure gives residents security
to invest in their homes but is slow; overall judgement on which strategy has
done most and why.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Questions on 1.7 often ask you to cover **both rural and urban areas**, or
**both people and the environment**. Underline these words in the question and
make sure each one appears in your answer. For data questions, show your
working and be precise about percentages versus percentage points.
