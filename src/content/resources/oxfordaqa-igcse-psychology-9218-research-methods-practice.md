## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following best describes a field experiment?
A The IV occurs naturally and the researcher only measures the DV
B The IV is manipulated by the researcher in an everyday setting
C Participants are observed without any variable being changed
D The IV is manipulated by the researcher in a controlled setting **[1]**

**2.** Explain the difference between random sampling and random allocation. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** ethical issues in the BPS guidelines and state how a researcher could deal with **each**. **[4]**

**4.** A researcher selects every fifth name from a school register. Identify the sampling method and give **one** weakness of it. **[2]**

**5.** A company has 120 office staff and 80 warehouse staff. A psychologist wants a stratified sample of 30. Calculate how many warehouse staff should be selected. Show your working. **[2]**

## Section B — Structured questions

**Use the following information to answer Questions 6 to 13.**

> A psychologist investigated whether the time of day affects short-term memory. Twenty students from
> her college took part. Each student completed a digit-span test at 9 am and again at 4 pm, using a
> different set of digits each time. The number of digits each student correctly recalled in order was
> recorded. The study took place in the same quiet room each time.

**6.** Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable in this study. **[2]**

**7.** Write a suitable alternative hypothesis for this study. **[2]**

**8.** Write a suitable null hypothesis for this study. **[1]**

**9.** Identify the experimental design used and explain **one** weakness of this design for this study. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how the psychologist could use counterbalancing in this study. **[2]**

**11.** Identify **two** extraneous variables that could affect the results, and explain how the psychologist could control **one** of them. **[3]**

**12.** The twenty students were the first twenty who replied to an email. Explain **one** reason why this sample may not be representative of all students at the college. **[2]**

**13.** The psychologist decides to repeat the study using an independent groups design. Explain **one** strength **and** **one** weakness of this change. **[4]**

**14.** Design a laboratory experiment to test whether people perceive pictures of food as brighter when they are hungry. Include the IV, the DV, the experimental design, **one** control and **one** ethical issue. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Random sampling is a way of **selecting participants from the target population**, giving everyone an equal chance of being chosen [1]. Random allocation is a way of **putting participants into conditions** so each has an equal chance of being in either condition [1].

**3.** Any two issues, 1 for the issue and 1 for the matching way of dealing with it: **informed consent** — use a consent form explaining the study [1] [1]; **right to withdraw** — tell participants at the start that they can leave and remove their data [1] [1]. (Also accept: deception — debrief fully; protection from harm — stop if distressed; confidentiality — use numbers not names.)

**4.** **Systematic sampling** [1]. Weakness: if the register has a pattern (for example, arranged by ability), the sample could be **unrepresentative** (accept: needs a complete list) [1].

**5.** 80 ÷ 200 = 0.4, or 40% [1]. 0.4 × 30 = **12 warehouse staff** [1].

**6.** IV: **the time of day** (9 am or 4 pm) [1]. DV: **the number of digits correctly recalled in order** [1].

**7.** For example: "Students will correctly recall **more digits in order at 9 am than at 4 pm**" [1] (accept a two-tailed version: "There will be a difference in the number of digits correctly recalled in order at 9 am and 4 pm") with **both conditions and a measurable DV** stated [1].

**8.** "There will be **no difference** in the number of digits correctly recalled in order between 9 am and 4 pm" [1].

**9.** **Repeated measures** [1]. Weakness: **order effects** [1] — for example, students may do better at 4 pm simply because they have **practised** the digit-span test at 9 am, so practice rather than time of day could explain the difference (accept boredom or fatigue) [1].

**10.** Half the students should do the test at 9 am first and 4 pm on a later day [1], while the other half do 4 pm first and 9 am later, so **practice effects are spread evenly** across both conditions [1].

**11.** Any two extraneous variables, 1 mark each: **how much sleep** the students had; **caffeine**; **whether they had just had a lesson or exam**; **difficulty of the two digit sets** [1] [1]. Control: for example, ensure **both digit sets are of equal length and difficulty** (or ask students not to have caffeine beforehand) [1].

**12.** It is an **opportunity sample**: students who reply quickly to emails may be more **organised or motivated** than others [1], so their memory performance may not be typical of all students, and the results may not **generalise** [1].

**13.** Strength: **no order effects**, because each student is tested only once, so practice cannot explain any difference [1] [1]. Weakness: **participant variables** — the 9 am group might simply have better short-term memories than the 4 pm group [1], so differences may be due to the people rather than the time of day [1].

**14.** Award 1 mark for each element, up to 6:
- **IV:** hunger — participants who have not eaten for several hours versus participants who have just eaten [1].
- **DV:** brightness rating of food pictures on a scale (for example 1-10) [1].
- **Design:** independent groups, so participants cannot guess the aim from doing both conditions [1].
- **Random allocation** of participants to the hungry or fed condition [1].
- **Control:** the same pictures, shown on the same screen at the same brightness, with standardised instructions [1].
- **Ethics:** protection from harm — the period without food is kept short and safe, and participants can withdraw and are given food afterwards (accept informed consent with a correct method) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a hypothesis that does not say how the DV is measured.
- Naming "order effects" without explaining what they would do in the given study.
- Confusing random allocation with random sampling.
- Identifying extraneous variables that would not actually affect digit span.
- Giving a design without a reason linked to the scenario.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is research methods examined on both papers in 9218?**
Yes. Section D of each paper is research methods, and the specification states that the Paper 2 topic contains the same material as Paper 1.

**Do I need to calculate anything in research methods?**
Yes. Data handling includes percentages, ratios, fractions, means, medians, modes and ranges, and sample-size calculations like the stratified example above are a common application.

**How detailed should a design answer be?**
Each decision should be specific to the scenario, such as the actual IV conditions and how the DV is measured, and ideally include a brief reason.
