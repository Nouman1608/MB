## The specification in brief

- What business aims and business objectives are.
- When starting up: financial (survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security) and non-financial (social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control).
- Why aims and objectives differ between businesses.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aim | A long-term, general goal for the business |
| Objective | A specific, measurable target with a time limit that moves the business towards its aim |
| Financial objective | A target expressed in money or sales terms |
| Non-financial objective | A target about people, purpose or the owner's personal goals |
| Survival | Continuing to trade |
| Market share | Business sales ÷ total market sales × 100 (standard formula) |
| Percentage change | (new figure − original figure) ÷ original figure × 100 |
| Conflicting objectives | Objectives where progress on one makes another harder |

## Condensed recall

### What makes an objective useful
1. Says what is measured.
2. Has a target figure.
3. Has a time limit.

"Increase sales" is weak. "Increase sales revenue by 10% by the end of next year" is useful.

### Checking an objective
- Work out the target figure (for example original × 1.15 for a 15% rise).
- Compare the actual figure with the target, not just with last year.
- State clearly: met or not met, and by how much.

Mini example: target of 120 regular customers by December; actual 108. Shortfall = 12 customers, so not met, although customer numbers may have grown.

### Common conflicts
- Sales or market share against profit (price cuts, heavy promotion).
- Profit against social objectives (higher wages, local suppliers).
- Independence and control against growth (needing an investor).
- Financial security against challenge (safe income against risk).

### Why objectives differ
| Reason | Example effect |
|---|---|
| Owner's motives | Hobby-based owner values personal satisfaction |
| Stage | Start-up focuses on survival |
| Finance | Loan repayments push profit up the list |
| Type of organisation | Community groups set social objectives |
| Market conditions | Strong rivals push survival or market share |

Objectives also change over time for the same business: survival first, then sales growth, then market share or profit.

## Mini case for recall: Foxglove Florist

Ines opened Foxglove Florist a year ago after leaving an office job. Her aim is to become the town's first choice for wedding flowers.

- **Objective 1 (sales):** book 30 weddings in year 2. She booked 24 in year 1, so this is a 25% rise ((30 − 24) ÷ 24 × 100).
- **Objective 2 (social):** buy at least half of her flowers from local growers.
- **Conflict:** local flowers cost more, so meeting objective 2 may cut her profit on each wedding.
- **Why her objectives differ from a supermarket's flower section:** she is a sole trader who left a job for independence and personal satisfaction, and she competes on quality and local sourcing rather than low price.

## Exam traps

- Calling an aim an objective (no number or deadline).
- Saying a target was met because the figure went up.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure.
- Calling independence a financial objective, or market share a non-financial one.
- Explaining why objectives differ in general terms, without the case.
- Ignoring conflicts when evaluating which objective matters most.

## Self-test

1. Define a business objective.
2. List the five financial aims and objectives in the specification.
3. List the four non-financial aims and objectives in the specification.
4. Give the three features of a useful objective.
5. Rewrite "get more customers" as a useful objective.
6. Sales revenue was £50,000 last year. The objective is a 12% rise. What is the target?
7. Actual sales were £55,000. Calculate the percentage increase from £50,000.
8. Was the objective in question 6 met?
9. A business sells £30,000 in a market worth £240,000. Calculate its market share.
10. Give one example of two objectives that conflict and explain why.
11. Why might a community business set social objectives rather than profit objectives?
12. Explain why a business's objectives may change as it becomes established.

### Answers

1. A specific, measurable target with a time limit that helps the business move towards its aim.
2. Survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security.
3. Social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control.
4. What is measured, a target figure, a time limit.
5. For example: "Gain 50 new regular customers within the next six months."
6. £50,000 × 1.12 = £56,000.
7. (£55,000 − £50,000) ÷ £50,000 × 100 = 10%.
8. No: sales rose by 10%, short of the 12% target, by £1,000.
9. £30,000 ÷ £240,000 × 100 = 12.5%.
10. For example, market share against profit: winning share by cutting prices reduces the profit made on each sale.
11. It exists to help people or the local area, and no owner takes the profit, so its targets are about the help it gives, while it covers its costs.
12. Once it has survived and has regular customers, it can afford to aim higher, for example at growth in sales, market share or profit.
