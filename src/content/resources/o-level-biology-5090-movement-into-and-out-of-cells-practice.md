## Questions

**1.** Define the term *diffusion*. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the role of water as a solvent in digestion, excretion and transport in humans. **[3]**

**3.** Diffusion and osmosis are passive processes.

**(a)** State the source of energy for osmosis. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why oxygen diffuses faster at a higher temperature. **[2]**

**4.** Two blocks of pink indicator agar are put into the same dilute acid. Block P is 2 cm × 2 cm × 2 cm; block Q is 1 cm × 1 cm × 8 cm. The colour disappears as acid diffuses in.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each block. Show your working. **[3]**
**(b)** Predict which block will go colourless first, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**5.** A student places a drop of dye in the centre of a dish of agar and measures how far the colour spreads in 30 minutes, at 15 °C, 25 °C, 35 °C and 45 °C.

**(a)** State the independent variable and the dependent variable. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** variables she should keep constant. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest how she could make her results more reliable. **[1]**

**6.** Red blood cells and onion epidermis cells are each placed in distilled water.

**(a)** Define the term *osmosis*. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe and explain what happens to the red blood cells. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the onion cells do not burst. **[2]**

**7.** Cylinders of carrot were weighed, left in sucrose solutions for 40 minutes, blotted and reweighed.

| Sucrose / mol per dm³ | Start mass / g | End mass / g | Change in mass / % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 3.20 | 3.52 | +10.0 |
| 0.1 | 3.00 | 3.15 | +5.0 |
| 0.2 | 3.10 | 3.10 | 0.0 |
| 0.3 | 3.00 | 2.85 | |
| 0.4 | 3.20 | 2.88 | −10.0 |
| 0.5 | 3.00 | 2.58 | −14.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mass at 0.3 mol per dm³. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the results to estimate the concentration of sucrose solution that has the same water potential as the carrot cells. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result at 0.0 mol per dm³. Use the terms *turgid* and *turgor pressure*. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain the result at 0.5 mol per dm³. Use the terms *flaccid* and *plasmolysis*. **[3]**
**(e)** Explain why the results are given as percentage change rather than change in mass. **[1]**
**(f)** Suggest why the cylinders were blotted before reweighing. **[1]**

**8.** Dialysis tubing filled with concentrated sucrose solution is attached to a capillary tube and placed in distilled water. The height of liquid in the capillary tube is recorded.

| Time / min | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Height / mm | 0 | 10 | 19 | 27 | 34 | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of rise over the 20 minutes. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the liquid rises up the capillary tube. **[3]**
**(c)** The rate of rise falls during the experiment. Suggest why. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe a suitable control for this experiment. **[1]**

**9.** A potted bean plant is left without water on a hot, sunny windowsill. After two days its leaves and young stem droop. Explain why the plant droops, with reference to its cells. **[4]**

**10.** Two sets of barley roots were kept in the same potassium ion solution, one with air bubbled through and one with nitrogen. Potassium ion uptake was measured.

| Time / h | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uptake with air / arbitrary units | 0 | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |
| Uptake with nitrogen / arbitrary units | 0 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in uptake after 4 hours when nitrogen is used instead of air. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two sets of results. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why there is some uptake in nitrogen during the first hour. **[1]**

**11.** A root hair cell takes in water and nitrate ions from the soil. Compare the ways in which these two substances enter the cell. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The net movement of molecules or ions from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration [1], down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Net" and "random movement" are what separate a two-mark definition from a one-mark one; "high to low" alone usually earns only the first mark.

**2.** Digestion: food molecules and enzymes are in water, and the soluble products dissolve so they can be absorbed [1]. Excretion: urea (and excess salts) are removed dissolved in water, as urine [1]. Transport: plasma, which is mostly water, carries dissolved substances such as glucose, amino acids or carbon dioxide [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* One mark is given per process, so each needs a named example of something dissolved; three general statements about "carrying things" earn one mark at most.

**3. (a)** Kinetic energy of the random movement of (water) molecules [1].
**(b)** At a higher temperature molecules have more kinetic energy [1], so they move faster and more of them move down the concentration gradient in a given time [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Particles move faster" scores only if it is linked to kinetic energy; answers that mention enzymes or respiration for diffusion score nothing.

**4. (a)** P: surface area = 6 × (2 × 2) = 24 cm², volume = 8 cm³, SA:V = **3 : 1** [1]. Q: surface area = 2(1 × 1) + 4(1 × 8) = 2 + 32 = 34 cm², volume = 8 cm³ [1], SA:V = 34 : 8 = **4.25 : 1** [1].
**(b)** **Q** goes colourless first [1]. It has the larger surface area to volume ratio and no part of it is more than 0.5 cm from the surface, so acid has a shorter distance to diffuse [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show both surface area and volume; a wrong bare ratio with no working earns nothing.

**5. (a)** Independent: temperature [1]. Dependent: distance the colour has spread (in 30 minutes) [1].
**(b)** Two from: volume of dye drop, concentration of dye, depth of agar, time allowed [1] [1].
**(c)** Repeat each temperature (for example three times) and calculate a mean [1].
*Examiner insight:* Vague answers such as "same conditions" or "same amount" earn no credit for controlled variables.

**6. (a)** Net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential [1], through a partially permeable membrane [1].
**(b)** Distilled water has a higher water potential than the cytoplasm of the red blood cells [1]. Water enters the cells by osmosis [1]. The cells swell and burst because they have no cell wall [1].
**(c)** Onion cells have a cellulose cell wall [1], which resists the pressure of the expanding vacuole, so the cells become turgid instead of bursting [1].
*Examiner insight:* Saying red blood cells burst "because of too much water", without water potential and osmosis, gains only the description mark.

**7. (a)** (2.85 − 3.00) / 3.00 × 100 [1] = **−5.0 %** [1].
**(b)** **0.2 mol per dm³** [1], because there is no change in mass, so there is no net movement of water: the water potentials inside and outside are equal [1].
**(c)** The solution has a higher water potential than the carrot cells [1], so water enters by osmosis [1]. Vacuoles swell and push on the walls (turgor pressure); cells become turgid and the cylinder gains mass [1].
**(d)** The solution has a lower water potential than the carrot cells [1], so water leaves by osmosis [1]. Cells lose turgor pressure and become flaccid; in some, the membrane and cytoplasm pull away from the wall (plasmolysis) [1].
**(e)** The starting masses were different, so percentage change allows a fair comparison [1].
**(f)** To remove surface solution, so only water taken into or lost from the cells affects the mass [1].
*Examiner insight:* The minus sign is needed for the accuracy mark in (a); in (c) and (d) state which side has the higher water potential and use the named terms.

**8. (a)** 40 mm / 20 min [1] = **2.0 mm per min** [1].
**(b)** Distilled water has a higher water potential than the sucrose solution [1]. Water enters the tubing by osmosis through the partially permeable membrane [1]. Sucrose cannot pass out, so the volume inside increases and liquid is pushed up the tube [1].
**(c)** Water entering dilutes the sucrose solution [1], so the water potential gradient becomes less steep and osmosis slows [1].
**(d)** The same set-up with distilled water inside the tubing instead of sucrose solution [1].
*Examiner insight:* A rate needs a unit for the second mark; "2" alone loses it even with correct working.

**9.** The plant loses water from its leaves faster than it takes water up from the dry soil [1]. Its cells lose water by osmosis [1], lose turgor pressure and become flaccid [1]. Flaccid cells no longer press outwards on their walls, so cannot support the leaves and stem [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The link between turgor pressure and support is the key mark; answers that only say "the plant has no water" score at most one mark.

**10. (a)** (48 − 6) / 48 × 100 [1] = **87.5 %** [1].
**(b)** Potassium ions are taken up by active transport [1], against a concentration gradient [1]. This needs energy released during respiration [1]. Without oxygen (in nitrogen) aerobic respiration stops, so little energy is released and uptake almost stops [1].
**(c)** Any one [1]: some ions diffuse in while the concentration inside the root is still lower than in the solution; some oxygen remains in the roots at first, so a little respiration continues.
*Examiner insight:* "Energy" must be linked to respiration and to oxygen; a statement that nitrogen "is toxic" or "blocks the ions" gains no credit.

**11.** Water enters by osmosis; nitrate ions by active transport [1]. Water moves from higher to lower water potential [1]; nitrate ions move from lower to higher concentration, against a concentration gradient [1]. Osmosis uses only the kinetic energy of molecules [1]; active transport uses energy released during respiration [1]. Both cross the cell membrane, helped by the large surface area of the root hair [1]. **[6]** (Also accept: lack of oxygen reduces nitrate uptake but does not directly stop osmosis.)
*Examiner insight:* A comparison question needs matched points for both substances; describing only one substance in detail caps the answer at about half the marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining osmosis in terms of "water concentration" or "solution concentration" instead of water potential.
- Leaving out the partially permeable membrane in the osmosis definition.
- Leaving the minus sign off a percentage loss in mass.
- Giving a rate without a unit, or a ratio with no working.
- Saying a plant cell bursts in water, or that the cell wall shrinks in plasmolysis.
- Writing "energy" for active transport without "released during respiration".
- Listing vague controlled variables ("same conditions", "same amount").
