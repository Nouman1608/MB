These notes condense section 2.5 (price determination) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: market equilibrium (2.5.1) and market disequilibrium (2.5.2). All figures are invented. Full explanations are in the [price determination study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-price-determination-cambridge-igcse-economics/); test yourself with the [price determination practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-price-determination-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/).

## 2.5.1 Market equilibrium

- **Market equilibrium:** at the current price, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so there is no pressure for the price to change.
- **Equilibrium price:** the price where buyers' and sellers' plans match (market-clearing price).
- **Equilibrium sales:** the quantity bought and sold at that price.
- Equilibrium lasts only while the conditions of demand and supply are unchanged.

### From a schedule

1. Work out demand minus supply in each row.
2. Equilibrium is where the gap is zero, or between the two rows where it changes from positive (shortage) to negative (surplus).
3. Total spending (sellers' revenue) = equilibrium price x equilibrium sales.

Example (weekly market for school bags, invented):

| Price | Demanded | Supplied | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 340 | 160 | +180 |
| 12 | 300 | 220 | +80 |
| 14 | 260 | 280 | -20 |
| 16 | 220 | 340 | -120 |

```
Gap changes sign between 12 and 14
Each rise of 2 in price closes the gap by 40 + 60 = 100
Shortage at 12 is 80, so price must rise by 80 / 100 x 2 = 1.6
Equilibrium price = 13.60
Demanded = 300 - (0.8 x 40) = 268; supplied = 220 + (0.8 x 60) = 268
Revenue = 13.60 x 268 = 3,644.80
```

### On a diagram

- Price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis.
- Demand slopes down, supply slopes up; they cross at E.
- Dotted lines from E to Pe and Qe.

## 2.5.2 Market disequilibrium

| Price is | Result | Size | Quantity traded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below equilibrium | Shortage (excess demand) | Demanded minus supplied | Quantity supplied |
| Above equilibrium | Surplus (excess supply) | Supplied minus demanded | Quantity demanded |

Rule: the quantity traded is always the **smaller** of the two quantities.

Example from the bag market:

```
At 10: shortage = 340 - 160 = 180; traded = 160; spending = 10 x 160 = 1,600
At 16: surplus = 340 - 220 = 120; traded = 220; spending = 16 x 220 = 3,520
```

### Adjustment chains

- **Shortage:** queues and early sell-outs -> sellers raise price -> quantity demanded contracts, quantity supplied extends -> gap closes at equilibrium.
- **Surplus:** unsold stock and waste -> sellers cut price -> quantity demanded extends, quantity supplied contracts -> gap closes at equilibrium.
- Neither curve shifts during adjustment.

### Why disequilibrium can last

- Prices fixed in advance (tickets, price lists, contracts).
- Costs of changing prices.
- Government maximum or minimum prices (section 2.11).

Consequences: shortages bring queues, waiting lists and resale at higher prices; surpluses bring storage costs, discounting and waste.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market equilibrium | Quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at the current price |
| Equilibrium price | The price at which the market clears |
| Market disequilibrium | Quantity demanded and supplied are unequal at the current price |
| Shortage (excess demand) | Quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied |
| Surplus (excess supply) | Quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded |
| Extension | Movement along a curve that raises the quantity |
| Contraction | Movement along a curve that lowers the quantity |

## Exam traps

- A price below equilibrium gives a shortage, not a surplus.
- At a disequilibrium price, the smaller quantity is traded.
- Adjustment uses "extend" and "contract", never "increase" or "decrease".
- Do not shift the curves to show a market returning to equilibrium.
- Choose the equilibrium from the sign change in the gap, not the row with the smallest gap.
- Label Pe and Qe with units.

## Quick self-test

1. Define market equilibrium.
2. At a price of 8, quantity demanded is 120 and quantity supplied is 90. Name and measure the disequilibrium.
3. How much is traded at a price of 8 in question 2?
4. At a price of 11, quantity supplied is 150 and quantity demanded is 70. What will happen to the price?
5. Equilibrium price is 4 and equilibrium sales are 2,500. Calculate total spending.
6. In a schedule the gap is +60 at a price of 5 and -40 at a price of 6. Between which prices is equilibrium?
7. In question 6 the gap closes by equal steps. Estimate the equilibrium price.
8. During adjustment from a surplus, what happens to quantity supplied?
9. Give one reason a shortage might last.
10. Give one consequence of a lasting surplus of a perishable product.
11. On a diagram, how do you show the size of a shortage?
12. Why is more traded at equilibrium than at a price below it?

### Answers

1. The situation in which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at the current price, so there is no pressure for the price to change.
2. Shortage (excess demand) of 120 - 90 = 30.
3. 90, the smaller quantity.
4. It will fall, because there is a surplus of 80.
5. 4 x 2,500 = 10,000.
6. Between 5 and 6.
7. The gap closes by 100 per 1 rise; 60 / 100 = 0.6, so about 5.60.
8. It contracts.
9. Any one: the price is fixed in advance; changing prices is costly; a government maximum price.
10. Waste, as unsold stock goes off; or storage costs and losses for sellers.
11. Draw a horizontal line at that price; the horizontal distance between the supply curve and the demand curve along it.
12. Below equilibrium, sellers offer less than buyers want, so the quantity traded is limited by supply.
