This practice set covers syllabus section **1.3** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first
assessment 2021), common to SL and HL: geometric sequences and series with a finite number of terms, sigma
notation and applications. Section A is calculator-free, like Paper 1; Section B expects technology, like
Paper 2. Give answers exactly or to 3 significant figures, and money to the nearest penny.

Learn the methods first in the [section 1.3 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-3/)
and the [section 1.3 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-3-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) Consider the geometric sequence 5, 15, 45, …

**(a)** Find u₇. **[2]**
**(b)** Find S₇. **[2]**

**2.** (calculator-free) A geometric sequence has u₄ = 24 and u₆ = 96. Find the possible values of the common
ratio and of the first term. **[4]**

**3.** (calculator-free) Evaluate the sum from k = 1 to k = 6 of 2 × 3^k. **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free) The finite geometric sequence 1/2, 1, 2, …, 256 has n terms.

**(a)** Find n. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the sum of the terms. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator-free) The expressions x − 2, x + 1 and 3x + 3 are consecutive terms of a geometric
sequence with a non-zero common ratio. Find x and the common ratio. **[4]**

**6.** (calculator-free) Show that the sum of the first n terms of 2 + 4 + 8 + … is 2ⁿ⁺¹ − 2. **[2]**

**7.** (calculator-free) Given that the sum from r = 1 to n of 3 × 2^(r − 1) equals 3069, find n. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** (calculator allowed) A culture contains 800 bacteria at the start of an experiment, and the number
increases by 12% each hour.

**(a)** Find the number of bacteria after 10 hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the least whole number of hours after which there are more than 5000 bacteria. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) Ravi's salary is £36 000 in year 1 and rises by 2.5% each year.

**(a)** Find his salary in year 8. **[2]**
**(b)** Find his total earnings over the first 8 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the least number of years for his total earnings to exceed £500 000. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) In a model of an outbreak, there are 3 new cases on day 1, and each day the
number of new cases is 1.25 times the number on the previous day.

**(a)** Find the number of new cases on day 12. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total number of cases in the first 12 days. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A pattern is made of squares. The first square has side 10 cm, and each square's
side is 80% of the previous square's side.

**(a)** Explain why the areas of the squares form a geometric sequence with ratio 0.64. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the total area of the first 8 squares. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) Two savings plans start in week 1. Plan A saves £100 in week 1 and £20 more each
week after that. Plan B saves £20 in week 1 and 15% more each week after that.

**(a)** Find the first week in which plan B saves more than plan A in that week. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the total saved by each plan after 26 weeks, and state which is larger. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 5 × 3⁶ [1] = **3645** [1]
**(b)** 5(3⁷ − 1)/(3 − 1) [1] = 5 × 2186/2 = **5465** [1]

**2.** r² = 96/24 = 4 [1], so **r = 2 or r = −2** [1]. u₁ = u₄/r³: **u₁ = 3** when r = 2 [1], and
**u₁ = −3** when r = −2 [1]

**3.** First term 2 × 3 = 6, ratio 3, 6 terms [1]; 6(3⁶ − 1)/(3 − 1) [1] = 3 × 728 = **2184** [1]

**4. (a)** (1/2) × 2^(n − 1) = 256, so 2^(n − 1) = 512 = 2⁹ [1]; **n = 10** [1]
**(b)** (1/2)(2¹⁰ − 1)/(2 − 1) [1] = **511.5** [1]

**5.** (x + 1)² = (x − 2)(3x + 3) [1]; x² + 2x + 1 = 3x² − 3x − 6, so 2x² − 5x − 7 = 0, (2x − 7)(x + 1) = 0
[1]; x = −1 gives terms −3, 0, 0, so it is rejected, and **x = 3.5** [1]; terms 1.5, 4.5, 13.5, so
**r = 3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Testing both roots in the original terms is what earns the rejection mark.

**6.** u₁ = 2, r = 2, so S_n = 2(2ⁿ − 1)/(2 − 1) [1] = 2ⁿ⁺¹ − 2 [1]

**7.** 3(2ⁿ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 3069 [1]; 2ⁿ − 1 = 1023, so 2ⁿ = 1024 [1]; **n = 10** [1]

**8. (a)** 800 × 1.12¹⁰ [1] = 2484.6…, so **2485 bacteria** [1]
**(b)** 800 × 1.12ⁿ > 5000 [1]; n > ln 6.25/ln 1.12 = 16.17 [1]; **17 hours** [1]

**9. (a)** 36 000 × 1.025⁷ [1] = **£42 792.69** [1]
**(b)** 36 000(1.025⁸ − 1)/(1.025 − 1) [1] = **£314 500.17** [1]
**(c)** 36 000(1.025ⁿ − 1)/0.025 > 500 000 [1]; S₁₂ = £496 639.91 and S₁₃ = £545 055.90 [1]; **13 years**
[1]
*Examiner insight:* Year 8 is u₈, so the power is 7, not 8.

**10. (a)** 3 × 1.25¹¹ [1] = 34.9…, so **35 new cases** [1]
**(b)** 3(1.25¹² − 1)/(1.25 − 1) [1] = 162.6…, so **163 cases** [1]

**11. (a)** Each side is multiplied by 0.8, so each area is multiplied by 0.8² = 0.64 [1]
**(b)** u₁ = 100 cm² [1]; S₈ = 100(1 − 0.64⁸)/(1 − 0.64) [1] = **270 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**12. (a)** Plan A week n: 100 + 20(n − 1); plan B week n: 20 × 1.15^(n − 1) [1]. Week 25: A = £580,
B = £572.50 [1]; week 26: A = £600, B = £658.38, so **week 26** [1]
**(b)** A: (26/2)(200 + 25 × 20) = **£9100** [1]; B: 20(1.15²⁶ − 1)/(1.15 − 1) = **£4914.24** [1];
**plan A** has saved more [1]
*Examiner insight:* Plan B's weekly amount overtakes A's long before its total does; questions often test
whether you compare terms or sums.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only one value of r when an even power of r is found.
- Using rⁿ instead of rⁿ⁻¹ for a year-n value.
- Reading the first term of a sigma sum as the coefficient.
- Confusing new cases on a day with total cases.
- Rounding a least-n answer down.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the sum to infinity not in this set?**
It is section 1.8 of the guide. This set keeps to finite sums from section 1.3.

**How do I choose between logarithms and a GDC table for least-n questions?**
Either is acceptable on Paper 2. Whichever you use, show the inequality and check the values either side of
your answer.
