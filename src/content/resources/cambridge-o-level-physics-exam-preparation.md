Cambridge O Level Physics (5054) is not tiered. Paper 1 is Multiple Choice (1 hour, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 2 is Theory (1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). The practical component is a choice between Paper 3 Practical Test (1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) and Paper 4 Alternative to Practical (1 hour, 40 marks, 20%), both externally assessed. Revision pages such as [forces and motion](/resources/forces-and-motion/), [kinematics and motion graphs](/resources/kinematics/) and [energy, work and power](/resources/energy-work-and-power/) support this structure.

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice

Paper 1 gives 60 minutes for 40 marks, which is 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per question. The six syllabus areas are motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics, so multiple choice can move quickly from one to another. Prepare by working through varied short questions rather than long single-topic sets.

A reliable technique is to read the stem, cover the options, decide what the answer should be, and then match. For calculation items, work on the question paper and check units. If two options look alike, test them against a limiting case, for example what happens if a quantity doubles or becomes zero. Do not leave any answer blank.

## Paper 2: Theory

Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes for 80 marks and is worth 50%, the largest single component. That is 105 / 80 = 1.3125 minutes per mark, so a 4-mark part deserves about 5 minutes. Expect a mixture of definitions, descriptions, explanations, calculations and interpretation of graphs and diagrams.

Keep a formula sheet for yourself and know which relationships you must remember. In explanations, link observation to principle: for example, say what happens to particle motion and then why the measured quantity changes. For calculations, set out the equation, the substitution with units and the answer with its unit. For graphs, practise finding gradient and area and saying what each represents. Use the [elastic deformation, moments and centre of gravity](/resources/elastic-deformation-moments-and-centre-of-gravity/) page for topics where diagrams and reasoning go together, and the [energy resources notes](/resources/energy-resources-revision-notes/) for descriptive sections where precise wording gains marks.

## Paper 3 or Paper 4: the practical component

Per the record, a candidate takes either Paper 3, a Practical Test of 1 hour 30 minutes (40 marks, 20%), or Paper 4, an Alternative to Practical of 1 hour (40 marks, 20%). They are alternatives, and your centre decides which you sit.

For either route, rehearse the same skills: recording measurements with sensible precision and units, drawing tables, plotting graphs with suitable scales, drawing a line of best fit, calculating from results, identifying sources of error, and suggesting realistic improvements. If you are preparing for Paper 4, remember that you cannot handle apparatus in the exam, so rely on thorough hands-on experience in lessons and practise interpreting experimental descriptions and diagrams.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 105 / 80 = 1.3125 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.

The weightings 30% + 50% + 20% = 100% for the papers you sit. Because Paper 2 is both the longest and the heaviest, protect revision time for it, but remember that the practical share is easily lost through careless units, poor graphs or untidy tables.

## Command words and question styles

State and give need a short fact. Define needs a precise meaning. Describe asks what happens or how something is done. Explain asks why. Calculate needs working. Determine may mean obtain from a graph or calculation. Suggest asks you to apply knowledge to a new situation. Sketch needs shape and key points, and plot needs accuracy. Read the verb first, then the data, then the question.

## Worked practice scenario

An electric heater rated at 3.0 kW is used to heat 2.0 kg of water from 18 degrees Celsius to 58 degrees Celsius. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg degrees Celsius). (a) Calculate the energy needed. (b) Calculate the time taken if all the energy from the heater goes into the water. (c) Calculate the time if only 80% of the electrical energy reaches the water.

**Model answer.**

(a) Temperature rise = 58 - 18 = 40 degrees Celsius.
Energy needed Q = m x c x change in temperature = 2.0 x 4200 x 40 = 336 000 J.

(b) Power is energy divided by time, so time = energy / power.
Power = 3.0 kW = 3000 W.
Time = 336 000 / 3000 = 112 s.

(c) With 80% efficiency, the useful power is 0.80 x 3000 = 2400 W.
Time = 336 000 / 2400 = 140 s.

The longer time in part (c) makes sense because less of the heater's output reaches the water. Write each unit conversion explicitly (kW to W), as that is where candidates commonly slip.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are entered for Paper 3 or Paper 4.
- List every equation you must recall and test yourself on them.
- Practise multiple choice at 1.5 minutes per question.
- Practise theory answers with correct units and significant figures.
- Revise graph skills, including gradient and area under a graph.
- Bring a calculator, ruler and sharp pencil.
- Convert units before substituting into equations.
- Use the last few minutes to check signs, powers of ten and units.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Paper 2, and what is its weighting?
2. Which papers are alternatives to each other?
3. How many minutes per mark does Paper 3 allow?
4. What is the total weighting for the papers a candidate sits?
5. In the worked scenario, why does the time increase in part (c)?

**Answers:** 1. 80 marks and 50%. 2. Paper 3 and Paper 4. 3. 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes per mark. 4. 30% + 50% + 20% = 100%. 5. Because only 80% of the electrical energy heats the water, the effective power is lower, so more time is needed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Physics 5054?**
A candidate sits three components: Paper 1 Multiple Choice, Paper 2 Theory, and one practical paper, either Paper 3 or Paper 4.

**How long and how many marks is each paper?**
Paper 1 is 1 hour and 40 marks, Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes and 80 marks, Paper 3 is 1 hour 30 minutes and 40 marks, and Paper 4 is 1 hour and 40 marks.

**What are the weightings?**
Paper 1 is 30%, Paper 2 is 50% and the practical component is 20%.

**Do I sit both Paper 3 and Paper 4?**
No. The practical component is a choice of one or the other, and both are externally assessed.

**Is the subject tiered?**
No. It is not tiered, and the series window is 2026-2028 (June and November).
