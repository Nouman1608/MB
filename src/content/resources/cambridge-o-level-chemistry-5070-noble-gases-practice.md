## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the group of the Periodic Table that contains the noble gases. **[1]**

**2.** Which statement about all the noble gases is correct?

A: they have eight electrons in their outer shell

B: they exist as diatomic molecules

C: they have a full outer shell of electrons

D: they form 1− ions

**[1]**

**3.** Give the electronic configuration of argon. **[1]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by the term monatomic. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the noble gases are unreactive. **[2]**

**6.** Write the formula of helium gas. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The particles below all have the same electronic configuration as one noble gas.

F⁻   Na⁺   Mg²⁺   O²⁻

**(a)** Name the noble gas. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the electronic configuration of these particles. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why magnesium atoms form Mg²⁺ ions. **[2]**

**8.** Hydrogen and helium are both gases at r.t.p.

**(a)** Hydrogen is diatomic and helium is monatomic. Explain this difference in terms of electrons. **[3]**

**(b)** State the number of electrons each hydrogen atom has in its outer shell after bonding in H₂, and name the noble gas with this configuration. **[2]**

**9.** The table gives the boiling points of some noble gases.

| Noble gas | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|
| Neon | −246 |
| Argon | −186 |
| Krypton | −153 |
| Xenon | −108 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in boiling point. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the boiling point of radon, which is below xenon. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why all of these elements are gases at r.t.p. **[2]**

**10.** A student predicts that "radon will be the most reactive noble gas, because reactivity increases down a group, as in Group I."

Evaluate this prediction. **[2]**

**11.** The relative atomic mass of argon is 40 and of chlorine is 35.5.

**(a)** State the relative formula mass of argon gas and of chlorine gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the two answers are calculated differently. **[1]**

**12.** Calcium reacts with chlorine to form calcium chloride, CaCl₂.

**(a)** Give the electronic configurations of a calcium ion and a chloride ion. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the noble gas that has the same electronic configuration as both ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why calcium and chlorine react but argon does not react with either. **[2]**

**13.** The electronic configurations of five **atoms**, V to Z, are given.

| Atom | V | W | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Configuration | 2,8 | 2,8,1 | 2,8,8 | 2,7 | 2 |

**(a)** Identify the **three** atoms that are noble gases. **[1]**

**(b)** Atoms W and Y react together. Give the formula of the compound formed, using the letters, and the charges on its ions. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why atom V does not react with atom Y. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why atom Z is placed in the same group as V and X although it has only two outer electrons. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Group VIII (also printed as Group 0) [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** 2,8,8 [1].

**4.** Exists as single atoms / atoms not bonded to each other [1].

**5.** They have a full outer shell [1], so they have no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons [1].

**6.** He [1].

**7. (a)** Neon [1].

**(b)** 2,8 [1].

**(c)** A magnesium atom (2,8,2) loses its two outer electrons [1] to obtain a full outer shell / the noble gas configuration of neon [1].

**8. (a)** A hydrogen atom has one electron and needs one more to fill its shell [1]; two hydrogen atoms share a pair of electrons / form a covalent bond [1]; a helium atom already has a full shell (two electrons), so its atoms do not bond [1].

**(b)** Two [1]; helium [1].

**9. (a)** Boiling point increases down the group [1].

**(b)** Any value above −108 °C and below room temperature (actual value about −62 °C) [1].

**(c)** The atoms are separate and only weakly attracted to each other [1], so little energy is needed to separate them / boiling points are far below room temperature [1].

**10.** The prediction is incorrect [1]: all noble gases have a full outer shell, so radon is also unreactive; the Group I trend depends on losing an outer electron, which noble gases have no tendency to do [1].

**11. (a)** Argon: 40 [1]. Chlorine: 71 [1].

**(b)** Argon is monatomic (formula Ar), whereas chlorine is diatomic (formula Cl₂), so its mass is 2 × 35.5 [1].

**12. (a)** Ca²⁺: 2,8,8 [1]. Cl⁻: 2,8,8 [1].

**(b)** Argon [1].

**(c)** Calcium and chlorine atoms do not have full outer shells, and by transferring electrons both reach a full outer shell [1]; argon already has a full outer shell, so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons [1].

**13. (a)** V, X and Z [1].

**(b)** WY [1]; W⁺ and Y⁻ [1].

**(c)** V has a full outer shell, so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons with Y [1].

**(d)** Its only shell is full (it holds a maximum of two electrons), so it is unreactive and monatomic like the other noble gases [1].

*Marking note:* in (a), all three letters are needed for the mark. In (b), W (2,8,1) loses one electron and Y (2,7) gains one, so the ions are 1+ and 1−; both reach the configuration 2,8.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying "eight outer electrons" for all noble gases.
- Stopping at "stable" or "full shell" without the electron consequence.
- Defining monatomic as "one type of atom".
- Doubling the relative atomic mass of a noble gas as if it were diatomic.
- Carrying the Group I reactivity trend into Group VIII.
