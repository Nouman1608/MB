These questions cover section 3.1.7, Oxidation, reduction and redox equations, of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for AS and A-level exams from June 2016 onwards. The whole section is AS content and is also part of the full A-level; none of it is marked "A-level only". The specification assesses it in Paper 1 at AS and A-level, and A-level Paper 3 can include any content. Question 10 also uses mole calculations from section 3.1.2.

## Questions

**1.** Define oxidation in terms of electrons, and state what an oxidising agent does in a redox reaction. **[2]**

**2.** Deduce the oxidation state of the stated element in each species.

**(a)** phosphorus in H₃PO₄ **[1]**
**(b)** nitrogen in N₂H₄ **[1]**
**(c)** chromium in CrO₄²⁻ **[1]**
**(d)** oxygen in OF₂ **[1]**

**3.** State the oxidation state of hydrogen in calcium hydride, CaH₂, and explain why it differs from the usual value for hydrogen. **[2]**

**4.** Magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. The ionic equation is:

Mg + 2H⁺ → Mg²⁺ + H₂

**(a)** Identify the species that is oxidised. Give the change in its oxidation state. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the oxidising agent. **[1]**

**5.** In an iodine titration, thiosulfate ions react with iodine:

2S₂O₃²⁻ + I₂ → S₄O₆²⁻ + 2I⁻

**(a)** Deduce the oxidation state of sulfur in S₂O₃²⁻ and in S₄O₆²⁻. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the oxidation process and a half-equation for the reduction process. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the oxidising agent. **[1]**

**6.** Copper reacts with dilute nitric acid. Nitrate ions, NO₃⁻, are reduced to nitrogen monoxide, NO, and copper is oxidised to Cu²⁺ ions.

**(a)** Give the oxidation state of nitrogen in NO₃⁻ and in NO. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of NO₃⁻ to NO in acidic solution. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to write an overall ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**7.** Concentrated sulfuric acid oxidises bromide ions to bromine. The sulfuric acid is reduced to sulfur dioxide.

**(a)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of H₂SO₄ to SO₂. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an overall equation for the reaction of H₂SO₄ with H⁺ and Br⁻ ions. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the reducing agent. **[1]**

**8.** Iodate(V) ions, IO₃⁻, react with iodide ions in acidic solution to form iodine.

**(a)** Show that the oxidation state of iodine in IO₃⁻ is +5. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of IO₃⁻ to I₂ in acidic solution. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the overall ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why iodine in this reaction is both a product of oxidation and a product of reduction. **[1]**

**9.** A student writes this equation for the reaction of hydrogen peroxide with iodide ions in acid:

H₂O₂ + I⁻ + 2H⁺ → I₂ + 2H₂O

Explain what is wrong with the equation and write the correct equation. **[2]**

**10.** An acidified solution of potassium dichromate(VI) is used to find the concentration of iron(II) ions. Dichromate(VI) ions are reduced to Cr³⁺ ions.

**(a)** Give the oxidation state of chromium in Cr₂O₇²⁻ and in Cr³⁺. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of Cr₂O₇²⁻ to Cr³⁺ in acidic solution. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the overall ionic equation for the reaction of Cr₂O₇²⁻ with Fe²⁺ ions in acid. **[2]**
**(d)** A 25.0 cm³ sample of an iron(II) solution reacts exactly with 18.75 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium dichromate(VI). Calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺ ions in mol dm⁻³. **[4]**

**11.** An ion XO₃⁻ contains an unknown element X. In acidic solution, 0.00200 mol of XO₃⁻ reacts exactly with 0.00300 mol of tin(II) ions. The tin(II) ions are oxidised to tin(IV) ions.

**(a)** Deduce the oxidation state of X in XO₃⁻. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the oxidation of tin(II) ions. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of electrons transferred. **[2]**
**(d)** Deduce the oxidation state of X after the reaction. Show your reasoning. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why tin(II) ions act as the reducing agent. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Oxidation is the loss of electrons [1]. An oxidising agent accepts electrons (and is itself reduced) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Gain of oxygen" alone does not earn the first mark, because the specification defines oxidation in terms of electrons.

**2. (a)** 3(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0, so **+5** [1]
**(b)** 2x + 4(+1) = 0, so **−2** [1]
**(c)** x + 4(−2) = −2, so **+6** [1]
**(d)** F is always −1, so x + 2(−1) = 0, giving **+2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each answer needs a sign; "5" or "5+" for phosphorus is usually not credited as an oxidation state.

**3.** Hydrogen is **−1** [1]. Calcium is a Group 2 metal and is +2, so in this metal hydride hydrogen takes the negative value (it is bonded to a less electronegative metal, not to a non-metal) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation mark needs a reason tied to the compound (metal hydride, Ca is +2), not just "it is an exception".

**4. (a)** **Magnesium (Mg)** is oxidised [1]; its oxidation state goes from **0 to +2** [1].
**(b)** **H⁺** (hydrogen ions) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "hydrogen" alone is ambiguous between H⁺ and H₂; name the ion that accepts the electrons.

**5. (a)** S₂O₃²⁻: 2x + 3(−2) = −2, so **+2** [1]; S₄O₆²⁻: 4x + 6(−2) = −2, so **+2.5** [1]
**(b)** Oxidation: **2S₂O₃²⁻ → S₄O₆²⁻ + 2e⁻** [1]; reduction: **I₂ + 2e⁻ → 2I⁻** [1]
**(c)** **I₂** (iodine) [1]
*Examiner insight:* +2.5 is accepted as an average oxidation state; rounding it to +2 or +3 loses the mark.

**6. (a)** **+5** in NO₃⁻ and **+2** in NO (both needed) [1]
**(b)** Correct species with H⁺ and H₂O balanced: NO₃⁻ + 4H⁺ → NO + 2H₂O [1]; electrons correct: **NO₃⁻ + 4H⁺ + 3e⁻ → NO + 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ is multiplied by 3 and the nitrate half-equation by 2, so that 6 electrons are transferred [1]; **3Cu + 2NO₃⁻ + 8H⁺ → 3Cu²⁺ + 2NO + 4H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the second mark is for the fully balanced equation; any electrons left in the final equation lose it, though multiples are accepted.

**7. (a)** Species balanced with H₂O and H⁺: H₂SO₄ + 2H⁺ → SO₂ + 2H₂O [1]; with electrons: **H₂SO₄ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → SO₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(b)** Oxidation half-equation 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ combined with (a), electrons equal [1]; **H₂SO₄ + 2H⁺ + 2Br⁻ → SO₂ + Br₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **Br⁻** (bromide ions) [1]
*Examiner insight:* An error carried from (a) into (b) can still earn credit in (b) if the combining step is correct (error carried forward).

**8. (a)** x + 3(−2) = −1, so x = +5 [1]
**(b)** Balanced atoms with H₂O and H⁺: 2IO₃⁻ + 12H⁺ → I₂ + 6H₂O [1]; **2IO₃⁻ + 12H⁺ + 10e⁻ → I₂ + 6H₂O** [1]
**(c)** Iodide half-equation 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻ multiplied by 5, then added and simplified [1]; **IO₃⁻ + 5I⁻ + 6H⁺ → 3I₂ + 3H₂O** [1]
**(d)** Iodine forms from I⁻ (−1 → 0, oxidation) and from IO₃⁻ (+5 → 0, reduction) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that" in (a), the working must appear; writing "+5" alone repeats the question and earns nothing.

**9.** Iodine atoms do not balance: one I on the left and two on the right (the charge does not balance either: +1 on the left, 0 on the right) [1]. Correct equation: **H₂O₂ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → I₂ + 2H₂O** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "It is not balanced" is too vague for the first mark; say which atom or charge is unbalanced.

**10. (a)** **+6** in Cr₂O₇²⁻ and **+3** in Cr³⁺ (both needed) [1]
**(b)** Atoms balanced: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O [1]; **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O** [1]
**(c)** Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ multiplied by 6 [1]; **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O** [1]
**(d)** Amount of Cr₂O₇²⁻ = 0.01875 dm³ × 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ = 3.75 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]
Amount of Fe²⁺ = 6 × 3.75 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.25 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
Concentration = 2.25 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 dm³ [1] = **0.0900 mol dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), using a 1 : 1 ratio instead of the 1 : 6 ratio from (c) loses the ratio mark, but the volume step can still be credited by error carried forward.

**11. (a)** x + 3(−2) = −1, so **+5** [1]
**(b)** **Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(c)** Each Sn²⁺ loses 2 electrons [1]; 2 × 0.00300 = **0.00600 mol** of electrons [1]
**(d)** Electrons gained per X = 0.00600 ÷ 0.00200 = 3 [1]; new oxidation state = +5 − 3 = **+2** [1]
**(e)** Sn²⁺ donates electrons (its oxidation state rises from +2 to +4), so it reduces X [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d) the reasoning mark needs electrons per X atom; the answer +2 with no working may not get both marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the oxidising agent "the species oxidised"; it is reduced.
- Leaving the sign off an oxidation state, or writing it as a charge (2+ instead of +2).
- Forgetting fluorine is always −1, so missing that oxygen is +2 in OF₂.
- Treating hydrogen as +1 in a metal hydride such as CaH₂.
- Rounding an average oxidation state such as +2.5.
- Balancing charge with H⁺ rather than electrons in a half-equation.
- Leaving e⁻, or H⁺ and H₂O on both sides, in the overall equation.
- Using the wrong mole ratio from the overall equation in a titration calculation.
- Writing "hydrogen" or "chlorine" when the question needs a named ion such as H⁺ or Br⁻.
