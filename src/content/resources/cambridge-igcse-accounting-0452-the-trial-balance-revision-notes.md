Condensed notes for sub-topic **3.1 The trial balance**. A full worked trial balance and a corrected draft are in the [3.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-trial-balance/); test yourself with the [3.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-trial-balance-practice/).

## What 3.1 asks for

- The purposes and limitations of a trial balance.
- Preparing a trial balance from a list of balances, and amending one that contains errors.
- The six errors that do not affect the trial balance: commission, compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry, principle.

## Purposes and limitations

| Purposes | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry | Equal totals do not prove the records are free of errors |
| Shows that an error exists if totals differ | Does not show where an error is |
| Summarises all balances for preparing financial statements | Only a list at one date; not a financial statement |

## Debit or credit?

| Debit balances | Credit balances |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets at cost | Capital |
| Opening inventory | Loans |
| Trade receivables, other receivables | Trade payables, other payables |
| Bank (in funds), cash | Bank overdraft |
| Purchases, sales returns | Sales, purchases returns |
| All expenses, including carriage inwards and outwards | All income, such as rent received and commission received |
| Discount allowed | Discount received |
| Irrecoverable debts | Provision for depreciation |
| Drawings | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |

**Not in the trial balance:** closing inventory (it is a year-end figure given as a note), and the owner's private assets.

## Amending a trial balance

- Move each misplaced balance to its correct side.
- Each move changes **both** totals: one down by the amount, the other up by the amount.
- One balance on the wrong side makes the totals differ by **twice** its value. So if the difference is 1,240, look for a balance of 620 on the wrong side.
- Redraft in full with new totals unless only totals are asked for.

**Example:** draft totals are debit 48,900 and credit 47,700 (difference 1,200). Discount received of 600 is shown as a debit. Moving it: debit 48,300, credit 48,300.

## The six errors that do not affect the trial balance

| Error | Key test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Wrong account, **same type**, correct side and amount | Payment from A Khan credited to A Kahn |
| Principle | Wrong **type** of account | Purchase of a computer debited to purchases |
| Omission | Not recorded **at all** | Credit sale invoice never entered |
| Original entry | Wrong **amount** in both entries | 540 entered as 450 in the journal and ledger |
| Complete reversal | Right accounts and amount, sides **swapped** | Cash sale: debit sales, credit cash |
| Compensating | Two errors that **cancel** | Purchases overcast by 50 and sales overcast by 50 |

**Memory aid:** "POOR CC" (Principle, Omission, Original entry, Reversal, Commission, Compensating).

## Errors that do affect the trial balance

- One side of an entry omitted.
- Different amounts on the debit and credit.
- Both entries on the same side.
- An addition (casting) error in one account.
- A balance left out of the trial balance, or entered on the wrong side.
- A balance copied into the trial balance wrongly.

These lead to a suspense account (sub-topic 3.2).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trial balance | A list of all ledger balances at a date, in debit and credit columns |
| Arithmetical accuracy | Debits equal credits in the records |
| Casting | Adding up a column; overcast means added too high |
| Error of commission | Correct amount, correct side, wrong account of the same type |
| Error of principle | Entry in the wrong type of account |
| Error of original entry | Wrong amount used for both entries |
| Error of omission | Transaction left out completely |
| Complete reversal | Debit and credit entries swapped |
| Compensating errors | Errors that cancel each other out |

## Exam traps

- **Discount received** and **purchases returns** are credits.
- **Carriage outwards** is an expense, so it is a debit, the same as carriage inwards.
- An error of principle often **changes profit** even though the trial balance agrees.
- Complete reversal creates a difference in the **account balances** of twice the amount, yet the trial balance totals still agree.
- Do not describe compensating errors as "two errors": they must be of **equal** amounts on opposite sides of the totals.

## Self-test

**1.** State two purposes of a trial balance.

**2.** Give one limitation of a trial balance.

**3.** On which side is a provision for depreciation shown?

**4.** Why is closing inventory not in the trial balance?

**5.** Debit total 62,400, credit total 61,000. One balance is on the wrong side. What is its value, and on which side has it wrongly been placed?

**6.** A cheque from P Shah was credited to P Shaw's account. Name the error.

**7.** Office furniture was debited to the office expenses account. Name the error.

**8.** An invoice for 830 was entered as 380 in the purchases journal and posted. Name the error.

**9.** Wages paid of 1,200 were debited to bank and credited to wages. Name the error.

**10.** Name an error that **does** cause the trial balance totals to differ.

**11.** Which error type is shown by: sales overcast by 200 and rent overcast by 200?

**12.** Is the allowance for irrecoverable debts a debit or a credit balance?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Any two: checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry; shows that errors exist if totals differ; provides a summary of balances for preparing financial statements.

**2.** Any one: six types of error do not affect it; it does not show where an error is; it is only a list at one date.

**3.** The credit side.

**4.** It is calculated at the year end from a stock count and is not in the ledger accounts at the date of the trial balance.

**5.** 700 (half of 1,400); it is a credit balance wrongly shown on the debit side.

**6.** Error of commission.

**7.** Error of principle.

**8.** Error of original entry.

**9.** Complete reversal of entries.

**10.** Any one: one side omitted; different amounts on each side; both entries on the same side; casting error in one account; a balance omitted from the trial balance.

**11.** Compensating errors (sales is a credit balance and rent a debit balance, so both totals rise by 200).

**12.** A credit balance.
