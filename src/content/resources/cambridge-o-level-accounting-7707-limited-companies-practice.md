## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term "equity" for a limited company. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** features of debentures. **[2]**

**3.** Brockley Paints Ltd issued 120,000 ordinary shares of $1.50 each and has called $1.20 a share. Holders of 5,000 shares have not paid the amount called.

**(a)** Calculate the issued share capital. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the called-up share capital. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the paid-up share capital. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why limited liability makes it easier for a company to raise capital. **[2]**

**5.** State the financial statement in which each item appears.

**(a)** Debenture interest for the year. **[1]**

**(b)** Ordinary dividend paid. **[1]**

**(c)** Closing balance of the general reserve. **[1]**

**6.** Tolland Engineering Ltd has $60,000 of 7% preference shares of $1 and 300,000 ordinary shares of $0.50. The directors pay the full preference dividend and an ordinary dividend of $0.04 a share. Calculate the total dividends paid. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Rowan Bay Freight Ltd is a service company. At 1 April 2028 its equity was: ordinary shares of $1, $150,000; general reserve, $12,000; retained earnings, $33,500. During the year ended 31 March 2029:

- 50,000 ordinary shares of $1 were issued at $1 each, fully paid, on 1 October 2028
- profit for the year was $46,800
- $7,000 was transferred to the general reserve
- an ordinary dividend of $0.05 a share was paid on 31 March 2029 on all shares then in issue.

**(a)** Prepare the statement of changes in equity for the year ended 31 March 2029. **[7]**

**(b)** Explain why the transfer to the general reserve does not change total equity. **[2]**

**8.** Halden Print Ltd has a gross profit of $164,000 for the year ended 31 December 2028. Expenses before adjustments total $98,750. The following have not been recorded:

- electricity accrued, $1,250
- insurance prepaid, $900
- depreciation of machinery at 10% a year on cost of $85,000.

The company has $50,000 of 7% debentures. Interest of $1,750 has been paid during the year and is not included in the expenses above.

**(a)** Calculate the profit from operations. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**

**(c)** State the debenture interest shown under other payables at 31 December 2028. **[1]**

**9.** Selina and Tariq run a furniture partnership. They need $200,000 to open a second workshop and are considering converting the business into a limited company.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages to Selina and Tariq of operating as a limited company. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages of operating as a limited company. **[4]**

**(c)** Advise Selina and Tariq whether to convert. Justify your answer. **[2]**

**10.** Calder Mills Ltd has 200,000 ordinary shares of $1 in issue and no other share capital or loans. It needs $100,000 for new machinery and expects profit from operations of $40,000 a year afterwards. It can either issue 100,000 more ordinary shares of $1 at $1 each, or issue $100,000 of 8% debentures.

**(a)** Calculate the expected profit for the year under each option. **[2]**

**(b)** The directors plan to pay all of the profit for the year as an ordinary dividend. Calculate the dividend per share under each option. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** reason why the existing ordinary shareholders might prefer each option. **[4]**

**11.** The draft statements of Mereford Foods Ltd for the year ended 30 June 2028 show profit for the year of $61,400 and retained earnings at 30 June 2028 of $88,000. These errors were then found:

- closing inventory was overstated by $2,600
- rent of $1,200 paid for July 2028 was charged in full as an expense
- debenture interest of $900 owing at 30 June 2028 was not recorded.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the corrected retained earnings at 30 June 2028. **[1]**

**(c)** State the effect of correcting the rent error on the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**12.** The following balances relate to Ingleby Tools Ltd at 30 September 2028:

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Ordinary shares of $0.50 (240,000 shares issued, fully paid) | 120,000 |
| 6% preference shares of $1 | 40,000 |
| General reserve | 25,000 |
| Retained earnings | 47,300 |
| 9% debentures, repayable 2035 | 30,000 |
| Trade payables | 18,450 |
| Debenture interest owing | 1,350 |
| Bank overdraft | 4,200 |

**(a)** Prepare the equity and liabilities section of the statement of financial position at 30 September 2028. **[6]**

**(b)** State how the debentures would be classified if they were repayable in six months' time. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The shareholders' interest in the company [1], equal to total assets less total liabilities (share capital plus reserves) [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "What the company owns" describes assets, not equity; the deduction of liabilities is the point.

**2.** Any two: they are long-term loans to the company [1]; interest is at a fixed rate and must be paid whether or not a profit is made [1]; they are repaid on an agreed date; holders are lenders, not owners, and do not vote; they are shown as a non-current liability. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 120,000 x $1.50 = **$180,000** [1].

**(b)** 120,000 x $1.20 = **$144,000** [1].

**(c)** Unpaid: 5,000 x $1.20 = $6,000 [1]; paid-up = 144,000 - 6,000 = **$138,000** [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Investors know they can lose no more than the amount paid, or unpaid, on their shares, so their personal assets are safe [1]. The lower risk encourages more people to buy shares, so the company can raise more capital [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Statement of profit or loss (finance costs) [1].

**(b)** Statement of changes in equity [1].

**(c)** Statement of financial position (also accept: statement of changes in equity, closing line) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Preference dividend: 7% x 60,000 = $4,200 [1]. Ordinary dividend: 300,000 x $0.04 = $12,000 [1]. Total = **$16,200** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The ordinary dividend uses the number of shares, not the $150,000 nominal value.

**7. (a)**

```
Rowan Bay Freight Ltd
Statement of changes in equity for the year ended 31 March 2029
                          Ordinary  General  Retained    Total
                             share  reserve  earnings
                           capital
                               USD      USD       USD      USD
At 1 April 2028            150,000   12,000    33,500  195,500
Shares issued               50,000                      50,000
Profit for the year                            46,800   46,800
Transfer to general reserve          7,000    (7,000)        -
Ordinary dividend paid                        (10,000) (10,000)
At 31 March 2029           200,000   19,000    63,300  282,300
```

Marks: heading and opening balances with total $195,500 [1]; shares issued $50,000 [1]; profit for the year $46,800 [1]; transfer shown in both columns [1]; dividend 200,000 x $0.05 = $10,000 [1]; closing share capital $200,000 and general reserve $19,000 [1]; closing retained earnings $63,300 and total $282,300 [1].

**(b)** The transfer moves $7,000 out of retained earnings and into the general reserve [1]. Both are parts of equity, so one rises as the other falls and no asset or liability changes [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The dividend is on the 200,000 shares in issue on 31 March, not the 150,000 held at the start.

**8. (a)** Expenses = 98,750 + 1,250 - 900 [1] + 8,500 depreciation [1] = $107,600 [1]. Profit from operations = 164,000 - 107,600 = **$56,400** [1].

**(b)** Finance cost = 7% x 50,000 = $3,500 [1]. Profit for the year = 56,400 - 3,500 = **$52,900** [1].

**(c)** 3,500 - 1,750 = **$1,750** [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Deducting only the $1,750 paid gives $54,650 and loses the finance-cost mark; the charge is always the full year's interest.

**9. (a)** Any two, each explained: limited liability [1], so the partners would not risk their homes if the expansion failed [1]; more capital can be raised by issuing shares to other investors [1], which could provide the $200,000 without relying only on their savings [1]; continuity, as the company carries on if a shareholder dies or leaves [1], giving lenders and customers more confidence [1].

**(b)** Any two, each explained: setting up costs more and needs legal formalities [1], which uses money they need for the workshop [1]; accounts must follow legal rules and may be available to the public [1], so competitors could see their results [1]; if new shareholders join, profit is shared more widely [1] and Selina and Tariq could lose some control [1].

**(c)** A justified decision, for example: they should convert [1], because borrowing or investing $200,000 is a large risk and limited liability protects their personal assets, which outweighs the extra cost [1]. Also credit a reasoned decision not to convert, for example if they wish to keep full control and can fund the expansion themselves. **[10]**

**10. (a)** Shares: no finance cost, so profit for the year = **$40,000** [1]. Debentures: 40,000 - (8% x 100,000 = 8,000) = **$32,000** [1].

**(b)** Shares: 40,000 / 300,000 = **$0.13 a share** (13.33 cents) [1]. Debentures: 32,000 / 200,000 = **$0.16 a share** [1].

**(c)** Prefer debentures: the dividend per share is higher, and no new shareholders join, so existing owners keep their share of control [1], with an explanation linked to $0.16 against $0.13 [1]. Prefer shares: there is no fixed interest to pay [1], so if profit falls in a bad year the company is not forced to pay $8,000, which lowers the risk to the business [1]. **[8]**

**11. (a)** 61,400 - 2,600 [1] + 1,200 [1] - 900 [1] = **$59,100** [1].

**(b)** Profit falls by 2,300, so retained earnings = 88,000 - 2,300 = **$85,700** [1].

**(c)** Other receivables (current assets) increase by $1,200 [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Each correction changes profit for the year and retained earnings by the same amount, so (b) needs no new working.

**12. (a)**

*Examiner insight:* A bank overdraft is a current liability, even though it is a form of borrowing.
