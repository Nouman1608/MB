Condensed recall for **4.7.1 Adaptations, interdependence and competition** (4.7.1.1 to 4.7.1.4) of the AQA
GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016, assessed on Paper 2. Everything here is for
both tiers.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [study guide for this section](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-adaptations-interdependence-and-competition/).
Then try the [practice questions for this section](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-adaptations-interdependence-and-competition-practice/).
The whole-topic summary is in the [Ecology revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-ecology-revision-notes/).

## Levels of organisation

```
individual → population (one species) → community (all species) → ecosystem (community + abiotic parts)
```

- **Habitat**: the place where an organism lives.
- **Ecosystem**: the **interaction** of a community of living organisms (biotic) with the non-living (abiotic)
  parts of their environment.

## Competition

| Who | Competes for |
|---|---|
| Plants | Light, space, water, mineral ions |
| Animals | Food, mates, territory |

- Competition can be within one species or between different species.
- In a "suggest" question, choose the resource that is **limited** in the habitat described.

## Interdependence

- Species depend on each other for **food, shelter, pollination, seed dispersal**.
- Removing one species can affect the **whole community**.
- **Stable community**: all species and environmental factors in balance; population sizes fairly constant.

**Chain-of-effects frame:** change → effect on organism 1 (increase or decrease) → effect on organism 2 →
effect on the organism in the question.

Worked reminder: a disease kills most of the hedgehogs in a garden. Hedgehogs eat slugs. Slugs eat lettuce.

```
fewer hedgehogs → fewer slugs eaten → slug numbers increase
→ more lettuce eaten → lettuce decreases
```

## The seven abiotic factors

| Factor | Key link |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | Photosynthesis |
| Temperature | Enzyme activity |
| Moisture levels | Water for photosynthesis and support; drying out |
| Soil pH and mineral content | Which plants grow; nitrates for protein |
| Wind intensity and direction | Water loss and heat loss; physical damage |
| Carbon dioxide levels (plants) | Raw material for photosynthesis |
| Oxygen levels (aquatic animals) | Aerobic respiration |

## The four biotic factors

1. Availability of food.
2. New predators arriving.
3. New pathogens.
4. One species outcompeting another so its numbers are no longer sufficient to breed.

## Adaptations

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Structural | A body feature | Small ears reduce heat loss in cold climates; a thick waxy cuticle reduces water loss in desert plants |
| Behavioural | Something the organism does | Huddling together for warmth; being active at night in the desert |
| Functional | A process inside the body | Making very concentrated urine; hibernation with a lowered metabolic rate |

**Extremophiles**: organisms living in very extreme conditions of **temperature, pressure or salt
concentration**. Example from the specification: **bacteria in deep-sea vents**.

## Explaining an adaptation in three steps

```
feature → what it does → how that helps survival in this habitat
```

Example: a cactus has spines instead of leaves → smaller surface area for water loss → it conserves water in a
dry desert.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population | All the organisms of one species living in a habitat |
| Community | All the populations of different species living in a habitat |
| Ecosystem | A community interacting with the abiotic parts of its environment |
| Abiotic factor | A non-living factor that affects a community |
| Biotic factor | A living factor that affects a community |
| Competition | Organisms trying to obtain the same limited resource |
| Interdependence | Species depending on each other, so a change to one affects others |
| Stable community | Species and environmental factors in balance, populations fairly constant |
| Adaptation | A feature that enables an organism to survive in its normal conditions |
| Extremophile | An organism living in an extremely hot, high-pressure or salty environment |

## Exam traps

- An ecosystem includes **abiotic** parts; a community does not.
- Plants do **not** compete for food.
- Oxygen is listed for **aquatic animals**; carbon dioxide is listed for **plants**.
- A chain of effects needs **increase** or **decrease** at every step.
- "It is adapted to its environment" earns nothing; name the feature and explain it.
- Correlation in data does not prove that one factor causes the change.

## Self-test

1. Put these in order from smallest to largest: community, individual, ecosystem, population.
2. Define an ecosystem.
3. Name four things plants compete for.
4. Name three things animals compete for.
5. Give three things a species may depend on another species for.
6. What is a stable community?
7. Name four abiotic factors from the specification.
8. Which abiotic factor is listed specifically for aquatic animals?
9. Name the four biotic factors in the specification.
10. State the type of adaptation shown by birds migrating south in autumn.
11. What is an extremophile? Give the example named in the specification.
12. Owls eat voles; voles eat grass seeds. Explain what could happen to grass seeds if owl numbers fell.

### Answers

1. Individual, population, community, ecosystem.
2. The interaction of a community of living organisms with the non-living parts of their environment.
3. Light, space, water, mineral ions.
4. Food, mates, territory.
5. Any three: food, shelter, pollination, seed dispersal.
6. One where all the species and environmental factors are in balance, so population sizes remain fairly
   constant.
7. Any four: light intensity; temperature; moisture levels; soil pH and mineral content; wind intensity and
   direction; carbon dioxide levels for plants; oxygen levels for aquatic animals.
8. Oxygen levels.
9. Availability of food; new predators arriving; new pathogens; one species outcompeting another so its numbers
   are too low to breed.
10. Behavioural.
11. An organism that lives in an extreme environment (high temperature, pressure or salt concentration);
    bacteria living in deep-sea vents.
12. Fewer owls → fewer voles eaten → vole numbers increase → more grass seeds eaten → fewer grass seeds.
