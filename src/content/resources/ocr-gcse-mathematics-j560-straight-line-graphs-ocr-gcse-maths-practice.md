These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 7.02, Straight line graphs. Questions 1 to 10 are for both tiers. Questions 11 to 13 use content that is **Higher tier only** and are labelled. No diagrams are needed; sketch your own axes where it helps. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [straight line graphs study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-straight-line-graphs-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [straight line graphs revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-straight-line-graphs-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Find the gradient of the line through (−3, 4) and (5, −2). **[2]**

**2.** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 6x − 3y = 9. **[2]**

**3.** Here are five lines.

A: y = 3x + 2

B: 2y = 6x − 5

C: y = 2 − 3x

D: 3y = x + 6

E: 6x − 2y = 1

Which two lines are parallel to line A? **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** Show that the point (4, −5) lies on the line 3x + 2y = 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Does the point (2, 1) lie on the same line? Give a reason. **[1]**

**5.** Find the equation of the line with gradient −4 that passes through (2, 3). **[2]**

**6.** Describe the sketch of y = 6 − 1.5x, giving the coordinates of the points where it crosses both axes. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A line passes through (−1, 8) and (3, −4).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where the line crosses the x-axis. **[1]**

**8.** A tank drains at a constant rate. After 3 minutes it holds 74 litres. After 8 minutes it holds 44 litres. The volume V litres after t minutes is a straight-line graph.

**(a)** Find the gradient of the graph. **[2]**

**(b)** Find an equation for V in terms of t. **[1]**

**(c)** Interpret the gradient and the intercept in this context. **[2]**

**(d)** Find how long it takes for the tank to empty. **[1]**

**9.** Line L passes through (0, −3) and (4, 5).

**(a)** Find the equation of L. **[2]**

**(b)** Line M is parallel to L and passes through (−1, 6). Find the equation of M. **[2]**

**(c)** Find where line M crosses the x-axis. **[1]**

**10.** Find the equation of the line through (−4, 1) and (2, 5). Give your answer in the form ax + by = c, where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Find the equation of the line that passes through (5, −2) and is perpendicular to 2x + 5y = 3. **[4]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** P is (−3, 1) and Q is (5, 5).

**(a)** Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of PQ. **[4]**

**(b)** Show that the point (3, −1) lies on the perpendicular bisector. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** A circle has equation x² + y² = 45.

**(a)** Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at the point (6, −3). **[4]**

**(b)** The tangent meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. O is the origin. Find the area of triangle OAB. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** m = (−2 − 4)/(5 − (−3)) = −6/8 [1]; **m = −3/4** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep the order of the points the same on top and bottom. Mixing the order gives the right size with the wrong sign.

**2.** −3y = −6x + 9, so y = 2x − 3 [1]; **gradient 2, y-intercept −3** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing by −3 changes every sign. The intercept is the constant term in y = mx + c form, not the 9 in the original.

**3.** B is y = 3x − 2.5 and E is y = 3x − 0.5 [1]; **B and E** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* C has gradient −3 and D has gradient 1/3; neither matches. Rearrange every line before comparing.

**4. (a)** 3(4) + 2(−5) = 12 − 10 = 2, which matches the right-hand side [1]

**(b)** **No**: 3(2) + 2(1) = 8, not 2 [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer ends with a statement that the two sides agree.

**5.** 3 = −4(2) + c, so c = 11 [1]; **y = −4x + 11** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Substituting the point into y = mx + c is quicker and safer than guessing the intercept from a sketch.

**6.** Crosses the y-axis at **(0, 6)** and the x-axis at **(4, 0)**, since 6 − 1.5x = 0 gives x = 4 [1]; a straight line **falling** from left to right through these points (negative gradient) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A sketch is judged on the direction of slope and the labelled intercepts. Plotting a table of values is not needed.

**7. (a)** m = (−4 − 8)/(3 − (−1)) = −12/4 = −3 [1]; 8 = −3(−1) + c, so c = 5 [1]; **y = −3x + 5** [1]

**(b)** 0 = −3x + 5, x = 5/3, so **(5/3, 0)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Check with the other point: −3(3) + 5 = −4. It takes seconds and catches sign errors.

**8. (a)** (44 − 74)/(8 − 3) [1]; **−6** [1]

**(b)** 74 = −6(3) + c gives c = 92, so **V = −6t + 92** [1]

**(c)** The tank loses **6 litres per minute** [1]; it held **92 litres at the start** (t = 0) [1]

**(d)** 0 = −6t + 92, t = 15⅓, so **15 minutes 20 seconds** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A negative gradient in context means the quantity is decreasing. Say "decreases by 6 litres per minute"; "the gradient is −6" alone does not interpret it.

**9. (a)** m = (5 − (−3))/(4 − 0) = 2 [1]; **y = 2x − 3** [1]

**(b)** Gradient 2; 6 = 2(−1) + c, c = 8 [1]; **y = 2x + 8** [1]

**(c)** 0 = 2x + 8, so **(−4, 0)** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The point (0, −3) is on the y-axis, so it gives c straight away.

**10.** m = (5 − 1)/(2 − (−4)) = 4/6 = 2/3 [1]; 5 = (2/3)(2) + c, c = 11/3 [1]; 3y = 2x + 11, so **2x − 3y = −11** (or −2x + 3y = 11) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Multiply every term by 3 to clear the fractions. Check with (−4, 1): −8 − 3 = −11.

**11.** 5y = −2x + 3, gradient −2/5 [1]; perpendicular gradient 5/2 [1]; −2 = (5/2)(5) + c, c = −14.5 [1]; **y = 2.5x − 14.5** (or 2y = 5x − 29) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Flip the fraction and change the sign. Writing 2/5 or −5/2 each earns no credit for the perpendicular gradient.

**12. (a)** Midpoint (1, 3) [1]; gradient PQ = 4/8 = 1/2 [1]; perpendicular gradient −2 [1]; 3 = −2 + c, c = 5, so **y = −2x + 5** [1]

**(b)** −2(3) + 5 = −1, so (3, −1) lies on the line [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The perpendicular bisector must pass through the midpoint. Using P or Q instead of the midpoint is the most common error.

**13. (a)** 36 + 9 = 45, so the point is on the circle; gradient of radius = −3/6 = −1/2 [1]; tangent gradient 2 [1]; −3 = 2(6) + c, c = −15 [1]; **y = 2x − 15** [1]

**(b)** A is (7.5, 0) and B is (0, −15) [1]; area = ½ × 7.5 × 15 = **56.25 square units** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The tangent is perpendicular to the radius, so its gradient is the negative reciprocal of the radius gradient. Use lengths (positive) for the area, even though B has a negative coordinate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the order of the points between the top and bottom of the gradient formula.
- Losing signs when subtracting negative coordinates.
- Comparing gradients before rearranging into y = mx + c.
- Forgetting to divide every term when rearranging.
- Interpreting a gradient without units or without saying increase or decrease.
- Perpendicular gradients with only half the change made.
- Using an end point instead of the midpoint for a perpendicular bisector.
- Using the radius gradient for a tangent.
