Condensed notes for sub-topic 3.2, Global supply chain management: types of chain, its features, benefits, six types of risk and why risk assessment matters.

## Four facts to start with

1. A **simple** chain is supplier, manufacturer, consumer. A **complex** chain adds agent, wholesaler and retailer.
2. The chain has three parts: **upstream**, **the enterprise** and **downstream**.
3. Benefits include lower production and labour costs, lower purchasing costs, and better logistics and communication.
4. Risks are environmental, natural disasters, financial, ethical, technological and political.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Supply chain | Sequence of businesses and activities that move a product to the consumer |
| Global supply chain | A supply chain that crosses national borders |
| Upstream | Suppliers and activities before the business |
| The enterprise | The business itself, turning inputs into products |
| Downstream | Distribution and selling after the business |
| Supplier | Business that provides materials, parts or services |
| Risk assessment | Identifying and judging risks and deciding what to do about them |

## The two types of chain

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| Supplier → manufacturer → consumer | Supplier → agent → wholesaler → retailer → consumer |
| Few links | Many links |
| Lower added cost | Each link adds cost but does a job |

## Upstream, enterprise, downstream

| Part | Example for a bakery |
|---|---|
| Upstream | Farm growing wheat; mill making flour |
| The enterprise | The bakery making bread |
| Downstream | Delivery van; supermarket; customer |

## Importance of suppliers

Reliable delivery; right quality; fair prices; credit terms; extra supply when demand rises. Using more than one supplier lowers the risk of failure.

## Benefits of global supply chain management

- Lower cost of production
- Lower labour costs
- Lower cost of purchasing
- Advancement in logistics
- Advancement in communication

## The six risks

| Risk | Example |
|---|---|
| Environmental | Pollution; deforestation |
| Natural disasters | Flood, earthquake, storm |
| Financial | Supplier bankruptcy; exchange rate changes |
| Ethical | Child labour; unsafe conditions |
| Technological | System failure; hacking |
| Political | War; new tariffs; unstable government |

## Worked example: per-unit cost

Home cost $8.50. Overseas price $6.20 plus $0.90 shipping and customs = $7.10. Saving = $1.40 per unit. For 10,000 units: $14,000.

## Model answers to learn

**Why is risk assessment important?** It identifies possible problems and their effects before they happen. The business can then take action, such as using a second supplier or holding extra stock, so that production and deliveries are not stopped.

**Explain why a business may use more than one supplier.** If one supplier fails because of a flood or bankruptcy, another can supply the goods, so production continues.

## Developing a risk point

| Risk | Weak point | Developed point |
|---|---|---|
| Natural disaster | "Floods are bad" | "A flood closes the port, so parts do not arrive and production stops" |
| Financial | "Money problems" | "The supplier goes bankrupt, so the firm must find a new supplier quickly, probably at a higher price" |
| Political | "Politics" | "A new tariff raises the price of imported parts, so costs rise" |
| Technological | "Computers fail" | "A hacked order system cannot place orders, so stock runs out" |

## Benefits against risks

| Benefits of a global chain | Risks of a global chain |
|---|---|
| Lower production and labour costs | Delays over long distances |
| Lower purchasing costs | Disasters, political and financial problems |
| Better logistics and communication | Ethical and environmental harm to reputation |

## Exam traps

- **Upstream and downstream swapped.**
- **Simple chain given with a wholesaler.**
- **Risk named but not explained.**
- **Only benefits described** when asked to evaluate.
- **Forgetting shipping costs** in cost comparisons.
- **Ethical and environmental confused.** Ethical concerns fairness to people; environmental concerns nature.

## Self-test

1. Name the links in a simple supply chain.
2. Name the links in a complex supply chain.
3. What does upstream mean?
4. Give two reasons suppliers are important.
5. Give two benefits of global supply chain management.
6. Name the six types of supply chain risk.
7. Give an example of a political risk.
8. What is risk assessment?
9. Why might a business use two suppliers?
10. A part costs $5.00 plus $0.75 shipping. What is the cost per unit?

**Answers**

1. Supplier, manufacturer, consumer.
2. Supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer.
3. Suppliers and activities before the business, such as sourcing materials.
4. Any two: on-time delivery, right quality, fair prices, credit terms, extra supply.
5. Any two: lower cost of production, lower labour costs, lower purchasing costs, better logistics, better communication.
6. Environmental, natural disasters, financial, ethical, technological, political.
7. Any one: war, a new tariff, an unstable government, a change in law.
8. Identifying risks, judging how likely and serious they are, and deciding what to do.
9. If one fails, the other can supply, so production continues.
10. $5.75.

## Command words to expect

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Identify, State, Give | A short answer with no explanation |
| Define | A precise meaning |
| Describe | The main points or features |
| Explain | A reason, linked to the situation, with a development |
| Analyse | Break the issue into parts and show how they connect |
| Assess, Consider | A reasoned judgement about importance or effect |
| Discuss | Both sides of an issue, in a structured way |
| Justify | A case backed with evidence |
| Evaluate | A judgement of value, ending with a conclusion |
