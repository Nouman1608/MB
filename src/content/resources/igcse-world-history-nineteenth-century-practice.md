## Section B

**4.** Bismarck fought three wars between 1864 and 1871.

**(a)** Name the three opponents. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe Bismarck's method, in three steps. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the Ems Telegram was significant. **[4]**

**5.** Study this source.

> *"The Serbian government has replied to nearly every demand we made. There is
> now no reason for war. Yet Vienna presses on, and Berlin does not restrain her."*
> — Diary entry of a German diplomat, July 1914

**(a)** Using NOP, assess how useful this source is to a historian studying the outbreak of war. **[6]**

**(b)** A student writes: "This source is biased because it is one person's opinion, so it is not useful." Explain why this reasoning is wrong. **[3]**

**6.** "The assassination of Franz Ferdinand was the main cause of the First World War." How far do you agree? **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: the revolutionaries were **divided between liberals and radicals** [1]; the **middle classes withdrew support** once property seemed threatened [1]; the peasantry remained largely uninvolved; the conservative powers **kept control of their armies**.

**2.** Triple Alliance: **Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy** [1] [1]. Triple Entente: **Britain, France, Russia** [1] [1].

**3.** **M**ilitarism [1], **A**lliances [1], **N**ationalism [1], **I**mperialism [1], **A**ssassination [1].

**4. (a)** **Denmark** (1864) [1], **Austria** (1866) [1], **France** (1870–71) [1].

**(b)** **Isolate** each opponent diplomatically first [1]; fight a **limited war for a defined objective** [1]; **stop once that objective is achieved** rather than pursuing total victory [1].

**(c)** Bismarck edited the telegram to make it appear insulting [1], which **provoked France into declaring war** [1]. Because France appeared the aggressor, the **southern German states joined Prussia voluntarily** [1] rather than being annexed — so the result was a **German Empire rather than a Prussian conquest** [1].

**5. (a)** **Nature** — a private **diary entry**, so it was not written for publication and the author had no reason to distort for an audience [1] [1].
**Origin** — a **German diplomat**, writing in **July 1914**, so it is contemporary and from someone with **inside knowledge** of Berlin's position [1] [1].
**Purpose** — a personal record, suggesting **candour**, though it reflects only **one individual's view** and he may not have known the full diplomatic picture [1] [1].

**(b)** Being one person's opinion does **not** make a source useless [1]. It is **highly useful as evidence of how the crisis was viewed from inside the German diplomatic service** [1]. Usefulness depends on the **question being asked** — it is weak evidence of what Vienna actually decided, but strong evidence of contemporary German attitudes [1].
*"Biased therefore useless" inverts the skill being tested.*

**6.** *Indicative content — a full answer should:*

**Argue the assassination was the main cause:** it was the **immediate trigger** [1]; without it the July Crisis would not have begun when it did [1]; it activated the alliance obligations directly [1].

**Argue other causes mattered more:** **militarism** and the naval race created readiness for war [1]; the **alliance system** turned a Balkan dispute into a continental war [1]; **nationalism** in the Balkans made conflict there likely regardless [1]; the **Schlieffen Plan** meant military timetables overrode diplomacy once Russia mobilised [1].

**Reach a judgement:** the assassination was the **spark, not the cause** [1] — another incident could have served, precisely because the long-term causes had already made the material combustible [1]. State the criterion on which the judgement rests [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dismissing a source as useless because it is biased.
- Describing a source rather than evaluating it with NOP.
- Listing causes without explaining how each contributed.
- A conclusion that summarises rather than judges.
- Vague evidence with no dates, names or treaties.
