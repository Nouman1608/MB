## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 6: up to four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Towards the Ka'aba in Makkah (the Qiblah) [1].

**2.** Khutbah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] for each correct pair: Fajr, 2; Zuhr, 4; Asr, 4; Maghrib, 3; Isha, 4.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: shortening four-rak'ah prayers to two when travelling; combining prayers when travelling; praying sitting or lying down if ill; using Tayammum when water is unavailable or harmful; a shortened prayer in danger.

**5.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and developed [1].

- **Congregation** [1]: at the mosque Muslims pray behind an imam in rows, which shows unity and equality, while at home prayer can be individual or family [1].
- **Privacy** [1]: at home a person can pray quietly and add extra prayers, while the mosque is public [1].
- **Special prayers** [1]: Jummah, Eid and Taraweeh are held at the mosque, while many sunnah prayers are prayed at home [1].
- **Reward** [1]: praying in congregation is described as 27 times more rewarding [1].

**6.** The Azaan announces that the prayer time has come [1] and calls people to the mosque [1]. It contains the Shahadah and "Allahu Akbar", so it reminds Muslims of Tawhid several times a day [1] and publicly proclaims the faith to the community [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Structure and discipline** [1]: five set times divide the day, so a Muslim organises work and rest around prayer [1].
- **Remembrance of Allah** [1]: regular prayer keeps Allah in mind and gives peace [1].
- **Moral guidance** [1]: standing before Allah reminds a Muslim to be honest and kind [1].
- **Unity and equality** [1]: praying in rows behind one imam and facing one direction shows that all Muslims are equal [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Qur'an 29:45) [2].
- "Establish prayer for My remembrance" (20:14) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Command of Allah** [1]: the Qur'an tells believers to go to the remembrance of Allah on Friday and leave trade [1].
- **Community** [1]: Muslims of a town gather weekly in the mosque, which builds friendship and unity [1].
- **Education** [1]: the khutbah teaches Muslims about their faith and community matters [1].
- **Special day** [1]: Friday is described as the best day, so the prayer has particular reward [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade" (Qur'an 62:9) [2].
- The Hadith that Friday is the best day on which the sun rises (Sahih Muslim) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Verse 9 gives Salah priority over trade.
- The five prayers fall at set times, and the Qur'an says they are prescribed for specific times.
- Prayer reminds a Muslim of Allah and keeps work in perspective.
- **Against the statement:** verse 10 tells believers to "disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah" after prayer, so work, study and family duties are also part of faith; Islam permits concessions, such as shortening or combining prayers, so that daily life is not made impossible; some duties, such as caring for a sick person, can be a good reason to delay.
- The balance in the passage shows that prayer comes first at its time, but is not the only thing in life.
- **Judgement:** Salah has priority in time and importance, but Islam balances it with work and service, so it is not an excuse to neglect other duties.

*Use of the passage*

- "proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade" supports the statement.
- "That is better for you, if you only knew" gives the reason: worship is of higher value.
- "disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah" challenges the statement by showing balance.
- "they rushed to it and left you standing" warns against being distracted by trade or amusement.
- "What is with Allah is better than diversion and than a transaction" shows the reward of putting Allah first.
