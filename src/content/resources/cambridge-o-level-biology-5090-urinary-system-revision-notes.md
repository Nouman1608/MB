These notes condense subtopic **13.2 Urinary system** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the organs of the urinary system, why urea must be removed, the structure and function of a nephron, and the liver's role in assimilation and deamination. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [13.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-urinary-system/). Then try the [13.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-urinary-system-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Excretion | Removal of toxic materials and waste products of metabolism from organisms |
| Urea | Toxic waste made in the liver from excess amino acids |
| Kidney | Removes urea and excess salts and water from the blood as urine |
| Why excrete urea | It is toxic, so a build-up would harm cells |

## Organs of the urinary system

| Organ | Function |
|---|---|
| Kidneys (2) | Remove urea and excess salts and water from the blood as urine |
| Ureters (2) | Carry urine from the kidneys to the bladder |
| Bladder | Stores urine |
| Urethra | Carries urine from the bladder out of the body |

**Order of flow:** kidney, ureter, bladder, urethra, outside.

## The liver and urea

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Assimilation of amino acids | The liver builds amino acids into proteins, such as plasma proteins |
| Deamination | The nitrogen-containing part of an excess amino acid is removed |
| Fate of the nitrogen-containing part | Made into urea, which goes into the blood to the kidneys |
| Fate of the rest | Used in respiration for energy, or changed to carbohydrate or fat |

- Amino acids reach the liver in the hepatic portal vein after digestion.
- The body cannot store amino acids, so the excess is deaminated.
- The liver **makes** urea. The kidney **removes** it.

## Structure of a nephron

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| Bowman's capsule | Cup-shaped; surrounds the glomerulus |
| Glomerulus | Knot of capillaries inside the capsule; high pressure |
| Tubules | Coiled tubes where reabsorption happens |
| Loop of Henle | Hairpin-shaped part of the tubule |
| Collecting duct | Collects liquid from several nephrons and leads to the ureter |

Capillaries surround the tubules and take back the reabsorbed substances.

## Function of a nephron

1. **Filtration** in the glomerulus. High pressure forces water, glucose, urea and ions out of the blood into the Bowman's capsule. Proteins and blood cells are too large and stay in the blood.
2. **Reabsorption** in the tubules. All the glucose, some of the ions and most of the water are taken back into the blood.
3. **Urine formation.** What is left contains urea, excess water and excess ions. It passes to the collecting duct, ureter and bladder.

Details of the processes are not required at this level.

## Blood, filtrate and urine compared

| Substance | Blood plasma | Filtrate | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | Present | Absent | Absent |
| Glucose | Present | Present | Absent (all reabsorbed) |
| Urea | Present | Present | Present, more concentrated |
| Water | Present | Present | Present, but most reabsorbed |
| Ions | Present | Present | Some present (excess) |

## Worked calculation

*A person's kidneys filter 150 dm3 of liquid from the blood in a day and produce 1.5 dm3 of urine. What percentage of the filtered liquid is reabsorbed?*

1. Volume reabsorbed = 150 - 1.5 = 148.5 dm3.
2. Percentage = 148.5 divided by 150 x 100 = 99 per cent.

Use the starting volume (the filtered volume) as the divisor. The figures are for practising the method only.

## The path of urea in order

1. Protein is digested to amino acids.
2. Amino acids are absorbed into the blood and carried to the liver.
3. Excess amino acids are deaminated and urea is made.
4. Urea is carried in the blood to the kidney.
5. Urea is filtered at the glomerulus and passes along the nephron.
6. Urine runs down the ureter to the bladder and out through the urethra.

## What to say about each stage of a diagram question

- **Glomerulus:** high pressure, filtration, small molecules only.
- **Bowman's capsule:** collects the filtrate.
- **Tubule:** reabsorption of glucose, some ions and water.
- **Loop of Henle:** hairpin-shaped part of the tubule.
- **Collecting duct:** carries urine to the ureter.

## Short model answers

**Explain the need for excretion.** Urea is toxic. If it were not removed it would build up in the blood and tissue fluid and harm cells.

**Describe what happens in the glomerulus.** High pressure forces small molecules (water, glucose, urea and ions) out of the blood into the Bowman's capsule. Large molecules such as protein stay in the blood.

**Explain why glucose is not found in the urine of a healthy person.** It is filtered but then all reabsorbed into the blood in the tubules.

**Describe deamination.** The nitrogen-containing part of an excess amino acid is removed in the liver and made into urea.

**State the function of the bladder.** It stores urine until it is released.

## Exam traps

- The **liver** makes urea. The **kidney** removes it.
- Ureter (kidney to bladder) is not the urethra (bladder to outside).
- The bladder stores urine. It does not make it.
- Only the **excess** water and ions are lost.
- Glucose is filtered **and** then fully reabsorbed.
- Deamination removes the nitrogen-containing part, not the whole amino acid.
- Excretion is not egestion. Egestion removes undigested food.
- Do not use "filtration" for the tubules. That is reabsorption.

## Self-test

1. Define excretion.
2. Name the four organs of the urinary system in the order urine passes through them, starting with the kidney.
3. State the function of the ureter.
4. Explain why urea must be removed from the body.
5. Where is urea made?
6. Name the process in which the nitrogen-containing part of an amino acid is removed.
7. Name the knot of capillaries in the nephron.
8. Name the structure that surrounds the knot of capillaries.
9. Name one substance that is filtered but absent from the urine of a healthy person, and one that is present in urine.
10. Which part of the nephron receives liquid from several nephrons?
11. State what happens to all the glucose in the filtrate.
12. State what the liver does to amino acids that are needed for protein.

### Answers

1. The removal of toxic materials and waste products of metabolism from organisms.
2. Kidney, ureter, bladder, urethra.
3. It carries urine from the kidney to the bladder.
4. Urea is toxic, so it would build up and harm cells.
5. In the liver, from excess amino acids.
6. Deamination.
7. The glomerulus.
8. The Bowman's capsule.
9. Glucose is filtered but then all reabsorbed, so it is absent from urine. Urea is present in urine.
10. The collecting duct.
11. It is reabsorbed into the blood.
12. It builds them into proteins (assimilation).
