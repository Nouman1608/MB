## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the de Broglie wavelength. **[1]**

**2.** For each of the following, state whether it provides evidence for the wave nature or the particulate nature of electromagnetic radiation.

**(a)** Diffraction of light by a grating. **[1]**

**(b)** Emission of electrons from a zinc plate by ultraviolet radiation. **[1]**

**(c)** Interference of microwaves from two slits. **[1]**

**3.** A neutron of mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg moves at 2200 m s⁻¹. Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how the existence of a threshold frequency in the photoelectric effect provides evidence for the particulate nature of electromagnetic radiation. **[2]**

**5.** A dust grain of mass 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ kg drifts at 1.0 × 10⁻³ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the grain will never show diffraction effects. **[1]**

**6.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 2.0 kV.

**(a)** Calculate its kinetic energy in joules. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its momentum. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** In an electron diffraction tube, a beam of electrons passes through a thin layer of polycrystalline graphite and strikes a fluorescent screen.

**(a)** Describe the pattern seen on the screen. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how this pattern provides evidence for the wave nature of electrons. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why graphite is a suitable target, and why the pattern has circular symmetry. **[2]**

**8.** The accelerating p.d. in the tube of Question 7 is increased from 2.0 kV to 8.0 kV.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio new wavelength ÷ old wavelength. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe and explain the change in the pattern on the screen. **[3]**

**9.** An electron and a proton are each accelerated from rest through the same potential difference of 2.0 kV.

**(a)** Show that the ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths, λₑ/λₚ, is equal to √(mₚ/mₑ). **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate this ratio. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why protons of this energy would not produce a visible diffraction pattern with a graphite target. **[1]**

**10.** A photon and an electron both have a wavelength of 2.75 × 10⁻¹¹ m.

**(a)** Calculate the momentum of each. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy of the photon. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the electron. **[2]**

**(d)** Comment on your answers to (b) and (c). **[1]**

**11.** A researcher needs electrons with a de Broglie wavelength of 5.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m.

**(a)** Calculate the momentum of each electron. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of each electron. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the accelerating potential difference required. **[2]**

**12.** A student writes: "Light is a wave while it passes through a grating, then it turns into a particle when it reaches a metal surface."

Explain what is meant by wave-particle duality and why the student's description is misleading. **[3]**

**13.** An alpha particle of mass 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg has a kinetic energy of 5.0 MeV.

**(a)** Convert this kinetic energy to joules. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the alpha particle. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest the size of object that could produce noticeable diffraction of these alpha particles. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The wavelength associated with a moving particle, equal to the Planck constant divided by its momentum (λ = h/p) [1].

**2. (a)** Wave nature [1].

**(b)** Particulate nature [1].

**(c)** Wave nature [1].

**3.** p = mv = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2200 = 3.67 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹ [1]; λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ 3.67 × 10⁻²⁴ = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ m [1].

**4.** Below the threshold frequency no electrons are emitted, however intense the radiation [1]; this is explained if radiation arrives as photons of energy hf, each giving its energy to one electron, so emission requires hf to be at least the work function [1].

**5. (a)** λ = h/(mv) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ (1.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.0 × 10⁻³) = 6.6 × 10⁻²² m [1].

**(b)** Diffraction needs a gap or obstacle comparable with the wavelength, and nothing exists that is anywhere near 10⁻²² m (far smaller than a nucleus) [1].

**6. (a)** E_k = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2000 = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1].

**(b)** p = √(2mₑE_k) = √(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁶) = 2.41 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ 2.41 × 10⁻²³ = 2.75 × 10⁻¹¹ m [1].

**7. (a)** A bright central spot surrounded by concentric bright and dark rings [1].

**(b)** The rings are a diffraction pattern [1]; diffraction (and interference of the scattered waves) is a property of waves, so the electrons must have a wave nature [1].

**(c)** The spacing between atoms in graphite is comparable with the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons, so diffraction is noticeable [1]; the graphite consists of many tiny crystals at random orientations, so the diffracted beams are spread symmetrically around the axis into rings [1].

**8. (a)** λ ∝ 1/p and p ∝ √V, so λ ∝ 1/√V [1]; ratio = √(2.0 ÷ 8.0) = 0.50 [1].

**(b)** The rings become smaller (closer to the centre) [1]. The electrons have greater kinetic energy and momentum, so a shorter de Broglie wavelength [1]; a shorter wavelength is diffracted through smaller angles [1].

**9. (a)** Both gain kinetic energy eV, so p = √(2meV) for each [1]; λ = h/p, so λₑ/λₚ = √(2mₚeV) ÷ √(2mₑeV) = √(mₚ/mₑ) [1].

**(b)** √(1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 43 [1].

**(c)** The proton wavelength is about 43 times shorter (about 6 × 10⁻¹³ m), much smaller than the atomic spacing, so the diffraction angles would be too small to see [1].

**10. (a)** p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ 2.75 × 10⁻¹¹ = 2.41 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹ for both [1].

**(b)** E = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.75 × 10⁻¹¹ = 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ J [1].

**(c)** E_k = p²/(2mₑ) [1] = (2.41 × 10⁻²³)² ÷ (2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1].

**(d)** The photon has about 23 times more energy than the electron, even though their momenta and wavelengths are equal [1].

**11. (a)** p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻¹¹ = 1.33 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** v = p/mₑ = 1.33 × 10⁻²³ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 1.5 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** E_k = p²/(2mₑ) = 9.65 × 10⁻¹⁷ J [1]; V = E_k/e = 9.65 × 10⁻¹⁷ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 600 V [1].

**12.** Wave-particle duality means electromagnetic radiation has both a wave nature and a particulate nature [1]; interference and diffraction show the wave nature, and the photoelectric effect shows the particulate nature [1]. Radiation does not change from one into the other: both properties belong to it all the time, and the experiment determines which one is observed [1].

**13. (a)** E_k = 5.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹³ J [1].

**(b)** p = √(2mE_k) = √(2 × 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ × 8.0 × 10⁻¹³) = 1.03 × 10⁻¹⁹ kg m s⁻¹ [1]; λ = h/p = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ m [1].

**(c)** An object of similar size, about 10⁻¹⁵ to 10⁻¹⁴ m, which is the size of an atomic nucleus [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Matching the photoelectric effect to the wave model, or diffraction to the particle model.
- Describing the electron diffraction pattern without saying that diffraction is a wave property.
- Stating that the rings expand when the accelerating p.d. increases.
- Using λ ∝ 1/V instead of λ ∝ 1/√V for accelerated electrons.
- Leaving the p.d. in kilovolts, or using volts as if they were joules.
- Using p = E/c for an electron, which applies only to photons.
