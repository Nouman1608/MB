All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Bayview Bikes.

> **Case study: Bayview Bikes**
>
> Bayview Bikes is a bicycle shop with 1,500 regular customers, who each
> spend an average of $180 a year on bikes, parts and repairs. A recent
> survey of 400 customers found that 340 were satisfied with the service,
> but some customers complained that staff did not know much about the
> electric bikes, and that repairs took too long. The owner believes that
> poor service has lost 8% of regular customers this year. She plans
> to spend $14,000 a year on an online chat service, staff training in
> product knowledge and a repair booking system. She expects this to
> keep 6% of her regular customers who would have left. The shop also
> has a social media page, where one customer recently posted a
> complaint about a delayed repair.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a post-sales service? **[1]**
- A. Greeting customers at the door
- B. Free training on how to use a product
- C. A shop window display
- D. A discount for large orders

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a danger of poor customer service? **[1]**
- A. Customer loyalty
- B. Positive word of mouth
- C. A poor reputation
- D. Fewer complaints

**3.** Define the term "customer service". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which a business can use social media for customer service. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of good product knowledge among its staff. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the percentage of surveyed customers who were satisfied. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the number of regular customers Bayview believes it has lost this year. **[1]**

**8.** Calculate the sales lost each year from these customers. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the number of customers and the sales value of the customers the owner expects to keep with her $14,000 plan. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** ways the owner's plan could improve the shop's customer service. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse the possible effects of the delayed repair complaint on social media on Bayview Bikes. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Bayview Bikes should spend $14,000 a year on improving customer service. Use your answer to Question 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The help and support a business gives to customers [1] before, during and after a sale [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: answering questions quickly [1]; responding to complaints [1]; sharing news and offers [1]; gathering customer opinions [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Staff who know the products can recommend the right one [1], so customers are satisfied, buy with confidence and are less likely to return the product [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 340 / 400 x 100 [1] = **85%** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 8% x 1,500 = **120** customers [1]. **[1]**

**8.** 120 x $180 [1] = **$21,600** a year [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Customers kept = 6% x 1,500 = 90 [1]; sales value = 90 x $180 [1] = **$16,200** a year [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Product knowledge training:** staff would learn about electric bikes [1], so they could answer customers' questions and recommend the right bike [1].
**Repair booking system:** it would organise repair jobs [1], so repairs are finished on time and fewer customers are kept waiting [1]. Accept online chat with a reason, such as quick answers outside shop hours [1] that make customers feel valued [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Reputation:** the post is public, so many potential customers could read it [1], and they may decide to go to another shop [1].
**Lost sales:** each regular customer is worth $180 a year [1], so even a few lost customers reduce revenue [1].
**Opportunity:** if the owner responds quickly and fixes the problem, the public response may show that Bayview cares about service [1], which can rebuild trust [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For:** the plan costs $14,000 and keeps 90 customers worth $16,200 a year, a net gain of $2,200 [1]; the training should fix the complaint that staff do not know about electric bikes [1]; better service may increase loyalty, positive reviews and the satisfaction rate of 85% [1]; the benefits may continue in later years [1].
**Against:** the gain is small compared with the cost, only $2,200 a year [1]; the figure of 6% is only an estimate, and if only 4% (60 customers worth $10,800) were kept, the plan would lose money [1]; the shop could achieve some improvement more cheaply, for example by training alone [1].
**Judgement:** the plan is probably worth doing, because the figures show a net gain and it addresses the complaints that customers actually made [1]. Bayview should check results after a year, and adjust or cut the parts that are least effective [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating satisfaction as 340 / 340 or dividing the wrong way round.
- Using the shop's total number of customers instead of 6% or 8% of them.
- Forgetting to compare the benefit with the $14,000 cost.
- Describing ICT without saying how it affects customers or sales.
- Writing only about benefits and not the risk that the estimate is wrong.
