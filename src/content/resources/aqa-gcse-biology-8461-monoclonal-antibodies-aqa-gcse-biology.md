This guide covers section **4.3.2 Monoclonal antibodies** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for
first teaching from 2016: 4.3.2.1 Producing monoclonal antibodies and 4.3.2.2 Uses of monoclonal antibodies.
The whole section is marked **Biology only** and **Higher tier only**, so it is not in Combined Science and is
not examined on Foundation tier papers. It is assessed on Paper 1.

For the place of this section in Topic 3, see the [Infection and response study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-infection-and-response/).
Condense this page with the [Monoclonal antibodies revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-monoclonal-antibodies-aqa-gcse-biology-revision-notes/)
and test yourself with the [Monoclonal antibodies practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-monoclonal-antibodies-aqa-gcse-biology-practice/).

## What you must be able to do

- Describe how monoclonal antibodies are produced.
- Describe some of the ways they are used: diagnosis (such as pregnancy tests), measuring hormones and other
  chemicals in blood, detecting pathogens, locating molecules in research, and treating some diseases such as
  cancer.
- Explain how a test or treatment works **when you are given information about it**. You are not expected to
  recall any specific test or treatment.
- Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of monoclonal antibodies.

## The key idea: specificity

You already know from 4.3.1 that white blood cells make **antibodies**, proteins that bind to **antigens** on
the surface of pathogens. The binding works because the shape of the antibody is complementary to one antigen.

The specification states that monoclonal antibodies are **specific to one binding site on one protein
antigen**. That precision is the reason they are useful: they can be made to target **a specific chemical or
specific cells** in the body and ignore everything else.

"Monoclonal" tells you how they are made. *Mono* means one and *clonal* means from a clone, so monoclonal
antibodies are **produced from a single clone of cells**. Every cell in the clone is genetically identical,
so every antibody it makes is identical.

## 4.3.2.1 Producing monoclonal antibodies

A lymphocyte that makes the antibody you want cannot keep dividing in a culture, so on its own it cannot
supply large amounts of antibody. A tumour cell divides repeatedly but makes no useful antibody. Fusing the two
gives a cell with both properties.

1. A mouse is injected with the chosen antigen. This **stimulates mouse lymphocytes to make a particular
   antibody**.
2. The lymphocytes are collected and **combined with a particular kind of tumour cell**.
3. The fused cell is called a **hybridoma cell**. It can **both divide and make the antibody**.
4. A **single hybridoma cell is cloned** to produce many identical cells that all produce the same antibody.
5. A large amount of the antibody is **collected and purified**.

**Annotated model answer**

*Question: Describe how monoclonal antibodies are produced. [4]*

> Mouse lymphocytes are stimulated to make a particular antibody [1]. The lymphocytes are combined with a tumour
> cell to make a hybridoma cell [1]. A single hybridoma cell is cloned to make many identical cells [1]. These
> all make the same antibody, which is collected and purified [1].

Each sentence carries one idea in the right order. Answers lose marks by writing "a tumour is injected into the
mouse" (the fusion happens outside the mouse), by leaving out the word **hybridoma**, or by skipping the
**single** cell that is cloned.

## 4.3.2.2 Uses of monoclonal antibodies

In every use, the antibody does one job: it **binds to one specific target**. What makes each use different is
what is attached to the antibody, and what you can then see or do.

| Use | What the antibody binds to | What it carries or shows |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis, such as a pregnancy test | A hormone found only in the urine of pregnant women | A coloured bead, so a coloured line appears |
| Laboratory tests on blood | A hormone or other chemical in a blood sample | A marker, so the amount can be measured |
| Detecting pathogens | An antigen on a particular pathogen | A marker showing the pathogen is present |
| Research | A specific molecule in a cell or tissue | A fluorescent dye, so the molecule's position glows under a microscope |
| Treating cancer | An antigen on the surface of cancer cells | A radioactive substance, a toxic drug, or a chemical that stops cells growing and dividing |

### How a pregnancy test strip works

This is the classic example of explaining a test from given information.

- Urine is placed on one end of the strip and soaks along it.
- Near that end are **mobile antibodies** attached to coloured beads. They bind to the pregnancy hormone if it is
  present, and move along the strip with the urine.
- At the **test line**, antibodies are **fixed** to the strip. They also bind the hormone, so hormone with beads
  attached is held there and a coloured line appears.
- At the **control line**, fixed antibodies bind to the mobile antibodies themselves, whether or not hormone is
  present. A line here shows the test has worked.

So two lines mean pregnant; one line (control only) means not pregnant; no control line means the test has
failed.

### Treating cancer

For cancer, the monoclonal antibody is bound to a **radioactive substance, a toxic drug or a chemical which
stops cells growing and dividing**. The antibody binds only to antigens on the cancer cells, so it **delivers
the substance to the cancer cells without harming other cells** in the body. Compare this with a drug given
on its own, which travels to all body cells and can damage healthy tissue.

### Worked example: interpreting test results

A laboratory uses a monoclonal antibody test that turns a sample blue when a particular pathogen is present.
Explain why the test gives a blue result for that pathogen but not for a different species of the same type of
pathogen.

> The monoclonal antibody is specific to one antigen [1]. Only the first pathogen has that antigen on its
> surface, so the antibody binds only to it and the colour appears only when that pathogen is present [1].

## Evaluating monoclonal antibodies

The specification asks you to appreciate their power, consider ethical issues and evaluate them.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Bind to one specific target, so tests are precise | They create more side effects than expected |
| Cancer treatments reach cancer cells without harming most other cells | They are not yet as widely used as everyone hoped when they were first developed |
| Can be used for diagnosis, measuring, detecting pathogens, research and treatment | Production uses mice, which raises ethical concerns for some people |
| Large amounts can be produced from one clone | |

A good evaluation weighs these points against the context in the question and ends with a judgement, for
example that a treatment is worth trialling because it targets cancer cells, provided the side effects are
monitored.

## Common mistakes

- Saying monoclonal antibodies "kill" cancer cells by themselves. In the treatment described, the substance they
  carry does the damage; the antibody delivers it.
- Describing the hybridoma as a tumour "grown in the mouse".
- Forgetting that the hybridoma can **both divide and make the antibody**; that dual property is the point.
- Mixing up mobile and fixed antibodies on a pregnancy test, or forgetting the control line.
- Writing "antibodies are specific to a disease". They are specific to one binding site on one antigen.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Describe** (production): give the steps in order using the key words lymphocyte, tumour cell, hybridoma,
  clone, collect and purify.
- **Explain** (a test or treatment): start from specificity, then say what is attached and what result follows.
- **Evaluate**: use the information in the question, give at least one advantage and one disadvantage, and make a
  justified conclusion.
- **Suggest**: you are expected to apply the idea of specificity to an unfamiliar test, so use the information
  given rather than recalled detail.
