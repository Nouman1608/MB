## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Recombinant DNA | DNA made by joining DNA from two or more different sources |
| Genetic engineering | Deliberate manipulation of genetic material to modify specific characteristics; may transfer a gene so it is expressed |
| Gene editing | Insertion, deletion or replacement of DNA at specific sites in the genome |
| Vector | Carries a gene into a host cell, e.g. a plasmid |
| Plasmid | Small circular DNA in bacteria that replicates independently |
| Sticky ends | Single-stranded overhangs left by a staggered cut |
| cDNA | Complementary DNA made from mRNA by reverse transcriptase |
| Promoter | DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription |
| Marker gene | Gene with an easily detected product, e.g. GFP, used to identify transformed cells |
| Primer | Short single-stranded DNA complementary to one end of the target |
| Probe | Single-stranded DNA of known sequence that hybridises with a complementary sequence |

## Three sources of a gene (19.1.3)

| Source | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|
| Extracted from donor DNA (restriction enzymes) | Gene in its natural form | Eukaryotic gene contains introns; hard to find in the genome |
| Synthesised from donor mRNA (reverse transcriptase) | No introns; many mRNA copies in cells that make the protein | Needs the right cell type |
| Synthesised chemically from nucleotides | No donor cells; codons can be altered | Sequence must be known |

## The five tools (19.1.4)

| Tool | Role |
|---|---|
| Restriction endonuclease | Cuts DNA at a specific (often palindromic) recognition sequence; staggered cuts give sticky ends |
| DNA ligase | Forms phosphodiester bonds to seal the backbone |
| Plasmid | Vector carrying the gene into bacteria |
| DNA polymerase | Makes the second strand of cDNA; copies DNA in PCR (Taq) |
| Reverse transcriptase | Makes single-stranded cDNA from an mRNA template |

**Same enzyme on gene and plasmid → complementary sticky ends.**

*Worked reminder:* EcoRI site 5′-GAATTC-3′, cut between G and A → AATT overhangs.

## Promoters, markers, editing (19.1.5–19.1.7)

- **Promoter**: host RNA polymerase must recognise it; without it, no transcription, no protein. Can be chosen to target a tissue.
- **Fluorescent marker** (e.g. GFP) under the same promoter: glowing cells **contain and express** the inserted DNA; select them without killing.
- **Gene editing**: targeted change at a specific site (e.g. guide RNA leads Cas9 to cut).

## Gene transfer in steps

1. Get the gene (cDNA from mRNA, or cut out, or synthesise).
2. Cut plasmid and gene with the same restriction enzyme.
3. Mix; sticky ends pair; ligase seals → recombinant plasmid.
4. Include promoter and marker gene.
5. Bacteria take up plasmids.
6. Select fluorescent colonies; culture.

## PCR (19.1.8)

| Step | Temperature | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Denature | about 95 °C | H bonds break; strands separate |
| Anneal | about 55–65 °C | Two primers bind to ends of target |
| Extend | about 72 °C | Taq polymerase adds nucleotides from primers |

- **Taq polymerase**: from *Thermus aquaticus*; thermostable, not denatured at 95 °C; optimum about 72 °C.
- Copies after n cycles = starting number × 2ⁿ (maximum).

*Worked reminder:* 3 copies, 12 cycles → 3 × 4096 = **12 288**.

## Gel electrophoresis (19.1.9)

- DNA negative (phosphate groups) → moves to **anode (+)**.
- **Shorter fragments move further.**
- Ladder of known sizes for comparison; stain or label to see bands.
- Linear DNA cut n times → n + 1 fragments; circular → n fragments.

## Microarrays and databases (19.1.10–19.1.11)

- Probes at known positions; labelled sample DNA or cDNA **hybridises**; fluorescence = present / expressed.
- mRNA → cDNA (reverse transcriptase) for expression studies; brightness ∝ amount.
- Two colours: green or red = one sample only; yellow = both; dark = neither.
- Databases (e.g. GenBank, UniProt, Protein Data Bank): free shared data; compare and identify sequences; design primers and probes; protein structures for drug design; evolutionary studies.

## Exam traps

- Ligase does not form hydrogen bonds.
- Reverse transcriptase makes **DNA** from RNA.
- A eukaryotic gene cut from DNA keeps its introns; bacteria cannot remove them.
- PCR needs **two** different primers.
- DNA runs to the **positive** electrode.
- A fluorescent marker shows **expression**, not just uptake.

## Quick self-test

1. Define recombinant DNA.
2. Why is cDNA made from mRNA preferred to a gene cut from human DNA for expression in bacteria?
3. What bond does DNA ligase form?
4. Why must the plasmid and the gene be cut with the same restriction enzyme?
5. Why may a promoter need to be transferred with a gene?
6. How does a GFP marker identify successful cells?
7. Give the temperature and event of the annealing step of PCR.
8. Why is Taq polymerase used?
9. Ten copies are amplified for 15 cycles. Give the maximum number of copies.
10. A circular plasmid is cut at three sites. How many fragments are produced?
11. In a two-colour microarray, what does a yellow spot mean?
12. Give two benefits of protein structure databases.

### Answers

1. DNA made by joining DNA from two or more different sources.
2. cDNA has no introns, which bacteria cannot remove; many copies of mRNA are present in cells making the protein.
3. **Phosphodiester** bonds.
4. To produce complementary sticky ends that base-pair.
5. The host's RNA polymerase may not recognise the donor promoter, so the gene would not be transcribed.
6. Cells expressing the inserted DNA make the fluorescent protein and glow under blue or ultraviolet light.
7. About 55–65 °C; primers bind to complementary sequences at the ends of the target.
8. It is thermostable, so not denatured at 95 °C, and need not be replaced each cycle.
9. 10 × 2¹⁵ = 10 × 32 768 = **327 680**.
10. **Three**.
11. The gene is expressed in both samples at similar levels.
12. Any two: explain how proteins work; design drugs that fit binding sites; compare proteins between species; avoid repeating work.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the enzyme cuts the DNA" without "at a specific recognition sequence".
- Leaving out reasons for each PCR temperature.
- Not checking that fragment sizes add up to the original length.
- Describing microarrays without the word **hybridise**.
