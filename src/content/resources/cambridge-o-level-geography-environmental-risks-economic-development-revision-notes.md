Condensed for revision. For the full explanations and case study, read
the [Environmental Risks study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-environmental-risks-economic-development/).

## The syllabus in brief

- Describe how economic activities **threaten the environment and
  people, locally and globally**.
- Show the **need for sustainable development and management**.
- Understand the **importance of resource conservation**.
- Case study: **an area where economic development is causing
  environmental risk**.

## The answer chain

**Activity → process → threat → effect on people and environment.**
Naming the threat alone earns little; tracing the chain earns the
explanation marks.

## Threats at a glance

| Threat | Main causes | Key effects |
|---|---|---|
| Soil erosion | Deforestation, ploughing slopes, overgrazing | Lower yields, gullies, silted rivers and reservoirs |
| Desertification | Overgrazing, over-cultivation, fuelwood cutting, drought | Land unusable, food shortage, migration (e.g. the Sahel) |
| Enhanced global warming | Burning fossil fuels, deforestation, methane from cattle, rice and landfill | Higher temperatures, sea-level rise, extreme weather |
| Water pollution | Sewage, factory waste, fertiliser run-off, oil, mining | Disease, fish deaths, algal blooms |
| Air pollution | Power stations, factories, vehicles, burning | Breathing illness, smog, acid rain |
| Noise pollution | Airports, roads, quarries | Stress, poor sleep, wildlife disturbed |
| Visual pollution | Quarries, pylons, large factories, litter | Spoiled landscapes, lost tourism |

**Scale**: quarry dust and noise are local; acid rain crosses borders;
greenhouse gases are global.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
| Resource conservation | Using resources carefully so they last longer and cause less harm |
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Extra warming caused by human release of greenhouse gases |
| Leaching | Nutrients washed down through soil by rain, out of reach of plants |
| Algal bloom | Rapid algae growth from excess nutrients, which uses up oxygen in water |
| Acid rain | Rain made acidic by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides |
| Desertification | Dry land becoming desert-like, largely through human misuse |

## Management toolkit

- **Laws**: emissions limits, protected areas, fines.
- **Technology**: waste-water treatment, chimney filters, renewables.
- **Farming**: terraces, contour ploughing, windbreaks, herd limits.
- **Restoration**: replanting, landscaping quarries.
- **Monitoring**: satellites detect clearance.
- **International agreements**: emissions targets.
- **Conservation**: reduce, reuse, recycle; efficiency; renewables.

## Case study outline: the Amazon, Brazil

- Activities: cattle ranching (largest cause), soya, logging, mining
  (Carajás iron ore; gold with mercury), roads (Trans-Amazonian
  Highway), HEP (Belo Monte dam, Xingu River).
- Risks: soil erosion and leaching, carbon release, mercury and silt in
  rivers, biodiversity loss, indigenous land lost.
- Management: protected areas and indigenous territories, Forest Code,
  INPE satellite monitoring, Soy Moratorium (2006).
- Judgement: clearance fell when enforcement was strong, then rose again
  in some later years, so success depends on enforcement.

## Second case study outline: winter smog in Lahore, Pakistan

- **Activities**: vehicle exhaust in a fast-growing city; factories and brick kilns burning coal,
  wood or waste; burning of rice stubble after the harvest in Pakistani and Indian Punjab.
- **Process**: in late autumn and winter, cool, calm air near the ground can be trapped under
  warmer air above (a **temperature inversion**), so pollutants cannot disperse and build up
  into thick smog.
- **Effects on people**: breathing and eye problems, hospital admissions, school closures in
  bad spells, poor visibility causing road accidents and flight delays.
- **Scale**: local and regional; smoke crosses the India–Pakistan border, so neither country can
  solve it alone.
- **Management**: bans on crop burning, conversion of brick kilns to cleaner **zig-zag**
  technology, vehicle emission checks, temporary closures of polluting sites and schools during
  smog emergencies.
- **Evaluation**: emergency measures reduce exposure but not the causes; enforcement is difficult
  when farmers lack affordable alternatives to burning stubble.

## Worked example: the answer chain in action

*Explain how an economic activity can cause water pollution.* Model answer: "Farmers spread
nitrate fertiliser to increase yields. Heavy rain washes the excess into rivers. The nutrients
cause algae to grow rapidly, forming an algal bloom. When the algae die, bacteria decompose them
and use up the oxygen in the water, so fish die and the river is no longer safe for drinking
water or fishing."

Activity → process → threat → effect on the environment **and** on people.

## Weighing management

- **Laws** work only where they are enforced and monitored.
- **Technology** (filters, treatment works) is effective but costly for poorer countries.
- **International agreements** matter for global threats, because one country cannot cut global
  warming alone.
- **Local action** (terraces, tree planting such as Pakistan's Billion Tree Tsunami in Khyber
  Pakhtunkhwa) is cheap and involves communities, but takes years to show results.

## Exam traps

- Threat named, activity missing.
- Natural greenhouse effect confused with enhanced warming.
- Desertification blamed only on climate.
- "Sustainable" defined vaguely as "lasting".
- Calling the ozone hole the cause of global warming; they are different issues.
- Management named with no evaluation of cost or enforcement.
- Treating smog as only a local problem when smoke crosses borders.

## Self-test

1. Give two economic activities that cause soil erosion.
2. Name two greenhouse gases and one source of each.
3. Explain how fertiliser use can harm a river.
4. Give one local and one global environmental threat.
5. Define sustainable development.
6. Give two ways of conserving resources.
7. Name one activity causing deforestation in the Amazon and one
   management measure.
8. Give two causes of winter smog in Lahore.
9. What is a temperature inversion, and why does it worsen smog?
10. Why is cross-border co-operation needed to tackle smog in Punjab?
11. Give one strength and one limitation of laws as a management
    method.

**Answers:** 1. Any two of: forest clearance for farming or ranching,
ploughing steep slopes, overgrazing, mining. 2. Carbon dioxide (burning
fossil fuels or forest) and methane (cattle, rice paddies or landfill).
3. Run-off carries nutrients into the river, causing an algal bloom that
uses up oxygen, so fish die. 4. Local: for example quarry noise or dust;
global: enhanced global warming. 5. Development that meets the needs of
people today without reducing the ability of future generations to meet
their own needs. 6. Any two of: recycling, reusing, reducing waste,
using renewable energy, improving efficiency. 7. Activity: for example
cattle ranching or soya farming; measure: for example INPE satellite
monitoring, protected areas, the Forest Code or the Soy Moratorium.
8. Any two: vehicle exhaust; brick kilns and factories; burning crop
stubble. 9. A layer of warmer air above cooler air near the ground,
which stops air rising, so pollutants are trapped close to the surface.
10. Smoke from crop burning drifts across the India–Pakistan border, so
action by one country alone cannot remove it. 11. Strength: they can
set clear limits and fines; limitation: they fail without monitoring and
enforcement.
