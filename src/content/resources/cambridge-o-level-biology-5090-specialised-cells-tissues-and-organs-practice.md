## Section A

**1.** Which of these is an organ?

A: blood

B: leaf

C: xylem

D: guard cell

**[1]**

**2.** A cell 15 µm long appears 45 mm long in a photograph. What is the magnification?

A: ×30

B: ×300

C: ×3000

D: ×30 000

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term "organ system" and give one example. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how **two** features of a xylem vessel are related to its function. **[4]**

**5.** State the **three** features of a sperm cell named in the syllabus, and explain how each helps the sperm to fertilise an egg. **[6]**

## Section B

**6.** A photomicrograph of plant tissue has a scale bar 15 mm long, labelled 30 µm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the photomicrograph. **[2]**

**(b)** A cell on the photomicrograph measures 63 mm. Calculate its actual length in micrometres. **[2]**

**7.** Root hair cells absorb water and mineral ions from the soil.

**(a)** Describe the shape of a root hair cell and explain how it helps. **[2]**

**(b)** Mineral ions are taken up by active transport. Explain why root hair cells contain mitochondria. **[2]**

**8.** Goblet cells and ciliated cells line the airways leading to the lungs. Explain how these two types of cell work together to protect the gas exchange system. **[4]**

**9.** Classify each of the following as a cell, a tissue, an organ or an organ system: heart, phloem, red blood cell, circulatory system, lung, muscle tissue, root, palisade mesophyll cell. **[4]**

**10.** Describe the **two** features of an egg cell named in the syllabus and explain how each is related to its function. **[4]**

**11.** A leaf is an organ.

**(a)** Explain what makes a leaf an organ rather than a tissue. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the position and contents of palisade mesophyll cells suit their function. **[2]**

**12.** A red blood cell has an actual diameter of 7.5 µm. A student draws it at a magnification of ×2000.

**(a)** Calculate the diameter of the drawing in millimetres. **[2]**

**(b)** Another student calculates 15 000 mm. Identify the mistake. **[1]**

**13.** Describe what is meant by a stem cell. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Blood and xylem are tissues; a guard cell is a single cell.

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert first: 15 µm = 0.015 mm, and 45 ÷ 0.015 = 3000.

**3.** A group of organs working together to carry out a body function [1]; for example the digestive system or the circulatory system [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Thick walls strengthened with lignin [1] give support and stop the vessel collapsing [1]. No cell contents and no cross walls, with cells joined end to end [1], form a continuous empty tube so water and mineral ions flow freely [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Hollow" alone is too vague; say there are no cell contents and no cross walls, then link this to unobstructed flow.

**5.** Flagellum [1], which moves so the sperm can swim to the egg [1]. Many mitochondria [1], which release energy by aerobic respiration for swimming [1]. Enzymes in the acrosome [1], which digest a way through the outer layer of the egg so the sperm can enter [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each feature with its own explanation; a list of three features followed by one general sentence earns about half the marks.

**6. (a)** 30 µm = 0.03 mm [1]; magnification = 15 ÷ 0.03 = ×500 [1].

**(b)** Actual size = 63 ÷ 500 = 0.126 mm [1] = 126 µm [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Measuring the scale bar and using its label is the same formula as any other magnification question; the only extra step is the unit conversion.

**7. (a)** It has a long, thin extension into the soil [1], giving a large surface area for absorption [1].

**(b)** Active transport needs energy [1], which mitochondria release by aerobic respiration [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Goblet cells secrete mucus [1], which traps dust particles and pathogens [1]. Ciliated cells have cilia that beat [1], moving the mucus and trapped material up and away from the lungs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Cilia do not trap anything themselves; the mucus traps, and the cilia move it.

**9.** Cells: red blood cell, palisade mesophyll cell [1]. Tissues: phloem, muscle tissue [1]. Organs: heart, lung, root [1]. Organ system: circulatory system [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* One mark per correct group; a single misplaced item loses that group's mark.

**10.** Energy stores [1] supply energy for the early development of the embryo after fertilisation [1]. A jelly coat that changes at fertilisation [1], so that no more sperm can enter once one has fertilised the egg [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The syllabus limits egg cell features to these two, so do not spend time on others.

**11. (a)** A leaf is made of several different tissues [1], such as epidermis, mesophyll, xylem and phloem, working together for a function such as photosynthesis [1].

**(b)** They are near the upper surface of the leaf, where most light falls [1], and contain many chloroplasts to absorb as much light as possible for photosynthesis [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** 7.5 µm = 0.0075 mm [1]; image size = 0.0075 × 2000 = 15 mm [1].

**(b)** The student did not convert micrometres to millimetres before multiplying (the answer 15 000 is in µm) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A sense check helps: a drawing 15 000 mm (15 m) across is clearly impossible.

**13.** An unspecialised cell [1] that divides by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised for specific functions [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a feature but not saying how it helps.
- Calling blood or xylem an organ.
- Forgetting to convert µm to mm before using the formula.
- Giving magnification with a unit.
- Adding extra sperm or egg features while missing the named ones.
