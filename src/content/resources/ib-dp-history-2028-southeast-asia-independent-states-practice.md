This **higher level (HL) only** set covers the regional study of the emergence of independent states in Southeast Asia (1900 to 1990) from the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Every Paper 3 question on this study requires you to refer to one state; the answers below use Indonesia, and you may substitute your own. Study first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-southeast-asia-independent-states/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-southeast-asia-independent-states-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain two ways in which colonial policies of "divide and rule" affected one Southeast Asian state. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "The occupiers called themselves brothers, and took our rice and our young men. Yet they also handed us rifles and a platform, and taught us that the white master could be beaten." (A memoir by a former youth organiser, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the effects of Japanese rule. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of a memoir as evidence for the effects of Japanese rule. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the social and economic impact of Japanese rule in one Southeast Asian state. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent was the Second World War the main reason for the emergence of an independent state in Southeast Asia? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the achievements and challenges of the first ten years of independence in one Southeast Asian state. **[15]**

**6.** Explain the importance of external support in the achievement of independence in one Southeast Asian state. **[6]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which shared identity contributed to the growth of nationalism. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the way, 1 for explanation). The Dutch relied on local aristocrats and customary law, which kept regional elites loyal and apart; they used an army drawn from some groups (such as Ambonese) and treated Chinese and Arab traders as a separate legal class, which created lasting distrust; they let each region be governed differently, which made a common national movement harder until nationalists built a shared language and Youth Pledge; after independence the memory of favoured groups fed regional tensions. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests that Japan took food and labour under the cover of Asian brotherhood (1) and that it also gave nationalists weapons and a public platform (1). It suggests that Japan's victory undermined belief in European superiority (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. A memoir is written after the event, so memory may be shaped by later success or by the writer's wish to present nationalists as always resisting (1); it reflects one young activist's view and may not represent peasants who suffered more from the requisitions (1); it cannot show how widespread such training was, or Japan's reasons (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): forced labour (*romusha*) and rice requisitions caused deaths and hunger (up to 2); the disruption of shipping and trade and rising prices harmed ordinary households (up to 1); Japan removed Dutch officials and gave Indonesians higher posts (up to 1); the training of youth in PETA and other groups, and the mass organisations, built skills and organisation (up to 1); some groups, such as the Chinese and women forced into brothels, suffered specific harms (up to 1). **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "main reason" (up to 2).
- The war: the fall of the Dutch in 1942, the destruction of colonial prestige, Japanese training and mass organisations, the vacuum of August 1945 (up to 5).
- Earlier factors: Budi Utomo, Sarekat Islam, the PNI and the Youth Pledge; economic grievance and the Depression (up to 3).
- Post-war factors: guerrilla war, United Nations and US pressure, Dutch weakness (up to 2).
- Perspectives: historians who stress Japan against those who stress long-term nationalism (up to 1).
- A reasoned judgement, for example that the war was necessary for the timing but not sufficient without the earlier movement (up to 2). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis that weighs achievement against challenge (up to 2).
- Achievements: sovereignty in 1949, the national language, expanded schools, the free election of 1955, Pancasila as a unifying idea, the Bandung Conference (up to 4).
- Challenges: unstable cabinets, no stable majority, the economy dominated by foreign firms, shortage of trained officials (up to 4).
- Ethnic and religious tensions: Darul Islam, the South Moluccas, revolts of 1957 to 1958 (up to 2).
- Perspectives on Sukarno's turn to Guided Democracy (up to 1).
- A judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): the United Nations Security Council and its call for a ceasefire after the police actions (up to 2); US pressure on the Netherlands, including the threat to cut Marshall Aid, after the Republic crushed the Madiun revolt (up to 2); support from India, Australia and Arab states (up to 1); a judgement on whether external help was decisive or only supported an internal struggle (up to 1). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the way, 1 for explanation). A common language, Malay becoming Indonesian, allowed people from different islands to communicate and write together; a shared religion, Islam, supported the first mass movement, Sarekat Islam; common colonial borders and common treatment as colonial subjects encouraged people to see themselves as one; ideas such as the Youth Pledge of 1928 expressed a single homeland. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**I studied Vietnam or Burma. Do the questions work?**
Yes. Each question asks for one state. Use the same structure and your own evidence.

**How should I handle the Cold War?**
Use it where it affects your state's route to independence, as with Madiun and US policy, but keep the focus on emergence.
