Condensed notes for sub-topic 2.1 of Cambridge O Level Geography (2217), 2026 series.
**Case studies required:** an earthquake **and** a volcano.

## Structure and plates

- Inner core (solid) → outer core (liquid) → mantle (slowly flowing hot rock) → crust.
- **Oceanic crust:** thinner, denser, can be subducted. **Continental crust:** thicker, less
  dense, not subducted.
- Plates move a few cm a year, often explained by convection currents in the mantle.

## Plate boundaries

| Boundary | Movement | Earthquakes? | Volcanoes? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constructive (divergent) | Apart | Small, shallow | Yes, shield type, runny lava | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Iceland |
| Destructive (convergent) | Together; oceanic subducted | Powerful | Yes, explosive composite | Nazca under South American (Andes) |
| Collision | Together; continental–continental | Powerful | **No** | Indian and Eurasian (Himalayas) |
| Conservative | Side by side | Yes | **No** | San Andreas Fault |

**Distribution:** narrow belts on plate boundaries — Pacific Ring of Fire, Mid-Atlantic Ridge,
Alpine–Himalayan belt. **Exception:** hotspots (Hawaii).

## Volcano types

- **Strato-volcano (composite cone):** steep; layers of ash and lava; viscous lava; explosive;
  destructive boundaries (Mount Fuji, Mount St Helens).
- **Shield volcano:** gentle slopes, wide; runny lava; frequent gentle eruptions; constructive
  boundaries or hotspots (Mauna Loa).
- **Label:** magma chamber, main vent, secondary vent, crater, lava flow, ash cloud.

## Earthquake features

- **Focus** = underground point of rupture. **Epicentre** = surface point directly above.
- **Magnitude** = energy released (moment magnitude scale; logarithmic — one step ≈ 10× ground
  movement, ≈ 32× energy).
- Shallow focus → more surface damage.

## Effects

- **Earthquake primary:** collapse of buildings and bridges, deaths, broken roads and pipes.
- **Earthquake secondary:** fires, tsunamis, landslides, disease, homelessness, economic loss.
- **Volcano primary:** lava flows, pyroclastic flows, ash fall, gases.
- **Volcano secondary:** lahars, crop loss, flight disruption, short-term climate cooling.
- **Impact depends on:** magnitude, depth, distance, population density, building quality,
  time of day, wealth, preparedness.

## Opportunities of volcanoes

Fertile soils (Etna's vineyards) · geothermal energy (Iceland) · tourism · minerals and building
stone. People also stay because of family, land and infrequent eruptions.

## Reducing impacts: the three Ps plus planning

- **Prediction/monitoring:** eruptions often forecast (small quakes, ground swelling, gas);
  earthquakes not reliably predicted; tsunami warning systems.
- **Protection:** earthquake-resistant buildings (shock absorbers, cross-bracing, deep
  foundations, flexible pipes); lava diversion (Heimaey, 1973: seawater cooled lava).
- **Preparation:** drills, kits, education (Japan's Disaster Prevention Day, 1 September);
  evacuation plans.
- **Planning:** hazard maps and exclusion zones.

## Case-study hooks

- **Volcano:** Mount St Helens, USA, 18 May 1980 — Juan de Fuca under North American Plate;
  landslide triggered lateral blast; 57 deaths; lahars; exclusion zone reduced deaths.
- **Earthquake:** Tōhoku, Japan, 11 March 2011, magnitude 9.0 — tsunami, Fukushima Daiichi
  nuclear accident. Or Haiti, 12 January 2010, magnitude 7.0 — shallow, near Port-au-Prince,
  poorly built buildings, cholera outbreak later in 2010.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Subduction | One plate sinking beneath another into the mantle |
| Magma / lava | Molten rock underground / molten rock at the surface |
| Pyroclastic flow | Fast, very hot cloud of gas, ash and rock moving down a volcano |
| Lahar | Volcanic mudflow of ash and water |
| Focus | The point underground where an earthquake starts |
| Epicentre | The point on the surface directly above the focus |
| Magnitude | The energy released by an earthquake |
| Tsunami | Series of large sea waves caused by sudden displacement of water |
| Hotspot | Plume of magma rising through the middle of a plate |

## Self-test

1. Why does oceanic crust sink at a destructive boundary?
2. Name a boundary type where earthquakes occur but volcanoes do not.
3. Give two differences between a shield volcano and a strato-volcano.
4. Define the epicentre of an earthquake.
5. Give two opportunities that volcanoes offer people.
6. Why are volcanic eruptions easier to forecast than earthquakes?

**Answers:** 1. It is denser than continental crust. 2. Conservative or collision. 3. Any two:
shield has gentle slopes, runny lava and gentle eruptions; strato-volcano is steep with layers
of ash and lava, viscous lava and explosive eruptions. 4. The point on the surface directly
above the focus. 5. Any two of: fertile soils, geothermal energy, tourism, minerals. 6. Magma
rising beneath a volcano gives warning signs (small earthquakes, ground swelling, gas changes);
stress on a fault gives no reliable advance signal.
