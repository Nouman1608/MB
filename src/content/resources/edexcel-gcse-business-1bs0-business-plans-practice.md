## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is **not** listed in the specification as something a business plan should identify? **[1]**

A: Sources of finance

B: Location

C: The names of all future employees

D: Cash-flow forecast

**2.** Define the term business plan. **[2]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are purposes of a business plan named in the specification? **[2]**

A: Minimising risk

B: Avoiding all taxes

C: Obtaining finance

D: Guaranteeing profit

E: Setting the minimum wage

**4.** State **one** reason why a business plan includes a cash-flow forecast. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business plan can reduce the risk of starting a business. **[3]**

**6.** Corrie's plan for Bloom Box, a flower subscription service, forecasts 320 subscriptions a month at £22 each. Variable costs are £9 per subscription and fixed costs are £2,860 a month.

**(a)** Calculate forecast monthly revenue. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate forecast monthly profit. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the break even number of subscriptions. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Using your answers to Question 6, calculate Bloom Box's margin of safety, and outline what it tells a bank considering a loan. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **one** limitation of a business plan for a new business. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows part of the cash-flow forecast in Sami's plan for Rally Rentals, which hires out e-bikes.

| | Month 1 (£) | Month 2 (£) | Month 3 (£) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening balance | 3,000 | | |
| Cash inflows | 2,400 | 4,100 | 5,200 |
| Cash outflows | 6,800 | 3,900 | 4,000 |
| Net cash-flow | | | |
| Closing balance | | | |

**(a)** Complete the table. **[4]**

**(b)** Outline **one** action the forecast suggests Sami should take before opening. **[2]**

**10.** Outline **one** reason why market research is an important part of a business plan. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse how a business plan could help Dina obtain a £15,000 loan to open a pottery studio offering classes. **[6]**

**12.** Kofi is opening a street-food van. He has £8,000 of savings, needs no loan, and says writing a business plan would waste three weeks he could spend trading. Evaluate whether Kofi should still write a business plan. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A written document setting out what a business intends to do and how [1], including its aims, marketing and financial forecasts [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: to show months when the business may run short of cash; to show whether loan repayments can be met; to plan how much finance is needed [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Writing a plan makes the owner research whether there is demand for the idea [1]. If the research shows too few customers, the owner can change or drop the idea [1] before money is spent on premises or stock, so less is lost [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** 320 × £22 = **£7,040** [1]

**(b)** Total costs = £2,860 + (320 × £9) = £2,860 + £2,880 = £5,740 [1]. Profit = £7,040 − £5,740 = **£1,300** [1]

**(c)** £2,860 ÷ (£22 − £9) = £2,860 ÷ £13 [1] = **220 subscriptions** [1] **[5]**

**7.** Margin of safety = 320 − 220 = **100 subscriptions** [1]. Sales could fall by 100 subscriptions a month, nearly a third of the forecast, before Bloom Box makes a loss [1], so the bank can see there is some room for error and the loan is less risky [1]. **[3]**

**8.** A new business has no past sales figures, so its forecasts are estimates [1]. If the owner is too optimistic, revenue may be much lower than planned [1], which means the plan could give false confidence and the business could run short of cash despite having a plan [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Net cash-flow: Month 1 = 2,400 − 6,800 = −4,400; Month 2 = 4,100 − 3,900 = 200; Month 3 = 5,200 − 4,000 = 1,200 [1] [1]. Opening and closing balances: Month 1 closing = 3,000 − 4,400 = −1,400; Month 2 opening −1,400, closing −1,200; Month 3 opening −1,200, closing 0 [1] [1].

**(b)** Sami should arrange an overdraft or extra finance of at least £1,400 before opening [1], because the forecast shows Rally Rentals overdrawn in Months 1 and 2, mainly due to high outflows in Month 1 [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Market research shows who the customers are and how many might buy [1], which gives evidence for the sales forecast on which the revenue, profit and cash-flow forecasts depend [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The bank will want evidence that Dina can repay £15,000 [1]. Her market research could show how many local people want pottery classes and what they would pay [1], so the bank can see that the revenue forecast is based on evidence rather than hope [1]. Her cash-flow forecast could show enough cash each month to cover repayments [1], which reduces the bank's risk and makes a loan more likely [1]. However, the studio is new, so the forecasts are estimates and the bank may still ask for evidence such as bookings already taken before lending [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Kofi needs no loan, so one main purpose of a plan, obtaining finance, does not apply to him [1]. The three weeks spent planning could cost him trading revenue [1], and a street-food van is small and flexible, so he can change his menu or pitch quickly if sales are poor [1].

However, a plan also minimises risk [1]. Researching his target market and location could show which pitches have the most customers, so he avoids spending his £8,000 on a poor pitch [1]. Forecasting revenue and costs would show whether his prices cover his costs and how many meals he must sell to break even [1], and a cash-flow forecast would show whether his savings last through quiet early weeks [1].

Judgement: Kofi should still write a plan, but a short one, because the risk of losing his own £8,000 is greater than the cost of a few weeks' planning [1]. This depends on how much he already knows: if he has run a stall before and knows his costs and customers, a brief forecast may be enough [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Evaluate needs both sides and a supported conclusion. Noticing that Kofi needs no loan, and still finding a reason to plan, shows full understanding of both purposes in the specification.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing plan contents when the question asks why the plan matters.
- Forgetting that each opening balance is the previous month's closing balance.
- Saying a plan guarantees a loan or success.
- Ignoring the point that forecasts are only estimates.
