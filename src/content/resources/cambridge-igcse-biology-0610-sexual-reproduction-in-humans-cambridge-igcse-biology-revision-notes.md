These notes condense sub-topic **16.4 Sexual reproduction in humans** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 8 are Core; outcomes 9 and 10 are **Extended only**.

For explanations and model answers, read the [study guide for human reproduction](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-humans-cambridge-igcse-biology/). Then try the [practice questions for human reproduction](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-humans-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## Male system -- Core

Route of sperm: **testes → sperm ducts → (prostate gland adds fluid) → urethra → out through the penis**.

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes | produce sperm (and testosterone) |
| Scrotum | holds testes outside the body, at a slightly lower temperature |
| Sperm ducts | carry sperm from testes to urethra |
| Prostate gland | adds fluid; sperm + fluid = semen |
| Urethra | carries semen (and, at other times, urine) through the penis |
| Penis | becomes erect to place semen in the vagina |

## Female system -- Core

Route of egg: **ovary → oviduct (fertilisation) → uterus (implantation)**.

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Ovaries | produce and release egg cells (and oestrogen, progesterone) |
| Oviducts | carry egg cell to uterus; site of fertilisation |
| Uterus | embryo implants in lining; fetus develops |
| Cervix | ring of muscle at neck of uterus; holds fetus in place; widens at birth |
| Vagina | receives penis and semen; birth canal |

## Fertilisation -- Core

The **fusion of the nuclei** of a sperm and an egg cell, normally in an **oviduct**.

## Gamete adaptations -- Core

| Gamete | Feature | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Sperm | flagellum | swims towards the egg |
| Sperm | many mitochondria | aerobic respiration releases energy for swimming |
| Sperm | enzymes in acrosome | digest a way through the jelly coat into the egg |
| Egg | energy stores | energy for early cell divisions before implantation |
| Egg | jelly coat that changes at fertilisation | stops more than one sperm entering |

## Comparing gametes -- Core

| Heading | Sperm | Egg cell |
|---|---|---|
| Size | much smaller | much larger |
| Structure | flagellum, acrosome, many mitochondria | no flagellum; energy stores; jelly coat |
| Motility | motile (swims) | not motile |
| Numbers | very large numbers produced | usually one released per menstrual cycle |

## Development -- Core

Zygote → divides → **embryo (ball of cells)** → **implants in uterus lining** → fetus.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Placenta | exchange between mother's and fetus's blood |
| Umbilical cord | carries fetus's blood to and from the placenta |
| Amniotic sac | membrane enclosing fetus and fluid |
| Amniotic fluid | cushions and protects the fetus |

## Placenta exchange -- Extended only

- Bloods are close together but **do not mix**; substances move by **diffusion**.
- Mother → fetus: **oxygen, glucose, amino acids**, water, mineral ions.
- Fetus → mother: **carbon dioxide, urea**.
- Umbilical cord: carries fetal blood with carbon dioxide and urea **to** the placenta; carries blood with oxygen and nutrients **back** to the fetus.
- Some **pathogens** (for example the rubella virus, HIV) and **toxins** (for example nicotine, alcohol) **can cross the placenta** and affect the fetus.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gamete | sex cell: sperm or egg cell |
| Fertilisation | fusion of the nuclei of a sperm and an egg cell |
| Zygote | cell formed at fertilisation |
| Embryo | ball of cells formed from the zygote |
| Implantation | the embryo embedding in the uterus lining |
| Fetus | the developing baby after the embryo stage |
| Acrosome | tip of the sperm head containing enzymes |
| Semen | sperm plus fluid from the prostate gland |
| Placenta | organ of exchange between mother and fetus |

## Exam traps

- Sperm are made in the **testes**, not the sperm ducts.
- **Urethra** (out of body) is not **ureter** (kidney to bladder).
- Fertilisation is in the **oviduct**, implantation in the **uterus**.
- Mitochondria **release** energy by respiration; they do not "make" energy.
- A "compare" answer must mention **both** gametes in each point.
- (Extended) Bloods **never mix** in the placenta.
- (Extended) Amniotic fluid **protects**; it does not feed.
- (Extended) Give substances **and** direction of movement.

## Self-test

**1.** Name the organ that produces sperm.

**2.** State the function of the prostate gland.

**3.** State the function of the scrotum.

**4.** Where does fertilisation usually happen?

**5.** State the function of the cervix.

**6.** How do the mitochondria in a sperm help it reach the egg?

**7.** What do the enzymes in the acrosome do?

**8.** Why does the jelly coat of an egg change at fertilisation?

**9.** Compare sperm and egg cells in terms of motility.

**10.** What happens to the embryo after it reaches the uterus?

**11.** *(Extended)* Name two substances that pass from the fetus's blood to the mother's blood.

**12.** *(Extended)* Why can smoking during pregnancy harm the fetus?

### Answers

**1.** The testes.

**2.** It adds fluid to the sperm, forming semen in which sperm can swim.

**3.** It holds the testes outside the body, where the temperature is slightly lower.

**4.** In an oviduct.

**5.** Holds the fetus in place during pregnancy and widens during birth.

**6.** They carry out aerobic respiration, releasing energy for the flagellum to move.

**7.** Digest a way through the jelly coat into the egg cell.

**8.** So that no more sperm can enter after one sperm has entered.

**9.** Sperm are motile and swim; egg cells are not motile.

**10.** It implants into the lining of the uterus.

**11.** Carbon dioxide and urea.

**12.** Toxins such as nicotine can cross the placenta into the fetus's blood and affect its development.
