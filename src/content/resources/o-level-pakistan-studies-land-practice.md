## Section A

**1.** State the three lines of longitude specified in the syllabus
for locating Pakistan. **[3]**

**2.** Name three cities from the syllabus list that lie in the
northern half of the country. **[3]**

**3.** Name the three named deserts of Pakistan. **[3]**

**4.** Name the four climate zones found in Pakistan. **[4]**

**5.** State what is explicitly not required when studying Pakistan's
landforms. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A question asks candidates to name the mountain ranges found in
the north and west of Pakistan.

**(a)** Name three of these ranges. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how this mountainous terrain has influenced Pakistan's
transport networks. **[3]**

**7.** The Indus River and its tributaries are central to Section 1's
coverage of drainage.

**(a)** Name three tributaries of the Indus listed in the syllabus. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest one way this river system might influence where people
settle and farm. **[2]**

**8.** A student answers a question about Pakistan's climate by
writing: "Pakistan has a harsh climate that makes life difficult."

**(a)** Explain why this answer is unlikely to score well. **[2]**
**(b)** Rewrite the point as a stronger, more specific answer,
naming a climate zone, a season, and a precise human effect. **[3]**

**9.** Explain the difference between Pakistan's administrative
geography and its physical geography, using Quetta and the Balochistan
Plateau as an example. **[3]**

**10.** Explain the causes of the monsoon and why it is described as
the single most consequential climatic feature for Pakistan's
agriculture and water supply. **[3]**

**11.** A candidate is asked to compare conditions in Pakistan's
highland regions with its lowland regions.

**(a)** Describe one climatic challenge faced by highland communities. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe one climatic challenge that can affect lowland
regions during the monsoon season, and one that can affect them
during a dry season. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why understanding Section 1 (The Land of Pakistan)
thoroughly is described as one of the most efficient uses of Paper 2
revision time, even though it carries its own separate marks. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 64°E, 70°E and 76°E [3].

**2.** Any three of: Islamabad, Murree, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, Chitral,
Gilgit [3].

**3.** Thar, Thal and Kharan [3].

**4.** Arid, semi-arid, humid and highland [4].

**5.** How Pakistan's landforms were geologically formed [1].

**6. (a)** Any three of: Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Himalaya, Sulaiman
Range, Safed Koh [3].
**(b)** Steep, high-altitude terrain makes road and rail construction
difficult and expensive [1], which has historically concentrated
transport routes along valleys and mountain passes rather than
allowing a dense, evenly spread transport network across the region
[2].

**7. (a)** Any three of: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, Kabul, Hab,
Dasht [3].
**(b)** The river system provides a reliable water supply for
irrigation and drinking, which encourages denser settlement and more
intensive farming along the river plains compared with drier areas
further from the rivers [2].

**8. (a)** This answer is too vague — it does not name a specific
climate zone, a season, or a precise human effect, so it does not
demonstrate the detailed understanding the syllabus requires [2].
**(b)** For example: "In the arid regions of southern Pakistan, the
lack of monsoon rainfall during the dry season contributes to drought
and water shortages that limit agricultural output" [3, allow any
answer that correctly names a zone, a season/timing element, and a
specific effect on people or the economy].

**9.** Administrative geography and physical geography are assessed
as two distinct layers in this section [1]. Quetta is an
administrative location — a named city — belonging to the syllabus's
coverage of administrative areas and cities [1], while the Balochistan
Plateau it sits within is a physical landform belonging to the
syllabus's coverage of natural topography; treating a city as though
it were itself a landform, or vice versa, loses marks for imprecision
even when the underlying facts are broadly correct [1].

**10.** The monsoon is caused by a seasonal reversal of wind direction
and pressure systems [1], which brings the bulk of Pakistan's annual
rainfall during the monsoon season [1]. It is described as the single
most consequential climatic feature because so much of the country's
agriculture and water supply depends on this concentrated seasonal
rainfall, meaning a strong or weak monsoon directly affects crop
yields, water availability, and the risk of both drought and flooding
across the year [1].

**11. (a)** Highland communities face low temperatures, ice and snow,
which shape daily life and can restrict access and agriculture for
part of the year [2].
**(b)** During the monsoon season, lowland regions can face flooding
from heavy rain storms [1]; during a dry season, they can face drought
and water shortage [1].

**12.** Location, administrative geography, topography and climate
introduced in Section 1 recur as context throughout the rest of Paper
2, including in later sections on natural resources, agriculture,
industry and population [1]. A candidate who has not secured this
foundational content will lose time re-deriving basic context (such as
the Indus tributaries or the climate zones) in the middle of answering
a later question that assumes this knowledge is already secure [1].

## A note on exam technique for this topic

This section rewards map-based fluency as much as written recall, so
practise locating every named city, mountain range, river and desert
on a blank outline map until it is automatic, rather than only
reciting lists in words. For written answers, use the fixed chain
examiners are looking for: name the specific physical feature, state
the physical constraint or characteristic it creates, and conclude
with the precise human consequence (on transport, farming, water
supply, or settlement) — a vague reference to "harsh climate" or
"difficult terrain" without this chain of reasoning will not access
full marks, however accurate the underlying geography is.
