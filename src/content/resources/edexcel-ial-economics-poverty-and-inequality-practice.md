## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between absolute poverty and relative poverty. **[2]**

**2.** In Country Beta the median household income is 2,400 a month and a household is counted in relative poverty if its income is under 60% of that median. Household H earns 1,500 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the poverty line and state whether Household H is in relative poverty. **[2]**

**(b)** The median rises 10% and Household H's income rises 4%. Show that Household H is now in relative poverty and state what this suggests about absolute poverty. **[2]**

**3.** Country Beta had 50 million people, of whom 12 million were in absolute poverty. Later it had 55 million people, of whom 11 million were in absolute poverty.

**(a)** Calculate the headcount ratio in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Comment on the change in the number of people in poverty. **[1]**

**4.** Country M has income shares by fifth, poorest to richest, of 6%, 10%, 15%, 22% and 47%.

**(a)** Calculate the cumulative income shares. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Gini coefficient. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe the shape of the Lorenz curve for Country M compared with the line of equality. **[1]**

**5.** After taxes and benefits Country M's income shares become 9%, 13%, 17%, 22% and 39%.

**(a)** Calculate the new Gini coefficient. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the change in the Gini coefficient shows and state one limitation of the measure. **[2]**

**6.** Distinguish between income inequality and wealth inequality, and explain why wealth is usually more unequally distributed. **[4]**

**7.** Country A has income per head of 30,000 and Country B has 1,500. Country A's income per head grows 1.5% and Country B's grows 5%.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of A's income per head to B's before and after growth. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the absolute gap before and after growth. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on your results. **[1]**

**8.** A worker in Country D earns an extra 200 a month. Means-tested benefits are withdrawn at a rate of 45% of each extra unit earned and the worker pays 20% tax on the extra income. Calculate the effective marginal tax rate and show the worker's net gain. **[4]**

**9.** In Country E total income rises from 1,000 to 1,100 but the share of the poorest fifth falls from 6% to 5%. Evaluate the view that economic growth is the best way to reduce poverty. **[10]**

**10.** Assess the view that inequality harms enterprise, savings and education. **[10]**

**11.** Evaluate the significance of the free market economy for inequality. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Absolute poverty means income below a fixed real level needed for basic needs [1]. Relative poverty means income far below the typical income of the society, so the line moves with average incomes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* answers tend to gain credit when they say which line is fixed and which moves, rather than describing "poor" and "very poor".

**2. (a)** Line = 0.6 x 2,400 = **1,440** [1]. Household H earns 1,500, which is above the line, so it is **not** in relative poverty [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** New median 2,640, new line 0.6 x 2,640 = 1,584 [1]. H's income is 1,500 x 1.04 = 1,560, which is 24 below the line. Absolute poverty may have fallen because H's real income rose [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "show that" answers need the new line and the new income both calculated, followed by the comparison.

**3. (a)** Year one: 12 / 50 = **24%** [1]. Later: 11 / 55 = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The number in poverty fell by 1 million even though the population rose by 5 million, so the fall in poverty is greater in proportion than in number [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* give the ratio first, then comment on the number; the two measures say different things.

**4. (a)** Cumulative shares: 6, 16, 31, 53, 100 [2]. **[2]**
**(b)** Area under the Lorenz curve B = 0.2 x [(0 + 0.06)/2 + (0.06 + 0.16)/2 + (0.16 + 0.31)/2 + (0.31 + 0.53)/2 + (0.53 + 1)/2] [1] = 0.2 x 1.56 = 0.312 [1]. Gini = 1 - 2 x 0.312 = **0.376** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The curve bows below the 45-degree line, starting at the origin and ending at 100% on both axes, showing unequal incomes [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* show the area calculation and the final formula; an unsupported Gini gives little to credit if it is wrong.

**5. (a)** Cumulative: 9, 22, 39, 61, 100 [1]. B = 0.2 x (0.045 + 0.155 + 0.305 + 0.5 + 0.805) = 0.362, so the Gini is 1 - 0.724 = **0.276** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The Gini fell by 0.1 (0.376 to 0.276), so taxes and benefits made the distribution more equal [1]. Limitation: grouped data hide the top, or one number does not show where the change occurred [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* say the direction of change and what it means, using the figures.

**6.** Income is a flow earned over a period [1]. Wealth is the stock of what a household owns after subtracting what it owes [1]. Wealth accumulates over a lifetime and is inherited, so gaps build up [1]. Wealth earns income, so owners gain further, widening the gap [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* contrast stock and flow explicitly; an explanation of "why" needs a mechanism such as inheritance or returns on assets.

**7. (a)** Before: 30,000 / 1,500 = **20** [1]. After: A = 30,450, B = 1,575, ratio **19.3** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Before: 28,500 [1]. After: 30,450 - 1,575 = **28,875** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The ratio narrows but the absolute gap widens, so the verdict depends on the measure used [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* state which measure your comment refers to.

**8.** Benefits lost = 0.45 x 200 = 90 [1]. Tax = 0.20 x 200 = 40 [1]. Net gain = 200 - 90 - 40 = **70** [1]. Effective marginal tax rate = (90 + 40) / 200 = **65%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* include both the withdrawn benefit and the tax in the rate.

**9.** Bottom fifth's income: 60 becomes 55, a fall of 8.3% [1]. Total income rose 10% [1]. So growth did not reduce poverty here because distribution worsened [1]. Growth can reduce poverty where it creates jobs and tax revenue for services [1]. A poverty rate that falls with mean income shows growth works [1]. Education and training raise earnings [1]. Benefits and progressive taxes target the poor directly [1]. Aid or peace may matter more in some countries [1]. Judgement: growth is necessary but not sufficient; it works best when broad-based and combined with redistribution and skills [1]. A reasoned conclusion linked to context [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* strong evaluations use the data given, compare policies against each other and reach a supported judgement.

**10.** Enterprise: reward encourages risk-taking [1], but poor people face credit and demand barriers [1]. Savings: the rich have a higher marginal propensity to save [1], which funds investment, but weak consumption demand may offset it [1]. Education: poor families cannot fund schooling [1], so human capital is wasted [1], and productivity and growth suffer [1]. Size and time: effects depend on how extreme the inequality is and on the length of the period [1]. Policy context, such as state schooling, changes the outcome [1]. Judgement: moderate inequality may help incentives, but extreme inequality harms education most [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* cover all three areas with a counter-point each, then weigh them.

**11.** Prices reward productivity and risk, so unequal rewards follow [1]. This creates incentives and can raise growth and average incomes [1]. Inheritance, market power and unequal access to credit widen gaps [1]. No automatic redistribution, so the poor may be left behind [1]. Merit goods may be underprovided [1]. Governments use progressive taxes, transfers and services [1]. Intervention can reduce incentives and efficiency [1]. Judgement: the market produces inequality but the size depends on policy [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* answers gain credit by linking market features to inequality and then judging how far intervention changes it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating a poverty line from the wrong base year.
- Giving a headcount ratio without the units, or confusing it with the number of poor people.
- Forgetting the starting point at zero in cumulative shares.
- Reporting the area under the Lorenz curve as the Gini.
- Reading a higher Gini as more equality.
- Not distinguishing a ratio from an absolute gap.
- Describing effects of inequality with no counter-point.
- Ending an evaluation with a summary and no judgement.
