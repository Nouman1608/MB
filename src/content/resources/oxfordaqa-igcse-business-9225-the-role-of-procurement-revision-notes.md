Condensed recall for section 3.3.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-the-role-of-procurement/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Evaluate** JIT stock management for a given business: reduced costs against **more frequent deliveries** and **lost purchasing economies of scale**.
- The benefits of **spare (buffer) stock** against the **cost of holding** it.
- **Analyse** factors affecting the **choice of supplier**: **price, quality, reliability**.
- No drawing or interpreting of stock control charts.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Procurement | Finding and buying the materials and services a business needs |
| Stock | Materials, part-finished goods and finished goods held by a business |
| JIT | Stock delivered just as it is needed; very low stock held |
| Buffer stock | A minimum safety stock held in case of late delivery or a rise in demand |
| Holding costs | Storage, insurance, security, waste and the cost of money tied up in stock |
| Purchasing economies of scale | Discounts a business gets for buying in large quantities |
| Supplier | A business that provides goods or services to another business |
| Lead time | Time between placing an order and receiving it |

## Holding stock

| Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|
| Production continues if a delivery is late | Storage, heating, security |
| Meets sudden demand | Money tied up |
| Bulk discounts | Waste, damage, theft, out-of-date items |
| Protection against price rises | Insurance |

## JIT: balance the benefits and costs

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Lower holding costs | More frequent deliveries cost more |
| Less cash tied up | Lost bulk discounts |
| Less waste and damage | Late delivery can stop production |
| More space | Cannot respond quickly to a sudden rise in demand |

**Best for:** perishable goods, reliable local suppliers, steady demand. **Poor for:** unreliable or distant suppliers, uncertain demand.

## Buffer stock

- A **minimum level** kept as a safety margin.
- Protects production and sales if a delivery is late or demand jumps.
- Cost: storage, insurance, money tied up, risk of waste.
- Size depends on how much is used per day and how late a delivery might be: 100 a day x 3 days late = 300 units.

## Supplier choice

| Factor | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Price | Cost per unit? Delivery charges? Discounts? Credit terms? |
| Quality | Meets the standard? Few defects? |
| Reliability | On time? Right quantity? Reacts to problems? |

The cheapest supplier is not always the best value: late or faulty supplies can cost more than the price saving.

## Calculations

- Holding cost = average stock x cost to hold one unit.
- Bulk: 1,200 x $5.00 = $6,000 + $100 delivery = $6,100. JIT: 1,200 x $5.40 = $6,480 + 4 x $100 = $6,880. Extra cost $780, less holding saving $225 gives net extra $555.
- Total supplier cost = price x quantity + expected cost of late deliveries + cost of defects.
- Cost per usable unit = total cost / usable units.

## How to evaluate a JIT decision

1. State the benefits: lower holding cost, less waste.
2. State the costs: delivery, lost discounts, risk of stoppage.
3. Use figures from the case.
4. Consider the supplier's reliability and how predictable demand is.
5. Give a clear judgement.

## Exam traps

- JIT does not mean zero stock.
- Smaller, more frequent orders often mean a **higher** price per unit.
- Buffer stock is the safety margin, not all stock.
- Price is only one supplier factor; include quality and reliability.
- State the **effect** of a late delivery: production stops, customers wait, profit falls.
- Do not draw stock control charts.

## Model answer outline: a 9-mark "evaluate" question

Question style: "Evaluate whether a restaurant should use just in time stock control."

- **For JIT:** fresh ingredients, little waste, less storage space, less cash tied up.
- **Against:** deliveries more often (higher delivery costs), no bulk discounts, a late delivery could mean no food to serve.
- **Context:** a restaurant uses perishable stock and usually has local suppliers, so JIT suits it more than it would a hardware shop.
- **Calculation:** compare the saving on holding costs with the extra cost of deliveries and lost discounts.
- **Judgement:** JIT is probably right for perishable goods, with a small buffer stock of items that do not spoil, because a stockout would lose customers.

## Revision checklist

- I can define procurement, stock, JIT and buffer stock.
- I can list the benefits and costs of holding stock.
- I can balance JIT savings against delivery costs and lost economies of scale.
- I can work out a buffer stock from daily use and possible delay.
- I can name the three supplier factors: price, quality, reliability.
- I can compare two suppliers using total cost, not price alone.
- I can say which businesses are suited to JIT and which are not.

## Self-test

1. Define procurement.
2. State two costs of holding stock.
3. State two benefits of JIT.
4. Explain one drawback of JIT.
5. Define buffer stock.
6. Give one cost of holding buffer stock.
7. Name three factors affecting the choice of supplier.
8. A business holds an average of 2,000 items and each costs $0.50 a month to hold. Calculate monthly holding cost.
9. With JIT, average stock falls to 300 items. Calculate the saving on holding costs.
10. A supplier charges $3.00 per unit; another $3.30. The business needs 5,000 units. Calculate the extra cost of the second supplier.
11. Why might the dearer supplier be cheaper overall?
12. A business uses 50 items a day and a delivery might be 4 days late. What buffer stock would cover this?

### Answers

1. Finding and buying the materials, components and services a business needs.
2. Any two: storage costs; insurance; money tied up; waste, damage or theft.
3. Any two: lower holding costs; less cash tied up; less waste; more space.
4. For example, more frequent deliveries cost more; or a late delivery can stop production.
5. A minimum quantity of stock kept as a safety margin against late deliveries or sudden demand.
6. For example, storage and insurance costs.
7. Price, quality, reliability.
8. 2,000 x $0.50 = **$1,000**.
9. Holding cost with JIT = 300 x $0.50 = $150; saving = $1,000 - $150 = **$850**.
10. 5,000 x ($3.30 - $3.00) = 5,000 x $0.30 = **$1,500**.
11. It may deliver on time and supply fewer faulty items, so there are fewer stoppages and less waste, which can cost more than the price difference.
12. 50 x 4 = **200** items.
