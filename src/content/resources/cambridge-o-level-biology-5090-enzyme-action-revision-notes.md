## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction and is not changed by the reaction |
| Enzyme | A protein that functions as a biological catalyst; involved in all metabolic reactions |
| Metabolism | All the chemical reactions in a living organism |
| Substrate | The molecule an enzyme acts on |
| Active site | The region of the enzyme where the substrate binds; its shape is complementary to the substrate |
| Enzyme-substrate complex | The temporary structure formed when the substrate is bound in the active site |
| Product | The molecule(s) formed by the reaction and released from the active site |
| Specificity | Each enzyme catalyses only one reaction (or very similar reactions) |
| Complementary | Shapes that fit together, like a lock and its key; not identical |

## Why enzymes matter

- Metabolic reactions would be far too slow at body temperature without catalysts.
- Raising temperature to speed them up would damage cells.
- Enzymes speed reactions up at the organism's own temperature.
- Because they are not changed, the same enzyme molecule is used again and again.

## Enzyme action in six steps

1. Enzyme and substrate molecules collide.
2. Substrate enters the active site (complementary shape).
3. Enzyme-substrate complex forms.
4. Reaction occurs in the active site.
5. Product(s) released.
6. Enzyme unchanged; active site free for the next substrate.

**Breakdown:** one substrate → smaller products (amylase: starch → maltose).
**Synthesis:** smaller substrates joined → one larger product, held together in the active site.

## Lock and key

- Lock = active site; key = substrate.
- Only a complementary substrate fits, so only one reaction is catalysed.
- A molecule of a different shape does not fit: no complex, no reaction.
- So the body needs different enzymes for different substrates.

## Enzymes you will meet in 5090

| Enzyme | Substrate | Product(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | Maltose |
| Maltase | Maltose | Glucose |
| Protease (pepsin, trypsin) | Protein | Amino acids |
| Lipase | Lipids | Fatty acids and glycerol |

Name clue: "-ase" ending; the first part often names the substrate (maltase → maltose; lipase → lipids).

## Full-mark sentence

"The substrate fits into the active site, which has a complementary shape, forming an enzyme-substrate complex; the reaction occurs and the products are released, leaving the enzyme unchanged to be used again."

## Reading an enzyme diagram

- Stage 1: enzyme and substrate apart. Label the **active site** as the notch or pocket in the enzyme.
- Stage 2: substrate sitting in the active site. This is the **enzyme-substrate complex**.
- Stage 3: substrate split (or joined) while still in the active site.
- Stage 4: **products** leaving; the enzyme drawn exactly as in stage 1, showing it is unchanged.
- A second molecule drawn with a different outline that does not fit the pocket shows **specificity**: no complex, no reaction.

## Short model answers

**Describe what is meant by an enzyme. [2]** A protein that acts as a biological catalyst, increasing the rate of a metabolic reaction without being changed.

**Explain why the body produces both protease and lipase. [2]** Each enzyme is specific: the active site of protease fits only protein molecules and that of lipase only lipid molecules, so different enzymes are needed to digest each nutrient.

**Explain why only a small amount of enzyme is needed. [2]** The enzyme is not changed by the reaction, so each molecule is released and reused many times.

**Starch is digested in two enzyme-controlled steps to glucose. Name the enzymes and the intermediate product. [3]** Amylase breaks starch down to maltose; maltase then breaks maltose down to glucose.

## Exam traps

- Catalyst definition needs both "increases the rate" and "not changed".
- "Same shape" is wrong; use "complementary".
- Enzymes are not alive and are not used up.
- The active site fits the substrate, not the product.
- An enzyme is specific to one substrate, not to a whole group such as "all carbohydrates".
- Keep 5.1 answers about mechanism; temperature and pH belong to 5.2 unless asked.
- Enzymes are proteins; shape depends on how the amino acid chain is folded.

## Self-test

1. Give the two parts of the definition of a catalyst.
2. What type of biological molecule is an enzyme?
3. Why do organisms need enzymes?
4. Name the region of the enzyme where the substrate binds.
5. What is formed when the substrate binds to the enzyme?
6. Why can one enzyme molecule catalyse many reactions?
7. Explain why amylase cannot break down protein.
8. Name the substrate and product of maltase.
9. What are the products of lipase action?
10. Why is "the active site has the same shape as the substrate" not credited?
11. Describe a synthesis reaction catalysed by an enzyme.
12. Suggest the substrate of an enzyme called "lactase".

### Answers

1. It increases the rate of a chemical reaction; it is not changed by the reaction.
2. A protein.
3. Metabolic reactions would be too slow at body temperature; enzymes speed them up without needing higher temperatures.
4. The active site.
5. An enzyme-substrate complex.
6. It is not changed by the reaction, so it is released and can bind another substrate molecule.
7. Protein does not have a shape complementary to amylase's active site, so it cannot bind and no enzyme-substrate complex forms.
8. Substrate: maltose. Product: glucose.
9. Fatty acids and glycerol.
10. The shapes are complementary (they fit together), not identical.
11. Two or more smaller substrate molecules bind in the active site and are joined to form one larger product.
12. Lactose (the name of the enzyme comes from its substrate).
