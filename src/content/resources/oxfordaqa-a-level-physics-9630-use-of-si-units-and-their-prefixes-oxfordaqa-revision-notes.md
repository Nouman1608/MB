These notes condense **3.1.1 Use of SI units and their prefixes** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 and used in every later unit. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [SI units and prefixes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions with mark schemes, use the [SI units and prefixes practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Base quantities and units: mass (kg), length (m), time (s), amount of substance (mol), temperature (K), electric current (A).
- SI derived units.
- The prefixes T, G, M, k, c, m, μ, n, p, f, their values, and standard form.
- Converting between units of the same quantity, such as J and eV, and J and kW h.
- Not required: the candela; recalling definitions of base quantities; dimensional analysis.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base (fundamental) unit | One of the six SI units from which all others are built |
| Derived unit | A unit formed from base units by a defining equation, such as N = kg m s⁻² |
| Prefix | A symbol placed before a unit that multiplies it by a power of ten |
| Standard form | A number written as a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10 |
| Electronvolt (eV) | Energy transferred when one electron moves through 1 V: 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J |
| Kilowatt-hour (kW h) | Energy transferred by 1 kW in 1 hour: 3.6 × 10⁶ J |

## Prefix ladder

| Symbol | T | G | M | k | c | m | μ | n | p | f |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power of ten | 12 | 9 | 6 | 3 | −2 | −3 | −6 | −9 | −12 | −15 |

From T down to k, and from m down to f, each step is a factor of 1000. The unit itself sits between k and m, so 1 km = 10⁶ mm; c (10⁻²) is the one prefix off the 1000-step pattern.

## Derived units to know in base form

| Unit | Comes from | Base units |
|---|---|---|
| N | F = ma | kg m s⁻² |
| J | W = Fs | kg m² s⁻² |
| W | P = W/t | kg m² s⁻³ |
| Pa | p = F/A | kg m⁻¹ s⁻² |
| C | Q = It | A s |
| Hz | f = 1/T | s⁻¹ |
| N m⁻¹ (spring constant) | k = F/ΔL | kg s⁻² |
| N s (impulse, momentum) | FΔt = Δ(mv) | kg m s⁻¹ |

## Method boxes

**Converting a prefixed value**

1. Replace the prefix with its power of ten.
2. If the unit is squared or cubed, square or cube the power of ten too.
3. Write the result in standard form.

**Expressing a unit in base units**

1. Write the defining equation for the quantity.
2. Replace each quantity with its unit.
3. Replace any named unit (N, J, Pa) with its base units and simplify the indices.

**Energy conversions**

- eV → J: × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹. J → eV: ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹.
- kW h → J: × 3.6 × 10⁶. J → kW h: ÷ 3.6 × 10⁶.
- For E = Pt in kW h: P in kW, t in hours.

## Squared and cubed prefixes

| Convert | Factor |
|---|---|
| cm² → m² | 10⁻⁴ |
| mm² → m² | 10⁻⁶ |
| μm² → m² | 10⁻¹² |
| cm³ → m³ | 10⁻⁶ |
| mm³ → m³ | 10⁻⁹ |
| g cm⁻³ → kg m⁻³ | × 10³ |
| km h⁻¹ → m s⁻¹ | ÷ 3.6 |

## Worked reminders

- **Wavelength.** 633 nm = 633 × 10⁻⁹ m = 6.33 × 10⁻⁷ m.
- **Area.** A plate of 4.0 cm by 2.5 cm has area 10 cm² = 10 × 10⁻⁴ m² = 1.0 × 10⁻³ m².
- **Volume.** A 500 cm³ bottle holds 500 × 10⁻⁶ m³ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ m³.
- **Particle energy.** A 5.0 MeV particle has 5.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹³ J.
- **Household energy.** A 2.0 kW heater for 90 minutes transfers 2.0 × 1.5 = 3.0 kW h = 1.08 × 10⁷ J.
- **Temperature.** 27 °C = 300 K; a rise of 15 °C is a rise of 15 K.

## Exam traps

- kg, not g, is the base unit of mass.
- mm² to m² is 10⁻⁶, not 10⁻³.
- M is mega, m is milli; k for kilo is lower case.
- "ms" is milliseconds; write "m s⁻¹" with a space.
- In J → eV, the eV value must come out larger than the J value.
- Using minutes in E = Pt for joules, or seconds for kW h.
- Mixing kelvin and Celsius for an absolute temperature.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the six SI base quantities in this specification and give their units.
2. Give the value of the prefix p.
3. Express the pascal in SI base units.
4. Express the watt in SI base units.
5. Convert 0.75 mm² to m².
6. Convert 250 cm³ to m³.
7. Convert 54 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
8. Convert 7.9 g cm⁻³ to kg m⁻³.
9. An alpha particle has energy 4.8 MeV. Express this in J.
10. A photon has energy 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Express this in eV.
11. A 1.2 kW kettle runs for 3.0 minutes. Find the energy in kJ and in kW h.
12. Express 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m using a suitable prefix.

### Answers

1. Mass (kg), length (m), time (s), amount of substance (mol), temperature (K), electric current (A).
2. **10⁻¹²**.
3. Pa = N m⁻² = **kg m⁻¹ s⁻²**.
4. W = J s⁻¹ = **kg m² s⁻³**.
5. 0.75 × 10⁻⁶ = **7.5 × 10⁻⁷ m²**.
6. 250 × 10⁻⁶ = **2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³**.
7. 54 ÷ 3.6 = **15 m s⁻¹**.
8. 7.9 × 10³ = **7900 kg m⁻³**.
9. 4.8 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **7.7 × 10⁻¹³ J**.
10. 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **2.5 eV**.
11. 1200 × 180 = 2.16 × 10⁵ J = **216 kJ**; 1.2 × 0.050 = **0.060 kW h**.
12. **45 fm** (4.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ = 45 × 10⁻¹⁵).

## Where marks are usually lost

- A correct method spoiled by one unconverted prefix in the substitution.
- Final answers with no unit, or with a unit that does not match the quantity.
- Base-unit answers with named units left in, such as "N m⁻²" when base units are asked for.
