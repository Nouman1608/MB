## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a Brønsted–Lowry acid. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by a Brønsted–Lowry base. **[1]**

**3.** Give the formula of the ion formed when a proton bonds to a water molecule. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a conjugate acid–base pair. **[1]**

**5.** Give the conjugate base of each species.

**(a)** HCOOH **[1]**

**(b)** NH₄⁺ **[1]**

**(c)** H₂O **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Hydrofluoric acid is a weak acid: HF(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + F⁻(aq)

**(a)** Identify the Brønsted–Lowry acid and base in the forward reaction and in the reverse reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the two conjugate acid–base pairs. **[2]**

**7.** Methylamine reacts with methanoic acid: CH₃NH₂ + HCOOH → CH₃NH₃⁺ + HCOO⁻. Identify the Brønsted–Lowry acid and base, explaining your answer. **[2]**

**8.** This question is about the hydrogencarbonate ion, HCO₃⁻.

**(a)** Write equations to show HCO₃⁻ acting as an acid with OH⁻ and as a base with H₃O⁺. **[2]**

**(b)** State the term for a species that can act as either an acid or a base. **[1]**

**9.** Phosphoric acid, H₃PO₄, is a polyprotic acid.

**(a)** Write equations for the first two proton transfers from phosphoric acid to water. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify a species in your equations that can act as both an acid and a base. **[1]**

**10.** In the nitration of benzene, the electrophile forms in this reaction:

HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻

**(a)** State the role of HNO₃ in this reaction and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this shows that "acid" and "base" describe a role in a reaction rather than a fixed property. **[1]**

**11.** Write ionic equations for the reactions of hydrochloric acid with:

**(a)** sodium carbonate solution **[1]**

**(b)** aqueous ammonia **[1]**

**(c)** solid calcium oxide **[1]**

**(d)** Name the Brønsted–Lowry base in each of (a), (b) and (c). **[1]**

**12.** Pure water ionises slightly: H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻. Explain how this equation shows water acting as both a Brønsted–Lowry acid and a Brønsted–Lowry base. **[2]**

**13.** The proton transfer from hydrochloric acid to water is written with a single arrow, but that from ethanoic acid to water is written with a reversible arrow. Explain the difference. **[2]**

**14.** Ethanoate ions react with water: CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ CH₃COOH(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

**(a)** Identify the Brønsted–Lowry acid and base in the forward reaction and in the reverse reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the equation to explain whether a solution of sodium ethanoate is acidic or alkaline. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A proton (H⁺) donor [1]

**2.** A proton (H⁺) acceptor [1]

**3.** H₃O⁺ (oxonium ion) [1]

**4.** Two species that differ by one proton (H⁺) [1]

**5. (a)** HCOO⁻ [1]

**(b)** NH₃ [1]

**(c)** OH⁻ [1]

**6. (a)** Forward: acid HF, base H₂O [1]; reverse: acid H₃O⁺, base F⁻ [1]

**(b)** HF / F⁻ [1]; H₃O⁺ / H₂O [1]
*Examiner insight:* In each pair the acid has one more H and a charge one higher than its conjugate base. Use this to check.

**7.** HCOOH is the acid: it donates a proton and becomes HCOO⁻ [1]; CH₃NH₂ is the base: it accepts the proton (using the nitrogen lone pair) and becomes CH₃NH₃⁺ [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Methylamine is a base because it is an amine" does not score. Say what it does: it accepts a proton.

**8. (a)** HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻ → CO₃²⁻ + H₂O [1]; HCO₃⁻ + H₃O⁺ → H₂CO₃ + H₂O [1]

**(b)** Amphiprotic (also accept amphoteric) [1]

**9. (a)** H₃PO₄ + H₂O ⇌ H₂PO₄⁻ + H₃O⁺ [1]; H₂PO₄⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HPO₄²⁻ + H₃O⁺ [1]

**(b)** H₂PO₄⁻ (or H₂O) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each step removes one proton and lowers the charge by one. Writing H₃PO₄ → 3H⁺ + PO₄³⁻ in one step does not answer the question.

**10. (a)** HNO₃ acts as a **base** [1]; it accepts a proton from H₂SO₄ (H₂SO₄ is the proton donor) [1]

**(b)** Nitric acid, usually an acid in water, behaves as a base when it meets a stronger proton donor, so its role depends on its reaction partner [1]
*Examiner insight:* The name "nitric acid" is a common name, not a statement of its role in every reaction.

**11. (a)** 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → H₂O + CO₂ [1]

**(b)** H⁺ + NH₃ → NH₄⁺ [1]

**(c)** 2H⁺ + CaO → Ca²⁺ + H₂O [1]

**(d)** CO₃²⁻; NH₃; O²⁻ (in CaO) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Spectator ions such as Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are left out of ionic equations; including them in (a) or (b) means the equation is not ionic.

**12.** One water molecule donates a proton, becoming OH⁻, so it acts as an acid [1]; the other accepts the proton, becoming H₃O⁺, so it acts as a base [1]

**13.** Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid: proton transfer to water is complete [1]; ethanoic acid is a weak acid: only a small fraction of molecules transfer a proton, and an equilibrium is set up [1]

**14. (a)** Forward: acid H₂O, base CH₃COO⁻ [1]; reverse: acid CH₃COOH, base OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** The ethanoate ion accepts a proton from water, producing OH⁻ ions, so [OH⁻] > [H⁺] and the solution is alkaline [1]
*Examiner insight:* This is why the equivalence point of a weak acid–strong base titration lies above pH 7, which you will use later in section 3.1.10.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions that mention "hydrogen" or "H⁺ ions in solution" instead of "proton donor".
- Labelling only the forward direction of an equilibrium.
- Wrong charges on conjugate species, especially for HPO₄²⁻ and CO₃²⁻.
- Calling nitric acid the acid in the nitronium ion equation.
- Writing full rather than ionic equations when ionic equations are asked for.
