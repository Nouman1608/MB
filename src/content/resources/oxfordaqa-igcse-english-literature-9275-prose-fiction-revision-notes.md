## Section A at a glance

- Paper 1, Section A: one question on your set prose text, **30 marks**.
- Paper 1 is 1 hour 30 minutes, **closed book**, and taken on **both routes**.
- Choice: an **essay-style** question or a **passage-based** question (extract printed).
- Aim for about **45 minutes**, with five minutes of planning.
- Set texts come from a prescribed list that changes: check the list for your exam year.

## What the marks reward

| AO | In one line |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Engage with themes, ideas and contexts, and keep to the question |
| AO2 | Analyse how the writer's language, structure and form create effects |
| AO3 | Give an informed personal response with accurate terminology and clear writing |

The jump from a mid-level to a high-level answer is usually AO2: explaining **how** and **why**, not
**what happens**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters in an answer |
|---|---|---|
| First-person narrator | The story is told by "I", a character in it | Limits what we know; can create sympathy or doubt |
| Retrospective narration | An older narrator looks back | Creates a gap between the younger self's view and the older self's judgement |
| Third-person limited | Narration stays close to one character | We share their perceptions while seeing them from outside |
| Omniscient narrator | Narration knows and moves between many minds | Allows comment, irony and a wide social view |
| Unreliable narrator | A narrator the reader learns to question | Meaning lies in the gap between telling and truth |
| Foreshadowing | An early hint of later events | Builds suspense or inevitability; links two points in the text |
| Frame narrative | A story set inside another story | Shapes how far we trust the inner story |
| Motif | A repeated image, object or idea | Gives a line of argument across the whole novel |
| Pathetic fallacy | Weather or setting reflecting feeling | Externalises a character's mood |
| Dialogue | Characters' direct speech | Reveals relationships, power and what is left unsaid |
| Turning point | A moment where direction changes | Ideal anchor for a paragraph on development |

## Two question types, two strategies

| Passage-based | Essay-style |
|---|---|
| Start from the printed words | Start from your memorised quotation bank |
| Do exactly what the wording asks: extract only, or extract and the wider text | Range across beginning, middle and end |
| Close, line-by-line AO2 analysis | Development and pattern across the text |
| Risk: drifting away from the extract | Risk: retelling the plot in order |

## The analytical paragraph

**Claim → short embedded evidence → method → effect on the reader → link to the question or the
wider text.**

Keep quotations to two to six words. Write "the writer presents..." so that the writer's choices stay
in view.

## Context: use it, do not bolt it on

One precise contextual point placed where it explains a choice in the text is worth more than a
paragraph of history. Ask: what did the writer's society assume, and does the novel reflect,
question or challenge it?

## Closed-book revision checklist

- Eight to ten versatile quotations, each tagged with its method and two possible uses.
- A one-page timeline of turning points.
- Two or three central themes, each with evidence from more than one stage of the text.
- Timed paragraph practice: one paragraph in eight to ten minutes from memory.

## Exam traps

- Retelling the plot instead of analysing it.
- Naming a device without its effect.
- Long, misremembered quotations.
- Context with no link to the text.
- Mixing in details from a film adaptation or a different edition.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Section A worth, and is Paper 1 open or closed book?
2. What two types of question can you choose between?
3. What is the difference between third-person limited and omniscient narration?
4. Why is a motif useful in an essay-style answer?
5. Which assessment objective covers the writer's methods?
6. What should every analytical paragraph contain after the quotation?
7. How long should you spend on Section A?

**Answers:** 1. 30 marks; closed book. 2. An essay-style question on the whole text or a
passage-based question with a printed extract. 3. Limited stays close to one character's perceptions;
omniscient moves between many minds and can comment freely. 4. It recurs, so tracing it gives you
evidence from several points and a line of argument across the novel. 5. AO2. 6. The method, its
effect on the reader and a link back to the question. 7. About 45 minutes, including about five
minutes of planning.
