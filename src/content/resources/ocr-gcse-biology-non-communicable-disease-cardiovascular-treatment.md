## B6.3r: non-communicable diseases and multiple causes

A **non-communicable disease** cannot be passed from one person to another. No pathogen is involved. Most are long-lasting.

Most are also **multifactorial**: they develop because several factors interact, not because of one cause. The factors fall into three groups.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Inherited | Gene variants that raise the risk of a disease |
| Lifestyle | Smoking, alcohol, diet, lack of exercise |
| Environment | Exposure to radiation or to chemicals in the air or workplace |

The specification names these examples.

- **Cardiovascular diseases** affect the heart and blood vessels. Coronary heart disease is the one to know in detail.
- **Cancers** (many forms).
- **Bronchitis**, a lung disease in which the airways are inflamed and produce extra mucus.
- **Cirrhosis**, a liver disease in which healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue.
- **Type 2 diabetes**, a disease influenced by nutrition. Body cells respond poorly to insulin, so blood glucose stays high. Being overweight raises the risk.

"Interaction" matters. A person can carry a risk-raising gene variant and never become ill. Another person with the same variant who smokes and eats a poor diet has a much higher risk. One factor may make another more harmful.

A common misconception is that cancer is a genetic disease you inherit. Most cancers start with changes in body cells during a lifetime and are not passed to children. Inherited variants can raise the risk, but they are only one factor.

## B6.3s: treating cardiovascular disease

In **coronary heart disease** fatty material builds up inside the coronary arteries and narrows them. The heart muscle gets less oxygenated blood. Narrowing can cause chest pain on exertion. If a coronary artery becomes blocked, part of the heart muscle is starved of oxygen and a heart attack follows. Look back at the coronary arteries in the [circulatory system guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-biology-exchange-surfaces-and-the-human-circulatory-system/) if you need to.

There are three types of treatment to evaluate.

### Lifestyle changes

Stop smoking, eat less saturated fat and salt, keep to a healthy body mass, take regular exercise and drink less alcohol.

- Benefits: cheap, no operation, and they lower the risk of several other diseases.
- Drawbacks: they need sustained effort, results come slowly, and they cannot remove a blockage that already exists.

### Medical treatment (drugs)

| Drug type | Purpose | Points to weigh up |
|---|---|---|
| Statins | Lower blood cholesterol, which slows the build-up of fatty deposits | Taken for years; can cause side effects such as muscle pain; acts on a risk factor (cholesterol) and does not clear existing deposits |
| Drugs that lower blood pressure | Reduce the strain on the heart and artery walls | Long-term use; effects must be monitored |
| Anticoagulants or anti-clotting drugs | Make clots in arteries less likely | Increased risk of bleeding |

Drugs avoid surgery and suit many patients. They have to be taken regularly, and benefits depend on the patient following the course.

### Surgical treatment

| Operation | What it does | Points to weigh up |
|---|---|---|
| Stent | A wire mesh tube is expanded inside a narrowed coronary artery and holds it open | Quick recovery, but a blood clot can form on the stent and the artery can narrow again |
| Coronary bypass | A length of blood vessel (often taken from the leg) is joined around the blocked section | Major open surgery, longer recovery, but can treat several blockages |
| Heart valve replacement | A damaged valve is replaced by a mechanical or biological valve | Mechanical valves can need long-term anti-clotting drugs |
| Heart transplant | A damaged heart is replaced by a donor heart | Donors are scarce; the immune system can reject the organ, so drugs that suppress it are needed |

### How to evaluate

Evaluate means weigh the advantages against the disadvantages and reach a judgement. Use a pattern: benefit, then drawback, then a conclusion that depends on the patient. For example, lifestyle change is the first choice for everyone, drugs suit a patient with high cholesterol, and surgery is for a patient whose artery is already seriously blocked.

### Worked example 1: evaluating a statin trial

In a trial, 1000 people took a statin and 1000 took a placebo for five years. The statin group had mean blood LDL cholesterol of 3.1 mmol/dm³ at the end, compared with 4.6 mmol/dm³ at the start. In the placebo group 71 people had a heart attack. In the statin group 48 did.

1. Change in cholesterol = 3.1 − 4.6 = −1.5 mmol/dm³.
2. Percentage change = (−1.5 ÷ 4.6) × 100 = **−32.6%** (a fall of 32.6%).
3. Fewer heart attacks = 71 − 48 = **23** per 1000 people.
4. As a percentage of the placebo figure, 23 ÷ 71 × 100 = **32.4%** fewer heart attacks.

Conclusion: the statin lowered cholesterol and was linked to fewer heart attacks. A good answer also notes the limits: five years only, and the figures do not show side effects.

## B6.3t: lifestyle factors and the incidence of disease

**Incidence** counts the people who newly develop a disease during one stated period. To compare places of different sizes, convert to a rate, for example cases per 100 000 people.

| Lifestyle factor | Diseases it is linked with |
|---|---|
| Smoking | Lung cancer, bronchitis, coronary heart disease |
| Alcohol | Cirrhosis; excess also raises the risk of some cancers |
| Diet | Obesity, type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease |
| Exercise | Regular exercise lowers the risk of coronary heart disease and type 2 diabetes |

Smoking gives an example of cause. Tar in cigarette smoke contains chemicals that damage the DNA of lung cells and can lead to cancer. Smoking also damages the lining of the airways, which leads to bronchitis, and damages artery walls, which helps fatty deposits form.

### Local, national and global

- **Local**: a town or region. Incidence can differ between neighbouring areas because of differences in smoking rates, diet, income and age.
- **National**: a whole country. Governments collect data and run health campaigns.
- **Global**: comparing countries. Type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease are becoming more common in places where diets and activity levels have changed.

### Worked example 2: comparing incidence

Town A had 36 new cases of a lung disease in one year and a population of 48 000. Town B had 52 new cases and a population of 91 000.

- Town A: 36 ÷ 48 000 × 100 000 = **75.0 per 100 000**.
- Town B: 52 ÷ 91 000 × 100 000 = **57.1 per 100 000**.

Town B has more cases but the lower rate. Comparing raw numbers would mislead.

### Interpreting data

**Frequency tables and histograms.** The ages at which 200 people were first diagnosed with coronary heart disease:

| Age (years) | 40 to 49 | 50 to 59 | 60 to 69 | 70 to 79 | 80 to 89 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 34 | 71 | 58 | 25 |

The total is 12 + 34 + 71 + 58 + 25 = 200. The modal class is 60 to 69, which holds 71 ÷ 200 × 100 = **35.5%** of cases. Because the classes are equal in width, the bars of a histogram are the same width and touch. Their heights show frequency.

**Scatter diagrams and correlation.** Six regions had mean weekly alcohol intake per adult (units): 4, 6, 9, 11, 14, 17. Their new cirrhosis cases per 100 000 were 6, 8, 13, 14, 21, 24. Plot intake on the x-axis and cases on the y-axis. The points rise from left to right, so there is a **positive correlation**. A line of best fit could be drawn through them.

**Correlation is not proof of cause.** To argue that alcohol causes cirrhosis, you need more: a biological mechanism (alcohol is broken down in the liver, and heavy drinking damages liver cells), and consistent results from different studies. Other factors, such as diet or income, may differ between regions.

**Sampling.** A study cannot test everyone. A sample should be large enough and chosen at random so that it represents the population. A small, self-selected sample can give a biased result. Diseases also take years to develop, so a long study is needed.

## B6.3u: cancer

Cancer is the result of changes in cells that lead to uncontrolled growth and division. Normally the cell cycle is controlled and cells divide only when the body needs new cells. In a cancer cell a change (a mutation) in the genes that control division makes the cell divide again and again by mitosis. See the [mitosis and stem cells guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-biology-transport-across-membranes-mitosis-and-stem-cells/) and the [mutations guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-biology-genome-alleles-variants-and-mutations/).

A mass of abnormal cells is called a **tumour**.

| | Benign tumour | Malignant tumour |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Grows in one place | Invades neighbouring tissue |
| Spread | Does not spread | Cells break off, travel in the blood and form **secondary tumours** elsewhere |
| Description | Not cancer | Cancer |

Known risk factors (things that raise the chance of a mutation) include tobacco smoke, ultraviolet radiation, ionising radiation and some inherited variants. The disease is multifactorial, which links back to B6.3r.

### Worked example 3: why uncontrolled division becomes serious

One cancer cell divides every 24 hours, and each division doubles the number of cells.

- After 10 divisions: 2¹⁰ = **1024** cells.
- After 20 divisions: 2²⁰ = **1 048 576** cells.
- After 30 divisions: 2³⁰ = **1 073 741 824** cells, after 30 days.

The cell number grows exponentially. Real tumours grow more slowly because some cells die and some cannot get enough oxygen, but the pattern explains why early growth goes unnoticed and later growth is fast.

## Common errors

- Writing that cancer is "caused by a virus" or "catching it". Cancer is not communicable.
- Saying a factor "causes" a disease on the strength of a correlation alone.
- Comparing the number of cases instead of a rate per 100 000.
- Listing the treatments for cardiovascular disease without a drawback for each, in an "evaluate" answer.
- Saying a stent "removes" fatty material. It holds the artery open.
- Calling every tumour cancer. A benign tumour does not spread.
