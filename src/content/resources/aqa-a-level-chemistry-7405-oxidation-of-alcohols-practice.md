These questions cover section **3.3.5.2 Oxidation of alcohols** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Oxidation of alcohols study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-oxidation-of-alcohols/) and the [Oxidation of alcohols revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-oxidation-of-alcohols-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** Classify each alcohol as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** cyclohexanol **[1]**

**(b)** 2-methylbutan-1-ol **[1]**

**(c)** 3-methylpentan-3-ol **[1]**

**2.** Write equations, using [O], for these oxidations.

**(a)** butan-2-ol to butanone **[1]**

**(b)** 3-methylbutan-1-ol to 3-methylbutanal **[1]**

**(c)** 3-methylbutan-1-ol to 3-methylbutanoic acid **[1]**

**3.** 2-methylpropan-2-ol is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

**(a)** State what is observed. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the observation in terms of structure. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how ethanal can be prepared from ethanol in the laboratory, and explain how the method prevents the formation of ethanoic acid. **[4]**

**5.** A student wants to make propanoic acid from propan-1-ol.

**(a)** Explain why the mixture is heated under reflux rather than distilled straight away. **[2]**

**(b)** State why the top of the reflux condenser must not be sealed. **[1]**

**6.** The boiling points of propanal, propan-1-ol and propanoic acid are about 48 °C, 97 °C and 141 °C. Explain why propanal has the lowest boiling point. **[2]**

**7.** Propanal is warmed with Tollens' reagent.

**(a)** State what is observed and what happens to the silver(I) ions. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation, using [O], for the reaction of propanal. **[1]**

**8.** Write equations, using [O], for the complete oxidation under reflux of:

**(a)** propane-1,3-diol, HOCH₂CH₂CH₂OH **[1]**

**(b)** propane-1,2-diol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH **[1]**

**9.** A and C are isomers with formula C₃H₈O. A is oxidised and the product B is distilled off as it forms; B gives a red precipitate with Fehling's solution. C is oxidised to D, which gives no change with Fehling's solution. Identify A, B, C and D. **[4]**

**10.** 7.40 g of butan-1-ol (Mᵣ = 74.0) is heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI). 6.60 g of butanoic acid (Mᵣ = 88.0) is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**11.** A student heats propan-2-ol under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI), expecting to make a carboxylic acid. Explain why no carboxylic acid forms. **[2]**

**12.** Three unlabelled bottles contain propanal, propanone and propan-2-ol. Using Tollens' reagent and acidified potassium dichromate(VI), describe how you would identify each liquid. Give the observations you would expect. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Secondary** [1]

**(b)** **Primary** [1]

**(c)** **Tertiary** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the branch is on C2, but the C–OH carbon (C1) is bonded to only one other carbon.

**2. (a)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₂CH₃ + H₂O [1]

**(b)** (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH + [O] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CHO + H₂O [1]

**(c)** (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂COOH + H₂O [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** The solution stays orange [1]

**(b)** The carbon bonded to –OH has no hydrogen atom, so it cannot be oxidised (without breaking C–C bonds) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Warm ethanol, in excess, and add acidified potassium dichromate(VI) slowly [1]; distil the ethanal off as soon as it forms, collecting it in a cooled receiver [1]. Ethanal has a lower boiling point than ethanol because there is no hydrogen bonding between its molecules [1], so it leaves the flask before it can be oxidised further, and with ethanol in excess there is no spare oxidant to oxidise it [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** The aldehyde forms first and is volatile [1]; under reflux it condenses and returns to the flask, so it stays with the excess oxidant until it is fully oxidised to the acid [1]

**(b)** Heating a sealed system would build up pressure and could make the apparatus explode [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Propanal molecules have no O–H bond, so they cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other [1]; the alcohol and acid can, so they have stronger intermolecular forces and higher boiling points [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** A silver mirror forms on the inside of the tube [1]; silver(I) ions are reduced to silver metal [1]

**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂COOH** [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** HOCH₂CH₂CH₂OH + 4[O] → HOOCCH₂COOH + 2H₂O [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH + 3[O] → CH₃COCOOH + 2H₂O [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Count each –OH separately: a primary group needs 2[O] and gives one H₂O; a secondary group needs 1[O] and gives one H₂O.

**9.** A is **propan-1-ol** [1]; B is **propanal** [1]; C is **propan-2-ol** [1]; D is **propanone** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** n(butan-1-ol) = 7.40 ÷ 74.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; n(butanoic acid) = 6.60 ÷ 88.0 = 0.0750 mol [1]; yield = 0.0750 ÷ 0.100 × 100 = **75.0%** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Propan-2-ol is a secondary alcohol, so it is oxidised to a ketone, propanone [1]; ketones are not easily oxidised further, so no carboxylic acid forms however long it is heated [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Warm a sample of each with Tollens' reagent in a water bath [1]; only propanal gives a silver mirror, identifying it [1]. Warm samples of the other two with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]; propan-2-ol turns the solution from orange to green [1]; propanone leaves it orange [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Dichromate alone cannot separate propanal from propan-2-ol, because both turn it green; the order of tests matters.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying from the branch position instead of the C–OH carbon.
- Writing H₂O in the aldehyde-to-acid step.
- Describing distillation without saying why the aldehyde escapes before oxidation.
- Missing the "no change" observation for a ketone.
- Forgetting that a secondary alcohol stops at the ketone.
