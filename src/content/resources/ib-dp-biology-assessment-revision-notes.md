Condensed for quick recall of how the course is assessed. For the full subject overview, use the

## Why 80/20 rather than an even split

The 80% external / 20% internal balance reflects that the IA tests focused practical and investigative skill on one self-chosen topic, while the two written papers are needed to sample understanding across the whole syllabus breadth -- a single IA, however well executed, cannot demonstrate the same content coverage that two full papers can assess. Keeping this in mind when allocating revision time -- proportionally more time on paper-style practice once a solid IA draft exists -- reflects where the majority of the final grade actually sits.

## The assessment split

| Component | Weight | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 36% | Multiple-choice and data-based questions across the whole syllabus (the data-based section still requires written "state"/"outline" answers, not just selecting an option) |
| Paper 2 | 44% | Data-based, short-answer and extended-response questions requiring sustained scientific writing |
| Internal assessment | 20% | An individual scientific investigation, written up in a report of up to 3,000 words |

External examination (Papers 1 + 2) = **80%**. Internal assessment = **20%**. This split is the
same at both SL and HL — the papers differ in length and demand, not in this weighting.

## Two things not to confuse

- The graded **internal assessment** is an *individually* designed, carried out and written
  investigation.
- The **interdisciplinary collaborative sciences project**, done with peers from other group 4
  subjects, builds investigative skills but is **not itself graded** — don't confuse the two when
  planning your IA timeline.

## Why this split matters for revision

Because Paper 2 (44%) outweighs Paper 1 (36%), and Paper 2 specifically requires sustained,
extended-response scientific writing (not just short answers), practising **structuring a longer
written scientific answer** is worth dedicated revision time, not just content recall.

## The internal assessment in more depth

The IA report is graded against criteria covering the whole investigative process: formulating a focused, testable research question grounded in a clear scientific rationale; designing a method capable of generating sufficient, reliable data to answer it; recording and processing that data appropriately, including a genuine treatment of uncertainty and error; and evaluating the investigation critically, including specific, actionable improvements rather than generic ones. A common weakness examiners note is an evaluation section that lists limitations without connecting them concretely to how they affected the specific data collected -- a strong evaluation ties each limitation back to its actual impact on the results, and proposes a feasible, specific improvement rather than a vague one such as "use better equipment."

## Command terms across both papers

Both papers draw from the same command-term glossary shared across the DP sciences: **state**, **define**, **identify** and **describe** at the lower end (recall and straightforward description); **explain**, **compare**, **deduce** and **suggest** in the middle; **evaluate**, **discuss** and **justify** at the top, requiring a reasoned judgement or synthesis across multiple pieces of evidence. The command terms are not assigned to either paper. Paper 2 carries the larger share of the external mark (44 of the 80 percentage points, or 55%), so practising **evaluate** and **discuss**-style answers -- weighing evidence, acknowledging limitations, reaching a justified conclusion -- is worth the time.

## How Biology's assessment compares to its sibling sciences

DP Chemistry and DP Physics share the same overall assessment architecture -- two external written papers plus an individually graded internal assessment, alongside the ungraded collaborative sciences project -- but each subject organises its papers around its own framework rather than Biology's broader thematic structure across its topic areas. If you are also studying another Group 4 science, recognising that the *shape* of assessment (external papers plus IA plus collaborative project) transfers directly, while the specific content organisation does not, avoids wasted time trying to map one subject's structure onto another's.

## Why extended-response writing deserves dedicated practice

Because Paper 2 specifically requires sustained scientific writing across multiple linked questions, rather than a series of short, disconnected answers, it rewards a different skill from Paper 1's multiple-choice and data-based format: structuring a coherent, well-sequenced argument that draws on evidence and terminology across a topic, not just recalling isolated facts. Practising full extended-response answers under timed conditions -- not just outlining what would be included -- is the most direct way to build this specific skill, since planning an answer and actually writing it fluently under time pressure are genuinely different capabilities.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is IB DP Biology assessed?**
IB DP Biology is assessed by Paper 1 (36%), Paper 2 (44%) and an internal assessment (20%), so external examination makes up 80% of the grade.

**Is the IB Biology weighting different at SL and HL?**
No. The 80% external and 20% internal split is the same at SL and HL; the papers differ in length and demand, not in weighting.

**How long can the IB Biology internal assessment report be?**
The internal assessment is an individual scientific investigation written up in a report of up to 3,000 words.

**Is the collaborative sciences project graded?**
No. The interdisciplinary collaborative sciences project, done with peers from other group 4 subjects, builds investigative skills but is not itself graded, unlike the individual internal assessment.
