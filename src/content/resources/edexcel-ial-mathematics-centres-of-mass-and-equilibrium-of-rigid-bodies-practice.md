## Questions

**1.** A force of 15 N acts at the end A of a light rod OA of length 1.6 m, at 50° to OA.

**(a)** Find the moment of this force about O. **[2]**
**(b)** A second force of 8 N acts at the midpoint of OA, perpendicular to the rod, and turns it the opposite way. Find the resultant moment about O and its sense. **[2]**

**2.** A light rod AB of length 2.4 m carries particles of mass 3 kg at A, 2 kg at a point 1.0 m from A, and 5 kg at B. Find the distance from A of the point where a single support would balance the rod. **[3]**

**3.** Particles of mass 2 kg, 3 kg and 5 kg are at the points (1, 3), (5, −2) and (−3, 4) respectively.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the centre of mass. **[3]**
**(b)** A fourth particle of mass 10 kg is added so that the centre of mass of all four is at (1, 3). Find its coordinates. **[3]**

**4.** A uniform circular lamina has centre O and radius 6 cm. A circular hole of radius 2 cm is cut out, with its centre 3 cm from O.

**(a)** Find the distance of the centre of mass of the remaining lamina from O, and say on which side of O it lies. **[4]**
**(b)** Point B is on the rim, with OB perpendicular to the line through O and the centre of the hole. The lamina hangs freely from B. Find the angle OB makes with the vertical. **[2]**

**5.** A uniform lamina is a rectangle 6 cm wide and 4 cm tall with an isosceles triangle of height 3 cm on its top 6 cm edge.

**(a)** Find the distance of the centre of mass from the base of the rectangle. **[4]**
**(b)** The lamina is suspended from one bottom corner A, with AB the base. Find the angle AB makes with the vertical. **[3]**

**6.** A uniform semicircular lamina has radius 0.9 m and diameter AB. It hangs freely from A. Find the angle AB makes with the vertical. **[4]**

**7.** A uniform rectangular lamina ABCD has AB = 1.2 m, BC = 0.8 m and mass 6 kg. It can turn freely in a vertical plane about a smooth horizontal axis through A. It is held with AB horizontal and C vertically below B by a horizontal force of magnitude P N applied at C, directed away from the line AD.

**(a)** Find P. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude and direction of the force exerted on the lamina by the axis. **[4]**

**8.** A uniform rectangular lamina ABCD has AB = 0.7 m and AD = 0.4 m. It stands with AB on a rough plane that is slowly tilted, with coefficient of friction 0.45.

**(a)** Find the angle of the plane at which the lamina would be about to topple, if it did not slip. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the lamina slips before it topples. **[2]**

**9.** A uniform beam AB has length 5 m and mass 30 kg. It rests on supports at C and D, where AC = 1 m and AD = 3 m. A 5 kg load is placed at B.

**(a)** Find the reactions at C and D. **[4]**
**(b)** The load at B is replaced by a heavier one. Find the mass at which the beam is about to tilt, and about which support. **[2]**

**10.** A uniform ladder AB has length 4 m and mass 12 kg. Its foot A is on rough level ground, where μ = 0.45, and its top B is against a smooth vertical wall. The ladder is at 55° to the ground. A man of mass 52 kg stands on it 1.5 m from A.

**(a)** Find the reaction at the wall. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the ladder does not slip. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the greatest distance from A that the man can climb before the ladder slips. **[3]**
**(d)** State one modelling assumption you made about the man. **[1]**

**11.** A uniform ladder AB, of weight W, rests with A on rough horizontal ground and B against a rough vertical wall. The coefficient of friction is 0.5 at the ground and 0.25 at the wall. The ladder is about to slip at both ends.

**(a)** Show that the normal reaction at the ground is 8W/9. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the angle the ladder makes with the horizontal. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** moment = 15 × 1.6 × sin 50° [1] = **18.4 N m** [1]
**(b)** moment of the 8 N force = 8 × 0.8 = 6.4 N m [1]
resultant = 18.385 − 6.4 = **12.0 N m**, in the sense of the 15 N force [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* show the perpendicular distance as 1.6 sin 50° and state the sense of the resultant. A bare number leaves the answer incomplete.

**2.** Σm = 10 [1]
Σ(m x) = 3(0) + 2(1.0) + 5(2.4) = 14 [1]
x̄ = 14/10 = **1.4 m from A** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the rod is light, so it adds nothing to either sum.

**3. (a)** Σm = 10 [1]
Σ(m x) = 2 + 15 − 15 = 2, so x̄ = 0.2 [1]
Σ(m y) = 6 − 6 + 20 = 20, so ȳ = 2; the centre of mass is **(0.2, 2)** [1]
**(b)** total mass 20; x: 2 + 10p = 20 × 1 [1], so p = 1.8 [1]
y: 20 + 10q = 20 × 3, so q = 4; coordinates **(1.8, 4)** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) use total mass 20 on the right-hand side.

**4. (a)** whole area 36π, hole area 4π [1]
take O as origin and the hole's side as positive: 36π(0) − 4π(3) = 32π x̄ [1]
x̄ = −12/32 = −3/8 [1]
the centre of mass is **0.375 cm from O**, on the side away from the hole [1]
**(b)** G is on the line through O and the hole, so angle BOG = 90°, and G is vertically below B: tan θ = OG/OB = 0.375/6 [1]
θ = **3.58°** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* show the hole as a negative area, and say which side of O the centre of mass lies on.

**5. (a)** areas: rectangle 24, triangle 9 [1]
heights of centres of mass: rectangle 2; triangle 4 + 3/3 = 5 [1]
ȳ = (24 × 2 + 9 × 5)/33 [1]
= 93/33 = 31/11 = **2.82 cm** [1]
**(b)** M is the midpoint of AB; AG is vertical and triangle AMG is right-angled at M [1]
tan(GAB) = MG/AM = (31/11)/3 = 31/33 [1]
AB makes **43.2°** with the vertical [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* the symmetry axis fixes the horizontal coordinate, so one calculation is enough. Keep 31/11 exact until the last step.

**6.** distance OG = 4(0.9)/(3π) = 1.2/π = 0.382 m, where O is the midpoint of AB [1]
G is on the symmetry axis, perpendicular to AB at O, and vertically below A [1]
in right-angled triangle AOG, tan θ = OG/AO = (1.2/π)/0.9 = 4/(3π) [1]
θ = **23.0°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the radius cancels, so keep r symbolic.

**7. (a)** moments about A; the weight acts at the centre, 0.6 m to the right of A: 6g(0.6) [1]
P acts at C, 0.8 m below A: moment P(0.8) [1]
0.8P = 3.6g, so **P = 44.1 N** [1]
**(b)** horizontal component of the axis force X = P = 44.1 N [1]
vertical component Y = 6g = 58.8 N up [1]
magnitude = √(44.1² + 58.8²) = **73.5 N** [1]
direction: tan⁻¹(58.8/44.1) = **53.1° above the horizontal**, pointing upwards and towards the side of AD [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* moments about A remove the unknown axis force. Name the line your angle is measured from.

**8. (a)** G is 0.2 m above AB and 0.35 m from the lowest edge horizontally [1]
topples when G is above that edge: tan α = 0.35/0.2 = 1.75 [1]
α = **60.3°** [1]
**(b)** slipping occurs when tan α = μ = 0.45, giving α = 24.2° [1]
24.2° < 60.3°, so it **slips first** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that", end with both angles and a sentence saying which is smaller.

**9. (a)** moments about C: 2R_D = 30g(1.5) + 5g(4) [1]
R_D = 32.5g = **318.5 N** [1]
vertically: R_C + R_D = 35g [1]
R_C = 2.5g = **24.5 N** [1]
**(b)** tilting about D means R_C = 0 [1]
moments about D: m g(2) = 30g(0.5), so **m = 7.5 kg** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* say which reaction is zero before writing the equation.

**10. (a)** moments about A: S(4 sin 55°) = 12g(2 cos 55°) + 52g(1.5 cos 55°) [1]
4 sin 55° = 3.277 and the right-hand side is 102g cos 55° = 573.3 [1]
S = 573.3/3.277 = **175 N** (174.98) [1]
**(b)** vertically R = 64g = 627.2 N [1]
friction needed = S = 175.0 N, and μR = 0.45 × 627.2 = 282.2 N; 175.0 < 282.2, so no slip [1]
**(c)** limiting: S = μR = 282.24 N [1]
282.24(4 sin 55°) = 12g(2 cos 55°) + 52g(x cos 55°), which is 924.8 = 134.9 + 292.3x [1]
x = **2.70 m** [1]
**(d)** the man is a **particle**, so his weight acts at one point [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) quote the friction needed and the maximum available, then compare. In (c) keep S unrounded.

**11. (a)** horizontal: S = 0.5R, where S is the wall reaction and R the ground reaction [1]
wall friction 0.25S acts upwards [1]
vertical: R + 0.25S = W [1]
R + 0.125R = W, so R = 8W/9 (and S = 4W/9) [1]
**(b)** moments about A, with ladder length L and angle α: S L sin α + 0.25S L cos α [1]
= W(L/2) cos α [1]
substituting S = 4W/9: (4/9)(tan α + 0.25) = 1/2 [1]
tan α = 9/8 − 1/4 = 7/8 [1]
α = **41.2°** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* draw both friction forces first. At the wall friction acts upwards, and it appears in the vertical equation and in the moments.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the length along a rod where the perpendicular distance is needed.
- Forgetting to make a hole negative, or using lengths rather than areas.
- Stating that the beam tilts without saying which reaction is zero.
- Using F = μR when the ladder is not on the point of slipping.
- Dropping the friction at a rough wall from the vertical resolution.
