## What the specification lists

One statement: evaluate the following influences on accounting and finance: social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological and international.

## Core recall

### The eight influences and their effects

| Influence | Examples | Effect on accounting and finance decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Social | Ethical investing; crowdfunding; changing spending habits | Cost and availability of funds; pattern of sales and cash |
| Legal | Company law, reporting and audit rules, tax law, late payment rules | Compliance cost; reassurance for lenders; penalties if broken |
| Ethical | Honest reporting; fair dealing with suppliers; responsible tax behaviour | Trust and reputation against short-term cost |
| Environmental | Green investment; environmental taxes; lenders' climate risk checks | New spending; lower running costs; cheaper green finance |
| Economic | Interest rates, inflation, recession, unemployment | Cost of borrowing; demand; bad debts; availability of credit |
| Political | Tax rates, grants, subsidies, regulation | Profit after tax; cost of investment |
| Technological | Accounting software, online payments, crowdfunding platforms, analytics, cyber risk | Savings in admin; new funding routes; security costs |
| International | Exchange rates, foreign tax and reporting rules, overseas funds | Import and export profit; exchange rate risk |

### Evaluating: five questions

1. What changes?
2. How big is it (use a figure)?
3. How fast and how lasting?
4. Can the business respond?
5. Who gains and who loses?

### Responses a business can make

| Influence | Possible response |
|---|---|
| Interest rate rise | Repay debt, fix the rate, delay investment |
| Recession | Tighter credit, credit insurance, build cash reserves |
| Tax rise | Use allowances, rely less on retained profit, adjust dividends |
| Exchange rate fall | Fix a rate, change prices, switch supplier |
| Green pressure | Invest for savings, seek green finance |
| Technology | Automate routine accounting; improve security |

### Judging importance

- Large and fast effects matter more than small and slow ones.
- Larger firms and those with reserves can respond better.
- Influences that hit all rivals equally (tax) matter less for competitiveness than ones that hit only some (exchange rates for importers).

## Quick worked reminder

All invented. Interest on a £600,000 variable loan rises from 5% to 7%: £30,000 to £42,000 (+£12,000); interest cover on operating profit of £210,000 falls from 7.0 to 5.0. A $500,000 import falls from £400,000 (at $1.25) to £454,545 (at $1.10), +£54,545. Bad debts on £2,000,000 of credit sales rise from 0.5% to 2%, +£30,000. A tax rise from 20% to 25% on £260,000 cuts profit after tax from £208,000 to £195,000.

## Writing an evaluation: a pattern

1. **Name** the influence and the finance decision it affects (for example, interest rates and the cost of a loan).
2. **Size** it with a figure (extra interest, change in profit after tax, extra cost of imports).
3. **Time**: is the effect quick or slow, short-lived or lasting?
4. **Response**: what can the business do, and what does that cost?
5. **Stakeholders**: who gains and who loses (owners, lenders, customers, employees)?
6. **Judgement**: which influence matters most in this case, and what would change that view?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | The cost of borrowing, as a percentage |
| Exchange rate | The price of one currency in terms of another |
| Recession | A fall in output and spending across the economy |
| Bad debt | A debt that will not be paid |
| Crowdfunding | Raising small sums from many people, usually online |
| Window dressing | Presenting accounts to look better than the true position |
| Corporation tax | A tax on company profit |
| Ethical investing | Choosing investments by social and environmental values |
| Audit | An independent check of the accounts |

## Exam traps

- Do not list all eight; choose the most important for the case.
- Always link the influence to a finance decision.
- Put a figure on the effect.
- Not every influence is a threat.
- Say whether the business can respond, and at what cost.

## Self-test

1. List the eight influences in the specification.
2. Give one economic influence on the cost of finance.
3. A loan of £400,000 rises from 4% to 6.5%. Calculate the extra interest.
4. Operating profit is £160,000. Calculate interest cover at 4% and at 6.5%.
5. Give one ethical influence on finance.
6. A tax rate rises from 20% to 25% on £180,000 profit. Calculate profit after tax before and after.
7. Explain how a recession could affect bad debts.
8. Give one way in which technology has changed raising finance.
9. A business receives $300,000 when £1 = $1.20. How much is that in pounds?
10. How much is it worth if £1 = $1.35?
11. Give one environmental influence on investment.
12. Why might a firm with reserves handle external changes better?

### Answers

1. Social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological, international.
2. Interest rates (or inflation, or the state of the economy).
3. 6.5% × £400,000 = £26,000; 4% × £400,000 = £16,000; extra **£10,000**.
4. At 4%: 160,000 ÷ 16,000 = **10.0**. At 6.5%: 160,000 ÷ 26,000 = **6.2** (6.15).
5. Any one: honest reporting without window dressing; paying suppliers on time; responsible tax behaviour.
6. Before: 80% × £180,000 = **£144,000**. After: 75% × £180,000 = **£135,000**.
7. Customers may be unable to pay, so more debts become bad debts and profit and cash fall.
8. Online platforms and crowdfunding give new routes to raise money.
9. $300,000 ÷ 1.20 = **£250,000**.
10. $300,000 ÷ 1.35 = **£222,222** (£27,778 less).
11. Any one: green investment to save energy; environmental taxes; lenders' climate checks.
12. They can absorb losses, delay investment or borrow, so they are less likely to be forced into poor decisions.
