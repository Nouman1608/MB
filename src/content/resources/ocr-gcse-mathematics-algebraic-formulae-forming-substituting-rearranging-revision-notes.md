| 6.02d | Pythagoras a² + b² = c² | Both tiers |
| 6.02d | sin θ = o/h, cos θ = a/h, tan θ = o/a | Both tiers |

| 6.02e | s = ut + ½at² | Both tiers |

For 6.02e, a is constant acceleration, u initial velocity, v final velocity, s displacement from the position at t = 0, and t time taken. The specification says you do not need to know the definition of each letter, so a question will define them.

## 6.02a Forming formulae

```
Method
1. Give each quantity a letter.
2. Fixed amount: a number on its own.
3. Rate: number × letter.
4. Add the parts. Brackets if a sum is multiplied.
5. Test with one easy value.
```

Reminder: a hire firm charges £18 plus £0.45 per mile. For m miles, C = 18 + 0.45m. Test m = 20: 18 + 9 = 27, which is sensible.

Reminder: a frame is x cm wide and (x + 9) cm long. Its perimeter is P = 2(x + x + 9) = 4x + 18.

## 6.02b Substituting

```
Method
1. Write the formula, then rewrite it with each value in brackets.
2. Brackets, then powers and roots, then × and ÷, then + and −.
3. Algebraic fraction: work out top and bottom separately.
4. Root sign: finish everything under it first.
```

Reminder: 3a² − b with a = −2, b = −5.

```
3(−2)² − (−5) = 3(4) + 5 = 17
```

Distinction: (−2)² = 4, but −2² = −4. Brackets decide.

## 6.02c Changing the subject

```
Method
1. Aim: get the new subject alone.
2. Remove fractions by multiplying both sides.
3. Expand brackets.
4. Collect every term with the new subject on one side.
5. Factorise out the subject (if it appears twice).
6. Divide, or undo the power or root.
```

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| Subject once | Undo operations in reverse order |
| Subject twice | Collect, factorise, divide |
| Power of subject | Isolate the power, then take the root |
| Root of subject | Isolate the root, then square both sides |
| Reciprocal of subject | Combine into one fraction, then invert |

Reminder (power): m = k/n². Then mn² = k, so n² = k/m and n = √(k/m).

Reminder (twice): ab = 3a + 5. Then ab − 3a = 5, a(b − 3) = 5, so a = 5/(b − 3).

Reminder (reciprocal): 1/f = 1/u + 1/v gives v = fu/(u − f).

Reminder (root): x = 3√(y/z) gives y = x²z/9. Square the whole left side, including the 3.

## 6.02d Standard formulae in use

**Circle.** For a circle with diameter 10 cm, C = 10π cm and A = 25π cm². Leave π in the answer when asked for exact form.

**Right-angled triangles.**

```
Find hypotenuse:   add the squares, then √
Find shorter side: subtract the squares, then √
Find a side with an angle:   choose sin, cos or tan from the sides involved
Find an angle:   use the inverse function
```

SOH-CAH-TOA: label the sides first (hypotenuse opposite the right angle, opposite the angle, adjacent to it).

**Higher tier only: quadratic formula.** Write a, b, c with their signs, work out b² − 4ac, then take both ± values. If a negative b is squared, the result is positive.

**Higher tier only: choosing a rule for a non-right-angled triangle.**

| You know | Use |
|---|---|
| A side and its opposite angle, plus one more side or angle | Sine rule |
| Two sides and the angle between them | Cosine rule for the third side |
| All three sides | Cosine rule for an angle |
| Two sides and the angle between them (area) | ½ab sin C |

Reminder: for sides 5 and 8 with 40° between them, a² = 25 + 64 − 80 cos 40° = 27.72, so a = 5.26. The area is ½ × 5 × 8 × sin 40° = 12.9.

If the cosine of an angle is negative, the angle is obtuse.

## 6.02e Kinematics

| Quantity wanted | Quantities given | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| v | u, a, t | v = u + at |
| s | u, a, t | s = ut + ½at² |
| v, u or a | the other two of u, v, a plus s | v² = u² + 2as |

```
Method
1. List u, v, a, s, t. Mark the unknown.
2. Hidden values: "starts from rest" means u = 0; "stops" means v = 0.
3. Choose the formula with exactly one unknown.
4. Keep signs consistent (upwards positive means a = −9.8 for gravity).
```

Reminder: from rest with a = 2.5 for 6 s, v = 0 + 2.5 × 6 = 15 and s = ½ × 2.5 × 36 = 45.

**Higher tier only:** if the unknown is t in s = ut + ½at², you get a quadratic in t. Reject a negative time.

## Checking your answer

Each topic has a quick test that takes under a minute.

- Formed formula: put in a value you can work out in your head.
- Rearranged formula: substitute a simple pair of numbers into both versions and see that they agree. For v = fu/(u − f), try f = 2 and u = 3: 1/2 = 1/3 + 1/v gives 1/v = 1/6, so v = 6, and 2 × 3/(3 − 2) = 6.
- Right-angled triangle: the hypotenuse must be the longest side.
- Cosine rule or sine rule (Higher tier only): the longest side must be opposite the largest angle.
- Kinematics: if a is negative and the object is slowing, v must be smaller than u.
- Units: lengths in cm or m, areas in cm² or m², time in s, velocity in m/s and acceleration in m/s².

## Must-know distinctions

- Expression, formula and equation: an expression has no equals sign, a formula links quantities, an equation is solved for an unknown.
- Substituting (find a value) versus rearranging (change the layout with no numbers).
- Find a hypotenuse (add) versus find a shorter side (subtract).
- Sine rule (pairs of side and opposite angle) versus cosine rule (two sides and the angle between).
- Circumference (length, cm) versus area (cm²).

## Quick self-test

1. A taxi costs £4 plus 90p per kilometre. Write a formula for the cost £C of k km and find the cost for 12 km.
2. Find 3a² − b when a = −2 and b = −5.
3. Make x the subject of y = 6x − 11.
4. Make r the subject of V = ⅓πr²h.
5. Make p the subject of 3p + 2 = q(p − 4).
6. A circle has diameter 10 cm. Give the circumference and area in terms of π.
7. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
8. In a right-angled triangle the adjacent side is 6 cm and the hypotenuse is 10 cm. Find the angle between them to 1 decimal place.
9. Using v = u + at, find a when u = 8, v = 26 and t = 6.
10. A particle has u = 5, a = 2 and s = 18. Use v² = u² + 2as to find v to 2 decimal places.
11. (Higher tier only) Solve x² + 6x + 2 = 0 to 2 decimal places.

### Answers

1. C = 4 + 0.9k, so C = 4 + 10.8 = £14.80.
2. 3(4) + 5 = 17.
3. x = (y + 11)/6.
4. r = √(3V/(πh)).
5. 3p − qp = −4q − 2, so p(3 − q) = −4q − 2 and p = (4q + 2)/(q − 3).
6. C = 10π cm, A = 25π cm².
7. √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17 cm.
8. cos θ = 6/10 = 0.6, so θ = 53.1°.
9. 26 = 8 + 6a, so a = 3.
10. v² = 25 + 72 = 97, so v = 9.85.
11. x = (−6 ± √(36 − 8))/2 = (−6 ± √28)/2 = −0.35 or −5.65.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping brackets when substituting a negative number.
- Squaring only part of an expression when undoing a square root.
- Leaving the new subject on both sides after rearranging.
- Dividing only one term in a numerator.
- Adding instead of subtracting when finding a shorter side.
- Rounding a middle value too early, then losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Giving a cosine rule angle without checking whether it should be obtuse.
- Giving only one quadratic root when two are needed.
- Choosing the wrong kinematics formula because a hidden value such as u = 0 was missed.
- Writing a final answer with no units, or the wrong ones (cm instead of cm²).
