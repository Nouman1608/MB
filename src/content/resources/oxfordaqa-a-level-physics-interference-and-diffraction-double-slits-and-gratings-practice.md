## Questions

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by coherent sources. **[2]**
**(b)** State the path difference, in terms of wavelength, that gives destructive interference between two sources that are in phase. **[1]**

**2.** Two loudspeakers are connected to one signal generator set to 850 Hz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound. **[1]**
**(b)** A microphone is 4.30 m from one loudspeaker and 5.10 m from the other. State and explain whether the sound is loud or quiet there. **[2]**
**(c)** At a second position the distances are 3.00 m and 3.60 m. State and explain whether the sound is loud or quiet. **[2]**

**3.** A laser of wavelength 610 nm illuminates two slits 0.25 mm apart. The screen is 3.0 m from the slits.

**(a)** Calculate the fringe spacing. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance from the central fringe to the fourth bright fringe. **[2]**

**4.** A student uses a laser to demonstrate Young's slits.

**(a)** Give two safety precautions. **[2]**
**(b)** Give two reasons a laser is more suitable than a filament lamp. **[2]**

**5.** A microwave transmitter has frequency 10.7 GHz and is placed in front of a metal plate with two slits.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**
**(b)** A detector is 0.620 m from one slit and 0.676 m from the other. Show that it records a maximum. **[2]**
**(c)** State the smallest path difference that would give a minimum. **[1]**

**6.** In a Young's slits experiment the slit separation is 0.45 mm and the screen is 2.10 m away. The distance across ten fringe spacings is 31.5 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the light. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why ten spacings are measured rather than one. **[1]**
**(c)** The ruler reading is uncertain by ±1 mm. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the ten-spacing distance and in a single spacing, and comment. **[2]**

**7.** Slits 0.60 mm apart are lit with white light. The screen is 1.20 m away. Red light has wavelength 680 nm and blue light 450 nm.

**(a)** Describe the pattern seen. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the fringe spacing for red and for blue. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the separation of the second-order red and blue maxima. **[2]**

**8.** A single narrow slit is placed in a light beam, with a screen behind it.

**(a)** Describe the pattern for monochromatic light. **[2]**
**(b)** State how the width of the central maximum changes when the slit is made narrower. **[1]**
**(c)** State how it changes when red light is replaced with blue light. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe the pattern for white light. **[2]**

**9.** Derive d sin θ = nλ for a plane transmission grating at normal incidence. **[4]**

**10.** Violet light of wavelength 405 nm is incident normally on a grating of 800 lines per mm.

**(a)** Calculate the grating spacing. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the angle of each of the first two maxima either side of the centre. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the highest order and the total number of maxima. **[2]**
**(d)** A screen is 1.50 m from the grating. Calculate the distance of the first order maximum from the centre. **[2]**

**11.** A grating has 3000 lines per cm. Light of unknown wavelength gives a third-order maximum at 31.4°.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[3]**
**(b)** State the highest order observed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the angle of the fourth-order maximum. **[1]**

**12.** White light (400 nm to 700 nm) is incident normally on a grating of 450 lines per mm.

**(a)** Calculate the grating spacing. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the angles of the first-order maxima for 400 nm and 700 nm. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that the third-order violet overlaps the second-order spectrum. **[3]**
**(d)** State one application of a diffraction grating. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Same frequency [1] and a constant phase difference [1].
**(b)** An odd number of half wavelengths, (n + ½)λ [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Same wavelength" alone is incomplete; the answer must say that the phase difference stays constant.

**2. (a)** λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 850 = **0.40 m** [1]
**(b)** Path difference = 5.10 − 4.30 = 0.80 m = 2λ [1]. The waves arrive in phase, so the sound is **loud** [1].
**(c)** Path difference = 0.60 m = 1.5λ [1]. The waves arrive in antiphase, so the sound is **quiet** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A loud or quiet answer gains little without the path difference expressed as a number of wavelengths.

**3. (a)** w = λD ÷ s = 610 × 10⁻⁹ × 3.0 ÷ 0.25 × 10⁻³ [1] = **7.3 × 10⁻³ m** [1]
**(b)** 4w = 4 × 7.32 mm [1] = **29 mm** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded spacing (7.32 mm) into part (b) and round only the final answer.

**4. (a)** Any two from: never look into the beam or at reflections; use a low-power laser; stop the beam with a matt block; keep the beam away from eye level [1] [1].
**(b)** It is monochromatic, so the fringes are distinct and the spacing relates to one wavelength [1]. It is bright, narrow and coherent across its width, so a clear pattern forms without extra slits or filters [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Be careful" is too vague for a safety mark; name the specific action.

**5. (a)** λ = c ÷ f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 10.7 × 10⁹ [1] = **0.0280 m** [1]
**(b)** Path difference = 0.676 − 0.620 = 0.056 m [1] = 2λ, a whole number of wavelengths, so a maximum [1].
**(c)** **λ ÷ 2 = 0.014 m** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer needs the path difference divided by the wavelength, giving the whole number.

**6. (a)** w = 31.5 ÷ 10 = 3.15 mm [1]. λ = ws ÷ D = 3.15 × 10⁻³ × 0.45 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2.10 [1] = **6.75 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]
**(b)** The ruler uncertainty is shared over ten spacings, so the percentage uncertainty is smaller [1].
**(c)** Ten spacings: 1 ÷ 31.5 = **3.2%** [1]. One spacing: 1 ÷ 3.15 = **32%**, ten times larger, so measuring across ten spacings is far better [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Comment questions need a comparison of the two figures, not just two numbers.

**7. (a)** The central fringe is white, because all colours have zero path difference there [1]. On either side the fringes are coloured, with blue nearer the centre and red further out [1].
**(b)** Red: 680 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.20 ÷ 0.60 × 10⁻³ = **1.36 mm** [1]. Blue: 450 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.20 ÷ 0.60 × 10⁻³ = **0.90 mm** [1].
**(c)** Second-order separation = 2 × (1.36 − 0.90) [1] = **0.92 mm** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain why the centre is white (zero path difference for every colour), not just that it is white.

**8. (a)** A bright, wide central maximum [1], with narrower, dimmer maxima either side separated by dark minima [1].
**(b)** It becomes **wider** [1].
**(c)** It becomes **narrower** [1].
**(d)** A white central maximum [1] with coloured side maxima, blue at the inner edge and red at the outer edge [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The direction of the change matters; "changes" earns nothing.

**9.** A plane wave arrives normally, so the light is in phase at every slit [1]. For waves from adjacent slits (spacing d) leaving at angle θ, the path difference is d sin θ [1]. Maxima need this to be a whole number of wavelengths, nλ [1], so that waves from all slits reinforce: **d sin θ = nλ** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw the right-angled triangle with hypotenuse d and mark θ; the sin θ step is where credit is gained.

**10. (a)** d = 1 ÷ 800 000 = **1.25 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1]
**(b)** n = 1: sin θ = 405 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 1.25 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.324 [1], θ = **18.9°** [1]. n = 2: sin θ = 0.648, θ = **40.4°** [1].
**(c)** d ÷ λ = 3.09, so highest order is **3** [1]. Maxima at 0, ±1, ±2, ±3: **7** [1].
**(d)** x = 1.50 × tan 18.9° [1] = **0.51 m** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use sin in the grating equation but tan for position on a screen; mixing them is a frequent error.

**11. (a)** d = 1 ÷ 300 000 = 3.33 × 10⁻⁶ m [1]. λ = d sin θ ÷ n = 3.33 × 10⁻⁶ × sin 31.4° ÷ 3 [1] = **5.79 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]
**(b)** d ÷ λ = 5.76 [1], so the highest order is **5** [1].
**(c)** sin θ = 4 × 579 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 3.33 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.695, θ = **44.0°** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert lines per cm to metres carefully; a factor of 100 slip changes every later answer.

**12. (a)** d = 1 ÷ 450 000 = **2.22 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1]
**(b)** 400 nm: sin θ = 0.18, θ = **10.4°**. 700 nm: sin θ = 0.315, θ = **18.4°** [1] [1]
**(c)** Third-order violet: sin θ = 3 × 400 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 2.22 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.54, θ = 32.7° [1]. Second-order spectrum runs from 21.1° (violet) [1] to 39.1° (red). 32.7° lies within this range, so the orders overlap [1].
**(d)** For example, measuring wavelengths of light from a lamp or star to identify elements [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" overlap answers need the angle ranges of both spectra, not only one value.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating the path difference as a number of wavelengths when explaining loud or quiet regions.
- Leaving out "constant phase difference" in a definition of coherence.
- Using lines per millimetre or per centimetre as d without conversion.
- Dividing by the number of fringes instead of the number of spacings.
- Using tan in the grating equation or sin for the screen position.
- Getting the direction wrong for slit width or wavelength and central maximum width.
- Giving vague laser safety such as "be careful".
- Writing a non-whole highest order, or counting maxima on one side only.
