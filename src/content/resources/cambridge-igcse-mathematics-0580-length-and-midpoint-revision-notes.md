These notes condense subtopic **3.4 Length and midpoint** from Topic 3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The whole subtopic is **Extended only** (C3.4 is "Extended content only"), so it appears on Papers 2 and 4. The two statements are: calculate the length of a line segment, and find the coordinates of the midpoint of a line segment. For full explanations, read the [length and midpoint study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-length-and-midpoint/); for exam-style questions, use the [length and midpoint practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-length-and-midpoint-practice/).

## The two formulas

Neither formula is printed on the paper's formula list.

```
Length   AB = sqrt( (x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2 )     differences, squared, added, rooted
Midpoint M  = ( (x1 + x2)/2 , (y1 + y2)/2 )         sums, halved
```

Memory hook: **length is Pythagoras**, **midpoint is an average**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line segment | The part of a line between two end points |
| Length | Distance from one end point to the other |
| Midpoint | The point halfway along a segment |
| Surd | A root left in exact form, e.g. √50 = 5√2 |
| Exact answer | No rounding: a whole number, fraction or surd |
| Diameter | Chord through the centre; its midpoint is the centre |

## Length: quick examples

```
(0, 0) and (6, 8):     sqrt(36 + 64)  = sqrt(100) = 10
(-2, -1) and (3, 11):  sqrt(25 + 144) = sqrt(169) = 13
(1, -4) and (-3, 2):   sqrt(16 + 36)  = sqrt(52)  = 2 sqrt(13)   (= 7.21 to 3 s.f.)
```

Useful Pythagorean triples: 3, 4, 5; 5, 12, 13; 6, 8, 10; 8, 15, 17. If the horizontal and vertical changes match one, the length is a whole number.

## Midpoint: quick examples

```
(2, 9) and (8, -3):     ((2 + 8)/2, (9 - 3)/2)      = (5, 3)
(-5, -6) and (2, 1):    ((-5 + 2)/2, (-6 + 1)/2)    = (-1.5, -2.5)
```

## Reverse midpoint

Given one end A and the midpoint M, the other end B is one more "step" from M.

```
B = (2 x xM - xA , 2 x yM - yA)

A(3, -2), M(1, 4):   B = (2 - 3, 8 - (-2)) = (-1, 10)
```

## Unknown coordinates

Set up the length equation, square both sides, and expect **two** answers when a square root is taken.

```
Distance from (k, 3) to (2, -1) is 5:
(2 - k)^2 + 16 = 25
(2 - k)^2 = 9
2 - k = 3 or -3   ->   k = -1 or k = 5
```

For a midpoint unknown, set each coordinate of the midpoint equal to the average and solve: midpoint of (p, 6) and (8, q) is (5, 1) gives p = 2 and q = –4.

## Where these appear in longer questions

| Task | Use |
|---|---|
| Is a triangle isosceles? | Compare lengths (or squared lengths) |
| Is it right-angled? | Check a² + b² = c² with squared lengths |
| Centre of a circle from a diameter | Midpoint |
| Radius from centre and a point on the circle | Length |
| Centre of a rectangle or parallelogram | Midpoint of either diagonal |
| Perimeter of a shape on a grid | Add the lengths of the sides |

## Exam traps

- Leaving the answer as the squared length (52 instead of √52).
- Adding coordinates in the length formula, or subtracting in the midpoint formula.
- Forgetting to divide by 2 for the midpoint.
- Typing –4² on the calculator and getting –16. Use brackets: (–4)².
- Giving only one value of an unknown coordinate.
- Taking the midpoint of A and M when asked for the other end B.
- On Paper 2, not simplifying √72 to 6√2 when the simplest surd form is requested.

## Self-test

1. Find the length of the segment from (2, 1) to (8, 9).
2. Find the length from (–3, 4) to (2, –8).
3. Find the midpoint of (–6, 5) and (4, –1).
4. Find the midpoint of (0.5, 3) and (2.5, –4).
5. Find the length from (1, 1) to (4, 4), as a simplified surd.
6. M(2, –3) is the midpoint of AB, and A is (–1, 2). Find B.
7. The midpoint of (a, 7) and (9, b) is (6, 2). Find a and b.
8. The distance from (k, 0) to (0, 8) is 10. Find the possible values of k.
9. A circle has centre (3, –1) and passes through (8, 11). Find its radius.
10. Show that (0, 0), (4, 2) and (1, 3) form an isosceles triangle.
11. Find, to 3 significant figures, the length from (–2.4, 1.7) to (3.1, –2.6).
12. Rectangle PQRS has P(–2, –1) and R(6, 5) as opposite vertices. Find the point where its diagonals cross.

### Answers

1. √(36 + 64) = **10**
2. √(25 + 144) = **13**
3. **(–1, 2)**
4. **(1.5, –0.5)**
5. √18 = **3√2**
6. B = (2 × 2 – (–1), 2 × (–3) – 2) = **(5, –8)**
7. (a + 9)/2 = 6 and (7 + b)/2 = 2: **a = 3, b = –3**
8. k² + 64 = 100, k² = 36: **k = 6 or k = –6**
9. √(25 + 144) = **13**
10. Lengths: √20 from (0, 0) to (4, 2), √10 from (0, 0) to (1, 3), √10 from (4, 2) to (1, 3); **two sides are √10**, so it is isosceles
11. √(5.5² + 4.3²) = √(30.25 + 18.49) = √48.74 = **6.98**
12. Midpoint of PR = **(2, 2)**
