These questions cover sub-topic **2.3 Books of prime entry**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Name the book of prime entry used to record credit purchases of goods for resale. **[1]**

**2.** A customer pays for goods with a debit card. Where is this recorded in the cash book?

A: cash column, debit side

B: bank column, debit side

C: bank column, credit side

D: discount column, debit side **[1]**

**3.** A petty cash float is 250. At the end of the month the tin holds 61.20 and vouchers for the rest. How much is needed to restore the float?

A: 61.20

B: 188.80

C: 250.00

D: 311.20 **[1]**

**4.** State **two** types of transaction recorded in the general journal. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by the dual function of the cash book. **[2]**

**6.** A customer owes 1,240 and pays within the terms, taking 2.5% cash discount. Calculate the discount and the amount received. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Name the book of prime entry in which Fernhill Pottery first records each transaction.

(i) A credit note sent to a customer.

(ii) A cheque received from a customer.

(iii) A credit purchase of clay for making pots for sale.

(iv) A credit purchase of a new kiln.

(v) Bus fares of 4.80 paid by the office junior from the tin.

(vi) An invoice sent to a gift shop.

(vii) Goods returned to the clay supplier.

(viii) The owner pays an extra 5,000 of capital into the bank by transfer. **[8]**

**8.** Fernhill Pottery allows 25% trade discount to shops. In September 2027 it made these credit sales: 3 September, Oak Lane Gifts, list price 1,600; 11 September, C Moyo, a private customer, 480 with no trade discount; 24 September, Oak Lane Gifts, list price 800.

**(a)** Prepare the sales journal for September 2027.

**(b)** State the ledger entries made from the sales journal. **[6]**

**9.** Fernhill Pottery's transactions for October 2027 were:

**(a)** Prepare the three-column cash book for October 2027, balanced at 31 October and with balances brought down.

**(b)** State how the totals of the two discount columns are posted. **[10]**

**10.** Fernhill Pottery runs its petty cash on the imprest system with a float of 180. Vouchers in November 2027 were: 2 Nov, cleaning 22.00; 5 Nov, postage 13.75; 9 Nov, travel 18.40; 14 Nov, stationery 9.60; 20 Nov, postage 21.30; 27 Nov, cleaning 22.00.

**(a)** Prepare the petty cash book for November with analysis columns for cleaning, postage, travel and stationery, showing the restoration of the float on 30 November.

**(b)** Explain the purpose of the imprest system. **[8]**

**11.** Prepare general journal entries, with narratives, for these transactions of Fernhill Pottery.

(i) 4 December: bought a kiln on credit from Heatcraft, 6,200.

(ii) 10 December: the owner took pots costing 140 for her own use.

(iii) 18 December: T Webb's debt of 310 was written off as irrecoverable. **[6]**

**12.** Fernhill Pottery keeps each day's cash takings in a drawer in the workshop and banks them once a week. All its books are kept by hand.

**(a)** Explain **two** limitations of keeping cash at the business property in this way, and suggest **one** improvement.

**(b)** Discuss whether Fernhill Pottery should change to digital methods for the original entry of its transactions. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Purchases journal [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **B** [1]. 250.00 - 61.20 = 188.80. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Any two [2]: opening entries; credit purchase or sale of non-current assets; correction of errors; irrecoverable debts written off; year-end transfers or adjustments; goods taken by the owner. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** It is a book of prime entry, because cash and bank transactions are first recorded in it [1], and it is also the ledger account for cash and bank, so no separate accounts are kept [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Discount 1,240 x 2.5% = **31.00** [1]; amount received **1,209.00** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7.** (i) Sales returns journal [1]; (ii) cash book [1]; (iii) purchases journal [1]; (iv) general journal [1]; (v) petty cash book [1]; (vi) sales journal [1]; (vii) purchases returns journal [1]; (viii) cash book [1]. Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The kiln in (iv) is a non-current asset, so it never goes in the purchases journal, whatever the payment terms.

**12. (a)** Two limitations, each explained [2], for example: a week's takings in an unlocked drawer could be stolen or lost, so the business loses money; the cash could be miscounted or taken by staff, and the records would not agree with the cash held; the money is not in the bank to pay bills. One improvement [1], for example: bank takings daily, use a safe, or encourage card payments.

**(b)** For digital entry, any two developed points [2]: totals and postings are made automatically, saving time; fewer arithmetic errors; reports such as a trial balance are produced quickly; card and bank transactions can be imported. Against, any two developed points [2]: cost of software, equipment and training; reliance on power and internet; risk of hacking or data loss; errors made at input are repeated in every report. A justified conclusion for Fernhill Pottery [1], for example: change, because the time saved and fewer errors outweigh the costs for a growing business with card sales. Total: 8 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting non-current assets bought on credit in the purchases journal.
- Starting an overdraft on the debit side of the cash book.
- Recording card sales in the cash column.
- Reimbursing petty cash with the amount left in the tin.
- Omitting narratives when a question asks for them.
