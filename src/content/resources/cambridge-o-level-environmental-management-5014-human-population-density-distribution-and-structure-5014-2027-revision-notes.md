## Density versus distribution

| | Density | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Number of people living in an area | How people are spread over an area |
| Unit | People per km² | No unit; described as a pattern |
| Formula | Population / area | — |
| Example language | "650 people per km²" | "Clustered along the coast"; "uneven" |

**World pattern**
- Dense: South, East and South-East Asia; much of Europe; river valleys (for example the Nile); coastal
  plains.
- Sparse: hot deserts (for example the Sahara); polar regions; high mountains; dense tropical forest (for
  example the Amazon basin).

**Describing a map**: pattern → named places → values from the key with units → exception.

## Reading a pyramid

- **Axes**: age bands up the middle (youngest at bottom); percentage or number along the bottom; males on
  one side, females on the other.
- **Young dependants**: 0-14.
- **Working population**: 15-64.
- **Elderly dependants**: 65 and over.
- **Female and male**: compare the sides, especially at the top (women usually live longer).

| Shape clue | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wide base | High birth rate |
| Narrow base | Low birth rate |
| Rapidly narrowing sides | High death rate; low life expectancy |
| Wide top | Low death rate; high life expectancy |
| Notch | Past war, epidemic or low births |
| Uneven sides at working ages | Migration of one sex |

## Calculations

- **Density** = population / area (km²).
- **Dependent population (%)** = % aged 0-14 + % aged 65+.
- **Dependency ratio** = dependants / working population x 100.
- **Share of one sex in an age group** = that sex's % / group total x 100.

*Invented example*: 0-14 = 26%, 15-64 = 62%, 65+ = 12%. Dependent = 38%. Dependency ratio =
38 / 62 x 100 = 61.3.

## World Bank income groups

- Classified by **gross national income (GNI) per person**.
- **LICs** (low-income), **MICs** (middle-income, split into lower-middle and upper-middle), **HICs**
  (high-income).
- Thresholds change each year: do not quote dollar figures unless a question gives them.

## LIC versus HIC

| | LIC | HIC |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Wide | Narrow |
| Sides | Concave, steep narrowing | Straighter |
| Top | Narrow | Wide |
| Birth rate | High | Low |
| Life expectancy | Lower | Higher |
| Main dependants | Young | Increasingly elderly |
| Needs | Schools, child health, future jobs | Pensions, elderly healthcare, care |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sparsely populated | Containing few people |
| Densely populated | Containing many people |
| Population density | Number of people living in an area, usually people per km² |
| Population distribution | How people are spread over an area |
| Population structure | Make-up of a population by age and sex |
| Population pyramid | Back-to-back bar chart of age groups by sex |
| Dependent population | Young (under 15) and elderly (65+) people who rely on others |
| Working population | People aged 15-64 |
| Dependency ratio | Dependants per 100 working-age people |
| Life expectancy | Average number of years a person is expected to live |
| GNI per person | Gross national income divided by population |
| LIC / MIC / HIC | Low-, middle-, high-income country |

## Exam traps

- Density without a unit, or in m².
- Describing distribution with a single number.
- Quoting one sex's bar as if it were the age group's total.
- Comparing pyramids in two separate paragraphs; use "whereas" and comparatives.
- Mixing up the dependency ratio (per 100 workers) with the dependent percentage (of the whole
  population).
- Quoting World Bank thresholds from memory; they change.

## Self-test

1. What is the difference between population density and population distribution?
2. Name two sparsely populated types of environment.
3. Calculate the density of a region with 2.4 million people in 16 000 km².
4. Which age groups are dependants?
5. What does a narrow base on a pyramid show?
6. Give one reason why a pyramid may have more women than men at the top.
7. A pyramid shows 0-14 = 35%, 65+ = 5%. Calculate the dependency ratio.
8. What does the World Bank use to classify countries into income groups?
9. Name the three income groups in the syllabus.
10. Give two differences between the pyramid of a typical LIC and a typical HIC.
11. Suggest what a large notch in the 20-24 male bar might show.
12. Why might an HIC with a wide pyramid top need more healthcare spending?

### Answers

1. Density is the number of people in an area (people per km²); distribution is how they are spread over
   the area.
2. Any two: hot deserts; polar regions; high mountains; dense tropical forest.
3. 2 400 000 / 16 000 = 150 people per km².
4. Young (0-14) and elderly (65 and over).
5. A low birth rate.
6. Women usually have a longer life expectancy than men.
7. Working population = 60%; 40 / 60 x 100 = 66.7.
8. Gross national income (GNI) per person.
9. Low-income (LICs), middle-income (MICs) and high-income (HICs) countries.
10. Any two: LIC has a wider base, narrower top, sides narrowing faster, more young dependants; HIC has a
    narrower base, wider top, more elderly dependants.
11. Emigration of young men for work, or deaths in a war.
12. It has many elderly dependants, who need more medical care, while fewer workers pay taxes.
