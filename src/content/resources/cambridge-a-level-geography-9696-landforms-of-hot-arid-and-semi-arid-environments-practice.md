## Section A

**1.** Define the term "deflation". **[2]**

**2.** State two effects of salt weathering on desert landforms. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between a bahada and a pediment. **[2]**

**4.** Outline why desert rivers suffer transmission loss. **[3]**

**5.** Outline the difference between a barchan dune and a star dune. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why wind can move sand but rarely moves pebbles by saltation. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A barchan dune on the fictional Erg Talmout moved 45 m downwind in 3 years. A road lies 120 m downwind of its slip face.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of movement. **[1]**

**(b)** At this rate, calculate how many years it will take to reach the road. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest two reasons why the actual time may differ. **[2]**

**8.** A survey along the fictional Oued Seffra alluvial fan measured mean clast size at three points.

| Position | Distance from apex (km) | Mean clast size (mm) | Gradient (°) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apex | 0 | 210 | 6.0 |
| Mid-fan | 2.5 | 48 | 3.0 |
| Toe | 5.0 | 6 | 1.0 |

**(a)** Describe the changes from apex to toe. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain these changes. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how inselbergs may form. **[5]**

**10.** Explain how yardangs form. **[4]**

**11.** Describe the evidence that many desert landforms were formed under wetter climates in the past. **[6]**

**12.** "Water, not wind, is the main agent shaping landforms in hot arid environments." How far do you agree? **[20]**

## Answers

**1.** The removal of loose, fine particles from the ground surface by wind [1], which lowers the surface and can form deflation hollows [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Honeycomb hollows (tafoni) in rock faces [1]. Crumbling and undercutting at the base of cliffs and slopes, especially near playas [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A bahada is a depositional surface of merged alluvial fans [1], whereas a pediment is an erosional rock surface, thinly covered with debris, at the foot of a mountain front [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Flood water flows over dry, permeable channel beds of sand and gravel [1]. Water soaks into the bed and banks [1], so discharge falls downstream instead of rising, and the flood dies out [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A barchan is a crescent with horns pointing downwind [1], formed by one dominant wind with limited sand, and it migrates [1]. A star dune has radiating arms formed by winds from many directions and grows upward rather than moving far [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Wind has low density, so it can lift only small, light particles [1]. Sand grains are light enough to be lifted briefly and bounce [1], but pebbles are too heavy to be lifted and can only be rolled or pushed by strong winds or impacts [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 45 / 3 = 15 m per year [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 120 / 15 = 8 years [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Wind speed and direction vary from year to year [1]. The dune may grow or shrink as sand supply changes, and larger dunes move more slowly [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Clast size falls from 210 mm at the apex to 6 mm at the toe [1]. Most of the fall occurs in the first 2.5 km [1]. Gradient falls from 6.0° to 1.0° [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** At the apex the channel leaves the mountain front and loses confinement [1], so flow spreads and velocity falls [1]. The largest clasts are dropped first, near the apex [1]. As gradient decreases and water is lost to the fan surface, smaller particles are deposited further out, so the fan is sorted by size [1]. **[4]**

**9.** One explanation is deep chemical weathering under past wetter conditions [1], which was most effective where joints were closely spaced [1]. Later erosion stripped the weathered material, leaving massive, less jointed rock standing as inselbergs [1]. An alternative explanation is long-continued parallel retreat of scarps, leaving isolated remnants [1]. Bornhardts may form in massive rock and kopjes where rock has broken into boulders [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Yardangs form in soft rock or old lake sediments [1]. Abrasion by saltating sand and deflation of loose material [1] cut troughs along lines of weakness parallel to the dominant wind [1]. Ridges between them are streamlined, often with a blunt upwind end and a tapering downwind tail [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Old shorelines and lake sediments in now-dry basins show former lakes [1], such as those of Lake Bonneville in Utah [1]. Dry valley networks are too large to have been cut by present rainfall [1]. Stabilised dunes covered with vegetation and soil show past drier and then wetter phases [1]. Fossil groundwater in aquifers such as the Nubian Sandstone was recharged in wetter times [1]. Rock art in the central Sahara shows water-dependent animals such as giraffes and cattle [1]. **[6]**

**12.** The real exam marks this essay with levels of response, not point by point. The points below are indicative creditworthy content.

*Introduction:* define aeolian and fluvial processes [1] and note that present and past processes must both be considered [1].

*Water as main agent:* wadis and arroyos are cut by flash floods [1]. Alluvial fans, bahadas and playas are fluvial [1]. Pediments are explained by sheet floods or lateral planation [1]. One flash flood can move more sediment than years of wind [1]. Sand seas cover only a minority of most deserts [1].

*Past water:* pluvial lakes, dry valleys and stabilised dunes show wetter conditions [1]. Inselbergs may result from deep weathering in wetter times [1].

*Wind as main agent:* ergs such as the Rub' al Khali are shaped by wind [1]. Yardangs and zeugen are wind-sculptured [1]. Deflation creates hollows and pavements, and exports dust across oceans [1].

*Interaction:* much dune sand began as river or lake sediment [1]. Wind reshapes old lake beds into yardangs [1]. Weathering prepares material for both agents [1].

*Variation:* the driest, sandiest deserts show the greatest wind role [1]; semi-arid areas with more rain show the greatest water role [1].

*Judgement:* water is the main agent in most deserts, especially over long timescales [1]; wind dominates locally where sand is abundant [1]; a reasoned final view on "how far" [1]. **[20]**
