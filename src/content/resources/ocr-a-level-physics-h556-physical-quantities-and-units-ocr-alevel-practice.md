## Section A

**1.** From this list, identify the units that are S.I. base units: newton, kelvin, coulomb, ampere, joule, mole. **[2]**

**2.** Write the base units of:

**(a)** power **[1]**

**(b)** specific heat capacity **[1]**

**(c)** resistivity **[1]**

**3.** Write each quantity in S.I. units, in standard form.

**(a)** 25 pF **[1]**

**(b)** 0.60 cm² **[1]**

**(c)** 1.2 g cm⁻³ **[1]**

**4.** A column in a results table is headed λ / 10⁻⁷ m. One entry is 5.9.

**(a)** Write this wavelength in metres. **[1]**

**(b)** Write it in nanometres. **[1]**

**5.** A student substitutes θ = 20 °C directly into the ideal gas equation pV = nRT. Explain the error and state the value that should be used. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The luminosity L of a star of radius r and surface temperature T is given by L = 4πσr²T⁴. Determine the base units of the constant σ. **[3]**

**7.** The speed v of waves on deep water of wavelength λ is given by v = √(gλ/2π). Show that this equation is homogeneous. **[3]**

**8.** The resistive force F on a small sphere moving slowly through oil at speed v is modelled as F = bv. At higher speeds a second model, F = cv², is used.

**(a)** Find the base units of b. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the base units of c. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why b and c cannot have the same units. **[1]**

**9.** A student writes the energy W transferred in a resistor of resistance R carrying current I for time t as W = I²R/t. Use base units to show that this equation is wrong, and suggest the correct equation. **[3]**

**10.** A bottle holds 500 cm³ of water. The molar mass of water is 0.018 kg mol⁻¹. Estimate the amount of substance of water in the bottle, stating the assumption you make. **[3]**

**11.** Estimate the kinetic energy of a sprinter during a 100 m race. State your assumptions. **[3]**

**12.** A student measures the current I in milliamperes as a capacitor discharges and plans a graph of ln I against time t.

**(a)** Write a suitable heading for the current column of the table. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a suitable label for the vertical axis, using the current in amperes. **[1]**

**(c)** One reading is 25.0 mA. Calculate the value plotted on the vertical axis. **[1]**

**13.** A copper wire has a cross-sectional area of 0.12 mm² and a length of 250 cm. The resistivity of copper is 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m. Calculate the resistance of the wire using R = ρL/A. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Base units: **kelvin, ampere and mole** [2]. One mark for two correct with none wrong. The newton, coulomb and joule are derived. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The coulomb is a common wrong choice; charge is defined from current and time, so the ampere is the base unit.

**2. (a)** P = W/t, so W = J s⁻¹ = **kg m² s⁻³** [1].

**(b)** c = E/(mΔθ): kg m² s⁻² ÷ (kg K) = **m² s⁻² K⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** ρ = RA/L: kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² × m² ÷ m = **kg m³ s⁻³ A⁻²** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer that still contains J or Ω is not in base units.

**3. (a)** 25 × 10⁻¹² = **2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ F** [1].

**(b)** 0.60 × (10⁻²)² = **6.0 × 10⁻⁵ m²** [1].

**(c)** 1.2 × 10⁻³ kg ÷ 10⁻⁶ m³ = **1.2 × 10³ kg m⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The prefix on a squared or cubed unit must be squared or cubed too.

**4. (a)** λ / 10⁻⁷ m = 5.9, so λ = **5.9 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1].

**(b)** 5.9 × 10⁻⁷ m = **590 nm** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The ideal gas equation needs the absolute (thermodynamic) temperature in kelvin, the S.I. base unit [1]. T = 20 + 273 = **293 K** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** σ = L / (4πr²T⁴) [1]. L is a power, kg m² s⁻³, and r²T⁴ has units m² K⁴ [1]. σ has units kg m² s⁻³ ÷ (m² K⁴) = **kg s⁻³ K⁻⁴** (4π has no unit) [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Make the constant the subject before substituting; collecting powers is then straightforward.

**7.** Left side: v has units **m s⁻¹** [1]. Right side: gλ has units m s⁻² × m = m² s⁻²; 2π has no unit [1]. √(m² s⁻²) = **m s⁻¹**, so both sides have the same base units and the equation is homogeneous [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** b = F/v = kg m s⁻² ÷ m s⁻¹ [1] = **kg s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** c = F/v² = kg m s⁻² ÷ m² s⁻² = **kg m⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** v and v² have different units, so the constants must have different units for each product to be a force [1]. **[4]**

**9.** W = I²R/t has units A² × kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² ÷ s = kg m² s⁻⁴ [1]. Energy has units kg m² s⁻², so the equation is not homogeneous and must be wrong [1]. The correct equation is **W = I²Rt**, which has units kg m² s⁻³ × s = kg m² s⁻² [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The unit check suggests the correction, but the reasoning still needs the result kg m² s⁻² stated explicitly.

**10.** Assume the density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³, so 500 cm³ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ has mass 0.50 kg [1]. Amount = mass ÷ molar mass = 0.50 ÷ 0.018 [1] = 27.8, so about **30 mol** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An estimate to one or two significant figures is expected; quoting 27.78 mol suggests a precision the assumptions cannot support.

**11.** Assume mass about 70 kg [1] and top speed about 10 m s⁻¹ [1]. Ek = ½mv² = 0.5 × 70 × 10² = 3500 J, about **4 × 10³ J** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Accept any sensible range (roughly 2 × 10³ to 5 × 10³ J) when the assumed values are stated and the equation is correct.

**12. (a)** **I / mA** [1].

**(b)** **ln (I / A)** [1].

**(c)** ln (0.0250) = **−3.69** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A logarithm can only be taken of a pure number, so the unit goes inside the bracket, divided into the quantity.

**13.** A = 0.12 × 10⁻⁶ = 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ m² and L = 2.50 m [1]. R = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ × 2.50 ÷ 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = **0.35 Ω** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Converting mm² with 10⁻³ instead of 10⁻⁶ gives a resistance a thousand times too small.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving N, J, V or Ω in an answer that asks for base units.
- Applying the prefix factor once to a squared or cubed unit.
- Using °C where an equation needs kelvin.
- Estimates with no assumptions, no unit or too many significant figures.
- Writing a unit after a logarithm on a graph axis.
- Concluding that a homogeneous equation must be correct.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many marks does a homogeneity check usually need?**
Work the left and right sides separately, show each simplification and finish with a sentence of conclusion. Each step you write is something an examiner can credit, so do not jump straight to the final units.

**Can an equation with a logarithm or a sine be checked with units?**
Yes, partly. The argument of ln, lg, sin or cos must have no unit, so the check tests whatever sits inside the function, for example that ωt is a pure number.

**Should I use 273 or 273.15 to convert to kelvin?**
Either is acceptable unless a question specifies; 273 is the value most often used at A Level.
