Condensed notes for sub-topic **3.2 Corrections of errors**. A full worked example with five errors, a suspense account and a corrected profit is in the [3.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-correction-of-errors/); test yourself with the [3.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-correction-of-errors-practice/).

## What 3.2 asks for

- Correcting errors using journal entries.
- Using a suspense account as a temporary measure to balance the trial balance.
- Correcting errors using a suspense account.
- Adjusting profit or loss after correcting errors.
- The effect of corrections on the statement of financial position.

## The three-step method

1. Write what **was** done.
2. Write what **should have been** done.
3. The correcting journal is the **difference**.

**Example:** a payment of 250 for repairs was debited to motor vehicles. Done: Dr Motor vehicles 250, Cr Bank 250. Should be: Dr Repairs 250, Cr Bank 250. Correction: Dr Repairs 250, Cr Motor vehicles 250.

## Does it need suspense?

| Error | Trial balance affected? | Suspense in correction? |
|---|---|---|
| Commission, principle, omission, original entry, complete reversal, compensating | No | No |
| One side of an entry missing | Yes | Yes |
| Different amounts on debit and credit | Yes | Yes |
| Both entries on the same side | Yes | Yes |
| Casting (addition) error in one account or journal total | Yes | Yes |
| Balance omitted from, or misplaced in, the trial balance | Yes | Yes |

**Suspense rules**

- The trial balance difference goes on the side of the **smaller** total.
- After all corrections the suspense account must have **no balance**.
- The suspense account is never in the statement of profit or loss.

## Amounts to watch

| Situation | Correction amount |
|---|---|
| Complete reversal | Twice the amount |
| Entry made on the wrong side of one account only | Twice the amount (through suspense) |
| Wrong figure in one account only | The difference between the two figures (through suspense) |
| Original entry error | The difference, between the two accounts used (no suspense) |

**Example:** a credit sale of 690 was debited to the customer and debited to sales. Correction: Cr Sales 1,380, Dr Suspense 1,380.

## Adjusting profit

Start with the draft profit, then for each correction look at the account that is **not** suspense:

| Correction | Effect on profit |
|---|---|
| Income account credited (increased) | Add |
| Income account debited (reduced) | Less |
| Expense account debited (increased) | Less |
| Expense account credited (reduced) | Add |
| Only asset, liability or personal accounts involved | No effect |
| Expense moved to a non-current asset | Add (less any depreciation required) |

**Statement layout:** draft profit; each adjustment with a label and add or less; corrected profit.

## Effect on the statement of financial position

- Check every account in each journal.
- Asset or liability accounts corrected change that section directly.
- Every change in profit changes owner's equity by the same amount.
- A suspense balance in a draft statement disappears.

**Example:** goods bought on credit for 520 were not recorded. Journal: Dr Purchases 520, Cr Supplier 520. Profit falls by 520, trade payables rise by 520, and capital falls by 520.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Correcting journal | A general journal entry that puts an error right |
| Suspense account | A temporary account holding the trial balance difference until errors are found |
| Narrative | An explanation written below a journal entry |
| Draft profit | Profit calculated before errors were corrected |
| Overcast | Added up too high |
| Undercast | Added up too low |
| Corrected profit | Draft profit adjusted for the effects of the corrections |

## Exam traps

- Errors of the six "trial balance agrees" types **never** use suspense.
- Reversal and wrong-side errors need **double** amounts.
- Do not adjust profit for the suspense entry itself.
- An error of principle that moves cost into a non-current asset **raises** profit; one that moves an asset into expenses lowers it.
- When a question says "ignore depreciation", do not charge any; when it gives a policy, apply it to the corrected asset.

## Self-test

**1.** Why are errors corrected with journal entries rather than by crossing out?

**2.** Trial balance totals: debit 44,300, credit 44,900. On which side is the suspense balance and how much is it?

**3.** Correct: a credit sale of 380 to H Ford was debited to H Forde.

**4.** Correct: purchases journal undercast by 200.

**5.** Correct: cash drawings of 150 debited to wages.

**6.** Correct: rent received of 700 was credited to bank and debited to rent received.

**7.** Correct: insurance paid of 640 was entered in the insurance account as 460; the cash book was correct.

**8.** What is the effect on profit of question 5?

**9.** What is the effect on profit of question 7?

**10.** Draft profit 12,800. Corrections: sales increased by 300; wages reduced by 150; discount received reduced by 60. Calculate corrected profit.

**11.** A computer of 900 was debited to purchases. State the effect of correcting it on non-current assets and profit, ignoring depreciation.

**12.** What should the suspense account show after all corrections?

## Self-test answers

**1.** So there is a record of each change and the reason for it, and so the double entry is preserved.

**2.** Debit side, 600 (the debit total is smaller).

**3.** Dr H Ford 380, Cr H Forde 380.

**4.** Dr Purchases 200, Cr Suspense 200.

**5.** Dr Drawings 150, Cr Wages 150.

**6.** Dr Bank 1,400, Cr Rent received 1,400.

**7.** Dr Insurance 180, Cr Suspense 180.

**8.** Profit increases by 150.

**9.** Profit decreases by 180.

**10.** 12,800 + 300 + 150 - 60 = 13,190.

**11.** Non-current assets increase by 900; profit increases by 900.

**12.** No balance.
