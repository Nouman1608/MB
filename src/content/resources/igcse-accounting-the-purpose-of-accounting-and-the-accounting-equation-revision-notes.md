## 1.1 Book-keeping versus accounting

| | Book-keeping | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Recording transactions | Using the records to summarise, analyse and communicate |
| Question it answers | What happened? | What does it mean and what should we do? |
| Typical outputs | Entries in the books, ledger accounts | Statement of profit or loss, statement of financial position, comments, recommendations |
| Judgement needed | Little: follow set rules | A lot: interpret, compare, advise |

One-line test: if the task is writing an entry down, it is book-keeping; if it is summarising, interpreting or advising, it is accounting.

## 1.1 Measuring profit and loss

**Profit = income earned in the period − expenses of earning it.** A negative result is a **loss**. The figure belongs to a stated period and appears in the statement of profit or loss.

Purposes to be able to name and apply:

- Judge whether the business is succeeding and whether the owner's effort is rewarded.
- Decide how much the owner can take as drawings without weakening the business.
- Compare with earlier periods, with plans, or with similar businesses.
- Give lenders and tax authorities a recognised measure.
- Support decisions: pricing, cost control, expansion, closure.

**Percentage change** = change ÷ original × 100. Profit falling from 9,000 to 6,750 is a fall of 2,250, which is 2,250 ÷ 9,000 = 25.0% down.

## 1.1 Information for monitoring and decisions

| Role | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring progress | Comparing results with a past period or a target | Revenue up 12% but profit flat, so expenses are rising |
| Decision-making | Choosing between options using the facts | Do not buy a second vehicle while the bank loan is still large and profit is falling |

Accounting information supports the decision. It does not make the decision, and it cannot show things that are not measured in money.

## 1.2 The three elements

| Element | Meaning | Examples | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset | Resource the business owns | Premises, equipment, inventory, trade receivables, bank, cash | Owned or owed to us |
| Liability | Amount the business owes outsiders | Trade payables, loan, bank overdraft | We owe it |
| Owner's equity | Owner's claim = assets − liabilities | Capital paid in + profit − drawings | What is left for the owner |

**Owner's equity goes up with:** capital introduced, profit.
**Owner's equity goes down with:** loss, drawings.

## 1.2 The accounting equation

**Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity**

| To find | Rearrange to |
|---|---|
| Owner's equity | Assets − Liabilities |
| Liabilities | Assets − Owner's equity |
| Assets | Liabilities + Owner's equity |

Method for a missing figure: write the equation, put in the two known figures, solve for the third, then check by substituting back.

## 1.2 Effect of common transactions

| Transaction | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner pays in capital | Up | No change | Up |
| Buy equipment for cash or bank | One up, one down (total same) | No change | No change |
| Buy equipment on credit | Up | Up | No change |
| Take out a loan (cash received) | Up | Up | No change |
| Repay part of a loan | Down | Down | No change |
| Sell goods above cost, cash or credit | Up by the profit | No change | Up by the profit |
| Pay an expense | Down | No change | Down |
| Owner takes drawings | Down | No change | Down |
| Customer pays what is owed | One up, one down (total same) | No change | No change |
| Pay a supplier | Down | Down | No change |

Only profit or loss, capital introduced and drawings move owner's equity. Everything else leaves it alone.

## Worked reminder: following the equation

Fernandez Tile Supplies starts with assets of 60,000, liabilities of 22,000 and so owner's equity of 38,000 (all in dollars).

| Step | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening position | 60,000 | 22,000 | 38,000 |
| Buys a van for 12,000 on credit | 72,000 | 34,000 | 38,000 |
| Owner takes 1,500 cash as drawings | 70,500 | 34,000 | 36,500 |
| Repays 5,000 of a loan from the bank | 65,500 | 29,000 | 36,500 |
| Sells goods that cost 800 for 1,250 on credit | 65,950 | 29,000 | 36,950 |
| Owner pays 6,000 into the bank | 71,950 | 29,000 | 42,950 |

Check the last line: 29,000 + 42,950 = 71,950, so the equation balances. The sale adds 450 to both assets and owner's equity, because 1,250 − 800 = 450.

## Profit from the movement in owner's equity

**Profit = closing equity − opening equity − capital introduced + drawings**

Opening equity 31,200, closing equity 38,650, capital introduced 2,500, drawings 7,200:
profit = 38,650 − 31,200 − 2,500 + 7,200 = **12,150**.

## Distinctions to keep clear

- **Profit versus cash.** Profit is a measure of performance over a period. Cash is one of the assets.
- **Drawings versus expenses.** An expense is a cost of running the business. Drawings are the owner taking value out.
- **Receivables versus payables.** Receivables are owed to the business (asset). Payables are owed by it (liability).
- **Loan versus capital.** A loan is owed to an outsider. Capital belongs to the owner.
- **Overdraft versus bank balance.** An overdrawn account is a liability.

## Quick self-test

1. State the accounting equation.
2. Assets are 83,600 and liabilities are 29,400. Find owner's equity.
3. Liabilities are 15,250 and owner's equity is 40,750. Find assets.
4. Assets are 27,800 and owner's equity is 18,300. Find liabilities.
5. Classify each as asset, liability or owner's equity: trade receivables, bank overdraft, capital paid in by the owner.
6. A machine costing 7,400 is bought on credit. State the effect on assets, liabilities and owner's equity.
7. The owner takes 1,500 from the bank for personal use. State the effect on each element.
8. Goods that cost 650 are sold on credit for 940. By how much do assets and owner's equity change?
9. Opening equity 31,200, closing equity 38,650, capital introduced 2,500, drawings 7,200. Find profit.
10. Give two purposes of measuring profit.
11. Distinguish book-keeping from accounting in one sentence each.
12. Profit falls from 9,000 to 6,750. State the percentage change.

### Answers

1. Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity.
2. 83,600 − 29,400 = 54,200.
3. 15,250 + 40,750 = 56,000.
4. 27,800 − 18,300 = 9,500.
5. Trade receivables: asset. Bank overdraft: liability. Capital paid in: owner's equity.
6. Assets up 7,400, liabilities up 7,400, owner's equity no change.
7. Assets down 1,500, owner's equity down 1,500, liabilities no change.
8. Both increase by 290, because 940 − 650 = 290.
9. 38,650 − 31,200 − 2,500 + 7,200 = 12,150.
10. Any two from: judge performance, guide drawings, compare over time, inform lenders or tax calculations, support decisions.
11. Book-keeping records transactions. Accounting summarises, analyses and communicates what the records show.
12. A fall of 25.0% (2,250 ÷ 9,000 × 100).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining accounting as "keeping the books" with no mention of analysis or communication.
- Giving a purpose of profit that is only a definition, such as "to find the profit".
- Answers that say "to see if the business is doing well" without saying how it is then used.
- Putting drawings in the expenses when finding profit.
- Treating a bank overdraft or trade payables as assets.
- Saying a loan raises owner's equity.
- Showing only one side of a transaction and forgetting the second change.
- Working out a percentage change against the new figure instead of the original.
- Writing figures without units or a stated period where the question asks for them.
