These questions practise the three Breadth Study question types of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE
History 4HI1 Paper 2, Section B: explaining two differences between two points in time (6), explaining
two causes (8) and a 16-mark essay on change across at least 25 years, guided by two bullet points. No
sources are used in this section.

## Set 1

**1(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the power of the federal government in 1789 was different from
its power under the Articles of Confederation in 1786. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** causes of the secession of the Southern states in 1860–61. **[8]**

**1(c)** "The problem of slavery was the main reason for divisions within the United States in the years
1820–61." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Missouri Compromise (1820)
- the Kansas–Nebraska Act (1854)

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 2

**2(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the size of the United States in 1850 was different from its size
in 1800. **[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** causes of the Union's victory in the Civil War. **[8]**

**2(c)** "Government policy brought about more change for Native Americans than for African Americans in
the years 1830–77." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Indian Removal Act (1830)
- the 14th and 15th Amendments

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

- **Taxation and finance:** under the Articles Congress could not tax and relied on requests to states;
  the Constitution gave Congress power to levy taxes.
- **Executive and enforcement:** in 1786 there was no president and the government could not raise an army
  to deal with Shays' Rebellion; in 1789 an elected president led an executive able to enforce federal law.

About 3 marks per developed difference with precise detail.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Lincoln's election (1860):** Southern states saw the Republican victory, without a single Southern
  electoral vote, as a threat to the expansion and future of slavery.
- **Fear for slavery after years of conflict:** Bleeding Kansas and John Brown's raid (1859) convinced many
  Southerners that the North intended to destroy slavery; states' rights arguments justified leaving.

Credit also: economic differences between the cotton South and industrial North; South Carolina's lead
in December 1860.

### 1(c) [16]

**Agree:** each major crisis — Missouri (1820), the Compromise of 1850 and the Fugitive Slave Act,
Kansas–Nebraska (1854) and Bleeding Kansas, Dred Scott (1857), John Brown (1859) — turned on slavery's
expansion; the new Republican Party was built on opposing it.
**Other factors:** states' rights (rooted in Jefferson's ideas and nullification arguments); economic
differences and tariffs; westward expansion, which kept reopening the question; political breakdown,
with the collapse of the Whig Party.
**Judgement:** most divisions were connected to slavery, because expansion and states' rights mattered
mainly in relation to it. Top answers cover the whole period and show how the problem intensified.

### 2(a) [6]

- **Territory:** in 1800 the USA ended at the Mississippi; by 1850 it included the Louisiana Purchase,
  Florida, Texas, Oregon and the lands taken from Mexico, reaching the Pacific.
- **Population and states:** by 1850 settlement and the Gold Rush had pushed people west, with new states
  such as Texas (1845) and California (1850).

### 2(b) [8]

- **Economic and military resources:** greater population, industry and railways let the Union supply
  larger armies, while the naval blockade cut Southern trade.
- **Military and political leadership:** Grant and Sherman pursued total war (Vicksburg, the March through
  Georgia), and Lincoln's leadership and the Emancipation Proclamation kept Europe out and added black
  soldiers to the Union armies.

### 2(c) [16]

**For Native Americans:** the Indian Removal Act and the Trail of Tears forced eastern nations west;
expansion after 1845 and the Gold Rush brought settlers onto their lands; Grant's Peace Policy and the
reservation system; after Little Bighorn (1876) the Sioux lost the Black Hills. Policy transformed their
lives, mostly for the worse.
**For African Americans:** emancipation (1863) and the 13th Amendment (1865) ended slavery; the Freedmen's
Bureau, the Civil Rights Acts of 1866 and 1875, the 14th and 15th Amendments gave citizenship and votes.
**Limits:** Black Codes, the Klan and the end of Reconstruction in 1877 reversed many gains.
**Judgement:** both experienced profound change; for African Americans legal change was greater, while for
Native Americans the loss of land was more complete. Reward a reasoned comparison over the full period.

## Set 3 — extra practice

**3(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the South in 1860 was different from the South in 1866. **[6]**

**3(b)** Explain **two** causes of the Missouri Compromise (1820). **[8]**

### 3(a) [6]

- **Slavery:** in 1860 about four million people were enslaved; by 1866 the 13th Amendment had freed them,
  though Black Codes tried to keep them as cheap, controlled labour.
- **Economy and politics:** in 1860 the cotton economy was prosperous and Southern states controlled their
  own affairs; by 1866 railways, farms and cities such as Atlanta lay in ruins and the states' return to
  the Union was being decided by Washington.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Balance in the Senate:** admitting Missouri as a slave state would have given slave states a majority
  in the Senate, so Maine was admitted as a free state to keep the balance.
- **Westward expansion:** the Louisiana Purchase had created vast territories, and Congress needed a rule
  for slavery in them — the 36°30' line.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), describing one date only instead of comparing two.
- In (b), listing causes without explaining how each led to the outcome.
- In (c), covering only one key topic; the question spans at least 25 years.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the Paper 2 breadth study question structured?**
Part (a) asks you to explain two ways something was different at two dates (6 marks), part (b) to explain two causes (8 marks) and part (c) is a 16-mark essay on change, continuity or significance across the period.

**How did federal power change between 1786 and 1789?**
Under the Articles of Confederation Congress could not tax and relied on requests to the states; the Constitution gave Congress the power to levy taxes.

**What is a common mistake in part (a)?**
Describing one date only instead of comparing the two dates in the question.
