## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost | Cost that does not change with output in the short run (rent, insurance) |
| Variable cost | Cost that rises and falls with output (materials, energy, hourly wages) |
| Short run | Period in which at least one factor of production is fixed |
| Long run | Period in which all factors can be varied, so all costs are variable |
| Total cost (TC) | Fixed cost + variable cost |
| Average cost (AC) | TC ÷ output; cost per unit |
| Average fixed cost (AFC) | Fixed cost ÷ output; always falls as output rises |
| Average variable cost (AVC) | Variable cost ÷ output |
| Total revenue (TR) | Price × quantity sold |
| Average revenue (AR) | TR ÷ quantity = price; the AR curve is the demand curve |
| Marginal revenue (MR) | Change in TR from selling one more unit |
| Profit | TR − TC; a loss if negative |

## Formula sheet

| Find | Use |
|---|---|
| TC | FC + VC, or AC × Q |
| AC | TC ÷ Q, or AFC + AVC |
| TR | P × Q, or AR × Q |
| MR per unit | Change in TR ÷ change in Q |
| Profit | TR − TC, or (AR − AC) × Q |

## Worked reminders

**Costs.** A print shop pays £500 a day in fixed costs and £2 per job in variable costs.

| Jobs | TC (£) | AC (£) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 600 | 12.00 |
| 100 | 700 | 7.00 |
| 250 | 1,000 | 4.00 |

AC falls because fixed cost is spread over more jobs.

**Revenue.** A bakery sells 300 loaves at £2.50 (TR £750). Cutting the price to £2.25 raises sales to 360 (TR £810). Price falls 10% and quantity rises 20%, so PED = **−2.0** and TR rises by £60.

**Profit per unit.** At an output of 400, AR is £15 and AC is £12, so profit = (15 − 12) × 400 = **£1,200**.

## PED and total revenue

| Demand | Price rise | Price cut |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic | TR falls | TR rises |
| Unitary | TR unchanged | TR unchanged |
| Inelastic | TR rises | TR falls |

TR is at its maximum where demand is unit elastic, which is where MR = 0.

## Objectives and output

| Objective | Output chosen | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| Profit maximisation | Where TR − TC is greatest | Owner-managed firm, shareholders |
| Revenue maximisation | Where TR is greatest (MR = 0) | Managers paid on sales |
| Covering costs | Largest output with TR at least equal to TC | Social enterprise, community scheme |

Rule of thumb: revenue maximisation gives a higher output and lower price than profit maximisation, because the extra units still add revenue but add more to cost.

## Shapes to recall

- AFC falls continuously, since a fixed sum is divided by ever more units.
- With constant AVC, AC falls towards AVC but never reaches it.
- In most firms AVC eventually rises as the fixed factor becomes crowded, so short-run AC is U-shaped.
- The AR curve slopes down for a firm that must cut price to sell more; MR lies below it.

## Exam traps

- A large cost is not necessarily fixed: ask whether it varies with output.
- There are no fixed costs in the long run.
- Revenue up does not mean profit up.
- AR equals price; TR is the rectangle under the price on a demand diagram.
- A price cut lowers the price on every unit, so MR is below price.
- Give units for every money figure and state "per unit" for averages.

## Self-test

1. Classify as fixed or variable: rent, flour for a bakery, insurance, electricity for ovens.
2. Define the short run.
3. A firm has fixed costs of £2,000 and variable costs of £5 per unit. Find TC and AC at 400 units.
4. In question 3, find AFC at 400 units and at 800 units.
5. A firm sells 1,000 units at £8. Find TR and AR.
6. A price cut from £8 to £7 raises sales from 1,000 to 1,300. Calculate PED and the change in TR.
7. TR rises from £5,000 to £5,400 when output rises from 500 to 600. Find MR per unit.
8. At an output of 200, AR is £20 and AC is £23. Calculate profit or loss.
9. Why is MR less than price when a firm must cut its price to sell more?
10. Why might a firm aiming for revenue produce more than one aiming for profit?
11. At what value of PED is TR at its maximum?
12. Why does AFC always fall as output rises?

### Answers

1. Rent: fixed. Flour: variable. Insurance: fixed. Electricity for ovens: variable.
2. The period in which at least one factor of production, usually capital, cannot be changed.
3. TC = 2,000 + 5 × 400 = **£4,000**; AC = 4,000 ÷ 400 = **£10**.
4. AFC = 2,000 ÷ 400 = **£5**; at 800 units **£2.50**.
5. TR = **£8,000**; AR = **£8**.
6. Price −12.5%, quantity +30%, so PED = **−2.4** (elastic). TR rises from £8,000 to £9,100, up **£1,100**.
7. MR = 400 ÷ 100 = **£4** per unit.
8. (20 − 23) × 200 = **−£600**, a loss.
9. To sell an extra unit the lower price applies to all units, so the revenue lost on earlier units partly offsets the price of the extra one.
10. Beyond the profit-maximising output, extra units still add to revenue (MR is positive) but add more to cost, so a revenue maximiser keeps expanding while a profit maximiser stops.
11. At unitary elasticity (PED = −1), where MR = 0.
12. The same fixed sum is divided by a larger number of units.
