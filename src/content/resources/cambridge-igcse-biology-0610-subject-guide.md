Cambridge IGCSE Biology (0610) is a broad introduction to the life
sciences, usually taken at around the end of lower secondary school. It
covers living organisms from their classification and cells through
nutrition, transport, coordination and reproduction to inheritance,
ecosystems and biotechnology. The syllabus edition covered here is for
examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Every candidate sits three papers on
either the Core or the Extended route: a multiple-choice paper worth 30%,
a theory paper worth 50%, and one practical component worth 20%.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has 21 numbered topics. They are not split into stages, but
they fall into clear groups that make planning easier.

**Foundations (Topics 1 to 5).** Characteristics and classification of
living organisms, Organisation of the organism, Movement into and out of
cells (diffusion, osmosis and active transport), Biological molecules and
Enzymes. These topics supply the vocabulary and cell-level ideas that the
rest of the course uses.

**Nutrition, transport and gas exchange (Topics 6 to 11).** Plant
nutrition and Human nutrition, then Transport in plants (xylem and
phloem, transpiration and translocation) and Transport in animals (heart,
blood vessels and blood), followed by Diseases and immunity and Gas
exchange in humans.

**Energy release, excretion and control (Topics 12 to 15).** Respiration
(aerobic and anaerobic), Excretion in humans, Coordination and response
(including sense organs, hormones, homeostasis and tropic responses) and
Drugs.

**Reproduction, inheritance and variation (Topics 16 to 18).**
Reproduction in plants and humans, Inheritance (chromosomes, genes and
proteins, mitosis, meiosis and monohybrid inheritance) and Variation and
selection.

**Ecology and applied biology (Topics 19 to 21).** Organisms and their
environment, Human influences on ecosystems, and Biotechnology and
genetic modification.

## How it's assessed

0610 offers two routes. Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3; Extended
candidates take Papers 2 and 4. Candidates on either route also take one
practical component, either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

| Paper | Title | Route | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice (Core) | Core | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 3 | Theory (Core) | Core | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Multiple Choice (Extended) | Extended | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 4 | Theory (Extended) | Extended | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 5 | Practical Test | Both | 75 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Paper 6 | Alternative to Practical | Both | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

Paper 5 is a practical test and Paper 6 is a written alternative; only
one of the two is taken, and the same pair serves both routes. The
assessment is component-based, so all three of your papers are sat in the
same examination series. Your school chooses the route and the practical
option, so ask which papers you are entered for before you start past
paper work.

## How the parts fit together

Several ideas recur across the 21 topics, and seeing them early saves
time. Diffusion and osmosis from Topic 3 reappear in gas exchange, water
uptake in plants, absorption in the gut and excretion. Enzymes from Topic
5 return in chemical digestion, respiration and biotechnology. Cell
division from the Inheritance topic underpins both asexual and sexual
reproduction. Energy flow and nutrient cycles in Topic 19 connect back to
photosynthesis and respiration.

Because the theory paper can draw on any topic, a question about
transpiration might also need leaf structure from Plant nutrition, and a
question on homeostasis might rely on what you know about hormones and
the blood. The practical component works the same way: whether you sit
Paper 5 or Paper 6, the experimental contexts come from the content you
study, so practical skills are best built alongside the topics rather
than saved for the end.

## Planning your study

Working through the topics roughly in syllabus order suits most
students, since the early topics feed almost everything later. A general
plan might look like this:

- **Early in the course:** Topics 1 to 5, with plenty of simple practical
  work on diffusion, osmosis, food tests and enzyme activity.
- **Middle of the first year:** Topics 6 to 11, linking nutrition,
  transport and gas exchange as one connected story.
- **End of the first year into the second:** Topics 12 to 18, from
  respiration and coordination to reproduction and inheritance.
- **Later in the second year:** Topics 19 to 21, then whole-course
  revision.

If you are on the Extended route, make sure your notes cover the
additional depth your teacher sets for each topic, not just the Core
material. Begin using past-paper questions topic by topic as soon as a
group is finished; keep full timed papers and paper-specific technique
for the revision period, using the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between Core and Extended in IGCSE Biology 0610?**
Core candidates take Paper 1 (Multiple Choice, Core) and Paper 3 (Theory, Core). Extended candidates take Paper 2 (Multiple Choice, Extended) and Paper 4 (Theory, Extended). Both routes add one practical component.

**Do I take both Paper 5 and Paper 6?**
No. Paper 5 (Practical Test) and Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical) are alternatives, and you take only one. Each is worth 20% and carries 40 marks.

**How long are the IGCSE Biology papers?**
The multiple-choice papers last 45 minutes, the theory papers 75 minutes, Paper 5 lasts 75 minutes and Paper 6 lasts 60 minutes.

**How many topics are in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610?**
There are 21 topics, from Characteristics and classification of living organisms to Biotechnology and genetic modification.

**Which years does this syllabus cover?**
This 0610 syllabus is for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028.
