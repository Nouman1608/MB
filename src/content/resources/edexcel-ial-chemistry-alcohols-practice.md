## Questions

**1.** Name each alcohol and state whether it is primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[2]**
**(b)** (CH₃CH₂)₃COH **[2]**
**(c)** HOCH₂C(CH₃)₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[2]**

**2.** Pentan-1-ol, C₅H₁₁OH, burns completely in oxygen.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of oxygen needed to burn 2.64 g of pentan-1-ol completely. (Mr: C₅H₁₁OH 88.0, O₂ 32.0) **[3]**

**3.** Phosphorus(V) chloride is added to a dry sample of butan-2-ol.

**(a)** State what you would see, and give a test that confirms the gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a positive result does not prove that the sample is an alcohol. **[1]**

**4.** 1-iodobutane is made from butan-1-ol.

**(a)** Give the reagents and condition. **[1]**
**(b)** Write two equations to show how the iodoalkane forms. **[2]**
**(c)** 5.55 g of butan-1-ol gives 9.20 g of 1-iodobutane. Calculate the percentage yield. (Mr: C₄H₉OH 74.0, C₄H₉I 183.9) **[3]**

**5.** 2-bromopropane is made by warming propan-2-ol with potassium bromide and 50% concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write two equations for the reactions in the flask. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the sulfuric acid is diluted to 50%. **[2]**
**(c)** The overall equation is C₃H₇OH + KBr + H₂SO₄ → C₃H₇Br + KHSO₄ + H₂O. Calculate the atom economy by mass for 2-bromopropane. (Mr: C₃H₇Br 122.9, KHSO₄ 136.2, H₂O 18.0) **[2]**

**6.** Alcohols are dehydrated by heating with concentrated phosphoric acid.

**(a)** Name the two alkenes formed from 2-methylhexan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why neither alkene shows E-Z isomerism. **[2]**
**(c)** 1.50 g of propan-2-ol (Mr 60.0) gives 444 cm³ of propene, measured at room temperature and pressure. Calculate the percentage yield. Use a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹. **[3]**

**7.** Classify each reaction as substitution, elimination or oxidation.

**(a)** Ethanol with PCl₅ **[1]**
**(b)** Propan-1-ol heated with concentrated phosphoric acid **[1]**
**(c)** Butan-2-ol heated with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) **[1]**

**8.** Three alcohols, A, B and C, are isomers of C₅H₁₂O. Each is warmed with potassium dichromate(VI) in dilute sulfuric acid.

- A: the mixture stays orange.
- B (unbranched chain): the mixture turns green; the product, distilled as it forms, gives a red precipitate with Benedict's solution.
- C (unbranched chain): the mixture turns green; the product does not react with Benedict's solution or with sodium hydrogencarbonate. C has a symmetrical structure.

**(a)** Identify A and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify B. Write equations, using [O], for its oxidation with distillation and under reflux. **[3]**
**(c)** Identify C and name its oxidation product. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe a test, with the result, that shows the reflux product of B is a carboxylic acid. **[2]**

**9.** A student makes crude 1-bromopentane by heating pentan-1-ol with potassium bromide and 50% concentrated sulfuric acid, then purifies it. 1-bromopentane is denser than water and does not mix with it.

**(a)** Explain why the mixture is heated under reflux. **[2]**
**(b)** Next, the student washes the crude liquid with aqueous sodium hydrogencarbonate. State what this wash removes, and why gas pressure builds up during it. **[2]**
**(c)** State which layer contains the 1-bromopentane. **[1]**
**(d)** Name a drying agent and state how you know the liquid is dry. **[2]**
**(e)** Use this value: 1-bromopentane boils at 130 °C. The student's final product boils between 124 °C and 133 °C. Comment on this. **[1]**

**10.** Core Practical 7. A student makes propanal from 4.50 cm³ of propan-1-ol (density 0.80 g cm⁻³) using acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Use these boiling temperatures: propanal 49 °C, propan-1-ol 97 °C, propanoic acid 141 °C.

**(a)** Explain why the oxidising agent is added slowly to an excess of the alcohol. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the receiving flask is cooled in ice. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain, in terms of intermolecular forces, why propanal distils over first. **[3]**
**(d)** The student collects 1.74 g of propanal. Calculate the percentage yield. (Mr: propan-1-ol 60.0, propanal 58.0) **[3]**
**(e)** Give a test, with the result, to show the distillate contains an aldehyde. **[1]**

**11.** You are given four unlabelled liquids: propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol and propanoic acid. Describe a sequence of tests to identify each one. Give reagents and observations. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **hexan-2-ol** [1]; **secondary** [1]
**(b)** **3-ethylpentan-3-ol** [1]; **tertiary**, the C–OH carbon is bonded to three carbons [1]
**(c)** **2,2-dimethylpentan-1-ol** [1]; **primary** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the longest chain has five carbons, not three; a name built on propan- gains no credit.

**2. (a)** **2C₅H₁₁OH + 15O₂ → 10CO₂ + 12H₂O** [1]
**(b)** n(pentan-1-ol) = 2.64 ÷ 88.0 = 0.0300 mol [1]; n(O₂) = 7.5 × 0.0300 = 0.225 mol [1]; mass = 0.225 × 32.0 = **7.20 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The 15 : 2 ratio from the equation must be used; an answer that uses 1 : 1 cannot gain the ratio mark.

**3. (a)** **Steamy fumes** [1]; they turn damp blue litmus red, or form white smoke with ammonia [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + PCl₅ → CH₃CHClCH₂CH₃ + POCl₃ + HCl** [1]
**(c)** Any compound with an –OH group, such as a carboxylic acid (or water), also gives HCl [1]
*Examiner insight:* "A gas is given off" is too vague for (a); say "steamy fumes" and give a test for HCl.

**4. (a)** **Red phosphorus and iodine, warmed** [1]
**(b)** 2P + 3I₂ → 2PI₃ [1]; 3C₄H₉OH + PI₃ → 3C₄H₉I + H₃PO₃ [1]
**(c)** n(butan-1-ol) = 5.55 ÷ 74.0 = 0.0750 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.0750 × 183.9 = 13.8 g [1]; yield = 9.20 ÷ 13.8 × 100 = **66.7%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing 9.20 by 5.55 compares masses of different substances and earns no credit; work through moles.

**5. (a)** KBr + H₂SO₄ → KHSO₄ + HBr [1]; CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃ + H₂O [1]
**(b)** Fully concentrated sulfuric acid oxidises HBr to bromine [1]; so less HBr is available and the yield of bromoalkane falls [1]
**(c)** 122.9 ÷ (122.9 + 136.2 + 18.0) × 100 [1] = 122.9 ÷ 277.1 × 100 = **44.4%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Atom economy uses the molar masses of all products in the equation; leaving out KHSO₄ gives 87.2%, which is wrong for this overall equation.

**6. (a)** **2-methylhex-1-ene** [1]; **2-methylhex-2-ene** [1]
**(b)** In 2-methylhex-1-ene, C1 carries two hydrogen atoms [1]; in 2-methylhex-2-ene, C2 carries two methyl groups; E-Z isomerism needs two different groups on each C=C carbon [1]
**(c)** n(propan-2-ol) = 1.50 ÷ 60.0 = 0.0250 mol [1]; theoretical volume = 0.0250 × 24.0 = 0.600 dm³ = 600 cm³ [1]; yield = 444 ÷ 600 × 100 = **74.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), convert to the same unit before dividing: 444 cm³ against 600 cm³.

**7. (a)** **substitution** [1] **(b)** **elimination** [1] **(c)** **oxidation** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answer (b) with the specification's term, elimination, rather than only "dehydration".

**8. (a)** **2-methylbutan-2-ol** [1]; it is the only tertiary isomer, and tertiary alcohols are not oxidised, so the dichromate stays orange [1]
**(b)** **Pentan-1-ol** [1]; CH₃(CH₂)₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃(CH₂)₃CHO + H₂O [1]; CH₃(CH₂)₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃(CH₂)₃COOH + H₂O [1]
**(c)** **Pentan-3-ol** [1]; product **pentan-3-one** [1]
**(d)** Add sodium hydrogencarbonate (or sodium carbonate) [1]; effervescence, and the gas turns limewater milky [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the reflux equation needs 2[O]; writing [O] with a carboxylic acid product leaves the equation unbalanced.

**9. (a)** The reaction is slow, so it needs heating for a long time [1]; the condenser returns volatile reactants and product to the flask so none is lost [1]
**(b)** It neutralises and removes acid left in the crude product [1]; carbon dioxide forms, so pressure must be released [1]
**(c)** **The lower layer** [1]
**(d)** Anhydrous magnesium sulfate (or calcium chloride or sodium sulfate) [1]; the liquid turns from cloudy to clear, with some solid still loose [1]
**(e)** It boils over a wide range, so it is impure [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (a), give both ideas: prolonged heating, and no loss of volatile substances.

**10. (a)** With the alcohol in excess, there is too little oxidising agent to take propanal further [1]; slow addition lets propanal distil off as it forms, before it is oxidised to propanoic acid [1]
**(b)** Propanal is very volatile, so cooling stops it evaporating from the receiver [1]
**(c)** Propan-1-ol and propanoic acid molecules have O–H groups and form hydrogen bonds with each other [1]; propanal has no O–H, so only weaker London forces and permanent dipole forces act between its molecules [1]; less energy is needed to separate propanal molecules, so it has the lowest boiling temperature [1]
**(d)** mass = 4.50 × 0.80 = 3.60 g; n = 3.60 ÷ 60.0 = 0.0600 mol [1]; theoretical mass of propanal = 0.0600 × 58.0 = 3.48 g [1]; yield = 1.74 ÷ 3.48 × 100 = **50.0%** [1]
**(e)** Warm with Benedict's or Fehling's solution: blue to **red precipitate** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "propanal has weaker bonds" is unlikely to score; name hydrogen bonding between alcohol molecules and its absence in propanal.

**11.** Add sodium hydrogencarbonate to each [1]; only propanoic acid fizzes (CO₂) [1]; warm the other three with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]; 2-methylpropan-2-ol stays orange, the other two turn green [1]; distil the products and warm each with Benedict's solution [1]; the product from propan-1-ol (propanal) gives a red precipitate, the product from propan-2-ol (propanone) does not [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each test needs a reagent and an observation for each liquid; naming a reagent alone identifies nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by branching elsewhere in the molecule instead of by the C–OH carbon.
- Writing a colour change for a tertiary alcohol with dichromate(VI).
- Using [O] instead of 2[O] for the carboxylic acid.
- Leaving POCl₃ or HCl out of the PCl₅ equation.
- Naming one alkene when two non-equivalent neighbouring carbons can each lose a hydrogen.
- Skipping the density step when a liquid volume is given.
- Using the wrong test: Benedict's is for aldehydes, sodium hydrogencarbonate for acids.
