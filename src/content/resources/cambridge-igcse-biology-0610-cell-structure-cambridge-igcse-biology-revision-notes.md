## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partially permeable | Lets some substances through but not others |
| Cellulose | The carbohydrate that makes plant cell walls |
| Cell sap | The solution of sugars and salts in a plant cell's vacuole |
| Chlorophyll | The green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light |
| Circular DNA | The loop of genetic material in a bacterial cell |
| Plasmid | A small ring of DNA in a bacterial cell |
| Specialised cell | A cell with a structure suited to a specific function |
| Micrograph | A photograph taken through a microscope |

## Which cell has what

| Structure | Plant | Animal | Bacterial |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Yes (cellulose) | No | Yes (not cellulose) |
| Cell membrane | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Nucleus | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| Cytoplasm | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Chloroplasts | In green parts | No | No |
| Ribosomes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Mitochondria | Yes | Yes | Not in the syllabus list |
| Large permanent vacuole | Yes | No (small, temporary if any) | No |
| Circular DNA | No | No | Yes |
| Plasmids | No | No | Yes |

**Plant only:** cell wall, chloroplasts, large permanent vacuole. **Bacteria only:** circular DNA, plasmids, no nucleus.

## Functions in one line

- **Cell wall:** strengthens, keeps shape, stops bursting.
- **Cell membrane:** controls what enters and leaves.
- **Nucleus:** holds genetic material that controls the cell.
- **Cytoplasm:** site of most chemical reactions.
- **Ribosomes:** protein synthesis.
- **Mitochondria:** aerobic respiration releases energy.
- **Chloroplasts:** absorb light for photosynthesis.
- **Vacuole:** stores cell sap; pressure supports the cell.
- **Circular DNA:** controls the bacterial cell.
- **Plasmids:** carry extra genes.

## Answer frame: compare two cells

- **Similarities first:** "Both have a cell membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm, ribosomes and mitochondria."
- **Then differences, both cells in each sentence:** "The plant cell has a cellulose cell wall, but the animal cell does not."
- **Bacterial comparisons:** "The bacterial cell has circular DNA free in the cytoplasm, whereas the plant cell's DNA is inside a nucleus."
- Use "but", "whereas" or "however" so the examiner sees a comparison, not two separate lists.

## Spotting structures in images

- Thick, regular outer line → cell wall.
- Dark, round, stained → nucleus.
- Green ovals round the edge → chloroplasts.
- Large clear centre → vacuole.
- Ribosomes and mitochondria → seen clearly only in electron micrographs.
- Small cell, no nucleus, small loops → bacterium with plasmids.

## New cells

New cells are produced by **division of existing cells**: for growth, repair and replacement, and for reproduction in bacteria.

## The six specialised cells

| Cell | Function |
|---|---|
| Ciliated cell | Movement of mucus in the trachea and bronchi |
| Root hair cell | Absorption |
| Palisade mesophyll cell | Photosynthesis |
| Neurone | Conduction of electrical impulses |
| Red blood cell | Transport of oxygen |
| Sperm and egg cells (gametes) | Reproduction |

**Useful links to structure:** root hair → large surface area; palisade → many chloroplasts; red blood cell → haemoglobin, no nucleus; sperm → tail and mitochondria; egg → food store; ciliated cell → cilia beat.

## Levels of organisation

**Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.**

| Level | Key words for the definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue | Group of cells, **similar** structures, **shared function** | Palisade mesophyll |
| Organ | Group of **tissues**, specific functions | Heart, leaf |
| Organ system | Group of **organs**, related functions | Digestive system |

## Exam traps

- Animal cells have **no cell wall**.
- Root cells have **no chloroplasts**.
- The membrane **controls** movement; the wall gives **strength**.
- Mitochondria **release** energy by respiration; they do not "make energy".
- Bacteria have **no nucleus**; their wall is **not cellulose**.
- Use the syllabus wording for specialised cells.
- A tissue is made of **similar** cells; an organ of several **tissues**.

## Quick self-test

1. Name three structures found in plant cells but not animal cells.
2. State the function of ribosomes.
3. State the function of the cell membrane.
4. Name the genetic material of a bacterial cell, and state where it is found.
5. What is a plasmid?
6. What is the bacterial cell wall not made of?
7. State the function of a root hair cell.
8. State the function of a ciliated cell, using the syllabus wording.
9. How are new cells produced?
10. Define a tissue.
11. Is the stomach a tissue, an organ or an organ system?
12. Why might a plant root cell have no chloroplasts?

### Answers

1. Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
2. Protein synthesis (making proteins).
3. It controls what enters and leaves the cell.
4. Circular DNA, free in the cytoplasm.
5. A small ring of DNA in a bacterial cell.
6. Cellulose.
7. Absorption (of water and mineral ions).
8. Movement of mucus in the trachea and bronchi.
9. By division of existing cells.
10. A group of cells with similar structures working together to perform a shared function.
11. An organ.
12. Roots are underground and receive no light, so they do not photosynthesise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do animal cells have vacuoles?**
Some have small, temporary vacuoles, but only plant cells have a large, permanent vacuole filled with cell sap.

**Which specialised cells must I know?**
Ciliated cells, root hair cells, palisade mesophyll cells, neurones, red blood cells, and sperm and egg cells.
