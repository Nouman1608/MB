Learn the content first with the [fertilisers study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-fertilisers/) and the [fertilisers revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-fertilisers-revision-notes/).

Use Ar values: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, P = 31, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39.

## Section A

**1.** State the **three** elements provided by an NPK fertiliser. **[1]**

**2.** Which compound is **not** a nitrogen fertiliser?

A: ammonium nitrate

B: potassium nitrate

C: ammonium sulfate

D: potassium chloride

**[1]**

**3.** State the name of the ion with the formula NH₄⁺ and the name of the ion with the formula NO₃⁻. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why ammonium salts and nitrates can be taken up by the roots of plants. **[2]**

**5.** Name the elements supplied by each compound.

**(a)** KNO₃ **[1]**

**(b)** (NH₄)₃PO₄ **[1]**

**6.** Complete the word equation.

ammonia + sulfuric acid → ............ **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, is a widely used fertiliser.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass, Mr, of ammonium nitrate. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the balanced symbol equation for the reaction of ammonia with nitric acid to form ammonium nitrate. **[1]**

**8.** A farmer compares two fertilisers: ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, and ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in each. **[4]**

**(b)** Both fertilisers cost the same per kilogram. State which gives more nitrogen for the money. **[1]**

**9.** A field needs 21 kg of nitrogen. Calculate the mass of ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, that supplies this nitrogen. **[2]**

**10.** A farmer spreads calcium hydroxide on a field and adds ammonium sulfate fertiliser at the same time.

**(a)** Name the gas given off. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this wastes the fertiliser. **[2]**

**(c)** Complete the balanced equation: Ca(OH)₂ + (NH₄)₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + ......H₂O + ......NH₃ **[1]**

**11.** A student has three unlabelled fertiliser solutions, R, S and T. Each contains one of these compounds: ammonium chloride, potassium nitrate, potassium chloride.

| Test | R | S | T |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm with aqueous sodium hydroxide | ammonia given off | no gas | no gas |
| Warm with aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil | ammonia given off | ammonia given off | no gas |
| Flame test | no lilac colour | lilac | lilac |

**(a)** Identify R, S and T. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how the student showed that the gas given off was ammonia. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why R gives ammonia with aluminium foil even though it contains no nitrate ions. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how NPK fertilisers improve crop yields, and explain why their use can harm life in rivers. **[4]**

**13.** A farmer wants an NPK fertiliser and can choose from ammonium nitrate, potassium nitrate, ammonium phosphate and ammonium sulfate. Suggest the **smallest** number of these compounds that could be mixed to supply N, P and K, and name them. Explain your answer. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (all three for the mark) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** D [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* potassium chloride supplies potassium, not nitrogen; check every formula for N.

**3.** Ammonium [1]; nitrate [1]. **[2]**

**4.** They are soluble in water [1], so they dissolve in soil water and their ions can be absorbed through the roots [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Potassium and nitrogen [1]

**(b)** Nitrogen and phosphorus [1]

**[2]**

**6.** Ammonium sulfate [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80 [1]

**(b)** 2 × 14 = 28 [1]; 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35% [1]

**(c)** NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* using only one nitrogen atom gives 17.5%, the most common wrong answer.

**8. (a)** (NH₄)₂SO₄: Mr = 132 [1]; % N = 28 ÷ 132 × 100 = 21.2% [1]. NH₄Cl: Mr = 53.5 [1]; % N = 14 ÷ 53.5 × 100 = 26.2% [1].

**(b)** Ammonium chloride [1].

**[5]**

**9.** 80 kg of NH₄NO₃ contains 28 kg of N [1]; mass needed = 21 × 80 ÷ 28 = 60 kg [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Ammonia [1]

**(b)** Calcium hydroxide is a base which reacts with the ammonium salt [1]; the nitrogen escapes into the air as ammonia gas, so it is not available to the plants [1].

**(c)** 2 and 2 [1]

**[4]**

**11. (a)** R: ammonium chloride [1]; S: potassium nitrate [1]; T: potassium chloride [1].

**(b)** Damp red litmus paper held in the gas turned blue [1].

**(c)** Ammonium ions react with sodium hydroxide on warming to give ammonia whether or not aluminium is present [1].

**[5]**

**12.** Any four [1] each:

- NPK fertilisers supply nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium
- these elements are needed for plant growth / replace elements removed when crops are harvested
- the compounds are soluble, so rain washes excess into rivers
- nitrates and phosphates lead to deoxygenation of the water
- which damages or kills aquatic life

**[4]**

**13.** Two compounds [1]: potassium nitrate and ammonium phosphate [1]. Potassium nitrate supplies K and N, and ammonium phosphate supplies N and P, so together they provide all three elements; no single compound in the list contains both P and K [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting one nitrogen atom in NH₄NO₃, or not expanding the bracket in (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- Writing "ammonia" when the answer is the ammonium ion.
- Saying that ammonium salts release ammonia with acids rather than bases.
- Mixing up the symbols P and K.
- Writing a full account of eutrophication instead of deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life.
