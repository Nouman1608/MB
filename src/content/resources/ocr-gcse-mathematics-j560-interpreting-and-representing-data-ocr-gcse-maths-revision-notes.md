## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical data | Data in named groups, not numbers | Favourite fruit |
| Discrete data | Numerical data that can take only certain values | Number of siblings |
| Continuous data | Numerical data that can take any value in a range | Height, time |
| Frequency table | Table of each value or class with how often it occurs | Tally and frequency columns |
| Vertical line chart | Thin vertical lines at each discrete value, height = frequency | Goals per match |
| Pictogram | Chart using symbols, with a key for the value of one symbol | One symbol = 6 cups |
| Composite bar chart | Each bar split into stacked sections | Lunch choice by year group |
| Multiple bar chart | Bars for different groups side by side | Boys and girls for each sport |
| Two-way table | Table classifying data by two features, with totals | Year group by travel method |
| Time series | Values of one quantity recorded at equal time intervals | Quarterly sales |
| Trend | The general direction of a time series over time | Rising, falling, steady |
| Seasonal variation | A pattern that repeats over a fixed period | Peak every summer |
| Cumulative frequency | Running total of frequencies (Higher) | 6, 21, 49, 68, 80 |
| Frequency density | Frequency ÷ class width; the height of a histogram bar (Higher) | 36 ÷ 10 = 3.6 |

## Which chart?

| Data | Use |
|---|---|
| Categorical | Bar chart (gaps between bars), pie chart, pictogram |
| Ungrouped discrete | Vertical line chart, or bar chart |
| Over time | Line graph with time on the horizontal axis |
| Grouped continuous (Higher) | Histogram; cumulative frequency graph for running totals |

## Charts for both tiers: the checklist

**Pie charts.**

```
degrees per item = 360 ÷ total
angle            = frequency × degrees per item
frequency        = angle ÷ 360 × total
```

Angles must add to 360°. A pie chart shows **proportions**: two charts with the same angle can stand for different numbers if their totals differ.

**Pictograms.** Number of symbols = frequency ÷ value of one symbol. Read part symbols as fractions of the key.

**Composite bars.** Section value = top edge − bottom edge. The top of the whole bar is the total.

**Multiple bars.** Read each bar from the axis. Good for comparing one category across groups.

**Two-way tables.**

1. Fill any cell where a row or column has only one missing value.
2. Use the grand total to fill missing totals.
3. Check that every row and column adds up.

**Designing a table.** Categories must not overlap, must cover every possible answer, and need an "other" class where answers cannot all be predicted. Include tally and frequency columns.

**Time series.**

- Seasonal variation: the same high and low points recur each cycle (each year, each week).
- Trend: compare each season with the **same season** in the cycle before.
- Predictions assume the pattern continues; say so.

## Higher tier only: grouped data

**Cumulative frequency graphs.**

1. Add a running total column.
2. Plot each running total at the **upper class boundary**.
3. Start at (lowest boundary, 0).
4. Join with a smooth curve (or straight lines if told to).
5. "More than x": total − reading at x. "Less than x": reading at x.

**Histograms.**

```
frequency density = frequency ÷ class width
frequency         = frequency density × class width   (bar area)
```

- Bars touch; widths match the class widths exactly.
- The tallest bar is not always the largest frequency.
- For part of a class, assume values are spread evenly: use the fraction of the width.
- With no scale on the vertical axis, use a bar with a known frequency to find what one unit of area is worth.

## Exam traps

- **Composite sections:** subtract the two edges; do not read the top value.
- **Pie chart comparisons:** same angle does not mean same frequency.
- **Gaps:** bar charts for categories have gaps; histograms do not.
- **Time series trend:** compare like seasons, not neighbouring points.
- **Overlapping classes** such as "10–20, 20–30" in a designed table.
- **Cumulative frequency plotted at midpoints** instead of upper boundaries.
- **Histogram bar height read as frequency.**
- **"More than" from a cumulative frequency graph** read directly, without subtracting from the total.

## Quick self-test

1. Which diagram suits the number of pets owned by each of 30 households?
2. 45 people choose a drink: tea 18, coffee 20, juice 7. Find the pie chart angles.
3. A pie chart represents 600 people. One sector is 48°. How many people does it represent?
4. In a pictogram, one symbol represents 8 books. How many books do 3¾ symbols represent?
5. A section of a composite bar runs from 35 to 62. What frequency does it show?
6. Pie chart A (90 people) and pie chart B (210 people) each have a 120° sector for football. How many chose football in each?
7. Of 100 adults, 45 are men. 38 adults wear glasses, of whom 30 are men. How many women do not wear glasses?
8. A café's quarterly sales (hundreds of pounds) are 12, 18, 25, 15 in Year 1 and 14, 21, 28, 17 in Year 2. Describe the seasonal variation and the trend.
9. A survey offers the categories "0–5 hours", "5–10 hours", "10–15 hours". Give two faults.
10. (Higher) The cumulative frequency up to 20 is 18. The class 20<x≤30 has frequency 12. Which point is plotted next?
11. (Higher) The class 15<x≤35 has frequency 50. Find its frequency density.
12. (Higher) A histogram bar over 40<x≤55 has frequency density 1.6. Find its frequency.

### Answers

1. **A vertical line chart** (or a bar chart): the data are ungrouped and discrete.
2. 360 ÷ 45 = 8° each: **tea 144°, coffee 160°, juice 56°** (total 360°).
3. 48 ÷ 360 × 600 = **80 people**.
4. 3.75 × 8 = **30 books**.
5. 62 − 35 = **27**.
6. A: 90 ÷ 3 = **30**; B: 210 ÷ 3 = **70**.
7. Women = 55; women with glasses = 38 − 30 = 8; women without = 55 − 8 = **47**.
8. Seasonal: sales are **highest in the third quarter** and lowest in the first each year. Trend: each quarter is higher than a year earlier, so the trend is **upward**.
9. The classes **overlap** (5 and 10 each fit two classes), and there is **no class above 15 hours**.
10. **(30, 30)**: plotted at the upper boundary, with 18 + 12 = 30.
11. 50 ÷ 20 = **2.5**.
12. 1.6 × 15 = **24**.
