## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between primary and secondary data, and between quantitative and qualitative data. **[4]**

**2.** Define validity, reliability and representativeness. **[3]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of using questionnaires in sociological research. **[8]**

**4.** Explain the difference between participant and non-participant observation, and between covert and overt research, giving one ethical problem with covert research. **[6]**

**5.** Explain the difference between primary and secondary socialisation, naming the main agency of each and giving **three** agencies of secondary socialisation. **[7]**

**6.** Explain **three** sociological explanations of social class inequality. **[9]**

---

## Answers

**1. Primary data** is **collected first-hand by the researcher** for the purpose of the study; **secondary data** already exists, having been collected by someone else [1] [1]. **Quantitative data** is **numerical and can be counted and analysed statistically**; **qualitative data** is **descriptive, expressed in words, capturing meanings and feelings** [1] [1].

**2. Validity** — whether the data gives a **true and authentic picture of what it claims to measure** [1]. **Reliability** — whether the method would **produce the same results if repeated** by another researcher [1]. **Representativeness** — whether the sample **accurately reflects the wider population**, so findings can be generalised [1].

**3. Advantages:** they can be distributed to a **large, geographically spread sample cheaply and quickly**, so results are more likely to be representative and generalisable [1] [1]. They are **standardised, so they are reliable and easy to replicate**, and closed questions produce quantitative data that is quick to analyse and compare [1] [1].
**Disadvantages:** **response rates are often very low**, and those who reply may differ systematically from those who do not, undermining representativeness [1] [1]. They **lack depth and validity** — respondents cannot explain their answers, may misunderstand a question with nobody to clarify it, and may give socially desirable answers rather than truthful ones [1] [1].

**4.** In **participant observation** the researcher **joins in the group's activities**; in **non-participant observation** they **watch without taking part** [1] [1]. **Overt** research means the group **knows they are being studied**; **covert** means the researcher's identity and purpose are **concealed** [1] [1].
Ethical problem with covert research: participants **cannot give informed consent** [1], and the researcher must deceive them, which may also require observing or even participating in illegal or harmful behaviour without intervening [1].

**5. Primary socialisation** takes place in **early childhood**, where the child learns basic norms, values and language; the main agency is the **family** [1] [1]. **Secondary socialisation** takes place **later, beyond the family**, teaching the norms of wider society [1] [1].
Agencies: the **education system**, through both the formal curriculum and the hidden curriculum of punctuality and obedience [1]; **peer groups**, which exert pressure to conform and confer status [1]; the **media**, which presents role models and shapes expectations; **religion** and the **workplace** [1].

**6.** Any three, 3 marks each: **Functionalist (Davis and Moore)** — inequality is **functional and inevitable**: the most important roles require the greatest talent and longest training, so **higher rewards are needed as an incentive** to attract the most able [1] [1]; society is thus a meritocracy in which position reflects ability and effort [1].
**Marxist** — inequality arises from the **ownership of the means of production**; the bourgeoisie **exploit the proletariat by extracting surplus value** from their labour [1] [1]; ideology transmitted through education, religion and the media produces **false consciousness**, so the working class accept a system that disadvantages them [1].
**Weberian** — class position is determined not only by property but by **market situation — the skills and qualifications a person brings to the labour market** [1]; Weber added **status and party** as separate dimensions of stratification, so inequality is **multi-dimensional rather than a simple two-class model** [1] [1].
**Feminist** — inequality is structured by **patriarchy as well as class**; women are concentrated in low-paid, part-time work and carry the "dual burden" of paid work and domestic labour [1] [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing reliability with validity.
- Giving questionnaire advantages without linking them to representativeness or reliability.
- Naming the family as an agency of secondary socialisation.
- Describing theories without explaining the mechanism of inequality.
