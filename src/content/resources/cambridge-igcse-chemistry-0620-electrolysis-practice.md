## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. The electrolyte is the molten or aqueous substance that undergoes electrolysis.

**2.** **B** [1]. Sodium is more reactive than hydrogen, so hydrogen forms.

**3.** **Platinum** [1]; **carbon / graphite** [1].

**4.** Cathode: **calcium** [1]; anode: **chlorine** [1].

**5.** **Metals or hydrogen** form at the cathode [1]; **non-metals (other than hydrogen)** form at the anode [1].

**6.**

**(a)** Solid lead(II) bromide is ionic, but the **ions are held in fixed positions** in the lattice [1]; when it melts the **ions become free to move** and carry the current [1].

**(b)** Cathode: **silvery-grey liquid metal** (lead) collects [1]. Anode: **red-brown fumes** (bromine) [1].

**7.**

**(a)** **Bubbles of colourless gas** at both electrodes [1].

**(b)** The **cathode** (negative electrode) [1]; **hydrogen** [1].

**8.**

**(a)** The **handle is the cathode** [1]; the **anode is nickel** [1]; the electrolyte is a **solution of a nickel salt**, e.g. nickel(II) sulfate [1].

**(b)** So the nickel **sticks evenly** to the surface; grease or rust would stop it coating properly [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Cathode: **hydrogen** [1]; anode: **bromine** [1].

**(b)** Cathode: **hydrogen** [1]; anode: **oxygen** [1].

**(c)** Cathode: **silver** (less reactive than hydrogen) [1]; anode: **oxygen** (no halide present) [1].

**10.**

**(a)** Cathode: **Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu** [1]. Anode: **2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻** [1].

**(b)** The **anode** half-equation [1]; chloride ions **lose electrons**, and loss of electrons is oxidation [1].

**11.** **Electrons** flow from the power supply through the wire to the **cathode** [1]. **Na⁺ ions move to the cathode** and gain electrons; **Cl⁻ ions move to the anode** [1]. At the anode, chloride ions **lose electrons** [1]. These electrons flow from the **anode through the external circuit** back to the power supply [1].

**12.**

**(a)** The anode **loses mass** [1] because copper atoms **lose electrons and go into solution** as Cu²⁺ ions [1].

**(b)** **Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻** [1].

**(c)** Copper ions **enter the solution at the anode** at the same rate [1] as copper ions are **removed at the cathode**, so the **concentration of Cu²⁺ stays constant** [1].

**13.** **4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻** [1]. Left: 4 × (1−) = 4−. Right: O₂ and H₂O are neutral, plus 4e⁻ = 4−, so the charges balance [1].

**14.**

**(a)** **Molten sodium chloride** [1].

**(b)** Solid sodium chloride: its **ions are fixed** in the lattice and cannot move [1]. Glucose solution: glucose is covalent, so it contains **no ions** to carry the current [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving product names when the question asks for observations.
- Predicting sodium or potassium at the cathode from an aqueous solution.
- Predicting a halogen at the anode from a dilute halide solution.
- Placing electrons on the wrong side of a half-equation or leaving the charge unbalanced.
- Describing electrons moving through the electrolyte in charge-transfer answers.
- Making the object the anode in electroplating.
- Saying the lamp lights because "electrons are free to move" in molten lead(II) bromide — it is the ions.
