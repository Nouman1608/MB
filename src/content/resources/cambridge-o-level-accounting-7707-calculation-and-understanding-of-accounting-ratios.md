This guide goes deeper into section **6.1 Calculation and understanding of accounting ratios** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. Candidates "should have an understanding of and know how to calculate" ten ratios: gross profit margin, mark-up, profit margin, return on capital employed (ROCE), current (working capital) ratio, acid test (liquid) ratio, rate of inventory turnover (times), inventory turnover (days), trade receivables turnover (days) and trade payables turnover (days). The syllabus adds a firm rule: "Candidates must use the formulas given in the Accounting ratios included at the end of this section. These are the only formulas accepted in candidate responses."

The shared [calculating and interpreting accounting ratios guide](/resources/igcse-accounting-calculating-and-interpreting-accounting-ratios/) covers 6.1 and 6.2 together. This page stays with calculation and meaning: which figure goes into each formula, how the ratios link to each other, and the slips that cost marks in Paper 1 and Paper 2. Interpretation is in the [interpretation of accounting ratios guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-interpretation-of-accounting-ratios/). For recall use the [ratio calculation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios-revision-notes/), and practise with the [ratio calculation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios-practice/). All figures are invented and amounts are in dollars.

## The ten formulas and what each measures

| Ratio | Syllabus formula | Answer as | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit / Revenue x 100 | % | Gross profit earned on each $100 of sales |
| Mark-up | Gross profit / Cost of sales x 100 | % | Gross profit added to each $100 of cost |
| Profit margin | Profit for the year / Revenue x 100 | % | Profit left from each $100 of sales after all expenses |
| ROCE | Profit for the year before interest / Capital employed x 100 | % | Return earned on the long-term finance used |
| Current (working capital) ratio | Current assets / Current liabilities | ratio, e.g. 2.4 : 1 | Ability to pay short-term debts from current assets |
| Acid test (liquid) ratio | (Current assets - inventory) / Current liabilities | ratio | The same, without relying on selling inventory |
| Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales / Average inventory | times | How often inventory is sold and replaced in a year |
| Inventory turnover | Average inventory / Cost of sales x 365 | days | Average days inventory is held |
| Trade receivables turnover | Trade receivables / Credit sales x 365 | days | Average days credit customers take to pay |
| Trade payables turnover | Trade payables / Credit purchases x 365 | days | Average days taken to pay credit suppliers |

Capital employed = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities. For a sole trader, the owner's capital takes the place of shares and reserves.

## Choosing the right figure

Most lost marks come from using a figure close to the correct one.

- **Profit for the year before interest** (ROCE): add any loan or debenture interest back to profit for the year. Do not use gross profit.
- **Average inventory**: (opening inventory + closing inventory) / 2. Use closing inventory alone only if the question gives no opening figure or tells you to.
- **Credit sales** and **credit purchases**: leave out cash sales and cash purchases. If the question says all sales are on credit, revenue is credit sales.
- **Current assets less inventory** for the acid test: deduct only inventory. Trade receivables, other receivables and bank stay in.
- **Current (working capital) ratio** uses current assets, not working capital itself. Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities, a dollar figure.

## Worked example: Solway Traders Ltd

Extracts for the year ended 31 December 2028:

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Revenue (credit sales $288,000; cash sales $72,000) | 360,000 |
| Opening inventory | 26,000 |
| Purchases, all on credit | 224,000 |
| Closing inventory | 34,000 |
| Expenses other than interest | 86,400 |
| Debenture interest | 4,000 |
| Issued ordinary shares | 200,000 |
| General reserve | 30,000 |
| Retained earnings | 60,000 |
| 8% debentures | 50,000 |
| Trade receivables | 31,560 |
| Bank | 6,440 |
| Trade payables | 24,000 |
| Other payables | 6,000 |

**Preliminary figures.** Cost of sales = 26,000 + 224,000 - 34,000 = $216,000. Gross profit = 360,000 - 216,000 = $144,000. Profit for the year = 144,000 - 86,400 - 4,000 = $53,600. Current assets = 34,000 + 31,560 + 6,440 = $72,000. Current liabilities = 24,000 + 6,000 = $30,000. Average inventory = (26,000 + 34,000) / 2 = $30,000. Capital employed = 200,000 + 30,000 + 60,000 + 50,000 = $340,000.

| Ratio | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | 144,000 / 360,000 x 100 | 40.00% |
| Mark-up | 144,000 / 216,000 x 100 | 66.67% |
| Profit margin | 53,600 / 360,000 x 100 | 14.89% |
| ROCE | (53,600 + 4,000) / 340,000 x 100 | 16.94% |
| Current ratio | 72,000 / 30,000 | 2.40 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | (72,000 - 34,000) / 30,000 | 1.27 : 1 |
| Rate of inventory turnover | 216,000 / 30,000 | 7.20 times |
| Inventory turnover | 30,000 / 216,000 x 365 | 50.69 days |
| Trade receivables turnover | 31,560 / 288,000 x 365 | 40.00 days |
| Trade payables turnover | 24,000 / 224,000 x 365 | 39.11 days |

Two traps appear in this example. Using all revenue of $360,000 for receivables would give 32.00 days and hide how long credit customers really take. Using profit for the year without adding back interest would give an ROCE of 15.76%.

## How the ratios link

- **Margin and mark-up** describe the same gross profit. With a 40% margin, every $100 of revenue has $60 of cost, so the mark-up is 40 / 60 = 66.67%. The mark-up is always the larger percentage when there is a gross profit.
- **Inventory times and days** are two views of one fact: 365 / 7.2 = 50.69 days.
- **Gross profit margin and profit margin**: the gap between them is expenses (including interest) as a percentage of revenue. Here 40.00% - 14.89% = 25.11%.
- **Current ratio and acid test**: the difference comes only from inventory. A business with large inventory has a big gap between the two.

## Understanding, not just calculating

The syllabus asks for understanding as well as calculation, so be ready to say what one result means in a sentence. "The trade receivables turnover of 40 days means that, on average, credit customers take 40 days to pay." "The acid test ratio of 1.27 : 1 means the company has $1.27 of liquid assets for every $1 of current liabilities." Each statement uses the result and its unit and says what it measures, without judging it. Judging whether it is good is 6.2.

## Common mistakes

- Swapping margin and mark-up denominators.
- Giving the current ratio as a percentage, or as "2.4" without ": 1" when the syllabus says "answer presented as a ratio".
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory when both figures are given.
- Using total sales or total purchases for the days ratios.
- Forgetting "x 365" for the days ratios, or using 360.
- Rounding too early: keep full figures until the last step.

## Exam technique

In Paper 1, the options for a ratio question usually include the results of the common slips, so a wrong method lands on a listed answer. Write the formula first, then the figures, then the result with its unit. In Paper 2, **calculate** questions award a mark for the correct formula with figures, so show the working line even when using a calculator. Round only where the question says; two decimal places is a sensible default. If a question asks you to **state** what a ratio shows, give its meaning in one sentence.
