## Why there's no SL/HL split at MYP level

Like every MYP subject, Language Acquisition does not distinguish Standard and Higher Level in the DP sense -- differentiation instead happens through the six-phase proficiency continuum described below, which lets students at very different starting points be assessed fairly against criteria calibrated to their own phase, rather than through parallel courses of different overall demand.

## No SL/HL split — four criteria instead

| Criterion | Skill |
|---|---|
| A | Listening |
| B | Reading |
| C | Speaking |
| D | Writing |

Each scored on an **8-point scale (1-8)**, equally weighted -- a classroom total of 32 across the four
criteria. This "1-8" describes the four descriptor bands; the MYP subject-group guides additionally
recognise a **level of 0** for work that does not reach any band descriptor, so a single criterion's
attainable range is 0-8 and the four-criterion classroom total is 0-32, not 4-32. This is a separate scale from the eAssessment blueprint below, which allocates 32 marks
to each criterion (96 marks for the on-screen examination, which covers A, B and D, plus 32 for speaking). The
figure 32 means something different on each scale: a classroom total, or the marks for one criterion.

## Proficiency phases, not year groups

Teaching is organised into **six proficiency phases** forming a continuum. Students start at
whatever phase matches their prior experience and progress **at their own pace** — two students
in the same class can legitimately sit in different phases.

## If your school offers MYP eAssessment

Assessed at one of three overall levels — **emergent, capable, proficient**:

- On-screen exam: three tasks of about 35 minutes each, **1 hour 45 minutes** in all and **96 marks**.
  Tasks 1 and 2 each pair a written-visual text with an audio-visual text and assess listening (A) and
  reading (B) equally, 16 marks each per task, so each task is worth 32 marks. Task 3 is writing in
  response to multimodal texts (D), worth 32 marks. That gives A 32, B 32 and D 32 marks in the
  eAssessment blueprint. The distribution of marks in an eAssessment may vary by no more than three
  marks from the blueprint.
- Speaking (criterion C) assessed **separately**, in an individual examination of 15 to 17 minutes
  including preparation time, worth **32 marks**, internally assessed then **externally moderated** by
  the IB.
- Source for these marks and timings: International Baccalaureate Organization, Middle Years Programme Language acquisition guide, for use from September 2020/January 2021 ("MYP eAssessment" and "Language acquisition examination blueprint", pages 51 and 52).

## The four criteria in more depth

Each of the four skill criteria (A listening, B reading, C speaking, D writing) is assessed against level descriptors organised in bands, with higher bands rewarding increasingly sophisticated understanding, response and production appropriate to the student's proficiency phase -- crucially, the *standard expected* within each band is calibrated to the phase a student is working in, not to a fixed absolute standard, so a phase 2 student and a phase 5 student are both assessed against "what a strong response looks like at this phase," not against the same benchmark. This is why comparing scores directly across students in different phases is not meaningful -- the criteria measure progress appropriate to each phase, not a single universal scale.

## Why the six proficiency phases exist

The six-phase continuum exists because language acquisition genuinely does not map onto age or school-year groupings -- a student who has studied a language since early childhood and a complete beginner in the same MYP year group need fundamentally different tasks, texts and vocabulary demand to be assessed fairly. Phases typically span from initial exposure to the language (phase 1) through to near-native fluency in academic and social contexts (phase 6), and a student's placement is reviewed periodically as their proficiency develops, meaning genuine phase changes during the programme are expected and normal, not exceptional.

## The eAssessment structure in more depth

Under eAssessment, the on-screen exam assesses listening, reading and writing (criteria A, B and D) together, carrying 32 marks each, 96 marks in all for those three components combined (listening and reading in Tasks 1 and 2, writing in Task 3), calibrated to one of three overall proficiency levels -- emergent, capable or proficient -- rather than to the full six-phase continuum used in ongoing classroom assessment. Speaking (criterion C) is deliberately assessed separately because it requires live interaction rather than a written or listened response; it is recorded and marked by the student's own teacher first, then externally moderated by the IB to ensure consistency with the same overall standard applied to the other three skills.

## How the four key concepts shape task design

Communication, connections, creativity and culture are not abstract theory but the actual lens through which tasks are designed and marked -- a strong response typically demonstrates awareness of how the target language connects to its cultural context (for example, using an idiom appropriately rather than translating literally from the student's first language), and creatively adapts language to a specific communicative purpose rather than reproducing memorised phrases. Preparing around these four concepts explicitly, rather than only drilling vocabulary and grammar in isolation, better matches how MYP Language Acquisition tasks are actually constructed and marked.

## Exam traps

- Assuming your proficiency phase matches your MYP year group — it doesn't, it's based on actual language proficiency.
- Neglecting speaking practice because it's assessed separately/internally, when it's still externally moderated and equally weighted.
- Preparing generically instead of around the specific key concepts (communication, connections, creativity, culture) that structure tasks.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is IB MYP Language Acquisition graded?**
Each of the four criteria (A listening, B reading, C speaking, D writing) is scored on an eight-point scale and the criteria are equally weighted. With a level of 0 for work that reaches no band descriptor, a classroom total runs from 0 to 32.

**How many marks is the MYP Language Acquisition eAssessment worth?**
In the eAssessment blueprint, listening, reading and writing (criteria A, B and D) each carry 32 marks on the on-screen exam, which is worth 96 marks in all, and speaking (criterion C) is assessed separately and is worth 32 marks. This is a separate scale from the 0–32 classroom total.

**What are the emergent, capable and proficient levels in MYP Language Acquisition?**
They are the three overall levels at which the MYP Language Acquisition eAssessment is taken. Classroom teaching and assessment use the full six-phase proficiency continuum instead.

**How long is the MYP Language Acquisition on-screen exam?**
The guide's blueprint gives 1 hour 45 minutes for the on-screen exam: three tasks of about 35 minutes each. Confirm the arrangements for your sitting with your school.
