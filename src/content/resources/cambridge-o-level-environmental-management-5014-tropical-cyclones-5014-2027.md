Sub-topic 6.2 of the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014 syllabus (2027-2029) has four outcomes: the location and conditions needed for a tropical cyclone to form (6.2.1), its impacts on people and the environment (6.2.2), strategies for managing those impacts before, during and after the event (6.2.3), and a discussion of the benefits and limitations of those strategies (6.2.4). The formation conditions are given as precise numbers in the syllabus, so learn them exactly. This guide explains why each condition matters, works through a data example, and shows how to build a discussion answer on management.

For an overview of all four hazards, see the [natural hazards study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-natural-hazards-study-guide/).

## What counts as a tropical cyclone

The syllabus uses **tropical cyclone** as the umbrella term for **storms, hurricanes and typhoons**. They are the same kind of weather system: a large, rotating area of very low pressure with strong winds and heavy rain, which forms over warm tropical oceans. The name depends on where it occurs: *hurricane* is used in the Atlantic and north-east Pacific, *typhoon* in the north-west Pacific, and *cyclone* in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific. A weaker system with lower wind speeds is called a tropical storm.

## Where and when they form (6.2.1)

The syllabus gives three conditions and a location.

| Condition | Syllabus value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | Between **5° and 30°** north or south of the Equator | Closer than 5° the Earth's rotation is too weak to make the air spin; beyond about 30° the sea is usually too cool |
| Ocean temperature | At least **27 °C** | Warm water evaporates rapidly, supplying the moist air whose condensation releases the heat that powers the storm |
| Ocean depth | At least **60 m** | A deep layer of warm water means the storm does not quickly stir up cold water from below, which would cut off its energy |

### How the energy supply works

Warm, moist air rises rapidly from the sea surface. As it rises it cools and the water vapour condenses into towering clouds, releasing heat. The heat makes the air rise faster, lowering the pressure at the surface, so more air rushes in and is drawn upwards. The Earth's rotation turns the inflowing air into a spiral. In the centre, air sinks to form a calm, cloud-free **eye**. The storm keeps strengthening as long as it stays over warm, deep water. Over land or cold water it loses its supply of moisture and heat, and it weakens.

Because sea temperatures are highest in late summer and early autumn, that is when most tropical cyclones occur in each hemisphere.

## Worked example 1: applying the conditions

A question gives data for four ocean locations and asks which could produce a tropical cyclone. *The data are invented for practice.*

| Site | Latitude | Sea temperature / °C | Ocean depth / m |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 3° N | 29 | 200 |
| B | 15° N | 28 | 80 |
| C | 22° S | 26 | 150 |
| D | 12° S | 28 | 40 |

- **A:** temperature and depth are suitable, but 3° is too close to the Equator. **No.**
- **B:** 15° is within 5°-30°, 28 °C is at least 27 °C, and 80 m is at least 60 m. **Yes.**
- **C:** latitude and depth are suitable, but 26 °C is below 27 °C. **No.**
- **D:** latitude and temperature are suitable, but 40 m is too shallow. **No.**

Only site B meets all three conditions. In a full answer, state the condition that fails for each rejected site, with its value; that is what earns the marks.

## Impacts on people and the environment (6.2.2)

The syllabus lists ten impacts. Link each to the part of the cyclone that causes it.

- **Strong winds:** damage to buildings and infrastructure (roofs, power lines, bridges, ports); loss of crops; injury and death of livestock and destruction of habitats such as mangroves and coral reefs.
- **Heavy rain:** river **flooding** and **landslides** on saturated slopes.
- **Low pressure and wind pushing the sea onshore (storm surge):** coastal flooding, salt water on farmland, and loss of coastal habitats.
- **Follow-on impacts:** evacuation of people and livestock; contamination of drinking-water supplies by floodwater and sewage; water-related disease; financial losses from damage, lost harvests, lost tourism and rebuilding; and effects on human health, from injuries to illness and stress.

### An impact chain

Heavy rain and storm surge → flooding of homes and sewers → drinking water contaminated → people crowded into shelters → water-related disease spreads. A chain like this is the best way to answer "explain" questions on impacts, because each step is a separate creditable idea.

## Managing the impacts (6.2.3 and 6.2.4)

The syllabus lists seven strategies for tropical cyclones. Note that **land use zoning is not one of them** (it is listed for tectonic events and for flooding), so do not rely on it here.

| Stage | Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | **Monitoring and warning**: satellites, aircraft and weather radar track the storm; forecasts give its likely path | Cyclones develop over days, so warnings can be given well in advance | The path and strength can change; warnings must reach remote communities; repeated false alarms reduce trust |
| Before | **Structure of buildings**: strong roofs tied to walls, storm shutters, raised floors in surge areas | Reduces damage and injuries from wind and water | Costly; many existing homes are informal or old |
| Before | **Disaster preparation**: plans, drills, emergency supplies and emergency rescue teams | People know where to go; supplies are ready | Needs funding and regular practice |
| During | **Evacuation** of people and livestock from coasts and low-lying land | Saves many lives, especially from storm surge | Needs transport, roads and trust; people may stay to protect property and animals |
| During | **Shelters**: strong, raised buildings | Protect people who cannot leave the area | Can be overcrowded; poor sanitation spreads disease |
| After | **Rebuilding of damaged areas** | Restores homes and services; chance to build stronger | Slow and expensive |
| After | **International aid**: money, food, water, medical teams, equipment | Fills gaps the country cannot fill alone | Can be delayed; may not reach the worst-hit areas |

### Worked example 2: annotated model answer

**Question:** Discuss the benefits and limitations of monitoring and warning and of shelters in reducing deaths from tropical cyclones. **[6]**

**Answer:** Monitoring by satellite allows a cyclone to be tracked for several days before it reaches land, so warnings can be issued *(benefit, 1)*. This gives time for evacuation and for people to secure property *(benefit, 1)*. However, a warning only helps if it reaches everyone, including remote villages without electricity or phones, and if people believe it *(limitation, 1)*. Shelters protect people who cannot travel far, such as the elderly and families in low-lying villages *(benefit, 1)*. But they can become overcrowded, and poor water and sanitation can spread disease *(limitation, 1)*. The two strategies depend on each other: a warning is of little use without a safe place to go, and shelters only fill if people are warned in time *(judgement, 1)*.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "warm sea" without the value. The syllabus gives at least 27 °C.
- Saying cyclones form on the Equator. They form between 5° and 30°.
- Forgetting the depth condition of at least 60 m.
- Using land use zoning as a cyclone strategy; it is not in the 6.2.3 list.
- Treating the three names as three different hazards. Storms, hurricanes and typhoons are all tropical cyclones.
- Writing impacts as a list in an "explain" question, without saying which part of the cyclone causes each.

## Exam technique

**Describe the location** using latitude and named oceans or continents. **Explain formation** using the three conditions and the energy supply. **Suggest** questions often give a map of a storm track or a table of damage: use the data, quote values with units and link them to the conditions or impacts. **Discuss** a strategy: give at least one benefit and one limitation, then a judgement about which strategy matters most at which stage.

## Check your understanding

1. Between which latitudes do tropical cyclones form? *Between 5° and 30° north or south of the Equator.*
2. What minimum ocean depth is needed? *60 m.*
3. Why does a cyclone weaken over land? *It loses its supply of warm, moist air from the ocean.*
4. Name the strategies used during a cyclone. *Evacuation and shelters.*
5. Explain why water-related disease may follow a cyclone. *Flooding contaminates drinking water with sewage, and crowded shelters help disease spread.*
