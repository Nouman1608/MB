These notes condense quantitative skill QS3 from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification: "understand and use the terms mean, median (for A2 assessments, students will also need to understand and use relevant quantiles)". The specification says the quantitative skills could be assessed in any of the assessments. For full worked examples, read the [QS3 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs3-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics/); to practise, use the [QS3 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs3-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented; "cu" means currency units.

## Definitions and formulae

```
Mean                   sum of values / number of values
Mean from a table      Σ(value × frequency) / Σfrequency
Combined mean          (n₁ × mean₁ + n₂ × mean₂) / (n₁ + n₂)
Weighted mean          Σ(weight × value) / Σweights
Median (list)          middle value of the ordered data
Grouped median         L + (n/2 − F)/f × w
Grouped quantile       L + (position − F)/f × w,  position = n × fraction
IQR                    Q3 − Q1
90:10 ratio            90th percentile / 10th percentile
```

In the grouped formula, L is the lower boundary of the class containing the quantile, F is the cumulative frequency before that class, f is the class frequency and w is the class width.

## Which average when

| Situation | Better measure | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Income or wealth data | Median | Not pulled up by a few very high values |
| Total output shared across a population | Mean (GDP per capita) | Links directly to the total |
| Prices bought in different quantities | Weighted mean | Reflects what was actually spent |
| Spread of the middle half | IQR | Ignores extremes |
| Gap between rich and poor | Percentile ratio or quintile shares | Compares the ends of the distribution |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean | The arithmetic average |
| Median | The middle value of ordered data; the 50th percentile |
| Quantile | A value dividing ordered data into equal-sized groups |
| Quartile / quintile / decile / percentile | Divides into 4 / 5 / 10 / 100 groups |
| Positive skew | A long tail of high values; mean above median |
| Weighted mean | A mean in which some values count more than others |
| Lorenz curve | Cumulative share of income plotted against cumulative share of households |
| Gini coefficient | 0 for perfect equality, 1 for complete inequality; interpreted, not calculated, in 9640 |

## Grouped quantile in four lines

Sixty workers earn: under 300 cu (12), 300 to under 500 (24), 500 to under 900 (24).

1. Median position = 60/2 = 30; cumulative frequencies are 12, 36, 60.
2. Position 30 lies in the 300 to 500 class, with 12 values before it.
3. Median = 300 + (30 − 12)/24 × 200 = 450 cu.
4. Upper quartile, position 45: 500 + (45 − 36)/24 × 400 = 650 cu.

## From quintile shares to a Lorenz curve

Shares of 6%, 10%, 15%, 23% and 46% give cumulative shares of 6%, 16%, 31%, 54% and 100% at 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% and 100% of households. Plot these points; the line of equality runs from (0, 0) to (100, 100). The bigger the gap between the curve and that line, the more unequal the distribution and the higher the Gini coefficient.

## Effect of changes

- A **percentage rise for everyone** raises the mean and median by that percentage and leaves ratios unchanged.
- A **flat amount for everyone** raises the mean and median by that amount and lowers ratios such as 90:10.
- **One extreme value** moves the mean, not the median or the IQR.

## Exam traps

- Averaging two group means without weighting by group size.
- Taking the median from an unordered list.
- Using (n + 1)/2 for grouped data, then mixing methods; state the position rule you use.
- Forgetting the cumulative frequency column.
- Treating a grouped estimate as exact.
- Saying "the richest 46%" when the data mean "the richest fifth receive 46%".
- Claiming a higher mean income means most people are better off.
- Calculating a Gini coefficient when only an interpretation is asked for.

## Self-test

1. Find the mean and the median of 4, 7, 9, 12, 18.
2. 100 workers have a mean wage of 400 cu and 300 workers have a mean wage of 300 cu. Find the combined mean.
3. 200 units sell at 5 cu and 300 units at 6 cu. Find the weighted mean price.
4. State the midpoint of the class "100 to under 150".
5. A class 20 to under 30 has frequency 20, and the cumulative frequency before it is 40. Estimate the value at position 50.
6. In a grouped table of 80 values, at which position is the lower quartile?
7. The 90th percentile is 60,000 cu and the 10th percentile is 12,000 cu. Find the 90:10 ratio.
8. Quintile shares are 5%, 9%, 15%, 24% and 47%. What share do the poorest 40% receive?
9. What does a mean well above the median suggest about a distribution?
10. Every income rises by 5%. What happens to the 90:10 ratio?
11. What do Gini coefficients of 0 and 1 mean?
12. Which of the mean, the median and the IQR changes noticeably when one billionaire moves into a town?

### Answers

1. Mean 50/5 = 10; median 9.
2. (100 × 400 + 300 × 300)/400 = 130,000/400 = 325 cu.
3. (1,000 + 1,800)/500 = 5.60 cu.
4. 125.
5. 20 + (50 − 40)/20 × 10 = 25.
6. 80 × 1/4 = 20th position.
7. 60,000/12,000 = 5.
8. 5 + 9 = 14%.
9. Positive skew: a few high values pull the mean up.
10. It stays the same, because both percentiles rise by 5%.
11. 0: perfect equality; 1: one household receives all the income.
12. The mean.
