Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.4.5 Species and taxonomy**. Worked examples on reading classifications, antibody data and sequence differences are in the [species and taxonomy study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-species-and-taxonomy/). Test yourself with the [species and taxonomy practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-species-and-taxonomy-practice/).

## Species

- **Definition:** two organisms belong to the same species if they are able to produce **fertile offspring**.
- Infertile hybrid (for example the mule, from horse × donkey) → parents are different species.
- No offspring → different species, unless something else prevented mating.

## Courtship behaviour

Specification roles:

1. A **necessary precursor to successful mating**.
2. **Species recognition**: signals are species specific.

Why species recognition matters: mating with another species gives no offspring or infertile ones, wasting energy, time and gametes.

Other roles often taught: recognising the opposite sex, a partner ready to mate, pair bonding.

Data reading: if females respond only to their own species' signal, the signal works in species recognition.

## Phylogenetic classification

- Groups species by **evolutionary origins and relationships** (shared ancestry).
- **Hierarchy:** smaller groups inside larger groups.
- **No overlap** between groups.
- Each group = a **taxon** (plural taxa).

| Order | Taxon |
|---|---|
| 1 (largest) | Domain |
| 2 | Kingdom |
| 3 | Phylum |
| 4 | Class |
| 5 | Order |
| 6 | Family |
| 7 | Genus |
| 8 (smallest) | Species |

**Lowest shared taxon** → closest relationship. Sharing a genus implies a more recent common ancestor than sharing only a family.

Three domain and five kingdom systems: not required.

## Binomials

- Genus + species, for example *Homo sapiens*.
- Genus capitalised; species lower case; italics (underline each word when handwritten).
- Universal: same name in every language.
- Same first word → same genus.

## Evidence that clarifies relationships

| Method | What is compared | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Immunology | A protein from different species, using antibodies raised against one species' protein | More similar protein shape → more antibody binding → more precipitate → more closely related |
| Genome sequencing | DNA base sequences of genes or whole genomes | Fewer base differences → more recent common ancestor |

- Sequencing gives large amounts of direct data and can show relationships hidden by similar appearance.
- One protein or one gene is weaker evidence than many.

## Reading a distance table

1. Find the smallest difference: those two are most closely related.
2. Find which species is most different from all others: it branched off earliest.
3. Draw the pair on neighbouring branches; join the outlier further back.

## Answer frames that score

- **Relatedness from a classification:** "A and B share every taxon down to genus, whereas C differs from them at family, so A and B share a more recent common ancestor."
- **Immunology data:** "More precipitate forms with species B, so B's protein has a more similar shape (tertiary structure), so a more similar amino acid sequence and base sequence, so B is more closely related."
- **Limitation:** "Only one protein (or gene) was compared, so the conclusion may not apply to the whole genome."
- **Courtship data:** "Females responded only to signals of their own species, so the signal allows species recognition and prevents interbreeding with other species."

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Species | Organisms able to produce fertile offspring together |
| Courtship behaviour | Behaviour before mating that leads to successful mating and allows species recognition |
| Phylogenetic classification | Arrangement of species into groups based on evolutionary origins and relationships |
| Taxon | A group in a classification hierarchy |
| Hierarchy | Smaller groups within larger groups, with no overlap |
| Binomial | Two-part scientific name: genus then species |
| Common ancestor | An ancestral species from which two or more species evolved |

## Exam traps

- "Can breed together" is not enough: "produce fertile offspring".
- Courtship answers need **species recognition**.
- Hierarchy order: domain first, species last.
- Binomial formatting: *Ursus arctos*, not *Ursus Arctos*.
- More precipitate = more closely related.
- Do not over-claim from one protein or gene.
- Appearance can mislead; molecular data are more reliable for relationships.

## Quick self-test

1. State the definition of a species.
2. A horse and a donkey produce a mule, which is infertile. What does this show?
3. Give the two roles of courtship named in the specification.
4. Why is courtship behaviour usually species specific?
5. What does "phylogenetic" mean?
6. List the eight taxa in order from domain.
7. What does "no overlap" mean in a hierarchy?
8. Write the binomial for humans correctly.
9. Two species share genus *Felis*. A third shares only the family. Which two are most closely related?
10. Explain how antibodies can show how closely related two species are.
11. Why does genome sequencing give more reliable evidence of relationships than comparing appearance?
12. Species X and Y differ at 3% of bases in a gene; X and Z at 14%. Which pair shares the more recent common ancestor?

### Answers

1. Two organisms belong to the same species if they can produce fertile offspring.
2. Horses and donkeys are different species.
3. A necessary precursor to successful mating; species recognition.
4. So that animals respond only to members of their own species and avoid mating that gives no offspring or infertile ones.
5. Based on evolutionary origins and relationships.
6. Domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species.
7. Each taxon belongs to only one group at the rank above.
8. *Homo sapiens*.
9. The two in genus *Felis*.
10. Antibodies raised against a protein from one species bind to the same protein from another; the more similar the protein, the more antibodies bind, so the more precipitate and the closer the relationship.
11. It compares base sequences directly, gives much more data, and is not misled by organisms that look similar because they live in similar environments.
12. X and Y.
