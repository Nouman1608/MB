## Key terms

| Term | Meaning (standard wording, not a quotation) |
|---|---|
| Ethical issue | A choice between doing what is right and doing what is cheapest or most profitable |
| Ethical business | One that acts on principles of right and wrong, even where the law does not force it to |
| Child labour | Employing children in work unsuitable for their age or that stops them going to school |
| Fair wage | Pay that reflects the work done and lets the employee live decently |
| Fair price to a supplier | A price that covers the supplier's costs and lets it stay in business |
| Pressure group | An organised group that campaigns to influence the decisions of businesses or governments |
| Boycott | Customers deliberately refusing to buy from a business |
| Lobbying | Trying to persuade the government to make or change a law |

## The four ethical issues in the syllabus (6.4.1)

1. Child labour.
2. Paying fair wages to employees.
3. Paying fair prices to suppliers.
4. Using suppliers who do not damage the environment.

These are given as examples, so be ready for others in a case study. Link the issue to the people affected: employees, suppliers, customers, the community and owners.

## How businesses may respond

| Response | One-line evaluation |
|---|---|
| Legal minimum only | Cheapest, but risks criticism and bad publicity |
| Written ethical policy | Clear rules, but only works if enforced |
| Inspect suppliers, drop those that fail | Reduces risk in the supply chain, costs time and money |
| Pay higher wages | Better motivation and retention, higher labour costs |
| Pay fair prices to suppliers | More reliable supply, higher material costs |
| Switch to ethical suppliers or materials | Matches the policy, may raise unit costs |
| Publicise the policy | Builds reputation, but claims must be true |
| Meet or answer a pressure group | Protects reputation, may need changes in practice |
| Ignore or deny | Saves money in the short run, risks sales and reputation |

### Method for a "how could the business respond" question

1. Name the issue from the stimulus.
2. State one specific response.
3. Explain what it changes, such as supplier checks that reduce the chance of child labour.
4. Say what it costs or gains the business, using any figures given.

## Advantages and disadvantages of being ethical

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Better reputation and more sales | Higher costs |
| More loyal customers | Higher prices can lose price-sensitive customers |
| More motivated, longer-serving staff | Lower profit in the short run |
| Easier recruitment | Time and money spent checking suppliers |
| Less risk of campaigns and bad publicity | Rivals who ignore ethics may be cheaper |
| Co-operative, reliable suppliers | Benefits are slow and hard to prove |
| Possibly a higher price | Untrue claims damage reputation |

The balance depends on the business. A strong brand with customers who care has more to gain. A business whose customers buy only on price may find the costs hard to recover.

## Pressure groups (6.5.1)

How a pressure group can influence business decisions:

- publicity through the media and social media
- calling for a boycott
- petitions and protests
- lobbying the government to change the law
- negotiating with managers
- praising businesses that improve

What affects the group's influence:

- level of public and media support
- how much the target depends on its reputation
- whether customers can switch easily
- how clear and affordable the demands are
- whether the group can reach the government

A pressure group cannot order a business to change. It works through sales, reputation and the law. Not every group is hostile: some work with businesses and give them credit.

### Business responses to a pressure group

Ignore, defend, negotiate, change practice, or change practice and publicise it. Judge each by cost, speed and the effect on reputation.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Legal vs ethical | The law is compulsory. Ethics goes beyond what the law demands |
| Pressure group vs trade union | A pressure group campaigns on a cause. A trade union represents workers in their workplaces |
| Boycott vs lobbying | A boycott hits sales directly. Lobbying aims to change the law |
| Fair wage vs fair price | One is paid to employees. The other is paid to suppliers |
| Ethical cost vs ethical benefit | Costs are usually certain and immediate. Benefits are often uncertain and later |
| Stakeholder vs pressure group | A stakeholder has a direct link to the business. A pressure group may have none |

## Worked reminders

### Fair price to a supplier

Dorrance Bakery buys 60 tonnes of flour a year at 500 a tonne. A fair-price agreement lifts the price to 560.

- Old cost = 60 × 500 = 30,000.
- New cost = 60 × 560 = 33,600.
- Extra cost = 3,600, which is 12% more.
- Sales revenue is 420,000, so the extra cost is about 0.9% of sales revenue.

This is a small share of revenue, so the bakery may judge the move affordable, especially if it wins loyal customers.

### Cost of a boycott

Harlequin Toys sells 40,000 toys at 12 each, so sales revenue is 480,000. A pressure group campaign reduces sales by 5%, and the contribution on each sale is 30%.

- Lost revenue = 5% × 480,000 = 24,000.
- Lost contribution = 30% × 24,000 = 7,200.

If changing its practice would cost more than 7,200 a year, Harlequin might wait. If the campaign is growing, the loss could be larger, so the decision is not just about this year's figure.

## Command words used in this topic

The syllabus lists these command words: calculate, consider, define, explain, identify, justify, outline and state. Use them like this:

- **Define** and **state**: give a short, precise answer with no development.
- **Identify**: name the issue or response from the information.
- **Outline**: set out the main points briefly.
- **Explain**: give a reason and develop it, using the business in the question.
- **Consider** and **justify**: support a case with evidence. Give a point for and a point against, then reach a decision and say why.

The papers also reward analysis and evaluation, so expect to follow every reason with its effect on the business.

## Quick self-test

1. State two ethical issues which may affect a business.
2. Define the term pressure group.
3. Outline two ways a pressure group can influence a business.
4. Identify two ways a business can respond to evidence of child labour in its supply chain.
5. State one advantage to a business of being ethical.
6. State one disadvantage to a business of being ethical.
7. Explain why paying fair prices to suppliers might help a business in the long run.
8. A business raises an hourly wage from 5.00 to 6.00. Calculate the percentage increase.
9. A business makes 50,000 items and pays 0.12 more per item to suppliers. Calculate the extra yearly cost.
10. A business with sales revenue of 200,000 loses 5% of it during a boycott. Calculate the revenue lost.
11. Explain why a well-known brand may respond quickly to a pressure group campaign.

### Answers

1. Any two of: child labour, paying unfair wages, paying unfair prices to suppliers, using suppliers who damage the environment.
2. An organised group that campaigns to influence the decisions of businesses or governments.
3. Any two of: publicity, boycott, petition or protest, lobbying the government, negotiation.
4. Any two of: inspect suppliers, drop the supplier, set an ethical policy, change to a different supplier, tell customers what it has done.
5. For example, a better reputation which may increase sales.
6. For example, higher costs which may reduce profit.
7. Suppliers who are paid fairly are more likely to keep trading and to supply reliable quality, so the business has a more secure supply.
8. (6.00 − 5.00) ÷ 5.00 × 100 = **20%**.
9. 50,000 × 0.12 = **6,000**.
10. 5% × 200,000 = **10,000**.
11. Its sales depend on its reputation, so bad publicity could cost it many customers, and it has more to lose than a little-known business.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "it is good for the image" with no development of why that increases sales.
- Treating ethical and legal as the same thing.
- Describing a pressure group but not saying how it changes the business's decision.
- Giving one-sided answers to "consider" and "justify" questions.
- Forgetting to use figures from the data in a calculation or comment.
- Quoting the word "boycott" without saying what the customers do.
- Listing ethical issues without saying who is affected.
- Writing a long answer to a 2-mark "state" question.
- Stating a conclusion with no reason attached.
