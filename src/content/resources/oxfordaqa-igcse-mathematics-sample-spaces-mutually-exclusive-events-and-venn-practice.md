## Questions

**1.** The nine letters of the word COMMITTEE are written on separate cards. One card is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that the card shows T. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the card shows a vowel (A, E, I, O or U). **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why "the card shows T" and "the card shows a vowel" are mutually exclusive, and find the probability that the card shows T or a vowel. **[2]**

**2.** Four tiles are numbered 2, 5, 7 and 8. Two tiles are taken one after the other, without replacement. The first tile gives the tens digit and the second gives the units digit of a two-digit number.

**(a)** List all the possible two-digit numbers. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the number is greater than 60. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that the number is a multiple of 5. **[1]**

**3.** A fair spinner has four equal sections coloured red (R), yellow (Y), blue (B) and green (G). It is spun twice.

**(a)** Complete the table of outcomes. The first row and first column are filled in. **[2]**

| 1st \ 2nd | R | Y | B | G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | RR | RY | RB | RG |
| Y | YR | | | |
| B | BR | | | |
| G | GR | | | |

**(b)** Find the probability that both spins give the same colour. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that at least one spin gives red. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the probability that one spin gives red and the other gives blue. **[1]**

**4.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Event A is "the number is less than 3". Event B is "the number is greater than 4". Event C is "the number is odd".

**(a)** For each pair of A, B and C, state whether the two events are mutually exclusive. Give a reason for each pair. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(A or B). **[1]**
**(c)** Are A, B and C exhaustive? Give a reason. **[1]**

**5.** A four-sided spinner shows the numbers 1, 2, 3 and 4. The probabilities are shown below.

| Number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | x | x + 0.1 | 2x | 0.3 |

**(a)** Work out the value of x. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the spinner does not land on 3. **[1]**

**6.** A shop records what each customer buys. The probability that a customer buys bread is 0.55. The probability that a customer buys milk is 0.3. The probability that a customer buys neither is 0.25.

**(a)** Find the probability that a customer buys bread or milk (or both). **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability that a customer buys both. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the probability that a customer buys milk but not bread. **[1]**

**7.** A year group of 36 pupils is asked about three clubs: art, band and chess. The results are:

15 pupils are in art, 12 in band and 11 in chess. Of these, 2 are in all three clubs, 3 are in art and band only, 4 are in art and chess only, and 1 is in band and chess only.

**(a)** Work out the number in art only, band only and chess only. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out the probability that a randomly chosen pupil is in no club. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the probability that a pupil is in exactly two clubs. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the probability that a pupil is in art but not chess. **[1]**

**8.** The whole numbers from 1 to 36 are sorted using a Venn diagram. Set A is the square numbers. Set B is the multiples of 4.

**(a)** List the numbers in each of the four regions, or give how many there are in each. **[3]**
**(b)** A number is chosen at random from 1 to 36. Find the probability that it is neither a square nor a multiple of 4. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that it is in exactly one of the sets. **[1]**

**9.** One spinner has three equal sections numbered 2, 3 and 4. A second has three equal sections numbered 1, 3 and 5. Both are spun and the score is the sum of the two numbers.

**(a)** Complete a table showing all the possible scores. **[2]**
**(b)** Event L is "the score is 4 or less". Event M is "the score is 5, 6 or 7". Event N is "the score is 8 or more". Find P(L), P(M) and P(N). **[3]**
**(c)** Show that L, M and N are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. **[1]**
**(d)** Use an addition of probabilities to find P(L or N). **[2]**

**10.** In a group of 50 students, 24 study French, 20 study Spanish and 9 study neither language. Let x be the number who study both.

**(a)** Write an equation in x and solve it. **[3]**
**(b)** Complete a two-circle Venn diagram for the group. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that a student chosen at random studies French only. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the probability that a student studies exactly one of the two languages. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** There are 2 Ts among 9 cards, so P(T) = **2/9** [1]
**(b)** The vowels are O, I, E, E: 4 cards, so P(vowel) = **4/9** [1]
**(c)** No card is both T and a vowel, so the events cannot happen together [1]. P(T or vowel) = 2/9 + 4/9 = 6/9 = **2/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason for "mutually exclusive": no card can be both.

**2. (a)** The first tile has 4 options and the second 3, so there are 12 numbers: 25, 27, 28, 52, 57, 58, 72, 75, 78, 82, 85, 87 [2]
**(b)** Greater than 60: 72, 75, 78, 82, 85, 87, which is 6 of 12, so **1/2** [1]
**(c)** Multiples of 5: 25, 75, 85, which is 3 of 12, so **1/4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Without replacement, 22 or 55 cannot occur. Check that your list has 4 × 3 = 12 entries.

**3. (a)** The completed table [2]:

| 1st \ 2nd | R | Y | B | G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | RR | RY | RB | RG |
| Y | YR | YY | YB | YG |
| B | BR | BY | BB | BG |
| G | GR | GY | GB | GG |

**(b)** The matching pairs RR, YY, BB, GG are 4 of 16, so **1/4** [1]
**(c)** Row R has 4 cells and column R adds 3 more, so 7 of 16: **7/16** [1]
**(d)** RB and BR are 2 of 16, so **1/8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Count each cell once: the RR cell must not be counted twice in part (c).

**4. (a)** A and B: mutually exclusive, because no number is both less than 3 and greater than 4 [1]. A and C: not mutually exclusive, because 1 is in both. B and C: not mutually exclusive, because 5 is in both [1]
**(b)** P(A or B) = 2/6 + 2/6 = 4/6 = **2/3** [1]
**(c)** Not exhaustive: the number 4 is in none of A, B or C [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming a number that sits in both events is a precise reason for "not mutually exclusive".

**5. (a)** The probabilities sum to 1: x + (x + 0.1) + 2x + 0.3 = 1 [1]. This gives 4x + 0.4 = 1 [1], so 4x = 0.6 and **x = 0.15** [1]
**(b)** P(3) = 2 × 0.15 = 0.3, so P(not 3) = 1 − 0.3 = **0.7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say why the equation holds ("the probabilities sum to 1"), then check by adding.

**6. (a)** P(bread or milk) = 1 − 0.25 = **0.75** [1]
**(b)** P(both) = 0.55 + 0.3 − 0.75 [1] = **0.1** [1]
**(c)** Milk but not bread = 0.3 − 0.1 = **0.2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Adding 0.55 + 0.3 to get 0.85 would leave the regions not summing to 1, so check the regions total 1.

**7. (a)** Art only = 15 − 2 − 3 − 4 = 6 [1]. Band only = 12 − 2 − 3 − 1 = 6 [1]. Chess only = 11 − 2 − 4 − 1 = 4 [1]
**(b)** The seven regions total 6 + 6 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 1 + 2 = 26 [1]. So 36 − 26 = 10 are in no club and P = 10/36 = **5/18** [1]
**(c)** Exactly two clubs: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8, so P = 8/36 = **2/9** [1]
**(d)** Art but not chess: art only (6) plus art and band only (3) = 9, so P = 9/36 = **1/4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each "only" value needs three subtractions. Totalling the regions first helps you catch a slip.

**8. (a)** Squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, ..., 36 (9 numbers). In both: 4, 16, 36 [1]. Square only: 1, 9, 25 [1]. Multiple of 4 only: 8, 12, 20, 24, 28, 32 [1]
**(b)** In at least one set: 3 + 3 + 6 = 12, so neither = 24 and P = 24/36 = **2/3** [1]
**(c)** Exactly one set: 3 + 6 = 9, so P = 9/36 = **1/4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* List each set's members first. The number 1 is a square and is easy to overlook.

**9. (a)** The completed table [2]:

| 1st \ 2nd | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
| 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |

**(b)** There are 9 outcomes [1]. Scores of 4 or less: 3, 4, so P(L) = **2/9**. Scores of 5 to 7: 5, 7, 6, 5, 7, so P(M) = **5/9** [1]. Scores of 8 or more: 8, 9, so P(N) = **2/9** [1]
**(c)** Every score is in exactly one group, so the events cannot overlap and cover everything. Also 2/9 + 5/9 + 2/9 = 1 [1]
**(d)** L and N are mutually exclusive, so P(L or N) = P(L) + P(N) [1] = 2/9 + 2/9 = **4/9** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, give both reasons: no overlap, and no score left out. The sum to 1 supports the claim but does not replace it.

**10. (a)** The regions are French only (24 − x), both (x), Spanish only (20 − x) and neither (9), totalling 50 [1]. So (24 − x) + x + (20 − x) + 9 = 50, which gives 53 − x = 50 [1], so **x = 3** [1]
**(b)** French only 21, both 3, Spanish only 17, neither 9 [1]
**(c)** P(French only) = **21/50** [1]
**(d)** Exactly one language: 21 + 17 = 38 [1], so P = 38/50 = **19/25** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Define the unknown on the diagram, then write the equation from the total.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing outcomes that are not equally likely, such as one outcome per colour.
- Losing one of a pair like (3, 5) and (5, 3), or including an impossible pair.
- Adding probabilities for events that overlap, or claiming "mutually exclusive" with no reason.
- Putting circle totals into the circles instead of the "only" and "both" values.
- Leaving out the "neither" region, or not checking that the regions total the group size.
- Giving a count (such as 21) when the question asks for a probability (such as 21/50).
