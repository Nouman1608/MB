## Questions

**1.** Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm of a cell. Describe the stages of glycolysis. **[4]**

**2.** A culture of yeast cells oxidises 45 molecules of glucose by glycolysis. Work out how many molecules are involved of each of these: ATP used, ATP made, net ATP gain, reduced NAD formed and pyruvate formed. **[5]**

**3.** A sprinter's leg muscle cells run short of oxygen. Explain how the formation of lactate allows the muscle cells to go on making some ATP. **[4]**

**4.** Compare the formation of ethanol in yeast with the formation of lactate in muscle. **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** State how pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the link reaction. **[3]**

**6.** A cell passes 18 molecules of acetyl CoA through the Krebs cycle.
**(a)** Calculate the number of CO₂, reduced NAD, reduced FAD and ATP molecules produced. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the process that makes the ATP in the Krebs cycle. **[1]**

**7.** Explain how the electron transfer chain and ATP synthase work together to make ATP in a mitochondrion. **[6]**

**8.** A chemical makes the inner mitochondrial membrane leaky to protons. Predict and explain the effect on:
**(a)** the rate of ATP synthesis **[2]**
**(b)** oxygen uptake **[2]**
**(c)** the energy released by the electron transfer chain. **[1]**

**9.** An 18-carbon fatty acid is broken down completely into two-carbon units.
**(a)** Calculate the number of acetyl CoA molecules formed and the number of reduced NAD produced when they enter the Krebs cycle. **[2]**
**(b)** State the molecule formed from the fatty acid units that enters the Krebs cycle. **[1]**
**(c)** State where an amino acid enters respiration after its amino group is removed. **[1]**

**10.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on the respiration of yeast in a glucose solution. CO₂ was collected in a gas syringe for 4 minutes. Each temperature was repeated three times.

| Temperature (°C) | CO₂ volume (cm³) |
|---|---|
| 15 | 3.0, 3.4, 3.2 |
| 25 | 7.1, 7.5, 7.3 |
| 35 | 14.8, 15.2, 14.6 |
| 45 | 11.0, 19.9, 10.6 |
| 55 | 1.2, 1.0, 1.4 |

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result and calculate the mean at 45 °C without it. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate of CO₂ production at 35 °C in cm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe and explain the trend from 15 °C to 55 °C. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest one improvement to the method. **[1]**

**11.** Assume each reduced NAD gives 2.5 ATP at the electron transfer chain and each reduced FAD gives 1.5 ATP.
**(a)** Calculate the total ATP made per glucose in aerobic respiration, given 10 reduced NAD, 2 reduced FAD and 4 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation. **[3]**
**(b)** A cell needs 640 ATP molecules. Calculate the glucose needed aerobically and anaerobically. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why anaerobic respiration gives much less ATP per glucose. **[1]**

**12.** A mutation stops ATP synthase working in the muscle cells of a patient, while the electron transfer chain stays intact. Explain the likely effects on:
**(a)** the proton gradient across the inner membrane **[2]**
**(b)** the link reaction and Krebs cycle **[3]**
**(c)** the muscle cells' ATP supply and the pyruvate produced. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Phosphorylation of glucose to glucose phosphate using ATP [1]. Production of two triose phosphate molecules [1]. Oxidation of triose phosphate to pyruvate [1], with a net gain of ATP and reduced NAD [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "describe" answer scores best when the stages are in order and each names its key molecule. Writing only "glucose splits to pyruvate" leaves out the points the question is looking for.

**2.** ATP used: 45 × 2 = **90** [1]. ATP made: 45 × 4 = **180** [1]. Net ATP: 180 − 90 = **90** [1]. Reduced NAD: 45 × 2 = **90** [1]. Pyruvate: 45 × 2 = **90** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each multiplication. Writing the "used" and "made" values separately shows your method clearly if a single number slips.

**3.** Without oxygen, reduced NAD cannot be reoxidised at the electron transfer chain [1]. Pyruvate accepts hydrogen from reduced NAD [1] and is converted to lactate [1]. NAD is regenerated and can be used in glycolysis, which continues to make ATP [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link NAD supply to glycolysis continuing. Stating only that "lactate is made" or that it "makes ATP" misses the central idea.

**4.** Ethanol formation releases CO₂ (via ethanal) but lactate formation does not [1]. Both use reduced NAD to convert pyruvate [1]. Both regenerate NAD so glycolysis can continue [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "compare" answer needs at least one similarity and one difference stated clearly. Two separate descriptions without a link back to each other are weaker.

**5. (a)** Active transport [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Pyruvate is oxidised to acetate [1], producing reduced NAD [1]. Acetate combines with coenzyme A to form acetyl CoA [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "oxidised" and "reduced NAD". "Broken down" is too vague to show you know what the link reaction does.

**6. (a)** CO₂: 18 × 2 = **36** [1]. Reduced NAD: 18 × 3 = **54** [1]. Reduced FAD: **18** [1]. ATP: **18** [1].
**(b)** Substrate-level phosphorylation. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out the per-turn figure first and then multiply. If a calculation is wrong, written per-turn values show where the error began.

**7.** Reduced NAD and reduced FAD give up electrons to the electron transfer chain [1]. Electrons pass along carriers in oxidation-reduction reactions, releasing energy [1]. The energy drives proton pumping out of the matrix and across the inner membrane [1]. A proton concentration gradient forms across the inner membrane [1]. Protons move back into the matrix via ATP synthase [1], and the flow drives synthesis of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name which side the protons are pumped to and what moves through ATP synthase. Saying "the electrons make ATP" is a common way to lose these marks.

**8. (a)** ATP synthesis falls [1] because the proton gradient is not maintained, so fewer protons pass through ATP synthase [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Oxygen uptake rises [1] because the chain keeps running and oxidising reduced coenzymes, with electrons still being passed to oxygen [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The energy is released as heat rather than used to make ATP. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "predict and explain" question needs both a direction and a reason. Prediction alone, such as "ATP falls", is not enough.

**9. (a)** Acetyl CoA: 18 ÷ 2 = **9** [1]. Reduced NAD in the Krebs cycle: 9 × 3 = **27** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Acetyl CoA. [1]
**(c)** It enters as a Krebs cycle intermediate or as acetyl CoA. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Read what the calculation asks for. The Krebs cycle count excludes reduced coenzymes made earlier while breaking down the fatty acid.

**10. (a)** 19.9 cm³ is anomalous because it is far from the other two results [1]. Mean = (11.0 + 10.6) ÷ 2 = **10.8 cm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mean at 35 °C = (14.8 + 15.2 + 14.6) ÷ 3 = 14.87 [1]. Rate = 14.87 ÷ 4 = **3.7 cm³ per min** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Rate rises from 15 °C to 35 °C [1] because enzymes and substrate have more kinetic energy, so enzyme-substrate complexes form more often [1]. At 45 °C and 55 °C it falls as enzymes denature, with the rate near zero at 55 °C [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Use a water bath to keep the temperature constant, or take more repeats. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the anomaly's value and the reason for excluding it. For the trend, quote figures from the table, since a general statement about "enzymes" scores less.

**11. (a)** Reduced NAD: 10 × 2.5 = 25 [1]. Reduced FAD: 2 × 1.5 = 3 [1]. Total: 25 + 3 + 4 = **32 ATP** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Aerobic: 640 ÷ 32 = **20** glucose [1]. Anaerobic: 640 ÷ 2 = **320** glucose [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Only glycolysis makes ATP, as the ethanol or lactate step makes none and there is no oxidative phosphorylation. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the ATP contribution of each type of coenzyme before adding. Marks are easier to earn from clear working than from a bare total.

**12. (a)** Protons cannot flow back through ATP synthase [1], so the gradient builds up until pumping is opposed and slows [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The chain slows, so reduced NAD and reduced FAD are not oxidised [1]. NAD and FAD run short [1], so the link reaction and Krebs cycle slow down [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** ATP supply falls to the small amount from glycolysis and substrate-level phosphorylation [1]. Pyruvate cannot be fully oxidised and accumulates [1], so is converted to lactate to regenerate NAD [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-step "explain" question, chain the effects in order: ATP synthase, then gradient, then reduced coenzymes, then the earlier stages. Jumping to the end loses the middle steps.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving glycolysis a yield of 2 ATP without the 4 made and 2 used.
- Using two carbons for pyruvate or three for acetate.
- Forgetting that every count doubles for each glucose, because two pyruvate form.
- Saying lactate or ethanol formation makes ATP, rather than regenerating NAD.
- Stating that oxygen is used in the Krebs cycle.
- Saying protons are pumped by ATP synthase or that electrons flow through it.
- Describing a trend without figures from the data.
- Calculating a mean that includes an anomalous result.
- Writing rates without units such as cm³ per min.
