This page is a guide to OCR AS Level Economics (H060) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and for June 2025 and June 2024 a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from OCR's examiner reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's own site or its teacher platform.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR AS Level Economics (H060) qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/economics-h060-h460-from-2019/)
- [OCR AS Level Economics assessment page, where examiner reports are listed](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/economics-h060-h460-from-2019/assessment/)
- [OCR past paper finder](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/past-paper-finder/)
- [OCR grade boundaries page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/)

OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder, and grade boundaries on the page above. Examiner reports for each series are listed on the assessment page for the qualification. The full question papers and mark schemes that the reports refer to are held on Teach Cambridge, OCR's teacher site, so ask your teacher or exams officer if you cannot open them.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the examiner report for that paper: it tells you why answers that looked right still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. H060 is a linear qualification with two compulsory written papers, each 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks and 50% of the AS, so 120 marks in total. Paper 1 (H060/01) is Microeconomics and Paper 2 (H060/02) is Macroeconomics. Both papers have the same format: Section A is 15 multiple-choice questions (15 marks), Section B is data response (25 marks), and Section C is one 20-mark extended essay chosen from two titles. The examiner reports we read for June 2024 and June 2025 describe the same qualification, and OCR runs one examination series a year, in May and June. Our assessment records for this qualification give a first-assessment year of 2016, but the covers of the reports we read say the qualification is for first teaching in 2019. We have flagged that for review and you should confirm the dates on OCR's qualification page.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe strong command of core ideas in the multiple-choice and data sections, with the extended answers separating the grades. On both papers, the best scripts picked the evidence they needed from the case material, drew labelled diagrams and then actually used the diagrams in the written argument. The weaker scripts knew a great deal but did not shape it to the question set.

**Where marks were lost – H060/01 (Microeconomics)**

- Multiple choice: the demand curve and revenue, the factors that move a demand curve, cross elasticity and the effects of government failure caused the most errors. Some answers were not written clearly enough to be credited.
- Data response: growth in a data series was judged by comparing the size of bars instead of working out a percentage change, and some figures were rounded the wrong way or more than one answer was given.
- Diagrams: axes were labelled with the wrong term for a market diagram, and some diagrams were small or unclear. Some candidates ignored an instruction to draw one, or drew a correct diagram and never referred to it.
- Instructions to use given information were sometimes ignored, so an answer explained a general effect rather than the one the data pointed to.
- Evaluation answer: weaker scripts wrote about the business only when the question asked about the effect on people in a particular country, and evaluation was less developed than analysis.
- Essays: surplus questions were mostly handled with a diagram, but some answers confused consumer and producer surplus, and answers that noted the importance of elasticity without explaining why stayed in the lowest level. On the production possibilities essay, weaker scripts used the diagram without linking it to the effect on consumers, and did not separate short-run from long-run effects.

**Where marks were lost – H060/02 (Macroeconomics)**

- Multiple choice: unemployment and some graph-reading calculations were hard, and a misreading of what the question asked was suspected on one item.
- Terminology: interest rates and exchange rates were confused, and microeconomic labels were put on macroeconomic diagrams.
- Calculation: some candidates did not know how to find a real wage change from a nominal wage and inflation.
- Policy explanation: candidates named a supply-side policy but did not explain how it would help the producers in the case study.
- Prepared answers: many candidates had expected a question on unemployment and tried to bend a rehearsed answer to the question set, so the link from inflation to the economic agent in the question was thin.
- Essays: strong scripts took the word sustainable seriously and weighed short-term gains against longer-term costs; weaker scripts confused sustainable development with development indicators. The essay on a loss of confidence in the economy was often answered without focusing on confidence at all, and evaluation was the weaker part of most essays.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports have the same message: precise terminology, labelled diagrams that are referred to in the writing, and answers that stay on the question set. The reports single out prepared material as the main way strong candidates lost marks in the essays.

**Where marks were lost – H060/01 (Microeconomics)**

- Multiple choice: composite demand, free goods, maximum prices and elasticity of supply were the difficult areas, and unclear answer changes lost some marks.
- Data response: most candidates read data well, but a few gave a figure outside the accepted range for a value read from a graph.
- Diagrams: some candidates labelled a market diagram with the wrong axis terms, and some drew bar charts where an economic diagram was wanted.
- Relationships between goods were sometimes confused, and a question about the link between a crop and a product made from it was often answered without noticing that link.
- Evaluation answer: the strongest scripts separated the effects on different groups of producers and used two diagrams; weaker ones repeated an earlier answer with little development.
- Essays: candidates who had expected a market failure essay tried to adapt it to a different title. The incentives essay was the least popular and was usually a list of statements with no chain of reasoning. In the farming essay, evaluation often discussed the cost of the policy instead of how well it worked, and some answers drifted into buffer stocks or market failure when the question was about something else.

**Where marks were lost – H060/02 (Macroeconomics)**

- Multiple choice: causes of inflation, labour markets, short-run aggregate supply and calculations of nominal values were weaker.
- Data response: trade and the Human Development Index were not well understood. Some candidates confused a trade deficit with a budget deficit, or wrote that a currency "fell" instead of using the correct term, and a number could not draw an exchange rate diagram, using an aggregate demand and supply diagram instead.
- Currency direction: some answers showed the wrong currency appreciating.
- Circular flow: candidates picked suitable data but often did not state that the overall flow would fall.
- Evaluation answer: a phrase about the same period a year earlier led some candidates to write about the cause of rising unemployment rather than its effects.
- Essays: many candidates had prepared answers on government borrowing and interest rates and did not tie them to the question. Candidates who chose a production possibilities diagram for the government spending essay struggled to build a two-sided argument, and some candidates found it hard to choose a suitable diagram for the second essay.

## What examiners keep saying

Across the two series with reports, the same points come up on both papers.

- Use precise economic terms. Interest rates and exchange rates, trade deficits and budget deficits, and consumer and producer surplus are all pairs that were mixed up.
- Draw the diagram, label it correctly (price and quantity on a market diagram, price level and real output on a macroeconomic one) and refer to it in your written analysis. A good diagram that is never used does not earn the higher levels.
- Use the data and the stem. Answers that pick figures from the case material and apply them score better than general statements.
- Answer the question set. Prepared essays on unemployment, market failure, government borrowing or money supply were the commonest way for knowledgeable candidates to lose marks.
- Evaluate, do not just list. Strong answers weigh both sides and reach a justified decision.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR AS Level Economics (H060) past papers?**
OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder. Grade boundaries are on OCR's grade boundaries page, and examiner reports are listed on the assessment page for the qualification.

**Which H060 papers should I sit?**
Both. Paper 1 (Microeconomics) and Paper 2 (Macroeconomics) are compulsory, each 60 marks and 1 hour 30 minutes.

**How are the grade thresholds used?**
The overall grade comes from adding the two paper marks, out of 120, and comparing the total with that series' boundaries. The paper rows show how each paper performed on its own.

**What mistakes do H060 examiners report most often?**
Mixing up paired terms such as interest rates and exchange rates, mislabelled or unused diagrams, prepared essays that do not fit the title, and evaluation that is thin or off the point.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
