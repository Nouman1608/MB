It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. ## Questions

**1.** Give the reagent and the condition used to convert ethanol into ethene in the laboratory. **[2]**

**2.** Propan-1-ol is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**3.** Outline the mechanism for the elimination of water from 2-methylpropan-2-ol, (CH₃)₃COH, in the presence of an acid catalyst. **[4]**

**4.** Hexan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid. Name all the alkenes that could form. **[4]**

**5.** 2-methylpentan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, undergoes acid-catalysed elimination.

**(a)** Name the two alkenes formed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why neither alkene shows E/Z isomerism. **[1]**

**6.** From butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, cyclohexanol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol, identify every alcohol that gives only one alkene on elimination. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why methanol does not form an alkene when heated with concentrated sulfuric acid. **[1]**

**8.** A secondary alcohol with formula C₅H₁₂O gives only two alkenes on elimination: 3-methylbut-1-ene and 2-methylbut-2-ene. Identify the alcohol and explain how the products support your answer. **[2]**

**9.** The hydration of an alkene and the elimination of water from an alcohol both use an acid catalyst. State how the conditions are chosen so that elimination happens rather than hydration. **[2]**

**10.** A company plans to make poly(propene) from propan-1-ol produced from plant material.

**(a)** Give the reagent for converting propan-1-ol into propene. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the type of polymerisation used to make poly(propene) from propene. **[1]**

**(c)** Give one environmental advantage of this route over making propene from crude oil. **[1]**

**11.** In Required practical 5, cyclohexanol (boiling point about 161 °C) is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid and the cyclohexene (boiling point about 83 °C) is distilled off.

**(a)** Explain why the cyclohexene can be distilled from the reaction mixture as it forms. **[2]**

**(b)** State which layer in the separating funnel contains the cyclohexene, and give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why anti-bumping granules are added to the flask. **[1]**

**(d)** Describe a test to show that the product is an alkene. **[1]**

**12.** 8.00 g of cyclohexanol (Mᵣ = 100.0) produces 4.43 g of pure cyclohexene (Mᵣ = 82.0).

**(a)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest two reasons why the yield is less than 100%. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Concentrated sulfuric acid or concentrated phosphoric acid [1]; heat [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O** [1]

**(b)** **Propene** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Arrow from a lone pair on O to H⁺, giving (CH₃)₃C–OH₂⁺ [1]; arrow from the C–O bond to O, water leaves [1]; tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₃C⁺ shown with its charge [1]; arrow from a C–H bond on a methyl group to the C–C bond, forming 2-methylpropene and releasing H⁺ [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The third arrow must start at the C–H bond, not at the H atom; drawing it from the H loses the last mark.

**4.** Hex-2-ene: **(E)-hex-2-ene** [1] and **(Z)-hex-2-ene** [1]. Hex-3-ene: **(E)-hex-3-ene** [1] and **(Z)-hex-3-ene** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The two neighbouring carbons, C2 and C4, are different, so two positions of C=C are possible, and each alkene has two different groups on both carbons of the double bond.

**5. (a)** **2-methylpent-1-ene** [1] and **2-methylpent-2-ene** [1]

**(b)** In each alkene one carbon of the C=C carries two identical groups (two H atoms on C1 of 2-methylpent-1-ene; two methyl groups on C2 of 2-methylpent-2-ene) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Butan-1-ol, cyclohexanol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol for two marks; any two of these with no incorrect alcohol for one mark. Butan-2-ol gives three alkenes. **[2]**

**7.** Methanol has only one carbon atom, so there is no neighbouring carbon to lose an H and form a C=C bond [1]. **[1]**

**8.** **3-methylbutan-2-ol**, (CH₃)₂CHCH(OH)CH₃ [1]. Loss of H from the CH₃ on C1 gives 3-methylbut-1-ene, and loss of H from C3 gives 2-methylbut-2-ene; neither has E/Z isomers, so only two alkenes form [1]. **[2]**

**9.** For elimination, concentrated acid is used with little water present [1], and the alkene is removed (distilled off) as it forms; hydration instead uses excess steam [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Concentrated phosphoric acid (or concentrated sulfuric acid), heated [1]

**(b)** **Addition polymerisation** [1]

**(c)** Uses a renewable raw material, so it saves crude oil (or can lower net CO₂ emissions) [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Cyclohexene has a much lower boiling point than cyclohexanol [1], so at the temperature of the flask it vaporises and distils over while cyclohexanol remains [1]

**(b)** The upper layer, because cyclohexene is less dense than water and immiscible with it [1]

**(c)** They allow smooth boiling and prevent large bubbles forming violently (bumping) [1]

**(d)** Shake with bromine water: it changes from orange to colourless [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** n(cyclohexanol) = 8.00 ÷ 100.0 = 0.0800 mol [1]; n(cyclohexene) = 4.43 ÷ 82.0 = 0.0540 mol [1]; yield = 0.0540 ÷ 0.0800 × 100 = **67.5%** [1]

**(b)** Any two: incomplete reaction [1]; product lost during transfers or left in the separating funnel [1]; some product absorbed on the drying agent or left in the flask after redistillation. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing the masses (4.43 ÷ 8.00) gives 55.4%; always convert to moles first.

## Where marks are usually lost

- "Acid" without "concentrated".
- Protonation arrow drawn the wrong way.
- Missing E/Z isomers, or claiming them where a carbon has two identical groups.
- Placing the organic product in the lower layer.
- Percentage yield from masses rather than moles.
