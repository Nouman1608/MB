These questions cover subtopic 1.2 Sets in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 8 use Core content (C1.2) and suit every candidate. Questions 9 to 12 are labelled **(Extended)**: they use E1.2 notation (∈, ∉, ∅, ⊆, ⊈), three-set diagrams or sets of points. All questions are non-calculator. Venn diagrams are described in words; sketch them as you work.

Learn the methods first in the [sets study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-sets/) and the [sets revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-sets-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) ℰ = {x: x is an integer, 10 ⩽ x ⩽ 20}, E = {even numbers} and F = {multiples of 5}.

**(a)** List the elements of E ∩ F. **[1]**

**(b)** Find n(E ∪ F). **[1]**

**(c)** List the elements of (E ∪ F)′. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) List the elements of {x: x is an integer, −2 < x ⩽ 3} and write down the number of elements. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Use set notation to describe the elements that are

**(a)** in B but not in A **[1]**

**(b)** in neither A nor B. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) n(ℰ) = 50, n(A) = 31, n(B) = 24 and n(A ∩ B) = 13. Find n((A ∪ B)′). **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Explain why the set {x: 2 < x < 3} cannot be written as a list. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) L = {letters in the word MISSISSIPPI}. List the elements of L and find n(L). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) In a class of 45 students, 22 study French and 19 study Spanish. The number who study neither language is twice the number who study both. Let x be the number who study both.

**(a)** Write expressions in x for the number in each of the four regions of a Venn diagram. **[2]**

**(b)** Form and solve an equation to find x. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the number of students who study exactly one of the languages. **[1]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A two-set Venn diagram has regions "A only", "both", "B only" and "outside". State which regions make up

**(a)** A′ ∩ B′ **[1]**

**(b)** (A ∩ B)′ **[1]**

**(c)** A ∪ B′. **[1]**

**9.** (Extended) (non-calculator) ℰ = {x: x is an integer, 1 ⩽ x ⩽ 12}, A = {factors of 12} and B = {odd numbers}.

**(a)** List the elements of A ∩ B. **[1]**

**(b)** Is the statement 9 ∈ A true or false? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Is {1, 3} ⊆ A true or false? **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why B ⊈ A. **[1]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) 80 tourists were asked which of a castle (C), a museum (M) and a park (P) they visited. 38 visited the castle, 35 the museum and 30 the park. 15 visited the castle and the museum, 12 the museum and the park, and 10 the castle and the park. 7 visited all three.

**(a)** Find the number of tourists in each of the seven regions inside the circles. **[4]**

**(b)** Find how many tourists visited none of the three. **[1]**

**(c)** Find how many tourists visited exactly two of the three. **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A = {(x, y): y = 2x + 3}, B = {(x, y): x + y = 9} and C = {(x, y): y = 2x − 4}.

**(a)** Find A ∩ B. **[2]**

**(b)** Find A ∩ C, explaining your answer. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down n(B ∩ C). **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) ℰ = {x: x is an integer, 1 ⩽ x ⩽ 30}, D = {multiples of 6} and T = {multiples of 4}.

**(a)** Describe D ∩ T in words. **[1]**

**(b)** Find n(D ∩ T). **[1]**

**(c)** W = {multiples of 12}. Use set notation to describe how W is related to D, and say how this would be shown on a Venn diagram. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** {10, 20} [1]

**(b)** E = {10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20}, F = {10, 15, 20}; n(E ∪ F) = 6 + 3 − 2 = **7** [1]

**(c)** {11, 13, 17, 19} [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b) the answer is a number, not a list; in (c) it is a list in curly brackets.

**2.** {−1, 0, 1, 2, 3} [1]; **5** elements [1]

*Examiner insight:* −2 < x excludes −2, but x ⩽ 3 includes 3. Zero is an integer and must be listed.

**3. (a)** **A′ ∩ B** [1]

**(b)** **(A ∪ B)′** (or A′ ∩ B′) [1]

**4.** n(A ∪ B) = 31 + 24 − 13 = 42 [1]; n((A ∪ B)′) = 50 − 42 = **8** [1]

**5.** It contains every number between 2 and 3, such as 2.1 and 2.75, and there are infinitely many of them [1].

**6.** **{m, i, s, p}** [1]; n(L) = **4** [1]

**7. (a)** French only 22 − x; both x; Spanish only 19 − x [1]; neither 2x [1]

**(b)** (22 − x) + x + (19 − x) + 2x = 45, so 41 + x = 45 [1]; **x = 4** [1]

**(c)** (22 − 4) + (19 − 4) = 18 + 15 = **33** [1]

*Examiner insight:* writing 22 in the "French only" region is the usual slip; check the four regions (18, 4, 15, 8) add up to 45.

**8. (a)** Outside only [1]

**(b)** A only, B only and outside [1]

**(c)** A only, both and outside [1]

**9. (a)** A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}, so A ∩ B = **{1, 3}** [1]

**(b)** False: 9 is not a factor of 12 [1]

**(c)** True [1]

**(d)** For example, 5 ∈ B but 5 ∉ A [1]

*Examiner insight:* (d) needs a named element; "not all odd numbers are factors of 12" without an example is usually not enough.

**10. (a)** Centre 7; castle and museum only 15 − 7 = 8; museum and park only 12 − 7 = 5; castle and park only 10 − 7 = 3 [1] [1]; castle only 38 − 8 − 3 − 7 = 20 [1]; museum only 35 − 8 − 5 − 7 = 15 and park only 30 − 5 − 3 − 7 = 15 [1]

**(b)** 80 − (7 + 8 + 5 + 3 + 20 + 15 + 15) = 80 − 73 = **7** [1]

**(c)** 8 + 5 + 3 = **16** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the pair totals include the centre, so the 7 must be removed before the "only" regions are found.

**11. (a)** 2x + 3 = 9 − x, so x = 2 [1]; y = 7, so A ∩ B = **{(2, 7)}** [1]

**(b)** A and C both have gradient 2 and different intercepts, so the lines are parallel and never meet: A ∩ C = **∅** [1]

**(c)** **1** (the lines have different gradients, so they meet at exactly one point) [1]

**12. (a)** Multiples of 12 (numbers that are multiples of both 6 and 4) [1]

**(b)** {12, 24}, so n(D ∩ T) = **2** [1]

**(c)** **W ⊆ D** [1]; circle W is drawn entirely inside circle D [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a list when a number is asked for (n(A)), or a number when a list is asked for.
- Including an endpoint excluded by < or leaving out zero from a list of integers.
- Putting a whole-set total in the "only" region of a Venn diagram.
- Forgetting the outside region when checking a total.
- (Extended) Using ∈ between two sets, or writing {0} for the empty set.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do Core candidates need ∈ and ⊆?**
No. In 0580, ∈, ∉, ∅, ⊆ and ⊈ appear only in the Extended notation list. Core uses ℰ, n(A), A′, ∪ and ∩.
