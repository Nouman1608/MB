## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Independent variable | The factor you deliberately change |
| Dependent variable | The factor you measure to see the effect |
| Controlled variable | A factor kept constant so that the test is fair |
| Control experiment | A set-up that shows the effect is due to the independent variable |
| Repeat | Doing the same measurement again so that a mean can be calculated and anomalies seen |
| Pilot study | A short trial to check range, timing and apparatus |
| Range | The lowest to the highest value of the independent variable |
| Interval | The gap between successive values of the independent variable |
| Resolution | The smallest change a measuring instrument can show |

## Design checklist: 1.1.1(a)

1. State the question in terms of what is changed and what is measured.
2. Name the **independent**, **dependent** and **controlled** variables.
3. Choose a **range** and at least **five** values, evenly spaced.
4. Plan **three or more repeats** and a mean for each value.
5. Choose apparatus for the measurement and give reasons.
6. Write steps in order with **numerical values** (volumes, times, temperatures).
7. State a **control** where one is needed.
8. State **hazards** and a way to reduce each risk.
9. State what you will calculate and how you will present it.

## Variables and how to control them

| Variable | How to hold it constant |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Thermostatic water bath; pre-warm solutions before mixing |
| pH | Buffer solutions of known pH |
| Volumes | Syringe or graduated pipette, the same volume each time |
| Concentration of enzyme or substrate | Same stock solution, measured with the same pipette |
| Size or mass of tissue | Same cork borer and ruler; one source organism |
| Time | Stopwatch; start and stop at the same point |
| Light intensity (photosynthesis) | Fixed lamp distance, heat shield such as a water tank between the lamp and the plant |

Uncontrollable variables (natural variation, room temperature) are reduced with large samples and means, and recorded.

## Apparatus choices

| Measurement | Better choice | Weaker choice |
|---|---|---|
| Gas volume | Gas syringe | Counting bubbles |
| Small liquid volume | Graduated pipette or syringe | Measuring cylinder |
| Constant temperature | Water bath | Beaker on a heater |
| Colour or turbidity | Colorimeter | Eyes |
| Change in mass | Balance to 0.01 g | Balance to 1 g |

## Dilution series

A series of 10 cm³ samples from a 100 % stock:

| Concentration | Stock / cm³ | Water / cm³ |
|---|---|---|
| 20 % | 2 | 8 |
| 40 % | 4 | 6 |
| 60 % | 6 | 4 |
| 80 % | 8 | 2 |
| 100 % | 10 | 0 |

The total volume must be the same in every tube. A twofold serial dilution halves the concentration at each step.

## Percentage change

percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100

A potato cylinder that goes from 2.40 g to 2.64 g has a change of +10.0 %. Percentages allow for different starting masses.

## Evaluating whether a method is appropriate: 1.1.1(c)

| Question | Good sign | Poor sign |
|---|---|---|
| Does it measure the dependent variable? | Numerical measurement | Judgement by eye |
| Is the range suitable? | Five or more values covering the key region | Two values |
| Are variables controlled? | Each one named and held constant | Several vary |
| Is the instrument precise enough? | Resolution much smaller than the change | Resolution larger than the change |
| Are there repeats? | Three or more, with a mean | One reading |

State the **effect on the results** and an **improvement** for every weakness.

## Writing good answers

- Put **values** in a plan: "5 cm³ of enzyme at 40 °C", not "some enzyme".
- Explain controls using the biology: pH affects the shape of the active site, so pH is held constant.
- Say how you will make sure the temperature is reached before the reaction starts.
- Use "because" in evaluation answers. Link the weakness to its effect on the results.

## Exam traps

- "Keep everything else the same" scores nothing.
- Changing two variables at once.
- Using a control that is not a fair comparison, for example a different volume.
- Using a weak measuring method (counting bubbles) when a better one is available.
- Forgetting repeats or a mean.
- Omitting a hazard or a risk reduction.
- Saying "make it more accurate" without saying how.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the independent, dependent and two controlled variables for an investigation of the effect of temperature on the rate of amylase breaking down starch.
2. Why is a water bath better than heating a beaker?
3. State two reasons for repeating each measurement.
4. How would you make 10 cm³ of a 30 % solution from a 100 % stock?
5. A potato cylinder of 3.10 g ends at 2.79 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass.
6. Why should the enzyme and substrate be warmed separately before mixing?
7. Give one improvement to a method that uses a measuring cylinder to measure 2 cm³ of enzyme.
8. What is the purpose of a control experiment containing boiled enzyme?

### Answers

1. Independent: temperature. Dependent: rate of starch breakdown (for example time for starch to disappear with iodine). Controlled, any two: pH, enzyme concentration, starch concentration, volumes, time of sampling.
2. It holds the temperature steady, so the temperature stays at the stated value for the whole experiment.
3. To calculate a mean, and to identify anomalies.
4. 3 cm³ of stock plus 7 cm³ of distilled water.
5. (2.79 − 3.10) ÷ 3.10 × 100 = −10.0 % (a decrease of 10.0 %).
6. So the reaction begins at the test temperature, not at the temperature of the room.
7. Use a syringe or graduated pipette, which has a smaller resolution.
8. It shows that substrate does not break down without active enzyme, so the result is due to the enzyme.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many values of the independent variable should a plan include?**
Five or more, evenly spaced, so a trend can be seen. The mark scheme for a given question may accept a smaller number if the context is limited.

**Do I need to name an exact piece of apparatus?**
Yes, if you can. "A gas syringe" is better than "something to collect gas". Give the volume or the resolution if you know it.

**What is the difference between a control variable and a control experiment?**
A controlled variable is a factor held constant in every test. A control experiment is an extra test set up to show that the effect is due to the independent variable.
