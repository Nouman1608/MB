## Section A

**1.** State which regression line should be used to estimate the value of x for a given value of
y. **[1]**

**2.** For a set of bivariate data, the regression line of y on x is y = 3.2x + 5 and the regression
line of x on y is x = 0.25y − 0.9. Find the mean point (x̄, ȳ). **[3]**

**3.** Using the lines in question 2, estimate the value of x when y = 12. **[2]**

**4.** A student says that the lines y = 2x + 1 and x = −0.3y + 4 are the y on x and x on y
regression lines of the same data set. Explain why the student must be wrong. **[2]**

**5.** For a group of phones, the regression line of x on y is x = −0.085y + 14.2, where x is the
battery life in hours and y is the screen brightness as a percentage.

**(a)** Interpret the value −0.085 in context. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the line to estimate the battery life at 60% brightness. **[1]**

**6.** For a data set, the regression lines of y on x and of x on y are the same line. Write down the
value or values that r could take. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A forester measured the age, x years, and trunk circumference, y cm, of ten trees of one
species.

| x (years) | 8 | 12 | 15 | 20 | 24 | 30 | 35 | 40 | 46 | 52 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y (cm) | 34 | 41 | 58 | 62 | 80 | 85 | 104 | 110 | 128 | 141 |

**(a)** Write down the value of Pearson's product-moment correlation coefficient, r. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the equation of the regression line of x on y. **[2]**

**(c)** A tree of this species has a circumference of 95 cm. Use your answer to part (b) to estimate
its age. **[2]**

**(d)** Comment on the reliability of your estimate in part (c). **[2]**

**8.** For a set of data, the regression line of y on x is y = 2.5x + k and the regression line of x
on y is x = 0.32y + 1.2. The mean of the y values is 20.

**(a)** Find x̄. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the value of k. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate x when y = 25. **[1]**

**9.** The altitude, x m, and the mean July temperature, y °C, were recorded at eight weather
stations.

| x (m) | 120 | 340 | 560 | 780 | 950 | 1200 | 1430 | 1700 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y (°C) | 24.1 | 23.0 | 20.9 | 20.2 | 18.4 | 17.1 | 15.3 | 13.6 |

**(a)** Find the regression line of x on y. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate, to the nearest metre, the altitude of a station with a mean July temperature of
15 °C. **[2]**

**(c)** A hiker uses the same line to estimate the altitude of a place with a mean July temperature
of 8 °C. Explain why this estimate is unreliable. **[1]**

**10.** For data set P, r = 0.98. For data set Q, r = 0.45. In each case a student rearranges the
y on x line to estimate x. Explain in which data set the student's estimate is likely to be further
from the estimate given by the x on y line. **[2]**

**11.** For a set of data with mean point (10, 40), the regression line of x on y has gradient
(dx/dy) equal to 0.15.

**(a)** Find the equation of the regression line of x on y in the form x = cy + d. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence estimate x when y = 46. **[1]**

**12.** Twelve students recorded the time, x minutes, they spent on homework and the time, y
minutes, they spent on social media one evening. The regression line of y on x is y = −0.62x + 98
and the regression line of x on y is x = −1.05y + 121.

**(a)** Find the mean point. **[3]**

**(b)** Estimate the homework time of a student who spent 60 minutes on social media. **[2]**

**(c)** Without calculating r, state whether the correlation is positive or negative. Give a
reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The regression line of x on y. [1] **[1]**

**2.** Both lines pass through the mean point, so substitute y = 3.2x + 5 into the x on y line [1]:
x = 0.25(3.2x + 5) − 0.9 = 0.8x + 0.35, so 0.2x = 0.35 and x̄ = 1.75 [1]. Then
ȳ = 3.2(1.75) + 5 = 10.6 [1]. Mean point (1.75, 10.6). **[3]**

**3.** Predicting x from y, so use the x on y line [1]: x = 0.25(12) − 0.9 = 2.1 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The two regression lines of one data set have gradients of the same sign as r [1]. These
gradients have opposite signs (2 is positive, −0.3 is negative), so they cannot come from the same
data [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** For each increase of 1 percentage point in screen brightness [1], the estimated battery
life decreases by 0.085 hours (about 5 minutes) [1].

**(b)** x = −0.085(60) + 14.2 = 9.1 hours [1]. **[3]**

**6.** r = 1 or r = −1 [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** r = 0.995 (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** With y as the independent list: x = 0.410y − 6.36 [1 for 0.410, 1 for −6.36].

**(c)** x = 0.40992 × 95 − 6.3566 [1] = 32.6 years (3 s.f.) [1].

**(d)** r is very close to 1, so the linear relationship is very strong [1]; 95 cm lies within the
measured circumferences of 34 to 141 cm, so this is interpolation [1]. The estimate is reliable.
**[7]**

**8. (a)** The x on y line passes through the mean point, so x̄ = 0.32(20) + 1.2 [1] = 7.6 [1].

**(b)** The y on x line also passes through (7.6, 20): 20 = 2.5(7.6) + k [1], so k = 20 − 19 = 1
[1].

**(c)** x = 0.32(25) + 1.2 = 9.2 [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** With y as the independent list: x = −148y + 3710 (3 s.f.) [1 for −148, 1 for 3710].

**(b)** x = −147.919… × 15 + 3706.56… [1] = 1488 m [1]. (Rearranging the y on x line gives about
1491 m, which is the wrong method.)

**(c)** 8 °C is outside the observed temperature range of 13.6 to 24.1 °C, so this is extrapolation
and the linear pattern may not continue [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Data set Q [1]. The weaker the correlation, the further apart the two regression lines are,
so rearranging the y on x line moves further from the x on y estimate when r = 0.45 than when
r = 0.98, where the lines almost coincide [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** x = 0.15y + d passes through (10, 40): 10 = 0.15(40) + d [1], so d = 4 and
x = 0.15y + 4 [1].

**(b)** x = 0.15(46) + 4 = 10.9 [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Substitute y = −0.62x + 98 into the x on y line [1]:
x = −1.05(−0.62x + 98) + 121 = 0.651x − 102.9 + 121 = 0.651x + 18.1, so 0.349x = 18.1 and
x̄ = 51.9 (3 s.f.) [1]. ȳ = −0.62(51.86…) + 98 = 65.8 (3 s.f.) [1]. Mean point (51.9, 65.8).

**(b)** Use the x on y line [1]: x = −1.05(60) + 121 = 58 minutes [1].

**(c)** Negative, because both regression lines have negative gradients, and these share the sign of
r [1]. **[6]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 to 6 check the facts that Paper 1 can test without data: which line to use, the mean
point, signs of gradients and the case r = ±1. Questions 7 and 9 are full GDC questions in two
contexts where predicting x from y is the natural direction (age from size, altitude from
temperature). Questions 8, 11 and 12 combine the mean point with a prediction, which is where
students most often reach for the wrong line.
