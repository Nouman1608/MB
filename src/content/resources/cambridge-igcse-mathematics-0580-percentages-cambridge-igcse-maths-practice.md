These questions cover subtopic 1.13 Percentages of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 9 use content common to Core and Extended. Questions 10 to 12 are labelled **(Extended)**: they use repeated percentage change and reverse percentages, which are Extended only. Give money answers to the nearest cent unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Find 35% of 260 kg. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 42 as a percentage of 56. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A television costs $480. In a sale, its price is reduced by 15%. Find the sale price. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) The population of a town rose from 24 600 to 27 060. Find the percentage increase. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A trader buys a chair for $150 and sells it for $123.

**(a)** Find the loss. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the loss as a percentage of the cost price. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) Rania invests $4500 at 2.4% per year simple interest. Find the total value of her investment after 6 years. **[2]**

**6.** (calculator) $2000 is invested at 3.5% per year compound interest. Find the interest earned after 4 years. **[3]**

**7.** (non-calculator) Kemi's weekly pay rises from $520 to $546.

**(a)** Find the percentage increase in her pay. **[2]**

**(b)** Write her new pay as a percentage of her old pay. **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** (calculator) A laptop has a cash price of $850. Leon buys it by paying a 20% deposit followed by 12 monthly payments of $62.50.

**(a)** Find the deposit. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the total amount Leon pays. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the extra amount he pays as a percentage of the cash price. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator) Sofia has $6000 to invest for 5 years.

- Bank A pays 4% per year simple interest.
- Bank B pays 3.8% per year compound interest.

Which bank gives the greater total after 5 years, and by how much? Show your working. **[4]**

**10.** (Extended) (calculator)

**(a)** A shop increases its prices by 25% and later reduces the new prices by 20%. Show that the prices return to their original values. **[2]**

**(b)** Another shop increases its prices by 20% and later reduces the new prices by 20%. Find the overall percentage change. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (calculator)

**(a)** A dealer sells a motorbike for $1053, making a profit of 17%. Find the cost price. **[2]**

**(b)** In a sale, all prices are reduced by 35%. A pair of boots costs $57.85 in the sale. Find the price before the sale. **[2]**

**12.** (Extended) (calculator) The value of a painting rises by 12% in the first year, falls by 5% in the second year and rises by 8% in the third year. At the end of the third year it is worth $14 364.

**(a)** Find the overall percentage change in value over the three years. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the value of the painting at the start of the first year. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.35 × 260 = **91 kg** [1]

**(b)** 42 ÷ 56 × 100 = **75%** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 15% of 480 = 72, or 480 × 0.85 [1]; **$408** [1] **[2]**

**3.** Increase = 2460 [1]; 2460 ÷ 24 600 × 100 = **10%** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** 150 − 123 = **$27** [1]

**(b)** 27 ÷ 150 [1] × 100 = **18%** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the cost price is the original amount, so divide by 150. Dividing by 123 gives 22.0% and loses the accuracy mark.

**5.** Interest = 4500 × 0.024 × 6 = $648 [1]; total = **$5148** [1] **[2]**

**6.** 2000 × 1.035⁴ [1] = 2295.046... = $2295.05 [1]; interest = **$295.05** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the question asks for the interest, so subtract the principal. $2295.05 alone earns the first two marks only.

**7. (a)** Increase = $26 [1]; 26 ÷ 520 × 100 = **5%** [1]

**(b)** 546 ÷ 520 × 100 = **105%** [1] **[3]**

**8. (a)** 0.2 × 850 = **$170** [1]

**(b)** 12 × 62.50 = $750 [1]; 170 + 750 = **$920** [1]

**(c)** Extra = 920 − 850 = $70 [1]; 70 ÷ 850 × 100 = **8.24%** (3 s.f.) [1] **[5]**

**9.** Bank A: interest = 6000 × 0.04 × 5 = $1200, total $7200 [1]; Bank B: 6000 × 1.038⁵ [1] = 7229.995... = $7230.00 [1]; **Bank B**, by **$30.00** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* compare totals, not rates. The lower compound rate wins here because each year's interest earns further interest.

**10. (a)** Overall multiplier = 1.25 × 0.8 [1] = 1, so the final price equals the original price [1]

**(b)** 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 [1]; a **4% decrease** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* in (a) a numerical check with one price, such as $100 → $125 → $100, also earns full marks, but the multiplier method shows it is true for every price.

**11. (a)** 1053 ÷ 1.17 [1] = **$900** [1]

**(b)** Sale price = 65% of the original, so 57.85 ÷ 0.65 [1] = **$89** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* in (b), 57.85 × 1.35 = $78.10 is the most common wrong answer. The 35% is a percentage of the original price, so you must divide by 0.65.

**12. (a)** 1.12 × 0.95 × 1.08 = 1.14912 [1]; an increase of **14.9%** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** 14 364 ÷ 1.14912 [1] = **$12 500** [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change, as in Questions 3, 4 and 7.
- Giving the total when the interest was asked for, as in Question 6, or the reverse.
- Treating 12 monthly payments as the whole cost and forgetting the deposit.
- Comparing interest rates instead of comparing the final totals.
- (Extended) Adding percentages for successive changes.
- (Extended) Multiplying by 1.35 instead of dividing by 0.65 in a reverse percentage.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the simple and compound interest formulas given in the 0580 exam?**
No. The syllabus says formulas are not given, so learn both methods: principal × rate × years for simple interest, and principal × multiplierⁿ for the compound total.

**Do Core candidates meet reverse percentages?**
No. Reverse percentages and repeated percentage change are Extended only in 0580.

**How should I write a percentage over 100%?**
As it is, for example 105% or 125%. It simply means the amount is larger than the original.
