This guide covers subtopic **3.2 Active transport** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to understand active transport as the movement of molecules or ions into or out of a cell through the cell membrane, from a region of their lower concentration to a region of their higher concentration (against a concentration gradient), using energy released during respiration, and to explain the importance of active transport in ion uptake by root hair cells.

This page sets out the definition clause by clause, compares active transport with diffusion, explains root hair cells in detail and shows how to handle data questions. ## The definition, clause by clause

**Active transport is the movement of molecules or ions into or out of a cell through the cell membrane, from a region of their lower concentration to a region of their higher concentration (against a concentration gradient), using energy released during respiration.**

Break it into four parts. A full-mark answer contains all four.

1. **Movement of molecules or ions** into or out of the cell, through the cell membrane. It is a movement across the membrane.
2. **From lower concentration to higher concentration.** The substance moves from where there is less of it to where there is more of it.
3. **Against the concentration gradient.** This is the opposite of diffusion, in which substances move down the gradient.
4. **Using energy from respiration.** The cell must supply the energy. Without respiration, active transport stops.

The extra detail that carrier (protein) molecules in the membrane pick up the particles and move them across is widely taught, but you do not need it to answer the syllabus statement. A definition that says "uses energy released by respiration" and "against a concentration gradient" earns the marks.

## Why diffusion is not enough

In diffusion, substances move down their gradient without energy from the cell. That works well when the cell needs a substance that is more concentrated outside the cell than inside. Sometimes a cell needs more of a substance than there is outside. Then diffusion would work in the wrong direction, because particles would leave the cell, not enter it. Active transport allows the cell to **build up a higher concentration** inside than outside.

| Feature | Diffusion | Active transport |
|---|---|---|
| Direction relative to the gradient | Down (high to low) | Against (low to high) |
| Energy from respiration | Not needed | Needed |
| Effect of stopping respiration | No effect | Stops |
| Cell membrane involved | Not always | Always |
| Result | Concentrations become equal | A concentration difference is built or kept |

## Ion uptake by root hair cells

Plants need mineral ions such as nitrate and magnesium. These are in the soil water, usually at a **very low concentration**. The concentration inside the root hair cells is higher. Diffusion alone would not move the ions into the cells, because that would be against the gradient. Instead, the root hair cells use **active transport** to take in the ions.

Features of root hair cells that make this work:

- A long, thin extension of the cell increases the **surface area** for taking up ions and water.
- Many **mitochondria** release energy by aerobic respiration to power the active transport.
- A thin cell wall and membrane keep the distance short.

### Worked example: how much higher is the concentration inside?

*The nitrate concentration in soil water is 0.02 millimoles per dm cubed (mmol per dm cubed). In a root hair cell it is 0.50 mmol per dm cubed. How many times higher is the concentration in the cell? What does this show?*

1. Divide the cell concentration by the soil concentration: 0.50 divided by 0.02 = 25.
2. The cell concentration is **25 times higher** than that of the soil water.
3. Nitrate must therefore enter against a concentration gradient. Diffusion would move it out of the cell. This shows that the cell takes in nitrate by **active transport**, using energy from respiration.

### Why the ions matter

The ions taken up by active transport are used in the plant. Nitrate ions are used to make amino acids and so proteins, and magnesium ions are used to make chlorophyll. These uses are covered in subtopic 6.3. Without active transport, a plant growing in poor soil could not collect enough of them.

## Evidence that ion uptake uses energy

Several observations show that root hair cells use active transport.

- If roots are given **less oxygen**, aerobic respiration slows down, and ion uptake falls.
- If a **respiratory poison** is added, the uptake stops.
- A **lower temperature** slows the enzyme reactions of respiration, and so reduces ion uptake.
- Uptake carries on **against** the concentration gradient, which diffusion cannot do.

### Worked example: interpreting oxygen data

*Roots were placed in an ion solution. In air, ion uptake was 8.4 units per hour. In nitrogen gas (no oxygen), uptake was 1.2 units per hour. Calculate the percentage fall in uptake and explain it.*

1. Fall in uptake = 8.4 - 1.2 = 7.2 units per hour.
2. Percentage fall = fall divided by original x 100 = 7.2 divided by 8.4 x 100 = 85.7 per cent.
3. **Explanation**: without oxygen, aerobic respiration cannot release much energy. Active transport needs this energy, so uptake falls. The small remaining uptake (1.2 units) may be due to some diffusion or to anaerobic respiration.

## Where else do cells use active transport?

The syllabus specifies root hair cells, but active transport also occurs in other places. In subtopic 8.3 you will meet the absorption of nutrients in the small intestine, where **absorption** takes place by diffusion, osmosis and active transport. Glucose can be absorbed by active transport when its concentration in the gut falls below its concentration in the blood. The same logic applies: moving against the gradient requires energy from respiration, so the cells concerned contain many mitochondria.

## Diffusion, osmosis and active transport side by side

Questions often ask you to name the process in a given situation. Use these three questions to decide.

1. **Is the substance water, crossing a partially permeable membrane?** If so, the process is osmosis, which moves water from higher to lower water potential.
2. **Is the substance moving from high to low concentration with no energy from the cell?** If so, the process is diffusion.
3. **Is the substance moving from low to high concentration, and does the cell use energy from respiration?** If so, the process is active transport.

| Situation | Process | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen moves from the air in the alveoli into the blood | Diffusion | Down a gradient, no energy needed |
| Water moves into a root hair cell from damp soil | Osmosis | Water, higher to lower water potential |
| Nitrate enters a root hair cell from soil at a lower concentration | Active transport | Against the gradient, uses energy |
| Carbon dioxide leaves a respiring muscle cell | Diffusion | Down a gradient |

## Writing a full-mark answer on root hair cells

*Explain why root hair cells need active transport to take up nitrate ions.* A strong answer links four ideas in order.

1. The concentration of nitrate in the soil water is lower than inside the root hair cell.
2. Diffusion moves substances down a concentration gradient, so it would not bring nitrate into the cell.
3. Active transport moves ions against the gradient, from low to high concentration.
4. It uses energy released by respiration, which is why root hair cells have many mitochondria.

Notice that every sentence either states a fact about concentration or gives a reason. Examiners reward the links between the sentences as well as the facts, so words such as "because", "so" and "therefore" are worth including.

## Common mistakes

- Saying active transport "uses energy" with no reference to **respiration**.
- Saying that active transport moves substances "from high to low concentration".
- Writing that diffusion cannot happen in root hair cells. Water enters by osmosis and some ions can enter by diffusion when their concentration is higher outside.
- Saying that active transport only happens in plants. It occurs in all living cells.
- Writing "active transport makes energy". Respiration **releases** energy and active transport **uses** it.
- Forgetting the reason for mitochondria in a root hair cell.

## Command words

- **Define**: give the precise meaning of active transport.
- **State**: give the key point with no explanation, for example one use of the energy.
- **Explain**: give the reason, for example why root hair cells have many mitochondria.
- **Compare**: give similarities and differences between active transport and diffusion.
