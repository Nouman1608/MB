These questions cover sub-topic 5.4 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): applying the scientific method, random and systematic sampling, and the seven named sampling techniques. All data is invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a *hypothesis*. **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between a sampling strategy and a sampling technique. **[2]**

**3.** Name the sampling technique best suited to each organism:
(a) beetles moving across a woodland floor at night; **[1]**
(b) grasshoppers in long grass; **[1]**
(c) a herd of elephants on open grassland. **[1]**

**4.** State the purpose of a pilot study. **[1]**

**5.** Describe how a pooter is used. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student investigates whether the number of daisy plants changes with distance from a school building.
(a) Identify the independent and dependent variables. **[2]**
(b) Explain why systematic sampling is more suitable than random sampling for this investigation. **[2]**
(c) State **two** control variables. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the number of woodlice found under ten stones in each of two gardens.

| Garden | Counts under each stone |
|---|---|
| P | 3, 5, 4, 6, 2, 4, 5, 3, 4, 4 |
| Q | 7, 8, 6, 9, 31, 8, 7, 6, 8, 7 |

(a) Identify the anomaly. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the mean for garden P, and the mean for garden Q excluding the anomaly. Show your working. **[3]**
(c) Suggest **one** reason for the anomaly. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how random sampling with quadrats would be carried out in a meadow. **[3]**

**9.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of pitfall traps for measuring the populations of ground-living invertebrates. **[4]**

**10.** A conservation group wants to monitor a shy, night-active mammal in dense forest over a whole year. Discuss the benefits and limitations of using automated sampling for this purpose. **[4]**

**11.** Explain the difference between repeats and replicates, and how each improves an investigation. **[4]**

**12.** Plan an investigation to test the hypothesis: "More species of invertebrate live in long grass than in mown grass." **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A testable prediction about the relationship between two variables **[1]**.

**2.** A sampling strategy decides where samples are taken, such as random or systematic **[1]**; a sampling technique is the method used to catch or count organisms, such as a quadrat or sweep net **[1]**.

**3.** (a) Pitfall trap **[1]**. (b) Sweep net **[1]**. (c) Aerial photography or drones **[1]**.

**4.** To test the method on a small scale so that problems can be found and fixed before the main investigation **[1]**.

**5.** The user places the end of one tube over a small invertebrate and sucks through a second tube covered with gauze **[1]**; the animal is drawn into a collecting chamber without being harmed **[1]**.

**6.** (a) Independent: distance from the building **[1]**; dependent: number of daisy plants **[1]**.
(b) The hypothesis concerns change along a gradient away from the building **[1]**; samples at regular intervals along a transect show that change clearly, while random points might cluster and leave gaps along the gradient **[1]**.
(c) Any two: same quadrat size; same day or weather; same person counting; same interval between quadrats; same counting rule for plants on the edge of the quadrat. **[2]**

**7.** (a) 31 in garden Q **[1]**.
(b) Garden P: total = 40, mean = 40 ÷ 10 = 4.0 **[1]**. Garden Q excluding 31: total = 66 **[1]**, mean = 66 ÷ 9 = 7.3 to one decimal place **[1]**.
(c) Any one: that stone was over a damp hollow or rotting wood, which woodlice prefer; a nest or cluster under one stone; a counting or recording error. **[1]**

**8.** Lay two tape measures at right angles along the edges of the meadow to form a grid **[1]**; use random numbers, from a calculator or table, as coordinates **[1]**; place a quadrat at each coordinate and count the plants, repeating for a large number of quadrats and calculating a mean **[1]**.

**9.** Up to 2 for benefits and up to 2 for limitations. Benefits: cheap and easy to set up **[1]**; work unattended, including at night, catching animals that are hard to see **[1]**. Limitations: catch only active, ground-living animals, so results do not show the whole community **[1]**; predators in the trap may eat other animals, and rain can flood traps, so traps need covers and frequent checking **[1]**. **[4]**

**10.** Benefits: camera traps or sound recorders work day and night for long periods **[1]** and record without people present, so the shy mammal is not disturbed **[1]**. Limitations: equipment is expensive and can be stolen, damaged by weather or run out of battery in remote forest **[1]**; a year of recordings produces a large amount of data to sort, and the same animal may be counted more than once **[1]**. **[4]**

**11.** A repeat is a measurement taken again in the same way at the same place **[1]**; it reduces the effect of measuring or counting errors **[1]**. A replicate is an extra sample taken at another place under the same conditions **[1]**; it shows whether one sample is typical of the area and allows a reliable mean **[1]**.

**12.** Any six points: select an area of long grass and an area of mown grass in the same field, with similar soil and aspect **[1]**; use random sampling in each area, using random numbers as grid coordinates **[1]**; use a sweep net with a set number of sweeps, such as ten, at each point **[1]**; count the number of different invertebrate species caught **[1]**; control variables: same time of day, same weather, same person sweeping **[1]**; take at least five replicates in each area and calculate a mean **[1]**; carry out a pilot study to decide the number of sweeps **[1]**; release animals after counting **[1]**; compare means with a bar chart and conclude whether the hypothesis is supported **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a strategy when a technique is asked for, or the reverse.
- Writing "repeat to make it accurate" instead of saying how repeats improve reliability.
- Including an anomaly in a mean without comment.
- Planning without control variables, sample numbers or units.
- Giving only benefits in "discuss" questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Fieldwork investigations questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Which paper tests fieldwork methods?**
The syllabus places practical, method-based fieldwork questions on Paper 2, Environmental Management in Context, though data handling appears in both papers.

**How many techniques do I need for 5.4?**
Seven: pitfall traps, pooters, sweep nets, quadrats, transects, aerial photography and drones, and automated sampling. Learn a benefit and a limitation for each.
