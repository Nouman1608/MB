This page is a guide to AQA A Level English Literature B (7717) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below. English Literature A (7712) is a separate specification and is not covered here.

- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it with the mark scheme, which shows what each level of response needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked sound still sat in a lower level. The notes for each series below are a quick way into those reports. Each report is written for one option, so read the one that matches the texts you studied.

Check which specification a paper was set on. The 7717 record we hold is AQA's updated specification for first teaching from September 2025, with its first A-level exams in 2027. It is linear and untiered, with Paper 1 (Literary genres, 75 marks, 40 per cent, a choice of tragedy or comedy), Paper 2 (Texts and genres, 75 marks, 40 per cent, a choice of crime writing or political and social protest writing) and a non-exam assessment, Theory and independence (50 marks, 20 per cent). The papers on this page are from June 2024, June 2025 and June 2026, which come before that first assessment, so they were set on the earlier version of 7717. The June 2025 report for the crime-writing paper notes that a new text is being introduced for first examination in 2027. Set texts, and possibly other details, will therefore differ from the 2027 papers, so check your own text list before relying on a question.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports for the four options share one message: marks follow relevance to the exact question, and the reports say this is the main concern of examiners. Overall performance was similar to the previous year, with slightly fewer answers in the lowest bands on the crime-writing paper.

**Where marks were lost**

- Papers 7717/1A and 7717/1B (tragedy and comedy): answers that pursued the candidate's own agenda and ignored the terms of the question, sketchy knowledge of the text leading to errors of fact, and learned material about the period or about critical views pushed in whatever the question asked. Answers that were long, loose and vague also lost marks. The reports also give guidance on significance questions: the best answers first pinned down exactly what they were asked to explore, then dealt with the meanings and values that arise and the methods the writer uses to produce them.
- Paper 7717/2A (crime writing): generic expectations about the genre imposed on the text instead of working outwards from it, sweeping generalisations about texts and contexts, and weak planning that produced answers which were confused, digressive and too long.
- Paper 7717/2B (political and social protest writing): the same pattern, with general context about the period replacing discussion of the text and answers that did not stay with the question.
- NEA (7717/C): tasks that did not set up a debate, which limited independent thought, and weak use of the Critical Anthology. Biographical or historical bolt-ons were now rare.

**What stronger answers did:** kept the question in view from start to finish, knew the stories in detail and chose which parts of the text best served the argument, thought independently, and wrote tightly. In the NEA, the best essays dealt with form and structure as well as language, and used the theory with care.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports note a slight rise in entries and describe the specification as one where texts are linked by genre and a tight focus on the question matters most.

**Where marks were lost**

- Papers 7717/1A, 1B, 2A and 2B: texts not known securely, answers that did not keep to the question or forced prepared material into it, and loose discussion of context. Several candidates misread the grammatical subject of the question and wrote about something else. On Paper 2, weaker answers did not use the open book thoughtfully to choose relevant passages.
- NEA (7717/C): a small number of centres appeared to offer one critical approach twice instead of two different ones, and some work went well over the word limit, which usually weakened the argument. Tasks that asked only how something is represented gave descriptive answers with no line of argument.

**What stronger answers did:** knew the sequence of events in each text, worked from the story, and chose references to fit the question. In the NEA, stronger folders set up a genuine debate, read theory beyond the anthology and showed independence in the choice of texts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA 7717 past papers?**
AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment-resources page for 7717, and its past-paper finder lists papers by series.

**Do these papers match the 2027 specification?**
Not necessarily. The series here were set before the first exams of the version for first teaching in 2025, so check the set texts and the specification before using a question.

**Why are there four rows of thresholds?**
AQA publishes a subject boundary for each pairing of a Paper 1 option with a Paper 2 option, so the boundaries differ by the combination of options taken.

**What do examiners say most often about 7717?**
They ask candidates to answer the exact question set, to know the text as a story, and to avoid bolting on learned contextual or critical material that does not fit the question.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
