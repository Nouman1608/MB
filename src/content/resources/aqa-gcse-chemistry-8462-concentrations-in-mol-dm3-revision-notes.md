These notes condense **4.3.4 Using concentrations of solutions in mol/dm³ (chemistry only) (HT only)** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). The whole subsection is
**Higher Tier only**. It is Paper 1 content. For full explanations, see the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-concentrations-in-mol-dm3/); then practise with the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-concentrations-in-mol-dm3-practice/).

## Core facts

- Concentration of a solution can be measured in **mol/dm³**.
- You can calculate the **amount in moles**, or the **mass in grams**, of solute in a given volume of solution
  from its concentration in mol/dm³.
- If the volumes of two solutions that react completely are known, and the concentration of one is known, the
  concentration of the other can be calculated.
- You must be able to **explain how the concentration in mol/dm³ is related to the mass of the solute and the
  volume of the solution**.

## Equations

```
concentration (mol/dm3) = moles / volume (dm3)
moles = concentration x volume (dm3)
concentration (g/dm3) = concentration (mol/dm3) x Mr
volume (dm3) = volume (cm3) / 1000
```

| Unit | Conversion |
|---|---|
| 1 dm³ | 1000 cm³ |
| 250 cm³ | 0.250 dm³ |
| 25.0 cm³ | 0.0250 dm³ |

## Titration method

1. Balanced equation and mole ratio.
2. Moles of the known solution = concentration × volume (dm³).
3. Moles of the unknown, using the ratio.
4. Concentration of the unknown = moles ÷ its volume (dm³).
5. Convert to g/dm³ if required (× Mr).

| Example | Working |
|---|---|
| 25.0 cm³ NaOH, 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ HCl (1 : 1) | 0.00200 mol HCl = 0.00200 mol NaOH; ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0800 mol/dm³ |
| 25.0 cm³ NaOH, 12.5 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ H₂SO₄ (1 : 2) | 0.00250 mol H₂SO₄ gives 0.00500 mol NaOH; ÷ 0.0250 = 0.200 mol/dm³ |
| Mass of Na₂CO₃ for 250 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ | 0.0250 mol × 106 = 2.65 g |

## Linking mass, volume and concentration

mass (g) → ÷ Mr → moles → ÷ volume (dm³) → concentration (mol/dm³)

- Doubling the mass in the same volume doubles the concentration.
- Doubling the volume for the same mass halves the concentration.
- Concentration in g/dm³ is Mr times larger than the concentration in mol/dm³.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration | Amount of solute in a given volume of solution |
| mol/dm³ | Moles of solute per cubic decimetre of solution |
| Titration | Reacting measured volumes of two solutions to find a concentration |
| Neutralise | React an acid and an alkali until neither is in excess |
| Mole ratio | The ratio of balancing numbers in the equation |
| Solute | The substance dissolved |

## Worked examples in brief

**1.** 0.050 mol in 250 cm³: 0.050 ÷ 0.250 = 0.20 mol/dm³.

**2.** 0.20 mol/dm³ NaOH → 0.20 × 40 = 8.0 g/dm³.

**3.** 1.0 mol/dm³ HCl → 36.5 g/dm³.

**4.** Moles in 40.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol/dm³: 0.250 × 0.0400 = 0.0100 mol.

## Preparing a solution and diluting (HT only)

1. Moles required = concentration × volume (dm³).
2. Mass = moles × Mr.
3. Weigh, dissolve in a beaker, transfer to a volumetric flask, rinse, fill to the mark, mix.

Dilution: the moles stay the same. Example: 25.0 cm³ of 0.400 mol/dm³ contains 0.0100 mol. Diluted to 100 cm³ the
concentration is 0.0100 ÷ 0.100 = 0.100 mol/dm³.

## Titration technique notes

- A pipette measures the fixed volume of one solution accurately.
- A burette delivers the other solution; read the meniscus at eye level.
- Repeat until two or more results are concordant, then use their mean.
- The end point is shown by an indicator changing colour.

## Step-by-step layout

| Line | Write |
|---|---|
| 1 | Balanced equation and mole ratio |
| 2 | Volume of the known solution in dm³ |
| 3 | Moles of the known solution |
| 4 | Moles of the unknown, using the ratio |
| 5 | Concentration of the unknown, with units |
| 6 | If asked, convert to g/dm³ using Mr |

## Exam traps

- **cm³ not converted to dm³.**
- **Ratio inverted** in a titration.
- **Using Mr for g/dm³ the wrong way:** multiply to go from mol/dm³ to g/dm³, divide to go back.
- **Using the wrong volume at the end.**
- **Writing an answer in moles when the question asks for concentration.**
- **No working shown.** Method marks are lost.

## Quick self-test (all HT only)

1. Convert 450 cm³ to dm³.
2. Calculate the concentration if 0.30 mol is dissolved to make 600 cm³.
3. Calculate the moles in 40.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol/dm³ solution.
4. Calculate the mass of NaOH (Mr 40) in 25.0 cm³ of 0.10 mol/dm³.
5. Convert 0.50 mol/dm³ NaOH into g/dm³.
6. Convert 73 g/dm³ HCl (Mr 36.5) into mol/dm³.
7. In NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O, 0.0020 mol HCl reacts. How many moles of NaOH react?
8. In H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O, 0.0025 mol H₂SO₄ reacts. How many moles of NaOH react?
9. Explain what happens to the concentration if the volume is halved with the same mass of solute.
10. Calculate the mass of NaCl (Mr 58.5) needed for 500 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ solution.

### Answers

1. 0.450 dm³.
2. 0.30 ÷ 0.600 = 0.50 mol/dm³.
3. 0.250 × 0.0400 = 0.0100 mol.
4. 0.10 × 0.0250 × 40 = 0.10 g.
5. 0.50 × 40 = 20 g/dm³.
6. 73 ÷ 36.5 = 2.0 mol/dm³.
7. 0.0020 mol.
8. 0.0050 mol.
9. It doubles.
10. 0.200 × 0.500 × 58.5 = 5.85 g.
