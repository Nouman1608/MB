**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 3.1 Electronegativity and bonding. Covalent character in ionic compounds is not assessed; Pauling values are given where needed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electronegativity | The power of an atom to attract electrons to itself (the bonding pair in a covalent bond) |
| Pauling scale | Unitless scale of electronegativity; fluorine 4.0 is the highest |
| Nuclear charge | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Shielding | Reduction in attraction for outer or bonding electrons caused by inner electrons |
| Non-polar covalent bond | Electrons shared equally (no electronegativity difference) |
| Polar covalent bond | Electrons shared unequally; partial charges δ+ and δ− |
| Ionic bond | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions; forms when the difference is large |

## Factors

| Factor | Higher electronegativity when… |
|---|---|
| Nuclear charge | more protons |
| Atomic radius | atom smaller (bonding pair closer) |
| Shielding | fewer inner shells and sub-shells |

## Trends with data

| Period 2 | Li 1.0 | Be 1.6 | B 2.0 | C 2.6 | N 3.0 | O 3.4 | F 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Period 3** | **Na 0.9** | **Mg 1.3** | **Al 1.6** | **Si 1.9** | **P 2.2** | **S 2.6** | **Cl 3.2** |

| Group 17 | F | Cl | Br | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electronegativity | 4.0 | 3.2 | 3.0 | 2.7 |

- **Across a period: increases.** Nuclear charge up; same shell; shielding roughly constant; radius down.
- **Down a group: decreases.** Extra shell; larger radius and more shielding outweigh greater nuclear charge.
- No dips across a period (unlike ionisation energy).
- Most electronegative: F, then O, then Cl and N.
- Noble gases: usually no values.

## Same factors, three trends

| Property | Across a period | Down a group | Controlled by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases | Nuclear charge, shells, shielding |
| First ionisation energy | Increases (with dips at Groups 13 and 16) | Decreases | Nuclear charge, radius, shielding, spin-pair repulsion |
| Electronegativity | Increases steadily | Decreases | Nuclear charge, radius, shielding |

An explanation written for one of these trends can be adapted for the others: change the conclusion from "radius" to "energy to remove an electron" to "attraction for the bonding pair".

## Electronegativity difference and bond type

| Difference | Bond type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Non-polar covalent | Cl–Cl, H–H |
| Small | Polar covalent; more electronegative atom δ− | H–Cl (1.0), O–H (1.2) |
| Large (roughly 2 or more, no sharp cut-off) | Ionic | NaCl (2.3), MgO (2.1), KF (3.2) |

**Method: predict bond type**
1. Look up both Pauling values (given).
2. Subtract the smaller from the larger.
3. Compare: zero, small or large.
4. State the type and, for a polar bond, which atom is δ−.

**Bond polarity ranking (C 2.6):** C–F 1.4 > C–Cl 0.6 > C–Br 0.4 > C–I 0.1.

## Model comparison answers

**F more electronegative than Cl**
- F smaller: bonding pair in shell 2, not shell 3.
- F less shielded: fewer inner shells.
- These outweigh chlorine's larger nuclear charge.

**O more electronegative than N**
- O has greater nuclear charge (8 against 7).
- Same shell, similar shielding, smaller radius.
- Stronger attraction for the bonding pair.

## Exam traps

- Confusing electronegativity with electron affinity (gaining an electron to form an ion).
- Units on electronegativity values.
- "More shielding" across a period.
- Nuclear charge alone down a group.
- δ− on the less electronegative atom.
- Treating "difference of 2" as an exact rule.
- Discussing covalent character in ionic compounds (not assessed here).

## Self-test

**1.** Define electronegativity.

**2.** State the three factors that affect electronegativity.

**3.** State and explain the trend in electronegativity from Na to Cl.

**4.** State and explain the trend from F to I.

**5.** Why does electronegativity show no dip between Mg and Al, while first ionisation energy does?

**6.** Which is more electronegative, sulfur or oxygen? Explain.

**7.** Predict the type of bonding in CaO (Ca 1.0, O 3.4).

**8.** Predict the type of bonding in PCl₃ (P 2.2, Cl 3.2) and show the partial charges.

**9.** Which atom is δ+ in an N–H bond (N 3.0, H 2.2)?

**10.** Rank O–H, N–H and C–H by polarity.

**11.** Why is the bond in Br₂ non-polar?

**12.** Lithium has electronegativity 1.0 and caesium 0.8. Explain the difference.

## Self-test answers

**1.** The power of an atom to attract electrons to itself (the bonding pair in a covalent bond).

**2.** Nuclear charge, atomic radius, and shielding by inner shells and sub-shells.

**3.** Increases: nuclear charge increases, the bonding electrons are in the same shell with similar shielding, and the radius decreases, so the bonding pair is attracted more strongly.

**4.** Decreases: each element has an extra shell, so the bonding pair is further away and more shielded, outweighing the increased nuclear charge.

**5.** Electronegativity concerns attraction for a bonding pair and follows the overall pull of the nucleus; the ionisation energy dip depends on removing one electron from a higher-energy 3p sub-shell.

**6.** Oxygen: smaller atom with fewer inner shells, so less shielding; this outweighs sulfur's larger nuclear charge.

**7.** Difference 2.4: ionic.

**8.** Difference 1.0: polar covalent, P δ+ and each Cl δ−.

**9.** H is δ+ (N is more electronegative).

**10.** O–H (1.2) > N–H (0.8) > C–H (0.4).

**11.** Both atoms are identical, so they have the same electronegativity and share the electrons equally.

**12.** Caesium's atom has more shells, so its outer (bonding) electrons are much further from the nucleus and more shielded; this outweighs its greater nuclear charge.
