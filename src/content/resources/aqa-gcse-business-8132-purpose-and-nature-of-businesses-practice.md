## Questions

**1.** Define the term *opportunity cost*. **[2]**

**2.** Give **one** example of a good and **one** example of a service that a hairdressing salon might sell. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the reason for starting a business shown in each statement.

**(a)** "Joel buys fresh fish from the harbour each morning and delivers it to hotels." **[1]**
**(b)** "Nobody in the village offered bicycle repairs, so Priya started a repair van." **[1]**
**(c)** "All profits from the shop pay for free meals for elderly residents." **[1]**

**4.** Pennick Print has four resources: its owner, who started the firm and takes the risk; a large digital printer; the unit it rents; and three employees. Identify the factor of production that each represents. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business selling wants may be affected more than a business selling needs when household incomes fall. **[3]**

**6.** Pennick Print has £15,000 to spend and three options:

| Option | Estimated extra profit per year |
|---|---|
| A second printer | £5,200 |
| A delivery van (saves courier costs) | £4,600 |
| An advertising campaign | £3,900 |

**(a)** Identify the option the owner should choose if the aim is the highest extra profit. **[1]**
**(b)** State the opportunity cost of that choice. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the opportunity cost is not £8,500. **[2]**

**7.** Tarn Valley Dairy keeps cows, makes cheese from their milk and sells the cheese in its own café.

**(a)** Identify the sector of each of the three activities. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how a fall in the farm's milk output could affect the café. **[2]**

**8.** Amira left her job at a bank after being passed over for promotion. She now bakes celebration cakes at home, working around her children's school day.

**(a)** Identify **two** objectives of Amira as an entrepreneur, using evidence from the case. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a willingness to take a risk is important for Amira. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** characteristics of an entrepreneur that would help someone starting a mobile phone repair business. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse how changes in technology could affect a small independent bookshop on a high street. **[6]**

**11.** Fenwick Outdoor sells camping equipment online. Customers increasingly expect plastic-free packaging. Switching to recycled card would add £0.40 to the cost of packing each order. Fenwick sends 6,000 orders a year and makes an average profit of £6.50 per order. Several rivals have already switched.

**(a)** Calculate the extra cost per year of switching to card packaging. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Fenwick Outdoor should switch to card packaging. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The **next best alternative** [1] that is **given up when a choice is made** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "What you give up" alone earns one mark; the words "next best" are what make the definition precise.

**2.** Good: any physical product, such as shampoo or hair straighteners [1]. Service: any activity, such as a haircut or colouring [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A haircut is a service even though you can see the result; the customer pays for the activity.

**3. (a)** Distributing products [1]
**(b)** Fulfilling a business opportunity [1]
**(c)** Providing a good or service to benefit others [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the specification's own wording; "to help people" is less secure than "to benefit others".

**4.** Owner: enterprise [1]. Printer: capital [1]. Unit: land [1]. Employees: labour [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The rented unit is land because it is the site; the money used to pay rent is not capital.

**5.** When incomes fall, households keep buying needs such as food and paying rent [1], so they cut back on wants such as a new phone or meals out [1]. This means the business selling wants sees a larger fall in sales and revenue than one selling needs [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The third mark comes from saying what happens to the business, not just to the customers.

**6. (a)** The second printer [1]
**(b)** The delivery van and the £4,600 a year it would have saved [1]
**(c)** Opportunity cost is only the **single next best alternative** [1]; £8,500 adds the van and the advertising together (£4,600 + £3,900), but the advertising is the third choice, not the next best [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding up every option is the most common error on this topic.

**7. (a)** Keeping cows: primary [1]; making cheese: secondary [1]; café: tertiary [1]
**(b)** Less milk means less cheese can be made [1], so the café may run out of its own cheese, lose sales or have to buy from another supplier at a higher cost [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* (b) tests the link between sectors; a two-step chain earns both marks.

**8. (a)** Dissatisfaction with current job: she left after being passed over for promotion [1]. Flexible working hours: she works around her children's school day [1].
**(b)** She has given up a regular salary and may have spent savings on equipment [1], so if sales are poor she could lose money; without accepting this risk she would not have started at all [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each objective needs its evidence; "to be her own boss" is possible but the case gives no direct evidence for it.

**9.** Any two developed points, 2 marks each. **Innovative** [1]: she might offer a same-day repair-while-you-wait service that rivals do not, attracting customers [1]. **Organised** [1]: she must track parts, bookings and repairs so phones are returned on time, keeping customers satisfied [1]. **Hard working** [1]: long hours may be needed at first to build a customer base [1]. **Willing to take a risk** [1]: buying tools and parts before any income arrives [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply each characteristic to phone repairs; generic definitions stay at one mark each.

**10.** Indicative chains, up to 6.
- Online retailers sell books at low prices with home delivery [1], so some customers stop visiting the shop, reducing its revenue [1].
- E-readers and audiobooks reduce demand for printed books [1], so the shop may need to sell other products or services, such as events [1].
- The bookshop can use technology itself: a website and social media to promote events [1], or an online ordering service, which could recover some sales but costs time and money to set up [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" rewards chains of cause and effect; two well-developed chains score more than six unexplained points.

**11. (a)** £0.40 × 6,000 [1] = **£2,400** a year [1]
**(b)** Indicative points, one mark each up to 9.
- The switch costs £2,400 a year, which reduces profit if prices do not rise [1].
- Total profit is about £6.50 × 6,000 = £39,000, so the cost is about 6% of profit, which is manageable [1].
- Customers increasingly expect plastic-free packaging, so not switching risks losing orders [1].
- Rivals have already switched, so Fenwick may look worse by comparison and lose customers to them [1].
- Losing only about 370 orders (£2,400 ÷ £6.50) would cost as much profit as the switch itself [1].
- Card packaging could be used in marketing to attract environmentally aware customers [1].
- Fenwick could add a small charge or raise prices slightly to cover the cost [1].
- However, if customers are price-sensitive, a price rise could reduce sales [1].
- Judgement: Fenwick should switch, because the cost is small compared with total profit and the risk of losing customers to rivals is high, as long as it promotes the change [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a judgement that follows from the figures. Using your answer to (a) and the profit per order makes the argument specific to Fenwick.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining opportunity cost without "next best".
- Adding all the alternatives together.
- Treating money as capital in the factors of production.
- Classifying a business by its name instead of each activity.
- Naming objectives without evidence from the case.
- Analysis answers that list changes but never explain the effect on sales, costs or profit.
- Evaluations that end with a summary but no decision.
