These notes condense content item 2.1.4 Ethics, the environment and business, from Topic 2.1 Growing the business in Theme 2 of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [ethics and the environment study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-ethics-the-environment-and-business/), and exam-style questions are in the [ethics and the environment practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-ethics-the-environment-and-business-practice/). Businesses and figures are invented.

## The three things 2.1.4 tests

1. How ethical considerations influence business activity, and the possible trade-offs between ethics and profit.
2. How environmental considerations influence business activity, and the possible trade-offs between the environment, sustainability and profit.
3. The potential impact of pressure group activity on the marketing mix.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Moral principles about what is right and wrong |
| Ethical decision | A choice that is fair and honest to those affected, often going beyond what the law requires |
| Trade-off | Gaining more of one thing by accepting less of another |
| Environmental considerations | A business's effects on the natural world: pollution, waste, energy, packaging, resource use |
| Sustainability | Meeting present needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs |
| Pressure group | An organised group that campaigns to change the behaviour of businesses or governments |
| Boycott | Customers deliberately refusing to buy from a business as a protest |

## Ethics and profit

| Ethical choice costs | Ethical choice can bring |
|---|---|
| Higher wages or supplier prices | Loyal customers who value fairness |
| Time and money spent checking supply chains | Motivated staff who are less likely to leave |
| Possibly higher selling prices, losing price-sensitive buyers | A stronger brand and the chance to charge more |
| Lower profit in the short run | Avoiding the cost of a scandal or boycott |

**Quick calculation.** Fernhill Tea agrees to pay a growers' co-operative 15p more per kilogram for 20,000 kg a year. Extra cost = £0.15 × 20,000 = **£3,000**. If profit was £60,000, the fall is £3,000 ÷ £60,000 × 100 = **5%**, before any extra sales.

## Environment, sustainability and profit

- **Costs:** greener materials and packaging, new equipment, redesigns, measuring and reporting impact.
- **Gains:** lower energy and waste bills, customers who prefer greener firms, readiness for tighter rules, a better reputation.
- **Timing:** costs often come first; savings and sales benefits build over time.
- **Sustainability is broader than recycling.** It covers using renewable resources, cutting waste and making products last.

**Quick calculation.** Ashby Print spends £6,000 fitting low-energy lighting, which cuts its electricity bill by £1,500 a year. The cost is recovered in £6,000 ÷ £1,500 = **4 years**; after that the saving adds to profit. This is a trade-off over time: the money goes out at the start, while the benefit builds in later years.

## Pressure groups and the marketing mix

| Element | How a campaign can change it |
|---|---|
| Product | New ingredients, materials or packaging; a product withdrawn |
| Price | Higher costs passed on in prices, or lower profit per unit |
| Promotion | Bad publicity to answer; adverts that show the change |
| Place | Retailers or venues stop stocking or hosting the business |

**What decides the impact?** How much publicity the group gets; whether customers care; how easily customers can switch; how costly the change is for the business.

## Linking phrases for trade-off answers

These are suggestions, not specification wording.

- "In the short run this raises costs by ..., so profit falls to ..."
- "However, customers who value ... may be willing to pay more, which could ..."
- "The size of the trade-off depends on ..."
- "The campaign affects the promotion element because ..."

## Exam traps

- Saying an ethical or green choice "always" reduces profit.
- Treating obeying the law as an ethical choice. The law is the minimum.
- Mixing up ethical and environmental issues.
- Answering a pressure group question without naming a marketing mix element.
- Calculating a cost and stopping, without saying what it means for the business.

## Self-test

1. Define the term sustainability.
2. Give two examples of ethical considerations for a business.
3. What does "trade-off" mean in 2.1.4?
4. State one way an ethical choice can increase a business's costs.
5. State one way an ethical choice can increase a business's sales.
6. A café switches to compostable cups costing 4p more each and uses 30,000 cups a year. Calculate the extra cost.
7. If the café's profit was £24,000, calculate the percentage fall in profit caused by the change in question 6.
8. Give two methods a pressure group might use.
9. Which element of the marketing mix is affected when a supermarket stops stocking a product after a campaign?
10. Explain one reason a pressure group campaign may have little effect on a business.
11. Give one environmental change that could lower a business's costs over time.
12. Why might a business accept lower profit to act sustainably?

### Answers

1. Meeting the needs of the present without harming the ability of future generations to meet their needs.
2. Any two: pay and conditions of staff; fair prices for suppliers; honest advertising; animal welfare; working conditions in supplier factories.
3. Gaining more of one aim, such as ethical behaviour, by accepting less of another, such as profit.
4. Higher wages, dearer materials from checked suppliers, or the cost of checking supply chains.
5. Customers who value ethical behaviour may choose the business over rivals or pay a higher price.
6. £0.04 × 30,000 = **£1,200**.
7. £1,200 ÷ £24,000 × 100 = **5%**.
8. Any two: petitions; boycotts; protests; social media campaigns; seeking media coverage.
9. Place.
10. Customers may not care about the issue or may have no easy alternative, so they keep buying and sales do not fall.
11. Cutting energy use (for example, efficient lighting or solar panels) or reducing waste, which lowers bills.
12. To attract customers who prefer greener businesses, to protect its reputation, to prepare for tighter rules, or because the owners believe it is right.
