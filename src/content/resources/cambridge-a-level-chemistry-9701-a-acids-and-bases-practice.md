These questions cover subtopic 25.1 (Acids and bases) of Cambridge International AS & A Level
Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 298 K. Ka and Ksp values
are supplied in each question. Revise first with the
[Acids and bases study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-acids-and-bases/)

[Acids and bases revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify the two conjugate acid–base pairs in
H₂PO₄⁻ + NH₃ ⇌ HPO₄²⁻ + NH₄⁺. **[2]**

**2.** What is the pH of 0.0010 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide at 298 K?

A: 3.00

B: 11.00

C: 10.00

D: 14.00

**[1]**

**3.** Which mixture is a buffer solution?

A: hydrochloric acid and sodium chloride

B: ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate

C: sodium hydroxide and sodium chloride

D: equal amounts of nitric acid and sodium hydroxide

**[1]**

**4.** Define pKa and state how its value relates to the strength of a weak acid. **[2]**

**5.** Write the Ksp expression for calcium phosphate, Ca₃(PO₄)₂, and give its units. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** 10.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is diluted with water to 250 cm³. Calculate
the pH of the diluted solution. **[2]**

**7.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl is mixed with 30.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Calculate
the pH of the resulting solution. **[3]**

**8.** Lactic acid is a weak monoprotic acid with Ka = 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of a 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ solution of lactic acid. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate pKa for lactic acid. **[1]**

**9.** A 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak acid HA has pH 3.12.

**(a)** Calculate Ka. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of HA molecules that are dissociated, and comment on whether
the usual approximation is justified. **[1]**

**10.** A buffer contains 0.120 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid and 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ sodium ethanoate.
Ka(CH₃COOH) = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Explain, with an equation, how this buffer resists a fall in pH when a small amount of
acid is added. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of the buffer. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the ratio [CH₃COO⁻] : [CH₃COOH] needed for a buffer of pH 5.00. **[2]**

**11.** 500 cm³ of a buffer contains 0.0400 mol methanoic acid and 0.0400 mol sodium methanoate
(Ka = 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³). 0.00200 mol of solid NaOH is dissolved in it. Calculate the pH
before and after the addition. **[3]**

**12.** Silver sulfate, Ag₂SO₄, has Ksp = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ mol³ dm⁻⁹. Calculate its solubility in
pure water in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**13.** Ksp(CaSO₄) = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol² dm⁻⁶.

**(a)** 20.0 cm³ of 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ CaCl₂ is mixed with 30.0 cm³ of 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³
Na₂SO₄. Deduce whether a precipitate of CaSO₄ forms. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the solubility of CaSO₄ in 0.100 mol dm⁻³ Na₂SO₄. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this solubility is lower than in pure water. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** H₂PO₄⁻ / HPO₄²⁻ (acid / conjugate base) [1]; NH₃ / NH₄⁺ (base / conjugate acid) [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0010 = 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ mol dm⁻³.

**3.** **B** [1]. A weak acid with a salt of its conjugate base.

**4.** pKa = −log₁₀ Ka [1]. The smaller the pKa, the stronger the acid [1].

**5.** Ksp = [Ca²⁺]³[PO₄³⁻]² [1]; units mol⁵ dm⁻¹⁵ [1].

**6.** [H⁺] = 0.500 × 10.0 ÷ 250 = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.70** [1].

**7.** Moles HCl = 2.50 × 10⁻³; moles NaOH = 3.00 × 10⁻³; excess OH⁻ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1].
[OH⁻] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0550 = 9.09 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]. [H⁺] = 1.10 × 10⁻¹²;
pH = **11.96** [1].

**8. (a)** [H⁺] = √(1.4 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.0800) = 3.35 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.48** [1].

**(b)** pKa = −log(1.4 × 10⁻⁴) = **3.85** [1].

**9. (a)** [H⁺] = 10⁻³·¹² = 7.59 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; Ka = (7.59 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 0.0400 =
giving **1.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³** [1].

**(b)** 7.59 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0400 × 100 = **1.9%**, so [HA] is almost unchanged and the approximation
is justified [1].

**10. (a)** The ethanoate ions react with added H⁺: CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃COOH [1]. There is a large
reservoir of CH₃COO⁻, so the ratio [CH₃COOH] : [CH₃COO⁻] changes very little and the pH hardly
changes [1].

**(b)** [H⁺] = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × (0.120 ÷ 0.0800) = 2.7 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **4.57** [1].

**(c)** [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1]; [CH₃COO⁻] ÷ [CH₃COOH] = Ka ÷ [H⁺] = **1.8 : 1** [1].

**11.** Before: equal amounts, so pH = pKa = −log(1.6 × 10⁻⁴) = **3.80** [1]. After:
HCOOH + OH⁻ → HCOO⁻ + H₂O, so HCOOH = 0.0380 mol and HCOO⁻ = 0.0420 mol [1].
[H⁺] = 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ × (0.0380 ÷ 0.0420) = 1.45 × 10⁻⁴; pH = **3.84** [1].

**12.** Ag₂SO₄ ⇌ 2Ag⁺ + SO₄²⁻, so Ksp = (2s)² × s = 4s³ [1]. s³ = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 4 =
3.0 × 10⁻⁶ [1]; s = **1.4 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³** [1].

**13. (a)** After mixing (total 50.0 cm³): [Ca²⁺] = 5.0 × 10⁻³ × 20.0 ÷ 50.0 = 2.0 × 10⁻³ and
[SO₄²⁻] = 3.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]. Ionic product = 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol² dm⁻⁶ [1]. This is less than
Ksp, so **no precipitate** forms [1].

**(b)** [Ca²⁺] = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 0.100 = **2.4 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³** [1].

**(c)** The added sulfate ions (common ion) shift CaSO₄(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) to the left,
so less CaSO₄ dissolves [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking pH = −log[OH⁻] for an alkali instead of using Kw.
- Forgetting to find the excess reagent, or using the wrong total volume, after mixing an acid
  and an alkali.
- Using initial moles rather than moles after reaction in a buffer calculation.
- Not halving or rescaling concentrations after mixing before comparing with Ksp.
- Writing Ksp expressions without powers, or with the wrong units.
- Explaining buffer action without an equation.
