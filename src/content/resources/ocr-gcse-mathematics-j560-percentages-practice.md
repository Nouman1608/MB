These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 2.03, Percentages (2.03a to 2.03c). All of the content is for both tiers. Questions 1 to 6 are non-calculator; a calculator may be used for Section B. All prices and figures are invented. Section A has short questions; Section B has problems in context.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Write

**(a)** 45% as a fraction in its simplest form, **[1]**

**(b)** 7/40 as a percentage, **[1]**

**(c)** 0.065 as a percentage, **[1]**

**(d)** 1.8 as a percentage. **[1]**

**2.** Without a calculator, find

**(a)** 15% of £36, **[2]**

**(b)** 0.5% of 6 400. **[1]**

**3.** Write 27 as a percentage of 120. **[2]**

**4.** Write down the multiplier for

**(a)** an increase of 6%, **[1]**

**(b)** a decrease of 22%, **[1]**

**(c)** an increase of 1/4. **[1]**

**5.** Increase £2.80 by 15%. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the percentage change represented by each multiplier.

**(a)** 1.35 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.6 **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A phone costs £320. In a sale its price is reduced by 35%.

**(a)** Find the sale price. **[2]**

**(b)** Later the price is reduced to £176. Write £176 as a percentage of the original £320. **[2]**

**8.** The population of a town rises from 12 500 to 13 400. Find the percentage increase. **[2]**

**9.** In a sale with 30% off, a coat costs £94.50.

**(a)** Find the original price of the coat. **[3]**

**(b)** Kim says, "Just add 30% of £94.50 back on." Show that Kim's method does not give the original price. **[1]**

**10.** A bill including VAT at 20% is £186. How much of the bill is VAT? **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** Find the simple interest on £3 600 at 2.5% per year for 4 years. **[2]**

**(b)** £950 is invested at a fixed rate of simple interest. After 6 years it is worth £1 064. Find the yearly rate. **[3]**

**12.** A shop buys a lamp for £48 and sells it for £66.

**(a)** Find the percentage profit on the buying price. **[2]**

**(b)** The shop wants a 45% profit on a different lamp that costs it £56. Find the selling price. **[2]**

**13.** The same jacket is sold in two shops.

Shop A: £84, with 1/3 off.

Shop B: £75, with 20% off.

Which shop is cheaper, and by how much? **[4]**

**14.** After a 3% pay rise, Omar's salary is £30 900.

**(a)** Find his salary before the rise. **[2]**

**(b)** How much was the rise? **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 45/100 = **9/20** [1]

**(b)** 0.175, so **17.5%** [1]

**(c)** **6.5%** [1]

**(d)** **180%** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A decimal above 1 gives a percentage above 100%, so 1.8 is 180%, not 18%.

**2. (a)** 10% = 3.60 and 5% = 1.80 [1]; **£5.40** [1]

**(b)** 1% = 64, so 0.5% = **32** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Show each building block on its own line on a non-calculator paper.

**3.** 27/120 × 100 [1]; **22.5%** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The reference value, 120, goes on the bottom.

**4. (a)** **1.06** [1]

**(b)** **0.78** [1]

**(c)** **5/4** (or 1.25) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A decrease multiplier is less than 1. 0.22 would find the decrease itself, not the new value.

**5.** 2.80 × 1.15, or 2.80 + 0.42 [1]; **£3.22** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* By hand: 10% is 28p and 5% is 14p, so 15% is 42p.

**6. (a)** **35% increase** [1]

**(b)** **40% decrease** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 0.6 keeps 60% of the value, so 40% has been removed.

**7. (a)** 320 × 0.65 [1]; **£208** [1]

**(b)** 176/320 × 100 [1]; **55%** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the question asks for £176 as a percentage of £320, not for the percentage reduction (which would be 45%).

**8.** Increase 900, and 900/12 500 × 100, or 13 400 ÷ 12 500 = 1.072 [1]; **7.2% increase** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original population, 12 500. Dividing by 13 400 gives 6.7%, which is wrong.

**9. (a)** Sale price is 70% of the original, multiplier 0.7 [1]; 94.50 ÷ 0.7 [1]; **£135** [1]

**(b)** 94.50 × 1.3 = £122.85, which is not £135 (and 122.85 × 0.7 ≠ 94.50) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The 30% was taken from the original price, which is larger than £94.50, so adding 30% of £94.50 adds too little.

**10.** Bill is 120% of the pre-VAT amount [1]; 186 ÷ 1.2 = £155 [1]; VAT = 186 − 155 = **£31** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 20% of £186 is £37.20, which is wrong: VAT is 20% of £155.

**11. (a)** 3 600 × 0.025 = £90 a year [1]; × 4 = **£360** [1]

**(b)** Interest 1 064 − 950 = £114 [1]; per year 114 ÷ 6 = £19 [1]; 19/950 × 100 = **2% per year** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Find the interest first. The £1 064 includes the original £950.

**12. (a)** Profit £18, 18/48 × 100 [1]; **37.5%** [1]

**(b)** 56 × 1.45 [1]; **£81.20** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Percentage profit is based on what the shop paid, the original value in this context.

**13.** Shop A: 84 × 2/3 [1] = £56 [1]; Shop B: 75 × 0.8 = £60 [1]; **Shop A is cheaper by £4** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "1/3 off" leaves 2/3 of the price. Give a clear conclusion that names the shop and the difference.

**14. (a)** 30 900 ÷ 1.03 [1]; **£30 000** [1]

**(b)** 30 900 − 30 000 = **£900** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 3% of £30 900 is £927, not the rise, because the 3% was calculated on the old salary.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the percentage itself (0.35) instead of the multiplier (0.65) for a decrease.
- Dividing by the new value when finding a percentage change.
- Adding the percentage back in original value problems.
- Taking VAT as 20% of a price that already includes VAT.
- Forgetting to subtract the principal before finding a simple interest rate.
- Answering "percentage of" when the question asks for "percentage change", or the other way round.
- Leaving out "increase" or "decrease".
- Writing money without two decimal places.
