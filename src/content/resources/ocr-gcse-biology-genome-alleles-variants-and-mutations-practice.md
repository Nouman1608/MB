Questions 9 to 11 test B5.1e, which is Higher Tier only. ## Questions

**1.** Define each term. **(a)** Gene **[1]** **(b)** Allele **[1]** **(c)** Heterozygous **[1]** **(d)** Phenotype **[1]**

**2.** In an invented snail species, shell banding is controlled by one gene. The allele B (banded) is dominant to b (plain).

**(a)** State the phenotype and whether the genotype is homozygous or heterozygous for BB, Bb and bb. **[3]**
**(b)** A snail has a banded shell. Explain why you cannot be sure of its genotype. **[2]**

**3.** A genome contains 1 500 000 000 base pairs. Of these, 2% are coding DNA.

**(a)** Define the term genome. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of coding base pairs. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of non-coding base pairs. **[1]**

**4.** **(a)** Classify each feature as continuous or discontinuous variation: adult height, eye colour, ABO blood group, body mass. **[2]**
**(b)** State the type of graph usually used for each kind of variation. **[2]**

**5.** Identical twins were brought up in different places. At age 18 twin A is 176 cm tall and twin B is 171 cm tall.

**(a)** Explain why identical twins are very similar in many features. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two environmental factors that could explain the difference in height. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the height of twin B as a percentage below twin A. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**6.** The masses of 60 seeds from one plant species were recorded.

| Mass class (mg) | 130-149 | 150-169 | 170-189 | 190-209 | 210-229 | 230-249 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of seeds | 4 | 11 | 21 | 14 | 7 | 3 |

**(a)** State the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of seeds in the modal class. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the distribution. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why this is continuous variation. **[2]**

**7.** In a study of 250 new variants, 210 had no effect on the phenotype, 35 influenced it and 5 determined it.

**(a)** State what is meant by a mutation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of variants with no effect. **[2]**
**(c)** State one factor that increases the rate of mutation. **[1]**

**8.** A student writes: "A dominant allele is the stronger one. It stops the recessive allele working, so dominant alleles are always the most common." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A variant of an enzyme was tested against the normal enzyme at three substrate concentrations. Activity is in arbitrary units.

| Substrate concentration | 0.2 | 0.4 | 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal enzyme | 2.0 | 3.6 | 5.6 |
| Variant enzyme | 0.5 | 0.9 | 1.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the activity of the variant as a percentage of the normal enzyme at concentration 0.8. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what the data show. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a mutation in the gene could cause this result. **[2]**
**(d)** A different variant changes an amino acid far from the active site and the enzyme works normally. Suggest why. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) Data supplied: AUG = Met, CCU = Pro, GGA = Gly, GGC = Gly, AAA = Lys, UGG = Trp, UAA = stop. A normal mRNA reads AUG CCU GGA AAA UGG UAA.

**(a)** Variant X changes GGA to GGC. Explain the effect on the protein. **[1]**
**(b)** Variant Y changes AAA to UAA. State the amino acid sequence made and explain why the protein is unlikely to work. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) Gene P codes for protein P. In most adults the gene is switched off. A person has a variant in the non-coding DNA next to gene P, and the amino acid sequence of protein P is normal. The person makes protein P as an adult.

**(a)** State what is meant by non-coding DNA. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how a variant in non-coding DNA can change the phenotype when the protein itself is normal. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A section of DNA that codes for a protein [1]. **(b)** A different version of the same gene [1]. **(c)** Having two different alleles of a gene [1]. **(d)** The observable characteristic of an organism, produced by its genotype and environment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition of gene that says only "what you inherit" or "part of a chromosome" is too vague to gain credit; the link to coding for a protein is what is needed.

**2. (a)** BB: banded, homozygous [1]. Bb: banded, heterozygous [1]. bb: plain, homozygous [1]. **(b)** A banded snail may be BB or Bb [1], because one B is enough to give the banded phenotype, so both genotypes look the same [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation needs both possible genotypes named and the reason (one dominant allele is enough); naming only one genotype does not answer "cannot be sure".

**3. (a)** The entire genetic material of an organism [1]. **(b)** 1 500 000 000 × 0.02 [1] = **30 000 000** base pairs [1]. **(c)** 1 500 000 000 − 30 000 000 = **1 470 000 000** base pairs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication line; a bare figure gives the reader nothing to follow if it is wrong, whereas clear working shows your method.

**4. (a)** Adult height and body mass: continuous [1]. Eye colour and ABO blood group: discontinuous [1]. **(b)** Discontinuous: bar chart with gaps between the bars [1]. Continuous: histogram or smooth bell-shaped curve, with no gaps [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the graph is not enough on its own; saying whether the bars are separate or touching shows you understand why the graph differs.

**5. (a)** Identical twins have the same genome [1], so they have the same alleles for the same genes [1]. **(b)** Any two from: nutrition or diet; illness in childhood; exercise or lifestyle; access to healthcare **[1] [1]**. **(c)** (176 − 171) ÷ 176 × 100 [1] = 2.84%, so **2.8%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage difference is measured against the starting value stated in the question (twin A); dividing by 171 gives a different number.

**6. (a)** 170-189 mg [1]. **(b)** 21 ÷ 60 × 100 [1] = **35%** [1]. **(c)** Most seeds are near the middle of the range [1], with fewer at the lowest and highest masses, giving a bell shape with no gaps [1]. **(d)** Mass can take any value in a range [1] and is influenced by many genes together with the environment [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Normal distribution" is a fair label for the shape, but a description using the data (most in the middle, few at the ends) is safer than the label alone.

**7. (a)** A change in the base sequence of DNA [1]. **(b)** 210 ÷ 250 × 100 [1] = **84%** [1]. **(c)** Ionising radiation, ultraviolet light or certain chemicals (any one) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give one named factor, not "the environment", which is too general to be a specific cause.

**8.** The statement is mostly incorrect. A dominant allele is not stronger and does not stop the recessive allele working [1]; it is simply the one whose characteristic shows when both alleles are present [1]. Dominant alleles are not always the most common; Huntington's disease is caused by a dominant allele yet is rare [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement plus reasons; supporting evidence such as a named example of a rare dominant allele is stronger than assertion.

**9. (a)** 1.4 ÷ 5.6 × 100 = **25%** [1]. **(b)** The variant has lower activity than the normal enzyme at every concentration [1]; it works at 25% of the normal rate at all three concentrations, and both increase with substrate concentration [1]. **(c)** The mutation changes the amino acid sequence, so the enzyme folds into a different shape [1], changing the active site so the substrate fits less well [1]. **(d)** The change does not alter the shape of the active site [1], so the substrate still binds as before [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures when describing data; "the variant is lower" without numbers is weaker than "25% of normal at each concentration".

**10. (a)** GGC also codes for Gly, so the amino acid sequence is unchanged [1]. **(b)** The protein is Met-Pro-Gly [1], because the new UAA codon is a stop codon, so translation ends early [1]; the polypeptide is much shorter and so has a different shape and is unlikely to work [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Read the sequence codon by codon from the start; give the three amino acids in order, not just "shorter".

**11. (a)** DNA that does not code for a protein [1]. **(b)** The variant changes how the gene is expressed [1]; it may stop the control that normally prevents transcription of the gene to mRNA, so the gene stays switched on [1]; more protein P is made in adulthood, so the phenotype changes even though the protein is normal [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The answer must say that the protein is unchanged but the amount or timing changes; saying "the protein is altered" describes a coding variant, not this one.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing definitions that are vague, such as "a gene is what you inherit".
- Confusing genotype (letters) and phenotype (characteristic).
- Saying a dominant phenotype proves the genotype is homozygous.
- Calling the dominant allele "stronger" or "more common".
- Not stating whether bars on a graph are separate or touching.
- Using the wrong base value for a percentage difference.
- Describing data without quoting figures.
- Skipping the chain from mutation to amino acids to shape to active site.
- Saying non-coding variants change the protein, when they change how much or when it is made.
