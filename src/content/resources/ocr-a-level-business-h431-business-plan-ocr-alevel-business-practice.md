## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain **one** purpose of a business plan for a business that is applying for a bank loan. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** contents of a business plan. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a business plan includes a cash-flow forecast. **[3]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by the "Plan-Do-Review" cycle. **[3]**

**5.** Distinguish between a strategic review and a review of a monthly budget. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Corran Coffee runs a coffee van. Its business plan, prepared for a bank, contains this cash-flow forecast. The opening balance in month 1 is £6,000.

| £ | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receipts | 5,200 | 6,800 | 7,400 |
| Payments | 8,900 | 6,100 | 6,300 |

**(a)** Calculate the closing balance for each month. **[3]**

**(b)** Receipts turn out to be 15% lower than forecast each month, while payments are unchanged. Calculate the new closing balance for each month. **[3]**

**(c)** Evaluate how useful this cash-flow forecast is to the bank in deciding whether to lend to Corran Coffee. **[6]**

**7.** Ashby Dental Practice planned 2,400 appointments last quarter but completed 2,160. Its plan allowed for 150 missed appointments, where patients did not arrive; there were 310. Analyse how the Plan-Do-Review cycle could improve the performance of Ashby Dental Practice. **[6]**

**8.** Tarrow Brewing Co wants to build a second brewery. It is asking a bank for a £150,000 loan and an investor for £200,000 in return for 25% of the shares. It has written a detailed business plan. Evaluate the impact and importance of the business plan to **two** of Tarrow Brewing Co's stakeholders. **[9]**

**9.** Pip & Tuck is a new online business selling children's clothing designed by its two founders. They are funding it entirely from their own savings of £18,000. Fashions in the market change every few months. One founder wants to spend three weeks writing a full business plan; the other says they should start selling immediately. Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages to Pip & Tuck of having a business plan. **[9]**

**10.** Hesketh Hardware has three hardware shops in market towns. Its sales revenue has fallen for four years in a row as customers buy online, and one shop now makes a loss. The new managing director has announced a strategic review. Evaluate the impact and importance of a strategic review to Hesketh Hardware and its stakeholders. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** To persuade the bank that the business can repay the loan with interest [1], by showing forecasts of sales, profit and cash flow supported by evidence such as market research [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: executive summary; aims and objectives; market research; marketing plan; operations and staffing; cash-flow and profit forecasts; finance needed and its sources; risks and contingencies [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** It shows the expected timing of cash coming in and going out [1], so the owner and lenders can see whether the business will have enough cash to pay its bills each month [1], and can arrange an overdraft in advance for any months when the balance falls too low [1]. **[3]**

**4.** A repeating process [1] in which a business sets objectives and targets (plan), carries them out (do) [1], then compares the results with the plan, finds the causes of any differences and uses the lessons in the next plan (review) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A budget review compares actual income and costs with the budget for a short period to control spending [1]. A strategic review is wider and less frequent [1]: it asks whether the strategy, objectives and markets still suit the business and its environment [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Month 1: 6,000 + 5,200 − 8,900 = **£2,300** [1]. Month 2: 2,300 + 6,800 − 6,100 = **£3,000** [1]. Month 3: 3,000 + 7,400 − 6,300 = **£4,100** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Receipts: £4,420, £5,780, £6,290. Month 1: 6,000 + 4,420 − 8,900 = **£1,520** [1]. Month 2: 1,520 + 5,780 − 6,100 = **£1,200** [1]. Month 3: 1,200 + 6,290 − 6,300 = **£1,190** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Useful: it shows that the balance stays positive in every month, even with receipts 15% lower [1], so the business appears able to meet its payments [1]. Limitations: it covers only three months, so the bank cannot see whether the loan can be repaid over its full term [1]; the receipts are the owner's own estimates for a weather-dependent business, so they may be optimistic [1]; with lower receipts the balance falls to about £1,200, a thin margin for an unexpected repair to the van [1]. Judgement: it is useful as evidence of short-term survival, but the bank would want a longer forecast and evidence for the sales figures before lending [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Shortfall: 2,400 − 2,160 = 240 appointments, 10% below plan [1]; missed appointments were 160 more than planned, so missed appointments explain most of the shortfall [1].
Review: the practice can find the cause, for example patients forgetting or booking slots they cannot attend [1].
Next plan: introduce text reminders or a deposit for booking, and set a target to cut missed appointments to, say, 150 [1].
Performance improves because more of the dentists' time is used, raising revenue without extra fixed costs [1]; the next review then checks whether the reminders worked, so the loop continues to improve results [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Bank: the plan's cash-flow and profit forecasts show whether the £150,000 loan and interest can be repaid [1]; the plan is therefore decisive, since without it the bank is unlikely to lend [1]. The bank will also look at the owners' own investment and at the risks section, since the new brewery may take time to reach full output [1].
Investor: the investor is buying 25% of the shares, which values the business at £800,000 (200,000 ÷ 0.25) [1], so they will judge whether forecast growth justifies that value [1]; the plan's market research and management section matter most to them, as their return depends on long-term growth rather than repayment [1].
Evaluation: the plan is important to both, but for different reasons: the bank cares about security and cash flow, the investor about growth [1]. Its usefulness depends on how realistic the forecasts are; an over-optimistic plan might win the finance but leave Tarrow unable to meet expectations [1]. Judgement: the plan is more important to the investor, who has no security and cannot easily withdraw, whereas the bank may take security over the brewery [1]. **[9]**

**9.** Advantages: writing a plan forces the founders to research customers and competitors before spending their £18,000 [1]; a cash-flow forecast would show how long the savings will last if sales are slow [1]; it would help two founders agree on roles and targets, reducing conflict [1].
Disadvantages: three weeks of writing delays sales in a market where fashions change every few months [1], so the designs may be out of date when the plan is complete [1]; they do not need outside finance, so one of the main purposes of a plan, persuading lenders, does not apply [1]; forecasts for a new online brand are likely to be guesses [1].
Evaluation: a full, detailed plan is probably not worth three weeks, but no plan at all risks running out of savings [1]. Judgement: a short plan with a cash-flow forecast and simple targets, written in a few days and reviewed monthly, would give most of the benefits at a fraction of the cost [1]. **[9]**

**10.** What the review examines: whether the strategy of selling through three market-town shops still suits a market moving online [1], including Hesketh's strengths, such as local advice, and weaknesses, such as higher costs than online rivals [1].
Impact on the business: it may lead to closing the loss-making shop, which cuts costs [1], or to new strategies such as a click-and-collect website or specialising in trade customers [1]; these options need investment and management time [1].
Stakeholders: employees may face redundancy if a shop closes, or retraining for online work, so uncertainty may lower morale during the review [1]; customers in the town that loses a shop lose a local service [1]; owners may need to invest more money or accept lower profit in the short term [1]; suppliers may lose orders if ranges are cut, or gain if online sales grow [1].
Importance: sales have fallen for four years, so continuing unchanged is likely to lead to further losses [1], which makes the review important rather than optional [1].
Judgement: the review is very important to Hesketh's survival, but its value depends on the managing director acting on its findings quickly and communicating clearly with staff, so that uncertainty does not drive good employees away [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming plan contents without explaining who uses them and why.
- Forgetting to carry each month's closing balance forward as the next opening balance.
- Treating a cash-flow forecast as proof that a loan can be repaid over its whole term.
- Describing Plan-Do-Review without linking the review to a change in the next plan.
- Discussing a strategic review without saying how each stakeholder is affected.
