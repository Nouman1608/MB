Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **9.4 Reactivity series**. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Reactivity series study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-reactivity-series/).

## The order (learn exactly)

```
K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > (C) > Zn > Fe > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au
```

Potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, **carbon**, zinc, iron, **hydrogen**, copper, silver, gold.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reactivity series | Metals listed in order of reactivity, most reactive first |
| Tendency to form positive ions | How readily a metal atom loses electrons; greater tendency = more reactive |
| Displacement | A more reactive metal replaces a less reactive metal in a compound or solution |
| Spectator ion | Ion present but unchanged (e.g. SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻), left out of ionic equations |
| Oxide layer | Thin, unreactive aluminium oxide coating that hides aluminium's true reactivity |
| Apparent unreactivity | Aluminium seems unreactive only because of its oxide layer |

## Why carbon and hydrogen are in the list

- **Hydrogen:** metals above it give hydrogen with dilute acids; metals below it do not react.
- **Carbon:** metals below it can be extracted from their oxides by heating with carbon.

## Displacement grid (five named metals)

| Added ↓ / ions → | Mg²⁺ | Zn²⁺ | Fe²⁺ | Cu²⁺ | Ag⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mg | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Zn | ✗ | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Fe | ✗ | ✗ | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Cu | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | — | ✓ |
| Ag | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | — |

Rule: a metal displaces every metal **below** it. Count ticks per row to rank.

## Ionic equations to know

```
Mg + Zn²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Zn
Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu
Fe + Cu²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Cu
Mg + Fe²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Fe
Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag
```

Check that the charges balance: in the last equation, 2+ on each side.

## Displacement observations

| Reaction | What you see |
|---|---|
| Zn or Fe or Mg in CuSO₄(aq) | Pink-brown copper forms; blue colour fades; warms up |
| Cu in AgNO₃(aq) | Silvery-grey crystals form; solution turns blue |
| Mg in FeSO₄(aq) | Dark grey iron forms; pale green colour fades |
| Any metal in its own ions or ions of a more reactive metal | No change |

## Reactions in the syllabus

| Metal | Cold water | Steam | Dilute HCl |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potassium | Very violent; lilac flame | — | — |
| Sodium | Vigorous; melts, fizzes | — | — |
| Calcium | Steady fizzing; cloudy solution | — | — |
| Magnesium | Very slow | Reacts: MgO + H₂ | Fast fizzing |
| Zinc | — | — | Steady fizzing |
| Iron | — | — | Slow fizzing |
| Copper, silver, gold | — | — | No reaction |

Products: cold water → hydroxide + H₂; steam → oxide + H₂; HCl → chloride + H₂.

## Aluminium in one paragraph

Aluminium is above zinc but often appears unreactive. A thin, tightly bonded layer of **aluminium oxide** covers the surface and stops air, water and acid reaching the metal. Remove the layer and aluminium reacts as its position predicts.

## Deducing an order: method

1. For displacement data, count how many other metals each one displaces.
2. For rate data, rank by vigour (fastest bubbling, biggest temperature rise).
3. Check the comparison was fair (same mass, surface area, acid concentration, temperature).
4. Write the order most reactive first, using the letters given.

## Exam traps

- Aluminium is **above** zinc, despite its oxide layer.
- The **more** reactive metal does the displacing.
- Copper, silver and gold do **not** react with dilute hydrochloric acid at all.
- Steam gives the **oxide**; cold water gives the **hydroxide**.
- "Reactive" should be explained as a tendency to **form positive ions** (lose electrons).
- Balance electrons in ionic equations: Cu + **2**Ag⁺.
- Include carbon and hydrogen when asked to state the series.
- Metal ions are not displaced by the same metal: zinc in zinc sulfate shows no change.

## Self-test

1. State the reactivity series from potassium to gold, including carbon and hydrogen.
2. Explain what "more reactive" means in terms of ions.
3. Name the products of magnesium with steam.
4. Which metals in the syllabus list do not react with dilute hydrochloric acid?
5. Write the ionic equation for iron with copper(II) ions.
6. Give two observations when zinc is added to copper(II) sulfate solution.
7. Explain why copper displaces silver from silver nitrate solution.
8. Why does aluminium appear unreactive?
9. Metal X displaces copper but not zinc. Place X, copper and zinc in order.
10. Name the products of calcium with cold water.
11. Which is the spectator ion when magnesium reacts with copper(II) sulfate solution?
12. Why are carbon and hydrogen included in the series?

**Answers:** 1. K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, C, Zn, Fe, H, Cu, Ag, Au. 2. It has a greater tendency to lose electrons and form positive ions. 3. Magnesium oxide and hydrogen. 4. Copper, silver, gold. 5. Fe + Cu²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Cu. 6. Pink-brown solid forms; blue colour fades (and the mixture warms). 7. Copper is more reactive than silver: copper atoms lose electrons to silver ions, forming Cu²⁺ and silver. 8. A thin oxide layer on its surface stops air, water and acids reaching the metal. 9. Zinc > X > copper. 10. Calcium hydroxide and hydrogen. 11. Sulfate ion, SO₄²⁻. 12. As reference points: hydrogen shows which metals react with dilute acids; carbon shows which metal oxides can be reduced by carbon.
