These questions cover **4.2.3 Domestic uses and safety** (4.2.3.1 and 4.2.3.2) of AQA GCSE Physics (8463),
assessed on Paper 1 at both tiers. Several are extended "explain" questions: each mark in the answers is one link

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the frequency and the potential difference of the UK domestic electricity supply. **[2]**

**2.** Name the type of potential difference supplied by a battery. **[1]**

**3.** Match each wire to its insulation colour: live, neutral, earth. **[3]**

**4.** State the potential of the earth wire and when it carries a current. **[2]**

**5.** An alternating supply completes 120 cycles in 2.0 s. Calculate its frequency. **[2]**

**6.** A pd–time graph of an alternating supply shows that one complete cycle takes 0.020 s. Show that this matches the UK mains frequency. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain the difference between direct potential difference and alternating potential difference, and describe how each would appear on a graph of pd against time. **[4]**

**8.** An electrician says: "The neutral wire and the earth wire are both at about 0 V, so they do the same job."

**(a)** Explain why the electrician's statement is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** State the pd between the live wire and the neutral wire. **[1]**

**9.** A ceiling lamp is switched off at the wall switch. A person changing the bulb touches a bare part of the live wire on the supply side of the switch.

**(a)** Explain why the person can receive an electric shock even though the lamp is switched off. **[4]**
**(b)** State what the person should have done before starting work. **[1]**

**10.** A metal kettle is connected to the mains by three-core cable. Inside the kettle the insulation on the live wire wears through, and the live wire touches the metal body.

**(a)** Explain how the earth wire prevents the user getting a shock. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the connection between the live wire and earth is itself dangerous. **[3]**

**11.** A faulty metal lamp has an earth wire that has come loose from its casing. The live wire then touches the casing.

**(a)** State the potential of the casing. **[1]**
**(b)** A person standing on the ground touches the casing. Explain what happens. **[3]**
**(c)** Compare this with what would happen if the earth wire were still connected. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **50 Hz** [1]; **about 230 V** [1]

**2.** Direct potential difference (dc) [1]

**3.** Live – brown [1]; neutral – blue [1]; earth – green and yellow stripes [1]

**4.** 0 V [1]; only when there is a fault [1]

**5.** f = 120 ÷ 2.0 [1]; f = **60 Hz** [1]

**6.** f = 1 ÷ 0.020 [1]; f = 50 Hz, which is the UK mains frequency [1]

**7.** A direct pd acts in one direction only, so current always flows the same way [1]; an alternating pd keeps reversing direction, so the current keeps changing direction [1]; on a pd–time graph a direct pd is a horizontal line on one side of zero [1]; an alternating pd is a repeating wave that goes above and below zero [1]

**8. (a)** The neutral wire completes the circuit, so it carries current whenever the appliance is in use [1]; the earth wire is a safety wire that only carries current if there is a fault [1]
**(b)** About 230 V [1]

**9. (a)** The switch only breaks the circuit through the lamp; the live wire on the supply side is still at about 230 V [1]; the person is at, or close to, 0 V because they are in contact with earth [1]; so there is a pd of about 230 V across their body [1]; this drives a current through the body to earth, which is the shock [1]
**(b)** Switch off the supply to that circuit at the mains (isolate it) before starting work, so the live wire is no longer at about 230 V [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two marks most often missed in (a) are the person being at 0 V and the pd across the body; "the electricity is still there" scores nothing.

**10. (a)** The earth wire is connected to the metal body of the kettle [1]; when the live wire touches the body, current flows through the earth wire to earth [1]; the body stays at 0 V, so it does not become live and there is no large pd across the user [1]
**(b)** The live wire is at about 230 V and the earth path has a very low resistance [1]; so a very large current flows [1]; this heats the wires strongly and can cause a fire [1]

**11. (a)** About 230 V (the casing is live) [1]
**(b)** There is a pd of about 230 V between the casing and the ground, across the person [1]; a current flows through the person's body to earth [1]; the person receives an electric shock, which can be fatal [1]
**(c)** With the earth wire connected, the current would flow through the earth wire instead [1]; the casing would stay at 0 V, so no large pd would be across the person and there would be no shock [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the neutral wire carries no current".
- Giving 230 Hz or 50 V.
- Explaining a shock without the pd across the body.
- Saying the earth wire "stops electricity" without describing where the current goes.
- Missing the fire risk from the very large current in a live-to-earth fault.
