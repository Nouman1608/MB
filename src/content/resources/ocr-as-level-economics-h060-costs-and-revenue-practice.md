These questions cover **Costs and revenue** for the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification, for first assessment in 2016, Component 01, Microeconomics. The current specification names PED and a firm's total revenue at AS, with fuller cost and revenue calculation at A Level, so check the scope with your teacher. The mark totals are our own and every firm and figure is invented. Explanations are in the [costs and revenue study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-costs-and-revenue/) and the [costs and revenue revision notes](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-costs-and-revenue-revision-notes/).

Questions 7 and 11 use this invented data for Firm Kestrel.

| Output (units) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price (£) | 50 | 45 | 40 | 35 | 30 |
| Total cost (£) | 520 | 700 | 900 | 1,180 | 1,500 |

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Distinguish between fixed costs and variable costs, giving an example of each. **[2]**

**2.** Which statement about the long run is correct?

A: All costs are fixed

B: All costs are variable

C: Fixed costs are greater than variable costs

D: Average cost is constant **[1]**

**3.** A café has fixed costs of £900 a week and variable costs of £1.50 per meal.

**(a)** Calculate total cost at 600 meals a week. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate average cost at 600 meals. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate average cost at 900 meals. **[1]**

**4.** A firm sells 2,500 units at £6.00. It raises the price to £6.60 and sales fall to 2,400.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue before and after the price rise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the price elasticity of demand. **[2]**

**5.** Define average revenue and explain why it equals price when all units are sold at the same price. **[2]**

**6.** Total revenue rises from £3,600 to £3,900 when sales rise from 300 to 350 units. Calculate marginal revenue per unit. **[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Use the data for Firm Kestrel.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue and profit at each output. **[3]**

**(b)** Identify the profit-maximising output and the revenue-maximising output in the table. **[2]**

**(c)** Identify the output with the lowest average cost. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why a cinema's average cost per customer falls as more customers attend. **[3]**

**9.** Explain, with the aid of a diagram, how total revenue changes as price falls along a straight-line demand curve. **[6]**

**10.** A theatre's costs for a performance are almost entirely fixed. Explain why it might sell unsold seats at a large discount shortly before the show. **[4]**

**11.** Firm Kestrel's managers receive a bonus based on sales revenue. Using the data, explain how this could affect the firm's output and profit. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether a firm should cut its price in order to increase sales. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Fixed costs do not change with output in the short run, such as rent [1]. Variable costs change with output, such as raw materials [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1]. In the long run all factors of production can be changed. **[1]**

**3. (a)** 900 + 1.50 × 600 = **£1,800** [1].

**(b)** 1,800 ÷ 600 = **£3.00** [1].

**(c)** (900 + 1,350) ÷ 900 = **£2.50** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* AC falls because the fixed £900 is spread over more meals; say so if asked to explain.

**4. (a)** Before: 6.00 × 2,500 = £15,000; after: 6.60 × 2,400 = £15,840 [1]. TR rises by £840 [1].

**(b)** % change in price = +10%, % change in quantity = −100 ÷ 2,500 = −4% [1]. PED = −4 ÷ 10 = **−0.4** [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Average revenue is total revenue divided by the number of units sold [1]. If every unit sells at the same price, TR = P × Q, so TR ÷ Q = P [1]. **[2]**

**6.** MR = 300 ÷ 50 = **£6** per unit [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** TR = £500, £900, £1,200, £1,400 and £1,500 [1]. Profit = −£20, £200 and £300 at 10, 20 and 30 units [1]; £220 and £0 at 40 and 50 units [1].

**(b)** Profit is maximised at **30 units** (£300) [1]; revenue is maximised at **50 units** (£1,500) [1].

**(c)** AC is £52, £35, £30, £29.50 and £30, so the lowest is at **40 units** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The profit-maximising output need not be the lowest-cost output, because profit depends on revenue as well as cost.

**8.** Most of a cinema's costs, such as rent, equipment and staff for each showing, are fixed [1]. As attendance rises, these fixed costs are spread over more customers, so average fixed cost falls [1]. The variable cost of an extra customer is very small, so average cost per customer falls [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Diagram of a straight-line demand curve with price and quantity axes, with revenue rectangles at a high and a low price [1]. Above the midpoint demand is price elastic [1], so a price cut raises quantity proportionately more and TR rises [1]. At the midpoint PED is −1 and TR is at its maximum [1]. Below the midpoint demand is inelastic [1], so further price cuts reduce TR [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Fixed costs must be paid whether or not the seats are sold [1], and an extra customer adds almost nothing to cost [1]. Any revenue from a discounted seat therefore adds to profit, or reduces the loss [1]. Last-minute buyers are likely to be price sensitive (elastic demand), so a discount can fill many seats [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Managers would push output towards 50 units, where TR is highest at £1,500 [1], instead of 30 units where profit is highest [1]. At 50 units profit is zero, compared with £300 at 30 units [1], so shareholders lose £300 of profit while managers gain a larger bonus [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both outputs and both profit figures; the contrast is the point of the question.

**12.** Award up to 8 marks across knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation.
- If demand is price elastic, a price cut raises TR [1], because quantity rises proportionately more than price falls [1].
- Higher sales may also spread fixed costs, lowering average cost [1].
- But extra sales add variable costs, so profit may fall even if revenue rises [1].
- If demand is inelastic, the cut reduces TR and profit [1].
- Rivals may match the cut, so the expected rise in sales does not happen [1].
- A cut may suit other objectives, such as market share or growth [1].
- Judgement: the firm should cut price only if demand is elastic, rivals are unlikely to follow, and the extra revenue exceeds the extra cost [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a cost fixed because it is large.
- Treating revenue and profit as the same thing.
- Multiplying instead of dividing when finding average cost.
- Leaving out units or "per unit".
- Giving one-sided answers to evaluation questions without a judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is profit not maximised where revenue is maximised?**
Near the revenue maximum, extra units add little to revenue but still add to cost, so profit starts to fall before revenue peaks.

**Do fixed costs affect the best price to charge?**
Fixed costs must be paid whatever price is chosen, so the profit-maximising price depends on how revenue and variable costs change with output.
