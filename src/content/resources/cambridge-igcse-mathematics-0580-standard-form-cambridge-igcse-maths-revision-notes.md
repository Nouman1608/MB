These notes condense subtopic **1.8 Standard form** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core (C1.8) and Extended (E1.8) have the same statements, but the syllabus notes that **Core candidates calculate with standard form only on Paper 3**; Extended candidates may calculate with it on Papers 2 and 4. For full worked examples, use the [standard form study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-standard-form-cambridge-igcse-maths/); to test yourself, use the [standard form practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-standard-form-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The rule

**A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ⩽ A < 10 and n is an integer.**

| Size of number | Sign of n | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 10 or more | positive | 52 000 = 5.2 × 10⁴ |
| 1 up to 10 | zero | 5.2 = 5.2 × 10⁰ |
| between 0 and 1 | negative | 0.0052 = 5.2 × 10⁻³ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standard form | A × 10ⁿ with 1 ⩽ A < 10, n an integer |
| A | The number in front; one non-zero digit before the point |
| n | The power of 10: how many places the point has moved |
| Ordinary number | The number written out in full (52 000, 0.0052) |

## Conversions in steps

```
INTO standard form
1. Put the point after the first non-zero digit -> A.
2. Count the places the point moved -> size of n.
3. Big number -> n positive; small number -> n negative.

OUT of standard form
Move the point n places: right if n > 0, left if n < 0.

FIXING A
A too big   (e.g. 63 × 10⁴):   ÷ 10 and n + 1  -> 6.3 × 10⁵
A too small (e.g. 0.63 × 10⁴): × 10 and n − 1  -> 6.3 × 10³
```

## Operations

| Operation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| × | Multiply As, add powers, fix A | (2 × 10³)(7 × 10⁵) = 14 × 10⁸ = 1.4 × 10⁹ |
| ÷ | Divide As, subtract powers, fix A | (3 × 10²) ÷ (6 × 10⁻⁴) = 0.5 × 10⁶ = 5 × 10⁵ |
| + or − | Match powers or use ordinary numbers | 2.3 × 10⁴ + 9 × 10³ = 23 000 + 9000 = 32 000 = 3.2 × 10⁴ |
| Power | Raise A, multiply the power | (3 × 10⁻²)² = 9 × 10⁻⁴ |
| Square root | Make the power even first | √(3.6 × 10⁵) = √(36 × 10⁴) = 6 × 10² |

## Ordering

1. Compare n first: the larger power is the larger number (for positive numbers).
2. If the powers are equal, compare A.
3. Convert any ordinary numbers to standard form first.

Example, smallest first: 6 × 10⁻⁵, 2 × 10⁻⁴, 0.0003, 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ → 6 × 10⁻⁵, 1.5 × 10⁻⁴, 2 × 10⁻⁴, 0.0003.

## Calculator (Papers 3 and 4)

- Use the ×10ˣ (EXP) key so each number is entered whole.
- Bracket standard form numbers in divisions if typing "× 10 ^".
- Rewrite the display in proper notation; never copy the calculator's own format.
- To 3 significant figures in standard form means A has two decimal places: 6.02 × 10⁻³.

## Exam traps

- 47 × 10⁴ has the right value but is not in standard form.
- Small numbers need negative powers.
- Adding or subtracting: never add the powers.
- After multiplying, A may reach 10 or more; after dividing, A may drop below 1. Always fix it.
- "Write as an ordinary number" means no powers of 10 in the answer.
- Core: standard form calculations are expected on Paper 3 only, but conversions can appear on Paper 1.

## Quick self-test

1. Write 708 000 in standard form.
2. Write 0.000 036 in standard form.
3. Write 9.04 × 10⁻³ as an ordinary number.
4. Write 260 × 10⁻⁵ in standard form.
5. Work out (8 × 10⁶) × (4 × 10⁻²) in standard form.
6. Work out (1.8 × 10³) ÷ (9 × 10⁷) in standard form.
7. Work out 5.2 × 10⁵ − 4 × 10⁴ in standard form.
8. Work out (5 × 10³)² in standard form.
9. Which is larger: 9.9 × 10⁶ or 1.1 × 10⁷?
10. Order, smallest first: 4 × 10⁻², 0.035, 3.6 × 10⁻³.
11. (calculator) Work out (6.3 × 10⁸) ÷ (2.7 × 10⁻³), giving your answer in standard form to 3 significant figures.
12. A bacterium is 2 × 10⁻⁶ m long. How many, end to end, make 1 cm?

## Where marks are usually lost

- Correct value, wrong form (A outside 1 ⩽ A < 10).
- Sign of n wrong for small numbers.
- Adding powers when adding numbers.
- Not converting units before dividing in context questions.
- Rounding the ordinary number to 3 s.f. but then writing A with the wrong power.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does A have to be a decimal?**
No. A can be a whole number from 1 to 9, as in 9 × 10⁻². It just has to satisfy 1 ⩽ A < 10.

**How do I check a standard form answer quickly?**
Estimate with the powers of 10 alone. For (6.3 × 10⁸) ÷ (2.7 × 10⁻³), the powers give 10¹¹ and 6.3 ÷ 2.7 is about 2, so an answer near 2 × 10¹¹ is expected.
