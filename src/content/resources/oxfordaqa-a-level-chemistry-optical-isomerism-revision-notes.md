## What the specification asks for

Section 3.3.7.1 says you should be able to:

- draw the structural formulas and displayed formulas of enantiomers
- understand how racemic mixtures (racemates) are formed and why they are optically inactive.

It also states that optical isomerism is limited to molecules with a **single chiral centre**. The section lists no practical activity.

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Stereoisomers | Same structural formula; the atoms differ in how they are arranged in space |
| Optical isomerism | Stereoisomerism that results from chirality in a molecule |
| Asymmetric carbon atom (chiral centre) | Carbon with four different atoms or groups attached |
| Enantiomers (optical isomers) | Mirror-image forms that cannot be superimposed; they affect plane-polarised light differently |
| Plane-polarised light | Light with all its vibrations in one plane |
| Optically active | Turns the plane of plane-polarised light |
| Racemic mixture (racemate) | Both enantiomers mixed in equal amounts |

## Spotting a chiral centre

**Never chiral:** a carbon in C=C, C=O or C≡N; any CH₃ or CH₂; any carbon in a benzene ring.

**Check fully:** every CH carbon and every carbon with no hydrogen. Follow each of its four groups to the end of the chain before deciding two groups are the same.

**Quick contrasts**

| Compound | Carbon checked | Its four groups | Chiral? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Butane-1,2-diol, HOCH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ | C2 | H, OH, CH₂OH, CH₂CH₃ | Yes |
| 1-phenylethanol, C₆H₅CH(OH)CH₃ | the C–OH carbon | H, OH, CH₃, C₆H₅ | Yes |
| 1-chloro-2-methylpropane, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂Cl | C2 | H, CH₃, CH₃, CH₂Cl | No |
| 3-methylhexan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂C(OH)(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH₃ | C3 | OH, CH₃, C₂H₅, C₃H₇ | Yes, with no H on it |

In butane-1,2-diol, C1 is a CH₂ group, so C2 is the only chiral centre. In 1-phenylethanol, none of the ring carbons can be chiral.

## Drawing enantiomers: method in steps

1. Mark the asymmetric carbon with an asterisk.
2. Give it two flat bonds, a wedge (pointing at you) and a hashed bond (pointing away).
3. Put one of the four groups on each bond.
4. Draw a vertical mirror line.
5. Redraw the molecule as its reflection: left and right swap; the wedge and hashed bond keep their groups.
6. For **structural formulas**, write the groups as CH₃, OH, COOH and so on.
7. For **displayed formulas**, keep the same arrangement at the chiral carbon but show every bond in every group, including C=O and O–H.

**Worked reminder: butane-1,2-diol.** C2 carries H, OH, CH₂OH and C₂H₅. With CH₂OH and C₂H₅ in the plane, OH on the wedge and H on the hashed bond:

```
              OH          :          OH
              ▼           :          ▼
   HOCH2  --  C  --  C2H5 : C2H5 --  C  --  CH2OH
              ⋮           :          ⋮
              H           :          H
```

(Key: ▼ wedge, out of the page; ⋮ hashed, into the page; plain line, flat on the page.)

## Plane-polarised light: the facts

- One enantiomer rotates the plane of plane-polarised light one way.
- The other rotates it by the **same angle** the **opposite way**, under the same conditions.
- Otherwise their physical properties match: identical melting points, boiling points and densities.
- A sample with only one enantiomer is optically active. A racemate is not.

## Racemates

**Definition:** equal amounts of the two enantiomers.

**Why optically inactive (three points):**

1. The two enantiomers turn the plane through equal angles.
2. One turns it clockwise and the other anticlockwise.
3. With equal numbers of each, the turning effects cancel: no net rotation.

**How one forms (four points):**

1. The carbon that becomes chiral is **planar** in the reactant or the intermediate.
2. The attacking species can reach **either side** of that plane.
3. Neither side is favoured: the chance of attack is **the same** for both.
4. So the two enantiomers form in **equal amounts**.

**Worked reminder: KCN with 2-methylpropanal (section 3.3.8.1).** 2-methylpropanal, (CH₃)₂CHCHO, gives 2-hydroxy-3-methylbutanenitrile, (CH₃)₂CHCH(OH)CN. C2 carries H, OH, CN and CH(CH₃)₂, so it is chiral. The C=O carbon is planar, so the cyanide ion has an equal chance of adding from above or below, and the product is a racemate. C3 carries two CH₃ groups and is not chiral.

**Worked reminder: bromine with propene (section 3.3.4.2).** Propene and Br₂ give 1,2-dibromopropane, CH₂BrCHBrCH₃. C2 carries H, Br, CH₃ and CH₂Br, so it is chiral. The intermediate carbocation is planar at C2, so Br⁻ adds to either face equally and the product is a racemate. So the product molecules are chiral, yet the sample shows no optical activity.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Chiral molecule vs optically active sample.** A racemate is made of chiral molecules but is not optically active.
- **Enantiomer vs mirror image.** Every molecule has a mirror image. Only a chiral molecule's mirror image cannot be superimposed on it.
- **Optical vs E–Z isomerism.** Optical: a carbon with four different groups. E–Z: a C=C that cannot rotate, where both carbons carry two unlike groups.
- **Aldehyde or unsymmetrical ketone vs symmetrical ketone** with KCN then dilute acid. Aldehydes and unsymmetrical ketones lead to a racemate. With a symmetrical ketone, the new C–OH carbon carries two identical groups, so the product is not chiral. Methanal gives no chiral product either, because it has two hydrogens on its C=O carbon.
- **Structural vs displayed formula.** Both must show the 3D arrangement at the chiral carbon. Only the displayed formula shows every bond.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the term *asymmetric carbon atom*.
2. State what is meant by a racemic mixture.
3. Does 3-chloro-2-methylhexane, (CH₃)₂CHCHClCH₂CH₂CH₃, have a chiral centre? If so, give the four groups.
4. Is 1-chloro-2-methylpropane chiral? Explain.
5. Explain why a solution of one enantiomer rotates the plane of plane-polarised light but a racemate does not.
6. Hexanal, CH₃(CH₂)₄CHO, reacts with KCN followed by dilute acid. Name the product and state whether it is a racemate.
7. How many stereoisomers does 1-phenylethanol have?
8. A sample contains 0.0250 mol of racemic 2-bromohexane, C₆H₁₃Br. Calculate the mass of each enantiomer in it. (Ar: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, Br = 79.9)
9. Which carbon in 2-hydroxy-2,4-dimethylpentanenitrile, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂C(OH)(CH₃)CN, is chiral?
10. But-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃, shows stereoisomerism. Which kind, and does it show optical isomerism?

### Answers

1. A carbon carrying four different atoms or groups.
2. A mixture of equal amounts of the two enantiomers of a compound.
3. Yes. C3 carries H, Cl, CH(CH₃)₂ and CH₂CH₂CH₃. (C2 carries two CH₃ groups, so it is not chiral.)
4. No. C2 carries two identical CH₃ groups, C1 is a CH₂ and the other carbons are CH₃.
5. Each enantiomer rotates the plane by the same angle in opposite directions. A single enantiomer gives a rotation. A racemate contains the two in a 1:1 ratio, so the two rotations cancel out.
6. 2-hydroxyheptanenitrile, CH₃(CH₂)₄CH(OH)CN. Yes: C2 carries H, OH, CN and C₅H₁₁, and the two faces of the flat C=O group are equally open to cyanide attack.
7. Two: one pair of enantiomers.
8. Mr of C₆H₁₃Br = 6(12.0) + 13(1.0) + 79.9 = 164.9. Each enantiomer is 0.0125 mol, so mass = 0.0125 × 164.9 = **2.06 g**.
9. C2: it carries OH, CN, CH₃ and CH₂CH(CH₃)₂. C4 carries two CH₃ groups.
10. E–Z isomerism. It has no carbon with four different groups, so it does not show optical isomerism.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the word "non-superimposable" when describing enantiomers.
- Leaving "equal amounts" out of the racemate definition.
- Saying a racemate is inactive because it "contains no chiral molecules". The molecules are chiral; the rotations cancel.
- Writing "the light is rotated" instead of "the **plane** of plane-polarised light is rotated".
- Putting an asterisk on a CH₂ or C=O carbon, or on a carbon in a benzene ring.
- Missing a chiral centre with no hydrogen on it, such as C3 in 3-methylhexan-3-ol.
- Drawing a "displayed formula" that still shows CH₃ or COOH as groups.
- Drawing the second enantiomer with all bonds flat, or with two groups swapped as well as reflected.
- Explaining racemate formation without "planar", "either side" and "equally likely".
