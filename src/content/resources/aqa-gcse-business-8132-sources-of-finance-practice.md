## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *retained profit*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** external sources of finance. **[2]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following is an internal source of finance? A: an overdraft. B: selling unwanted assets. C: a government grant. D: trade credit. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** disadvantage of a mortgage as a source of finance. **[2]**

**5.** Lannow Logistics buys a lorry on hire purchase. It pays a £6,000 deposit and 36 monthly payments of £1,250. The cash price is £48,000. Calculate how much more Lannow Logistics pays than the cash price. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why trade credit is useful to a business with a cash shortage. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Godrow Glass Ltd has 20,000 shares, all owned by its two founders. It plans to issue 5,000 new shares at £12 each to an investor.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of finance the share issue would raise. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the company the investor would own. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain **one** disadvantage to the founders of the share issue. **[2]**

**8.** Carnow Cleaning borrows £12,000 from a bank. It repays the loan in 30 monthly instalments of £452.

**(a)** Calculate the total amount repaid. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost of borrowing. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain **one** advantage of a bank loan compared with an overdraft for buying cleaning equipment. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why family and friends are often an important source of finance for a new business. **[3]**

**10.** Ludwell Leather is a sole trader. The owner says she will issue shares to raise money for a new workshop. Explain why this is not possible and suggest **one** alternative source. **[3]**

**11.** Perrow Pottery is an established business with £30,000 of retained profit. It needs £25,000 for a new kiln. A bank has offered a loan that would cost £4,500 in interest. Analyse whether Perrow Pottery should use retained profit or the loan. **[6]**

**12.** Saltow Smoothies is a new business opening a juice bar. The owner needs £35,000 for equipment and fitting out. She has £8,000 of savings and no trading record. She is considering a bank loan, a government grant for new businesses in the town centre, and money from family. Evaluate the most suitable source or combination of sources for Saltow Smoothies. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Profit that is kept in the business [1] rather than paid out to the owners, so it can be reinvested [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: family and friends, a new share issue, a loan, a mortgage, an overdraft, trade credit, hire purchase, government grants [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The loan is secured on property [1], so if the business cannot keep up repayments, the lender can take the property, and the business may lose its premises [1]. Also accept interest over a long period, with development. **[2]**

**5.** Instalments: 36 × £1,250 = £45,000 [1]. Total paid: £6,000 + £45,000 = £51,000 [1]. Extra cost: £51,000 − £48,000 = **£3,000** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The business receives goods now but pays the supplier later [1], so it can sell the goods and use that cash to pay, easing the shortage [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 5,000 × £12 = **£60,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Total shares 20,000 + 5,000 = 25,000 [1]; 5,000 ÷ 25,000 × 100 = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The founders now own only 80% [1], so they share future profits and control with the investor, who may disagree with their decisions [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 30 × £452 = **£13,560** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** £13,560 − £12,000 = **£1,560** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** A loan has fixed repayments over a set period [1], so Carnow can plan its cash, and the bank cannot suddenly withdraw it as it can an overdraft [1]. Interest on a loan is usually lower than on an overdraft, making it more suitable for long-lasting equipment [1]. **[3]**

**9.** A new business has no trading record [1], so banks may refuse to lend or charge high interest [1]. Family and friends know and trust the owner, so they may lend on easy terms with little or no interest [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Only companies can issue shares; a sole trader is not a company [1]. Alternative: a bank loan or mortgage [1], because a workshop is a long-term asset suited to long-term borrowing [1]. Also accept retained profit or a grant with a reason. **[3]**

**11.** Retained profit avoids the £4,500 interest cost [1] and keeps full ownership and control [1]. Perrow has £30,000, so it can afford the £25,000 [1]. However, using it leaves only £5,000 as a cash reserve [1], which might not cover an unexpected cost, so the business could be short of cash [1]. Judgement: retained profit, because it saves £4,500 and an established business can rebuild reserves from future profits; a loan would only be better if Perrow expects large unexpected costs [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: a loan must be repaid with interest; a grant usually does not; family money may be cheap and flexible [1].
Application: Saltow needs £35,000 but has only £8,000, leaving £27,000 to find [1]; it is new with no trading record [1].
Analysis: a bank may refuse a loan or charge high interest because of the lack of track record, though savings of £8,000 show commitment [1]; a grant for town-centre start-ups fits exactly and need not be repaid, so it reduces the debt the business must cover from early sales [1]; family money could fill the remaining gap on easy terms [1].
Evaluation: a grant is slow and competitive and may not cover the full amount [1]; family may not have enough and a business failure could damage relationships [1]. Judgement: a combination of savings, the grant and family money, with a smaller bank loan only if needed, because it keeps borrowing costs low for a new business with no trading record [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers calculate the £27,000 gap and match each source to the new business's lack of a track record.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a sole trader or partnership can issue shares.
- Leaving the deposit out of a hire purchase total.
- Recommending a loan for a new business without considering whether a bank would lend.
- Ignoring the interest cost or the effect on cash reserves when comparing sources.
- Ending an evaluation without a decision, or recommending every source with no reasoning.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I recommend a combination of sources?**
Yes. Many businesses use more than one source; justify each part using the amount needed and the business's situation.

**What should I always consider for a new business?**
Its lack of retained profit and trading record, which limits borrowing and makes savings, family and friends and grants more important.
