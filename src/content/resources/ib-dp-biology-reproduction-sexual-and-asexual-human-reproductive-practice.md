## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between sexual and asexual reproduction, giving one advantage of each. **[4]**

**2.** Explain the roles of meiosis and fusion of gametes in a sexual life cycle. **[3]**

**3.** A drawing of a human egg cell has a diameter of 84 mm. The actual diameter is 120 µm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a sperm cell is smaller than an egg cell and why far more sperm than eggs are produced. **[3]**

**4.** State the function of each of these male structures: **(a)** scrotum, **(b)** epididymis, **(c)** seminal vesicle. **[3]**

**5.** The table shows hormone concentrations in one menstrual cycle, in arbitrary units.

| Day | 4 | 8 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 18 | 22 | 26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oestradiol | 40 | 90 | 260 | 220 | 90 | 160 | 170 | 50 |
| LH | 6 | 7 | 10 | 48 | 20 | 6 | 5 | 5 |
| Progesterone | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 14 | 16 | 3 |

**(a)** Suggest the day on which ovulation most likely happened. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in LH between day 12 and day 13. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the rise in LH between day 12 and day 13. **[2]**
**(d)** State the source of the progesterone on day 22 and one role it has. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain the changes in the hormones between day 22 and day 26 and the effect on the endometrium. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the events of fertilization in humans that follow fusion of the sperm and egg cell membranes. **[5]**

**7.** In an IVF cycle, 14 eggs were collected and 10 were fertilized.

**(a)** Outline how hormones are used before the eggs are collected. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of eggs that were fertilized, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one risk of using hormones in this way. **[1]**

**8.** Describe the sequence of events in a flowering plant from pollination to formation of an embryo, and explain why a hermaphrodite flower still shows sexual reproduction. **[6]**

**9.** Describe four features of an insect-pollinated flower, linking each feature to its function. **[4]**

**10.** Cross-pollination is promoted in many species.

**(a)** Outline three methods of promoting cross-pollination. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why inbreeding is a disadvantage to a plant population. **[2]**
**(c)** A stigma belongs to a plant with self-incompatibility alleles S2 and S3. Pollen from donor A (alleles S1 and S2) and donor B (alleles S1 and S4) lands on the stigma. State the percentage of pollen from each donor that can grow, and explain. **[3]**

**11.** The table shows the dry mass of a single germinating seed.

| Day | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dry mass (mg) | 52 | 49 | 44 | 38 | 36 | 40 | 52 |

**(a)** Describe the change in dry mass from day 0 to day 12. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change from day 0 to day 8. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in dry mass from day 0 to day 8. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the change after day 8. **[2]**
**(e)** Distinguish between seed dispersal and pollination. **[2]**

**12.** A grower has a strawberry variety that is well suited to the farm and propagates it with runners.

**(a)** State why runners produce plants that are genetically identical to the parent. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest one advantage and one disadvantage of this method if a new fungal disease arrives. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a plant breeder who wants disease-resistant plants grows strawberries from seed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Asexual reproduction involves one parent and no gametes, giving genetically identical offspring [1]. Sexual reproduction involves fusion of two gametes made by meiosis, giving varied offspring [1]. Asexual advantage: offspring keep a genotype that suits the present environment, and reproduction is fast [1]. Sexual advantage: new gene combinations, so some offspring may be adapted to a changed environment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs a difference stated for both sides; naming only one process loses the comparison, and an advantage must be linked to environment or variation, not just "better".

**2.** Meiosis halves the chromosome number to produce haploid gametes [1]. It breaks up parental combinations of alleles, so gametes differ [1]. Fusion of gametes restores the diploid number and creates a new combination of alleles in the zygote [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what each process does to the chromosome number and to allele combinations.

**3. (a)** Convert 84 mm to 84 000 µm [1]. Magnification = 84 000 ÷ 120 = **×700** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The sperm swims to the egg, so a small, light cell with little food reserve is enough [1]. The egg does not travel and needs food reserves for the early embryo, so it is larger [1]. Many sperm are made because few reach the egg, while few eggs are made because each is large and costly to produce [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A magnification answer needs the units converted before dividing; a correct method with mixed units gives a number that is a thousand times out.

**4. (a)** The scrotum keeps the testes outside the abdomen at a temperature a little below body temperature [1]. **(b)** The epididymis stores sperm while they mature [1]. **(c)** The seminal vesicle adds fluid that nourishes the sperm and helps carry them [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a function, not just a position; "next to the testis" does not describe what the structure does.

**5. (a)** Day 14 (just after the LH peak on day 13) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Increase = 48 − 10 = 38 [1]. Percentage = 38 ÷ 10 × 100 = **380%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Oestradiol from the growing follicle is high, at 260 units on day 12 [1]. High oestradiol over time acts by positive feedback on the pituitary, causing an LH surge [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The corpus luteum (the remains of the follicle) secretes it [1]. It maintains the thick endometrium (or inhibits FSH and LH) [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** The corpus luteum breaks down, so progesterone falls from 16 to 3 and oestradiol falls from 170 to 50 [1]. With no hormone support the endometrium is shed in menstruation [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both the hormone and the data values when you explain a graph or table. State the type of feedback by name, because "hormone rises" alone does not explain the surge.

**6.** The sperm nucleus enters the egg [1]. The sperm tail and mitochondria are destroyed, so they are not passed on [1]. The nuclear membranes of both nuclei dissolve [1]. All the condensed chromosomes, 23 from each parent, take part in a joint mitosis [1]. This gives two diploid nuclei, each with 46 chromosomes [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the events in order and note that mitosis, not meiosis, follows; "the nuclei fuse" alone misses the dissolution of the membranes.

**7. (a)** Natural hormone secretion is suspended with drugs [1]. Injections of FSH (with LH) are given in artificial doses [1]. This induces superovulation, so many follicles mature at once [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 10 ÷ 14 × 100 = 71.43 [1], so **71.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Over-stimulation of the ovaries, or a multiple pregnancy if several embryos are transferred [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both parts of the idea: normal secretion stopped, then artificial doses given. The final answer should be given to the number of significant figures the question asks for.

**8.** Pollen is transferred from an anther to a stigma [1]. The pollen grain germinates and a pollen tube grows down the style [1]. The tube carries the male gamete to the ovule [1]. A male gamete fuses with the egg cell to make a zygote [1]. The zygote divides by mitosis to form an embryo [1]. Even a hermaphrodite flower makes gametes by meiosis, and these fuse, so it is sexual reproduction [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep pollination, pollen tube growth and fertilization as separate stages; answers that merge them lose marks even when all the words are present.

**9.** Any four, each feature with its function: large coloured petals attract insects [1]; nectar in nectaries rewards insects [1]; anthers on filaments where an insect touches them [1]; sticky or spiny pollen clings to the insect [1]; sticky stigma that brushes the insect and catches pollen. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a feature and its function together. Listing parts without functions is a common way to lose marks on an annotation task.

**10. (a)** Different maturation times for anthers and stigma [1]. Separate male and female flowers, or separate male and female plants [1]. Animals or wind carry pollen between plants [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Inbreeding lowers genetic diversity, so the population cannot adapt as well to change [1]. It also reduces vigour (fitness) of offspring [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Donor A: S2 pollen is blocked because it matches the stigma, S1 pollen can grow, so **50%** [1]. Donor B: neither S1 nor S4 matches S2 or S3, so **100%** can grow [1]. The match to an allele in the plant's own tissue blocks the pollen, so only pollen from other plants succeeds [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In genetic crosses of this kind, show which allele is blocked and which is not; a bare percentage is hard to credit if the reasoning is missing.

**11. (a)** Dry mass falls from 52 mg to 36 mg by day 8 [1], then rises back to 52 mg by day 12 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The seed takes in water, and the embryo releases gibberellin [1]. This triggers production of amylase, which digests stored starch to sugars [1]. The sugars are used in respiration and lost as carbon dioxide, so dry mass falls [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** (36 − 52) ÷ 52 × 100 [1] = **−30.8%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The seedling has leaves that photosynthesise [1]. It now makes more organic matter than it uses in respiration, so dry mass increases [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Dispersal moves seeds away from the parent plant [1]. Pollination is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage change must carry its sign. In an explanation question, name the process (respiration, photosynthesis) and the molecule involved rather than writing that the seed "uses energy".

**12. (a)** Runners grow by mitosis from the parent, so they carry the same genes [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Advantage: the plants stay well suited to the farm and are quick to produce [1]. Disadvantage: all plants are identical, so a disease that can infect one can infect all [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Sexual reproduction by seed uses meiosis and fusion of gametes [1]. This gives new combinations of alleles, so some seedlings may carry resistance [1]. Those can be selected and then grown on [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "suggest" question, tie the idea to the situation named, here the disease.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up pollination, fertilization and dispersal.
- Not naming the hormone, or not naming the type of feedback.
- Forgetting to convert units in magnification.
- Omitting the sign on a percentage decrease.
- Writing that gametes are made by mitosis.
- Saying a hermaphrodite plant reproduces asexually.
- Giving a flower part without its function.
- Describing IVF as only "mixing eggs and sperm" without the use of hormones.
