These questions cover section 3.1 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: class limits, boundaries and mid-points, and class intervals for grouped frequency distributions. Recall the methods from the [study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-frequency-distributions/) or the [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-frequency-distributions-revision-notes/). Show your working. Give answers exactly unless a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A shop records the number of emails it receives each day, grouped as 1-5, 6-10, 11-15 and 16-20.

**(a)** State the lower and upper boundaries of the class 6-10. **[2]**
**(b)** State the width of this class. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the mid-point of this class. **[1]**

**2.** Times to complete a task are recorded to 1 decimal place of a minute and grouped as 4.0-4.4, 4.5-4.9 and 5.0-5.4. For the class 4.5-4.9, find the boundaries, the width and the mid-point. **[4]**

**3.** The masses m kg of some parcels are grouped as 60 ≤ m < 70, 70 ≤ m < 85, 85 ≤ m < 100 and 100 ≤ m < 130.

**(a)** Explain why you do not need to adjust these classes to find boundaries. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the width and the mid-point of each class. **[4]**

**4.** The ages of people at a club are recorded in completed years and grouped as 20-24, 25-29 and 30-39.

**(a)** Marta is 24 years 11 months old. State her class. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the exact range of ages covered by 20-24 and state its width. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the mid-point of the class 30-39. **[1]**

**5.** A student groups some whole-number test scores as 0-10, 10-20, 20-30 and 40-50.

**(a)** Give two faults in these classes. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a corrected set of classes, with equal widths, that covers scores from 0 to 59. **[2]**

**6.** Data are recorded as whole numbers.

**(a)** A class has boundaries 24.5 and 34.5. State its limits, width and mid-point. **[3]**
**(b)** Another class has a mid-point of 47 and a width of 5. Find its lower and upper limits. **[3]**

**7.** The times, in minutes, that 24 customers waited are: 23, 41, 17, 35, 28, 52, 19, 33, 44, 26, 38, 15, 47, 31, 22, 29, 36, 12, 40, 25, 34, 55, 21, 30.

**(a)** Copy and complete a tally and frequency table using five classes of width 10 beginning at 10-19. **[3]**
**(b)** State the boundaries of the class 30-39 and its mid-point. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that your table accounts for every one of the customers. **[1]**

**8.** A teacher has 60 scores between 3 and 98 and wants to group them. She considers class widths of 1, 10 and 50. Evaluate each choice and recommend one. **[4]**

**9.** A clinic records waiting times in minutes.

| Time t (minutes) | 0 ≤ t < 5 | 5 ≤ t < 15 | 15 ≤ t < 30 | 30 ≤ t < 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 14 | 12 | 8 |

**(a)** State the width of each class. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the mid-point of each class. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the mean waiting time to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(d)** Give a reason why the mean found in (c) is only approximate. **[1]**

**10.** Heights are measured and recorded to the nearest centimetre, then grouped as 150-154, 155-159 and 160-164.

**(a)** Find the boundaries of 155-159. **[2]**
**(b)** A girl's true height is 154.6 cm. State the class she is placed in and explain. **[2]**
**(c)** State the smallest and largest true heights that can be placed in 155-159, using inequalities. **[2]**

**11.** Rainfall is recorded to 1 decimal place of a millimetre. A student works on the class 5.0-9.9 and writes: "The next class starts at 10.0, so the boundaries are 4.9 and 10.0. The width is 5.1 and the mid-point is (5.0 + 10.0) ÷ 2 = 7.5."

**(a)** Identify the three errors. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the correct boundaries, width and mid-point. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The gap between 5 and 6 is 1, so the half-gap is 0.5 [1]. Boundaries **5.5 and 10.5** [1].
**(b)** 10.5 − 5.5 = **5** [1].
**(c)** (5.5 + 10.5) ÷ 2 = **8** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A width of 4 from 10 − 6 is a common slip. Working from the boundaries, 10.5 − 5.5, avoids it.

**2.** The gap is 0.1, so the half-gap is 0.05 [1]. Boundaries **4.45 and 4.95** [1]. Width 4.95 − 4.45 = **0.5** [1]. Mid-point (4.45 + 4.95) ÷ 2 = **4.7** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using 0.5 as the half-gap for decimal data gives boundaries that cut into the next class, so check that your upper boundary is lower than the next class's lower limit.

**3. (a)** The classes are written with inequalities, so the numbers printed are already the boundaries [1].
**(b)** Widths: 70 − 60 = **10** [1], 85 − 70 = **15**, 100 − 85 = **15**, 130 − 100 = **30** [1]. Mid-points: **65, 77.5, 92.5, 115** [2].
*Examiner insight:* When a question gives four classes and asks for two measures, a small table keeps each value easy to find.

**4. (a)** She has not yet had her 25th birthday, so her class is **20-24** [1].
**(b)** The class holds exact ages from 20 up to but not including 25, so **20 ≤ age < 25** [1]. Width **5** [1].
**(c)** The class covers exact ages from 30 to 40, so the mid-point is (30 + 40) ÷ 2 = **35** [1].
*Examiner insight:* For ages in completed years, the exact range ends at the next whole number, so a mid-point of 34.5 taken from the printed limits of 30 and 39 does not match the ages being described.

**5. (a)** The classes overlap, because 10 and 20 each appear in two classes [1]. The range 30 to 40 has no class, so a score in it cannot be placed [1].
**(b)** **0-9, 10-19 and so on in steps of ten, ending with 50-59** [1]. Each class has width 10, no value is in two classes, and nothing is missing [1].
*Examiner insight:* A corrected set must show no overlap, no gap and equal widths, and a one-line check of each condition makes it easy to credit.

**6. (a)** Limits **25 and 34** [1]. Width 34.5 − 24.5 = **10** [1]. Mid-point (24.5 + 34.5) ÷ 2 = **29.5** [1].
**(b)** Half the width is 2.5, so the boundaries are 47 − 2.5 = 44.5 and 47 + 2.5 = 49.5 [1]. Moving each inwards by 0.5 gives the limits [1]: **45 and 49** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Working backwards from a mid-point is safer through the boundaries first, because a slip in a jump straight to the limits is hard to trace.

**7. (a)** Tallies give 10-19: 4 (12, 15, 17, 19) and 20-29: 7 [1]; 30-39: 7 and 40-49: 4 [1]; 50-59: 2 [1]. Frequencies **4, 7, 7, 4, 2**.
**(b)** Boundaries **29.5 and 39.5** [1]. Mid-point **34.5** [1].
**(c)** 4 + 7 + 7 + 4 + 2 = 24, which equals the 24 customers [1].
*Examiner insight:* Adding up the frequencies is the standard check for a tally, and writing the sum out shows it was done.

**8.** Width 1 would need 96 classes for only 60 values, so most rows would be empty or hold one value and the shape would not show [1]. Width 50 gives only 2 classes, which hides the shape of the data [1]. Width 10 gives 10 classes, with an average of about 6 values per class, which is enough to show the shape [1]. Recommend **width 10**, such as 0-9, 10-19 and so on up to 90-99 [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation scores best when it uses the numbers in the question, here 60 values and a range of 95, and not a general remark such as "too many".

**9. (a)** Widths **5, 10, 15, 30** [2].
**(b)** Mid-points **2.5, 10, 22.5, 45** [2].
**(c)** Σfx = 6 × 2.5 + 14 × 10 + 12 × 22.5 + 8 × 45 = 15 + 140 + 270 + 360 = 785 [1]. Σf = 40 [1]. Mean = 785 ÷ 40 = 19.625 = **19.6 minutes** [1].
**(d)** The actual times are not known, and each value is taken to be at the mid-point of its class [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the products f × mid-point in a row or column, so a slip in one product can be separated from the method.

**10. (a)** The gap is 1, so the half-gap is 0.5 [1]. Boundaries **154.5 and 159.5** [1].
**(b)** 154.6 rounds to 155 [1], so she is in **155-159**, not 150-154 [1].
**(c)** **154.5 ≤ h < 159.5** [2].
*Examiner insight:* Inequality signs are better than the words "154.5 to 159.5", because the signs show which end is included.

**11. (a)** The gap is 0.1, so the half-gap is 0.05, not 0.1 [1]. The width should be 5.0, not 5.1, because the boundaries were wrong [1]. The mid-point used the limit 10.0 instead of the upper limit 9.9 or the boundary 9.95 [1].
**(b)** Boundaries **4.95 and 9.95** [1]. Width 9.95 − 4.95 = **5.0** [1]. Mid-point (4.95 + 9.95) ÷ 2 = **7.45** [1].
*Examiner insight:* When asked to identify errors, name each one and say what should have been done, since a bare corrected answer does not show that the fault was found.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the width as upper limit minus lower limit, giving 4 for 6-10.
- Using a half-gap of 0.5 for data recorded to 1 decimal place.
- Writing boundaries as whole numbers, such as 5 and 10, instead of 5.5 and 10.5.
- Adjusting a class written with inequalities, when its printed numbers are already boundaries.
- Using the printed limits for the mid-point of an age class recorded in completed years.
- Giving a range of true values without inequality signs.
- Commenting on class choices with no figures from the question.
- Not checking that the frequency column totals the size of the sample.
