AQA A-Level Biology (7402) takes students from the molecules that make up living things to whole populations
and ecosystems, with practical skills woven through every stage. It is a common choice for students considering
medicine, veterinary science, biomedical sciences, environmental science or any course that values careful
quantitative reasoning about living systems. The qualification is linear and not tiered, and it is assessed by
three written papers at the end of the course: two worth 91 marks and 35% each, and a third worth 78 marks and
30%, with every paper lasting 2 hours.

## How the course is organised

The content is divided into eight topics, split evenly between the AS stage and the A-level stage.

**AS-stage topics (1 to 4).** Topic 1, Biological molecules, covers monomers and polymers, carbohydrates, lipids,
proteins, nucleic acids, ATP, water and inorganic ions. Topic 2, Cells, moves through eukaryotic and prokaryotic
structure, viruses, methods of studying cells, cell division, transport across membranes, and cell recognition
and the immune system. Topic 3, Organisms exchange substances with their environment, deals with surface area to
volume ratio, gas exchange, digestion and absorption, and mass transport. Topic 4, Genetic information,
variation and relationships between organisms, covers DNA, genes and chromosomes, protein synthesis, mutation
and meiosis, adaptation, species and taxonomy, and biodiversity and how to investigate it.

**A-level-stage topics (5 to 8).** Topic 5, Energy transfers in and between organisms, covers photosynthesis,
respiration, energy in ecosystems and nutrient cycles. Topic 6, Organisms respond to changes in their internal
and external environments, covers stimuli and responses, nervous coordination, skeletal muscle and homeostasis.
Topic 7, Genetics, populations, evolution and ecosystems, deals with inheritance, populations, speciation and
populations in ecosystems. Topic 8, The control of gene expression, covers mutations that alter proteins, the
control of gene expression, genome projects and gene technologies.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7402/1 Paper 1 | 2 hours | 91 | 35% | Any content from topics 1-4, including practical skills |
| 7402/2 Paper 2 | 2 hours | 91 | 35% | Topics 5-8, including practical skills |
| 7402/3 Paper 3 | 2 hours | 78 | 30% | Any content from topics 1-8, including practical skills |

The papers are built from different question types:

- **Paper 1**: 76 marks of short and long answer questions and 15 marks of extended response.
- **Paper 2**: 76 marks of short and long answer questions and 15 marks of comprehension.
- **Paper 3**: 38 marks of structured questions, including practical techniques; 15 marks of critical analysis
  of given experimental data; and a 25-mark essay chosen from two titles.

The total across the three papers is 260 marks. The specification was first taught in September 2015 and first
assessed at A-level in June 2017. Because the course is linear, the AS-stage topics are examined at the end of
the second year alongside the A-level-stage topics.

## How the parts fit together

The paper structure mirrors the topic structure. Paper 1 maps onto the first four topics and Paper 2 onto the
last four, which makes it tempting to treat the course as two separate halves. Paper 3 is the reason not to:
it can draw on any of the eight topics, and its 25-mark essay rewards students who can connect ideas from
different parts of the specification under a single title.

Several threads run through the whole course. Protein structure from topic 1 returns in enzymes, membrane
transport, the immune system, muscle contraction and gene expression. DNA and protein synthesis in topic 4 is the
foundation for topic 8's work on mutations and gene technologies. Exchange and mass transport in topic 3 links
directly to respiration and photosynthesis in topic 5, and the work on species and biodiversity in topic 4 sets
up populations and evolution in topic 7.

Practical skills are the other constant. Every paper includes practical skills, and Paper 3 sets aside marks for
practical techniques and for the critical analysis of experimental data. Students who keep careful records of
their practical work, and understand why each method was chosen, will find those skills useful on every paper.

## Planning your study

A sensible order largely follows the topic numbers, because later topics depend on earlier ones:

1. **Begin with topics 1 and 2.** Molecules and cells provide the vocabulary for everything else.
2. **Continue with topics 3 and 4** to complete the AS-stage content by the end of the first year.
3. **In the second year, teach topics 5 and 6**, linking back to exchange, transport and protein structure.
4. **Finish with topics 7 and 8**, which draw heavily on the genetics from topic 4.

Throughout both years, build a habit of short synoptic links: after each new topic, note two or three places
where it connects to earlier content. That habit is the most direct preparation for the Paper 3 essay. Topic
questions can be used from the start; full past papers become most useful once both halves of the content have
been taught, with Paper 1 practice possible at the end of the first year. Paper-specific exam strategy is covered
on the exam preparation page rather than here.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does AQA A-Level Biology have?**
There are three written papers, each 2 hours long. Papers 1 and 2 are worth 91 marks and 35% each; Paper 3 is worth 78 marks and 30%.

**Which topics are on each AQA A-Level Biology paper?**
Paper 1 covers any content from topics 1-4, Paper 2 covers topics 5-8, and Paper 3 can cover any content from topics 1-8. All three include practical skills.

**Is there an essay in AQA A-Level Biology?**
Yes. Paper 3 includes a 25-mark essay, with a choice of two titles, alongside structured questions and a critical analysis of experimental data.

**Is AQA A-Level Biology tiered or modular?**
Neither. It is not tiered, and it is linear, so all three papers are taken at the end of the course.

**How many marks is the whole qualification out of?**
The three papers total 260 marks.
