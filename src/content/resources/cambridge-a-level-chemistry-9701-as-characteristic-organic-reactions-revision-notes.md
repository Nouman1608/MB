These notes condense subtopic **13.2, Characteristic organic reactions** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Worked examples are in the [13.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-characteristic-organic-reactions/). The [Introduction to Organic Chemistry revision notes](/resources/as-chemistry-organic-introduction-revision-notes/) cover Topic 13 as a whole.

## The terms the syllabus lists

(a) homologous series; (b) saturated, unsaturated; (c) homolytic, heterolytic fission; (d) free radical, initiation, propagation, termination; (e) nucleophile, electrophile, nucleophilic, electrophilic; (f) addition, substitution, elimination, hydrolysis, condensation; (g) oxidation, reduction with [O] and [H]. Mechanism types: free-radical substitution, electrophilic addition, nucleophilic substitution, nucleophilic addition.

## Compounds

- **Homologous series**: same functional group, same general formula, successive members differ by CH₂, similar chemical properties, gradual trend in physical properties.
- **Saturated**: C–C single bonds only. **Unsaturated**: at least one C=C. Unsaturated compounds decolourise bromine water.

## Bond fission

```
Homolytic:    Cl-Cl  ->  Cl.  +  Cl.            each atom keeps one electron -> two radicals
Heterolytic:  C2H5-Br -> C2H5+ + Br-            one atom keeps both electrons -> two ions
```

**Free radical**: a species with an unpaired electron.

## Radical chain stages (ethane + bromine, UV)

```
Initiation:    Br2 -> 2Br.                       radicals made
Propagation:   Br. + C2H6 -> C2H5. + HBr         1 radical in, 1 out
               C2H5. + Br2 -> C2H5Br + Br.
Termination:   Br. + Br. -> Br2                  2 radicals in, 0 out
               C2H5. + Br. -> C2H5Br
               C2H5. + C2H5. -> C4H10
```

## Attacking species

| | Nucleophile | Electrophile |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Electron-pair donor** | **Electron-pair acceptor** |
| Attacks | Electron-deficient (δ+) carbon | Electron-rich region (C=C) |
| Examples | OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O | H⁺, δ+ H in HBr, polarised Br₂ |

## Reaction types

| Type | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Addition | Two molecules → one product |
| Substitution | One atom or group replaced by another |
| Elimination | Small molecule removed; C=C forms |
| Hydrolysis | Breakdown by reaction with water |
| Condensation | Two molecules join; small molecule (often H₂O) lost |
| Oxidation | Gain of O or loss of H (use [O]) |
| Reduction | Gain of H or loss of O (use [H]) |

A reaction can carry two labels: bromoethane + water → ethanol + HBr is hydrolysis **and** nucleophilic substitution.

## [O] and [H] equations

```
CH3CH2OH + [O]   ->  CH3CHO + H2O
CH3CH2OH + 2[O]  ->  CH3COOH + H2O
CH3CH2CHO + 2[H] ->  CH3CH2CH2OH
CH3COCH3 + 2[H]  ->  CH3CH(OH)CH3
```

Always count every atom, including those in water.

## The four mechanisms

| Mechanism | Attacker | Substrate |
|---|---|---|
| Free-radical substitution | Radical | Alkane |
| Electrophilic addition | Electrophile | Alkene |
| Nucleophilic substitution | Nucleophile | Halogenoalkane |
| Nucleophilic addition | Nucleophile | Aldehyde or ketone |

**Curly arrows**: show the movement of an electron **pair**; start at a **bond or a lone pair**; never at a positive charge.

## Spotting the reaction type from an equation

- **Two reactants, one product**: addition.
- **Two reactants, two products, one group swapped**: substitution.
- **One organic reactant gives an alkene plus a small molecule**: elimination.
- **Water on the left and the molecule split in two**: hydrolysis.
- **Two molecules join and water appears on the right**: condensation.
- **[O] or [H] in the equation**: oxidation or reduction.

## Matching the attacking species

| Reagent | Attacking species | Type |
|---|---|---|
| NaOH(aq) with a halogenoalkane | OH⁻ | Nucleophile |
| KCN in ethanol with a halogenoalkane | CN⁻ | Nucleophile |
| NH₃ in ethanol with a halogenoalkane | NH₃ | Nucleophile |
| HBr with an alkene | δ+ H of HBr | Electrophile |
| Br₂ with an alkene | Polarised Br₂ | Electrophile |
| Cl₂ or Br₂ with an alkane in UV | Cl• or Br• | Free radical |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free radical | Species with an unpaired electron |
| Initiation | Step that forms radicals |
| Propagation | Step in which a radical reacts and a new radical forms |
| Termination | Step in which two radicals combine |
| Nucleophile | Electron-pair donor |
| Electrophile | Electron-pair acceptor |
| [O] | One oxygen atom from an oxidising agent |
| [H] | One hydrogen atom from a reducing agent |

## Exam traps

- "Same molecular formula" for a homologous series.
- Radicals described as ions.
- Propagation steps with radicals on one side only.
- Nucleophile defined only as "attracted to positive" without "electron-pair donor".
- Unbalanced [O] equations (missing water).
- "Substitution" given without "nucleophilic" or "free-radical".
- Curly arrows starting from atoms or charges.
- Forgetting that condensation and hydrolysis are reverse processes in esterification.

## Self-test

1. Define a homologous series.
2. What is meant by *unsaturated*?
3. Write an equation for the homolytic fission of a bromine molecule.
4. Define a nucleophile and give two examples.
5. Define an electrophile and give one example.
6. Identify the stage: CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•.
7. Identify the stage: CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆.
8. Classify: CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CH₂Br.
9. Classify: CH₃CH₂I + OH⁻ → CH₃CH₂OH + I⁻.
10. Write the equation, using [O], for oxidising propan-1-ol to propanal.
11. Write the equation, using [H], for reducing butanone to butan-2-ol.
12. Name the mechanism by which HCN reacts with ethanal.

**Answers:**

1. A family of compounds with the same functional group and general formula, in which successive members differ by CH₂, with similar chemical properties and a gradual trend in physical properties.
2. Containing at least one carbon–carbon double (multiple) bond.
3. Br₂ → 2Br•
4. An electron-pair donor; for example OH⁻ and CN⁻ (or NH₃, H₂O).
5. An electron-pair acceptor; for example H⁺ (or polarised Br₂).
6. Propagation.
7. Termination.
8. Electrophilic addition.
9. Nucleophilic substitution (it is also described as the hydrolysis of the halogenoalkane).
10. CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O
11. CH₃COCH₂CH₃ + 2[H] → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃
12. Nucleophilic addition.
