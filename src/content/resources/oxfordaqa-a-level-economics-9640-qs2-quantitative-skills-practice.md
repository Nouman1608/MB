These questions cover quantitative skill QS2, "calculate, use and understand percentages and percentage changes", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification. Every country, firm and figure is invented; "cu" means currency units. Give percentages to 2 decimal places unless told otherwise. Read the [QS2 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs2-quantitative-skills/) first, or refresh the methods with the [QS2 revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs2-quantitative-skills-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** The average price of a meal rises from 64 cu to 72 cu. Calculate the percentage change. **[1]**

**2.** The inflation rate falls from 4.5% to 3%. State the change in percentage points and calculate the percentage change in the inflation rate. **[2]**

**3.** After a 15% fall, the price of a television is 68 cu. Calculate its original price. **[2]**

**4.** A firm sells 45 cu million in a market worth 360 cu million. Calculate its market share. **[1]**

**5.** Exports rise by 8% in one year and fall by 8% the next. Calculate the overall percentage change over the two years. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** In Country A, 2.4 million people are unemployed in a labour force of 48 million. A year later, 2.5 million are unemployed in a labour force of 52 million.

**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** State the change in the unemployment rate in percentage points. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in the number unemployed. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain how the number unemployed can rise while the unemployment rate falls. **[2]**

**7.** In Country B, real GDP grows by 2.5% in a year and population grows by 1.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in real GDP per capita. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why real GDP per capita may be a better guide than real GDP to changes in average living standards. **[1]**

**8.** An index of real GDP for Country C rises from 100 to 128 over 6 years.

**(a)** Calculate the average annual growth rate. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why dividing the total growth by 6 gives a misleading average. **[1]**

**9.** A cinema raises its ticket price by 12%. The number of tickets sold falls by 20%.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the cinema's total revenue. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the price elasticity of demand over this range. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why total revenue fell. **[1]**

**10.** The table shows the components of Country D's GDP, in cu billion, in two years.

| Component | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Consumption | 720 | 744 |
| Investment | 180 | 186 |
| Government spending | 240 | 252 |
| Net exports | 60 | 66 |
| GDP | 1,200 | 1,248 |

**(a)** Calculate the growth rate of GDP. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate each component's contribution to GDP growth in percentage points. **[3]**

**(c)** Identify the component that grew fastest in percentage terms, and explain why it was not the main contributor to growth. **[2]**

**11.** Country E's budget deficit is 54 cu billion when its GDP is 1,080 cu billion. The next year the deficit is 60 cu billion and GDP is 1,250 cu billion.

**(a)** Calculate the deficit as a percentage of GDP in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** A commentator says the deficit "has grown". Using your answers, assess this statement. **[2]**

**12.** The price of a good including a 20% sales tax is 54 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the price before tax. **[2]**

**(b)** The tax rate rises to 25% and the pre-tax price is unchanged. Calculate the percentage rise in the price consumers pay. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (72 − 64)/64 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Total: 1

**2.** Change = **−1.5 percentage points** [1]. Percentage change = −1.5/4.5 × 100 = **−33.33%** [1]. Total: 2

**3.** The new price is 85% of the original, so the original = 68/0.85 [1] = **80 cu** [1]. Total: 2

**4.** 45/360 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Total: 1

**5.** 1.08 × 0.92 = 0.9936 [1], so exports fall by **0.64%** overall [1]. Total: 2

**6. (a)** Year 1: 2.4/48 × 100 = **5.00%** [1]. Year 2: 2.5/52 × 100 = **4.81%** [1].

**(b)** **−0.19 percentage points** [1].

**(c)** (2.5 − 2.4)/2.4 × 100 = **+4.17%** [1].

**(d)** The rate is the unemployed as a share of the labour force [1]. The labour force grew by 8.33%, faster than the number unemployed, so the share fell even though the number rose [1]. Total: 6

**7. (a)** 1.025/1.015 = 1.0099 [1], so GDP per capita grows by **0.99%** [1].

**(b)** It divides output by the number of people, so it shows the change in average output per person rather than in total output [1]. Total: 3

**8. (a)** (128/100)^(1/6) = 1.0420 [1], so the average annual growth rate is **4.20%** [1].

**(b)** Growth compounds: each year's growth applies to a larger base, so 28/6 = 4.67% overstates the annual rate [1]. Total: 3

**9. (a)** 1.12 × 0.80 = 0.896 [1], so revenue falls by **10.4%** [1].

**(b)** PED = −20/12 = **−1.67** [1].

**(c)** Demand is price elastic, so the percentage fall in quantity is larger than the percentage rise in price [1]. Total: 4

**10. (a)** (1,248 − 1,200)/1,200 × 100 = **4%** [1].

**(b)** Consumption: 24/1,200 × 100 = **2.0 points** [1]. Investment: 6/1,200 × 100 = **0.5 points**; government spending: 12/1,200 × 100 = **1.0 point** [1]. Net exports: 6/1,200 × 100 = **0.5 points**, and the four sum to 4.0 [1].

**(c)** Net exports grew fastest, by 6/60 = 10% [1], but they are a small part of GDP, so their rise added only 0.5 points, while consumption grew by only 3.3% but is the largest component and added 2.0 points [1]. Total: 6

**11. (a)** Year 1: 54/1,080 × 100 = **5.0%** [1]. Year 2: 60/1,250 × 100 = **4.8%** [1].

**(b)** In money terms the deficit grew, by 6 cu billion (11.1%) [1]. Relative to the size of the economy it fell by 0.2 percentage points, so the statement is true only for the level, not as a share of GDP [1]. Total: 4

**12. (a)** 54 is 120% of the pre-tax price [1], so the pre-tax price = 54/1.2 = **45 cu** [1].

**(b)** New price = 45 × 1.25 = 56.25 cu [1]. Rise = (56.25 − 54)/54 × 100 = **4.17%** [1]. Total: 4

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a change from 4.5% to 3% a fall of "1.5%" without saying "percentage points".
- Taking 15% of 68 and adding it on, instead of dividing by 0.85.
- Adding the price and quantity changes to get the revenue change.
- Dividing total growth by the number of years.
- Confusing the fastest-growing component with the largest contributor.
- Stating a percentage without interpreting it when the question says "explain" or "assess".
