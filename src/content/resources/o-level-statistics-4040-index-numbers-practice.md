Index numbers measure how a price or cost has changed relative to a base year, where the base value is 100. Cambridge O Level Statistics 4040 (Topic 8) and IGCSE Statistics 0479 (Topic 10) expect you to calculate price relatives and weighted aggregate indices, use them to find prices, and explain their limitations.

Study the method first: [index numbers study guide](/resources/o-level-statistics-4040-index-numbers/). Related: [index numbers revision notes](/resources/o-level-statistics-4040-index-numbers-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** The price of a bus ticket rose from Rs 80 in 2023 to Rs 92 in 2025. Using 2023 as
the base year, calculate the price relative for 2025. **[1]**

**2.** The price relative of a textbook in 2025, with 2022 as the base year, is 135. The
textbook costs Rs 270 in 2025. Find its price in 2022. **[2]**

**3.** A cost-of-living index for a city uses four groups of spending. The weights and
the 2025 price relatives (2020 = 100) are:

| Group | Weight | Price relative |
|---|---|---|
| Food | 45 | 118 |
| Housing | 30 | 106 |
| Transport | 15 | 124 |
| Other | 10 | 110 |

(a) Calculate the weighted aggregate index for 2025. **[3]**

(b) Interpret your answer. **[1]**

(c) Transport has the highest price relative, but changes to it affect the index
less than changes to food. Explain why. **[1]**

**4.** A bakery buys three ingredients. Its prices (Rs per kg) and the quantities it used
in the base year, 2024, are:

| Ingredient | Price 2024 | Price 2025 | Quantity used in 2024 (kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 60 | 75 | 40 |
| Butter | 250 | 270 | 8 |
| Sugar | 30 | 36 | 50 |

(a) Calculate the price relative for each ingredient, using 2024 as the base year. **[2]**

(b) Calculate a weight for each ingredient from base-year expenditure. **[2]**

(c) Calculate the weighted aggregate index for 2025. **[3]**

**5.** By 2025 the bakery in Question 4 uses much less butter and more margarine, which
is not in the index. Give two reasons why the index in 4(c) may no longer measure the
change in the bakery's costs accurately. **[2]**

**6.** The price of a monthly mobile data package was Rs 1 500 in 2022. Its price
relative for 2025, with 2022 as the base year, is 84.

(a) State what the price relative of 84 tells you about the price of the package. **[1]**

(b) Calculate the price of the package in 2025. **[1]**

**7.** The cost-of-living index in Question 3 was 114.5 in 2025 (2020 = 100). In 2024
the same index was 108.0.

(a) A household in the city spent Rs 60 000 a month in 2020 on the goods and services
in the index. Estimate the monthly cost in 2025 of buying the same goods and
services. **[1]**

(b) Calculate the percentage increase in prices from 2024 to 2025. **[2]**

(c) A student says prices rose by 6.5% from 2024 to 2025. Explain the error. **[1]**

**8.** A new survey of the city in Question 3 finds that households now spend a larger
share on transport. The index is recalculated with the same price relatives but new
weights: food 35, housing 30, transport 25, other 10.

(a) Calculate the index with the new weights. **[2]**

(b) Explain why the index has changed, and what has caused the change in the
weights. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 92 ÷ 80 × 100 = **115** [1].

**2.** Price in 2022 = 270 ÷ 135 × 100 [1] = **Rs 200** [1].

**3.** (a) 45 × 118 + 30 × 106 + 15 × 124 + 10 × 110 = 5 310 + 3 180 + 1 860 + 1 100 = 11 450 [1];
sum of weights = 100 [1]; index = 11 450 ÷ 100 = **114.5** [1].

(b) On average, weighted by spending, prices in the city rose by 14.5% between 2020
and 2025 [1].

(c) Food has a weight of 45 against 15 for transport, so a one-point change in the
food price relative moves the index three times as much [1].

**4.** (a) Flour 75 ÷ 60 × 100 = **125**; butter 270 ÷ 250 × 100 = **108**;
sugar 36 ÷ 30 × 100 = **120** [2] (1 mark for two correct).

(b) Flour 60 × 40 = **2 400**; butter 250 × 8 = **2 000**; sugar 30 × 50 = **1 500** [2]
(1 mark for two correct). Total 5 900.

(c) (2 400 × 125 + 2 000 × 108 + 1 500 × 120) ÷ 5 900 [1]
= (300 000 + 216 000 + 180 000) ÷ 5 900 = 696 000 ÷ 5 900 [1]
= **118.0** (to 1 decimal place) [1].

**5.** Any two, linked to the bakery:
- The weights are based on 2024 spending; the bakery now buys less butter, so butter's weight is too large [1].
- Margarine is now part of the bakery's costs but is not in the index at all [1].
- The index assumes the same quantities are bought each year, so it does not reflect the bakery switching to a cheaper ingredient [1].

**6.** (a) The package cost 84% of its 2022 price in 2025: its price **fell by 16%** [1].

(b) 1 500 × 84 ÷ 100 = **Rs 1 260** [1].

**7.** (a) 60 000 × 114.5 ÷ 100 = **Rs 68 700** a month [1].

(b) Increase = 114.5 − 108.0 = 6.5 index points [1];
6.5 ÷ 108.0 × 100 = **6.0%** (to 1 decimal place) [1].

(c) 6.5 is the rise in index points, measured against the 2020 base. The percentage
change must be found relative to the 2024 value of 108.0, not 100 [1].

**8.** (a) 35 × 118 + 30 × 106 + 25 × 124 + 10 × 110 = 4 130 + 3 180 + 3 100 + 1 100
= 11 510 [1]; 11 510 ÷ 100 = **115.1** [1].

(b) Ten points of weight have moved from food (price relative 118) to transport
(price relative 124, the highest), so items with a larger price rise now count for
more and the index rises [1]. The weights changed
because households changed their consumption, spending a larger share of their
budget on transport [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Averaging price relatives without weights: in 4(c) that gives
  (125 + 108 + 120) ÷ 3 = 117.7, not 118.0.
- Using the quantities (40, 8, 50) as the weights instead of base-year
  expenditure (price × quantity).
- Dividing by the number of items instead of by the sum of the weights.
- In 3(b), saying prices rose by 114.5%.
- In 7(b), giving the change in index points (6.5) as a percentage, or dividing
  by 100 instead of by the 2024 index.
- In Question 5, writing general limitations ("weights change") without saying
  which weight and why.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a price relative?**
A price relative is the current price divided by the base-year price, multiplied by 100. A price relative of 115 means the price is 15% higher than in the base year; 84 means it is 16% lower.

**How do you calculate a weighted aggregate index?**
Multiply each item's price relative by its weight, add the products, and divide by the sum of the weights. Weights are often given, or found from base-year expenditure (price times quantity).

**Why is a change of 6.5 index points not a 6.5% rise?**
Index points are measured against the base year's value of 100. To find the percentage change between two later years, divide the change in points by the earlier year's index, not by 100.

**What are the limitations of a weighted index?**
The weights reflect spending in one year and can go out of date as buying habits change, and items that become important (such as a substitute product) may not be in the index at all.
