**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 11 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): revolution, reform and foreign relations in the Middle East, circa 1945 to 2020. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-middle-east-revolution-reform/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| 1979 revolution in Iran | Shah's authoritarian modernisation, foreign influence, Khomeini and religious networks |
| Post-revolution Iran | Islamic Republic, Supreme Leader, hostility with the USA, mixed record for women |
| Iran-Iraq War | Eight-year war; chemical weapons; economic ruin; regional alignments |
| Nasser's Egypt | Authoritarian reform, Arab socialism, Pan-Arabism, Suez, defeat in 1967 |
| 2011 revolution | Sadat's and Mubarak's rule, youth unemployment, repression, Tunisian example |
| Lebanese Civil War | Sectarian politics, PLO, Syrian and Israeli intervention, Hezbollah |

## Iran: key facts

- 1951: Mosaddeq nationalises oil. August 1953: coup restores the Shah.
- 1963: White Revolution; Khomeini protests, exiled 1964.
- 1970s: oil boom, inflation, SAVAK, one-party state.
- 1978: mass protests and strikes. 16 January 1979: Shah leaves. 1 February: Khomeini returns.
- Spring 1979: referendum; Islamic Republic. December 1979: constitution.
- November 1979 to January 1981: US embassy hostages.

## Post-revolution Iran

- **Political:** Supreme Leader; Guardian Council; elected president and Majlis; Revolutionary Guards.
- **Economic:** oil dependence; nationalisations; war costs; sanctions.
- **Foreign:** "neither East nor West"; hostility to the USA; support for Hezbollah; nuclear dispute; 2015 JCPOA; US withdrawal 2018.
- **Women:** compulsory dress rules and legal limits, but more girls in school and university.
- **Minorities:** Baha'i persecuted; Kurds and Baluch under pressure; recognised minorities with limited rights.

## Iran-Iraq War

| Aspect | Points |
|---|---|
| Start | Iraq invades, September 1980; Shatt al-Arab, Khuzestan, fear of Shi'a revolution |
| Course | Iran recovers by 1982; attrition; war of the cities; tanker war |
| Technology | Iraqi chemical weapons, including Halabja in March 1988 |
| International | Gulf and Western support for Iraq; arms to Iran from various sources, including Iran-Contra |
| End | UN Resolution 598; ceasefire August 1988 |
| Impact | Huge casualties (estimates vary); debt in Iraq; regime consolidation in Iran |

## Nasser

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Rise | Free Officers 1952; leader by 1954; president 1956 |
| Politics | Parties banned; Brotherhood repressed; state-run organisations |
| Economy | Land reform 1952; nationalisations from 1961; Aswan High Dam |
| Society | Free education; women's vote 1956; radio |
| Region | Suez 1956; UAR 1958 to 1961; Yemen; Six-Day War defeat 1967 |
| Legacy | Arab pride and social change, but authoritarian system and military defeat |

## The 2011 revolution

- **Sadat:** *infitah*; peace with Israel 1979; assassinated October 1981.
- **Mubarak:** emergency law; managed elections; police abuse; cronyism.
- **Economy and demography:** young population; graduate unemployment; rising food prices.
- **External:** Tunisia; Al Jazeera and social media; US support for the regime.
- **Events:** 25 January 2011; Tahrir Square; army refuses to fire; Mubarak resigns 11 February.

## Lebanese Civil War

- 1943 National Pact; strained by Palestinian refugees and PLO after 1970.
- April 1975 war begins; Syria intervenes 1976; Israel invades 1978 and 1982.
- UNIFIL 1978; multinational force 1982 to 1984; Beirut barracks bombings 1983.
- Sabra and Shatila, September 1982.
- Hezbollah forms with Iranian help in the early 1980s.
- Taif Agreement 1989; war ends 1990.

## Arguments to practise

| Claim | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| The Shah fell because of foreign dependence | 1953 coup memory; US arms | Domestic economy and repression |
| The Iran-Iraq War strengthened the revolution | Rally round the flag; Guards | Economic damage; war weariness |
| Nasser's legacy was positive | Social reforms; Arab pride | Authoritarian rule; 1967 |
| Social media caused the 2011 revolution | Mobilisation | Deeper economic and political causes |

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was the 1967 war the end of Nasser's influence?"

- For: damage to prestige and military; his resignation attempt; reliance on Soviet help; the death in 1970.
- Against: the huge popular reaction demanding he stay; his role in the War of Attrition; his legacy for later Arab politics.
- Judgement: 1967 ended his claim to lead the Arab world but not his national authority.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Iran and Iraq as the same sort of regime.
- Forgetting the Cold War context.
- Giving no evidence on women or minorities, which are named content.
- Confusing the 1956 and 1967 wars.

## Self-test

1. Which company did Mosaddeq nationalise?
2. What was the "White Revolution"?
3. Which year did Iraq invade Iran?
4. What was the United Arab Republic?
5. What happened on 25 January 2011 in Egypt?
6. Which group formed in Lebanon with Iranian help?

**Answers:** 1. The Anglo-Iranian Oil Company. 2. The Shah's 1963 programme of land reform, votes for women and other reforms. 3. 1980. 4. The union of Egypt and Syria (1958 to 1961). 5. Mass protests began, centred on Tahrir Square. 6. Hezbollah.

## Key terms

- **SAVAK:** the Shah's secret police.
- **Velayat-e faqih:** guardianship of the Islamic jurist, the basis of the Supreme Leader's role.
- **Free Officers:** the army group that seized power in Egypt in 1952.
- **Infitah:** Sadat's economic opening.
- **Confessionalism:** a political system sharing power among religious communities, as in Lebanon.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use Iran as my case in more than one question?** Yes, but your two essays must come from different regional studies.

**How do I show perspectives?** Use schools of thought: modernisation, religion, class and contingency, even without naming scholars.
