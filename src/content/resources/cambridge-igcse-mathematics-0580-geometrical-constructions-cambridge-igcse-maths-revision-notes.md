These notes condense subtopic **4.2 Geometrical constructions** from Topic 4 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical: measure and draw lines and angles; construct a triangle given all three sides using a ruler and pair of compasses only; draw, use and interpret nets. Constructions of perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors are **not required**. For worked examples, read the [geometrical constructions study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-constructions-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [geometrical constructions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-constructions-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Equipment and rules

- Pair of compasses, protractor, ruler: needed for every paper.
- A ruler for **all** straight edges.
- Construction arcs **must be shown**: never rub them out.
- Sharp pencil; small, neat labels at vertices.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Construct | Make an accurate drawing (here with ruler and compasses) |
| Construction arcs | The compass arcs that locate a point; they are evidence of method |
| Reflex angle | Between 180° and 360° |
| Net | Flat shape that folds into a solid |
| Cross-section | Shape of the cut through a prism, parallel to its ends |
| Surface area | Total area of all the faces (the area of the net) |

## Measuring

```
Lines:   zero mark at the start of the line; read to the nearest mm
Angles:  centre on the vertex, zero line on one arm,
         read the scale that starts at 0 on that arm
Check:   acute must read < 90, obtuse > 90
Reflex:  measure the other side, then 360 - that reading
```

Drawing a reflex angle of 250°: draw 360 – 250 = **110°** and mark the outside as the 250° angle.

## Triangle from three sides

```
0. Check: each side < sum of the other two
1. Draw one side (often the longest) with a ruler
2. Compasses to the 2nd length, point on one end: arc
3. Compasses to the 3rd length, point on the other end: arc
4. Join the crossing point to both ends
5. Leave both arcs
```

Possible or not?

| Sides (cm) | Check | Triangle? |
|---|---|---|
| 5, 6, 10 | 5 + 6 = 11 > 10 | yes |
| 4, 5, 9 | 4 + 5 = 9, not more than 9 | no (flat) |
| 3, 3, 7 | 3 + 3 = 6 < 7 | no |

After constructing, the three measured angles should add to 180°: a useful check on your protractor readings.

## Quadrilaterals from two triangles

| Shape | Draw the diagonal, then arcs… |
|---|---|
| Rhombus, side s | radius s from both ends, on both sides of the diagonal |
| Kite | radius a from one end and b from the other, on both sides |
| Parallelogram | above: a from one end, b from the other; below: swap a and b |

## Nets

**Cuboid**: 6 rectangles in 3 matching pairs. **Cube**: 6 squares, 11 possible nets. **Triangular prism**: 2 triangles and 3 rectangles. **Square-based pyramid**: 1 square and 4 triangles. **Pentagonal prism**: 2 pentagons and 5 rectangles.

Interpreting a cube net:

- In a straight row of four squares, squares 1 and 3 are opposite, and 2 and 4 are opposite.
- Squares that touch in the net are never opposite.
- When folded, edges that meet must be the same length.

Calculating from a net:

```
Surface area = sum of the areas of all faces in the net
Prism volume = area of cross-section x length          (on the formula list)
Pyramid volume = 1/3 x base area x vertical height      (on the formula list)
```

Reminder: a net of a cuboid 5 cm × 2 cm × 3 cm has area 2(10 + 15 + 6) = **62 cm²**, and the cuboid has volume **30 cm³**.

For a pyramid, the triangle heights in the net are **slant** heights. The vertical height needs Pythagoras: square base 8 cm, slant height 5 cm, so vertical height = √(25 – 16) = 3 cm and volume = ⅓ × 64 × 3 = **64 cm³**.

## Exam traps

- Arcs rubbed out, or no arcs at all.
- Lines drawn without a ruler.
- Reading the inner scale instead of the outer one, or the reverse.
- Measuring the smaller angle when the reflex angle is asked for.
- Swapping the two radii, so the triangle has the right sides in the wrong places.
- Constructing a parallelogram with the same radii on both sides of the diagonal (which gives a kite).
- Using a slant height as the vertical height of a pyramid.
- A net with a face missing or with edges that do not match.

## Self-test

1. Can a triangle have sides 4 cm, 7 cm and 12 cm? Explain.
2. To draw a reflex angle of 290°, which angle do you measure with the protractor?
3. A measured angle looks obtuse and the two scales read 64° and 116°. Which is it?
4. In a triangle construction, which instruments are allowed?
5. What must you not do with construction arcs?
6. Name the shape made from two congruent isosceles triangles on a shared base, one on each side.
7. How many faces does the net of a hexagonal prism have?
8. A cube net is a row of four squares A, B, C, D with E above B and F below B. Which face is opposite A?
9. Which face is opposite E in the net in question 8?
10. Find the surface area of a cube of edge 3 cm from its net.
11. A triangular prism's net has two right-angled triangles with legs 6 cm and 8 cm, and its length is 10 cm. Find its volume.
12. A student measures the angles of a constructed triangle as 48°, 58° and 81°. What does this tell you?

### Answers

1. **No**: 4 + 7 = 11, which is less than 12
2. 360 – 290 = **70°**
3. **116°** (obtuse)
4. **Ruler and pair of compasses only**
5. **Rub them out**: they must be shown
6. **Rhombus**
7. 2 hexagons + 6 rectangles = **8 faces**
8. **C**
9. **F**
10. 6 × 9 = **54 cm²**
11. ½ × 6 × 8 × 10 = **240 cm³**
12. They add up to 187°, not 180°, so **at least one reading is wrong**: measure each angle again
