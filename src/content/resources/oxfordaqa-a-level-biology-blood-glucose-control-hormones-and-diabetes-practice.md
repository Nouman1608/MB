## Questions

**1.** Explain why it is important that the concentration of glucose in the blood is kept stable. **[4]**

**2.** Name the process in the liver that:

**(a)** converts glucose to glycogen **[1]**
**(b)** converts glycogen to glucose **[1]**
**(c)** produces glucose from glycerol and amino acids **[1]**

**3.** Describe how insulin lowers the concentration of glucose in the blood after a meal. **[5]**

**4.** A person has not eaten for 14 hours.

**(a)** Describe how glucagon increases the concentration of glucose in the blood. **[3]**
**(b)** State one way in which the action of adrenaline on a liver cell is the same as that of glucagon. **[1]**

**5.** Adrenaline acts on liver cells through a second messenger.

**(a)** Describe the sequence of events from adrenaline binding to a liver cell to the release of glucose. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest why a very small amount of adrenaline can cause a large release of glucose. **[2]**

**6.** Compare the causes and the control of Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. **[4]**

**7.** A boy with untreated Type 1 diabetes has glucose in his urine, passes a large volume of urine and is very thirsty.

**(a)** Explain why there is glucose in his urine. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why he passes a large volume of urine and is thirsty. **[3]**

**8.** Adults aged 45 to 64 were followed for several years. The table shows new cases of Type 2 diabetes in groups with different amounts of moderate physical activity.

| Activity per week | Under 1 hour | 1 to 3.9 hours | 4 hours or more |
|---|---|---|---|
| New cases per 1000 people per year | 14.2 | 9.6 | 6.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by which incidence is lower in the 4 hours or more group than in the under 1 hour group. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the incidence is in the least active group than in the most active group. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the relationship shown by the data. **[1]**
**(d)** Evaluate the claim that low physical activity causes Type 2 diabetes. **[3]**

**9.** Liver cells were kept in a buffer for 15 minutes under different conditions. The glucose released into the buffer was measured.

| Treatment | Glucose released / micromol per 100 mg of cells |
|---|---|
| No hormone | 1.5 |
| Glucagon | 13.8 |
| Adrenaline | 12.0 |
| Glucagon plus an inhibitor of protein kinase | 2.4 |
| A form of cAMP that is not broken down, no hormone | 12.9 |
| That form of cAMP plus the inhibitor of protein kinase | 2.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of glucose release with glucagon, in micromol per 100 mg per minute, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater glucose release was with glucagon than with no hormone. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what the result with glucagon plus the inhibitor shows. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain what the results with the form of cAMP, with and without the inhibitor, show. **[2]**

**10.** A patient and a control subject fasted overnight, then drank a glucose solution at time 0. Blood glucose was measured at intervals.

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control / mmol dm⁻³ | 4.9 | 9.3 | 6.8 | 5.3 | 4.8 | 4.7 |
| Patient / mmol dm⁻³ | 10.4 | 17.9 | 19.6 | 18.8 | 16.9 | 14.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall from the peak to 180 minutes for the control and for the patient. Give each answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two differences between the patient's results and the control's. **[2]**
**(c)** The patient's blood contained more insulin than normal. Suggest why this points to Type 2 rather than Type 1 diabetes. **[2]**
**(d)** State one change in lifestyle that could help to control the patient's blood glucose. **[1]**

**11.** An adult with a high body mass has just been diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes. A man with Type 1 diabetes injects his usual insulin dose but then misses a meal.

**(a)** Suggest how increased exercise could lower the adult's blood glucose. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the man's blood glucose may fall too low and how his body could respond. **[4]**
**(c)** Evaluate the statement "People with Type 2 diabetes never need insulin". **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Low glucose: less glucose for respiration, so less ATP is made [1], and brain cells are affected, for example confusion or loss of consciousness [1]. High glucose: the water potential of the blood falls [1], so water leaves cells by osmosis and they lose water [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Energy" alone is vague; name respiration and ATP, and link high glucose to water potential and osmosis.

**2. (a)** **Glycogenesis** [1].
**(b)** **Glycogenolysis** [1].
**(c)** **Gluconeogenesis** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Spell each term carefully; a near-miss such as "glycolysis" names a different pathway.

**3.** Insulin attaches to receptors on the surface of target cells [1]. Its shape is complementary to the receptor, so only target cells respond [1]. More channel proteins are included in the surface membrane [1]. Glucose uptake into the cell therefore increases [1]. Insulin activates enzymes that convert glucose to glycogen [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that insulin "activates enzymes", not that it converts glucose itself, and link channel proteins to the membrane.

**4. (a)** Glucagon attaches to receptors on the surface of liver cells [1]. It activates enzymes that convert glycogen to glucose [1]. It also switches on enzymes that make glucose from glycerol and amino acids [1].
**(b)** Either it attaches to receptors on the surface of the cell, or it activates enzymes that convert glycogen to glucose [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "state one way" question needs one clear statement; adding a second, contradictory statement weakens the answer.

**5. (a)** Adrenaline binds to its receptor on the cell surface membrane [1]. This activates adenyl cyclase [1], which converts ATP to cAMP [1]. cAMP activates protein kinase, which activates enzymes that convert glycogen to glucose [1].
**(b)** One adrenaline molecule leads to many cAMP molecules being made [1]. Each activated enzyme catalyses many reactions, so the signal is amplified at each stage [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* List the molecules in order and name all three: adenyl cyclase, cAMP and protein kinase. For "suggest why", an amplification point must say that numbers increase at a stage.

**6.** Type 1: the immune system destroys the insulin-secreting cells, so little or no insulin is made [1]. Type 2: target cells respond poorly to insulin (fewer or less responsive receptors) [1]. Type 1 is controlled by injecting insulin with attention to diet [1]. Type 2 is controlled first by regulating diet, losing weight and exercising [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "compare" question is rewarded when each point sets the two types side by side. Two unconnected descriptions leave the comparing to the reader.

**7. (a)** The glucose concentration of the filtrate is too high [1]. Carrier proteins in the kidney tubule are saturated, so not all of the glucose is reabsorbed [1].
**(b)** Glucose left in the filtrate lowers its water potential [1], so the kidney recovers less water and more urine is produced [1]. The water loss lowers the water content of the blood, so he is thirsty [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying only "he has diabetes" repeats the question. Name the water potential change and the reduced reabsorption.

**8. (a)** (14.2 − 6.1) ÷ 14.2 × 100 [1] = **57%** [1].
**(b)** 14.2 ÷ 6.1 = **2.3** times [1].
**(c)** As activity increases, incidence falls (negative correlation) [1].
**(d)** A correlation does not prove cause [1]. Other variables, such as body mass, diet or age, may differ between the groups [1]. A plausible mechanism exists, as active muscle uses more glucose, but the data do not show it, and the number of people followed is not given [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a judgement based on reasons. "Not proven, because other variables may differ" scores better than a list of unconnected criticisms. Show your working for percentages.

**9. (a)** 13.8 ÷ 15 = **0.92 micromol per 100 mg per minute** [1].
**(b)** 13.8 ÷ 1.5 = **9.2 times** [1].
**(c)** Release fell to 2.4, close to the control value [1], so glucagon needs protein kinase to cause the release [1].
**(d)** The cAMP form alone gave a release similar to glucagon, so no hormone is needed once cAMP is present [1]. The inhibitor removed that effect, so cAMP acts through protein kinase [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figures when you interpret a table, and compare each result with the control.

**10. (a)** Control: (9.3 − 4.7) ÷ 9.3 × 100 = **49%** [1]. Patient: (19.6 − 14.2) ÷ 19.6 × 100 = **28%** [1].
**(b)** The patient started higher (10.4 compared with 4.9) [1]. The patient's peak was higher and later (19.6 at 60 minutes compared with 9.3 at 30 minutes), and glucose fell far less by 180 minutes [1].
**(c)** In Type 1 diabetes little or no insulin is made, so a high insulin level suggests the insulin-making cells work [1]. Glucose stays high because target cells respond poorly to insulin, as in Type 2 [1].
**(d)** Lose weight, increase exercise or reduce refined sugar in the diet [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* For "describe two differences", pair values from both rows. A difference quoted with only one subject's data does not show a comparison.

**11. (a)** Active muscle cells use more glucose in respiration [1]. Glucose is taken up from the blood, so its concentration falls [1]. Exercise may help weight loss, which could improve the response of cells to insulin [1].
**(b)** The injected insulin still acts: glucose uptake into cells rises and glucose is converted to glycogen [1], but no new glucose is absorbed from a meal, so blood glucose falls [1]. Low glucose means less respiration and ATP, affecting brain cells [1]. Glucagon and adrenaline could raise glucose by activating enzymes that convert glycogen to glucose [1].
**(c)** Many can be controlled by diet, weight loss and exercise [1], but insulin may be needed if these fail, because target cells respond poorly or insulin production falls [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A statement containing "never" invites a counter-example. Name a case where the claim fails and give the reason it fails.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying insulin or glucagon "converts" substances instead of activating enzymes.
- Omitting water potential when explaining why high glucose or the glucose in urine matters.
- Confusing glycogen (the store) with glucagon (the hormone).
- Missing one or more of adenyl cyclase, cAMP and protein kinase, or putting them in the wrong order.
- Giving the same cause for both types of diabetes.
- Concluding that a correlation in risk-factor data proves cause.
- Giving a percentage change calculated from the wrong starting value.
- Quoting no figures when asked to interpret a table.
