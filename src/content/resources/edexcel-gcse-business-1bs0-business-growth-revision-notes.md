These notes condense content item 2.1.1, Business growth, in Topic 2.1 (Growing the business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [business growth study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-growth/) compares three ways of funding a new branch; test yourself with the [business growth practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-growth-practice/). The wider [business growth and changing aims revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-business-growth-and-changing-aims-revision-notes/) also cover 2.1.2.

## What the specification lists

- **Methods of growth and their impact.** Internal (organic): new products (innovation, research and development); new markets (changing the marketing mix, taking advantage of technology and/or expanding overseas). External (inorganic): merger, takeover.
- **Ownership for growing businesses:** public limited company (plc).
- **Sources of finance for growing and established businesses.** Internal: retained profit, selling assets. External: loan capital, share capital, including stock market flotation (plcs).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organic (internal) growth | Growth using the business's own resources, by selling more |
| Innovation | Turning a new idea into a product or process customers will buy |
| Research and development (R&D) | Investigation and testing to create or improve products |
| External (inorganic) growth | Growth by joining with or buying another business |
| Merger | Two businesses agree to join together as one |
| Takeover | One business buys enough of another to control it |
| Public limited company (plc) | A company with limited liability whose shares can be sold to the public on the stock market |
| Stock market flotation | Offering a company's shares for sale to the public on the stock market for the first time |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business rather than paid to owners |
| Loan capital | Money borrowed and repaid with interest |
| Share capital | Money raised by selling shares in the business |

## Organic v external growth

| | Organic | External |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Risk | Lower | Higher |
| Cost | Spread over time | Large sum at once |
| Control | Owners keep it | May be shared (merger) |
| Gains | Builds on existing strengths | New products, skills, customers, locations |
| Problems | Limited by finance and demand | Culture clash, job losses, harder to manage |

## Routes to organic growth

- New products: innovation, R&D (costly, uncertain, but can create an advantage).
- New markets: change the mix; use technology (sell online); expand overseas.

## plc: impact

**For:** large sums raised; higher profile; limited liability.

**Against:** loss of control; takeover risk; public scrutiny; cost of flotation.

## Finance for growth

| Source | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|
| Retained profit | No interest; no loss of control | Only if profitable; slow; owners receive less |
| Selling assets | Quick; no interest | Asset lost; may sell cheaply |
| Loan capital | Owners keep control | Interest; repayments regardless of sales |
| Share capital | No repayment | Ownership and profit shared |
| Flotation | Very large sums | Cost; loss of control; takeover risk |

## Formulae

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Interest on loans (%) | (total repayment − borrowed amount) ÷ borrowed amount × 100 |
| Ownership after new shares (%) | founders' shares ÷ total shares × 100 |
| Percentage growth | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |

## Exam traps

- Organic growth is not "natural" or slow by definition; it is growth from within.
- A merger is agreed; a takeover is one firm buying control.
- A plc is a growth option, not a start-up option.
- Share capital is not repaid, but it dilutes control.
- Retained profit is not a pile of cash; it may already be invested.
- Always weigh control, cost and risk when choosing finance.

## Sentence starter

The writer's suggestion: "Growing by [method] would allow the business to..., but it would need finance from..., which means..."

## Self-test

1. Define organic growth.
2. Give two ways a business can grow organically by entering new markets.
3. What is the difference between a merger and a takeover?
4. Give one benefit and one drawback of external growth.
5. Define research and development.
6. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of becoming a plc.
7. Name the two internal sources of finance in 2.1.1.
8. A business borrows £60,000 and repays £69,000. Calculate the interest as a percentage.
9. Founders own 40,000 shares. 10,000 new shares are sold. Calculate the founders' percentage ownership.
10. Why might a business choose a loan rather than share capital?
11. Why is a plc more at risk of a takeover than a private limited company?
12. A firm's sales rise from £2.4 million to £2.76 million. Calculate the percentage growth.

### Answers

1. Growth using the business's own resources, by increasing its sales.
2. Any two: changing the marketing mix; using technology such as selling online; expanding overseas.
3. A merger is an agreed joining of two businesses into one; a takeover is one business buying control of another.
4. Benefit, any one: rapid growth; gains products, skills or customers; removes a competitor. Drawback, any one: expensive; culture clash; job losses; harder to manage.
5. Investigation and testing carried out to create new products or improve existing ones.
6. Advantage: large sums from selling shares to the public. Disadvantage, any one: loss of control; takeover risk; public scrutiny; cost.
7. Retained profit and selling assets.
8. Interest = £9,000; £9,000 ÷ £60,000 × 100 = 15%.
9. 40,000 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = 80%.
10. To keep control and all future profit with the existing owners.
11. Its shares can be bought by anyone on the stock market, so another business can buy enough to gain control.
12. Increase £0.36 million; 0.36 ÷ 2.4 × 100 = 15%.
