## Section A

**1.** State the minimum number of practical activities that a learner must complete for the Practical Endorsement, and state how the result is reported. **[2]**

**2.** The Common Practical Assessment Criteria (CPAC) have five competencies. Name the competency that each of these learners is showing.

**(a)** Aisha follows the method sheet exactly and notes each stage as she completes it. **[1]**

**(b)** Ben identifies the hazards of a practical, assesses the risks and wears eye protection. **[1]**

**(c)** Carla uses a spreadsheet to process her data and lists the books she used. **[1]**

**3.** A learner writes this in a practical record: "Added some catalase to peroxide. Lots of bubbles. Worked well." Give **three** ways in which the record should be improved. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why measurements should be recorded at the time they are made. **[2]**

**5.** Complete a risk assessment for the use of a scalpel in a dissection by stating the hazard, the risk and one control measure. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A learner types three repeats of the volume of oxygen collected in 2 minutes, 14.0, 14.5 and 15.0 cm³, into cells A2, B2 and C2 of a spreadsheet.

**(a)** Write a formula that calculates the mean in cell D2. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a formula that calculates the mean rate in cell F2 and give the value and unit. **[2]**

**7.** In a group practical, five learners share one set of results. Explain what each learner must still show for the Practical Endorsement. **[2]**

**8.** A learner finds two websites about enzymes. One is by a university department and lists the sources of its data. The other is an anonymous blog with no references. Give **two** criteria for judging which is the more reliable source. **[2]**

**9.** A learner uses information from a book written in 2023 by an author named Lopez. Write a citation in the text and a reference list entry for the book. Invent a title and publisher. **[2]**

**10.** State which Practical Activity Group (PAG) each activity belongs to.

**(a)** Using a light microscope to study mitosis in a root tip. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculating species diversity from a field survey. **[1]**

**(c)** Investigating the effect of antibiotics on bacterial growth. **[1]**

**(d)** Separating amino acids using paper chromatography. **[1]**

**11.** A learner's trial of a catalase reaction shows that the oxygen is released so fast that the syringe fills in under 5 seconds. Describe what the learner should do and name the skill from 1.2.1 that this shows. **[3]**

**12.** Explain how the assessment of practical skills in the Practical Endorsement differs from the assessment of practical skills in the written papers. **[3]**

## Answers

**How these answers work.** Marking points are shown in brackets. The mark total for each question is the sum of the points. Alternative wording that carries the same meaning is credited.

**1.** A minimum of 12 practical activities [1]. The result is reported separately from the A Level grade, alongside it on the certificate, and learners who meet the standard receive a Pass [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Follows written procedures [1]. **(b)** Safely uses a range of practical equipment and materials [1]. **(c)** Researches, references and reports [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Any three [1 each]: give the quantities, concentrations and temperature used; record measurements with units, such as the volume of gas collected and the time; describe the observation precisely (for example the rate or size of bubbles); give the date, title and aim; record any problem or change to the method; add a conclusion or evaluation. **[3]**

**4.** The record is accurate and complete, because details and problems are not forgotten [1]. It provides evidence of the learner's own work at the time it was done, which the teacher can use for assessment [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Hazard: the scalpel is sharp [1]. Risk: a cut to the hand or fingers [1]. Control, any one [1]: cut on a dissection board or tile; cut away from the body; keep fingers behind the blade; carry the scalpel with the cover on. **[3]**

**6. (a)** =AVERAGE(A2:C2) [1].

**(b)** =D2/2 [1]. Value 14.5 ÷ 2 = **7.25 cm³ min⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Each learner must show independent evidence of their own competence [1], including a personal record of the work and independent application of investigative approaches and methods [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Any two [1 each]: the author is named and has expertise; the source gives references or evidence for its data; it is a primary or well-reviewed source; it is recent or up to date; other sources agree with it. **[2]**

**9.** In text: (Lopez, 2023) [1]. Reference entry with author, year, title and publisher, for example: Lopez, A. (2023) *Cells and enzymes explained*. Imaginary Science Press, London. [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** PAG1 (microscopy) [1]. **(b)** PAG3 (sampling techniques) [1]. **(c)** PAG7 (microbiological techniques) [1]. **(d)** PAG6 (chromatography or electrophoresis) [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Change the method so that the reaction is slower [1], for example use a lower concentration of hydrogen peroxide, less enzyme, or a lower temperature, and repeat the trial [1]. This shows independent thinking, applying investigative approaches and methods to solve a practical problem (1.2.1(a)) [1]. **[3]**

**12.** In the Practical Endorsement, the teacher assesses the learner's competence in at least 12 practical activities against the CPAC, and the result is reported separately [1]. In the written papers, practical skills are assessed by questions on planning, implementing, analysis and evaluation [1], and these may draw on, or range beyond, the practical activities carried out [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: stating a number of practicals that is not a minimum, or saying the result is part of the grade.
- Question 2: describing the skill without naming the competency.
- Question 3: writing only "add more detail".
- Question 5: giving "be careful" instead of a control measure.
- Question 6: writing a formula that does not start with an equals sign or uses the wrong cells.
- Question 9: citing a source in the text without giving a reference list entry.
- Question 10: confusing PAG6 and PAG9.
- Question 11: changing two variables at once.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to remember every PAG number?**
Know the main ones: PAG1 microscopy, PAG2 dissection, PAG3 sampling, PAG4 enzyme rates, PAG5 colorimeter or potometer, PAG6 chromatography or electrophoresis, PAG7 microbiology, PAG8 transport in and out of cells, PAG9 qualitative testing, PAG10 data logger or modelling, PAG11 plant or animal responses and PAG12 research skills.

**Is the Practical Endorsement marked out of a number?**
No. It is a Pass or not-passed judgement made by the teacher against the CPAC.
