Topic 3 is AS Level content and is assumed knowledge for A Level Papers 3 and 4. All countries, households and figures are invented.

## Questions

**1.** Explain the difference between income as a flow and wealth as a stock, using one example of each. **[3]**

**2.** In Country P the income Gini coefficient is 0.41 before taxes and benefits and 0.33 after them. The wealth Gini coefficient is 0.74.

**(a)** Explain what the change from 0.41 to 0.33 shows. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the wealth Gini coefficient is higher than the income Gini coefficient. **[3]**

**3.** Country R taxes income at 0% up to 12,000, 25% from 12,000 to 40,000 and 45% above 40,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid on an income of 30,000. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average rate of tax on an income of 80,000. **[2]**
**(c)** State the marginal rate faced by someone earning 80,000. **[1]**

**4.** Explain two limitations of using the Gini coefficient to compare inequality between countries. **[4]**

**5.** In a low-skill labour market, demand is L = 900 − 25w and supply is L = −100 + 25w, where w is the hourly wage and L is thousand hours a week.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium wage and employment. **[3]**
**(b)** A minimum wage of 24 is introduced. Calculate the unemployment created. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the elasticity of labour demand between the two wages. **[2]**
**(d)** State what happens to the total wage bill. **[1]**

**6.** A government pays a universal benefit of 40 a week to 500,000 households. It considers a means-tested benefit of 80 a week to the 200,000 poorest households.

**(a)** Calculate the weekly saving. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of the means-tested benefit. **[4]**

**7.** An estate of 640,000 is taxed at 35% above a threshold of 300,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax due and the effective rate as a percentage of the estate. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons why an inheritance tax may fail to reduce wealth inequality. **[4]**

**8.** Two households have incomes of 12,000 and 84,000. The government provides each with health and education worth 6,000.

**(a)** Calculate the value of this provision as a percentage of each income. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the richer household's income to the poorer household's before and after the provision. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage of state provision as a way of reducing inequality. **[2]**

**9.** A household invests 100,000 at 5% a year, with interest reinvested.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the investment after 15 years and the gain. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how this helps to explain why wealth is unequally distributed. **[3]**

**10.** In Country S, occupation X has demand L = 500 − 10w and supply L = −100 + 20w. Occupation Y has demand L = 420 − 20w and supply L = −60 + 40w.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium wage in each occupation. **[4]**
**(b)** Demand for X rises to L = 560 − 10w. Calculate the new wage and employment. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why wages differ between occupations. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate whether progressive income taxes are the most effective policy for reducing income inequality. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Income is a flow: money coming in over a period such as a month [1]. Wealth is a stock: what is owned, net of what is owed, on a given date [1]. Example: monthly wages (income) and a house or savings account balance (wealth) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* bring in the time dimension (over a period versus on a date); an example alone does not show the distinction.

**2. (a)** Taxes and benefits reduced the Gini coefficient [1], so inequality in post-tax income is lower than in market income [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Wealth is accumulated over time from saved income [1]. Higher earners save more [1]. It is passed on by inheritance and grows with asset price rises, while poorer households may hold debt [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* read the direction correctly: a lower Gini means less inequality, and tie the explanation to the figures given.

**3. (a)** Taxable amount in the 25% band = 30,000 − 12,000 = 18,000 [1]. Tax = 18,000 × 0.25 = **4,500** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Tax = 28,000 × 0.25 + 40,000 × 0.45 = 7,000 + 18,000 = 25,000 [1]. Average rate = 25,000 ÷ 80,000 = **31.25%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **45%** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* tax each band separately; applying 45% to the whole income is a common slip that gives the wrong tax and the wrong average rate.

**4.** Any two, each developed. One number hides the shape of the distribution [1], so two countries with different distributions can have the same value [1]. It does not show absolute living standards [1], because a low value can mean everyone is equally poor [1]. Another valid limitation is differences in the measure used (income or wealth, before or after tax, household or individual), which reduce comparability. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give each limitation a consequence for the comparison, rather than only naming it.

**5. (a)** 900 − 25w = −100 + 25w, so 1,000 = 50w [1]. w = **20** [1]. L = 900 − 500 = **400** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Demand at 24 = 900 − 600 = 300 [1]. Supply = −100 + 600 = 500, so unemployment = **200** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** %ΔL = −100 ÷ 400 = −25% and %Δw = 4 ÷ 20 = +20% [1]. Elasticity = **−1.25** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The bill falls from 20 × 400 = 8,000 to 24 × 300 = 7,200 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* unemployment is supply minus demand at the new wage, not the fall in employment.

**6. (a)** Universal cost = 500,000 × 40 = 20m [1]. Means-tested cost = 200,000 × 80 = 16m, so the saving is **4m a week** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Advantage: money is concentrated on the poorest, who get twice as much [1], so poverty falls more per unit of spending [1]. Disadvantage: means testing costs money to administer, and some who qualify may not claim [1]. Withdrawal as earnings rise may reduce the incentive to work [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* tie each point to the figures given or to how the benefit is designed; a bare "it is fairer" gains little.

**7. (a)** Tax = (640,000 − 300,000) × 0.35 = 340,000 × 0.35 = **119,000** [1]. Effective rate = 119,000 ÷ 640,000 [1] = **18.6%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Avoidance through gifts, trusts or moving assets abroad [1] means less wealth is taxed [1]. Raises little revenue relative to total wealth, so little to spend on redistribution [1]. Unequal wealth in the stock is untouched during life, as the tax applies only at transfer [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* explain how the weakness limits the effect on the distribution, not just that it exists.

**8. (a)** 6,000 ÷ 12,000 = **50%** [1]. 6,000 ÷ 84,000 = **7.1%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Before: 84,000 ÷ 12,000 = **7.0** [1]. After: 90,000 ÷ 18,000 = **5.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Opportunity cost: the funding must come from taxes or other spending [1]. Better-off families may use the services as much, or get higher quality, so the gain to the poor is reduced [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* show the working for both ratios; the comparison is the evidence for the fall.

**9. (a)** 100,000 × 1.05¹⁵ [1] = **207,893**, a gain of **107,893** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Returns on assets are a flow of income needing no labour [1]. Interest reinvested compounds [1], so those who start with more assets gain more in absolute terms and the gap widens [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the number is evidence; the marks come from explaining why it widens the gap.

**10. (a)** X: 500 − 10w = −100 + 20w, so 600 = 30w and **w = 20** [2]. Y: 420 − 20w = −60 + 40w, so 480 = 60w and **w = 8** [2]. **[4]**
**(b)** 560 − 10w = −100 + 20w, so 660 = 30w and **w = 22** [1]. L = 560 − 220 = **340** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** X pays more because its supply is limited by training or skills [1], and because demand for it is strong [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* name the demand and supply forces in your own words and refer to the numbers.

**11.** Award credit for a balanced, developed argument. A strong answer includes:

- Analysis: a progressive tax raises the average rate with income, taking a larger share from high earners [1]. Revenue can fund transfers and state provision [1]. For example, a post-tax ratio of 8.5 against a pre-tax ratio of 12.5 shows a narrower gap [1]. It operates automatically as incomes change [1].
- Comparison: the minimum wage acts before tax but does not help the unemployed [1]. Transfers target poverty but cost money [1]. State provision builds human capital over time [1]. Inheritance and capital taxes act on the stock of wealth, which income tax does not [1].
- Evaluation: high marginal rates may reduce work or saving, and some will avoid tax or emigrate [1]. The effect depends on rate, bands, elasticity of labour supply and how revenue is used [1]. Short-run and long-run effects differ [1].
- Judgement: a reasoned conclusion, for example that progressive taxes are central because they fund other policies, but are most effective in combination [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* a list of policies is descriptive. Compare them against the question's criterion, effectiveness, and finish with a supported judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating income as a stock, or wealth as a flow.
- Interpreting a rising Gini coefficient as a move towards equality.
- Taxing the whole income at the top rate.
- Giving the marginal rate where the average rate was asked for.
- Giving unemployment as the fall in employment instead of excess supply of labour.
- Saying a minimum wage always causes job losses, without elasticity.
- Listing policies without comparing them or reaching a judgement.
- Ignoring that transfers and state provision are funded by taxes.
