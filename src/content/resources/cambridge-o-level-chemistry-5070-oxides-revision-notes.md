Condensed recall for subtopic 7.2 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070. For explanations and a worked classification, use the [oxides study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-oxides/); to practise, try the [oxides practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-oxides-practice/).

## The two outcomes

1. **Describe** amphoteric oxides as oxides that react with acids **and** bases to produce a salt and water.
2. **Classify** oxides as acidic (including SO₂, CO₂), basic (including CuO, CaO) or amphoteric (limited to Al₂O₃ and ZnO), related to metallic and non-metallic character.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Oxide | Compound of an element with oxygen |
| Acidic oxide | Reacts with bases to form a salt and water; usually a non-metal oxide |
| Basic oxide | Reacts with acids to form a salt and water; a metal oxide |
| Amphoteric oxide | Reacts with both acids and bases to form a salt and water |
| Alkali | A soluble base; e.g. Ca(OH)₂ formed when CaO reacts with water |
| Metallic character | Tendency of an element to behave as a metal; decreases across a period |

## Summary table

| Type | Element | Reacts with acid? | Reacts with alkali? | Named examples | In water |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acidic | Non-metal | No | Yes | SO₂, CO₂ | Acidic solution if soluble |
| Basic | Metal | Yes | No | CuO, CaO | Alkaline solution if soluble (CaO); CuO insoluble |
| Amphoteric | Al, Zn | Yes | Yes | Al₂O₃, ZnO | Insoluble |

## Equations to know

```
Basic:       CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O
             CaO + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O
             CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
Acidic:      CO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O
             SO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + H₂O
             CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O     (limewater turns milky)
Amphoteric:  ZnO + 2HNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + H₂O
             ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O    (sodium zincate)
             Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O
             Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O  (sodium aluminate)
Acidic + basic: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃          (slag)
```

## Naming the salt formed

| Oxide + reagent | Salt formed |
|---|---|
| Metal oxide + hydrochloric acid | Metal chloride |
| Metal oxide + sulfuric acid | Metal sulfate |
| Metal oxide + nitric acid | Metal nitrate |
| CO₂ + sodium hydroxide | Sodium carbonate |
| SO₂ + sodium hydroxide | Sodium sulfite |
| ZnO + sodium hydroxide | Sodium zincate |
| Al₂O₃ + sodium hydroxide | Sodium aluminate |

Water is the second product every time.

## Predicting from the Periodic Table

- Group I and Group II metals: basic oxides; their oxides dissolve in water to give alkaline solutions (e.g. Na₂O, CaO).
- Transition metals: basic oxides, usually insoluble (e.g. CuO).
- Non-metals in Groups IV to VI: generally acidic oxides (e.g. CO₂, SiO₂, SO₂).
- Aluminium (Group III) and zinc: amphoteric.

## Period 3 pattern

Na₂O, MgO (basic) → Al₂O₃ (amphoteric) → SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂ (acidic). The change follows the change from metallic to non-metallic character across the period.

## Two-test method for an unknown oxide

1. Add to dilute acid: reacts or dissolves?
2. Add to dilute sodium hydroxide: reacts or dissolves?

Acid only → basic. Alkali only → acidic. Both → amphoteric.

If it dissolves in water, universal indicator gives a quick extra clue: pH above 7 suggests basic; pH below 7 suggests acidic.

## Applications

- **Acid rain:** SO₂ (acidic oxide) dissolves in rainwater, lowering its pH.
- **Flue gas desulfurisation:** CaO (basic) removes SO₂ (acidic) from waste gases.
- **Blast furnace slag:** CaO (basic) removes SiO₂ (acidic) impurities as CaSiO₃.
- **Limewater test:** CO₂ (acidic) reacts with Ca(OH)₂ (alkali) to form insoluble CaCO₃.

## Exam traps

- Not every metal oxide is basic: Al₂O₃ and ZnO are amphoteric.
- Amphoteric does not mean neutral or unreactive.
- Every reaction of an oxide with an acid or base gives **salt + water**; don't forget the water.
- CuO is a base, not an alkali: it is insoluble in water.
- Classify insoluble oxides with acid and alkali tests, not with water.
- Aluminium oxide is Al₂O₃: reacting with hydrochloric acid needs **6**HCl.
- Only Al₂O₃ and ZnO are required as amphoteric examples; don't add others.
- Acidic oxides react with **bases**, not with acids; basic oxides react with **acids**, not with alkalis.
- Calcium oxide is basic and also forms an alkali in water; copper(II) oxide is basic but forms no alkali. Both are bases.
- When classifying from test results, use both tests: "dissolves in acid" alone cannot separate basic from amphoteric.

## Self-test

1. Define an amphoteric oxide.
2. Name the two amphoteric oxides required by the syllabus.
3. Classify CO₂, CaO, ZnO and SO₂.
4. Why is copper(II) oxide described as a base but not an alkali?
5. Write the equation for calcium oxide reacting with water.
6. Write the equation for carbon dioxide reacting with sodium hydroxide.
7. Write the equation for aluminium oxide reacting with hydrochloric acid.
8. An oxide dissolves in dilute nitric acid but not in sodium hydroxide. Classify it.
9. An oxide dissolves in both dilute acid and dilute alkali. Classify it.
10. Predict the type of oxide formed by phosphorus, and explain your prediction.
11. Explain how calcium oxide removes sulfur dioxide from power-station waste gases.
12. Name the salt formed when zinc oxide reacts with sodium hydroxide.

**Answers**

1. An oxide that reacts with both acids and bases to produce a salt and water.
2. Aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, and zinc oxide, ZnO.
3. CO₂ acidic; CaO basic; ZnO amphoteric; SO₂ acidic.
4. It neutralises acids, but it is insoluble in water.
5. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂.
6. CO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O.
7. Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O.
8. Basic.
9. Amphoteric.
10. Acidic: phosphorus is a non-metal, and non-metals form acidic oxides.
11. Calcium oxide is a basic oxide; it reacts with the acidic oxide sulfur dioxide, forming a salt, so the SO₂ is not released.
12. Sodium zincate.
