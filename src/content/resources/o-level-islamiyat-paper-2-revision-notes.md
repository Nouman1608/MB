Condensed recall notes for **Paper 2** of Cambridge O Level Islamiyat
(2058), 2024–2025 series — identical content structure to sibling
Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat (0493). Paper 2 is a 1.5-hour exam of five
questions, of which candidates must answer Question 1, Question 2 and
two others, covering the Hadiths of the Prophet, the Rightly Guided
Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam. Use alongside
the full study guide on Paper 2 for explanation and context — these
notes are for fast recall in the final stretch of revision.

## 1. Major teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet

- Two groups of set passages: those on **individual conduct** and
  those on **life in the community**.
- Study what each passage teaches Muslims to **believe** and how it
  teaches them to **act**.
- Passages are studied in both the **original Arabic** and the
  **English translation** — both appear in the exam.
- The exact set passages are listed in the syllabus's Appendix 2 and
  reviewed periodically, so always revise from the current series'
  list.
- Exam question: four passages set, candidates choose any **two**.
  [8 marks]

## 2. The history and importance of the Hadiths

- The **history of compilation** and the **earliest collections**.
- The **main musnad and musannaf collections** and their compilers.
- The **isnad** (chain of transmitters) and **matn** (text) methods
  used to test a Hadith's reliability.
- The **six major Sunni collections** and the **four major Shi'a
  collections**.
- The **major themes** running through the Hadiths, in both the set
  passages and similar material.
- Their use in **legal thinking**, alongside the Qur'an, consensus
  (**ijma'**) and analogy (**qiyas**).
- Their overall **significance in Muslim thought and action**.
- Exam question: usually multi-part, essay-length answer. [14 marks]

## 3. The period of rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and their importance as leaders

- The **main events** of each of the four Caliphs' rule.
- Their **policies** for maintaining and expanding the Islamic state.
- Their individual **approaches to leadership**.
- Their **main achievements** and **main difficulties**.
- Their **significance as models of leadership**, including for
  government today.
- Their importance as examples for how Muslim communities relate to
  other states.
- Boundary with Paper 1: the Caliphs' roles **during the Prophet's
  lifetime** are examined under Paper 1 — this topic covers only their
  **period of rule after** his death.
- Exam question: one or two questions, multi-part, essay-length
  answers. [14 marks]

## 4. The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam

**The Six Articles of Faith:**

- **God** — what Muslims believe about Him
- **Angels** — their nature and duties
- **Books** — their contents and purpose
- **Prophets** — their character and function
- **God's predestination and decree** — meaning and significance
- **Resurrection and the Last Day** — main events and significance

**Jihad** — its three meanings: physical, mental and spiritual.

**The Five Pillars of Islam:**

- **Shahada** (declaration of faith) — significance of its content
- **Salat** (prayer) — preparation, performance, congregational Friday
  and festival prayers, prayer times, place, private and delayed
  prayer
- **Zakat** (alms-giving) — how performed, significance in the
  community
- **Sawm** (fasting) — observance, significance, who is exempted
- **Hajj** (pilgrimage) — main observances and significance

- Candidates need **significance**, not just description, for every
  item.
- Exam question: one or two questions, multi-part, essay-length
  answers. [14 marks]

## Quick recall checklist

- Can I name the two categories of Hadith passages and explain the
  individual-conduct vs community-life distinction?
- Do I know the difference between isnad and matn?
- Can I list the four Rightly Guided Caliphs in order, each with one
  achievement and one difficulty?
- Can I state all Six Articles of Faith and all Five Pillars of Islam
  without prompting?
- Do I understand jihad's three meanings rather than just the most
  commonly assumed one?

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing content covered in Paper 1 versus Paper 2** for the
  Rightly Guided Caliphs — remember the "during the Prophet's lifetime"
  boundary.
- **Describing without explaining significance** on the Articles of
  Faith and Pillars, which caps marks even with accurate factual
  recall.
- **Treating isnad and matn as interchangeable terms** rather than the
  two distinct methods (chain of transmission versus wording) they
  represent.

## Cross-checking with Paper 1

Paper 1 covers the Qur'an and the life of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh),
including the Emigrants and Helpers and the four Rightly Guided
Caliphs' role *during* his lifetime; Paper 2's topic 3 picks up their
story only from his death onward. Candidates who blur this boundary
sometimes answer a Paper 2 Caliphs question with material that
actually belongs to Paper 1's account of the first Islamic community —
keep a clear personal note of exactly where "during the Prophet's
lifetime" ends and "period of rule" begins, since this distinction is
stated explicitly in the syllabus itself, not left to interpretation.

## How to approach it

Because Question 1 and Question 2 are compulsory every year, prioritise
rock-solid recall of the set Hadith passages (topic 1) and the history
of the Hadiths (topic 2) before spending equal time on your two
optional questions from topics 3 and 4. For the Caliphs, build a simple
four-row table — one row per Caliph, columns for main events,
achievements, difficulties — since exam questions often ask candidates
to compare or evaluate rather than simply narrate. For the Articles of
Faith and Pillars, practise stating significance in one sentence per
item, since "describe" answers that stop short of explaining
significance are the most common way marks are lost on this topic.
