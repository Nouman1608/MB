## Questions

**1.** Define a *zero-hour contract* and explain **one** advantage to a business of using zero-hour contracts. **[3]**

**2.** Explain the difference between *core* workers and *peripheral* workers. **[2]**

**3.** A theme park needs many extra staff during the summer holidays but far fewer in winter. Explain **one** advantage to the theme park of employing its extra summer staff on temporary contracts. **[3]**

**4.** Explain the difference between *hard* and *soft* human resource management (HRM). **[2]**

**5.** Define *management by objectives (MBO)* and state **one** limitation of it. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by a *matrix structure* and state **one** disadvantage of using it. **[2]**

**7.** Identify **two** of the four competencies of emotional intelligence described by Goleman, and explain why **one** of them matters for a leader. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A zero-hour contract is an employment contract with no guaranteed minimum number of hours; the employee works only when the business needs them [1]. One advantage is that the business pays wages only when there is work to do [1], so labour costs rise and fall with demand, which helps keep unit costs and prices competitive when trade is slow [1].

*Examiner insight (June 2024):* definitions needed to go further than just naming contract types; for example, stating that a zero-hour contract gives no guaranteed hours. Advantages to employees, such as a better work-life balance, were only credited when linked to a benefit for the business, such as higher productivity.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, June 2024, Paper 32, Question 1.

**2.** Core workers are permanent, usually full-time employees with key skills who are central to the business's main activities [1]. Peripheral workers are employed on flexible terms, such as part-time, temporary or zero-hour contracts, and can be taken on or released as demand changes [1].

*Mark-scheme insight (June 2024):* employing peripheral workers on temporary or zero-hour contracts to match seasonal production was accepted as relevant context when analysing flexible contracts.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, June 2024, Paper 32, Question 1.

**3.** Temporary contracts let the theme park take on staff only for the busy summer months [1], so it avoids paying wages to extra workers in winter when there are few visitors [1]. This keeps labour costs in line with seasonal demand, lowering costs over the year and protecting profit [1]. (Also valid: the park can judge temporary workers' performance before offering any of them permanent jobs.)

*Examiner insight (June 2024):* the best answers linked flexible contracts directly to seasonal demand for labour in the business's own situation, and then developed a chain of reasoning to lower costs and higher profit or more competitive prices. Only advantages were needed, so time spent on drawbacks earned nothing.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, June 2024, Paper 32, Question 1.

**4.** Hard HRM treats employees as a resource to be used efficiently, with a focus on cost, close control and short-term contracts [1]. Soft HRM treats employees as a valuable asset to be developed, with a focus on training, motivation, involvement and long-term commitment [1].

*Tip:* a useful link is that hard HRM often goes with a flexible, peripheral workforce, while soft HRM is more common for core staff.

**5.** MBO is a method of management in which managers and employees agree specific objectives for each employee that are linked to the objectives of the business, and progress is reviewed regularly [1]. One limitation is that objectives may become out of date quickly in a fast-changing market, or it can be time-consuming to agree and review objectives for every employee [1].

*Tip:* the word "agreed" is important in the definition: under MBO objectives are agreed with the employee, not simply imposed.

**6.** A matrix structure groups staff into project teams that draw people from different functional departments, so each employee reports both to a functional manager and to a project manager [1]. One disadvantage is that having two managers can cause confusion or conflict over priorities, because the employee may receive competing instructions [1].

*Tip:* say who the employee reports to. Reporting to two managers is the key feature of a matrix structure.

**7.** Any two from: self-awareness, self-management (self-regulation), social awareness (empathy), relationship management (social skill) [1]. For example, social awareness matters because a leader who understands how employees feel [1] can respond to concerns early, which keeps motivation high and reduces conflict or labour turnover [1].

*Tip:* learn the four competencies as a set; a question often asks you to name some before applying one to a leader.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining an advantage to employees when the question asks for an advantage to the business.
- Defining flexible contracts only by naming them, without saying what makes them flexible (for example, no guaranteed hours).
- Adding drawbacks or evaluation when the question asks only for advantages.
- Confusing hard HRM with strict or unpleasant management; it is about treating labour as a cost to be controlled.
- Describing a matrix structure without mentioning that staff report to two managers.
