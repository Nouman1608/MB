## Definitions to learn exactly

- **Covalent bonding**: electrostatic attraction between the **nuclei of two atoms** and a **shared pair of electrons**.
- **Coordinate (dative covalent) bond**: a covalent bond in which **both electrons** of the shared pair come from **one atom**.
- **σ bond**: formed by **direct overlap** of orbitals between the bonding atoms.
- **π bond**: formed by **sideways overlap of adjacent p orbitals**, above and below the σ bond.
- **Bond energy**: energy required to break **one mole** of a particular covalent bond **in the gaseous state**.
- **Bond length**: the **internuclear distance** of two covalently bonded atoms.

## σ and π counting

    single bond  = 1 σ
    double bond  = 1 σ + 1 π
    triple bond  = 1 σ + 2 π

| Molecule | σ | π | Lone pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂, Cl₂, HCl | 1 | 0 | Cl: 3 each |
| O₂ | 1 | 1 | 2 on each O |
| N₂ | 1 | 2 | 1 on each N |
| CO₂ | 2 | 2 | 2 on each O |
| NH₃ | 3 | 0 | 1 on N |
| CH₄ | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| C₂H₆ | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| C₂H₄ | 5 | 1 | 0 |
| HCN | 2 | 2 | 1 on N |

## How the bonds form

- **H₂**: 1s + 1s direct overlap → σ.
- **C₂H₆**: sp³ + sp³ (C–C σ); sp³ + 1s (C–H σ).
- **C₂H₄**: sp² orbitals form the σ framework; the unhybridised p orbitals overlap sideways → π.
- **HCN**: sp carbon; σ to H and σ to N; two π bonds C≡N from two pairs of p orbitals.
- **N₂**: one σ (direct overlap) and two π (sideways overlap).
- A π bond is **weaker** than a σ bond (sideways overlap is less effective).

## Hybridisation

| Hybrid | Mixed orbitals | Hybrids | Angle | Spare p orbitals | Carbon with… |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sp³ | s + 3p | 4 | 109.5° | 0 | four single bonds |
| sp² | s + 2p | 3 | 120° | 1 | one double bond |
| sp | s + p | 2 | 180° | 2 | a triple bond or two double bonds |

## Expanded octets (Period 3 only)

| Species | Around central atom | Electrons |
|---|---|---|
| PCl₅ | 5 bonding pairs | 10 |
| SF₆ | 6 bonding pairs | 12 |
| SO₂ | 2 double bonds + 1 lone pair | 10 |

- Possible because the third shell has **d orbitals** available and the atom is **larger**.
- Period 2 atoms (C, N, O, F) are limited to **8**: NCl₅ and OF₆ do not exist.

## Coordinate bonding

| Example | Donor (lone pair) | Acceptor (empty orbital) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl | N in NH₃ | H⁺ | NH₄⁺ with 4 identical N–H bonds |
| 2AlCl₃ → Al₂Cl₆ | Cl in each AlCl₃ unit | Al in the other unit (6 outer electrons) | 2 coordinate bonds; 2 bridging Cl; each Al has 8 electrons |
| H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺ | O in H₂O | H⁺ | 3 identical O–H bonds |

Represent a coordinate bond with an **arrow from donor to acceptor**.

## Bond energy, bond length and reactivity

- More shared pairs → **shorter and stronger** bond: C–C 0.154 nm, C=C 0.134 nm, C≡C 0.120 nm.
- Larger atoms → **longer, weaker** bonds.
- A weaker bond breaks more easily, so the molecule is more reactive at that bond.

| Comparison | Bond energy order | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| N₂ | N≡N very high | N₂ is unreactive |
| Hydrogen halides | H–Cl > H–Br > H–I | HI decomposes most easily on heating |
| Halogenoalkanes | C–Cl > C–Br > C–I | iodoalkanes hydrolysed fastest |
| Alkenes | π weaker than σ | π bond breaks in addition reactions |

## Exam traps

- "Sharing electrons" is not a full definition; mention **attraction to both nuclei**.
- A double bond is **not** two σ bonds.
- After forming, a coordinate bond is **identical** to the other bonds in NH₄⁺.
- Al₂Cl₆ has **two** coordinate bonds, not one.
- Only **Period 3** atoms expand the octet in this subtopic.
- Bond energy is defined for the **gaseous** state and for **one mole** of bonds.
- For halogenoalkane reactivity, **bond energy** matters more than bond polarity.

## Self-test

1. Define covalent bonding.
2. State the numbers of σ and π bonds in N₂.
3. Describe how a π bond forms.
4. Give the hybridisation of each carbon atom in ethene.
5. How many σ bonds are there in C₂H₆?
6. State the number of electrons around sulfur in SF₆.
7. Explain why phosphorus can form PCl₅ but nitrogen cannot form NCl₅.
8. Describe the formation of the coordinate bond when ammonia reacts with hydrogen chloride.
9. Explain why AlCl₃ forms a dimer, Al₂Cl₆.
10. Define bond energy.
11. Explain why nitrogen gas is unreactive.
12. Explain why 1-iodobutane is hydrolysed faster than 1-chlorobutane.

**Answers:**

1. Electrostatic attraction between the nuclei of two atoms and a shared pair of electrons.
2. 1 σ and 2 π.
3. Sideways overlap of adjacent p orbitals, giving electron density above and below the σ bond.
4. Both are sp².
5. Seven (one C–C and six C–H).
6. Twelve.
7. Phosphorus is in Period 3 and can expand its octet (it has available 3d orbitals and is larger); nitrogen is in Period 2 and limited to eight outer electrons.
8. The lone pair on N in NH₃ is donated to H⁺ (from HCl), forming a fourth N–H bond in NH₄⁺; the product is ammonium chloride.
9. Al in AlCl₃ has only six outer electrons; a lone pair on a Cl atom of each unit is donated to the Al of the other, forming two coordinate bonds so each Al has eight.
10. The energy required to break one mole of a particular covalent bond in the gaseous state.
11. The N≡N triple bond has a very high bond energy, so a lot of energy is needed to break it.
12. The C–I bond is longer and has a lower bond energy than C–Cl, so it breaks more easily.
