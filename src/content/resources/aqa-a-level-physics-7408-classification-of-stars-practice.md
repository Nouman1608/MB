These questions cover sub-section 3.9.2 Classification of stars of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards), part of the Astrophysics option (A-level only, Paper 3 Section B).

Revise from the [classification of stars study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-classification-of-stars/) and [classification of stars revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-classification-of-stars-revision-notes/) first. Mixed option questions are in the [Astrophysics practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-astrophysics-practice/).

Data: σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴; Wien constant 2.9 × 10⁻³ m K; G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; solar mass 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg; solar radius 6.96 × 10⁸ m; 1 pc = 3.08 × 10¹⁶ m; 1 light year = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m; total energy output of the Sun over its lifetime 1.2 × 10⁴⁴ J.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define apparent magnitude and explain why brightness is described as a subjective scale. **[2]**

**2.** Define the parsec and show that 1 pc is about 3.3 light years. **[2]**

**3.** State two assumptions made when applying the inverse square law to the radiation from a star. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the shape of the light curve of a typical type 1a supernova. **[2]**

**5.** State one defining property of a neutron star and one defining property of a black hole. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** **(a)** A star has apparent magnitude 8.2 and is 520 pc from Earth. Calculate its absolute magnitude. **[2]**
**(b)** Another star has absolute magnitude −2.0 and apparent magnitude +3.0. Calculate its distance in parsecs. **[2]**

**7.** The spectrum of star P peaks at 420 nm.

**(a)** Calculate its surface temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** State its spectral class and intrinsic colour. **[1]**
**(c)** Star Q has a surface temperature of 4000 K and a power output of 2.4 × 10²⁹ W. Calculate its radius in solar radii. **[3]**
**(d)** State the region of the HR diagram in which star Q lies. **[1]**

**8.** A star has a power output of 3.9 × 10²⁸ W and is 25 pc from Earth. Calculate the intensity of its radiation at Earth. **[3]**

**9.** A star's spectrum shows weak hydrogen Balmer lines and strong lines of neutral metals. Suggest its spectral class and explain the weakness of the Balmer lines. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** State the position of the Sun on the HR diagram. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the path on the HR diagram of a star similar to the Sun from the main sequence to the end of its life, naming each stage. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** A gamma ray burst releases 3.0 × 10⁴⁴ J. Compare this with the Sun's lifetime output. **[2]**
**(b)** A neutron star has a mass of 2.0 solar masses and a radius of 12 km. Calculate its mean density. **[2]**

**12.** A type 1a supernova reaches a peak apparent magnitude of 12.7. Take its peak absolute magnitude as −19.3.

**(a)** Calculate its distance in parsecs and in metres. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how observations of distant type 1a supernovae led to the idea of dark energy, and why the idea is controversial. **[2]**

**13.** A galaxy has a supermassive black hole of 2.0 × 10⁸ solar masses at its centre. Calculate the radius of its event horizon. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Apparent magnitude is a measure of how bright a star appears from Earth, on the Hipparcos scale [1]; brightness depends on human perception (the original scale was set by eye), whereas intensity is a measured physical quantity [1].

**2.** The distance at which one astronomical unit subtends an angle of one arcsecond [1]; 3.08 × 10¹⁶/9.46 × 10¹⁵ = **3.26 ly** [1].

**3.** The star emits radiation equally in all directions [1]; no radiation is absorbed or scattered between the star and Earth [1].

**4.** A rapid rise to a peak [1]; followed by a slower decline over weeks to months [1].

**5.** Neutron star: composed almost entirely of neutrons, with a density similar to nuclear density [1]. Black hole: escape velocity greater than the speed of light [1].

**6. (a)** M = 8.2 − 5 log(520/10) [1] = 8.2 − 8.58 = **−0.38** [1]
**(b)** 3.0 − (−2.0) = 5 log(d/10), so log(d/10) = 1.0 [1]; d = **100 pc** [1]

**7. (a)** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³/420 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = **6.9 × 10³ K** [1]
**(b)** Class **F**, **white** [1]
**(c)** A = P/σT⁴ = 2.4 × 10²⁹/(5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 4000⁴) [1]; r = √(A/4π) = 3.6 × 10¹⁰ m [1]; r/r_sun = **52** solar radii [1]
**(d)** The giant region, top right (cool but luminous) [1].

**8.** d = 25 × 3.08 × 10¹⁶ = 7.7 × 10¹⁷ m [1]; I = P/4πd² = 3.9 × 10²⁸/(4π × (7.7 × 10¹⁷)²) [1] = **5.2 × 10⁻⁹ W m⁻²** [1]

**9.** Class **K** [1]; Balmer lines need hydrogen atoms with electrons in the n = 2 state [1]; at this low temperature few atoms are excited to n = 2, so there is little Balmer absorption [1].

**10. (a)** On the main sequence at about 5800 K (class G) with absolute magnitude about +5 [1].
**(b)** It leaves the main sequence and moves up and to the right as it expands and cools into a red giant [1]; it then loses its outer layers [1]; the remaining core moves to the left and down to become a white dwarf [1].

**11. (a)** 3.0 × 10⁴⁴/1.2 × 10⁴⁴ [1] = **about 2.5 times** the Sun's lifetime output [1]
**(b)** ρ = M/V = 2.0 × 1.99 × 10³⁰/((4/3)π × (1.2 × 10⁴)³) [1] = **5.5 × 10¹⁷ kg m⁻³** [1]

**12. (a)** m − M = 12.7 + 19.3 = 32.0 [1]; d = 10 × 10^(32.0/5) = **2.5 × 10⁷ pc** [1] = **7.7 × 10²³ m** [1]
**(b)** Distant type 1a supernovae were fainter than expected, so further away than predicted, implying the expansion of the Universe is accelerating, which is attributed to dark energy [1]; controversial because the nature of dark energy is unknown, and the result assumes every type 1a supernova has the same peak absolute magnitude [1].

**13.** Rs = 2GM/c² = 2 × 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 2.0 × 10⁸ × 1.99 × 10³⁰/(3.00 × 10⁸)² [1] = **5.9 × 10¹¹ m** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using metres in the distance modulus.
- Mixing up the sign in m − M when M is negative.
- Forgetting to square-root A/4π for the radius.
- Explaining Balmer weakness without mentioning n = 2.
- Converting solar masses to kilograms incorrectly.
