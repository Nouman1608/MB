These questions cover section 3.3.1 Setting marketing objectives of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [marketing objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-marketing-objectives/) and the [marketing objectives revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-marketing-objectives-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** A market was worth £200 million last year and is worth £230 million this year. What was the rate of market growth? **[1]**

A: 13%

B: 15%

C: 30%

D: 87%

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a marketing objective rather than a marketing strategy? **[1]**

A: Launch a new range aimed at students

B: Raise market share from 9% to 12% within two years

C: Advertise on social media in the summer

D: Sell through a second website

**3.** Define the term "brand loyalty". **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a business might prefer an objective for sales value rather than sales volume. **[3]**

**5.** Ferndale Snacks sold 90,000 packs at £2.40 last year. This year it sold 99,000 packs at £2.20.

**(a)** Calculate the sales value in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain whether Ferndale met an objective of increasing sales volume and an objective of increasing sales value. **[2]**

**6.** Corvane Coffee sells ground coffee. Last year the market was worth £150 million and Corvane's sales were £18 million. This year the market is worth £162 million and Corvane's sales are £21.06 million.

**(a)** Calculate the growth in the market. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Corvane's sales growth. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate Corvane's market share in each year. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain what the results suggest about Corvane's performance. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Explain **two** limitations of setting marketing objectives. **[4]**

**8.** Analyse **two** benefits to a business such as Corvane Coffee of setting marketing objectives. **[6]**

**9.** Hollin Bakeries sells in a mature, slow-growing market. It has a 6% market share and has set an objective of reaching 10% within three years. Assess whether this is an appropriate marketing objective. **[9]**

**10.** Nurelle Skincare is launching its first range. Its owner is considering setting an objective for either sales volume or brand loyalty in the first two years. Evaluate which objective is more appropriate. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. (230 − 200) ÷ 200 × 100 = 15%. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. It states a measurable target to be achieved by a date; the other options describe how to reach a target, so they are strategies or tactics. **[1]**

**3.** The extent to which customers keep buying a brand rather than switching to rivals [1], shown for example by repeat purchases or loyalty scheme membership [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Sales value takes account of price as well as the number sold [1]. A business selling premium goods may sell fewer units but earn more revenue per unit [1], so an objective for volume could push it to discount and damage its brand image, while a value objective protects revenue and image [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Last year: 90,000 × £2.40 = **£216,000** [1]. This year: 99,000 × £2.20 = **£217,800** [1].

**(b)** Volume rose by 9,000 packs (10%), so a volume objective was met [1]. Value rose only £1,800 (under 1%) because the price was cut, so a value objective was met only just [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** (162 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **8%** [1].

**(b)** (21.06 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 [1] = **17%** [1].

**(c)** Last year: 18 ÷ 150 × 100 = **12%** [1]. This year: 21.06 ÷ 162 × 100 = **13%** [1].

**(d)** Corvane's sales grew by 17%, more than twice the market's 8% [1], so its share rose by one percentage point [1], which suggests it is winning customers from rivals (for example through better promotion or stronger loyalty) rather than just benefiting from a growing market [1]. **[9]**

**7.** Limitation 1: objectives may be unrealistic if they are set without good market research [1], which can demotivate staff when targets are missed [1]. Limitation 2: a narrow objective can distort behaviour, for example chasing sales volume with discounts that cut profit [1], and external events such as a recession can make a target impossible whatever marketing does [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Benefit 1: direction and measurement [1]. A target such as a 14% market share tells the marketing team what to aim for [1], and results can be compared with the target to judge whether a campaign worked [1]. Benefit 2: coordination of functions [1]. A sales target tells operations how much coffee to roast and finance how much working capital to provide [1], so stock shortages and cash problems are less likely [1]. Credit also: focus of limited budget on the activities that serve the objective. **[6]**

**9.** For: a clear, measurable target with a time limit [1]; a higher share would bring buying power with suppliers and economies of scale [1]; it would give the bakery a stronger brand presence [1]. Against: in a mature market with little growth, extra share must be taken from rivals [1], which may require heavy promotion or price cuts that reduce profit [1]. Going from 6% to 10% is a rise of two-thirds, which may be unrealistic in three years without extra capacity [1]. Factors: the strength of competitors' responses [1], Hollin's capacity and finance [1]. Judgement: the objective is appropriate only if supported by evidence of unmet demand and by capacity to supply; otherwise a smaller step, such as 8%, or an objective for loyalty and sales value might be more realistic [1]. **[9]**

**10.** Volume: gets the product into customers' hands and builds awareness quickly [1]; higher output may lower unit costs [1]; a new business needs revenue to cover its costs [1]. Loyalty: a new brand with a small range needs repeat buyers to survive [1]; loyal customers recommend the brand and cost less to keep than new customers cost to win [1]; but loyalty takes time to build, so it is difficult to measure in two years [1]. Volume also produces sales data showing which products customers prefer [1]. Volume risks: heavy discounting to hit the target could harm a skincare brand's image [1]. Factors: how much capacity and cash the owner has [1] and how quickly competitors respond [1]. Judgement: volume is likely to matter more in year one to establish the business, with loyalty measures (such as repeat purchase rate) added as a secondary objective for year two [1], because without sales there is no customer base to be loyal [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers name the condition the judgement depends on, such as capacity, cash or life cycle stage, and then apply it to the business in the case.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a tactic and calling it an objective.
- Using different bases in a market share calculation.
- Saying a share rose "by 1%" when it rose by one percentage point.
- Treating higher volume as higher sales value without checking price.
- Giving benefits of objectives in general terms without linking them to the business in the case.
- Reaching a conclusion that does not say what the choice depends on.
