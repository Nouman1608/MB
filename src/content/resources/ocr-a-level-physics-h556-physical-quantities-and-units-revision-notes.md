Quick recall for section 2.1, Physical quantities and units, of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017: learning outcomes 2.1.1(a)-(b) and 2.1.2(a)-(f). For full explanations and worked examples, read the [physical quantities and units study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-h556-physical-quantities-and-units/), then test yourself with the [physical quantities and units practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-h556-physical-quantities-and-units-practice/).

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical quantity | A property that can be measured, written as a number multiplied by a unit |
| Base quantity | One of the six S.I. quantities from which all others are defined |
| Derived unit | A unit formed from base units through a defining equation |
| Homogeneous equation | Every term has the same base units |
| Prefix | A symbol placed before a unit to multiply it by a power of ten |
| Estimate | An approximate value of a quantity from stated, sensible assumptions |

## The six base units

| Quantity | Unit |
|---|---|
| mass | kg |
| length | m |
| time | s |
| current | A |
| temperature | K |
| amount of substance | mol |

Charge (C = A s) and energy (J) are derived. The kilogram is the base unit despite its prefix.

## Derived units to know in base form

| Quantity | Unit | Base units |
|---|---|---|
| momentum | kg m s⁻¹ | kg m s⁻¹ |
| density | kg m⁻³ | kg m⁻³ |
| force | N | kg m s⁻² |
| pressure, stress, Young modulus | Pa | kg m⁻¹ s⁻² |
| energy | J | kg m² s⁻² |
| power | W | kg m² s⁻³ |
| charge | C | A s |
| p.d., e.m.f. | V | kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ |
| resistance | Ω | kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² |
| resistivity | Ω m | kg m³ s⁻³ A⁻² |
| spring constant | N m⁻¹ | kg s⁻² |
| specific heat capacity | J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ | m² s⁻² K⁻¹ |
| frequency | Hz | s⁻¹ |
| gravitational constant G | N m² kg⁻² | m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² |

## Method: base units of any quantity

1. Pick the simplest equation containing the quantity.
2. Make the quantity the subject.
3. Replace each symbol by its base units.
4. Collect powers of kg, m, s, A, K and mol separately.

## Method: homogeneity check

1. Write the base units of each term separately.
2. Simplify each term.
3. Compare: all terms on both sides must match.
4. Conclusion: "not homogeneous, so wrong" or "homogeneous, so it may be correct". Numbers such as ½ and 2π cannot be checked.

## Prefixes

| p | n | μ | m | c | d | k | M | G | T |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁻¹² | 10⁻⁹ | 10⁻⁶ | 10⁻³ | 10⁻² | 10⁻¹ | 10³ | 10⁶ | 10⁹ | 10¹² |

Powers apply to prefixes: 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m², 1 mm³ = 10⁻⁹ m³, 1 dm³ = 10⁻³ m³.

## Worked reminders

- 4.7 μF = 4.7 × 10⁻⁶ F.
- 680 nm = 6.8 × 10⁻⁷ m.
- 0.50 mm² = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m².
- 7.9 g cm⁻³ = 7.9 × 10³ kg m⁻³.
- 2.0 kW h = 2.0 × 10³ × 3600 = 7.2 × 10⁶ J.
- Young modulus E = stress / strain: strain has no unit, so E has the unit of stress, Pa = kg m⁻¹ s⁻².

## Estimates

- State every assumed value with its unit.
- Use one or two significant figures in the answer.
- Typical values: adult 70 kg, room height 3 m, walking 1.5 m s⁻¹, car 30 m s⁻¹ on a motorway, water 1000 kg m⁻³, air 1.2 kg m⁻³, atmosphere 1 × 10⁵ Pa.

## Labelling

- Column and axis headings: **quantity / unit**, such as V / mV or v / m s⁻¹.
- Entries are then pure numbers, with no unit in each cell.
- A power of ten in a heading: λ / 10⁻⁷ m means an entry of 6.5 is 6.5 × 10⁻⁷ m.
- Logs: **ln (R / Ω)** or **lg (f / Hz)**, never a unit after the log.

## Exam traps

- Calling charge a base quantity.
- Using °C in an equation that needs K; a temperature difference is the same in both.
- Treating m (milli) and M (mega) as the same.
- Converting cm³ to m³ with 10⁻² instead of 10⁻⁶.
- Saying an equation is correct because it is homogeneous.
- Writing "lg f (Hz)" on a log axis.
- Giving an estimate to three or more significant figures with no assumptions.

## Quick self-test

1. List the six S.I. base quantities named in OCR H556.
2. Write the joule in base units.
3. Write the ohm in base units.
4. Show that the unit of momentum is the same as N s.
5. Convert 250 cm³ to m³.
6. Convert 0.35 mm² to m².
7. Write the pascal in base units.
8. Explain why "the equation is homogeneous" does not prove it is correct.
9. Is P = I²R homogeneous? Show your working.
10. Give the base units of the Young modulus.
11. A table column is headed I / μA and an entry reads 48. What is the current?
12. Estimate the weight of water in a full bath, stating assumptions.

### Answers

1. Mass, length, time, current, temperature and amount of substance.
2. J = N m = **kg m² s⁻²**.
3. Ω = V A⁻¹ = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ ÷ A = **kg m² s⁻³ A⁻²**.
4. N s = kg m s⁻² × s = **kg m s⁻¹**, the same as kg m s⁻¹ for mv.
5. 250 × 10⁻⁶ = **2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³**.
6. 0.35 × 10⁻⁶ = **3.5 × 10⁻⁷ m²**.
7. Pa = N m⁻² = **kg m⁻¹ s⁻²**.
8. Pure numbers such as ½ or 2π have no units, so a wrong numerical factor still passes the check.
9. I²R: A² × kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² = kg m² s⁻³, which is the watt, so **yes**.
10. **kg m⁻¹ s⁻²**, the same as stress, because strain has no unit.
11. **48 μA = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ A**.
12. For example, volume 1.5 m × 0.6 m × 0.2 m ≈ 0.18 m³, mass ≈ 1000 × 0.18 = 180 kg, weight ≈ 180 × 9.81 ≈ **2 × 10³ N**. Any sensible assumptions with a matching answer are fine.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Answers to "base units" questions that still contain N, J or V.
- Squaring the number but not the prefix in area and volume conversions.
- Missing out a step in a "show that" so the examiner cannot see the cancellation.
- Leaving the unit off an estimate.
- Plotting from a table whose headings do not show units.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which base units does OCR A Level Physics A require?**
Six: kilogram, metre, second, ampere, kelvin and mole, listed in learning outcome 2.1.2(a). Every other unit in the specification can be expressed in terms of these.

**How accurate should an estimate be?**
One or two significant figures, or the nearest power of ten, with the assumptions stated. A sensible method with reasonable assumed values earns the credit, not a particular final number.

**How do I label a logarithmic axis?**
Take the logarithm of the quantity divided by its unit, for example ln (I / A). The logarithm of a pure number has no unit, so nothing follows the bracket.
