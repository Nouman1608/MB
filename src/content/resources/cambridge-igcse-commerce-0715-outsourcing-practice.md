## Section A - short questions

**1.** A toy company designs its toys but pays a factory owned by another firm to make them. This is: **[1]**
A contract logistics   B contract manufacturing   C in-house production   D franchising

**2.** An online bookshop pays a specialist firm to store its books, pack orders and deliver them. This is: **[1]**
A contract logistics   B wholesaling   C contract manufacturing   D a cooperative

**3.** Which is the most likely reason a business keeps production in-house rather than outsourcing it? **[1]**
A lower cost per unit   B access to specialist expertise   C protecting a secret design   D avoiding the cost of machinery

**4.** Define outsourcing. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** services provided by a contract logistics firm. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** reasons for the growth of outsourcing. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** Explain how **two** developments in transport and communication have contributed to the growth of contract manufacturing. **[4]**

**8.** *PeakWear, a small sportswear brand, has designed a new range of running jackets and is considering contract manufacturing.*
(a) Explain **two** benefits to PeakWear of contract manufacturing. (4 marks)
(b) Explain **two** risks to PeakWear and how it could reduce each one. (4 marks) **[8]**

**9.** *Spice Route Foods makes 8,000 jars of sauce a year. In-house production costs: equipment lease $5,000, labour $7,400, ingredients and jars $0.90 per jar, utilities $600. A contract manufacturer quotes $1.75 per jar, including ingredients and jars. Delivery from the contractor costs $700 a year.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of in-house production. (2 marks)
(b) Calculate the total cost of outsourcing. (2 marks)
(c) Calculate the saving per jar from the cheaper option. (2 marks) **[6]**

**10.** *HomeGlow sells candles online. Orders are about 600 a month for most of the year but rise to 4,000 a month in the two months before a major festival.*
Explain why contract logistics may be suitable for HomeGlow. **[4]**

**11.** *A logistics firm charges $6 per order. Running an in-house warehouse would cost $10,800 a month.*
(a) Calculate the number of orders a month at which the two options cost the same. (2 marks)
(b) Explain which option is cheaper for a business sending 1,500 orders a month, using a calculation. (2 marks) **[4]**

**12.** *Bloom Skincare is growing fast. Its directors disagree: one wants to outsource production to a large overseas contract manufacturer; the other wants to build its own factory.*
Evaluate whether Bloom Skincare should outsource its production. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Another firm makes the products to the company's design. **[1]**

**2.** A. Storage, packing and delivery are logistics activities. **[1]**

**3.** C. In-house production keeps control of information; the others are benefits of outsourcing. **[1]**

**4.** Paying another business [1] to carry out an activity, such as making or delivering goods, that the business could do itself [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: receiving stock [1]; storage [1]; picking and packing orders [1]; transport and delivery [1]; handling returns [1]; stock records [1]. **[max 2]**

**6.** Any two: globalisation of production [1]; better transport [1]; digital communication [1]; growth of ecommerce [1]; specialisation on core activities [1]; cost pressure [1]; need for flexibility [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Any two, 2 marks each (development plus how it helped):
Container shipping and air freight have made it cheaper and quicker to move goods long distances [1], so a brand can have products made in another country and still bring them to market economically [1].
Email, video calls and shared online systems allow designs and orders to be sent instantly [1], so a business can manage and check a distant manufacturer without being there [1].
Electronic data interchange lets orders pass automatically between systems [1], reducing errors and delays in working with a contractor [1]. **[max 4]**

**8.** (a) Any two, 2 marks each: access to expertise, because a specialist manufacturer has the machinery and skilled staff for technical fabrics, so jacket quality may be higher [2]; cost savings, because the manufacturer produces in large volumes and can make each jacket more cheaply [2]; PeakWear does not need to buy a factory or machinery, so it can spend its limited capital on marketing the new range [2]; managers can concentrate on design and selling [2]. (max 4 marks)
(b) Any two risks with a matching control, 2 marks each: quality may fall below PeakWear's standard [1], reduced by approving samples and inspecting before shipping [1]; the design could be copied [1], reduced by a confidentiality clause in the contract [1]; deliveries could be late [1], reduced by penalties for lateness or holding safety stock [1]; poor working conditions at the factory could damage the brand [1], reduced by auditing the factory before signing [1]. (max 4 marks) **[8]**

**9.** (a) Ingredients and jars: 8,000 x 0.90 = $7,200 [1]. Total = 5,000 + 7,400 + 7,200 + 600 = **$20,200** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Contract price: 8,000 x 1.75 = $14,000 [1]. Total = 14,000 + 700 = **$14,700** [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Saving = 20,200 - 14,700 = $5,500 [1]; 5,500 / 8,000 = **$0.69 per jar** (0.6875) [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**10.** Up to 4 marks for an explanation applied to HomeGlow:
Demand is uneven: 600 orders most months but 4,000 before the festival [1]. An in-house warehouse would need space and staff for the peak, which would stand idle for the rest of the year [1]. A contract logistics firm charges per order, so HomeGlow pays only for the orders it sends [1], and the firm can provide extra packing staff and delivery capacity in the busy months, so customers still receive candles on time [1]. **[4]**

**11.** (a) 10,800 / 6 [1] = **1,800 orders** a month [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Contract logistics: 1,500 x 6 = $9,000, which is less than the in-house cost of $10,800 [1], so outsourcing is cheaper by $1,800 at this volume, because orders are below the 1,800 level where costs are equal [1]. (2 marks) **[4]**

**12.** Up to 2 for arguments for outsourcing, up to 2 for arguments against, up to 2 for a justified judgement.
For: avoids the large capital cost and delay of building a factory, so Bloom can grow faster [1]; a large contractor has expertise and scale, so cost per unit may be lower [1]; output can be increased quickly as demand grows [1].
Against: skincare quality and safety are vital to Bloom's reputation, and control is harder overseas [1]; formulas could be copied [1]; long transport times and dependence on one contractor create supply risks [1]; an owned factory may be cheaper per unit once volumes are very high [1].
Judgement: for example, outsource now, with strict quality checks and a confidentiality clause, while growth is uncertain, and review building a factory once sales are high and stable [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining growth as "it is cheaper" without saying why.
- In cost comparisons, forgetting to add delivery or inspection costs to the contract price.
- Writing about contract logistics when the question asks about contract manufacturing.
- Ignoring the scenario: seasonal demand, a secret formula or limited capital usually decide the answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does the 0715 syllabus say about outsourcing?** Section 2.2 covers the growth of outsourcing through contract manufacturing and logistics.

**Is outsourcing always cheaper?** No. It often lowers cost at low or uneven volumes, but at high, steady volumes doing the work in-house can cost less per unit.
