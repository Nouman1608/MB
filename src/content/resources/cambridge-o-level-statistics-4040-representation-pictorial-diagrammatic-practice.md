These questions cover section 2.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Representation in pictorial or diagrammatic form". Revise the constructions in the [diagram study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-representation-pictorial-diagrammatic/) or the [diagram revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-representation-pictorial-diagrammatic-revision-notes/). Where a question asks for a drawing, these answers give the calculated values and features the drawing must show. All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A pictogram is to show the numbers of books borrowed on four days: 32, 20, 44 and 28.

**(a)** Suggest a suitable key, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** How many symbols represent 44 books with your key? **[1]**

**2.** In a pictogram, one symbol represents 12 cars. How many cars are represented by 3¾ symbols? **[1]**

**3.** Twenty-four pupils choose a fruit: apple 9, banana 7, orange 5, pear 3. Calculate the angles for a pie chart. **[3]**

**4.** A pie chart represents 240 people.

**(a)** How many people are represented by a sector of 81°? **[1]**

**(b)** What angle represents 34 people? **[1]**

**5.** Comparative pie charts are drawn for totals of 640 and 1000. The first chart has radius 4 cm. Calculate the radius of the second. **[2]**

**6.** Two comparative pie charts have radii 3 cm and 4.5 cm. The smaller chart represents 800 tonnes. Find the total represented by the larger chart. **[2]**

**7.** The times, in seconds, taken by ten pupils to solve a puzzle are:

4.7, 5.2, 3.9, 4.1, 5.8, 4.4, 3.6, 5.0, 4.9, 4.2

Draw an ordered stem-and-leaf diagram with a key. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

**8.** In a class of 60 pupils, 34 like mathematics, 29 like science and 9 like neither. x pupils like both.

**(a)** Form and solve an equation for x. **[2]**

**(b)** How many like mathematics only? **[1]**

**(c)** How many like exactly one of the two subjects? **[1]**

**9.** 100 tourists are asked whether they visited a museum (M), a gallery (G) or a park (P). 40 visited M, 30 visited G and 35 visited P. 12 visited M and G, 9 visited G and P, and 10 visited M and P; these figures include the 5 who visited all three.

**(a)** Find the number in each region of a three-set Venn diagram, including those who visited none. **[4]**

**(b)** How many visited exactly one place? **[1]**

**(c)** How many visited M or P, but not G? **[1]**

**10.** Three branches of a shop have the following staff.

| Branch | Full-time | Part-time | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 30 | 10 | 40 |
| B | 45 | 30 | 75 |
| C | 12 | 13 | 25 |

**(a)** A sectional bar chart uses 1 cm for 10 staff. Give the height of the top of each section for each branch. **[2]**

**(b)** A percentage bar chart uses bars 10 cm high, with full-time staff at the bottom. Give the height of the full-time section for each branch. **[2]**

**(c)** State one thing the percentage bar chart shows more clearly than the sectional bar chart. **[1]**

**11.** The marks of 11 pupils in a test are:

23, 27, 31, 31, 34, 38, 40, 42, 45, 49, 56

**(a)** Find the five values needed for a box-and-whisker diagram. **[3]**

**(b)** The diagram is drawn on a scale of 1 cm to 5 marks. Find the length of the box, and the distance of the median line from the left-hand end of the box. **[2]**

**12.** A cinema records the number of tickets sold in four price bands over two years.

| Band | Child | Student | Adult | Senior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 150 | 75 | 100 | 50 |
| Year 2 | 125 | 100 | 175 | 75 |

**(a)** A dual bar chart uses 1 cm for 25 tickets. Give the heights of all eight bars. **[2]**

**(b)** State two features, apart from the bars, that the dual bar chart must include. **[2]**

**(c)** Which band shows the greatest change between the two years? **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** One symbol = 8 books, because 32, 20, 44 and 28 then need 4, 2½, 5½ and 3½ symbols, which can be drawn clearly [1]. (A key of 4 is also acceptable.) **[1]**

**(b)** 44 ÷ 8 = 5½ symbols [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 3.75 × 12 = 45 cars [1]. **[1]**

**3.** One pupil = 360 ÷ 24 = 15° [1]. Apple 135°, banana 105° [1], orange 75°, pear 45° (total 360°) [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** 81/360 × 240 = 54 people [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 34/240 × 360 = 51° [1]. **[1]**

**5.** r = 4 × √(1000/640) [1] = 4 × 1.25 = 5 cm [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Total = 800 × (4.5/3)² [1] = 800 × 2.25 = 1800 tonnes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The ratio of radii is 1.5, but the ratio of totals is 1.5² = 2.25.

**7.** Correct stems and leaves [1], leaves in order [1], key with units such as "4 | 1 means 4.1 seconds" [1].

```
3 | 6 9
4 | 1 2 4 7 9
5 | 0 2 8
```

**[3]**

**8. (a)** 34 + 29 − x + 9 = 60 [1], so 72 − x = 60 and x = 12 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 34 − 12 = 22 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Mathematics only 22 + science only 17 = 39 [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** Centre 5; M and G only 7, G and P only 4, M and P only 5 [1]. M only = 40 − 7 − 5 − 5 = 23 [1]. G only = 30 − 7 − 4 − 5 = 14 and P only = 35 − 5 − 4 − 5 = 21 [1]. In at least one = 23 + 14 + 21 + 7 + 4 + 5 + 5 = 79, so none = 21 [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** 23 + 14 + 21 = 58 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** M only 23 + P only 21 + M and P only 5 = 49 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the centre from each pair total before using it.

**10. (a)** Full-time tops at 3 cm, 4.5 cm and 1.2 cm for A, B and C [1]; totals at 4 cm, 7.5 cm and 2.5 cm [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** A: 30/40 × 10 = 7.5 cm; B: 45/75 × 10 = 6 cm [1]; C: 12/25 × 10 = 4.8 cm [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** That the proportion of full-time staff is highest in A and lowest in C, even though B has the most full-time staff [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** Minimum 23 and maximum 56 [1]; median (6th value) 38 [1]; Q1 = 31 (median of 23, 27, 31, 31, 34) and Q3 = 45 (median of 40, 42, 45, 49, 56) [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Box length (45 − 31) ÷ 5 = 2.8 cm [1]; median line (38 − 31) ÷ 5 = 1.4 cm from the left end [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** Year 1: 6, 3, 4, 2 cm [1]. Year 2: 5, 4, 7, 3 cm [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Any two, for [1] each: a key showing which bar is each year; a labelled vertical scale starting at zero; category labels; a title; gaps between pairs. **[2]**

**(c)** Adult, which rose by 75 tickets [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding 360 ÷ total before multiplying.
- Comparative pie radii scaled by the ratio of totals rather than its square root.
- Putting pair totals into Venn regions without removing the centre.
- Sectional bars drawn from zero for every section.
- Stem-and-leaf diagrams with no units in the key.
- Medians drawn by eye at the centre of the box.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to draw the diagram if the question says "draw"?**
Yes. These answers list the values the drawing must show; in the exam, accuracy marks depend on plotting them to scale with the required labels and key.

**What if my key for a pictogram differs from the mark scheme?**
Any key that represents every value with clear, drawable symbols is usually acceptable, provided it is stated.
