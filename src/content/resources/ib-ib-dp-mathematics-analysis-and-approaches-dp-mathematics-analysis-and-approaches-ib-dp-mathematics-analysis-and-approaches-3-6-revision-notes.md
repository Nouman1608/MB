## Core results

| Result | Equivalent forms |
|---|---|
| cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 | sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ; cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ; (1 − cos θ)(1 + cos θ) = sin²θ |
| tan θ = sin θ / cos θ | sin θ = tan θ cos θ |
| sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ | sin θ cos θ = ½ sin 2θ |
| cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ | = 2cos²θ − 1 = 1 − 2sin²θ |
| Power reduction | cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2; sin²θ = (1 − cos 2θ)/2 |

All of these hold for every angle, so they can be applied with any angle in place of θ: sin 6x = 2 sin 3x cos 3x, and cos θ = 2cos²(θ/2) − 1.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity | A statement true for every value where both sides are defined; you prove it |
| Equation | A statement true only for particular values; you solve it |
| Double angle | An angle that is twice another, such as 2θ compared with θ |
| Exact value | A value written with integers, fractions, surds or π, not a rounded decimal |
| Quadrant | One of the four regions of the unit circle; it fixes the sign of each ratio |
| Reference angle | The acute angle between the radius and the x-axis |

Signs by quadrant: first, all ratios positive; second, sin only; third, tan only; fourth, cos only.

## Which form of cos 2θ?

- You know cos θ only: use 2cos²θ − 1.
- You know sin θ only: use 1 − 2sin²θ.
- You want to cancel a "+1": 1 + cos 2θ = 2cos²θ.
- You want to cancel a "−1" or a "1 −": 1 − cos 2θ = 2sin²θ.
- You see cos²θ − sin²θ or cos⁴θ − sin⁴θ: it is cos 2θ (the second after factorising).

## Method: one ratio from another

1. Find the size of the missing ratio with the identity or a right-angled triangle.
2. Read the sign from the quadrant given in the question.
3. With no quadrant given, give both signs.
4. For a double angle, substitute into 2 sin θ cos θ or the right form of cos 2θ; the signs then follow automatically.

**Quick examples**

- sin θ = 5/13, θ obtuse: cos θ = −12/13, tan θ = −5/12, sin 2θ = −120/169, cos 2θ = 119/169.
- cos θ = −0.6 with π < θ < 3π/2: sin θ = −0.8, so sin 2θ = 2(−0.8)(−0.6) = 0.96.
- cos 2A = 1/9 with A acute: 2cos²A − 1 = 1/9 gives cos²A = 5/9, so cos A = √5/3.
- tan θ = 3/4 and sin θ < 0: θ is in the third quadrant, so sin θ = −3/5 and cos θ = −4/5.

## Method: show that an identity holds

1. Start with the more complicated side.
2. Replace with identities chosen to cancel terms; write one step per line.
3. End with the other side exactly as printed.
4. State any condition, such as cos θ ≠ 0, when you divide.

Worked reminder: (1 − cos 2θ)/sin 2θ = 2sin²θ / (2 sin θ cos θ) = sin θ / cos θ = tan θ.

## Exam traps

- **sin 2θ is not 2 sin θ.** Test with θ = π/2: sin π = 0, but 2 sin(π/2) = 2.
- **Square roots need a sign.** sin²θ = 1/4 means sin θ = ±1/2 until the quadrant is used.
- **The sign of sin 2θ is not the sign of sin θ.** For θ obtuse, sin θ > 0 but sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ < 0.
- **No squares cancel.** cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 does not give cos θ + sin θ = 1.
- **Notation.** sin²θ = (sin θ)²; sin θ² is the sine of θ².
- **Meeting in the middle.** Working on both sides of a "show that" without one clear chain can lose the final mark.
- **Exact means exact.** On Paper 1, 0.894 is not accepted for 2√5/5.
- **Missing the hidden double angle.** 4 sin x cos x = 2 sin 2x; its greatest value is 2, not 4.

## Self-test

1. Given cos θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find sin 2θ.
2. Given sin θ = −1/3 and π < θ < 3π/2, find cos θ exactly.
3. Given sin x = 2/5, find cos 2x.
4. Given cos x = −1/3, find cos 2x.
5. Simplify 2 sin 3x cos 3x.
6. Simplify 1 − 2sin²(π/8) and give its exact value.
7. Given tan θ = 1/2 and θ is reflex with sin θ < 0, find sin θ and cos θ.
8. Write cos²x in terms of cos 2x.
9. Simplify cos⁴x − sin⁴x.
10. Given cos 2A = −7/9 and A is acute, find sin A.
11. Find the greatest value of 10 sin x cos x.
12. Show that (sin θ + cos θ)² = 1 + sin 2θ.

### Answers

1. sin θ = 4/5, so sin 2θ = 2(4/5)(3/5) = 24/25.
2. cos²θ = 8/9 and cos is negative in the third quadrant: cos θ = −2√2/3.
3. 1 − 2(4/25) = 17/25.
4. 2(1/9) − 1 = −7/9.
5. sin 6x.
6. cos(π/4) = √2/2.
7. Third quadrant: sin θ = −1/√5 = −√5/5, cos θ = −2/√5 = −2√5/5.
8. cos²x = (1 + cos 2x)/2.
9. (cos²x − sin²x)(cos²x + sin²x) = cos 2x.
10. 1 − 2sin²A = −7/9 gives sin²A = 8/9, so sin A = 2√2/3.
11. 10 sin x cos x = 5 sin 2x, so the greatest value is 5.
12. (sin θ + cos θ)² = sin²θ + 2 sin θ cos θ + cos²θ = 1 + sin 2θ.
