OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245), listed on Marlbridge under
World History, is a new course first taught from 2026. It combines
twentieth-century international relations with studies of how societies
and ideas changed, from interwar Germany to the long history of medicine
and technology. It is a linear qualification assessed by two written
papers, each lasting 105 minutes, each marked out of 60 and each worth
50%. The first exams are in May/June 2028, so students beginning the
course now are the first cohort to sit them.

## How the course is organised

Each paper is divided into two sections, and in every section you study
one option from a list. Your school chooses the options, so check which
ones you are following.

**Paper 1, International Relations Depth Studies: Conflict and Peace in
the 20th Century**

- *Section A* (one of two): Peacemaking and the League of Nations,
  c1919-1939; or Crises of the Cold War, 1960-1975.
- *Section B* (one of four): The origins of the First World War,
  c1890-1915; Hitler's foreign policy and the origins of the Second World
  War, 1933-1941; The origins and development of the Cold War,
  1945-1960; or Détente and the collapse of communism, c1969-1991.

**Paper 2, Studies in Change**

- *Section A, Societies in change* (one of three): Life in Germany,
  1919-1949; Life in Russia and the USSR, 1914-1953; or Life in the USA,
  1920-1968.
- *Section B, Thematic studies* (one of two): Technology and change,
  c800-present day; or Medicine and change, c800-present day.

So every candidate studies four options in total: two depth studies in
international relations, one society in change, and one long-run
thematic study.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | International Relations Depth Studies: Conflict and Peace in the 20th Century | 105 minutes | 60 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Studies in Change | 105 minutes | 60 | 50% |

Paper 1 and Paper 2 are externally marked written exams, and there is
no tiering. Within each paper you answer on the options you have studied.
Some sections use pre-released sources, which means you may be given
source material to study before the exam; your teacher will tell you
which of your options this applies to and when the material is
released.

The qualification is linear, so both papers are taken together at the
end of the course. Because the specification is new, no exam has yet
been sat on it. Note that OxfordAQA publishes the course as
International GCSE History and does not offer History at AS or A-level.
This page follows Version 1.1 of the specification.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers ask different kinds of historical question, and seeing
that difference helps you study both well:

- **Depth versus change.** Paper 1 looks closely at relatively short
  periods of international tension and diplomacy. Paper 2 asks how and
  why things changed over time, whether across a few decades in one
  country or across many centuries in a theme.
- **The depth studies overlap.** Several Paper 1 options form a
  continuous story: the origins of the First World War, the peace
  settlement and the League, Hitler's foreign policy, and the Cold War
  from its origins through its crises to détente and the collapse of
  communism. Knowing the neighbouring periods gives context to the one
  you study.
- **Paper 2 societies connect to Paper 1.** Life in Germany, Russia and
  the USA overlaps in time with the international relations options, so
  domestic developments can help explain foreign policy, and the reverse.
- **The thematic studies train long-term thinking.** Technology and
  change and Medicine and change ask you to identify turning points,
  continuities and the factors that drive change, skills that also help
  you explain causation in the shorter periods.

Across the course you will need to explain causes and consequences,
analyse and evaluate sources, and reach supported judgements.

## Planning your study

A clear order makes the four options easier to manage:

- **First year:** begin with your Paper 1 options, in chronological
  order, so that the twentieth-century international story builds
  logically. Keep a single timeline to which you add each event.
- **Second year, first part:** your Paper 2 society in change, linking it
  to the international context you already know, then the thematic study,
  which ranges across many centuries and benefits from a dedicated
  timeline of its own.
- **Second year, final months:** revise all four options, study any
  pre-released sources carefully once they are available, and practise
  answering on both papers.

With no past papers yet for this specification, practise with
teacher-set questions and any sample material your school provides, and
start writing answers as soon as your first option is taught. For
section-by-section technique, use the course's exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is OxfordAQA IGCSE History 9245 first examined?**
First teaching began in 2026, and the first exams are in May/June 2028.

**How many options do I study?**
Four: one from Paper 1 Section A, one from Paper 1 Section B, one society in change in Paper 2 Section A, and one thematic study in Paper 2 Section B.

**How long are the papers and how much are they worth?**
Each paper lasts 105 minutes, is marked out of 60 and is worth 50% of the qualification.

**What are pre-released sources?**
Some sections use sources released before the exam. Your teacher will tell you which of your options use them and when they become available.

**Why is the course listed as World History?**
OxfordAQA calls it International GCSE History; Marlbridge groups it under World History alongside equivalent courses from other boards.
