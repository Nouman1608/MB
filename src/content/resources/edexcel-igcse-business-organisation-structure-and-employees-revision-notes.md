## Key definitions

The specification names these terms but does not define them. The meanings here and in the later definitions are general working definitions written for these notes, not specification wording.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational chart | Diagram of posts, levels and reporting lines |
| Hierarchical structure | Many levels of authority; tall and narrow |
| Flat structure | Few levels; fewer managers between top and bottom |
| Centralised | Important decisions made at the top, often head office |
| Decentralised | Decisions passed down to branch or regional managers |
| Span of control | Number of subordinates reporting directly to one manager |
| Chain of command | Line of authority from top to bottom |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to a subordinate |
| Compliance | Following the law, safety rules and business policies |
| Accountability | Being answerable for results and for how a task was done |

## Tall versus flat

| | Hierarchical (tall) | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Levels | Many | Few |
| Span of control | Usually narrow | Usually wide |
| Chain of command | Long | Short |
| Communication | Slower; messages can change | Faster |
| Control | Close supervision | Looser; more trust |
| Promotion | Many steps | Few steps |
| Management cost | Higher | Lower |

Method for a chart question, in steps:

1. Count the levels from top to bottom.
2. For one manager, count only the people directly below.
3. For total employees, add every post, including the top post.
4. For chain of command, list each post from the person to the top.
5. Link your comment to the business in the case.

Worked reminder. A firm has 1 owner, 4 team leaders and 8 staff under each team leader. Total employees = 1 + 4 + (4 × 8) = 37. Levels = 3. Each team leader has a span of 8. The owner has a span of 4. An ordinary member of staff has two links above them: team leader and owner.

## Centralised versus decentralised

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | Top managers | Local managers too |
| Speed | Slower for local issues | Faster |
| Consistency | High | Lower |
| Local knowledge | Less used | More used |
| Motivation of local managers | May be lower | May be higher |
| Risk | Top managers overloaded | Poor local decisions |

Must-know distinction: tall or flat describes the number of levels. Centralised or decentralised describes where decisions are made. A business can be flat and centralised, or tall and decentralised. State which question you are answering.

## Compliance, accountability and delegation

- Compliance: the employee follows the rules; the manager checks that the team does too.
- Accountability: stays with the person who holds the post, even after delegation.
- Delegation passes authority and the job to do. It does not pass accountability.
- A good delegator gives clear instructions, suitable training and a way to check progress.
- Delegation helps because managers free time and staff gain skills. It harms if the task is done badly or the person is overloaded.

## Functional areas at a glance

| Area | Specification activities |
|---|---|
| Human resources | Workforce planning; recruitment and selection; training; health and safety; staff welfare; employment issues; industrial relations; disciplinary and grievance procedure; dismissal, unfair dismissal and redundancy |
| Finance | Wages and salaries; cash-flow forecasting; budgets; accounting |
| Marketing | Market research; product planning; pricing; sales promotion; advertising; customer service; public relations; packaging and distribution |
| Production | Manufacturing the product; designing new products; quality control; stock control |

### Human resources: short definitions

- Workforce planning: working out how many staff and which skills are needed, now and later.
- Disciplinary procedure: steps used when an employee breaks the rules.
- Grievance procedure: steps an employee uses to complain formally.
- Dismissal: ending a contract for conduct or performance reasons.
- Unfair dismissal: a dismissal where the reason or handling was not fair. The employee may be able to challenge it.
- Redundancy: the post itself disappears. It is not about the person's behaviour.
- Industrial relations: the relationship between management and employees or their representatives.
- Staff welfare: support for employees' wellbeing at work.

### Finance: working method

Gross wage = basic pay + overtime pay. Overtime at time and a half = hours × normal rate × 1.5. Worked reminder: 36 hours at 12.50 = 450.00; 4 hours of overtime at time and a half = 4 × 12.50 × 1.5 = 75.00; gross wage = 525.00. Cash-flow forecasts must include this as cash out.

### Links between areas

| Decision in one area | Effect on another |
|---|---|
| Marketing launches a promotion | Production must have stock; finance must fund it |
| Production adds quality control | Costs rise (finance); fewer returns (marketing) |
| Finance cuts the budget | Less training (HR); less advertising (marketing) |
| HR plans a recruitment drive | Wages bill rises (finance) |

## Command words in this topic

The specification's own wording for the command words is useful for planning an answer.

| Command word | What the specification says the answer needs | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| Define | Define a term from the content | Give a short, exact meaning of span of control, delegation and so on |
| Give | Recall from the content | A named benefit, limitation or activity |
| Identify | Select from a graph or table | Read a number or post from a chart |
| Calculate | Use the given data; workings should be given | Wages, totals of employees, spans |
| Outline | Two linked points, placed in context | Benefit, then what it leads to for the named business |
| Explain | A statement of fact with two expansion points, no context | Point, then two developing steps |
| Analyse | An extended answer in context, with expansion | Build a chain of reasons about one structure or area |
| Justify | Use the information given to recommend one of two options | Choose between two structures, using the figures |
| Evaluate | Extended answer reaching a supported conclusion | Weigh both sides and decide |

## Worked reminder: a chain of reasoning

Question style: analyse one effect of a long chain of command on a business with 90 employees and 5 levels.

1. Point: messages take longer to travel.
2. Development: an instruction from the director passes through four managers before it reaches an operative.
3. Effect: customers wait longer for a response, so some may go to competitors.
4. Link: for a business of 90 employees, 5 levels is a lot, so removing a level could help.

Each step answers "so what?" for the named business. Use the same pattern for centralised decision-making, narrow spans or any functional area.

## Worked reminder: judging two options

When you must justify or evaluate, write in this order.

1. Quote the figures that matter (employees, levels, salaries, days of delay).
2. Give one strength of each option.
3. Give one weakness of each option.
4. Choose, and say why that choice suits the business's aim better.
5. Add one condition, such as training for new team leaders, that makes the choice work.

## Quick self-test

1. Define span of control.
2. A manager has 3 supervisors, and each supervisor has 7 workers. How many employees are below the manager, and what is the manager's span of control?
3. State one benefit of a flat structure.
4. State one limitation of a hierarchical structure.
5. Give one benefit of decentralised decision-making.
6. A manager delegates a task and it goes wrong. Who is accountable to the director?
7. Which functional area deals with quality control?
8. Which functional area deals with a grievance procedure?
9. What is the difference between dismissal and redundancy?
10. An employee works 36 hours at 12.50 an hour and 4 hours of overtime at time and a half. Calculate gross pay.

### Answers

1. The number of subordinates who report directly to one manager.
2. 3 + (3 × 7) = 24 employees; span of control = 3.
3. Faster communication (or lower management costs, or more responsibility for staff).
4. Slow communication because messages pass through many levels (or higher management costs).
5. Quicker decisions made by managers who know local customers.
6. The manager who delegated.
7. Production.
8. Human resources.
9. Dismissal relates to the employee's conduct or performance. Redundancy happens because the job is no longer needed.
10. 36 × 12.50 = 450.00; 4 × 12.50 × 1.5 = 75.00; total = 525.00.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the total number of staff as the span of control.
- Writing "flat" when the question asks about centralised decision-making.
- Saying that delegation passes accountability down.
- Listing generic benefits such as "it is better" with no reason.
- Not using the figures in a case study, such as the number of employees or levels.
- Placing pricing in finance rather than marketing, or training in production.
- Giving a one-sided answer to a justify or evaluate question, with no judgement.
- Describing a structure but not explaining its effect on communication, cost or motivation.
- Mixing up redundancy and dismissal.
- Leaving out units or working in a wage calculation.
