This set covers Topic 20: Organic Synthesis, outcomes 20.1 to 20.5 with Core Practical 16, as set out in Issue 1 (September 2017) of Pearson Edexcel's International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry specification, YCH11. It is International A2 (IA2) content in Unit 5 (Transition Metals and Organic Nitrogen Chemistry). Start with the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-organic-synthesis/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-organic-synthesis-revision-notes/) if needed. The [course hub](/boards/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) and [checklist](/checklists/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) list every topic; the free [diagnostic quizzes](/diagnostics/) show weak areas.

Use these values: C 12.0, H 1.0, N 14.0, O 16.0. Proton NMR shifts: alkyl CH about δ 0.9–1.7; CH next to C=O about δ 2.0–2.6; CH on C–O about δ 3.3–4.2; aromatic H about δ 6.5–8.0.

## Questions

**1.** Ethylmagnesium bromide is made in the laboratory.

**(a)** State the reagent and solvent used to make it from bromoethane. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why all apparatus must be dry. **[1]**

**2.** Complete combustion of 0.348 g of compound Q, which contains only C, H and O, gives 0.792 g of CO₂ and 0.324 g of H₂O. M⁺ in its mass spectrum is at m/z = 116. Calculate the empirical and molecular formulae of Q. **[4]**

**3.** Methylmagnesium iodide reacts with each compound below in dry ether, followed by dilute acid. Name each organic product.

**(a)** carbon dioxide **[1]**
**(b)** methanal **[1]**
**(c)** butanal **[1]**
**(d)** propanone **[1]**

**4.** Amine R contains C 61.0%, H 15.3% and N 23.7% by mass. M⁺ is at m/z = 59.

**(a)** Show that the molecular formula is C₃H₉N. **[2]**
**(b)** The proton NMR spectrum of R has three peaks with relative areas 6 : 1 : 2. Identify R, giving a reason. **[2]**

**5.** Compound T, C₅H₁₀O, gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH. It does not change Fehling's solution and gives no precipitate with iodine and sodium hydroxide. Its IR spectrum has a strong band at 1715 cm⁻¹ and no broad O–H band. Its ¹³C NMR spectrum has three peaks. Its proton NMR spectrum has a quartet and a triplet, areas 2 : 3. Deduce the structure of T, explaining each step. **[6]**

**6.** 4-Hydroxypentan-2-one is CH₃COCH₂CH(OH)CH₃.

**(a)** Predict what you would see when it is treated with each reagent: (i) 2,4-DNPH; (ii) Tollens' reagent, warmed; (iii) acidified potassium dichromate(VI), warmed; (iv) iodine and sodium hydroxide, warmed; (v) sodium carbonate solution. **[5]**
**(b)** Predict, with a reason, whether it is soluble in water. **[1]**

**7.** Methyl 2-methylpropanoate is made by heating 2-methylpropanoic acid with methanol and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Explain why the mixture is heated under reflux. **[1]**
**(b)** Next, the student washes the crude ester with aqueous sodium carbonate. Give the purpose of this step and one precaution. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a suitable drying agent and the sign that drying is complete. **[1]**
**(d)** State how the pure ester is finally obtained. **[1]**

**8.** A student plans a Grignard reaction. Use the hazard data to suggest one control measure for each substance.

| Substance | Hazard |
|---|---|
| ethoxyethane | extremely flammable liquid |
| 1-bromobutane | harmful vapour |
| ethanoic anhydride | corrosive; reacts with water |
| magnesium turnings | flammable solid |

**[4]**

**9.** In Core Practical 16, 4.83 g of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (Mr 138.0) reacts with excess ethanoic anhydride. Aspirin has Mr 180.0.

**(a)** Write the equation, using molecular formulae. **[1]**
**(b)** The dry crude product weighs 5.11 g and the recrystallised product 3.78 g. Calculate both percentage yields. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the crude yield overstates how much aspirin was made. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe how the crude aspirin is recrystallised. **[2]**
**(e)** The recrystallised sample melts over the range 128–132 °C. Pure aspirin melts at 136 °C. State what this shows. **[1]**

**10.** Pentan-1-amine is to be made from 1-bromobutane.

**(a)** Give reagents and conditions for a four-step route that uses a Grignard reagent and methanal, naming each intermediate. **[4]**
**(b)** Give a two-step route with reagents and conditions. **[2]**
**(c)** The four steps have yields of 85%, 70%, 75% and 60%. The two steps have yields of 80% and 70%. Calculate both overall yields. **[2]**

**11.** Compound W contains C 70.6%, H 5.9% and O 23.5%. M⁺ is at m/z = 136. Its IR spectrum has a strong band at 1765 cm⁻¹ but no broad O–H band. Its proton NMR spectrum shows a peak of area 5 at δ 7.0–7.4 and a singlet of area 3 at δ 2.3. Its ¹³C NMR spectrum has six peaks.

**(a)** Find the molecular formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the structure of W. Explain why it is not methyl benzoate. **[5]**

**12.** A fragrant oil is isolated from crushed cloves. The oil is volatile, does not mix with water and breaks down near its own boiling temperature.

**(a)** Explain why steam distillation suits this oil. **[1]**
**(b)** The distillate is cloudy, with the oil spread through the water. Describe how to obtain a dry sample of the oil. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why three small portions of extracting solvent are better than one large portion. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Magnesium** (turnings) [1]; in **dry ether** (ethoxyethane) [1]
**(b)** Water reacts with the Grignard reagent, forming ethane and destroying it [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Ether" without "dry" is often not enough for the solvent mark.

**2.** n(C) = 0.792 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0180 mol; n(H) = 2 × 0.324 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0360 mol [1]; mass O = 0.348 − 0.216 − 0.036 = 0.096 g, so 0.00600 mol [1]; ratio 3 : 6 : 1, empirical formula **C₃H₆O** [1]; 116 ÷ 58.0 = 2, so **C₆H₁₂O₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the oxygen found by difference; a ratio with no oxygen step loses method credit.

**3. (a)** **Ethanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** **Ethanol** [1]
**(c)** **Pentan-2-ol** [1]
**(d)** **2-Methylpropan-2-ol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Count carbons: the Grignard adds one, the carbonyl compound supplies the rest.

**4. (a)** C 61.0 ÷ 12.0 = 5.08; H 15.3 ÷ 1.0 = 15.3; N 23.7 ÷ 14.0 = 1.69 [1]; ratio 3 : 9 : 1, C₃H₉N has Mr 59.0, matching M⁺ [1]
**(b)** **Propan-2-amine, (CH₃)₂CHNH₂** [1]: two equivalent CH₃ groups (6), one CH (1) and NH₂ (2) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link each area to a group; the other C₃H₉N amines give one or four peaks.

**5.** Orange precipitate and the 1715 cm⁻¹ band show C=O [1]; no Fehling's change, so a ketone [1]; no iodoform, so no CH₃CO– group [1]; three ¹³C peaks for five carbons means a symmetrical molecule [1]; quartet and triplet in a 2 : 3 ratio show equivalent CH₃CH₂– groups next to C=O [1]; T is **pentan-3-one, CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each piece of evidence earns its own mark only when you state what it shows.

**6. (a)** (i) orange precipitate [1]; (ii) no silver mirror, as it has no aldehyde group [1]; (iii) orange to green, as the secondary alcohol is oxidised [1]; (iv) pale yellow precipitate [1]; (v) no effervescence, as there is no COOH [1]
**(b)** Soluble: the OH and C=O groups form hydrogen bonds with water [1]
*Examiner insight:* A colour change needs both colours; "turns green" alone may not score.

**7. (a)** Allows long heating without losing volatile methanol or ester [1]
**(b)** Removes acid impurities (sulfuric acid and unreacted 2-methylpropanoic acid) [1]; invert and open the tap often to release CO₂ pressure [1]
**(c)** Anhydrous magnesium sulfate; the liquid turns from cloudy to clear [1]
**(d)** Distillation, collecting the fraction near the ester's boiling temperature [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name which impurity each wash removes; "to purify it" is too vague.

**8.** Ethoxyethane: no naked flames; heat with a water bath or electric heater [1]. 1-Bromobutane: use a fume cupboard [1]. Ethanoic anhydride: gloves and eye protection; keep it dry [1]. Magnesium: keep away from flames [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each control must answer that substance's own hazard; repeated "wear goggles" scores once at most.

**9. (a)** C₇H₆O₃ + C₄H₆O₃ → C₉H₈O₄ + C₂H₄O₂ [1]
**(b)** n = 4.83 ÷ 138.0 = 0.0350 mol; theoretical mass = 0.0350 × 180.0 = 6.30 g [1]; crude = 5.11 ÷ 6.30 × 100 = **81.1%** [1]; recrystallised = 3.78 ÷ 6.30 × 100 = **60.0%** [1]
**(c)** The crude solid also contains impurities such as unreacted 2-hydroxybenzoic acid [1]
**(d)** Dissolve in the minimum volume of hot solvent and filter hot [1]; cool to crystallise, collect by suction filtration, rinse with ice-cold solvent, then dry [1]
**(e)** The sample is still impure: it melts below 136 °C and over a range [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the theoretical mass for both yields; a yield based on the crude mass mixes two different quantities.

**10. (a)** Mg in dry ether gives butylmagnesium bromide [1]; add methanal in dry ether, then dilute acid, giving pentan-1-ol [1]; KBr with 50% H₂SO₄ (or PCl₅), giving 1-bromopentane (or 1-chloropentane) [1]; excess ammonia in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube, giving pentan-1-amine [1]
**(b)** KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux, giving pentanenitrile [1]; LiAlH₄ in dry ether [1]
**(c)** 0.85 × 0.70 × 0.75 × 0.60 = **26.8%** [1]; 0.80 × 0.70 = **56.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Conditions count: "ammonia" without excess, ethanol and sealed tube may lose the mark.

**11. (a)** C 5.88, H 5.9, O 1.47, giving 4 : 4 : 1, so C₄H₄O (68.0) [1]; 136 ÷ 68.0 = 2, **C₈H₈O₂** [1]
**(b)** 1765 cm⁻¹ with no O–H shows an ester C=O [1]; area 5 at δ 7.0–7.4 is a C₆H₅ ring [1]; a CH₃ singlet with no adjacent H, at δ 2.3, is next to C=O [1]; W is **phenyl ethanoate, CH₃COOC₆H₅** [1]; in methyl benzoate the CH₃ is on oxygen, so its singlet would be near δ 3.7–4.0 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the given shift ranges to place the CH₃ group.

**12. (a)** The oil distils with the steam below 100 °C, so it never reaches the temperature at which it decomposes [1]
**(b)** Shake the distillate with an immiscible organic solvent in a separating funnel and keep the organic layer [1]; dry it with anhydrous magnesium sulfate, filter, then evaporate or distil off the solvent [1]
**(c)** Each portion removes a fraction of the remaining oil, so repeated extractions remove more in total [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link steam distillation to the low distillation temperature; naming the technique alone does not explain the choice.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out oxygen by difference in combustion data.
- "Ether" without "dry" for Grignard reactions.
- Grignard products with the wrong number of carbons.
- Colour changes with only one colour given.
- Reagents with no conditions in route questions.
- Control measures that do not match the hazard given.
- A melting temperature described as "higher" when the sample is impure.
