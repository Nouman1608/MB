These questions cover section **4.2 Kinematics of motion in a straight line** of Mechanics (Paper 4) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. Take g = 10 m s⁻². Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [4.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-kinematics-of-motion-in-a-straight-line-cambridge/) and the [4.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-kinematics-of-motion-in-a-straight-line-cambridge-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A particle moves 5 m east and then 12 m west along a straight line, taking 4 s in total. Find the distance travelled, the final displacement, the average speed and the average velocity. **[3]**

**2.** A car travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ decelerates uniformly at 2.5 m s⁻² until it stops. Find the time taken and the distance travelled while decelerating. **[3]**

**3.** A particle moving with constant acceleration passes a point A at 3 m s⁻¹ and a point B, 60 m further on, at 9 m s⁻¹. Find the acceleration and the time taken from A to B. **[3]**

**4.** A particle moves in a straight line so that its displacement from O after t seconds is s = t³ − 9t² + 24t metres, for t ≥ 0. Find the times at which the particle is at rest. **[3]**

**5.** A particle starts from rest, accelerates uniformly to 8 m s⁻¹ in 4 s, and then decelerates uniformly to rest in a further 12 s. Find the total distance travelled and the deceleration. **[3]**

**6.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards from ground level at 20 m s⁻¹. Find the greatest height reached and the time taken to return to the ground. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A particle moves in a straight line from O. Its velocity t seconds later is v = 3t² − 12t + 9 m s⁻¹, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 4.

**(a)** Find the times at which the particle is at rest.

**(b)** Find the acceleration when t = 4.

**(c)** Find the displacement of the particle from O when t = 4.

**(d)** Find the total distance travelled from t = 0 to t = 4. **[8]**

**8.** A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates uniformly for 8 s to a speed of V m s⁻¹. She rides at V m s⁻¹ for 30 s, then decelerates uniformly at 1.5 m s⁻² until she stops. The total distance is 456 m.

**(a)** Sketch the velocity–time graph.

**(b)** Show that V² + 102V − 1368 = 0.

**(c)** Find V and the total time for the journey. **[8]**

**9.** Car A passes a point X at 10 m s⁻¹ and moves with constant acceleration 1 m s⁻² along a straight road. Four seconds later, car B passes X travelling at a constant 30 m s⁻¹ in the same direction. Let t seconds be the time after A passes X.

**(a)** Write down expressions for the displacements of A and B from X, for t ≥ 4.

**(b)** Find the time when B first draws level with A, and the distance from X at that moment.

**(c)** Explain the meaning of the other root of your equation. **[6]**

**10.** A particle P starts from rest at O and moves in a straight line. Its velocity t seconds later is v = 4t − t^(3/2) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the maximum velocity of P.

**(b)** Find the value of t when P is next at rest.

**(c)** Find the distance travelled by P between leaving O and next being at rest. **[9]**

**11.** Stone A is released from rest at the top of a tower 80 m high. One second later, stone B is thrown vertically downwards from the same point at 15 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the time taken by A to reach the ground.

**(b)** Find the time taken by B to reach the ground after it is thrown.

**(c)** State which stone reaches the ground first, and by how much time. **[5]**

**12.** The displacement of a particle from O at time t seconds is s = 12t − 3t² metres, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5.

**(a)** Sketch the displacement–time graph.

**(b)** Find the greatest positive displacement.

**(c)** Find the total distance travelled in the 5 seconds.

**(d)** Find the velocity when t = 5. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Distance **17 m**; displacement **7 m west** [1]. Average speed = 17/4 = **4.25 m s⁻¹** [1]. Average velocity = 7/4 = **1.75 m s⁻¹ west** [1]

**2.** 0 = 25 − 2.5t, so **t = 10 s** [1]. 0 = 25² − 2(2.5)s [1], so **s = 125 m** [1]

**3.** 9² = 3² + 2a(60) [1], so **a = 0.6 m s⁻²** [1]. t = (9 − 3)/0.6 = **10 s** [1]

**4.** v = 3t² − 18t + 24 [1] = 3(t − 2)(t − 4) [1]. **t = 2 s and t = 4 s** [1]

**5.** Area = ½ × 16 × 8 [1] = **64 m** [1]. Deceleration = 8/12 = **0.667 m s⁻²** [1]

**6.** Up positive: 0 = 20² − 2(10)s [1], so the greatest height is **20 m** [1]. s = 0: 20t − 5t² = 0, so **t = 4 s** [1]

**7.** **(a)** 3(t² − 4t + 3) = 3(t − 1)(t − 3) = 0 [1]. **t = 1 and t = 3** [1]

**(b)** a = 6t − 12 = **12 m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** s = t³ − 6t² + 9t [1]. s(4) = 64 − 96 + 36 = **4 m** [1]

**(d)** s(1) = 4 and s(3) = 0 [1]. Distances: 0 to 1: 4 m; 1 to 3: 4 m; 3 to 4: 4 m [1]. Total **12 m** [1]

*Examiner insight:* integrating from 0 to 4 in one step gives 4 m, the displacement; the particle reverses twice, so the distance is three times that.

**8.** **(a)** Straight line from (0, 0) to (8, V), horizontal to (38, V), straight line down to the t-axis [1]. Axes labelled, with V and the times marked [1]

**(b)** Deceleration lasts V/1.5 = 2V/3 s [1]. Area = ½(8)V + 30V + ½(2V/3)V = 456 [1]. 34V + V²/3 = 456, so V² + 102V − 1368 = 0 [1]

**(c)** (V − 12)(V + 114) = 0 [1]. V > 0, so **V = 12** [1]. Deceleration time 8 s, so the total time is 8 + 30 + 8 = **46 s** [1]

**9.** **(a)** A: s = 10t + ½t² [1]. B: s = 30(t − 4) [1]

**(b)** 10t + ½t² = 30t − 120, so t² − 40t + 240 = 0 [1]. t = 20 − 4√10 = **7.35 s** [1]. Distance = 30(7.351 − 4) = **101 m** [1]

**(c)** t = 20 + 4√10 = 32.6 s is when A, which keeps accelerating, draws level again and overtakes B [1]

**10.** **(a)** a = 4 − 1.5t^(½) [1]. a = 0 when √t = 8/3, so t = 64/9 [1]. v = 4(64/9) − (8/3)³ = 256/9 − 512/27 = 256/27 [1] = **9.48 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** t^(3/2) = 4t, so √t = 4 [1] and **t = 16** [1]

**(c)** s = 2t² − 0.4t^(5/2), with s = 0 at t = 0 [1]. s(16) = 512 − 0.4 × 1024 [1] = **102 m** (102.4 m); v ≥ 0 throughout, so this is also the distance [1]

**11.** **(a)** 80 = 5t², so **t = 4 s** [1]

**(b)** Down positive: 80 = 15τ + 5τ² [1], so τ² + 3τ − 16 = 0 [1]. τ = (−3 + √73)/2 = **2.77 s** [1]

**(c)** B lands 1 + 2.77 = 3.77 s after A is released, so **B lands first**, by 4 − 3.772 = **0.228 s** [1]

**12.** **(a)** Inverted parabola from (0, 0), maximum at (2, 12), crossing the t-axis at t = 4 [1], reaching (5, −15) [1]

**(b)** v = 12 − 6t = 0 at t = 2 [1]. Greatest displacement **12 m** [1]

**(c)** s(5) = 60 − 75 = −15 [1]. Distance = 12 + (12 + 15) = **39 m** [1]

**(d)** v = 12 − 30 = **−18 m s⁻¹** (moving at 18 m s⁻¹ in the negative direction) [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving displacement instead of distance when the particle reverses.
- Using the constant-acceleration formulae for a velocity that depends on t.
- Forgetting the starting condition when integrating.
- Mixing the time variables of two particles that start at different times.
- Sign errors in vertical motion; state the positive direction.
- Not interpreting the second root of a quadratic in t.
