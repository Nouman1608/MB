## Definitions

- **Empirical formula**: the simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
- **Molecular formula**: the actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule of a compound.
- **Relationship**: molecular formula = n × empirical formula, where n = Mr ÷ empirical formula mass.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Composition by mass | Mass of each element in a sample of the compound |
| Percentage by mass | Mass of each element in 100 g of the compound |
| Empirical formula mass | Sum of Ar values in one empirical formula unit |
| Mole ratio | Ratio of amounts of atoms of each element |
| Complete combustion | Burning in excess oxygen so that all C forms CO₂ and all H forms H₂O |
| By difference | Finding the mass of an element (often O) by subtracting the masses of the others from the total |

## The ratio method in five steps

1. Mass or percentage of each element.
2. Divide by Ar.
3. Divide by the smallest answer.
4. Multiply up if needed: 0.5 → ×2; 0.33 or 0.67 → ×3; 0.25 or 0.75 → ×4.
5. Write the formula; then use Mr to find the molecular formula.

Lay the working out as a table. Keep three significant figures until step 3.

## Quick examples

**Hydrocarbon.** 85.7% C, 14.3% H: C 7.14, H 14.3; ratio 1 : 2; empirical formula CH₂ (mass 14.0). If Mr = 56.0, n = 4, so C₄H₈.

**Acid.** 26.7% C, 2.2% H, 71.1% O: C 2.23, H 2.20, O 4.44; ratio 1 : 1 : 2; empirical formula CHO₂ (mass 45.0). If Mr = 90.0, n = 2, so C₂H₂O₄.

**Salt.** 32.4% Na, 22.6% S, 45.0% O: Na 1.41, S 0.704, O 2.81; ratio 2 : 1 : 4; Na₂SO₄.

## Combustion analysis

- n(C) = n(CO₂) = mass of CO₂ ÷ 44.0
- n(H) = 2 × n(H₂O) = 2 × mass of H₂O ÷ 18.0
- mass of O in the sample = total mass − mass of C − mass of H
- Then use the ratio method on n(C), n(H) and n(O).

Example: 0.440 g of a compound gives 0.880 g CO₂ and 0.360 g H₂O. n(C) = 0.0200 mol (0.240 g); n(H) = 0.0400 mol (0.0400 g); mass of O = 0.160 g, n(O) = 0.0100 mol. Ratio 2 : 4 : 1, empirical formula C₂H₄O.

## Hydrates from composition

Treat the salt and the water as two "components": divide the mass of anhydrous salt by its Mr and the mass of water by 18.0, then find the ratio. MgSO₄·xH₂O that is 51.2% water: MgSO₄ 48.8 ÷ 120.4 = 0.405; H₂O 51.2 ÷ 18.0 = 2.84; ratio 1 : 7, so x = 7.

## Sources of Mr

- Given in the question.
- Molecular ion peak in a mass spectrum.
- Gas data with pV = nRT.

## Exam traps

- Divide by Ar, not by atomic number or by the Mr of O₂.
- Do not round 1.5, 1.33 or 1.25; multiply through.
- Rounding early (for example 1.2545 to 1.3) can turn 2 : 3 into 1 : 1.
- In combustion, each H₂O carries two H atoms.
- Check for oxygen by difference whenever the compound is not a hydrocarbon.
- An ionic compound has only an empirical formula.
- If empirical formula mass equals Mr, the molecular formula equals the empirical formula.

## Self-test

1. Give the empirical formula of C₆H₁₂.
2. Give the empirical formula of C₂H₄O₂.
3. Two compounds both have the empirical formula CH. Name two possible molecular formulae.
4. Atom ratio after dividing by the smallest is 1 : 1.33. What do you multiply by?
5. A compound is 75.0% C and 25.0% H. Find its empirical formula.
6. A compound has empirical formula CH₂O and Mr 60.0. Give its molecular formula.
7. 2.40 g of carbon combines with 6.40 g of oxygen. Find the empirical formula.
8. 0.540 g of water forms when a hydrocarbon burns. How many moles of H atoms were in the sample?
9. A compound is 52.2% C, 13.0% H and 34.8% O. Find the empirical formula.
10. Why is the formula of sodium chloride described as an empirical formula?
11. An oxide of nitrogen is 30.4% N. Find its empirical formula and, given Mr = 92.0, its molecular formula.
12. A hydrated salt has the ratio CuSO₄ : H₂O = 0.0100 : 0.0500. Write its formula.

### Answers

1. CH₂
2. CH₂O
3. For example C₂H₂ and C₆H₆.
4. 3, giving 3 : 4.
5. C 6.25, H 25.0; ratio 1 : 4; CH₄.
6. Empirical mass 30.0; n = 2; C₂H₄O₂.
7. C 0.200 mol, O 0.400 mol; CO₂.
8. 0.540 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0300 mol H₂O, so 0.0600 mol H atoms.
9. C 4.35, H 13.0, O 2.18; ratio 2 : 6 : 1; C₂H₆O.
10. It is a giant ionic lattice with no molecules; the formula gives only the simplest ratio of ions.
11. N 2.17, O 4.35; ratio 1 : 2; NO₂ (mass 46.0); n = 2; N₂O₄.
12. CuSO₄·5H₂O
