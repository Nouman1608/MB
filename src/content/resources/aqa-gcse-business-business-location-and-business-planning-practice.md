## Questions

**1.** State **two** factors that a business might consider when choosing where to locate. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term *fixed costs* and give **one** example. **[2]**

**3.** Dovesmere Foods sells 600 jars of chutney a month at £4.50 each. The variable cost of making each jar is £1.80. Fixed costs are £1,350 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the revenue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total costs. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the profit. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** way in which a business plan helps a new business to raise finance. **[3]**

**5.** A new business is preparing a business plan.

**(a)** Identify **three** sections that a business plan might contain. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how setting objectives and detailing how functions will be organised in the plan could help the owner. **[4]**

**6.** Hobbsley Print makes posters. It sells 350 posters in a month at £9 each. The variable cost of each poster (paper and ink) is £3.20 and fixed costs are £2,400 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the profit or loss for the month. **[4]**
**(b)** The owner forecasts that sales will rise to 500 posters a month. Calculate the profit or loss at this level of sales. **[3]**

**7.** Analyse **one** benefit and **one** drawback of business planning for a business that is just starting up. **[6]**

**8.** Brambleton Pottery makes plates and bowls from clay. Most of its sales are online. It must choose between two units.

| Monthly figure | Unit A: rural, beside the clay supplier | Unit B: city edge |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | £1,500 | £2,700 |
| Clay transport charges | £600 | £150 |
| Skilled potters living within 10 miles | 2 | 25 |
| Other pottery studios within 10 miles | 0 | 3 |

Evaluate which unit Brambleton Pottery should choose. **[9]**

**9.** Tumbleford Tea Rooms is preparing a business plan for a bank. The owner forecasts an average spend of £8.50 per customer. The variable cost per customer is £3.10 and fixed costs are £5,200 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the profit or loss if the tea rooms serve 900 customers in a month. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit or loss if it serves 1,200 customers in a month. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate how useful these forecasts are to the bank in deciding whether to lend. **[5]**

**10.** Explain the difference between revenue and profit. **[3]**

**11.** Hobbsley Print's costs include paper, unit rent, ink and business insurance.

**(a)** Identify the **two** costs that are variable. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why total costs rise when more posters are printed, even though fixed costs stay the same. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: proximity to the market; availability of raw materials; labour; competition; costs [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "state" question a short answer is enough, so spend your time elsewhere. The five factors named in the specification are the safest choices.

**2.** Fixed costs are costs that **do not change as output or sales change** [1]. Example: **rent** (or insurance) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that only says "costs that stay the same" can be too vague. Add "as output changes". An example that is a variable cost, such as materials, cannot support the definition.

**3. (a)** Revenue = 600 × £4.50 = **£2,700** [1]
**(b)** Total variable costs = 600 × £1.80 = £1,080 [1]. Total costs = £1,080 + £1,350 = **£2,430** [1]
**(c)** Profit = £2,700 − £2,430 = **£270** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage. If an early figure is wrong, clear working lets the examiner see what you did and where the slip occurred. Include the £ sign.

**4.** A business plan includes **forecasts of sales, costs and cash flow** [1]. A lender can use these to judge **whether the business will earn enough to repay a loan with interest** [1], so the plan reduces the risk the lender sees and makes it more willing to lend [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A three-mark "explain" usually needs a point, a development and a link back to the question. A bare statement such as "banks like plans" stays at one point.

**5. (a)** Any three of: executive summary; business idea and objectives; target market and market research; marketing plan; operations; people; financial forecasts [1] [1] [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Objectives give the owner **clear targets**, such as a level of sales in the first year [1], so actual results can be compared with them and action taken if the business falls short [1]. Detailing how functions will be organised shows **who is responsible for operations, marketing, finance and people** [1], so tasks are not missed or duplicated, which helps the business run smoothly from the start [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question names two items, so address both. An answer that covers only objectives leaves half of the question unanswered.

**6. (a)** Revenue = 350 × £9 = £3,150 [1]. Total variable costs = 350 × £3.20 = £1,120 [1]. Total costs = £1,120 + £2,400 = £3,520 [1]. Profit = £3,150 − £3,520 = −£370, so a **loss of £370** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Revenue = 500 × £9 = £4,500 [1]. Total costs = 500 × £3.20 + £2,400 = £1,600 + £2,400 = £4,000 [1]. Profit = £4,500 − £4,000 = **£500** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say whether the result is a profit or a loss. Writing "−£370" alone is clear, but naming it a loss shows you understand the term. Fixed costs stay at £2,400 in part (b); using a higher figure would be a common slip.

**7.** **Benefit:** The plan makes the owner research the market and work out costs before spending money [1]. This can show that an idea would make a loss, so the owner can change it or stop before losing savings [1], which matters for a start-up with little money to waste [1]. **Drawback:** The financial figures are forecasts with no trading history behind them [1]. If sales turn out lower than predicted, the owner or a lender may rely on figures that are too optimistic [1], which could lead to cash running short in the early months [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" asks for a chain of reasoning, not a list. State the point, then what follows from it, then why it matters to a new business. One benefit and one drawback are asked for, so choose your best of each rather than listing several.

**8.** **Costs:** Unit A costs £1,500 + £600 = £2,100. Unit B costs £2,700 + £150 = £2,850 [1]. Unit A is £750 a month cheaper [1]. **Raw materials:** Unit A's closeness to the clay supplier cuts transport charges by £450 a month, a useful saving because clay is heavy [1]. **Labour:** Unit B has 25 skilled potters nearby against 2 at Unit A, so recruiting would be easier and quicker at B, whereas A might have to pay more or train staff [1]. **Market:** Most sales are online, so being near customers matters little, which weakens the case for the city edge [1]. **Competition:** Unit B has three other studios nearby, which could compete for the same potters and local customers, though it might also attract buyers to the area [1]. **Judgement:** Unit A is probably better because the lower costs and heavy raw material suit the business and customers are online [1], as long as the owner can recruit enough skilled staff [1]. The balance would shift to B if the business found it could not hire potters, because production would then be limited [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In an "evaluate" question, a judgement that follows from the data is expected, not just a list of factors. Using figures from the table, such as the £750 difference, helps to show you have applied the case.

**9. (a)** Revenue = 900 × £8.50 = £7,650 [1]. Total variable costs = 900 × £3.10 = £2,790 [1]. Total costs = £2,790 + £5,200 = £7,990 [1]. Profit = £7,650 − £7,990 = −£340, a **loss of £340** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Revenue = 1,200 × £8.50 = £10,200 [1]. Total costs = 1,200 × £3.10 + £5,200 = £3,720 + £5,200 = £8,920 [1]. Profit = £10,200 − £8,920 = **£1,280** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** **For:** The forecasts show the owner has worked out costs and revenue, so the bank can see the business needs roughly 960 customers a month before it stops making a loss [1]. They show profit of £1,280 at 1,200 customers, which suggests the business can generate money to repay a loan [1]. **Against:** The figures are forecasts for a new business with no trading record, so the number of customers is uncertain [1]. A change in the number of customers from 900 to 1,200 moves the result from a loss to a profit, so a small shortfall in sales would matter a great deal [1]. **Judgement:** The forecasts are useful as a starting point, but the bank should also look at the market research behind the customer numbers before lending [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use your own results from (a) and (b) in (c). Staying consistent with your earlier numbers makes the argument credible. A one-sided answer is weaker on an evaluate question.

**10.** **Revenue** is the income from selling goods or services, found by price × quantity sold [1]. **Profit** is what is left after total costs have been taken away from revenue [1]. A business can have high revenue but still make a loss if its costs are higher, for example revenue of £5,000 and total costs of £5,400 gives a loss [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both terms defined and the link between them. Describing only one term leaves the comparison incomplete.

**11. (a)** **Paper** [1] and **ink** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Each extra poster needs more paper and ink, so **total variable costs rise with output** [1]. Rent and insurance do not change with output, so they stay the same, and total costs equal fixed costs plus variable costs, which therefore rise [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* If the question asks for two costs, give exactly two. Listing all four, including the fixed ones, suggests you are unsure which is which.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a location factor without saying how it affects sales or costs.
- Picking the cheapest option in a location question and ignoring labour, market or competition.
- Not using the figures in the table when a question gives data.
- Giving only benefits of planning, or only drawbacks, when the command word is analyse or evaluate.
- Forgetting to multiply the variable cost per unit by the quantity before adding fixed costs.
- Treating fixed costs as if they change with the quantity sold.
- Giving a loss as a profit, or leaving out the £ sign.
- Ending an evaluation with a summary of the points and no judgement.
- Describing the plan's purpose only as raising finance and leaving out setting objectives, starting up and organising functions.
