This guide goes deeper into content item 1.2.4, The competitive environment, in Topic 1.2 Spotting a business opportunity of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022, part of Theme 1 (Paper 1, 1BS0/01). The broader [Spotting a business opportunity study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-spotting-a-business-opportunity-and-customer-needs/) lists the five factors and the main responses to competition; this page shows how to gather evidence on each factor, build and read a competitor grid, use market share figures, and argue how competition should change a decision. ## What 1.2.4 asks you to know

The heading is "Understanding the competitive environment".

| Strand | Specification wording |
|---|---|
| Judging competitors | strengths and weaknesses of competitors based on: price, quality, location, product range and customer service |
| Effect of competition | the impact of competition on business decision making |

Appendix 2 of the specification lists market data, including market share, changes in costs and changes in prices, among the data you may have to interpret.

## Part 1: the five factors and where the evidence comes from

A strength or weakness only means something when compared with other businesses and with what customers want. For each factor, know what to look for and how an entrepreneur could find out.

| Factor | A strength looks like | A weakness looks like | Evidence (links to 1.2.2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | Lower than rivals, or high but accepted because of quality | Higher than rivals with nothing extra to justify it | Price lists, websites, visiting as a customer |
| Quality | Good reviews, durable products, skilled staff | Complaints, returns, poor reviews | Online reviews, buying and testing the product |
| Location | Easy for target customers to reach; good online presence | Out of the way; poor parking; no online sales | Observation, maps, footfall counts |
| Product range | Choice that suits target customers | Narrow range, or so wide it is poorly stocked | Visiting, websites, catalogues |
| Customer service | Helpful staff, quick responses, easy returns, after-sales care | Slow replies, rude staff, no follow-up | Reviews, mystery shopping, social media comments |

A competitor can be strong on one factor and weak on another. Low prices often come with a narrower range or less service; high quality often comes with a higher price.

## Part 2: building a competitor grid

A grid scores each competitor on each factor, so that strengths and weaknesses can be compared at a glance. The scores are judgements, so they should be based on evidence and treated with care.

### Worked example: Clearway Driving School

Owen plans to start Clearway Driving School in a town with three rivals. He scores each from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) on each factor. For price, 5 means the cheapest.

| Factor | RoadReady | Mirror Signal | Ace Drive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| Quality (reviews of teaching) | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| Location (pick-up area covered) | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| Product range | 2 | 4 | 1 |
| Customer service | 3 | 4 | 2 |
| **Total** | **16** | **18** | **13** |

Further facts: only Mirror Signal offers lessons in an automatic car, and it is the most expensive. Ace Drive is cheapest but has many reviews complaining about cancelled lessons.

**Reading the grid.**
- Mirror Signal has the highest total and is strong on quality and range, but weak on price.
- Ace Drive wins on price but is weak on quality, range and service.
- RoadReady is in the middle on most factors.
- The totals treat every factor as equally important. Customers may not: a learner on a tight budget may care far more about price than range.

**Weakness to exploit.** Demand for automatic lessons can only be met by the most expensive rival. Owen could offer automatic lessons at a price close to RoadReady's, with reliable lesson times to contrast with Ace Drive's cancellations.

### Using market share

Market share (%) = the business's sales ÷ total market sales × 100. This is the standard form; the specification does not list it in Appendix 3.

The town's learners book about 1,200 lessons a week: RoadReady 480, Mirror Signal 300 and Ace Drive 420.
- RoadReady = 480 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 40%
- Mirror Signal = 300 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 25%
- Ace Drive = 420 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 35%

Ace Drive has the lowest grid total but the second-largest share, which suggests many learners choose on price. If Owen won 10% of a market that stayed at 1,200 lessons, he would give 120 lessons a week. Those lessons would mostly be taken from the rivals, so he should expect them to respond.

## Part 3: the impact of competition on decision making

Competition affects nearly every decision a small business makes. The table links each decision to the five factors.

| Decision | How competition shapes it |
|---|---|
| Whether to enter the market at all | Strong, well-liked rivals with large shares make entry riskier |
| Price | Set to match, undercut or sit above rivals, depending on what else is offered |
| Quality | Raised to match or beat rivals' reviews, which can raise costs |
| Location | Away from rivals to serve an unserved area, or near them where customers come to compare |
| Product range | Filling a gap in rivals' ranges, or specialising to avoid head-on competition |
| Customer service | Improved where rivals are weak, giving customers a reason to switch |
| Promotion | Stressing what the business does better than rivals |

Two further effects are worth noting:
- **Costs.** Competing on quality or service usually costs money, and competing on price cuts the money earned on each sale. Either way, competition can squeeze profit.
- **Response from rivals.** A new business's choices can trigger a reaction, such as a price cut by an established rival. A good decision anticipates this.

## Annotated model answer

**Question (Analyse):** Analyse the impact of competition on Clearway Driving School's pricing decision. **[6]**

> Ace Drive is the cheapest school and has 35% of the market, so many learners in the town appear to choose on price (evidence). If Clearway priced well above Ace Drive, it could struggle to win these price-sensitive learners (consequence). However, Ace Drive is weak on reliability, with complaints about cancelled lessons (evidence), so Clearway does not need to be the cheapest if it offers dependable lesson times (development). Mirror Signal is the only school offering automatic lessons and charges the highest price (evidence), so Clearway could price automatic lessons below Mirror Signal and still charge more than Ace Drive (development). Competition therefore pushes Clearway towards a mid-range price, backed by reliability and range, rather than a price war it might not win (conclusion).

## Common mistakes

- Listing the five factors with no comparison between rivals.
- Treating the highest grid total as the business customers prefer; market share may tell a different story.
- Assuming the best response to competition is always to cut prices.
- Ignoring that rivals may react.
- Forgetting that improving quality or service raises costs.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Calculate:** market shares and price differences, showing workings.
- **Identify:** a strength or weakness from a table.
- **Explain:** one way competition affects a decision, developed to sales or profit.
- **Analyse:** use the rivals' figures to argue how the business should respond.
- **Justify / Evaluate:** choose between responses, such as cutting price or improving service, and weigh the likely reaction of rivals.
