Globalisation of trade is the buying and selling of goods and services across national borders, and the
supply chains that link producers and consumers in different countries. In Cambridge O Level Commerce
(7100, 2026-2028) Topic 3 has two parts: 3.1 International trade and 3.2 Global supply chain management.
Expect calculations of the Balance of Trade and Balance of Payments, and questions that ask you to weigh
the benefits and risks of trading internationally.

For the full explanation see the [Globalisation of trade study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-commerce-globalisation-of-trade/);
then test yourself with the [Globalisation of trade practice questions](/resources/o-level-commerce-globalisation-of-trade-practice/).

## Why do countries trade internationally? (3.1.1)

Five benefits to learn: **wider choice** of goods; **higher standard of living**; **lower cost of imports**
where other countries produce more cheaply; **more investment**; **creation of jobs** in exporting
industries.

## What makes international trade difficult? (3.1.2)

Use the memory chain **"Distance, Language, Payment, Paperwork"** and then the money and law risks:

| Challenge | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Distance | Longer delivery times and higher transport costs |
| Language | Misunderstandings in orders and contracts |
| Methods of payment | Need for secure international payment, e.g. documentary credit |
| Documentation | Customs forms, certificates and shipping documents |
| Transport | Choosing and arranging suitable international transport |
| Customs duties | Add to the price of imports |
| Non-payment | Hard to recover debts from foreign buyers |
| Currencies and exchange rates | Price and profit change when rates move |
| Competition | Rival firms from many countries |
| Different legal systems | Contracts and product rules vary |

## How do you calculate the Balance of Trade and Balance of Payments? (3.1.3)

- **Balance of Trade** = value of exports of goods - value of imports of goods (visible trade only).
- **Balance of Payments** = a record of **all** a country's financial transactions with other countries:
  goods, services (invisible trade such as tourism, banking and insurance), income and investment flows.
- A **surplus** means more money flowing in than out; a **deficit** means more flowing out.

**Worked example** ($ million)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Exports of goods | 420 |
| Imports of goods | 510 |
| Exports of services | 260 |
| Imports of services | 140 |
| Net investment inflow | 30 |

- Balance of Trade = 420 - 510 = **-90 (deficit)**.
- Services balance = 260 - 140 = +120.
- Balance of Payments (on these items) = -90 + 120 + 30 = **+60 (surplus)**.

Interpretation: the country imports more goods than it exports, but strong service exports and investment
inflows more than make up for it.

**How commercial activity affects the balances**: a manufacturer winning export orders improves the Balance
of Trade; a country's airlines or banks earning from foreign customers improve the Balance of Payments;
retailers importing more goods worsen the Balance of Trade.

## What are free trade, trading blocs and trade restrictions? (3.1.4)

- **Free trade**: trade between countries without restrictions such as tariffs or quotas.
- **Trading bloc**: a group of countries that agree to reduce or remove trade barriers between themselves,
  often with a common external tariff and common rules.

| Joining a trading bloc - advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Tariff-free access to a larger market | More competition from member countries |
| Cheaper imports of materials | Must follow common rules |
| More investment from foreign firms | Trade with non-members may face barriers |
| Easier movement of goods | Loss of some independence over trade policy |

**Trade restrictions**
- **Tariff**: a tax on imports, raising their price.
- **Quota**: a limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported.
- **Embargo**: a ban on trade with a country or in a product.
- Effects on commerce: higher costs for importers; less choice for consumers; protection for domestic
  producers; possible retaliation against exporters.

**Support for exporters**: international trade fairs; cheaper bank loans; insurance guarantees against
non-payment.

## How are global supply chains managed? (3.2)

- **Simple supply chain**: supplier → manufacturer → consumer.
- **Complex supply chain**: supplier → agent → wholesaler → retailer → consumer.
- **Three parts of supply chain management**: **upstream** (suppliers of materials), **the enterprise** itself,
  and **downstream** (distribution to customers).
- **Suppliers matter** because their quality, price and reliability decide whether the enterprise can
  produce on time.
- **Benefits**: lower costs of production, labour and purchasing; advances in logistics and communication.
- **Risks**: environmental (pollution, deforestation), natural disasters, financial, ethical, technological
  and political.
- **Risk assessment** identifies these threats in advance so the business can plan alternatives, such as a
  second supplier or extra stock.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Including services in the Balance of Trade - it covers goods only.
- Getting the sign wrong: imports greater than exports gives a negative balance (deficit).
- Confusing a quota (quantity limit) with a tariff (tax).
- Describing a complex supply chain without naming the agent, wholesaler and retailer.
- Listing risks without explaining their effect on the business.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the Balance of Trade and the Balance of Payments?** The Balance of Trade
compares exports and imports of goods only. The Balance of Payments records all transactions with other
countries, including services, income and investment.

**Is a trade deficit always bad?** Not necessarily. A goods deficit can be offset by service exports or
investment, and imports may include machinery that helps future growth.

**What support does 7100 list for exporters?** International trade fairs, cheaper bank loans and insurance
guarantees.
