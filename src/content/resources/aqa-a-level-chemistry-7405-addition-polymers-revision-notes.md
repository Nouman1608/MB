These notes condense section **3.3.4.3 Addition polymers** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Addition polymers study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-polymers/), and exam-style questions are in the [Addition polymers practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-polymers-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [Addition reactions of alkenes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-reactions-of-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Draw the **repeating unit** from a monomer, and from a section of polymer chain.
- Draw the **monomer** from a section of polymer.
- Use **IUPAC rules** to name addition polymers.
- Explain why addition polymers are **unreactive**.
- Explain the **intermolecular forces** between polyalkene molecules.
- State uses of **PVC** and how a **plasticiser** modifies it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monomer | Small molecule that joins with others to form a polymer |
| Polymer | Very large molecule built from many repeating units |
| Repeating unit | Smallest section that repeats along the chain |
| Addition polymerisation | Monomers with C=C join with no other product |
| Plasticiser | Small molecules added between chains to make the polymer flexible |
| London forces | Weak attractions from instantaneous and induced dipoles |

## Addition polymerisation

```
n CH₂=CHX → –[CH₂–CHX]ₙ–
```

- Only monomers with a **C=C bond** are used.
- The C=C opens and becomes a **single bond** in the chain.
- The backbone is **two carbons per monomer**.
- **Nothing else** forms, so atom economy is 100 %.

## Monomers, repeating units and names

| Monomer | Repeating unit | Polymer |
|---|---|---|
| Ethene | –[CH₂–CH₂]ₙ– | Poly(ethene) |
| Propene | –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]ₙ– | Poly(propene) |
| Chloroethene | –[CH₂–CHCl]ₙ– | Poly(chloroethene), PVC |
| Phenylethene | –[CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)]ₙ– | Poly(phenylethene) |
| Tetrafluoroethene | –[CF₂–CF₂]ₙ– | Poly(tetrafluoroethene) |
| But-2-ene | –[CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)]ₙ– | Poly(but-2-ene) |

Name: **poly(** IUPAC name of the monomer **)**.

## Method: monomer to repeating unit

1. Draw the monomer with the C=C at the centre.
2. Turn C=C into C–C.
3. Draw bonds extending from each carbon.
4. Put brackets round the two carbons and their side groups; add n.

## Method: chain section to repeating unit

1. Find the shortest pattern that repeats.
2. For a polymer from one alkene, the pattern contains **two backbone carbons**.
3. Draw it in brackets with extended bonds.

## Method: chain section to monomer

1. Take one repeating unit (two backbone carbons).
2. Remove the extended bonds.
3. Replace with a **C=C** between the backbone carbons.
4. Keep side groups where they were.

## Why polyalkenes are unreactive

- The backbone has **strong C–C and C–H bonds**.
- These bonds are **non-polar**, so nucleophiles and electrophiles are not attracted.
- No C=C bond, no lone pairs, so no reactive site.
- Result: unaffected by acids, alkalis and water, and not broken down by microbes.

## Intermolecular forces

| Polymer | Forces between chains | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Poly(ethene), poly(propene) | London forces only | Flexible, lower melting point |
| PVC | London forces plus permanent dipole–dipole (polar C–Cl) | Stiffer, rigid |

Longer chains and closer packing give stronger overall forces.

## PVC and plasticisers

| | Unplasticised PVC | Plasticised PVC |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Chains close, strong forces | Small molecules between chains, weaker forces |
| Properties | Hard, rigid | Flexible, soft |
| Uses | Window frames, pipes, guttering | Cable insulation, flooring, flexible tubing |

## Development over time

- Polymers were discovered and developed through research, sometimes by accident.
- Poly(ethene) was first made at high pressure in the 1930s.
- Later catalysts allowed lower pressures and control of chain structure.
- Knowledge of structure and properties has improved production and uses.

## Calculations

- n = relative molecular mass of polymer ÷ Mᵣ of repeating unit.
- Mass of polymer from monomer = mass of monomer (100 % atom economy).

Example: poly(propene), Mr of unit 42.0. A chain of Mr 84 000 has n = 84 000 ÷ 42.0 = 2000.

## Worked example to copy

**Question.** Draw the repeating unit and name the polymer formed from CH₂=CH–CH₂CH₃.

1. The backbone is the two carbons of the C=C.
2. The side group on the second carbon is –CH₂CH₃.
3. Repeating unit: –[CH₂–CH(CH₂CH₃)]ₙ–, with bonds extending through both brackets.
4. The monomer is but-1-ene, so the polymer is poly(but-1-ene).

Check: no double bond remains, and the unit has two backbone carbons.

## Quick check on drawing

Before leaving a polymer answer, run through four checks. First, is there any C=C left? There should not be. Second, are there exactly two backbone carbons for each alkene monomer? Third, do bonds extend through the brackets at both ends? Fourth, is the subscript n outside the bracket? Four ticks mean the drawing is complete.

## Exam traps

- Double bond left in the repeating unit.
- All carbons of but-2-ene put into the backbone.
- Missing extended bonds or the n.
- Naming the polymer from its repeating unit rather than from the monomer.
- "Polythene" given when poly(ethene) is asked for.
- Unreactivity explained as "stable" without referring to strong non-polar bonds.
- PVC forces given as London forces only.
- Plasticiser described as strengthening the polymer.

## Self-test

1. What is a monomer?
2. What type of monomer forms addition polymers?
3. Draw the repeating unit of poly(propene).
4. Name the monomer of PVC.
5. What is the IUPAC name of the polymer made from tetrafluoroethene?
6. Why is the atom economy of addition polymerisation 100 %?
7. Give the monomer for –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.
8. Explain why polyalkenes are unreactive.
9. What forces exist between poly(ethene) chains?
10. Why is there an extra type of force in PVC?
11. How does a plasticiser change PVC?
12. How many repeating units are in poly(chloroethene) of Mᵣ 31 250? (Mᵣ of C₂H₃Cl = 62.5)

### Answers

1. A small molecule that can join with many others to form a polymer.
2. Alkenes or substituted alkenes with a C=C bond.
3. –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]ₙ– with extended bonds.
4. Chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl.
5. Poly(tetrafluoroethene).
6. All atoms of the monomer are in the polymer; there is no other product.
7. Propene, CH₂=CHCH₃.
8. Strong, non-polar C–C and C–H bonds, saturated, so there is no site for attack.
9. London forces.
10. The C–Cl bond is polar, giving permanent dipole–dipole attractions.
11. Small molecules push chains apart, weakening forces, so the polymer becomes flexible.
12. 31 250 ÷ 62.5 = 500.
