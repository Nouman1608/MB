This set covers the section 3.4 statement on the advantages and disadvantages of sole traders, partnerships and limited companies, with the related distinction between share capital and loan capital. Revise first with the [business organisations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-business-organisations-compared/) and the [business organisations revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-business-organisations-compared-revision-notes/). All people, businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define limited liability. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** sources of long-term finance available to a limited company that are not available to a sole trader. **[2]**

**3.** Which of the following is **not** an advantage of operating as a sole trader?

A: The owner keeps all of the profit

B: Decisions can be made quickly

C: The owner's personal assets are protected from business debts

D: The business is easy to set up

**[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a sole trader of having unlimited liability. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the type of business organisation whose statement of financial position shows:

**(a)** capital accounts and current accounts **[1]**

**(b)** ordinary share capital and retained earnings **[1]**

**(c)** a single capital account, increased by profit and reduced by drawings. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why debenture interest is an expense in a company's income statement but dividends are not. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Marlow Pumps Ltd has 120,000 ordinary shares of 1 each and 7% debentures of 40,000. Retained earnings at the start of the year were 35,000. Profit for the year, after debenture interest, was 22,000, and dividends of 9,000 were paid.

**(a)** Calculate the annual debenture interest. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate retained earnings at the end of the year. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate total equity at the end of the year. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the debentures are not included in equity. **[2]**

**8.** Shilpa runs a catering business as a sole trader. Ravi, an experienced chef, has offered to join her as a partner and invest 15,000.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages to Shilpa of forming the partnership. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages to Shilpa of forming the partnership. **[4]**

**9.** The directors of Hollins Glass Ltd need 200,000 for a new furnace. They can either issue 200,000 ordinary shares of 1 each or issue 10% debentures of 200,000. Profit from operations is expected to be 60,000 a year after the furnace is installed. The three founders currently own all the shares.

**(a)** Calculate the annual interest if debentures are issued. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the expected profit after interest if debentures are issued. **[1]**

**(c)** Assess which source of finance the directors should choose. **[6]**

**10.** Kemi Adeyemi is a sole trader. Her capital at the start of the year was 25,000. Her profit for the year was 14,200 and her drawings were 16,000.

**(a)** Calculate her capital at the end of the year. **[2]**

**(b)** Comment on the result. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a bank may ask for security before lending to a small limited company. **[2]**

**12.** Two sisters plan to open a restaurant. Each will invest 20,000, and they will need a bank loan of 30,000 to fit out the premises. Assess whether they should run the restaurant as a partnership or a limited company. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Shareholders' liability for the company's debts is restricted [1] to the amount they have paid, or agreed to pay, for their shares, so their personal assets are protected [1].

**2.** Issuing (selling) shares [1]; issuing loan capital such as debentures [1].

**3.** C [1]. A sole trader has unlimited liability.

**4.** If the business cannot pay its debts, creditors can claim the owner's personal assets, such as a house or savings [1], so the owner could lose far more than the amount invested in the business, which may discourage expansion [1].

**5. (a)** Partnership [1].

**(b)** Limited company [1].

**(c)** Sole trader [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Interest is a cost of borrowing that must be paid under the loan agreement whether or not there is a profit, so it is charged in arriving at profit [1]. Dividends are a share of profit paid to the owners at the directors' discretion, so they are a distribution of profit, deducted from retained earnings, not an expense [1].

**7. (a)** 7% × 40,000 = **2,800** [1].

**(b)** 35,000 + 22,000 − 9,000 [1] = **48,000** [1].

**(c)** 120,000 + 48,000 = **168,000** [1].

**(d)** Debenture holders are lenders, not owners [1]; the debentures must be repaid, so they are a liability, shown under non-current liabilities [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Any two advantages, each stated and developed: Ravi's 15,000 adds capital, so Shilpa can buy equipment or take larger contracts without borrowing; Ravi's skills as a chef widen the menu and improve quality; the workload is shared, so Shilpa can spend time on sales; losses are shared.

First advantage [1] developed [1]; second advantage [1] developed [1].

**(b)** Any two disadvantages, each stated and developed: profits must be shared with Ravi, so her share may fall unless the business grows; she has unlimited liability for debts Ravi creates through his decisions; disagreements may slow decisions she now makes alone.

First disadvantage [1] developed [1]; second disadvantage [1] developed [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** 10% × 200,000 = **20,000** [1].

**(b)** 60,000 − 20,000 = **40,000** [1].

**(c)** Debentures: the founders keep full control because no new shareholders vote [1]; the 20,000 interest is a fixed charge that must be paid even if the furnace does not raise profit as expected, increasing risk [1]; the 200,000 must be repaid on a set date [1]. Shares: no compulsory interest or repayment, so less risk if profits fall [1]; but new shareholders would hold 200,000 shares and could outvote the founders if the founders hold fewer, and future dividends are shared [1]. Judgement: if profit is reliably above the 20,000 interest, as the forecast 60,000 suggests, debentures keep control with the founders and are the better choice; if the forecast is uncertain, shares are safer [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** 25,000 + 14,200 − 16,000 [1] = **23,200** [1].

**(b)** Capital has fallen by 1,800 [1] because drawings are greater than profit; if this continues, the owner is withdrawing more than the business earns and its resources will shrink [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The shareholders have limited liability, so the bank cannot claim their personal assets if the company fails [1]. Security, such as a charge over the company's property or a personal guarantee from a director, reduces the bank's risk of not being repaid [1].

**12.** Partnership: easy and cheap to set up, and the sisters keep all decisions between themselves [1]; but liability is unlimited, so if the restaurant fails with the 30,000 loan unpaid, their personal assets are at risk [1]. Limited company: limited liability protects their personal assets, which matters because restaurants carry significant risk and they are borrowing 30,000 [1]; but there are more legal rules and costs [1], and the bank may still ask for personal guarantees, which reduces the benefit [1]. Judgement supported by the case, for example: a limited company is better because of the borrowing and the risk, provided the extra costs are affordable [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing general advantages without using the names and figures in the question.
- Describing dividends as an expense or debentures as equity.
- Forgetting that limited liability protects owners, not the business itself.
- Giving one-sided answers to "assess" questions with no judgement.
