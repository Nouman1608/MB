## Types of agriculture

- **Arable** crops; **pastoral** animals; **mixed** both.
- **Subsistence** for the family; **commercial** for sale.
- **Intensive**: high inputs per area for high yields.
- **Monoculture**: one crop over a large area.
- One farm can be several types at once.

## Definition

**Sustainable food production**: the production of sufficient food for the present generation using
methods that ensure future generations can grow food from the same land.

## Twelve strategies

| Strategy | How yield rises |
|---|---|
| Mixed cropping, intercropping, crop rotation | Nutrients used evenly; pest and disease cycles broken; legumes add nitrogen |
| Trickle/drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting, automated watering | Water reaches roots with little waste; water stored for dry periods |
| Mechanisation | Larger areas farmed faster with less labour |
| GMOs | Genes changed for pest, disease or drought resistance |
| Greenhouses, hydroponics, aeroponics | Conditions controlled; crops grown without soil |
| Managed grazing: livestock rotation | Pasture recovers; no overgrazing |
| Urban farming | Food grown close to consumers on unused city space |
| Agroforestry | Trees shelter crops, hold soil, add organic matter and products |
| Inorganic NPK fertilisers | Replace nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium quickly |
| Organic fertilisers: crop residue, manure, mulch | Add nutrients slowly and organic matter for structure |
| Chemical pest control: insecticide, herbicide, fungicide | Kill insects, weeds, fungi |
| Biological control of pests | Natural predator or parasite reduces pests without chemicals |

**Hydroponics** = roots in nutrient solution. **Aeroponics** = roots in air with nutrient mist.

## Nine impacts as chains

| Practice | Process | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Overproduction | Surplus not sold or eaten | Food wastage; inputs wasted |
| Cash crops and biofuel crops | Replace food crops on the land | Food shortages, higher prices |
| Mismanaged irrigation | Water table rises; water evaporates leaving salts; run-off | Water logging; salinisation; soil erosion |
| Overuse of pesticides | Survivors breed; predators killed; non-target insects killed | Pesticide resistance; pest resurgence; harm to pollinators |
| Overuse of fertilisers | Leaching into water | Nutrient enrichment; eutrophication |
| No nutrient replacement | Organic content and ions used up | Exhausted soil; lower yields |
| Removal of natural vegetation | Deforestation, overcultivation, overgrazing | Bare soil, habitat loss |
| Monoculture and intensification | One crop; heavy chemical use | Soil and water pollution; loss of biodiversity |
| Soil erosion | Wind and water remove bare soil | Loss of topsoil and nutrients |

**Eutrophication sequence:** fertiliser leaches into water → nutrients increase → algae grow rapidly →
algae block light and die → bacteria decompose them, using up oxygen → fish and other animals die.

**Salinisation sequence:** irrigation in a hot climate → water evaporates from the surface → salts left
behind → salts build up → crops cannot take up water → yields fall.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Subsistence farming | Producing food mainly for the farmer's family |
| Commercial farming | Producing food for sale |
| Intensive farming | High inputs per area for high yields |
| Monoculture | Growing one crop over a large area |
| Intercropping | Growing two or more crops in a pattern in the same field |
| Crop rotation | Changing the crop grown in a field each season |
| GMO | Organism whose genes have been removed, changed or inserted to give favourable characteristics |
| Hydroponics | Growing plants in nutrient solution without soil |
| Agroforestry | Growing trees with crops or livestock |
| Salinisation | Build-up of salts in soil |
| Pest resurgence | Rebound of pest numbers after their predators are killed |
| Eutrophication | Nutrient enrichment of water leading to algal growth and oxygen loss |

## Exam traps

- Pesticide **resistance** (pests survive spraying) ≠ pest **resurgence** (pests rebound after predators
  die).
- Fish die in eutrophication from **lack of oxygen**, not poisoning.
- Salinisation comes from **evaporation of irrigation water**, not the sea.
- Each strategy needs **how** it increases yield.
- Food shortages are caused by **cash crops and biofuel crops replacing food crops**: name the examples if
  asked (coffee, cotton, soya beans, palm oil).
- Crop rotation is a yield strategy here, not a soil erosion strategy.

## Self-test

1. What is the difference between subsistence and commercial farming?
2. Define sustainable food production.
3. Name **two** controlled environments in the syllabus.
4. How does crop rotation help yields?
5. Why does drip irrigation waste less water than flooding a field?
6. What is meant by a GMO?
7. Name the **three** types of pesticide in the syllabus and what each controls.
8. Explain how overuse of fertilisers can kill fish in a lake.
9. Explain pest resurgence.
10. How can growing palm oil cause food shortages?
11. Describe how salinisation happens.
12. Give **two** benefits of agroforestry.

**Answers:** 1. Subsistence produces food mainly for the farmer's family; commercial produces food for
sale. 2. The production of sufficient food for the present generation using methods that ensure future
generations can grow food from the same land. 3. Any two: greenhouses, hydroponics, aeroponics.
4. Different crops use different nutrients and break pest and disease cycles. 5. Water goes directly to
the roots, so less evaporates or runs off. 6. An organism whose genetic material has been changed by
removing, changing or inserting individual genes to give favourable characteristics. 7. Insecticide
(insects), herbicide (weeds), fungicide (fungi). 8. Fertiliser leaches into the lake, algae grow rapidly
and die, bacteria decomposing them use up oxygen, and fish die. 9. Pesticides kill the pests' natural
predators, so pest numbers rebound even higher. 10. Land used for palm oil is no longer used to grow food
for local people, so less food is available and prices rise. 11. In a hot climate irrigation water
evaporates, leaving salts that build up in the soil. 12. Any two: shelters crops from wind; roots hold
soil; leaves add organic matter; provides fruit, nuts or timber.
