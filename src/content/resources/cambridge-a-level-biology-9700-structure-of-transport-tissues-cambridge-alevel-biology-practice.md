These questions cover sub-topic **7.1 Structure of transport tissues** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AS Level, Paper 1 and Paper 2 style, with Paper 3 drawing skills). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All measurements are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [structure of transport tissues study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-structure-of-transport-tissues-cambridge-alevel-biology/) and the [structure of transport tissues revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-structure-of-transport-tissues-cambridge-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which describes the arrangement of xylem and phloem in a transverse section of a herbaceous dicotyledonous stem?

A: a central star of xylem with phloem between its arms

B: a ring of vascular bundles, each with phloem towards the centre

C: a ring of vascular bundles, each with xylem towards the centre

D: vascular bundles scattered throughout the stem **[1]**

**2.** Which feature is found in a companion cell but **not** in a mature sieve tube element?

A: cell surface membrane

B: cellulose cell wall

C: nucleus

D: plasmodesmata **[1]**

**3.** State **two** features of a correctly drawn plan diagram. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which a mature xylem vessel element differs from a sieve tube element. **[2]**

**5.** A transverse section of a leaf has been mounted upside down on a slide. Explain how you could tell which surface of the leaf is the upper surface by looking at the midrib. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student views a TS of a young root through a microscope fitted with an eyepiece graticule. The root is 80 eyepiece units (epu) across and its central vascular core is 24 epu across.

**(a)** The student draws the root 120 mm across. Calculate the diameter of the vascular core on the drawing. Show your working.

**(b)** Describe the distribution of xylem and phloem that the student should draw inside the vascular core.

**(c)** State **two** errors that would lose marks in this plan diagram, other than incorrect proportions. **[6]**

**7.** An electron micrograph of phloem has a scale bar labelled 10 µm that measures 25 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the micrograph.

**(b)** A companion cell in the micrograph is 30 mm wide. Calculate its actual width in µm.

**(c)** Name **two** organelles visible in the companion cell in this micrograph that could not be seen clearly with a light microscope, and give the function of each in the companion cell. **[7]**

**8.** Relate the structure of a xylem vessel to its function of transporting water. **[5]**

**9.** Explain why each of the following features of a sieve tube element helps the transport of assimilates.

**(a)** It has very little cytoplasm and no nucleus.

**(b)** Its end walls are sieve plates.

**(c)** It has a cell surface membrane. **[4]**

**10.** A longitudinal section of xylem shows lignin laid down in some vessels as separate rings and in others as a continuous wall broken only by small unthickened areas.

**(a)** Name the unthickened areas.

**(b)** Suggest **one** advantage of a continuous lignified wall over separate rings.

**(c)** Explain why water can still leave a vessel with a continuous lignified wall. **[3]**

**11.** Compare the distribution of vascular tissue in a dicotyledonous stem and root, and suggest how each distribution suits the forces acting on that organ. **[5]**

**12.** A scientist treats a plant with a chemical that stops companion cells making ATP. Suggest and explain the effect on the sieve tube elements next to those companion cells. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Any two from: no individual cells drawn; tissues in correct proportions; no shading; clear, continuous single lines; ruled label lines ending on the tissue [2]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Any two from: xylem is dead, sieve tube element is living; xylem walls are lignified, sieve tube element walls are cellulose only; xylem has no end walls, sieve tube elements have sieve plates; xylem has pits, sieve tube elements do not; xylem has no cytoplasm, sieve tube elements have a thin layer of cytoplasm [2]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** In the midrib, xylem lies towards the upper surface and phloem towards the lower surface [1]; so the surface nearer the xylem is the upper surface (the palisade layer also lies next to it) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6. (a)** Scale = 120 ÷ 80 = 1.5 mm per epu [1]; core = 24 × 1.5 = **36 mm** [1].

**(b)** Xylem in the centre as a star or cross shape [1]; phloem in separate groups between the arms of the xylem [1].

**(c)** Any two from: drawing individual cells; shading; broken or sketchy lines; label lines crossing or not touching the tissue [2]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* the proportion of the core (24 ÷ 80 = 0.3 of the diameter) must survive the scaling; a core drawn at 60 mm would lose the mark.

**7. (a)** 25 mm = 25 000 µm [1]; 25 000 ÷ 10 = **× 2500** [1].

**(b)** 30 mm = 30 000 µm; 30 000 ÷ 2500 = **12 µm** [1].

**(c)** Mitochondria [1], which make ATP for the proton pumps that load sucrose [1]. Ribosomes [1], which make proteins needed by both the companion cell and the sieve tube element, which has none of its own [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**8.** Any five from: dead cells with no cytoplasm, so water flows with little resistance; no end walls, so a continuous column of water forms; walls thickened with lignin, so vessels do not collapse under tension; lignin waterproofs the wall, so water stays in the vessel; pits allow water to move sideways into other vessels or cells; pits let water bypass a vessel blocked by an air bubble; narrow lumen, so adhesion to the walls helps support the column [5]. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* every mark needs a feature and its effect; "has lignin" alone scores nothing.

**9. (a)** The lumen is almost clear, so there is little resistance to the flow of sap [1].

**(b)** Sap can pass through the pores from one element to the next [1].

**(c)** It is partially permeable, so sucrose is kept inside [1] and water entering by osmosis can build up a high hydrostatic pressure for mass flow [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**10. (a)** Pits [1].

**(b)** Gives more strength or support, so the vessel resists collapse better [1].

**(c)** Water moves out through the pits, where there is no lignin [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**11.** Stem: vascular tissue in separate bundles arranged in a ring near the outside [1]. Root: a single central vascular core [1]. In the stem, lignified xylem near the outside resists bending (by wind) [1]. In the root, a central core resists pulling forces as the shoot moves [1]. Both have xylem and phloem in separate regions (or both have xylem inside phloem in the stem bundle and phloem between the xylem arms in the root) [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**12.** Proton pumps in the companion cell need ATP, so sucrose cannot be loaded [1]; less sucrose enters the sieve tube element through plasmodesmata [1]; the sieve tube element has no nucleus and few organelles, so it depends on the companion cell, and less mass flow occurs (or the sieve tube element may die) [1]. Total: 3 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the stem and root patterns.
- Putting phloem above xylem in a leaf vein.
- Mixing mm and µm in magnification.
- Giving features without functions in "relate" answers.
- Saying water passes through lignin rather than through pits.
