## Questions

**1.** Country E (illustrative) imports 90 000 tonnes of ceramic tiles a year at a world price of 40 per tonne. It adds a 15% tariff, and imports fall to 66 000 tonnes.

**(a)** Calculate the price per tonne after the tariff. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the tariff revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in imports. **[2]**
**(d)** Divide your answer to (c) by the percentage price rise and comment on the result. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a tariff and an import quota. **[3]**

**3.** In Country A (illustrative), home demand is Qd = 300 − 2P and home supply is Qs = 2P − 60. The world price is 60. A tariff of 10 per unit is introduced.

**(a)** State imports before and after the tariff. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate tariff revenue. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in consumer surplus. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the net welfare loss. **[2]**

**4.** Use the market in question 3. The government replaces the tariff with an import quota of 80 units.

**(a)** Show that the home price becomes 70. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the value of the quota licences. **[1]**
**(c)** Licences go to foreign exporters. Calculate the net loss to Country A and compare it with the tariff. **[3]**
**(d)** Give one reason a government might still choose a quota. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a government might impose an embargo on a product, and one likely effect on the home price. **[4]**

**6.** A firm in Country G (illustrative) has a unit cost of 90. Rivals sell at 84. The government pays an export subsidy of 12 per unit.

**(a)** Find the lowest price at which the firm can export without a loss. **[1]**
**(b)** It exports 150 000 units. Calculate the cost to the government. **[1]**
**(c)** The sales support 360 jobs. Calculate the cost per job. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one drawback of the subsidy. **[3]**

**7.** A consignment of fresh fruit from Country H (illustrative) is worth 25 000. Paperwork costs 750. The consignment waits 3 days and loses 2% of its value a day.

**(a)** Express the paperwork cost as a percentage of value. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total extra cost as a percentage of value. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare it with a 5% tariff. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why a government might choose red tape over a tariff. **[2]**

**8.** A new firm in Country J (illustrative) has a unit cost of 130. The world price is 100. Its cost is expected to fall by 8% a year.

**(a)** Calculate the tariff rate needed to match the world price. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the first year in which its cost is below 100. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain two risks of this protection. **[2]**

**9.** A firm in Country K (illustrative) sells 400 000 units abroad at 55. Its unit cost is 60 and its home price is 70.

**(a)** Calculate its total loss on these sales. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the duty that would raise the price to the home price, as a percentage of 55. **[2]**
**(c)** State one reason a firm may sell at a loss. **[1]**

**10.** Country N (illustrative) has a steel industry. A 20% tariff would protect 500 jobs and raise steel prices by 12%. Steel-using firms spend 900 million a year on steel. Evaluate whether the tariff should be introduced. **[10]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 15% of 40 = 6, so the price is **46** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Tariff per tonne = 6 [1]. Revenue = 6 × 66 000 = **396 000** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Fall = 24 000 [1]. 24 000 ÷ 90 000 = **26.7%** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** 26.7 ÷ 15 = **−1.78** [1]. Imports respond more than proportionally to the price rise, so the tariff cuts imports strongly [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Revenue uses the imports left after the tariff. Answers gain credit for showing the per-unit figure before multiplying.

**2.** A tariff is a tax on imports [1]. A quota is a limit on the quantity imported [1]. A tariff works through price and raises revenue, while a quota fixes quantity and its price effect follows from that [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both items compared. Define each, then state the difference.

**3. (a)** Before: Qd = 300 − 120 = 180, Qs = 120 − 60 = 60, imports **120** [1]. After: price 70, Qd = 160 [1], Qs = 80, imports **80** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** 10 × 80 = **800** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** ½ × (180 + 160) × 10 = **1 700** [2] **[2]**
**(d)** Producer gain = ½ × (60 + 80) × 10 = 700 [1]. Net loss = 1 700 − 700 − 800 = **200** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The net loss is two triangles. Checking that 100 + 100 matches the answer shows the working is consistent.

**4. (a)** Qd − Qs = 80 gives 300 − 2P − (2P − 60) = 80 [1]. 360 − 4P = 80, so P = **70** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (70 − 60) × 80 = **800** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Consumer loss 1 700 less producer gain 700 = **1 000** [1]. No revenue reaches Country A, so the loss is 1 000 [1], compared with 200 under the tariff [1] **[3]**
**(d)** Imports are fixed at 80 whatever happens to demand [1], so the government controls the quantity precisely [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The tariff and the quota give the same price here. The difference is who receives the 800, and answers gain credit for saying so.

**5.** Possible reasons are political, security, health or ethical [1]. For example, a ban on a country's goods in response to its conduct [1]. With imports stopped, supply falls [1], so the home price rises towards the no-trade level [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason and an effect both need developing. Naming a reason without explaining it earns little.

**6. (a)** 90 − 12 = **78** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** 12 × 150 000 = **1 800 000** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** 1 800 000 ÷ 360 = **5 000** [1] **[1]**
**(d)** Taxpayers pay 1.8 million for the benefit [1], which may keep a high-cost firm going [1], and foreign rivals may retaliate against Country G's exports [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that link the drawback to the figure just calculated read as more convincing than a general statement.

**7. (a)** 750 ÷ 25 000 = **3%** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Spoilage = 3 × 2% = 6% [1]. Total = 3% + 6% = **9%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** 9% is 4 percentage points higher than 5% [1] **[1]**
**(d)** Red tape is harder to see than a tariff [1], and can be presented as safety or process rules [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage points and percentages are different. Using the right term avoids a lost mark.

**8. (a)** (130 − 100) ÷ 100 = **30%** [2] **[2]**
**(b)** Cost = 130 × 0.92 per year [1]. Year 3: 101.23, still above 100 [1]. Year 4: 93.13, so **year 4** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** The protection may never be removed, because the industry lobbies to keep it [1]. The cost may not fall as forecast [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Checking the year before and the year of the crossing shows the answer is not a guess.

**9. (a)** Loss per unit = 60 − 55 = 5 [1]. Total = 5 × 400 000 = **2 000 000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Duty = 70 − 55 = 15 [1]. 15 ÷ 55 = **27.3%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** To win market share and drive rivals out [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* State what the percentage is of. Here the base is 55, not 70.

**10.** Evaluation points, each worth one:
- Steel firms and 500 workers gain protection [1].
- Steel prices rise 12% [1].
- Steel-using firms pay 900 × 12% = 108 million more [1].
- Cost per job = 108 ÷ 500 = 0.216 million, or 216 000 [1].
- This is a high cost per job unless wages are very high [1].
- Higher costs may reduce output in steel-using industries [1].
- Retaliation may hit exporters [1].
- Strategic arguments may justify protection [1].
- A temporary tariff is better than a permanent one [1].
- Judgement: the tariff is hard to justify on cost grounds alone, unless steel is strategic and the tariff has an end date [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that reach a supported judgement and compare cost with benefit read as evaluation. A list of points does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using pre-tariff imports for revenue.
- Giving the consumer loss as the net loss.
- Forgetting that licence value goes to someone.
- Confusing percentage points with percentages.
- Calling red tape a tax.
- Listing arguments with no judgement.
- Missing the effect on consumers or foreign exporters.
- Giving a percentage with no base.
