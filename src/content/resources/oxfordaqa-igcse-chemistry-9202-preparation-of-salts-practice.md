This set practises section 3.5.2 Preparation of salts (statements a to c) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A checks the reasons behind each step; Section B asks you to plan, evaluate and calculate. The course is untiered.

## Section A

**1.** Which salt would be made by precipitation?

A: potassium sulfate

B: barium sulfate

C: zinc sulfate

D: ammonium sulfate

**[1]**

**2.** Give one metal that is too reactive to be added to dilute acid to make a salt, and one that is not reactive enough. **[2]**

**3.** In the insoluble-base method, state how you know when enough base has been added. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a salt solution is not heated until all the water has gone. **[1]**

**5.** Name the two solutions you would mix to make silver chloride. **[2]**

**6.** When making sodium chloride from sodium hydroxide solution and hydrochloric acid, explain why the reaction is carried out a second time without indicator. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student plans to make magnesium sulfate crystals by adding magnesium ribbon to dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe what the student would see as the magnesium is added in excess. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the steps after the reaction has finished to obtain dry crystals. **[4]**

**8.** Suggest a method to make each salt. For each, name the two reactants and the route.

**(a)** copper(II) chloride **[2]**

**(b)** lithium nitrate **[2]**

**(c)** ammonium chloride **[2]**

**9.** Describe how to make a pure, dry sample of copper(II) carbonate, which is insoluble. Include a balanced equation. **[5]**

**10.** A student wrote this method to make crystals of zinc nitrate:

*"Add a little zinc oxide to dilute nitric acid. Stir until it all dissolves. Pour the solution into an evaporating basin and heat until only a dry white powder remains."*

**(a)** Identify **two** errors in the method and explain how each should be corrected. **[4]**

**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**11.** 2.40 g of magnesium oxide reacts completely with excess hydrochloric acid.

MgO + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O (Aᵣ: Mg = 24, O = 16, Cl = 35.5)

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of magnesium oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of anhydrous magnesium chloride that could be obtained. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest one reason why the mass of dry crystals obtained is less than this. **[1]**

**12.** A factory releases effluent containing dissolved copper(II) ions and phosphate ions. Another water supply contains a high concentration of calcium ions.

**(a)** Explain how precipitation could remove the copper(II) ions from the effluent. Name the precipitate. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a substance that could be added to remove the calcium ions from the water supply, and name the precipitate formed. **[2]**

**(c)** State how the precipitates are separated from the water. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1].

**2.** Too reactive: sodium, potassium or lithium [1]. Not reactive enough: copper, silver or gold [1].

**3.** Some solid stays undissolved even after stirring [1].

**4.** The salt may decompose or lose its water of crystallisation [1].

**5.** Silver nitrate solution [1] and sodium chloride solution (or another soluble chloride) [1].

**6.** So that the salt solution is not contaminated with indicator [1].

**7. (a)** Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂ [1].

**(b)** Fizzing / bubbles of gas [1]; fizzing stops and some magnesium is left over [1].

**(c)** Filter to remove excess magnesium [1]; heat the filtrate to evaporate some water until it is saturated [1]; leave to cool so crystals form [1]; filter off the crystals and dry between filter papers [1].

**8. (a)** Copper(II) oxide (or hydroxide or carbonate) with hydrochloric acid [1], adding the solid in excess and filtering [1].

**(b)** Lithium hydroxide solution and nitric acid [1], using an indicator (titration) method then repeating without indicator [1].

**(c)** Ammonia solution and hydrochloric acid [1], using the indicator method [1].

*Examiner insight:* For (a), "copper and hydrochloric acid" scores zero; copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series.

**9.**

- Mix copper(II) sulfate solution (or copper(II) nitrate solution) [1] with sodium carbonate solution [1].
- Filter to collect the precipitate [1].
- Wash it with distilled water and dry it in a warm oven [1].
- CuSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → CuCO₃ + Na₂SO₄ [1].

**10. (a)** Any two, 2 marks each:

- Only "a little" zinc oxide is added and it all dissolves [1], so acid remains; add zinc oxide **in excess** until some is left, then filter off the excess [1].
- The solution is heated to dryness [1]; instead heat until saturated, then leave to cool and crystallise [1].

**(b)** ZnO + 2HNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + H₂O: formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**11. (a)** Mᵣ MgO = 24 + 16 = 40 [1]; 2.40 ÷ 40 = **0.0600 mol** [1].

**(b)** 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.0600 mol MgCl₂; Mᵣ = 24 + 71 = 95 [1]; mass = 0.0600 × 95 = **5.70 g** [1].

**(c)** Some salt remains dissolved in the solution after crystallising / some is lost during filtering or transfer [1].

**12. (a)** Add an alkali (such as calcium hydroxide or sodium hydroxide) to supply hydroxide ions [1]; copper(II) hydroxide precipitates [1].

**(b)** Sodium carbonate (or another soluble carbonate) [1]; calcium carbonate [1].

**(c)** Allow the solid to settle and filter (or decant) the water [1].

*Examiner insight:* In water treatment the precipitate is the unwanted product; say what is removed and how.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the metal route for copper or a Group 1 metal.
- Omitting "excess" or "filter" from a soluble-salt plan.
- Writing "evaporate all the water" in place of crystallising.
- Naming ions or elements instead of two named solutions.
- Forgetting to wash a precipitate.
- Using the wrong Mᵣ for magnesium chloride: two chlorine atoms, 2 × 35.5 = 71.
