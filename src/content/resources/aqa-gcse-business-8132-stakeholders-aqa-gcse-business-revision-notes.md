## Core recall

- A stakeholder is any individual or group affected by, or able to affect, a business.
- The specification's main stakeholders: owners, employees, customers, local community, suppliers.
- Named objectives: maximising pay for workers, minimising environmental impact on the local community, high dividend payments for owners.
- A shareholder is one type of stakeholder: an owner of shares in a company.
- Internal stakeholders (owners, employees) are part of the business; external ones (customers, suppliers, community) are outside it.
- Business decisions have impacts on stakeholders, and stakeholders influence businesses and their objectives.
- Influence depends on power and on how much the group cares; organised groups have more influence.
- Conflicts arise because one decision helps some groups and harms others.
- Responses: prioritise, compromise, consult, reduce the harm.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder | Individual or group with an interest in a business |
| Shareholder | Owner of shares in a limited company |
| Internal stakeholder | A stakeholder inside the business: owners, employees |
| External stakeholder | A stakeholder outside the business: customers, suppliers, local community |
| Impact | The effect of a business decision on a stakeholder |
| Influence | The ability of a stakeholder to change a business's decisions or objectives |
| Stakeholder conflict | When a decision benefits one group at the expense of another |
| Trade union | Organisation that represents employees in dealings with employers |
| Industrial action | Action such as a strike taken by employees to put pressure on an employer |
| Boycott | Customers refusing to buy from a business |
| Planning objection | A formal complaint to the local council about a proposed development |

## Objectives and influence by group

| Stakeholder | Main objectives | How they influence the business |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | High dividends, growth, profit | Vote, appoint directors, set objectives |
| Employees | Maximising pay, job security, good conditions | Productivity, trade unions, industrial action, leaving |
| Customers | Quality, fair prices, good service | Buying or not, switching, reviews, boycotts |
| Suppliers | Regular orders, fair prices, prompt payment | Prices, quality, delivery, credit terms |
| Local community | Minimising environmental impact, local jobs | Planning objections, campaigns, local media |

## Common conflicts

| Conflict | Who gains | Who loses |
|---|---|---|
| Pay rise | Employees | Owners (lower profit and dividends) |
| Price rise | Owners | Customers |
| Paying suppliers later | The business's cash flow | Suppliers |
| Factory expansion or longer hours | Owners, employees, suppliers | Local community (noise, traffic) |
| Switching to cheaper overseas supplier | Owners, customers (lower prices) | Local suppliers, possibly employees |

## Method for a stakeholder question

1. Name the groups affected by the decision.
2. For each, give a gain or a loss, applied to the case.
3. Say which effect is strongest and why.
4. Link back: how might that group respond, and what does it mean for the business?
5. For evaluate: decide whose interests should come first and how to reduce harm to the others.

## Exam traps

- Writing "shareholders" when the question is about stakeholders.
- Listing groups with no impact or no reason.
- Covering only one direction (business on stakeholders, or stakeholders on business).
- "They will be unhappy" with no consequence for the business.
- Assuming every group can be fully satisfied.
- Naming people from the case instead of stakeholder groups.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the term stakeholder.
2. Distinguish a stakeholder from a shareholder.
3. Name the five main stakeholders in the specification.
4. Classify each as internal or external: (a) employees; (b) suppliers; (c) owners; (d) the local community.
5. State the main objective the specification gives for workers, for the local community and for owners.
6. Give two ways employees can influence a business.
7. Why does a customer with many alternative shops have more influence?
8. A business sells 8,000 units at £10 with a variable cost of £4. It raises the price to £11 and sales fall to 7,200. Calculate the change in revenue minus variable costs.
9. Which stakeholder gains and which loses from the change in question 8?
10. Twelve staff get a 40p an hour rise, each working 35 hours a week for 52 weeks. Calculate the extra yearly cost.
11. Give one stakeholder conflict caused by opening a factory at night.
12. State two ways a business can handle stakeholder conflict.

### Answers

1. Any individual or group with an interest in a business because it affects them or they can affect it.
2. A shareholder owns shares in a company; a stakeholder is anyone with an interest, so shareholders are one type of stakeholder.
3. Owners, employees, customers, local community, suppliers.
4. (a) Internal; (b) external; (c) internal; (d) external.
5. Workers: maximising pay. Local community: minimising environmental impact. Owners: high dividend payments.
6. Any two: effort and productivity; joining a trade union; industrial action; leaving the business; suggesting ideas.
7. They can easily switch to a rival, so the business must keep them satisfied to keep their custom.
8. Before 8,000 × £6 = £48,000; after 7,200 × £7 = £50,400; a rise of **£2,400**.
9. Owners gain higher profit; customers lose by paying more, and 800 stop buying.
10. 12 × £0.40 × 35 × 52 = **£8,736**.
11. Owners and employees gain from more output and work, while the local community loses sleep through noise and lorry traffic.
12. Any two: prioritise the most important group; compromise; consult; reduce the harm.
