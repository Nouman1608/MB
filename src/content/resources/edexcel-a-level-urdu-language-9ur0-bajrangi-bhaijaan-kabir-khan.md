This guide covers **5.6** in the prescribed literary texts and films of Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0): the
film **بجرنگی بھائی جان** (*Bajrangi Bhaijaan*), listed in Appendix 1 of the specification with the name
**کبیر خان** (Kabir Khan) and the year **2015**. It is examined in Section C of Paper 2, the film essay. The
[Bajrangi Bhaijaan revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-bajrangi-bhaijaan-revision-notes/)
already give the outline, themes, techniques and vocabulary in brief. This guide goes further: how to turn your
viewing into evidence, which Urdu terms and structures earn the language marks, and how to build an essay that
argues rather than retells. Practise afterwards with the
[Bajrangi Bhaijaan practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-bajrangi-bhaijaan-kabir-khan-practice/).
For the whole of Paper 2, read the [Translation into Urdu and Written Response to Works study guide](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-works/).

## What the specification asks of a film essay

The specification's assessment information for Paper 2 sets these conditions:

- There is a **choice of two questions** for each film; you answer one.
- **All questions are set in Urdu and must be answered in Urdu.**
- As a guide, Pearson advises **approximately 320–370 words** per essay, although the whole essay is marked
  regardless of length.
- You must **justify points of view, arguments and conclusions with evidence from the work**, selecting relevant
  material, developing arguments, drawing conclusions and evaluating different aspects of the film.
- **Dictionaries are not permitted**, and you may not take any documentation about the works into the exam.

Each essay is marked on three grids, applied separately to each of your two essays:

| Grid | Assessment objective | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Critical and analytical response | AO4 | 20 |
| Range of grammatical structures and vocabulary | AO3 | 15 |
| Accuracy of language | AO3 | 10 |

Two points in these grids shape everything below. The AO4 grid rewards interpretations "consistently justified
with appropriately selected evidence from the work"; evidence that is merely descriptive keeps an answer in the
lower bands. The range grid explicitly rewards **terminology appropriate for literary and cinematic analysis**
and **complex language**.

## Turning viewing into evidence: a scene bank

You cannot take notes into the exam, so your evidence must be remembered. A scene bank keeps it organised. For each
key sequence, record five things: where it falls in the journey, who is involved, a one-line summary, the film
technique you noticed, and the theme it supports. Write the theme and technique columns in Urdu from the start.

| Sequence | One-line summary | Technique to analyse | Theme (Urdu) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation | Shahida, who cannot speak, is separated from her mother in India on the way home | Pace and music build anxiety; the child's silence makes her helpless | بچھڑنا، بے بسی |
| The cricket match | Her reaction to a match reveals she is Pakistani, shocking the household | Reaction shots of the family; comedy turning to tension | قومی شناخت، تعصب |
| The border | Pawan refuses to slip into Pakistan without permission | Humour from his honesty, set against real danger | ایمان داری، اصول |
| The shrine | A qawwali at a Sufi shrine | Music, crowd and close-ups carry shared devotion | مذہبی رواداری، روحانیت |
| The video | Chand Nawab's footage of Pawan's mission spreads online | The camera inside the story becomes the voice of the people | میڈیا کی طاقت |
| The ending | Crowds gather on both sides of the border; Shahida finds her voice | Climax built on crowds, music and silence breaking | انسانیت، امید |

Check every row against your own viewing and add the precise details you remember. Two or three sequences, known
well, give stronger evidence than a summary of the whole plot.

## The five angles, applied to this film

Pearson's guidance asks you to prepare each work for **form and technique, key themes and issues,
characterisation, plot structure, and social and cultural setting**. For this film:

- **Form and technique.** A feature film tells its story through images, sound and editing as well as dialogue.
  Ask what the camera shows us first in a scene, what the music tells us to feel, and how landscape and crowds are
  framed.
- **Themes and issues.** Humanity beyond borders, religious tolerance, honesty, the power of the media, and the
  barriers created by borders and visas.
- **Characterisation.** Pawan develops from a naive, rule-bound man into someone whose principles challenge two
  states; Chand Nawab moves from a reporter chasing a story to a witness who speaks for ordinary people; Shahida's
  silence makes her the centre of everyone else's choices.
- **Plot structure.** A road journey, in which each stage brings a new obstacle and a new helper, ending at the
  border that has shaped every obstacle along the way.
- **Social and cultural setting.** Ordinary life in India and Pakistan, places of worship, and the suspicion between
  states, contrasted with the kindness of individuals. This links to Theme 2's sub-theme on the media and to
  Theme 3's interest in migration and borders.

## Film terminology in Urdu

The range grid rewards "frequent" and then "consistent" use of terminology for literary and cinematic analysis.
Learn a set and use it naturally.

| Urdu | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ہدایت کار / ہدایت کاری | director / direction (Kabir Khan directed this film) |
| منظر، مناظر | scene, scenes |
| مکالمہ | dialogue |
| کردار نگاری | characterisation |
| پلاٹ / کہانی کا ڈھانچہ | plot / plot structure |
| کیمرے کا زاویہ | camera angle |
| کلوز اپ (قریبی شاٹ) | close-up |
| پس منظر موسیقی | background music |
| نقطۂ عروج | climax |
| علامت | symbol |
| ناظرین | viewers, audience |
| تضاد | contrast |
| طنز و مزاح | satire and humour |
| جذباتی اثر | emotional effect |

Use a term only when you can say what it achieves: «کلوز اپ کے ذریعے ناظرین شاہدہ کی بے بسی کو قریب سے محسوس
کرتے ہیں» (through the close-up, viewers feel Shahida's helplessness at close quarters) does more than naming the
shot.

## Complex language that fits film analysis

The specification counts these as complex language: conceptually challenging tenses such as the pluperfect and
future perfect, the passive voice, the subjunctive mood, subordination, and extended sentences that express
abstract ideas. Each can be built into a point about the film.

1. **Passive:** «فلم میں یہ دکھایا گیا ہے کہ انسانیت کسی ایک مذہب کی میراث نہیں۔» (The film shows that humanity is
   the inheritance of no single religion.)
2. **Pluperfect:** «جب پون کی ملاقات شاہدہ سے ہوئی، تب تک وہ اپنی ماں سے بچھڑ چکی تھی۔» (By the time Pawan met
   Shahida, she had already been separated from her mother.)
3. **Hypothetical condition:** «اگر پون جھوٹ بول دیتا تو اس کا سفر شاید آسان ہو جاتا۔» (If Pawan had lied, his
   journey might have become easier.)
4. **Concession:** «اگرچہ فلم میں کئی مزاحیہ مناظر ہیں، پھر بھی اس کا موضوع بہت سنجیدہ ہے۔» (Although the film has
   many comic scenes, its subject is very serious.)
5. **Future perfect:** «فلم ختم ہونے تک ناظرین سمجھ چکے ہوں گے کہ دشمنی ریاستوں کی ہے، عوام کی نہیں۔» (By the end of
   the film, viewers will have understood that the hostility belongs to states, not to people.)

Agreement matters on the accuracy grid: **فلم** and **ویڈیو** are feminine, while **منظر**, **پیغام** and **سفر** are
masculine.

## Planning a 320–370-word essay

| Part | Purpose | Approximate words |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Answer the question directly and state your position | 40–50 |
| Paragraph 1 | First argument, one sequence, one technique | 80–90 |
| Paragraph 2 | Second argument, a different sequence | 80–90 |
| Paragraph 3 | Counter-view or evaluation, then your judgement | 70–80 |
| Conclusion | Return to the question; link to the wider social theme | 40–50 |

Plan for three or four minutes before writing. Note your position, the two or three sequences you will use, and
two complex structures you intend to include.

## Model paragraph, annotated

**Question:** «فلم "بجرنگی بھائی جان" میں پون کی ایمان داری کس طرح اس کے کردار اور فلم کے پیغام کو تشکیل دیتی ہے؟»
(How does Pawan's honesty shape his character and the film's message?)

> پون کی ایمان داری اس کی کمزوری بھی ہے اور طاقت بھی۔ سرحد کے منظر میں وہ چوری چھپے پاکستان میں داخل ہونے کے
> بجائے اجازت لینے پر اصرار کرتا ہے، اور یہی سادگی ناظرین کو ہنساتی بھی ہے اور فکر مند بھی کرتی ہے۔ ہدایت کار نے
> اس منظر میں مزاح اور خطرے کا تضاد بڑی مہارت سے پیش کیا ہے۔ اگر پون جھوٹ بول دیتا تو شاید اس کا سفر آسان ہو جاتا،
> لیکن تب وہ ایسا کردار نہ رہتا جس پر دونوں ملکوں کے لوگ بھروسا کر سکیں۔ اس طرح فلم یہ پیغام دیتی ہے کہ سچائی سرحدوں
> سے زیادہ مضبوط ہے۔

**Translation:** Pawan's honesty is both his weakness and his strength. In the border scene, instead of entering
Pakistan secretly, he insists on getting permission, and this very simplicity both makes viewers laugh and makes
them anxious. The director presents the contrast between humour and danger in this scene with great skill. If
Pawan had lied, his journey might have been easier, but then he would no longer be a character whom people of
both countries could trust. In this way the film gives the message that truth is stronger than borders.

**Why it works:** the opening sentence is a position, not a plot point (AO4). The evidence is one precise sequence,
analysed for its effect. The paragraph uses film terminology (منظر، ہدایت کار، تضاد), a hypothetical condition and
a relative clause (AO3 range), and ends by linking character to message. In a full essay, a later paragraph should
test this view, for example by asking whether such honesty is believable.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a plot summary in time order. Select sequences that answer the question.
- Naming techniques without saying what they make the viewer feel or think.
- Writing in a mixture of English and Urdu: film terms are needed in Urdu too.
- Writing a political essay on India and Pakistan rather than an analysis of the film.
- Producing far more than the recommended length at the cost of accuracy.
- Learning a prepared essay by heart and forcing it onto a different question.
