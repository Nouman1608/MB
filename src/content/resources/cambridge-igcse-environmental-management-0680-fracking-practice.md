## Section A

**1.** State what the word "hydraulic" refers to in hydraulic fracturing. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** differences between a conventional gas deposit and gas held in shale. **[2]**

**3.** State the purpose of the sand in fracking fluid. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a fracking well is lined with steel casing and cement. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **one** benefit of fracking at a national scale and **one** limitation at a global scale. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why gas obtained by fracking is a finite resource. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A company plans eight wells. Each well needs 18,000 m³ of water for fracturing. A nearby town uses 3,600 m³ of water a day. The figures are invented for practice.

**(a)** Calculate the total volume of water needed for the eight wells. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many days of the town's water supply this represents. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this water use would be a more serious limitation in a region with a dry climate. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which fracking could contaminate ground water. **[4]**

**9.** A fracking site is proposed near a rural village. Suggest the likely view of each of these people, and give a reason for each view:

- a farmer who irrigates crops with water from a well
- a young person from the village who is unemployed
- the country's energy minister, whose country imports most of its gas. **[6]**

**10.** Explain why fracking may both reduce and increase greenhouse gas emissions. **[4]**

**11.** Describe **two** ways of reducing the risks of earthquakes and ground water pollution from fracking, and state **one** limitation of these measures. **[3]**

**12.** Country Q imports most of its gas, has strong winds along its coast and has large shale deposits beneath a national park that attracts many tourists. Discuss the benefits and limitations of allowing fracking in the national park. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The use of a liquid (water-based fluid) under high pressure [1].

**2.** Any two: in a conventional deposit the gas has moved into permeable rock, whereas shale gas is still held in its source rock [1]; conventional gas is trapped beneath an impermeable cap rock, whereas shale gas is trapped within impermeable rock itself [1]; conventional gas flows to a well on its own, whereas shale must be fractured first [1].

**3.** To hold the cracks in the shale open so that gas or oil can flow out [1].

**4.** To seal the well from the surrounding rock [1], so that gas, fracking fluid or wastewater cannot leak into layers holding ground water [1].

**5.** National benefit, any one: energy security; fewer imports; tax income; lower gas prices [1]. Global limitation, any one: methane leaks add to the enhanced greenhouse effect; burning the gas releases carbon dioxide; delays the switch to renewables [1].

**6.** Natural gas is a fossil fuel that took millions of years to form [1], and it is being used up much faster than it is replaced [1].

**7. (a)** 8 × 18,000 = 144,000 m³ [1].

**(b)** 144,000 ÷ 3,600 = 40 days [1].

**(c)** In a dry region, water is already scarce [1], so using it for fracking competes with drinking water and irrigation and could cause shortages for people and farms [1].

**8.** Two ways, each explained, 2 marks each:
- **Failure of the well casing**: if the steel or cement cracks [1], gas or fracking fluid can escape into ground water layers the well passes through [1].
- **Surface spills**: fracking chemicals or flowback stored at the site can spill or leak [1] and soak down into the ground water [1].
- **Poor wastewater disposal**: wastewater containing chemicals and salts [1] may be released or stored unsafely and reach aquifers [1].

**9.** Each view with a valid reason, 2 marks each:
- **Farmer**: likely to oppose [1], because fluid or gas leaking into ground water could make well water unsafe for irrigation, or fracking could take water the farm needs [1].
- **Unemployed young person**: likely to support [1], because drilling and transport create jobs and income in the village [1].
- **Energy minister**: likely to support [1], because domestic gas reduces imports and improves energy security, and brings tax income [1].

Credit other views if the reason is valid, for example a farmer who would receive payments for land used.

**10.** Reduce: if gas from fracking replaces coal in power stations [1], less carbon dioxide is released for the same energy, because gas emits less than coal [1]. Increase: methane, a powerful greenhouse gas, can leak from wells and pipes [1]; cheap gas keeps the country using fossil fuels and may delay investment in renewables [1].

**11.** Any two measures: monitoring ground movement and stopping operations if tremors occur [1]; strict construction rules and inspections of well casing [1]; testing ground water before, during and after fracking [1]; treating wastewater before disposal [1]; locating sites away from homes [1]. Maximum 2. Limitation, any one: measures are expensive, or they only work if they are enforced [1].

**12.** Levels-based.

- **Level 3 (7–8 marks)**: benefits and limitations both explained and clearly linked to Country Q's imports, coastal winds, national park and tourism; a reasoned judgement.
- **Level 2 (4–6 marks)**: benefits and limitations explained with some use of the source; judgement may be simple.
- **Level 1 (1–3 marks)**: points listed with little explanation; may be one-sided.

Indicative content:
- **Benefits**: domestic gas reduces imports and improves energy security; jobs and tax income; gas replacing coal lowers carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide emissions.
- **Limitations**: well pads, roads, lorries and noise damage the landscape and habitats of the national park; loss of tourism income and jobs; risk of ground water contamination and small earthquakes; methane leaks and continued fossil fuel use.
- **Alternative from the source**: strong coastal winds suggest wind power could improve energy security without damaging the park.
- **Judgement**: for example, refuse fracking in the national park because the damage to habitats and tourism is long-lasting, and invest in wind power and energy conservation instead.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: stating only that shale is impermeable, without contrasting it with the conventional deposit.
- **Question 7(c)**: repeating that fracking uses a lot of water without linking it to scarcity in a dry climate.
- **Question 9**: giving a view without a reason. Each mark pair needs both.
- **Question 10**: explaining only one direction. The question asks for both.
- **Question 12**: ignoring the national park and tourism, or missing the clue about coastal winds.
