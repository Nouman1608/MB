## Where Paper 1 sits

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Reading** | **2 h 15** | **50** | **50% of AS · 25% of A Level** |
| 2 | Writing | 2 h | 50 | 50% of AS · 25% of A Level |
| 3 | Language Analysis | 2 h 15 | 50 | 25% of A Level |
| 4 | Language Topics | 2 h 15 | 50 | 25% of A Level |

Papers 1 and 2 are the AS content; 3 and 4 complete the A Level.

## Paper 1 structure

Two compulsory questions, one per section, **25 marks each**. Each responds to an **unseen text of roughly 550–750 words**.

### Section A — Directed response (Q1)

| Part | Task | Marks | AOs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1(a) | Directed writing in response to the text, **150–200 words** | 10 | AO1, AO2 |
| 1(b) | **Compare** your response in (a) with the original text — form, structure and language | 15 | AO1, AO3 |

Part (b) carries more marks than the writing itself. You are analysing **your own text against the source**, relating features to purpose, audience and context.

### Section B — Text analysis (Q2)

Read a second unseen text and comment on its **form, structure and language**, relating features to meaning, context and audience, and organising your answer in an appropriate style. Assesses AO1 and AO3.

## Text types you may meet

Advertisements · brochures · leaflets · editorials · news stories · articles · reviews · blogs · investigative journalism · letters · podcasts · (auto)biographies · travel writing · diaries · essays · scripted speech · narrative and descriptive writing.

Recognising the **conventions** of the form quickly is half of Q1(a).

## Analytical framework

Work through the levels rather than commenting on whatever stands out first:

- **Form** — the text type and its conventions.
- **Structure** — opening, development, shifts, closing; where information is placed and why.
- **Language** — lexis and semantic fields, connotation, register, grammar and sentence types, imagery.
- **Context, purpose, audience** — every feature must be related back to these.

Every analytical point: **identify → quote → explain the effect in relation to purpose and audience.**

## Exam traps

- Treating 1(b) as a summary of what you wrote — it is a **comparison** of form, structure and language.
- Feature-spotting without linking to purpose, audience or context.
- Writing outside 150–200 words in 1(a).
- Neglecting form conventions when the task specifies a text type.
- Analysing content rather than language in Q2.
- Dictionaries may **not** be used.

## Self-test

1. How long is Paper 1 and what is it worth at A Level?
2. How are the 25 marks split within Question 1?
3. What is the word range for Question 1(a)?
4. What exactly does Question 1(b) ask you to compare?
5. Which assessment objectives does Question 2 target?

**Answers:** 1. 2 hours 15 minutes, 50 marks, 25% of the A Level (50% of the AS). 2. 10 marks for the directed writing in 1(a), 15 marks for the comparison in 1(b). 3. 150–200 words. 4. The form, structure and language of your own response in part (a) against those of the original text, relating features to purpose, audience and context. 5. AO1 and AO3.
