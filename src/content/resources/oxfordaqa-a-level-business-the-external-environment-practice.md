Practice for section 3.1.1.3, The external environment, in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business specification (9625 for current cohorts; the 9725 document was used for the wording). All businesses and figures are invented, and money is in currency units (cu). ## Questions

**1.** Define the term "barrier to entry". **[2]**

**2.** Dellwick Dairy sells in a regional milk market. In Year 1 the market sold 8.0 million litres at an average price of 1.20 cu. In Year 2 it sold 7.6 million litres at an average price of 1.35 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the market in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in market volume and in market value. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain what these results suggest about the average price. **[2]**

**3.** The biscuit market in one country is worth 110 million cu. The four largest firms have sales of 33.0m, 24.2m, 16.5m and 11.0m cu. Harcombe Biscuits is the third largest.

**(a)** Calculate Harcombe's market share. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the four firm concentration ratio. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain what the ratio suggests about competition in this market. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **two** barriers that a new firm might face when trying to enter the market for private dental clinics. **[4]**

**5.** Analyse how high barriers to entry might affect existing businesses and customers in a market. **[6]**

**6.** Explain how a saturated market might affect the decisions and profits of a business selling phone cases. **[4]**

**7.** Wolsingham Sofas records that when average income rose 6%, sales of its mid-range sofa rose from 12,500 to 13,625 units.

**(a)** Calculate the income elasticity of demand. **[3]**
**(b)** State whether this is a normal or inferior good, and whether demand is income elastic or inelastic. **[2]**
**(c)** Income is forecast to fall by 5%. Sales are currently 12,000 units and the elasticity is the same. Calculate the expected sales. **[3]**

**8.** A discount grocer sells a store brand of rice with income elasticity of demand of -0.5. Incomes fall by 6% and sales are currently 40,000 packs. Calculate the expected sales and explain why the result is useful to the grocer. **[4]**

**9.** Ostrenby Print borrows 450,000 cu at a variable interest rate of 4.0%. The rate rises to 5.5%. Its customers often buy new presses on credit. Calculate the extra annual interest and analyse **two** ways the rise affects Ostrenby. **[6]**

**10.** Wolsingham Textiles imports yarn costing 90,000 foreign currency units (FC) per order and exports shirts priced at 40 cu. The exchange rate changes from 1 cu = 3.0 FC to 1 cu = 2.5 FC. Calculate the cost of an order in cu before and after, and the price of a shirt in FC before and after. Analyse the effect on the business. **[6]**

**11.** Harcombe Furniture sells 20,000 chairs a year at 60 cu. Unit cost is 42 cu, of which 20 cu is timber. Timber prices rise 15%. A new rival has started selling a similar chair at 58 cu. Harcombe's owner is considering raising price by 5% and expects sales to fall by 10%. Evaluate whether Harcombe should raise its price. **[9]**

**12.** Penhallow Pet Foods is considering entering the premium cat food market. The market was worth 40m cu last year and 44m cu this year. The four largest firms hold 28%, 20%, 14% and 10% of the market. Entry would cost about 3m cu and customers show strong loyalty to existing brands. Justify whether Penhallow should enter. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Anything that makes it difficult or costly for a new firm to enter a market [1], such as high start-up costs or customer loyalty to existing brands [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* a definition gains more credit when it says what the barrier does (restricts new entrants) rather than only listing examples.

**2. (a)** Year 1: 8.0 × 1.20 = **9.6m cu** [1]. Year 2: 7.6 × 1.35 = **10.26m cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Volume: (7.6 - 8.0) / 8.0 × 100 = **-5.0%** [1]. Value: (10.26 - 9.6) / 9.6 × 100 = 6.875, so **+6.9%** [1]. Both use the old figure as the divisor and keep the sign [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Volume fell but value rose, so the average price must have risen [1] by more than enough to offset the fall in litres sold (1.20 to 1.35, a rise of 12.5%) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* percentage change answers usually need the correct sign, so write the minus for a decline.

**3. (a)** 16.5 / 110 × 100 = **15.0%** [1][1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (33.0 + 24.2 + 16.5 + 11.0) / 110 [1] = 84.7 / 110 [1] = **77.0%** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** A ratio of 77% means a few large firms hold most of the market [1], so a new or small firm faces strong rivals and competition is concentrated among those few [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* give the ratio as a percentage, and make the interpretation specific to the figure rather than stating the formula again.

**4.** Any two, 2 marks each: **specialist knowledge**, as clinics need qualified dentists, which takes years and money to acquire [1], so few can set up quickly [1]; **legal requirements**, as registration and safety rules must be met before trading [1], which adds cost and delay [1]. (Entry costs for equipment, or patient loyalty, also gain credit.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* name the barrier, then say why it blocks entry in this market; a list of labels alone earns less.

**5.** Existing businesses: new rivals are kept out [1], so they keep a stable share and profit [1], and feel less pressure to cut prices or innovate [1]. Customers: fewer suppliers means less choice [1], prices may be higher [1], and weaker pressure to improve quality [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* analysis improves when each effect is developed in a chain (cause, effect, consequence), covering both groups asked about.

**6.** Total demand barely grows, so a gain in sales must come from rivals [1]. The business may respond with differentiation or promotion, or look for new customer segments [1]. Price competition may squeeze margins [1], so profit is under pressure unless costs are cut or the product is distinctive [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* tie the answer to the product named in the question, such as phone cases, rather than writing a general definition.

**7. (a)** Quantity change: (13,625 - 12,500) / 12,500 × 100 = 9% [1]. YED = 9 / 6 [1] = **+1.5** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Positive, so a **normal good** [1]; greater than 1, so **income elastic** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** % change in quantity = 1.5 × -5 = -7.5% [1]. 12,000 × 0.925 [1] = **11,100 units** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* show the sign of YED, since the sign decides normal against inferior.

**8.** % change in quantity = -0.5 × -6 = +3% [1]. 40,000 × 1.03 = **41,200 packs** [1]. Rice is an inferior good, so demand rises when income falls [1], and the grocer can plan stock and promotion for a downturn [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* a negative times a negative gives a rise, so check the direction of your final figure.

**9.** Interest before: 450,000 × 4.0% = 18,000; after: 450,000 × 5.5% = 24,750 [1]; extra = **6,750 cu** a year [1]. Costs: higher repayments cut profit unless prices rise [1], and may delay planned investment [1]. Demand: customers buying presses on credit face dearer loans, so orders may fall [1], reducing sales revenue and profit [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* apply the effect to this business, here both its own loan and its customers' credit.

**10.** Before: 90,000 / 3.0 = 30,000 cu [1]; after: 90,000 / 2.5 = 36,000 cu [1], a rise of 20%. Shirt: 40 × 3.0 = 120 FC before; 40 × 2.5 = 100 FC after [1], about 17% cheaper abroad [1]. The home currency has weakened. Higher input costs squeeze margins [1], but cheaper exports may lift overseas demand [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* name which direction the currency moved before explaining effects, and consider both imports and exports.

**11.** Costs: timber 20 × 1.15 = 23 cu, so unit cost 45 cu [1]. Margin falls from 18 cu to 15 cu per chair [1]; contribution from 360,000 to 300,000 cu [1]. For raising: new price 63 cu gives margin 18 cu; with sales down 10% to 18,000, contribution = 324,000 cu, above 300,000 [1][1]. Against: the rival's chair is 58 cu, so a 63 cu price widens the gap and sales may fall by more than 10% [1]; loyalty and quality may protect demand [1]. Judgement: raise, but only if the sales loss stays below about 16.7%, because contribution at 63 cu equals 300,000 at 16,667 chairs [1]; the reasoned recommendation should state this limit and monitor sales [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* evaluation answers gain from a clear judgement supported by the calculation, and by a condition (how far sales can fall) rather than a one-line opinion.

**12.** For entering: the market grew (44 - 40) / 40 × 100 = 10% [1], so demand is rising [1]. Against: the four firm concentration ratio is 28 + 20 + 14 + 10 = 72% [1], so a few firms dominate [1]; loyalty to existing brands [1] and 3m cu entry costs are barriers [1]. Judgement, weighing growth against barriers [1]: entry is justified only if Penhallow can fund the entry costs and win loyalty through a clearly different product or lower price [1]; otherwise the risk is high despite growth [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* justify means a decision with reasons on both sides, and the data (10% growth, 72% ratio) should be used rather than only mentioned.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure rather than the old.
- Leaving out the minus sign for a decline or a negative YED.
- Giving the concentration ratio as a sum of sales rather than a percentage.
- Listing barriers to entry with no explanation of why they block entry.
- Writing about high barriers from one side only, existing businesses or customers.
- Writing "profit falls" with no chain from the external change.
- Mixing up a strong and a weak currency, especially for exporters.
- Giving a conclusion in an evaluation question with no reasons or numbers behind it.
