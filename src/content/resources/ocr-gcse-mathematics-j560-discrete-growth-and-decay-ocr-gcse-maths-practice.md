Questions 11 to 13 use content that is **Higher tier only** and are labelled. Questions 4 and 5 are intended without a calculator. All amounts of money are invented for practice. ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the single multiplier for each change.

**(a)** An increase of 7% **[1]**

**(b)** A decrease of 12.5% **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the percentage change represented by the multiplier 0.97. **[1]**

**2.** £2,750 is invested at 3.6% per year simple interest for 4 years.

**(a)** Work out the total interest earned. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the total amount in the account after 4 years. **[1]**

**3.** £1,800 earns £297 simple interest in 3 years. Calculate the annual rate of interest. **[3]**

**4.** Do not use a calculator. £5,000 is invested at 10% compound interest per year.

**(a)** Work out the value of the investment after 2 years. **[2]**

**(b)** Jo says that 10% simple interest would give the same value after 2 years. Is Jo correct? Show how you decide. **[1]**

**5.** Do not use a calculator. The price of a game rises by 25%. The new price then falls by 20%. Sam says the final price is the same as the original price. Is Sam correct? Show how you decide. **[2]**

**6.** A bike costs £640. Its value falls by 15% in the first year and by 10% in the second year. Calculate its value after 2 years. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Ana invests £8,400 for 3 years. Account P pays 2.4% compound interest per year. Account Q pays 2.5% simple interest per year.

**(a)** Calculate the value of Account P after 3 years. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the interest Account P earns. **[1]**

**(c)** Which account earns more interest over the 3 years, and by how much? **[3]**

**8.** A machine is bought for £36,000. Its value falls by 22% in the first year, 18% in the second year and 12% in the third year.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the machine after 3 years. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the overall percentage decrease in value, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the overall decrease is not 52%. **[1]**

**9.** A debt of £1,200 grows by 1.5% each month. No payments are made.

**(a)** Calculate the debt after 12 months, to the nearest penny. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that this is more than the debt would be after one year at 18% simple interest per year. **[2]**

**10.** Work backwards to find each original value.

**(a)** After 4 years of 3% compound interest per year an account holds £6,753.05. Calculate the amount first invested, to the nearest pound. **[3]**

**(b)** Do not use a calculator. A phone loses 20% of its value each year. After 2 years it is worth £256. Find its value when new. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** A lake contains 2,500 fish. The number of fish increases by 12% each year.

**(a)** Write a formula for the number of fish F after n years. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your formula to estimate the number of fish after 6 years. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the first year in which the number of fish is greater than 6,000. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** The value £V of a van n years after it is bought is modelled by V = 15000 × 0.82ⁿ.

**(a)** Write down the value of the van when new and the percentage decrease per year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the value of the van after 5 years. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the number of years after which the value first falls below £5,000. **[3]**

**(d)** Give one reason why the model may not be reliable for predicting the value after 20 years. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** Do not use a calculator. An investment grows from £2,000 to £2,420 in 2 years at the same compound rate each year. Find the annual rate of interest. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1.07** [1]

**(b)** **0.875** [1]

**(c)** A **decrease of 3%** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 1 − 0.97 = 0.03, so the change is a 3% fall. A multiplier below 1 always means a decrease.

**2. (a)** One year's interest is 2750 × 0.036 = £99 [1]; 99 × 4 = **£396** [1]

**(b)** 2750 + 396 = **£3,146** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (a) asks for the interest only and part (b) for the total. Check which the question wants before you write the final line.

**3.** Interest per year = 297 ÷ 3 = £99 [1]; 99 ÷ 1800 = 0.055 [1]; rate = **5.5%** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing 297 by 1800 first gives the rate for all 3 years together. Find one year's interest before comparing it with the principal.

**4. (a)** Multiplier 1.1² = 1.21 [1]; 5000 × 1.21 = **£6,050** [1]

**(b)** Simple interest gives 2 × £500 = £1,000, so £6,000, which is less than £6,050; **Jo is not correct** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In year 2 the compound interest is 10% of £5,500, which is £550, not £500. That extra £50 is interest earned on interest.

**5.** 1.25 × 0.8 = 1 [1]; the overall multiplier is 1, so **Sam is correct** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* This pair happens to cancel, but most do not. Always multiply the multipliers rather than relying on the percentages.

**6.** 640 × 0.85 = £544 [1]; 544 × 0.9 [1]; **£489.60** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Taking 25% off £640 gives £480, which is wrong. The 10% is taken from £544, not from £640.

**7. (a)** 8400 × 1.024³ [1]; 9019.4313..., so **£9,019.43** [1]

**(b)** 9019.43 − 8400 = **£619.43** [1]

**(c)** Account Q interest = 8400 × 0.025 × 3 [1]; = £630 [1]; **Account Q** earns more, by 630 − 619.43 = **£10.57** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Compound interest does not always beat simple interest. Over a short time a slightly higher simple rate can earn more, so compare the actual amounts.

**8. (a)** 36000 × 0.78 = 28,080 [1]; 28080 × 0.82 = 23,025.60 [1]; 23025.60 × 0.88 = **£20,262.53** [1]

**(b)** 20262.53 ÷ 36000 = 0.5628..., or 0.78 × 0.82 × 0.88 = 0.562848 [1]; decrease = **43.7%** [1]

**(c)** Each percentage is taken from the value at the start of that year, which is smaller each time, so the percentages cannot be added [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* For part (b), the overall multiplier gives the answer in one step: 1 − 0.562848 = 0.437152.

**9. (a)** 1200 × 1.015¹² [1]; **£1,434.74** [1]

**(b)** 18% simple interest: 1200 × 0.18 = 216, giving £1,416 [1]; £1,434.74 > £1,416, so the monthly compound debt is larger [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* There are 12 intervals of one month. Using 1.015 once, or 1.18 once, are both common errors.

**10. (a)** original × 1.03⁴ = 6753.05 [1]; original = 6753.05 ÷ 1.03⁴ = 6753.05 ÷ 1.12550881 [1]; **£6,000** [1]

**(b)** Multiplier 0.8² = 0.64 [1]; 256 ÷ 0.64 = **£400** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Adding 20% twice to £256 gives £368.64, which is wrong. The original was multiplied by 0.64, so divide by 0.64.

**11. (a)** Multiplier 1.12 [1]; **F = 2500 × 1.12ⁿ** [1]

**(b)** 2500 × 1.12⁶ = 4934.55... [1]; about **4,935 fish** [1]

**(c)** n = 7 gives 5,526.7 (below 6,000) [1]; n = 8 gives 6,189.9 (above 6,000) [1]; first in **year 8** [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Show the value for the year before the target as well as the year that passes it. A single trial cannot show that it is the first.

**12. (a)** **£15,000** when new [1]; 1 − 0.82 = 0.18, a decrease of **18% per year** [1]

**(b)** 15000 × 0.82⁵ [1]; **£5,561.10** [1]

**(c)** n = 5 gives £5,561.10 (above £5,000) [1]; n = 6 gives £4,560.10 (below £5,000) [1]; **6 years** [1]

**(d)** For example: the van is unlikely to keep losing exactly 18% every year for 20 years; its condition or mileage could change the rate [1] **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* For "interpret" or "comment" parts, tie your reason to the assumption in the model: a constant percentage change for every interval.

**13.** multiplier² = 2420 ÷ 2000 = 1.21 [1]; multiplier = √1.21 = 1.1 [1]; rate = **10%** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Halving the overall 21% increase gives 10.5%, which ignores compounding. Undo the square with a square root.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a percentage decrease as the wrong multiplier, such as 0.15 instead of 0.85.
- Adding successive percentage changes instead of multiplying the multipliers.
- Giving the interest when the question asks for the total value, or the other way round.
- Using one multiplier for a whole year when interest is added monthly.
- Reversing a percentage change by adding the percentage back on.
- Rounding to the nearest penny in the middle of a calculation.
- Giving only one trial value, or a decimal number of years, in a "first year" question.
- Saying "yes" or "no" without the calculation that supports it.
