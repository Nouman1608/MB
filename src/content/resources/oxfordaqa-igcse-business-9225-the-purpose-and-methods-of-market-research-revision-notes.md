Condensed recall for section 3.5.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-the-purpose-and-methods-of-market-research/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Purpose**: identify market opportunities; insight into customers and competitors.
- **Methods**: qualitative and quantitative; primary and secondary.
- **Benefits and drawbacks** of techniques (including e-commerce); **select the best method** for a business.
- **Use of findings** to help decisions; **manipulate and interpret data** from tables and charts.
- **Why research can be inaccurate** and misinform a business.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market research | Collecting and analysing information about a market, customers and competitors |
| Primary research | New information collected first-hand for the business's own purpose |
| Secondary research | Using information that already exists |
| Quantitative | Numerical data |
| Qualitative | Opinions, feelings and reasons |
| Sample | A smaller group chosen to represent the target market |
| Representative | Matches the target market in age, gender, location and so on |
| Focus group | Small group discussing a product, led by a researcher |
| Test marketing | Selling a product in a limited area before a full launch |
| Bias | Results pushed in one direction |

## Purpose of market research

- Identify opportunities.
- Understand customers (likes, spending, buying habits).
- Understand competitors.
- Reduce the risk of failure.
- Measure market size and trends.
- Support requests for finance.
- Check the success of marketing.

## Primary against secondary

| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Source | First-hand | Existing sources |
| Fit to need | Specific | May not fit |
| Up to date | Yes | May be old |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Examples | Surveys, interviews, focus groups | Government statistics, reports, websites, past sales |

## Quantitative against qualitative

| | Quantitative | Qualitative |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Numbers | Opinions and reasons |
| Methods | Questionnaires, sales data | Interviews, focus groups |
| Strength | Easy to compare, large samples | Depth and reasons |
| Weakness | No explanation | Hard to analyse, small samples |

## Methods: quick comparison

| Method | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Many people; cheap | Low response; untruthful answers |
| Interview | Detailed | Costly; interviewer bias |
| Focus group | Reasons and feelings | Not representative |
| Observation | Shows real behaviour | Does not show why |
| Test marketing | Real sales evidence | Costly; rivals can see |
| Sales / loyalty data | Cheap, accurate | Past behaviour only |
| Online (e-commerce) | Fast, cheap, wide reach | Only online users; privacy; biased reviews |
| Secondary sources | Cheap, wide | Out of date; not specific |

## Choosing a method

- Small budget: online surveys, social media, secondary data.
- Large launch: surveys, focus groups, test marketing.
- Need reasons: focus group or interview.
- Need numbers: questionnaire or analytics.

## Data skills

- Percentage = part / total x 100. 100 / 250 x 100 = 40%.
- Combine categories: (100 + 85) / 250 x 100 = 74%.
- Scaling up: percentage of sample x population. 62% x 4,000 = 2,480.
- Modal class = the group with the highest frequency.
- Always state what the figures **show**, what to **do**, and what they **cannot tell you**.

## Why research can be wrong

- Sample too small or not representative.
- Leading or unclear questions.
- Respondents not truthful, or say they will buy and do not.
- Out-of-date data.
- Interviewer bias.
- Poor analysis.
- Low response rate.

**Improve it:** bigger, representative sample; pilot the questionnaire; neutral questions; use more than one method; check against other sources.

## Exam traps

- Primary/secondary is about **source**. Qualitative/quantitative is about **type**.
- Primary research is not always better; it costs more.
- Survey results are **estimates**, not facts.
- Explain why a method suits the business, not just what it is.
- E-commerce data only covers online customers.
- Use the correct total when finding percentages.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "select the best method" question

Question style: "Recommend a method of market research for a small bakery thinking of selling gluten-free bread."

1. **Method:** a short questionnaire given to customers in the shop on several days, plus an online poll.
2. **Reason:** it is cheap, reaches existing customers and gives quantitative data.
3. **Add qualitative data:** a few interviews to find out why customers want gluten-free bread.
4. **Secondary data:** an online report on market growth costs little.
5. **Limits:** sample bias, untruthful answers; try a small test batch before launching.

## Revision checklist

- I can define primary, secondary, qualitative and quantitative research.
- I can give seven purposes of market research.
- I can describe six methods with a benefit and a drawback for each.
- I can choose a method for a given business.
- I can calculate percentages from a table and scale up a sample.
- I can state six reasons why research might be inaccurate.
- I can suggest how to make research more accurate.

## Self-test

1. Define market research.
2. Distinguish between primary and secondary research.
3. Distinguish between qualitative and quantitative research.
4. State two purposes of market research.
5. Give one benefit and one drawback of a questionnaire.
6. Give one benefit of a focus group.
7. Explain one benefit of online market research.
8. 45 of 150 people say they would buy. Calculate the percentage.
9. 35% of 2,000 customers in a sample say they prefer red. Calculate the number.
10. A business has 8,000 customers. A sample of 400 is 5%. Calculate the sample as a percentage of customers.
11. State two reasons why research results may be inaccurate.
12. Recommend a method for a small cafe on a tight budget, with a reason.

### Answers

1. Collecting and analysing information about a market, its customers and its competitors.
2. Primary is new information collected first-hand; secondary uses information that already exists.
3. Quantitative is numerical data; qualitative is opinions, feelings and reasons.
4. Any two: identify opportunities; understand customers; understand competitors; reduce risk.
5. Benefit: collects data from many people cheaply. Drawback: low response rate (or untruthful answers).
6. It shows people's feelings and the reasons for their views.
7. It is quick and cheap and can reach many people, or shows real behaviour such as clicks and purchases.
8. 45 / 150 x 100 = **30%**.
9. 35% x 2,000 = **700**.
10. 400 / 8,000 x 100 = **5%**.
11. Any two: sample too small; not representative; leading questions; untruthful answers; out-of-date data.
12. An online survey or a short customer comment card, because it is cheap and reaches existing customers directly.
