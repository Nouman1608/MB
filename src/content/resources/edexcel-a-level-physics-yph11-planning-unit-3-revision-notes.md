For the full explanation and a worked plan, use the [Unit 3 planning study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-planning-unit-3/); test yourself with the [Unit 3 planning practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-planning-unit-3-practice/).

## The plan frame

You plan an experiment Pearson sets; you do not carry it out. Write the answer in this order:

1. **Aim and physics**: the equation linking the variables, if one is given or known.
2. **Variables**: independent (changed), dependent (measured), controls (kept constant).
3. **Apparatus**: every item, with range and resolution of each measuring instrument.
4. **Calibration**: zero check or check against a known value.
5. **Method**: numbered steps, with the technique for each measurement.
6. **Range and number**: at least five or six values of the independent variable, spread widely.
7. **Repeats**: whether appropriate, and why.
8. **Safety**: hazard + precaution, specific to this apparatus.
9. **Data**: what to plot, and how the gradient or intercept gives the answer.
10. **Uncertainty**: main sources, and how each is reduced or eliminated.
11. **Implications and context**: a benefit or risk, in a social, environmental or historical setting.

## Instruments

| Instrument | Resolution | Uncertainty (single reading) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metre rule | 1 mm | 0.5 mm | Lengths of 0.1 m to 1 m |
| Vernier calipers | 0.1 mm | 0.05 mm | Diameters of balls, rods, tubes, 5 mm to 150 mm |
| Micrometer screw gauge | 0.01 mm | 0.005 mm | Thin wires and sheets, below 25 mm |
| Stopwatch (hand timing) | 0.01 s display | Reaction time dominates, about 0.1 s to 0.2 s | Long intervals, many oscillations |
| Light gates and timer | Typically 0.001 s | Small | Short intervals, speeds |
| Top-pan balance | 0.01 g or 0.1 g | Half the resolution | Masses |
| Digital meter | Last digit | Half the last digit | Current, p.d. |

Choosing: calculate (half resolution ÷ quantity) × 100% for each option and pick the one well under 1–2%. Check the range: the quantity must fit the instrument.

## Calibration checks

- Micrometer, Vernier: close the jaws; non-zero reading = zero error; subtract it from every reading.
- Meter: reads zero with nothing connected; adjust or subtract the offset.
- Newton-meter: hang a known mass; compare reading with mg.
- Signal generator: compare displayed frequency with f = 1/T from an oscilloscope trace.
- Thermometer: melting ice should read 0 °C.

Calibration removes **systematic** error. Repeats reduce **random** error. Neither does the other's job.

## Unit 1 and 2 core practicals: planning points

| Core practical | Key control | Main uncertainty and fix | Hazard and precaution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Free fall | Same ball, same release method | Timing a short fall: use light gates or an electromagnet release and timer | Falling ball: catch box or sand tray |
| 2 Viscosity, falling ball | Liquid temperature, ball size and material | Ball not yet at terminal velocity: start timing well below the surface; parallax at markers | Spilt liquid: wipe up, slip risk |
| 3 Young modulus | Same wire, constant temperature | Small extension: long wire, fixed marker against a scale; diameter by micrometer at several places | Wire snapping: eye protection; masses falling: sand tray |
| 4 Speed of sound | Constant signal frequency | Locating in-phase positions: move through several wavelengths and divide | Loud sound: keep amplitude low |
| 5 Vibrating string | Length between bridges, same wire | Judging resonance: approach from above and below | Snapping wire: eye protection |
| 6 Laser and grating | Grating-to-screen distance | Locating spot centres: measure between orders on both sides | Laser: never look into the beam; remove reflective objects |
| 7 Resistivity | Wire temperature: small current, switch off between readings | Diameter: micrometer at several places and orientations | Hot wire: low current |
| 8 E.m.f. and internal resistance | Cell temperature: switch off between readings | Meter resolution: use the most sensitive range | Short circuit heating: include a protective resistor |

## Repeats: when they help

- Help: readings that scatter randomly, such as hand timing, judging resonance, reading a swinging pointer.
- Do not help: a zero error; a digital reading that does not change; a length set by you with a rule (the set value is not a random measurement).
- With repeats: mean, then uncertainty = half the range.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resolution | The smallest measuring interval of an instrument |
| Range (instrument) | The span of values an instrument can measure |
| Range (data) | Lowest to highest values of a variable used |
| Calibration | Checking an instrument against a known reference |
| Zero error | A non-zero reading when the true value is zero |
| Systematic error | An error that shifts every reading the same way |
| Random error | Unpredictable scatter about the true value |
| Control variable | A variable kept constant to make a fair test |
| Accuracy | How close a result is to the true value |
| Precision | How closely repeated values agree |

## Exam traps

- Quoting resolution as uncertainty: the uncertainty of a single reading is half the resolution.
- "Repeat to remove systematic error": wrong; calibrate instead.
- A safety line that names no hazard.
- Plotting y against x when the equation needs y against x² or √x.
- Forgetting the context point: it is on the Unit 3 list, not the Unit 6 list.
- Choosing a micrometer for a quantity larger than its range.

## Self-test

**1.** State the resolution of a micrometer screw gauge.
**2.** What is the uncertainty in a single Vernier reading?
**3.** Which instrument is best for a 0.40 mm wire, and what percentage uncertainty does it give?
**4.** Why do repeat readings not remove a zero error?
**5.** How could you calibrate a newton-meter?
**6.** Give the control that matters most in a resistivity experiment, and how it is kept.
**7.** What graph gives g from a free-fall experiment with s and t measured?
**8.** Why are at least five or six values of the independent variable planned?
**9.** Give a hazard and precaution for the laser diffraction grating practical.
**10.** Repeated times are 2.46 s, 2.52 s and 2.49 s. Give the mean and its uncertainty.
**11.** Which planning point appears in Unit 3 but not in Unit 6?
**12.** In the vibrating-wire plan, why is f plotted against √T rather than T?

### Answers

**1.** 0.01 mm.
**2.** Half of 0.1 mm, so 0.05 mm.
**3.** Micrometer: 0.005 ÷ 0.40 × 100% = 1.25%, about 1.3%.
**4.** A zero error shifts every reading by the same amount, so the mean is shifted too.
**5.** Hang a known mass and check the reading equals mg (for 0.200 kg, 1.96 N).
**6.** Wire temperature: use a small current and switch off between readings.
**7.** s against t²; the gradient is g/2.
**8.** So a line of best fit can be drawn with confidence and an anomalous point can be seen.
**9.** The beam can damage the eye: never look along it and remove reflective surfaces.
**10.** Mean 2.49 s; uncertainty (2.52 − 2.46) ÷ 2 = 0.03 s.
**11.** Implications and context of the physics; identifying sources of uncertainty and systematic error.
**12.** f ∝ √T, so f against √T is a straight line through the origin, which is easy to test.
