Quick recall for section 1.7, External factors, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [external factors study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors/); exam-style questions are in the [external factors practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors-practice/). The specification names the four factors without describing them, so the examples here are standard ones. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists (1.7.1)

The external factors affecting business decisions: social, technological, environmental, political.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| External factor | Something outside a business's control that affects its decisions |
| Social factor | Changes in how people live, what they want and what they believe |
| Technological factor | New equipment, software and ways of producing, selling and communicating |
| Environmental factor | Concern about pollution, waste, resources and climate |
| Political factor | Government decisions and stability, including policies, elections and change of government |
| Obsolete | Out of date, replaced by something better |

## Examples and decisions

| Factor | Example of change | Possible decision |
|---|---|---|
| Social | More demand for healthy food | Reformulate products; add healthier lines |
| Social | Ageing population | Target older customers; adapt products |
| Social | Concern about how workers are treated | Check suppliers; change sourcing |
| Technological | Cheaper automation | Buy machines; retrain or reduce staff |
| Technological | Online shopping and apps | Sell online; reduce shop space |
| Environmental | Pressure to cut plastic | Switch packaging |
| Environmental | High energy prices and climate concern | Invest in energy saving |
| Political | Coming election | Delay investment until policies are clear |
| Political | Unrest in a country | Avoid locating there; limit investment |

## The three-link chain

Factor → effect on the business (cost, demand, reputation, risk) → decision.

Example: fewer people carry cash (social and technological) → customers want to pay by card → a market stall buys a card reader.

## Costing a response

- Recovery time = cost of the change ÷ yearly saving or gain.
- Reminder: an energy-saving boiler costs 9,000 and saves 1,800 a year: recovery 5 years.
- Reminder: a machine costs 40,000, saves wages of 25,000 a year and costs 5,000 a year to run: saving 20,000; recovery 2 years.
- Remember non-financial effects: staff morale, reputation, risk of the technology dating.

## Judging which factor matters most

1. Size of the effect on costs or revenue.
2. Speed of the change.
3. Cost and ease of the response.

Then: decision, using the case.

## Small and large businesses

- Paper 1 (small): feels local social change quickly; may lack money for new technology or greener methods; affected by local political decisions such as licensing.
- Paper 2 (large): faces different social and political conditions in each country; can afford technology and spreads its cost over large output; reputation is exposed worldwide, so environmental pressure matters.

## How each factor reaches the business

- Social: mainly through demand. Customers want different products, at different times or places, or from businesses that behave in certain ways.
- Technological: through costs (automation, software) and through new ways to sell and communicate, which also let new rivals in.
- Environmental: through customers, the government and the public; responses cost money now but may save energy or win customers later.
- Political: through government decisions on tax, spending, laws and trade, and through how stable the government is, which affects the risk of investing.

## Factors that combine

- Concern about waste (environmental) leads to a tax on plastic bags (political); shoppers start carrying their own (social).
- Online payments spread (technological); fewer people carry cash (social); a small shop must accept cards to keep customers.

## Command words applied

- Outline: factor plus one effect on the business in the case.
- Explain: factor, effect, decision (no context needed).
- Analyse: a chain from factor to decision for the named business.
- Evaluate: weigh two or more factors or responses and decide, using size, speed and cost.

## Exam traps

- A list of factors with no decision.
- Internal problems (poor managers, low morale) described as external factors.
- "Political" reduced to "new laws".
- Assuming every response only adds cost.
- Ignoring how quickly the factor is changing.
- No link between factors that clearly interact in the case.

## Self-test

1. List the four external factors in 1.7.1.
2. Define an external factor.
3. Classify: more people want to buy locally made goods.
4. Classify: a new robot halves assembly time.
5. Classify: a general election is due next month.
6. Classify: customers object to plastic straws.
7. Give one decision a restaurant could make in response to more vegetarian customers.
8. A machine costs 30,000 and saves 12,000 a year. Calculate the recovery time.
9. Why might a business delay investment before an election?
10. Give one way technology can be a threat to a business.
11. Why might environmental factors matter more to a large multinational than to a corner shop?
12. Give an example of two external factors working together.

### Answers

1. Social, technological, environmental, political.
2. Something outside the business's control that affects its decisions.
3. Social.
4. Technological.
5. Political.
6. Environmental (and social, since it reflects customers' attitudes).
7. Add vegetarian dishes to the menu (or advertise to vegetarian customers).
8. 30,000 ÷ 12,000 = 2.5 years.
9. It does not yet know which tax, spending or trade policies will apply.
10. It can make the business's products or skills obsolete (or let new rivals reach its customers).
11. Its brand is known in many countries, so bad publicity spreads widely and can hit sales everywhere.
12. Concern about waste (environmental) leads to a tax on plastic packaging (political).
