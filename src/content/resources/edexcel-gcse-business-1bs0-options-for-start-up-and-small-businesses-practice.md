## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term limited liability. **[2]**

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of a sole trader? **[1]**

A: The owner's liability is limited to the amount invested

B: The business is owned by shareholders

C: The owner keeps all the profit

D: Shares can be sold to the general public

**3.** Which **two** of the following are advantages of franchising for the franchisee? **[2]**

A: Freedom to set any prices

B: An established brand name

C: No ongoing payments to the franchisor

D: Training and support from the franchisor

E: Control over the quality of other outlets

**4.** State **one** disadvantage of a partnership for the partners, other than liability. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** implication of unlimited liability for a business owner. **[3]**

**6.** Hollin Tutoring is a franchise. The franchisee's sales revenue last year was £72,000 and the royalty is 8% of sales revenue.

**(a)** Calculate the royalty paid. **[2]**

**(b)** The franchisee's profit before royalty was £19,500. Calculate the profit after royalty. **[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Callum and Bea run Northgate Plumbing as an ordinary partnership. Callum invested £12,000 and Bea invested £8,000. They share profit in the ratio of their capital. Profit this year was £45,000.

**(a)** Calculate Bea's share of the profit. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline **one** risk for Bea of being in an ordinary partnership with Callum. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **one** reason why investors may be more willing to buy shares in a private limited company than to lend to a sole trader. **[3]**

**9.** Lena is starting Fernside Cakes from her kitchen. She has £2,500 of savings, will not borrow and wants to make every decision herself. Outline **one** reason why operating as a sole trader may be suitable for Lena. **[2]**

**10.** Marcus plans to open a climbing wall. He needs £160,000, of which £40,000 is his own savings. Two friends have offered to invest £30,000 each, and a bank may lend the rest. Analyse the benefits to Marcus of setting up the climbing wall as a private limited company. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** disadvantage of franchising for the franchisor. **[3]**

**12.** Asha wants to open a coffee shop. She is choosing between two options.

Option 1: open an independent coffee shop. Start-up cost £55,000. Forecast annual sales revenue £120,000 and profit £14,000.

Option 2: buy a coffee franchise. Start-up cost £55,000 plus an initial fee of £15,000. Forecast annual sales revenue £165,000. Profit before royalty £31,000, and a royalty of 5% of sales revenue.

Asha has never run a business. She enjoys designing menus and wants her shop to feel unique. Justify which option Asha should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The owner's loss is restricted to the amount they have invested in the business [1], so their personal possessions cannot be used to pay the business's debts [1]. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** B [1]. D [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A is wrong because franchisees usually follow the franchisor's pricing; C is wrong because royalties are ongoing; E belongs to the franchisor, not the franchisee.

**4.** Any one from: profit must be shared between the partners; partners may disagree about decisions; decisions may take longer because partners must agree [1]. **[1]**

**5.** If the business cannot pay its debts, the owner must pay them personally [1]. This means the owner's savings or possessions, such as a house or car, could be used to repay creditors [1]. As a result, the owner may be cautious about borrowing to expand the business, even when the opportunity is good [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** £72,000 × 8% [1] = **£5,760** [1]

**(b)** £19,500 − £5,760 = **£13,740** [1] **[3]**

**7. (a)** Ratio 12,000 : 8,000 = 3 : 2, so Bea receives 2 of 5 parts [1]. £45,000 ÷ 5 × 2 = **£18,000** [1]

**(b)** Ordinary partners have unlimited liability, so if Callum makes a poor decision that leaves the plumbing business in debt [1], Bea could have to use her own savings to help repay it, even though she did not make the decision [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Shareholders in a private limited company have limited liability [1], so the most they can lose is the money they paid for their shares [1]. A lender to a sole trader relies on one person's ability to repay, and that person may have little capital, so the risk of not being repaid can feel higher [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Lena is not borrowing and her savings are only £2,500, so the risk from unlimited liability is small [1]; being a sole trader lets her keep full control of Fernside Cakes and all its profit with very little paperwork [1]. **[2]**

**10.** As a private limited company, the climbing wall would be separate in law from Marcus, so he would have limited liability [1]. This matters because the bank may lend up to £60,000, and if the business failed his loss would be limited to his £40,000 investment rather than his personal possessions [1]. The two friends would become shareholders, investing £60,000 between them [1], and they may only be willing to invest if their own loss is capped, so the company form helps him raise the £160,000 [1]. The business could also continue if one friend later wanted to sell their shares [1]. However, Marcus would share profit and control with his friends, owning only part of the company, so he may not have the final say on decisions [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Every point uses a figure or person from the source. Answers that list advantages of companies in general, without Marcus's £160,000 or his friends, stay in the lowest levels.

**11.** The franchisor has less direct control over how each outlet is run [1]. A franchisee may offer poor service or low-quality products [1], which can damage the reputation of the whole brand and reduce sales at other outlets [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Option 2 royalty = 5% × £165,000 = £8,250 [1]. Profit after royalty = £31,000 − £8,250 = £22,750, which is £8,750 a year more than Option 1's £14,000 [1]. The extra £15,000 fee is recovered in under two years from that extra profit (£15,000 ÷ £8,750 = 1.7 years) [1].

For Option 2: Asha has never run a business, so a tested format, training and a known brand reduce her risk of failure [1], and the brand helps explain the higher forecast sales revenue [1].

For Option 1: Asha wants to design menus and make the shop unique, which a franchise would not allow because the franchisor sets the products and style [1], and she pays no royalty, so all extra sales would benefit her [1].

Judgement: Asha should choose the franchise, because it is forecast to earn £8,750 more profit each year and gives her support while she lacks experience [1]. This depends on the brand delivering the forecast sales revenue; if she would be unhappy without control over the menu, her motivation could fall, and an independent shop might suit her better once she has gained experience [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Justify asks for one option to be recommended using the information given. Strong answers calculate the royalty, compare both options and say what the recommendation depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that an ordinary partnership has limited liability.
- Forgetting to take the royalty away before comparing franchise profit with independent profit.
- Giving advantages of franchising without saying they are for the franchisee or the franchisor.
- Writing about companies in general in Question 10 instead of using Marcus's figures.
- Recommending an option in Question 12 without comparing it with the other.
