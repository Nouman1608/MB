## Section A

**1.** What is the only chemical product of a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell?

A: carbon dioxide

B: hydrogen peroxide

C: water

D: oxygen

**[1]**

**2.** State what a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell is used to produce, and name its two reactants. **[2]**

**3.** Write a balanced symbol equation for the overall reaction in a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell. **[2]**

**4.** A student says: "A fuel cell car produces no products at all." Correct this statement. **[1]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* Which of these is a disadvantage of hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells compared with petrol engines?

A: They produce carbon monoxide.

B: Hydrogen is difficult to store.

C: They are less efficient.

D: They produce oxides of nitrogen.

**[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Hydrogen can be burned in air, or it can be used in a fuel cell.

**(a)** State one similarity between the two processes. **[1]**

**(b)** State two differences between the two processes. **[2]**

**7.** *(Supplement)* A petrol engine releases carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide and oxides of nitrogen.

**(a)** Explain why a fuel cell does not release carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how oxides of nitrogen form in a petrol engine, and why a fuel cell does not produce them. **[2]**

**8.** *(Supplement)* Explain why a fuel cell vehicle is generally more efficient than a petrol-engined vehicle. **[2]**

**9.** *(Supplement)* Describe two disadvantages of storing and supplying hydrogen for vehicles, compared with petrol. **[4]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* A company advertises its fuel-cell bus as "completely carbon-free". Evaluate this claim. **[3]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* Hydrogen for fuel cells can be produced in two ways: from methane, or by the electrolysis of water.

**(a)** Explain why producing hydrogen from methane releases carbon dioxide. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest the condition under which hydrogen from the electrolysis of water would give the lowest overall carbon dioxide emissions. **[1]**

**12.** *(Supplement)* Describe two advantages and two disadvantages of using hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells instead of petrol engines in cars. **[4]**

**13.** *(Supplement)* A fuel cell uses 8.0 g of hydrogen. Using 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, calculate the mass of water produced. (Ar: H = 1, O = 16) **[2]**

**14.** *(Supplement)* At room temperature and pressure, 1 mol of any gas occupies 24 dm³. Using 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, calculate the volume of oxygen needed to react completely with 48 dm³ of hydrogen. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** **Electricity** [1]; **hydrogen and oxygen** [1].

**3.** Correct formulae H₂, O₂ and H₂O [1]; balanced: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O** [1].

**4.** It produces **water** (as its only chemical product) [1].

**5.** **B** [1].

**6.**

**(a)** Both react hydrogen with oxygen to form **water** (and release energy) [1].

**(b)** Any two: burning has a **flame** but a fuel cell does not [1]; burning releases energy **mainly as heat**, a fuel cell **mainly as electricity** [1]; (or) a fuel cell needs an external circuit to deliver its energy.

**7.**

**(a)** Hydrogen contains **no carbon**, so no carbon compounds can form [1].

**(b)** In the hot engine, **nitrogen and oxygen from the air react** at the high temperature [1]; a fuel cell has **no combustion / does not reach such high temperatures**, so this reaction does not happen [1].

**8.** A fuel cell converts **chemical energy directly into electrical energy** [1]; a petrol engine converts chemical energy into heat and then movement, **losing much of the energy as heat** [1].

**9.** Any two of the following, each with its explanation [2] [2]: hydrogen is a **gas with very low density**, so it must be stored in **high-pressure tanks** that are heavy and bulky, whereas petrol is a liquid; hydrogen is **highly flammable / explosive**, so leaks are dangerous; there are **few refuelling stations**, unlike petrol, which is widely available.

**10.** The bus itself releases **only water** — no CO₂ at the point of use [1]. However, the hydrogen may be **made from methane**, or by **electrolysis using electricity from fossil fuels**, both of which release CO₂ [1]. So the claim is only true if the hydrogen is made using **renewable energy**; otherwise it is misleading [1].

**11.**

**(a)** Methane, CH₄, **contains carbon**, which ends up as carbon dioxide during the process [1].

**(b)** When the electricity used comes from a **renewable source** (such as solar, wind or hydroelectric power) [1].

**12.** Any two advantages [2]: **only water** is produced, so no CO₂, CO or oxides of nitrogen; **more efficient**, because chemical energy is converted directly to electrical energy; hydrogen can be **made from water** rather than crude oil.

Any two disadvantages [2]: hydrogen is **difficult and dangerous to store** (high-pressure tanks, flammable); hydrogen is often **made from fossil fuels**, releasing CO₂; **few refuelling stations**; fuel-cell vehicles are **expensive**.

**13.** 8.0 g H₂ = 8.0 ÷ 2 = 4.0 mol, giving 4.0 mol H₂O [1]; mass = 4.0 × 18 = **72 g** [1].

**14.** Hydrogen : oxygen = 2 : 1 by moles, so also by volume for gases [1]; volume of oxygen = 48 ÷ 2 = **24 dm³** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "no products" or "no emissions" instead of "water is the only product".
- Describing the fuel cell as burning hydrogen.
- Giving unexplained one-word points such as "cleaner" or "safer".
- Comparing with batteries when the question asks about petrol engines.
- Forgetting that hydrogen production can release carbon dioxide.
- Listing four advantages when the question asks for two advantages **and** two disadvantages.
