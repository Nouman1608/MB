## Questions

**1.** Define the astronomical unit, the light-year and the parsec. **[3]**

**2.** A star is 2.6 pc from the Sun. Calculate its distance in metres and in light-years. **[2]**

**3.** A nearby star has a parallax of 0.045 seconds of arc.

**(a)** Calculate its distance in pc and in ly. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the parallax of a star is measured. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the method cannot be used for very distant stars. **[1]**

**4.** State the Cosmological principle. **[3]**

**5.** The calcium K line has a laboratory wavelength of 393.4 nm. In the light from a galaxy it is measured as 405.1 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the speed at which the galaxy is moving relative to the Earth. **[3]**
**(b)** A star in our own galaxy approaches the Earth at 25 km s⁻¹. Calculate the change in wavelength of a 500 nm line from it. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows recession speeds for five galaxies.

| d / Mpc | 30 | 60 | 90 | 130 | 170 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v / km s⁻¹ | 2250 | 4300 | 6500 | 9200 | 11 900 |

**(a)** State the shape of a graph of v against d that agrees with Hubble's law. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the data to find a value for the Hubble constant, in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. **[2]**
**(c)** Convert your value to s⁻¹. **[2]**
**(d)** Use H0 = 71 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ to estimate the age of the universe in years. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the red shift of light from distant galaxies supports a model of an expanding universe, and why this does not mean that the Earth is at the centre. **[5]**

**8.** The cosmic microwave background radiation has a temperature of 2.7 K. Describe this radiation and explain why it is evidence for the Big Bang theory. **[4]**

**9.** A measurement gives H0 = 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹. Calculate the estimated age of the universe in seconds and in years, and state one reason why the estimate may be wrong. **[4]**

**10.** Describe, in the correct order, three stages in the history of the universe between the first few minutes and the formation of the first stars. **[4]**

**11.**

**(a)** State the approximate percentages of dark energy, dark matter and ordinary matter in the universe. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one observation that suggests dark matter exists. **[1]**
**(c)** State what dark energy is associated with. **[1]**

**12.** In the light from a galaxy, a spectral line is measured with Δλ/λ = 0.0085. Take H0 = 68 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the recession speed of the galaxy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance to the galaxy in Mpc. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that this distance is about 1.2 × 10⁸ ly. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the Doppler equation is suitable here. **[1]**
**(e)** Compare the time the light has travelled with the age of the universe, taken as 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** AU: the mean distance from the Earth to the Sun [1]. Light-year: the distance light travels in a vacuum in one year [1]. Parsec: the distance at which a star shows a parallax of one second of arc (an AU subtends one second of arc) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A light-year must be described as a distance travelled by light in a vacuum in one year. Writing only "how far light goes in a year" is vague, and "a time" loses the idea completely.

**2.** Distance = 2.6 × 3.09 × 10¹⁶ = **8.0 × 10¹⁶ m** [1]. In ly: 8.03 × 10¹⁶ / 9.46 × 10¹⁵ = **8.5 ly** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded value from the first conversion for the second, and give units on both answers. A number with no unit is not a complete answer.

**3. (a)** d = 1/p = 1/0.045 = **22 pc** [1]. In ly: 22.2 × 3.09 × 10¹⁶ / 9.46 × 10¹⁵ = **73 ly** [1].
**(b)** Measure the position of the star relative to very distant stars [1]. Measure again six months later, when the Earth is on the opposite side of its orbit [1]. The parallax angle is half the angular shift in position between the two observations [1].
**(c)** For distant stars the parallax angle is too small to measure [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For the method, state both observation times and say "half" of the shift. The commonest slip is giving the full shift as p.

**4.** On a large scale the universe is homogeneous (the same average density and structure everywhere) [1]. It is isotropic (it looks the same in every direction) [1]. The laws of physics are the same everywhere (universal) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain each word rather than only naming it. Writing "uniform" for both homogeneous and isotropic does not separate them.

**5. (a)** Δλ = 405.1 − 393.4 = 11.7 nm [1]. v = c Δλ/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 11.7 / 393.4 [1]. v = **8.9 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** (the galaxy is receding) [1].
**(b)** Δλ = λ v/c = 500 × 25 × 10³ / 3.00 × 10⁸ [1]. Δλ = **0.042 nm**, a decrease (blue shift) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert km s⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ before using c. State the direction of the shift as well as its size, since the question gave a direction of motion.

**6. (a)** A straight line through the origin [1].
**(b)** v/d values are 75, 71.7, 72.2, 70.8 and 70.0, or a best-fit line through the origin [1]. H0 ≈ **71 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹** (accept about 70 to 72) [1].
**(c)** 1 Mpc = 3.09 × 10¹⁹ km, so H0 = 71 / 3.09 × 10¹⁹ [1] = **2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** t = 1/H0 = 1 / 2.30 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 4.35 × 10¹⁷ s [1]. In years: 4.35 × 10¹⁷ / 3.16 × 10⁷ = **1.4 × 10¹⁰ years** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A single end point is a weak basis for a gradient. Show that you used all the data, either by a best-fit line or by averaging the ratios. Carry the unrounded H0 into part (d).

**7.** The light from distant galaxies is shifted to longer wavelength (red shift) [1]. This is interpreted as the galaxies moving away from us [1]. The shift, and so the speed, increases with distance (Hubble's law) [1]. This is what a uniform expansion of space gives, as all separations grow [1]. Observers in any other galaxy would see the same pattern, so the Earth is not at a centre; the expansion has no centre [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Go beyond the observation to the reasoning. Stating that every observer would see the same pattern is the point to include.

**8.** The CMB is microwave radiation reaching the Earth equally from all directions [1]. It has a spectrum corresponding to a temperature of 2.7 K [1]. At the start the universe was extremely hot, packed tightly and full of radiation [1]. Expansion stretched the wavelength of this radiation and cooled it, which gives the observed low temperature; this matches a prediction of the Big Bang theory [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the observation to the theory. Describing the CMB without explaining why a hot early universe predicts it gives only part of the credit available.

**9.** t = 1/H0 = 1 / 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ [1] = **4.2 × 10¹⁷ s** [1]. In years: 4.17 × 10¹⁷ / 3.16 × 10⁷ = **1.3 × 10¹⁰ years** [1]. The estimate assumes a constant expansion rate (the rate may have changed), or H0 has experimental uncertainty [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason that is specific to this calculation. "Human error" is not a reason about the physics.

**10.** A model answer has three stages in the right order. After a few minutes, fusion forms light nuclei, mainly hydrogen and helium [1]. After a few hundred thousand years, the universe cools enough for electrons to join nuclei and form atoms [1]; radiation can then travel freely, and this is the cosmic microwave background [1]. Over the following hundreds of millions of years, gravity pulls gas together to form the first stars and galaxies [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use time-ordering words and approximate timescales. A list of events without an order, or with a wrong order, shows fewer correct points.

**11. (a)** Dark energy about 68% [1], dark matter about 27% and ordinary matter about 5% [1].
**(b)** Galaxies rotate faster than the visible matter alone can explain (or similar gravitational evidence) [1].
**(c)** The accelerating expansion of the universe [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentages need to be about right and in the right order. Do not say that dark energy and dark matter are the same thing.

**12. (a)** v = c Δλ/λ = 0.0085 × 3.00 × 10⁸ [1] = **2.6 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** (2550 km s⁻¹) [1].
**(b)** d = v/H0 = 2550 / 68 [1] = **38 Mpc** (37.5) [1].
**(c)** 37.5 × 10⁶ pc × 3.26 ly per pc [1] = 1.22 × 10⁸ ly, about 1.2 × 10⁸ ly [1].
**(d)** The speed is about 0.85% of c, so v is much less than c [1].
**(e)** The light travelled for about 1.2 × 10⁸ years [1], which is 0.9% of the age of the universe (1.2 × 10⁸ / 1.4 × 10¹⁰), a small fraction [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, show the unrounded value before the given figure. Keep distances and speeds in consistent units: km s⁻¹ with Mpc, then Mpc to ly.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Not converting km s⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ before using v/c.
* Quoting distances without units, or mixing pc, ly and m.
* Using the full shift instead of half the shift for parallax.
* Taking a gradient from one point rather than all the data.
* Leaving H0 in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ in t = 1/H0.
* Describing expansion as galaxies moving through space from a centre.
* Describing the CMB without linking it to the hot early universe.
* Mixing up dark matter and dark energy.
