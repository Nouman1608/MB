Section 11.2 of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028) has three learning outcomes. You must be able to name and draw the structural and displayed formulae of unbranched alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids and "the products of the reactions stated in sections 11.4–11.7", all "containing up to four carbon atoms per molecule"; state the type of compound from a name ending in -ane, -ene, -ol or -oic acid, or from a formula; and name and draw the unbranched esters made from alcohols and carboxylic acids with up to four carbon atoms each. 5070 has no Core/Extended split, so every outcome is examined for every candidate.

The syllabus names the alkenes but-1-ene and but-2-ene and the alcohols propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol specifically, so position numbers are examinable. The broader [Organic Chemistry: Formulae and Naming guide](/resources/organic-chemistry-formulae-and-naming/) introduces the stems and endings; this guide gives the full set of names you need, the numbering rules, the reaction products and a reliable method for esters in both directions.

## A name has two parts

| Stem (number of carbons) | | Ending (type of compound) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| meth- | 1 | -ane | alkane |
| eth- | 2 | -ene | alkene |
| prop- | 3 | -ol | alcohol |
| but- | 4 | -oic acid | carboxylic acid |

The stem counts **every** carbon in the longest chain, including the carbon in a –COOH group. So ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, has two carbons, and butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH, has four.

## Every compound you must name

| Carbons | Alkane | Alkene | Alcohol | Carboxylic acid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | methane CH₄ | (none) | methanol CH₃OH | methanoic acid HCOOH |
| 2 | ethane CH₃CH₃ | ethene CH₂=CH₂ | ethanol CH₃CH₂OH | ethanoic acid CH₃COOH |
| 3 | propane CH₃CH₂CH₃ | propene CH₃CH=CH₂ | propan-1-ol CH₃CH₂CH₂OH; propan-2-ol CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ | propanoic acid CH₃CH₂COOH |
| 4 | butane CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ | but-1-ene CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂; but-2-ene CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | butan-1-ol CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH; butan-2-ol CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃ | butanoic acid CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH |

## Position numbers

A number shows which carbon atom carries the functional group.

1. Number the carbon chain **from the end nearer the functional group**.
2. For an alkene, give the number of the **first** carbon of the C=C bond.
3. For an alcohol, give the number of the carbon carrying the –OH.
4. Put the number just before the ending, between hyphens: but-**1**-ene, propan-**2**-ol.

Some names never need a number. Ethene, propene and ethanol have only one possible position. In carboxylic acids the –COOH carbon is always carbon 1, so the name is just propanoic acid or butanoic acid.

**Worked example 1.** Name CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ and draw its displayed formula.

*Answer.* The chain has four carbons, so the stem is but-. The –OH makes it an alcohol: butanol. Numbering from the nearer end, the –OH is on carbon 2. Name: **butan-2-ol**.

```
      H   H   H   H
      |   |   |   |
  H - C - C - C - C - H
      |   |   |   |
      H   O   H   H
          |
          H
```

Numbering from the other end would give "butan-3-ol". This is never correct: always choose the lower number.

## Naming reaction products (sections 11.4–11.7)

Each reaction in the following sections gives a product you may be asked to name.

| Section | Reaction | Example product |
|---|---|---|
| 11.4 | alkane + chlorine in ultraviolet light (monosubstitution) | methane gives chloromethane, CH₃Cl; ethane gives chloroethane, CH₃CH₂Cl |
| 11.5 | alkene + bromine | ethene gives 1,2-dibromoethane, CH₂BrCH₂Br |
| 11.5 | alkene + hydrogen (nickel catalyst) | propene gives propane |
| 11.5 | alkene + steam (acid catalyst) | ethene gives ethanol |
| 11.6 | combustion of alcohols | carbon dioxide and water |
| 11.7 | carboxylic acid + metal, base or carbonate | ethanoic acid gives an ethanoate, e.g. sodium ethanoate, CH₃COONa |
| 11.7 | oxidation of ethanol | ethanoic acid |
| 11.7 | carboxylic acid + alcohol (acid catalyst) | an ester, e.g. ethyl ethanoate |

Two of these need care.

- **Bromine adds across the double bond**, so one bromine atom goes on each carbon of the former C=C. Propene gives 1,2-dibromopropane, CH₃CHBrCH₂Br; but-2-ene gives 2,3-dibromobutane, CH₃CHBrCHBrCH₃.
- **Salts of carboxylic acids** take the ending -oate: methanoate, ethanoate, propanoate, butanoate. The metal is named first, as with any salt: magnesium ethanoate, (CH₃COO)₂Mg.

## Naming esters

An ester is made from an alcohol and a carboxylic acid. Its name has two words:

- the **first word** comes from the **alcohol**, ending in -yl: methyl, ethyl, propyl, butyl
- the **second word** comes from the **acid**, ending in -oate: methanoate, ethanoate, propanoate, butanoate

The confusing part is that the structural formula is usually written **acid part first**, while the name puts the **alcohol part first**. In CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, the CH₃COO– part came from ethanoic acid and the –CH₂CH₃ part came from ethanol, so the name is ethyl ethanoate.

**Worked example 2.** Name the ester formed from propan-1-ol and ethanoic acid, and write its structural formula.

*Answer.* Alcohol part: propan-1-ol gives **propyl**. Acid part: ethanoic acid gives **ethanoate**. Name: **propyl ethanoate**. Structural formula: CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.

**Worked example 3.** Name the alcohol and the acid needed to make methyl butanoate, and draw the displayed formula of the ester.

*Answer.* Methyl comes from methanol; butanoate comes from butanoic acid. The ester is CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃.

```
      H   H   H   O       H
      |   |   |   ‖       |
  H - C - C - C - C - O - C - H
      |   |   |           |
      H   H   H           H
```

Check the ester by counting: the acid side has four carbons including the C=O carbon; the alcohol side has one.

## Identifying the type of compound

From a **name**, look at the ending: -ane alkane, -ene alkene, -ol alcohol, -oic acid carboxylic acid, -yl ...-oate ester.

From a **formula**, look for the functional group:

- C=C present: alkene
- –OH present, no C=O: alcohol
- –COOH at the end of the chain: carboxylic acid
- –COO– between two carbon groups: ester
- only C and H with single bonds: alkane

C₃H₇OH is an alcohol; C₂H₅COOH is a carboxylic acid; CH₃COOC₂H₅ is an ester, even though it contains the letters "COO" and "H".

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to count the –COOH carbon in the stem: CH₃COOH is ethanoic, not methanoic, acid.
- Writing "butan-3-ol" or "but-3-ene" instead of numbering from the nearer end.
- Putting a position number on propene, ethanol or a carboxylic acid where none is needed.
- Naming esters backwards, such as "ethanoate ethyl" or calling CH₃COOC₂H₅ "ethyl methanoate".
- Writing "ethanoic" for a salt; the salt is sodium ethan**oate**.
- Putting both bromine atoms on the same carbon in an addition product.

## Exam technique: the command words

- **Name**: spelling counts where it changes meaning; include the hyphens and numbers.
- **Draw**: displayed formulae need every atom and bond; structural formulae must still be unambiguous.
- **State** (type of compound): one word or phrase, such as "alcohol".
- **Deduce** (ester): name the alcohol and acid, then combine -yl and -oate.
