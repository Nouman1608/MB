## 5.1.1 Chromosome: the five features

| Feature | One-line description |
|---|---|
| DNA | One long molecule per chromatid |
| Histone proteins | DNA wound around them; allow condensation before division |
| Sister chromatids | Two identical copies made in S phase |
| Centromere | Joins sister chromatids; spindle attachment point |
| Telomeres | Repeated non-coding sequences at the ends |

- **Chromatin:** uncoiled DNA and histones between divisions, allowing transcription.
- **Condensed chromosome:** tightly coiled before mitosis, visible with a light microscope, moved without tangling.

### Counting rule

Count **chromosomes** by centromeres. Count **DNA molecules** by chromatids.

| Stage | Chromosomes (cell with 2n = 8) | DNA molecules |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | 8 | 8 |
| G2 to metaphase | 8 | 16 |
| Anaphase | 16 (two sets of 8) | 16 |
| Each daughter cell | 8 | 8 |

## 5.1.2 Importance of mitosis

Genetically identical daughter nuclei because each chromosome is copied exactly in S phase and each daughter gets one chromatid of every chromosome.

| Purpose | Example |
|---|---|
| Growth | Shoot tip cells dividing |
| Replacement | Cells lining the small intestine; red blood cells |
| Repair | New bone cells at a fracture; skin cells closing a cut |
| Asexual reproduction | *Hydra* budding; plant runners or tubers |

## 5.1.3 The cell cycle

| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| G1 | Growth; protein synthesis, including enzymes for replication; organelles made |
| S | DNA replication: one chromatid becomes two |
| G2 | Further growth; proteins for division (e.g. spindle proteins) made |
| Mitosis | Nuclear division into two identical nuclei |
| Cytokinesis | Cytoplasm divides |

**DNA content per cell:** 1 unit in G1 → rises through S → 2 units in G2 and mitosis → 1 unit in each daughter.

**Time in a phase** = (cells in phase ÷ total cells) × cycle length. Assumes cells divide at random times.

```
Example: 900 of 3 000 cells in S; cycle 24 h
900 / 3 000 = 0.30 -> 0.30 x 24 = 7.2 h
```

## 5.1.4 Telomeres

1. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only to the 3′ end.
2. The very end of each new strand cannot be fully copied.
3. A little DNA is lost from each end at every replication.
4. That loss comes from telomeres (non-coding), not genes.
5. Telomeres shorten with each division in most body cells.

## 5.1.5 Stem cells

- **Definition:** an unspecialised cell that can divide repeatedly by mitosis and whose daughters can differentiate into specialised cells.
- One daughter can remain a stem cell; the other differentiates.
- Bone marrow stem cells replace blood cells; intestinal and skin stem cells repair and replace lining cells.

## 5.1.6 Tumour formation in four steps

1. Mutation in a gene that controls the cell cycle.
2. Cell divides by mitosis repeatedly, without control.
3. All daughter cells inherit the mutation and divide without control.
4. A mass of cells forms: a tumour.

Risk factors raise mutation rate: ultraviolet radiation, ionising radiation, some chemicals.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chromatid | One of two identical copies of a replicated chromosome |
| Centromere | Region holding sister chromatids together |
| Histone | Protein that DNA is wound around |
| Telomere | Repeated non-coding DNA at a chromosome end |
| Interphase | G1, S and G2 together |
| Cytokinesis | Division of the cytoplasm |
| Stem cell | Unspecialised cell able to divide and give rise to specialised cells |
| Differentiation | A cell becoming specialised for a particular function |
| Tumour | A mass of cells produced by uncontrolled mitosis |

## Exam traps

- DNA replicates in S phase, not in mitosis.
- Before anaphase, two sister chromatids are still one chromosome.
- Telomeres stop **genes** being lost, not DNA.
- A stem cell must be able to divide **and** differentiate.
- A tumour needs a mutation and its inheritance by daughter cells.
- Interphase is not a resting phase.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the five features of a chromosome in the syllabus.
2. What is the role of histone proteins?
3. A cell has 12 chromosomes in G1. How many DNA molecules does it have in metaphase?
4. In which phase does DNA replicate?
5. Give two events of G1.
6. Why are daughter cells from mitosis genetically identical?
7. Give one example each of mitosis for repair and for asexual reproduction.
8. Why is a little DNA lost from chromosome ends at each replication?
9. Explain how telomeres prevent loss of genes.
10. Define a stem cell.
11. 400 of 2 500 cells have DNA content between 1 and 2 units. The cycle is 25 hours. How long is S phase?
12. Explain why a tumour grows even though only one cell first had the mutation.

### Answers

1. DNA, histone proteins, sister chromatids, centromere, telomeres.
2. DNA is wound around them, packaging it and allowing it to condense before division.
3. 24.
4. S phase.
5. Any two: growth; protein synthesis (including replication enzymes); organelles made.
6. Every chromosome is copied exactly in S phase and each daughter nucleus receives one chromatid of each chromosome.
7. Repair: bone cells at a fracture or skin closing a cut. Asexual reproduction: *Hydra* budding or plant runners.
8. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only to the 3′ end, so the very end of a new strand cannot be completed.
9. The DNA lost each round comes from the repeated non-coding telomere sequence, not from genes.
10. An unspecialised cell that can divide repeatedly by mitosis and give rise to cells that differentiate.
11. 400 ÷ 2 500 = 0.16; 0.16 × 25 = 4 hours.
12. Every daughter cell inherits the mutation, so all descendants also divide without control.
