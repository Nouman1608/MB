## Core definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monetary policy | Control of the money supply and interest rates by the central bank to influence aggregate demand |
| Inflation targeting | A published numerical inflation goal, with interest rates adjusted to meet it |
| Nominal interest rate | The rate quoted, not adjusted for inflation |
| Real interest rate | Nominal rate adjusted for inflation: nominal - inflation |
| Expansionary policy | Lower rates or a larger money supply: AD shifts right |
| Contractionary policy | Higher rates or a smaller money supply: AD shifts left |
| Recessionary (deflationary) gap | Actual real GDP below potential output |
| Inflationary gap | Actual real GDP above potential output |
| Minimum reserve requirement (HL only) | The fraction of deposits banks must hold as reserves |
| Quantitative easing (HL only) | Electronic creation of reserves to buy assets in bulk |

## Goals of monetary policy: recall list

1. Low and stable inflation, including inflation targeting.
2. Low unemployment.
3. Reducing business cycle fluctuations.
4. A stable environment for long-term growth.
5. External balance.

Potential conflict: pursuing goal 2 by cutting rates can threaten goal 1.

## Formulas and calculations

| Item | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Real interest rate | nominal rate - inflation rate | 5.5% - 2.0% = 3.5% |
| Inflation from an index | (new index - old index) / old index x 100 | (128.5 - 125.0) / 125.0 x 100 = 2.8% |
| Output gap (% of potential) | (actual - potential) / potential x 100 | (1,140 - 1,200) / 1,200 x 100 = -5% |
| Money multiplier (HL only) | 1 / reserve ratio | 1 / 0.125 = 8 |
| Maximum deposits (HL only) | initial deposit x multiplier | 500 x 10 = 5,000 at a 10% ratio |
| New money (HL only) | maximum deposits - initial deposit | 5,000 - 500 = 4,500 |

### Method: real interest rate

1. Write down the nominal rate.
2. Write down the inflation rate over the same period.
3. Subtract.
4. State whether the result is positive or negative, and what that means for savers and borrowers.

A saver earning 2.0% when inflation is 3.5% has a real return of 2.0% - 3.5% = -1.5%.

## Transmission: expansionary chain

Rate cut → cheaper borrowing and a smaller reward for saving → C and I rise → possibly a lower exchange rate, so X - M rises → AD shifts right → real GDP rises, unemployment falls, the price level rises.

Contractionary chain: the reverse.

Small worked reminder: a 150,000 tav loan at 4% costs 6,000 a year in interest; at 3% it costs 4,500, saving 1,500. Investment rule: go ahead if the expected return exceeds the borrowing rate, so a 4.5% project fails at 5.0% and passes at 3.5%.

## HL only: tools at a glance

| Tool | Expansionary | Contractionary |
|---|---|---|
| Open market operations | Buy bonds | Sell bonds |
| Minimum reserve requirement | Lower | Raise |
| Central bank minimum lending rate | Cut | Raise |
| Quantitative easing | Create reserves to buy assets | Stop, or sell the assets |

### Method: money creation (HL only)

1. Find the reserve ratio as a decimal.
2. Multiplier = 1 / ratio.
3. Maximum total deposits = initial deposit x multiplier.
4. New money created = total deposits - initial deposit.
5. Say it is a maximum: banks holding extra reserves or customers holding cash lower it.

Worked reminder: a deposit of 800 with a ratio of 12.5% gives a multiplier of 8, total deposits 6,400 and new money 5,600.

### Method: money market numbers (HL only)

With Md = 500 - 40r and a money supply of 300: 500 - 40r = 300, so r = 5%. If supply becomes 340: r = 4%. If it becomes 260: r = 6%.

## Effectiveness: evaluation grid

| Point | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Flexibility | Incremental, flexible, easily reversible | Fine-tuning needs good forecasts |
| Speed | Short time lags | The full effect still takes time |
| Low rates | Cheap credit supports spending | Little room to cut when rates are near zero |
| Confidence | Cheaper credit encourages spending if people are optimistic | Low consumer and business confidence blunts the effect |
| Inflation | Credible target anchors expectations | Weak against cost-push inflation without losing output |
| Unemployment | Lower rates raise AD | Does not solve structural unemployment |
| Growth | Stability helps long-term investment | Acts on demand, not on productive capacity |

For a good evaluation: make a point, support it with a reason, give a counter-point, then judge which matters more in the case given.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Nominal vs real rate.** Nominal ignores inflation; real subtracts it.
- **Expansionary vs contractionary.** AD right vs AD left.
- **Recessionary vs inflationary gap.** Output below potential vs above.
- **Money supply vs money demand (HL only).** The central bank sets supply; interest rates (the opportunity cost of holding cash) influence demand.
- **Demand-side vs supply-side.** Monetary policy shifts AD; it does not directly change the productive capacity of the economy.

## Quick self-test

1. Define monetary policy.
2. State two goals of monetary policy.
3. Nominal rate 7.0%, inflation 3.0%. Find the real rate.
4. Nominal rate 1.5%, inflation 2.5%. Find the real rate.
5. Which direction does AD move when the central bank raises rates?
6. A price index rises from 125.0 to 128.5. Find the inflation rate.
7. Give one reason why cutting rates may not raise spending in a deep recession.
8. State one strength of monetary policy listed in the guide.
9. (HL only) A reserve ratio is 20%. Find the money multiplier.
10. (HL only) A deposit of 500 and a ratio of 10%. Find the maximum new money created.
11. (HL only) With Md = 500 - 40r and a supply of 340, find r.
12. (HL only) Is open market selling of bonds expansionary or contractionary?

### Answers

1. Control of the money supply and interest rates by the central bank to influence the economy.
2. Any two of: low and stable inflation, low unemployment, reduced business cycle fluctuations, a stable environment for long-term growth, external balance.
3. 7.0% - 3.0% = **4.0%**.
4. 1.5% - 2.5% = **-1.0%**.
5. **Left** (contractionary), because C and I fall.
6. (128.5 - 125.0) / 125.0 x 100 = **2.8%**.
7. Low consumer or business confidence: households and firms may not borrow even when credit is cheap. Rates may also already be close to zero.
8. Incremental, flexible and easily reversible; or short time lags.
9. 1 / 0.20 = **5**.
10. Multiplier 10, so total deposits 5,000, new money 5,000 - 500 = **4,500**.
11. 500 - 40r = 340, so 40r = 160 and r = **4%**.
12. **Contractionary**: it removes reserves from banks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding inflation to the nominal rate instead of subtracting it.
- Leaving out the units or the sign: -1.5% is not 1.5%.
- Unlabelled diagrams: no axes, no equilibrium, no gap marked.
- Shifting AS when the policy is a change in interest rates.
- Describing the transmission chain with no link to AD at the end.
- One-sided answers to evaluation questions, with no counter-argument or judgement.
- Mixing up the money market diagram with the AD/AS diagram (the vertical axis is the interest rate there).
- Giving HL tools to an SL-only question without linking them to the question set.
- Writing "the central bank prints money" and stopping, with no effect on interest rates or lending.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is monetary policy in IB DP Economics?**
Monetary policy is the central bank's control of the money supply and interest rates to influence the economy. It is a demand-side policy and is sub-topic 3.5 of the IB DP Economics guide.

**What is the difference between the nominal and real interest rate?**
The nominal rate is the rate quoted, with no adjustment for inflation. The real interest rate takes inflation into account and is roughly the nominal rate minus the inflation rate.

**What are the HL tools of monetary policy?**
Open market operations, where the central bank buys or sells government bonds; changes to the minimum reserve requirement; and changes to the minimum lending rate it charges commercial banks.

**What are the goals of monetary policy?**
The goals are low and stable inflation, low unemployment, reducing business cycle fluctuations, a stable economic environment for long-term growth, and external balance.
