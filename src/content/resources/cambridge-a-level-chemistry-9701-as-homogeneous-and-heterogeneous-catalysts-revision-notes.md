These notes condense subtopic **8.3, Homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [8.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-homogeneous-and-heterogeneous-catalysts/). The [Reaction Kinetics revision notes](/resources/as-chem-kinetics-revision-notes/) cover Topic 8 as a whole.

## What you must be able to do

- Explain that with a catalyst a reaction has a **different mechanism, of lower activation energy**.
- Explain the effect using the **Boltzmann distribution**.
- **Construct and interpret** a pathway diagram with and without a catalyst.
- Classify a catalyst as homogeneous or heterogeneous.

## Core facts

- **Catalyst**: increases the rate of a reaction and is chemically unchanged at the end.
- **Catalysis**: the speeding up of a reaction by a catalyst.
- The catalyst **takes part**: it opens an alternative route, often through an intermediate, and is regenerated.
- Unchanged by a catalyst: **ΔH**, **position of equilibrium**, **equilibrium yield**, **temperature**, **the Boltzmann curve**.
- Forward and reverse Eₐ are both lowered by the **same amount**, so both rates rise by the same factor and equilibrium is reached sooner.

## Boltzmann distribution with a catalyst

- **One** curve (same temperature).
- **Two** Eₐ lines: Eₐ(uncatalysed) and Eₐ(catalysed), with Eₐ(catalysed) to the **left**.
- Larger area beyond Eₐ(catalysed) → greater proportion of molecules with enough energy → more frequent effective collisions → faster rate.

**Temperature moves the curve. A catalyst moves the line.**

## Pathway diagram checklist

- Axes: **enthalpy** (vertical) and **progress of reaction** (horizontal).
- Reactants and products at the **same levels** for both routes.
- Uncatalysed: one high hump.
- Catalysed: a lower path, often **two humps with a trough** (the trough is an intermediate).
- Eₐ measured from **reactants** to the **highest** point of each route.
- ΔH drawn once, from reactants to products.
- Reverse barrier: Eₐ(reverse) = Eₐ(forward) − ΔH.

**Quick example (invented values).** ΔH = −50 kJ mol⁻¹; uncatalysed Eₐ = +180 kJ mol⁻¹; catalysed humps at +95 and +70 kJ mol⁻¹ above the reactants. Catalysed Eₐ = **+95 kJ mol⁻¹**. Reduction = 85 kJ mol⁻¹. Reverse barriers: 230 and 145 kJ mol⁻¹, both lowered by 85 kJ mol⁻¹.

## The phase test

| Type | Definition | Examples from 9701 |
|---|---|---|
| **Homogeneous** | Catalyst in the **same phase** as the reactants | Concentrated H₂SO₄ in esterification; NO₂(g) in the oxidation of SO₂(g); Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺(aq) in the I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction |
| **Heterogeneous** | Catalyst in a **different phase** from the reactants | Fe(s) in the Haber process; Ni or Pt in alkene hydrogenation; Pt, Pd and Rh in catalytic converters |

Phase, not state: two immiscible liquids are two phases.

Showing NO₂ is a catalyst:

SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO, then NO + ½O₂ → NO₂. Overall: SO₂ + ½O₂ → SO₃. NO₂ is used, then regenerated, and is absent from the overall equation.

The step-by-step mode of action of each type (adsorption, bond weakening and desorption for heterogeneous catalysts) is A Level Topic 26 content.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | Substance that increases the rate of reaction and is unchanged chemically at the end |
| Catalysis | The process of increasing a rate using a catalyst |
| Alternative route (mechanism) | A different sequence of steps from reactants to products, with a lower Eₐ |
| Intermediate | A species formed in one step and used in a later step; a trough on a pathway diagram |
| Homogeneous catalyst | Catalyst in the same phase as the reactants |
| Heterogeneous catalyst | Catalyst in a different phase from the reactants |
| Phase | A region of uniform composition and properties, separated from others by a boundary |

## Exam traps

- "Lowers the activation energy" without "alternative route" or "different mechanism".
- Drawing a second Boltzmann curve for the catalysed reaction.
- Catalysed route ending at a different enthalpy level from the uncatalysed route.
- Measuring the catalysed Eₐ to the smaller hump, or adding the two humps.
- "The catalyst does not take part in the reaction."
- "A catalyst shifts the equilibrium to the right" or "increases the yield".
- Classifying by physical state rather than by phase.

## Self-test

1. Define a catalyst.
2. What does a catalyst provide that makes the reaction faster?
3. Describe how a catalyst is shown on a Boltzmann distribution.
4. Why is the Boltzmann curve unchanged when a catalyst is added?
5. What does a trough between two humps on a catalysed pathway represent?
6. Uncatalysed Eₐ = +210 kJ mol⁻¹; the catalysed route has humps of +120 and +85 kJ mol⁻¹ above the reactants. What is the catalysed Eₐ?
7. For question 6, ΔH = −40 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the reverse Eₐ for each route.
8. Why does a catalyst not change the position of equilibrium?
9. Define homogeneous catalysis.
10. Classify the nickel catalyst in the hydrogenation of an alkene, with a reason.
11. Show by adding equations that NO₂ acts as a catalyst in the oxidation of SO₂.
12. State two things that change when the temperature is raised but do not change when a catalyst is added.

**Answers:**

1. A substance that increases the rate of a reaction and is chemically unchanged at the end.
2. An alternative route (different mechanism) with a lower activation energy.
3. The same curve, with a second Eₐ line further to the left; the area beyond it is larger.
4. The temperature has not changed, so the distribution of molecular energies is the same.
5. An intermediate.
6. **+120 kJ mol⁻¹** (the highest point above the reactants).
7. Uncatalysed: 210 − (−40) = **250 kJ mol⁻¹**. Catalysed: 120 − (−40) = **160 kJ mol⁻¹**.
8. It lowers the forward and reverse activation energies by the same amount, so both rates increase by the same factor.
9. Catalysis in which the catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants.
10. Heterogeneous: solid nickel is in a different phase from the reactants.
11. SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO; NO + ½O₂ → NO₂. Adding: SO₂ + ½O₂ → SO₃, so NO₂ is regenerated and does not appear overall.
12. The shape of the Boltzmann curve, and the collision frequency (both change with temperature but not with a catalyst).
