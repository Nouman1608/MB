## Questions

**1.** Neutrons in a reactor coolant at 310 K are in thermal equilibrium. The mass of a neutron is 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
**(a)** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of a neutron in eV. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the corresponding speed. **[1]**

**2.** A reactor has k = 1.02, with 1000 fissions in one generation.
**(a)** Calculate the number of fissions per generation after a further 50 generations. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the operator brings k back to 1. **[2]**

**3.** A neutron of kinetic energy 1.5 MeV collides elastically and head-on with a helium-4 nucleus at rest, taken as 4 times the neutron mass.
**(a)** Show that the neutron keeps 0.36 of its kinetic energy. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of such collisions needed to reduce the energy to 0.030 eV. **[2]**

**4.** A gas-cooled reactor produces 600 MW of thermal power. The gas has specific heat capacity 1.1 × 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ and warms by 200 K.
**(a)** Calculate the mass of gas that must pass through the core each second. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one property that makes carbon dioxide suitable as a coolant and one drawback shown by your answer. **[2]**

**5.** A power station has electrical output 900 MW at 33% efficiency. Each fission releases 190 MeV.
**(a)** Calculate the thermal power. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many fissions occur each second. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the mass of uranium-235 (235 u per nucleus) that undergoes fission in one day. **[2]**

**6.** A reactor operates at 2400 MW thermal.
**(a)** Explain why spent fuel is moved by remote handling under water. **[2]**
**(b)** The control rods are fully inserted in an emergency. Immediately afterwards, decay heat is 4.0% of the full power. Calculate this power and explain why it still needs cooling. **[3]**

**7.** A store holds waste with a strontium-90 activity of 3.6 × 10¹⁵ Bq. The half-life is 28.8 years.
**(a)** Calculate the activity after 100 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time for the activity to fall to 1.0% of its initial value. **[2]**
**(c)** State one way this class of waste is stored. **[1]**

**8.** A country plans to replace coal-fired stations with fission reactors. Evaluate this plan. **[6]**

**9.** Compare two fusion reactions: deuterium with tritium, and helium-4 with helium-4. Treat each pair as touching spheres of radius R₀A^(1/3).
**(a)** Calculate the electrical potential energy for deuterium-tritium at closest approach. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the same energy for two helium-4 nuclei. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why hydrogen isotopes are the chosen fuel. **[2]**

**10.** A star radiates 1.6 × 10²⁷ W by turning hydrogen into helium. Atomic masses: H-1 1.007825 u and He-4 4.002603 u.
**(a)** Calculate the energy released, in MeV, when four hydrogen atoms become one helium atom. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of hydrogen consumed per second. Proton mass 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of the hydrogen mass converted to energy. **[2]**

**11.** In a magnetic confinement reactor a deuteron of kinetic energy 20 keV (mass 3.344 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) moves at right angles to a field of 4.0 T.
**(a)** Calculate the radius of its path. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the temperature for which the mean kinetic energy is 20 keV. **[1]**
**(c)** State two problems that must be solved to make a practical fusion reactor. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** KE = (3/2)kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 310 = 6.42 × 10⁻²¹ J [1]. Divide by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ to get **0.040 eV** [1].
**(b)** v = √(2 × 6.42 × 10⁻²¹ ÷ 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷) = **2.8 × 10³ m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bare answer in joules when eV is asked is a common slip, so write the conversion line.

**2. (a)** Fissions = 1000 × 1.02⁵⁰ [1] = **2.7 × 10³** [1].
**(b)** Control rods are pushed further into the core [1]. They absorb neutrons, so fewer cause fission and k falls to 1 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say that the rods absorb neutrons. Saying they slow them is a different component's job.

**3. (a)** Momentum: m u = m v + 4m V [1]. Elastic: speed of approach equals speed of separation, u = V − v [1]. Then v = −0.6u, and KE kept = (0.6)² = **0.36** [1].
**(b)** n = ln(1.5 × 10⁶ ÷ 0.030)/ln(1/0.36) = 17.73 ÷ 1.0217 = 17.35 [1], so **18 collisions** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer tends to need each line of the derivation, and a number of collisions is rounded up.

**4. (a)** P = (m/t) c ΔT [1]. m/t = 600 × 10⁶ ÷ (1.1 × 10³ × 200) = **2.7 × 10³ kg s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** It absorbs few neutrons (or does not corrode the core) [1]. Its specific heat capacity is low, so a very large mass flow is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* The drawback should be tied to the value you calculated, not stated generally.

**5. (a)** 900 ÷ 0.33 = **2.73 × 10³ MW** [1].
**(b)** Energy per fission = 190 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 3.04 × 10⁻¹¹ J [1]. Rate = 2.73 × 10⁹ ÷ 3.04 × 10⁻¹¹ = **9.0 × 10¹⁹ s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Mass of one nucleus = 235 × 1.661 × 10⁻²⁷ = 3.90 × 10⁻²⁵ kg. Mass = 8.97 × 10¹⁹ × 86 400 × 3.90 × 10⁻²⁵ [1] = **3.0 kg** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Efficiency applies to the electrical output. Dividing by it gives thermal power, and multiplying is a classic error.

**6. (a)** The fuel is intensely radioactive, so people must be kept away [1]. Water absorbs radiation and removes the heat the fuel still produces [1].
**(b)** 0.040 × 2400 MW = **96 MW** [1]. Fission products continue to decay [1], releasing energy that would melt the fuel without cooling [1].
*Examiner insight:* A good answer links decay heat to fission products, not to the chain reaction still running.

**7. (a)** A = 3.6 × 10¹⁵ × (1/2)^(100 ÷ 28.8) [1] = **3.2 × 10¹⁴ Bq** [1].
**(b)** (1/2)ⁿ = 0.010, n = ln 100/ln 2 = 6.64 [1]. Time = 6.64 × 28.8 = **1.9 × 10² years** [1].
**(c)** High-level waste is cooled, sealed in glass and steel and stored deep underground [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the number of half-lives. It is easy to check, and it protects against an arithmetic slip.

**8.** Benefits: far more energy per kilogram of fuel [1]; no carbon dioxide from the reactor in normal operation [1]. Drawbacks: accidents are rare but severe and widespread [1]; long-lived radioactive waste needs safe storage for a very long time [1]. Weighing: the risk is probability multiplied by consequence, and strict safety systems reduce the probability [1]. Judgement: a reasoned conclusion, for example that the plan is justified if waste storage and safety are guaranteed, or that it needs careful long-term planning [1].
*Examiner insight:* Extended answers tend to earn credit for balance and a clear conclusion, not for a long list.

**9. (a)** r = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ × (2^⅓ + 3^⅓) = 3.24 × 10⁻¹⁵ m [1]. Ep = (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 3.24 × 10⁻¹⁵) = **7.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ J** [1].
**(b)** r = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 2 × 4^⅓ = 3.81 × 10⁻¹⁵ m [1]. Ep = 4 × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 3.81 × 10⁻¹⁵) = **2.4 × 10⁻¹³ J** [1].
**(c)** The helium barrier is about 3.4 times larger [1]. Hydrogen nuclei have one proton each, so less repulsion and a lower temperature is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* For helium, remember each nucleus has charge 2e, so the charge product is 4e² and not 2e².

**10. (a)** Δm = 4 × 1.007825 − 4.002603 = 0.028697 u [1]. Energy = 0.028697 × 931.5 = **26.7 MeV** [1].
**(b)** Energy per helium = 26.7 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 4.28 × 10⁻¹² J. Reactions per second = 1.6 × 10²⁷ ÷ 4.28 × 10⁻¹² = 3.74 × 10³⁸ [1]. Hydrogen = 4 × 3.74 × 10³⁸ × 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ = **2.5 × 10¹² kg s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Fraction = 0.028697 ÷ (4 × 1.007825) [1] = 0.00712, which is **0.71%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the full masses until the subtraction, because rounding early destroys a small difference.

**11. (a)** KE = 20 × 10³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ J, so v = √(2 × 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ ÷ 3.344 × 10⁻²⁷) = 1.38 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ [1]. Bqv = mv²/r so r = mv/(Bq) [1]. r = 3.344 × 10⁻²⁷ × 1.38 × 10⁶ ÷ (4.0 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **7.2 × 10⁻³ m** [1].
**(b)** T = 2KE/(3k) = 2 × 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ ÷ (3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³) = **1.5 × 10⁸ K** [1].
**(c)** Any two: heating the plasma to about 10⁸ K without touching walls; confining it long enough at high density; neutron damage and activation of the structure; tritium supply [2].
*Examiner insight:* Name the problems precisely. "It is hard" earns nothing without the physics reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up moderator, control rods and coolant.
- Not rounding up a number of collisions.
- Forgetting the 1/2 when two nuclei share the Coulomb energy.
- Using Celsius for temperature in (3/2)kT.
- Using charge 1 for helium nuclei.
- Not converting MeV to joules, or hours and years to seconds.
- Describing waste without naming a class.
- Listing risks and benefits without any judgement.
