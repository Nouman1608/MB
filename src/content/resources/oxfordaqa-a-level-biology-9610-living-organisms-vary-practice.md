## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Distinguish between intraspecific variation and interspecific variation. **[2]**

**2.** The heights of eight seedlings were 14, 17, 15, 19, 16, 18, 15 and 16 cm. Calculate the mean height and the range. **[2]**

**3.** Which of the following describes a random sample?

A: Individuals are chosen because they are easy to reach.

B: Every individual has an equal chance of being chosen.

C: The largest individuals are chosen first.

D: Individuals are chosen to give the same number from each group. **[1]**

**4.** In a normal distribution, approximately what percentage of values lies within two standard deviations of the mean? **[1]**

**5.** State why a sample of 5 individuals is less likely to represent a population than a sample of 100. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** A student investigated the number of daisy plants in a field of 20 m by 20 m.

**(a)** Describe how the student could place quadrats at random in the field. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the student's results from two separate sets of 10 quadrats gave different means, although the sampling was random. **[2]**

**7.** The mean tail length of a species of mouse was measured on an island and on the mainland. Forty mice were measured in each place.

| Population | Mean tail length / mm | Standard deviation / mm |
|---|---|---|
| Island | 82.0 | 3.0 |
| Mainland | 86.5 | 3.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the range of values that includes approximately 95 % of the island mice. Assume a normal distribution. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the same range for the mainland mice. **[1]**

**(c)** A student concludes that island mice and mainland mice have different tail lengths. Use your answers to evaluate this conclusion. **[3]**

**8.** Seedlings of the same species were grown from seeds taken from a single parent plant, so they were genetically very similar. Eight seedlings were grown in full sun and eight were grown in shade. The mean height of those in the sun was 145.3 mm and the mean height of those in the shade was 172.3 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference in mean height between the shade seedlings and the sun seedlings, relative to the sun seedlings. **[2]**

**(b)** What does this suggest about the cause of the difference in height? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** A second student says that the difference is genetic. Suggest what further evidence would be needed to test this idea. **[2]**

**9.** Two samples of ten leaves each had a mean length of 45.0 mm. The standard deviation of sample A was 1.8 mm and that of sample B was 5.8 mm. Explain what this shows about the two samples. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how an investigator could use measurements to decide whether two groups of birds belong to the same species or to different species, and state one limitation of this approach. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Intraspecific variation is variation between members of the same species [1]. Interspecific variation is variation between members of different species [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Mean = 130 / 8 = 16.25 cm [1]. Range = 19 - 14 = 5 cm [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** About 95 % [1]. **[1]**

**5.** A small sample is more affected by chance, so a few extreme individuals can change the mean greatly [1]. A larger sample reduces the effect of chance, so its mean is more likely to be close to the population mean [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Lay out two tape measures along two sides of the field to form a grid [1]. Use a random number generator or table to obtain pairs of coordinates [1]. Place a quadrat at each coordinate and count the daisy plants inside [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Each set of quadrats is a different sample, and the plants in them differ by chance [1]. Only ten quadrats were used in each set, so each mean is likely to differ from the true mean for the field [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 82.0 plus or minus (2 x 3.0) [1] = 76.0 to 88.0 mm [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 86.5 plus or minus (2 x 3.5) = 79.5 to 93.5 mm [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** The means differ by 4.5 mm, which suggests that tail lengths differ [1]. However, the 95 % ranges overlap between 79.5 and 88.0 mm, so many mice from the two places have similar tail lengths [1]. The conclusion is supported on average but not for individuals, and a statistical test would be needed to show that the difference is unlikely to be due to chance [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 172.3 - 145.3 = 27.0 mm [1]. 27.0 / 145.3 x 100 = 18.6 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The difference is probably caused by the environment (light) [1], because the seedlings are genetically very similar, so a difference in height in different environments cannot be due mainly to different genes [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Grow genetically different seedlings (from different parents) in the same environment and compare heights [1]. If they differ, genetic factors are involved [1]. (Also accept: repeat with larger samples and use a statistical test.) **[2]**

**9.** The means are the same, so the centres of the two samples are identical [1]. The standard deviation of B is much larger, so the leaf lengths in B are much more spread out [1]. Sample B is therefore more variable, and a comparison using the mean alone would hide this difference [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Measure a characteristic, such as wing length, in a large random sample from each group [1]. Calculate the mean and standard deviation for each [1]. If the means differ and the ranges barely overlap, the groups are likely to be different species [1]. Limitation: the difference could be due to environmental factors, not genetic ones, or the groups may overlap so that individuals cannot be classified from one characteristic [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using intraspecific and interspecific the wrong way round.
- Describing random sampling without a random number method.
- Comparing means without commenting on spread.
- Saying that the cause is genetic or environmental without reasoning from the design of the experiment.
- Forgetting the units.
