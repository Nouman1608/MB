**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Early preaching of Islam: "The private and public preaching and Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) invitation to Islam, his call to Tawhid and rejection of idol worshiping" and "The early teachings of Islam, including the rights for women, the outlawing of alcohol and gambling."** The page follows the Prophet's (pbuh) mission from the first quiet invitations to the open call, then explains the main teachings of this early period and how to use the prescribed passage, Qur'an 1:1-7 (Surah al-Fatihah).

## The private phase

After the first revelation and the pause, the Prophet (pbuh) began to call people to Islam in secret. This phase lasted about three years. He chose to speak first to the people closest to him, who knew his character.

| Early believer | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hazrat Khadija (RA) | First to accept; supported him with her wealth and trust |
| Hazrat Ali (RA) | A boy of about ten in his household |
| Hazrat Zayd ibn Harithah (RA) | His freed servant and adopted son |
| Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) | His closest friend, who invited others to Islam |

Through Abu Bakr (RA), several notable men accepted Islam, including Uthman ibn Affan, Zubair ibn al-Awwam, Abdur Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Talha ibn Ubaydullah (may Allah be pleased with them). Many early believers were young, poor, or without tribal protection. They included slaves such as **Bilal** and people such as Ammar and his parents.

**Why was it private?** There were three reasons. The new community was tiny and weak. The Quraysh were powerful and might attack before the believers were ready. The Prophet (pbuh) also needed time to teach the first believers the Qur'an and how to pray. The small group met in secret, and many studied at the house of **al-Arqam** near Mount Safa. This place was useful because it was close to the Ka'aba but did not attract attention. Muslims prayed in the valleys away from view.

## The public phase

After about three years, the Prophet (pbuh) was told to make the message public. The Qur'an commands him to warn his closest relatives (26:214) and then to "declare what you are commanded" (15:94).

1. **The meal for his family.** He invited the clan of Banu Hashim to a meal and called them to Islam. Only Ali (RA), a boy, offered his support at that meeting.
2. **The call from Mount Safa.** He climbed Mount Safa and called the Quraysh. When they gathered, he asked them whether they would believe him if he said an army was behind the hill. They replied that they would, because he had never lied to them. He then told them that he was a warner of a great punishment, and that they must worship Allah alone. His uncle **Abu Lahab** replied with insults, which is why Surah al-Masad (111) was later revealed about him.
3. **Open preaching.** From then on he spoke at the Ka'aba and during the pilgrimage season, and he met tribal leaders and visitors to Makkah.

His method was a calm and honest invitation. The Qur'an says to "invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction" (16:125).

## The call to Tawhid and the rejection of idols

The heart of the message was **Tawhid**, the oneness of Allah. "There is no god but Allah" was the central claim. It meant:

- **Allah alone creates, sustains and deserves worship.** No idol, spirit or person shares in this.
- **Idols are powerless.** They cannot hear, help or harm. The Qur'an says the idols are only names the Arabs invented (53:23).
- **Worship goes straight to Allah.** There is no need for a go-between.
- **All people are accountable to Allah.** On the Day of Judgement, each person answers for their own actions.

For the Quraysh, this was shocking. It condemned the faith of their ancestors, and it threatened the status of the Ka'aba as a centre of pilgrimage and income. This is the origin of the opposition studied in the next topic.

## Surah al-Fatihah as the prescribed passage

Qur'an 1:1-7 is the opening chapter of the Qur'an. It is recited in every unit of the daily prayer. It states the beliefs that the Prophet (pbuh) was teaching.

| Words | Link to early preaching |
|---|---|
| "In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful" | Allah's mercy; the invitation was one of compassion |
| "[All] praise is [due] to Allah, Lord of the worlds" | Tawhid: one Lord of all |
| "Sovereign of the Day of Recompense" | Accountability on the Day of Judgement |
| "It is You we worship and You we ask for help" | Worship of Allah alone; rejection of idols |
| "Guide us to the straight path" | The need for revelation and guidance |
| "not of those who have earned [Your] anger or of those who are astray" | The warning against rejecting the truth or going astray |

## Early teachings: the rights of women

The early message brought a change in the position of women, many of whom had few rights.

- **Ending infanticide.** The Qur'an forbade the burying of girls alive (17:31; 81:8-9).
- **Equal worth before Allah.** Men and women will be rewarded for their deeds alike (3:195; 33:35).
- **Inheritance.** Women were given a fixed share of inheritance (4:7; 4:11), instead of being treated as property to be inherited.
- **Property and mahr.** A woman owns her wealth, and the bridegroom must give her a marriage gift (mahr) which belongs to her (4:4).
- **Consent in marriage.** A woman must agree to marry.
- **Respect.** The Prophet (pbuh) taught kindness to daughters, wives and mothers. Asked who most deserved his good company, he answered "your mother" three times before naming the father.

Some of these detailed rules were revealed more fully in Madinah. In an exam answer, state clearly that these were teachings of Islam from its early period onwards and link them to a source.

## Early teachings: alcohol and gambling

In Arabia, drinking and betting with arrows were common. Both led to quarrels, poverty and neglect of family and prayer. Islam prohibited them in **stages**.

1. **Early guidance.** The Qur'an said that in both there is great sin and some benefit, but their sin is greater (2:219). Believers were told not to pray while intoxicated (4:43).
2. **Final prohibition.** Alcohol and gambling were called "defilement from the work of Satan", and believers were told to avoid them (5:90-91).

The reasons given are that alcohol and gambling cause hatred, enmity and distraction from the remembrance of Allah and prayer. Today Muslims avoid alcohol, gambling, betting and lotteries.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the Prophet (pbuh) began by preaching in private. **[4]**

> The group of believers was small and weak [1], so open preaching could have led the powerful Quraysh to attack it before it was ready [1]. He also needed time to teach the first Muslims the Qur'an and how to pray [1], so that they would be a firm community when the message became public [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Saying that Islam began with a public speech.** There were about three years of private preaching first.
- **Giving the private and public phases the wrong order or length.**
- **Writing that alcohol was banned in one step.** The ban came in stages.
- **Listing women's rights without sources.** Link each right to a verse.
- **Describing Tawhid only as "one God".** Explain what it meant for idols, worship and accountability.

## Exam technique

**Give** questions want a name or term (al-Arqam, Tawhid). **Explain** questions need a teaching and its effect. In the 12-mark question on Surah al-Fatihah, quote "Lord of the worlds" for Tawhid, "It is You we worship" for rejecting idols, and "the straight path" for guidance. Evaluate: the surah is a summary of the early message, but the details of daily life, such as women's rights and the ban on alcohol, are found in other parts of the Qur'an.
