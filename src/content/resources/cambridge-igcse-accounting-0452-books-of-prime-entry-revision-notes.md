Condensed notes for sub-topic **2.3 Books of prime entry**. A full three-column cash book and petty cash book are worked in the [2.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-books-of-prime-entry/); test yourself with the [2.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-books-of-prime-entry-practice/).

## What 2.3 asks for

- Processing data in the seven books: cash book, petty cash book, sales journal, purchases journal, sales returns journal, purchases returns journal, general journal.
- Posting ledger entries from them.
- Trade discount and cash discount.
- The dual function of the cash book.
- Receipts and payments by cash, cheques, debit and credit cards, online and bank transfers.
- The purpose and operation of the imprest system.
- Benefits and limitations of keeping cash at the business property, and of manual and digital original entry.

## Posting rules

| Book | Individual entries | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Debit each customer (sales ledger) | Credit sales |
| Purchases journal | Credit each supplier (purchases ledger) | Debit purchases |
| Sales returns journal | Credit each customer | Debit sales returns |
| Purchases returns journal | Debit each supplier | Credit purchases returns |
| Cash book, debit side | Credit the account named in details | |
| Cash book, credit side | Debit the account named in details | |
| Cash book, discount allowed column | Credit each customer | Debit discount allowed |
| Cash book, discount received column | Debit each supplier | Credit discount received |
| Petty cash book | | Debit each analysis column total to its expense |
| General journal | Post each debit and credit as written | |

**Memory aid:** the journals list amounts *owed*; the cash book lists money that *moves*.

## The general journal

Used for transactions with no other book, such as:

- opening entries for a new set of books;
- buying or selling non-current assets on credit;
- correcting errors;
- writing off irrecoverable debts;
- year-end transfers and adjustments.

Layout: date, account debited, account credited (indented), amounts, then a **narrative** explaining the entry.

## The cash book

**Dual function:** a book of prime entry *and* the ledger account for cash and bank.

**Columns:** discount, cash and bank on each side. Debit side: money in, discount allowed. Credit side: money out, discount received.

| Method | Column |
|---|---|
| Notes and coins | Cash |
| Cheque | Bank |
| Debit card or credit card | Bank |
| Online payment or bank transfer | Bank |

**Contra entries:** cash paid into the bank (debit bank, credit cash), or cash withdrawn from the bank for the office (debit cash, credit bank). Mark them with a "C" or "contra"; no ledger posting.

**Balancing:** cash and bank are balanced; discount columns are only totalled. A credit balance in the bank column is an overdraft. The cash column can never have a credit balance.

## Discounts

- **Trade discount:** deducted on the invoice; never recorded.
- **Cash discount:** recorded only when payment is made within the terms; customer's account is credited with the payment and the discount.

**Example:** a customer owes 1,600 and pays within the period with 3% discount. Discount = 48; bank receives 1,552; customer credited 1,552 + 48 = 1,600.

## Imprest system

1. Petty cashier receives a fixed float.
2. Every payment needs a signed voucher.
3. Vouchers are entered in the petty cash book with analysis columns.
4. At the period end, the main cashier pays in the total spent, restoring the float.

**Purpose:** control (cash + vouchers = float at all times), fewer interruptions for the main cashier, and only column totals are posted.

**Example:** float 200; spent 163.45; cash left 36.55; reimbursement 163.45.

## Cash at the business property

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Immediate payment of small expenses | Theft or loss |
| Change for customers | Counting errors |
| Serves customers who pay cash | Time to bank takings |
| No bank charges on small amounts | May exceed insurance cover |

## Manual and digital original entry

| | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Manual | Low cost, no power needed, simple | Slow, arithmetic errors, hard to copy, single copy at risk |
| Digital | Automatic totals and postings, fast reports, fewer errors, transactions imported from the bank | Set-up and training costs, needs power and internet, hacking and data loss, input errors spread |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book of prime entry | Where a transaction is first recorded before ledger posting |
| Narrative | A short explanation written under a general journal entry |
| Dual function | The cash book acting as both prime entry book and ledger account |
| Contra entry | A cash book entry with both sides in the cash book |
| Imprest | A fixed petty cash float restored to the same amount each period |
| Petty cash voucher | The source document for a petty cash payment |
| Analysis column | A column grouping petty cash payments of one type |
| Discount allowed | Cash discount given to customers; an expense |
| Discount received | Cash discount received from suppliers; income |

## Exam traps

- Credit purchases of **non-current assets** go in the general journal, not the purchases journal.
- **Card receipts** go in the bank column.
- Restore petty cash with the **amount spent**, not the cash remaining.
- Discount allowed is on the **debit** side of the cash book but is an **expense**.
- A "C" contra must appear on **both** sides.

## Self-test

**1.** Which book records goods returned by a credit customer?

**2.** How is the total of the purchases journal posted?

**3.** State the dual function of the cash book.

**4.** A customer pays by credit card. Which column is used?

**5.** Cash of 500 is paid into the bank. Give the cash book entries.

**6.** A supplier is owed 2,000 and is paid within the terms with 2.5% discount. How much is paid and how much is debited to the supplier?

**7.** How is the total of the discount allowed column posted?

**8.** Float 120; vouchers total 94.30. How much is reimbursed?

**9.** State the purpose of the imprest system.

**10.** Give one benefit and one limitation of keeping cash on the premises.

**11.** Where is a van bought on credit first recorded?

**12.** Give one limitation of digital original entry.

## Self-test answers

**1.** The sales returns journal.

**2.** Debit the purchases account with the total.

**3.** It is a book of prime entry and the ledger account for cash and bank.

**4.** The bank column.

**5.** Debit bank column 500, credit cash column 500 (contra).

**6.** Paid 1,950; supplier debited 1,950 + 50 discount received = 2,000.

**7.** Debited to the discount allowed account.

**8.** 94.30.

**9.** To control petty cash: cash plus vouchers always equals the float, so shortages are found quickly, and small payments are handled by one person.

**10.** Benefit: small expenses paid immediately, change for customers. Limitation: theft, loss or miscounting.

**11.** The general journal.

**12.** Any one: cost of software and training; needs power and internet; risk of hacking or data loss; one input error affects all reports.
