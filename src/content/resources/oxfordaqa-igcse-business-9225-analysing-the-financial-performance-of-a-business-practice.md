All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Halden Hardware.

> **Case study: Halden Hardware**
>
> Halden Hardware is a chain of shops selling tools and building
> supplies. Its income statements for the last two years are below.
>
> | | Last year ($) | This year ($) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Revenue | 840,000 | 900,000 |
> | Cost of sales | 546,000 | 603,000 |
> | Gross profit | 294,000 | (a) |
> | Expenses | 210,000 | 225,000 |
> | Net profit | 84,000 | (b) |
>
> A competitor of similar size has a gross profit margin of 36% and a
> net profit margin of 9%. Halden's statement of financial position at the
> end of this year shows: non-current assets $400,000; inventories
> $90,000; receivables $45,000; cash $15,000; current liabilities $80,000;
> and a non-current bank loan of $150,000. The owner says that this year
> was better than last year because revenue and profit both rose.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an asset of a business? **[1]**
- A. A bank loan
- B. Money owed to suppliers
- C. Machinery
- D. An overdraft

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a non-current liability? **[1]**
- A. A long-term bank loan
- B. Money owed to a supplier due next month
- C. Cash in the bank
- D. Stock held for resale

**3.** State **two** users of financial statements. **[2]**

**4.** Define the term "gross profit". **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why the statement of financial position is described as a "snapshot in time". **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the figures shown as (a) and (b) in this year's income statement. **[4]**

**7.** Calculate Halden's gross profit margin for last year and for this year. **[4]**

**8.** Calculate Halden's net profit margin for this year, to one decimal place if needed. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate Halden's total assets, total liabilities and net assets (equity) at the end of this year. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why the gross profit margin may have fallen. **[2]**

**11.** Compare Halden's performance this year with the competitor's, using your answers to Questions 7 and 8. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the owner's statement that this year was better than last year. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: owners or shareholders [1]; managers [1]; employees [1]; banks or lenders [1]; suppliers [1]; the government [1]; potential investors [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Revenue minus cost of sales [2]. Award 1 mark for "profit before expenses are deducted". **[2]**

**5.** It shows the assets, liabilities and equity on a single date [1], so the figures would be different a week later, unlike the income statement, which covers a whole year [1]. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Gross profit = $900,000 - $603,000 = **$297,000** [2 marks: 1 for method, 1 for answer]; (b) net profit = $297,000 - $225,000 = **$72,000** [2 marks: 1 for method, 1 for answer]. **[4]**

**7.** Last year: 294,000 / 840,000 [1] x 100 = **35%** [1]. This year: 297,000 / 900,000 [1] x 100 = **33%** [1]. **[4]**

**8.** 72,000 / 900,000 x 100 [1] = **8%** [1]. Last year's net margin was 84,000 / 840,000 x 100 = 10% (not required). **[2]**

**9.** Current assets = 90,000 + 45,000 + 15,000 = $150,000; total assets = $400,000 + $150,000 = **$550,000** [2]. Total liabilities = $80,000 + $150,000 = **$230,000** [1]. Net assets = $550,000 - $230,000 = **$320,000** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The cost of sales rose faster than revenue [1], because Halden may have paid more for its goods or cut its selling prices, so it kept less of each dollar of sales as gross profit [1]. Cost of sales rose by 10.4% (from $546,000 to $603,000), while revenue rose by 7.1%. **[2]**

**11.** Gross margin: Halden 33% compared with the competitor's 36% [1], so Halden keeps less gross profit on each dollar of sales than the competitor [1]. Net margin: Halden 8% compared with the competitor's 9% [1], so Halden is slightly less profitable overall [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **Supporting the owner:** revenue rose by 7.1% from $840,000 to $900,000 [1]; gross profit rose from $294,000 to $297,000 [1]; the business is still making a profit of $72,000, and has more assets than liabilities (net assets $320,000) [1].
**Against the owner:** net profit fell from $84,000 to $72,000, a fall of 14.3% [1]; the net margin fell from 10% to 8% [1]; the gross margin fell from 35% to 33% [1]; expenses rose by 7.1%, as fast as revenue, and cost of sales rose faster [1].
**Judgement:** the owner is wrong, because profit and both margins fell, even though revenue rose [1]. The business sold more but kept less of each sale, and is below the competitor on both margins [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing gross profit by cost of sales or by expenses, instead of by revenue.
- Writing that profit rose because revenue rose, without checking the net profit figure.
- Treating a bank loan of $150,000 as an asset.
- Confusing net assets with total assets.
- Giving a verdict without any figures from the income statement.
