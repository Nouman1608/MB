**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.8 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Supporters of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh): "The support given to Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) and the Muhajirun by the Ansaar in Madinah and the early Sahabah (companions), including Hazrat Ayub Ansari (RA), Hazrat Hamza (RA), Hazrat Talha (RA) and Hazrat Zubair (RA)."** The page explains who the Ansaar and Muhajirun were, how the people of Madinah helped, and what each of the four named companions did. It ends with the prescribed passage, a hadith from Jami at-Tirmidhi about the generosity of Umar (RA) and Abu Bakr (RA) (may Allah be pleased with them).

## Who were the supporters?

| Group | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Muhajirun | "The emigrants": Muslims who left Makkah and moved to Madinah |
| Ansaar | "The helpers": Muslims of Madinah from the Aws and Khazraj tribes who welcomed them |
| Sahabah | The companions of the Prophet (pbuh): those who met him, believed in him and died as Muslims |

The Sahabah were the first generation of Muslims. They protected the Prophet (pbuh), learned the Qur'an from him, and passed on his teaching. Their examples of courage, loyalty and generosity are studied by Muslims as models.

## The Ansaar and the Muhajirun

The Muhajirun came to Madinah with almost nothing. Most had left homes, lands and businesses, and some had been separated from their families. The Ansaar of Madinah responded with remarkable generosity.

- **Homes.** Families opened their houses and shared rooms and food.
- **Brotherhood.** Each Muhajir was paired with an Ansari as a brother. The Ansaar offered to share their property, orchards and even their wealth. Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' (RA) offered Abdur Rahman ibn Awf (RA) half of his wealth and one of his wives to marry after her divorce; Abdur Rahman (RA) refused, and asked only to be shown the market, where he began trading and soon supported himself.
- **Work and land.** The Ansaar shared the harvest of date orchards with the migrants.
- **Protection.** The Ansaar had pledged at Aqaba to protect the Prophet (pbuh) as they protected their own families, and they kept that promise.

The Qur'an praises their attitude: "those who were settled in the Home [Madinah] and [adopted] the faith before them, they love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given, but give them preference over themselves, even though they are in privation" (59:9). The word "preference" shows the main virtue, *ithar*, which means putting the needs of others before your own.

**Why this support mattered:**

- It saved the migrants from hunger and homelessness.
- It ended the idea that loyalty belonged only to the tribe and replaced it with loyalty in faith.
- It made it possible for the Prophet (pbuh) to build a community in Madinah.
- It gave later generations a model of generosity.

## Four early companions

### Hazrat Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (RA)

Abu Ayyub (Khalid ibn Zayd) was from the Banu Najjar of the Khazraj, a clan related to the Prophet's (pbuh) grandfather Abdul Mutalib through his mother. When the Prophet's (pbuh) camel knelt near his house, Abu Ayyub (RA) carried the Prophet's (pbuh) luggage inside, and the Prophet (pbuh) stayed in his house for about seven months until the mosque and its rooms were ready.

- **Generous host.** Out of respect, he and his wife first tried to live on the upper floor, so as not to walk above the Prophet (pbuh). The Prophet (pbuh) chose the ground floor to make it easier for visitors.
- **Loyal fighter.** He took part in Badr, Uhud, the Trench and later campaigns.
- **Lifelong service.** In old age he joined an army to Constantinople and died during the campaign in about 52 AH. He asked to be buried as far in the enemy's land as possible, and his grave is revered today.
- **Qualities:** hospitality, humility, loyalty.

### Hazrat Hamza (RA)

Hamza ibn Abdul Muttalib (RA) was the Prophet's (pbuh) uncle and foster brother, close to him in age. He was a famous hunter and wrestler.

- **Acceptance of Islam.** In about the sixth year of the mission, Abu Jahl insulted the Prophet (pbuh) near the Ka'aba. When Hamza (RA) heard, he went and struck Abu Jahl with his bow, and then declared that he followed the Prophet's (pbuh) religion. His conversion gave the Muslims great strength and respect, since Quraysh knew that a man of his rank would defend them.
- **Fighter at Badr.** He fought bravely in the first battle of Islam.
- **Martyrdom.** He was killed at Uhud in 3 AH by Wahshi, and his body was mutilated. The Prophet (pbuh) grieved deeply and honoured him with the title **Sayyid al-Shuhada** (Master of the Martyrs). Earlier, he had been called "the Lion of Allah".
- **Qualities:** courage, loyalty and defence of the weak.

### Hazrat Talha ibn Ubaydullah (RA)

Talha (RA) was a merchant of the Quraysh who accepted Islam early through Abu Bakr (RA). He was one of the ten companions given the good news of Paradise.

- **Hardship in Makkah.** He was tied up and beaten by his own people for his faith.
- **Defence at Uhud.** When the Muslim line broke and the Prophet (pbuh) was attacked, Talha (RA) shielded him, using his own body and hand to take arrows and blows. His hand was damaged and he had many wounds. The Prophet (pbuh) said that Paradise was due to him.
- **Generosity.** He was known for spending his wealth on the poor and on Muslims in need, and was called "Talha (RA) the Generous".
- **Qualities:** bravery, sacrifice and generosity.

### Hazrat Zubair ibn al-Awwam (RA)

Zubair was the Prophet's (pbuh) cousin, the son of Safiyyah (RA), the Prophet's (pbuh) aunt, and a nephew of Khadija (RA). He accepted Islam as a youth, around the age of fifteen or sixteen, and was also among the ten promised Paradise.

- **Early courage.** He is said to have been the first to draw a sword in defence of the Prophet (pbuh).
- **Migration.** He migrated to Abyssinia and later to Madinah, taking part in both hardships.
- **Soldier.** He fought at Badr, Uhud, the Trench and other battles. The Prophet (pbuh) called him his *hawari*, a faithful helper, because of his readiness to volunteer in dangerous tasks.
- **Qualities:** early faith, courage and readiness to serve.

## Comparing the four

| Companion | Link to the Prophet (pbuh) | Main contribution | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abu Ayyub (RA) | Ansari host | Housed the Prophet (pbuh) in Madinah; fought in many campaigns | Hospitality |
| Hamza (RA) | Uncle and foster brother | Strengthened early Islam by his conversion; martyred at Uhud | Courage |
| Talha (RA) | Early convert from the Quraysh | Shielded the Prophet (pbuh) at Uhud | Sacrifice |
| Zubair (RA) | Cousin | Early convert; fought in many battles; called the Prophet's (pbuh) *hawari* | Readiness to serve |

## The prescribed passage: Jami at-Tirmidhi 3675

Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) reported that the Prophet (pbuh) ordered the Muslims to give charity. Umar (RA) had some wealth and thought, "Today I will outdo Abu Bakr (RA)." He brought half of his wealth. The Prophet (pbuh) asked, "What have you left for your family?" Umar (RA) replied, "The same amount." Then Abu Bakr (RA) brought everything he had. When asked what he had left for his family, he said, "Allah and His Messenger." Umar (RA) said, "By Allah, I will never do better than Abu Bakr (RA)."

| Words | Lesson |
|---|---|
| "ordered us to give charity" | The Sahabah responded quickly to the Prophet's (pbuh) call |
| "I will outdo Abu Bakr (RA)" | Healthy competition in doing good |
| "half of my wealth" | Great generosity |
| "everything he had" | Complete sacrifice and trust in Allah |
| "Allah and His Messenger" | The highest priority was faith, and trust in Allah's provision |
| "I will never do better than Abu Bakr (RA)" | Humility and respect for another's excellence |

The hadith shows the spirit shared by the companions: eagerness to give, to sacrifice and to support the Prophet (pbuh) and the cause of Islam. It links to the generosity of the Ansaar and the sacrifice of Talha (RA) and Hamza (RA).

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which the Ansaar supported the Muhajirun. **[4]**

> They shared their homes and food with the migrants [1], so that those who had left everything in Makkah were not left homeless or hungry [1]. Each Ansari also took a Muhajir as a brother and offered to share their property [1], which created a bond of faith stronger than the tribal ties of the past [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up Ansaar and Muhajirun.** The Ansaar are the helpers of Madinah; the Muhajirun are the emigrants.
- **Saying that Hamza (RA) was killed at Badr.** He was killed at Uhud.
- **Forgetting the role of Abu Ayyub (RA) as host.** He is a key person in the arrival in Madinah.
- **Listing the companions without their contribution.** Add what each did.
- **Not using the hadith.** The passage's key phrase is Abu Bakr's (RA) reply, "Allah and His Messenger".

## Exam technique

**Give** questions need a name or a fact (Abu Ayyub, Uhud, Sayyid al-Shuhada). **Explain** questions need a contribution and its effect. In the 12-mark question on the Tirmidhi hadith, use "half of my wealth" and "everything he had" to compare giving, and "Allah and His Messenger" for trust. Evaluate whether such generosity is a model for all Muslims or a special sacrifice, noting that Islam also teaches balance and care for one's family.
