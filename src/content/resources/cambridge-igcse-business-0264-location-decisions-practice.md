## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Identify two factors that influence the location of a manufacturing business. **[2]**

**2.** Identify two factors that influence the location of a service business. **[2]**

**3.** Define the term **footfall**. **[2]**

**4.** State two factors a business could consider when deciding which country to locate its operations in. **[2]**

**5.** A sugar refinery moves 12,000 tonnes of sugar cane 35 km. Transport costs $0.08 per tonne per km. Calculate the transport cost. **[2]**

**6.** Outline why a call centre does not need to be located in a busy city centre. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Halsted Glass makes large glass bottles for drinks companies. Its main raw material is sand. The bottles are bulky and break easily. Explain why Halsted Glass may decide to locate near its customers rather than near its supply of sand. **[3]**

**8.** Moreland Sports is choosing between two shops. It opens 310 days a year.

| | Site 1: town centre | Site 2: edge of town |
|---|---|---|
| Rent per year | $48,000 | $26,000 |
| Expected customers per day | 140 | 95 |
| Average spend per customer | $25 | $30 |
| Parking | None | Free car park |

**(a)** Calculate the expected yearly revenue at each site. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference in yearly revenue and the difference in rent between the two sites. **[2]**

**(c)** Recommend and justify which site Moreland Sports should choose. **[6]**

**9.** Calder Electronics wants to build a factory abroad to supply customers in Region Z. It is choosing between two countries.

| | Country R | Country S |
|---|---|---|
| Average wage per hour | $3 | $5 |
| Inside Region Z? | No: goods sent into Region Z pay a 15% import tariff | Yes: no import tariff |
| Political stability | Three changes of government in four years | Stable for many years |
| Workers with electronics skills | Few | Many |

Consider the advantages and disadvantages of each country. Justify which country Calder Electronics should choose. **[6]**

**10.** Explain why a business might not choose the location with the lowest costs. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how a change in exchange rates could affect a business that has located its factory in another country. **[3]**

**12.** Explain two factors a dental clinic should consider when choosing its location. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: nearness to raw materials; nearness to the market; availability and cost of labour; cost of land; transport links; power and water supply; government grants or rules; room to expand. One mark for each of two different factors [1] [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: nearness to customers; footfall; visibility; rent; competitors nearby; parking and public transport; availability of skilled staff; communications. One mark for each of two different factors [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The number of people [1] who pass a particular site or shop in a given time [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two from: wage and other costs; access to markets and trade barriers; political stability; laws; government incentives; infrastructure; skills and language; exchange rates; culture. One mark for each of two different factors [1] [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 12,000 x 35 x 0.08 [1] = **$33,600** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Customers contact a call centre by phone or online and never visit [1], so it can choose a location with lower rent and wages, as long as communications are reliable and staff are available [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Finished bottles take up much more space than the sand used to make them, so moving bottles costs more than moving sand [1]. Bottles also break easily, so long journeys increase the number damaged [1]. Locating near its customers keeps transport costs and breakages down, and lets Halsted deliver quickly to drinks companies [1]. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Site 1: 140 x 25 x 310 = **$1,085,000** [1]. Site 2: 95 x 30 x 310 = **$883,500** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Revenue difference: 1,085,000 - 883,500 = **$201,500** more at Site 1 [1]. Rent difference: 48,000 - 26,000 = **$22,000** more at Site 1 [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** For Site 1: it is expected to bring $201,500 more revenue a year, far more than the extra $22,000 in rent [1], because more people pass a town centre shop [1]. For Site 2: lower rent and free parking suit customers buying bulky items such as bicycles or gym equipment, and customers there are expected to spend more each [1]; but fewer customers mean lower revenue overall [1]. Recommendation: Moreland should choose Site 1 [1], because the extra revenue is much larger than the extra rent, provided the customer estimates are realistic; it should check footfall figures first, since the lack of parking may put off some shoppers [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Comparing the revenue gap with the rent gap is the key piece of analysis. Revenue is not profit, so a strong answer notes that the cost of the goods sold must also be paid.

**9.** Country R: wages are $2 an hour lower, which cuts production costs [1]; but goods sent to Region Z pay a 15% tariff, which could cancel out the wage saving, and there are few workers with electronics skills, so training costs would be high [1]. R is also politically unstable, so taxes and rules could change suddenly [1]. Country S: it is inside Region Z, so there is no tariff and goods reach customers more easily, and many skilled workers are available [1]; but wages are higher, so costs per hour of labour are greater [1]. Judgement: Calder should choose Country S, because avoiding a 15% tariff on every product sold in Region Z, a skilled workforce and a stable government outweigh the $2 an hour wage saving in Country R [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The tariff applies to the value of the goods, not just the labour in them, so it can easily be larger than the wage saving. Using that point shows analysis, not just listing.

**10.** The cheapest location may have disadvantages that cost more in the long run [1], such as few customers passing a shop, or no skilled workers near a factory [1]. Lower revenue, or extra training and recruitment costs, could outweigh the savings in rent or wages [1]. **[3]**

**11.** If the currency of the country where the factory is located rises in value [1], the business's costs there (wages, rent, materials paid in local currency) become more expensive in terms of its home currency [1], which reduces its profit unless it can raise its prices [1]. **[3]**

Also accept: a fall in the value of that country's currency makes costs there cheaper, but makes any profits sent home worth less, with the same chain of reasoning.

**12.** Nearness to customers [1]: patients need to travel to the clinic for appointments, so a site near homes or on a bus route makes it easier for them to attend regularly [1]. Availability of skilled staff [1]: the clinic needs qualified dentists and nurses, so it should locate where it can recruit them, or where staff are willing to travel to [1]. Also accept parking, rent or competitors, with explanation. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing factors without linking them to the business in the question.
- Choosing the cheapest site without comparing revenue or other factors.
- Treating revenue as if it were profit.
- Mentioning only wages when choosing a country, and ignoring tariffs, skills and stability.
- Giving a recommendation with no deciding reason or risk.
