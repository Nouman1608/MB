This set tests **3.2.2.1 Human resource objectives** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). Section A has short-answer and calculation questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Every business and figure is invented. Revise first with the [human resource objectives study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725/) and the [human resource objectives revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term employee engagement. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a job description and a person specification. **[2]**

**3.** Pennine Care Homes employs 320 staff. It expects labour turnover of 25% next year and will need 350 staff. Calculate how many staff it will need to recruit. **[3]**

**4.** Pennine needs 30 registered nurses next year. It has 26 now, and expects 5 of them to leave. Calculate the shortfall of registered nurses. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** selection methods other than interviews. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** advantage of internal recruitment for a bank appointing a new branch manager. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why a restaurant should give new waiting staff induction training. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a clothing retailer of employing sales staff on zero hours contracts. **[3]**

## Section B

**9.** Brayford Construction has won a contract to build 400 homes over three years. Analyse the value to Brayford of human resource planning. **[6]**

**10.** Quayside Insurance recruits 25 graduates each year from over 600 applicants.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why Quayside might use an assessment centre to select its graduates. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage of using an assessment centre. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways in which training could help a business achieve its objective of improving employee retention. **[4]**

**12.** Larchmont Logistics runs warehouses and a fleet of delivery vans. It has 40 driver vacancies before its busiest season, which runs from October to January. Its labour turnover among drivers is 28%, and its latest staff survey gave an engagement score of 52%, down from 61% two years ago. An agency can supply qualified drivers within a week, at a higher daily cost than permanent drivers. Recruiting permanent drivers usually takes eight weeks, including training on Larchmont's routes and systems. Evaluate whether Larchmont should fill the vacancies with agency drivers or permanent employees. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** How committed and involved employees are [1] in their work and in the goals of the business [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A job description sets out the duties, responsibilities and title of the job [1]; a person specification sets out the qualifications, skills and qualities the person doing the job should have [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Leavers = 320 × 25% = 80 [1]. Staff remaining = 320 − 80 = 240 [1]. Recruit = 350 − 240 = **110 staff** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Nurses available = 26 − 5 = 21 [1]. Shortfall = 30 − 21 = **9 nurses** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: CV [1]; application form [1]; references; testing; assessment centre. **[2]**

**6.** An internal candidate already knows the bank's systems, products and customers [1], so they can manage the branch effectively from the first day with little induction [1]. Promoting from within also shows other staff a route to progress, which can raise engagement and retention [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Induction teaches new staff the restaurant's food safety and hygiene procedures [1], so mistakes that could make customers ill, or lead to complaints, are less likely [1]. Staff who feel welcomed and know what to do are also less likely to leave in their first weeks [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Staff on zero hours contracts have no guaranteed income [1], so they may take other jobs and be unavailable when the retailer needs them, such as during a sale [1]. Lower commitment may also show in weaker customer service, which affects sales [1]. **[3]**

**9.** HR planning would show how many workers with each skill, such as bricklayers and electricians, Brayford needs at each stage of the three years [1]. Skilled trades can be hard to recruit quickly, so planning early gives time to recruit or train apprentices [1], avoiding delays that could breach the contract and lead to penalties [1]. Planning also shows when numbers can fall as the project ends, so Brayford can use temporary contracts or plan redeployment to other sites instead of redundancies [1]. However, the plan depends on the building schedule and expected labour turnover, which may change [1], so it must be updated regularly to remain useful [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** With over 600 applicants for 25 places, Quayside needs a method that compares candidates fairly on the same tasks [1]; group exercises and tests show how each candidate performs side by side [1]. Graduates have little work experience, so a CV says little about their potential [1]; tasks such as presentations and team problems show skills like communication and teamwork that the job will need [1].

**(b)** Assessment centres are expensive because they need several assessors and a full day or more [1], so Quayside can only invite a shortlist and may screen out good applicants with a cheaper earlier stage [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Induction training helps new staff settle quickly and feel confident [1], reducing the number who leave in their first months [1]. Off-the-job training that leads to qualifications shows employees they can progress [1], so they are more likely to stay for promotion than to move to a rival [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Credit chains of reasoning and a judgement, for example:

- Agency drivers can start within a week, so the 40 vacancies are filled before the October peak [1].
- Permanent recruitment takes eight weeks, so some vacancies might still be open when the season starts [1], risking late deliveries and lost contracts [1].
- Agency drivers cost more per day, raising labour costs and reducing profit [1].
- But they are only paid for while needed, so costs fall after January when demand drops [1].
- Agency drivers do not know Larchmont's routes and systems, so delivery times and accuracy may suffer [1].
- Engagement has fallen from 61% to 52%; relying on agency staff may make permanent drivers feel less valued [1].
- Labour turnover of 28% suggests deeper problems; filling vacancies without tackling them means Larchmont will face the same gap again [1].
- Permanent drivers trained on Larchmont's systems support the retention and skill-level objectives [1].
- A mix is possible: agency drivers to cover the peak while permanent drivers are recruited and trained [1].
- Larchmont should also investigate why drivers leave, as reducing turnover lowers future recruitment needs [1].
- Judgement: Larchmont should use agency drivers for this season only, while starting permanent recruitment now, because the peak cannot wait eight weeks but long-term reliance on agency drivers would worsen engagement and cost more [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to subtract expected leavers before calculating recruitment need.
- Describing selection methods without saying what they reveal about the candidate.
- Treating zero hours contracts as having only benefits for the business.
- Evaluating staffing choices without using the timing and cost information in the case.
- Ignoring the link between engagement, turnover and retention.
