The questions test every learning outcome, 6.2.1(a), (b)(i) and (b)(ii), from Module 6, Organic chemistry and analysis. H432 is the full A level. Questions 9 to 11 are longer problems; two of them draw on nitration (6.1.1) and nitrile reduction (6.2.4).

## Questions

**1.** Explain why amines can act as bases. **[2]**

**2.** Three amines are shown: **P** (CH₃)₂CHNHCH₃, **R** CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂NH₂ and **T** (CH₃CH₂)₂NCH₃.

**(a)** Classify each amine as primary, secondary or tertiary. **[3]**
**(b)** Write an equation for **P** reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**3.** Hexane-1,6-diamine, H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂, is shaken with excess dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an ionic equation to show how the free diamine can be recovered from the salt. **[1]**

**4.** 4-Methylpentan-1-amine is made from 1-chloro-4-methylpentane, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂Cl.

**(a)** State the reagent and conditions. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the balanced equation for this preparation. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the solvent is ethanol rather than water. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the reagent is used in excess, and give the structural formula of one by-product that forms if it is not. **[3]**

**5.** N-methylbutan-1-amine, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NHCH₃, is to be made from methylamine.

**(a)** Identify the organic reagent needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction, using two moles of methylamine, and explain why two moles are needed. **[2]**
**(c)** The product can react further with the same organic reagent. Give the structural formula and class of the amine formed. **[2]**

**6.** 2-Fluorophenylamine is made from 1-fluoro-2-nitrobenzene, FC₆H₄NO₂.

**(a)** State the reagents and conditions for the reduction. **[2]**
**(b)** Using [H] to represent the reducing agent, give the equation for this change. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why aqueous sodium hydroxide is added at the end. **[1]**

**7.** A solution of a primary aliphatic amine, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NH₂, has a concentration of 7.52 g dm⁻³. A 25.0 cm³ portion is neutralised by 21.60 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write a general equation for the reaction, using RNH₂. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the molar mass of the amine and deduce its molecular formula. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest a structural formula for the amine. **[1]**

**8.** A 6.39 g sample of 1,3,5-trinitrobenzene, C₆H₃(NO₂)₃, is fully reduced with tin and concentrated HCl. After the alkali step, 2.46 g of benzene-1,3,5-triamine, C₆H₃(NH₂)₃, is collected.

Give the equation, using [H], and calculate the percentage yield. **[4]**

**9.** 2,5-Dimethylphenylamine is made from 1,4-dimethylbenzene in two stages. Stage 1 gives 1,4-dimethyl-2-nitrobenzene, (CH₃)₂C₆H₃NO₂.

**(a)** State the reagents for stage 1. **[1]**
**(b)** State the reagents and conditions for stage 2, including the final step. **[2]**
**(c)** Write an equation for stage 2 using [H]. **[1]**
**(d)** In stage 2, 7.55 g of the nitro compound gives 4.84 g of 2,5-dimethylphenylamine. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**
**(e)** All 4.84 g of the amine is reacted with dilute sulfuric acid. Write an equation and calculate the mass of salt formed. **[3]**

**10.** Two routes to hexan-1-amine are proposed.

Route A: 1-bromohexane is heated with excess ethanolic ammonia.
Route B: 1-bromopentane is converted into a nitrile with cyanide ions, then the nitrile is reduced with hydrogen and a nickel catalyst.

**(a)** Write an equation for route A. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the structural formula of the nitrile in route B and write an equation for its reduction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why route B gives hexan-1-amine from a five-carbon haloalkane. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one advantage of route B over route A for obtaining a pure primary amine, and explain it. **[2]**

**11.** Amine **X** is a saturated, non-cyclic secondary amine. **X** reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to give a salt that contains 25.8% chlorine by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the molar mass of the salt and of **X**, and deduce the molecular formula of **X**. **[3]**
**(b)** **X** is made by heating butan-1-amine with a chloroalkane in ethanol. Identify the chloroalkane and write an equation using two moles of butan-1-amine. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the structural formula of a tertiary amine that could also form in this reaction. **[1]**
**(d)** State how **X** could be released from its hydrochloride salt. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Nitrogen in an amine carries a lone pair [1], which can bond datively to an H⁺ ion, so amines are proton acceptors [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both ideas are needed: "lone pair" alone or "accepts H⁺" alone is half an answer, and the lone pair must be placed on nitrogen.

**2. (a)** **P** secondary [1]; **R** primary [1]; **T** tertiary [1]
**(b)** **(CH₃)₂CHNHCH₃ + HCl → (CH₃)₂CHNH₂⁺CH₃ Cl⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* **R** is primary: only one carbon is bonded to its nitrogen, even though that carbon carries three others. Count carbons on N.

**3. (a)** H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ + 2HCl [1] → **Cl⁻ ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺ Cl⁻** [1]
**(b)** **⁺H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺ + 2OH⁻ → H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* With excess acid both nitrogens are protonated; an equation with 1 HCl leaves one lone pair unreacted and does not match "excess".

**4. (a)** Excess ammonia dissolved in ethanol [1]; heated in a sealed vessel [1]
**(b)** **(CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl** [1]
**(c)** In water, hydroxide ions or water would substitute the chlorine and give an alcohol [1]
**(d)** The amine product has a lone pair on N, so it is also a nucleophile [1]; it can react with more haloalkane, and excess ammonia makes reaction with ammonia much more likely [1]; by-product: **((CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂)₂NH** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Heat" on its own, or "aqueous ammonia", misses the conditions; the words "excess" and "ethanol" each carry meaning here.

**5. (a)** **1-bromobutane** (or another 1-halobutane) [1]
**(b)** **2CH₃NH₂ + CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Br⁻** [1]; the HBr formed is an acid and protonates a second methylamine molecule [1]
**(c)** **(CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂)₂NCH₃** [1]; a **tertiary** amine [1]
*Examiner insight:* An equation ending in HBr is acceptable chemistry only if you then show where the HBr goes; when two moles are asked for, the salt must appear as a product.

**6. (a)** Reagents: Sn with concentrated HCl [1]; conditions: heat, under reflux [1]
**(b)** **FC₆H₄NO₂ + 6[H] → FC₆H₄NH₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** In the acid the amine is present as its protonated salt, FC₆H₄NH₃⁺; OH⁻ removes the proton to release the free amine [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Dilute HCl" or "HCl" without "concentrated" is a common way to lose the reagent point; check the [H] count by balancing H atoms on both sides.

**7. (a)** **RNH₂ + HCl → RNH₃⁺Cl⁻** [1]
**(b)** n(HCl) = 0.100 × 21.60/1000 = 0.00216 mol, so n(amine) = 0.00216 mol [1]; concentration = 0.00216 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0864 mol dm⁻³ [1]; M = 7.52 ÷ 0.0864 = 87.0 g mol⁻¹ [1]; 14n + 17 = 87.0 gives n = 5, so **C₅H₁₃N** (C₅H₁₁NH₂) [1]
**(c)** Any primary C₅ amine, e.g. **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the 1 : 1 ratio and each conversion; a molar mass with no working gives nothing to credit if the arithmetic slips.

**8.** **C₆H₃(NO₂)₃ + 18[H] → C₆H₃(NH₂)₃ + 6H₂O** [1]; n(trinitro) = 6.39 ÷ 213.0 = 0.0300 mol [1]; n(triamine) = 2.46 ÷ 123.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; yield = 0.0200 ÷ 0.0300 × 100 = **66.7%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Three nitro groups need 18[H] and give 6H₂O; check by counting H (21 on each side), since the 1 : 1 mole ratio hides an unbalanced equation.

**9. (a)** Concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid [1]
**(b)** Tin and concentrated HCl, heated under reflux [1]; then excess NaOH(aq) [1]
**(c)** **(CH₃)₂C₆H₃NO₂ + 6[H] → (CH₃)₂C₆H₃NH₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(d)** n(C₈H₉NO₂) = 7.55 ÷ 151.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; n(C₈H₁₁N) = 4.84 ÷ 121.0 = 0.0400 mol [1]; yield = 0.0400 ÷ 0.0500 × 100 = **80.0%** [1]
**(e)** **2(CH₃)₂C₆H₃NH₂ + H₂SO₄ → ((CH₃)₂C₆H₃NH₃⁺)₂SO₄²⁻** [1]; n(salt) = 0.0400 ÷ 2 = 0.0200 mol [1]; M = 2(122.0) + 96.1 = 340.1 g mol⁻¹, so mass = 0.0200 × 340.1 = **6.80 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (e) the 2 : 1 ratio is the deciding step; a 1 : 1 ratio doubles the moles of salt and gives an answer twice too big.

**10. (a)** **CH₃(CH₂)₅Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃(CH₂)₅NH₂ + NH₄Br** [1]
**(b)** Nitrile: **CH₃(CH₂)₄CN** (hexanenitrile) [1]; **CH₃(CH₂)₄CN + 2H₂ → CH₃(CH₂)₅NH₂** [1]
**(c)** The cyanide carbon adds a sixth carbon to the chain and becomes the CH₂ next to NH₂ [1]
**(d)** Route B avoids secondary and tertiary amines from further substitution [1], because the haloalkane is used up making the nitrile before any amine forms, so less separation is needed [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), "higher yield" without a reason linked to further substitution in route A does not answer the question.

**11. (a)** M(salt) = 35.5 ÷ 0.258 = 137.6 g mol⁻¹ [1]; M(X) = 137.6 − 36.5 = 101 g mol⁻¹ [1]; 14n + 17 = 101 gives n = 6, so **C₆H₁₅N** [1]
**(b)** **Chloroethane** [1]; **2CH₃(CH₂)₃NH₂ + CH₃CH₂Cl → CH₃(CH₂)₃NHCH₂CH₃ + CH₃(CH₂)₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻** [1]
**(c)** **CH₃(CH₂)₃N(CH₂CH₃)₂** [1]
**(d)** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide (or another strong alkali) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract HCl (36.5), not Cl (35.5), to get from the salt to the amine; the salt contains the extra proton as well as the chloride.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting carbons on the wrong atom when deciding whether an amine is primary, secondary or tertiary (question 2).
- Protonating only one nitrogen in a diamine with excess acid (question 3).
- Writing "ammonia, heat" with no mention of excess or ethanol (question 4).
- Leaving HX as a product without showing it react with a second base molecule (question 5).
- Omitting "concentrated" with Sn/HCl, or leaving out the NaOH step (questions 6 and 9).
- Getting the 2 : 1 amine to sulfuric acid ratio wrong (question 9).
- Subtracting 35.5 instead of 36.5 when working back from a hydrochloride salt (question 11).
