These notes condense sub-topic **4(b) Energy transfers** (statements 4.2 to 4.10) of Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE Physics 4PH1, Issue 4. For the full explanations and worked examples, use the
[Energy transfers study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-energy-transfers/); for exam-style questions, the
[Energy transfers practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-energy-transfers-practice/).

## Stores and pathways

**Eight stores:** chemical, kinetic, gravitational, elastic, thermal, magnetic, electrostatic, nuclear.
**Four pathways:** mechanically, electrically, by heating, by radiation (light and sound).

Template for any "describe the energy transfers" question:

```
[store] → [pathway] → [store]   (then the same for the wasted energy)
```

Example: a falling apple, gravitational store → mechanically (gravity does work) → kinetic store; a little to the
thermal store of the air through air resistance.

## Conservation and efficiency

- Energy is never created or destroyed: total energy output = total energy supplied.
- Wasted energy usually ends in the **thermal store of the surroundings**, spread out and no longer useful.

```
efficiency = (useful energy output / total energy output) × 100%
useful = efficiency × total        total = useful / efficiency
```

Efficiency is never above 100% (or 1) and has no unit.

## Sankey diagrams

- Arrow width ∝ energy; one scale for the whole diagram.
- Input enters on the left; useful output goes straight on; wasted outputs curve away.
- Check: sum of output widths = input width.
- To read: count squares across each arrow × scale.

## Thermal transfer

| Method | How | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | Vibrating particles pass energy to neighbours; free electrons carry it fast in metals | Mainly solids |
| Convection | Heated fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler denser fluid sinks; a convection current forms | Liquids and gases only |
| Radiation | Infrared electromagnetic waves | Anywhere, including a vacuum |

**Convection chain:** heated → expands → less dense → rises; cooled → denser → sinks.
**Everyday convection:** room heaters; sea breezes (land warms faster, air rises over land, cooler sea air moves in);
kettle element at the bottom; freezer compartment at the top of a fridge.

**Radiation rules:**
- hotter objects emit more radiation per second;
- dark matt surfaces: best emitters and best absorbers;
- light shiny surfaces: poorest emitters and absorbers (best reflectors).

## The practical (4.9)

- **Conduction:** equal rods of different metals, wax-fixed pins at the far ends, heat the near ends equally; first
  pin to fall marks the best conductor.
- **Convection:** coloured crystal at the bottom edge of a beaker of water, heat gently below it; the colour traces
  the current.
- **Radiation:** cube with different faces filled with hot water, infrared detector or thermometer at equal distance
  from each face; or identical cans with different surfaces cooling.
- **Control variables:** same size and shape, same starting temperature, same distance, same heating.

## Reducing unwanted transfer

| Feature | Method it reduces | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vacuum between walls | Conduction and convection | No particles in a vacuum |
| Silvered surfaces | Radiation | Shiny surfaces reflect infrared |
| Stopper | Convection (and conduction) | Stops warm air leaving; poor conductor |
| Trapped air (lagging, clothing, fleece) | Conduction and convection | Air is a poor conductor and cannot circulate when trapped |
| Plastic handle | Conduction | Plastic is a poor conductor |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Energy store | Where energy is held, such as the kinetic store of a moving object |
| Energy transfer | Movement of energy between stores: mechanically, electrically, by heating or by radiation |
| Conservation of energy | Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred |
| Dissipated energy | Energy spread into the surroundings, usually as thermal energy, where it is no longer useful |
| Efficiency | Fraction of the total energy output that is useful, as a decimal or percentage |
| Sankey diagram | Diagram whose arrow widths show energy amounts to scale |
| Convection current | Circulating flow of a fluid caused by density differences from heating |
| Infrared radiation | Electromagnetic radiation emitted by all objects; more from hotter ones |

## Exam traps

- "Light energy" or "heat energy" as stores.
- "Energy is lost". Write "transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings".
- "Heat rises" instead of "the warmer, less dense air (or water) rises".
- Saying "particles expand" in convection: the fluid expands because its particles move further apart.
- Saying the vacuum in a flask stops radiation.
- Efficiency above 100%, or with a unit.
- A Sankey diagram whose outputs do not add up to the input.

## Self-test

1. Name the store and the pathway as a stretched catapult fires a stone.
2. Describe the energy transfers when a battery-powered fan is switched on.
3. A device has a total energy output of 500 J and wastes 150 J. Calculate its efficiency.
4. A motor is 60% efficient and transfers 240 J usefully. Calculate the total energy output.
5. In a Sankey diagram drawn at 1 square = 20 J, the input arrow is 25 squares wide. What energy is supplied?
6. Why does convection not happen in a solid?
7. Explain why a sea breeze blows from sea to land on a sunny day.
8. Which surface is the best absorber of infrared radiation?
9. Why are metals better conductors than non-metals?
10. Explain why the vacuum in a vacuum flask reduces energy transfer.
11. Name two variables to control in the conduction practical.
12. State the principle of conservation of energy.

**Answers:** 1. Elastic store of the catapult → mechanically → kinetic store of the stone. 2. Chemical store of the
battery → electrically → motor → mechanically → kinetic store of the blades and air; wasted to the thermal store of
the surroundings by heating and as sound. 3. useful = 500 − 150 = 350 J; efficiency = 350 ÷ 500 × 100% = 70%.
4. total = 240 ÷ 0.60 = 400 J. 5. 25 × 20 = 500 J. 6. The particles cannot move from place to place, so no fluid can
flow. 7. The land warms faster than the sea; air above the land warms, becomes less dense and rises; cooler, denser
air from over the sea flows in to replace it. 8. A dark, matt surface (matt black). 9. Metals contain free electrons
that move through the metal and carry energy quickly, in addition to particle vibrations. 10. A vacuum has no
particles, so energy cannot be transferred across it by conduction or convection. 11. Any two: length of rod, thickness
of rod, heating (same flame and distance), starting temperature. 12. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only
transferred from one store to another.
