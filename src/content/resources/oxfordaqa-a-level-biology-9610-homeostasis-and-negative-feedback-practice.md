## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *homeostasis*. **[2]**

**2.** Which statement describes negative feedback?

A: A change causes a response that increases the change.

B: A change causes a response that restores the original level.

C: A change causes no response.

D: A change is detected by an effector. **[1]**

**3.** Name the part of a control system that detects a change from the set point. **[1]**

**4.** Give two responses that help to raise core temperature in a cold environment. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** Explain why it is important that the core temperature of a mammal is kept stable. **[4]**

**6.** The pH of blood is kept close to 7.4. In a patient the pH fell to 7.2.

**(a)** Calculate the factor by which the hydrogen ion concentration increased. pH is the negative logarithm (base 10) of the hydrogen ion concentration. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this change could affect the metabolism of the patient. **[3]**

**7.** A volunteer entered a hot room at time 0. The core temperature and the sweat rate were recorded.

| Time / min | Core temperature / degrees Celsius | Sweat rate / g per hour |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 37.0 | 10 |
| 10 | 37.5 | 40 |
| 20 | 37.9 | 90 |
| 30 | 37.5 | 60 |
| 40 | 37.1 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of increase in core temperature between 0 and 20 minutes, in degrees Celsius per minute. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the sweat rate between 0 and 20 minutes. **[2]**

**(c)** Use the data to explain how the results show negative feedback. **[4]**

**8.** Explain the advantage of having separate negative feedback mechanisms to control a rise and a fall in core temperature, rather than one mechanism. **[3]**

**9.** The core temperature of a patient with heat stroke was recorded.

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature / degrees Celsius | 40.0 | 41.0 | 42.5 | 44.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of increase in temperature in each 30-minute period, in degrees Celsius per minute. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the data show positive feedback. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why this positive feedback is harmful. **[2]**

**10.** In a nerve cell, the entry of sodium ions during depolarisation causes more sodium channels to open. Explain why this is an example of positive feedback, and suggest why it is not harmful. **[3]**

**11.** Compare negative feedback with positive feedback. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The maintenance of the internal environment [1] within restricted limits [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Constant" is too strong. Conditions vary a little around the set point.

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A receptor [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: vasoconstriction (less blood flows near the skin surface) [1]; shivering (muscle contraction releases heat) [1]; raising the hairs to trap air [1]; reduced sweating [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Enzymes have an optimum temperature [1]. If the temperature is too high, hydrogen bonds break, the tertiary structure and active site change, and the enzymes are denatured [1]. If the temperature is too low, molecules have less kinetic energy, so there are fewer enzyme-substrate collisions and reactions are too slow [1]. A stable temperature keeps the rate of metabolic reactions steady and appropriate [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** The concentration is 10^(-pH), so the ratio is 10^(7.4 - 7.2) = 10^0.2 [1] = 1.6 (accept 1.58 or 1.585) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** A change in pH alters the charges on the R-groups of amino acids [1], so ionic and hydrogen bonds change and the tertiary structure of enzymes is altered [1]. The active site no longer fits the substrate, so enzyme activity falls and metabolic reactions slow [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** (37.9 - 37.0) / 20 [1] = 0.045 degrees per minute [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** (90 - 10) / 10 [1] x 100 = 800 % [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The temperature rose to 37.9 in the hot room (a deviation from 37.0) [1], and the sweat rate increased from 10 to 90 g per hour [1]. Sweat evaporates and removes heat from the body [1], so the temperature fell back to 37.1 by 40 minutes, and the sweat rate also fell, because the correction reversed the change [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Each mechanism reverses a change in one direction [1], so the system can correct a fall as well as a rise [1], giving faster and finer control with smaller variation around the set point [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** 0 to 30 min: 1.0 / 30 = 0.033 degrees per minute [1]. 30 to 60 min: 1.5 / 30 = 0.050 [1]. 60 to 90 min: 2.0 / 30 = 0.067 [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** The temperature is moving further from the set point [1], and the rate of increase is itself increasing, so the change causes a response that increases the change [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The higher temperature increases the rate of metabolic reactions, which releases more heat and raises the temperature further [1]. At high temperatures enzymes are denatured, so the control system and cell reactions fail [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The entry of sodium ions causes further depolarisation, which opens more sodium channels, so the change increases itself [1]. It is not harmful because the process is limited: the sodium channels close and the potential is restored [1], so the system does not run out of control [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Any four from: negative feedback reverses a change but positive feedback increases it [1]; negative feedback restores the original level whereas positive feedback causes a greater departure [1]; negative feedback keeps conditions stable, so it is the basis of homeostasis, whereas positive feedback is associated with breakdown of control [1]; both use receptors and effectors [1]; a graph of a negative feedback system returns to the set point but that of a positive feedback system moves away from it [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "constant" in the definition of homeostasis.
- Saying enzymes die or are killed.
- Describing the response without naming how heat is lost or gained.
- Not using values from the data when asked to explain.
- Confusing negative feedback with a harmful effect.
