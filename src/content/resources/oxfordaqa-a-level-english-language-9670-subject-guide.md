OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Language (9670) is the
study of how English works: how texts are shaped by their context, how
language varies between social groups, how children learn language, how
English is used around the world, and how to investigate language data
for yourself. It suits students who enjoy close analysis and writing for
different purposes. The course has four units. Two AS papers, each
worth 20% of the A-level, are followed by a compulsory A-level paper
worth 30%, and the final 30% comes from either a Language Exploration
exam (Route A) or a non-exam language investigation (Route B). Every
component is marked out of 50.

## How the course is organised

**Unit 1 – Language and Context (AS).** Section A, Understanding Texts,
develops the analysis of how writers and speakers adapt language to
audience, purpose and context. Section B, Directed Writing, asks you to
produce your own text for a specified situation.

**Unit 2 – Language and Society (AS).** Section A, Language and Social
Groups – Texts, looks at how language reflects and constructs group
identity. Section B, Language and Social Groups – Writing, turns that
knowledge into your own extended writing.

**Unit 3 – Language Variation (A-level).** Section A, Learning Language,
covers how language is acquired. Section B, International English, covers
how English varies and changes across the world.

**Unit 4 – Language Exploration (A-level).** The final unit asks you to
explore language independently, either in an exam or through your own
investigation, depending on the route your school takes.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Language and Context | 120 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Language and Society | 120 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Language Variation | 120 minutes | 50 | 30% |
| Route A: A-level Paper 2 | Language Exploration (exam) | 150 minutes | 50 | 30% |
| Route B: NEA | Language investigation | Not timed | 50 | 30% |

Students take one route only for the final unit. Route A is an externally
assessed exam. Route B is a non-exam assessment, marked by your teacher
and moderated externally. The qualification is modular: the AS papers can
be sat at the end of the first year and stand alone as an International
AS, and the A-level components follow. The specification was first
taught in September 2017, with first AS exams in May/June 2018 and first
A-level exams in May/June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The units build from describing language in a single text towards
explaining patterns across many speakers and situations.

Unit 1 gives you the analytical toolkit: the frameworks for describing
vocabulary, grammar, meaning and structure, and the link between those
choices and context. Unit 2 applies the same tools to group identity,
which is a step from one text to a community of users. Unit 3 widens the
lens again, to how individuals acquire language and how English varies
internationally. Unit 4 then asks you to use everything you know on
language that you have not been prepared for in advance, or on data you
have collected yourself.

Writing runs alongside analysis. The AS units both include a writing
section, so producing your own text for a purpose is assessed from the
start, and the ability to write clearly and precisely is what carries an
analysis in Units 3 and 4. Accurate terminology is the other constant: the
same terms for describing language are used in every unit.

## Planning your study

In the first year, Unit 1 usually comes first because its analytical
frameworks are needed everywhere else. Unit 2 then applies those
frameworks to social groups. Keep a glossary of linguistic terms from the
first week and add to it throughout the course.

In the second year, Unit 3's two sections can be taught in either order,
but both rely on secure first-year analysis. If your school follows Route
B, find out early when the investigation will be planned, researched and
written, since that work runs alongside exam teaching. If you follow
Route A, regular practice with unfamiliar data matters most.

Begin past-paper questions on each unit once it has been taught, and sit
full papers in the run-up to each exam series. The course's
exam-preparation page explains the Route A and Route B choice in more
detail, so use it for decisions about the final unit.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are there in OxfordAQA A-level English Language 9670?**
Four: Unit 1 Language and Context and Unit 2 Language and Society at AS, and Unit 3 Language Variation and Unit 4 Language Exploration at A-level.

**What is the difference between Route A and Route B?**
Route A assesses the final unit through A-level Paper 2, a 150-minute exam on Language Exploration. Route B replaces it with a non-exam language investigation, marked by your teacher and externally moderated. Both are worth 30% of the A-level and 50 marks.

**How are the AS papers weighted?**
AS Paper 1 and AS Paper 2 are each 120 minutes long, worth 50 marks and 20% of the A-level.

**Is directed writing assessed?**
Yes. Unit 1 Section B is Directed Writing, and Unit 2 Section B is a writing section on language and social groups.

**When was 9670 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2017, with first AS exams in May/June 2018 and first A-level exams in May/June 2019.
