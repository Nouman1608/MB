## Section A

**1.** State the term for an organism, such as a mosquito, that carries a disease-causing organism from one host to another. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why only female mosquitoes transmit malaria. **[1]**

**3.** Name **two** personal protection strategies against malaria. **[2]**

**4.** State why boiling water helps to control cholera. **[1]**

**5.** Explain how biological control can reduce the number of mosquitoes in a pond. **[2]**

**6.** Name **two** places around a home where mosquitoes could breed. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows rainfall and malaria cases in a district. The figures are invented for practice.

| Month | Jan | Mar | May | Jul | Sep | Nov |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rainfall (mm) | 10 | 30 | 180 | 260 | 120 | 20 |
| Malaria cases | 40 | 35 | 210 | 480 | 390 | 90 |

(a) Describe the relationship between rainfall and malaria cases. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
(b) Explain why malaria cases increase after the wettest months. **[2]**

**8.** Village A was given insecticide-treated nets; Village B was not. Malaria cases per 1,000 people were recorded before and after. The figures are invented for practice.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Village A | 180 | 110 |
| Village B | 175 | 170 |

(a) Calculate the percentage fall in cases in Village A, to one decimal place. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the results from Village B are useful. **[1]**
(c) Suggest **two** reasons why malaria cases remain high in Village A. **[2]**

**9.** A new irrigation scheme with open channels has been built in a dry valley. Malaria cases have risen.
(a) Explain why the irrigation scheme has increased malaria cases. **[2]**
(b) Describe **two** vector control strategies suitable for the scheme. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why improving sanitation and sewage treatment can control cholera more effectively in the long term than vaccination alone. **[3]**

**11.** An earthquake has damaged a city's water treatment works and sewers.
(a) Describe **two** ways households can make their water safe to drink. **[2]**
(b) Describe **two** other actions that would reduce the spread of cholera in the city. **[2]**

**12.** A rural area has many flooded rice fields and few health clinics. Discuss the benefits and limitations of vector control compared with personal protection for reducing malaria in this area. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Vector **[1]**.

**2.** Only females bite, because they need a blood meal to produce eggs **[1]**.

**3.** Any two of nets, insect repellent, vaccination and antimalarial drugs: first strategy **[1]**; second strategy **[1]**.

**4.** Heat kills the cholera bacteria in the water **[1]**.

**5.** A natural enemy, such as a fish that eats mosquito larvae, is added to the pond **[1]**, so fewer larvae survive to become adult mosquitoes **[1]**.

**6.** Uncovered water tanks or buckets **[1]**; discarded tyres, puddles or blocked drains **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Cases rise as rainfall rises, from 35 cases in March to a peak of 480 in July, when rainfall is highest at 260 mm **[1]**; cases stay high in September (390) even though rainfall has fallen to 120 mm, so cases lag behind rainfall **[1]**.
(b) Rain leaves pools of still water where mosquitoes lay eggs and larvae develop **[1]**; it takes time for larvae to become adults and for the parasite to spread through people, so the peak in cases comes after the peak in rainfall **[1]**.

**8.** (a) 180 − 110 = 70 **[1]**; 70 ÷ 180 × 100 = 38.9% **[1]**.
(b) Village B shows what happened without nets; its cases barely changed, so the fall in Village A is likely to be due to the nets rather than another factor such as a dry year **[1]**.
(c) People are bitten outdoors in the evening before they go to bed **[1]**; some nets are torn, not used every night, or there are not enough for every family member **[1]**. Also accept no vector control, so mosquito numbers remain high.

**9.** (a) Open channels hold still or slow water **[1]**, creating new breeding sites for Anopheles mosquitoes in a valley that was previously dry **[1]**.
(b) Cover channels or replace them with pipes, or drain standing water at the edges **[1]**; introduce fish that eat larvae into the channels, or spray insecticide on breeding areas **[1]**.

**10.** Sanitation and sewage treatment remove the source, because human waste no longer reaches drinking water **[1]**. This protects everyone, including people who have not been vaccinated, and also reduces other water-borne infections **[1]**. Vaccine protection fades over time and does not remove the contamination, so new outbreaks are still possible **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Boil drinking water **[1]**; add chlorine tablets or liquid to stored water **[1]**.
(b) Provide handwashing stations with soap **[1]**; provide temporary latrines and repair sewers so waste does not reach water sources, or vaccinate people in the worst-affected areas **[1]**.

**12.** One mark per point; at least one benefit and one limitation of each approach and a judgement for full marks.
- Vector control benefit: draining or treating breeding areas and biological control in rice fields reduce mosquito numbers for the whole community, not just one person **[1]**.
- Vector control limitation: flooded rice fields cannot be drained without stopping farming, and spraying them could harm fish and other organisms and lead to resistance **[1]**.
- Vector control limitation: spraying and sterile male release need repeated funding and expertise **[1]**.
- Personal protection benefit: insecticide-treated nets are cheap and effective at night, and families can use them without specialist help **[1]**.
- Personal protection limitation: with few clinics, antimalarial drugs and vaccination are hard to deliver, and nets do not protect people working outdoors in the evening **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, nets and biological control in the rice fields together are most suitable, because both are low-cost and do not depend on clinics **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: saying only females "carry" malaria without the reason (blood meal for eggs).
- **Question 7(a)**: not noticing that cases peak after rainfall peaks.
- **Question 8(b)**: not explaining that Village B acts as a comparison.
- **Question 9(b)**: giving personal protection (nets) when the question asks for vector control.
- **Question 12**: listing strategies without linking them to rice fields and few clinics.
