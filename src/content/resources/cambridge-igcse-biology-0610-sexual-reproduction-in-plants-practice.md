## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which part of a flower receives pollen grains during pollination? **[1]**

A: anther

B: ovary

C: sepal

D: stigma

**2.** Name:

**(a)** the **two** parts that make up a stamen **[1]**

**(b)** the **three** parts that make up a carpel. **[1]**

**3.** State the function of:

**(a)** the petals of an insect-pollinated flower **[1]**

**(b)** the ovary. **[1]**

**4.** Define pollination. **[1]**

**5.** Which description fits the pollen grains of a wind-pollinated flower? **[1]**

A: large, spiky and made in small amounts

B: large, sticky and made in large amounts

C: small, light and made in large amounts

D: small, sticky and made in small amounts

**6.** *(Extended)* Pollen from the anther of one flower on a pea plant is carried to the stigma of another flower on the same plant. Name this type of pollination. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A grass flower has no petals. Its anthers hang outside the flower on long, thin filaments, and its stigmas are large and feathery.

**(a)** State whether this flower is insect-pollinated or wind-pollinated, and give **two** pieces of evidence from the description. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why grass flowers produce very large amounts of pollen. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the position of the anthers helps pollination. **[1]**

**8.** A student viewed pollen from two flowers, X and Y, under a microscope.

| Pollen | Diameter / mm | Surface |
|---|---|---|
| X | 0.025 | smooth |
| Y | 0.080 | covered in spikes |

**(a)** State which pollen comes from an insect-pollinated flower, and give **two** reasons for your answer. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the diameter of pollen Y is than that of pollen X. **[1]**

**9.** Four flasks each contained 20 seeds of the same type. The table shows the conditions and the results after five days.

| Flask | Conditions | Seeds germinated |
|---|---|---|
| A | moist cotton wool, air, 20 °C, light | 18 |
| B | dry cotton wool, air, 20 °C, light | 0 |
| C | moist cotton wool, nitrogen gas instead of air, 20 °C, light | 0 |
| D | moist cotton wool, air, 20 °C, dark | 18 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of seeds that germinated in flask A. **[1]**

**(b)** State what flasks B and C show about the conditions needed for germination. **[2]**

**(c)** State what flask D shows when compared with flask A. **[1]**

**(d)** State **two** variables that were kept the same in all four flasks. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Describe what happens between a pollen grain landing on a stigma and fertilisation of the ovule. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A species of orchid is pollinated only by one species of bee. The orchid must be cross-pollinated. In one region the number of these bees is falling. Discuss the effects of cross-pollination on this orchid population. **[4]**

**12.** A student writes: "When the bee landed on the flower, it fertilised it." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]

**2. (a)** **Anther and filament** [1]

**(b)** **Stigma, style and ovary** [1]

*Examiner insight:* All the parts in the list are needed for each mark; "stigma and ovary" alone does not score in (b).

**3. (a)** To **attract insects** (by colour or scent) [1]

**(b)** To **contain and protect the ovules** [1]

**4.** The **transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma** [1].

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** **Self-pollination** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Pollination between two flowers on the same plant is still self-pollination; cross-pollination needs a different plant.

**7. (a)** **Wind-pollinated** [1]. Any two of: **no petals**, so no need to attract insects; **anthers hang outside** on long filaments; **stigmas are large and feathery** [2].

**(b)** Most pollen carried by the wind **does not land on a stigma** of the same species, so large amounts make pollination more likely [1].

**(c)** Hanging outside the flower, the anthers are **shaken by the wind**, so pollen is released into the air [1].

**8. (a)** **Y** [1]. It is **larger** [1] and its **spikes** help it **cling to an insect's body** [1].

**(b)** 0.080 ÷ 0.025 = **3.2 times** [1]

**9. (a)** 18 ÷ 20 × 100 = **90%** [1]

**(b)** Flask B: **water** is needed [1]. Flask C: **oxygen** is needed [1].

**(c)** **Light is not needed** for germination [1].

**(d)** Any two of: type of seed; number of seeds; temperature; time [2].

*Examiner insight:* Temperature was the same in every flask, so this investigation cannot show that a suitable temperature is needed; that needs a flask kept cold.

**10.** A **pollen tube grows out of the pollen grain** [1] and grows **down through the style** to the ovary [1]. The tube **enters the ovule** (through the micropyle) [1]. The **pollen nucleus** moves down the tube and **fuses with the nucleus in the ovule** [1].

**11.** Cross-pollination produces **more variation** in the offspring [1], so the population has a greater **capacity to respond to changes in the environment**, such as a new disease [1]. However, the orchid **relies entirely on the bee** to carry pollen between plants [1], so as bee numbers fall, fewer flowers are pollinated, fewer seeds form and the population may decline [1].

*Examiner insight:* The syllabus names three ideas for this outcome: variation, capacity to respond to change and reliance on pollinators. Full marks need both sides.

**12.** The bee carries out **pollination**, the transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma [1]. **Fertilisation** happens later, when a **pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing pollination with fertilisation.
- Placing the stigma in the stamen or the anther in the carpel.
- Describing wind pollen as sticky.
- Giving a feature without its reason in "explain" questions.
- (Extended) Treating pollination between two flowers on one plant as cross-pollination.
- (Extended) Saying the pollen grain moves down the style instead of the pollen tube and nucleus.
