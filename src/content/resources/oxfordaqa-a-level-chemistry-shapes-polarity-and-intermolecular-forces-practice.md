## Questions

**1.** Write the formula of (a) aluminium sulfate, (b) ammonium carbonate and (c) magnesium nitride. **[3]**

**2.** Ammonia reacts with boron trifluoride, BF₃, to form H₃N→BF₃.

**(a)** State what is meant by a co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why NH₃ can form this bond and why BF₃ can accept it. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the bond between N and B is drawn in a displayed formula. **[1]**
**(d)** A nitrogen molecule contains a triple bond. State how many electrons are shared. **[1]**

**3.** Magnesium melts at about 650 °C, far above sodium at about 98 °C.

**(a)** Describe the bonding in magnesium metal. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why magnesium has the higher melting point. **[3]**

**4.** Four solids, W to Z, were tested.

| Solid | Melting point / °C | Conducts as solid? | Conducts when molten? | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | 1540 | Yes | Yes | Can be hammered into sheets |
| X | above 3000 | No | Does not melt readily | Insoluble in all solvents tried |
| Y | 54 | No | No | Soluble in hexane |
| Z | 780 | No | Yes | Dissolves in water |

For each solid, give the type of structure and explain your choice using the data. **[8]**

**5.**
**(a)** Explain why melting ice absorbs energy but freezing water releases energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Sodium chloride melts at about 801 °C and iodine at about 114 °C. Explain the difference in terms of what is
overcome on melting. **[3]**

**6.** Draw a labelled diagram of one layer of the sodium chloride structure containing nine ions, and name the force
that holds the ions together. **[4]**

**7.** Give the shape and the bond angle (or a statement of the angle) for each species. **[8]**

**(a)** BCl₃ **(b)** NH₄⁺ **(c)** ClF₃ **(d)** XeF₂

**8.**
**(a)** Use electron-pair repulsion theory to deduce the shape of the ICl₄⁻ ion. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the H–O–H angle in water (104.5°) is smaller than the H–N–H angle in ammonia (107°). **[3]**

**9.**
**(a)** Define electronegativity. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the P–Cl bond in PCl₃ is polar by adding partial charges. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why PCl₃ has a permanent dipole but SF₆, which has six polar S–F bonds, does not. **[3]**

**10.** Approximate boiling points: HF 20 °C, HCl −85 °C, HBr −67 °C, HI −35 °C.

**(a)** Explain the increase from HCl to HI. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why HF does not fit this trend. **[3]**

**11.** Ethanol and methoxymethane both have the formula C₂H₆O. Ethanol boils at about 78 °C and methoxymethane at about
−24 °C.

**(a)** Name the types of intermolecular force in each liquid. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference in boiling point. **[2]**
**(c)** Show a hydrogen bond between two ethanol molecules, including lone pair and partial charges. **[2]**

**12.** The density of liquid water at 0 °C is 1.000 g cm⁻³ and that of ice is 0.917 g cm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in volume when 275 g of water freezes. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why ice is less dense than liquid water. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻ need two and three respectively to balance: **Al₂(SO₄)₃** [1]. (b) NH₄⁺ and CO₃²⁻: **(NH₄)₂CO₃** [1]. (c) Mg²⁺ and N³⁻: **Mg₃N₂** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Brackets matter. A compound ion needed more than once written without brackets changes the formula and is likely to lose the credit.

**2.** (a) A shared pair of electrons where both electrons come from one atom. [1]
**(b)** N in NH₃ has a lone pair to donate [1]. B in BF₃ has only six electrons in its outer shell, so it has room to accept a pair [1].
**(c)** An arrow pointing from N to B. [1]
**(d)** **Six** electrons (three shared pairs). [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which atom donates and which accepts, and name the lone pair. An arrow drawn from B to N shows the bond the wrong way round.

**3.** (a) A lattice of Mg²⁺ ions [1] attracted to a sea of delocalised electrons [1].
**(b)** Mg²⁺ has a higher charge than Na⁺ and each atom gives two electrons, so there are more delocalised electrons [1]. The attraction between ions and electrons is stronger [1], so more energy is needed to overcome it [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both substances. Mentioning only the charge on magnesium is less secure than also linking it to the number of delocalised electrons.

**4.** **W is metallic** [1]: it conducts as a solid because delocalised electrons move, and is malleable [1]. **X is macromolecular (giant covalent)** [1]: very high melting point from covalent bonds throughout, with no mobile charges [1]. **Y is molecular** [1]: low melting point as only weak intermolecular forces are overcome, and no charged particles [1]. **Z is ionic** [1]: conducts only when molten because ions are then free to move, and dissolves in water [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A type with no reason from the data earns little. Link each property to the particles that carry charge or the forces that must be broken.

**5.** (a) Melting needs energy to overcome the hydrogen bonds between molecules [1]. When water freezes these bonds form and energy is released [1].
**(b)** Melting sodium chloride overcomes strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions [1]. Melting iodine overcomes only weak induced dipole–dipole forces between I₂ molecules, and the I–I covalent bonds are not broken [1]. Much less energy is needed, so iodine melts at a lower temperature [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For a molecular solid, an answer that says covalent bonds break on melting is a common way to lose credit. Name the intermolecular force.

**6.** A 3 × 3 square grid [1], with ions alternating so each Na⁺ has Cl⁻ neighbours [1], labelled Na⁺ and Cl⁻ with five of one and four of the other [1]:
```
Na+ Cl- Na+
Cl- Na+ Cl-
Na+ Cl- Na+
```
The force is **electrostatic attraction** between oppositely charged ions. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the count and the alternation. Two like ions touching along a row is a common slip in a sketch.

**7.** (a) **Trigonal planar** [1], **120°** [1]. (b) **Tetrahedral** [1], **109.5°** [1]. (c) **T-shaped** (five pairs, two lone) [1], angle slightly less than 90° [1]. (d) **Linear** (five pairs, three lone) [1], **180°** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A shape name alone is only half the task. Give the angle, and for lone-pair species mention that the pairs are not all bonding.

**8.** (a) Electrons: 7 + 4 + 1 = 12, so six pairs [1]; four bonding and two lone [1]; the lone pairs sit opposite each other, giving **square planar**, 90° [1].
**(b)** Both have four electron pairs in a tetrahedral arrangement [1]. Water has two lone pairs and ammonia has one [1]. Lone pair–lone pair and lone pair–bond pair repulsion are greater than bond pair–bond pair repulsion, so water's bonds are squeezed closer [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the repulsion order in your own words. Saying "lone pairs repel more" without comparing them to bonding pairs is thin.

**9.** (a) How strongly an atom pulls the shared pair of a covalent bond towards itself. [1]
**(b)** P^δ+–Cl^δ−. [1]
**(c)** PCl₃ is pyramidal, so its three dipoles do not cancel [1]. SF₆ is octahedral and symmetrical [1], so its six equal S–F dipoles cancel [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A "polar bonds, therefore polar molecule" answer is incomplete. State the shape and the cancellation.

**10.** (a) The molecules have more electrons, so stronger induced dipole–dipole forces [1], needing more energy to overcome [1].
**(b)** HF has a hydrogen atom bonded to fluorine, which has a lone pair [1], so it forms hydrogen bonds between molecules [1]. These are stronger than the forces in HCl, so more energy is needed and the boiling point is higher than the trend predicts [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what is overcome (intermolecular forces), not the H–F bond.

**11.** (a) Ethanol: hydrogen bonding, permanent dipole–dipole and induced dipole–dipole forces [1]. Methoxymethane: permanent dipole–dipole and induced dipole–dipole forces only [1].
**(b)** Ethanol has an O–H group, so its molecules hydrogen bond [1]. Methoxymethane has no hydrogen on oxygen, so less energy is needed to separate the molecules [1].
**(c)** A dotted line from the δ+ hydrogen of one ethanol molecule to a lone pair on the δ− oxygen of the other [1], with the O–H···O atoms in a straight line and the δ+ and δ− shown [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A hydrogen bond drawn to the oxygen atom but not to a lone pair is easy to mark down. Show the lone pair.

**12.** (a) Liquid volume = 275 ÷ 1.000 = 275 cm³ [1]. Ice volume = 275 ÷ 0.917 = 299.9 cm³ [1]. Increase = 24.9 ÷ 275 × 100 = **9.1 %** [1].
**(b)** In ice each molecule forms hydrogen bonds to four others [1] in an open tetrahedral lattice with gaps [1]. These hydrogen bonds hold molecules further apart than in liquid water [1]. On melting some bonds break and the molecules pack more closely [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the working to 3 significant figures at least, and avoid saying the bonds "expand". The lattice holds molecules apart.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "bonds break" for a molecular substance when the answer needs intermolecular forces.
- Stating a bond angle without the lone pair reasoning, or giving 109.5° for a molecule with lone pairs.
- Counting only the atoms around the centre and forgetting the charge of an ion when finding electron pairs.
- Saying a molecule is non-polar because the bonds are non-polar, instead of explaining cancellation.
- Missing the lone pair and the N, O or F link in hydrogen bonding answers.
- Describing a conductor without naming the particles that move.
- Using "weaker forces" without saying which forces are weaker, or comparing only one substance.
- Drawing dative bonds as plain lines or lattices with like ions side by side.
