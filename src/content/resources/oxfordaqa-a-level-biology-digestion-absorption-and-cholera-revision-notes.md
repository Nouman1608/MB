## 3.2.2.1 Digestive system at a glance

Food path: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, duodenum, ileum, colon, rectum. Glands you need: salivary glands and pancreas.

| Part | One-line job |
|---|---|
| Salivary glands | Saliva with amylase |
| Oesophagus | Moves food by muscular contraction |
| Stomach | Churns food; protein digestion starts |
| Pancreas | Pancreatic juice into the duodenum |
| Duodenum | Receives pancreatic juice and bile salts |
| Ileum | Main absorption site |
| Colon | Absorbs remaining water |
| Rectum | Stores faeces |

Key terms:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Digestion | Hydrolysis of large molecules by enzymes into smaller ones |
| Absorption | Products cross the gut wall into the body |
| Assimilation | Absorbed substances become part of cells and tissues |
| Egestion | Undigested material leaves as faeces |

## 3.2.2.2 Digestion

| Food type | Enzyme | Substrate and products |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Amylase (saliva and pancreas) | Starch to maltose |
| Carbohydrate | Membrane-bound disaccharidases | Maltose to glucose + glucose; sucrose to glucose + fructose; lactose to glucose + galactose |
| Lipid | Lipase | Triglyceride to monoglyceride + fatty acids |
| Protein | Endopeptidase | Cuts internal peptide bonds, giving shorter chains |
| Protein | Exopeptidase | Removes amino acids from the ends of chains |
| Protein | Membrane-bound dipeptidase | Dipeptide to two amino acids |

Role of bile salts in steps:

1. Bile salts reach the duodenum.
2. They emulsify lipids into small droplets.
3. Surface area increases, so lipase works faster.
4. Later they help form micelles.

Remember: bile salts are not enzymes, and they do not chemically digest fat.

Method for peptide-bond questions:

1. Bonds in a chain = amino acids − 1.
2. Each endopeptidase cut removes one bond and adds one fragment.
3. Fragments = cuts + 1 (for one chain).
4. Remaining bonds are hydrolysed by exopeptidases and dipeptidases.

Worked reminder: a 60-amino-acid chain has 59 bonds. Three cuts leave 56 bonds and give 4 fragments.

## 3.2.2.3 Absorption

### Ileum histology

| Feature | Link to function |
|---|---|
| Villi and microvilli | Large surface area |
| Single layer of epithelium | Short diffusion path |
| Capillary network | Maintains gradients by removing products |
| Lacteal | Carries chylomicrons |
| Goblet cells | Mucus |
| Mitochondria | ATP for active transport |

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size, with both in one unit (convert first). If a drawing is 36 mm and the real structure is 0.45 mm, magnification is 36 ÷ 0.45 = ×80.

### Absorption of glucose and amino acids

Method in steps:

1. Sodium-potassium pump in the basal membrane removes sodium ions from the cell using ATP.
2. Cell sodium ion concentration is low.
3. Sodium ions enter from the lumen down their gradient through a co-transporter.
4. Glucose or an amino acid enters with them, against its own gradient.
5. Glucose or amino acid leaves into the blood by facilitated diffusion.

| Process | Needs ATP? | Direction relative to gradient |
|---|---|---|
| Diffusion (facilitated) | No | Down |
| Active transport | Yes, directly | Against |
| Co-transport | Not directly (the pump uses it) | Solute enters against its gradient, sodium ions down theirs |

Worked reminder: uptake of 50 units falls to 14 units when sodium ions are removed. Fall = (50 − 14) ÷ 50 × 100 = 72%.

### Lipids

1. Fatty acids and monoglycerides join with bile salts to form micelles.
2. Micelles release the products at the epithelial surface.
3. Products diffuse across the membrane because they are lipid-soluble.
4. Inside, they are rebuilt into triglycerides and packaged with protein and phospholipid as chylomicrons.
5. Chylomicrons leave by exocytosis and enter the lacteals, then the lymph.

## 3.2.3.1 Cholera

Pathogen: *Vibrio cholerae*, usually spread through contaminated water or food. It secretes a toxin.

Sequence:

1. The toxin increases chloride ion secretion into the lumen.
2. Sodium ions follow, so lumen ion concentration rises.
3. Lumen water potential falls below that of the cells and blood.
4. Water moves out by osmosis.
5. Severe watery diarrhoea and dehydration follow.

Worked reminder: a 50 kg patient loses 500 mL of stool per hour for 10 hours. Volume = 0.5 × 10 = 5 L, or 5 ÷ 50 × 100 = 10% of body mass if 1 L is about 1 kg.

## 3.2.3.2 Oral rehydration solutions

Contents: water, glucose and salts including sodium ions.

Why it works: glucose and sodium ions are still absorbed by co-transport, and water follows by osmosis. Why not just water: water alone supplies no glucose to drive sodium ion uptake, so less water follows by osmosis.

Worked reminder: 1.5 litres of an ORS with 75 mmol of glucose per litre needs 112.5 mmol of glucose, which is 20.3 g using 180.16 g per mol.

Discussion points:

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Applications | Cheap, can be used outside hospital, less stool output when concentration is reduced, useful for other diarrhoeal diseases |
| Implications | Needs clean water; treats the symptom, not the infection; costs of change; severe cases still need intravenous fluid |
| Ethics of trials | Informed consent (parents for children); right to withdraw; no untreated placebo; ethics committee approval; avoid exploiting vulnerable people; fair access afterwards |

## Where each enzyme acts

| Enzyme or secretion | Source and site |
|---|---|
| Salivary amylase | Salivary glands; mouth |
| Endopeptidase (acid conditions) | Stomach |
| Pancreatic amylase, lipase and peptidases | Pancreas; duodenum |
| Bile salts | Enter the duodenum in bile |
| Disaccharidases and dipeptidases | Cell-surface membrane of ileum epithelial cells |

Link each location to the pH or conditions: stomach contents are acidic, while pancreatic juice makes the duodenum less acidic so that pancreatic enzymes can work. Salivary amylase does not continue to work for long once food meets stomach acid.

## Evaluating trial data

Use this order when a question gives you results from a trial of a new ORS:

1. Compare the two means with units, and calculate the difference.
2. Express the difference as a percentage of the standard treatment.
3. Comment on sample size and spread, because a difference between two means may be down to chance.
4. Check that the groups were similar and that only the ORS differed.
5. Give a conclusion that does not go beyond the data.

Worked reminder: standard ORS group, mean duration of diarrhoea 70 hours; new ORS group, 56 hours. Difference = 70 − 56 = 14 hours. Percentage shorter = 14 ÷ 70 × 100 = 20%. You cannot say the new ORS is better without knowing sample size and variation.

## Must-know distinctions

- Digestion (breakdown) versus absorption (crossing the wall) versus assimilation (use in the body).
- Egestion (undigested food) versus excretion (metabolic waste).
- Endopeptidase (inside) versus exopeptidase (ends) versus dipeptidase (dipeptides, on the membrane).
- Micelle (carries products to the surface) versus chylomicron (carries triglycerides out of the cell).
- Active transport versus co-transport.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two glands whose secretions contain amylase.
2. State the substrate and the product of amylase.
3. Give the products of lactose digestion.
4. Explain why bile salts speed up lipid digestion.
5. A 75-amino-acid chain is cut by an endopeptidase at 4 places. How many bonds remain and how many fragments form?
6. Where in the epithelial cell is the sodium-potassium pump found?
7. Describe how a lacteal contributes to absorption.
8. A micrograph shows a villus 52 mm long. The real villus is 0.65 mm. Calculate the magnification.
9. Explain why cholera toxin causes water to enter the lumen.
10. Suggest why an ORS contains glucose.
11. State two ethical points about trialling a new ORS in children.

### Answers

1. Salivary glands and pancreas.
2. Starch; maltose.
3. Glucose and galactose.
4. They emulsify lipid into small droplets, increasing surface area for lipase.
5. 74 bonds in the chain; 70 remain; 5 fragments.
6. In the basal membrane (the side next to the blood).
7. It takes up chylomicrons into the lymph.
8. 52 ÷ 0.65 = ×80.
9. The toxin raises chloride ion secretion, which lowers lumen water potential, so water moves out by osmosis.
10. Glucose is co-transported with sodium ions, so sodium ions and water are absorbed.
11. For example: informed consent from a parent or guardian; no untreated placebo for dehydrated patients. Ethics committee approval and the right to withdraw are also acceptable.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "enzymes break down food" without naming the substrate and the product.
- Describing bile salts as enzymes.
- Giving "increases surface area" for the ileum without saying which structure (villi, microvilli).
- Leaving out the pump when explaining co-transport.
- Confusing a micelle with a chylomicron.
- Saying cholera toxin "stops water absorption" instead of explaining chloride ion secretion and the water potential gradient.
- Giving the units wrongly in magnification: convert mm and micrometres before dividing.
- One-sided ethics answers with no reason attached to each point.
