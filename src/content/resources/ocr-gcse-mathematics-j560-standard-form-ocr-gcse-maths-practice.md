These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 3.02, Standard form (3.02a and 3.02b). All of it is content for both tiers. Questions 10 and 11(b) need a calculator; work the rest without one. All masses, populations and distances are invented. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer and context questions.

Revise first with the [3.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-standard-form-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [3.02 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-standard-form-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Write each number in standard form.

**(a)** 5 400 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.0071 **[1]**

**(c)** 38 600 000 **[1]**

**2.** Write each number as an ordinary number.

**(a)** 2.05 × 10⁴ **[1]**

**(b)** 9 × 10⁻⁶ **[1]**

**3.** Here are four numbers: 0.7 × 10³, 7 × 10⁻², 10 × 10⁴, 1.07 × 10⁹. Write down the two numbers that are in standard form. **[2]**

**4.** Write 56 × 10⁻³ in standard form. **[1]**

**5.** Write these numbers in order, smallest first: 6.2 × 10⁻³, 5.9 × 10⁻², 0.0061, 60 × 10⁻⁴ **[2]**

**6.** Work out (3 × 10⁵) × (2 × 10⁻²). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Work out, giving each answer in standard form.

**(a)** (7 × 10⁶) × (5 × 10³) **[2]**

**(b)** (1.8 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (6 × 10²) **[2]**

**8.** Work out, giving each answer in standard form.

**(a)** 4.6 × 10⁸ + 5 × 10⁷ **[2]**

**(b)** 2 × 10⁻³ − 6 × 10⁻⁴ **[2]**

**9.** Work out (6 × 10⁻³)². Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**10.** *(Calculator allowed.)* Give each answer in standard form.

**(a)** (8.4 × 10¹²) ÷ (3.5 × 10⁻⁴) **[2]**

**(b)** √(6.4 × 10⁹) **[2]**

**11.** A grain of sand has a mass of 6.7 × 10⁻⁷ kg.

**(a)** Find the mass of 3 × 10⁹ grains. Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**(b)** *(Calculator allowed.)* Find the number of grains in 1 kg. Give your answer in standard form, correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** Country A has a population of 6.8 × 10⁷. Country B has a population of 3.4 × 10⁵.

**(a)** How many times larger is the population of A than the population of B? **[2]**

**(b)** Find the total population of the two countries, in standard form. **[2]**

**13.** Tom writes (4 × 10³) + (5 × 10³) = 9 × 10⁶.

**(a)** Explain Tom's mistake. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the correct answer. **[1]**

**14.** A signal travels at 3 × 10⁸ metres per second. How many seconds does it take to travel 1.5 × 10¹¹ metres? **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **5.4 × 10³** [1]

**(b)** **7.1 × 10⁻³** [1]

**(c)** **3.86 × 10⁷** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the point moves three places to the right, so the index is negative.

**2. (a)** **20 500** [1]

**(b)** **0.000009** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* For 10⁻⁶ there are five zeros after the point before the 9, because the 9 is in the sixth decimal place.

**3.** **7 × 10⁻²** [1]; **1.07 × 10⁹** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 0.7 is below 1 and 10 is not less than 10, so neither of the others qualifies.

**4.** **5.6 × 10⁻²** [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* 56 = 5.6 × 10, so the index goes up by 1: 10 × 10⁻³ = 10⁻².

**5.** All values in a comparable form, such as 6.2 × 10⁻³, 59 × 10⁻³, 6.1 × 10⁻³, 6.0 × 10⁻³ [1]; **60 × 10⁻⁴, 0.0061, 6.2 × 10⁻³, 5.9 × 10⁻²** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 5.9 × 10⁻² is the largest because 10⁻² is a bigger power of 10 than 10⁻³.

**6.** 6 × 10³ from 3 × 2 and 10⁵⁺⁽⁻²⁾ [1]; **6 × 10³** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Adding 5 and −2 gives 3.

**7. (a)** 35 × 10⁹ [1]; **3.5 × 10¹⁰** [1]

**(b)** 0.3 × 10⁻⁶ [1]; **3 × 10⁻⁷** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b), 10⁻⁴ ÷ 10² = 10⁻⁶. Then 0.3 needs tidying, so the index drops by 1.

**8. (a)** 4.6 × 10⁸ + 0.5 × 10⁸ [1]; **5.1 × 10⁸** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 10⁻³ − 0.6 × 10⁻³ [1]; **1.4 × 10⁻³** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Match the powers of 10 before adding or subtracting. The indices are not added.

**9.** 6² × (10⁻³)² = 36 × 10⁻⁶ [1]; **3.6 × 10⁻⁵** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Square both parts of the bracket.

**10. (a)** Correct entry with the ×10ˣ key, display 2.4 × 10¹⁶ [1]; **2.4 × 10¹⁶** [1]

**(b)** 80 000 [1]; **8 × 10⁴** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A calculator display that shows 2.4 with a small 16 beside it must be written as 2.4 × 10¹⁶.

**11. (a)** 6.7 × 3 = 20.1 and 10⁻⁷ × 10⁹ = 10² [1]; 20.1 × 10² = **2.01 × 10³ kg** [1]

**(b)** 1 ÷ (6.7 × 10⁻⁷) = 1 492 537... [1]; **1.49 × 10⁶** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b), bracket the divisor or use the ×10ˣ key, otherwise the calculator divides by 6.7 and then multiplies by 10⁻⁷.

**12. (a)** (6.8 × 10⁷) ÷ (3.4 × 10⁵) [1]; **200 times** [1]

**(b)** 6.8 × 10⁷ + 0.034 × 10⁷ [1]; **6.834 × 10⁷** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer of 2 × 10² in part (a) is also correct. "How many times" calls for a division.

**13. (a)** He multiplied the powers of 10 (or added the indices) when he should only add the 4 and the 5, because both are thousands [1]

**(b)** **9 × 10³** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 4 thousand plus 5 thousand is 9 thousand. Indices are added only when numbers are multiplied.

**14.** (1.5 × 10¹¹) ÷ (3 × 10⁸) [1]; **500 seconds** (5 × 10²) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Time = distance ÷ speed. 1.5 ÷ 3 = 0.5 and 10¹¹ ÷ 10⁸ = 10³, so 0.5 × 10³ = 500.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing A outside the range 1 to 10.
- Getting the sign of the index wrong when converting small numbers.
- Adding indices in an addition or subtraction sum.
- Tidying in the wrong direction.
- Squaring only part of a bracket.
- Copying a calculator display instead of writing standard form.
- Comparing the A values before the powers of 10 when ordering.
