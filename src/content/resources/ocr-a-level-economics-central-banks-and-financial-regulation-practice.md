It is macroeconomics content with no separate stage. Every figure is invented, and Country K and Country P are illustrative. ## Questions

**1.** Explain two functions of a central bank. **[4]**

**2.** In Country K the price index rises from 150.0 to 156.6 in one year. The inflation target is 2.0%.
**(a)** Calculate the rate of inflation and its gap from the target. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one action the central bank might take. **[1]**

**3.** The central bank cuts its policy rate by 1.0 percentage point. Commercial banks pass on 60% to variable-rate loans.
**(a)** Calculate the monthly saving on an interest-only loan of CU 120,000. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why pass-through might be less than 100%. **[2]**

**4.** A bond pays a fixed coupon of CU 4 per CU 100 of face value. The central bank buys bonds and the market price rises from CU 100 to CU 105.
**(a)** Calculate the change in the yield, to two decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how this could support a secondary objective of the central bank. **[2]**

**5.** A bank has risk-weighted assets of CU 600m and capital of CU 54m.
**(a)** Calculate its capital ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** The regulator raises the minimum to 12%. Calculate the capital shortfall. **[2]**
**(c)** If the bank cannot raise capital, calculate the fall in its risk-weighted assets and as a percentage. **[2]**
**(d)** State one benefit of the higher requirement. **[1]**

**6.** A bank has deposits of CU 500m and liquid assets of CU 60m. A rumour leads 25% of depositors to withdraw.
**(a)** Calculate the liquidity shortfall. **[2]**
**(b)** The central bank accepts collateral of CU 80m with a 15% haircut. Calculate how much it will lend and say whether this covers the shortfall. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the interest on CU 65m borrowed for 14 days at 6% a year, to the nearest CU 100. **[2]**

**7.** Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of a central bank acting as lender of last resort. **[8]**

**8.** Evaluate the effectiveness of an independent central bank compared with one under government control. **[9]**

**9.** A supply shock raises costs, and inflation moves well above the target while output falls. Evaluate the case for a central bank continuing to target inflation. **[8]**

**10.** A pool of mortgages worth CU 800m has a junior tranche of 6% of the pool that absorbs the first losses. The loss on each defaulted mortgage is 50%.
**(a)** Calculate the loss if 5% of the pool defaults, and say whether the junior tranche absorbs it. **[2]**
**(b)** Repeat for a default rate of 14%. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two ways in which changes in the regulation of financial institutions may contribute to a financial crisis such as 2007-08. **[4]**

**11.** Country P has a budget deficit of 8% of a GDP of CU 50bn. As a condition of a loan, an international organisation asks it to cut the deficit to 5%. Assume the cut is all in government spending and the multiplier is 1.5.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in government spending needed and the fall in GDP. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate the role of the IMF and the World Bank in regulating the global financial system. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Function stated, such as monetary policy, issuing currency, banker to the government, banker to banks, lender of last resort or supervision [1]. Development of how it works, for example setting the policy rate to influence demand and inflation [1]. A second function [1]. Development, for example lending to solvent but illiquid banks to prevent contagion [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two named functions with no explanation usually earn less than one function that is clearly developed, so give each a "which means" sentence.

**2. (a)** (156.6 ÷ 150.0 − 1) × 100 [1] = **4.4%** [1]. Gap = 4.4 − 2.0 = **2.4 percentage points** [1]. **(b)** Raise the policy rate, which raises borrowing costs and slows demand and price rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula and the unit. A bare "4.4" leaves the reader to guess whether it is a percentage or points.

**3. (a)** Pass-through is 1.0 × 0.60 = 0.6 percentage points [1]. Yearly saving = 120,000 × 0.006 = CU 720 [1]. Monthly = 720 ÷ 12 = **CU 60** [1]. **(b)** Fixed-rate borrowers do not benefit until they refinance [1]. Banks may keep part of the cut to protect their margins, or because their own funding costs have not fallen [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Banks do not pass it on" is a restatement. The reason has to say why, such as fixed rates or margins.

**4. (a)** Before: 4 ÷ 100 = 4.00% [1]. After: 4 ÷ 105 = 3.81% [1]. Change = **a fall of 0.19 percentage points** [1]. **(b)** Lower long-term yields reduce borrowing costs for firms and households, so investment and spending rise [1]. This raises output and employment, which supports the growth and employment objective if inflation is on target [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say whether the yield rises or falls. A positive-looking "0.19" without "fall" can be read as a rise.

**5. (a)** 54 ÷ 600 = **9.0%** [1]. **(b)** Required = 600 × 0.12 = CU 72m [1]. Shortfall = 72 − 54 = **CU 18m** [1]. **(c)** Supportable assets = 54 ÷ 0.12 = CU 450m [1]. Fall = 150m, which is **25%** of 600m [1]. **(d)** The bank can absorb larger losses, so failure is less likely [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), identify that the bank cuts lending rather than just repeating the shortfall. This links the number to its economic effect.

**6. (a)** Withdrawals = 500 × 0.25 = CU 125m [1]. Shortfall = 125 − 60 = **CU 65m** [1]. **(b)** Lending = 80 × 0.85 = **CU 68m** [1]. This covers the CU 65m shortfall [1]. **(c)** 65,000,000 × 0.06 × 14 ÷ 365 = CU 149,589 [1], which is **about CU 149,600** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a verdict when asked "say whether". A figure of 68 with no comment on the shortfall leaves the point unfinished.

**7.** Advantages: stops a run on a solvent bank spreading, because lending restores confidence [1]; protects the payment system and credit supply to firms and households [1]. Disadvantages: moral hazard, since banks expect rescue and take more risk [1], developed, for example a risky bet looks more attractive when losses are capped [1]; taxpayers bear the cost if collateral loses value [1]; in a crisis it is hard to distinguish a solvent from an insolvent bank, so support may go to a failed firm [1]. Evaluation: penalty rates, haircuts and tough regulation reduce moral hazard [1]. Judgement, for example that the benefits outweigh the costs provided the support is conditional and paired with strong regulation [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A balanced list is not yet a judgement. End with a conclusion that names the condition on which your view depends.

**8.** For independence: insulated from the election cycle, so no pre-election cuts [1]; credible target lowers expected inflation and wage demands [1]; expert decisions [1]. Against independence: unelected officials, a democratic deficit [1]; monetary policy may conflict with government fiscal policy [1]; may be slow to support growth if focused on inflation [1]. For control: co-ordination and direct accountability [1]. Evaluation: much depends on institutions, accountability and clarity of the target, and governments often set the target for an independent bank [1]. Judgement with a reason [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two models throughout. Writing a paragraph on each and no direct comparison tends to leave the evaluation thin.

**9.** For: a clear target anchors expectations so a one-off price rise does not become a wage-price spiral [1]; credibility is hard to rebuild if a target is dropped [1]; accountability continues [1]. Against: the shock is cost-push, so tight policy cuts output and employment further without removing the cause [1]; the secondary objectives suffer [1]; data are backward-looking and policy has a lag [1]. Evaluation: a flexible time horizon, or looking through a temporary shock, reduces the cost [1]. Judgement, for example the target is worth keeping if the bank explains its path and the shock is temporary [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the argument to the supply shock in the stem. Generic inflation-targeting points do not use the context.

**10. (a)** 800 × 0.05 × 0.50 = **CU 20m** [1]. The junior tranche is 0.06 × 800 = CU 48m, so it absorbs the loss [1]. **(b)** 800 × 0.14 × 0.50 = **CU 56m** [1]. The loss exceeds CU 48m by **CU 8m**, which passes to other tranches [1]. **(c)** Any two, each with a link. For example: looser rules separating retail and investment banking let deposit-taking banks take on trading risk [1], so losses threatened depositors [1]. Securitisation and shadow banking sat outside capital and liquidity rules [1], so risk built up unseen and spread through the system [1]. Reliance on ratings and models is another valid pair. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each change needs its consequence. Naming "deregulation" alone is not an explanation.

**11. (a)** Deficit now = 0.08 × 50 = CU 4.0bn [1]. Target = 0.05 × 50 = CU 2.5bn, so spending falls by **CU 1.5bn** [1]. GDP falls by 1.5 × 1.5 = **CU 2.25bn** [1], which is **4.5%** of GDP [1]. **(b)** The IMF monitors economies, lends to members with balance of payments problems and gives advice and training [1]. The World Bank provides longer-term development finance [1]. Neither supervises individual banks, which is a national task [1]. Conditionality can deepen a downturn, as in part (a) [1]. Judgement, for example their influence depends on whether governments follow advice and on the fairness of voting power, so they support rather than replace national regulators [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use your answer to part (a) in the evaluation. It shows how the condition could affect the country, which general criticism does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a calculation without the formula, or leaving out the unit (percent, percentage points, CU).
- Using "banks do not pass it on" as the reason for incomplete pass-through, without saying why.
- Describing the lender of last resort without moral hazard, or with no judgement.
- Treating independence as meaning the government has no say.
- Applying an inflation-target argument to demand-pull inflation when the stem describes a supply shock.
- Naming deregulation without a causal chain to the crisis.
- Calling the IMF a regulator of banks.
- Not linking a number to its economic meaning, such as lending falling when capital rules tighten.
