This guide goes deeper into section **3.1.3 Animal tissues, organs and systems** of the OxfordAQA International
GCSE Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered
and any part of the specification may be assessed on either paper.

The [Organisation study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation/) covers the whole topic briefly.
This page works through the three tissues, the stomach and the digestive system in detail, with new worked examples
on classifying tissues in organs, volumes of digestive juices and the absorption of water. Afterwards, use the
[Organisation revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation-revision-notes/) and the
[Animal tissues, organs and systems practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-animal-tissues-organs-and-systems-practice/).
Digestion itself (enzymes, bile, absorption) is developed in 3.2.4, so this page only describes the structure.

## What 3.1.3 asks you to know

| Statements | Content |
|---|---|
| a | Three animal tissues: muscular, glandular and epithelial, and what each does |
| b | The stomach as an organ containing all three tissues, and what each tissue does there |
| c | The digestive system as an organ system, and the job of each part |

## The three tissues

A **tissue** is a group of cells with similar structure and function (3.1.2). The specification names three animal
tissues. Learn each as a pair: the tissue and its job.

| Tissue | What it does | Where you might meet it |
|---|---|---|
| Muscular tissue | Can **contract** to bring about movement | Heart wall, gut wall, limb muscles |
| Glandular tissue | Can **produce substances** such as enzymes and hormones | Pancreas, salivary glands |
| Epithelial tissue | **Covers** some parts of the body | The outside and inside surfaces of organs |

Notice that the key verbs are different: muscles *contract*, glands *produce*, epithelium *covers*. A cell or tissue
can only do the job the specification gives it, so choose the verb carefully in an answer.

### Why the tissue matches the job

- Muscle tissue is made of cells that can shorten. When the cells shorten together, the tissue pulls and the part
  of the body moves.
- Glandular tissue is made of cells that make a product and release it. Cells that make enzymes (proteins) have
  many ribosomes, and they need energy, so they also have many mitochondria (see 3.1.1).
- Epithelial tissue forms a layer, so it separates the inside of an organ from the surroundings and protects the
  tissues beneath.

### Worked example 1: which tissue?

Invented descriptions. Decide which of the three tissues is described.

| Description | Tissue | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cells in the wall of the heart that shorten together | Muscular | They contract to bring about movement |
| Cells in a salivary gland that release saliva | Glandular | They produce and release a substance |
| A thin layer of cells on the inner surface of a tube | Epithelial | It covers a surface |
| Cells in the pancreas that release the hormone insulin | Glandular | Produce hormones |
| Cells that squeeze food along the gut | Muscular | They contract to move the contents |

## The stomach: one organ, three tissues

An organ is made of tissues, and one organ may contain several. The specification's example is the **stomach**.

| Tissue in the stomach | Job in the stomach |
|---|---|
| Muscular tissue | Allows the contents to move through the digestive system |
| Glandular tissue | Produces digestive juices |
| Epithelial tissue | Covers the outside and the inside of the stomach |

The stomach is therefore not "a muscle" or "a gland". It is an **organ** because it combines the three tissues, and
the combination can do more than any one tissue alone. The muscle churns the food and mixes it with juice, the
gland supplies the juice, and the epithelium forms the covering layer.

### Worked example 2: classifying the tissues in a described organ

Invented stimulus. "A section of the wall of an organ shows an inner layer of cells with a smooth surface, a
middle layer of cells that are long and able to shorten, and, in tiny pits, groups of cells that release a
fluid." Name the three tissues and one function of each.

```
inner layer       → epithelial tissue → covers the inside of the organ
middle layer      → muscular tissue   → contracts to move the contents
cells in the pits → glandular tissue  → produces a fluid (a digestive juice)
```

Because the section contains three tissues, the structure is an organ, and the combination matches the stomach.

## The digestive system as an organ system

An **organ system** is a group of organs that perform a particular function. The digestive system is a system in
which humans and other mammals **exchange substances with the environment**. The specification lists these parts.

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Glands (for example the pancreas and salivary glands) | Produce digestive juices |
| Stomach and small intestine | Where digestion occurs |
| Liver | Produces bile |
| Small intestine | Where the absorption of soluble food occurs |
| Large intestine | Where water is absorbed from the undigested food, producing faeces |

Several organs have more than one job: the small intestine is named twice, once for digestion and once for
absorption. Read questions carefully for which job is being asked about.

A note on wording. The liver **produces** bile; the specification does not say where bile is stored, so you
should write "the liver produces bile" and no more unless the question gives you more information. Also, the
glands (pancreas, salivary glands) are organs that make digestive juices; they are not the same as the glandular
tissue of the stomach, though both contain glandular tissue.

### Worked example 3: volumes of digestive juice

Invented data. In one day a person's digestive glands might release these volumes.

| Gland or organ | Saliva | Stomach juice | Pancreatic juice | Bile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume (dm³) | 1.5 | 2.0 | 1.2 | 0.8 |

```
total = 1.5 + 2.0 + 1.2 + 0.8 = 5.5 dm³
percentage from the stomach = 2.0 ÷ 5.5 × 100 = 36.4%  (3 s.f.)
percentage from the pancreas = 1.2 ÷ 5.5 × 100 = 21.8%  (3 s.f.)
```

The stomach's glandular tissue contributes the largest share of the juices in this dataset. A glandular organ that
releases a large volume needs a good blood supply to provide water and raw materials. The numbers are invented
for practice, so do not memorise them.

### Worked example 4: water in the large intestine

In a model of the large intestine, 1.50 dm³ of fluid enters in a day. Of this, 1.40 dm³ is absorbed.

```
water left to leave in faeces = 1.50 − 1.40 = 0.10 dm³
percentage absorbed = 1.40 ÷ 1.50 × 100 = 93.3%  (3 s.f.)
```

The large intestine absorbs water from the undigested food, so the faeces contain only a small proportion of the
water that entered. If the large intestine absorbed less water, the faeces would be more watery and the body
could lose too much water.

## Moving between levels

A well-formed answer follows the levels: cell, tissue, organ, organ system.

- *Cells* of the stomach wall are specialised (muscle cells, gland cells, epithelial cells).
- Groups of similar cells form *tissues*.
- Three tissues make the stomach, an *organ*.
- The stomach, small intestine, liver, glands and large intestine form the digestive *system*.

## Comparing organs in the system

| Organ | Main tissue types you can name | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Stomach | Muscular, glandular, epithelial | Digestion; mixes food with juice |
| Small intestine | Not specified | Digestion and absorption of soluble food |
| Pancreas | Glandular (a gland) | Produces digestive juices |
| Salivary gland | Glandular | Produces digestive juice (saliva) |
| Liver | Not specified | Produces bile |
| Large intestine | Not specified | Absorbs water, producing faeces |

Only the stomach's tissues are fixed by the specification. For other organs, questions will give you information
and ask you to apply the tissue names. Use the verbs: contract, produce, cover.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that muscular tissue "produces movement" with no mention of **contracting**.
- Saying that glandular tissue produces "enzymes and hormones" only. It can produce **substances such as** these,
  including digestive juices.
- Treating the stomach as a tissue, or listing only one tissue for it.
- Saying that digestion occurs in the large intestine. Water absorption occurs there.
- Writing that the liver "makes enzymes" for digestion. The specification says the liver produces **bile**.
- Mixing up where soluble food is absorbed (small intestine) and where water is absorbed from undigested food
  (large intestine).

## Exam technique

- **Name** and **state** questions: use the exact tissue name and the verb from the specification.
- **Explain** questions: link tissue, organ and job in one chain, for example "glandular tissue produces
  digestive juices, which is needed because ...".
- Where a question gives a new organ, apply the three tissue names to each described feature, as in worked
  example 2.
- In calculations, give the unit (dm³) and the percentage to a sensible number of significant figures.
