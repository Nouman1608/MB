**Data for all questions:**

| Carbon | δ / ppm | Proton | δ / ppm |
|---|---|---|---|
| alkyl C–C | 5–55 | alkyl C–H | 0.7–1.8 |
| C–Cl | 30–70 | H–C–C=O | 2.0–2.7 |
| C–N | 30–65 | H–C–N | 2.3–3.0 |
| C–O | 50–90 | H–C–Cl | 3.0–4.5 |
| alkene C=C | 115–150 | H–C–O | 3.3–4.5 |
| aromatic C | 110–160 | aromatic H | 6.5–8.2 |
| acid, ester C=O | 160–185 | carboxylic acid O–H | 10.0–12.5 |
| aldehyde, ketone C=O | 190–220 | aldehyde H | 9.4–10.0 |
| C≡N | 110–125 | alcohol O–H, amine N–H | 0.5–5.0 |

IR: alcohol O–H 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ (broad); acid O–H 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ (very broad); N–H 3300–3500 cm⁻¹; C=O 1630–1820 cm⁻¹. Use Ar values of 12.0 for carbon, 1.0 for hydrogen, 14.0 for nitrogen and 16.0 for oxygen.

## Questions

**1.** State the chemical shift of the tetramethylsilane (TMS) signal, and give two reasons why TMS is the NMR standard. **[3]**

**2.** A student dissolves a sample in CHCl₃ for proton NMR. Explain why this is a poor choice and state what to use instead. **[2]**

**3.** State the number of lines in the carbon-13 NMR spectrum of each compound.

**(a)** propane-1,2,3-triol, HOCH₂CH(OH)CH₂OH **[1]**
**(b)** 4-methylphenol, CH₃C₆H₄OH **[1]**
**(c)** 3-methylbut-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH(CH₃)₂ **[1]**
**(d)** hexane-2,5-dione, CH₃COCH₂CH₂COCH₃ **[1]**

**4.** Predict the high resolution proton NMR spectrum of butanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl. Give the number of signals, their relative areas and the splitting of each, with reasons. **[4]**

**5.** Several isomers have the formula C₄H₈.

**(a)** Isomer A gives two carbon-13 lines, at δ 126 and δ 17. Identify A, and explain why it cannot be cyclobutane. **[2]**
**(b)** Isomer B gives three carbon-13 lines, at δ 142, 112 and 24. Identify B, and explain why it cannot be methylcyclopropane. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict the proton NMR spectrum of B. **[2]**

**6.** Compound C, C₅H₁₀O₂, is either methyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃, or propyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃. Its proton spectrum has a 3H singlet at δ 3.7 and a 2H triplet at δ 2.3.

**(a)** Explain which compound C is. **[3]**
**(b)** State the expected shift range of the other compound's 3H singlet. **[1]**

**7.** Compound D, C₅H₁₀O₂, has IR absorptions at 3450 cm⁻¹ (broad) and 1710 cm⁻¹ (strong). Its proton spectrum has three singlets: δ 1.38 (6H), 2.22 (3H) and 3.7 (1H, broad, absent after shaking with D₂O).

**(a)** Deduce the structure of D, explaining the evidence. **[4]**
**(b)** State the number of lines in the carbon-13 spectrum of D. **[1]**

**8.** Predict the NMR spectra of 2-chloropropanoic acid, CH₃CHClCOOH.

**(a)** the carbon-13 spectrum: number of lines and a shift range for each **[2]**
**(b)** the proton spectrum: relative areas, shift ranges and splitting **[4]**

**9.** Compound E contains 65.8% C, 15.1% H and 19.1% N by mass. Mass spectrum: m/z 73 (small), 58 and 44 (tallest). IR: two sharp absorptions near 3300 and 3370 cm⁻¹, none at 1630–1820 cm⁻¹. Carbon-13: δ 49, 33, 23 and 10. Proton: δ 0.90 (3H, triplet), 1.05 (3H, doublet), 1.2 (2H, broad singlet, absent after shaking with D₂O), 1.35 (2H, quintet), 2.80 (1H, sextet).

**(a)** Show that the molecular formula of E is C₄H₁₁N. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the structure of E from the IR and NMR data. **[5]**
**(c)** Suggest the formula of the ion responsible for the peak at m/z 44. **[1]**

**10.** Compound F contains 72.0% C, 6.7% H and 21.3% O by mass. Mass spectrum: m/z 150, 105 (tallest) and 77. IR: strong absorption at 1720 cm⁻¹, no broad absorption at 2500–3600 cm⁻¹. Carbon-13: seven lines, δ 166.6, 61.0, 14.4 and four at δ 128–133. Proton: δ 1.39 (3H, triplet), 4.37 (2H, quartet), 7.4–8.1 (5H).

**(a)** Determine the molecular formula of F. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the structure of F from the IR and NMR data. **[4]**
**(c)** Identify the ion at m/z 105. **[1]**

**11.** The proton spectrum of compound G, C₇H₁₄O₂, is:

| δ / ppm | Splitting | Step height / mm |
|---|---|---|
| 0.92 | doublet | 36 |
| 1.52 | quartet | 12 |
| 1.68 | nine peaks | 6 |
| 2.04 | singlet | 18 |
| 4.09 | triplet | 12 |

**(a)** Give the proton ratio and show it fits the formula. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the groups responsible for the signals at δ 2.04 and δ 4.09. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the nine-peak signal and give the structure of G. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** δ = 0 [1]. Its 12 equivalent H give one sharp signal [1]. The signal is away from sample signals, or it is inert, or volatile and easily removed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "It has 12 hydrogens" is not enough; say they are equivalent and give one signal.

**2.** The H in CHCl₃ gives a large solvent signal that swamps the sample's signals [1]. Use a deuterated solvent such as **CDCl₃**, which has no ¹H [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a deuterated solvent; "use a different solvent" is too vague.

**3. (a)** **2**: the two CH₂OH carbons, and the CH carbon [1].
**(b)** **5**: CH₃, ring C–CH₃, two pairs of ring C–H, ring C–OH [1].
**(c)** **4**: =CH₂, =CH, CH, two equivalent CH₃ [1].
**(d)** **3**: C=O, CH₂, CH₃, each pair equivalent by symmetry [1].
*Examiner insight:* Sketch the molecule and mark its symmetry first; parts (b) and (d) are easy to overcount.

**4.** Three signals, relative areas 3 : 2 : 2 [1]. CH₃: triplet, 2 adjacent H [1]. Middle CH₂: sextet, 3 + 2 = 5 adjacent H [1]. CH₂ next to COCl: triplet, 2 adjacent H [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the number of adjacent H with each pattern to show the n + 1 rule being applied.

**5. (a)** A is **but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃** (either geometric isomer); δ 126 is alkene C [1]. Cyclobutane would give one line and no alkene carbon [1].
**(b)** B is **2-methylpropene, (CH₃)₂C=CH₂** [1]. Methylcyclopropane has no C=C, so it would give no line at δ 115–150 [1].
**(c)** Two signals, ratio 6 : 2 (3 : 1) [1]. Both are singlets, because the only carbon next to each set of protons is the central =C<, which carries no hydrogen [1].
*Examiner insight:* To rule out an isomer, quote the evidence it fails to match.

**6. (a)** The 3H singlet is a CH₃ with no adjacent H [1]. δ 3.7 is in the H–C–O range, so the CH₃ is on oxygen [1]. The δ 2.3 triplet is a CH₂ next to C=O, not on oxygen: C is **methyl butanoate** [1].
**(b)** δ **2.0–2.7** (CH₃ next to C=O) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Both isomers have a 3H singlet, so the shift decides what the CH₃ is bonded to.

**7. (a)** IR: alcohol O–H and C=O; with no aldehyde proton, a ketone [1]. The δ 3.7 signal disappears with D₂O, so it is the O–H [1]. δ 2.22 (3H, singlet) is CH₃ on C=O [1]. δ 1.38 (6H, singlet) comes from a pair of equivalent methyl groups on a carbon that carries no hydrogen, so D is **CH₃COC(OH)(CH₃)₂, 3-hydroxy-3-methylbutan-2-one** [1].
**(b)** **4** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In a "deduce" question, tie every signal to a group; a bare structure shows no reasoning.

**8. (a)** **3** lines [1]: C=O at δ 160–185, C–Cl at δ 30–70, CH₃ at δ 5–55 [1].
**(b)** Ratio 3 : 1 : 1 [1]. CH₃: δ 0.7–1.8, doublet (1 adjacent H) [1]. CH: δ 3.0–4.5, quartet (3 adjacent H) [1]. COOH: δ 10.0–12.5, singlet [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each proton environment needs shift, area and splitting; leaving one out loses credit.

**9. (a)** C: 65.8 ÷ 12.0 = 5.483; H: 15.1 ÷ 1.0 = 15.1; N: 19.1 ÷ 14.0 = 1.364 [1]. Dividing by 1.364 gives 4.02 : 11.07 : 1, so the empirical formula is C₄H₁₁N [1]. Its mass is 73.0, which matches the molecular ion at m/z 73, so the molecular formula is **C₄H₁₁N** [1].
**(b)** IR: N–H and no C=O, so an amine [1]. Four carbon environments, with δ 49 a C–N carbon [1]. The 2H signal removed by D₂O is NH₂: a primary amine [1]. Triplet (3H) + quintet (2H) are CH₃CH₂ next to a CH; the doublet (3H) is CH₃ on that CH, whose sextet shows 3 + 2 = 5 neighbours [1]. E is **CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₂CH₃, butan-2-amine** [1].
**(c)** **CH₃CHNH₂⁺** (C₂H₆N⁺), from loss of the ethyl group [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer needs the division and ratio, not the given formula repeated.

**10. (a)** C: 72.0 ÷ 12.0 = 6.0; H: 6.7 ÷ 1.0 = 6.7; O: 21.3 ÷ 16.0 = 1.331 [1]. Ratio 4.51 : 5.03 : 1, doubled gives C₉H₁₀O₂ [1]. Mass 150.0 matches m/z 150, so the molecular formula is **C₉H₁₀O₂** [1].
**(b)** C=O with no O–H, and a line at δ 166.6: an ester [1]. Four aromatic lines and 5H at δ 7.4–8.1: a C₆H₅ group [1]. The 3H triplet and 2H quartet at δ 4.37: OCH₂CH₃ [1]. F is **C₆H₅COOCH₂CH₃, ethyl benzoate** [1].
**(c)** **C₆H₅CO⁺** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Do not round a ratio such as 4.51 to 5; multiply through by 2 when a ratio ends in about .5.

**11. (a)** Dividing by 6 mm gives 6 : 2 : 1 : 3 : 2, total 14, matching the 14 H in C₇H₁₄O₂ [1].
**(b)** δ 2.04 (3H, singlet): CH₃ next to C=O with no adjacent H [1]. δ 4.09 (2H, triplet): CH₂ on oxygen next to a CH₂ [1].
**(c)** Nine peaks means 8 adjacent H: a CH between two CH₃ (6H) and a CH₂ (2H) [1]. G is **CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂, 3-methylbutyl ethanoate** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the trace's step heights, not peak heights, and simplify the ratio before assigning groups.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting adjacent protons on one side only, so a sextet is called a triplet.
- Splitting a signal by an O–H or N–H proton, or by protons in the same equivalent group.
- Assigning a C=O line at δ 160–185 to a ketone.
- Saying D₂O makes a signal shift or split, rather than disappear.
- Rounding an empirical ratio of 4.5 up or down instead of doubling.
- Not checking that integration totals match the formula.
