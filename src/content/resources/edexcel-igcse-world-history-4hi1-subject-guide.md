Pearson Edexcel International GCSE History (4HI1) is a modern world history course built
around choice. Rather than every student covering the same narrative, schools select a
small number of options from a long menu of depth studies, historical investigations and
breadth studies, which means two students taking 4HI1 in different schools may study
quite different periods. What they share is the assessment: two externally assessed
written papers, each 90 minutes long, each worth 60 marks and each carrying 50% of the
qualification. This overview explains how the options are arranged, how the papers use
them, and how to plan a route through the course.

## How the course is organised

The content is divided between the two papers, and each paper has its own pool of
options.

**Paper 1: Depth Studies.** There are eight depth studies, and candidates study at least
two of them:

- The French Revolution, c1780–99
- Development of a Nation: Unification of Italy, 1848–70
- Germany: Development of Dictatorship, 1918–45
- Colonial Rule and the Nationalist Challenge in India, 1919–47
- Dictatorship and Conflict in the USSR, 1924–53
- A World Divided: Superpower Relations, 1943–72
- A Divided Union: Civil Rights in the USA, 1945–74
- South Africa: From Union to the End of Apartheid, 1948–94

A depth study looks closely at a relatively short period, asking how and why events
unfolded as they did and what the consequences were for the people involved.

**Paper 2: Investigation and Breadth Studies.** This paper draws on two separate lists.
The first contains five historical investigations: the origins and course of the First
World War, 1905–18 (A1); Russia and the Soviet Union, 1905–24 (A2); the USA, 1918–41
(A3); the Vietnam conflict, 1945–75 (A4); and East Germany, 1958–90 (A5). The second
contains eight breadth studies in change: America from new nation to divided union,
1783–1877 (B1); changes in medicine, c1848–c1948 (B2); Japan in transformation,
1853–1945 (B3); China, 1900–89 (B4); the League and the UN, 1919–c2011 (B5); the
changing nature of warfare and international conflict, 1919–2011 (B6); the Middle East,
1917–2012 (B7); and diversity, rights and equality in Britain, 1914–2010 (B8).

A breadth study is the opposite of a depth study in scale: it follows one theme across a
long span of time, so the focus shifts from detail to patterns of change and continuity.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4HI1/01 | Paper 1: Depth Studies | 90 minutes | 60 | 50% |
| 4HI1/02 | Paper 2: Investigation and Breadth Studies | 90 minutes | 60 | 50% |

In Paper 1 candidates answer two questions, one on each of two depth studies chosen from
the eight. In Paper 2 they answer on one historical investigation chosen from the five
and one breadth study chosen from the eight. The qualification is not tiered, so every
candidate sits the same papers, and it is linear, with both papers taken at the end of
the course. Pearson offers both papers as paper-based or onscreen assessment, so check
with your school which format you will sit. The current specification was first taught
in September 2017 and first examined in June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The structure asks you to think about history at three different scales. The depth
studies train close knowledge of a short period: names, dates, decisions and their
immediate effects. The investigation adds a second detailed topic, examined alongside the
breadth study on Paper 2. The breadth study asks you to step back and explain change
over decades or even centuries.

Some options sit naturally together. Germany, 1918–45, and the USSR, 1924–53, are both
studies of dictatorship, so the questions you ask of one carry over to the other. Superpower relations,
1943–72, connects with the Vietnam conflict investigation and with East Germany,
1958–90. The League and the UN breadth study overlaps in period with several depth
studies, which can help when you need context for a question. Your school's choice of
options will often reflect links like these, so it is worth asking why your particular
combination was chosen.

Whatever the options, the same historical skills run through both papers: explaining
causes and consequences, judging significance, analysing change and continuity, and
supporting every point with precise, relevant knowledge.

## Planning your study

Because the papers are equal in weight and length, give them roughly equal time across
the course. A common pattern is to teach the two depth studies first, since they build
the habit of detailed, well-evidenced explanation, then move to the investigation, and
finish with the breadth study, which benefits from the confidence to handle a long
chronology.

Within each option, start with a clear timeline before working on individual
themes. For a breadth study in particular, a single page showing the key turning points
across the whole period is a useful anchor for everything else you learn.

Keep notes organised by option rather than by lesson, so that by the final term you have
one complete set per depth study, investigation and breadth study. Begin timed answers
on finished topics early, rather than leaving all exam practice to the last few weeks,
and use the exam preparation page for paper-by-paper technique once the content is in
place.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel IGCSE History 4HI1?**
There are two written papers. Paper 1 (Depth Studies) and Paper 2 (Investigation and Breadth Studies) each last 90 minutes, carry 60 marks and are worth 50% of the qualification.

**How many depth studies do you study for 4HI1 Paper 1?**
Candidates study at least two of the eight depth studies, and in the exam they answer two questions on two depth studies.

**What do you answer on 4HI1 Paper 2?**
Paper 2 covers one historical investigation chosen from five options and one breadth study in change chosen from eight options.

**Is Edexcel IGCSE History tiered?**
No. 4HI1 is not tiered, so all candidates sit the same two papers. Both are available as paper-based or onscreen assessment.

**When was the current 4HI1 specification first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2017 and first examined in June 2019.
