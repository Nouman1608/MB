This set practises **3.3.6.2 Mass spectrometry** from section 3.3.6 Organic analysis of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [mass spectrometry study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-2/) or the [mass spectrometry revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-2-revision-notes/).

Use these precise relative masses: ¹H = 1.0078, ¹²C = 12.0000, ¹⁴N = 14.0031, ¹⁶O = 15.9949. For percentage composition use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** These formulae all have a whole-number Mr of 72. Which has the **highest** precise molecular mass?

A: C₄H₈O

B: C₅H₁₂

C: C₃H₄O₂

D: C₃H₈N₂ **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the precise molecular mass of propanone, C₃H₆O. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why two compounds with the same whole-number Mr can be distinguished by high-resolution mass spectrometry. **[2]**

**4.** A gas gives a molecular ion at m/z = 28.0312. Is it CO, N₂ or C₂H₄? **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by the molecular ion, M⁺. **[1]**

**6.** A hydrocarbon gives a molecular ion at m/z = 58.0780. Show that its formula is C₄H₁₀ rather than C₃H₆O. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Compound K gives a molecular ion at m/z = 86.0729. Its formula is one of C₅H₁₀O, C₄H₆O₂, C₆H₁₄ and C₄H₁₀N₂.

**(a)** Use precise masses to identify the molecular formula of K. Show your working. **[3]**

**(b)** K gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent and has an unbranched chain. Suggest the name of K. **[1]**

**(c)** An isomer of K gives no silver mirror and leaves bromine water orange. Suggest a name for this isomer. **[1]**

**8.** Compound L contains 66.7 % carbon, 11.1 % hydrogen and 22.2 % oxygen by mass. Its molecular ion appears at m/z = 72.0573.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of L. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of L and confirm it with a precise mass calculation. **[2]**

**9.** A compound containing nitrogen gives a molecular ion at m/z = 75.0319. Show whether its formula is C₂H₅NO₂ or C₃H₉NO. **[2]**

**10.** A student calculates the precise masses of C₂H₄O₂ and C₃H₈O and rounds each total to one decimal place. Explain why this is a problem and what the student should do. **[2]**

**11.** Compound M contains only C, H and O. Its molecular ion is at m/z = 100.0885. M turns acidified potassium dichromate(VI) green and decolourises bromine water. It does not react with Fehling's solution.

**(a)** Show that M is C₆H₁₂O rather than C₅H₈O₂ or C₇H₁₆. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a structure for M that fits all the evidence. **[2]**

**12.** Carbon monoxide and nitrogen both have Mr = 28.

**(a)** Calculate the difference between their precise molecular masses. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why a low-resolution instrument that measures m/z to the nearest whole number cannot distinguish them. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** (C₅H₁₂ = 72.0936) [1]

**2.** 3(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 15.9949 = **58.0417** [1]

**3.** Relative isotopic masses (except ¹²C) are not exact whole numbers [1]; so different formulae with the same whole-number Mr have different precise masses, which a high-resolution instrument measures to four decimal places [1]

**4.** **C₂H₄** [1]

**5.** The ion formed when a molecule loses one electron (M⁺); its m/z gives the molecular mass [1]

**6.** C₄H₁₀: 4(12.0000) + 10(1.0078) = 58.0780 [1]; C₃H₆O: 3(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 58.0417, which does not match, so the formula is C₄H₁₀ [1]

**7. (a)** C₅H₁₀O: 5(12.0000) + 10(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 86.0729 [1]; C₄H₆O₂ = 86.0366; C₆H₁₄ = 86.1092; C₄H₁₀N₂ = 86.0842 (all other totals shown) [1]; only C₅H₁₀O matches, so K is **C₅H₁₀O** [1]

**(b)** **Pentanal** [1]

**(c)** **Pentan-2-one** (or pentan-3-one, or 3-methylbutan-2-one) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Any ketone with formula C₅H₁₀O fits: no silver mirror rules out the aldehyde and the orange bromine water rules out C=C.

**8. (a)** C: 66.7 ÷ 12.0 = 5.56; H: 11.1 ÷ 1.0 = 11.1; O: 22.2 ÷ 16.0 = 1.39 [1]; divide by 1.39: C 4.01, H 8.00, O 1.00, so **C₄H₈O** [1]

**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 72, and the whole-number Mr is 72, so the molecular formula is **C₄H₈O** [1]; 4(12.0000) + 8(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 72.0573, which matches [1]

**9.** C₂H₅NO₂: 2(12.0000) + 5(1.0078) + 14.0031 + 2(15.9949) = 75.0319 [1]; C₃H₉NO = 75.0682, so the formula is **C₂H₅NO₂** [1]

**10.** C₂H₄O₂ = 60.0210 and C₃H₈O = 60.0573 both round to 60.0, so they appear identical and cannot be distinguished [1]; the student should keep four decimal places throughout [1]

**11. (a)** C₆H₁₂O: 6(12.0000) + 12(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 100.0885 [1]; C₅H₈O₂ = 100.0522 and C₇H₁₆ = 100.1248, which do not match [1]

**(b)** M has C=C (decolourises bromine water) and a primary or secondary alcohol group (dichromate turns green; not an aldehyde, since Fehling's is negative) [1]; for example hex-5-en-1-ol, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂OH, or any C₆H₁₂O alcohol with one C=C and the –OH on a carbon bearing at least one H [1]

**12. (a)** 28.0062 − 27.9949 = **0.0113** [1]

**(b)** Both give m/z = 28 to the nearest whole number, so their molecular ion peaks appear at the same position [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using rounded Ar values instead of precise masses.
- Rounding totals before comparing them.
- Not showing totals for the rejected candidates.
- Treating the molecular formula as if it gave the structure.
- Ignoring other evidence (test tube results) when asked to suggest a structure.
