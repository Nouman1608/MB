These questions cover **3.1.2.4 Empirical and molecular formula** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, drawn on by Units 2 and 5). Ar values: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Mg 24.3, S 32.1, Fe 55.8. Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ where needed. For the method, see the [empirical and molecular formula study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-4/) or the [empirical and molecular formula revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-4-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the term empirical formula. **[1]**

**2.** Give the empirical formula of each compound.

**(a)** C₆H₁₂ **[1]**

**(b)** N₂O₄ **[1]**

**(c)** C₂H₄O₂ **[1]**

**3.** Which pair of compounds have the same empirical formula?

A: C₂H₂ and C₆H₆

B: C₂H₄ and C₂H₆

C: CO and CO₂

D: H₂O and H₂O₂

**[1]**

**4.** A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass. Its Mr is 56.0. Find its empirical formula and its molecular formula. **[3]**

**5.** After dividing by the smallest value, a student obtains the ratio Fe : O = 1.00 : 1.33. Give the empirical formula of the oxide. **[1]**

**6.** A student heats 1.20 g of magnesium ribbon in a crucible until it has all reacted. The oxide formed has a mass of 2.00 g. Use these results to find the empirical formula of magnesium oxide. **[3]**

**7.** A compound has the empirical formula CH₂O. Explain why its Mr must be a multiple of 30.0. **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A white solid contains 32.4% sodium, 22.6% sulfur and 45.0% oxygen by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of the solid. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why this formula is described as an empirical formula, even though it gives the correct ratio of ions. **[1]**

**9.** Hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, contains 51.2% water by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the value of x (Mr of MgSO₄ = 120.4). **[3]**

**(b)** Give one reason why an experiment to find x by heating the hydrate might give a value that is too low. **[1]**

**10.** 0.440 g of an organic compound containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen burns completely to give 0.880 g of CO₂ and 0.360 g of H₂O. Its Mr is 88.0.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of carbon and the mass of hydrogen in the sample. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of oxygen in the sample. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(d)** Deduce the molecular formula. **[1]**

**11.** A liquid hydrocarbon contains 92.3% carbon and 7.7% hydrogen by mass. 0.390 g of the liquid is vaporised; the vapour occupies 150 cm³ at 373 K and 103 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the hydrocarbon from the gas data. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the molecular formula. **[1]**

**12.** A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 13.3% hydrogen and 46.7% nitrogen by mass. Its Mr is 60.0.

**(a)** Calculate its empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce its molecular formula. **[1]**

**(c)** A student rounds the mole values to one significant figure before dividing by the smallest. Explain why this is poor practice. **[1]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** The simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound [1] **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** CH₂ [1]

**(b)** NO₂ [1]

**(c)** CH₂O [1] **[3]**

**3.** **A** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Both reduce to CH. In D, H₂O₂ reduces to HO, not H₂O.

**4.** C 85.7 ÷ 12.0 = 7.14; H 14.3 ÷ 1.0 = 14.3; ratio 1 : 2 [1]; empirical formula **CH₂**, mass 14.0 [1]; 56.0 ÷ 14.0 = 4, so **C₄H₈** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Multiply by 3: **Fe₃O₄** [1] **[1]**

**6.** Mass of oxygen = 2.00 − 1.20 = 0.80 g [1]; n(Mg) = 1.20 ÷ 24.3 = 0.0494 mol and n(O) = 0.80 ÷ 16.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; ratio 1 : 1, so **MgO** [1] **[3]**

**7.** The molecular formula is a whole-number multiple of CH₂O, whose formula mass is 30.0 [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** Na 32.4 ÷ 23.0 = 1.41; S 22.6 ÷ 32.1 = 0.704; O 45.0 ÷ 16.0 = 2.81 [1]; divide by 0.704: 2.00 : 1.00 : 3.99 [1]; **Na₂SO₄** [1]

**(b)** It is an ionic compound with a giant lattice and no molecules, so it can only have an empirical formula [1] **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** MgSO₄ = 48.8%; 48.8 ÷ 120.4 = 0.405 [1]; H₂O 51.2 ÷ 18.0 = 2.84 [1]; ratio 2.84 ÷ 0.405 = 7.0, so **x = 7** [1]

**(b)** The sample was not heated to constant mass, so some water of crystallisation remained [1] **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** Mass of C = (0.880 ÷ 44.0) × 12.0 = 0.240 g [1]; mass of H = 2 × (0.360 ÷ 18.0) × 1.0 = 0.0400 g [1]

**(b)** Mass of O = 0.440 − 0.240 − 0.0400 = 0.160 g [1]

**(c)** n: C 0.0200, H 0.0400, O 0.0100 mol [1]; ratio 2 : 4 : 1, so **C₂H₄O** [1]

**(d)** Empirical mass = 44.0; 88.0 ÷ 44.0 = 2, so **C₄H₈O₂** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Without (b), the compound would wrongly appear to be a hydrocarbon; always test whether the masses of C and H add up to the sample mass.

**11.**

**(a)** C 92.3 ÷ 12.0 = 7.69; H 7.7 ÷ 1.0 = 7.7 [1]; ratio 1 : 1, so **CH** [1]

**(b)** n = (103 000 × 1.50 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 373) = 4.98 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; Mr = 0.390 ÷ 4.98 × 10⁻³ = **78.2** [1]

**(c)** Empirical mass 13.0; 78.2 ÷ 13.0 = 6.0, so **C₆H₆** [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** C 40.0 ÷ 12.0 = 3.33; H 13.3 ÷ 1.0 = 13.3; N 46.7 ÷ 14.0 = 3.34 [1]; ratio 1 : 4 : 1, so **CH₄N** [1]

**(b)** Empirical mass 30.0; 60.0 ÷ 30.0 = 2, so **C₂H₈N₂** [1]

**(c)** Rounding early introduces large errors in the ratio, which can give the wrong whole numbers [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding ratios such as 1.33 or 1.5 instead of multiplying through.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 for hydrogen in water.
- Missing oxygen found by difference.
- Stopping at the empirical formula when Mr is given.
