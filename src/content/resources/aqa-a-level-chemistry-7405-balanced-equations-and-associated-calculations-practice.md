These questions cover section **3.1.2.5 Balanced equations and associated calculations** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Balanced equations study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-balanced-equations-and-associated-calculations/) and the [Balanced equations revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-balanced-equations-and-associated-calculations-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, Mg = 24.3, Al = 27.0, Cl = 35.5, Ca = 40.1, Ti = 47.9. R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Balance each equation.

**(a)** Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂ **[1]**

**(b)** C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O **[1]**

**2.** Write an ionic equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of solid calcium carbonate with hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**3.** Titanium is made by the reaction TiCl₄ + 2Mg → Ti + 2MgCl₂.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of titanium that can be made from 1.00 kg of titanium(IV) chloride. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for making titanium by this reaction. **[2]**

**4.** 10.0 cm³ of ethene gas is burned in 50.0 cm³ of oxygen: C₂H₄(g) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l). All volumes are measured at the same temperature and pressure. Calculate the total volume of gas present after the mixture is cooled to the starting temperature. **[3]**

**5.** 0.540 g of aluminium reacts with excess hydrochloric acid: 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂. Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed, in cm³, at 293 K and 101 kPa. **[4]**

**6.** Aspirin, C₉H₈O₄, is made from salicylic acid, C₇H₆O₃ (Mᵣ = 138.0), and ethanoic anhydride, C₄H₆O₃ (Mᵣ = 102.0): C₇H₆O₃ + C₄H₆O₃ → C₉H₈O₄ + C₂H₄O₂.

**(a)** 2.00 g of salicylic acid gives 2.15 g of aspirin (Mᵣ = 180.0). Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for making aspirin by this reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** State one reason why the percentage yield was less than 100%. **[1]**

**7.** Hydrogen can be made by steam reforming of methane: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for making hydrogen by this reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest two reasons why a company would prefer a process with a high atom economy. **[2]**

**8.** A student titrates 25.0 cm³ portions of potassium hydroxide solution with 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid. The titres are 20.40 (rough), 19.85, 19.75 and 19.80 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the mean titre the student should use. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide, in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**(c)** Each burette reading has an uncertainty of ±0.05 cm³. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mean titre. **[2]**

**9.** 2.86 g of hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, is heated until its mass is constant at 1.06 g. Calculate the value of x. **[3]**

**10.** An acidified solution of manganate(VII) ions oxidises iron(II) ions: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺. Show that this ionic equation is balanced for both atoms and charge. **[2]**

**11.** A lime kiln heats calcium carbonate: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. A company needs 10.0 tonnes of calcium oxide (Mᵣ = 56.1) and the process has a yield of 85.0%. Calculate the mass of calcium carbonate (Mᵣ = 100.1) required, in tonnes. **[3]**

**12.** A reaction has a yield of 96% but an atom economy of 28%. Explain how both statements can be true, and say which is more useful to a company judging the environmental effect of the process. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂** [1]

**(b)** **2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) [1] → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) with the equation balanced for atoms and charge, and the state symbols shown [1]. Chloride ions are spectators and are left out. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Mᵣ(TiCl₄) = 47.9 + 4 × 35.5 = 189.9; n = 1000 ÷ 189.9 = 5.266 mol [1]; n(Ti) = 5.266 mol (ratio 1 : 1) [1]; m = 5.266 × 47.9 = **252 g** [1]

**(b)** Atom economy = 47.9 ÷ (189.9 + 2 × 24.3) × 100 [1] = 47.9 ÷ 238.5 × 100 = **20.1%** [1]. **[5]**

**4.** The equation shows that 10.0 cm³ of ethene uses 30.0 cm³ of oxygen and forms 20.0 cm³ of carbon dioxide [1]. Oxygen left over = 50.0 − 30.0 = 20.0 cm³ [1]. Water is a liquid after cooling, so total gas = 20.0 + 20.0 = **40.0 cm³** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** n(Al) = 0.540 ÷ 27.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; n(H₂) = 0.0200 × 3 ÷ 2 = 0.0300 mol [1]; V = nRT ÷ p = 0.0300 × 8.31 × 293 ÷ 1.01 × 10⁵ [1] = 7.23 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = **723 cm³** [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** n(salicylic acid) = 2.00 ÷ 138.0 = 0.01449 mol [1]; theoretical mass of aspirin = 0.01449 × 180.0 = 2.61 g [1]; yield = 2.15 ÷ 2.61 × 100 = **82.4%** [1]

**(b)** 180.0 ÷ (138.0 + 102.0) × 100 [1] = **75.0%** [1]

**(c)** Any one of: the reaction did not go to completion, a side reaction occurred, or product was lost on the filter paper or in transfer or recrystallisation [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** Mᵣ of reactants = 16.0 + 18.0 = 34.0; Mᵣ of desired product = 3 × 2.0 = 6.0 [1]; atom economy = 6.0 ÷ 34.0 × 100 = **17.6%** [1]

**(b)** Any two of: less waste to dispose of or treat, lower cost of raw materials per tonne of product, less cost to separate by-products, fewer resources consumed or less pollution [1 each]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** The rough titre is excluded; the concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) are 19.85, 19.75 and 19.80; mean = **19.80 cm³** [1]

**(b)** n(H₂SO₄) = 0.0500 × 19.80 ÷ 1000 = 9.90 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(KOH) = 2 × 9.90 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.98 × 10⁻³ mol (H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O) [1]; c = 1.98 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0792 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** Titre uncertainty = 2 × 0.05 = ±0.10 cm³ [1]; percentage = 0.10 ÷ 19.80 × 100 = **0.51%** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A rough titre is a trial run and is never averaged. The 1 : 2 ratio is the usual lost mark.

**9.** n(Na₂CO₃) = 1.06 ÷ 106.0 = 0.0100 mol [1]; m(H₂O) = 2.86 − 1.06 = 1.80 g, so n = 1.80 ÷ 18.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; ratio = 0.100 ÷ 0.0100, so **x = 10** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Atoms: Mn 1 and 1, O 4 and 4, H 8 and 8, Fe 5 and 5 [1]. Charge: left = (−1) + 8 + 5 × 2 = +17; right = +2 + 5 × 3 = +17, so charge is balanced [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Theoretical mass of CaO needed = 10.0 ÷ 0.850 = 11.76 tonnes [1]; mass of CaCO₃ = 11.76 × 100.1 ÷ 56.1 [1] = **21.0 tonnes** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The yield is used to scale up the amount needed, so you divide by it. Multiplying by 0.850 gives a smaller, wrong answer.

**12.** Yield measures how much of the possible product is obtained, so 96% means the reaction goes almost to completion [1]. Atom economy is calculated from the equation, so a low value means most of the mass of the reactants ends up in by-products, which is waste unless used [1]. Atom economy is the more useful measure of how much waste is built into the process, because even a perfect yield cannot reduce it [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Changing a subscript to balance an equation.
- Using the wrong coefficient in the mole ratio, especially 1 : 2 acid-base reactions.
- Not dividing by 1000 for cm³ in n = cV.
- Multiplying by, instead of dividing by, the percentage yield when scaling up.
- Counting a liquid product as part of a gas volume.
- Averaging a rough titre.
