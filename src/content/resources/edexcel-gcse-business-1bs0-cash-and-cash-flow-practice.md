## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a cash outflow? **[1]**

A: A bank loan received

B: Cash sales

C: Payment of wages

D: Money invested by the owner

**2.** Which **one** of the following is the correct formula for the closing balance? **[1]**

A: Opening balance − net cash flow

B: Opening balance + net cash flow

C: Cash inflows + cash outflows

D: Net cash flow − cash outflows

**3.** Define the term insolvency. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** things the specification says a business needs cash to pay. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** difference between cash and profit. **[3]**

**6.** *Wrenbury Mobile Café* has the following cash-flow forecast. Complete the table. **[4]**

| | Apr | May | Jun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash inflows | 4,200 | 4,800 | 3,500 |
| Cash outflows | 4,500 | 3,900 | 4,400 |
| Net cash flow | −300 | ? | ? |
| Opening balance | 600 | ? | 1,200 |
| Closing balance | ? | 1,200 | ? |

**7.** In one month a business's opening balance is £2,500, its closing balance is £1,700 and its cash inflows are £6,300. Calculate its cash outflows for the month. **[2]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**8.** *Kestle Farm Shop* supplies vegetables to restaurants. The restaurants pay one month after delivery. Sales made in December were £5,000. Each month, purchases from farmers cost 50% of that month's sales and are paid in the same month; other outflows are £2,600 a month. The opening balance in January is £1,000.

| | Jan | Feb | Mar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales made (for reference) | 6,000 | 9,000 | 12,000 |
| Receipts from restaurants | 5,000 | ? | 9,000 |
| Purchases from farmers | 3,000 | 4,500 | 6,000 |
| Other outflows | 2,600 | 2,600 | 2,600 |
| Total outflows | 5,600 | 7,100 | 8,600 |
| Net cash flow | −600 | −1,100 | ? |
| Opening balance | 1,000 | 400 | −700 |
| Closing balance | 400 | −700 | ? |

**(a)** State the receipts from restaurants in February. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the net cash flow and the closing balance for March. **[2]**

**(c)** In February, Kestle Farm Shop makes a profit of £1,900. Explain why its net cash flow for February is negative. **[3]**

**(d)** The owner is considering two ways to deal with the forecast negative balances.

Option 1: arrange a bank overdraft with a limit of £1,000.

Option 2: offer restaurants a 5% discount if they pay in the same month as delivery.

Justify which option Kestle Farm Shop should choose. **[9]**

**9.** Analyse why a cash-flow forecast is important for a business that is starting up. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why a bank loan received is a cash inflow but does not increase profit. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C: Payment of wages [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B: Opening balance + net cash flow [1]. **[1]**

**3.** When a business cannot pay its debts when they fall due [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: suppliers [1]; overheads [1]; employees. **[2]**

**5.** Profit is revenue minus costs over a period, and a sale counts as revenue when it is made [1]. Cash is only counted when money is actually received or paid out [1], so if a customer buys on credit the business can make a profit on the sale but have no extra cash until the customer pays [1]. **[3]**

**6.** April closing balance = 600 + (−300) = **300**, which is also May's opening balance [1]. May net cash flow = 4,800 − 3,900 = **900** (check: 300 + 900 = 1,200, May's closing balance) [1]. June net cash flow = 3,500 − 4,400 = **−900** [1]. June closing balance = 1,200 + (−900) = **300** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* April's closing balance (300) must equal May's opening balance; check this link before moving on.

**7.** Net cash flow = £1,700 − £2,500 = −£800 [1]. Outflows = £6,300 − (−£800) = **£7,100** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **£6,000** (January's sales, paid one month later) [1].

**(b)** Net cash flow = 9,000 − 8,600 = **400** [1]. Closing balance = −700 + 400 = **−300** [1].

**(c)** The restaurants pay one month after delivery, so February's receipts are only January's sales of £6,000 [1]. Meanwhile the shop pays for February's larger purchases of £4,500 straight away, plus £2,600 of other outflows [1]. Because sales are growing, cash going out on this month's purchases is greater than cash coming in from last month's smaller sales, so cash falls by £1,100 even though the shop is profitable [1].

**(d)** Option 1: an overdraft covers the worst forecast balance of −£700 within a £1,000 limit [1]. It is flexible, as the shop only borrows when its balance is negative and repays as restaurant payments arrive [1]. Interest is charged on the amount overdrawn, adding a cost, and the bank could reduce the limit [1].

Option 2: if restaurants paid in the month of delivery, February's receipts would be closer to £9,000, removing the negative balance [1]. However, the discount costs 5% of sales: on February's £9,000 of sales that would be £450 if every restaurant took it [1], which is a permanent reduction in revenue, not just for the problem months [1]. Restaurants may also refuse, because they are used to a month's credit [1].

Judgement: Kestle Farm Shop should choose Option 1. The cash problem is temporary and caused by growth, and by March net cash flow is already positive, so a short-term overdraft is cheaper than giving up 5% of all future sales [1]. If the shop expected to keep growing quickly for many months, faster payment from customers might be worth more [1]. **[15]**

**9.** A start-up usually spends heavily before it has regular customers, for example on equipment and stock [1]. A forecast shows the months when outflows are expected to exceed inflows and the balance could become negative [1], so the owner can arrange finance, such as an overdraft, before the problem happens [1]. This helps the business pay its suppliers, overheads and employees on time [1], avoiding insolvency, which is a major cause of new businesses closing [1]. A forecast can also be shown to a bank as evidence that the owner has planned, making a loan more likely to be offered, although its figures are only estimates [1]. **[6]**

**10.** A loan brings cash into the business's bank account, so it is recorded as an inflow on a cash-flow forecast [1]. However, it is not money earned from selling goods or services, so it is not revenue [1]. It must be repaid with interest, so it adds to the business's costs rather than to its profit [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using sales made instead of receipts when customers pay later.
- Carrying forward the opening balance instead of the closing balance.
- Adding a negative net cash flow as if it were positive.
- Saying a negative balance means the business is making a loss.
- Recommending a solution without matching it to how long the cash gap lasts.
