Condensed recall for **3.2.1 Biopsychology** (OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Psychology 9685, Unit 2, Section A). For explanation and evaluation, read the [biopsychology study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-psychology-9685-biopsychology/); then try the [biopsychology practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-psychology-9685-biopsychology-practice/).

## What the specification lists

- Divisions of the nervous system: central and peripheral (somatic and autonomic).
- Structure and function of sensory, relay and motor neurons; synaptic transmission, including neurotransmitters, excitation and inhibition.
- The endocrine system: glands and hormones; the fight or flight response, including adrenaline.
- Localisation of function and hemispheric lateralisation: motor, somatosensory, visual, auditory and language centres; Broca's and Wernicke's areas; split-brain research; plasticity and functional recovery after trauma.

## Nervous system in five lines

- **CNS:** brain (processing, control) and spinal cord (relay, reflexes).
- **PNS:** all nerves outside the CNS.
- **Somatic:** voluntary movement and sensory input from skin, muscles, joints.
- **Autonomic:** involuntary control of organs and glands.
- **Sympathetic** (arousal) and **parasympathetic** (rest and digest) branches act antagonistically.

## Neurons

| Neuron | Carries impulses | Dendrites | Axon | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Receptors to CNS | Long | Short | PNS, cell body to one side |
| Relay | Within CNS, sensory to motor | Short | Short | CNS |
| Motor | CNS to muscles and glands | Short | Long | Cell body in CNS, axon out to the body |

Parts: dendrites, cell body (nucleus), axon, myelin sheath, nodes of Ranvier, axon terminals.

## Synaptic transmission: six steps

1. Action potential reaches the presynaptic terminal.
2. Vesicles release neurotransmitter into the synaptic cleft.
3. Neurotransmitter diffuses across the cleft.
4. It binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
5. The postsynaptic neuron becomes more likely (excitation) or less likely (inhibition) to fire.
6. Neurotransmitter is removed by reuptake or enzymes.

**Summation:** the net effect of all excitatory and inhibitory inputs decides whether the threshold is reached. **One-way:** vesicles only in the presynaptic terminal, receptors only on the postsynaptic membrane.

## Endocrine system

| Gland | Hormone | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothalamus | Releasing hormones | Links nervous and endocrine systems; controls pituitary |
| Pituitary | ACTH and others | "Master gland" |
| Adrenal medulla | Adrenaline | Fight or flight |
| Adrenal cortex | Cortisol | Longer-term stress response |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine | Metabolism |
| Pineal | Melatonin | Sleep and wake cycles |
| Testes / ovaries | Testosterone / oestrogen | Sexual development |

## Fight or flight sequence

Amygdala detects threat -> hypothalamus -> sympathetic nervous system -> adrenal medulla -> adrenaline -> increased heart rate and blood pressure, faster breathing, dilated pupils, glucose released, blood to muscles, digestion inhibited -> parasympathetic restores rest.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Neurotransmitter | Chemical messenger released at a synapse |
| Excitatory | Makes the postsynaptic neuron more likely to fire |
| Inhibitory | Makes the postsynaptic neuron less likely to fire |
| Hormone | Chemical messenger carried in the blood to target cells |
| Localisation of function | Specific brain areas have specific functions |
| Lateralisation | Some functions are processed mainly in one hemisphere |
| Corpus callosum | Band of nerve fibres connecting the two hemispheres |
| Equipotentiality | Lashley's idea that intact areas of cortex can take over functions |
| Plasticity | The brain's ability to change structure and function with experience |
| Functional recovery | Regaining function after damage as the brain reorganises |
| Axonal sprouting | Growth of new nerve endings to form new connections |

## Studies at a glance

| Study | Finding | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Broca (1861) | Patient with little speech had left frontal damage | Localisation of speech production |
| Wernicke (1874) | Fluent but meaningless speech with left temporal damage | Localisation of comprehension |
| Lashley (1950) | Rats' maze learning not tied to one cortical area | Challenge to strict localisation |
| Sperry (split brain) | Right visual field items named; left visual field items picked out with left hand | Lateralisation |
| Maguire et al. (2000) | Larger posterior hippocampus in taxi drivers, linked to years driving | Plasticity |
| Gray (1988) | Freeze often comes first | Evaluating fight or flight |
| Taylor et al. (2000) | Tend and befriend in females | Evaluating fight or flight |

## Exam traps

- Putting the autonomic system in the CNS.
- Writing that sensory neurons have long axons.
- Forgetting that motor and somatosensory control is contralateral.
- Saying the right visual field goes to the right hemisphere.
- Describing synaptic transmission with no mention of receptors or the cleft.
- Confusing the adrenal medulla (adrenaline) with the adrenal cortex (cortisol).

## Self-test

1. Name the two divisions of the peripheral nervous system.
2. What does the parasympathetic branch do?
3. What is the function of a relay neuron?
4. Why is synaptic transmission one-way?
5. What is summation?
6. Which gland releases adrenaline?
7. Give three effects of adrenaline.
8. Where is Broca's area, and what does it do?
9. In split-brain patients, why could an object shown to the left visual field not be named?
10. What did Lashley (1950) find?
11. Name two mechanisms of functional recovery.
12. Give one limitation of Maguire et al. (2000) as evidence for plasticity.

**Answers:** 1. Somatic and autonomic. 2. Calms the body and restores the resting state ("rest and digest"). 3. Connects sensory and motor neurons within the CNS. 4. Vesicles are only in the presynaptic terminal and receptors only on the postsynaptic membrane. 5. Adding together excitatory and inhibitory inputs to decide whether the neuron fires. 6. The adrenal medulla. 7. For example increased heart rate, dilated pupils, glucose released. 8. Usually the left frontal lobe; speech production. 9. It was processed by the right hemisphere, and language is usually in the left, which could not receive the information. 10. Maze learning was not lost by removing any single area of cortex. 11. For example axonal sprouting and recruitment of homologous areas. 12. It is correlational: people with larger hippocampi may have been more likely to become taxi drivers.
