## Section A

**1.** Which size is within the range studied in nanoscience?

A: 0.1 nm

B: 50 nm

C: 500 nm

D: 5 µm

**[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by a **nanoparticle**. **[2]**

**3.** Convert 7 × 10⁻⁸ m into nanometres. **[1]**

**4.** State the property of nanoparticles, compared with bulk materials, that the specification links to their new uses. **[1]**

**5.** Name **two** possible applications of nanoparticles. **[2]**

**6.** A particle is 0.03 µm across. Show whether it is a nanoparticle. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A cube-shaped nanoparticle has sides of length 20 nm.

**(a)** Calculate its surface area. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its volume. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. **[1]**

**(d)** A second cube has sides of 2 nm. Without a full calculation, state its surface area to volume ratio and explain how you found it. **[2]**

**8.** Information: a catalyst used to break down a pollutant gas is made of metal oxide Q. In test 1, 1.0 g of Q as a powder with particles 10 µm across breaks down 5% of the gas in one minute. In test 2, 1.0 g of Q as nanoparticles 10 nm across breaks down 90% of the gas in one minute.

**(a)** Calculate how many times smaller the nanoparticles are than the powder particles. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the difference between the two tests. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest one advantage, for a manufacturer, of using nanoparticles of Q. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why nanoparticles of a substance can have different properties from the same substance in bulk. **[2]**

**10.** Information: a gas sensor contains a film of nanoparticles. The film's electrical resistance changes when molecules of one particular gas attach to the surface of the nanoparticles. Other gases do not attach.

**(a)** Suggest why the sensor can detect very small amounts of the gas. **[2]**

**(b)** State the term used in the specification for a sensor that responds to only one substance. **[1]**

**11.** Information: a construction material contains nanoscale fibres. A beam made from it has the same strength as a steel beam but half its mass.

**(a)** Use the information to give one advantage of the new material for building. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest one piece of further information an engineer would need before choosing the new material. **[1]**

**12.** Three samples of the same substance are compared: X has particles 100 nm across, Y 1 nm across and Z 10 µm across.

**(a)** Put the samples in order of surface area to volume ratio, highest first. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the surface area to volume ratio of Y is than that of X. **[1]**

**(c)** State which sample, if any, is **not** a nanoparticle sample. **[1]**

**13.** A student writes: "Nanoparticles are useful because each one has a very large surface area." Explain why this statement is incorrect and rewrite it correctly. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A particle 1–100 nm in size [1], containing of the order of a few hundred atoms [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 7 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 1 × 10⁻⁹ = 70 nm [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A high surface area to volume ratio [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two [2]: new computers; new catalysts; new coatings; highly selective sensors; stronger and lighter construction materials; new cosmetics such as suntan creams and deodorants. **[2]**

**6.** 0.03 µm × 1000 = 30 nm [1]; this is between 1 and 100 nm, so it is a nanoparticle [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 6 × 20² = 2400 nm² [1]

**(b)** 20³ = 8000 nm³ [1]

**(c)** 2400 ÷ 8000 = 0.3 per nm [1]

**(d)** 3 per nm [1]: the side is 10 times smaller, so the ratio is 10 times larger (or 6 ÷ 2 = 3) [1].

**[5]**

**8. (a)** 10 µm = 10 000 nm, so 10 000 ÷ 10 = 1000 times smaller [1].

**(b)** The nanoparticles have a much larger (1000 times larger) surface area to volume ratio [1]; for the same mass, much more of Q is at the surface [1], so more gas molecules can contact the catalyst and react in the same time [1].

**(c)** Less catalyst is needed for the same effect / lower cost of material [1].

**[5]**

**9.** Nanoparticles have a very high surface area to volume ratio [1], so a much larger proportion of their atoms are at the surface, which changes how the substance behaves [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** The nanoparticles have a high surface area to volume ratio, giving a large surface [1] for gas molecules to attach to, so even a few molecules cause a measurable change [1].

**(b)** Highly selective (sensor) [1].

**[3]**

**11. (a)** Lighter beams of the same strength / less mass to support / easier to transport [1].

**(b)** Any one [1]: cost; how long it lasts; how it behaves in fire or in weather; whether it is safe to make and use.

**[2]**

**12. (a)** Y, X, Z [1]

**(b)** 100 ÷ 1 = 100 times [1]

**(c)** Z (10 µm = 10 000 nm, above 100 nm) [1]

**[3]**

**13.** A single nanoparticle has a very small surface area; it is the **ratio** of surface area to volume that is large [1]. Correct version: "Nanoparticles are useful because they have a very high surface area to volume ratio, so for a given mass more of the material is at the surface." [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing nanometres with micrometres.
- Writing "large surface area" without "to volume ratio".
- Not quoting the information given in the question.
- Multiplying when converting metres to nanometres by the wrong power of ten.
- Squaring the size factor when it is the ratio that is asked for: a side 10 times smaller gives a ratio 10 times larger.
- Treating nanoparticles as a different substance from the bulk material.
