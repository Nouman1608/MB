Pearson Edexcel International A Level Business (YBS11) studies how
businesses meet customer needs, manage people and money, make strategic
decisions and compete internationally. It suits students who are
interested in how organisations work and who are comfortable combining
numerical analysis with written argument. The qualification is modular
and built from four units, each a 2-hour written paper out of 80 marks
and worth 25% of the full International A Level. Units 1 and 2 lead to
the International Advanced Subsidiary (XBS11); all four units are
needed for the full International A Level. First teaching was September
2018 and first examination January 2019, under Specification Issue 1.

## How the course is organised

The four units divide neatly into two stages.

**IAS stage**

- **Unit 1: Marketing and People** covers meeting customer needs, the
  market, the marketing mix and strategy, managing people, and
  entrepreneurs and leaders. It introduces how a business understands
  and reaches its customers and how it organises its workforce.
- **Unit 2: Managing Business Activities** covers planning a business
  and raising finance, financial planning, and managing finance. This
  is where the course's quantitative content is introduced.

**IA2 stage**

- **Unit 3: Business Decisions and Strategy** moves from day-to-day
  operations to the choices made by managers about direction and
  performance.
- **Unit 4: Global Business** places those decisions in an
  international context.

## How it's assessed

| Unit | Paper code | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1: Marketing and People | WBS11/01 | 2 hours | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 2: Managing Business Activities | WBS12/01 | 2 hours | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 3: Business Decisions and Strategy | WBS13/01 | 2 hours | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 4: Global Business | WBS14/01 | 2 hours | 80 | 25% |

All four units are externally assessed. The papers are built around
source material, but the section layout differs between the stages:

- **Units 1 and 2:** Sections A and B each contain short- and
  extended-response questions based on a different source booklet,
  worth 30 marks each. Section C is one 20-mark essay drawn from a
  source.
- **Units 3 and 4:** Section A contains short- and extended-response
  questions based on sources, worth 40 marks. Sections B and C each
  contain one 20-mark essay question.

The shift is worth noticing. At IAS, most marks come from
source-based questions, with one essay. At IA2, sustained essay writing
makes up half the paper. Because the course is modular, units can be
sat across different series, and the IAS can be certificated after
Units 1 and 2. Under this specification, IAS certification was first
available in August 2019 and full International A Level certification
in August 2020.

## How the parts fit together

Business is a subject in which topics constantly overlap, and YBS11 is
designed with that in mind.

Unit 1's work on markets and customers explains why a business makes
the marketing decisions it does, while its content on people and
leadership shows how those decisions are carried out. Unit 2 then adds
the financial dimension: whether a plan is affordable, how it will be
funded and how its results can be monitored. Together they give a
complete picture of a business at a single point in time.

The IA2 units ask what a business should do next. Strategic decisions
in Unit 3 depend on understanding markets, people and finance from the
IAS stage. Unit 4 extends those ideas beyond a single country, so
issues such as entering new markets or managing international
operations draw on marketing, finance and human resources at the same
time.

The skills develop in parallel. Reading a source booklet quickly and
picking out relevant evidence is essential in every unit. Calculation
appears wherever financial content is involved, and evaluation, which
means reaching a supported judgement rather than listing points, is
what distinguishes stronger answers across all four papers, especially
in the 20-mark essays.

## Planning your study

A clear route follows the unit order:

1. **Unit 1 first,** taking marketing and then people. Practise
   applying each concept to a short case study as you go, since that is
   how it will be assessed.
2. **Unit 2 next,** spending extra time on financial planning and
   managing finance so that calculations become routine.
3. **Unit 3 in the second stage,** revisiting IAS topics each time a
   strategic decision depends on them.
4. **Unit 4 last,** using the international context to bring the whole
   course together.

Your school will set the series in which each unit is sat, so confirm
the plan early. Begin timed practice on source-based questions as soon
as the first topics are taught, and add essay practice steadily,
particularly before the IA2 units. The exam-preparation page explains
source-booklet technique and timing in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Edexcel International A Level Business?**
Four. Each is a 2-hour written paper out of 80 marks and worth 25% of the full qualification.

**Which units make up the IAS in Business?**
Units 1 and 2, Marketing and People and Managing Business Activities, make up the International Advanced Subsidiary (XBS11). The full International A Level (YBS11) needs all four units.

**What is the format of IAL Business Unit 1 and Unit 2?**
Sections A and B each contain short- and extended-response questions based on a different source booklet, worth 30 marks each, and Section C is one 20-mark essay drawn from a source.

**How are Units 3 and 4 different?**
Section A has source-based short- and extended-response questions worth 40 marks, and Sections B and C each contain one 20-mark essay question, so essays carry more of the marks.

**Is there coursework in IAL Business?**
No. All four units are externally assessed written papers.
