These notes condense subtopic **13.1 Excretion** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definition of excretion, carbon dioxide as a waste product of respiration excreted through the lungs, and urea as a toxic waste product made in the liver from the breakdown of excess amino acids. ## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Excretion | The removal of toxic materials and the waste products of metabolism from organisms |
| Metabolism | All the chemical reactions in an organism |
| Egestion | Removal of undigested food (faeces) from the gut |
| Secretion | Release of a useful substance from a cell or gland |
| Deamination | Removal of the nitrogen-containing part of an amino acid in the liver |

## Excretion, egestion and secretion

| | Excretion | Egestion | Secretion |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is removed or released | Metabolic waste or toxic material | Undigested food | A useful substance |
| Made by body cells? | Yes | No | Yes |
| Example | Carbon dioxide, urea | Faeces | Saliva, enzymes, hormones |

## Carbon dioxide

- A waste product of **respiration**.
- Diffuses from cells into the blood, travels in the plasma to the lungs.
- Diffuses into the alveoli and is breathed out.
- **Excreted through the lungs.**
- Must be removed because it forms an acid in water and would disturb the pH and enzymes.
- Expired air has about 4% carbon dioxide, inspired air about 0.04%, which is 100 times less.

## Urea

- A **toxic** waste product.
- **Made in the liver** from the breakdown of **excess amino acids**.
- Amino acids cannot be stored, so excess ones are broken down.
- The nitrogen-containing part is removed (deamination) and made into urea.
- The remainder is used in respiration or converted to carbohydrate or fat.
- Urea travels in the blood plasma to the **kidneys** and is removed in urine.

### Process in order

1. Protein eaten is digested to amino acids.
2. Amino acids are absorbed.
3. Excess amino acids go to the liver.
4. The liver removes the nitrogen part and makes urea.
5. Urea is carried in the blood to the kidneys.
6. The kidneys remove it in the urine.

## Worked data

Urea in the hepatic portal vein is 2.0 and in the hepatic vein 5.0 (mmol per dm cubed) after a protein-rich meal.

- Difference = 5.0 - 2.0 = 3.0.
- Ratio = 5.0 divided by 2.0 = 2.5 times.
- Reason: the liver makes urea from excess amino acids.

## Short model answers

**Define excretion.** The removal of toxic materials and the waste products of metabolism from organisms.

**State where urea is made and from what.** In the liver, from the breakdown of excess amino acids.

**Explain why faeces are not excreted.** They are undigested food that never entered the body cells and was not made by metabolism, so their removal is egestion.

**Describe how carbon dioxide leaves the body.** It is made by respiring cells, carried in the blood to the lungs, diffuses into the alveoli and is breathed out.

**Explain why excess amino acids are broken down.** They cannot be stored, so the liver removes the nitrogen part as urea and the rest is used for energy or stored as carbohydrate or fat.

## Exam traps

- Urea is made in the **liver**, not the kidneys.
- Carbon dioxide is excreted by the **lungs**.
- Faeces are **egested**, not excreted.
- Urea comes from **excess amino acids**, not carbohydrates or fats.
- Excretion is of **metabolic** waste; say "metabolic".
- Oxygen is not a waste product of respiration.
- Say "toxic" for urea.

## Self-test

1. Define excretion.
2. What is metabolism?
3. Name a waste product of respiration.
4. Through which organ is carbon dioxide excreted?
5. Why must carbon dioxide be removed from the body?
6. Where is urea made?
7. From what is urea made?
8. Why are excess amino acids broken down?
9. How is urea carried to the kidneys?
10. Why are faeces not an example of excretion?
11. Name the process that removes the nitrogen part of an amino acid.
12. The concentration of urea is 3.0 in the hepatic portal vein and 7.5 in the hepatic vein. How many times higher is it in the hepatic vein?

### Answers

1. The removal of toxic materials and the waste products of metabolism from organisms.
2. All the chemical reactions in an organism.
3. Carbon dioxide.
4. The lungs.
5. It forms an acid in water and would upset the pH and the working of enzymes.
6. In the liver.
7. The breakdown of excess amino acids.
8. They cannot be stored.
9. Dissolved in the blood plasma.
10. Faeces are undigested food that was never part of the body's metabolism; removing them is egestion.
11. Deamination.
12. 7.5 divided by 3.0 = 2.5 times.
