These questions cover subtopic 8.4 Transition elements of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028). Revise first with the [Transition elements study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-transition-elements/) and the [Transition elements revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-transition-elements-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State **two** physical properties of transition elements that are not shared by Group I metals. **[2]**

**2.** Which property is typical of transition elements?

A: they form only white compounds

B: they have low densities

C: they have variable oxidation numbers

D: they react vigorously with cold water

**[1]**

**3.** Deduce the oxidation number of copper in CuO and in Cu₂O. **[2]**

**4.** Name the compound with the formula FeCl₃. **[1]**

**5.** Name the catalyst used in the Contact process and state whether it is a transition element or a compound of one. **[2]**

**6.** A student says that copper metal is coloured, so it is a transition element. Explain why this is not the property described in the syllabus. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table gives data for four metals.

| Metal | Melting point / °C | Density / g cm⁻³ | Colour of sulfate |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | 63 | 0.86 | White |
| X | 1085 | 8.96 | Blue (hydrated) |
| Y | 650 | 1.74 | White |
| Z | 1455 | 8.91 | Green (hydrated) |

**(a)** Identify the **two** metals that are transition elements. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table for your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Metal W reacts vigorously with cold water. Suggest which group W is in. **[1]**

**8.** Manganese forms several oxides and ions.

**(a)** Calculate the oxidation number of manganese in MnO₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the oxidation number of manganese in the MnO₄⁻ ion. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how these two answers illustrate a property of transition elements. **[1]**

**9.** Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to solutions of four ions: Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ and Zn²⁺.

**(a)** Give the colour of the precipitate formed with each of Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the result for Zn²⁺ cannot be used as evidence of a transition element. **[1]**

**10.** Transition elements often act as catalysts.

**(a)** State what is meant by a catalyst. **[2]**

**(b)** Give one example of a transition **element** acting as a catalyst, naming the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one advantage to an industrial process of using a catalyst. **[1]**

**11.** Chromium forms the oxide Cr₂O₃.

**(a)** Deduce the oxidation number of chromium in Cr₂O₃ and name the oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** Chromium(III) ions give a green precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Name **one** other ion in the syllabus tests that also gives a green precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a green precipitate alone does not identify chromium(III). **[1]**

**12.** Compare iron with potassium in terms of **four** properties described in the syllabus. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** High density [1]; high melting point [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** CuO: +2 [1]. Cu₂O: +1 [1].

**4.** Iron(III) chloride [1].

**5.** Vanadium(V) oxide [1]; a compound (of a transition element) [1].

**6.** The syllabus property is that transition elements form **coloured compounds**; the colour of the metal itself is not the property [1].

**7. (a)** X and Z [1].

**(b)** Any two: high melting points (1085 °C and 1455 °C); high densities (8.96 and 8.91 g cm⁻³); coloured sulfates [2].

**(c)** Group I [1] (it has a low melting point and low density, and reacts vigorously with water).

**8. (a)** +4 [1].

**(b)** Four oxygens: 4 × (−2) = −8; total charge −1 [1], so Mn = −1 + 8 = +7 [1].

**(c)** Manganese has more than one oxidation number / variable oxidation numbers [1].

**9. (a)** Cu²⁺: light blue [1]. Fe²⁺: green [1]. Fe³⁺: red-brown [1].

**(b)** Zinc gives a white precipitate, like non-transition metals such as aluminium and calcium, so it shows no colour [1].

**10. (a)** A substance that increases the rate of a reaction [1] and is not used up / is chemically unchanged at the end (lowers the activation energy) [1].

**(b)** Iron in the Haber process / nickel in the hydrogenation of alkenes [1].

**(c)** Any one: faster rate so more product per hour; lower temperature can be used, saving energy / cost; the catalyst is not used up so can be reused [1].

**11. (a)** +3 [1]; chromium(III) oxide [1].

**(b)** Iron(II), Fe²⁺ [1].

**(c)** Iron(II) also gives a green precipitate, so a further test is needed (e.g. excess sodium hydroxide: chromium(III) precipitate dissolves, iron(II) does not) [1].

*Marking note:* for 10(a), "speeds up a reaction" earns the first mark; "is not used up" or "lowers the activation energy" earns the second. "Changes the rate" is not enough, because it does not say the rate increases.

**12.** Any four comparisons [4]:
- Iron has a higher melting point than potassium [1].
- Iron has a higher density than potassium [1].
- Iron has variable oxidation numbers (+2 and +3); potassium only +1 [1].
- Iron forms coloured compounds; potassium compounds are white [1].
- Iron acts as a catalyst (Haber process); potassium does not [1].
- Potassium is far more reactive with water than iron [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "strong" or "hard" for the physical properties.
- Calling the metal coloured rather than its compounds.
- Omitting the charge on the ion when calculating an oxidation number in MnO₄⁻.
- Describing properties of iron without a comparative when the question says "compare".
- Naming vanadium (the element) as the Contact process catalyst instead of vanadium(V) oxide.
