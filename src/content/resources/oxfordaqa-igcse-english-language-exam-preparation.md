OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270) is a linear qualification with a compulsory
component and a route choice. **Every candidate sits Paper 1 (Literary Non-Fiction and Composition)**
-- 2 hours, 80 marks, and 60% of the qualification. The remaining 40% comes from **either Route A's
Paper 2** (Source-Based Reading and Directed Writing, a 2-hour, 80-mark examination) **or Route B's
Non-Exam Assessment** (a written task and commentary, 60 marks, teacher assessed and AQA moderated).
An optional, separately certificated Speaking and Listening endorsement also exists but carries no
marks toward this qualification's grade. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Literary Non-fiction and Composition](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-english-language-paper-1/) and
[Source-Based Reading and Directed Writing](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-english-language-paper-2/).

## Confirm your route before you plan any revision

Route A and Route B carry the same 40% but demand entirely different preparation: a timed examination
against unseen sources on one hand, a written task and commentary produced over time on the other.
**Exam-preparation priority**: establish your route with your centre early. Rehearsing exam technique
for a paper you are not sitting, or under-developing coursework because you expected an exam, forfeits
40% of the grade to an avoidable misunderstanding.

## Paper 1 is 60% -- it deserves the majority of revision time

The compulsory component carries more weight than the route component by half again. **Exam-
preparation priority**: weight revision accordingly. Within Paper 1, the composition element is
generally the most improvable through deliberate practice, because writing technique responds to
drilling in a way that reading response develops more slowly.

## Literary non-fiction reading rewards analysis of effect, not identification

Naming a technique -- "the writer uses a simile" -- is not yet analysis. The mark comes from explaining
what the choice does to a reader and why. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise a three-part
sentence for every point: the choice, the effect, and the reason it produces that effect. Practise it
on unseen non-fiction, since the exam works from material you will not have studied.

## Composition is judged on control, not on ambition

An over-reached piece with an unfinished ending scores below a well-shaped, complete one. **Exam-
preparation priority**: practise planning a shape -- an opening, three developments, a close -- and
finishing within the time. A deliberate, controlled ending is worth more than an extra paragraph of
description that runs out of clock.

## The Speaking and Listening endorsement carries no marks toward the grade

Because it is separately certificated, time spent on it is time not spent on components that determine
the grade. **Exam-preparation priority**: confirm whether you are entered for it, and if so timetable
its preparation separately rather than letting it absorb Paper 1 revision time.

## On Route B, the commentary is assessed, not just the task

Route B's non-exam assessment is a written task **and** a commentary. The commentary is where the
candidate demonstrates conscious control of choices. **Exam-preparation priority**: draft the
commentary against the finished task, explaining specific decisions -- why this register, why this
structure, why this word -- rather than describing the piece in general terms.

## Worked routine: planning a composition under time

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Minutes 0-4: plan the shape, not the sentences
Decide the ending first, then three stages that lead to it, then
the opening image or line. Knowing the destination prevents the
drift that consumes the last ten minutes.

Minutes 4-8: the opening
Establish voice and situation quickly. Avoid a long scene-setting
preamble -- it is the most common way to run short later.

Minutes 8-32: the three developments
One paragraph cluster each. Vary sentence length deliberately at
least once per stage.

Minutes 32-38: the close
Written to the ending you chose at minute 4, not improvised.

Minutes 38-42: targeted check
Check for your own recurring errors specifically -- the punctuation
or agreement mistakes you personally repeat -- rather than
re-reading generally.
```

Deciding the ending first is the step that most reliably produces a complete piece: an unplanned
composition tends to expand in the middle and stop rather than conclude.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: confirm whether you are on Route A or Route B; weight revision towards Paper
  1's 60%; practise effect-based analysis on unseen non-fiction; practise composition planning to a
  timed shape; if entered for the Speaking and Listening endorsement, timetable it separately.
- **On Route B**: draft the commentary against the finished task, explaining specific choices.
- **During Paper 1**: plan the ending before writing, and reserve four minutes for a targeted
  accuracy check.
- **In every reading answer**: give the choice, the effect, and the reason for the effect.

## Self-test

1. What is compulsory, and what is the choice?
2. Why should Paper 1 receive the larger share of revision?
3. What turns feature-spotting into analysis?
4. What is assessed in Route B beyond the written task itself?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 1 (Literary Non-Fiction and Composition, 60%) is compulsory for everyone; the
remaining 40% is either Route A's Paper 2 examination or Route B's non-exam assessment. 2. Because it
carries 60% against the route component's 40%, and its composition element responds particularly well
to deliberate practice. 3. Explaining what the writer's choice does to a reader and why it produces
that effect, rather than naming the technique. 4. The commentary, in which the candidate explains
specific choices -- register, structure, particular words -- made in the task.
