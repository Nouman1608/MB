These notes condense section 1.5 (Growth and evolution) of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024, for SL and HL: internal and external economies and diseconomies of scale; internal and external growth; reasons to grow and to stay small; and the external growth methods of mergers and acquisitions, takeovers, joint ventures, strategic alliances and franchising. Worked examples are in the [1.5 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-1-5/), and exam-style questions in the [1.5 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-business-1-5-practice/). Sections 1.5 and 1.6 are also summarised in the [growth and evolution and MNCs revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-growth-and-evolution-and-multinational-companies-mncs-revision-notes/). Definitions are standard textbook forms; figures are invented, in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unit (average) cost | Total cost ÷ output |
| Economies of scale | Falls in unit cost as output rises |
| Diseconomies of scale | Rises in unit cost as output rises beyond some point |
| Internal economies | Savings from the firm's own growth |
| External economies | Savings from the growth of the industry or area |
| Internal (organic) growth | Expansion using the firm's own resources |
| External (inorganic) growth | Expansion by involving another business |
| Merger | Two firms agree to combine into one |
| Acquisition / takeover | One firm gains control of another, usually by buying its shares; hostile if against the target board's wishes |
| Joint venture | Two or more firms create a new, jointly owned entity |
| Strategic alliance | Firms cooperate on agreed aims but stay legally separate, with no new entity |
| Franchising | A franchisor lets a franchisee trade under its brand and methods for fees and royalties |

## Economies of scale: types and how to show them

| Internal economy | How it works |
|---|---|
| Purchasing | Bulk buying lowers the input price per unit |
| Technical | Large machines and automation cost less per unit when spread over more output |
| Financial | Large firms borrow more cheaply |
| Marketing | One campaign's cost is spread over more sales |
| Managerial | Specialist managers become affordable and efficient |
| Risk-bearing | Wider product range or markets spread risk |

**Method:** calculate unit cost before and after, then split the saving by cause.

*Reminder.* Fixed cost 9,000 over 3,000 units is 3.00 a unit; over 6,000 units it is 1.50, a technical or spreading saving of 1.50 a unit.

## Diseconomies of scale

- **Communication**: more layers, slower and less accurate messages.
- **Coordination**: harder to align sites and departments.
- **Motivation**: staff feel remote; absence and staff turnover rise.
- **External**: congestion, higher local rents and wages, competition for skilled staff.

## Growth or staying small

| Reasons to grow | Reasons to stay small |
|---|---|
| Economies of scale | Keep control and personal service |
| Higher market share and power | Serve a niche too small for big firms |
| Spread risk | Avoid diseconomies and stay flexible |
| Higher profit and owner wealth | Avoid the debt and risk of expansion |
| Better survival chances | Lack of finance, staff or premises |
| Managers' status and pay | Owner's lifestyle or social objectives |

**Judging size:** consider the market, the economies available, owner objectives, finance and the competition.

## Choosing a growth method

| Need | Usually favours |
|---|---|
| Speed | Acquisition, takeover, alliance, franchising |
| Full control | Organic growth, acquisition |
| Little capital | Organic growth, alliance, franchising |
| Shared risk and local knowledge | Joint venture, alliance |
| Easy exit | Strategic alliance |

**Payback-style comparison:** outlay ÷ expected annual profit = years to recover the outlay.

*Reminder.* A joint venture costing 3.0m that earns 0.6m a year for the firm recovers its outlay in 5 years.

**Franchisee check:** profit = gross profit − royalty − other costs; subtract the up-front fee in year one. Compare with the independent option.

## Exam traps

- Naming an economy of scale with no link to unit cost or the case.
- Saying growth always lowers costs.
- Mixing up internal economies (own growth) and internal growth (organic expansion).
- Calling a strategic alliance a joint venture.
- Ignoring owner objectives when recommending growth.
- Comparing methods on cost alone.
- An evaluation with no judgement or condition.

## Quick self-test

1. Define diseconomies of scale.
2. Distinguish internal economies of scale from internal growth.
3. Unit cost falls from 4.20 to 3.50 as output rises. Find the percentage fall.
4. A firm's input price falls from 1.10 to 0.95 per unit after bulk ordering. Which economy is this, and what is the saving per unit?
5. Give one example of an external diseconomy of scale.
6. Give two reasons a business might stay small.
7. Distinguish a joint venture from a strategic alliance.
8. What makes a takeover hostile?
9. Which external growth method lets a business expand quickly using other people's capital?
10. An acquisition costs 9.0m and is expected to add 1.2m profit a year. How many years to recover the cost?
11. A franchisee has sales of 200,000, a gross margin of 55%, a 5% royalty and other costs of 80,000. Find the annual profit.
12. Give one reason many mergers fail to deliver the savings expected.

### Answers

1. Rises in unit cost when a firm's output grows beyond a certain point.
2. Internal economies are cost savings from the firm's own larger size; internal growth is the method of expanding using its own resources.
3. 0.70/4.20 × 100 = **16.7%**.
4. A **purchasing** economy; saving **0.15** per unit.
5. For example: road congestion in a crowded industrial area raises transport costs for every firm.
6. Any two: keep control, serve a niche, stay flexible, avoid debt, lack of finance, lifestyle aims.
7. A joint venture creates a new jointly owned entity; an alliance is cooperation without a new entity.
8. It goes ahead against the wishes of the target company's board, by buying shares directly from shareholders.
9. **Franchising**.
10. 9.0/1.2 = **7.5 years**.
11. Gross profit 110,000; royalty 10,000; profit 110,000 − 10,000 − 80,000 = **20,000**.
12. For example: a culture clash between the two workforces, or the buyer paying more than the target was worth.
