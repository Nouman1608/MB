## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain the difference between magnification and resolution. **[2]**

**2.** A plant cell appears 45 mm wide in a photograph taken at a magnification of x1,500. Calculate the real width of the cell in micrometres. Show your working. **[2]**

**3.** Which statement about the transmission electron microscope is correct?

A: It can be used to observe living cells.

B: Electrons pass through a very thin specimen.

C: It has a lower resolution than an optical microscope.

D: It produces a three-dimensional image of the surface. **[1]**

**4.** State the function of the Golgi apparatus and name one other organelle that has a double membrane. **[2]**

**5.** Which organelle contains hydrolytic enzymes? **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** An electron micrograph of a liver cell has a scale bar 20 mm long, labelled 2 µm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the micrograph. **[2]**

**(b)** A mitochondrion in the micrograph is 36 mm long. Calculate its real length in micrometres. **[2]**

**7.** Liver tissue was homogenised and the cell components were separated by ultracentrifugation.

**(a)** The tissue was homogenised in a cold, isotonic buffered solution. Explain the reason for each of the three conditions. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the homogenate was centrifuged at a low speed first and then at higher speeds. **[2]**

**(c)** State which organelle would be found in the first pellet. **[1]**

**8.** A student compares two cells. Cell X is from a pancreas and produces large amounts of digestive enzyme for secretion. Cell Y is from a muscle and contracts frequently.

**(a)** Cell X has extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum. Explain how this suits its function. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why cell Y contains many mitochondria. **[2]**

**9.** A drawing of a bacterium is 12 mm long. The real length of the bacterium is 1.5 µm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** A student says that ribosomes in the bacterium could be seen if the drawing had been made from an optical microscope with a higher magnification. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** Compare the structure of a bacterial cell with the structure of a cell from a plant leaf. **[4]**

**11.** Compare the use of a transmission electron microscope with that of a scanning electron microscope to study the surface of a cell. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Magnification is how many times larger the image is than the real object [1]. Resolution is the smallest distance between two points that can still be seen as separate [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A larger image is not necessarily a clearer one. Resolution is the limit on detail.

**2.** Real size = image size / magnification = 45 mm / 1,500 [1] = 0.03 mm = 30 µm [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* If you give 0.03 with no unit, or 0.03 mm, you have not finished. The question asks for micrometres.

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* TEM specimens are dead and in a vacuum. Option D describes a scanning electron microscope.

**4.** It modifies and packages proteins (and lipids) [1]. Mitochondrion, chloroplast or nucleus [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Lysosome [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 2 µm = 0.002 mm [1]. Magnification = 20 / 0.002 = x10,000 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Real length = 36 mm / 10,000 [1] = 0.0036 mm = 3.6 µm [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the scale bar first, then use that magnification for everything else on the micrograph.

**7. (a)** Cold: slows down enzymes, so that enzymes released from lysosomes do not digest the organelles [1]. Isotonic: the solution has the same water potential as the cells, so organelles do not burst or shrink by osmosis [1]. Buffered: keeps the pH constant, so enzymes and other proteins are not denatured [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** The largest and densest organelles settle at a low speed, so they can be separated first [1]. The supernatant is spun faster each time so that progressively smaller, less dense organelles pellet [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Nuclei [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** Ribosomes on the rough endoplasmic reticulum make protein (enzymes) [1]. The protein passes into the cisternae (the space between the membranes) [1] and is folded and transported in vesicles towards the Golgi apparatus for secretion [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Muscle contraction needs a lot of ATP [1]. Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration, which produces ATP [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 1.5 µm = 0.0015 mm [1]. Magnification = 12 / 0.0015 = x8,000 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that your answer is sensible. The drawing is much larger than the bacterium, so the magnification must be in the thousands.

**(b)** The limit is resolution, not magnification [1]. An optical microscope cannot resolve structures as small as ribosomes, however much the image is enlarged, because of the longer wavelength of light [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any four from: both have a cell wall, but the bacterium's contains murein while the plant's is made of cellulose [1]; the bacterium's DNA is circular and free in the cytoplasm, but the plant's DNA is linear and inside a nucleus [1]; the bacterium has no membrane-bounded organelles, but the plant cell has mitochondria and chloroplasts [1]; the bacterium has small ribosomes, but plant ribosomes are larger [1]; the bacterium is smaller [1]; the bacterium may have a capsule, plasmids or flagella [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point must compare both cells. A list of bacterial features alone earns little.

**11.** The TEM passes electrons through a very thin specimen, whereas the SEM scans electrons across the surface [1]. The TEM shows internal detail, whereas the SEM shows surface detail [1]. The SEM gives a three-dimensional image, whereas the TEM image is two-dimensional [1]. The TEM has the higher resolution, but both need a specimen in a vacuum, so neither can be used on living cells [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing magnification with resolution.
- Not converting millimetres and micrometres.
- Giving a reason for "isotonic" that does not mention osmosis or water potential.
- Writing only about the bacterium in a comparison question.
- Saying electron microscopes can image living material.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.1.2 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**Do I need to remember the exact order of pellets in ultracentrifugation?**
Know that nuclei pellet first and the smallest structures, such as ribosomes, last. The principle (density and size) matters more than a memorised list.
