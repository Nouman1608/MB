All data you need for calculations appears in the questions. Use 24.0 dm³ for one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure.

## Questions

**1.** Cyanide ions react with haloalkanes and with carbonyl compounds.

**(a)** State what is meant by a nucleophile. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the atom in CN⁻ whose lone pair is used to make the new C–C bond. **[1]**

**2.** 1-Bromo-2-ethylbutane, (CH₃CH₂)₂CHCH₂Br, is heated under reflux with potassium cyanide in ethanol.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation using KCN. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the nitrile, following IUPAC rules. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why chemists find this reaction useful when building larger molecules. **[1]**

**3.** (Chloromethyl)cyclopentane, C₅H₉CH₂Cl, reacts with sodium cyanide in ethanol.

**(a)** Name the type of mechanism. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the mechanism. For each curly arrow, state its start and end points. Give both products. **[3]**

**4.** Cyclohexanecarbaldehyde, C₆H₁₁CHO, reacts with NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq).

**(a)** Give the structural formula and the name of the organic product. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the mechanism, using H⁺ for the final step. **[3]**

**5.** Nonanenitrile, CH₃(CH₂)₇CN, is hydrogenated over a nickel catalyst.

**(a)** Give a balanced equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**
**(c)** 5.56 g of nonanenitrile (Mᵣ 139.0) is reduced completely. Calculate the volume of hydrogen used at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

**6.** The product of question 4 is converted into a carboxylic acid.

**(a)** State the reagent and conditions. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction using hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**7.** Benzene reacts with 2-bromo-2-methylbutane, CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂Br, in the presence of iron(III) bromide.

**(a)** Write the overall equation and name the organic product. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation to show how the electrophile forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the equation in which the FeBr₃ catalyst is re-formed. **[1]**

**8.** Six reactions are listed.

A: 1-chloropropane with NaOH(aq)
B: 2-iodobutane with KCN in ethanol
C: propanone with NaBH₄
D: butanal with NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq)
E: benzene with ethanoyl chloride and AlCl₃
F: ethanenitrile with H₂/Ni

Give the letters of the three reactions that form a new carbon–carbon bond. **[3]**

**9.** Bromocycloheptane, C₇H₁₃Br, is converted in two steps into cycloheptanecarboxylic acid, C₇H₁₃COOH.

**(a)** For each step, state the reagent and conditions. Name the intermediate. **[3]**
**(b)** State which step forms a carbon–carbon bond. **[1]**

**10.** Benzene reacts with heptanoyl chloride, CH₃(CH₂)₅COCl, and aluminium chloride.

**(a)** Write the overall equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Show, with an equation, how AlCl₃ produces the electrophile. **[1]**
**(c)** 3.90 g of benzene reacts with excess heptanoyl chloride, and 6.97 g of the ketone C₁₃H₁₈O is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. Mᵣ: benzene 78.0, C₁₃H₁₈O 190.0. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the atom economy for making the ketone. Mᵣ of heptanoyl chloride = 148.5. **[2]**

**11.** Compound A, C₇H₁₅Br, has an unbranched carbon chain, and its molecule is symmetrical. A is heated with KCN in ethanol to give B. B is reduced with H₂/Ni to give C. B is heated under reflux with dilute hydrochloric acid to give D.

**(a)** Give the structural formula and name of A. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the structural formula and name of B. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the structural formulae of C and D. **[2]**
**(d)** 7.16 g of A (Mᵣ 178.9) gives 3.25 g of B (Mᵣ 125.0). Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**12.** A chemist plans to make 2-hydroxy-2-phenylbutanoic acid, C₆H₅C(OH)(CH₂CH₃)COOH, from benzene in three steps.

**(a)** Describe the route. Give the reagents and conditions for each step, the structure of each intermediate, and state which steps form carbon–carbon bonds. **[6]**
**(b)** The step yields are 72%, 80% and 55%. Calculate the mass of benzene (Mᵣ 78.0) needed to make 2.00 g of the acid (Mᵣ 180.0). **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A species able to donate a lone pair of electrons, forming a dative or new covalent bond [1]
**(b)** The **carbon** atom, which carries a lone pair [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Something attracted to positive charge" is too vague; the idea of donating an electron pair is what earns the mark.

**2. (a)** (CH₃CH₂)₂CHCH₂Br + KCN → (CH₃CH₂)₂CHCH₂CN + KBr (or with CN⁻ and Br⁻) [1]
**(b)** **3-Ethylpentanenitrile** [1]
**(c)** A new C–C bond forms, so the carbon chain is lengthened by one carbon [1]
*Examiner insight:* Number from the nitrile carbon; keeping the haloalkane's "butane" stem gives the wrong name.

**3. (a)** Nucleophilic substitution [1]
**(b)** Dipole Cδ+–Clδ− shown; curly arrow starting at the cyanide carbon's lone pair and ending at the δ+ CH₂ carbon [1]. The C–Cl bonding pair moves onto chlorine (second arrow) [1]. Products: **C₅H₉CH₂CN** (2-cyclopentylethanenitrile) and **Cl⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Arrows must start at a lone pair or a bond; an arrow from the ring or from the minus sign on CN⁻ is unlikely to gain credit.

**4. (a)** **C₆H₁₁CH(OH)CN** [1]; **2-cyclohexyl-2-hydroxyethanenitrile** [1]
**(b)** First arrow starts at the cyanide carbon's lone pair and ends at the δ+ C of C=O [1]. Second arrow moves the π pair of C=O onto oxygen, giving the intermediate C₆H₁₁CH(CN)O⁻ [1]. Arrow from an O⁻ lone pair to H⁺, giving the product [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the negative charge on the oxygen of the intermediate; the protonation arrow then has a clear starting point.

**5. (a)** CH₃(CH₂)₇CN + 2H₂ → CH₃(CH₂)₇CH₂NH₂ [1]
**(b)** **Nonan-1-amine** [1]
**(c)** Amount of nitrile = 5.56 ÷ 139.0 = 0.0400 mol, so H₂ = 0.0800 mol [1]; volume = 0.0800 × 24.0 = **1.92 dm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take two moles of H₂ per mole of nitrile from your equation in (a); a 1 : 1 ratio halves the volume.

**6. (a)** Dilute hydrochloric acid, heated under reflux [1]
**(b)** C₆H₁₁CH(OH)CN + 2H₂O + HCl → **C₆H₁₁CH(OH)COOH** [1] + NH₄Cl, correctly balanced [1]
*Examiner insight:* The OH group is not changed; only –CN becomes –COOH, and the nitrogen ends up as ammonium chloride.

**7. (a)** C₆H₆ + CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂Br → C₆H₅C(CH₃)₂CH₂CH₃ + HBr [1]; **(2-methylbutan-2-yl)benzene** (2-methyl-2-phenylbutane) [1]
**(b)** CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂Br + FeBr₃ → CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂⁺ + FeBr₄⁻ [1]
**(c)** H⁺ + FeBr₄⁻ → FeBr₃ + HBr [1]
*Examiner insight:* The inorganic product here is HBr, not HCl; copy the halogen from the reagents given.

**8.** **B** [1], **D** [1], **E** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A, C and F change functional groups without changing the carbon skeleton; list only the reactions that join carbon to carbon.

**9. (a)** Step 1: KCN (or NaCN) in ethanol, heat under reflux [1]. Intermediate: **cycloheptanecarbonitrile**, C₇H₁₃CN [1]. Step 2: dilute HCl (or H₂SO₄), heat under reflux [1]
**(b)** Step 1 [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Acid" alone is too vague for step 2; name a dilute acid and give reflux.

**10. (a)** C₆H₆ + CH₃(CH₂)₅COCl → C₆H₅CO(CH₂)₅CH₃ + HCl [1]
**(b)** CH₃(CH₂)₅COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃(CH₂)₅CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ [1]
**(c)** Amount of benzene = 3.90 ÷ 78.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.0500 × 190.0 = 9.50 g [1]; yield = 6.97 ÷ 9.50 × 100 = **73.4%** [1]
**(d)** Total Mᵣ of reactants = 78.0 + 148.5 = 226.5 [1]; atom economy = 190.0 ÷ 226.5 × 100 = **83.9%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Atom economy uses the balanced equation, not the masses measured; the catalyst is not included because it is regenerated.

**11. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CHBrCH₂CH₂CH₃, **4-bromoheptane** [1]
**(b)** (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₂CHCN [1]; **2-propylpentanenitrile** [1]
**(c)** C: (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₂CHCH₂NH₂ [1]; D: (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₂CHCOOH [1]
**(d)** Amount of A = 7.16 ÷ 178.9 = 0.0400 mol [1]; theoretical mass of B = 0.0400 × 125.0 = 5.00 g [1]; yield = 3.25 ÷ 5.00 × 100 = **65.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the longest chain through the nitrile carbon has five carbons, not eight; naming it as an octanenitrile ignores the branch.

**12. (a)** Step 1: propanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂COCl, with AlCl₃ (halogen carrier), dry [1]. Intermediate: C₆H₅COCH₂CH₃, 1-phenylpropan-1-one [1]. Step 2: NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) [1]. Intermediate: C₆H₅C(OH)(CN)CH₂CH₃, 2-hydroxy-2-phenylbutanenitrile [1]. Step 3: dilute HCl, heat under reflux [1]. Steps 1 and 2 both form C–C bonds [1]
**(b)** Overall yield = 0.72 × 0.80 × 0.55 = 0.3168 [1]; amount of acid = 2.00 ÷ 180.0 = 0.01111 mol [1]; benzene = 0.01111 ÷ 0.3168 × 78.0 = **2.74 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Plans gain credit step by step, so give reagent, condition and structure for each stage even if one later step is uncertain.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the CN⁻ arrow at N rather than at the carbon atom's lone pair (questions 3 and 4).
- Leaving out the O⁻ intermediate or its protonation in nucleophilic addition.
- Writing "aqueous KCN" for the haloalkane reaction instead of cyanide in ethanol.
- Naming the nitrile from the wrong end or without counting the CN carbon (questions 2 and 11).
- Using one H₂ per nitrile in reduction equations and volume calculations.
- Writing NH₃ instead of NH₄⁺ or NH₄Cl as the hydrolysis by-product.
- Leaving HCl or HBr off a Friedel–Crafts equation, or mixing up the halogen of the carrier.
- Multiplying by the yield instead of dividing when working back to a starting mass (question 12).
