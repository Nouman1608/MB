Amounts are in dollars ($).

## The seven syllabus points

1. Preparing and commenting on statements comparing results for different years.
2. Interpreting the 6.1 ratios.
3. Suggestions and recommendations for improving profitability, liquidity and
   working capital.
4. Gross profit margin and profit margin as indicators of profitability.
5. How gross profit is affected by inventory valuation, sales quantity and changes
   in selling and purchasing prices.
6. How profit for the year is affected by changes in gross profit, other income
   and expenses.
7. Reasons for the difference between cash and profit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Comparison statement | Two or more years side by side with the change and % change |
| Profitability | Ability to earn profit from sales and from capital |
| Liquidity | Ability to pay debts as they fall due |
| Working capital | Current assets less current liabilities |
| Efficiency | How quickly inventory sells and cash is collected and paid |
| % change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |

## Reading each ratio

| Ratio | Higher usually means | But watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | better buying or pricing | an overstated closing inventory |
| Profit margin | expenses well controlled | a one-off gain in other income |
| ROCE | finance used efficiently | new capital not yet earning |
| Current ratio | more cover for current debts | too much idle inventory or cash |
| Acid test | can pay without selling inventory | — |
| Inventory days | goods selling slowly (worse) | building up for a new range |
| Receivables days | customers paying slowly (worse) | longer credit used to win sales |
| Payables days | business paying slowly (helps cash) | loss of supplier goodwill or discounts |

## What moves gross profit

| Change | Gross profit | Gross profit margin |
|---|---|---|
| More units sold, same price and cost | Up | No change |
| Higher selling price | Up | Up |
| Higher purchase price | Down | Down |
| Closing inventory overstated | Up | Up (misleading) |
| Closing inventory understated | Down | Down (misleading) |

## What moves profit for the year

Profit for the year = gross profit + other income − expenses. A rise in profit
margin needs gross profit margin to rise, other income to rise, or expenses to fall
as a percentage of revenue. Compare expenses ÷ revenue for each year to see which.

## Writing a comment

**Figures → cause → consequence.**

"Trade receivables turnover rose from 30 to 41 days because longer credit was given
to trade customers; cash is collected more slowly, which weakens liquidity."

## Recommendations bank

| Problem | Suggestions |
|---|---|
| Low gross profit margin | Cheaper supplier, bulk or trade discounts, careful price rise, reduce damage and theft |
| Low profit margin | Cut or control expenses, increase other income |
| Weak liquidity | Reduce inventory, collect receivables faster, long-term finance, sell unused assets |
| Long receivables days | Cash discounts, credit checks, chase overdue accounts, shorter terms |
| Short payables days | Negotiate longer credit with suppliers |

Add a drawback to show evaluation, for example "a price rise may reduce sales volume".

## Cash versus profit

| Cash lower than profit | Cash higher than profit |
|---|---|
| Inventory increased | Depreciation charged (non-cash) |
| Trade receivables increased | Trade payables increased |
| Trade payables decreased | Inventory or receivables decreased |
| Non-current assets bought | Loan received or capital introduced |
| Drawings or dividends paid; loan repaid | Non-current assets sold |

## Exam traps

- **Volume does not change margins** on its own.
- **Current ratio up, acid test down** usually means inventory has built up.
- **Do not judge one ratio in isolation;** link profitability to efficiency and liquidity.
- **Use the scenario:** every cause should come from the information given.
- **Profit is not cash.**

## Self-test

1. Revenue rose from 80,000 to 92,000. Calculate the % change.
2. Can selling more units at the same price and cost raise the gross profit margin?
3. Give one reason why profit margin may fall while gross profit margin rises.
4. The current ratio rose but the acid test fell. What does this suggest?
5. Give two ways to reduce trade receivables turnover.
6. What is a drawback of paying suppliers more slowly?
7. State one reason profit may exceed the increase in cash.
8. State one reason cash may increase by more than profit.
9. How does an understated closing inventory affect the gross profit margin?
10. Expenses were 30,000 on revenue of 120,000. Express expenses as a % of revenue.
11. Why might a fall in ROCE after a large investment be temporary?
12. Name the three parts of a full comment on a ratio.

### Answers

1. 12,000 ÷ 80,000 × 100 = **15.00%**.
2. No: gross profit rises in proportion to revenue.
3. Expenses rose faster than revenue (or other income fell).
4. Inventory has built up, so liquid assets have not kept pace.
5. For example, offer cash discounts and chase overdue accounts.
6. Suppliers may refuse credit or withdraw discounts.
7. For example, an increase in inventory or receivables.
8. For example, depreciation is charged but no cash is paid.
9. It makes the margin too low.
10. **25.00%**.
11. The new asset may take time to generate extra profit.
12. The figures, the cause, the consequence.
