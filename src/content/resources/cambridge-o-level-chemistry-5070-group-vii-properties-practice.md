Revise first with the [Group VII properties study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-vii-properties/) and the [Group VII properties revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-vii-properties-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the appearance of chlorine and of iodine at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

**2.** Which halogen is a liquid at r.t.p.?

A: chlorine

B: bromine

C: iodine

D: fluorine

**[1]**

**3.** State the trend in density down Group VII and give evidence for it from the states of the elements at r.t.p. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the formula of bromine is written as Br₂ and not Br. **[1]**

**5.** Bromine water is added to aqueous sodium chloride. State what is observed and explain why. **[2]**

**6.** Name the products when chlorine is bubbled into aqueous sodium iodide. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student adds aqueous chlorine, bromine and iodine separately to aqueous potassium chloride, potassium bromide and potassium iodide.

**(a)** Copy and complete the table, writing the colour seen or "no change". **[3]**

| Added | KCl(aq) | KBr(aq) | KI(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine | — | | |
| Bromine | | — | |
| Iodine | | | — |

**(b)** Use the results to place the three halogens in order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[1]**

**8.** Chlorine reacts with aqueous potassium bromide.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation and identify the spectator ion. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the half-equation for the change to the chlorine and state whether chlorine is oxidised or reduced. **[2]**

**9.** Explain, in terms of electrons, why bromine displaces iodine from iodide ions but iodine does not displace bromine from bromide ions. **[3]**

**10.** Astatine is the element below iodine in Group VII.

**(a)** Predict the state of astatine at r.t.p. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict whether astatine would displace iodine from potassium iodide solution. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Give the formula of an astatine molecule and of the astatide ion. **[2]**

**11.** Fluorine is the element above chlorine in Group VII.

**(a)** Predict how the reactivity of fluorine compares with that of chlorine. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an ionic equation for the reaction of fluorine with bromide ions. **[1]**

**(c)** State the role of fluorine in this reaction (oxidising agent or reducing agent) and give a reason. **[2]**

**12.** Aqueous potassium iodide is used to test for oxidising agents. A colourless solution of an unknown substance Y is added to aqueous potassium iodide, and the mixture turns brown.

**(a)** Name the substance that causes the brown colour. **[1]**

**(b)** State what has happened to the iodide ions, in terms of electrons. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest a halogen that could be Y and explain your choice. **[2]**

**13.** A technician has three unlabelled bottles. Each contains one of aqueous sodium chloride, aqueous sodium bromide or aqueous sodium iodide. She has only chlorine water and bromine water to test them with.

**(a)** Describe how she could use these two reagents to identify all three solutions. Give the observation expected for each solution. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why she could not identify the solutions using chlorine water alone if two of the bottles gave colours that looked similar in dim light. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Chlorine: pale yellow-green gas [1]. Iodine: grey-black solid [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** Density increases down the group [1]; chlorine is a gas, bromine a liquid and iodine a solid [1].

**4.** Bromine exists as diatomic molecules / two atoms joined by a covalent bond [1].

**5.** No change / stays orange [1]; bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so it cannot displace chlorine from chloride ions [1].

**6.** Sodium chloride [1]; iodine [1].

**7. (a)** One mark per correct row [3]:
- Chlorine: KBr orange; KI brown.
- Bromine: KCl no change; KI brown.
- Iodine: KCl no change; KBr no change.

**(b)** Chlorine > bromine > iodine [1].

**8. (a)** Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂ [1].

**(b)** Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ [1]; spectator ion K⁺ [1].

**(c)** Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ [1]; reduced (it gains electrons) [1].

**9.** Bromine is more reactive than iodine / gains electrons more readily [1]; so bromine takes electrons from iodide ions, forming bromide ions and iodine [1]; iodine is less reactive, so it cannot take electrons from bromide ions [1].

**10. (a)** Solid [1].

**(b)** No [1]; astatine is less reactive than iodine because reactivity decreases down the group [1].

**(c)** At₂ [1]; At⁻ [1].

**11. (a)** More reactive [1].

**(b)** F₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2F⁻ + Br₂ [1].

**(c)** Oxidising agent [1]; it gains electrons / is reduced, and oxidises the bromide ions [1].

**12. (a)** Iodine [1].

**(b)** They have lost electrons / been oxidised [1].

**(c)** Chlorine or bromine (aqueous) [1]; it is more reactive than iodine, so it displaces iodine from iodide ions [1].

**13. (a)** Add bromine water to a sample of each solution [1]; only sodium iodide turns brown, so it is identified [1]. Add chlorine water to fresh samples of the other two [1]; sodium bromide turns orange and sodium chloride shows no change [1].
*Marking note:* a method that adds chlorine water first is also creditworthy if it then uses bromine water to separate bromide from iodide.

**(b)** Chlorine displaces both bromine (orange) and iodine (brown), so it gives a colour with two of the solutions; bromine water distinguishes them because it reacts only with iodide [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "chloride" when the element chlorine is meant.
- Ionic equations with single halogen atoms or one halide ion.
- Giving the colour of the element (red-brown, grey-black) instead of the colour of its solution.
- Leaving "no reaction" boxes empty in results tables.
- Saying the halogen added is oxidised: it gains electrons, so it is reduced.
- Using one reagent to tell three solutions apart when it reacts with two of them; plan the order of tests so each result is unique.
