## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes respiration? **[1]**

A: The movement of air into and out of the lungs

B: The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism

C: The taking in of materials for energy, growth and development

D: The removal of the waste products of metabolism

**2.** State the characteristic of living organisms shown in each example.

**(a)** A fruit fly lays eggs that hatch into fruit flies. **[1]**

**(b)** A cat eats a fish. **[1]**

**(c)** Carbon dioxide passes out of a fish's gills. **[1]**

**3.** Name the characteristic of living organisms described as "the ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment". **[1]**

**4.** State **two** differences between respiration and breathing. **[2]**

**5.** A child's mass rises by 0.5 kg after drinking a large glass of water. Explain why this is not growth. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student germinates bean seeds. Every two days she removes ten seedlings, dries them in an oven until their mass stops changing, and records their total dry mass.

| Day | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dry mass of ten seedlings / g | 4.0 | 3.6 | 3.4 | 3.8 | 4.6 |

**(a)** Describe the change in dry mass over the eight days. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the dry mass fell between day 0 and day 4. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why the dry mass rose after day 4. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the student measured dry mass rather than fresh mass. **[1]**

**7.** A robot vacuum cleaner moves around a room, detects walls and changes direction, and plugs itself into the mains to recharge its battery.

**(a)** Identify **two** characteristics of living organisms that the robot appears to show. **[2]**

**(b)** Use **two other** characteristics of living organisms to explain why the robot is not alive. **[4]**

**8.** Describe how a flowering plant shows each of these characteristics.

**(a)** Movement **[1]**

**(b)** Excretion **[2]**

**(c)** Nutrition **[2]**

**9.** Sweat removes water and salts from the body. Salt is taken in with food and is not made by the body's metabolism. Use the definition of excretion to explain why removing extra salt still counts as excretion. **[2]**

**10.** Yeast is a single-celled fungus. When yeast is placed in a warm sugar solution, bubbles of carbon dioxide appear and the number of yeast cells increases. Identify **three** characteristics of living organisms shown, giving the evidence for each. **[3]**

**11.** Describe nutrition as a characteristic of living organisms, and explain how it is linked to respiration and to growth. **[3]**

**12.** A student says: "A crystal of copper sulfate hanging in a solution gets bigger every day, so it is alive." Explain whether the student is correct. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A is breathing, C is nutrition and D is part of excretion. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Reproduction [1].

**(b)** Nutrition [1].

**(c)** Excretion [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Sensitivity [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: respiration is a set of chemical reactions, breathing is a physical movement of air [1]; respiration happens in every cell, breathing involves the lungs or breathing system [1]; respiration releases energy, breathing uses energy. **[2]**

**5.** The increase in mass is only water [1], and it is temporary, so there is no **permanent** increase in size and **dry mass** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Dry mass falls from 4.0 g to 3.4 g by day 4 [1], then rises to 4.6 g by day 8 [1].

**(b)** Stored food in the seeds is broken down in respiration to release energy [1], and the seedlings cannot yet photosynthesise to replace it [1].

**(c)** Leaves have grown and the seedlings photosynthesise, making new materials [1] faster than they are used in respiration, so new cells are made and dry mass increases [1].

**(d)** Water content changes with conditions, so only dry mass shows whether new material has been made [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* In (a), quote the figures with units at the turning point (day 4). "It goes down then up" earns at most one mark.

**7. (a)** Movement [1]; sensitivity (it detects walls and responds) [1]. Nutrition (taking in energy from the mains) is also accepted.

**(b)** Any two developed, for example:

Growth: the robot does not show a permanent increase in size and dry mass [1], because it does not make new material [1].

Reproduction: it cannot carry out processes that make more of the same kind [1]; new robots are built in a factory [1].

Respiration is also accepted as an alternative: the robot has no cells, so no chemical reactions in cells break down nutrient molecules to release energy. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Any one: leaves turn towards light; flowers open and close; roots grow downwards [1].

**(b)** Oxygen made in photosynthesis is removed through the leaves [1]; carbon dioxide made in respiration is also removed through the leaves [1].

**(c)** The plant takes in carbon dioxide, water and mineral ions [1] and uses them to make its own food by photosynthesis [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Excretion includes the removal of **substances in excess of requirements**, not only waste made by metabolism [1]. Extra salt is more than the body needs, so removing it in sweat is excretion [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any three, each with evidence: respiration, because carbon dioxide is released [1]; reproduction, because the number of cells increases [1]; nutrition, because the yeast takes in sugar [1]; excretion, because carbon dioxide, a waste product of respiration, is removed. **[3]**

**11.** Nutrition is the taking in of materials for energy, growth and development [1]. Some of the materials, such as glucose, are broken down in respiration to release energy [1]. Others provide the raw materials, such as amino acids, to make new cells, so the organism grows [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The student is not correct [1]. The crystal does increase in size, but this is not growth by an organism: copper sulfate is added from the solution, not made by the crystal [1]. A crystal does not show the other characteristics, for example it has no cells and so does not respire [1], and it cannot reproduce or detect and respond to changes. Living organisms show all seven characteristics [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Explain whether" needs a clear decision plus reasons. Naming one missing characteristic and using the definition is the minimum for a good answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "respiration produces energy" instead of "releases energy".
- Leaving "permanent" or "dry mass" out of growth.
- Treating breathing and respiration as the same process.
- Describing the trend in a table without quoting figures and units.
- Concluding "not alive" without naming a characteristic that is missing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are there Extended questions on characteristics of living organisms?**
No. Sub-topic 1.1 is Core only in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610, though Extended papers can still ask about it.

**How do I answer an "is it alive" question?**
Name the characteristics the object seems to show, then name at least one it lacks using the definition, and conclude that living things show all seven.
