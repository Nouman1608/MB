These questions cover sub-topic 4(b) Crude oil of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.7 to 4.18, none of which has a C reference. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, S = 32. Revise with the [crude oil study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-crude-oil/) and the [organic chemistry revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a fuel. **[1]**

**2.** Name the fraction of crude oil used to surface roads. **[1]**

**3.** State the trend in viscosity from refinery gases to bitumen. **[1]**

**4.** Name the products of the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon. **[2]**

**5.** Name the catalyst used in catalytic cracking. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Crude oil is separated into fractions by fractional distillation.

**(a)** State why crude oil can be separated by distillation. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the fractions are separated in the fractionating column. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why the fraction collected at the bottom has a higher boiling point than the fraction collected at the top. **[2]**

**7.** Propane, C₃H₈, is used as a fuel in bottled gas.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide made when 22 g of propane (Mr = 44) burns completely. **[3]**
**(c)** A poorly adjusted heater burns propane with limited oxygen. Name the poisonous gas that may form and explain why it is dangerous. **[3]**

**8.** A car engine burns petrol in air.

**(a)** Explain how oxides of nitrogen are formed in the engine. **[2]**
**(b)** State two harmful effects of acid rain. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how sulfur dioxide gets into the exhaust gases. **[2]**

**9.** A tanker carries 500 kg of fuel oil that contains 0.50% sulfur by mass. Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide formed if all the sulfur burns. Ar (S) = 32; Mr (SO₂) = 64. **[3]**

**10.** Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, can be cracked.

**(a)** State the conditions for catalytic cracking. **[2]**
**(b)** Complete the equation: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + ......... **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the alkene product is useful. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why oil companies crack long-chain hydrocarbons, using the idea of supply and demand. **[3]**

**12.** The compound C₂₀H₄₂ (Mr = 282) is cracked: C₂₀H₄₂ → C₁₀H₂₂ + 2C₅H₁₀. Calculate the mass of C₅H₁₀ (Mr = 70) made from 14.1 g of C₂₀H₄₂. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A substance that releases heat energy when it is burned [1].

**2.** Bitumen [1].

**3.** Viscosity increases (the fractions become thicker) [1].

**4.** Carbon dioxide [1] and water [1].

**5.** Silica or alumina [1].

**6. (a)** Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons with different boiling points [1].
**(b)** Crude oil is heated until it vaporises and enters the column [1]. The column is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top [1]. Vapours rise and condense when they reach a level where the temperature is below their boiling point [1]. Fractions with long chains condense near the bottom and those with short chains near the top, and the liquids are drawn off at different levels [1].
**(c)** Molecules in the bottom fraction are longer [1], so the intermolecular forces are stronger and more energy is needed to separate them [1].

**7. (a)** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O [2] (1 mark for the correct formulae and 1 mark for the balancing).
**(b)** Moles of propane = 22 / 44 = 0.50 mol [1]. Moles of CO₂ = 3 x 0.50 = 1.5 mol [1]. Mass = 1.5 x 44 = 66 g [1].
**(c)** Carbon monoxide [1]. It stops the blood carrying oxygen round the body [1], so the brain and other organs are starved of oxygen, causing unconsciousness or death [1].

**8. (a)** The temperature in the engine is high enough [1] for nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react and form oxides of nitrogen [1].
**(b)** Any two from: harms fish and other life in lakes; damages trees; erodes limestone and marble buildings; speeds up corrosion of metals [2].
**(c)** Fuels contain sulfur compounds as impurities [1]. When the fuel burns, the sulfur is oxidised to sulfur dioxide [1].

**9.** Mass of sulfur = 0.50% x 500 = 2.5 kg [1]. S : SO₂ is 1 : 1 by moles, so the mass is multiplied by 64 / 32 [1]. Mass of SO₂ = 2.5 x 2 = 5.0 kg [1].

**10. (a)** A catalyst of silica or alumina [1]; a temperature of 600 to 700 °C [1].
**(b)** 2C₂H₄ [1].
**(c)** Alkenes are used to make plastics (polymers) or as raw materials for other chemicals [1].

**11.** There is more demand for short-chain hydrocarbons such as gasoline than the amount in crude oil [1]. There is a surplus of long-chain hydrocarbons that have fewer uses [1]. Cracking converts the surplus long chains into shorter, more useful alkanes and alkenes, matching supply to demand [1].

**12.** Moles of C₂₀H₄₂ = 14.1 / 282 = 0.050 mol [1]. Moles of C₅H₁₀ = 2 x 0.050 = 0.10 mol [1]. Mass = 0.10 x 70 = 7.0 g [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing out water as a product of complete combustion.
- Writing that crude oil is a compound or that fractions are pure substances.
- Saying that nitrogen in oxides of nitrogen comes from the fuel.
- Forgetting the 1 : 2 ratio between the alkane and the alkene in cracking equations.
- Getting the direction of the trend in boiling point the wrong way round.
- Explaining carbon monoxide's harm without mentioning oxygen transport.
