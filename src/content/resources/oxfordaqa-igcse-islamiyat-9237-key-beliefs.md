**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Key beliefs: "the meaning and importance of the key beliefs for Muslims, including Tawhid, Malaikah, Kutub, Risalah, Yaum e Akhirah, Al-Qadr and all good and bad is from Allah".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 2:285-286. The [Islam: Beliefs and Teachings study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-islamiyat-beliefs-and-teachings/) names the beliefs. This page explains what each belief means, how the beliefs fit together, why Muslims treat them as essential, and how to write about them in the exam.

## What is a "key belief"?

In Islam, belief (*iman*) means more than agreeing in the mind. It means accepting something in the heart, saying it with the tongue and showing it in action. The key beliefs are the things a Muslim must accept to be a believer. Sunni Muslims usually list six, and the specification follows that list. The Qur'an gives parts of it in several places:

- Qur'an 2:285 lists belief in Allah, the angels, the books and the messengers.
- Qur'an 4:136 adds the Last Day: "whoever disbelieves in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, and the Last Day has certainly gone far astray."
- Qur'an 54:49 teaches the decree: "Indeed, all things We created with predestination."
- The Hadith of Jibril in Sahih Muslim puts the six together: faith is "to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in the divine decree, its good and its bad."

The version of the Hadith of Jibril printed as a prescribed passage for the Five Pillars (Sahih al-Bukhari 50) lists a shorter set. Different reports of the same event contain different wording, and this is why it is safest to quote the six from the Muslim report or from the verses above.

## The six key beliefs in turn

### 1. Tawhid: the oneness of Allah

Allah is one, without partner, child or equal. The other five beliefs depend on this one, because angels, books, messengers, the Last Day and the decree are all about the relationship of creation to the One Creator. (The full detail is in the [Tawhid study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-oneness-of-god-tawhid/).)

**Importance:** it is the heart of the Shahadah and gives equality, sincerity and purpose.

### 2. Malaikah: belief in angels

Angels are beings created from light who obey Allah completely and cannot disobey. They carry out tasks such as bringing revelation, recording deeds and taking souls. Belief in them reminds Muslims that the unseen world is real and that their actions are noticed.

**Importance:** it strengthens accountability and respect for revelation, because the Qur'an came by the angel Jibril (AS).

### 3. Kutub: belief in the books

Muslims believe Allah sent guidance in books: the Suhuf of Ibraheem (AS), the Tawrat given to Musa (AS), the Zabur given to Dawud (AS), the Injil given to Isa (AS), and finally the Qur'an given to Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh). Muslims believe the Qur'an is the last and fully preserved word of Allah (15:9) and that it confirms and corrects earlier books.

**Importance:** it gives Muslims a source of law and guidance and links them to earlier believers.

### 4. Risalah: belief in the messengers

Allah sent prophets and messengers to every community to teach the same message of Tawhid and good conduct. Muslims respect them all and "make no distinction between any of His messengers" (2:285). Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) is believed to be the final prophet, the Seal of the Prophets (33:40).

**Importance:** the messengers show believers how to live; the Prophet's Sunnah is the model.

### 5. Yaum e Akhirah: belief in the Last Day

This is belief that this life will end, that all people will be raised and judged, and that the final home is Paradise or Hell. It is explained fully in the [life after death guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-belief-in-life-after-death/).

**Importance:** it makes people accountable and gives hope that injustice will be put right.

### 6. Al-Qadr: belief in the decree

Allah knows and has written all things, and nothing happens outside His knowledge and will. Humans still have choice and responsibility. This is covered in the [predestination guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-belief-in-predestination-al-qadr/).

**Importance:** it brings patience in hardship, gratitude in ease and freedom from despair.

## "All good and bad is from Allah"

The specification adds a statement that needs careful handling: all good and bad is from Allah. It is part of belief in the decree and means that nothing, pleasant or painful, happens without Allah's knowledge and permission. The Qur'an says, "No disaster strikes except by permission of Allah" (64:11).

This does **not** mean that Allah likes or commands evil. Muslims draw three points from the teaching.

1. **Allah is the Creator of everything**, including the circumstances in which people choose. Nothing is outside His power.
2. **Humans are responsible for moral choices.** The Qur'an says that good comes from Allah's favour and that harm which people do comes through their own wrongdoing (4:79). A thief cannot excuse theft by saying "it was written".
3. **Hardship has meaning.** The Qur'an teaches that Allah tests people with fear, hunger and loss (2:155), so illness or poverty is a test of patience and not a sign that Allah has abandoned someone.

**Example:** a family loses its home in a flood. A Muslim believes the flood happened within Allah's decree, so they respond with patience and trust (*sabr* and *tawakkul*). They also help each other, because helping is a duty Allah has set, and the people who caused a harm by neglect remain answerable for it.

## How the beliefs connect

| Belief | Answers the question | Effect on a Muslim |
|---|---|---|
| Tawhid | Who is God? | Worships and trusts Allah alone |
| Malaikah | Who is watching and serving? | Behaves well even in private |
| Kutub | Where is the guidance? | Studies the Qur'an |
| Risalah | Who shows how to live? | Follows the Prophet's example |
| Yaum e Akhirah | Where is life heading? | Lives with accountability |
| Al-Qadr | Who controls events? | Patient and hopeful |

A useful way to remember them is that they form a chain: *Allah* sends *angels* with *books* to *messengers*, who warn of the *Last Day*, and everything happens by Allah's *decree*.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 2:285-286

The passage works well because it includes the key beliefs and then shows what belief does to a person.

- **Belief:** "All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers." Four beliefs appear in a single sentence.
- **Unity of the messengers:** "We make no distinction between any of His messengers."
- **Obedience:** "We hear and we obey."
- **Humility and hope:** "[We seek] Your forgiveness, our Lord, and to You is the [final] destination" (a reference to the Last Day).
- **Fairness of the decree:** "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford."
- **Responsibility:** "All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss."
- **Prayer:** the verses end with a prayer asking for forgiveness, mercy and help, showing that believers rely on Allah.

## Different Muslim views

Sunni Muslims count six beliefs. Twelver Shi'a Muslims commonly teach five "roots of religion" (*usul ad-din*): Tawhid, justice ('Adl), prophethood (Nubuwwah), leadership of the Imams (Imamah) and the Return (Ma'ad). Both traditions share belief in one God, the prophets and the Last Day. Mention the difference only if a question asks about differences between Muslims, and keep it brief.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why belief in the Last Day is important for Muslims. **[4]**

> It is important because it makes Muslims accountable [1]. If every action will be judged, a Muslim tries to be honest and kind even when nobody is watching [1]. It is also important because it gives hope [1]. People who suffer injustice in this life believe that Allah will put things right on the Day of Judgement, which helps them to stay patient [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which the key beliefs influence the life of a Muslim. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> Belief in Tawhid means that a Muslim worships Allah alone, so they pray five times a day and ask only Allah for help [1 and 1]. Belief in Al-Qadr means they accept difficulties with patience, knowing that nothing happens without Allah's knowledge, and they do not despair [1 and 1]. The passage says that "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford", which teaches that every test is within a person's ability and encourages them to keep trying [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Giving five beliefs and missing "all good and bad is from Allah".** It is in the specification wording.
- **Using the Arabic names without meaning.** Write "Malaikah (angels)" the first time.
- **Saying evil is approved by Allah.** Say it is within His decree and permission, while humans are responsible.
- **Treating the beliefs as separate lists.** Show how they connect.
- **Skipping the passage.** The 12-mark question needs direct use of Qur'an 2:285-286.
