This guide covers **4.2.4.2 Uses of nanoparticles** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching
from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). It is part of topic 4.2.4, Bulk and surface properties of matter
including nanoparticles, assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier. No statement in it is marked
(HT only), and there is no required practical. The subsection is mainly about **evaluation**: reading
information about a use of nanoparticles and judging it.

For quick recall use the [uses of nanoparticles revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles-revision-notes/),
and test yourself with the [uses of nanoparticles practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles-practice/).
The size and surface area ideas are in the
[sizes of particles study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-sizes-of-particles-and-their-properties/).

## What the specification asks for

| Statement or skill | What to be ready for |
|---|---|
| Nanoparticles have many applications in medicine, in electronics, in cosmetics and sun creams, as deodorants, and as catalysts | Naming areas of use |
| New applications for nanoparticulate materials are an important area of research | Recognising that the subject is developing |
| Consider the advantages and disadvantages of the applications | Weighing up both sides |
| You do not need to know specific examples or properties other than those specified | Using information given in the question |
| Given appropriate information, evaluate the use of nanoparticles for a specified purpose | Writing an evaluation |
| Explain that there are possible risks associated with the use of nanoparticles | Stating risks |

## Where nanoparticles are used

| Area | Why nanoparticles are attractive |
|---|---|
| Medicine | Very small, so they may be able to enter the body's cells; a small amount may be effective |
| Electronics | Very small components allow smaller, faster devices |
| Cosmetics and sun creams | Very small particles can spread thinly and evenly on the skin |
| Deodorants | Small amounts can be effective because of the high surface area |
| Catalysts | High surface area to volume ratio, so a small mass gives a large effect |

The links come from two properties covered in the previous subsection: the **size** of nanoparticles and their
**high surface area to volume ratio**. You are not expected to learn specific examples, so questions will provide
the context.

## Advantages and disadvantages

Every use has benefits and possible drawbacks.

| | Possible advantages | Possible disadvantages or risks |
|---|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Smaller quantities needed because of the high surface area | New properties may behave in unexpected ways |
| Cost | Less material may be needed | Producing nanoparticles can be expensive |
| Health | New medical treatments may become possible | Very small particles might enter the body through the skin or lungs, and the long-term effects may be unknown |
| Environment | Less raw material used | Nanoparticles may be released into water or soil, and their effects are not fully understood |
| Knowledge | New uses are being researched all the time | Risks may not yet be fully tested |

The specification wants you to be able to **explain that there are possible risks**. The word "possible" is
important: the risks are not all proven, and some are not yet known. Questions may give information about a
study, the cost or the benefit, and ask you to decide.

## How to write an evaluation

When a question says **evaluate**, you should:

1. state a **benefit** (or several) and link it to a property of nanoparticles;
2. state a **risk or drawback**;
3. use any **data or information** in the question;
4. reach a **conclusion**: say whether the use is a good idea, or what further information would help.

A conclusion without reasons does not get the marks. A list of points without a conclusion often loses the final
mark.

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: sun cream

*A sun cream contains nanoparticles of a substance that blocks ultraviolet light. The nanoparticles spread
evenly, so the cream is clear on the skin. Some people are concerned that the nanoparticles might pass into the
body. Evaluate the use of nanoparticles in sun cream.*

```
Benefit:   they spread thinly and evenly, so the cream is clear (cosmetic advantage);
           their high surface area to volume ratio means less material is needed.
Risk:      very small particles might pass through the skin into the body; the long-term effects
           may be unknown.
Conclusion: they have clear advantages for the user, but more testing is needed to show that
           they are safe over a long time before concluding that they are suitable.
```

### Worked example 2: a data question

*A study compares two samples of a catalyst. Sample A is in pieces of side 1 cm. Sample B is made of
nanoparticles. Both have a mass of 5 g. The reaction with sample B is much faster. Explain.*

```
Nanoparticles have a much larger surface area to volume ratio.
For the same mass, sample B has a much larger surface area in contact with the reactants.
More collisions can occur at the surface, so the reaction is faster.
```

### Worked example 3: cost and benefit

*A company says that using nanoparticles in a catalyst reduces the mass of catalyst needed from 50 kg to 5 kg per
batch. The nanoparticle catalyst costs £400 per kg and the normal catalyst costs £60 per kg. Calculate the cost
of each and comment.*

```
Normal catalyst: 50 kg x £60 = £3000
Nanoparticle:     5 kg x £400 = £2000
Difference: £3000 - £2000 = £1000 less with nanoparticles
```

On cost alone the nanoparticle catalyst is cheaper per batch, because much less is needed. The company would also
need to consider the cost of making nanoparticles reliably, and whether any nanoparticles escape into the
environment.

### Worked example 4: stating a risk

*Give one possible risk of using nanoparticles in cosmetics.*

Nanoparticles are very small, so they might pass through the skin or be breathed in, and their long-term effects
on health are not fully known.

### Worked example 5: new areas of research

*Explain why scientists continue to research the use of nanoparticles.*

New applications for nanoparticulate materials are an important area of research. Nanoparticles have different
properties from bulk materials, so there may be uses that are not yet known. Research is also needed to find out
whether they are safe and how they affect the environment.

## Common mistakes

- Writing only advantages, or only risks, in an "evaluate" question.
- Saying that nanoparticles "are dangerous". The specification says there are **possible** risks.
- Not linking a benefit to a property such as high surface area to volume ratio.
- Writing "nano" as if it were a material. It describes a size.
- Making up specific facts that are not in the question. If the question gives information, use that.
- Ending without a conclusion.

## Exam technique

- **Evaluate:** advantage + risk + use of the data + conclusion.
- **Explain a risk:** say what could happen and why that is uncertain (size, entering the body, unknown
  long-term effects).
- **Explain an effect:** use the surface area to volume ratio.
- **Calculate:** show each step in cost or ratio calculations.
