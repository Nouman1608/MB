## Section A

**1.** State where a prepayment and where an accrual are shown in the statement of financial position. **[2]**

**2.** At the start of the year, insurance of 380 had been paid in advance. During the year 1,520 was paid. At the year end, 410 had been paid in advance. Calculate the insurance expense for the year. **[2]**

**3.** A landlord received rent of 6,000 during the year. Of this, 500 related to the previous year and 750 related to the following year. Calculate the rent income for the year. **[3]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by a provision and state **one** way in which it differs from an accrual. **[3]**

**5.** A machine cost 36,000. Calculate the depreciation charge for the year using:

**(a)** the straight-line method at 15% on cost **[1]**

**(b)** the reducing balance method at 30%, when the provision for depreciation brought forward is 10,800. State also the net book value at the year end. **[3]**

**6.** At the year end trade receivables are 24,700. A debt of 700 is to be written off. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is 800 and is to be 5% of the remaining trade receivables. Calculate the irrecoverable debts expense for the year and the net trade receivables to be shown in the statement of financial position. **[5]**

**7.** Trade receivables after writing off irrecoverable debts are 20,000. The existing allowance is 650 and is to be reduced to 2% of receivables. Calculate the change in the allowance and state how it is treated in the statement of profit or loss. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Mirza Traders' profit before year-end adjustments was 36,400. Calculate the adjusted profit for the year after these items:

- wages of 650 are owing
- rent of 400 was paid in advance
- depreciation of 3,200 is to be charged
- the allowance for irrecoverable debts is to be increased by 180
- interest received of 120 is accrued

Show each step. **[6]**

**9.** Explain **two** reasons why a business creates provisions. **[4]**

**10.** The following information relates to Hartley Stores at 31 December 2026.

Trade receivables before adjustment 14,600, of which 600 is to be written off. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is 500 and is to be 5% of the remaining receivables. Rates paid during the year were 4,800, of which 1,200 relates to the following year. Telephone paid was 2,150 and 340 is owing. Rent received was 3,600, of which 900 relates to the following year.

**(a)** Calculate the expense or income for the year for rates, telephone, rent received and irrecoverable debts. **[4]**

**(b)** Prepare the current assets and current liabilities section of the statement of financial position, as far as the information allows. **[6]**

**11.** In 2026 a customer whose debt of 300 was written off in 2025 pays in full. Explain how this is recorded and its effect on profit. **[3]**

**12.** A trader says: "The allowance for irrecoverable debts is only an estimate, so it should be ignored." Evaluate this view. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A prepayment is shown as a current asset (prepayments) [1]; an accrual is shown as a current liability (accruals) [1].

**2.** 1,520 + 380 [1] − 410 = **1,490** [1].

**3.** Cash received 6,000 [1]; less 500 belonging to the previous year and less 750 belonging to the next year [1]; 6,000 − 500 − 750 = **4,750** [1].

**4.** A provision is an amount set aside out of profit for a likely cost or loss from a past event [1] whose amount or timing is uncertain [1]. Difference from an accrual: an accrual is for an expense that has been incurred and where the amount is known or can be measured fairly exactly [1].

**5. (a)** 36,000 × 15% = **5,400** [1]

**(b)** Net book value before the charge 36,000 − 10,800 = 25,200 [1]; charge 25,200 × 30% = **7,560** [1]; new provision 10,800 + 7,560 = 18,360 so net book value 36,000 − 18,360 = **17,640** [1].

**6.** Remaining receivables 24,700 − 700 = 24,000 [1]; new allowance 24,000 × 5% = 1,200 [1]; increase 1,200 − 800 = 400 [1]; expense 700 + 400 = **1,100** [1]; net receivables 24,000 − 1,200 = **22,800** [1].

**7.** New allowance 20,000 × 2% = 400 [1]; change 650 − 400 = a **decrease of 250** [1]; it is credited to the statement of profit or loss (reducing expenses) [1].

**8.**

| Step | Working | Profit |
|---|---|---:|
| Profit before adjustments | | 36,400 |
| Wages owing | 36,400 − 650 | 35,750 |
| Rent prepaid | 35,750 + 400 | 36,150 |
| Depreciation | 36,150 − 3,200 | 32,950 |
| Increase in allowance | 32,950 − 180 | 32,770 |
| Interest accrued | 32,770 + 120 | **32,890** |

Wages deducted [1]; rent prepaid added [1]; depreciation deducted [1]; allowance increase deducted [1]; interest accrued added [1]; final profit **32,890** [1].

**9.** Any two reasons, each with a development, 2 marks each:

- Prudence: likely losses are recognised when they are expected, not when they happen [1], so profit and assets are not overstated, for example an allowance for irrecoverable debts reduces receivables to the amount likely to be collected [1].
- Matching (accruals): the cost belongs to the period of the related income [1], for example depreciation spreads the cost of a machine over the years in which it produces revenue [1].
- Reliable information for users: assets are shown at a fairer amount [1], so owners and lenders are not misled about net assets [1].

**10. (a)** Rates 4,800 − 1,200 = **3,600** [1]; telephone 2,150 + 340 = **2,490** [1]; rent received 3,600 − 900 = **2,700** [1]; irrecoverable debts: remaining receivables 14,600 − 600 = 14,000, new allowance 14,000 × 5% = 700, increase 200, expense 600 + 200 = **800** [1].

**(b)** Hartley Stores: extract from the statement of financial position at 31 December 2026

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Current assets** | | |
| Trade receivables | 14,000 | |
| Less allowance for irrecoverable debts | (700) | 13,300 |
| Prepayments | | 1,200 |
| **Current liabilities** | | |
| Accruals | | 340 |
| Deferred income | | 900 |

Trade receivables 14,000 [1]; allowance 700 deducted [1]; net receivables 13,300 [1]; prepayments 1,200 as a current asset [1]; accrual 340 as a current liability [1]; deferred income 900 as a current liability [1].

**11.** Debit bank 300 [1] and credit irrecoverable debts recovered 300 [1]. Profit increases by 300 because the recovery is income of the year in which it is received [1].

**12.** For ignoring the allowance: it is an estimate, and the amounts may turn out wrong [1]; ignoring it would keep receivables at the full figure and make the business look wealthier than it is [1].
Against: the prudence concept requires losses that are likely to be recognised [1]; an estimate based on past experience is more reliable than assuming all debts will be collected [1]; the allowance matches the cost of bad debts to the sales that caused them [1].
Judgement: the view is not justified; an estimate should be made and reviewed each year, because ignoring it overstates profit and net assets [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding a prepayment to the expense instead of deducting it (Questions 2, 8 and 10).
- Charging the whole allowance rather than the change (Question 6).
- Calculating the allowance before writing off the debt.
- Treating a decrease in the allowance as an expense (Question 7).
- Forgetting the opening balances when working back from cash paid.
- Giving a one-sided evaluation with no judgement (Question 12).

## Approaching these questions

For every adjustment, ask whether the expense or income should rise or fall, then check that your arithmetic agrees. For allowances, always write the steps in the same order: write off, remaining receivables, new allowance, change, expense, net receivables. Record each balance in the statement of financial position as an asset or a liability.

**Is the allowance for irrecoverable debts a liability?**
No. It is deducted from trade receivables, so it reduces a current asset.
