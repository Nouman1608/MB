These questions cover section 3.8.3, Electric potential, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [electric potential study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-potential-oxfordaqa/) or the [electric potential revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-potential-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first.

Use 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻², e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg. Treat air as a vacuum.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the absolute electric potential at a point. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why no work is done when a charge is moved along an equipotential surface. **[2]**

**3.** Which of these quantities is a scalar?

A: electric field strength

B: electric potential

C: electric force

D: displacement of a charge

**[1]**

**4.** Calculate the potential 0.25 m from the centre of a small sphere carrying −30 nC. **[2]**

**5.** A charge of −3.0 nC is moved from a point where the potential is +200 V to a point where it is −100 V. Calculate the work done on the charge by the external agent moving it. **[2]**

**6.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a p.d. of 450 V. Calculate its kinetic energy and its final speed. **[3]**

### Section B

**7.** A point charge of +12 nC is isolated in space.

**(a)** Calculate the potential 0.20 m from the charge. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the radius of the 270 V equipotential surface. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why equipotential surfaces for equal steps of potential get further apart with distance from the charge. **[1]**

**8.** A charge of +4.0 nC is at x = 0 and a charge of −6.0 nC is at x = 0.50 m.

**(a)** Show that the potential at x = 0.20 m is zero. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the field strength at x = 0.20 m, and explain why it is not zero. **[3]**

**(c)** State the work needed to bring a small positive charge from infinity to x = 0.20 m. **[1]**

**9.** A proton with kinetic energy 1.2 MeV travels directly towards a stationary nucleus of charge +26e.

**(a)** Calculate the distance of closest approach. **[3]**

**(b)** State how the distance of closest approach changes if the proton's kinetic energy is doubled. **[1]**

**10.** The potential near a charged sphere is measured at several distances from its centre.

| r / m | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V / V | 600 | 450 | 360 | 300 |

**(a)** Show that the data are consistent with V ∝ 1/r. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge on the sphere. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean field strength between 0.20 m and 0.25 m. **[2]**

**11.** The field strength near a different charged sphere is given below.

| r / m | 0.10 | 0.15 | 0.20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| E / V m⁻¹ | 9000 | 4000 | 2250 |

**(a)** Use two trapezia to estimate the potential difference between 0.10 m and 0.20 m. **[2]**

**(b)** The true value is 450 V. Explain why the estimate is too large. **[2]**

**12.** Describe how the potential and the field strength vary with distance r from an isolated negative point charge, and how the two graphs differ in shape. **[3]**

**13.** Two parallel plates 0.040 m apart are held at 0 V and +200 V. Equipotential surfaces are drawn every 50 V.

**(a)** State the spacing of the equipotential surfaces. **[1]**

**(b)** An electron moves from the 50 V surface to the 150 V surface. Calculate the change in its potential energy, and state whether it gains or loses kinetic energy if no other force acts. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The work done per unit positive charge [1] in bringing a small test charge from infinity to that point [1]. **[2]**

**2.** All points on the surface are at the same potential, so ΔV = 0 [1]; the work done is ΔW = QΔV = 0 (the field is perpendicular to the surface) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** V = −8.99 × 10⁹ × 30 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.25 [1] = **−1.1 × 10³ V** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** ΔW = Q(V_final − V_initial) = −3.0 × 10⁻⁹ × (−100 − 200) [1] = **+9.0 × 10⁻⁷ J** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Ek = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 450 = **7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ J** [1]; v = √(2Ek/m) = √(2 × 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **1.3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.20 = **540 V** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** r = 107.9 / 270 = **0.40 m** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** V ∝ 1/r, so each equal fall in V needs a larger increase in r as the field weakens [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** V from +4.0 nC = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.20 = +180 V; V from −6.0 nC = −8.99 × 10⁹ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.30 = −180 V [1]; sum = 0 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** E from +4.0 nC = 899 N C⁻¹ and E from −6.0 nC = 599 N C⁻¹ [1]; both point in the +x direction (away from the positive, towards the negative) [1], so E = **1.5 × 10³ N C⁻¹** in the +x direction; fields are vectors and do not cancel just because the scalar potentials do [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** **Zero**, since V = 0 there and ΔW = QΔV [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** Ek = 1.2 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.92 × 10⁻¹³ J [1]; at closest approach Ek = e × 26e/(4πε₀r) [1]; r = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 26 × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / 1.92 × 10⁻¹³ = **3.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** r ∝ 1/Ek, so it **halves** [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** V × r = 90 V m at every distance (0.15 × 600, 0.20 × 450, 0.25 × 360, 0.30 × 300) [1]; a constant product means V ∝ 1/r [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Q = 4πε₀ × V r = 90 / 8.99 × 10⁹ [1] = **1.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** E = ΔV/Δr = (450 − 360) / 0.05 [1] = **1.8 × 10³ V m⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** 0.05 × (9000 + 4000)/2 + 0.05 × (4000 + 2250)/2 = 325 + 156 [1] = **4.8 × 10² V** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Between data points the E–r curve sags below the straight line joining them, because E falls steeply at first and then levels off [1]; the straight tops of the trapezia lie above the curve, so they include extra area [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The potential is negative and rises towards zero as r increases, varying as 1/r [1]. The field strength is directed towards the charge and its magnitude falls towards zero as 1/r² [1]. The field strength falls more steeply with distance than the potential [1]. **[3]**

**13. (a)** Four equal steps across 0.040 m: **0.010 m** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** ΔEp = QΔV = −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × (+100) = **−1.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ J** [1]; potential energy falls, so it **gains** kinetic energy [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using r² in the potential equation.
- Dropping a minus sign for a negative charge or a negative potential.
- Assuming that zero potential means zero field.
- Using the charge of the moving particle as the source charge in closest-approach problems.
- Getting the direction of energy change wrong for a negative charge moving to higher potential.
- Not saying whether a graph estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate, and why.
