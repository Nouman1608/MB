These questions cover section 3.2.2, **Graphs** (A8 to A16), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification, for teaching from September 2015 with exams from May/June 2017 (version 1.0). Questions and parts marked **(Higher)** use content from the specification's "Higher content only" column; everything else suits both tiers. Each question says whether it is non-calculator (Paper 1 style) or calculator (Papers 2 and 3 style).

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator) A(−3, 2), B(1, 2) and C(1, −4) are three corners of a rectangle ABCD.

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of D. **[1]**
**(b)** State which quadrant C is in. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A line has equation y = 4 − 3x.

**(a)** Complete the table of values for x = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the point where the line crosses the y-axis. **[1]**
**(c)** Show whether the point (5, −11) lies on the line. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Line L has equation 5x + 2y = 14.

**(a)** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of L. **[2]**
**(b)** Which of these lines is parallel to L? y = 2.5x + 1, 2y = −5x + 3, y = −5x + 7, 5y = 2x **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A line passes through (−4, 5) and (2, 2).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(b)** (Higher) Find the equation of the line perpendicular to it that passes through (1, 1). **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) A plumber's total charge, £C, for a job lasting h hours is shown by a straight-line graph through (1, 70) and (4, 145).

**(a)** Work out the gradient of the line. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line, then explain what the gradient and the intercept mean in this context. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) The curve y = x² − 4x − 12 is drawn on a grid.

**(a)** Find the roots of x² − 4x − 12 = 0 by factorising. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the y-intercept. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the symmetry of the curve to find the turning point. **[2]**
**(d)** State the line you would draw on the grid to solve x² − 4x − 12 = 9, and give the solutions. **[2]**

**7.** (non-calculator) (Higher) f(x) = x² + 8x + 11.

**(a)** Write f(x) in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the turning point of y = f(x), and state whether it is a maximum or a minimum, with a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the turning point of y = f(x) + 5. **[1]**
**(d)** Write down the turning point of y = f(x − 4). **[1]**
**(e)** Write down the turning point of y = −f(x). **[1]**

**8.** (non-calculator) Match each description to one of the equations y = x², y = 1/x, y = 2 − x, y = x³.

**(a)** A straight line with negative gradient crossing the y-axis at 2. **[1]**
**(b)** Two separate curves in the first and third quadrants that never meet the axes. **[1]**
**(c)** A curve through the origin, symmetrical about the y-axis, with a minimum at (0, 0). **[1]**
**(d)** A curve through the origin rising from bottom left to top right, flat at (0, 0). **[1]**

**9.** (calculator) (Higher)

**(a)** Complete a table of values for y = 2ˣ for x = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your graph of y = 2ˣ to estimate the solution of 2ˣ = 10. **[2]**
**(c)** For y = sin x with 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°, write down the maximum value of y and the value of x where it occurs. **[1]**
**(d)** Given sin 30° = 0.5, use the graph of y = sin x to solve sin x = 0.5 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator) Amira leaves home at 09:00 and cycles 6 km to a park, arriving at 09:20. She stays until 09:45, then cycles home, arriving at 10:15. Her journey is shown on a distance-time graph.

**(a)** Work out her speed on the way to the park, in km/h. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out her speed on the way home, in km/h. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the section of the graph from 09:20 to 09:45 and say what it shows. **[1]**

**11.** (calculator) (Higher) A car's velocity, v m/s, t seconds after it starts is shown by a smooth curve through these points.

| t (s) | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v (m/s) | 0 | 5.25 | 9 | 11.25 | 12 |

**(a)** Use four strips of equal width to estimate the distance travelled in the first 20 seconds. **[3]**
**(b)** The curve bends downwards between these points. Is your answer an overestimate or an underestimate? Give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** A tangent drawn to the curve at t = 10 passes through (0, 3) and (20, 15). Estimate the acceleration at t = 10. **[2]**

**12.** (non-calculator) (Higher) A circle has equation x² + y² = 45. P is the point (6, 3).

**(a)** Show that P lies on the circle. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the radius in the form a√5. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. **[4]**
**(d)** The tangent meets the x-axis at Q. Find the coordinates of Q. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** D has the x-coordinate of A and the y-coordinate of C: **D(−3, −4)** [1]
**(b)** x positive, y negative: **fourth quadrant** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Coordinates written the wrong way round, as (−4, −3), score zero even when the point is marked correctly on a diagram.

**2. (a)** y = 7, 4, 1, −2, −5 [1] for any three correct, all five correct [1]
**(b)** **(0, 4)** [1]
**(c)** 4 − 3(5) = −11, so **yes, it lies on the line** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) a bare "yes" earns nothing; the substitution must be shown with a conclusion.

**3. (a)** 2y = −5x + 14, so y = −2.5x + 7 [1]; **gradient −5/2, y-intercept 7** [1]
**(b)** 2y = −5x + 3 gives y = −2.5x + 1.5, so **2y = −5x + 3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Giving the gradient as 5 or −5 shows the equation was not rearranged and loses both marks in (a).

**4. (a)** m = (2 − 5) ÷ (2 − (−4)) = −3/6 = −1/2 [1]; 5 = (−1/2)(−4) + c, so c = 3 [1]; **y = −½x + 3** [1]
**(b)** Perpendicular gradient = 2 [1]; 1 = 2(1) + c gives c = −1, so **y = 2x − 1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A correct method for c with a wrong gradient from (a) usually earns follow-through credit, so always show the substitution.

**5. (a)** Gradient = (145 − 70) ÷ (4 − 1) [1] = **25** [1]
**(b)** 70 = 25(1) + c gives c = 45, so **C = 25h + 45** [1]. The gradient means **£25 for each hour**, and the intercept means a **fixed £45 charge** before any time is worked [1]
*Examiner insight:* The interpretation mark needs both meanings in context with units; "25 is the gradient" restates the maths and scores nothing.

**6. (a)** (x − 6)(x + 2) = 0 [1]; **x = 6 or x = −2** [1]
**(b)** **(0, −12)** [1]
**(c)** Line of symmetry x = (−2 + 6) ÷ 2 = 2 [1]; y = 4 − 8 − 12 = −16, so **(2, −16)** [1]
**(d)** Draw **y = 9** [1]; it meets the curve at **x = −3 and x = 7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), drawing y = 9 is the method mark; the solutions must match the readings at both intersections, not just one.

**7. (a)** (x + 4)² − 16 + 11 [1] = **(x + 4)² − 5** [1]
**(b)** **(−4, −5)** [1]; a **minimum**, because (x + 4)² ≥ 0 so y is never below −5 [1]
**(c)** **(−4, 0)** [1]
**(d)** **(0, −5)** [1]
**(e)** **(−4, 5)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) a turning point of (4, −5) shows the sign error in reading (x + p)²; follow-through is then allowed in (c) to (e) only if each transformation is applied correctly.

**8. (a)** **y = 2 − x** [1]
**(b)** **y = 1/x** [1]
**(c)** **y = x²** [1]
**(d)** **y = x³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each match is a separate mark, so one wrong pairing does not cost the others, but using one equation twice loses a mark.

**9. (a)** y = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 [1] for at least four correct, all six correct [1]
**(b)** Draw y = 10 to meet the curve [1]; **x ≈ 3.3** (accept 3.2 to 3.4) [1]
**(c)** Maximum **1** at **x = 90°** [1]
**(d)** x = 30° [1]; by symmetry about 90°, **x = 30° and x = 150°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer read from a graph is accepted within a range around the true value; a trial-and-improvement answer with no line drawn may lose the method mark.

**10. (a)** 6 km in 20 minutes [1]; 6 × 3 = **18 km/h** [1]
**(b)** 6 km in 30 minutes (09:45 to 10:15) [1]; 6 × 2 = **12 km/h** [1]
**(c)** A **horizontal line**: she is **stationary** at the park [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing 6 by 20 and giving 0.3 earns the method mark only when km/h is asked; the time must be converted to hours.

**11. (a)** Strip width 5 [1]; area = (5/2) × [0 + 2(5.25 + 9 + 11.25) + 12] [1] = 2.5 × 63 = **157.5 m** [1]
**(b)** **Underestimate**: the curve bends downwards, so the tops of the trapezia lie below the curve [1]
**(c)** Gradient = (15 − 3) ÷ (20 − 0) [1] = **0.6 m/s²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An estimate from drawn tangents and trapezia is credited within a tolerance, but units are expected; "157.5" alone may lose the final mark on a units question.

**12. (a)** 6² + 3² = 36 + 9 = 45, so **P is on the circle** [1]
**(b)** √45 = √9 × √5 = **3√5** [1]
**(c)** Gradient of OP = 3/6 = 1/2 [1]; tangent gradient = −2 [1]; 3 = −2(6) + c [1]; c = 15, so **y = −2x + 15** [1]
**(d)** 0 = −2x + 15, x = 7.5: **Q(7.5, 0)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the radius gradient 1/2 as the tangent gradient loses the second and final marks in (c), though the substitution mark can still be earned.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the gradient from 5x + 2y = 14 without making y the subject.
- Subtracting coordinates in different orders, so the gradient has the wrong sign.
- Keeping the same sign when finding a perpendicular gradient.
- Giving one root, or one intersection, when there are two.
- Writing (4, −5) instead of (−4, −5) as the turning point of (x + 4)² − 5.
- Moving y = f(x − 4) left instead of right.
- Leaving time in minutes when the speed is asked for in km/h.
- Leaving out the over- or underestimate reason, or the units, for areas and gradients.
- Using the radius gradient as the tangent gradient.
