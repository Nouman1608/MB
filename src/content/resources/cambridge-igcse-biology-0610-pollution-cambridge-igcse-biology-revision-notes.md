These notes condense sub-topic **20.3 Pollution** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 are Core. Outcome 4 (explaining eutrophication) is **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [pollution study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-pollution-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [pollution practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-pollution-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [human influences on ecosystems revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-human-influences-on-ecosystems-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 20.

## Core recall: sewage and fertiliser in water

| Pollutant | What happens | Effect on the ecosystem |
|---|---|---|
| Untreated sewage | organic matter decomposed by bacteria, which respire aerobically | dissolved oxygen falls; fish and other oxygen-needing animals die or leave |
| Untreated sewage | releases nitrate and phosphate ions | rapid growth of algae |
| Excess fertiliser | nitrate and other ions washed into water | rapid growth of algae and plants; surface algae block light; plants below die |
| Either | dead producers decomposed | more aerobic respiration by decomposers; oxygen falls; animals die |

**Reading river data:** oxygen is lowest just downstream of the outfall, then recovers as organic matter is used up and oxygen dissolves from the air and is produced by photosynthesis.

## Core recall: non-biodegradable plastics

**Non-biodegradable:** cannot be broken down by decomposers, so it persists.

- **Aquatic:** entanglement (drowning, cannot feed); swallowed as food, blocks the gut; breaks into tiny pieces that pass along food chains.
- **Terrestrial:** fills landfill; animals eat or are trapped by litter; habitats made less suitable.

## Core recall: methane and carbon dioxide

| Gas | Sources |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | combustion of fossil fuels; burning forests |
| Methane | livestock digestion (cattle); rice fields; decomposing waste in landfill |

**Enhanced greenhouse effect in five steps:**

1. Sun's radiation warms the Earth's surface.
2. Surface gives out thermal energy.
3. Carbon dioxide and methane absorb thermal energy, so less is lost to space.
4. More of these gases → more thermal energy absorbed (enhanced greenhouse effect).
5. Mean global temperature rises → **climate change**.

**Climate change effects:** melting ice, rising sea levels, changing rainfall, more extreme weather, species forced to move or decline.

## Extended recall: eutrophication in six steps

1. Increased availability of **nitrate and other ions**.
2. Increased **growth of producers** (algae).
3. Increased **decomposition** after death of producers.
4. Increased **aerobic respiration by decomposers**.
5. **Reduction in dissolved oxygen**.
6. **Death of organisms requiring dissolved oxygen** (fish).

**Memory hook:** "Ions, Insane growth, Die, Decompose and breathe, Drop in oxygen, Death."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pollution | Release of substances that harm organisms or the environment |
| Untreated sewage | Human waste and waste water that has not been processed |
| Dissolved oxygen | Oxygen gas dissolved in water, needed by aquatic animals for respiration |
| Algal bloom | Rapid growth of algae, often covering the water surface |
| Non-biodegradable | Cannot be broken down by decomposers |
| Greenhouse gas | A gas, such as carbon dioxide or methane, that absorbs thermal energy |
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Extra absorption of thermal energy caused by increased greenhouse gases |
| Climate change | Long-term changes in weather patterns caused by a rise in mean global temperature |
| Eutrophication (Extended) | The sequence from extra ions in water to death of organisms needing oxygen |

## Exam traps

- **"Sewage is poisonous."** Describe oxygen loss from decomposers' respiration.
- **Algae use up oxygen.** Decomposers do, after the algae die.
- **Skipping step 3.** The producers must **die** before decomposition increases.
- **Plastics in one ecosystem only.** Give both aquatic and terrestrial effects when asked.
- **Ozone.** The ozone layer is not part of this sub-topic.
- **"Global warming" alone.** Use **enhanced greenhouse effect** and **climate change**.
- **Methane sources.** Cattle, rice fields, landfill; not car exhausts.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** pollutant effects: change + consequence for organisms.
- **Explain** eutrophication: all six steps in order, joined by "so".
- **Interpret** river data: quote values, link them to distance from the source.

## Self-test

1. Why does untreated sewage reduce the dissolved oxygen in a river?
2. How does excess fertiliser reach a river?
3. Why do water plants below an algal bloom die?
4. What does non-biodegradable mean?
5. Give two effects of plastics on aquatic animals.
6. Give one effect of plastics in terrestrial ecosystems.
7. State two sources of carbon dioxide pollution.
8. State three sources of methane.
9. Explain how increased carbon dioxide raises the mean global temperature.
10. Give two effects of climate change.
11. (Extended) List the six steps of eutrophication.
12. (Extended) Which organisms' respiration causes the fall in dissolved oxygen during eutrophication?

### Answers

1. Bacteria decompose the organic matter and use dissolved oxygen in aerobic respiration.
2. Rain washes (leaches) nitrate and other ions from the soil into the water.
3. The algae block light, so the plants cannot photosynthesise.
4. It cannot be broken down by decomposers.
5. Any two: entanglement leading to drowning or starvation; swallowing plastic, blocking the gut; small pieces entering food chains.
6. Any one: fills landfill; animals eat or are trapped by litter; habitats made less suitable.
7. Combustion of fossil fuels; burning of forests.
8. Cattle and other livestock; rice fields; decomposition of waste in landfill.
9. Carbon dioxide absorbs thermal energy given out by the Earth, so less is lost to space; more carbon dioxide means more energy absorbed, so the temperature rises.
10. Any two: melting ice, rising sea levels, changing rainfall, more extreme weather, species moving or declining.
11. More nitrate and ions; more producer growth; more decomposition after producers die; more aerobic respiration by decomposers; less dissolved oxygen; death of organisms that need oxygen.
12. The decomposers (bacteria).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the decomposers in sewage and fertiliser answers.
- Writing eutrophication steps out of order or missing one.
- Describing plastic effects only in the sea.
- Mixing up the sources of methane and carbon dioxide.
- Linking the greenhouse effect to the ozone layer.
