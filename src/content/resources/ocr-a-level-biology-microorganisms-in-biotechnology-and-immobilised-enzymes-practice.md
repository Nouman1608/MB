## Questions

**1.** State three reasons why microorganisms are used in biotechnological processes. **[3]**

**2.** Describe four precautions that make up good aseptic technique when inoculating an agar plate, and give the reason for each. **[4]**

**3.** A bacterial culture in a closed flask was sampled each hour. The log₁₀ of the viable count per cm³ was: 0 h, 3.0; 1 h, 3.0; 2 h, 3.3; 3 h, 3.9; 4 h, 4.5; 5 h, 5.1; 6 h, 5.7; 7 h, 6.0; 8 h, 6.0; 9 h, 5.7.
**(a)** Name the phase from 0 to 1 h and describe what the cells are doing. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gradient from 2 h to 6 h, with units. **[2]**
**(c)** Use log₁₀ 2 = 0.301 to find the generation time in minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one cause of the plateau from 7 to 8 h. **[1]**

**4.** A culture starts with 125 cells and has a generation time of 40 minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 4 hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many hours it takes to reach 1.0 × 10⁶ cells. **[2]**

**5.** A culture was diluted tenfold four times. A volume of 0.2 cm³ of the final dilution was spread on three plates, giving 27, 31 and 29 colonies. Calculate the number of viable cells per cm³ in the original culture. **[4]**

**6.**
**(a)** Compare batch and continuous fermentation. **[4]**
**(b)** Penicillin is usually made by batch fermentation. Suggest why. **[2]**

**7.** Give two advantages and two disadvantages of mycoprotein as a food for humans. **[4]**

**8.** A student placed discs soaked in 0, 25, 50 and 100 µg of an antibiotic on a lawn of bacteria. The clear zone diameters, which include the 6 mm disc, were 6, 10, 16 and 22 mm.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in zone area between the 25 µg and 50 µg discs. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what the clear zone shows. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the 0 µg disc was included. **[2]**

**9.** Describe two methods of immobilising an enzyme. **[4]**

**10.** Lactase is entrapped in alginate beads and used to remove lactose from milk.
**(a)** Beads of diameter 3 mm and 5 mm are compared. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each, using 3/r. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which beads give the faster reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** A column produced 18.0 µmol of glucose in a 6 minute run when new and 14.4 µmol in the fifth run. Calculate the percentage activity retained and suggest one cause of the loss. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate the use of an immobilised glucose isomerase column to make fructose from glucose rather than adding free enzyme to a tank. **[6]**

**12.** Plan an investigation into the effect of temperature on the growth of yeast in broth. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Short life cycle, so fast growth and quick production [1]. Simple, cheap growth requirements, including waste substrates [1]. Any one of: grown in any climate in a small space; fewer ethical concerns than animals; can be genetically modified to make useful products [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when each reason is stated as a feature of the organism; the word "cheap" alone is vague.

**2.** Flame the loop until red hot, to kill microorganisms already on it [1]. Keep the dish lid on or lifted only slightly, to stop airborne contaminants entering [1]. Flame the neck of bottles, to sterilise it and create an updraught away from the culture [1]. Tape the lid in two or three places and incubate inverted, so air can enter and condensation does not drip onto the agar [1]. Other valid pairs include disinfecting the bench and autoclaving used plates. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each precaution needs its reason. A list with no reasons leaves available marks unearned.

**3. (a)** **Lag phase** [1]. Cells are adjusting to the medium, making enzymes and taking up nutrients, with little division [1].
**(b)** (5.7 − 3.3) / (6 − 2) [1] = **0.6 log₁₀ cells per cm³ per hour** [1].
**(c)** Doubling adds 0.301 to the log, so time = 0.301 / 0.6 = 0.50 h [1] = **30 minutes** [1].
**(d)** Nutrient depletion, accumulation of toxic waste or a change in pH [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the units for a gradient from a log graph. Saying only "0.6" is incomplete.

**4. (a)** n = 240 / 40 = 6 [1]; N = 125 × 2⁶ = **8000 cells** [1].
**(b)** 2^n = 1.0 × 10⁶ / 125 = 8000, so n = 12.97 [1]; time = 12.97 × 40 = 519 minutes = **8.6 hours** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the time to the same unit as the generation time before dividing. Show the value of n so that the reader can follow each step.

**5.** Mean = (27 + 31 + 29) / 3 = 29 [1]. 29 / 0.2 = 145 per cm³ of the final dilution [1]. Multiply by the dilution factor 10⁴ [1]. **1.45 × 10⁶ cells per cm³** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both the plated volume and the dilution factor must be used. Omitting either gives a wrong power of ten.

**6. (a)** In batch, nutrients are added once at the start; in continuous they are added steadily [1]. Batch is harvested at the end; continuous culture is drawn off continuously [1]. Batch goes through all growth phases; continuous is held in exponential growth [1]. A contaminated batch is lost and the vessel is then cleaned; continuous runs are harder to keep sterile for long periods [1].
**(b)** Penicillin is a secondary metabolite made mainly as growth slows [1], so keeping the culture in exponential growth gives no gain [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparisons need both sides stated. Describing only batch does not earn the comparison mark.

**7.** Advantages, any two: high protein, low saturated fat and no cholesterol; suitable for vegetarians; cheap, fast production from waste carbohydrate [1][1]. Disadvantages, any two: some people dislike the texture or taste; allergy or digestive upset in some; RNA content must be reduced; costly sterile fermenters [1][1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Healthy" is too vague; name the feature, such as low saturated fat.

**8. (a)** Area at 25 µg = π × 5² = 78.5 mm² [1]; area at 50 µg = π × 8² = 201 mm² [1]; increase = (201 − 78.5) / 78.5 × 100 = **156%** [1].
**(b)** The antibiotic diffused from the disc [1] and prevented growth or killed bacteria in the zone [1].
**(c)** It is a control [1]; it shows the disc and solvent alone do not stop growth, so the zones are due to the antibiotic [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change is relative to the original value. Using areas rather than diameters is the expected approach.

**9.** Adsorption: the enzyme sticks to the surface of a support such as a resin by weak bonds [1]; it can wash off [1]. Entrapment: the enzyme is held in a gel such as alginate [1]; substrate and product diffuse in and out through the gel [1]. Covalent bonding or membrane separation are also valid. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A description of the method needs how the enzyme is held, not just the name.

**10. (a)** 3 / 1.5 = **2.0 mm⁻¹**; 3 / 2.5 = **1.2 mm⁻¹** [1][1].
**(b)** The 3 mm beads have the larger ratio [1], so substrate diffuses to more of the enzyme and the reaction is faster [1].
**(c)** 14.4 / 18.0 × 100 = **80%** [1]; some enzyme leaked from the beads, or beads broke [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs a reason from the numbers, here the ratio, and not only "smaller is better".

**11.** The column can be reused many times, which lowers enzyme cost [1]. The product is not contaminated with enzyme, so less purification is needed [1]. Continuous flow is possible [1]. The enzyme is often more stable to temperature and pH [1]. Against: higher set-up cost, and slower reaction if diffusion limits it [1]. Judgement, for example that the column is better for large-scale, repeated production [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation should reach a conclusion. A list of points with no judgement scores below the maximum.

**12.** Independent variable: at least five temperatures, for example 15 to 55 °C [1]. Same volume of sterile broth, same starting yeast concentration, same pH in every tube [1]. Aseptic technique with a sterile control [1]. Inoculate each tube and keep it in a water bath at its temperature [1]. Incubate for the same time, shaken at the same rate [1]. Measure turbidity with a colorimeter, using sterile broth to zero it, or take viable counts by serial dilution [1]. Repeat each temperature at least three times and calculate a mean [1]. Safety: use a non-pathogenic yeast and autoclave waste [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Plans earn most when each control is named and the measurement is specific. "Keep everything else the same" is too vague.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting time to the unit of the generation time before finding n.
- Leaving out the dilution factor or the plated volume in viable counts.
- Quoting a gradient from a log graph without units.
- Giving precautions in aseptic technique with no reason.
- Describing the stationary phase as the point where cells stop growing without naming a limiting factor.
- Comparing batch and continuous fermentation by describing only one of them.
- Comparing zones by diameter when area is needed.
- Calling a control "the same" without saying what it contains.
- Stating that immobilised enzymes always work faster, with no mention of diffusion.
