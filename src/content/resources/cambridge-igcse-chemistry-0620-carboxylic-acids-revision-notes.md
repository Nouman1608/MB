Condensed recall for **11.7 Carboxylic acids**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). The full explanation is in the [carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-carboxylic-acids/); exam-style questions are in the [carboxylic acids practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-carboxylic-acids-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcome 1 (reactions of ethanoic acid, names and formulae of salts) is Core. Outcomes 2 (making ethanoic acid by oxidation) and 3 (esters) are Supplement, marked *(Extended)*, as are naming acids other than ethanoic acid and naming esters (11.2).

## Core facts

- Functional group: **–COOH** (carboxyl). General formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH**.
- Ethanoic acid: **CH₃COOH**. Its salts are **ethanoates**, containing the ion **CH₃COO⁻**.
- Ethanoic acid reacts like other acids with metals, bases and carbonates.

## The three reactions

| Reacts with | Products | Observation | Example equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| metal | salt + **hydrogen** | fizzing; metal dissolves | 2CH₃COOH + Zn → (CH₃COO)₂Zn + H₂ |
| base (oxide or hydroxide) | salt + **water** | solid base dissolves | CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O |
| carbonate | salt + **water** + **carbon dioxide** | fizzing; gas turns limewater milky | 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ |

## Ethanoate formulae

| Metal ion charge | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1+ | CH₃COOM | CH₃COONa, CH₃COOK |
| 2+ | (CH₃COO)₂M | (CH₃COO)₂Mg, (CH₃COO)₂Ca, (CH₃COO)₂Zn, (CH₃COO)₂Cu |

2+ metals need **2CH₃COOH** in the equation. So does any carbonate of a 1+ metal (Na₂CO₃ has two Na⁺ to balance).

*(Extended)* Ethanoic acid is a **weak acid** (partially dissociated), so it reacts **more slowly** than a strong acid of equal concentration.

## Naming acids *(Extended)*

| Carbons | Acid | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | methanoic acid | HCOOH |
| 2 | ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH |
| 3 | propanoic acid | CH₃CH₂COOH |
| 4 | butanoic acid | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH |

The –COOH carbon is included in the count.

## Making ethanoic acid *(Extended)*

| Method | What happens | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) | oxidises ethanol on warming | **purple → colourless** |
| Bacterial oxidation | bacteria oxidise ethanol in wine exposed to air | gives **vinegar**; needs **oxygen** |

```
CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O
```

## Esters *(Extended)*

```
carboxylic acid + alcohol  --acid catalyst-->  ester + water
```

- Linkage: **–COO–**.
- Name = **alcohol part (-yl)** + **acid part (-oate)**.
- In the formula, the acid part is usually written first: CH₃COOCH₃ is **methyl ethanoate**.

| Formula | Acid part | Alcohol part | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCOOCH₃ | methanoic | methanol | methyl methanoate |
| CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ | ethanoic | ethanol | ethyl ethanoate |
| CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ | propanoic | methanol | methyl propanoate |
| CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ | ethanoic | butan-1-ol | butyl ethanoate |

To name from a formula: split at –COO–; the side with C=O is the acid.

## Displayed formulae

Ethanoic acid (Core) – show the C=O and the O–H:

```
    H   O
    |   ||
H — C — C — O — H
    |
    H
```

Ethyl ethanoate *(Extended)* – the ester linkage sits in the middle:

```
    H   O       H   H
    |   ||      |   |
H — C — C — O — C — C — H
    |           |   |
    H           H   H
```

## Ethanoic acid vs hydrochloric acid *(Extended)*

| Same concentration | Hydrochloric acid | Ethanoic acid |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | strong: fully dissociated | weak: partially dissociated |
| H⁺ concentration | higher | lower |
| pH | lower | higher (still below 7) |
| Rate with Mg or a carbonate | faster | slower |
| Products | chloride salts | ethanoate salts |

## Equations to be able to write

- 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂
- CH₃COOH + KOH → CH₃COOK + H₂O
- 2CH₃COOH + CaO → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + H₂O
- 2CH₃COOH + CaCO₃ → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂
- CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O *(Extended)*

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carboxyl group | –COOH, the functional group of carboxylic acids |
| Ethanoate | salt of ethanoic acid, containing CH₃COO⁻ |
| Weak acid *(Extended)* | an acid that is only partially dissociated in water |
| Oxidation *(Extended)* | here, ethanol gains oxygen and loses hydrogen to become ethanoic acid |
| Ester *(Extended)* | product of a carboxylic acid and an alcohol; contains –COO– |
| Acid catalyst *(Extended)* | speeds up esterification without being used up |

## Exam traps

- (CH₃COO)₂Mg, **not** CH₃COOMg.
- Salts are **ethanoates** – not "ethanates" or "acetates".
- Carbonate reactions have **three** products; metal reactions give **hydrogen**, not water.
- The manganate(VII) must be **acidified**, and goes **purple to colourless**. *(Extended)*
- Vinegar formation **needs air**; fermentation **excludes** it.
- Ester names start with the **alcohol** part. *(Extended)*

## Self-test

1. Give the formula of the ethanoate ion.
2. Name the gas produced when ethanoic acid reacts with magnesium.
3. Write the formula of calcium ethanoate.
4. Write the balanced equation for ethanoic acid reacting with sodium hydroxide.
5. Name all the products when ethanoic acid reacts with potassium carbonate.
6. Describe a test for the gas produced in question 5.
7. *(Extended)* Name the reagent used to oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid in the laboratory, and state the colour change.
8. *(Extended)* State what is needed for bacteria to turn wine into vinegar.
9. *(Extended)* Name the ester formed from methanoic acid and ethanol.
10. *(Extended)* Name the catalyst type used in esterification.
11. *(Extended)* Name CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH.
12. *(Extended)* Explain why ethanoic acid reacts more slowly with zinc than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration.

**Answers:**

1. CH₃COO⁻.
2. Hydrogen.
3. (CH₃COO)₂Ca.
4. CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O.
5. Potassium ethanoate, water and carbon dioxide.
6. Bubble it through limewater: it turns milky.
7. Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII); purple to colourless.
8. Oxygen (air).
9. Ethyl methanoate.
10. An acid catalyst.
11. Butanoic acid.
12. It is a weak acid, only partially dissociated, so it has a lower concentration of H⁺ ions.
