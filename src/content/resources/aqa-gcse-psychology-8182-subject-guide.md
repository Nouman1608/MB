AQA GCSE Psychology (8182) is an introduction to the scientific study
of the mind and behaviour. It looks at how we remember, perceive and
develop, how other people influence us, how language and the brain
shape thought, and how psychological problems are understood.
Assessment is by two
written papers, each lasting 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 100 marks
(50% of the GCSE).

## How the course is organised

The content is divided into two halves of four topics each, and each
half matches one exam paper.

**Paper 1: Cognition and Behaviour**

- *Memory:* how we remember and why remembering can go wrong.
- *Perception:* how we interpret the information our senses provide.
- *Development:* how people change as they grow up.
- *Research Methods:* how psychologists plan and carry out studies and
  make sense of the results.

**Paper 2: Social Context and Behaviour**

- *Social Influence:* how other people affect what we do.
- *Language, Thought and Communication:* how language, thinking and
  communication relate to one another.
- *Brain and Neuropsychology:* how the brain is linked to behaviour.
- *Psychological Problems:* how psychologists understand problems
  affecting mental health.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered: everyone sits the same two
papers at the end of the course, and there is no coursework or
controlled assessment.

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8182/1 | Paper 1: Cognition and behaviour | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| 8182/2 | Paper 2: Social context and behaviour | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed and equally weighted, so neither
half of the course matters more than the other. The specification was
first taught in September 2017 and first examined in June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

Research methods is listed under Paper 1, but it is the skill that
holds the whole course together. Every topic on both papers is built
on studies, and you are expected to know how those studies were
carried out and what their strengths and weaknesses are. A question on
social influence, for example, may ask you to comment on how a study
was designed just as readily as a memory question may. Learning research
methods thoroughly early on makes every other topic easier to
evaluate.

There are also content links between the two papers. Perception on
Paper 1 and Language, Thought and Communication on Paper 2 both ask how
the mind organises and makes sense of information. Memory and
Development pair naturally with Brain and Neuropsychology, which adds a
biological angle to questions about thinking and change. Psychological
Problems brings these strands together and relies on the kind of
evidence that research methods teaches you to judge.

Across both papers you are asked to do three things: describe
psychological theories and studies accurately, apply them to everyday
scenarios, and evaluate them. Practising all three for each topic
prepares you for the full range of questions.

## Planning your study

1. **Begin with research methods.** Even a short introduction to how
   studies are designed gives you the vocabulary for the rest of the
   course.
2. **Teach the Paper 1 topics.** Memory, perception and development can
   follow, with research methods revisited through the studies in each
   one.
3. **Move to the Paper 2 topics.** Social influence, language and
   communication, brain and neuropsychology, and psychological problems
   complete the content.
4. **Keep both halves active.** Short quizzes on Paper 1 topics while
   you are studying Paper 2 stop the earlier material fading.
5. **Build an evaluation bank.** For each key study, note one strength
   and one weakness linked to its method.

It also helps to treat the two papers as equal partners in your
timetable. Because each carries exactly half of the GCSE, a weak topic
on either paper costs the same as a weak topic on the other, and there
is no safe half to neglect. A simple tracker listing the eight topics,
with a note of when you last reviewed each one and how confident you
feel, makes gaps visible long before the exams.

When you revise a study, aim to be able to say four things about it:
what it was trying to find out, how it was carried out, what it found,
and one way its method could be criticised. Those four points cover
most of what you need for description, application and evaluation, and
they make research methods part of every revision session rather than
a separate chore.

Topic questions should start as soon as each topic is taught. Full
papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page are most useful in
the last few months, once all eight topics are complete.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in AQA GCSE Psychology?**
There are two written papers, Cognition and behaviour and Social context and behaviour. Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks and carries 50% of the GCSE.

**Is AQA GCSE Psychology tiered?**
No. The qualification is not tiered, so all students sit the same two papers.

**Is there coursework in GCSE Psychology 8182?**
No. Both papers are externally assessed written exams, with no coursework or controlled assessment.

**Which paper is research methods on?**
Research Methods is listed as a Paper 1 topic, but the skills it teaches are needed to evaluate studies throughout the course.

**When was AQA GCSE Psychology 8182 first examined?**
It was first taught from September 2017 and first examined in June 2019.
