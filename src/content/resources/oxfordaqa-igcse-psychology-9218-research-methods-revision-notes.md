Quick-recall notes for **3.1.4 Research Methods** in Paper 1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE
Psychology (9218), focused on experiments. For full explanations, use the
[Research Methods (Paper 1) study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-research-methods/).
Non-experimental methods and data handling are condensed in the
[Research Methods (Paper 2) revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-research-methods-paper2-revision-notes/);
both sets of content can be examined on either paper.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alternative hypothesis** | Predicts an effect of the IV on the DV, or a relationship |
| **Null hypothesis** | Predicts no effect or relationship |
| **Operationalise** | State a variable in a measurable way |
| **Independent variable** | The variable the researcher changes or compares |
| **Dependent variable** | The variable measured |
| **Extraneous variable** | Any other variable that might affect the DV |
| **Target population** | The group a researcher wants to generalise to |
| **Standardised procedure** | Same method, timings and materials for every participant |
| **Randomisation** | Using chance to decide the order of materials or trials |
| **Random allocation** | Using chance to put participants into conditions |
| **Counterbalancing** | Half do A then B, half do B then A |
| **Reliability** | Consistency: repeating the study would give similar results |
| **Validity** | Accuracy: the study measures what it claims to measure |

## Writing hypotheses: a three-step check

1. Name both conditions of the IV.
2. Say exactly how the DV is measured.
3. For the null, say "there will be **no difference**" (or "no relationship").

Weak: "Music affects memory." Strong: "Students who revise with music playing will recall fewer of 20
key terms than students who revise in silence."

## Experiments

| | Laboratory | Field | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setting | Controlled | Everyday | Everyday or controlled |
| IV | Manipulated | Manipulated | Occurs naturally |
| Control | High | Lower | Lowest |
| Realism | Lower | Higher | Higher |
| Main issue | Artificial tasks | Extraneous variables | Groups may differ in other ways |

## Experimental designs

| Design | Main problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Independent groups | Participant variables | Random allocation |
| Repeated measures | Order effects (practice, fatigue) | Counterbalancing |
| Matched pairs | Imperfect, time-consuming matching | Pre-test on the key variable |

## Sampling

| Method | One-line how | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random | Equal chance for all, e.g. names from a hat | Unbiased | Needs full list |
| Opportunity | Whoever is available | Quick | Unrepresentative |
| Systematic | Every nth from a list | No researcher choice | Can hit a pattern |
| Stratified | Subgroups in proportion | Representative | Time-consuming |

**Stratified maths:** proportion of subgroup in population × sample size. Example: 25% of employees
are managers; sample of 40 → 0.25 × 40 = 10 managers.

## Ethics (BPS)

| Issue | Deal with it by |
|---|---|
| Informed consent | Consent form; parents for children |
| Deception | Only when justified; full debrief |
| Protection from harm | Stop if distressed; offer support |
| Confidentiality | Anonymise; store data securely |
| Right to withdraw | Tell participants at the start; allow data removal |
| Debriefing | Explain aims; reassure |

## Exam traps

- **Random sampling** is about choosing participants; **random allocation** is about assigning them to
  conditions.
- Opportunity samples are not random, even if the researcher "picked at random".
- A natural experiment **still has an IV**, but the researcher does not manipulate it.
- Order effects are only a problem in **repeated measures**.
- Counterbalancing **spreads** order effects; it does not remove them.
- High control improves **reliability**, but artificial tasks can lower **validity**.
- Name the specific extraneous variable in the scenario (for example, "noise from the corridor"),
  not just "extraneous variables".

## Paper 1 scenario prompts

| Paper 1 study type | IV | DV |
|---|---|---|
| Memory: effect of rehearsal on recall | Rehearse versus no rehearsal | Words recalled |
| Perception: effect of expectation | Letter context versus number context | Whether "B" or "13" is reported |
| Biopsychology: effect of caffeine on reaction time | Caffeine versus no caffeine | Reaction time in milliseconds |

## Self-test

1. Write a null hypothesis for a study of whether background noise affects recall.
2. What is the difference between an IV and a DV?
3. Give one example of an extraneous variable in a memory experiment.
4. Which experiment type has an IV that the researcher does not control?
5. Which design has the problem of order effects?
6. What does counterbalancing involve?
7. Which sampling method selects every nth person?
8. A college has 200 science and 300 arts students. How many arts students should be in a stratified
   sample of 50?
9. Give one weakness of opportunity sampling.
10. What should happen after a study that used deception?
11. What does reliability mean?
12. Give one way to protect confidentiality.

**Answers:**
1. "There will be no difference in the number of words recalled between participants who learn with
   background noise and those who learn in silence."
2. The IV is changed or compared by the researcher; the DV is the variable measured.
3. Any one, such as time of day, word familiarity, tiredness or noise outside the room.
4. A natural experiment.
5. Repeated measures.
6. Half the participants do condition A then B and half do B then A.
7. Systematic sampling.
8. 300 ÷ 500 = 60%; 60% of 50 = 30 arts students.
9. It is often unrepresentative, because only available people are included.
10. Participants should be fully debriefed as soon as possible and offered the chance to withdraw
    their data.
11. Consistency: the same results would be found if the study were repeated.
12. Use participant numbers instead of names, or store data securely.
