Subtopic 18.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the electric field as a field of force, the definition of electric field strength as force per unit positive charge, the equation F = qE and the representation of fields by field lines. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Electric fields and field lines study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-fields-and-field-lines/); to apply the ideas, try the [Electric fields and field lines practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-fields-and-field-lines-practice/).

## Core ideas

- **Field of force:** a region where an object experiences a non-contact force. Electric field: the property is **charge** (gravitational: mass).
- **Definition:** electric field strength = **force per unit positive charge** on a small stationary test charge.
- E is a **vector**: direction = direction of force on a positive charge.
- "Small" test charge so it does not disturb the charges producing the field.

```
E = F / q          F = qE          unit N C⁻¹ (= V m⁻¹, see 18.2)
```

In SI base units, N C⁻¹ = kg m s⁻² ÷ A s = **kg m s⁻³ A⁻¹**.

## Direction of the force

| Charge | Force relative to E |
|---|---|
| Positive (proton, alpha particle, positive ion) | Same direction as E |
| Negative (electron, negative ion) | Opposite to E |

Method: find |F| = |q|E, then assign the direction from the sign.

## Field-line rules

1. Arrows show the direction of force on a **positive** charge.
2. Start on **+**, end on **−** (or at infinity).
3. Never cross.
4. Closer lines = stronger field.
5. Meet conductor surfaces at **90°**.

## Patterns

| Arrangement | Key features to draw |
|---|---|
| Isolated + charge | Radial, outward arrows, lines spreading apart |
| Isolated − charge | Radial, inward arrows |
| + and − (equal) | Curved lines from + to −; densest on the line between them; symmetric |
| + and + (equal) | Lines bend away from each other; **neutral point** midway; no lines through it |
| Parallel plates | Straight, parallel, evenly spaced, + to −; edges bow outwards |
| Charged sphere | Same as point charge at its centre, outside the sphere; lines normal to the surface |
| Point charge and flat plate | Radial near charge, curving to meet the plate at 90° |

## Combining fields

- E is a vector: add fields from separate charges as vectors.
- Along a line: add with signs. At right angles: Pythagoras, angle by tan⁻¹.
- **Neutral point:** where the resultant field is zero; no force on any charge placed there.

## Numbers worth recognising

- Electron in 1 N C⁻¹: electric force 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ N, weight 8.9 × 10⁻³⁰ N; ratio about 2 × 10¹⁰. Gravity is negligible for electrons and ions in any practical field.
- For a charged oil drop (mass about 10⁻¹⁵ kg), gravity is **not** negligible: balancing needs qE = mg.
- Proton vs alpha particle in the same E: alpha feels twice the force but has half the acceleration.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electric field | Region in which a charge experiences a force |
| Electric field strength | Force per unit positive charge at a point; vector, N C⁻¹ |
| Test charge | Small positive charge used (in principle) to measure the field without disturbing it |
| Field line | Line showing the direction of the force on a positive charge |
| Uniform field | Field with the same strength and direction everywhere: parallel, equally spaced lines |
| Radial field | Field whose lines spread out from (or converge on) a point |
| Neutral point | Point where the resultant field strength is zero |

## Exam traps

- Definition without "positive" or without "per unit charge".
- Arrows missing or reversed on field lines.
- Lines crossing, or radial lines drawn evenly spaced at all distances.
- Electron force drawn in the field direction.
- Lines meeting a conductor at an angle other than 90°.
- Adding field magnitudes from two charges without considering direction.

## Self-test

1. Define electric field strength.
2. Why must the test charge be small?
3. Express N C⁻¹ in SI base units.
4. Calculate the force on an electron in a field of 2.0 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹ and give its direction.
5. A charge of −4.0 nC feels a force of 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ N to the left. Find E (magnitude and direction).
6. Why can field lines never cross?
7. Where is the neutral point for two equal positive charges?
8. What does even spacing of field lines indicate?
9. A drop of mass 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg is held still in a field of 4.9 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹. Find its charge in units of e.
10. Two perpendicular fields of 6.0 × 10³ N C⁻¹ and 8.0 × 10³ N C⁻¹ act at a point. Find the resultant field strength.
11. At what angle do field lines meet a conductor surface, and why?
12. A proton and an alpha particle are in the same field. Compare their accelerations.

**Answers:** 1. The force per unit positive charge acting on a small stationary charge at that point. 2. So that it does not disturb the charges producing the field. 3. kg m s⁻³ A⁻¹. 4. F = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ N, opposite to the field. 5. E = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 4.0 × 10⁻⁹ = 5.0 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹, to the right (opposite to the force on a negative charge). 6. The field has only one direction at each point. 7. Midway between them. 8. A uniform field. 9. q = mg/E = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 9.81 ÷ 4.9 × 10⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C = 2e. 10. √(6.0² + 8.0²) × 10³ = 1.0 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹. 11. 90°; any component along the surface would move the free charges until it disappeared. 12. The alpha particle's acceleration is about half the proton's (twice the charge, four times the mass).
