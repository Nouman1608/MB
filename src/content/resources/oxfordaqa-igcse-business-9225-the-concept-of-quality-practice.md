All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Pennant Phones.

> **Case study: Pennant Phones**
>
> Pennant Phones makes 20,000 mobile phones a month. Currently 4% of the
> phones are faulty, and each faulty phone costs $25 to repair or replace.
> Pennant is considering a TQM programme costing $6,000 a month, which
> it expects to cut the defect rate to 1.5%. Pennant is also considering
> outsourcing the assembly of its budget phone to another company. The
> assembler would charge less, but it has a defect rate of 6%. Recent
> customer reviews complain that Pennant phones are "reliable but
> dull". Pennant's customers expect the phones to work reliably for at
> least three years.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a measure of quality? **[1]**
- A. Defect rate
- B. Number of staff
- C. Rent of the factory
- D. Number of shops

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of TQM? **[1]**
- A. Only inspectors are responsible for quality
- B. All employees are responsible for quality
- C. Quality is checked only at the end
- D. Quality is not measured

**3.** Define the term "quality". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** consequences for a business of poor quality. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way a business could identify quality problems. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the number of faulty phones Pennant makes each month at present. **[1]**

**7.** Calculate the monthly cost of faulty phones at present. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the monthly cost of faulty phones with TQM, and the net monthly benefit of TQM after paying for the programme. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the extra monthly cost of faulty phones if all 20,000 phones were made by the outsourced assembler instead of by Pennant. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to Pennant of TQM. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** problems with quality that Pennant might face if it outsources assembly. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Pennant should introduce TQM. Use your answers to Questions 7 and 8. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** How well a good or service [1] meets the needs and expectations of the customer [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: lost sales [1]; damaged reputation [1]; extra costs of refunds, repairs or replacement [1]; legal action [1]; low staff morale [1]. **[2]**

**5.** For example, a business could count returns or complaints [1], which shows what proportion of customers are unhappy and which faults are most common [1]. Accept: inspection, defect rate, customer surveys or mystery shoppers with a reason. **[2]**

**6.** 4% x 20,000 = **800** phones [1]. **[1]**

**7.** 800 x $25 [1] = **$20,000** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Faulty phones with TQM = 1.5% x 20,000 = 300 [1]; cost = 300 x $25 = $7,500 [1]; saving = $20,000 - $7,500 = $12,500 [1]; net benefit = $12,500 - $6,000 = **$6,500** a month [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Faulty at the assembler = 6% x 20,000 = 1,200 phones [1]; cost = 1,200 x $25 = $30,000 [1]; extra cost = $30,000 - $20,000 = **$10,000** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Advantage:** TQM would reduce faulty phones from 800 to 300 a month [1], cutting the cost of repairs and replacement by $12,500 [1].
**Disadvantage:** it costs $6,000 a month [1], and staff may resist the extra training and changes, so results may be slower than expected [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Less control:** Pennant would not supervise the assembler's staff and methods [1], so faults might not be found until phones reach customers [1].
**Higher defect rate:** the assembler's defect rate of 6% is higher than Pennant's 4% [1], so more phones would need repair, costing an extra $10,000 a month on 20,000 phones [1].
**Reputation:** customers expect phones to work reliably for three years [1], and they would blame Pennant, not the assembler, if the phones failed [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For TQM:** it saves $12,500 a month in faults and costs $6,000, a net benefit of $6,500 [1], which is $78,000 a year [1]; fewer faulty phones protect Pennant's reputation for reliability [1]; staff involvement may raise motivation [1].
**Against:** the defect rate of 1.5% is only expected, not guaranteed [1]; the programme takes time and money before results appear [1]; reviews describe the phones as "dull", so TQM may not address what customers want [1].
**Judgement:** Pennant should introduce TQM, because the figures show a clear net saving and customers already value reliability [1]. It should monitor the defect rate to check the programme works, and keep the programme only if the saving stays above $6,000 a month [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to subtract the cost of the programme from the saving.
- Confusing a percentage with the number of phones. Multiply the rate by 20,000.
- Writing that high quality means high price.
- Describing consequences of poor quality without saying how they affect the business.
- Ignoring Pennant's reliability reputation in the evaluation.
