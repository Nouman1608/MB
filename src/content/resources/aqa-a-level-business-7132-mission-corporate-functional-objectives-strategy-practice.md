These questions cover section 3.7.1 Mission, corporate objectives, functional objectives and strategy of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-mission-corporate-functional-objectives-strategy/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-mission-corporate-functional-objectives-strategy-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a tactic rather than a strategy? **[1]**

A: Entering three new overseas markets over five years

B: Acquiring a competitor

C: Offering a free delivery weekend to boost sales

D: Moving all production to an automated factory

**2.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to cause short-termism in a plc? **[1]**

A: Directors' bonuses based on the share price this year

B: A family shareholding that has been held for 40 years

C: A mission to benefit the community

D: A five-year investment plan

**3.** Define the term "corporate objective". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** pressures that can lead a business to focus on short-term results. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a business with a skilled and loyal workforce may set bolder corporate objectives than one with high staff turnover. **[3]**

**6.** Brindle & Marsh Ltd sells reusable kitchenware. Its mission is "to help every household waste less".

**(a)** Identify **one** corporate objective consistent with this mission. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the objective you chose in part (a) is consistent with the mission. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Halden Tea plc is owned mainly by pension funds that review its results every six months. The directors are considering a ten-year plan to plant new tea estates, which will cut profit for the first four years. Analyse **two** ways in which short-termism might affect this decision. **[6]**

**8.** Marrow Street Bakery has a strategy of opening 20 shops in three years. Explain how this strategy might affect the objectives of its operations and human resources functions. **[4]**

**9.** Kestrel Outdoor sells walking gear. A SWOT analysis lists: Strengths: well-known brand, 12 stores; Weaknesses: little online presence, ageing stock system; Opportunities: growth in hiking, rise in online sales; Threats: low-cost online rivals, rising rents. Analyse **one** strategy the business could follow based on this SWOT analysis. **[6]**

**10.** Dunmore Hotels was founded by a chef who set the mission "great food, warmly served". The company is now a plc, and new shareholders want faster growth in profit. Assess the extent to which the influence of ownership will change the company's corporate objectives. **[9]**

**11.** Explain the difference between a strategy and a tactic, using an example of your own. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the value of SWOT analysis to a business planning its strategy for the next five years. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. A weekend promotion is short term, small in scale and easily changed. The other options commit large resources over years. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. A bonus tied to this year's share price rewards short-term results. The other options encourage a longer view. **[1]**

**3.** A measurable target [1] for the whole business, to be achieved over a stated period and set to deliver the mission [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: reporting results quarterly or half-yearly [1]; bonuses linked to current profit or share price [1]; threat of takeover if the share price is low [1]; lenders demanding quick repayment [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A skilled workforce can deliver more complex or ambitious targets [1], for example higher quality or faster growth. Loyal staff mean lower recruitment and training costs and fewer disruptions [1], so managers can be more confident of reaching targets [1]. High turnover makes ambitious targets risky. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Any sensible objective, for example "increase sales of refillable products to 40% of revenue within five years" [1]. **(b)** The objective moves customers towards reusable products [1], which reduces household waste, which is the purpose in the mission [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Effect 1: pressure to cut or delay [1]. Pension funds review results every six months, so they may sell shares if profit falls for four years [1], and the lower share price makes a takeover more likely, so directors might drop or shrink the plan [1]. Effect 2: damage to long-term growth [1]. If the plan is cut to protect short-term profit, new estates are not planted and output falls behind demand later [1], which may let rivals take market share [1]. (Credit also: directors may seek to explain the plan to investors to win patient backing.) **[6]**

**8.** Operations: the objective might be to raise capacity or standardise recipes so that each new shop can open on time [1], for example opening a central bakery to supply all shops at a lower unit cost [1]. Human resources: the objective might be to recruit and train enough bakers and shop staff before each opening [1], for example fill 90% of roles two weeks before launch, so that opening is not delayed by staff shortages [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Strategy: build an online store [1]. This uses the strength of a well-known brand [1] to take advantage of the rise in online sales [1], and reduces the weakness of little online presence [1]. It also helps to respond to the threat from low-cost online rivals, because customers who know and trust the brand may buy direct [1]. Application: the 12 stores could act as collection points, but the ageing stock system may struggle, so it needs upgrading first [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Why ownership matters: new shareholders expect returns [1], and a plc is judged on profit, dividends and share price, so objectives are likely to shift towards profit growth, for example by opening more hotels [1]. Directors' pay may be linked to results, which reinforces this [1]. Why the change may be limited: the mission is part of the brand and what customers pay for [1], so objectives that cut quality to raise profit could damage the reputation [1]. Founders and managers may keep influence through their shareholding [1]. The competitive market means growth has to come from satisfied customers [1]. Judgement: ownership will have a significant effect on the level of the profit targets [1], but a sensible board will set objectives that respect the mission if the brand is the main source of value [1]. **[9]**

**11.** A strategy is a medium- to long-term plan that commits significant resources to achieve the corporate objectives [1], for example building a new factory to serve a new region [1]. A tactic is a short-term action to carry out part of a strategy and is easy to change [1], for example a promotional offer to attract customers in the new region [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For: SWOT is quick and cheap, so it can be done by many managers and updated often [1]. It forces consideration of both internal and external factors [1]. It gives a clear base for choosing a strategy that uses strengths and opportunities [1]. It can involve staff from all functions, which builds support for the plan [1]. Against: it is only a list and does not rank issues [1], it is subjective, so different people produce different results [1], it shows one moment in time, so a five-year plan needs regular updating [1], and it does not say what to do [1]. Factors: how accurate and data-based the information is [1], how stable the market is over five years [1], and whether SWOT is used with other tools such as financial analysis and Porter's five forces. Judgement: SWOT is a valuable first step but not enough alone [1]; it is most valuable when each point is backed by data and linked to specific objectives [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers apply each point to the business in the case, and judge the value of a tool by what it is used for.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating the mission as a measurable target.
- Calling every large decision strategic.
- Saying short-termism is always harmful, with no balance.
- Listing SWOT points without using them to justify a strategy.
- Ignoring the owners when asked about the influences on objectives.
