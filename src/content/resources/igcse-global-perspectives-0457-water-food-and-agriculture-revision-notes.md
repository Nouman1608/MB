These notes cover the topic "Water, food and agriculture" from the Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives (0457) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027, Version 2 (published April 2025). The topic is on the topic list in section 3, which the syllabus says is for all components: Component 1 Written Exam, Component 2 Individual Report and Component 3 Team Project. The skills come from section 2 (assessment objectives) and section 4 (details of the assessment).

**Topic knowledge is not assessed.** The syllabus says "Knowledge of content is not assessed in this syllabus." These notes show how to use this topic to practise the assessed skills. Do not learn crop statistics for their own sake.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition | Water, food and farming use |
|---|---|---|
| Issue | "An important subject or problem for discussion" (syllabus) | Who should get water in a drought |
| Perspective | "A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning" (syllabus) | A smallholder's view on paying for well water |
| Research | "The systematic study of a global issue to find evidence and develop understanding" (syllabus) | Measuring canteen waste before and after a campaign |
| Evidence | Information that helps develop understanding or prove something true or false (syllabus) | Well-level readings, harvest weights |
| Argument | Statements with reasons and evidence supporting a claim (syllabus) | "Water pricing works because..." |
| Fact | Can be checked as true or false | The date a dam opened |
| Opinion | Personal view; cannot be proved | "Local food is always better" |
| Claim | Put forward as true; needs support | "This seed survives any drought" |
| Prediction | About the future; cannot yet be checked | "Bread prices will double next year" |
| Value | Belief about what matters | "Water is a human right, not a product" |
| Generalisation | Broad statement from limited cases | "City people waste all their food" |
| Bias | One-sided presentation | A dairy advert that lists only health benefits |
| Vested interest | Gain or loss from the outcome | A seed company reporting its own trial |
| Food security | All people, at all times, having access to enough safe, nutritious food (1996 World Food Summit) | Measures whether families can get and afford food |

## Real examples at a glance

You will not be marked for knowing these. Use them as context for research and to test your reasoning.

| Example | What it is | What it shows | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human right to water and sanitation (UN General Assembly, 2010) | Formal recognition of a right | A global value-based perspective | A right on paper does not build pipes; it depends on national action |
| Sustainable Development Goals 2 and 6 (2015) | Zero hunger; clean water and sanitation | Global targets for action | Goals are not binding, so progress varies by country |
| FAO: agriculture about 70% of freshwater withdrawals | Estimate from the UN food agency | Why farming is central to water issues | Withdrawals are not the same as water used up; some returns to rivers |
| UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024 | About a fifth of food available to consumers wasted in 2022; households the largest share | Scale of waste; a global estimate | Many countries measure waste differently, so some figures are estimates |
| France's food waste law (2016) | Large supermarkets must not destroy edible unsold food and must offer it to charities | A national course of action | Relies on charities being able to collect and store food |
| Black Sea Grain Initiative (July 2022 to July 2023) | UN and Turkey brokered safe passage for Ukrainian grain ships | How conflict links global supply to local prices | Ended when Russia withdrew, so it depended on all sides staying in |
| India's rice export ban (July 2023) | Ban on exports of non-basmati white rice | National vs global perspectives on food prices | Protected domestic buyers; raised costs for importers |
| Cape Town "Day Zero" (2018) | 50 litres per person per day limit from February 2018; Day Zero avoided | Household and city responses to drought | Shows demand cuts work, but rain also returned, so not all the credit goes to the limits |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Fact vs claim.** "The well is 30 metres deep" can be checked. "Wells are drying because of export farms" is a claim about a cause and needs evidence.
- **Opinion vs perspective.** An opinion can stand alone. A perspective rests on evidence and reasoning, such as a farmer's account of costs and yields.
- **Value vs evidence.** "Water belongs to everyone" is a value. It shapes an argument but proves nothing about what is happening.
- **Vested interest vs bias.** A vested interest is a reason a source might be biased. Bias shows in the content: missing evidence, one-sided wording.
- **Correlation vs cause.** If wells and export farms both increased, that alone does not prove one caused the other.
- **Physical vs economic scarcity.** The best action differs: a new pipe solves one; it cannot solve the other.
- **Global, national, local.** Component 2 needs local and/or national and global perspectives. Component 3 starts from a local issue.
- **Several courses of action vs one.** In Component 2, consider several, then propose a single course of action and explain how it could be implemented and its impact.

## Mini essay plan

**Typical extended task:** evaluate which of three courses of action would best reduce food waste in a city (in the Written Exam, the actions and evidence come from the source insert).

1. **Set the criteria.** Amount of waste cut, cost, fairness, and whether it lasts. Say which matters most and why.
2. **Action 1, e.g. a law making supermarkets donate unsold food.** Source evidence on how much shops throw away. Limitation: shops may not be where most waste happens; charities may lack storage.
3. **Action 2, e.g. a household waste-awareness campaign.** May reach the largest source of waste. Limitation: awareness does not always change behaviour; hard to measure.
4. **Action 3, e.g. charging households by the weight of food waste collected.** Gives a direct money reason. Limitation: may hit poorer, larger families hardest; risk of illegal dumping.
5. **Compare directly.** Which action does best on each criterion? Use figures from the sources.
6. **Judgement.** Choose one, or a justified combination. Say why it beats the others and what must be true for it to work.

Each paragraph: point from the source → apply it → analyse the effect → evaluate with a limitation.

## Coursework ideas at a glance

**Individual Report (Component 2).** The issue must divide opinion, and your title is a global research question.

| Possible question | Perspectives to research |
|---|---|
| Should farmers pay for the water they use in the same way as households? | Local smallholders; a national water ministry; international water and food agencies |
| Should countries ban food exports when prices rise at home? | Local traders and shoppers; an exporting government; importing countries |
| Should genetically modified crops be used more widely to improve food security? | Local farmers; national regulators; international scientists and campaign groups |

**Team Project (Component 3).** Start from a local issue the team can act on and measure.

| Local issue | Possible action | How to measure success |
|---|---|---|
| Plate waste in the canteen | "Clean plate" campaign with weekly weigh-in | Kilograms per lunch, before and after, on matched days |
| Leaking taps and cisterns | Water audit and repair requests to the school | Number of leaks found vs fixed; meter readings if available |
| Students unsure where food comes from | Short assembly and a school vegetable bed | Simple quiz before and after; harvest weight |

For the rules, see the [Individual Report revision notes](/resources/global-perspectives-individual-report-revision-notes/) and the [Team Project revision notes](/resources/igcse-global-perspectives-team-project-revision-notes/).

## Quick self-test

1. What does the 0457 syllabus say about knowledge of content?
2. Give the syllabus definition of an issue.
3. "Every farmer floods his fields." Which term best describes this statement?
4. What is the difference between physical and economic water scarcity?
5. Why does a seed company's trial report need extra checking?
6. Give one global, one national and one local issue within this topic.
7. What does "virtual water" mean?
8. Why is "Food waste" a poor Individual Report title?
9. What must the Component 2 report do after considering several courses of action?
10. How long must the Explanation of Research and Planning be?
11. Give one limitation of the UNEP Food Waste Index figures.

### Answers

1. Knowledge of content is not assessed. The topics are a context for building skills.
2. An important subject or problem for discussion.
3. A generalisation. It makes a broad statement from limited cases.
4. Physical scarcity: the water is not there. Economic scarcity: the water exists but people cannot reach or afford it.
5. The company has a vested interest, because it gains if the seed looks good.
6. For example: global, the right to safe water; national, whether to ban food exports; local, waste in a school canteen.
7. The water used to produce a good, such as the water used to grow imported wheat.
8. It is a topic, not a question. It does not name an issue that divides opinion.
9. Propose a single course of action, explaining how it could be implemented and what its impact would be.
10. 300 to 400 words.
11. Countries measure food waste in different ways, or not at all, so some figures are estimates.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Bringing in outside facts about droughts or famines in Component 1 instead of using the sources.
- Naming a term ("this is a value") without quoting the words and explaining why.
- Rejecting a farmer's or company's view only because of who said it, without testing the evidence.
- Treating two trends that rise together, such as wells and export farms, as proof of cause.
- Accepting trial results without asking how many farms, who chose them and what else changed.
- Describing courses of action one by one with no comparison or final judgement.
- Ignoring fairness: who pays, and whether smallholders lose more than large farms.
- In the Individual Report, giving only "for" and "against" with no named groups or reasons.
- In the Team Project, planning an action with no before-and-after measure.
