## Section A

**1.** Write each number in standard form.

**(a)** 450 000 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.00072 **[1]**

**(c)** 6 000 000 000 **[1]**

**2.** Write each number as an ordinary number.

**(a)** 3.8 × 10⁶ **[1]**

**(b)** 5.1 × 10⁻³ **[1]**

**3.** Explain why 45 × 10³ is not in standard form, and write it in standard form. **[2]**

**4.** Work out (3 × 10⁴) × (2.5 × 10³). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**5.** Work out (6 × 10⁵) × (5 × 10⁴). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**6.** Work out (9.6 × 10⁷) ÷ (1.2 × 10³). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**7.** Which is larger, 7.2 × 10⁻³ or 8.1 × 10⁻⁴? Give a reason. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Work out (4.2 × 10⁻³) ÷ (6 × 10⁵). Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**9.** Work out 3.2 × 10⁵ + 4.5 × 10⁴. Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**10.** Work out 6.1 × 10⁻³ - 2.4 × 10⁻⁴. Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**11.** Light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s. The Sun is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from Earth. Work out the time the light takes to reach Earth. Give your answer in seconds, in standard form. **[3]**

**12.** There are 365 days in a year. Work out the number of seconds in 365 days, giving your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**13.** **(a)** Work out (2.5 × 10⁶)². **[2]**

**(b)** Work out √(1.6 × 10⁷). **[2]**

**14.** *Calculator.* Work out (6.7 × 10⁸) ÷ (3.2 × 10⁻⁵), giving your answer in standard form correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**15.** A dust particle has mass 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ g. How many of these particles have a total mass of 1 g? Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **4.5 × 10⁵** **[1]**

**(b)** **7.2 × 10⁻⁴** **[1]**

**(c)** **6 × 10⁹** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Count the places the decimal point moves. A small number (below 1) has a negative power.

**2. (a)** **3 800 000** **[1]**

**(b)** **0.0051** **[1]** **[2]**

**3.** The front number 45 is not between 1 and 10 **[1]**; 45 × 10³ = 4.5 × 10 × 10³ = **4.5 × 10⁴** **[1]** **[2]**

**4.** 3 × 2.5 = 7.5 and 10⁴ × 10³ = 10⁷ **[1]**; **7.5 × 10⁷** **[1]** **[2]**

**5.** 6 × 5 = 30 and 10⁵ × 10⁴ = 10⁹ **[1]**; 30 × 10⁹ = **3 × 10¹⁰** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 30 × 10⁹ is the right value but is not standard form. The last mark needs the adjustment.

**6.** 9.6 ÷ 1.2 = 8 **[1]**; 10⁷ ÷ 10³ = 10⁴, so **8 × 10⁴** **[1]** **[2]**

**7.** **7.2 × 10⁻³** is larger **[1]**, because the power -3 is greater than the power -4 **[1]** **[2]**

**8.** 4.2 ÷ 6 = 0.7 **[1]**; 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁵ = 10⁻⁸ **[1]**; 0.7 × 10⁻⁸ = **7 × 10⁻⁹** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Subtract the powers in the right order: -3 - 5 = -8. Then adjust 0.7 to 7, which lowers the power by one more.

**9.** 320 000 + 45 000 **[1]** = 365 000 **[1]**; **3.65 × 10⁵** **[1]** **[3]**

**10.** 0.0061 - 0.00024 **[1]** = 0.00586 **[1]**; **5.86 × 10⁻³** **[1]** **[3]**

**11.** Time = distance ÷ speed = (1.5 × 10¹¹) ÷ (3 × 10⁸) **[1]**; 0.5 × 10³ **[1]**; **5 × 10² seconds** **[1]** **[3]**

**12.** 365 × 24 × 3600 **[1]** = 31 536 000 **[1]**; **3.1536 × 10⁷** (or 3.15 × 10⁷ to 3 significant figures) **[1]** **[3]**

**13. (a)** 2.5² = 6.25 and (10⁶)² = 10¹², so **6.25 × 10¹²** **[2]**

**(b)** √(16 × 10⁶) **[1]** = **4 × 10³** **[1]** **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Rewrite 1.6 × 10⁷ as 16 × 10⁶ so that the power of 10 is even and the square root is exact.

**14.** 2.09375... × 10¹³ **[1]**; **2.09 × 10¹³** **[1]** **[2]**

**15.** 1 ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻⁶) **[1]** = 0.4 × 10⁶ = **4 × 10⁵** **[1]** **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 7.2 × 10⁴ for 0.00072 in Question 1(b), by using the wrong sign of the power.
- Leaving 30 × 10⁹ unadjusted in Question 5.
- Adding the powers when adding the numbers in Question 9.
- Leaving 0.7 × 10⁻⁸ without adjusting in Question 8.
- Writing a calculator display such as 2.09E13 in Question 14.
- Subtracting in the wrong order in Question 10.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why must the front number be between 1 and 10?**
It makes the form unique, so two numbers can be compared by looking at the powers of 10 first.

**Can n be zero?**
Yes. A number such as 5 is 5 × 10⁰ in standard form.

**Do I round the front number or the whole number?**
Round the front number to the accuracy asked. Keep the power of 10 unchanged unless rounding takes A up to 10.
