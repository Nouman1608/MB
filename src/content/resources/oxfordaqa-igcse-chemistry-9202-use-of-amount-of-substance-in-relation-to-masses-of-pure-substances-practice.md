This set practises section 3.6.2 Use of amount of substance in relation to masses of pure substances (statements a to c) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A is short calculations; Section B is multi-step formula work from data. The course is untiered.

Use these relative atomic masses throughout: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Al = 27, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39, Fe = 56, Cu = 63.5.

## Section B

**7.** A white solid contains 32.4% sodium, 22.5% sulfur and 45.1% oxygen by mass. Calculate its empirical formula. **[3]**

**8.** A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass. Its relative formula mass is 42.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by mass of hydrogen. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the molecular formula. **[2]**

**9.** A student heats 1.27 g of copper powder in air until its mass stops changing. The copper oxide formed has a mass of 1.59 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of oxygen that combined with the copper. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the empirical formula of the copper oxide. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the student heats until the mass stops changing. **[1]**

**10.** Urea, CO(NH₂)₂, and ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, are both nitrogen fertilisers.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of urea. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in urea. **[2]**

**(c)** The percentage of nitrogen in ammonium sulfate is 21.2%. Calculate the mass of nitrogen in a 50 kg bag of urea, and state which fertiliser supplies more nitrogen per bag of the same mass. **[2]**

**11.** Two iron ores are magnetite, Fe₃O₄, and haematite, Fe₂O₃.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in each ore. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of iron in 1.00 tonne of magnetite. **[1]**

**12.** 2.86 g of hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, is heated until all the water is lost. 1.06 g of anhydrous sodium carbonate remains.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of water lost. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the value of x. **[3]**

**13.** An oxide of nitrogen contains 30.4% nitrogen and 69.6% oxygen by mass. Its relative formula mass is 92.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula, showing your working. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2 × 23 + 32 + 4 × 16 = **142** [1].

**2.** 27 + 3 × (14 + 3 × 16) [1] = 27 + 186 = **213** [1].

**3.** Na₂CO₃ = 106 [1]; 106 + 10 × 18 = **286** [1].

**4.** Mᵣ KCl = 39 + 35.5 = 74.5 [1]; 39 ÷ 74.5 × 100 = **52.3%** [1].

**5. (a)** CH₂O [1]

**(b)** NO₂ [1]

**6.** An empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms [1]; a molecular formula gives the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule [1].

**7.** Na 32.4 ÷ 23 = 1.41; S 22.5 ÷ 32 = 0.703; O 45.1 ÷ 16 = 2.82 [1]. Divide by 0.703: Na 2.0, S 1, O 4.0 [1]. Empirical formula **Na₂SO₄** [1].

**8. (a)** 100 − 85.7 = **14.3%** [1].

**(b)** C 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14; H 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3 [1]; ratio 1 : 2.00, so **CH₂** [1].

**(c)** Empirical formula mass = 14 [1]; 42 ÷ 14 = 3, so **C₃H₆** [1].

**9. (a)** 1.59 − 1.27 = **0.32 g** [1].

**(b)** Cu 1.27 ÷ 63.5 = 0.0200 [1]; O 0.32 ÷ 16 = 0.0200 [1]; ratio 1 : 1, so **CuO** [1].

**(c)** To make sure all the copper has reacted (so the oxygen mass is not too low) [1].

*Examiner insight:* If some copper had not reacted, the oxygen mass would be too small and the formula would wrongly appear to contain extra copper.

**10. (a)** 12 + 16 + 2 × (14 + 2) = **60** [1].

**(b)** 2 × 14 = 28 [1]; 28 ÷ 60 × 100 = **46.7%** [1].

**(c)** 46.7% of 50 kg = **23.3 kg** [1]; urea supplies more nitrogen [1].

**11. (a)** Fe₃O₄: Mᵣ = 168 + 64 = 232 [1]; 168 ÷ 232 × 100 = **72.4%** [1]. Fe₂O₃: 112 ÷ 160 × 100 = **70.0%** [1].

**(b)** 0.724 × 1.00 = **0.724 tonnes** [1].

**12. (a)** 2.86 − 1.06 = **1.80 g** [1].

**(b)** Moles Na₂CO₃ = 1.06 ÷ 106 = 0.0100 [1]; moles H₂O = 1.80 ÷ 18 = 0.100 [1]; x = 0.100 ÷ 0.0100 = **10** [1].

**13. (a)** N 30.4 ÷ 14 = 2.17; O 69.6 ÷ 16 = 4.35 [1]; ratio 1 : 2, so **NO₂** [1].

**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 14 + 32 = 46 [1]; 92 ÷ 46 = 2, so **N₂O₄** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Ignoring brackets or the water of crystallisation when working out Mᵣ.
- Forgetting that urea and ammonium sulfate each contain two nitrogen atoms.
- Using percentages or masses of compounds, rather than of elements, in empirical tables.
- Rounding intermediate values to one significant figure, which can turn 2.49 into 2.
- Stating a molecular formula without showing the Mᵣ ÷ empirical-mass step.
