Take the speed of electromagnetic waves in free space as c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Treat air as free space.

---

## Section A

**1.** State two properties that are the same for all electromagnetic waves. **[2]**

**2.** State an approximate wavelength in free space for **(a)** X-rays, **(b)** radio waves and **(c)** infrared radiation. **[3]**

**3.** State the range of wavelengths in free space that is visible to the human eye, and state which end of the range corresponds to red light. **[2]**

**4.** A student states that radio waves travel more slowly through space than γ-rays because radio waves have a lower frequency. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**5.** Name the region of the electromagnetic spectrum to which each of the following free-space wavelengths belongs: **(a)** 2 × 10⁻⁷ m, **(b)** 3 × 10⁻² m, **(c)** 1 × 10⁻⁵ m, **(d)** 6 × 10⁻⁷ m. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** A wireless network transmits at a frequency of 5.0 GHz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the radiation. **[2]**

**(b)** State the region of the electromagnetic spectrum to which it belongs. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the time taken for the signal to travel 30 m. **[1]**

**7.** The human eye detects electromagnetic radiation in the range 400–700 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the range of frequencies that the eye detects. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the highest visible frequency to the lowest visible frequency. **[1]**

**8.** Light of free-space wavelength 600 nm enters water, in which its speed is 2.25 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the light. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of the light in water. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the syllabus specifies visible wavelengths "in free space". **[1]**

**9.** A long-wave radio signal has a wavelength of 1500 m. A γ-ray has a wavelength of 1.0 × 10⁻¹² m.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of the radio wavelength to the γ-ray wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency of each. **[2]**

**10.** An air-traffic radar emits pulses of microwaves of frequency 9.0 GHz. A pulse reflected from an aircraft returns to the radar 0.20 ms after it was emitted.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from the radar to the aircraft. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of the microwaves. **[1]**

**11.** Radiation of wavelength 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m is detected in a laboratory. Explain why it could be described as either X-rays or γ-rays, and state what usually determines which name is used. **[2]**

**12.** An infrared detector responds most strongly to radiation of wavelength 9.7 μm.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of this radiation. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times longer this wavelength is than the wavelength of green light of 500 nm. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the electric and magnetic fields in this radiation are arranged, and why this makes it a transverse wave. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** All electromagnetic waves are **transverse** [1], and all travel at the **same speed c in free space** [1].

**2. (a)** About 10⁻¹⁰ m (any value from about 10⁻¹³ m to 10⁻⁸ m) [1].

**(b)** About 10 m to 10³ m (any value longer than about 10⁻¹ m) [1].

**(c)** About 10⁻⁵ m (any value from about 7 × 10⁻⁷ m to 10⁻³ m) [1].

**3.** **400 nm to 700 nm** [1]; red is at the **700 nm** (long-wavelength) end [1].

**4.** In free space **all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed c**, whatever their frequency [1]. A lower frequency means a **longer wavelength** (c = fλ), not a lower speed [1].

**5. (a)** Ultraviolet [1]. **(b)** Microwave [1]. **(c)** Infrared [1]. **(d)** Visible [1].

**6. (a)** λ = c/f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.0 × 10⁹ [1] = **0.060 m** [1].

**(b)** **Microwave** [1].

**(c)** t = 30 ÷ 3.00 × 10⁸ = **1.0 × 10⁻⁷ s** [1].

**7. (a)** f = c/λ [1]. At 700 nm: 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 7.00 × 10⁻⁷ = **4.3 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]. At 400 nm: 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 4.00 × 10⁻⁷ = **7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1].

**(b)** 7.5 ÷ 4.3 = **1.75** (equivalently 700 ÷ 400) [1].

**8. (a)** f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 6.00 × 10⁻⁷ = **5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1].

**(b)** The frequency is unchanged in water [1]; λ = 2.25 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.0 × 10¹⁴ = **4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m** (450 nm) [1].

**(c)** The wavelength depends on the medium, because the speed changes while the frequency does not, so a single unambiguous value is the one in free space [1].

**9. (a)** 1500 ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻¹² = **1.5 × 10¹⁵** [1].

**(b)** Radio: f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1500 = **2.0 × 10⁵ Hz** [1]. γ-ray: f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻¹² = **3.0 × 10²⁰ Hz** [1].

**10. (a)** The pulse travels to the aircraft and back, so distance = c × t ÷ 2 [1] = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.20 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2 = **3.0 × 10⁴ m** (30 km) [1].

**(b)** λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.0 × 10⁹ = **0.033 m** [1].

**11.** The approximate wavelength ranges of X-rays and γ-rays **overlap**, and 10⁻¹¹ m lies in both [1]. The name usually depends on how the radiation was **produced**: γ-rays are emitted by nuclei [1].

**12. (a)** f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.7 × 10⁻⁶ [1] = **3.1 × 10¹³ Hz** [1].

**(b)** 9.7 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ = **19** times longer [1].

**(c)** The electric and magnetic fields oscillate **perpendicular to each other** [1] and both **perpendicular to the direction of travel**, so the oscillations are at right angles to the direction of energy transfer [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only one of the two shared properties (Question 1).
- Wavelength values in the wrong order of magnitude, or with no unit (Question 2).
- Saying lower-frequency waves travel more slowly in free space (Question 4).
- Forgetting to convert nm, μm or GHz to SI units before using c = fλ (Questions 6, 7 and 12).
- Assuming the frequency changes when light enters water (Question 8).
- Forgetting to halve the round-trip time for a reflected pulse (Question 10).
