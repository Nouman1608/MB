> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **20.4 Conservation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (conserving forests and fish stocks, reasons for conservation, AI and IVF, and genetic variation); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [conservation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-conservation-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [conservation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-conservation-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is the description of a sustainable resource?

A: a resource that is found in large amounts

B: a resource that is produced as rapidly as it is removed from the environment so that it does not run out

C: a resource that can be recycled after use

D: a resource that is protected by law

**[1]**

**2.** Which conservation method stores a plant species outside its natural habitat so that it can be grown again in the future?

A: captive breeding

B: education

C: protected areas

D: seed banks

**[1]**

**3.** State the **two** resources that the syllabus says can be conserved and managed sustainably. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how pollution of a river can cause a species of fish to become endangered. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State what is meant by a closed season and explain how it helps to conserve fish stocks. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Maintaining resource provision is one reason for conservation programmes. Name **two** resources that ecosystems provide. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A fish stock contains 250 000 tonnes of fish. Reproduction and growth add 40 000 tonnes each year. Boats currently catch 55 000 tonnes each year.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the stock after one year at this rate of fishing. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain whether this fishing is sustainable. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain how quotas and monitoring could be used together to make the fishing sustainable. **[3]**

**8.** A species of flightless bird lives only on one island. Its numbers have fallen sharply. Rats, brought to the island by ships, eat its eggs. Much of its forest has been cleared for farming.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why the bird has become endangered. **[4]**

**(b)** Describe **two** ways in which the bird could be conserved. **[2]**

**9.** A rare species of frog is threatened by a fungal disease in its stream habitat. A zoo begins a captive breeding programme.

**(a)** Describe how a captive breeding programme could help to conserve the frog. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why releasing frogs into the stream may fail unless the habitat is also monitored and protected. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Two zoos in different countries each keep a few individuals of a rare large mammal. The animals rarely mate in captivity.

**(a)** Describe how artificial insemination could be used to breed the animals. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** advantage of artificial insemination over moving an animal between the zoos. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how in vitro fertilisation could be used instead. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why breeders choose unrelated parents. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A forest covers 20 000 hectares. Trees take 40 years to grow large enough to harvest. A timber company wants to harvest the forest sustainably by replanting every area it fells.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum area the company could fell each year. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why felling more than this each year would not be sustainable. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how protected areas and education could also help to conserve this forest. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)*

**(a)** A wild relative of wheat grows in a threatened grassland. It carries an allele for resistance to a fungal disease of wheat. Explain how conserving this grassland is an example of maintaining an ecosystem function. **[2]**

**(b)** The population of a wild goat falls to 12 individuals. Explain the risks to the species of this small population size. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** **D** [1].

**3.** Forests [1]; fish stocks [1].

**4.** Pollutants kill fish / reduce dissolved oxygen so fish die, or reduce their breeding [1]; deaths exceed births, so the population falls to very low numbers [1].

**5.** A period, usually the breeding season, when fishing is not allowed [1]; adult fish can breed, so young fish replace those caught [1].

**6.** Any two from: food; drugs / medicines; fuel; genes [2].

**7. (a)** 250 000 + 40 000 − 55 000 [1] = 235 000 tonnes [1].

**(b)** Not sustainable [1]; more fish are removed (55 000 tonnes) than are produced (40 000 tonnes) each year, so the stock decreases and could run out [1].

**(c)** Monitoring counts / estimates the size of the fish stock each year [1]. A quota limits the mass of fish that can be caught [1]; the quota is set at or below the 40 000 tonnes produced, and adjusted if monitoring shows the stock is falling [1].

**8. (a)** Two reasons, each explained, two marks each:

- introduced species: rats eat the eggs [1], so fewer young hatch and the birth rate falls below the death rate [1]
- habitat destruction: forest cleared for farming [1] removes food, shelter and nesting sites, so fewer birds survive and breed [1]

**(b)** Any two from: monitoring and protecting the bird and its habitat (for example a reserve, removing rats); education of islanders and visitors; captive breeding and release [2].

**9. (a)** Frogs are bred in the zoo, safe from the fungal disease [1]; their numbers are increased [1]; offspring are released into the wild / a protected habitat [1].

**(b)** The fungal disease (or other threat) may still be present in the stream, so released frogs may die [1].

**10. (a)** Sperm is collected from a male in one zoo [1] and inserted into the reproductive tract of a female in the other zoo [1].

**(b)** Any one: frozen sperm is easier and safer to transport than a large animal; the animal is not stressed by moving; a male can father offspring in several zoos [1].

**(c)** Eggs are collected from a female and fertilised with sperm outside the body / in a laboratory [1]; the embryos are placed into the uterus of a female to develop [1].

**(d)** To keep / increase genetic variation in the captive population [1]; related parents are more likely to pass on the same harmful recessive alleles / their offspring are less able to survive disease or change [1].

**11. (a)** 20 000 ÷ 40 = 500 hectares per year [1].

**(b)** Trees would be removed faster than they regrow [1], so the forest area of harvestable trees would decrease and eventually run out [1].

**(c)** Protected areas ban felling in parts of the forest, so habitats and species are kept [1]. Education helps people understand the value of forests and choose timber from sustainable sources [1].

**12. (a)** Ecosystems provide resources, including genes [1]; conserving the wild relative keeps the resistance allele available to breed into wheat, which protects a food supply [1].

**(b)** Genetic variation is reduced / few alleles remain [1]; if a new disease or change in the environment occurs, few or no goats may have alleles to survive it [1]; closely related goats will breed, so harmful recessive alleles are more likely to be inherited together / the species is at greater risk of extinction [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving half of the sustainable resource description.
- Naming a cause of endangerment without its effect on births or deaths.
- Describing captive breeding without release into the wild.
- Confusing AI and IVF.
- Explaining small-population risk without referring to alleles or genetic variation.
