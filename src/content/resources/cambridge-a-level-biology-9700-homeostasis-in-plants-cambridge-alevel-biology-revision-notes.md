These notes condense section **14.2 Homeostasis in plants** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples read the [14.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-plants-cambridge-alevel-biology/); for exam-style questions use the [14.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-plants-cambridge-alevel-biology-practice/).

## Core recall

### The trade-off

- Open stomata: CO₂ diffuses **in** for photosynthesis, but water vapour diffuses **out** in transpiration.
- Regulating aperture balances **CO₂ uptake** against **water loss**.
- Stomata respond to environmental conditions: open in light and when internal CO₂ is low; close in darkness, at high internal CO₂ and in water stress.

### Daily rhythms

- Stomata open by day and close at night, on a cycle of about 24 hours.
- They may start to open before dawn.
- The rhythm continues for a while in constant light or constant darkness, showing an internal timing mechanism.
- Closing at night saves water when no CO₂ is needed.

### Guard cell structure and function

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| Paired cells around a pore | Change shape together to open or close the stoma |
| Thick, inelastic wall next to the pore; thinner outer wall | Outer wall stretches more, so cells bow apart when turgid |
| Radial cellulose microfibrils | Cells lengthen rather than widen |
| Chloroplasts and many mitochondria | Supply ATP for proton pumps |
| Proton pumps and ion channels in membrane | Control ion content and so water potential |
| Large vacuole | Stores the ions taken in |

### Opening versus closing

| Step | Opening | Closing (with ABA) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Light; low internal CO₂ | Water stress; ABA binds to receptors |
| Proton pumps | Active, pump H⁺ out (ATP) | Inhibited |
| Second messenger | Not required by the syllabus | Ca²⁺ enters cytoplasm |
| K⁺ movement | Into guard cells | Out of guard cells |
| Anions | Accumulate inside | Leave through opened channels |
| Water potential of guard cells | Falls | Rises |
| Water movement by osmosis | Into guard cells | Out of guard cells |
| Guard cells | Turgid | Flaccid |
| Stoma | Opens | Closes |

### ABA in five steps

1. Water stress → ABA produced.
2. ABA binds to receptors on guard cell surface membrane.
3. Ca²⁺ enters cytoplasm from outside and from the vacuole; Ca²⁺ is the second messenger.
4. Ion channels open, K⁺ and anions leave; proton pumps inhibited.
5. Water leaves by osmosis → flaccid → stoma closes → transpiration falls.

### Two hormones compared

| | ABA (guard cell) | Glucagon (liver cell) |
|---|---|---|
| Binds to | Surface receptor | Surface receptor |
| Second messenger | Calcium ions | Cyclic AMP |
| Response | Stoma closes | Glycogen broken down |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stoma (plural stomata) | Pore in the epidermis bounded by two guard cells |
| Stomatal aperture | Width of the pore |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from the plant, mainly through stomata |
| Daily rhythm | A cycle of activity with a period of about 24 hours |
| Abscisic acid (ABA) | Plant hormone made in water stress that causes stomatal closure |
| Second messenger | Molecule or ion inside a cell that relays a signal from a receptor (Ca²⁺ here) |
| Proton pump | Membrane protein that uses ATP to move H⁺ across a membrane |
| Turgid / flaccid | Cell pressing firmly against its wall / not pressing on its wall |

## Exam traps

- H⁺ is pumped **out**; K⁺ moves **in** when stomata open.
- Water moves only by **osmosis**: never write that water is "pumped" or "actively transported".
- ABA is the hormone; **Ca²⁺** is the second messenger.
- State water potential directions with signs: −1600 kPa is **lower** than −800 kPa.
- Name the **uneven wall thickness** and the **radial microfibrils** when explaining shape change.
- A rhythm that continues in constant light is the evidence for an internal mechanism; a response that stops in constant light is not a rhythm.
- In "explain the trade-off" questions, give **both** CO₂ uptake and water loss.
- Calculate percentage change from the **control** value, not from the treated value.

## Self-test

1. Which gas diffuses into the leaf through open stomata, and which vapour diffuses out?
2. Why do stomata close at night?
3. What evidence shows that the daily rhythm of stomata is controlled internally?
4. Which wall of a guard cell is thicker?
5. How are the cellulose microfibrils arranged in guard cell walls, and why does it matter?
6. Which ion is pumped out of guard cells when stomata open?
7. Which ion enters guard cells as a result?
8. Explain why water enters guard cells when they take up ions.
9. Name the hormone that closes stomata in water stress.
10. What is the second messenger in the response to this hormone?
11. Give two effects of the second messenger in guard cells.
12. Aperture falls from 7.5 µm to 3.0 µm after hormone treatment. Calculate the percentage decrease.

### Answers

1. CO₂ diffuses in; water vapour diffuses out.
2. No photosynthesis occurs, so no CO₂ is needed, and closing reduces water loss.
3. The opening and closing cycle continues with a period of about 24 hours in constant light (or constant darkness).
4. The wall next to the pore.
5. Radially; the cells can lengthen but not widen, so they bow apart when turgid.
6. Hydrogen ions (protons).
7. Potassium ions.
8. Ions lower the water potential of the guard cells below that of neighbouring cells, so water enters by osmosis.
9. Abscisic acid (ABA).
10. Calcium ions.
11. Any two: open channels so K⁺ and anions leave; inhibit proton pumps; lead to a rise in guard cell water potential.
12. (7.5 − 3.0) ÷ 7.5 × 100 = **60 %**.
