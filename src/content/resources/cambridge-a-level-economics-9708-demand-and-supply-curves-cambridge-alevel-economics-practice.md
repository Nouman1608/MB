## Section A

**1.** Which change would cause a movement along the demand curve for mangoes rather than a shift of it?

A: A rise in consumers' incomes

B: A fall in the price of mangoes

C: A health campaign promoting fruit

D: A rise in the price of papayas, a substitute

**[1]**

**2.** Which change would shift the supply curve of cotton shirts to the left?

A: A fall in the price of raw cotton

B: A subsidy paid to shirt makers

C: A rise in the wages of garment workers

D: A rise in the price of cotton shirts

**[1]**

**3.** Define effective demand. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why market demand is found by adding individual demands horizontally. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how a rise in income could cause the demand curve for a good to shift to the left. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a supply curve usually slopes upwards. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Three households buy eggs in an invented village. Quantity is eggs per week.

| Price (cu per egg) | Household X | Household Y | Household Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | 14 | 10 | 6 |
| 0.30 | 12 | 7 | 3 |
| 0.40 | 10 | 4 | 0 |
| 0.50 | 8 | 1 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate market demand at each price. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what happens to the slope of the market demand curve above a price of 0.40 and why. **[2]**

**(c)** Household Y's income rises and it now buys 3 more eggs at every price. Calculate the new market demand at 0.30 and state which curve has shifted and in which direction. **[2]**

**8.** Demand for an invented brand of headphones is Qd = 900 - 15P, where P is the price in cu and Q is units a month.

**(a)** Calculate the quantity demanded at a price of 40 cu. **[1]**

**(b)** After a celebrity endorsement, demand becomes Qd = 1,050 - 15P. Calculate the quantity demanded at a price of 40 cu and the highest price consumers will now pay for the quantity in (a). **[2]**

**(c)** Explain whether the endorsement causes an extension of demand or an increase in demand. **[2]**

**9.** Twenty identical firms supply bottled juice. Each firm's supply is Qs = 4P - 8, where P is the price in cu per bottle and Q is thousand bottles a week.

**(a)** Write the market supply equation and calculate market supply at a price of 5 cu. **[2]**

**(b)** Five more identical firms enter. Calculate the new market supply at 5 cu and explain what has happened to the supply curve. **[2]**

**10.** For each event, state which curve in the market for petrol cars shifts, the direction, and the determinant involved.

**(a)** A rise in the price of electric cars. **[2]**

**(b)** A rise in the cost of steel. **[2]**

**(c)** Higher interest rates on car loans. **[2]**

**11.** A rise in the price of coffee beans raises the price of coffee in cafes. A newspaper reports: "Coffee demand has fallen sharply as prices rose." Explain, with reference to shifts and movements, why the economist would describe this differently, and identify which curve in the cafe coffee market has shifted. **[4]**

**12.** Explain two reasons why the supply of rice in a country might decrease, other than a change in the price of rice. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve; each other option shifts it.

**2.** **C** [1]. Higher wages raise costs, so less is supplied at each price. Option D is a movement along the supply curve.

**3.** The quantity of a good consumers are **willing and able to buy** [1] **at each price over a given period** [1].

**4.** Market demand is the total quantity all buyers want **at each given price** [1], so the quantities of each buyer are added at the same price, which on a diagram is adding horizontally along the quantity axis [1].

**5.** If the good is an **inferior good** [1], higher income leads consumers to switch to better-quality alternatives, so less is demanded at every price and the curve shifts left [1].

**6.** A higher price makes producing the good **more profitable** [1] and allows firms to cover the **higher costs of producing extra units**, so they are willing to supply more [1].

**7. (a)** 0.20: 30; 0.30: 22; 0.40: 14; 0.50: 9 [2]. (All four correct for 2 marks; two or three correct for 1 mark.)

**(b)** Above 0.40, Household Z buys nothing, so only X and Y respond to price [1]. Market quantity changes by less for each price change, so the curve becomes steeper (a kink at 0.40) [1].

**(c)** New market demand at 0.30 = 12 + 10 + 3 = **25** [1]. Household Y's and therefore the **market demand curve shifts right** [1].

**8. (a)** 900 - 15(40) = 900 - 600 = **300 units** [1].

**(b)** 1,050 - 600 = **450 units** at 40 cu [1]. For 300 units: 300 = 1,050 - 15P, so 15P = 750 and **P = 50 cu** [1].

**(c)** It is an **increase in demand** [1], because a non-price determinant (tastes) changed, so the whole curve shifts right; an extension would need a fall in the headphones' own price [1].

**9. (a)** Qs = 20(4P - 8) = **80P - 160** [1]. At 5 cu: 80(5) - 160 = **240 thousand bottles** [1].

**(b)** 25 firms: Qs = 100P - 200, so at 5 cu supply is **300 thousand bottles** [1]. More is supplied at each price, so the market **supply curve has shifted to the right** [1].

**10. (a)** **Demand** shifts **right** [1]; electric cars are a substitute that has become dearer (price of a related good) [1].

**(b)** **Supply** shifts **left** [1]; steel is an input, so costs of production rise [1].

**(c)** **Demand** shifts **left** [1]; cars are often bought on credit, so the cost of borrowing is a determinant of demand [1].

**11.** The rise in the cost of coffee beans raises cafes' **costs**, so the **supply curve of cafe coffee shifts left** [1]. The higher price then causes a **contraction in quantity demanded**, a movement along the existing demand curve [1]. The **demand curve has not shifted**, because no determinant of demand changed [1]. So the economist would say "quantity demanded fell because supply decreased", not "demand fell" [1].

**12.** First reason: **higher costs of production**, such as dearer fertiliser or fuel [1]; each tonne now costs more to grow, so farmers are willing to supply less at every price and supply shifts left [1]. Second reason: **poor weather or flooding** [1], which cuts yields, so less rice is available at every price [1].

Other creditworthy reasons: a rise in the price of an alternative crop, so farmers switch land away from rice; a new indirect tax on rice; fewer farms, for example because land is sold for housing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a price-driven change in quantity a "change in demand" in Question 11.
- Adding prices instead of quantities in Question 7.
- Missing the inferior-good case in Question 5.
- Naming a determinant without explaining why it changes quantity at each price.
