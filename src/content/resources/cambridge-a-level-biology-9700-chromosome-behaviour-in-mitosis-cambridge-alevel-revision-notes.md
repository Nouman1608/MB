## 5.2.1 The four stages in one grid

| | Chromosomes | Nuclear envelope | Spindle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prophase** | Condense; visible as two sister chromatids joined at centromere | Breaks down at the end; nucleolus disappears | Forms; centrioles move to poles (animal cells) |
| **Metaphase** | Line up on the equator | Absent | Microtubules attach to each centromere |
| **Anaphase** | Centromeres divide; sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles, centromere first (V-shapes) | Absent | Attached microtubules shorten |
| **Telophase** | Reach the poles and uncoil | Re-forms around each set; nucleoli reappear | Breaks down |

**Mnemonic for order:** **P**lease **M**ake **A** **T**ea (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase).

## Cytokinesis and the cell surface membrane

| Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|
| Membrane drawn inwards at the equator (furrow) | Wall prevents furrowing |
| Furrow deepens until two cells form | Vesicles fuse at the equator to form a cell plate |
| Centrioles present; organise the spindle | No centrioles; spindle still forms |
| Cell rounds up during division | Shape held by the wall |

## Counting during mitosis (cell with 4 chromosomes in G1)

| Stage | Chromosomes | Chromatids |
|---|---|---|
| Prophase | 4 | 8 |
| Metaphase | 4 | 8 |
| Anaphase | 8 (two groups of 4) | 8, each now counted as a chromosome |
| Telophase | 4 at each pole | 4 at each pole |

## 5.2.2 Identification checklist

1. Round nucleus, nucleolus, no threads → **interphase**.
2. Threads or thick chromosomes scattered; nucleus outline may remain → **prophase**.
3. Chromosomes in one line across the middle → **metaphase**.
4. Two groups of V-shapes moving apart → **anaphase**.
5. Two dense groups at the poles; furrow or cell plate → **telophase**.

**Always** give the stage **and** the feature that identifies it.

### Common confusions

| Pair | Decider |
|---|---|
| Prophase / metaphase | Scattered or in one line? |
| Metaphase / anaphase | One line or two groups moving apart? |
| Anaphase / telophase | Moving or already at the poles and clumped? |
| Telophase / interphase | Dense irregular groups or round nuclei with nucleoli? |

## Root tip squash in steps

1. Cut a growing root tip (garlic or onion); the dividing region lies just behind the tip.
2. Warm in dilute hydrochloric acid: separates cells, stops division.
3. Stain chromosomes with a DNA stain.
4. Squash under a coverslip into a single layer.
5. Find dividing cells at low power; identify stages at high power.

## Drawing rules

- Sharp pencil, single continuous lines, no shading.
- Draw what is seen, large enough to show detail.
- Ruled label lines that do not cross.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Prophase | Chromosomes condense; envelope breaks down; spindle forms |
| Metaphase | Chromosomes line up on the equator |
| Anaphase | Sister chromatids separate to opposite poles |
| Telophase | Chromosomes at poles uncoil; envelopes re-form |
| Spindle | Structure of microtubules that moves chromosomes |
| Centriole | Microtubule structure at each pole in animal cells |
| Equator | Plane midway between the poles |
| Cell plate | Structure from fused vesicles that divides a plant cell |
| Photomicrograph | Photograph taken through a microscope |

## Exam traps

- The nuclear envelope breaks down at the **end** of prophase.
- In anaphase, **sister chromatids** separate, not "chromosomes".
- Metaphase chromosomes are on the spindle equator, not inside a nucleus.
- Plant cells lack centrioles but still form a spindle.
- Plant cytokinesis uses a cell plate, not a furrow.
- A stage named without evidence may not score.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four stages of mitosis in order.
2. In which stage does the nuclear envelope break down?
3. Describe the position of chromosomes in metaphase.
4. What happens to the centromeres at the start of anaphase?
5. Why do chromatids look V-shaped in anaphase?
6. What happens to the spindle in telophase?
7. How does cytokinesis differ in plant and animal cells?
8. Which structure organises the spindle in animal cells but is absent from plant cells?
9. A cell in metaphase has 16 chromatids. How many chromosomes does each daughter cell receive?
10. Why are root tips used to observe mitosis?
11. What is the purpose of warming root tips in hydrochloric acid?
12. A cell shows two dense groups of chromosomes at opposite ends and a line forming across the middle. Name the stage and give the evidence.

### Answers

1. Prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
2. At the end of prophase.
3. In a single line across the equator of the spindle, attached by centromeres.
4. They divide, so sister chromatids separate.
5. The centromere is pulled ahead by the shortening microtubules and the arms trail behind.
6. It breaks down.
7. Animal: membrane furrows inwards. Plant: vesicles fuse at the equator to form a cell plate and a new wall.
8. Centrioles.
9. 8.
10. Cells just behind the tip divide frequently, so many stages are visible.
11. To separate the cells so they spread when squashed (and to stop division).
12. Telophase: chromosomes have reached the poles, and a cell plate (or furrow) is forming.
