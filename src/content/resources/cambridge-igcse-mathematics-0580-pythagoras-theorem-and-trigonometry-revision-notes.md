These notes condense subtopic **E6.6 Pythagoras’ theorem and trigonometry in 3D** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is **Extended only**; C6.6 reads "Extended content only". For full explanations and worked examples, read the [3D trigonometry study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry/); for exam-style questions, use the [3D trigonometry practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry-practice/).

## What the syllabus says

Carry out calculations and solve problems in three dimensions using Pythagoras’ theorem and trigonometry, **including calculating the angle between a line and a plane**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Space diagonal | A line through the inside of a cuboid joining opposite corners, such as AG |
| Face diagonal | A diagonal of one face, such as AC on the base |
| Perpendicular height | The vertical distance from a vertex to the base, meeting it at 90° |
| Projection | The "shadow" of a line on a plane, from dropping a perpendicular |
| Angle between a line and a plane | The angle between the line and its projection on the plane |
| Slant height (cone) | Distance from the apex to the edge of the base along the surface |

## The routine

```
1. Name the length or angle (use vertex letters).
2. Find a right angle: vertical edge meets base; height meets base.
3. Draw that triangle FLAT, label sides, mark the 90°.
4. Missing side? Get it from another triangle first (often a base diagonal).
5. Solve with Pythagoras / SOH CAH TOA. Keep values unrounded.
```

## Standard solids

| Solid | Triangle to use | Key lengths |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid a × b × c | base triangle, then vertical triangle | base diagonal √(a² + b²); space diagonal √(a² + b² + c²) |
| Cube of side s | as cuboid | face diagonal s√2; space diagonal s√3 |
| Pyramid on rectangle | vertical triangle through the apex and centre M | MA = half the base diagonal; MN = half a side for an edge midpoint N |
| Cone | vertical cross-section through the apex | r² + h² = l² |
| Prism or wedge | base triangle, then vertical triangle | find the projection on the base first |

## Angle between a line and a plane

```
Line AG meets plane ABCD at A.
G is directly above C, so the projection is AC.
Angle wanted = angle GAC.
tan(GAC) = CG / AC
```

Check: the right angle is always where the perpendicular meets the plane (at C here), never at A.

## A two-triangle chain in brief

Square-based pyramid, base side 8 cm, vertex V 7 cm above the centre M. Find the angle between edge VA and the base.

```
Triangle 1 (base, flat):   AC = √(8² + 8²) = 8√2,  so MA = 4√2 = 5.656...
Triangle 2 (vertical):     right angle at M,  tan θ = VM / MA = 7 / 5.656...
                           θ = 51.05...° → 51.1°
```

Triangle 1 supplies the projection MA; triangle 2 supplies the angle. Most 3D questions follow this two-step pattern.

## Exam traps

- Measuring the angle to a base edge (AB) instead of the projection (AC).
- Using the whole base diagonal of a pyramid instead of half of it.
- Treating a slant edge as the perpendicular height.
- Assuming the angle from a vertex to an edge midpoint equals the angle to a corner. They use different projections.
- Rounding the base diagonal early.
- Giving an angle such as 13° when 1 decimal place is expected: write 13.0°.

## Self-test

1. (calculator) Find the space diagonal of a cuboid 6 cm by 8 cm by 5 cm.
2. (calculator) Find the angle between that diagonal and the 6 cm by 8 cm base.
3. (non-calculator) Find the space diagonal of a cuboid 2 cm by 3 cm by 6 cm.
4. (non-calculator) Write down the exact length of a face diagonal of a cube of side 5 cm.
5. (calculator) A square-based pyramid has base edges 6 cm and height 4 cm. Find the length of a sloping edge.
6. (calculator) Find the angle between a sloping edge of the pyramid in question 5 and its base.
7. (calculator) A cone has radius 3 cm and slant height 7 cm. Find its vertical height.
8. (calculator) Find the angle between the slant edge of the cone in question 7 and its base.
9. In cuboid ABCDEFGH (E above A, F above B, G above C, H above D), name the angle between BH and the base ABCD.
10. Name the angle between EC and the base ABCD in the same cuboid.
11. A vertical post 6 m tall stands on level ground. A point on the ground is 8 m from its foot. Find the distance from that point to the top of the post.
12. Why is the angle between AG and the base different from the angle between AG and the face ABFE in a non-cube cuboid?

### Answers

1. √(36 + 64 + 25) = √125 = **11.2 cm**
2. Base diagonal 10 cm; tan θ = 5 ÷ 10, **θ = 26.6°**
3. √(4 + 9 + 36) = √49 = **7 cm**
4. **5√2 cm**
5. Half diagonal = 3√2 = 4.242…; edge = √(18 + 16) = √34 = **5.83 cm**
6. tan θ = 4 ÷ 3√2, **θ = 43.3°**
7. √(49 − 9) = √40 = **6.32 cm**
8. cos θ = 3 ÷ 7, **θ = 64.6°**
9. **Angle HBD** (H is above D, so the projection of BH is BD)
10. **Angle ECA** (E is above A, so the projection of EC is CA)
11. √(36 + 64) = **10 m**
12. The projections are different lines (AC on the base, AF on the face ABFE), with different lengths, so the right-angled triangles are different.
