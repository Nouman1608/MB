**This practice set covers topic 1.1 Schizophrenia in Specialist Option 1, Clinical Psychology, of Cambridge International A Level Psychology 9990 (syllabus for 2024 to 2026).** Section A uses Paper 3 style short answers and a structured essay. Section B uses Paper 4 style questions on the key study, research methods and planning. All scenarios are fictional.

## Section A: Paper 3 style questions

**1.** Outline the difference between positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia, with an example of each. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a *delusion of control*. **[2]**

**3.** Outline **one** piece of evidence for the dopamine hypothesis of schizophrenia. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the genetic explanation cannot fully explain schizophrenia, using twin evidence. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Describe cognitive-behavioural therapy as a treatment for schizophrenia. **[6]**

**5. (b)** Evaluate cognitive-behavioural therapy as a treatment for schizophrenia, including a discussion of reductionism versus holism. **[10]**

## Section B: Paper 4 style questions

**6. (a)** Freeman et al. (2003) used a virtual reality library. Explain why a virtual environment was more suitable than a real library for investigating persecutory ideation. **[2]**

**6. (b)** Describe the sample used by Freeman et al. and explain **one** limitation it creates. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** strength and **one** weakness of using a case study, such as the Genain quadruplets, to study schizophrenia. **[4]**

**8.** Amira is a psychiatrist. Her patient Dev has been prescribed a typical antipsychotic, but he has developed involuntary movements of his face and tongue.

**(a)** Identify the side effect Dev has developed. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest an alternative biological treatment and explain why it may be more suitable. **[3]**

**9.** A psychologist wants to repeat Freeman et al.'s study with people diagnosed with schizophrenia.

**(a)** Describe **two** ethical issues she would need to consider. **[4]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** quantitative measure she could use for persecutory thoughts. **[2]**

**10.** Sensky et al. (2000) followed patients up nine months after therapy ended. Explain why a longitudinal follow-up was important in this study. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Up to 3 marks. Positive symptoms are additions to normal experience (1), for example auditory hallucinations such as hearing voices (1). Negative symptoms are losses of normal functioning, for example avolition, a lack of motivation to start activities (1).

**2.** Up to 2 marks. A fixed false belief that one's thoughts, feelings or actions are being controlled by an outside force (1), for example believing that a radio signal makes one's arms move (1).

**3.** Up to 2 marks. For example: drugs that increase dopamine activity, such as amphetamines, can produce psychotic symptoms in people without schizophrenia (1), which suggests that excess dopamine can cause positive symptoms (1). Or: antipsychotics that block D2 receptors reduce positive symptoms.

**4.** Up to 3 marks. Identical twins share all their genes (1), so if genes alone caused schizophrenia, concordance would be 100% (1). Gottesman (1991) reported a lifetime risk of about 48% for an identical twin, so environmental factors must also contribute (1).

**5. (a)** Up to 6 marks for a detailed description. CBT assumes that distressing symptoms are maintained by the way experiences are interpreted (1). The therapist and patient work collaboratively, first building trust and agreeing goals (1). The patient learns to examine the evidence for and against a delusional belief (1) and considers alternative explanations, for example that a voice could be inner speech (1). Behavioural experiments test beliefs in practice (1). Coping strategies for voices, such as distraction or talking back, are taught, and the therapy often lasts several months alongside medication (1).

**5. (b)** Level-based: 9–10 marks for a detailed, balanced evaluation, reductionism developed, and a conclusion. Indicative content:

- *Evidence*: Sensky et al. (2000) found CBT benefits lasted at follow-up, unlike befriending, for patients whose symptoms resisted medication.
- *Reductionism versus holism*: CBT considers thoughts, feelings and behaviour, and the patient's life, which is more holistic than antipsychotics alone; but it can still be reductionist if it treats symptoms as just faulty thinking and ignores biological causes.
- *Suitability*: it needs engagement and some insight, so it may be less suitable for acutely ill patients or those with severe negative symptoms.
- *Side effects*: no physical side effects, which is an advantage over drugs and ECT.
- *Practical*: requires trained therapists over many sessions, which is costly.
- *Conclusion*: an effective addition to medication rather than a replacement.

**6. (a)** Up to 2 marks. The VR characters could be programmed to behave neutrally (1), so any persecutory thoughts must be unfounded, and every participant met exactly the same social situation (1).

**6. (b)** Up to 3 marks. 24 participants (1) from a non-clinical population, not people with schizophrenia (1). The findings may not generalise to people with persecutory delusions, whose thoughts are more severe and persistent (1).

**7.** 2 marks per point. Strength: rich, detailed data collected over many years (1), so the development of the disorder in genetically identical people could be traced, showing the role of environment (1). Weakness: the sisters were one unusual family (1), so findings cannot be generalised to everyone with schizophrenia (1).

**8.** **(a)** 1 mark: tardive dyskinesia.

**(b)** Up to 3 marks. An atypical antipsychotic such as clozapine (1). It acts on serotonin as well as dopamine receptors (1) and carries a lower risk of movement side effects, so Dev's symptoms should be controlled with less risk of further tardive dyskinesia; he would need regular blood monitoring (1).

**9.** **(a)** 2 marks each for two issues explained. Protection from harm: entering a VR scene with strangers could increase paranoia and distress (1), so the session should be brief, monitored and stoppable at any time (1). Valid consent: some patients may have limited capacity during an acute episode (1), so consent should be sought when they are well, with a clinician's assessment of capacity (1). Accept right to withdraw or debriefing.

**(b)** Up to 2 marks. For example: a rating scale on which participants rate agreement with statements such as "Some of the people in the library wanted to harm me" from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much) (1), with the total giving a persecution score (1).

**10.** Up to 2 marks. Both groups had improved by the end of therapy, so a difference only appeared at follow-up (1); without it, the lasting advantage of CBT over befriending would not have been detected (1).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much detail do I need on the Genain quadruplets?**
Enough to explain why they are a case study (four genetically identical sisters studied in depth) and what they suggest about nature and nurture.

**Will Freeman et al. be tested on Paper 3 or Paper 4?**
Key studies are the main focus of Paper 4 Section A, but they also make strong evidence in Paper 3 evaluations.
