This guide covers **Paper 1 Language, the Individual and Society**,
one of two examined papers in AQA A-level English Language (7702), a
linear qualification first teaching September 2015. Methods of
language analysis are integrated into each paper rather than assessed
as a stand-alone topic, so analytical technique is developed alongside
content throughout.

## Where this fits in 7702

Paper 1 pairs the analysis of real texts with the study of how
children acquire language -- two distinct strands united by a shared
focus on how individuals use and develop language. It complements
Paper 2 (Language Diversity and Change), which shifts the focus from
the individual to language variation across society, and the
Non-exam Assessment (Language in Action).

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA A-LEVEL ENGLISH LANGUAGE (7702) — PAPER 1 LANGUAGE, THE
INDIVIDUAL AND SOCIETY**

Paper 1 is a 2 hour 30 minute written exam worth 100 marks (40% of the
A-level), split into two sections:

- Section A: Textual Variations and Representations — two texts, one
contemporary and one older, linked by topic or theme; one question
analysing the first text (25 marks), one analysing the second (25
marks), and one comparing the two (20 marks)
- Section B: Children's Language Development (ages 0-11) — a
discursive essay on children's language development, with a choice of
two questions, where the supporting data focuses on spoken, written or
multimodal language (30 marks)

## How to approach it

Section A rewards close, precise linguistic analysis of unseen texts
-- practise applying a consistent analytical framework (for example,
lexis, grammar, phonology, pragmatics and discourse) to a range of
text types under timed conditions, since the paper presents texts
students will not have seen before. Section B benefits from knowing
key child language acquisition theories and stages well enough to
apply them to transcript data, not just recall them -- practise
annotating real or sample child speech transcripts and linking
features you notice back to named theories. Because both sections sit
in the same paper, build revision time for both rather than
over-preparing for one at the expense of the other.
