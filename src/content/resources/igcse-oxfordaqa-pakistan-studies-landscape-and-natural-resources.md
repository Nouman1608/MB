This guide covers **3.2.1 The Landscape and Natural Resources of
Pakistan**, the compulsory topic that opens Paper 2 (The Human and
Physical Geography of Pakistan) in OxfordAQA International GCSE
Pakistan Studies (9236), Version 1.9. Unlike Sections 2.3 and 2.4 of
this paper, which are optional, 3.2.1 is examined for every candidate
in Paper 2, Section A.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE PAKISTAN STUDIES (9236) — 3.2.1 THE LANDSCAPE AND NATURAL RESOURCES OF PAKISTAN**

- 3.2.1.1 Physical landscape — distribution and characteristics of
major biomes in Pakistan, including deserts, forests and mangroves;
distribution and characteristics of the landscape, including mountains,
plateaus, rivers, flood plains, deserts, and coastal areas; formation,
processes and landforms of rivers and glaciers; human and physical
causes and impacts of physical hazards including earthquakes, tsunamis
and floods
- 3.2.1.2 Climate — seasonal and regional differences in Pakistan's
climate and the factors affecting these differences; the influence of
the monsoon and western disturbances on the environment and people;
causes of tropical cyclones and their impacts; natural and human causes
of climate change and its impacts on people, the economy and the
environment
- 3.2.1.3 Natural resources — distribution of natural resources
including minerals, forests and water, and factors affecting their use;
distribution of mineral resources including metallic and non-metallic
minerals, and factors affecting their use; processes and environmental
impacts of mineral extraction; the importance of natural resources for
economic development
- 3.2.1.4 Energy and water — changes in the production and consumption
of non-renewable (coal, natural gas, oil, nuclear) and renewable
(hydel, solar, wind, tidal, bioenergy, geothermal) energy resources;
advantages and disadvantages of non-renewable versus renewable energy
resources for the economy and environment; uses of water as a natural
resource and the management of water supplies

## How to approach it

This section rewards candidates who can connect physical processes to
human consequences rather than describing landscape features in
isolation. For 3.2.1.1, don't just list landform types — be ready to
explain how a named hazard (an earthquake, flood, or glacial process)
has both physical causes (tectonic setting, monsoon rainfall, glacial
melt) and human impacts (settlement damage, displacement, economic
loss), since this cause-and-impact structure is exactly what the
specification's wording ("human and physical causes and impacts")
signals will be tested.

For 3.2.1.2, keep the monsoon and western disturbances distinct: the
monsoon brings the bulk of summer rainfall from the southwest, while
western disturbances are winter weather systems bringing rain and snow
from the Mediterranean region — conflating the two is a common source
of lost marks when explaining regional rainfall differences.

For 3.2.1.3 and 3.2.1.4, practise linking a specific named resource
(for example natural gas, hydel power, or a named mineral) to both its
distribution within Pakistan and its importance for economic
development, since purely descriptive answers about where a resource is
found score lower than answers that also explain why its location and
use matter.

## Worked example: renewable vs non-renewable energy

A question asks candidates to compare the advantages and disadvantages
of renewable and non-renewable energy resources for Pakistan.

```
Non-renewable (e.g. natural gas):
  Advantage:     established infrastructure and reliable, high-output
                 supply for industry and households
  Disadvantage:  finite reserves, import dependency once domestic
                 reserves decline, and environmental/climate impact

Renewable (e.g. hydel power):
  Advantage:     abundant potential given Pakistan's river systems and
                 glacial meltwater, with lower long-term running costs
  Disadvantage:  high upfront construction cost, dependency on seasonal
                 river flow, and potential environmental disruption from
                 dam construction
```

Structuring the answer resource-by-resource with a clear advantage and
disadvantage for each, rather than making general claims about
"renewables are better," matches how this content is assessed.

## Common mistakes

Describing landforms or biomes without linking them to the hazards or
resources associated with them. Treating the monsoon and western
disturbances as the same weather system. Discussing natural resources
only in terms of where they are found, without explaining their
importance for economic development, which the specification explicitly
requires. Giving one-sided answers on renewable versus non-renewable
energy that ignore genuine disadvantages of the "preferred" option.

## Key terms to define precisely

Be ready to define these accurately in your own words rather than
relying on vague description: **biome** (a large-scale ecological
community shaped by climate, such as desert, forest or mangrove),
**flood plain** (the low-lying land alongside a river that is subject
to periodic flooding and typically has fertile, deposited soil),
**western disturbance** (a winter weather system originating over the
Mediterranean and Caspian regions that brings rain and snow to
northern and western Pakistan), **non-renewable resource** (a resource
such as coal, gas or oil that exists in a finite supply and cannot be
replenished within a human timescale), and **renewable resource** (a
resource such as solar, wind or hydel power that is naturally
replenished and does not run out with use). Confusing a flood plain
with a delta, or describing all of Pakistan's rainfall as monsoon-driven
without acknowledging the separate contribution of western
disturbances in winter, are both common inaccuracies that cost marks.

## Quick revision checklist

- Learn Pakistan's major biomes and landscape types with their
distribution.
- Prepare one named physical hazard with both causes and human impacts.
- Distinguish the monsoon from western disturbances as separate causes
of regional climate variation.
- Link at least one named mineral, forest or water resource to its
importance for economic development.
- Prepare a balanced advantage/disadvantage comparison of one renewable
and one non-renewable energy resource.
