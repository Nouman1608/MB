Condensed recall for **7.2 Ethical considerations**: the need for an ethical framework; the fundamental principles of integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour; the significance to stakeholders and society. People and figures below are invented.

## Why a framework is needed

- Users rely on figures they **cannot check** themselves.
- Accountants face **pressure** to make profit look higher or lower.
- Many figures need **judgement** (depreciation, inventory, allowances).
- Accountants hold **confidential** information.
- Principles set **in advance** apply to everyone, however senior the person asking.

## Key terms: the five principles

| Principle | Memory line | Red-flag words in a question |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | Honest and straightforward; no misleading figures | "make it look", "leave it out", "record a sale that has not happened" |
| Objectivity | No bias, conflict of interest or undue influence | "bonus", "relative", "friend", "the director insists" |
| Professional competence and due care | Have the skill, keep it current, take care | "has never prepared", "no time to check", "out-of-date rules" |
| Confidentiality | Do not disclose or use work information without authority | "a friend asks", "a rival wants to know", "new employer" |
| Professional behaviour | Obey the law; do not discredit the profession | "pay less tax", "hide", "exaggerate qualifications" |

**Other terms:** *ethical framework*, a set of agreed principles guiding behaviour; *stakeholder*, anyone affected by the business's figures; *conflict of interest*, a personal interest that could influence professional judgement.

## Distinctions that earn marks

- **Integrity vs objectivity.** Integrity: is it honest? Objectivity: is the judgement free from influence?
- **Competence vs due care.** Competence: having the skill. Due care: applying it carefully and on time.
- **Confidentiality limits.** Disclosure is allowed with proper authority, such as the client's permission or a legal requirement. Using information for personal gain is also a breach.
- **Professional behaviour vs integrity.** Breaking the law or damaging the profession's reputation points to professional behaviour, even if integrity is breached too.

## Scenario method

1. **Facts**: what is asked, by whom, and why it is a problem.
2. **Principle**: name the one each fact threatens.
3. **Consequence**: who is harmed, with figures if given.
4. **Action**: refuse; explain the correct treatment; ask for help, time or supervision; withdraw from the decision.

**Quick figures.** Draft profit $52,000. An unrecorded December repair bill of $2,600 is moved into next year to impress a lender. Correct profit is $49,400, so profit is overstated by 2,600 ÷ 49,400 × 100 = **5.26%**. Principles: integrity (misleading figures); professional behaviour (misleading a lender). Concept: matching.

## Significance to stakeholders and society

| Group | Benefit of ethical accounting |
|---|---|
| Owners and shareholders | Decide on true results |
| Lenders and banks | Lend to businesses that can repay |
| Suppliers | Give credit on reliable figures |
| Employees | Jobs and pay based on the real position |
| Customers | Rely on the business continuing |
| Tax authorities | Collect the correct tax |
| Society | Trust in financial information; money flows to genuinely successful businesses; tax for public services; fewer sudden collapses |

## Exam traps

- **One reason for two principles.** Give each its own link to the facts.
- **Society means beyond the business.** Mention trust, tax and public services, not just the owner.
- **Confidentiality is not absolute.** Proper authority allows disclosure.
- **Do not stop at the principle.** "Advise" needs an action.
- **Use the concepts too.** Moving a December bill into next year breaks matching as well as integrity.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why accounting needs an ethical framework.
2. Name the five fundamental principles.
3. A book-keeper is offered a bonus based on profit and chooses a lower allowance for irrecoverable debts. Which principle is most threatened?
4. An accountant uses an old version of the tax rules because he has not kept up to date. Which principle is breached?
5. Distinguish between integrity and objectivity.
6. When may confidential information be disclosed?
7. An employee tells a friend the business's customer list. Which principle is breached?
8. An owner asks the accountant to leave cash sales out of the records to pay less tax. Which two principles are breached?
9. Draft profit is $40,000; an unrecorded expense of $1,600 belongs to this year. If it is left out, by what percentage is profit overstated?
10. Give two ways society benefits when accountants apply the ethical framework.
11. What should a trainee do if asked to prepare accounts she has not been trained to prepare?
12. Why might a supplier be harmed if a customer's accounts are deliberately misleading?
