This study guide covers **section 1.9, Standard form**, in topic 1 (Numbers and the number system) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017). ## What standard form is

A number in standard form has two parts:

- a number **a** with **1 ≤ a < 10**: exactly one non-zero digit before the point;
- a power of 10, **10ⁿ**, where n is an integer (positive, negative or zero).

The power tells you how many places the digits have moved. Large numbers have positive n; numbers between 0 and 1 have negative n.

## Converting to and from standard form

**Ordinary number → standard form.** Place the point after the first non-zero digit, then count how many places it moved.

```
52 700 000   -> 5.27, point moved 7 places left   -> 5.27 × 10⁷
0.000 038    -> 3.8, point moved 5 places right   -> 3.8 × 10⁻⁵
```

**Standard form → ordinary number.** Move the point n places: right for positive n, left for negative n.

```
9.06 × 10⁵   = 906 000
4.1 × 10⁻³   = 0.0041
```

**Not quite standard form.** 34 × 10⁵ and 0.62 × 10⁻² have the right shape but the wrong first number. Adjust a and the power in opposite directions:

```
34 × 10⁵     = 3.4 × 10¹ × 10⁵  = 3.4 × 10⁶
0.62 × 10⁻²  = 6.2 × 10⁻¹ × 10⁻² = 6.2 × 10⁻³
```

If a gets smaller, n gets bigger, and the reverse.

## Interpreting and ordering

To compare numbers in standard form, compare the powers first: a bigger power means a bigger number (for positive numbers). Only if the powers are equal do you compare the a values.

**Worked example 1.** Write in order of size, smallest first: 0.0041, 3.9 × 10⁻², 4.5 × 10⁻³, 0.000 47.

```
0.0041     = 4.1 × 10⁻³
3.9 × 10⁻² 
4.5 × 10⁻³
0.000 47   = 4.7 × 10⁻⁴
order: 0.000 47, 0.0041, 4.5 × 10⁻³, 3.9 × 10⁻²
```

Remember that −4 is less than −3, so 4.7 × 10⁻⁴ is smaller than any number × 10⁻³.

## Multiplying and dividing

Group the a values and the powers of 10, then use the index laws on the powers. Finish by checking the answer is in standard form.

**Worked example 2.** Work out (a) (7 × 10⁴) × (6 × 10⁻⁷) and (b) (2.1 × 10³) ÷ (7 × 10⁻⁵).

```
(a) 7 × 6 = 42;   10⁴ × 10⁻⁷ = 10⁻³
    42 × 10⁻³ = 4.2 × 10⁻²

(b) 2.1 ÷ 7 = 0.3;   10³ ÷ 10⁻⁵ = 10⁸
    0.3 × 10⁸ = 3 × 10⁷
```

## Adding and subtracting

Powers of 10 do not combine when adding. Either convert to ordinary numbers or rewrite both with the same power.

```
6.2 × 10⁵ + 4.5 × 10⁴ = 620 000 + 45 000 = 665 000 = 6.65 × 10⁵
or: 6.2 × 10⁵ + 0.45 × 10⁵ = 6.65 × 10⁵
```

## Using a calculator

Enter standard form with the ×10ˣ (or EXP) key: 4.7 × 10⁸ is typed as 4.7 ×10ˣ 8, with no extra "× 10". Put brackets round each number when squaring or dividing, for example (4.7 × 10⁸)² ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻³) = 8.836 × 10¹⁹.

Some calculators show this result as 8.836 followed by a letter E and the power 19; it means 8.836 × 10¹⁹. Always write the answer in proper notation, as a × 10ⁿ: the calculator display is not accepted as standard form.

## Problems involving standard form (Higher tier only)

Higher questions put standard form into context: science, populations, data storage, money. Write each step with its unit.

**Worked example 3.** Use the values: mass of the Earth 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, mass of the Moon 7.35 × 10²² kg. How many times heavier is the Earth than the Moon?

```
(5.97 × 10²⁴) ÷ (7.35 × 10²²) = 0.8122… × 10² = 81.2 (3 s.f.)
```

**Worked example 4.** A country has a population of 6.7 × 10⁷ and an area of 2.4 × 10⁵ km². Find the population density.

```
(6.7 × 10⁷) ÷ (2.4 × 10⁵) = 279.16… ≈ 279 people per km²
```

A sensible answer here is an ordinary number, because the question does not ask for standard form.

**Worked example 5.** Light travels at 3.0 × 10⁸ m per second. Taking one year as 3.15 × 10⁷ seconds, find how far light travels in a year.

```
(3.0 × 10⁸) × (3.15 × 10⁷) = 9.45 × 10¹⁵ m
```

**Roots and powers.** Make the power of 10 even before a square root: √(3.6 × 10⁷) = √(36 × 10⁶) = 6 × 10³. For a letter power, use the index laws: if x = 2 × 10ᵏ, then x³ = 8 × 10³ᵏ.

## Common mistakes

- Writing 52.7 × 10⁶ and calling it standard form: a must be less than 10.
- Moving the point the wrong way for negative powers: 4.1 × 10⁻³ is 0.0041, not 4100.
- Adding the powers when adding numbers: 6 × 10⁵ + 4 × 10⁵ = 10 × 10⁵ = 1 × 10⁶, not 10 × 10¹⁰.
- Leaving 0.3 × 10⁸ unadjusted at the end of a division.
- Typing "× 10 ×10ˣ 8" on a calculator, which gives a value ten times too big.
- Comparing only the a values, so 9 × 10³ is thought bigger than 2 × 10⁵.

## Exam technique

- **Give your answer in standard form**: the final answer must have 1 ≤ a < 10; an equivalent ordinary number may lose the mark.
- **Write as an ordinary number**: write out every zero, grouping digits in threes.
- **Work out** with standard form: show the separate a and power steps so method marks can be given.
- **Give your answer to 3 significant figures**: round a, not the power.
- In context questions, keep units and say what the number means, for example "about 81 times heavier".
