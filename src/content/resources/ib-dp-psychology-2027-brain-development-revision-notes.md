These notes condense the **brain development** area of the human development context (SL and HL, first assessment 2027). The guide gives three learning objectives: the role of brain maturation, the extent to which critical periods explain development, and the role of neuroplasticity. They sit under models of development, where you study one or more of cognitive, social, moral or language development. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-brain-development/), then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-brain-development-practice/).

## The three areas at a glance

| Area | Learning objective (summarised) | Key idea | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain maturation | Role in human development | Synaptogenesis, pruning, myelination; prefrontal cortex matures into the twenties | Longitudinal MRI studies |
| Critical periods | The extent to which they explain development | Window when experience is needed; sensitive period is weaker | Hubel and Wiesel (kittens); Genie; Johnson and Newport (1989); English and Romanian Adoptees study |
| Neuroplasticity | Role in human development | Brain changes with experience | Maguire et al. (2000); Draganski et al. (2004) |

## Key definitions

- **Maturation:** ordered biological change with age, mostly driven by genes.
- **Synaptogenesis:** formation of synapses.
- **Synaptic pruning:** removal of unused connections.
- **Myelination:** coating of axons with myelin, which speeds up signals.
- **Prefrontal cortex:** region for planning, judgement and impulse control.
- **Critical period:** a limited window when experience is needed for normal development.
- **Sensitive period:** a window of heightened responsiveness, where later development is still possible.
- **Neuroplasticity:** the brain's capacity to change structure and function with experience.
- **Natural experiment:** a real-world situation used as if it were an experiment.
- **Confounding variable:** an uncontrolled factor that could explain the result.

## Brain maturation

- Different regions mature at different rates: sensory and motor early, prefrontal late (into the early twenties).
- **Cognitive:** planning and abstract thinking improve with prefrontal maturation.
- **Social and moral:** reward systems mature before control systems, which may help explain greater risk-taking with peers in adolescence.
- **Language:** speech production depends on maturing motor and language areas.
- **Evaluate:** objective and longitudinal; correlational; Western samples; reductionist; experience also shapes maturation.
- **Concepts:** causality, measurement, perspective.

## Critical periods

| Evidence | Finding | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Hubel and Wiesel (kittens) | Early covering of one eye led to lasting loss of cortical response for that eye | Strong causal control; animals and deprivation raise ethical issues |
| Genie (Curtiss and colleagues) | Isolated until about 13, learned words but limited grammar | Case study confounded by abuse and malnutrition; single case |
| Johnson and Newport (1989) | Earlier arrival in the US linked to better English grammar in adulthood | Differences in motivation, education and exposure among arrivals |
| English and Romanian Adoptees study | Longer early deprivation linked to more lasting problems; many children recovered | Natural experiment; wide spread of outcomes; supports sensitive period |

- **Judgement on "the extent to which":** strong for some sensory functions and parts of language; for most human abilities the evidence supports sensitive periods with partial recovery.
- **Limits:** no true experiments on humans for ethical reasons.
- **Concepts:** causality, responsibility, change, bias.

## Neuroplasticity

- **Maguire et al. (2000):** taxi drivers had a larger posterior hippocampus, related to years of driving. Correlational.
- **Draganski et al. (2004):** adults learning to juggle showed grey matter increases that partly reversed when practice stopped. Longitudinal with a control group.
- **Environmental enrichment:** rats in enriched cages developed a thicker cortex; generalisation to humans is cautious.
- **Recovery after early brain injury:** children often recover language better than adults.
- **Application:** early intervention, teaching and practice can change development.
- **Evaluate:** plasticity may be maladaptive; imaging changes are small; animal studies have ethical issues.

## Linking the three ideas

- Maturation sets a timetable.
- Sensitive periods show when experience matters most.
- Plasticity shows how experience changes the brain and why later change is still possible.
- Together they illustrate the **interaction** of nature and nurture, which a sociocultural account also needs.

## Concept links to prepare

- **Causality:** imaging and case studies are correlational; experiments are limited by ethics.
- **Change:** development as maturation and as experience-driven change; can early deficits be reversed?
- **Measurement:** MRI, longitudinal and cross-sectional designs.
- **Bias:** small samples, researcher interpretation, Western samples.
- **Perspective:** biological and sociocultural accounts of the same development.
- **Responsibility:** deprivation research, consent for children, use of animals.

## A model answer plan (Section B style)

Scenario: a child adopted at age 3 after early neglect struggles with language, and then improves after intensive help.
1. Name the terms: sensitive period and neuroplasticity.
2. Apply: early neglect during a sensitive period limited language input; improvement shows plasticity.
3. Add the qualification: outcomes vary, and many factors (nutrition, attachment) were involved.

## Common mistakes

- Treating critical and sensitive periods as the same.
- Presenting Genie as proof without noting confounds.
- Saying that imaging "proves" a brain change causes behaviour.
- Describing brain structures with no link to development.
- Neglecting the "extent to which" judgement.

## Self-test

1. What is synaptic pruning?
2. Distinguish a critical period from a sensitive period.
3. Why is Genie's case difficult to interpret?
4. Why is the Draganski et al. (2004) juggling study stronger evidence than Maguire et al. (2000)?
5. Suggest one reason for the difference in outcomes among adopted children.
6. Why is the prefrontal cortex relevant to adolescent behaviour?
7. Name one ethical issue in critical period research.

**Answers:** 1. The removal of unused synaptic connections, which makes the brain more efficient. 2. A critical period is a strict window when experience is needed for normal development; a sensitive period is a time of heightened responsiveness where later development is still possible. 3. Her isolation was combined with abuse and malnutrition, so language limits cannot be attributed to missing the critical period alone, and it is a single case. 4. It measured the same people before and after learning and had a control group, so it supports experience causing change, whereas Maguire et al. compared groups at one time. 5. Differences in the length of deprivation, quality of later care, health, genes or age at adoption. 6. It governs planning and impulse control and is still maturing, so judgement can be less consistent. 7. Deliberate deprivation of children or animals would cause harm, so evidence comes from natural experiments, and vulnerable case-study participants need protection.
