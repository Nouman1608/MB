## Questions

**1.** Identify the four contents of a marketing plan. **[4]**

**2.** Bramhollow Foods has sales revenue of £3.6m in a market worth £45m. Its marketing objective is a 10% market share after one year. The market is forecast to grow by 10% over that year.
**(a)** Calculate Bramhollow's current market share. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the sales revenue needed to reach its objective. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in sales revenue this requires. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** benefit and **one** limitation of marketing planning for Bramhollow Foods. **[4]**

**4.** Maplecombe Joinery is a small firm with three staff. It makes hand-built oak kitchens priced at about £18 000. The owner plans a regional television campaign and a "50% off" summer sale. Analyse why this strategy may not be consistent with the business, the product and the market. **[6]**

**5.** Fenwater Skincare sells a premium natural range at £28 a bottle. Its packaging is plain, its advertising says "lowest everyday prices" and it is stocked in discount supermarkets. Explain **two** ways in which the strategy is not coordinated. **[4]**

**6.** Fenwater Skincare changes its marketing objective from raising market share to protecting a premium image. Analyse how its marketing mix should change. **[6]**

**7.** Yarlwick Books is an online bookseller with sales revenue of £5.0m. It is considering AI tools to recommend books and set prices automatically. The tools cost £150 000 a year. Yarlwick expects sales revenue to rise by 6%, and gross profit is 30% of sales revenue. Evaluate whether Yarlwick should invest in the AI tools. **[8]**

**8.** Hartleap Toys scores three overseas markets from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). A high score for competition and trade barriers means weaker competition and fewer barriers.

| Criterion | Weight | Market P | Market R | Market T |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market size | 35 | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| Growth | 25 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| Competition | 20 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| Trade barriers | 20 | 2 | 4 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the weighted total for each market. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend a market and comment on the reliability of the scoring. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** implications for a business's marketing of increased globalisation and economic collaboration. **[4]**

**10.** Hartleap Toys sells wooden toys in one design, one brand and one pack worldwide. A new market has strong traditions of locally made toys and different safety rules. Evaluate whether Hartleap should keep a pan-global approach or maintain local differences. **[10]**

**11.** Hartleap can enter another market through an export agent (set-up cost £50 000, profit £60 000 a year) or its own subsidiary (set-up cost £360 000, profit £150 000 a year).
**(a)** Calculate the net return over three years and the payback period for each method. **[4]**
**(b)** Justify which method Hartleap should use. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Objectives [1]; resources [1]; research [1]; marketing mix [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* list answers gain credit for the four named contents. Writing only "the four Ps" covers one of them.

**2. (a)** 3.6 ÷ 45 = **8%** [1].
**(b)** Market after growth = 45 × 1.10 = £49.5m [1]. Target = 10% × 49.5 = **£4.95m** [1].
**(c)** Increase = 4.95 − 3.6 = £1.35m [1]; 1.35 ÷ 3.6 = **37.5%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* applying 10% to the old market size of £45m gives a target that is too low. Grow the market first and show each step, so that one slip does not lose everything.

**3.** Benefit: it gives the whole business one target (10% share) and a budget, so spending is deliberate and can be compared with results [2]. Limitation: the plan depends on forecasts such as 10% market growth, and if the market grows more slowly the target and budget may be unrealistic [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* each point needs a reason. A bare "it saves time" or "it can be wrong" is a statement, so link it to the Bramhollow target or forecast.

**4.** Business: with three staff and little cash, a television campaign may cost more than the firm can afford, and one oak kitchen is a large share of annual output [2]. Product: a high-priced, hand-built kitchen suits a quality image, but a 50% sale suggests low value and may damage that image [2]. Market: buyers of £18 000 kitchens are a small group in the region, so mass television reaches many people who will never buy [2]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* answers that treat business, product and market as three separate tests, each tied to a detail from the stem, tend to show analysis. General remarks about advertising earn little.

**5.** The price message conflicts with the product: a £28 premium product advertised as lowest price confuses customers about its value [2]. Distribution and packaging do not match the position: discount supermarkets and plain packs do not signal premium quality, so the mix elements send different messages [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* explain what is inconsistent and what effect it has on customers. Naming "price" and "place" alone earns less than saying how they clash.

**6.** Price: keep the price high and avoid discounting, so the price signals quality [2]. Place: move to selective outlets such as department stores or specialist shops that suit the image [2]. Promotion and product: use quality-based promotion and premium packaging, rather than price claims [2]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the strongest answers start from the new objective and show why each change serves it. Listing all four Ps without a reason is description.

**7.** Extra sales revenue = 6% × £5.0m = £300 000 [1]. Extra gross profit = 30% × 300 000 = £90 000 [1]. This is £60 000 below the £150 000 annual cost [1]. For: personal recommendations may raise repeat purchases and automatic pricing can respond quickly to demand [2]. Against: the cost exceeds the first-year gain, and poor data or privacy worries could weaken results [1]. Judgement: on these figures Yarlwick should not invest yet; it should do so only if evidence shows the sales gain will grow, or if costs can be cut, because the benefit may build over several years [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* use the figures in the stem. Evaluation is stronger when the judgement states a condition, such as what sales growth would make the tools worthwhile.

**8. (a)** P = 140 + 75 + 100 + 40 = 355 [1]; R = 105 + 125 + 40 + 80 = 350 [1]; T = 175 + 50 + 60 + 60 = 345 [1].
**(b)** Recommend Market P, the highest score [1]. The totals are close (10 points between first and last) and the scores are judgements, so a small change in weights or scores could change the ranking [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* a recommendation that only names the highest total is incomplete. Comment on how sensitive the result is.

**9.** Opportunity: larger potential markets and lower barriers between collaborating countries, so a business can sell more and spread its risk [2]. Threat: overseas rivals can enter the home market more easily, so the business must research customers and perhaps change its mix to keep its share [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give an implication for marketing, not just a description of globalisation. Another valid implication earns credit if it is explained.

**10.** Pan-global: one design and pack gives economies of scale, lower promotion cost and a consistent brand [2]. Local differences: the new market values locally made toys and has different safety rules, so an unchanged product may sell poorly or be unlawful to sell [2]. Local adaptation raises costs and may dilute the global brand [1]. Application: Hartleap could keep the brand and core design but alter materials, pack and message to meet the rules and tastes [2]. Judgement: it should maintain local differences in this market because legal compliance and acceptance matter more than scale savings, provided the extra cost does not remove profit [3]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* evaluation answers are strongest when they weigh both sides and then decide for this market. Avoid presenting the two approaches as all-or-nothing.

**11. (a)** Export agent: net = 3 × 60 − 50 = **£130 000** [1]; payback = 50 ÷ 60 = **0.83 years** [1]. Subsidiary: net = 3 × 150 − 360 = **£90 000** [1]; payback = 360 ÷ 150 = **2.4 years** [1].
**(b)** The export agent has the higher return over three years and repays in under a year, so it carries less risk and needs less cash [1]. The subsidiary gives more control over the brand and quality, and its cumulative return overtakes exporting after about 3.4 years [1]. Choose the export agent if Hartleap is cautious about cash and risk [1]. Judgement: the subsidiary suits a long-term commitment if the market grows, so the best choice depends on the planning period and risk appetite [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "justify" needs a reasoned choice supported by the figures. Using the payback and net return, and naming the time horizon, shows the decision is evidence-based.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the marketing mix when asked for the contents of the plan.
- Calculating target sales from the old market size rather than the grown one.
- Making general points about planning, strategy or AI that are not tied to the business in the stem.
- Treating consistency as one idea instead of testing business, product and market separately.
- Saying a mix is coordinated without showing the shared message.
- Giving only benefits of IT and AI, or only drawbacks.
- Recommending the top weighted score without commenting on the weights.
- Ending an evaluation with two lists of points and no reasoned final choice.
