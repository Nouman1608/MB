## Questions

**1.** A block rests on a rough horizontal table. A string attached to the block passes over a pulley at the table edge and carries a hanging mass.

**(a)** Why can the block be modelled as a particle? **[1]**
**(b)** State the consequence of modelling the string as (i) light and (ii) inextensible. **[2]**
**(c)** State the consequence of modelling the pulley as smooth. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** The force F = (−4i + 7j) N. Find its magnitude and the angle it makes with the positive j direction, to 1 decimal place. **[4]**
**(b)** A rope pulls a crate with force 40 N at 25° above the horizontal. Taking i horizontal and j upwards, write the force as a vector (3 significant figures). **[2]**
**(c)** A boat travels at 7 m s⁻¹ on a bearing of 250°. Write its velocity in the form ai + bj, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**3.** Three forces act on a particle: F₁ = (3i + pj) N, F₂ = (qi − 5j) N and F₃ = (−2i + 3j) N. Their resultant is (8i + 4j) N.
**(a)** Find p and q. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the resultant and the angle it makes with i, giving the angle to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**4.** At time t = 0 a particle P is at the point with position vector (−10i + 12j) m, moving with constant velocity (4i − 3j) m s⁻¹.
**(a)** Find the speed of P. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the position vector of P when t = 6. **[2]**
**(c)** Find when P is due north of the origin O, and its distance from O then. **[3]**

**5.** A particle moves with constant acceleration. Its velocity is (2i + 7j) m s⁻¹ at one instant and (−8i + 2j) m s⁻¹ five seconds later. Find the acceleration as a vector, its magnitude, and the acute angle it makes with the negative i direction (1 decimal place). **[4]**

**6.** A cyclist increases her speed uniformly from 3 m s⁻¹ to 9 m s⁻¹ while travelling 60 m in a straight line.
**(a)** Find her acceleration. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time taken. **[2]**

**7.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 21 m s⁻¹ from a point 3.5 m above horizontal ground. It moves freely under gravity.
**(a)** Find the greatest height of the ball above the ground. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the time taken to reach the ground, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**8.** A tram leaves a stop and accelerates uniformly at 0.8 m s⁻² for 15 s. It then travels at constant speed, and finally decelerates uniformly at 1.2 m s⁻² to rest at the next stop, 1050 m from the first.
**(a)** Find the maximum speed and the time spent decelerating. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time spent at constant speed. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the total journey time and the average speed. **[2]**

**9.** A particle moves on a straight line. Its velocity is 4 m s⁻¹ at t = 0 and increases uniformly to 12 m s⁻¹ at t = 5. It stays at 12 m s⁻¹ until t = 8, then decreases uniformly to −6 m s⁻¹ at t = 11.
**(a)** Find the acceleration in each of the three stages. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the time at which the particle is instantaneously at rest. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the displacement from the start at t = 11. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the total distance travelled. **[1]**

**10.** Car A passes a point O at t = 0 travelling at a constant 6 m s⁻¹ along a straight road. Car B passes O from rest at t = 6, accelerating uniformly at 1.5 m s⁻² in the same direction. Time t is in seconds.
**(a)** Show that B's distance from O at time t > 6 is 0.75(t − 6)². **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the cars are level when t² − 20t + 36 = 0. **[2]**
**(c)** Solve this equation and state, with a reason, which root is valid. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the speed of B when the cars are level. **[2]**
**(e)** State one modelling assumption about the cars. **[1]**

**11.** A drone moves from O with constant velocity (3i + 4j) m s⁻¹ for 20 s, then with constant velocity (−4i + 6j) m s⁻¹. Time t is in seconds from the start.
**(a)** Find the position vector of the drone when t = 20. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that for t > 20 its position vector is (140 − 4t)i + (6t − 40)j. **[2]**
**(c)** Find when the drone is due north of O, and how far from O it is then. **[3]**
**(d)** Find its speed during the second stage, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Its size and rotation do not matter, so its mass is treated as at one point. [1]
**(b)** (i) Light: negligible mass, so the tension is the same throughout the string. [1] (ii) Inextensible: the string does not stretch, so block and hanging mass have the same speed and acceleration magnitude. [1]
**(c)** Smooth pulley: no friction acts on the string there, so the tension is unchanged as it passes over the pulley. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link each assumption to its consequence for the equations; naming the word alone gives little to be credited.

**2. (a)** Magnitude = √((−4)² + 7²) [1] = √65 = **8.1 N** [1]
Angle with j: tan θ = 4/7 [1], so θ = **29.7°** [1]
**(b)** Horizontal 40 cos 25° [1]; force = **(36.3i + 16.9j) N** [1]
**(c)** Bearing 250° gives east 7 sin 250° [1] and north 7 cos 250°, so velocity = **(−6.58i − 2.39j) m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Sketch the right-angled triangle before choosing sin or cos, and state which line an angle is measured from.

**3. (a)** i: 3 + q − 2 = 8 [1], so **q = 7**. j: p − 5 + 3 = 4 [1], so **p = 6** [1]
**(b)** Magnitude = √(8² + 4²) [1] = √80 = **8.94 N** [1]
Angle: tan α = 4/8, so α = **26.6°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Equate i and j components on separate lines so each step is easy to follow.

**4. (a)** Speed = √(4² + (−3)²) [1] = **5 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** r = (−10i + 12j) + 6(4i − 3j) [1] = **(14i − 6j) m** [1]
**(c)** Due north means the i component is zero: −10 + 4t = 0 [1], so **t = 2.5 s**. j component = 12 − 3(2.5) = 4.5, so distance = **4.5 m** [1] from O. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Position is r₀ + vt. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, so it has no direction. Answer both parts of (c).

**5.** a = (change in velocity)/time = ((−8 − 2)i + (2 − 7)j)/5 [1]; **a = (−2i − j) m s⁻²** [1]
Magnitude = √(4 + 1) = **2.24 m s⁻²** [1]
tan α = 1/2, so **α = 26.6°** below the negative i direction. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract initial from final velocity, not the reverse; a sign error here affects everything after it.

**6. (a)** v² = u² + 2as: 81 = 9 + 120a [1], so **a = 0.6 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** v = u + at: 9 = 3 + 0.6t [1], so **t = 10 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Pick the formula that avoids the unknown you do not need; listing s, u, v, a, t first shows why.

**7. (a)** At the top v = 0: 0 = 21² − 2(9.8)s [1]; s = 441/19.6 = 22.5 m [1]; height above ground = 22.5 + 3.5 = **26.0 m** [1]
**(b)** Take up as positive: −3.5 = 21t − 4.9t² [1]. Then 4.9t² − 21t − 3.5 = 0 gives t = (21 + √509.6)/9.8 = **4.45 s**, rejecting the negative root. [1]
*Examiner insight:* State your sign convention; the ground is below the start, so the displacement is −3.5.

**8. (a)** v = 0.8 × 15 = **12 m s⁻¹** [1]; decelerating time = 12/1.2 = **10 s** [1]
**(b)** Acceleration stage: ½(12)(15) = 90 m [1]; deceleration stage: ½(12)(10) = 60 m [1]. Constant stage: 12T = 1050 − 150, so **T = 75 s** [1]
**(c)** Total time = 15 + 75 + 10 = **100 s** [1]; average speed = 1050/100 = **10.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A speed-time sketch keeps the areas organised. Average speed is total distance over total time, not the mean of the speeds.

**9. (a)** Stage 1: (12 − 4)/5 = **1.6 m s⁻²** [1]. Stage 2: **0** [1]. Stage 3: (−6 − 12)/3 = **−6 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** 12 − 6s = 0 after t = 8, so s = 2 and **t = 10** [1]
**(c)** Areas under the graph: ½(4 + 12)(5) = 40 [1]; 12 × 3 = 36; 8 to 10 gives ½(12)(2) = 12; 10 to 11 gives −½(6)(1) = −3 [1]. Displacement = **85 m** [1]
**(d)** Distance = 40 + 36 + 12 + 3 = **91 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Treat the region below the axis separately: adding it gives distance, subtracting it gives displacement.

**10. (a)** B starts from rest at t = 6, so it has travelled for (t − 6) s. [1] s = ½(1.5)(t − 6)² = **0.75(t − 6)²** [1]
**(b)** A's distance is 6t. Level when 6t = 0.75(t − 6)² [1]. Then 8t = (t − 6)², so t² − 12t + 36 − 8t = 0, giving **t² − 20t + 36 = 0**. [1]
**(c)** (t − 2)(t − 18) = 0 [1], so t = 2 or 18 [1]. t = 2 is invalid because B only passes O at t = 6, so **t = 18**. [1]
**(d)** B has been moving for 12 s: v = 1.5 × 12 [1] = **18 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(e)** The cars are modelled as particles. [1]
*Examiner insight:* After solving a quadratic, say why you reject a root, tied to the situation. Here t is measured from A's start, not B's.

**11. (a)** r = 20(3i + 4j) [1] = **(60i + 80j) m** [1]
**(b)** r = (60i + 80j) + (t − 20)(−4i + 6j) [1]. i: 60 − 4t + 80 = 140 − 4t. j: 80 + 6t − 120 = 6t − 40. Hence shown. [1]
**(c)** Due north: 140 − 4t = 0 [1], so **t = 35 s**. j component = 6(35) − 40 = 170 [1], so the distance is **170 m**. [1]
**(d)** Speed = √((−4)² + 6²) [1] = √52 = **7.21 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a show-that question, write the substitution and expanded components. The second stage starts at t = 20.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a modelling assumption without its effect on the motion.
- Giving an angle without saying which direction it is measured from.
- Using sin where cos is needed, because no triangle was drawn.
- Subtracting velocities in the wrong order when finding acceleration.
- Confusing speed with velocity, or distance with displacement.
- Using time from the start of the whole journey in a formula for a later stage.
- Counting an area below the axis as positive when displacement is wanted.
- Forgetting to reject a root that is physically impossible.
- Leaving the sign convention unstated in vertical motion.
