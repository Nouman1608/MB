Accounting 0452 syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029. All businesses are sole

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A trader's capital was 20,000 at the start of the year and 26,000 at the
end. Drawings were 9,000 and the trader paid in additional capital of 4,000. What
was the profit for the year?

A: 2,000

B: 11,000

C: 19,000

D: 6,000

**[1]**

**2.** A business uses a mark-up of 25%. What is its gross profit margin?

A: 20%

B: 25%

C: 33⅓%

D: 75%

**[1]**

**3.** State two reasons why a sole trader may not keep a full double entry system. **[2]**

**4.** During the year a trader received 34,500 from credit customers. Trade
receivables were 2,800 at the start of the year and 3,350 at the end. Calculate
credit sales. **[2]**

**5.** Revenue was 90,000 and the gross profit margin is 30%. Calculate cost of
sales and gross profit. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why drawings are added to the change in capital when calculating
profit for the year. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Tom Baxter does not keep full records. His assets and liabilities were:

| | 1 January 2028 | 31 December 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment | 12,000 | 10,800 |
| Inventory | 3,900 | 4,600 |
| Trade receivables | 1,100 | 1,450 |
| Bank | 1,750 | 600 overdrawn |
| Trade payables | 1,600 | 1,900 |
| Loan (repayable 2031) | 5,000 | 4,000 |
| Electricity accrued | – | 150 |

During 2028 Tom's drawings were 15,600, and he paid into the business 2,500
that he had inherited.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of affairs at 1 January 2028. **[3]**

**(b)** Prepare a statement of affairs at 31 December 2028. **[4]**

**(c)** Calculate the profit for the year ended 31 December 2028. **[3]**

**8.** Asha sells kitchenware. During 2028 she paid suppliers 52,300 and received
29,800 from credit customers. Her cash sales were 36,500. Trade payables were 4,100
at the start of the year and 3,600 at the end; trade receivables were 2,250 and
2,700. Inventory was 6,800 at the start and 7,300 at the end.

**(a)** Calculate purchases for the year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate revenue for the year. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate gross profit. **[2]**

**9.** A trader's cost of sales was 84,000 and the rate of inventory turnover was
7 times. Closing inventory was 13,000.

**(a)** Calculate opening inventory. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate purchases. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate inventory turnover in days, to two decimal places. **[1]**

**10.** A trader's revenue was 63,000, all sold at a mark-up of 40%. Opening
inventory was 7,500 and purchases 46,200. Calculate cost of sales, gross profit and
closing inventory. **[4]**

**11.** Calculate the statement of profit or loss charge for each expense.

**(a)** Insurance: paid 2,600; prepaid 400 at the start and 450 at the end. **[2]**

**(b)** Electricity: paid 1,900; accrued 220 at the start and 180 at the end. **[2]**

**12.** Rosa runs a busy café and keeps only bank statements and a box of invoices.
She wants to borrow 20,000 to expand. Advise Rosa whether she should start
keeping a full set of accounting records. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. 26,000 − 20,000 + 9,000 − 4,000 = 11,000. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. Cost 100, profit 25, price 125: 25 ÷ 125 = 20%. **[1]**

**3.** Any two from: it costs time or an accountant's fee; the owner lacks
bookkeeping knowledge; the business is small and mostly cash-based; the owner
feels the bank balance shows enough [1] [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 34,500 + 3,350 − 2,800 [1] = **35,050** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Cost of sales = 90,000 × 70% = **63,000** [1]; gross profit = **27,000** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Drawings reduce capital [1], but they are not a loss of the business, so
they are added back to show how much capital would have grown from profit alone [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

```
Statement of affairs at 1 January 2028
Equipment 12,000 + inventory 3,900 + receivables 1,100 + bank 1,750  = 18,750
Less trade payables 1,600 and loan 5,000                              (6,600)
Capital                                                               12,150
```

Assets 18,750 [1]; liabilities 6,600 [1]; capital 12,150 [1].

**(b)**

```
Statement of affairs at 31 December 2028
Equipment 10,800 + inventory 4,600 + receivables 1,450               = 16,850
Less overdraft 600, payables 1,900, loan 4,000, electricity 150      (6,650)
Capital                                                               10,200
```

Assets 16,850 [1]; overdraft treated as a liability [1]; accrual included [1];
capital 10,200 [1].

**(c)** 10,200 − 12,150 = (1,950) [1]; add drawings 15,600 [1]; less capital
introduced 2,500, profit = **11,150** [1]. **[10]**

**8. (a)** 52,300 + 3,600 − 4,100 [1] = **51,800** [1].

**(b)** Credit sales = 29,800 + 2,700 − 2,250 = 30,250 [1]; add cash sales 36,500 [1];
revenue = **66,750** [1].

**(c)** Cost of sales = 6,800 + 51,800 − 7,300 = 51,300 [1]; gross profit = 66,750
− 51,300 = **15,450** [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** Average inventory = 84,000 ÷ 7 = 12,000 [1]. Opening = (12,000 × 2)
− 13,000 = **11,000** [1].

**(b)** Purchases = 84,000 + 13,000 − 11,000 = **86,000** [1].

**(c)** 12,000 ÷ 84,000 × 365 = **52.14 days** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Cost of sales = 63,000 × 100/140 = **45,000** [1]. Gross profit = **18,000** [1].
Closing inventory = 7,500 + 46,200 − 45,000 [1] = **8,700** [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** 2,600 + 400 − 450 [1] = **2,550** [1].

**(b)** 1,900 − 220 + 180 [1] = **1,860** [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Full records would show Rosa her revenue, gross profit and each expense, so
she could control costs and set prices [1]. A lender is likely to want reliable
financial statements before lending 20,000, which incomplete records may not give [1].
Against: full records take time or cost an accountant's fee [1]. Because she wants to
borrow and the café is busy, the benefits outweigh the cost, so she should keep full
records, for example with accounting software [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to adjust the change in capital for drawings and capital introduced.
- Treating a bank overdraft as an asset.
- Using cash received instead of sales.
- Confusing mark-up and margin.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory in turnover calculations.
