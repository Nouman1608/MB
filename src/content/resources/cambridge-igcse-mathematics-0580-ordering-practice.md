These questions cover subtopic 1.5 Ordering in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 12 use content common to Core (C1.5) and Extended (E1.5). Questions 13 and 14 are labelled **(Extended)** because they use recurring decimal notation from E1.4. All questions are non-calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [ordering study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-ordering/) and the [ordering revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-ordering-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write the correct symbol, <, > or =, between each pair.

**(a)** −4 ... −1 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.45 ... 9/20 **[1]**

**(c)** 0.7² ... 0.7 **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) List the integers n such that −5 ⩽ n < −1. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: 0.506, 0.56, 0.065, 0.605, 0.5 **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: −3.4, −3.04, −3.44, −3⅓ **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) x ⩾ −2.

**(a)** Write this inequality in words. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the smallest integer value of x. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Which is larger, 7/9 or 0.78? Show how you decide. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: 2/3, 0.67, 66%, 0.6, 13/20 **[3]**

**8.** (non-calculator) Write these masses in order, heaviest first: 1.3 kg, 1 kg 30 g, 1290 g, 1¼ kg **[3]**

**9.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first, showing your working: √40, 6.3, 19/3, 2.5², 6.4 **[4]**

**10.** (non-calculator) The temperatures in five cities at midnight were −3.5 °C, 2 °C, −7 °C, 0.5 °C and −0.8 °C.

**(a)** Write the temperatures in order, coldest first. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the difference between the highest and the lowest temperature. **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) Four runners' times are 2.4 min, 2 min 25 s, 143 s and 2 5/12 min.

**(a)** Write the times in order, fastest first. **[2]**

**(b)** Which two times are equal? **[1]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A ride has the rule "height h metres must satisfy h ⩾ 1.2".

**(a)** Can a child of height 119 cm go on the ride? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** A lift can carry at most 8 people. Write an inequality for the number of people, p, in the lift. **[1]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: 0.4̇, 0.45, 0.4̇5̇, 0.45̇ **[2]**

**14.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write the correct symbol, <, > or =, between 0.9̇ and 1, and prove your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** −4 **<** −1 [1]

**(b)** 0.45 **=** 9/20 (9/20 = 45/100) [1]

**(c)** 0.7² **<** 0.7 (0.7² = 0.49) [1]

**2.** **−5, −4, −3, −2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* ⩽ includes −5, but < excludes −1. Lists that include −1 or leave out −5 earn no mark.

**3.** **0.065, 0.5, 0.506, 0.56, 0.605** [2]. One mark for four in the correct relative order.

*Examiner insight:* pad to three decimal places (0.500, 0.560) before comparing; 0.5 is smaller than 0.506.

**4.** −3⅓ = −3.333… [1]; **−3.44, −3.4, −3⅓, −3.04** [1]

*Examiner insight:* order the sizes as if positive (3.04, 3.333…, 3.4, 3.44) and then reverse for negatives.

**5. (a)** x is greater than or equal to −2 [1]

**(b)** **−2** [1]

**6.** 7/9 = 0.777… [1]; 0.777… < 0.78, so **0.78** is larger [1]

**7.** Decimals: 0.666…, 0.67, 0.66, 0.6, 0.65 [1]; **0.6, 13/20, 66%, 2/3, 0.67** [2]. One of the two marks for the order is lost for a single pair reversed.

*Examiner insight:* 2/3 and 0.67 are close; 0.666… is less than 0.670, so 2/3 comes first.

**8.** In grams: 1300, 1030, 1290, 1250 [1]; **1.3 kg, 1290 g, 1¼ kg, 1 kg 30 g** [2]

*Examiner insight:* 1 kg 30 g is 1030 g, not 1300 g; the 30 g needs a zero in the hundreds place.

**9.** 2.5² = 6.25 [1]; 6.3² = 39.69 < 40, so √40 > 6.3 [1]; (19/3)² = 361/9 = 40.11… > 40, so √40 < 19/3, and 19/3 = 6.333… < 6.4 [1]; order **2.5², 6.3, √40, 19/3, 6.4** [1]

*Examiner insight:* squaring 19/3 avoids having to find √40 to several decimal places; any valid comparison earns the method marks.

**10. (a)** **−7, −3.5, −0.8, 0.5, 2** (°C) [1]

**(b)** 2 − (−7) = **9 °C** [1]

**11. (a)** In seconds: 144, 145, 143, 145; **143 s, 2.4 min, 2 min 25 s and 2 5/12 min** (last two equal) [2]. One mark for converting all four to seconds correctly.

**(b)** **2 min 25 s and 2 5/12 min** (both 145 s) [1]

*Examiner insight:* 2.4 min is 2 min 24 s, not 2 min 40 s; fastest first means the shortest time first.

**12. (a)** No: 119 cm = 1.19 m and 1.19 < 1.2, so it does not satisfy h ⩾ 1.2 [1]

**(b)** **p ⩽ 8** [1]

**13.** 0.4444…, 0.4500, 0.4545…, 0.4555… [1]; **0.4̇, 0.45, 0.4̇5̇, 0.45̇** [1]

**14.** **0.9̇ = 1** [1]. Proof: x = 0.999…, 10x = 9.999…, so 9x = 9 and x = 1 [1]

*Examiner insight:* "0.9̇ is slightly less than 1" is a common belief but is false; the subtraction method used for converting recurring decimals proves equality.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Judging decimals by how many digits they have instead of by place value.
- Ordering negative numbers by size rather than by position on the number line.
- Not converting all values to one unit or one form, or not writing the conversions down.
- Answering with converted decimals rather than the values given.
- Misreading "heaviest first" or "fastest first".

## Approaching ordering questions

Make a quick two-column list: the value as given, and its decimal (in a common unit). Pad the decimals to the same length, order them, and copy the original values across in that order. When roots are involved on a non-calculator paper, square the neighbouring numbers rather than trying to evaluate the root.
