These notes condense sub-topic **9.2 Heart** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 6 are Core; outcomes 7 to 11 are **Extended only**.

## Structures to identify

**Core:** muscular wall, septum, left atrium, right atrium, left ventricle, right ventricle, one-way valves, coronary arteries.

**Extended only:** atrioventricular valves (between atria and ventricles) and semilunar valves (at the base of the aorta and the pulmonary artery).

Diagram rules:

- Left side of the heart = right side of the page.
- Thickest wall = left ventricle.
- Atria on top, ventricles below.
- Coronary arteries run over the outside surface.

## Vessels attached to the heart

| Chamber | Vessel | Artery or vein | Blood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right atrium | vena cava (in) | vein | deoxygenated |
| Right ventricle | pulmonary artery (out) | artery | deoxygenated |
| Left atrium | pulmonary vein (in) | vein | oxygenated |
| Left ventricle | aorta (out) | artery | oxygenated |

Arteries = **away** from the heart. Veins = **back** to the heart. Oxygen content does not decide the name.

## Monitoring the heart (Core)

- **ECG:** electrical activity.
- **Pulse rate:** count at wrist or neck, convert to beats per minute.
- **Stethoscope:** sounds of valves **closing** (first sound: atrioventricular valves; second: semilunar valves).

Converting counts: beats in 15 s × 4, or beats in 30 s × 2 = beats per minute. From an ECG: 60 ÷ time between peaks in seconds = beats per minute.

## One heartbeat (Extended only)

1. Atria contract → blood pushed through **open** atrioventricular valves into ventricles.
2. Ventricles contract → pressure rises → atrioventricular valves **close** (no backflow to atria).
3. Semilunar valves **open** → blood leaves in aorta and pulmonary artery.
4. Ventricles relax → semilunar valves **close** (no backflow to ventricles).

Valves move because of pressure differences. Both atria contract together, then both ventricles.

## Thickness and septum (Extended only)

| Feature | Reason |
|---|---|
| Left ventricle wall thicker than right | pumps to whole body at higher pressure; right pumps only to nearby lungs |
| Atria walls thinner than ventricles | atria pump only into ventricles; ventricles pump out of the heart |
| Septum | separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, so the body gets the maximum oxygen |

Same volume from both ventricles; different pressure.

## Heart rate and physical activity

- **Core:** heart rate rises during activity and returns to resting rate afterwards.
- **Investigation:** change intensity or duration of activity; measure pulse; keep person, activity type, duration and timing the same; rest between trials; repeat and take a mean.
- **Extended only:** muscle cells respire faster → need more oxygen and glucose, produce more carbon dioxide → faster heart rate delivers blood faster.

## Coronary heart disease (Core)

- **What it is:** blockage of the coronary arteries.
- **Effect:** heart muscle gets less oxygen and glucose → cannot respire → may die.
- **Risk factors:** diet, lack of exercise, stress, smoking, genetic predisposition, age, sex.
- **Changeable:** diet, exercise, stress, smoking. **Not changeable:** genetic predisposition, age, sex.
- **Diet:** less saturated fat and cholesterol (fewer fatty deposits), less salt (lower blood pressure).
- **Exercise:** controls body mass, lowers blood pressure, strengthens heart muscle.
- **Limitation:** neither removes genetic predisposition, age or sex.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atrium | Upper chamber; receives blood from veins |
| Ventricle | Lower chamber; pumps blood into arteries |
| Septum | Wall dividing left and right sides |
| Coronary arteries | Supply the heart muscle with oxygenated blood |
| Atrioventricular valve | Valve between atrium and ventricle (Extended) |
| Semilunar valve | Valve at the base of the aorta or pulmonary artery (Extended) |
| ECG | Electrocardiogram; records the heart's electrical activity |
| Coronary heart disease | Blockage of the coronary arteries |

## Exam traps

- Left and right swapped in a diagram.
- "Thicker to pump more blood": the credited idea is higher pressure over a longer distance.
- Semilunar valves placed between atria and ventricles.
- "Valves pump" or "valves contract".
- Pulmonary artery called a vein because it carries deoxygenated blood.
- Heart sounds described as "muscle beating" instead of valves closing.
- CHD answers that say "blood cannot reach the heart" instead of "the heart muscle".
- Discuss answers with benefits only and no limitation.

## Self-test

1. Name the chamber that receives blood from the pulmonary vein.
2. Which structure separates the left and right sides of the heart?
3. State the function of the coronary arteries.
4. Give three ways the activity of the heart can be monitored.
5. A pulse count is 21 beats in 15 seconds. Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute.
6. Describe coronary heart disease.
7. Name two risk factors for coronary heart disease that a person can change.
8. (Extended) Where are the semilunar valves found?
9. (Extended) What happens to the atrioventricular valves when the ventricles contract, and why does it matter?
10. (Extended) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle.
11. (Extended) Explain the importance of the septum.
12. (Extended) Explain why heart rate increases during physical activity.

### Answers

1. The left atrium.
2. The septum.
3. They supply the heart muscle with blood carrying oxygen and glucose.
4. ECG, pulse rate, listening to the sounds of the valves closing.
5. 21 × 4 = 84 beats per minute.
6. Blockage of the coronary arteries, so heart muscle receives less oxygen and glucose and may die.
7. Any two of: diet, lack of exercise, stress, smoking.
8. At the base of the aorta and of the pulmonary artery.
9. They close, preventing blood flowing back into the atria.
10. It pumps blood round the whole body, which needs a higher pressure; the right ventricle pumps only to the lungs.
11. It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood apart, so blood sent to the body carries as much oxygen as possible.
12. Muscles respire faster, so they need more oxygen and glucose and must remove more carbon dioxide; a faster heart rate delivers blood faster.
