## Condensed recall

**Definitions**
- Weather: atmospheric conditions over hours or days.
- Climate: average weather over a long period, usually 30 years or more.
- Biome: a group of ecosystems with similar climate and similar communities, found on different
  continents.

**Controls on biomes**
- Temperature and precipitation are the main controls; insolation sets temperature and light.
- Evaporation rises with temperature, so the same rainfall supports less vegetation where it is hot.
- Biome diagram: mean annual temperature against annual precipitation; each biome has its own
  region.

**Climate graph checklist**
- Total precipitation; driest month and dry season; mean temperature; annual range; growing season;
  hemisphere; biome; explanation.

**Biome, factor, adaptation**
- Tropical rainforest: light at ground level, poor soils → emergents, drip tips, buttress roots,
  epiphytes.
- Hot desert: water → deep or wide roots, succulence, small or spiny leaves, nocturnal animals.
- Tundra: cold, short season, permafrost → low cushion plants, shallow roots, thick fur, migration.
- Boreal forest: cold, acidic soils → needles, evergreen, conical shape.
- Temperate deciduous forest: winter cold → leaf fall, hibernation.
- Tropical grassland: drought, fire → deep roots, thick bark, migration following rain.
- Temperate grassland: summer drought → deep dense roots, growth from base.

**Productivity and diversity**
- Highest where warmth, light and water are all plentiful year-round.
- Lowest where one factor is severely limiting.
- Diversity tracks productivity, stability and vertical layering.

**Aquatic biomes**
- Limiting factors: light (depth) and nutrients.
- Open ocean: low per area, high in total. Upwelling: very productive. Coral reefs: high diversity,
  tight nutrient recycling.

**Altitude**
- Environmental lapse rate about 6.5 °C per 1,000 m.
- Mountain zones mimic latitudinal biomes over short distances.
- Height shift = warming ÷ lapse rate × 1,000.

**Climate change**
- Biomes shift poleward and upslope; species move at different speeds.
- Fire regimes intensify; productivity rises in some cold areas, falls in drying areas.
- Reefs face bleaching and acidification.

## Method in steps: biome from climate data

```
1. Add the precipitation; note any dry months.
2. Find the warmest and coldest months; work out the range.
3. Count months warm enough for growth.
4. Decide the main limiting factor: water, temperature or light.
5. Name the biome that matches.
6. Justify with two or three quoted values.
```

Mini example (invented): annual rainfall 1,450 mm with a dry season of four months under 30 mm;
temperature 24–29 °C all year. Hot all year with a strong dry season fits **tropical grassland
(savanna)**, where the movement of the rain belt brings rain for only part of the year.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insolation | Incoming solar radiation |
| Biome | Collection of ecosystems sharing a similar climate and community type |
| Biome diagram | Graph placing biomes by temperature and precipitation |
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply that restricts growth |
| Growing season | Period warm and wet enough for plant growth |
| Annual temperature range | Warmest month minus coldest month |
| Permafrost | Ground frozen for at least two consecutive years |
| Emergent | Tree rising above the main rainforest canopy |
| Xerophyte | Plant adapted to dry conditions |
| Upwelling | Rise of cold, nutrient-rich deep water to the surface |
| Environmental lapse rate | Fall in temperature with height in the atmosphere |
| Tree line | Upper (or poleward) limit of tree growth |

## Exam traps

- **Quote the data.** "Low rainfall" earns less than "16 mm a year".
- **Desert means dry, not hot.** Cold deserts exist.
- **Link feature to factor.** An adaptation without its reason scores half or less.
- **Weather is not climate.** One wet week says nothing about a biome.
- **Lapse-rate answers need units.** Metres for height, °C for temperature.
- **Communities do not migrate intact.** Species shift separately and at different rates.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish weather from climate.
2. Name the two main climatic controls on biomes.
3. Why can the same rainfall support forest in a cool place but grassland in a hot one?
4. Give two adaptations of tundra plants and the factor each overcomes.
5. Why do boreal trees keep their needles all year?
6. Why are nutrients a limiting factor in much of the open ocean?
7. Why are upwelling zones so productive?
8. Using 6.5 °C per 1,000 m, how much colder is a summit 2,400 m above a valley?
9. Warming of 0.65 °C shifts a temperature-limited tree line by how much?
10. Give one reason the tree line lags behind warming.
11. Why are tropical rainforests the most productive land biome?
12. Give one effect of climate change on fire in boreal forest.

### Answers

1. Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions; climate is the long-term average, usually over
   30 years or more.
2. Temperature and precipitation.
3. Higher temperatures increase evaporation, leaving less water available to plants.
4. Any two: low cushion growth (reduces wind exposure and heat loss); shallow roots (permafrost
   below); rapid flowering (short growing season).
5. They can photosynthesise as soon as conditions allow in a short summer, without the cost of
   regrowing leaves.
6. Organic matter sinks out of the sunlit surface layer, taking nutrients with it.
7. Cold, nutrient-rich water is brought into the sunlit surface layer.
8. 2.4 × 6.5 = **15.6 °C** colder.
9. 0.65 ÷ 6.5 × 1,000 = **100 m** upslope.
10. Trees grow and disperse slowly, and soils take time to develop.
11. Light, warmth and water are plentiful all year, so photosynthesis continues year-round.
12. Hotter, drier summers increase the frequency or intensity of fires.
