Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-gamma-camera-pet-scanning-and-ultrasound/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-gamma-camera-pet-scanning-and-ultrasound-revision-notes/). The course hub is [OCR A Level Physics](/boards/ocr/a-level/physics/) and the printable [checklist](/checklists/ocr/a-level/physics/) lists each statement. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s. Where a question gives no speed, use 1540 m s⁻¹ for ultrasound in soft tissue.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State the type of radiation emitted by technetium-99m and by fluorine-18. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why a half-life of about 6 hours suits technetium-99m. **[2]**

**2.** State the function of the collimator, the scintillator and the photomultiplier tubes in a gamma camera. **[3]**

**3.** A batch of fluorine-18 has activity 480 MBq when made. It is injected 120 minutes later. The half-life is 110 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the activity at injection. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken for the activity to fall to 10% of its initial value. **[2]**

**4.** A positron and an electron, both essentially at rest, annihilate. Each photon produced has energy 0.511 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate this energy in joules. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why two photons are produced, travelling in opposite directions. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the wavelength of each photon. **[1]**

**5.** Describe how a PET scanner forms an image of the distribution of fluorine-18 in a patient. **[4]**

**6.** Explain why a PET scan using a glucose-like tracer shows a growing tumour as a bright region. **[3]**

**7.** A hospital is deciding whether to buy a PET scanner. Discuss the issues it should consider. **[4]**

**8.** An A-scan trace shows two echoes, at 40 μs and 120 μs after the pulse was sent.

**(a)** Calculate the depth of each boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the thickness of tissue between the two boundaries. **[2]**

**9.** Medium A has density 1010 kg m⁻³ and ultrasound speed 1500 m s⁻¹. Medium B has density 1850 kg m⁻³ and ultrasound speed 3900 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the acoustic impedance of each. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction of intensity reflected when a beam in A meets B at right angles. **[2]**
**(c)** The incident intensity is 2.5 W m⁻². Calculate the reflected intensity. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why it is hard to image structures beyond B. **[1]**

**10.** Air has acoustic impedance 410, skin 1.58 × 10⁶ and a scanning gel 1.50 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the fraction reflected at an air-skin boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction reflected at a gel-skin boundary. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the gel improves the scan. **[2]**

**11.** A 2.5 MHz ultrasound beam meets blood at 35° to its direction of flow. The reflected frequency differs from the emitted frequency by 1.1 kHz.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the blood. **[3]**
**(b)** State why the beam is not directed at 90° to the vessel. **[1]**
**(c)** The probe is moved so that the angle is 50°. The speed is unchanged. Calculate the new frequency shift. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the factor 2 in the Doppler equation for this situation. **[1]**

**12.** **(a)** Describe how a transducer emits and detects ultrasound, and how a B-scan image is built up. **[5]**
**(b)** A scanner must image to a depth of 24 cm. Calculate the shortest time between pulses and the greatest number of pulses per second. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Tc-99m emits gamma radiation [1]; F-18 emits positrons (β⁺) [1].
**(b)** Long enough to prepare, inject and scan [1]; short enough that the activity and dose fall quickly afterwards [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the radiation for each tracer separately. A single phrase such as "radioactive" does not answer a question that asks for the type.

**2.** The collimator lets through only photons travelling along its holes, so the image is sharp [1]. The scintillator converts each absorbed gamma photon to a flash of light [1]. The photomultiplier tubes convert the light to electrical pulses, amplified by the dynode chain [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each component needs its own job stated. Describing the camera as a whole earns little for a "state the function" question.

**3.** **(a)** Fraction = (1/2)^(120/110) = 0.469 [1]; A = 480 × 0.469 = **225 MBq** [1].
**(b)** 0.10 = (1/2)^n, so n = log 10 / log 2 = 3.32 half-lives [1]; t = 3.32 × 110 = **365 min (6.1 h)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the time in the same unit as the half-life, and keep unrounded values until the end to avoid drifting from the true answer.

**4.** **(a)** E = 0.511 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = **8.18 × 10⁻¹⁴ J** [1].
**(b)** Momentum is conserved and the total before is about zero [1]; one photon would carry momentum, so two with equal and opposite momenta are produced [1].
**(c)** λ = hc/E = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 8.18 × 10⁻¹⁴ = **2.43 × 10⁻¹² m** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution. Quoting only a final number leaves nothing to credit if a slip is made.

**5.** The patient is surrounded by a ring of detectors [1]. Each annihilation sends two photons in opposite directions, detected by two detectors at almost the same time [1]. The annihilation lies on the line between those two detectors [1]. The computer combines very many lines and finds where they cross most often, building an image of where the tracer is concentrated [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the words "opposite directions" and "at the same time" explicitly. Answers that say only "detectors detect gamma rays" miss the idea that gives position.

**6.** The tracer is attached to a glucose-like molecule that cells take up [1]. Tumour cells use glucose faster, so more F-18 collects there [1]. More annihilations, so more detected photon pairs, come from that region, which appears brighter [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link each step to the next: uptake, then concentration, then count rate, then brightness. Saying "the tumour is hot" alone is only a restatement.

**7.** Any four points, including at least one for and one against: the high cost of buying and running the scanner [1]; a nearby supply of F-18 is needed because of its short half-life [1]; PET shows tissue activity and can give an earlier or more accurate diagnosis [1]; the dose to patients and staff must be justified by the benefit [1]. Also acceptable: trained staff, how many patients would benefit, and travel or waiting times without a scanner.
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" question asks for both sides. A list of costs only is one-sided and leaves out the benefit that justifies the decision.

**8.** **(a)** d = ct/2 [1]; d₁ = 1540 × 40 × 10⁻⁶ / 2 = 0.0308 m = **3.08 cm** and d₂ = 1540 × 120 × 10⁻⁶ / 2 = **9.24 cm** [1].
**(b)** Difference in depth [1]: 9.24 − 3.08 = **6.16 cm** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The factor of 2 for the there-and-back path is the usual slip, so write d = ct/2 before substituting.

**9.** **(a)** Z = ρc: Z_A = 1010 × 1500 = **1.52 × 10⁶** [1]; Z_B = 1850 × 3900 = **7.22 × 10⁶** kg m⁻² s⁻¹ [1].
**(b)** Ir/I0 = (7.215 − 1.515)² / (7.215 + 1.515)² [1] = **0.426** [1].
**(c)** Ir = 0.426 × 2.5 = **1.07 W m⁻²** [1].
**(d)** Over 40% is reflected, so less of the beam reaches and returns from deeper structures [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded impedances for part (b). Using the rounded values from part (a) may shift the third figure.

**10.** **(a)** (1.58 × 10⁶ − 410)² / (1.58 × 10⁶ + 410)² [1] = **0.999** [1].
**(b)** (1.58 − 1.50)² / (1.58 + 1.50)² [1] = **6.7 × 10⁻⁴** [1].
**(c)** The gel fills the air gap between transducer and skin [1]. Its impedance is close to that of skin (impedance matching), so little is reflected and nearly all the ultrasound enters the body [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason as impedance matching, with the removal of air. "Lubricant" or "helps contact" does not address the physics.

**11.** **(a)** v = cΔf / (2f cos θ) [1] = 1540 × 1.1 × 10³ / (2 × 2.5 × 10⁶ × cos 35°) [1] = **0.41 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** cos 90° = 0, so there would be no shift [1].
**(c)** Δf ∝ cos θ: Δf = 1.1 × 10³ × cos 50° / cos 35° [1] = **0.86 kHz** [1].
**(d)** The moving blood receives the wave at a shifted frequency and then re-emits at a further shifted frequency, so the shift applies twice [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert MHz and kHz to hertz before substituting. Mixing prefixes gives an answer wrong by a power of ten.

**12.** **(a)** An alternating p.d. is applied to a piezoelectric crystal [1]; the crystal changes shape and vibrates, emitting a pulse of ultrasound [1]. Echoes make the same crystal vibrate, producing a small p.d. [1]. The delay gives the depth and the echo strength gives the brightness [1]. Many A-scans in different directions are combined by a computer into a two-dimensional image [1].
**(b)** Time between pulses ≥ 2d/c = 2 × 0.24 / 1540 [1] = **3.1 × 10⁻⁴ s (312 μs)** [1]; rate = 1/312 μs = **3.2 × 10³ pulses per second** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A description question is better with a short sequence: emit, echo, delay, brightness, combine. Keep each step to one clear statement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the gamma camera detects positrons or that PET detects a single photon.
- Omitting "opposite directions" or "at the same time" in the PET explanation.
- Calling the gel a lubricant instead of explaining impedance matching.
- Using d = ct instead of d = ct/2.
- Forgetting to square the bracket in the reflection equation.
- Mixing MHz, kHz and Hz in the Doppler equation.
- Forgetting cos θ, or using the angle to the surface instead of to the flow.
- Giving a one-sided answer to a discussion question.
