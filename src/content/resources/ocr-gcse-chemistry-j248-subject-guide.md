OCR GCSE (9-1) Chemistry A (J248) belongs to OCR's Gateway Science suite and is taken by
students studying chemistry as a separate science. The course starts with particles and
atoms, works through bonding, reactions and their control, and ends with the chemistry
of industrial processes, organic compounds and the Earth's systems. Assessment is by
two written exams sat at the end of the course, at Foundation or Higher tier. Each paper
is 1 hour 45 minutes long, is marked out of 90 and makes up 50% of the grade. J248 was
first examined in 2018. Below is a guide to how its content is arranged and how the
papers use it.

## How the course is organised

The specification has six topics, numbered C1 to C6.

| Topic | Title | Sub-topics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Particles | The particle model; atomic structure |
| C2 | Elements, compounds and mixtures | Purity and separating mixtures; bonding; properties of materials |
| C3 | Chemical reactions | Introducing chemical reactions; energetics; types of chemical reactions; electrolysis |
| C4 | Predicting and identifying reactions and products | Predicting chemical reactions; identifying the products of chemical reactions |
| C5 | Monitoring and controlling chemical reactions | Monitoring chemical reactions; controlling reactions; equilibria |
| C6 | Global challenges | Improving processes and products; organic chemistry; interpreting and interacting with Earth systems |

C1 to C3 lay the foundations: what substances are made of, how their particles are
held together, and what happens when they react. C4 to C6 use those ideas to predict
reactions, measure and control them, and apply chemistry to real-world challenges.

## How it's assessed

| Tier | Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | J248/01 Paper 1 | C1 to C3 and practical skills | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Foundation | J248/02 Paper 2 | C4 to C6 and practical skills | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J248/03 Paper 3 | C1 to C3 and practical skills | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J248/04 Paper 4 | C4 to C6 and practical skills | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |

Each candidate takes a pair of papers at one tier: Papers 1 and 2 for Foundation, or
Papers 3 and 4 for Higher. The course is linear, so both are sat at the end.

The second paper of each pair is not limited to C4 to C6. OCR states that it assumes
knowledge of the first three topics and includes synoptic assessment, so a question on
equilibria or organic chemistry may also need bonding or reaction types from earlier in
the course. Practical skills are tested on both papers at both tiers.

## How the parts fit together

Several threads run from the first half of the course into the second.

- **Particles and structure.** The particle model and atomic structure in C1 explain the
  bonding and properties of materials in C2, which in turn explain why substances behave
  as they do in C4 and C6.
- **Reactions and equations.** The reaction types and energetics introduced in C3 are
  the basis for predicting reactions in C4 and for understanding rates and equilibria in
  C5.
- **Electrolysis and products.** Electrolysis in C3 connects to identifying the
  products of reactions in C4 and to improving industrial processes in C6.

Alongside the content, two skills appear throughout. The first is quantitative work:
writing and balancing equations and carrying out chemical calculations. The second is
practical work: planning, observing, measuring and evaluating, which both papers
assess. Building both skills steadily is more effective than leaving them to the final
revision period.

## Planning your study

The topic numbers give a natural sequence, since each topic depends on its
predecessors. One way to pace the course:

- **First phase:** C1 to C3, with plenty of equation-writing and calculation practice
  and the relevant practical activities.
- **Second phase:** C4 to C6, returning to C1 to C3 whenever a new topic relies on them.
- **Final phase:** mixed revision across all six topics, full papers at your tier and
  targeted practice on calculations and practical questions.

Your teachers will discuss tier entry with you; whichever tier you sit, both papers
cover the same split of topics. Keep a single page of the equations and calculation
methods you need and add to it as each topic finishes. Start exam-style questions as
soon as a topic is taught and use the exam preparation page for detailed paper
technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OCR GCSE Chemistry A assessed?**
By two written papers at Foundation or Higher tier. Each paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 90 marks and counts for 50% of the GCSE.

**Which topics are on J248 Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 (Foundation) and Paper 3 (Higher) cover C1 to C3; Paper 2 (Foundation) and Paper 4 (Higher) cover C4 to C6. Practical skills are assessed on all of them.

**Is the second J248 paper synoptic?**
Yes. Papers 2 and 4 assume knowledge of C1 to C3 and include synoptic assessment.

**What are the six topics in OCR GCSE Chemistry A?**
Particles; elements, compounds and mixtures; chemical reactions; predicting and identifying reactions and products; monitoring and controlling chemical reactions; and global challenges.

**Can I mix Foundation and Higher papers?**
No. Both papers are taken at the same tier: Papers 1 and 2 at Foundation or Papers 3 and 4 at Higher.
