These questions cover key question 1.5 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, on the motives for empire and its effects in
Africa, India, Cuba and the Philippines.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the decisions of the Berlin Conference of 1884–85. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** military or strategic reasons for gaining colonies. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the French policy of assimilation in Senegal. **[4]**

**4.** Describe how Lugard governed Northern Nigeria. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** people who campaigned against Leopold II's rule in the Congo. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the terms of the Treaty of Paris, 1898. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did British rule in India change after 1857? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why was there opposition within the United States to keeping the Philippines? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "The impact of European rule on Africans was much the same whatever system was used." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The United States went to war with Spain in 1898 mainly for economic reasons." How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined report by a missionary in the Congo, 1903. "The village was silent when we
arrived. The men were in the forest gathering rubber, and the women had been taken to the post until the baskets were
full. The chief showed me the basket of the last collection. 'If it is light,' he said, 'the soldiers count our
hands.'"

What can you learn from Source A about the methods used to collect rubber in the Congo Free State? Use the source and
your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study Source B, which is invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary American cartoon from 1899, written for this page. Uncle Sam
stands in a classroom, holding a book labelled "Civilisation". On the front bench sit four small, unhappy children
labelled "Philippines", "Puerto Rico", "Cuba" and "Hawaii". Behind them, a row of older, smiling pupils labelled with
the names of US states read their books.

What is the message of the cartoon described in Source B? Use details of the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: fourteen states met in Berlin, called by Bismarck (1); it set rules for claiming African
territory (1); a claim required effective occupation (1); powers had to notify others of new claims (1); it recognised
Leopold II's Congo Free State (1); it declared free trade and navigation on the Congo and Niger rivers (1); it
condemned the slave trade (1); no Africans were represented (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: naval bases (1); coaling stations for steamships (1); control of sea routes such as the
route through Suez (1); recruiting colonial soldiers (1); denying strategic positions to rivals (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: the aim was to make Africans culturally French (1); Africans who adopted the French
language, education and way of life could in theory become citizens (1); citizens in the Four Communes elected a
deputy to the French parliament (1); French schools were set up, including one for chiefs' sons at Saint-Louis (1);
rule was direct, through French officials (1); in practice most Senegalese stayed subjects without rights (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: through indirect rule (1); the emirs kept their positions after the conquest of Kano and
Sokoto in 1903 (1); they collected taxes for the British (1); they kept their Islamic courts (1); British residents
advised and supervised them (1); Christian missionaries were limited in the north (1); it was cheap because Britain had
few officials (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Roger Casement (1); E. D. Morel (1); George Washington Williams (1); Mark Twain, who
wrote a satire against Leopold (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: signed in December 1898 (1); Spain gave up its claim to Cuba (1); Spain ceded Puerto Rico
(1) and Guam (1) to the USA; Spain ceded the Philippines (1); the USA paid $20 million (1); the US Senate ratified it in
February 1899 after a fierce debate (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **the rising showed
the Company could not govern safely**, so Parliament transferred power to the Crown in 1858; **religious grievances**
had helped cause the rising, so the Queen's Proclamation promised not to interfere with religion; **annexations** had
angered rulers, so the Doctrine of Lapse was abandoned and princes were treated as allies; **the sepoy army had
rebelled**, so the proportion of British troops was increased and recruitment changed. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **principle**: ruling people without their consent contradicted the
Declaration of Independence and the USA's own anti-colonial origins; **the war against Aguinaldo** from 1899 showed
Filipinos did not want US rule and cost American lives; **cost and danger**: a distant colony would need a large army
and navy and could drag the USA into Asian conflicts; **racial and economic fears**: some opponents did not want new
peoples to become part of the USA or to compete with American workers and farmers. The Anti-Imperialist League,
including Mark Twain and Andrew Carnegie, organised opposition. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:** in all
three cases Africans lost political independence after military conquest; economies were reshaped for European needs
(groundnuts, taxes, rubber); Africans had few rights. **Disagree:** in Northern Nigeria the emirs kept power, courts and
religion; in Senegal a minority gained citizenship and representation; in the Congo forced labour and terror caused
huge loss of life, far worse than elsewhere. **Judgement:** the loss of independence was common to all, but daily
experience varied enormously, so the statement is only partly true. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** American investment in Cuban sugar and trade was damaged by the war of
independence; business interests wanted markets in Asia, for which the Philippines offered a route. **Disagree:**
public sympathy for Cubans suffering in Spanish camps, stirred by the press; the explosion of the *Maine* and the
belief that Spain was responsible; strategic arguments for bases, influenced by Mahan; the Teller Amendment, which ruled
out annexing Cuba, suggests motives were not simply about gain; many business leaders at first opposed war as bad for
trade. **Judgement:** for example, public opinion and the *Maine* explain why war came in April 1898, while economic and
strategic motives explain what the USA kept afterwards. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5–6): developed inferences supported from the source and context. Level 2 (2–4): inferences
or details explained. Level 1 (1): copies or paraphrases. Indicative content: whole villages were forced to work, with
men sent into the forest; women were taken as hostages until quotas were met; quotas were enforced by soldiers of the
Force Publique; "count our hands" refers to the cutting off of hands as punishment or proof that bullets had been used
on people; people lived in fear. Context: rubber demand rose in the 1890s; reports such as Casement's (1904)
confirmed these methods. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (6–7): explains a message using details and context. Level 3 (4–5): explains a message
supported by details. Level 2 (2–3): describes details or gives an unsupported message. Level 1 (1): surface reading.
Indicative content: **message**: the USA presented itself as a teacher bringing civilisation to new territories, which
were treated as children not ready to govern themselves; the unhappy faces suggest they did not want the lesson;
**context**: acquisitions of 1898 (the Treaty of Paris and the annexation of Hawaii), the debate over imperialism and
the war in the Philippines from 1899; the cartoon reflects the "civilising mission" argument, either supporting or
mocking it. **[7]**
