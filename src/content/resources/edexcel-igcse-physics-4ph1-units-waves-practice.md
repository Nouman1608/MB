These questions cover sub-topic 3(a), Units (statement 3.1), of Pearson
Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1). Where needed, the speed of
electromagnetic waves in free space is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

## Section A

**1.** State the unit of frequency and the unit of wavelength. **[2]**

**2.** Convert:

**(a)** 3.5 kHz to Hz **[1]**
**(b)** 8.0 ms to s **[1]**
**(c)** 720 nm to m, in standard form **[1]**

**3.** State why refractive index has no unit. **[1]**

**4.** A light ray strikes a glass block at 20° to the surface. State the angle of incidence. **[1]**

**5.** Show that the unit of frequency × wavelength is m/s. **[2]**

**6.** A student's calculator shows sin 30 = −0.988. State what the student must change. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A wave on a rope has a time period of 0.25 s and a wavelength of 1.2 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wave speed. **[2]**

**8.** A mobile phone signal has a frequency of 1.8 GHz.

**(a)** Write this frequency in Hz, in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of the signal, in cm. **[3]**

**9.** Ultraviolet radiation has a frequency of 1.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz.

**(a)** Calculate its wavelength in metres. **[2]**
**(b)** Convert your answer to nanometres. **[1]**

**10.** A student measures the speed of water waves in a ripple tank. She counts 15 waves passing a point in 5.0 s. The distance between one crest and the next is 1.8 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the waves. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the waves in m/s. **[3]**

**11.** An oscilloscope displays a sound wave. The time base is set to 2.0 ms per division. One complete wave covers 2.5 divisions.

**(a)** Calculate the time period of the wave in seconds. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency of the sound. **[2]**
**(c)** The sound travels at 340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength. **[2]**

**12.** A ray of light passes from air into a plastic block. The angle between the incident ray and the surface of the block is 50°. The angle of refraction is 27°.

**(a)** State the angle of incidence. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the refractive index of the plastic. **[2]**
**(c)** A student writes the answer to (b) with the unit "degrees". Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**13.** A student calculates the wavelength of a 5.0 kHz sound travelling at 340 m/s and gets 68 m.

**(a)** Identify the mistake the student made. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the correct wavelength. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a sense check on the size of the answer could have shown that 68 m was wrong. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Frequency: hertz (Hz) [1]. Wavelength: metre (m) [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** 3500 Hz [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 0.0080 s [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 7.2 × 10⁻⁷ m [1]. **[1]**

**3.** It is the ratio of two quantities with the same kind of value (sin i ÷ sin r), so the units cancel [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 90° − 20° = 70° (angles are measured from the normal) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** 1 Hz = 1/s [1]; so Hz × m = (1/s) × m = m/s [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Set the calculator to degree mode (it is in radian mode) [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ 0.25 [1] = 4.0 Hz [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** v = f × λ = 4.0 × 1.2 [1] = 4.8 m/s [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 1.8 × 10⁹ Hz [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** λ = v ÷ f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.8 × 10⁹ [1] = 0.167 m [1] = 17 cm (16.7 cm) [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** λ = v ÷ f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.5 × 10¹⁵ [1] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁷ m [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 200 nm [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** f = 15 ÷ 5.0 [1] = 3.0 Hz [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** λ = 1.8 cm = 0.018 m [1]; v = f × λ = 3.0 × 0.018 [1] = 0.054 m/s [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** T = 2.5 × 2.0 ms = 5.0 ms [1] = 0.0050 s [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** f = 1 ÷ 0.0050 [1] = 200 Hz [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 200 [1] = 1.7 m [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** 90° − 50° = 40° [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** n = sin i ÷ sin r = sin 40° ÷ sin 27° = 0.643 ÷ 0.454 [1] = 1.4 (1.42) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** n is a ratio of two sines, which are pure numbers, so it has no unit; degrees are the unit of the angles, not of n [1]. **[1]**

**13. (a)** The frequency was used as 5.0 Hz instead of being converted from 5.0 kHz to 5000 Hz [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** λ = 340 ÷ 5000 [1] = 0.068 m (6.8 cm) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** A sound of several thousand hertz has a wavelength of centimetres, not tens of metres; a 68 m wavelength would need a frequency of only 5 Hz [1]. **[1]**
