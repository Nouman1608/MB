## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by soil erosion. **[1]**

**2.** Name the **two** agents that erode soil. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** types of mass movement that can follow soil erosion. **[3]**

**4.** State **two** strategies that reduce wind speed and wind erosion. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by contour ploughing. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how heavy rain erodes soil from a bare hillside. **[4]**

**7.** Explain how overgrazing can cause soil erosion. **[3]**

**8.** A forest on a steep slope above a village is cleared for farming. Describe **three** impacts this
could have on the village. **[3]**

**9.** *Invented data.* The table shows soil lost from three plots on a farm in one year.

| Plot | Treatment | Soil lost (tonnes per hectare) |
|---|---|---|
| A | Bare soil after harvest | 36 |
| B | Cover crop planted after harvest | 9 |
| C | Crop residue and manure added to bare soil | 24 |

(a) Calculate the percentage reduction in soil loss on Plot B compared with Plot A.

(b) Explain why Plot B lost less soil than Plot A.

(c) Explain why Plot C lost less soil than Plot A. **[6]**

**10.** Explain how soil erosion on farmland can lead to malnutrition. **[3]**

**11.** Describe and explain how terracing and bunds reduce soil erosion. **[4]**

**12.** A government wants to reduce soil erosion in a dry, windy region where farmers grow crops on flat
land. Suggest and explain the most suitable strategies, and give **one** limitation of your choice. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The removal of soil, especially the topsoil, faster than it forms [1].

**2.** Wind [1]; water [1].

**3.** Landslides [1]; rockslides [1]; mudslides [1].

**4.** Bunds [1]; wind breaks [1].

**5.** Ploughing furrows across a slope, following the contour lines, instead of up and down it [1].

**6.** Any four:

- Raindrops hit the bare soil and splash particles loose [1].
- Rain falls faster than it can soak in, so water flows over the surface as run-off [1].
- On the slope, run-off speeds up and has more energy [1].
- Run-off carries loose soil downhill [1].
- Run-off gathers into channels (rills and gullies), removing more soil [1].
- With no plants, there are no roots to bind the soil [1].

**7.** Any three:

- Too many animals eat the vegetation faster than it regrows [1].
- The soil is left bare [1].
- Roots no longer bind the soil [1].
- Animals' hooves trample and loosen the surface [1].
- Bare, loose soil is easily removed by wind and water [1].

**8.** Any three:

- Mass movement such as landslides or mudslides could bury homes, fields or roads [1].
- Eroded soil could silt the river, increasing flooding in the village [1].
- Crop yields on the cleared slope fall as topsoil is lost, reducing food and income [1].
- People may be displaced if homes or farmland are destroyed [1].
- Loss of forest habitat reduces biodiversity and forest products [1].

**9.** (a) (36 - 9) / 36 x 100 = 27 / 36 x 100 = **75%** [2: 1 for the working, 1 for the answer].
(b) The cover crop maintains vegetation cover [1]: its roots bind the soil and the plants reduce surface
run-off and absorb water [1].
(c) Organic matter improves soil structure [1], binding particles into larger crumbs that are harder for
wind and water to carry away [1].

**10.** Any three:

- Erosion removes the fertile topsoil, with its nutrients and organic matter [1].
- Crops grown in the thin, infertile soil that remains give lower yields [1].
- Less food is produced for farming families [1].
- People do not get enough food or a varied diet, leading to malnutrition [1].
- Lower income means families cannot buy food to make up the shortfall [1].

**11.** Terracing, any two:

- The slope is cut into a series of flat steps [1].
- Water is held on each step and soaks in [1].
- This reduces movement of soil and surface run-off [1].

Bunds, any two:

- Low banks of earth or stones are built along the contours [1].
- They hold back water, reducing surface run-off [1].
- They also reduce wind speed at ground level and wind erosion [1].

**12.** Suitable strategies, each suggested [1] and explained [1], any two:

- **Wind breaks** of trees across the prevailing wind [1] reduce wind speed and wind erosion and protect
  crops [1].
- **Maintaining vegetation cover**, such as leaving stubble or planting cover crops [1]: roots bind the
  soil and it is not left bare to the wind [1].
- **Bunds** [1] reduce wind speed at ground level and wind erosion [1].
- **Adding organic matter** [1] improves soil structure, so particles are less easily lifted by wind [1].

Limitation, any one, with explanation (2 marks):

- Wind breaks take land out of crop production [1] and take years to grow tall enough to work [1].
- Cover crops need water, which is scarce in a dry region [1], so they may not establish [1].
- Organic matter may be in short supply [1], because manure and crop residue are also used as fuel or
  animal feed [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Suggesting terracing or contour ploughing for flat, windy land in question 12.
- In 9(a), dividing by 9 instead of the original 36.
- Naming a strategy without its mechanism.
- Stopping at "yields fall" in question 10 without linking to food and people.
- Writing about weathering instead of erosion.
