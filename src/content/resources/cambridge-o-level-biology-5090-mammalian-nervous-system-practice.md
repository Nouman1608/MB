## Section A

**1.** Which parts make up the central nervous system?

A: the brain and the nerves

B: the brain and the spinal cord

C: the spinal cord and the nerves

D: the nerves and the sense organs

**[1]**

**2.** Which neurone carries an impulse from the central nervous system to a muscle?

A: sensory neurone

B: relay neurone

C: motor neurone

D: receptor neurone

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a reflex action. **[2]**

**4.** State what is stored in the vesicles at a synapse. **[1]**

**5.** Name the two kinds of effector. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student touches a hot beaker and pulls her hand away before she feels pain.

**(a)** Name the receptor type that detects the stimulus and state where it is found. **[2]**

**(b)** List, in order, the parts of the reflex arc from the receptor to the response. **[5]**

**(c)** Explain why this response is rapid. **[2]**

**(d)** State why this type of response is useful. **[1]**

**7.** The diagram of a synapse shows the end of one neurone, a gap and the start of a second neurone.

**(a)** Name the gap between the two neurones. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the events that occur at the synapse when an impulse arrives at the first neurone. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why an impulse cannot travel from the second neurone back to the first across the synapse. **[2]**

**8.** In a reflex pathway, an impulse travels 0.75 m along a sensory neurone and 0.75 m along a motor neurone. Impulses travel at 75 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the total time taken for impulses to travel along the neurones. **[2]**

**(b)** The measured time between stimulus and response was longer than your answer. Suggest **one** reason. **[1]**

**9.** A student measures reaction time by dropping a ruler. The distance the ruler fell before it was caught was 12 cm, 15 cm and 9 cm in three trials.

**(a)** Calculate the mean distance. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the student should have the same starting position for each trial. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why catching a ruler is a voluntary action and not a reflex. **[2]**

**10.** Distinguish between the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why the nervous system is described as a coordinating system. **[2]**

**12.** A drug blocks the receptor proteins at one synapse in a reflex arc, so that neurotransmitter can no longer bind to them. Predict the effect on the reflex response and explain your prediction. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A rapid [1] and automatic response to a stimulus [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Neurotransmitter molecules [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Muscles [1] and glands [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Heat (temperature) receptors [1] in the skin of the hand [1].

**(b)** Receptor [1], sensory neurone [1], relay neurone [1], motor neurone [1], effector (a muscle) [1].

**(c)** The pathway is short with few neurones [1] and few synapses, and the brain does not have to decide first [1].

**(d)** It protects the body from damage [1]. **[10]**

**7.**

**(a)** The synaptic gap [1].

**(b)** The impulse stimulates vesicles to release neurotransmitter into the gap [1]. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap and binds to receptor proteins on the second neurone [1]. This stimulates an impulse in the second neurone [1].

**(c)** Vesicles containing neurotransmitter are only in the first neurone [1], and receptor proteins are only on the second, so transmission can only go one way [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** Total distance = 0.75 + 0.75 = 1.5 m [1]. Time = 1.5 divided by 75 = 0.02 s [1].

**(b)** The time spent at the synapses [1] (or the time taken for the effector to respond). **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** (12 + 15 + 9) divided by 3 = 36 divided by 3 = 12 cm [1].

**(b)** So that the only variable is the speed of the response and the distance is a fair measure [1].

**(c)** The student has to decide to catch it, using the brain [1]. A reflex is automatic, so it needs no decision [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The CNS is the brain and spinal cord [1]. The PNS is the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord [1]. **[2]**

**11.** It detects stimuli and sends impulses to the CNS [1], which sends impulses to effectors so that the body parts work together as needed [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The response would be reduced or absent [1]. Neurotransmitter is released but cannot bind to the receptor proteins [1], so no impulse is stimulated in the next neurone and the effector does not receive a message [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "quick" instead of "rapid and automatic" for a reflex.
- Putting the relay neurone outside the CNS.
- Saying that the impulse jumps across the gap, rather than that a chemical diffuses across it.
- Leaving out the unit in a time calculation.
- Naming the effector as the "response" instead of the muscle or gland.
