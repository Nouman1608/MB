## Questions

**1.** Explain the difference between microeconomics and macroeconomics. **[3]**

**2.** Classify each of the following as microeconomic or macroeconomic.

**(a)** The wage paid by one bakery. **[1]**
**(b)** The total output of a country. **[1]**
**(c)** A farmer's choice between two crops. **[1]**
**(d)** The rate of unemployment in an economy. **[1]**

**3.** Identify two decision makers studied in microeconomics and state one decision made by each. **[4]**

**4.** Country K (invented) has real output of 1,250 units in year 1 and 1,310 units in year 2. In year 2 it has 12,000 people in its labour force, of whom 900 are unemployed.

**(a)** Calculate the growth rate of output, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the unemployment rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why these figures are macroeconomic. **[1]**

**5.** The table shows the market for candles at a craft fair (invented). Price is per candle and quantities are per day.

| Price | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 160 | 40 |
| 8 | 130 | 70 |
| 10 | 100 | 100 |
| 12 | 70 | 130 |
| 14 | 40 | 160 |

**(a)** Identify the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the shortage at a price of 6 and the surplus at a price of 14. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the revenue sellers receive at a price of 12. **[2]**

**6.** Using the table in question 5, explain how the market moves from a price of 12 to equilibrium. **[4]**

**7.** Explain the roles of buyers and sellers in allocating scarce resources in a market system. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why the economic problem creates three key questions for any society. **[5]**

**9.** A small tailoring workshop (invented) can make 40 shirts a day in two ways. Method 1 uses 5 workers and tools costing 80 a day. Method 2 uses 2 workers and a cutting machine costing 260 a day. The daily wage per worker is w.

**(a)** Calculate the total daily cost of each method when w = 70. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost per shirt of each method when w = 70. **[2]**
**(c)** The wage falls to 40. Which method is cheaper, and what does this show about how production is decided? **[2]**

**10.** Explain the three functions of the price mechanism. **[6]**

**11.** In the candle market in question 5, demand rises by 30 candles at every price. The original demand is Qd = 250 - 15P and supply is Qs = 15P - 50.

**(a)** Find the excess demand at a price of 10 once demand has risen. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the price and quantity at which the market now settles. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate by what percentage the price has risen compared with before. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how the price mechanism restores equilibrium after the rise in demand. **[3]**
**(e)** Explain what happens to the resources used to make candles. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss whether a market system is a fair way to decide for whom goods and services are produced. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Microeconomics studies the choices of individual decision makers and individual markets [1]. Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole [1]. For example, the price of one product is micro, and the general price level is macro [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs both sides stated, and a pair of examples shows the contrast clearly.

**2.** **(a)** Micro [1]. **(b)** Macro [1]. **(c)** Micro [1]. **(d)** Macro [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Learn the test: one decision maker or market is micro, the whole economy is macro.

**3.** Household or consumer [1]: decides which goods to buy, or how much to save [1]. Firm [1]: decides what to produce, which method to use, or what price to charge [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A worker is another decision maker you could name; make sure the decision you state belongs to the one you named.

**4.** **(a)** Growth = (1,310 - 1,250) / 1,250 x 100 [1] = 60 / 1,250 x 100 = **4.8%** [1].
**(b)** Unemployment rate = 900 / 12,000 x 100 [1] = **7.5%** [1].
**(c)** They measure output and unemployment for the whole country, not one firm or market [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the formula and each substitution line by line, so your working is easy to follow and a slip is easy to spot.

**5.** **(a)** Equilibrium price is 10 [1] and equilibrium quantity is 100 [1].
**(b)** At 6, shortage = 160 - 40 = **120** [1]. At 14, surplus = 160 - 40 = **120** [1].
**(c)** Only 70 are bought, the smaller quantity [1], so revenue = 12 x 70 = **840** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), use the smaller of the two quantities, because that is what is actually traded.

**6.** At 12, quantity supplied is 130 and quantity demanded is 70, a surplus of 60 [1]. Stock goes unsold, so sellers lower the price [1]. A lower price makes buyers want more and sellers offer less [1]. This continues until the price reaches 10, where both are 100 and the surplus has gone [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the steps in order: cause, seller response, buyer and seller reaction, end point. Quote figures from the table.

**7.** Buyers show what they want by being willing and able to pay, which makes up demand [1]. Sellers supply goods and services to earn profit, which makes up supply [1]. Resources are scarce, so they go to products that buyers will pay enough for sellers to cover their costs [1]. Their interaction sets a price and quantity, which settles where resources are used [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each side to scarce resources rather than only describing demand and supply.

**8.** Wants are unlimited but resources are scarce [1], so society must choose how to use its resources. This creates the questions of what to produce, for example more food or more housing [1], how to produce, for example by hand or by machine [1], and for whom, for example who gets the output [1]. Each choice has an opportunity cost because using resources one way means giving up another [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An example for each question shows you understand what the question means.

**9.** **(a)** Method 1 = 5 x 70 + 80 = **430** [1]. Method 2 = 2 x 70 + 260 = **400** [1].
**(b)** Method 1 = 430 / 40 = **10.75** per shirt [1]. Method 2 = 400 / 40 = **10.00** per shirt [1].
**(c)** At w = 40, Method 1 = 5 x 40 + 80 = 280 and Method 2 = 2 x 40 + 260 = 340, so **Method 1 is cheaper** [1]. How goods are produced depends on the relative prices of labour and capital, so firms choose the cheapest method [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the cost for both methods before you compare them, so the conclusion is supported by working.

**10.** Signalling [1]: prices tell producers what buyers want; a rising price shows demand is strong [1]. Incentive [1]: a higher price means more profit for producers, so they supply more, and buyers have a reason to buy less [1]. Rationing [1]: when goods are scarce the price rises until only those willing and able to pay buy them [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For each function, name it and then say what it does, ideally with a short example.

**11.** **(a)** At 10, new Qd = 130 and Qs = 100, so excess demand = **30** [1].
**(b)** New Qd = 280 - 15P. Set 280 - 15P = 15P - 50 [1], so 30P = 330, **P = 11**, and Q = 280 - 165 = **115** [1].
**(c)** (11 - 10) / 10 x 100 = **10%** [1].
**(d)** At the old price there is a shortage of 30 [1]. Sellers see excess demand and raise the price [1]. A higher price contracts quantity demanded and extends quantity supplied until both are 115 at a price of 11 [1].
**(e)** More resources move into candle making [1], because the higher price and profit encourage sellers to supply more, which draws land, labour and capital from other uses [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) does best when it follows the chain from shortage to price change to new equilibrium and gives both figures.

**12.** For: people who earn more can buy more, and the system rewards effort and skill [1]. Buyers choose what suits them, and no one decides for them [1]. The same price rule applies to everyone alike [1]. Against: output goes to those with the ability to pay, not those with greatest need, so a low-income household may go without essentials [1]. A person's income may reflect luck or inheritance as well as effort, so the result may not be seen as fair [1]. A person with no income has no demand, however great the need [1]. Judgement: whether it is fair depends on what fair means. It rewards effort but leaves unequal outcomes [1], so a justified conclusion states which side is stronger and why [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" calls for both sides and a reasoned conclusion. Strong answers define fairness before they judge.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Answering a question on the difference between two terms by describing only one.
- Describing micro and macro by size alone.
- Using the larger quantity to find revenue at a disequilibrium price.
- Saying a surplus raises the price.
- Showing no working for growth or unemployment rates.
- Giving the three allocation questions without saying why scarcity creates them.
- Writing "for whom" answers in terms of need rather than ability to pay.
- Listing the price mechanism's functions with no explanation of each.
- Stopping at one side of a "discuss" question or giving no conclusion.
