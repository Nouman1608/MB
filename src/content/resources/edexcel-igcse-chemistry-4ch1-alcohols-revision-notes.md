These notes condense sub-topic 4(e) Alcohols (points 4.29C to 4.33C) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). Every point has a C reference, so the whole sub-topic is **Paper 2 only**. Worked examples and model answers are in the [alcohols study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alcohols/); then try the [alcohols practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alcohols-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alcohol | Organic compound with the -OH functional group |
| Oxidation | Here, gain of oxygen: ethanol to ethanoic acid |
| Fermentation | Conversion of glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide by yeast enzymes |
| Hydration | Addition of water (steam) to a molecule; ethene to ethanol |
| Denature | Permanent change in an enzyme's shape, so it stops working |
| Catalyst | Speeds up the reaction without being used up |

## The series

| Name | Formula | Structural formula |
|---|---|---|
| Methanol | CH₃OH | CH₃OH |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH | CH₃CH₂OH |
| Propan-1-ol (propanol) | C₃H₇OH | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| Butan-1-ol (butanol) | C₄H₉OH | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH |

- **General formula:** CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH.
- Only the 1-ol forms of propanol and butanol are required.
- In displayed formulae the oxygen has **two** bonds: one to carbon and one to hydrogen. Draw the O-H bond.
- Names ending -ol; "propanol" and "butanol" are acceptable.

## Oxidation of ethanol

| Route | Conditions | Product | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combustion | Burn in air or oxygen | CO₂ + H₂O | Clean flame; heat released |
| Microbial oxidation | Air plus bacteria | Ethanoic acid (vinegar) | Sour taste |
| Chemical oxidation | Heat with potassium dichromate(VI) in dilute sulfuric acid | Ethanoic acid | **Orange to green** |

- Combustion: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
- Oxidation by dichromate(VI): the dichromate(VI) is the oxidising agent and is reduced to green chromium(III).
- An opened bottle of wine turns sour because the ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid.

## Manufacture of ethanol

| | Hydration of ethene | Fermentation of glucose |
|---|---|---|
| Equation | C₂H₄ + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅OH | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ |
| Catalyst | Phosphoric acid | Enzymes in yeast |
| Temperature | About 300 °C | About 30 °C |
| Pressure | 60 to 70 atm | Atmospheric |
| Air | Not relevant | Absent |
| Raw material | Ethene from crude oil (non-renewable) | Sugar or glucose from plants (renewable) |
| Rate | Fast | Slow |
| Process | Continuous | Batch |
| Product | Pure | Dilute; needs distillation |

### Why fermentation conditions matter (4.33C)

- **No air:** otherwise the ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid, and aerobic respiration forms carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol.
- **About 30 °C:** below this the enzymes work slowly; above about 40 °C the enzymes denature and the yeast stops working.
- Use an airlock to let carbon dioxide out and keep air out.

## Calculation pattern

1. Moles = mass / Mr (ethanol 46, glucose 180, ethene 28).
2. Use the equation ratio: 1 glucose gives 2 ethanol and 2 CO₂; 1 ethene gives 1 ethanol; 1 ethanol burns to give 2 CO₂.
3. Mass = moles x Mr. Gas volume at room temperature and pressure = moles x 24 dm³.
4. Percentage yield = actual / theoretical x 100.

## Answer frames

- **State why fermentation is done without air:** "so that the ethanol is not oxidised to ethanoic acid".
- **Explain the temperature:** "too low, the enzymes are too slow; too high, they denature".
- **Describe the dichromate test:** "heat with acidified potassium dichromate(VI); orange to green".
- **Compare routes:** use renewable or non-renewable raw material, rate, purity, energy cost.

## Exam traps

- Ethanol oxidises to ethanoic acid, not to ethane or ethene.
- The colour change is orange **to** green, not the other way round.
- Fermentation produces carbon dioxide as well as ethanol.
- Conditions are a pair: the catalyst and the temperature. For hydration, also quote the pressure.
- Do not say that enzymes are "killed" by high temperature. They are denatured.
- This whole sub-topic is Paper 2 only: it cannot appear on Paper 1.

## Self-test

1. Name the functional group in alcohols.
2. Write the structural formula of propan-1-ol.
3. State the general formula of the alcohols.
4. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethanol.
5. State the colour change when ethanol is heated with acidified potassium dichromate(VI).
6. State the conditions for the hydration of ethene.
7. State two conditions for fermentation.
8. 9.2 g of ethanol (Mr = 46) burns completely. Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure.
9. 36 g of glucose (Mr = 180) is fermented. Calculate the mass of ethanol formed.
10. Actual yield of ethanol is 14.72 g in question 9. Calculate the percentage yield.

### Answers

1. -OH (hydroxyl).
2. CH₃CH₂CH₂OH.
3. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH.
4. C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
5. Orange to green.
6. Steam, a phosphoric acid catalyst, about 300 °C and 60 to 70 atm.
7. Any two from: yeast (its enzymes); absence of air; about 30 °C.
8. Moles of ethanol = 9.2 / 46 = 0.20 mol; moles of CO₂ = 0.40 mol; volume = 0.40 x 24 = **9.6 dm³**.
9. Moles of glucose = 36 / 180 = 0.20 mol; moles of ethanol = 0.40 mol; mass = 0.40 x 46 = **18.4 g**.
10. 14.72 / 18.4 x 100 = **80%**.
