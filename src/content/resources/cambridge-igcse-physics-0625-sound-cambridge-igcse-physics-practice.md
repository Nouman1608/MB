## Section A

**1.** State what all sources of sound have in common. **[1]**

**2.** This question is about the frequency of sound.

**(a)** State the approximate range of frequencies audible to humans. **[1]**
**(b)** Define ultrasound. **[1]**

**3.** State the approximate speed of sound in air. **[1]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by an echo. **[1]**

**5.** Sound is a longitudinal wave. Describe how the particles of air move as a sound wave passes. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** An electric bell rings inside a sealed glass jar. A pump slowly removes the air from the jar.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the sound heard. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain this observation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the hammer of the bell can still be seen moving. **[1]**

**7.** Two sounds are displayed on a screen using the same settings. Trace A has peaks 2.0 cm high and shows 4 complete waves across the screen. Trace B has peaks 1.0 cm high and shows 8 complete waves across the screen.

**(a)** State which sound is louder and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** State which sound has the higher pitch and give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** The frequency of sound A is 250 Hz. State the frequency of sound B. **[1]**

**8.** Two students measure the speed of sound in air in a large, open field.

**(a)** Describe a method they could use. Include what they measure and the equipment they use. **[4]**
**(b)** They obtain a distance of 660 m and a mean time of 1.96 s. Calculate the speed of sound. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why they repeat the measurement several times. **[1]**

**9.** A student stands 60 m from a tall wall and claps so that each clap coincides with the echo of the one before. A friend times 30 intervals between claps as 10.8 s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of sound. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the friend times 30 intervals instead of one. **[1]**

**10.** During a storm, a girl sees a flash of lightning and hears the thunder 3.0 s later. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from the girl to the lightning. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the time taken for the light to reach her can be ignored. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A loudspeaker cone vibrates backwards and forwards, producing sound of frequency 2000 Hz.

**(a)** Describe how the vibrating cone produces compressions and rarefactions in the air. **[2]**
**(b)** State the order of the speed of sound in solids, liquids and gases, starting with the fastest. **[1]**
**(c)** Sound travels at 340 m/s in air and 1500 m/s in water. Calculate the wavelength of the 2000 Hz sound in each. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* An engineer tests a steel plate for internal cracks using ultrasound. The speed of ultrasound in the steel is 6000 m/s.

**(a)** State the minimum frequency of ultrasound. **[1]**
**(b)** The echo from the back surface of the plate returns 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ s after the pulse is sent. Calculate the thickness of the plate. **[2]**
**(c)** A second, earlier echo returns after 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ s. Calculate the depth of the crack below the surface. **[2]**
**(d)** State why this is called non-destructive testing. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* A ship uses sonar. The speed of sound in sea water is 1500 m/s.

**(a)** A pulse reflects from the sea bed and returns after 0.80 s. Calculate the depth of the sea bed. **[2]**
**(b)** As the ship passes over a shoal of fish, an extra echo returns after 0.20 s. Calculate the depth of the shoal. **[1]**
**(c)** State one medical use of ultrasound. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** They are all **vibrating** [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** **20 Hz to 20 000 Hz** [1].
**(b)** Sound with a frequency **higher than 20 kHz** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any value in the range **330–350 m/s** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A **reflection** of sound waves from a surface [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The particles vibrate backwards and forwards about fixed positions [1], **parallel** to the direction in which the wave travels [1]. **[2]**

*Common mistake:* "the particles move from the source to the listener". They only vibrate; energy is what travels.

**6. (a)** The sound becomes fainter (quieter), until it can hardly be heard [1].
**(b)** Sound needs a medium (particles) to travel through [1]; as air is removed there are fewer particles to pass on the vibrations, and a vacuum cannot transmit sound [1].
**(c)** Light does not need a medium; it can travel through a vacuum [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** **A** [1]; it has the larger amplitude [1].
**(b)** **B** [1]; it has the higher frequency (more waves in the same time) [1].
**(c)** Twice A's frequency: **500 Hz** [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* linking the taller trace to higher pitch. Height shows loudness; spacing shows pitch.

**8. (a)** Measure a large distance (at least 100 m) between the students with a long tape or trundle wheel [1]. One student makes a sound with a visible action, such as banging two blocks together above the head [1]. The other starts a stopwatch on **seeing** the action and stops it on **hearing** the sound [1]. Speed = distance ÷ time [1].
**(b)** speed = 660 ÷ 1.96 [1] = **337 m/s** (340 m/s) [1].
**(c)** To reduce the effect of random errors such as reaction time, by averaging [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** One interval: t = 10.8 ÷ 30 = 0.36 s [1]; sound travels to the wall and back: 2 × 60 = 120 m [1]; speed = 120 ÷ 0.36 = **333 m/s** [1].
**(b)** One interval is too short to time accurately; reaction time would be a large fraction of it [1]. **[4]**

*Common mistake:* using 60 m, which gives 167 m/s. The echo travels there and back.

**10. (a)** distance = speed × time = 340 × 3.0 [1] = **1020 m** [1].
**(b)** Light travels so much faster than sound (about a million times) that its travel time is negligible [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** As the cone moves forward it pushes air particles together, forming a compression [1]; as it moves back the particles spread out, forming a rarefaction [1].
**(b)** **Solids, liquids, gases** [1].
**(c)** Air: λ = 340 ÷ 2000 = **0.17 m**; water: λ = 1500 ÷ 2000 = **0.75 m** [1 each]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** **20 kHz** (20 000 Hz) [1].
**(b)** thickness = (6000 × 8.0 × 10⁻⁵) ÷ 2 [1] = **0.24 m** [1].
**(c)** depth = (6000 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁵) ÷ 2 [1] = **0.090 m** (9.0 cm) [1].
**(d)** The plate is tested without being damaged or cut open [1]. **[6]**

*Common mistake:* forgetting to halve. The time is for the pulse to travel to the reflecting surface and back.

**13. (a)** depth = (1500 × 0.80) ÷ 2 [1] = **600 m** [1].
**(b)** (1500 × 0.20) ÷ 2 = **150 m** [1].
**(c)** Medical scanning of soft tissue, for example scanning a fetus [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing sound as transverse or as moving air.
- Missing the factor of 2 in every echo or ultrasound calculation.
- Linking loudness to frequency or pitch to amplitude.
- Describing a speed-of-sound method without saying what is measured and how.
- Saying sound is fastest in gases.
- Calling ultrasound faster than audible sound, rather than higher in frequency.
