**These revision notes compress Section 2 of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059 Paper 1, The
Emergence of Pakistan 1906–47, into timelines.** Each row gives the event, the date, who was
involved and why it mattered to the Pakistan Movement, which is exactly what 4-mark "describe" and
7-mark "explain" questions ask for. Learn the significance column as carefully as the dates.

## Syllabus coverage

**2059 Paper 1, Section 2 (2026 syllabus):** Key Question 6 (development to 1927), Key Question 7
(Khilafat Movement), Key Question 8 (1927–39), Key Question 9 (1940–47) and Key Question 10 (Jinnah,
Allama Iqbal and Rahmat Ali).

## Timeline 1: 1905–1927 (Key Question 6)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1905 | Partition of Bengal (Curzon) | Muslim-majority East Bengal; Muslims gained; Hindus protested |
| Oct 1906 | Simla Deputation (Aga Khan to Minto) | Demand for separate electorates accepted in principle |
| Dec 1906 | All-India Muslim League founded, Dhaka | First Muslim political party for all India |
| 1909 | Morley–Minto Reforms | Separate electorates granted |
| 1911 | Partition of Bengal reversed | Muslims felt British could not be relied on |
| 1913 | Jinnah joins the League | Brings a leading Congress figure into Muslim politics |
| 1916 | Lucknow Pact | Congress accepts separate electorates; high point of unity |
| 1919 | Rowlatt Act; Amritsar Massacre | Repression; anti-British feeling grows |
| 1919 | Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms | Dyarchy in provinces; disappointed Indian leaders |
| 1920–22 | Non-Cooperation Movement | Joint Hindu–Muslim protest, called off by Gandhi |
| 1920s | Shuddhi and sangathan; tabligh and tanzim | Rising communalism and riots |
| 1927 | Delhi Proposals | Muslims offer joint electorates for safeguards |

## Timeline 2: the Khilafat and Hijrat Movements (Key Question 7)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1919 | Khilafat Committee formed; Ali brothers lead | Muslims protest at treatment of the Ottoman caliph |
| 1920 | Delegation to Britain rejected | British would not change peace terms |
| 1920 | Hijrat to Afghanistan | Migrants lose property; Afghanistan closes border |
| 1921 | Moplah uprising | Violence damages Hindu–Muslim trust |
| 1922 | Chauri Chaura; Gandhi suspends Non-Cooperation | Muslims feel let down by Congress |
| 1924 | Turkey abolishes the caliphate | Movement loses its purpose |

**Impact on Muslims:** political mobilisation of ordinary people; new leadership; loss of trust in
Congress; economic hardship from Hijrat.

## Timeline 3: 1927–1939 (Key Question 8)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1927 | Simon Commission appointed (all-British) | Boycotted; Indians asked to write their own constitution |
| 1928 | Nehru Report | Rejects separate electorates and one-third Muslim seats |
| 1929 | Jinnah's 14 Points | Clear statement of Muslim demands |
| Dec 1930 | Iqbal's Allahabad address | Calls for a Muslim state in north-west India |
| 1930–32 | Three Round Table Conferences | No agreement on minorities |
| 1932 | Communal Award | Separate electorates retained |
| 1933 | Rahmat Ali, *Now or Never* | Name "Pakistan" proposed |
| 1935 | Government of India Act | Provincial autonomy; federation never introduced |
| 1937 | Provincial elections | Congress wins most provinces; League weak |
| 1937–39 | Congress ministries | Bande Mataram, Wardha and Vidya Mandir schemes anger Muslims |
| 22 Dec 1939 | Day of Deliverance | League celebrates end of Congress rule |

## Timeline 4: 1940–1947 (Key Question 9)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 23 Mar 1940 | Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution | Formal demand for separate Muslim states |
| 1942 | Cripps Mission | Dominion status after the war; rejected by both parties |
| Aug 1942 | Quit India Movement | Congress leaders jailed; League grows |
| Sep 1944 | Gandhi–Jinnah talks | Fail over the two-nation theory |
| Jun–Jul 1945 | Simla Conference (Wavell) | Fails over who nominates Muslim members |
| 1945–46 | Central and provincial elections | League wins all Muslim central seats |
| 1946 | Cabinet Mission Plan | Groups of provinces; collapses |

| Aug 1947 | Radcliffe Award | Punjab and Bengal boundaries drawn |

## Key individuals (Key Question 10)

- **Muhammad Ali Jinnah:** Lucknow Pact 1916; 14 Points 1929; returned to lead the League in the mid-1930s; 1940 Resolution; negotiator with Cripps, Gandhi, Wavell, the Cabinet Mission and Mountbatten; first Governor-General.
- **Allama Muhammad Iqbal:** poet-philosopher; Allahabad address 1930; urged Jinnah to lead Muslims; died 1938.
- **Choudhary Rahmat Ali:** Cambridge student; *Now or Never* (1933); coined "Pakistan"; wanted complete separation, unlike Iqbal's proposal of a Muslim state in the north-west.

## Worked example

**Question:** Why did the Cabinet Mission Plan fail? **[7]**

**Model answer outline:** The plan proposed a weak centre with three groups of provinces, giving
Muslims autonomy in the north-west and east [context]. The League accepted it because the grouping
offered much of what Pakistan would provide [point 1]. Nehru then stated that Congress would not be
bound by the grouping arrangements once the Constituent Assembly met, so the League withdrew its
acceptance [point 2]. Disagreement over the interim government, including Congress's insistence on
nominating a Muslim, deepened mistrust [point 3]. The League's call for Direct Action and the
violence that followed showed that compromise had collapsed [point 4].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Confusing the 1909 and 1919 reforms.** Morley–Minto (1909) = separate electorates; Montagu–Chelmsford (1919) = dyarchy.
- **Treating the Nehru Report and 14 Points as the same.** The 14 Points were Jinnah's response to the Nehru Report.
- **Narrating instead of explaining.** For 7-mark answers, make each paragraph a reason, beginning with the point.
- **Missing the judgement.** 14-mark answers must say which factor mattered most and why.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was the Communal Award?**
A British decision in 1932 that kept separate electorates for
Muslims and other minorities after the Round Table Conferences failed to agree.

**Why was the 1940 Resolution important?**
It turned the idea of a separate homeland into the League's
official goal.
