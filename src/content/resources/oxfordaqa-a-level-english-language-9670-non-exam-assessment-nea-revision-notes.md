These notes condense the non-exam assessment route of Unit 4 (section 3.4.2) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Language (9670) specification, Version 5.2. For the full explanation and a worked plan, use the [NEA study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-non-exam-assessment-nea/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-non-exam-assessment-nea-practice/). The other route is in the [written unit revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-written-unit-revision-notes/).

## The task in brief

- A **language investigation**: research an aspect of language use.
- **Devise a research question, collect your own data, analyse it.**
- Report of **approximately 2,500 words, excluding data** (section 3.4.2). The overview table says **2,500 to 3,000 words**. Check which figure your centre uses.
- **50 marks.** 30% of the International A-level.
- **Teacher assessed and externally moderated.**
- Any topic is acceptable if its **central focus is language**.
- International A-level only. A student takes this route or the written route.

## Topic areas (examples in the specification)

Representations of individuals, groups and nationalities; local varieties; social varieties; language and power; new communication technologies; children's language; norms and variation; the media; performance and play; puzzles and games; language in the environment; literary language; rituals and routines; code switching and mixing; language of occupations; historical change.

## Four question types

| Type | Asks | Key word |
|---|---|---|
| Genre | What are the distinctive features of this type of language use? | Features |
| Function or use | What is the language used to do? | Purpose |
| Attitudes | How do people feel about this language? | Views |
| User | Who uses this type of language? | People |

## Kinds of data

Spoken; written; multimodal; word lists; attitudes to language; uses of language; views about language.

## Six sections of the report

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Aim(s) | A statement of the aim or aims |
| 2. Method | The nature of the data, how collected, how it will be analysed |
| 3. Analysis | Findings in categories suited to the topic, explored in detail |
| 4. Conclusion | Conclusions connected with the aims |
| 5. References | Sources cited |
| 6. Appendix: Data | The data themselves |

## Planning table (suggestion only)

| Section | Words |
|---|---|
| Aim(s) | 150 |
| Method | 350 |
| Analysis | 1,600 |
| Conclusion | 400 |
| Total | 2,500 |

References and the appendix are outside the word count.

## Mark bands

| Level | AO1 (out of 20) | AO2 (out of 20) | AO3 (out of 10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 17 to 20 | 17 to 20 | 9 to 10 |
| 4 | 13 to 16 | 13 to 16 | 7 to 8 |
| 3 | 9 to 12 | 9 to 12 | 5 to 6 |
| 2 | 5 to 8 | 5 to 8 | 3 to 4 |
| 1 | 1 to 4 | 1 to 4 | 1 to 2 |

### What each objective rewards

- **AO1.** An overview of issues; different views and approaches; precise aims, scope and focus from the start; frameworks matched with the data; accurate terminology.
- **AO2.** Evaluating language use and representations according to context; recognising complex patterns; meanings explored as negotiated; detailed examples; language as a social and cultural phenomenon.
- **AO3.** Form used with control; a sustained, effective register; clear signposting and links between points; clarity and accuracy.

### Moving up a level

| From | To | What to add |
|---|---|---|
| Level 2 | Level 3 | A workable aim; frameworks that fit the data; terms used correctly |
| Level 3 | Level 4 | Scope and focus made clear; patterns traced and linked to context |
| Level 4 | Level 5 | Precise aims from the start; an overview of issues; analysis set in wider social and cultural contexts |

## Method section checklist

- [ ] What data and how much.
- [ ] Why this data suits the aim.
- [ ] How and when it was collected.
- [ ] Consent and anonymity where people are involved.
- [ ] Transcription conventions if speech is recorded.
- [ ] The frameworks and levels to be used.

## Exam-style traps

- A topic about a subject, not about language.
- An aim too broad to answer in about 2,500 words.
- Data that are not analysed, or an analysis that ignores the data.
- Terminology used without being applied.
- Description of patterns without explaining meaning or context.
- A conclusion that goes beyond the data.
- No references, no appendix.

## Quick self-test

1. How many marks is the NEA worth, and what percentage of the A-level?
2. What is the word count in section 3.4.2?
3. Does the word count include the data?
4. Name the six sections of the report.
5. Give the four kinds of question that can start an investigation.
6. Which question type asks "who uses this type of language?"
7. Name three kinds of data you could collect.
8. What does the Method section need to explain?
9. Which AO is marked out of 10?
10. What mark range is Level 4 for AO2?
11. Give one example of a topic about language in the environment.
12. Who is responsible for ensuring the NEA is conducted in line with OxfordAQA's instructions?

### Answers

1. 50 marks; 30%.
2. Approximately 2,500 words (the overview table says 2,500 to 3,000).
3. No. The data are excluded.
4. Aim(s), Method, Analysis, Conclusion, References, Appendix: Data.
5. Genre-based, function or use-based, attitudes-based, user-based.
6. User-based.
7. Any three from spoken, written, multimodal, word lists, attitudes, uses, views.
8. The nature of the data, how it was collected and how it will be analysed.
9. AO3.
10. 13 to 16 marks.
11. For example, signage or shop names.
12. The head of the school or college.
