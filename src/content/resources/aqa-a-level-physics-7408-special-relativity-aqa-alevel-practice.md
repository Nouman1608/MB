These questions cover section **3.12.3 Special relativity** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Turning points in physics option. Learn the content first in the [special relativity study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-special-relativity-aqa-alevel/) and the [special relativity revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-special-relativity-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

Data: c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; electron rest mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, rest energy 0.512 MeV; proton rest mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, rest energy 939 MeV; 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J. The particle in question 8 is a hypothetical unstable particle.

## Section A

**1.** Define an inertial frame of reference. **[1]**

**2.** State the two postulates of Einstein's theory of special relativity. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by proper time. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the value of 1/√(1 − v²/c²) for v = 0.75c. **[1]**

**5.** A rod of proper length 4.0 m moves along its length at 0.92c relative to an observer. Calculate the length the observer measures. **[2]**

**6.** Calculate the speed at which an object's measured length is 75% of its proper length. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** This question is about the Michelson-Morley experiment.

**(a)** Describe how the interferometer produces an interference pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the experimenters expected to see when the apparatus was rotated through 90°, and why. **[2]**
**(c)** State the result and its significance. **[2]**

**8.** An unstable particle has a proper half-life of 18 ns. A beam of these particles travels 30 m along a laboratory beam pipe at 0.98c.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken for the journey in the laboratory frame. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the half-life of the particles in the laboratory frame. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fraction of the particles that survive the journey, and the fraction that classical physics would predict. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the length of the beam pipe in the particles' frame of reference. **[1]**

**9.** Explain how measurements of muons produced in the upper atmosphere provide evidence for time dilation. **[3]**

**10.** An electron is accelerated to a kinetic energy of 2.0 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate its total energy in MeV. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its speed as a fraction of c. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed predicted by Newtonian mechanics and comment on the result. **[2]**

**11.** A proton is accelerated to a kinetic energy of 1.0 GeV.

**(a)** Calculate the value of 1/√(1 − v²/c²). **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the proton. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its relativistic mass. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how the graph of kinetic energy against speed for an electron predicted by special relativity differs from the graph predicted by Newtonian mechanics. **[3]**

**13.** Describe Bertozzi's experiment and explain its significance. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A frame of reference in which Newton's first law holds (it is not accelerating) [1]

**2.** Physical laws have the same form in all inertial frames [1]; the speed of light in free space is invariant (the same for all inertial observers) [1]

**3.** The time interval between two events measured by an observer for whom the events occur at the same place (at rest relative to the clock) [1]

**4.** 1/√(1 − 0.5625) = **1.51** [1]

**5.** l = 4.0 × √(1 − 0.92²) [1]; = **1.6 m** [1]

**6.** √(1 − v²/c²) = 0.75, so v²/c² = 1 − 0.5625 = 0.4375 [1]; v = **0.66c** [1]

**7. (a)** A semi-silvered plate splits light from a monochromatic source into two beams at right angles, which are reflected back along the arms by mirrors [1]; the beams recombine and, having travelled different optical paths, interfere to form fringes [1]
**(b)** A shift of the fringes [1]; because motion through the ether should make the light's travel times along the two arms differ, and rotation through 90° would reverse that difference [1]
**(c)** No fringe shift was observed [1]; absolute motion through an ether cannot be detected, and the speed of light is invariant [1]

**8. (a)** t = 30/(0.98 × 3.00 × 10⁸) = **1.02 × 10⁻⁷ s** (102 ns) [1]
**(b)** 1/√(1 − 0.98²) = 5.03 [1]; half-life = 5.03 × 18 = **90 ns** [1]
**(c)** Relativity: (1/2)^(102/90) = **0.46** [1]; classical: (1/2)^(102/18) = **0.020** [1]
**(d)** l = 30/5.03 = **6.0 m** [1]

**9.** Muons are produced high in the atmosphere moving close to c, with a short proper half-life (about 1.5 µs) [1]; classically, the time to reach the ground is many half-lives, so very few should survive [1]; far more are detected at ground level, which agrees with their half-life being dilated in the Earth frame (time dilation) [1]

**10. (a)** E = 2.0 + 0.512 = **2.51 MeV** [1]
**(b)** 1/√(1 − v²/c²) = 2.51/0.512 = 4.90 [1]; v = c√(1 − 1/4.90²) = **0.98c** [1]
**(c)** v = √(2E_k/m₀) = √(2 × 2.0 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³/9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 8.4 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ [1]; this is 2.8c, which is impossible; Newtonian mechanics fails at such speeds [1]

**11. (a)** (1000 + 939)/939 = **2.06** [1]
**(b)** v = c√(1 − 1/2.06²) = **0.87c** [1]
**(c)** m = 2.06 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = **3.4 × 10⁻²⁷ kg** [1]

**12.** At low speeds both graphs are the same (E_k ≈ ½m₀v²) [1]; at high speeds the relativistic kinetic energy rises far more steeply than the Newtonian curve [1]; the relativistic curve tends to infinity as v approaches c (asymptote at c), while the Newtonian curve passes through c at a finite energy [1]

**13.** Electrons were accelerated to a range of known kinetic energies, and their time of flight over a known distance was measured to find their speed [1]; the kinetic energy was confirmed independently from the heating of the target [1]; the speed approached but never exceeded c, giving direct evidence that kinetic energy varies with speed as special relativity predicts [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Multiplying the proper length by the factor instead of dividing.
- Using the proper half-life with the laboratory travel time.
- Writing E_k = mc² instead of mc² − m₀c².
- Describing Michelson-Morley without the rotation through 90°.
- Sketching a relativistic graph that crosses v = c.
