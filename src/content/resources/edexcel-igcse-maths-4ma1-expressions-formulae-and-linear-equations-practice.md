These questions cover sections **2.3 Expressions and formulae** and **2.4 Linear equations** of Topic 2, Equations, formulae and identities, in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on it. Questions and parts labelled **(Higher)** use the Higher tier only statement on changing the subject (subject appearing twice, or a power or root of the subject). Everything else is for both tiers; question 7 is in the style of the Higher example for 2.4, which uses the same Foundation skill with more fractions. A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, but show your working: most marks are for method.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-expressions-formulae-and-linear-equations/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-expressions-formulae-and-linear-equations-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** Write each of these in its simplest algebraic form.

**(a)** a × 7 × b **[1]**
**(b)** n × n × n × 2 **[1]**
**(c)** Add 2 to x, then divide the result by 3. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the value of each expression. Show your substitution.

**(a)** 5a − 2b when a = −3 and b = −4.5 **[2]**
**(b)** (p² − q)/(2p) when p = 1/2 and q = −3/4 **[2]**

**3.** A phone contract costs £12 per month, plus £0.05 for each text sent.

**(a)** Write a formula for the total cost, £C, of m months on the contract with t texts sent in total. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate C when m = 6 and t = 340. **[2]**
**(c)** Over 12 months the total cost was £163. Calculate the number of texts sent. **[2]**

**4.** A rectangular lawn is x metres long and y metres wide. A path 1 metre wide runs all the way round the outside of the lawn.

**(a)** Derive a formula, in its simplest form, for the area A m² of the path. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the area of the path when x = 8.5 and y = 6. **[2]**

**5.** Rearrange each formula to make the letter in brackets the subject.

**(a)** s = (u + v)t/2 [u] **[2]**
**(b)** y = 5 − 3x/4 [x] **[2]**
**(c)** V = πr²h [r], where r > 0 **[2]**

**6.** Solve these equations. Show clear algebraic working.

**(a)** 7x − 4 = 3x + 18 **[2]**
**(b)** 3(2y + 5) = 4 − 2(y − 1) **[3]**
**(c)** (5w − 2)/3 = 2w + 1 **[2]**

**7.** Solve (x + 3)/4 − (2x − 5)/6 = 1/3. Show clear algebraic working. **[4]**

**8.** The angles of a quadrilateral are (2a + 15)°, (3a − 10)°, (a + 40)° and (4a − 5)°.

**(a)** Form an equation in a and solve it. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the size of the largest angle. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher)

**(a)** Make x the subject of y = (3x + 2)/(x − 4). **[4]**
**(b)** Use your answer to find x when y = 5. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher)

**(a)** Make a the subject of k = √((a + 2)/(a − 1)). **[4]**
**(b)** Find a when k = 2. **[1]**

**11.** At a cinema an adult ticket costs £a and a child ticket costs £4 less than an adult ticket. Two adult tickets and three child tickets cost £53 altogether.

**(a)** Show that 5a − 12 = 53. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the cost of an adult ticket and of a child ticket. **[2]**
**(c)** Write a formula for the total cost, £C, of x adult tickets and y child tickets, in terms of a, x and y. **[2]**
**(d)** (Higher) Make a the subject of your formula. **[3]**
**(e)** (Higher) At another cinema with the same pricing rule, 4 adults and 5 children pay £88. Use your answer to (d) to find the adult price there. **[1]**

**12.** Temperatures in degrees Fahrenheit, F, and degrees Celsius, C, are linked by F = 9C/5 + 32.

**(a)** Calculate F when C = −12.5. **[2]**
**(b)** Make C the subject of the formula. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the temperature at which the Celsius and Fahrenheit readings are equal. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **7ab** [1]
**(b)** **2n³** [1]
**(c)** **(x + 2)/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) writing x + 2/3 loses the mark, because without a bracket or a full fraction line only the 2 is divided by 3.

**2. (a)** 5(−3) − 2(−4.5) [1] = −15 + 9 = **−6** [1]
**(b)** Numerator (1/2)² − (−3/4) = 1/4 + 3/4 = 1 and denominator 2(1/2) = 1 [1]; value **1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The method mark is for a correct substitution shown; a wrong final answer with no substitution written down scores zero.

**3. (a)** 12m for the monthly charge and 0.05t for the texts [1]; **C = 12m + 0.05t** [1]
**(b)** C = 12(6) + 0.05(340) = 72 + 17 [1] = **89** [1]
**(c)** 163 = 12(12) + 0.05t, so 0.05t = 19 [1]; t = 19 ÷ 0.05 = **380 texts** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A formula must include the "C =" part; writing only 12m + 0.05t is an expression and usually loses the accuracy mark.

**4. (a)** Lawn plus path is (x + 2) by (y + 2) [1]; A = (x + 2)(y + 2) − xy [1] = **2x + 2y + 4** [1]
**(b)** A = 2(8.5) + 2(6) + 4 [1] = **33 m²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using x + 1 and y + 1 for the outer rectangle is the usual error; the path adds 1 m on each side, so 2 m to each dimension.

**5. (a)** 2s = (u + v)t, so u + v = 2s/t [1]; **u = 2s/t − v** [1]
**(b)** 3x/4 = 5 − y [1]; **x = 4(5 − y)/3** (or (20 − 4y)/3) [1]
**(c)** r² = V/(πh) [1]; **r = √(V/(πh))** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Any correct equivalent form scores full marks, but the final answer must have the new subject alone on one side with no copy of it on the other.

**6. (a)** 7x − 3x = 18 + 4, so 4x = 22 [1]; **x = 5.5** [1]
**(b)** 6y + 15 = 4 − 2y + 2 [1]; 8y = −9 [1]; **y = −9/8 = −1.125** [1]
**(c)** 5w − 2 = 3(2w + 1) = 6w + 3 [1]; **w = −5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Show clear algebraic working" means an answer found by trial and improvement, even if correct, may score no marks.

**7.** Multiply every term by 12: 3(x + 3) − 2(2x − 5) = 4 [1]; 3x + 9 − 4x + 10 = 4 [1]; −x = −15 [1]; **x = 15** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The second method mark depends on −2 × −5 = +10; writing −10 is the usual slip, and follow-through then applies only to the remaining steps.

**8. (a)** (2a + 15) + (3a − 10) + (a + 40) + (4a − 5) = 360 [1]; 10a + 40 = 360 [1]; **a = 32** [1]
**(b)** Angles 79°, 86°, 72°, 123° [1]; largest **123°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Giving a = 32 as the answer to (b) loses the mark; check the four angles add to 360° before choosing the largest.

**9. (a)** y(x − 4) = 3x + 2 [1]; xy − 4y = 3x + 2 [1]; xy − 3x = 4y + 2, so x(y − 3) = 4y + 2 [1]; **x = (4y + 2)/(y − 3)** [1]
**(b)** x = (20 + 2)/(5 − 3) = **11** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The factorising step x(y − 3) is the key mark; an answer that still has x on the right-hand side scores only the early method marks.

**10. (a)** k² = (a + 2)/(a − 1) [1]; k²a − k² = a + 2 [1]; k²a − a = k² + 2, so a(k² − 1) = k² + 2 [1]; **a = (k² + 2)/(k² − 1)** [1]
**(b)** a = (4 + 2)/(4 − 1) = **2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Square both sides before doing anything else; taking the square root of individual terms such as √a earns no credit.

**11. (a)** Child ticket costs a − 4, so 2a + 3(a − 4) = 53 [1]; 2a + 3a − 12 = 53, giving 5a − 12 = 53 [1]
**(b)** 5a = 65, a = 13 [1]; **adult £13, child £9** [1]
**(c)** ax for the adults and (a − 4)y for the children [1]; **C = ax + (a − 4)y** [1]
**(d)** C = ax + ay − 4y [1]; C + 4y = a(x + y) [1]; **a = (C + 4y)/(x + y)** [1]
**(e)** a = (88 + 20)/(4 + 5) = 108/9 = **£12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" the printed result earns nothing by itself; the line with the brackets, 2a + 3(a − 4), must appear.

**12. (a)** F = 9(−12.5)/5 + 32 = −22.5 + 32 [1] = **9.5** [1]
**(b)** F − 32 = 9C/5 [1]; **C = 5(F − 32)/9** [1]
**(c)** Set F = C: C = 9C/5 + 32 [1]; 5C = 9C + 160, so −4C = 160 [1]; **C = −40, so −40 °C = −40 °F** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the first mark is for replacing F by C (or C by F) to form one linear equation in one unknown, so write that equation down before solving.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Substituting a negative value without brackets, so −4.5 is subtracted instead of −2 × −4.5 being added.
- Writing an expression such as 12m + 0.05t when a formula "C = …" is asked for.
- Adding the path width once instead of twice to each side of a rectangle.
- Dividing only the last term when rearranging: u = 2s/t − v is right, u = 2s − v/t is not.
- Expanding −2(y − 1) as −2y − 2 instead of −2y + 2.
- Multiplying only one term by the LCM when clearing fractions.
- Higher: forgetting to factorise, so the subject is left on both sides.
- Higher: taking a square root of separate terms instead of squaring both sides first.
- Giving the value of the letter instead of the angles, prices or temperature asked for.
- Rounding an exact answer such as −9/8 when no rounding is requested.
