These questions cover section **3.3.11.2 Base properties** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Base properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amine-base-properties/) and the [Base properties revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amine-base-properties-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a Brønsted–Lowry base. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why amines can act as bases. **[2]**

**3.** Write an equation for the reaction of methylamine with:

**(a)** water **[1]**

**(b)** hydrochloric acid **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the reaction of methylamine with water is shown with a reversible arrow. **[2]**

**5.** Put ammonia, ethylamine and phenylamine in order of increasing base strength. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why ethylamine is a stronger base than ammonia. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why phenylamine is a weaker base than ammonia. **[3]**

**8.** Phenylamine is only slightly soluble in water but dissolves in dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction with hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the product is soluble in water. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how free phenylamine can be recovered from the solution. **[2]**

**9.** 20.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ ethylamine is neutralised by 0.200 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of ethylamine. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of hydrochloric acid required. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of ethylammonium chloride (Mᵣ = 81.5) formed. **[1]**

**10.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ methylamine is neutralised by 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid. Write the equation and calculate the volume of sulfuric acid needed. **[4]**

**11.** Compare the base strengths of methylamine and phenylamine, explaining the difference in terms of the electron density on nitrogen. **[4]**

**12.** A student says that "phenylamine is a weaker base than ammonia because it is bigger". Explain why this statement is not a valid scientific explanation and give the correct reason. **[2]**

**13.** Aqueous ethylamine and aqueous ammonia of the same concentration are tested with universal indicator. State and explain which solution has the higher pH. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A proton (H⁺) acceptor [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The nitrogen atom has a lone pair of electrons [1]; this forms a dative covalent bond to a proton [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** CH₃NH₂ + HCl → CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Methylamine is a weak base and is only partly protonated by water [1]; so the equilibrium lies on the left, with both reactants and products present [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Phenylamine < ammonia < ethylamine [1]. **[1]**

**6.** The ethyl group is electron-donating and pushes electron density towards the nitrogen [1]; so the lone pair is more available to accept a proton than in ammonia [1]. **[2]**

**7.** In phenylamine the nitrogen is bonded to the benzene ring [1]; its lone pair overlaps with the delocalised electrons of the ring and is partly delocalised into it [1]; so the lone pair is less available to accept a proton, and phenylamine is a weaker base [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** C₆H₅NH₂ + HCl → C₆H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]

**(b)** The product is an ionic salt, and its ions interact with water molecules [1]

**(c)** Add excess aqueous sodium hydroxide [1]; the phenylammonium ion loses its proton and free phenylamine is released and separates [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** n = 0.0200 × 0.250 = **0.00500 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(HCl) = 0.00500 mol (1 : 1) [1]; V = 0.00500 ÷ 0.200 = 0.0250 dm³ = **25.0 cm³** [1]

**(c)** mass = 0.00500 × 81.5 = **0.408 g** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** 2CH₃NH₂ + H₂SO₄ → (CH₃NH₃)₂SO₄ [1]; n(CH₃NH₂) = 0.0250 × 0.200 = 0.00500 mol [1]; n(H₂SO₄) = 0.00500 ÷ 2 = 0.00250 mol [1]; V = 0.00250 ÷ 0.100 = 0.0250 dm³ = **25.0 cm³** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Sulfuric acid is diprotic, so it reacts with two moles of a monoamine for each mole of acid.

**11.** Methylamine is a stronger base than phenylamine [1]; in methylamine the methyl group donates electron density to nitrogen, so the lone pair is more available [1]; in phenylamine the lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring [1]; this lowers the electron density on nitrogen, so the lone pair is less available to accept a proton [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Size is not the factor that controls base strength [1]; the correct reason is that the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalised into the benzene ring, so it is less available to accept a proton [1]. **[2]**

**13.** The ethylamine solution has the higher pH [1]; the ethyl group donates electron density to nitrogen, so ethylamine is a stronger base, accepts more protons from water and produces more hydroxide ions [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not mentioning the lone pair on nitrogen.
- Saying "the ring withdraws the lone pair" without using the word delocalised.
- Saying that alkyl groups pull electron density away from the nitrogen.
- A one-way arrow for amine with water.
- Writing the salt with no charges.
- Forgetting that sulfuric acid reacts with two moles of monoamine.
