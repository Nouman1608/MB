All amounts are in dollars. Harrowby Print Works Ltd is an invented business with 80,000 shares in issue. Its profit before loan interest is 82,000, and it has a bank loan of 50,000 at 6% a year.

## Questions

**1.** Name the three internal stakeholders listed in the specification. **[3]**

**2.** Name the five external stakeholders listed in the specification. **[5]**

**3.** State two interests that employees of Harrowby Print Works Ltd have in the business. **[2]**

**4.** State which stakeholder receives a dividend and which stakeholder receives loan interest. **[2]**

**5.** Harrowby Print Works Ltd pays interest to its bank.

**(a)** Calculate the annual loan interest. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate profit after loan interest. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the bank is interested in these figures. **[2]**

**6.** Harrowby Print Works Ltd proposes a dividend of 24,000 from the profit after loan interest.

**(a)** Calculate the dividend per share. **[1]**
**(b)** A shareholder holds 12,000 shares. Calculate the dividend this shareholder receives. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the profit retained. **[1]**
**(d)** State whether this shareholder is an internal or external stakeholder. **[1]**

**7.** Harrowby Print Works Ltd owes a paper supplier 18,500 and buys 222,000 of paper in the year. Explain why the supplier is interested in the company's financial position. **[3]**

**8.** Explain one interest of the government and one interest of the local community in Harrowby Print Works Ltd. **[4]**

**9.** The directors propose to raise the dividend from 24,000 to 60,000. Explain how this affects shareholders, lenders and employees. **[6]**

**10.** Classify each of these as an internal or an external stakeholder: customers, management, lenders, owners. **[4]**

**11.** Harrowby Print Works Ltd plans to move to a larger site. It would borrow a further 120,000 at 6% a year, create 15 new jobs and bring heavy delivery lorries to a residential road.

**(a)** Calculate the extra annual loan interest and the total loan interest after the new loan. **[2]**
**(b)** Discuss the effect of the plan on shareholders, employees, lenders and the local community, and give a reasoned view on whether the plan is good for the stakeholders as a whole. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Employees [1]. Management [1]. Owners/shareholders [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Recall questions with a mark per item reward the group names the specification uses, so do not substitute vague words such as "workers" or "staff" if the standard wording is easy to give.

**2.** Customers [1]. Suppliers [1]. Government [1]. Lenders [1]. Local community [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing "bank" instead of "lenders" may still be understood, but using the group name removes any doubt about what you mean.

**3.** Any two from: job security; pay, bonuses or pension; working conditions or training. One mark for each different interest [2]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two different interests are needed for two marks, so "pay" and "wages" would be one point written twice.

**4.** Owners/shareholders receive a dividend [1]. Lenders receive loan interest [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to reverse these, so keep one memory link: shares pay dividends, loans pay interest.

**5. (a)** Interest = 50,000 x 6% = **3,000** [1].
**(b)** Profit after interest = 82,000 - 3,000 = **79,000** [1].
**(c)** The bank wants to be repaid and receive interest [1]. Profit of 82,000 before interest is much larger than the 3,000 interest, which suggests the business can pay it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An explain part usually needs the interest plus a link to the figures, so quote the numbers instead of repeating that the bank is "interested in profit".

**6. (a)** 24,000 / 80,000 = **0.30 per share** [1].
**(b)** 12,000 x 0.30 = **3,600** [1].
**(c)** Retained profit = 79,000 - 24,000 = **55,000** [1].
**(d)** **Internal**, because shareholders are owners [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division or multiplication so that a slip in one part does not hide a correct method in the next.

**7.** The supplier sells on credit and is owed 18,500 [1]. It wants to be paid on time, so it needs the business to have enough cash and profit [1]. It also wants the business to carry on trading so that it keeps receiving orders [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A three-mark explain question is looking for three developed points, and a quoted figure from the scenario helps to show the link.

**8.** Government: it collects taxes related to profit, sales or wages, so it wants the business to earn and report accurately [2]. Local community: it is affected by jobs and local spending, or by noise and traffic, so it wants the business to remain a good neighbour [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each interest needs a reason after it. Naming "taxes" alone is a statement, and adding why the government cares turns it into an explanation.

**9.** Shareholders: they receive more, as the dividend per share rises from 0.30 to 0.75, so a holder of 12,000 shares gets 9,000 instead of 3,600 [2]. Lenders: retained profit falls from 55,000 to 19,000, so less is kept for repayments and they face more risk [2]. Employees: less is retained for growth or pay rises, so their job security or future pay may be affected [2]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Questions that name three groups need something specific about each one. Writing only about shareholders leaves most of the marks unavailable.

**10.** Customers are external [1]. Management is internal [1]. Lenders are external [1]. Owners are internal [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Lenders and customers are the usual mistakes because they deal with the business often, but they do not work in it, run it or own it.

**11. (a)** Extra interest = 120,000 x 6% = **7,200** [1]. Total interest = 3,000 + 7,200 = **10,200** [1].
**(b)** Shareholders: more profit may come from growth, but profit is reduced by 7,200 of extra interest, so their return depends on the move working [1]. Employees: 15 new jobs and a more secure future if the business grows [1]. Lenders: they are owed more and interest is higher at 10,200, but profit before interest of 82,000 is about 8.0 times that interest, so repayment looks affordable [1]. Local community: gains jobs and local spending [1] but suffers from heavy lorries on a residential road [1]. Judgement: the plan helps owners, employees and lenders if the growth is achieved, while the community's concerns could be reduced by agreed delivery times, so overall it is probably beneficial [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A discuss question rewards both sides and a reasoned conclusion. A list of effects with no judgement usually does not earn the final mark.

## How to use this set

Attempt Questions 1 to 4 from memory first, then check them against the lists in the study guide. Questions 5 to 8 apply the groups to figures, so write out each calculation before you explain. Questions 9 and 11 are the longest: plan one sentence per group before you write, and finish with a short reasoned conclusion. Mark yourself honestly. If you lose marks for a missing reason, add the word because and finish the sentence, then try the same question again a day later.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a group but not saying what it wants
- Giving "profit" as the interest of every stakeholder
- Reversing dividend and interest
- Placing lenders, suppliers or customers among the internal stakeholders
- Forgetting to use figures from the scenario in an explain answer
- Writing about one group when the question asks for several
- Listing effects on a decision without saying who gains and who loses
- Leaving out a conclusion in a discuss question
