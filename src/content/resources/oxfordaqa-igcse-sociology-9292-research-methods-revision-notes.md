## One topic, two papers

Research methods is **Section C (16 marks) of both papers**. **Paper 1**: short questions plus a structured question on **data about an unseen issue of global sociological significance**. **Paper 2**: short and extended response questions, usually applying methods to Paper 2 topics. Same content; Paper 1 tests reading data, Paper 2 tests extended evaluation.

## Approaches (3.1.3.1)

| Positivist | Interpretivist |
|---|---|
| Sociology as science | Understanding meanings |
| Quantitative data | Qualitative data |
| Questionnaires, structured interviews, official statistics | Unstructured interviews, participant observation, personal documents |
| Strong on reliability | Strong on validity |

**Primary** = collected by the researcher. **Secondary** = already exists.

## Methods (3.1.3.2)

| Method | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Postal or online survey | Large, cheap, wide reach | Low response rate |
| Telephone survey | Quick replies | Intrusive; people hang up |
| Structured interview | Reliable, comparable | Little depth; interviewer bias |
| Unstructured interview | Valid, detailed | Slow; hard to repeat |
| Group interview | Shared views emerge | Some voices dominate |
| Overt participant observation | Real behaviour in context | Hawthorne effect |
| Covert participant observation | Avoids Hawthorne effect | Deception; risky; hard to record |
| Non-participant observation | Researcher stays detached | Misses meanings |
| Laboratory experiment | Control of variables | Artificial |
| Field experiment | Natural setting | Less control; consent issues |
| Longitudinal study | Shows change over time | Costly; people drop out |
| Personal documents | Insight into feelings | Unrepresentative; may be selective |
| Official statistics | Cheap; whole population | Collected for other purposes |

**Triangulation**: combine methods to check findings. **Question types**: closed, open, scaled.

## Issues (3.1.3.3)

- **Operationalisation**: making a concept measurable.
- **Population** (whole group) and **sampling frame** (list used to sample).
- **Samples**: random, systematic, stratified random, quota, multistage, snowball.
- **Validity** (true picture) v **reliability** (repeatable).
- **Bias**: researcher or interviewer bias; Hawthorne effect; social desirability.
- **Practical**: time, funding, access.
- **Ethics**: informed consent, confidentiality, anonymity, wellbeing; with children, parental consent as well.
- **Correlation ≠ causation**.
- **Representativeness** → **generalisability**.

## Interpreting data (3.1.3.4)

Tables, charts and graphs: title, units, highest and lowest, trend, quantified difference, what is missing. Qualitative extracts: meanings, a short quoted phrase, observer effect.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sampling frame | The list from which a sample is chosen |
| Snowball sample | Participants recruit further participants |
| Hawthorne effect | Behaviour changes because people know they are studied |
| Social desirability | Giving answers that show oneself in a good light |
| Generalisability | Applying findings beyond the sample |
| Operationalisation | Defining a concept so it can be measured |

## Self-test

1. Which approach prefers qualitative data, and why?
2. What is a sampling frame?
3. How does a stratified random sample differ from a quota sample?
4. Why might covert observation be used despite ethical problems?
5. What is the difference between correlation and causation?
6. How do the two papers test research methods differently?

**Answers:** 1. Interpretivism, because it aims to understand the meanings people give to their actions. 2. The list of the population from which a sample is drawn. 3. Both divide the population into categories, but stratified random sampling selects randomly within each, while in quota sampling the researcher chooses who to ask until each quota is filled. 4. To study groups who would refuse or change their behaviour if they knew, avoiding the Hawthorne effect. 5. Correlation means two things vary together; causation means one produces the other. 6. Paper 1 includes a structured question on unseen data about a global issue; Paper 2 uses short and extended responses, often applied to Paper 2 topics.
