## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a reward of business activity named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Lack of security

B: Independence

C: Business failure

D: Financial loss

**2.** Which **one** of the following best describes lack of security for an entrepreneur? **[1]**

A: The business premises may be broken into

B: The owner has no guaranteed regular income

C: Competitors may copy the product

D: The bank may refuse a loan

**3.** Define the term business failure. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** risks of starting a business named in the specification. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why an entrepreneur may accept a lower income than they could earn as an employee. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why risk and reward are usually linked. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**7.** *Moorfield Food Van* sells hot meals at events. Each meal sells for £8. Total costs are £38,000 a year. The owner, Kemi, expects to sell 6,500 meals next year, but if bad weather cancels events she may sell only 4,000.

**(a)** Calculate the profit if Kemi sells 6,500 meals. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit or loss if Kemi sells 4,000 meals. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in meals sold from 6,500 to 4,000. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(d)** Outline **one** risk to Kemi shown by your answer to part (b). **[2]**

**8.** *Larchwood Lettering* designs shop signs. Its owner, Femi, has run it for four years and has steady profits. A large retail chain offers him a contract to make signs for 40 new shops. He would need to rent a bigger workshop and hire two staff before any payment from the chain arrives.

Analyse the risks and rewards to Femi of accepting the contract. **[6]**

**9.** *Oriel Climbing Centre* is a planned indoor climbing wall. Its founder, Nadia, currently earns £29,000 a year as a sports coach. She would invest £40,000 of savings and borrow the rest. She forecasts:

| Outcome | Visits a year | Price per visit (£) | Total costs (£) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected | 5,200 | 12 | 46,000 |
| Poor | 3,500 | 12 | 46,000 |

**(a)** Calculate Nadia's expected profit. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether Nadia should leave her job to open Oriel Climbing Centre. **[12]**

**10.** Explain **one** way market research could reduce the risk of starting a business. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B: Independence [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The other three options are the risks from the specification.

**2.** B: The owner has no guaranteed regular income [1]. **[1]**

**3.** When a business closes because it can no longer continue trading, for example because it cannot cover its costs or pay its debts [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: business failure [1]; financial loss [1]; lack of security. **[2]**

**5.** Running a business gives independence, so the entrepreneur makes their own decisions [1]. Some people value being their own boss more than a higher wage [1], so they are willing to give up income in return for control over what they do and when they work [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A second valid chain uses the chance of higher profit later: the business may earn little at first but grow, so the owner accepts a lower income now for a bigger reward in future.

**6.** Actions that could bring a larger profit usually need more money or time to be put in [1]. If those actions go wrong, more is lost, so the risk is greater [1]. Entrepreneurs therefore expect a bigger possible reward before they will take a bigger risk [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Revenue = 6,500 × £8 = £52,000 [1]. Profit = £52,000 − £38,000 = **£14,000** [1].

**(b)** Revenue = 4,000 × £8 = £32,000 [1]. £32,000 − £38,000 = −£6,000, a **loss of £6,000** [1].

**(c)** Fall = 6,500 − 4,000 = 2,500 meals. 2,500 ÷ 6,500 × 100 [1] = **38.5%** [1].

**(d)** Kemi faces financial loss: if events are cancelled she would make a £6,000 loss [1], so she would receive no income from the business and might have to use savings to pay her costs, showing her lack of security [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working in each Calculate part. In part (d), quote your figure from part (b).

**8.** Rewards: the contract for 40 shops could greatly increase Larchwood's sales and profit [1], and working for a large chain could build its reputation, bringing business success and further contracts [1]. Risks: Femi must pay rent on a bigger workshop and wages for two staff before the chain pays [1], so the business could run short of money and face financial loss if payment is late [1]. If the chain cancels after the costs are committed, Femi could be left with higher fixed costs and not enough work to cover them [1], which could threaten the survival of a business that has been steadily profitable for four years [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** Revenue = 5,200 × £12 = £62,400 [1]. Profit = £62,400 − £46,000 = **£16,400** [1].

**(b)** Rewards: in the expected outcome Nadia makes a profit of £16,400 [1], and she gains independence by running her own centre rather than working for someone else [1]. If the centre builds a good name, visits could rise and profit could exceed her salary in later years, which would be business success [1].

Risks: £16,400 is £12,600 less than her £29,000 salary [1], so even in the expected year she earns less, showing a lack of security [1]. In the poor outcome revenue is 3,500 × £12 = £42,000 [1], a loss of £4,000 [1]. She has invested £40,000 of savings and also borrowed, so she must make loan repayments even in a poor year [1], and if losses continue the centre could fail and she could lose much of her savings [1].

Judgement: Nadia should only leave her job if she has strong evidence that the expected 5,200 visits are realistic, such as market research [1], because the gap between the expected and poor outcomes turns a profit into a loss with her savings and a loan at stake [1]. A lower-risk alternative is to open part-time or keep some coaching work while the centre builds up [1]. **[14]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate needs a supported conclusion. Using both forecasts, the salary and the savings figure gives the judgement weight; the condition at the end shows what would change it.

**10.** Market research shows whether customers actually want the product and at what price [1]. The entrepreneur can then change the idea, or decide not to go ahead, before spending savings or taking a loan [1], which lowers the chance of financial loss or business failure, although it cannot remove the risk completely [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling independence a risk or lack of security a reward.
- Calculating revenue and calling it profit.
- Using the new figure, not the original, as the base for a percentage fall.
- Listing risks with no consequence for the named owner.
- Ignoring the owner's salary or savings when they are given.
- Concluding without a decision or a condition.
