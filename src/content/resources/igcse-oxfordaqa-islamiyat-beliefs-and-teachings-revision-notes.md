**These notes condense topic 3.1.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat 9237, Islam: Beliefs and
Teachings, examined in Paper 1 Section A.** Learn the Arabic terms with their meanings, the key facts
for each belief, and two or three points of importance for each, since 4- and 6-mark questions ask you
to explain why beliefs matter to Muslims.

## Syllabus coverage

**9237 topic 3.1.1 (Version 1.1):** 3.1.1.1 Tawhid and Shirk; 3.1.1.2 key beliefs; 3.1.1.3 names of
Allah; 3.1.1.4 angels and the Hadith of Jibril; 3.1.1.5 Al-Qadr; 3.1.1.6 life after death and death
rituals; 3.1.1.7 prophets and holy books, the Seal of Prophethood; 3.1.1.8 the four Rightly Guided
Caliphs.

## Tawhid and Shirk

- **Rububiyyah:** Allah alone is Lord and Creator.
- **Uluhiyyah:** Allah alone is worshipped.
- **Asma wa Sifat:** His names and attributes are unique.
- **Major shirk:** worshipping others; takes a person outside Islam.
- **Minor shirk:** riya' (showing off); reduces reward.
- *Reference:* Sura 112; Qur'an 4.48.

## Names of Allah (specified five)

| Name | Meaning | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Qudrah | All-Powerful | Reliance on Allah |
| Al-Khaliq | Creator | Care for creation; gratitude |
| Ar-Rahman | Most Gracious | Mercy to all creation |
| Ar-Rahim | Most Merciful | Hope of forgiveness |
| Al-Alim | All-Knowing | Sincerity; Allah knows hidden intentions |

## Angels

| Angel | Role |
|---|---|
| Jibril (AS) | Brings revelation |
| Mika'il (AS) | Rain and provision |
| Israfil (AS) | Blows the trumpet |
| Angel of Death | Takes souls |
| Kiraman Katibin | Record deeds (82.10–12) |

**Hadith of Jibril:** questions on Islam (five pillars), iman (six beliefs), ihsan, and the Hour;
teaches the three levels of religion and good manners in seeking knowledge.

## Al-Qadr

- Allah's knowledge, writing, will and creation of all events.
- Human free will and accountability.
- **Influence:** patience, gratitude, tawakkul, continued effort.

## Life after death

Death → Barzakh (questioning in the grave) → trumpet → resurrection → judgement with book of deeds →
Jannah or Jahannam.

**Death rituals:** ghusl; kafan; salat al-janazah; prompt burial facing the qibla; simple graves;
differing local and sectarian customs in mourning. **Importance:** honour the dead, remind the living
of death and judgement, show equality.

## Prophets and books

| Prophet | Book or key teaching |
|---|---|
| Adam (AS) | First human; repentance and forgiveness |
| Ibrahim (AS) | Suhuf; rejection of idols; Ka'ba |
| Musa (AS) | Tawrat; freedom from Pharaoh |
| Dawud (AS) | Zabur |
| Isa (AS) | Injil; miracles by Allah's permission |
| Muhammad (pbuh) | Qur'an; Seal of the Prophets (33.40) |

## Rightly Guided Caliphs

- **Abu Bakr (RA), 632–634:** unity after the Prophet's death; Qur'an gathered.
- **Umar (RA), 634–644:** expansion; administration; justice.
- **Uthman (RA), 644–656:** standard copies of the Qur'an.
- **Ali (RA), 656–661:** knowledge and justice; civil conflict. Shi'a Muslims regard him as the first Imam.

## Why the key beliefs matter: quick points

- **Tawhid:** gives life one purpose, worship of Allah, and rules out superstition.
- **Angels:** awareness that deeds are recorded encourages honesty.
- **Books:** the Qur'an is a complete guide for worship, law and conduct.
- **Prophets:** living examples to copy, above all the Sunnah of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh).
- **Last Day:** accountability motivates good deeds and repentance.
- **Al-Qadr:** brings peace of mind, patience and trust in Allah.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain two ways belief in angels affects how a Muslim lives. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Muslims believe the Kiraman Katibin record every deed, so they try to act well even
when no one else can see, for example by being honest in exams [2]. Belief that Jibril (AS) brought the
Qur'an from Allah gives Muslims confidence that it is true guidance, so they follow its teachings in
daily life [2].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Listing names of Allah without meaning or importance.** Each needs both.
- **Treating Al-Qadr as fate with no free will.** Explain both sides.
- **Forgetting differences in death rituals.** The specification asks for different rituals.
- **Using a prescribed passage only as decoration.** In the 12-mark answer, explain what the passage teaches and how it supports your argument.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many key beliefs are listed?** Six: Tawhid, angels, books, prophethood, the Last Day and
Al-Qadr, with the belief that all good and bad is from Allah.

**What does the Seal of the Prophets mean?** Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) is the final prophet, so his
message completes and confirms all earlier revelations.
