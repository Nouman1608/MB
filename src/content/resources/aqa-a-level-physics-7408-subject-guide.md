AQA A-Level Physics (7408) is a course in explaining the physical world
quantitatively, from subatomic particles and quantum phenomena to
fields, thermal physics and the nucleus. Alongside eight core sections,
every student studies one optional topic chosen from five. The
qualification is assessed by three written papers, each lasting 2
hours: Papers 1 and 2 are worth 85 marks each (34% each) and Paper 3 is
worth 80 marks (32%), for a total of 250 marks.

## How the course is organised

The content falls into three groups: foundations, the core sections
and the options.

**Foundations.** Section 1, measurements and their errors, covers SI
units and prefixes, the limitations of physical measurements and the
estimation of physical quantities. It is short, but its ideas about
uncertainty and orders of magnitude are used in every other section.

**Core sections (2 to 8).**

- *Particles and radiation:* particles, and electromagnetic radiation
  and quantum phenomena.
- *Waves:* progressive and stationary waves; refraction, diffraction and
  interference.
- *Mechanics and materials:* force, energy and momentum; materials.
- *Electricity:* current electricity.
- *Further mechanics and thermal physics:* periodic motion; thermal
  physics.
- *Fields and their consequences:* fields in general, gravitational
  fields, electric fields, capacitance and magnetic fields.
- *Nuclear physics:* radioactivity.

**Options (9 to 13).** You study one of the following, normally the
one your centre teaches:

- *Astrophysics:* telescopes, classification of stars and cosmology.
- *Medical physics:* physics of the eye and ear, biological
  measurement, non-ionising imaging, X-ray imaging, and radionuclide
  imaging and therapy.
- *Engineering physics:* rotational dynamics, thermodynamics and
  engines.
- *Turning points in physics:* the discovery of the electron,
  wave-particle duality and special relativity.
- *Electronics:* discrete semiconductor devices, analogue and digital
  signals, analogue signal processing, the operational amplifier,
  digital signal processing and data communication systems.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered, so all three papers are
taken at the end of the course.

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7408/1 | Sections 1 to 5 plus periodic motion (6.1) | 2 hours | 85 | 34% |
| 7408/2 | Thermal physics (6.2) and sections 7 and 8, assuming knowledge of sections 1 to 6.1 | 2 hours | 85 | 34% |
| 7408/3 | Section A: practical skills and data analysis; Section B: one optional topic | 2 hours | 80 | 32% |

Papers 1 and 2 each contain 60 marks of short and long answer
questions and 25 marks of multiple-choice questions. Paper 3 is
different in character: Section A (45 marks) is compulsory and tests
practical skills and data analysis, while Section B (35 marks) assesses
the optional topic you have studied. The specification was first
taught in September 2015 and first assessed in June 2017.

## How the parts fit together

The paper split follows a clear line through section 6. Periodic
motion is examined with the earlier mechanics on Paper 1, while
thermal physics is examined with fields and nuclear physics on Paper 2.
Paper 2 also assumes everything from sections 1 to 6.1, so the earlier
content is never really finished: energy, momentum and circuits all
reappear when you meet gravitational and electric fields, capacitors
and radioactive decay.

There are strong parallels within the core that are worth noticing as
you go. Gravitational and electric fields share the same mathematical
structure; the exponential decay of charge on a capacitor mirrors
radioactive decay; and wave ideas from section 3 return in quantum
phenomena and, for some options, in imaging or telescopes. Seeing these
links makes the course feel smaller and more coherent.

The practical strand ties everything together. Section 1's work on
uncertainty and measurement leads directly into Section A of Paper 3,
which tests practical skills and data analysis across the whole course.
Your chosen option then builds on the core: Astrophysics, Medical
physics and Turning points draw heavily on waves, particles and
fields, while Engineering physics and Electronics extend mechanics,
thermal physics and electricity.

## Planning your study

1. **Begin with measurement and particles.** Section 1 sets up good
   habits with units and uncertainties; particles and radiation and
   waves often follow early because they need relatively little
   mathematics.
2. **Build mechanics and electricity.** Mechanics and materials and
   current electricity give the quantitative toolkit used throughout
   the rest of the course.
3. **Move into the second-year core.** Periodic motion, thermal physics,
   fields and nuclear physics complete the content for Papers 1 and 2.
4. **Fit the option in where your centre places it.** Many centres teach
   it towards the end, once the core it depends on is secure.
5. **Practise data analysis throughout.** Treat each practical activity
   as preparation for Paper 3 Section A: record uncertainties, plot
   graphs and evaluate methods every time.

Short questions on each topic should begin as soon as it is taught.
Full papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page are most
useful in the final months, once you have covered the core and your
option.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA A-Level Physics?**
There are three written papers of 2 hours each. Papers 1 and 2 are worth 85 marks and 34% each; Paper 3 is worth 80 marks and 32%, for 250 marks in total.

**How many optional topics do I study in 7408?**
One. It is chosen from Astrophysics, Medical physics, Engineering physics, Turning points in physics and Electronics, and is assessed in Section B of Paper 3.

**What is on Paper 3 of AQA A-Level Physics?**
Section A is a compulsory 45-mark section on practical skills and data analysis. Section B is a 35-mark section on your optional topic.

**Are there multiple-choice questions in AQA A-Level Physics?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 each include 25 marks of multiple-choice questions alongside 60 marks of short and long answer questions.

**When was AQA A-Level Physics 7408 first assessed?**
It was first taught from September 2015 and first assessed in June 2017.
