These questions cover section **4.4.1 Reactivity of metals** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for
teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The topic is assessed on Paper 1. Questions 7 and 8, and
part (b) of Question 10, test oxidation and reduction in terms of electrons, which the specification marks
(HT only), and are labelled **Higher tier only**. Everything else is for both tiers.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactivity-of-metals/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactivity-of-metals-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State what is meant by oxidation, in terms of oxygen. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is meant by reduction, in terms of oxygen. **[1]**

**2.** A student adds four metals, P, Q, R and S, to dilute hydrochloric acid. P and R fizz; Q has no reaction; S
fizzes very quickly. R fizzes more quickly than P.

**(a)** Put the four metals in order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest which of the four could be copper. **[1]**

**3.** Sodium reacts with water.

**(a)** Name the two products. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**4.** Magnesium is added to copper(II) sulfate solution.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the word equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the reaction happens. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Copper(II) oxide is heated with carbon: 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂

**(a)** Identify the substance that is reduced and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the substance that is oxidised and give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this method could not be used to extract aluminium. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why gold is found in the Earth as the metal itself, but iron is found as compounds. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) Zinc is added to copper(II) sulfate solution: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a half equation to show zinc being oxidised. **[1]**
**(c)** Write a half equation to show copper ions being reduced. **[1]**
**(d)** Identify the spectator ions. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Iron reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid: Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂

**(a)** Identify the species that is oxidised and the species that is reduced, in terms of electrons. **[2]**
**(b)** Write half equations for both changes. **[2]**

**9.** A student plans to find the order of reactivity of magnesium, zinc, iron and copper using dilute
hydrochloric acid. Describe a method, state what the student should measure or observe, and say how the results
give the order. **[6]**

**10.** Magnesium burns in oxygen: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

**(a)** State which substance is oxidised in terms of oxygen. **[1]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Write a half equation to show magnesium being oxidised, and state why it is
oxidation in terms of electrons. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Gain of oxygen** [1]
**(b)** **Loss of oxygen** [1]

**2. (a)** **S, R, P, Q** [2] (1 mark for S most reactive and Q least; 1 for R above P). **[2]**
**(b)** **Q** (no reaction with acid) [1]

**3. (a)** **Sodium hydroxide** [1] and **hydrogen** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ [2] (1 for correct formulae, 1 for balancing). **[2]**

**4. (a)** Any two [1] [1]: the blue solution **fades** / becomes colourless; a **brown** (copper-coloured) solid
forms; the magnesium gets smaller; the solution may warm. **[2]**
**(b)** magnesium + copper(II) sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper [1]
**(c)** Magnesium is **more reactive** than copper [1], so it has a greater tendency to form positive ions and
displaces copper from its compound [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** **Copper(II) oxide** [1]; it loses oxygen [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Carbon** [1]; it gains oxygen (forming carbon dioxide) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Aluminium is **more reactive than carbon** [1]

**6.** Gold is **unreactive** so it does not form compounds easily [1]; iron is more reactive, so it has combined
with other elements (for example oxygen) [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu [1]
**(b)** Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]
**(c)** Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu [1]
**(d)** **Sulfate ions** (SO₄²⁻) [1]

**8. (a)** Iron is oxidised (it loses electrons) [1]; hydrogen ions are reduced (they gain electrons) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]; 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Marking points, up to 6:
- Add equal-sized pieces of each metal to separate tubes with the same volume and concentration of acid at the
  same temperature [1].
- Observe how quickly bubbles of hydrogen form, or measure the temperature rise or the volume of gas [1].
- Keep other variables the same, such as surface area and the amount of acid [1].
- The faster the fizzing / the larger the temperature rise or volume, the more reactive the metal [1].
- Magnesium is the fastest, then zinc, then iron [1].
- Copper shows no reaction, so it is least reactive [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** **Magnesium**: it gains oxygen [1]
**(b)** Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]; magnesium **loses electrons**, which is oxidation [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up oxidation and reduction.
- Writing that a less reactive metal displaces a more reactive one.
- Not naming what is oxidised or reduced and giving a reason.
- Forgetting charges or balancing electrons in half equations (HT only).
- Describing a method without controlling variables.
