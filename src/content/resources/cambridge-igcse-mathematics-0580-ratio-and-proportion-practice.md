These questions cover subtopic 1.11 Ratio and proportion of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is identical for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer problems in context. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write each ratio in its simplest form.

**(a)** 45 : 60 : 75 **[1]**

**(b)** 1.6 : 0.4 **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Write 750 ml : 2 litres in its simplest form. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Share $1350 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write the ratio 4 : 10 in the form 1 : n. **[1]**

**(b)** A drink is made from orange juice and water in the ratio 4 : 11. What fraction of the drink is orange juice? **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A recipe for 8 biscuits uses 120 g of butter and 180 g of flour. Find the amounts of butter and flour needed for 20 biscuits. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A map has a scale of 1 : 200 000. Two towns are 4.5 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance between the towns in kilometres. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) At a show, the ratio of adults to children is 7 : 3. There are 92 more adults than children. Find the number of children and the total number of people. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator) Green paint is made by mixing yellow paint and blue paint in the ratio 3 : 5. Mia has 2.4 litres of yellow paint and 3.5 litres of blue paint. Find the largest amount of green paint she can make. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator) A shop sells shampoo in three sizes.

| Size | Price |
|---|---|
| 250 ml | $3.10 |
| 400 ml | $4.80 |
| 750 ml | $9.30 |

Which size is the best value? Show your working. **[3]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Ahmed and Bina share some stamps in the ratio 5 : 2. Ahmed then gives Bina 30 stamps, and the ratio becomes 4 : 3. How many stamps did each person have at first? **[3]**

**11.** (calculator) A model car is made to a scale of 1 : 18.

**(a)** The real car is 4.05 m long. Find the length of the model in centimetres. **[2]**

**(b)** A wheel on the model has a diameter of 3.5 cm. Find the diameter of a real wheel. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator) A pancake recipe for 4 people uses 160 g of flour, 2 eggs and 300 ml of milk.

**(a)** Find the amount of each ingredient needed for 10 people. **[2]**

**(b)** Kofi has 600 g of flour, 8 eggs and 1 litre of milk. Find the largest whole number of people he can make pancakes for. Show your working. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** ÷ 15: **3 : 4 : 5** [1]

**(b)** 16 : 4, so **4 : 1** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 750 : 2000 [1]; ÷ 250: **3 : 8** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* the units must match before you simplify. Converting 2 litres to 2000 ml earns the first mark.

**3.** 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 parts and 1350 ÷ 9 = 150 [1]; **$300, $450, $600** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5, so **1 : 2.5** [1]

**(b)** 4 + 11 = 15 parts, so **4/15** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* the fraction uses the total number of parts as its denominator. 4/11 compares juice with water, not juice with the whole drink.

**5.** Scale factor 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5 [1]; butter **300 g**, flour **450 g** [1] **[2]**

**6.** 4.5 × 200 000 = 900 000 cm [1]; 900 000 ÷ 100 000 = **9 km** [1] **[2]**

**7.** Difference in parts = 7 − 3 = 4, and 92 ÷ 4 = 23 [1]; children = 3 × 23 = **69** [1]; total = 10 × 23 = **230** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 92 is the difference between the shares, so it matches the 4-part difference. Dividing 92 by 10 is the most common error.

**8.** Using all the yellow: 2.4 ÷ 3 = 0.8 litres per part, needing 5 × 0.8 = 4 litres of blue, which is more than 3.5 litres, so blue limits the amount [1]; using all the blue: 3.5 ÷ 5 = 0.7 litres per part, so yellow used = 3 × 0.7 = 2.1 litres [1]; green paint = 2.1 + 3.5 = **5.6 litres** [1] **[3]**

**9.** Cost per 100 ml: 250 ml → 3.10 ÷ 2.5 = $1.24; 400 ml → 4.80 ÷ 4 = $1.20 [1]; 750 ml → 9.30 ÷ 7.5 = $1.24 [1]; the **400 ml** bottle is the best value [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the largest size is not always the best value. Any consistent comparison (cost per ml, or ml per dollar) earns full marks if all three values are correct and a conclusion is given.

**10.** At first Ahmed has 5k and Bina has 2k; afterwards (5k − 30) : (2k + 30) = 4 : 3 [1]; 3(5k − 30) = 4(2k + 30), so 15k − 90 = 8k + 120 and 7k = 210, k = 30 [1]; Ahmed **150**, Bina **60** [1] **[3]**

*Check:* after the gift, 120 : 90 = 4 : 3. The total, 210 stamps, does not change, so the alternative method is to split 210 as 5 : 2 and then as 4 : 3 and compare: 150 → 120 is a fall of 30.

**11. (a)** 4.05 m = 405 cm [1]; 405 ÷ 18 = **22.5 cm** [1]

**(b)** 3.5 × 18 = **63 cm** [1] **[3]**

**12. (a)** Scale factor 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 [1]; flour **400 g**, eggs **5**, milk **750 ml** [1]

**(b)** Flour: 600 ÷ 160 × 4 = 15 people; eggs: 8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 16 people [1]; milk: 1000 ÷ 300 × 4 = 13.3... people [1]; milk limits it, so **13 people** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* convert 1 litre to 1000 ml before comparing with 300 ml. The answer is rounded down to 13, because there is not enough milk for 14 people.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving mixed units in a ratio, as in Question 2.
- Dividing a difference by the total parts, as in Question 7.
- Checking only one ingredient or one paint colour in a limiting problem.
- Giving a best-value answer with no working or no conclusion.
- Forgetting that a total stays the same when one person gives items to another.
- Converting cm to km with the wrong power of 10.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a ratio in simplest form contain decimals?**
No. Simplest form uses whole numbers with no common factor. The form 1 : n is different and may use a decimal, such as 1 : 2.5.

**Is the biggest pack always the best value?**
No. Question 9 shows a middle size with the lowest cost per 100 ml. Always work out a comparable value for each option.

**What is the quickest way to deal with a map scale?**
Multiply the map length by the scale number to get the real length in the same unit, then convert. For km, divide centimetres by 100 000.
