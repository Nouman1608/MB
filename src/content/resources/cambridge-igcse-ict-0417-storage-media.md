The second content row of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 Topic 3 is about **storage media**: the material that actually holds the data. The syllabus asks for the "characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of storage media including magnetic, optical and solid-state", and names **magnetic hard disks, magnetic tape, optical discs (CD, DVD, Blu-ray)** and **solid-state media including memory cards (SD, xD, CFast)**. The drives that read and write these media are covered in the [storage devices study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-devices/).

Condensed notes are in the [storage media revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-media-revision-notes/); test yourself with the [storage media practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-media-practice/).

## Why the medium matters separately

Two people with the same DVD drive can use quite different media: a read-only DVD of a film, a write-once DVD for a single backup, or a rewritable DVD used again and again. The medium decides whether data can be changed, how long it lasts and how it can be damaged. That is why questions often ask about the medium rather than the device.

## Magnetic media

### Magnetic hard disks

- **Characteristics**: rigid circular **platters**, usually of aluminium or glass, coated with a thin layer of magnetic material. Data is stored in concentric **tracks** divided into **sectors**, as tiny areas magnetised in one of two directions. A drive may contain several platters stacked on one spindle, with both surfaces of each platter used.
- **Uses**: the medium inside fixed and portable hard disk drives: storing operating systems, programs, documents, databases and media files.
- **Advantages**: large capacity; data can be read and rewritten many times; direct access to any track and sector; low cost per gigabyte.
- **Disadvantages**: can be damaged by strong magnetic fields; the surface can be scratched if a head touches it; data can be lost if the platter is damaged; sealed inside the drive, so it cannot be swapped like a disc.

### Magnetic tape

- **Characteristics**: a long, thin plastic strip coated with magnetic material, wound on reels inside a **cartridge**. Data is written in sequence along the tape. Access is serial.
- **Uses**: backups and long-term archives in large organisations; storing data that is processed in order.
- **Advantages**: very high capacity in a small cartridge; very low cost per gigabyte; cartridges are portable and can be stored safely for many years; data can be rewritten.
- **Disadvantages**: serial access makes finding one file slow; the tape can stretch or break with heavy use; affected by strong magnetic fields, heat and damp; needs a special tape drive to read it.

## Optical media

Optical discs are thin plastic discs with a reflective layer. Data is stored along a single **spiral track** as tiny **pits** (dips) and **lands** (flat areas), which a laser reads by the way light is reflected. Recordable discs have a dye or special layer that the laser changes when writing.

### Formats you should know

| Format | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ROM (CD-ROM, DVD-ROM, BD-ROM) | Read-only; data is pressed in during manufacture and cannot be changed |
| R (CD-R, DVD-R, BD-R) | Recordable once; after writing, the data can be read but not changed |
| RW (CD-RW, DVD-RW, BD-RE) | Rewritable; data can be erased and rewritten many times |

### CD (compact disc)

- **Characteristics**: the oldest and lowest-capacity optical format, holding about 700 MB; read with a red laser.
- **Uses**: music albums; small amounts of data; some software.
- **Advantages**: very cheap; readable in many older players and computers; read-only versions protect data.
- **Disadvantages**: small capacity; slow compared with other media; scratches can make data unreadable.

### DVD (digital versatile disc)

- **Characteristics**: the same size as a CD but with smaller pits closer together and, on some discs, **two layers**, giving a much larger capacity (about 4.7 GB for one layer).
- **Uses**: standard-definition films; software and games; backups of photographs.
- **Advantages**: much more capacity than a CD; cheap; widely compatible with DVD drives.
- **Disadvantages**: not enough capacity for many high-definition films; easily scratched; needs a DVD or Blu-ray drive.

### Blu-ray disc

- **Characteristics**: the same size again but read with a **blue-violet laser**, whose shorter wavelength allows even smaller pits, giving far greater capacity (about 25 GB for one layer, with multi-layer discs holding more). The disc has a harder protective coating.
- **Uses**: high-definition films; games; large backups and archives.
- **Advantages**: very large capacity for an optical disc; high-definition video and sound; more scratch-resistant than CDs and DVDs.
- **Disadvantages**: discs and drives cost more than CD and DVD; Blu-ray discs cannot be read in CD or DVD drives.

### Strengths and weaknesses of optical media in general

Optical discs are light, cheap and easy to post. They are not affected by magnetic fields, and read-only discs give strong protection against accidental changes. However, they hold far less than hard disks or tape, access is slower than solid-state media, and they can be damaged by scratches, heat or sunlight.

## Solid-state media: memory cards

Memory cards store data in **flash memory**, with no moving parts. They are small removable cards that are read by the device they are placed in, such as a camera, or by a **card reader** attached to a computer. The syllabus names three types.

### SD cards (Secure Digital)

- **Characteristics**: small, thin, rectangular cards. A smaller **microSD** version is used in phones and tablets.
- **Uses**: storing photographs and video in digital cameras; extra storage in smartphones, tablets, drones and games consoles; transferring files to a computer.
- **Advantages**: very small and light; widely compatible with many devices; robust, with no moving parts; large capacities available at low cost.
- **Disadvantages**: so small that they are easily lost; can be damaged by bending; can fail without warning, so important files still need a backup.

### xD cards

- **Characteristics**: a small flash memory card format used mainly in some older digital cameras.
- **Uses**: storing photographs in cameras designed for the format.
- **Advantages**: very small and light; no moving parts.
- **Disadvantages**: works only in devices designed for xD; many card readers and modern cameras do not support it; lower capacities than modern SD cards.

### CFast cards

- **Characteristics**: larger, higher-performance flash memory cards designed for **very fast data transfer**.
- **Uses**: professional video cameras and high-end digital cameras that record large amounts of high-resolution data each second.
- **Advantages**: very high read and write speeds, so high-quality video can be recorded without interruption; robust.
- **Disadvantages**: expensive; supported by fewer devices; larger than SD cards.

## Comparison table

| Medium | Technology | Capacity | Rewritable? | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard disk | Magnetic | Very large | Yes | Magnetic fields, head crash |
| Tape | Magnetic | Very large | Yes | Slow serial access, stretching |
| CD | Optical | Small | ROM no, R once, RW yes | Scratches |
| DVD | Optical | Medium | ROM no, R once, RW yes | Scratches |
| Blu-ray | Optical | Large for a disc | ROM no, R once, RE yes | Cost, compatibility |
| SD, xD, CFast | Solid-state | Small to large | Yes | Loss, compatibility (xD, CFast) |

## Worked example: choosing a medium

*A wedding photographer shoots high-resolution video and photos, then gives each couple a copy of their pictures. Recommend a storage medium for the camera and one for the couple's copy, and justify each. [4]*

> In the camera: an SD card (or CFast card for professional video) (1), because it is small enough to fit in the camera and its fast flash memory can record large files without delay (1). For the couple: a write-once optical disc such as a DVD-R or BD-R (1), because it is cheap, can be posted or handed over easily, and the photos cannot be accidentally deleted once written (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying a CD "uses magnetism". Optical media use a laser and pits and lands.
- Confusing R and RW: R can be written only once; RW can be rewritten.
- Calling an SD card a storage device. It is solid-state storage media.
- Saying Blu-ray discs are physically bigger than DVDs. They are the same size; the pits are smaller.
- Forgetting compatibility as a disadvantage, especially for xD and CFast cards.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** a medium from clues: pits and lands (optical), tracks and sectors (magnetic disk), flash memory and card reader (solid-state).
- **Describe** characteristics: technology, capacity, whether it can be rewritten, portability.
- **Justify** a choice: link one property of the medium to each need in the scenario.
- **Compare**: use capacity, access speed, durability, whether data can be changed, cost and compatibility.
