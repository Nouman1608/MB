**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The Hadith and Sunnah as a source of Shariah: "The relationship of the Hadith to the Qur'an as a source of guidance and Shariah law, including the importance of the teachings and Sunnah of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh)."** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 53:1-6.

Three words need to be clear from the start. They are often used together, so examiners expect you to separate them.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hadith** | A report about what the Prophet (pbuh) said, did, approved or was like. It is a piece of text, handed down through narrators. |
| **Sunnah** | The Prophet's (pbuh) way: his example in belief, worship, character and dealings with others. The Sunnah is what the Hadith record. |
| **Shariah** | The path or law of Islam: the guidance from Allah on how to live. It is drawn from the Qur'an and the Sunnah first, and then from Ijma and Qiyas. |

## The Qur'an and the Hadith: how they relate

The Qur'an is the first source. It sets out beliefs, principles and many rules, but often in broad terms. The Hadith, as the record of the Sunnah, relates to it in three ways.

**1. It explains and gives detail.** The Qur'an commands prayer, but does not state the number of rak'ahs, the movements or the times. The Prophet (pbuh) said, "Pray as you have seen me praying" (Sahih al-Bukhari 631). Zakah is commanded, and the Sunnah gives the minimum amount (nisab) and the rates. For Hajj he said, "Take your rites from me" (Sahih Muslim 1297), and the pilgrimage is performed as he performed it.

**2. It makes the general specific.** A general command can be limited by a Hadith. For example, the Qur'an gives rules of inheritance, and a Hadith clarifies that a murderer does not inherit from his victim.

**3. It adds guidance.** Some rulings are found in the Hadith and not stated in the Qur'an, such as the prohibition on marrying a woman and her paternal aunt at the same time. The Qur'an itself accepts this role: "whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from" (59:7).

Here is the order of authority. The Qur'an comes first. The Sunnah comes second, and cannot contradict the Qur'an. The Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) shows this: sent as a judge to Yemen, he said he would judge by the Book of Allah, then by the Sunnah of the Messenger, and then by his own reasoning, and the Prophet (pbuh) approved (Abu Dawud).

## Why does the Qur'an tell Muslims to follow the Sunnah?

The Qur'an gives the Prophet (pbuh) the role of teacher and model, and those verses are the foundation of the Sunnah's authority.

- **Obedience.** "He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah" (4:80). "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger" (4:59).
- **Example.** "There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent pattern" (33:21).
- **Love of Allah.** "Say, [O Muhammad], 'If you should love Allah, then follow me, [so] Allah will love you'" (3:31).
- **Explanation.** The Qur'an says the Prophet was sent "that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them" (16:44).

His wife Hazrat Aisha (RA) was asked about his character and answered that it was the Qur'an (Sahih Muslim). In other words, his life was the Qur'an put into practice.

## Kinds of Sunnah

| Kind | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sayings (*qawl*) | Things he said | "Actions are judged by intentions" |
| Actions (*fi'l*) | Things he did | How he prayed, fasted and performed Hajj |
| Approvals (*taqrir*) | Things done in his presence which he accepted by not objecting | Accepting a companion's practice in his presence |
| Character (*sifah*) | Descriptions of his nature and appearance | His honesty, kindness and patience |

The Sunnah is also divided into what is **obligatory** (such as the five daily prayers) and what is **recommended** (such as extra prayers at night). Muslims follow the first as a duty and the second for extra reward.

## Why the Hadith and Sunnah matter

- **Practical guidance.** Without the Sunnah, Muslims would not know how to perform the Five Pillars correctly.
- **Interpretation.** It protects the Qur'an from being read in ways that go against the Prophet's (pbuh) understanding.
- **A model of character.** His treatment of family, neighbours, enemies and the poor shows what Islamic character looks like.
- **Law.** Scholars use it to answer questions about worship, trade, family life and crime.
- **Unity.** Muslims all over the world pray, fast and perform Hajj in the same basic way.

## The Sunnah in the Five Pillars

The best way to see the Sunnah at work is to look at the pillars. The Shahadah is stated in the Qur'an, and the Sunnah shows how it is said and taught. Salah is commanded in the Qur'an, and the Sunnah gives its times, its form and the call to prayer. The Qur'an commands fasting in Ramadhan, and the Sunnah gives the pre-dawn meal, the breaking of the fast and the voluntary fasts. Zakah is commanded in the Qur'an, and the Sunnah gives the minimum amount and the rates. Hajj is commanded in the Qur'an, and the Sunnah gives the order of the rites. Without the Sunnah, the pillars would be known by name and not by practice.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 53:1-6

In the specification's translation the passage reads: "By the star when it descends, your companion [i.e., Muhammad] has not strayed, nor has he erred, nor does he speak from [his own] inclination. It is not but a revelation revealed, taught to him by one intense in strength [i.e., Jibril (AS)], one of soundness. And he rose to [his] true form."

| Phrase | How to use it |
|---|---|
| "your companion has not strayed, nor has he erred" | The Prophet (pbuh) was protected from error in his message, so his guidance can be trusted |
| "nor does he speak from [his own] inclination" | His religious teaching did not come from personal desire |
| "it is not but a revelation revealed" | The Prophet's guidance came from Allah; many scholars use this to support the authority of the Sunnah as well as the Qur'an |
| "taught to him by one intense in strength" | Jibril (AS) was the means of revelation |
| "and he rose to [his] true form" | A reference to the Prophet (pbuh) seeing Jibril (AS) in his real form |

When using the passage, be careful. Muslims agree that it proves the Qur'an is revelation. Many scholars also use it to argue that the Prophet's (pbuh) religious teaching is guided by Allah, so the Sunnah has authority. Others say the verses refer to the Qur'an only. In an answer, "many scholars use it to support" is the safest wording.

## An argument you may be asked to evaluate

**Statement:** "The Qur'an is enough; Muslims do not need the Hadith."

- **For:** the Qur'an describes itself as a clarification of all things (16:89), is complete (5:3) and is protected (15:9), whereas Hadith were written down later and contain some weak reports.
- **Against:** the Qur'an itself commands obedience to the Messenger (4:59, 59:7); it does not give the details of prayer or Zakah; the Hadith of Mu'adh shows the Sunnah as the second source; the Prophet's (pbuh) example is the model that the Qur'an points to; scholars have tested Hadith carefully and separate the reliable from the weak (see the guide on categorisation).
- **Judgement:** the Qur'an is the foundation, but it tells Muslims to follow the Messenger, so the Sunnah is needed to apply it.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which the Hadith helps Muslims to understand the Qur'an. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> The Hadith gives the detail of acts of worship [1]. The Qur'an commands prayer but does not describe it, and the Hadith records how the Prophet (pbuh) prayed, so Muslims can pray correctly [1]. The Hadith also shows how to apply general rules to real situations [1], such as the way the Prophet (pbuh) judged disputes and dealt with neighbours, which gives a living example of the Qur'an [1]. Qur'an 33:21 says that the Messenger of Allah is an excellent pattern for those who hope in Allah, which is why Muslims follow his example [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Using Hadith and Sunnah as if they were identical.** The Hadith is the report; the Sunnah is the practice it records.
- **Saying the Hadith can overrule the Qur'an.** The Sunnah explains the Qur'an and cannot contradict it.
- **Claiming that all Hadith are equally reliable.** They are graded (see the guide on compilation and categorisation).
- **Leaving out a Qur'anic reason for following the Sunnah.** Use 4:59, 33:21 or 59:7.
- **Using the passage too strongly.** Say "many scholars use 53:3-4 to support the Sunnah".
