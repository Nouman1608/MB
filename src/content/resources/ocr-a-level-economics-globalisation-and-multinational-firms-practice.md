## Questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by globalisation. **[2]**

**2.** Explain two characteristics of globalisation. **[4]**

**3.** Explain two causes of globalisation. **[4]**

**4.** A furniture maker produces 50,000 units a year at home at a unit cost of 52. In Country E its unit cost would be 41, including freight of 3. Setting up costs 2.2 million.
**(a)** Calculate the annual saving and the payback period. **[3]**
**(b)** Freight rises to 6 a unit. Recalculate the payback period and comment. **[2]**

**5.** Country E's real GDP grows 8% a year and its population 1.5%.
**(a)** Calculate the growth of real GDP per head. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why this may overstate the improvement in living standards. **[2]**

**6.** In a developed economy, money wages rise 8% for skilled and 2% for unskilled workers while prices rise 4%. Calculate the real change for each group and comment on the result. **[4]**

**7.** In Country D one crop is 65% of export earnings. The world price falls 30% and volumes are unchanged.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in total export earnings. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why such an economy may gain less from globalisation than a diversified one. **[2]**

**8.** A foreign-owned plant in Country D has value added of 200 a year: wages 70, taxes 20 and profit 110 (all owned abroad), of which 80 is sent abroad. The initial investment was 250.
**(a)** Calculate the share of value added that is income to residents. **[2]**
**(b)** After how many full years does cumulative profit sent abroad exceed the initial investment? **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one benefit of the plant that these figures omit. **[2]**

**9.** Evaluate the impact of multinational firms on the world economy. **[10]**

**10.** Country A's exports are 18% of its GDP, and 25% of its exports go to Country E. Country E's import growth falls by 6 percentage points.
**(a)** Calculate the effect on Country A's GDP growth. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one other way Country E's slowdown could affect Country A. **[2]**
**(c)** Assess whether the effect is likely to be large. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate the impact of globalisation on developed, emerging and developing economies. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Globalisation is the process by which economies become more closely linked and dependent on each other [1] as goods, services, capital, people, technology and ideas move across borders [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the process and at least two flows; "trade between countries" alone describes one channel.

**2.** Any two, each with development. Trade grows faster than output [1], so trade intensity rises [1]. Multinational firms spread production across countries [1], linking suppliers in global supply chains [1]. (Also acceptable: FDI, financial integration, migration, technology flows.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each characteristic needs a second step that says what it means; a label alone gains less.

**3.** Any two, each with a link. Lower transport costs [1] make distant production and sale profitable [1]. Information technology [1] lets firms coordinate suppliers and customers across time zones [1]. (Also acceptable: trade or capital liberalisation, opening of emerging economies.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how the cause leads to more cross-border activity, not just that it exists.

**4. (a)** Saving per unit = 52 - 41 = 11 [1]. Annual saving = 11 x 50,000 = 550,000 [1]. Payback = 2.2 million / 550,000 = **4 years** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** New cost = 41 + 3 = 44, saving 8, annual 400,000 [1]. Payback = **5.5 years**, longer, so the move is less attractive [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the saving per unit before multiplying, and state units; a comment should refer to the change in the figure.

**5. (a)** 1.08 / 1.015 - 1 [1] = **6.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Per head figures are averages [1], so gains may go to a few while others see little change, or profits may leave the country [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtracting population growth from GDP growth is a common slip; use the ratio of growth factors.

**6.** Skilled: 1.08 / 1.04 - 1 [1] = +3.8% [1]. Unskilled: 1.02 / 1.04 - 1 [1] = -1.9% [1]. Skilled workers gain in real terms and unskilled workers lose, so inequality widens. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment on the contrast adds more than restating the two figures.

**7. (a)** 0.65 x 30 = **19.5%** fall [1] in total export earnings [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Reliance on one product exposes it to price swings [1], and a fall in earnings reduces income and its ability to pay for imports and investment [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State that the fall is in total earnings, not in the crop alone.

**8. (a)** Resident income = 70 + 20 = 90 [1]; 90 / 200 = **45%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Cumulative outflow: 80, 160, 240, 320 [1], so **4 years** (240 is below 250 and 320 is above) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: technology and skills spread to local firms; local suppliers gain orders; export earnings. Explained in context [2]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b) check the year before the crossing to justify "full years".

**9.** Gains (up to 4): FDI moves capital to where it is productive [1]; technology and skills spread [1]; competition and scale lower prices [1]; jobs and exports raise incomes in host economies [1].
Costs (up to 3): profits sent abroad and transfer pricing reduce income and tax for hosts [1]; market power crowds out local firms [1]; footloose behaviour and weak standards leave hosts exposed [1].
Evaluation (up to 3): net effect depends on local linkages and tax rules [1]; on the host's bargaining power and regulation [1]; judgement on balance with a reason, such as gains are larger where the host captures spillovers [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover the world economy, not only one host, and build the judgement from the conditions you named.

**10. (a)** Effect = 0.18 x 0.25 x 6 [1] = **0.27 percentage points** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Lower commodity prices if Country E slows, which helps importers and hurts exporters [1]; or less investment from Country E, or exchange rate effects, explained [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The direct effect is small (0.27 points) [1], but larger if exports are concentrated on Country E or other markets cannot replace the lost demand [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote units as percentage points, and make the judgement depend on stated conditions.

**11.** Developed (up to 3): cheaper imports and larger markets raise real incomes [1]; competition and FDI raise productivity [1]; structural unemployment and pressure on low-skilled wages arise [1].
Emerging (up to 3): export-led growth, FDI and technology raise output per head [1]; poverty falls [1]; dependence on foreign demand and inequality between regions are risks [1].
Developing (up to 3): access to markets, aid and cheaper capital goods [1]; reliance on volatile primary exports and weak bargaining power [1]; limited infrastructure and skills may deter FDI [1].
Evaluation (up to 3): outcomes depend on starting position and institutions [1]; on policies such as retraining and diversification [1]; judgement on balance with a reason, such as gains are widest where institutions allow the poor to share them [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat the three groups separately and say who gains within each; a general list of pros and cons does not show this.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing globalisation as trade alone.
- Naming a characteristic or cause without saying what it does.
- Subtracting population growth from GDP growth instead of using growth factors.
- Mixing up percentage and percentage-point changes.
- Forgetting that profit sent abroad reduces income to residents.
- Writing one list of pros and cons for all economies in an evaluation of three groups.
- Concluding "it depends" without naming the condition that decides the answer.
- Quoting a payback or share without units or comment.
