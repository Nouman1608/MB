These notes condense the class practical requirements of the IB Psychology course (first assessment 2027). They apply to SL and HL. For the full explanation, read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-class-practicals/) and then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-class-practicals-practice/).

## Quick facts

- One practical for **each context**; minimum four; about 30 teaching hours.
- Each practical uses one of the **four methods** that the internal assessment research proposal can use.
- Examples in the guide are suggestions only.
- Practicals are normally done in lesson time; the teacher can be the researcher and the class the participants.
- Student-led extras need explicit permission on the class practical research form.
- Keep a brief record: aim, procedure, sampling technique, sample findings, ethics. It is not assessed.
- **Paper 2 Section A** tests the method, not the context.

## The practicals

| Context | Method | Minimum sample (student-led) | Example suggestions in the guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health and well-being | Interview: structured, semi-structured or focus group | 1 individual, or 3 to 8 in a focus group | Focus group on managing stress; interview on exercise and mood |
| Human development | Observation: naturalistic or controlled, overt or covert, participant or non-participant | 1 | Playground observation; language class |

## Method toolkit

**Interview**
- Structured: fixed questions, comparable, little depth.
- Semi-structured: planned questions plus follow-ups, rich data, harder to compare.
- Focus group: moderated discussion, shows group dynamics, risk of dominant members and conformity.
- Data: mainly qualitative; analysis by thematic analysis or content analysis.
- Biases: interviewer bias, social desirability, leading questions.

**Observation**
- Naturalistic = real setting; controlled = set up by the researcher.
- Overt = participants know; covert = they do not.
- Participant = observer joins in; non-participant = observer watches.
- Strengths: ecological validity, behaviour not claims. Limits: observer bias, no cause and effect, ethics of covert work.
- Use a behavioural checklist or time sampling to reduce bias, and check inter-rater reliability.

**Survey/questionnaire**
- Closed questions: quantitative, easy to analyse. Open questions: qualitative.
- Strengths: many participants, anonymity, cheap. Limits: social desirability, response bias, question wording, low response rates.
- Pilot it first.

**Experiment**
- Independent variable (manipulated) and dependent variable (measured). Control extraneous variables.
- True experiment: random allocation, strongest for cause and effect.
- Quasi-experiment: pre-existing groups, so no random allocation, weaker causality.
- Designs: independent measures, repeated measures, matched pairs. Counterbalance for order effects.

## Sampling techniques

| Technique | How | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-selected | Volunteers respond | Easy, consenting | Biased to the motivated |
| Opportunity | Whoever is available | Quick | Unrepresentative |
| Stratified | Proportion from each subgroup | Representative | Needs population data |
| Random | Equal chance for all | Least biased | Needs a full list |
| Snowball | Participants recruit others | Reaches hidden groups | Not representative |

## Ethics rules to learn

- **No harm.** No anxiety, stress, pain or discomfort. Teacher supervision.
- **No conformity or obedience studies.** Not permitted under any circumstances.
- **Partial deception** only if harmless, followed by a full debrief, with the right to withdraw data.
- **Informed consent** by consent form; **implied consent is not acceptable**.
- **Under 16:** written consent from parents or guardians, and from teachers if in school. **16 and over:** parental consent not required.
- **No animals** in class practicals.
- **Sensitive topics:** do not interview peers on mental health diagnoses, bullying or self-harm. Better: stress reduction, healthy habits, media use.
- **Naturalistic observation:** public places where people expect to be seen by strangers are generally acceptable; the teacher decides.
- **Data protection:** confidential storage, anonymity kept after the practical, use only for the agreed purpose, delete online data afterwards.

## Paper 2 Section A technique

| Part | What it tests | Marks | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Knowledge of the method | 4 | Define terms accurately and use correct vocabulary |
| 1b | Application of a concept | 4 | Link the concept to your practical at least twice |
| 1c | Compare and contrast | 6 | Use comparison words; cover both similarities and differences |
| 1d | Design | 6 | Ordered, repeatable steps: sampling, materials, procedure, ethics, analysis |

## Mini model: designing an observation (1d style)

1. State the aim and what behaviour is being observed (for example, the number of cooperative acts between children at break).
2. Choose the type: naturalistic, non-participant, overt or covert, with a reason.
3. Define the behavioural categories in observable terms (operationalise) and prepare a checklist.
4. Choose the sampling technique and the time sampling (for example, 10 minutes every hour).
5. Decide who observes and how inter-rater reliability will be checked.
6. Describe consent and ethics: permission from the school, no names, secure storage.
7. Say how you will analyse the tally data (descriptive statistics).

## Common mistakes

- Writing about the findings of the practical instead of the method.
- Mixing up overt/covert with naturalistic/controlled.
- Saying "a quasi-experiment has random allocation".
- Giving ethical rules without applying them.

## Self-test

1. Name the method used in each of the four contexts.
2. What is the minimum sample for a student-led survey?
3. Why is a quasi-experiment weaker than a true experiment for causal claims?
4. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of snowball sampling.
5. What age does the guide set for requiring parental consent?
6. Which three topics does the guide suggest are more suitable than mental health for peer interviews?
7. What does Paper 2 Section A focus on?

**Answers:** 1. Interview, observation, survey/questionnaire, experiment. 2. 10 participants. 3. Participants are not randomly allocated and the independent variable may not be manipulated, so other differences between groups may explain the result. 4. Advantage: reaches hidden groups; disadvantage: not representative. 5. Under 16 needs written consent from parents or guardians; 16 and older does not. 6. Stress reduction techniques, healthy lifestyle habits and media consumption. 7. The research method, not the context.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to use the practicals the guide lists?**
No. They are suggestions only. You can use another practical if it relates to the context and follows the ethics rules.

**Are my class practical records marked?**
No. They are not assessed, but they help with Paper 2 Section A and the internal assessment.

**Can I run a study on a sensitive topic such as self-harm?**
No. The guide says it would be inappropriate for students to interview each other on such topics.
