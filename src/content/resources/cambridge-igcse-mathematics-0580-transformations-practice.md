These questions cover subtopic 7.1, Transformations, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use Core content (C7.1) and suit every candidate. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use E7.1 content: reflection in any line, rotation about any centre, negative scale factors and combinations. Shapes are given by the coordinates of their vertices, so you can draw them on squared paper. Learn the methods first with the [transformations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-transformations/) and the [transformations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-transformations-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** The point P is (4, −3). Find the image of P after

**(a)** a reflection in the line y = 1, **[1]**

**(b)** a reflection in the line x = 6. **[1]**

**2.** The point Q is (3, 5). Find the image of Q after

**(a)** a rotation of 90° anticlockwise about the origin, **[1]**

**(b)** a rotation of 180° about the origin. **[1]**

**3.** Triangle R has vertices (2, 2), (4, 2) and (2, 6). Find the vertices of its image after an enlargement with scale factor 3, centre (1, 1). **[2]**

**4.** A translation maps the point (6, −1) onto the point (2, 3).

**(a)** Write down the column vector of the translation. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps (2, 3) back onto (6, −1). **[1]**

**5.** (Extended) The point S is (6, 2). Find the image of S after

**(a)** a reflection in the line y = −x, **[1]**

**(b)** a reflection in the line y = x. **[1]**

**6.** A triangle has an area of 36 cm². It is enlarged by scale factor ½. Find the area of the image. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Triangle A has vertices (1, 2), (4, 2) and (1, 4). Describe fully the single transformation that maps triangle A onto

**(a)** triangle B, with vertices (−1, 2), (−4, 2) and (−1, 4), **[2]**

**(b)** triangle C, with vertices (2, −1), (2, −4) and (4, −1), **[3]**

**(c)** triangle D, with vertices (4, −1), (7, −1) and (4, 1). **[2]**

**8.** Triangle E has vertices (2, 1), (3, 1) and (3, 3). Triangle F has vertices (4, 3), (6, 3) and (6, 7).

**(a)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps E onto F. **[3]**

**(b)** How many times larger is the area of F than the area of E? **[1]**

**9.** (Extended) Triangle A from question 7 is rotated through 90° anticlockwise about the point (−1, 1). Find the vertices of the image. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) Triangle G has vertices (1, 2), (2, 2) and (1, 4).

**(a)** Find the vertices of the image of G after an enlargement with scale factor −2, centre (0, 1). **[3]**

**(b)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps the image back onto G. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) A shape is reflected in the x-axis and then the image is reflected in the line y = x. Describe fully the single transformation that has the same effect. **[3]**

**12.** (Extended) A reflection maps the point (1, 4) onto the point (5, 0). Find the equation of the mirror line. **[3]**

**13.** (Extended) A rotation of 90° clockwise maps the point (3, 1) onto (0, 0) and the point (5, 1) onto (0, −2). Find the centre of rotation. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **(4, 5)** [1]

**(b)** **(8, −3)** [1]

**2. (a)** **(−5, 3)** [1]

**(b)** **(−3, −5)** [1]

**3.** Distances from (1, 1) are (1, 1), (3, 1) and (1, 5); multiplied by 3 they become (3, 3), (9, 3) and (3, 15) [1]; vertices **(4, 4), (10, 4), (4, 16)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* multiply the distance from the centre, not the coordinates themselves. Tripling (2, 2) to get (6, 6) is an enlargement from the origin.

**4. (a)** **(−4, 4)** [1]

**(b)** **Translation by the column vector (4, −4)** [1]

**5. (a)** **(−2, −6)** [1]

**(b)** **(2, 6)** [1]

**6.** Area scale factor = (½)² = ¼ [1]; image area = **9 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* halving the area to 18 cm² uses the length scale factor for an area, a common slip.

**7. (a)** **Reflection** [1] in the line **x = 0** (the y-axis) [1]

**(b)** **Rotation** [1], **90° clockwise** [1], about the **origin (0, 0)** [1]

**(c)** **Translation** [1] by the column vector **(3, −3)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b) check the rule (x, y) → (y, −x) on every vertex. A rotation description with no centre loses a mark even if the angle and direction are right.

**8. (a)** **Enlargement** [1], **scale factor 2** [1], **centre (0, −1)** [1]

**(b)** 2² = **4 times** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the side from (2, 1) to (3, 1) has length 1 and its image has length 2, giving the scale factor. The centre comes from (2 × object − image) ÷ (2 − 1) = 2(2, 1) − (4, 3) = (0, −1).

**9.** Subtract (−1, 1): (2, 1), (5, 1), (2, 3) [1]; rotate 90° anticlockwise: (−1, 2), (−1, 5), (−3, 2) [1]; add (−1, 1) back: **(−2, 3), (−2, 6), (−4, 3)** [1]

**10. (a)** Distances from (0, 1): (1, 1), (2, 1), (1, 3) [1]; × (−2): (−2, −2), (−4, −2), (−2, −6) [1]; vertices **(−2, −1), (−4, −1), (−2, −5)** [1]

**(b)** **Enlargement, centre (0, 1)** [1], **scale factor −½** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the reverse of an enlargement with scale factor k has scale factor 1/k about the same centre, so −2 becomes −½, not +½ or 2.

**11.** (x, y) → (x, −y) after the first reflection, then → (−y, x) after the second [1]; this is the rule for a **rotation** [1] of **90° anticlockwise about the origin** [1]

**12.** The mirror line passes through the midpoint (3, 2) [1]; the segment has gradient (0 − 4) ÷ (5 − 1) = −1, so the mirror line has gradient 1 [1]; y − 2 = 1(x − 3), so **y = x − 1** [1]

**13.** The centre is the same distance from (3, 1) and (0, 0): 3x + y = 5 [1]; and from (5, 1) and (0, −2): 5x + 3y = 11 [1]; solving gives the centre **(1, 2)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check the answer: (3, 1) − (1, 2) = (2, −1); rotated 90° clockwise this is (−1, −2); adding (1, 2) gives (0, 0) as required. Tracing paper on a grid is an acceptable way to find the centre when a diagram is given.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing the centre, angle or direction in a rotation description.
- Giving the mirror line in words instead of as an equation.
- Enlarging from the origin when another centre is given.
- Using the length scale factor for an area.
- Describing two transformations instead of a single one.
- *(Extended)* Getting the order of combined transformations wrong, or the sign of a negative scale factor.
