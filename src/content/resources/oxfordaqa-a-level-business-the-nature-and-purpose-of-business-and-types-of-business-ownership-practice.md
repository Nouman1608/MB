## Questions

**1.** State **two** non-financial reasons why people set up in business. **[2]**

**2.** Leyla has £20,000 to spend. She can buy a delivery van or a second oven for her bakery, and she buys the van. Define opportunity cost and identify Leyla's opportunity cost. **[3]**

**3.** Fordwych Freight plc has 5,000,000 ordinary shares in issue. It pays a total dividend of £850,000. The share price is £2.50.

**(a)** Calculate the dividend per share. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the dividend yield. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the market capitalisation. **[2]**

**4.** Ploverdale Candles must choose how to raise output. Option C is a new melting line costing £35,000. It has a 0.6 chance of returning £70,000 and a 0.4 chance of returning £20,000. Option D is second-hand equipment costing £18,000. It has a 0.5 chance of returning £48,000 and a 0.5 chance of returning £30,000.

**(a)** Calculate the net gain of each option. **[4]**
**(b)** Justify which option Ploverdale should choose. **[4]**

**5.** Rukmini's objective for her bakery is "to be the best-known bakery in the district as soon as possible".

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why this is not a SMART objective. **[2]**
**(b)** Rewrite it as a SMART objective. **[2]**

**6.** Leyla and Rohan run Wydcombe Print Studio as a partnership. Leyla does the design work and Rohan runs the presses. Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of their business form. **[4]**

**7.**

**(a)** Identify the sector (primary, secondary or tertiary) of a fish farm, a furniture workshop and a dental clinic. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what is meant by a public sector organisation. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways a government might support start-ups, and **one** reason why many start-ups fail. **[6]**

**9.** Anneke is writing a business plan for a new catering firm.

**(a)** State **two** key elements of a business plan. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse **one** benefit and **one** limitation of the plan to Anneke. **[4]**

**10.** Wickersley Textiles plc has 3,000,000 shares. Its share price falls from £4.00 to £3.20.

**(a)** Calculate the fall in market capitalisation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **two** influences on a company's share price. **[4]**

**11.** Dilnoza owns Harnscar Bikes, a sole trader business that made a profit of £46,000 last year. She wants to open a second shop costing £80,000. She has £30,000 of savings. The bank will lend the rest only if she uses her house as security. Her cousin offers to pay £50,000 for 40% of the shares if she converts the business to a private limited company. Evaluate whether Dilnoza should convert. **[10]**

**12.** Skerrowmoor Dairy is a family-run business with 14 employees. The family plan to buy milking equipment for £90,000. The business would then need 9 employees, and each employee costs £24,000 a year.

**(a)** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of being a family business when making this decision. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate the likely impact of the plan on the stakeholders and recommend whether the family should go ahead. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each, from: independence or being your own boss; using a skill or following a passion; flexible working hours; helping others or the community [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Earning more money is a financial reason, so it would not answer a question asking for non-financial ones.

**2.** Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative forgone when a choice is made [1]. Leyla's opportunity cost is the second oven [1], meaning the extra output or sales it would have brought [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition on its own is unlikely to earn the application mark. Name the actual alternative that was given up.

**3. (a)** Dividend per share = 850,000 / 5,000,000 [1] = **17p (£0.17)** [1].
**(b)** Yield = 0.17 / 2.50 x 100 [1] = **6.8%** [1].
**(c)** Market capitalisation = 2.50 x 5,000,000 [1] = **£12,500,000** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep pence and pounds consistent. Dividing 17 by 2.50 is wrong by a factor of 100, and writing the formula first shows your method.

**4. (a)** EV of C = 0.6 x 70,000 + 0.4 x 20,000 = **£50,000** [1]; net gain = 50,000 - 35,000 = **£15,000** [1]. EV of D = 0.5 x 48,000 + 0.5 x 30,000 = **£39,000** [1]; net gain = 39,000 - 18,000 = **£21,000** [1].
**(b)** D has the higher net gain, by £6,000 [1]. In the worst case D still gains £12,000 (30,000 - 18,000), whereas C loses £15,000 (20,000 - 35,000) [1]. The probabilities are only estimates and the figures ignore non-financial factors such as quality [1]. Judgement: choose D, because it is better on both expected net gain and worst case, unless Ploverdale has strong evidence that demand is much higher than 0.6 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that stops at expected value, without subtracting the cost, is comparing the wrong figures. A justification gains more when it weighs the worst case as well as the average.

**5. (a)** It is not measurable, because "best-known" has no number to check [1]. It has no deadline, because "as soon as possible" is not a time target [1].
**(b)** For example: raise recognition in a local household survey from 18% to 30% [1] by 31 December 2027, with the marketing manager responsible [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A rewrite gains credit by containing a measure and a deadline, not by adding "quickly" to the original.

**6.** Advantage: the partners bring different skills, with Leyla on design and Rohan on production [1], so the studio offers a fuller service [1]. Disadvantage: partners usually have unlimited liability [1], so if Rohan agrees a large debt for the studio, Leyla's personal assets could be used to pay it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" means a chain of reasoning. State the point, then say why it matters for this studio rather than for partnerships in general.

**7. (a)** Fish farm: primary [1]. Furniture workshop: secondary [1]. Dental clinic: tertiary [1].
**(b)** A public sector organisation is owned and run by government [1], usually to provide a public service rather than to make a profit for owners [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not confuse public sector with public limited company. A plc is owned by private shareholders and is part of the private sector.

**8.** Two ways, 2 marks each, for example: grants or low-interest loans [1] that lower the cost of finance for a start-up [1]; advice and training [1], which improves skills in areas where the founder lacks experience [1]. One reason for failure: poor cash-flow control [1], because it runs out of cash to pay suppliers and wages [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each "explain" point needs the support or reason plus its effect. A bare list of types of help leaves the development unearned.

**9. (a)** Any two from: ownership; objectives; target market and market analysis; competitor analysis; unique selling point; financial analysis [1] [1].
**(b)** Benefit: writing the plan forces Anneke to analyse her target market and competitors [1], so weaknesses show up before she spends money [1]. Limitation: the plan depends on forecasts of sales and costs [1], which may prove wrong [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie the benefit and limitation to a catering firm, for example bookings that are hard to forecast.

**10. (a)** Before: 4.00 x 3,000,000 = £12,000,000. After: 3.20 x 3,000,000 = £9,600,000 [1]. Fall = **£2,400,000** [1].
**(b)** Any two, 2 marks each, for example: lower profit or weaker profit forecasts [1] make the shares less attractive, so more people sell than buy and the price falls [1]; a rise in interest rates [1] makes saving more attractive, so some investors sell shares to move their money [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link every influence to the demand for or supply of the shares. "The economy" alone shows no reasoning.

**11.** Points for converting: as a sole trader Dilnoza has unlimited liability, so her house is at risk if the second shop fails [1]; a private limited company gives limited liability [1]. Her £30,000 plus the cousin's £50,000 gives exactly the £80,000 needed with no bank loan [1]. Against: the cousin would own 40% and take 40% of dividends [1], although Dilnoza keeps 60% and so majority control [1]. The cousin could disagree with her decisions [1], and a company has more formal duties [1]. Alternative: borrowing keeps all profit but needs security and interest [1]. Judgement: converting looks better, because it protects her home on an untested second shop [1], but it depends on how far she trusts her cousin and how confident she is of demand [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation gains more when both routes are compared using this owner's figures and the choice states what it depends on.

**12. (a)** Advantage: family members share values and a long-term view [1], so they may accept a large outlay that pays back over many years [1]. Disadvantage: the family may disagree, or may be reluctant to make relatives or long-serving staff redundant [1], which can delay a decision [1].
**(b)** Annual wage saving = 5 x 24,000 = £120,000 [1]. Owners gain from lower costs and higher profit, but must find £90,000 and bear the risk the equipment fails [1]. Five employees lose their jobs and the rest may face more pressure [1]. The community loses local jobs and spending [1]. The supplier gains an order, and customers may see consistent quality [1]. Creditors will want reassurance they can be repaid [1]. Judgement: go ahead if the family can fund it without risking the business, since the saving is large relative to cost [1], but it depends on how the redundancies are handled and the effect on the firm's local reputation [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Stakeholder answers gain from naming the group, the change and the effect on it. One word per group stays at description.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping a decision-tree answer at expected value, without subtracting the cost of each option.
- Mixing pence and pounds in dividend and yield calculations.
- Giving generic advantages of a business form with no link to the case.
- Writing "limited liability" without saying that the shareholder's loss is limited to the amount invested.
- Treating a plc as public sector.
- Listing stakeholders instead of explaining how the decision changes things for each.
- Ending an "evaluate" or "justify" answer with a choice but no reason or condition.
