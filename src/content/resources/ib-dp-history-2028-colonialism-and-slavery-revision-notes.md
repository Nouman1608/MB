**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. The topic is broad, so notes concentrate on what you can use as evidence in an evaluative essay. The [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-colonialism-and-slavery/) gives the explanation, and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-colonialism-and-slavery-practice/) let you test it.

## Paper 3 reminder

Two 15-mark essays, each from a different regional study. Questions ask you to weigh an argument: understand it, evaluate perspectives on it, and reach a substantiated judgement.

## Key terms

- **Encomienda**: grant of Indigenous tribute and labour to a Spanish settler.
- **Mita**: colonial adaptation of the Inca labour draft, especially at Potosí.
- **Yanaconaje**: dependent service tied to a landholder or mine.
- **Repartimiento**: rotating forced labour draft, notably in Mexico.
- **Columbian Exchange**: movement of plants, animals, people and diseases between hemispheres.
- **Asiento**: contract to supply enslaved people to Spanish colonies.
- **Code Noir**: French law (1685) regulating slavery.
- **Maroon / quilombo**: community of escaped enslaved people.
- **Casta**: term for mixed-descent groups in Spanish America.
- **Abolitionism**: movement to end the slave trade and slavery.

## Initial impact of exploration

- Conquest causes: local allies, civil war in the Inca state, tribute resentment, disease, weapons and horses.
- Resources: silver (Potosí, Zacatecas), gold, sugar, tobacco, fur.
- Columbian Exchange: maize, potatoes, tobacco and cacao out; wheat, sugar cane, horses, cattle, pigs and disease in.
- Population collapse: large, regionally uneven. Weigh disease, violence, forced labour and famine.

## Treatment of Indigenous peoples

- Encomienda, mita, yanaconaje, repartimiento.
- Reform: Laws of Burgos, New Laws, Las Casas. Enforcement was weak.
- Society: peninsulares, criollos, castas, Indigenous people, Africans.
- Missions and conversion: Franciscans, Dominicans, Jesuits. Assimilation was partial.
- Women: forced labour (obrajes), sexual exploitation, defence of land through courts, cultural survival.

## Reasons for transatlantic slavery

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic | Sugar and other crops needed a large controllable workforce; Indigenous populations had fallen; indentured servants were temporary |
| Ideas | Religious justifications and growing racial theory |
| Political | Monopolies, asiento, laws making slavery hereditary, naval protection |
| Powers | Portugal (Brazil), Spain (asiento buyer), Netherlands (Brazil, Suriname), France (Saint-Domingue), Britain (largest 18th-century carrier) |

## Impact of the system

- Wealth for merchants, ports and planters; debate over its role in industrialisation.
- Planters held political power.
- African influence on language, religion, music and food (Candomblé, Vodou, Obeah).
- Brutal conditions on sugar plantations, high mortality.
- Women: fieldwork, domestic work, sexual violence; status followed the mother in many colonies.

## Resistance

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Day-to-day | Slowdowns, tool breaking, feigned illness, short flight |
| Cultural | Religion, language, music, family networks |
| Rebellion | Tacky's War (1760), Berbice (1763), Haiti (from 1791), Demerara (1823) |
| Escape | Maroons in Jamaica and Suriname; Palmares in Brazil (destroyed 1694) |

## Early opposition

- Ideas: Quaker belief, evangelical Christianity, Enlightenment natural rights.
- People: Quakers, Clarkson, Wilberforce, Granville Sharp.
- Formerly enslaved voices: Equiano (1789).
- Technology: printing, pamphlets, the *Brookes* plan (1788).
- The enslaved themselves: Haiti and other revolts changed the debate.

## Debates

- Disease versus conquest in Indigenous decline: most say both.
- Economics versus racism as the cause of slavery: Williams argued that economic need came first and racism followed.
- Abolition: Williams (economic decline) versus Drescher (still profitable, moral and popular campaign, plus resistance).
- Credit given to white reformers versus enslaved people.

## Essay skeleton

1. State the argument and your overall line in one sentence.
2. Use at least two colonial powers or regions as evidence.
3. Rank factors, do not list them.
4. Include a named view (for example, Williams) and test it against evidence.
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent did enslaved people themselves bring about the end of slavery in the Americas to 1830?"

1. Line: their actions were essential in some places (Haiti) and a strong pressure everywhere, but abolitionist politics and economics also mattered.
2. Evidence for: day-to-day resistance, maroon communities, Tacky's War, Demerara, the Haitian Revolution and its effect on planters' fear.
3. Other factors: abolitionist campaigns, Quakers and evangelicals, the 1807 Act, British naval action, changing economic arguments.
4. Perspective: Williams (economic change), Drescher (campaigning and politics), and writers who stress the agency of the enslaved.
5. Judgement: resistance shaped the timing and the terms, and in Haiti it was decisive, while elsewhere it worked with political action in Europe.

## Common mistakes

- Treating all colonial systems as one.
- Ignoring Indigenous experience or ignoring African experience.
- Moving to the US Civil War, which is outside this study.
- Stating cruelty without analysis.

## Self-test

1. Name two reasons why Cortés defeated the Aztec state.
2. What was the mita?
3. Give two reasons why enslaved Africans replaced Indigenous labour in the Caribbean.
4. Name one rebellion and say what it achieved.
5. Name two groups or individuals in early abolitionism.
6. What did Eric Williams argue about the end of slavery?

**Answers:** 1. Any two: local allies such as Tlaxcala, disease, horses and weapons, Aztec tribute resentment. 2. A colonial draft of Indigenous labour, adapted from Inca practice, used especially in mining. 3. Indigenous population decline; sugar needed a large workforce; indentured labour was temporary and limited. 4. For example, Haiti (from 1791) led to an independent state in 1804. 5. Any two, for example Quakers, Thomas Clarkson, William Wilberforce, Olaudah Equiano. 6. That slavery became less profitable, so abolition followed economic change (a view contested by others).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I cover so much material?**
Build five or six evidence sets, such as the conquest, silver and labour, sugar, Haiti and British abolitionism, and use them flexibly.

**Which colonial power should I pick?**
Know at least two. Spain and Britain, or Portugal and France, give a useful contrast.
