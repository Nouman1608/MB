## Questions

**1.** Terms for lenses.

**(a)** State what a convex lens does to parallel rays of light. **[1]**
**(b)** State what a concave lens does to parallel rays of light. **[1]**
**(c)** Define the focal length of a lens. **[1]**

**2.** A convex lens has a focal length of 10 cm. For each object distance, describe the image, saying whether it is real or virtual, upright or inverted, and larger or smaller than the object.

**(a)** The object is 30 cm from the lens. **[2]**
**(b)** The object is 15 cm from the lens. **[2]**
**(c)** The object is 6 cm from the lens. **[2]**

**3.** Magnification.

**(a)** An image is 4.5 cm tall and the object is 1.5 cm tall. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(b)** A lens has a magnification of 0.25 and the object is 6.0 mm tall. Calculate the height of the image. **[2]**
**(c)** A microscope image of an insect hair is 18 mm long and the magnification is 12. Calculate the real length of the hair. **[2]**

**4.** A student draws a ray diagram on graph paper with 1 cm squares. The convex lens is a vertical line through x = 0 and the axis is y = 0. The focal length is 2 cm. The object is an arrow 2.0 cm tall with its foot at x = −6 cm on the axis.

**(a)** Describe the two rays you would draw from the tip of the arrow and where each goes. **[3]**
**(b)** State the distance of the image from the lens and its height. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the nature of the image. **[1]**

**5.** A convex lens of focal length 6.0 cm is used as a magnifying glass to look at a stamp detail 1.5 mm tall. The detail is 4.0 cm from the lens.

**(a)** Explain why the lens acts as a magnifying glass at this distance. **[1]**
**(b)** A ray diagram drawn on graph paper shows the image 12 cm from the lens, on the same side as the stamp. Describe the image. **[3]**
**(c)** The image is 4.5 mm tall. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**

**6.** A concave lens has a focal length of 3.0 cm. An object 4.5 cm tall is placed 6.0 cm from it.

**(a)** Describe the image. **[3]**
**(b)** A ray diagram shows that the image is 1.5 cm tall. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**

**7.** State the function of each part of the eye.

**(a)** Cornea **[1]**
**(b)** Iris **[1]**
**(c)** Lens **[1]**
**(d)** Ciliary muscle **[1]**
**(e)** Suspensory ligaments **[1]**
**(f)** Retina **[1]**

**8.** A person looks up from a book to a tree on a distant hill. Explain how the eye changes so that the tree is in focus. **[5]**

**9.** A person has a near point of 80 cm.

**(a)** State whether a book held 30 cm from the eye can be seen clearly without glasses. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two possible causes of this defect. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain where the image forms without correction, and how a lens corrects the problem. **[3]**

**10.** A person's near point is 25 cm and the far point is 2.0 m.

**(a)** State the usual position of the far point for a typical eye. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one possible cause of this person's defect. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why distant objects look blurred and how a lens corrects this. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate this person's range of vision in centimetres. **[1]**

**11.** Lasers.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a laser. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two uses of lasers other than in the eye. **[2]**
**(c)** State how lasers can be used in eye surgery. **[1]**

**12.** A camera and an eye both form images.

**(a)** Name the part of the eye that is equivalent to the CCD sensor in a camera. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare how the camera and the eye change focus when looking from a distant object to a near object. **[4]**
**(c)** A person 1.8 m tall is photographed. The image on the sensor is 12 mm tall. Calculate the magnification, giving your answer to 2 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Rays are brought together (converge) at the principal focus [1].
**(b)** Rays spread out (diverge), as if they came from the principal focus [1].
**(c)** The length from the lens to the point where parallel rays meet (convex) or appear to start (concave) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Meet at a point" scores only if you say the rays were parallel to the axis, so keep that condition in your wording.

**2. (a)** The object is further than twice the focal length (20 cm). The image is real and inverted [1] and smaller [1]. (It is 15 cm from the lens and half the height.)
**(b)** The object is between f and 2f. The image is real and inverted [1] and larger [1]. (It is 30 cm from the lens.)
**(c)** The object is inside the principal focus. The image is virtual and upright [1] and larger [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give all three features each time. An answer such as "real" without orientation or size usually leaves marks unclaimed.

**3. (a)** Magnification = 4.5 ÷ 1.5 [1] = **3.0** [1].
**(b)** Image height = 0.25 × 6.0 [1] = **1.5 mm** [1].
**(c)** Object height = 18 ÷ 12 [1] = **1.5 mm** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the rearranged equation or the substitution gives the examiner something to credit if your final number slips. Magnification is a ratio, so do not write a unit for it.

**4. (a)** Ray 1 goes from the tip parallel to the axis to the lens, then through the principal focus at (2, 0) on the far side [1]. Ray 2 goes from the tip through the centre of the lens at (0, 0) without bending [1]. The image tip is where they cross [1].
**(b)** Ray 1 runs from (0, 2) through (2, 0), and ray 2 runs from (−6, 2) through (0, 0). They cross at (3, −1) [1]. The image is **3 cm from the lens and 1.0 cm tall** [1].
**(c)** Real, inverted and smaller (height 1.0 cm against 2.0 cm) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* On graph paper, draw with a ruler and extend the rays until they cross clearly. Read values from the squares, and give the position and the height as separate quantities.

**5. (a)** The stamp is closer to the lens than the principal focus (4.0 cm is less than 6.0 cm) [1].
**(b)** The image is virtual [1], upright [1] and larger than the object [1].
**(c)** Magnification = 4.5 ÷ 1.5 [1] = **3.0** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Both heights are in millimetres here, so no conversion is needed. Using the lens-to-image distance instead of the heights is a common wrong route.

**6. (a)** The image is virtual [1], upright [1] and smaller than the object [1]. (It is on the same side as the object.)
**(b)** Magnification = 1.5 ÷ 4.5 [1] = **0.33** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Whatever the object distance, a concave lens gives the same type of image. Saying "real" for any case suggests the rays have been drawn crossing.

**7. (a)** Refracts light entering the eye [1].
**(b)** Controls the size of the pupil, and so how much light enters [1].
**(c)** Refracts the light and changes shape to focus it on the retina [1].
**(d)** Changes the shape of the lens [1].
**(e)** Connect the ciliary muscle to the lens and pull the lens thinner when the muscle relaxes [1].
**(f)** Contains light-sensitive cells where the image is focused [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a function with a verb, such as "refracts" or "controls". Saying where a part is does not describe what it does.

**8.** The ciliary muscle relaxes [1]. The suspensory ligaments are pulled tight [1]. The lens is pulled thinner and less curved [1]. It refracts light less, so its focal length is longer [1]. Light from the distant tree is then focused on the retina [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation of a change gains credit for a chain of linked statements. List muscle, ligaments, lens shape and effect in order.

**9. (a)** No, it cannot be seen clearly, because 30 cm is closer than the near point of 80 cm [1].
**(b)** Any two from: the eyeball is too short [1]; the eye lens is unable to focus a sharp image on the retina [1].
**(c)** Without correction the image of a near object forms behind the retina [1]. A convex lens is used [1]. It converges the rays slightly before they enter the eye, so the eye can focus them on the retina [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), two different causes are needed. Two wordings of one cause earn one mark at most.

**10. (a)** Infinity [1].
**(b)** The eyeball is too long, or the eye lens is unable to focus a sharp image on the retina [1].
**(c)** Light from distant objects focuses in front of the retina [1]. A concave lens is used [1]. It spreads the rays slightly before they enter the eye so that the image falls on the retina [1].
**(d)** Range = 200 − 25 [1] = **175 cm**. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The far point was given in metres but the answer is required in centimetres, so convert before subtracting. A bare number without a unit is risky.

**11. (a)** A concentrated source of light [1].
**(b)** Any two from cutting, cauterising and burning [1] [1].
**(c)** To correct visual defects [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explaining how a laser produces light earns nothing here. Name the uses.

**12. (a)** The retina [1].
**(b)** The camera changes the distance between the lens and the sensor [1], moving the lens further away from the sensor for a nearer object [1]. The eye changes the shape of its lens [1], making it thicker and more curved for the nearer object [1].
**(c)** Convert: 12 mm = 0.012 m [1]. Magnification = 0.012 ÷ 1.8 [1] = 0.00667, so **6.7 × 10⁻³** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the two heights in the same unit before dividing. An answer of 6.7 comes from a unit mismatch, so check that the size is sensible.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an image with fewer than three features.
- Treating every convex-lens image as real. Inside the principal focus it is virtual.
- Writing that the lens in the eye moves instead of changing shape.
- Mixing up which lens corrects long sight (convex) and short sight (concave).
- Describing the pupil as the part that focuses light, rather than as an opening whose size the iris changes.
- Forgetting to convert both heights to the same unit before finding magnification.
- Drawing ray 2 with a bend at the lens, instead of straight through the centre.
