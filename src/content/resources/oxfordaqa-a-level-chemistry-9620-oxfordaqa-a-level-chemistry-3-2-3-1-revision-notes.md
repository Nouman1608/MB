These notes condense **3.2.3.1 Trends in properties** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: electronegativity and boiling point trends, oxidising ability of the halogens (displacement), reducing ability of the halide ions (solid sodium halides with concentrated sulfuric acid), and the acidified silver nitrate test with ammonia. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1.

Full equations and worked examples are in the [trends in properties study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-3-1/). ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electronegativity | The power of an atom to attract the pair of electrons in a covalent bond |
| Oxidising agent | Electron acceptor; it is reduced |
| Reducing agent | Electron donor; it is oxidised |
| Displacement | A more reactive halogen oxidises the halide ion of a less reactive one |
| Misty fumes | Hydrogen halide gas forming droplets with moisture in air |
| Complex ion | A central metal ion with ligands, such as [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ |

## Trends at a glance (down the group, F → I)

| Property | Trend | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Electronegativity | decreases | more shells, more shielding, bonding pair further from nucleus |
| Boiling point | increases | more electrons, stronger van der Waals forces between molecules |
| Oxidising ability (X₂) | decreases | electron gained into a shell further out and more shielded |
| Reducing ability (X⁻) | increases | electron lost from a shell further out and more shielded |
| Solubility of AgX in NH₃ | decreases | AgCl > AgBr > AgI |

## Displacement

- Cl₂ oxidises Br⁻ and I⁻. Br₂ oxidises I⁻ only. I₂ oxidises neither.
- Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (solution turns orange-yellow)
- Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂ (brown)
- Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂ (brown)
- In cyclohexane: Br₂ orange, I₂ violet.

## Sodium halides with concentrated sulfuric acid

Step 1 for all: NaX + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HX (misty fumes).

| Halide | Redox? | Sulfur product (oxidation state) | Halogen product | Key observations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NaF, NaCl | no | none (stays +6) | none | misty fumes only |
| NaBr | yes | SO₂ (+4) | Br₂ | misty fumes, orange-brown vapour, choking gas |
| NaI | yes | SO₂ (+4), S (0), H₂S (−2) | I₂ | misty fumes, purple vapour / black solid, yellow solid, bad-egg smell |

Equations to know:
- 2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O
- 6HI + H₂SO₄ → 3I₂ + S + 4H₂O
- 8HI + H₂SO₄ → 4I₂ + H₂S + 4H₂O

**Role of H₂SO₄**: acid in step 1; oxidising agent in the later steps.

## Silver nitrate test

1. Add dilute nitric acid (removes carbonate and hydroxide ions that would also precipitate with Ag⁺).
2. Add silver nitrate solution: Ag⁺ + X⁻ → AgX(s).
3. Add ammonia to confirm.

| Ion | Precipitate | Dilute NH₃ | Concentrated NH₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| F⁻ | none | not applicable | not applicable |
| Cl⁻ | white | dissolves | dissolves |
| Br⁻ | cream | stays | dissolves |
| I⁻ | yellow | stays | stays |

AgCl(s) + 2NH₃(aq) → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

Never acidify with hydrochloric acid: it adds Cl⁻.

## Half-equations to know

| Change | Half-equation |
|---|---|
| Halogen reduced | X₂ + 2e⁻ → 2X⁻ |
| Halide oxidised | 2X⁻ → X₂ + 2e⁻ |
| Sulfuric acid to SO₂ | H₂SO₄ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → SO₂ + 2H₂O |
| Sulfuric acid to S | H₂SO₄ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻ → S + 4H₂O |
| Sulfuric acid to H₂S | H₂SO₄ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ → H₂S + 4H₂O |

To build an overall equation, multiply the halide half-equation so the electrons match, then add. For example, SO₂ needs 2e⁻, supplied by 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻.

## Identifying an unknown halide in two tests

1. Acidified AgNO₃: no precipitate means F⁻ (or no halide); white, cream or yellow suggests Cl⁻, Br⁻ or I⁻.
2. Ammonia: dissolves in dilute = Cl⁻; dissolves only in concentrated = Br⁻; insoluble in both = I⁻.

A displacement test is a useful cross-check: chlorine water turns a bromide solution orange and an iodide solution brown.

## Exam traps

- Explaining boiling points with X–X bond strength.
- Saying fluorine is least electronegative.
- Including Na⁺ or K⁺ in displacement ionic equations.
- Calling HX fumes "white smoke".
- Giving SO₂ as the only product for NaI.
- Describing iodine as "orange" in cyclohexane.
- Forgetting the reason for each reagent in the silver nitrate test.

## Quick self-test

1. State and explain the trend in electronegativity down Group 7.
2. Explain why bromine is a liquid at room temperature but chlorine is a gas.
3. Write the ionic equation for chlorine reacting with iodide ions.
4. Which halide ions can bromine oxidise?
5. What colour is iodine in cyclohexane?
6. What forms when solid sodium chloride reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid?
7. Which sodium halide produces hydrogen sulfide with concentrated sulfuric acid, and what is the oxidation state of sulfur in H₂S?
8. Write the equation for HBr reacting with sulfuric acid.
9. Why is nitric acid added before silver nitrate?
10. What colour is silver bromide, and how does it behave in ammonia?
11. Write an equation for silver chloride dissolving in ammonia.
12. Why does fluoride give no precipitate with silver nitrate?

### Answers

1. **Decreases**: atoms have more shells and more shielding, so the bonding pair is further from the nucleus and attracted less strongly.
2. Br₂ has more electrons than Cl₂, so van der Waals forces between Br₂ molecules are stronger and more energy is needed to separate them.
3. **Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂**
4. Only **iodide** ions.
5. **Violet** (purple).
6. **NaHSO₄ and HCl** (misty fumes); no redox occurs.
7. **Sodium iodide**; sulfur is **−2**.
8. **2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O**
9. To remove carbonate (and hydroxide) ions, which would otherwise form a precipitate with silver ions.
10. **Cream**; insoluble in dilute ammonia, soluble in concentrated ammonia.
11. **AgCl + 2NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + Cl⁻**
12. Silver fluoride is soluble in water.
