## Section A

**1.** Explain what is meant by the statement that electromagnetic radiation has a particulate nature. **[2]**

**2.** Define the electronvolt. **[1]**

**3.** Show that the energy of a photon of wavelength 430 nm is about 2.9 eV. **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the momentum of the photon in Question 3. **[1]**

**5.** A photon has an energy of 3.2 eV. Calculate its energy in joules and its wavelength. **[3]**

**6.** Which quantity is the same for all photons travelling in a vacuum?

A: energy

B: frequency

C: momentum

D: speed

## Section B

**7.** A lamp emits 0.50 W of light, all of wavelength 590 nm, equally in all directions.

**(a)** Calculate the energy of one photon. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of photons emitted per second. **[2]**

**(c)** A detector of area 1.0 cm² faces the lamp at a distance of 2.0 m. Calculate the number of photons reaching the detector per second. **[3]**

**8.** A gamma-ray photon has an energy of 2.0 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate its energy in joules. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its momentum. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its frequency. **[1]**

**9.** A laser beam of power 2.0 W falls at right angles on a surface.

**(a)** The surface absorbs the beam completely. Explain why the beam exerts a force on it, and calculate the force. **[3]**

**(b)** The surface is replaced by a mirror that reflects the beam straight back. Explain why the force doubles. **[2]**

**10.** A red laser (650 nm) and a blue laser (450 nm) each emit a beam of power 5.0 mW.

**(a)** Determine which laser emits more photons per second, and by what factor. **[3]**

**(b)** State the ratio of the momentum of one blue photon to that of one red photon. **[1]**

**11.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 2500 V.

**(a)** State the energy it gains in eV and calculate this energy in J. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of a photon that has the same energy. **[2]**

**12.** An infrared photon has an energy of 0.10 eV.

**(a)** Calculate its wavelength. **[2]**

**(b)** A student says that a very bright infrared lamp must emit photons with more energy than a dim ultraviolet lamp. Explain whether the student is correct. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Electromagnetic energy is emitted, transferred and absorbed in discrete packets (quanta) called photons [1], each of energy hf, rather than continuously [1].

**2.** The energy transferred when an electron moves through a potential difference of one volt [1].

**3.** E = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 430 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = 4.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]; ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.89 eV, about 2.9 eV [1].

**4.** p = E/c = 4.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 3.00 × 10⁸ = 1.5 × 10⁻²⁷ kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**5.** E = 3.2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 5.12 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]; λ = hc/E = 1.989 × 10⁻²⁵ ÷ 5.12 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = 3.9 × 10⁻⁷ m [1].

**6.** D [1].

**7. (a)** E = hc/λ = 1.989 × 10⁻²⁵ ÷ 590 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = 3.37 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1].

**(b)** N = P/E = 0.50 ÷ 3.37 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = 1.5 × 10¹⁸ s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** Intensity at 2.0 m = 0.50 ÷ (4π × 2.0²) = 9.9 × 10⁻³ W m⁻² [1]; power on detector = 9.9 × 10⁻³ × 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 9.9 × 10⁻⁷ W [1]; photons per second = 9.9 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 3.37 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.0 × 10¹² s⁻¹ [1].

**8. (a)** E = 2.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.2 × 10⁻¹³ J [1].

**(b)** p = E/c = 1.1 × 10⁻²¹ kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** f = E/h = 4.8 × 10²⁰ Hz [1].

**9. (a)** Photons carry momentum; when they are absorbed their momentum is transferred to the surface [1]. Force = rate of change of momentum = P/c [1] = 2.0 ÷ 3.00 × 10⁸ = 6.7 × 10⁻⁹ N [1].

**(b)** Each reflected photon's momentum is reversed, from +p to −p [1], so the change of momentum per photon is 2p and the rate of change of momentum (the force) is doubled [1].

**10. (a)** Number per second = P/E = Pλ/(hc), so for the same power N ∝ λ [1]; the red laser emits more photons per second [1], by a factor 650/450 = 1.4 [1].

**(b)** p = h/λ, so blue : red = 650/450 = 1.4 [1].

**11. (a)** 2500 eV [1]; 2500 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1].

**(b)** λ = hc/E = 1.989 × 10⁻²⁵ ÷ 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ [1] = 5.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m [1].

**12. (a)** E = 0.10 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.6 × 10⁻²⁰ J [1]; λ = hc/E = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ m [1].

**(b)** Not correct: the energy of each photon depends only on its frequency (E = hf) [1]; ultraviolet has a higher frequency, so each ultraviolet photon has more energy; the bright lamp just emits more photons per second [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wavelength in E = hf, or forgetting c in E = hc/λ.
- Converting between eV and J the wrong way.
- Writing 430 nm as 430 × 10⁻⁶ m.
- Rounding the photon energy too early before dividing a power by it.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 when a beam is reflected.
- Linking intensity, rather than frequency, to the energy of each photon.
