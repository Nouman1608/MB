This guide teaches section 3.1, General properties of waves, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Core statements 1 to 8 cover what waves are, their features, v = fλ, the two types of wave and the three wave behaviours. Supplement statements 9 and 10, on how wavelength and gap size affect diffraction, are examined only on Papers 2 and 4 and are labelled **Extended only**. The [Waves study guide](/resources/igcse-physics-waves/) describes ropes, springs and the ripple tank set-up; this page goes further into the definitions, the calculations and what changes, and what does not, in each wave behaviour.

## Energy, not matter

A wave transfers **energy** from one place to another **without transferring matter**. A cork on a pond bobs up and down as ripples pass under it but ends up where it started; the energy of the wave moves outwards across the pond. A Mexican wave in a stadium is a good picture: the wave travels round, but each person only stands up and sits down.

## Describing a wave

| Feature | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Crest (peak) | highest point of a transverse wave | -- |
| Trough | lowest point | -- |
| Amplitude | maximum displacement from the rest (undisturbed) position: from the middle to a crest, **not** crest to trough | m |
| Wavelength, λ | distance between two neighbouring crests (or any two neighbouring corresponding points) | m |
| Frequency, f | number of waves passing a point (or produced by the source) per second | hertz, Hz |
| Wave speed, v | distance travelled by the wave per second | m/s |
| Wavefront | a line joining points on the same part of a wave, such as all the crests of a ripple | -- |

On a wavefront diagram, the distance between neighbouring wavefronts is one wavelength.

**Counting carefully.** If 10 wavefronts are spread over 18 cm, there are only **9** gaps between them, so λ = 18 ÷ 9 = 2.0 cm. If a source produces 5 complete waves in 2.0 s, f = 5 ÷ 2.0 = 2.5 Hz.

## The wave equation

```
v = f λ        v in m/s,  f in Hz,  λ in m
f = v / λ      λ = v / f
```

**Worked example 1 (ripple tank).** Ten wavefronts span 18 cm on the screen below a ripple tank, and the dipper vibrates at 12 Hz. Find the wave speed.

```
λ = 18 cm / 9 = 2.0 cm = 0.020 m
v = f λ = 12 × 0.020 = 0.24 m/s
```

**Worked example 2 (a range of waves).**

```
sound, v = 340 m/s, f = 680 Hz:        λ = 340 / 680 = 0.50 m
radio, v = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, λ = 3.0 m:   f = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 3.0 = 1.0 × 10⁸ Hz
water, f = 0.25 Hz, λ = 20 m:          v = 0.25 × 20 = 5.0 m/s
```

Convert prefixes first: 1 kHz = 1000 Hz, 1 MHz = 10⁶ Hz, 1 cm = 0.01 m.

## Transverse and longitudinal waves

| | Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of vibration | at right angles to the direction the wave travels (propagation) | parallel to the direction of propagation |
| Modelled as this type | electromagnetic radiation, water waves, seismic S-waves (secondary) | sound waves, seismic P-waves (primary) |
| Shown on a spring by | moving the end from side to side | pushing and pulling the end along the spring |

A useful check: in a transverse wave the particles move across the wave's path; in a longitudinal wave they move back and forth along it.

## Reflection, refraction and diffraction

### Reflection at a plane surface

Waves bounce off a flat barrier. In a ripple tank, straight wavefronts hitting a straight barrier at an angle leave at the same angle on the other side of the normal. **Speed, wavelength and frequency are all unchanged**; only the direction changes.

### Refraction due to a change of speed

When a wave enters a region where it travels at a **different speed**, it refracts. In a ripple tank, a glass or plastic plate makes part of the water **shallower**; waves travel **more slowly** in shallow water.

- The **frequency does not change**: it is set by the source.
- Because v = fλ and f is fixed, a lower speed means a **shorter wavelength**: the wavefronts are closer together over the shallow region.
- If the wavefronts meet the boundary at an angle, the change of speed also changes their **direction**. If they meet it head-on, they slow down and bunch up but do not change direction.

**Worked example 3.** Waves of frequency 12 Hz travel at 0.24 m/s in deep water and at 0.18 m/s in shallow water. Find the wavelength in each.

```
deep:     λ = 0.24 / 12 = 0.020 m = 2.0 cm
shallow:  λ = 0.18 / 12 = 0.015 m = 1.5 cm
```

### Diffraction through a gap and at an edge

**Diffraction** is the spreading of waves as they pass through a gap or past the edge of an obstacle. In the ripple tank, straight waves passing through a gap between two barriers spread out on the far side; waves passing the end of a single barrier curve round into the region behind it. **Wavelength, frequency and speed are unchanged** by diffraction.

### How wavelength and gap size matter (Extended only)

- **Gap about the same size as the wavelength:** the waves spread out strongly, emerging as nearly semicircular wavefronts.
- **Gap much wider than the wavelength:** the waves pass through mostly straight, with only slight spreading at the edges.
- For a given gap, a **longer wavelength** diffracts **more**; for a given wavelength, a **narrower gap** gives **more** diffraction.
- **At an edge:** longer wavelengths bend further round the edge into the "shadow" region than shorter wavelengths do.

Consequences: you can hear someone talking round the corner of a doorway, because sound wavelengths (around a metre) are comparable to the doorway, but you cannot see them, because light wavelengths are far smaller. Long-wavelength radio waves diffract round hills better than short-wavelength ones.

When drawing diffracted wavefronts, keep the **spacing the same** before and after the gap: diffraction does not change the wavelength.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring amplitude from crest to trough (that is twice the amplitude).
- Counting wavefronts instead of the gaps between them when finding λ.
- Saying the frequency changes during refraction.
- Drawing wavefronts closer together (or further apart) after diffraction.
- Saying sound is transverse, or light is longitudinal.
- Leaving cm or kHz unconverted in v = fλ.
- *(Extended)* Saying a wider gap gives more diffraction.

## Exam technique: command words

- **State** the meaning of a feature: use the table definitions with "neighbouring" for wavelength and "from the rest position" for amplitude.
- **Describe** a ripple tank demonstration: what you set up (plane barrier, glass plate, gap, edge), what you see, and what stays the same.
- **Calculate**: v = fλ with units in m, Hz and m/s.
- **Draw** wavefronts: use a ruler; keep spacing correct (equal for reflection and diffraction; closer in the slower region for refraction).
- **Explain** *(Extended)*: compare the gap width with the wavelength in your answer.
