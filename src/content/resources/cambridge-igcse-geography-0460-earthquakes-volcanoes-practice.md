These questions cover topic 2.1 Earthquakes and volcanoes in Theme 2 (The natural environment) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. The earthquakes in Question 7 are fictional and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Earthquakes and Volcanoes study guide](/resources/igcse-geography-earthquakes-and-volcanoes/) and the [Earthquakes and Volcanoes revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-earthquakes-volcanoes-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the terms "focus" and "epicentre" of an earthquake. **[2]**

**2.** State two differences between a strato-volcano (composite cone) and a shield volcano. **[2]**

**3.** Name the type of plate boundary where two plates move apart, and give one landform found there. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by a "magma chamber" and a "vent". **[2]**

**5.** Give two opportunities that volcanoes offer to people. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why earthquakes occur at conservative plate boundaries. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows information about four fictional earthquakes.

| Earthquake | Magnitude | Depth of focus (km) | Deaths | Income level of country |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 7.8 | 15 | 8,900 | Low |
| B | 7.9 | 20 | 65 | High |
| C | 6.1 | 10 | 2,300 | Low |
| D | 6.3 | 600 | 0 | Middle |

**(a)** Identify the earthquake with the deepest focus. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare the deaths caused by earthquakes A and B. **[2]**
**(c)** Earthquakes C and D had similar magnitudes. Suggest reasons why C caused far more deaths than D. **[3]**

**8.** Earthquake magnitude is measured on a logarithmic scale.

**(a)** State how many times greater the ground shaking (amplitude) of a magnitude 7 earthquake is than that of a magnitude 5 earthquake. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how buildings can be designed to reduce damage from earthquakes. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why many people continue to live close to active volcanoes. **[4]**

**10.** Describe and explain the global distribution of volcanoes. **[5]**

**11.** For a named earthquake you have studied, describe its causes and its effects on people and the environment. **[7]**

**12.** "The impacts of an earthquake depend more on the level of development of a country than on the strength of the earthquake." To what extent do you agree? Use examples. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Focus: the point underground where the earthquake starts, where rocks break or slip [1]. Epicentre: the point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The epicentre is not "where the earthquake is strongest" but the point directly above the focus.

**2.** Strato-volcanoes have steep sides, whereas shield volcanoes have gentle slopes and a wide base [1]. Strato-volcanoes erupt viscous lava explosively and are built of layers of ash and lava, whereas shield volcanoes erupt runny lava gently and are built mainly of lava flows [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Constructive (divergent) boundary [1]. A mid-ocean ridge, a rift valley or a shield volcano [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Magma chamber: a store of molten rock (magma) beneath the volcano [1]. Vent: the opening through which magma reaches the surface [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Fertile soils from weathered volcanic material, good for farming [1]. Tourism, which brings jobs and income [1]. Also credit geothermal energy or minerals. **[2]**

**6.** At a conservative boundary two plates slide past each other [1]. Friction makes them stick, so pressure builds up over time [1]. When the pressure is released suddenly, the plates jerk past each other and the energy is released as seismic waves, causing an earthquake [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** D [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Earthquake A caused 8,900 deaths, whereas B caused only 65, so A caused about 137 times as many deaths [1], even though B had a slightly higher magnitude (7.9 compared with 7.8) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** C had a very shallow focus (10 km), so strong shaking reached the surface, whereas D's focus was 600 km deep, so much energy was lost before reaching the surface [1]. C occurred in a low-income country, where buildings are less likely to be earthquake-resistant [1], and emergency services and healthcare may be less able to rescue and treat injured people [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The best answers use evidence from more than one column of the table, here both depth and income level.

**8. (a)** 100 times (10 × 10) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Deep foundations, or rubber or spring shock absorbers in the foundations, absorb the movement of the ground [1]. A steel frame or cross-bracing lets the building flex without collapsing [1]. Lightweight roofs and shatterproof glass reduce injuries from falling material [1]. Also credit counterweights at the top of tall buildings. **[3]**

**9.** Volcanic soils are very fertile, so farmers can grow good crops [1]. Volcanoes attract tourists, creating jobs in hotels and guiding [1]. Geothermal energy can be used to generate electricity and heat homes [1]. Many people have lived there for generations, cannot afford to move, or believe an eruption is unlikely in their lifetime [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Description: most volcanoes are found in long, narrow belts [1], especially around the edge of the Pacific Ocean (the Pacific Ring of Fire) [1], and along the middle of the Atlantic Ocean, for example in Iceland [1]. Explanation: most occur along plate boundaries, at destructive boundaries where an oceanic plate is subducted and melts, and at constructive boundaries where magma rises as plates move apart [1]. A few, such as those in Hawaii, occur away from boundaries over hot spots [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe" marks need named oceans or regions; "near plate boundaries" is explanation, not description.

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content:

Named earthquake with location and date [1]. Cause linked to the plate boundary type and movement, such as subduction of one named plate beneath another [1]. Magnitude or depth of focus [1]. Primary effect on people, such as collapsed buildings and deaths [1]. Secondary effect, such as a tsunami, fires or landslides [1]. Effect on the environment, such as coastal flooding, landslides or contamination [1]. Place-specific detail that could only apply to that earthquake [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that could describe any earthquake are capped; one or two accurate figures or place names lift an answer to the top level.

**12.** Indicative content:

Agree: high-income countries can afford earthquake-resistant buildings, so fewer collapse [1]. Agree: they have early warning systems, drills and well-equipped emergency services [1]. Agree: low-income countries have more people living in poorly built housing on unsafe land, so death tolls are often higher [1]. Disagree: very large earthquakes can overwhelm defences, as the 2011 tsunami in Japan showed [1]. Disagree: depth of focus affects shaking, so a deep earthquake may cause little damage anywhere [1]. Disagree: distance from the epicentre, population density and time of day matter [1]. Use of named examples to support both sides [1]. Judgement: level of development has the biggest effect on deaths, but the strength and depth of the earthquake decide how severe the shaking is [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the focus and the epicentre.
- Mixing up constructive and destructive boundaries.
- Describing distribution with reasons instead of named places and patterns.
- Data answers that quote one column of a table and ignore the others.
- Case studies with no name, date or figures.
