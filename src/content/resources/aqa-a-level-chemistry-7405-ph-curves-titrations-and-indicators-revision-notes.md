These notes condense section **3.1.12.5 pH curves, titrations and indicators** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. The wider topic is in the [Acids and bases revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Perform **titration calculations** from experimental results.
- **Sketch and explain** the pH curves for all combinations of weak and strong monoprotic acids and bases.
- **Select an appropriate indicator** from a pH curve.
- Describe **Required practical 9**: pH changes when a weak acid reacts with a strong base and a strong acid with a weak base.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| pH curve | Graph of pH against volume of titrant added |
| Equivalence point | Amounts of acid and base are in the ratio of the equation |
| End point | Where the indicator changes colour |
| Vertical section | Region of rapid pH change around equivalence |
| Buffer region | Part of a weak acid or weak base curve where pH changes slowly |
| Half-equivalence | Half the equivalence volume; pH = pKa for a weak acid |
| Indicator | Weak acid HIn whose colour differs from that of In⁻ |

## The four curves at a glance

Flask: 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³; titrant 0.100 mol dm⁻³; equivalence at 25.0 cm³.

| Combination | Start pH | Vertical section | pH at equivalence | Suitable indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong acid + strong base | 1 | about 3.7 to 10.3 | 7 | Methyl orange or phenolphthalein |
| Weak acid + strong base | about 2.9 | about 7 to 10 | about 8.7 | Phenolphthalein |
| Strong acid + weak base | about 11 | about 7 to 4 | about 5.3 | Methyl orange |
| Weak acid + weak base | varies | none | about 7 | None; use a pH meter |

Reasons for the equivalence pH:

- Weak acid + strong base: the anion of the weak acid is a base, so the salt solution is alkaline (CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻).
- Strong acid + weak base: the cation is a weak acid, so the salt solution is acidic (NH₄⁺ ⇌ NH₃ + H⁺).

## Reading a curve

1. Starting pH: strong acid is low (about 1); weak acid is higher (about 3).
2. Buffer region (weak acid or weak base only): a gentle slope.
3. Half-equivalence: pH = pKa of the weak acid (or of the conjugate acid of the weak base).
4. Vertical section: mid-point is the equivalence volume.
5. End: the pH approaches that of the excess titrant.

## Indicators

| Indicator | Colour change | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Methyl orange | Red to yellow | 3.1 to 4.4 |
| Phenolphthalein | Colourless to pink | 8.3 to 10.0 |

**Rule:** the indicator range must fall inside the vertical section.

Reasons for the wrong choices:

- Methyl orange with a weak acid and strong base: it changes colour in the buffer region, long before equivalence.
- Phenolphthalein with a strong acid and weak base: it changes colour after the vertical section has ended, in the buffer region.

## Titration calculations

1. n(titrant) = c × V (V in dm³).
2. Use the equation ratio to find n(sample).
3. c(sample) = n ÷ V(sample).
4. Scale up for any dilution.

Examples:

```
25.0 cm³ NaOH, mean titre 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl
n(HCl) = 0.100 × 0.02240 = 2.24 × 10⁻³ mol
c(NaOH) = 2.24 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0896 mol dm⁻³
```

```
Ba(OH)₂ + 2HCl → BaCl₂ + 2H₂O
18.20 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl with 25.0 cm³ Ba(OH)₂
n(HCl) = 1.82 × 10⁻³ mol, n(Ba(OH)₂) = 9.10 × 10⁻⁴ mol
c = 9.10 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0364 mol dm⁻³
```

If the sample was diluted (for example 25.0 to 250 cm³), multiply the final concentration by the dilution factor (10).

## Ka from half-equivalence

At half-equivalence [HA] = [A⁻], so Ka = [H⁺] and pH = pKa.
If pH = 4.35 at half-equivalence, Ka = 10^(–4.35) = 4.47 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

## Required practical 9

- Calibrate the pH meter with buffer solutions before use.
- Add titrant in 1 cm³ portions, then in drops near the vertical section.
- Stir and record the pH after each addition; plot pH against volume.
- Find the equivalence point from the mid-point of the vertical section.
- Rinse the electrode between measurements.

## Exam traps

- Same vertical section for every titration.
- Choosing an indicator by colour rather than by pH range.
- Using methyl orange for a weak acid with a strong base.
- Starting a weak acid curve at pH 1.
- Forgetting the buffer region.
- Equivalence pH given as 7 for all combinations.
- Not halving moles for Ba(OH)₂ or doubling for H₂SO₄.
- Ignoring a dilution factor.
- Weak–weak: suggesting an indicator.
- Using pH = pKa at any point, not just at half-equivalence.

## Self-test

1. What is the pH at equivalence for strong acid + strong base at 298 K?
2. Give the approximate pH at equivalence for ethanoic acid + sodium hydroxide, and the reason.
3. Which indicator suits strong acid + weak base?
4. Which indicator suits weak acid + strong base?
5. State the rule for choosing an indicator.
6. What is the relationship between pH and pKa at half-equivalence?
7. 25.0 cm³ of NaOH needs 20.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Calculate c(NaOH).
8. Why is there no suitable indicator for weak acid + weak base?
9. Why is the buffer region flat?
10. Why is the starting pH of a weak acid higher than for a strong acid of equal concentration?
11. What does the equivalence point mean?
12. Why add alkali in drops near the vertical section?

### Answers

1. 7.00.
2. About 8.7; ethanoate is a base, so the solution is alkaline.
3. Methyl orange.
4. Phenolphthalein.
5. The indicator's pH range must lie within the vertical section of the curve.
6. They are equal.
7. n(HCl) = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol; c = 2.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0800 mol dm⁻³.
8. There is no vertical section, so no sharp colour change.
9. The flask contains a weak acid and its salt, which resist pH change.
10. A weak acid is only partly dissociated, so [H⁺] is lower.
11. The moles of acid and base are in the ratio in the balanced equation.
12. The pH changes rapidly there, so small additions are needed to locate the mid-point.
