Environmental Systems and Societies (first assessment 2026). The subtopic title is provisional,
because the IB's subject brief does not list Topic 2's subtopics. The
[2.5 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-environmental-systems-and-societies-dp-environmental-systems-and-societies-ib-dp-environmental-systems-and-societies-2-5/)
has the worked transect and disturbance data.

## Condensed recall

**Zonation (space)**
- Change in community along an environmental gradient.
- Gradients: altitude, rocky shore exposure, distance inland on dunes, depth at a lake edge.
- Cause: each species' tolerance covers part of the gradient; competition decides dominance where
  ranges overlap.
- Rocky shores: upper limits set by drying stress; lower limits often by competition or predation.

**Sampling zonation**
- Line transect: species touching the tape at intervals.
- Belt transect: continuous quadrats; most complete.
- Interrupted belt transect: quadrats at intervals; for long gradients.
- Record abiotic factors at every point to explain the pattern.
- Kite diagram: band width ∝ abundance along the transect.

**Succession (time)**
- Primary: bare surface, no soil (new rock, retreating glacier, fresh sand).
- Secondary: soil present after disturbance (fire, flood, abandoned field); faster.
- Sere = whole sequence; seral stage = one step; pioneer → intermediate → climax.

**Psammosere**
- Embryo dunes (sea couch grass) → yellow dunes (marram) → grey dunes (mosses, lichens, flowering
  plants) → dune slacks (wet hollows) → heath or woodland.
- Inland: pH falls (carbonate leached, organic acids added); organic matter rises.

**Hydrosere**
- Open water → sediment build-up → reed swamp → fen or marsh → carr woodland (alder, willow).

**Mechanisms**
- Facilitation: early species improve conditions (organic matter, shelter, nitrogen, water).
- Competitive replacement: later species out-compete pioneers.
- r-strategists early, K-strategists late.

**Climax types**
- Climatic climax: set by climate.
- Edaphic climax: set by local soil or drainage.
- Plagioclimax: held by human activity (deflected or arrested succession).

**Disturbance**
- Natural: fire, storms, floods, wild grazers. Human: clearing, ploughing, livestock, burning.
- Intermediate disturbance hypothesis: diversity peaks at moderate disturbance.
- Old-field succession: annual weeds → perennials → shrubs → woodland.
- Climate change can alter the possible climax or trigger a regime shift.

## Method in steps: describing transect data

```
1. Name the gradient and its direction (e.g. inland from the strandline).
2. For each species: where it starts, peaks and stops, with values.
3. Quote the abiotic trend with values at both ends.
4. Link each species' distribution to an abiotic change.
5. Add competition where species replace each other.
6. If the gradient is also an age gradient, link to succession.
```

Mini example (invented): on a rocky shore, barnacle cover is 0% at 0.5 m above low tide, peaks at
70% at 2.5 m and falls to 5% at 4.0 m, where exposure time is greatest. Barnacles are limited above
by drying and below by competition from seaweeds and predation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zonation | Change in community along a spatial environmental gradient |
| Environmental gradient | Gradual change in an abiotic factor across space |
| Belt transect | Line of adjacent quadrats along a gradient |
| Kite diagram | Chart showing abundance along a transect as symmetrical bands |
| Succession | Change in community at one place over time |
| Sere | Whole sequence of stages in a succession |
| Psammosere | Succession on sand dunes |
| Hydrosere | Succession in fresh water |
| Pioneer species | First colonisers, tolerant of harsh conditions |
| Facilitation | Early species making conditions suitable for later ones |
| Climatic climax | Stable end community set by climate |
| Edaphic climax | Stable end community set by local soil or drainage |
| Plagioclimax | Community held below climax by human activity |
| Intermediate disturbance hypothesis | Diversity is highest at moderate levels of disturbance |

## Exam traps

- **Space versus time.** Zonation is a gradient in space; succession is change over time.
- **Explain each stage.** "Marram binds sand" earns more than "marram is next".
- **Two reasons for zones.** Tolerance and competition, not tolerance alone.
- **Disturbance can raise diversity.** Moderate disturbance often does.
- **Quote values.** Transect answers need numbers at specific distances.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish zonation from succession.
2. Give two abiotic factors that change up a rocky shore.
3. Why does a belt transect give more complete data than a line transect?
4. What does the width of a band on a kite diagram show?
5. Name the dominant plant of yellow dunes and one adaptation.
6. Why does soil pH fall inland across a dune system?
7. List the stages of a hydrosere in order.
8. Define facilitation.
9. Distinguish a climatic climax from an edaphic climax.
10. Give one example of a plagioclimax.
11. State the intermediate disturbance hypothesis.
12. Why is secondary succession faster than primary succession?

### Answers

1. Zonation is change in community across space along a gradient; succession is change in
   community over time at one place.
2. Any two: time exposed to air; drying; temperature range; wave action; salinity changes.
3. It samples every point along the gradient, so no zone or boundary is missed.
4. The abundance of the species at that point along the transect.
5. Marram grass; it grows up through burial, or long roots and rhizomes bind sand.
6. Carbonate from shell fragments is leached out and organic acids from decaying litter accumulate.
7. Open water → reed swamp → fen or marsh → carr woodland (with sediment build-up throughout).
8. Early species change conditions in ways that allow later species to establish.
9. A climatic climax is set by climate; an edaphic climax is set by local soil or drainage.
10. Grazed pasture, a mown lawn or heather moorland burned in rotation.
11. Diversity is highest at moderate disturbance, lower at very frequent or very rare disturbance.
12. Soil, seeds, roots and nutrients are already present.
