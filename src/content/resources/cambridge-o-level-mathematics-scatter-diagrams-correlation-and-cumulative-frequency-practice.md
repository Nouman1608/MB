These questions cover sections 9.5 (Scatter diagrams) and 9.6 (Cumulative frequency diagrams) of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus is not tiered. You need graph paper and a ruler. Readings from hand-drawn curves differ slightly, so answers near the stated values are reasonable. Use the [study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-scatter-diagrams-correlation-and-cumulative-frequency/) first if you need the methods, or the [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-scatter-diagrams-correlation-and-cumulative-frequency-revision-notes/) for a quick recap. ## Questions

**1.** State whether you expect positive, negative or zero correlation between each pair of quantities.
**(a)** The hours of daylight in a day and the amount of electricity used for lighting. **[1]**
**(b)** The age of a child, from 5 to 12 years, and the child's height. **[1]**
**(c)** The telephone numbers of pupils in a class and their test marks. **[1]**

**2.** Eight students recorded their daily screen time, x hours, and the hours of sleep they had that night, y hours.

| x | 1 | 2 | 2.5 | 3 | 4 | 4.5 | 5 | 6.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 9.1 | 8.8 | 8.2 | 8.0 | 7.4 | 7.5 | 6.6 | 6.0 |

**(a)** Plot the eight points on a scatter diagram, with x from 0 to 7 and y from 5 to 10. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**
**(c)** Draw a line of best fit and use it to estimate the sleep for a student with 5.5 hours of screen time. **[3]**

**3.** Three pupils each draw a "line of best fit" on the same scatter diagram. Pupil A draws a line that stops halfway along the data. Pupil B draws a smooth curve through the points. Pupil C draws a ruled line with every point above it. Explain what is wrong with each. **[3]**

**4.** A shop records the price, x pence, of a notebook and the number sold per day, y. A line of best fit is drawn through (20, 70) and (60, 40).
**(a)** Calculate the gradient of the line. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the gradient means in this context. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the line in the form y = mx + c. **[2]**
**(d)** Use the line to estimate the number sold when the price is 50 pence. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why the line should not be used for a price of 120 pence. **[1]**

**5.** The masses of 100 parcels, m kg, are summarised below.

| Mass | 0 < m ≤ 10 | 10 < m ≤ 20 | 20 < m ≤ 30 | 30 < m ≤ 40 | 40 < m ≤ 50 | 50 < m ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 7 | 19 | 31 | 24 | 14 | 5 |

Complete a cumulative frequency table for these data and show how you can check it. **[3]**

**6.** Use your table from question 5. Write down the coordinates of the points you would plot for a cumulative frequency diagram, and describe how you mark and join them. **[3]**

**7.** Draw the cumulative frequency diagram for question 5 and use it to estimate the median, the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[4]**

**8.** Use your diagram from question 7.
**(a)** Estimate the number of parcels heavier than 45 kg. **[2]**
**(b)** Write your answer to part (a) as a percentage of all the parcels. **[1]**
**(c)** The heaviest 10% of parcels need special handling. Estimate the mass above which a parcel needs special handling. **[2]**

**9.** The ages of 100 members of a club are summarised by this cumulative frequency table.

| Age, x years | x ≤ 20 | x ≤ 40 | x ≤ 60 | x ≤ 80 | x ≤ 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 12 | 35 | a | 88 | 100 |

The frequency for 40 < x ≤ 60 is 31.
**(a)** Find a. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the frequencies for 60 < x ≤ 80 and 80 < x ≤ 100. **[2]**
**(c)** State the class that contains the median. **[1]**

**10.** The ages of 100 customers at each of two cafés were recorded.

| Age, x years | x ≤ 20 | x ≤ 30 | x ≤ 40 | x ≤ 50 | x ≤ 60 | x ≤ 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Café A | 0 | 6 | 23 | 58 | 84 | 100 |
| Café B | 0 | 11 | 40 | 78 | 93 | 100 |

**(a)** Draw both cumulative frequency curves on one grid and estimate the median and interquartile range for Café A. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the median and interquartile range for Café B. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare the ages of customers at the two cafés. **[2]**

**11.** A student draws a cumulative frequency diagram for 80 values. She plots each point at the middle of its class, does not plot a starting point, joins the points with a ruler, and reads the lower quartile at height 25.
**(a)** Identify three errors. **[3]**
**(b)** State the three heights she should use for the lower quartile, the median and the upper quartile. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** More daylight means less lighting is needed, so **negative** correlation. [1]
**(b)** Children grow as they get older, so **positive** correlation. [1]
**(c)** There is no link between a telephone number and a mark, so **zero** correlation. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the type and give a reason tied to the context. A bare word is a weaker answer than a short explanation.

**2. (a)** The eight points are plotted correctly, for example (1, 9.1) and (6.5, 6.0). [1] Each is marked clearly, as a small cross. [1]
**(b)** The points fall from left to right: **strong negative correlation**. More screen time goes with less sleep. [1]
**(c)** Mean x = 28.5 ÷ 8 ≈ 3.56 and mean y = 61.6 ÷ 8 = 7.7, so a line through about (3.56, 7.7) is a good guide. [1] Rule one straight line across all the data with points balanced on both sides. [1] Reading at x = 5.5 gives about **6.6 hours** (anything from 6.4 to 6.8 is reasonable). [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show guide lines on your graph, so an estimate has visible working.

**3.** Pupil A: the line does not extend across the full data set. [1] Pupil B: a line of best fit should be a single ruled straight line, not a curve. [1] Pupil C: there should be a roughly even distribution of points either side of the line, but all the points are on one side. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match each fault to a property of a good line. One reason per pupil is enough.

**4. (a)** Gradient = (40 − 70) ÷ (60 − 20). [1] = −30 ÷ 40 = **−0.75**. [1]
**(b)** For each extra 1 pence in price, about 0.75 fewer notebooks are sold per day (3 fewer for every 4 pence). [1]
**(c)** y = −0.75x + c. Using (20, 70): 70 = −15 + c. [1] c = 85, so **y = 85 − 0.75x**. [1]
**(d)** y = 85 − 0.75 × 50 = 47.5, so about **47 or 48 notebooks**. [1]
**(e)** At 120 pence the equation gives 85 − 90 = −5, which is impossible. The price is far outside the data, so the straight-line pattern may not continue. [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) mention both variables, such as "per 1 pence" and "notebooks sold".

**5.** Cumulative frequencies: 7, 7 + 19 = 26, 26 + 31 = 57, 57 + 24 = 81, 81 + 14 = 95, 95 + 5 = 100. [2]

| Mass | m ≤ 10 | m ≤ 20 | m ≤ 30 | m ≤ 40 | m ≤ 50 | m ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 7 | 26 | 57 | 81 | 95 | 100 |

Check: the last value, 100, equals the total number of parcels. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show each running total so that one slip is easy to find.

**6.** Points: (0, 0), (10, 7), (20, 26), (30, 57), (40, 81), (50, 95), (60, 100). [1] Each cumulative frequency goes at its upper class boundary, and (0, 0) is the start point where the first class begins. [1] Mark each point with a small cross and join them with a smooth curve, not with ruled lines. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Crosses at the upper boundary, a start point and a smooth curve are three separate things to check on your own diagram.

**7.** The heights to read are 25, 50 and 75. [1] Median: about **28 kg**. [1] Lower quartile about **19.5 kg** and upper quartile about **37.5 kg**. [1] IQR = 37.5 − 19.5 = about **18 kg**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write each reading down. An IQR is a difference, so give one number with a unit.

**8. (a)** Up from 45 kg to the curve, then across: about 88 parcels weigh 45 kg or less. [1] So 100 − 88 = **12 parcels** weigh more than 45 kg. [1]
**(b)** 12 ÷ 100 × 100 = **12%**. [1]
**(c)** The heaviest 10% lie above the 90th percentile, at height 0.9 × 100 = 90. [1] Reading across at 90 gives about **46 kg**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The curve counts values at or below a point. "Heavier than" needs a subtraction from the total, and "the heaviest 10%" points to the 90th percentile.

**9. (a)** a is the cumulative frequency at x ≤ 60: 35 + 31 = **66**. [1]
**(b)** For 60 < x ≤ 80: 88 − 66 = **22**. [1] For 80 < x ≤ 100: 100 − 88 = **12**. [1]
**(c)** The median is the 50th value. Since 35 < 50 ≤ 66, it lies in the class **40 < x ≤ 60**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The median class is a class, not a number. Quote the whole class interval, such as 40 < x ≤ 60.

**10. (a)** Heights 25, 50 and 75 on Café A's curve give a lower quartile of about 40.6, a median of about **48 years** and an upper quartile of about 56.5. [1] IQR = 56.5 − 40.6 = about **16 years**. [1]
**(b)** For Café B the lower quartile is about 34.8, the median about **43 years** and the upper quartile about 49.2. [1] IQR = 49.2 − 34.8 = about **14 years**. [1]
**(c)** Café A's median is higher (about 48 against 43), so its customers are typically older. [1] Café B's IQR is smaller (about 14 against 16), so its customers' ages are more consistent. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Compare an average and a spread, and say what each means for the customers. Quoting the numbers alone is not a comparison.

**11. (a)** Any three of: plotting at class midpoints instead of upper boundaries; no starting point at the lower boundary of the first class; joining with a ruler instead of a smooth curve; reading the lower quartile at height 25 instead of the correct height. [3]
**(b)** For n = 80: lower quartile at 80 ÷ 4 = **20**, median at 80 ÷ 2 = **40**, upper quartile at 3 × 80 ÷ 4 = **60**. [2]
*Examiner insight:* Quartile heights depend on n, so always work out n ÷ 4 before you read the graph. Never assume 25.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting at class midpoints instead of upper class boundaries.
- Forgetting the starting point (lower boundary of the first class, 0).
- Joining a cumulative frequency graph with ruled segments, or drawing a line of best fit as a curve.
- Using the wrong height for quartiles and percentiles, for example 25 whatever the total.
- Reading a count at or below a value when the question asks for "more than", without subtracting from n.
- Describing correlation without naming its type, or without a reason from the context.
- Using a line of best fit far outside the data and giving no warning.
