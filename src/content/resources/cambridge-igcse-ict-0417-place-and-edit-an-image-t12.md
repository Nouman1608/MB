Topic 12 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 opens with a list of practical image skills under the heading "Place and edit an image". You must be able to "place an image with precision", "resize an image as specified to maintain or adjust the aspect ratio", "crop", "rotate", "reflect (flip) an image horizontally or vertically", "adjust the brightness" and "adjust the contrast", and "group and layer images including grouping and ungrouping, moving to the front or back". The [Images study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-images-study-guide/) gives an overview of each operation. This guide concentrates on doing each one exactly as an instruction specifies, with the measurements and checks that earn the marks.

For quick recall use the [place and edit an image revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-place-and-edit-an-image-t12-revision-notes/), and test yourself with the [place and edit an image practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-place-and-edit-an-image-t12-practice/).

## Placing an image with precision

"With precision" means the position can be checked against the instruction. Dragging an image until it looks about right is rarely precise enough. Use the software's tools instead.

- **Position settings**: most word processors and presentation packages have a layout or position dialog in which you enter horizontal and vertical positions, measured from the page edge, the margin or a paragraph.
- **Alignment tools**: align left, centre or right relative to the margins or page, and align top, middle or bottom.
- **Anchoring**: an image positioned on the page is attached (anchored) to a paragraph. If text is added above, the image moves with that paragraph. Check that it is still in the right place after editing.
- **Inline images**: an image set *in line with text* sits on a line like a large character, so it can be aligned with the paragraph's alignment buttons.

### Reading a placement instruction

*Instruction:* "Place the image at the top of the page, aligned to the right margin, with the top of the image level with the top of the first line of body text."

A precise approach:

1. Insert the image and set wrapping to square, so it can be positioned freely while text flows around it.
2. In the position settings, set horizontal alignment to *right* relative to the *margin*.
3. Set the vertical position so the top of the image aligns with the top margin, where the first line of text begins.
4. Check in print preview that the image does not extend past the right margin and that the text wraps neatly.

## Resizing to maintain or adjust the aspect ratio

The **aspect ratio** is the relationship between width and height. The syllabus says "as specified", so read which kind of resize is asked for.

- **Maintain the aspect ratio**: keep *lock aspect ratio* switched on and enter one dimension; the software calculates the other. Alternatively drag a **corner** handle, which scales both dimensions together in most software.
- **Adjust the aspect ratio**: switch the lock off and enter both dimensions exactly as instructed. This deliberately changes the shape, which is correct only when the task asks for it.

### Worked example: maintaining the ratio

An image is 15 cm wide and 10 cm high. The task says: "Resize the image to a height of 4 cm, maintaining the aspect ratio."

- Scale factor = 4 / 10 = 0.4.
- New width = 15 x 0.4 = **6 cm**.
- Check: 6 / 4 = 1.5, and 15 / 10 = 1.5, so the ratio is unchanged.

### Worked example: adjusting the ratio

The task now says: "Resize the image to exactly 6 cm wide and 6 cm high." Unlock the aspect ratio and enter both values. The image becomes square and appears stretched vertically: the width scale is 6 / 15 = 0.4, but the height scale is 6 / 10 = 0.6. Because the task specified both dimensions, this is the required result.

### Resizing by percentage

Some software lets you scale by a percentage. At 50%, a 15 cm by 10 cm image becomes 7.5 cm by 5 cm. If the task gives a size in centimetres, enter centimetres; do not convert to a percentage and risk rounding errors.

## Cropping

**Cropping** removes unwanted parts from the edges of an image without shrinking what remains. Use the crop tool and drag the crop handles inward, or enter exact amounts to crop from each side.

*Example:* a photograph is 12 cm wide. Cropping 2 cm from the left and 1 cm from the right leaves 12 - 2 - 1 = **9 cm** visible width. The height is unchanged.

Resizing and cropping change different things: resizing makes the whole picture larger or smaller; cropping changes how much of the picture is shown. If an instruction asks for both, crop first and then resize to the final dimensions, so that the final size is exactly as specified.

## Rotating

**Rotation** turns an image through an angle, clockwise or anticlockwise, about its centre. Use the rotation setting to enter an exact angle, such as 90° or 15°, rather than dragging the rotation handle.

Rotating by 90° swaps the width and height: a 6 cm by 4 cm landscape picture becomes 4 cm wide and 6 cm high. After rotating, check that the image is still within the margins.

## Reflecting (flipping)

A **reflection** or **flip** produces a mirror image.

- **Flip horizontally**: the left and right sides swap, as if reflected in a vertical mirror line. A face looking left now looks right.
- **Flip vertically**: the top and bottom swap, as if reflected in a horizontal mirror line, so the picture appears upside down. Unlike a 180° rotation, the left and right sides stay where they were.

Flipping is not the same as rotating. Text inside a flipped image appears as mirror writing, whereas text in a rotated image is turned but its letters are not reversed. Rotating by 180° gives the same result as flipping both horizontally and vertically.

## Adjusting brightness and contrast

- **Brightness** makes every part of the image lighter or darker. Increasing it helps a photo taken in poor light; too much makes the image look washed out.
- **Contrast** changes the difference between the light and dark areas. Increasing it makes the image look sharper and more vivid; reducing it makes it look flat and grey.

Software usually adjusts these by a percentage. Enter the value given in the task, for example "+20% brightness", rather than choosing a preset that looks similar.

## Grouping and layering

### Layering

When objects overlap, each sits on a **layer**. The order controls which is visible.

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| Bring to front | Moves the object to the top of all layers |
| Send to back | Moves the object behind all other objects |
| Bring forward | Moves it up one layer |
| Send backward | Moves it down one layer |

*Example:* a text box saying "Sale" has been placed on a photograph, but the photograph covers it. Select the photograph and **send it to the back**, or select the text box and **bring it to the front**.

### Grouping and ungrouping

**Grouping** joins several selected objects into one, so they move, resize and copy together and keep their relative positions. **Ungrouping** separates them again so one part can be edited. For example, a logo made from a shape, an image and a text box should be grouped before it is moved; to change the text, ungroup, edit and then group again.

## Common mistakes

- Dragging a side handle, which changes only one dimension and distorts the image when the ratio should be maintained.
- Resizing first and then cropping, leaving the final image smaller than specified.
- Confusing a horizontal flip with a vertical flip.
- Rotating when the task says flip, which leaves text readable but faces still pointing the original way.
- Choosing a brightness preset instead of entering the exact value.
- Moving part of a logo because the objects were not grouped.

## Exam technique

In practical tasks, break the instruction into parts (position, size, crop, rotation, flip, brightness, contrast, layer, group) and check each one after you finish. In theory questions, **describe** the operation by saying what changes in the image, and **explain** why it is used in the scenario, such as "flipped horizontally so the person faces into the page, towards the text".

## Check your understanding

1. An image of 20 cm by 8 cm is resized to a width of 5 cm with the ratio maintained. What is the new height? *8 x 5 / 20 = 2 cm.*
2. What is the difference between cropping and resizing? *Cropping removes parts at the edges; resizing changes the size of the whole image.*
3. What happens to text in an image that is flipped horizontally? *It reads backwards (mirror writing).*
4. Why should a logo made of several objects be grouped? *So the parts move and resize together and stay in position.*
