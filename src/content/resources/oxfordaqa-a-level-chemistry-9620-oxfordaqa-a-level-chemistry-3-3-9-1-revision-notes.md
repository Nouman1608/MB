These notes condense **3.3.9.1 Carboxylic acids and esters** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 4 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples read the [carboxylic acids and esters study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-1/); then try the [carboxylic acids and esters practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-1-practice/).

## The subtopic in six lines

1. Carboxylic acids contain –COOH; esters contain –COO– between two carbon groups.
2. Carboxylic acids are weak (partly dissociated) but still release CO₂ from carbonates.
3. Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, with an acid catalyst.
4. Esters are used as solvents, plasticisers, perfumes and food flavourings.
5. Oils and fats are triesters of propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol); alkaline hydrolysis gives soap and glycerol.
6. Biodiesel is a mixture of methyl esters made from a vegetable oil, methanol and a catalyst.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Carboxyl group | –COOH: C=O and O–H on the same carbon |
| Ester link | –COO– joining the acid part to the alcohol part |
| Weak acid | Only partly dissociates into H⁺ and its anion in water |
| Esterification | Reversible reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol, acid-catalysed, giving an ester and water |
| Hydrolysis | Splitting a bond by reaction with water (here, the ester link) |
| Saponification | Alkaline hydrolysis of a fat or oil, giving soap and glycerol |
| Soap | Sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid |
| Glycerol | Propane-1,2,3-triol, HOCH₂CH(OH)CH₂OH |
| Biodiesel | Mixture of methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids |
| Plasticiser | Additive (often an ester) that sits between polymer chains and makes the plastic flexible |

## Naming esters in three checks

1. Find the C=O carbon. The chain it belongs to is the **acid part** and gives the second word (-oate).
2. The group on the other oxygen is the **alcohol part** and gives the first word (methyl, ethyl, propyl...).
3. Ignore the order on the page: C₂H₅OOCH and HCOOC₂H₅ are both ethyl methanoate.

Esters and acids with the same carbon count are isomers (CₙH₂ₙO₂). Only the acid fizzes with Na₂CO₃.

## Reactions you must write

| Reaction | Example equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Acid + carbonate | 2HCOOH + K₂CO₃ → 2HCOOK + CO₂ + H₂O | Ratio 2 : 1; fizzing, limewater cloudy |
| Acid + hydrogencarbonate | CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + CO₂ + H₂O | Ratio 1 : 1 |
| Esterification | CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + H₂O | Conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, warm |
| Acid hydrolysis | CH₃COOCH(CH₃)₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + (CH₃)₂CHOH | Reflux, dilute acid; incomplete |
| Alkaline hydrolysis | CH₃COOCH(CH₃)₂ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + (CH₃)₂CHOH | Reflux, NaOH(aq); complete |
| Saponification | (RCOO)₃C₃H₅ + 3NaOH → 3RCOONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃ | Fat : soap : glycerol = 1 : 3 : 1 |
| Biodiesel | (RCOO)₃C₃H₅ + 3CH₃OH → 3RCOOCH₃ + C₃H₅(OH)₃ | Catalyst; methanol, not ethanol |

After alkaline hydrolysis, add a strong acid such as dilute HCl to turn the carboxylate back into the free acid.

## Why alkali goes to completion

- In alkali the acid formed is deprotonated to RCOO⁻.
- RCOO⁻ does not react with the alcohol, so the ester cannot re-form.
- In dilute acid the products are an acid and an alcohol, which can esterify again, so an equilibrium mixture results.

## Esterification and equilibrium

- Catalyst: speeds up both directions equally; no change to the yield.
- Higher yield: excess of one reactant, or remove a product (distil off the ester or remove water).
- Kc = [ester][H₂O] ÷ ([acid][alcohol]). Equal moles on each side, so Kc has no units and the volume cancels.
- If the remaining acid is found by titration, subtract the titre due to the sulfuric acid catalyst.

## Uses: one reason each

| Use | Property behind it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Solvent | Dissolves organic substances; volatile | Ethyl ethanoate in glues |
| Plasticiser | Molecules between polymer chains weaken intermolecular forces | Esters added to PVC |
| Perfume | Volatile, pleasant smell | Many small esters |
| Food flavouring | Fruity smell and taste | 3-Methylbutyl ethanoate (banana smell) |

## Calculation patterns

- **Carbonate**: n(CO₂) = ½ n(acid) for a monocarboxylic acid with a carbonate.
- **Gas volume**: V = nRT ÷ p in SI units (m³, Pa, K); multiply m³ by 10⁶ for cm³.
- **Soap**: mass of salt = 3 × n(fat) × Mr(salt).
- **Biodiesel**: methanol needed = 3 × n(oil); a mixed oil gives a mixture of methyl esters in the ratio of its chains.
- **Mr shortcut**: Mr(ester) = Mr(acid) + Mr(alcohol) − 18.0.

## Exam traps

- "Weak because it reacts slowly with carbonates": wrong. Weak means partly dissociated.
- One acid molecule per carbonate ion: wrong for a monocarboxylic acid. Use two.
- Naming CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ "ethyl methanoate" or "propyl methanoate": it is methyl propanoate.
- The acid catalyst "shifts the equilibrium": it does not.
- Free acid as the product of alkaline hydrolysis: give the salt.
- Writing "no reaction" for an ester with sodium carbonate in a test answer: give the observation, "no effervescence".
- Biodiesel made with ethanol, or described as one compound: wrong on both counts.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Give the general formula shared by carboxylic acids and esters with one C=O group and no rings.
2. Name HCOOCH₂CH₃.
3. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂OOCCH₃.
4. Write the equation for ethanoic acid reacting with calcium carbonate.
5. Explain why ethanoic acid is described as a weak acid.
6. Name the catalyst and conditions for esterification.
7. Give one change that increases the equilibrium yield of an ester, and explain it.
8. Give the products of heating methyl butanoate under reflux with aqueous sodium hydroxide.
9. Explain why the reaction in question 8 is not reversible.
10. What is the systematic name of glycerol?
11. How many moles of soap form from 0.15 mol of a fat with three identical chains?
12. State what biodiesel is and how it is made.

### Answers

1. CₙH₂ₙO₂.
2. Ethyl methanoate.
3. Propyl ethanoate (the C=O carbon is in the two-carbon part).
4. 2CH₃COOH + CaCO₃ → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + CO₂ + H₂O.
5. It only partly dissociates into H⁺ and CH₃COO⁻ ions in water.
6. Concentrated sulfuric acid (a few drops), warm or heat.
7. Use an excess of the alcohol (or acid), or remove the ester or water; the equilibrium shifts right to oppose the change.
8. Sodium butanoate and methanol.
9. The butanoate ion formed does not react with methanol, so the ester cannot re-form.
10. Propane-1,2,3-triol.
11. 0.45 mol.
12. A mixture of methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids, made by reacting a vegetable oil with methanol in the presence of a catalyst.
