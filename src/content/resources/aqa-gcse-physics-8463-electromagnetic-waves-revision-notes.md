These notes condense section **4.6.2 Electromagnetic waves** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification,
including required practical 10. It is examined on Paper 2 at both tiers; (HT only) statements are marked
**Higher tier only**, and lenses and visible light are physics only. For full explanations, read the
[electromagnetic waves study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-electromagnetic-waves/), then
test yourself with the
[electromagnetic waves practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-electromagnetic-waves-practice/).
The rest of Topic 6 is in the [Waves revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-waves-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic wave | Transverse wave that transfers energy from a source to an absorber |
| Continuous spectrum | The groups merge into each other with no gaps |
| Refraction | Change in direction of a wave at a boundary, caused by a change in speed |
| Normal | Line at 90° to a boundary where a ray meets it |
| Ionising radiation | Radiation that can cause gene mutation and cancer (X-rays, gamma rays) |
| Radiation dose | Measure of the risk of harm from exposure of the body to radiation (Sv) |
| Principal focus | Point where a convex lens brings parallel rays to a focus |
| Focal length | Distance from the lens to the principal focus |
| Real image | Formed where light rays actually meet; can be shown on a screen |
| Virtual image | Formed where rays only appear to come from; cannot be shown on a screen |
| Specular / diffuse reflection | Reflection in one direction from a smooth surface / scattered from a rough surface |

## The spectrum

```
long λ, low f                                                 short λ, high f
radio  microwave  infrared  visible (red→violet)  ultraviolet  X-rays  gamma
```

- All travel at the same speed in a vacuum or air (questions give 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s).
- v = f λ: shorter wavelength ↔ higher frequency.
- Eyes detect only visible light.

*Worked reminder.* Microwaves at 12 GHz: λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 12 × 10⁹ = **0.025 m** (2.5 cm).

## Refraction

- Air → glass: bends **towards** the normal (slows down).
- Glass → air: bends **away from** the normal (speeds up).
- Along the normal: no change in direction (speed still changes).
- **Higher tier only:** wave fronts: the part entering first slows first, so the front turns; wave fronts are
  closer together in the slower medium.
- **Higher tier only:** absorption, transmission, refraction and reflection vary with wavelength (bone absorbs
  X-rays that soft tissue transmits).

## Required practical 10

```
Leslie cube filled with hot water → infrared detector at the SAME distance from each face
→ record readings → compare surfaces
result: matt black emits most; shiny silver least
```

Control variables: distance from the face, water temperature at the time of reading, the detector used.

## Origins and hazards

- Changes in atoms and nuclei generate or absorb EM waves over a wide range of frequencies.
- Gamma rays come from changes in the **nucleus**.
- **Higher tier only:** oscillations in circuits produce radio waves; absorbed radio waves induce an
  alternating current of the **same frequency**.

| Radiation | Hazard |
|---|---|
| Ultraviolet | Premature skin ageing; increased risk of skin cancer |
| X-rays, gamma | Ionising: gene mutation and cancer |

Effects depend on the **type** of radiation and the **size of the dose**. 1000 mSv = 1 Sv.

## Uses

| Group | Uses | Key property (Higher tier only) |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | TV and radio | Made and detected by circuits |
| Microwaves | Satellite communications; cooking | Pass through atmosphere; absorbed by water |
| Infrared | Heaters; cooking; infrared cameras | Absorbed by surfaces; emitted by warm objects |
| Visible | Fibre optic communications | Travels along glass fibres |
| Ultraviolet | Energy efficient lamps; sun tanning | Makes coatings emit visible light; tans skin |
| X-rays, gamma | Medical imaging and treatments | Penetrate soft tissue; high energy kills cells |

## Lenses (physics only)

| | Convex | Concave |
|---|---|---|
| Effect on parallel rays | Brings them to a focus | Spreads them out |
| Image | Real or virtual | Always virtual |
| Symbol in diagrams | Line with outward-pointing arrowheads | Line with inward-pointing arrowheads |

**Ray diagram rules (convex):** parallel ray → through principal focus; ray through centre → straight on.

```
magnification = image height ÷ object height      (no units; same unit for both heights)
```

*Worked reminder.* Object 8.0 mm, image 2.0 cm = 20 mm: magnification = 20 ÷ 8.0 = **2.5**.

## Visible light (physics only)

- Each colour: its own narrow band of wavelength and frequency.
- Opaque object: colour = wavelengths most strongly reflected; others absorbed.
- Reflects all equally → white; absorbs all → black.
- Filter: transmits its own colour, absorbs others.
- Transparent: see clearly through it. Translucent: light passes but is scattered.

## Exam traps

- Spectrum order reversed, or infrared and ultraviolet swapped.
- "Gamma rays travel faster": all EM waves have the same speed in a vacuum.
- Refraction described without the change in speed (HT explanation).
- Frequencies left in MHz or GHz.
- Magnification with a unit, or heights in different units.
- "A concave lens can form a real image."
- A red object through a blue filter: it looks **black**, not purple.

## Quick self-test

1. List the EM groups in order of increasing frequency.
2. State one property shared by all EM waves.
3. Calculate the wavelength of radio waves of frequency 1.5 × 10⁶ Hz (speed 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s).
4. A ray passes from air into glass at an angle. How does its direction change?
5. (Higher tier only) Explain, using wave fronts, why light changes direction entering glass.
6. Which EM waves come from changes in the nucleus?
7. Give one hazard of ultraviolet and one of X-rays.
8. (Higher tier only) Why are microwaves suitable for satellite communications?
9. Define the focal length of a convex lens.
10. An object 2.4 cm tall forms an image 6.0 cm tall. Calculate the magnification.
11. Describe the difference between specular and diffuse reflection.
12. Why does a black object look black in white light?

### Answers

1. Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
2. Any one: transverse; transfer energy from source to absorber; same speed in a vacuum.
3. 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.5 × 10⁶ = **200 m**.
4. It bends towards the normal.
5. One end of each wave front enters the glass first and slows down while the rest is still faster in air,
   so the wave front turns and the direction changes.
6. Gamma rays.
7. UV: premature skin ageing or increased risk of skin cancer. X-rays: ionising, can cause gene mutation and
   cancer.
8. They pass through the atmosphere to and from the satellite.
9. The distance from the lens to the principal focus.
10. 6.0 ÷ 2.4 = **2.5**.
11. Specular: from a smooth surface, in a single direction. Diffuse: from a rough surface, scattered.
12. It absorbs all wavelengths of visible light and reflects none.
