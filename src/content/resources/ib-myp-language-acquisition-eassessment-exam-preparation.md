The [full syllabus guide](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-syllabus-guide/) sets out the four
criteria and the emergent/capable/proficient proficiency levels eAssessment results are reported
against (day-to-day teaching instead uses a separate, finer six-phase continuum -- see below).
These notes focus specifically on preparing for the two separately assessed eAssessment components,
alongside the [subject overview](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-subject-guide/) and
[assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-assessment-revision-notes/) already
on the site.

## Component 1: the on-screen examination (listening, reading, writing)

This component covers three of the four criteria in one sitting of three tasks, each of about 35
minutes. Tasks 1 and 2 each pair a written-visual text with an audio-visual text and assess listening
(criterion A) and reading (criterion B) equally, 16 marks each per task, so each task is worth 32 marks.
Task 3 is writing in response to multimodal texts (criterion D), worth 32 marks. Criteria A, B and D are
therefore worth 32 marks each, 96 marks in all, in the eAssessment blueprint (a separate scale from the
8-level, 32-mark-total classroom assessment). Because all three criteria are assessed together,
exam-preparation time should be distributed across all three skills rather than concentrated on
whichever feels most comfortable. A practical preparation routine: for listening, practise with
authentic or course-level audio at your assessed proficiency level and note down key information while
listening rather than only afterwards; for reading, practise skimming a multimodal text (one combining
written and visual/spatial elements) for overall meaning before rereading for detail; for writing,
practise producing short pieces in different registers (formal, informal, social, academic) to
different audiences, since criterion D specifically rewards recognising which register a task
actually calls for.

On duration: the guide's blueprint puts the on-screen examination at 1 hour 45 minutes in all, three
tasks of about 35 minutes each. Confirm the exact arrangements for your sitting with your school's exams
office.

## Component 2: the speaking examination

Unlike the other three criteria, speaking (criterion C) is assessed separately as an internally
assessed, IB-moderated interactive task, marked first by your own classroom teacher and then checked
by the IB against a sample of your school's students. It is worth 32 marks and lasts 15 to 17 minutes,
including preparation time. Because it is genuinely interactive rather than
a prepared monologue, the highest-value preparation is practising spontaneous, unscripted
conversation on topics of personal, local and global interest under time pressure -- rehearsing only
a fixed set of prepared answers risks being caught out if the actual interaction goes in an unexpected
direction. Recording yourself in mock speaking interactions and reviewing afterward for fluency,
range of vocabulary and how naturally you respond to unexpected follow-up questions is a more
realistic preparation method than rehearsing a script.

## Why treating the two components separately matters

Because all four criteria are independently marked and equally weighted at 32 marks each in the
eAssessment blueprint, strong
performance on the written on-screen paper does not substitute for oral preparation, and vice versa.
A student who is confident on paper but has not rehearsed spontaneous speaking under pressure is
under-prepared for a full quarter of the total assessment, regardless of how strong their listening,
reading and writing scores might be.

## Six phases for teaching, three levels for reporting

Teaching and learning are organised into **six phases**, which do not correspond to age or year
group and which students can enter or leave at any point, while eAssessment reports course results
at **three proficiency levels** -- emergent, capable and proficient. These are two different things,
not two names for the same scale. For exam preparation, what matters is the proficiency level your
assessment targets: pitch practice material and past-paper style questions at that **level**.

## Confirming your proficiency level before you prepare

Because MYP eAssessment results are reported at one of three proficiency levels -- emergent,
capable or proficient -- rather than by year group, confirm with your teacher which level your
own assessment targets before beginning focused exam preparation. Practising with material pitched at
the wrong level either underprepares you (if you practise below your actual level) or wastes revision
time on unrealistic expectations (if you practise above it). Year group and proficiency level do not
necessarily align, which is why this check matters.

## Preparation checklist

- Confirm your assessed proficiency level (emergent, capable or proficient) with your teacher before
  selecting practice material.
- Practise listening and reading with multimodal texts (not audio- or text-only), since both criteria
  explicitly include interpreting spatial and visual elements alongside spoken or written content.
- Practise writing in more than one register (formal, informal, social, academic) rather than only the
  register you find most natural.
- Build a separate, dedicated speaking-practice routine involving spontaneous interaction, not just
  written revision -- ideally with a partner or teacher who can ask genuine follow-up questions.
- Review recordings of your own speaking practice critically, listening specifically for fluency,
  vocabulary range and how naturally you handle unexpected questions.

## Self-test

1. Which three criteria are assessed together in the on-screen examination, and which criterion is
   assessed separately?
2. Why is rehearsing only a fixed set of prepared answers a weaker speaking-preparation strategy than
   practising spontaneous interaction?
3. What could go wrong if you prepare using material pitched at the wrong proficiency level?
4. Name one practical preparation habit specific to the writing component (criterion D).
5. Why can't strong written performance substitute for speaking preparation, or vice versa?

**Answers:** 1. Listening (A), reading (B) and writing (D) are assessed together in the on-screen
examination; speaking (C) is assessed separately as an internally assessed, IB-moderated task.
2. Because the speaking examination is genuinely interactive, and an unexpected follow-up question can
expose a student who has only rehearsed a fixed script, whereas practising spontaneous interaction
builds the flexibility the actual task requires. 3. Practising below your actual proficiency level
leaves you underprepared for the real assessment's complexity; practising above it wastes revision
time on unrealistic expectations you are not yet being assessed against. 4. Practising short pieces in
different registers (formal, informal, social, academic) so you can recognise and produce the register
a given task actually calls for. 5. Because all four criteria are independently marked and equally
weighted, so strength in one skill area does not compensate for weakness in another -- each is a
genuinely separate quarter of the total assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the IB MYP Language Acquisition on-screen exam?**
The guide's blueprint gives 1 hour 45 minutes: three tasks of about 35 minutes each. Confirm the arrangements for your sitting with your school's exams office.

**What is in the MYP Language Acquisition on-screen examination?**
The on-screen exam has three tasks and is worth 96 marks. Tasks 1 and 2 each pair a written-visual text with an audio-visual text and assess listening (Criterion A) and reading (Criterion B), 16 marks each per task. Task 3 is writing in response to multimodal texts (Criterion D), 32 marks. That makes 32 marks each for Criteria A, B and D in the eAssessment blueprint. Speaking (Criterion C) is assessed separately and is worth 32 marks.

**What is the difference between MYP Language Acquisition phases and proficiency levels?**
Teaching is organised into six phases that do not correspond to age or year group, while eAssessment reports results at three proficiency levels: emergent, capable and proficient. They are two different things, so pitch exam practice at the level your assessment targets.

**How should I practise for the MYP Language Acquisition speaking examination?**
Practise spontaneous, unscripted conversation on topics of personal, local and global interest, ideally with a partner who asks genuine follow-up questions. Recording mock interactions and reviewing them for fluency, vocabulary range and handling of unexpected questions is more realistic than rehearsing a script.
