These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 8.04, Properties of polygons. Questions 1 to 12 are for both tiers. Question 13 is a formal proof and is **Higher tier only**. No diagrams are given: sketch each shape from its description, with the vertices in order round the shape, and mark equal sides and parallel lines. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the properties first in the [properties of polygons study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-properties-of-polygons-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [properties of polygons revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-properties-of-polygons-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotation symmetry of

**(a)** a parallelogram that is not a rectangle or rhombus **[1]**

**(b)** a kite that is not a rhombus **[1]**

**(c)** a regular hexagon. **[1]**

**2.** One angle of an isosceles triangle is 48°. Find the two possible sets of angles for the triangle. **[2]**

**3.** Write down the name of the quadrilateral whose diagonals

**(a)** are equal, bisect each other and are perpendicular **[1]**

**(b)** are perpendicular, with only one of them cut in half by the other. **[1]**

**4.** In parallelogram PQRS, angle P = (3y + 5)° and angle Q = (2y − 15)°. Find angle R. **[3]**

**5.** The diagonals of a rhombus are 18 cm and 24 cm. Find the perimeter of the rhombus. **[3]**

**6.** The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle are x° and (2x + 6)°. Find x. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** ABCD is a kite with AB = AD and CB = CD. Angle BAD = 50° and angle BCD = 84°.

**(a)** Find angle ABC. **[2]**

**(b)** The diagonal AC is drawn. Find angle BAC and angle ACB. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the angle between the two diagonals. **[1]**

**8.** In rectangle ABCD, the diagonals meet at M. AM = (4x − 3) cm and BM = (2x + 5) cm.

**(a)** Find x and the length AM. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the length of the diagonal BD. **[1]**

**(c)** Angle MAB = 35°. Find angle AMB, giving a reason. **[2]**

**9.** ABCD is a rhombus. The diagonal AC is drawn. Prove that AC bisects angle DAB. **[3]**

**10.** ABCDE is a regular pentagon. The diagonal AC is drawn.

**(a)** Find angle BAC. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle CAE. **[1]**

**11.** ABCD is an isosceles trapezium with AB parallel to DC and AD = BC. Angle DAB = 68°.

**(a)** Write down angle ABC. **[1]**

**(b)** Find angle ADC. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the number of lines of symmetry of ABCD. **[1]**

**12.** **(a)** A quadrilateral has rotation symmetry of order 2 and no lines of symmetry. Write down its name. **[1]**

**(b)** A quadrilateral has exactly one line of symmetry and perpendicular diagonals. Write down its name. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the name of a triangle with exactly one line of symmetry. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** A, B and C are points on a circle with centre O, and O lies inside triangle ABC. Prove that angle AOB = 2 × angle ACB. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **0 lines; order 2** [1]

**(b)** **1 line; order 1** [1]

**(c)** **6 lines; order 6** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Fold a parallelogram along a diagonal and the corners do not meet, so it has no lines of symmetry, even though a half turn maps it onto itself.

**2.** 48°, 48°, 84° (48° is a base angle) [1]; 48°, 66°, 66° (48° is the top angle) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "One angle is 48°" does not say which angle. Consider both cases.

**3. (a)** **Square** [1]

**(b)** **Kite** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A rhombus also has perpendicular diagonals, but both of its diagonals are bisected.

**4.** 3y + 5 + 2y − 15 = 180, because co-interior angles add up to 180° [1]; 5y = 190, y = 38, so angle P = 119° [1]; angle R = angle P = **119°**, because opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Angles P and Q are neighbours, not opposites, so they add to 180° rather than being equal.

**5.** Half-diagonals 9 cm and 12 cm meet at 90° [1]; side = √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 cm [1]; perimeter = **60 cm** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, which creates four right-angled triangles. Using 18 and 24 directly gives 30 cm, twice the true side.

**6.** x + 2x + 6 = 90 [1]; **x = 28** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle add up to 90°, not 180°.

**7. (a)** Angles ABC and ADC are equal (the angles between the unequal sides) [1]; (360 − 50 − 84) ÷ 2 = **113°** [1]

**(b)** AC bisects angles A and C: angle BAC = **25°** [1]; angle ACB = **42°** [1]

**(c)** **90°** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Check with triangle ABC: 25 + 113 + 42 = 180. If the check fails, one of the bisected angles is wrong.

**8. (a)** The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, so 4x − 3 = 2x + 5 [1]; x = 4 and **AM = 13 cm** [1]

**(b)** **26 cm** [1]

**(c)** MA = MB, so triangle AMB is isosceles and angle MBA = 35° [1]; angle AMB = 180 − 70 = **110°** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Because all four half-diagonals are equal, the diagonals split a rectangle into two pairs of isosceles triangles.

**9.** AB = BC (sides of a rhombus), so triangle ABC is isosceles and angle BAC = angle BCA [1]; AD is parallel to BC, so angle DAC = angle BCA (alternate angles are equal) [1]; therefore angle BAC = angle DAC, so AC bisects angle DAB [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Every line of a proof needs its reason. The final sentence must say what has been proved.

**10. (a)** Angle ABC = 108° and AB = BC, so triangle ABC is isosceles [1]; angle BAC = (180 − 108) ÷ 2 = **36°** [1]

**(b)** 108 − 36 = **72°** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The interior angle of a regular pentagon is 180 − 360 ÷ 5 = 108°. The diagonal cuts angle BAE into 36° and 72°.

**11. (a)** **68°** (base angles of an isosceles trapezium are equal) [1]

**(b)** 180 − 68 = **112°** (co-interior angles add up to 180°) [1]

**(c)** **1** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The line of symmetry of an isosceles trapezium joins the midpoints of the two parallel sides.

**12. (a)** **Parallelogram** [1]

**(b)** **Kite** [1]

**(c)** **Isosceles** triangle [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A rhombus also has perpendicular diagonals, but it has two lines of symmetry, so it does not fit part (b).

**13.** Draw the line from C through O to a point D on the other side; OA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles OAC and OBC are isosceles, with angle OCA = angle OAC = x and angle OCB = angle OBC = y [1]; angle AOD = x + x = 2x, because an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles [1]; similarly angle BOD = 2y [1]; so angle AOB = 2x + 2y = 2(x + y) = 2 × angle ACB [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* This is a formal proof that uses isosceles triangle properties, as the specification's example for 8.04 suggests. Using letters for the unknown angles keeps it general.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a parallelogram or rectangle lines of symmetry along its diagonals.
- Treating neighbouring angles of a parallelogram as equal.
- Using whole diagonals instead of half-diagonals with Pythagoras.
- Choosing the wrong pair of equal angles in a kite.
- Considering only one case for an isosceles triangle.
- Proof steps without reasons, or no final statement.
