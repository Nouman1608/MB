These notes condense subtopic **14.3 Mammalian hormones** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definition of a hormone, the endocrine glands and the hormones they make, the role of adrenaline, and the comparison of nervous and hormonal control. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [14.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-hormones/). Then try the [14.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-hormones-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Hormone | A chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs |
| Endocrine gland | A gland that releases hormones directly into the blood |
| Target organ | An organ whose cells respond to a particular hormone |
| Carried by | Blood plasma |

## Glands and hormones

| Gland | Hormone(s) |
|---|---|
| Adrenal glands | Adrenaline |
| Pancreas | Insulin and glucagon |
| Pituitary gland | FSH and LH |
| Testes | Testosterone |
| Ovaries | Oestrogen and progesterone |

**Positions to remember:** adrenal glands sit on top of the kidneys; the pituitary is at the base of the brain; the pancreas is near the stomach; the testes are in the scrotum; the ovaries are in the lower abdomen.

## Adrenaline

| Effect | Reason |
|---|---|
| Increases heart rate | Faster blood flow delivers oxygen and glucose to muscles |
| Increases blood glucose concentration | Glucose is released from the liver so muscles can respire faster |

**Situations:** danger or fear, excitement, stress, sudden shock, and the start of exercise or a race. The body is prepared for action ("fight or flight").

## Nervous and hormonal control

| Feature | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Message | Electrical impulses along neurones | Chemicals in the blood |
| Speed | Very fast | Slower |
| Duration | Short | Longer |
| Examples | Reflexes | Adrenaline, insulin, sex hormones |

**Why the differences?** Impulses travel along neurones almost instantly. Hormones travel in the blood and must reach the target organ. An impulse stops when the stimulus stops. A hormone stays in the blood until it is broken down.

## Route from gland to response

1. A stimulus is detected.
2. The gland secretes the hormone into the blood.
3. The blood carries it round the body.
4. The target organ responds.
5. The hormone is broken down and the effect fades.

## What each hormone does (links to other subtopics)

| Hormone | Made by | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| Adrenaline | Adrenal glands | Increases heart rate and blood glucose concentration in danger or excitement |
| Insulin | Pancreas | Lowers blood glucose concentration |
| Glucagon | Pancreas | Raises blood glucose concentration |
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Helps an egg to develop in the ovary (menstrual cycle) |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Triggers the release of an egg (menstrual cycle) |
| Testosterone | Testes | Controls male secondary sexual characteristics at puberty |
| Oestrogen | Ovaries | Controls female secondary sexual characteristics; repairs the uterus lining |
| Progesterone | Ovaries | Maintains the lining of the uterus |

For this subtopic, the main requirement is to match the gland to the hormone. The jobs are developed in blood glucose control and in sexual reproduction in humans.

## Worked example: linking cause and effect

*A student is told to explain why a frightened person's heart pounds.*

1. The brain detects the danger and stimulates the adrenal glands.
2. Adrenaline is released into the blood.
3. The blood carries adrenaline to its target organs, including the heart.
4. The heart rate increases and the blood glucose concentration rises.

Keep the order: stimulus, gland, hormone, blood, target organ, response. This sequence earns marks in almost any hormone question.

## Typical comparison table to learn

| Question wording | Nervous side | Hormonal side |
|---|---|---|
| How fast? | Fast | Slower |
| How long? | Short | Longer |
| What carries the message? | Impulses along neurones | Hormones in the blood |
| Which parts respond? | Effectors (muscles or glands) | Target organs |

## Worked calculation

*A person's resting pulse is 70 beats per minute. After a shock it rises to 98 beats per minute. Find the percentage increase.*

1. Increase = 98 - 70 = 28 beats per minute.
2. Percentage = 28 divided by 70 x 100 = 40 per cent.

Divide by the starting value, which is the resting pulse.

## Short model answers

**Define a hormone.** A chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.

**State two effects of adrenaline.** It increases the heart rate and increases the blood glucose concentration.

**Compare nervous and hormonal control.** Nervous control is fast and short-lived. Hormonal control is slower and lasts longer.

**Explain why the effect of a hormone lasts longer than that of a nerve impulse.** The hormone stays in the blood until it is broken down, so it continues to act on its target organ.

**State where adrenaline is made.** In the adrenal glands.

## Exam traps

- Hormones are carried by the **blood**, not by neurones.
- Say **specific target organs** in a definition.
- A hormone is the chemical; the **gland** makes it.
- Adrenaline **raises** blood glucose. Insulin lowers it.
- The pancreas makes insulin and glucagon, **not** adrenaline.
- The pituitary makes FSH and LH. The ovaries make oestrogen and progesterone.
- Compare speed and duration in the same answer; write both sides.
- Do not mix up the adrenal glands with the kidneys.
