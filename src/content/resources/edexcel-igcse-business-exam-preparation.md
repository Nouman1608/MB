Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Business (4BS1) is not tiered and has two externally assessed written papers, both linear: Paper 1, Investigating small businesses (4BS1/01), and Paper 2, Investigating large businesses (4BS1/02). Each lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, is worth 80 marks and carries 50% of the qualification, and each is made up of four compulsory 20-mark questions. Calculators may be used. Useful starting points are the topic notes on [costs and break-even analysis](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-costs-and-break-even-analysis-revision-notes/) and [business objectives and types of organisation](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-business-objectives-and-types-of-organisation-revision-notes/).

## Paper 1: Investigating small businesses

Paper 1 sets its scenarios around a small business, defined in the specification as one with up to 49 employees. With four compulsory questions of 20 marks each, there is no choice of question, so every part of the course that could be set in a small-business context needs some revision. Think about what is distinctive about a small firm: limited finance, a small team, owner involvement and a local or niche market. When a scenario describes such a business, your answers should refer to those details rather than offering a textbook definition.

Ways to prepare:

- Revise each of the five specification areas (business activity and influences on business; people in business; business finance; marketing; business operations) with a small business in mind, using the example of a shop, a cafe or a workshop.
- Practise applying a concept to a named business: define it briefly, link it to the scenario, and then say what it means for that business.
- Include numerical practice in every session, because calculators are permitted and numeric questions are a source of reliable marks.

## Paper 2: Investigating large businesses

Paper 2 uses the same shape of paper (90 minutes, 80 marks, four compulsory 20-mark questions) but with scenarios based on a large business, defined as one with more than 250 employees. Here the context changes the arguments. Large firms may have more resources, more layers of management and operations on a bigger scale, and your answers should reflect that. The same five specification areas are examined, so the knowledge base is shared; what changes is the setting you apply it to.

Ways to prepare:

- Take a concept you have already revised for a small business and rewrite your notes for a large business, such as how raising finance or communicating with staff might differ at scale.
- Practise the extended-style responses that require you to weigh two sides and reach a conclusion for a specific business.
- Link topics, such as how a decision about operations affects costs and then profit.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Each paper: 90 minutes for 80 marks is 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark.
- Each 20-mark question: 20 x 1.125 = 22.5 minutes, and four questions use 4 x 22.5 = 90 minutes.
- Both papers together: 180 minutes for 160 marks, still 1.125 minutes per mark.

Because 22.5 minutes is the average per question with nothing left over, plan about 20 minutes per question and keep roughly 10 minutes for checking and for finishing any question you left. Do not spend 30 minutes on a favourite topic at the expense of another 20-mark question, because every question is compulsory and the marks have the same value.

## Question styles and command words

General guidance that holds for business exams of this type:

- **State** or **identify**: one word or short phrase, no development.
- **Calculate**: show the formula, the substitution and the answer with units.
- **Explain**: make a point and develop it with a reason or consequence.
- **Analyse**: follow a chain of cause and effect, using the context.
- **Evaluate** or **justify**: weigh up the options, reach a supported decision, and say why the alternative is weaker.

For the numerical questions, practise the arithmetic in [the costs and break-even analysis topic](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-costs-and-break-even-analysis/) until the formulae are automatic.

## Worked practice scenario: a break-even decision

Question: "A small bakery sells loaves at 8.00 each. Variable cost per loaf is 3.50 and fixed costs are 12,000 per year. (a) Calculate the break-even output. (b) Expected sales are 3,500 loaves. Calculate the margin of safety and the expected profit. (c) The owner suggests raising the price. Comment on this."

```
(a) Contribution per loaf = 8.00 - 3.50 = 4.50
    Break-even output = fixed costs / contribution = 12,000 / 4.50
                      = 2,666.67, so 2,667 loaves (rounded up)

(b) Margin of safety = 3,500 - 2,667 = 833 loaves
    Profit = (3,500 x 4.50) - 12,000 = 15,750 - 12,000 = 3,750
    (check: revenue 28,000 - variable costs 12,250 - fixed costs 12,000 = 3,750)
```

(c) A higher price raises the contribution per loaf and lowers the break-even output, so the margin of safety widens on the same sales. However, customers may buy fewer loaves, especially if nearby shops charge less, so sales could fall below the level assumed. A good answer concludes by weighing the two effects for this bakery, for instance recommending a small rise tested against local competitor prices.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm which paper you are sitting (small or large business) and prepare the matching context.
- Pack a calculator and spare pens, and check the time and room.
- Write 20 minutes beside each of the four questions as soon as you start.
- Read the scenario twice and underline details such as size, location, products and recent events.
- Show formulae and workings, with units, in every calculation.
- Use scenario details in each extended answer, not just general theory.
- Finish with a conclusion on evaluation questions.
- Keep 10 minutes at the end to return to unfinished parts.

## Self-test

1. How many questions are on each paper, and how many marks is each worth?
2. How many minutes per mark does each paper allow?
3. What size of business is the focus of Paper 2?
4. Calculate break-even output where fixed costs are 6,000, price is 5.00 and variable cost per unit is 2.00.
5. Why should an evaluation answer end with a conclusion?

**Answers:** 1. Four compulsory questions of 20 marks each. 2. 1.125 minutes per mark (90 / 80). 3. A large business, with more than 250 employees. 4. Contribution is 5.00 - 2.00 = 3.00, so break-even output is 6,000 / 3.00 = 2,000 units. 5. Because evaluation requires a supported judgement, and the conclusion is where you show you have weighed the arguments.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel IGCSE Business 4BS1?**
There are two externally assessed written papers, Paper 1 (4BS1/01) and Paper 2 (4BS1/02), and the qualification is not tiered.

**How long is each paper and what is it worth?**
Each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 80 marks, which is 50% of the qualification.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 is titled Investigating small businesses and uses scenarios based on a small business (up to 49 employees). Paper 2 is titled Investigating large businesses and uses scenarios based on a large business (more than 250 employees).

**Are there optional questions?**
No. Each paper consists of four compulsory 20-mark questions, so there is no choice of question.

**Is a calculator allowed, and when are the exams?**
Calculators may be used on both papers. The qualification is linear, with June and November availability.
