OxfordAQA International GCSE History 9245 Paper 2 Section B sets four questions on your thematic study, worth 30 marks. Two full sets follow for each option, so that between them they reach all six periods.

## Technology and change, c800-present

### Set A

**1.** Give three uses of the astrolabe in the Islamic Golden Age. **[3]**

**2.** Explain one way in which the printing press affected the Reformation. **[6]**

**3.** Explain two ways in which steam power changed transport. **[9]**

**4.** "Individuals were the most important factor in technological change from c1710 to c1950." How far do you agree? **[12]**

#### Worked answers

**1.** Measuring the height of stars and the sun; telling the time; finding the direction of Mecca for prayer; navigation; surveying. (Any three.)

**2.** One way was **speed of spreading ideas**. After Luther published his criticisms of the Church in 1517, printers produced thousands of copies of his pamphlets in German, *so* his ideas reached ordinary readers across the German states within weeks, before the Church could suppress them.

**3.** *Way 1 -- railways:* Trevithick's high-pressure engine (1804) led to steam locomotives, which carried goods and passengers faster and more cheaply, linking cities and markets. *Way 2 -- shipping:* steamships no longer depended on wind, so voyages became regular and quicker, expanding international trade and migration.

**4.** *Individuals:* Newcomen, Watt and Trevithick in steam; Morse, Bell and Marconi in communications; Ford in car manufacturing. *Other factors:* business and profit (demand for coal, faster communication, mass markets); science and earlier inventions (each built on others' work); war and government (regulation of radio, military uses). *Judgement:* individuals made the breakthroughs, but economic demand explains why their inventions spread.

### Set B

**1.** Give three ways in which the car changed everyday life in the twentieth century. **[3]**

**2.** Explain one way in which Alan Turing contributed to the development of computing. **[6]**

**3.** Explain two ways in which the World Wide Web changed the world of work. **[9]**

**4.** "The Communications Revolution of 1830-1950 changed people's lives more than computing and the internet." How far do you agree? **[12]**

#### Worked answers

**1.** Suburbs grew because people could travel to work; new leisure such as day trips and holidays; jobs in car factories and garages; road building; pollution and accidents. (Any three.)

**2.** One way was **theory**. In 1936 Turing described a theoretical "universal" machine that could carry out any calculation given the right instructions, *so* he set out the idea behind the modern stored-program computer; his wartime codebreaking work also showed what electronic machines could do.

**3.** *Way 1 -- communication:* email and websites allowed businesses to contact customers and colleagues instantly across the world. *Way 2 -- where and how people work:* online tools allowed remote and flexible working and created new jobs in web design and online retail, while some older jobs disappeared.

**4.** *Communications Revolution:* the telegraph and telephone ended the isolation of distance; radio and cinema created mass entertainment; office machines opened jobs to women. *Computing and the internet:* instant global communication, social media, online work and AI, but also a digital divide. *Judgement:* both transformed life; one argument is that the earlier revolution was more fundamental because it first separated communication from transport.

## Medicine and change, c800-present

### Set C

**1.** Give three features of bimaristans (hospitals) in the medieval Islamic world. **[3]**

**2.** Explain one way in which the Black Death changed society. **[6]**

**3.** Explain two ways in which Pasteur's germ theory changed medicine. **[9]**

**4.** "Individuals were the most important factor in medical progress in the nineteenth century." How far do you agree? **[12]**

#### Worked answers

**1.** Free care for patients regardless of wealth; separate wards for different illnesses; trained physicians; teaching of medical students; pharmacies; care for the mentally ill. (Any three.)

**2.** One way was **labour shortages**. So many workers died that survivors could demand higher wages, *so* in parts of Europe peasants gained more freedom and landowners lost power, weakening the old feudal system.

**3.** *Way 1 -- understanding disease:* germ theory replaced miasma and spontaneous generation, showing that specific microbes caused disease, which led Koch to identify the bacteria behind diseases such as tuberculosis and cholera. *Way 2 -- surgery and prevention:* Lister applied germ theory with carbolic acid (1867), reducing infection; vaccines against diseases such as anthrax and rabies followed from Pasteur's own work.

**4.** *Individuals:* Jenner, Snow, Pasteur, Koch, Lister and Nightingale. *Other factors:* science and technology (better microscopes); government (public health laws, sanitation); communication (journals spreading ideas); rivalry between France and Germany driving research. *Judgement:* individuals made key breakthroughs, but government action was needed for them to improve health widely.

### Set D

**1.** Give three ways penicillin was developed between 1928 and 1945. **[3]**

**2.** Explain one way in which the World Health Organization has improved global health. **[6]**

**3.** Explain two ways in which women contributed to twentieth-century medicine. **[9]**

**4.** "War was the most important factor in medical change between c1880 and the present." How far do you agree? **[12]**

#### Worked answers

**1.** Fleming noticed mould killing bacteria (1928); Florey and Chain purified and tested it (from 1940); American companies mass-produced it with government support; it was used to treat Allied troops by D-Day (1944). (Any three.)

**2.** One way was **vaccination campaigns**. The WHO co-ordinated a worldwide smallpox vaccination programme, *so* smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980 -- the first human disease to be eliminated.

**3.** *Way 1 -- research:* Dorothy Crowfoot Hodgkin determined the structure of penicillin and other molecules, helping drug development; Gertrude Elion developed new drugs. *Way 2 -- clinical practice:* Virginia Apgar devised a simple test of newborn babies' health that is used around the world.

**4.** *War:* mobile X-ray units (Marie Curie) in the First World War; blood storage and transfusion; mass production of penicillin; national health systems shaped by war. *Other factors:* science and technology (DNA research, transplants, keyhole surgery); individuals (Curie, Cushing, Franklin); international co-operation (WHO); industry. *Judgement:* war speeded change, but science and co-operation explain most long-term progress.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: one long fact instead of three.
- Questions 2 and 3: names without explanation of impact.
- Question 4: only one factor discussed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I learn every person listed?**
Yes -- each named individual can be the focus of a question.

**How do I link periods in question 4?**
Choose examples from at least two periods and show how a factor's importance changed over time.
