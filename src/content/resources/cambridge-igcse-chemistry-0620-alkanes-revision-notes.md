Condensed recall for **11.4 Alkanes**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). Explanations and worked examples are in the [alkanes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alkanes/); exam-style questions are in the [alkanes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alkanes-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcomes 1–2 are Core. Outcomes 3–4 (the definition of substitution, and photochemical substitution with chlorine) are Supplement, marked *(Extended)* below.

## Core facts

- Alkanes are **hydrocarbons**: hydrogen and carbon only.
- Bonding: **single covalent** bonds only.
- Alkanes are **saturated**: every carbon–carbon bond is a single bond.
- General formula: **CₙH₂ₙ₊₂**.
- Alkanes are **generally unreactive**. Exceptions: **combustion** and **substitution by chlorine**.

## The first four alkanes

| n | Name | Molecular formula | Structural formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | methane | CH₄ | CH₄ |
| 2 | ethane | C₂H₆ | CH₃CH₃ |
| 3 | propane | C₃H₈ | CH₃CH₂CH₃ |
| 4 | butane | C₄H₁₀ | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ |

Core candidates must name and draw methane and ethane; Extended candidates must also name and draw unbranched alkanes up to four carbons.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Saturated | all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds |
| Hydrocarbon | compound of hydrogen and carbon only |
| Complete combustion | burning in plentiful oxygen, giving carbon dioxide and water |
| Substitution reaction *(Extended)* | one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms |
| Photochemical reaction *(Extended)* | a reaction started by light; for alkanes and chlorine, ultraviolet light |
| Activation energy, Ea *(Extended)* | the minimum energy colliding particles need to react; UV light supplies it here |
| Monosubstitution *(Extended)* | only one hydrogen atom is replaced |

## Combustion equations

Balance C first, then H, then O.

| Alkane | Balanced equation for complete combustion |
|---|---|
| methane | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| ethane | 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O |
| propane | C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| butane | 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O |

Pattern: alkanes with an **even** number of carbon atoms need the whole equation **doubled** to avoid half an O₂.

Limited oxygen → incomplete combustion → carbon monoxide (and soot) instead of carbon dioxide.

## Substitution with chlorine *(Extended)*

| | |
|---|---|
| Reagent | chlorine, Cl₂ |
| Condition | **ultraviolet light** (provides Ea) |
| Type | substitution; photochemical |
| Products | chloroalkane + **hydrogen chloride** |

```
CH₄  + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl  + HCl     (chloromethane)
C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl     (chloroethane)
```

Propane gives **two** possible monosubstituted products, because its hydrogen atoms are in two different positions:

- CH₂ClCH₂CH₃ (chlorine on an end carbon)
- CH₃CHClCH₃ (chlorine on the middle carbon)

Both are C₃H₇Cl: structural isomers.

## Substitution vs addition

| Substitution (alkanes) | Addition (alkenes, 11.5) |
|---|---|
| two products | one product only |
| one atom swaps for another | atoms add across C=C |
| needs UV light with chlorine | bromine reacts at room temperature |

## Drawing checklist

Before you finish any displayed formula, check:

- every **carbon** has exactly **four** bonds;
- every **hydrogen** and every **chlorine** has exactly **one** bond;
- every bond is drawn, including each C–H;
- the number of each atom matches the molecular formula.

A structural formula such as CH₃CH₂Cl must show the arrangement unambiguously: C₂H₅Cl is a molecular formula, and is acceptable only in an equation.

## Isomers among alkanes *(Extended)*

From 11.1, butane, C₄H₁₀, has two structural isomers:

- CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ (unbranched chain)
- CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ (branched chain)

Same molecular formula, different structural formula. The same idea explains why propane gives two monosubstituted chloro products.

## Why "generally unreactive" matters in real use

- Alkanes do not react with acids, alkalis or aqueous bromine under normal conditions, which is why they are stored and transported safely as fuels.
- Combustion needs a flame or spark to supply the activation energy; once started, the heat released keeps the reaction going.
- Substitution needs UV light; in the dark, methane and chlorine can be mixed without reacting. *(Extended)*

## Exam traps

- "Saturated" is about **single C–C bonds**, not about containing only C and H.
- Do not say alkanes are "unreactive": the syllabus says **generally** unreactive.
- The condition is **ultraviolet light**, not "light", "sunlight" alone or "heat". *(Extended)*
- Every substitution equation has **HCl** as the second product. *(Extended)*
- Carbon has **four** bonds in every product you draw. CH₄Cl₂ is impossible.
- Monosubstitution means **one** Cl. Do not draw CH₂Cl₂ unless asked.
- Alkanes **do not** decolourise aqueous bromine; that is the alkene result.

## Self-test

1. State the type of bonding in alkanes.
2. What does "saturated" mean?
3. Give the molecular formula of the alkane with five carbon atoms.
4. Name the two types of reaction alkanes undergo.
5. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane.
6. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethane.
7. Name the toxic gas formed when methane burns in a limited supply of oxygen.
8. *(Extended)* Define a substitution reaction.
9. *(Extended)* State the condition needed for methane to react with chlorine, and its role.
10. *(Extended)* Write the equation for the monosubstitution of ethane by chlorine.
11. *(Extended)* How many different monosubstituted products can propane form with chlorine?
12. Predict what is seen when aqueous bromine is shaken with hexane.

**Answers:**

1. Single covalent bonds.
2. All the carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds.
3. C₅H₁₂.
4. Combustion; substitution (with chlorine).
5. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.
6. 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O.
7. Carbon monoxide.
8. A reaction in which one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms.
9. Ultraviolet light; it provides the activation energy.
10. C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl.
11. Two (CH₂ClCH₂CH₃ and CH₃CHClCH₃).
12. No change: the bromine stays orange, because hexane is saturated.
