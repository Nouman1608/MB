This guide follows OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, specification Version 2.1 (August 2026), statements 10.05a, 10.05b and 10.05c. The 2D work and the exact values are in the Foundation tier and Higher tier content. Applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in more complex figures, including 3D figures, is **Higher tier only**. The specification says that, unless it refers to calculator use, all content may be assessed on either the calculator or the non-calculator papers, so practise both ways. The sine rule and cosine rule are separate statements and are not covered here.

## Pythagoras' theorem (10.05a)

In a right-angled triangle the longest side, opposite the right angle, is the **hypotenuse**. If its length is c and the other two sides are a and b:

**a² + b² = c²**

### Deriving it

Take four copies of a right-angled triangle with short sides a and b and hypotenuse c. Arrange them round a square of side c, so that the four triangles sit inside a larger square of side (a + b). The large square has area (a + b)². It is also four triangles plus the tilted inner square:

```
(a + b)² = 4 × (½ab) + c²
a² + 2ab + b² = 2ab + c²
a² + b² = c²
```

### Finding the hypotenuse

Square both short sides, add, then square root.

**Example 1.** Short sides 11 cm and 6 cm.

```
c² = 11² + 6² = 121 + 36 = 157
c = √157 = 12.5 cm (3 s.f.)
```

### Finding a shorter side

Subtract. The hypotenuse is always the largest square.

**Example 2.** Hypotenuse 17.4 cm, one short side 9.3 cm.

```
a² = 17.4² − 9.3² = 302.76 − 86.49 = 216.27
a = √216.27 = 14.7 cm (3 s.f.)
```

### Testing for a right angle

If the squares of the two shorter sides add to the square of the longest side, the triangle is right-angled.

**Example 3.** Is a triangle with sides 8, 15 and 17 right-angled?

```
8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289
17² = 289
```

The two results are equal, so the triangle is right-angled.

### Pythagoras inside other shapes

Draw a perpendicular to split the shape into right-angled triangles.

**Example 4.** An isosceles triangle has equal sides of 25 cm and a base of 14 cm. Find its perpendicular height and area.

```
The height meets the base at its midpoint, so a short side is 7 cm.
h² = 25² − 7² = 625 − 49 = 576
h = 24 cm
Area = ½ × 14 × 24 = 168 cm²
```

On a coordinate grid, the distance between A(−2, 3) and B(5, 9) uses a horizontal step of 7 and a vertical step of 6:

```
AB² = 7² + 6² = 85
AB = √85 = 9.22 (3 s.f.)
```

### Complex figures and 3D (Higher tier only)

Break the problem into right-angled triangles, one at a time, and use a length from one triangle in the next. Keep exact values such as √100 or √125 unrounded until the last step.

**Example 5 (Higher tier only).** A cuboid measures 8 cm by 6 cm by 5 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal, which runs from one corner to the opposite corner.

```
Diagonal of the 8 by 6 base: √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10 cm
Space diagonal: √(10² + 5²) = √125 = 11.2 cm (3 s.f.)
```

Both steps combine into one result: d² = 8² + 6² + 5².

## Right-angled trigonometry (10.05b)

Label the sides from the angle θ you are working with:

| Side | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypotenuse | Opposite the right angle |
| Opposite | Across the triangle from θ |
| Adjacent | Next to θ, and not the hypotenuse |

```
sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent
```

The ratios depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle. All right-angled triangles with the same angles are similar, so the ratios of their corresponding sides are equal (this links to similar shapes, 9.04c).

### Choosing the ratio

Name the two sides involved: the one you know and the one you want. Then pick the ratio that uses exactly those two.

### Finding a length

**Example 6.** Hypotenuse 12 m, angle 38°. Find the side opposite the angle.

```
sin 38° = opp / 12
opp = 12 × sin 38° = 7.39 m (3 s.f.)
```

**Example 7.** Adjacent side 6.5 cm, angle 52°. Find the opposite side.

```
tan 52° = opp / 6.5
opp = 6.5 × tan 52° = 8.32 cm (3 s.f.)
```

**Example 8.** The unknown is the hypotenuse. Opposite 5.4 cm, angle 28°.

```
sin 28° = 5.4 / h
h = 5.4 / sin 28° = 11.5 cm (3 s.f.)
```

When the unknown is on the bottom of the fraction, divide.

### Finding an angle

Use the inverse function: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹.

**Example 9.** Opposite 9 cm, adjacent 14 cm.

```
tan θ = 9/14
θ = tan⁻¹(9/14) = 32.7° (1 d.p.)
```

**Example 10.** A 6.5 m ladder has its foot 2.5 m from a vertical wall. Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground, and how high it reaches.

```
cos θ = 2.5/6.5, so θ = cos⁻¹(2.5/6.5) = 67.4° (1 d.p.)
height² = 6.5² − 2.5² = 42.25 − 6.25 = 36
height = 6 m
```

The angle of elevation is measured upward from the horizontal. The angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal.

### More complex figures (Higher tier only)

**Example 11 (Higher tier only).** In triangle ABC, D lies on AC and BD is perpendicular to AC. AB = 14 cm, angle A = 35° and angle C = 50°. Find BC.

```
In triangle ABD: BD = 14 × sin 35° = 8.03 cm
In triangle BDC: sin 50° = BD / BC
BC = 8.03 / sin 50° = 10.5 cm (3 s.f.)
```

Keep the unrounded value of BD for the second step.

### 3D figures (Higher tier only)

To measure how steeply a line meets a plane, compare the line with its shadow on the plane, as if a light shone straight down. The line, the shadow and the vertical drop form a right-angled triangle.

**Example 12 (Higher tier only).** In the cuboid of Example 5, find the angle between the space diagonal and the 8 by 6 base.

```
The shadow of the diagonal on the base is the base diagonal, 10 cm.
The vertical edge is 5 cm.
tan θ = 5/10, so θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) = 26.6° (1 d.p.)
```

**Example 13 (Higher tier only).** A pyramid has a square base of side 10 cm. Its apex is 12 cm vertically above the centre of the base. Find the length of a sloping edge, and the angle between the sloping edge and the base.

```
Half of the base diagonal = ½ × √(10² + 10²) = 5√2 = 7.07 cm
Sloping edge² = (5√2)² + 12² = 50 + 144 = 194
Sloping edge = √194 = 13.9 cm (3 s.f.)
tan θ = 12 / (5√2), so θ = 59.5° (1 d.p.)
```

The angle between a sloping face and the base uses the perpendicular from the apex to the middle of a base edge instead: tan φ = 12/5, so φ = 67.4°. The slant height of that face is √(12² + 5²) = 13 cm.

Two triangles give every value you need.

**Right-angled isosceles triangle** with both short sides 1: the hypotenuse is √2 and the angles are 45°, 45°, 90°.

**Equilateral triangle** with side 2, cut in half: the base of each half is 1, the hypotenuse is 2, and the height is √(4 − 1) = √3. The angles are 30°, 60°, 90°.

Read the ratios from them:

| θ | 0° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sin θ | 0 | 1/2 | √2/2 | √3/2 | 1 |
| cos θ | 1 | √3/2 | √2/2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| tan θ | 0 | √3/3 | 1 | √3 | not required |

The specification asks for tan θ at 0°, 30°, 45° and 60° only. Note that √3/3 is the same as 1/√3, and √2/2 is the same as 1/√2. The cos row is the sin row reversed.

**Example 14.** Work out sin 60° × tan 30° ÷ cos 45°, giving an exact answer.

```
(√3/2) × (√3/3) = 3/6 = 1/2
(1/2) ÷ (√2/2) = 1/√2 = √2/2
```

**Example 15.** A right-angled triangle has a 30° angle and a hypotenuse of 8 cm. Find the side opposite the 30° angle.

```
opp = 8 × sin 30° = 8 × 1/2 = 4 cm
```

**Example 16.** A right-angled triangle has a 60° angle and an adjacent side of 5 cm. Find the opposite side exactly.

```
opp = 5 × tan 60° = 5√3 cm
```

## Common errors

- Adding the squares when you should subtract. If you are finding a short side, you are subtracting.
- Taking the answer as c² and forgetting the final square root.
- Naming the opposite and adjacent sides from the wrong angle. Re-label whenever the angle changes.
- Dividing when you should multiply (or the other way round) when the unknown is on the bottom of the fraction.
- Writing sin 30° = √3/2. The two values 1/2 and √3/2 are swapped between sin 30° and sin 60°.
- Rounding a length part-way through a 2-step problem. Carry full values, then round once.
- In 3D, using a sloping edge as if it lay in the base. Draw the right-angled triangle separately and label it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you test whether a triangle is right-angled?**
Check whether the squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the longest side. For sides 8, 15 and 17, 64 + 225 = 289 = 17², so the triangle is right-angled.

**How do you label the opposite and adjacent sides in trigonometry?**
Label them from the angle you are working with: the opposite side is across the triangle from it and the adjacent side is next to it but is not the hypotenuse. Re-label whenever you change angle.

**How do you find the angle between a line and a plane in 3D?**
Compare the line with its shadow on the plane, as if a light shone straight down. The line, the shadow and the vertical drop form a right-angled triangle, so use trigonometry on it; this is Higher tier only.

**Do you need the exact value of tan 90° for OCR GCSE Maths?**
No. The OCR GCSE Mathematics J560 specification asks for exact values of tan θ at 0°, 30°, 45° and 60° only, and of sin θ and cos θ at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°.
