Cambridge IGCSE Sociology (0495) introduces the ideas sociologists use
to study society: how we gain our identities, why inequality exists,
how institutions such as the family and education work, and why crime
and deviance occur. Much of the syllabus is framed as questions to
investigate rather than facts to learn, which makes it a good first
encounter with social science. Two written papers, each worth 50%,
make up the assessment: Paper 1 lasts 120 minutes and Paper 2 lasts
105 minutes, and both carry 80 marks. This overview covers the
2025-2027 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has six topic areas, three examined in each paper. Every
area is broken down into key questions.

### Paper 1 – Research Methods, Identity and Inequality

- **Research methods** asks how sociologists approach the study of
  society, what types of data and evidence they use, and how they
  investigate society.
- **Identity: self and society** asks how we learn our identity, how
  society controls us, and what influences our social identity.
- **Social stratification and inequality** asks what social
  stratification is, what attempts have been made to reduce social
  inequalities, and how globalisation has affected inequalities between
  societies.

### Paper 2 – Family, Education and Crime

- **Family** asks what the different types of families are, what role
  the family plays for the individual and society, and how family life
  is changing.
- **Education** asks what education and schooling are, what the roles
  and functions of education are, and what factors help to explain
  differences in educational achievement and experience.
- **Crime, deviance and social control** asks what crime, deviance and
  social control are, what the patterns of and explanations for crime
  and deviance are, and how crime is dealt with in different societies.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Research Methods, Identity and Inequality | 120 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Family, Education and Crime | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Both papers are written exams, externally assessed, and the course is
not tiered. It is a linear qualification, so both papers are sat at the
end of the course.

The two papers handle question choice differently. Paper 1 has a
compulsory Question 1, followed by a choice between Question 2 and
Question 3. Paper 2 offers three optional questions, of which you
answer two. The syllabus is examined in the June and November series.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 is not just "the first half" of the course; it supplies the
concepts and methods that Paper 2 applies.

- **Research methods** underpin everything. When you study family
  change, differences in educational achievement or patterns of crime,
  you need to judge how the evidence was gathered and how reliable it
  is.
- **Identity and social control** connect directly to Paper 2: the
  family and education are major agents of socialisation, and the
  criminal justice system is a formal agent of social control.
- **Stratification and inequality** reappear when you explain why
  educational achievement differs between groups and why some groups
  are more likely to be recorded as offenders.

The compulsory question in Paper 1 also means that research methods
cannot be left to chance; it is the safest area to know thoroughly. In
Paper 2, answering two of three questions gives some choice, but
preparing all three areas keeps your options open on the day.

## Planning your study

A logical order is to begin with research methods, since the
vocabulary of data, sampling and methods is used throughout the course.
After that:

1. Study identity: self and society, which introduces socialisation and
   social control.
2. Move to social stratification and inequality, finishing Paper 1.
3. Turn to the family, then education, both of which build on
   socialisation and inequality.
4. Finish with crime, deviance and social control, which draws on
   identity, inequality and research evidence together.

The key questions themselves make a practical study framework. Turn
each one into a page heading, and under it record the main concepts,
the competing explanations and the evidence for each. By the end of
the course you will have a set of notes that mirrors the structure of
the syllabus exactly, which makes it easy to see which areas are secure
and which still need work.

Throughout the course, keep a glossary of key terms and a short list of
studies or examples for each key question. Start short-answer practice
on each area as it is taught, then move to longer, structured
questions once a full paper's content is complete. Paper-by-paper
technique, including how to manage the question choices, is covered in
the exam preparation guide.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Sociology 0495?**
Two. Paper 1 Research Methods, Identity and Inequality lasts 120 minutes; Paper 2 Family, Education and Crime lasts 105 minutes. Each carries 80 marks and 50% of the qualification.

**Do I have to answer every question in Paper 1?**
No. Question 1 is compulsory, and you then choose either Question 2 or Question 3.

**How many questions do I answer in Paper 2?**
You answer two of the three optional questions.

**Is IGCSE Sociology tiered?**
No. All candidates sit the same two papers.

**When is IGCSE Sociology examined?**
The 2025-2027 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.
