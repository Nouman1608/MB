Condensed notes for subtopic **8.5 Noble gases**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. This subtopic is **Core only**: every candidate needs it. For a fuller explanation with data, read the [noble gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-noble-gases/); for exam-style questions, use the [noble gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-noble-gases-practice/).

## The syllabus statement, part by part

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Group | Group VIII |
| Unreactive | They do not readily react with other substances |
| Monatomic | They exist as single atoms, not molecules |
| Gases | All are gases at room temperature and pressure |
| Explanation | Full outer shell of electrons, so no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Noble gases | Group VIII elements: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn |
| Monatomic | Made of single atoms |
| Diatomic | Made of molecules containing two atoms (H₂, N₂, O₂, Cl₂) |
| Full outer shell | Outer shell holding its maximum number of electrons |
| Electronic configuration | Arrangement of electrons in shells, e.g. 2,8,8 |
| Noble gas configuration | A full outer shell like a noble gas, reached by other atoms when they form ions or covalent bonds |

## Configurations to know

| Element | Proton number | Configuration |
|---|---|---|
| Helium | 2 | 2 |
| Neon | 10 | 2,8 |
| Argon | 18 | 2,8,8 |

Helium: full outer shell with **two** electrons (first shell only). Neon and argon: **eight** outer electrons.

## The explanation chain

1. Atoms react to obtain a full outer shell.
2. Noble gas atoms already have a full outer shell.
3. So they have no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.
4. So they are **unreactive** and do not bond to each other: they are **monatomic**.
5. Only very weak forces between separate atoms → very low boiling points → **gases** at r.t.p.

## Monatomic versus diatomic

| Gas | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Argon | Ar | Full outer shell: no bonding needed |
| Neon | Ne | Full outer shell |
| Chlorine | Cl₂ | 7 outer electrons: shares a pair to reach 8 |
| Hydrogen | H₂ | 1 electron: shares a pair to reach 2 (like helium) |
| Oxygen | O₂ | Diatomic molecules |
| Nitrogen | N₂ | Diatomic molecules |

Particle diagrams: noble gases drawn as **separate single circles**, far apart, random.

## Noble gas configurations in bonding

| Particle | Configuration | Matches |
|---|---|---|
| Li⁺ | 2 | Helium |
| Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ | 2,8 | Neon |
| F⁻, O²⁻ | 2,8 | Neon |
| K⁺, Ca²⁺ | 2,8,8 | Argon |
| Cl⁻, S²⁻ | 2,8,8 | Argon |

## Reading electron-shell diagrams

- Count the shells → period number.
- Count the outer electrons → group number (I–VII).
- A full outer shell (2 in the first shell only, or 8) → Group VIII, noble gas.
- An atom with configuration 2,8,8 is argon; an ion with 2,8,8 could be Cl⁻, S²⁻, K⁺ or Ca²⁺. Check the charge before naming it.

## Model answer

*Describe the noble gases and explain their properties in terms of electronic configuration.* (4 marks)

> The noble gases are unreactive, monatomic gases in Group VIII. Their atoms have a full outer shell of electrons, so they have no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons. This means they do not react with other substances and their atoms do not bond to each other, so they exist as single atoms.

## Noble gases in air

Clean, dry air ≈ 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, remainder **noble gases and carbon dioxide**.

## Data handling (trend skill from 8.1, Extended)

Boiling points (real data): He −269 °C, Ne −246 °C, Ar −186 °C, Kr −153 °C, Xe −108 °C.

- Trend: boiling point **increases** down the group.
- Prediction: radon above −108 °C, still a gas at r.t.p.

## Exam traps

- Helium has **2** outer electrons, not 8.
- "Stable" alone is not an explanation; mention the **full outer shell** and **electrons**.
- Write Ar, Ne, He: never Ar₂.
- Noble gases are made of **atoms**, not molecules.
- They do **not** form ions.
- Being a gas does not explain unreactivity (chlorine is a reactive gas).
- Use **Group VIII**, the syllabus term.
- In "explain" questions, link the configuration to **both** unreactive and monatomic if both are asked.
- A configuration of 2,8,8 with a charge is an **ion** (Cl⁻, K⁺), not an argon atom.
- Give the cause **and** the consequence: full outer shell, **so** no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.

## Self-test

1. Name the group the noble gases are in, using the syllabus term.
2. Give the electronic configuration of argon.
3. How many outer-shell electrons does helium have?
4. What does monatomic mean?
5. Why are noble gases unreactive?
6. Why is argon monatomic but chlorine diatomic?
7. Why are all noble gases gases at room temperature?
8. Which noble gas has the same electronic configuration as a sodium ion?
9. Which noble gas has the same electronic configuration as a chloride ion?
10. Give the formula of neon gas.
11. Name two gases, other than nitrogen and oxygen, present in clean, dry air.
12. *(Extended)* Given the boiling points of krypton (−153 °C) and xenon (−108 °C), predict whether radon's boiling point is higher or lower than xenon's.

**Answers**

1. Group VIII.
2. 2,8,8.
3. Two.
4. Exists as single atoms.
5. They have a full outer shell, so they have no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.
6. Argon has a full outer shell so does not bond; each chlorine atom has seven outer electrons and shares a pair with another chlorine atom to complete its outer shell.
7. The single atoms have only very weak forces between them, so boiling points are very low.
8. Neon (2,8).
9. Argon (2,8,8).
10. Ne.
11. A noble gas (e.g. argon) and carbon dioxide.
12. Higher.
