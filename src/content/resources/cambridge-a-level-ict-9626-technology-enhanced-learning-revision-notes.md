These notes condense **section 12.5 Technology enhanced learning** (Topic 12 IT in society) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. Full explanations are in the [technology enhanced learning study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-technology-enhanced-learning/); test yourself with the [technology enhanced learning practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-technology-enhanced-learning-practice/).

## The three strands

1. Methods of delivery: computer-based training, online tutorials, networked courses, MOOCs, video-conferencing.
2. Impact on student and teacher **motivation**, **achievement** and **autonomy**.
3. Advantages and disadvantages of each method.

## Methods: recall

| Method | What it is | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Computer-based training (CBT) | Interactive software with text, video and quizzes, often installed locally | Own pace; repeatable; records progress; no tutor needed | No personal help; goes out of date; costly to make |
| Online tutorials | Short web lessons or videos on one skill | Free or cheap; any time; repeatable | Variable quality; no feedback; distraction |
| Networked courses | Materials and tasks on a school or company network, often in a virtual learning environment | One place for materials; teacher tracking and feedback; forums | Needs network and servers; set-up cost; home access may be unequal |
| MOOC | Open online course for huge numbers | Low cost; top teachers; flexible | Many do not finish; little support; qualifications may cost extra |
| Video-conferencing | Live video and audio link between places | Real-time questions; no travel; specialist teachers | Needs fast connection; faults; harder to manage a class |

## Impact: recall

| Area | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Student motivation | Interactive, instant feedback, progress charts | Distractions; boredom; isolation; faulty equipment |
| Teacher motivation | Less repeat marking; shared resources; more time for individuals | Time to learn systems and create materials; worry about being replaced |
| Achievement | Quick feedback; repeat until understood; focus on weak topics; wider resources | Poor-quality content; copying; digital divide |
| Autonomy | Choose what, when and how fast; builds independence and research skills | Needs self-discipline; struggling learners may go unnoticed |

**Autonomy:** the learner's control over their own learning.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Virtual learning environment | Online system holding course materials, tasks, quizzes, forums and marks |
| MOOC | Massive open online course |
| Synchronous | Happens at the same time for everyone, such as video-conferencing |
| Asynchronous | Learners study at different times, such as recorded tutorials |
| Digital divide | The gap between those with and without access to IT |
| Peer marking | Learners mark each other's work |

## Quick examples

**Completion.** A MOOC has 5,000 enrolled and 8% finish. Completers = 5,000 × 0.08 = **400**. If it costs £8,000 a year, the cost per completer is £8,000 ÷ 400 = **£20**, but the cost per enrolled learner is only **£1.60**.

**Quiz.** A CBT test has 40 questions, with a pass mark of 65%. Pass mark = 0.65 × 40 = **26** questions.

**Travel.** 12 students each travel 25 miles each way to a weekly class. Weekly travel = 12 × 25 × 2 = **600 miles**.

## Choosing a method

| Scenario | Likely choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Staff safety training repeated yearly | CBT | Consistent, repeatable, progress recorded |
| Learning to use a spreadsheet function today | Online tutorial | Quick, specific, free |
| A school with its own network and tracking needs | Networked course | Teachers can set and mark work |
| Thousands of adult learners worldwide | MOOC | Scale and low cost |
| A rural school needs a specialist teacher | Video-conferencing | Live access without travel |

## Building an evaluate answer

For a six-mark answer recommending a method:

1. State the **needs** from the scenario (number of learners, location, budget, type of learning).
2. Give two **strengths** of the chosen method, each linked to a need.
3. Give one or two **weaknesses**, and say how they could be reduced.
4. Explain briefly why the alternative suits the needs **less well**.
5. Give a clear **recommendation**.

**Example.** A hospital must train 3,000 staff on a new procedure. Computer-based training suits it because everyone gets the same content, staff can study between shifts and results are recorded. Its weakness is that nobody answers questions, so a short live session could follow. A MOOC is less suitable because the content is private and a general public course would not cover the hospital's own procedure.

## Exam traps

- Not saying how the method is delivered.
- Vague points such as "it is easier".
- Only positive effects on motivation.
- Leaving out the teacher.
- Mixing up a MOOC and a networked course.
- Treating autonomy as "working alone" instead of control over the learning.

## Self-test

1. What is computer-based training?
2. Give one disadvantage of online tutorials.
3. What is a virtual learning environment?
4. What is a MOOC?
5. Why do many MOOC learners not finish?
6. Give one advantage of video-conferencing for education.
7. State one way technology can raise student motivation.
8. State one way technology can lower teacher motivation.
9. Explain how instant feedback can improve achievement.
10. What is autonomy in learning?
11. State one risk to learners with high autonomy.
12. A CBT test has 50 questions and a pass mark of 70%. How many correct answers are needed?

### Answers

1. Learning from interactive software, often installed on a computer, with text, video, exercises and quizzes.
2. Any one: quality varies; no feedback on the learner's work; needs internet; easy to be distracted.
3. An online system that holds course materials, tasks, quizzes, forums and marks.
4. A massive open online course: an online course open to very large numbers of learners.
5. Little personal support, so motivation falls; learners need self-discipline.
6. Any one: live questions; no travel; access to specialist teachers.
7. Any one: interactive tasks; instant feedback; progress charts.
8. Any one: time to learn new systems; time to create materials; worry about being replaced.
9. Learners see errors straight away and can correct them and repeat the material until understood.
10. The learner's control over what, when and how fast they learn.
11. Learners without self-discipline may fall behind and nobody notices.
12. 0.70 × 50 = 35 correct answers.
