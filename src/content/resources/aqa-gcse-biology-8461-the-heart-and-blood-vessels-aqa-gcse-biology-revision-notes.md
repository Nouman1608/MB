These are condensed recall notes for **4.2.2.2 The heart and blood vessels** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, Version 1.0, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. It is Paper 1 content at both tiers; nothing is marked (HT only). Worked explanations are in the [heart and blood vessels study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-the-heart-and-blood-vessels-aqa-gcse-biology/); questions are in the [heart and blood vessels practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-the-heart-and-blood-vessels-aqa-gcse-biology-practice/).

## Specification limits

- Vessels: aorta, vena cava, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, coronary arteries only.
- Heart valve names are **not** required; their job is.
- Lungs: trachea, bronchi, alveoli, capillary network only.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Atrium (plural atria) | Upper chamber; receives blood returning to the heart |
| Ventricle | Lower chamber; pumps blood out of the heart |
| Valve | Flap that closes to prevent backflow |
| Double circulatory system | Blood passes through the heart twice per circuit: heart → lungs → heart → body |
| Coronary arteries | Carry oxygenated blood to the heart muscle |
| Pacemaker | Group of cells in the right atrium that controls the natural resting heart rate |
| Artificial pacemaker | Electrical device that corrects irregularities in heart rate |
| Alveoli | Air sacs where gas exchange happens |
| Lumen | The space inside a blood vessel |
| Rate | Quantity ÷ time |

## Route of blood

```
body -> vena cava -> right atrium -> right ventricle -> pulmonary artery -> lungs
lungs -> pulmonary vein -> left atrium -> left ventricle -> aorta -> body
```

- Right side: deoxygenated blood. Left side: oxygenated blood.
- Pulmonary artery: an artery carrying deoxygenated blood. Pulmonary vein: a vein carrying oxygenated blood.
- Arteries and veins are defined by **direction** (away from or towards the heart).

## One heartbeat

1. Atria fill with blood.
2. Atria contract → blood into ventricles.
3. Ventricles contract → blood into pulmonary artery and aorta.
4. Valves close → no backflow.

Wall thickness: atria thinnest; right ventricle thicker; **left ventricle thickest**, because it pumps at high pressure around the whole body.

## Why double circulation?

- Blood loses pressure in the lung capillaries; returning to the heart lets it be pumped to the body at high pressure.
- Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix, so the body receives fully oxygenated blood.

## Lungs: adaptations for gas exchange

| Feature | Effect |
|---|---|
| Millions of alveoli | Large surface area for diffusion |
| Thin alveolus and capillary walls | Short diffusion distance |
| Dense capillary network with flowing blood | Keeps concentration gradients steep |
| Breathing | Fresh air keeps oxygen high and carbon dioxide low in the alveoli |

Oxygen diffuses alveolus → blood; carbon dioxide diffuses blood → alveolus.

## Vessels: structure and function

| Vessel | Function | Structure | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artery | Away from heart, high pressure | Thick wall, muscle and elastic fibres, narrow lumen | Withstands pressure; stretches and recoils with each surge |
| Vein | Back to heart, low pressure | Thinner wall, wide lumen, valves | Easy flow; valves stop backflow |
| Capillary | Exchange with tissues | Wall one cell thick, narrow, very numerous | Short diffusion distance; every cell close to one; slow flow |

Pressure falls from aorta → arteries → capillaries → veins → vena cava; the largest drop is across the capillaries.

## Rate calculations

- Heart rate (beats/min) = beats counted ÷ seconds × 60.
- Volume per minute = volume per beat × beats per minute.
- Flow rate = volume ÷ time; 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³; divide by 60 to go from per minute to per second.
- Speed = distance ÷ time; 1 mm = 1000 µm.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Mini example: 75 cm³ per beat × 80 beats per minute = 6000 cm³/min = 6 dm³/min.

## Exam traps

- "Arteries carry oxygenated blood" is wrong as a definition.
- Both ventricles pump the same volume; the left pumps at higher pressure.
- Capillary walls are "one cell thick", not just "thin".
- Blood is oxygenated in the lungs, not in the heart.
- The pacemaker is in the **right atrium**.
- Comparisons need matched pairs: "whereas".
- Always give the unit with a rate.

## Self-test

1. Which chamber pumps blood to the lungs?
2. Which vessel carries blood from the left ventricle?
3. Why does the left ventricle have the thickest wall?
4. What is the function of the valves in the heart?
5. Where are the cells that control the natural resting heart rate?
6. What do the coronary arteries supply?
7. Give two advantages of a double circulatory system.
8. Name, in order, the structures air passes through from the trachea to the site of gas exchange.
9. Give two ways the alveoli are adapted for gas exchange.
10. Why do veins have valves?
11. A pulse of 18 beats is counted in 15 seconds. Calculate the heart rate.
12. 3.6 dm³ of blood flows through a vessel in one minute. What is this in cm³ per second?

### Answers

1. Right ventricle.
2. Aorta.
3. It pumps blood at high pressure around the whole body (a longer distance than to the lungs).
4. To prevent blood flowing backwards.
5. In the right atrium.
6. Oxygenated blood to the heart muscle.
7. Blood reaches the body at high pressure; oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix.
8. Trachea, bronchi, alveoli.
9. Any two: large surface area (many alveoli); thin walls (short diffusion distance); good blood supply from a capillary network; ventilation keeps gradients steep.
10. Blood is at low pressure in veins, so valves stop it flowing backwards.
11. 18 ÷ 15 × 60 = 72 beats per minute.
12. 3600 cm³ ÷ 60 = 60 cm³ per second.
