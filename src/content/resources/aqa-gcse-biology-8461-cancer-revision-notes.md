## What cancer is

Cancer is the result of **changes in cells** that lead to **uncontrolled growth and division**.

```
change in a cell (e.g. damage to DNA) -> the cell no longer controls when it divides
-> it divides again and again by mitosis -> a mass of abnormal cells (a tumour)
```

Normal cells divide only when new cells are needed for growth or repair. Cancer cells keep dividing.

## Benign vs malignant tumours

| | Benign tumour | Malignant tumour |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A growth of abnormal cells | A growth of abnormal cells that is a **cancer** |
| Where the cells stay | Contained in one area, usually within a membrane | Invade neighbouring tissues |
| Spread | Does not invade other parts of the body | Cells break off, travel in the **blood**, and form **secondary tumours** elsewhere |
| Is it cancer? | No | Yes |

A benign tumour can still be harmful: a large one can press on an organ (for example in the brain). "Benign"
means it does not spread, not that it is always harmless.

### Method in steps: describing how a cancer spreads

```
1. Cells in a malignant tumour divide uncontrollably.
2. They invade the tissue around the tumour.
3. Some cells break away and enter the bloodstream.
4. The blood carries them to other parts of the body.
5. There they settle and divide, forming secondary tumours.
```

## Risk factors for cancer

Scientists have identified **lifestyle** risk factors for various cancers. Some cancers also have **genetic**
risk factors.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Lifestyle | Smoking (lung, mouth and throat cancers); obesity (for example bowel cancer); drinking a lot of alcohol (for example mouth and liver cancers); too much exposure to ultraviolet light from the Sun or sunbeds (skin cancer) |
| Carcinogens in the environment | Ionising radiation (X-rays, gamma rays, radon gas); chemicals such as those in tobacco smoke |
| Genetic | Inheriting certain alleles makes some cancers more likely, for example alleles of the BRCA genes and breast cancer |
| Infection | Some viruses living in cells can trigger cancer, for example HPV and cervical cancer |

A risk factor raises the chance of a cancer. It does not make the cancer certain.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Benign vs malignant:** stays in one area within a membrane vs invades tissue and spreads in the blood.
- **Primary vs secondary tumour:** where the cancer starts vs a new tumour formed by cells that spread.
- **Tumour vs cancer:** malignant tumour cells are cancers, but a benign tumour is not cancer.
- **Lifestyle vs genetic risk factor:** something you do or are exposed to vs alleles you inherit.

## Quick self-test

1. Describe what cancer is.
2. Give two differences between benign and malignant tumours.
3. Which type of tumour cells are cancers?
4. How do malignant tumour cells reach other parts of the body?
5. What is a secondary tumour?
6. Why can a benign tumour still be dangerous?
7. Give two lifestyle risk factors for cancer.
8. Give one example of a genetic risk factor for cancer.

### Answers

1. Changes in cells lead to uncontrolled growth and division.
2. Any two: benign cells stay in one area and malignant cells invade neighbouring tissue; benign tumours are
   usually within a membrane; malignant cells spread in the blood to form secondary tumours, benign do not.
3. Malignant tumour cells.
4. They break away and are carried in the blood.
5. A tumour formed in a different part of the body by cells that spread from a malignant tumour.
6. It can grow large and press on an organ, such as the brain.
7. Any two: smoking; obesity; heavy drinking of alcohol; too much UV exposure.
8. Inheriting certain alleles, for example of the BRCA genes, which raise the risk of breast cancer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "cells divide" instead of **uncontrolled** growth and division.
- Saying benign tumours are cancers, or that they spread.
- Saying malignant cells "move" without naming the **blood** and **secondary tumours**.
- Giving only lifestyle factors when a question asks about genetic risk factors too.
- Saying that a risk factor always causes cancer.
