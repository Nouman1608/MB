## Questions

**1.** The Haber process is N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).

**(a)** Deduce the expression for Kc for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State the units of Kc. **[1]**

**2.** Calcium carbonate decomposes on heating in a closed container: CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g).

**(a)** Deduce the expression for Kc for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State the units of Kc. **[1]**

**3.** H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). At equilibrium at a certain temperature the concentrations are [H₂] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³, [I₂] = 0.15 mol dm⁻³ and [HI] = 0.90 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the value of Kc at this temperature. State its units, if any. **[3]**

**4.** 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). A mixture of 0.80 mol of SO₂ and 0.50 mol of O₂ is sealed in a 4.0 dm³ vessel. At equilibrium the vessel contains 0.60 mol of SO₃.

**(a)** Calculate the amounts, in mol, of SO₂ and O₂ at equilibrium. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the expression for Kc. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the value of Kc and state its units. **[3]**

**5.** Ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O. At a certain temperature Kc = 4.0. At equilibrium a mixture contains 0.30 mol of ethanoic acid, 0.20 mol of ethanol and 0.30 mol of water. Calculate the amount, in mol, of ethyl ethanoate present at equilibrium. **[3]**

**6.** 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g). At a certain temperature Kc = 170 dm³ mol⁻¹. At equilibrium [NO₂] = 0.020 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the equilibrium concentration of N₂O₄, in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**7.** The forward reaction in 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) is exothermic. State whether each change increases, decreases or has no effect on the value of Kc.

**(a)** Increasing the total pressure at constant temperature. **[1]**

**(b)** Adding a catalyst. **[1]**

**(c)** Increasing the temperature. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why the equilibrium yield of ammonia in the Haber process, N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), increases when the total pressure is increased at constant temperature. **[2]**

**9.** N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g). At equilibrium a mixture contains 0.50 mol of N₂O₄ and 1.00 mol of NO₂ at a total pressure of 200 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the mole fraction of each gas in the mixture. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the partial pressure of each gas. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the expression for Kp. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the value of Kp and state its units. **[2]**

**10.** At a certain temperature the equilibrium concentrations in the Haber process are [N₂] = 0.50 mol dm⁻³, [H₂] = 1.50 mol dm⁻³ and [NH₃] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the value of Kc and state its units. **[3]**

## Answers

Marking key: each point is worth **[1]**. **ecf** = error carried forward: a later mark can still be awarded for correct use of an earlier wrong value. Units must be correct to earn a units mark. Square brackets around a formula mean concentration in mol dm⁻³.

**1.**

**(a)** Kc = [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³) **[1]**.

**(b)** **mol⁻² dm⁶** [1]. (Concentration units: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)⁴.)

**2.**

**(a)** Kc = **[CO₂]** [1] (solids are not included).

**(b)** **mol dm⁻³** [1].

**3.** Kc = [HI]² ÷ ([H₂][I₂]) [1]; = 0.90² ÷ (0.20 × 0.15) [1]; = **27.0**, with **no units** because the units cancel [1].

**4.**

**(a)** n(SO₂) = 0.80 − 0.60 = **0.20 mol** [1]; n(O₂) = 0.50 − 0.30 = **0.20 mol** [1] (2 mol of SO₂ react with 1 mol of O₂ to make 2 mol of SO₃).

**(b)** Kc = [SO₃]² ÷ ([SO₂]²[O₂]) [1].

**(c)** Concentrations (÷ 4.0 dm³): [SO₃] = 0.15, [SO₂] = 0.050, [O₂] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³ [1]; Kc = 0.15² ÷ (0.050² × 0.050) = **180** [1]; units **dm³ mol⁻¹** [1]. (Using amounts in mol instead of concentrations gives 45 and loses the first two marks; ecf applies to the units mark.)

**5.** Kc = [CH₃COOC₂H₅][H₂O] ÷ ([CH₃COOH][C₂H₅OH]) [1]; because the total volume V cancels (same number of moles on each side), amounts can be used: 4.0 = (n × 0.30) ÷ (0.30 × 0.20) [1]; n = 4.0 × 0.30 × 0.20 ÷ 0.30 = **0.80 mol** [1].

**6.** Kc = [N₂O₄] ÷ [NO₂]² [1]; [N₂O₄] = 170 × (0.020)² [1]; = **0.068 mol dm⁻³** [1] (accept 6.8 × 10⁻²).

**7.**

**(a)** **No effect** [1].

**(b)** **No effect** [1].

**(c)** **Decreases** [1] (an exothermic forward reaction: raising the temperature moves the position towards the endothermic, reverse, direction, and the value of Kc falls).

**8.** There are 4 mol of gas on the left (1 + 3) and 2 mol on the right [1]; increasing the pressure moves the position of equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas, to reduce the pressure (Le Chatelier's principle), so more ammonia forms [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Total = 1.50 mol; x(N₂O₄) = 0.50 ÷ 1.50 = **0.333** and x(NO₂) = 1.00 ÷ 1.50 = **0.667** [1].

**(b)** p(N₂O₄) = 0.333 × 200 = **66.7 kPa**; p(NO₂) = 0.667 × 200 = **133 kPa** [1].

**(c)** Kp = p(NO₂)² ÷ p(N₂O₄) [1].

**(d)** Kp = 133.3² ÷ 66.67 = **267 kPa** [1 for the value, with ecf from (b)]; units **kPa** [1]. (Keep unrounded partial pressures: 266.7 kPa. Rounded 133 and 66.7 give 265 and lose no mark if working is shown.)

**10.** Kc = [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³) [1]; = 0.30² ÷ (0.50 × 1.50³) = 0.0900 ÷ 1.6875 = **0.0533** [1]; units **mol⁻² dm⁶** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including solids in the Kc expression (question 2) or leaving out a power from the balancing number.
- Using amounts in mol instead of concentrations when the numbers of moles on each side differ (question 4).
- Forgetting units, or writing units for a Kc whose units cancel (question 3).
- Saying a catalyst or a change in pressure alters the value of Kc; only temperature does.
- Using the wrong direction for the position shift when explaining the effect of pressure, or not naming the number of moles of gas on each side.
- Rounding the partial pressures before squaring in a Kp calculation.
