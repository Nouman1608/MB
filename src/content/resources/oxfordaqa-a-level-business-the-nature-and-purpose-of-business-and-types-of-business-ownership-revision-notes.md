## Key terms (9625)

| Term | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Inputs | Land, labour, capital, enterprise |
| Transformation process | What the business does to turn inputs into outputs |
| Outputs | Goods (physical) and services (not physical) |
| Adding value | Output worth more to customers than the input cost |
| Strategy / tactics | Long-term plan / short-term actions |
| Profit | Revenue minus costs |
| Opportunity cost | Next best alternative given up |
| Efficiency | Producing more from unchanged inputs, or the same from less |
| Competitiveness | How well the firm does against rivals |
| Risk | Chance the outcome is worse than planned |
| KPIs | Measures used to track performance against targets |
| Customers / consumers | Those who buy / those who use |
| Owners / managers | Risk-takers who supply capital / people who run the firm day to day |
| Business functions | Marketing, operations, human resources, finance and so on |
| Ethical influences | Views of right and wrong that shape decisions |

## Entrepreneurs

- **Motives (both):** 9725 names financial and non-financial; our examples are income, wealth, independence, passion, flexibility, helping others.
- **Characteristics (both; 9725 names them):** risk taker, resilient, hard working, creative, passionate, innovative, adaptable.
- **Challenges (both):** capital, uncertain demand, cash flow, long hours, personal risk.
- **Role as the firm grows (9725):** from doing everything to delegating and leading strategy.
- **Opportunity (9625):** a gap, an unmet need or a better way of doing something.
- **Environment and government (9625):** finance, tax, regulation, skills and stability shape success. Governments support start-ups for jobs, competition and tax, through grants, loans, tax breaks and advice.
- **Number and type of start-ups (9625):** economic conditions, ease of borrowing, attitudes to risk, skills.
- **Why start-ups fail (9625):** poor cash flow, weak research, too little capital, weak management, underestimated costs.
- **Impact on economy and society (9625):** jobs, incomes, tax, growth, choice; also pollution or lost local trade.

## Objectives, SMART and plans

| Point | Notes |
|---|---|
| Typical objectives | Survival, profit, growth, cash flow, social (both); personal and ethical (9625) |
| SMART (9725 wording) | Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific |
| Value of objectives | Direction, measuring success, motivation, basis for choosing |
| Plan elements (9725) | Ownership, objectives, target market and market analysis, competitor analysis, USP, financial analysis |
| Benefits of a plan | Clear thinking, early warning of weakness, helps win finance |
| Limitations | Time-consuming, forecasts can be wrong, goes out of date |
| Competitiveness (both; 9725 adds meaning and significance) | Ability to win customers against rivals |

**Method: writing a SMART objective.** Pick a measure, give a target number, add a deadline, name who is responsible, and check it is realistic.

## Decision trees (9625)

| Item | Standard form |
|---|---|
| Expected value | Sum of (probability x outcome) for each branch |
| Net gain | Expected value minus cost of that option |

**Method in steps.** (1) Draw squares for decisions and circles for chance events. (2) Check probabilities on each circle add to 1. (3) Work out expected value for each option. (4) Subtract that option's cost. (5) Compare net gains. (6) Comment on risk, the worst case and how reliable the probabilities are.

Worked reminder. Cost £15,000. 0.4 chance of £50,000 and 0.6 chance of £10,000. EV = 20,000 + 6,000 = £26,000. Net gain = 26,000 - 15,000 = £11,000.

**Limitations:** estimated probabilities, no non-financial factors, average outcome may never occur.

## Types of business

| Type | Owner and liability | Key point | Spec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | One owner, unlimited | Full control and profit; limited finance | both |
| Partnership | Two or more, usually unlimited | More capital and skills; disputes | 9725 |
| Ltd | Limited, shares not public | Can raise capital privately | both |
| plc | Limited, shares public | Large funds; scrutiny and takeover risk | both |
| Social enterprise / charity / non-profit | Social aim; surplus reinvested | Lower profit | both |
| Co-operative | Owned by members | Profit shared among members | 9625 |

- **Unlimited liability:** personal assets can pay business debts. **Limited liability:** the shareholder's loss is capped at the sum put in.
- **Compare using:** objectives, sources of finance, distribution of profits, liability (both); control (9725).
- **Influences on the business population (9625, our wording):** economy, ease of finance, regulation, attitudes to risk, skills.
- **Change of form:** a sole trader may become a Ltd for limited liability and investors; a Ltd may become a plc to raise large sums, accepting more regulation and loss of control.
- **Private sector:** in the hands of individuals or shareholders. **Public sector:** government owns and runs it. A plc is private sector.
- **Sectors:** primary (extract), secondary (make), tertiary (serve).
- **Family business:** loyalty and long-term view against conflict and succession problems.
- **Small business:** flexible, personal service against limited finance and buying power. **SMEs versus large (9625):** large firms gain economies of scale and finance but can be slower and less personal.

## Shareholders

```
Dividend per share = total dividend / number of shares
Dividend yield (%) = dividend per share / share price x 100
Market capitalisation = share price x number of shares issued
```

These are the standard forms (9725 asks for all three calculations; 9625 names yield calculation and market capitalisation). Worked reminder: 5,000,000 shares and a total dividend of £400,000 give 8p per share; at a price of £2.00 the yield is 4%. Why invest: dividends, rise in share price, a say in decisions, backing the firm's aims. **Share capital (equity)** is money raised by selling shares. **Share price** moves with profit and forecasts, dividends, the economy, interest rates, rivals, news, and demand for and supply of the shares. A fall can bring takeover risk and weaker confidence.

## Stakeholders

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Owners/shareholders, employees | Customers, suppliers, community, government, creditors (9725) |

For any change, name the stakeholder, state the effect and say whether it helps or harms. Interests often conflict, for example shareholders want dividends while employees want pay.

## Must-know distinctions

- Strategy (long-term plan) against tactics (short-term actions).
- Customer (buys) against consumer (uses).
- Owner (risk and capital) against manager (daily running).
- Plc (a type of company) against public sector (government-run).
- Dividend per share (money per share) against dividend yield (percentage of price) against market capitalisation (total value of the shares).
- Expected value (before cost) against net gain (after cost).

## Quick self-test

1. Define opportunity cost.
2. List the four inputs.
3. State four key elements of a business plan (9725).
4. A project costs £15,000. It has a 0.4 chance of returning £50,000 and a 0.6 chance of returning £10,000. Calculate the net gain.
5. A company has 2,000,000 shares and pays a total dividend of £120,000. Its share price is £1.50. Calculate the dividend per share, dividend yield and market capitalisation.
6. A company has 6,000,000 shares priced at 80p. Calculate its market capitalisation.
7. A dividend per share of 12p on a price of £2.40 gives what yield?
8. Classify a quarry, a bakery making loaves and a bank by sector.
9. Which type of business is listed only in 9725, and which only in 9625?
10. Why is a plc not part of the public sector?

### Answers

1. The value of the best alternative forgone.
2. Land, labour, capital, enterprise.
3. Any four of ownership, objectives, target market and market analysis, competitor analysis, USP, financial analysis.
4. EV = 0.4 x 50,000 + 0.6 x 10,000 = 20,000 + 6,000 = £26,000. Net gain = 26,000 - 15,000 = **£11,000**.
5. Dividend per share = 120,000 / 2,000,000 = **6p**. Yield = 0.06 / 1.50 x 100 = **4%**. Market capitalisation = 1.50 x 2,000,000 = **£3,000,000**.
6. 0.80 x 6,000,000 = **£4,800,000**.
7. 0.12 / 2.40 x 100 = **5%**.
8. Quarry primary, bakery secondary, bank tertiary.
9. Partnerships are in 9725 only. Co-operatives are in 9625 only.
10. A plc is owned by private shareholders, not by government, so it is private sector.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping at expected value and forgetting to subtract the cost to find net gain.
- Writing "limited liability protects the business" instead of saying the shareholder's loss is limited to the amount invested.
- Giving a list of stakeholders without saying how a named decision affects each.
- Describing a business plan's benefits with no limitation, or the reverse.
- Quoting yield as pence rather than a percentage, or mixing pence and pounds in a calculation.
- Mixing up customers and consumers when the case study separates them.
- Giving generic advantages of a company form with no tie to the case business.
- Using partnerships or co-operatives without checking which specification you follow.
- Making a recommendation with no reason for the choice and no condition on which it depends.
