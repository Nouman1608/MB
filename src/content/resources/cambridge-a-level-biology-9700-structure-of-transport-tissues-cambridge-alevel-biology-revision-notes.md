These notes condense sub-topic **7.1 Structure of transport tissues** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, with drawing skills used on Paper 3. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [structure of transport tissues study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-structure-of-transport-tissues-cambridge-alevel-biology/); for exam-style questions, use the [structure of transport tissues practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-structure-of-transport-tissues-cambridge-alevel-biology-practice/).

## The four outcomes in one line each

1. **Draw** plan diagrams of TS stem, root and leaf of herbaceous dicots.
2. **Describe** where xylem and phloem are in each.
3. **Draw and label** xylem vessel elements, sieve tube elements and companion cells from slides, photomicrographs and electron micrographs.
4. **Relate** the structure of those three cells to their functions.

## Where the vascular tissue is

| Organ (TS) | Pattern | Xylem | Phloem | Centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stem | Separate bundles in a ring near the outside | Inner side of each bundle | Outer side of each bundle | Pith (soft tissue) |
| Root | One central vascular core inside the endodermis | Star or cross shape in the middle | In groups between the xylem arms | Xylem; no pith |
| Leaf | Midrib bundle plus a network of smaller veins | Towards the upper surface | Towards the lower surface | Not applicable |

### Identifying an organ quickly

- Cuticle, mesophyll, flattened shape → **leaf**.
- Ring of bundles round a pith → **stem**.
- Single central core, star-shaped xylem, possible root hairs → **root**.
- Leaf orientation: palisade and xylem both face the **upper** side.

### Why that distribution (useful for "suggest")

- Stem: lignified tissue near the edge resists **bending**.
- Root: central core resists **pulling** and sits just inside the endodermis.
- Leaf: branching veins keep every mesophyll cell **close to a vein**.

## Plan diagram rules

- Tissue outlines only: **no cells**, no shading.
- **Correct proportions**: measure with the eyepiece graticule first.
- Single, clear, continuous lines.
- Show every bundle in a stem in its true position.
- Ruled label lines that touch the tissue and do not cross.
- Use at least half the space provided.

### Worked reminder: scaling a measurement

A stem is 50 epu across and its pith 22 epu across. You draw the stem 120 mm wide.

```
scale = 120 ÷ 50 = 2.4 mm per epu
pith on drawing = 22 × 2.4 = 52.8 mm ≈ 53 mm
```

## The three cells

| Feature | Xylem vessel element | Sieve tube element | Companion cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living? | No (dead when mature) | Yes | Yes |
| Nucleus | None | None | Present, large |
| Cytoplasm | None | Thin layer at the edge | Dense |
| Wall | Thickened with **lignin**; **pits** | Cellulose, thin | Cellulose, thin |
| End walls | Absent or reduced | **Sieve plates** with pores | Normal |
| Notable organelles | None | Few | Many **mitochondria** and **ribosomes** |
| Connection | Pits to neighbouring vessels | **Plasmodesmata** to companion cell | Plasmodesmata to sieve tube element |

### Feature → function pairs

- No contents, no end walls → **continuous, low-resistance column** of water.
- Lignin → **resists collapse under tension**; waterproofs; supports the plant.
- Pits → **lateral movement** of water; route round an air-blocked vessel.
- Narrow lumen → more water in contact with walls, so **adhesion** helps.
- Sieve plates → sap passes between elements; end walls still add strength.
- Sieve tube element nearly empty → **little resistance to mass flow**.
- Companion cell mitochondria → **ATP for proton pumps** in loading.
- Companion cell nucleus and ribosomes → **make proteins** for the sieve tube element.
- Plasmodesmata → sucrose passes **directly** into the sieve tube element.

## What each microscope shows

- **Light micrograph, TS**: xylem as large, thick-walled, empty outlines; phloem as smaller, thin-walled cells with tiny dense companion cells.
- **Light micrograph, LS**: lignin as rings, spirals or a network; sieve plates across the phloem.
- **Electron micrograph**: organelles visible, so this is where you show companion cell mitochondria, ribosomes and plasmodesmata, and the peripheral cytoplasm of the sieve tube element.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Herbaceous | Non-woody; only primary tissues present |
| Dicotyledonous | Flowering plant with two seed leaves; vascular bundles in a ring in the stem |
| Plan diagram | Low-power drawing showing tissue outlines and their proportions only |
| Vascular bundle | A strand containing xylem and phloem |
| Xylem vessel element | One dead, lignified cell of a xylem vessel |
| Lignin | Strong, waterproof polymer thickening xylem walls |
| Pit | Area of a xylem wall without lignin, letting water move sideways |
| Sieve tube element | One living, enucleate cell of a sieve tube |
| Sieve plate | Perforated end wall between sieve tube elements |
| Companion cell | Cell beside a sieve tube element that loads it and keeps it alive |
| Plasmodesma | Strand of cytoplasm through a cell wall linking two cells |

## Exam traps

- Drawing cells in a plan diagram, or shading tissues.
- Proportions guessed rather than measured.
- Root drawn with a ring of bundles; stem with a central core.
- Phloem placed above xylem in a leaf vein.
- "Xylem cells are living" or "sieve tube elements have a nucleus".
- "Lignin lets water through": water moves through pits, where lignin is absent.
- Giving a feature without its function in a "relate" question.
- Mixing mm and µm in magnification calculations.

## Self-test

1. Name the tissue at the centre of a dicot stem.
2. Describe the position of phloem in a dicot root.
3. In a leaf vein, which tissue lies nearer the upper surface?
4. Give two rules for a plan diagram.
5. Explain why xylem vessels do not collapse under tension.
6. State the function of pits.
7. Explain why sieve tube elements have little cytoplasm.
8. Why must a sieve tube element be a living cell?
9. Explain the large number of mitochondria in companion cells.
10. Which type of micrograph would you need to see plasmodesmata?
11. A scale bar of 50 µm measures 20 mm. Calculate the magnification.
12. A section has one central vascular core with star-shaped xylem. Name the organ.

### Answers

1. Pith.
2. In separate groups between the arms of the star-shaped xylem, in the central vascular core.
3. Xylem.
4. Any two: no individual cells; correct proportions; no shading; clear continuous lines; ruled label lines.
5. Their walls are thickened with lignin, which is strong and resists being pulled inwards.
6. Let water move sideways between vessels and to surrounding cells, including around a blocked vessel.
7. A nearly empty lumen gives little resistance to the mass flow of sap.
8. Its cell surface membrane must keep sucrose in and hold a high hydrostatic pressure.
9. They supply ATP for the proton pumps used to load sucrose.
10. An electron micrograph.
11. 20 mm = 20 000 µm; 20 000 ÷ 50 = **× 400**.
12. A root.
