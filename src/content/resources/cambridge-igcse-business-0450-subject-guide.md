Cambridge IGCSE Business introduces how businesses start, grow, organise
people, market their products, run their operations and manage their
finances, and how outside forces shape their decisions. The qualification
is changing code: 2026 is the last examination year for syllabus 0450,
and its replacement, 0264 (Cambridge IGCSE Business), is first assessed
in 2027 and covers the 2027-2029 series. Under both codes the assessment
is the same: two papers, each lasting 90 minutes, carrying 80 marks and
worth 50%.

## Which syllabus applies to you

| | 0450 | 0264 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status | Outgoing (legacy teach-out) | Current |
| First assessment | 2026 | 2027 |
| Final assessment | 2026 | Syllabus covers 2027, 2028 and 2029 |

If you are starting the course now, you will follow 0264, since 0450's
final examination year is 2026. The 0264 syllabus is offered in the June
and November series and in the March series in India, and the March 2027
India series is its first sitting. Cambridge has changed the syllabus name
as well as the code. Some Marlbridge pages were written against 0450 and
carry that code in their titles; the topic structure set out below is
taken from the 0264 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

The 0264 syllabus has six topics.

1. **Understanding business activity:** business activity, economic
   sectors, enterprise, business growth and size, types of business
   organisation, and business and stakeholder objectives.
2. **People in business:** human resource management, organisation and
   management, methods of communication and motivating employees.
3. **Marketing:** marketing and the market, market research, the
   marketing mix and legal controls.
4. **Operations management:** production of goods and services, the role
   of technology and sustainability in production, costs, scale of
   production and break-even analysis, quality, and location decisions.
5. **Financial information and decisions:** business finance, cash-flow
   forecasts, profit and loss, the statement of financial position and
   analysis of accounts.
6. **External influences on business activity:** economic issues, business
   and the international economy, business and the environment, ethical
   issues, and pressure groups.

Topic 1 sets up the language of the subject, Topics 2 to 5 cover the four
main business functions, and Topic 6 places the business in its wider
economic, environmental and ethical context.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Short Answer and Data Response | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Case Study | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |

All candidates take both papers, and both are externally assessed. The
course is not tiered, and 0264 candidates are eligible for grades A* to
G. Paper 1 has four questions mixing short answers with structured data
responses. Paper 2 has four questions based on a case study provided as
an insert with the paper. All questions on both papers are compulsory,
and the assessment is linear, with both papers sat in the same series.

## How the parts fit together

Because there are no optional questions and both papers range across the
syllabus, every topic matters. The topics also interlock. A location
decision in Topic 4 depends on costs and finance from Topic 5, affects
recruitment in Topic 2 and may be shaped by economic and environmental
issues in Topic 6. Break-even analysis links operations to finance, and
cash-flow forecasts link finance to day-to-day decisions about growth.

The two papers reward slightly different habits. Paper 1's data-response
questions ask you to work with information presented to you, so numerical
skills from the finance topic matter. Paper 2's case study asks you to
apply your knowledge to one business's situation and make judgements
about it. Building both habits steadily across all six topics works
better than treating them as separate skills to learn at the end.

## Planning your study

Topic 1 makes a natural starting point because its terms are used
everywhere else. After that, one sensible sequence is:

- **First year:** Topics 1 to 3, then begin Topic 4, keeping a running
  glossary of key terms.
- **Second year:** finish Topic 4, then Topics 5 and 6, with regular
  short calculation practice for cash flow, break-even and the analysis of
  accounts.
- **Final months:** revision of all six topics with a focus on applying
  ideas to unfamiliar businesses.

Try past-paper questions for each topic as soon as it is complete, and
note that 0450 papers remain useful practice because the paper structure
is the same. Leave full timed papers and paper-by-paper technique for the
revision period, using the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between IGCSE Business 0450 and 0264?**
0264 is the new code and name for Cambridge IGCSE Business from first assessment in 2027. 2026 is the last examination year for 0450. The two papers keep the same titles, durations, marks and weightings.

**Which code should I follow if I start the course now?**
0264, because 0450's final examination year is 2026. The 0264 syllabus covers exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029.

**Is Cambridge IGCSE Business tiered?**
No. All candidates take both papers, and 0264 candidates are eligible for grades A* to G.

**What is in the Paper 2 case study?**
Paper 2 has four compulsory questions based on a case study provided as an insert with the paper. It lasts 90 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50%.

**How many topics are in IGCSE Business?**
The 0264 syllabus has six topics, from Understanding business activity to External influences on business activity.
