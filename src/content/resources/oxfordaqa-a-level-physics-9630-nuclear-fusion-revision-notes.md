## Equations for the estimate

| Step | Equation |
|---|---|
| Radius of each nucleus | R = R₀A^(1/3), R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm |
| Separation at contact | r = R₁ + R₂ |
| Coulomb barrier | E_p = Z₁Z₂e²/(4πε₀r) |
| Energy each nucleus needs (equal, head-on) | E_p/2 |
| Temperature estimate | ³⁄₂kT = E_p/2, so T = E_p/3k |
| Energy released | (mass before − mass after) × 931.5 MeV u⁻¹ |

## Condensed recall

- Fusion: two light nuclei join to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy because binding energy per nucleon increases.
- Nuclei repel electrically. They must approach to within about the sum of their radii for the strong interaction to act.
- The barrier is proportional to Z₁Z₂, so nuclei with one proton (hydrogen isotopes) are easiest to fuse.
- **Suitable reactor fuels:** deuterium and tritium. Lowest barrier, large energy release (17.6 MeV), deuterium plentiful in water; tritium must be bred.
- Barrier estimates give temperatures of about 10⁹ K for hydrogen isotopes. Actual temperatures are lower (Sun's core about 1.5 × 10⁷ K; reactor plasmas about 10⁸ K) because some nuclei have far more than the average energy and quantum tunnelling lets some fuse below the barrier.
- **Plasma:** fully ionised gas of nuclei and free electrons.
- D–T fusion gives about 3.4 × 10¹⁴ J per kg of fuel, roughly four times as much as fission of uranium-235.
- **Solar hydrogen cycle:** ¹H + ¹H → ²H + e⁺ + ν; ²H + ¹H → ³He + γ; ³He + ³He → ⁴He + 2¹H. Overall 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν, releasing 26.7 MeV including positron annihilation.
- Step 3 fuses two nuclei of charge +2e, so it has the highest barrier in the cycle.

## Problems for a practical reactor

| Problem | Physics behind it |
|---|---|
| Very high temperature (about 10⁸ K) | Needed to overcome the Coulomb barrier at a useful rate |
| Confinement | No solid wall survives contact; magnetic fields hold the charged plasma |
| Density and confinement time | Energy out must exceed energy used to heat and confine |
| Neutron damage | Neutrons are uncharged, escape the field, damage the structure and make it radioactive |
| Tritium supply | Tritium is rare and radioactive; bred from lithium: ⁶Li + n → ⁴He + ³H |
| Energy extraction | Neutron energy is absorbed in a surrounding blanket as heat |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nuclear fusion | Joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier one |
| Coulomb barrier | Electric potential energy of two nuclei at the separation where the strong interaction takes over |
| Plasma | Ionised gas of nuclei and free electrons |
| Deuterium | Hydrogen-2, one proton and one neutron |
| Tritium | Hydrogen-3, one proton and two neutrons; radioactive |
| Magnetic confinement | Holding a plasma away from the walls using magnetic fields |
| Breeding | Producing tritium by neutron absorption in lithium |

## Exam traps

- Whole barrier energy given to one nucleus.
- Radius taken as R₀A, or only one nucleus's radius used for r.
- Charge of helium-3 taken as e instead of 2e.
- Claiming the Sun's core is as hot as the simple estimate.
- Solar cycle written as one step.
- Magnetic fields said to confine neutrons.
- Kinetic energy written as kT instead of ³⁄₂kT.

## Quick self-test

1. Why must nuclei have high kinetic energies to fuse?
2. Give two reasons deuterium and tritium are chosen as reactor fuels.
3. What is a plasma?
4. Estimate the Coulomb barrier, in MeV, for a proton and a tritium nucleus touching.
5. A nucleus in a plasma has mean kinetic energy 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ J. Estimate the plasma temperature.
6. Write the overall equation for the solar hydrogen cycle.
7. Why does the third step of the hydrogen cycle have the highest barrier?
8. Give two reasons fusion occurs in the Sun at about 1.5 × 10⁷ K.
9. Convert the 17.6 MeV released in D–T fusion into joules.
10. Why are neutrons a problem in a fusion reactor?
11. How can tritium be produced?
12. Why is the plasma confined by magnetic fields and not by a container wall?

### Answers

1. They repel electrically and must get close enough, about the sum of their radii, for the strong interaction to act.
2. Each has charge +e, so the barrier is lowest; D–T releases a large energy; deuterium is plentiful in water (any two).
3. A gas of fully ionised atoms: bare nuclei and free electrons.
4. r = 1.2 × (1 + 3^(1/3)) = 2.93 fm; E_p = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/2.93 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 7.9 × 10⁻¹⁴ J = 0.49 MeV.
5. T = 2E/3k = 2 × 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁴/(3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³) = 1.4 × 10⁹ K.
6. 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν (plus γ photons).
7. It fuses two helium-3 nuclei, each of charge +2e, so Z₁Z₂ = 4 instead of 1.
8. Some nuclei have energies far above the mean (energy spread), and quantum tunnelling allows fusion below the barrier.
9. 17.6 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 2.8 × 10⁻¹² J.
10. They are uncharged, so magnetic fields cannot hold them; they damage the structure and make it radioactive.
11. Neutrons strike lithium-6 in a blanket: ⁶Li + n → ⁴He + ³H.
12. Any wall touching the plasma would cool it and be damaged; charged particles spiral round magnetic field lines and are kept away from the walls.
