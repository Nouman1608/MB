## Questions

**1.** *(Extended)* A used car is bought for $18 500. Its value falls by 12% each year. Calculate its value after 4 years, giving your answer to the nearest dollar. **[3]**

**2.** *(Extended)* The population of a town is 42 000. It increases by 3.5% each year.

**(a)** Calculate the population after 6 years. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the number of complete years after which the population first exceeds 60 000. **[2]**

**3.** *(Extended)* A machine is bought for $24 000. After 5 years its value is 15 000 dollars. The value falls by the same percentage each year. Calculate this annual percentage decrease. **[3]**

**4.** *(Extended)* Simplify √72 + √50 − √18, giving your answer in the form k√2. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Rationalise the denominator and simplify 14 / (3 + √2). **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Expand and simplify (2 + √3)(5 − 2√3). **[2]**

**7.** *(Extended)* Without a calculator, show that √12 × √27 = 18. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* Amira invests $7 500 in a savings account. The account pays 2.8% compound interest each year.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the investment after 5 years. Give your answer to the nearest dollar. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the smallest whole number of years after which the investment is worth more than $9 000. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A culture of bacteria grows by 15% every hour. After 3 hours there are 12 000 bacteria. Calculate the number of bacteria at the start. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[3]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A rectangle has area (6 + 2√3) cm² and width (3 − √3) cm.

**(a)** Find the length of the rectangle in the form a + b√3, where a and b are integers. Show your working. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the rectangle in the form c + d√3. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Farrow Ltd, a fictional cleaning company, buys a floor-polishing machine for $38 000. Its value falls by 15% each year.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the machine after 3 years. Give your answer to the nearest dollar. **[2]**

**(b)** The company will sell the machine when its value first falls below $12 000. Find, with working, the number of years the company keeps it. **[3]**

**(c)** A second machine loses 9% of its value each year. After 4 years it is worth $22 400. Calculate its original price, to the nearest dollar. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)*

**(a)** Show that (3 + √2)² = 11 + 6√2. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that 1/(3 − √2) + 1/(3 + √2) = 6/7. **[3]**

**(c)** Write (√2 + 1)/(√2 − 1) in the form p + q√2, where p and q are integers. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Multiplier for a 12% decrease = 0.88 [1]. Value = 18 500 × 0.88⁴ [1] = 11 094.36…, so the value is **$11 094** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Taking 12% of the original price four times gives $9 620. Each year's 12% comes off the previous year's value, so use the repeated multiplier.

**2.** **(a)** 42 000 × 1.035⁶ [1] = 51 628.7… = **51 600** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** After 10 years: 42 000 × 1.035¹⁰ = 59 245, which is not above 60 000. After 11 years: 42 000 × 1.035¹¹ = 61 319 [1]. So the population first exceeds 60 000 after **11 years** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A 3.5% increase has multiplier 1.035, not 1.35 or 0.035. In "how many years" parts, show the year before and the year after the target so the method mark is secure.

**3.** 24 000 × m⁵ = 15 000, so m⁵ = 15 000 ÷ 24 000 = 0.625 [1]. m = 0.625^(1/5) = 0.9103… [1]. Annual decrease = 1 − 0.9103… = 0.0897…, so **8.97%** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Dividing the total fall of 37.5% by 5 gives 7.5% and scores nothing. The decrease is compound, so you need the fifth root of the overall multiplier.

**4.** √72 = 6√2, √50 = 5√2, √18 = 3√2 [1]. 6√2 + 5√2 − 3√2 = **8√2** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Writing √(72 + 50 − 18) = √104 loses both marks. Simplify each surd to a multiple of the same root before you add or subtract.

**5.** Multiply numerator and denominator by (3 − √2) [1]. Denominator: (3 + √2)(3 − √2) = 9 − 2 = 7 [1]. 14(3 − √2) ÷ 7 = 2(3 − √2) = **6 − 2√2** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Multiplying by (3 + √2) or by √2 alone leaves a surd in the denominator. Change the sign between the terms to get the conjugate, then cancel the 7 with the 14 before expanding.

**6.** 2 × 5 − 4√3 + 5√3 − 2√3 × √3 gives 10 − 4√3 + 5√3 − 6 [1], which simplifies to **4 + √3** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Treating √3 × √3 as 9, or leaving it as √9, loses the accuracy mark. √3 × √3 = 3, so −2√3 × √3 = −6.

**7.** √12 = 2√3 and √27 = 3√3 [1]. 2√3 × 3√3 = 6 × 3 = **18** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question the final line must be the stated value, with the working that reaches it. Writing √324 = 18 straight from a calculator does not show the method.

**8.** **(a)** 7 500 × 1.028⁵ [1] = 8 610.47…, so **$8 610** [1].

**(b)** After 6 years: 8 851.56; after 7 years: 8 851.56 × 1.028 = 9 099.41 [1]. The smallest whole number of years is **7** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Checking only the year that gives more than $9 000 loses the method mark. Showing the value for the year before as well proves that 7 is the smallest.

**9.** Multiplier = 1.15, so initial number × 1.15³ = 12 000 [1]. Initial number = 12 000 ÷ 1.15³ [1] = 7 890.19…, so **7 890** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Reverse problems need division by the multiplier. Multiplying 12 000 by 0.85³ is a common slip, because a 15% fall followed by a 15% rise does not undo itself.

**10.** **(a)** Length = area ÷ width = (6 + 2√3)/(3 − √3). Multiply top and bottom by (3 + √3) [1]. Numerator: (6 + 2√3)(3 + √3) = 18 + 6√3 + 6√3 + 6 = 24 + 12√3 [1]. Denominator: 9 − 3 = 6 [1]. Length = **4 + 2√3 cm** [1].

**(b)** Length + width = (4 + 2√3) + (3 − √3) = 7 + √3 [1]. Perimeter = 2(7 + √3) = **14 + 2√3 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In part (b), doubling only the 7 and forgetting the surd term is an easy slip. Add the two sides first, then double the whole bracket.

**11.** **(a)** 38 000 × 0.85³ [1] = 23 336.75, so **$23 337** [1].

**(b)** After 7 years: 38 000 × 0.85⁷ = 12 181.93, which is not below 12 000 dollars. After 8 years: 38 000 × 0.85⁸ = 10 354.64 [1]. The value first falls below 12 000 dollars during the 8th year, so the machine is kept for **8 years** [1] with the value at the end of 7 years and 8 years both shown [1].

**(c)** Original price × 0.91⁴ = 22 400 [1]. Original price = 22 400 ÷ 0.91⁴ [1] = 32 664.98…, so **$32 665** [1].

*Examiner insight:* This question rewards a clear sequence: multiplier, equation, solution. A wrong multiplier in (a) can still earn follow-through marks in (b) if the working is set out.

**12.** **(a)** (3 + √2)(3 + √2) = 9 + 3√2 + 3√2 + 2 [1] = **11 + 6√2** [1].

**(b)** Common denominator is (3 − √2)(3 + √2) = 9 − 2 = 7 [1]. Numerator: (3 + √2) + (3 − √2) [1] = 6, so the sum is **6/7** [1].

**(c)** Multiply top and bottom by (√2 + 1) [1]. Numerator: (√2 + 1)² = 2 + 2√2 + 1 = 3 + 2√2, and denominator: (√2 − 1)(√2 + 1) = 2 − 1 = 1 [1]. The result is **3 + 2√2**, so p = 3 and q = 2 [1].

*Examiner insight:* In "show that" questions, every line of working carries the mark, so do not skip the expansion. In (c) the denominator becomes 1, which is a good check that you used the correct conjugate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using simple (linear) change instead of a repeated multiplier for growth and decay.
- Writing the wrong multiplier, such as 0.12 instead of 0.88 for a 12% decrease.
- Stopping at the first year that looks close in "how many years" questions instead of checking the year before as well.
- Dividing a total percentage change by the number of years instead of taking a root of the overall multiplier.
- Reversing a percentage change by applying the opposite percentage instead of dividing by the multiplier.
- Adding surds with different roots, or not simplifying each surd fully first.
- Forgetting to use the conjugate when rationalising a denominator of the form a + √b.
- Making sign or coefficient slips when expanding brackets with surds, such as treating √3 × √3 as 9.
- Giving a rounded decimal when the question asks for an exact answer in surd form.
