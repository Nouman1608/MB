## Questions

**1.** *(Extended)* A used car is bought for $18 500. Its value falls by 12% each year. Calculate its value after 4 years, giving your answer to the nearest dollar. **[3]**

**2.** *(Extended)* The population of a town is 42 000. It increases by 3.5% each year.

**(a)** Calculate the population after 6 years. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

**(b)** Find the number of complete years after which the population first exceeds 60 000. **[4]**

**3.** *(Extended)* A machine is bought for $24 000. After 5 years its value is $15 000. The value falls by the same percentage each year. Calculate this annual percentage decrease. **[3]**

**4.** *(Extended)* Simplify √72 + √50 − √18, giving your answer in the form k√2. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Rationalise the denominator and simplify 14 / (3 + √2). **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Expand and simplify (2 + √3)(5 − 2√3). **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** *(Extended)* Multiplier for a 12% decrease = 0.88 [1]. Value = 18 500 × 0.88⁴ [1] = 11 094.36… so the value is **$11 094** [1].

*Common mistake:* Subtracting 12% of the original price four times (simple decrease) gives $9620, which is wrong: each year's 12% is taken from the previous year's value.

**2.** *(Extended)* **(a)** 42 000 × 1.035⁶ [1] = 51 628.7… = **51 600** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** After 10 years: 42 000 × 1.035¹⁰ = 59 245 (not yet above 60 000); after 11 years: 42 000 × 1.035¹¹ = 61 319 [1]. So the population first exceeds 60 000 after **11 years** [1].

*Common mistake:* Using 0.35 or 1.35 as the multiplier. A 3.5% increase means multiplying by 1.035 each year.

**3.** *(Extended)* 24 000 × m⁵ = 15 000, so m⁵ = 15 000 ÷ 24 000 = 0.625 [1]. m = 0.625^(1/5) = 0.9103 [1]. Annual decrease = 1 − 0.9103 = 0.0897, so **8.97%** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Common mistake:* Dividing the total fall of 37.5% by 5 to get 7.5% per year. That treats the decrease as simple, not compound; take the fifth root of the overall multiplier instead.

**4.** *(Extended)* √72 = 6√2, √50 = 5√2, √18 = 3√2 [1]. 6√2 + 5√2 − 3√2 = **8√2** [1].

*Common mistake:* Writing √72 + √50 − √18 as √(72 + 50 − 18) = √104. Surds can only be added or subtracted once they are written as multiples of the same root.

**5.** *(Extended)* Multiply numerator and denominator by (3 − √2) [1]. Denominator: (3 + √2)(3 − √2) = 9 − 2 = 7 [1]. 14(3 − √2) ÷ 7 = 2(3 − √2) = **6 − 2√2** [1].

*Common mistake:* Multiplying by (3 + √2) or by √2 alone. Use the conjugate (change the sign between the terms) so the surd disappears from the denominator.

**6.** *(Extended)* 2 × 5 − 4√3 + 5√3 − 2 × 3 [1] = 10 − 6 + √3 = **4 + √3** [1].

*Common mistake:* Treating √3 × √3 as 9 or as √9 left unsimplified. √3 × √3 = 3, so −2√3 × √3 = −6.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using simple (linear) change instead of a repeated multiplier for growth and decay.
- Writing the wrong multiplier, such as 0.12 instead of 0.88 for a 12% decrease.
- Stopping at the first year that looks close in "how many years" questions instead of checking the years either side.
- Adding surds with different roots, or not simplifying each surd fully first.
- Forgetting to use the conjugate when rationalising a denominator of the form a + √b.
