## Short-answer questions

Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** Which is a correct form of the accounting equation?

A: Assets = Liabilities − Owner's equity

B: Owner's equity = Assets + Liabilities

C: Liabilities = Assets − Owner's equity

D: Assets + Liabilities = Owner's equity

**[1]**

**2.** How is a bank overdraft classified?

A: Current asset

B: Current liability

C: Non-current liability

D: Owner's equity

**[1]**

**3.** Calculate the missing figure in each case.

**(a)** Assets $72,000; liabilities $18,500; owner's equity ? **[1]**

**(b)** Assets ?; liabilities $9,300; owner's equity $40,700 **[1]**

**(c)** Assets $33,600; liabilities ?; owner's equity $26,950 **[1]**

**4.** Classify each item as a non-current asset, current asset, current liability or non-current liability.

**(a)** Shop fittings. **[1]**

**(b)** Amounts owed by credit customers. **[1]**

**(c)** A bank loan repayable in eight years. **[1]**

**(d)** Amounts owed to suppliers for goods bought on credit. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why drawings reduce owner's equity but are not treated as an expense. **[2]**

**6.** State the effect on total assets, total liabilities and owner's equity of each transaction.

**(a)** A delivery van is bought for $12,000, paid by bank transfer. **[1]**

**(b)** The owner pays an extra $5,000 of her own money into the business bank account. **[1]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Jasper's business has the following balances.

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Premises | 60,000 |
| Equipment | 14,500 |
| Inventory | 8,300 |
| Trade receivables | 5,200 |
| Cash | 650 |
| Trade payables | 6,400 |
| Bank overdraft | 2,350 |
| Bank loan repayable in five years | 20,000 |

**(a)** Calculate total assets. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate total liabilities. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate owner's equity. **[1]**

**8.** A business starts the month with assets of $20,000, liabilities of $5,000 and owner's equity of $15,000. Copy and complete a table showing total assets, total liabilities and owner's equity after each transaction.

(i) Inventory is bought on credit for $2,000.

(ii) Rent of $800 is paid by bank transfer.

(iii) Goods that cost $1,200 are sold on credit for $1,900.

(iv) A bank loan of $4,000 is received.

(v) The owner withdraws $500 from the bank for personal use.

(vi) A supplier is paid $1,500.

**[6]**

**9.** At the start of the year, Rosewood Florists had assets of $56,000 and liabilities of $14,000. At the end of the year it had assets of $63,200 and liabilities of $16,700. During the year the owner took drawings of $12,000 and introduced extra capital of $5,000. Calculate the profit for the year, showing your workings. **[5]**

**10.** Answer both parts using the accounting equation.

**(a)** The owner of a shop says that buying a new till for $900 cash has made the business "$900 richer". Explain whether she is right. **[3]**

**(b)** The owner's home electricity bill of $240 was paid from the business bank account. Explain the effect on the equation and how it should be recorded. **[3]**

**11.** At the start of the year, Owen's equity was $24,000. At the end of the year it was $19,800. He took drawings of $3,000 and introduced no new capital.

**(a)** Calculate the profit or loss for the year. **[3]**

**(b)** Comment on what these figures show Owen. **[2]**

**12.** A student calculated a trader's owner's equity as follows: assets of equipment $9,000, inventory $3,200, bank overdraft $1,500 and trade receivables $2,100, a total of $15,800; less liabilities of trade payables $2,700; owner's equity $13,100.

**(a)** Identify the student's error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct owner's equity. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the student's answer is wrong by twice the overdraft. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Rearranging Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity gives this form. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. The business owes the bank and the overdraft is repayable on demand. **[1]**

**3. (a)** 72,000 − 18,500 = **$53,500** [1].

**(b)** 9,300 + 40,700 = **$50,000** [1].

**(c)** 33,600 − 26,950 = **$6,650** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Non-current asset [1].

**(b)** Current asset [1].

**(c)** Non-current liability [1].

**(d)** Current liability [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Drawings are resources the owner takes for private use, so they reduce the owner's claim on the business [1]. They are not a cost of running the business or earning revenue, so they do not reduce profit (business entity) [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Total assets unchanged (van +$12,000, bank −$12,000); liabilities and equity unchanged [1].

**(b)** Assets + $5,000; owner's equity + $5,000; liabilities unchanged [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 60,000 + 14,500 + 8,300 + 5,200 + 650 [1] = **$88,650** [1].

**(b)** 6,400 + 2,350 + 20,000 = **$28,750** [1].

**(c)** 88,650 − 28,750 = **$59,900** [1]. **[4]**

**8.** One mark per correct row.

| | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | 22,000 | 7,000 | 15,000 [1] |
| (ii) | 21,200 | 7,000 | 14,200 [1] |
| (iii) | 21,900 | 7,000 | 14,900 [1] |
| (iv) | 25,900 | 11,000 | 14,900 [1] |
| (v) | 25,400 | 11,000 | 14,400 [1] |
| (vi) | 23,900 | 9,500 | 14,400 [1] |

Check: 23,900 = 9,500 + 14,400. **[6]**

**9.** Opening equity 56,000 − 14,000 = 42,000 [1]. Closing equity 63,200 − 16,700 = 46,500 [1]. Increase 4,500 [1]. Profit = 4,500 + 12,000 drawings − 5,000 capital introduced [1] = **$11,500** [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** She is not right [1]. The till (an asset) increases by $900 and cash (an asset) decreases by $900 [1], so total assets, liabilities and owner's equity are unchanged; the business is no richer [1].

**(b)** Assets (bank) fall by $240 and owner's equity falls by $240 [1]. It is the owner's private expense, so under the business entity concept [1] it is recorded as drawings, not as an electricity expense of the business [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** 19,800 − 24,000 + 3,000 [1] = −1,200 [1], so a **loss of $1,200** [1].

**(b)** The business made a loss, and Owen still took $3,000 out, so his equity fell by $4,200 [1]. He needs to make the business profitable or reduce drawings, or his stake will keep shrinking [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** The bank overdraft has been treated as an asset instead of a liability [1].

**(b)** Assets 9,000 + 3,200 + 2,100 = 14,300 [1]; liabilities 2,700 + 1,500 = 4,200 [1]; owner's equity **$10,100** [1].

**(c)** Including the overdraft as an asset added $1,500 that should not be there, and leaving it out of liabilities failed to deduct $1,500, so equity was overstated by $3,000 [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating a bank overdraft as an asset.
- Treating drawings as an expense.
- Forgetting that a sale at a profit increases owner's equity.
- Adding capital introduced to profit rather than removing it.
- Leaving out workings in calculations.
