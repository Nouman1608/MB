Amounts are in dollars ($).

## The four syllabus points

1. The importance of matching costs and revenues.
2. Applying the matching / accruals concepts using accruals and pre-payments.
3. Journal entries and ledger accounts for accrued and prepaid expenses.
4. Journal entries and ledger accounts for accrued and prepaid incomes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Matching / accruals concept | Revenue earned and costs incurred in a period are recorded in that period, whatever the cash date | — |
| Accrued expense | Service used, not yet paid | Other payables (current liability) |
| Prepaid expense | Paid now, benefit in a later period | Other receivables (current asset) |
| Accrued income | Earned, not yet received | Other receivables (current asset) |
| Income received in advance | Received now, earned in a later period | Other payables (current liability) |
| Time-apportionment | Splitting an amount by months: amount × months in the year ÷ months covered | — |

## Which side is the balance?

| Item | Carried down (c/d) on | Brought down (b/d) on |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Debit | Credit |
| Prepaid expense | Credit | Debit |
| Accrued income | Credit | Debit |
| Income received in advance | Debit | Credit |

Memory aid: anything the business **owes** comes down as a **credit**;
anything **owed to** or **paid ahead by** the business comes down as a
**debit**.

## The two formulas

```
Expense charged = paid + closing accrued − opening accrued
                       − closing prepaid + opening prepaid

Income credited = received + closing accrued − opening accrued
                           − closing in advance + opening in advance
```

Rearrange either one to find a missing cash figure.

## Year-end journals

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense account | Other payables |
| Prepaid expense | Other receivables | Expense account |
| Accrued income | Other receivables | Income account |
| Income received in advance | Income account | Other payables |

At the start of the next year the balance is brought down in the same expense
or income account, so it is automatically set against next year's cash.

## Quick worked reminders

**Accrued expense.** Wages paid 18,400; owing at the start 520; owing at the end
610. Charge = 18,400 + 610 − 520 = **18,490**.

**Prepaid expense.** Year end 31 December. Rates of 2,400 paid on 1 October 2028
for twelve months. Charge for 2028 from this payment = 2,400 × 3/12 = 600;
prepaid = **1,800**.

**Accrued income.** A 15,000 deposit earns 4% a year: 600 for the year. Only 450
was received, so **150** is accrued income (other receivables).

**Income in advance.** Rent received 5,500 includes 500 for next year. Income
credited = **5,000**; other payables **500**.

**Mixed balances.** Commission received 2,950; accrued at the start 180; received
in advance at the end 240. Income = 2,950 − 180 − 240 = **2,530**.

## Effects of errors

| Error | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense omitted | Overstated by the accrual | Other payables understated |
| Prepaid expense omitted | Understated by the prepayment | Other receivables understated |
| Accrued income omitted | Understated by the amount | Other receivables understated |
| Income in advance omitted | Overstated by the amount | Other payables understated |
| Accrual treated as a prepayment | Overstated by twice the amount | Receivables overstated and payables understated by the amount |

Equity (capital) moves by the same amount as profit.

## Exam traps

- **Payment date is not the start date.** A premium paid on 15 March for the
  year from 1 April covers April to March.
- **Opening accruals reduce this year's charge.** The cash paid this year
  partly settled last year's bill.
- **Opening prepayments increase it.** Last year's cash bought this year's
  benefit.
- **Keep "other" separate from "trade".** Accrued rent is not a trade payable.
- **Income accounts are mirror images.** An accrued income is a debit balance,
  even though the account is normally a credit account.
- **Running balance format** still needs the accrual or prepayment entered
  before the transfer to profit or loss, so the balance falls to nil and the
  b/d figure appears on the first day of the next year.
- **"Explain"** questions on matching need the link to a correct profit for the
  period, not a definition alone.

## Self-test

1. State the concept that requires accruals and prepayments.
2. Insurance of 960 is paid on 1 September 2028 for twelve months. The year end is
   31 December 2028. How much is prepaid?
3. Give the journal entry for accrued electricity of 270.
4. Rent receivable of 400 is owed by a tenant at the year end. Where does it
   appear in the statement of financial position?
5. Telephone paid 1,320; owing at start 90; owing at end 140. Calculate the charge.
6. On which side is a prepaid expense brought down?
7. Interest received 780; 120 was accrued at the start of the year and 60 is
   accrued at the end. Calculate the income for the year.
8. The statement of profit or loss shows rent of 9,600. Rent was prepaid 800 at the
   start and 1,000 at the end. Calculate the rent paid.
9. An accrued expense of 350 was omitted. State the effect on profit.
10. Subscriptions to a trade magazine of 180 a year were paid two years in advance
    on the first day of the year. State the prepayment at the end of the first year.
11. Why are other payables shown separately from trade payables?
12. An income received in advance of 500 was recorded as accrued income. State the
    effect on profit.

### Answers

1. The matching / accruals concept.
2. 960 × 8/12 = **640** (January to August 2029).
3. Debit Electricity 270; credit Other payables 270.
4. Current assets, under other receivables.
5. 1,320 + 140 − 90 = **1,370**.
6. The debit side.
7. 780 − 120 + 60 = **720**.
8. Paid = 9,600 − 800 + 1,000 = **9,800**.
9. Profit is overstated by 350.
10. **180**: one year's subscription relates to the second year.
11. They do not arise from buying goods on credit, so showing them separately
    keeps the trade figures (and ratios such as trade payables turnover) accurate.
12. Profit is overstated by **1,000** (500 should have been deducted and 500
    was added instead).
