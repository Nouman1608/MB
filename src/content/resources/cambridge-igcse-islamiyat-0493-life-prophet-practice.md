**How to use this set.** In Paper 1, one or two of Questions 3–5 are set on this topic, each worth
14 marks: a knowledge part (a) worth 10 and an understanding part (b) worth 4. Section A checks
details; Section B gives full exam-style questions.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Apart from violence, state **three** ways the Quraysh tried to stop the Prophet (pbuh)
preaching. **[3]**

**2.** Describe what happened while the Prophet (pbuh) and Abu Bakr (RA) were in the cave of Thawr. **[3]**

**3.** Give **three** provisions of the Constitution of Madina. **[3]**

**4.** In 2 AH the qibla was changed. State from where to where, and explain **one** reason it mattered
to the Muslims. **[3]**

**5.** Name the commander of the archers at Uhud and state the order they were given. **[2]**

**6.** Give **two** examples of the Prophet's (pbuh) respect for women, and explain what each shows. **[4]**

**7.** After the Treaty of Hudaybiyya, the Prophet (pbuh) wrote to rulers of other states. Name
**two** of them and explain what this shows about his approach to other states. **[3]**

## Section B — Exam-style questions

**8. (a)** Describe the Hijra, from the pledges of Aqaba to the Prophet's (pbuh) arrival in Madina. **[10]**
**(b)** What does the Hijra teach Muslims about combining trust in God with careful planning? **[4]**

**9. (a)** Give an account of the Battle of Uhud. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain **two** lessons Muslims today can learn from the events of Uhud. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Describe the Battle of the Trench. **[10]**
**(b)** What does the Prophet's (pbuh) conduct during the siege teach leaders today? **[4]**

**11. (a)** Describe the Farewell Pilgrimage and the main teachings of the Farewell Sermon. **[10]**
**(b)** In your opinion, which teaching of the sermon is most needed in the world today? Give reasons. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Describe how the Prophet (pbuh) organised the community in Madina in the first two years
after the Hijra. **[10]**
**(b)** How can the Constitution of Madina guide Muslims who live in countries with people of many
faiths? **[4]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Levels of response: part (a) Level 4 = 8–10, Level 3 = 5–7, Level 2 =
3–4, Level 1 = 1–2; part (b) Level 2 = 3–4, Level 1 = 1–2. Each developed point below earns [2];
credit the best five in (a) (maximum 10) and the best two in (b) (maximum 4); in a "your opinion" part
(b), a clear judgement supported by two developed reasons reaches the top level. Other accurate
points of the same quality earn the same credit.

**1.** Any three, [1] each: mocking him as a poet, sorcerer or madman; offering him wealth, leadership
or kingship through Utba ibn Rabi'a; pressing Abu Talib to withdraw protection; boycotting the clan of
Hashim; sending envoys to the Negus to have the emigrants returned. Maximum 3.

**2.** They hid there for three days while the Quraysh searched for them [1]. When pursuers came close,
Abu Bakr (RA) feared they would be seen, and the Prophet (pbuh) told him not to grieve because God was with
them (Sura 9.40) [1]. Abu Bakr's (RA) son Abdullah brought news, Asma brought food and Amir ibn Fuhayra
grazed sheep over their tracks [1].

**3.** Any three, [1] each: Muslims, Jews and others formed one community; the Jewish tribes kept
their own religion; all would defend Madina together against attack; disputes were referred to the
Prophet (pbuh); no group would help the Quraysh. Maximum 3.

**4.** From Jerusalem to the Ka'ba in Makka [1]. Any one, [2]: it gave the Muslims a distinct identity
as a community linked to Ibrahim's house; it tested who would obey the Prophet (pbuh); it pointed
them towards Makka, which they would later return to. Maximum 3.

**5.** Abdullah ibn Jubayr [1]; not to leave the hill whatever happened, whether the Muslims were
winning or losing [1].

**6.** Any two, [2] each: he trusted and loved Khadija (RA), who was a successful trader, showing respect
for women's work and advice; he followed Umm Salama's (RA) advice at Hudaybiyya, showing that women's
counsel should be heard; he helped with work at home, showing that household tasks are shared; in the
Farewell Sermon he told men to treat their wives well, showing that wives have rights. Maximum 4.

**7.** Any two, [1] each: Heraclius, the Byzantine emperor; the Persian emperor; the Negus of
Abyssinia; the ruler of Egypt. Maximum 2. It shows he invited other states by peaceful letters and
diplomacy, not only by force [1].

**8. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- At the first pledge of Aqaba (621) twelve men from Yathrib accepted Islam, and Mus'ab ibn Umayr (RA) was
  sent to teach them; at the second (622) over seventy promised to protect the Prophet (pbuh) [2].
- The Muslims of Makka migrated to Yathrib in small groups, leaving homes and property [2].
- The Quraysh met and agreed that a young man from each clan would strike him together, so that
  blame would be shared [2].
- Ali (RA) slept in the Prophet's (pbuh) bed and stayed to return goods held in trust, while the Prophet (pbuh)
  left with Abu Bakr (RA) [2].
- They hid three days in the cave of Thawr; the Prophet (pbuh) said "God is with us" when pursuers
  came close [2].
- On the road Suraqa ibn Malik chased them for a reward, but his horse stumbled and he turned back [2].
- They stopped at Quba, where the first mosque was built, then entered Madina, where the Prophet (pbuh)
  (pbuh) stayed with Abu Ayyub al-Ansari [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- The Prophet (pbuh) arranged a guide, camels, a hiding place and someone to cover the tracks, so
  Muslims should plan carefully and use all lawful means [2].
- At the cave he relied on God when planning could do no more, so Muslims stay calm when outcomes are
  beyond their control [2].
- He left only when commanded, so decisions should be made with prayer as well as reasoning [2].

**9. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- In 3 AH the Quraysh, seeking revenge for Badr, marched on Madina with about 3,000 men [2].
- After consulting, the Prophet (pbuh) went out; Abdullah ibn Ubayy withdrew about a third of the
  army, leaving about 700 [2].
- Fifty archers under Abdullah ibn Jubayr were placed on a hill with orders not to leave [2].
- The Muslims gained the upper hand and the Makkans began to flee [2].
- Most archers left to collect spoils; Khalid ibn al-Walid's cavalry attacked from behind [2].
- Hamza (RA) was killed; the Prophet (pbuh) was wounded and a rumour of his death spread [2].
- The Muslims regrouped on the mountain; the Quraysh withdrew without taking Madina [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- Disobeying a clear instruction harmed everyone, so Muslims should follow lawful leadership [2].
- The desire for wealth caused defeat, so Muslims should keep worldly gain in its place [2].
- The Companions stood by the Prophet (pbuh) when wounded, so loyalty is tested in hardship [2].

**10. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- In 5 AH the Quraysh and allied tribes formed a confederate army of about 10,000 to destroy Madina [2].
- Salman al-Farsi (RA) advised digging a trench across the open side of the city, as was done in Persia [2].
- The Prophet (pbuh) dug alongside the Companions in cold weather and hunger [2].
- The cavalry could not cross; the siege lasted weeks with only small clashes [2].
- The Banu Qurayza broke their treaty, putting Madina in danger from within [2].
- Divisions among the enemy and a fierce cold wind made them abandon the siege [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- He accepted advice from a Persian Companion, so leaders should listen to good ideas from anyone [2].
- He shared the work and hunger, so leaders should not ask others to bear what they will not [2].
- He kept up the morale of his people, so leaders should give hope in a crisis [2].

**11. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- In 10 AH the Prophet (pbuh) led a very large number of Muslims on hajj, the only hajj he performed
  after the Hijra [2].
- At Arafat he gave the sermon, declaring lives, property and honour sacred, like that day and month [2].
- He abolished usury and the blood revenge of pre-Islamic times, beginning with his own family's claims [2].
- He told men to treat women well, as they have rights over each other [2].
- He taught that all Muslims are brothers and that no Arab is superior to a non-Arab, nor white to
  black, except in righteousness [2].
- He said he left them the Book of God, which would keep them from going astray, and asked "Have I
  conveyed the message?", calling God to witness [2].
- Sura 5.3, "This day I have perfected your religion", was revealed at this time [2].
**(b)** A clear judgement [1] supported by reasons [3] (maximum 4). For example: equality of races [1],
because racism still divides societies [1], because the Prophet (pbuh) made righteousness the only
measure of worth [1], and because Muslims of every race standing together on hajj show it can be
lived [1].

**12. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- He built the Prophet's (pbuh) mosque, used for prayer, teaching, meeting delegations and decisions [2].
- He paired each Emigrant with a Helper in brotherhood, sharing homes, work and wealth [2].
- He agreed the Constitution of Madina, making Muslims, Jews and others one community with shared
  defence [2].
- He set up the call to prayer, with Bilal (RA) as the first mu'adhdhin [2].
- The qibla was changed to the Ka'ba and fasting in Ramadan was made obligatory in 2 AH [2].
- He settled disputes between the Aws and Khazraj, uniting tribes that had fought each other [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- It gave people of other faiths freedom of religion, so Muslims should respect their neighbours'
  right to worship [2].
- All groups shared duties, such as defence, so Muslims should be loyal citizens who keep the law [2].
- Disputes went to an agreed authority, so differences should be settled through lawful courts [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the whole life when the question names one event.
- Uhud: forgetting the archers or the withdrawal of Abdullah ibn Ubayy.
- The Hijra: stopping at the cave and leaving out Quba and the arrival in Madina.
- Part (b): repeating events instead of giving lessons.
