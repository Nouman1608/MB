This set covers optional topic 3.1.4, **The Mughal Empire (c1526–c1850s)**, examined in Section C of
Paper 1 as the alternative to Islam in the subcontinent. Questions cover the three key developments
in the specification: the earlier Mughal emperors (c1526–1658), Aurangzeb's empire (c1658–1707),
and the decline and downfall of the empire (1707–c1850s). The short recall items check knowledge;
Questions 8 to 13 use the 4, 6 and 9-mark tariffs of the board's specimen paper.

Learn the content from the [Mughal Empire study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-mughal-empire/)
and the [Mughal Empire revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-mughal-empire-revision-notes/).

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** Name the battle of 1526 in which Babur defeated Sultan Ibrahim Lodi. **[1]**

**2.** Name the Afghan ruler who drove Humayun out of India in 1540. **[1]**

**3.** Name the ruler of Persia who sacked Delhi in 1739. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** features of Babur's character. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** cultural developments during the reign of Jahangir. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** reasons why trade and commerce grew in the Mughal Empire before 1658. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** problems faced by Aurangzeb's successors. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why Babur was able to defeat Ibrahim Lodi. **[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** ways in which Aurangzeb tried to govern the empire according to
the Sharia. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** ways in which the expansion of the Marathas weakened the Mughal Empire. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** reasons why Nadir Shah's invasion (1739) was important in the decline of the
Mughal Empire. **[6]**

**12.** Which was the more important reason for British expansion in the subcontinent between the
1750s and the 1850s?

- British military victories
- the weakness and division of Indian rulers

Explain your answer with reference to both bullet points. **[9]**

**13.** "Akbar's reign was the high point of the Mughal Empire before 1658."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer. **[9]**

## Answers

Recall and "state" items score 1 mark per correct answer. "Identify and explain" items score 1 for
each point and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and 9-mark questions are marked by levels, set out
after the answers. Credit other accurate points.

**1.** **The First Battle of Panipat** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **Sher Shah Suri** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** **Nadir Shah** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Two features, 1 mark each: he was a determined and skilled military leader who kept fighting
after losing his homeland of Fergana [1]; he was cultured, writing his memoir, the *Baburnama*, and
poetry [1]. Also credit: he loved gardens and laid them out wherever he settled. **[2]**

**5.** Two developments, 1 mark each: Mughal painting flourished, especially detailed studies of
birds, animals and plants [1]; Jahangir wrote a memoir, the *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri* [1]. Also credit:
Nur Jahan's patronage of buildings and gardens, such as the tomb of her father, Itimad-ud-Daula, at
Agra. **[2]**

**6.** Two reasons, 1 mark each: a large, unified empire with secure roads and rest-houses made it
safer for merchants to move goods [1]; a reliable silver currency made buying and selling easier [1].
Also credit: European trading companies were allowed to trade at ports such as Surat; demand
abroad for Indian cotton textiles. **[2]**

**7.** Two problems, 1 mark each: frequent wars of succession between princes [1]; powerful nobles,
such as the Sayyid brothers, who controlled emperors [1]. Also credit: an empty treasury after the
Deccan wars; Maratha and Sikh expansion; foreign invasion. **[2]**

**8.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Babur used field artillery and matchlock guns [1], which Ibrahim Lodi's army, relying on
  elephants and cavalry, could not match, and the noise panicked the elephants [1];
- Babur's tactics were better [1]: he protected his centre with carts tied together and used
  mounted archers to attack the enemy's flanks and rear, so his smaller army surrounded a larger one
  [1].

Also credit: the Lodi nobles were divided, and some, such as Daulat Khan Lodi, had invited Babur to
India. **[4]**

**9.** Two ways, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- he sponsored the compilation of the *Fatawa-e-Alamgiri* [1], a collection of Islamic legal rulings
  for judges to use across the empire [1];
- he appointed *muhtasibs* [1], officials who supervised public morality and checked that markets and
  conduct followed Islamic rules [1].

Also credit: he removed taxes that he regarded as un-Islamic; he reimposed the jizya in 1679; he
reduced spending on music and display at court. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Drain on resources:* Aurangzeb spent his last decades fighting the Marathas in the Deccan, and the
  cost emptied the treasury his successors inherited.
- *Loss of land and revenue:* in the 18th century the Marathas, led by the Peshwas, expanded into
  Malwa, Gujarat and the north, collecting their own taxes (*chauth*) from Mughal lands, so revenue
  that should have gone to Delhi went to them.
- *Loss of prestige:* Maratha forces raided as far as Delhi, showing that the emperor could no longer
  protect his capital; until their defeat by Ahmad Shah Abdali at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
  they were the strongest power in India. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Military humiliation:* Nadir Shah defeated Emperor Muhammad Shah's army at Karnal, showing that
  the Mughal army could no longer defend the empire.
- *Loss of wealth:* he sacked Delhi and carried away huge treasure, including the Peacock Throne,
  leaving the empire poorer and less able to pay soldiers and officials.
- *Encouraging others:* the empire's weakness was exposed, encouraging later invasions, such as those
  of Ahmad Shah Abdali, and governors who began to act independently. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Answers must address both bullet points. Indicative
content:
- *Military victories:* Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) gave the East India Company control of Bengal,
  and in 1765 it gained the right to collect Bengal's revenue (the *diwani*); later wars defeated
  Mysore (Tipu Sultan was killed in 1799) and the Marathas; Sindh was annexed in 1843 and the Punjab
  in 1849 after the Anglo-Sikh wars.
- *Weakness and division:* the Mughal emperor had little real power; Indian rulers fought each other
  and some allied with the British against rivals; Mir Jafar's betrayal decided Plassey; the
  subsidiary alliance system made rulers dependent on Company troops; after Ranjit Singh's death in
  1839 the Sikh kingdom was weakened by quarrels.
- *Judgement:* for example, division among Indian rulers was more important, because many British
  victories depended on allies and betrayals. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* Akbar expanded the empire across north India, Gujarat, Bengal and Kashmir; the
  *mansabdari* system and Raja Todar Mal's revenue reforms gave the empire a lasting structure; his
  alliances with Rajput rulers and religious tolerance won wide support; Fatehpur Sikri showed his
  ambition.
- *Disagree:* Shah Jahan's reign saw the greatest wealth and the finest architecture, including the
  Taj Mahal, the Red Fort and the Jama Masjid in Delhi; Jahangir's reign was a high point for
  painting; Akbar's religious ideas, such as the *Din-i-Ilahi*, were opposed by scholars such as Ahmad
  Sirhindi.
- *Judgement:* for example, Akbar's reign was the high point for the strength of government, Shah
  Jahan's for wealth and culture. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the three battles of Panipat: 1526 (Babur), 1556 (Akbar), 1761 (Abdali against the
  Marathas).
- Writing about Plassey in answers on Nadir Shah: they are separate causes of decline.
- Listing Akbar's achievements without comparing them with another reign in a "high point" question.
- Describing British battles without explaining why the British kept winning.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far into the 19th century does this topic go?**
To the 1850s, so British expansion up to the annexation of Awadh (1856) is relevant.

**Do I need to know Mughal rulers after Aurangzeb by name?**
Know Muhammad Shah (Nadir Shah's invasion) and Bahadur Shah Zafar (the last emperor).
