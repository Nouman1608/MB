## The specification in brief

- **What customer needs are:** price, quality, choice, convenience.
- **The importance of identifying and understanding customers:** generating sales, business survival.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Customer | A person or organisation that buys from a business |
| Customer need | What a customer wants from a purchase |
| Price (as a need) | Paying an amount the customer sees as fair for what they get; not always the lowest |
| Quality (as a need) | The product or service meets or exceeds what the customer expects |
| Choice | A range of options, such as sizes, flavours, styles or times |
| Convenience | Buying is quick and easy: location, opening hours, delivery, online ordering |
| Repeat customer | A customer who buys again because their needs were met |
| Trade-off | Improving one need makes another harder to meet, often by raising costs |

## Condensed recall

### Signals in a case study

| If the case mentions... | The key need is probably... |
|---|---|
| Students, tight budgets, "value", rivals cheaper | Price |
| Complaints, reviews, special occasions, durability | Quality |
| Different tastes, sizes, dietary requirements | Choice |
| Busy workers, commuters, late hours, delivery | Convenience |

### Trade-offs
- Better quality, more choice or more convenience usually raise costs.
- Higher costs push prices up, which conflicts with the price need.
- A business picks the needs its target customers value most.

### Needs vary
- Between customer groups (for example locals and tourists).
- For one customer in different situations (a quick lunch against a celebration meal).
- Over time, as tastes, incomes and technology change.

### Why it matters: two chains to learn
1. **Generating sales:** know the need → offer what customers want → more customers buy and return → higher sales revenue.
2. **Business survival:** misjudge the need → unsold stock and low sales → losses → risk of closure, especially for a new business with little cash.

## Data skills for this item

- Percentage of a group = number in category ÷ group total × 100.
- Check that percentages in a group add to 100%.
- Compare groups separately when the case gives them; a combined figure can hide differences.
- Mini example: of 80 gym members asked, 28 rank convenience first. 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35%.

## Mini case for recall: Rowan Tree Nursery

Rowan Tree is a children's nursery near a train station. Most parents commute to work in the city.

- **Convenience:** parents need it to open by 7am and close after their train home, so opening hours matter most.
- **Quality:** parents want qualified, caring staff; a poor review could cost several families at once.
- **Price:** fees matter, but many parents will pay more for hours that fit their working day.
- **Choice:** a choice of full or part days helps parents who work part-time.
- **Generating sales:** offering 7am to 7pm care could attract commuters from other nurseries.
- **Survival:** if a rival opens with longer hours, Rowan Tree could lose families it relies on.

## Answer frame

These are suggested sentence starters, not specification wording.

- "The customers in the case are ..., so the need that matters most is ..."
- "Meeting this need would ..., which increases sales because ..."
- "However, meeting it raises costs because ..., so the business must ..."

## Exam traps

- Writing a list of all four needs when the question asks for one in context.
- Assuming all customers want the lowest price.
- Saying a business should improve every need without mentioning cost.
- Ignoring the case evidence about who the customers are.
- Saying understanding customers "guarantees" sales; it makes sales more likely.
- Forgetting that needs change, so research must be repeated.

## Self-test

1. Name the four customer needs in the specification.
2. Give the two reasons the specification gives for identifying and understanding customers.
3. Why is "price" as a need not the same as "the lowest price"?
4. Give one example of convenience for a takeaway.
5. Why might offering more choice increase a business's costs?
6. A commuter coffee kiosk opens at 6am. Which need is it meeting?
7. Of 120 customers, 42 say quality matters most. Calculate the percentage.
8. Explain one trade-off between quality and price.
9. Why might a business find that one customer has different needs at different times?
10. Explain how meeting customer needs can lead to repeat customers.
11. Why is understanding customers especially important for a new business?
12. Why should a business keep checking customer needs after it has opened?

### Answers

1. Price, quality, choice, convenience.
2. Generating sales; business survival.
3. Customers want a fair price for what they get. Some will pay more for better quality or convenience.
4. Any one: online ordering, delivery, late opening, a location near customers, fast service.
5. It must buy and store more items, and some may not sell, creating waste.
6. Convenience.
7. 42 ÷ 120 × 100 = 35%.
8. Higher-quality materials cost more, so the business must charge a higher price, which may put off price-sensitive customers.
9. Their situation changes, for example needing speed on a workday but quality for a special occasion.
10. Customers whose needs are met are satisfied, so they are more likely to buy again and recommend the business, raising future sales.
11. It has little cash and no regular customers, so a product that misses customers' needs can quickly cause losses and closure.
12. Needs change with tastes, incomes and technology; checking lets the business adapt before sales fall.
