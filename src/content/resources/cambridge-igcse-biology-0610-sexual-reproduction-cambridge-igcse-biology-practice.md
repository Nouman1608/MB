## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes fertilisation? **[1]**

A: the transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma

B: the fusion of the nuclei of gametes

C: the division of a zygote to form an embryo

D: the release of an egg cell from an ovary

**2.** Complete the sentence using the correct words.

Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of the ________ of two ________ to form a ________. **[3]**

**3.** State where the male gamete and the female gamete are found in a flowering plant. **[2]**

**4.** Two puppies have the same mother and the same father, but they have different coat patterns. Explain, in terms of gametes, why they are genetically different. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State whether each of these nuclei is haploid or diploid.

**(a)** the nucleus of a sperm cell **[1]**

**(b)** the nucleus of a zygote **[1]**

**(c)** the nucleus of a leaf cell **[1]**

**(d)** the female gamete nucleus in an ovule **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Cabbage plants have 18 chromosomes in each body cell.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in the nucleus of a cabbage pollen grain. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in a cabbage zygote. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** These events happen during sexual reproduction in an animal, but they are not in the correct order.

P: the zygote divides to form an embryo

Q: the nuclei of the two gametes fuse

R: gametes are produced by the parents

S: a sperm cell reaches an egg cell

**(a)** Write the letters in the correct order. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the letter that shows fertilisation. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* The gametes of an animal species each contain 12 chromosomes.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a body cell of this species. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what would happen to the chromosome number over several generations if gametes contained the same number of chromosomes as body cells. **[2]**

**(c)** State the term used for a nucleus, such as the nucleus of a zygote, that contains two sets of chromosomes. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A plant breeder has two varieties of wheat. Variety A gives a high yield but is killed by a fungal disease. Variety B gives a low yield but is resistant to the disease.

**(a)** Explain how sexual reproduction could be used to produce wheat plants with a high yield and resistance to the disease. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the offspring of the cross between A and B are not all the same. **[1]**

**(c)** Give **two** disadvantages to a farmer of growing a crop from seeds produced by sexual reproduction. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A population of lizards lives on a small island and reproduces sexually. A new predator arrives on the island. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of sexual reproduction to this population. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A gardener had 50 young plants grown from seed produced by cross-pollination, and 50 plants grown from cuttings of a single parent plant. All the plants were exposed to the same fungal disease. After four weeks, 17 seed-grown plants had survived and none of the plants from cuttings had survived.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of seed-grown plants that survived. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the difference in survival between the two groups of plants. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest one variable the gardener should have kept the same for both groups. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student says: "Sexual reproduction is always better than asexual reproduction for a population in the wild." Give **one** piece of evidence that supports this view and **one** reason why it is not always true. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

*Examiner insight:* A is pollination, a common confusion. Fertilisation is the fusion of nuclei, which comes after pollination in plants.

**2.** **nuclei** [1]; **gametes** [1]; **zygote** [1]

**3.** Male gamete: (nucleus) in the **pollen grain** [1]. Female gamete: (nucleus) in the **ovule** [1].

**4.** Each parent produces gametes with **different combinations of alleles / genetic information** [1], and **which sperm fertilises which egg is random**, so each puppy receives a different combination [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Because they have different genes" restates the question; the marks come from the gametes and the randomness of fertilisation.

**5. (a)** **Haploid** [1]

**(b)** **Diploid** [1]

**(c)** **Diploid** [1]

**(d)** **Haploid** [1]

**6. (a)** **9** [1]

**(b)** **18** [1]

**7. (a)** R and S first, in that order [1]; then Q, then P [1]: **R, S, Q, P**.

**(b)** **Q** [1]

*Examiner insight:* S, the sperm reaching the egg, is not fertilisation; only the fusion of nuclei is.

**8. (a)** **24** [1]

**(b)** The zygote would have **twice** the normal number of chromosomes [1], so the number would **double in every generation** (24, 48, 96 ...) [1].

**(c)** **Diploid** [1]

**9. (a)** **Cross** variety A with variety B, transferring pollen from one to the other [1]; offspring may receive **alleles for high yield from A and for resistance from B**; choose and breed from those plants [1].

**(b)** Each offspring receives a **different combination of alleles**, because gametes differ and fertilisation is random [1].

**(c)** Any two of: crop is **less uniform** (for example in yield or ripening time); the good **combination of features may not be passed on** to every plant; growing from seed is **slower**; depends on **pollination** [2].

**10.** Advantages: offspring are **genetically different / show variation** [1], so some lizards may have features, such as faster running or better camouflage, that help them **escape the predator**; these survive and reproduce, so the population can adapt [1]. Disadvantages: lizards must **find a mate**, which takes time and energy and may expose them to the predator [1]; sexual reproduction is **slower**, so numbers lost to the predator are replaced slowly [1].

*Examiner insight:* For four marks you need both sides. Points tied to the predator score; a list of general facts about sexual reproduction scores less.

**11. (a)** 17 ÷ 50 × 100 = **34%** [1]

**(b)** Plants from cuttings are **genetically identical** [1], so if one is not resistant, **none** are resistant and all died [1]. Seed-grown plants show **variation**, so some had alleles giving **resistance** and survived [1].

**(c)** Any one of: age or size of plants; species / type of plant; amount of fungus applied; temperature; watering [1].

**12.** Supporting evidence: **variation** means some individuals may survive a change such as a new disease, so the population can adapt [1]. Not always true: in a **stable environment**, or for an isolated individual, asexual reproduction is **faster** and needs **no mate**, keeping the parent's well-suited features [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "fusion of gametes" instead of "fusion of the nuclei of gametes".
- Calling a sperm reaching an egg "fertilisation".
- (Extended) Calling the zygote haploid, or halving the chromosome number twice.
- (Extended) Discussing only advantages, or not using the context in the question.
- (Extended) Saying offspring "have different genes" without explaining why.
