These notes condense subtopic **1.6 The four operations** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027: calculations with integers, fractions and decimals, including the order of operations, brackets, negative numbers, improper fractions, mixed numbers and practical situations. Core (C1.6) and Extended (E1.6) are identical. For worked examples, use the [four operations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-the-four-operations/); to test yourself, use the [four operations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-the-four-operations-practice/).

## Order of operations

```
B   Brackets (innermost first; a fraction bar brackets top and bottom)
I   Indices: powers and roots
DM  Division and Multiplication, LEFT TO RIGHT
AS  Addition and Subtraction, LEFT TO RIGHT
```

| Calculation | Correct | Common wrong answer |
|---|---|---|
| 20 − 8 ÷ 4 | 20 − 2 = 18 | 3 |
| 18 ÷ 3 × 2 | 6 × 2 = 12 | 3 |
| 15 − 6 + 4 | 9 + 4 = 13 | 5 |
| 4 × 3² | 4 × 9 = 36 | 144 |
| (12 + 8) ÷ (7 − 2) | 20 ÷ 5 = 4 | — |

## Negative numbers

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Subtracting a negative = adding | 4 − (−6) = 10 |
| Adding a negative = subtracting | 4 + (−6) = −2 |
| Same signs × or ÷ → positive | (−7) × (−3) = 21 |
| Different signs × or ÷ → negative | 36 ÷ (−4) = −9 |
| Even power of a negative → positive | (−2)⁴ = 16 |
| Odd power of a negative → negative | (−2)³ = −8 |

**Temperature changes.** Rise = add; fall = subtract. Difference between two temperatures = higher − lower: from −6 °C to 3 °C is 3 − (−6) = 9 degrees.

## Fractions

| Operation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + and − | Common denominator; convert mixed numbers first | 5/6 − 3/8 = 20/24 − 9/24 = 11/24 |
| × | Convert mixed numbers; cancel; multiply tops and bottoms | 2½ × 1⅕ = 5/2 × 6/5 = 3 |
| ÷ | Convert; multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction | 4⅕ ÷ 1¾ = 21/5 × 4/7 = 12/5 = 2⅖ |
| "of" | Multiply | 3/5 of 45 = 27 |

**Mixed ↔ improper:** 3⅖ = (3 × 5 + 2)/5 = 17/5; 29/6 = 4⅚.

## Decimals

| Operation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + and − | Line up the decimal points; pad with zeros | 7 − 2.36 = 7.00 − 2.36 = 4.64 |
| × | Multiply as integers; total decimal places in the question = decimal places in the answer | 0.6 × 0.04 = 0.024 |
| ÷ | Scale both numbers so the divisor is an integer | 9.12 ÷ 0.04 = 912 ÷ 4 = 228 |

**Sense checks:** multiplying by a number between 0 and 1 makes the answer smaller; dividing by one makes it larger.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Integer | Whole number, including zero and negatives |
| Improper fraction | Numerator ⩾ denominator |
| Mixed number | Whole number + proper fraction |
| Reciprocal | 1 ÷ the number; for a/b it is b/a |
| Common denominator | A shared multiple of the denominators (the LCM is simplest) |
| Order of operations | The agreed order: brackets, indices, ÷ and ×, + and − |

## Exam traps

- Do not work strictly left to right: 6 + 4 × 2 = 14, not 20.
- Divide before multiplying only when division comes first in the line.
- Keep the sign with the number when a negative is squared: (−5)² = 25.
- 1/3 + 1/5 is 8/15, not 2/8.
- Convert mixed numbers before multiplying or dividing.
- When dividing fractions, invert the **second** fraction only.
- Count decimal places in **both** numbers when multiplying decimals.
- Read the context: "complete boxes" rounds down; "boxes needed" rounds up.

## Quick self-test

1. Work out 30 − 12 ÷ 3 × 2.
2. Work out (−8) × (−5) ÷ (−4).
3. Work out 7 − (−3) − 12.
4. Work out (4 − 9)² − 3 × 5.
5. The temperature was −11 °C and rose by 15 °C. Find the new temperature.
6. Work out 3/4 + 5/6.
7. Work out 2⅖ − 1⅔.
8. Work out 1⅞ × 2⅔.
9. Work out 5¼ ÷ 1⅖.
10. Work out 0.08 × 0.3.
11. Work out 7.56 ÷ 0.6.
12. Insert one pair of brackets to make 10 − 4 × 2 + 1 = 13 true.

### Answers

1. 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 4 × 2 = 8, 30 − 8 = 22
2. 40 ÷ (−4) = −10
3. 10 − 12 = −2
4. 25 − 15 = 10
5. −11 + 15 = 4 °C
6. 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12 = 1 7/12
7. 12/5 − 5/3 = 36/15 − 25/15 = 11/15
8. 15/8 × 8/3 = 15/3 = 5
9. 21/4 × 5/7 = 15/4 = 3¾
10. 8 × 3 = 24, three decimal places: 0.024
11. 75.6 ÷ 6 = 12.6
12. (10 − 4) × 2 + 1 = 6 × 2 + 1 = 13

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only the final answer on a non-calculator paper, so one slip loses every mark.
- Leaving a fraction answer unsimplified, or as an improper fraction when a mixed number was asked for.
- Sign errors when a negative number is subtracted or squared.
- Misplacing the decimal point after multiplying or dividing decimals.
- Using the wrong starting value in a temperature or bank balance problem with several steps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to show working for a simple calculation?**
On Papers 1 and 2, yes whenever it is more than one step. Method marks are available for correct intermediate values.

**Which written method for long multiplication is expected?**
Any clear method: column, grid or partitioning. What matters is that each partial product can be followed.
