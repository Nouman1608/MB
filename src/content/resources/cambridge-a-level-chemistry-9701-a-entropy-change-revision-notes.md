**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level**
subtopic 23.3: define entropy; predict and explain the sign of ΔS for changes of state,
temperature changes and reactions that change the number of gaseous molecules; calculate
ΔS⦵ from standard entropies. ΔS = ΔS<sub>surr</sub> + ΔS<sub>sys</sub> is not required.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entropy, S | the number of possible arrangements of the particles and their energy in a given system |
| Standard entropy, S⦵ | entropy of one mole of a substance at 298 K and 101 kPa |
| Entropy change, ΔS | S(final) − S(initial); for a reaction, products minus reactants |
| Units | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (joules, not kilojoules) |
| Feasibility link | ΔS combines with ΔH in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (subtopic 23.4) |

## Recall: what raises entropy

- More **freedom of movement** for the particles (solid → liquid → gas).
- A **larger volume** for the particles to spread through.
- **More particles**, especially more gas particles.
- **Higher temperature**: more energy quanta, more ways of sharing energy.

Typical order of S⦵: **gas ≫ liquid > solid** for the same substance. Example values
(J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): I₂(s) 116.1; Br₂(l) 152.2; Br₂(g) 245.5.

## Sign rules with reasons

| Process | ΔS | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Melting | + | particles leave fixed positions |
| Boiling | + (large) | particles separate fully and move freely |
| Freezing, condensing | − | fewer arrangements in the more ordered state |
| Solid dissolving in water | usually + | particles spread through the solution |
| Crystallisation, precipitation | − | particles become fixed in a lattice |
| Gas dissolving in water | − | gas molecules become confined in the liquid |
| Heating a substance | + | more accessible energy levels; more ways to share energy |
| Cooling a substance | − | fewer accessible energy levels |
| More moles of gas formed | + | gas has by far the most arrangements |
| Fewer moles of gas formed | − | gas particles lost |
| Gas moles unchanged | small; needs data | no dominant effect |

## Calculating ΔS⦵

    ΔS⦵ = ΣS⦵(products) − ΣS⦵(reactants)

Method:

1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Multiply each S⦵ by its coefficient.
3. Include elements; their S⦵ is **not** zero.
4. Subtract reactants from products.
5. Give sign and units, then check the sign against the change in gas moles.

**Example A** *(standard entropies supplied, J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: SO₂ 248.1; O₂ 205.0; SO₃(g) 256.8)*

    2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2SO₃(g)
    ΔS⦵ = 2(256.8) − [2(248.1) + 205.0] = 513.6 − 701.2 = −187.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Three gas moles become two: negative, as predicted.

**Example B** *(supplied: N₂O₄(g) 304.3; NO₂(g) 240.1)*

    N₂O₄(g) → 2NO₂(g)
    ΔS⦵ = 2(240.1) − 304.3 = +175.9 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

One gas mole becomes two: positive.

**Example C** *(supplied: H₂ 130.6; Cl₂ 223.1; HCl 186.9)*

    H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g)
    ΔS⦵ = 2(186.9) − (130.6 + 223.1) = +20.1 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Gas moles unchanged: the value is small, and the sign could not have been predicted.

## Explaining the size of ΔS

- A large negative ΔS usually means gas has been converted into a solid or liquid, or into
  fewer gas molecules.
- In 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s), most of the decrease comes from losing one mole of O₂ gas.
- Boiling has a much larger ΔS than melting for the same substance.

## Exam traps

- Definition must mention **particles and their energy**; "disorder" alone is not enough.
- Elements have non-zero standard entropies.
- Units are **J** K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; convert to kJ only when substituting into ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
- Count **gaseous** moles only; ignore (l), (s) and (aq) when predicting.
- Products **minus** reactants, never the reverse.
- When gas moles are equal, say the sign needs data rather than guessing.
- A gas dissolving has negative ΔS, unlike a solid dissolving.
- "More arrangements" is the explanation; "particles move more" alone may not earn the mark.
- Do not write ΔS<sub>total</sub> or ΔS<sub>surroundings</sub> answers; they are not
  required for 9701.

## Self-test

1. State the 9701 definition of entropy.
2. Give the units of standard entropy.
3. Predict the sign of ΔS when ethanol vapour condenses.
4. Predict the sign of ΔS when sodium chloride dissolves in water.
5. Predict the sign of ΔS when ammonia gas dissolves in water, and explain.
6. Explain why the entropy of a sample of copper increases when it is heated from 300 K to
   400 K.
7. Predict the sign of ΔS for 2H₂O₂(l) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g).
8. *(Supplied, J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: H₂ 130.6; O₂ 205.0; H₂O(l) 69.9.)* Calculate ΔS⦵ for
   2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l).
9. *(Supplied: C(graphite) 5.7; O₂ 205.0; CO₂ 213.6.)* Calculate ΔS⦵ for
   C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g) and explain why it is small.
10. For C₂H₄(g) + H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g), ΔS⦵ = −120.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. S⦵(C₂H₄) = 219.6 and
    S⦵(H₂) = 130.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate S⦵(C₂H₆).
11. Why does boiling give a larger entropy increase than melting?
12. Why can you not predict the sign of ΔS for H₂(g) + I₂(g) → 2HI(g) from the equation alone?

**Answers**

1. The number of possible arrangements of the particles and their energy in a given system.
2. J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
3. Negative.
4. Positive (usually): ions spread out from the lattice through the solution.
5. Negative: the gas molecules become confined in the liquid, so there are fewer possible
   arrangements.
6. The atoms have more energy, so more energy levels are accessible and there are more ways
   to distribute the energy.
7. Positive: no gas on the left, one mole of gas on the right.
8. 2(69.9) − [2(130.6) + 205.0] = 139.8 − 466.2 = **−326.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**.
9. 213.6 − (5.7 + 205.0) = **+2.9 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**; one mole of gas on each side, so there is no
   large change.
10. −120.6 = S⦵(C₂H₆) − (219.6 + 130.6), so S⦵(C₂H₆) = **229.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**.
11. In a gas the particles are completely separated and move freely through a much larger
    volume, so there are many more arrangements than in a liquid.
12. Two moles of gas on each side (I₂ shown as a gas), so there is no change in gas moles and
    ΔS is small; its sign needs data.
