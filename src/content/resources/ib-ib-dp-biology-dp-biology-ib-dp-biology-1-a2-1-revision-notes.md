## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pre-biotic | Before life existed |
| Abiogenesis | Origin of life from non-living matter |
| Protocell | Hypothetical early compartment holding self-replicating molecules |
| Vesicle | Small sphere bounded by a bilayer |
| Amphipathic | Having a hydrophilic part and a hydrophobic part |
| Ribozyme | RNA molecule that acts as a catalyst |
| RNA world | Hypothesis that early life used RNA for both heredity and catalysis |
| LUCA | Last universal common ancestor of all living organisms |
| Molecular clock | Estimating divergence time from the number of sequence differences |
| Hydrothermal vent | Opening in the seafloor releasing hot, mineral-rich water |

## A2.1.1: early Earth

- Little or no free oxygen; no ozone layer, so intense ultraviolet radiation.
- Energy sources: lightning, ultraviolet light, volcanic and vent heat.
- Without oxygen, organic molecules were not oxidised and could accumulate.
- Carbon compounds could form spontaneously in ways that do not happen today.

## A2.1.2: the smallest self-sustaining unit

- A cell carries out all functions of life itself: metabolism, growth, reproduction, response, homeostasis, nutrition, excretion.
- Viruses: genes and evolution, but no metabolism; replicate only in a host, so usually classed non-living.
- The boundary of "life" is a matter of definition.

## A2.1.3: four requirements

| Requirement | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Catalysis | Reactions fast enough to matter |
| Self-replication | Information copied and inherited |
| Self-assembly | Structures form without a builder |
| Compartmentalisation | Chemistry kept inside and concentrated |

The circular problem: DNA needs enzymes to be copied; enzymes need DNA to be made.

Testability: steps can be shown to be *possible*, not that they *happened*.

## A2.1.4: Miller–Urey

| | |
|---|---|
| Set-up | Water vapour, methane, ammonia, hydrogen; electric sparks; condenser |
| Result | Amino acids formed |
| Strength | Organic monomers form from inorganic molecules with energy, no life needed |
| Limitation | Atmosphere may have been less reducing; monomers only, no polymers or membranes |

## A2.1.5: vesicles

- Fatty acids are amphipathic.
- In water, they coalesce spontaneously into spherical bilayers.
- Provides self-assembly and compartmentalisation without enzymes.
- Benefits: keeps products together, different internal conditions, a unit for natural selection.

## A2.1.6: RNA first

- Stores information; copied by base pairing.
- Catalyses reactions as ribozymes.
- Solves the DNA–protein circle: one molecule, two roles.
- Supporting point: rRNA in the ribosome catalyses peptide bond formation.

## A2.1.7 to A2.1.9: LUCA

**Evidence LUCA existed:** universal genetic code; genes shared by all three domains (for example ribosomal components).

**Dating approaches:**

| Method | What it measures | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Radiometric dating | Age of rocks containing traces of life | Few very old rocks survive unaltered |
| Fossils | Preserved microbial structures | Hard to tell biological from non-biological structures |
| Molecular clock | Sequence differences ÷ rate of change | Rates vary; calibration uncertain |

**Vent evidence:** fossilised traces in ancient vent precipitates; conserved genes suggesting a heat-tolerant, anaerobic organism using hydrogen.

## Worked reminders

**Molecular clock.** 45 differences at 5 per 100 million years: 45 ÷ 5 × 100 = **900 million years** since divergence.

**Radiometric dating.** After two half-lives, 25% of the parent isotope remains; after three, 12.5%.

**Model evaluation line.** "This shows a step is possible under these conditions, but cannot show that this is what happened on early Earth."

## Must-know distinctions

- **Possible versus actual.** Experiments show a step could happen; they cannot show it did.
- **LUCA versus first cell.** LUCA is the latest ancestor shared by all surviving life; earlier cells existed.
- **Monomers versus polymers.** Miller–Urey made monomers; forming polymers is a separate problem.
- **Vesicle versus cell.** A vesicle is a compartment; a cell also needs replication and metabolism.

## Exam traps

- Miller and Urey "made life".
- Vesicles "made by enzymes".
- RNA first "because it is simpler".
- LUCA called the first cell.
- A molecular clock date stated as exact.
- An evaluation with no limitation.

## Quick self-test

1. Why did early Earth's surface receive more ultraviolet radiation than today?
2. Name the four requirements for the origin of cells.
3. Explain the circular problem that the RNA world hypothesis addresses.
4. Which gases did Miller and Urey use?
5. Give one strength and one limitation of the Miller–Urey experiment.
6. Why do fatty acids form vesicles spontaneously in water?
7. Give two benefits of a membrane-bound compartment for an early protocell.
8. What is a ribozyme?
9. Give two pieces of evidence that LUCA existed.
10. Name three approaches to estimating the date of the first cells.
11. Two lineages differ at 28 sites; differences accumulate at 4 per 100 million years. Estimate the divergence time.
12. Give two lines of evidence that LUCA lived near hydrothermal vents.

### Answers

1. There was little free oxygen, so no ozone layer to absorb it.
2. Catalysis, self-replication, self-assembly, compartmentalisation.
3. DNA needs protein enzymes to be copied and proteins need DNA to be made; RNA can both store information and catalyse.
4. Methane, ammonia, hydrogen and water vapour.
5. Strength: amino acids formed without life. Limitation: atmosphere may not be realistic, or only monomers formed.
6. They are amphipathic; tails avoid water and heads face it, forming a bilayer that closes into a sphere.
7. Keeps products concentrated; allows internal conditions to differ; makes a unit natural selection can act on (any two).
8. An RNA molecule that acts as a catalyst.
9. Universal genetic code; genes shared across all domains.
10. Radiometric dating, fossils, molecular clocks.
11. 28 ÷ 4 × 100 = **700 million years**.
12. Fossilised traces in ancient vent precipitates; conserved genes suggesting an organism suited to vent conditions.
