These questions cover section E9.6, Cumulative frequency diagrams, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus lists C9.6 as "Extended content only", so every question here is **(Extended)** and suits Papers 2 and 4. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Where you are asked to draw a curve, do it on graph paper; readings from your own smooth curve are accepted within the ranges given in the answers. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [cumulative frequency diagrams study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-cumulative-frequency-diagrams/) and the [cumulative frequency revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-cumulative-frequency-diagrams-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The finishing times of 60 runners are grouped below.

```
Time t (min)   20 < t ≤ 25   25 < t ≤ 30   30 < t ≤ 35   35 < t ≤ 40   40 < t ≤ 45
Frequency           5            14            21            13             7
```

Complete a cumulative frequency table for these times. **[2]**

**2.** (Extended) (non-calculator) For the data in question 1, write down the coordinates of all the points you would plot to draw the cumulative frequency diagram. **[2]**

**3.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A cumulative frequency diagram shows the data for 200 people. Write down the cumulative frequency at which you would read:

**(a)** the lower quartile and the upper quartile **[1]**

**(b)** the 80th percentile. **[1]**

**4.** (Extended) (non-calculator) For the 200 people in question 3, the curve gives a lower quartile of 34 and an upper quartile of 52. Find the interquartile range. **[1]**

**5.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Use your table from question 1 to find how many runners took more than 35 minutes. **[1]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Which one of these statements about drawing a cumulative frequency diagram is **wrong**?

A: Each point is plotted at the upper boundary of its class.

B: The points are joined with a smooth curve.

C: Each point is plotted at the midpoint of its class.

D: The curve starts at cumulative frequency 0 at the lowest class boundary.

**[1]**

### Section B

**7.** (Extended) (calculator) The journey times of 100 commuters are shown.

```
Time t (min)   0 < t ≤ 10   10 < t ≤ 20   20 < t ≤ 30   30 < t ≤ 40   40 < t ≤ 50
Frequency           8            22            20            35            15
```

**(a)** Complete a cumulative frequency table. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the cumulative frequency diagram. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the median journey time. **[1]**

**(d)** Estimate the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[3]**

**(e)** How many commuters had a journey of more than 40 minutes? **[1]**

**8.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The reaction times of two groups of drivers were recorded and a cumulative frequency diagram drawn for each group. The results are:

```
           Median    Lower quartile   Upper quartile
Group A    0.42 s        0.37 s           0.47 s
Group B    0.38 s        0.30 s           0.45 s
```

**(a)** Find the interquartile range for each group. **[2]**

**(b)** Make two comparisons between the reaction times of the two groups. **[2]**

**9.** (Extended) (calculator) A cumulative frequency diagram shows the marks of 160 students in a test.

**(a)** At what cumulative frequency would you read the 10th percentile and the 90th percentile? **[2]**

**(b)** The 10th percentile is 41 marks and the 90th percentile is 77 marks. Find the difference between them. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain what the 90th percentile of 77 marks means. **[1]**

**10.** (Extended) (calculator) The cumulative frequency curve for the marks of 120 students passes through the point (35, 48).

**(a)** The pass mark is 36. What percentage of the students passed? **[2]**

**(b)** The top 25% of students receive a merit. At what cumulative frequency should you read across to find the lowest mark for a merit? **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The cumulative frequency table shows the heights of 72 plants.

```
Height h (cm)          h ≤ 150   h ≤ 160   h ≤ 170   h ≤ 180   h ≤ 190
Cumulative frequency      6         20        47        66        72
```

All heights are more than 140 cm.

**(a)** Write down the frequency for each class: 140 < h ≤ 150, 150 < h ≤ 160, 160 < h ≤ 170, 170 < h ≤ 180 and 180 < h ≤ 190. **[2]**

**(b)** How many plants are taller than 170 cm? **[1]**

**(c)** Which class contains the median? **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Using the data in question 1, Ravi plots each cumulative frequency at the **midpoint** of its class, for example (22.5, 5) and (27.5, 19).

**(a)** Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the effect on his estimate of the median. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Running totals 5, 19, 40 [1]; 53, 60, against t ≤ 25, t ≤ 30, t ≤ 35, t ≤ 40, t ≤ 45 [1]

**2.** (20, 0) as the starting point [1]; (25, 5), (30, 19), (35, 40), (40, 53), (45, 60) [1]

**3. (a)** 200 ÷ 4 = **50** and 3 × 200 ÷ 4 = **150** [1]

**(b)** 0.8 × 200 = **160** [1] **[2]**

**4.** 52 − 34 = **18** [1]

**5.** 60 − 40 = **20** runners [1]

**6.** **C** [1]

**7. (a)** 8, 30, 50 [1]; 85, 100 against t ≤ 10, t ≤ 20, t ≤ 30, t ≤ 40, t ≤ 50 [1]

**(b)** Points (0, 0), (10, 8), (20, 30), (30, 50), (40, 85), (50, 100) plotted as crosses [1]; joined with a smooth curve [1]

**(c)** Read across at 50: the curve passes through (30, 50), so the median is **30 minutes** [1]

**(d)** LQ: across at 25, about **18 minutes** (accept 17 to 19) [1]; UQ: across at 75, about **37 minutes** (accept 36 to 38) [1]; IQR ≈ 37 − 18 = **19 minutes** (accept 17 to 21, consistent with your readings) [1]

**(e)** 100 − 85 = **15** [1] **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* the LQ height 25 lies between the points (10, 8) and (20, 30), so the LQ must be between 10 and 20 minutes; a reading outside that class is a misread scale.

**8. (a)** Group A: 0.47 − 0.37 = **0.10 s** [1]; Group B: 0.45 − 0.30 = **0.15 s** [1]

**(b)** Group B's median (0.38 s) is lower, so Group B typically reacted faster [1]; Group A's IQR (0.10 s) is smaller, so Group A's reaction times were more consistent [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** 0.1 × 160 = **16** [1]; 0.9 × 160 = **144** [1]

**(b)** 77 − 41 = **36 marks** [1]

**(c)** About **90% of the students scored 77 marks or less** (10% scored more) [1] **[4]**

**10. (a)** 48 students scored 35 or less, so 120 − 48 = 72 passed [1]; 72 ÷ 120 × 100 = **60%** [1]

**(b)** The lowest merit mark is the upper quartile: read across at 0.75 × 120 = **90** [1] **[3]**

**11. (a)** 6, 20 − 6 = 14, 47 − 20 = 27 [1]; 66 − 47 = 19, 72 − 66 = 6 [1]

**(b)** 72 − 47 = **25** [1]

**(c)** Median height 72 ÷ 2 = 36, between 20 and 47, so **160 < h ≤ 170** [1] **[4]**

**12. (a)** A cumulative frequency counts everything **up to the end of the class**, so it must be plotted at the **upper class boundary** (25, 30, 35, ...) [1]

**(b)** The curve is moved 2.5 minutes to the left, so his median is about **2.5 minutes too small** [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting at midpoints or lower boundaries, or missing the starting point at 0.
- Ruled straight lines between the points instead of a smooth curve.
- Reading quartiles from the horizontal axis instead of across from n/4 and 3n/4.
- Giving the cumulative frequency itself for a "more than" question.
- Not showing reading lines on the diagram, so a slightly misread value earns no method credit.
