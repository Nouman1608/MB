## The seven specification points

1. Explain the relationship between objectives, strategy and tactics.
2. Evaluate a business's current or proposed strategy.
3. Recommend and justify how a business could alter its strategy.
4. Evaluate the impact and importance of changing a business's strategy to the business and its stakeholders.
5. Explain why a business needs to plan its implementation strategy.
6. Evaluate the impact and importance of a strategy for a business and its stakeholders.
7. Recommend and justify a strategy for a business.

## Core recall

### The cascade

Corporate objective → corporate strategy → functional strategies (marketing, operations, HR, finance) → tactics → results, which feed back upwards.

- Objective: where the business wants to be (measurable, time-bound).
- Strategy: the long-term route there.
- Functional strategy: each department's part of the route.
- Tactic: a short-term action that delivers the strategy.
- If tactics work but the objective is still missed, suspect the strategy.

### Strategy or tactic?

| Test | Strategy | Tactic |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Long term | Short term |
| Scope | Whole business or function | One action or period |
| Cost | High | Low |
| Reversible? | Hard | Easy |
| Decided by | Directors | Middle managers, teams |

Quick check: could it be stopped next month with little loss? Then it is a tactic.

### Three tests for evaluating a strategy (not named in the specification)

| Test | Question | Evidence to use |
|---|---|---|
| Suitability | Does it fit the objectives, market and strengths? | Market data, SWOT, objectives |
| Feasibility | Can the business resource it? | Cash, borrowing, skills, capacity, time |
| Acceptability | Will stakeholders accept its returns, risks and changes? | Break-even, margin of safety, gearing, staff impact |

Finish with a judgement: which test is weakest, and what the success depends on.

### Altering a strategy

- Change the weakest part shown by the evaluation, not the whole strategy.
- Common alterations: phase or pilot; change the funding; narrow the target market; change the timescale.
- Structure: the change → why it fixes the weakness → what is kept → the trade-off.

### Changing strategy

- Costs: sunk costs lost, retraining, disruption, uncertainty, lost credibility if frequent.
- Benefits: avoids greater losses, renews focus, responds to the market.
- Importance depends on: severity of the problem, speed of market change, reversibility, credibility.

### Implementation plans

Contents: actions and sequence, responsibility, timeline and milestones, resources and budget, measures of success, communication, risks and fallback.

Why strategies fail at implementation: under-resourcing, unclear responsibility, resistance to change, poor sequencing, no measures.

Costs of planning: management time; rigidity if the plan cannot adapt.

## Quick worked reminder

Brightwater Gyms (invented): price £20 a month, variable cost £4, fixed costs £22,000 a month per site.

- Contribution = £16; break-even = 22,000 ÷ 16 = 1,375 members.
- Planned 1,500 members; margin of safety 125 (about 8%).
- Profit per site at 1,500 = £2,000 a month.
- Judgement: suitable, but high risk; alter by phasing two sites first.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Objective | A measurable, time-bound target |
| Strategy | A long-term plan to achieve the objectives |
| Functional strategy | The part of the strategy delivered by one department |
| Tactic | A short-term action that carries out a strategy |
| Implementation | Putting a chosen strategy into action |
| Milestone | A dated checkpoint in an implementation plan |
| Sunk cost | Money already spent that cannot be recovered |
| Resistance to change | Opposition from people affected by a change |

## Exam traps

- A discount or an advert is a tactic, not a strategy.
- "Evaluate a strategy" needs more than fit with objectives: test feasibility and acceptability too.
- Missing a target may be a tactics or implementation problem, not a strategy problem.
- Recommending an alteration without a trade-off is incomplete.
- Use case figures: break-even and margin of safety show risk better than words.

## Self-test

1. Put these in order: tactic, objective, functional strategy, corporate strategy.
2. Classify "a 20% off weekend sale" as a strategy or a tactic, and explain.
3. State the three tests for evaluating a strategy.
4. Give two pieces of evidence to test feasibility.
5. A site has fixed costs of £15,000 a month and contribution per customer of £12 a month. Find break-even customers.
6. Planned customers are 1,500 for the site in question 5. Find the margin of safety in customers and as a percentage of planned customers.
7. Give two ways a business could alter a high-risk strategy without abandoning it.
8. State three contents of an implementation plan.
9. Give two reasons why strategies fail at implementation.
10. Explain what a sunk cost is and why it should not stop a change of strategy.
11. Give two factors that affect the importance of changing strategy.
12. What should a recommendation of a strategy include?

### Answers

1. Objective, corporate strategy, functional strategy, tactic.
2. A tactic: short-term, low-cost and easy to stop.
3. Suitability, feasibility, acceptability.
4. Any two: cash available; ability to borrow; staff skills; production capacity; time.
5. 15,000 ÷ 12 = **1,250 customers**.
6. 1,500 − 1,250 = **250 customers**; 250 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = **16.7%**.
7. Any two: phase or pilot it; fund it differently; narrow the target market; extend the timescale.
8. Any three: actions and sequence; responsibility; timeline and milestones; resources and budget; measures of success; communication; risks and fallback.
9. Any two: under-resourcing; unclear responsibility; resistance to change; poor sequencing; no measures of progress.
10. Money already spent that cannot be recovered; it is lost whatever is decided, so only future costs and benefits should guide the choice.
11. Any two: how serious the problem is; how fast the market is changing; how reversible the change is; the effect on credibility.
12. The strategy, links to the objective and case evidence, a comparison with an alternative, the main risk and a condition.
