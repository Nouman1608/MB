**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Pre-Islamic Arabia: "The main religious, political, moral, and social features of Pre-Islamic Arabia, including Jahiliya (Age of ignorance)."** To understand the message of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), Muslims first study the world into which he was born. This page describes Arabia before Islam under the four headings the specification names, explains the term Jahiliya and shows how to use the prescribed passage, Qur'an 17:31.

## The setting

Arabia is a large desert peninsula. Most of its people were Arab tribes. Some were **Bedouin**, who moved with their camels and sheep in search of water and pasture. Others lived in oasis towns such as Yathrib (later Madinah) and Ta'if or in trading cities such as **Makkah**. Makkah lay on the caravan route between Yemen in the south and Syria in the north. It was a poor place to farm, but its position and the sacred house of the Ka'aba made it wealthy.

## The meaning of Jahiliya

**Jahiliya** is usually translated as "the Age of Ignorance". It does not mean that the Arabs were unintelligent. They were famous for their poetry, memory, generosity and skill in the desert. It means they were ignorant of Allah's guidance. They lacked a revealed book and a clear moral law, and many followed tradition, tribal pride and desire. The word is used in the Qur'an for ways of thinking that ignore Allah's guidance. Islam brought revelation, which Muslims believe replaced ignorance with knowledge.

## Religious features

**Idol worship.** Most Arabs were polytheists. The Ka'aba, which Muslims believe was built by Ibrahim (AS) and Ismail (AS) for the worship of one God, had been filled with idols, traditionally said to number about 360. The best-known were:

| Deity | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hubal | The main idol in the Ka'aba, used in drawing lots for decisions |
| Al-Lat | Worshipped at Ta'if |
| Al-Uzza | Worshipped near Makkah |
| Manat | Worshipped near Madinah |

The Arabs called Allah the high God and the creator, but they believed that the idols were intermediaries who would bring them closer to Him. The Qur'an says they claimed, "We only worship them that they may bring us nearer to Allah in position" (39:3). This is *shirk*, associating partners with Allah.

**Other practices.** People believed in omens, fortune-tellers and spirits. They made offerings to stones and trees. Every year tribes came to Makkah for pilgrimage, but the rites had been mixed with idol worship. Some even circled the Ka'aba without clothes.

**Other faiths.** Jewish and Christian communities lived in places such as Yathrib, Najran and Yemen. A few Arabs, called **hanifs**, rejected idols and followed the pure religion of Ibrahim (AS). Examples traditionally mentioned are Waraqa ibn Nawfal and Zayd ibn Amr. They were few, and had no organised community.

## Political features

- **No central government.** Arabia had no king, parliament or national law. Loyalty belonged to the **tribe** (*qabilah*) and to the clan within it.
- **Tribal leadership.** Each tribe was led by a chief (*sheikh*), chosen for age, wisdom, wealth, bravery and generosity. He consulted elders and his power depended on persuasion.
- **Makkah and the Quraysh.** The Quraysh tribe ruled Makkah through a council of clan leaders that met in the Dar al-Nadwa. Clans held duties around the Ka'aba, such as giving water and food to pilgrims. This gave the Quraysh power, honour and trade.
- **Blood feuds.** If a person was harmed, his tribe was bound to take revenge. Quarrels over wells and pastures, or small insults, could lead to wars lasting for years. One example is the war of Fijar, in which the Prophet (pbuh) took a small part as a young man.
- **Pacts and protection.** A person who had no tribe had no protection, so tribes made alliances. The Hilf al-Fudul was a pact in Makkah to defend victims of injustice, and the Prophet (pbuh) praised it later.
- **Great powers.** To the north was the Byzantine Empire and to the east the Persian Empire. They had influence over the border tribes, but did not rule central Arabia.

## Moral features

Arabia had both noble and wicked customs.

**Admired values.** Courage in battle, loyalty to the tribe, generosity to guests, keeping a promise and skill in poetry were all valued.

**Harmful practices.**

- **Female infanticide.** Some tribes buried baby girls alive because they feared poverty or shame. The Qur'an condemns this: "do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for them and for you. Indeed their killing is ever a great sin" (17:31), and speaks of the girl who was buried alive being asked for what sin she was killed (81:8-9).
- **Gambling and drinking.** Betting with arrows and drinking wine were common at feasts and poetry gatherings.
- **Usury (riba).** Moneylenders demanded interest that doubled a debt and trapped the poor.
- **Cheating and theft in trade.** Some merchants gave short measure.
- **Revenge and pride.** Tribal pride (*asabiyyah*) meant "my tribe, right or wrong".

## Social features

- **Social classes.** Free tribe members had status. **Slaves** had almost no rights and could be bought, sold or beaten. Freed slaves and foreigners depended on a protector.
- **Position of women.** Practice varied. A few women, such as Khadija (RA), owned businesses. But in general women had little protection. They could be inherited as property, had no fixed share in inheritance, and a man could marry many wives without limits.
- **Orphans and the poor.** Orphans and widows were often cheated of their property. The poor and weak were ignored.
- **Trade and wealth.** Makkan merchants ran caravans to Syria in summer and Yemen in winter (106:1-2). Wealth gathered in the hands of a few leading families.
- **Hospitality and poetry.** Guests were honoured, and poets were respected as the keepers of the tribe's memory and honour.

## Why this background matters

Understanding Jahiliya explains why the message of Islam was so powerful and so threatening:

- The call to **one God** challenged idol worship and the income from pilgrims.
- The call to **equality** challenged tribal pride and the position of slaves and the poor.
- The call to **justice and honest trade** challenged usury and cheating.
- The call to **respect women and protect the weak** challenged infanticide and neglect of orphans.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 17:31

"And do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for them and for you. Indeed their killing is ever a great sin."

- **"do not kill your children"** is a direct ban on infanticide, and shows the value Islam places on life.
- **"for fear of poverty"** gives the reason that people used: economic worry.
- **"We provide for them and for you"** teaches that Allah is the provider, so the excuse is false and trust in Allah is the answer.
- **"a great sin"** shows the seriousness of the act.

The verse is a good example of how Islam corrected a social wrong, and it links to the worth of every child, the status of girls and trust in Allah.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** moral problems in Pre-Islamic Arabia. **[4]**

> One problem was female infanticide [1]. Some families buried baby girls because they feared poverty, which showed little respect for human life and for women [1]. Another was the charging of interest, or usury [1], which trapped the poor in debt and made the rich richer [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Translating Jahiliya as "stupidity".** It means ignorance of Allah's guidance, not lack of intelligence.
- **Saying that all Arabs had no religion.** They had many forms of worship and some believed in Allah as creator but also worshipped idols.
- **Describing the features only as a list.** Say how each one shows the need for a new message.
- **Mixing the four headings.** Use religious, political, moral and social headings so that your answer is organised.
- **Forgetting the good qualities.** A balanced answer notes generosity, courage and hospitality.

## Exam technique

For **give** and **name** questions, state one item such as Hubal or Quraysh. For **explain** questions, state the feature and its effect. In the 12-mark question on 17:31, use "for fear of poverty" for the social pressure, "We provide for them and for you" for trust in Allah and "a great sin" for the value of life. Then judge: infanticide was one of the clearest wrongs, but the Qur'an also targets idolatry, injustice and greed, so Jahiliya was a wider condition.
