These notes condense sub-topic **16.1 Asexual reproduction** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2 are Core. Outcome 3, the advantages and disadvantages, is **Extended only**.

Full explanations and worked examples are in the [asexual reproduction study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-asexual-reproduction/). Practise with the [asexual reproduction practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-asexual-reproduction-practice/).

## Core recall

**Definition (16.1.1):** asexual reproduction is a process resulting in the production of **genetically identical offspring** from **one parent**.

Both parts are needed for the mark. There are **no gametes** and **no fertilisation**.

**Identifying examples (16.1.2):** look for the clues, not the name of the organism.

| Look for | Example |
|---|---|
| One cell splitting into two | bacteria |
| A bud growing from the parent and separating | yeast, *Hydra* |
| A swollen underground stem growing new shoots | potato tuber |
| A side stem along the ground with plantlets that root | strawberry runner |
| New bulbs beside an old one | onion, daffodil |
| Plantlets on leaf edges | *Bryophyllum* |
| A body breaking into parts that each regrow | some sea anemones |
| A piece of stem rooting (artificial) | cuttings |
| Spores formed without fusion of gametes | bread mould |

**Not asexual:**

- Seeds, even after **self-pollination**: they come from pollination and fertilisation.
- Identical twins: they come from a zygote, produced by fertilisation.

**Link to topic 17 (Extended):** the new cells are made by **mitosis**, which gives genetically identical cells.

## Advantages and disadvantages -- Extended only

### In a wild population

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| one parent is enough; an isolated individual can reproduce | no variation, so little capacity to adapt to environmental change |
| no energy or time spent finding a mate or a pollinator | one disease can affect every individual |
| fast, so new areas are colonised quickly | offspring compete with the parent for space, light, water and nutrients |
| good features of a well-adapted parent are kept in a stable environment | harmful features are passed to all offspring |

### In crop production

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| all plants share desirable features (yield, flavour, resistance) | one new disease or pest can destroy the whole crop |
| uniform crop: ripens and is harvested together | no new, improved varieties without sexual reproduction |
| quick; no wait for flowers, pollination and seed | the same weakness to drought or cold in every plant |
| seedless varieties can still be grown | the grower depends on a single variety |

**Speed in numbers (example):** if a bacterium divides every 20 minutes, after 2 hours there have been 120 ÷ 20 = 6 divisions, so one cell becomes 2⁶ = **64** genetically identical cells.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asexual reproduction | production of genetically identical offspring from one parent |
| Genetically identical | having exactly the same genes |
| Clone | a genetically identical copy of an organism |
| Binary fission | a single cell dividing into two, as in bacteria |
| Budding | a new individual growing out from the parent, as in yeast |
| Tuber | a swollen underground stem that can grow into a new plant |
| Runner | a horizontal stem along which new plantlets form |
| Variation | differences between individuals of the same species (topic 18) |
| Mitosis | nuclear division giving genetically identical cells (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- "One parent" alone is half the definition: add **genetically identical offspring**.
- "Offspring look the same" is weaker than "genetically identical".
- Self-pollination is **sexual**: seeds form after fertilisation.
- "Discuss" needs **both** advantages and disadvantages.
- Match the **context**: crop answers need crop points (uniform harvest, quick production, whole crop lost to one disease).
- Lack of variation does not **cause** disease; it means a disease can affect **every** individual.

## Self-test

**1.** Define asexual reproduction.

**2.** Name the type of offspring produced by asexual reproduction.

**3.** A strawberry plant produces runners. Explain why this is asexual reproduction.

**4.** A pea plant pollinates its own flowers. Is the reproduction asexual or sexual? Give a reason.

**5.** State two clues in a diagram that would show an organism is reproducing asexually.

**6.** Name the structure in potatoes that allows asexual reproduction.

**7.** *(Extended)* State one advantage of asexual reproduction to a plant growing alone on a newly formed island.

**8.** *(Extended)* Explain why a wild population that reproduces only asexually may be wiped out by a new disease.

**9.** *(Extended)* Give two advantages to a farmer of growing a crop asexually.

**10.** *(Extended)* Give one disadvantage to a farmer of growing a crop asexually.

**11.** *(Extended)* Explain why offspring produced asexually may compete with the parent.

**12.** If a bacterium divides every 30 minutes, how many cells come from one cell after 2 hours?

### Answers

**1.** A process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.

**2.** Genetically identical offspring (clones).

**3.** Only one parent is involved and the plantlets grow from part of it, with no gametes, so they are genetically identical to the parent.

**4.** Sexual: seeds are produced after pollination and fertilisation.

**5.** Any two of: only one parent; offspring growing from part of the parent; no flowers, gametes or fertilisation shown.

**6.** The tuber.

**7.** It does not need a mate or pollinator, so it can reproduce on its own.

**8.** The individuals are genetically identical, so if one is not resistant, none are resistant and all can be killed.

**9.** Any two of: all plants have the desirable features of the parent; uniform crop harvested together; quick production.

**10.** One disease or pest could destroy the whole crop, because all the plants have the same genes.

**11.** Offspring often grow close to the parent, so they need the same light, water and nutrients in the same place.

**12.** 120 ÷ 30 = 4 divisions, so 2⁴ = 16 cells.
