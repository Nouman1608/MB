## Key results

| Idea | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Photon energy | E = hf = hc/λ | One photon, one electron, one event |
| Photon momentum | p = h/λ | Used in the de Broglie relation |
| Einstein's equation | Emax = hf - Φ | HL only |
| Threshold | hf₀ = Φ | Emax = 0 at f₀ |
| Stopping potential | eVs = Emax | Vs against f is a straight line |
| de Broglie wavelength | λ = h/p | Any particle, with momentum p |
| Electron from rest through V | p = √(2meV) | Non-relativistic only |
| First minimum, single gap | sin θ = λ/b | b is the gap width |
| Compton shift | Δλ = λf - λi = (h/mec)(1 - cos θ) | Derivation not required |
| Compton constant | h/mec = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m | Maximum shift is twice this |

Energy conversions: 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Convert before substituting into formulas written in joules.

## The photoelectric effect

**Definitions**

* **Photoelectron:** an electron freed from a surface by radiation falling on it.
* **Threshold frequency f₀:** the lowest frequency of light that releases photoelectrons from a given metal.
* **Work function Φ:** the least energy that must be supplied to free an electron at the metal's surface.

**What changes what**

| You change | Number of electrons per second | Emax |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity up (same f above f₀) | Up | No change |
| Frequency up (same power, above f₀) | Fewer photons per second, each more energetic | Up |
| Frequency below f₀ | Zero | No emission |
| Metal with larger Φ | Fewer or none | Down |

**Features classical wave theory cannot explain** (the guide requires a discussion):

1. A threshold frequency below which nothing is emitted, whatever the intensity.
2. Emax rising with frequency but not with intensity.
3. Emission with no measurable delay, even for faint light.

**Photon explanation, in steps**

1. Light consists of photons of energy hf.
2. One photon gives all its energy to one electron.
3. The electron needs at least Φ to leave. The rest becomes kinetic energy.
4. So Emax = hf - Φ. If hf < Φ, nothing happens.

**Stopping potential graph.** Plot Vs on the vertical axis and f on the horizontal axis. Gradient = h/e. Horizontal intercept = f₀. Vertical intercept = -Φ/e. All metals give parallel lines (same gradient) with different intercepts.

**Worked reminder.** Light of wavelength 360 nm needs a stopping potential of 0.80 V. Photon energy = hc/λ = 5.52 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.45 eV. Φ = 3.45 - 0.80 = **2.65 eV**.

## Matter waves

* Electrons, neutrons and atoms diffract. Diffraction is a wave property, so this shows the wave nature of matter.
* **Wave-particle duality:** matter and light each show wave and particle behaviour. Which one you see depends on the experiment.
* λ = h/p. Larger momentum, shorter wavelength. A heavy, fast object has an immeasurably small wavelength.

**Method for a diffraction calculation**

1. Find p. For an electron through V, p = √(2meV). Otherwise p = mv.
2. Find λ = h/p.
3. For the first minimum, sin θ = λ/b.
4. If the speed increases, p rises, λ falls and the minimum moves toward the centre.

**Description of the experiment** (be ready to write this out): an electron beam of known energy in a vacuum, a narrow gap or a thin crystal with spacing comparable to λ, a detector or screen recording intensity against angle, and a pattern of maxima and minima as for waves. The position of the minimum fits the de Broglie wavelength.

**Worked reminder.** Doubling the accelerating voltage multiplies p by √2, so λ is divided by √2. Quadrupling it halves λ.

## Compton scattering

* A photon scatters from an electron and emerges with a **longer wavelength**, because it transfers energy to the electron.
* Δλ depends on the scattering angle θ only. It is 0 for no deflection, h/mec at 90° and 2h/mec at 180°.
* It is further evidence for the particle nature of light. It is more convincing than the photoelectric effect because the photon behaves like a particle in a collision, with the shift linked to the angle.
* Comparison with two solid balls: energy and momentum are exchanged in one event, but the photon has no rest mass and moves at c.

**Method in steps**

1. Write Δλ = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² × (1 - cos θ) m.
2. Add Δλ to λi to get λf.
3. For the energy given to the electron, subtract hc/λf from hc/λi (free electron at rest).

**Worked reminder.** A photon of wavelength 0.0300 nm is scattered through 60°. cos 60° = 0.5, so Δλ = 1.21 × 10⁻¹² m and λf = **3.12 × 10⁻¹¹ m**.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Photoelectric effect | Compton scattering |
|---|---|---|
| Photon fate | Absorbed completely | Scattered with lower energy |
| Wavelength | Not measured after | Increases |
| Key equation | Emax = hf - Φ | Δλ = (h/mec)(1 - cos θ) |
| What it shows | Energy comes in quanta | Photons also carry momentum |

Do not confuse the **work function** Φ (a property of the metal) with **Emax** (a property of the emitted electrons for a given light). Do not confuse the photon wavelength with the de Broglie wavelength of an electron.

## Quick self-test

1. A metal has Φ = 2.50 eV. Find the threshold frequency.
2. Light of frequency 9.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz falls on the same metal. Find Emax in eV.
3. Light of wavelength 280 nm falls on a metal with Φ = 4.20 eV. Find Emax in eV.
4. Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 1.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
5. State why raising the intensity of light above the threshold does not change Emax.
6. Electrons with λ = 0.40 nm pass through a gap of width 2.0 nm. Find the angle of the first minimum.
7. A photon of wavelength 0.0300 nm is Compton scattered through 60°. Find its new wavelength.
8. A 2.0 mW beam has wavelength 600 nm. How many photons arrive each second?
9. An electron's accelerating voltage is raised from 150 V to 600 V. By what factor does λ change?
10. Explain why a wave model predicts a delay before emission.

### Answers

1. f₀ = Φ/h = 2.50 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ = **6.03 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
2. hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 9.0 × 10¹⁴ = 5.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.73 eV; Emax = 3.73 - 2.50 = **1.23 eV**.
3. hc/λ = 4.44 eV; Emax = **0.24 eV**.
4. λ = h/(mv) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.0 × 10⁶) = **7.28 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**.
5. More intensity means more photons per second. Each photon still has energy hf, and each acts on one electron, so the maximum energy is unchanged.
6. sin θ = 0.40 / 2.0 = 0.20, so **θ = 11.5°**.
7. Δλ = 1.21 × 10⁻¹² m; λf = **3.12 × 10⁻¹¹ m**.
8. Photon energy = 3.31 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; N = 2.0 × 10⁻³ / 3.31 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **6.03 × 10¹⁵ s⁻¹**.
9. The voltage rises by a factor 4, so p rises by 2 and λ falls from 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ m to 5.01 × 10⁻¹¹ m: it **halves**.
10. A classical wave spreads energy continuously, so a dim wave would need time to deliver enough energy to one electron. Emission is observed to be immediate.

## Nature-of-science links

The guide pairs this content with questions about how ideas change. Have a sentence ready for each.

* **Falsifying a purely wave model of light.** The photoelectric observations contradicted a prediction of the wave model, so the model had to be extended. Light needs a particle description as well.
* **Compton scattering as stronger evidence.** The shift in wavelength is measured and fits a formula in the angle, so a particle-collision picture makes a quantitative prediction that can be tested.
* **Particles that diffract.** If diffraction is a defining behaviour of waves, an observed pattern of maxima and minima from electrons gives evidence that matter has a wave aspect.
* **The Bohr model.** The guide asks whether it can help explain the photoelectric effect. Be ready to say that it also treats energy as quantised.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Leaving the answer in joules when the question asks for eV, or the other way round.
* Writing Emax = hf instead of hf - Φ.
* Saying that "brighter light gives faster electrons".
* Saying "the electron needs more energy than the work function" when the correct statement is that the photon energy must be at least Φ.
* Describing the diffraction experiment without stating the vacuum, the gap size compared with λ, or what the detector records.
* Using p = mv for an accelerated electron without first finding its speed from the energy gained.
* Forgetting that Compton scattering increases the wavelength.
* Dropping the "1 -" in the Compton factor, or working in radians when the angle is given in degrees.
* Mixing the photon wavelength and the de Broglie wavelength of the electron in a single line of working.
