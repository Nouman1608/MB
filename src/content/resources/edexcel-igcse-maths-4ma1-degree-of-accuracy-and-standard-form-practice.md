These questions cover sections **1.8 (Degree of accuracy)** and **1.9 (Standard form)** of Topic 1 in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on it. Questions 1–7 use content on both tiers. Questions 8–12 are **(Higher)**: they use the Higher tier only statements on solving problems with bounds and with standard form. A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, but show your working: method marks depend on it. The questions are different from those in the [Numbers and the number system practice](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-number-practice/), so you can use both.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-degree-of-accuracy-and-standard-form/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-degree-of-accuracy-and-standard-form-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** Round 846 291

**(a)** to the nearest 1000 **[1]**
**(b)** to the nearest 100 000 **[1]**

**2.** Write

**(a)** 0.050 784 9 correct to 3 significant figures **[1]**
**(b)** 0.050 784 9 correct to 2 decimal places **[1]**
**(c)** 29.996 correct to 2 decimal places **[1]**

**3.**

**(a)** A plank is 47 cm long, correct to the nearest centimetre. Write down the lower bound and the upper bound of its length. **[2]**
**(b)** A bag of flour has a mass of 0.38 kg, correct to 2 significant figures. Write down the lower bound and the upper bound of the mass. **[2]**
**(c)** A distance d is 52 000 m, correct to 2 significant figures. Complete the inequality ... ≤ d < ... **[1]**

**4.** Work out an estimate for (7.93 × 0.512) ÷ 0.0198. You must show your working. **[3]**

**5.**

**(a)** Write 63 800 000 in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 0.000 905 in standard form. **[1]**
**(c)** Write 7.14 × 10⁻⁴ as an ordinary number. **[1]**

**6.** Write these numbers in order of size. Start with the smallest. **[2]**

4.2 × 10⁻³    0.000 39    3.9 × 10⁻²    4.02 × 10⁻⁴

**7.** Work out each of these. Give your answers in standard form.

**(a)** (6 × 10⁵) × (7 × 10⁻⁹) **[2]**
**(b)** (1.8 × 10⁴) ÷ (4.5 × 10⁻³) **[2]**
**(c)** 3.7 × 10⁵ − 9.4 × 10⁴ **[2]**

**8.** (Higher) A metal block has a mass of 640 g, correct to the nearest 10 g. Its volume is 82 cm³, correct to the nearest cm³. Density = mass ÷ volume.

**(a)** Calculate the upper bound and the lower bound of the density of the block. **[4]**
**(b)** Use your answers to (a) to write down the density to a suitable degree of accuracy. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher) A rectangular field is 86 m long and 54 m wide. Both measurements are correct to the nearest metre.

**(a)** Work out the upper bound of the perimeter of the field. **[2]**
**(b)** Fencing is sold in rolls of exactly 25 m. Work out the least number of rolls that must be bought to be sure of fencing the whole perimeter. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out the lower bound of the area of the field. **[2]**
**(d)** The owner says the area is definitely at least 4600 m². Is the owner right? Give a reason. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher) A server stores 4.8 × 10¹⁵ bytes of data. The mean size of a file on the server is 1.6 × 10⁶ bytes.

**(a)** Work out the number of files on the server. Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**
**(b)** The amount of data stored increases by 35%. Work out the new amount in standard form. **[2]**
**(c)** 1 terabyte = 10¹² bytes. Write 4.8 × 10¹⁵ bytes in terabytes, as an ordinary number. **[1]**
**(d)** The original data is copied at 2.5 × 10⁹ bytes per second. Work out how long the copy takes. Give your answer in days, correct to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher) p = 4 × 10ᵏ, where k is an integer.

**(a)** Write p² in standard form. Give your answer in terms of k. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 1/p in standard form. Give your answer in terms of k. **[2]**
**(c)** p² = 1.6 × 10⁹. Find the value of k. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher) A space probe is 4.52 × 10⁸ km from a receiving station, correct to 3 significant figures. A radio signal travels at 3.00 × 10⁵ km per second, correct to 3 significant figures.

Calculate the upper and lower bounds of the time the signal takes to reach the station. Hence write down the time, in seconds, to a suitable degree of accuracy. Give a reason for your answer. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **846 000** [1]
**(b)** **800 000** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer like "846" for the nearest 1000 changes the size of the number and scores 0; the place-value zeros are part of the answer.

**2. (a)** **0.0508** [1]
**(b)** **0.05** [1]
**(c)** 29.99|6 rounds up through 29.99 → 30.00, so **30.00** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) "30" is marked wrong because 2 d.p. requires two digits after the point; in (a) counting the leading zero of 0.05 as a significant figure gives 0.051, which scores 0.

**3. (a)** Lower bound **46.5 cm** [1]; upper bound **47.5 cm** [1]
**(b)** Unit is 0.01, half is 0.005 [1]: **0.375 kg** and **0.385 kg** [1]
**(c)** Unit is 1000, half is 500: **51 500 ≤ d < 52 500** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Bounds written the wrong way round (upper as lower) usually lose the mark for that pair, so label each one clearly.

**4.** 7.93 → 8, 0.512 → 0.5, 0.0198 → 0.02 [1]
(8 × 0.5) ÷ 0.02 = 4 ÷ 0.02 [1] = **200** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The first mark is for showing at least two of the numbers rounded to 1 s.f.; the calculator value 205.06 on its own scores 0 because no estimate is shown.

**5. (a)** **6.38 × 10⁷** [1]
**(b)** **9.05 × 10⁻⁴** [1]
**(c)** **0.000 714** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 63.8 × 10⁶ has the right value but is not standard form, so it does not earn the mark in (a).

**6.** Convert to the same form, e.g. 0.000 39 = 3.9 × 10⁻⁴ and 4.02 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.000 402 [1]
Order: **0.000 39, 4.02 × 10⁻⁴, 4.2 × 10⁻³, 3.9 × 10⁻²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark is usually available for three numbers in the correct order, so converting all four to one form first protects at least the method mark.

**7. (a)** 6 × 7 = 42 and 10⁵ × 10⁻⁹ = 10⁻⁴, giving 42 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **4.2 × 10⁻³** [1]
**(b)** 1.8 ÷ 4.5 = 0.4 and 10⁴ ÷ 10⁻³ = 10⁷, giving 0.4 × 10⁷ [1] = **4 × 10⁶** [1]
**(c)** 370 000 − 94 000 [1] = 276 000 = **2.76 × 10⁵** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The method mark is often earned by an unadjusted answer such as 42 × 10⁻⁴, but the accuracy mark needs it in proper standard form.

**8. (a)** Mass: 635 ≤ m < 645; volume: 81.5 ≤ V < 82.5 [1]
Upper bound = 645 ÷ 81.5 [1] = **7.914 g/cm³** (4 s.f.) [1]
Lower bound = 635 ÷ 82.5 = **7.697 g/cm³** (4 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** To 2 s.f. the bounds give 7.9 and 7.7, which differ; to 1 s.f. both give 8 [1]. Density = **8 g/cm³** (1 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 645 ÷ 82.5 for the upper bound is a standard wrong pairing and loses the method mark; the reason in (b) must refer to both bounds rounding to the same value.

**9. (a)** Upper bounds 86.5 m and 54.5 m [1]; 2 × (86.5 + 54.5) = **282 m** [1]
**(b)** 282 ÷ 25 = 11.28 [1], so **12 rolls** [1]
**(c)** Lower bounds 85.5 m and 53.5 m; 85.5 × 53.5 [1] = **4574.25 m²** [1]
**(d)** **No**: the lower bound of the area, 4574.25 m², is less than 4600 m², so the area could be under 4600 m² [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) using the measured perimeter, 280 m, gives 11.2 and still 12 rolls, but no method mark for bounds is earned; in (d) "No" with no comparison scores 0.

**10. (a)** (4.8 × 10¹⁵) ÷ (1.6 × 10⁶) [1] = **3 × 10⁹** files [1]
**(b)** 4.8 × 10¹⁵ × 1.35 [1] = **6.48 × 10¹⁵** bytes [1]
**(c)** (4.8 × 10¹⁵) ÷ 10¹² = **4800** terabytes [1]
**(d)** (4.8 × 10¹⁵) ÷ (2.5 × 10⁹) = 1.92 × 10⁶ seconds; ÷ (60 × 60 × 24) [1] = **22.2 days** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d) the time-conversion step must be visible; dividing by 3600 only (giving hours) and calling it days loses the accuracy mark.

**11. (a)** p² = 16 × 10²ᵏ [1] = **1.6 × 10²ᵏ⁺¹** [1]
**(b)** 1/p = (1/4) × 10⁻ᵏ = 0.25 × 10⁻ᵏ [1] = **2.5 × 10⁻ᵏ⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** 2k + 1 = 9, so **k = 4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 16 × 10²ᵏ earns the method mark only; the answer mark needs the front number between 1 and 10 with the power adjusted to 2k + 1.

**12.** Distance bounds: 4.515 × 10⁸ ≤ d < 4.525 × 10⁸ km [1]
Speed bounds: 2.995 × 10⁵ ≤ v < 3.005 × 10⁵ km/s [1]
Upper bound of time = upper distance ÷ lower speed = (4.525 × 10⁸) ÷ (2.995 × 10⁵) [1] = 1510.85 s [1]
Lower bound of time = lower distance ÷ upper speed = (4.515 × 10⁸) ÷ (3.005 × 10⁵) [1] = 1502.50 s [1]
To 3 s.f. the bounds give 1510 and 1500, which differ; to 2 s.f. both give 1500 [1]
Time = **1500 s (1.5 × 10³ s) to 2 s.f.** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs both bounds shown rounded to the chosen accuracy; giving 1510 s because it is the "more accurate" bound scores 0 for the conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping place-value zeros when rounding large numbers (846 instead of 846 000).
- Counting the zero in 0.05... as a significant figure.
- Writing 30 instead of 30.00 when 2 d.p. is asked for.
- Estimating with values rounded to the nearest whole number rather than 1 s.f.
- Leaving answers such as 42 × 10⁻⁴ or 0.4 × 10⁷ unadjusted.
- Subtracting 9.4 from 3.7 as if the powers matched.
- (Higher) Pairing upper ÷ upper for the upper bound of a quotient.
- (Higher) Using the measured value, not the upper bound, when the question says "to be sure".
- (Higher) Stating a "suitable degree of accuracy" without showing both bounds rounded.
