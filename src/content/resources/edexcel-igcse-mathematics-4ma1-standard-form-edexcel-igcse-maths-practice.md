This set covers **section 1.9, Standard form**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. **Tiers:** questions marked **(Higher tier only)** are problems involving standard form, which the specification lists for the Higher Tier only. Everything else is for both tiers.

## Section A

**1.** Write in standard form

**(a)** 408 000 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.000 072 **[1]**

**2.** Write as ordinary numbers

**(a)** 3.15 × 10⁴ **[1]**

**(b)** 6.02 × 10⁻³ **[1]**

**3.** Write 56 × 10⁻⁴ in standard form. **[1]**

**4.** Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: 0.000 31, 3.2 × 10⁻⁴, 2.9 × 10⁻⁴, 0.0003 **[2]**

**5.** Work out (5 × 10³) × (9 × 10⁶). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**6.** Work out (3.6 × 10⁻²) ÷ (1.2 × 10⁴). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Give each answer in standard form.

**(a)** Work out 8.1 × 10⁶ + 7.4 × 10⁵. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out 2.3 × 10⁻³ − 6 × 10⁻⁴. **[2]**

**8.** Use your calculator to work out (4.7 × 10⁸)² ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻³). Give your answer in standard form, correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** A grain of sand has a mass of 6.5 × 10⁻⁴ g. How many grains of sand of this mass are there in 2.6 kg of sand? Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** A country has a population of 4.8 × 10⁷ and an area of 5.0 × 10⁵ km².

**(a)** Work out the population density, in people per km². **[2]**

**(b)** The population increases by 12%. Work out the new population, giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Light travels at 3.0 × 10⁸ m per second. The distance from the Sun to the Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. Work out the time light takes to travel from the Sun to the Earth. Give your answer in minutes and seconds. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** x = 2 × 10ᵏ, where k is an integer.

**(a)** Write x³ in standard form, in terms of k. **[2]**

**(b)** Write 5x in standard form, in terms of k. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out √(4.9 × 10¹¹), without a calculator. Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**13.** Write 4 × 10³ + 5 × 10⁻¹ as an ordinary number. **[1]**

**14.** **(Higher tier only)** The mass of one atom of an element is 3.3 × 10⁻²³ g. Work out the number of atoms in 1 kg of the element. Give your answer in standard form, correct to 2 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **4.08 × 10⁵** [1]

**(b)** **7.2 × 10⁻⁵** [1]

**2. (a)** **31 500** [1]

**(b)** **0.006 02** [1]

**3.** **5.6 × 10⁻³** [1]

**4.** Writes all in the same form, e.g. 0.000 31 = 3.1 × 10⁻⁴ and 0.0003 = 3 × 10⁻⁴ [1]; **2.9 × 10⁻⁴, 0.0003, 0.000 31, 3.2 × 10⁻⁴** [1]

**5.** 45 × 10⁹ [1] = **4.5 × 10¹⁰** [1]

**6.** 3.6 ÷ 1.2 = 3 and 10⁻² ÷ 10⁴ = 10⁻⁶ [1]; **3 × 10⁻⁶** [1]

**7. (a)** 8 100 000 + 740 000 = 8 840 000 [1] = **8.84 × 10⁶** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Writing 7.4 × 10⁵ as 0.74 × 10⁶ and adding 8.1 + 0.74 gives the same result without writing out the zeros.

**(b)** 0.0023 − 0.0006 = 0.0017 [1] = **1.7 × 10⁻³** [1]

**8.** 8.836 × 10¹⁹ [1] = **8.84 × 10¹⁹** (3 s.f.) [1]

**9.** 2.6 kg = 2600 g = 2.6 × 10³ g [1]; (2.6 × 10³) ÷ (6.5 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = **4 × 10⁶ grains** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Dividing 2.6 by 6.5 gives 0.4, and 10³ ÷ 10⁻⁴ = 10⁷, so the unadjusted result is 0.4 × 10⁷; it must be rewritten as 4 × 10⁶.

**10. (a)** (4.8 × 10⁷) ÷ (5.0 × 10⁵) [1] = **96 people per km²** [1]

**(b)** 4.8 × 10⁷ × 1.12 [1] = **5.376 × 10⁷** [1]

**11.** (1.5 × 10¹¹) ÷ (3.0 × 10⁸) [1] = 500 seconds [1] = **8 minutes 20 seconds** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 500 seconds is 8.33… minutes; writing 8.33 minutes or "8 minutes 33 seconds" confuses decimal minutes with seconds.

**12. (a)** 2³ × 10³ᵏ [1] = **8 × 10³ᵏ** [1]

**(b)** 10 × 10ᵏ = **1 × 10ᵏ⁺¹** [1]

**(c)** √(49 × 10¹⁰) [1] = **7 × 10⁵** [1]

**13.** 4000 + 0.5 = **4000.5** [1]

**14.** 1 kg = 1000 g = 1 × 10³ g [1]; (1 × 10³) ÷ (3.3 × 10⁻²³) = 3.0303… × 10²⁵ [1]; **3.0 × 10²⁵ atoms** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The zero in 3.0 must be written: it shows the answer is given to 2 significant figures.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 40.8 × 10⁴ in question 1(a).
- Moving the point right instead of left in question 2(b).
- Ordering by the a values only in question 4.
- Leaving 45 × 10⁹ unadjusted in question 5.
- Forgetting to convert kg to g in question 9.
- Leaving 10 × 10ᵏ in question 12(b), where a = 10 is not allowed.
