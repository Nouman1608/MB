## What the specification lists

- Explain HRM objectives; how and why a business uses them.
- Explain the need for clear HRM objectives.
- Evaluate their usefulness to a business and its stakeholders.
- Evaluate their importance in achieving the business's objectives.
- Recommend and justify HR objectives for a business.

## Core recall

### Definitions

- **HRM:** managing the people who work for a business: planning numbers, recruiting, training, rewarding and managing performance and relations.
- **HR objective:** a target set by the HR function to ensure the business has the right people, with the right skills, working effectively at an acceptable cost.
- HR objectives are functional objectives, set within the corporate objectives.

### Types of HR objective

| Type | Example target | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers and skills | Recruit 150 staff for new sites within a year | Posts filled |
| Labour cost | Keep wages at 50% of revenue or below | Wage bill ÷ revenue |
| Retention | Cut labour turnover to 15% | Labour turnover |
| Productivity | Raise output per employee | Output ÷ employees |
| Motivation | Raise staff survey score | Survey, absence |
| Training | Close skills gaps | Training days, qualifications |
| Flexibility | Adjust staffing to demand | Share of flexible contracts |
| Compliance | Meet the law; no tribunal losses | Claims |
| Employer image | Attract good applicants | Applications per vacancy |

### SMART

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound. "Cut turnover from 27.5% to 15% in 18 months" is SMART; "improve retention" is not.

### How and why used

- Direction and coordination of HR activities.
- Resource allocation.
- Targets and measurement.
- Motivation of managers and staff.
- Support for the corporate strategy.

### Formulae (standard forms; the specification gives none)

- **Labour turnover (%)** = employees leaving in a period ÷ average employees × 100.
- **Absence rate (%)** = days lost ÷ total working days × 100.
- **Labour cost ratio (%)** = wage bill ÷ revenue × 100.

### Benefits and limits of objectives

| Benefits | Limits |
|---|---|
| Clarity and focus | People focus on what is measured |
| Measurement and review | Unrealistic targets cause cynicism |
| Planning | Objectives can conflict (cost against development) |
| Staff know what is expected | External factors (labour shortage) can block targets |
| Link to corporate aims | Too many objectives dilute effort |

### Linking to business objectives

- Growth: recruit and train for new sites.
- Profit: control labour cost; raise productivity.
- Quality: retain and train skilled staff.
- Survival: comply with the law.
- Conflicts: headcount cuts against service quality; short-term profit against training.

## Worked reminder

Larkfield Care (invented), 80 staff.

- Turnover: 22 ÷ 80 × 100 = **27.5%**. Target 15% means 12 leavers, 10 fewer.
- Saving at £3,200 a leaver: 10 × £3,200 = **£32,000**. Programme cost £18,000 gives net **£14,000**.
- Absence: 1,440 ÷ (80 × 240) × 100 = **7.5%**. Target 5% = 960 days, 480 fewer, saving 480 × £110 = **£52,800**.
- Labour cost ratio: £2,400,000 ÷ £4,800,000 = **50%**.

## Recommending objectives: a short method

1. Identify the main business objective and main people problem.
2. Choose two or three HR objectives that address it.
3. Make them measurable and time-bound.
4. State how and at what cost.
5. Note conflicts and the effect on stakeholders.
6. Say what it depends on.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HRM | Management of a business's people |
| Corporate objective | A goal for the whole business |
| Labour turnover | Proportion of the workforce leaving in a period |
| Retention | Keeping staff in the business |
| Absenteeism | Staff absence from work |
| Productivity | Output per employee or per hour |
| Engagement | Staff commitment to the business and its goals |

## Exam traps

- An objective is a target; recruitment and training are activities.
- Use the **average** number of staff in labour turnover.
- Some turnover is healthy; zero is not the aim.
- Link HR objectives to the business's own objectives.
- Identify conflicts, such as cutting cost against investing in people.
- Give a judgement that depends on the case.

## Self-test

1. Define an HR objective.
2. Why should HR objectives be linked to corporate objectives?
3. Write one SMART HR objective.
4. A business averages 200 staff and 30 leave. Calculate labour turnover.
5. Staff work 230 days each. Calculate total working days for 200 staff.
6. 2,300 days are lost to absence. Calculate the absence rate.
7. Wages are £1.5 million and revenue is £6 million. Calculate the labour cost ratio.
8. Give one reason why zero labour turnover is not a good target.
9. Give one way in which HR objectives may conflict with cost objectives.
10. Give two stakeholders who benefit from clear HR objectives.

### Answers

1. A target set by the HR function so that the business has the right people, with the right skills, at an acceptable cost.
2. So that people management supports what the whole business is trying to achieve, and resources are not wasted on unrelated aims.
3. For example: reduce labour turnover from 20% to 12% within two years.
4. 30 ÷ 200 × 100 = **15%**.
5. 200 × 230 = **46,000 days**.
6. 2,300 ÷ 46,000 × 100 = **5%**.
7. £1.5m ÷ £6m × 100 = **25%**.
8. Some turnover brings new ideas and allows poor performers to be replaced; zero turnover can mean stagnation.
9. Investing in training and pay to retain staff raises labour costs in the short term.
10. Any two: employees (know what is expected); owners (see people costs managed); customers (better service).
