## Section A

**1.** Write down the mathematical name for

**(a)** a line segment joining two points on the circumference, **[1]**

**(b)** the region between a chord and an arc, **[1]**

**(c)** the region between two radii and an arc. **[1]**

**2.** A tangent from a point P touches a circle with centre O at T. The radius is 8 cm and OP = 17 cm. Work out the length PT. **[2]**

**3.** Tangents from P touch a circle with centre O at A and B. Angle AOB = 118°. Work out angle APB. Give reasons. **[2]**

**4.** A chord of length 10 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 13 cm. Work out the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord. **[2]**

**5.** AB is a chord of a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = 96°. Work out angle OAB. Give a reason. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** **(Higher tier only)** A, B and C lie on a circle with centre O. C is on the major arc AB. Angle ACB = 53°.

**(a)** Work out angle AOB. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** D is a point on the minor arc AB. Work out angle ADB. Give a reason. **[2]**

**7.** **(Higher tier only)** AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. Angle ABC = 38°. Work out angle CAB. Give a reason. **[2]**

**8.** **(Higher tier only)** Two opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are (3x + 5)° and (2x + 25)°. Work out the value of x and the size of both angles. **[3]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** A tangent touches a circle with centre O at P. The chord PQ makes an angle of 71° with the tangent. R is a point on the circle in the alternate segment.

**(a)** Write down angle PRQ and name the theorem. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out angle POQ. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Chords AB and CD of a circle meet at X inside the circle. AX = 5 cm, XB = 8 cm and CX = 4 cm. Work out the length of CD. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** From a point P outside a circle, one straight line meets the circle at A and then B, with PA = 6 cm and AB = 10 cm. A second line from P meets the circle at C and then D, with PC = 8 cm. Work out CD. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral, with vertices in that order around the circle. The diagonals AC and BD are drawn. Angle BAD = 104° and angle ABD = 41°.

**(a)** Work out angle BCD. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out angle ACD. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** Work out angle ADB. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Chord [1].

**(b)** Segment [1].

**(c)** Sector [1].

**2.** Angle OTP = 90° (tangent perpendicular to radius), so PT² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225 [1]; PT = **15 cm** [1].

**3.** Angles OAP and OBP are 90° because the tangent is perpendicular to the radius [1]. Angles in a quadrilateral add to 360°, so angle APB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 118° = **62°** [1].

**4.** The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half the chord is 5 cm [1]. Distance = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = **12 cm** [1].

**5.** OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles with equal base angles [1]. Angle OAB = (180° − 96°) ÷ 2 = **42°** [1].

**6. (a)** Angle AOB = 2 × 53° = **106°** [1]; the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference [1].

**(b)** ACBD is a cyclic quadrilateral, so angle ADB = 180° − 53° = **127°** [1]; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° [1].

**7.** Angle ACB = 90° because the angle in a semicircle is a right angle [1]. Angle CAB = 180° − 90° − 38° = **52°** [1].

**8.** 3x + 5 + 2x + 25 = 180 (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°) [1]; 5x = 150, so x = **30** [1]; the angles are **95°** and **85°** [1].

**9. (a)** Angle PRQ = **71°** [1], by the alternate segment theorem [1].

**(b)** Angle POQ = 2 × 71° = **142°** [1] (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).

**10.** 5 × 8 = 4 × XD, so XD = 10 cm [1]; CD = 4 + 10 = **14 cm** [1].

**11.** PB = 6 + 10 = 16 cm [1]. 6 × 16 = 8 × PD, so PD = 12 cm [1]. CD = 12 − 8 = **4 cm** [1].

**12. (a)** Angle BCD = 180° − 104° = **76°** [1]; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° [1].

**(b)** Angles ABD and ACD both stand on the chord AD, on the same side, so angle ACD = **41°** [1]; angles in the same segment are equal [1].

**(c)** In triangle ABD, angle ADB = 180° − 104° − 41° = **35°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: swapping sector and segment. A segment is cut off by a chord; a sector is cut off by two radii.
- Question 3: halving 118° or subtracting it from 180°, instead of using the two right angles in the quadrilateral.
- Question 6(b): writing 53° because D is "also on the circumference". D is on the other arc, so the angles add to 180°.
- Question 11: using AB = 10 in place of PB = 16. Each length in the external rule is measured from P.
- Any question with "give reasons": writing "circle theorem" or "angles in a circle" instead of the full statement.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a sector and a segment?**
A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc, like a slice of pizza. A segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.

**When do angles at the circumference add to 180° instead of being equal?**
When they stand on the same chord but on opposite sides of it. The four points then form a cyclic quadrilateral, and opposite angles add to 180°.
