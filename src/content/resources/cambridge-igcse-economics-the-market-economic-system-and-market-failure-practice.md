## Questions

**1.** Define a market economic system. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **two** advantages of a market economic system. **[4]**

**3.** Explain **two** disadvantages of a market economic system. **[4]**

**4.** A fish-processing plant (invented) has a private cost of 35 per tonne. Its waste harms a nearby fishing village at a cost of 14 per tonne.

**(a)** Calculate the social cost per tonne. **[1]**
**(b)** The plant processes 3,000 tonnes. Calculate the total external cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the plant ignores this cost when it decides how much to produce. **[2]**

**5.** A village of 400 households (invented) needs a flood-warning siren costing 8,000. The cost would be shared equally, but payment is voluntary and only 60 households pay.

**(a)** Calculate each household's equal share. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the revenue collected and the shortfall. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this shows that a private firm is unlikely to provide the siren. **[3]**

**6.** Adult literacy classes (invented) give each learner a private benefit of 45 and an external benefit of 18.

**(a)** Define a merit good. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the social benefit per learner. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the classes are likely to be under-consumed if left to the market. **[3]**

**7.** In Country A (invented) 12,000 units a month of a harmful product are bought. The socially best level is 9,000.

**(a)** Calculate the over-consumption as a percentage of the socially best level. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why consumption of a demerit good is above the socially best level. **[3]**

**8.** Before one ferry company takes over all routes in Region S (invented), the fare is 40 and 5,000 passengers travel a week. Afterwards the fare is 52 and 3,500 travel. The cost of carrying each passenger is 30.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the fare and in the number of passengers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the company's weekly profit before and after the takeover. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this is an abuse of monopoly power and a market failure. **[3]**

**9.** A quarry in District V (invented) closes and 360 workers lose their jobs. A new depot offers 90 jobs locally.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the 360 workers who remain unemployed if all 90 jobs are filled by them. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how geographical and occupational immobility can each stop the remaining workers finding work. **[4]**

**10.** Good M is a merit good and Good N is a demerit good (both invented).

| | Quantity bought in the market | Socially best quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Good M | 2,400 | 3,200 |
| Good N | 7,800 | 6,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the under-consumption of Good M as a percentage of the socially best quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the over-consumption of Good N as a percentage of the socially best quantity. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what these figures show about the allocation of resources. **[4]**

**11.** "A market economic system always allocates resources efficiently." Discuss this view. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A system in which buyers and sellers decide what, how and for whom to produce through the price mechanism [1], with most resources privately owned and little government direction [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that mentions only "free trade" gains less than one that names the price mechanism and private ownership.

**2.** First advantage: consumer choice [1]; firms compete to supply what people will pay for, so a wide range of goods is available [1]. Second advantage: efficiency [1]; competition pushes firms to cut costs and prices, and profit rewards firms that use resources well [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each advantage needs a developed reason. A list of four labels with no explanation does not show understanding.

**3.** First disadvantage: public goods are not provided [1]; non-payers cannot be excluded, so firms cannot earn revenue [1]. Second disadvantage: inequality [1]; those with little income have little buying power, so their wants go unmet even when needs are great [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two different disadvantages are asked for. Two points about the same problem in different words show only one idea, so choose two clearly different ones.

**4. (a)** 35 + 14 = **49** per tonne [1].
**(b)** 14 × 3,000 = **42,000** [1].
**(c)** The plant pays only its private costs [1]. The village bears the harm and it does not appear in the plant's accounts, so it does not reduce the plant's profit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the addition and the multiplication. State which cost is external; do not use "social cost" for the 14.

**5. (a)** 8,000 ÷ 400 = **20** [1].
**(b)** Revenue = 60 × 20 = **1,200** [1]. Shortfall = 8,000 − 1,200 = **6,800** [1].
**(c)** The siren is non-excludable, so households that do not pay still benefit [1]. This gives them an incentive to free ride, as 340 did here [1]. The firm cannot cover its cost of 8,000, so it will not supply the siren although everyone would gain [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The words "free rider" are useful but need linking to lost revenue. Naming the term alone gains little.

**6. (a)** A good that society judges to be beneficial [1], and that is under-consumed if left to the market [1].
**(b)** 45 + 18 = **63** [1].
**(c)** Learners consider only the private benefit of 45 [1] and may undervalue the long-term gain or be unable to afford the classes [1]. The external benefit of 18 to others goes unrewarded, so less is bought than is socially worthwhile [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Under-consumption needs a reason. State what the buyer leaves out of the decision.

**7. (a)** Excess = 12,000 − 9,000 = 3,000 [1]. 3,000 ÷ 9,000 × 100 = **33.3%** [1].
**(b)** Consumers may underestimate the harm to themselves, because of poor information or habit [1]. They compare price with private benefit and ignore the full cost to health and to others [1]. Demand is therefore higher than it would be if the full cost were counted, so too many resources go to the product [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The base of a percentage must match the question. Here it is the socially best level, not the market level.

**8. (a)** Fare: (52 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **30%** rise [1]. Passengers: (5,000 − 3,500) ÷ 5,000 × 100 = **30%** fall [1].
**(b)** Before: (40 − 30) × 5,000 = **50,000** [1]. After: (52 − 30) × 3,500 = **77,000** [1].
**(c)** The firm has no rivals, so it can raise the fare without losing all its customers [1]. Output falls while profit rises, so consumers pay more for less [1]. Resources are under-used on this service relative to what passengers would buy at a competitive fare, which is misallocation [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both price and output, and say why the firm can do this (barriers to entry or no substitutes).

**9. (a)** Unemployed = 360 − 90 = 270 [1]. 270 ÷ 360 × 100 = **75%** [1].
**(b)** Geographical immobility: workers may not move to jobs in other regions because of family ties or high housing costs [1], so vacancies elsewhere stay unfilled [1]. Occupational immobility: quarry skills may not match the jobs on offer [1], so workers need retraining before they can take them, and some stay unemployed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the two types separate. Writing "they cannot move" for both loses the distinction.

**10. (a)** Shortfall = 3,200 − 2,400 = 800 [1]. 800 ÷ 3,200 × 100 = **25%** below [1].
**(b)** Excess = 7,800 − 6,000 = 1,800 [1]. 1,800 ÷ 6,000 × 100 = **30%** above [1].
**(c)** Good M: too few resources go to it, so benefits to society are lost [1], for example in health or skills [1]. Good N: too many resources go to it, so units are produced where social cost exceeds social benefit [1], and those resources could produce goods worth more [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each figure to a direction (too little or too much) and to the opportunity cost of misallocated resources.

**11.** Points for the view: prices signal what consumers want [1]; competition lowers costs [1]; profit directs resources to the most wanted goods [1]. Points against: public goods are not provided [1]; external costs and demerit goods lead to over-consumption [1]; merit goods and external benefits lead to under-consumption [1]; monopoly power restricts output [1]. Judgement: the view holds for goods that can be sold to individuals in competitive markets, but "always" is too strong because the market fails in the areas above [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs a final judgement that picks a side and gives a reason. Two lists with no conclusion are incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only one feature of a public good. Both non-excludable and non-rival are needed.
- Using "social cost" when only the external part is asked for.
- Describing under-consumption of a demerit good or over-consumption of a merit good.
- Treating a lack of money as the only reason for under-consumption of merit goods.
- Not saying why a monopoly can raise price (barriers to entry, no close substitutes).
- Writing a percentage with the wrong base.
- Leaving out units and working in a calculation.
- Ending an evaluation question without a judgement.
