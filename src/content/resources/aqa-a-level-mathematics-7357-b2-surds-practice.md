These questions cover content statement **B2** of AQA A-level Mathematics (7357), Section B: Algebra and functions: "Use and manipulate surds, including rationalising the denominator." Answer without a calculator and give every answer in exact form. Revise first with the [B2 surds study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-b2-surds/) and the [B2 revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-b2-surds-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Write 2√48 − √75 + √3 in the form k√3. **[2]**

**(b)** Write (√6 × √15)/√10 as an integer. **[1]**

**2.** Expand and simplify:

**(a)** (4 − √7)² **[2]**

**(b)** (2√3 + √5)(2√3 − √5) **[1]**

**3.** Without a calculator, write 3√6, 4√3 and 2√13 in ascending order. **[2]**

**4.** Rationalise the denominator of 12/(3√2 − √6), simplifying your answer. **[3]**

**5.** Solve x√7 − 3 = x + 1, giving your answer in the form a + b√7, where a and b are rational. **[3]**

**6.** Write (√5)³ − √20 in the form k√5. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Show that (5 + √3)/(2 + √3) + (5 − √3)/(2 − √3) = 14. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** Find the positive integers a and b such that (a − b√5)² = 49 − 12√5. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence write √(49 − 12√5) in the form p√5 + q, where p and q are integers. **[2]**

**9.** Solve x² − 2√3x − 9 = 0, giving your answers in simplest surd form. **[3]**

**10.** A right-angled triangle has its two shorter sides of length (√5 + 1) cm and (√5 − 1) cm.

**(a)** Find the exact area of the triangle. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the exact length of the hypotenuse, in simplest form. **[3]**

**11.** The points A and B have coordinates (1, √3) and (√3, 3).

**(a)** Show that the gradient of AB is √3. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact length of AB in the form p√3 + q, where p and q are integers. **[2]**

**12.** Solve 2ˣ × √8 = 16√2. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 2√48 = 8√3 and √75 = 5√3 [1]. 8√3 − 5√3 + √3 = **4√3** [1]

**(b)** √90/√10 = √9 = **3** [1]

**2. (a)** 16 − 8√7 + 7 [1] = **23 − 8√7** [1]

**(b)** (2√3)² − (√5)² = 12 − 5 = **7** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (a) the middle term is 2 × 4 × √7 = 8√7, and it is negative. Writing 16 + 7 = 23 alone misses it.

**3.** 3√6 = √54, 4√3 = √48 and 2√13 = √52 [1]. Ascending order: **4√3, 2√13, 3√6** [1]

**4.** Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 3√2 + √6 [1]. Denominator: (3√2)² − (√6)² = 18 − 6 = 12 [1]. So 12(3√2 + √6)/12 = **3√2 + √6** [1]

*Examiner insight:* square the coefficient 3 as well as √2. Using 6 − 6 = 0 for the denominator is a sign that (3√2)² was taken as 6.

**5.** x√7 − x = 4, so x(√7 − 1) = 4 [1]. x = 4/(√7 − 1) = 4(√7 + 1)/(7 − 1) [1] = (2√7 + 2)/3, so **x = 2/3 + (2/3)√7** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a and b may be fractions here, because the question says rational. Check: both sides simplify to (5 + 2√7)/3.

**6.** (√5)³ = 5√5 and √20 = 2√5 [1]. 5√5 − 2√5 = **3√5** [1]

**7.** First fraction: multiply by (2 − √3)/(2 − √3); the denominator is 4 − 3 = 1 [1]. (5 + √3)(2 − √3) = 10 − 5√3 + 2√3 − 3 = 7 − 3√3 [1]. Second fraction: (5 − √3)(2 + √3)/1 = 10 + 5√3 − 2√3 − 3 = 7 + 3√3 [1]. Sum: (7 − 3√3) + (7 + 3√3) = **14** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in a "show that", write both rationalised fractions in full before adding. Jumping straight to 14 because "the surds must cancel" earns only the final mark at best.

**8. (a)** (a − b√5)² = a² + 5b² − 2ab√5 [1]. So a² + 5b² = 49 and ab = 6 [1]. Testing factor pairs of 6: a = 2, b = 3 gives 4 + 45 = 49, so **a = 2, b = 3** [1]

**(b)** 2 − 3√5 is negative, and a principal square root is positive [1], so √(49 − 12√5) = 3√5 − 2, giving **p = 3, q = −2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the other factor pairs (1 and 6, 3 and 2, 6 and 1) give 181, 29 and 41, so testing them is quick. Writing √(49 − 12√5) = 2 − 3√5 loses the mark because that value is negative.

**9.** x = (2√3 ± √(12 + 36))/2 [1]. √48 = 4√3 [1]. x = (2√3 ± 4√3)/2, so **x = 3√3 or x = −√3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check with the product of the roots: 3√3 × (−√3) = −9, the constant term.

**10. (a)** Area = ½(√5 + 1)(√5 − 1) [1] = ½(5 − 1) = **2 cm²** [1]

**(b)** (√5 + 1)² = 6 + 2√5 and (√5 − 1)² = 6 − 2√5 [1]. Sum = 12 [1]. Hypotenuse = √12 = **2√3 cm** [1]

**11. (a)** Gradient = (3 − √3)/(√3 − 1) [1]. The numerator is √3(√3 − 1) [1], so the gradient is √3(√3 − 1)/(√3 − 1) = √3 [1]

(Rationalising instead: (3 − √3)(√3 + 1)/(3 − 1) = (3√3 + 3 − 3 − √3)/2 = √3 also earns full marks.)

**(b)** AB² = (√3 − 1)² + (3 − √3)² = (4 − 2√3) + (12 − 6√3) = 16 − 8√3 [1]. (2√3 − 2)² = 12 − 8√3 + 4 = 16 − 8√3 and 2√3 − 2 > 0, so **AB = 2√3 − 2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the quick route uses part (a): the rise is √3 times the run, so AB = (√3 − 1)√(1 + 3) = 2(√3 − 1).

**12.** √8 = 2^(3/2) [1]. 16√2 = 2⁴ × 2^(1/2) = 2^(9/2) [1]. So 2^(x + 3/2) = 2^(9/2), giving **x = 3** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Squaring k√n as k²√n or kn instead of k²n.
- Losing the middle term when squaring a binomial with a surd.
- Multiplying only the denominator by the conjugate.
- Not simplifying √48 or √12 in the final answer.
- Choosing the negative value for a principal square root.
- Giving decimal answers when exact forms are asked for.
