These questions cover section **3.3.9 Carboxylic acids and derivatives** of the AQA AS and A-level
Chemistry specification (7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for exams from June 2016 onwards:
3.3.9.1 Carboxylic acids and esters, 3.3.9.2 Acylation and Required practical 10. The whole section is
**A-level only**. It is examined on A-level Paper 2, and Paper 3 can test any content and any practical
skill. Show your working in every calculation.

Use Ar values: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** An ester has the structural formula CH₃CH₂COOCH₃.

**(a)** Name the ester. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the carboxylic acid and the alcohol that react to make it. **[2]**

**2.** Propanoic acid is a weak acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of propanoic acid with sodium carbonate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what is meant by a weak acid. **[1]**

**3.** Methyl ethanoate is made by warming ethanoic acid with methanol.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction and name a suitable catalyst. **[2]**
**(b)** 9.00 g of ethanoic acid reacts with excess methanol. 7.77 g of methyl ethanoate is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**4.** Methyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃, is hydrolysed.

**(a)** Give the organic products when it is heated under reflux with (i) dilute hydrochloric acid and (ii) sodium hydroxide solution. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why hydrolysis with sodium hydroxide goes to completion but hydrolysis with acid does not. **[2]**

**5.** Glyceryl trioctadecanoate, (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₃C₃H₅ (Mr = 890.0), is an animal fat. It is boiled with sodium hydroxide solution to make soap.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of sodium octadecanoate (Mr = 306.0) formed from 44.5 g of the fat. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the minimum mass of sodium hydroxide needed. **[1]**

**6.** A vegetable oil contains glyceryl trihexadecanoate, (C₁₅H₃₁COO)₃C₃H₅ (Mr = 806.0).

**(a)** State what biodiesel is. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of methyl hexadecanoate (Mr = 270.0) made from 403 g of this oil with methanol. **[3]**

**7.** Ethanoyl chloride reacts with methanol.

**(a)** Name the mechanism and outline it, describing each curly arrow and the intermediate. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**8.** Propanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂COCl, reacts with ammonia and with ethylamine.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction with excess ammonia. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the organic product with ethylamine and give its structural formula. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why two moles of ethylamine are used for each mole of propanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**9.** Write equations for the reactions of ethanoic anhydride with:

**(a)** water **[1]**
**(b)** propan-1-ol **[1]**
**(c)** excess methylamine. **[1]**

**10.** Aspirin, CH₃COOC₆H₄COOH (Mr = 180.0), is made from 2-hydroxybenzoic acid, HOC₆H₄COOH (Mr = 138.0). Ethanoic anhydride has Mr = 102.0 and ethanoyl chloride has Mr = 78.5.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction with ethanoic anhydride. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for aspirin by the anhydride route and by the acyl chloride route. **[3]**
**(c)** In a school preparation, 2.76 g of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid gives 2.52 g of aspirin. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**
**(d)** Industry uses ethanoic anhydride even though its atom economy is lower. Give two reasons. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about Required practical 10. A student has crude aspirin containing small amounts of soluble and insoluble impurities.

**(a)** Describe how the student should purify the aspirin by recrystallisation. **[4]**
**(b)** Sample P melts sharply at the data value. Sample Q melts over a range of several degrees, starting below the data value. Explain what this shows. **[2]**

**12.** Compound X, C₄H₈O₂, is a neutral liquid with a sweet smell. 4.40 g of X is heated under reflux with sodium hydroxide solution. The products are sodium ethanoate and an alcohol Z. When Z is heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI), ethanoic acid forms.

**(a)** Identify X and Z, explaining your reasoning. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of sodium hydroxide that reacts with 4.40 g of X. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest a reagent that converts Z into X in a reaction that is not reversible. **[1]**
**(d)** Outline how a pure sample of liquid X could be obtained after making it from ethanoic acid and Z. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Methyl propanoate** [1]
**(b)** Propanoic acid [1]; methanol [1]
*Examiner insight:* the alcohol part is written first in the name, so swapping the two words names a different ester and scores nothing.

**2. (a)** 2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + CO₂ + H₂O: correct formulas [1]; balanced [1]
**(b)** It only **partially dissociates** (ionises) in water [1]
*Examiner insight:* "does not fully dissociate" earns the mark; "has a high pH" or "reacts slowly" does not.

**3. (a)** CH₃COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₃ + H₂O [1]; concentrated sulfuric acid (an acid catalyst) [1]
**(b)** Moles of ethanoic acid = 9.00 ÷ 60.0 = 0.150 mol [1]
Theoretical mass = 0.150 × 74.0 = 11.1 g [1]
Yield = 7.77 ÷ 11.1 × 100 = **70.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* an error in the moles step can still earn the later marks by error carried forward, provided the working is shown.

**4. (a)** (i) Butanoic acid and methanol [1] (ii) sodium butanoate and methanol [1]
**(b)** Acid hydrolysis is reversible and reaches equilibrium [1]; in alkali the carboxylate ion forms, which does not react with methanol to re-form the ester [1]
*Examiner insight:* naming butanoic acid as the product in alkali loses the mark; the product is the salt.

**5. (a)** (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₃C₃H₅ + 3NaOH → 3C₁₇H₃₅COONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃: products [1]; 3NaOH and 3 salt balanced [1]
**(b)** Moles of fat = 44.5 ÷ 890.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]
Moles of salt = 3 × 0.0500 = 0.150 mol [1]
Mass = 0.150 × 306.0 = **45.9 g** [1]
**(c)** 0.150 × 40.0 = **6.00 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* a 1 : 1 ratio is a method error, so the final mark is lost even if the rest of the arithmetic is correct.

**6. (a)** A mixture of **methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids** [1]
**(b)** Moles of oil = 403 ÷ 806.0 = 0.500 mol [1]
Moles of methyl ester = 3 × 0.500 = 1.50 mol [1]
Mass = 1.50 × 270.0 = **405 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the product mass can exceed the oil mass, because methanol is also a reactant.

**7. (a)** Nucleophilic addition–elimination. Arrow from a lone pair on the methanol O to the δ+ carbonyl carbon [1]; arrow from the C=O bond onto the carbonyl O [1]; intermediate with O⁻ and a positive charge on the oxygen from methanol [1]; C=O re-forms, Cl⁻ leaves and H⁺ is lost from the O⁺ [1]
**(b)** **Methyl ethanoate** [1]
*Examiner insight:* each arrow must start from a lone pair or a bond, because the arrow shows where the electron pair comes from; arrows from an atom symbol or a charge are a common way to lose mechanism marks.

**8. (a)** CH₃CH₂COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CONH₂ + NH₄Cl: propanamide [1]; 2NH₃ and NH₄Cl [1]
**(b)** **N-ethylpropanamide** [1]; CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ [1]
**(c)** The second molecule acts as a base and reacts with the HCl formed, giving CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]
*Examiner insight:* when the question says excess ammonia, write the ammonium chloride product; leaving free HCl alongside excess base is chemically inconsistent and risks the balancing mark.

**9. (a)** (CH₃CO)₂O + H₂O → 2CH₃COOH [1]
**(b)** (CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + CH₃COOH [1]
**(c)** (CH₃CO)₂O + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃COO⁻CH₃NH₃⁺ [1]
*Examiner insight:* the anhydride by-product is ethanoic acid (or its salt), never HCl; mixing up the two reagents' by-products is a common slip.

**10. (a)** HOC₆H₄COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → CH₃COOC₆H₄COOH + CH₃COOH [1]
**(b)** Anhydride: 180.0 ÷ (138.0 + 102.0) × 100 = **75.0%** [1]
Acyl chloride: total reactant Mr = 138.0 + 78.5 = 216.5 [1]; 180.0 ÷ 216.5 × 100 = **83.1%** [1]
**(c)** Moles of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid = 2.76 ÷ 138.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]
Theoretical mass of aspirin = 0.0200 × 180.0 = 3.60 g [1]
Yield = 2.52 ÷ 3.60 × 100 = **70.0%** [1]
**(d)** Any two: the by-product is ethanoic acid, not corrosive, toxic hydrogen chloride [1]; the anhydride reacts less vigorously so the reaction is easier to control [1]; it is hydrolysed less readily by water so it is easier to store and handle
*Examiner insight:* "safer" alone is too vague for a mark; name the hazard (HCl gas) or the property (less vigorous, less readily hydrolysed).

**11. (a)** Dissolve the crude solid in the minimum volume of hot solvent [1]; filter while hot to remove insoluble impurities [1]; cool so crystals form while soluble impurities stay in solution [1]; filter under reduced pressure, wash with a little cold solvent and dry [1]
**(b)** P is pure [1]; Q contains impurities, which lower the melting point and widen the range [1]
*Examiner insight:* "minimum volume" and "hot" must both appear; "dissolve in solvent" alone does not explain why crystals form on cooling.

**12. (a)** Sodium ethanoate shows X is an ester of ethanoic acid [1]; Z is oxidised to ethanoic acid, so Z is **ethanol** (a primary alcohol with two carbons) [1]; X is **ethyl ethanoate**, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, which matches C₄H₈O₂ [1]
**(b)** Mr of X = 88.0; 4.40 ÷ 88.0 = **0.0500 mol** (1 : 1 with NaOH) [1]
**(c)** Ethanoyl chloride or ethanoic anhydride [1]
**(d)** Shake the distillate with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution in a separating funnel to remove acid, releasing pressure [1]; dry the organic layer with an anhydrous salt such as anhydrous sodium sulfate [1]; redistil and collect the fraction at the boiling point of X [1]
*Examiner insight:* in identification questions, link each piece of evidence to a conclusion; a correct name given without reasoning cannot earn the reasoning marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming esters with the acid part first.
- Giving the carboxylic acid, not its salt, as the product of alkaline hydrolysis.
- Writing esterification with a single arrow or without an acid catalyst.
- Using 1 : 1 instead of 1 : 3 for a triglyceride in soap or biodiesel calculations.
- Calling the mechanism "nucleophilic substitution" instead of nucleophilic addition–elimination.
- Curly arrows that start at atoms or charges, or a missing charge on the intermediate.
- Forgetting the second NH₃ or amine molecule in equations with excess reagent.
- Arguing that ethanoic anhydride is chosen for aspirin because of atom economy.
- Vague practical answers: no "minimum" volume or no sharp melting point.
- Omitting the step that removes acid (NaHCO₃ wash) when purifying an ester.
