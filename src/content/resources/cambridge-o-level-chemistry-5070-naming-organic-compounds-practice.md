## Section A

**1.** Name each compound.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH₂COOH **[1]**

**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ **[1]**

**2.** State the type of compound shown by each name.

**(a)** propene **[1]**

**(b)** methanoic acid **[1]**

**3.** Which compound is an ester?

B: CH₃COOCH₂CH₃

C: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃

D: CH₃CH=CH₂

**7.** Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol both have the molecular formula C₃H₈O.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of propan-2-ol. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Butane [1]

**(b)** Propanoic acid [1]

**(c)** But-1-ene [1]

**[3]**

**2. (a)** Alkene [1]

**(b)** Carboxylic acid [1]

**[2]**

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

**4.** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Sodium propanoate [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Methyl ethanoate [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Three carbons in a chain with the –O–H on the middle carbon [1]; all atoms and bonds shown, including the O–H bond and seven C–H bonds [1]:

```
      H   H   H
      |   |   |
  H - C - C - C - H
      |   |   |
      H   O   H
          |
          H
```

**(b)** The –OH group is on carbon atom 2 of the chain (the middle carbon) [1].

**(c)** Structural isomers [1].

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* in (a), a common error is to draw the oxygen on the middle carbon but forget its hydrogen, leaving the oxygen with only one bond. Every oxygen in an alcohol needs two bonds: one to carbon and one to hydrogen.

**8. (a)** Butan-2-ol [1]

**(b)** The chain must be numbered from the end nearer the –OH group, giving the lower number, 2 [1].

**[2]**

**9. (a)** 2,3-dibromobutane [1]; CH₃CHBrCHBrCH₃ [1]

**(b)** Butane [1]

**(c)** Butan-2-ol [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* bromine adds one atom to each carbon of the double bond. Here those are carbons 2 and 3, so the product is 2,3-dibromobutane, not 1,2-dibromobutane.

**10. (a)** Butyl propanoate [1]

**(b)** Butan-1-ol [1]; propanoic acid [1]

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* split the formula at the –COO–. Everything up to and including the C of COO is the acid (three carbons: propanoate); everything after the single-bonded O is the alcohol (four carbons: butyl).

**11. (a)** Ethanol [1]; butanoic acid [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ [1]

**(c)** Four-carbon acid part with C=O and C–O shown [1]; –O– linked to a two-carbon ethyl group, all H atoms and bonds shown [1]:

```
      H   H   H   O       H   H
      |   |   |   ‖       |   |
  H - C - C - C - C - O - C - C - H
      |   |   |           |   |
      H   H   H           H   H
```

**[5]**

**12. (a)** Chloromethane [1]

**(b)** 1,2-dibromoethane [1]

**(c)** Ethene undergoes addition: both bromine atoms add across the double bond [1]. Methane undergoes substitution: one chlorine atom replaces one hydrogen atom (the other chlorine forms hydrogen chloride) [1].

**[4]**

**13. (a)** Ester [1]

**(b)** Alkane [1]

**(c)** Carboxylic acid [1]

**(d)** Alcohol [1]

**[4]**

**14. (a)** Magnesium ethanoate [1]

**(b)** (CH₃COO)₂Mg [1]

**(c)** CH₃ group with three C–H bonds joined to the carboxyl carbon [1]; carboxyl carbon with C=O and C–O–H, including the O–H bond [1]:

```
      H   O
      |   ‖
  H - C - C - O - H
      |
      H
```

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* magnesium ions have a 2+ charge and each ethanoate ion has a 1− charge, so two ethanoate ions are needed. The salt name keeps the stem eth- because the ethanoate ion still contains two carbon atoms.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to count the –COOH carbon, so propanoic acid is named "ethanoic acid".
- Numbering from the wrong end of the chain.
- Writing ester names with the acid part first.
- Using "-oic" for salts instead of "-oate".
- Drawing displayed formulae with missing O–H bonds or with five bonds to a carbon.
