## Questions

**1.** Point P is (6, −4). Write the coordinates of the image of P after
**(a)** reflection in the line x = −1 **[1]**
**(b)** reflection in the line y = −x **[1]**
**(c)** rotation through 90° anticlockwise about the origin. **[1]**

**2.** Triangle L has vertices (−5, −1), (−2, −1) and (−2, −3). Find the vertices of the image of L after rotation through 180° about the point (−1, 1). **[3]**

**3.** Triangle S has vertices (2, 3), (6, 3) and (6, 5). Its image T has vertices (2, −1), (6, −1) and (6, −3). Describe fully the single transformation that maps S onto T. **[2]**

**4.** Triangle R has vertices (4, 2), (8, 2) and (8, 6) and area 8 square units.
**(a)** Find the vertices of the image of R after an enlargement with scale factor −1/2 and centre (2, 0). **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the area of the image. **[1]**

**5.** Triangle F has vertices (7, −2), (11, −2) and (11, 2). Its image G has vertices (5, −1), (7, −1) and (7, 1). Describe fully the single transformation that maps F onto G. **[4]**

**6.** A translation maps A(−2, 5) onto A′(4, 1).
**(a)** Write down the column vector of the translation. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the image of B(0, −3). **[1]**
**(c)** Find the point whose image is (1, 1). **[1]**

**7.** Shape M is reflected in the line x = 2. The image is then reflected in the line x = 7.
**(a)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps M onto its final image. **[3]**
**(b)** A vertex of M is at (3, 1). Find its final position. **[1]**

**8.** Triangle K has vertices (1, 2), (1, 5) and (3, 2). K is rotated through 90° clockwise about the origin to give K₁. K₁ is then reflected in the y-axis to give K₂.
**(a)** Find the vertices of K₁. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the vertices of K₂. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps K onto K₂. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** A photograph has area 12 cm². It is enlarged with scale factor 2.5. Find the area of the enlargement. **[2]**
**(b)** An enlargement maps a triangle of area 45 cm² onto a triangle of area 5 cm². Find the possible scale factors. **[2]**
**(c)** An enlargement has scale factor −3 and centre C. Point P is 4 cm from C. Describe where the image of P lies. **[2]**

**10.** Triangle A has vertices (−6, 2), (−3, 2) and (−3, 4).
**(a)** A is rotated through 90° clockwise about (−3, 2) to give B. Find the vertices of B. **[3]**
**(b)** B is enlarged with scale factor −2 and centre (−3, 2) to give C. Find the vertices of C. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the area of C and show it is 4 times the area of A. **[2]**

**11.** Triangle X has vertices (1, 1), (4, 1) and (4, 3). Triangle Y has vertices (0, 4), (0, 7) and (−2, 7). Triangle Z has vertices (4, 0), (4, 3) and (2, 3).
**(a)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps X onto Y. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps Y onto Z. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps X onto Z. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Image x = 2(−1) − 6 = −8, so **(−8, −4)** [1]. (b) (x, y) → (−y, −x) gives **(4, −6)** [1]. (c) (x, y) → (−y, x) gives **(4, 6)** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the rule you are using before you substitute. A slip in one part is then easy to see and does not spread.

**2.** For a half turn about (−1, 1), image = (−2 − x, 2 − y) [1]. (−5, −1) → (3, 3); (−2, −1) → (0, 3); (−2, −3) → **(0, 5)** [2]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A method shown for one vertex lets an examiner see what you intended if a later vertex is wrong. Sketch the triangle to check that the image is the right way round.

**3.** The x values are unchanged and the y values change, so it is a **reflection** [1] **in the line y = 1** [1], halfway between 3 and −1. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the mirror line as an equation. "Horizontal line through the middle" is not a full description.

**4.** (a) Positions relative to (2, 0): (2, 2), (6, 2), (6, 6) [1]. Multiply by −1/2: (−1, −1), (−3, −1), (−3, −3) [1]. Add (2, 0): **(1, −1), (−1, −1), (−1, −3)** [1]. (b) Area × (−1/2)² = 8 × 1/4 = **2 square units** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the relative positions. A negative fraction can easily send an image to the wrong side of the centre, and working lets the method be seen.

**5.** The image is smaller, so it is an **enlargement** [1]. Side FG is 4 and its image is 2, with the shape the same way up, so **scale factor 1/2** [1]. Draw lines through F and its image, and through G and its image [1]. They cross at **centre (3, 0)** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A description of an enlargement needs both scale factor and centre. Lines drawn with a ruler through matching points make the centre easy to read.

**6.** (a) (4 − (−2), 1 − 5) = **(6 over −4)** [1]. (b) (0 + 6, −3 − 4) = **(6, −7)** [1]. (c) Subtract the vector: (1 − 6, 1 + 4) = **(−5, 5)** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) works backwards. Check by adding the vector to your answer to see that it returns (1, 1).

**7.** (a) Two reflections in parallel lines give a **translation** [1]. The distance moved is 2 × (7 − 2) = 10 [1], in the direction perpendicular to the lines: **(10 over 0)** [1]. (b) (3, 1) → (1, 1) → **(13, 1)** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Doing part (b) point by point is a useful check on your answer to part (a).

**8.** (a) (x, y) → (y, −x): **(2, −1), (5, −1), (2, −3)** [2]. (b) Change the sign of each x value: **(−2, −1), (−5, −1), (−2, −3)** [1]. (c) K(1, 2) maps to (−2, −1), which fits (x, y) → (−y, −x) [1]. This is a **reflection in the line y = −x** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* To name a combination, follow one or two points through, then match the result to a rule or a picture.

**9.** (a) Area scale factor 2.5² = 6.25 [1]; 12 × 6.25 = **75 cm²** [1]. (b) Area ratio 5 ÷ 45 = 1/9 [1], so the scale factor is **1/3 or −1/3** [1]. (c) CP′ = 3 × 4 = **12 cm** [1], on the opposite side of C from P, on the straight line through P and C [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Area uses k², so a root gives two answers. Say that the negative value is allowed because the question does not rule it out.

**10.** (a) Positions relative to (−3, 2): (−3, 0), (0, 0), (0, 2) [1]. Clockwise (u, v) → (v, −u): (0, 3), (0, 0), (2, 0) [1]. Add (−3, 2): **(−3, 5), (−3, 2), (−1, 2)** [1]. (b) Multiply B's relative positions (0, 3), (0, 0), (2, 0) by −2: (0, −6), (0, 0), (−4, 0) [1]. Add (−3, 2): **(−3, −4), (−3, 2), (−7, 2)** [2]. (c) Area of A = 1/2 × 3 × 2 = 3, area of C = 1/2 × 6 × 4 = **12** [1]. 12 = 4 × 3, which matches (−2)² [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the centre fixed in your head. It is invariant under both transformations here, which is a quick check on your vertices.

**11.** (a) It is a **rotation** [1]. Side (1, 1) to (4, 1) points right and its image (0, 4) to (0, 7) points up, so **90° anticlockwise** [1]. The perpendicular bisector of (1, 1) to (0, 4) is x = 3y − 7, and that of (4, 1) to (0, 7) is 3y = 2x + 8 [1]. They meet at **centre (−1, 2)** [1]. (b) (0, 4) → (4, 0), so it is a **translation by (4 over −4)** [1]. (c) The sides turn through a quarter turn anticlockwise, so it is a **rotation** [1] of **90° anticlockwise** [1] about the point where the perpendicular bisectors of (4, 1) to (4, 3) and of (4, 3) to (2, 3) meet, y = 2 and x = 3: **centre (3, 2)** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A rotation followed by a translation is again a rotation of the same angle, with a new centre. Always test the centre you find on a second vertex.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the point a rotation or enlargement is about, or the direction of a turn.
- Describing a reflection as "a flip" or giving the mirror line in words only.
- Placing an enlargement from the shape's middle instead of the stated centre.
- Using a negative scale factor and keeping the image on the same side of the centre.
- Using the scale factor for areas without squaring it.
- Combining two transformations in the wrong order.
- Drawing sloping edges freehand, when a ruler must be used.
- Writing a translation as a coordinate pair when a column vector is asked for.
