These questions cover sub-topic 4(f) Carboxylic acids of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.34C to 4.37C. Every point has a C reference, so **all of these questions are Paper 2 only**. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Mg = 24, Ca = 40, and take the molar volume of a gas as 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure. Revise with the [carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-carboxylic-acids/) and the [carboxylic acids revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-carboxylic-acids-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** (Paper 2 only) Name the functional group in carboxylic acids. **[1]**

**2.** (Paper 2 only) Name the acid CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH. **[1]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) Give the structural formula of propanoic acid. **[1]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) Name the acid found in vinegar. **[1]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) State the observation when ethanoic acid is added to sodium carbonate. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** (Paper 2 only) Draw the displayed formula of ethanoic acid. **[2]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Magnesium reacts with aqueous ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two observations. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe a test for the gas. **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) 0.12 g of magnesium (Ar = 24) reacts with excess ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Calculate the moles of magnesium. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed in cm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the minimum mass of ethanoic acid (Mr = 60) needed to react with all the magnesium. **[2]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃, reacts with aqueous ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed from 5.3 g of Na₂CO₃ (Mr = 106) in excess acid. **[3]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) Vinegar is used to remove limescale (calcium carbonate) from a kettle. A kettle contains 2.5 g of calcium carbonate (Mr = 100). Vinegar contains 5.0% ethanoic acid by mass.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of ethanoic acid needed to react with the limescale. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the minimum mass of vinegar needed. **[1]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) A student compares two solutions of the same concentration: ethanoic acid and hydrochloric acid. Magnesium reacts more slowly with the ethanoic acid. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) Describe how ethanoic acid can be made from ethanol in two different ways. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** -COOH (carboxyl group) [1].

**2.** Butanoic acid [1].

**3.** CH₃CH₂COOH [1].

**4.** Ethanoic acid [1].

**5.** Fizzing (effervescence) [1].

**6.** CH₃ group bonded to a carbon, with the carbon having a double bond to one oxygen [1] and a single bond to an oxygen that is bonded to a hydrogen (O-H); all the bonds drawn [1].

**7. (a)** Mg + 2CH₃COOH → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ [1 for the formulae, 1 for the balancing].
**(b)** Any two from: the magnesium dissolves; bubbles (fizzing) are seen; the mixture warms slightly [2].
**(c)** A lit splint held at the mouth of the tube gives a squeaky pop [1].

**8. (a)** 0.12 / 24 = 0.0050 mol [1].
**(b)** Moles of H₂ = 0.0050 mol [1]; volume = 0.0050 x 24,000 = 120 cm³ [1].
**(c)** Moles of ethanoic acid = 2 x 0.0050 = 0.010 mol [1]; mass = 0.010 x 60 = 0.60 g [1].

**9. (a)** Na₂CO₃ + 2CH₃COOH → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ [2] (1 mark for the formulae, 1 mark for the balancing).
**(b)** Moles of Na₂CO₃ = 5.3 / 106 = 0.050 mol [1]. Moles of CO₂ = 0.050 mol (1 : 1) [1]. Volume = 0.050 x 24,000 = 1,200 cm³ [1].

**10. (a)** CaCO₃ + 2CH₃COOH → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂ [2] (1 mark for the formulae, 1 mark for the balancing).
**(b)** Moles of CaCO₃ = 2.5 / 100 = 0.025 mol [1]. Mass of acid = 2 x 0.025 x 60 = 3.0 g [1].
**(c)** 3.0 / 0.050 = 60 g of vinegar [1].

**11.** Ethanoic acid is a weak acid and ionises only partly in water [1], so it has fewer hydrogen ions in the solution at any moment, and fewer collisions with the magnesium [1].

**12.** Microbial oxidation: leave ethanol (for example in wine) open to the air [1]. Bacteria oxidise it with oxygen from the air to ethanoic acid [1]. Chemical oxidation: heat ethanol with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]. The orange solution turns green as ethanoic acid forms [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling CH₃COOH methanoic acid.
- Forgetting the 2 in front of CH₃COOH in equations, and then halving the moles of acid.
- Omitting water from the carbonate equations.
- Leaving out the O-H hydrogen in a displayed formula.
- Writing the salt as "ethanate".
- Answering the weak acid question with "ethanoic acid is less concentrated".
