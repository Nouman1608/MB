## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Disproportionation | A reaction in which the same element is both oxidised and reduced |
| Chlorate(I) ion | ClO⁻, with chlorine in the +1 oxidation state |
| Chloric(I) acid | HClO, formed when chlorine reacts with water |
| Sodium chlorate(I) | NaClO, the active substance in bleach |
| Water treatment | Adding chlorine (or chlorate(I)) to water supplies to kill bacteria |

## The three reactions

| Reaction | Equation | Cl oxidation states | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl₂ + water | Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO (ionic: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ 2H⁺ + Cl⁻ + ClO⁻) | 0 → −1 and +1 | disproportionation, reversible |
| Cl₂ + water in sunlight | 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂ | 0 → −1; O goes −2 → 0 | redox, not disproportionation |
| Cl₂ + cold, dilute NaOH | Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O (ionic: Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O) | 0 → −1 and +1 | disproportionation |

## Observations and facts

- Chlorine water is pale green and acidic.
- Universal indicator added to chlorine water turns **red** (acids present) and then **colourless** (bleached by chloric(I) acid).
- In sunlight, chlorine water gives off bubbles of oxygen and loses its bleaching power.
- With NaOH the reaction goes to completion because OH⁻ removes H⁺.

## Water treatment: the balance

**Advantages**
- Kills bacteria, preventing water-borne diseases such as cholera and typhoid.
- Some chlorine stays in the water, protecting it as it travels through pipes.

**Disadvantages**
- Chlorine gas is toxic; handling and storage are hazardous.
- Chlorine can react with organic matter to form potentially harmful chlorinated organic compounds.
- Taste and smell.

**Conclusion the specification expects:** the health benefits of chlorination outweigh its toxic effects, because the doses are small and the diseases prevented are serious.

**How society decides on chemicals in water:** benefit to health, risk at the dose used, lack of individual choice, and the alternatives available. Fluoridation is an example where opinions differ.

## Uses of the NaOH product

The solution of sodium chlorate(I) is used as **bleach**, and for **disinfecting** (killing bacteria on surfaces). It can also be used in water treatment.

## Checking for disproportionation in three steps

1. Write the oxidation state of chlorine in every chlorine-containing species.
2. If chlorine both rises and falls, the reaction is disproportionation.
3. If chlorine changes in one direction only, find the other element that changes; the reaction is ordinary redox.

Worked check for Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O: Cl₂ is 0, Cl⁻ is −1, ClO⁻ is +1 (because O is −2 and the ion has a 1− charge). Chlorine rises and falls, so it is disproportionation.

## Mole ratios to remember

| Reaction | Useful ratio |
|---|---|
| Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O | 1 Cl₂ : 1 NaClO : 1 NaCl : 2 NaOH |
| 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂ | 2 Cl₂ : 1 O₂ : 4 H⁺ |
| Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO | 1 Cl₂ : 1 HClO (reversible, so this is a maximum) |

Example: 0.0300 mol of NaOH reacts completely with chlorine, so 0.0150 mol of Cl₂ is needed and 0.0150 mol of NaClO forms.

## Exam traps

- Calling the sunlight reaction disproportionation.
- Writing NaClO₃ instead of NaClO.
- Writing ClO⁻ with chlorine as −1.
- Missing "cold" and "dilute" for the NaOH reaction.
- Unbalanced ionic equations: check charge as well as atoms.
- One-sided evaluation of chlorination.
- Writing "purifies water" instead of "kills bacteria".

## Quick self-test

1. State the oxidation state of chlorine in HClO.
2. Write an equation for chlorine reacting with water to form chloride and chlorate(I).
3. Explain why the reaction in Question 2 is a disproportionation.
4. Write an ionic equation for chlorine water reacting in sunlight.
5. Explain why the sunlight reaction is not a disproportionation.
6. Why is chlorine added to drinking water?
7. Give two disadvantages of using chlorine in water treatment.
8. Why do the benefits of chlorination outweigh its risks?
9. Write an equation for chlorine with cold, dilute sodium hydroxide.
10. Give a use of the solution formed in Question 9.
11. What do you see when universal indicator is added to chlorine water?
12. What mass of chlorine is needed to make 250 cm³ of 0.400 mol dm⁻³ NaClO with excess NaOH? (Mr Cl₂ = 71.0)

### Answers

1. **+1**
2. **Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO** (or Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ 2H⁺ + Cl⁻ + ClO⁻)
3. Chlorine is both reduced (0 to −1 in Cl⁻) and oxidised (0 to +1 in ClO⁻) in the same reaction.
4. **2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂**
5. Chlorine is only reduced (0 to −1); the element oxidised is oxygen (−2 to 0).
6. To kill bacteria and prevent water-borne disease.
7. Any two: chlorine is toxic; it can form harmful chlorinated organic compounds with organic matter; unpleasant taste or smell.
8. The doses used are low and the diseases prevented are serious and can spread widely.
9. **Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O**
10. Bleach (or disinfectant).
11. It turns **red**, then becomes **colourless**.
12. n(NaClO) = 0.400 × 0.250 = 0.100 mol = n(Cl₂); mass = 0.100 × 71.0 = **7.10 g**.
