## Questions

**1.** A student mixes four pairs of reagents, W to Z, in a polystyrene cup and records the temperature before and after mixing.

| Pair | Reagents | Start (°C) | After (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | calcium oxide + water | 21.5 | 30.3 |
| X | sodium hydrogencarbonate + citric acid solution | 20.5 | 15.3 |
| Y | sodium chloride solution + potassium nitrate solution | 22.0 | 22.0 |
| Z | magnesium powder + dilute hydrochloric acid | 19.5 | 41.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the temperature change for each pair. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the endothermic reaction. Explain your answer in terms of energy transfer. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what the result for pair Y shows. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** State what is meant by activation energy. **[1]**
**(b)** Burning natural gas is exothermic, but a gas hob needs a spark before the gas will burn. Explain why. **[2]**

**3.** Sketch and label a reaction profile for an endothermic reaction. Label the reactants, the products, the activation energy and the energy change. **[4]**

**4.** A reaction profile shows the reactants at 120 kJ/mol, the top of the curve at 300 kJ/mol and the products at 40 kJ/mol.

**(a)** Calculate the activation energy. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy change, including its sign. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic, and give a reason from the profile. **[1]**

**5.** *(Higher Tier only)* A student writes: "In an exothermic reaction, energy is released when the bonds in the reactants break." Explain what is wrong with this statement, and explain in terms of bonds why a reaction is exothermic. **[3]**

**6.** A student measures the temperature rise when a metal oxide reacts with dilute acid. Four repeats give rises of 7.2 °C, 7.8 °C, 9.9 °C and 7.5 °C.

**(a)** Which repeat does not fit the pattern of the others? **[1]**
**(b)** Leaving that value out, calculate the mean rise. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the uncertainty in the mean, using the range of the results you kept. **[1]**
**(d)** The student used a glass beaker with no lid. Explain how this affects the measured temperature rise, and suggest one improvement. **[2]**

**7.** *(Higher Tier only)* Ethene reacts with hydrogen to make ethane: CH₂=CH₂ + H–H → CH₃–CH₃. Calculate the energy change for this reaction. **[3]**

**8.** *(Higher Tier only)* Hydrazine, H₂N–NH₂, burns in oxygen: N₂H₄ + O₂ → N₂ + 2H₂O. Calculate the energy change for this reaction and state whether it is exothermic or endothermic. **[4]**

**9.** *(Higher Tier only)* Methanol, CH₃OH, burns: 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the energy change for the equation as written. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy change per mole of methanol burned. **[1]**

**10.** *(Higher Tier only)* Methane reacts with chlorine: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. The energy change is −114 kJ/mol. Calculate the bond energy of the C–Cl bond. **[3]**

**11.** *(Higher Tier only)* A company is comparing ethanol and methanol as fuels. Ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH, burns: CH₃CH₂OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the energy change when one mole of ethanol burns. **[4]**
**(b)** Use your answer to (a) and your answer to question 9(b) to calculate the energy released per gram of each fuel. Relative atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16. **[3]**
**(c)** State which fuel releases more energy per gram. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the values from bond energies are only estimates. **[1]**

**12.** A student adds sodium hydrogencarbonate powder to citric acid solution in a polystyrene cup. The temperature falls from 20.4 °C to 15.1 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature change. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy transfer, why the temperature falls. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the reaction profile for this reaction differs from the profile for an exothermic reaction. **[2]**
**(d)** The reaction fizzes and some solution splashes out of the cup. Suggest and explain one change to the method that would make the result more accurate. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** W: 30.3 − 21.5 = **+8.8 °C**; X: 15.3 − 20.5 = **−5.2 °C** [1]. Y: **0.0 °C**; Z: 41.0 − 19.5 = **+21.5 °C** [1]
**(b)** **X** is endothermic [1]. The temperature fell, so energy was transferred from the surroundings to the reacting chemicals [1].
**(c)** No measurable energy transfer, so probably **no reaction** took place [1].
*Examiner insight:* For classification, the direction of the change carries the credit, so a sign on every value makes the reasoning in (b) easy to follow.

**2. (a)** The energy needed for a reaction to occur (the minimum energy particles need when they collide) [1].
**(b)** The spark supplies the **activation energy** [1]. Once the gas is burning, the energy released by the reaction keeps it going, and overall more energy is released than taken in [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say the spark makes the reaction endothermic lose credit; tie the spark to activation energy, not to the overall energy change.

**3.** Axes labelled energy (vertical) and progress of reaction (horizontal) [1]. Products line drawn **higher** than the reactants line, labelled [1]. Activation energy arrow from the **reactants** line up to the peak [1]. Energy change arrow from reactants **up** to products [1].
*Examiner insight:* Arrows must start and end at the right lines; an activation energy arrow from the axis or from the products does not show the correct quantity.

**4. (a)** 300 − 120 = **180 kJ/mol** [1]
**(b)** 40 − 120 [1] = **−80 kJ/mol** [1]
**(c)** **Exothermic**: the products are lower in energy than the reactants [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the negative sign is part of the answer; a bare 80 does not show which way the energy moved.

**5.** Bond breaking **takes in** energy; it does not release it [1]. Energy is released when **new bonds form** in the products [1]. A reaction is exothermic when the energy released making bonds is **greater** than the energy taken in breaking bonds [1].
*Examiner insight:* The comparison in the final point needs both sides named; "making bonds releases energy" alone does not explain why the overall change is exothermic.

**6. (a)** **9.9 °C** [1]
**(b)** (7.2 + 7.8 + 7.5) ÷ 3 [1] = 22.5 ÷ 3 = **7.5 °C** [1]
**(c)** Range = 7.8 − 7.2 = 0.6 °C, so uncertainty = **±0.3 °C** [1]
**(d)** Energy is lost to the surroundings through the glass and the open top, so the measured rise is **smaller** than the true value [1]. Use a polystyrene cup with a lid [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Heat loss" earns more when you say which way it pushes the result (too small) and that it affects every repeat, which makes it a systematic error.

**7.** Bonds that change. Broken: C=C + H–H = 612 + 436 = 1048 kJ [1]. Made: C–C + 2 × C–H = 348 + 826 = 1174 kJ [1]. Energy change = 1048 − 1174 = **−126 kJ/mol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Counting every bond on both sides (2700 − 2826) gives the same answer; mixing the two approaches on one side does not.

**8.** Broken: N–N + 4 × N–H + O=O = 158 + 1564 + 498 = 2220 kJ [1]. Made: N≡N + 4 × O–H = 945 + 1856 = 2801 kJ [1]. Energy change = 2220 − 2801 [1] = **−581 kJ/mol**, **exothermic** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Hydrazine has a single N–N bond, while nitrogen gas has a triple bond; using the wrong one changes the answer by hundreds of kJ, so draw the displayed formulae first.

**9. (a)** Each CH₃OH has 3 C–H, 1 C–O and 1 O–H. Broken: 6 × C–H + 2 × C–O + 2 × O–H + 3 × O=O = 2478 + 716 + 928 + 1494 = 5616 kJ [1] [1]. Made: 4 × C=O + 8 × O–H = 3220 + 3712 = 6932 kJ [1]. Energy change = 5616 − 6932 = **−1316 kJ/mol** [1]
**(b)** −1316 ÷ 2 = **−658 kJ/mol** of methanol [1]
*Examiner insight:* The equation burns two moles of methanol, so a "per mole of fuel" answer must be halved; the working should show this division.

**10.** Broken: C–H + Cl–Cl = 413 + 243 = 656 kJ [1]. −114 = 656 − (C–Cl + 432) [1]. C–Cl = 656 + 114 − 432 = **338 kJ/mol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing the full broken − made equation before rearranging shows the method clearly, even if an arithmetic slip follows.

**11. (a)** Ethanol has 1 C–C, 5 C–H, 1 C–O and 1 O–H. Broken: 348 + 2065 + 358 + 464 + (3 × 498) = 4729 kJ [1] [1]. Made: 4 × C=O + 6 × O–H = 3220 + 2784 = 6004 kJ [1]. Energy change = 4729 − 6004 = **−1275 kJ/mol** [1]
**(b)** Mr: ethanol = 46, methanol = 32 [1]. Ethanol: 1275 ÷ 46 = **27.7 kJ/g** [1]. Methanol: 658 ÷ 32 = **20.6 kJ/g** [1]
**(c)** **Ethanol** [1]
**(d)** Bond energies are **average values** taken from many different compounds [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), state the relative formula masses before dividing; a correct method with a slip in Mr is easier to credit when every step is visible.

**12. (a)** 15.1 − 20.4 = **−5.3 °C** [1]
**(b)** The reaction is **endothermic** [1]: energy is transferred from the solution (the surroundings) to the reacting chemicals, so the solution cools [1].
**(c)** The products are **higher** in energy than the reactants [1], so the energy change arrow points **upwards** [1].
**(d)** Use a larger cup or a lid with a small hole for the thermometer [1], so no solution (and the energy it carries) is lost, and the reading reflects all of the reacting mixture [1].
*Examiner insight:* Improvement questions earn the second mark only when the change is linked to why it helps; naming a piece of equipment is not enough on its own.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying a reaction by how big the temperature change is, rather than whether it rose or fell.
- Writing that the reaction "made cold" instead of describing energy moving from the surroundings to the chemicals.
- Drawing activation energy from the products or the axis instead of from the reactants.
- Missing the sign on an energy change, or giving no unit.
- Reversing the bond energy rule: breaking bonds takes energy in, making bonds gives energy out.
- Forgetting the equation's numbers, such as 3O₂ (three O=O bonds) or 2CH₃OH.
- Confusing N–N in hydrazine with N≡N in nitrogen gas.
- Giving an answer for the whole equation when the question asks per mole of fuel.
- Including an anomalous result in a mean.
