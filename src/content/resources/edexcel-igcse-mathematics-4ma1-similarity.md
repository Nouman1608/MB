## What does similar mean? (A)

Two figures are **similar** if one is an enlargement of the other. Then

- all **corresponding angles** are equal, and
- all **corresponding lengths** are in the same ratio.

That common ratio is the **scale factor**. A scale factor larger than 1 makes a larger figure, and a scale factor between 0 and 1 makes a smaller one.

Similar is different from **congruent**. Congruent figures are identical in size and shape, so the scale factor is 1. Similar figures have the same shape but not necessarily the same size.

For triangles, equal angles are enough. If two angles of one triangle equal two angles of another, the third angles match too, and the triangles are similar. For other shapes you need both equal angles and sides in proportion. A square and a rectangle have all angles equal to 90°, yet they are not similar, because their sides are not in proportion.

## Finding a scale factor and a missing length

**scale factor = length in the new figure ÷ corresponding length in the old figure**

Always pair up lengths that match. Mark the equal angles first. The sides opposite equal angles are corresponding sides.

**Worked example 1.** Triangles ABC and PQR are similar. AB = 6 cm, BC = 8 cm, AC = 10 cm and PQ = 9 cm, where PQ corresponds to AB. Find QR and PR.

Scale factor = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5.

QR = 8 × 1.5 = **12 cm**.

PR = 10 × 1.5 = **15 cm**.

**Worked example 2.** Two similar quadrilaterals have corresponding sides of 4.5 cm and 3 cm. A side of 12 cm on the smaller quadrilateral corresponds to which length on the larger one? The scale factor from small to large is 4.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5. So 12 × 1.5 = **18 cm**.

You can also compare sides within one figure. In two similar triangles, the ratio of two sides in one triangle equals the ratio of the matching sides in the other. This is the same fact written differently, and it is helpful when no direct pair of sides is known.

**Worked example 3.** Are rectangles measuring 6 cm by 4 cm and 10 cm by 7 cm similar? The ratios of lengths are 10 ÷ 6 = 1.67 (to 3 s.f.) and 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75. They differ, so the rectangles are **not similar**. Rectangles of 6 by 4 and 9 by 6 are similar, because 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 and 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5.

## Similar triangles in diagrams

Similar triangles often appear inside other shapes. Look for these:

- **Parallel lines.** If DE is parallel to BC in triangle ABC (D on AB, E on AC), then triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC. Angle A is common, and the angles at D and B are equal (corresponding angles).
- **Crossing lines.** Two lines crossing between parallel lines make triangles with equal vertically opposite angles and equal alternate angles.
- **Shadows.** The Sun's rays are parallel, so an object and its shadow form a triangle similar to the one made by a taller object and its shadow.

**Worked example 4.** In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC, with DE parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 2 cm and DE = 5 cm. Find BC.

Triangles ADE and ABC are similar. AB = 4 + 2 = 6 cm. The scale factor from ADE to ABC is 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. So BC = 5 × 1.5 = **7.5 cm**.

A common mistake is to use AD ÷ DB = DE ÷ BC. The correct pairing is AD ÷ AB = DE ÷ BC, with the **whole** side of the larger triangle.

**Worked example 5 (shadows).** A vertical pole 1.8 m tall casts a shadow 2.4 m long. At the same time a tree casts a shadow 10 m long. How tall is the tree?

The triangles are similar because the sun's angle is the same. Scale factor = 10 ÷ 2.4. Height of tree = 1.8 × 10 ÷ 2.4 = **7.5 m**.

## Maps and scale drawings (B)

A **scale** tells you how a length on a map or drawing relates to the real length.

- A ratio scale such as **1 : 50 000** means 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm in real life.
- A statement such as "1 cm represents 5 m" means the same kind of thing in words.

To use a ratio scale, change the units sensibly. 50 000 cm = 500 m = 0.5 km. So a scale of 1 : 50 000 means 1 cm represents 0.5 km.

**Worked example 6.** A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000.

(a) Two towns are 3.4 cm apart on the map. Real distance = 3.4 × 50 000 = 170 000 cm = 1700 m = **1.7 km**.

(b) The real distance between two villages is 4.5 km, which is 450 000 cm. Map distance = 450 000 ÷ 50 000 = **9 cm**.

**Worked example 7.** A room measures 4.8 m by 3.6 m. A plan of the room uses a scale of 1 cm to 60 cm. Convert the room to centimetres: 480 cm by 360 cm. Divide by 60: **8 cm by 6 cm**.

Check that the unit of each answer matches the question. Map answers are usually in centimetres on paper and in metres or kilometres for real distances.

## Area and volume of similar figures (Higher tier only)

If the scale factor for lengths is k:

| Quantity | Multiplied by |
|---|---|
| Lengths (sides, perimeters, heights) | k |
| Areas (surface area, area of a face) | k² |
| Volumes | k³ |

In ratio form: lengths 1 : k, areas 1 : k², volumes 1 : k³. Perimeters scale like lengths, not like areas.

**Worked example 8.** Two similar shapes have corresponding lengths in the ratio 2 : 5. The smaller has an area of 12 cm². Find the area of the larger.

Scale factor for lengths = 5/2 = 2.5. Scale factor for area = 2.5² = 6.25. Area = 12 × 6.25 = **75 cm²**.

**Worked example 9.** Two similar jugs have heights of 12 cm and 18 cm. The smaller holds 240 ml. How much does the larger hold?

Scale factor for lengths = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5. For volume: 1.5³ = 3.375. Volume = 240 × 3.375 = **810 ml**.

**Going the other way.** If you are given the ratio of areas or volumes, take a root to find the scale factor for lengths.

- Areas in the ratio 9 : 25 give lengths in the ratio √9 : √25 = 3 : 5. Volumes are then in the ratio 27 : 125.
- Volumes in the ratio 8 : 27 give lengths in the ratio 2 : 3. Areas are then in the ratio 4 : 9.

**Worked example 10.** Two similar tins have label areas of 50 cm² and 128 cm². The smaller tin holds 250 ml. Find the volume of the larger tin.

Area scale factor = 128 ÷ 50 = 2.56. Length scale factor = √2.56 = 1.6. Volume scale factor = 1.6³ = 4.096. Volume = 250 × 4.096 = **1024 ml**.

**Worked example 11 (models).** A model ship is built at a scale of 1 : 50. Its surface area is 0.18 m². The real ship's surface area is 0.18 × 50² = 0.18 × 2500 = **450 m²**. A 1 : 20 model of a tank holds 5 litres. The real tank holds 5 × 20³ = 5 × 8000 = 40 000 litres, which is **40 m³**.

**Map areas.** On a 1 : 50 000 map, 1 cm represents 0.5 km, so 1 cm² represents 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 km². A lake with an area of 8 cm² on the map has a real area of 8 × 0.25 = **2 km²**.

**Scaling only some dimensions.** Doubling every length of a cylinder (radius and height) makes a similar cylinder with 8 times the volume. Doubling only the radius is not an enlargement, so the volume rule does not apply. In that case V = πr²h gives 4 times the volume.

## Exam technique

- **Label the corresponding sides** before you write a ratio. Writing the triangles' vertices in matching order, such as ABC and PQR, helps.
- **Show the scale factor** as a number, then use it. This earns method marks.
- **Choose the right power.** Length k, area k², volume k³. Write which one you are using.
- **Check the size.** If the second figure is larger, the answer should be larger.
- **For maps**, write the units each time you convert.

## Common mistakes

- Pairing up sides that do not correspond.
- Using AD ÷ DB instead of AD ÷ AB in the parallel line triangle.
- Using the scale factor for lengths on areas or volumes.
- Squaring when you should take a square root, or the other way round.
- Forgetting that perimeters follow the length ratio.
- Not converting cm to m or km on map questions.
