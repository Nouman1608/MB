Condensed notes on section **3.6.1 Setting human resource objectives** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [HR objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-human-resource-objectives/); exam-style questions are in the [HR objectives practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-human-resource-objectives-practice/).

## Specification content

- The value of setting HR objectives: employee engagement; talent development; training; diversity; alignment of employee and employer values; number, skills and location of employees.
- Methods of achieving HR objectives, including soft and hard HRM approaches.
- Throughout section 3.6: technology (changing nature of work, skills, remote working); ethical and environmental influences; market conditions including the labour market; competitiveness; links with other functions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HRM | Planning, recruiting, training, rewarding and organising a business's people |
| HR objective | Specific target for the workforce, set to support corporate aims |
| Engagement | Commitment to the business and discretionary effort |
| Talent development | Developing staff with potential for bigger roles |
| Training | Giving skills and knowledge for present or new tasks |
| Diversity | A workforce of varied ages, genders, ethnicities, abilities and backgrounds |
| Workforce planning | Having the right number of people, with the right skills, in the right place and time |
| Soft HRM | Treats employees as valuable assets to be developed |
| Hard HRM | Treats employees as a cost and resource to be controlled |

## Why set HR objectives

Link people to strategy; give measurable targets; coordinate with other functions; guide recruitment, training, pay and structure; support motivation; cut the cost of turnover and absence. **Limits:** harder to measure than money; budget pressure; targets without consultation cause resentment; labour market is outside the firm's control.

## The six objectives

| Objective | Aim | Typical measure | Benefit | Cost or difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engagement | Commitment and extra effort | Survey score, absence, turnover | Productivity, service, retention | Takes time and management effort |
| Talent development | Future managers from within | Promotions from within | Retention, pipeline | Staff may still leave |
| Training | Skills for present and new tasks | Hours of training, productivity | Quality, safety, coping with technology | Fees, time away, poaching |
| Diversity | Varied workforce, fair treatment | Mix in recruitment and promotion, pay gaps | Wider talent, understanding customers, reputation, legal compliance | Bias, changing practices |
| Value alignment | Staff beliefs match the mission | Culture surveys, retention | Commitment, consistent service | Needs careful recruitment and example |
| Number, skills, location | Right people, right place | Vacancies, skills gaps | Meets demand | Labour market, technology change |

## Workforce planning example

120,000 calls a month; one agent handles 2,000: agents needed = **60**. With 52 agents the gap is **8**. Close it by recruiting 8, or by raising productivity to 2,308 calls per agent (52 × 2,308 = 120,016). Consider skills and location (remote working widens the labour pool).

## Soft against hard HRM

| | Soft | Hard |
|---|---|---|
| View | Valuable assets | Resource and cost |
| Style | Involvement, development, trust | Control, targets, supervision |
| Practices | Training, consultation, teamwork, job enrichment | Tight targets, PRP, flexible contracts, monitoring |
| Strengths | Engagement, loyalty, innovation | Accountability, low cost, quick response |
| Weaknesses | Costly, slow | Low morale, high turnover |
| Suits | Skilled, creative, service roles; tight labour markets | Routine work; seasonal jobs |

Most businesses use a mix. Choice depends on the type of work, labour market, culture and objectives.

## Training worked example

40 employees make 300 units a month. Training costs £600 each = **£24,000**; raises productivity 5% to 315 units; extra output 15 × 40 = **600 units** a month; contribution £12 each = £7,200 a month = **£86,400** a year, 3.6 times the cost. If the gain were only 1%, extra contribution would be £17,280, below the cost. Staff who leave take the skills with them.

## Influences on HR objectives

Corporate strategy; labour market (skills shortages, unemployment); technology (automation, remote working); legal and ethical influences (equality, health and safety, fair treatment); competition for staff; culture; finance.

## Exam traps

- Soft and hard HRM are different views of the workforce, not "kind" and "cruel".
- An objective needs a measure and a time limit.
- Count the costs of training and engagement as well as the benefits.
- Training gains depend on staff staying.
- Link the approach to the type of work in the case.

## Self-test

1. Define "employee engagement".
2. Give two benefits of training.
3. Give one cost of training.
4. Give two benefits of a diverse workforce.
5. What is workforce planning?
6. 90,000 orders must be packed each month and one packer handles 1,500 orders. How many packers are needed?
7. There are 55 packers. How many more are required?
8. Give two features of soft HRM.
9. Give two features of hard HRM.
10. Which approach suits seasonal, routine work? Why?
11. Give one reason why HR objectives might need to change when a business introduces automation.
12. Give one way a labour shortage affects HR objectives.

### Answers

1. Employees' commitment to the business and willingness to put in extra effort.
2. Any two: higher productivity; better quality; better safety; coping with new technology; retention.
3. Any one: course fees; time away from work; risk of staff leaving after training.
4. Any two: wider talent pool; understanding of diverse customers; new ideas; reputation; legal compliance.
5. Ensuring the business has the right number of people with the right skills in the right place at the right time.
6. 90,000 ÷ 1,500 = **60 packers**.
7. 60 − 55 = **5 more**.
8. Any two: involvement; training and development; consultation; empowerment; teamwork.
9. Any two: tight targets; close supervision; performance-related pay; flexible contracts; monitoring.
10. Hard HRM, because jobs are routine and short-term, so the cost of development is hard to recover and flexibility is valuable.
11. Fewer workers may be needed, and those who remain need different, higher skills, so training and recruitment targets change.
12. It raises the importance of retention, training and attractive pay and conditions to keep and attract staff.
