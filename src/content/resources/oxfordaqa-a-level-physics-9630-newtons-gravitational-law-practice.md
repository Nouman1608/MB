These questions cover section **3.7.1 Newton's gravitational law** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: gravity as a universal attractive force and F = Gm₁m₂/r² for point masses. Section 3.7 is International A-level only. Marks are shown for each part. The asteroids, probe data and planet in Questions 7, 10 and 11 are invented for practice.

Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², g at the Earth's surface = 9.81 N kg⁻¹, Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m, Moon mass 7.35 × 10²² kg.

Revise first with the [Newton's gravitational law study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-newtons-gravitational-law/) and the [Newton's gravitational law revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-newtons-gravitational-law-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State Newton's law of gravitation. **[2]**

**2.** Which is a correct unit for the gravitational constant G?

A: N kg⁻²

B: N m kg⁻¹

C: N m² kg⁻²

D: N kg⁻¹ **[1]**

**3.** A lead sphere of mass 150 kg and a small sphere of mass 0.75 kg have their centres 0.20 m apart. Calculate the gravitational force between them. **[2]**

**4.** Both masses in a pair are doubled and their separation is tripled. State the factor by which the gravitational force changes. **[1]**

**5.** The Earth exerts a gravitational force F on the Moon. The force the Moon exerts on the Earth is

A: much smaller than F

B: equal to F, in the opposite direction

C: equal to F, in the same direction

D: much larger than F **[1]**

**6.** Explain why, when using Newton's law for a planet and a satellite, the separation is measured to the centre of the planet. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Two asteroids of mass 3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg and 1.2 × 10¹⁵ kg have centres 50 km apart. Treat them as point masses.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational force between them. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the smaller asteroid due to this force. **[2]**

**(c)** State the acceleration of the larger asteroid. **[1]**

**8.** A satellite of mass 800 kg is 2.0 × 10⁶ m above the Earth's surface.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational force on the satellite. **[3]**

**(b)** Express this as a fraction of the satellite's weight at the Earth's surface. **[1]**

**9.** A probe of mass 2000 kg lies on the line between the centres of the Earth and the Moon. It is 3.46 × 10⁸ m from the Earth's centre and 0.38 × 10⁸ m from the Moon's centre.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational force on the probe due to the Earth. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the gravitational force on the probe due to the Moon. **[1]**

**(c)** Determine the size and direction of the resultant gravitational force. **[2]**

**10.** A probe of mass 100 kg approaches a planet. The gravitational force on it is measured at three distances from the planet's centre.

| r / 10⁷ m | 1.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| F / N | 40.0 | 10.0 | 2.5 |

**(a)** Show that the data are consistent with an inverse square law. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of the planet. **[2]**

**(c)** State the shape and gradient of a graph of log F against log r for these data. **[1]**

**11.** A planet has 0.11 times the mass of the Earth and 0.53 times its radius.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of the gravitational force on an object at the planet's surface to the force on it at the Earth's surface. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the weight of a 60 kg astronaut on the planet's surface. **[1]**

**12.** The Earth and a falling apple exert equal and opposite gravitational forces on each other. Explain why the apple accelerates noticeably towards the Earth but the Earth's acceleration towards the apple is not noticed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The gravitational force between two point masses is proportional to the product of their masses [1] and inversely proportional to the square of their separation [1].

**2.** **C** [1]

**3.** F = Gm₁m₂/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 150 × 0.75/0.20² [1] = **1.9 × 10⁻⁷ N** [1]

**4.** (2 × 2)/3² = **4/9** [1]

**5.** **B** [1]

**6.** A uniform spherical planet acts on masses outside it as if all its mass were concentrated at its centre [1], so the planet behaves as a point mass at its centre and r must be measured from there, not from the surface [1].

**7.**

**(a)** F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 3.0 × 10¹⁵ × 1.2 × 10¹⁵/(5.0 × 10⁴)² [1] = **9.6 × 10¹⁰ N** [1]

**(b)** a = F/m [1] = 9.6 × 10¹⁰/1.2 × 10¹⁵ = **8.0 × 10⁻⁵ m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** 9.6 × 10¹⁰/3.0 × 10¹⁵ = **3.2 × 10⁻⁵ m s⁻²** [1]

**8.**

**(a)** r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 2.0 × 10⁶ = 8.37 × 10⁶ m [1]; F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 800/(8.37 × 10⁶)² [1] = **4.5 × 10³ N** [1]

**(b)** Surface weight = 800 × 9.81 = 7850 N, so the fraction is 4550/7850 = **0.58** [1]

**9.**

**(a)** F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 2000/(3.46 × 10⁸)² [1] = **6.65 N** towards the Earth [1]

**(b)** F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 7.35 × 10²² × 2000/(0.38 × 10⁸)² = **6.79 N** towards the Moon [1]

**(c)** The forces are in opposite directions, so the resultant is 6.79 − 6.65 = **0.14 N** [1], **towards the Moon** [1]

**10.**

**(a)** F × r² = 40.0 × (1.0 × 10⁷)² = 4.0 × 10¹⁵ N m² for the first row [1]; the same product, 4.0 × 10¹⁵ N m², is obtained for the other rows (or: each doubling of r divides F by 4), so F ∝ 1/r² [1].

**(b)** F r² = GMm, so M = 4.0 × 10¹⁵/(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 100) [1] = **6.0 × 10²³ kg** [1]

**(c)** A straight line with gradient **−2** [1]

**11.**

**(a)** Ratio = 0.11/0.53² [1] = **0.39** [1]

**(b)** W = 0.39 × 60 × 9.81 = **230 N** [1]

**12.** By Newton's third law the forces on the apple and on the Earth are equal in size [1]. Acceleration = force/mass [1], and the Earth's mass is enormously larger than the apple's, so its acceleration is far too small to notice [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the altitude instead of the distance from the Earth's centre (Question 8).
- Forgetting to square the power of ten in r (Questions 3 and 7).
- Adding forces that act in opposite directions, or omitting the direction of the resultant (Question 9).
- Showing an inverse square law with one pair of values only (Question 10).
- Squaring the mass ratio instead of the radius ratio (Question 11).
- Claiming the Earth pulls harder on the apple than the apple pulls on the Earth (Questions 5 and 12).
