## Imperialism and resistance (late nineteenth century)

**Causes of the partition of Africa**

- Economic: raw materials, markets, investment opportunities.
- Strategic: Britain's occupation of Egypt (1882) to protect the Suez Canal route.
- Political: nationalism, prestige, rivalry between European powers.
- Technological: quinine, steamships, railways, the machine gun.
- Diplomatic: the Berlin Conference (1884-85) and "effective occupation".

**Resistance timeline**

- 1879: Anglo-Zulu War; British defeat at Isandlwana, then Zulu defeat.
- 1885: Mahdist forces take Khartoum.
- 1 March 1896: Ethiopia defeats Italy at Adwa.
- 1898: Mahdist state defeated at Omdurman; Samori Touré captured by the French.
- 1900: Asante rising led by Yaa Asantewaa.
- 1904-08: Herero and Nama rising and its destruction in German South West Africa.
- 1905-07: Maji Maji rising in German East Africa.

## South Africa, 1899-1994

- 1899-1902: South African War.
- 1910: Union of South Africa.
- 1912: South African Native National Congress founded (renamed ANC in 1923).
- 1913: Natives Land Act.
- 1948: National Party wins; apartheid begins under D. F. Malan.
- 1950: Population Registration Act; Group Areas Act; Suppression of Communism Act.
- 1953: Bantu Education Act.
- 1955: Freedom Charter adopted at Kliptown.
- 1959: Pan Africanist Congress (PAC) formed.
- 21 March 1960: Sharpeville; ANC and PAC banned.
- 1961: Umkhonto we Sizwe begins sabotage; South Africa becomes a republic and leaves the Commonwealth.
- 1964: Rivonia Trial ends with life sentences for Mandela and others.
- 16 June 1976: Soweto uprising against Afrikaans-medium teaching.
- 1977: Steve Biko dies in police custody.
- 1983: tricameral parliament; United Democratic Front founded.
- 1985-86: states of emergency; growing international sanctions.
- 2 February 1990: de Klerk unbans the ANC; 11 February: Mandela released.
- 27 April 1994: first universal elections.

## Iran, 1951-1979

- 1951: Mosaddegh nationalizes oil.
- 1953: Mosaddegh overthrown in a coup backed by Britain and the USA.
- 1963: White Revolution; protests; Khomeini exiled (1964).
- 1975: Rastakhiz single-party system.
- 1978: mass protests, strikes; martial law.
- January 1979: the Shah leaves; 1 February: Khomeini returns.
- 1979: Islamic Republic established; US embassy hostage crisis begins (November).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Effective occupation | Berlin Conference principle that claims required actual control |
| Indirect rule | Ruling through existing local rulers, as in British Northern Nigeria |
| Apartheid | System of legal racial segregation in South Africa from 1948 |
| Bantustans | Ethnic "homelands" created under apartheid |
| Pass laws | Laws restricting the movement of Black South Africans |
| Pan-Arabism | Movement for Arab unity, associated with Nasser |
| Mandate | League of Nations territory administered by a European power |
| White Revolution | The Shah's reform programme from 1963 |

## Essay angles to prepare

| Content area | Question angle | Factors to weigh |
|---|---|---|
| Imperialism | Why was partition so rapid after 1880? | Rivalry, technology, economic motives, the Berlin Conference, African divisions |
| Resistance | Why did Ethiopia survive when most states fell? | Menelik's army and diplomacy, Italian errors, terrain, unity |
| South Africa | Why did apartheid last until 1990? | Repression, economic growth, Western Cold War support, opposition divisions |
| Egypt | How successful was Nasser? | Domestic reform against foreign policy, Suez against 1967 |
| Iran | Why did the Shah fall in 1979? | Modernization, repression, economic crisis, Khomeini, a broad coalition |

## Exam traps

- **Answering on "Africa" in general** when the question names a country.
- **Forgetting African agency** in questions on imperialism.
- **Confusing the dates of Middle Eastern wars.** 1948, 1956, 1967, 1973.
- **Apartheid's end explained by one factor.** Weigh internal resistance, economic pressure, the end of the Cold War and leadership.
- **Unsupported casualty figures.** Use only figures you are sure of.

## Self-test

1. Why did Britain occupy Egypt in 1882? *To secure the Suez Canal route to India and protect financial interests.*
2. What did the Berlin Conference decide about claims? *They needed "effective occupation".*
3. Which two African states were independent in 1914? *Ethiopia and Liberia.*
4. Where were the Mahdist forces defeated in 1898? *Omdurman.*
5. What did the Natives Land Act of 1913 do? *Restricted African land ownership to designated reserves, a small fraction of the land.*
6. Name three apartheid laws of 1950-53. *Population Registration Act, Group Areas Act, Bantu Education Act (also the Suppression of Communism Act).*
7. What was the Freedom Charter? *The Congress Alliance's 1955 statement of a non-racial democratic South Africa.*
8. What triggered the Soweto uprising? *The requirement to use Afrikaans as a medium of teaching.*
9. What was the Suez crisis? *The 1956 Israeli, British and French attack on Egypt after Nasser nationalized the canal company, ended by US and Soviet pressure.*
10. Which territories did Israel take in 1967? *Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan Heights.*
11. What happened to Mosaddegh in 1953? *He was overthrown in a coup backed by Britain and the USA.*
12. When did Khomeini return to Iran? *1 February 1979.*
