This guide covers subtopic **2.4 Internal and external communication** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). The combined [Recruitment, Selection, Training and Communication study guide](/resources/o-level-business-recruitment-selection-and-training-of-employees-and-internal-and-external/) introduces the methods and barriers. This page goes further into the process of communication, the factors that decide which method suits a message, worked recommendations for different situations, and how to trace a barrier back to its cause so that you can suggest a fix that actually works.

## What the syllabus asks for

- **2.4.1** Why effective communication is important and the methods used to achieve it:
  - effective communication and its importance to business
  - benefits and limitations of different communication methods, including those based on information technology (IT)
  - recommend and justify which communication method to use in given circumstances
- **2.4.2** Demonstrate an awareness of communication barriers:
  - how communication barriers arise and problems of ineffective communication; how communication barriers can be reduced or removed.

## The communication process

Communication is the transfer of a message from a **sender** to a **receiver**. It has five parts:

1. **Sender**: the person or department passing on the information.
2. **Message**: the information, instruction or idea.
3. **Medium**: the method used, such as a meeting, an email or a notice.
4. **Receiver**: the person or group the message is meant for.
5. **Feedback**: the receiver's response, which shows the message was understood.

Communication is **effective** when the receiver gets the message, understands it as intended and acts on it. **One-way** communication has no feedback, as with a notice on a board. **Two-way** communication includes feedback, as in a meeting where questions are asked. Two-way communication is slower but more likely to be understood.

**Internal communication** is between people within the business, such as a manager instructing a team. **External communication** is between the business and people outside it, such as customers, suppliers, banks or the government.

## Why effective communication matters

| Area | Effect of good communication | Effect of poor communication |
|---|---|---|
| Instructions | Tasks done correctly first time | Mistakes, waste and rework |
| Coordination | Departments plan together, for example production knows the sales forecast | Too much or too little stock; missed deadlines |
| Motivation | Employees feel informed and involved | Rumours, distrust, lower morale |
| Decisions | Managers receive accurate, timely information | Decisions based on old or wrong information |
| Customers | Orders, complaints and changes handled smoothly | Lost customers and a damaged reputation |
| Safety | Rules understood and followed | Accidents and legal claims |

## Methods and their benefits and limitations

| Method | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Face-to-face conversation | Immediate feedback; body language helps understanding | No permanent record; hard with many people |
| Meeting | Several people discuss and agree; questions answered | Time-consuming; some people may not speak up |
| Telephone | Quick; two-way over distance | No record unless noted; no body language |
| Letter | Formal; permanent record; can include enclosures | Slow; costly to post; feedback delayed |
| Notice board | Cheap; reaches many people | May not be read; no feedback |
| Report | Detailed information for decisions | Takes time to write and read |
| Email (IT) | Fast; cheap; same message to many; record kept; attachments | Information overload; can be ignored; tone may be misread |
| Text or instant messaging (IT) | Very fast; suits short, urgent messages | Too brief for complex information |
| Video conferencing (IT) | Face-to-face over distance; saves travel time and cost | Needs reliable equipment and internet; technical failures |
| Intranet (IT) | Information stored for all staff to find when needed | Staff must choose to look; set-up and upkeep costs |
| Website and social media (IT) | Reaches many customers at low cost; two-way with comments | Negative comments are public; must be kept up to date |

## Choosing a method: the factors

Ask these questions about the message:

- **Urgency**: does it need to arrive now?
- **Record**: is written proof needed, for legal or reference purposes?
- **Complexity**: does the receiver need detail or the chance to ask questions?
- **Number and location of receivers**: one person, a team, or every customer? On site or far away?
- **Confidentiality**: should others see it?
- **Cost**: how much time and money is justified?
- **Sensitivity**: is the news likely to upset people?

## Worked recommendations

**Situation 1 (internal).** A factory manager must tell 120 workers on three shifts that a safety rule has changed from tomorrow.

> "Use a short briefing by each shift supervisor, backed by a notice and an email. The briefing gives two-way communication, so workers can ask questions about a safety change that must be understood; the written notice provides a record and reaches anyone absent. A notice alone would be cheaper, but it gives no feedback and may not be read."

**Situation 2 (external).** A business must tell a supplier that it is cancelling a large order.

> "Phone the supplier first, so the news is delivered personally and questions can be answered at once, then confirm in a letter or email so there is a written record in case of a dispute about the cancellation."

**Situation 3 (internal and sensitive).** A manager needs to tell an employee that her post will be made redundant.

> "A private face-to-face meeting. The news is sensitive and confidential, so it should not be sent by email or notice; a meeting allows the manager to explain the reasons and the employee to respond. A letter should follow to confirm the details."

Each recommendation names the method, links it to two factors in the situation and rejects or adds to an alternative.

## Communication barriers

A barrier is anything that stops a message being received, understood or acted on. Tracing the barrier to one part of the process makes the solution clear.

| Where it arises | Barrier | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | Jargon or technical language the receiver does not know | Use simple language suited to the receiver |
| Sender | Message unclear, too long or badly organised | Plan the message; keep it short; check key points |
| Medium | Wrong method, such as a long email for a complex change | Choose the method using the factors above |
| Medium | Long chain of command, so the message changes as it passes down | Shorten the chain or send directly to those affected |
| Medium | Technical failure or noise | Back-up methods; quiet places for discussion |
| Receiver | Not listening, distrust of the sender, or too many messages | Build trust; limit messages to what matters |
| Receiver | Different first language or culture | Translation; visual aids; check understanding |
| Feedback | No feedback, so misunderstandings are not noticed | Use two-way methods; ask the receiver to confirm |

**Worked example (invented).** At Linden Logistics, drivers often go to the wrong address. Instructions pass from the customer to sales, then to the office manager, then to a dispatcher who texts the driver an abbreviated address. The barriers are a **long chain** (four links where details can be lost) and a **medium too brief** for the detail needed. Solutions: let customers enter delivery details directly into a system the driver can see on a phone, and ask drivers to confirm the address before setting off. The problems caused (wasted fuel, late deliveries, unhappy customers) should fall.

## Common mistakes

- Defining communication without mentioning that the message must be understood.
- Listing methods without benefits and limitations linked to the situation.
- Recommending email for everything because it is "fast and cheap".
- Naming a barrier without saying how it arises or how to reduce it.
- Forgetting that external communication includes customers, suppliers and the government.

## Exam technique for 7115 command words

- **Identify**: name a method or a barrier.
- **Explain**: give a benefit or limitation and link it to the receiver or message in the case.
- **Recommend and justify**: choose a method, use at least two factors such as urgency and need for a record, and say why an alternative is weaker.
