## Core recall

### What the specification lists

- Consequences of quality issues: customer expectations for goods and services; how businesses identify and measure quality problems; the consequences.
- Methods of maintaining consistent quality: total quality management (TQM), and its advantages.
- Costs and benefits of maintaining quality: additional sales, image/reputation, higher price, inspection costs, staff training, product recalls, the provision of services.
- Quality issues as businesses grow, particularly with outsourcing and franchising.

### Quality defined

Meeting customers' expectations for the price paid, consistently. Not the same as luxury.

### Expectations

| Goods | Services |
|---|---|
| Work properly and as claimed | Reliable: happens when promised |
| Last a reasonable time | Quick, with little waiting |
| Good finish and appearance | Friendly, knowledgeable staff |
| Safe | Accurate orders and bills |
| Consistent, as described | Pleasant, clean surroundings |

### Finding problems

Inspection and testing; complaints and returns; surveys and reviews; mystery shoppers; staff reports.

### Measures

- Defect rate = faulty items ÷ total produced × 100
- Complaint rate = complaints ÷ customers × 100
- Return rate = items returned ÷ items sold × 100
- Review scores and on-time rates

Compare each with targets, past figures or rivals.

### Consequences of poor quality

Returns and refunds; waste; lost sales; damaged reputation; product recalls; low staff morale.

### TQM

- Every employee in every department responsible for quality.
- Get it right first time; keep improving.
- Advantages: fewer defects, less waste, consistent quality, fewer complaints and recalls, staff involvement.
- Drawbacks: training cost, time to change culture, needs commitment from all.

### TQM against end-of-line inspection

| | End-of-line inspection | TQM |
|---|---|---|
| Responsibility | Inspectors | Everyone |
| Faults found | After production | At each stage |
| Waste | Faulty items already made | Fewer faults made |

### The seven listed items, sorted

- Benefits: additional sales; image/reputation; higher price.
- Costs: inspection costs; staff training; the provision of services.
- Product recalls: the cost of failure that spending on quality helps to avoid.

### Growth risks

- Outsourcing: another firm does the work; less direct control.
- Franchising: different owners run outlets; standards may vary.
- Protection: contracts with standards, training, inspections, mystery shoppers, monitoring reviews by outlet.

### Linking quality to the four functions

- Operations: checks at each stage, reliable suppliers, fewer defects.
- Human resources: training every employee under TQM; motivated staff take pride in their work.
- Marketing: a reputation for quality supports a higher price and attracts customers.
- Finance: spending on quality now reduces the cost of returns, waste and recalls later.

### Quick worked reminder

Figures invented. A hotel had 4,500 guests in a month and 135 complaints. Complaint rate = 135 ÷ 4,500 × 100 = 3%. After staff training, complaints fall to 54 from 4,500 guests: 1.2%.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality | Meeting customer expectations consistently |
| Defect | A fault in a product |
| Defect rate | Faulty items as a percentage of output |
| Inspection | Checking products to find faults |
| Mystery shopper | A person paid to use a service as a normal customer and report on it |
| Total quality management (TQM) | An approach that makes every employee responsible for quality at every stage |
| Product recall | Asking customers to return a faulty or unsafe product |
| Outsourcing | Paying another business to carry out an activity |
| Franchising | Allowing others to run outlets under the brand name and methods |
| Reputation | What customers and others believe about a business |

## Exam traps

- **Quality equals luxury.** It means meeting expectations for the price.
- **Goods only.** Services have quality too, and consistency is harder.
- **TQM as more inspection.** It makes everyone responsible.
- **Unsorted lists.** Say whether an item is a cost or a benefit, and why.
- **Missing growth.** Outsourcing and franchising are named in the specification.
- **Measures without comparison.** A 2% defect rate is only good or bad against a target or past figure.

## Self-test

1. Give three expectations customers have of a good.
2. Why is consistent quality harder for services than for goods?
3. Name three ways a business can identify quality problems.
4. 60 of 2,400 items are faulty. Calculate the defect rate.
5. Define TQM.
6. Give two advantages of TQM.
7. Give one drawback of TQM.
8. Name the three benefits of maintaining quality listed in the specification.
9. Name two costs of maintaining quality listed in the specification.
10. Why might outsourcing cause quality problems?
11. Give one way a franchisor can protect quality across outlets.
12. A shop sells 5,000 items and 150 are returned. Calculate the return rate.

### Answers

1. Any three: works properly, lasts, good finish, safe, as described, consistent.
2. Services are delivered by people at the moment of use, so they cannot be checked beforehand and can vary from one occasion to the next.
3. Any three: inspection and testing, complaints and returns, surveys and reviews, mystery shoppers, staff reports.
4. 60 ÷ 2,400 × 100 = 2.5%.
5. An approach in which every employee in every department is responsible for quality at every stage.
6. Any two: fewer defects, less waste, consistent quality, fewer complaints and recalls, staff involvement.
7. Training costs, time to change culture, or the need for commitment from all staff.
8. Additional sales, image/reputation, higher price.
9. Any two: inspection costs, staff training, the provision of services.
10. Another business does the work, so the brand has less direct control over standards.
11. Any one: written standards, training, regular inspections, mystery shoppers, monitoring reviews.
12. 150 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 3%.
