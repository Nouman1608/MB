## Counting rules

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| Protons | Z (atomic number) |
| Neutrons | A − Z |
| Electrons in a neutral atom | Z |
| Electrons in an ion | Z − charge (subtract a positive charge, add a negative one) |
| Polyatomic ion | Add protons and neutrons atom by atom, then adjust electrons for the overall charge |

Quick checks:

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ³¹P³⁻ (Z = 15) | 15 | 16 | 18 |
| ⁴⁰Ca²⁺ (Z = 20) | 20 | 20 | 18 |
| ²H (deuterium) | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| ¹⁶O¹H⁻ | 9 | 8 | 10 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atomic (proton) number, Z | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Mass number, A | Number of protons plus neutrons |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons |
| Relative isotopic mass | Mass of an atom of an isotope relative to one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Relative abundance | Proportion of an isotope in a sample, shown by peak height |
| m/z | Mass-to-charge ratio; equal to the relative mass for a 1+ ion |
| Molecular ion, M⁺ | Ion formed when a molecule loses one electron; its m/z gives Mr |
| Mononuclear ion | Ion containing only one atom |

## Why isotopes exist and how they behave

- An element is defined by its number of protons; nuclei with that number of protons can have different numbers of neutrons.
- Same electron configuration, so **same chemical properties**.
- Different masses, so slightly different physical properties that depend on mass, such as density.

## TOF mass spectrometer in five stages

1. **Ionisation**: gaseous sample turned into positive ions, for example by high-energy electrons: X(g) + e⁻ → X⁺(g) + 2e⁻.
2. **Acceleration**: an electric field gives **all ions the same kinetic energy**.
3. **Ion drift**: no field; ions move at constant speed. KE = ½mv², so lighter ions are faster and arrive first.
4. **Ion detection**: each ion gains an electron at the detector, producing a current proportional to the number of ions.
5. **Data analysis**: flight times become m/z values and currents become abundances, giving the spectrum.

Uses: accurate relative isotopic masses and abundances; identifying elements; finding Mr from the molecular ion peak.

## Relative atomic mass from a spectrum

Ar = Σ(m/z × abundance) ÷ Σ(abundance)

- Percentages: divide by 100.
- Peak heights: divide by their total, whatever it is.
- Check: Ar lies between the smallest and largest m/z, nearer the tallest peak.

Example: lithium-type spectrum, m/z 6 (7.6%) and m/z 7 (92.4%): Ar = (6 × 7.6 + 7 × 92.4) ÷ 100 = 6.92, which matches lithium (6.9).

**Reverse problems.** For two isotopes, let the fraction of the heavier be x: (lighter mass)(1 − x) + (heavier mass)x = Ar. Solve for x.

## Exam traps

- "Isotopes have different masses" is not a definition; state protons and neutrons.
- Chemical properties of isotopes are the same; only mass-dependent physical properties differ.
- Acceleration gives the same **kinetic energy**, not the same speed or velocity.
- The detector registers ions because they **gain electrons**, producing a current.
- Ions must be positive: negative or neutral particles are not accelerated towards the detector.
- Divide by the total of the peak heights, not automatically by 100.
- In an ion, electrons change but protons and neutrons do not.
- For Mr, use the molecular ion peak, not the tallest peak.

## Self-test

1. How many neutrons are in ⁵⁹Co (Z = 27)?
2. How many electrons are in ⁸¹Br⁻ (Z = 35)?
3. Give the numbers of protons and electrons in the ion ¹⁴N¹H₄⁺.
4. Define the term isotopes.
5. Why do ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl react in the same way?
6. Write an equation for the ionisation of a gaseous magnesium atom by electron impact.
7. What is the same for every ion after the acceleration stage?
8. Why do lighter ions reach the detector first?
9. How does the detector produce a signal?
10. An element has peaks at m/z 28 (92.2%), 29 (4.7%) and 30 (3.1%). Calculate Ar to one decimal place and identify the element.
11. A spectrum shows peaks at m/z 35 and 37 with heights 3 and 1. Calculate Ar.
12. The peak with the largest m/z in the spectrum of a compound is at 58. What does this show?

### Answers

1. 59 − 27 = 32.
2. 35 + 1 = 36.
3. Protons 11; electrons 10.
4. Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
5. They have the same electron configuration, and chemical reactions involve electrons.
6. Mg(g) + e⁻ → Mg⁺(g) + 2e⁻
7. Their kinetic energy.
8. With equal kinetic energy, ions of smaller mass travel faster, so they cross the drift region in less time.
9. Each positive ion gains an electron from the detector, creating a current proportional to the number of ions arriving.
10. (28 × 92.2 + 29 × 4.7 + 30 × 3.1) ÷ 100 = 28.1; silicon.
11. (35 × 3 + 37 × 1) ÷ 4 = 35.5.
12. It is the molecular ion peak, so Mr of the compound is 58.
