**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 1, Medieval kingdoms and empires (circa 750 to 1400), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. Every question requires **one example** of a medieval kingdom or empire. The model case here is the Carolingian Empire. For fuller explanation see the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-medieval-kingdoms-empires/).

## What the exam asks

Paper 3 is two hours long. You write two essays, each from a different regional study, and each is marked out of 15. The top band wants an in-depth understanding of the argument in the question, a well-balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement. The command terms you will meet include "To what extent", "Discuss", "Examine", "Explain" and "Analyse".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capitulary | A written royal order, divided into numbered chapters |
| Count | Royal official running a district, with court, army and tax duties |
| Missi dominici | "Envoys of the lord ruler", sent out in pairs to inspect counts |
| Benefice | Land granted in return for service |
| Partible inheritance | Dividing a realm among all sons |
| Carolingian minuscule | Clear, regular script developed in the period |
| Donation of Pippin | Pippin III's gift of Italian lands to the pope (756) |
| Treaty of Verdun | 843 division of the empire among Charlemagne's grandsons |

## Inquiry topic 1: emergence and expansion

- **Political and dynastic:** mayors of the palace overshadowed weak kings; papal approval for Pippin in 751; control of army and estates.
- **Economic:** plunder, tribute and land rewarded warriors, so war paid for itself.
- **Social and cultural:** warrior aristocracy; Christian mission as justification.
- **Conflict and settlement:** annual campaigns (Lombards, Saxons, Avars, Spanish March); settlement of counts, bishops and monasteries in conquered land; forced conversion in Saxony.
- Link: Charlemagne inherited strengths, so avoid a "great man only" argument.

## Inquiry topic 2: authority

- **Legal and political:** capitularies, counts, missi, oaths, annual assemblies.
- **Social:** the nobility ruled on the king's behalf; bishops and abbots were royal officials; marriage links.
- **Economic:** royal estates (*Capitulare de villis*), tolls, fines, booty, a silver penny coinage.
- **Force:** annual army summons, frontier garrisons, harsh punishment of rebels.
- **Legitimization:** imperial coronation (800), anointing, a reputation as defender of the Church.

## Inquiry topic 3: religion

- **Political:** sacred kingship; royal laws mixed religious and civil rules.
- **Institutions:** royal gifts enriched monasteries and cathedrals; Benedictine rule promoted.
- **Social:** priests taught belief and behaviour; the Church provided charity.
- **Papacy:** a partnership of protector and blessing; unresolved question of who was superior.

## Inquiry topic 4: culture and society

- **Technology:** heavy plough, mills and crop rotation spreading unevenly.
- **Arts:** palace school at Aachen, Alcuin, manuscripts, minuscule, Aachen chapel.
- **Women:** empresses, abbesses and noblewomen with influence; Dhuoda's handbook; most women worked on the land.
- **Marginalized groups:** the unfree, Jewish communities under protection and pressure, conquered peoples.

## Inquiry topic 5: decline

- **Internal:** revolts of the sons of Louis the Pious; civil war; Verdun in 843.
- **Economic and social:** end of plunder; land given away; local lords took over protection.
- **Political and external:** partition custom; Viking, Magyar and Muslim raids; weak successors.

## The leader (Charlemagne)

- **Emergence and expansion:** military leadership and a long list of conquests.
- **Rule:** the governmental system of counts, missi and capitularies.
- **Religion and culture:** reform, schooling, patronage of scholars.
- **Decline:** historians divide between blaming his successors, his heavy personal burden on the system, and structural weakness.

## Historians' views

- Pirenne: the Islamic expansion cut Mediterranean trade and shaped the Carolingian world; later work challenges the dating.
- Nelson and McKitterick: government worked through negotiation and the written word.
- Reynolds: doubts that "feudalism" is a helpful label.

## Question wording and how to respond

| Question starts | What it needs |
|---|---|
| "To what extent was the success of ... due to ...?" | Rank factors, weigh the named factor against others, give a judgement |
| "Discuss the role of religion in ..." | Both political and social effects, with evidence and different views |
| "Examine the reasons for the decline of ..." | Short-term and long-term reasons, with links between them |
| "Analyse how ... maintained authority" | Break down methods (law, force, economy) and judge which mattered most |

## Common mistakes

- Telling the story of a reign without answering the claim in the question.
- Using several examples when the question needs one.
- Treating a source such as Einhard as plain fact rather than as a view with a purpose.
- Listing methods of rule without ranking them.
- Forgetting women and marginalized groups when the question asks about society.

## Self-test

1. Name two methods used to check the counts.
2. Why was the coronation of 800 important to both king and pope?
3. Give one economic reason for expansion.
4. What was decided at Verdun in 843?
5. Give one reason historians doubt the term "Carolingian Renaissance".
6. Name one other example of a medieval kingdom the guide suggests.

**Answers:** 1. Missi dominici and annual assemblies or oaths. 2. It linked the king to Rome and the Church, and gave the pope a strong protector. 3. Plunder, tribute or new land to reward warriors. 4. The empire was divided among the three surviving sons of Louis the Pious. 5. It may overstate how many people benefited from the learning. 6. For example the Holy Roman Empire, Capetian France or the Kingdom of Poland.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need all five inquiry topics for one example?**
Yes, because any could be examined, and questions often combine two of them.

**How many historians should I name?**
Two or three that you can explain accurately are better than a long list.

**Can I mix kingdoms in one essay?**
No. Questions refer to one example, so use one.
