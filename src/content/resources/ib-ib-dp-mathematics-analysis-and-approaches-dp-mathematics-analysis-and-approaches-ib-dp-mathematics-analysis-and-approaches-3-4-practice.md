This set covers **section 3.4** (SL and HL) of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: radian measure, arc length and the area of a sector. Each question says whether it is calculator-free or calculator allowed. Angles are in radians unless stated; give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Learn the methods in the [radian measure study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-4/) and recap them in the [radian measure revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-4-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** (calculator-free) Write 330° in radians as an exact multiple of π. **[1]**
**(b)** (calculator-free) Write 4π/9 radians in degrees. **[1]**
**(c)** (calculator allowed) Write 0.45 radians in degrees. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator-free) A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 3π/5. Find its exact:

**(a)** arc length **[1]**
**(b)** area **[1]**
**(c)** perimeter **[1]**

**3.** (calculator-free) A sector of radius 4 cm has area 24 cm².

**(a)** Find its angle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its arc length. **[1]**

**4.** (calculator-free) An arc of a circle of radius 6 cm has length 7π/2 cm.

**(a)** Find the angle it subtends at the centre, in radians. **[2]**
**(b)** Write this angle in degrees. **[1]**

**5.** (calculator-free) A sector of radius 8 cm has perimeter 26 cm.

**(a)** Find its angle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its area. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) A pendulum of length 80 cm swings through an angle of 0.35 radians either side of the vertical. Find the length of the arc its tip travels from one end of the swing to the other. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator-free) A sector has radius r cm, angle θ, perimeter 28 cm and area 49 cm².

**(a)** Show that r² − 14r + 49 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Find r and θ. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) A circle with centre O has radius 15 cm. A chord AB has length 20 cm.

**(a)** Find angle AOB. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the minor arc AB. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the area of the minor segment. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the perimeter of the major segment. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator-free) A garden bed is the region between two concentric arcs of radii 6 m and 10 m, with angle 3π/8 at the centre.

**(a)** Find the exact area of the bed. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact perimeter of the bed. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) The minute hand of a clock is 11 cm long.

**(a)** Find the angle, in radians, that it turns through in 25 minutes. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the distance travelled by its tip in this time. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the area it sweeps out in this time. **[2]**

**11.** A sector OAB has radius r and angle θ, where 0 < θ < π. The area of the minor segment cut off by the chord AB is one quarter of the area of the sector.

**(a)** (calculator-free) Show that 3θ = 4 sin θ. **[3]**
**(b)** (calculator allowed) Find θ. **[2]**

**12.** (calculator-free) A sector has radius r and angle θ. The numerical value of its area equals the square of its arc length.

**(a)** Show that θ = ½. **[2]**
**(b)** Given that the perimeter of the sector is 50 cm, find r. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of the sector. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 330 × π/180 = 11π/6 [1]
**(b)** 4π/9 × 180/π = 80° [1]
**(c)** 0.45 × 180/π = 25.8° [1]

**2. (a)** 10 × 3π/5 = 6π cm [1]
**(b)** ½ × 100 × 3π/5 = 30π cm² [1]
**(c)** 20 + 6π cm [1]

**3. (a)** ½ × 16 × θ = 24 [1], θ = 3 radians [1]
**(b)** 4 × 3 = 12 cm [1]

**4. (a)** 6θ = 7π/2 [1], θ = 7π/12 [1]
**(b)** 105° [1]

**5. (a)** 16 + 8θ = 26 [1], θ = 1.25 radians [1]
**(b)** ½ × 64 × 1.25 = 40 cm² [1]

**6.** Total angle 2 × 0.35 = 0.7 radians [1]. Arc = 80 × 0.7 = 56.0 cm [1]

**7. (a)** 2r + rθ = 28, so rθ = 28 − 2r [1]. ½r²θ = ½r(rθ) = ½r(28 − 2r) = 49 [1]. 14r − r² = 49, so r² − 14r + 49 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (r − 7)² = 0, so r = 7 cm [1]; θ = (28 − 14)/7 = 2 radians [1]

**8. (a)** sin(θ/2) = 10/15 [1], θ = 2 sin⁻¹(2/3) = 1.46 radians [1]
**(b)** 15 × 1.4595 = 21.9 cm [1]
**(c)** Sector = ½ × 225 × 1.4595 = 164.19 [1]; triangle = ½ × 225 × sin 1.4595 = 111.80 [1]. Segment = 52.4 cm² [1]
**(d)** Major arc = 15(2π − 1.4595) = 72.36 cm [1]. Perimeter = 72.36 + 20 = 92.4 cm [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the calculator must be in radian mode; sin 1.4595 in degree mode gives a value near 0.025, and the segment area becomes far too large.

**9. (a)** ½(10² − 6²)(3π/8) [1] = 32 × 3π/8 = 12π m² [1]
**(b)** 10(3π/8) + 6(3π/8) + 2(4) [1] = 6π + 8 m [1]

**10. (a)** (25/60) × 2π = 5π/6 [1]
**(b)** 11 × 5π/6 = 28.8 cm [1]
**(c)** ½ × 121 × 5π/6 [1] = 158 cm² [1]

**11. (a)** Segment = ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ [1]. ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ = ¼ × ½r²θ [1]. Divide by ½r²: θ − sin θ = θ/4, so 3θ/4 = sin θ and 3θ = 4 sin θ [1]
**(b)** Solving 3θ = 4 sin θ with technology for 0 < θ < π [1]: θ = 1.28 radians [1]
*Examiner insight:* θ = 0 also satisfies the equation but is excluded by 0 < θ < π.

**12. (a)** ½r²θ = (rθ)² = r²θ² [1]. Divide by r²θ (both positive): θ = ½ [1]
**(b)** 2r + ½r = 50 [1], so r = 20 cm [1]
**(c)** ½ × 400 × ½ = 100 cm² [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using degrees in l = rθ or ½r²θ.
- Leaving out the radii in a sector perimeter, or the chord in a segment perimeter.
- Degree mode when evaluating sin θ for a segment.
- Not justifying the rejection of an impossible solution.
- Rounding θ early, which shifts the segment area in the third figure.
