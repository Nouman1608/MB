## Equations

| Equation | Use | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Q = mcΔθ | change of temperature | J |
| P = (m/t)cΔθ | continuous flow heating | W |
| Q = ml | change of state | J |
| pV = nRT | n moles of ideal gas | Pa, m³, mol, K |
| pV = NkT | N molecules | Pa, m³, K |
| k = R/N_A; N = nN_A | linking constants | J K⁻¹ |
| W = pΔV | work done at constant pressure | J |
| pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² | kinetic theory | Pa m³ |
| ½m(c_rms)² = (3/2)kT = (3/2)RT/N_A | mean kinetic energy per molecule | J |
| T (K) = θ (°C) + 273 | temperature conversion | K |

## 3.6.2.1 Thermal energy transfer: recall

- **Internal energy** = sum of randomly distributed kinetic and potential energies of particles.
- Increased by **heating** the system or doing **work on** it (qualitative first law).
- **Change of state**: potential energy changes, kinetic energy does not, so temperature is constant.
- **Heating curve** at constant power: slopes → mcΔθ; plateaus → ml; shallower slope → larger c.
- Heat loss to surroundings → calculated c or l **too large** (systematic error).
- **Method of mixtures**: energy lost by hot parts = energy gained by cold parts (include any latent heat).

## 3.6.2.2 Ideal gases: recall

| Law | Constant | Relationship | Straight-line graph |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boyle's | T | pV = constant | p against 1/V, through origin |
| Charles's | p | V/T = constant | V against T (K), through origin |
| Pressure | V | p/T = constant | p against T (K), through origin |

- **Absolute zero**: 0 K (about −273 °C), the temperature at which particles have minimum kinetic energy; found by extrapolating V–θ or p–θ graphs.
- **Molar mass**: mass of one mole (kg mol⁻¹). **Molecular mass**: molar mass/N_A.
- **Required practical 8**: Boyle (constant T, change pressure slowly); Charles (constant p, water bath, stir, wait for equilibrium).

## 3.6.2.3 Kinetic theory: recall

**Assumptions**

- Large number of identical molecules in random motion.
- Collisions with walls and each other are elastic.
- Volume of molecules negligible compared with the container.
- No intermolecular forces except during collisions.
- Collision time negligible compared with time between collisions.

**Derivation outline**: momentum change 2mu per hit → time between hits 2L/u → force mu²/L → sum over N molecules → mean u² = ⅓(c_rms)² → divide by area L² and use V = L³.

- **Brownian motion**: random motion of visible particles from uneven molecular bombardment; evidence for atoms.
- Gas laws are **empirical**; kinetic theory is **theoretical**.
- Ideal gas internal energy is **all kinetic**: U = (3/2)nRT.
- Same T → same mean E_k per molecule for all gases → c_rms ∝ 1/√m.
- c_rms = √(mean of squares of speeds), not the mean speed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Specific heat capacity | Energy to raise 1 kg by 1 K without change of state |
| Specific latent heat | Energy to change the state of 1 kg without change of temperature |
| Internal energy | Sum of random kinetic and potential energies of particles |
| Absolute zero | Lowest possible temperature, 0 K |
| Avogadro constant | Number of particles in one mole |
| Boltzmann constant | k = R/N_A, the gas constant per molecule |
| Ideal gas | Gas that obeys pV = nRT exactly, with the kinetic theory assumptions |
| Root mean square speed | Square root of the mean of the squared molecular speeds |
| Brownian motion | Random motion of small visible particles caused by molecular collisions |

## Exam traps

- Ratios such as p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂ fail in °C.
- cm³ to m³ means × 10⁻⁶, not × 10⁻³.
- A "fixed mass" condition matters: if gas leaks out, the gas laws alone no longer apply.
- Doubling the absolute temperature doubles mean E_k but multiplies c_rms by only √2.
- In continuous flow, use mass per second, not total mass.
- A plateau on a heating curve is not "no energy supplied".

## Self-test

**1.** Define specific latent heat.
**2.** Why is temperature constant while a solid melts?
**3.** A 2.0 kW heater warms water flowing at 0.040 kg s⁻¹ (c = 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹). Find the temperature rise.
**4.** Convert 27 °C to kelvin.
**5.** State Boyle's law.
**6.** A gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K is heated to 450 K at constant volume. Find the new pressure.
**7.** How many molecules are in 0.50 mol?
**8.** State two assumptions of the kinetic theory model.
**9.** What is the internal energy of an ideal gas made of?
**10.** By what factor does c_rms change if T rises from 300 K to 1200 K?
**11.** Why are gas laws described as empirical?
**12.** Calculate the work done when a gas expands by 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

## Self-test answers

**1.** Energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature.
**2.** Energy increases the potential energy of the particles (breaking bonds), not their kinetic energy.
**3.** Δθ = P/((m/t)c) = 2000/(0.040 × 4200) = 12 K.
**4.** 300 K.
**5.** For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pV is constant.
**6.** p = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 450/300 = 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa.
**7.** 0.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.0 × 10²³.
**8.** Any two of: elastic collisions; negligible molecular volume; no intermolecular forces except in collisions; negligible collision time; many molecules in random motion.
**9.** Only the kinetic energy of its molecules.
**10.** T × 4, so c_rms × √4 = × 2.
**11.** They come from experimental measurement, not from theory.
**12.** W = pΔV = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 200 J.
