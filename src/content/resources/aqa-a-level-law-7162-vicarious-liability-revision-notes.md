Condensed notes for the section 3.3 row **Vicarious liability**: "Nature and purpose of vicarious liability", "Testing employment status" and "Other areas of vicarious liability". For explanation and a worked problem, use the [vicarious liability study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-vicarious-liability/).

## Nature and purpose

- Liability of one person (D) for a **tort committed by another** (the wrongdoer).
- **Strict:** D's own care is irrelevant.
- Wrongdoer remains personally liable.
- **Purposes** (*Catholic Child Welfare Society*, 2012; *Cox*, 2016): ability to pay and insurance; enterprise risk; activity done for D's benefit; D created the risk; control and deterrence.

## The two-stage test (Barclays Bank, 2020)

```
Stage 1  RELATIONSHIP   employee, or relationship akin to employment?
Stage 2  CONNECTION     tort in course of employment / closely connected?
Both yes -> D vicariously liable for the wrongdoer's tort
```

## Stage 1: employment status

| Test | Question | Case |
|---|---|---|
| Control | Does D control what and how? | *Yewens v Noakes* (1880) |
| Integration | Is the work integral to the business? | *Stevenson Jordan & Harrison v Macdonald & Evans* (1952) |
| Multiple/economic reality | Wage for work; sufficient control; terms consistent | *Ready Mixed Concrete* (1968) |
| Own account | Who takes the financial risk? | *Market Investigations* (1969) |
| Labels | Not decisive | *Ferguson v John Dawson* (1976) |

**Akin to employment:**

| Case | Relationship | Result |
|---|---|---|
| *E v English Province* (2012) | Priest and diocese | Capable of VL |
| *Catholic Child Welfare Society* (2012) | Brothers and religious institute | Liable |
| *Cox v MoJ* (2016) | Prisoner working in kitchen | Liable |
| *Armes v Nottinghamshire CC* (2017) | Foster parents and local authority | Liable |
| *Barclays Bank* (2020) | Independent doctor with many clients | Not liable |

## Stage 2: course of employment

**In the course:**
- Prohibited way of doing the job: *Limpus v London General Omnibus* (1862), racing buses; *Rose v Plenty* (1976), boy helping milkman.
- Careless act at work: *Century Insurance* (1942), match thrown away while delivering petrol.

**Outside the course:**
- Unauthorised passenger not helping the business: *Twine v Bean's Express* (1946).
- Act wholly outside the job: *Beard v London General Omnibus* (1900), conductor driving.
- Frolic of his own: *Hilton v Thomas Burton* (1961).

**Close connection (deliberate wrongs):**

| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| *Lister v Hesley Hall* (2001), warden abuse | Liable |
| *Mattis v Pollock* (2003), doorman stabbing | Liable |
| *Mohamud v WM Morrison* (2016), kiosk attack | Liable: unbroken sequence |
| *WM Morrison v Various Claimants* (2020), data leak | Not liable: personal vendetta |
| *Barry Congregation v BXB* (2023), elder's assault | Not liable: no close connection |

## Other areas

- **Lent employees:** general employer usually stays liable: *Mersey Docks v Coggins & Griffith* (1947). Dual liability possible: *Viasystems* (2005).
- **Independent contractors:** no vicarious liability (*Barclays*). A **non-delegable duty** is a separate, personal duty: *Woodland v Swimming Teachers Association* (2013).
- **Employer's indemnity:** employer can recover from negligent employee: *Lister v Romford Ice* (1957).

## Evaluation points

- **For:** compensates victims from a party able to pay and insure; places risk on the enterprise that profits from the activity (*Cox*); encourages careful recruitment and supervision.
- **Against:** liability without fault conflicts with corrective justice; "fair and just" in the close connection test is open-ended.
- **Abuse cases:** *Lister* and *Catholic Child Welfare Society* filled a gap for victims, but extended liability to organisations far from traditional employers.
- **Certainty restored?** *Barclays* and *Morrison* (2020) drew limits, but cases such as *Mohamud* and *BXB* show how fine the line remains.
- **Gig economy:** workers labelled self-employed but controlled through an app sit close to the boundary; *Ferguson* means the label will not protect the business.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vicarious liability | Liability for a tort committed by another |
| Akin to employment | A relationship close enough in character to employment |
| Course of employment | Doing the job, even in an unauthorised way |
| Frolic of his own | An activity unconnected with the job |
| Close connection | Link between job and tort making liability fair and just |
| Enterprise risk | The business benefiting from an activity bears its risks |
| Non-delegable duty | A personal duty that cannot be discharged by hiring a contractor |
| Indemnity | Recovery by the employer of its loss from the employee |

## Exam traps

- Always identify the wrongdoer's tort first.
- Do not decide status from the contract's label.
- One act may be in the course of employment for one claimant but not another.
- *Rose v Plenty* (boy helping the round) and *Twine* (hitch-hiker) are distinguished by benefit to the business.
- *Woodland* is not vicarious liability.

## Self-test

1. Is vicarious liability fault-based? *No; it is strict.*
2. State the two stages. *Relationship of employment or akin to it; close connection between relationship and tort.*
3. Which case gives the multiple test? *Ready Mixed Concrete (1968).*
4. What did *Ferguson v John Dawson* decide? *A self-employed label does not decide status.*
5. Why was the prison liable in *Cox*? *The prisoner's work was integrated into the prison's activities and for its benefit.*
6. Why was the bank not liable in *Barclays*? *The doctor was an independent contractor in business on his own account.*
7. Distinguish *Rose v Plenty* and *Twine v Bean's Express*. *The boy helped the employer's business; the hitch-hiker did not.*
8. What was the frolic in *Hilton v Thomas Burton*? *Workmen driving off for tea in working hours.*
9. What test applies to deliberate wrongdoing? *Close connection (Lister).*
10. Why was Morrison not liable in 2020? *The employee was pursuing a personal vendetta.*
11. Who is liable for a lent employee in *Mersey Docks*? *The general employer, the harbour board.*
12. What did *Lister v Romford Ice* decide? *An employer can recover its loss from the negligent employee.*
