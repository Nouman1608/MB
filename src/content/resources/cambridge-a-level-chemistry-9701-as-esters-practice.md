**3.** Name the catalyst used when an ester is made from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Butyl ethanoate is used as a solvent.

**(a)** Name the carboxylic acid and the alcohol used to make butyl ethanoate. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for its formation. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the reaction does not go to completion, and suggest one way to increase the yield.
**[2]**

**7.** Methyl butanoate is heated under reflux with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how butanoic acid is then obtained from the reaction mixture, and write an equation for
this step. **[2]**

**8.** Ester G, C₆H₁₂O₂, is a straight-chain ester. Heating G with dilute acid gives propanoic acid and an
alcohol, H.

**(a)** Identify alcohol H. **[1]**

**(b)** Name G. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the structural formula of G. **[1]**

**9.** 4.60 g of ethanol is heated with excess methanoic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric
acid. 5.18 g of ethyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₃, is obtained.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of ethanol used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield of ethyl methanoate. **[2]**

**10.** 2.04 g of methyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃, is completely hydrolysed by excess aqueous sodium
hydroxide. Calculate the mass of sodium butanoate formed. **[2]**

**11.** Three compounds have the molecular formula C₃H₆O₂: propanoic acid and two esters.

**(a)** Name the two esters. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe a chemical test that distinguishes propanoic acid from the two esters. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why the hydrolysis of an ester by aqueous sodium hydroxide goes to completion, whereas
hydrolysis by dilute acid does not. **[2]**

**13.** A student mixes ethanoic acid, ethanol and a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid in a test tube at
room temperature. Very little ester forms. Suggest **two** changes to the method that would produce more
ester. **[2]**

**14.** An ester, J, is heated under reflux with dilute NaOH(aq). The alcohol produced is ethanol. The
solution left is acidified, and the carboxylic acid that forms reacts with LiAlH₄ to give propan-1-ol.

**(a)** Identify the carboxylic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Name ester J. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Propyl ethanoate [1].

**2.** CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ [1].

**3.** Concentrated sulfuric acid [1].

**4.** Methanoic acid [1] and ethanol [1].

**5.** Any two [2]: acid hydrolysis is reversible but alkaline hydrolysis goes to completion; acid
hydrolysis gives the carboxylic acid but alkaline hydrolysis gives the carboxylate salt; the acid is a
catalyst but the alkali is a reactant that is used up; the alkaline route needs acidification to give the
free acid.

**6. (a)** Ethanoic acid [1] and butan-1-ol [1].

**(b)** CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O [1].

**(c)** The reaction is reversible and reaches equilibrium [1]; use an excess of one reactant, such as
butan-1-ol (or remove the ester as it forms) [1].

**7. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa + CH₃OH [1].

**(b)** Acidify the mixture by adding a strong acid such as dilute HCl [1]:
CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa + HCl → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NaCl [1].

**8. (a)** Propanoic acid supplies three of the six carbons, so H has three carbons in a straight chain:
propan-1-ol [1].

**(b)** Propyl propanoate [1].

**(c)** CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ [1].

**9. (a)** Mr(C₂H₅OH) = 46.0; n = 4.60 / 46.0 = 0.100 mol [1].

**(b)** Mr(HCOOCH₂CH₃) = 3(12.0) + 6(1.0) + 2(16.0) = 74.0; n(ester) = 5.18 / 74.0 = 0.0700 mol [1];
yield = 0.0700 / 0.100 × 100 = 70.0% [1].

**10.** Mr(methyl butanoate) = 102.0, so n = 2.04 / 102.0 = 0.0200 mol, and n(sodium butanoate) =
0.0200 mol [1]. Mr(CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa) = 4(12.0) + 7(1.0) + 2(16.0) + 23.0 = 110.0; mass = 0.0200 × 110.0 =
2.20 g [1].

**11. (a)** Methyl ethanoate [1]; ethyl methanoate [1].

**(b)** Add sodium carbonate (or sodium hydrogencarbonate) [1]; propanoic acid gives effervescence of
CO₂, the esters do not [1].

**12.** In alkali, the carboxylic acid formed is immediately converted into its carboxylate ion (salt) [1].
The carboxylate ion does not react with the alcohol to re-form the ester, so the reverse reaction cannot
happen; with dilute acid the products can react back, so an equilibrium is reached [1].

**13.** Use concentrated (not dilute) sulfuric acid as catalyst [1]; heat the mixture, for example under
reflux [1].

**14. (a)** Propanoic acid: reduction by LiAlH₄ gives a primary alcohol with the same carbon chain, and
propan-1-ol has three carbons [1].

**(b)** Ethyl propanoate, CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing ester names, for example calling HCOOCH₂CH₃ "methyl ethanoate".
- Writing "dilute H₂SO₄" for esterification, or "concentrated" for hydrolysis.
- Giving the free carboxylic acid as the product of alkaline hydrolysis.
- Leaving water out of the esterification equation.
- Using the wrong Mr in yield calculations because the ester formula was written incorrectly.
- Forgetting that a branched alcohol part is outside the naming requirement, then guessing a branched
  structure when "straight-chain" is stated.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I split an ester's carbons between the acid and alcohol parts?**
Find the C=O. The C=O carbon and every carbon attached on that side belong to the acid part; the carbons
on the other side of the single-bonded oxygen belong to the alcohol part.

**Is the acid in acid hydrolysis used up?**
No. Dilute acid catalyses the hydrolysis and is not used up, whereas sodium hydroxide in alkaline
hydrolysis is a reactant and is used up as the salt forms.
