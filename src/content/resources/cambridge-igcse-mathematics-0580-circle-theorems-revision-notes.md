These notes condense subtopic **4.7 Circle theorems** from Topic 4 Geometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core (C4.7) has two properties; Extended (E4.7, "Circle theorems I") has those two and four more. For explanations and worked examples, read the [circle theorems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems/); to practise, use the [circle theorems practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-practice/).

## The six properties

| Property (write it like this) | How to spot it | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| angle in a semicircle = 90° | a triangle with one side a **diameter** and the third vertex on the circle | Core and Extended |
| angle between tangent and radius = 90° | a **tangent** and a line from the **centre** to the point of contact | Core and Extended |
| angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference | two lines from O and two lines from a point on the circle meeting the **same two points** | Extended |
| angles in the same segment are equal | two angles on the **same chord**, on the **same side** of it ("bow tie") | Extended |
| opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° | four vertices **all on the circle** | Extended |
| alternate segment theorem | a **tangent** and a **chord** meeting at the point of contact | Extended |

The syllabus expects these properties, as listed, to be used when giving reasons.

## Facts that travel with them

- **Two radii make an isosceles triangle.** OA = OB, so the angles at A and B in triangle OAB are equal.
- **Tangent + radius gives a right-angled triangle**, so Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry can follow on a calculator paper.
- **A diameter through the centre** splits angles at O on a straight line: they sum to 180°.
- The angle facts of subtopic 4.6 (straight lines, points, triangles, parallel lines) appear in almost every circle question.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chord | A straight line joining two points on the circle |
| Diameter | A chord through the centre |
| Tangent | A straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point |
| Arc | Part of the circumference; minor arc is the shorter, major arc the longer |
| Segment | The region between a chord and an arc |
| Cyclic quadrilateral | A quadrilateral with all four vertices on the circle |
| Subtended | An angle "stands on" or is subtended by the arc or chord its two lines reach |

## Recognising the angle at the centre

```
both angles must stand on the SAME arc AB
  centre angle AOB  ->  circumference angle ACB = AOB ÷ 2
  C on the minor arc  ->  use the REFLEX angle at O
  semicircle case: AOB = 180°, so ACB = 90°
```

The angle in a semicircle is the special case of the centre theorem with a straight angle at O.

## Recognising the alternate segment

Tangent at A, chord AB. The angle between the tangent and AB equals the angle at C, on the circle, in the segment on the **other side** of AB from that angle. Three quick checks:

1. One arm of the tangent angle is the chord.
2. The equal angle stands on that same chord.
3. It is in the opposite segment.

## Worked reminders

**Core.** PQ is a diameter; R is on the circle; angle QPR = 3x and angle PQR = 2x.
Angle PRQ = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so 5x = 90, x = 18, angle QPR = 54°.

**Extended.** ABCD is cyclic, angle ADC = 104°. Then angle ABC = 76° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°). If O is the centre, angle AOC (on the same arc as angle ABC) = 2 × 76° = 152°.

## Exam traps

- Putting the semicircle right angle at an end of the diameter instead of on the circumference.
- Treating a quadrilateral with a vertex at O as cyclic.
- Calling two angles "in the same segment" when they are on opposite sides of the chord; those add to 180°.
- Doubling instead of halving, or using the non-reflex centre angle when the circumference point is on the minor arc.
- Matching the tangent–chord angle to the angle in the **same** segment instead of the alternate one.
- Reasons such as "circle theorem", "cyclic quad" or "tangent rule": write the full property.
- Assuming a line is a diameter, or passes through O, because it looks like it. It must be stated or marked.

## Self-test

1. AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle ABC = 47°. Find angle BAC.
2. A tangent at T meets a line from the centre O at P. Angle TPO = 29°. Find angle TOP.
3. In question 2, OT = 6 cm and PT = 8 cm. Find OP.
4. O is the centre; OA and OB are radii; angle AOB = 110°. Find angle OAB.
5. (Extended) Angle AOB = 96° at the centre. C is on the major arc. Find angle ACB.
6. (Extended) In question 5, D is on the minor arc. Find angle ADB.
7. (Extended) A cyclic quadrilateral has angles 2y, 3y, 100° and 80°, with 2y opposite 3y. Find y.
8. (Extended) Angles ACB and ADB stand on chord AB, with C and D on the same side. Angle ACB = 37°. Find angle ADB.
9. (Extended) A tangent at A makes an angle of 72° with chord AB. Find the angle at C in the alternate segment.
10. (Extended) Can a quadrilateral with angles 95°, 85°, 100° and 80°, in that order, be cyclic?
11. (Extended) Angle ACB = 128° and C is on the minor arc AB. Find the reflex angle AOB and the angle AOB.
12. Why is a triangle with two radii as sides isosceles?

### Answers

1. 180 − 90 − 47 = **43°**
2. Angle OTP = 90°, so angle TOP = **61°**
3. √(36 + 64) = **10 cm**
4. (180 − 110) ÷ 2 = **35°**
5. **48°**
6. 180 − 48 = **132°**
7. 5y = 180, **y = 36°**
8. **37°**
9. **72°**
10. **No.** The opposite pairs are 95° and 100° (sum 195°) and 85° and 80° (sum 165°); neither pair sums to 180°.
11. Reflex AOB = 256°, so AOB = **104°**
12. Its two sides are radii of the same circle, so they are **equal in length**.
