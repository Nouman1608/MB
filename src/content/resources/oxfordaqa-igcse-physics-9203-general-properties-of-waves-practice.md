These questions cover **3.3.1 General properties of waves**, statements a to h, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Take the speed of electromagnetic waves as 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Learn the content first from the [General properties of waves study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-general-properties-of-waves/) and the [General properties of waves revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-general-properties-of-waves-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State **two** things that waves transfer. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the difference between a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each wave is transverse or longitudinal:

**(a)** a microwave **[1]**

**(b)** an ultrasound wave in the body. **[1]**

**4.** Define the period of a wave, and calculate the period of a wave with frequency 125 Hz. **[3]**

**5.** A wave on a string has a speed of 18 m/s and a wavelength of 0.60 m. Calculate its frequency. **[2]**

**6.** A mobile phone signal has a frequency of 1.8 GHz. Calculate its wavelength. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student sends waves along a long slinky spring lying on a bench. A small piece of tape is stuck to one coil.

**(a)** Describe how the student should move the end of the slinky to produce a longitudinal wave. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the tape moves as the longitudinal wave passes. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the movement of the tape shows that the wave transfers energy but not matter. **[2]**

**8.** A displacement-distance graph for a water wave shows 4.0 complete waves over a distance of 1.2 m. The crests are 1.5 cm above the rest line. The wave passes a point at a frequency of 2.5 Hz.

**(a)** Determine the wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** State the amplitude. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the wave speed. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the time it takes the wave to travel 6.0 m. **[2]**

**9.** In a ripple tank, straight water waves pass from deep water into shallow water. Their speed falls from 0.30 m/s to 0.20 m/s. The frequency of the vibrating bar is 5.0 Hz.

**(a)** State the frequency of the waves in the shallow water. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength in the deep water and in the shallow water. **[2]**

**(c)** The waves meet the boundary at an angle. State and explain what happens to their direction. **[2]**

**10.** Light travels from air into a glass block. Some of the light is seen as a reflection from the surface.

**(a)** Name the **three** things that can happen to a wave at a boundary between two materials. **[3]**

**(b)** State which of these happens to most of the light at the air-glass surface, and which produces the reflection that is seen. **[2]**

**11.** A house is in a deep valley behind a large hill from a transmitter. The transmitter broadcasts long-wave radio at 198 kHz and FM radio at 95 MHz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of each signal. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the long-wave signal is received clearly at the house but the FM signal is weak. **[3]**

**12.** Diffraction affects the design of optical instruments and medical ultrasound scanners.

**(a)** Explain why a telescope with a wider aperture can show finer detail. **[2]**

**(b)** An ultrasound scanner uses a frequency of 3.0 MHz in tissue where the wave speed is 1500 m/s. Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this short wavelength helps the scanner produce a detailed image. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Energy [1]; information [1]. **[2]**

**2.** In a transverse wave the oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer [1]; in a longitudinal wave they are parallel to it [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Transverse [1].

**(b)** Longitudinal [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The time for one complete wave to pass a point [1]; T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 125 [1] = **0.0080 s** (8.0 ms) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** f = v ÷ λ = 18 ÷ 0.60 [1] = **30 Hz** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** f = 1.8 × 10⁹ Hz [1]; λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.8 × 10⁹ [1] = **0.17 m** (0.167 m) [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Push and pull the end backwards and forwards along the length of the slinky [1].

**(b)** The tape moves backwards and forwards [1], parallel to the length of the slinky, about a fixed position [1].

**(c)** The tape returns to its starting position after the wave has passed, so the coil has not travelled along the slinky [1]; but the wave (and its energy) reaches the far end [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 1.2 ÷ 4.0 = **0.30 m** [1].

**(b)** **1.5 cm** (0.015 m) [1].

**(c)** v = 2.5 × 0.30 [1] = **0.75 m/s** [1].

**(d)** t = 6.0 ÷ 0.75 [1] = **8.0 s** [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** **5.0 Hz** [1].

**(b)** Deep: 0.30 ÷ 5.0 = **0.060 m** [1]; shallow: 0.20 ÷ 5.0 = **0.040 m** [1].

**(c)** The waves change direction (refract) [1] because their speed changes as they cross the boundary [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Reflection [1]; transmission [1]; absorption [1].

**(b)** Most of the light is transmitted [1]; the reflection seen is produced by reflection of a small part of the light [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Long wave: 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.98 × 10⁵ = **1500 m** (1515 m) [1]; FM: 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.5 × 10⁷ = **3.2 m** (3.16 m) [1].

**(b)** The long-wave wavelength is comparable to the size of the hill [1], so it diffracts around the hill into the valley [1]; the FM wavelength is much smaller than the hill, so it diffracts very little and the hill blocks most of it [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** Light diffracts as it passes through the aperture, which blurs fine detail [1]; a wider aperture is much larger than the wavelength of light, so there is less diffraction and the image is sharper [1].

**(b)** λ = v ÷ f = 1500 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁶ [1] = **5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m** (0.50 mm) [1].

**(c)** A short wavelength diffracts very little around small structures [1], so the beam stays narrow and small features can be distinguished [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* every diffraction answer needs the comparison between wavelength and size; "because it is small" is not enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Doubling the amplitude by measuring crest to trough.
- Forgetting to convert GHz, MHz or kHz.
- Saying frequency changes during refraction.
- Diffraction explanations with no comparison of wavelength and gap.
