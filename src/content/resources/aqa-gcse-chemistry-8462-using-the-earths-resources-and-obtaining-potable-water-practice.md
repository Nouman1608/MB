These questions cover section **4.10.1 Using the Earth's resources and obtaining potable water** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2.
Questions 9 and 10 test phytomining and bioleaching, which the specification marks (HT only), and are labelled
**Higher tier only**. Question 6 is based on required practical 8.

Learn the content first in the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-using-the-earths-resources-and-obtaining-potable-water/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-using-the-earths-resources-and-obtaining-potable-water-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Crude oil and timber are both natural resources.

**(a)** Classify each as finite or renewable and give a reason for each. **[4]**
**(b)** Give **one** example of a natural product that has been replaced by a synthetic product. **[1]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by sustainable development. **[2]**

**3.** A mineral has reserves of 6.0 × 10⁹ tonnes. World use is 2.0 × 10⁷ tonnes per year. Calculate how many years the reserves will last at this rate. **[2]**

**4.** Rain water in the UK collects in reservoirs. It is treated to become potable.

**(a)** State what is meant by potable water. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why potable water is not pure water. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the main steps in treating fresh water to make it potable, giving a reason for each step. **[4]**

**5.** A country with little rainfall obtains drinking water from the sea.

**(a)** Name **two** processes used to remove salt from sea water. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how one of these processes works. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why obtaining potable water from sea water is more costly than from a reservoir. **[1]**

**6.** A student investigates a sample of salty water.

**(a)** Describe how the student could find the mass of dissolved solids in 50.0 cm³ of the sample. **[3]**
**(b)** The residue has a mass of 1.75 g. Calculate the mass of dissolved solids in g/dm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the student could produce a pure sample of water from the salty water. **[3]**

**7.** Waste water from a town is treated at a sewage works.

**(a)** Put these stages in order: aerobic treatment of effluent; sedimentation; screening and grit removal; anaerobic digestion of sludge. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what happens in sedimentation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the treatment of industrial waste water may differ from that of sewage. **[1]**

**8.** Compare the relative ease of obtaining potable water from ground water, waste water and sea water. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Copper ores are becoming scarce. Phytomining is one new way of extracting copper.

**(a)** Describe how phytomining produces a copper compound. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of phytomining compared with traditional mining. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A leachate solution from bioleaching contains copper sulfate.

**(a)** Describe how the leachate is produced. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how scrap iron can be used to obtain copper from the solution, and write the word equation. **[3]**
**(c)** Name another method to obtain copper from the solution. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Crude oil: **finite** [1] because it takes millions of years to form, so it is used faster than it is replaced [1]. Timber: **renewable** [1] because new trees can be grown as fast as they are used (if replanted) [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Any one: synthetic rubber replacing natural rubber; nylon or polyester replacing cotton or wool; plastics replacing wood [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Development that **meets the needs of current generations** [1] **without compromising the ability of future generations** to meet their own needs [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Years = 6.0 × 10⁹ ÷ 2.0 × 10⁷ [1] = 3.0 × 10² = **300 years** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Water that is **safe to drink** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** It contains **dissolved substances** (such as salts) [1]; pure water contains only H₂O [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Choose a suitable **source** with low levels of dissolved salts [1]; **filter** through filter beds to remove insoluble solids [1]; **sterilise** with chlorine, ozone or ultraviolet light [1] to kill microbes [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** **Distillation** [1] and **reverse osmosis** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Distillation: the water is **boiled** [1], the steam is **condensed** and collected, leaving salts behind [1]. (Reverse osmosis: water is forced through a **membrane** [1] that does not let salt through [1].) **[2]**
**(c)** Both need **large amounts of energy** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Measure 50.0 cm³ of the sample into an evaporating basin of known mass [1]; **heat/evaporate** all the water [1]; **weigh** the basin and residue, and subtract the mass of the basin [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Volume = 0.0500 dm³ [1]; 1.75 ÷ 0.0500 = **35 g/dm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **Distillation**: heat the sample in a flask [1]; the steam passes through a **condenser** [1]; collect the liquid water, leaving the salts in the flask [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Screening and grit removal → sedimentation → anaerobic digestion of the sludge **and** aerobic treatment of the effluent [2 marks for the correct order; 1 for one error]. **[2]**
**(b)** The solids **settle** to form **sludge** [1], leaving a liquid called **effluent** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Industrial waste water may need **harmful chemicals** removed as well as organic matter [1]. **[1]**

**8.** Ground water is the **easiest**, because it has low levels of dissolved salts and only needs filtering and sterilising [1]. Waste water is **harder**, because it needs several stages to remove organic matter, microbes and harmful chemicals [1]. Sea water is the **hardest**, because desalination needs large amounts of energy [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** **Plants absorb** metal compounds from the soil [1]; the plants are **harvested and burned** [1]; the **ash** contains the copper compounds [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Advantage: works with **low-grade ores** / avoids digging and moving large amounts of rock [1]. Disadvantage: **slow** (plants take time to grow) / needs land [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** **Bacteria** are used to break down the ore [1] and produce a **leachate** solution containing the metal compounds [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Iron is **more reactive** than copper [1], so it **displaces** copper from the solution [1]. iron + copper sulfate → iron sulfate + copper [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** **Electrolysis** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying potable water is pure.
- Mixing up filtering and sterilising.
- Forgetting that desalination uses a lot of energy.
- Giving the sewage stages in the wrong order.
- Not converting cm³ to dm³.
- Mixing up phytomining (plants) and bioleaching (bacteria).
