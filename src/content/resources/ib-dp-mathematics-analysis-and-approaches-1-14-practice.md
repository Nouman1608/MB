This set covers **syllabus section 1.14** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021): conjugate roots of polynomials with real coefficients, De Moivre's theorem, and powers and roots of complex numbers. Every question is **HL only** and calculator-free, as on HL Paper 1. Give arguments in the range −π < θ ≤ π unless stated.

Study the methods first in the [section 1.14 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-14/) and the [section 1.14 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-14-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** The quadratic equation z² + bz + c = 0, where b, c ∈ ℝ, has a root 2 − 5i. Find b and c. **[3]**

**2.** Find (√3 − i)⁵ in the form a + bi. **[3]**

**3.** Find (cis(π/9))⁻³ in the form a + bi. **[2]**

**4.** Solve z³ = −8i, giving your answers in the form r cis θ. **[3]**

**5.** Write down the number of distinct solutions of z⁷ = 3 + 4i, and the modulus they share. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The equation z³ − 5z² + 17z − 13 = 0 has a root 2 + 3i.

**(a)** Write down a second root, giving a reason. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the third root. **[3]**

**7.** A polynomial p(z) of degree 4 has real coefficients and leading coefficient 1. Two of its roots are 2i and 1 − i. Find p(z) in expanded form. **[4]**

**8.** Use De Moivre's theorem and the binomial theorem to show that sin 3θ = 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ. **[5]**

**9.** Let z = cis θ.

**(a)** Expand (z − z⁻¹)³. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence show that sin³θ = (1/4)(3 sin θ − sin 3θ). **[4]**

**10.** Let ω = cis(2π/3).

**(a)** Show that 1, ω and ω² are the three solutions of z³ = 1, and that 1 + ω + ω² = 0. **[4]**
**(b)** Hence find the value of (1 + ω)(1 + ω²). **[2]**

**11.** Let w = 1 + i.

**(a)** Find w⁴. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence, or otherwise, find all four solutions of z⁴ + 4 = 0 in the form a + bi. **[3]**
**(c)** Hence write z⁴ + 4 as a product of two quadratic factors with real coefficients. **[2]**

**12.** Find the three values of (2 + 2i)^(2/3), giving your answers in the form a + bi. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The coefficients are real, so 2 + 5i is also a root [1]. Sum of roots = 4, product = 4 + 25 = 29 [1]. z² − 4z + 29 = 0, so **b = −4, c = 29** [1]

**2.** √3 − i = 2 cis(−π/6) [1]. (√3 − i)⁵ = 32 cis(−5π/6) [1] = 32(−√3/2 − i/2) = **−16√3 − 16i** [1]

**3.** cis(−3π/9) = cis(−π/3) [1] = **1/2 − (√3/2)i** [1]

**4.** −8i = 8 cis(−π/2 + 2kπ) [1]. z = 2 cis(−π/6 + 2kπ/3), k = 0, 1, 2 [1]: **2 cis(−π/6), 2 cis(π/2), 2 cis(−5π/6)** [1]

**5.** **Seven** solutions [1], each with modulus |3 + 4i|^(1/7) = **5^(1/7)** [1]

**6. (a)** **2 − 3i**, because the coefficients are all real [1]
**(b)** The pair gives the factor z² − 4z + 13 [1]. Comparing constant terms: (z² − 4z + 13)(z − c) with 13c = 13 [1], so the third root is **z = 1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check with the z² coefficient: −4 − c = −5 gives c = 1, which agrees.

**7.** The conjugates −2i and 1 + i are also roots [1]. Factors: (z² + 4) [1] and (z² − 2z + 2) [1]. p(z) = **z⁴ − 2z³ + 6z² − 8z + 8** [1]

**8.** By De Moivre, (cos θ + i sin θ)³ = cos 3θ + i sin 3θ [1]. Write c = cos θ, s = sin θ. By the binomial theorem, (c + is)³ = c³ + 3c²(is) + 3c(is)² + (is)³ [1] = (c³ − 3cs²) + i(3c²s − s³) [1]. Equating imaginary parts: sin 3θ = 3c²s − s³ [1] = 3(1 − s²)s − s³ = 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ [1]

**9. (a)** z³ − 3z² × z⁻¹ + 3z × z⁻² − z⁻³ [1] = **z³ − 3z + 3z⁻¹ − z⁻³** [1]
**(b)** z − z⁻¹ = 2i sin θ and z³ − z⁻³ = 2i sin 3θ [1]. Group (a): (z³ − z⁻³) − 3(z − z⁻¹) [1]. So (2i sin θ)³ = 2i sin 3θ − 6i sin θ, that is −8i sin³θ = 2i sin 3θ − 6i sin θ [1]. Dividing by −8i: sin³θ = (6 sin θ − 2 sin 3θ)/8 = (1/4)(3 sin θ − sin 3θ) [1]
*Examiner insight:* (2i)³ = 8i³ = −8i. Losing that minus sign reverses the final answer.

**10. (a)** 1 = cis(2kπ), so the cube roots are cis(2kπ/3), k = 0, 1, 2 [1], which are 1, ω and ω² [1]. Their sum is the sum of the roots of z³ − 1 = 0, which is minus the z² coefficient [1], and that coefficient is 0, so 1 + ω + ω² = 0 [1]
**(b)** (1 + ω)(1 + ω²) = 1 + ω + ω² + ω³ [1] = 0 + 1 = **1** [1]

**11. (a)** w² = 2i [1], so w⁴ = (2i)² = **−4** [1]
**(b)** z⁴ = −4 = 4 cis(π + 2kπ), so z = √2 cis(π/4 + kπ/2), k = 0, 1, 2, 3 [1]. These are w = 1 + i rotated by multiples of π/2 [1]: **1 + i, −1 + i, −1 − i, 1 − i** [1]
**(c)** Pair conjugates: 1 ± i give z² − 2z + 2 and −1 ± i give z² + 2z + 2 [1]. **z⁴ + 4 = (z² − 2z + 2)(z² + 2z + 2)** [1]

**12.** 2 + 2i = 2√2 cis(π/4 + 2kπ) [1]. Raise to the power 2/3: modulus (2√2)^(2/3) = 2 and argument (2/3)(π/4 + 2kπ) = π/6 + 4kπ/3, k = 0, 1, 2 [1]. Values: 2 cis(π/6), 2 cis(3π/2) = 2 cis(−π/2), 2 cis(17π/6) = 2 cis(5π/6) [1]: **√3 + i, −2i, −√3 + i** [1]
*Examiner insight:* (2√2)^(2/3) = (2^(3/2))^(2/3) = 2. Write the general argument before raising to the power, or two of the three values are lost.

## Where marks are usually lost

- The conjugate root stated without the reason "real coefficients".
- Arguments raised to a power instead of multiplied.
- Fewer than n roots of zⁿ = w.
- Arguments left outside the stated range.
- Quoting multiple-angle identities instead of deriving them from De Moivre's theorem.
- Sign errors in powers of i, such as i³ = −i.
