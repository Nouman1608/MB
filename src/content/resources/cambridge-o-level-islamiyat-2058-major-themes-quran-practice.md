**How to use this set.** In Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058 Paper 1, Question 1 is compulsory: three of
the fifteen passages in the syllabus appendix are printed in Arabic and English, and you write about
any two. Part (a) asks for the main theme(s) of each passage **[4]** and part (b) for their importance
in a Muslim's life today **[4]**. Section A below checks the knowledge behind the passages. Section B
gives five exam-style pairs, so you practise writing about two passages at a time. The English
wording is the syllabus translation; key Arabic words are given in transliteration so you can link
your answer to the original, which the syllabus expects you to know.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** The set passages are grouped under three themes. Name each theme and give **one** set passage
that belongs to it. **[6]**

**2.** Sura 112 says: "Say: He is Allah, the one and only; Allah, the eternal, absolute; He does not
beget, nor is He begotten; And there is none like Him." Explain what the words *ahad* and *al-samad*
tell Muslims about God. **[4]**

**3.** Sura 96.1–5 begins "Read! in the name of your Lord, who created". State when and where these
verses were revealed, and give **two** things they teach about God's relationship with human beings.
**[4]**

**4.** Sura 99 ends: "Then shall anyone who has done an atom's weight of good see it! And anyone who
has done an atom's weight of evil shall see it." Explain what the phrase "an atom's weight" teaches.
**[3]**

**5.** In Sura 6.75–79 the Prophet Ibrahim looks at a star, the moon and the sun. Describe how his
reasoning leads him to reject the worship of created things. **[4]**

**6.** Explain the meaning of *tawhid* and name **two** set passages, other than Sura 112, that teach
it. **[3]**

**7.** Sura 41.37 says: "Adore not the sun and the moon, but adore Allah, who created them." Explain
why the passage calls the night, day, sun and moon "signs". **[2]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Cambridge marks Question 1 by levels of response: part (a) Level 4 = 4,
Level 3 = 3, Level 2 = 2, Level 1 = 1; part (b) Level 2 = 3–4, Level 1 = 1–2. The two passages are
read together. The credit guides below show one way to reach each tariff: for each passage in (a), the
theme identified [1] and explained from the wording [1]; for each passage in (b), one reasoned point
about life today [2]. Other valid points of the same quality earn the same credit.

**1.** **God in himself** [1], for example Sura 2.255, 6.101–103, 41.37, 42.4–5 or 112 [1]. **God's
relationship with the created world** [1], for example Sura 1, 2.21–22, 96.1–5, 99 or 114 [1].
**God's Messengers** [1], for example Sura 2.30–37, 6.75–79, 5.110, 93 or 108 [1].

**2.** *Ahad* means one and unique: God is a single being who cannot be divided and has no partner,
which rejects shirk [2]. *Al-samad* means the eternal, absolute one on whom everything depends while
He depends on nothing; He needs no food, rest or help [1]. Together with "He does not beget, nor is He
begotten", the sura denies that God has a parent, a child or any equal, so nothing in creation can be
compared with Him [1].

**3.** They were the first verses revealed, in 610 [1], to the Prophet (pbuh) in the Cave of Hira,
brought by the angel Jibril [1]. They teach that God is the **Creator** who made humans from a clot
(*alaq*), so people owe their existence to Him [1], and that He is the **teacher** of humankind, who
"taught by the pen" (*qalam*) and "taught man what he did not know", so knowledge is a gift from Him
[1].

**4.** Nothing a person does is too small to be recorded and shown on the Day of Judgement [1]. Every
good deed, however minor, will be rewarded, which gives hope [1], and every wrong, however minor, will
be seen, so Muslims cannot dismiss "small" sins and must take full responsibility for their actions
[1].

**5.** Ibrahim sees a star and says "This is my Lord", but when it sets he says "I do not love things
that set" [1]. The same happens with the moon and then the sun, which he calls "the greatest" [1].
Each one disappears, which shows it is controlled by something else and cannot be God [1]. He
therefore turns "towards Him who created the heavens and the earth" and declares that he will never
give partners to God [1].

**6.** *Tawhid* is belief in the absolute oneness of God, who alone deserves worship [1]. Any two of:
Sura 2.255 ("There is no god but He"); Sura 6.101–103 ("there is no god but He, the Creator of all
things"); Sura 41.37 (worship only the Creator, not the sun or moon); Sura 2.21–22 ("do not set up
rivals to Allah") [1] [1].

**7.** A sign (*aya*) points beyond itself: the regular cycle of night and day and the movement of the
sun and moon show the power and order of their Creator [1]. They are created things, so worshipping
them would be giving the Creator's right to His creation [1].

**8. (a)** **Sura 6.101–103 — God in himself:** God is the Creator of all things and the only Lord
[1]; He has no son or consort, knows everything and is beyond human vision and comprehension, "yet is
acquainted with all things" [1]. **Sura 42.4–5 — God in himself:** everything in the heavens and earth
belongs to God, who is most high and most great [1]; His majesty is so great the heavens almost split,
the angels praise Him, and He is oft-forgiving and most merciful [1].
**(b)** Because God sees and knows all things, Muslims try to act rightly in private as well as in
public, and they reject any belief that God has a son or partner [2]. Knowing that even the angels
pray for forgiveness for people on earth gives Muslims hope; they turn to God in repentance rather
than despair after doing wrong [2].

**9. (a)** **Sura 1 — God's relationship with the created world:** God is the merciful Lord who
cherishes and sustains all the worlds (*Rabb al-alamin*) and is Master of the Day of Judgement [1];
humans respond by worshipping Him alone and asking only Him for help and guidance on the straight path
(*al-sirat al-mustaqim*) [1]. **Sura 2.21–22 — God's relationship with the created world:** God
created people and those before them [1], and provides for them by making the earth and sky and
sending rain that produces food, so they must worship Him and set up no rivals to Him [1].
**(b)** Muslims recite Sura 1 in every unit (*rak'a*) of prayer, so they renew each day their
commitment to worship God alone and ask for guidance in their decisions [2]. Seeing food and rain as
gifts from God leads Muslims to thank Him, to avoid waste and to share what they have with people in
need [2].

**10. (a)** **Sura 2.255 — God in himself:** God is the living, self-subsisting one (*al-Hayy
al-Qayyum*) who never sleeps or tires [1]; He owns all things, knows everything, and no one can
intercede with Him except by His permission [1]. **Sura 114 — God's relationship with the created
world:** God is Lord, King and God of all humankind [1], and people seek refuge in Him from the
whisperer (*al-waswas al-khannas*) who puts evil suggestions into hearts, whether from jinn or humans
[1].
**(b)** Many Muslims recite Sura 2.255 before sleeping or when afraid, trusting that God guards and
preserves them at all times [2]. Reciting Sura 114 when tempted to lie, cheat or follow harmful
friends reminds a Muslim to seek God's protection and resist evil suggestions [2].

**11. (a)** **Sura 93 — God's Messengers:** God reassures the Prophet (pbuh) that He has not forsaken
him, and promises that the Hereafter will be better than the present [1]; He reminds him that he was
an orphan, wandering and in need, and was given shelter, guidance and independence, so he must not be
harsh to orphans or the poor [1]. **Sura 108 — God's Messengers:** God has given the Prophet (pbuh)
abundance (*al-kawthar*) [1], so he should pray and sacrifice in thanks, and his enemy, not he, is
the one "cut off" (*abtar*) [1].
**(b)** Muslims facing hardship take comfort that God has not abandoned them, and they look after
orphans and the poor, for example through sponsorship and charity [2]. When mocked for their faith,
Muslims respond with prayer and sacrifice at Eid al-Adha rather than anger, trusting that God
honours His servants [2].

**12. (a)** **Sura 2.30–37 — God's Messengers:** God made Adam His vicegerent (*khalifa*) on earth and
taught him the names of all things, raising him above the angels in knowledge [1]; Iblis refused to
bow out of pride, Satan made Adam and his wife slip, and Adam repented and God turned to him in mercy
[1]. **Sura 5.110 — God's Messengers:** God supported Jesus with the holy spirit and taught him the
Book, Wisdom, the Law and the Gospel [1]; his miracles, such as healing the blind and lepers and
bringing forth the dead, happened only "by my leave", so he was a human messenger, not divine [1].
**(b)** As vicegerents, Muslims care for the earth and use knowledge responsibly, and when they sin
they repent at once as Adam did instead of persisting out of pride like Iblis [2]. Muslims respect
Jesus as a great prophet while believing he was human, which shapes respectful dialogue with
Christians about their shared and different beliefs [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a theme as a label ("tawhid", "judgement") without explaining it from the passage's wording.
- Writing a long paraphrase of the passage instead of identifying what it teaches.
- Repeating the theme in part (b) instead of giving a practical example from modern life.
- Writing about only one passage, or about all three in the exam.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn the Arabic of every passage?**
The syllabus says passages are set in both Arabic and English and that you should show knowledge of
the original, so learn the key Arabic words of each passage and use them in your answers.

**Can a passage have more than one theme?**
Yes. Sura 1, for example, speaks about God's mercy, His lordship over the worlds and judgement; credit
is given for any theme you can support from the wording.
