## Questions

**1.** Describe what happens when chlorophyll absorbs light, and what the electrons are then used for. **[3]**

**2.** Explain how ATP is made in the light-dependent reaction by the passage of protons across chloroplast membranes. **[5]**

**3.** A suspension of algae was given water containing a heavy isotope of oxygen, ¹⁸O, and ordinary carbon dioxide, and was kept in the light. The oxygen gas released contained ¹⁸O. A second suspension given ordinary water and carbon dioxide containing ¹⁸O released oxygen with no ¹⁸O.

**(a)** State the three products of photolysis. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what the results show. **[2]**
**(c)** 36 µmol of water is split. Calculate the amount of oxygen released and the amount of electrons released. **[2]**

**4.** Algae were illuminated until the amounts of two compounds were steady. The light was then switched off. The table shows relative amounts.

| Time after light off (min) | 0 | 1 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| GP | 100 | 160 | 210 |
| RuBP | 100 | 45 | 20 |

**(a)** Describe the changes, including the percentage change in GP between 0 and 3 minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in GP. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the change in RuBP. **[2]**

**5.** Nine molecules of carbon dioxide are fixed in the Calvin cycle.

**(a)** How many molecules of GP are formed? **[1]**
**(b)** How many molecules of ATP are used to reduce the GP? **[1]**
**(c)** How many molecules of triose phosphate are needed to regenerate RuBP? Show your working. **[2]**
**(d)** How many molecules of triose phosphate are available to leave the cycle? **[1]**

**6.** Describe how a plant uses triose phosphate to make **(a)** starch **[2]** and **(b)** a protein **[2]**.

**7.** Rates of photosynthesis (arbitrary units) were recorded at two carbon dioxide concentrations over a range of light intensities, with temperature held constant.

| Light intensity (arbitrary units) | 0 | 15 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate at 0.04% CO₂ | 0 | 2.5 | 5.0 | 7.5 | 8.0 | 8.0 |
| Rate at 0.08% CO₂ | 0 | 2.5 | 5.0 | 7.5 | 10.0 | 11.5 |

**(a)** Identify the limiting factor at light intensity 15 and justify your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the limiting factor at 0.04% CO₂ and light intensity 60, and justify your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in rate at light intensity 75 when CO₂ is raised from 0.04% to 0.08%. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest what limits the rate at 0.08% CO₂ and light intensity 75, and how you would test it. **[2]**

**8.** The rate of photosynthesis of a leaf (arbitrary units) at constant light intensity and CO₂ was: 2.5 at 15 °C, 5.4 at 25 °C, 9.6 at 35 °C, 4.3 at 45 °C and 0.8 at 55 °C.

**(a)** State how many times greater the rate is at 35 °C than at 15 °C. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the rate increases from 15 °C to 35 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage decrease in rate from 35 °C to 45 °C. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain the decrease. **[2]**

**9.** Plan an investigation into how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis in a water plant. **[8]**

**10.** In a Required practical 7 investigation, a student collected the oxygen from a pondweed in a capillary tube and measured the length of the gas column after 5 minutes, with the lamp at different distances.

| Distance (cm) | Length 1 (mm) | Length 2 (mm) | Length 3 (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 38 | 41 | 35 |
| 20 | 21 | 19 | 23 |
| 40 | 7 | 6 | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of gas collection at 40 cm in mm per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** Light intensity is proportional to 1 ÷ distance². Calculate the intensity at 20 cm relative to that at 10 cm. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times greater the mean length at 10 cm is than at 20 cm. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(d)** Compare your answers to (b) and (c) and suggest an explanation. **[2]**
**(e)** Room lighting was on during the experiment. Explain why this is a problem and how to avoid it. **[2]**

**11.** A grower compared tomato yields (kg per 100 plants) in four greenhouses. A: normal air and daylight, 52. B: extra CO₂, 64. C: extra lamps, 71. D: extra CO₂ and extra lamps, 98.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield in D compared with A. **[2]**
**(b)** The gains in B and C added together are less than the gain in D. Use the principle of limiting factors to explain this. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why yield may not rise further if CO₂ and light are increased even more. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Chlorophyll absorbs light energy [1]. This excites electrons, which leave the chlorophyll [1]. They pass along the electron transfer chain and the energy is used to make ATP and reduced NADP [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the electrons are excited by light energy and then say what their energy is used for. Answers that stop at "chlorophyll absorbs light" gain little.

**2.** Excited electrons pass along the electron transfer chain in the chloroplast membrane [1]. Energy is released at each transfer [1]. It is used to pump protons across the membrane [1], creating a proton gradient. The protons return through a channel in ATP synthase [1], and this enzyme catalyses ADP + inorganic phosphate → ATP [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the enzyme and say that protons pass through it. "Protons make ATP" does not show the role of the enzyme.

**3. (a)** Protons [1], electrons [1], oxygen [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The ¹⁸O appears in oxygen only when the water was labelled [1], so the oxygen released comes from water, not from carbon dioxide [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Oxygen = 36 ÷ 2 = **18 µmol** [1]; electrons = 18 × 4 = **72 µmol** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) refer to both samples. A conclusion quoted without the comparison is weaker than one that uses both results.

**4. (a)** GP rises from 100 to 210, an increase of 110 ÷ 100 × 100 = **110%** [1]; RuBP falls from 100 to 20 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** With no light, no ATP and reduced NADP are made [1]. GP is not reduced to triose phosphate, but is still made from CO₂ and RuBP, so GP builds up [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Less triose phosphate is made, so RuBP is not regenerated [1]. RuBP is still used up in reacting with CO₂ [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table. Link the change in each compound to the missing ATP and reduced NADP, not just to "no light".

**5. (a)** 9 × 2 = **18 GP** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** One ATP per GP, so **18 ATP** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Nine RuBP have 9 × 5 = 45 carbons [1]; 45 ÷ 3 = **15 triose phosphate** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 18 − 15 = **3 triose phosphate** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the carbon count so your reasoning can be followed.

**6. (a)** Two triose phosphate molecules join to make a hexose such as glucose [1], and hexose molecules are joined by condensation reactions into starch [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Triose phosphate is converted into amino acids [1], using nitrogen from nitrate ions absorbed by roots, and amino acids are joined by peptide bonds [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For protein, nitrogen must appear. Triose phosphate contains only carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and phosphate.

**7. (a)** Light [1]; both curves have the same rate, which rises with light intensity [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Carbon dioxide [1]; raising CO₂ to 0.08% increases the rate from 8.0 to 10.0 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (11.5 − 8.0) ÷ 8.0 [1] × 100 = **43.8%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Temperature [1]; repeat at a higher temperature and see whether the rate rises [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Justify a limiting factor with data from the table. Naming the factor without evidence rarely gains full credit.

**8. (a)** 9.6 ÷ 2.5 = 3.84, so **3.8 times** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** A rise in temperature increases kinetic energy [1], so there are more frequent collisions between enzymes (such as rubisco) and substrates, forming more enzyme-substrate complexes [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (9.6 − 4.3) ÷ 9.6 × 100 = **55.2%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Enzymes begin to denature as hydrogen bonds break [1], changing the shape of the active site, so substrates no longer fit [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide the change by the value you started from, not the one you ended on. For (d), "denature" must be explained in terms of active-site shape.

**9.** Any eight from: place pondweed in a boiling tube of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution as a source of CO₂ [1]; keep the same length of pondweed each time [1]; set a lamp a known distance from the tube, measured with a ruler [1]; use at least five distances [1]; stand the tube in a water bath or use a heat shield to keep temperature constant [1]; allow a few minutes for the plant to adjust [1]; count bubbles per minute, or collect and measure the gas [1]; repeat at least three times at each distance and calculate a mean [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* State what you alter, what you record and what you keep fixed. A plan that leaves out temperature control usually loses credit.

**10. (a)** Mean length = (7 + 6 + 8) ÷ 3 = 7.0 mm; 7.0 ÷ 5 = **1.4 mm per minute** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** (10 ÷ 20)² = **0.25** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Means are 38.0 mm at 10 cm and 21.0 mm at 20 cm; 38.0 ÷ 21.0 = **1.8 times** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Intensity at 10 cm is four times that at 20 cm, but the mean length is only 1.8 times greater [1]. Another factor, such as CO₂ concentration or temperature, limits the rate at high light intensity [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Room light adds to the lamp light, so the light intensity at the plant is not the value calculated from the distance [1]. Darken the room, or shield the apparatus, so the lamp is the only light source [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) quote both ratios. In (e) link the stray light to the independent variable.

**11. (a)** 98 − 52 = 46 [1]; 46 ÷ 52 × 100 = **88.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The gain from B (12) plus C (19) is 31, less than the gain of 46 in D [1]. In B, extra CO₂ helps but light is still limiting [1]. In C, extra light helps but CO₂ soon becomes limiting [1]. In D, both are raised, so neither limits and the rate is far higher [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Temperature becomes limiting [1]; the amount of enzyme such as rubisco, or chlorophyll, is limiting [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures from the table and name which factor is limiting in each greenhouse.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the electrons in the chain make ATP, without protons and ATP synthase.
- Giving the source of oxygen as carbon dioxide.
- Swapping GP and RuBP, or the number of carbons.
- Dividing percentage change by the final value.
- Stating a limiting factor with no data.
- Saying enzymes are "killed" rather than denatured.
