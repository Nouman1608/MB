## The three specification points

1. Explain the production process for the provision of services.
2. Explain what is meant by customer service in the context of the provision of services.
3. Evaluate the importance of high-quality customer service to a business and its stakeholders.

## Core recall

### How services differ from manufacturing

| Feature | Meaning for operations |
|---|---|
| Customer involvement | Customer is part of the process and affects the result |
| Simultaneous production and consumption | No stock; quality cannot be checked beforehand |
| Perishability | Unused capacity is lost; too little capacity causes queues |
| Variability | Quality differs; standard procedures and training needed |
| Front and back office | Front: customers meet staff. Back: unseen processing |

### Stages of a service process

Enquiry or booking, arrival or access, delivery, payment, follow-up. Map and time each stage to find delays and errors.

### Sizing capacity

- Calls (or customers) per agent = productive minutes ÷ minutes per customer.
- Agents needed = demand ÷ calls per agent.
- Capacity shortfall = demand − capacity.
- Compare the cost of an extra agent with the cost of lost customers.
- Smoothing demand: part-time staff, flexible rotas, call-back, self-service, booking systems.

### Customer service

Support given before, during and after a purchase.

| Stage | Examples |
|---|---|
| Before | Information, advice, easy booking |
| During | Friendly, competent staff; speed; accuracy |
| After | Follow-up, complaints, refunds, guarantees |

Measures: waiting time, first-contact resolution, complaints, repeat bookings, satisfaction scores.

### Importance of high-quality customer service

- Retains customers, who are cheaper to keep than to win.
- Supports reputation and word of mouth.
- Allows a premium price.
- Differentiates similar services.
- Reduces costs of complaints and refunds.

### Retention value

- Extra retained customers = customers × rise in renewal rate.
- Extra contribution = extra customers × contribution each.
- Net gain = extra contribution − cost of the improvement.
- Average customer life = 1 ÷ (1 − retention rate).
- Lifetime contribution = life × annual contribution.

### Stakeholders and limits

- Customers: better experience. Owners: higher retention and profit but extra cost. Employees: satisfaction if supported; pressure if targets are unrealistic. Suppliers: stable volumes.
- Limits: costs money; may not pay if customers want the lowest price (a low-cost airline limits service on purpose); results depend on the size of the effect on retention.

## Quick worked reminder

Support centre (invented): 600 calls a day; 6 minutes each; 400 productive minutes per agent. Each agent handles 66.7 calls, so 9 agents are needed (£1,080 at £120 a day, £1.80 a call). With 8 agents, capacity is 533 and about 67 calls are missed.

Gym chain: 4,000 members; contribution £37.50 each. Renewal 70% to 78% keeps 320 more members: +£12,000 less £9,000 cost = +£3,000. Life 3.3 years to 4.5 years; lifetime contribution £125 to £170. If renewal reached only 74%, the plan would lose £3,000.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Customer service | Support given to customers before, during and after a purchase |
| Front office | The part of a service customers see |
| Back office | The unseen processing and support work |
| Capacity | The maximum a business can provide in a period |
| Retention rate | The share of customers who stay from one period to the next |
| First-contact resolution | The share of problems solved at the first contact |
| Self-service | Letting customers carry out part of the service themselves |

## Evaluation prompts

- Does the cost of better service pay back through retention, and how sensitive is the result?
- Is service a main reason customers choose this business, or is price?
- Can capacity be matched to demand without large idle costs?
- How will quality be measured, and by whom?
- How are employees affected by service targets?
- Finish with a judgement for the service and customers in the case.

## Exam traps

- Services cannot be stored; link capacity to demand.
- Customer service includes before and after the sale.
- Compare the cost of better service with the benefit.
- Not every service business should aim for the highest service level.
- Include effects on employees.

## Command words

- **Explain:** describe the stages of the service process and say how they differ from manufacturing.
- **Calculate:** work out capacity per agent, agents needed and the retention gain step by step.
- **Evaluate:** compare the cost of better service with the benefit, and consider employees and customers.
- **Define:** customer service covers before, during and after the purchase.

## Self-test

1. Give two ways in which service production differs from manufacturing.
2. Distinguish front office from back office.
3. 300 customers a day, 4 minutes each, 240 productive minutes per agent. Find the agents needed.
4. Give two measures of customer service quality.
5. Give one reason why high-quality service improves profit.
6. Customers 2,000; retention rises by 5 percentage points. How many extra are retained?
7. Contribution £40 each; find the extra contribution in question 6.
8. Retention rate is 80%. Find the average customer life.
9. Give one limit of high-quality customer service.
10. Why might a low-cost airline deliberately limit service?
11. Give one way to smooth demand in a service.
12. Name the stages of a service process.

### Answers

1. Any two: customer is part of the process; produced and consumed together; cannot be stored; more variable.
2. Front office is where customers meet staff; back office is the unseen processing and support.
3. 240 ÷ 4 = 60 customers per agent; 300 ÷ 60 = **5 agents**.
4. Any two: waiting time; first-contact resolution; complaints; repeat bookings; satisfaction scores.
5. Any one: retains customers; allows a premium price; reduces complaints and refunds.
6. 2,000 × 0.05 = **100**.
7. 100 × 40 = **£4,000**.
8. 1 ÷ (1 − 0.80) = 1 ÷ 0.20 = **5 years**.
9. Any one: costs money; may not pay if customers want the lowest price; effect on retention uncertain.
10. Customers choose it for low fares, so cutting service keeps costs and fares down.
11. Any one: part-time staff; flexible rotas; booking systems; call-back; self-service.
12. Enquiry or booking, arrival, delivery, payment, follow-up.
