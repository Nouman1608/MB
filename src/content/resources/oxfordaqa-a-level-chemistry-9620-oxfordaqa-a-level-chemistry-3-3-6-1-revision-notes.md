## Specification in brief

- "The reactions of functional groups listed in the specification."
- Identify functional groups using those reactions.
- **Required practical 5:** tests for **alcohols, aldehydes, alkenes and carboxylic acids**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Functional group | Atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic reactions |
| Positive result | Observation showing the group is present |
| Negative result | Observation showing the group is absent (still an observation, such as "stays blue") |
| Effervescence | Fizzing as a gas is released |
| Silver mirror | Layer of silver metal deposited on glass when Tollens' reagent is reduced |
| Control | Same test run with water instead of the sample, for comparison |
| Interference | Another group giving the same positive result |

## Recall: the tests

| Group | Reagent and method | Positive | Negative | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C=C (alkene) | bromine water, shake | orange → colourless | stays orange | Br₂ adds across C=C |
| Primary or secondary alcohol | acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, warm (water bath) | orange → green | stays orange | alcohol oxidised; Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺ |
| Aldehyde | Fehling's solution, warm | blue → red precipitate | stays blue | aldehyde oxidised; Cu²⁺ → Cu₂O |
| Aldehyde | Tollens' reagent, warm | silver mirror | no silver mirror | aldehyde oxidised; Ag⁺ → Ag |
| Carboxylic acid | Na₂CO₃ (or NaHCO₃) solution | effervescence; gas turns limewater milky | no fizzing | CO₂ released |

**Carbonate equations:**

- 2RCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOONa + CO₂ + H₂O
- RCOOH + NaHCO₃ → RCOONa + CO₂ + H₂O

## Recall: which groups give each result

| Test | Positive for | Negative for |
|---|---|---|
| Bromine water | alkenes | alcohols, ketones, acids without C=C, alkanes* |
| Acidified dichromate(VI) | primary alcohols, secondary alcohols, aldehydes | tertiary alcohols, ketones, carboxylic acids |
| Fehling's / Tollens' | aldehydes | ketones, alcohols, carboxylic acids |
| Sodium carbonate | carboxylic acids | alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, alkenes |

*Aldehydes are tested for first, so the bromine water result is interpreted only for compounds already shown not to be aldehydes.

**Negative to all four:** ketone, tertiary alcohol, alkane or halogenoalkane. Use **infrared** to separate them.

## Recall: test order

1. Sodium carbonate → carboxylic acid.
2. Fehling's or Tollens' → aldehyde.
3. Bromine water → alkene.
4. Acidified dichromate(VI) → primary or secondary alcohol (now that aldehydes are excluded).

### Worked reminder: three C₄ compounds

Three liquids: butanoic acid, but-3-en-1-ol (CH₂=CHCH₂CH₂OH) and 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

- Sodium carbonate: only **butanoic acid** fizzes.
- Bromine water on the other two: **but-3-en-1-ol** decolourises it (C=C).
- 2-Methylpropan-2-ol gives no positive result at all: it is tertiary, so even dichromate stays orange. Its identity is confirmed by an O–H absorption in its infrared spectrum.

## Recall: technique (Required practical 5)

- Small volumes; hot **water bath**, no naked flame.
- Note colours **before and after**.
- Run a **control**.
- Test the **pure** compound: leftover acid gives false fizzing.

## Exam traps

- **Colourless**, not clear.
- "No reaction" is not an observation.
- Dichromate also turns green with **aldehydes**.
- **Tertiary alcohols** do not turn dichromate green.
- Fehling's → **red precipitate**; Tollens' → **silver mirror**.
- Alcohols do **not** fizz with carbonates.
- A molecule can contain **two** groups: check every test.
- Name the gas test: **limewater turns milky**.
- A compound negative to every test is not "unreactive": it may be a **ketone or tertiary alcohol**, so use infrared evidence next.
- Leftover acid catalyst from a preparation also fizzes with carbonate, so test a **purified** sample.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagent and positive result for the test for an alkene.
2. Which two kinds of compound turn acidified dichromate(VI) green, apart from primary alcohols?
3. Why should aldehydes be tested for before using acidified dichromate(VI)?
4. Give the observation when a ketone is warmed with Fehling's solution.
5. Write an equation for ethanoic acid reacting with sodium hydrogencarbonate.
6. Describe how to show that the gas from a carbonate test is carbon dioxide.
7. Name two types of compound that give a negative result in all four tests.
8. Why is a water bath used rather than a Bunsen burner?
9. Predict the result of prop-2-en-1-ol with bromine water and with acidified dichromate(VI).
10. A compound fizzes with sodium carbonate and decolourises bromine water. Which two groups are present?
11. How could you distinguish propanal from propanone using one test?
12. Why should a control be run alongside each test?

### Answers

1. **Bromine water**, shake: **orange to colourless**.
2. **Secondary alcohols** and **aldehydes**.
3. Aldehydes are also **oxidised by dichromate**, so they would be mistaken for alcohols.
4. The solution **stays blue**.
5. **CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + CO₂ + H₂O**
6. Bubble it through **limewater**, which turns **milky**.
7. Any two: **ketones, tertiary alcohols, alkanes, halogenoalkanes**.
8. Organic liquids are **flammable**.
9. Bromine water: **orange to colourless** (C=C); dichromate: **orange to green** (primary alcohol).
10. **Carboxylic acid** (–COOH) and **alkene** (C=C).
11. Warm with **Tollens' reagent** (silver mirror with propanal only) or **Fehling's solution** (red precipitate with propanal only).
12. So that any colour change can be **compared fairly** with a sample known not to react.
