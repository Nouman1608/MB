## Questions

**1.** (a) Define gross primary production. **[1]**
(b) Define net primary production and state the equation that links it to gross primary production. **[2]**

**2.** In a lake, the standing biomass of phytoplankton is 3.0 g m⁻² and of zooplankton is 11 g m⁻². The annual production is 450 g m⁻² for phytoplankton and 54 g m⁻² for zooplankton.

**(a)** State the shape of the pyramid of standing biomass. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how a smaller stock of phytoplankton can support the larger stock of zooplankton. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the zooplankton production as a percentage of phytoplankton production. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why a pyramid of energy for this lake cannot be inverted over a year. **[1]**

**3.** A meadow stores 30 000 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ in grass. Grasshoppers store 2 400 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ and shrews that eat them store 288 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of transfer from grass to grasshoppers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of transfer from grasshoppers to shrews. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the overall efficiency from grass to shrews. **[1]**
**(d)** Name one way in which energy is lost between the grasshoppers and the shrews. **[1]**

**4.** A wetland has a GPP of 14 200 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ and an NPP of 8 236 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹. Calculate the energy lost in plant respiration and express it as a percentage of GPP. **[3]**

**5.** A sheep ingests food containing 18 400 kJ in a day. Its faeces contain 5 520 kJ, its urine 920 kJ and its net production is 1 840 kJ.

**(a)** Calculate the energy lost in respiration. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate net production as a percentage of intake. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of intake lost in respiration. **[1]**

**6.** Calves in a feedlot ingest 48 000 kJ each per day. Faeces hold 9 600 kJ, urine 2 400 kJ and net production is 9 600 kJ. The farmer changes the housing so that respiration falls by 20%, with intake, faeces and urine unchanged.

**(a)** Calculate the original respiratory loss. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the new net production and give it as a percentage of intake. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two changes to housing that could reduce respiratory loss. **[2]**

**7.** The wasp *Encarsia formosa* is released into a glasshouse to reduce whitefly on tomato plants.

**(a)** Explain how this increases productivity of the tomato crop. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one risk of using an introduced biological agent. **[2]**

**8.** A grower compares two controls of whitefly in glasshouse tomatoes. Without control, the yield is 52 t ha⁻¹. Tomatoes sell at £340 per tonne.

| Control | Yield (t ha⁻¹) | Cost (£ ha⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical insecticide | 71 | 1 950 |
| Biological agent | 68 | 2 600 |

**(a)** Calculate the extra revenue from each control compared with no control. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain after subtracting cost for each control. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which control the grower should use. Refer to economic and environmental points. **[3]**

**9.** A crop stores 18 000 kJ in its edible tissue. Cattle fed on this crop store 6% of that energy in their bodies. People eating the beef retain 10% of the energy in the beef.

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in the cattle. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy in people from eating the beef. **[1]**
**(c)** Express your answer to (b) as a percentage of the energy in the crop. **[1]**
**(d)** Use your answer to explain why shorter food chains can feed more people per hectare. **[1]**

**10.** Pigs ingest 30 000 kJ per day. Faeces hold 4 500 kJ and urine 1 500 kJ. Respiratory loss is 16 800 kJ for pigs kept in a heated building and 19 500 kJ for pigs kept outdoors.

**(a)** Calculate net production for each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate net production as a percentage of intake for each group. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate the use of heated buildings with restricted movement, using the data and economic, environmental and ethical points. **[4]**

**11.** A wheat farmer uses an integrated system: crop rotation, a variety resistant to a fungal disease, parasitic wasps against aphids, and an insecticide spray only when aphid numbers pass a set threshold. Protected fields yield 6.8 t ha⁻¹ and unprotected fields 5.1 t ha⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage yield loss in the unprotected fields compared with the protected fields. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how two parts of this system increase the efficiency of energy transfer to the crop. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why the farmer sprays only above a threshold instead of regularly. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The chemical energy store in plant biomass in a given area or volume, in a given time. [1]
**(b)** The chemical energy store in plant biomass after respiratory loss (R) has been taken into account. [1] NPP = GPP - R. [1]
*Examiner insight:* include "in a given area (or volume), in a given time"; it turns a vague statement about energy into a definition of a rate.

**2. (a)** Inverted: zooplankton (11 g m⁻²) are greater than phytoplankton (3.0 g m⁻²). [1]
**(b)** Phytoplankton are replaced rapidly as they are eaten. [1] Production is 450 g m⁻² per year, far more than the 3.0 g m⁻² present at one time, so the stock supports a large flow of energy. [1]
**(c)** 54 ÷ 450 × 100 = **12.0%**. [1]
**(d)** Each level gets its energy from the level below and some is lost at each transfer, so there is less energy at each level. [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b), compare a stock (g m⁻²) with a rate (g m⁻² per year), not just "they grow fast".

**3. (a)** 2 400 ÷ 30 000 [1] × 100 = **8.0%**. [1]
**(b)** 288 ÷ 2 400 × 100 = **12.0%**. [1]
**(c)** 288 ÷ 30 000 × 100 = **0.96%**. [1]
**(d)** Any one of: some parts of a grasshopper not eaten; energy lost in the shrews' faeces; energy lost in urine; energy released in respiration (as heat). [1]
*Examiner insight:* show which value is divided by which. A "loss" must name a route, not say energy "disappears".

**4.** R = GPP - NPP [1] = 14 200 - 8 236 = **5 964 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹**. [1] 5 964 ÷ 14 200 × 100 = **42.0%** of GPP. [1]
*Examiner insight:* rearrange NPP = GPP - R first; adding the two values shows a misunderstanding of NPP.

**5. (a)** R = I - F - U - N [1] = 18 400 - 5 520 - 920 - 1 840 = **10 120 kJ**. [1]
**(b)** 1 840 ÷ 18 400 × 100 = **10.0%**. [1]
**(c)** 10 120 ÷ 18 400 × 100 = **55.0%**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* give units on energy values, and check that F, U, R and N add to I.

**6. (a)** R = 48 000 - (9 600 + 2 400 + 9 600) [1] = **26 400 kJ**. [1]
**(b)** New R = 26 400 × 0.80 = 21 120 kJ, so N = 48 000 - (9 600 + 2 400 + 21 120) = **14 880 kJ**. [1] 14 880 ÷ 48 000 × 100 = **31.0%**. [1]
**(c)** Any two of: restrict movement of the calves [1]; keep the building warm so less energy is used to maintain body temperature [1]; control feeding to reduce activity.
*Examiner insight:* link each housing change to less respiration; "keep them inside" gains more when it says why R falls.

**7. (a)** The wasps parasitise (and kill) whitefly, so there are fewer whitefly feeding on the tomato plants. [1] More of the energy fixed by the tomato plants stays in the tomato biomass, so the yield rises. [1]
**(b)** It may feed on or compete with non-target species. [1] It may become a pest itself by spreading beyond the glasshouse. [1]
*Examiner insight:* for a risk, state the harm and why it happens, not just its name.

**8. (a)** Chemical: (71 - 52) × 340 = **£6 460**. [1] Biological: (68 - 52) × 340 = **£5 440**. [1]
**(b)** Chemical: 6 460 - 1 950 = **£4 510**. [1] Biological: 5 440 - 2 600 = **£2 840**. [1]
**(c)** The chemical insecticide gives the higher yield and the greater gain (£4 510 compared with £2 840 per hectare). [1] The chemical may harm non-target insects, leave residues and lead to resistance in whitefly. [1] The biological agent costs more and yields slightly less, but avoids residues and resistance. A judgement is needed, for example chemical for short-term profit, biological for long-term control. [1]
*Examiner insight:* an evaluation needs a decision supported by figures and at least one environmental point, not just a list of facts.

**9. (a)** 18 000 × 0.06 = **1 080 kJ**. [1]
**(b)** 1 080 × 0.10 = **108 kJ**. [1]
**(c)** 108 ÷ 18 000 × 100 = **0.6%**. [1]
**(d)** Each extra trophic level loses most of the energy, so people eating the crop directly receive far more of it than people eating the beef. [1]
*Examiner insight:* multiply successive efficiencies. Adding 6% and 10% is a common wrong step.

**10. (a)** Heated: 30 000 - (4 500 + 1 500 + 16 800) = **7 200 kJ**. [1] Outdoor: 30 000 - (4 500 + 1 500 + 19 500) = **4 500 kJ**. [1]
**(b)** Heated: 7 200 ÷ 30 000 × 100 = **24.0%**. [1] Outdoor: 4 500 ÷ 30 000 × 100 = **15.0%**. [1]
**(c)** Economic: respiratory loss is 2 700 kJ lower indoors, so more feed becomes meat, though heating costs money. [1] Environmental: heating uses energy and concentrated pigs produce concentrated waste. [1] Ethical: restricted movement limits natural behaviour. [1] Judgement: efficiency is better indoors, but the decision depends on how costs and welfare are weighed. [1]
*Examiner insight:* use the figures from (a) and (b) in the evaluation. An answer on ethics alone does not evaluate the data.

**11. (a)** (6.8 - 5.1) ÷ 6.8 × 100 = **25.0%**. [1]
**(b)** Any two of these. Crop rotation breaks the life cycles of pests and disease, so fewer organisms feed on the crop [1] and more energy stays in the crop [1]. The resistant variety stops the fungus damaging leaves [1], so more energy goes into growth and yield [1]. Wasps kill aphids, so less sap is lost and more energy stays in the crop. Four marks maximum.
**(c)** Spraying only when needed reduces cost and the harm to non-target species. [1] It lowers the chance of insecticide resistance evolving in the aphids. [1]
*Examiner insight:* "explain" needs a cause and a consequence. Name how the part reduces energy loss, then say what happens to the yield.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining GPP or NPP without "in a given area or volume, in a given time".
- Putting the higher level under the fraction, or adding efficiencies instead of multiplying them.
- Leaving urine out of N = I - (F + U + R), or forgetting that all four terms are energy values.
- Missing units on energy values or a percentage sign.
- Giving a farming practice but not linking it to a reduced loss of energy.
- Evaluating with a single viewpoint when economic, environmental and ethical points are all asked for.
- Listing facts in an evaluation with no justified judgement.
