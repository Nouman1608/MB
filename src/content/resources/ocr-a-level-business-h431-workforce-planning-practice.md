## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by workforce planning. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of job sharing. **[2]**

**3.** Hallam Care expects to provide 312,000 hours of care next year. Each full-time carer provides 1,560 hours of care a year. Calculate the number of full-time carers needed. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** disadvantage of zero hours contracts for a business. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following describes compressed hours?

A: Working fewer total hours for the same pay.

B: Working full-time hours over fewer days.

C: Working a set number of hours across the year.

D: Taking time off instead of overtime pay.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Hallam Care employs 185 carers. It expects 12% to leave next year. It needs 200 full-time carers. External recruitment costs £2,200 for each carer.

**(a)** Calculate the number of carers expected to leave (to the nearest whole number) and the number remaining. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the shortfall and the cost of recruiting externally to fill it. **[3]**

**(c)** Evaluate the importance of workforce planning to Hallam Care and its stakeholders. **[8]**

**7.** Care demand at Hallam Care is higher in winter. It needs 8,000 extra hours of care over the winter months. The standard wage is £13 an hour. Overtime is paid at 1.5 times the standard rate.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of covering the extra hours by overtime and the extra cost compared with covering them at the standard rate. **[3]**

**(b)** Recommend a flexible working practice for Hallam Care to cover the winter demand. Justify your answer. **[9]**

**8.** Explain **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of home or remote working for a firm of accountants. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Workforce planning is forecasting the number and skills of staff a business will need in future [1], comparing this with the staff it expects to have and deciding how to close any gap [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Two people share one job, so the business has two sets of skills and experience [1], and cover is easier when one is absent or on holiday [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 312,000 ÷ 1,560 [1] = **200 carers** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Staff on zero hours contracts have no guaranteed income and may leave for more secure jobs [1], so the business suffers high labour turnover and repeated recruitment and training costs [1]. Accept: lower loyalty and commitment. **[2]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 12% × 185 = 22.2, so **22** carers leave [1]. Remaining = 185 − 22 = **163** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Shortfall = 200 − 163 = **37** [1]. Cost = 37 × £2,200 [1] = **£81,400** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Importance: care demand is fixed by the number of residents, so a shortfall of 37 carers (nearly 19% of the need) would cause poor care, missed visits and possibly breaches of care standards [1]. A plan gives time to recruit and train before staff leave, so quality is not damaged [1]. It lets the home budget for recruitment of £81,400 [1] and allows it to consider retention measures to cut the number of leavers.
Stakeholders: residents and families benefit from consistent care [1]; existing staff avoid overwork and stress from understaffing [1]; owners control agency costs, which are higher than the costs of direct employees.
Limits: the plan depends on forecasts of demand and turnover, which may be wrong [1]. In a tight labour market it may be difficult to recruit 37 carers whatever the plan [1].
Judgement: workforce planning is critical for Hallam because the work requires a fixed number of staff at all times, so it should review the plan every few months and put resources into retention [1]. **[8]**

**7. (a)** Overtime rate = £13 × 1.5 = £19.50 [1]. Cost = 8,000 × £19.50 = **£156,000** [1]. Standard cost = 8,000 × £13 = £104,000, so the extra cost of overtime = **£52,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** A strong answer could recommend annualised hours or part-time staff. Annualised hours: staff agree total hours for the year, with more hours in winter [1], so the extra 8,000 hours are paid at the standard rate and the £52,000 overtime premium is avoided [1]. Staff know their total pay in advance, which gives more security than zero hours [1]. Drawbacks: winter weeks are long, which causes tiredness and could affect the quality of care [1]; carers with childcare may not be able to work long winter weeks [1]; the system is harder to plan and record [1].
Alternatives: part-time carers added in winter give cover but add recruitment and training cost [1]. Zero hours contracts have the lowest cost when demand is low but cause insecurity and turnover, and in care continuity matters [1].
Judgement: Hallam should use annualised hours for existing carers, perhaps with a few extra part-time staff for the peak, because the demand is seasonal and predictable and the saving is large, provided it consults staff and monitors fatigue [1]. **[9]**

**8.** Advantages (up to 2 marks each). Lower costs: fewer staff need desks, so the firm can rent a smaller office [1], saving rent and utilities [1]. Wider recruitment: the firm can employ skilled accountants who live far from the office [1], which increases choice and may reduce wage pressure [1]. Disadvantage (up to 2 marks): weaker supervision and teamwork, because staff work apart [1], and it is harder to train junior accountants and to protect confidential client data on home networks [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to allow for leavers when working out supply.
- Not showing the gap between demand and supply.
- Giving a definition of a flexible practice without a benefit and drawback.
- Assuming flexible working is good for everyone.
- Not saying what the plan depends on.
