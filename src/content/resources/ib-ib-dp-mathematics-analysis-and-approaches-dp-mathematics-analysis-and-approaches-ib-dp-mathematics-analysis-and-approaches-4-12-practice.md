This set practises **section 4.12, standardization of normal variables (z-values)** (SL content,
examined at SL and HL). Section A can be done without a calculator except where a z-value from
technology is asked for. Section B needs a GDC, as on Paper 2. Give answers to three significant
figures unless told otherwise. All contexts and data are invented. Revise first with the
[4.12 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-12/)
and the
[4.12 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-12-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** X ~ N(80, 6²). Find the z-value of x = 71. **[1]**

**2.** X ~ N(40, 5²). Find the value of x whose z-value is 2.2. **[1]**

**3.** Alice scored 68 in a test where scores were N(60, 5²). Ben scored 75 in a different test where
scores were N(65, 8²). Determine who performed better relative to the other candidates in their test.
**[3]**

**4.** Z ~ N(0, 1).

**(a)** Find a such that P(Z < a) = 0.9. **[1]**

**(b)** Find b such that P(Z > b) = 0.9. **[1]**

**(c)** Find c such that P(−c < Z < c) = 0.9. **[2]**

**5.** X ~ N(μ, σ²). It is known that P(X < 47) = P(Z < −1.5) and P(X > 62) = P(Z > 1). Find μ and
σ. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** The masses of melons are normally distributed with standard deviation 12 g. 20% of the melons
weigh more than 500 g. Find the mean mass. **[4]**

**7.** The times taken to complete a puzzle are normally distributed with mean 42 seconds. 10% of
people take less than 35 seconds.

**(a)** Find the standard deviation of the times. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the probability that a randomly chosen person takes more than 50 seconds. **[2]**

**8.** The heights of a group of plants are normally distributed. 15% are shorter than 160 mm and
30% are taller than 175 mm.

**(a)** Write down the two z-values needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down two equations in μ and σ. **[2]**

**(c)** Find μ and σ. **[2]**

**9.** A machine fills bottles with a volume of liquid that is normally distributed with standard
deviation 4 ml.

**(a)** Find the mean volume that must be set so that only 2% of bottles contain less than 500 ml.
Give your answer to one decimal place. **[4]**

**(b)** The manufacturer prefers to set the mean at 505 ml and improve the machine instead. Find the
largest standard deviation that still keeps the proportion below 500 ml at 2%. **[3]**

**10.** Scores on an entrance test are N(58, 11²). Applicants in the top 10% receive a scholarship.

**(a)** Find the lowest score that receives a scholarship. **[3]**

**(b)** An applicant's score has a z-value of 1.1. Determine whether they receive a scholarship. **[2]**

**11.** X is normally distributed with P(X < 20) = 0.3 and P(X < 30) = 0.8. Find the mean and standard
deviation of X. **[6]**

**12.** X ~ N(μ, σ²) for unknown μ and σ.

**(a)** Explain why P(X < μ + 1.5σ) has the same value whatever the values of μ and σ. **[2]**

**(b)** Find this probability. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** z = (71 − 80)/6 = −1.5 [1]. **[1]**

**2.** x = 40 + 2.2 × 5 = 51 [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Alice: z = (68 − 60)/5 = 1.6 [1]. Ben: z = (75 − 65)/8 = 1.25 [1]. Alice's z-value is higher,
so she performed better relative to her group [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** a = 1.28 (1.2816) [1]

**(b)** By symmetry, b = −1.28 (−1.2816) [1]

**(c)** 5% in each tail, so use area 0.95 to the left [1]: c = 1.64 (1.6449) [1]. **[4]**

**5.** μ − 1.5σ = 47 [1] and μ + σ = 62 [1]. Subtracting: 2.5σ = 15, so σ = 6 [1] and μ = 56 [1].
**[4]**

**6.** P(X < 500) = 0.8 [1], so z = 0.8416… [1]. (500 − μ)/12 = 0.8416… [1], so
μ = 500 − 10.10 = 490 g (3 s.f.) [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Area 0.1 to the left gives z = −1.2816… [1]. (35 − 42)/σ = −1.2816… [1], so
σ = 7/1.2816… [1] = 5.46 s (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** X ~ N(42, 5.462…²): P(X > 50) [1] = 0.0715 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Area 0.15 to the left: z₁ = −1.0364… [1]. Area 0.70 to the left: z₂ = 0.5244… [1].

**(b)** 160 = μ − 1.0364σ [1] and 175 = μ + 0.5244σ [1].

**(c)** Subtracting: 15 = 1.5608σ, so σ = 9.61 mm [1]; μ = 160 + 1.0364 × 9.610 = 170 mm (3 s.f.)
[1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** Area 0.02 to the left: z = −2.0537… [1]. (500 − μ)/4 = −2.0537… [1], so
μ = 500 + 8.215 [1] = 508.2 ml [1].

**(b)** (500 − 505)/σ = −2.0537… [1], so σ = 5/2.0537… [1] = 2.43 ml (3 s.f.) [1]. **[7]**

**10. (a)** Area 0.9 to the left: z = 1.2816… [1]. x = 58 + 1.2816… × 11 [1] = 72.1 (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** z = 1.1 corresponds to x = 58 + 1.1 × 11 = 70.1 [1], which is below 72.1 (equivalently,
1.1 < 1.2816), so the applicant does not receive a scholarship [1]. **[5]**

**11.** z-values: area 0.3 gives −0.5244… [1]; area 0.8 gives 0.8416… [1].
20 = μ − 0.5244σ and 30 = μ + 0.8416σ [1]. Subtracting: 10 = 1.3660σ [1], so σ = 7.32 [1] and
μ = 20 + 0.5244 × 7.3205 = 23.8 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[6]**

**12. (a)** Standardizing, the z-value of μ + 1.5σ is ((μ + 1.5σ) − μ)/σ = 1.5 [1], so
P(X < μ + 1.5σ) = P(Z < 1.5), which does not depend on μ or σ [1].

**(b)** P(Z < 1.5) = 0.933 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[3]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 to 3 practise calculating and interpreting z. Question 4 works only with the standard
normal distribution, including the symmetric-interval idea. Question 5 is the calculator-free form of
a two-unknown problem. Questions 6 to 11 are the Paper 2 types: one unknown parameter, then two,
then parameter-setting in context. Question 12 explains why standardization works at all.
