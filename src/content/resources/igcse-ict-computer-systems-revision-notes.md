## Hardware and software

**Hardware** is physical; **software** is the programs.

- **System software** — operating system, utilities, **device drivers**, compilers. Runs the computer.
- **Application software** — word processors, spreadsheets, browsers, databases. Does the user's work.

A **device driver is system software**, even though it relates to one device. That classification is examined regularly.

## Input devices — justify the choice

Questions almost always ask *which device for this situation and why*. The answer always has two parts: name the device, then give the reason it suits **that** context.

| Device | Context and reason |
|---|---|
| **Barcode reader** | Supermarket checkout — fast and avoids typing errors |
| **Touchscreen** | Public kiosk — intuitive, no training, no loose parts to damage or steal |
| **OMR** | Multiple-choice marking — reads pencil marks at high speed |
| **OCR** | Converting printed documents into editable text |
| **MICR** | Bank cheques — hard to forge and readable even when the cheque is dirty |
| **Sensors** | Monitoring and control — continuous automatic readings, no human error |
| **Chip and PIN reader** | Payment — secure, encrypted |

## Output devices

Monitor (soft copy), laser printer (high volume, fast, low cost per page), inkjet (cheap to buy, good photo quality, expensive per page), plotter (large accurate line drawings for CAD and maps), 3D printer (prototypes), speakers, and **actuators** in control systems.

## Storage

| | RAM | ROM |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | **Volatile** — lost when power is off | Non-volatile |
| Contents | Programs and data in current use | Boot instructions |
| Writable | Yes | Read-only |

**Backing storage:**

- **Magnetic** (hard disk) — very large capacity, low cost per GB, but moving parts and vulnerable to shock.
- **Optical** (CD/DVD/Blu-ray) — cheap and portable, but slower and smaller.
- **Solid state** (SSD, flash) — fast, silent, robust, low power; higher cost per GB.

**Why an SSD suits a laptop:** no moving parts so it survives being carried and knocked, lower power consumption so battery life improves, faster and silent. The trade-off is price per gigabyte.

## Networks

- **LAN** — one site. **WAN** — geographically spread, often using third-party lines.
- Hardware: NIC, switch, router, cables or wireless access point, and a modem for internet access.

**Wired versus wireless:** wired is faster, more reliable and more secure; wireless is convenient, cheaper to install and allows mobility, but is slower, more prone to interference and easier to intercept.

**Network security:** firewalls, encryption, strong passwords, user access levels, and anti-malware.

**Encryption does not stop data being intercepted** — it makes intercepted data unreadable without the key. Saying it prevents interception is the standard error.

## Health, safety and security

**Health risks** (long-term physical effects) and **safety risks** (immediate danger) are different categories, and questions ask for the **hazard and its prevention** together:

| Health risk | Prevention |
|---|---|
| RSI | Wrist rest, correct posture, regular breaks |
| Eye strain | Anti-glare screen, breaks, suitable lighting |
| Back problems | Adjustable chair, screen at eye level |

| Safety risk | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Electrocution | Do not overload sockets; keep drinks away; check cables |
| Fire | Do not overload sockets; ventilate equipment; CO₂ extinguisher |
| Trailing cables | Cable ducts and tidy layout |
| Heavy equipment falling | Strong desks, secure placement |

**Data threats:** viruses, hacking, phishing, pharming, smishing, spyware. Prevention: anti-virus software, firewalls, awareness training, checking URLs, and never following links in unsolicited messages.

## The effects of using IT (syllabus topic 5)

The syllabus gives this topic exactly two subtopics, and exam questions are set on those.

**Microprocessor-controlled devices in the home** — smart heating, alarms, washing machines, fridges. Positive effects: more leisure time, remote monitoring and control, better security of the home. Negative effects: a more sedentary lifestyle and less physical fitness, less face-to-face social interaction, dependence on technology, and a risk to the **security of data** if a connected device is hacked.

**Microprocessor-controlled devices in transport** — engine management, cruise control, traffic control, **autonomous vehicles**. Positive effects: improved **transport safety** (fewer human errors) and more efficient journeys. Negative effects: a hacked system is a safety risk, the **security of data** such as tracked journeys, and over-reliance on automation that can fail.

**Health problems from prolonged use** — repetitive strain injury (RSI), back problems, eye problems and headaches, each with its cause and a prevention strategy (see the table above).

*Wider effects of ICT on employment and working patterns (teleworking, flexible hours) are useful background, but they are not part of this syllabus's "effects of using IT" topic and are not the content its questions are set on.*

## Exam traps

- Classifying a device driver as application software.
- Naming a device without justifying it for the scenario given.
- Saying RAM stores data permanently.
- Confusing OMR with OCR.
- Saying encryption prevents interception.
- Giving a risk without its prevention.
- Giving only positive effects of a microprocessor-controlled device, when the syllabus asks for positive and negative effects.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish system from application software, with two examples of each.
2. Give three differences between RAM and ROM.
3. Which input device suits a bank processing cheques, and why?
4. What does encryption actually achieve?
5. Give two health risks and two safety risks, each with a prevention.

**Answers:** 1. System software runs the computer — operating systems and device drivers; application software performs user tasks — word processors and browsers. 2. RAM is volatile, ROM is not; RAM holds data in current use, ROM holds boot instructions; RAM is writable, ROM is read-only. 3. MICR — the magnetic ink characters are difficult to forge and can be read reliably even if the cheque is dirty or creased. 4. It scrambles data so that anyone intercepting it cannot read it without the key; it does not prevent interception itself. 5. Health: RSI, prevented by wrist rests and breaks; eye strain, prevented by anti-glare screens and breaks. Safety: electrocution, prevented by not overloading sockets; trailing cables, prevented by cable ducts.
