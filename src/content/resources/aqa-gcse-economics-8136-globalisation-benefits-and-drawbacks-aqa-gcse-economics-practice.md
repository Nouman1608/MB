## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of the following is a feature of globalisation? **[1]**

A: Higher tariffs on imported goods

B: Firms making products only in their home country

C: Parts of one product being made in several countries

D: Fewer people moving between countries to work

**2.** Define foreign direct investment. **[1]**

**3.** State **two** features of globalisation other than an increase in international trade. **[2]**

**4.** A UK firm makes garden tools at a cost of £45 each. It could make them abroad for £28 each, plus £5 transport per tool to bring them to the UK.

**(a)** Calculate the cost saving per tool. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage saving, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** Country K exports goods and services worth 420 billion units and imports 460 billion units. Its GDP is 1,600 billion units. Calculate exports plus imports as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** Explain how the operations of multinational companies have contributed to the growth of globalisation. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **one** benefit of globalisation for UK producers. **[3]**

**8.** Read the extract.

*Harbour Toys, a UK firm, has moved the assembly of its toys from a factory in northern England to Country P, where wages are much lower. 300 UK jobs have gone and 900 jobs have been created in Country P. The firm has cut the price of its main toy by 10%.*

**(a)** Identify **one** group in the UK that gains and **one** group in the UK that loses from this decision. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why the workers who lost their jobs may find it hard to get new work. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **one** benefit for Country P's economy of the new jobs. **[3]**

**9.** Explain **one** drawback of globalisation for consumers in a less developed country. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how interdependence means that a problem in another country can raise prices in the UK. Use the idea of a supply chain in your answer. **[4]**

**11.** A UK supermarket flies fresh fruit in from countries thousands of miles away. Explain **one** sustainability consideration this raises. **[3]**

**12.** "Less developed countries gain more than they lose from globalisation." Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Higher tariffs and producing only at home are the opposite of globalisation, and globalisation increases migration for work. **[1]**

**2.** A firm from one country building, buying or investing in productive assets such as factories or offices in another country [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: global supply chains [1]; foreign direct investment by MNCs [1]; movement of capital between countries; movement of labour (migration); global brands or shared culture. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Cost abroad = 28 + 5 = £33, so saving = 45 − 33 = **£12** [1].

**(b)** 12 ÷ 45 × 100 [1] = **26.7%** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Students who forget the transport cost get a saving of £17 and lose the marks in both parts. Read every cost in the question.

**5.** (420 + 460) ÷ 1,600 × 100 = 880 ÷ 1,600 × 100 [1] = **55%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** MNCs locate different stages of production in different countries to cut costs [1], which creates global supply chains and increases trade in components [1]. They build or buy factories and offices abroad (foreign direct investment) [1], taking capital, technology and global brands into new markets, so economies become more closely linked [1]. **[4]**

**7.** For example: UK producers can sell to much larger world markets [1], so they can increase output and benefit from economies of scale [1], lowering average costs and raising profits [1]. An alternative chain earns the same marks: cheaper imported materials and components lower production costs, so firms can cut prices and become more competitive. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Gains: UK consumers (lower prices) or the firm's owners (lower costs, higher profits) [1]. Loses: the 300 UK workers who lost their jobs, or local suppliers and shops in northern England [1].

**(b)** Their skills may match assembly work, which is now done abroad [1], so they do not have the skills needed for the jobs that are available, such as in services [1]. This is structural unemployment, and retraining takes time, or the new jobs may be in other regions [1].

**(c)** The new jobs give workers in Country P an income, often higher than local alternatives [1]. They spend this income on goods and services [1], which raises demand for local firms and increases economic growth; the government may also gain tax revenue [1]. **[8]**

**9.** For example: local producers may be pushed out of the market by MNCs [1], so consumers lose access to local products and become dependent on imported brands [1]; if the MNC has market power it may raise prices [1]. An alternative chain earns the same marks: profits are sent back to the MNC's home country, so less income stays to be spent locally, limiting the rise in living standards for consumers. **[3]**

**10.** Interdependence means countries rely on each other for goods and components [1]. A product sold in the UK may use parts made in several countries along a supply chain [1]. If a factory in one country closes, for example after a natural disaster, the supply of parts falls [1], so UK firms face shortages and higher costs, and pass these on as higher prices to UK consumers [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Air freight produces large carbon emissions per unit transported [1], which add to climate change, an external cost not included in the price of the fruit [1]. This uses up resources and harms future generations, so it is not sustainable; the supermarket could source in season locally or ship by sea, though costs or choice may change [1]. **[3]**

**12.** *Gains:*

- New jobs created by MNCs give workers incomes that are often higher than in farming or informal work [1], raising living standards [1].
- Access to rich-country markets lets producers export and grow, increasing GDP [1].
- MNCs bring technology and skills that local firms can learn from [1].

*Losses:*

- Some workers face low pay, long hours and unsafe conditions [1], and jobs may leave again for an even cheaper country [1].
- Profits may flow back to the MNC's home country, so less income stays locally [1].
- Weak regulation may lead to pollution and use of resources that harm future generations [1].

*Judgement:* For example, globalisation is likely to bring more gains than losses where governments protect workers and spend the extra income on education and infrastructure, but where regulation is weak, the gains go mainly to MNCs, so the outcome depends on how well the country manages it [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The judgement must be supported. "It depends" earns credit only if you say what it depends on and why.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving transport or other costs out of an offshoring calculation.
- Defining an MNC or FDI by size rather than by operating or investing in more than one country.
- Writing about "people" instead of naming producers, workers or consumers.
- Describing a sustainability issue without saying why it harms future generations or the environment.
- Giving an evaluation with no supported judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I answer a question about a named group?**
Keep the named group (for example UK workers) in every sentence, and link the effect on them to a reason.

**Do I need real-world examples?**
They help, but the extract or data in the question are where application marks come from. Use them first.
