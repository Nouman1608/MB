These questions cover all four key questions of Depth Study A: The First World War, 1914-18.

## Section A

**1.** Give **three** reasons why the German advance through Belgium was slower than planned. **[3]**

**2.** Identify **three** hardships faced by soldiers living in the trenches. **[3]**

**3.** State **two** contributions made by Indian troops on the Western Front. **[2]**

**4.** Give **three** actions of the Arab Revolt against Ottoman forces. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** reasons why the convoy system reduced shipping losses. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **three** effects of the war on civilians in Germany. **[3]**

**7.** Give **two** ways in which the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk helped Germany. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe how the Schlieffen Plan was intended to work. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the events in Germany from the Kiel Mutiny to the Armistice. **[4]**

**10.** Why had a defensive war emerged on the Western Front by the end of 1914? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Why was the German offensive of 1918 unsuccessful? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** "New methods of warfare made little difference to the fighting on the Western Front." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**13.** "Britain won the war at sea." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: Belgium refused to let German troops pass and resisted (1); the forts at Liège held out
for days (1); Belgian troops destroyed bridges and railways (1); the BEF fought a delaying action at Mons (1); German
troops were exhausted by long marches (1); troops had been moved east to face Russia (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: mud and flooding (1); trench foot (1); rats and lice (1); constant shelling and snipers
(1); shell shock (1); cold and wet conditions in winter (1); boredom and lack of sleep (1); the smell of dead bodies
(1). **[3]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: they arrived in autumn 1914 when the BEF was short of men (1); they helped hold the line
at First Ypres (1); they fought at Neuve Chapelle in 1915 (1); Indian soldiers won Victoria Crosses, such as Khudadad
Khan (1); Indian cavalry stayed on the Western Front after the infantry left (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: the rising at Mecca in 1916 (1); attacks on the Hejaz railway (1); the capture of Aqaba
in 1917 (1); tying down Ottoman troops who might have fought the British elsewhere (1); guarding the flank of
Allenby's advance (1); entering Damascus in 1918 (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: escorting warships could attack U-boats that approached (1); ships in a group were harder
to find than ships sailing alone (1); U-boats risked depth charges if they attacked (1); air patrols could protect
convoys near the coast (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: food shortages caused by the British blockade (1); the "turnip winter" of 1916-17 (1);
rationing (1); rising prices and falling real wages (1); disease and malnutrition (1); strikes and protests (1); grief
at heavy casualties (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: it ended the war on the Eastern Front, so Germany fought on one front (1); troops could be
moved west for the 1918 offensive (1); Germany gained control of large areas including Ukraine, with food and
resources (1). **[2]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: Germany expected a war on two fronts against France and Russia (1); it assumed Russia
would take about six weeks to mobilise (1); most of the army would attack France first (1); it would go through
Belgium to avoid French border forts (1); the right wing would sweep round west of Paris (1); France would be defeated
quickly (1); troops would then move by rail to fight Russia (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: at the end of October 1918 sailors at Kiel refused to sail against the British fleet (1);
the mutiny spread to other ports and cities (1); workers' and soldiers' councils were set up (1); a republic was
declared in Bavaria (1); on 9 November the Kaiser abdicated and fled to the Netherlands (1); Ebert's government took
over (1); the Armistice was signed at Compiègne on 11 November (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **the failure of the Schlieffen Plan**, because after the Marne the Germans retreated to the Aisne and dug
trenches to hold the ground they had won; **the race to the sea**, because each attempt to outflank the enemy failed,
so by November the line ran unbroken from the Channel to Switzerland and there was no open flank left; **the power of
defensive weapons**, because machine guns, barbed wire and artillery made attacks across open ground very costly, so
digging in was the only way to survive; **exhaustion and losses**, because both sides had suffered huge casualties by
First Ypres and lacked the men and shells to break through. **[6]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content: **supply problems**, because the stormtroopers advanced faster
than food, ammunition and artillery could follow, so attacks lost momentum; **losses of the best troops**, because the
elite assault units suffered heavy casualties that could not be replaced; **American troops**, because large numbers
were arriving, so the Allies could replace losses and Germany could not; **Allied unity**, because Foch was made
supreme commander and co-ordinated reserves; **the longer front**, because the bulges created by the advance were
harder to defend and exposed to counter-attack, as at Amiens; **the blockade**, because hungry German soldiers stopped
to loot Allied supplies. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** gas caused fear but masks soon reduced its effect, and wind could blow it back; early tanks broke down,
were slow and stuck in mud, as on the Somme; aircraft were mainly used for reconnaissance and could not win battles;
the stalemate lasted until 1918 despite all of them. **Disagree:** the machine gun was the main reason defence was so
strong, so it shaped the whole war; aircraft spotting for artillery made bombardments more accurate; massed tanks
at Cambrai (1917) and Amiens (1918) helped break through; the combination of tanks, aircraft, creeping barrages and
infantry tactics brought victory in 1918. **Judgement:** for example, single weapons made little difference at first,
but used together in 1918 they ended the stalemate. **[10]**

**13.** Levels as for Question 12. Indicative content. **Agree:** the British blockade cut off German imports and
caused severe shortages; at Jutland the German fleet was unable to break British control and never seriously came out
again; convoys defeated the U-boat threat from 1917; Britain kept its supply routes and moved troops from the Empire
and the USA. **Disagree:** Britain lost more ships and men at Jutland; U-boats sank a huge tonnage of shipping and in
1917 brought Britain close to starvation; German raiders and mines caused losses; the war at sea was won by a
combination of the blockade, convoys and American naval help, not by battles. **Judgement:** for example, Britain won
the war at sea strategically, because it kept control of the seas and starved Germany, even though it did not win a
decisive battle. **[10]**
