## What section 12.5 covers

| Syllabus strand | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Methods of delivery | Describe computer-based training, online tutorials, networked courses, massive open online courses (MOOCs) and video-conferencing |
| Impact | Discuss the effect on student and teacher motivation, achievement and autonomy |
| Evaluation | Give advantages and disadvantages of each method of delivery |

**Technology enhanced learning** means using IT to support teaching and learning. The technology can replace some face-to-face teaching or add to it. The aim is to improve how well and how flexibly people learn.

## Methods of delivery

### Computer-based training (CBT)

CBT uses software installed on a computer, or on a disc, to teach a topic. It usually combines text, images, audio and video, with interactive exercises and quizzes. The software records scores and can move learners to harder or easier material. Typical uses: staff induction and safety training, learning to use a program, and driving theory practice.

- **Advantages:** learners work at their own pace; training can be repeated; no instructor needed; consistent content; progress is recorded automatically; no internet is needed if installed locally.
- **Disadvantages:** no personal help when the learner is stuck; content can go out of date until the software is replaced; development is expensive; some learners lose motivation without contact with others.

### Online tutorials

An online tutorial is a short lesson on the internet, often a video, a step-by-step guide or an interactive page, covering a single skill or topic. Users choose the tutorial they need, when they need it.

- **Advantages:** free or cheap; available at any time; can be paused, rewound and repeated; wide choice for any topic.
- **Disadvantages:** quality varies and may be inaccurate; no feedback on the learner's own work; needs an internet connection; learners may choose poorly and miss important steps; easy to be distracted.

### Networked courses

A networked course is delivered through a school, college or company network, often using a **virtual learning environment (VLE)**. The VLE holds materials, assignments, quizzes, forums and marks, and learners log in with their own accounts.

- **Advantages:** materials in one place for everyone; teachers can set work, track progress and give feedback; learners can collaborate through forums; work can be accessed from home if the VLE is available online.
- **Disadvantages:** needs a reliable network and servers; costs for setting up and maintaining the system; staff need training; students without a computer or internet access at home are disadvantaged.

### Massive open online courses (MOOCs)

A MOOC is an online course open to very large numbers of learners, usually with free or cheap access. It typically uses recorded lectures, readings, automatic quizzes and discussion forums, sometimes with peer marking.

- **Advantages:** learners anywhere can study courses from leading universities; low cost; learners choose topics and pace; huge numbers can take part.
- **Disadvantages:** many learners do not finish; little personal support from tutors; recognised qualifications may not be offered, or cost extra; requires self-discipline, and good internet access.

**Worked example 1: completion rates and cost.** A MOOC has 20,000 enrolled learners and 9% complete it. The provider's yearly costs are £36,000.

- Completers = 20,000 × 0.09 = **1,800**.
- Cost per enrolled learner = £36,000 ÷ 20,000 = **£1.80**.
- Cost per completer = £36,000 ÷ 1,800 = **£20**.

The cost per learner is very low, but the cost of each person who actually finishes is higher, and the low completion rate shows the weakness of this method of delivery: motivation and support.

### Video-conferencing

Video-conferencing allows people in different places to see and hear each other in real time, using cameras, microphones, screens and a network connection. In education it is used for live lessons with a remote teacher, for guest speakers, for connecting classes in different countries and for students who cannot attend in person.

- **Advantages:** real-time contact and questions; no travel; access to specialist teachers; sessions can be recorded for later; may include screen sharing and whiteboards.
- **Disadvantages:** needs fast, reliable connections and equipment; technical faults interrupt lessons; harder to see body language and to manage a class; time zones; learners may lose focus; less social contact.

**Worked example 2: travel saved by video-conferencing.** A college runs a weekly specialist session for 30 students, each living 40 miles from the campus. A round trip is 2 × 40 = 80 miles per student. For the group, that is 30 × 80 = **2,400 miles** of travel each week, which would also take hours and cost fuel. A video-conference replaces all of that with one connection, but it needs a stable connection for every student and loses face-to-face contact.

## Impact of technology enhanced learning

### On motivation

- **Students:** interactive tasks, games, videos and instant feedback keep many students interested. Progress charts and badges can encourage them. However, if the equipment is unreliable, or if work is boring or isolated, motivation falls, and distractions are only a click away.
- **Teachers:** less time spent on repetitive marking (for example automatic quizzes) and more time for individual help. Resources can be shared. However, teachers can feel pressure from the need to learn new systems and create digital materials, and from the worry that technology will replace them.

### On achievement

- Instant feedback lets students correct errors quickly. Material can be repeated until understood. Teachers can see results by topic and focus on weak areas.
- Students can access the best teachers and resources, which can raise results.
- Risks: poor-quality resources, copying from the internet, and students who skim content without learning it. Results may not improve if students lack access to devices at home, creating a **digital divide**.

**Worked example 3: reading a quiz result.** A CBT quiz has 25 questions and a pass mark of 60%. The pass mark is 0.60 × 25 = 15 questions. A learner answers 18 correctly, so scores 18 ÷ 25 = 0.72, or 72%, which is a pass. The software can show which of the 7 wrong questions belong to which topic, and direct the learner to repeat that part. This detailed feedback is one reason why achievement can improve.

### On autonomy

**Autonomy** means learners control their own learning: what to study, when, where and how fast. Technology enhanced learning increases autonomy because materials are available at any time, learners choose their order and pace, and they can search for extra help. It builds independence, self-management and research skills, which are useful for later study and work. But learners without enough self-discipline or study skills may fall behind with little supervision, and teachers have less chance to notice when a learner is struggling.

## Comparing the methods

| Method | Best for | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| CBT | Repeating fixed skills and safety training | No personal help; needs updating |
| Online tutorials | Learning one skill quickly | Variable quality; no feedback |
| Networked courses | A class using shared materials and tracking | Needs network, servers and training |
| MOOC | Reaching very large numbers at low cost | Low completion; little support |
| Video-conferencing | Live contact with distant teachers or classes | Needs good connections; harder to manage |

## Matching a method to a learner

| Learner and need | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New employees need the same safety training | CBT | Consistent content and recorded results |
| A pupil wants to learn one spreadsheet function tonight | Online tutorial | Quick and specific |
| A teacher sets weekly tasks for a class | Networked course | Materials, marks and feedback in one place |
| An adult wants a university-level subject at low cost | MOOC | Open access and flexible |
| A small school needs an expert in a rare language | Video-conferencing | Live access to a distant teacher |

## Common mistakes

- Describing a method without saying how it is delivered, such as on a network or over the internet.
- Saying "it is cheaper" without saying for whom, or comparing with what.
- Writing "students are more motivated" with no reason, and no mention of when they may be less motivated.
- Mixing up a MOOC (open to anyone, huge numbers) with a networked course (for registered members of one organisation).
- Forgetting the teacher's side of the impact.
- Not explaining autonomy, or confusing it with working alone.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** a method: what it is, how it is delivered, and who uses it.
- **Explain** an advantage or disadvantage: link a feature of the method to an effect.
- **Discuss** the impact: give effects on both students and teachers, positive and negative.
- **Evaluate**: choose the method that best suits the scenario (numbers, location, budget, type of learning) and justify it.
