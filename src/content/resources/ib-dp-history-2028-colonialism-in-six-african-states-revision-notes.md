**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 8 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): colonialism in Côte d'Ivoire, Kenya, Mozambique, Nigeria, Senegal and Uganda, 1890 to 1980. Every Paper 3 question on this study will require reference to **one prescribed country**, so choose two or three countries and learn each against all six inquiry topics. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-colonialism-in-six-african-states/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Establishing and maintaining political authority | Assimilation, direct rule, indirect rule, settler rule |
| Methods used to maintain power | African agents, law, security forces, coercion |
| Colonial economic policies | Cash crops, land, labour, railways, world markets |
| Social and cultural continuity and change | Missions, independent churches, cities, identity, attitudes |
| Impact on social groups | Settlers, elites, women, ethnic groups |
| Effectiveness of resistance | Political, cultural, everyday and armed |

## Establishing authority

- **Assimilation:** Four Communes of Senegal; Blaise Diagne, deputy 1914; *indigénat* for subjects; Portuguese *assimilado* status.
- **Direct rule:** French governors in Côte d'Ivoire; Portuguese company rule to the 1930s.
- **Indirect rule:** Lugard; 1914 amalgamation; Native Authorities; warrant chiefs in the east; Buganda Agreement 1900.
- **Settler colony:** Kenya's White Highlands; reserves; settler political demands.

## Maintaining power

- Chiefs, clerks, interpreters, police and soldiers (*tirailleurs*, King's African Rifles).
- Taxes, passes (*kipande*), native courts.
- Forced labour: *corvée*, *chibalo*; French reforms in 1946; Portuguese reforms in the early 1960s.
- Violence: the Emergency in Kenya; Hola, 1959; PIDE in Mozambique.

## Economic impact

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Land and crops | Senegal groundnuts; Ivorian cocoa; Ugandan cotton; Kenyan settler farms; Mozambique compulsory cotton |
| Labour | Taxes force cash work; forced labour; migrant labour to South Africa |
| Infrastructure | Uganda Railway 1901; Lagos to Kano 1911; Dakar-Niger railway; ports |
| Trade | Tied to world prices; Depression; war demand |

## Social and cultural change

- Missions, schools; Africanist churches (Harrist movement in Côte d'Ivoire, Aladura in Nigeria); Kikuyu independent schools.
- Cities: Dakar, Abidjan, Lagos, Nairobi, Kampala, Lourenço Marques.
- Identity: Négritude, Makerere, ethnic categories reinforced by colonial rule.
- Attitudes: civilising mission, racism, paternalism, African adaptation and criticism.

## Social groups

- **Settlers and officials:** privileged, especially in Kenya.
- **Elites:** Houphouët-Boigny, Senghor, Azikiwe, Kenyatta; Baganda chiefs.
- **Women:** heavier workloads; loss of rights; Aba Women's War 1929; support for Mau Mau.
- **Ethnic groups:** favoured and disadvantaged groups affected later politics.

## Resistance: effectiveness

| Type | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Political and legal | RDA, NCNC, KAU, unions | Reforms and independence 1960 to 1962 |
| Cultural | Négritude; independent churches | Identity and confidence |
| Day-to-day | Tax avoidance, flight, slow work | Limited colonial control, little change in policy |
| Armed | Mau Mau (1952 to 1960); FRELIMO (1964 to 1974) | Military defeat for Mau Mau but political pressure; success in Mozambique after Portugal's 1974 revolution |

## Arguments to practise

1. "Indirect rule respected African institutions." For: it kept chiefs. Against: it changed them and created warrant chiefs.
2. "French assimilation was a myth." For: few citizens. Against: Senegal's Four Communes had real political rights.
3. "Forced labour defined colonial economies." For: Mozambique, Côte d'Ivoire. Against: cash cropping by smallholders in Uganda and Senegal.
4. "Violence was the only way to end colonial rule." Compare Mozambique and Kenya with Senegal, Nigeria and Uganda.

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was colonial rule in Kenya based on settler interests?"

- Evidence for: land alienation, White Highlands, the kipande, settler influence on policy.
- Evidence against: the Colonial Office and Treasury imposed limits, African reserves and Asian traders also mattered, and Britain agreed to African rule in the 1960s.
- Judgement: settler interests dominated policy until the 1950s but were not the only force.

## Common mistakes

- Generalising from one country to all.
- Ignoring Mozambique because it was Portuguese.
- Writing about independence only; the dates up to 1980 matter too.
- Omitting African agents of colonial rule.

## Self-test

1. Which country had the Four Communes?
2. Who developed indirect rule in Nigeria?
3. What was *chibalo*?
4. Which Kenyan rising began in 1952?
5. When did FRELIMO start its armed struggle?
6. Name one Africanist church.

**Answers:** 1. Senegal. 2. Frederick Lugard. 3. Forced labour in Mozambique. 4. Mau Mau. 5. 1964. 6. The Harrist church or the Aladura churches.

## Key terms

- **Indigénat:** a French legal code for colonial subjects.
- **Indirect rule:** governing through existing local rulers.
- **Alienation of land:** taking land from Africans for settlers or companies.
- **Assimilado:** a Portuguese colonial status for a small number of Africans.
- **Native Authority:** a local ruler recognised and supervised by British officers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which countries are the most useful to pair?** A British country such as Kenya or Nigeria with a French or Portuguese one, such as Senegal or Mozambique, gives contrast.

**Does the guide expect dates for every country?** Know independence dates and the main turning points for the countries you choose.
