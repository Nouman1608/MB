compare.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| World city | A city with global influence in finance, business, culture and politics |
| International migration | Moving from one country to another |
| National (internal) migration | Moving within the same country |
| Deprivation | Lack of access to things most people have, such as income, good housing, health and education |
| Dereliction | Land and buildings left empty and decaying |
| Brownfield site | Previously built-on land available for reuse |
| Greenfield site | Land not built on before, often farmland at the city edge |
| Green belt | A protected zone of open land around a city where building is restricted |
| Urban sprawl | Unplanned outward spread of a city |
| Urban greening | Increasing parks, trees and green spaces in a city |
| Gentrification | Wealthier people moving into a poorer area, raising prices |
| Regeneration | Improving a run-down area economically, socially and environmentally |

## London fact file

- Capital of the UK, River Thames, south-east England; about 8.8 million
  (2021 census).
- On the Greenwich Meridian: trading day overlaps Asia and North America.
- Heathrow; Eurostar via the Channel Tunnel.
- Migration: Caribbean (Windrush, from 1948), South Asia, Africa,
  Eastern Europe after 2004; around four in ten born outside the UK.
  Young adults move in from the rest of the UK.
- Importance: City of London and Canary Wharf; Bank of England; London
  Stock Exchange; Westminster; West End; British Museum; Commonwealth
  Secretariat.
- Greening: Royal Parks; Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park; National Park
  City (2019).
- Challenges: deprivation in Tower Hamlets and Newham; housing costs;
  green belt pushes building onto brownfield sites; Congestion Charge
  (2003) and ULEZ (2019); Thames Tideway Tunnel for sewage overflows.

## New York fact file

- Largest US city; Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Hudson River;
  five boroughs; about 8.8 million (2020 census).
- Migration: Ellis Island (1892–1954) for European migrants; later
  Caribbean, Latin American and Asian; more than a third born abroad.
  Great Migration of African Americans from the South; Harlem.
- Importance: Wall Street, New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq; UN
  headquarters; Broadway; the Port of New York and New Jersey; JFK.
- Greening: Central Park; the High Line (from 2009); Freshkills Park.
- Challenges: poverty in parts of the Bronx; housing costs and
  gentrification; suburban sprawl; congestion pricing south of 60th
  Street (2025); rubbish exported since Fresh Kills closed (2001); water
  from Catskill and Delaware watersheds; Hurricane Sandy flooding (2012).

## Flagship regeneration: London Docklands

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why needed | Docks closed late 1960s–1981 as ships grew and container trade moved to Tilbury; derelict land, unemployment |
| Features | LDDC 1981–1998; Canary Wharf; DLR (1987); London City Airport (1987); Jubilee line extension (1999); housing; ExCeL |
| Economic impact | Many office jobs, often filled by commuters |
| Social impact | Better transport; housing too expensive for many locals |
| Environmental impact | Derelict land cleaned and waterside improved |
| Judgement | Economic success, unevenly shared |

## Exam traps

- No named places, dates or schemes.
- National and international migration confused.
- Challenges listed with no explanation of who is affected.
- Regeneration described without its reasons or judged impacts.

## Self-test

1. Define a world city.
2. Give one reason London's location helps global trading.
3. Give one example of international migration that shaped London or
   New York.
4. Name one urban greening project in each city.
5. Explain one reason why London builds on brownfield sites.
6. Why did London Docklands need regeneration?
7. Give one criticism of the Docklands project.

**Answers:** 1. A city with influence beyond its own country as a
centre of finance, business, culture and politics. 2. On the Greenwich
Meridian, so its working day overlaps with Asian and North American
markets. 3. For example Caribbean migration to London after 1948, or
European migrants entering New York through Ellis Island. 4. London:
Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park or the Royal Parks; New York: the High Line,
Central Park or Freshkills Park. 5. The Metropolitan Green Belt
restricts greenfield building at the city edge. 6. The docks closed
because ships grew too large and container trade moved downstream,
leaving derelict land and high unemployment. 7. Many new jobs went to
commuters and new housing was too expensive for many existing
residents.
