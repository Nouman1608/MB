This guide covers subtopic **1.14 Using a calculator** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. The subtopic is about using a scientific calculator accurately and sensibly. It matters on Paper 2, which requires a scientific calculator. Paper 1 is a non-calculator paper and the syllabus states that it covers all the subject content except this subtopic. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page explains the three skills the syllabus names, and the habits that protect marks.

Companion pages: the [Using a calculator revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-using-a-calculator-revision-notes/) and the [Using a calculator practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-using-a-calculator-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

The syllabus lists three outcomes:

1. Use a calculator efficiently. For example, know not to round values within a calculation and to round only the final answer.
2. Enter values appropriately on a calculator. For example, enter 2 hours 30 minutes as 2.5 hours, or as 2° 30′ 0″.
3. Interpret the calculator display appropriately. For example, in money a display of 4.8 means $4.80, and in time a display of 3.25 means 3 hours 15 minutes.

The assessment rules for Paper 2 also matter here. A scientific calculator is required, and algebraic or graphical calculators are not permitted. Candidates should give non-exact numerical answers correct to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place for angles in degrees, unless the question says otherwise. To earn accuracy marks, avoid rounding figures until the final answer. When a final answer from one part is needed later, use the unrounded value. Use the value of π from the calculator or 3.142.

## Using the calculator efficiently

### Do not round in the middle

Every time you round, you introduce a small error, and several rounded steps can push the final answer outside the accepted range. Keep full values on the display, or store them in the memory (or use the answer key) and round once at the end.

**Worked example.** Work out 2.34 × 5.67 × 1.5 and compare with an answer found after rounding the first product.

```
exact:    2.34 x 5.67 = 13.2678
          13.2678 x 1.5 = 19.9017  →  19.9 (3 s.f.)

rounded early:  13.2678 → 13.3
          13.3 x 1.5 = 19.95     →  20.0 (3 s.f.)
```

The two final answers differ in the third significant figure. The early rounding has cost accuracy, and an examiner would not award the accuracy mark for 20.0.

### Brackets, fractions and order of operations

A scientific calculator follows the standard order of operations: brackets first, then indices, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. You must put in the brackets that the problem needs.

```
12.8 ÷ (3.6 - 1.15) = 12.8 ÷ 2.45 = 5.2244...    →  5.22
12.8 ÷ 3.6 - 1.15   = 3.5555... - 1.15 = 2.4055... →  2.41   (not the same!)
```

A fraction bar acts like a pair of brackets round the top and the bottom. When you type a fraction on a calculator without a fraction key, put brackets round both the numerator and the denominator, e.g. (85.6) ÷ (1.2 + 0.35).

**Worked example.** Work out 5 + 4 × 3² ÷ 2 and say what a calculator that ignored the order of operations would show.

```
correct:  5 + 4 x 9 ÷ 2 = 5 + 18 = 23
ignoring the order (left to right): (5 + 4) x 3^2 ÷ 2 = 9 x 9 ÷ 2 = 40.5
```

Scientific calculators follow the correct order, so 23 will appear. If you ever see 40.5, an operation has probably been entered in the wrong order or a bracket is missing.

### Negative numbers and squares

Use the negative sign key (not the subtraction key) to enter a negative number. Put brackets round a negative number that is being squared.

```
(-3.2)^2 = 10.24
-3.2^2   = -10.24      (the square applies only to 3.2)
```

### Powers, roots and standard form

Use the power key for x^y and the root keys for √ and ∛. Put a fractional power in brackets: 7.2^(1/3) = 1.93. For standard form, use the dedicated key (labelled EXP or ×10ˣ). Enter (4.2 × 10⁶) × (3.5 × 10⁻³) by keying 4.2, the standard form key, 6, then ×, 3.5, the key, then the negative power. The result is 14 700, which is 1.47 × 10⁴.

### Fractions and mixed numbers

Many calculators have a fraction key. To work out 2 3/8 + 1 5/6, enter each mixed number using the key. The exact answer is 4 5/24 (101/24). The decimal display 4.2083... is the same value written as a decimal.

### Using π

Use the π key, or 3.142 as allowed by the syllabus. For a circle of radius 6.3 cm, the circumference is 2 × π × 6.3 = 39.584... cm, which is 39.6 cm to 3 significant figures. Using 3.142 gives 39.589 cm, which also rounds to 39.6.

## Entering values appropriately

Calculators use decimals for time and for angles, unless you use the degrees, minutes and seconds key. You must convert correctly.

### Time

Minutes must be changed to a fraction of an hour. Divide the minutes by 60.

```
2 hours 30 minutes = 2 + 30/60 = 2.5 hours
1 hour 45 minutes  = 1.75 hours
2 hours 15 minutes = 2.25 hours
```

**Worked example.** A car travels 160 km in 2 hours 30 minutes. Find its average speed.

```
speed = 160 ÷ 2.5 = 64 km/h
```

Do not enter 2.30 for 2 hours 30 minutes. That value means 2.3 hours, which is 2 hours 18 minutes.

### Angles

Degrees, minutes and seconds are 60 to the next unit. An angle of 2° 30′ 0″ is 2.5°. You can enter it directly with the D°M′S″ key, or as 2 + 30/60 = 2.5.

```
37.65° = 37° + 0.65 x 60′ = 37° 39′
```

## Interpreting the display

### Money

A calculator drops zeros at the end of a decimal. A display of 4.8 in a money question means $4.80, and 12.5 means $12.50. Write money with two decimal places.

### Time

A decimal display for time is not in hours and minutes. Convert the decimal part by multiplying by 60.

```
3.25 hours = 3 hours + 0.25 x 60 minutes = 3 hours 15 minutes
0.7 hours  = 0.7 x 60 = 42 minutes
```

A display of 3.25 does **not** mean 3 hours 25 minutes. This is a very common error.

### Percentages and decimals

A display of 0.0125 means 1.25%. Remember to multiply by 100 to convert.

### Recurring decimals and long displays

If the display shows a long string, such as 5.224489796, write the number to the accuracy asked for. Do not copy ten digits. For an exact answer, keep a fraction or a root in the answer and do not use the rounded decimal.

### Standard form on the display

Some calculators show 1.47 × 10⁴ as 1.47E4 or 1.47 04. Write it correctly as 1.47 × 10⁴.

## Rounding the final answer

On Paper 2:

- give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise
- give angles in degrees to 1 decimal place
- show the unrounded value in your working, then the rounded answer, for example 2.4555... then 2.46.

For money, round to the nearest cent. If the question asks for a whole number of items (for example, a number of coaches), think about whether to round up or down.

## Exam technique

- Write each step on paper, with the unrounded result. If you key in a wrong number, the unrounded value in your working may still earn a method mark.
- Work out the numerator and the denominator as separate lines, or use brackets and check the display.
- Estimate first (see the estimation guide). If the calculator answer is far from your estimate, you have probably mis-keyed.
- Do not use a graphical or algebraic calculator on the exam.

## Common mistakes

- Rounding in the middle of a calculation.
- Missing brackets round a denominator or a fractional power.
- Entering 2 hours 30 minutes as 2.30.
- Reading 3.25 hours as 3 hours 25 minutes.
- Writing a money answer as 4.8 rather than $4.80.
- Using the subtraction key instead of the negative key.
- Copying a calculator display such as 1.47E4.

## Where this leads

Careful calculator use supports every calculation topic on Paper 2: percentages, interest, trigonometry, mensuration, statistics and standard form.
