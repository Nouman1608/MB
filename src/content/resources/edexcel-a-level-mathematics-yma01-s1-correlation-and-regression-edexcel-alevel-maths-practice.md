## Section A

**1.** A researcher records the amount of fertiliser applied to plots of land and the mass of crop harvested from each plot. State, with a reason, which is the explanatory variable. **[2]**

**2.** The PMCC between two variables is 0.03, but the scatter diagram shows the points lying close to a U-shaped curve. Explain how both observations can be true. **[2]**

**3.** The regression line of y on x is y = 4.2 + 1.6x, and ȳ = 20. Find x̄. Given also that Sxx = 40, find Sxy. **[3]**

**4.** Data are coded by p = x − 1000 and q = (y − 3)/0.5. The PMCC between p and q is −0.74. State the PMCC between x and y, with a reason. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** For 10 pairs of values, Σx = 150, Σy = 420, Σx² = 2450, Σy² = 18 400 and Σxy = 6560.

**(a)** Find Sxx, Syy and Sxy. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the product moment correlation coefficient. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the equation of the regression line of y on x. **[3]**

**6.** The table shows x, the number of months since a phone battery was new, and y, the number of hours it lasts on a full charge.

| x | 1 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 30 | 27 | 22 | 20 | 14 | 9 |

You are given Σx² = 176, Σy² = 2790 and Σxy = 451.

**(a)** Find Sxx, Syy and Sxy. **[3]**

**(b)** Find r and interpret it in context. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the regression line of y on x. **[3]**

**(d)** Interpret the gradient in context. **[1]**

**(e)** Estimate y when x = 6 and when x = 15, commenting on the reliability of each estimate. **[4]**

**7.** Data on x and y are coded by p = (x − 20)/2 and q = (y − 100)/4. The coded data give Spp = 25, Sqq = 64, Spq = 36, p̄ = 1.5 and q̄ = 2.

**(a)** Find the PMCC between x and y. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the regression line of q on p. **[3]**

**(c)** Hence find the regression line of y on x. **[3]**

**8.** A study of 30 towns finds a PMCC of 0.91 between the number of cafés and the number of road accidents per year. A newspaper claims that cafés cause accidents. Comment on this claim. **[2]**

**9.** A company finds the regression line of monthly sales s (thousands of units) on advertising spend a (thousands of pounds), using values of a between 5 and 20: s = 3.4 + 0.75a.

**(a)** Interpret the value 0.75 in context. **[1]**

**(b)** The company plans to spend £60 000 on advertising. Explain why the line should not be relied on to predict sales. **[2]**

**10.** State two limitations of the product moment correlation coefficient. **[2]**

**11.** A data set of 12 points has r = 0.35. One further point, far from the others, is added and r becomes 0.82. Explain what this shows about the PMCC. **[2]**

**12.** Data are coded using u = (x − 5)/2 and v = (y − 30)/10. The coded summary statistics are Suu = 18 and Suv = −27. Find Sxx and Sxy, and hence the gradient of the regression line of y on x. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The amount of fertiliser [1], because it is chosen by the researcher and is used to explain the mass of crop, which responds to it [1].

**2.** The PMCC measures only linear association [1]. Points on a U-shaped curve have a strong non-linear relationship but almost no linear trend, so r is close to 0 [1].

**3.** The line passes through (x̄, ȳ): 20 = 4.2 + 1.6x̄ [1], so **x̄ = 9.875** [1]. Sxy = b × Sxx = 1.6 × 40 = **64** [1].

**4.** **r = −0.74** [1], because linear coding with positive scale factors does not change the PMCC [1].

**5. (a)** Sxx = 2450 − 150²/10 = **200**; Syy = 18 400 − 420²/10 = **760** [1] [1]; Sxy = 6560 − 150 × 420/10 = **260** [1].

**(b)** r = 260/√(200 × 760) [1] = **0.667** [1].

**(c)** b = 260/200 = 1.3 [1]; a = 42 − 1.3 × 15 = 22.5 [1]; **y = 22.5 + 1.3x** [1].

**6. (a)** Σx = 28 and Σy = 122. Sxx = 176 − 28²/6 = **45.33**; Syy = 2790 − 122²/6 = **309.3** [1] [1]; Sxy = 451 − 28 × 122/6 = **−118.3** [1].

**(b)** r = −118.33/√(45.33 × 309.33) [1] = **−0.999** [1]. Very strong negative linear correlation: the older the battery, the fewer hours it lasts on a charge [1].

**(c)** b = −118.33/45.33 = −2.610 [1]; a = 20.33 − (−2.610)(4.667) = 32.51 [1]; **y = 32.5 − 2.61x** [1].

**(d)** Each extra month of age reduces the time the battery lasts by about 2.6 hours [1].

**(e)** x = 6: y = 32.51 − 2.610 × 6 = **16.9 hours** [1], reliable because 6 is within the data range and r is close to −1 [1]. x = 15: y = 32.51 − 2.610 × 15 = **−6.6 hours** [1], which is impossible; this is extrapolation beyond the data, so it is unreliable [1].

**7. (a)** r = 36/√(25 × 64) [1] = **0.9**; coding does not change r [1].

**(b)** b = 36/25 = 1.44 [1]; a = 2 − 1.44 × 1.5 = −0.16 [1]; **q = −0.16 + 1.44p** [1].

**(c)** (y − 100)/4 = −0.16 + 1.44(x − 20)/2 [1], so y − 100 = −0.64 + 2.88(x − 20) [1], giving **y = 41.76 + 2.88x** [1].

**8.** A high r shows strong positive linear association, not causation [1]. Both are likely to depend on a third variable, such as the size of the town's population [1].

**9. (a)** Each extra £1000 spent on advertising is associated with about 750 extra units sold per month [1].

**(b)** £60 000 means a = 60, far outside the range 5 to 20 used to find the line [1]; this is extrapolation, and the linear relationship may not continue [1].

**10.** Any two: it measures only linear association; it is affected by outliers; correlation does not imply causation; it tells you nothing outside the range of the data [1] [1].

**11.** A single extreme point has changed r substantially [1], so the PMCC is sensitive to outliers and r should be interpreted alongside the scatter diagram [1].

**12.** Sxx = 2² × 18 = **72** [1]. Sxy = 2 × 10 × (−27) = **−540** [1]. Gradient b = −540/72 = **−7.5** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by Syy instead of Sxx for the gradient.
- Rounding b too early in Question 6, which shifts a.
- Interpreting the gradient without units or context.
- Calling the estimate at x = 15 in Question 6 merely "less accurate" instead of explaining it is extrapolation.
- Converting a coded line by changing the gradient and intercept separately instead of substituting.
