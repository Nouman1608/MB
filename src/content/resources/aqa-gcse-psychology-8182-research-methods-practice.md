## Section A

**1.** A researcher selects every 10th name from a school register. Which sampling method is this? **[1]**

A. Random
B. Opportunity
C. Systematic
D. Stratified

**2.** A psychologist tests whether drinking a cup of coffee affects how quickly people press a button when a light comes on.

**(a)** Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable, and operationalise the dependent variable. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a suitable null hypothesis for this study. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify **one** extraneous variable that should be controlled. **[1]**

**3.** Explain the difference between an **open** and a **closed** question, giving an example of each that could be used in a questionnaire about sleep. **[4]**

**4.** In a study, 18 of the 24 participants recalled the target word.

**(a)** Express this as a percentage. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the ratio of participants who recalled the word to those who did not, in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(c)** In a case study report, the proportion of people with a rare memory condition is given as 0.00045. Write this number in standard form. **[1]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by **interobserver reliability** and how a researcher could check it. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A researcher tested whether background music affects memory. Participants learned 15 words with music playing or in silence. The table shows the number of words each participant recalled.

| Music | 8 | 10 | 7 | 12 | 9 | 10 | 6 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Silence** | 11 | 13 | 12 | 10 | 14 | 12 | 9 | 15 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean, median, mode and range for the **music** condition. **[4]**
**(b)** The mean for the silence condition is 12. Using the means, state what the results suggest. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain **one** reason why the range is useful alongside the mean. **[1]**

**7.** A school has 600 students: 360 girls and 240 boys. A psychologist wants a **stratified** sample of 50 students.

**(a)** Calculate how many girls and how many boys should be in the sample. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** weakness of stratified sampling. **[2]**

**8.** A researcher finds that the more time teenagers spend on screens in the evening, the fewer hours they sleep.

**(a)** Identify the type of correlation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the researcher cannot conclude that screen time causes less sleep. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** strength of using a correlation in this study. **[1]**

**9.** In a repeated measures study, every participant completes a memory test in a quiet room and then the same kind of test in a noisy room.

**(a)** Explain how **order effects** could affect the results. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the researcher could use **counterbalancing** to deal with this. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the characteristics of a **normal distribution**. **[3]**

**11.** Plan a field experiment to investigate whether people are more likely to pick up litter when a sign asks them to. In your answer, refer to:

- the independent and dependent variables
- the experimental design and how participants are sampled
- the procedure
- **one** ethical issue and how you would deal with it. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** (systematic) [1].

**2. (a)** IV: whether the participant **drinks coffee or not** [1]. DV: **reaction time in milliseconds** from the light coming on to the button press [1].
**(b)** "There will be **no difference** in reaction time between participants who drink a cup of coffee and those who do not." [1]
**(c)** Any one: amount of sleep the night before; time of day; usual caffeine intake; practice with the task [1].

**3.** An **open question** lets participants answer in **their own words**, producing **qualitative** data [1], e.g. "Describe how you feel when you wake up." [1] A **closed question** has **fixed answer options**, producing **quantitative** data [1], e.g. "How many hours did you sleep last night? Under 6 / 6-8 / over 8" [1].

**4. (a)** 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = **75%** [1].
**(b)** 18 recalled, 6 did not: 18:6 = **3:1** [1].
**(c)** **4.5 × 10⁻⁴** [1].

**5.** Interobserver reliability is the **extent to which two or more observers agree** in their recordings of the same behaviour [1]. To check it, **two observers watch the same behaviour separately, using the same behaviour categories** [1], then **compare their records**; a high level of agreement (or a strong positive correlation between their tallies) shows good reliability [1].

**6. (a)** Mean: total 72 ÷ 8 = **9** [1]. Median: in order 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10, 10, 12; middle values 9 and 10, so **9.5** [1]. Mode: **10** [1]. Range: 12 - 6 = **6** [1].
**(b)** Participants recalled **more words in silence** (mean 12) than with music (mean 9), suggesting **music may reduce recall** [1].
**(c)** The range shows the **spread** of scores, so it tells the researcher whether the mean represents scores that are close together or widely spread, which the mean alone cannot show [1].

**7. (a)** Girls: 360 ÷ 600 × 50 = **30** [1]. Boys: 240 ÷ 600 × 50 = **20** [1].
**(b)** Any one, developed: it needs **detailed knowledge of the population** in advance [1], which can be **time-consuming or impossible** to obtain [1]. Or: within each stratum the individuals still have to be chosen, and if this is not done randomly **bias can still occur** [1] [1]. Or: only the chosen strata (here, gender) are represented, so **other differences** in the population may be missed [1] [1].

**8. (a)** **Negative** correlation [1].
**(b)** A correlation only shows that two variables are **related**; neither was **manipulated**, so cause cannot be established [1]. A **third variable**, such as stress about schoolwork, could lead to both more screen time and less sleep (or the direction could be reversed: teenagers who cannot sleep may use screens more) [1].
**(c)** Any one: it can study variables that would be **unethical or impractical to manipulate**, such as sleep loss [1]; it uses existing data, so it can be **quick** and can show where experiments could be done [1].

**9. (a)** Because everyone does the noisy condition **second**, they may do better through **practice** [1] or worse through **fatigue or boredom** [1], so differences may be caused by the order rather than the noise.
**(b)** **Half** the participants do the quiet room first and then the noisy room; **the other half** do the noisy room first [1]. Any practice or fatigue effects are then **balanced across both conditions**, so they do not favour one condition [1].

**10.** Any three, 1 mark each: the curve is **symmetrical** [1]; it is **bell-shaped** [1]; the **mean, median and mode are equal** and lie at the **centre** [1]; **most scores fall close to the mean**, with fewer and fewer towards the extremes [1]; the tails approach but do not touch the horizontal axis [1].

**11.** Marked by levels.

*Indicative content:* **IV** -- sign present ("Please pick up litter") or absent; **DV** -- whether each passer-by picks up a planted piece of litter (operationalised, e.g. picks it up within 5 metres); **design** -- independent groups, because different passers-by are in each condition; **sampling** -- opportunity sample of people walking through, for example, a park path; **procedure** -- the same piece of litter placed in the same spot; the sign alternated on different days at the same time of day; a hidden observer records each passer-by using a tally; standardise weather, time and location; **ethics** -- people cannot give **consent** in a field experiment, so the study should only record public behaviour, keep data **anonymous** (no names or photos) and involve no more risk than everyday life (**protection from harm**); or debrief where possible.

- **Level 3 (5-6):** a clear, workable plan covering all four bullet points, with appropriate detail and terminology.
- **Level 2 (3-4):** a reasonable plan with some detail; one bullet point may be missing or weak.
- **Level 1 (1-2):** a limited plan; several points missing or unclear.
- **0:** no relevant content.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a DV without saying how it is **measured**.
- Calculating the median without first putting the scores in **order**.
- Forgetting to show working in sampling calculations.
- Saying "correlation does not equal causation" without explaining a **third variable** or reversed direction.
- Naming an ethical issue in a plan without a **practical** way to deal with it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What research methods skills do these practice questions cover?**
They cover AQA GCSE Psychology 8182 section 3.1.4: hypotheses, variables, sampling, designs, correlation, observation, ethics, descriptive statistics, percentages, ratios, standard form and planning a study.

**How do I calculate the median with an even number of scores?**
Put the scores in order, find the two middle values, add them together and divide by two.
