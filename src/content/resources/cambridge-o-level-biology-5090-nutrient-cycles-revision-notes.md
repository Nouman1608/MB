These notes condense subtopic **19.2 Nutrient cycles** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the carbon cycle, the nitrogen cycle, and the role of fungi and bacteria in decomposition. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [19.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-nutrient-cycles/). Then try the [19.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-nutrient-cycles-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Energy | Flows through an ecosystem and is lost as heat |
| Nutrients | Recycled; the same atoms are used again |
| Decomposers | Fungi and bacteria that break down dead matter and waste |
| Nitrogen in air | About 78 per cent, but most organisms cannot use it directly |

## Carbon cycle

| Process | Effect on carbon dioxide in the air |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Removes it |
| Respiration (all organisms) | Adds it |
| Decomposition | Adds it (decomposers respire) |
| Combustion | Adds it |
| Feeding | Moves carbon along food chains (no change in the air) |
| Formation of fossil fuels | Stores carbon over millions of years |

**Summary path:** carbon dioxide in air → photosynthesis → carbohydrates in plants → feeding → animals → respiration, decomposition and combustion → carbon dioxide in air.

## Nitrogen cycle: stages in order

| Stage | Change |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen fixation | Nitrogen gas to nitrogen compounds, by lightning and by bacteria (in soil and in roots of legumes) |
| Absorption | Plants take up nitrate ions through the roots |
| Protein production | Plants make amino acids and proteins from nitrate |
| Feeding and digestion | Animals eat protein and digest it to amino acids |
| Decomposition | Dead matter and waste give ammonium ions |
| Nitrification | Ammonium ions to nitrate ions (bacteria, with oxygen) |
| Denitrification | Nitrate ions to nitrogen gas (bacteria, in waterlogged soil) |

## Decomposition

- Fungi and bacteria release enzymes onto dead matter and waste.
- The enzymes digest it to soluble molecules, which are absorbed.
- Decomposers respire, releasing carbon dioxide.
- Mineral ions such as ammonium return to the soil.
- It is faster when warm, moist and with oxygen.

## Worked examples

**Rate of decay.** Leaf litter lost 4 g at 10 degrees Celsius and 12 g at 20 degrees Celsius. The increase is 12 - 4 = 8 g, which is 8 divided by 4 x 100 = 200 per cent.

**Waterlogged soil.** Denitrification increases, so nitrate falls, so plants make less protein and grow poorly.

**Legumes.** Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the root nodules provide nitrogen compounds, so legumes can grow in poor soil and enrich it when they decay.

## Which organisms do what in the nitrogen cycle

| Group | Role |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (soil and legume roots) | Change nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds |
| Plants | Absorb nitrate ions; make amino acids and proteins |
| Animals | Eat and digest protein; make their own proteins |
| Decomposers (fungi and bacteria) | Break down protein and urea to ammonium ions |
| Nitrifying bacteria | Change ammonium ions to nitrate ions (need oxygen) |
| Denitrifying bacteria | Change nitrate ions to nitrogen gas (waterlogged soil) |

## Human effects linked to the cycles

- Burning fossil fuels and cutting down forests increases carbon dioxide in the air.
- Fertilisers add nitrate ions to the soil, which helps crops to grow.
- Planting legumes such as clover or beans improves the nitrogen content of the soil without fertiliser.
- Draining waterlogged land reduces denitrification and keeps nitrate in the soil.
- Composting uses decomposers to recycle plant waste into nutrients.

## Putting a cycle diagram into words

A diagram question may give the boxes and ask for the labels on the arrows. Work out each arrow with these tests.

1. Is a plant taking something up, or giving it out? Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide in; absorption takes nitrate in.
2. Is an animal eating? Then the arrow is feeding.
3. Is a living thing releasing carbon dioxide? Then the arrow is respiration (or decomposition if the organism is a decomposer).
4. Is dead matter going to ammonium ions? Then it is decomposition.
5. Is ammonium becoming nitrate? Nitrification.
6. Is nitrate becoming nitrogen gas? Denitrification.
7. Is nitrogen gas becoming a compound? Nitrogen fixation.

## Worked calculation

*Compost heaps are weighed. A heap loses 24 kg of its 80 kg in 8 weeks in summer and 6 kg in 8 weeks in winter. Find the percentage lost in each season.*

1. Summer: 24 divided by 80 x 100 = 30 per cent.
2. Winter: 6 divided by 80 x 100 = 7.5 per cent.

Decomposition is faster in warm conditions because the enzymes in the fungi and bacteria work faster.

## Short model answers

**Describe the carbon cycle.** Plants take in carbon dioxide in photosynthesis to make carbohydrates. Carbon passes to animals by feeding. Carbon dioxide is returned to the air by respiration, decomposition and combustion. Some carbon is stored in fossil fuels.

**Explain why plants need nitrate ions.** They use nitrate ions to make amino acids, which are built into proteins.

**State the role of nitrifying bacteria.** They change ammonium ions into nitrate ions.

**State the role of denitrifying bacteria.** They change nitrate ions into nitrogen gas, which returns to the air.

**Outline the role of decomposers.** They break down dead organisms and waste, releasing carbon dioxide and ammonium ions so that nutrients are recycled.

## Exam traps

- Plants take up **nitrate ions**, not nitrogen gas.
- **Nitrification** makes nitrate. **Denitrification** removes nitrate.
- Decomposers respire, so they **release carbon dioxide**.
- Fossil fuels take **millions of years** to form.
- Combustion releases carbon dioxide; it does not remove it.
- Energy flows; nutrients are **recycled**.
- Bacteria names are not required.
- Include the compounds formed (ammonium, nitrate), not only the process names.

## Self-test

1. Which process removes carbon dioxide from the air?
2. Name three processes that add carbon dioxide to the air.
3. What is stored in fossil fuels?
4. What percentage of the air is nitrogen (approximately)?
5. In what form do plants absorb nitrogen?
6. What do plants make from nitrate ions?
7. Name two ways in which nitrogen gas can be fixed.
8. What is produced when decomposers break down protein?
9. What is nitrification?
10. What is denitrification?
11. Why do decomposers release carbon dioxide?
12. Which conditions speed up decomposition?

### Answers

1. Photosynthesis.
2. Any three of: respiration; decomposition; combustion.
3. Carbon, from the remains of dead organisms.
4. About 78 per cent.
5. As nitrate ions.
6. Amino acids and then proteins.
7. By lightning and by nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
8. Ammonium ions.
9. The change of ammonium ions to nitrate ions by bacteria.
10. The change of nitrate ions to nitrogen gas by bacteria.
11. They respire.
12. Warmth, moisture and oxygen.
