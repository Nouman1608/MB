OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Accounting (9615) is a modular qualification for students who
want to understand how businesses record, report and use financial information. It starts with the
role of the accountant and the double entry model, and builds towards costing methods, investment
appraisal, the accounts of different types of organisation and the ethical questions accountants
face. The full A-level is assessed by four written papers: two AS papers of 120 minutes and 80 marks,
each worth 20% of the A-level, and two A2 papers of 135 minutes and 90 marks, each worth 30%. The
specification was first taught in September 2024, with first AS exams in May/June 2025 and first
A-level exams in May/June 2026.

## How the course is organised

The content is set out in twenty topics, split between the AS and A2 stages.

**AS stage (topics 1 to 10).** The AS opens with an introduction to the role of the accountant in
business and the types of business organisation, then moves to the technical core: the double entry
model, verification of accounting records, and the accounting concepts used in preparing them. With
those foundations in place, students prepare the financial statements of sole traders and limited
company accounts, analyse and evaluate financial information, and are introduced to management
accounting through budgeting and marginal costing.

**A2 stage (topics 11 to 20).** The second stage develops both sides of the subject further. On the
management accounting side it covers standard costing and variance analysis, absorption and activity
based costing, and capital investment appraisal. On the financial accounting side it covers
accounting for organisations with incomplete records, partnership accounts, accounting for limited
companies, manufacturing accounts, and clubs and non-profit making organisations. It finishes with
the interpretation, analysis and communication of accounting information and the impact of ethical
considerations.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Introduction to Financial Accounting | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Financial & Management Accounting | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Financial Accounting | 135 minutes | 90 | 30% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Accounting for Analysis and Decision Making | 135 minutes | 90 | 30% |

The qualification is modular. The two AS papers are taken at the end of the AS stage, and each is
worth 50% of the AS award as well as 20% of the full A-level. The two A2 papers complete the A-level,
contributing 30% each. All four are externally assessed written exams, and the course is untiered.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles show the shape of the course. Financial accounting, recording transactions and
preparing statements, runs through both stages, while management accounting, using information to
plan and decide, is introduced at AS and deepened at A2. The final A2 paper, Accounting for Analysis
and Decision Making, brings the two together.

Several threads connect topics across the stages. The double entry model and the accounting concepts
from AS underpin every set of financial statements that follows, from sole traders to partnerships,
limited companies, manufacturers and clubs. Incomplete records at A2 is essentially double entry
worked backwards, so weak first-year bookkeeping becomes visible quickly. Marginal costing at AS
leads naturally into absorption and activity based costing and standard costing at A2, and
budgeting connects to variance analysis because variances compare actual results with a plan.

Analysis and evaluation also run throughout. At AS, students interpret financial information; at
A2, they interpret, analyse and communicate it, and weigh ethical considerations. Written judgement,
not just accurate calculation, is part of what the course develops.

## Planning your study

In the first year, follow the AS topics broadly in order, because each depends on the one before.
Spend real time on the double entry model and verification of records; errors at that stage spread
into every later statement. Prepare sole trader statements until the layout is automatic before
moving to limited company accounts, and leave room at the end of the year for budgeting and
marginal costing, which use a different style of thinking.

In the second year, a sensible approach is to alternate between the financial and management
accounting topics rather than finishing one side completely before starting the other, since both
A2 papers draw on a range of content. Keep returning to AS techniques, particularly double entry and
the preparation of statements, as you meet partnerships, manufacturing accounts and clubs.

Start timed practice on full papers once each stage's content has been taught, and use the
exam-preparation page for how the four papers fit together and how to manage them.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International A-level Accounting?**
Four. There are two AS papers, each 120 minutes and 80 marks, and two A2 papers, each 135 minutes and 90 marks.

**How are the 9615 papers weighted?**
Each AS paper is worth 20% of the A-level (and 50% of the AS), and each A2 paper is worth 30% of the A-level.

**Is OxfordAQA A-level Accounting modular?**
Yes. The AS papers are taken at the end of the AS stage and the A2 papers at the end of the course, with the four papers making up the full A-level.

**When was 9615 first examined?**
First teaching was September 2024. The first AS exams were in May/June 2025 and the first A-level exams in May/June 2026.

**Does the course include management accounting?**
Yes. Budgeting and marginal costing are AS topics, and standard costing and variance analysis, absorption and activity based costing, and capital investment appraisal are A2 topics.
