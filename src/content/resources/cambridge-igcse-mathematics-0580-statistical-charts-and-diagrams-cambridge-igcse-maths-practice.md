These questions cover section 9.4, Statistical charts and diagrams, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. C9.4 and E9.4 are identical, so every question suits Core and Extended candidates. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Diagrams are described in words or as tables so you can sketch them yourself. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [statistical charts and diagrams study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-statistical-charts-and-diagrams/) and the [statistical charts revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-statistical-charts-and-diagrams-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) 90 people chose their favourite drink: tea 31, coffee 25, juice 18, water 16. Calculate the angle of each sector for a pie chart. **[3]**

**2.** (non-calculator) In a pictogram, one symbol represents 12 books. Monday shows 3½ symbols and Tuesday shows 2¾ symbols.

**(a)** How many books does Tuesday represent? **[1]**

**(b)** How many more books does Monday represent than Tuesday? **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) The marks of 12 students are:

```
47  52  38  61  45  59  52  40  36  55  63  49
```

Draw an ordered stem-and-leaf diagram for the marks, with a key. **[3]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A composite bar chart shows the members of a club. The bar for juniors has three sections: swimming from 0 to 18, tennis from 18 to 30, and athletics from 30 to 44.

**(a)** How many juniors do athletics? **[1]**

**(b)** How many juniors are in the club? **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) In a pie chart, a sector of 72° represents 18 people.

**(a)** How many people does the whole pie chart represent? **[1]**

**(b)** How many people does a sector of 120° represent? **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) The four sector angles of a pie chart are 80°, x, 2x and 3x + 40°. Find x and the largest angle. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** (calculator) A shop recorded its ice-cream sales in June and July.

```
           Vanilla   Chocolate   Strawberry   Total
June          40         55           25        120
July          60         70           30        160
```

**(a)** A composite bar chart is drawn with one bar for each month. Write down where each section of the July bar starts and ends. **[2]**

**(b)** Which flavour had the largest percentage increase in sales from June to July? Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** The shop wants to compare chocolate sales in June and July. Which is more suitable, a dual bar chart or a composite bar chart? Give a reason. **[1]**

**8.** (calculator) 120 students at School A were asked how they travel to school: walk 38, bus 45, car 22, cycle 15.

**(a)** Calculate the sector angles for a pie chart. **[3]**

**(b)** At School B there are 200 students. In School B's pie chart, the bus sector is 81°. How many School B students travel by bus? **[2]**

**(c)** Leo says "School A's bus sector is bigger, so more School A students travel by bus." Use your answers to comment on Leo's statement. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) The stem-and-leaf diagram shows the times, in seconds, taken by 15 people to solve a puzzle.

```
2 | 5 8
3 | 0 3 3 7
4 | 1 4 6 9
5 | 2 2 2 8
6 | 4
Key: 3 | 0 means 30 seconds
```

**(a)** Find the median time. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the range. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the mode. **[1]**

**(d)** What fraction of the people took more than 50 seconds? **[1]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A pictogram shows the visitors to a museum on four days. One symbol represents 8 visitors.

```
Thursday   ● ● ●
Friday     ● ● ● ◕
Saturday   (not yet drawn)
Sunday     ● ● ● ● ◑
Key: ● = 8 visitors; part symbols are quarters
```

**(a)** How many visitors came on Friday? **[1]**

**(b)** The total for the four days was 150 visitors. How many came on Saturday? **[2]**

**(c)** How many symbols should be drawn for Saturday? **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A dice is rolled 20 times. The scores are:

```
3  6  1  4  4  2  6  5  3  4
1  6  2  4  5  3  6  4  2  5
```

**(a)** Make a frequency table for the scores. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your table to find the total of all 20 scores. **[2]**

**(c)** What fraction of the rolls gave a score greater than 4? **[1]**

**12.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** A student draws this stem-and-leaf diagram for the values 14, 27, 19, 22, 11, 25. Write down two mistakes. **[2]**

```
1 | 4 9 1
2 | 7 2 5
```

**(b)** A pie chart has three sectors with angles 150°, 120° and 80°. Explain why it cannot be correct. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 360 ÷ 90 = 4° per person [1]; tea **124°**, coffee **100°** [1]; juice **72°**, water **64°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check that 124 + 100 + 72 + 64 = 360 before drawing; a total of 358° or 362° means an arithmetic slip.

**2. (a)** 2.75 × 12 = **33** [1]

**(b)** Monday = 3.5 × 12 = 42, so 42 − 33 = **9** [1] **[2]**

**3.** Stems 3, 4, 5, 6 with leaves [1]; leaves in order: 3 | 6 8, 4 | 0 5 7 9, 5 | 2 2 5 9, 6 | 1 3 [1]; key, for example **3 | 6 means 36** [1]

**4. (a)** 44 − 30 = **14** [1]

**(b)** **44** (the top of the full bar) [1] **[2]**

**5. (a)** 72° is one fifth of 360°, so 18 × 5 = **90** people [1]

**(b)** 120° is one third of 360°: 90 ÷ 3 = **30** people [1] **[2]**

**6.** 80 + x + 2x + 3x + 40 = 360, so 6x = 240 and **x = 40°** [1]; angles 80°, 40°, 80°, 160°, so the largest is **160°** [1]

**7. (a)** Vanilla from **0 to 60** [1]; chocolate from **60 to 130** and strawberry from **130 to 160** [1]

**(b)** Increases: vanilla 20 ÷ 40 = 50%, chocolate 15 ÷ 55 = 27.3%, strawberry 5 ÷ 25 = 20% [1]; **vanilla** [1]

**(c)** **Dual**: both chocolate bars start at 0, so their heights can be compared directly [1] **[5]**

**8. (a)** 360 ÷ 120 = 3° per student [1]; walk **114°**, bus **135°** [1]; car **66°**, cycle **45°** [1]

**(b)** 81 ÷ 360 × 200 [1] = **45** students [1]

**(c)** Leo is wrong: **45 students travel by bus at each school**; School A's sector is bigger only because School A has fewer students in total [1] **[6]**

**9. (a)** The 8th value: **44 seconds** [1]

**(b)** 64 − 25 = **39 seconds** [1]

**(c)** **52 seconds** [1]

**(d)** 52, 52, 52, 58, 64: **5/15 = 1/3** [1] **[4]**

**10. (a)** 3¾ × 8 = **30** [1]

**(b)** Thursday 24, Sunday 4½ × 8 = 36; 24 + 30 + 36 = 90 [1]; 150 − 90 = **60** [1]

**(c)** 60 ÷ 8 = **7½ symbols** [1] **[4]**

**11. (a)** Scores 1 to 6 listed [1]; frequencies 1: **2**, 2: **3**, 3: **3**, 4: **5**, 5: **3**, 6: **4** (total 20) [1]

**(b)** 1 × 2 + 2 × 3 + 3 × 3 + 4 × 5 + 5 × 3 + 6 × 4 [1] = 2 + 6 + 9 + 20 + 15 + 24 = **76** [1]

**(c)** 3 + 4 = 7, so **7/20** [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** Any two [1] [1]: the leaves are **not in order** (should be 1 | 1 4 9 and 2 | 2 5 7); there is **no key**

**(b)** 150 + 120 + 80 = 350°, but the angles of a pie chart must add up to **360°** [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Pie chart angles that do not total 360°, or sectors with no labels.
- Reading the top of a stacked section instead of top minus bottom.
- Counting pictogram symbols without using the key, especially part-symbols.
- Stem-and-leaf diagrams without ordered leaves or a key.
- Comparing pie charts by angle when the two totals are different.
