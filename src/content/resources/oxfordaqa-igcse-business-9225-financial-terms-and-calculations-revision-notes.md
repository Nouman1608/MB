Condensed recall for section 3.6.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-financial-terms-and-calculations/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Variable, fixed and total costs**; **revenue, costs, profit and loss**.
- **Investment projects** (new machinery, buildings, vehicles) and the **payback period**.
- **Break-even output**; **interpret break-even charts**; identify **break-even output and margin of safety** from a chart; **evaluate** break-even analysis.
- You will **not** draw break-even charts or use the break-even formula.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Do not change with output (rent, insurance) |
| Variable costs | Change with output (materials, packaging) |
| Total costs | Fixed + variable costs |
| Revenue | Price x quantity sold |
| Profit | Revenue - total costs (when positive) |
| Loss | Total costs - revenue (when costs are higher) |
| Break-even output | Output where revenue = total costs |
| Margin of safety | Actual output - break-even output |
| Payback period | Time for an investment's net cash flows to equal its cost |
| Investment project | Spending on machinery, buildings or vehicles to earn more later |

## Formulae to learn

- Variable costs = variable cost per unit x output
- Total costs = fixed costs + variable costs
- Revenue = price x quantity
- Profit = revenue - total costs
- Margin of safety = actual output - break-even output
- Payback = years until cumulative net cash flow reaches the cost (add the fraction of the last year)

## Break-even chart: how to read it

- **Horizontal axis:** output (units). **Vertical axis:** money.
- **Fixed costs line:** horizontal.
- **Total costs line:** starts at the fixed costs level and slopes up.
- **Revenue line:** starts at zero and slopes up more steeply.
- **Break-even point:** where revenue and total costs cross. Read down for output, across for money.
- **Left of break-even:** loss. **Right of break-even:** profit.
- **Margin of safety:** distance between actual output and break-even output.

## Worked example

Price $20; variable cost $8; fixed costs $18,000.

| Output | Revenue | Total costs | Profit or loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 20,000 | 26,000 | -6,000 |
| 1,500 | 30,000 | 30,000 | 0 |
| 2,000 | 40,000 | 34,000 | 6,000 |

Break-even = 1,500 units. Expected sales 2,200: margin of safety = 2,200 - 1,500 = 700 units (31.8%). Profit = 700 x $12 = $8,400.

## What moves break-even?

| Change | Break-even |
|---|---|
| Price up | Falls |
| Variable costs up | Rises |
| Fixed costs up | Rises |
| Fixed costs down | Falls |

## Evaluating break-even analysis

| Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Shows sales needed to avoid a loss | Assumes all output is sold |
| Helps set price and targets | Assumes constant price and costs |
| Shows margin of safety and risk | Estimates may be wrong |
| Supports plans and loan applications | Hard with many products |
| Easy "what if" changes | Ignores competitors and demand |

## Payback period

Machine costing $60,000 with net cash flows of $12,000, $18,000, $24,000, $24,000:

| Year | Cumulative |
|---|---|
| 1 | 12,000 |
| 2 | 30,000 |
| 3 | 54,000 |
| 4 | 78,000 |

After 3 years $6,000 remains; 6,000 / 24,000 = 0.25. **Payback = 3 years 3 months.**

Even cash flows: payback = cost / annual net cash flow. $40,000 / $10,000 = 4 years.

**Evaluation:** shorter is usually better, but payback ignores profits after the payback date and the timing of cash flows.

## Exam traps

- Total costs start at the **fixed cost** level, not zero.
- Fixed costs do not change with output; variable costs do.
- Margin of safety is a **number of units** above break-even, not profit.
- Output is on the horizontal axis.
- For payback, use **cumulative** cash flows and include the fraction of the final year.
- Break-even is a guide, not a prediction.

## Model answer outline: a 9-mark "evaluate" question

Question style: "Evaluate the usefulness of break-even analysis to a new juice business."

- **Useful:** shows how many bottles must be sold (6,000) to avoid a loss; shows the margin of safety; helps set price and sales targets; supports a loan application.
- **Limits:** assumes all output is sold and prices and costs stay the same; based on estimates; ignores competitors.
- **Calculation:** margin of safety = 8,500 - 6,000 = 2,500 bottles.
- **Judgement:** it is a useful guide for a new business, but should be combined with market research, and updated as costs and prices change.

## Revision checklist

- I can define fixed, variable and total costs, revenue, profit and loss.
- I can calculate profit from costs and revenue.
- I can read break-even output from a table or chart.
- I can calculate the margin of safety.
- I can say how price and cost changes affect break-even.
- I can calculate the payback period, including part-years.
- I can state advantages and limits of break-even and payback.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between fixed and variable costs.
2. Calculate total costs for 400 units with fixed costs of $2,000 and variable costs of $5 a unit.
3. Calculate the revenue from 400 units at $12.
4. Using Questions 2 and 3, calculate the profit or loss.
5. Define break-even output.
6. On a chart, what does the point where the revenue and total cost lines cross show?
7. Break-even is 800 units and the firm expects to sell 1,100. Calculate the margin of safety.
8. State what happens to break-even if fixed costs rise.
9. Machine A costs $20,000 and brings $5,000 a year. Calculate the payback.
10. Give one limitation of break-even analysis.
11. Give one limitation of the payback period.
12. A vehicle costs $24,000. Net cash flows are $8,000, $10,000, $10,000. Calculate payback.

### Answers

1. Fixed costs do not change with output; variable costs change with output.
2. Variable = 400 x $5 = $2,000; total = $2,000 + $2,000 = **$4,000**.
3. 400 x $12 = **$4,800**.
4. $4,800 - $4,000 = **$800 profit**.
5. The output at which revenue equals total costs, so there is no profit or loss.
6. The break-even point.
7. 1,100 - 800 = **300 units**.
8. It rises, because more must be recovered before profit.
9. $20,000 / $5,000 = **4 years**.
10. Any one: assumes everything made is sold; assumes constant prices and costs; relies on estimates.
11. Any one: ignores profit after payback; ignores timing within the period; relies on forecasts.
12. After 2 years: $18,000; $6,000 remains; year 3 brings $10,000, so 6,000 / 10,000 = 0.6 year. Payback = **2.6 years** (2 years 7 months to the nearest month).
