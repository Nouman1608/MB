These notes condense **3.2.1.3 Inventory and supply chain management** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). Worked chart examples are in the [inventory and supply chain management study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-inventory-and-supply-chain-management-9725/); exam-style questions are in the [inventory and supply chain management practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-inventory-and-supply-chain-management-9725-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **Inventory**: reasons to hold it; influences on the amount; control charts (lead time, re-order quantity, re-order level, buffer stock); JIT versus JIC; value of effective control.
- **Supply chain**: purpose and value of managing it (supplier relationships, logistics in and out); influences on sourcing, including number and choice of suppliers; impact of supply chain issues; transparency and third party accreditation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Stocks of raw materials, work in progress and finished goods |
| Buffer stock | The minimum inventory kept as a safety margin |
| Lead time | Time between placing an order and receiving it |
| Re-order level | The inventory level at which a new order is placed |
| Re-order quantity | The amount ordered each time |
| Maximum inventory level | The highest level inventory reaches, just after a delivery |
| Stock-out | Running out of inventory, so orders or production cannot be met |
| Just in time (JIT) | Inputs arrive only as needed; minimal inventory |
| Just in case (JIC) | Large inventory held as protection against disruption or demand surges |
| Supply chain | All the stages and organisations from raw materials to the final customer |
| Logistics | Moving and storing goods, from suppliers and to customers |
| Supply chain transparency | Knowing and disclosing who supplies the business and how inputs are produced |
| Third party accreditation | Independent certification that a supplier meets a set standard |

## Chart rules

```
Re-order level    = buffer + (usage per period × lead time)
Re-order quantity = maximum − buffer (when deliveries arrive at the buffer)
Usage per period  = fall in inventory ÷ periods
```

- Lead time is read **horizontally** (time); levels and quantities **vertically**.
- If inventory dips below the buffer before a delivery: usage has risen or the lead time has lengthened.
- **Fixes**: raise the re-order level, raise the re-order quantity, enlarge the buffer, or find a faster or more reliable supplier.
- If inventory is still well above the buffer when deliveries arrive, too much is being held: lower the re-order level.

## Reasons to hold inventory / influences on amount

| Reasons | Influences on how much |
|---|---|
| Meet demand immediately | Predictability of demand |
| Cover late deliveries | Supplier lead time and reliability |
| Bulk-buying discounts | Storage space and cost |
| Prepare for seasonal peaks | Perishability or obsolescence |
| Keep production running | Cash available; JIT or JIC approach |

## JIT versus JIC in one line each

- **JIT**: low storage cost and cash tied up, less waste; needs reliable, nearby suppliers and steady demand; one failed delivery can stop work.
- **JIC**: protects against disruption and surges; costs more to store and finance; risk of waste and outdated stock.
- Judgement depends on the **cost of a stock-out** compared with the **cost of holding** inventory.

## Supply chain essentials

- **Value**: lower costs, reliability, consistent quality, faster response to customers.
- **Sourcing influences**: price, quality, reliability, lead time, location, supplier capacity, ethics, number and choice of suppliers.
- **Single supplier**: close relationship and discounts, but all eggs in one basket.
- **Several suppliers**: risk spread and competition, but more administration and weaker relationships.
- **Supply chain issues** (delays, shortages, supplier failure) hit operations, marketing, finance and HR.
- **Transparency and accreditation**: reassure customers and investors, support ethical claims, reduce risk of scandal; cost money and are checked only at intervals.

## Exam traps

- Re-order **level** is a level; re-order **quantity** is an amount.
- Lead time is in weeks or days, not units.
- Do not say "hold more inventory" as the only fix; say why and at what cost.
- Do not call JIT risk-free or JIC wasteful without context.
- Trace a supply chain problem to effects on named functions.

## Self-test

1. Usage is 250 units a week, buffer stock 300 units and lead time 3 weeks. Calculate the re-order level.
2. Maximum inventory is 2,000 units and buffer stock 300 units. Calculate the re-order quantity.
3. Inventory falls from 1,800 to 600 units in 4 weeks. Calculate weekly usage.
4. An order is placed at the end of week 6 and arrives at the end of week 8. What is the lead time?
5. State two reasons why a business holds inventory.
6. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of JIT.
7. Why might a hospital prefer JIC for vital medicines?
8. A chart shows inventory falling below the buffer before each delivery. Give two possible causes.
9. State one advantage of using several suppliers rather than one.
10. Define supply chain transparency.
11. Give one value of third party accreditation for a clothing retailer.
12. Give one effect of a supplier's failure on the finance function.

### Answers

1. 300 + (250 × 3) = **1,050 units**.
2. 2,000 − 300 = **1,700 units**.
3. (1,800 − 600) ÷ 4 = **300 units a week**.
4. **2 weeks**.
5. Any two: meet unexpected demand, cover late deliveries, bulk discounts, seasonal peaks, keep production running.
6. Advantage: lower storage costs and less cash tied up. Disadvantage: production stops if a delivery fails.
7. Running out could endanger patients, a far greater cost than storing extra stock.
8. Usage has risen; lead time has lengthened (or deliveries are late).
9. Spreads the risk if one supplier fails (or competition keeps prices down).
10. Knowing, and often publishing, who supplies the business and how inputs are produced.
11. Independent checks reassure customers that garments are not made in unsafe or exploitative conditions, protecting the brand.
12. Revenue falls while fixed costs continue, or emergency supplies cost more, squeezing cash flow.
