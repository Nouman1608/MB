Condensed notes for the section 3.2 row **Preliminary offence**: attempt under s1 Criminal Attempts Act 1981. For explanation and a worked problem, use the [attempt study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-preliminary-offence/).

## The offence

**s1(1):** with intent to commit an offence, a person does an act which is more than merely preparatory to its commission.

| Provision | Effect |
|---|---|
| s1(1) | Actus reus (act more than merely preparatory) and mens rea (intent) |
| s1(2) | Impossibility is no defence |
| s1(3) | D judged on the facts as he believed them |
| s1(4) | Applies to indictable (including either-way) offences, not summary ones |
| s4(1) | Same maximum sentence as the full offence |
| s4(3) | Judge: is there evidence? Jury: was the act more than merely preparatory? |

## Actus reus cases

**Test:** has D "embarked on the crime proper"? (*Gullefer*, 1990). Has he moved from planning or preparation to execution or implementation? (*Geddes*, 1996). The last act is not needed.

| Not yet an attempt | An attempt |
|---|---|
| *Gullefer* (1990): on the track, still preparing | *Jones* (1990): loaded gun pointed at the victim |
| *Campbell* (1991): outside the post office | *Tosti* (1997): examining the padlock |
| *Geddes* (1996): in school toilets with kit | *Boyle and Boyle* (1987): at the damaged door |
| | *A-G's Ref (No 1 of 1992)* (1993): last act not needed |

**Tip:** in a problem, find the case with the closest facts and argue by analogy.

## Mens rea

- **Intention to commit the full offence:** *Mohan* (1976): a decision to bring about the offence, whether or not desired.
- **Attempted murder = intent to kill only:** *Whybrow* (1951).
- **Oblique intention possible:** *Walker and Hayles* (1990).
- **Recklessness as to circumstances can suffice:** *Khan* (1990); *A-G's Ref (No 3 of 1992)* (1994).
- **Conditional intention:** charge carefully (*Easom*, 1971; *A-G's Refs (Nos 1 and 2 of 1979)*).

## Impossibility

```
Anderton v Ryan (1985)  - HL: no attempt to handle goods believed stolen but not
        |
        | overruled using the Practice Statement 1966
        v
R v Shivpuri (1987)     - HL: guilty of attempting drug offence; substance harmless
```

Impossibility through facts (empty pocket, fake drugs, dead victim) is no defence under s1(2) and s1(3).

## Answer sequence for an attempt problem

1. Name the full offence and check it is not summary-only (s1(4)).
2. Actus reus: compare the last thing D did with the closest case (*Campbell*, *Geddes*, *Jones*, *Tosti*).
3. Note the s4(3) split between judge and jury.
4. Mens rea: intention for the full offence; for murder, intent to kill only.
5. Impossibility, if raised: s1(2), s1(3), *Shivpuri*.
6. Conclude on the attempt; if it fails, check whether any completed lesser offence exists.

**Mini application:** X, intending to steal from a car, is seen trying door handles along a street.
- Trying handles is arguably the start of the theft itself, not mere preparation (compare *Tosti*).
- Intention to steal is shown by his conduct.
- Likely attempted theft, for the jury.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inchoate offence | An offence that is incomplete or preliminary |
| More than merely preparatory | Beyond preparation; embarked on the crime proper |
| Last act test | Old test requiring the final act before the crime; no longer required |
| Specific intent | Attempt can be committed only with intention as to the full offence |
| Factual impossibility | The crime could not be completed because of the facts |
| Practice Statement 1966 | Allowed the House of Lords (now Supreme Court) to depart from its own decisions |
| Withdrawal | Changing one's mind; no defence once the attempt is complete |

## Evaluation points

- **Vagueness:** "more than merely preparatory" gives inconsistent outcomes (*Campbell*, *Geddes* versus *Tosti*): maximum certainty problem.
- **Policy:** police may have to wait until the victim is at risk (*Geddes*).
- **Fault:** subjective intention fits the fault principle; but intent to kill for attempted murder is narrower than murder.
- **Harm:** no harm caused, yet same maximum sentence.
- **Impossibility:** *Shivpuri* is logical given s1(3), but punishes a guilty mind with a harmless act.
- **Reform:** Law Commission consultation in 2007, including a criminal preparation offence; no change made.

## Exam traps

- Requiring the last act.
- Using GBH intent for attempted murder.
- Citing *Anderton v Ryan* as good law.
- Attempting a summary offence such as battery.
- Forgetting to identify the full offence first.

3. Why was Campbell not guilty of attempted robbery?
4. Why was Jones guilty of attempted murder?
5. What did *A-G's Reference (No 1 of 1992)* decide?
6. Who decides whether an act is more than merely preparatory?
7. What mens rea is needed for attempted murder? Give a case.
8. How did *Mohan* define intention for attempts?
9. What does s1(2) say about impossibility?
10. Which case overruled *Anderton v Ryan*, and how?
11. Can a person attempt to commit battery?
12. What is the maximum sentence for an attempt?
