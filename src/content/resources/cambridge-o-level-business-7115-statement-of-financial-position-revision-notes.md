Condensed notes on section **5.4 Statement of financial position** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (for examination in 2026). For the full explanation and a two-year worked example, use the [statement of financial position study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-statement-of-financial-position/). For exam-style questions, use the [statement of financial position practice set](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-statement-of-financial-position-practice/).

**Remember:** constructing the statement is **not assessed**. You classify items, read it, and make deductions from it.

## The syllabus in two lines

- **5.4.1** Main classifications of assets and liabilities, with examples.
- **5.4.2** Interpret a simple statement and make deductions: how the business is financed, what assets it owns, selling inventories to raise finance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asset | Something the business owns |
| Liability | Something the business owes to outsiders |
| Non-current asset | Kept and used for more than a year: land, buildings, machinery, vehicles |
| Current asset | Turned into cash or used up within a year: inventories, receivables, cash |
| Current liability | Due within a year: payables, overdraft, tax due |
| Non-current liability | Due after more than a year: long-term loan, mortgage |
| Inventories | Stocks of materials, work in progress and finished goods |
| Receivables | Money owed to the business by customers (debtors) |
| Payables | Money the business owes to suppliers (creditors) |
| Equity | The owners' stake: share capital plus retained profit |
| Net assets | Total assets − total liabilities, always equal to equity |
| Working capital | Current assets − current liabilities |

## The balancing idea

Assets − liabilities = equity. Everything owned was paid for by **owners** (equity) or **lenders** (liabilities).

Effects to know:

- Take a loan: cash up, liabilities up, equity unchanged.
- Buy a machine for cash: cash down, non-current assets up, totals unchanged.
- Sell inventory at cost for cash: inventory down, cash up, totals unchanged.
- Sell inventory **below** cost: cash rises by less than inventory falls; the loss reduces retained profit.

## Classification traps

- An **overdraft** is always a current liability.
- A **loan** is a liability; the cash from it is an asset.
- Classify by use: vans are non-current assets for a delivery firm but inventory for a van dealer.
- **Inventories** are not cash. **Retained profit** is not cash.

## Four deduction questions

1. **What does it own?** Mainly non-current (factory, hotel) or current (stock-heavy retailer)? Does that fit the business?
2. **How is it financed?** Owners' funds or loans? Work out loans as a share of long-term finance (equity + non-current liabilities).
3. **Can it pay short-term debts?** Compare current assets with current liabilities, then strip out inventory and compare again.
4. **What changed?** Link movements: new assets with new loans; higher retained profit with a profitable year; falling cash with rising inventory.

## Selling inventories to raise finance

| Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|
| Quick source of cash | May have to be sold at a discount, causing a loss |
| No interest or repayment | Too little stock can mean lost sales |
| No loss of ownership or control | Only works if there is surplus or slow-moving stock |

## Limits of the statement

- A snapshot of one date: the position may be different a month later.
- Says nothing directly about profit or cash flow during the period.
- Asset values may not match what they would sell for.

## Exam traps

- Describing each line instead of making a deduction.
- Saying a business is "rich" because it has large retained profit.
- Ignoring the type of business when judging the asset mix.
- Forgetting to look inside current assets at how much is inventory.
- Saying selling inventory "raises profit": it raises cash.

## Self-test

1. Classify: a bank overdraft; a five-year bank loan; receivables; a company's office building.
2. A taxi firm owns 12 cars. Are they current or non-current assets? Would the answer be the same for a car dealer?
3. Total assets are $420,000 and total liabilities are $150,000. Calculate equity.
4. Current assets are $64,000 and current liabilities are $40,000. Calculate working capital.
5. Of the current assets in question 4, $45,000 is inventory. What deduction can you make?
6. Equity is $300,000 and the long-term loan is $100,000. What percentage of long-term finance comes from the loan?
7. A business takes out a $20,000 loan. State the effect on cash, liabilities and equity.
8. Inventory costing $12,000 is sold for $9,000 cash. State the effect on cash, inventory and retained profit.
9. Retained profit rose by $30,000 between two years. What does this suggest?
10. Non-current assets rose by $80,000 and the bank loan rose by $75,000. What deduction can you make?
11. Give one drawback of selling inventories to raise finance.
12. State one limitation of a statement of financial position.

### Answers

1. Overdraft: current liability. Five-year loan: non-current liability. Receivables: current asset. Office building: non-current asset.
2. Non-current assets for the taxi firm, which keeps and uses them. For a car dealer, cars held for sale are inventory, a current asset.
3. 420,000 − 150,000 = **$270,000**.
4. 64,000 − 40,000 = **$24,000**.
5. Only $19,000 is cash and receivables against $40,000 of current liabilities, so the business depends on selling inventory to pay its debts.
6. 100,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **25%**.
7. Cash +$20,000; liabilities +$20,000; equity unchanged.
8. Cash +$9,000; inventory −$12,000; retained profit −$3,000 (the loss).
9. The business made a profit in the year and kept part of it in the business.
10. The new assets were financed mainly by borrowing.
11. Any one: may be sold at a discount and make a loss; too little stock left to meet customer orders; only works if there is surplus stock.
12. Any one: shows one date only; does not show profit or cash flow for the period; asset values may differ from what they would sell for.
