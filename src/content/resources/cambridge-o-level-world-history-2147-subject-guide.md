Cambridge O Level History (2147) is a modern history course that pairs
a wide-ranging study of international history with a closer look at a
single country or war. Students learn to explain change, weigh up
causes and make judgements, and to treat historical documents as
evidence rather than as simple statements of fact. Assessment is
straightforward: two compulsory written papers, Paper 1 Structured
Questions worth 55% and Paper 2 Document Questions worth 45%, with no
coursework. This overview is based on the 2024-2026 syllabus, examined
in the June and November series.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has two kinds of content, and each student studies a
selection from both.

**Core content** comes in two options, each framed as a series of
enquiry questions:

- **Option A, the nineteenth century: the development of modern nation
  states, 1848–1914.** Its questions move from the revolutions of 1848
  and the unification of Italy and Germany to the American Civil War,
  the expansion of overseas empires and the causes of the First World
  War.
- **Option B, the twentieth century: international relations since
  1919.** Its questions cover the Treaty of Versailles, the League of
  Nations, Hitler's foreign policy and the outbreak of war in 1939, the
  Cold War, the United States' containment of communism, and Soviet
  control over Eastern Europe from 1948 to c.1989.

**Depth studies** narrow the focus to one subject in detail:

- A: The First World War, 1914–18
- B: Germany, 1918–45
- C: Russia, 1905–41
- D: The United States, 1919–41
- E: The Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific, 1939–c.1945

Schools select the core option and the depth study they teach, so
check both with your teacher before you start.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Structured Questions | 2 hours | 60 | 55% |
| Paper 2 | Document Questions | 1 hour 45 minutes | 40 | 45% |

Both papers are externally assessed and both are compulsory. The
qualification is linear, so the two papers are taken in the same
series at the end of the course.

- **Paper 1** requires two answers on core content and one on the
  depth study. It is the paper where breadth of knowledge and
  well-organised explanation count most.
- **Paper 2** centres on historical documents and rewards careful
  reading, comparison and evaluation of evidence.

O Level 2147 differs from the related Cambridge IGCSE syllabus (0470),
which shares the same content but adds a coursework component or an
alternative paper and splits its weighting differently. At O Level
there is no coursework and Paper 1 alone carries more than half of the
marks.

A later 2027-2028 edition of the syllabus has been published. Students
sitting exams from 2027 onwards should confirm the version that applies
to them.

## How the parts fit together

The value of the course lies in the way its two kinds of content
reinforce each other. The core option gives the long view: how nation
states formed, how wars began, how peace settlements succeeded or
failed. The depth study then shows those big themes at close range,
through the politics, society and experiences of one country or
conflict.

Several links are especially useful. The final core question in
Option A, on the causes of the First World War, prepares the ground for
Depth Study A. The Option B questions on Versailles and the League of
Nations explain the conditions that shaped Weimar and Nazi Germany in
Depth Study B. Hitler's foreign policy, in Option B, leads straight into
the Second World War in Depth Study E.

The two papers also share a core skill. Paper 2 makes source
evaluation its main focus, but the same habit of asking how reliable
and useful a piece of evidence is will improve the supporting detail
in Paper 1 answers. With only two papers, there is no separate
component to fall back on, so steady progress in both matters.

## Planning your study

One workable approach to a two-year course:

- **Term by term through the core.** Take the core option's questions
  one at a time. For each, gather the key events, then practise writing
  a short explanation that reaches a clear judgement.
- **Add the depth study once the core is under way.** Because Paper 1
  combines both, it helps to finish at least part of the core before
  starting the depth study, so the links between them are visible.
- **Practise with documents throughout.** Source work for Paper 2 is a
  skill rather than a topic, and it improves with regular short
  exercises from early in the course.
- **Review and timed practice.** In the final months, revisit every
  core question and the whole depth study, and write under time
  pressure.

Begin full past papers once all content has been covered, and turn to
the exam-preparation page for detailed advice on approaching each
paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does Cambridge O Level History 2147 have?**
Two. Paper 1 Structured Questions lasts 2 hours, is worth 60 marks and carries 55%. Paper 2 Document Questions lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 40 marks and carries 45%.

**Is there coursework in O Level History 2147?**
No. Unlike the IGCSE syllabus 0470, the O Level has no coursework option. Both papers are compulsory written exams.

**What do I answer in Paper 1?**
Paper 1 asks for two questions on core content and one on your depth study.

**Which depth studies are available?**
There are five: the First World War, Germany 1918–45, Russia 1905–41, the United States 1919–41, and the Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific. Your school chooses which one you study.

**When can I sit O Level History 2147?**
The 2024-2026 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.
