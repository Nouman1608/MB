**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 10 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): independence movements in Algeria, Angola, Ghana, Guinea, Namibia and Tanganyika, circa 1900 to 2000. Every Paper 3 question on this study requires reference to **one prescribed movement**, so choose at least two (ideally one non-violent and one armed). The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-african-independence-movements/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Domestic reasons | Discrimination, economic grievance, exclusion from government, settlers |
| External reasons | Ideas, other countries' success, imperial weakness, Cold War |
| Parties | Leaders, organisation, strategy, divisions |
| Non-violence | Campaigns, strikes, negotiation, colonial response |
| Armed struggle | Warfare, colonial reaction, foreign help, military leaders |
| Impact | New leaders, identity, women, marginalised groups |

## Movement profiles

| | Ghana | Guinea | Tanganyika | Algeria | Angola | Namibia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | Nkrumah | Sékou Touré | Nyerere | Ben Bella and FLN leaders | Neto, Roberto, Savimbi | Nujoma |
| Party | CPP (1949) | PDG | TANU (1954) | FLN (1954) | MPLA, FNLA, UNITA | SWAPO (1960) |
| Method | Positive Action, elections | Union strikes, "No" vote 1958 | Elections, negotiation | War from 1 Nov 1954 | Rising 1961; guerrilla war | PLAN from 1966; UN diplomacy |
| Independence | 6 March 1957 | 2 October 1958 | 9 December 1961 | 5 July 1962 | 11 November 1975 | 21 March 1990 |

## Domestic causes

- **Social:** settler privilege in Algeria; assimilado system in Angola; apartheid extended to Namibia.
- **Economic:** cocoa prices and swollen shoot in Ghana; forced cotton in Angola; post-war inflation; ex-servicemen.
- **Administration:** excluded educated elites; chiefs under indirect rule; Meru land case in Tanganyika.
- **Settlers:** blocked reform in Algeria; growth in Angola.

## External causes

- Ideas: Pan-Africanism (1945 Manchester Congress), Atlantic Charter, UN Charter, India's independence.
- Examples: Ghana 1957; Bandung 1955; the Algerian war.
- Imperial weakness: Britain and France after WWII; Dien Bien Phu 1954; Suez 1956; Portugal's dictatorship.
- Cold War: Soviet and Cuban support for MPLA and SWAPO; US, China and South African support for rivals in Angola.

## Parties compared

| Party | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| CPP | Mass base, Nkrumah's skill, slogan "self-government now" | Break with the older UGCC elite |
| TANU | Swahili, unity across ethnic lines, UN petitions | Depended on British cooperation |
| FLN | Disciplined armed wing, wide support | Violent rivalry with MNA; internal purges |
| MPLA, FNLA, UNITA | Support in different regions | Divisions led to civil war |
| SWAPO | Links with UN and frontline states | Based mainly on the Ovambo majority; rivals |

## Non-violence

- **Ghana:** Positive Action (January 1950); Nkrumah jailed, then wins 1951 election; independence talks.
- **Guinea:** strikes; referendum on the French Community in 1958, with about 95 per cent voting "No"; French withdrawal.
- **Tanganyika:** TANU wins 1958 to 1960 elections; Governor Turnbull cooperates; 1961 independence.
- **Namibia (diplomacy):** UN revokes the mandate in 1966; Resolution 435 in 1978.

**Limits:** British and French willingness to leave tropical Africa; imprisonment of leaders; settlers not present in these cases.

## Armed struggle

| Case | Key events | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Algeria | 1 Nov 1954; Battle of Algiers 1956 to 1957; 1958 crisis and de Gaulle; Evian March 1962 | Independence; about 800,000 Europeans leave; harkis targeted |
| Angola | 1961 risings; guerrilla war; Portuguese coup April 1974 | Independence 1975; long civil war |
| Namibia | PLAN from 1966; Cuito Cuanavale 1987 to 1988; 1988 agreements | Independence 1990 |

**Colonial response:** large armies, torture, forced resettlement, bases abroad attacked.

## Impact

- **Leaders:** Nkrumah, Touré, Nyerere, Ben Bella, Neto, Nujoma; many moved to one-party rule.
- **Identity:** Pan-Africanism; Swahili and *ujamaa*; Arab and Islamic identity in Algeria.
- **Women:** FLN fighters and couriers; CPP market women; SWAPO; reduced roles after independence.
- **Marginalised:** harkis; settlers who left; Angolan civilians; Herero and Nama.

## Arguments to practise

1. "Independence was won mainly through the efforts of the nationalist leaders." For: Nkrumah, Nyerere. Against: structural change, colonial weakness.
2. "Armed struggle was necessary where settlers were present." For: Algeria, Angola, Namibia. Against: Kenya's settlers and Mau Mau; Portugal's collapse was political.
3. "The Cold War delayed independence." For: Angola, Namibia. Against: it also provided support for movements.

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was external pressure the main reason for the achievement of independence in one prescribed country?"

- Choose Tanganyika. External pressure: UN Trusteeship Council, British post-war weakness, example of Ghana.
- Internal factors: TANU's organisation, Nyerere's leadership, absence of strong settler opposition.
- Perspectives: older accounts stress British decolonisation policy; others stress African nationalism.
- Judgement: internal organisation gave the movement strength, but the external context made quick transfer possible.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up Tanganyika (independent 1961) with Zanzibar (union in 1964).
- Writing about only one party in Angola.
- Treating armed struggle as always successful.
- Giving casualty figures without "about".

## Self-test

1. What does "Positive Action" refer to?
2. In what year did the Guinean voters reject the French Community?
3. What was TANU's first language of unity?
4. What happened on 1 November 1954?
5. Name the three Angolan movements.
6. When did Namibia become independent?

**Answers:** 1. Nkrumah's 1950 campaign of strikes and non-cooperation. 2. 1958. 3. Swahili. 4. The FLN began its war against French rule in Algeria. 5. MPLA, FNLA, UNITA. 6. 21 March 1990.

## Key terms

- **Pan-Africanism:** the idea of unity and shared interests among people of African descent.
- **Pieds-noirs:** Europeans born or living in French Algeria.
- **Harkis:** Algerians who served in French forces.
- **Trust territory:** a territory administered under the UN Trusteeship system.
- **Mandate:** League of Nations authority over a territory, such as South West Africa.
