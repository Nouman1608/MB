This set covers **section 3.5** (SL and HL) of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: the unit circle definitions of cos θ, sin θ and tan θ, exact values of 0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2 and their multiples, and the ambiguous case of the sine rule. Questions are calculator-free unless marked "(calculator allowed)". Revise first with the [unit circle study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-5/) and the [unit circle revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-5-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Find the exact value of:

**(a)** cos(5π/6) **[1]**
**(b)** sin(−2π/3) **[1]**
**(c)** tan(9π/4) **[1]**
**(d)** cos(7π/2) **[1]**

**2.** The point Q(−0.6, −0.8) is reached by rotating from (1, 0) through an angle θ, where 0 < θ < 2π.

**(a)** Show that Q lies on the unit circle. **[1]**
**(b)** Write down cos θ and sin θ, and find tan θ. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the coordinates of the point at angle θ − π. **[1]**

**3.** Find the equation of the straight line through the origin that makes an angle of 2π/3 with the positive x-axis. **[2]**

**4.** The line y = −x passes through the origin.

**(a)** Find the angle θ, with 0 < θ < π, that it makes with the positive x-axis. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the exact coordinates of the point where it meets the unit circle in the second quadrant. **[2]**

**5.** Given that sin θ = 1/2 and π/2 < θ < π:

**(a)** Write down the value of θ. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the exact values of cos θ and tan θ. **[2]**

**6.** Find the exact coordinates of the point at angle 4π/3 on the circle of radius 6 centred at the origin. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Using the unit circle, explain why sin(π − θ) = sin θ and cos(π − θ) = −cos θ. **[2]**
**(b)** Given that cos θ = 0.3, write down the values of cos(π − θ) and cos(π + θ). **[2]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) In triangle PQR, PQ = 14 cm, QR = 9 cm and angle QPR = 36°.

**(a)** Show that two triangles satisfy this information. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the two possible values of angle PRQ. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the two possible lengths of PR. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) In triangle ABC, AB = 12 cm and angle BAC = 40°.

**(a)** Show that no triangle exists with BC = 6 cm. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the range of values of BC for which two triangles exist. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the value of BC for which exactly one triangle exists and it is right-angled. **[1]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) In triangle ABC, AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm and angle BAC = 42°. Let AC = x cm.

**(a)** Show that x² − (16 cos 42°)x + 28 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the two possible values of x. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the two possible areas of the triangle. **[2]**

**11.** Given that cos θ = −√2/2 and tan θ > 0, where 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π:

**(a)** Find the exact value of sin θ. **[1]**
**(b)** Find θ. **[2]**

**12.** The point P on the unit circle is at angle θ. The point Q is obtained by rotating P anticlockwise about the origin through π/2.

**(a)** Explain why Q has coordinates (−sin θ, cos θ). **[2]**
**(b)** Hence show that cos(2π/3) = −1/2, using θ = π/6. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Second quadrant, reference π/6, cos negative: −√3/2 [1]
**(b)** Clockwise 2π/3 reaches the third quadrant, reference π/3: −√3/2 [1]
**(c)** 9π/4 = 2π + π/4: tan(π/4) = 1 [1]
**(d)** 7π/2 = 2π + 3π/2, the point (0, −1): 0 [1]

**2. (a)** (−0.6)² + (−0.8)² = 0.36 + 0.64 = 1 [1]
**(b)** cos θ = −0.6 and sin θ = −0.8 [1]; tan θ = (−0.8)/(−0.6) = 4/3 [1]
**(c)** Rotating back by π reflects Q through the origin: (0.6, 0.8) [1]

**3.** Gradient tan(2π/3) = −√3 [1], so y = −√3x [1]

**4. (a)** tan θ = −1, so θ = 3π/4 [1]
**(b)** (cos(3π/4), sin(3π/4)) [1] = (−√2/2, √2/2) [1]

**5. (a)** θ = 5π/6 [1]
**(b)** cos θ = −√3/2 [1]; tan θ = (1/2)/(−√3/2) = −√3/3 [1]

**6.** (6 cos(4π/3), 6 sin(4π/3)) [1] = (−3, −3√3) [1]

**7. (a)** The point at angle π − θ is the reflection of (cos θ, sin θ) in the y-axis [1], so it is (−cos θ, sin θ): the y-coordinate is unchanged and the x-coordinate changes sign [1]
**(b)** cos(π − θ) = −0.3 [1]; cos(π + θ) = −0.3, since the point at π + θ is (−cos θ, −sin θ) [1]

**8. (a)** PQ sin 36° = 8.229 cm [1]. Since 8.229 < 9 < 14, there are two triangles [1]
**(b)** sin R / 14 = sin 36° / 9, so sin R = 0.9143 [1]. R = 66.1° [1] or 113.9° [1]
**(c)** Angle PQR = 77.9° or 30.1° [1]. PR = 9 sin Q / sin 36° [1] = 15.0 cm or 7.68 cm [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check each obtuse candidate: 36° + 113.9° = 149.9° < 180°, so the second triangle is valid.

**9. (a)** AB sin 40° = 7.71 cm [1]. 6 < 7.71, so BC cannot reach the line AC and no triangle exists [1]
**(b)** 7.71 < BC < 12 [2]
**(c)** BC = 12 sin 40° = 7.71 cm [1]

**10. (a)** By the cosine rule on BC: 36 = x² + 64 − 2(8)x cos 42° [1], so x² − (16 cos 42°)x + 28 = 0 [1]
**(b)** x = 8.66 cm [1] or x = 3.24 cm [1]
**(c)** ½ × 8 × x × sin 42° [1] = 23.2 cm² or 8.66 cm² [1]

**11. (a)** cos θ < 0 and tan θ > 0 give the third quadrant, so sin θ = −√2/2 [1]
**(b)** Reference angle π/4 [1], so θ = π + π/4 = 5π/4 [1]

**12. (a)** A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends (1, 0) to (0, 1) and (0, 1) to (−1, 0) [1], so (x, y) maps to (−y, x), and (cos θ, sin θ) maps to (−sin θ, cos θ) [1]
**(b)** Q is at angle θ + π/2, so cos(θ + π/2) = −sin θ [1]. With θ = π/6: cos(2π/3) = −sin(π/6) = −1/2 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Decimal approximations instead of exact values.
- Rotating the wrong way for negative angles.
- The wrong sign because the quadrant was not identified.
- Quoting only one angle in the ambiguous case, or not checking the angle sum.
- Not stating the test value b sin A when asked to show that two triangles exist.
