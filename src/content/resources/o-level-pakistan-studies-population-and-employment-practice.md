**These two Paper 2 style questions cover Section 8 Population and employment, each worth 25 marks
in parts (a) to (d).** Question 1 is about growth, structure and migration; Question 2 about
distribution, density and employment. Resource figures are illustrative unless a census source is
named.

## Question 1: Growth, structure and movement

**Fig. 1.1** (illustrative): birth and death rates for Pakistan, per 1,000 per year.

| Decade | Birth rate | Death rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1960s | 44 | 20 |
| 1980s | 42 | 12 |
| 2000s | 32 | 8 |
| 2020s | 27 | 7 |

**Fig. 1.2** (described): a population pyramid with a broad base, the widest bars in the 10–24 age
groups, and a narrow top above age 60.

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 1.1, calculate the natural increase in the 1960s. **[1]**

**(ii)** Using Fig. 1.1, describe the trend in the death rate. **[2]**

**(iii)** Using Fig. 1.2, describe **two** features of the population structure. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** social and **two** economic reasons why families in rural Pakistan tend to
have more children than families in cities. **[4]**

**(c) (i)** Explain the pull factors that attract migrants to Karachi. **[3]**

**(ii)** Describe the problems faced by people living in a katchi abadi. **[3]**

**(d)** Using Fig. 1.2 and your own knowledge, evaluate whether Pakistan's large youth population
is more of an advantage or a disadvantage for its future development. **[10]**

## Question 2: Where people live and how they work

**Fig. 2.1** (from the 2023 census, rounded): population and area by province.

| Province | Population (millions) | Area (thousand km²) |
|---|---|---|
| Punjab | 127.7 | 205 |
| Sindh | 55.7 | 141 |
| Khyber Pakhtunkhwa | 40.9 | 102 |
| Balochistan | 14.9 | 347 |

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 2.1, calculate the population density of Balochistan to the nearest whole
number. **[1]**

**(ii)** State the difference between *distribution* and *density* of population. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the physical **and** economic factors that make northern and central Punjab densely
populated. **[4]**

**(c) (i)** Describe the distribution of population within Gilgit-Baltistan (the Northern Area)
and explain it. **[3]**

**(ii)** Explain the difference between unemployment and underemployment, using a rural example
of each. **[3]**

**(iii)** Explain why the proportion of workers in primary occupations in Pakistan is falling.
**[3]**

**(d)** "Improving education and training for women in rural areas is the most important way to
increase Pakistan's GDP." How far do you agree? **[9]**

## Mark scheme: Question 1

**(a) (i)** 24 per 1,000 (44 − 20) [1]. Unit or "per 1,000" required.

**(ii)** The death rate fell throughout [1], most steeply between the 1960s and 1980s (from 20 to
12) and then more slowly, to 7 per 1,000 [1].

**(iii)** Any two: broad base, showing a high birth rate [1]; widest in the 10–24 groups, showing a
large youth bulge [1]; narrow top, showing few elderly people and lower past life expectancy [1].

**(b)** One mark per developed reason, two social and two economic. Social: early marriage gives a
longer child-bearing period [1]; preference for sons means families keep having children until
sons are born [1]; low female literacy and less access to family planning in villages [1].
Economic: children work on the farm or with livestock from a young age [1]; children support
parents in old age because there are few pensions [1]; children cost less to raise in villages
than in cities [1].

**(c) (i)** Any three: jobs in factories, the port, construction and services [1]; higher wages
than in rural areas [1]; schools, colleges and hospitals [1]; relatives and community networks
already there [1]; a chance of informal-sector work even without qualifications [1].

**(ii)** Any three: poor-quality housing built of scrap materials [1]; no piped clean water or
drainage, so disease spreads [1]; no legal title, so the risk of eviction [1]; overcrowding [1];
illegal electricity connections and fire risk [1]; distance from formal jobs [1].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (8–10) balanced, developed, uses Fig. 1.2 and reaches a judgement; Level 2
(4–7); Level 1 (1–3).

**Advantage:** a large working-age population can raise output and savings (a "demographic
dividend"); young workers suit growing service and IT jobs; a big domestic market attracts
investment; remittances from young emigrants. **Disadvantage:** millions need jobs each year and
unemployment and underemployment are already high; heavy demand for schools, health care, housing
and water; growing pressure on cities and katchi abadis; frustration and unrest if expectations are
not met. A judgement such as "an advantage only if education, skills and job creation keep pace"
earns the top level.

## Mark scheme: Question 2

**(a) (i)** 43 people per km² (14.9 million ÷ 347,000 km² = 42.9) [1].

**(ii)** Distribution is the pattern of where people live [1]; density is the number of people per
unit area, usually per km² [1].

**(b)** Physical (up to 2): flat alluvial plain easy to build and farm on [1]; fertile soils [1];
rivers and canals supplying water [1]. Economic (up to 2): irrigated farming supporting many people
per hectare [1]; industrial cities such as Lahore, Faisalabad, Gujranwala and Sialkot [1]; dense
road and rail links and markets [1]. Maximum 4, at least one of each.

**(c) (i)** People live in narrow valley floors and on alluvial fans along the Indus, Gilgit and
Hunza rivers, with towns such as Gilgit and Skardu [1]; the high mountains and glaciers are almost
empty [1]; because only valley floors have flat land, soil and irrigation water from glacier
streams, while high ground is too steep, cold and barren [1].

**(ii)** Unemployment: having no work while seeking it, for example a landless labourer who cannot
find farm work [1–2]; underemployment: having some work but not enough, for example a labourer
employed only during wheat harvest and cotton picking [1–2]. Maximum 3.

**(iii)** Any three: mechanisation (tractors, threshers, harvesters) needs fewer workers [1];
holdings split by inheritance become too small to support families [1]; young people migrate to
cities for higher-paid secondary and tertiary jobs [1]; growth of services and education draws
workers out of farming [1].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (7–9); Level 2 (4–6); Level 1 (1–3).

**Agree:** rural women are a large, under-used workforce; literacy is lowest among them; educated
women earn more, start businesses, raise farm and livestock productivity, and have fewer, healthier
children, which lowers dependency; training (e.g. in dairy, crafts, health work) adds to output.
**Disagree, or other priorities:** benefits take years; jobs must exist for educated women;
cultural barriers and distance to schools limit take-up; other factors such as reliable power,
infrastructure, investment in industry and boys' technical training also raise GDP. A Level 3
answer judges which matters most and why, for example that female education is essential but must
be combined with job creation.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I set out a density calculation?**
Divide population by area in the same units, show the working, round as asked and give the unit,
for example "43 people per km²".

**What counts as an "economic" factor for population density?**
Anything to do with making a living: jobs, industry, irrigated farming, trade, transport links and
markets.

**How can I use a pyramid in a part (d) answer?**
Quote a feature, such as the wide 10–24 bars, and link it directly to the advantage or disadvantage
you are arguing.
