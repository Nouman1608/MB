**What do these questions practise?** All four parts of Section 8 Population and employment in
Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448 Paper 2: structure and growth, movements of population,
distribution and density, and employment. Question 7 uses a small data table, as Paper 2 often
gives figures to interpret.

## Section A: recall

**1.** Define *natural increase*. **[1]**

**2.** State the difference between *distribution* and *density* of population. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** push factors that cause rural–urban migration in Pakistan. **[2]**

**4.** What is a *katchi abadi*? **[1]**

**5.** Name the province of Pakistan with the lowest population density. **[1]**

**6.** Define *underemployment*. **[1]**

## Section B: interpretation and explanation

**7.** The table shows illustrative birth and death rates (per 1,000 per year) for a country.

| Year | Birth rate | Death rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1960 | 45 | 22 |
| 1990 | 40 | 11 |
| 2020 | 28 | 7 |

**(a)** Calculate the natural increase in 1990, as a rate per 1,000. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the trends shown. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons for the fall in the death rate. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **three** social or economic reasons why families in rural Pakistan have many
children. **[3]**

**9.** Describe the problems caused in large cities by rapid rural–urban migration. **[4]**

**10.** Explain the physical factors that cause low population density in Balochistan. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how unemployment and underemployment affect Pakistan's GNP and GDP. **[3]**

## Section C: evaluation

**12.** Evaluate self-help schemes as a way of improving living conditions in katchi abadis. **[6]**

**13.** "Educating girls is the most effective way to reduce population growth in Pakistan." How
far do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Birth rate minus death rate (usually per 1,000 per year) [1].

**2.** Distribution is the pattern of where people live [1]; density is the number of people per
square kilometre [1].

**3.** Any two: lack of jobs; low farm wages; landlessness; poor schools and health care; floods or
drought; mechanisation reducing farm work [2].

**4.** An unplanned shanty settlement in a Pakistani city, often without legal title or basic
services [1].

**5.** Balochistan [1].

**6.** Having some work but not enough to be fully employed, such as only seasonal farm work [1].

**7. (a)** 40 − 11 = 29 per 1,000 [1].
**(b)** The birth rate fell, slowly at first then faster after 1990 [1]. The death rate fell sharply,
halving between 1960 and 1990 [1]. Natural increase rose from 23 to 29 per 1,000 and then fell to
21 per 1,000 [1].
**(c)** Any two: vaccination programmes; better medical care and hospitals; clean water and
sanitation; better nutrition [2].

**8.** Any three: children help with farm work and add income [1]; sons support parents in old age
[1]; early marriage lengthens the child-bearing years [1]; high infant mortality in the past
encouraged families to have more children [1]; limited access to family planning [1].

**9.** Any four: growth of katchi abadis with poor housing [1]; shortage of clean water and
sanitation, spreading disease [1]; overcrowded schools and hospitals [1]; traffic congestion and air
pollution [1]; unemployment and low-paid informal work [1]; crime [1].

**10.** Much of the province is plateau and mountain with steep, rocky land unsuitable for farming
[1]; low and unreliable rainfall means little water for crops [1]; deserts such as the Kharan have
poor soils [1]; extreme temperatures, very hot summers and cold winters in the uplands, make living
conditions hard [1].

**11.** Unemployed people produce nothing, so national output is lower than it could be [1].
Underemployed people work below their capacity, adding little output [1]. Lower output means lower
GDP and GNP, less tax revenue and less money for development [1].

**12.** For: residents use their own labour and money, so costs are low and more homes benefit [1];
the Orangi Pilot Project in Karachi showed residents could build lane sewers at low cost [1];
communities gain skills and ownership [1]; improves health by reducing disease [1]. Against: needs
technical advice and organisation [1]; poorest residents may not afford contributions [1]; without
legal title people may not invest [1]; better conditions can attract more migrants, adding
pressure [1]. Judgement needed. Maximum 6.

**13.** For: educated women tend to marry later and have fewer children [1]; they know more about
health and family planning [1]; they can earn income, reducing the need for child labour [1].
Against: family planning services must also be available and affordable [1]; social attitudes
favouring large families and sons also need to change [1]; lowering infant mortality reduces the
need for many births [1]; economic development and old-age security matter too [1]. A supported
judgement, for example that girls' education is very important but works best with other
measures, gains the top mark. Maximum 6.
