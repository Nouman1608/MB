> Paper 2 Section A, Directed Writing. The two source texts are original and shorter than the real
> 400–450 words. Question 12 is a full task at the syllabus tariff of 25 marks (15 writing, 10 reading);
> the other questions practise its separate skills with practice marks.

## Section A

**1.** Write a suitable greeting and sign-off for an email (a) to your head teacher, Mr Daud, and (b) to your cousin Zara. **[2]**

**2.** Rewrite this opening so that it gets to the point: "Hello. I hope you are having a nice week. I am writing to you today because there is something I wanted to write about." The email asks the sports teacher to start a girls' cricket team. **[2]**

**3.** Rewrite this sentence for an email to a company's customer services team: "ur delivery was super late and the box was wrecked!!" **[2]**

**4.** Write a specific subject line for an email recommending that the school buy more laptops for the library. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why this sign-off is unsuitable: "Dear Ms Singh, ... Yours faithfully." **[1]**

**6.** Write a closing sentence that gives a clear next step in an email to a club organiser. **[1]**

## Section B

Read the two texts (original, written for this practice set).

> **Text 1: from the Student Council's survey report**
>
> In our survey of 250 students, 180 said they would use the library after school if it stayed open
> until 6 p.m. Many said they have no quiet place to study at home. Students in examination years
> were the most enthusiastic. Other schools in the city already open their libraries in the evening.
>
> **Text 2: from the school librarian's reply**
>
> I would love to see more students in the library. However, I already work until 4.30 p.m., and the
> school would need to pay another member of staff to supervise until six. Last year, when we tried
> opening late for one month, only about twenty students came each evening. Some students also need to
> catch the last school bus at 4.15 p.m.

**7.** Identify one point from each text you would use, and one claim from either text that you would evaluate, in an email supporting late opening. **[3]**

**8.** Write the first paragraph (about 40 words) of an email to the head teacher supporting late opening. **[3]**

**9.** Write a paragraph (about 70 words) that compares the survey result with last year's trial and evaluates both. **[4]**

**10.** Write a paragraph (about 50 words) that suggests a practical solution to one of the librarian's concerns. **[2]**

**11.** The task changes: *write an email to a friend at another school, telling them about the proposal and asking whether their library's late opening works.* Explain two changes of register you would make. **[2]**

**12.** Write an email to the head teacher giving your views on whether the library should stay open until 6 p.m. Use and evaluate ideas from both texts. Write about 250–350 words. **[25]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) "Dear Mr Daud" with "Kind regards" or "Yours sincerely" **[1]** (b) "Hi Zara" with "Speak soon" or "Love" and your name **[1]**

**2.** For example: "Dear Miss Rao, I am writing to ask whether the school could start a girls' cricket team next term." **[2]** One mark for removing the empty sentences, one for stating the request at once.

**3.** For example: "I am writing to let you know that my order arrived five days late and the box was badly damaged." **[2]** One mark for formal, accurate register, one for keeping the complaint clear and specific.

**4.** For example: "Request: more laptops for the library" **[1]**.

**5.** "Yours faithfully" follows an unnamed greeting such as "Dear Sir or Madam", not a named person, so "Kind regards" or "Yours sincerely" is needed **[1]**.

**6.** For example: "I would be happy to meet you next week to discuss this, if that would help." **[1]**

**7.** One mark each:
- Use from Text 1: many students say they have no quiet place to study at home **[1]**.
- Use from Text 2: the school would need to pay another member of staff to supervise **[1]**.
- Evaluate: the survey shows students *say* they would use the library, but last year only about twenty came each evening, so stated interest may not match attendance **[1]**.

**8.** Model:

> Dear Mrs Okafor,
>
> I am writing on behalf of Year 11 to support the Student Council's proposal to keep the library open
> until 6 p.m. I believe it would help the students who need it most, provided that the cost is planned
> carefully.

Marking: suitable greeting and register **[1]**; purpose stated at once **[1]**; clear, qualified view **[1]**.

**9.** Model:

> The survey is encouraging: 180 out of 250 students said they would use the library. However, saying
> and doing are different, and last year's trial attracted only about twenty students an evening. That
> trial lasted one month, though, and may not have been well advertised, so it is not conclusive
> either. A longer trial, timed for the weeks before examinations, would give the school real evidence.

Marking: uses the survey figure **[1]**; uses the trial figure **[1]**; evaluates both **[1]**; develops a sensible conclusion **[1]**.

**10.** Model:

> To help students who rely on the 4.15 p.m. bus, the library could open until six on two evenings a
> week, and parents could be asked whether they could collect students on those days.

Marking: addresses a specific concern **[1]**; practical development not in the sources **[1]**.

**11.** For example: a friendly greeting and sign-off ("Hi Rana", "Speak soon") and contractions **[1]**; a more conversational tone and a direct question to the friend ("Does anyone actually use yours?") instead of a formal recommendation **[1]**.

**12.** Marked out of 25: reading (R3, R5) and writing (W1–W5).
- **Reading:** uses points from both texts; develops them (exam-year students, study conditions at home, bus times); evaluates them (survey intentions against trial attendance, the short trial, cost against benefit, the comparison with other schools) **[10]**.
- **Writing:** email conventions (suitable greeting, purpose in the first lines, short purposeful paragraphs, a clear recommendation and next step, suitable sign-off); register respectful to a head teacher; varied vocabulary and sentences; accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar **[15]**.

Support, opposition or a qualified recommendation can all score fully if argued from the sources. Lower-level responses retell the texts, use bullet points instead of sentences, or adopt a casual register.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2:** keeping "I hope you are having a nice week" as the first sentence.
- **Question 9:** treating the survey figure as proof, without the trial.
- **Question 11:** changing only the greeting.
- **Question 12:** ending without a recommendation or next step.
