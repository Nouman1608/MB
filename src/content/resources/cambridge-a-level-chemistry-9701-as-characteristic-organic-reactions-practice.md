## Answers

**1.** Any two of: same functional group; same general formula; successive members differ by CH₂; similar chemical properties; gradual trend in physical properties [2].

**2.** A saturated hydrocarbon has only C–C single bonds; an unsaturated one has at least one C=C double bond [1].

**3.** A species with an unpaired electron [1].

**4.** An electron-pair acceptor [1].

**5.** NH₃ [1] and CN⁻ [1] (each has a lone pair to donate).

**6.** CH₃OH + [O] → HCHO + H₂O [1].

**7.** In homolytic fission each atom keeps one electron from the bond, forming two radicals [1]: CH₃Br → CH₃• + Br• [1]. In heterolytic fission one atom keeps both electrons, forming two ions; bromine is more electronegative, so it takes the pair [1]: CH₃Br → CH₃⁺ + Br⁻ [1].

**8. (a)** Cl₂ → 2Cl• [1].

**(b)** Cl• + CH₄ → CH₃• + HCl [1]. CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl• [1].

**(c)** CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆ [1].

**(d)** In propagation there is one radical on each side, so a radical is used and another is formed [1]. In termination two radicals react and no radical is formed [1].

**9. (a)** Elimination [1].

**(b)** Addition [1].

**(c)** Condensation (accept esterification) [1].

**(d)** Hydrolysis [1].

**10. (a)** Electrophilic addition [1].

**(b)** Nucleophilic addition [1].

**(c)** Nucleophilic substitution [1].

**(d)** Free-radical substitution [1].

**11. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O [1].

**(b)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH [1].

**(c)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O [1].

**12. (a)** CH₃CH₂Br + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH + HBr [1].

**(b)** Water breaks the C–Br bond and the molecule is split by reaction with water, so it is hydrolysis [1]. Water uses a lone pair on oxygen to attack the δ+ carbon and replaces the bromine atom, so it is also nucleophilic substitution [1].

**13. (a)** A curly arrow must start at a bond or a lone pair, not at an atom; also, the C=C bond is the electron-rich species, so electrons move from it towards the H [1].

**(b)** The first arrow starts at the C=C double bond and points to the δ+ hydrogen of HBr [1]. The second arrow starts at the H–Br bond and points to the bromine atom, which leaves as Br⁻ [1].

**14. (a)** Nucleophilic substitution [1].

**(b)** Ammonia, NH₃, is the nucleophile [1]. It has a lone pair on the nitrogen atom that it can donate to an electron-deficient carbon [1].

**(c)** The C–Br bond is polar because bromine is more electronegative than carbon, so the carbon is δ+ and attracts the nucleophile [1]. Ethane has no polar bond and no δ+ carbon, so a nucleophile is not attracted to it [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying members of a homologous series have the same molecular formula.
- Describing radicals as charged.
- Writing propagation steps that do not regenerate a radical.
- Leaving water out of [O] equations.
- Naming a mechanism by its reaction type only.
- Starting curly arrows at atoms or charges.
