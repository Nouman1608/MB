assessed on Paper 1. Questions 11 and 12 are on 4.3.3.3, which is **Higher tier only**. pV = constant is given on

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe the motion of the molecules in a gas. **[1]**

**2.** State what the temperature of a gas is related to. **[1]**

**3.** State the direction of the force that a gas exerts on the wall of its container. **[1]**

**4.** A fixed mass of gas at constant temperature has a pressure of 80 kPa and a volume of 3.0 m³. Its volume is reduced to 2.0 m³. Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**

**5.** A balloon contains 0.0040 m³ of air at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. At constant temperature its volume increases to 0.0050 m³. Calculate the new pressure. **[3]**

**6.** State two conditions that must apply for pV = constant to be used. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A sealed glass flask of air is placed in hot water.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure of the air in the flask increases. **[4]**
**(b)** State why the volume of the air does not change. **[1]**

**8.** A student traps air in a syringe and changes the volume slowly, recording the pressure.

| Volume (cm³) | 60 | 48 | 40 | 30 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure (kPa) | 100 | 125 | 150 | 190 | 250 |

**(a)** Use the data to show that pressure × volume is approximately constant, and identify the reading that does not fit. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure that the anomalous reading should have shown. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the student changes the volume slowly. **[1]**

**9.** Use the particle model to explain why increasing the volume of a gas at constant temperature decreases its pressure. **[3]**

**10.** A diver's air tank has a volume of 0.010 m³ and holds air at 2.0 × 10⁷ Pa. Calculate the volume this air would occupy at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa, at the same temperature. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A student pumps up a bicycle tyre quickly and notices the pump barrel becomes warm.

**(a)** State what is meant by work. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the air in the pump becomes warmer. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A gas is compressed quickly in a sealed cylinder. Explain why its pressure rises by more than pV = constant predicts. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Constant random motion (in all directions, at a range of speeds) [1]

**2.** The average kinetic energy of its molecules [1]

**3.** At right angles to the wall (a net force perpendicular to the surface) [1]

**4.** p₂ = (80 × 3.0) ÷ 2.0 [1]; p₂ = **120 kPa** [1]

**5.** p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ [1]; p₂ = (1.0 × 10⁵ × 0.0040) ÷ 0.0050 [1]; p₂ = **8.0 × 10⁴ Pa** (80 000 Pa) [1]

**6.** Fixed mass of gas [1]; constant temperature [1]

**7. (a)** The temperature of the air rises, so the average kinetic energy of the molecules increases [1]; the molecules move faster [1]; they hit the walls more often [1]; and each collision exerts a larger force, so the force per unit area (pressure) increases [1]
**(b)** The flask is sealed and rigid, so the gas cannot expand [1]

**8. (a)** 60 × 100 = 6000; 48 × 125 = 6000; 40 × 150 = 6000; 24 × 250 = 6000 [1]; 30 × 190 = 5700 [1]; so the reading at 30 cm³ does not fit [1]
**(b)** p = 6000 ÷ 30 [1]; p = **200 kPa** [1]
**(c)** So the temperature of the gas stays constant (compressing quickly would warm it) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Calculating p × V for every row is what earns the marks in (a); spotting the odd reading by eye, without the products, does not show the relationship.

**9.** At constant temperature the molecules' average speed does not change [1]; with a larger volume, the molecules hit each unit area of the wall less often [1]; so the force on each unit area is smaller and the pressure decreases [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer saying the molecules "slow down" contradicts the constant temperature and loses the first mark.

**10.** p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ [1]; V₂ = (2.0 × 10⁷ × 0.010) ÷ (1.0 × 10⁵) [1]; V₂ = **2.0 m³** [1]

**11. (a)** The transfer of energy by a force [1]
**(b)** Pushing the piston in applies a force through a distance, so work is done on the gas [1]; this transfers energy to the gas, increasing its internal energy [1]; the average kinetic energy of the molecules increases, so the temperature rises [1]

**12.** Work is done on the gas as it is compressed quickly, so its internal energy and temperature increase [1]; pV = constant only applies at constant temperature [1]; the hotter molecules move faster and hit the walls harder as well as more often, so the pressure is higher than the constant-temperature prediction [1]
*Examiner insight:* The key step is recognising that the condition for pV = constant has been broken; an answer that only describes the pump getting warm does not explain the extra pressure.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying only "more collisions" without "more often" and "harder".
- Claiming molecules slow down when a gas expands at constant temperature.
- Mixing Pa and kPa, or cm³ and m³, within one calculation.
- Missing the anomalous reading because p × V was not calculated for every row.
- Explaining bicycle-pump heating without mentioning work done on the gas.
