## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Define the term **mechanisation**. **[2]**

**2.** State what CAM stands for and give one example of its use. **[2]**

**3.** Identify two examples of technology that improve productivity in the service sector. **[2]**

**4.** State two advantages to a business of using 3D printing. **[2]**

**5.** A school kitchen produces 2,700 meals a week with 9 employees. Calculate its labour productivity. **[2]**

**6.** Outline one difference between automation and mechanisation. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Darnley Ceramics makes plates. At present 15 workers make 3,600 plates a week. After installing computer-controlled (CAM) shaping machines, 10 workers make 4,500 plates a week. Each worker is paid $420 a week.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity before and after the change. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in labour productivity. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the wage cost per plate before and after the change. **[3]**

**8.** Rydal Supermarket is replacing six of its staffed tills with twelve self-service checkouts, supervised by one assistant.

**(a)** Explain two advantages to Rydal Supermarket of self-service checkouts. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain two disadvantages to Rydal Supermarket's employees of this change. **[4]**

**9.** Kerslake Dental Lab makes crowns that fit each patient's teeth exactly. Outline why 3D printing is suitable for this business. **[3]**

**10.** At Penhallow Café, taking payment in cash takes about 45 seconds per customer. Contactless payment takes about 12 seconds.

**(a)** Calculate how many payments one till could take in an hour using each method. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain one disadvantage to Penhallow Café of relying on contactless payments. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how new technology could change the skills an employee needs. **[3]**

**12.** Ashby Garments employs 30 machinists who sew shirts using sewing machines that each worker operates. It is considering an automated sewing line that would need only 8 workers to supervise it. Sales of its shirts have been steady for several years. The automated line would cost $600,000.

Consider the advantages and disadvantages of Ashby Garments introducing the automated line. Justify whether it should go ahead. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Using machines to do work that was done by hand [1], with a person still operating or controlling each machine [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Computer-Aided Manufacture [1]. An example: computer-controlled machines cutting furniture parts to an exact design [1]. Accept any suitable example of computers controlling production machines. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: contactless payments; online booking; self-service checkouts; ordering apps or table tablets; online banking. One mark for each of two different examples [1] [1]. **[2]**

**4.** No mould or special tool is needed, so one-off items or small numbers are cheap to make [1]. Designs can be changed and reprinted quickly [1]. Also accept: little wasted material; useful for prototypes. **[2]**

**5.** 2,700 / 9 [1] = **300 meals per employee per week** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** In mechanisation a person operates each machine [1], whereas in automation machines or computer systems carry out tasks with little or no human input once set up [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Before: 3,600 / 15 = **240 plates per worker per week** [1]. After: 4,500 / 10 = **450 plates per worker per week** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** (450 - 240) / 240 x 100 [1] = **87.5%** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Before: 15 x 420 = $6,300; 6,300 / 3,600 = **$1.75** per plate [1]. After: 10 x 420 = $4,200 [1]; 4,200 / 4,500 = **$0.93** per plate (to the nearest cent) [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Find the weekly wage bill first, then divide by output. Dividing the wage of one worker by total output is a common error.

**8.** **(a)** One assistant can supervise twelve checkouts instead of six staff serving at six tills [1], so Rydal's wage costs fall [1]. More checkouts are open at once, so queues are shorter at busy times [1], which may attract customers who dislike waiting and increase sales [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** Fewer till staff are needed, so some employees may lose their jobs or have their hours cut [1], reducing their income [1]. Staff who remain may worry about further changes and feel less secure [1], which can lower their motivation [1]. Also accept: the need to retrain; work becoming less varied. **[4]**

**9.** Every crown is a different shape because each patient's teeth are different [1]. 3D printing builds each item from a digital design with no mould or special tool [1], so making one-off items is quick and does not need expensive tooling for every design [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** Cash: 3,600 / 45 = **80 payments per hour** [1]. Contactless: 3,600 / 12 = **300 payments per hour** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** If the card system or internet connection fails, the café cannot take payment from customers without cash [1], so it may lose sales and customers may go elsewhere at a busy time [1]. **[2]**

Also accept: the café pays a fee on each card payment, which reduces its profit on every sale.

**11.** Tasks that were done by hand may be taken over by machines, so manual skills are needed less [1]. Instead, employees need skills such as operating computer systems, programming or checking machines [1], so they may need retraining; those who learn the new skills may move into better-paid roles, while others may struggle to find work [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Advantages: the line needs 8 workers instead of 30, so the wage bill falls greatly and labour productivity rises [1]; automated sewing should give more consistent quality, with fewer faulty shirts [1]. Disadvantages: $600,000 is a large cost, and because sales are steady Ashby cannot sell much extra output, so the gain comes almost only from cutting 22 jobs [1]; job losses may lower the morale of remaining staff and damage Ashby's reputation in the local community, and a breakdown would stop all production [1]. Judgement: Ashby should go ahead only if it can afford the $600,000 and expects the wage savings to cover the cost within a few years [1]; because sales are steady, a phased change, such as automating part of the line and retraining some machinists, may be a safer choice [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Using the case facts (steady sales, 30 to 8 workers, $600,000) is what earns the application marks. A decision for or against can score full marks if it follows from the points made.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing mechanisation with automation.
- Giving productivity without units, or dividing the wrong way round.
- Using one worker's wage instead of the wage bill when finding wage cost per unit.
- Writing about factory machines when the question asks about services.
- Discussing only effects on the business when the question asks about employees.
- Ending a "consider" answer without a decision linked to the case.
