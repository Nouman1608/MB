## Definition

**Ore**: rock containing minerals and metals. **Ore grade**: how much of the useful metal the ore holds.

## Methods at a glance

| Group | Named methods | Suits | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | Opencast, open-pit, open-cut, strip | Shallow deposits; strip for flat seams | Cheaper, safer, recovers more | Destroys all surface habitat; large scars |
| Subsurface | Deep mining, shaft mining | Deep deposits | Less surface disturbance | Costly; collapse, flooding, gas risks |
| Biological | Phytomining, bioleaching | Low-grade ore, mine waste | Reuses damaged land; low disturbance | Slow; small yields |

- **Phytomining**: grow plants → they absorb metal compounds → harvest and burn → recover metal from ash.
- **Bioleaching**: bacteria release metal compounds into solution → metal extracted from the solution.

## Eight decision factors

| Factor | How it affects the decision |
|---|---|
| Exploration | Finds whether a deposit exists and how big it is |
| Geology | Rock type and structure affect safety and cost of digging |
| Accessibility and terrain | Remote or steep sites raise costs |
| Quantity and quality (ore grade) | Large, high-grade deposits are more worth working |
| Climate | Extreme cold, heat or rain makes work harder and dearer |
| Environmental impact assessment | Can block a project or set conditions |
| Supply and demand | High demand and short supply raise prices |
| Cost and profit | Goes ahead only if income exceeds total costs |

## Seven impacts

- **Loss of habitat and biodiversity**: clearing land removes food and shelter for species.
- **Air, land, noise, visual and water pollution**: dust; spoil heaps; blasting; scarred landscape;
  polluted run-off into rivers.
- **Water usage**: processing uses water needed by farms and homes.
- **Waste management**: waste rock and processing waste must be stored safely.
- **Changes in employment opportunities**: jobs gained when the mine opens, lost when it closes.
- **Local and national economies**: wages, local businesses, taxes and exports.
- **Facilities and infrastructure**: roads, power, housing and services.

Grouping: environmental (first four), economic (employment, economies), social (employment, facilities,
pollution affecting health). Several fit more than one group.

## Managing damaged land

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement of overburden | Fills the pit and reshapes the land | Costly earth-moving; settles over time |
| Soil improvement | Lets vegetation re-establish | Fertility takes years to recover |
| Bioremediation | Removes pollutants without digging them out | Slow; works only for some pollutants |
| Tree planting | Binds soil, habitat, stores carbon | Decades to mature |
| Landfill | Fills the hole; income from waste disposal | Leachate risk; limited later use |
| Lakes | Wetland habitat, water storage | Steep sides unsafe; water may be polluted |
| Recreation | Income and amenity | Needs investment; may disturb wildlife |
| Nature reserves | Biodiversity gain | Little income; slow to establish |

## Ore grade calculation pattern

mass of metal = ore grade (%) / 100 x mass of ore. *Invented example:* 0.6% of 50 000 tonnes = 300
tonnes of metal, leaving 49 700 tonnes of waste.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ore | Rock containing minerals and metals |
| Ore grade | Proportion of useful metal in the ore |
| Overburden | Soil and rock lying above a deposit |
| Spoil heap | Pile of waste rock from mining |
| Phytomining | Using plants to absorb metal from low-grade ore or waste |
| Bioleaching | Using bacteria to release metal from ore into a solution |
| Environmental impact assessment | Study of a project's likely effects before permission is given |
| Bioremediation | Using organisms to remove or break down pollutants |
| Repurposing | Giving damaged land a new use |

## Exam traps

- Bioleaching uses **bacteria**; phytomining uses **plants**.
- "Pollution" alone is too vague: name the type and how it is caused.
- Factors need an effect: "climate" alone scores little; "heavy rain floods pits and halts work" scores.
- Not all impacts are negative: jobs, taxes and new roads are benefits.
- Restoration (back towards original) differs from repurposing (new use).
- "Discuss" needs both benefits and limitations, ideally with a short conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Define an ore.
2. Name **four** surface extraction methods.
3. Which extraction method suits a shallow, flat coal seam?
4. Describe phytomining in three steps.
5. What living things are used in bioleaching?
6. State **two** factors that decide whether a deposit can be reached and worked.
7. What is an environmental impact assessment?
8. How can rising demand make an old deposit worth reopening?
9. Explain how mining can pollute a river.
10. Give **one** economic benefit and **one** social cost of a new mine.
11. Name **two** land restoration strategies and **two** repurposing strategies.
12. Calculate the metal in 80 000 tonnes of ore with a grade of 2.5%.

**Answers:** 1. Rock containing minerals and metals. 2. Opencast, open-pit, open-cut and strip mining.
3. Strip mining. 4. Plants are grown on low-grade ore or waste and absorb metal compounds; the plants are
harvested and burned; the metal is recovered from the ash. 5. Bacteria. 6. Accessibility and terrain;
climate (geology also accepted). 7. A study of a project's likely effects on people and the environment
before permission is given. 8. Higher demand raises the price, so income can now exceed the cost of
extraction. 9. Rain washes through spoil heaps and processing waste, carrying chemicals and sediment into
the river. 10. Economic: jobs or tax income. Social: noise and dust affecting residents' health, or jobs
lost when it closes. 11. Restoration: any two of replacement of overburden, soil improvement,
bioremediation, tree planting. Repurposing: any two of landfill, lakes, recreation, nature reserves.
12. 2.5 / 100 x 80 000 = 2000 tonnes.
