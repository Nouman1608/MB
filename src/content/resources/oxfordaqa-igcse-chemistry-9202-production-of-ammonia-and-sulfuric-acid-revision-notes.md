## Haber process: the facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Product | Ammonia, NH₃ |
| Raw materials | Nitrogen (from the air); hydrogen (from natural gas or other sources) |
| Equation | N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g); forward reaction exothermic |
| Catalyst | Iron |
| Temperature | About 450 °C |
| Pressure | About 200 atmospheres |
| Separation | Cooling: ammonia liquefies and is removed |
| Unreacted gases | Recycled to the reactor |

Order of steps: purify → compress → react over iron → cool and remove liquid ammonia → recycle.

## Contact process: the facts

| Stage | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | S(s) + O₂(g) ⇌ SO₂(g) | Sulfur burned in air |
| 2 | 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) | Reversible, exothermic; V₂O₅ catalyst; about 450 °C; atmospheric pressure |
| 3 | H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq) | Sulfur trioxide with water |

The specification prints Stage 1 with a reversible arrow, as shown.

## Haber and contact Stage 2 compared

| | Haber process | Contact process Stage 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Forward reaction | Exothermic, reversible | Exothermic, reversible |
| Gas molecules | 4 → 2 | 3 → 2 |
| Catalyst | Iron | Vanadium(V) oxide |
| Temperature | About 450 °C | About 450 °C |
| Pressure | About 200 atmospheres | Atmospheric |
| Reason for the pressure | Big gain in yield justifies the cost | Yield already high, so high pressure not worth the cost |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Haber process | Industrial manufacture of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen |
| Contact process | Industrial manufacture of sulfuric acid in three stages |
| Raw material | Starting substance taken from a natural source |
| Catalyst | Speeds up a reaction without being used up; does not change yield |
| Compromise conditions | Conditions balancing yield, rate and cost |
| Recycling | Returning unreacted gases to the reactor |
| Fertiliser | Substance added to soil to supply nutrients such as nitrogen |
| Vanadium(V) oxide | V₂O₅, the Stage 2 catalyst |

## Explaining the conditions

**Haber, 450 °C:** forward exothermic → low temperature favours ammonia → but too slow → compromise.
**Haber, 200 atm:** 4 molecules → 2 → high pressure favours ammonia and increases rate → limited by cost and safety.
**Haber, iron:** faster attainment of equilibrium; same yield.
**Contact Stage 2, V₂O₅:** faster rate in both directions; same yield.
**Contact Stage 2, about 450 °C:** forward exothermic → lower temperature favours SO₃ → but too slow → compromise.
**Contact Stage 2, atmospheric pressure:** 3 molecules → 2, so high pressure would help a little; yield is already high, so the cost of high pressure is not justified.

## Fertilisers: the chain

Growing population → more food needed → farmland limited → more food per field → plants need nitrogen compounds; harvests remove them → ammonia-based fertilisers replace them → maximum yields.

Fertiliser reactions:

- NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ (ammonium nitrate)
- 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ (ammonium sulfate, made using both processes' products)

## Useful mass ratios

| Reaction | Mass ratio |
|---|---|
| N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ | 28 : 6 : 34 |
| 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ | 128 : 32 : 160 |
| SO₂ → H₂SO₄ (overall) | 64 : 98 |
| 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ | 34 : 98 : 132 |

Gas volumes react in the mole ratio: 1 volume of N₂ + 3 volumes of H₂ → 2 volumes of NH₃.

## Exam traps

- Swapping the catalysts (iron for Haber; V₂O₅ for contact).
- Giving 200 atmospheres for Stage 2 of the contact process.
- "The catalyst increases yield."
- Leaving out recycling, or saying ammonia is removed as a gas.
- Hydrogen "from the air".
- Writing H₂SO₄(l) in Stage 3; the specification shows (aq).
- Fertiliser answers that stop at "plants grow better".

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two raw materials for the Haber process and their sources.
2. State the temperature, pressure and catalyst used in the Haber process.
3. How is ammonia separated from unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen?
4. What happens to the unreacted gases?
5. Why is the Haber process run at about 450 °C rather than at a lower temperature?
6. Give the equation for Stage 2 of the contact process.
7. Name the catalyst in Stage 2.
8. Why is atmospheric pressure used in Stage 2?
9. Write the equation for Stage 3.
10. Give two reasons why the world needs fertilisers.
11. Name a fertiliser made from both ammonia and sulfuric acid, and give its formula.
12. What volume of hydrogen reacts with 50 dm³ of nitrogen in the Haber process (same conditions)?

### Answers

1. Nitrogen from the air; hydrogen from natural gas (or other sources).
2. About 450 °C, about 200 atmospheres, iron catalyst.
3. The mixture is cooled; ammonia liquefies and is removed.
4. They are recycled to the reactor.
5. At a lower temperature the yield would be higher but the reaction too slow; 450 °C is a compromise.
6. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)
7. Vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅.
8. The yield is already high at atmospheric pressure, so the cost of high pressure is not worthwhile.
9. H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq)
10. Growing population needs more food; limited farmland means higher yields per field are needed; harvests remove nitrogen compounds from soil (any two).
11. Ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄.
12. 150 dm³.
