These questions cover section **4.6.1 Reproduction** (4.6.1.1 to 4.6.1.8) of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461)
specification, for first teaching from 2016. Questions 3, 5 and 11 are on Biology only content. Questions or
parts marked **Higher tier only** cover (HT only) content. The organisms and data in questions 7 and 8 are
invented examples.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State whether each statement describes sexual reproduction, asexual reproduction, or both.

**(a)** Involves the fusion of gametes **[1]**
**(b)** Produces genetically identical offspring **[1]**
**(c)** Involves meiosis **[1]**
**(d)** Needs only one parent **[1]**

**2.** A horse's body cells each contain 64 chromosomes.

**(a)** How many chromosomes are in a horse sperm cell? **[1]**
**(b)** How many chromosomes are in a fertilised horse egg? **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the cells of a horse embryo all contain the same number of chromosomes as the fertilised egg. **[1]**

**3.** Daffodils produce seeds sexually and also reproduce asexually by bulb division. Give **one** advantage of
each method to the daffodil. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by:

**(a)** genotype **[1]**
**(b)** homozygous **[1]**

**5.** Name the **four** bases found in DNA. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Describe how a protein is made from the instructions in a gene. **[4]**
**(b)** A mutation occurs in a part of DNA that does not code for a protein. Explain how it could still change
the phenotype. **[2]**

**7.** In a plant species, purple flowers (P) are dominant to white flowers (p). Two purple plants are crossed.
They produce 92 purple and 28 white offspring.

**(a)** Give the ratio of purple to white offspring in its simplest whole-number form, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(b)** State the genotypes of the two parent plants. **[1]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) One purple offspring is crossed with a white plant. About half of the new offspring are
white. Use a Punnett square to show the genotype of the purple offspring. **[3]**

**8.** A condition is caused by a recessive allele, n. In one family, two parents without the condition have two
children. Child X has the condition. Child Y does not.

**(a)** Explain how this family shows that the allele is recessive. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the genotypes of the parents. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the probability that their next child will have the condition. **[1]**
**(d)** (Higher tier only) Child Y does not have the condition. Give the probability that child Y is a carrier. **[1]**

**9.** The whole human genome has now been studied. Explain **three** ways this is important. **[3]**

**10.** A couple who are both carriers of cystic fibrosis are offered embryo screening during IVF. Embryos with
two recessive alleles would not be implanted. Evaluate the use of embryo screening for this couple. **[4]**

**11.** Malarial parasites reproduce asexually in the human host and sexually in the mosquito. Suggest the advantage
of each type of reproduction to the parasite. **[3]**

**12.** Explain how meiosis and fertilisation together keep the chromosome number constant from one generation to
the next and produce variation in the offspring. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Sexual [1]
**(b)** Asexual [1]
**(c)** Sexual (meiosis forms the gametes) [1]
**(d)** Asexual [1]
*Examiner insight:* Asexual reproduction involves only mitosis, so any answer linking meiosis to asexual reproduction is wrong.

**2. (a)** 32 [1]
**(b)** 64 [1]
**(c)** The embryo cells are produced by mitosis, which produces genetically identical cells with the same chromosome number [1]

**3.** Sexual (seeds): produces variation, which may give a survival advantage if the environment changes [1]; asexual (bulbs): any one of only one parent needed, faster, or many identical offspring when conditions are favourable [1]

**4. (a)** The alleles present in an organism [1]
**(b)** Having two identical alleles of a gene [1]

**5.** A, C, G, T (adenine, cytosine, guanine, thymine); all four needed [1]

**6. (a)** One mark for each point:
- the protein is made on a ribosome [1]
- according to a template from the gene [1]
- carrier molecules bring specific amino acids in the correct order (set by the order of bases) [1]
- the completed chain folds into a unique shape [1]
**(b)** Non-coding DNA can switch genes on and off [1]; so the mutation could change how a gene is expressed, changing the phenotype [1]

**7. (a)** 92 ÷ 28 = 3.3, so **3 : 1** [1]
**(b)** Pp and Pp [1]
**(c)** Punnett square of Pp × pp drawn correctly [1]; offspring 2 Pp : 2 pp, so half are white [1]; so the purple offspring is heterozygous, **Pp** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A cross of PP × pp gives no white offspring; showing this as a contrast is good practice but not required.

**8. (a)** Neither parent has the condition, but child X does [1]; so the parents must each carry a hidden (recessive) allele that is not expressed when a dominant allele is present [1]
**(b)** Nn and Nn [1]
**(c)** 0.25 (1 in 4, 25%) [1]
**(d)** 2/3 (Y is NN or Nn; of the three unaffected outcomes, two are Nn) [1]

**9.** Searching for genes linked to different types of disease [1]; understanding and treating inherited disorders [1]; tracing human migration patterns from the past [1]

**10.** One mark for each point:
- each embryo has a 25% chance of having cystic fibrosis, so screening means the couple can avoid having a child with the disorder [1]
- this may prevent suffering for the child [1]
- some people object because embryos with the alleles are destroyed, or because of worries about choosing embryos for other characteristics [1]
- a justified conclusion, such as: screening is reasonable for this couple because both are carriers, but the decision involves ethical views as well as science [1]

**11.** Asexual reproduction in the human is fast, so many identical parasites are produced while conditions are favourable [1]; sexual reproduction in the mosquito mixes genetic information, producing variation [1]; variation means some parasites may survive if conditions change, for example if a drug is used [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- meiosis halves the chromosome number, so each gamete has a single set [1]
- fertilisation joins two gametes, restoring the full number [1]
- the fertilised cell divides by mitosis, so every cell of the embryo has the full number [1]
- meiosis produces gametes that are all genetically different [1]
- at fertilisation genetic information from two parents is mixed [1]
- which gametes fuse is random, so offspring vary [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers that explain only the chromosome number, or only variation, cannot reach the top marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying gametes have "23 pairs" of chromosomes.
- Rounding a ratio such as 3.3 : 1 without giving a whole-number ratio.
- Forgetting to show the Punnett square when asked to use one.
- Treating 2/3 and 1/2 as the same when a child is known to be unaffected.
- Evaluations without a conclusion.
