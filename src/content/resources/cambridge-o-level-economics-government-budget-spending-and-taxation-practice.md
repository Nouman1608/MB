## Questions

**1.** Define the government budget. **[2]**

**2.** Country B plans to spend 300 (billions of currency units) this year: welfare 78, health 66, education 54, other 48, transport 30 and defence 24.

**(a)** Calculate the share of the budget planned for health. **[2]**
**(b)** The government moves 6 from other spending to education. Calculate education's new share of the budget. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why a government spends on health and one effect of that spending. **[3]**

**3.** Classify each tax as direct or indirect: **(a)** income tax, **(b)** corporation tax, **(c)** sales tax, **(d)** excise duty on fuel. **[4]**

**4.** In Country B the first 6,000 of income is untaxed, income from 6,000 to 24,000 is taxed at 25%, and income above 24,000 is taxed at 40%.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid by a worker earning 12,000 and by a worker earning 50,000. **[3]**
**(b)** Find each worker's average rate of tax. **[2]**
**(c)** State, with a reason, the type of tax shown. **[1]**

**5.** Country B charges every household the same refuse fee of 180 a year. Household M has an income of 3,600 and household N has an income of 18,000.

**(a)** Work out the fee as a percentage of each household's income. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the fee is progressive, regressive or proportional. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why this may be considered unfair. **[1]**

**6.** Country B proposes a tax of 50 on every adult, collected by post offices. Assess the proposal against two qualities of a good tax. **[4]**

**7.** In the market for school bags in Country B, Qd = 900 − 40P and Qs = −300 + 80P, where P is the price in currency units and Q is the number of bags.

**(a)** Calculate the price and quantity before any tax. **[2]**
**(b)** A tax of 6 per bag is then imposed on sellers. Find the new equilibrium price and quantity. **[3]**
**(c)** Work out how the tax revenue is shared between the burden on consumers and the burden on producers. **[3]**

**8.** Goods A and B each sell 600 units a week at a price of 20. Each is given a tax of 4 per unit, which is passed on in full to buyers. The price elasticity of demand is −0.75 for good A and −1.6 for good B.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise in the price of each good. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the new quantity and tax revenue for good A. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the new quantity and tax revenue for good B. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain which good the government would prefer to tax to raise revenue. **[2]**

**9.** A firm earns a profit of 150,000 before tax. Corporation tax is 20%.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid and the profit the firm keeps. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two ways a higher rate of corporation tax could affect producers or the wider economy. **[4]**

**10.** **(a)** State two reasons why governments levy taxes. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a government might tax a demerit good. **[3]**

**11.** Country B plans to raise its sales tax from 4% to 9% and to cut its proportional income tax from 15% to 13%. Household L earns 14,000 and spends 9,000 on goods that carry the sales tax. Household H earns 70,000 and spends 21,000 on such goods. Assume neither changes its spending.

**(a)** Calculate the net change in tax paid by household L. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the net change in tax paid by household H. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the reform is fair to low-income households. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The government's plan [1] of its expected spending and revenue over a period, usually a year [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition with only "spending" misses half the idea. Mention both spending and revenue and say that it is a plan.

**2. (a)** Share = 66 ÷ 300 × 100 [1] = **22%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Education becomes 54 + 6 = 60 [1]. 60 ÷ 300 × 100 = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Health is a merit good, so people may use less than is best for society and the benefits spread to others [1]. A healthier workforce is more productive [1], but it costs more as the population ages, and the money has an opportunity cost in other areas [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the share formula before the number so your method is visible even if the arithmetic slips. In part (c), a reason and an effect are different things, so state both separately.

**3.** (a) Direct [1]; (b) direct [1]; (c) indirect [1]; (d) indirect [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Direct and indirect describes how a tax is collected. If you are unsure, ask whether the tax is on income or on spending.

**4. (a)** Worker on 12,000: (12,000 − 6,000) × 25% = 1,500 [1]. Worker on 50,000: 18,000 × 25% = 4,500 [1] plus 26,000 × 40% = 10,400, so total tax = **14,900** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 1,500 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. 14,900 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = **29.8%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **Progressive**, because the average rate rises as income rises [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply each rate only to the slice of income in its band. Applying 40% to all 50,000 gives a wrong total and the later parts then use the wrong figure. Support "progressive" by quoting the two average rates.

**5. (a)** M: 180 ÷ 3,600 × 100 = **5%** [1]. N: 180 ÷ 18,000 × 100 = **1%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Regressive**, because the average rate falls as income rises [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The poorer household gives up a bigger share of its income, so the burden does not match ability to pay [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the percentages before naming the type. A bare "regressive" is a claim, and the rates are the evidence.

**6.** Equity: every adult pays the same 50, which is a bigger share of a poor adult's income, so it is not fair on ability to pay [1], and the tax is regressive [1]. Economy or certainty: one fixed amount is easy to understand and cheap to collect through existing post offices [1], so it scores well on certainty and economy, although fairness is weak [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name each quality, then apply it to the facts given. A list of qualities with no link to the proposal is weak. A judgement on which quality matters most strengthens the answer.

**7. (a)** 900 − 40P = −300 + 80P, so 1,200 = 120P [1]. **P = 10 and Q = 900 − 400 = 500** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** With the tax, sellers receive P − 6, so Qs = −300 + 80(P − 6) = −780 + 80P [1]. 900 − 40P = −780 + 80P gives 1,680 = 120P [1]. **P = 14 and Q = 900 − 560 = 340** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Revenue = 6 × 340 = 2,040 [1]. Consumers pay 14 − 10 = 4 more per bag, so they bear 4 × 340 = **1,360** [1]. Producers bear (6 − 4) × 340 = 2 × 340 = **680** [1]. The two add to 2,040. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The tax shifts supply, so subtract 6 from P in the supply equation. Adding 6 to the supply price is a common slip. Check that the two shares add to total revenue.

**8. (a)** 4 ÷ 20 × 100 = **20%** for both [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Good A: quantity falls by 0.75 × 20% = 15% [1]. New quantity 510, revenue 4 × 510 = **2,040** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Good B: quantity falls by 1.6 × 20% = 32% [1]. New quantity 408, revenue 4 × 408 = **1,632** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Good A, because demand is inelastic [1], so fewer buyers drop out in percentage terms, more units stay in the market and revenue is higher (2,040 against 1,632) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Elasticity is applied to the percentage price change, not to the tax in currency units. In part (d), quote both revenue figures to support the comparison.

**9. (a)** Tax = 20% × 150,000 = **30,000** [1]. Profit kept = **120,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Lower profit after tax leaves less to reinvest in machinery or new products [1], which may reduce future output and jobs [1]. Higher tax may also discourage new firms from starting, or move firms to places with lower tax [1], which reduces the tax base and local employment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each effect should be developed with a link to a consequence. "It reduces profit" is only the start, since the chain to investment and output makes the answer convincing.

**10. (a)** Any two of: raise revenue for spending; redistribute income; discourage demerit goods; protect domestic producers [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A demerit good is overconsumed because users ignore harm to themselves and others [1]. The tax raises its price [1], so consumption falls, and the government also gains revenue [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) asks for two separate reasons. Two wordings of the same reason are really one reason. In part (b), say why the good is a demerit good before describing the tax.

**11. (a)** Extra sales tax = 5% × 9,000 = 450 [1]. Income tax saving = 2% × 14,000 = 280 [1]. Net = 450 − 280 = **170 more tax** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Extra sales tax = 5% × 21,000 = 1,050 [1]. Income tax saving = 2% × 70,000 = 1,400, so net change = **350 less tax** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** For: household L pays 170 more while H pays 350 less, so the change makes the system more regressive, which is unfair on ability to pay [1]. Sales tax at 9% takes 810 ÷ 14,000 = 5.8% of L's income against 1,890 ÷ 70,000 = 2.7% for H, a heavier burden on the poorer household [1]. Lower income tax may raise work incentives [1]. Against: higher sales tax may cut spending on harmful goods and is hard to avoid, so collection is cheap [1]. Judgement: the reform is unfair to low-income households, because the shift to indirect tax moves the burden toward them, unless the extra revenue is spent on support for them [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Bring the earlier results into the evaluation. A verdict needs a reason, and "it depends" is weak unless you say what the verdict turns on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining the budget as "the money a government has" instead of a plan of spending and revenue.
- Applying a tax rate to the whole income instead of the slice in each band.
- Naming a tax progressive on the strength of the money paid, with no comparison of average rates.
- Adding the tax to the supply price instead of replacing P with P − t in the supply equation.
- Not checking that the consumer and producer shares add to total revenue.
- Giving a reason for spending but no effect, or an effect with no reason.
