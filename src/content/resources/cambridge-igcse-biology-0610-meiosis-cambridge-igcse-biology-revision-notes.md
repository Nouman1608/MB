These notes condense sub-topic **17.3 Meiosis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The whole sub-topic is Supplement content: **Extended only**.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [meiosis study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-meiosis-cambridge-igcse-biology/). Then try the [meiosis practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-meiosis-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## The two statements to learn

1. Meiosis is **involved in the production of gametes**.
2. Meiosis is a **reduction division** in which the **chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid**, resulting in **genetically different cells**.

Stages of meiosis are **not required**.

## Where meiosis happens

| Organism | Organ | Gametes produced |
|---|---|---|
| Human male | testes | sperm |
| Human female | ovaries | egg cells |
| Flowering plant | anthers | male nuclei in pollen grains |
| Flowering plant | ovules | female nucleus in the ovule |

Body cells divide by **mitosis**, never meiosis.

## What "halved" means

- Diploid cell: **pairs** of chromosomes (two sets).
- Haploid cell: **one chromosome from each pair** (one set).
- Chromosomes are not cut in half; the **number** halves.
- Each gamete therefore carries **one allele** of each gene.

**Example:** a cell with three pairs (long, medium, short) gives gametes with one long, one medium and one short chromosome.

## Life-cycle numbers

```
body cell (2 sets) --meiosis--> gamete (1 set)
gamete + gamete --fertilisation--> zygote (2 sets)
zygote --mitosis--> body cells (2 sets)
```

| Organism | Body cell | Gamete | Zygote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human | 46 | 23 | 46 |
| Garden pea | 14 | 7 | 14 |
| Fruit fly | 8 | 4 | 8 |
| Horse | 64 | 32 | 64 |

**Why it matters:** halving in meiosis + fusion at fertilisation = **constant chromosome number** from generation to generation.

## Why the cells differ

- Each gamete gets one chromosome of each pair.
- **Which** chromosome of each pair goes into a gamete **varies**.
- The two chromosomes of a pair can carry **different alleles**.
- So gametes carry **different combinations of alleles**.
- Meiosis is one of the sources of **genetic variation** (with mutation, random mating and random fertilisation) listed in 18.1.

## Worked recall

**From parent to gametes in a genetic cross.** A pea plant is heterozygous for seed shape, Rr. The two alleles are on the two chromosomes of one pair. Meiosis puts one chromosome of the pair into each gamete, so half the gametes carry **R** and half carry **r**. This is why genetic diagrams in 17.4 show each gamete with **one** allele.

**A plant life cycle.** A plant has 20 chromosomes in its leaf cells. Meiosis in the anthers gives pollen grain nuclei with **10**; meiosis in the ovules gives female nuclei with **10**. At fertilisation 10 + 10 = **20** in the zygote, and mitosis then produces a seedling with 20 in every body cell.

**Spotting a wrong answer.** A student says a gamete of a species with four pairs of chromosomes "contains two pairs". This is wrong: a haploid gamete contains **four single chromosomes**, one from each pair, and no pairs at all.

## Mitosis and meiosis

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Chromosome number | maintained | halved |
| New cells | genetically identical | genetically different |
| Human sites | throughout the body | testes, ovaries |
| Role | growth, repair, replacement, asexual reproduction | gamete production |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Meiosis | reduction division producing haploid, genetically different cells; involved in gamete production |
| Reduction division | a division that halves the chromosome number |
| Diploid | a nucleus with two sets of chromosomes |
| Haploid | a nucleus with a single set of chromosomes |
| Gamete | a sex cell with a haploid nucleus |
| Genetic variation | differences in alleles between individuals |

## Exam traps

- Meiosis **produces** gametes; it does not happen **in** gametes.
- Body cells use **mitosis**.
- Halving means one **whole** chromosome from each pair, not half chromosomes.
- Meiosis gives **different** cells; mitosis gives **identical** cells.
- Gametes are **haploid**; the zygote is **diploid**.
- Do not describe the stages; they are not required.

## Answer frames

- **Describe meiosis (3 marks):** reduction division → chromosome number halved, diploid to haploid → genetically different cells.
- **Why gametes are made by meiosis:** halving + fertilisation restores diploid → constant chromosome number across generations.
- **Why siblings differ:** meiosis gives gametes with different combinations of alleles, and fertilisation is random.

## Self-test

**1.** State the role of meiosis.

**2.** Name the organs in which meiosis happens in a human male and a human female.

**3.** Name two places where meiosis happens in a flowering plant.

**4.** Describe meiosis.

**5.** A cat's body cells have 38 chromosomes. How many are in a cat sperm cell?

**6.** A gamete of a species has 12 chromosomes. How many are in a body cell?

**7.** What would happen to the chromosome number over generations if gametes were made by mitosis?

**8.** Why does every gamete carry one allele of each gene?

**9.** Give one difference between the cells produced by meiosis and by mitosis.

**10.** Name the type of division that happens in a root tip.

**11.** State two sources of genetic variation other than meiosis.

**12.** Is the nucleus of a pollen grain haploid or diploid?

### Answers

**1.** It is involved in the production of gametes.

**2.** Testes; ovaries.

**3.** Anthers and ovules.

**4.** A reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid, resulting in genetically different cells.

**5.** 19.

**6.** 24.

**7.** It would double in every generation.

**8.** Each gamete receives one chromosome from each pair.

**9.** Meiosis: haploid and genetically different; mitosis: same chromosome number and genetically identical.

**10.** Mitosis.

**11.** Any two of: mutation, random mating, random fertilisation.

**12.** Haploid.
