## Questions

**1.** Dalia Ashby runs a small shop and keeps only bank statements and filed invoices.
**(a)** State three reasons why she may not keep full double entry records. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two disadvantages to her of keeping incomplete records. **[4]**

**2.** Hamid Vanterpool sells fruit and vegetables. At 1 January 2028 his balances were: equipment 5,600; inventory 2,300; trade receivables 1,450; cash 120; bank overdraft 680; trade payables 1,900; a loan repayable in 2032, 4,000. Prepare his statement of affairs at 1 January 2028 and state his capital. **[4]**

**3.** Hamid's capital at 31 December 2028 was 6,100 and at 1 January 2028 was 2,890. During the year he took 9,000 from the bank and goods that cost 520 for private use, and he introduced 1,500 of his own money. Calculate his profit or loss for the year. **[4]**

**4.** Oldcastle Books (credit and cash sales): trade receivables were 6,200 at the start and 7,050 at the end of the year. Customers paid 58,400, debts of 380 were written off, and cash sales were 12,300.
**(a)** Calculate credit sales. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate revenue. **[1]**

**5.** Delacroix Paints has cost of sales of 31,500 and adds a mark-up of 40%.
**(a)** Calculate gross profit and revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gross profit margin as a percentage to one decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** Ingleby Tools has revenue of 90,000 and a gross profit margin of 35%. Opening inventory was 7,800 and the rate of inventory turnover was 9 times.
**(a)** Calculate cost of sales. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate average inventory, then closing inventory. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate purchases. **[1]**

**7.** Grisedale Supplies paid credit suppliers 46,900. Trade payables were 3,950 at the start and 4,350 at the end. The owner took goods that cost 600 for her own use. Opening inventory was 5,100 and closing inventory 6,300.
**(a)** Calculate credit purchases. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate cost of sales. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate each figure for the statement of profit or loss or drawings. **(a)** A vehicle cost 18,000 with accumulated depreciation of 4,500; depreciation is 25% reducing balance. **[2]**
**(b)** Loose tools were valued at 1,900 at the start, 700 were bought and they were valued at 1,650 at the end: the depreciation by revaluation. **[2]**
**(c)** Rent received was 4,800; 400 was accrued income at the start and 500 at the end: the rent income for the year. **[2]**
**(d)** The business paid 340 for the owner's private insurance. State where this is recorded. **[1]**

**9.** Tamsin Corwin sells electrical goods. The year ended 31 December 2028.

| | 1 Jan 2028 | 31 Dec 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment (cost 20,000) accumulated depreciation | 5,000 | to find |
| Inventory | 9,200 | 10,400 |
| Trade receivables | 4,800 | 5,300 |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | 240 | 5% of receivables |
| Rent prepaid | nil | 500 |
| Bank | 3,100 | 9,200 |
| Trade payables | 5,300 | 5,900 |
| Wages owing | 300 | 350 |

Bank receipts: credit customers 74,600; cash sales 14,900. Bank payments: suppliers 52,300; wages 9,100; rent 6,000; insurance 1,260 (300 was private); general expenses 2,740; drawings 12,000. Irrecoverable debts of 420 were written off and are not in closing receivables. She took goods costing 700. Depreciation is 20% reducing balance. Prepare the statement of profit or loss for the year. **[10]**

**10.** Using question 9, prepare the statement of financial position at 31 December 2028. Opening capital must be calculated. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Any three: no bookkeeping knowledge [1]; cost of a bookkeeper or software [1]; small business so the owner sees most transactions [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two, each identified and explained: no trial balance [1], so errors are not found [1]; theft or fraud is harder to spot [1] because there is no full record to compare with [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A disadvantage reads as a stronger answer when you add why it matters to the owner. Rewording the same point a second time adds little.

**2.**
```
Assets: equipment 5,600 + inventory 2,300 + trade receivables 1,450 + cash 120 = 9,470
Liabilities: overdraft 680 + trade payables 1,900 + loan 4,000 = 6,580
Capital = 9,470 - 6,580 = 2,890
```
Overdraft treated as a liability [1]; assets 9,470 [1]; liabilities 6,580 [1]; capital **2,890** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Putting the bank overdraft among assets is a common slip. Layout headings help you see which side each balance belongs on.

**3.** Drawings = 9,000 + 520 = 9,520 [1]. Profit = 6,100 - 2,890 [1] + 9,520 [1] - 1,500 = **11,230** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A candidate who leaves out the goods taken or the capital introduced has a different profit figure. Write each adjustment on its own line.

**4. (a)** 58,400 + 7,050 [1] + 380 [1] - 6,200 = **59,630** [1]. **(b)** 59,630 + 12,300 = **71,930** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The debt written off is added because customers owed that value but did not pay in cash. Showing each figure lets the method be seen.

**5. (a)** Gross profit = 40% x 31,500 = **12,600** [1]; revenue = 31,500 + 12,600 = **44,100** [1].
**(b)** Gross profit margin = gross profit / revenue x 100 = 12,600 / 44,100 x 100 [1] = **28.6%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A mark-up of 40% is not a margin of 40%. Use the syllabus formula and state it before substituting.

**6. (a)** Gross profit = 35% x 90,000 = 31,500 [1]; cost of sales = 90,000 - 31,500 = **58,500** [1].
**(b)** Average inventory = 58,500 / 9 = 6,500 [1]; closing inventory = 2 x 6,500 - 7,800 = **5,200** [1].
**(c)** Purchases = 58,500 + 5,200 - 7,800 = **55,900** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Work in order, since each answer feeds the next. Rearranging the average inventory formula to find closing inventory is worth showing.

**7. (a)** 46,900 + 4,350 [1] - 3,950 = **47,300** [1].
**(b)** Purchases for cost of sales = 47,300 - 600 = 46,700 [1]; cost of sales = 5,100 + 46,700 - 6,300 [1] = **45,500** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Goods taken by the owner belong in drawings. Show where the 600 has been deducted so the examiner can see you have not forgotten it.

**8. (a)** 25% x (18,000 - 4,500) = 25% x 13,500 [1] = **3,375** [1].
**(b)** 1,900 + 700 - 1,650 [1] = **950** [1].
**(c)** 4,800 - 400 + 500 [1] = **4,900** [1].
**(d)** Drawings [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Reducing balance uses the carrying amount, not cost. Say which base you are using and note that the opening accrued income is deducted.

**9.**
```
Tamsin Corwin
Statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2028
Revenue (75,520 + 14,900)                          90,420
Less Cost of sales
  Opening inventory                      9,200
  Purchases (52,900 - 700)              52,200
                                        61,400
  Closing inventory                     10,400       51,000
Gross profit                                         39,420
Less Expenses
  Wages (9,100 - 300 + 350)              9,150
  Rent (6,000 - 500)                     5,500
  Insurance (1,260 - 300)                  960
  General expenses                       2,740
  Irrecoverable debts                      420
  Increase in allowance (265 - 240)         25
  Depreciation (20% x 15,000)            3,000      21,795
Profit for the year                                  17,625
```
Credit sales = 74,600 + 5,300 + 420 - 4,800 [1] = 75,520 [1]; revenue 90,420 [1]; cost of sales 51,000 after purchases 52,900 less goods taken 700 [1]; gross profit 39,420 [1]; wages 9,150 [1]; rent 5,500 and insurance 960 [1]; irrecoverable debts 420 with allowance increase 25 [1]; depreciation 3,000 [1]; profit **17,625** [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* On a long question, label each line with its working so that a correct method stays visible even if an earlier figure is wrong.

```

Equipment at cost 20,000 less accumulated depreciation 8,000       12,000
Inventory                                   10,400
Trade receivables 5,300 less allowance 265   5,035
Rent prepaid                                   500

Current assets                              25,135
Trade payables 5,900 + wages owing 350       6,250

                                                                   30,885

                                            43,885

```
Non-current assets 12,000 [1]; trade receivables net of allowance 5,035 [1]; current assets 25,135 [1]; current liabilities 6,250 [1]; opening capital = 15,000 + 9,200 + 4,560 + 3,100 - 5,600 = 26,260 [1]; profit 17,625 carried in [1]; drawings 13,000 [1]; both totals **30,885** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* If the capital total and the net assets total differ, one adjustment is missing, so go back and find it rather than forcing a balance.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving goods taken and private expenses out of drawings.
- Forgetting to add back a debt written off when finding credit sales.
- Using mark-up and margin as if they were the same.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory for turnover.
- Charging the full allowance rather than its increase.
- Calculating reducing balance depreciation on cost.
