## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which element is placed between aluminium and zinc in the reactivity series?

A: carbon

B: hydrogen

C: iron

D: magnesium **[1]**

**2.** Which metal reacts with steam but only very slowly with cold water?

A: copper

B: magnesium

C: potassium

D: silver **[1]**

**3.** Potassium is added to a trough of water. Describe **three** observations. **[3]**

**4.** Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction of calcium with water. **[2]**

**5.** Gold and silver are both placed in dilute hydrochloric acid. State and explain what happens. **[2]**

**6.** Name the salt formed when zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid, and give its formula. **[2]**

**7.** *(Extended)* Write an ionic equation for the reaction of zinc with silver ions, Ag⁺. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** A student adds small pieces of four metals, R, S, T and U, to separate tubes of the same dilute hydrochloric acid and records the volume of gas collected in two minutes.

| Metal | Volume of gas in 2 minutes / cm³ |
|---|---|
| R | 0 |
| S | 46 |
| T | 12 |
| U | 29 |

**(a)** Deduce the order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify which metal could be copper. Explain your answer using the reactivity series. **[2]**

**(c)** State **two** variables the student must keep the same to make this a fair comparison. **[2]**

**9.** Magnesium ribbon is heated in steam.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the word equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the product is not magnesium hydroxide. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Three metals, X, Y and Z, are each added to solutions of the nitrates of the other two. ✓ means a reaction occurred and ✗ means no reaction.

| Metal added | X nitrate | Y nitrate | Z nitrate |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | – | ✓ | ✗ |
| Y | ✗ | – | ✗ |
| Z | ✓ | ✓ | – |

**(a)** Deduce the order of reactivity of X, Y and Z, most reactive first. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of electrons, what happens when Z is added to a solution of X ions. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A piece of copper wire is placed in colourless silver nitrate solution.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Identify which species is oxidised and which is reduced. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student puts a piece of aluminium foil into dilute hydrochloric acid. For the first few minutes there are very few bubbles. After about ten minutes the foil fizzes vigorously.

**(a)** Explain why there are very few bubbles at first. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the reaction later becomes vigorous. **[1]**

**(c)** The student concludes that aluminium is less reactive than iron. Explain why this conclusion is wrong. **[2]**

**13.** Sodium, calcium and magnesium are each added to cold water. Explain the differences in their reactions in terms of their positions in the reactivity series. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1].

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** Any three: floats [1]; melts into a ball [1]; moves rapidly over the surface [1]; fizzes violently [1]; the hydrogen burns with a lilac flame [1]. Maximum [3].

**4.** Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**5.** No reaction [1]: gold and silver are below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so they cannot displace hydrogen from the acid [1].

**6.** Zinc chloride [1], ZnCl₂ [1].

**7.** Zn + 2Ag⁺ → Zn²⁺ + 2Ag: correct species [1]; balanced [1].

**8. (a)** S, U, T, R [2]; one mark if one adjacent pair is reversed.

**(b)** R [1]: it produced no gas, and copper is below hydrogen so it does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid [1].

**(c)** Any two: mass of metal [1]; particle size / surface area [1]; volume of acid [1]; concentration of acid [1]; starting temperature [1]. Maximum [2].

**9. (a)** Bright white glow / light [1]; white solid forms [1].

**(b)** magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide [1] + hydrogen [1].

**(c)** Metals that react with steam form the metal **oxide** (and hydrogen), not the hydroxide [1].

**10. (a)** Z, X, Y [2]. One mark for Z most reactive or Y least reactive.

**(b)** Z is more reactive, so it has a greater tendency to form positive ions [1]; Z atoms lose electrons to form Z ions [1]; X ions gain these electrons and form X metal [1].

**11. (a)** Grey / silvery crystals form on the wire [1]; the solution turns blue [1].

**(b)** Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag: correct species [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Copper is oxidised (loses electrons) [1]; silver ions are reduced (gain electrons) [1].

**12. (a)** Aluminium is covered by a thin layer of aluminium oxide [1], which stops the acid reaching the metal [1].

**(b)** The acid gradually removes the oxide layer, exposing the reactive aluminium underneath [1].

**(c)** Aluminium is above iron in the reactivity series [1]; the slow start was caused by the oxide layer, not by low reactivity, so the test was not a fair measure of the metal's reactivity [1].

**13.** Sodium reacts most vigorously (floats, melts, moves around) because it is highest in the series [1]. Calcium reacts steadily (sinks and fizzes) because it is below sodium [1]. Magnesium reacts only very slowly with cold water because it is below calcium [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that copper, silver or gold react "slowly" with dilute acid.
- Giving magnesium hydroxide as the product of magnesium with steam.
- Misplacing hydrogen or carbon in the series.
- Ranking metals without quoting the evidence from the table.
- Forgetting that silver ions are Ag⁺, so the equation needs 2Ag⁺ and 2Ag.
- Describing displacement only as "more reactive" without electron transfer at Extended level.
- Treating aluminium's slow start as evidence that aluminium is unreactive.
