## Questions

**1.** A barnacle (adult) is fixed to a rock. A seagull flies along the shore.

**(a)** Identify which is sessile and which is motile. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe one movement shown by a sessile organism of your choice. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one benefit to the seagull of being motile. **[1]**

**2.** In a sample of relaxed muscle a sarcomere is 3.0 µm long. When contracted it is 2.2 µm long. The A band is 1.6 µm in both states. (Data supplied for this question.)

**(a)** Calculate the percentage shortening of the sarcomere. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in the combined length of the I bands. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain what the constant A band shows about the mechanism of contraction. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the roles of calcium ions and ATP in the cross-bridge cycle. **[5]**

**4.** **(a)** State two roles of the protein titin in a sarcomere. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why skeletal muscles occur in antagonistic pairs. **[2]**

**5.** A muscle has 8 small motor units of 25 fibres each and 5 large motor units of 400 fibres each. Assume each active fibre exerts 0.10 N.

**(a)** Define the term motor unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the force when all 8 small units are active. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the force when 4 small units and 1 large unit are active. **[3]**

**(d)** Explain why muscles that make fine movements tend to have small motor units. **[2]**

**6.** Compare exoskeletons and endoskeletons in how they support muscle action. **[3]**

**7.** A person holds a 40 N load in the hand, 35 cm from the elbow. The biceps tendon attaches 5 cm from the elbow. Treat the forearm as a lever.

**(a)** Calculate the force the biceps must exert to hold the arm still. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain one advantage of the muscle attaching close to the joint. **[2]**

**8.** The hip is a synovial joint between the femur and the pelvis.

**(a)** State the type of joint at the hip. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the roles of cartilage, synovial fluid and ligaments at the joint. **[3]**

**(c)** Distinguish between a tendon and a ligament. **[2]**

**9.** A student used a goniometer to measure hip flexion and hip abduction in the same person. Five readings each, in degrees. Flexion: 62, 60, 65, 63, 60. Abduction: 48, 50, 47, 49, 46.

**(a)** Calculate the mean for each movement. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the flexion mean exceeds the abduction mean. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two sources of error in using a goniometer. **[2]**

**(d)** State the advantage of repeating the readings. **[1]**

**10.** Explain how the external and internal intercostal muscles act as an antagonistic pair, and how titin contributes. **[6]**

**11.** **(a)** State four reasons why animals show locomotion, giving a named example of each. **[4]**

**(b)** A caribou herd travels 3000 km in 30 days. Calculate the mean distance per day. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one cost of migration. **[1]**

**12.** Seals, whales and dolphins are adapted for swimming.

**(a)** Explain how streamlining, flippers and the fluke help a dolphin to swim. **[4]**

**(b)** A dolphin swims at 4.5 m per second with a tail beat of 1.5 Hz. Calculate the distance travelled per tail beat. **[1]**

**(c)** One breath replaces 9 L of the 12 L of air in the lungs. A resting human replaces about 15% per breath. Compare the two. **[2]**

**(d)** Outline how changes to the airways allow periodic breathing between dives. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The barnacle is sessile [1]; the gull is motile.
**(b)** Any one: an oak tree turns its leaves towards the light; a root grows downwards in response to gravity; stomata open and close. [1]
**(c)** Any one: it can search a large area for food, escape predators or find a mate. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A movement of the whole body is not credited as sessile movement; name a part and what it does.

**2. (a)** Shortening = 3.0 − 2.2 = 0.8 µm [1]; 0.8 ÷ 3.0 × 100 = **26.7%** [1]
**(b)** I bands before = 3.0 − 1.6 = 1.4 µm [1]; after = 2.2 − 1.6 = 0.6 µm [1]; change = (0.6 − 1.4) ÷ 1.4 × 100 = **−57.1%**, a fall of 57.1% [1]
**(c)** The thick filaments keep their length [1]; the filaments slide past each other, so the overlap increases rather than the filaments shortening [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the starting length as the denominator; using the contracted length gives a different figure.

**3.** Calcium ions leave the sarcoplasmic reticulum and enter the cytoplasm [1]; they attach to troponin, and tropomyosin shifts, uncovering binding sites on actin [1]; a myosin head binds to actin, forming a cross-bridge [1]; the power stroke pulls the thin filament, and ATP binds to the head, causing detachment [1]; ATP is hydrolysed, which resets the head for another cycle [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the role of each substance separately; saying "ATP provides energy" alone is too vague for these marks.

**4. (a)** Helps the sarcomere recoil after stretching [1]; prevents overstretching [1]
**(b)** A muscle exerts force only while contracting, so it can pull but not push [1]; a second muscle pulling the opposite way is needed to return the bone, and its contraction stretches the first [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Muscles relax" is not an explanation; the point is that relaxation does not itself produce the return movement.

**5. (a)** A motor neuron and all the muscle fibres it controls [1]
**(b)** 8 × 25 = 200 fibres [1]; 200 × 0.10 = **20 N** [1]
**(c)** 4 × 25 = 100 fibres and 1 × 400 = 400 fibres [1]; total 500 fibres [1]; 500 × 0.10 = **50 N** [1]
**(d)** Each unit adds only a few fibres [1], so force can be changed in small steps [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the fibre count before multiplying; clear working lets a reader follow your method even if an arithmetic slip occurs.

**6.** Both give muscles a firm structure to anchor to and act as levers [1]; in an exoskeleton the muscles attach to the inside of the hard external covering (arthropods) [1]; in an endoskeleton the muscles attach to the outside of internal bones through tendons (vertebrates) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs a similarity or a difference stated for both types, not two separate descriptions.

**7. (a)** Moments balance: force × 5 = 40 × 35 [1]; force = 1400 ÷ 5 [1]; **280 N** [1]
**(b)** The hand moves 7 times as far as the insertion point [1]; so a small contraction gives a large range and speed of movement, at the cost of needing more force [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the unit; a bare number for a force does not show the answer is complete.

**8. (a)** Ball-and-socket joint [1]
**(b)** Cartilage covers bone ends and reduces friction and absorbs shock [1]; synovial fluid lubricates and supplies nutrients to cartilage [1]; ligaments join bone to bone, stabilising the joint and limiting excess movement [1]
**(c)** A tendon joins muscle to bone [1]; a ligament joins bone to bone [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the bones involved (femur and pelvis) when describing the joint; general statements about "bones" are weaker.

**9. (a)** Flexion: (62 + 60 + 65 + 63 + 60) ÷ 5 = **62°** [1]; abduction: (48 + 50 + 47 + 49 + 46) ÷ 5 = **48°** [1]
**(b)** (62 − 48) ÷ 48 × 100 [1]; = **29.2%** [1]
**(c)** Any two: arms not lined up with the bones [1]; the pivot not placed over the joint centre, or the starting posture differing between readings [1]
**(d)** It allows a mean to be calculated and reduces the effect of random error [1]
*Examiner insight:* A mean should be given with units; a reader needs to know the values are in degrees.

**10.** The two layers have fibres running in different directions [1]; external intercostals contract and raise the ribs up and out [1]; this increases the volume of the thorax, for inhalation [1]; internal intercostals contract and pull the ribs down and in [1]; when one layer contracts, it stretches the other [1]; the stretch stores potential energy in titin, which helps the stretched layer to recoil [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the direction of rib movement for both layers; many answers name the muscles but omit the direction.

**11. (a)** Foraging for food (wolf pack hunting) [1]; escaping from danger (scallop swimming away from a starfish) [1]; searching for a mate (male moth following pheromones) [1]; migration (wildebeest following rainfall) [1]
**(b)** 3000 ÷ 30 = **100 km per day** [1]
**(c)** Any one: uses large amounts of energy, risk of predation on route, or loss of body condition [1]
*Examiner insight:* An example must be a named organism; "animals escape predators" repeats the reason and earns no extra credit.

**12. (a)** Streamlining reduces drag [1]; flippers, from modified forelimbs, are used to steer and balance [1]; the fluke is moved up and down by powerful muscles [1]; this provides thrust to push the animal forward [1]
**(b)** 4.5 ÷ 1.5 = **3.0 m** [1]
**(c)** Dolphin: 9 ÷ 12 × 100 = 75% [1]; this is five times the human value of 15%, so the dolphin renews far more of its lung air per breath [1]
**(d)** The nostrils are moved to the top of the head as a blowhole, so the animal can breathe at the surface with little of its body exposed [1]; a large proportion of the air is replaced in each breath, so one breath can supply oxygen for a long dive [1]
*Examiner insight:* Distinguish the fluke (up and down) from a fish tail (side to side); stating the wrong direction is a common reason for losing credit here.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying filaments shorten instead of sliding.
- Forgetting that the A band is constant.
- Dividing by the new value when working out percentage change.
- Leaving out units for force, length and angle.
- Treating tendons and ligaments as the same structure.
- Saying that muscles push.
- Not stating the direction of rib movement for each intercostal layer.
- Describing a reason for locomotion without a named example.
- Writing that the fluke moves side to side.
