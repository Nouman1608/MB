These notes condense sub-topic **16.3 Sexual reproduction in plants** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 8 are Core; outcomes 9 to 12 are **Extended only**.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [study guide for sexual reproduction in plants](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-plants/). Then try the [practice questions for sexual reproduction in plants](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-plants-practice/).

## Flower parts and functions -- Core

| Part | Group | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Sepal | outer | protects the flower in bud |
| Petal | outer | attracts insects (colour, scent) |
| Anther | stamen (male) | makes and releases pollen |
| Filament | stamen (male) | holds the anther up |
| Stigma | carpel (female) | receives pollen |
| Style | carpel (female) | holds up the stigma; pollen tube grows through it (Extended) |
| Ovary | carpel (female) | contains and protects the ovules |
| Ovule | inside ovary | contains the female gamete; becomes a seed |

**Groups:** stamen = anther + filament. Carpel = stigma + style + ovary.

**Drawing:** clear single lines, no shading, correct proportions, ruled label lines touching each part.

## Insect versus wind -- Core

| | Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated |
|---|---|---|
| Petals | large, coloured, scented | small, green or absent |
| Anthers | inside flower | hang outside on long filaments |
| Stigmas | inside, sticky | large, feathery, hang outside |
| Pollen amount | less | very large amounts |
| Pollen grains | larger, sticky or spiky | smaller, light, smooth |

**Reasons to quote:** spiky or sticky pollen clings to insects; light, smooth pollen is carried by air; feathery stigmas give a large surface to trap pollen; dangling anthers let wind shake pollen out; huge numbers are needed because most wind-borne pollen is lost.

## Pollination and fertilisation -- Core

- **Pollination:** transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.
- **Fertilisation:** a pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule.
- Pollination comes first. Pollination is transfer; fertilisation is fusion.

## Self- and cross-pollination -- Extended only

- **Self-pollination:** anther to stigma of the **same flower** or **another flower on the same plant**.
- **Cross-pollination:** anther to stigma of a flower on a **different plant of the same species**.

| Effect on population | Self | Cross |
|---|---|---|
| Variation | less | more |
| Capacity to respond to environmental change | lower | higher |
| Reliance on pollinators | lower | higher |

## Pollen tube -- Extended only

Pollen lands on stigma → **pollen tube grows** from the grain → down through the **style** → into the **ovary** → enters the **ovule** (through the micropyle) → **pollen nucleus** moves down the tube → **fuses with the ovule nucleus** → zygote.

Not required: endosperm and later development.

## Germination -- Core

| Condition | Reason |
|---|---|
| Water | seed swells; enzymes work; food moved to embryo |
| Oxygen | aerobic respiration releases energy |
| Suitable temperature | enzymes work at a suitable rate; too hot denatures them |

Light is **not** needed.

**Investigation set-up (compare each with the control):**

| Tube | Conditions | Tests for |
|---|---|---|
| Control | moist cotton wool, air, warm | all conditions present |
| 1 | dry cotton wool, air, warm | water |
| 2 | boiled, cooled water with oil layer, warm | oxygen |
| 3 | moist cotton wool, air, cold | temperature |

Keep constant: seed type, number of seeds, time. Calculate percentage germination: germinated ÷ total × 100.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stamen | male part: anther and filament |
| Carpel | female part: stigma, style and ovary |
| Pollination | transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma |
| Fertilisation | fusion of a pollen nucleus with a nucleus in an ovule |
| Self-pollination | to the same flower or another flower on the same plant (Extended) |
| Cross-pollination | to a flower on a different plant of the same species (Extended) |
| Pollen tube | tube that grows from the pollen grain to the ovule (Extended) |
| Germination | the start of growth of a seed into a seedling |

## Exam traps

- Pollination and fertilisation are different events.
- The male gamete is the **nucleus** in the pollen grain.
- Stigma, style and ovary are **female**; anther and filament are **male**.
- Wind pollen is **smooth and light**, not sticky.
- Never add light to the germination conditions.
- (Extended) Self-pollination includes another flower **on the same plant**.
- (Extended) The tube grows; the **nucleus** travels.

## Self-test

**1.** Name the two parts of a stamen.

**2.** State the function of the stigma.

**3.** State the function of the sepals.

**4.** Describe the stigmas of a wind-pollinated flower.

**5.** Give two differences between the pollen of wind- and insect-pollinated flowers.

**6.** Define pollination.

**7.** State what happens at fertilisation in a flowering plant.

**8.** Name the three conditions needed for germination.

**9.** Why are seeds put in boiled, cooled water under oil in a germination experiment?

**10.** *(Extended)* Define cross-pollination.

**11.** *(Extended)* Give one advantage and one disadvantage to a population of self-pollination.

**12.** *(Extended)* Describe the route of the pollen tube.

### Answers

**1.** Anther and filament.

**2.** It receives pollen grains.

**3.** They protect the flower when it is a bud.

**4.** Large and feathery, hanging outside the flower.

**5.** Wind pollen is smaller, lighter and smooth, and made in larger amounts; insect pollen is larger and sticky or spiky.

**6.** The transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.

**7.** A pollen nucleus fuses with a nucleus in an ovule.

**8.** Water, oxygen and a suitable temperature.

**9.** Boiling removes dissolved oxygen and the oil stops more oxygen dissolving, so the seeds have water but no oxygen.

**10.** Transfer of pollen from the anther of a flower to the stigma of a flower on a different plant of the same species.

**11.** Advantage: less reliance on pollinators. Disadvantage: less variation, so less capacity to respond to environmental change.

**12.** From the pollen grain on the stigma, down through the style, into the ovary and into the ovule.
