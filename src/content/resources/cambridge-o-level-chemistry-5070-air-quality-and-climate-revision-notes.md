These notes condense section 10.3, Air quality and climate, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All eight outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [air quality and climate study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-air-quality-and-climate/); then try the [air quality and climate practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-air-quality-and-climate-practice/).

## The eight outcomes in one line each

1. Clean, dry air: about 78% N₂, 21% O₂, remainder noble gases and CO₂.
2. Sources of CO₂, CO, particulates, CH₄, oxides of nitrogen and SO₂.
3. Adverse effects of each of these pollutants.
4. How CO₂ and CH₄ cause global warming: absorb, reflect, emit thermal energy; reduce loss to space.
5. Strategies against climate change and acid rain, stated and explained.
6. How oxides of nitrogen form in engines; removal by catalytic converters.
7. Photosynthesis: CO₂ + water → glucose + oxygen, with chlorophyll and light.
8. Word and symbol equations for photosynthesis.

## Pollutant summary

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | complete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | increased global warming → climate change |
| CO | incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | toxic gas |
| Particulates | incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels | respiratory problems, cancer risk |
| CH₄ | decomposing vegetation; digestion in animals | increased global warming → climate change |
| Oxides of nitrogen | car engines | acid rain, photochemical smog, respiratory problems |
| SO₂ | burning fossil fuels containing sulfur compounds | acid rain |

Memory hook: **two warmers** (CO₂, CH₄), **two acid-rain makers** (SO₂, NOₓ), **two incomplete-combustion products** (CO, particulates).

## Equations to know

| Process | Equation |
|---|---|
| Complete combustion of methane | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion giving CO | 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion giving carbon | CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O |
| Sulfur in fuel burning | S + O₂ → SO₂ |
| Nitrogen monoxide in an engine | N₂ + O₂ → 2NO |
| Nitrogen dioxide forming | 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ |
| Catalytic converter | 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ |
| Flue gas desulfurisation | CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃ |
| Photosynthesis | 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ |

## Global warming in five steps

1. Sun's energy reaches Earth; some is reflected.
2. The surface absorbs energy and warms.
3. The surface emits thermal energy.
4. CO₂ and CH₄ absorb this energy and re-emit it in all directions, some back to Earth.
5. Less thermal energy is lost to space; average temperature rises.

## Strategies

| Problem | Strategy | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Climate change | plant trees | photosynthesis removes CO₂ |
| Climate change | less livestock farming | less CH₄ from digestion |
| Climate change | fewer fossil fuels | less CO₂ from combustion |
| Climate change | hydrogen fuel | only product of burning is water |
| Climate change | wind, solar power | no fuel burned, no CO₂ |
| Acid rain | catalytic converters | NO converted to N₂ |
| Acid rain | low-sulfur fuels | less SO₂ produced |
| Acid rain | flue gas desulfurisation (CaO) | basic CaO reacts with acidic SO₂ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Complete combustion | burning in plenty of oxygen; carbon forms CO₂ |
| Incomplete combustion | burning in too little oxygen; forms CO and/or carbon particulates |
| Particulates | tiny solid particles, such as soot, in the air |
| Greenhouse gas | gas that absorbs and emits thermal energy, reducing loss to space, e.g. CO₂, CH₄ |
| Global warming | rise in the Earth's average temperature |
| Climate change | long-term change in weather patterns caused by global warming |
| Acid rain | rain made acidic by dissolved SO₂ or oxides of nitrogen |
| Catalytic converter | device in a vehicle exhaust where a catalyst converts harmful gases to less harmful ones |
| Flue gas desulfurisation | removing SO₂ from waste gases using calcium oxide |
| Photosynthesis | reaction of CO₂ and water to form glucose and oxygen, using light and chlorophyll |

## Exam traps

- CO₂ does not cause acid rain at this level; SO₂ and oxides of nitrogen do.
- The nitrogen in NOₓ comes from **air**, not the fuel; the **high temperature** is the cause.
- Catalytic converters produce CO₂; they do not reduce global warming.
- Greenhouse gases do not "trap sunlight" or damage the ozone layer; use absorb, emit, less loss to space.
- Chlorophyll and light go **over** the arrow in photosynthesis, not as reactants.
- Air figures are for **dry** air; do not include water vapour.
- "Explain" a strategy: name the pollutant it reduces and how.

## Self-test

1. State the percentages of nitrogen and oxygen in clean, dry air.
2. Name one pollutant from the complete combustion and one from the incomplete combustion of a carbon-containing fuel.
3. Give two sources of methane.
4. Why do car engines produce oxides of nitrogen?
5. Write the equation for the reaction in a catalytic converter.
6. State **two** effects of oxides of nitrogen.
7. Explain how flue gas desulfurisation removes sulfur dioxide.
8. Explain why using hydrogen as a fuel helps reduce climate change.
9. Describe how carbon dioxide reduces thermal energy loss to space.
10. Write the symbol equation for photosynthesis.
11. State the two conditions needed for photosynthesis.
12. A 200 cm³ sample of clean, dry air: what volume is oxygen?

## Answers

1. Nitrogen 78%; oxygen 21%.
2. Incomplete: carbon monoxide (or particulates). Complete: carbon dioxide.
3. Decomposition of vegetation; waste gases from digestion in animals.
4. The high temperature in the engine makes nitrogen and oxygen from the air react.
5. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
6. Any two: acid rain; photochemical smog; respiratory problems.
7. Calcium oxide, a basic oxide, reacts with sulfur dioxide, an acidic oxide, removing it from the waste gases (CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃).
8. Hydrogen burns to form only water, so no carbon dioxide is released.
9. It absorbs thermal energy emitted by the Earth's surface and re-emits some back towards the Earth, so less thermal energy escapes to space.
10. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
11. Light (energy) and chlorophyll.
12. 21% of 200 cm³ = 42 cm³.
