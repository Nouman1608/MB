## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term just in time (JIT) stock control. **[2]**

**2.** On a bar gate stock graph, what does the lowest point in each normal cycle usually show? **[1]**

A: Maximum stock

B: Reorder quantity

C: Buffer stock

D: Lead time

**3.** The specification lists three parts of delivery in relationships with suppliers. Which **two** of the following are among them? **[2]**

A: Speed

B: Trust

C: Reliability

D: Quality

E: Availability

**4.** Moss Lane Café records its milk stock in litres at the end of each day. It uses the same amount every day. Its supplier takes 2 days to deliver.

| End of day | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 (before delivery) | 5 (after delivery) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Litres | 240 | 200 | 160 | 120 | 80 | 40 | 240 |

**(a)** Identify the daily usage of milk. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the reorder quantity. **[1]**

**(c)** The café keeps 40 litres as buffer stock. Calculate the reorder level. **[2]**

**5.** Ashfield Hardware holds 2,400 boxes of screws and uses 300 boxes a week.

**(a)** Calculate how many weeks the stock will last with no deliveries. **[1]**

**(b)** Usage rises by 25%. Calculate how many weeks the stock will now last. **[2]**

**6.** Bexley Bakes buys its flour from Millstone Flour because Millstone always delivers on the day it promises, even though its flour costs 5% more than other suppliers'. State **one** supplier factor that Bexley Bakes values most. **[1]**

**7.** Explain **one** drawback to a business of using JIT stock control. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Harper's Toy Shop relies on one supplier for its most popular toy. In December the supplier's delivery arrived a week late. Outline **one** way this could affect customer satisfaction at Harper's. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** reason why trust is important in a business's relationship with its suppliers. **[3]**

**10.** Fieldfare Fashion sells clothes online and sends 40,000 parcels a year. It currently pays a courier £4.20 a parcel for next-day delivery. A cheaper courier charges £3.40 a parcel but takes three to five days.

**(a)** Calculate the annual saving from switching courier. **[2]**

**(b)** Analyse the impact on Fieldfare Fashion's reputation and customer satisfaction of switching to the cheaper courier. **[6]**

**11.** Wexcombe Kitchens fits 2,000 worktops a year. It is considering a change in how it manages worktop stock.

Option 1: keep its current method. It holds worktops worth about £60,000 in a rented store costing £9,000 a year.

Option 2: switch to JIT with a supplier ten miles away, which delivers each worktop the day before fitting. The store could shrink, costing £1,500 a year, but the supplier charges £6 more per worktop. The supplier delivered on time for 96% of orders last year.

Justify which option Wexcombe Kitchens should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Highcliff Hotel buys meat and vegetables from a farm three miles away for £52,000 a year. The farm delivers fresh produce daily and the hotel's menu names the farm. A national food distributor offers to supply similar produce for £44,000 a year, delivered twice a week, with a wider range available all year.

Evaluate whether Highcliff Hotel should switch to the national food distributor. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Ordering stock so that it arrives just when it is needed for production or sale [1], so the business holds little or no buffer stock [1]. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The third part is cost of delivery. Trust, quality and availability are separate factors in the same bullet point.

**4. (a)** **40 litres** a day [1]

**(b)** From 40 to 240 litres, so **200 litres** [1]

**(c)** (40 × 2) + 40 [1] = **120 litres** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The table confirms the answer: stock is 120 litres at the end of day 3, and the delivery arrives two days later.

**5. (a)** 2,400 ÷ 300 = **8 weeks** [1]

**(b)** New usage = 300 × 1.25 = 375 boxes a week [1]. 2,400 ÷ 375 = **6.4 weeks** [1] **[3]**

**6.** Reliability of delivery [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Bexley pays more, so cost is not its priority; the case points to delivery on the promised day.

**7.** With JIT the business holds very little buffer stock [1], so if a supplier delivers late or demand suddenly rises [1], it may run out, stopping production or leaving customers without products and losing sales [1]. **[3]**

**8.** The toy may have been out of stock during the busiest shopping weeks, so parents could not buy the present they wanted [1], which may leave them disappointed and make them shop at a rival next year [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A trusted supplier keeps its promises on quality and delivery [1], so the business can plan production and hold less buffer stock [1]. This lowers stock-holding costs and makes methods such as JIT possible, which would be too risky with a supplier the business could not rely on [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** £4.20 − £3.40 = £0.80 a parcel [1]. £0.80 × 40,000 = **£32,000** [1]

**(b)** Online clothing customers often expect fast delivery, so a wait of three to five days may disappoint those who want an outfit for an event [1]. Dissatisfied customers may leave poor reviews, which could damage Fieldfare's reputation and put off new buyers [1]. Some may switch to rivals that offer next-day delivery, reducing repeat sales [1]. On the other hand, the £32,000 saving could be used to lower prices or improve products, which might raise satisfaction among price-sensitive customers [1]. Fieldfare could offer next-day delivery as a paid option, keeping customers who need speed while saving on the rest [1]. The overall effect depends on how much its customers value speed compared with price [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* The specification names costs, reputation and customer satisfaction. Using all three, with the £32,000 figure, gives the answer balance.

**11.** Option 1 annual stock cost = **£9,000** storage [1]. Option 2 = £1,500 + (£6 × 2,000) = £1,500 + £12,000 = **£13,500** [1][1]. Option 2 costs £4,500 a year more [1]. However, Option 2 frees most of the £60,000 tied up in worktops, which could be used elsewhere in the business, such as paying for a new van or marketing [1]. JIT also reduces the risk of worktops being damaged or going out of fashion in storage [1]. The risk is reliability: at 96% on time, about 80 of 2,000 worktops would arrive late, delaying fittings and upsetting customers [1]. Each delayed fitting could harm Wexcombe's reputation in a business that relies on recommendations [1]. **Judgement:** keep Option 1, because it is cheaper each year and avoids late fittings; Wexcombe should reconsider JIT if the supplier's reliability improves or if it needs to release the £60,000 for growth [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Converting the 96% figure into roughly 80 late worktops makes the reliability risk concrete.

**12.** The distributor saves £52,000 − £44,000 = **£8,000** a year [1]. It offers a wider range all year, so the menu would not depend on what the farm has in season [1]. Against switching: daily deliveries from three miles away give fresher produce than twice-weekly deliveries, which matters for a hotel's food quality [1]. Naming the farm on the menu is part of Highcliff's reputation; guests who value local food may be disappointed if it disappears [1]. The farm relationship has built trust over time; a new supplier is untested [1]. Twice-weekly deliveries mean the hotel must store more stock, adding storage costs and waste, so the true saving is less than £8,000 [1]. A larger distributor may be more reliable at busy times and offer better availability [1]. The hotel could keep the farm for key items it promotes and buy the rest from the distributor, gaining some saving without losing the local message [1]. **Conclusion:** Highcliff should not switch completely, because freshness and its local reputation are likely to be worth more to guests than an £8,000 saving that will shrink once storage and waste are counted; a partial switch is a sensible compromise [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Evaluate answers are stronger when they question the headline saving and suggest a solution that fits the case.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the reorder level from the lowest point of the graph.
- Forgetting the buffer stock when calculating a reorder level.
- Choosing a supplier on price without considering delivery and quality.
- Ignoring the cash freed by JIT, or the risk it carries.
- Writing about logistics without linking to costs, reputation or customer satisfaction.
