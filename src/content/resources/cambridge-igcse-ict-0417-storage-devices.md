Topic 3 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 has two content rows: storage devices and storage media. This guide covers the first. The syllabus asks for the "characteristics, uses, media, advantages and disadvantages of storage devices including magnetic, optical and solid-state", and names: **fixed and portable magnetic hard drives, magnetic tape drives, optical drives (CD, DVD, Blu-ray)** and **fixed and portable solid-state drives (SSD), pen drives and flash drives**. A storage device is the hardware that **reads data from and writes data to** a storage medium. For the media themselves, see the [storage media study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-media/).

Condensed notes are in the [storage devices revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-devices-revision-notes/); test yourself with the [storage devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-devices-practice/).

## Ideas that apply to every device

- **Capacity**: how much data can be stored.
- **Access speed**: how quickly data can be found and read or written.
- **Access method**: **direct (random) access** means the device can go straight to any piece of data; **serial (sequential) access** means it must read through the data in order until it reaches what it needs.
- **Portability**: whether the device is **fixed** inside a computer or **portable**, so it can be moved between computers.
- **Durability**: how well it survives knocks, heat and magnetic fields.
- **Cost per gigabyte**.

## Magnetic storage devices

Magnetic devices store data by magnetising tiny areas of a magnetic surface in one of two directions, representing 1s and 0s.

### Fixed magnetic hard disk drive

- **Characteristics**: one or more rigid **platters** coated with magnetic material spin at high speed. **Read-write heads** on an arm move across the surfaces, a tiny distance above them, to read or change the magnetised areas. Access is **direct**. Fitted inside the computer.
- **Uses**: storing the operating system, programs and files on desktop computers; **servers** that store large amounts of data, such as websites and company databases; network storage.
- **Advantages**: very large capacity; low cost per gigabyte; fast direct access to any file; long life for read-write use.
- **Disadvantages**: has **moving parts**, so it can be damaged if knocked while running ("head crash"); slower than an SSD; uses more power and is noisier than an SSD; heavier.

### Portable magnetic hard disk drive

- **Characteristics**: the same technology in a protective case, connected externally, usually by a USB cable.
- **Uses**: **backing up** files; transferring large files between computers; extra storage for laptops.
- **Advantages**: large capacity at low cost; can be stored away from the computer, so the backup survives if the computer fails or is stolen.
- **Disadvantages**: more easily dropped and damaged because of its moving parts; can be lost or stolen, so data should be encrypted; slower than portable SSDs.

### Magnetic tape drive

- **Characteristics**: reads and writes data on a long strip of magnetic tape in a cartridge, which is wound past a read-write head. Access is **serial**: to find a file, the drive winds through the tape until it reaches it.
- **Uses**: **backing up and archiving** very large amounts of data in organisations, such as banks and hospitals; long-term storage of data that is rarely accessed; processing large data files in order, such as utility billing runs.
- **Advantages**: very high capacity; very low cost per gigabyte; tape cartridges are robust and can be stored for many years; cartridges can be removed and kept off-site.
- **Disadvantages**: **very slow** to find a particular file because access is serial; tape drives themselves are expensive; data must be read back in sequence; unsuitable for files that change often.

## Optical storage devices

Optical drives use a **laser** to read and write data on a spinning disc. To read, the laser beam reflects differently from **pits** and **lands** on the disc surface. Writable discs are written by the laser changing a dye or special layer on the disc.

### CD, DVD and Blu-ray drives

- **Characteristics**: the drive spins the disc and moves a laser along its spiral track. **Blu-ray drives** use a **blue-violet laser**, which has a shorter wavelength than the red laser of CD and DVD drives, so it can read much smaller pits packed more closely, giving far greater capacity. Many drives can read and write several formats. Access is direct.
- **Uses**: playing music (CD), films (DVD, Blu-ray) and games; installing software from disc; storing photographs and archives; distributing data that should not be altered (read-only discs).
- **Advantages**: discs are cheap and portable; read-only discs cannot be changed, protecting the data; Blu-ray holds high-definition films.
- **Disadvantages**: slower access than hard drives and SSDs; lower capacity than hard drives; many modern laptops no longer have an optical drive; discs are easily scratched; a drive can usually read only some formats (a CD-only drive cannot read DVDs or Blu-ray).

## Solid-state storage devices

Solid-state devices store data in **flash memory** chips, by controlling the movement of electrons in transistors. There are **no moving parts**.

### Fixed solid-state drive (SSD)

- **Characteristics**: flash memory chips in a sealed unit fitted inside the computer; direct access; no moving parts.
- **Uses**: main storage in laptops, tablets and smartphones; increasingly used in desktops and servers for speed.
- **Advantages**: **very fast** access, so the computer starts and opens files quickly; no moving parts, so more reliable and robust if dropped; silent; uses little power, so batteries last longer; light and thin.
- **Disadvantages**: higher cost per gigabyte than magnetic hard drives; each part of the memory can only be written to a limited number of times, although modern drives spread writes out to last for years; less capacity than a hard drive for the same price.

### Portable SSD, pen drive and flash drive

- **Characteristics**: small solid-state devices connected externally, usually through a USB port. A **pen drive** (also called a flash drive or memory stick) is a small, light device with a built-in USB connector.
- **Uses**: transferring files between computers; carrying work between home and school; backing up small amounts of data; storing files that need to be read quickly on the move.
- **Advantages**: very portable; robust, with no moving parts; fast; no separate power supply needed; works with almost any computer with a USB port.
- **Disadvantages**: very easily lost or stolen, so data may be exposed unless encrypted; can carry viruses between computers; smaller capacity than portable hard drives at the same price; can be damaged by being bent or by static electricity.

## Comparison table

| Device | Technology | Access | Moving parts | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed hard disk drive | Magnetic | Direct | Yes | Desktops, servers |
| Portable hard disk drive | Magnetic | Direct | Yes | Backups, large transfers |
| Magnetic tape drive | Magnetic | Serial | Yes | Archives and large backups |
| CD, DVD, Blu-ray drive | Optical | Direct | Yes | Films, music, software, read-only distribution |
| Fixed SSD | Solid-state | Direct | No | Laptops, tablets, fast computers |
| Portable SSD, pen drive | Solid-state | Direct | No | Carrying and transferring files |

## Worked example: matching device to need

*A hospital stores every patient's scan images. Recent scans are viewed many times a day; scans older than ten years must be kept but are almost never looked at. Recommend a storage device for each and justify your answer. [4]*

> Recent scans: a fixed hard disk drive or SSD in a server (1), because direct access means any patient's scan can be opened quickly when a doctor needs it (1). Old scans: a magnetic tape drive (1), because tape has a very large capacity at low cost per gigabyte, and the slow serial access does not matter for files that are rarely needed (1).

## Common mistakes

- Calling a CD a storage device. The CD is the medium; the CD drive is the device.
- Saying that magnetic tape is "out of date". It is still used for large backups because of its capacity and low cost.
- Saying SSDs "store data magnetically". They use flash memory.
- Saying an SSD is "cheaper". It is usually more expensive per gigabyte.
- Writing "fast" or "slow" without saying why: direct or serial access, moving parts or none.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** a device from a description: look for clues such as laser (optical), platters and heads (magnetic), no moving parts (solid-state), serial access (tape).
- **Give uses**: link each device to a real situation.
- **Explain** advantages and disadvantages: give the feature and its consequence.
- **Compare** devices: use capacity, speed, access method, durability, portability and cost.
