## Definitions in one line each

- **Habitat**: where an organism lives.
- **Species**: organisms that can breed together to produce fertile offspring.
- **Population**: all of one species in an area.
- **Community**: all the populations of different species in an area.
- **Niche**: the role of a species, including what, where and when it feeds and how it interacts.
- **Prey**: an animal hunted and eaten.
- **Predator**: an animal that hunts and eats others.
- **Apex predator**: top of the food chain; no natural predators.

Habitat = address; niche = job.

## Biotic and abiotic

**Biotic roles**: producers → primary consumers → secondary consumers → tertiary consumers; decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead matter and waste.

**Abiotic factors (7)**: temperature, water, oxygen, carbon dioxide, salinity, light, pH.

Memory aid: **T**he **W**ise **O**wl **C**an **S**ee **L**ittle **P**rey.

## Interactions

| Interaction | Key point |
|---|---|
| Native species | Occurs naturally in the area |
| Invasive species | Introduced; spreads rapidly, often with no natural enemies; reduces native species |
| Competition | For limited light, space, food, water or mates; within or between species |
| Predation | Predator and prey numbers rise and fall in linked cycles |
| Pollination | Plants depend on pollinators; pollinators get food |

## Pollination

Pollen moves from **anther → stigma**.
- Insect pollination: insect pollinator carries pollen.
- Wind pollination: light pollen blown between plants.
- Then: fertilisation → seed and fruit formation.

Flower part names other than anther and stigma are **not** required.

## The paired equations

| | Photosynthesis | Aerobic respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Word equation | carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen | glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water |
| Energy | Light energy captured by chlorophyll | Energy released from glucose |
| Where | Producers with chlorophyll | All living organisms |
| Carbon dioxide | Taken in | Given out |

## Energy flow and pyramids

- About **10%** of energy passes to the next trophic level.
- Losses: **movement, respiration, digestion, excretion**.
- Energy at level n+1 = energy at level n × 0.1. Example: 60,000 → 6,000 → 600 → 60 kJ.
- Efficiency = energy at higher level ÷ energy at lower level × 100.
- **Pyramid of energy**: always narrows upwards.
- **Pyramid of numbers**: can be inverted (one large tree, many insects) or irregular (many parasites on one host).

## Carbon cycle: six processes

| Process | From → to |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Air → producers |
| Respiration | Organisms → air |
| Feeding | Producers → consumers; prey → predators |
| Decomposition | Dead matter → air (decomposers respire) |
| Formation of fossil fuels | Buried remains → coal, oil, gas (millions of years) |
| Combustion | Fuels → air |

Human effect: combustion and deforestation tip the balance towards more carbon dioxide in the air.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ecosystem | A community interacting with its abiotic environment |
| Biotic | Living components |
| Abiotic | Non-living components |
| Producer | Organism that makes food by photosynthesis |
| Decomposer | Bacterium or fungus that breaks down dead matter |
| Trophic level | A feeding level in a food chain |
| Food web | Linked food chains in an ecosystem |
| Invasive species | Introduced species that spreads and harms native species |
| Chlorophyll | Pigment that captures light energy |
| Salinity | Saltiness of water or soil |

## Exam traps

- Arrows in food chains point **towards the eater**.
- Plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
- The 10% figure is approximate; use the figure a question gives.
- A pyramid of numbers is not always pyramid-shaped; a pyramid of energy is.
- Decomposition releases carbon dioxide because decomposers respire.
- Do not name flower parts beyond anther and stigma; they earn no marks.

## Answers

1. The role of a species in its ecosystem, including what it eats and how it interacts with others.
2. A population is all of one species in an area; a community is all the populations of different species.
3. A predator at the top of a food chain with no natural predators.
4. Any four of: temperature, water, oxygen, carbon dioxide, salinity, light, pH.
5. They often have no natural predators or diseases in their new area.
6. The transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma, by an insect pollinator or by wind.
7. It captures light energy for photosynthesis.
8. carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen.
9. Movement, respiration, digestion, excretion.
10. 30,000 → 3,000 → 300 kJ.
11. One large producer, such as a tree, can support many small consumers.
12. Combustion (of fossil fuels).
