These questions cover section 3.12 Turning points in physics of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, version 1.3, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards), sub-sections 3.12.1 to 3.12.3. Turning points is one of the options (sections 3.9 to 3.13); you study one option, assessed in Paper 3 Section B. All of it is A-level only. A calculator is allowed.

Review the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-turning-points-in-physics/) and [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-turning-points-in-physics-revision-notes/) first; see also the [course hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/physics/), [checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/physics/) and free [diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

Use e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, mass of hydrogen ion = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H m⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Describe how cathode rays are produced in a discharge tube. **[3]**

**2.** An electron gun has an anode pd of 4.5 kV.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of an electron leaving the gun. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the cathode is heated. **[1]**

**3.** An electron beam passes undeflected through crossed fields: plates 40 mm apart at 1200 V, and 1.5 mT. With the electric field off, the beam moves in a circle of radius 7.6 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the electrons. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the specific charge of the electron. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the significance of this value compared with the hydrogen ion's specific charge. **[2]**

**4.** An oil drop (density 870 kg m⁻³) falls between horizontal plates 8.0 mm apart. Air viscosity is 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ N s m⁻². With no pd, the drop falls at a terminal speed of 8.5 × 10⁻⁵ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Show that the radius of the drop is about 9.0 × 10⁻⁷ m. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of the drop. **[2]**
**(c)** The drop is held stationary when the pd is 324 V. Calculate its charge and express it in terms of e. **[3]**
**(d)** State what Millikan concluded from many such results. **[1]**

**5.** **(a)** Give two reasons why Newton's corpuscular theory was preferred to Huygens' wave theory. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, in general terms, how Young's double slits produce bright and dark fringes. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Calculate the permittivity of free space ε₀. **[2]**
**(b)** In a Hertz-type experiment, stationary radio waves have nodes 0.75 m apart. The frequency is 200 MHz. Calculate the speed of the waves. **[2]**
**(c)** State what ε₀ relates to. **[1]**

**7.** In a Fizeau-type experiment a wheel has 400 teeth and the mirror is 7.5 km away. The returning light is first blocked at 25 revolutions per second.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of light. **[3]**
**(b)** State the implication of this result when compared with 1/√(μ₀ε₀). **[1]**

**8.** **(a)** Describe the ultraviolet catastrophe. **[2]**
**(b)** State Planck's interpretation. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain three ways classical wave theory fails to account for photoelectricity. **[3]**
**(d)** State the significance of Einstein's explanation. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Estimate the anode pd needed to give electrons a de Broglie wavelength of 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. **[3]**
**(b)** In low-energy electron diffraction the pd is increased. Explain the effect on the pattern. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a TEM sample must be very thin. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how a scanning tunnelling microscope works in constant-current mode. **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** Outline the Michelson-Morley experiment as a means of detecting absolute motion. **[3]**
**(b)** State the significance of its result. **[2]**
**(c)** Define an inertial frame of reference. **[1]**
**(d)** Muons at 0.98c pass detectors 1800 m apart vertically; proper half-life 1.5 μs. 1000 pass the upper detector. Calculate how many reach the lower one with and without time dilation. **[4]**
**(e)** Show that an observer travelling with the muons predicts the same number. **[2]**

**12.** An electron has kinetic energy 3.0 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate its speed. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate its mass. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that Newtonian mechanics gives an impossible speed. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how Bertozzi's experiment showed that kinetic energy varies with speed as relativity predicts. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Low-pressure gas with a high pd between cathode and anode [1]. Positive ions strike the cathode and release electrons [1]. The electrons accelerate towards the anode; these beams are the cathode rays [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Electrons come off the cathode" alone does not say why, so scores only one mark.

**2. (a)** Ek = eV = **7.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]
**(b)** v = √(2Ek/m) [1] = √(2 × 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **3.98 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Heating gives conduction electrons enough energy to leave the metal (thermionic emission) [1].
*Examiner insight:* A wrong (a) carried into a correct method in (b) is usually allowed as error carried forward.

**3. (a)** E = V/d = 1200/0.040 = 3.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]; eE = Bev, so v = E/B = **2.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Bev = mv²/r, so e/m = v/(Br) [1] = 2.0 × 10⁷ / (1.5 × 10⁻³ × 0.076) = **1.75 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** It is about 1800 times the hydrogen ion's value (9.6 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹) [1]; for equal charge the electron is far less massive than any atom, so it is a sub-atomic particle [1].
*Examiner insight:* A comparison alone earns one mark in (c); the second needs the conclusion about mass.

**4. (a)** mg = 6πηrv with m = (4/3)πr³ρ [1]; r = √(9ηv/(2ρg)) [1] = √(9 × 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 8.5 × 10⁻⁵ / (2 × 870 × 9.81)) = 8.98 × 10⁻⁷ m [1]
**(b)** m = (4/3)π(8.98 × 10⁻⁷)³ × 870 [1] = **2.64 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg** [1]
**(c)** Q = mgd/V [1] = 2.64 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 9.81 × 8.0 × 10⁻³ / 324 = 6.39 × 10⁻¹⁹ C [1]; ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4.0, so Q = 4e** [1]
**(d)** Charge is quantised: always a whole-number multiple of e [1].
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that", give the answer to more significant figures than the stated value (8.98, not 9.0) or the final mark is lost.

**5. (a)** Two from: Newton's reputation [1]; it explained reflection and refraction [1]; the speed of light in water could not yet be measured. **[2]**
**(b)** Waves from the two slits overlap; where they arrive in phase they reinforce to give bright fringes [1]; in antiphase they cancel to give dark fringes [1].
*Examiner insight:* No calculation is expected in (b); a formula without the phase explanation earns nothing.

**6. (a)** ε₀ = 1/(μ₀c²) [1] = 1/(4π × 10⁻⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)²) = **8.84 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** λ = 2 × 0.75 = 1.50 m [1]; c = fλ = 200 × 10⁶ × 1.50 = **3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** The electric field strength due to a charged object in free space [1].
*Examiner insight:* Node spacing is λ/2; taking 0.75 m as λ loses the first mark, and the second may be allowed as error carried forward.

**7. (a)** t = 1/(2Nf) = 1/(2 × 400 × 25) = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ s [1]; light travels 2d = 15 km [1]; c = 15 000 / 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ = **3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The values agree, so light is an electromagnetic wave [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using d instead of 2d halves the answer and loses the last two marks.

**8. (a)** Classical theory predicted black-body intensity rising without limit as wavelength decreases [1], but the observed intensity peaks and falls to zero at short wavelengths [1].
**(b)** Energy is emitted in quanta of E = hf [1].
**(c)** Below a threshold frequency no electrons are emitted, however intense the light [1]; emission is instant, with no build-up time at low intensity [1]; maximum kinetic energy depends on frequency, not intensity [1].
**(d)** EM radiation has a particle (photon) nature [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each mark in (c) needs the observation, not just "wave theory is wrong".

**9. (a)** p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 5.53 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹ [1]; eV = p²/(2m) [1]; V = (5.53 × 10⁻²⁴)² / (2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **105 V** [1]
**(b)** Electrons go faster, so momentum rises and wavelength falls [1]; less diffraction, so the rings get smaller [1].
**(c)** Electrons must pass through it [1]; a thick sample slows them, changing their wavelength and blurring the image [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Estimate" still needs the working; a bare 105 V gets only the final mark.

**10.** A fine conducting tip is held about 1 nm above a conducting surface with a small pd [1]. Electrons cross the gap by quantum tunnelling [1]. The current depends very strongly on the gap width [1]. The tip height is adjusted to keep the current constant, so the tip follows the surface contours [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying "electrons jump the gap" without the word tunnelling usually loses that mark.

**11. (a)** A semi-silvered plate splits light into two perpendicular beams, reflected back by mirrors [1]; the beams recombine to give fringes [1]; motion through the ether should change the travel times, so rotating the apparatus by 90° should shift the fringes [1].
**(b)** No shift was detected, so absolute motion cannot be detected [1]; the speed of light is invariant [1].
**(c)** A frame that is not accelerating, in which Newton's first law holds [1].
**(d)** Earth-frame time = 1800/(0.98 × 3.00 × 10⁸) = 6.12 μs [1]; γ = 5.03, dilated half-life = 7.54 μs [1]; with dilation 1000 × 0.5^(6.12/7.54) = **570** [1]; without, 1000 × 0.5^(6.12/1.5) = **59** [1]
**(e)** Contracted distance = 1800/5.03 = 358 m, time = 358/(0.98c) = 1.22 μs [1]; 1.22/1.5 = 0.81 half-lives, giving the same **570** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pair the proper half-life with muon-frame time, or the dilated half-life with Earth-frame time; mixing frames scores nothing.

**12. (a)** m₀c² = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ J [1]; E = 4.80 × 10⁻¹³ + 8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ = 5.62 × 10⁻¹³ J, γ = 6.85 [1]; v = c√(1 − 1/γ²) = **0.989c = 2.97 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** m = γm₀ = **6.24 × 10⁻³⁰ kg** [1]
**(c)** v = √(2Ek/m₀) = √(2 × 4.80 × 10⁻¹³ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 1.03 × 10⁹ m s⁻¹ [1], which exceeds c [1].
**(d)** Electrons of increasing known energy were timed over a known distance [1]; speed approached but never exceeded c, matching the relativistic graph [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert MeV to joules (× 1.60 × 10⁻¹³) before combining with m₀c²; mixing units loses the method mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a "show that" Millikan radius only to 2 s.f.
- Taking node spacing as the wavelength.
- Using d instead of 2d in Fizeau's method.
- Writing "light is particles" instead of naming the photoelectric observations.
- Describing the STM without tunnelling, or the TEM without a thin sample.
- Mixing reference frames in muon calculations.
