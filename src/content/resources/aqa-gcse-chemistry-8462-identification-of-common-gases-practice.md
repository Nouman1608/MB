These questions cover section **4.8.2 Identification of common gases** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 at both Foundation and
Higher Tier. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is labelled Higher tier
only.

Learn the content first in the
[identification of common gases study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-identification-of-common-gases/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-identification-of-common-gases-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Match each gas with its test and positive result. Give the test and the result for each.

**(a)** Hydrogen **[2]**
**(b)** Oxygen **[2]**
**(c)** Carbon dioxide **[2]**
**(d)** Chlorine **[2]**

**2.** Zinc is added to dilute sulfuric acid in a test tube. Bubbles of gas form.

**(a)** Name the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how to test for it and the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the word equation for the reaction of zinc with sulfuric acid. **[2]**

**3.** A student bubbles a gas through limewater. The limewater turns milky.

**(a)** Name the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the compound that makes the limewater milky. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the balanced symbol equation, including state symbols, for the reaction in the limewater. **[2]**

**4.** A student decomposes hydrogen peroxide using manganese(IV) oxide and collects the gas.

**(a)** Name the gas and describe the test. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the manganese(IV) oxide does in the reaction. **[1]**

**5.** A colourless gas is collected from a reaction. A lit splint is put into the gas and goes out. A glowing splint does not relight. The gas does not turn limewater milky.

**(a)** State which of the four gases in the specification have been ruled out. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the splint going out is not enough to name a gas. **[1]**

**6.** A technician electrolyses sodium chloride solution using inert electrodes.

**(a)** Name the gas made at each electrode. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe a test for each gas. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why the test for the gas at the anode is done in a fume cupboard. **[1]**

**7.** A student tests a gas with damp blue litmus paper and with dry blue litmus paper. Only the damp paper is bleached.

**(a)** Name the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the paper must be damp. **[1]**
**(c)** State the colour of the bleached paper. **[1]**

**8.** Calcium carbonate is added to dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Describe how to show that the gas given off is carbon dioxide. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation for the reaction between calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**9.** Four test tubes contain hydrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and chlorine, but they are unlabelled. Plan a series of tests to identify each gas. **[6]**

**10.** A student bubbles carbon dioxide into limewater for several minutes. The limewater first turns milky and then becomes clear again.

**(a)** State what the student should do to make sure the test result is clear. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest why the student should use fresh limewater each time. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Burning (lit) splint at the open end of the tube [1]; burns rapidly with a **pop** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **Glowing splint** inserted into the gas [1]; it **relights** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Bubble through **limewater** [1]; it turns **milky (cloudy)** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** **Damp litmus paper** in the gas [1]; it is **bleached and turns white** [1] **[2]**

**2. (a)** **Hydrogen** [1]
**(b)** Hold a **lit splint** at the open end of the tube [1]; a **squeaky pop** shows hydrogen [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** zinc + sulfuric acid → zinc sulfate [1] + hydrogen [1] **[2]**

**3. (a)** **Carbon dioxide** [1]
**(b)** **Calcium carbonate** (insoluble) [1]
**(c)** Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l) [formulae and balance 1; state symbols 1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** **Oxygen** [1]; a **glowing splint** relights [1] **[2]**
**(b)** It is a **catalyst**, speeding up the reaction (and is not used up) [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** **Oxygen** (the glowing splint did not relight) [1]; **carbon dioxide** (limewater stayed clear) [1]; **hydrogen** (no pop; the splint went out rather than popping) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Many gases put out a flame (for example nitrogen), so it **does not identify** a specific gas [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Chlorine is not ruled out by these three tests. A fourth test, damp litmus paper, is needed.

**6. (a)** Cathode: **hydrogen**; anode: **chlorine** [1] [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Hydrogen: **lit splint**, a **pop** [1] [1]; chlorine: **damp litmus paper**, **bleached white** [1] [1] **[4]**
**(c)** Chlorine is **toxic** [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** **Chlorine** [1]
**(b)** Chlorine must **react with water** on the paper to bleach it [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** **White** [1]

**8. (a)** Collect the gas (or lead it through a delivery tube) into **limewater** [1]; shake or bubble the gas [1]; the limewater turns **milky** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [formulae 1; balanced 1] **[2]**

**9.** Any six of the following [1] each: test a sample of each gas in turn; a **lit splint** at the open end of a tube: the gas that burns with a **pop** is hydrogen; a **glowing splint**: the gas that **relights** it is oxygen; **limewater**: the gas that turns it **milky** is carbon dioxide; **damp litmus paper**: the gas that **bleaches it white** is chlorine; use only small samples in a fume cupboard because chlorine is toxic; state the result expected for each test; do the tests so that each gas gives a different result (the other gases give no positive result). **[6]**

**10. (a)** Stop bubbling as soon as the limewater first turns milky / shake gently with a small volume of gas [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Old limewater may have **absorbed carbon dioxide from the air** and be already milky or weaker [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the result without the test, or the test without the result.
- Using the glowing splint for hydrogen or the lit splint for oxygen.
- Writing "goes cloudy" without saying that the reagent is limewater.
- Using dry litmus for chlorine.
- Saying a gas "puts out a lit splint" is a test for carbon dioxide.
