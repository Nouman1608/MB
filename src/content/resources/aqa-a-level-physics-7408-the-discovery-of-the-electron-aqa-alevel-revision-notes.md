Full explanations and worked examples are in the [discovery of the electron study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-the-discovery-of-the-electron-aqa-alevel/); test yourself with the [discovery of the electron practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-the-discovery-of-the-electron-aqa-alevel-practice/).

## Equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| ½mv² = eV | Speed of electrons from an electron gun |
| eE = Bev, so v = E/B | Balanced (crossed) fields: undeflected beam |
| E = V/d | Field between parallel plates |
| Bev = mv²/r, so e/m = v/(Br) | Circular path in a magnetic field |
| e/m = 2V/(B²r²) | Fine-beam tube (combines the two above) |
| F = 6πηrv | Stokes' law for the drag on a drop |
| r = √(9ηv/(2ρg)) | Drop radius from field-off terminal speed |
| QV/d = mg | Drop held stationary |
| QV/d = mg + 6πηrv | Drop rising at terminal speed |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Discharge tube | Low-pressure gas between electrodes at a high p.d.; produces cathode rays and a glow |
| Cathode rays | Streams of electrons from the cathode of a discharge tube |
| Thermionic emission | Release of electrons from a heated metal surface |
| Electron gun | Heated filament plus accelerating anode in a vacuum, giving a narrow beam |
| Specific charge | Charge per unit mass (C kg⁻¹) |
| Terminal speed | Speed at which the resultant force on the drop is zero |
| Quantisation of charge | Charge exists only in whole-number multiples of e |

## Discharge tube in four steps

1. Stray positive ions accelerate to the cathode and release electrons.
2. Electrons accelerate towards the anode, ionising atoms (sustaining the discharge).
3. Electrons excite atoms by collision.
4. Atoms de-excite, emitting photons: the gas glows.

Low pressure is needed so electrons travel far enough between collisions to gain sufficient energy.

## e/m: one method to learn

- Electron gun at anode p.d. V_a produces the beam.
- Crossed E and B fields adjusted for zero deflection: v = E/B.
- Remove E: circular path of radius r in B: e/m = v/(Br). Or use e/m = v²/(2V_a).
- Accepted value: about 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹.

## Significance

- **Thomson**: e/m independent of gas and cathode material, so electrons are in all matter; about 1800 times the specific charge of the hydrogen ion (9.6 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹), so the electron is far lighter than any atom: a sub-atomic particle.
- **Millikan**: every charge a whole-number multiple of 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, so charge is quantised; with e/m, gave the electron's mass.

## Millikan method

1. Field off: time the drop's fall at terminal speed v₁.
2. Weight = Stokes' drag gives r, then m = (4/3)πr³ρ.
3. Field on: adjust V to hold the drop still (Q = mgd/V), or time it rising at v₂ (QV/d = mg + 6πηrv₂).
4. Repeat for many drops; values cluster at multiples of e.

Upthrust from the air is ignored.

## Exam traps

- Confusing thermionic emission (heated filament, vacuum) with the discharge tube (gas, high p.d.).
- Saying the glow is caused by ionisation alone: it comes from de-excitation.
- Using the field-on equation to find r.
- Putting drag upwards for a rising drop.
- Forgetting to convert plate separation from mm to m.
- Using v = E/B when the beam is deflected.
- Giving Thomson's significance without the hydrogen-ion comparison.

## Self-test

1. Why must the gas in a discharge tube be at low pressure?
2. What causes the glow in a discharge tube?
3. Calculate the speed of electrons from a 3000 V electron gun.
4. A beam is undeflected with E = 1.5 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ and B = 0.60 mT. Calculate v.
5. The same beam moves in a circle of radius 0.24 m in the magnetic field alone. Calculate e/m.
6. State the specific charge of the hydrogen ion and how the electron's compares.
7. Why did Thomson conclude that electrons are in all matter?
8. State Stokes' law.
9. A drop of mass 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg is held still between plates 5.0 mm apart at 490 V. Calculate its charge.
10. How many electronic charges is this?
11. Why is the radius found with the field off?
12. What did Millikan's results show?

### Answers

1. So electrons travel far enough between collisions to gain **enough kinetic energy to ionise** atoms.
2. Electrons **excite** gas atoms; the atoms **de-excite**, emitting photons.
3. v = √(2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 3000/9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **3.2 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹**.
4. v = E/B = **2.5 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹**.
5. e/m = v/(Br) = 2.5 × 10⁷/(0.60 × 10⁻³ × 0.24) = **1.7 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹**.
6. About **9.6 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹**; the electron's is about **1800 times larger**.
7. The same e/m was found **whatever gas** filled the tube and **whatever metal** formed the cathode.
8. The viscous drag on a sphere is **F = 6πηrv**.
9. Q = mgd/V = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 9.81 × 5.0 × 10⁻³/490 = **3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**.
10. **2e**.
11. With no electric force, **weight equals drag** at terminal speed, so r can be found without knowing Q.
12. Charge is **quantised** in units of e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was Thomson's measurement of e/m a turning point?**
It showed the same particle comes from every gas and cathode, so it is part of all matter, and its specific charge was about 1800 times the hydrogen ion's. Atoms therefore contain much lighter, charged particles and are not indivisible.

**How does Millikan's experiment find the mass of a drop?**
With the field off, the drop falls at terminal speed, where its weight equals the Stokes' law drag. That gives the radius, and the oil density then gives the mass.
