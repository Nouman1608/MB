This guide covers section 6.2, Right-angled triangles, in the Trigonometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus has four outcomes: know and use the sine, cosine and tangent ratios for acute angles in calculations involving sides and angles of a right-angled triangle; solve problems in two dimensions using Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry; know that the perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance to the line; and carry out calculations involving angles of elevation and depression. Angles are given in degrees, and answers should be in degrees with decimals correct to one decimal place. Knowledge of bearings may be required. After this guide, use the [right-angled triangles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-right-angled-triangles-revision-notes/) and the [right-angled triangles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-right-angled-triangles-practice/).

## What section 6.2 asks for

1. Choose the correct ratio and use it to find a side or an angle.
2. Combine trigonometry with Pythagoras' theorem in two-dimensional problems.
3. Use bearings, elevation and depression to set up right-angled triangles.
4. Use the shortest-distance fact.

The trigonometric ratios are not in the List of formulas, so you must remember them. Make sure your calculator is in **degree** mode.

## Naming the sides

For a given acute angle θ in a right-angled triangle:

- the **hypotenuse** is opposite the right angle (the longest side),
- the **opposite** side is across from θ,
- the **adjacent** side is next to θ, and is not the hypotenuse.

The opposite and adjacent sides change when you change which angle you are using. Label them every time.

## The three ratios

```
sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent
```

A memory aid is SOH CAH TOA. The ratios depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle.

## Finding a side

1. Label the sides O, A and H relative to the given angle.
2. Pick the ratio that includes the side you know and the side you want.
3. Write the equation, rearrange and calculate.

### Worked example 1

A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 12 cm and an angle of 35°. Find the side opposite 35° and the side adjacent to it.

```
opposite: sin 35° = O/12, so O = 12 × sin 35° = 6.88 cm
adjacent: cos 35° = A/12, so A = 12 × cos 35° = 9.83 cm
```

### Worked example 2

The side adjacent to an angle of 40° is 8 cm. Find the opposite side.

```
tan 40° = O/8
O = 8 × tan 40° = 6.71 cm
```

### Worked example 3

The side opposite an angle of 28° is 7 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

```
sin 28° = 7/H
H = 7 ÷ sin 28° = 14.9 cm
```

When the unknown is in the denominator, it ends up on the bottom of the division. A quick check: the hypotenuse must be longer than 7 cm.

## Finding an angle

Use the inverse function on the calculator (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹).

### Worked example 4

A right-angled triangle has opposite side 5 cm and adjacent side 12 cm. Find the angle.

```
tan θ = 5/12
θ = tan⁻¹(5/12) = 22.6°
```

### Worked example 5

Find the angles in a right-angled triangle with sides 7 cm and hypotenuse 10 cm, where 7 cm is opposite angle A.

```
sin A = 7/10, so A = sin⁻¹(0.7) = 44.4°
the other acute angle = 90° − 44.4° = 45.6°
```

Give angles to one decimal place. Do not round the ratio before using the inverse.

## Using Pythagoras and trigonometry together

Many problems need both. Find a missing side with Pythagoras, then an angle with trigonometry, or the reverse.

### Worked example 6

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3 cm and 4 cm. Find the angles.

```
hypotenuse = 5 cm (Pythagoras)
tan A = 3/4, so A = 36.9°
the other angle = 90° − 36.9° = 53.1°
```

## Angles of elevation and depression

The **angle of elevation** is the angle above the horizontal, looking up from the observer to an object. The **angle of depression** is the angle below the horizontal, looking down. The horizontal line through the observer is the reference.

### Worked example 7

From a point on level ground 50 m from the base of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 32°. Find the height of the tower.

```
tan 32° = height / 50
height = 50 × tan 32° = 31.2 m
```

### Worked example 8

From the top of a vertical cliff 80 m high, the angle of depression of a ship is 25°. How far is the ship from the foot of the cliff?

```
the angle between the line of sight and the horizontal is 25°
the angle at the ship, inside the triangle, is also 25° (alternate angles)
tan 25° = 80 / distance
distance = 80 ÷ tan 25° = 172 m
```

The angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the ship, because they are alternate angles between parallel horizontals.

### Worked example 9

From a point A, the angle of elevation of the top of a building is 30°. From a point B, 20 m nearer the building and in line with it, the angle of elevation is 45°. Find the height of the building.

```
let the height be h and the distance from B to the base be x
tan 45° = h/x, so h = x
tan 30° = h/(x + 20), so h = 0.5774 (x + 20)
x = 0.5774x + 11.547
0.4226x = 11.547
x = 27.3, so the height is 27.3 m
```

## Bearings

A bearing is measured clockwise from north. A ship's path forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose legs point north–south and east–west.

### Worked example 10

A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 070°. How far east and how far north has it travelled?

```
east = 20 × sin 70° = 18.8 km
north = 20 × cos 70° = 6.84 km
```

The angle is measured from north, so the north leg is adjacent and the east leg is opposite.

## Shortest distance from a point to a line

The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular distance. To find it, drop a perpendicular from the point to the line and use trigonometry or Pythagoras on the right-angled triangle.

### Worked example 11

Point C is 12 cm from A, and the line AC makes an angle of 35° with a line AB. Find the shortest distance from C to AB.

```
perpendicular distance = 12 × sin 35° = 6.88 cm
```

Any other point on AB is further away from C than the foot of the perpendicular, because the segment from C to that point would be the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.

## Isosceles triangles and slopes

Split an isosceles triangle into two right-angled triangles by drawing its line of symmetry.

### Worked example 12

An isosceles triangle has two sides of 10 cm and an apex angle of 50°. Find the base and the height.

```
half the apex angle = 25°
half the base = 10 × sin 25°, so the base = 2 × 10 × sin 25° = 8.45 cm
height = 10 × cos 25° = 9.06 cm
```

### Worked example 13

A ramp 5 m long rises 0.8 m. Find its angle of slope.

```
sin θ = 0.8/5 = 0.16
θ = sin⁻¹(0.16) = 9.2°
```

### Worked example 14

A rectangle measures 9 cm by 5 cm. Find the angle between a diagonal and the longer side, and the length of the diagonal.

```
tan θ = 5/9, so θ = 29.1°
diagonal = √(9² + 5²) = √106 = 10.3 cm
check: 9 ÷ cos 29.1° = 10.3 cm
```

### Worked example 15

A straight line joins (0, 0) to (8, 6). Find the angle between the line and the x-axis.

```
the horizontal step is 8 and the vertical step is 6
tan θ = 6/8, so θ = 36.9°
```

The gradient of the line is the tangent of the angle it makes with the x-axis.

## Common mistakes

- Having the calculator in radian or gradian mode.
- Labelling the opposite and adjacent sides from the wrong angle.
- Using the wrong ratio, so that the side or angle comes out too big.
- Rounding the ratio before taking the inverse.
- Putting the unknown on the wrong side of the division.
- Confusing angle of elevation with angle of depression.
- Measuring a bearing from east instead of north.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** an angle: show the ratio, then the inverse function, then the answer to 1 decimal place.
- **Calculate** a length: show the equation, the rearranged form and the answer to 3 significant figures.
- Draw and label a right-angled triangle for each part.
- Check that the answer is sensible: the hypotenuse is longest.
