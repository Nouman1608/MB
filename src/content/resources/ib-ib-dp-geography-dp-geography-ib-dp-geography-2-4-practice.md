## Section A

**1.** Define maximum sustainable yield. **[1]**

**2.** Outline how individual transferable quotas remove the "race to fish". **[2]**

**3.** State two environmental problems associated with coastal shrimp farming. **[2]**

**4.** Define marine spatial planning. **[1]**

**5.** Outline one reason why the plastic pollution negotiations of 2024 and 2025 did not produce an agreed text. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** An invented fish stock has a carrying capacity of 300,000 tonnes and a growth rate of 0.3 per year. Annual growth = r x N x (1 - N / K).

**(a)** Calculate the maximum sustainable yield. **[2]**

**(b)** The stock has fallen to 90,000 tonnes. Calculate its annual growth. **[2]**

**(c)** The fleet continues to catch the amount calculated in (a). Explain what will happen to the stock. **[2]**

**7.** Explain two reasons why a marine protected area may not increase the total fish stock in a region. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why climate change makes fixed fisheries agreements harder to manage. **[3]**

**9.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of offshore wind farms for coastal communities. **[4]**

## Section C

**10.** "Aquaculture is the most sustainable way to meet future demand for seafood." Evaluate this statement. **[10]**

**11.** Discuss the extent to which international agreements can protect ocean resources beyond national jurisdiction. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The largest catch that can be taken from a stock year after year without reducing its size [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Each owner holds a guaranteed share of the total allowable catch [1], so there is no benefit in fishing faster than others before the cap is reached, and owners gain from keeping the stock healthy because their share then becomes more valuable [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two [1] each: clearing of mangroves for ponds; pollution of coastal water by waste, feed and chemicals; spread of disease; salinisation of nearby farmland. **[2]**

**4.** Allocating areas of sea to different uses, such as energy, fishing, shipping and conservation, to reduce conflict between them [1]. **[1]**

**5.** States disagreed over whether the agreement should limit the production of plastic or focus on waste management and recycling [1], because plastic-producing countries and industries opposed limits on production [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** MSY occurs at N = K / 2 = 150,000 tonnes [1]. Growth = 0.3 x 150,000 x (1 - 0.5) = **22,500 tonnes a year** [1].

**(b)** Growth = 0.3 x 90,000 x (1 - 90,000 / 300,000) = 0.3 x 90,000 x 0.7 [1] = **18,900 tonnes a year** [1].

**(c)** The catch of 22,500 tonnes exceeds growth of 18,900 tonnes by 3,600 tonnes, so the stock falls [1]; a smaller stock grows more slowly, so the gap widens each year and the stock can collapse unless the catch is cut [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Fishing effort may move to unprotected waters nearby [1], increasing pressure there and cancelling out gains inside the reserve [1]. The reserve may be too small, or poorly enforced [1], so fish move out of it or are taken illegally, giving little net recovery [1]. (Accept: climate change moving stocks out of the protected area.) **[4]**

**8.** Warming water is moving many fish stocks towards the poles or into deeper water [1]. Agreements that allocate catches by country assume stocks stay in the same waters [1], so as stocks cross into another state's exclusive economic zone, allocations become unfair or outdated and disputes arise [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Advantage: jobs in construction, maintenance and port services, and a share of revenue, can support the local economy [1] [1]. Disadvantage: turbines and cables can exclude fishing boats from traditional grounds [1], reducing catches and income for fishing communities [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Indicative content:

- **For:** reduces pressure on wild stocks at a time when many are fully or over-exploited [1]; production can be expanded and controlled near markets [1]; shellfish and seaweed farming need no feed and can improve water quality [1].
- **Against:** farmed carnivorous fish depend on fishmeal and fish oil from wild catches [1]; mangrove clearance for shrimp ponds removes coastal protection and nursery habitat [1]; pollution from waste and chemicals [1]; disease, parasites and escapes affect wild fish [1].
- **Alternatives:** well-managed wild fisheries using quotas and protected areas can also be sustainable [1].
- **Depends on:** species farmed, feed source and regulation of sites [1].
- **Conclusion:** aquaculture will supply most growth in seafood, but it is the most sustainable option only for low-impact forms such as shellfish and seaweed, or where regulation is strong [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Indicative content:

- **What agreements provide:** UNCLOS sets out zones and the high seas regime [1]; the High Seas Treaty, adopted in 2023 and in force from 2026, creates a route to high-seas marine protected areas and environmental assessments [1]; the WTO fisheries subsidies agreement targets subsidies behind illegal fishing and overfished stocks [1].
- **Strengths:** shared rules give legitimacy and a framework for cooperation [1]; they can address the tragedy of the commons where no single state has control [1].
- **Limits:** they bind only states that join [1]; enforcement far from coasts is difficult, and illegal fishing persists [1]; economic interests block agreement, as in the plastics negotiations [1]; developing states may lack capacity to monitor their waters [1].
- **Conclusion:** agreements are essential but only partly effective; their success depends on participation, monitoring technology and enforcement [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* Questions about the future reward awareness of change: new agreements, shifting stocks and new uses such as offshore energy. Show the reader you know what has changed recently, without inventing figures.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using MSY as if it applied at any stock size.
- Treating an agreement's adoption as proof that it works.
- Writing about aquaculture without distinguishing types of farming.
- Ignoring small-scale fishers when discussing quotas and offshore energy.
