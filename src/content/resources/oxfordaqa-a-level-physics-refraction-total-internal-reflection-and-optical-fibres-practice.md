Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and treat air as having refractive index 1. Use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-refraction-total-internal-reflection-and-optical-fibres/) and [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-refraction-total-internal-reflection-and-optical-fibres-revision-notes/) first, and see the [course hub](/boards/oxfordaqa/a-level/physics/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/oxfordaqa/a-level/physics/) and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

## Questions

**1.** Light travels at 1.95 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in a plastic. Calculate its refractive index. **[2]**

**2.** A ray in air meets the surface of a liquid of refractive index 1.36 at 62° to the normal. Calculate the angle of refraction. **[3]**

**3.** A ray travels in glass of refractive index 1.62 towards a boundary with a liquid of refractive index 1.45.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle at this boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** The angle of incidence is 38°. Calculate the angle of refraction in the liquid. **[2]**

**4.** A transparent block has a critical angle of 44.0° when surrounded by air.

**(a)** Show that its refractive index is 1.44, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its critical angle when it is immersed in water of refractive index 1.33. **[2]**

**5.** A right-angled glass prism of refractive index 1.55 reflects a ray that meets its long face at 45°.

**(a)** Show that the ray is totally internally reflected in air. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the ray is not totally internally reflected if the prism is immersed in water of refractive index 1.33. **[2]**

**6.** A step index optical fibre has a core and a cladding. Describe the structure of the fibre and explain the functions of the cladding. **[5]**

**7.** A step index fibre is 800 m long. The core has refractive index 1.52 and the cladding has refractive index 1.48.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle at the core-cladding boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the largest angle to the fibre axis at which a ray can travel in the core and still be guided. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the difference in transit time between a ray along the axis and a ray travelling at the critical angle. **[3]**

**8.** Explain what is meant by material dispersion in an optical fibre, and why a laser gives less material dispersion than a light-emitting diode. **[4]**

**9.** A fibre loses 18% of the power entering each kilometre.

**(a)** Calculate the fraction of the input power that remains after 6.0 km. **[2]**
**(b)** A receiver needs at least 10% of the input power. Calculate the greatest length of fibre that can be used without an amplifier. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the process in the fibre that causes this loss. **[1]**

**10.** A step index fibre of length 5.0 km has core refractive index 1.50 and cladding refractive index 1.47.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle at the core-cladding boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the difference in transit time between the axial ray and the ray at the critical angle. **[3]**
**(c)** Assume that pulses can be sent no closer together than the time difference in (b). Calculate the greatest number of pulses per second. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest two changes that would allow a higher pulse rate. **[2]**

**11.** A student measures angles of incidence i in air and angles of refraction r in a rectangular block.

| i / degrees | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r / degrees | 13 | 20 | 26 | 31 | 35 | 39 |

**(a)** The student plots sin i against sin r. Use the first and last points to find the gradient of the line, and state what the gradient represents. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of light in the block. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why plotting a graph is better than using one pair of angles, and state one way to reduce the uncertainty in each angle. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** n = c / cs [1] = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.95 × 10⁸ = **1.54** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the ratio the right way round: a refractive index below 1 for a solid shows that c and cs have been swapped.

**2.** 1 × sin 62° = 1.36 × sin θ [1]. sin θ = 0.8829 / 1.36 = 0.6492 [1]. θ = **40.5°** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution into Snell's law before the answer, and check that the refracted angle is smaller than 62°, as it must be for a ray entering a denser medium.

**3. (a)** sin θc = 1.45 / 1.62 [1] = 0.8951, so θc = **63.5°** [1]
**(b)** 38° is less than θc, so the ray refracts. 1.62 × sin 38° = 1.45 × sin θ [1]. sin θ = 0.9973 / 1.45 = 0.6878, so θ = **43.5°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing 38° with the critical angle first shows that refraction, not total internal reflection, occurs; the angle then means something.

**4. (a)** n = 1 / sin 44.0° [1] = 1 / 0.6947 = **1.44** [1]
**(b)** sin θc = 1.33 / 1.44 = 0.9236 [1], so θc = **67.5°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question write the unrounded value (1.4396) before the given 1.44, so that the last step is visible.

**5. (a)** sin θc = 1 / 1.55 = 0.645, so θc = 40.2° [1]. The angle of incidence, 45°, is greater than 40.2°, and light is going from glass to air, so there is total internal reflection [1]
**(b)** In water, sin θc = 1.33 / 1.55, so θc = 59.1° [1]. The ray's 45° is now less than the critical angle, so most of the light is refracted out [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison of the incident angle with the critical angle, in words, is needed as well as the number. The critical angle is larger because the two refractive indices are closer.

**6.** The fibre has a thin central core of glass or plastic [1] surrounded by cladding of lower refractive index [1]. The lower index in the cladding means light meeting the boundary above the critical angle is totally internally reflected, keeping it in the core [1]. The cladding protects the core surface from scratches that would let light escape [1]. It stops light passing between neighbouring fibres in contact, so signals do not cross [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Lower refractive index" is the key phrase. A function such as protection earns credit only when it is tied to light escaping or leaking.

**7. (a)** sin θc = 1.48 / 1.52 [1] = 0.9737, so θc = **76.8°** [1]
**(b)** 90° − 76.8° = **13.2°** [1]
**(c)** Axial time = 800 × 1.52 / 3.00 × 10⁸ = 4.053 × 10⁻⁶ s [1]. Critical-angle path is 1.52 / 1.48 times longer, so time = 4.163 × 10⁻⁶ s [1]. Difference = **1.10 × 10⁻⁷ s** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The path length at the critical angle is L / sin θc, so the ratio of times is 1 / sin θc. Using the angle in degrees as the ratio is a common slip.

**8.** The refractive index of the glass depends on wavelength, so different wavelengths travel at different speeds [1]. A pulse from a source with a range of wavelengths therefore spreads out in time as the wavelengths arrive at different times [1]. An LED emits a wide range of wavelengths [1]. A laser emits a very narrow range, so there is little difference in speed within the pulse and less broadening [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the cause (index depends on wavelength) before the cure. A reference to a single wavelength alone does not explain why the pulse spreads.

**9. (a)** Fraction per km = 0.82 [1]. 0.82⁶ = **0.304** [1]
**(b)** 0.82ˣ = 0.10 [1]. x = ln 0.10 / ln 0.82 [1] = **11.6 km** [1]
**(c)** **Absorption** (and scattering) of the light by the glass [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The loss is multiplicative. Subtracting 18% for each kilometre (6 × 18 = 108%) is a clear sign the power law has been missed.

**10. (a)** sin θc = 1.47 / 1.50 [1] = 0.98, so θc = **78.5°** [1]
**(b)** Axial time = 5000 × 1.50 / 3.00 × 10⁸ = 2.50 × 10⁻⁵ s. Critical-angle time = 2.50 × 10⁻⁵ × 1.50 / 1.47 = 2.551 × 10⁻⁵ s [2]. Difference = **5.1 × 10⁻⁷ s** [1]
**(c)** Rate = 1 / 5.1 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = **1.96 × 10⁶ pulses per second** [1]
**(d)** Any two of: use a narrower (single-mode) core so only the axial path is possible; reduce the difference between core and cladding refractive indices; shorten the length between regenerators [2] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) each suggestion needs an effect on the path or time difference, not simply "better fibre". Give two distinct changes rather than one change reworded.

**11. (a)** sin 70° = 0.9397, sin 20° = 0.3420, sin 39° = 0.6293, sin 13° = 0.2250 [1]. Gradient = (0.9397 − 0.3420) / (0.6293 − 0.2250) = **1.48** [1]. The gradient is the refractive index of the block [1]
**(b)** cs = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.48 = **2.03 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** A graph uses all the readings, so one poor measurement shows up as a point off the line and its effect is reduced [1]. Draw a thin ray and mark it with distant points, or use a protractor with a fine scale and a normal drawn with a set square [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A gradient from two widely spaced points on the line is better than two adjacent readings, and the answer should carry no unit because n is a ratio.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking angles from the surface of the boundary instead of the normal.
- Using n2/n1 the wrong way round and getting a sine greater than 1.
- Calculating a critical angle for light moving into a denser medium, where there is none.
- Stopping at "total internal reflection occurs" with no comparison of the angles.
- Describing the function of the cladding without the words "lower refractive index".
- Saying that dispersion is caused by absorption, or that absorption lengthens pulses.
- Subtracting a percentage loss per kilometre instead of multiplying.
- Giving a refractive index with a unit.
- Rounding intermediate values and carrying the error into a time difference that is small.
