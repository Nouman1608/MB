## Questions

**1.** Explain why the muscles that move the knee must work as an antagonistic pair. **[3]**

**2.** **(a)** Describe what you would see in a longitudinal section of skeletal muscle under an optical microscope. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the arrangement of thick and thin filaments in one sarcomere. **[3]**

**3.** Two electron micrographs of one myofibril are taken at ×18 000. On the first, relaxed, a sarcomere is 41.4 mm long. On the second, contracted, it is 31.5 mm long. The A band is 27 mm long on both images.

**(a)** Calculate the actual lengths of the relaxed and contracted sarcomere, in µm. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in sarcomere length, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the length of the I band within one sarcomere when relaxed and when contracted. **[2]**
**(d)** State why the A band stays the same length. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how calcium ions and ATP bring about the shortening of a sarcomere. **[6]**

**5.** A sprinter's muscle contains 4.5 mmol of ATP and 14.5 mmol of phosphocreatine per kg. During a sprint, ATP is used at 2.0 mmol per kg per second. Assume one phosphocreatine regenerates one ATP.

**(a)** Calculate how long the ATP store alone would last. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how long the ATP and phosphocreatine stores last together. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why phosphocreatine suits the start of a sprint better than aerobic respiration. **[2]**
**(d)** State how the phosphocreatine store is restored after the sprint. **[1]**

**6.** The table shows data supplied for two muscle fibres from the same leg muscle.

| Property | Fibre P | Fibre Q |
|---|---|---|
| Capillaries touching each fibre | 2.5 | 5.5 |
| Myoglobin (mg per g) | 1.4 | 6.3 |
| Glycogen (mg per g) | 24 | 10 |
| Time to peak tension (ms) | 35 | 90 |

**(a)** Identify the slow twitch fibre, using two pieces of evidence from the table. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more myoglobin fibre Q has than fibre P. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one place in the body where you would expect to find fibre P. **[1]**

**7.** Five volunteers squeezed a hand grip as many times as they could in each of four consecutive 30 second blocks, with no rest. The table shows the number of squeezes.

| Volunteer | Block 1 | Block 2 | Block 3 | Block 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 62 | 58 | 49 | 41 |
| 2 | 70 | 63 | 55 | 48 |
| 3 | 55 | 52 | 44 | 38 |
| 4 | 66 | 60 | 50 | 40 |
| 5 | 59 | 55 | 47 | 39 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of squeezes in block 1 and in block 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the mean from block 1 to block 4, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the number of squeezes falls. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why a body becomes stiff soon after death. **[3]**

**9.** Explain the roles of calcium ions and tropomyosin when a muscle relaxes after stimulation stops. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why the muscles of the back suit slow twitch fibres, while the muscles that move the eye suit fast twitch fibres. **[4]**

**11.** A sample of 250 fibres was counted from a leg muscle of three people.

| Person | Fibres counted | Fibres that were fast twitch |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinter | 250 | 175 |
| Marathon runner | 250 | 45 |
| Untrained person | 250 | 120 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of slow twitch fibres in each person. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the fibre composition of the marathon runner suits her event. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two limitations of drawing conclusions from this data. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Muscles can only pull when they contract [1]; the quadriceps contracts to straighten the knee while the hamstring relaxes, and the reverse bends it, so one muscle cannot move a bone both ways [1]; the incompressible skeleton acts as a lever, so the second muscle is needed to return the bone [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming which muscle contracts and which relaxes shows the explanation; "they work together" alone describes less.

**2. (a)** Fibres lie parallel, with several nuclei in each fibre [1]; regular light and dark bands (striations) across the fibres [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Thin actin filaments are attached to the Z lines [1]; thick myosin filaments lie in the middle, in the A band [1]; thin and thick filaments overlap at the ends of the A band, with a central H zone of thick filaments only [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which filament is where, and name the bands; "light band and dark band" alone leaves out the filaments.

**3. (a)** Relaxed: 41.4 ÷ 18 000 = 0.0023 mm = **2.3 µm** [1]; contracted: 31.5 ÷ 18 000 = 0.00175 mm = **1.75 µm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (2.3 − 1.75) ÷ 2.3 × 100 = **23.9%** [1]
**(c)** A band = 27 ÷ 18 000 = 1.5 µm; relaxed I band = 2.3 − 1.5 = **0.8 µm** [1]; contracted I band = 1.75 − 1.5 = **0.25 µm** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** The A band is the length of the thick filaments, which do not shorten as the thin filaments slide [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the unit conversion on its own line; a slip in powers of ten is easy to make and hard to spot otherwise.

**4.** Calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum [1]; they cause tropomyosin to move, exposing binding sites on actin [1]; myosin heads bind to actin, forming actinomyosin bridges [1]; the heads tilt, which drags the thin filaments inwards [1]; ATP binds to the heads, which detaches them, and ATPase hydrolyses ATP so the heads return to their original position [1]; the cycle repeats, so the Z lines move closer and the sarcomere shortens [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the events in order and say what each does; a list of names without functions gains less.

**5. (a)** 4.5 ÷ 2.0 = **2.25 s** [1]
**(b)** Total = 4.5 + 14.5 = 19.0 mmol per kg [1]; 19.0 ÷ 2.0 = **9.5 s** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Phosphocreatine regenerates ATP in one step, so ATP is available very quickly [1]; it needs no oxygen, whereas aerobic respiration is slower to supply ATP at the start of exercise [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Creatine is rephosphorylated using ATP from aerobic respiration during recovery [1]
*Examiner insight:* A suggestion needs a reason; "it is quick" is stronger when you say why quick matters at the start.

**6. (a)** Fibre Q is slow twitch [1]; it has more capillaries (5.5 against 2.5), so more oxygen for aerobic respiration [1]; it has more myoglobin (6.3 against 1.4), or a longer time to peak tension (90 ms against 35 ms) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** 6.3 ÷ 1.4 = **4.5** [1]
**(c)** Eye muscles (or the muscles used in sprinting) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote both values from the table when you compare; a bare statement that one is higher does not use the data.

**7. (a)** Block 1: (62 + 70 + 55 + 66 + 59) ÷ 5 = **62.4** [1]; block 4: (41 + 48 + 38 + 40 + 39) ÷ 5 = **41.2** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (62.4 − 41.2) ÷ 62.4 × 100 [1] = **34.0%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The muscle uses ATP faster than aerobic respiration can supply it, so anaerobic glycolysis makes lactate [1]; lactate lowers pH, which reduces enzyme activity, and less ATP is available for cross-bridge cycling [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the first value, not the last, for a decrease; use the figures in the table when describing the trend.

**8.** ATP is no longer made after death [1]; without ATP, myosin heads cannot detach from actin [1]; the actinomyosin bridges stay in place, so the muscle remains contracted and stiff [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The link between no new ATP and no detachment is the point being tested.

**9.** Nerve impulses stop, so calcium ions are no longer released [1]; calcium ions are actively transported back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum [1]; tropomyosin moves back over the binding sites on actin [1]; myosin heads cannot bind, so the bridges break and the muscle relaxes [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the active transport, as that is where ATP is needed; this is a common omission.

**10.** Back muscles keep posture for long periods, so they need fibres that resist fatigue [1]; slow twitch fibres have many mitochondria and much myoglobin and make ATP aerobically [1]; eye muscles need very rapid movements [1]; fast twitch fibres contract quickly and powerfully, using phosphocreatine and glycolysis for rapid ATP supply [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each structure to its function; naming a muscle and a fibre type is not an explanation.

**11. (a)** Sprinter: slow = 250 − 175 = 75, so 75 ÷ 250 × 100 = **30%** [1]; marathon runner: 250 − 45 = 205, so **82%** [1]; untrained: 250 − 120 = 130, so **52%** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** She has a high proportion of slow twitch fibres (82%) [1]; these resist fatigue and make ATP aerobically, so they supply energy over a long race [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Only one muscle was sampled in each person, which may not represent the whole body [1]; there are only three people, so no pattern can be shown [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A limitation must be specific to this data; "more repeats" alone is not tied to the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the A band, or the filaments themselves, shorten.
- Writing that calcium ions bind to actin or myosin instead of moving tropomyosin.
- Not saying that ATP is needed to detach the head.
- Leaving out the active transport of calcium ions back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
- Calling phosphocreatine a fuel instead of a phosphate donor.
- Dividing by the later value when working out a percentage decrease.
- Mixing mm and µm in micrograph calculations.
- Comparing fibre types without quoting figures from the table.
