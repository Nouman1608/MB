These notes condense section 2.1 of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028), whose one outcome is to "describe the differences between elements, compounds and mixtures". Explanations and worked examples are in the [elements, compounds and mixtures study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-elements-compounds-and-mixtures/); exam-style questions are in the [elements, compounds and mixtures practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-elements-compounds-and-mixtures-practice/).

## Definitions to write word for word

- **Element**: a substance made of only one type of atom; cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions.
- **Compound**: a substance made of two or more different elements chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio.
- **Mixture**: two or more substances that are not chemically bonded together.
- **Pure substance**: a single element or a single compound, with nothing else present.

## The differences at a glance

| | Element | Compound | Mixture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kinds of atom | one | two or more, bonded | two or more substances, not bonded |
| Composition | fixed | fixed ratio | variable |
| Properties | its own | new; unlike its elements | those of each component |
| Made by | — | chemical reaction, with energy change | mixing, little or no energy change |
| Separated by | cannot be split chemically | chemical reaction only | physical methods |
| Melting / boiling | sharp, fixed | sharp, fixed | over a range |
| Formula | symbol, e.g. Ne or O₂ | formula, e.g. CaCO₃ | none |

## Physical separation methods for mixtures

| Mixture type | Method |
|---|---|
| Insoluble solid in a liquid | filtration |
| Dissolved solid from its solution | evaporation or crystallisation |
| Solvent from a solution | simple distillation |
| Miscible liquids | fractional distillation |
| Coloured dissolved substances | chromatography |
| Magnetic metal from other solids | magnet |

The point for section 2.1 is only that these are **physical** methods; the details of each technique belong to the experimental techniques topic.

## Particle-diagram rules

1. Count the **kinds of particle** in the box. One kind = pure substance. More than one = mixture.
2. Look **inside each particle**. All atoms identical = element. Different atoms bonded = compound.

| Box shows | Answer |
|---|---|
| single identical atoms | element |
| identical two-atom molecules, both atoms the same | element |
| identical molecules made of different atoms | compound |
| two kinds of element molecule | mixture of elements |
| element molecules and compound molecules | mixture of an element and a compound |
| two kinds of compound molecule | mixture of compounds |

## Examples to quote

- **Elements**: iron, neon, diamond, graphite, oxygen (O₂), ozone (O₃), chlorine (Cl₂).
- **Compounds**: water, carbon dioxide, sodium chloride, magnesium oxide, calcium carbonate, methane.
- **Mixtures**: air, seawater, any aqueous solution, alloys such as brass and steel, crude oil, ink.

Property-change example: sodium (reactive metal) + chlorine (toxic green gas) → sodium chloride (white, unreactive solid).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atom | the smallest particle of an element |
| Molecule | two or more atoms chemically bonded together |
| Chemically bonded | joined by a chemical bond; only a chemical reaction can separate them |
| Fixed ratio | the same proportion of each element in every sample of a compound |
| Alloy | a mixture of a metal with other elements |
| Solution | a mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent |
| Physical method | separation that does not change the substances, e.g. filtering, distilling |

## Exam traps

- Molecules made of one kind of atom (O₂, N₂, H₂, Cl₂, O₃) are **elements**, not compounds.
- Solutions and alloys are **mixtures**, however uniform they look.
- A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen is not water: the atoms are not bonded until they react.
- "Compounds keep the properties of their elements" is wrong; mixtures do.
- When asked for differences, write comparisons ("whereas"), not two separate lists.
- Diamond and graphite are elements even though they are giant structures.

## Self-test

1. Define a compound.
2. Give **two** differences between a compound and a mixture.
3. Classify chlorine gas, Cl₂, and explain your answer.
4. Classify steel, and explain your answer.
5. A box contains identical molecules, each made of one carbon atom bonded to four hydrogen atoms. Classify the contents.
6. A box contains some O₂ molecules and some CO₂ molecules. Classify the contents.
7. How could you show that a sample of liquid is a mixture rather than a pure compound, using its boiling point?
8. Why can sodium chloride not be separated into sodium and chlorine by filtering or distilling?
9. Give **one** piece of evidence that a chemical reaction has formed a compound when two elements are heated together.
10. Is ozone, O₃, an element or a compound?
11. Is a solution of sugar in water pure? Explain.
12. Which of these is a pure substance: air, distilled water, seawater?

## Answers

1. A substance made of two or more different elements chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio.
2. Any two: a compound has a fixed composition whereas a mixture's is variable; a compound has new properties whereas a mixture's components keep theirs; a compound needs a chemical reaction to separate it whereas a mixture can be separated physically; forming a compound usually involves an energy change whereas mixing usually does not.
3. Element: each molecule contains only chlorine atoms.
4. Mixture: an alloy of iron with carbon (and sometimes other elements), not bonded in a fixed ratio.
5. A compound (methane).
6. A mixture of an element and a compound.
7. A pure compound boils at a fixed temperature; a mixture boils over a range of temperatures.
8. The elements are chemically bonded; only a chemical reaction (electrolysis of the molten compound) can separate them.
9. Any one: heat or light given out; a new substance with different properties forms; the product cannot be separated by physical methods.
10. An element: every atom is oxygen.
11. No. It is a mixture of two substances, sugar and water, that are not chemically bonded.
12. Distilled water.
