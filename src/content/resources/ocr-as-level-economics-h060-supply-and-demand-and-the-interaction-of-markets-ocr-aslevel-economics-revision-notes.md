## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Demand | Quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at each price over a period |
| Supply | Quantity producers are willing and able to sell at each price over a period |
| Extension / contraction | Movement along a curve caused by a change in the good's own price |
| Increase / decrease | Shift of the whole curve caused by any other factor |
| Equilibrium | The price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied |
| Excess demand (shortage) | Quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price |
| Excess supply (surplus) | Quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price |
| Consumer surplus | The difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they pay |
| Producer surplus | The difference between the price received and the lowest price producers would accept |
| Joint demand | Goods bought together (complements) |
| Competitive demand | Goods that are substitutes |
| Derived demand | Demand for a factor or input that comes from demand for the final good |
| Composite demand | Demand for one good that has several uses |
| Joint supply | Two goods produced by the same process |
| Competitive supply | Goods that compete for the same resources |

## Shift factors

| Demand shifts right when | Supply shifts right when |
|---|---|
| Income rises (normal good) | Costs of production fall |
| A substitute's price rises | Technology improves |
| A complement's price falls | Indirect taxes fall or subsidies rise |
| Tastes or advertising favour the good | More firms enter |
| Population grows | Good weather (for crops) |

## Method for equations

1. Set Qd = Qs and solve for P.
2. Substitute P into either equation for Q.
3. Find the price-axis intercepts: set Qd = 0 and Qs = 0.
4. Consumer surplus = 0.5 x (demand intercept - P) x Q.
5. Producer surplus = 0.5 x (P - supply intercept) x Q.
6. For a shift, rewrite the shifted equation and repeat.
7. To find a shortage or surplus at a given price, put that price into both equations and subtract.

**Strawberry reminder:** Qd = 900 - 60P and Qs = 120P - 180 give P = £6, Q = 540 thousand; consumer surplus £2,430 thousand; producer surplus £1,215 thousand. After supply becomes Qs = 120P - 360: shortage of 180 thousand at £6, new P = £7, Q = 480 thousand, consumer surplus £1,920 thousand.

## Adjustment chains

- **Shortage:** price rises, demand contracts, supply extends, until equilibrium.
- **Surplus:** price falls, demand extends, supply contracts, until equilibrium.
- **Demand shifts right:** higher price and quantity; producer surplus rises.
- **Supply shifts left:** higher price, lower quantity; consumer surplus falls.

## Related markets in one line each

- Substitute's price up: demand for this good shifts right.
- Complement's price up: demand for this good shifts left.
- Demand for the final good up: demand for its inputs and workers shifts right.
- More of one jointly supplied good: supply of the other shifts right.
- More resources to one good in competitive supply: supply of the other shifts left.

## Exam traps

- Shifting demand for a change in the good's own price.
- Calling a shortage a "decrease in supply".
- Shifting both curves without two causes.
- Using the wrong triangle for consumer or producer surplus.
- Mixing up joint supply (beef and leather) with joint demand (printers and ink).
- Unlabelled diagrams: always label axes, curves, old and new price and quantity.
- Forgetting that both curves shifting can leave one variable uncertain.

## Self-test

1. What causes a movement along a demand curve?
2. Give two causes of a rightward shift of supply.
3. Qd = 400 - 20P and Qs = 20P - 40. Find equilibrium price and quantity.
4. Using question 3, calculate consumer surplus.
5. Using question 3, calculate producer surplus.
6. In question 3 the price is fixed at 9. Find the shortage or surplus.
7. Incomes rise and the good is normal. What happens to equilibrium price and quantity?
8. The price of a complement rises. What happens to demand for this good?
9. Give an example of joint supply.
10. Why is demand for builders a derived demand?
11. Demand rises and supply rises at the same time. Which variable is certain to change, and in which direction?
12. Why might a surplus of fresh fish be cleared faster than a surplus of houses?

### Answers

1. A change in the good's own price.
2. Any two: lower costs, better technology, lower indirect taxes, higher subsidies, more firms, good weather.
3. 400 - 20P = 20P - 40, so P = 11 and Q = 180.
4. Demand intercept is P = 20, so 0.5 x (20 - 11) x 180 = 810.
5. Supply intercept is P = 2, so 0.5 x (11 - 2) x 180 = 810.
6. Qd = 220, Qs = 140, so a shortage of 80.
7. Demand shifts right; price and quantity both rise.
8. It decreases: the curve shifts left.
9. Beef and leather, or petrol and diesel from crude oil.
10. Builders are wanted because people want new buildings, not for their own sake.
11. Quantity rises; the change in price depends on which shift is larger.
12. Fresh fish perishes, so sellers must cut prices quickly, while houses can be held unsold and their supply adjusts slowly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a "change in demand" the same as a "change in quantity demanded"?**
No. A change in demand is a shift of the curve; a change in quantity demanded is a movement along it caused by the good's own price.

**Why does consumer surplus fall when supply shifts left?**
Consumers pay a higher price and buy fewer units, so the triangle between demand and price shrinks.
