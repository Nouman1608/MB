**These notes condense key question 1.2, "How was Italy unified?", for Cambridge O Level History 2147.** It is the
Paper 2 prescribed topic for November 2026, so learn the people and dates well enough to test sources against them.
The full explanation is in the [Italy study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-unification-italy/);
exam practice is in the [Italy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-unification-italy-practice/).

## Italy in 1848: who ruled what

- **Piedmont-Sardinia** (capital Turin): House of Savoy; the only state free of Austrian control.
- **Lombardy and Venetia**: ruled directly by Austria, defended by the Quadrilateral fortresses.
- **Tuscany, Modena, Parma**: duchies under rulers tied to the Habsburgs.
- **Papal States**: ruled by the Pope from Rome.
- **Kingdom of the Two Sicilies** (Naples and Sicily): Bourbon monarchy, the largest and poorest state.

Austrian troops had crushed risings in 1821 and 1831, so Austria was the main obstacle to any change.

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Why not unified in 1848–49?**
- Radetzky's army recovered and beat Piedmont at Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849).
- Nationalists split: monarchists wanted a bigger Piedmont, republicans wanted a republic.
- Pius IX refused to fight Austria (April 1848), ending hopes of a papal federation.
- French troops crushed the Roman Republic (1849); Venice fell to Austria (August 1849).
- Peasants were largely uninvolved.

**Did Cavour help or hinder?**
- Helped: economic modernisation, Crimean War and Congress of Paris, Plombières deal, war of 1859, plebiscites in
  central Italy (1860), invasion of the Papal States to link with the south.
- Hindered or limited: aimed only at a northern kingdom; gave away Nice and Savoy; distrusted Garibaldi; hurried
  annexation of the south caused resentment.

**How important was Garibaldi?**
- Won Sicily and Naples with about a thousand volunteers (May–September 1860).
- Handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel at Teano (October 1860) instead of founding a republic.
- Failed against Rome at Aspromonte (1862) and Mentana (1867).

**Importance for other countries?**
- Austria weakened by defeats in 1859 and 1866; France gained Nice and Savoy but upset Catholics; Prussia gained an
  ally in 1866; the Pope lost his temporal power.

|---|---|
| Giuseppe Mazzini | Republican nationalist; founded Young Italy; led the Roman Republic of 1849 |
| Charles Albert | King of Piedmont who fought Austria in 1848–49 and abdicated after Novara |
| Victor Emmanuel II | King of Piedmont from 1849, first King of Italy from 1861 |
| Camillo di Cavour | Piedmont's prime minister; architect of the French alliance |

| Pius IX | Pope who refused to lead the national cause and lost the Papal States |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Risorgimento | "Resurgence": the movement for Italian national revival and unity |
| Young Italy | Mazzini's society for a united democratic republic |
| Statuto | The 1848 constitution of Piedmont, kept after 1849 |
| Quadrilateral | Four Austrian fortresses guarding Lombardy and Venetia |
| Plombières | Secret 1858 meeting where Napoleon III agreed to fight Austria |
| Villafranca | July 1859 armistice in which France made peace without Venetia |
| Plebiscite | A vote of the people on a single question, used to approve annexation |
| The Thousand (Redshirts) | Garibaldi's volunteers in the 1860 expedition |
| Roman Question | The dispute over the Pope's loss of Rome and his lands |
| Brigandage | Armed resistance in the south after 1861 against the new state |

## Exam traps

- **"Italy was unified in 1861."** Only partly: Venetia (1866) and Rome (1870) were still to come.
- **Treating Villafranca as a victory.** It was a setback: Napoleon III stopped the war, so Venetia stayed Austrian and
  Cavour resigned for a time.
- **Calling Garibaldi Cavour's agent.** Cavour neither planned nor fully controlled the expedition.
- **Ignoring foreign help.** Every major gain except the south (1860) depended on French or Prussian armies.
- **On Paper 2, treating a cartoon as a photograph.** Ask what the artist wanted readers to think and why.

## Self-test

1. Which Italian state was free of Austrian control before 1848?
2. What did Gioberti propose in 1843?
3. Name the two battles in which Austria defeated Piedmont in 1848–49.
4. Why did Pius IX's statement of April 1848 damage the national cause?
5. Who sent the troops that ended the Roman Republic in 1849?
6. What did Napoleon III expect to gain from the Plombières agreement?
7. Which territory did Piedmont gain in 1859, and which did it fail to gain?
8. How were Tuscany, Modena and Parma added to Piedmont in 1860?
9. Where did Garibaldi land in Sicily in May 1860?
10. What happened at Teano in October 1860?
11. How did Italy gain Venetia?
12. Why were Italian troops able to enter Rome in 1870?

**Answers:** 1. Piedmont-Sardinia. 2. A federation of Italian states with the Pope as president. 3. Custoza (1848) and
Novara (1849). 4. He refused to fight Catholic Austria, ending the idea of a papal-led Italy and dividing Catholics.
5. Louis Napoleon, president of France (later Napoleon III). 6. Nice and Savoy, plus influence in Italy at Austria's
expense. 7. It gained Lombardy but not Venetia. 8. By plebiscites in March 1860. 9. Marsala. 10. Garibaldi met Victor
Emmanuel and handed over his southern conquests. 11. Italy allied with Prussia in 1866; after Prussia beat Austria,
Venetia was transferred to Italy through France. 12. Napoleon III withdrew the French garrison to fight in the
Franco-Prussian War.
