## Section A

**1.** Define the term *isotopes*. **[2]**

**2.** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in the ion ⁴⁴₂₀Ca²⁺. **[3]**

**3.** Identify the **two** symbols that represent isotopes of the same element.

³⁹₁₉K &nbsp; ⁴⁰₁₈Ar &nbsp; ⁴⁰₁₉K &nbsp; ⁴⁰₂₀Ca **[1]**

**4.** A particle has 9 protons, 10 neutrons and 10 electrons. Write its symbol, showing mass number, proton number and charge. **[2]**

**5.** Gallium consists of 60.1% gallium-69 and 39.9% gallium-71. Calculate the relative atomic mass of gallium to one decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** State **one** difference between an atom of hydrogen-1 and an atom of hydrogen-2. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Lithium-6 and lithium-7 are isotopes. Explain why they have the same chemical properties. **[3]**

**8.** Silicon has three isotopes.

| Isotope | silicon-28 | silicon-29 | silicon-30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abundance / % | 92.2 | 4.7 | 3.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of silicon to two decimal places. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the answer is close to 28. **[1]**

**9.** An invented element J has two isotopes, J-44 and J-46. The relative atomic mass of J is 44.4. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows four particles.

| Particle | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 16 | 16 | 18 |
| B | 15 | 16 | 15 |
| C | 16 | 18 | 16 |
| D | 17 | 18 | 17 |

**(a)** Identify the **two** particles that are isotopes of the same element. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the particle that is an ion and give its symbol, including mass number, proton number and charge. **[2]**

**11.** A bar chart of relative abundance against mass for an element shows two bars: height 1 at mass 10 and height 4 at mass 11.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of the element. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the Periodic Table to identify the element. **[1]**

**12.** A student writes: "Carbon-14 is heavier than carbon-12 because it has two more protons." Explain what is wrong with this statement and correct it. **[2]**

**13.** Hydrogen-1 and hydrogen-2 both form water molecules with oxygen-16.

**(a)** Complete the information for the two hydrogen atoms: number of protons, neutrons and electrons in each. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of a water molecule made from two hydrogen-2 atoms and one oxygen-16 atom. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict whether water made from hydrogen-2 reacts with sodium in the same way as ordinary water. Explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Different atoms of the **same element** with the **same number of protons** [1] but **different numbers of neutrons** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Protons **20** [1]; neutrons 44 − 20 = **24** [1]; electrons 20 − 2 = **18** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** ³⁹₁₉K and ⁴⁰₁₉K (same proton number, different mass number) [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* ⁴⁰₁₈Ar, ⁴⁰₁₉K and ⁴⁰₂₀Ca share a mass number but are three different elements.

**4.** ¹⁹₉F: mass number 19 and proton number 9 correct [1]; charge **−** (F⁻), since there is one more electron than protons [1]. **[2]**

**5.** (69 × 60.1 + 71 × 39.9) ÷ 100 [1] = 6979.8 ÷ 100 = **69.8** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Hydrogen-2 has **one neutron**, hydrogen-1 has none / hydrogen-2 has a mass number of 2, hydrogen-1 of 1 / hydrogen-2 atoms are heavier (any one) [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Both have **3 protons** [1], so both have **3 electrons** [1] and therefore the **same electronic configuration** (2,1); chemical properties depend on the electrons [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** (28 × 92.2 + 29 × 4.7 + 30 × 3.1) ÷ 100 [1] = 2810.9 ÷ 100 = **28.11** [1]

**(b)** Silicon-28 is by far the **most abundant** isotope (92.2%), so the weighted average is close to its mass [1]

**[3]**

**9.** Let J-44 be *x*%: 44*x* + 46(100 − *x*) = 44.4 × 100 [1]
4600 − 2*x* = 4440, so *x* = **80** [1]
J-44 = **80%**, J-46 = **20%** [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** **A and C** [1]: both have **16 protons** (sulfur) but different numbers of neutrons (16 and 18) [1]

**(b)** **A** [1]: ³²₁₆S²⁻ (mass number 32, proton number 16, charge 2−) [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* B and C have the same number of neutrons but different proton numbers, so they are different elements.

**11. (a)** (10 × 1 + 11 × 4) ÷ (1 + 4) [1] = 54 ÷ 5 = **10.8** [1]

**(b)** **Boron** (Ar 10.8) [1]

**[3]**

**12.** Isotopes of carbon all have the **same number of protons** (6); carbon-14 has **two more neutrons** than carbon-12 [1]. It is heavier because of the extra **neutrons**: 8 neutrons compared with 6 [1]. **[2]**

**13. (a)** Hydrogen-1: 1 proton, 0 neutrons, 1 electron [1]. Hydrogen-2: 1 proton, 1 neutron, 1 electron [1]

**(b)** 2 × 2 + 16 = **20** [1]

**(c)** Yes, it reacts in the same way [1]: hydrogen-2 has the same number of electrons and the same electronic configuration as hydrogen-1, so the chemical properties are the same [1]

**[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "different numbers of protons" or "different number of electrons" in the definition.
- Explaining identical chemistry without mentioning electrons or electronic configuration.
- Dividing by 100 when the abundances are bar heights that do not total 100.
- Rounding before the last step, or giving the wrong number of decimal places.
- Treating particles with the same mass number as isotopes.
