These notes condense content item 2.5.1, Organisational structures, from Theme 2 Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples and model answers are in the [organisational structures study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-organisational-structures/). Test yourself afterwards with the [organisational structures practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-organisational-structures-practice/). All businesses mentioned are invented.

## The item at a glance

- **Structures, and when each is appropriate:** hierarchical and flat; centralised and decentralised.
- **Effective communication:** the impact of insufficient or excessive communication on efficiency and motivation; barriers.
- **Ways of working:** part-time, full-time and flexible hours; permanent, temporary and freelance contracts; technology's impact on efficiency and remote working.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Organisational structure | How jobs, layers of management and lines of authority are arranged in a business |
| Hierarchical structure | Many layers of management; each manager looks after a small group |
| Flat structure | Few layers; each manager looks after a larger group |
| Centralised | Main decisions made by a small group at the top or at head office |
| Decentralised | Decisions passed down to branches, departments or teams |
| Effective communication | The right message reaching the right people at the right time in a usable form |
| Insufficient communication | Too little information shared, or shared too late |
| Excessive communication | So much information that time is wasted and key messages are lost |
| Barrier to communication | Anything that stops a message arriving or being understood |
| Flexible hours | The worker has some choice over when hours are worked |
| Temporary contract | Employment for a fixed period or task |
| Freelance | Self-employed person paid for a particular job, not an employee |
| Remote working | Working away from the business's premises, often from home, using technology |

## Structures: recall in pairs

**Hierarchical vs flat (layers)**

| | Hierarchical | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Large businesses needing control and checking | Small businesses or those needing speed |
| Communication | Slower; message may change through layers | Quicker; fewer people pass it on |
| Cost | More managers' salaries | Fewer managers |
| Staff | Clear promotion ladder | Fewer promotion steps; managers may be overloaded |

**Centralised vs decentralised (decisions)**

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Small firms; decisions where consistency or safety matters | Many sites; local knowledge matters; skilled staff |
| Strength | Consistent, controlled decisions | Faster, local decisions; staff feel trusted |
| Weakness | Slow for distant branches; top managers overloaded | Branches may act inconsistently |

A business can mix them: prices centralised, local stock ordering decentralised.

## Communication: cause and effect chains

- Insufficient → errors and duplicated work → **efficiency falls**; staff feel left out → **motivation falls**.
- Excessive → time lost reading and replying, urgent messages missed → **efficiency falls**; staff frustrated → **motivation falls**.

Barriers to name and fix:

- Jargon or unclear wording → use plain language.
- Wrong method → detail in writing, sensitive news face to face.
- Too many layers → remove a layer or allow direct contact.
- Distance or remote staff → scheduled calls, shared online channels.
- Language differences → translations, demonstrations.
- Technology failure → a back-up method.

## Ways of working: which suits which need

| Need | Usually suits |
|---|---|
| A role needing continuity every day | Full-time, permanent |
| Busy weekends or lunchtimes only | Part-time |
| Keeping staff with caring responsibilities | Flexible hours |
| Seasonal peak or cover for an absence | Temporary |
| Occasional specialist task | Freelance |

Hours and contracts are separate choices: a worker can be permanent and part-time.

**Technology:** efficiency gains (faster routine tasks, fewer errors) against set-up and training costs; remote working saves premises costs and travel time but weakens supervision and team contact.

## Command words for this item

- **Explain:** fact + two expansion points, no context.
- **Outline:** two linked points in the context given.
- **Analyse:** extended chain of reasoning in context.
- **Justify:** recommend one of two options, using evidence about both.
- **Evaluate:** supported conclusion, including what it depends on.

## Exam traps

- Confusing layers (hierarchical/flat) with decision making (centralised/decentralised).
- Recommending "more communication" when the case shows overload.
- Calling a freelancer a temporary employee.
- Giving advantages of a structure with no link to the business's size, sites or staff.
- Forgetting the motivation effect when a question asks about communication: the specification names both efficiency and motivation.
- Choosing permanent full-time staff for a short peak without mentioning cost.

## Self-test

1. What does a hierarchical structure have many of?
2. Give one situation in which centralised decision making is appropriate.
3. Can a flat business be centralised?
4. Name the two things the specification says communication problems affect.
5. Give one sign in a case study of excessive communication.
6. Give two barriers to effective communication.
7. What is the difference between a temporary worker and a freelancer?
8. A café is busy only at weekends. Which way of working suits this?
9. Give one benefit and one drawback of remote working for a business.
10. A firm employs 4 extra staff for 10 weeks at 20 hours a week and £11.50 an hour. Calculate the cost.
11. Why might a business that is taking on many new, inexperienced staff keep a layer of supervisors?
12. Which structure is likely to have the longest time for a message to reach shop-floor staff?

**Answers**

1. Layers of management.
2. Any one: a small business; when every branch must act the same way; when mistakes would be costly.
3. Yes: few layers, but the owner still makes the main decisions.
4. Efficiency and motivation.
5. Any one: long or frequent meetings; very many emails; staff copied in on everything; urgent messages missed.
6. Any two: jargon; wrong method; too many layers; distance; language differences; technology failure.
7. A temporary worker is an employee for a fixed period; a freelancer is self-employed and paid for a job.
8. Part-time working (weekend staff).
9. Benefit: lower premises costs, or recruiting from a wider area. Drawback: harder supervision, or weaker team contact.
10. 4 × 10 × 20 × £11.50 = **£9,200**.
11. Supervisors give close guidance and checking, which new staff need; removing the layer would save salaries but risk more mistakes.
12. A hierarchical structure, because the message passes through more layers.
