This guide covers **sub-theme 1.2, work (کام)**, of Theme 1 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0), in more depth than the [work revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-work-revision-notes/). The specification names three aspects:

- **پیشوں میں تبدیلیاں**: changes in professions and occupations
- **کام کی جگہ پر عورتیں**: women in the workplace
- **کارکنوں کے حقوق**: workers' rights

Like the rest of Theme 1, the sub-theme is set in Pakistan. It is not a research subject, so it reaches you through translation (Paper 1 Section A, Paper 2 Section A), reading (Paper 1 Section B) and listening, summary and evaluation (Paper 3). The [Theme 1 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-theme-1-study-guide/) shows how it fits alongside family and education. When you have finished, try the [work practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-kaam-work-practice/).

## Aspect 1: changes in professions

The aspect is about **change over time**, so the best answers compare then and now and explain why. Organise the material in three layers.

**Which jobs are growing or shrinking.** Farming has long employed a large share of rural workers, but mechanisation and the division of land between heirs push young people towards towns. Services have grown: banking, retail, telecommunications, call centres, private education and health. Digital work is newer still: software development, online selling, delivery and ride-hailing apps, and freelancing for clients abroad.

**Which jobs carry prestige.** Many families still regard medicine, engineering and the civil service, entered through the competitive examination known as **مقابلے کا امتحان**, as the safest and most respected careers. A frequent theme in exam texts is the young person who wants to follow a different path, such as design, media or starting a business, and the family's reaction. This links work back to family attitudes in sub-theme 1.1.

**Why change is happening.** Group your reasons: technology (**ٹیکنالوجی**), education and a younger, larger workforce, urbanisation, and global markets. Then add a limit: new kinds of work do not reach everyone, and digital jobs need internet access, devices and skills that rural and poorer young people may lack.

Terms to add to the revision-notes list: **پیشہ ور** (professional), **ہنر مند** (skilled), **غیر ہنر مند** (unskilled), **خود روزگاری** (self-employment), **کاروباری** (entrepreneur, business person), **ڈیجیٹل معیشت** (digital economy), **مشینی کاشت** (mechanised farming).

## Aspect 2: women in the workplace

The revision notes list the main barriers. To write at A Level, separate three questions that texts often blur.

1. **Access**: can women enter the job at all? Issues include family permission, safe and affordable transport, distance and hiring practices.
2. **Conditions**: once employed, are they treated fairly? Issues include equal pay (**مساوی اجرت**), harassment, separate facilities, maternity leave and childcare.
3. **Progress**: can they rise? Issues include promotion, leadership roles and the expectation that women leave work after marriage.

Pakistan's Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act was passed in 2010; it requires organisations to have a code of conduct and an inquiry committee, and provides for an ombudsperson (**محتسب**) to hear complaints. You can use it as an example of a legal measure, then evaluate it: a law can exist while many women still hesitate to report harassment for fear of losing their job or their reputation.

Balance the barriers with change. Women work as doctors, teachers, bankers, pilots, police officers, journalists and entrepreneurs; home-based and online work lets others earn without travelling. A strong paragraph names a barrier, gives an example of change, and explains what makes the difference (education, family support, safe transport, or employer policy).

## Aspect 3: workers' rights

This aspect gives you the vocabulary of fairness at work. The key ideas:

- **The right to a fair wage**: governments announce a minimum wage, but in the large informal sector many workers have no contract and are paid less. Quote a current figure only from a named, dated source.
- **Safe conditions**: factory fires, brick-kiln work and mining accidents are often reported as examples of poor safety. Use a reported case from a reliable source if you refer to one.
- **Freedom from forced labour**: Pakistan's constitution prohibits forced labour, and bonded labour (**بندھوا مزدوری**, work to pay off a debt that never ends) was abolished by the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act of 1992. Reports suggest it has not disappeared, especially in brick kilns and agriculture, which is a good example of the gap between law and practice.
- **Child labour**: links work to education (sub-theme 1.3). Children who work are usually out of school.
- **Organisation**: the right to form unions (**مزدور یونین**) and to bargain collectively. Supporters see unions as protection; critics say strikes damage business. Show both.
- **Social protection**: pensions, health cover and holidays, which formal-sector workers are more likely to have than informal workers.

Use **حقوق** (rights) with **فرائض** (duties) for balance: employers have duties to workers, and workers have duties to employers.

## Worked example: translating into Urdu (Paper 2, Section A)

The Paper 2 translation is marked in short sections, one mark each, and verb and adjective endings must be correct. Here is an original English passage and a model, with the decisions explained.

> Many young women in Pakistan now work in offices and hospitals. However, some of them face difficulties on the way to work, and their families worry about their safety.

**Model:** پاکستان میں اب بہت سی نوجوان خواتین دفتروں اور اسپتالوں میں کام کرتی ہیں۔ تاہم ان میں سے کچھ کو کام پر جاتے ہوئے مشکلات کا سامنا کرنا پڑتا ہے، اور ان کے گھر والے ان کی حفاظت کے بارے میں فکر مند رہتے ہیں۔

**Decisions explained:**

- **بہت سی نوجوان خواتین ... کرتی ہیں**: the quantifier takes feminine form (سی), and the verb is feminine plural. "Now" (اب) sits naturally after the place phrase.
- **دفتروں اور اسپتالوں میں**: both nouns are in the oblique plural after the postposition میں.
- **ان میں سے کچھ کو ... کا سامنا کرنا پڑتا ہے**: "face" is best rendered with **سامنا کرنا** inside an obligation frame. The infinitive phrase is the grammatical subject, so the verb is masculine singular پڑتا ہے.
- **کام پر جاتے ہوئے**: "on the way to work" becomes a participle phrase meaning "while going to work". A word-for-word "کام کے راستے پر" sounds unnatural.
- **فکر مند رہتے ہیں**: "worry" as a state is idiomatic with رہنا; گھر والے is masculine plural.

## Reading technique for Paper 1, Section B

Section B mixes multiple-choice and open questions on several texts. On a work text, expect questions that test:

- **Detail**: a figure, a job title, a reason. Find the exact sentence before answering.
- **Inference**: what a speaker's tone suggests, for instance whether a factory owner sounds defensive. Look for words of attitude such as **بدقسمتی سے** (unfortunately) or **خوش قسمتی سے** (fortunately).
- **Distractors in multiple choice**: an option may repeat words from the text but change the meaning, for example turning **کم ہو رہی ہے** (is decreasing) into **ختم ہو گئی ہے** (has ended). Check tense and degree, not just vocabulary.

## Common mistakes

- Describing jobs without discussing **change**, which is the focus of the first aspect.
- Treating women's employment as one issue instead of separating access, conditions and progress.
- Confusing **مزدور** (labourer), **ملازم** (employee) and **کارکن** (worker, activist); the specification uses کارکن in "workers' rights".
- Writing **حقوق** with a singular verb: it is plural, so **مزدوروں کے حقوق محفوظ نہیں ہیں**.
- In translation into Urdu, putting the verb in the middle of the sentence as in English; Urdu is verb-final.

## Exam technique

- In Paper 3 summaries, attribute views clearly: **ایک ماہرِ معاشیات کے مطابق** (according to an economist), **ایک خاتون ملازم کا کہنا ہے کہ**.
- When evaluating, weigh the **need for jobs** against the **need for fair conditions**; examiners reward answers that see both.
- Use conditional structures (**اگر حکومت ... تو ...**) and passives (**قوانین بنائے گئے ہیں مگر ان پر عمل نہیں کیا جاتا**) to show the complex language the mark grids describe.
- Keep a short bank of linking phrases for cause and effect: **جس کے نتیجے میں** (as a result of which), **اس کی بڑی وجہ یہ ہے کہ** (the main reason for this is that).

## Before you move on

Check that you can, in Urdu: name the three aspects; give two growing and two shrinking types of work with reasons; separate access, conditions and progress for women; and explain one workers' right with a gap between law and practice. Then work through the [work practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-kaam-work-practice/).
