This guide covers sub-topic **8.4 Translocation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Sub-topic 8.4 has **no Core statements**: all three statements are Supplement content, so the whole sub-topic is **Extended only** and is tested on Papers 2 and 4. Core candidates still need the Core fact from 8.1 that phloem transports sucrose and amino acids.

The [transport in plants study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-plants/) defines sources and sinks and works through a young leaf becoming a source. This page goes further: where the sucrose and amino acids come from, what sinks do with them, how to read the direction of flow in a whole plant, more examples of parts that switch roles, and how to write full answers. Condense it with the [translocation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-translocation-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [translocation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-translocation-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## What 8.4 asks you to do

| Statement | Command word | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 8.4.1 | Describe | Translocation as the movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources to sinks |
| 8.4.2 | Describe | (a) sources as the parts of plants that release sucrose or amino acids; (b) sinks as the parts of plants that use or store sucrose or amino acids |
| 8.4.3 | Explain | Why some parts of a plant may act as a source and a sink at different times |

Every full answer contains four elements: **sucrose and amino acids**, **phloem**, **from sources**, **to sinks**.

## Where the substances come from

- **Sucrose.** Leaves make glucose in photosynthesis. Much of it is converted to sucrose, which is soluble and is the form of sugar carried in the phloem. A leaf can also store some starch during the day and convert it back to sugar at night, so it can keep releasing sucrose in the dark.
- **Amino acids.** Plants make amino acids using the sugars from photosynthesis and nitrate ions absorbed from the soil. Amino acids are needed wherever new proteins are made.

Glucose itself is **not** the sugar translocated in 0610. Write sucrose.

## Sources and sinks

### Sources: release sucrose or amino acids

| Source | Why it releases |
|---|---|
| Mature, photosynthesising leaf | makes more sugar than it uses itself |
| Storage organ in its "using" phase, such as a potato tuber sprouting in spring | breaks down its stored starch to sugar, which is sent to new shoots |
| Food store of a germinating seed | its stored food is broken down and sent to the growing root and shoot |

### Sinks: use or store sucrose or amino acids

| Sink | What it does with them |
|---|---|
| Growing shoot tips and root tips | **use** sucrose in respiration for energy and to make cellulose for new cell walls; use amino acids to make proteins |
| Roots in general | **use** sucrose for respiration, because roots cannot photosynthesise |
| Developing fruits and seeds | **store** food, for example as starch, for the next generation |
| Storage organ being filled, such as a potato tuber in summer | **store** sucrose as starch |
| Flowers | **use** sugar to grow and to make nectar, which attracts insects for pollination |

Learn both verbs in the definition: a sink **uses or stores**. Defining a sink only as a store loses the mark, because growing tips and roots use the sugar rather than storing it.

## Direction of flow

Water in xylem moves only upwards. Translocation in phloem can move substances **up or down** the plant, because the direction depends only on where the sources and sinks are.

### Worked example 1: reading a whole plant

A tomato plant in summer has mature leaves in the middle of its stem, a growing shoot tip at the top, ripening fruits on side branches, and a root system below. **Describe the movement of sucrose in the phloem.** **[4]**

- The mature leaves are the source; they release sucrose into the phloem [1].
- Sucrose moves **upwards** to the growing shoot tip, which uses it for growth and respiration [1].
- Sucrose moves to the developing fruits, which store it [1].
- Sucrose moves **downwards** to the roots, which use it in respiration [1].

The one source supplies sinks above and below it, which is why "phloem carries food down to the roots" is too narrow an answer.

### Comparing the two transport systems

| | Water transport | Translocation |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue | xylem | phloem |
| Substances | water and mineral ions | sucrose and amino acids |
| Direction | upwards only | up or down, from source to sink |
| Driven by | transpiration pull from the leaves | the location of sources and sinks |

## Why parts switch roles (8.4.3)

A part of the plant is a source when it releases more sucrose or amino acids than it uses, and a sink when it uses or stores more than it releases. As the part grows, matures or changes season, the balance can flip.

### Worked example 2: a seed

**Explain why a seed is a sink at one time and a source at another.** **[4]**

> While the seed is developing inside a fruit on the parent plant, it receives sucrose and amino acids through the phloem and stores them as food [1], so it is a sink [1]. When the seed germinates, its stored food is broken down and released to the growing root and shoot of the seedling [1], so the food store is then a source until the seedling's leaves can photosynthesise [1].

### Worked example 3: a carrot

Some plants, such as carrot, take two years to complete their life cycle.

- **First year:** the leaves photosynthesise; the swollen root receives sucrose and stores it as starch. The root is a **sink**.
- **Second year:** the stored starch in the root is broken down, and sugar is sent up to the new shoot and flowers. The root is now a **source**.

### The pattern to apply

For any organ, ask two questions:

1. At this time, is it **making or releasing** more than it needs? Then it is a source.
2. At this time, is it **growing or storing**? Then it is a sink.

This works for organs you have never studied, which is exactly what "explain" questions on 8.4.3 test.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "glucose" or "food" instead of **sucrose and amino acids**.
- Saying translocation happens in xylem.
- Defining a sink as "a store" without "uses".
- Saying phloem only carries substances downwards.
- Saying a leaf is always a source. A young, growing leaf is a sink.
- Calling roots a source because they absorb water and mineral ions. For translocation, roots are usually sinks.
- In switching-role answers, naming the two roles without giving the reason for each.

## Exam technique for 8.4

- **Describe** translocation: substances + phloem + sources + sinks, in one sentence.
- **Describe** a source or sink: use the syllabus verbs "release" and "use or store".
- **Explain** a role switch: give the stage when it is a sink and why, then the stage when it is a source and why.
- In plant-diagram questions, identify the source first, then trace every route to a sink.
