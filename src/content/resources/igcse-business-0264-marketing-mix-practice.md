## Questions

**1.** State two functions of the packaging of a food product. **[2]**

**2.** Nettlebrook Preserves makes jams and chutneys sold in small shops.
**(a)** Define brand image. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one disadvantage to Nettlebrook of developing a new product. **[2]**

**3.** Nettlebrook's cherry chutney had these yearly sales (units).

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 1,500 | 6,200 | 14,800 | 21,000 | 21,400 | 19,500 | 12,000 |

**(a)** Identify the stage of the product life cycle in year 2 and in year 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in sales from year 6 to year 7. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend and justify an extension strategy for the chutney. **[4]**

**4.** Saffrane Skincare makes a face cream at a unit cost of 18.50.
**(a)** Calculate the cost-plus price if it uses a mark-up of 60%. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of cost-plus pricing. **[2]**

**5.** Ludmoor Outdoor is launching a new headlamp. The unit cost is 40. Pricing at 95 is expected to sell 3,000 units. Pricing at 60 is expected to sell 7,000 units.
**(a)** Calculate the profit under each price. **[4]**
**(b)** Justify which pricing approach Ludmoor should choose. **[5]**

**6.** Define dynamic pricing and give one example of where it is used. **[3]**

**7.** Vellmar Garden Tools sells spades to a wholesaler at 12. The wholesaler sells them to retailers at 15. Retailers sell them to the public at 21.
**(a)** Calculate the wholesaler's mark-up as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the public price exceeds the price Vellmar charges. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason Vellmar may still prefer to use a wholesaler. **[2]**

**8.** Halgarth Bakery makes celebration cakes for families in one town.
**(a)** State two reasons why Halgarth might promote its cakes. **[2]**
**(b)** A cake normally costs 85. Calculate the price with a 20% discount. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend and justify one method of advertising for Halgarth. **[5]**

**9.** Rowanfield Tickets sells event tickets. Last year it sold 160,000 tickets, of which 38,000 were sold on its website.
**(a)** Calculate the share of tickets sold online, as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two advantages of ecommerce to Rowanfield. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage of ecommerce to its customers. **[2]**

**10.** Marlowe Candles is launching scented candles in recycled glass jars for buyers aged 18 to 25, who shop mostly online and compare prices. It has a marketing budget of 60,000, planned as 50% for social media advertising, 30% for discount vouchers and 20% for packaging design.
**(a)** Calculate the amount planned for each. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate which element of the marketing mix is most important to the success of this launch. **[6]**

**11.** Cairnbrook Furniture sells 2.4 million dollars of furniture a year at home. A study suggests it could sell a further 0.3 million dollars a year in a neighbouring country.
**(a)** Calculate the extra sales as a percentage of current sales. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage of entering this market. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage of entering this market. **[2]**
**(d)** Justify whether Cairnbrook should enter. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: protects the goods from damage [1]; keeps food fresh or gives information such as ingredients, or attracts customers and shows the brand [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two separate functions are needed; "protects" and "keeps safe" are one point repeated.

**2. (a)** The picture or impression customers have of the brand [1], for example its quality or values [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Developing it costs money for research and testing [1] and if customers do not buy it the cost may never be earned back [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A disadvantage scores best when the consequence is stated; "it costs money" alone is a bare statement.

**3. (a)** Year 2: **growth** [1]. Year 7: **decline** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (12,000 - 19,500) / 19,500 × 100 [1] = **38.5% fall** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Choice, for example adapting the product (new flavour) [1]; sales have fallen for two years from a peak of 21,400, so the product may look dated [1]; a new flavour is cheaper than a new product and keeps existing shops as customers [1]; advertising alone would not solve a falling trend and may stop working when spending stops [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table and label the fall; a recommendation with no use of the data stays at a general answer.

**4. (a)** Mark-up = 18.50 × 0.60 [1] = 11.10 [1]; price = 18.50 + 11.10 = **29.60** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Advantage: simple and every sale covers the cost [1]. Disadvantage: ignores what customers will pay or rivals charge [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the mark-up step; if a slip occurs, visible working shows the method you intended.

**5. (a)** Price 95: (95 - 40) × 3,000 [1] = **165,000** [1]. Price 60: (60 - 40) × 7,000 [1] = **140,000** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Skimming (95) gives the higher profit, 25,000 more [1], and suits a new product with few rivals [1]. But penetration gives revenue of 420,000 against 285,000 and builds share and brand awareness faster [1]. Skimming sells only 3,000 units and may attract rivals while customers wait [1]. Judgement, for example: choose skimming because the profit is higher and the headlamp is new, unless the aim is to build market share [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A justification must reach a decision; weighing the two prices without choosing one leaves the question unanswered.

**6.** Prices change according to demand, time or customer [1]; the aim is to charge more when demand is high [1]; example such as online ticketing or flights, where prices rise near the event [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the term first and then give a clearly different example; an example alone is not a definition.

**7. (a)** (15 - 12) / 12 × 100 [1] = **25%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (21 - 12) / 12 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The wholesaler buys in bulk and delivers to many small shops [1], which saves Vellmar the cost of selling and delivering to each shop itself [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the producer's price (12) as the base in (b); using the public price gives a different percentage.

**8. (a)** Any two: to inform people about the cakes [1]; to persuade families to buy [1]; to increase sales or compete with rivals. **[2]**
**(b)** 85 × 0.8 [1] = **68** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Method, for example leaflets in the local area [1]; the customers live in one town, so a cheap local method reaches them [1]; social media with a local focus is an alternative [1], but a small bakery may lack time to post often [1]; judgement tying the method to local families and a small budget [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A method must be linked to this business; "billboards reach many people" is general and wasteful for one town.

**9. (a)** 38,000 / 160,000 × 100 [1] = **23.75%** (23.8%) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Wider market: customers outside the area can buy [1] which raises sales [1]. Lower costs: fewer sales staff or premises needed [1] which can raise profit [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Customers may worry about the security of payment details [1], so some will not buy online or may be defrauded [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) asks about customers; an answer about the business's website costs does not answer it.

**10. (a)** Social media 60,000 × 0.50 = **30,000** [1]; vouchers 60,000 × 0.30 = **18,000** [1]; packaging 60,000 × 0.20 = **12,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Price: buyers compare prices, so a competitive price is vital [1]. Promotion: the target group is online, so social media reaches them cheaply, and half the budget is placed there [1]. Product: recycled jars fit the buyers' values, but a good product still needs customers to hear of it [1]. Place: online selling fits where they shop [1]. Judgement: price or promotion is most important, for example promotion because a new brand with no awareness cannot sell [1], with a reason why the other matters less [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a named choice and a reason it beats the alternatives; a tour of all four Ps gives no judgement.

**11. (a)** 0.3 / 2.4 × 100 [1] = **12.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** More sales [1] so more revenue and profit, or risk is spread if home sales fall [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Cultural differences, for example different style tastes [1] so the furniture may not sell as expected [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** For: a 12.5% rise in sales [1]. Against: it lacks knowledge of the market and the legal rules [1]. Judgement: enter, but test the market first through an agent or a small launch [1], because extra sales justify the risk if costs are controlled [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A justification needs a decision and a condition or reason; repeating the advantages from (b) and (c) adds nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong base in percentage change; divide by the starting value.
- Giving a calculation answer with no units or currency.
- Describing a method generally instead of linking it to the business in the stem.
- Defining a term by repeating the term itself.
- Writing two advantages that are the same point in different words.
- Answering for the business when the question asks about customers, or the reverse.
- Listing options in a recommendation question and never choosing one.
- Calling a flat peak decline, or the reverse, in a life cycle table.
