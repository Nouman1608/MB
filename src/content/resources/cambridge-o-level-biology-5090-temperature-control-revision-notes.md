These notes condense subtopic **14.5 Temperature control** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the structures of the skin, insulation, the control centre in the brain, and how sweating, shivering, hair erector muscles and the width of arterioles keep the body temperature steady. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [14.5 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-temperature-control/). Then try the [14.5 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-temperature-control-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Core body temperature | About 37 degrees Celsius, kept constant by negative feedback |
| Why constant | Enzymes work best at their optimum temperature |
| Control centre | The hypothalamus in the brain |
| Detectors | Temperature receptors in the skin and in the brain |
| Effectors | Sweat glands, skeletal muscles, hair erector muscles, arterioles in the skin |

## Skin structures

| Structure | Role |
|---|---|
| Hair | Traps air when raised; insulates |
| Hair erector muscle | Raises or lowers the hair |
| Sweat gland | Makes sweat for evaporative cooling |
| Receptor | Detects temperature |
| Sensory neurone | Carries impulses to the CNS |
| Blood vessel (arteriole and capillaries) | Carries blood near the surface; width changes heat loss |
| Fatty tissue | Insulation under the skin |

## Insulation

- Fatty tissue and hair reduce heat loss.
- Trapped air is a poor conductor of heat.
- Insulation does not produce heat; it slows heat loss.

## The control loop

1. Receptors detect a change from 37 degrees Celsius.
2. Sensory neurones carry impulses to the hypothalamus.
3. The hypothalamus sends impulses along motor neurones to the effectors.
4. Effectors change heat loss or heat production.
5. Temperature returns to the set point.

## Responses compared

| Response | When hot | When cold |
|---|---|---|
| Sweating | More sweat; evaporation takes heat from the skin | Little or no sweat |
| Arterioles in skin | Vasodilation; more blood to the surface; more heat lost | Vasoconstriction; less blood to the surface; less heat lost |
| Hair erector muscles | Relax; hairs flat; less insulation | Contract; hairs upright; more insulation |
| Shivering | None | Skeletal muscles contract rapidly; respiration releases heat |

## Key explanations

- **Sweating cools** because the evaporation of water needs energy, and this energy is taken from the skin.
- **Vasodilation:** the muscle in the arteriole wall relaxes, the arteriole widens, and more blood flows through the capillaries at the surface, so more heat is lost by radiation.
- **Vasoconstriction:** the muscle in the wall contracts, the arteriole narrows, and less blood reaches the surface, so less heat is lost.
- **Shivering:** rapid muscle contractions use respiration, which releases heat that warms the blood.
- **Hairs upright:** trap a thicker layer of air next to the skin.

## Link to negative feedback

| Part of the loop | In temperature control |
|---|---|
| Set point | About 37 degrees Celsius |
| Receptors | Skin temperature receptors; receptors in the brain monitoring blood |
| Control centre | Hypothalamus |
| Effectors | Sweat glands, arterioles, hair erector muscles, skeletal muscles |
| Response | Gain or loss of heat, returning the temperature to the set point |

**If too hot:** more heat lost (sweating, vasodilation, hairs flat). **If too cold:** heat conserved and made (vasoconstriction, hairs upright, shivering, less sweat). When the core temperature returns to normal, the responses reduce.

## Quick diagram checklist for the skin

- Label the hair, hair erector muscle and sweat gland, with the sweat pore on the surface.
- Show the arteriole to the skin with capillaries near the surface, and a vein carrying blood away.
- Show the layer of fatty tissue beneath the skin.
- Show receptors in the skin with a sensory neurone leading away to the CNS.
- Show a motor neurone from the CNS to the effectors.

## Worked calculation

*Skin blood flow is 24 units in a warm room and 9 units in a cold room. Find the percentage decrease.*

1. Decrease = 24 - 9 = 15 units.
2. Percentage = 15 divided by 24 x 100 = 62.5 per cent.

Divide by the starting value, 24. The data are consistent with vasoconstriction in the cold.

## Skin appearance

| Condition | Appearance | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Hot | Red, sweaty | Vasodilation brings more blood near the surface; sweat is made |
| Cold | Pale, goose pimples | Vasoconstriction reduces surface blood flow; hairs are raised |

## Short model answers

**Explain how sweating helps to cool the body.** Sweat is released onto the skin. When it evaporates, it takes energy as heat from the skin, so the body is cooled.

**Explain what happens to skin arterioles on a cold day.** They undergo vasoconstriction. The muscle in the walls contracts, so the arterioles narrow and less blood flows through the surface capillaries, so less heat is lost.

**State the role of the hypothalamus.** It receives impulses from temperature receptors, acts as the control centre and sends impulses to the effectors.

**Explain why shivering warms the body.** Skeletal muscles contract rapidly and respire, releasing heat that warms the blood.

**Describe the role of fatty tissue.** It insulates the body, reducing heat loss.

## Exam traps

- Blood vessels do **not** move towards or away from the skin. The **arterioles** widen or narrow.
- Capillaries cannot dilate or constrict. Do not write "capillaries dilate".
- Sweat cools by **evaporation**, not because it is cold.
- Vasodilation means **more** heat lost; vasoconstriction means **less**.
- The hypothalamus is part of the **brain**, not a gland on the skin.
- Hairs trap air; air is the insulator.
- Shivering is involuntary and releases heat from respiration.
- Do not forget the receptors and the sensory neurones in a full description.

## Self-test

1. What is the normal core body temperature?
2. Why must it stay constant?
3. Name the part of the brain that controls temperature.
4. Name two layers of insulation in a mammal.
5. What happens to the sweat glands when the body is too hot?
6. Why does evaporation of sweat cool the skin?
7. What is vasodilation?
8. What is vasoconstriction?
9. What do hair erector muscles do in the cold?
10. Why does shivering raise body temperature?
11. Why do the hairs lie flat in the heat?
12. Which neurones carry impulses from the skin receptors?

### Answers

1. About 37 degrees Celsius.
2. Enzymes work best at their optimum temperature.
3. The hypothalamus.
4. Fatty tissue under the skin and hair.
5. They release more sweat.
6. Evaporation needs energy, which is taken as heat from the skin.
7. The widening of arterioles that supply the skin capillaries, so more blood flows near the surface.
8. The narrowing of arterioles that supply the skin capillaries, so less blood flows near the surface.
9. They contract and raise the hairs, trapping more air.
10. The muscles respire rapidly, releasing heat.
11. Less air is trapped, so less insulation and more heat loss.
12. Sensory neurones.
