These notes condense section **3.3.10.2 Electrophilic substitution** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Electrophilic substitution study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electrophilic-substitution-benzene/), and exam-style questions are in the [Electrophilic substitution practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electrophilic-substitution-benzene-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [Bonding in benzene revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-benzene-bonding-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- State that electrophilic attack on benzene gives **substitution**, limited to **monosubstitution**.
- Outline the mechanism of **nitration**, including generation of the **nitronium ion**.
- Outline the mechanism of **Friedel–Crafts acylation** using **AlCl₃** as catalyst.
- State uses: nitration for **explosives** and **amines**; acylation as a **synthesis step**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrophile | Electron-pair acceptor |
| Electrophilic substitution | An electrophile replaces an H atom on the ring |
| Nitronium ion | NO₂⁺, the electrophile in nitration |
| Acylium ion | RCO⁺, the electrophile in acylation |
| Intermediate | Positively charged species between the two steps |
| Catalyst | Speeds up the reaction and is regenerated |
| Anhydrous | Containing no water |

## The two-stage pattern

```
C₆H₆ + E⁺ → [C₆H₆E]⁺ → C₆H₅E + H⁺
```

1. **Arrow from the delocalised ring to E⁺.** The intermediate has H and E on the same carbon; the + is spread over the other five carbons (horseshoe).
2. **Arrow from the C–H bond into the ring.** The ring is restored and H⁺ is lost.
3. H⁺ reacts with the anion from the catalyst, regenerating the catalyst.

## Nitration

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reagents | Concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ |
| Temperature | About 50 °C (below 55 °C to prevent more than one NO₂ group) |
| Electrophile | NO₂⁺ |
| Product | Nitrobenzene, C₆H₅NO₂ |

Generation of the electrophile:

```
HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O
```

Catalyst regenerated: H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → H₂SO₄.

Equation: C₆H₆ + HNO₃ → C₆H₅NO₂ + H₂O.

Uses: explosives (such as TNT); reduction of nitro compounds to **aromatic amines** (phenylamine) for **dyes**.

## Friedel–Crafts acylation

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reagents | Acyl chloride (for example CH₃COCl) and anhydrous AlCl₃ |
| Conditions | Reflux, dry conditions |
| Electrophile | Acylium ion, CH₃CO⁺ |
| Product | Phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃ (for CH₃COCl) |

Generation of the electrophile:

```
CH₃COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
```

Catalyst regenerated: H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl.

Equation: C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl.

## Mechanism marking points

| Mark | For |
|---|---|
| 1 | Generation of the electrophile (equation) |
| 2 | Arrow from the ring to the electrophile |
| 3 | Intermediate with H and E on one carbon and a + in the ring |
| 4 | Arrow from the C–H bond back into the ring |
| 5 | Products: substituted benzene and H⁺ |
| 6 | Regeneration of the catalyst (when asked) |

## Why a strong electrophile and a catalyst?

- The ring is **stable** because the electrons are delocalised.
- Its electron density is lower than at a localised C=C.
- So it attacks only strongly electrophilic species: NO₂⁺ and RCO⁺.

## Comparing nitration and acylation

| | Nitration | Acylation |
|---|---|---|
| Electrophile | NO₂⁺ | RCO⁺ |
| Catalyst | H₂SO₄ | AlCl₃ |
| By-product | H₂O | HCl |
| Special condition | About 50 °C | Anhydrous |

## Calculations

- Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100.
- Benzene to nitrobenzene is 1 : 1, so n(nitrobenzene) = n(benzene).
- Benzene to phenylethanone is 1 : 1.

Example: 0.200 mol of benzene gives at most 0.200 × 123.0 = 24.6 g of nitrobenzene (Mᵣ 123.0).

## Exam traps

- Arrow starting at the electrophile rather than at the ring.
- Positive charge placed on one carbon only in the intermediate.
- No arrow to restore the ring.
- Catalyst regeneration missing.
- Dilute acids, or only one acid, for nitration.
- A high temperature for nitration.
- Wet or aqueous AlCl₃ in acylation.
- NO₂ or HNO₃ given as the electrophile.

## Self-test

1. What is meant by electrophilic substitution?
2. Give the reagents and temperature for nitration.
3. Write an equation for the generation of the nitronium ion.
4. What is the role of sulfuric acid?
5. Why is the temperature kept below 55 °C?
6. Name one use of nitrobenzene derivatives in industry.
7. Give the reagents for Friedel–Crafts acylation.
8. Write an equation for the formation of the acylium ion from ethanoyl chloride.
9. Why must the conditions be anhydrous?
10. Where does the first arrow start in the mechanism?
11. Why does the final step restore the ring?
12. Name the product of benzene with ethanoyl chloride and AlCl₃.

### Answers

1. An electrophile replaces a hydrogen atom on the ring.
2. Concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids at about 50 °C.
3. HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O.
4. It is a catalyst: it helps form NO₂⁺ and is regenerated.
5. To prevent substitution of more than one hydrogen.
6. Explosives, or reduction to amines for dyes.
7. An acyl chloride and anhydrous aluminium chloride, heated under reflux.
8. CH₃COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻.
9. AlCl₃ reacts with water, so it would stop working as a catalyst.
10. On the delocalised ring (the circle).
11. Loss of H⁺ returns the electrons to the ring, which restores the stable delocalised system.
12. Phenylethanone.
