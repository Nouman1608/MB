These questions cover outcomes 1.5.1 to 1.5.3 (the Unit 1 ratios, appraising sole traders and partnerships, and using ratios for projections) in Unit 1 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11). All amounts are in dollars and all businesses are invented. Use 365 days in a year and give percentages and days to one decimal place, and ratios to two decimal places.

## Section A

**1.** A business has revenue of 60,000 and gross profit of 18,000. Calculate the gross profit as a percentage of revenue and the percentage mark-up (one decimal place). **[3]**

**2.** A business adds a mark-up of 50% to cost. Calculate the gross profit as a percentage of revenue. **[2]**

**3.** Cost of sales was 84,000. Inventory was 9,000 at the start of the year and 11,000 at the end. Calculate the inventory turnover in times and in days (one decimal place). **[3]**

**4.** Trade receivables are 15,500 and credit sales for the year were 124,000. Calculate the trade receivables collection period in days. **[2]**

**5.** Current assets are 36,000, including inventory of 14,000. Current liabilities are 18,000. Calculate the current ratio and the liquid ratio (acid test). **[3]**

## Section B

Harper Bakes is a sole trader. Extracts from the financial statements are below.

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 150,000 | 168,000 |
| Cost of sales | 105,000 | 121,000 |
| Expenses | 33,000 | 36,600 |
| Credit sales (80% of revenue) | 120,000 | 134,400 |
| Credit purchases | 100,000 | 118,000 |
| Average inventory | 14,000 | 17,500 |
| Inventory at year end | 15,000 | 20,000 |
| Trade receivables | 12,000 | 21,000 |
| Bank | 3,000 | 1,000 |
| Trade payables | 9,100 | 13,000 |
| Non-current assets | 80,000 | 80,000 |

**6. (a)** Calculate for both years: gross profit as a percentage of revenue, profit for the year as a percentage of revenue, and return on capital employed. Capital employed is non-current assets plus current assets less current liabilities. **[6]**

**(b)** Calculate for both years: inventory days, trade receivables collection period and trade payables payment period. **[6]**

**(c)** Calculate for both years: the current ratio and the liquid ratio. **[4]**

**(d)** Evaluate the performance of Harper Bakes in 2026 compared with 2025, and make **one** recommendation. **[8]**

**7.** For 2027, Harper Bakes forecasts revenue 5% higher than 2026, a gross profit percentage of 28%, and 80% of sales on credit. The receivables collection period is expected to be 60 days. Calculate the forecast gross profit, cost of sales and trade receivables. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** limitations of using ratios to appraise a business. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why a very high current ratio might not be a sign of good liquidity management. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Gross profit percentage 18,000 ÷ 60,000 × 100 = **30.0%** [1]; cost of sales 60,000 − 18,000 = 42,000 [1]; mark-up 18,000 ÷ 42,000 × 100 = **42.9%** [1].

**2.** For cost of 100, gross profit is 50 and revenue is 150 [1]; margin = 50 ÷ 150 × 100 = **33.3%** [1].

**3.** Average inventory (9,000 + 11,000) ÷ 2 = 10,000 [1]; turnover 84,000 ÷ 10,000 = **8.4 times** [1]; days 10,000 ÷ 84,000 × 365 = **43.5 days** [1].

**4.** 15,500 ÷ 124,000 × 365 [1] = **45.6 days** [1].

**5.** Current ratio 36,000 : 18,000 = **2.00 : 1** [1]; liquid assets 36,000 − 14,000 = 22,000 [1]; liquid ratio 22,000 : 18,000 = **1.22 : 1** [1].

**6. (a)**

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross profit | 45,000 | 47,000 |
| Profit for the year | 12,000 | 10,400 |
| Current assets | 30,000 | 42,000 |
| Capital employed | 100,900 | 109,000 |
| Gross profit % | 30.0% | 28.0% |
| Profit for the year % | 8.0% | 6.2% |
| ROCE | 11.9% | 9.5% |

Gross profit % 45,000 ÷ 150,000 and 47,000 ÷ 168,000 [2]; profit % 12,000 ÷ 150,000 and 10,400 ÷ 168,000 [2]; ROCE with capital employed 100,900 (80,000 + 30,000 − 9,100) and 109,000 (80,000 + 42,000 − 13,000) [2]. Profit 2026: 47,000 − 36,600 = 10,400. Profit 2025: 45,000 − 33,000 = 12,000.

**(b)**

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory days | 14,000 ÷ 105,000 × 365 = **48.7** | 17,500 ÷ 121,000 × 365 = **52.8** |
| Receivables days | 12,000 ÷ 120,000 × 365 = **36.5** | 21,000 ÷ 134,400 × 365 = **57.0** |
| Payables days | 9,100 ÷ 100,000 × 365 = **33.2** | 13,000 ÷ 118,000 × 365 = **40.2** |

One mark for each correct answer in the table [6].

**(c)**

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current ratio | 30,000 : 9,100 = **3.30 : 1** | 42,000 : 13,000 = **3.23 : 1** |
| Liquid ratio | 15,000 : 9,100 = **1.65 : 1** | 22,000 : 13,000 = **1.69 : 1** |

Current ratio for both years [2]; liquid ratio for both years [2].

**(d)** Profitability: gross profit percentage fell from 30.0% to 28.0% [1], which may mean lower selling prices or higher purchase costs [1]. Profit for the year percentage fell from 8.0% to 6.2%, and ROCE from 11.9% to 9.5% [1], even though revenue grew by 12% [1]. Use of assets: customers took 57.0 days to pay compared with 36.5, a big deterioration [1], and inventory is held for longer (52.8 days compared with 48.7) [1]. Liquidity: the current and liquid ratios are almost unchanged and appear healthy [1], but bank fell to 1,000 and a large part of current assets is slow receivables and inventory [1]. Maximum 6 marks for analysis. Judgement: performance worsened in 2026 because profits and returns fell and cash is tied up in receivables [1]. Recommendation: reduce the collection period, for example by chasing overdue accounts or offering early payment discount, and review pricing [1].

**7.** Revenue 168,000 × 1.05 = 176,400 [1]; gross profit 176,400 × 28% = **49,392** [1]; cost of sales 176,400 − 49,392 = **127,008** [1]; credit sales 176,400 × 80% = 141,120, so receivables 141,120 × 60 ÷ 365 = **23,198** [1].

**8.** Any two with a development: ratios use historical figures [1], so they do not show what will happen in future [1]; different businesses use different accounting policies [1], so comparison can be misleading [1]; ratios ignore non-financial factors such as service or staff skills [1], which affect performance [1]; year-end figures may not be typical, for example in seasonal businesses [1], so the ratios may be distorted [1].

**9.** A high current ratio may mean too much cash or inventory is idle and could be used to earn a return [1]; inventory and receivables may be slow-moving or doubtful, so they are not as liquid as the ratio suggests [1]; the liquid ratio and receivables period should be considered alongside [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong base for mark-up and margin (Questions 1 and 2).
- Using closing inventory when the question gives average inventory.
- Using total revenue in place of credit sales for the collection period (Question 6).
- Calculating ratios without units or showing no formula.
- Describing changes without giving a reason or a judgement (Question 6(d)).
- Forgetting that the liquid ratio excludes inventory.

## Approaching these questions

Write each formula, then the substitution, then the answer with units. Group your commentary under profitability, use of assets and liquidity. Use figures from both years, give a possible reason for each change, and finish with a judgement and a recommendation.
