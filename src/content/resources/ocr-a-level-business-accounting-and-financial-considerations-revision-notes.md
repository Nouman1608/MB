## Objectives and sources of finance

- Accounting and finance objectives are measurable finance targets (profit, cash-flow, ROCE, gearing). Needed for control, comparison and to support the business objective.
- Evaluate by stakeholder: owners (return), lenders (security), managers (clear targets).
- Internal sources: retained profit, sale of non-current assets, tighter working capital. External: shares, loans, debentures, overdraft, trade credit, leasing, factoring, venture capital.
- Short-term: overdraft, trade credit, factoring. Long-term: shares, loans, debentures, retained profit.

| Factor on choice of finance | Ask |
|---|---|
| Time | How long is the money needed? |
| Legal structure | Can this business issue shares? |
| Quantitative | How much, at what interest, can it repay? |
| Qualitative | Loss of control? Attitude to risk? |
| External influences | Interest rates, economy, credit availability? |

## Accounting concepts

- True and fair view: accounts free from material error and bias.
- GAAP: Generally Accepted Accounting Practice (specific rules not required).
- Conventions: consistency, going concern, accruals (matching), materiality, objectivity, prudence (conservatism), realisation.
- Apply them by naming the convention, then linking it to the case: for example prudence means providing for a likely bad debt; accruals means putting a cost in the period it helps earn revenue.
- Evaluate: they give comparable, reliable accounts, but allow judgement and can understate value (prudence).

## Costs, revenue and profit

| Term | Meaning or formula |
|---|---|
| Total cost | fixed + variable |
| Average cost | total cost / output |
| Total revenue | price x quantity |
| Average revenue | total revenue / quantity = price |
| Profit | total revenue - total cost |
| Direct cost | traced to a unit |
| Indirect cost | supports several units |
| Overheads | indirect running costs |

- Cost centre: collects costs. Profit centre: earns revenue and costs, so profit can be measured.
- Full costing: all costs charged to units. Absorption costing: overheads added by a rate (for example overheads / total hours). Marginal costing: only variable costs per unit; fixed costs are a period cost.
- Allocation of overheads changes reported product profit, and so pricing and drop decisions.

## Break-even analysis

| Item | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | selling price less variable cost per unit |
| Break-even output | fixed costs divided by contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety | expected or actual sales volume less the break-even volume |
| Target profit output | fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by contribution per unit |

Method in steps for a change question:
1. Work out the new fixed cost, variable cost or price.
2. Recalculate contribution, then break-even.
3. Compare with the old break-even and the margin of safety.
4. Judge: is the change realistic, and what happens to demand?

- Stepped fixed cost: jumps at output thresholds, such as extra premises.
- Graph: fixed cost line is flat; total cost starts at fixed cost; revenue starts at the origin; crossing is break-even.
- Lower break-even: raise price, cut variable cost, cut fixed cost; each has a trade-off.
- Special order: accept if contribution is positive, there is spare capacity and regular sales are unaffected.
- Limits: assumes constant price and costs, and that all output is sold.

## Investment appraisal

| Method | How | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Payback | time for cumulative inflows to equal cost | ignores later cash and timing |
| ARR | (total inflow - cost) / years / cost x 100 | ignores timing |
| NPV | sum of discounted inflows - cost | needs a discount rate |

- Purpose: judge whether a project is worth its cost and choose between projects.
- Positive NPV adds value; accept if the rate is realistic.
- Qualitative aspects: risk, staff, brand, strategic fit, environmental impact. Recommend by balancing both.

## Budgets and variances

- Budget: plan for a future period. Variance: actual minus budget, labelled favourable (raises profit) or adverse (lowers profit).
- Higher revenue than planned is favourable, while higher costs than planned are adverse.
- Benefits: control, coordination, motivation. Risks: unrealistic targets, rigid, imposed budgets, variances not investigated.

## Cash-flow and working capital

- Cash-flow forecast: future receipts and payments. Cash-flow statement: what happened.
- Cash-flow is money moving; profit is revenue minus costs, including credit sales and non-cash items such as depreciation.
- Layout: opening balance + receipts - payments = closing balance, which becomes next month's opening balance.
- If revenue falls, receipts fall, so closing cash falls by the same amount each month it applies.
- Fixes: overdraft, delay payments, speed up collection, cut inventory, sale and leaseback, cut costs. Evaluate cost, risk and speed.
- Working capital = current assets - current liabilities. Working capital cycle: cash out for inventory, then production, sale, credit period, cash in.

## Final accounts

Income statement layout, top to bottom: revenue; less cost of sales gives gross profit; less operating expenses gives operating profit; less interest gives profit before tax (PBT); taking off tax gives profit for the year; taking off dividends gives retained profit.

Statement of financial position layout: start with non-current assets, add current assets (cash, receivables, inventory), deduct current liabilities (payables) to show working capital, deduct non-current liabilities to reach net assets, and match this to equity (share capital plus retained earnings). Net assets always equal total equity.

- Asset valuation: non-current assets at cost less depreciation; inventory at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower; receivables less bad debts; patents and goodwill intangible and subjective; investments at cost or market value.
- Depreciation: straight line = (cost - residual value) / life. Reducing balance = fixed percentage of net book value. Provision matches cost to use and avoids overstated profit. It reduces profit and asset value, not cash.

## Ratios

| Group | Ratio | Standard form |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Current ratio | current assets over current liabilities |
| | Acid test | current assets excluding inventory, over current liabilities |
| Solvency | Gearing | non-current liabilities / capital employed x 100 |
| | Interest cover | operating profit / interest |
| Efficiency | Debtor turnover | receivables / revenue x 365 |
| | Creditor turnover | payables / cost of sales x 365 |
| | Non-current assets turnover | revenue / non-current assets |
| | Stock turnover | cost of sales / inventory |
| Profitability | Gross profit margin | gross profit over revenue, then x 100 |
| | Net profit margin | profit for the year over revenue, then x 100 |
| | ROCE | operating profit / capital employed x 100 |
| | Return on equity | profit for the year / equity x 100 |
| Shareholder | Dividend per share | dividends / number of shares |
| | Dividend yield | dividend per share / share price x 100 |
| | Earnings per share | profit for the year / number of shares |
| | Price/earnings | share price / earnings per share |

Capital employed = equity + non-current liabilities. These are standard forms; use the form your course or the question gives and state it. Evaluate the financial position by comparing with earlier years, competitors and targets, and say which stakeholder cares.

## External influences and finance strategy

- Social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological, international: for each, name the influence and its effect on finance (for example interest rates change borrowing costs; exchange rates change overseas income).
- Financial strategy: objectives, funding mix, investment, budgets and controls. Recommend with a stated risk and justify against the business's size, gearing and outlook.

## Quick self-test

1. Fixed costs £18,000, price £9, variable cost £5: find break-even output.
2. Actual output is 8,000 and break-even is 6,000: find the margin of safety as units and as a percentage of output.
3. A machine costs £30,000, residual value £3,000, life 5 years. Find the straight line charge.
4. Find the first two years' reducing balance depreciation at 30% on £20,000, and the net book value after year 2.
5. Current assets £54,000, inventory £18,000, current liabilities £36,000: find the current ratio and acid test.
6. A cost budget of £15,000 has actual cost £16,200. State the variance.
7. A £30,000 project returns £12,000 a year. Find payback.
8. A £40,000 project returns £56,000 in total over four years. Find ARR.
9. Dividend per share is 6p and the share price is £1.50. Find the dividend yield.
10. Non-current liabilities are £90,000 and equity £210,000. Find gearing.
11. Distinguish cash-flow from profit.
12. Define the working capital cycle.

### Answers

1. 18,000 / 4 = **4,500 units**.
2. 8,000 - 6,000 = **2,000 units**; 2,000 / 8,000 = **25%**.
3. 27,000 / 5 = **£5,400**.
4. Year 1: 20,000 x 0.30 = **£6,000**; year 2: 14,000 x 0.30 = **£4,200**; net book value **£9,800**.
5. 54,000 / 36,000 = **1.5**; 36,000 / 36,000 = **1.0**.
6. **£1,200 adverse**.
7. Cumulative 12,000, 24,000, 36,000, so 2 + 6,000/12,000 = **2.5 years**.
8. Profit 16,000 over four years is 4,000 a year; 4,000 / 40,000 = **10%**.
9. 6 / 150 x 100 = **4%**.
10. 90,000 / (210,000 + 90,000) x 100 = **30%**.
11. Cash-flow is money actually received and paid; profit is revenue less costs, which includes credit sales and non-cash items, so a profitable business can still run short of cash.
12. The time from paying for inventory, through production, sale and the credit period, to receiving cash from customers.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong base: gearing needs capital employed, not equity alone.
- Quoting a ratio without units (times, days, %) or without interpreting it.
- Treating depreciation as cash leaving the business.
- Labelling variances by arithmetic sign instead of effect on profit.
- Giving break-even in pounds when the question asks for units.
- Listing sources of finance without linking the choice to the business in the case.
- Reaching a recommendation without weighing both sides and the qualitative factors.
- Forgetting to carry closing cash into next month's opening balance.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does depreciation reduce a business's cash?**
No. Depreciation spreads the cost of a non-current asset over its useful life, lowering reported profit and net assets, but no cash leaves the business when it is charged.

**What units should ratio answers have?**
Give each ratio its unit, such as times, days or %, and interpret what it shows. A ratio quoted without units or interpretation earns less.

**How do you calculate break-even output for OCR A Level Business H431?**
Break-even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit, where contribution per unit = price − variable cost. Give the answer in units when the question asks for units, not in pounds.

**What should a sources of finance recommendation include?**
Link the choice to the business in the case, weigh both sides and include qualitative factors such as loss of control and attitude to risk, not only the cost.
