## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lens | Curved transparent object that forms an image by refracting light (3.3.6 a) |
| Convex (converging) lens | Thicker in the middle. Parallel rays are brought to a focus at the principal focus (b) |
| Concave (diverging) lens | Thinner in the middle. Parallel rays diverge, appearing to come from the principal focus (c) |
| Focal length | Distance from the lens to the principal focus (d) |
| Real image | Rays actually meet. Can be shown on a screen |
| Virtual image | Rays seem to diverge from it but never meet there. Cannot be shown on a screen |
| Nature of an image | Its size relative to the object, upright or inverted, real or virtual (e) |
| Accommodation | The eye changing the shape of its lens to focus on objects at different distances |
| Near point | Closest distance at which the eye focuses a sharp image, about 25 cm |
| Far point | Furthest distance at which the eye focuses a sharp image, infinity for a typical eye |
| Range of vision | The distance between near point and far point (i) |

## The equation

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Magnification | image height ÷ object height | none (a ratio) |

Rearrangements: image height = magnification × object height; object height = image height ÷ magnification. Convert both heights to the same unit first.

## Convex lens: image summary

Use f for the focal length and measure the object distance from the lens.

| Object distance | Image |
|---|---|
| More than 2f | real, inverted, smaller |
| Exactly 2f | real, inverted, same size |
| Between f and 2f | real, inverted, larger |
| Exactly f | no image (emerging rays parallel) |
| Less than f | virtual, upright, larger (magnifying glass) |

Memory hook: if the object is outside the principal focus the image is real and inverted. Inside the principal focus it is virtual and upright. A real image grows as the object moves closer to the lens, and it is larger than the object once the object is nearer than 2f.

## Concave lens: image summary

For any object distance the image is virtual, upright and smaller, on the same side of the lens as the object, between the object and the lens.

## Drawing ray diagrams: method in steps

1. Draw the axis, then the lens as a straight vertical line. Mark the principal focus on each side at the focal length.
2. Draw the object as an upright arrow on the axis.
3. From the tip, draw ray 1 parallel to the axis up to the lens.
4. Convex lens: refract ray 1 through the principal focus on the far side. Concave lens: refract it so that it spreads away from the axis, and draw a dotted line back to the principal focus on the object's side.
5. From the tip, draw ray 2 straight through the centre of the lens, undeviated.
6. Mark the tip of the image where the two rays cross. If they only appear to cross (virtual image), extend both backwards with dotted lines.
7. Draw the image arrow from the axis to that point. Read its height and distance from the grid.
8. State the nature of the image: size, upright or inverted, real or virtual.

On graph paper, use the same scale on both axes and read values from the squares rather than estimating by eye.

## Worked reminders

**Real image, convex lens.** Focal length 4 cm, object 3 cm tall at 12 cm. The rays meet 6 cm beyond the lens, 1.5 cm below the axis. Image: real, inverted, smaller. Magnification = 1.5 ÷ 3 = 0.5.

**Magnifying glass.** Focal length 5 cm, object 2 cm tall at 3 cm (inside the principal focus). The rays appear to come from 7.5 cm on the object side, 5 cm tall. Image: virtual, upright, larger. Magnification = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5.

**Concave lens.** Focal length 6 cm, object 3 cm tall at 12 cm. Image 4 cm from the lens on the object side, 1 cm tall. Image: virtual, upright, smaller. Magnification = 1 ÷ 3 = 0.33.

**Magnification.** A cell 0.040 mm across appears 6.0 mm across: 6.0 ÷ 0.040 = 150.

## The eye: parts and functions

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Transparent front of the eye. Refracts light entering the eye |
| Pupil | Opening through which light enters |
| Iris | Muscle around the pupil. Changes the pupil size to control the light entering |
| Lens | Variable focus lens. Adjusts focusing so the image is sharp on the retina |
| Ciliary muscle | Changes the shape of the lens |
| Suspensory ligaments | Connect the ciliary muscle and lens, and pull the lens thinner when the muscle relaxes |
| Retina | Contains light-sensitive cells. The image must be focused on it |

Light is refracted by the cornea as well as by the lens. The eye detects only visible light, a limited range of the electromagnetic waves.

## Accommodation at a glance

| | Near object | Distant object |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliary muscle | contracts | relaxes |
| Suspensory ligaments | slack | taut |
| Lens | thick, strongly curved | thin, weakly curved |
| Bending of light | more | less |

Say all four links in your answer: muscle, ligaments, lens shape, focal length.

## Range of vision

Typical near point is about 25 cm and the far point is infinity. The eye focuses on objects between these two points. So the typical range of vision is from about 25 cm to infinity.

## Sight defects

| | Long sight | Short sight |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | eyeball too short, or lens unable to focus a sharp image | eyeball too long, or lens unable to focus a sharp image |
| Problem | near objects blurred | distant objects blurred |
| Image without correction | behind the retina | in front of the retina |
| Near or far point | near point further than normal | far point nearer than infinity |
| Correction | convex lens | concave lens |

Method for an explanation: state where the image forms without correction, say which lens is used, and say what the lens does to the rays before they reach the eye.

## Lasers and the camera

- A laser is a concentrated source of light. Uses: cutting, cauterising and burning.
- Lasers can be used in eye surgery to correct visual defects. How lasers work is not required.

| Eye | Camera |
|---|---|
| Retina | Film or CCD sensor |
| Image focused by changing the shape of the lens | Image focused by changing the distance between lens and film or sensor |

## Must-know distinctions

- Real versus virtual: a real image can be projected, a virtual image cannot.
- Convex versus concave: convex converges the rays, concave diverges them.
- Long sight versus short sight: the lens used is opposite in each case, and the blurred distance is near for long sight and far for short sight.
- Cornea versus lens: both refract light. The lens is the one that changes shape.
- Eye versus camera: the eye changes the lens shape, the camera moves the lens.

## Quick self-test

1. What is the principal focus of a convex lens?
2. A convex lens has a focal length of 8 cm. An object is 24 cm from it. Describe the image.
3. An image is 36 mm tall and the object is 12 mm tall. Find the magnification.
4. A lens gives a magnification of 0.50 and the image is 2.5 cm tall. How tall is the object?
5. An object is placed 3 cm from a convex lens of focal length 5 cm. Describe the image and say what the arrangement is used as.
6. Describe the image formed by a concave lens.
7. Explain how the eye focuses on a book held close.
8. A person cannot see distant road signs clearly. Name the defect and the lens used to correct it.
9. State the usual range of vision.
10. Give one similarity and one difference in focusing between the eye and a camera.

### Answers

1. The point where rays parallel to the axis are brought together after passing through the lens.
2. The object is further than 2f (16 cm). The image is real, inverted and smaller (it is 12 cm from the lens, half the height).
3. 36 ÷ 12 = 3.0.
4. Object height = 2.5 ÷ 0.50 = 5.0 cm.
5. The object is inside the principal focus. The image is virtual, upright and larger (magnification 2.5). The lens is used as a magnifying glass.
6. Virtual, upright and smaller, on the same side as the object.
7. The ciliary muscle contracts, the suspensory ligaments slacken, and the lens becomes thicker and more curved. It refracts light more, so the image falls sharply on the retina.
8. Short sight. A concave lens spreads the rays so the image forms on the retina rather than in front of it.
9. From about 25 cm (near point) to infinity (far point).
10. Similarity: both have a lens that focuses an image onto a light-sensitive surface (retina, or film or CCD sensor). Difference: the eye changes the shape of its lens, while a camera changes the distance between lens and film or sensor.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an image with only one word. Give size, orientation and real or virtual.
- Writing that the image formed by a convex lens is always real. It is virtual when the object is inside the principal focus.
- Saying the eye lens moves back and forth like a camera lens. The shape of the lens changes.
- Mixing up the lenses for long sight and short sight.
- Saying the pupil focuses light. The pupil is an opening, and the iris changes its size.
- Giving a magnification with a unit, or dividing heights in different units.
- Drawing ray 1 so that it does not pass through the principal focus, or drawing ray 2 bent at the lens.
- Forgetting dotted construction lines for a virtual image.
- Forgetting that light is refracted by the cornea as well as by the lens.
- Naming laser eye surgery without saying that it is used to correct a visual defect.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the focal length of a convex lens?**
Rays parallel to the principal axis meet at the principal focus on the far side of the lens. The focal length is the distance from the middle of the lens to that point.

**What kind of image does a concave lens form?**
For any object distance, a concave lens forms an image that is virtual, upright and smaller, on the same side of the lens as the object.

**Which lens corrects short sight?**
Short sight is corrected with a concave lens. Long sight, where near objects are blurred and the image forms behind the retina, is corrected with a convex lens.

**What does the iris do?**
The iris is a ring of muscle that changes the size of the pupil, so it controls how much light enters the eye.
