These questions cover section **3.1.12.5 pH curves, titrations and indicators** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [pH curves, titrations and indicators study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-ph-curves-titrations-and-indicators/) and the [pH curves, titrations and indicators revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-ph-curves-titrations-and-indicators-revision-notes/). Indicator ranges: methyl orange 3.1 to 4.4; phenolphthalein 8.3 to 10.0. Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ and Mᵣ(CH₃COOH) = 60.0.

## Questions

**1.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Describe the shape of the pH curve, giving the starting pH, the approximate pH range of the vertical section and the pH at the equivalence point. **[3]**

**2.** Choose the more suitable indicator, methyl orange or phenolphthalein, for each titration and give a reason.

**(a)** ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide **[2]**

**(b)** hydrochloric acid with aqueous ammonia **[2]**

**3.** Explain why the pH at the equivalence point is above 7 when ethanoic acid is titrated with sodium hydroxide. Include an equation. **[2]**

**4.** 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution needs 18.20 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid for neutralisation. Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide solution. **[3]**

**5.** 25.0 cm³ of barium hydroxide solution needs 18.20 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid for neutralisation: Ba(OH)₂ + 2HCl → BaCl₂ + 2H₂O. Calculate the concentration of the barium hydroxide solution. **[3]**

**6.** A sample of vinegar is analysed. 25.0 cm³ of vinegar is diluted to 250 cm³ in a volumetric flask. A 25.0 cm³ portion of the diluted solution requires 21.60 cm³ of 0.0950 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar in g dm⁻³. **[4]**

**7.** 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide is mixed with 10.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. Calculate the pH of the mixture at 298 K. **[4]**

**8.** In a titration of a weak monoprotic acid HA with sodium hydroxide, the pH is 4.19 when exactly half of the volume needed to reach the equivalence point has been added.

**(a)** State the value of pKa. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate Ka. **[1]**

**9.** The pH curve for ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide has a region, before the equivalence point, where the pH changes only slowly. Explain why. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why methyl orange is not suitable for the titration of ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide. **[2]**

**11.** A student carries out Required practical 9, measuring the pH of 25.0 cm³ of weak acid as sodium hydroxide is added.

**(a)** Explain why the pH meter is calibrated before use. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the student adds the alkali in drops near the equivalence point. **[2]**

**12.** 0.550 g of a solid monoprotic acid is dissolved in water and made up to 100 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion needs 22.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the relative molecular mass of the acid. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why there is no suitable indicator for the titration of ethanoic acid with aqueous ammonia. **[2]**

**14.** A student sketches the pH curve for 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. The curve starts at pH 1 and has its vertical section centred on pH 7. Identify the two errors. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The pH starts at about 1 and rises slowly [1]; there is a sharp vertical section from about pH 3 to about pH 10 around 25.0 cm³ [1]; the pH at equivalence is 7 and the curve levels off at about pH 13 after excess alkali [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** Phenolphthalein [1]; the vertical section is above pH 7 (about 7 to 10), which includes the range 8.3 to 10.0 [1]

**(b)** Methyl orange [1]; the vertical section is below pH 7 (about 7 to 4), and methyl orange changes colour within it [1]. **[4]**

**3.** The ethanoate ion is the conjugate base of a weak acid and reacts with water: CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻ [1]; this produces OH⁻ ions, so the solution is alkaline at equivalence [1]. **[2]**

**4.** n(HCl) = 0.100 × 0.01820 = 1.82 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(KOH) = 1.82 × 10⁻³ mol (1:1) [1]; c = 1.82 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0728 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** n(HCl) = 1.82 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(Ba(OH)₂) = 1.82 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2 = 9.10 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; c = 9.10 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0364 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** n(NaOH) = 0.0950 × 0.02160 = 2.052 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(CH₃COOH) in 25.0 cm³ = 2.052 × 10⁻³ mol, so c(diluted) = 0.08208 mol dm⁻³ [1]; c(original) = 0.08208 × 10 = 0.8208 mol dm⁻³ [1]; mass concentration = 0.8208 × 60.0 = **49.2 g dm⁻³** [1]. **[4]**

**7.** n(NaOH) = 0.150 × 0.0200 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol and n(HCl) = 0.200 × 0.0100 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; excess OH⁻ = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol in 0.0300 dm³ gives [OH⁻] = 0.0333 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0333 = 3.00 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.52** [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** pKa = **4.19** (at half-neutralisation [HA] = [A⁻], so pH = pKa) [1]

**(b)** Ka = 10^(–4.19) = **6.5 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** In this region the flask contains a mixture of unreacted ethanoic acid and ethanoate ions, which is a buffer [1]; added OH⁻ reacts with CH₃COOH to form CH₃COO⁻ and water, so [H⁺] changes little [1]; because pH depends on the ratio [CH₃COOH] ÷ [CH₃COO⁻], which changes only slowly, the pH rises slowly [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Methyl orange changes colour between pH 3.1 and 4.4 [1]; this is in the buffer region, long before the equivalence point, so the colour change would be at the wrong volume and the end-point would not be sharp [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** To make sure the readings are accurate, using solutions of known pH [1]

**(b)** The pH changes very rapidly near the equivalence point [1]; small additions allow the volume at the middle of the vertical section to be found precisely [1]. **[3]**

**12.** n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 0.02200 = 2.20 × 10⁻³ mol, so n(acid) in 25.0 cm³ = 2.20 × 10⁻³ mol (monoprotic, 1:1) [1]; n(acid) in 100 cm³ = 4 × 2.20 × 10⁻³ = 8.80 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; Mᵣ = mass ÷ moles = 0.550 ÷ 8.80 × 10⁻³ [1]; Mᵣ = **62.5** [1]. **[4]**

**13.** With both acid and base weak, the pH changes gradually, so there is no sharp vertical section [1]; no indicator changes colour sharply at the equivalence point, so a pH meter is needed [1]. **[2]**

**14.** The starting pH should be about 2.9, not 1, because ethanoic acid is weak and only partially dissociated [1]; the vertical section should be centred above pH 7 (equivalence at about pH 8.7) because the ethanoate ion is basic [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Examiners award separate marks for the starting pH, the vertical section and the pH at the equivalence point, so each feature is worth checking.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing an indicator without quoting the vertical section range.
- Starting a weak acid curve at pH 1.
- Forgetting that the buffer region exists.
- Not dividing by two for Ba(OH)₂ with HCl.
- Forgetting to scale up for a diluted sample.
