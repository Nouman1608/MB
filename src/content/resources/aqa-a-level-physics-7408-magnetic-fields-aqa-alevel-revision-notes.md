## 3.7.5.1–3.7.5.2 Forces: recall

- **Left-hand rule**: First finger Field, seCond finger Current (conventional), thuMb Motion.
- **Tesla**: B giving 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A perpendicular to the field.
- No force if current is parallel to B.
- **Required practical 10**: magnet on top pan balance, fixed wire; F = Δm g; vary I (and l, B) and plot F against I, gradient Bl.
- Electrons: point the second finger **opposite** to their motion.
- BQv is perpendicular to v → no work → constant speed → circular path.
- Faster particle → larger radius, **same period**.
- **Cyclotron**: two hollow semicircular electrodes in a uniform B; fixed-frequency alternating p.d. accelerates particles at each gap crossing; they spiral outwards.

## 3.7.5.3 Flux and flux linkage: recall

- Φ = BA when B is normal to A; flux linkage = NΦ.
- Coil normal parallel to B (θ = 0): maximum linkage BAN.
- Coil plane parallel to B (θ = 90°): zero linkage.
- **Required practical 11**: search coil + oscilloscope in an alternating field; induced peak emf ∝ flux linkage ∝ cos θ.

## 3.7.5.4 Induction: recall

- An emf is induced whenever flux linkage **changes** (moving magnet, moving coil, changing current nearby).
- **Faraday**: emf = rate of change of flux linkage.
- **Lenz**: induced current opposes the change causing it (conservation of energy).
- Rotating coil: emf maximum when the coil plane is **parallel** to B (linkage zero but changing fastest).
- Faster rotation → larger peak emf and higher frequency.
- emf is the gradient of the NΦ–t graph (with a minus sign for direction).

## 3.7.5.5 Alternating current: recall

| Quantity | Relation to peak V₀ |
|---|---|
| rms | V₀/√2 |
| peak-to-peak | 2V₀ |
| UK mains | 230 V rms, 325 V peak, 651 V peak-to-peak |

- rms = steady d.c. value giving the same mean power in a resistor.
- **Oscilloscope**: voltage = divisions × Y-gain (V div⁻¹); time = divisions × time base (s div⁻¹); f = 1/T.
- d.c. input shifts the trace; ac with time base off gives a vertical line of length V_pp.

## 3.7.5.6 Transformers: recall

- Alternating primary current → changing flux in core → emf induced in secondary.
- Step-up: Nₛ > Nₚ; step-down: Nₛ < Nₚ.
- Works only with **changing** current (ac), not steady d.c.

| Loss | Reduction |
|---|---|
| Eddy currents heating the core | laminated core with insulated layers |
| Resistance of windings | thick, low-resistance wire |
| Magnetising and demagnetising core | soft iron core |
| Flux leakage | coils wound together on one core |

- **Transmission**: for fixed P, higher V → lower I → much lower I²R loss (loss ∝ 1/V²).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetic flux density | Force per unit current per unit length on a perpendicular wire |
| Magnetic flux | Φ = BA, with B normal to A |
| Flux linkage | NΦ for a coil of N turns |
| Weber | Unit of flux, 1 T m² |
| Faraday's law | Induced emf equals rate of change of flux linkage |
| Lenz's law | Induced current opposes the change producing it |
| rms value | Equivalent steady value delivering the same power |
| Eddy currents | Induced currents circulating in a conductor such as a core |

## Exam traps

- Measure θ from the **normal**, not from the plane of the coil.
- Peak emf when flux linkage is **zero**, not maximum.
- Convert rpm to rad s⁻¹ for ω.
- Use the cable resistance and current for the loss, not the transmission voltage.
- An "average emf" uses the total change in flux linkage over the total time.
- Electrons deflect the opposite way to protons in the same field.

## Self-test

**1.** Define the tesla.
**2.** A 0.20 m wire carries 4.0 A at right angles to a 0.15 T field. Find F.
**3.** Why does a magnetic field not change a charged particle's speed?
**4.** How does the radius change if a particle's speed doubles?
**5.** State Faraday's law.
**6.** State Lenz's law.
**7.** A 100-turn coil of area 0.010 m² is in 0.50 T, normal along B. Find the flux linkage.
**8.** That flux linkage falls to zero in 0.25 s. Find the average emf.
**9.** Mains peak voltage for 230 V rms?
**10.** Why are transformer cores laminated?
**11.** A transformer steps 230 V down to 11.5 V with 1000 primary turns. Find Nₛ.
**12.** By what factor does transmission loss fall if the voltage is increased tenfold at the same power?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The flux density producing a force of 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field.
**2.** F = 0.15 × 4.0 × 0.20 = 0.12 N.
**3.** The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so no work is done on the particle.
**4.** It doubles (r = mv/BQ).
**5.** Induced emf equals the rate of change of flux linkage.
**6.** The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change producing it.
**7.** NΦ = 0.50 × 0.010 × 100 = 0.50 Wb turns.
**8.** 0.50/0.25 = 2.0 V.
**9.** 230√2 = 325 V.
**10.** To reduce eddy currents and the heating they cause.
**11.** Nₛ = 1000 × 11.5/230 = 50.
**12.** 100.
