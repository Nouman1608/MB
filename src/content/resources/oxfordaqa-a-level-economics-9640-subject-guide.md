OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) is a modular
course covering how markets allocate resources, how national economies
perform, how firms and labour markets behave, and how countries develop
within a global economy. It suits students who like arguments backed by
evidence and who are comfortable working with data. The specification
was first taught in September 2020, with first AS exams in May/June 2021
and first A-level exams in May/June 2022. Assessment is by four written
papers: two AS papers of 105 minutes and 80 marks, each 20% of the
A-level, and two A-level papers of 120 minutes and 90 marks, each 30%.

## How the course is organised

The content falls into four areas, plus a set of quantitative skills that
apply throughout.

**AS: microeconomics.** The operation of markets, market failure and the
role of government begins with the economic problem and methodology, then
covers how markets work, an introduction to production, costs, revenue and
profit, competitive and concentrated markets, and market failure and
government intervention.

**AS: macroeconomics.** The national economy in a global environment
covers the measurement of macroeconomic performance, how the macroeconomy
works, economic performance, and macroeconomic policy.

**A-level: microeconomics in depth.** The economics of business behaviour
and the distribution of income returns to firms with more rigour: the
objectives of individuals and firms; costs, revenue and profits; perfect
competition, imperfectly competitive markets and monopoly; the labour
market; and poverty and inequality.

**A-level: the global economy.** Economic development and the global
economy covers globalisation and trade; the balance of payments, exchange
rates and financial markets; and economic growth and development.

**Quantitative skills.** Throughout, students must be able to work with
ratios, fractions, percentages and percentage changes, mean and median,
index numbers, graphs, cost, revenue and profit calculations, conversions
from money to real terms and elasticity, and must interpret information
presented in written, graphical and numerical forms. At A-level the
requirements extend to relevant quantiles and marginal values.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | The Operation of Markets, Market Failure and the Role of Government | 105 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | The National Economy in a Global Environment | 105 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | The Economics of Business Behaviour and the Distribution of Income | 120 minutes | 90 | 30% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Economic Development and the Global Economy | 120 minutes | 90 | 30% |

All four are externally assessed written exams with no tiers. Each AS
paper is worth 50% of the International AS, so a student can stop after
the first year with an AS qualification. Across the full A-level, the AS
content contributes 40% of the final marks and the A-level papers the
remaining 60%.

## How the parts fit together

The structure is a clear spiral. Each A-level paper revisits an AS area
at greater depth.

AS Paper 1 and A-level Paper 1 are the microeconomic pair. The
introduction to costs, revenue and profit and to competitive and
concentrated markets in the first year becomes, in the second, a full
treatment of market structures from perfect competition to monopoly, with
labour markets and inequality added. Market failure and government
intervention at AS prepares the ground for the policy questions that
arise around business behaviour and income distribution.

AS Paper 2 and A-level Paper 2 are the macroeconomic pair. The
measurement of performance and macroeconomic policy at AS widens into
trade, exchange rates, the balance of payments and development at
A-level.

The quantitative skills tie all four papers together. Elasticity, index
numbers and real-terms conversions appear in micro and macro questions
alike, so practising them only in one context leaves gaps. Equally, the
habit of explaining a chain of cause and effect, then weighing it against
other factors, is needed on every paper.

## Planning your study

In the first year, many schools teach the microeconomic area before the
macroeconomic one, because supply and demand analysis gives you the tools
for aggregate demand and supply later. Build the quantitative skills as
you go rather than separately: each time a new diagram or calculation
appears, practise it with real figures.

In the second year, revisit your AS notes on costs and market structures
before starting business behaviour, and your AS macroeconomics before
starting the global economy. Keep a running file of current economic
examples from different countries, since applied examples strengthen
written answers across all four papers.

Start answering past-paper questions on each area as soon as it has been
taught, and sit full timed papers in the months before each exam series.
The course's exam-preparation page explains why the A-level papers carry
more weight than the AS papers and how to plan revision around that, so
use it for paper-level strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does OxfordAQA A-level Economics 9640 have?**
Four: AS Paper 1 and AS Paper 2, each 105 minutes, 80 marks and 20% of the A-level, and A-level Paper 1 and A-level Paper 2, each 120 minutes, 90 marks and 30%.

**Is 9640 modular or linear?**
It is modular. The AS papers can be taken at the end of the first year, where each is worth 50% of the International AS, and the A-level papers follow in the second year.

**How much of the final A-level grade comes from AS content?**
The AS content contributes 40% of the final marks and the A-level content the remaining 60%.

**What maths do I need for 9640?**
The quantitative skills include ratios, fractions, percentages, mean and median, index numbers, graphs, cost, revenue and profit calculations, real-terms conversions and elasticity. At A-level you also need relevant quantiles and marginal values.

**Is there coursework in 9640?**
No. All four components are externally assessed written exams.
