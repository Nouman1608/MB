The Spoken Language Endorsement is an optional, separately reported part of Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE English Language A (4EA1). You give a **prepared presentation of no more than 10 minutes to an
audience that always includes your teacher**, then listen and respond to questions and feedback. There
are no marks: you are graded Not Classified, Pass, Merit or Distinction against AO6, and the grade
appears on your certificate alongside, not inside, your English Language grade. For the full skills
guide, use the
[Spoken Language Endorsement study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-english-language-spoken-language-endorsement-study-guide/).

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Specification requirement (Issue 7) |
|---|---|
| Status | Optional; paper code 4EA1/E |
| Task | A prepared presentation, followed by questions and feedback |
| Forms | (a) a speech or talk followed by audience questions; (b) a formal debate or dialogue, such as an interview with prepared extended responses to prompts shared in advance, followed by audience questions |
| Length | Prepared, and no longer than 10 minutes |
| Audience | Must always include the teacher; size and make-up agreed between teacher and student |
| Teacher-only audience | Allowed, but the presentation should be designed for a potentially wider audience (for example, replicating a television interview) |
| Assessment | Live or from recordings |
| Outcome | Not Classified, Pass, Merit or Distinction (no marks) |
| Effect on grade | None; reported separately on the certificate |

## AO6: what is assessed

AO6, Speaking and listening skills, has three strands:

1. Demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting.
2. Listen and respond appropriately to spoken language, including to questions and feedback to
   presentations.
3. Use spoken Standard English effectively in speeches and presentations.

## The general criteria (needed for every grade)

To be awarded Pass, Merit or Distinction you must:

- be **audible**, and
- use **spoken Standard English**, which for this assessment means being intelligible and generally using
  language appropriate to the formal setting.

## Pass, Merit and Distinction compared

You must meet **all** the criteria for a grade to be awarded it.

| Strand | Pass | Merit | Distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideas and vocabulary | Expresses straightforward ideas/information/feelings | Expresses challenging ideas/information/feelings using a range of vocabulary | Expresses sophisticated ideas/information/feelings using a sophisticated repertoire of vocabulary |
| Organisation and audience | Makes an attempt to organise and structure the presentation; makes an attempt to meet the needs of the audience | Organises and structures clearly and appropriately to meet the needs of the audience | Organises and structures using an effective range of strategies to engage the audience |
| Purpose | — | Achieves the purpose of the presentation | Achieves the purpose of the presentation |
| Listening and responding | Responds to questions/feedback appropriately in a straightforward manner | Responds formally and in some detail | Listens and responds perceptively and, if appropriate, elaborates with further ideas and information |

**Key progression words:** straightforward → challenging → sophisticated; attempt → clearly → effective
range of strategies; straightforward response → in some detail → perceptively, with elaboration.

## How moderation works

- Centres record a **sample** of presentations, not every student, for each June and/or November series.
- Centres with 30 or fewer students record all students; over 30, a sample of 30, including at least 10 at
  each grade (Distinction, Merit, Pass) where available. Students assessed as Not Classified are not
  included in the sample.
- A record sheet (Appendix 3 of the specification) is completed for every student.
- All work is submitted digitally.

## Planning a presentation that can reach Distinction

| Step | What to do | Criterion it serves |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Topic | Choose a subject with an argument or a problem, not only facts | "Sophisticated ideas" |
| 2. Purpose | Write one sentence: "By the end, my audience will…" | "Achieves the purpose" |
| 3. Structure | Hook, three main points, a clear ending; signpost transitions | "Organises and structures" |
| 4. Engagement | Questions to the audience, anecdote, a visual, varied pace | "Effective range of strategies" |
| 5. Questions | Predict five likely questions and prepare evidence | "Responds perceptively… elaborates" |
| 6. Timing | Rehearse to finish within 10 minutes, leaving time for questions | Format requirement |

## Worked example: answering a question at three grades

Question from the audience: "Isn't banning phones in schools unfair to students who need them for
emergencies?"

- **Pass:** "Yes, maybe, but I still think they should be banned."
- **Merit:** "That's a fair point. Schools could keep phones in a locker so students can reach them in an
  emergency, while still removing the distraction in lessons."
- **Distinction:** "That's the strongest objection, and it depends on what we mean by 'ban'. If the ban
  means no phones on site, I agree it's unfair. But the evidence I quoted was about phones in lessons,
  so a locker system answers your concern and keeps the benefit. It would also mean parents could call the
  office, which is how emergencies were handled before phones."

The Distinction answer acknowledges, distinguishes, refers back to the presentation and elaborates.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Reading a script word for word.** It weakens audience engagement and can make the language less
  natural; use cue cards.
- **Going over time.** The presentation must last no longer than 10 minutes.
- **Informal language.** Slang or fillers ("like", "basically") undermine "spoken Standard English".
- **Preparing the talk but not the questions.** Listening and responding is part of every grade.
- **A topic with no purpose.** Merit and Distinction both require the presentation to achieve its purpose.
- **Forgetting the teacher-only rule.** If the teacher is the only audience, design the talk as if for a
  wider audience.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the Spoken Language Endorsement change my English Language grade?**
No. It is reported as a separate grade on the certificate, and there are no marks for it.

**How long should my presentation be?**
The specification says it should be prepared and last no longer than 10 minutes. Leave time for the
questions that follow.

**Can I do an interview instead of a speech?**
Yes. A formal debate or dialogue, such as an interview with prompts shared in advance, is allowed,
followed by audience questions.

**Do I have to meet every criterion for a grade?**
Yes. To achieve a grade you must meet all of the criteria for that grade, plus the general criteria.

## Self-test

1. What is the maximum length of the presentation? (10 minutes.)
2. Who must always be in the audience? (The teacher.)
3. Name the four possible outcomes. (Not Classified, Pass, Merit, Distinction.)
4. What two general criteria apply to all grades? (Audible; spoken Standard English.)
5. What extra listening skill is required for Distinction? (Responding perceptively and elaborating where appropriate.)
