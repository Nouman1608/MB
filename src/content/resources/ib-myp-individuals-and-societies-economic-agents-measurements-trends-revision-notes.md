## Economic agents in one table

| Agent | Does | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| Households / consumers | Buy goods and services; supply labour; save | Most satisfaction from limited income |
| Firms / producers | Use land, labour, capital and enterprise to produce; employ; invest | Profit, growth, survival |
| Government | Taxes, spends on public services, pays benefits, regulates | Citizens' well-being, stable prices, jobs, growth (and re-election) |
| Banks | Take deposits, lend, handle payments | Profit from the gap between interest charged and interest paid |
| Central bank | Bank of the government and of banks; usually sets the main interest rate | Stable prices and a stable financial system |

**Factor incomes:** land → rent; labour → wages; capital → interest; enterprise → profit.

**Circular flow:** households → factors → firms; firms → incomes → households; households → spending → firms.

| Leakages (money out) | Injections (money in) |
|---|---|
| Saving | Investment |
| Taxes | Government spending |
| Imports | Exports |

Memory aid: each leakage pairs with the injection on its row (saving–investment through banks; taxes–government spending; imports–exports).

## Formulas

| Measure | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| GDP (expenditure) | C + I + G + (X − M) | currency |
| GDP per capita | GDP ÷ population | currency per person |
| Real GDP | nominal GDP ÷ price index × 100 | base-year prices |
| Growth rate | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | % |
| Weighted price change | Σ(weight × % change) ÷ Σ weights | % |
| Inflation rate | (CPI now − CPI before) ÷ CPI before × 100 | % |
| Labour force | employed + unemployed | people |
| Unemployment rate | unemployed ÷ labour force × 100 | % |
| HDI | cube root of (health index × education index × income index) | 0 to 1 |
| Dimension index | (actual − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum) | 0 to 1 |

**HDI goalposts (UNDP):** life expectancy 20–85 years; expected schooling 0–18 years; mean schooling 0–15 years; GNI per capita (PPP) 100–75,000, using natural logarithms. Education index = average of the two schooling indices. Groups: very high 0.800+, high 0.700–0.799, medium 0.550–0.699, low below 0.550.

## Definitions to learn word for word

- **GDP** -- the total value of final goods and services produced within a country in a year.
- **Economic growth** -- an increase in real GDP.
- **Inflation** -- a sustained rise in the general price level.
- **Deflation** -- a sustained fall in the general price level.
- **Disinflation** -- a fall in the rate of inflation; prices still rise, more slowly.
- **Unemployed** -- of working age, without a job, available for work and actively seeking it.
- **Economically inactive** -- neither employed nor unemployed (students, retired people, full-time carers).
- **HDI** -- a UNDP index, first published in 1990, combining health, education and income.
- **PPP (purchasing power parity)** -- an adjustment so that money is compared by what it buys in each country.

## Method in steps

**Calculating GDP and GDP per capita**

1. Write the formula: C + I + G + (X − M).
2. Put brackets round (X − M) and keep its sign.
3. Check units (billions? millions?) before dividing by population.
4. Give the answer with units: "30,000 crowns per person".

**Nominal to real**

1. Divide nominal GDP by the price index and multiply by 100.
2. Work out the growth rate from the real figures, not the nominal ones.

**Weighted inflation**

1. Multiply each weight by its price change (keep negative signs).
2. Add the products; divide by the sum of the weights.
3. New index = old index × (1 + rate ÷ 100).

**Describing a trend (TRAL)**

1. **T**rend -- overall direction, with first and last figures and years.
2. **R**ate -- when the change was fastest or slowest.
3. **A**nomalies -- peaks, dips, reversals, with years.
4. **L**inks -- between two variables, worded "is consistent with", never "proves".

Small reminder: CPI 140 → 147 is inflation of 7 ÷ 140 × 100 = 5%.

## Types of unemployment

| Type | Cause | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Frictional | Between jobs | A nurse moving to a new hospital |
| Seasonal | Seasonal demand | A ski instructor in summer |
| Structural | Decline of an industry; skills mismatch | Shipyard workers after a yard closes |
| Cyclical | Low spending in a recession | Car-factory workers laid off as sales fall |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Nominal vs real**: nominal uses current prices; real removes inflation.
- **GDP vs GDP per capita**: total output vs output per person. A large country can have high GDP and low GDP per capita.
- **GDP vs GNI**: GDP is produced *within* the country; GNI adds income residents earn from abroad and subtracts income sent abroad. The HDI uses GNI per capita.
- **Inflation falling vs prices falling**: 6% → 3% inflation is disinflation; prices are still rising.
- **Percent vs percentage points**: a rate going from 4% to 5% rises by 1 percentage point, which is a 25% rise.
- **Unemployed vs inactive**: a full-time student is not unemployed.
- **Arithmetic vs geometric mean**: the HDI multiplies the three indices and takes the cube root, so one very low index pulls the HDI down strongly.
- **Correlation vs causation**: two series moving together do not prove one causes the other.

## Evaluating the measures

| Measure | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| GDP per capita | Widely collected; easy to compare over time | Hides inequality, unpaid work, informal economy, environmental damage |
| CPI inflation | Based on a real household basket | Basket and weights may not match your household; quality changes are hard to measure |
| Unemployment rate | Clear international definition | Misses people who stop looking and underemployment |
| HDI | Adds health and education to income | Ignores inequality, freedom and the environment; averages hide differences within a country |

## Quick self-test

1. C = 300, I = 80, G = 120, X = 90, M = 110 (billions). Calculate GDP.
2. GDP is 480 billion and the population is 12 million. Calculate GDP per capita.
3. Nominal GDP is 550 billion and the price index is 110. Calculate real GDP.
4. The CPI rises from 140 to 147. Calculate the inflation rate.
5. 11.4 million people are employed and 0.6 million unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate.
6. The unemployment rate rises from 4% to 5%. State the change in percentage points and in percent.
7. Dimension indices are health 0.9, education 0.6, income 0.8. Calculate the HDI to 3 d.p.
8. Inflation falls from 5% to 2%. Is this deflation or disinflation?
9. Money wages rise 4% and inflation is 6%. What happens to real wages, roughly?
10. Classify: (a) a ski instructor out of work in summer; (b) coal miners after a pit closes.
11. Which agent usually sets a country's main interest rate?
12. Is each an injection or a leakage: (a) exports; (b) saving?

### Answers

1. 300 + 80 + 120 + (90 − 110) = **480 billion**.
2. 480,000,000,000 ÷ 12,000,000 = **40,000 per person**.
3. 550 ÷ 110 × 100 = **500 billion** (base-year prices).
4. 7 ÷ 140 × 100 = **5%**.
5. Labour force 12.0 million; 0.6 ÷ 12.0 × 100 = **5.0%**.
6. **+1 percentage point**; (5 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 = **25% rise**.
7. (0.9 × 0.6 × 0.8)^(1/3) = 0.432^(1/3) = **0.756** (high).
8. **Disinflation** -- prices still rise, more slowly.
9. They **fall by about 2%** (exactly 1.04 ÷ 1.06 − 1 ≈ −1.9%).
10. (a) **Seasonal**; (b) **structural**.
11. The **central bank**.
12. (a) **Injection**; (b) **leakage**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding imports in the GDP formula instead of subtracting them.
- Losing the minus sign on net exports or on a falling price.
- Calculating growth from nominal figures when the question asks for real growth.
- Using a simple average of price changes when weights are given.
- Dividing by the working-age population, not the labour force, for the unemployment rate.
- Writing "1%" when the answer is "1 percentage point".
- Saying prices fell when only the inflation rate fell.
- Using the arithmetic mean for the HDI, or forgetting the education index averages two indicators.
- Describing a trend with no years, no figures or no units.
- Claiming one variable caused another because they move together.
