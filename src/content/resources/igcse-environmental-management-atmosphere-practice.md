of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029 syllabus). They include recall items, a

Environmental Management and Paper 2 Environmental Management in Context.

## Section A

**1.** Name the four layers of the atmosphere in order, starting at the Earth's surface. **[2]**

**2.** State the percentage of nitrogen and of oxygen in clean air. **[2]**

**3.** Define *climate*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the layer of the atmosphere that contains the ozone layer. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** Describe the natural greenhouse effect. **[4]**

**6.** (a) Define a *greenhouse gas*. **[1]**
(b) Name **two** agricultural sources of methane. **[2]**
(c) Explain how cement manufacture adds carbon dioxide to the atmosphere. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows mean annual atmospheric carbon dioxide concentration at one monitoring station.

| Year | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO2 (ppm) | 370 | 390 | 414 |

(a) Calculate the percentage increase in carbon dioxide between 2000 and 2020. Show your working. **[2]**
(b) Describe the trend shown. **[2]**
(c) Explain how this trend could lead to climate change. **[3]**

**8.** Describe **four** impacts of climate change on coastal communities. **[4]**

**9.** A low-lying coastal city is planning for climate change.
(a) Define *climate change adaptation*. **[1]**
(b) Describe **two** adaptation strategies the city could use. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how acid rain forms from a coal-fired power station. **[3]**

**11.** Describe the impacts of acid rain on a lake ecosystem. **[3]**

**12.** State the cause of ozone depletion and describe **two** of its impacts on people. **[3]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of using taxation and international agreements to reduce
carbon footprints. **[6]**

**14.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of flue-gas desulfurisation and catalytic converters as
strategies to manage acid rain. **[6]**

**15.** "The strategies used to manage ozone depletion have been more successful than those used to
manage climate change." Discuss this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere. **[2]** (1 mark for all four named, 1 for the
correct order.)

**2.** Nitrogen 78%; oxygen 21%. **[2]**

**3.** The weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years. **[1]**

**4.** The stratosphere (lower stratosphere). **[1]**

**5.** Any four: solar radiation passes through the atmosphere **[1]**; some is absorbed by land and
oceans, heating the Earth **[1]**; some is reflected back into space **[1]**; some is absorbed and
re-emitted back to the surface by greenhouse gases and clouds **[1]**; this keeps the Earth warm enough to
support life **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**6.** (a) A gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy. **[1]**
(b) Any two: digestion by cattle; decomposition of manure; rice fields. **[2]**
(c) Limestone (calcium carbonate) is heated **[1]** to make lime (calcium oxide), releasing carbon
dioxide **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Increase = 414 - 370 = 44 ppm; 44 / 370 x 100 = **11.9%**. **[2]** (1 for working, 1 for answer.)
(b) CO2 increased throughout **[1]**; the rate increased - 20 ppm from 2000-2010, 24 ppm from 2010-2020
**[1]**.
(c) More carbon dioxide absorbs more radiation **[1]**, increasing the greenhouse effect (enhanced
greenhouse effect) **[1]**, causing global warming, which changes long-term weather patterns **[1]**.

**8.** Any four, each described: sea-level rise from melting ice sheets and glaciers causing loss of land
**[1]**; more frequent and severe storms causing flooding **[1]**; warmer, more acidic oceans disrupting
fish food chains and catches **[1]**; loss of habitats such as wetlands **[1]**; forced migration of people
**[1]**; financial losses. **[max 4]**

**9.** (a) Altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the impacts of climate change.
**[1]**
(b) Any two: improve flood defences such as sea walls **[1]**; legislation limiting building near the
coast or on floodplains **[1]**; building design and materials, such as raised floors **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**10.** Coal contains sulfur compounds; burning it releases sulfur dioxide **[1]**; the high temperature also
makes oxides of nitrogen **[1]**; these gases react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere to form
acid rain **[1]**. (Accept nitrogen oxides from high-temperature combustion; the syllabus links them to
vehicle engines.)

**11.** The water becomes acidified **[1]**; fish populations fall **[1]**; aquatic food webs are disrupted as
species that feed on fish or invertebrates decline **[1]**.

**12.** Cause: CFCs from aerosols and refrigerants **[1]**. Impacts, any two: more solar radiation reaching
the surface **[1]**; increased rates of skin cancer **[1]**; increased cataracts **[1]**; lower crop yields
affecting food supply **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**13.** Indicative content, levels-marked **[6]**:
- Taxation benefit: makes fossil fuels more expensive, so people and firms use less and switch to
  cleaner options; revenue can fund renewable energy.
- Taxation limitation: hits poorer households; can be unpopular, so governments may cut it.
- Agreement benefit: shared targets, such as the Paris Agreement of 2015, involve almost all countries
  and encourage action on a global problem.
- Agreement limitation: targets are hard to enforce; countries may miss them or withdraw; emissions are
  still rising.
- For full marks, a balanced conclusion, such as that the two work best together.

**14.** **[6]**
- Flue-gas desulfurisation: benefit - removes sulfur dioxide from power-station chimneys by reacting it
  with calcium carbonate or calcium oxide; limitation - expensive to install and run, needs quarried
  limestone, and does not reduce carbon dioxide.
- Catalytic converters: benefit - remove oxides of nitrogen from vehicle exhaust; limitation - only work
  on vehicles fitted with them, need replacing, and do not reduce the number of vehicles.
- Conclusion: each targets only one of the acid gases, so they work best together with transport
  policies.

**15.** **[6]**
- Ozone: one main cause (CFCs), a clear alternative, and a single agreement (Montreal Protocol, 1987)
  followed by a ban; CFC use fell sharply.
- Limitation for ozone: CFCs persist, so recovery takes decades; old appliances need safe disposal.
- Climate: many causes (fossil fuels, farming, deforestation, cement, population growth) linked to
  energy and food, so strategies are costlier and harder to agree; emissions continue to rise.
- Conclusion: the statement is largely true because the ozone problem had fewer causes and cheaper
  alternatives.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

"Describe" needs features; "explain" needs a reason linked by "because" or "so"; "discuss" needs both a
benefit and a limitation, and a short conclusion. In data questions, quote figures with units and always
show your working for percentage change.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are "discuss" questions marked?** They are usually marked in levels. A list of strategies stays in the
lowest level; balanced benefits and limitations with a conclusion reach the top level.

**Do I need to learn specific international agreements?** The syllabus says "international agreements and
policies". Naming the Montreal Protocol and the Paris Agreement adds precision, but the marks come from
explaining how the agreement helps.

**What units should I use for gas concentrations?** Parts per million (ppm) is the usual unit for carbon
dioxide in source material.
