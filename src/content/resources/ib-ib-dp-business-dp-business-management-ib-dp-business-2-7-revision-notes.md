## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Industrial (employee) relations | The relationship between employers and employees, often through representatives |
| Trade union | An organisation that represents employees in dealings with employers |
| Collective bargaining | Negotiation between employers and employee representatives on behalf of a group |
| Work-to-rule | Staff do exactly what contracts and rules require and no more |
| Strike action | Staff stop work completely to put pressure on the employer |
| Threat of redundancies | Employer warns that jobs will be cut if terms are not accepted |
| Change of contract | Employer alters terms such as hours, duties or pay |
| Lockout | Employer prevents staff entering the workplace until they accept terms |
| Closure | Employer shuts a site or the business |
| Conciliation | A neutral third party helps both sides reach their own agreement |
| Arbitration | A neutral third party hears both sides and gives a ruling |
| Industrial democracy | Employees share in decision-making, for example through works councils or board representation |
| No-strike agreement | A union agrees not to strike, usually in return for another way of settling disputes |
| Single-union agreement | The employer recognises one union to represent all its employees |

## Sources of conflict (AO2)

* Pay and benefits, especially when living costs rise.
* Working conditions: hours, shifts, safety, workload.
* Job security: redundancies, closures, outsourcing.
* Change: new technology, restructuring, new methods.
* Management style and poor communication.

Root cause: employers seek lower costs, flexibility and speed; employees seek higher pay, security and a voice.

## Bargaining power checklist

Employees stronger: high union membership, scarce skills, product cannot be stored, strong demand, public support.

Employers stronger: high unemployment, large stocks, work can move or be automated, losses make closure believable.

## Approaches: quick evaluation

| Approach | By | Pressure | Cost to the side using it | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collective bargaining | Both | Low | Low | Slow; may not settle |
| Work-to-rule | Employees | Medium | Low (pay continues) | Goodwill lost for longer |
| Strike action | Employees | High | Lost pay | Damages the business staff rely on |
| Changes of contract | Employer | Medium | Low at first | May provoke strikes |
| Threats of redundancies | Employer | Medium to high | Low at first | Trust and skilled staff lost |
| Lockout | Employer | High | Lost output | Both sides harmed |
| Closure | Employer | Final | Very high | Capacity and reputation lost |

## Resolution methods

* **Conciliation**: helps the sides agree; nothing imposed. Best when both still want a deal.
* **Arbitration**: third party decides. Best when a decision is needed; one side may resent it.
* **Employee participation and industrial democracy**: preventive; gives staff a voice; slows decisions.
* **No-strike agreement**: protects continuity; staff need a fair alternative route.
* **Single-union agreement**: simpler bargaining; some groups may feel unrepresented.

Settling a current dispute: conciliation, arbitration. Preventing future disputes: participation, no-strike, single-union.

## Worked mini-example

Halverton Port (invented) employs 150 crane operators on 40,000 a year. The union claims 5%; the port offers 3%.

* Payroll: 150 × 40,000 = 6,000,000.
* Claim: 300,000 a year. Offer: 180,000 a year. Gap: 120,000, or 800 per operator.
* Ships diverted during a strike may move to rival ports permanently, so the port's real loss can exceed the gap. Conciliation is the logical first step for both sides.

## Exam traps

* Calling a lockout or closure an employee action.
* Calling work-to-rule a strike.
* Mixing up conciliation and arbitration.
* Evaluating an approach with no reference to bargaining power or case facts.
* Recommending a preventive method to settle a dispute that is already under way, without saying so.
* Forgetting that rules on strikes differ by country; keep to the case.

6. Give two factors that strengthen employees' bargaining power.
7. When is a threat of closure most believable?

11. Which resolution methods are mainly preventive?

### Answers

1. HL only.
2. Collective bargaining, work-to-rule, strike action.
3. Collective bargaining, threats of redundancies, changes of contract, closure, lockouts.
4. Conciliation helps the two sides reach their own agreement; arbitration gives a ruling from a neutral third party.
5. Staff keep being paid while still putting pressure on the employer by withdrawing goodwill.
6. Any two: high union membership, scarce skills, product cannot be stored, strong demand, public support.
7. When the business is making losses or can move work elsewhere.
8. An agreed alternative way of settling disputes, such as arbitration, and often recognition or improved terms.
9. Advantage: simpler, faster bargaining. Disadvantage: one union may not represent all groups fairly.
10. Payroll 2,400,000; claim 96,000; offer 36,000; gap 60,000.
11. Employee participation and industrial democracy, no-strike agreements and single-union agreements.
12. Staff lose pay, and lost customers may weaken the business and threaten jobs later.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between conciliation and arbitration?**
In conciliation a neutral third party helps both sides talk and reach their own agreement. In arbitration a neutral third party hears both sides and gives a ruling.

**Is a lockout an employer or employee action?**
A lockout is an employer action: the employer stops staff from entering the workplace until they accept the terms offered.
