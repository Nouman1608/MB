These questions cover section 10.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: understanding of seasonal variation, including calculation of mean seasonal variation and use of a trend line and seasonal component in prediction. Recall the methods from the [seasonal variation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-understanding-of-seasonal-variation/) or the [seasonal variation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-understanding-of-seasonal-variation-revision-notes/). All data are invented. Give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** In one quarter a shop's sales were 132 and the centred moving average for that quarter was 120.5.

**(a)** Find the individual seasonal variation. **[1]**

**(b)** Interpret your answer. **[1]**

**2.** The individual seasonal variations for Q2 in three years are −4.25, −5.75 and −5.0. Find the mean seasonal variation for Q2. **[2]**

**3.** The mean seasonal variations for Q1, Q2 and Q3 of a quarterly series are 18.5, −6.0 and −14.0. Estimate the mean seasonal variation for Q4, giving a reason. **[2]**

**4.** The estimated trend value for a future quarter is 410, and the mean seasonal variation for that quarter is −35.

**(a)** Predict the value for that quarter. **[1]**

**(b)** Interpret the seasonal component −35. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the individual seasonal variations for the same season are usually not equal from one year to the next. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The number of guests (hundreds) at a hotel each quarter for three years is shown.

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 30 | 46 | 58 | 34 |
| Year 2 | 33 | 50 | 63 | 37 |
| Year 3 | 36 | 54 | 66 | 41 |

**(a)** Calculate the centred 4-point moving averages. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the individual seasonal variations. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean seasonal variation for each quarter. **[3]**

**(d)** Check your answers to (c) and comment. **[1]**

**7.** A trend line passes through 200 at Year 1 Q3 and 228 at Year 3 Q3. The mean seasonal variations are Q1 +12, Q2 −20, Q3 −6 and Q4 +14.

**(a)** Find the increase in the trend per quarter. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict the values for Year 4 Q1 and Year 4 Q4. **[4]**

**(c)** State which of your two predictions is less reliable, giving a reason. **[1]**

**8.** A theatre runs three seasons a year. Ticket sales (thousands) are shown.

| | Season 1 | Season 2 | Season 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 45 | 62 | 28 |
| Year 2 | 48 | 66 | 33 |
| Year 3 | 54 | 69 | 36 |

**(a)** Calculate the 3-point moving averages. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean seasonal variation for each season. **[3]**

**(c)** Using the first and last moving averages to estimate the trend, predict the ticket sales for Year 4 Season 1. **[3]**

**9.** A café's individual seasonal variations for Mondays, from three weeks of data, are −22, −18 and −20 customers.

**(a)** Find the mean seasonal variation for Mondays and interpret it. **[2]**

**(b)** The estimated trend value for next Monday is 310. Predict the number of customers. **[1]**

**10.** A student's working for a prediction is shown.

"Q2 seasonal variation: moving average − actual = 64.5 − 58 = 6.5, and 66.0 − 59 = 7.0, so mean = 6.75. The last centred moving average is 70.0, so next year's Q2 = 70.0 + 6.75 = 76.75."

Identify two errors in the student's working and explain how each should be corrected. **[4]**

**11.** A firm predicts sales of 380 for Q3 next year. The Q3 mean seasonal variation is −24, the Q2 mean seasonal variation is +10, and the trend rises by 4 each quarter.

**(a)** Find the trend value used for Q3. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict sales for Q2 of the same year. **[3]**

**12.** The individual seasonal variations for Q1 are 8, 9 and 7. Those for Q3 are 20, −2 and 12.

**(a)** Find the mean seasonal variation for each quarter. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain which mean gives the more reliable basis for prediction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 132 − 120.5 = **11.5** [1].

**(b)** Sales in that quarter were 11.5 above the trend [1]. **[2]**

**2.** (−4.25 − 5.75 − 5.0) ÷ 3 = −15 ÷ 3 [1] = **−5.0** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The four mean seasonal variations should total roughly zero [1], so Q4 ≈ −(18.5 − 6.0 − 14.0) = **1.5** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 410 − 35 = **375** [1].

**(b)** In that quarter values are typically 35 below the trend [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Each actual value also contains random variation [1], which differs from year to year, so the difference from the trend changes even though the seasonal effect is similar [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Totals 168, 171, 175, 180, 183, 186, 190, 193, 197 [1]. Centred values (totals in pairs ÷ 8): 42.375, 43.25, 44.375, 45.375, 46.125, 47.0, 47.875, 48.75 [1] [1], from Year 1 Q3 to Year 3 Q2 [1].

**(b)** Actual − moving average: 15.625, −9.25, −11.375, 4.625, 16.875, −10.0, −11.875, 5.25 [2] (1 mark for at least four correct).

**(c)** Q1: (−11.375 − 11.875) ÷ 2 = **−11.625** [1]. Q2: (4.625 + 5.25) ÷ 2 = **4.9375** [1]. Q3: (15.625 + 16.875) ÷ 2 = **16.25**; Q4: (−9.25 − 10.0) ÷ 2 = **−9.625** [1].

**(d)** Total −11.625 + 4.9375 + 16.25 − 9.625 = −0.0625, close to zero, so the values are consistent [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Lay the work out in one table with columns for actual, moving average and difference; it makes grouping by quarter much easier.

**7. (a)** Year 1 Q3 to Year 3 Q3 is 8 quarters [1]. (228 − 200) ÷ 8 = **3.5 per quarter** [1].

**(b)** Year 4 Q1 is 2 quarters after Year 3 Q3: trend 228 + 7 = 235 [1], prediction 235 + 12 = **247** [1]. Year 4 Q4 is 5 quarters after: trend 228 + 17.5 = 245.5 [1], prediction 245.5 + 14 = **259.5** [1].

**(c)** **Year 4 Q4**, because the trend line is extended further beyond the data [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** **45, 46, 47.3, 49, 51, 52, 53** [2] (1 mark for at least four correct), from Year 1 Season 2 to Year 3 Season 2.

**(b)** Season 1: (48 − 47.33 + 54 − 52) ÷ 2 = (0.67 + 2) ÷ 2 = **1.33** [1]. Season 2: (17 + 17 + 16) ÷ 3 = **16.7** [1]. Season 3: (−18 − 18) ÷ 2 = **−18** [1].

**(c)** Trend rate = (53 − 45) ÷ 6 = 1.33 per season [1]. Year 4 Season 1 is two seasons after Year 3 Season 2, so trend ≈ 53 + 2.67 = 55.7 [1]. Prediction ≈ 55.7 + 1.33 = **57 thousand** [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** (−22 − 18 − 20) ÷ 3 = **−20** [1]; Mondays typically have about 20 fewer customers than the trend [1].

**(b)** 310 − 20 = **290 customers** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** First error: the seasonal variation should be actual − moving average [1], giving −6.5 and −7.0 and a mean of −6.75 [1]. Second error: the prediction should use the trend value read from the trend line extended to next year's Q2, not the last centred moving average [1], and then add the corrected component −6.75 [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Trend = 380 + 24 = **404** [1].

**(b)** Q2 is one quarter earlier, so trend = 404 − 4 = 400 [1]. Prediction = 400 + 10 [1] = **410** [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Q1: 24 ÷ 3 = **8** [1]. Q3: 30 ÷ 3 = **10** [1].

**(b)** The **Q1** mean is more reliable [1], because its individual values are very close together, whereas the Q3 values vary widely (from −2 to 20), so a mean of 10 may not represent a typical Q3 [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the subtraction, so every seasonal variation has the wrong sign.
- Averaging all individual values together rather than season by season.
- Predicting from the last moving average instead of the extended trend line.
- Miscounting the number of periods when extending the trend.
- Adding a negative seasonal component as if it were positive.
- Omitting the zero-total check when asked to comment.
