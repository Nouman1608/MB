These questions cover section 3.3.1 Metals of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards: the opening statements 3.3.1 a to c, the reactivity series (3.3.1.1 a to i) and metal carbonates (3.3.1.2 a and b). The qualification is not tiered. Questions 7, 8 and 11 are longer problems that combine several statements.

## Questions

**1.** A lump of gold is beaten into a very thin sheet.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of structure, why gold can be beaten into a sheet without breaking. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why gold conducts electricity. **[2]**

**2.** A foundry is choosing a material for a ship's propeller, which turns in sea water for many years.

| Material | Composition by mass | Relative hardness | Corrosion in sea water | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J | 88% copper, 12% tin | 7 | very slow | medium |
| K | 60% copper, 40% zinc | 6 | slow; zinc is gradually lost | low |
| L | 99.9% copper | 3 | very slow | medium |

**(a)** Explain why J and K are alloys but L is not. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of tin in a 25.0 kg propeller made from J. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the data to choose the best material for the propeller. Justify your choice by comparing it with the other two. **[3]**

**3.** In a house, copper is used for the coil inside a hot-water cylinder and for the cable that supplies an electric oven. Explain why copper is suitable for both of these uses. **[3]**

**4.** Small pieces of four metals, A to D, are tested with cold water and with dilute hydrochloric acid. The metals are calcium, copper, iron and lithium.

| Metal | Cold water | Dilute hydrochloric acid |
|---|---|---|
| A | sinks; fizzes steadily; water turns cloudy | not tested |
| B | no visible reaction | slow bubbles |
| C | floats; fizzes steadily; does not melt | not tested |
| D | no reaction | no reaction |

**(a)** Identify metals A, B, C and D. **[4]**
**(b)** Give the balanced symbol equation when metal A reacts with water. **[2]**

**5.** A student adds pieces of magnesium, zinc, iron and an unknown metal X to solutions of each metal's sulfate. A tick means a reaction was seen.

| Metal added | Magnesium sulfate | Zinc sulfate | Iron(II) sulfate | X sulfate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Magnesium | -- | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Zinc | ✗ | -- | ✓ | ✓ |
| Iron | ✗ | ✗ | -- | ✗ |
| X | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | -- |

**(a)** Use the results to rank all four metals from most to least reactive. **[2]**
**(b)** X forms X²⁺ ions. Write the ionic equation for the reaction of X with iron(II) sulfate solution. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain, in terms of electrons, which species is oxidised and which is reduced in your equation for (b). **[2]**

**6.** Gold is found in the ground as the metal. Calcium is found only in compounds.

**(a)** Explain why gold is found as the metal. **[1]**
**(b)** Calcium is more reactive than carbon. Suggest how calcium metal is obtained from calcium chloride, and explain why the calcium produced is expensive. **[3]**

**7.** Some iron ores contain magnetite, Fe₃O₄. In a blast furnace, carbon monoxide reduces it:

Fe₃O₄ + 4CO → 3Fe + 4CO₂

**(a)** Name **two** raw materials, other than the iron ore, that are added to a blast furnace. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the formation of carbon monoxide from carbon dioxide in the furnace. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the substance that is reduced in the reaction above. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be obtained from 464 tonnes of Fe₃O₄. (Aᵣ: Fe = 56, O = 16) **[3]**

**8.** A mining company has a large heap of waste rock that contains only a small percentage of copper compounds.

**(a)** Describe how a phytomining process would recover copper compounds from this heap. **[2]**
**(b)** State what bacteria produce when the heap is treated by bioleaching. **[1]**
**(c)** The solution from the heap contains copper sulfate. Scrap iron is added to it. Write the ionic equation for the reaction and explain why it is a redox reaction. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of iron that reacts when 16.0 kg of copper is produced. (Aᵣ: Fe = 56, Cu = 64) **[3]**
**(e)** Instead, some of the copper sulfate solution is electrolysed using carbon electrodes. Write the half equation for the reaction at each electrode. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows illustrative energy data for producing a metal.

| Source of metal | Energy needed per kg / MJ |
|---|---|
| Extracted from ore | 60.0 |
| Recycled scrap | 9.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of energy saved by recycling instead of extracting from ore. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy saved when 250 kg of the metal is recycled instead of extracted. **[1]**
**(c)** Give **two** reasons, other than saving energy, why metals should be recycled. **[2]**

**10.** A student heats 4.20 g of a white metal carbonate to constant mass. A white solid of mass 2.00 g remains. The solid stays white while hot and when cold. The carbonate is magnesium, zinc or calcium carbonate.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide given off. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the results fit magnesium carbonate and not calcium carbonate. (Aᵣ: Mg = 24, Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16) **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how the colour of the residue rules out zinc carbonate. **[1]**
**(d)** Another student suggests repeating the test with sodium carbonate. Predict what would happen to its mass in a Bunsen flame. **[1]**

**11.** 2.50 g of zinc carbonate, ZnCO₃, is added to excess dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation for this reaction. Name the salt formed. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe a test to show that the gas given off is carbon dioxide. Give the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide produced, in cm³. (Aᵣ: Zn = 65, C = 12, O = 16; one mole of gas occupies 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure) **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Gold atoms are arranged in layers [1]. The layers slide over each other when the gold is beaten, so it changes shape rather than breaking [1].
**(b)** Gold has delocalised electrons [1] that move through the structure and carry charge [1].
*Examiner insight:* An answer about "ions moving" in a solid metal usually gains no credit for (b); the moving particles must be electrons.

**2. (a)** J and K are mixtures of two metals; L is (almost) a single element [1].
**(b)** Mass of tin = 12/100 × 25.0 [1] = **3.00 kg** [1].
**(c)** Choose J [1]. J is harder than L, so it keeps its shape better, and corrodes more slowly than K [1]. K is cheaper but loses zinc in sea water, and L is too soft [1].
*Examiner insight:* Full credit for "justify by comparing" needs a data-based reason for rejecting each other material.

**3.** Good conductor of heat, so it heats the water in the cylinder [1]. Good conductor of electricity, so it suits the oven cable [1]. It bends into a coil but is hard enough to keep its shape, and does not react with water [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link every property to a use; a bare list of properties loses credit.

**4. (a)** A = **calcium** [1]; B = **iron** [1]; C = **lithium** [1]; D = **copper** [1].
**(b)** Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), check the hydroxide formula from the ion charges: calcium forms Ca²⁺, so it is Ca(OH)₂, not CaOH.

**5. (a)** Magnesium, zinc, X, iron: magnesium first and iron last [1]; zinc above X [1].
**(b)** **X + Fe²⁺ → X²⁺ + Fe** [1]
**(c)** X atoms lose electrons, so X is oxidised [1]. Fe²⁺ ions are reduced because they gain electrons [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) name the particles precisely: "X atoms" and "iron(II) ions". Writing "iron is oxidised" or leaving out the ion makes the answer ambiguous.

**6. (a)** Gold is very unreactive, so it has not combined with other elements [1].
**(b)** By electrolysis [1] of molten calcium chloride [1]. Electrolysis uses large amounts of energy, which makes the metal expensive [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Electrolysis of calcium chloride" without "molten" is incomplete; the compound must be melted so the ions can move.

**7. (a)** Any two from: coke (carbon); limestone; hot air [1] [1].
**(b)** CO₂ + C → 2CO [1]
**(c)** Fe₃O₄ (magnetite) is reduced [1] because it loses oxygen [1].
**(d)** Mᵣ Fe₃O₄ = 232 [1]. Amount = 464 ÷ 232 = 2.00 million mol, so iron = 6.00 million mol [1]. Mass = 6.00 × 56 = **336 tonnes** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d) a mass-ratio method (232 t of ore gives 168 t of iron) is equally creditworthy if the working is shown.

**8. (a)** Plants grown on the heap absorb copper compounds [1]. They are harvested and burned; the ash contains copper compounds [1].
**(b)** A leachate (solution) containing copper compounds [1].
**(c)** **Fe + Cu²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Cu** [1]. Iron atoms lose electrons and are oxidised [1]; copper ions gain electrons and are reduced, so both happen together [1].
**(d)** Amount of copper = 16.0 ÷ 64 = 0.250 kmol [1]. Ratio Fe : Cu = 1 : 1, so 0.250 kmol of iron [1]. Mass = 0.250 × 56 = **14.0 kg** [1].
**(e)** Negative electrode (cathode): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu [1]. Positive electrode (anode), where oxygen forms: 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Redox" needs both halves in (c); explaining only the oxidation of iron earns partial credit at best.

**9. (a)** Energy saved = 60.0 − 9.0 = 51.0 MJ per kg [1]. Percentage = 51.0 ÷ 60.0 × 100 = **85%** [1].
**(b)** 51.0 × 250 = **12 750 MJ** [1].
**(c)** Ores are limited resources [1]; mining and extraction damage the environment, for example waste rock or carbon dioxide [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) "it is better for the environment" alone is too vague to gain credit; name an effect such as waste rock or carbon dioxide.

**10. (a)** 4.20 − 2.00 = **2.20 g** [1].
**(b)** MgCO₃ (Mᵣ 84) → MgO (Mᵣ 40) [1]. 4.20 × 40/84 = **2.00 g**, matching the result [1]. CaCO₃ (Mᵣ 100) → CaO (Mᵣ 56) would leave 4.20 × 56/100 = 2.35 g, which does not match [1].
**(c)** Zinc oxide is yellow while hot; this residue stayed white [1].
**(d)** It would not change mass, because sodium carbonate does not decompose at Bunsen burner temperatures [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer needs every calculated value written down, not just the final 2.00 g.

**11. (a)** ZnCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂ [1]; the salt is **zinc sulfate** [1].
**(b)** Bubble the gas through limewater [1]; the limewater turns cloudy white [1].
**(c)** Mᵣ ZnCO₃ = 65 + 12 + 48 = 125, so amount = 2.50 ÷ 125 = 0.0200 mol [1]. Ratio ZnCO₃ : CO₂ = 1 : 1, so 0.0200 mol of CO₂ [1]. Volume = 0.0200 × 24 000 = **480 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), 24 dm³ is 24 000 cm³; an answer of 0.48 needs the unit dm³.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling an alloy a compound, or saying an alloy must contain only metals.
- Leaving spectator ions in an ionic equation, or not balancing charge.
- Saying the metal ion is oxidised in a displacement reaction.
- Mixing up phytomining (plants, burning, ash) with bioleaching (bacteria, leachate).
- Writing hydrogen at the cathode when copper ions are present; copper is deposited.
- Treating every carbonate as decomposing in a Bunsen flame.
