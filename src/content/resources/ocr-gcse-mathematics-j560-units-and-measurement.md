This guide covers section 10.01, Units and measurement, of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Every statement in 10.01 sits in the first two columns of the specification, so it is content for **both tiers**. It is often the hidden first step of a longer problem, on calculator and non-calculator papers alike.

The [units, compound measures, maps, bearings and perimeter guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-units-compound-measures-maps-bearings-perimeter/) covers 10.01 alongside 10.02. This page goes further on 10.01 alone: capacity from dimensions, average speed over a two-part journey, density and pressure with mixed units, scales in different forms, and bearings found with angle facts. ## What the specification asks

| Ref | Statement in the specification |
|---|---|
| 10.01a Units of measurement | "Use and convert standard units of measurement for length, area, volume/capacity, mass, time and money"; also "in algebraic contexts" |
| 10.01b Compound units | "Use and convert simple compound units (e.g. for speed, rates of pay, unit pricing)"; "other compound units (e.g. density, pressure)"; know and apply speed = distance ÷ time and density = mass ÷ volume; compound units "in algebraic contexts" |
| 10.01c Maps and scale drawings | "Use the scale of a map, and work with bearings. Construct and interpret scale drawings." |

Two formulae are named for you to know: speed = distance ÷ time and density = mass ÷ volume. Pressure is listed only as an example of a compound unit, so work with the relationship and units the question gives.

## Converting standard units

To a **smaller** unit, multiply; to a **larger** unit, divide. For area the length factor is squared, and for volume it is cubed.

| Length factor | Area factor | Volume factor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm² = 100 mm² | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = 10 000 cm² | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| 1 m = 1000 mm | 1 m² = 1 000 000 mm² | 1 m³ = 1000 litres |

**Worked example 1 (capacity from dimensions).** A fish tank is 80 cm long, 45 cm wide and 30 cm deep. A hose fills it at 12 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill?

```
volume = 80 × 45 × 30 = 108 000 cm³
1 litre = 1000 cm³, so capacity = 108 000 ÷ 1000 = 108 litres
time = 108 ÷ 12 = 9 minutes
```

Converting to litres first keeps the units matched: litres ÷ litres per minute gives minutes.

## Time and money

Time is not decimal. Change minutes to hours by dividing by 60, and hours to minutes by multiplying by 60.

- 4.45 hours is 4 hours and 0.45 × 60 = 27 minutes.
- 1 450 minutes is 1 450 ÷ 60 = 24 remainder 10, so 24 hours 10 minutes.
- A film starting at 19:45 and lasting 2 h 38 min ends at 19:45 + 2 h = 21:45, then + 38 min = 22:23.

**Worked example 2 (comparing prices in two currencies).** The exchange rates are £1 = $1.25 and £1 = €1.15 (invented rates). A jacket costs $90 in one shop and €80 in another. Which is cheaper, and by how much in pounds?

```
$90 ÷ 1.25 = £72.00
€80 ÷ 1.15 = £69.565... = £69.57
difference = 72.00 − 69.57 = £2.43
```

The €80 jacket is cheaper by £2.43. Round money to the nearest penny only at the end.

## Units in algebraic contexts

The same conversions apply when a quantity is a letter. Write the conversion next to the letter, then simplify.

| Context | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A bottle holds v litres. How many 250 ml glasses? | 1000v ÷ 250 | 4v glasses |
| A walk takes t hours. How many minutes? | 60 × t | 60t minutes |
| Tiles are x cm wide. How many fit along 3 m? | 300 ÷ x | 300/x tiles |
| A rug is a m by b m. Area in cm²? | ab × 10 000 | 10 000ab cm² |

## Compound units

A compound unit combines two units, such as km/h or g/cm³.

### Speed and average speed

speed = distance ÷ time, distance = speed × time, time = distance ÷ speed.

**Worked example 3 (average speed for a journey in two parts).** A driver travels 90 km at 60 km/h, then 50 km in 40 minutes. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

```
time for part 1 = 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 h
time for part 2 = 40 min = 40/60 = 2/3 h
total distance  = 90 + 50 = 140 km
total time      = 1.5 + 2/3 = 13/6 h   (2 h 10 min)
average speed   = 140 ÷ 13/6 = 140 × 6/13 = 64.6 km/h (3 s.f.)
```

Part 2 is at 75 km/h, but averaging 60 and 75 gives 67.5, which is wrong: more time is spent at the slower speed.

**Converting speed.** Convert the top and bottom separately. 25 m/s means 25 × 3600 = 90 000 m in an hour, which is 90 km/h. So m/s to km/h multiplies by 3.6, and km/h to m/s divides by 3.6.

### Density

density = mass ÷ volume, so mass = density × volume and volume = mass ÷ density.

**Worked example 4.** A metal block measures 5 cm by 4 cm by 2.5 cm. Its density is 8.9 g/cm³. Find its mass in kilograms.

```
volume = 5 × 4 × 2.5 = 50 cm³
mass   = 8.9 × 50 = 445 g = 0.445 kg
```

**Why 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.** One cubic metre is 1 000 000 cm³, so at 1 g/cm³ it has mass 1 000 000 g = 1000 kg. A stone of mass 2.4 kg and volume 0.0008 m³ has density 2.4 ÷ 0.0008 = 3000 kg/m³, which is 3 g/cm³.

### Pressure

Pressure is force per unit area, measured in N/m² or N/cm². The trap is an area given in cm² when the answer must be in N/m².

**Worked example 5.** A crate exerts a force of 600 N on a base 40 cm by 30 cm. Find the pressure in N/cm² and in N/m².

```
area = 40 × 30 = 1200 cm² = 1200 ÷ 10 000 = 0.12 m²
pressure = 600 ÷ 1200 = 0.5 N/cm²
pressure = 600 ÷ 0.12 = 5000 N/m²
```

### Rates of pay, unit pricing and other rates

- **Pay:** £296.40 for 26 hours is 296.40 ÷ 26 = £11.40 per hour.
- **Best buy:** a 330 ml can costs 65p and a 2 litre bottle costs £2.10. Use the same units: 65 ÷ 330 = 0.197 p per ml, and 210 ÷ 2000 = 0.105 p per ml. The bottle is better value.
- **Algebraic rates:** a car uses f litres per 100 km. For d km it uses f × d/100 = fd/100 litres.

## Maps and scale drawings

A scale can be written as a ratio (1 : 25 000) or in words (4 cm represents 1 km). To change words into a ratio, put both sides in the same unit.

```
1 km = 100 000 cm, so 4 cm : 100 000 cm = 1 : 25 000
```

On a 1 : 25 000 map, 1 cm represents 25 000 cm = 250 m. A path measuring 6.8 cm is 6.8 × 250 = 1700 m = 1.7 km. A real distance of 3.2 km is 320 000 ÷ 25 000 = 12.8 cm on the map.

### Bearings found with angle facts

A bearing is measured clockwise from north, with three figures. North lines at different points are parallel, so the angle facts for parallel lines apply.

**Worked example 6.** The bearing of B from A is 058°. Find the bearing of A from B.

The angle between the north line at A and AB is 58°. At B, the north line and the line BA make co-interior angles with the north line at A, so the angle at B measured anticlockwise from north to BA is 180° − 58° = 122°. Measured clockwise, that is 360° − 122° = **238°**. Adding 180° gives the same answer, but a "show" question needs the angle reasoning.

### Locating a point from two bearings

**Worked example 7.** Port P is 12 km due east of lighthouse L. A ship S is on a bearing of 045° from L and 315° from P. Using a scale of 1 cm to 2 km, find how far the ship is from L.

```
LP = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm on the drawing
at L: angle between east (090°) and 045° = 45°
at P: angle between west (270°) and 315° = 45°
```

Draw LP 6 cm long with a north line at each end, and measure 045° at L and 315° at P. The lines cross at S. LS measures about 4.2 cm, so S is about **8.5 km** from L. The triangle has two 45° angles, so by calculation LS = 12 ÷ √2 = 8.49 km, which agrees.

## Common mistakes

- Using 100 for m² to cm², or 1000 for m³ to cm³.
- Reading 4.45 hours as 4 hours 45 minutes.
- Averaging two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
- Dividing a force by an area in cm² and giving the answer in N/m².
- Measuring a bearing anticlockwise, from the wrong point, or with two figures.

## Exam technique

- **"Work out"** on a non-calculator paper: show each conversion on its own line, with its unit.
- **"Give your answer in …"**: convert at the end to the unit asked for.
- **"Show that"**: write every step, including the conversion factor you used.
- **Scale drawings**: draw a north line at each point and state your scale. Give measured answers as "about", and check by calculation if you can.
