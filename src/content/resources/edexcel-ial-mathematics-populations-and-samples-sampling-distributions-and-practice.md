## Questions

**1.** A sports club with 480 members keeps a list of every member. It wants opinions on a new kit from a sample of members.

**(a)** State the population and the sampling unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify a suitable sampling frame. **[1]**

**2.** A company tests each fuse in a batch by passing a current until it blows.

**(a)** Explain why a census of the batch is not sensible. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one advantage of using a sample. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one disadvantage of using a sample survey. **[1]**

**3.** A population has values 2, 4 and 6, each equally likely. Samples of size 2 are taken with replacement. T is the smaller sample value.

**(a)** Find the sampling distribution of T. **[3]**
**(b)** Find E(T). **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why T is a statistic. **[1]**

**4.** A website says 25% of visitors sign up for its newsletter. After a redesign the owner thinks the proportion has fallen. In a random sample of 28 visitors, 3 sign up. Let X ~ B(28, p) be the number who sign up. Use P(X ≤ 1) = 0.0033, P(X ≤ 2) = 0.0166, P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0551 for p = 0.25.

**(a)** State suitable hypotheses. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the critical region for a test at the 5% level. **[3]**
**(c)** State the conclusion of the test, in context. **[1]**

**5.** A machine makes components. Of its components, 10% are defective. After servicing, an engineer suspects the proportion defective has risen. In a random sample of 40 components, 9 are defective. Test at the 1% level. For X ~ B(40, 0.1): P(X ≥ 8) = 0.0419, P(X ≥ 9) = 0.0155, P(X ≥ 10) = 0.0051.

**(a)** State hypotheses. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the critical region is X ≥ 10 and state the actual significance level. **[4]**
**(c)** State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

**6.** A survey says 45% of commuters cycle weekly. A researcher tests whether this has changed, using 18 commuters, at the 5% level (2.5% in each tail). For X ~ B(18, 0.45): P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0120, P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0411, P(X ≥ 12) = 0.0537, P(X ≥ 13) = 0.0183.

**(a)** State hypotheses. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the critical region. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the actual significance level. **[1]**
**(d)** In the sample, 13 commuters cycle weekly. State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

**7.** A call centre receives complaints at a mean rate of 3.2 per day. After a promotion the manager expects an increase. Over 5 days there are 25 complaints. Test at the 5% level. For Po(16): P(X ≥ 23) = 0.0582, P(X ≥ 24) = 0.0367.

**(a)** State hypotheses and the distribution of the number of complaints in 5 days under H₀. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the critical region. **[2]**
**(c)** State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

**8.** A server records failed logins at a mean of 0.8 per minute. After an update the administrator thinks the rate has changed. In 10 minutes there are 2. Test at the 10% level (5% in each tail). For Po(8): P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0424, P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0996, P(X ≥ 13) = 0.0638, P(X ≥ 14) = 0.0342.

**(a)** State hypotheses and the distribution of X under H₀. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the critical region. **[4]**
**(c)** State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

**9.** A theatre says 15% of bookings are cancelled. After introducing a deposit, the manager thinks fewer are. Of 200 bookings, 22 are cancelled. Test at the 5% level using a normal approximation.

**(a)** State hypotheses and the normal distribution that approximates X, the number cancelled, under H₀. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the probability, using a continuity correction, that X is at most 22. **[3]**
**(c)** State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

**10.** A fitness app says 25% of users complete a 7-day streak. After adding reminders, the developer thinks this has risen. In a random sample of 12 users, 6 complete a streak. Test at the 5% level. For X ~ B(12, 0.25): P(X ≥ 6) = 0.0544, P(X ≥ 7) = 0.0143.

**(a)** State hypotheses. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the critical region. **[3]**
**(c)** State the conclusion in context. **[2]**
**(d)** State the actual significance level. **[1]**
**(e)** Give one comment on the sample size and one assumption the binomial model needs. **[2]**

**11.** A water company logs blockages at a mean of 2.5 per hour. An engineer tests whether a new filter has reduced the rate, using 4 hourly periods chosen at random from the 720 hours in a month.

**(a)** State the sampling unit and a suitable sampling frame. **[2]**
**(b)** State hypotheses and the distribution of the total count over the 4 hours under H₀. **[2]**
**(c)** For a 5% level, find the critical region, given P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0293 and P(X ≤ 5) = 0.0671. **[3]**
**(d)** The total over the 4 hours is 3. State the conclusion in context. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Population: all 480 members [1]. Sampling unit: one member [1].
**(b)** The secretary's list of all members [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A frame must list every unit.

**2. (a)** Testing destroys each fuse [1], so a census would leave nothing to sell [1].
**(b)** Cheaper or quicker than testing every fuse [1].
**(c)** Results vary from sample to sample, or a poorly chosen sample may be unrepresentative [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "A census takes too long" ignores the context. Tie the reason to the testing.

**3. (a)** Nine pairs, each probability 1/9 [1]. T = 2 for 22, 24, 26, 42, 62: P(T = 2) = 5/9 [1]. T = 4 for 44, 46, 64: 3/9. T = 6 for 66: 1/9 [1].
**(b)** E(T) = (2×5 + 4×3 + 6×1)/9 [1] = 28/9 ≈ 3.11 [1].
**(c)** It is calculated only from the sample values and contains no unknown parameter [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* List all nine pairs first.

**4. (a)** H₀: p = 0.25, H₁: p < 0.25 [1].
**(b)** P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0551 > 0.05 [1]. P(X ≤ 2) = 0.0166 < 0.05 [1]. Critical region: X ≤ 2 [1].
**(c)** 3 is outside the critical region, so H₀ stands. At the 5% level the sample does not give enough evidence that the proportion signing up has fallen [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both boundary probabilities.

**5. (a)** H₀: p = 0.1, H₁: p > 0.1 [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 9) = 0.0155 > 0.01, so 9 is not in the region [1]. P(X ≥ 10) = 0.0051 < 0.01 [1]. Critical region X ≥ 10 [1]. Actual significance level 0.0051 (0.51%) [1].
**(c)** 9 is outside the critical region, so H₀ stands. At the 1% level the sample does not give enough evidence that the proportion defective has risen [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The same count can reject at 5% but not at 1%, so read the level carefully.

**6. (a)** H₀: p = 0.45, H₁: p ≠ 0.45 [1].
**(b)** Lower: P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0411 > 0.025  [1]; P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0120 < 0.025, region X ≤ 3 [1]. Upper: P(X ≥ 12) = 0.0537 > 0.025  [1]; P(X ≥ 13) = 0.0183 < 0.025, region X ≥ 13 [1]. Critical region: X ≤ 3 or X ≥ 13.
**(c)** 0.0120 + 0.0183 = **0.0303** [1].
**(d)** 13 is in the critical region, so reject H₀. At the 5% level the sample gives enough evidence that the proportion cycling weekly has changed [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare each tail with 0.025, not 0.05, and state both regions.

**7. (a)** H₀: λ = 3.2 per day, H₁: λ > 3.2 [1]. Over 5 days X ~ Po(16) [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 23) = 0.0582 > 0.05 [1]; P(X ≥ 24) = 0.0367 < 0.05, so critical region X ≥ 24 [1].
**(c)** 25 is in the critical region, so reject H₀. At the 5% level the sample gives enough evidence that the daily rate of complaints has increased [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Scaling the mean to 16 is easy to skip.

**8. (a)** H₀: λ = 0.8 per minute, H₁: λ ≠ 0.8 [1]. X ~ Po(8) [1].
**(b)** Lower: P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0996 > 0.05 [1]; P(X ≤ 3) = 0.0424 < 0.05, region X ≤ 3 [1]. Upper: P(X ≥ 13) = 0.0638 > 0.05 [1]; P(X ≥ 14) = 0.0342 < 0.05, region X ≥ 14 [1]. Critical region X ≤ 3 or X ≥ 14.
**(c)** 2 is in the critical region, so reject H₀. At the 10% level the sample gives enough evidence that the rate of failed login attempts has changed [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A two-tailed 10% test uses 5% in each tail.

**9. (a)** H₀: p = 0.15, H₁: p < 0.15 [1]. Mean = 200 × 0.15 = 30 [1]. Variance = 200 × 0.15 × 0.85 = 25.5, so X ≈ N(30, 25.5) [1].
**(b)** P(X ≤ 22) ≈ P(Y < 22.5) using the continuity correction [1]. z = (22.5 − 30)/√25.5 = −1.485 [1]. P(Z < −1.485) = **0.0687** [1].
**(c)** 0.0687 > 0.05, so H₀ stands. At the 5% level the sample does not give enough evidence that the proportion of cancelled bookings has fallen [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 22 instead of 22.5 changes the probability.

**10. (a)** H₀: p = 0.25, H₁: p > 0.25 [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 6) = 0.0544 > 0.05 [1]. P(X ≥ 7) = 0.0143 < 0.05 [1]. Critical region X ≥ 7 [1].
**(c)** 6 is outside the critical region: do not reject H₀ [1]. At the 5% level the sample does not give enough evidence that the proportion completing a streak has risen [1].
**(d)** 0.0143 (1.43%) [1].
**(e)** The sample is small, so a modest rise is unlikely to be detected [1]. Users must be independent with the same probability of a streak [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* 0.0544 against 0.05 is a "do not reject". Do not write that the app has had no effect.

**11. (a)** Sampling unit: one hourly period [1]. Sampling frame: the list of all 720 hourly periods in the month [1].
**(b)** H₀: λ = 2.5 per hour, H₁: λ < 2.5 [1]. Over 4 hours X ~ Po(10) [1].
**(c)** P(X ≤ 5) = 0.0671 > 0.05 [1]. P(X ≤ 4) = 0.0293 < 0.05 [1]. Critical region X ≤ 4 [1].
**(d)** 3 is in the critical region, so reject H₀. At the 5% level the sample gives enough evidence that the hourly rate of blockages has fallen [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The sampling units are hourly periods, not blockages.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing hypotheses about X instead of p or λ.
- Leaving the Poisson mean unscaled for a longer interval.
- Setting a single tail against the whole level in a two-tailed test.
- A critical value that overshoots the level, or not checking the next value down.
- A missing or reversed continuity correction.
- A conclusion with no context, or "H₀ accepted".
