## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain the purpose of a trial balance.
- (b) Prepare a trial balance.
- (c) Understand the limitations of a trial balance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trial balance | List of all ledger balances at one date in debit and credit columns |
| Balance b/d | The figure brought down after balancing an account; the trial balance figure |
| Debit balance | Debits exceed credits: assets, expenses, drawings |
| Credit balance | Credits exceed debits: liabilities, income, capital, provisions |
| Suspense account | Temporary account for a trial balance difference while errors are found |
| Transposition | Two digits swapped, such as 1,290 written as 1,920 |
| Compensating errors | Two equal errors on opposite sides that cancel |

## Three purposes

1. Check that total debits equal total credits.
2. Provide the figures for the financial statements.
3. Help find errors: the size of a difference is a clue.

## DEAD CLIC

Debits: Expenses, Assets, Drawings. Credits: Liabilities, Income, Capital.

## Column checklist

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets at cost | Provision for depreciation |
| Opening inventory | Provision for irrecoverable debts |
| Purchases, sales returns | Sales, purchases returns |
| Carriage inwards and outwards | Discount received |
| Discount allowed, irrecoverable debts | Trade payables, loans |
| All other expenses | Bank overdraft |
| Trade receivables, bank (in funds), cash | Capital |
| Drawings | Rent received, commission received |

## Preparing: the routine

1. Balance every ledger account.
2. Head it "Trial balance at [date]".
3. One line per account, one column per balance.
4. Total both columns on the same line.
5. If one figure is missing (often capital), it is the difference between the totals.

Mini-example: debits total 84,600; credits other than capital total 31,950. Capital = 52,650.

## Limitations

Totals agree even with these errors:

| Error | Short example |
|---|---|
| Omission | Sales invoice never recorded |
| Commission | Payment from A. Patel credited to A. Patil |
| Principle | New computer debited to office expenses |
| Original entry | Invoice of 1,470 recorded as 1,740 on both sides |
| Complete reversal | Cash sale debited to sales, credited to cash |
| Compensating | Purchases overcast 100 and sales overcast 100 |

Also: balances at one date only; no check on whether amounts are reasonable; year-end adjustments may be missing.

## Error-finding tests

| Test | Suggests |
|---|---|
| A balance equals the difference | That balance omitted |
| A balance equals half the difference | That balance in the wrong column |
| Difference divisible by 9 | Transposed digits |

Worked check: a trial balance shows debits 40,600 and credits 41,500. The difference is 900. If wages of 900 are missing from the debit column, adding them makes both totals 41,500. If instead a debit balance of 450 has been placed in the credit column, moving it lowers credits to 41,050 and raises debits to 41,050.

## Exam traps

- Closing inventory in the trial balance (only opening inventory, unless told otherwise).
- Drawings in the credit column.
- Provisions in the debit column.
- Carriage inwards treated as income.
- Heading "for the year ended" instead of "at".
- Claiming an agreed trial balance proves the books are correct.
- Showing totals on different lines.

## Quick self-test

1. State two purposes of a trial balance.
2. Which column: discount allowed?
3. Which column: provision for depreciation?
4. Which column: bank overdraft?
5. Which column: carriage inwards?
6. Debits total 152,300; credits excluding capital total 97,480. What is capital?
7. The trial balance difference is 1,240 and a balance of 620 exists. What error is suggested?
8. The difference is 270. What error might be suspected?
9. Name four errors that do not affect the agreement of the trial balance.
10. A new van is debited to motor expenses. Which error is this, and why does the trial balance still agree?
11. Why is opening, not closing, inventory in the trial balance?
12. Why does an agreed trial balance not prove the accounts are correct?

### Answers

1. Any two: check arithmetical accuracy; provide figures for the financial statements; help locate errors.
2. Debit.
3. Credit.
4. Credit.
5. Debit.
6. 152,300 − 97,480 = 54,820.
7. The 620 balance has been put in the wrong column.
8. Transposed digits, since 270 = 9 × 30.
9. Any four: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating.
10. An error of principle; a debit and a credit of equal amount were still made.
11. Closing inventory is counted at the year end and recorded when the financial statements are prepared; the ledger still holds the opening figure.
12. It checks only that debits equal credits; errors with equal debits and credits are not revealed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is capital always the missing figure in a trial balance question?**
Often, because it is easiest to find as the difference, but any single missing balance can be found the same way.

**What is the first step if a trial balance does not agree?**
Check the size of the difference against the balances: equal to a balance, equal to twice a balance, or divisible by 9.
