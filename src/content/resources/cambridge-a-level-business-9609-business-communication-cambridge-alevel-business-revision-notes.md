All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## The communication process

Sender → message → method → channel → receiver → feedback. Anything that distorts the message is **noise**.

Use the process to locate a failure:

- Badly worded message: a **sender** problem.
- Right words, wrong medium: a **method** problem.
- Delayed or changed on the way: a **channel** problem.
- Not read, not trusted, overloaded: a **receiver** problem.
- Nobody checked: a **feedback** problem.

## 7.2.1 When communication is essential

Justify each with the cost of failure:

- Safety and legal duties: injury, fines, claims.
- Change: rumour, resistance, falling morale.
- Coordination between departments: stock-outs, idle capacity, broken promises to customers.
- Targets and feedback: staff cannot judge their own performance.
- Crisis: inconsistent messages damage reputation.
- External stakeholders: customers, suppliers, lenders and investors lose trust.

## 7.2.2 Matching method to message

| Message feature | Best method |
|---|---|
| Urgent | Spoken or electronic |
| Complex or detailed | Written, with visual for figures |
| Sensitive | Face-to-face spoken first, then written |
| Record needed | Written or electronic |
| Large or dispersed audience | Electronic |
| Trends in numbers | Visual (charts, dashboards) |

Rule of thumb: spoken for understanding, written or electronic for the record. Many situations need both.

## 7.2.3 Channels

- **One-way**: cheap and quick; no check on understanding. Suits routine, simple messages.
- **Two-way**: slower and uses more staff time; reveals misunderstanding and involves staff.
- **Vertical downward**: instructions and targets. Problem: distortion through many levels.
- **Vertical upward**: reports and suggestions. Problem: filtering, because staff tell managers what they want to hear.
- **Horizontal**: same level, across departments. Problem: weak in functional structures, so departments become isolated.

Illustrative distortion check: 90% accuracy at each of four levels leaves 0.9⁴ = 0.656, about 66% of the meaning. Fewer levels, less distortion.

## 7.2.4 Barriers and remedies by source

| Source | Barrier | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | Jargon, unclear or overlong message | Plain language, one main point |
| Method | Wrong medium for the message | Match method to message features |
| Channel | Too many levels; system failure | Shorter chain; direct briefings; backup method |
| Receiver | Overload; mistrust; lack of interest | Prioritise; explain relevance; consistent honesty |
| Organisation | Distance, time zones, isolated departments | Video meetings; shared platforms; cross-department teams |

## 7.2.5 Management's role

- **Informal communication (grapevine)**: fast, builds relationships, reveals real staff views; but spreads rumour, especially when formal messages are late. Work with it; do not try to ban it.
- **Efficiency**: fewer errors and less rework, quicker decisions, less duplicated work, lower labour turnover.
- **Improving communication in a given situation**: diagnose the barrier → choose method and channel → build in feedback → monitor results.

**Worked reminder: Tidewell Print.** 1,500 jobs a month; 4% need reprinting after misunderstood customer instructions, at $30 each: 1,500 × 0.04 × 30 = $1,800 a month. A standard order form with a confirmation call cuts reprints to 1.5%: 1,500 × 0.015 × 30 = $675. Saving $1,125 a month before the cost of the calls.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Method | The medium used: spoken, written, electronic or visual |
| Channel | The route the message takes through the organisation |
| Feedback | The receiver's response, showing whether the message was understood |
| Noise | Anything that distorts or blocks the message |
| One-way communication | From sender to receiver with no response |
| Two-way communication | Includes feedback and discussion |
| Vertical communication | Up or down the hierarchy |
| Horizontal communication | Between people at the same level |
| Informal communication | Outside official channels, such as the grapevine |
| Barrier | Anything that stops a message being received and understood as intended |

## Exam traps

- Confusing method (email) with channel (downward vertical).
- Assuming electronic means effective: overload and no feedback are common.
- Listing barriers without saying how each would be overcome.
- Recommending a remedy that does not match the barrier shown in the case.
- Treating informal communication as only negative.
- Forgetting cost: two-way methods take staff time, which can be calculated.
- In "advise" questions, failing to give one clear recommendation.

## Self-test

1. List the six parts of the communication process.
2. What is meant by noise in communication?
3. Give the main weakness of one-way communication.
4. Which channel problem is caused by staff telling managers only good news?
5. Which method best suits a sensitive message, and why?
6. Why is horizontal communication often weak in a functional structure?
7. Give one benefit and one drawback of informal communication.
8. A firm has five levels and each passes on 80% of the meaning. What share reaches the bottom? (Illustrative.)
9. Name one barrier caused by the receiver and its remedy.
10. A call centre handles 8,000 calls a week; 5% are repeat calls caused by unclear information, each costing $4. Calculate the weekly cost.
11. State the four steps for improving communication in a given situation.
12. What is the difference between "advise" and "evaluate"?

### Answers

1. Sender, message, method, channel, receiver, feedback.
2. Anything that distorts or blocks the message, such as background sound, unclear wording or distraction.
3. The sender cannot check that the message has been understood.
4. Filtering in upward vertical communication.
5. Face-to-face spoken, because the receiver can ask questions and the sender can judge reactions and show respect; follow with a written record.
6. Staff are grouped by function with their own targets and managers, so there are few routine links across departments.
7. Benefit: spreads information quickly and shows managers real staff views. Drawback: spreads rumour and inaccurate information.
8. 0.8⁵ = 0.328, about 33%.
9. Overload; prioritise messages and send summaries. (Or mistrust; consistent, honest communication.)
10. 8,000 × 0.05 = 400 repeat calls; 400 × 4 = **$1,600** a week.
11. Diagnose the barrier, choose method and channel, build in feedback, monitor results.
12. Advise asks for a recommended course of action with justification; evaluate asks you to weigh both sides and judge quality or value.
