These questions cover **3.2.8 Conservation of energy** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [conservation of energy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-conservation-of-energy-oxfordaqa-alevel/) or the [conservation of energy revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-conservation-of-energy-oxfordaqa-alevel-revision-notes/) first. Take g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State the principle of conservation of energy. **[1]**

**2.** Two balls, of mass 0.10 kg and 0.50 kg, are dropped from the same height. Air resistance is negligible. Which statement is correct as they reach the ground?

A: They have the same speed.

B: They have the same kinetic energy.

C: The heavier ball is moving faster.

D: The lighter ball has more kinetic energy.

**[1]**

**3.** A 0.40 kg ball is dropped from rest from a height of 3.2 m. Calculate its kinetic energy and its speed just before it lands, ignoring air resistance. **[2]**

**4.** A gymnast leaves a trampoline vertically at 7.0 m s⁻¹. Calculate the maximum height she rises above the trampoline, ignoring air resistance. **[1]**

**5.** Explain, in terms of energy, why the swings of a real pendulum gradually become smaller. **[2]**

**6.** A car of mass 1200 kg slows from 25 m s⁻¹ to 10 m s⁻¹ on a level road. Calculate the energy transferred by the brakes. **[2]**

**7.** The speed of an object is tripled. By what factor does its kinetic energy increase?

A: 3

B: 6

C: 9

D: 27

**[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A roller-coaster car of mass 500 kg starts from rest at the top of a hill 30 m above the ground. It runs down to ground level and then up a second hill 18 m high.

**(a)** Ignoring resistive forces, calculate the speed of the car at ground level. **[2]**

**(b)** Ignoring resistive forces, calculate its speed at the top of the second hill. **[2]**

**(c)** In practice the car reaches the top of the second hill at 12 m s⁻¹. Calculate the work done against resistive forces between the two hilltops. **[2]**

**9.** A 2.0 kg block is released from rest at the top of a ramp. It slides 4.0 m down the ramp, dropping a vertical height of 1.5 m. The average frictional force on it is 3.0 N.

**(a)** Calculate the work done against friction on the ramp. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the block at the bottom of the ramp. **[3]**

**(c)** The block then slides across a horizontal floor where the frictional force is 5.0 N. Calculate how far it travels before stopping. **[2]**

**10.** A toy launcher uses a spring of spring constant 600 N m⁻¹ compressed by 0.050 m to fire a 0.030 kg ball vertically upwards.

**(a)** Calculate the elastic potential energy stored in the compressed spring. **[1]**

**(b)** Ignoring air resistance, calculate the maximum height of the ball above its starting position. **[2]**

**(c)** In a test, the ball rises only 2.1 m. Calculate the percentage of the stored energy transferred to other stores. **[2]**

**11.** A bungee jumper of mass 70 kg steps off a platform. The cord is 20 m long when unstretched, and the jumper's lowest point is 50 m below the platform.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfers from the moment the jumper steps off until the lowest point. **[3]**

**(b)** Ignoring air resistance and the mass of the cord, calculate the elastic potential energy stored in the cord at the lowest point. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the jumper's speed at the moment the cord becomes taut. **[2]**

**12.** A 0.16 kg ball is thrown at 15 m s⁻¹, at 40° above the horizontal, from the top of a cliff 30 m above the sea.

**(a)** Ignoring air resistance, calculate the speed of the ball when it reaches the sea. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the angle of the throw was not needed in (a). **[1]**

**(c)** With air resistance, the ball reaches the sea at 24 m s⁻¹. Calculate the work done against air resistance. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one store to another, so the total energy of a closed system is constant [1] **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* v = √(2gh) contains no mass. The heavier ball has five times the kinetic energy but the same speed.

**3.** Ek = mgΔh = 0.40 × 9.81 × 3.2 = **12.6 J** [1]; v = √(2 × 12.6 ÷ 0.40) = **7.9 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**

**4.** h = 7.0² ÷ (2 × 9.81) = **2.5 m** [1] **[1]**

**5.** Air resistance (and friction at the pivot) does work against the motion on every swing [1]; this transfers energy to internal energy of the air and surroundings, so the total of kinetic and gravitational potential energy falls and the bob rises less high [1] **[2]**

**6.** ΔEk = ½ × 1200 × (25² − 10²) [1] = **3.2 × 10⁵ J** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* ½ × 1200 × (25 − 10)² = 1.35 × 10⁵ J is a common wrong answer. Find each kinetic energy, then subtract.

**7.** **C** [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** ½v² = 9.81 × 30 [1]; v = **24 m s⁻¹** (24.3) [1]

**(b)** Net drop from start = 30 − 18 = 12 m [1]; v = √(2 × 9.81 × 12) = **15 m s⁻¹** (15.3) [1]

**(c)** Ep lost from first hilltop to second = 500 × 9.81 × 12 = 58 900 J; Ek at second hilltop = ½ × 500 × 12² = 36 000 J [1]; work against resistance = **2.3 × 10⁴ J** [1] **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** 3.0 × 4.0 = **12 J** [1]

**(b)** Ep lost = 2.0 × 9.81 × 1.5 = 29.4 J [1]; Ek = 29.4 − 12 = 17.4 J [1]; v = √(2 × 17.4 ÷ 2.0) = **4.2 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** All 17.4 J is transferred against friction: 5.0 × d = 17.4 [1]; d = **3.5 m** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Friction acts along the 4.0 m path, not the 1.5 m height. Using the height gives 4.5 J of work against friction, and a speed that is too high.

**10.**

**(a)** ½ × 600 × 0.050² = **0.75 J** [1]

**(b)** 0.030 × 9.81 × h = 0.75 [1]; h = **2.5 m** [1]

**(c)** Ep at 2.1 m = 0.030 × 9.81 × 2.1 = 0.62 J [1]; transferred elsewhere = (0.75 − 0.62) ÷ 0.75 × 100 = **18%** [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** During free fall, gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy [1]; once the cord is taut, gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy are transferred to elastic potential energy in the cord [1]; at the lowest point kinetic energy is zero, and throughout some energy is transferred to internal energy of the air and cord [1]

**(b)** Ep lost = 70 × 9.81 × 50 = **3.4 × 10⁴ J**, all stored as elastic potential energy [1]

**(c)** v = √(2 × 9.81 × 20) [1] = **20 m s⁻¹** (19.8) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the full 50 m drop counts, not the 30 m stretch: the gravitational store keeps falling while the cord stretches.

**12.**

**(a)** v² = u² + 2gΔh = 15² + 2 × 9.81 × 30 = 814 [1]; v = **28.5 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Kinetic energy depends only on speed, not direction, and the change in gravitational potential energy depends only on the height fallen [1]

**(c)** Work = ½ × 0.16 × (28.5² − 24²) = ½ × 0.16 × (813.6 − 576) [1] = **19 J** [1] **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Height and path length confused in friction questions.
- Kinetic energy change found from the change in speed squared, not the change in v².
- Answers that say "energy is lost" without naming the store it goes to.
- Using the stretch of a bungee cord, not the total drop, for gravitational potential energy.
