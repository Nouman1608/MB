Cambridge IGCSE Physics (0625) is a broad introduction to physics for
students who may go on to study the subject further or who simply want
a secure grounding in how the physical world works. It covers six
content areas, from motion and energy through to space physics, and it
is examined at two levels, Core and Extended. Every candidate sits
three papers: a multiple-choice paper worth 30%, a theory paper worth
50% and a practical component worth 20%. This overview describes the
syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028.

## How the course is organised

The six areas of the syllabus are:

1. **Motion, forces and energy** – physical quantities and measurement
   techniques, motion, mass and weight, density, forces, momentum,
   energy, work and power, and pressure. This is the largest area and
   the foundation for much of the rest.
2. **Thermal physics** – the kinetic particle model of matter, thermal
   properties and temperature, and transfer of thermal energy.
3. **Waves** – general properties of waves, light, the electromagnetic
   spectrum and sound.
4. **Electricity and magnetism** – simple phenomena of magnetism,
   electrical quantities, electric circuits, electrical safety and
   electromagnetic effects.
5. **Nuclear physics** – the nuclear model of the atom and
   radioactivity.
6. **Space physics** – the Earth and the Solar System, and stars and the
   Universe.

Core and Extended candidates study the same six areas. Extended
candidates cover additional material within them, which is why the
Extended route opens up the higher grades.

## How it's assessed

| Route | Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Paper 1 | Multiple Choice (Core) | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Core | Paper 3 | Theory (Core) | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Extended | Paper 2 | Multiple Choice (Extended) | 45 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Extended | Paper 4 | Theory (Extended) | 75 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Both | Paper 5 | Practical Test | 75 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Both | Paper 6 | Alternative to Practical | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

A Core candidate takes Papers 1 and 3; an Extended candidate takes
Papers 2 and 4. On top of that, every candidate sits **one** of Paper 5
or Paper 6, so only one practical component counts towards the total.
Paper 5 is a practical test carried out with apparatus; Paper 6 is a
written paper that assesses the same practical skills without hands-on
work in the exam. Your school decides which one you take.

The route affects the grades available: Core candidates are eligible
for grades C to G, while Extended candidates are eligible for A* to G.
All papers are externally assessed, and exams run in the June and
November series.

## How the parts fit together

Neither the multiple-choice paper nor the theory paper is linked to a
particular content area; both can test any part of the syllabus for
your route. That means the six areas need to be learned as a connected
whole rather than as separate units.

Some of the strongest links are:

- **Energy** appears in motion and forces, in thermal physics, in
  electricity and in nuclear physics, so the ideas of work, power and
  energy transfer from area 1 keep returning.
- **Waves** connect to the electromagnetic spectrum, which in turn
  matters for radiation in nuclear physics and for light from stars in
  space physics.
- **The particle model** from thermal physics helps explain pressure
  and density from area 1.

Practical skills run through everything. Whether you take Paper 5 or
Paper 6, you need to be confident with measuring, recording results in
tables, plotting and interpreting graphs, and commenting on the
reliability of a method. Those skills are best built steadily through
class experiments rather than crammed at the end.

## Planning your study

The syllabus order is a sensible teaching order, because motion,
forces and energy supplies the vocabulary used everywhere else. In
general terms:

- **Early in the course:** physical quantities and measurement, then
  motion, forces, momentum, energy and pressure.
- **Middle of the course:** thermal physics and waves, followed by
  electricity and magnetism.
- **Later in the course:** nuclear physics and space physics, which
  draw on earlier ideas about energy, waves and forces.

Decide with your teacher early whether you are on the Core or Extended
route, since it determines which papers you will sit and which material
you need. Begin topic practice questions as each area is taught, and
start using past-paper questions on completed areas well before the
final term. Detailed, paper-by-paper preparation is best left to the
months leading up to your exam series.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between Core and Extended in IGCSE Physics 0625?**
Core candidates take Paper 1 and Paper 3 and can achieve grades C to G. Extended candidates take Paper 2 and Paper 4 and can achieve grades A* to G.

**Do I take both Paper 5 and Paper 6?**
No. You sit one of them: Paper 5 Practical Test (75 minutes) or Paper 6 Alternative to Practical (60 minutes). Each is worth 40 marks and 20%.

**How much is the theory paper worth?**
The theory paper, Paper 3 for Core or Paper 4 for Extended, is 75 minutes long, carries 80 marks and is worth 50% of the qualification.

**What topics does IGCSE Physics cover?**
Six areas: motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics.

**When are the exams?**
This syllabus is examined in the June and November series, for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028.
