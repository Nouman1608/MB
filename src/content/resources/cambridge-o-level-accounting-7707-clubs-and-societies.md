This guide goes deeper into section **5.5 Clubs and societies** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The syllabus lists "receipts and payments accounts and income and expenditure accounts", how to prepare receipts and payments accounts, "accounts for revenue-generating activities, e.g. refreshments, subscriptions", income and expenditure accounts and statements of financial position, the adjustments detailed in 5.1, and how to "define and calculate the accumulated fund".

The shared [clubs and societies study guide](/resources/igcse-accounting-clubs-and-societies/) introduces each account. This page follows one club through a complete year, from the opening accumulated fund to the closing statement of financial position, so you can see how every figure links. Use the [clubs and societies revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-clubs-and-societies-revision-notes/) for recall and the [clubs and societies practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-clubs-and-societies-practice/) to test yourself. All clubs and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Why clubs use different accounts

A club exists to provide an activity for its members, not to make a profit for an owner. It therefore has no capital account and no drawings. The members' stake is the **accumulated fund**: the club's total assets less its total liabilities. An excess of income over expenditure is a **surplus**, not a profit, and the reverse is a **deficit**.

| Receipts and payments account | Income and expenditure account |
|---|---|
| A summary of the cash book or bank account for the year | The club's version of a statement of profit or loss |
| Records cash actually received and paid | Records income earned and expenses incurred for the year |
| Includes capital items, such as new equipment | Revenue items only |
| Ignores accruals, prepayments and depreciation | Includes all 5.1 adjustments |
| Closing figure is the cash or bank balance | Closing figure is the surplus or deficit |

A receipts and payments account is easy to prepare but cannot show whether the club covered its running costs, because it mixes capital and revenue items and ignores amounts owing.

## Worked example: Larkfield Tennis Club

**Balances at 1 January 2028:** equipment (net book value) $8,400; bank $3,150; refreshment inventory $620; subscriptions in arrears $240; subscriptions in advance $180; owing to refreshment suppliers $410.

**Step 1: opening accumulated fund.** Assets = 8,400 + 3,150 + 620 + 240 = $12,410. Liabilities = 180 + 410 = $590. Accumulated fund = 12,410 - 590 = **$11,820**.

**Receipts in 2028:** subscriptions $9,360; refreshment sales $6,480; tournament entry fees $1,750. **Payments in 2028:** refreshment suppliers $3,940; court rent $4,800; tournament prizes and costs $1,120; coach's fees $2,400; new equipment $1,500; general expenses $610.

**Step 2: receipts and payments account.**

```
Larkfield Tennis Club
Receipts and payments account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Receipts                 USD    Payments                     USD
Balance b/d            3,150    Refreshment suppliers      3,940
Subscriptions          9,360    Court rent                 4,800
Refreshment sales      6,480    Tournament costs           1,120
Tournament fees        1,750    Coach's fees               2,400
                                New equipment              1,500
                                General expenses             610
                                Balance c/d                6,370
                      20,740                              20,740
```

**At 31 December 2028:** refreshment inventory $540; owing to refreshment suppliers $530; subscriptions in arrears $300; subscriptions in advance $260; court rent owing $400. Equipment, including the new purchase, is depreciated at 10% of its year-end book value before depreciation.

**Step 3: subscriptions for the year.** Cash received is not the same as income earned. Start with the cash and adjust for each opening and closing balance.

| Working | $ |
|---|---|
| Subscriptions received | 9,360 |
| Less arrears at the start (belong to 2027) | (240) |
| Add advance at the start (belong to 2028) | 180 |
| Add arrears at the end (2028 income not yet received) | 300 |
| Less advance at the end (belong to 2029) | (260) |
| Subscriptions income for 2028 | 9,340 |

The same answer comes from a subscriptions account. Its debit side holds the opening arrears ($240), the transfer to income and expenditure ($9,340) and the closing advance ($260), totalling $9,840. Its credit side holds the opening advance ($180), the bank receipts ($9,360) and the closing arrears ($300), also $9,840.

**Step 4: refreshments.** Purchases for the year = paid 3,940 - owing at start 410 + owing at end 530 = $4,060. Cost of refreshments sold = opening inventory 620 + purchases 4,060 - closing inventory 540 = $4,140. Profit on refreshments = 6,480 - 4,140 = **$2,340**. The tournament gives a surplus of 1,750 - 1,120 = $630.

**Step 5: income and expenditure account.**

```
Larkfield Tennis Club
Income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 December 2028
                                                USD        USD
Income
  Subscriptions                                          9,340
  Profit on refreshments                                 2,340
  Surplus on tournament                                    630
                                                        12,310
Less Expenditure
  Court rent (4,800 + 400)                    5,200
  Coach's fees                                2,400
  General expenses                              610
  Depreciation of equipment (9,900 x 10%)       990
                                                         9,200
Surplus of income over expenditure                       3,110
```

Only the profit on refreshments enters the account, not the sales and purchases separately. The $1,500 spent on equipment is capital expenditure, so it appears only through depreciation.

**Step 6: statement of financial position.**

```
Larkfield Tennis Club
Statement of financial position at 31 December 2028
                                                USD        USD
Non-current assets
  Equipment (8,400 + 1,500 - 990)                        8,910
Current assets
  Refreshment inventory                         540
  Subscriptions in arrears                      300
  Bank                                        6,370
                                                         7,210
Total assets                                            16,120
Accumulated fund
  Balance at 1 January 2028                  11,820
  Add Surplus for the year                    3,110
                                                        14,930
Current liabilities
  Owing to refreshment suppliers                530
  Subscriptions in advance                      260
  Court rent owing                              400
                                                         1,190
Total accumulated fund and liabilities                  16,120
```

The closing accumulated fund equals the opening fund plus the surplus, and the statement balances.

## How one change moves the figures

Suppose Larkfield's committee decides that the $300 of subscriptions in arrears at 31 December 2028 will never be collected. Subscriptions income falls from $9,340 to $9,040, so the surplus falls from $3,110 to $2,810. Current assets fall by $300 because the arrears are no longer an asset, and the closing accumulated fund falls to 11,820 + 2,810 = $14,630. The bank balance does not change, because no cash moved.

## Applying the 5.1 adjustments

- **Accruals and prepayments** adjust each expense, as with the court rent.
- **Depreciation** of equipment or premises is expenditure; the asset is shown at book value.
- **Subscriptions in arrears that will not be collected** are written off as irrecoverable, reducing subscriptions income, so they are not carried forward as an asset.
- **Subscriptions in arrears** are an other receivable; **subscriptions in advance** are an other payable.

## Common mistakes

- Including the purchase of equipment in the income and expenditure account.
- Copying the subscriptions received instead of adjusting for arrears and advances.
- Treating arrears at the start as this year's income: they were earned last year.
- Showing refreshment sales and purchases in full in the income and expenditure account.
- Forgetting the opening accumulated fund, or calling a surplus a profit.

## Exam technique

Paper 2 tasks on clubs are often split: **calculate** the opening accumulated fund, **prepare** a subscriptions account or refreshment account, then **prepare** the income and expenditure account. Set out each working separately and label it, as the steps above do. Use the club's own words, surplus, deficit and accumulated fund, because the question will. For an **explain** question on why a club treasurer should prepare an income and expenditure account, link it to what the receipts and payments account cannot show: whether the year's income covered the year's running costs.
