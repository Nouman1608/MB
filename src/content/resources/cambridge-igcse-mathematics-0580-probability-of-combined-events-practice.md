These questions cover subtopic 8.3, Probability of combined events, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label suit both tiers and are with replacement or independent. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use E8.3 content: events without replacement and the notation P(A ∩ B) and P(A ∪ B). Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Learn the methods first with the [combined events study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-probability-of-combined-events/) and the [combined events revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-probability-of-combined-events-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (non-calculator) Two fair six-sided dice are rolled and the scores are added. Find the probability that the total is 9. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A fair coin is tossed and a fair six-sided dice is rolled. Find the probability of a head and an even number. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Events A and B are independent, with P(A) = 0.6 and P(B) = 0.3. Find the probability that

**(a)** both A and B happen, **[1]**

**(b)** neither A nor B happens. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) In a group of 60 people, 14 own only a cat, 9 own a cat and a dog, 21 own only a dog and 16 own neither. One person is chosen at random. Find the probability that the person owns

**(a)** a cat and a dog, **[1]**

**(b)** a cat or a dog or both. **[1]**

**5.** (Extended) (non-calculator) P(A) = 0.45, P(B) = 0.3 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.6. Find P(A ∩ B). **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A fair spinner numbered 1 to 5 is spun twice. Find the probability of

**(a)** two fives, **[1]**

**(b)** at least one five. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (non-calculator) A bag contains 4 red and 5 blue counters. A counter is taken at random, its colour is noted and it is replaced. A second counter is then taken.

**(a)** Draw a tree diagram, with outcomes at the ends of the branches and probabilities beside them. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that both counters are blue. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the probability that the counters are different colours. **[2]**

**(d)** Find the probability that at least one counter is red. **[1]**

**8.** (non-calculator) Two fair six-sided dice are rolled and the difference between the scores is recorded (larger minus smaller, or 0 if equal).

**(a)** Draw a sample space diagram for the difference. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that the difference is 0. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the probability that the difference is 2. **[1]**

**(d)** Find the probability that the difference is 3 or more. **[1]**

**9.** (calculator) The probability that a bus is late on any day is 0.15, independently of other days. Find the probability that over Monday and Tuesday the bus is

**(a)** late on exactly one day, **[3]**

**(b)** late on both days. **[1]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A box contains 6 red, 3 green and 2 yellow pens. Two pens are taken at random without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that both pens are green. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that the pens are different colours. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) There are 2 winning tickets among 10 tickets in a box. Tickets are taken at random, one at a time without replacement, until a winning ticket is taken.

**(a)** Find the probability that the first winning ticket is the second ticket taken. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that more than two tickets must be taken. **[2]**

**12.** (calculator) The probability that an archer hits a target with each shot is 0.7, independently. She takes three shots. Find the probability that she hits the target

**(a)** with all three shots, **[1]**

**(b)** at least once, **[2]**

**(c)** exactly once. **[2]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) For events A and B, P(A) = 0.62, P(B) = 0.3 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.12. Find

**(a)** P(A ∪ B), **[1]**

**(b)** P(A′ ∩ B′), **[1]**

**(c)** P(A ∩ B′). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Totals of 9: (3, 6), (4, 5), (5, 4), (6, 3), so 4 of 36 outcomes [1]; **4/36 = 1/9** [1]

**2.** 1/2 × 1/2 [1] = **1/4** [1]

**3. (a)** 0.6 × 0.3 = **0.18** [1]

**(b)** 0.4 × 0.7 = **0.28** [1]

**4. (a)** **9/60 = 3/20** [1]

**(b)** 14 + 9 + 21 = 44; **44/60 = 11/15** [1]

**5.** P(A) + P(B) counts the overlap twice: 0.45 + 0.3 = 0.75 [1]; P(A ∩ B) = 0.75 − 0.6 = **0.15** [1]

**6. (a)** 1/5 × 1/5 = **1/25** [1]

**(b)** P(no fives) = 4/5 × 4/5 = 16/25 [1]; 1 − 16/25 = **9/25** [1]

**7. (a)** First branches R 4/9, B 5/9; second branches the same from each, R 4/9, B 5/9 [1]; outcomes RR, RB, BR, BB at the ends [1]

**(b)** 5/9 × 5/9 = **25/81** [1]

**(c)** RB + BR = 20/81 + 20/81 [1] = **40/81** [1]

**(d)** 1 − 25/81 = **56/81** [1]

*Examiner insight:* because the counter is replaced, the second-stage probabilities are the same as the first. Using 3/8 or 4/8 on the second branches is the without-replacement method and is wrong here.

**8. (a)** A 6 × 6 grid with the difference in each cell: 0 on the diagonal, then 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 moving away from it [1]

**(b)** 6/36 = **1/6** [1]

**(c)** 8/36 = **2/9** [1]

**(d)** Differences 3, 4 and 5: 6 + 4 + 2 = 12 cells; 12/36 = **1/3** [1]

**9. (a)** Late Monday only: 0.15 × 0.85 = 0.1275 [1]; late Tuesday only: 0.85 × 0.15 = 0.1275 [1]; total = **0.255** [1]

**(b)** 0.15 × 0.15 = **0.0225** [1]

**10. (a)** 3/11 × 2/10 [1] = 6/110 = **3/55** [1]

**(b)** P(same) = 6/11 × 5/10 + 3/11 × 2/10 + 2/11 × 1/10 = 30/110 + 6/110 + 2/110 = 38/110 [1]; P(different) = 1 − 38/110 [1] = 72/110 = **36/55** [1]

*Examiner insight:* finding "different" as 1 − "same" needs three paths instead of six. Listing all six different-colour paths also works but takes longer and invites slips.

**11. (a)** Not a winner, then a winner: 8/10 × 2/9 [1] = 16/90 = **8/45** [1]

**(b)** The first two are both not winners: 8/10 × 7/9 [1] = 56/90 = **28/45** [1]

**12. (a)** 0.7³ = **0.343** [1]

**(b)** P(no hits) = 0.3³ = 0.027 [1]; 1 − 0.027 = **0.973** [1]

**(c)** Three paths (HMM, MHM, MMH), each 0.7 × 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.063 [1]; 3 × 0.063 = **0.189** [1]

**13. (a)** A only = 0.5, B only = 0.18, overlap = 0.12; P(A ∪ B) = **0.8** [1]

**(b)** 1 − 0.8 = **0.2** [1]

**(c)** A but not B = **0.5** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding instead of multiplying along a path.
- Missing the second order (such as RB and BR).
- Using without-replacement fractions when the item is replaced, or the reverse.
- Treating sums or differences of two dice as equally likely values.
- Adding P(A) and P(B) for "A or B" when the events overlap.
