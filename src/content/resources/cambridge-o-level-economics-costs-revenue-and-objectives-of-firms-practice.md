## Questions

**1.** A small laundry is open all week. (a) Define fixed cost. **[1]** (b) State whether each cost is fixed or variable: rent of the shop; detergent; the manager's monthly salary; electricity used by the machines for each load. **[4]**

**2.** A firm has fixed cost of 2,000 and variable cost of 3,500 when it makes 250 units. Find TC, ATC, AFC and AVC. **[4]**

**3.** At 150 units a firm has average total cost of 14 and average variable cost of 9. Calculate its average fixed cost, fixed cost, variable cost and total cost. **[4]**

**4.** A lantern workshop has fixed cost of 900. Its variable cost is shown.

| Lanterns | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Variable cost | 500 | 860 | 1,080 | 1,480 | 2,100 |

**(a)** Calculate total cost, AFC, AVC and ATC at 80 lanterns. **[4]**
**(b)** State the output in the table with the lowest AVC and the output with the lowest ATC. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the lowest ATC is at a higher output than the lowest AVC. **[2]**

**5.** A firm has fixed cost of 300 and a variable cost of 5 for each unit made.

**(a)** Calculate total cost at outputs of 0 and 40. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the TC curve compares with the VC curve. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what happens to average total cost as output rises from 20 to 60. **[3]**

**6.** **(a)** Define total revenue. **[1]** **(b)** A firm sells 240 units at 15 each. Calculate its total revenue. **[1]** **(c)** Another firm receives 4,500 from selling 360 units. Calculate its average revenue. **[2]**

**7.** In a sale, a shop sells 200 mugs: 120 at 8.50 and 80 at 6.00.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate average revenue. **[1]**
**(c)** Compare average revenue with 8.50 and explain the difference. **[1]**

**8.** A parcel service records its deliveries at four prices.

| Price per delivery | 20 | 18 | 16 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deliveries | 100 | 115 | 140 | 150 |

**(a)** Work out TR at each of the four prices. **[2]**
**(b)** Which price brings in the most? **[1]**
**(c)** Use percentage changes to explain the drop in TR when the price goes from 16 to 14. **[3]**

**9.** A furniture maker has fixed cost of 1,000 and sells each chair for 60. Variable costs are in the table.

| Chairs | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Variable cost | 350 | 600 | 900 | 1,380 | 1,950 | 2,700 |

**(a)** Calculate total revenue and profit at 40 and at 50 chairs. **[4]**
**(b)** State the output in the table with the highest profit and explain why 60 chairs is worse. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that the firm breaks even at an output between 30 and 40 chairs. **[2]**

**10.** A village runs a bus service. Option A: fare 2.00, 3,000 passengers a month, total cost 8,400. Option B: fare 3.00, 2,200 passengers, total cost 7,900 because fewer buses run.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue and profit or loss for each option. **[4]**
**(b)** State which option is closer to profit maximisation and which is closer to social welfare. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which option the village should choose. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A cost that stays the same whatever the output [1].
**(b)** Rent: fixed [1]. Detergent: variable [1]. Manager's monthly salary: fixed [1]. Electricity per load: variable [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* give the word fixed or variable for each item, not a repeat of the item; a one-word label with a short reason is safest.

**2.** TC = 2,000 + 3,500 = **5,500** [1]. ATC = 5,500 ÷ 250 = **22** [1]. AFC = 2,000 ÷ 250 = **8** [1]. AVC = 3,500 ÷ 250 = **14** [1]. Check: 8 + 14 = 22. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* show each division on the page so the reader can see how you reached every figure.

**3.** AFC = 14 − 9 = **5** [1]. FC = 5 × 150 = **750** [1]. VC = 9 × 150 = **1,350** [1]. TC = 14 × 150 = **2,100** [1]. Check: 750 + 1,350 = 2,100. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* a one-line check such as FC + VC = TC shows you are testing your answer.

**4. (a)** TC = 900 + 1,480 = **2,380** [1]. AFC = 900 ÷ 80 = **11.25** [1]. AVC = 1,480 ÷ 80 = **18.50** [1]. ATC = 2,380 ÷ 80 = **29.75** [1].
**(b)** AVC is 25, 21.5, 18, 18.5, 21, so lowest at **60** lanterns [1]. ATC is 70, 44, 33, 29.75, 30, so lowest at **80** lanterns [1].
**(c)** Above 60 lanterns AVC rises, but only by 0.50 from 60 to 80 [1]. AFC still falls by 3.75 over the same range, so ATC keeps falling until 80 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* for part (c), quote the figures for both changes; a bare statement that AFC is falling gives only part of the reasoning.

**5. (a)** TC at 0 = **300** [1]. TC at 40 = 300 + 5 × 40 = **500** [1].
**(b)** TC is parallel to VC [1] and lies above it by 300, the fixed cost, at every output [1].
**(c)** ATC falls from 20 (400 ÷ 20) to 10 (600 ÷ 60) [1]. AVC stays at 5, so the whole fall comes from AFC [1], because the fixed cost of 300 is shared among more units, 15 each at 20 and 5 each at 60 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in a curve question, state where the curve starts as well as its direction.

**6. (a)** Receipts from all units sold, equal to price × quantity [1].
**(b)** 240 × 15 = **3,600** [1].
**(c)** AR = TR ÷ quantity = 4,500 ÷ 360 [1] = **12.50** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* write the formula before substituting; units such as money per unit make the answer complete.

**7. (a)** 120 × 8.50 = 1,020 and 80 × 6.00 = 480 [1]. TR = **1,500** [1].
**(b)** AR = 1,500 ÷ 200 = **7.50** [1].
**(c)** AR is 1.00 below 8.50 because 80 mugs were sold at the lower sale price [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* a comment that names the cause (the discount) earns the point; "it is lower" alone does not explain anything.

**8. (a)** 2,000 and 2,070 [1]; 2,240 and 2,100 [1].
**(b)** **16** [1].
**(c)** The price falls 12.5% (2 ÷ 16) [1]. Deliveries rise from 140 to 150, about 7.1% (10 ÷ 140) [1]. Sales rose by a smaller percentage than the price fell, so TR fell from 2,240 to 2,100 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* compare the two percentages directly; the conclusion follows from which is larger.

**9. (a)** At 40: TR = 60 × 40 = 2,400 [1]; TC = 1,000 + 1,380 = 2,380, so profit = **20** [1]. At 50: TR = 3,000 and TC = 1,000 + 1,950 = 2,950 [1], so profit = **50** [1].
**(b)** **50 chairs** [1]. At 60, TR = 3,600 but TC = 3,700, so there is a loss of 100 [1].
**(c)** At 30, TR = 1,800 and TC = 1,900, a loss of 100 [1]. At 40 there is a profit of 20, so profit moves from negative to positive between 30 and 40 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* in "show that", put the numbers for both sides of the break-even point on the page.

**10. (a)** A: TR = 2.00 × 3,000 = 6,000, and profit = 6,000 − 8,400 = **−2,400** [1][1]. B: TR = 3.00 × 2,200 = 6,600, and profit = 6,600 − 7,900 = **−1,300** [1][1].
**(b)** B is closer to profit maximisation because it has the smaller loss [1]. A is closer to social welfare because it has lower fares and 800 more passengers [1].
**(c)** A costs 1,100 more in losses than B [1]. Its benefit is that more villagers can travel, at lower fares [1]. But a loss of 2,400 each month needs a subsidy or donations, and if none exist the service may not survive [1]. A fair judgement: choose A if funding is secure, otherwise B or a fare between the two, because a service that closes helps nobody [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* an evaluation needs a conclusion that depends on something stated, here whether funding exists.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reporting TC when ATC was asked.
- Forgetting to add FC to VC to reach TC.
- Using the wrong output when a table has several columns.
- Calling AFC constant.
- Starting the TC curve at the origin.
- Skipping the formula and showing only a bare number.
- Naming an objective without pointing to evidence about the firm.
- Ending an evaluation with no conclusion or no condition.
