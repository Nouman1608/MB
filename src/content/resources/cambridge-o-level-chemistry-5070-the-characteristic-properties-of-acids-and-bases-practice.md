## Answers

**1.** H⁺ [1]; OH⁻ [1].

**2.** An acid is a proton donor [1]; a base is a proton acceptor [1].

**3.** Acidic: colourless [1]. Alkaline: blue [1].

**4.** Magnesium sulfate [1] + hydrogen [1].

**5.** **B** [1].

**6.** HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻ [1]; single arrow with (aq) on every species [1].

**7. (a)** CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(b)** 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** CaCO₃ + 2H⁺ → Ca²⁺ + H₂O + CO₂ [1].

**8.** HCl is the acid because it donates a proton (H⁺) [1]; NH₃ is the base because it accepts the proton, forming NH₄⁺ [1].

**9. (a)** 2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(b)** Hold damp red litmus paper in the gas [1]; it turns blue [1].

**10. (a)** P [1].

**(b)** S [1].

**(c)** Q [1].

**(d)** R [1].

**11.** Both fizz and give carbon dioxide, but hydrochloric acid fizzes faster [1]. Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid, completely dissociated, whereas ethanoic acid is only partially dissociated [1], so hydrochloric acid has a higher concentration of H⁺ ions, giving more frequent collisions with the marble [1].

**12.** CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq) [1]. The reversible arrow shows that dissociation is partial [1]: most of the ethanoic acid remains as molecules, in equilibrium with a small proportion of ions [1].

**13.** "Weak" describes the degree of dissociation: a weak acid is only partially dissociated [1]. "Dilute" describes concentration: a small amount of acid per dm³ of solution; a weak acid can be concentrated and a strong acid can be dilute [1].

**14.** K⁺ and NO₃⁻ [1]. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 4: writing "magnesium sulfate + water". Acid + metal gives hydrogen, not water.
- Question 5: choosing copper(II) oxide or zinc oxide. Both are bases but insoluble, so not alkalis.
- Question 6: using ⇌ for a strong acid.
- Question 7(b): unbalanced hydrogen. Six HCl give three H₂.
- Question 9(b): using blue litmus, which cannot turn blue.
- Question 10(c): choosing P. Ethanoic acid is weak, so at an ordinary concentration its pH is only moderately low.
- Question 11: saying ethanoic acid gives less gas. With excess marble and equal amounts of acid, the total is the same; only the rate differs.
- Question 13: repeating the definition of weak without explaining what dilute means.
- Question 14: including K⁺ or NO₃⁻ in the ionic equation. Spectator ions are left out.
- Question 8: saying NH₃ is the base "because it is an alkali". The question asks for the proton definition, so say which species accepts the proton.

## Checking your equations

Before moving on from any equation question, run three checks:

1. **Atoms:** count each element on both sides. In Question 7(b), 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂ has 2 Al, 6 H and 6 Cl on each side.
2. **Charges:** in an ionic equation the total charge must match. In Question 7(c), the left has 2+ (from 2H⁺) and the right has 2+ (from Ca²⁺).
3. **Arrows and states:** the arrow type is itself a marking point in dissociation questions. Use a single arrow for a strong acid's dissociation, ⇌ for a weak acid; (aq) for dissolved ions, (s) for solids, (l) for water and (g) for gases.
