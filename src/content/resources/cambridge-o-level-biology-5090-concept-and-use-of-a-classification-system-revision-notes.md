## Key terms

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Classification | Sorting organisms into groups according to the features they share |
| Species | A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring |
| Genus | A group of closely related species; the first part of a scientific name |
| Binomial system | An internationally agreed naming system; every species has a two-part scientific name (genus then species) |
| Dichotomous key | A key that divides a set of organisms in two at every step, using paired statements about observable features |
| Fertile | Able to produce offspring |
| Sterile | Unable to produce offspring |

## Why we classify

- Organises the enormous variety of living things so that it is easier to study.
- Makes identification easier: match an unknown organism to a group's features.
- Gives scientists in every country the same names for the same organisms.
- Helps prediction: a new organism placed in a group probably shares the group's other features.

Choose features that are **observable** and **consistent** within a group (for example presence of wings or fur). Avoid features that change, such as size or colour.

## The species definition

**A species is a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.**

- Mating on its own does not make two organisms the same species.
- Horse x donkey gives a mule. Mules are sterile, so horse and donkey are **different species**.
- Different breeds of dog interbreed and their offspring are fertile, so they are **one species**.
- Differences inside a species (size, colour, coat) do not create new species.

## Binomial names

1. Two parts: **genus** first, **species** second.
2. Genus begins with a **capital**; species begins with a **lower-case** letter.
3. *Italics* in print; **underline each word separately** in handwriting.
4. Internationally agreed, so every biologist uses the same name whatever their language.

| Common name | Scientific name |
|---|---|
| Human | *Homo sapiens* |
| Lion | *Panthera leo* |
| Tiger | *Panthera tigris* |
| Domestic cat | *Felis catus* |

Lion and tiger share a genus, so they are more closely related than lion and cat.

## Using a dichotomous key

1. Start at step 1 and read **both** statements before choosing.
2. Follow the number, or the name, that goes with the true statement.
3. Continue until you reach a name.
4. Write down the **path** (for example 1b, 4a) if asked to show your working.

## Constructing a dichotomous key

1. List the differences between the organisms.
2. Choose a feature that splits the group into two, ideally equal, parts.
3. Write it as **two opposite statements** and give each its next step or its answer.
4. Repeat on each part until every organism is on its own.
5. Test the key on every organism.

A key is good when it uses visible, fixed features, when each pair is a true opposite, and when every organism ends at one name only.

## Worked key (five animals)

| Step | Statement | Go to / answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | Legs present | 2 |
| 1b | Legs absent | 4 |
| 2a | Six legs | housefly |
| 2b | More than six legs | 3 |
| 3a | Eight legs | spider |
| 3b | More than eight legs | centipede |
| 4a | Shell present | snail |
| 4b | No shell | earthworm |

Path for a spider: 1a, 2b, 3a. Always check that each organism ends at one name and that no organism fits both statements of a pair.

## Short model answers

**Define the term species.** A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.

**Explain why a horse and a donkey are classed as different species although they can breed.** Their offspring, the mule, is sterile, so they do not produce fertile offspring.

**Describe the binomial system.** Each species has a two-part scientific name, the genus followed by the species. The genus has a capital letter and the species a lower-case letter. The system is agreed internationally, so the same name is used all over the world.

**Suggest why size is not a good feature for a key.** Size varies between young and adult individuals and between individuals of the same species, so the same organism could fit either statement.

## Exam traps

- "Species" needs the word **fertile**.
- Genus capital, species lower case, both italic or underlined.
- Never use size, colour or "looks like" in a key.
- Both statements in a pair must be opposites.
- Show the path through a key if the question says "show your working".
- A binomial name is a scientific name, not a common name.

## Self-test

1. What is meant by classification?
2. Give two reasons why classifying organisms is useful.
3. Define a species.
4. Why are a horse and a donkey different species?
5. What are the two parts of a binomial name called?
6. Write the scientific name of humans correctly.
7. Why do scientists use an internationally agreed system of names?
8. What does "dichotomous" mean?
9. Give one feature that suits a key and one that does not.
10. A key has the pair "large leaves" and "small leaves". Give a reason why this is a weak pair.
11. Two dog breeds produce fertile puppies. Are they the same species? Explain.
12. Lion is *Panthera leo* and tiger is *Panthera tigris*. What does this show?

### Answers

1. Sorting organisms into groups according to the features they share.
2. Any two of: easier to study the variety of life; easier to identify organisms; scientists share names; helps predict features of a newly found organism.
3. A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
4. They can breed, but the mule is sterile, so they do not produce fertile offspring.
5. Genus and species.
6. *Homo sapiens* (genus with capital, species lower case, italic or each word underlined).
7. So that every scientist uses the same name for an organism, whatever language they speak.
8. Divided into two.
9. Suits: presence of wings (observable and fixed). Does not suit: size or colour, which vary.
10. "Large" and "small" are relative and vary with age, so they are not clear opposites that always give the same answer.
11. Yes, because they breed and the offspring are fertile.
12. They are different species of the same genus, so they are closely related.
