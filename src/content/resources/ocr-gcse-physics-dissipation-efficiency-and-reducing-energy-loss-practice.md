## Questions

**1.** Describe, with an example, what is meant by energy being dissipated. **[3]**

**2.** A battery-powered cordless drill is used to make a hole in a wall. Describe the energy transfers from the battery to the useful store, and give two ways energy is wasted. **[4]**

**3.** A hair dryer is plugged into the a.c. mains. It contains a heating element and a small fan motor. Describe how energy is transferred, and state which stores are useful and which are wasted. **[4]**

**4.** A toaster has a power rating of 1.2 kW. It is switched on for 4.5 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred, in joules. **[3]**

**5.** A 2.0 kW heater and a 0.8 kW heater are each switched on for 30 minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred by each heater, in kW h. **[2]**
**(b)** A friend says the 2.0 kW heater must be the more efficient one. Explain why the power rating does not show this. **[2]**

**6.** An electric motor takes 540 J from its supply. It gives out 378 J of useful kinetic energy.
**(a)** Show that the efficiency of the motor is 70%. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much energy is wasted. **[1]**

**7.** An LED lamp has an input power of 8.0 W and an efficiency of 0.45. It is on for 10 minutes. Calculate the useful light energy and the wasted energy. **[4]**

**8.** Lamp A takes 60 J each second and gives out 3 J of light each second. Lamp B takes 11 J each second and gives out 2.2 J of light each second.
**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of each lamp. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wasted energy each second for each lamp. **[2]**
**(c)** Which lamp is better to use? Give a reason. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain how oil on the axle of a wheel increases the efficiency of a machine. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how loft insulation reduces the energy needed to heat a house. **[3]**

**10.** Two huts are built to the same shape. Hut X has thin metal walls. Hut Y has thick brick walls. Both start at the same temperature on a cold day.
**(a)** Describe how the thickness of the walls affects the rate of cooling. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the thermal conductivity of the wall material affects the rate of cooling. **[2]**
**(c)** A class tests this with two copper cans of hot water, one wrapped in insulation. State two things they should keep the same. **[2]**

**11.** A battery-powered winch lifts a 9.0 kg load through 3.2 m in 25 s. The battery supplies 420 J. Take g = 9.8 N/kg.
**(a)** Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy of the load. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of the winch. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the average input power. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest two changes that would increase the efficiency of the winch. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Energy spreads out to the surroundings [1]. It is stored in less useful ways, mainly the thermal store of the surroundings [1]. Example: friction in a bicycle chain warms the chain and the air [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say energy is "lost" or "used up" tend to lose credit. Say where it goes.

**2.** The battery's chemical store transfers energy electrically through the circuit [1]. The motor transfers it to the kinetic store of the drill bit [1]. Wasted: thermal energy in the wires and motor windings because of resistance [1], and thermal energy and sound from friction in bearings and gears [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer names a store or a mechanism for each wasted transfer. Writing only "heat" and "sound" is thinner.

**3.** The a.c. mains transfers energy electrically to the device [1]. The element transfers it to the thermal store of the air, which is useful [1]. The motor transfers it to the kinetic store of the moving air, which is useful [1]. Wasted: thermal energy in the casing and wires, and sound from the fan and vibration [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate the two parts of the device. Marks are easier to earn when each component has its own useful and wasted store.

**4.** 1.2 kW = 1200 W [1]. 4.5 minutes = 270 s [1]. E = P t = 1200 × 270 = **324 000 J** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert both quantities to matching units before multiplying. A wrong or missing unit on the answer is an easy way to lose credit.

**5. (a)** 2.0 kW × 0.5 h = **1.0 kW h** [1]. 0.8 kW × 0.5 h = **0.4 kW h** [1].
**(b)** The power rating tells you how much energy is transferred each second [1]. Efficiency is useful energy divided by input, and the rating gives no information about how much is useful [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming both definitions, rate of transfer and fraction useful, is a clear way to show the difference.

**6. (a)** Efficiency = useful output / input = 378 / 540 [1] = 0.70, which is 70% [1].
**(b)** Wasted = 540 − 378 = **162 J** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing useful by input is the safe order. Showing the substituted fraction gives you something to be credited for if the arithmetic slips.

**7.** Input energy = P t = 8.0 × 600 = 4800 J [1]. Useful = 0.45 × 4800 [1] = **2160 J** [1]. Wasted = 4800 − 2160 = **2640 J** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Ten minutes must become 600 s. A forgotten conversion is a common reason for a wrong answer here.

**8. (a)** A: 3 / 60 = **0.05 (5%)** [1]. B: 2.2 / 11 = **0.20 (20%)** [1].
**(b)** A: 60 − 3 = **57 J** each second [1]. B: 11 − 2.2 = **8.8 J** each second [1].
**(c)** Lamp B, because it is more efficient and wastes far less energy for a similar amount of light [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers you have worked out in the final comparison, rather than a general statement.

**9. (a)** The oil forms a layer between the surfaces [1]. This reduces friction, so less work is done against friction [1]. Less energy is stored thermally, so more of the input goes into useful motion [1].
**(b)** Loft insulation is a layer of material that traps air [1]. Trapped air is a poor conductor and cannot circulate, so conduction and convection are reduced [1]. The house loses energy more slowly, so the heating needs less energy to keep the temperature up [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Insulation keeps heat in" is too vague. Explain the mechanism and link it to the rate of energy transfer.

**10. (a)** Thicker walls cool more slowly [1], because energy has a longer path to travel through the wall [1].
**(b)** A material with high thermal conductivity, like metal, transfers energy faster, so the building cools faster [1]. A material with low thermal conductivity, like brick, slows the cooling [1].
**(c)** Same volume of water [1] and same starting temperature [1]. The same lid or the same room would also be accepted as sensible controls. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "rate of cooling" and compare the huts directly. Answers that only say "Y is warmer" tend to leave out the reason.

**11. (a)** GPE = m g h = 9.0 × 9.8 × 3.2 [1] = **282 J** (282.24 J) [1].
**(b)** Efficiency = 282.24 / 420 [1] = 0.672, so **0.67 or 67%** [1].
**(c)** Power = E / t = 420 / 25 [1] = **16.8 W** [1].
**(d)** Lubricate the moving parts [1] and use thicker wires with lower resistance [1]. Other sensible changes, such as reducing friction in the gears, would also be reasonable. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula and substitution before the answer. Quote the result to 2 significant figures, as the data given support.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that energy is "lost" or "used up", not dissipated or stored thermally.
- Treating a high power rating as a sign of high efficiency.
- Dividing the wasted energy by the input instead of the useful energy.
- Leaving minutes or hours unconverted when using E = P t with watts.
- Giving an efficiency above 100% or forgetting to convert a decimal to a percentage when asked.
- Describing insulation as stopping energy transfer, when it only slows it.
- Answering a cooling question without mentioning rate, thickness or conductivity.
- Skipping the controlled variables in a fair test.
