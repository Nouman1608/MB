## The definition to learn exactly

**Active transport is the movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration (i.e. against a concentration gradient), using energy from respiration.**

Three things every answer needs: **through a cell membrane**, **lower → higher (against the gradient)**, **energy from respiration**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration gradient | A difference in concentration between two regions |
| Against the gradient | From lower to higher concentration |
| Respiration | Chemical reactions in cells that release energy from nutrient molecules |
| Mitochondria | Where aerobic respiration releases energy |
| Mineral ions | Ions such as nitrate and magnesium that plants absorb from the soil |
| Protein carrier (Extended) | A protein in the membrane that moves specific molecules or ions across |
| Respiratory poison | A substance, such as cyanide, that stops respiration |

## Three processes side by side

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moves | Particles | Water | Particles (ions, glucose) |
| Direction | High → low concentration | Dilute → concentrated | **Low → high** |
| Energy from respiration | No | No | **Yes** |

**Spot the process:** if a substance builds up to a **higher** concentration inside the cell than outside, it is active transport.

## Evidence for energy from respiration

| Change | Effect on active transport | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Remove oxygen | Much slower | Less aerobic respiration, less energy |
| Lower temperature | Slower | Respiration enzymes slower |
| Add a respiratory poison | Almost stops | No respiration, no energy |
| Kill the cells | Stops | Dead cells do not respire |

Diffusion would not be slowed by a lack of oxygen.

## Calculation patterns

- **Concentration ratio:** inside 60 units, outside 4 units → 60 ÷ 4 = **15 times** higher inside.
- **Comparing uptake:** 48 units with oxygen, 8 without → 48 ÷ 8 = **6 times** more with oxygen.
- **Rate:** 36 units in 4 hours → 36 ÷ 4 = **9 units per hour**.

## Extended: importance

- Cells can take up substances when the outside concentration is **lower** than inside.
- **Root hair cells:** absorb mineral ions (such as nitrate and magnesium) from soil water, where the ion concentration is lower than in the cells.
- Uses: nitrate for amino acids and proteins; magnesium for chlorophyll.
- Roots need **oxygen** from soil air spaces for respiration; waterlogged soil reduces ion uptake.
- **Small intestine:** cells of the villi absorb glucose actively.
- Cells doing a lot of active transport have **many mitochondria**.

## Extended: protein carriers

**Protein carriers move molecules or ions across a membrane during active transport.**

1. Particle binds to a specific carrier in the membrane.
2. Energy from respiration changes the carrier's shape.
3. Particle released on the other side, at the higher concentration.
4. Carrier returns to its original shape.

## Exam traps

- **Against** the gradient, not down it.
- Energy from **respiration**, not "from the sun" or "from food".
- Water never moves by active transport; it moves by osmosis.
- (Extended) Ion uptake by root hairs is active transport, not diffusion.
- (Extended) Name **protein carriers**.
- Quote data with units and compare both conditions.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the definition of active transport.
2. State the source of energy for active transport.
3. How does the direction of active transport differ from diffusion?
4. A cell contains 30 units of an ion; the solution outside contains 2 units. The ion keeps entering. Name the process and explain.
5. Calculate how many times more concentrated the ion is inside the cell in question 4.
6. Why does removing oxygen reduce active transport?
7. Why does cyanide stop active transport?
8. Name the process by which water enters a root hair cell.
9. (Extended) Why must root hair cells use active transport to absorb mineral ions?
10. (Extended) Give one use of nitrate ions in a plant.
11. (Extended) State what moves ions across the membrane during active transport.
12. (Extended) Why do cells carrying out much active transport have many mitochondria?

### Answers

1. The movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration (against a concentration gradient), using energy from respiration.
2. Respiration.
3. Active transport moves particles from lower to higher concentration; diffusion from higher to lower.
4. Active transport, because the ion moves from a lower concentration outside to a higher concentration inside.
5. 30 ÷ 2 = **15 times**.
6. Less aerobic respiration takes place, so less energy is released for active transport.
7. It stops respiration, so no energy is released.
8. Osmosis.
9. The concentration of ions in the soil water is usually lower than inside the cells, so diffusion would not bring them in.
10. To make amino acids (and so proteins).
11. Protein carriers.
12. Mitochondria release energy by aerobic respiration, which active transport needs.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is any of active transport Core?**
Yes. The definition (outcome 3.3.1) is Core; outcomes 3.3.2 and 3.3.3 are Extended only.

**What is the easiest way to tell active transport from diffusion in a question?**
Check the direction: movement from a lower to a higher concentration, or movement that stops without oxygen, is active transport.
