These questions cover section 5.3, Circles, arcs and sectors, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [circles, arcs and sectors study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors/), and the [circles, arcs and sectors revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors-revision-notes/) list the formulas. Use the π key on your calculator. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** A circle has radius 6 cm. Find its circumference and its area, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**2.** A circle has diameter 14 cm. Find its circumference and its area, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**3.** The circumference of a circle is 18π cm. Find its radius, and its area in terms of π. **[3]**

**4.** A circle has area 100 cm². Find its radius. **[2]**

**5.** A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 40°. Find its arc length and its area. **[3]**

**6.** A major sector has radius 4 cm and angle 300°. Find its arc length and its area. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A sector of a circle with radius 12 cm has area 24π cm². Find the angle of the sector. **[3]**

**8.** An arc of length 8π cm is part of a circle of radius 18 cm. Find the angle at the centre. **[3]**

**9.** A semicircle has diameter 12 cm. Find its perimeter and its area. **[4]**

**10.** A 400 m running track has two straight sections, each 120 m long, and two semicircular ends. Find the radius of the semicircles. **[3]**

**11.** A wheel has radius 30 cm.

**(a)** Find the distance the wheel moves in one complete turn.

**(b)** Find the number of complete turns the wheel makes to travel 1 km. **[3]**

**12.** A circular lawn has radius 6 m and is surrounded by a path of width 4 m. Find the area of the path, giving your answer in terms of π and to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**13.** A quarter circle has radius 8 cm. Find its perimeter and its area. **[4]**

**14.** A pie chart is a circle of radius 12 cm. One sector represents 25% of the data. Find the angle of the sector and its area, in terms of π. **[3]**

**15.** A sector of a circle with radius 10 cm has a perimeter of 40 cm. Find the angle of the sector, to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**16.** Two sectors are cut from the same circle. Their angles are 50° and 150°. The smaller sector has area 20 cm². Find the ratio of their arc lengths and the area of the larger sector. **[3]**

**17.** A circular table has a circumference of 3.77 m. Find its diameter to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C = 2π × 6 = 12π = **37.7 cm** [1]. A = π × 6² = 36π = **113 cm²** [1].

**2.** Radius = 7 cm. C = π × 14 = **44.0 cm** [1]. A = π × 49 = **154 cm²** [1].

**3.** 2πr = 18π [1], so **r = 9 cm** [1]. Area = π × 9² = **81π cm²** [1].

**4.** πr² = 100 [1], so r² = 31.83 and **r = 5.64 cm** [1].

**5.** Fraction = 40/360 = 1/9. Arc = 1/9 × 2π × 9 = 2π = **6.28 cm** [1][1]. Area = 1/9 × π × 81 = 9π = **28.3 cm²** [1].

**6.** Fraction = 300/360 = 5/6. Arc = 5/6 × 2π × 4 = 20π/3 = **20.9 cm** [1][1]. Area = 5/6 × π × 16 = 40π/3 = **41.9 cm²** [1].

**7.** 24π = θ/360 × π × 144 [1]. 24 = 0.4θ [1], so **θ = 60°** [1].

**8.** 8π = θ/360 × 2π × 18 [1]. 8 = θ/10 [1], so **θ = 80°** [1].

**9.** Radius 6. Curved part = 1/2 × 2π × 6 = 6π [1]. Perimeter = 6π + 12 = **30.8 cm** [1][1]. Area = 1/2 × π × 36 = 18π = **56.5 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The perimeter of a semicircle includes the diameter.

**10.** The two semicircles together have circumference 2πr [1]. 240 + 2πr = 400, so 2πr = 160 [1]. r = 160 ÷ 2π = **25.5 m** [1].

**11. (a)** C = 2π × 30 = 60π = **188 cm** [1]. **(b)** 1 km = 100 000 cm [1]. 100 000 ÷ 188.5 = 530.5, so **530 complete turns** [1].

**12.** Outer radius = 10 m [1]. Area = π × 10² − π × 6² = 100π − 36π [1] = **64π m²** = **201 m²** [1].

**13.** Arc = 1/4 × 2π × 8 = 4π [1]. Perimeter = 4π + 16 = **28.6 cm** [1][1]. Area = 1/4 × π × 64 = 16π = **50.3 cm²** [1].

**14.** Angle = 0.25 × 360 = **90°** [1]. Area = 1/4 × π × 144 = **36π cm²** [1][1].

**15.** Arc length = 40 − 2 × 10 = 20 cm [1]. 20 = θ/360 × 2π × 10 [1]. θ = 20 × 360 ÷ 20π [1] = 114.59, so **114.6°** [1].

**16.** The ratio of angles is 50 : 150 = 1 : 3, so the ratio of arc lengths is **1 : 3** [1][1]. The areas are in the same ratio, so the larger area is 3 × 20 = **60 cm²** [1].

**17.** d = 3.77 ÷ π [1] = 1.2, so the diameter is **1.2 m** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter in πr², or the radius in πd.
- Leaving out the two radii in the perimeter of a sector, or the diameter in a semicircle.
- Writing θ/360 upside down.
- Rounding π to 3.14 or rounding in the middle of the working.
- Subtracting radii instead of areas for a ring.
