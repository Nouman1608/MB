Condensed notes for section 4.6 (Location decisions) of the Cambridge IGCSE Business 0264 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029, content statement 4.6.1. A cement-works transport example, a pharmacy revenue example and a model answer are in the [location decisions study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-location-decisions/). Test yourself with the [location decisions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-location-decisions-practice/). The combined [quality and location notes](/resources/igcse-business-0264-quality-of-goods-and-services-and-location-decisions-revision-notes/) also cover section 4.5.

All examples are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Syllabus points

1. Factors influencing the location of a manufacturing business.
2. Factors influencing the location of a service business.
3. Factors to consider when choosing which country to locate in.
4. Recommend and justify a location in a given situation.

## Manufacturing factors

| Factor | Most important when... |
|---|---|
| Raw materials | Inputs are heavy or bulky and the product weighs less than its inputs (cement, steel, sugar refining) |
| Market | The product is bulky, fragile or perishable (fresh bread, large tanks, furniture) |
| Labour | Specific skills are needed, or labour is a large share of costs |
| Land and premises | A large site is needed; land costs differ greatly between areas |
| Transport links | Large volumes move in and out: ports, rail, main roads |
| Power and water | Production uses a lot of energy or water |
| Government | Grants or tax help in some regions; planning rules elsewhere |
| Room to expand | Growth is expected |

**Rule of thumb:** locate near whichever is more expensive to move, the inputs or the output.

## Service factors

| Factor | Most important when... |
|---|---|
| Nearness to customers | Customers must visit (shops, salons, dentists) |
| Footfall and visibility | Sales depend on passing trade |
| Rent | Margins are small; busy areas cost more |
| Competitors | Can split sales, or attract shoppers who come to compare |
| Parking and public transport | Customers drive or travel to the service |
| Skilled staff | Quality depends on trained people (clinics, law firms) |
| Communications | Service is delivered by phone or internet (call centres, online banks) |

Online and phone-based services can choose low-cost locations because customers never visit.

## Choosing a country

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Wages and other costs | Lower costs can mean lower prices or higher profit |
| Access to markets | Producing inside a market avoids its import tariffs and quotas and cuts transport time |
| Political stability | Unstable governments may change taxes or rules suddenly |
| Laws | Employment, safety and environmental laws affect costs |
| Government incentives | Grants or tax breaks for foreign firms |
| Infrastructure | Reliable ports, roads, power and internet |
| Skills and language | Workers must be able to do the job; managers must communicate |
| Exchange rates | Changes affect costs and the value of profits sent home |
| Culture | Customer tastes and ways of doing business may differ |

## Calculations you may meet

**Transport cost** = tonnes x distance (km) x cost per tonne per km.

Worked reminder: 20,000 tonnes moved 50 km at $0.12 per tonne per km.

```
20,000 x 50 x 0.12 = $120,000
```

**Revenue at a site** = customers per day x average spend x days open.

Worked reminder: 90 customers x $20 x 310 days.

```
90 x 20 x 310 = $558,000
```

**Total cost at a site** = add rent, wages, transport and other costs given.

## Recommend and justify: four steps

1. Find the deciding factor for this business.
2. Compare the options on it, with figures where given.
3. Check one factor that could change the answer (skills, access, risk).
4. Recommend, give the deciding reason and one risk.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Location decision | Choosing where a business will operate |
| Footfall | The number of people passing a site |
| Infrastructure | Transport, power, water and communication networks |
| Political stability | How likely a country's government and laws are to stay settled |
| Import tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Import quota | A limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported |
| Government incentive | Help such as a grant or lower tax to attract businesses |

## Exam traps

- The cheapest site is not always best: check revenue, skills and access.
- Not every service needs customers nearby; online services do not.
- Low wages alone do not justify moving abroad; productivity, skills and risk matter.
- A list of factors scores little; pick the ones that matter for *this* business.
- When calculating, keep units and the time period consistent (per year, per month).

## Quick self-test

1. State three factors that influence the location of a manufacturer.
2. Why should a cement works usually locate near its raw materials?
3. Why should a bakery supplying fresh bread locate near its market?
4. State three factors that influence the location of a service business.
5. Why might a new jeweller choose a street with several other jewellers?
6. Why can an online bank locate where rent is low?
7. Give two factors a business might consider when choosing a country.
8. Explain how an import tariff could encourage a business to locate inside a foreign market.
9. Calculate the transport cost of moving 8,000 tonnes 120 km at $0.05 per tonne per km.
10. A shop expects 150 customers a day spending $12 on average and opens 300 days. Calculate yearly revenue.
11. Define footfall.
12. Give one risk of locating in a politically unstable country.

### Answers

1. Any three: raw materials; market; labour; land costs; transport links; power and water; government help; room to expand.
2. Its inputs are heavier than its output, so moving the finished cement is cheaper than moving the limestone.
3. Bread is perishable and must reach shops fresh and early.
4. Any three: nearness to customers; footfall; visibility; rent; competitors; parking and transport; skilled staff; communications.
5. Customers go to that street to compare jewellers, so the new shop gains from the crowd.
6. Customers never visit; the service is delivered online, so it needs reliable communications, not a busy site.
7. Any two: costs; access to markets; political stability; laws; incentives; infrastructure; skills and language; exchange rates; culture.
8. Goods made inside the market avoid the tariff, so they can be sold at a lower price than imports.
9. 8,000 x 120 x 0.05 = $48,000.
10. 150 x 12 x 300 = $540,000.
11. The number of people passing a site.
12. Any one: sudden tax or law changes; unrest disrupting production; risk of the government taking over foreign-owned property.
