These notes cover subtopic **3.3 Linear momentum and its conservation** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## The principle (learn the wording)

> The total momentum of a system of interacting objects remains constant, provided no resultant external force acts on the system.

**Why it holds:** the forces between colliding objects are a Newton's third-law pair, equal and opposite and acting for the same time, so the changes in momentum are equal and opposite and cancel.

## Equations

```
one dimension:   m1 u1 + m2 u2 = m1 v1 + m2 v2        (signs for direction)
stuck together:  m1 u1 + m2 u2 = (m1 + m2) v
from rest:       0 = m1 v1 + m2 v2                    (velocities opposite)
two dimensions:  conserve x-momentum and y-momentum separately
elastic only:    u1 - u2 = v2 - v1                    (approach speed = separation speed)
```

## Collision types

| Type | Momentum | Kinetic energy | Separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elastic | Conserved | Conserved | Relative speed of separation = relative speed of approach |
| Inelastic | Conserved | Decreases | Separation speed less than approach speed |
| Perfectly inelastic (stick together) | Conserved | Largest possible decrease | Zero separation speed |
| Explosion or recoil from rest | Conserved (stays zero) | Increases (from stored energy) | Objects fly apart |

Kinetic energy "lost" goes to internal energy (heating), sound and permanent deformation. The syllabus does not require the coefficient of restitution.

## Solving checklist

1. Sketch before and after, with arrows and values.
2. Choose and state a positive direction.
3. Write momentum before = momentum after, with signs.
4. For a second unknown in an **elastic** collision, use the relative-speed equation, not kinetic energy.
5. Check: does the answer make physical sense (no object passes through another)?
6. If asked, compare total kinetic energy before and after.

## Recall examples

- **Stick together:** 0.25 kg at 3.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 0.50 kg; v = 0.75 ÷ 0.75 = 1.0 m s⁻¹. KE falls from 1.125 J to 0.375 J.
- **Elastic, light hits heavy:** 1.0 kg at 5.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 4.0 kg elastically; the 1.0 kg rebounds at 3.0 m s⁻¹ and the 4.0 kg moves off at 2.0 m s⁻¹. Check: 5.0 = −3.0 + 4(2.0); KE 12.5 J before and after.
- **Recoil:** a 1.5 kg launcher on wheels fires a 0.050 kg ball at 12 m s⁻¹; recoil speed = 0.60 ÷ 1.5 = 0.40 m s⁻¹.
- **2D, equal masses, one at rest, elastic:** the two move off at 90° to each other.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| System | The group of objects whose total momentum is considered |
| Closed (isolated) system | No resultant external force acts on it |
| Internal force | Force between objects inside the system |
| External force | Force from outside the system, such as friction from the ground |
| Relative speed of approach | Rate at which the separation decreases before the collision |
| Relative speed of separation | Rate at which the separation increases after the collision |

## Exam traps

- Leaving out the condition "no resultant external force".
- Forgetting the negative sign for an object moving the other way.
- Using kinetic energy conservation for a collision not stated to be elastic.
- Using the relative-speed rule for an inelastic collision.
- Claiming momentum is lost in an inelastic collision.
- Adding magnitudes, rather than components, in two-dimensional problems.
- Saying kinetic energy is conserved in an explosion: it increases.

## Self-test

1. State the principle of conservation of momentum.
2. Explain, using Newton's third law, why momentum is conserved in a collision.
3. What is conserved in an elastic collision that is not conserved in an inelastic one?
4. A 2.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley; afterwards the 2.0 kg trolley moves at 1.5 m s⁻¹ in the same direction. Find the velocity of the 1.0 kg trolley.
5. Is the collision in question 4 elastic? Show how you know.
6. A stationary 2.4 kg object explodes into 1.0 kg and 1.4 kg pieces. The 1.0 kg piece moves at 21 m s⁻¹. Find the speed of the other piece.
7. Where does the kinetic energy in question 6 come from?
8. A 1500 kg car at 12 m s⁻¹ runs into the back of a 1000 kg car moving at 6.0 m s⁻¹ in the same direction. They lock together. Find their common velocity.
9. Why can friction from the road be ignored during the collision in question 8?
10. How is momentum conserved in a two-dimensional collision?
11. In an elastic collision, the relative speed of approach is 7.0 m s⁻¹. What is the relative speed of separation?
12. Name two forms of energy into which kinetic energy is transferred in an inelastic collision.

### Answers

1. The total momentum of a system remains constant provided no resultant external force acts on it.
2. The objects exert equal and opposite forces on each other for the same time, so their momentum changes are equal and opposite and the total is unchanged.
3. Total kinetic energy.
4. 6.0 = 3.0 + 1.0v, so v = 3.0 m s⁻¹ forwards.
5. KE before = 9.0 J; after = 2.25 + 4.5 = 6.75 J, so it is inelastic. (Also: separation speed 1.5 m s⁻¹ is less than approach speed 3.0 m s⁻¹.)
6. 1.0 × 21 = 1.4v, so v = 15 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction.
7. From chemical energy stored in the explosive.
8. (18 000 + 6000) ÷ 2500 = 9.6 m s⁻¹ forwards.
9. The collision is very brief, so the change in momentum caused by friction during it is negligible compared with the momentum of the cars.
10. Separately in each of two perpendicular directions.
11. 7.0 m s⁻¹.
12. Any two: internal (thermal) energy, sound, energy of permanent deformation.
