## 8.1 Three neurones

| Neurone | Carries impulses | Cell body | Fibre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Receptor to CNS | Off to one side, outside the cord | Long |
| Relay | Inside the CNS | In the CNS | Short |
| Motor | CNS to effector | In the CNS, with dendrites | Long axon with terminals |

- **Schwann cell:** wraps its membrane round an axon many times.
- **Myelin sheath:** the lipid-rich layers formed. An electrical insulator.
- **Node of Ranvier:** the gap between Schwann cells where the axon membrane is exposed.

### Must-know distinctions

- Sensory (towards CNS) versus motor (away from CNS).
- Axon (carries impulses away from the cell body) versus dendrite (short, receives).
- Myelin (the sheath) versus node (the gap).

## 8.2 The response chain

Stimulus, receptor, sensory neurone, coordinator (CNS), motor neurone, effector, response.

- Receptor: converts stimulus energy into an electrical change.
- Effector: a muscle (contracts) or a gland (secretes).
- Synapses sit between neurones and between motor neurone and effector.

## 8.3 Spinal reflex arc

| Part | Where | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| Grey matter | Central, H-shaped | Cell bodies of relay and motor neurones, synapses |
| White matter | Around the grey | Myelinated axons running up and down |
| Dorsal root | Back | Sensory neurones entering; swelling holds cell bodies |
| Ventral root | Front | Motor neurones leaving |

Reflex arc in steps:

1. Receptor stimulated.
2. Sensory neurone to the cord by the dorsal root.
3. Synapse with a relay neurone in the grey matter.
4. Synapse with a motor neurone.
5. Motor neurone leaves by the ventral root.
6. Effector responds.

Why reflexes are quick: short pathway, few synapses, no conscious thought. They protect the body.

## 8.4 Action potential

| Quantity | Value to quote |
|---|---|
| Resting potential | About −70 mV |
| Threshold | About −55 mV |
| Peak | About +40 mV |
| Pump | 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, uses ATP |

### Method in steps

1. A stimulus opens a few sodium channels (voltage-gated) and Na⁺ moves in by diffusion.
2. At threshold many more open (positive feedback), giving rapid depolarisation.
3. At the peak the sodium channels shut.
4. Voltage-gated potassium channels open and K⁺ moves out, so the membrane repolarises.
5. The K⁺ channels shut slowly, giving a brief hyperpolarisation.
6. The pump and K⁺ leak channels restore the resting potential.

Key ideas:

- **All-or-nothing:** below threshold, nothing. Above it, same size every time.
- **Strength of stimulus:** coded by frequency and number of neurones, not size.
- **Refractory period:** Na⁺ channels cannot open. Gives one-way travel, separate impulses and a ceiling on frequency.
- **Conduction:** Na⁺ entering spreads sideways inside the axon, bringing the next region to threshold.

### Small worked reminders

- Maximum frequency = 1 / refractory time. A 5.0 ms gap gives 1 / 0.0050 = 200 per second.
- Speed = distance / time. 0.30 m in 6.0 ms is 0.30 / 0.0060 = 50 m/s.
- Amplitude = peak minus resting = 40 − (−70) = 110 mV.
- Reflex time: add the axon times and the synaptic delays. Axon time = length / speed. Two axons of 0.40 m at 40 m/s take 10 ms each, so 20 ms; with two synapses at 1.0 ms each the total is 22 ms.
- Pupil area: diameter 4.0 mm gives π × 2.0² = 12.6 mm². Halving the diameter to 2.0 mm gives π × 1.0² = 3.14 mm², a quarter of the area.

## 8.5 Saltatory conduction

- Myelin insulates, so ions cross only at nodes.
- Local currents link node to node inside the axon.
- Action potentials occur only at nodes.
- Faster: fewer sites to regenerate the impulse.
- Cheaper: fewer ions to pump back, so less ATP.
- If myelin is lost, impulses slow or fail.

Unmyelinated axon: the depolarisation spreads continuously along the whole membrane, so it is slower.

## 8.6 (i) Synapse

Parts: presynaptic knob (mitochondria, vesicles of ACh, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels), synaptic cleft, postsynaptic membrane (receptors on Na⁺ channels).

### Sequence

1. Impulse reaches the knob.
2. Voltage-gated calcium channels open and Ca²⁺ floods in.
3. Vesicles move to the knob membrane, fuse and empty their ACh into the cleft (exocytosis).
4. ACh crosses the gap by diffusion and attaches to receptor sites.
5. Linked Na⁺ channels open, so Na⁺ rushes in and the postsynaptic side depolarises.
6. Threshold reached: a new action potential starts.
7. Acetylcholinesterase breaks ACh into choline and ethanoate; products reabsorbed; ACh resynthesised using ATP.

### Properties

- One direction only, because only the presynaptic side releases transmitter.
- Synaptic delay.
- Summation: effects from several presynaptic neurones add together.
- Some transmitters inhibit by hyperpolarising the postsynaptic membrane.

## 8.6 (ii) Pupil

| Condition | Circular muscle | Radial muscle | Pupil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bright light | Contracts | Relaxes | Constricts |
| Dim light | Relaxes | Contracts | Dilates |

- The two muscles are antagonistic.
- Constriction protects the retina.
- Area = π r², so a small change in diameter gives a larger change in area.

## 8.7 Drugs

| Drug | Target | Effect in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Nicotine | Postsynaptic ACh receptors | Mimics ACh; not broken down by acetylcholinesterase; prolonged stimulation |
| Lidocaine | Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels | Blocked; no action potentials; local anaesthetic |
| Cobra alpha toxin | Postsynaptic ACh receptors | Blocks them; no depolarisation; paralysis |
| L-DOPA | Brain | Converted to dopamine; treats Parkinson's symptoms |
| MDMA | Serotonin synapses | Releases serotonin and blocks reuptake |

- Nicotine and alpha toxin act on the same receptors with opposite outcomes (activate versus block).
- Lidocaine acts on the axon, not on the synapse.
- L-DOPA, not dopamine, is given because it crosses into the brain.

## Side-by-side comparisons

| Feature | Unmyelinated axon | Myelinated axon |
|---|---|---|
| Where action potentials occur | Along the whole length | Only at nodes of Ranvier |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Ion movement across membrane | Along the whole length | Only at nodes |
| ATP use for restoring ion gradients | Higher | Lower |

| Feature | Reflex | Voluntary response |
|---|---|---|
| Pathway | Short, few synapses | Longer, involves the brain |
| Conscious thought | Not needed | Needed |
| Speed | Very fast | Slower |
| Value | Protects the body | Flexible and varied |

| Feature | Nicotine | Cobra alpha toxin |
|---|---|---|
| Binds to | ACh receptors | ACh receptors |
| Channel result | Opens | Stays closed |
| Postsynaptic membrane | Depolarised | Not depolarised |
| Muscle | Overstimulated | Paralysed |

## Reading graphs and tables

- **Action potential trace:** read the baseline first (resting potential), then the threshold where the line steepens, the peak, the dip below resting (hyperpolarisation) and the return. Quote values with units.
- **Time between peaks:** the interval between two peaks gives the frequency. Frequency = 1 / interval.
- **Drug data:** compare each result with the control. Say what changed, quote a figure, then link the change to a channel or receptor.
- **Percentage change:** (new − old) / old × 100. Show the subtraction before dividing.
- **Rates:** include the unit, such as m/s or per second, in the final line.

## Memory lines

- Reflex path: receptor, sensory, relay, motor, effector.
- Action potential: in with sodium, out with potassium.
- Synapse: calcium in, vesicles fuse, ACh across, sodium in, enzyme clears.
- Pupil: bright light, circular contracts; dim light, radial contracts.
- Drugs: nicotine copies, toxin blocks, lidocaine silences the axon, L-DOPA refills, MDMA floods serotonin.

## Quick self-test

1. Which type of neurone has its cell body outside the spinal cord?
2. What are the gaps between Schwann cells called?
3. Which region of the spinal cord holds relay neurone cell bodies?
4. Which ions enter the axon during depolarisation?
5. An axon has a refractory period of 2.0 ms. What is its maximum firing rate?
6. An impulse covers 0.24 m in 4.0 ms. What is the speed?
7. Which ion enters the presynaptic knob before vesicles fuse?
8. Which enzyme removes acetylcholine from the cleft?
9. Which pupil muscle contracts in bright light?
10. What does lidocaine block?
11. Why is L-DOPA given rather than dopamine?
12. What is the percentage decrease if a pupil narrows from 6.0 mm to 3.0 mm in diameter?

### Answers

1. Sensory neurone.
2. Nodes of Ranvier.
3. Grey matter.
4. Sodium ions (Na⁺).
5. 1 / 0.0020 = 500 per second.
6. 0.24 / 0.0040 = 60 m/s.
7. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺).
8. Acetylcholinesterase.
9. The circular muscle.
10. Voltage-gated sodium channels.
11. L-DOPA crosses into the brain, where it is converted to dopamine.
12. (6.0 − 3.0) / 6.0 × 100 = 50%.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "sodium moves in" without saying it diffuses through voltage-gated channels.
- Leaving out potassium ions when explaining repolarisation.
- Describing a "bigger" action potential for a bigger stimulus.
- Missing calcium ions in the synapse sequence.
- Saying that acetylcholine is "destroyed" instead of naming acetylcholinesterase and its products.
- Describing the impulse as an "electrical message" when the question asks for the ion movements.
- Giving saltatory conduction as "faster because of insulation" without saying that action potentials occur only at nodes.
- Mixing up nicotine (activates receptors) with alpha toxin (blocks them).
- Forgetting units for speed, potential and area.
