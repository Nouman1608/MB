These questions cover section **4.6.3 The development of understanding of genetics and evolution** of the AQA
GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. Questions 3, 4, 7, 9, 11 and 12(b) draw on the
biology-only statements 4.6.3.1 to 4.6.3.3. All data in the questions are invented for practice. A calculator is
useful for questions 8 and 10.

## Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by a species. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the scientist who independently proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection and published
joint writings with Darwin. **[1]**

**2.** A fossil beetle is found trapped in tree resin, and a fossil footprint is found in mudstone.

**(a)** Name the way the footprint fossil was formed. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest why the beetle in the resin did not decay. **[2]**

**3.** Put these developments in the order in which they happened, earliest first. **[2]**

- A -- the structure of DNA was determined
- B -- Mendel bred plants and described inherited "units"
- C -- genes were proposed to be located on chromosomes
- D -- the behaviour of chromosomes during cell division was observed

**4.** Giraffes have long necks. Explain how Lamarck and Darwin would each have explained the evolution of long
necks. **[4]**

**5.** A species of flightless bird lives only on one small island. Suggest **three** factors that could lead to
its extinction. **[3]**

**6.** MRSA is a strain of bacteria that is resistant to many antibiotics.

**(a)** Explain why bacteria can evolve more rapidly than animals. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a new resistant strain can spread quickly through a hospital. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A population of lizards lives on a mainland covered in grassland. A storm carries a few lizards to a
forested island 80 km away. Thousands of years later, scientists find that the island lizards are a different
species.

**(a)** Describe how the new species could have formed. **[5]**
**(b)** Describe how scientists could show that the island lizards are a separate species. **[1]**

**8.** A hospital records the percentage of infections caused by a strain of bacteria resistant to antibiotic X,
and how often antibiotic X is prescribed.

| Year | 2010 | 2014 | 2018 | 2022 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resistant infections (%) | 4 | 9 | 18 | 31 |
| Prescriptions per 1000 patients | 120 | 150 | 165 | 170 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the percentage of resistant infections was in 2022 than in 2010. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship between prescriptions and resistant infections. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how increased prescribing could lead to more resistant infections. **[3]**
**(d)** Give **one** reason why these data do not prove that prescribing caused the rise. **[1]**

**9.** Gregor Mendel carried out breeding experiments on pea plants in the mid-19th century.

**(a)** State what Mendel observed about the inheritance of each characteristic. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the importance of his work was not recognised until after his death. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how observations made in the late 19th and early 20th centuries supported Mendel's ideas. **[2]**

**10.** Scientists measure the mean body length of fossils of one group of animals from rock layers of
different ages.

| Age of rock (millions of years) | 55 | 40 | 30 | 15 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean body length (m) | 0.4 | 0.6 | 0.9 | 1.2 | 1.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean body length from 55 to 5 million years ago. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of increase in body length between 30 and 15 million years ago. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** reason scientists cannot be certain that the fossils show every stage in the change. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection was only gradually accepted, and why it is
widely accepted today. **[6]**

**12.** Soil near an old copper mine contains a high concentration of copper, which kills most grass plants. A
population of grass growing on this soil is tolerant of copper.

**(a)** Explain how the copper-tolerant population could have developed by natural selection. **[4]**
**(b)** The tolerant grass flowers at a different time from grass on nearby normal soil. Explain how this could
eventually lead to a new species forming. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring [1]
**(b)** (Alfred Russel) Wallace [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Organisms that look alike" does not define a species; the mark needs fertile offspring.

**2. (a)** As a preserved trace (trace fossil) [1]
**(b)** One or more conditions needed for decay were absent, for example no oxygen inside the resin [1]; so
microorganisms (decomposers) could not break the beetle down [1]
*Examiner insight:* The second mark needs the link to microorganisms or decay, not just "it was sealed".

**3.** B, D, C, A [2] (award one mark if B is first and A is last but the middle two are swapped)
*Examiner insight:* Mendel's work came before chromosome behaviour was observed; that order is why his work was
overlooked.

**4.** Lamarck: giraffes stretched their necks during their lifetime to reach leaves [1]; the longer neck
gained during life was passed to their offspring [1]. Darwin: giraffes varied in neck length, and those with
longer necks were more likely to survive and breed [1]; they passed on the characteristic, so over many
generations long necks became more common [1]
*Examiner insight:* For Darwin, the variation must already exist; "the giraffes grew longer necks to survive"
is Lamarckian and scores only for Lamarck.

**5.** Any three of the following, 1 mark each [3]: a new disease or pathogen; a new predator, such as rats or
cats brought to the island; a new competitor for food or nesting sites; destruction of its habitat; a change in
climate or environment; a catastrophic event such as a volcanic eruption
*Examiner insight:* "Hunting" can be credited as a new predator; each factor must be different.

**6. (a)** They reproduce at a fast rate (so many generations pass in a short time) [1]
**(b)** People are not immune to the new strain [1]; and there is no effective treatment, so infected patients
are not cured and can pass it on [1]

**7. (a)** The island population was isolated from the mainland population (no interbreeding) [1]; there was
genetic variation in each population, with new mutations [1]; conditions on the island (forest) differed from
the mainland (grassland) [1]; so natural selection favoured different alleles in each population [1]; over a
long time the populations became so different that they could not interbreed to produce fertile offspring [1]
**(b)** Breed island and mainland lizards together and show that they cannot produce fertile offspring [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that stops at "they evolved differently" misses the selection and fertile-offspring
marks.

**8. (a)** 31 ÷ 4 = **7.75 times** [1]
**(b)** As prescriptions increase, the percentage of resistant infections increases (positive correlation) [1]
**(c)** The antibiotic kills non-resistant bacteria [1]; resistant bacteria survive and reproduce [1]; the more
often the antibiotic is used, the more often resistant bacteria are selected, so the resistant population rises
[1]
**(d)** Correlation does not show cause; another factor, such as agricultural use of antibiotics or patients not
completing courses, could also be involved [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "the bacteria become immune" is wrong; bacteria are resistant, people are immune.

**9. (a)** The inheritance of each characteristic is determined by units passed on to descendants unchanged [1]
**(b)** Chromosomes, genes and DNA were unknown at the time [1]; so there was no explanation of what his units were
(or few scientists read or understood his work) [1]
**(c)** In the late 19th century chromosome behaviour during cell division was observed [1]; in the early 20th
century chromosomes and Mendel's units were seen to behave in similar ways, so the units (genes) were proposed to
be on chromosomes [1]

**10. (a)** 1.6 − 0.4 = 1.2 m; 1.2 ÷ 0.4 × 100 [1] = **300%** [1]
**(b)** (1.2 − 0.9) ÷ 15 [1] = **0.02 m per million years** [1]
**(c)** The fossil record is incomplete: some organisms did not form fossils, or fossils were destroyed by
geological activity [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the time interval is 30 − 15 = 15 million years; dividing by 30 is a common slip.

**11.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer covers both parts):
- the theory challenged the idea that God made all the animals and plants on Earth [1]
- there was insufficient evidence at the time to convince many scientists [1]
- the mechanism of inheritance and variation was not known until 50 years after publication [1]
- it is now known that characteristics are passed on to offspring in genes [1]
- the fossil record provides evidence of how organisms have changed [1]
- antibiotic resistance in bacteria shows evolution happening in a short time [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that explains only why acceptance was slow, with no evidence from today, cannot reach
the top level.

**12. (a)** There was variation in copper tolerance in the original grass, caused by different alleles (from
mutation) [1]; plants without tolerance were killed by the copper [1]; tolerant plants survived and reproduced,
passing on the alleles for tolerance [1]; over many generations the whole population became tolerant [1]
**(b)** The two populations cannot interbreed because they flower at different times, so they are isolated [1];
over time they become so different that they could not produce fertile offspring even if they did breed, so a
new species forms [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that organisms change "in order to" survive, which is Lamarck's idea, not natural selection.
- Saying the antibiotic causes mutations, or that bacteria become "immune".
- Leaving out "many generations" at the end of a natural selection answer.
- Speciation answers that never mention isolation or fertile offspring.
- Dividing by the wrong time interval when calculating a rate from fossil data.
- Treating a correlation in data as proof of cause.
