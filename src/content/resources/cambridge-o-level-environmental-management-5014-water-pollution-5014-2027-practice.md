## Section A

**1.** State **two** pollutants that enter rivers from agricultural practices. **[2]**

**2.** Define *biomagnification*. **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by *leaching*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the infectious bacterial disease that the syllabus links to water polluted by sewage. **[1]**

**5.** Describe **two** effects of acid rain on organisms living in a lake. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how domestic waste from a town can pollute a nearby river. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Heavy rain falls on a field the day after it has been sprayed with fertiliser. The field slopes down to a small lake. Two weeks later, many fish in the lake are found dead. Describe and explain the sequence of events that led to the death of the fish. **[5]**

**8.** A pesticide used on rice fields drains into a lake. The table shows its concentration in a lake food chain. The figures are invented for practice.

| Level | Pesticide concentration (ppm) |
|---|---|
| Lake water | 0.00005 |
| Zooplankton | 0.04 |
| Small fish | 0.5 |
| Heron | 12 |

(a) Calculate how many times more concentrated the pesticide is in herons than in the lake water. **[1]**
(b) Explain why the concentration is highest in the herons. **[3]**
(c) Suggest why people who eat fish from the lake regularly may be at risk. **[1]**

**9.** Dissolved oxygen was measured in a river at four sites. A pipe releasing untreated sewage enters the river at site 2. The figures are invented for practice.

| Site | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distance downstream of site 1 (km) | 0 | 1 | 4 | 9 |
| Dissolved oxygen (mg per litre) | 8.5 | 2.0 | 4.5 | 8.0 |

(a) Describe how dissolved oxygen changes from site 1 to site 4. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
(b) Explain why dissolved oxygen is lowest at site 2. **[2]**
(c) Suggest **one** reason why dissolved oxygen recovers by site 4. **[1]**

**10.** A fast-growing city releases its sewage into a river without treatment. Describe **three** strategies that could improve the water quality of the river. **[3]**

**11.** A village takes its drinking water from a river. Upstream there is a metal-processing factory and a large poultry farm.
(a) Explain **one** way the factory and **one** way the poultry farm could make the river water harmful to people in the village. **[4]**
(b) Suggest **two** laws or controls the government could introduce to protect the village's water. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of relying on pollution control and legislation, rather than building new sewage treatment works, to improve river water quality in a low-income country. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Fertilisers (nitrate or phosphate) **[1]**; animal waste or pesticides **[1]**.

**2.** The increase in concentration of a toxic substance as it is passed up a food chain **[1]**.

**3.** The washing of dissolved substances, such as fertiliser ions, through the soil into ground water or rivers **[1]**.

**4.** Cholera **[1]**.

**5.** Lower pH stops fish eggs and young fish developing, so fish populations fall **[1]**; sensitive invertebrates disappear, so animals that feed on them lose food and the food web is disrupted **[1]**.

**6.** Waste dumped on river banks or in open tips is washed into the river by rain **[1]**, adding food waste, detergents, oils or household chemicals to the water **[1]**.

**7.** Rain washes fertiliser off the slope and leaches dissolved nitrate and phosphate into the lake **[1]**. The extra nutrients cause rapid growth of algae, an algal bloom **[1]**, which blocks light, so plants below the surface cannot photosynthesise and die **[1]**. Bacteria decompose the dead plants and algae and use up dissolved oxygen in respiration **[1]**. Fish die because there is not enough oxygen in the water **[1]**.

**8.** (a) 12 ÷ 0.00005 = 240,000 times **[1]**.
(b) The pesticide is not broken down or excreted, so it stays in each organism's tissues **[1]**. Each consumer eats large numbers of organisms from the level below **[1]**, so the pesticide from all of them is concentrated in the predator, and herons at the top of the chain have the highest concentration (biomagnification) **[1]**.
(c) People eating fish are consumers at the top of the chain, so the pesticide can build up in their bodies over time (bioaccumulation) **[1]**.

**9.** (a) Oxygen falls sharply from 8.5 mg per litre at site 1 to 2.0 mg per litre at site 2 **[1]**, then rises gradually to 4.5 at site 3 and 8.0 at site 4, almost back to the upstream level **[1]**.
(b) The sewage adds organic matter at site 2 **[1]**; bacteria decomposing it use dissolved oxygen in respiration **[1]**.
(c) Most of the organic matter has been broken down or diluted by 9 km, so less oxygen is used; or moving water and photosynthesis by plants add oxygen back **[1]**.

**10.** Build sewage treatment works so solids are removed and bacteria break down organic matter before release **[1]**; improve sanitation with sewers and toilets so that waste does not enter the river directly **[1]**; introduce legislation limiting discharges, with monitoring and fines **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Factory: heavy metals or toxic chemicals are discharged into the river **[1]**; they accumulate in fish and in people who drink the water or eat the fish, causing long-term harm **[1]**. Poultry farm: animal waste washes into the river and adds organic matter and bacteria **[1]**, so the water carries a risk of infectious disease and may become eutrophic, reducing its quality **[1]**.
(b) A legal limit on what the factory may discharge, checked by regular monitoring and fines **[1]**; rules requiring the farm to store waste safely away from the river **[1]**. Also accept a permit system or a ban on discharging untreated waste.

**12.** One mark per point; at least one benefit, one limitation and a judgement for full marks.
- Benefit: legislation is much cheaper to introduce than building treatment works **[1]**.
- Benefit: limits and fines apply to every factory and farm, so polluters pay to reduce their own waste **[1]**.
- Limitation: laws need inspectors, testing equipment and courts to be enforced, which a low-income country may lack **[1]**.
- Limitation: legislation cannot treat household sewage that is already flowing into the river, so the cholera risk remains **[1]**.
- Limitation: diffuse run-off from many small farms is hard to trace to one polluter **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, legislation is a useful first step for industry, but treatment works and sanitation are still needed for sewage, perhaps funded in stages **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 7**: missing the bacteria step, or saying the fertiliser poisoned the fish.
- **Question 8(b)**: describing the figures without explaining why they increase at each level.
- **Question 9(a)**: describing only the fall and not the recovery, or giving no figures.
- **Question 11(a)**: naming the pollutant without saying how it harms people.
- **Question 12**: writing only about legislation and not comparing it with treatment works.
