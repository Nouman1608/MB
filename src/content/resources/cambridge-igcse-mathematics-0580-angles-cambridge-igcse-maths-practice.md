These questions cover subtopic 4.6, Angles, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use irregular polygons, which only the Extended statement includes; every other question suits both tiers. All questions are non-calculator, and diagrams are described in words, so sketch each one before you start. Where a question asks for reasons, use the full geometrical wording.

For explanations, read the [angles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a summary, use the [angles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Two angles on a straight line are (4x + 12)° and (2x − 6)°. Find the value of x. **[2]**

**2.** Triangle PQR is isosceles with PQ = PR. Angle QPR = 38°. Find angle PQR, giving reasons. **[2]**

**3.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°.

**(a)** Find the number of sides. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the sum of the interior angles. **[1]**

**4.** Show that a regular polygon can have an interior angle of 165°, and find its number of sides. **[2]**

**5.** AB and CD are parallel lines, with A and C on the same side of a transversal. The transversal meets AB at G and CD at H. Angle AGH = 115°.

**(a)** Find angle GHC. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle GHD. Give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** The angles of a quadrilateral are x°, 3x°, (2x + 20)° and 100°. Find the largest angle. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** ABCD is a trapezium with AB parallel to DC. Angle DAB = 72°, angle ABC = 2x° and angle BCD = (x + 15)°.

**(a)** Find x, giving a reason. **[3]**

**(b)** Find angle ADC, giving a reason. **[2]**

**8.** PQ and RS are parallel lines, with PQ above RS and Q and S to the right of P and R. A point T lies between the lines, to the left of both P and R. Angle QPT = 125° and angle SRT = 140°. Find angle PTR, giving reasons. **[4]**

**9.** ABCDEFGH is a regular octagon.

**(a)** Find the size of one interior angle. **[1]**

**(b)** Find angle CBD, giving reasons. **[2]**

**(c)** Find angle ABD. **[1]**

**10.** Two regular pentagons and one other regular polygon meet at a point, fitting together with no gaps or overlaps. Find the number of sides of the other polygon. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) The exterior angles of a hexagon are x°, 2x°, 50°, 70°, 3x° and 2x°.

**(a)** Find x. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the largest interior angle of the hexagon. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) The interior angles of a polygon add up to 1980°.

**(a)** Find the number of sides. **[2]**

**(b)** Five of the interior angles are each 160°. The others are all equal. Find the size of each of the others. **[2]**

**13.** In triangle ABC, the side BC is extended beyond C to a point D. Angle BAC = a° and angle ABC = b°. Show that angle ACD = (a + b)°, giving a reason for each step. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (4x + 12) + (2x − 6) = 180 [1]; 6x = 174, **x = 29** [1]

**2.** Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal and the angle sum of a triangle is 180° [1]; angle PQR = (180° − 38°) ÷ 2 = **71°** [1]

**3. (a)** 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1]

**(b)** (12 − 2) × 180° = **1800°** [1]

**4.** Exterior angle = 180° − 165° = 15° [1]; 360 ÷ 15 = 24, a whole number, so the polygon exists with **24 sides** [1]

**5. (a)** **65°** [1]; co-interior angles sum to 180° [1]

**(b)** **115°** [1]; alternate angles are equal [1]

*Examiner insight:* angles AGH and GHC are between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal, so they are co-interior; AGH and GHD are on opposite sides, so they are alternate.

**6.** x + 3x + 2x + 20 + 100 = 360 (angle sum of a quadrilateral) [1]; 6x = 240, x = 40 [1]; angles 40°, 120°, 100°, 100°, so the largest is **120°** [1]

**7. (a)** Angles ABC and BCD are co-interior between the parallel lines AB and DC, so they sum to 180° [1]; 2x + x + 15 = 180, 3x = 165 [1]; **x = 55** [1]

**(b)** Angles DAB and ADC are co-interior, so angle ADC = 180° − 72° [1] = **108°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check with the angle sum: 72° + 110° + 70° + 108° = 360°.

**8.** Draw a line UTV through T parallel to PQ, with V to the right of T [1]. Angle PTV = 180° − 125° = 55° (co-interior angles sum to 180°) [1]. Angle RTV = 180° − 140° = 40° (co-interior angles sum to 180°) [1]. Angle PTR = 55° + 40° = **95°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the new line must be stated as parallel; without that, co-interior angles cannot be used as a reason.

**9. (a)** Exterior angle = 360° ÷ 8 = 45°, so interior angle = **135°** [1]

**(b)** BC = CD (sides of a regular octagon), so triangle BCD is isosceles [1]; angle CBD = (180° − 135°) ÷ 2 = **22.5°** [1]

**(c)** 135° − 22.5° = **112.5°** [1]

**10.** Interior angle of a regular pentagon = 108° [1]; the third angle = 360° − 2 × 108° = 144° (angles at a point sum to 360°) [1]; exterior angle 36°, so 360 ÷ 36 = **10 sides** [1]

**11. (a)** Exterior angles sum to 360°: 8x + 120 = 360 [1]; **x = 30** [1]

**(b)** The smallest exterior angle (30°) gives the largest interior angle: 180° − 30° = **150°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the interior angles are 150°, 120°, 130°, 110°, 90° and 120°, which add to 720°, the interior angle sum of a hexagon.

**12. (a)** (n − 2) × 180 = 1980 [1]; n − 2 = 11, **n = 13** [1]

**(b)** The other 8 angles sum to 1980° − 5 × 160° = 1180° [1]; each = 1180° ÷ 8 = **147.5°** [1]

**13.** Angle ACB = 180° − a − b (angle sum of a triangle is 180°) [1]. Angles ACB and ACD lie on the straight line BCD, so angle ACD = 180° − (180° − a − b) (angles on a straight line sum to 180°) [1] = a + b, as required [1]

*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question the answer is given, so every mark is for the working and the reasons; jumping straight to a + b earns nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving x instead of the angle the question asks for.
- Writing "co-interior angles are equal" or "Z angles" as a reason.
- Using parallel-line facts on lines that are not stated to be parallel, including a line you drew yourself without saying so.
- Using 360° ÷ n as an interior angle.
- (Extended) Dividing the angle sum equally between the angles of an irregular polygon.
- Missing the isosceles triangle hidden inside a regular polygon.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many reasons does an angle question need?**
One for each step that uses a geometrical fact. If a question says "give reasons", a correct angle with no reasons will not get full credit.

**How can I check an angle answer?**
Use a different fact on the same diagram: the angles of the triangle or quadrilateral you found should still add to 180° or 360°.
