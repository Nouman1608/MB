## Questions

**1.** (a) State what is meant by the terms genotype and phenotype. **[2]**
**(b)** Two clones of a plant have the same alleles. One is grown in full light and one in shade, and the shaded plant is paler. Explain the difference. **[1]**

**2.** In a freshwater fish, scale colour is controlled by one gene. Allele Fᴳ gives gold scales and Fᴮ gives blue scales. They are codominant, and heterozygotes have gold-and-blue patched scales.
**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram for a cross between two patched fish. **[4]**
**(b)** Give the numbers of each phenotype expected among 200 offspring. **[1]**

**3.** Wing colour in a butterfly has three alleles. Cᵇ (brown) is dominant to Cʸ (yellow), which is dominant to Cʷ (white). A brown butterfly heterozygous for brown and white is crossed with a yellow butterfly heterozygous for yellow and white.
**(a)** Give the genotypes and phenotype ratio of the offspring. **[3]**
**(b)** What is the probability that a brown offspring carries the yellow allele? **[1]**

**4.** In a gourd, disc fruit (D) is dominant to sphere (d), and striped skin (S) is dominant to plain (s). The genes are on different chromosomes. A plant DdSs is crossed with a plant ddss.
**(a)** State the expected phenotype ratio and the numbers among 480 offspring. **[2]**
**(b)** Two DdSs plants are crossed. Calculate the probability that an offspring has disc fruit and plain skin. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the ddss plant is used in the first cross. **[1]**

**5.** In fruit flies, red eyes (Xᴿ) are dominant to white eyes (Xʳ). The gene is on the X chromosome.
**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram for a cross between a heterozygous red-eyed female and a white-eyed male. **[4]**
**(b)** A red-eyed male is crossed with a white-eyed female. Predict the eye colour of the sons and daughters and explain. **[2]**

**6.** In chickens, females are ZW and males ZZ. Barred plumage (Zᴮ) is dominant to non-barred (Zᵇ).
**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram for a cross between a heterozygous barred male and a non-barred female. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why no female chicken can be heterozygous for this gene. **[1]**

**7.** In a beetle, dark elytra (D) are dominant to pale (d), and long antennae (A) are dominant to short (a). A heterozygous beetle was test crossed with a pale, short-antennae beetle. Of 700 offspring: 301 dark long, 288 pale short, 56 dark short and 55 pale long.
**(a)** Identify the parental classes and give the arrangement of alleles in the heterozygous parent. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of recombinant offspring. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the recombinants arose. **[2]**
**(d)** A chi-squared test against 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 gives 327 (degrees of freedom 3, critical value 7.81 at p = 0.05). State the conclusion. **[2]**

**8.** Flower colour in a plant needs two enzymes in sequence: a colourless precursor is converted by enzyme 1 (made from allele C) to a colourless intermediate, then by enzyme 2 (made from allele P) to purple pigment. Alleles c and p give no functional enzyme. Two plants CcPp are crossed.
**(a)** Explain why 7 of every 16 offspring are expected to be white. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the expected numbers among 320 offspring. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the type of gene interaction shown. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the ratio is not 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. **[1]**

**9.** A cross between two heterozygous gourd plants (two genes, independent assortment) gave 640 offspring: 352 disc striped, 135 disc plain, 108 sphere striped and 45 sphere plain. Use a chi-squared test (critical value 7.81 at p = 0.05, 3 degrees of freedom) to test whether the results fit 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. **[6]**

**10.** A student test crossed a heterozygous plant with a homozygous recessive plant and expected 1 : 1. The 160 offspring were 87 with the dominant phenotype and 73 with the recessive phenotype.
**(a)** Calculate χ². **[2]**
**(b)** Use 3.84 as the critical value (p = 0.05) and state the conclusion. **[2]**
**(c)** The student converted the counts to percentages before testing. Explain why this is not valid. **[1]**

**11.** Plan an investigation, using fruit flies, to find out whether two genes are linked. Include how you would analyse the results. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Genotype: the genetic constitution of an organism (its alleles) [1]; phenotype: the expression of the genetic constitution and its interaction with the environment [1].
**(b)** The genotype is the same, so the difference is caused by the environment (light affects pigment or growth) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Define phenotype as the expression of the genes plus the environment, because an answer that gives only appearance leaves out why identical clones can differ.

**2. (a)** Parental genotypes FᴳFᴮ × FᴳFᴮ, with a key [1]. Gametes Fᴳ and Fᴮ from each [1]. Offspring FᴳFᴳ, FᴳFᴮ (twice), FᴮFᴮ [1]. Phenotypes gold : patched : blue = 1 : 2 : 1 [1].
**(b)** 50 gold, 100 patched, 50 blue [1].
*Examiner insight:* A key and labelled rows (parents, gametes, offspring) are the easiest way to show the examiner each step of a diagram.

**3. (a)** Gametes Cᵇ, Cʷ and Cʸ, Cʷ [1]. Offspring CᵇCʸ, CᵇCʷ, CʸCʷ, CʷCʷ [1]. Phenotypes 2 brown : 1 yellow : 1 white [1].
**(b)** 1/2 (CᵇCʸ is one of the two brown genotypes) [1].
*Examiner insight:* With three alleles, write the dominance order in your key; the ratio is 2 : 1 : 1 because two genotypes share one phenotype.

**4. (a)** The ddss parent makes only ds gametes, so the ratio is 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 [1] with 120 of each phenotype (disc striped, disc plain, sphere striped, sphere plain) [1].
**(b)** P(disc) = 3/4 and P(plain) = 1/4 [1]. 3/4 × 1/4 = **3/16** [1].
**(c)** It produces only recessive gametes, so the phenotype of each offspring shows which alleles came from the other parent [1].
*Examiner insight:* For probability, show each single-gene probability and the multiplication so that a slip in the final fraction is still easy to follow.

**5. (a)** Parents XᴿXʳ × XʳY [1]. Gametes Xᴿ, Xʳ and Xʳ, Y [1]. Offspring XᴿXʳ, XʳXʳ, XᴿY, XʳY [1]. Red female, white female, red male, white male = 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 [1].
**(b)** Daughters all XᴿXʳ, red eyed [1]. Sons all XʳY, white eyed, as a son receives the Y from his father and his only X from his mother [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write X and Y in the genotypes rather than just letters, so it is clear the trait is sex-linked.

**6. (a)** Parents ZᴮZᵇ × ZᵇW [1]. Gametes Zᴮ, Zᵇ and Zᵇ, W [1]. Offspring ZᴮZᵇ, ZᵇZᵇ, ZᴮW, ZᵇW [1]. Barred male, non-barred male, barred female, non-barred female = 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 [1].
**(b)** A female has only one Z chromosome, so she has only one allele of this gene [1].
*Examiner insight:* Read which sex has the two different sex chromosomes before you start; in birds it is the female, the reverse of humans.

**7. (a)** Parental classes: dark long and pale short [1]. The heterozygote has D and A on one chromosome and d and a on the homologue [1].
**(b)** (56 + 55) ÷ 700 × 100 = **15.9%** [1].
**(c)** Crossing over between chromatids of homologous chromosomes in prophase I of meiosis [1], exchanging alleles to give gametes with new combinations (Da and dA) [1].
**(d)** 327 is greater than 7.81, so the difference from 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 is significant, so the null hypothesis is rejected [1]. The genes do not assort independently, which is consistent with linkage [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name both the pattern in the data (large parental classes) and the cause (crossing over) when asked to explain linkage.

**8. (a)** Purple needs at least one C and one P, which happens in 9 of 16 offspring [1]. The other 7 (3 C_pp, 3 ccP_, 1 ccpp) lack a functional enzyme [1], so the pathway is blocked and no pigment forms [1].
**(b)** 180 purple and 140 white [1].
**(c)** Epistasis [1].
**(d)** Alleles at one locus affect expression of the other locus, so the phenotype classes merge [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link the genotypes to the pathway: examiners look for the missing enzyme and the blocked step, not only the counting of boxes.

**9.** Null hypothesis: any difference between the counts and the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 expectation is due to chance [1]. Expected: 360, 120, 120, 40 [1]. χ² = (352−360)²/360 + (135−120)²/120 + (108−120)²/120 + (45−40)²/40 [1] = 0.178 + 1.875 + 1.200 + 0.625 = **3.88** [1]. Degrees of freedom = 3 and 3.88 < 7.81 [1], so the difference is not significant: the results fit 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 and any difference is probably due to chance [1].
*Examiner insight:* Finish with a conclusion about the genetics; a bare comparison of two numbers is less convincing.

**10. (a)** Expected 80 and 80 [1]. χ² = 49/80 + 49/80 = **1.23** [1].
**(b)** Degrees of freedom = 1 and 1.23 < 3.84 [1], so the difference is not significant and the results fit a 1 : 1 ratio [1].
**(c)** The value of χ² depends on the number of individuals counted, and percentages hide the sample size [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the expected values from the total and the ratio, not the observed counts.

**11.** Any eight of: cross a true-breeding (homozygous) dominant fly with a homozygous recessive fly to obtain heterozygous offspring [1]; test cross the heterozygous females with double-recessive males [1]; rear all flies under the same conditions, such as temperature and food, so phenotype reflects genotype [1]; count offspring in each of the four phenotype classes [1]; use a large sample or replicate crosses [1]; state the null hypothesis of independent assortment, expected 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 [1]; calculate χ² with 3 degrees of freedom and compare with 7.81 at p = 0.05 [1]; if significant and the parental classes are larger, the genes are linked and recombinants arose by crossing over [1].
*Examiner insight:* A plan scores better when it names the cross, what is counted, the expected ratio and how the result is interpreted, in that order.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the key, or using different letters for alleles of the same gene.
- Writing gametes with two alleles of the same gene.
- Stating a ratio without the phenotypes.
- Treating X-linked and autosomal crosses alike; sons and daughters can differ.
- Using observed values as expected values in chi-squared.
- Taking degrees of freedom as sample size minus one.
- Concluding that a non-significant result proves a ratio.
- Describing epistasis as dominance between genes instead of interaction between loci.
