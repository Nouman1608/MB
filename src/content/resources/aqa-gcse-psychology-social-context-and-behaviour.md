This guide covers **Paper 2 Social Context and Behaviour**, the second
of two equally-weighted papers in AQA GCSE Psychology (8182), first
teaching 2017. Like Paper 1, it is examined entirely by written
assessment, with research methods and mathematical skills assessed
across all four topic areas rather than as a standalone unit.

## Where this fits in 8182

Where Paper 1 looks inward at memory, perception and development,
Paper 2 turns outward: how people behave in groups, how they
communicate, what the brain contributes to behaviour, and how
psychology understands and treats mental health difficulties. The
four topics are taught as separate blocks but examined with the same
skills as Paper 1 -- description, application to novel scenarios, and
evaluation -- so a student who has built solid habits on Paper 1
content should apply the same technique here rather than treating
Paper 2 as a fresh start.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE PSYCHOLOGY (8182) — PAPER 2 SOCIAL CONTEXT AND BEHAVIOUR**

- 2.1 Social influence — how social factors (group size, anonymity,
task difficulty) and dispositional factors (personality, expertise)
affect conformity to majority influence, including Asch's study;
Milgram's Agency Theory of obedience, covering agency, authority,
culture and proximity, plus dispositional explanations including
Adorno's Authoritarian Personality theory; bystander behaviour,
covering how social factors (presence of others, cost of helping)
and dispositional factors (similarity to victim, expertise) affect
intervention, including Piliavin's subway study; and prosocial and
antisocial behaviour in crowds, covering social factors (social
loafing, deindividuation, culture) and dispositional factors
(personality, morality)
- 2.2 Language, thought and communication — the possible relationship
between language and thought, comparing Piaget's theory (language
depends on thought) with the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis (thinking depends
on language), including variation in recall and colour recognition
across cultures; differences between human and animal communication,
including Von Frisch's bee study; non-verbal communication, covering
eye contact, body language, postural echo, touch and personal space;
and explanations of non-verbal behaviour, contrasting Darwin's
evolutionary account with evidence it is learned, including Yuki's
study of emoticons
- 2.3 Brain and neuropsychology — the structure and function of the
nervous system, including the autonomic nervous system, fight-or-flight,
and the James-Lange theory of emotion; neuron structure and function,
covering sensory, relay and motor neurons, synaptic transmission and
Hebb's theory of learning; the structure and function of the brain,
including the four lobes, the cerebellum, and localisation of function,
with Penfield's study of the interpretive cortex; and an introduction
to neuropsychology, covering cognitive neuroscience, CT/PET/fMRI
scanning, Tulving's memory study, and how neurological damage affects
behaviour
- 2.4 Psychological problems — an introduction to mental health and how
its incidence changes over time; the effects of significant mental
health problems on individuals and society; the characteristics of
clinical depression, including the ICD approach to diagnosis; theories
and interventions for depression, contrasting the biological (serotonin
imbalance) and psychological (negative schemas) explanations with
antidepressant medication and CBT, including Wiles' effectiveness
study; the characteristics of addiction, including the ICD dependence
syndrome; and theories and interventions for addiction, contrasting
biological (genetic vulnerability, Kaij's twin study) and psychological
(peer influence) explanations with aversion therapy and self-management
programmes

## How to approach it

The four topics in Paper 2 share a consistent shape worth noticing:
each pairs a biological or "nature" explanation with a psychological
or "nurture" explanation, and exam questions frequently ask students to
compare the two rather than describe either alone. Build revision
around that structure -- for depression, know the serotonin account and
the negative-schema account side by side, not as separate topics. The
named studies (Asch, Milgram, Piliavin, Von Frisch, Penfield, Tulving,
Wiles, Kaij) are also common sources of application questions, so know
what each study actually did, not just the theory it supports. Brain
and neuropsychology tends to be the topic students find least intuitive
because it is the most biological; revising the four lobes and their
functions with a labelled diagram, rather than a bullet list, usually
makes the material stick better than rereading prose.

## Common mistakes

Confusing Piaget's view (language depends on thought) with Sapir-Whorf's
(thinking depends on language) -- they are opposite claims. Describing
conformity and obedience as the same process, when the specification
treats them as distinct with different named studies. Missing the
difference between addiction/dependence and substance misuse when
defining addiction. Treating antidepressants and CBT as competing
rather than complementary interventions -- both appear on real mark
schemes as valid treatment routes.

## Worked example

A student is asked: "Compare the biological and psychological
explanations of depression." What does a strong answer include?

```
BIOLOGICAL: an imbalance of neurotransmitters, particularly serotonin,
            in the brain -- treated with antidepressant medication.

PSYCHOLOGICAL: negative schemas and attributions learned through
                experience -- treated with cognitive behaviour therapy.

COMPARISON: the biological account treats depression as something that
            happens TO the brain (nature); the psychological account
            treats it as something learned through experience
            (nurture). A strong answer notes that treatments often
            combine both routes in practice, and that neither
            explanation alone accounts for why some people exposed to
            the same biological or environmental risk factors do not
            develop depression.
```

Simply listing both explanations without comparing them, or without
naming their associated treatment, will not access the higher mark
bands -- the question asks for comparison, not two descriptions side
by side.

## Quick revision checklist

- Explain conformity and obedience separately, each with its own named
study (Asch; Milgram) and both social and dispositional factors.
- Describe bystander behaviour and crowd behaviour, distinguishing the
factors that affect each.
- State Piaget's and Sapir-Whorf's positions on language and thought as
opposites, and give one piece of supporting cross-cultural evidence.
- List the four brain lobes and one function of each, plus the three
scanning techniques used in neuropsychology.
- For both depression and addiction, know one biological and one
psychological explanation, each paired with its matching intervention.
- Practise applying every factor and study to a novel scenario, since
Paper 2 -- like Paper 1 -- rewards application over pure recall.
