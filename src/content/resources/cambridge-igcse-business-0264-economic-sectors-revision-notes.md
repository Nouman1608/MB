Condensed notes for section 1.2 (Economic sectors) of the Cambridge IGCSE Business 0264 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The content statement is 1.2.1 Types of economic sector: "primary, secondary and tertiary sectors" and "private and public sectors". Full explanations and a worked chain of production are in the [economic sectors study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-economic-sectors/). Test yourself afterwards with the [economic sectors practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-economic-sectors-practice/). The wider [business activity and economic sectors notes](/resources/igcse-business-0264-business-activity-and-economic-sectors-revision-notes/) also cover section 1.1.

All examples are invented.

## Two separate classifications

| Question asked | Possible answers | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| What kind of work is done? | Primary, secondary, tertiary | The main activity of the business |
| Who owns and controls it? | Private, public | Whether individuals or the government own it |

Every organisation gets one answer from each row. "A state hospital is tertiary and public" is a complete classification; "a state hospital is public" is only half.

## Activity sectors in one line each

- **Primary:** extracting, growing or rearing natural resources.
- **Secondary:** manufacturing, processing, assembling or building, which turns materials into new products.
- **Tertiary:** providing services to people or to other businesses.

### Sorting drill

| Primary | Secondary | Tertiary |
|---|---|---|
| Cattle ranch | Steel works | Insurance broker |
| Gold mine | Clothing factory | Hairdresser |
| Logging company | Sugar refinery | Bus operator |
| Prawn farm | House builder | Car repair garage |
| Stone quarry | Printing works | Online travel agent |

### The tricky ones

- **Construction is secondary.** Building is a making activity.
- **Repairs, transport and retail are tertiary.** They handle physical goods but do not make them.
- **Rearing animals or fish is primary**, even indoors or in tanks.
- **Businesses can span sectors.** A tea estate that grows, dries and packs tea and runs a visitor café is primary, secondary and tertiary. Classify by main activity and mention the others if the data shows them.

## The chain of production

Raw material (primary) leads to a finished product (secondary), which reaches the customer through services (tertiary). Value is added at each stage.

Short chain to remember: cotton grown, then spun and woven into cloth and made into shirts, then transported and sold in shops.

Interdependence points for "explain" answers:

- Secondary firms need primary firms for materials; a shortage raises their costs.
- Primary firms need secondary firms as buyers; if factories cut output, demand for materials falls.
- All sectors need tertiary services: transport, banking, insurance, retailing.
- A problem at one stage spreads along the chain.

## Ownership sectors

| | Private sector | Public sector |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | Individuals or groups: sole traders, partners, shareholders | National or local government |
| Usual aim | Mainly profit | Mainly a service for the public |
| Money from | Owners, profit, loans, sales | Mainly government funding from taxes; some fees |
| Decisions made by | Owners or their managers | Government or the managers it appoints |

Remember:

- Size does not decide ownership. A tiny council library is public; a giant shareholder-owned airline is private.
- Aims are tendencies, not rules. A private business may have social aims; a public organisation must still keep within its budget.
- A private firm can work *for* the government (building a school, collecting rubbish) and still be private sector.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary sector | Businesses that extract, grow or rear natural resources |
| Secondary sector | Businesses that manufacture, process or build, turning materials into goods |
| Tertiary sector | Businesses that provide services |
| Private sector | Organisations owned and controlled by individuals or groups of individuals |
| Public sector | Organisations owned and controlled by national or local government |
| Chain of production | The stages a product passes through, from raw material to final customer, across the sectors |
| Interdependence | The way businesses in each sector rely on businesses in the other sectors |

## Exam traps

- "Serves the public" is not a definition of the public sector. Private shops serve the public too.
- Do not call a builder tertiary or a garage secondary.
- Do not write "the government sector" and stop. Say national or local government owns and controls it.
- In a case study, check the ownership details. A business described as "owned by its founder" or "a public limited company" is private sector, even though "public" appears in the name of the legal form.
- When asked to explain, tie the sector to an activity in the data, then say why it matters, such as a link to a supplier or customer in another sector.

## Command words for this topic

- **Identify / state:** one label per organisation; two if both classifications are asked for.
- **Define:** say what the sector does or who owns it, in one precise sentence.
- **Outline:** give the main points briefly, such as the activity and the resulting sector.
- **Explain:** label, then link to the data, then give the consequence.
- **Consider / justify:** weigh both sides, such as private versus public provision of a service, and reach a decision with a reason.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three sectors classified by type of activity.
2. Which sector is a road-building contractor in?
3. Which sector is a fish farm in?
4. A garage repairs and services cars. Which activity sector is it in?
5. Define the public sector.
6. State one usual difference in aims between private and public sector organisations.
7. A large company is owned by thousands of shareholders. Is it private or public sector?
8. A coffee grower roasts and packs its own beans. Which two activity sectors does it work in?
9. Give one way a secondary sector business depends on the tertiary sector.
10. A council-run swimming pool: state its activity sector and its ownership sector.
11. Explain why a public limited company is in the private sector.
12. Explain one way a poor harvest could affect a secondary sector business.

### Answers

1. Primary, secondary and tertiary.
2. Secondary, because construction is a building activity.
3. Primary, because rearing fish is a primary activity.
4. Tertiary, because repairs are a service.
5. Organisations owned and controlled by government, national or local.
6. Private sector organisations usually aim mainly for profit; public sector organisations usually aim mainly to provide a service to the public.
7. Private sector, because it is owned by individuals (shareholders), not by government.
8. Primary (growing) and secondary (roasting and packing).
9. Any one, for example it needs transport firms to deliver its goods, banks for loans, or shops to sell its products.
10. Tertiary (it provides a leisure service) and public (a local council owns and runs it).
11. Its shares are owned by private individuals or groups of investors, not by the government, so it is private sector; the word "public" refers to its shares being available to the public to buy.
12. The business would find its raw material scarcer, so it might pay more for it, which raises its costs, or it might not get enough to keep production going.
