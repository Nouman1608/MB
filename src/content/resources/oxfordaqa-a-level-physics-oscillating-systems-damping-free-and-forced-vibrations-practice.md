## Questions

**1.** A 0.40 kg mass hangs on a spring of stiffness 64 N m⁻¹. Calculate the period and frequency of its vertical oscillations. **[3]**

**2.** A simple pendulum on the Moon (g = 1.62 m s⁻²) must have a period of 3.0 s. Calculate its length. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** A mass-spring system has a period of 0.90 s. The mass is increased to four times its value. State the new period. **[2]**
**(b)** A pendulum has a period of 2.4 s. Its length is reduced to one-ninth of the original. State the new period. **[2]**

**4.** A pendulum bob of mass 0.080 kg is released from rest with its centre 2.5 cm above its lowest point.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum kinetic energy and the maximum speed. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how the kinetic energy, potential energy and total energy vary with time during one oscillation. **[3]**

**5.** The amplitude of a damped oscillator falls from 9.0 cm to 5.0 cm in 8 oscillations. The energy is proportional to the amplitude squared.

**(a)** Calculate the fraction of the original energy that remains. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the lost energy goes and the effect of light damping on the period. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the motion of a displaced system that is **(a)** lightly damped, **(b)** critically damped, **(c)** heavily damped. **[3]**
**(d)** Name one device that relies on critical damping. **[1]**

**7.** A trolley on springs has a natural frequency of 1.5 Hz. A motor shakes its base at an adjustable frequency.

**(a)** Once the motion has settled, state the frequency of the trolley when the motor is at 2.5 Hz. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the effect when the motor is at 1.5 Hz and describe the amplitude. **[2]**
**(c)** Friction pads are now added to the trolley. Describe two changes to the amplitude-frequency graph. **[2]**

**8.** A loose panel in a car has a natural frequency of 25 Hz. The engine shakes the car once per revolution.

**(a)** Calculate the engine speed, in revolutions per minute, at which the panel rattles most. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the rattling is greatest at this speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to reduce the shaking. **[1]**

**9.** A string of length 0.60 m is fixed at both ends. The speed of waves on it is 48 m s⁻¹. A vibration generator drives it.

**(a)** Calculate the lowest driving frequency that gives resonance. **[2]**
**(b)** State the next two resonant frequencies. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the amplitude is small at 60 Hz. **[2]**
**(d)** A 680 Hz tuning fork is held over a tube closed at the bottom. Calculate the two shortest air column lengths at which resonance occurs. Ignore end effects. **[3]**

**10.** In a pendulum investigation, a student measures the time for 10 oscillations as 18.9 s, with an uncertainty of 0.2 s, for a length of 0.85 m measured from the pivot to the top of the bob.

**(a)** Explain why 10 oscillations are timed, and say where timing should start and why. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate T, and the percentage uncertainties in T and T². **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the value of g from these results. **[2]**
**(d)** The percentage difference from 9.81 m s⁻² is larger than the uncertainty in T². Suggest one systematic error that explains this. **[1]**

**11.** *International A-level only.* A 0.40 kg mass oscillates on a spring of stiffness 90 N m⁻¹ with amplitude 0.030 m.

**(a)** Calculate ω and the period. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total energy. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed at a displacement of 0.018 m. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** T = 2π√(m/k) [1]. T = 2π√(0.40/64) = **0.50 s** [1]. f = 1/T = **2.0 Hz** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the formula and the substitution gives a clear route to credit even if the arithmetic slips.

**2.** Rearrange: l = gT²/4π² [1]. l = 1.62 × 9.0 / 39.48 [1] = **0.37 m** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearranging before substituting shows the algebra step separately. Using T instead of T² is an easy slip.

**3. (a)** T ∝ √m [1], so T doubles to **1.8 s** [1]
**(b)** T ∝ √l, so T is multiplied by √(1/9) = 1/3 [1], giving **0.80 s** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the proportionality used. A bare number can be right, but the reasoning is what shows understanding.

**4. (a)** Maximum Ep = mgh = 0.080 × 9.81 × 0.025 = 0.0196 J [1]. Maximum Ek equals this at the lowest point, **0.020 J** [1]. v = √(2Ek/m) = **0.70 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The total energy stays constant (no energy lost) [1]. Ek and Ep each vary between zero and 0.020 J, with Ek maximum when Ep is zero [1]. Each completes two cycles in one oscillation, so the frequency is double that of the motion [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A good graph description names the frequency relationship, the maximum value and the phase of the two curves. A sketch with labelled axes supports it.

**5. (a)** Energy ratio = (5.0/9.0)² [1] = **0.31** [1]
**(b)** Thermal energy of the surroundings (air or the suspension) [1]. The period is almost unchanged [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Lost" is not enough for the energy. Say it is transferred to thermal energy of the surroundings by resistive forces.

**6.** **(a)** It oscillates, with the amplitude falling each cycle [1]. **(b)** It settles at equilibrium in the least possible time, with no oscillation [1]. **(c)** It returns slowly without oscillating [1]. **(d)** Car suspension, or a door closer [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Critical and heavy damping both avoid oscillation. What separates them is the return time, so say that.

**7. (a)** **2.5 Hz** (the motor's frequency) [1]
**(b)** Resonance [1]. The amplitude is a maximum [1]
**(c)** The peak is lower [1] and broader (the peak also moves slightly lower in frequency) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For part (c) give two distinct changes. "Lower" and "smaller" count as one idea.

**8. (a)** 25 revolutions per second [1] × 60 = **1500 rev min⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** At 1500 rev min⁻¹ the engine's frequency matches the panel's natural frequency, which is resonance [1]. The energy transferred to the panel is greatest, so the amplitude is large [1]
**(c)** Add damping (a rubber pad), or stiffen the panel to change its natural frequency [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the two frequencies explicitly. Writing "resonance" without saying what is equal is usually incomplete.

**9. (a)** λ = 2L = 1.2 m [1]. f = v/λ = **40 Hz** [1]
**(b)** **80 Hz and 120 Hz** [1]
**(c)** 60 Hz is not a natural frequency of the string, so no stationary wave fits [1] and energy is transferred poorly from the driver [1]
**(d)** λ = 340/680 = 0.50 m [1]. Shortest column: L = λ/4 = **0.125 m** [1]. Next: 3λ/4 = **0.375 m** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For a closed tube, explain that the closed end is a node and the open end an antinode. Using λ/2 for the tube is an easy slip.

**10. (a)** Timing 10 oscillations reduces the percentage uncertainty from reaction time and resolution [1]. Start at equilibrium, where the bob moves fastest and the position is easiest to judge (use a fiducial marker) [1]
**(b)** T = 18.9/10 = **1.89 s** [1]. Percentage uncertainty in T = 0.2/18.9 = **1.1%** [1]; in T² = **2.1%** [1]
**(c)** g = 4π²l/T² = 39.48 × 0.85 / 1.89² [1] = **9.4 m s⁻²** [1]
**(d)** The length was measured to the top of the bob, so it is too short (it should go to the centre), making g too small [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A systematic error shifts every result the same way; compare the size of the discrepancy with the uncertainty before blaming random error.

**11. (a)** ω = √(k/m) = √(90/0.40) = **15 rad s⁻¹** [1]. T = 2π/ω = **0.42 s** [1]
**(b)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 90 × 0.030² [1] = **0.0405 J** [1]
**(c)** v = ω√(A² − x²) [1] = 15 × √(0.030² − 0.018²) [1] = 15 × 0.024 = **0.36 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** v max = ωA = 15 × 0.030 = **0.45 m s⁻¹** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) can also be done by subtracting Ep from the total. Either route is valid when each step is shown.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring the pendulum length to the top of the bob, or forgetting the units.
- Failing to rearrange for l or m when squaring, or squaring only one side.
- Saying amplitude or bob mass changes a pendulum period.
- Describing energy against time without the doubled frequency.
- Saying "energy is lost" without stating it becomes thermal energy.
- Confusing critical damping with heavy damping.
- Writing "resonance occurs at high frequency" rather than equal frequencies.
- Using λ/2 rather than λ/4 for a closed tube.
- Giving a graph gradient without the 4π² step.
