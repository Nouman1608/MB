These notes condense section 21.1 of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028: why bacteria are useful in biotechnology and genetic modification. Core statement 21.1.1 is tested on every theory and multiple-choice paper. Supplement statement 21.1.2, marked "Extended only", is tested only on Papers 2 and 4. For full explanations and a model answer, use the [section 21.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification-cambridge-igcse-biology/); to practise, use the [section 21.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## The four reasons in one line each

| Reason | Tier | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid reproduction rate | Core | A large population, and a large amount of product, in a short time |
| Ability to make complex molecules | Core | Bacteria build proteins (including enzymes) and, once modified, human proteins such as insulin |
| Few ethical concerns over manipulation and growth | Extended only | Single cells with no nervous system; far fewer objections than using animals |
| Presence of plasmids | Extended only | Small DNA loops that can be cut, given a new gene and put back into bacteria |

Learn the Core wording "rapid reproduction rate" and "ability to make complex molecules" exactly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biotechnology | Using living organisms, usually microorganisms, to make useful products |
| Genetic modification | Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes |
| Bacterium | Single-celled organism with a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, circular DNA and plasmids, but no nucleus |
| Circular DNA | The main loop of DNA in a bacterial cell, lying free in the cytoplasm |
| Plasmid | Small circular loop of DNA, separate from the main circular DNA |
| Ribosome | Structure in the cytoplasm where proteins are made |
| Complex molecule | Large molecule built from smaller units, such as a protein built from amino acids |
| Asexual reproduction | Reproduction from one parent, giving genetically identical offspring |
| Recombinant plasmid | Plasmid that contains DNA from another organism, such as a human gene |

## Condensed recall

### Core: rapid reproduction rate

- Bacteria reproduce asexually: one cell divides into two.
- In good conditions numbers double at regular intervals, so the population grows very fast.
- Consequence: a large amount of product in a short time, at a lower cost than slower-growing organisms.
- Offspring are genetically identical, so if one cell carries a useful gene, all its descendants do.

### Core: ability to make complex molecules

- Bacteria have ribosomes and enzymes that build large molecules from small ones.
- A gene is a length of DNA coding for a protein; a bacterium will make the protein coded for by any gene it carries.
- So bacteria can be used to make molecules they would not make naturally, once the gene is inserted.
- Only genetically modified bacteria make human insulin. Penicillin comes from a fungus, not a bacterium.

### Extended only: few ethical concerns

- No nervous system, so no pain or suffering in the way that arises with animals.
- Not kept like farm or laboratory animals, so welfare objections largely do not apply.
- Huge numbers can be grown, modified and destroyed with few objections.
- "Few", not "none": some people object to any GM, and modified microorganisms must not escape, so fermenters are sealed and sterilised.

### Extended only: presence of plasmids

- Plasmids can be cut open with restriction enzymes and a gene inserted; DNA ligase rejoins the DNA.
- The recombinant plasmid carries the gene into a bacterium.
- When the bacterium divides, the plasmid is copied, so every new cell has the gene.
- The bacterium expresses the gene and makes the protein.

## Doubling calculations

- Number of doublings = total time ÷ doubling time.
- Final number = starting number × 2^(number of doublings).

*Illustrative example:* 500 bacteria, doubling every 20 minutes, for 2 hours. Doublings = 120 ÷ 20 = 6. Final number = 500 × 2⁶ = 500 × 64 = **32 000**.

Check units first: convert hours to minutes (or the other way) before dividing.

## Linking chains to learn

- **Rapid reproduction chain:** reproduce quickly → large population quickly → large quantity of product quickly → product made cheaply.
- **Plasmid chain:** plasmid cut → human gene inserted → recombinant plasmid put into bacterium → bacterium divides, copying plasmid → all offspring make the human protein.

## Exam traps

- **No nucleus.** Never write that the gene is inserted "into the nucleus" of a bacterium.
- **Plasmid is not the main DNA.** It is small, circular and separate.
- **"Few" ethical concerns.** Not "no" ethical concerns.
- **Feature without reason.** "Bacteria have plasmids" alone rarely gains more than one mark; say what plasmids allow.
- **Core versus Extended.** A Core question expects rapid reproduction and complex molecules. Extended answers can add ethics and plasmids.
- **Organism mix-ups.** Penicillin and mycoprotein come from fungi. Yeast is a fungus, not a bacterium.
- **Linear growth.** Doubling is not adding the same number each time; 1000 → 2000 → 4000 → 8000.

## Command words

- **State:** short answer, no explanation needed.
- **Explain:** feature plus reason.
- **Suggest:** apply the four features to an unfamiliar situation, such as comparing bacteria with plants or animals.
- **Describe:** give features, for example of a plasmid in a cell diagram: small, circular loop of DNA, separate from the main DNA.

## Quick self-test

1. State the two Core reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology.
2. Name the structure in a bacterial cell where proteins are made.
3. Where is the main DNA of a bacterium found?
4. Describe a plasmid.
5. Why are all the bacteria in a fermenter able to make the same product if they came from one modified cell?
6. Give one reason why using bacteria raises few ethical concerns. (Extended)
7. Explain why plasmids make bacteria useful in genetic modification. (Extended)
8. A student writes, "Bacteria make penicillin." Correct the statement.
9. A culture of 300 bacteria doubles every 25 minutes. How many bacteria are present after 100 minutes?
10. Why does the growth of bacteria in a fermenter eventually slow down if conditions are not controlled?
11. Give one ethical concern that remains when bacteria are genetically modified. (Extended)
12. Suggest why bacteria are used instead of a crop plant to produce a human protein.

### Answers

1. Rapid reproduction rate; ability to make complex molecules.
2. Ribosomes.
3. As a single loop of circular DNA in the cytoplasm (there is no nucleus).
4. A small circular loop of DNA, separate from the main circular DNA.
5. They were produced by asexual reproduction, so they are genetically identical and all carry the inserted gene.
6. Bacteria have no nervous system, so they cannot suffer (or: their manipulation and growth raise far fewer objections than using animals).
7. A plasmid can be cut open and a gene inserted; the recombinant plasmid is put into a bacterium, copied when it divides, and the bacterium makes the protein coded for by the gene.
8. Penicillin is made by a fungus, not by bacteria.
9. 100 ÷ 25 = 4 doublings; 300 × 2⁴ = 300 × 16 = **4800**.
10. Nutrients are used up and waste products build up, which can change the pH or become toxic.
11. Some people object to changing any organism's genes, or worry about modified bacteria escaping.
12. Bacteria reproduce much more rapidly than plants, so a large amount of protein is produced in a much shorter time (they also have plasmids that can carry the gene).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating "bacteria grow quickly" without linking it to the quantity of product.
- Placing plasmids or inserted genes in a nucleus.
- Treating a plasmid as the main chromosome.
- Writing "no ethical concerns".
- Using fungal products as bacterial examples.
- Calculating doubling as a steady increase rather than repeated multiplication by 2.
