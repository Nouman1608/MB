**These notes condense Cambridge 9489 AS topic 9, International history 1929-39: the rise of extremism and the road to war, for fast recall.** The key questions are the effect of extremism on international relations (9.1), the League's failure (9.2), appeasement (9.3) and the outbreak of war in 1939 (9.4). Full explanations are in the [International history 1929-39 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-international-history-1929-39/); exam practice is in the [International history 1929-39 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-international-history-1929-39-practice/).

**Rotation (2027-2029 syllabus):** Paper 2 (Outline Study) in 2027 and 2028; Paper 1 (Historical Sources) in 2029.

## How the Depression changed four powers

| Power | Political effect | International effect |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | Collapse of Weimar coalition politics; Nazis largest party (1932); Hitler Chancellor (1933) | Rearmament; exit from League and Disarmament Conference |
| Japan | Party government discredited; assassinations; army dominant | Manchuria (1931); exit from League (1933) |
| Italy | Pressure on Mussolini to restore prestige | Abyssinia (1935); move towards Germany |
| USA | Isolationism strengthened | Neutrality Acts; no role in collective security |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Extremism | Political movements rejecting democracy: fascism, Nazism, militarism, communism |
| Militarism | Dominance of the armed forces and military values in politics |
| Isolationism | US policy of avoiding foreign commitments |
| Equality of rights | German demand to be treated as other powers on armaments |
| Collective security | Joint action by League members against aggression |
| Sanctions | Economic penalties imposed on an aggressor |
| Non-intervention | Agreement not to supply either side in Spain |
| International Brigades | Foreign volunteers who fought for the Spanish Republic |
| Appeasement | Avoiding war by negotiating concessions to grievances |
| Ten-Year Rule | British assumption of no major war within ten years (dropped 1932) |
| Maginot Line | French defensive fortifications on the German border |
| *Lebensraum* | "Living space" in eastern Europe for Germans |
| Secret protocol | Hidden part of the Nazi-Soviet Pact dividing eastern Europe |

## The League's two great tests

| | Manchuria, 1931-33 | Abyssinia, 1935-36 |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressor | Japan (Kwantung Army) | Italy |
| League action | Lytton Commission; report adopted | Aggressor named; partial sanctions |
| What was missing | Any sanctions; US membership | Oil sanctions; closure of Suez |
| Great-power behaviour | Britain and France unwilling to act in Asia | Hoare-Laval Plan undermined sanctions |
| Result | Japan left the League | Conquest completed; sanctions lifted |

## Appeasement -- motives, actions, verdict

- **Motives (Britain):** Depression and cost; the Ten-Year Rule's legacy; public opinion (Oxford Union, Peace Ballot); fear of bombing; Versailles thought unfair; fascism as a barrier to communism; imperial commitments.
- **Motives (France):** economic weakness; political division; fear of communism; Popular Front (1936); Maginot defensive strategy; dependence on Britain.
- **Actions:** Naval Agreement (1935); Rhineland (1936); Anschluss (1938); Munich (1938); response to Prague (1939).
- **Successes:** war postponed; time for rearmament; public and Dominion consent in 1939.
- **Failures:** dictators gained resources and confidence; opposition grew; the USSR was alienated.

## Exam traps

- **Anti-Comintern Pact v. Pact of Steel.** The first (1936) was against communism; the second (May 1939) was a German-Italian military alliance.
- **Abyssinia and Stresa.** British and French hesitation over sanctions came partly from the wish to keep Italy in the Stresa Front.
- **Spain.** The question is about international responses, not the battles.
- **Munich.** Czechoslovakia was not represented, and the USSR was not invited.
- **1939.** The guarantee to Poland was given in March; war came in September after the Nazi-Soviet Pact.

## Self-test

1. Why did Japan's government become more militarist after 1930? *The Depression discredited party government, and assassinations by nationalist officers left the army dominant.*
2. What was France's position at the Disarmament Conference? *Security must come before French disarmament.*
3. What did the Stresa Front agree? *To oppose German rearmament and uphold Locarno and Austrian independence.*
4. Which two events broke the Stresa Front? *The Anglo-German Naval Agreement and Italy's invasion of Abyssinia.*
5. What did the Lytton Report recommend? *Japanese withdrawal and an autonomous Manchuria under Chinese sovereignty.*
6. Name two goods left out of the sanctions on Italy. *Any two: oil, coal, steel.*
7. What was the Hoare-Laval Plan? *A secret Anglo-French proposal to give Italy much of Abyssinia.*
8. Why did the Non-Intervention Agreement fail? *Germany, Italy and the USSR intervened, and Britain and France did not enforce it.*
9. What did the Peace Ballot suggest? *Strong public support for the League and disarmament.*
10. What did the Anglo-German Naval Agreement allow? *A German fleet of 35 per cent of British surface strength.*
11. Give two reasons for the Nazi-Soviet Pact from Stalin's point of view. *Any two: distrust of Britain and France after Munich; slow Anglo-French talks; buying time; territory in eastern Europe.*
12. What two German demands on Poland led to war? *Danzig and a route across the Polish Corridor.*
