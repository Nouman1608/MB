These notes condense **3.1.2 Limitation of physical measurements** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1, and its methods are used in every practical question, including Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [limitation of physical measurements study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-limitation-of-physical-measurements-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions, use the [limitation of physical measurements practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-limitation-of-physical-measurements-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Identify random and systematic errors and suggest how to remove them.
- Precision, repeatability, reproducibility, resolution and accuracy.
- Absolute, fractional and percentage uncertainties in a final answer.
- Combine uncertainties for sums, differences, products, quotients and powers (not trigonometric or logarithmic functions).
- Error bars; uncertainty in gradient and intercept, with or without error bars.
- Significant figures linked to uncertainty.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Random error | Unpredictable scatter of readings about the true value |
| Systematic error | A shift of every reading in the same direction |
| Zero error | Instrument reads non-zero when the true value is zero; a systematic error |
| Precise | Little spread about the mean; depends only on random errors |
| Repeatable | Same experimenter, method and equipment give the same results |
| Reproducible | A different experimenter, technique or equipment gives the same results |
| Resolution | Smallest change in the quantity that gives a perceptible change in the reading |
| Accurate | Close to the true value |
| Absolute uncertainty | The ± range in the units of the quantity |
| Fractional uncertainty | Absolute uncertainty ÷ value |
| Percentage uncertainty | Fractional uncertainty × 100 |

## Errors: cause and cure

| Error | Typical cause | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Random | Judging a moving pointer or a passing object; small fluctuations | Repeat and average; measure a larger quantity; line of best fit |
| Systematic | Zero error; consistent parallax; uncalibrated meter | Correct zero; calibrate; read at eye level; change method |

Repeats improve precision, not accuracy. A systematic error can show up as an unexpected intercept on a graph.

## Uncertainty of a reading

- Analogue scale: ± half a division per reading; a length from two rule readings: ±1 mm.
- Digital: ± the last digit.
- Repeats: ± half the range about the mean; never quote less than the resolution.

## Combining rules

| Calculation | Combine |
|---|---|
| a + b, a − b | Absolute uncertainties add |
| ab, a/b | Percentage uncertainties add |
| aⁿ | n × percentage uncertainty |
| ka (k exact) | Percentage unchanged |

## Graph method in steps

1. Plot points with error bars.
2. Draw the line of best fit.
3. Draw the steepest and shallowest lines through all error bars (or through the scatter, if there are no error bars).
4. Gradient uncertainty = (steepest − shallowest)/2; or best minus worst acceptable. State which.
5. Intercept uncertainty: the same method using the worst lines' intercepts.
6. If the intercept range includes the value theory predicts (often zero), there is no evidence of systematic error.

## Significant figures

- Uncertainty to 1 s.f. (occasionally 2).
- Round the value to the same decimal place as the uncertainty: 9.7234 ± 0.3 becomes 9.7 ± 0.3.
- No stated uncertainty: match the least precise data value.

## Worked reminders

- **Fractional and percentage.** l = 82.0 ± 0.5 cm: fractional 0.0061, percentage 0.61%.
- **Ratio.** Speed from s = 1.500 ± 0.002 m and t = 0.84 ± 0.02 s: 0.13% + 2.4% = 2.5%; v = 1.79 ± 0.04 m s⁻¹. The timing dominates.
- **Square.** Kinetic energy ½mv² with m ± 0.5% and v ± 1.5%: 0.5 + 2 × 1.5 = 3.5%.

## Exam traps

- "Repeating reduces systematic error": wrong.
- "Reliable" is not a specification term; use repeatable, reproducible or precise.
- Adding percentages for a difference: add absolute values instead.
- Forgetting the power when a quantity is squared or cubed.
- Uncertainty quoted to more figures than its value warrants.
- Worst lines that miss some error bars.
- Naming an error without its effect or a specific remedy.
- Calling an error "human error" without saying whether it is random or systematic.
- Giving an improvement that does not tackle the largest uncertainty.
- Combining percentage uncertainties but forgetting to convert back to an absolute value for the final answer.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a precise set of measurements.
2. What is the difference between repeatable and reproducible?
3. State the resolution of a micrometer screw gauge.
4. A length is 4.50 ± 0.02 cm. Give its fractional and percentage uncertainty.
5. Four timings are 3.42 s, 3.38 s, 3.47 s and 3.41 s. Give the mean with its uncertainty.
6. Two lengths of 25.0 ± 0.1 cm and 13.5 ± 0.1 cm are joined end to end. Give the total length with uncertainty.
7. A rectangle measures 8.0 ± 0.1 cm by 5.0 ± 0.1 cm. Find its area with absolute uncertainty.
8. A quantity y = a³. If a has a 0.7% uncertainty, what is the percentage uncertainty in y?
9. Worst lines on a graph have gradients 1.84 and 1.62. Find the uncertainty in the gradient.
10. Round 0.048276 ± 0.002 correctly.
11. A balance reads 0.03 g with nothing on it. Name the error type and give the remedy.
12. Explain why drawing a line of best fit reduces the effect of random errors.

### Answers

1. Repeated readings have very little spread about their mean.
2. **Repeatable**: same person, method and equipment; **reproducible**: different person, technique or equipment.
3. **0.01 mm**.
4. 0.02/4.50 = **0.0044**; **0.44%**.
5. Mean 13.68/4 = 3.42 s; half range (3.47 − 3.38)/2 = 0.045 s; **3.42 ± 0.05 s**.
6. **38.5 ± 0.2 cm** (absolute uncertainties add).
7. 1.25% + 2.0% = 3.25%; 40.0 cm² × 0.0325 = 1.3; **40 ± 1 cm²**.
8. 3 × 0.7 = **2.1%**.
9. (1.84 − 1.62)/2 = **0.11**.
10. **0.048 ± 0.002**.
11. **Systematic** (zero error); subtract 0.03 g from each reading or re-zero (tare) the balance.
12. The line averages the scatter of points either side of it, so no single random error controls the result.
