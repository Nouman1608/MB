Constant: ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹.

## Equations

| Equation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C = Q/V | Any capacitor | Q is the charge on one plate |
| C = Aε₀εr/d | Parallel plate capacitor | A = overlap area; d in metres |
| εr = C(with dielectric)/C(vacuum) | Measuring relative permittivity | No unit |
| E = ½QV = ½CV² = ½Q²/C | Energy stored | Area under the Q–V graph |
| ΔE = ½C(V₂² − V₁²) | Energy added between two p.d.s | Not ½C(ΔV)² |

Prefixes: μF = 10⁻⁶ F, nF = 10⁻⁹ F, pF = 10⁻¹² F. 1 F = 1 C V⁻¹.

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Capacitance | Charge stored per unit p.d. across the capacitor, C = Q/V |
| Farad | Capacitance that stores 1 C per 1 V |
| Relative permittivity εr | Ratio of the permittivity of a material to that of free space; the factor by which C rises when the material fills the gap |
| Dielectric constant | Another name for the relative permittivity of a dielectric |
| Dielectric | Insulating material between the plates |
| Polar molecule | Molecule with a permanent separation of positive and negative charge |

## Condensed recall

- Q ∝ V for a capacitor: the Q–V graph is a straight line through the origin, gradient C.
- C rises with A and εr; C falls as d rises.
- Air is treated as a vacuum, εr = 1.
- Stored energy is the area under the Q–V graph: a triangle, so ½QV.
- The supply does work QV; half is stored, the rest is dissipated in the circuit resistance during charging.
- Disconnected capacitor: Q is fixed. Connected capacitor: V is fixed.

## Dielectric action in six steps

1. Polar molecules point randomly with no field.
2. The field exerts opposite forces on the two charged ends: a couple.
3. The couple rotates the molecules into partial alignment with the field.
4. Negative ends face the positive plate; positive ends face the negative plate.
5. These surface layers set up a field opposing the applied field, so the resultant field falls.
6. For the same Q, V is smaller, so C = Q/V is larger.

## What changes when you alter the capacitor

| Change | Disconnected (Q fixed) | Connected (V fixed) |
|---|---|---|
| d doubled | C halves, V doubles, energy doubles | C halves, Q halves, energy halves |
| Dielectric εr inserted | C × εr, V ÷ εr, energy ÷ εr | C × εr, Q × εr, energy × εr |
| Overlap area halved | C halves, V doubles, energy doubles | C halves, Q halves, energy halves |

## Worked reminders

- 47 μF at 12 V: Q = **5.6 × 10⁻⁴ C**; E = ½ × 47 × 10⁻⁶ × 144 = **3.4 × 10⁻³ J**.
- Plates 0.15 m × 0.10 m, 0.50 mm apart, εr = 3.0: C = 0.015 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 3.0 / 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **8.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ F**.
- 470 μF from 5.0 V to 15 V: ΔE = ½ × 470 × 10⁻⁶ × (225 − 25) = **0.047 J**.

## Exam traps

- Using total charge (zero) instead of the charge on one plate.
- Forgetting to convert d from mm or C from μF.
- Using ½C(ΔV)² for an increase that does not start from zero.
- Writing that the dielectric molecules "become polarised by gaining charge". Polar molecules already have charged ends; the field rotates them.
- Saying the field inside the dielectric is zero.
- Holding Q constant for a capacitor still connected to its supply.

## Self-test

1. Define capacitance and give its unit.
2. A 220 μF capacitor stores 2.2 mC. Find the p.d.
3. What is the gradient of a Q–V graph for a capacitor?
4. A capacitor is 330 pF in air and 1.32 nF with a slab filling the gap. Find εr.
5. Which three changes would each increase the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor?
6. Why is the energy stored ½QV and not QV?
7. Find the energy stored when 3.0 mC is on a 50 μF capacitor.
8. A 9.0 V battery charges a 100 μF capacitor fully. How much work does the battery do, and how much energy is stored?
9. An isolated charged capacitor has its plate separation tripled. What happens to its stored energy?
10. Where does the extra energy in question 9 come from?
11. In dielectric action, what produces the turning effect on each molecule?
12. A capacitor stays connected to a battery while a dielectric of εr = 2.5 is inserted. By what factor does the charge change?

### Answers

1. Charge stored per unit p.d., C = Q/V; the **farad** (C V⁻¹).
2. V = 2.2 × 10⁻³ / 220 × 10⁻⁶ = **10 V**.
3. The capacitance C.
4. 1320/330 = **4.0**.
5. Larger overlap area, smaller separation, dielectric of higher εr.
6. The p.d. rises from zero to V as charge is added, so the average p.d. is V/2; the area under the Q–V graph is a triangle.
7. ½Q²/C = (3.0 × 10⁻³)² / (2 × 50 × 10⁻⁶) = **0.090 J**.
8. Work = QV = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.0 = **8.1 × 10⁻³ J**; stored = **4.1 × 10⁻³ J**.
9. C falls to a third and Q is fixed, so ½Q²/C **triples**.
10. Work done by the person pulling the oppositely charged, attracting plates apart.
11. A couple: equal and opposite forces on the two charged ends of the molecule.
12. C rises by 2.5 at fixed V, so Q rises by a factor of **2.5**.
