These questions cover section **3.10.4 Non-ionising imaging** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408): ultrasound, fibre optics and endoscopy, and the MR scanner. It is part of the A-level only Medical physics option. Learn the content first in the [non-ionising imaging study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-non-ionising-imaging-aqa-alevel/) and the [non-ionising imaging revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-non-ionising-imaging-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

Data: speed of ultrasound in soft tissue c = 1540 m s⁻¹. Tissue impedances are illustrative values for practice.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the piezoelectric effect. **[2]**

**2.** Muscle has density 1080 kg m⁻³ and the speed of ultrasound in it is 1580 m s⁻¹. Calculate its acoustic impedance. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why ultrasound gives poor images of structures inside the adult skull. **[2]**

**4.** State the function of the coherent and of the non-coherent fibre bundle in a flexible endoscope. **[2]**

**5.** Give two advantages to the patient of keyhole surgery using an endoscope. **[2]**

**6.** In an MR scanner, name the particles whose spin is used to form the image, and state the type of signal they emit as they de-excite. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** An ultrasound pulse passes from fat (Z = 1.38 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹) into muscle (Z = 1.71 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹), then meets bone (Z = 7.0 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹).

**(a)** Calculate the fraction of the intensity reflected at the fat–muscle boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction reflected at the muscle–bone boundary. **[1]**
**(c)** Ignoring attenuation, calculate the fraction of the original intensity that returns into the fat as the echo from the bone. **[2]**

**8.** An A-scan of a patient's eye shows echoes at 28 µs and 62 µs after the pulse is sent. Take the speed of ultrasound in the eye as 1540 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the depth of each reflecting surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance between the two surfaces. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the ultrasound pulse must be short. **[2]**

**9.** A scanner can use probes of frequency 2.0 MHz or 12 MHz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength in soft tissue at each frequency. **[2]**
**(b)** State and explain which probe should be used to image an organ about 15 cm below the skin, and which for a structure just under the skin. **[3]**

**10.** A probe images structures up to 0.15 m deep.

**(a)** Calculate the round-trip time for an echo from 0.15 m. **[1]**
**(b)** Hence calculate the maximum number of pulses that can be sent each second. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how a piezoelectric transducer generates an ultrasound pulse and detects its echo. **[3]**

**11.** An optical fibre in an endoscope has a core of refractive index 1.53 and a cladding of refractive index 1.48.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle at the core–cladding boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons why the fibre has a cladding. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the endoscope forms an image of the inside of the stomach that a doctor can view. **[3]**

**12.** Describe the basic principles of how an MR scanner produces an image of a cross-section of a patient. **[6]**

**13.** A pregnant patient needs a scan of her fetus. Compare ultrasound and MR for this scan in terms of resolution, convenience and safety. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A piezoelectric crystal changes shape (deforms) when a p.d. is applied across it [1]; and produces a p.d. across it when it is deformed [1]

**2.** Z = ρc = 1080 × 1580 [1]; = **1.71 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹** [1]

**3.** The acoustic impedance of bone is very different from that of soft tissue [1]; so most of the ultrasound is reflected at the skull, and too little reaches and returns from the brain to form a clear image [1]

**4.** Coherent bundle: carries the image from inside the body to the eyepiece or camera [1]; non-coherent bundle: carries light from the source into the body to illuminate the area [1]

**5.** Only small incisions are needed [1]; so recovery is faster with less pain, scarring or risk of infection [1]

**6.** Hydrogen nuclei (protons) [1]; radio-frequency (RF) signals [1]

**7. (a)** I_r/I_i = ((1.71 − 1.38)/(1.71 + 1.38))² [1]; = **0.011** [1]
**(b)** ((7.0 − 1.71)/(7.0 + 1.71))² = **0.37** [1]
**(c)** Fraction = 0.989 × 0.369 × 0.989 [1]; = **0.36** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The echo crosses the fat–muscle boundary twice, so the transmitted fraction appears twice in (c).

**8. (a)** d = ct/2: 1540 × 28 × 10⁻⁶/2 = **0.022 m** [1]; 1540 × 62 × 10⁻⁶/2 = **0.048 m** [1]
**(b)** 0.0477 − 0.0216 = **0.026 m** (26 mm) [1]
**(c)** A long pulse would still be being emitted (or received) when the echo from a nearby surface returns [1]; so echoes from close surfaces would overlap and could not be separated; short pulses improve depth resolution [1]

**9. (a)** λ = c/f: 1540/2.0 × 10⁶ = **7.7 × 10⁻⁴ m** [1]; 1540/12 × 10⁶ = **1.3 × 10⁻⁴ m** [1]
**(b)** 2.0 MHz for the deep organ: attenuation is lower at lower frequency, so the pulse penetrates 15 cm and returns [1]; 12 MHz for the shallow structure [1]; its shorter wavelength gives better resolution, and the high attenuation does not matter over a short distance [1]

**10. (a)** t = 2 × 0.15/1540 = **1.9 × 10⁻⁴ s** [1]
**(b)** 1/(1.95 × 10⁻⁴) = **5100 pulses per second** (about 5 × 10³) [1]
**(c)** A short burst of alternating p.d. at the crystal's resonant frequency makes it vibrate and emit ultrasound [1]; backing material damps the vibration so the pulse is short [1]; a returning echo makes the crystal vibrate, generating a small p.d. that is detected and amplified [1]

**11. (a)** sin c = 1.48/1.53 [1]; c = **75.3°** [1]
**(b)** It has a lower refractive index than the core, so total internal reflection occurs at the core–cladding boundary [1]; it protects the core surface from scratches and stops light passing between fibres that touch [1]
**(c)** Light is carried into the stomach by the non-coherent bundle [1]; an objective lens at the end forms an image on the end of the coherent bundle [1]; the coherent fibres keep their relative positions, so the image is carried to an eyepiece or camera for viewing [1]

**12.** The patient lies in the strong field of a superconducting magnet [1]; hydrogen nuclei (protons) align with spins parallel to the field and precess about the field lines [1]; gradient coils vary the field so that one cross-section is selected [1]; short RF pulses excite protons in successive small regions, changing their spin state [1]; the protons emit RF signals as they de-excite [1]; the signals are detected and processed by a computer to give an image [1]

**13.** Resolution: MR gives higher resolution and better soft-tissue detail than ultrasound [1]; convenience: ultrasound is cheap, quick, portable and gives real-time images, while MR is expensive and slow, and the patient must lie still in a confined, noisy scanner [1]; safety: both are non-ionising, so neither carries an ionising radiation risk to the fetus; MR requires the patient to have no metal implants [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using depth = ct instead of ct/2.
- Forgetting that an echo crosses each earlier boundary twice.
- Saying higher frequencies penetrate further.
- Swapping the jobs of coherent and non-coherent bundles.
- Leaving out the gradient coils or the computer in MR descriptions.
