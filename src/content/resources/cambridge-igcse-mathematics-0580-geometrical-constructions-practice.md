These questions cover subtopic 4.2 Geometrical constructions of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is the same for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. You need a ruler, a pair of compasses and a protractor. Leave all construction arcs visible. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) An angle is clearly acute. The two scales on a protractor read 38° and 142° along its second arm. Write down the size of the angle. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** The smaller angle at a point between two lines is 125°. Find the reflex angle. **[1]**

**(b)** Tomas wants to draw a reflex angle of 305°. Write down the angle he should measure with his protractor. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) State whether a triangle can be constructed with these side lengths. Give a reason.

**(a)** 6 cm, 8 cm and 15 cm **[1]**

**(b)** 6 cm, 8 cm and 13 cm **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Describe how to construct a triangle with sides 7 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm using only a ruler and a pair of compasses. **[3]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A net of a cube is a row of four squares A, B, C and D. Square E is attached above A and square F is attached below D. When the net is folded, which face is opposite

**(a)** B, **[1]**

**(b)** E? **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Write down the shapes of the faces in a net of

**(a)** a square-based pyramid, **[1]**

**(b)** a triangular prism. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Using a ruler and a pair of compasses only, construct triangle ABC with AB = 9 cm, BC = 7 cm and AC = 5 cm. **[2]**

**(b)** Measure angle BAC. **[1]**

**8.** (calculator) A rhombus PQRS has sides of 6 cm and diagonal PR = 10 cm.

**(a)** Describe how to construct the rhombus using two triangles. **[3]**

**(b)** The diagonals of a rhombus cross at right angles and bisect each other. Calculate the length of QS that an accurate construction should give. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) Lena wants to construct a parallelogram EFGH with EF = 6 cm, FG = 4 cm and diagonal EG = 8 cm.

**(a)** She draws EG first. State the radius she should use from E, and from G, to find F above EG and H below EG. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what shape she would get if she used the same radius from E above and below EG. **[1]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A net of a cuboid is made of six rectangles: two 8 cm by 5 cm, two 8 cm by 2 cm and two 5 cm by 2 cm.

**(a)** Write down the length, width and height of the cuboid. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the surface area of the cuboid. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the volume of the cuboid. **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) The net of a triangular prism has two right-angled triangles with sides 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm, and three rectangles 15 cm long.

Find the surface area and the volume of the prism. **[4]**

**12.** (calculator) The net of a square-based pyramid is a square of side 10 cm with an isosceles triangle on each side. Each triangle has height 13 cm, measured from the midpoint of its base.

**(a)** Find the surface area of the pyramid. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the vertical height of the pyramid. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the volume of the pyramid. **[1]**

**13.** (non-calculator) Sami is asked to measure the reflex angle XYZ. The angle on the other side of Y measures 62°, and Sami writes down 62°.

**(a)** Explain Sami's mistake. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the correct size of the reflex angle. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **38°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* estimating first shows the answer must be under 90°, which rules out 142°.

**2. (a)** 360° – 125° = **235°** [1]

**(b)** 360° – 305° = **55°** [1]

**3. (a)** **No**: 6 + 8 = 14, which is less than 15 [1]

**(b)** **Yes**: 6 + 8 = 14 is greater than 13 (and each other side is shorter than the sum of the remaining two) [1]

**4.** Draw one side, for example 7 cm, with a ruler [1]; set the compasses to 5 cm and draw an arc from one end, then to 6 cm and draw an arc from the other end so the arcs cross [1]; join the crossing point to both ends and leave the arcs visible [1]

**5. (a)** **D** [1]

**(b)** **F** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in a row of four, faces two apart are opposite; the two squares attached to the row become the top and bottom.

**6. (a)** **One square and four triangles** [1]

**(b)** **Two triangles and three rectangles** [1]

**7. (a)** AB drawn 9 cm long [1]; arcs of radius 5 cm from A and 7 cm from B crossing at C, with the triangle completed and arcs shown [1]

**(b)** About **51°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a protractor reading of about 129° means the wrong scale was used; the angle at A is clearly acute.

**8. (a)** Draw PR = 10 cm [1]; with the compasses set to 6 cm, draw arcs from P and from R above PR, crossing at Q [1]; repeat below PR to find S, then join PQ, QR, RS and SP, keeping all arcs [1]

**(b)** Half of QS = √(6² – 5²) = √11, so QS = 2√11 = **6.63 cm** (about 6.6 cm on a drawing) [1]

**9. (a)** F: **6 cm from E and 4 cm from G** [1]; H: **4 cm from E and 6 cm from G** [1]

**(b)** A **kite** (EF = EH and GF = GH, two pairs of equal adjacent sides) [1]

**10. (a)** **8 cm by 5 cm by 2 cm** [1]

**(b)** 2(40) + 2(16) + 2(10) [1] = **132 cm²** [1]

**(c)** 8 × 5 × 2 = **80 cm³** [1]

**11.** Triangle area = ½ × 5 × 12 = 30 cm² [1]; rectangles = (5 + 12 + 13) × 15 = 450 cm² [1]; surface area = 2 × 30 + 450 = **510 cm²** [1]; volume = 30 × 15 = **450 cm³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the right angle is between the 5 cm and 12 cm sides, so these are the base and height of the triangle, not 13 cm.

**12. (a)** 10 × 10 + 4 × ½ × 10 × 13 [1] = 100 + 260 = **360 cm²** [1]

**(b)** √(13² – 5²) = √144 = **12 cm** [1]

**(c)** ⅓ × 100 × 12 = **400 cm³** [1]

**13. (a)** Sami measured the **smaller** angle at Y instead of the reflex angle [1]

**(b)** 360° – 62° = **298°** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Construction arcs missing or rubbed out.
- Reading the wrong protractor scale.
- Measuring the smaller angle when the reflex one is asked for.
- Swapping the radii when building the second triangle of a parallelogram.
- Using a slanted side instead of the perpendicular height in a triangular face.
- Using the slant height as the vertical height of a pyramid.
