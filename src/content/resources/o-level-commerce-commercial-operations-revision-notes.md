Condensed recall notes for **Topic 2 Commercial Operations**, the
second of six topics in Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100), 2026–2028
series — identical structure to sibling Cambridge IGCSE Commerce
(0715). Use these alongside the full study guide on Topic 2 for
explanation and worked examples — these notes are for fast recall of
the topic's six sub-topics in the final stretch of revision.

## 2.1 Commercial enterprises

- **Private vs public sector**: know the core difference in ownership
  and objective.
- **Private sector types**: sole trader, cooperative, partnership,
  private limited company, public limited company — plus the features
  of **public corporations** and the reasons they exist.
- Be ready to judge the **appropriateness of a given ownership type**
  for a described business scenario, not just define each type.
- **Multinational companies (MNCs)**: their features, why they exist in
  the global economy, and both the **positive and negative impacts** on
  a host country.
- **Franchises**: features, advantages and disadvantages.
- **Location factors**:
  - Physical: labour, markets, transport, power, raw materials,
    competition, suppliers, communication, government influence
  - Ecommerce: wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists, logistics
  - Be able to judge which factors matter most for a given scenario.

## 2.2 Outsourcing

- Growth of outsourcing is driven by **contract manufacturing** and
  **logistics** — know these as the two named growth areas.

## 2.3 Retailing

- **Types of retailer**: itinerant retailers, vending machines,
  independent retailers, street markets, speciality shops, online
  retailers, multiple chain stores, supermarkets, hypermarkets,
  department stores — with the features and pros/cons of each.
- **Why small-scale retailers survive**: personal service, flexible
  opening hours, additional services.
- **Retail selling techniques**: branding, own brands, logos,
  packaging, self-service, after-sales service, barcoding, direct
  mail, loyalty programmes, informal credit, cash on delivery.
- **Digital retail trends**: omnichannel retailing, cashless stores,
  EPoS, click and collect, self-service checkouts, subscription
  commerce, QR codes, VR showrooms, dropshipping, mobile/smartphone
  apps, multi-channel retailing.
- **Ecommerce's effect on traditional commerce**: positives (24/7
  selling, more customers, increased revenue, targeted marketing,
  lower store costs) versus negatives (delivery/training/warehousing
  costs, website maintenance).

## 2.4 Wholesaling

- The **functions and services** the wholesaler provides.
- The wholesaler's **importance to both manufacturer and retailer** —
  learn this as a two-sided relationship, not a single function.

## 2.5 Trading documents

- The named documents used in commercial transactions and their
  individual purposes — learn each document's role in the sequence of
  a trade (order, dispatch, payment, receipt).

## 2.6 Communication

- **Methods of communication**: including web-based chat (chatbots),
  social media, posters, among others.
- **Factors affecting choice of communication method**: urgency, cost,
  security, distance, availability of networks, importance of the
  message — be ready to judge which method suits a given scenario using
  these named factors.

## Command word reminders

"Describe" and "state" questions on named lists (enterprise types,
retailer types, communication methods) want accurate, specific recall
— naming the wrong sub-category (for example, confusing a cooperative
with a partnership) loses marks even when the surrounding description
is otherwise sound. "Discuss" or "evaluate" questions on advantages and
disadvantages, or on ecommerce's effects, expect both sides weighed
against each other, ideally with a scenario-specific judgement at the
end rather than a neutral list.

## Connecting Topic 2 forward to Topics 3 and 4

Global supply chain management (Topic 3) and logistics (Topic 4) both
build on ideas introduced here: outsourcing (2.2) is a first, simple
version of the supply chain relationships explored in more depth later,
and the retailing and wholesaling content in 2.3–2.4 underpins later
questions about how goods move from producer to consumer across a
whole supply chain, not just a single commercial operation. Treating
Topic 2 as the foundation for these later topics, rather than a
self-contained unit, makes revision of Topics 3 and 4 faster later.

## Quick recall checklist

- Can I list the five named private-sector enterprise types and one
  feature of each?
- Can I state one positive and one negative impact of an MNC locating
  in a host country?
- Do I know the physical vs ecommerce location factor lists separately?
- Can I name at least four types of retailer and one advantage plus one
  disadvantage for each?
- Can I explain why small-scale retailers survive despite competition
  from larger chains?
- Do I know the wholesaler's importance to both ends of the supply
  chain — manufacturer and retailer?
- Can I select an appropriate communication method for a scenario using
  the five named factors?

## Common mistakes

- **Listing enterprise types without judging appropriateness** for a
  given scenario, when exam questions typically ask which is most
  suitable and why.
- **Treating physical and ecommerce location factors as one combined
  list** rather than two related but distinct sets.
- **Describing retail selling techniques and digital retail trends as
  the same list** — they are related but separately named categories.
- **Giving only positive or only negative effects of ecommerce** on
  traditional commerce, when a balanced answer is expected.

## How to approach it

Because 2.3 Retailing is the longest and most detail-dense sub-topic,
build a single table of retailer types with columns for features,
advantages and disadvantages, and revise it as a standalone unit before
moving to selling techniques and digital trends. For 2.1's ownership
types and 2.6's communication methods, practise applying the named
factors (appropriateness of ownership; urgency/cost/security/distance
for communication) to short scenarios, since these two sub-topics are
most often examined through applied "which would you recommend and
why" questions rather than pure recall.
