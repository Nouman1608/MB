## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term "relocation". **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** location factors that matter most to a business that delivers its service online. **[2]**

**3.** Outline **one** reason why a sugar refinery is often located close to the farms that grow sugar cane. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a hairdresser and a plumber in the same town might choose very different locations. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** ways in which legal controls can affect where a business locates. **[2]**

**6.** Ravensby Metals is offered a one-off government grant of $45,000 if it builds its new factory at Site North. Transport costs at Site North would be $9,000 a year higher than at Site South. All other costs are the same.

**(a)** Calculate how many years it would take for the extra transport costs to equal the grant. **[2]**

**(b)** State **one** other factor Ravensby Metals should consider before accepting the grant. **[1]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Marisol Juices makes fresh fruit juice from oranges grown on farms 150 km from the capital city, where most of its customers live. It is choosing between building its factory near the farms or near the city. Estimated transport costs per year:

| | Near the farms | Near the city |
|---|---|---|
| Moving oranges to the factory | $12,000 | $46,000 |
| Moving juice to customers | $38,000 | $9,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the total annual transport cost for each location. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason, other than transport cost, why a juice maker might prefer to locate near the farms. **[3]**

**(c)** Justify which location Marisol Juices should choose. **[4]**

**8.** Tamsin Garments is choosing between Country R and Country S for a new clothing factory.

- Country R: wages are $4 an hour and a worker makes 2 garments an hour. Power cuts are frequent, and there was unrest after the last election.
- Country S: wages are $9 an hour and a worker makes 5 garments an hour. It is close to Tamsin's main market and has a trade agreement with it, so no import tariffs are charged.

**(a)** Calculate the labour cost per garment in each country. **[2]**

**(b)** Using your answer to (a), explain why lower wages do not always mean lower costs. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how political instability in Country R could affect Tamsin Garments. **[3]**

**(d)** Identify **two** factors, other than labour cost, that favour Country S. **[2]**

**9.** Harbourside Seafoods wants to build a fish processing plant beside a beach used by tourists. The government is also offering incentives to businesses that locate in a nearby town with high unemployment. Explain **two** ways in which the government could influence where Harbourside Seafoods locates. **[4]**

**10.** Brightwell Print plans to relocate from the city centre to an industrial estate 30 km away. The move would save $36,000 a year in rent but cost $54,000 in one-off moving expenses.

**(a)** Outline **two** possible reasons why a business might decide to relocate. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how long it would take for the rent savings to cover the moving expenses. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** risks Brightwell Print faces in relocating. **[4]**

**11.** Cinnamon Cafe is choosing between a site in the railway station and a site at an out-of-town retail park. It opens 30 days a month and the average customer spends $5.

| | Station | Retail park |
|---|---|---|
| People passing per day | 2,400 | 900 |
| Percentage who buy | 3% | 6% |
| Rent per month | $4,200 | $1,900 |

**(a)** Calculate the number of customers per day at each site. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly revenue minus rent for each site. **[4]**

**(c)** Recommend and justify which site Cinnamon Cafe should choose. **[6]**

**12.** Okafor Furniture can locate a new factory in a region of high unemployment, where it would receive a government grant, or near its existing factory, where its skilled workers live. Consider whether the government grant should be the most important factor in the decision. Justify your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Moving an existing business operation [1] from its current site to a new one [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The word "existing" separates relocation from a first location decision.

**2.** Any two, for example: the cost of premises [1]; the availability of suitably skilled staff (or reliable internet connections, or wage costs) [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Footfall and visibility gain no credit here because online customers never visit the site.

**3.** Sugar cane is heavy and bulky and loses much of its weight when refined [1], so it is cheaper to move the finished sugar than the raw cane, which keeps transport costs down [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The point is the cost of moving inputs compared with outputs, not simply "it is near the farms".

**4.** A hairdresser's customers come to the business [1], so it needs a visible site with high footfall, parking or good public transport [1]. A plumber travels to customers [1], so it needs a central base in the area it serves with cheap premises, and visibility matters little [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Both businesses must be covered, and each factor must be tied to how that business reaches its customers.

**5.** Any two, for example: laws may prevent certain activities near homes or on protected land [1]; permission may be needed to build, which causes delay (or conditions on noise or emissions add cost, or incentives encourage certain areas) [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** $45,000 ÷ $9,000 [1] = **5 years** [1].

**(b)** Any one, for example: whether skilled workers are available at Site North, closeness to customers, or room to expand [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* After five years the grant is used up but the higher transport cost continues, which is why a grant alone rarely decides a location.

**7. (a)** Near the farms: 12,000 + 38,000 = **$50,000** [1]. Near the city: 46,000 + 9,000 = **$55,000** [1].

**(b)** Oranges are perishable [1], so processing them soon after picking keeps the juice fresh and of higher quality [1], which reduces waste from fruit spoiling on long journeys and protects the brand's reputation [1].

**(c)** Recommendation: near the farms [1]. It is $5,000 a year cheaper in transport [1], and fresher oranges mean better quality juice and less waste [1]. However, juice is also bulky and must reach city customers quickly, so the decision depends on whether deliveries to the city can be made reliably from 150 km away [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A strong justification uses the calculated difference and adds a factor the table does not show.

**8. (a)** Country R: $4 ÷ 2 = **$2.00** per garment [1]. Country S: $9 ÷ 5 = **$1.80** per garment [1].

**(b)** Workers in Country S are more productive [1], so even though their hourly wage is more than double, each garment costs $0.20 less in labour [1].

**(c)** Unrest could close the factory or stop goods reaching the ports [1], so orders would be delivered late [1], and customers might switch to other suppliers, reducing Tamsin's sales and reputation [1].

**(d)** Any two: closeness to the main market [1]; no import tariffs because of the trade agreement (or no reported power cuts or unrest) [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the figures from part (a). "Cheap labour" is only cheap if productivity is considered.

**9.** First, legal controls could prevent or restrict a processing plant beside a tourist beach [1], for example through limits on smells, waste or water pollution, so Harbourside may be refused permission or face extra costs there [1]. Second, the incentives could make the nearby town more attractive [1], because a grant or tax relief lowers the cost of setting up, and the high unemployment means workers should be easy to recruit [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* One way restricts and one way encourages; together they show both sides of the government's role.

**10. (a)** Any two, for example: the current premises are too small for growth [1]; rent has risen or the lease is ending (or customers or suppliers have moved) [1].

**(b)** $54,000 ÷ $36,000 = **1.5 years** [1].

**(c)** Risk 1: some staff may not travel the extra 30 km and leave [1], so Brightwell must spend time and money recruiting and training replacements [1]. Risk 2: printing may stop during the move [1], so orders are delayed and some customers may switch to rival printers [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Each risk needs a consequence for the business, not just a label.

**11. (a)** Station: 2,400 × 3% = **72 customers** [1]. Retail park: 900 × 6% = **54 customers** [1].

**(b)** Station revenue: 72 × $5 × 30 = $10,800 [1]; minus rent $4,200 = **$6,600** [1]. Retail park revenue: 54 × $5 × 30 = $8,100 [1]; minus rent $1,900 = **$6,200** [1].

**(c)** Recommendation, for example the station [1]. It earns $400 a month ($4,800 a year) more after rent [1]. It also has far more people passing, so a small rise in the percentage who buy would add much more revenue than at the retail park [1]. However, its rent is more than double, so if passenger numbers fell the cafe would be more exposed [1]. The retail park converts twice the proportion of passers-by and may offer parking and room to expand [1]. Judgement: the station is better on these figures, but the margin is small, so the decision depends on how reliable the footfall estimates are [1]. (Credit a reasoned choice of the retail park that uses the figures.) **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* For "recommend and justify", the calculation supports the decision; it does not replace it.

**12.** For the grant: it lowers the cost of building the factory [1], and high unemployment means a large pool of workers and possibly lower wages [1]. Against: a grant is usually paid once, while extra costs continue [1]; furniture making needs skilled workers, and if they live near the existing factory Okafor may have to train new staff, raising costs and risking poorer quality at first [1]. Being near the existing factory may also let the two sites share managers and transport [1]. Judgement: the grant should not be the most important factor unless it covers the cost of training and the region offers suitable skills; access to skilled labour is likely to matter more for this business [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "Consider... justify" needs both sides and a clear conclusion that says what the answer depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using footfall or visibility for businesses whose customers never visit.
- Calling labour "cheap" without checking output per worker.
- Treating a one-off grant as if it lasted for ever.
- Giving risks of relocation without saying what happens to the business as a result.
- Recommending a site without using the calculated figures, or without weighing the rejected site.
- Ending a "consider" answer with no judgement.
