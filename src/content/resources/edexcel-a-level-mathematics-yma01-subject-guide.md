Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01) is a modular course
for students who want a full A Level in mathematics, with a core of pure
mathematics and a choice of applied content in mechanics, statistics or
decision mathematics. The full qualification is made up of six units: the
four compulsory Pure Mathematics units, P1 to P4, plus one pair of applied
units. Every unit is an externally assessed written exam lasting 90 minutes,
carrying 75 marks and worth 16.67% (one-sixth) of the qualification. The
current specification is Issue 3, April 2019, with first assessment in 2019.

## How the course is organised

The specification lists nine units. Four are compulsory and five are
applied options, of which you take two.

**Pure Mathematics (all compulsory)**

- **P1: Pure Mathematics 1** covers algebra and functions, coordinate
  geometry in the (x, y) plane, trigonometry, differentiation and
  integration.
- **P2: Pure Mathematics 2** adds proof, sequences and series, and
  exponentials and logarithms, and extends algebra, coordinate geometry,
  trigonometry and calculus.
- **P3: Pure Mathematics 3** develops algebra and functions, trigonometry,
  exponentials and logarithms, differentiation and integration further, and
  introduces numerical methods.
- **P4: Pure Mathematics 4** covers proof, algebra and functions,
  coordinate geometry, the binomial expansion, differentiation, integration
  and vectors.

**Applied units (choose one valid pair)**

- **M1: Mechanics 1** covers mathematical models in mechanics, vectors in
  mechanics, kinematics and dynamics of a particle, statics of a particle,
  and moments.
- **M2: Mechanics 2** covers kinematics in a straight line or plane, centres
  of mass, work and energy, collisions and statics of rigid bodies.
- **S1: Statistics 1** covers mathematical models in probability and
  statistics, representation and summary of data, probability, correlation
  and regression, discrete random variables and distributions, and the
  Normal distribution.
- **S2: Statistics 2** covers the Binomial and Poisson distributions,
  continuous random variables and distributions, samples and hypothesis
  tests.
- **D1: Decision Mathematics 1** covers algorithms, algorithms on graphs,
  critical path analysis and linear programming.

## How it's assessed

Every unit has the same format: a 90-minute written exam, 75 marks,
externally assessed, worth 16.67% of the full award.

| Unit | Paper code | Status |
|---|---|---|
| P1: Pure Mathematics 1 | WMA11/01 | Compulsory |
| P2: Pure Mathematics 2 | WMA12/01 | Compulsory |
| P3: Pure Mathematics 3 | WMA13/01 | Compulsory |
| P4: Pure Mathematics 4 | WMA14/01 | Compulsory |
| M1: Mechanics 1 | WME01/01 | Applied option |
| S1: Statistics 1 | WST01/01 | Applied option |
| D1: Decision Mathematics 1 | WDM11/01 | Applied option |
| M2: Mechanics 2 | WME02/01 | Applied option |
| S2: Statistics 2 | WST02/01 | Applied option |

The applied pair must be one of five named combinations: **M1 + S1**,
**M1 + D1**, **M1 + M2**, **S1 + D1** or **S1 + S2**. Notice that M2 can only
be taken with M1 and S2 only with S1, so the second-level applied units
always follow their first-level partner. Four pure units plus two applied
units make six papers of equal weight; the one-sixth shares round to 16.67%
each.

The International AS in Mathematics is a separate, smaller award (code
XMA01: P1 and P2 plus one applied unit). Further Pure units and the M3 and
S3 units belong to other awards and are not part of YMA01.

## How the parts fit together

The pure units form a ladder. P1 sets out the basic tools of algebra,
coordinate geometry, trigonometry and calculus; P2 extends each of them and
adds proof, sequences and series, and logarithms; P3 and P4 take the same
strands further, adding numerical methods, the binomial expansion and
vectors. Each unit assumes the one before it, so gaps in P1 tend to show up
again in P3 and P4.

The applied units draw on the pure content. Mechanics relies on vectors,
trigonometry and calculus to model motion and forces. Statistics uses
algebra and, in S2, integration for continuous random variables. Decision
Mathematics is the most self-contained, focusing on algorithms, graphs and
linear programming, though it still depends on careful algebra.

Your choice of applied pair therefore matters. M1 + M2 suits students who
enjoy modelling physical situations; S1 + S2 suits those drawn to data and
probability; the mixed pairs give breadth. Your centre will normally decide
which pair it teaches, so check before the course begins.

## Planning your study

A common two-year order is P1 and P2 with one applied unit in the first year,
then P3 and P4 with the second applied unit in the second year. That order
keeps the pure ladder in sequence and places the second-level applied units
(M2 or S2), where taken, after their first-level partners. Ask your teacher
which series each unit will be entered for, since modular courses allow
different timetables.

Throughout the course, keep a single notebook of methods and standard
results organised by strand (algebra, trigonometry, calculus), adding to it
as each pure unit extends the strand. Practise unit-specific questions as
each topic is taught, then move to full timed papers once a whole unit is
complete. The exam-preparation page covers how to approach the papers
themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which units make up Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics YMA01?**
The full award is the four Pure Mathematics units P1 to P4 plus one pair of applied units, six units in total.

**Which applied unit pairs are allowed?**
There are five valid pairs: M1 + S1, M1 + D1, M1 + M2, S1 + D1 and S1 + S2.

**How long is each YMA01 exam?**
Every unit is a 90-minute written exam worth 75 marks and 16.67% of the full qualification.

**Can I take M2 without M1?**
No. M2 only appears in the M1 + M2 pair, and S2 only appears in the S1 + S2 pair.

**Is the International AS the same as YMA01?**
No. The International AS (XMA01) is a separate award made up of P1, P2 and one applied unit.
