This page is a guide to OCR A Level Economics (H460) past papers from June 2023 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and, for June 2023 to June 2025, a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from OCR's own examiners' reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR A Level Economics (H060, H460) qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/economics-h060-h460-from-2019/)
- [OCR A Level Economics assessment page, with question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/economics-h060-h460-from-2019/assessment/)
- [OCR grade boundaries](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/grade-boundaries-archive/grade-boundaries-archive.aspx)

Use paper, mark scheme and examiners' report together. Sit the paper under timed conditions, mark it strictly with the mark scheme, then read the report for that component to see why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series are a quick way into the report, not a replacement for it.

Check the structure before you rely on an older paper. H460 is linear, with three written components of 80 marks and 2 hours each, equally weighted: Microeconomics (H460/01), Macroeconomics (H460/02) and Themes in economics (H460/03), 240 marks in total. Paper 3 is synoptic and draws on both micro and macro content with an unseen theme. Every series below uses this structure. For topic study, see our [aggregate supply and AD/AS notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-aggregate-supply-and-the-ad-as-model/).

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe accessible papers and well-prepared candidates. What separated scripts was the quality of the economics inside the answer: whether diagrams were accurate and woven into the writing, whether the extract was used rather than copied, and whether a judgement said what the answer depends on.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H460/01): answers with no economic concepts or theory; long descriptions of real-world examples without theory; rephrasing the extract instead of using it; confused theory; discussing only one point in an essay. A closing judgement that just repeated earlier points did not help, including on the shorter extended questions.
- Paper 2 (H460/02): diagrams drawn but not labelled or explained; generic textbook answers that ignored the stimulus; misapplying formulae, such as the terms of trade index; mixing up core models, for example long-run aggregate supply shifts with unemployment, or treating the short-run Phillips curve as fixed. Some candidates spent too long on the last parts of the first question and had too little time for the essays.
- Paper 3 (H460/03): incorrect, missing or unlabelled diagrams; rewriting the extracts; basic concept errors; opening with a descriptive introduction instead of starting the chain of analysis; answering a question about one market from a whole-economy perspective, or the reverse. Multiple-choice marks were high for many candidates.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports describe papers that were accessible and well timed. Diagrams were increasingly part of the analysis, but supported judgements, accurate calculation and use of the extracts still separated the top scripts.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H460/01): inaccurate diagrams, or diagrams not explained in the writing; asserting rather than explaining; conclusions that only summarised the answer; not comparing data when asked; long answers to the later sections that repeated earlier arguments. The housing market featured in Section A.
- Paper 2 (H460/02): missing the opening step in a chain of reasoning, so answers were vague; gaps in the links between points; answering the question the candidate preferred; not covering the full scope of a question, such as both tax and government spending for fiscal policy; discussing the wrong subject in an answer or drifting into alternative policies; not using the extract in Section A.
- Paper 3 (H460/03): multiple-choice questions and calculations were harder than usual, especially index numbers; spreading an answer over many points rather than one in depth; summing up at the end instead of finishing with a supported judgement; rewriting the extracts; contradictory or unrealistic statements.

## June 2023

In June 2023 all three reports say the papers discriminated well. The best answers had long chains of reasoning, accurate diagrams built into the analysis and a clear focus on what the question asked.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H460/01): imprecise knowledge; generic responses; diagrams that were wrong or not explained; asserting that a curve shifts without explaining why; ignoring the context in the stimulus. Examiners noted that demerit goods and goods with negative externalities were used interchangeably, and that explaining market failure for public goods was a weak area.
- Paper 2 (H460/02): imprecise use of key terms; drifting from the question; weak understanding of the difference between direct and indirect taxes as government revenue. Stronger scripts explained links such as how higher spending could cut unemployment through derived demand and the multiplier.
- Paper 3 (H460/03): missing knowledge of basic definitions and of the financial sector; careless diagrams; difficulty with calculations and interpreting data; answering about a general topic rather than the specific one in the question. Stronger scripts used the extracts as a starting point and gave a counter-argument.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR H460 past papers and mark schemes?**
On the assessment page for OCR A Level Economics, linked above, which lists question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports by series.

**How is the overall H460 grade decided?**
The three papers are added to give a mark out of 240, and the overall boundaries in each table apply to that total.

**Do diagrams matter in H460?**
Examiners in every series described accurate diagrams that are explained in the writing as a mark of stronger scripts, and unlabelled or unexplained diagrams as a common weakness.

**What mistakes do examiners report most often?**
Generic answers that ignore the extract, assertion in place of a chain of reasoning, conclusions that repeat earlier points rather than judge, and answers that miss the full scope of the question.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and are only a guide for the series they belong to.
