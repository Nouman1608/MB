Condensed notes for section 6.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, two worked examples and an annotated answer, use the [consumer protection study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-consumer-protection/).

## The syllabus in two lines

1. The **reasons for consumer protection**.
2. The **actions taken by buyers and sellers** to resolve consumer protection complaints.

## Meaning

**Consumer protection** is the set of rules, organisations and practices that protect buyers from unsafe goods, unfair treatment and dishonest selling.

## Reasons for consumer protection

Mnemonic **"Some Quiet Hens Find Useful Veggies Online, Fairly"**:

| Letter | Reason | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S | Safety | Toys with small parts |
| Q | Quality | Goods that fail quickly |
| H | Honest information | False labels |
| F | Fair prices and practices | Hidden fees |
| U | Unequal power | One buyer against a big firm |
| V | Vulnerable groups | Children, older people |
| O | Online and distance selling | Item differs from the photograph |
| F | Fair competition | Rivals making false claims |

Benefit to sellers: customers who trust the market buy more, and honest firms are not undercut by dishonest ones.

## Typical complaints

Faulty or unsafe goods; not as described; late or missing delivery; price different from the advert; poor service; refusal of a promised refund or repair.

## Buyer actions, in order

| Step | Action | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep receipts, photographs, messages | Proof |
| 2 | Contact the seller; state what is wanted | Chance to put it right |
| 3 | Complain in writing, with a deadline | Record and pressure |
| 4 | Contact a consumer organisation or agency | Advice and support |
| 5 | Ask the bank or card company for a refund | Recover payment |
| 6 | Mediation or ombudsman | Neutral settlement |
| 7 | Legal action (small claims) | Last resort |
| 8 | Honest review | Warn others |

## Seller actions

| Action | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Listen and apologise | Keep goodwill |
| Investigate | Find the real fault |
| Offer a remedy (refund, repair, replacement, credit note, compensation) | Settle the complaint |
| Act quickly | Reduce cost and anger |
| Clear complaints policy | Customers and staff know the process |
| Recall unsafe goods | Prevent harm |
| Correct the cause | Stop it happening again |
| Train staff | Fair and calm handling |
| Co-operate with agencies | Resolve disputes |

## Choosing a remedy

| Remedy | Suits |
|---|---|
| Refund | Customer no longer wants the item |
| Repair | Easily fixed item; cheaper for the seller |
| Replacement | Customer still wants the product |
| Compensation | Loss or injury caused |
| Credit note | Customer will buy again from the seller |

Factors: cost to the seller, what the customer wants, how serious the fault is, and the seller's reputation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consumer | A person who buys goods or services for personal use |
| Complaint | A statement that goods or service are not satisfactory |
| Refund | Money returned to the buyer |
| Replacement | A new item given in place of a faulty one |
| Compensation | Payment for loss or harm |
| Credit note | A voucher to spend with the same seller |
| Recall | Request to return unsafe products |
| Mediation | A neutral person helps both sides to agree |
| Ombudsman | An independent official who investigates complaints |
| Small claims court | Court for lower-value disputes |

## Exam traps

- Give a **reason** that links to a **risk** to the consumer.
- Remember that sellers also take **actions**.
- Buyers start with **evidence and the seller**; courts come **last**.
- Do not say consumer protection only benefits buyers.
- A refund returns money; a credit note keeps the value inside the seller's business.
- Do not assume one country's laws apply everywhere.

## Self-test

1. Define consumer protection.
2. State **two** reasons why consumers need protection.
3. Why does unequal power matter?
4. Give **two** types of complaint.
5. State **two** actions a buyer can take.
6. What evidence should a buyer keep?
7. Give **one** situation in which mediation is useful.
8. State **two** actions a seller can take.
9. What is a recall?
10. Give **one** advantage of repair over refund for a seller.
11. How do honest sellers benefit from consumer protection?
12. A seller can refund $120, repair for $35 or replace at a cost of $70. Which is cheapest and how much cheaper is it than replacement?

**Answers**

1. Rules, organisations and practices that protect buyers from unsafe goods, unfair treatment and dishonest selling.
2. Any two: safety; quality; honest information; fair prices; unequal power; vulnerable groups; online selling; fair competition.
3. One buyer is weak compared with a large firm and cannot easily challenge it.
4. Any two: faulty goods; not as described; late delivery; wrong price; poor service; refused refund.
5. Any two: contact the seller; complain in writing; contact a consumer organisation; ask the bank for a refund; use mediation; take legal action.
6. Receipts, order number, photographs, packaging, messages.
7. When buyer and seller disagree and a neutral person can help them agree without court.
8. Any two: apologise and listen; investigate; offer a remedy; recall goods; train staff; set a clear policy.
9. A request for buyers to return a product that is unsafe or faulty.
10. It costs less and keeps the customer.
11. Customers trust the market and buy more; dishonest rivals cannot undercut them.
12. Repair is cheapest at $35. It is 70 - 35 = $35 cheaper than replacement.
