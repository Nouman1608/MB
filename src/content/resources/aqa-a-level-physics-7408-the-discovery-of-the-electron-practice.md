These questions cover section **3.12.1 The discovery of the electron** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Turning points in physics option. Learn the content first in the [discovery of the electron study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-the-discovery-of-the-electron/) and the [discovery of the electron revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-the-discovery-of-the-electron-revision-notes/).

Data: e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; mass of hydrogen ion = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; g = 9.81 m s⁻². Ignore upthrust on oil drops.

## Section A

**1.** Explain what is meant by thermionic emission. **[2]**

**2.** State why the tube of an electron gun must be evacuated. **[1]**

**3.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a p.d. of 450 V. Calculate its kinetic energy and its speed. **[3]**

**4.** State what is meant by the specific charge of a particle. **[1]**

**5.** Explain how the light is produced in a gas discharge tube. **[2]**

**6.** State the significance of Millikan's oil drop results. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In a fine-beam tube, electrons accelerated through 320 V enter a uniform magnetic field of 1.2 mT at right angles and move in a circle of diameter 10.0 cm.

**(a)** Show that e/m = 2V/(B²r²). **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate e/m for the electron. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare your answer with the specific charge of the hydrogen ion and state what Thomson concluded from this comparison. **[2]**

**8.** A beam of electrons passes between parallel plates 30 mm apart with a p.d. of 600 V across them. A magnetic field of 0.80 mT, perpendicular to both the beam and the electric field, is adjusted so that the beam is undeflected.

**(a)** Explain why the beam is undeflected. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the electrons. **[2]**
**(c)** Taking e/m = 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹, calculate the anode p.d. of the electron gun. **[2]**

**9.** In an oil drop experiment the plates are 6.0 mm apart. The oil has density 900 kg m⁻³ and the viscosity of air is 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ N s m⁻². With no p.d., a drop falls at a terminal speed of 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Show that the radius of the drop is about 9.6 × 10⁻⁷ m. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of the drop. **[1]**
**(c)** The drop is held stationary when the p.d. is 305 V. Calculate its charge and the number of electronic charges it carries. **[3]**

**10.** The p.d. across the plates in question 9 is increased to 800 V and the drop rises at a steady speed.

**(a)** Draw up a force balance for the drop and explain the direction of the drag force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the terminal speed at which it rises. **[3]**

**11.** A student measures the charges on five oil drops as 3.2, 4.8, 8.0, 6.4 and 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Discuss what these results suggest and comment on the value 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. **[3]**

**12.** Compare how free electrons are produced in a gas discharge tube and in an electron gun. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** When a metal is heated, some conduction electrons gain enough kinetic energy [1]; to escape from the metal surface [1]

**2.** So electrons are not scattered, slowed or absorbed by collisions with gas molecules [1]

**3.** E_k = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 450 = **7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ J** [1]; v = √(2E_k/m) = √(2 × 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷/9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1]; = **1.26 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]

**4.** The ratio of its charge to its mass (in C kg⁻¹) [1]

**5.** Electrons accelerated by the p.d. collide with gas atoms and excite them [1]; the atoms de-excite, emitting photons of particular wavelengths [1]

**6.** All the charges measured were whole-number multiples of about 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, so charge is quantised [1]; this gave the charge of the electron, and with e/m its mass [1]

**7. (a)** In the field the magnetic force provides the centripetal force: Bev = mv²/r, so v = Ber/m [1]; from the gun, ½mv² = eV, so v² = 2eV/m [1]; substituting: (Ber/m)² = 2eV/m, which gives e/m = 2V/(B²r²) [1]
**(b)** r = 0.050 m; e/m = 2 × 320/((1.2 × 10⁻³)² × 0.050²) [1]; = **1.8 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** e/m(H⁺) = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹/1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 9.6 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹, so the electron's is about 1800 times larger [1]; the electron is a sub-atomic particle with a mass much smaller than that of a hydrogen atom, present in all matter [1]

**8. (a)** The electric force on each electron (eE) is equal and opposite to the magnetic force (Bev) [1]; so the resultant force is zero and there is no deflection [1]
**(b)** E = 600/0.030 = 2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]; v = E/B = 2.0 × 10⁴/0.80 × 10⁻³ = **2.5 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** eV = ½mv², so V = v²/(2e/m) = (2.5 × 10⁷)²/(2 × 1.76 × 10¹¹) [1]; = **1.8 × 10³ V** [1]

**9. (a)** At terminal speed, weight = drag: (4/3)πr³ρg = 6πηrv, so r = √(9ηv/(2ρg)) [1]; r = √(9 × 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 1.0 × 10⁻⁴/(2 × 900 × 9.81)) = 9.58 × 10⁻⁷ m [1]
**(b)** m = (4/3)π(9.58 × 10⁻⁷)³ × 900 = **3.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg** [1]
**(c)** Q = mgd/V [1]; = 3.31 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 9.81 × 6.0 × 10⁻³/305 = **6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C** [1]; = **4** electronic charges [1]

**10. (a)** Electric force upwards = weight + drag [1]; the drag opposes the motion, so for a rising drop it acts downwards [1]
**(b)** Electric force = QV/d = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 800/6.0 × 10⁻³ = 8.53 × 10⁻¹⁴ N [1]; drag = 8.53 × 10⁻¹⁴ − 3.25 × 10⁻¹⁴ = 5.28 × 10⁻¹⁴ N [1]; v = drag/(6πηr) = 5.28 × 10⁻¹⁴/(6π × 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 9.58 × 10⁻⁷) = **1.6 × 10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹** [1]

**11.** Four values (3.2, 4.8, 6.4 and 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) are whole-number multiples (2, 3, 4, 5) of 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, supporting quantisation of charge [1]; 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C would be 2.5e, which is not possible if charge is quantised [1]; it is most likely an experimental error (for example in timing or the radius), so the measurement should be repeated [1]

**12.** Discharge tube: positive ions in the low-pressure gas are accelerated into the cathode and release electrons [1]; electron gun: electrons are released from a heated filament by thermionic emission [1]; the discharge tube needs gas and a very high p.d. to sustain ionisation, while the electron gun works in a vacuum and gives a narrow beam of known energy eV [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to halve the diameter to get r.
- Squaring B but not r (or neither) in e/m = 2V/(B²r²).
- Converting plate separations from mm wrongly.
- Putting the drag force upwards for a rising drop.
- Stating Thomson's significance without the comparison with the hydrogen ion.
