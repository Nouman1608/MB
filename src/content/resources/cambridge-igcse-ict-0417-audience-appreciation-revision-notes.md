Section 9.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 asks you to show a clear sense of audience and purpose, to plan ICT solutions that are responsive to and respectful of an audience's needs, and to analyse those needs. These notes condense it for recall. The [audience appreciation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-audience-appreciation/) has a full worked analysis, and the [audience appreciation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-audience-appreciation-practice/) let you apply it.

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus wording | Recall |
|---|---|
| Show a clear sense of audience and purpose | Know who it is for and what it must achieve |
| Planning ICT solutions that are responsive to and respectful of the needs of an audience | Every decision meets a need; nothing excludes or offends |
| Analyse the needs of an audience when creating ICT solutions | Find out who they are, what they know, why, how and where they will use it, and any particular needs |

## Audience + purpose

| Purpose | Key features |
|---|---|
| Inform | Clear headings, short sections, tables and charts, neutral tone |
| Instruct | Numbered steps, one action per step, screenshots |
| Persuade | Headline, images, benefits, call to action |
| Entertain | Images, colour, sound or animation, lively style |

Same audience + different purpose = different solution.

## Six analysis questions

1. **Who?** Age, role, number of people.
2. **What do they know?** Subject knowledge and ICT skills.
3. **Why?** What they want to find out or do.
4. **How and where?** Print or screen; phone, desktop or big screen; quick look or detailed reading.
5. **Particular needs?** Sight, hearing, reading, mobility, language.
6. **Expectations and values?** Culture, tone, what would offend.

### Methods of finding out

- Questionnaires to a sample of the audience.
- Interviews with a few members.
- Observing them using similar materials.
- Looking at existing materials that work for them.
- Testing a draft and changing it from feedback.

## Responsive: match each decision to a need

| Need | Responsive decision |
|---|---|
| Young readers | Short sentences, large text, pictures |
| Experts | Technical terms, detail, data |
| Busy managers | Summary first, bullet points, charts |
| Phone users | Single column, large buttons, small image files |
| Beginners with ICT | Simple navigation, clear labels, step-by-step help |
| Group in a hall | Large text on few slides, high contrast |

## Respectful: include everyone, offend no one

- **Accessibility**: alt text; captions or transcripts; high contrast; enlargeable text; never use colour alone to show meaning.
- **Culture**: suitable images, symbols, colours, examples; awareness of differing customs and dates.
- **Language**: inclusive, no stereotypes, no talking down, no excluding slang.
- **Content**: suitable for the audience's age.
- **Privacy**: no personal data or identifiable photos without permission.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Audience | The people a solution is created for |
| Purpose | What the solution must achieve |
| Audience appreciation | Understanding the audience and adapting the solution to it |
| Needs analysis | Finding out what the audience requires before designing |
| Responsive | Designed to meet the needs found in the analysis |
| Respectful | Not excluding, embarrassing or offending the audience |
| Accessibility | Making a solution usable by people with disabilities |
| Alternative (alt) text | A text description of an image read out by screen readers |
| Call to action | A clear instruction telling the reader what to do next |
| Tone | The level of formality and feeling in the language |

## Exam traps

- **Feature + need**: "large buttons, because elderly users may have less precise finger control". A feature alone scores little.
- **Name the purpose** as well as the audience when justifying.
- **"Colourful" and "easy to use"** are vague: say what and why.
- **Respectful ≠ polite**: think accessibility, inclusion, culture, age-appropriateness and privacy.
- **Analyse means find out**: give methods (questionnaire, interview, testing a draft), not just a list of features.
- **Mixed audiences** (children and parents; visitors from many countries) need features for each group.

## Self-test

1. What is the difference between audience and purpose?
2. Give two features suitable for a solution whose purpose is to instruct.
3. Give two features suitable for a solution whose purpose is to persuade.
4. State three questions a designer should answer when analysing an audience.
5. Give two methods of finding out about an audience's needs.
6. Why should a draft be tested with members of the audience?
7. What does it mean for a solution to be responsive to an audience?
8. Give two ways of making a web page accessible to visually impaired users.
9. Why should a designer not rely on colour alone to show meaning?
10. Give one example of making a solution culturally respectful.
11. A presentation will be shown on a large screen in a hall. Give one responsive design decision.
12. Why might the same audience need two different solutions?

## Self-test answers

1. The audience is who the solution is for; the purpose is what it must achieve.
2. Any two of: numbered steps; one action per step; screenshots or diagrams; clear plain language.
3. Any two of: strong headline; persuasive images; benefits listed; call to action.
4. Any three of: who they are; what they know; why they will use it; how and where; particular needs; expectations and values.
5. Any two of: questionnaires; interviews; observation; studying existing materials; testing a draft.
6. Designers may assume the audience knows more than it does; feedback shows what to change before the final version.
7. Each design decision meets a need found in the analysis.
8. Any two of: alt text on images; high contrast; enlargeable text; compatible with screen readers.
9. Some users cannot distinguish certain colours, so they would miss the meaning.
10. For example: choose images and symbols that will not offend any group in the audience.
11. Large text and few words on each slide, with high contrast.
12. Because the purposes differ, such as persuading them to attend an event and instructing them how to pay online.
