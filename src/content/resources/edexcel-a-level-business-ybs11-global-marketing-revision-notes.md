Condensed recall for section 4.3.3 of Unit 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Global marketing study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-marketing/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-marketing-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Landed cost       = ex-factory price + freight + tariff
Tariff            = tariff rate x (ex-factory price + freight)
Retail price      = landed cost x (1 + distributor margin) x (1 + retailer margin)
Extra contribution = extra revenue x contribution margin
Net gain from adapting = extra contribution - extra cost of adapting
```

## Key strategies

| Strategy | Meaning | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Global (standardised) | One product, brand and message everywhere | Needs are similar; economies of scale matter; technology products |
| Glocalisation | Global brand with local adaptation | Tastes differ but the brand has global value |
| Local (fully adapted) | Different mix in each country | Markets differ greatly and are large enough |

## Marketing approaches

| Approach | Summary | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic or ethnocentric | Home-country methods used abroad | Cheap and consistent | Ignores local needs; risk of offence |
| Mixed or geocentric | Global brand and core, some regional variation | Scale plus local fit | Needs strong co-ordination |
| International or polycentric | Separate approach for each country | Best local fit | Costly; inconsistent brand |

## The 4Ps in global markets

| P | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Product | Flavour, size, language, features, safety standards |
| Price | Local incomes, tariffs, transport, distributor margins, exchange rates |
| Place | Local distributors, online sales, retail structure |
| Promotion | Local media, language, celebrities, cultural calendar |

- **Pricing abroad** is rarely the home price converted. Costs are added at each stage.
- **Standardise** to save cost and protect the brand. **Adapt** to win sales where tastes differ.

## Ansoff and Porter applied globally

| Model | Global application |
|---|---|
| Ansoff: penetration | More sales in countries already served |
| Ansoff: market development | Existing products to new countries (most common) |
| Ansoff: product development | New or adapted products for overseas customers |
| Ansoff: diversification | New products in new countries; highest risk |
| Porter: cost leadership | Standard, low-priced goods for a mass global market |
| Porter: differentiation | Distinct, often premium, global brand |
| Porter: focus | Chosen segment or niche in selected countries |

## Niche markets

- A **niche** is a small, specialised segment with particular needs.
- **Cultural diversity** creates many global niches: groups with shared interests but different values across the world.
- **Features:** distinct group, loyal customers, premium prices, limited competition, reachable online.
- **4Ps for a niche:** specialised product; premium price; online or specialist places; targeted promotion (communities, influencers, events).
- **Risks:** dependence on a small customer base; large rivals may enter.

## Cultural and social factors

| Factor | Risk | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural differences | Offence; product unsuitable for local customs | Market research; local advisers |
| Tastes and preferences | Low sales | Product testing; adapt recipes or designs |
| Language and unintended meanings | Embarrassing translation or brand name | Native-speaker checks |
| Inappropriate branding and promotion | Boycotts; reputation damage | Local review of images, colours, symbols |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glocalisation | Global brand with local adaptation |
| Ethnocentric | Home-country approach used everywhere |
| Geocentric | Global approach with regional variation |
| Polycentric | Separate approach for each market |
| Niche market | Small specialised segment |
| Landed cost | Cost of the product on arrival in the importing country, including freight and tariff |

## Exam traps

- Glocalisation is not the same as standardisation or total adaptation.
- Do not mix up the three approaches. State whose viewpoint drives each decision.
- Include tariffs, freight and margins when judging an overseas price.
- A niche can be profitable. Do not treat "small" as "weak".
- A language or cultural error is a risk to sales and reputation, not just embarrassment.
- For **assess**, tie the choice between standardising and adapting to the size of the market and how different its culture is.

## Answer frames

- **Adapting the mix:** name the P, give a local reason for the change, state the effect on sales or cost.
- **Choosing an approach:** compare two approaches on cost, brand and local fit, then judge.
- **Cultural risk:** identify the risk, give the likely consequence (boycott, lost sales) and one way to prevent it.
- **Niche strategy:** describe the niche, the adaptation of one or two Ps and the main risk.

## Self-test

1. An ex-factory price is $15 and freight is $1. A 20% tariff applies to the sum. Calculate the tariff and the landed cost.
2. A distributor adds 25% and a retailer adds 50% to the landed cost. Calculate the retail price.
3. A local rival sells at $32. By how much and by what percentage is the imported product dearer?
4. Adapting a campaign in six countries costs $0.3m each. A standard campaign costs $1.5m. Calculate the extra cost of adapting.
5. Adapting is expected to raise sales by 8% on revenue of $3m, with a contribution margin of 50%. Calculate the extra contribution.
6. Extra adaptation cost for this country is $90,000. Calculate the net gain.
7. Which approach treats each country separately?
8. Which approach uses home-country methods abroad?
9. Give one risk of a niche strategy.
10. Give one example of a problem with language in global marketing.

### Answers

1. Tariff = 0.20 x 16 = **$3.20**; landed cost = 16 + 3.20 = **$19.20**.
2. 19.20 x 1.25 = 24.00; 24.00 x 1.50 = **$36.00**.
3. 36 - 32 = **$4**; 4 / 32 x 100 = **12.5%** dearer.
4. 6 x 0.3m = $1.8m; extra = 1.8m - 1.5m = **$0.3m**.
5. Extra revenue = 8% x 3m = $240,000; extra contribution = 50% x 240,000 = **$120,000**.
6. 120,000 - 90,000 = **$30,000**.
7. International or polycentric.
8. Domestic or ethnocentric.
9. Any one: dependence on a small customer base; large rivals may enter; limited growth.
10. A brand name or slogan that has an unintended or offensive meaning in another language.

## Linking the ideas

- The choice between standardising and adapting is a trade-off between cost (scale economies) and fit (local sales). Quantify both whenever the source gives figures.
- Pricing, place and promotion connect: a longer distribution chain raises the retail price, which may force a change of channel or a lower-priced pack.
- Cultural errors are marketing risks and ethical risks at once: they can damage sales and reputation together.
- Niche strategies and global strategies are not opposites. A niche brand can be global if the internet reaches its customers in many countries.
- In essays, use Ansoff or Porter as a framework, but always return to evidence about the specific market.
