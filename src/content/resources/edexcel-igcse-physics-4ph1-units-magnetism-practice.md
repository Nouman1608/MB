## Section B

**6.** A laptop charger contains a transformer that changes 230 V mains to 19 V. The output current is 3.0 A. Assume the transformer is 100% efficient.

**(a)** Calculate the output power. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the input current, in mA. **[3]**
**(c)** The primary coil has 2300 turns. Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. **[2]**

**7.** A power station generates 600 MW of electrical power.

**(a)** Calculate the current if this power is transmitted at 20 kV. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the current if the voltage is first stepped up to 400 kV. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the power is transmitted at 400 kV rather than 20 kV. **[2]**

**8.** A student moves a magnet into a coil connected to a sensitive voltmeter. The meter reads 18 mV.

**(a)** Write this voltage in volts. **[1]**
**(b)** State two ways the student could make the induced voltage larger. **[2]**

**9.** A transformer has a primary voltage of 230 V and a primary current of 0.50 A. Its secondary voltage is 11 V and the secondary current is 9.5 A.

**(a)** Calculate the input power. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the output power. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the efficiency of the transformer. **[2]**

**10.** A student calculates the turns ratio of a transformer that changes 11 kV to 230 V as 11 ÷ 230 = 0.048. Explain the mistake and calculate the correct turns ratio. **[3]**

**11.** An electromagnet in a scrapyard crane model runs from a 6.0 V supply and takes a current of 1.5 A.

**(a)** Calculate its power. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred if it is switched on for 5.0 minutes. **[2]**

**12.** A doorbell transformer changes 230 V to 8.0 V. When the bell rings, it takes a current of 0.60 A. Assume the transformer is 100% efficient.

**(a)** Calculate the power supplied to the bell. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the current in the primary coil, in mA. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The volt [1]; V [1].

**2. (a)** 15 ÷ 1000 = 0.015 A [1].
**(b)** 132 × 1000 = 132 000 V [1].

**3.** power = current × voltage (P = I × V) [1].

**4.** It is a number of turns divided by a number of turns (equal to a voltage divided by a voltage), so the units cancel [1].

**5.** The input power of the transformer [1], in watts (W) [1].

**6. (a)** P = V_s × I_s = 19 × 3.0 [1] = 57 W [1].
**(b)** Input power = output power = 57 W [1]. I_p = 57 ÷ 230 = 0.248 A [1] = 248 mA (250 mA to 2 s.f.) [1].
**(c)** N_s = N_p × V_s ÷ V_p = 2300 × 19 ÷ 230 [1] = 190 turns [1].

**7. (a)** P = 600 × 10⁶ W and V = 20 × 10³ V [1]. I = P ÷ V = 600 × 10⁶ ÷ 20 × 10³ [1] = 30 000 A [1].
**(b)** I = 600 × 10⁶ ÷ 400 × 10³ [1] = 1500 A [1].
**(c)** At the higher voltage the current is much smaller for the same power [1], so the cables are heated less and less energy is wasted during transmission [1].

**8. (a)** 0.018 V [1].
**(b)** Any two: move the magnet faster; use a stronger magnet; use a coil with more turns [1] [1].

**9. (a)** P = V_p × I_p = 230 × 0.50 [1] = 115 W [1].
**(b)** 11 × 9.5 = 104.5 W (105 W) [1].
**(c)** efficiency = 104.5 ÷ 115 × 100% [1] = 91% [1].

**10.** The student divided kilovolts by volts without converting, so the units did not cancel [1]. 11 kV = 11 000 V [1]. Turns ratio = 11 000 ÷ 230 = 48 (47.8 : 1) [1].

**11. (a)** P = I × V = 1.5 × 6.0 [1] = 9.0 W [1].
**(b)** t = 5.0 × 60 = 300 s; E = P × t = 9.0 × 300 [1] = 2700 J [1].

**12. (a)** P = V_s × I_s = 8.0 × 0.60 [1] = 4.8 W [1].
**(b)** Input power = 4.8 W [1]. I_p = 4.8 ÷ 230 = 0.021 A [1] = 21 mA [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to convert kV to V in the turns-ratio equation?**
Only if the two voltages are in different units. If both are in kV, or both in V, the unit cancels. In Question 10,
one is in kV and the other in V, so one must be converted.
