## Section A

**1.** Which statement describes DNA after one round of semi-conservative replication of a ¹⁵N-labelled molecule in a medium containing only ¹⁴N?

A: One molecule contains only ¹⁵N and one contains only ¹⁴N.

B: Both molecules contain one ¹⁵N strand and one ¹⁴N strand.

C: Both molecules contain ¹⁵N and ¹⁴N mixed in patches along each strand.

D: Both molecules contain only ¹⁴N.

**[1]**

**2.** Give two reasons why multicellular organisms need to replicate their DNA. **[2]**

**3.** State the type of bond broken by helicase and the type of bond formed by DNA polymerase. **[2]**

**4.** A template strand reads 3' TTACGGCTA 5'. Deduce the base sequence of the new strand, labelling its 5' and 3' ends. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Bacteria grown for many generations in ¹⁵N were transferred to ¹⁴N. DNA samples were taken after each generation and centrifuged in a density gradient.

**(a)** Predict the bands expected after two generations if replication were conservative. **[2]**

**(b)** Two bands of equal intensity, intermediate and light, were observed after two generations. Explain why this result rules out dispersive replication. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of DNA molecules that would be in the light band after four generations, assuming semi-conservative replication. **[2]**

**8.** A scientist wants to amplify a 300 base pair region of a gene. The top strand begins 5' ACCTGAGTCC 3' and ends 5' GGATCATTGC 3' (short sequences shown for clarity).

**(a)** State the sequence of the primer that pairs with the bottom strand at the start of the region. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the sequence of the primer that pairs with the top strand at the end of the region, giving it in the 5' to 3' direction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the primers must be added in large excess at the start of the reaction. **[2]**

**(d)** A sample contains 50 copies of the target. Calculate the ideal number of copies after 20 cycles, giving your answer in standard form to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows fragment sizes, in base pairs, at four short tandem repeat regions in a paternity case.

| Region | Mother | Child | Man X | Man Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 140, 156 | 140, 168 | 168, 172 | 156, 168 |
| 2 | 88 | 88, 100 | 92, 100 | 88, 100 |
| 3 | 210, 226 | 218, 226 | 202, 218 | 210, 218 |
| 4 | 115, 131 | 115, 123 | 123, 139 | 119, 135 |

**(a)** For each region, identify the band the child must have inherited from the father. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether Man X or Man Y could be the father. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why a profile based on four regions is more reliable than one based on two. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) At a replication fork moving to the left, the upper template strand runs 5' (left) to 3' (right).

**(a)** Deduce whether the new strand on the upper template is the leading or the lagging strand. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why RNA primers are needed repeatedly on the lagging strand but only once on the leading strand. **[3]**

**11.** (HL only) A bacterial strain has a mutation that inactivates DNA ligase at high temperature.

**(a)** Predict the effect on the lagging strand when these bacteria are moved to the high temperature. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the leading strand is much less affected. **[1]**

**(c)** State the enzyme that would have removed the RNA primers before ligase acts. **[1]**

**12.** (HL only) DNA polymerase III has two activities: adding nucleotides and removing them from the 3' end.

**(a)** Describe how proofreading corrects a mismatched base. **[2]**

**(b)** A polymerase without proofreading makes 1 error per 10⁵ nucleotides added. Proofreading removes 99 % of these errors. Calculate the error rate with proofreading, as errors per nucleotide added. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why proofreading can only correct a mismatch at the 3' end of the new strand. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Each daughter molecule keeps one parental ¹⁵N strand and gains one new ¹⁴N strand. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** Any two from: growth, because every new cell made by mitosis needs a full set of genes [1]; tissue replacement or repair, because replacement cells need the full genome [1]; reproduction, because offspring need a full copy of the genome [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**3.** Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between complementary bases [1]. DNA polymerase forms covalent bonds between the sugar and phosphate of adjacent nucleotides [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** Correct bases AATGCCGAT [1], labelled 5' AATGCCGAT 3' [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*
*Examiner insight:* the new strand is antiparallel, so a template written 3' to 5' gives a new strand written 5' to 3' in the same left-to-right order.

**5.** PCR includes a denaturation step at about 95 °C [1]. Taq polymerase, from a bacterium of hot springs, is not denatured at this temperature, whereas human DNA polymerase would be, so Taq does not need replacing each cycle [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**6.** DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate groups, so it moves towards the positive electrode [1]. Every fragment has the same charge per unit length, so charge does not separate them [1]. The gel acts as a mesh, and shorter fragments move through it faster, so they travel further in a given time [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**7. (a)** A heavy band (the original molecule) [1] and a light band, in the ratio 1 heavy : 3 light [1].

**(b)** Dispersive replication would give every molecule a mixture of old and new DNA [1], so all DNA would form a single band of the same density (between intermediate and light), not two separate bands [1].

**(c)** After four generations there are 2⁴ = 16 molecules, of which 2 are hybrid and 14 light [1]. 14 ÷ 16 × 100 = 87.5 % [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**8. (a)** 5' ACCTGAGTCC 3' [1], the same as the top strand, because it pairs with the bottom strand.

**(b)** The primer must be complementary to 5' GGATCATTGC 3' [1], and written 5' to 3' it reads 5' GCAATGATCC 3' [1].

**(c)** Primers are used up because one is built into every new strand [1]; excess primers are also more likely to bind the template strands than the separated strands are to pair with each other again on cooling [1].

**(d)** 50 × 2²⁰ [1] = 52 428 800 = 5.24 × 10⁷ copies [1]. *(Total: 7 marks)*
*Examiner insight:* for (b), write the complement first, then reverse it; leaving it in the 3' to 5' order without labels is the usual error.

**9. (a)** Paternal bands: region 1, 168; region 2, 100; region 3, 218; region 4, 123. Two marks for all four correct; one mark for two or three correct [2].

**(b)** Man X has the paternal band at all four regions (168, 100, 218 and 123) [1]. Man Y lacks the paternal band 123 at region 4 [1], so Man Y is excluded and Man X could be the father, although the match shows a high probability rather than certainty [1].

**(c)** The chance of an unrelated man matching at each independent region multiplies [1], so each extra region makes a coincidental match much less likely [1]. *(Total: 7 marks)*
*Examiner insight:* at region 3 the child's 226 band comes from the mother, so 218 must come from the father; finding the maternal band first avoids errors.

**10. (a)** The new strand on the upper template runs antiparallel, 3' (left) to 5' (right), so it grows 5' to 3' from right to left [1]. The fork moves left, so this strand grows in the same direction as the fork and is the leading strand [1].

**(b)** DNA polymerase can only extend an existing strand from its 3' end, so it needs a primer to start [1]. The leading strand is extended continuously towards the fork, so one primer is enough [1]. The lagging strand is built away from the fork in short Okazaki fragments, each started as new template is exposed, so each needs a new primer [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**11. (a)** Okazaki fragments would still be made and their primers replaced [1], but the nicks between fragments would not be sealed, so the lagging strand would stay as separate fragments [1].

**(b)** The leading strand is made continuously, so it has very few gaps that need sealing [1].

**(c)** DNA polymerase I [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**12. (a)** The polymerase detects a nucleotide at the 3' end whose base is not paired correctly with the template [1], removes it and adds a correctly paired nucleotide in its place [1].

**(b)** 1 % of errors remain: 0.01 × (1 ÷ 10⁵) [1] = 1 error per 10⁷ nucleotides, or 1 × 10⁻⁷ errors per nucleotide [1].

**(c)** The polymerase is only working at the 3' end, where it has just added the nucleotide; bases further back have already been passed [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Predicting bands without the ratio, or forgetting that the conservative model keeps a heavy band.
- Giving a primer sequence without 5' and 3' labels, or forgetting to reverse the complement.
- Writing that gel electrophoresis separates by charge.
- Treating a full profile match as proof rather than as a probability.
- (HL) Mixing up the jobs of DNA polymerase I and DNA ligase.
- (HL) Describing the lagging strand as made 3' to 5'.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you find the primer sequence for the far end of a PCR target?**
Write the complement of the top strand at that end, then reverse it so the primer reads 5' to 3'. Always label both ends of any sequence you give.

**Why did Meselson and Stahl need two generations of data?**
The first generation ruled out conservative replication, but semi-conservative and dispersive models both predict one intermediate band. Only the second generation, with two separate bands, ruled out dispersive replication.

**Which parts of D1.1 are HL only?**
Directionality of DNA polymerase, leading and lagging strands, the roles of primase, polymerase I, polymerase III and ligase, and proofreading (D1.1.6 to D1.1.9).
