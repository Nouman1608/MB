Condensed notes on the Selection content (5.6 on this site) in the area of study The workforce, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and a costed example are in the [selection study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-selection/). Test yourself with the [selection practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-selection-practice/). All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- What is meant by selection.
- Content and purpose of selection methods: application forms, curriculum vitae (CV), testing, interviews, work trial, references and letter of application.
- Usefulness of methods; impact of selection on a business and its stakeholders.
- Recommend and justify methods of selection.

## Core recall

### Definition

- **Selection:** choosing the most suitable candidate from the applicants, by comparing each against the person specification. It follows recruitment.
- A good process is **valid**, **reliable**, **fair** and **affordable**.

### The methods

| Method | Purpose | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application form | Same information from all; fair shortlisting | Easy to compare | Time-consuming; little on personality |
| CV | Applicant's own summary of experience | Cheap; shows presentation | Formats differ; may exaggerate |
| Letter of application | Shows motivation and writing | Personal; shows understanding | May have outside help; slow to read |
| Testing (aptitude, psychometric, practical) | Objective measure of skills or traits | Consistent; predicts specific skills | Cost; may be unfair or irrelevant |
| Interview | Assess communication, motivation and fit | Two-way; allows probing | Costly; subjective; bias |
| Work trial | See real performance | Best evidence of ability | Time; disruption; candidate availability |
| References | Check facts; independent view | Confirms honesty | Usually positive; slow |

### The funnel

Screen → shortlist → test or trial → interview → references and checks → offer. Cheap methods early; costly ones for fewer candidates.

### Impact of selection

- Good: right person in right job; higher productivity; lower turnover; better teamwork; legal protection.
- Poor: wasted pay; errors; low morale; early resignations; re-recruitment cost.

### Matching method to post

| Post | Likely methods |
|---|---|
| Manager | Application, interview, psychometric test, references |
| Technical or craft | Practical test or work trial, interview |
| High-volume, low-skill | Form and short interview |
| Sensitive duties | Thorough references and checks |

## Worked reminder

Cardew Engineering (invented), 120 applications.

- Screening 120 × (10 ÷ 60) hour × £25 = **£500**; test 24 × £15 = **£360**; interviews 8 × 1.5 × 2 × £30 = **£720**; trials 3 × £140 = **£420**. Full process **£2,000**; short process (screen and interview) **£1,220**.
- Cost of a poor appointment £7,500; risk 25% (short) or 8% (full).
- Short: £1,220 + £1,875 = **£3,095**. Full: £2,000 + £600 = **£2,600**. Net benefit of the full process **£495**.
- If the full process cut the risk only to 18%, its cost would be £3,350, above the short process.

## Evaluating methods: six questions

1. Valid: does it test what the job needs?
2. Reliable and fair: consistent across assessors; no group disadvantaged?
3. Affordable: cost against the cost of a poor appointment?
4. Suited to the post and the number of candidates?
5. How does it feel to the candidate?
6. Legal: no discrimination?

## Quick case practice

A business (invented) receives 40 applications. Screening takes 6 minutes per application at £25 an hour. Five candidates are interviewed for one hour by two interviewers, each valued at £30 an hour.

- Screening = 40 × (6 ÷ 60) × £25 = **£100**.
- Interviews = 5 × 1 × 2 × £30 = **£300**.
- Total = **£400**.

Model response outline: the cost is small compared with the cost of a poor appointment. If the post needs a practical skill, add a work trial for the final two candidates. Judge the choice of methods by validity (does it test the job), fairness and cost, and use references to check facts late in the process.

A second check: if the work trial for the last two candidates costs £140 each, the full process costs £400 + £280 = £680. If it cuts the chance of a £6,000 poor appointment from 20% to 8%, the expected saving is 12% × £6,000 = £720, which is more than the extra £280. The trial is therefore worth its cost on these estimates, but only if the estimates are reliable.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Selection | Choosing the best candidate from the applicants |
| Shortlist | The candidates chosen to go forward |
| Psychometric test | Test of personality or reasoning |
| Work trial | A short period of doing the job under observation |
| Referee | A person who provides a reference |
| Valid | Measures what really predicts success |
| Reliable | Gives consistent results |

## Exam traps

- Selection follows recruitment; do not mix them up.
- Unstructured interviews are subjective; do not call them the best method for every job.
- Match methods to the job in the case.
- Include the cost of a poor appointment.
- Tests are objective only if they test the right thing.
- References are usually positive, so they check facts more than they discriminate.

## Self-test

1. Define selection.
2. State one purpose of an application form.
3. State one weakness of a CV compared with an application form.
4. Name three types of test.
5. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of an interview.
6. Why might a business use a work trial for a chef?
7. Screening 60 applications takes 10 minutes each at £30 an hour. Calculate the cost.
8. A poor appointment costs £6,000, and a process lowers the risk from 20% to 5%. Calculate the reduction in the expected cost.
9. Why are cheaper methods used first?
10. Give one stakeholder who is affected by selection and how.

### Answers

1. Choosing the most suitable candidate from the applicants.
2. To collect the same information from every applicant so they can be compared fairly.
3. CV formats differ, so comparing candidates is harder.
4. Any three: aptitude; psychometric; practical; personality; numeracy or literacy.
5. Advantage: two-way, allows probing. Disadvantage: costly and subjective.
6. It shows actual cooking skill and how the chef works in a kitchen team.
7. 60 × (10 ÷ 60) = 10 hours × £30 = **£300**.
8. Before: 20% × £6,000 = £1,200. After: 5% × £6,000 = £300. Reduction = **£900**.
9. They cost less per candidate and reduce the number who need the costlier methods.
10. Any one: colleagues (working with a good or poor team member); customers (service quality); unsuccessful candidates (fair treatment).
