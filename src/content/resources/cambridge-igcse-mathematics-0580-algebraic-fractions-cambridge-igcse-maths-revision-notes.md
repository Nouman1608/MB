These notes condense subtopic **2.3 Algebraic fractions** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). The whole subtopic is **Extended only** (E2.3); there is no Core content at C2.3. It is examined on Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). For full worked examples, read the [algebraic fractions study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-algebraic-fractions-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for marked exam-style questions, use the [algebraic fractions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-algebraic-fractions-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The two statements

- **E2.3.1** Manipulate algebraic fractions: add, subtract, multiply and divide, with numerical or linear algebraic denominators.
- **E2.3.2** Factorise and simplify rational expressions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Algebraic (rational) expression | A fraction whose numerator or denominator contains a letter, e.g. (x + 1)/(x² − 4) |
| Factor | Something that multiplies the whole numerator or the whole denominator |
| Term | Something added or subtracted; terms never cancel |
| Common denominator | A denominator that every fraction can be rewritten over |
| Lowest common denominator (LCD) | The smallest such denominator: each different factor appears once, at its highest power |
| Reciprocal | The fraction turned upside down; dividing by a/b is multiplying by b/a |
| Simplest form | No factor is shared by the numerator and the denominator |

## Condensed recall

### Simplifying

```
1. Factorise the numerator fully.
2. Factorise the denominator fully.
3. Cancel factors that appear in both.
4. Write what remains; leave it factorised.
```

Factorising patterns you will meet (from subtopic 2.2):

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Common factor | 4x + 12 = 4(x + 3) |
| Difference of two squares | x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) |
| Quadratic | x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3) |
| Quadratic, a ≠ 1 | 2x² − 7x + 3 = (2x − 1)(x − 3) |
| Reversed bracket | 10 − 5x = −5(x − 2) |

A bracket and its reverse, such as (a − b) and (b − a), cancel to leave **−1**.

### Adding and subtracting

```
1. Factorise each denominator.
2. Build the LCD: every different factor once, at its highest power.
3. Multiply each numerator by whatever its denominator is missing.
4. Combine numerators over the LCD, using brackets for anything subtracted.
5. Simplify the numerator, factorise it, and cancel if possible.
```

Quick example: 1/(x − 3) − 6/(x² − 9). LCD (x − 3)(x + 3). Numerator (x + 3) − 6 = x − 3. Result (x − 3)/((x − 3)(x + 3)) = 1/(x + 3).

### Multiplying and dividing

- **Multiply:** factorise everything, cancel any factor on a top with the same factor on any bottom, then multiply what is left.
- **Divide:** invert the second fraction only, then multiply.

Quick example: (2a/5) × (15/(4a²)) = 30a/(20a²) = 3/(2a).

### Which method? Read the operation first

| You see | First move | Typical final form |
|---|---|---|
| One fraction, "simplify" | Factorise top and bottom | A shorter fraction, e.g. (x + 3)/(x − 2) |
| Fractions joined by + or − | Factorise denominators, find the LCD | One numerator over a factorised denominator |
| Fractions joined by × | Factorise everything, cancel diagonally too | Often a whole expression or a simple fraction |
| Fractions joined by ÷ | Factorise, then invert the second fraction | As for × |

## Exam traps

- **Cancelling across a plus or minus.** (x + 6)/(x + 3) is already in its simplest form; the answer is not 2 and not 6/3.
- **The subtracted numerator.** In (3x + 1)/5 − (x − 2)/2 the second numerator becomes −5(x − 2) = −5x + 10. Writing −5x − 10 is the commonest single error.
- **The −1 from a reversed bracket.** (x² − 3x)/(9 − x²) simplifies to −x/(x + 3), not x/(x + 3).
- **A non-lowest common denominator.** Multiplying x(x + 3) by (x − 3)(x + 3) gives (x + 3) twice. The answer may still be correct after cancelling, but the extra algebra costs time and invites errors.
- **Stopping before the end.** Always factorise the final numerator. 1/(x − 3) − 6/(x² − 9) gives (x − 3)/((x − 3)(x + 3)), which still cancels.
- **Checking with a forbidden value.** A substitution check must avoid any value that makes a denominator zero.

## Self-test

1. Simplify 6x²y/(9xy³).
2. Simplify (4x + 12)/(x² − 9).
3. Simplify (x² + 5x + 6)/(x² − 4).
4. Simplify (2x² − 7x + 3)/(4x² − 1).
5. Simplify (10 − 5x)/(x² − 4x + 4).
6. Write x/4 + (2x − 1)/6 as a single fraction.
7. Write (3x + 1)/5 − (x − 2)/2 as a single fraction.
8. Write 3/(x + 1) + 2/(x − 4) as a single fraction.
9. Write 1/(x − 3) − 6/(x² − 9) as a single fraction in its simplest form.
10. Simplify (2a/5) × (15/(4a²)).
11. Simplify ((x² − 16)/(3x)) ÷ ((x + 4)/(9x²)).
12. Simplify (x² − 3x)/(9 − x²).

### Answers

1. **2x/(3y²)** (divide 6 and 9 by 3; cancel one x and one y).
2. 4(x + 3)/((x − 3)(x + 3)) = **4/(x − 3)**.
3. (x + 2)(x + 3)/((x − 2)(x + 2)) = **(x + 3)/(x − 2)**.
4. (2x − 1)(x − 3)/((2x − 1)(2x + 1)) = **(x − 3)/(2x + 1)**.
5. −5(x − 2)/(x − 2)² = **−5/(x − 2)**.
6. (3x + 2(2x − 1))/12 = **(7x − 2)/12**.
7. (2(3x + 1) − 5(x − 2))/10 = (6x + 2 − 5x + 10)/10 = **(x + 12)/10**.
8. (3(x − 4) + 2(x + 1))/((x + 1)(x − 4)) = **5(x − 2)/((x + 1)(x − 4))**.
9. (x + 3 − 6)/((x − 3)(x + 3)) = **1/(x + 3)**.
10. **3/(2a)**.
11. ((x − 4)(x + 4)/(3x)) × (9x²/(x + 4)) = **3x(x − 4)**.
12. x(x − 3)/(−(x − 3)(x + 3)) = **−x/(x + 3)**.
