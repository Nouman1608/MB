These questions cover sub-topic 4(b), **Feeding relationships** (specification points 4.6 to 4.9), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered and all four points can be examined on either paper. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

## Questions

**1.** Here is a food chain: oak leaves → caterpillar → blue tit → sparrowhawk.

**(a)** Name the producer. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the secondary consumer. **[1]**
**(c)** State what the arrows in the food chain show. **[1]**
**(d)** Name the type of organism that releases nutrients from dead sparrowhawks back into the soil. **[1]**

**2.** The food web shows these feeding relationships: grass is eaten by rabbits and grasshoppers; grasshoppers are eaten by frogs and shrews; frogs are eaten by snakes; shrews and rabbits are eaten by owls; snakes are eaten by owls.

**(a)** Write out one food chain with **four** organisms. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** trophic levels occupied by the owl in this web. **[2]**
**(c)** Disease kills most of the grasshoppers. Explain **one** effect this has on the frogs and **one** effect on the grass. **[4]**

**3.** The table gives the dry mass of organisms in a lake at one time.

| Trophic level | Dry mass in kg |
|---|---|
| Algae | 600 |
| Water fleas | 120 |
| Small fish | 30 |
| Pike | 6 |

**(a)** Explain why dry mass is used and not fresh mass. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of the algae as a multiple of the mass of the pike. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the pyramid of biomass. **[1]**

**4.** Producers in a field capture 12 000 kJ per m² per year. The primary consumers receive 1080 kJ and the secondary consumers receive 97.2 kJ.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the energy transferred from producers to primary consumers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage transferred from primary to secondary consumers. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare your answers with the figure usually quoted for the transfer of energy between trophic levels. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **three** reasons why not all of the energy in the food eaten by a primary consumer becomes new biomass in its body. **[3]**

**6.** A lamb takes in 200 kJ of energy in grass. Of this, 60 kJ is lost in faeces, 110 kJ is released in respiration and 10 kJ is lost in urine. The rest is used for growth.

**(a)** Calculate the energy used for growth. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate this energy as a percentage of the energy taken in. **[1]**
**(c)** State what happens to the energy released by respiration. **[1]**

**7.** One oak tree supports 2500 caterpillars. The caterpillars are eaten by 30 blue tits.

**(a)** Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers for this food chain. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a pyramid of biomass for the same chain would have a different shape. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why most food chains have no more than four or five trophic levels. **[3]**

**9.** Farmers in a region can use a field to grow wheat for people to eat, or to graze cattle that people eat as beef. The field gets the same amount of sunlight in each case.

Use ideas about energy transfer to suggest why the field can feed more people if it is used to grow wheat. **[4]**

**10.** Compare the way in which energy and carbon compounds move through an ecosystem. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Oak leaves [1]
**(b)** Blue tit [1]
**(c)** The direction in which energy (and substances) move / from the food to the feeder [1]
**(d)** Decomposers (accept bacteria and fungi) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "secondary consumer" eats the primary consumer. Count from the producer, not from the top of the chain.

**2.** **(a)** Any valid four-organism chain, for example grass → grasshopper → frog → snake [2] (1 mark for the correct organisms, 1 mark for arrows in the correct direction)
**(b)** Any two of: secondary consumer (eating rabbits, which are primary consumers); tertiary consumer (eating shrews, which are secondary consumers); quaternary consumer (eating snakes, which are tertiary consumers) [2]
**(c)** Frogs: they lose a food source, so their numbers fall (or they eat more of other prey) [2]; grass: fewer grasshoppers eat it, so the amount of grass increases (accept rabbits eat more grass if they increase) [2]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the owl sits at more than one level because it eats from several chains. Work out the level of each prey animal first, then add one.

**3.** **(a)** The water content of organisms varies, so dry mass gives a fairer comparison of the actual living material [1]
**(b)** 600 ÷ 6 = **100 times** [1]
**(c)** The bars become narrower at each higher level, giving a pyramid shape [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the answer is a multiple, so no unit is needed. "100 kg" is not correct.

**4.** **(a)** 1080 ÷ 12 000 × 100 [1] = **9.0%** [1]
**(b)** 97.2 ÷ 1080 × 100 [1] = **9.0%** [1]
**(c)** Both are close to (about) 10%, the value usually quoted [1]
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the energy at the lower level in each case. Dividing by the energy of the upper level gives 11.1%, which is wrong.

**5.** Any three of: some energy is released as heat in respiration [1]; some parts, such as bones, roots or shells, are not eaten [1]; some food is not digested and leaves in faeces [1]; some energy is lost in excretion (urea) [1]; energy is used for movement or to keep warm [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each reason must be separate. "Lost as heat" and "released in respiration" are the same point.

**6.** **(a)** 200 − 60 − 110 − 10 = **20 kJ** [1]
**(b)** 20 ÷ 200 × 100 = **10%** [1]
**(c)** It is released as heat to the surroundings (accept used for movement and then lost as heat) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Energy that leaves the lamb is transferred, not destroyed. "Used up" does not earn the mark.

**7.** **(a)** The bottom bar (oak tree) is narrow / one organism [1]; the middle bar (caterpillars) is very wide and the top bar (blue tits) is narrow, so it is not a pyramid shape [1]
**(b)** Biomass takes account of the mass of each organism, and one large oak has a very large mass [1]; so the bottom bar is wide and the pyramid narrows upward [1]
*Examiner insight:* The fact that the oak tree is large is the key explanation. Saying "biomass is different" is not enough.

**8.** Only about 10% of the energy passes to the next trophic level [1]; so the amount of energy falls rapidly at each level [1]; after four or five levels there is not enough energy left to support another level / population [1]
*Examiner insight:* Include the figure "about 10%" in your answer. It supports the reasoning and is easy to credit.

**9.** Wheat is eaten by people directly, so people are the primary consumers [1]; beef involves an extra trophic level, as people eat cattle that eat the plants [1]; about 90% of the energy is lost between trophic levels, mostly as heat and in waste [1]; so more of the energy captured by the plants reaches people when they eat wheat [1]
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer counts the trophic levels. "Plants are healthier" is not an energy argument.

**10.** Energy enters ecosystems as light, absorbed by producers [1]; energy moves along the chain as chemical energy in food and is lost as heat [1]; energy is not recycled, so a constant supply from the Sun is needed [1]; carbon compounds pass along the chain as organisms eat each other [1]; decomposers return carbon (as carbon dioxide or in waste) so carbon compounds are recycled [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides. Marks go for naming one thing that is recycled and one thing that is not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up the order of arrows in a food chain.
- Counting trophic levels from the top.
- Dividing by the wrong energy value in an efficiency calculation.
- Saying energy is "used up" or "recycled".
- Using fresh mass for a pyramid of biomass.
- Forgetting that an omnivore sits at two trophic levels.
