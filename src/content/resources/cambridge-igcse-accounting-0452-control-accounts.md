This guide covers sub-topic **3.4 Control accounts**. The syllabus asks for "the purposes of purchases ledger and sales ledger control accounts", "the books of prime entry as sources of information for the control account entries", the preparation of both accounts "to include credit purchases and sales, receipts and payments, cash discounts, returns, irrecoverable debts, dishonoured cheques, interest on overdue accounts, contra entries, refunds, opening and closing balances (debit and credit within each account)", and "the impact of digital recording of business transactions on the preparation and use of control accounts". It notes that candidates "do not need to reconcile control account balances with the sales and purchases ledger balances".

The examples follow Westbrook Electrical for June 2027. Amounts are in dollars. Use this guide with the [3.4 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-control-accounts-revision-notes/) and [3.4 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-control-accounts-practice/). The broader [control accounts guide](/resources/igcse-accounting-control-accounts/) has further examples.

## 1. What a control account is for

A **control account** is a summary account. The sales ledger control account records, in total, every entry made in all the individual customers' accounts; the purchases ledger control account does the same for suppliers. Its closing balance should equal the total of the individual balances in that ledger.

**Purposes**

- **An arithmetic check on one ledger.** If the trial balance does not agree, the control accounts show whether the error is likely to be in the sales ledger, the purchases ledger or elsewhere.
- **Quick totals.** Total trade receivables and trade payables are available for the trial balance and financial statements without adding up every account.
- **Control over staff.** If one person keeps the individual accounts and another the control account, an error or a fraud by one is more likely to be noticed.
- **Information for managers**, such as how much customers owe in total, which helps with credit control.

## 2. Where each entry comes from

The control account is written up from the **totals** in the books of prime entry, not from the individual ledger accounts.

| Item | Sales ledger control | Purchases ledger control | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit sales / credit purchases | Debit | Credit | Sales journal / purchases journal |
| Returns | Credit (sales returns) | Debit (purchases returns) | Sales returns journal / purchases returns journal |
| Receipts from customers / payments to suppliers | Credit | Debit | Cash book |
| Cash discounts | Credit (discount allowed) | Debit (discount received) | Cash book discount columns |
| Dishonoured cheques | Debit (customer's cheque dishonoured) | Credit (our cheque dishonoured) | Cash book |
| Refunds | Debit (refund paid to a customer) | Credit (refund received from a supplier) | Cash book |
| Irrecoverable debts | Credit | | General journal |
| Interest on overdue accounts | Debit (charged to customers) | Credit (charged by suppliers) | General journal |
| Contra entries | Credit | Debit | General journal |

**Two reasoning shortcuts.** Anything that increases what customers owe is a debit in the sales ledger control account; anything that increases what the business owes suppliers is a credit in the purchases ledger control account. A **contra** sets off an amount owed by a customer who is also a supplier: both what the customer owes and what the business owes the supplier fall, so it is a credit in sales and a debit in purchases.

**Items that never appear:** cash sales and cash purchases (no customer or supplier account is involved), trade discount, and the allowance for irrecoverable debts (it is a separate account; only debts actually written off go in the control account).

The cash sales of 5,100 are left out. The closing **debit** balance is the missing figure: the debit side totals 43,850, and the credit entries other than it total 24,330, so it is 43,850 - 24,330 = 19,520. Both closing balances are brought down: 19,520 is trade receivables, and the 135 owed *to* customers who have overpaid is shown with other payables.

The refund received is a **credit**: it reverses part of an earlier payment, which was a debit. The closing credit balance of 12,730 is the missing figure: 28,335 - (95 + 540 + 13,920 + 310 + 740) = 12,730. The contra of 740 appears in both control accounts, on opposite sides.

## 5. Using a control account to find a missing figure

Because the account must balance, any one unknown can be found. Suppose Westbrook's records for July were incomplete and the credit sales figure was lost. With every other July entry and both closing balances known, total the credit side, deduct the known debit entries, and the remainder is credit sales. This is the same skill used when records are incomplete in Topic 5.

## 6. Digital recording and control accounts

| Effect of digital recording | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Control accounts are produced automatically as transactions are posted | They are always up to date and need no separate preparation |
| Totals come from the same postings as the individual accounts | Arithmetic differences between the two are almost impossible, so the arithmetic check matters less |
| Input errors still pass through | A receipt posted to the wrong customer, or an invoice entered at the wrong amount, affects both the control account and the ledger equally and is not revealed |
| Reports can be produced at any time | Managers can use the control totals for credit control, such as listing overdue accounts |
| Access rights can be set for different staff | Some of the separation-of-duties control can be kept |

So in a digital system the control account is still useful as a summary and for management, but it is no longer an independent check that a second person has prepared.

## Common mistakes

- Including cash sales or cash purchases.
- Putting a dishonoured cheque on the credit side of the sales ledger control account; it reinstates a debt, so it is a debit.
- Entering the allowance for irrecoverable debts, or a change in it, in the control account.
- Putting a contra on the same side in both control accounts.
- Forgetting to bring down **both** closing balances when the account has debit and credit balances.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** a control account with dates, clear details naming the source or the other account, a balance c/d on each side where needed, and balances b/d.
- When a list includes distractors, tick each item as used or rejected; cash sales, trade discount and the allowance are the usual traps.
- For a missing figure, label it clearly (for example "Balance c/d (missing figure)") and show the subtraction.
- In **explain** questions on purposes, link the purpose to a benefit: "it shows which ledger contains an error, so less time is spent searching".
- The syllabus does not require you to reconcile a control account with a list of ledger balances.
