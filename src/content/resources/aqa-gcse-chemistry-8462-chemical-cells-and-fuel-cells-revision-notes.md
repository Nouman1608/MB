These notes condense **4.5.2 Chemical cells and fuel cells (chemistry only)** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 1 content for the separate chemistry
qualification. Writing fuel cell half equations is Higher tier only. For explanations use the
[chemical cells and fuel cells study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-cells-and-fuel-cells/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-cells-and-fuel-cells-practice/).

## Core facts

- A **cell** contains chemicals that react to produce **electricity**.
- A **simple cell**: **two different metals** in contact with an **electrolyte**.
- Voltage depends on the **type of electrode** and the **electrolyte**.
- The **bigger the difference in reactivity** of the two metals, the **bigger the voltage**.
- A **battery** is **two or more cells connected in series** (greater voltage).
- **Non-rechargeable** (for example alkaline): reactions stop when a reactant is **used up**.
- **Rechargeable**: reactions are **reversed** by an external current.

## Simple cell data pattern

| Pair | Voltage / V | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Mg and Cu | 2.7 | Large difference in reactivity |
| Zn and Cu | 1.1 | Smaller difference |
| Fe and Cu | 0.8 | Smaller still |
| Cu and Cu | 0.0 | Same metal, no difference |

Rule: **order the metals by reactivity** and the voltage follows the gap. The more reactive metal is the one
that loses electrons.

## Cells and batteries compared

| Feature | Non-rechargeable | Rechargeable |
|---|---|---|
| Reactions | Irreversible in use | Can be reversed |
| Lifetime | Single discharge | Many charge cycles |
| Example uses | Torch, clock | Phone, laptop |
| Drawbacks | Waste when flat | Wear out, charger needed |

## Hydrogen fuel cells

- Supplied from outside with **hydrogen (fuel)** and **oxygen or air**.
- Fuel is **oxidised electrochemically** in the cell to give a **potential difference**.
- Overall: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**.
- Only product at point of use: **water**.
- Does not need recharging while fuel is supplied.
- A potential alternative to rechargeable cells and batteries.

## Higher tier only: fuel cell half equations

| Electrode | Half equation (acidic electrolyte) | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ | oxidation |
| Positive | O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O | reduction |

Electrons and H⁺ cancel when added, leaving 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. In an alkaline cell: 2H₂ + 4OH⁻ → 4H₂O + 4e⁻ and
O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.

## Evaluation: fuel cell versus rechargeable battery

| Fuel cell | Rechargeable battery |
|---|---|
| Quick to refuel | Long to recharge |
| Only water at point of use | No emissions in use |
| Runs as long as fuel supplied | Limited by stored charge |
| Hydrogen is flammable and hard to store | Simple and widely available |
| Hydrogen often made from fossil fuels | Electricity may come from fossil fuels |
| Expensive cell and few filling stations | Cheaper for small devices |

Finish with a **judgement**: which suits which use, with a reason.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cell | A unit that uses a chemical reaction to produce a voltage |
| Battery | Two or more cells connected in series |
| Electrolyte | Liquid or solution that conducts through its ions |
| Fuel cell | Cell supplied with fuel and oxygen, giving electricity |
| Series | Connected end to end so voltages add |
| Rechargeable | Reactions can be reversed by an external current |

## Exam traps

- **Same metal twice.** A simple cell needs two **different** metals.
- **"Burning" in a fuel cell.** The fuel is oxidised electrochemically, not in a flame.
- **"No pollution."** Hydrogen production may release carbon dioxide.
- **Cell and battery mixed up.** A battery is two or more cells.
- **Missing the reason a battery goes flat.** A reactant has been used up.
- **Electrons on the wrong side.** Oxidation: electrons on the right of the arrow.
- **One-sided evaluation.** Give both advantages and disadvantages, then a conclusion.

## Quick self-test

1. What is needed to make a simple cell?
2. How does the voltage change as the reactivity gap between the metals increases?
3. What is a battery?
4. Why does an alkaline battery stop working?
5. How are rechargeable batteries recharged?
6. What is the fuel in a hydrogen fuel cell?
7. Give the overall equation for the hydrogen fuel cell.
8. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a hydrogen fuel cell compared with a rechargeable battery.
9. A cell using zinc and copper gives 1.1 V. One using magnesium and copper gives 2.7 V. Which metal is more reactive, zinc or magnesium?
10. (Higher tier only) Write the half equation for the reaction of hydrogen at the negative electrode in an acidic fuel cell.
11. (Higher tier only) Write the half equation for oxygen at the positive electrode.
12. (Higher tier only) Is oxygen oxidised or reduced in the fuel cell? Explain.

### Answers

1. Two different metals as electrodes in contact with an electrolyte.
2. The voltage increases.
3. Two or more cells connected in series.
4. One of the reactants has been used up.
5. By passing an external electrical current, which reverses the chemical reactions.
6. Hydrogen (with oxygen or air).
7. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
8. Advantage: only water is produced / quick to refuel. Disadvantage: hydrogen is hard to store / flammable / few refuelling points.
9. Magnesium (the larger voltage with the same partner metal shows a bigger reactivity difference from copper).
10. 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻.
11. O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O.
12. Reduced, because it gains electrons.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain why a cell using magnesium and copper gives a higher voltage than one using zinc and copper.** "Magnesium
is more reactive than zinc, so the difference in reactivity between magnesium and copper is larger, which gives a
larger voltage."

**Explain why an alkaline battery cannot be recharged.** "The reactions cannot be reversed by passing a current, and
the battery stops when a reactant is used up."

**Give one environmental advantage of a hydrogen fuel cell.** "The only product at the point of use is water, so
there are no carbon dioxide emissions from the vehicle."

## Interpreting voltage tables

1. Rank the pairs by voltage.
2. Match the order to the reactivity of the metal paired with the common metal.
3. A pair of identical metals gives 0 V.
4. Cells in series add their voltages: three 1.1 V cells give **3.3 V**.
