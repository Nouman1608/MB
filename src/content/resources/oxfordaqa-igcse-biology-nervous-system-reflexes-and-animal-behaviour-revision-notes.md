## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | A change in the surroundings |
| Receptor | A cell that detects a stimulus |
| Neurone | A nerve cell that carries impulses |
| Impulse | The electrical signal in a neurone |
| CNS | Central nervous system: brain and spinal cord |
| Coordinator | Part of the CNS that processes information and sends instructions |
| Effector | A muscle or a gland |
| Synapse | Junction between two neurones where a chemical is released |
| Reflex | An automatic, rapid response, often involving sensory, relay and motor neurones |

## 3.4.1 The human nervous system

### Main points

- The nervous system lets humans react to surroundings and coordinate behaviour.
- Receptors send impulses along neurones to the CNS. The brain coordinates the response.
- Reflexes are automatic and rapid and often protect the body.

### The reflex arc in order

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Stimulus detected by a receptor |
| 2 | Impulses pass along a sensory neurone to the CNS |
| 3 | Chemical released at the synapse to the relay neurone, which sends an impulse |
| 4 | Chemical released at the synapse to the motor neurone, which sends impulses |
| 5 | Motor neurone carries impulses to the effector |
| 6 | Muscle contracts or gland secretes |

### Neurones at a glance

| Type | Direction | Located |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Receptor to CNS | Mostly outside the CNS |
| Relay | Between sensory and motor | Inside the CNS |
| Motor | CNS to effector | Mostly outside the CNS |

### Effectors

| Effector | Response |
|---|---|
| Muscle | Contracts |
| Gland | Releases (secretes) chemical substances |

### Method: analysing any behaviour

1. Name the **stimulus** (the change).
2. Name the **receptor** that detects it (by organ or cell type).
3. Name the **coordinator** (CNS: brain or spinal cord).
4. Name the **effector** (muscle or gland).
5. State the **response** (contracts, secretes, moves).

Worked reminder: a bright light shone in the eye makes the pupil narrower. Stimulus: bright light. Receptor: light receptors in the eye. Coordinator: the CNS. Effector: muscle in the iris. Response: the pupil narrows.

Another: the smell of food makes the mouth water. Effector: salivary gland. Response: saliva is secreted.

### Must-know distinctions

- Impulse (electrical, along a neurone) versus chemical (crosses the synapse).
- Muscle (contracts) versus gland (secretes).
- Sensory (towards the CNS) versus motor (away from the CNS).
- Reflex (automatic, fast) versus voluntary action (conscious, slower).

### Speed check

Time = distance / speed. A 0.9 m pathway at 60 m/s takes 0.9 / 60 = 0.015 s, which is 15 ms. Fewer neurones and synapses means a quicker response.

## 3.4.6 Behaviour

### Courtship and mating

- Finding and selecting a suitable mate is part of sexual reproduction.
- Courtship behaviour can advertise the quality of an individual.
- Mating strategies: a mate for life, several mates over a lifetime, a mate for a breeding season, several mates over one breeding season.

| Strategy | Example | Possible advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Mate for life | Albatrosses | Both parents work as a pair to rear young |
| Several mates over a lifetime | Birds with a new partner in later years | Chance to choose again if a partner is lost |
| Mate for a breeding season | Emperor penguins | Both parents share care for one cycle |
| Several mates in a season | Male red deer | More offspring carrying his genes |

### Parental care

- Special behaviours for rearing young: feeding, guarding, warming, nest building.
- Benefits: more offspring survive, and more parental genes are passed on by the offspring.
- Risks to parents: energy used, no time to feed, injury from predators, easier for predators to find, less energy for later broods.

Worked reminder: 26 of 40 guarded eggs survive, which is 65 %. 2 of 40 unguarded eggs survive, which is 5.0 %.

### Types of behaviour

| Type | Key idea | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Innate | Inborn, not learned | Hatchling sea turtles heading to the sea |
| Imprinting | Early attachment to the first moving object seen | Goslings following a parent |
| Habituation | Learning to ignore a repeated harmless stimulus | Pigeons and traffic |
| Classical conditioning | Link between a neutral stimulus and a natural stimulus | Dog salivating to a bell |
| Operant conditioning | Trial and error, with reward or punishment | Rat pressing a lever for food |

Classical conditioning is the same as "classic conditioning" in the specification.

### Training animals

| Animal | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sniffer dog | Reward when the target scent is found | The dog repeats the search and indicates the scent |
| Police horse | Repeated safe exposure to crowds, flags and noise, with rewards for calm | The horse stops reacting to the stimuli |

### Communication signals

| Signal | Example | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sound | Bird song, alarm calls | Carries far, works in the dark | Can attract predators |
| Chemical | Ant trails, scent marking | Lasts after the animal leaves | Slow to change |
| Visual | Peacock tail display, posture | Quick and clear | Needs light and line of sight |

Worked reminder (habituation): a snail withdrawn for 42 s on tap 1 and 4 s on tap 20 shows a (42 - 4) / 42 x 100 = 90.5 % decrease.

## Model short answers to learn

**Why is a reflex action quick?** It is automatic, so there is no time spent on conscious decision-making. The pathway often has only three neurones and two synapses, and the response does not wait for conscious thought.

**Why is a reflex useful?** It protects the body from harm, for example by pulling a hand away from a sharp object before serious damage occurs.

**Describe what happens at a synapse.** Impulses reach the end of one neurone. A chemical is released. It crosses the gap and causes an impulse in the next neurone.

**Why do some animals use courtship?** It lets an animal advertise its quality to a possible mate and lets the other animal judge it before mating. Two animals of the same species find each other and are ready to mate together.

**Explain how parental care helps the parent's genes survive.** The offspring are fed, kept warm and protected, so more of them survive to adulthood. When they breed they pass on copies of the parent's genes.

**Explain how a sniffer dog is trained.** The handler rewards the dog with food or a toy when it finds the target scent. The dog links finding the scent with the reward and repeats the behaviour. This is operant conditioning.

**Explain why a police horse is exposed to crowds and flags during training.** The horse learns that the stimuli are harmless. It stops showing a fear response (habituation) and stays calm on duty. Rewarding calm behaviour adds operant conditioning.

**Compare sound and chemical signals.** Sound reaches a distant animal quickly and works at night, but it stops when the animal stops calling. A chemical signal stays in the environment after the animal has left, but it changes slowly and is not easy to direct.

## Worked data reminders

| Situation | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage survival, 18 of 30 | 18 / 30 x 100 | 60 % |
| Percentage decrease, 42 s to 4 s | (42 - 4) / 42 x 100 | 90.5 % |
| Saliva drops after 15 pairings compared with 5 pairings (11 and 2) | 11 / 2 | 5.5 times as many |
| Reflex path 1.2 m at 60 m/s | 1.2 / 60 | 0.020 s (20 ms) |

## Quick self-test

1. What is the CNS made of?
2. Which type of neurone carries impulses from a receptor to the CNS?
3. Where is the relay neurone located?
4. What crosses a synapse?
5. Name the two kinds of effector and what each does.
6. Give the order: stimulus, response, coordinator, receptor, effector.
7. A reflex pathway is 0.6 m long and impulses travel at 60 m/s. How long does the impulse take to travel it?
8. Give two risks of parental care to a parent.
9. Which type of behaviour is learned by linking a neutral stimulus with a natural one?
10. A horse stops reacting to a flag after repeated safe exposure. What is this called?
11. Name three types of signal used in animal communication.
12. In a fish nest, 18 of 30 guarded eggs hatch and survive. What is the percentage?

### Answers

1. The brain and spinal cord.
2. A sensory neurone.
3. Inside the CNS.
4. A chemical (released from one neurone, causing an impulse in the next).
5. Muscle (contracts) and gland (secretes).
6. Stimulus, receptor, coordinator, effector, response.
7. 0.6 / 60 = 0.010 s (10 ms).
8. Any two from: uses up energy reserves; cannot feed while guarding; injury or death from predators; attracts predators to the nest; less energy for later breeding.
9. Classical conditioning.
10. Habituation.
11. Sound, chemical, visual.
12. 18 / 30 x 100 = 60 %.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "message" or "nerve" instead of "impulse" and "neurone".
- Saying a muscle secretes or a gland contracts.
- Putting the relay neurone outside the CNS.
- Missing the chemical at the synapse, or saying an electrical impulse jumps across it.
- Giving only one risk of parental care when two are asked for.
- Describing imprinting without the early, short period.
- Confusing habituation with conditioning.
- Naming a signal type without saying what it is used for in the example.
- Leaving out units in a speed or time calculation.
