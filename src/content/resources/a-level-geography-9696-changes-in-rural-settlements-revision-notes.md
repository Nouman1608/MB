Condensed recall for **6.1 Changes in rural settlements** (depopulation, service provision and a required case study). For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rural settlement | Village, hamlet or farm outside urban areas |
| Rural depopulation | Fall in rural population, usually through out-migration |
| Counterurbanisation | Movement from cities to rural areas |
| Commuter village | Village whose residents work in a nearby town |
| Service provision | Shops, schools, health, transport, broadband |
| Gentrification | Wealthier incomers raise property prices |
| Nucleated / dispersed / linear | Clustered / scattered / along a route |

## Contrasting contexts

| | LIC / MIC | HIC |
|---|---|---|
| Main trend | Rural-urban migration, depopulation, farmland pressure | Counterurbanisation near cities; depopulation in remote areas |
| Issues | Poor services, low incomes, remittances, ageing left-behind households | Ageing, house prices, service closures, second homes |

## Cycle of rural decline

Out-migration of young adults, fewer children, school or shop closes, services decline, further out-migration. In accessible areas the reverse can occur: incomers raise demand and prices, which can price out local residents.

## Responses

Affordable housing, community-run shops and transport, broadband, rural business support, planning policy to protect services, and development of tourism with careful limits. Evaluate: schemes can be costly, depend on volunteers and may not reverse population loss.

## Required case study

The syllabus requires a case study of **a rural settlement or area**. Cover: location, recent population and economic change, causes (migration, urban growth), impacts on people and services, and responses with an evaluation. Use only facts you have checked.

## Exam traps

- Distinguish depopulation (loss) from counterurbanisation (gain).
- Do not claim all rural areas decline: accessibility matters.
- Impacts differ for long-standing residents and newcomers.

## Self-test

1. Define rural depopulation.
2. What is a commuter village?
3. Why might house prices rise in accessible rural areas?
4. Give one response to service decline.
5. Name three rural settlement patterns.
6. Why does rural-urban migration cause ageing?

**Answers:** 1. Loss of rural population, usually by out-migration. 2. A village whose residents work in a nearby town or city. 3. Incomers with higher incomes increase demand. 4. Community-run shops, rural transport, broadband or affordable housing. 5. Nucleated, dispersed, linear. 6. Young adults leave, so a larger share of those remaining are old.
