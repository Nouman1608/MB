## Answers

**1.** A [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** They protect the flower in the bud [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The transfer of pollen [1] from an anther to a stigma [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Water [1], oxygen [1], a suitable temperature [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

**(a)** Flower P [1].

**(b)** Anthers hang outside, so the wind can blow the pollen away [1, 1]. Stigma is large and feathery and outside, so it can catch pollen from the air [1, 1].

**(c)** Insect-pollinated pollen is sticky or spiny and sticks to insects [1]; wind-pollinated pollen is smooth and light and made in large numbers [1]. **[7]**

**7.** The pollen grain grows a pollen tube [1]. The tube grows down the style [1] towards the ovary and enters the ovule [1]. The nucleus of the male gamete passes down the tube [1] and fuses with the nucleus of the egg cell, which is fertilisation [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** Self-pollination is the transfer of pollen to a stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant [1]. Cross-pollination is the transfer to a flower on a different plant of the same species [1].

**(b)** Cross-pollination gives more variation [1], because genes from two different plants are combined [1]. It gives a better chance of some offspring surviving a change in the environment [1] as natural selection can act on the variation [1]. **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** 23 divided by 25 x 100 = 92 per cent [1].

**(b)** Seeds need water to germinate [1], to rehydrate the seed and allow the enzymes to work [1].

**(c)** The seeds had little oxygen [1], so they could not respire aerobically to release the energy for growth [1].

**(d)** It is the control [1]. **[6]**

**10.**

**(a)** Sycamore: wind dispersal [1]. Burdock: animal dispersal [1].

**(b)** The wing increases the surface area so that the wind carries the fruit away [1]. The hooks catch on the fur of animals and are carried away [1].

**(c)** Any two of: colonise new areas; reduce competition with the parent plant; reduce competition between seedlings [1 each]. **[6]**

**11.** When the seed takes in water, enzymes become active [1]. They break down stored food, for example starch to sugars and proteins to amino acids [1]. The soluble products are used by the embryo for respiration and growth [1]. **[3]**

**12.** (a) It protects the seed [1]. (b) It is the embryo root [1]. (c) They store food for the embryo [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing pollination with fertilisation.
- Saying that pollen is the female gamete.
- Writing that the ovary becomes the seed.
- Forgetting oxygen as a condition for germination.
- Not linking a feature (such as hooks or wings) to how it helps dispersal.
