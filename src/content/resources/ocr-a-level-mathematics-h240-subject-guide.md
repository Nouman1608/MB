OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240) is a linear A Level for students who want a full grounding in
mathematics after GCSE, whether as a subject in its own right or as support for physics,
engineering, economics, computing or the sciences. The course has three strands, pure mathematics,
statistics and mechanics, and every candidate sits all three written components at the end of the
course: H240/01, H240/02 and H240/03, each 120 minutes long and worth 100 marks. OCR prints each
paper's weighting as 33⅓%; Marlbridge records these as 33.33%, 33.33% and 33.34% so that the total
comes to exactly 100%.

## How the course is organised

The specification content falls into three topic areas, and they are not equal in size.

**Pure mathematics** is the largest strand and the foundation for everything else. It covers proof;
algebra and functions; coordinate geometry in the x-y plane; sequences and series; trigonometry;
exponentials and logarithms; differentiation; integration; numerical methods; and vectors. Algebraic
fluency built in the early pure topics is used constantly later, so this strand is where most of the
first year's teaching time goes.

**Statistics** covers statistical sampling; data presentation and interpretation; probability;
statistical distributions; and statistical hypothesis testing. It moves from describing data to
modelling it with distributions and then to making formal decisions with hypothesis tests.

**Mechanics** covers quantities and units in mechanics; kinematics; forces and Newton's laws; and
moments. It applies pure techniques, especially calculus and vectors, to bodies in motion and in
equilibrium.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H240/01 | Pure Mathematics | 120 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| H240/02 | Pure Mathematics and Statistics | 120 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| H240/03 | Pure Mathematics and Mechanics | 120 minutes | 100 | 33.34% |

The qualification is linear and untiered: there is no coursework, no AS stage that counts towards
the A Level, and students must take all three components in the same series. According to OCR's
specification-at-a-glance page, Component 02 is roughly half pure mathematics and half statistics,
and some of its questions are set on a pre-release data set. Component 03 is roughly half pure
mathematics and half mechanics. Component 01 is entirely pure. This specification has been examined
since 2018.

## How the parts fit together

The structure of the papers tells you something important: pure mathematics appears on all three.
Roughly two-thirds of the total assessment draws on pure content in some form, so weaknesses in
algebra, trigonometry or calculus show up everywhere, including inside statistics and mechanics
questions.

The applied strands depend directly on pure topics. Kinematics with variable acceleration needs
differentiation and integration; forces problems in two dimensions need vectors and trigonometry;
statistical distributions and hypothesis testing lean on probability and on confident handling of
algebraic expressions. Proof, listed first in the pure content, is also a habit rather than an
isolated topic: clear, logically ordered working is rewarded in every component.

The pre-release data set is the one part of the course that is not purely about technique. Because
some Component 02 questions refer to it, familiarity with its context and variables is part of the
statistics preparation, not something to look at for the first time in the exam hall.

## Planning your study

A sensible route through H240 starts with the pure foundations: algebra and functions, coordinate
geometry, and an introduction to differentiation and integration. Those topics unlock most of what
follows. Statistics topics such as sampling, data presentation and probability can run alongside
because they need relatively little calculus, while mechanics is easier once kinematics can draw on
basic calculus.

In the first year, aim to cover the core of every strand: the main pure topics, sampling and data
presentation, probability and the start of statistical distributions, and kinematics and forces.
In the second year, extend each strand: the harder pure content (further trigonometry, exponentials
and logarithms in depth, advanced calculus, numerical methods and vectors), hypothesis testing in its
fuller forms, and moments alongside more demanding forces problems.

Throughout both years, revisit earlier pure topics deliberately rather than assuming they will stay
fresh. Start working with the pre-release data set as soon as it is available to your centre. Begin
timed practice on whole papers once all three strands have been taught at least once, and switch to
the exam-preparation page for the paper-by-paper detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OCR A Level Mathematics A?**
There are three written components, H240/01 Pure Mathematics, H240/02 Pure Mathematics and Statistics, and H240/03 Pure Mathematics and Mechanics. Each lasts 120 minutes and is worth 100 marks, and all three must be taken.

**Are the three H240 papers equally weighted?**
Yes. OCR prints each one as 33⅓% of the A Level. Marlbridge records the weightings as 33.33%, 33.33% and 33.34% only so the total adds to exactly 100%.

**Is statistics or mechanics optional in H240?**
No. Both applied strands are compulsory: statistics is assessed in Component 02 and mechanics in Component 03, each alongside roughly half pure mathematics.

**What is the pre-release data set used for?**
Some questions in Component 02 are set on a pre-release data set, so students should become familiar with it before the exam as part of their statistics preparation.

**Is OCR A Level Mathematics A linear?**
Yes. All three components are taken at the end of the course, and there is no coursework or tiering.
