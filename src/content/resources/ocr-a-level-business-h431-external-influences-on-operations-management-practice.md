## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Distinguish between a legal and an ethical influence on operations management. **[3]**

**2.** Explain **one** way in which a rise in interest rates could affect the operations of a manufacturer. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** way in which a social change could affect how a supermarket staffs its stores. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by a tariff and give **one** effect it might have on a business that imports components. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is an environmental influence on operations?

A: A rise in the minimum wage.

B: A new tax on plastic packaging.

C: A change in the value of the pound.

D: A rival adopting robots.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Kestrel Packaging buys 250 tonnes of plastic film a year from a supplier in the euro area at €900 a tonne.

**(a)** Calculate the annual cost in pounds at £1 = €1.25 and at £1 = €1.10. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the extra annual cost if the pound falls from €1.25 to €1.10. **[1]**

**(c)** Evaluate how Kestrel Packaging could respond to the risk that the pound falls. **[8]**

**7.** Kestrel Packaging employs 20 operatives, each paid £21,000 a year, and makes 140,000 units a year. The law requires a 7% rise in the wage rate.

**(a)** Calculate the extra annual cost and the extra cost per unit. **[3]**

**(b)** If the selling price is £8.00, calculate the percentage price rise needed to pass on the whole increase, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Evaluate the likely impact of this legal change on Kestrel Packaging and its stakeholders. **[8]**

**8.** A government introduces a tax of £12 per tonne on non-recycled plastic, and Kestrel Packaging uses 3,500 tonnes of such plastic a year. A redesign costing £18,000 would cut non-recycled plastic by 20%.

**(a)** Calculate the annual tax before the redesign and the annual saving from the redesign. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the payback period for the redesign, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**(c)** Evaluate the importance of environmental influences on the operations of a packaging manufacturer. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A legal influence is a requirement of the law, which a business must follow or face penalties [1]. An ethical influence concerns what is considered morally right, beyond what the law requires [1]. Ethical pressures come from customers, the media and pressure groups rather than from enforcement [1]. **[3]**

**2.** The cost of borrowing rises [1], so the manufacturer may delay buying new machines or find that loan repayments on existing equipment are higher, reducing investment in capacity [1]. **[2]**

**3.** More people want to shop outside traditional hours, or an ageing population needs more help in store [1], so the supermarket may need more flexible shift patterns, extra evening staff or more assistance staff [1]. Accept other reasoned social changes. **[2]**

**4.** A tariff is a tax on imported goods [1]. It raises the cost of imported components, which may push the business to look for UK suppliers or raise its prices [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Total cost in euros = 250 × €900 = €225,000 [1]. At €1.25: £225,000 ÷ 1.25 = **£180,000** [1]. At €1.10: £225,000 ÷ 1.10 = **£204,545** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** £204,545 − £180,000 = **£24,545** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Fixed-price or forward contract: Kestrel agrees the exchange rate in advance with its bank [1], so the cost is certain and it can plan, but it loses the benefit if the pound rises [1].
Switch to a UK supplier: this removes the exchange-rate risk [1], but the UK price may be higher, or the quality or supply less reliable [1].
Pass the cost on to customers: price rises may be possible if rivals face the same cost [1], but may lose orders if customers can switch [1].
Hold stock when the pound is strong: reduces average cost [1], but ties up cash and storage space.
Judgement: a forward contract is the most practical first step because it gives certainty at low cost; Kestrel should combine this with a review of UK suppliers, because the extra cost of £24,545 is large compared with a small firm's profit [1]. **[8]**

**7. (a)** Wage bill = 20 × £21,000 = £420,000 [1]. Extra cost = 7% × £420,000 = **£29,400** [1]. Per unit = £29,400 ÷ 140,000 = **£0.21** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** £0.21 ÷ £8.00 [1] × 100 = **2.6%** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The cost rise of £29,400 is a direct reduction in profit unless it is recovered [1]. Passing it on needs a price rise of 2.6%, which may be possible if all UK rivals face the same law [1].
Kestrel could raise productivity, for example with automation or better training, to produce the same output with fewer hours [1], but this needs investment and may mean job losses [1].
Stakeholders: employees gain higher pay [1], but if the business cuts staff to save cost, some may lose their jobs [1]. Customers may pay higher prices [1]. Overseas competitors not subject to this law may gain a cost advantage.
Judgement: the effect is moderate (2.6% of price) and can probably be absorbed through a small price rise and efficiency gains, unless Kestrel competes with overseas firms [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Tax before = 3,500 × £12 = **£42,000** [1]. Reduction in plastic = 20% × 3,500 = 700 tonnes [1]. Saving = 700 × £12 = **£8,400** a year [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Payback = £18,000 ÷ £8,400 = **2.1 years** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Importance: a packaging manufacturer uses large amounts of material, energy and waste disposal, so environmental rules and taxes bear directly on cost [1]. The tax of £42,000 shows how large the cost can be [1]. Customers, especially retailers, now ask for recyclable packaging, so environmental performance affects sales as well as cost [1].
Responses: redesign to use less plastic, as in the example, pays back in 2.1 years [1], and switching to recycled materials may reduce the tax and appeal to customers [1]. Both need investment and may change the quality or cost of the product [1].
Other stakeholders: the community benefits from less waste and pollution [1]; employees may need training in new materials and processes.
Evaluation: the importance depends on the speed and size of tax and customer change [1]. Judgement: environmental influences are among the most important for a packaging business, because they affect both costs and what customers will buy, so Kestrel should treat redesign as a strategic priority and not just as compliance [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about sales or marketing when the question is about operations.
- Confusing legal and ethical influences.
- Not calculating the effect on cost, or forgetting to compare it with profit or price.
- Describing responses without evaluating them.
- Treating each influence in isolation when two or more act together.
