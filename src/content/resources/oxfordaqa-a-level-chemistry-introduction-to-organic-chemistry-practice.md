These questions cover section **3.3.1 Introduction to organic chemistry** of the OxfordAQA International AS
and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3, for International AS and A-level exams May/June 2020
onwards: 3.3.1.1 Nomenclature, 3.3.1.2 Reaction mechanisms and 3.3.1.3 Isomerism. It is entirely International
AS content; nothing is International A-level only. Unit 2 (Organic 1 and Physical 1) is where the
specification assesses it, and questions there can also use amount of substance (3.1.2).

Study the ideas first in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-introduction-to-organic-chemistry/)
and [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-introduction-to-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/).
Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, Cl = 35.5, Br = 79.9.

## Questions

**1.** This question is about definitions.

**(a)** Define the term *stereoisomers*. **[1]**
**(b)** Apart from containing the same functional group, state two features shared by the members of a
homologous series. **[2]**

**2.** 1,4-Dibromobutane has the structural formula BrCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂Br.

**(a)** Give its molecular formula. **[1]**
**(b)** Give its empirical formula. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe its skeletal formula. **[1]**

**3.** Compound R contains 17.8% carbon, 3.0% hydrogen and 79.2% bromine by mass. Its Mr is 201.8.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of R. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of R. **[1]**
**(c)** R has an unbranched chain with one bromine atom on each end carbon. Name R. **[1]**

**4.** Give the IUPAC name of each compound.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(b)** CH₂=CHCH₂CH(CH₃)CH₃ **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₃ **[1]**
**(d)** (CH₃)₃CCHO **[1]**

**5.** This question is about drawing and reading formulas.

**(a)** Give the structural formula of 2-bromo-3-methylbutane. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the structural formula of 2-methylpentanenitrile. **[1]**
**(c)** A skeletal formula shows a hexagon with one double line in the ring and Br on the corner next to the
double bond. Give the molecular formula of this compound. **[1]**

**6.** Each name below breaks an IUPAC rule. Give the correct name and state the rule broken.

**(a)** 2-ethylbutane **[2]**
**(b)** 2-methylbutan-3-ol **[2]**

**7.** Dichloromethane, CH₂Cl₂, reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light to form trichloromethane, CHCl₃, by a
free-radical mechanism.

**(a)** Give the equation for the step that forms chlorine radicals. **[1]**
**(b)** Write equations for the two steps that together convert CH₂Cl₂ into CHCl₃ and regenerate a chlorine
radical. **[2]**
**(c)** Two •CHCl₂ radicals meet. Write the equation for this step and name the organic product. **[2]**

**8.** This question is about curly arrows.

**(a)** Iodoethane reacts with cyanide ions: CH₃CH₂I + CN⁻ → CH₃CH₂CN + I⁻. Two curly arrows are needed. Give the
start point and the end point of each. **[4]**
**(b)** A water molecule bonds to the carbocation (CH₃)₃C⁺. State where the curly arrow starts and ends, and
give the formula of the species formed. **[2]**

**9.** Hexane, C₆H₁₄, has several structural isomers.

**(a)** Name the five structural isomers of C₆H₁₄, including hexane. **[5]**
**(b)** State the type of structural isomerism they show. **[1]**

**10.** For each alkene, state whether it has E–Z isomers and give a reason.

**(a)** hex-3-ene **[1]**
**(b)** 3-ethylpent-2-ene **[1]**
**(c)** 1-bromoprop-1-ene **[1]**
**(d)** 2-methylpent-2-ene **[1]**

**11.** An alkene has this structure.

```
    Br       CH(CH₃)₂
      \     /
       C = C
      /     \
    Cl       CH₂CH₂CH₃
```

**(a)** Apply the CIP priority rules to decide which group ranks higher on each carbon of the double bond,
explaining each decision. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce whether the structure shown is the E isomer or the Z isomer. **[1]**

**12.** Compound X contains 53.0% carbon, 7.7% hydrogen and 39.3% chlorine by mass. Its Mr is 90.5.

**(a)** Show that the molecular formula of X is C₄H₇Cl. **[3]**
**(b)** X has an unbranched chain, a C=C between carbon 1 and carbon 2, and its chlorine atom on carbon 1.
Name X. **[1]**
**(c)** Draw the Z isomer of X, using the CIP rules to justify your answer. **[3]**
**(d)** Name a position isomer of X that has no E–Z isomers, and explain why it has none. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Compounds that share a structural formula but have their atoms arranged differently in space [1].
**(b)** Any two, one mark each: same general formula; successive members differ by CH₂; similar chemical
reactions; gradual trend in physical properties such as boiling point [2]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A stereoisomer definition built on "molecular formula" describes isomers in general;
the key point is the same *structural* formula.

**2. (a)** **C₄H₈Br₂** [1]
**(b)** **C₂H₄Br** (divide by 2) [1]
**(c)** A zigzag of three lines (four carbons, counting both ends), with Br written on each end [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An empirical formula must be fully simplified; C₄H₈Br₂ given for (b) only repeats (a)
rather than answering the question.

**3. (a)** Moles: C = 17.8 / 12.0 = 1.483; H = 3.0 / 1.0 = 3.0; Br = 79.2 / 79.9 = 0.991 [1]. Divide by
0.991: C = 1.50, H = 3.03, Br = 1.00 [1]. Multiply by 2: **C₃H₆Br₂** [1].
**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 36.0 + 6.0 + 159.8 = 201.8, equal to Mr, so the molecular formula is
**C₃H₆Br₂** [1].
**(c)** **1,3-dibromopropane** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 1.50 to 2 gives a wrong formula; a ratio ending in .5 is a signal to double
every value.

**4. (a)** **3-methylhexane** [1]
**(b)** **4-methylpent-1-ene** [1]
**(c)** **4-methylpentan-2-ol** [1]
**(d)** **2,2-dimethylpropanal** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the –OH sets the numbering, so "2-methylpentan-4-ol" is not a correct name even
though the methyl locant looks lower.

**5. (a)** CH₃CHBrCH(CH₃)CH₃ [1]
**(b)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CN [1]
**(c)** **C₆H₉Br** (cyclohexene is C₆H₁₀; one H replaced by Br) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the CN carbon is C1 of the five-carbon stem, so only four more chain carbons follow
it; writing CN on the end of a five-carbon chain adds a carbon.

**6. (a)** **3-methylpentane** [1]. The longest chain has five carbons; the "ethyl" carbons are part of the
main chain [1].
**(b)** **3-methylbutan-2-ol** [1]. The –OH group must get the lowest possible locant [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for the rule as well as the corrected name, so state the rule in words
rather than leaving the new name to speak for itself.

**7. (a)** Cl₂ → 2Cl• [1]
**(b)** Cl• + CH₂Cl₂ → •CHCl₂ + HCl [1]; •CHCl₂ + Cl₂ → CHCl₃ + Cl• [1]
**(c)** 2•CHCl₂ → CHCl₂CHCl₂ [1]; **1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethane** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that each equation balances and that every radical carries its dot; curly arrows
are not required here, so do not spend time on them.

**8. (a)** Arrow 1 starts from the lone pair on the carbon of CN⁻ [1] and ends at the carbon bonded to iodine
(the δ+ carbon) [1]. Arrow 2 starts from the C–I bond [1] and ends at the iodine atom, which leaves as I⁻ [1].
**(b)** From a lone pair on the oxygen of H₂O to the positive carbon [1]; forms **(CH₃)₃COH₂⁺** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Drawing the first arrow from the nitrogen, or from the minus sign, does not show where the
new C–C bond comes from; the carbon lone pair forms it.

**9. (a)** hexane [1]; 2-methylpentane [1]; 3-methylpentane [1]; 2,2-dimethylbutane [1];
2,3-dimethylbutane [1].
**(b)** Chain isomerism [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "4-methylpentane" or "2-ethylbutane" are repeats of isomers already listed under a
wrong name and add nothing.

**10. (a)** Yes: each C=C carbon carries H and CH₂CH₃ [1].
**(b)** No: carbon 3 carries two identical CH₂CH₃ groups [1].
**(c)** Yes: carbon 1 carries Br and H; carbon 2 carries CH₃ and H [1].
**(d)** No: carbon 2 carries two identical CH₃ groups [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the yes or no together with a reason tied to the groups on a named carbon of the
C=C; a bare yes or no answers only half the question.

**11. (a)** Left carbon: Br (atomic number 35) beats Cl (17) [1]. Right carbon: both groups attach through
C, a tie [1]; next atoms are (C, C, H) for CH(CH₃)₂ and (C, H, H) for CH₂CH₂CH₃, so **CH(CH₃)₂** has the
higher priority [1].
**(b)** Br and CH(CH₃)₂ are on the same side: **Z** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the tie before comparing further out; "the branched group is bigger" is not a CIP
reason.

**12. (a)** Moles: C = 53.0 / 12.0 = 4.417; H = 7.7 / 1.0 = 7.7; Cl = 39.3 / 35.5 = 1.107 [1]. Divide by
1.107: C = 3.99, H = 6.96, Cl = 1.00, so the empirical formula is C₄H₇Cl [1]. Its mass is 48.0 + 7.0 + 35.5
= 90.5 = Mr, so the molecular formula is **C₄H₇Cl** [1].
**(b)** **1-chlorobut-1-ene** [1].
**(c)** Carbon 1: Cl (17) beats H (1) [1]. Carbon 2: CH₂CH₃ (C, 6) beats H [1].

```
    Cl       CH₂CH₃
      \     /
       C = C
      /     \
     H       H
   (Z)-1-chlorobut-1-ene
```

Cl and CH₂CH₃ drawn on the same side [1].
**(d)** **2-chlorobut-1-ene** (or 3-chlorobut-1-ene or 4-chlorobut-1-ene) [1]; carbon 1 of the C=C carries
two H atoms, so there are no E–Z isomers [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" the final formula is already given, so show every stage: moles, ratio and
empirical formula mass compared with Mr.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding a ratio such as 1.50 instead of doubling every value.
- Giving an empirical formula that can still be simplified.
- Leaving the CN carbon out of a nitrile's stem, or adding an extra one.
- Numbering an alcohol from the end nearer a branch instead of the –OH.
- Repeating an isomer under a second, wrong name.
- Writing radicals without dots or radical equations that do not balance.
- Starting a curly arrow on a charge or the wrong atom of an ion.
- Deciding CIP priority by size, mass or number of atoms.
- Labelling a drawing Z when the higher-priority groups are on opposite sides.
