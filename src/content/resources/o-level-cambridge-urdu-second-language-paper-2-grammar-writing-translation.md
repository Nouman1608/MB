This guide covers **Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation**, the
second of two papers in Cambridge O Level Second Language Urdu (3248),
2024–2026 series. Marlbridge's existing O-Level Urdu resources cover
Paper 1 (Reading and Writing, shared with sibling syllabus 3247 First
Language Urdu) and 3247's own Paper 2 (Texts); this guide is the first
Marlbridge resource dedicated specifically to 3248's own distinct
second paper, which has no equivalent in 3247.

## Where this fits in Cambridge O-Level Urdu

Cambridge offers two separate, currently examined O Level Urdu
specifications: **3247 First Language Urdu**, whose Paper 2 (Texts)
tests literary set texts, and **3248 Second Language Urdu**, whose
Paper 2 (Grammar, Writing and Translation) instead tests applied
language skills — grammar in context, extended writing and translation
from English into Urdu. Candidates take one specification or the
other, never both, in the same series, so this guide is specifically
for 3248 candidates; 3247 candidates should use Marlbridge's Paper 2
Texts guide instead.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL SECOND LANGUAGE URDU 3248 — PAPER 2: GRAMMAR, WRITING AND TRANSLATION**

- Written paper, **1 hour 30 minutes**, **50 marks**, weighted **50%**
  of the qualification (alongside Paper 1 Reading and Writing, also
  50%)
- Candidates attempt **all four exercises** — there is no choice
- For Exercises 1–3, questions and responses are in **Urdu**; for
  Exercise 4, the question is in **English** and the response in
  **Urdu**
- Exercise 1 — **Sentence transformation** (5 marks): candidates read
  five short sentences and transform each using a given prompt,
  without changing the original meaning
- Exercise 2 — **Multiple-choice cloze passage** (10 marks): candidates
  read a short text with gaps, filling each from multiple-choice
  options, testing grammatical usage in context — verb conjugation,
  subject/verb agreement, comparatives, superlatives, parts of speech,
  nouns, prepositions, postpositions, articles and context-appropriate
  vocabulary
- Exercise 3 — **Extended writing** (20 marks): a piece of continuous
  prose of approximately 200 words, from a stimulus of short prompts,
  with a specified purpose, format and audience
- Exercise 4 — **Translation** (15 marks): translating a short passage
  of approximately 150 words from English into Urdu

## Exercise 1: Sentence transformation

This exercise is worth the fewest marks (5) but tests a precise skill:
rewriting a sentence according to a given prompt (for example, changing
tense, voice, or sentence type) while preserving its original meaning
exactly. Because only five sentences are set, each is worth a full
mark, so accuracy on grammatical structures — verb endings, correct
postposition use, word order — matters more here than on longer
exercises where partial credit is easier to earn.

## Exercise 2: Multiple-choice cloze passage

The cloze passage tests grammatical usage **in context** rather than
grammar as an abstract rule set. The syllabus names the specific
grammar points candidates should expect: conjugation of verbs,
subject/verb agreement, comparatives, superlatives, parts of speech,
nouns, prepositions, postpositions, articles, and vocabulary
appropriate to the passage's context. Because every gap is
multiple-choice, this exercise rewards recognising which option fits
grammatically *and* makes sense in context — an option can be
grammatically valid in isolation but wrong for the specific passage,
so read a full sentence around each gap before choosing.

## Exercise 3: Extended writing

Candidates write approximately 200 words of continuous prose from a
set of short stimulus prompts, with the purpose, format and audience
specified on the question paper — for example, a letter, a diary
entry, or a description written for a particular reader. At 20 marks,
this is the single highest-value exercise on the paper, so revision
time should weight extended writing practice accordingly: prepare
vocabulary and structures across the common purposes (narrative,
descriptive, persuasive, informational) rather than only one, since
the specific purpose set varies by series.

## Exercise 4: Translation

Candidates translate an English passage of approximately 150 words
into Urdu. Unlike Exercises 1–3, this exercise's question is presented
in English, which is a deliberate design choice testing candidates'
ability to move between the two languages rather than only comprehend
or produce Urdu in isolation. At 15 marks for a relatively short
passage, precision matters: a literal, word-for-word translation that
produces unnatural Urdu is marked down compared with an accurate
translation that also reads naturally in the target language.

## Common mistakes

- **Changing the meaning of a sentence while transforming its
  structure** in Exercise 1 — the instruction is always to preserve
  meaning, not just alter form.
- **Choosing a cloze option that is grammatically possible but
  contextually wrong** in Exercise 2 — always read beyond the gap
  itself.
- **Ignoring the specified purpose, format or audience** in Exercise
  3's extended writing, which caps marks regardless of language
  accuracy.
- **Translating word-for-word** in Exercise 4 rather than producing
  natural Urdu that preserves the original meaning.

## How to approach it

Because Exercises 3 and 4 together carry 35 of the paper's 50 marks,
prioritise timed practice on extended writing and translation once the
grammar points in Exercises 1–2 are secure. For grammar specifically,
build a personal checklist from the syllabus's own named points (verb
conjugation, subject/verb agreement, comparatives and superlatives,
parts of speech, prepositions and postpositions, articles) and test
yourself against each one individually, since cloze passages are
designed to probe exactly this list.
