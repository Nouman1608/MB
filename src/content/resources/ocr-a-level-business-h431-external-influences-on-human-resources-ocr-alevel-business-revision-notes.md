## What the specification lists

One statement: evaluate the following influences on human resources: social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological and international.

## Core recall

### The eight influences and their typical effects

| Influence | Examples | Typical effect on human resource decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Social | Ageing population; wish for flexible work; changing family patterns | Retention of older workers; flexible contracts; wider recruitment pool |
| Legal | Employment, equality, health and safety, data protection law | Compliance cost and admin; lower risk of claims |
| Ethical | Fair pay, honest recruitment, respect for staff | Better motivation and reputation; may cost more |
| Environmental | Green skills; remote working; extreme weather | New skills and training; changed work patterns |
| Economic | Unemployment, inflation, the business cycle, exchange rates | Ease of recruiting; pressure on pay; hiring freezes or redundancies |
| Political | Taxes on employers, training funding, immigration rules, public pay | Changes in labour cost and supply of skills |
| Technological | Automation, online recruitment, e-learning, monitoring software | New skills; fewer routine jobs; changed training |
| International | Migration, overseas wages, trade deals, exchange rates | Offshoring; access to overseas labour; competition on cost |

### Five questions for evaluating an influence

1. What changes (supply, cost or skills of labour, or rules)?
2. How big is the effect? Use figures.
3. How fast does it happen?
4. Can the business respond, and at what cost?
5. Who gains and who loses?

### Opportunity or threat?

- Low unemployment is a threat to recruiting but an opportunity for staff.
- Automation is a threat to routine jobs but an opportunity for new skilled roles.
- An ageing population is a threat to supply but an opportunity for firms that value experience.

### Judging importance

- Large and fast effects matter more than small and slow ones.
- Larger firms can respond more easily.
- Labour-intensive firms feel labour-market changes most.
- A legal change affects every competitor; a technology change may affect only those who adopt it.

### Linking to human resource decisions

Workforce planning, recruitment, training, pay and rewards, structure, relations with staff, redundancy.

## Quick worked reminder

Carrow Warehousing (invented): low unemployment doubles the cost per hire from £900 to £1,800 and needs a wage premium of £0.80 an hour. For 30 hires: recruitment rises from £27,000 to £54,000, and the premium is 30 × £1,560 = £46,800, so extra cost is £73,800. Brannock Packing (invented): a robot cell cuts labour cost from £552,000 to £377,200 (saving £174,800); after £60,000 running costs, payback on £600,000 is about 5.2 years, but 8 posts are lost.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Labour market | The supply of and demand for workers |
| Unemployment | People who are able and willing to work but cannot find a job |
| Business cycle | Alternating periods of growth and recession |
| On-costs | Costs of employing someone beyond wages, such as pension and employer contributions |
| Offshoring | Moving work to another country |
| Automation | Using machines or software to do tasks done by people |
| Redeployment | Moving staff to other jobs in the same business |
| Flexible working | Variation in hours, place or pattern of work |

## Exam traps

- Do not list all eight influences; select the most important for the case.
- State which human resource decision each influence affects.
- Show the size with a figure or comparison.
- Legal influences apply to all competitors; say whether the business is affected more than others.
- Reach a judgement on which influence matters most.

## Self-test

1. List the eight influences in the specification.
2. Give one social influence on human resources.
3. Explain how low unemployment affects recruitment.
4. A firm hires 24 staff. Cost per hire rises from £700 to £1,500. Calculate the extra recruitment cost.
5. Give one way an ethical approach could affect pay.
6. Give one environmental influence on human resources.
7. Give one example of a political influence.
8. How might automation change the skills a business needs?
9. Give one risk of offshoring work.
10. What does it mean to evaluate an influence?
11. Why might an influence be an opportunity rather than a threat?
12. A business has 80 staff and 25% will retire in five years. Replacing each costs £3,500. Calculate the total replacement cost.

### Answers

1. Social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological, international.
2. An ageing population, or a demand for flexible working.
3. Workers are scarce, so there are fewer applicants, recruitment takes longer and costs more, and pay may need to rise.
4. 24 × £700 = £16,800 and 24 × £1,500 = £36,000, so the extra cost is **£19,200**.
5. The business may pay a living wage above the legal minimum, raising labour costs but improving motivation and reputation.
6. Demand for green skills, or remote working to cut commuting.
7. A government training subsidy, a tax on employers, or immigration rules.
8. Fewer routine jobs and more demand for technical and maintenance skills.
9. Lower service quality, exchange rate changes or reputational damage.
10. To weigh the size and direction of the effect on the business and stakeholders and reach a judgement.
11. It can open a new market, widen the recruitment pool or lower costs for businesses that respond well.
12. 80 × 25% = 20 staff; 20 × £3,500 = **£70,000**.
