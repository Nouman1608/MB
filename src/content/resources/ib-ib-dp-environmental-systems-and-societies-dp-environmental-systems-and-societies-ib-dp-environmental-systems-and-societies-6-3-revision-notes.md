## Condensed recall

### Net zero and budgets

- Warming from CO₂ stops rising only when net CO₂ emissions reach about zero.
- **Net zero**: remaining emissions balanced by equal removals.
- **Carbon budget**: CO₂ still emittable for a given warming limit.
- Test a pledge: date, gases and sectors covered, reliance on removals, near-term action.

### Ranking options

- Rank by cost per tonne abated; take the cheapest first until the target is met.
- Some options have negative cost (they save money), such as insulation.
- Total cost = Σ(tonnes used × cost per tonne); average = total cost ÷ tonnes.
- Short reminder: 40 000 t at $5 plus 60 000 t at $35 = $200 000 + $2 100 000 = $2 300 000; average **$23 per t**.
- Also weigh speed, co-benefits, equity and acceptance.

### Carbon pricing

| | Carbon tax | Cap-and-trade |
|---|---|---|
| Fixes | Price per tonne | Total emissions |
| Certain | Cost to firms | Amount cut |
| Uncertain | Amount cut | Permit price |
| Example | Various national taxes | EU Emissions Trading System (2005) |

- Risks: falls hardest on poorer households unless revenue is returned; **carbon leakage** to unpriced countries.

### Carbon removal

- **Natural**: afforestation and reforestation; **blue carbon** (mangroves, seagrass, salt marsh sediments); soil carbon and peat restoration.
- **Technological**: CCS (prevents new emissions, not removal by itself); **BECCS** (net removal, large land need); **DAC** (energy-intensive, costly).
- Removal by area = area × uptake per hectare. Offsetting large emitters needs very large areas.

### Solar geoengineering risks

- Does not stop ocean acidification.
- Could shift rainfall, creating regional winners and losers.
- **Termination shock** if stopped suddenly.
- Governance: who decides?
- **Moral hazard**: may weaken pressure to cut emissions.

### Adaptation

- **Hard**: sea walls, barriers, reservoirs, raised buildings.
- **Soft**: early warnings, insurance, planning, crop calendars, education.
- **Ecosystem-based**: mangroves, wetlands, floodplains.
- **Managed retreat**: moving away from exposed land.
- **Maladaptation**: increases vulnerability or emissions later (sea walls shifting erosion; fossil-powered air conditioning; building behind defences).
- **Resilience**: ability to absorb shocks and recover.

### International responsibility

- **Common but differentiated responsibilities** (UNFCCC, 1992).
- Paris NDCs reviewed by a **global stocktake** (first completed 2023).
- **Climate finance**, e.g. the Green Climate Fund.
- **Loss and damage** fund agreed at COP27 (2022).
- Technocentric: removal technologies, geoengineering research. Ecocentric: lower consumption, ecosystem protection, local resilience.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Net zero | Emissions balanced by equal removals |
| Carbon budget | Remaining CO₂ that can be emitted for a warming limit |
| Abatement cost | Cost of reducing one tonne of emissions |
| Carbon tax | Fixed charge per tonne emitted |
| Cap-and-trade | Fixed emissions limit with tradable permits |
| Carbon leakage | Emissions moving to places without carbon pricing |
| Blue carbon | Carbon stored by coastal and marine ecosystems |
| BECCS | Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage |
| Direct air capture | Extracting CO₂ directly from the air |
| Termination shock | Rapid warming if solar geoengineering stops suddenly |
| Maladaptation | Adaptation that increases long-term vulnerability or emissions |
| Loss and damage | Harm from climate change beyond what adaptation can prevent |

## Exam traps

- CCS on a power station reduces new emissions; it is not removal.
- Tree planting cannot offset all current emissions: show the area needed.
- Geoengineering masks warming only.
- Not all defences are good adaptation: consider maladaptation.
- Carbon tax fixes price; cap-and-trade fixes quantity.
- Evaluations need cost, speed, equity and a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Why must net CO₂ emissions reach about zero for warming to stop?
2. Define carbon budget.
3. An option abates 80 000 t at $15 per tonne. What is its annual cost?
4. Why can some mitigation options have a negative cost?
5. Which system fixes the quantity of emissions: a carbon tax or cap-and-trade?
6. Define carbon leakage.
7. Why do mangroves store carbon so effectively?
8. Why is CCS on a coal power station not carbon removal?
9. Give two risks of stratospheric aerosol injection.
10. Distinguish between hard and soft adaptation, with an example of each.
11. Give one example of maladaptation.
12. What was agreed at COP27 in 2022?

### Answers

1. Warming from CO₂ depends on cumulative emissions, so it continues while net emissions add to the total.
2. The total CO₂ that can still be emitted while keeping warming below a chosen limit.
3. 80 000 × 15 = **$1 200 000** per year.
4. They save more money over their lifetime (for example, on fuel) than they cost to install.
5. Cap-and-trade.
6. Movement of emissions to countries without carbon pricing when firms relocate production.
7. Their waterlogged sediments slow decomposition, so organic carbon builds up.
8. It captures CO₂ before release, preventing new emissions, but does not take existing CO₂ out of the air.
9. Any two: no effect on acidification; changed rainfall; termination shock; governance disputes; moral hazard.
10. Hard adaptation uses engineered structures (a sea wall); soft adaptation changes behaviour, planning or institutions (an early-warning system).
11. Any one: a sea wall that increases erosion elsewhere; fossil-powered air conditioning; building in flood zones behind defences.
12. To set up a fund for loss and damage to help vulnerable countries.
