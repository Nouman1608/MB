## Section A

**1.** State what happens when **(a)** an S pole is brought near an S pole, and **(b)** an S pole is brought near an N pole. **[2]**

**2.** From the list below, name **all** the materials that are magnetic.

copper, nickel, aluminium, steel, brass, cobalt, glass **[2]**

**3.** Define a *magnetic field*. **[1]**

**4.** State how the direction of a magnetic field at a point is defined. **[1]**

**5.** On a diagram of field lines, region X has lines much closer together than region Y. State what this shows. **[1]**

**6.** State **two** differences between the magnetic properties of soft iron and steel. **[2]**

**7.** Name **two** uses of permanent magnets and **two** uses of electromagnets. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** A student has three identical-looking metal bars, P, Q and R. One is a magnet, one is unmagnetised iron and one is aluminium. She brings one end of each bar in turn up to each pole of a known bar magnet.

| Bar | Near N pole of magnet | Near S pole of magnet |
|---|---|---|
| P | Attracted | Attracted |
| Q | No effect | No effect |
| R | Repelled | Attracted |

**(a)** Identify which bar is the magnet, which is iron and which is aluminium. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why bar P is attracted to both poles. **[2]**

**9.** An iron nail is held near the N pole of a bar magnet, without touching it. A small iron pin is found to cling to the far end of the nail.

**(a)** Name the pole induced at the end of the nail nearest the magnet. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the pin clings to the far end of the nail. **[2]**
**(c)** The magnet is taken away and the pin falls off. State what this shows about the material of the nail. **[1]**

**10.** Describe how a plotting compass is used to draw one magnetic field line around a bar magnet, including how the direction of the line is found. **[4]**

**11.** Iron filings are sprinkled on a card placed over a bar magnet.

**(a)** State what must be done to the card for the filings to form a clear pattern. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the filings line up along the field. **[1]**
**(c)** State **one** piece of information that a plotting compass gives but the iron filings do not. **[1]**

**12.** Describe the pattern and direction of the magnetic field around a single bar magnet, as you would show it in a sketch. Your answer should refer to the shape of the lines, the direction and the spacing. **[3]**

**13.** A scrapyard crane uses a large electromagnet.

**(a)** Explain why the core of the electromagnet is made of soft iron rather than steel. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** reasons why an electromagnet is more suitable than a permanent magnet for this crane. **[2]**
**(c)** A permanent magnet is used for the needle of a compass. Explain why steel is suitable. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** They **repel** [1].
**(b)** They **attract** [1].

**2.** **Nickel, steel and cobalt** [2] — all three for 2 marks; two correct with no incorrect material for 1 mark.

**3.** A **region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force** [1].

**4.** It is the **direction of the force on an N pole** placed at that point [1].

**5.** The **magnetic field is stronger at X** than at Y [1].

**6.** Soft iron is **easily magnetised; steel is harder to magnetise** [1]. Soft iron **loses its magnetism when the magnetising field is removed (temporary); steel keeps it (permanent)** [1].

**7.** Permanent magnets, any two, 1 mark each: **compass needle; door catch or fridge seal; loudspeaker; electric motor or generator**. Electromagnets, any two, 1 mark each: **scrapyard crane; relay; electric bell; electric door lock; separating iron and steel from waste**.

**8. (a)** **R is the magnet** [1]; **P is the iron** [1]; **Q is the aluminium** [1].
**(b)** P is **unmagnetised magnetic material**, so a **pole is induced in it by whichever pole is brought near** [1]; the induced pole is always the **opposite (unlike) pole**, so it is always attracted [1].

**9. (a)** An **S pole** [1].
**(b)** The nail becomes an **induced magnet**, with an **N pole at its far end** [1]; that pole **induces magnetism in the pin**, so the pin is attracted to the nail [1].
**(c)** The nail is **soft iron (a temporary magnet)**: it loses its induced magnetism when the magnet is removed [1].

**10.** Place the magnet on paper and put the **plotting compass near the N pole**; **mark a dot at the end the needle's N pole points to** [1]. **Move the compass so the tail of the needle is over that dot** and mark a new dot at the N end [1]. **Repeat until the S pole is reached, then join the dots with a smooth curve** [1]. **Draw an arrow on the line in the direction the compass needle's N pole pointed**, which is the field direction (from N to S) [1].

**11. (a)** **Tap the card gently** [1].
**(b)** Each filing **becomes an induced magnet** and turns to line up with the field [1].
**(c)** The **direction** of the field [1].

**12.** Lines **leave the N pole and curve round to enter the S pole**, symmetrical on both sides of the magnet, with no lines crossing [1]. **Arrows point from N to S** outside the magnet [1]. The lines are **closest together near the poles**, where the field is strongest, and **spread out further away** [1].

**13. (a)** Soft iron is **easily magnetised, making the field much stronger** [1], and it **loses its magnetism when the current is switched off**, so the load is released (steel would stay magnetised) [1].
**(b)** It can be **switched off to drop the scrap** [1]; its **strength can be changed by changing the current** [1].
**(c)** Steel **keeps its magnetism (is a permanent magnet)**, so the needle stays magnetised for years [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a bar is a magnet because it is attracted. Only repulsion is a reliable test.
- Including aluminium, copper or brass in lists of magnetic materials.
- Writing that a magnetic field is "where a magnet works" instead of "a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force".
- Leaving arrows off field lines, or drawing them from S to N outside the magnet.
- Explaining the soft-iron core only as "iron is magnetic" without saying that it loses its magnetism when switched off.
- Claiming iron filings show the field direction.
