## Section A

**1.** Which unit is equivalent to the pascal?

A: N m

B: N m⁻²

C: kg m⁻³

D: kg m s⁻² **[1]**

**2.** A solid object is fully submerged in a liquid. On which quantities does the upthrust depend?

A: the density of the object and its volume

B: the density of the liquid and the volume of the object

C: the depth of the object and the density of the liquid

D: the mass of the object and the depth of the liquid **[1]**

**3.** Define pressure. **[1]**

**4.** A person of weight 550 N balances on one shoe heel of area 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ m². Calculate the pressure on the floor. **[2]**

**5.** A water storage tank has its surface 32 m above a tap. Calculate the difference in water pressure between the tank surface and the tap. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the pressure at the bottom of a tall narrow jar of water is the same as at the bottom of a wide bowl of water of the same depth. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Starting from the definitions of density and pressure, derive the equation Δp = ρgΔh for the pressure difference due to a column of liquid of height Δh. **[4]**

**8.** A container holds a 0.40 m layer of water with a 0.20 m layer of oil of density 850 kg m⁻³ floating on top. The atmosphere above is at 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure at the oil-water boundary due to the oil. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total pressure at the base of the container. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the oil floats on the water. **[1]**

**9.** A solid cylinder stands upright, fully submerged in a liquid of density 1200 kg m⁻³. Its flat ends each have area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m², its height is 0.12 m, and its top is 0.30 m below the surface.

**(a)** Calculate the force exerted by the liquid on the top surface, ignoring atmospheric pressure. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the force exerted by the liquid on the bottom surface. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answers to find the upthrust, and check it using F = ρgV. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why atmospheric pressure can be ignored in this calculation. **[1]**

**10.** A stone of mass 0.75 kg and volume 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ hangs from a spring balance and is fully submerged in water.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the stone. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the weight of the stone and the upthrust on it. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the reading on the spring balance. **[1]**

**(d)** The stone is lowered further, staying fully submerged. State and explain what happens to the reading. **[2]**

**11.** A block of wax of density 900 kg m⁻³ floats in a salt solution of density 1150 kg m⁻³.

**(a)** Explain, using upthrust, why the block floats with part of it above the surface. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the block's volume below the surface. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The normal (perpendicular) force per unit area [1]. **[1]**

**4.** p = 550 ÷ 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **4.6 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Δp = 1000 × 9.81 × 32 [1] = **3.1 × 10⁵ Pa** (3.14 × 10⁵ Pa) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The pressure depends only on ρ, g and the vertical depth, Δp = ρgΔh [1]. A wider container has more water above the base, but the weight is spread over a proportionally larger area, so the pressure is unchanged [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Volume of the column V = AΔh, so its mass m = ρV = ρAΔh (definition of density) [1]. Weight W = mg = ρAΔhg [1]. Pressure is force per unit area, so Δp = W/A [1] = ρAΔhg/A = ρgΔh [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** p = 850 × 9.81 × 0.20 = **1670 Pa** [1].

**(b)** Water: 1000 × 9.81 × 0.40 = 3920 Pa [1]; liquids total 1670 + 3920 = 5590 Pa [1]; total pressure = 1.01 × 10⁵ + 5590 = **1.07 × 10⁵ Pa** [1].

**(c)** The oil is less dense than the water [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** F_top = 1200 × 9.81 × 0.30 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = **17.7 N** [1].

**(b)** F_bottom = 1200 × 9.81 × 0.42 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = **24.7 N** [1].

**(c)** Upthrust = 24.7 − 17.7 = **7.1 N** [1]; check: V = 5.0 × 10⁻³ × 0.12 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³, F = 1200 × 9.81 × 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 7.1 N [1].

**(d)** It adds the same pressure to the top and the bottom, so the extra forces are equal and opposite and cancel [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** ρ = 0.75 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **2500 kg m⁻³** [1].

**(b)** W = 0.75 × 9.81 = 7.36 N [1]; upthrust = 1000 × 9.81 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.94 N [1].

**(c)** Reading = 7.36 − 2.94 = **4.4 N** [1].

**(d)** The reading does not change [1], because the stone still displaces the same volume of water, so the upthrust is unchanged (the pressure difference between top and bottom stays the same) [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** When fully submerged, the upthrust would exceed the block's weight because wax is less dense than the solution [1]. It rises until the volume below the surface displaces seawater of weight equal to its own weight, so upthrust equals weight with part above the surface [1].

**(b)** Fraction submerged = 900 ÷ 1150 [1] = 0.783, so **78%** [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the object's density, or its total volume when only partly submerged, in F = ρgV.
- Forgetting atmospheric pressure when asked for the total pressure.
- Treating layered liquids as one liquid with a single density.
- Missing out the density step when deriving Δp = ρgΔh.
- Saying the upthrust increases as a submerged object goes deeper.
