## Core recall

- **Acid** = proton donor.
- **Base** = proton acceptor (uses a lone pair to bond to H⁺).
- **Acid–base equilibria involve the transfer of protons**, in both directions.
- A proton is H⁺. In water it exists as **H₃O⁺**; H⁺(aq) and H₃O⁺(aq) mean the same thing.
- A species is an acid or a base **in a particular reaction**; water, HCO₃⁻ and even HNO₃ can play either role.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton | H⁺, a hydrogen atom without its electron |
| Brønsted–Lowry acid | A proton donor |
| Brønsted–Lowry base | A proton acceptor |
| Oxonium ion | H₃O⁺, formed when H⁺ bonds to a water molecule |
| Conjugate acid–base pair | Two species that differ by one H⁺, such as NH₄⁺ / NH₃ |
| Amphiprotic | Able to act as an acid or a base, such as H₂O, HCO₃⁻, HSO₄⁻, H₂PO₄⁻ |
| Polyprotic acid | An acid that can donate more than one proton, one step at a time |

## Identifying acids and bases: the routine

1. Pair each species on the left with one on the right that differs by one H⁺.
2. Lost H⁺ (charge down by 1) → **acid**; gained H⁺ (charge up by 1) → **base**.
3. For ⇌, repeat for the reverse reaction.

## Worked reminders

| Equation | Forward acid | Forward base | Reverse acid | Reverse base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺ | CH₃COOH | H₂O | H₃O⁺ | CH₃COO⁻ |
| CO₃²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻ | H₂O | CO₃²⁻ | HCO₃⁻ | OH⁻ |
| HSO₄⁻ + NH₃ ⇌ SO₄²⁻ + NH₄⁺ | HSO₄⁻ | NH₃ | NH₄⁺ | SO₄²⁻ |
| CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ | H₂O | CH₃NH₂ | CH₃NH₃⁺ | OH⁻ |

## Amphiprotic species

- **H₂O**: base with acids (→ H₃O⁺); acid with bases (→ OH⁻).
- **HCO₃⁻**: base with H₃O⁺ (→ H₂CO₃); acid with OH⁻ (→ CO₃²⁻).
- **HSO₄⁻** and **H₂PO₄⁻**: formed part-way through polyprotic acids losing protons.

## Polyprotic acids

- Protons are transferred **one at a time**.
- H₂SO₄ → HSO₄⁻ → SO₄²⁻.
- H₃PO₄ → H₂PO₄⁻ → HPO₄²⁻ → PO₄³⁻.
- Write each step separately; each lowers the charge by one.

## Proton transfer beyond water

- HCl(g) + NH₃(g) → NH₄Cl(s): HCl acid, NH₃ base.
- HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻: **H₂SO₄ is the acid**, **HNO₃ is the base**.

## Neutralisation as proton transfer

- H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O
- 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → H₂O + CO₂
- H⁺ + NH₃ → NH₄⁺
- 2H⁺ + O²⁻ → H₂O

## Checking an answer with charges

Every proton transfer changes two charges by one unit each, in opposite directions:

- The acid's charge **falls by 1**: HCOOH (0) → HCOO⁻ (−1); NH₄⁺ (+1) → NH₃ (0); HPO₄²⁻ (−2) → PO₄³⁻ (−3).
- The base's charge **rises by 1**: H₂O (0) → H₃O⁺ (+1); OH⁻ (−1) → H₂O (0); CO₃²⁻ (−2) → HCO₃⁻ (−1).
- The **total charge** is the same on both sides of a balanced equation. If it is not, a formula is wrong.

## How far the transfer goes

- **Strong acid** (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ first step): proton transfer to water is essentially complete → single arrow.
- **Weak acid** (CH₃COOH, HCOOH, HF): only a small fraction transfers a proton → reversible arrow, equilibrium mostly on the left.
- **Weak base** (NH₃, amines): only a small fraction accepts a proton from water → reversible arrow.
- "Strong" and "weak" describe the **extent** of proton transfer, not the concentration. These ideas are used in the pH, Kw and Ka points that follow 3.1.10.1.

## Exam traps

- "An acid contains hydrogen" or "releases H⁺": use **proton donor**.
- Only the forward direction labelled for an equilibrium.
- Wrong charges on conjugate species.
- Calling water the base when it becomes OH⁻.
- Assuming HNO₃ is always an acid.
- Writing H₂SO₄ → 2H⁺ + SO₄²⁻ as one proton transfer when steps are asked for.
- Using a single arrow for a weak acid's proton transfer, or ⇌ where a question states complete dissociation.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a Brønsted–Lowry base.
2. What is the formula of the oxonium ion?
3. In HCOOH + H₂O ⇌ HCOO⁻ + H₃O⁺, name the acid and base in the forward reaction.
4. Name the acid and base in the reverse reaction of question 3.
5. In C₆H₅NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ C₆H₅NH₃⁺ + OH⁻, which species is the acid in the forward reaction?
6. Write the conjugate base of H₂PO₄⁻.
7. Write the conjugate acid of HPO₄²⁻.
8. Write an equation showing HSO₄⁻ acting as a base towards H₃O⁺.
9. Write an equation showing H₂O acting as an acid towards NH₃.
10. In HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻, which species is the base?
11. Identify the base in 2H⁺ + O²⁻ → H₂O.
12. Why is "a substance containing hydrogen" not a good definition of an acid?

### Answers

1. A proton acceptor.
2. **H₃O⁺**.
3. Acid **HCOOH**; base **H₂O**.
4. Acid **H₃O⁺**; base **HCOO⁻**.
5. **H₂O** (it becomes OH⁻).
6. **HPO₄²⁻**.
7. **H₂PO₄⁻**.
8. HSO₄⁻ + H₃O⁺ → H₂SO₄ + H₂O
9. NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
10. **HNO₃** (it accepts a proton).
11. **O²⁻**.
12. Many compounds containing hydrogen, such as methane, do not donate protons; the definition must describe what the species does (donates H⁺).
