## 3.2.1 Photosynthesis at a glance

| Item | Recall |
|---|---|
| Word equation | carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light energy) |
| Symbol equation | 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ |
| Light absorbed by | Chlorophyll, in chloroplasts (some plant cells and algae) |
| Carbon dioxide from | The air |
| Water from | The soil |
| By-product | Oxygen |

Atom count: the same three totals (carbon, hydrogen, oxygen) appear on both sides.

## Uses of glucose (3.2.1 d, e)

| Product or use | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Respiration | Energy release |
| Starch | Insoluble storage |
| Fat or oil | Storage, for example in seeds |
| Cellulose | Strengthens cell walls |
| Protein | Growth and repair; needs nitrate ions from the soil |

Memory hook: starch is the store you can carry without water problems; cellulose is the wall; protein needs nitrogen.

## Limiting factors (3.2.1 c)

- Three factors: low temperature, shortage of carbon dioxide, shortage of light.
- They interact. The one in shortest supply is the limiting factor.
- Rising part of a graph: the x-axis factor is limiting. Flat part: another factor is limiting.
- To find the new limiting factor, name something that was constant and could now be the cause, then support it with data.

Method in steps for a limiting-factor question:
1. Look at the section of the line the question points to.
2. Is the rate still changing with the x-axis factor? If yes, that factor limits.
3. If flat, compare a second line, or name the factor held constant.
4. Quote two numbers from the data.

## Greenhouse economics

| Factor | How a grower raises it | Pays off when |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Heating | Cold limits the rate and the extra crop is worth more than the fuel |
| Carbon dioxide | Burning fuel or releasing the gas | Carbon dioxide is limiting |
| Light intensity | Lamps | Light is limiting, especially in short winter days |

Work it through: extra yield × price per kg − extra cost = net gain. Negative means not worth it.

Small reminder: a greenhouse yield rises from 48 kg to 60 kg per bed. Percentage increase = (60 − 48) ÷ 48 × 100 = 25%.

## Required practical: rate of photosynthesis

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| Independent variable | One of lamp distance, carbon dioxide supply (hydrogencarbonate) or temperature |
| Dependent variable | Bubbles per minute, or volume of oxygen per minute |
| Control variables | The other two factors, same piece of pondweed, time allowed per setting |
| Reliability | Repeat three or more times, calculate a mean, leave out anomalies |
| Safety | Keep mains lamps away from water; take care with hot water |

Worked reminder: bubbles in 4 minutes were 60, 64 and 62. Mean = 62. Rate = 62 ÷ 4 = 15.5 bubbles per minute.

Common improvements: heat shield between lamp and plant, longer counting time, measuring gas volume rather than counting bubbles.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply, which sets the maximum rate at that moment |
| Stoma (plural stomata) | A pore in the leaf surface, through which gases diffuse |
| Guard cells | A pair of cells around each stoma that change shape to open or close it |
| Wilting | Loss of firmness when water leaves cells faster than it is replaced |
| Transpiration stream | Movement of water from roots, through the xylem, out of the leaves |
| Translocation | Movement of dissolved sugars in the phloem from leaves to the rest of the plant |
| Root hair | A tiny extension of a root cell that increases surface area |

## Worked reminder: using data to name the limiting factor

A grower records the rate of photosynthesis (arbitrary units) as light increases, at 15 °C and at 25 °C, with the same carbon dioxide supply.

| Light (units) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate at 15 °C | 4 | 8 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Rate at 25 °C | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 17 |

At light 1 and 2 the rates are identical at both temperatures, so light limits. At light 4, the rate is 10 at 15 °C but 16 at 25 °C, so temperature limits at 15 °C. Percentage increase from warming at light 4 = (16 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 = 60%. Model sentence: "At 15 °C the rate is flat from 3 to 5 units because temperature, not light, is limiting; warming to 25 °C raises it from 10 to 16."

## Photosynthesis and respiration side by side

| | Photosynthesis | Respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Cells with chloroplasts | All living cells |
| Light needed | Yes | No |
| Reactants | Carbon dioxide and water | Glucose and oxygen |
| Energy | Absorbed from light | Released |
| When | When light is available | All the time |

## 3.2.2 Exchange in plants

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| Stomata | Let carbon dioxide in and oxygen out by diffusion; help control water loss |
| Root hairs | Increase surface area for water and mineral ion uptake |
| Flat, thin leaf | Large surface area to volume ratio |
| Internal air spaces | Let gases diffuse to cells inside the leaf |
| Guard cells | Surround each stoma and control its size |

Surface area to volume ratio, worked: leaf 6 cm × 2 cm × 0.1 cm has surface area 2(12 + 0.6 + 0.2) = 25.6 cm² and volume 1.2 cm³, so the ratio is 21.3 : 1. Thin shapes give high ratios.

## Water loss and wilting

- Water vapour is lost mainly from leaves, mostly through stomata.
- Faster in hot, dry and windy conditions.
- If water is lost faster than roots replace it, the stomata can close. This prevents wilting but reduces carbon dioxide uptake.
- Water entering the guard cells opens the pore; water leaving them closes it.

Small reminder: a shoot loses 5.0 g of water in a hot room and 3.5 g in a cooler room. The cooler room loses (5.0 − 3.5) ÷ 5.0 × 100 = 30% less.

## Xylem and phloem

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water, mineral ions | Dissolved sugars |
| Direction | Roots to stem and leaves | From leaves to growing regions and storage organs |
| Named process | Transpiration stream | Translocation |
| Structure | Hollow dead tubes, strong walls with lignin | Living sieve tubes with pores, companion cells |
| Why it suits the job | Open tube for flow; walls resist collapse | Pores allow flow; companion cells supply energy |

Must-know distinction: the transpiration stream carries water upwards to be lost at the leaves; translocation carries sugar away from the leaves. Both are in separate tissues.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the symbol equation for photosynthesis, balanced.
2. Where is chlorophyll found?
3. Name two substances a plant makes from glucose for storage.
4. Why does a plant need nitrate ions?
5. A graph of rate against light intensity flattens. What does that tell you?
6. A greenhouse yield rises by 90 kg, selling at £1.50 per kg, and the extra cost is £110. Is it worth it?
7. Give two ways of making the pondweed practical more reliable.
8. How do root hairs help a plant?
9. Which cells control the size of a stoma?
10. Name the tissue that carries dissolved sugars, and the process.

### Answers

1. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light above the arrow).
2. In chloroplasts, found in some plant cells and in algae.
3. Any two of starch, fat or oil.
4. To make proteins (with glucose-derived molecules).
5. Another factor is now limiting, not light.
6. Income 90 × 1.50 = £135; net = 135 − 110 = £25 gain, so yes, marginally.
7. Repeat and take a mean; use a heat shield; measure gas volume.
8. They increase the surface area for uptake of water and mineral ions.
9. Guard cells.
10. Phloem; translocation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- A symbol equation that does not balance, or a missing energy source.
- Saying "plants take in oxygen for photosynthesis".
- Explaining a flat graph with "the plant is full" rather than naming another limiting factor.
- Giving an economics answer with no numbers when the question supplies costs and prices.
- Describing the practical without naming the variable that is kept constant.
- Writing "stomata open and close" without saying that guard cells change shape.
- Saying water moves up the xylem "because the roots push it" and forgetting the pull from evaporation in leaves.
- Naming xylem as the sugar carrier.
