## Key ideas at a glance

| Statement | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| P8.3d | All bodies emit radiation. Hotter means greater intensity and a distribution shifted to shorter wavelengths |
| P8.3e | Eight planets, minor planets, moons, artificial satellites. Geostationary and polar orbits differ |
| P8.3f | Gravity acts towards the centre, so velocity changes while speed stays constant |
| P8.3g | A stable orbit has one speed for its radius. Change the speed and the radius must change |
| P8.3h | Temperature depends on absorbed radiation against emitted radiation. Equilibrium means they are equal |

## P8.3d: emitted radiation (Higher Tier only)

- Every body emits electromagnetic radiation at every temperature.
- The emission is a **continuous range** of wavelengths, not one value.
- Raise the temperature and:
  - the total energy emitted per second per square metre (intensity) increases
  - the strongest emission moves to a shorter wavelength
- Cool objects: mainly infrared. Very hot objects: visible light, then bluer light.
- Heated metal: no glow, then dull red, orange, yellow-white.

Sketch to remember: two smooth humps on one axis. The hot one is taller and sits further left. The cool one is lower and sits further right.

**Method for a two-mark comparison:** one mark for intensity (hotter body, greater), one for wavelength (hotter body, strongest emission at shorter wavelength).

## P8.3e: solar system facts (Higher Tier only)

Order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

| Feature | Inner planets | Outer planets |
|---|---|---|
| Names | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune |
| Make-up | Rocky, solid surface | Gas giants and ice giants |
| Size and density | Smaller, denser | Larger, less dense |
| Moons | None to two | Many |

- **Minor planets:** smaller bodies orbiting the Sun, for example in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. Dwarf planets such as Pluto and Ceres.
- **Moon:** a natural satellite of a planet.
- **Artificial satellite:** a human-made body in orbit. Gravity is the inward force on both types.

| Orbit | Height and period | Position | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geostationary | About 36 000 km above the surface, 24 hours | Above the equator, appears fixed in the sky | Communications, television, weather |
| Polar | Low, around one and a half hours | Passes over both poles, Earth turns below | Mapping, environment monitoring, weather, surveillance |

Similar to natural satellites: held by gravity, same laws. Different: built and launched, small, chosen orbit, working life.

## P8.3f and P8.3g: circular orbits (Higher Tier only)

Remember the chain:

1. Gravity acts towards the centre.
2. Gravity is at right angles to the velocity.
3. So the direction changes, but not the speed.
4. Changing velocity is an acceleration, so a resultant force is needed.

| Quantity | Constant in a circular orbit? |
|---|---|
| Speed | Yes |
| Direction | No |
| Velocity | No |
| Resultant force (size) | Yes |
| Resultant force (direction) | No, always towards the centre |

**Stable orbit rules (qualitative):**

- Lower orbit: stronger gravity, faster speed, shorter period.
- Higher orbit: weaker gravity, slower speed, longer period.
- A satellite that speeds up moves into a larger orbit radius. A satellite that slows moves into a smaller one.
- A stable circular orbit cannot keep the same radius if its speed changes.

Useful relation from speed = distance / time: orbital speed = 2πr / T.

**Worked reminder.** The Moon orbits at radius 3.84 × 10⁸ m, period 27.3 days. T = 27.3 × 86 400 = 2.359 × 10⁶ s. Speed = 2π × 3.84 × 10⁸ / 2.359 × 10⁶ = 1020 m/s.

## P8.3h: radiation balance (Higher Tier only)

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Absorbed greater than emitted | Warms |
| Emitted greater than absorbed | Cools |
| Absorbed equals emitted | Constant temperature (equilibrium) |

- A warmer body emits more, so heating stops rising once emission matches absorption.
- Net power = absorbed power − emitted power. Energy = power × time.
- Dark, matt surfaces absorb well. Light, shiny surfaces reflect.

**Earth's temperature depends on:**

- radiation arriving from the Sun
- the fraction reflected by clouds, ice and light surfaces
- infrared emitted by the surface and atmosphere
- atmosphere composition: ozone absorbs ultraviolet; greenhouse gases absorb infrared from the surface and re-emit it in all directions

**Method for a greenhouse-gas question:** (1) more greenhouse gas absorbs more outgoing infrared, (2) less escapes to space, so emitted is less than absorbed, (3) Earth warms, (4) it emits more until the balance returns at a higher temperature.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Speed and velocity | Speed has size only. Velocity has size and direction |
| Intensity and wavelength | Intensity is how much energy is emitted per second per square metre. Wavelength says where in the spectrum the emission is strongest |
| Absorbed and reflected | Absorbed radiation raises the energy of the body. Reflected radiation does not |
| Emitted and reflected | Emitted radiation comes from the body's own temperature. Reflected radiation just bounces off |
| Moon and artificial satellite | Natural against built and launched |
| Geostationary and polar | One fixed point, 24 hour period, higher; whole surface over a day, low, short period |
| Ozone and greenhouse gases | Ozone absorbs ultraviolet from the Sun. Greenhouse gases absorb infrared from Earth |

## Short worked examples

**Orbit speed from data.** Radius 4.22 × 10⁷ m, period 86 400 s.

1. Circumference = 2π × 4.22 × 10⁷ = 2.652 × 10⁸ m
2. Speed = 2.652 × 10⁸ / 86 400 = 3070 m/s

**Comparing two orbits without numbers.** A is closer to the planet than B. Gravity at A is stronger, so A needs a higher speed to stay in a stable circular orbit, and A completes an orbit in a shorter time.

**Heating balance.** A drinks can in sunshine absorbs 25 W and emits 10 W. Net gain = 15 W. In 4 minutes it gains 15 × 240 = 3600 J. It keeps warming until emission is 25 W.

**Explaining a glow.** Question: why does a hotter filament look whiter? Answer: higher temperature moves the strongest emission to shorter wavelength, so the visible output includes more of the shorter (blue and green) wavelengths as well as red, and the intensity is greater.

## Quick self-test

1. State what happens to the wavelength of strongest emission when a body is heated.
2. Why does a metal rod glow red before white?
3. Name the four outer planets in order from the Sun.
4. State one similarity and one difference between a moon and an artificial satellite.
5. Why can a geostationary satellite stay above one place on Earth?
6. A satellite orbits with radius 8.0 × 10⁶ m and period 7100 s. Calculate its speed to 3 significant figures.
7. Explain why a planet in a circular orbit is accelerating at constant speed.
8. A satellite speeds up and keeps a stable orbit. What must happen to its radius?
9. A roof absorbs 400 W and emits 340 W. Calculate the energy gained in 5 minutes.
10. Explain why extra carbon dioxide warms Earth until a new equilibrium.

### Answers

1. It gets shorter.
2. At lower temperature the strongest emission is at long wavelength (infrared) with only a little red light. As temperature rises, the distribution moves to shorter wavelengths, so more yellow, green and blue light is added and the glow turns white.
3. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
4. Similarity: both held in orbit by gravity. Difference: a moon is natural; an artificial satellite is built and launched.
5. Its period is 24 hours, it orbits above the equator and in the same direction as Earth spins.
6. Circumference = 2π × 8.0 × 10⁶ = 5.027 × 10⁷ m. Speed = 5.027 × 10⁷ / 7100 = **7080 m/s**.
7. Its direction changes, so its velocity changes; a change in velocity is an acceleration, caused by the resultant force of gravity towards the centre.
8. The radius must increase (a larger orbit).
9. Net power = 60 W. Energy = 60 × 300 = **18 000 J**.
10. More infrared from the surface is absorbed and re-emitted, so less escapes, emission falls below absorption, the surface warms and emits more, and balance returns at a higher temperature.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the speed changes direction". Speed has no direction; say velocity.
- Leaving out which way gravity acts. State it acts towards the centre of the orbit.
- Giving only one half of a two-part comparison of hot and cool bodies.
- Saying "heat" is emitted instead of radiation.
- Forgetting that the new equilibrium on a warming Earth is at a higher temperature, not a return to the old one.
- Mixing up geostationary and polar orbits. Geostationary: fixed above the equator. Polar: over the poles and covers the whole surface.
- Not converting days or hours to seconds before using speed = distance / time.
- Describing a satellite's orbit as a straight line because "speed is constant". Constant speed in a circle still means a changing velocity.
- Treating the Sun as a planet. It is a star, and the planets orbit it.
