## Late Qing China, 1839-1912

### Chronology

- 1839-42: First Opium War; Treaty of Nanjing (1842).
- 1850-64: Taiping Rebellion.
- 1856-60: Second Opium War; Summer Palace burned (1860).
- 1861-95: Self-Strengthening movement; arsenals, shipyards, the Zongli Yamen (foreign office).
- 1894-95: Sino-Japanese War; Treaty of Shimonoseki (Taiwan ceded; Korea's independence recognized).
- 1897-98: scramble for concessions.
- 1898: Hundred Days' Reform; Cixi's coup.
- 1899-1901: Boxer Uprising; Boxer Protocol (1901).
- 1901-11: New Policies; examinations abolished (1905); New Army; provincial assemblies (1909).
- 1905: Tongmenghui founded under Sun Yat-sen.
- 1908: Cixi and the Guangxu Emperor die; Puyi succeeds.
- 1911: railway nationalization protests; Wuchang uprising (10 October).
- February 1912: Puyi abdicates; Yuan Shikai becomes president.

### Causes of collapse, sorted

| Type | Factors |
|---|---|
| External | Defeats and unequal treaties; indemnities; loss of territory; Japanese example |
| Internal rebellion | Taiping and other risings; growth of regional armies |
| Reform | Self-Strengthening's limits; New Policies raised expectations and empowered elites |
| Ideology | Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People: nationalism, democracy, people's livelihood |
| Trigger | Railway nationalization; Wuchang mutiny; provincial secessions |

## Japan, 1912-1941

### Chronology

- 1912: Taishō era begins.
- 1915: Twenty-One Demands on China.
- 1918: Rice riots; Hara Takashi becomes prime minister.
- 1921: Hara assassinated.
- 1923: Great Kantō earthquake.
- 1925: universal male suffrage; Peace Preservation Law.
- 1926: Shōwa era begins.
- 1930: London Naval Treaty; Prime Minister Hamaguchi shot.
- 18 September 1931: Mukden Incident.
- 1932: Manchukuo; Inukai assassinated (15 May).
- 1933: Japan leaves the League of Nations.
- 26 February 1936: attempted army coup.
- November 1936: Anti-Comintern Pact with Germany.
- 7 July 1937: Marco Polo Bridge incident; war with China.
- December 1937: Nanjing massacre.
- September 1940: Tripartite Pact.
- July-August 1941: US asset freeze and oil embargo.
- 7 December 1941: Pearl Harbor.

### Why militarism rose

- **Constitutional structure:** armed forces answerable to the emperor; service ministers had to be serving officers, so the army and navy could bring down cabinets.
- **Economic crisis:** the Depression and the collapse of silk prices devastated rural Japan.
- **Ideology:** nationalism, emperor worship and pan-Asian ideas.
- **Political violence:** assassinations and coups by young officers and nationalist societies.
- **Foreign policy success:** the easy conquest of Manchuria made the army popular.
- **International context:** Western tariffs and immigration restrictions, and the weakness of the League of Nations.

## China under Deng, 1976-1997

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| September 1976 | Mao dies |
| October 1976 | Gang of Four arrested |
| December 1978 | Third Plenum: "reform and opening" |
| 1978-79 | Democracy Wall; Wei Jingsheng imprisoned |
| 1979 | Four Cardinal Principles; one-child policy introduced (1979-80) |
| 1980 | Special Economic Zones, including Shenzhen |
| April-June 1989 | Tiananmen protests; 4 June crackdown |
| 1992 | Southern Tour revives reform |
| February 1997 | Deng dies |
| July 1997 | Hong Kong returns to China |

**Four Modernizations:** agriculture, industry, national defence, science and technology.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unequal treaties | Treaties forced on China giving foreigners privileges such as treaty ports and extraterritoriality |
| Extraterritoriality | Foreigners subject to their own countries' courts, not Chinese courts |
| Self-Strengthening | Qing policy of adopting Western military and industrial technology |
| Tongmenghui | Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary alliance (1905) |
| Taishō democracy | The period of party government in Japan in the 1910s and 1920s |
| Kwantung Army | Japanese army in Manchuria |
| Household responsibility system | Chinese reform giving farm households control over production after quotas |
| Special Economic Zone | Area with incentives for foreign investment and market activity |

## Essay angles to prepare

| Content area | Question angle | Factors to weigh |
|---|---|---|
| Late Qing | Why did reform fail to save the dynasty? | Conservative resistance, foreign pressure, regionalism, the effects of the New Policies |
| 1911 | How important was Sun Yat-sen? | Ideology and organization against the army, provincial elites and the railway crisis |
| Japan | Why did party government collapse after 1930? | Depression, military independence, assassinations, the Manchurian success |
| Road to war | Why did Japan attack the USA in 1941? | Oil embargo, war in China, the resources of South-East Asia, Axis alignment |
| Deng | How far did China change under Deng? | Economic reform against political continuity |

## Exam traps

- **Writing the whole nineteenth century** when the question asks about 1901-1911.
- **Ignoring the Japanese constitutional structure** in explaining militarism.
- **Treating Deng as a liberal.** Economic reform came with political control.
- **Neglecting Oceania or South-East Asia** if your sections include them.
- **Dates outside the section.** Paper 3 questions are tightly dated.

## Self-test

1. What did the Treaty of Shimonoseki give Japan? *Taiwan, and Chinese recognition of Korea's independence (along with an indemnity).*
2. Why did the Taiping Rebellion weaken the Qing in the long term? *Regional armies under provincial leaders defeated it, shifting power away from Beijing.*
3. What was the Boxer Protocol? *The 1901 settlement imposing a huge indemnity and foreign troops in Beijing.*
4. When was the civil service examination abolished? *1905.*
5. What were the Three Principles of the People? *Nationalism, democracy and people's livelihood.*
6. Who was the first commoner prime minister of Japan? *Hara Takashi (1918).*
7. What did the Peace Preservation Law of 1925 do? *Restricted left-wing and anti-system political activity.*
8. Which prime minister was assassinated on 15 May 1932? *Inukai Tsuyoshi.*
9. What incident began full-scale war with China in 1937? *The Marco Polo Bridge incident (7 July).*
10. What were the Four Modernizations? *Agriculture, industry, national defence, science and technology.*
11. What was the Democracy Wall? *A 1978-79 movement of posters in Beijing calling for reform, ended by the government.*
12. What did Deng's Southern Tour of 1992 achieve? *It revived market reform after the conservative reaction following 1989.*
