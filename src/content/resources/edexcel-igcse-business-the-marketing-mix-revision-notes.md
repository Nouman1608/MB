## Product (4.3.1)

**Definitions**

- Good: a physical item. Service: an activity done for the customer.
- Product portfolio: the full set of products a business offers.
- Extension strategy: action to delay decline of a product.
- Brand: the identity customers link with a product or business.

**Goods v services**

| | Goods | Services |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Physical | Activity |
| Stock | Can be stored | Usually cannot |
| Judging quality | Can inspect first | Often judged after use |
| Depends on | Materials and production | People delivering it |

**New product development in steps.** Idea, market research, design and test a sample, cost and price, launch. Purpose: lower the risk of a costly failure.

**Packaging: five jobs.** Protect; give information; ease transport and display; promote; support the image. Cost is the main drawback.

**Product life cycle**

| Phase | Sales | Likely action |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low | Build awareness |
| Growth | Rising fast | Expand output and outlets |
| Maturity | Levelling | Defend share |
| Decline | Falling | Extend, cut costs or withdraw |

**Extension strategies:** new market or segment; new packaging; new feature or flavour; price change; fresh promotion. Choose one that gives customers a new reason to buy.

**Boston matrix (standard form).**

| Market growth | High relative share | Low relative share |
|---|---|---|
| High | Star | Question mark |
| Low | Cash cow | Dog |

Method in steps:

1. Relative share = own share ÷ largest rival's share.
2. Decide whether share is above 1 (leader) or below.
3. Decide whether market growth is fast or slow.
4. Place the product in a box.
5. Recommend: back stars, use cash cows to fund others, research question marks, review dogs.

Worked reminder: share 12%, largest rival 24%, market growth 9%. Relative share 12 ÷ 24 = 0.5, growth is fast, so question mark.

## Price (4.3.2)

| Strategy | What the business does | When it might suit |
|---|---|---|
| Cost plus | Unit cost plus a mark-up | Wants a sure margin; few comparable rivals |
| Penetration | Low launch price | Wants quick share; price-sensitive customers |
| Skimming | High launch price, lowered later | New or distinctive product; early buyers will pay more |
| Competition | Price close to rivals | Similar products; customers compare prices |
| Promotional | Temporary price cut | Clearing stock; attracting trial; short-term boost |

**Cost plus in steps.**

1. Unit cost = variable cost per unit + (fixed costs ÷ output).
2. Mark-up = unit cost × mark-up percentage.
3. Price = unit cost + mark-up.

Worked reminder: variable cost 6.00, fixed costs 9,000, output 3,000, mark-up 50%. Unit cost 6.00 + 3.00 = 9.00; mark-up 4.50; price **13.50**.

**Promotional pricing check.** Compare profit, not only revenue. Profit = (price − unit cost) × units. A bigger discount needs a bigger rise in sales to break even on profit.

## Place (4.3.3)

| | Retailer | E-tailer (e-commerce) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Shop selling to the public | Business selling online |
| For | Customers can see and try; wide reach; shop visibility | Low premises cost; wide market; open any time |
| Against | Retailer takes a share of the price; less control | Delivery and returns cost; cannot try the item; needs a good website |

Many firms use both. The right mix depends on the product, the customers and cost.

## Promotion (4.3.4)

| Method | Points to make |
|---|---|
| Advertising | Wide reach; paid; can be costly |
| Sponsorship | Brand linked to an event or team; audience should match target market |
| Product trials | Lets customers try; lowers risk; good for new products |
| Special offers | Quick sales boost; lower profit per sale |
| Branding | Recognition, loyalty, ability to charge more |

**Above and below the line**

| | Above the line | Below the line |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Paid media aimed at a wide audience | Other methods, often direct or targeted |
| Examples | TV, radio, press, cinema | Special offers, trials, vouchers, direct mail |

**Public relations.** Managing how the public sees the business (news stories, charity support, handling complaints) to improve company image and brand. Not paid advertising.

**Technology in promotion**

- Targeted advertising online: shown to chosen groups by age, location or interests.
- Viral advertising via social media: shared by users; cheap but hard to control.
- E-newsletters: emails to people who signed up; cheap but limited to existing contacts.

**Segments.** Match the method to location, demographics, lifestyle, income or age. Always tie your answer to the segment in the question.

**Why a brand matters.** Loyalty; support for a higher price; easier launch of new products; stands out from rivals.

## Answering marketing mix questions

The specification lists command words such as state, identify, define, calculate, outline, explain, analyse, justify and evaluate. A reliable pattern for the longer ones:

1. **Point.** Name the strategy, channel or method.
2. **Evidence.** Use a figure or fact from the case, such as the number of employees, the price, the age of the customers.
3. **Link.** Say what follows for the business (more sales, lower profit, wider reach).
4. **Judgement.** For evaluate and justify, weigh the other side and finish with a decision that depends on the facts given.

Answers that use the context usually earn more than a reply that would fit any business.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Penetration v skimming:** low price first, versus high price first.
- **Cost plus v competition pricing:** based on your costs, versus based on rivals' prices.
- **Promotional v penetration:** temporary cut on an existing product, versus a low launch price for a new product.
- **Above v below the line:** wide paid media, versus other (often targeted) methods.
- **PR v advertising:** managing public image, versus paid messages.
- **Market share v relative market share:** your share, versus your share divided by the largest rival's share.

## Quick self-test

1. State two differences between a good and a service.
2. Name the four phases of the product life cycle.
3. Give two extension strategies.
4. A product has 20% market share, the largest rival has 40%, and the market is growing slowly. Find its relative market share and place it on the Boston matrix.
5. Unit cost is 8.00 and the mark-up is 25%. Find the price.
6. Name the pricing strategy where a new product is launched at a high price.
7. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of selling through a retailer.
8. Classify as above or below the line: (a) a radio advert, (b) a discount voucher.
9. State one benefit and one risk of viral advertising via social media.
10. Explain one way a strong brand helps a business.

### Answers

1. Any two: a good is physical and a service is an activity; goods can be stored but services usually cannot; goods can be inspected before purchase but services are often judged after use; service quality depends on the people providing it.
2. Introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
3. Any two: new market, new packaging, new feature, price change, fresh promotion.
4. 20 ÷ 40 = 0.5; low share in a slow-growth market, so a **dog**.
5. 8.00 × 0.25 = 2.00; price **10.00**.
6. Skimming.
7. Advantage: customers can see and try the product, or reach is wide. Disadvantage: the retailer takes a share of the selling price, or the producer has less control.
8. (a) Above the line. (b) Below the line.
9. Benefit: spreads cheaply to many people. Risk: the business cannot control how the message is shared or changed.
10. For example: loyal customers keep buying, so sales are more secure; or customers accept a higher price for a trusted brand.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a strategy but not saying when it suits the business in the question.
- Describing a Boston matrix box without working out relative share.
- Calling a promotion successful because revenue rose, with no profit check.
- Writing "it gets the product to the customer" for place without naming retailer or e-tailer.
- Defining a method (such as sponsorship) but not linking it to a segment.
- Giving advantages only on an evaluate question, with no judgement.
- Confusing penetration and promotional pricing.
- Leaving out units or the currency in a pricing calculation.
