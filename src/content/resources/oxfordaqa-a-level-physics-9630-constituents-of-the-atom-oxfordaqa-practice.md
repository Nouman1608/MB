Data: e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; proton and neutron mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A magnesium ion is written ²⁶₁₂Mg²⁺. State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons it contains. **[3]**

**2.** Calculate the specific charge of the ion in Question 1, ignoring the mass of its electrons. **[2]**

**3.** Write the nuclide notation for a nucleus of calcium (symbol Ca) containing 20 protons and 24 neutrons. **[1]**

**4.** State the relative charge and approximate relative mass of a neutron and of an electron. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the alpha-scattering experiment was carried out in a vacuum, and why gold was used for the foil. **[2]**

**6.** Uranium-235 and uranium-238 nuclei both have proton number 92. Explain which has the larger specific charge. **[2]**

**7.** The radius of an atom is about 10⁻¹⁰ m and the radius of its nucleus about 10⁻¹⁵ m. Estimate the ratio of the volume of the atom to the volume of the nucleus. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** A sulfide ion is ³²₁₆S²⁻.

**(a)** State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the ion. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the specific charge of the ion, including the mass of its electrons. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in your answer to (b) if the electron mass is ignored. **[1]**

**9.**

**(a)** An ion has 19 nucleons and proton number 9. Its specific charge is −5.05 × 10⁶ C kg⁻¹, ignoring electron mass. Determine the charge on the ion and the number of electrons it has. **[3]**

**(b)** A bare nucleus has 20 nucleons and a specific charge of 4.79 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹. Determine its proton number. **[2]**

**10.** In the alpha-scattering experiment, a small fraction of alpha particles are deflected through large angles. Predict and explain how the number of large-angle deflections would change if:

**(a)** the alpha particles had less kinetic energy **[2]**

**(b)** the gold foil were replaced by an aluminium foil of similar thickness **[2]**

**(c)** a much thicker gold foil were used, and why the results would then be harder to interpret. **[2]**

**11.**

**(a)** Describe the plum pudding model of the atom. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this model predicts that alpha particles are deflected only through small angles. **[2]**

**(c)** A helium nucleus has a charge of +2e but a mass about four times that of a proton. Explain why this observation supported the idea of a neutral particle in the nucleus. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** Calculate the specific charge of a helium-3 nucleus and of a helium-4 nucleus. Both have proton number 2. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the two values and explain why it has this value. **[2]**

**13.** A teacher builds a scale model of an atom, using a marble of diameter 3.0 cm to represent the nucleus.

**(a)** Estimate the diameter the model atom would need. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the density of the nucleus is very much greater than the average density of the atom. **[2]**

**(c)** State which result of the scattering experiment the large empty region of the model represents. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 12 protons [1]; 26 − 12 = 14 neutrons [1]; 12 − 2 = 10 electrons [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A positive ion has lost electrons: subtract the charge from Z.

**2.** Charge = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and mass = 26 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 4.34 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; specific charge = 7.37 × 10⁶ C kg⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Use the net charge of the ion (+2e), not the charge of the 12 protons.

**3.** ⁴⁴₂₀Ca (nucleon number 44 at top left, proton number 20 at bottom left) [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* The top number is protons plus neutrons, 20 + 24.

**4.** Neutron: relative charge 0, relative mass 1 [1]; electron: relative charge −1, relative mass about 1/1830 (accept 0.0005) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Writing the electron's relative mass as 0 does not earn the mark.

**5.** Air molecules would absorb or scatter the alpha particles, which have a short range in air [1]; gold can be made into an extremely thin foil, so each alpha particle is deflected by at most one nucleus (and gold nuclei have a large positive charge) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "To make it accurate" is not a reason; link each choice to what it prevents or allows.

**6.** Both have the same charge, 92e [1]; uranium-235 has three fewer neutrons and so a smaller mass, giving it the larger specific charge [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* State which quantity is the same and which differs before concluding.

**7.** Radius ratio = 10⁻¹⁰ / 10⁻¹⁵ = 10⁵ [1]; volume ∝ r³, so the volume ratio is about (10⁵)³ = 10¹⁵ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Forgetting to cube the radius ratio is the usual slip.

**8. (a)** 16 protons [1]; 16 neutrons [1]; 18 electrons [1]

**(b)** Charge = −2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = −3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C [1]; mass = 32 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ + 18 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 5.346 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; specific charge = −5.99 × 10⁶ C kg⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** Ignoring electrons gives −5.988 × 10⁶ C kg⁻¹, a change of about 0.03% [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep the minus sign, and keep at least four significant figures in the mass, or the change in (c) disappears in rounding.

**9. (a)** Mass = 19 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 3.17 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; charge = −5.05 × 10⁶ × 3.17 × 10⁻²⁶ = −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, which is −1e [1]; electrons = 9 + 1 = 10 [1]

**(b)** Z/A = 4.79 × 10⁷ / 9.58 × 10⁷ = 0.500 [1], so Z = 0.500 × 20 = 10 [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing the charge by e and rounding to a whole number is the expected check that the answer is a whole number of elementary charges.

**10. (a)** More large-angle deflections [1]; with less kinetic energy, the same repulsive force from the nucleus changes the direction of the alpha particle more [1]

**(b)** Fewer large-angle deflections [1]; aluminium nuclei have a much smaller charge (13e compared with 79e), so the repulsion is weaker [1]

**(c)** Alpha particles may be deflected by several nuclei in turn, and more are absorbed [1]; so a deflection angle no longer shows the effect of a single nucleus, which is what the conclusions rely on [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Each prediction needs a direction (more or fewer) and a reason based on the electrostatic repulsion.

**11. (a)** Positive charge spread evenly through the volume of the atom, with electrons embedded in it [1]

**(b)** The positive charge is spread out, so the electric force on an alpha particle passing through is weak everywhere [1]; the alpha particles are massive and fast, so they are deflected only slightly [1]

**(c)** Two protons account for the charge but only half the mass [1]; a particle with mass similar to the proton and no charge would explain the extra mass without changing the charge [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* For (b), "nothing to deflect them" is wrong: the model predicts small deflections, not none.

**12. (a)** Helium-3: 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ / (3 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = 6.39 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹ [1]; helium-4: 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ / (4 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = 4.79 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** Ratio = 6.39 / 4.79 = 1.33 (4 : 3) [1]; the charges are equal and the masses are in the ratio 3 : 4, so the specific charges are in the inverse ratio [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A ratio question rewards the reasoning from the two masses, not only the number.

**13. (a)** 10⁵ × 3.0 cm = 3.0 × 10⁵ cm, about 3 km [1]

**(b)** Nearly all the atom's mass is in the nucleus [1], but the nucleus occupies only about 10⁻¹⁵ of the atom's volume, so its density is about 10¹⁵ times the average density of the atom [1]

**(c)** Most alpha particles pass straight through the foil with little or no deflection [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Density questions need both mass and volume; mentioning only "the nucleus is small" earns one mark at most.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Z for the charge of an ion instead of its net charge.
- Using the neutral atom's electron count for an ion.
- Dropping the sign of a negative ion's specific charge.
- Forgetting to cube a ratio of radii.
- Predictions with no reason, or reasons that mention attraction.
- Stating the plum pudding model predicts no deflection.
