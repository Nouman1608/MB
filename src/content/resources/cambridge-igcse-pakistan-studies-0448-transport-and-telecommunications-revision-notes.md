**These notes summarise Section 7 Transport and telecommunications of Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan
Studies 0448 Paper 2.** Learn the pattern of the networks, what helps and hinders them, the
international crossings and ports, the meaning of a dry port, and the role of telecommunications.
Full explanations are in the [Transport and telecommunications study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-pakistan-studies-0448-transport-and-telecommunications/);
test yourself with the [Transport and telecommunications practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-pakistan-studies-0448-transport-and-telecommunications-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**0448 Paper 2, Section 7 (2026 syllabus)**
- **7(a) Internal transport:** density and pattern from maps; factors helping and hindering; recent improvements and feasibility; comparing road, rail and air; evaluating motorways and airports
- **7(b) International transport:** cross-border roads (with passes) and railways; Keamari, Qasim and Gwadar; international airports; location factors; dry ports
- **7(c) Telecommunications:** importance in education, industry, services and trade; problems of provision; role in development

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Network density | How closely routes are spaced in an area |
| Linear pattern | Routes running in lines, for example along a valley |
| Feasibility | Whether a scheme is practical, affordable and worthwhile |
| Motorway | A dual carriageway with controlled access, built for fast long-distance traffic |
| Pass | A route through a mountain range, often used by a cross-border road |
| Hinterland | The area a port serves |
| Dry port | An inland terminal where goods are cleared by customs and containerised |
| Containerisation | Moving goods in standard sealed containers between ship, rail and road |
| Telecommunications | Sending information at a distance: radio, television, phones, fax, email, internet |
| Digital divide | The gap between areas or people with good access to telecommunications and those without |

## Patterns to describe from a map

- **Dense** in the Indus Plain of Punjab and northern Sindh.
- **Linear** along the Indus: Peshawar – Lahore – Multan – Hyderabad – Karachi.
- **Sparse** in Balochistan, the Thar and Cholistan deserts and the northern mountains.
- **Air** serves large cities and remote northern towns such as Gilgit, Skardu and Chitral.

## Helps and hinders in one line each

- **Relief:** flat plains help; mountains hinder.
- **Rivers:** valleys give routes; floods and wide channels need costly bridges.
- **Climate:** monsoon floods, snow, avalanches and heat damage routes.
- **Ground:** landslides in mountains; shifting sand in deserts.
- **Demand:** many people and industries justify building; sparse areas do not.
- **Money and security:** investment helps; debt, high maintenance and unrest hinder.

## Recent improvements

Motorways (M-2 Lahore–Islamabad and others, including CPEC links); Karakoram Highway upgrade with
tunnels around the Attabad lake; plans to upgrade the main railway line; new Islamabad International
Airport (2018); bus rapid transit and Lahore's Orange Line.

## Road, rail and air compared

| Mode | Best for | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Door-to-door delivery, perishables, short and medium distances, remote villages | Congestion, accidents, fuel cost, road damage |
| Rail | Heavy, bulky goods and many passengers over long distances | Slow, ageing track and engines, fixed routes |
| Air | Speed, high-value goods, remote mountain towns | Expensive, small loads, weather delays |

## International links

| Neighbour | Road (pass or crossing) | Rail |
|---|---|---|
| China | Karakoram Highway, Khunjerab Pass (closed by winter snow) | None |
| Afghanistan | Khyber Pass (Torkham); Chaman, via the Khojak Pass from Quetta | — |
| Iran | Quetta to Taftan | Quetta to Taftan and on into Iran |
| India | Wagah, near Lahore | Wagah and Khokhrapar (passenger services suspended since 2019) |

**Seaports:** Keamari (Karachi, busiest, congested); Port Qasim (east of Karachi, bulk cargo);
Gwadar (deep water, Balochistan, CPEC).

**International airports:** include Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, Faisalabad
and Sialkot.

**Dry ports:** customs and containers inland; examples Lahore, Faisalabad, Sialkot, Multan,
Peshawar, Sust; developed to cut congestion at Karachi, save time and cost, and reduce theft and
damage.

## Telecommunications

- **Education:** distance learning by radio, television and internet (Allama Iqbal Open University,
  Virtual University).
- **Industry:** orders, designs and payments sent instantly.
- **Services:** mobile banking, call centres, software, telemedicine.
- **Trade:** exporters contact buyers; farmers check prices.
- **Problems:** mountains, scattered villages, cost of masts and cables, power cuts, low incomes and
  literacy, floods, insecurity.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why dry ports have been developed in many cities of Pakistan. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Customs clearance can be done near the factories, so exporters save time and
travel [1]. Goods are sealed in containers inland, which reduces theft and damage on the journey [1].
Less paperwork and waiting at Karachi reduces congestion at the seaport [1]. Containers can travel
by rail, which is cheaper for long distances [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Naming the Bolan Pass as a border crossing: it links Sibi and Quetta.
- Describing a map pattern without places or directions.
- Saying air transport is best for bulky goods.
- Forgetting maintenance in "help and hinder" answers.
- Listing telecommunications without linking each to a field.

## How is this section examined?

Paper 2 (1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks): three questions from a choice of five, each worth 25 marks
with parts (a) to (d), using maps, photographs and data. Part (d) tests analysis, often an
evaluation of a scheme such as a motorway or airport.

## Self-test

1. In which part of Pakistan is the road and rail network densest?
2. Name the pass on the Karakoram Highway at the Chinese border.
3. Name the border crossing on the Khyber Pass route.
4. Which pass does the route from Quetta to Chaman use?
5. Name the three seaports in the syllabus.
6. What is a dry port?
7. Give one dry port on the Karakoram Highway.
8. Which mode of transport is best for perishable fruit over a short distance, and why?
9. Give two reasons why roads are difficult to maintain.
10. Name two cities with international airports, other than Karachi.
11. Give one way telecommunications help farmers.
12. What does "feasibility" mean?

### Answers

1. The Indus Plain of Punjab and northern Sindh. 2. Khunjerab. 3. Torkham. 4. The Khojak Pass.
5. Keamari (Karachi Port), Port Qasim and Gwadar. 6. An inland terminal where goods are cleared
through customs and packed into containers for the seaport. 7. Sust. 8. Road, because it is
door-to-door and quick. 9. Any two: floods, landslides, overloaded lorries, heat, lack of money.
10. Any two: Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, Faisalabad, Sialkot. 11. Checking market
prices or weather by phone. 12. Whether a scheme is practical, affordable and worthwhile.
