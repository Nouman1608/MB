Pearson Edexcel International A Level English Literature (YET01) is a
modular course for students who enjoy reading closely and writing
critically about poetry, prose and drama. It moves from contemporary
writing to Shakespeare and pre-1900 poetry, so students meet a wide span of
literary history. The qualification is made up of four externally assessed
units, each a 120-minute paper worth 50 marks and 25% of the full award.
Units 1 and 2 form the International AS stage and Units 3 and 4 the A2
stage. It was first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June
2016, and the specification has since been reissued several times.

## How the course is organised

Each unit is defined by the kind of literature it studies.

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1: Post-2000 Poetry and Prose** has two parts, poetry and prose,
  and introduces close reading of recent writing.
- **Unit 2: Drama** covers pre-1900 and post-1900 drama, so students compare
  how plays from different periods work on the page and in performance.

**A2 stage**

- **Unit 3: Poetry and Prose** returns to the two forms met in Unit 1, this
  time at A2 depth.
- **Unit 4: Shakespeare and Pre-1900 Poetry** pairs a Shakespeare play with
  poetry written before 1900.

This page does not list set texts. Check with your teacher which texts you
will read for each unit.

## How it's assessed

All four units are externally assessed written papers of the same length
and value.

| Unit | Paper code | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 1: Post-2000 Poetry and Prose | WET01/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 50 | 25% |
| Unit 2: Drama | WET02/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 50 | 25% |
| Unit 3: Poetry and Prose | WET03/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 50 | 25% |
| Unit 4: Shakespeare and Pre-1900 Poetry | WET04/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 50 | 25% |

Each paper is divided into two sections. In Units 1, 2 and 4 the two
sections carry 25 marks each. Unit 3 is the exception: one section is worth
20 marks and the other 30, so the two parts of that paper deserve different
amounts of time and planning.

## How the parts fit together

The course is built in two loops. Poetry and prose appear at AS in Unit 1
and again at A2 in Unit 3; drama appears at AS in Unit 2 and Shakespeare at
A2 in Unit 4. The A2 units therefore extend skills you have already
practised rather than starting from nothing. Analysis of a contemporary poem
in Unit 1 prepares you for the wider poetry work in Unit 3, and writing about
how a pre-1900 play builds tension in Unit 2 is direct preparation for
writing about Shakespeare in Unit 4.

The skills that run across all four units are the core skills of literary
study: close analysis of language, form and structure; an awareness of the
contexts in which texts were written and received; comparison between texts
and between periods; and the ability to build a sustained, well-supported
argument. Because every unit is an essay-based paper of the same length,
your essay writing improves across the whole course, and feedback on one
unit's essays usually applies to the others too.

Range also matters. Over the four units you move from post-2000 writing back
to Shakespeare and pre-1900 poetry, and seeing how earlier writing differs
from modern writing in vocabulary, conventions and assumptions makes both
easier to discuss.

## Planning your study

In the first year, read the Unit 1 and Unit 2 texts early and more than once.
A first reading for story and character, followed by a second reading with a
notebook for quotations, themes and techniques, is a reliable habit.
Short practice paragraphs on a single passage build analytical precision
before you attempt full essays.

In the second year, begin the Unit 3 and Unit 4 texts as soon as possible,
since pre-1900 language often takes longer to read with confidence. Build a
compact bank of short quotations for each text, organised by theme or
character, so that you can use them flexibly rather than relying on
memorised paragraphs.

Wider reading helps across all four units. Reading other poems, stories or
plays from the same periods as your set texts, and short critical pieces about
them, gives you points of comparison and a clearer sense of how each text
relates to its time. Keep brief notes on this reading so that it can be used
in essays rather than forgotten.

Practise full essays under timed conditions once each unit's texts are
taught, and ask your teacher which series each unit will be entered for.
For advice on pacing the two sections of each paper, use the
exam-preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Pearson Edexcel International A Level English Literature?**
There are four units. Units 1 and 2 make up the International AS stage and Units 3 and 4 the A2 stage. Each is an externally assessed paper worth 50 marks and 25%.

**How long is each YET01 exam?**
Each unit paper lasts 120 minutes (2 hours).

**What does each unit cover?**
Unit 1 is Post-2000 Poetry and Prose, Unit 2 is Drama (pre-1900 and post-1900), Unit 3 is Poetry and Prose, and Unit 4 is Shakespeare and Pre-1900 Poetry.

**How are the marks split within each paper?**
Units 1, 2 and 4 each have two sections of 25 marks. Unit 3 has one section of 20 marks and one of 30 marks.

**When was YET01 first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2016.
