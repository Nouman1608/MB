## First: confirm which two options your course teaches

A school studies **2 of these 4** options, and **both** are examined on
**both** Paper 3 and Paper 4 (different topic areas on each paper) —
the syllabus doesn't fix which two. **Confirm with your
teacher** which specific options apply to you before revising the
wrong two.

## The four options at a glance

| Option | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 Clinical | Classification/diagnosis of disorders; treatment approaches |
| 2 Consumer | Psychology applied to consumer behaviour, advertising, decision-making |
| 3 Health | Psychological factors in health, illness, health-related behaviour |
| 4 Organisational | Psychology applied to workplace behaviour, motivation, management |

Each option applies the **core approaches** (Biological, Cognitive,
Learning, Social) from AS Paper 1 to a specific applied context.

## What makes a strong answer in each option

| Option | Strong-answer skill |
|---|---|
| Clinical | Discuss **more than one** classification/treatment approach, weighing relative strengths — not one uncontested model |
| Consumer | Apply concepts (persuasion, decision-making biases) to **real, specific** advertising/purchasing scenarios |
| Health | Discuss psychological factors **alongside** biological ones; use named models to explain why advice doesn't translate into behaviour change |
| Organisational | Apply named concepts (motivation/leadership theories) directly to a **specific workplace scenario** |

## Research Methods runs through every option

Each specialist option is assessed **alongside Research Methods
applied to that context** — this is tested as consistently as in the
core Paper 1/2 content. Be ready to **suggest and evaluate a research
method** for studying a question within your chosen option, not just
recall the option's theoretical content in isolation.

## Worked example: applying an approach in Health Psychology

Question: explain why a patient does not follow medical advice, using
a named psychological approach.

```
Weak answer:    "The patient forgot" or "didn't care" -- no
                psychological framework at all.
Strong answer:  A cognitive explanation (how the patient perceives
                their own illness risk) OR a social explanation
                (influence of family/community attitudes) applied
                DIRECTLY to the scenario -- not described in the
                abstract.
Evaluation:     Note a specific limitation, e.g. a purely cognitive
                explanation may understate social/economic barriers
                -- rather than treating one explanation as complete.
```

This apply-then-evaluate structure transfers directly to Consumer and
Organisational scenario questions too.

## Second worked example: applying a theory in Organisational Psychology

Question: explain why introducing a bonus scheme failed to increase
productivity in a described workplace scenario.

```
Weak answer:    "Employees didn't care about the bonus" -- no named
                theory applied.
Strong answer:  Apply Herzberg's two-factor theory -- pay is
                classified as a hygiene factor that prevents
                dissatisfaction but does not, on its own, generate
                genuine motivation, which instead requires
                motivators such as recognition or job enrichment.
                This directly explains why a purely financial
                incentive might fail to shift productivity.
Evaluation:     Note a limitation, e.g. Herzberg's theory was
                developed from research in specific occupational
                contexts and its findings may not generalise fully
                to all types of workplace or culture.
```

The same apply-a-named-theory-then-evaluate-it structure used for
Health Psychology above works identically here -- the pattern, not
the specific content, is what transfers across all four specialist
options.

## Exam traps

- Assuming all four options are studied, rather than confirming
which two your course actually covers.
- Discussing an applied scenario without linking it back to a named
psychological approach or concept.
- Treating the specialist option as disconnected from Research
Methods, rather than being ready to suggest/evaluate a method for
studying it.
- Giving only common-sense explanations for behaviour instead of
applying the specific theory the question tests.
- Presenting a single classification/treatment/theory as the
complete answer without evaluation.

## Why the choice of options is a school decision, not fixed

Because the school chooses which two of the four options to teach
(and those two are then examined on both Paper 3 and Paper 4), two candidates at different schools may
study a completely different pair of options despite sitting the same
qualification. This matters directly for revision: past-paper
practice should be sourced against the exact options your own course
teaches, since a past paper covering an option your school did not
teach (e.g. Consumer Psychology when your course actually covers
Clinical and Health) provides no useful practice material at all,
however well-written the questions are.

## Worked example: applying a concept in Consumer Psychology

Question: explain why a limited-time discount offer might increase
impulse purchases, using a named psychological concept.

```
Weak answer:    "People like discounts" -- no psychological
                framework applied.
Strong answer:  Apply the concept of scarcity/loss aversion --
                framing an offer as time-limited increases perceived
                urgency, and the prospect of losing access to a
                discount is processed as a potential loss, which
                behavioural research suggests people weight more
                heavily than an equivalent potential gain.
Evaluation:     Note a limitation, e.g. this effect may be weaker for
                planned, high-value purchases than for low-cost,
                impulse-driven ones, so the explanation may not
                generalise across all types of consumer decision.
```

This mirrors the apply-then-evaluate structure already shown for
Health and Organisational Psychology above — the same reasoning shape
carries across all four specialist options, which is worth
recognising explicitly rather than relearning it separately for each
one.

## Self-test

1. How many of the four specialist options does a typical course
study, and on which papers are they examined?
2. Name the four specialist options.
3. What core approaches does each option apply to its context?
4. Why is Research Methods relevant to a specialist-option question?
5. What distinguishes a strong Consumer Psychology answer from a
weak one?

**Answers:** 1. Two of the four — both examined on both Paper 3 and Paper 4, with different topic areas on each paper.
2. Clinical, Consumer, Health, Organisational. 3. Biological,
Cognitive, Learning, Social — the same four core approaches examined
at AS Level, applied to the specific option's context. 4. Because
each specialist option is assessed alongside Research Methods applied
to that context, so candidates must be able to suggest and evaluate a
method for studying a question within the option, not just recall
theory. 5. A strong answer applies concepts like persuasion or
decision-making biases to a real, specific advertising or purchasing
scenario; a weak answer discusses consumer behaviour only in general,
unapplied terms.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which specialist options are on the Cambridge A Level Psychology 9990 syllabus?**
There are four: Clinical, Consumer, Health and Organisational Psychology. Your school chooses two, so confirm with your teacher which ones you are taking before you revise.

**What approaches do the specialist options apply?**
Each option applies the four core approaches from AS Paper 1, which are the biological, cognitive, learning and social approaches, to a specific applied context.

**How does Herzberg's two-factor theory explain a failed bonus scheme?**
Herzberg classifies pay as a hygiene factor, which can prevent dissatisfaction but does not on its own create motivation. Motivation instead needs motivators such as recognition or job enrichment.

**Why should past-paper practice match your own options?**
A past paper on an option your school did not teach gives you no useful practice, however good the questions are. Source practice against the two options your course covers.
