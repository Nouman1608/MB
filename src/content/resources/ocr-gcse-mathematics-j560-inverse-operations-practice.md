## Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the inverse of each operation.

**(a)** Multiply by 9 **[1]**

**(b)** Cube **[1]**

**(c)** Subtract 14 **[1]**

**2.** Use compensation to work out

**(a)** 756 + 198 **[2]**

**(b)** 1 205 − 699 **[2]**

**3.** Use doubling and halving to work out

**(a)** 15 × 28 **[2]**

**(b)** 125 × 32 **[2]**

**4.** By first multiplying both numbers by 2, work out 3 600 ÷ 45. **[2]**

**5.** Find the missing number in each calculation.

**(a)** □ + 487 = 1 003 **[1]**

**(b)** 1 512 ÷ □ = 24 **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** I think of a number. I multiply it by 6, subtract 15, then divide by 3. The answer is 19. Find my number. **[3]**

**8.** I think of a positive number. I add 3, square the result, then subtract 20. The answer is 101. Find my number. **[3]**

**9.** Mia thinks of a number. She subtracts it from 75, then halves the result. Her answer is 26. Mia says her number must be 127.

**(a)** Show that 127 does not give 26. **[1]**

**(b)** Find Mia's number. **[2]**

**10.** A number machine multiplies the input by 4, then subtracts 9.

**(a)** Find the output when the input is 13. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the input when the output is 71. **[2]**

**(c)** To find an input from an output, Ravi says "add 9, then divide by 4". Sara says "divide by 4, then add 9". Who is correct? Give a reason. **[1]**

**11.** Find the digits a and b in this subtraction. Show your reasoning.

7a2 − b46 = 356 **[3]**

**12.** Find the digits a and b in this multiplication. Show your reasoning.

4a8 × 7 = 3b16 **[3]**

**13.** Leo divides 144 by his number, then subtracts 5. The answer is 7.

**(a)** Find Leo's number. **[2]**

**(b)** Leo says the inverse of "divide 144 by the number" is "multiply by 144". Use your answer to part (a) to show that he is wrong. **[1]**

**14.** Ayesha works out 2 916 ÷ 54 and gets 64. Use an inverse operation to show that she is wrong, then find the correct answer. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Divide by 9** [1]

**(b)** **Cube root** [1]

**(c)** **Add 14** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Name the operation precisely. "Root" alone for part (b) is not enough, because the inverse of a cube is the cube root.

**2. (a)** 756 + 200 − 2 [1]; **954** [1]

**(b)** 1 205 − 700 + 1 [1]; **506** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b), taking away 700 removes 1 too many, so 1 is added back.

**3. (a)** 30 × 14 [1]; 60 × 7 = **420** [1]

**(b)** 250 × 16 or 1 000 × 4 [1]; **4 000** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep halving the even factor until the other factor becomes a power of 10 or the calculation is easy.

**4.** 7 200 ÷ 90 [1]; **80** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Both numbers must be doubled. Doubling only 45 would halve the answer.

**5. (a)** 1 003 − 487 = **516** [1]

**(b)** 1 512 ÷ 24 = **63** [1]

**(c)** 38 × 17 = **646** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the box is the divisor, so divide 1 512 by 24. Multiplying 1 512 by 24 is the most common error.

**6.** 1 786 ÷ 38 (or 1 786 ÷ 47) attempted with method [1]; = 47 (or 38), which matches, so the product is correct [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* State the conclusion. A calculation without "so 47 × 38 = 1 786 is correct" does not finish the check.

**7.** Reverse order with inverses: 19 × 3 = 57 [1]; 57 + 15 = 72 [1]; 72 ÷ 6 = **12** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check forwards: 12 × 6 = 72, 72 − 15 = 57, 57 ÷ 3 = 19.

**8.** 101 + 20 = 121 [1]; √121 = 11 [1]; 11 − 3 = **8** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The question says the number is positive, so the negative root, −11, is not needed.

**9. (a)** 75 − 127 = −52, and half of −52 is −26, not 26 [1]

**(b)** 26 × 2 = 52, so 75 − n = 52 [1]; n = 75 − 52 = **23** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Mia added 75 instead of subtracting from 75. "Subtract the number from 75" is undone by subtracting the result from 75.

**10. (a)** 13 × 4 − 9 = **43** [1]

**(b)** 71 + 9 = 80 [1]; 80 ÷ 4 = **20** [1]

**(c)** **Ravi**: the last step, subtract 9, must be undone first [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Sara's order gives 71 ÷ 4 + 9 = 26.75, and 26.75 × 4 − 9 is not 71.

**11.** Units: 2 − 6 needs an exchange, 12 − 6 = 6 [1]; tens: after the exchange a − 1 − 4 must end in 5, which needs another exchange: 10 + a − 5 = 5, so **a = 0** [1]; hundreds: 7 − 1 − b = 3, so **b = 3** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check by the inverse: 356 + 346 = 702.

**12.** Units: 8 × 7 = 56, units digit 6 matches, carry 5 [1]; tens: 7a + 5 ends in 1, so 7a ends in 6, giving **a = 8** [1]; 488 × 7 = 3 416, so **b = 4** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Only one digit from 0 to 9 makes 7a end in 6, so the answer is unique. Check: 3 416 ÷ 7 = 488.

**13. (a)** 7 + 5 = 12, so 144 ÷ n = 12 [1]; n = 144 ÷ 12 = **12** [1]

**(b)** 12 × 144 = 1 728, which is not Leo's number, so multiplying by 144 does not undo the step [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* When the unknown is the divisor, divide the starting number by the result.

**14.** 64 × 54 = 3 456 [1]; 3 456 ≠ 2 916, so 64 is wrong [1]; correct answer **54**, since 54 × 54 = 2 916 [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An estimate also shows the problem: 3 000 ÷ 50 = 60, so the answer is near 60, but the inverse check proves which value is right.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Undoing the steps of a puzzle in the original order.
- Treating "subtract from" or "divide into" as if the unknown came first.
- Compensating in the wrong direction.
- Changing only one number when simplifying a division.
- Multiplying when the box is the divisor.
- Leaving out carries and exchanges in missing-digit columns.
- Writing a check calculation with no conclusion.
