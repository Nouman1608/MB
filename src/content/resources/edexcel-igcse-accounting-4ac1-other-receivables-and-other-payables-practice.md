## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State whether each item at the year end is an other receivable or an other payable.

**(a)** Electricity used in December, billed in January. **[1]**
**(b)** Insurance paid for the next six months. **[1]**
**(c)** Rent received from a tenant for January of next year. **[1]**
**(d)** Interest earned on savings but not yet credited by the bank. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why it is necessary to account for other payables. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between other receivables and other payables. **[2]**

**4.** Electricity paid during the year was 4,120. At the start of the year 380 was owed and at the end 455 was owed. Calculate the electricity expense for the year. **[2]**

**5.** An insurance premium of 2,880 for 12 months was paid on 1 May. Calculate the insurance expense for the year and the other receivable at the year end. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Talbot Garage owed wages of 1,150 at 1 January 2027. During 2027 it paid wages of 58,400. At 31 December 2027 wages of 1,320 were owed.

Prepare the wages account for 2027, showing the transfer to the income statement and the balance brought down. **[5]**

**7.** Fellside Bakery pays insurance each year on 1 July for the following 12 months. On 1 July 2026 it paid 4,200; on 1 July 2027 it paid 4,560.

**(a)** Calculate the other receivable at 1 January 2027 and at 31 December 2027. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the insurance account for 2027. **[4]**

**8.** Kirkby Hall lets a room for 650 a month. At 1 January 2027 the tenant owed two months' rent. During 2027 the business received 9,750 from the tenant.

**(a)** Prepare the rent receivable account for 2027. **[5]**
**(b)** State how the closing balance is classified in the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**9.** Marston Opticians has a draft profit for the year of 34,750. The following have not been recorded:

1. Rent owed at the year end, 1,200.
2. Advertising paid for next year, 640.
3. Interest receivable owed by the bank, 85.
4. Rent received in advance from a sub-tenant, 450.
5. Electricity owed at the year end, 395.

**(a)** Calculate the adjusted profit, showing the effect of each item. **[5]**
**(b)** Calculate the total of other receivables and of other payables at the year end. **[2]**

**10.** A bookkeeper charged the whole of a 12-month insurance premium of 3,000, paid on 1 October, as this year's expense.

**(a)** Calculate the correct expense. **[1]**
**(b)** State the effect of the error on profit and on current assets. **[2]**

**11.** The electricity bill for December arrives in January, after the year end. Explain, using the accruals concept, why it is included in this year's accounts and how it is shown. **[3]**

**12.** Commission earned by Ridgeway Agencies for 2027 was 4,800. During 2027 it received 4,350, which included 300 owed from 2026.

Prepare the commission receivable account for 2027. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Other payable [1]. (b) Other receivable [1]. (c) Other payable [1]. (d) Other receivable [1]. **[4]**

**2.** The accruals concept requires expenses to be matched to the period in which they are incurred, even if unpaid [1]; omitting them would overstate profit and leave liabilities out of the statement of financial position [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Other receivables are current assets: expenses paid in advance or income earned but not yet received [1]. Other payables are current liabilities: expenses incurred but not yet paid or income received in advance [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 4,120 − 380 + 455 [1] = **4,195** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Monthly cost 2,880 ÷ 12 = 240; May to December is 8 months, so expense = 8 × 240 = **1,920** [1]. Other receivable = 4 × 240 = **960** [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

```
Wages account (2027)
Debit                                  Credit
Bank                      58,400       1 Jan  Balance b/d          1,150
31 Dec Balance c/d         1,320       31 Dec Income statement    58,570
                          59,720                                  59,720
                                       2028
                                       1 Jan  Balance b/d          1,320
```

Opening accrual on the credit side [1]. Bank 58,400 [1]. Closing accrual 1,320 carried down on the debit side [1]. Transfer 58,400 − 1,150 + 1,320 = 58,570 [1]. Balance brought down on the credit side [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** 1 January 2027: six months of the 2026 policy prepaid, 4,200 × 6/12 = **2,100** [1]. 31 December 2027: six months of the 2027 policy prepaid, 4,560 × 6/12 = **2,280** [1].
**(b)**

```
Insurance account (2027)
Debit                                  Credit
1 Jan  Balance b/d         2,100       31 Dec Income statement     4,380
1 Jul  Bank                4,560       31 Dec Balance c/d          2,280
                           6,660                                   6,660
2028
1 Jan  Balance b/d         2,280
```

Opening prepayment on the debit side [1]. Payment 4,560 [1]. Transfer 4,380 (2,100 for January to June plus 2,280 for July to December) [1]. Closing balance 2,280 carried down and brought down on the debit side [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Income for 2027 = 12 × 650 = 7,800. Opening owed = 2 × 650 = 1,300.

```
Rent receivable account (2027)
Debit                                  Credit
1 Jan  Balance b/d         1,300       Bank                        9,750
31 Dec Income statement    7,800
31 Dec Balance c/d           650
                           9,750                                   9,750
                                       2028
                                       1 Jan  Balance b/d            650
```

Opening balance 1,300 on the debit side [1]. Bank 9,750 [1]. Income statement 7,800 [1]. Balance c/d 650 on the debit side [1]. Balance b/d on the credit side [1].
**(b)** The tenant has paid one month in advance, so 650 is an other payable under current liabilities [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Check: 9,750 received = 1,300 for 2026 + 7,800 for 2027 + 650 for January 2028.

**9. (a)** Rent owed: − 1,200 [1]. Advertising prepaid: + 640 [1]. Interest receivable: + 85 [1]. Rent received in advance and electricity owed: − 450 − 395 [1]. Adjusted profit = 34,750 − 1,200 + 640 + 85 − 450 − 395 = **33,430** [1].
**(b)** Other receivables = 640 + 85 = **725** [1]. Other payables = 1,200 + 450 + 395 = **2,045** [1]. **[7]**

**10. (a)** October to December = 3 months: 3,000 × 3/12 = **750** [1].
**(b)** Profit is understated by 3,000 − 750 = 2,250 [1]; current assets are understated by 2,250, because the other receivable has been left out [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The electricity was used in December, so the cost was incurred in this year [1]; the accruals concept matches expenses to the period in which they are incurred, not paid [1]. The amount is added to the electricity expense and shown as an other payable in current liabilities [1]. **[3]**

**12.**

```
Commission receivable account (2027)
Debit                                  Credit
1 Jan  Balance b/d           300       Bank                        4,350
31 Dec Income statement    4,800       31 Dec Balance c/d            750
                           5,100                                   5,100
2028
1 Jan  Balance b/d           750
```

Opening accrued income 300 on the debit side [1]. Receipts 4,350 [1]. Income statement 4,800 [1]. Closing other receivable of 750 brought down on the debit side [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Charging cash paid instead of the period's expense.
- Bringing balances down on the wrong side.
- Leaving out the opening balance.
- Treating income received in advance as income of the year.
- Counting the months in a part year incorrectly.
- Stating "affects profit" without a direction and figure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is an accrued expense carried down on the debit side?**
Writing the balance on the debit side makes the account balance, and it is then brought down on the credit side, where a liability belongs.

**How do I check a rent receivable account?**
Split the cash received into the part for last year, this year and next year. The part for this year must equal the income transferred to the income statement.
