## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ligand | species with a lone pair that forms a dative covalent bond to a central metal atom or ion |
| Complex | molecule or ion formed by a central metal atom or ion surrounded by one or more ligands |
| Monodentate ligand | forms one dative bond: H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻ |
| Bidentate ligand | forms two dative bonds: en (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂), C₂O₄²⁻ |
| Polydentate ligand | forms more than two: EDTA⁴⁻ (six) |
| Coordination number | number of dative bonds to the central ion |
| Ligand exchange | replacement of one ligand by another in a complex |

## Shapes

| Shape | Coordination number | Angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| linear | 2 | 180° | [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ |
| square planar | 4 | 90° | [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] |
| tetrahedral | 4 | 109.5° | [CuCl₄]²⁻, [CoCl₄]²⁻ |
| octahedral | 6 | 90° | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ |

## Formula and charge

    charge = metal ion charge + Σ(ligand charges)

    Cr³⁺ + 6H₂O        → [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺
    Fe³⁺ + 3C₂O₄²⁻     → [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻     (CN 6)
    Ni²⁺ + 3en          → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺       (CN 6)
    Co²⁺ + 4Cl⁻         → [CoCl₄]²⁻         (CN 4)
    Fe³⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻       → [Fe(EDTA)]⁻       (CN 6)

## Copper(II): reactions and observations

| Reagent | Product | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| (start) | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | pale blue solution |
| NaOH(aq) | Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ | pale blue precipitate |
| a little NH₃(aq) | Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ | pale blue precipitate |
| excess NH₃(aq) | [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | deep blue solution |
| conc. HCl | [CuCl₄]²⁻ (tetrahedral) | yellow(-green) solution |

    [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O
    [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O

## Cobalt(II): reactions and observations

| Reagent | Product | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| (start) | [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | pink solution |
| NaOH(aq) or a little NH₃(aq) | Co(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ | blue precipitate |
| excess NH₃(aq) | [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ | pale brown (straw) solution, darkening in air |
| conc. HCl | [CoCl₄]²⁻ (tetrahedral) | blue solution |

    [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O
    [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O

**Remember:** NH₃ replaces all six waters on Co²⁺ but only four on Cu²⁺. Cl⁻ is larger than H₂O,
so only four fit (octahedral → tetrahedral, 2+ → 2−).

## Feasibility from E⦵

    E⦵cell = E⦵(reduced) − E⦵(oxidised);  positive → feasible

| Test | E⦵ values / V | E⦵<sub>cell</sub> / V | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| MnO₄⁻ oxidises Cl⁻? | 1.52, 1.36 | +0.16 | yes: do not acidify with HCl |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ oxidises Cl⁻? | 1.33, 1.36 | −0.03 | no (standard conditions) |
| MnO₄⁻ oxidises Fe²⁺? | 1.52, 0.77 | +0.75 | yes |
| Fe³⁺ oxidises I⁻? | 0.77, 0.54 | +0.23 | yes |

## Titration equations and ratios

| System | Equation | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| MnO₄⁻ / Fe²⁺ | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O | 1 : 5 |
| MnO₄⁻ / C₂O₄²⁻ | 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O | 2 : 5 |
| Cu²⁺ / I⁻ | 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂ | 2 Cu²⁺ : 1 I₂ |
| I₂ / S₂O₃²⁻ | I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ | 1 : 2 |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ / Fe²⁺ | Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O | 1 : 6 |

- Manganate(VII): self-indicating (colourless → first permanent pale pink); acidify with dilute
  H₂SO₄.
- Ethanedioate: warm to about 60 °C; slow start, then faster as Mn²⁺ catalyses (autocatalysis).
- Copper: overall Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 1; starch near the end point; blue-black → colourless
  (with white CuI precipitate present).

*Quick check (invented data):* 0.268 g Na₂C₂O₄ (M = 134.0) = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol, so it needs
8.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol MnO₄⁻ = 40.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄.

## Exam traps

- Coordination number counts **bonds**, not ligands.
- Include the ligand charges when giving the charge on the complex.
- Excess NH₃ with Cu²⁺ gives [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺, not a hexaammine.
- A little NH₃ gives the **hydroxide precipitate**, not a complex.
- Give state and colour: "pale blue precipitate", "deep blue solution".
- Do not acidify manganate(VII) with HCl.
- Each I₂ comes from **two** Cu²⁺ ions.

## Self-test

1. Define a ligand.
2. What is the coordination number of Fe in [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻?
3. Give the formula and charge of the complex formed by Ni²⁺ with three en ligands.
4. State the shape and bond angle of [CoCl₄]²⁻.
5. Describe what is seen when aqueous ammonia is added dropwise, then in excess, to CuSO₄(aq).
6. Write an equation for the reaction of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess ammonia.
7. Why does [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ change shape when it reacts with concentrated HCl?
8. What colour change shows that water has been added to [CoCl₄]²⁻?
9. Why is dilute sulfuric acid, not hydrochloric acid, used in manganate(VII) titrations?
10. State the mole ratio of MnO₄⁻ to C₂O₄²⁻.
11. 25.0 cm³ of CuSO₄(aq) with excess KI needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ S₂O₃²⁻.
    Calculate [Cu²⁺].
12. Why does the MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻ reaction speed up after it starts?

**Answers**

1. A species with a lone pair of electrons that forms a dative covalent bond to a central metal
   atom or ion.
2. 6.
3. [Ni(en)₃]²⁺.
4. Tetrahedral, 109.5°.
5. Pale blue precipitate, which dissolves in excess to give a deep blue solution.
6. [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O.
7. Chloride ions are larger than water molecules, so only four fit around Cu²⁺: octahedral
   becomes tetrahedral.
8. Blue to pink.
9. MnO₄⁻ can oxidise Cl⁻ (E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = +0.16 V), which would use up extra manganate(VII).
10. 2 : 5.
11. mol Cu²⁺ = 2.24 × 10⁻³; concentration = 2.24 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0896 mol dm⁻³**.
12. Mn²⁺ formed in the reaction catalyses it (autocatalysis).
