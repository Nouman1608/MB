These questions cover sub-topic 5.2, Forest ecosystems, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level Environmental
Management (5014) syllabus: the causes and impacts of deforestation, the definition of sustainable forest
management, and the reasons it is needed. The contexts are a mining frontier, a cattle-ranching region and
a forest-cover data table.

## Section A

**1.** Define *sustainable management of forests*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** causes of deforestation that involve extracting resources from the forest itself. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by *genetic depletion*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the process by which leaves release water vapour into the atmosphere. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** The table shows forest cover in a region. *The data are invented for practice.*

| Year | Forest cover / km² |
|---|---|
| 2005 | 12 000 |
| 2015 | 10 200 |
| 2025 | 9 180 |

(a) Calculate the percentage loss of forest between 2005 and 2015. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the mean annual loss between 2015 and 2025. **[1]**
(c) Describe how the rate of forest loss changed over the whole period. Use data in your answer. **[2]**

**6.** A company opens an open-pit copper mine in a tropical forest and builds a 60 km road to the coast.
(a) Describe **two** ways the mine itself causes deforestation. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the road may cause more deforestation than the mine. **[2]**

**7.** Explain the difference between deforestation for subsistence farming and for commercial farming.
**[3]**

**8.** Explain how a hydro-electric power station can lead to deforestation. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how deforestation contributes to global warming. **[3]**

**10.** A cattle-ranching region has lost most of its forest. Rainfall in the region has become lower and less
reliable.
(a) Explain how deforestation may have changed rainfall patterns. **[3]**
(b) Suggest **one** other impact on the region's soil. **[1]**

**11.** Describe how forests help to prevent soil erosion. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why forests are described as a *genetic resource*. **[2]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the view that forests are worth more to a country if they are managed sustainably than if
they are cleared for farming. **[6]**

**14.** Discuss whether climate change or human clearance is the greater threat to forest ecosystems.
**[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Balancing the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans **[1]** while conserving forests for future
generations **[1]**.

**2.** Any two: logging and timber extraction **[1]**; rock, ore and mineral extraction **[1]**.

**3.** Loss of genetic variety within a species (for example, when its population becomes small or
isolated). **[1]**

**4.** Transpiration. **[1]**

**5.** (a) 12 000 - 10 200 = 1 800 km² **[1]**; 1 800 / 12 000 x 100 = **15%** **[1]**.
(b) (10 200 - 9 180) / 10 = 1 020 / 10 = **102 km² per year** **[1]**.
(c) The rate of loss slowed **[1]**: 180 km² per year from 2005 to 2015, falling to 102 km² per year from
2015 to 2025 (or 15% then 10% loss per decade) **[1]**.

**6.** (a) Any two: trees and soil are stripped from above the ore body **[1]**; waste rock is dumped on
surrounding forest **[1]**; land is cleared for buildings, housing or power lines **[1]**. **[max 2]**
(b) The road opens up remote forest to loggers, farmers and settlers **[1]**, who clear land along both sides
of it, over a far larger area than the mine **[1]**.

**7.** Subsistence farming clears small plots to grow food for the farmer's family **[1]**. Commercial
farming clears large areas for crops or cattle sold for profit **[1]**. Commercial clearance tends to be
permanent because the land stays in production, while small subsistence plots may be abandoned and
regrow **[1]**.

**8.** A dam is built and the valley behind it is flooded to form a reservoir **[1]**, drowning the forest; roads
and power lines built for the scheme clear more forest **[1]**.

**9.** Trees store carbon **[1]**; burning or decay of cleared trees releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse
gas **[1]**; fewer trees remain to absorb carbon dioxide by photosynthesis, so the carbon sink is lost **[1]**.

**10.** (a) Trees take up water through roots and release it by transpiration **[1]**; with fewer trees, less
water vapour enters the atmosphere **[1]**, so less condenses to form cloud and rain over the region **[1]**.
(b) Any one: soil erosion; desertification; loss of soil fertility after erosion. **[1]**

**11.** The canopy and leaf litter intercept rain, reducing rain splash on the soil surface **[1]**; roots bind
the soil together **[1]**; slower infiltration and water uptake reduce surface run-off that would wash soil
away **[1]**.

**12.** Forest species and varieties contain a wide range of genes **[1]**, which may be needed in future, for
example for breeding crops or developing medicines **[1]**.

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six, with credit for both sides and a conclusion. Sustainable
management: keeps carbon sinks and stores, helping regulate climate **[1]**; protects the water cycle and
reduces flood and erosion costs downstream **[1]**; provides a continuing supply of timber, food, medicine
and raw materials **[1]**; earns income from ecotourism **[1]**. Clearance for farming: brings food security,
jobs and export income **[1]**; the gain may fall as soils erode and lose fertility **[1]**. Conclusion: the
forest is worth more over the long term if managed sustainably, but only if local people gain income from it
**[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Human clearance: directly removes large areas through logging,
farming, roads, mining and reservoirs **[1]**; is often permanent **[1]**; can be reduced by laws and
management **[1]**. Climate change: drought and heat stress trees **[1]**; increases wildfires **[1]**; is
harder to control locally because it is a global problem **[1]**. Links: clearance releases carbon dioxide
that adds to climate change, so the two threats reinforce each other **[1]**. Conclusion: human clearance is
the greater direct threat now, but climate change may grow in importance **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving methods of management when the question asks why management is needed.
- Calculating percentage change from the new value instead of the original value.
- Naming an impact such as "flooding" without the process that links it to the loss of trees.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many causes of deforestation do I need to know?**
Six: logging and timber extraction; subsistence and commercial farming; roads and settlements; rock, ore
and mineral extraction; hydro-electric power stations; and climate change.

**Is climate change a cause or an impact of deforestation?**
Both appear in 5.2. Climate change is listed as a cause, and global warming and changes to rainfall
patterns are listed as impacts.
