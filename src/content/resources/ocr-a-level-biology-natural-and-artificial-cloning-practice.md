## Questions

**1.** Describe how a potato plant reproduces by vegetative propagation and explain why the new plants are genetically identical to the parent. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how you would take and root a stem cutting from a *Pelargonium*. Give a reason for each of three steps. **[6]**

**3.** A grower treats cuttings with rooting powder containing auxin. Each group has 40 cuttings. After four weeks, 22 untreated cuttings have rooted, 31 cuttings treated with 0.1% auxin have rooted and 26 treated with 0.8% auxin have rooted.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of cuttings that rooted in each group. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest one conclusion and one limitation of the investigation. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how micropropagation is used to produce many identical plantlets of a rare orchid from one parent plant. **[6]**

**5.** In a laboratory, every shoot is cut into 4 new shoots every 3 weeks, so the number is multiplied by 4 each cycle, starting from one shoot.

**(a)** Calculate the number of shoots after 15 weeks. **[2]**
**(b)** The laboratory needs more than 20 000 shoots. Calculate the minimum number of weeks required. **[2]**

**6.** A nursery proposes to use micropropagation to supply a new ornamental shrub. Evaluate this proposal. **[6]**

**7.** Explain how identical (monozygotic) twins form in humans and why they may still differ in phenotype. **[4]**

**8.** Artificial embryo twinning is used to produce cattle.

**(a)** Describe how it is carried out. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the calves are identical to each other but not to either parent. **[2]**

**9.** Dairy farmers use somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT) to clone a high-yielding cow.

**(a)** Describe the stages of SCNT. **[5]**
**(b)** State whose nuclear DNA the clone carries and explain one way in which its genome is not identical to the donor of the somatic cell. **[2]**

**10.** In a trial of SCNT, 200 reconstructed eggs gave 80 embryos. 16 pregnancies followed, 6 births and 4 animals alive at one year.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of reconstructed eggs that developed into embryos. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of embryos that led to a pregnancy. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of births that survived to one year. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest two concerns about SCNT that the data support. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate the use of cloning of animals in agriculture and medicine, including issues of longevity. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The potato tuber is a swollen underground stem that stores starch. [1] Buds (eyes) on the tuber grow into shoots, using the stored food, to form a new plant. [1] The cells are produced by mitosis without meiosis or fertilisation, so all have the same alleles as the parent. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain more credit when the structure is named correctly as a stem, and when mitosis is given as the reason for identity.

**2.** Cut a healthy shoot just below a node with a sharp, clean blade; the node region has tissue that can form roots. [1][1] Remove the lower leaves; this reduces water loss by transpiration. [1][1] Dip in rooting powder (auxin) and place in moist compost under a cover or in humid air; the auxin promotes root growth, and the humidity prevents wilting before roots can take up water. [1][1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each step needs its own reason. A list of steps with no "because" leaves the explanation unfinished.

**3. (a)** Untreated: 22 ÷ 40 × 100 = 55%. [1] 0.1% auxin: 31 ÷ 40 × 100 = 77.5%. [1] 0.8% auxin: 26 ÷ 40 × 100 = 65%. [1]
**(b)** Conclusion: auxin increased rooting, and 0.1% gave the highest percentage. [1] Limitation: one trial with one species and no repeats, so the difference could be chance. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the percentages when you draw a conclusion, and name a specific limitation rather than writing "not accurate".

**4.** Take a small explant, such as a shoot tip, from the parent. [1] Sterilise it (for example with dilute bleach) and use aseptic technique. [1] Place it on sterile agar containing sugar, minerals and vitamins. [1] Add plant growth regulators; cytokinin and auxin in the right ratio give shoots and roots, and a callus may form first. [1] Divide the shoots and sub-culture to multiply the numbers. [1] Root the plantlets, transfer to compost and acclimatise them gradually. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer keeps the steps in order and gives at least one condition for sterility and one for the medium.

**5. (a)** 15 weeks is 5 cycles. [1] 4⁵ = **1024 shoots**. [1]
**(b)** 4⁷ = 16 384, which is less than 20 000, and 4⁸ = 65 536. [1] 8 cycles × 3 = **24 weeks**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the number of cycles before giving weeks, so that the method is visible even if the arithmetic slips.

**6.** For: many identical plants quickly from one parent. [1] Plants can be produced all year in little space. [1] Meristem culture can give disease-free stock. [1] Against: costly, with skilled staff and sterile facilities. [1] Clones are genetically uniform, so one disease could destroy all plants; plantlets may be lost on transfer to soil. [1] Judgement: worthwhile for a valuable variety sold in large numbers, but less so for a cheap plant that grows well from cuttings. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a conclusion linked to the context. Listing points on both sides without a decision is usually incomplete.

**7.** After fertilisation, the zygote divides by mitosis to form a small group of cells. [1] The early embryo splits into two groups, each of which develops into an individual with the same genome as it came from one zygote. [1] Phenotype can differ because of different environments such as diet or disease. [1] Mutations after the split may also differ. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the twins come from one fertilised egg, and link the differences to the environment rather than to "different genes".

**8. (a)** Eggs and sperm from chosen cattle are used for in vitro fertilisation. [1] The embryo is allowed to divide to an early stage when its cells are totipotent. [1] The embryo is split into separate groups of cells, which are cultured. [1] The embryos are implanted into surrogate cows. [1]
**(b)** The calves came from one zygote by mitosis, so they have the same genotype. [1] The zygote was produced by a sexual cross with new combinations of alleles, so it is not identical to either parent. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking the lack of identity with the parents to meiosis and fertilisation is usually the clearest way of showing understanding.

**9. (a)** Take a somatic cell from the cow to be cloned. [1] Remove the nucleus from an unfertilised egg (enucleation). [1] Fuse the somatic cell with the egg, using a small electric pulse, or inject its nucleus. [1] Stimulate the egg to divide and culture it to an early embryo. [1] Implant the embryo into a surrogate cow. [1]
**(b)** The nuclear DNA matches the somatic cell donor. [1] Mitochondrial DNA comes from the cytoplasm of the egg donor, so this part of the genome differs. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the roles separate: somatic cell donor, egg donor and surrogate. Mixing them up is an easy way to lose credit.

**10. (a)** 80 ÷ 200 × 100 = **40%**. [1]
**(b)** 16 ÷ 80 × 100 = **20%**. [1]
**(c)** 4 ÷ 6 × 100 = **66.7%**. [1]
**(d)** Only 4 ÷ 200 = 2% of reconstructed eggs produced a surviving animal, so the method is inefficient and costly. [1] Most pregnancies and births did not give a surviving animal (16 pregnancies, 4 survivors), which raises animal welfare concerns. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division you used. Use the numbers from the question in the concern, not just a general statement.

**11.** Agriculture, for: copy animals with high yield or good disease resistance, giving a uniform herd. [1] Medicine, for: animals can produce human proteins in milk, and can be used as disease models. [1] Against: reduced genetic diversity, so one disease could affect all the clones. [1] Against: low success and many failed pregnancies raise welfare and ethical concerns. [1] Against: cost is high. [1] Longevity: Dolly was euthanised at about six and a half with lung disease and arthritis, and short telomeres were reported. [1] Later cloned sheep aged 7 to 9 were reported as ageing normally, so the evidence is conflicting and limited. [1] Judgement based on the balance of benefit and harm, with a reason. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Include evidence for the longevity point, and say why it is not conclusive. A one-sided list leaves the evaluation unfinished.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a clone from SCNT is identical to the egg donor or the surrogate.
- Giving steps for cuttings or micropropagation without a reason for each.
- Leaving out sterility, or the role of auxin and cytokinin, in tissue culture.
- Using "identical" without saying that the environment can still change phenotype.
- Describing artificial embryo twinning as copying an adult animal.
- Giving percentages without showing the division or the units.
- Evaluating with a one-sided list and no judgement.
- Writing about longevity with no evidence, or treating the evidence as settled.
