## Section A

**1.** Simplify 5ab − 2a²b + 3ba − 4ba². **[2]**

**2.** Expand and simplify 3(4x − 1) − 5(x − 2). **[2]**

**3.** Expand and simplify (4x − 3)(2x + 5). **[2]**

**4.** Factorise fully:

**(a)** 8x²y − 12xy² **[1]**

**(b)** 49x² − 36 **[1]**

**(c)** 9x² + 30x + 25 **[1]**

**5.** Factorise 2pq − 6p + q − 3. **[2]**

**6.** Factorise 2x² − 7x − 15. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Expand and simplify (x − 2)(x + 4)(x − 1). **[3]**

**8.** Expand and simplify (2x − 3y)². **[2]**

**9.** Factorise fully:

**(a)** 12x² − 27y² **[2]**

**(b)** 2x³ − 18x² + 40x **[2]**

**(c)** x² − 4x + 3xy − 12y **[2]**

**10.** Show that (x + 3)² − (x − 1)² is a multiple of 8 for every integer x. **[3]**

**11.** Write x² + 10x − 6 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**

**12.** Write 4x² + 8x − 5 in the form a(x + p)² + q. **[3]**

**13.** **(a)** Write −x² − 6x + 4 in the form a(x + p)² + q. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence state the greatest value of −x² − 6x + 4 and the value of x at which it occurs. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** ab terms: 5ab + 3ab = 8ab [1]; a²b terms: −2a²b − 4a²b = −6a²b. Answer **8ab − 6a²b** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 12x − 3 − 5x + 10 [1] = **7x + 7** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* −5 × −2 = +10. Writing −10 gives 7x − 13.

**3.** 8x² + 20x − 6x − 15 [1] = **8x² + 14x − 15** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** **4xy(2x − 3y)** [1]

**(b)** **(7x − 6)(7x + 6)** [1]

**(c)** **(3x + 5)²** [1] **[3]**

**5.** 2p(q − 3) + 1(q − 3) [1] = **(2p + 1)(q − 3)** [1] **[2]**

**6.** ac = −30, sum −7 → −10 and 3; 2x² − 10x + 3x − 15 = 2x(x − 5) + 3(x − 5) [1] = **(2x + 3)(x − 5)** [1] **[2]**

**7.** (x − 2)(x − 1) = x² − 3x + 2 [1]; (x² − 3x + 2)(x + 4) = x³ + 4x² − 3x² − 12x + 2x + 8 [1] = **x³ + x² − 10x + 8** [1] **[3]**

*Check:* x = 2 gives 0 in the factorised form, and 8 + 4 − 20 + 8 = 0. ✓

**8.** (2x − 3y)(2x − 3y) = 4x² − 6xy − 6xy + 9y² [1] = **4x² − 12xy + 9y²** [1] **[2]**

**9. (a)** 3(4x² − 9y²) [1] = **3(2x − 3y)(2x + 3y)** [1]

**(b)** 2x(x² − 9x + 20) [1] = **2x(x − 4)(x − 5)** [1]

**(c)** x(x − 4) + 3y(x − 4) [1] = **(x − 4)(x + 3y)** [1] **[6]**

**10.** (x² + 6x + 9) − (x² − 2x + 1) [1] = 8x + 8 [1] = 8(x + 1), which is 8 times an integer, so it is a multiple of 8 [1]. **[3]**

**11.** (x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25, so x² + 10x − 6 = (x + 5)² − 25 − 6 [1] = **(x + 5)² − 31** [1] **[2]**

**12.** 4(x² + 2x) − 5 [1] = 4[(x + 1)² − 1] − 5 [1] = **4(x + 1)² − 9** [1] **[3]**

*Check:* 4(x² + 2x + 1) − 9 = 4x² + 8x − 5. ✓

**13. (a)** −(x² + 6x) + 4 [1] = −[(x + 3)² − 9] + 4 [1] = **−(x + 3)² + 13** [1]

**(b)** −(x + 3)² is never positive, so the greatest value is **13** [1], when **x = −3** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* With a = −1, the −9 inside the bracket becomes +9. Writing −(x + 3)² − 5 is the usual error.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Combining 5ab with −2a²b in Question 1. They are not like terms.
- Missing the middle term when squaring a bracket (Question 8).
- Stopping at 3(4x² − 9y²) or 2x(x² − 9x + 20) in Question 9.
- Expanding (x − 1)² inside a subtraction without brackets, so that only the first term changes sign (Question 10).
- Forgetting to multiply the constant by a when a ≠ 1 (Question 12) or a = −1 (Question 13).
- Giving the turning point with the wrong sign of x: (x + 3)² is zero when x = −3.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I check first when factorising in 4024?**
A common factor, every time. Many expressions only show a difference of two squares or a trinomial after the common factor is taken out.

**How do I know grouping has worked?**
After factorising each pair, the two brackets must be identical. If they differ only in sign, take out a negative factor from the second pair.

**Why use completed square form?**
It shows the least or greatest value of a quadratic and where it occurs, and it gives one method of solving quadratic equations in subtopic 2.5.
