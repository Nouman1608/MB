Subtopic 22.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the particulate nature of electromagnetic radiation, the photon as a quantum of electromagnetic energy, E = hf, the electronvolt as a unit of energy, and photon momentum p = E/c. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Energy and momentum of a photon study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-energy-and-momentum-of-a-photon/); to apply the ideas, try the [Energy and momentum of a photon practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-energy-and-momentum-of-a-photon-practice/).

## Core ideas

- Electromagnetic radiation has a **particulate nature**: its energy is emitted, carried and absorbed in discrete packets.
- A **photon** is a quantum (discrete packet) of electromagnetic energy.
- Each photon of frequency f has energy hf; a beam can only carry whole numbers of photons.
- Higher intensity at the same frequency → more photons per second, same energy per photon.

## The equations

```
E = hf = hc/λ              h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s
p = E/c   (= h/λ)          c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹
1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
photons per second = P / E
force from absorbed beam = P/c;   reflected beam = 2P/c
```

## The electronvolt

- Energy transferred when an electron moves through a p.d. of 1 V.
- From W = QV with Q = e.
- A particle of charge 2e through 1 V gains 2 eV.

## Typical values

| Radiation | λ | E |
|---|---|---|
| Red light | 700 nm | 1.8 eV |
| Green light | 500 nm | 2.5 eV |
| Violet light | 400 nm | 3.1 eV |
| X-ray | 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m | 50 keV |

Photon momentum for visible light is about 10⁻²⁷ kg m s⁻¹.

## Beam questions in three steps

1. Energy of one photon: E = hc/λ (keep three significant figures; do not round further yet).
2. Photons per second: N = P/E, where P is the power of the beam (or the power reaching the surface).
3. If a force is needed: momentum delivered per second = N × (E/c) = P/c; double it for a reflecting surface.

For a beam spreading equally in all directions from a point source, the power reaching an area A at distance r is P × A/(4πr²).

## Quick calculations

**From eV.** A 2.0 eV photon: E = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; f = E/h = 4.8 × 10¹⁴ Hz; λ = c/f = 6.2 × 10⁻⁷ m; p = E/c = 1.1 × 10⁻²⁷ kg m s⁻¹.

**Photon rate.** A 10 mW beam at 532 nm: E = 3.74 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; N = 0.010 ÷ 3.74 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.7 × 10¹⁶ photons per second.

**From keV.** A 1.0 keV photon: E = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ J; λ = hc/E = 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ m.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photon | A quantum of electromagnetic energy, energy hf |
| Quantum | Smallest discrete amount of a quantity |
| Particulate nature | Radiation behaving as a stream of discrete particles (photons) |
| Planck constant h | Constant linking photon energy and frequency; 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s |
| Electronvolt (eV) | Energy gained by an electron moved through 1 V; 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J |
| Photon momentum | p = E/c; non-zero even though a photon has no rest mass |
| Intensity | Power per unit area; set by the number of photons per second |

## Exam traps

- Using wavelength in E = hf.
- Converting eV to J the wrong way, or twice.
- nm treated as 10⁻⁶ m.
- Saying brighter light has higher-energy photons.
- Using p = mv for a photon.
- Missing the factor 2 for reflection.
- Rounding the photon energy early, then dividing a power by it.
