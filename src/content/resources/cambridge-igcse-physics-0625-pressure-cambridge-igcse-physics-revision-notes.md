These notes condense section 1.8, Pressure, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Supplement content, marked **Extended only**, is examined on Papers 2 and 4. Worked examples are in the [study guide on pressure](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-pressure-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Motion, forces and energy revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy-revision-notes/) cover the whole of Topic 1.

## Definition and equations

- **Pressure:** force per unit area.

| Equation | Units | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| p = F / A | Pa (= N/m²), N, m² | Core |
| Δp = ρ g Δh | Pa, kg/m³, N/kg, m | Extended only |

Rearranged: F = pA; A = F/p; Δh = Δp/(ρg); ρ = Δp/(gΔh).

## Area conversions

| From | To m² | Example |
|---|---|---|
| cm² | ÷ 10 000 | 40 cm² = 0.0040 m² |
| mm² | ÷ 1 000 000 | 2.0 mm² = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ m² |
| cm × cm | convert each length to m first | 50 cm × 20 cm = 0.50 m × 0.20 m = 0.10 m² |

Prefixes: 1 kPa = 1000 Pa; 1 MPa = 10⁶ Pa.

## Force, area and pressure (Core)

- Same area, larger force → larger pressure.
- Same force, smaller area → larger pressure.
- Same force, larger area → smaller pressure.

**Low pressure wanted (large area):** snowshoes, skis, tractor tyres, tank tracks, wide bag straps, building foundations, a camel's wide feet on sand.

**High pressure wanted (small area):** knife blades, scissors, needles, nails, drawing-pin points, football studs, ice-skate blades.

**Describing an example:** "The [object] has a [large/small] area, so for the same force the pressure is [low/high], so it [does not sink / cuts easily]."

## Pressure in liquids (Core)

- Increases with **depth**.
- Increases with the liquid's **density**.
- Same depth, same liquid → same pressure, whatever the container shape.
- Applications: dams thicker at the base; water spurts faster from lower holes; water towers placed high; ears hurt deeper underwater.

## Δp = ρgΔh (Extended only)

- Δh = vertical depth **below the surface** (or vertical height difference between two points).
- Density must be in **kg/m³** (1.0 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³).
- Gives the pressure due to the liquid only; total pressure = atmospheric pressure + ρgΔh if atmospheric pressure is given.
- Doubling depth doubles Δp; doubling density doubles Δp.

### Method in steps

1. Convert density to kg/m³ and depth to m.
2. Substitute into Δp = ρgΔh.
3. Add atmospheric pressure only if the question asks for the total and gives the value.
4. Give the unit Pa (or kPa).

**Worked reminder.** Mercury has a density of 13.6 g/cm³ = 13 600 kg/m³. At 0.20 m below its surface: Δp = 13 600 × 9.8 × 0.20 = 26 656 Pa ≈ **27 kPa**, over thirteen times the value at the same depth in water. Leaving the density as 13.6 would give 26.7 Pa, a thousand times too small.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pressure | Force per unit area |
| Pascal (Pa) | Unit of pressure; 1 Pa = 1 N/m² |
| Area of contact | Area over which the force acts |
| Depth | Vertical distance below the surface of the liquid |
| Density | Mass per unit volume; needed for liquid pressure |

## Exam traps

- cm² → m²: divide by **10 000**, not 100.
- Using **mass** instead of **weight** as the force (multiply kg by g first).
- "A sharp knife needs less pressure": it needs less **force**, because the small area gives a large pressure.
- Thinking a wider tank has a bigger pressure at the bottom at the same depth.
- *(Extended)* Measuring Δh from the bottom of the tank.
- *(Extended)* Using g/cm³ in Δp = ρgΔh.
- *(Extended)* Adding atmospheric pressure when the question asks only for the change in pressure.

## Quick self-test

1. Define pressure.
2. A box of weight 240 N rests on an area of 0.60 m². Find the pressure.
3. Convert 80 cm² into m².
4. A force of 50 N acts on an area of 25 cm². Find the pressure in Pa.
5. Explain why a camel's wide feet help it walk on sand.
6. Explain why a drawing pin goes into a board point first, not head first.
7. State **two** factors that affect the pressure beneath the surface of a liquid.
8. Why is a dam thicker at the bottom than at the top?
9. *(Extended)* Find the pressure due to the water 12 m below the surface of a lake (ρ = 1000 kg/m³).
10. *(Extended)* At what depth in oil of density 900 kg/m³ is the pressure due to the oil 18 000 Pa?
11. *(Extended)* A liquid gives a pressure of 5880 Pa at a depth of 0.50 m. Find its density.
12. *(Extended)* Atmospheric pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Find the total pressure 20 m below the surface of fresh water.

### Answers

1. **Force per unit area.**
2. 240 ÷ 0.60 = **400 Pa**.
3. 80 ÷ 10 000 = **0.0080 m²**.
4. 50 ÷ 0.0025 = **20 000 Pa**.
5. Large area → for the same weight, **lower pressure** → the camel does not sink so far into the sand.
6. The point has a tiny area, so the same force gives a **very high pressure** on the board; the head's large area keeps the pressure on the thumb low.
7. **Depth** and **density** of the liquid.
8. Water pressure **increases with depth**, so the force on the dam is greatest at the bottom.
9. 1000 × 9.8 × 12 = **117 600 Pa** (1.2 × 10⁵ Pa).
10. 18 000 ÷ (900 × 9.8) = **2.0 m** (2.04 m).
11. 5880 ÷ (9.8 × 0.50) = **1200 kg/m³**.
12. 1000 × 9.8 × 20 = 196 000 Pa; total = 196 000 + 100 000 = **296 000 Pa** (3.0 × 10⁵ Pa).
