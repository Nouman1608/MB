Cambridge International AS & A Level Literature in English (9695) is a course in reading literature closely and writing about it with insight. Across its two stages you study drama, poetry and prose, including Shakespeare, writing from before and after 1900, and passages you have not seen before. It suits students who enjoy reading, discussing interpretations and building an argument about a text. The 2024-2026 syllabus is assessed by four written papers, each lasting 120 minutes, each marked out of 50 and each worth 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1 Drama and Poetry, Paper 2 Prose and Unseen, Paper 3 Shakespeare and Drama, and Paper 4 Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose.

## How the course is organised

Each paper has two sections, and the sections tell you which kinds of writing you will study.

**AS Level**

| Paper | Section A | Section B |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 Drama and Poetry | Drama | Poetry |
| Paper 2 Prose and Unseen | Prose | Unseen |

At AS you study set drama, poetry and prose, and you also learn to respond to unseen writing: a passage met for the first time in the examination.

**A Level**

| Paper | Section A | Section B |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 3 Shakespeare and Drama | Shakespeare | Drama |
| Paper 4 Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose | Pre-1900 Poetry and Prose | Post-1900 Poetry and Prose |

At A Level the focus widens to Shakespeare, further drama, and poetry and prose from either side of 1900, which brings questions of period and context more firmly into view.

This page does not list set texts. Your teacher will confirm which texts you are studying for each section.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Drama and Poetry | 120 minutes | 50 | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Prose and Unseen | 120 minutes | 50 | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Shakespeare and Drama | 120 minutes | 50 | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose | 120 minutes | 50 | 25% |

9695 is a staged qualification with two possible outcomes:

- **AS Level.** Papers 1 and 2 can be certificated as a standalone AS Level, in which each paper is worth 50% of the AS grade.
- **A Level.** Candidates can continue to Papers 3 and 4, and the four papers together make up the full A Level, each worth 25%.

All four papers are written examinations, and the papers are equal in length, marks and weighting, so no single paper dominates the final grade.

**Syllabus editions.** This page describes the 2024-2026 syllabus, first assessed in 2024. Cambridge has also published a newer 2027-2028 edition for future cohorts, so if your examinations fall in 2027 or later, check that syllabus as well.

## How the parts fit together

The four papers cover different genres and periods, but they test a shared set of abilities.

- **Close reading.** Every section asks you to look at how a writer uses language, form and structure to create meaning. The unseen section in Paper 2 tests this most directly, because you have nothing to rely on except your reading of the passage in front of you.
- **Knowledge of whole texts.** Set-text sections require you to know your texts well enough to choose relevant moments and quotations quickly and to discuss the work as a whole.
- **Argument.** Each answer is an essay with a line of argument, not a list of observations.
- **Genre awareness.** Drama appears at both stages, in Paper 1 and Paper 3. Watching or reading plays as performance, and thinking about staging, helps across both papers. Poetry and prose likewise appear at AS and again at A Level.

Unseen work at AS is a good preparation for A Level. The habit of analysing an unfamiliar passage carefully makes you a sharper reader of set texts, including the pre-1900 writing in Paper 4, where older language can at first feel unfamiliar.

## Planning your study

Literature rewards early, unhurried reading. A sensible plan is to read each set text in full before detailed study of it begins, then return to it more slowly with your class.

**AS stage**

- Read the Paper 1 and Paper 2 set texts early, ideally before they are taught.
- Study drama and poetry alongside prose, so that you are not working on only one genre for a long stretch.
- Practise unseen analysis regularly throughout, using short passages of poetry and prose from a wide range of writers and periods.

**A Level stage**

- Start the Shakespeare play early, since it often needs the most rereading.
- Pair your Paper 4 texts by period, reading pre-1900 and post-1900 writing side by side to notice how style and concerns change.
- Keep revisiting AS skills, especially unseen-style close reading, to stay sharp.

At both stages, build a notebook for each text: key passages, quotations, themes, characters and different interpretations. Begin writing full essays under time pressure once a text has been studied, and move to complete papers in the final phase. The course's exam-preparation page explains how to approach each paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Literature in English 9695?**
Four. Paper 1 Drama and Poetry and Paper 2 Prose and Unseen make up the AS Level; Paper 3 Shakespeare and Drama and Paper 4 Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose complete the A Level.

**How long is each 9695 paper?**
Each paper lasts 120 minutes and is marked out of 50.

**How are the papers weighted?**
Equally: 25% each towards the full A Level. Taken as a standalone AS Level, Papers 1 and 2 count for half of the AS grade each.

**Is there an unseen element in 9695?**
Yes. Section B of Paper 2 is the Unseen section.

**Is there a newer syllabus?**
Yes. A 2027-2028 edition has been published for future cohorts. This page describes the 2024-2026 syllabus.
