## Paper 1's three sections

| Section | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | Cultural/historical background — reform movements, decline of the Mughal Empire |
| 1.2 | The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47 |
| 1.3 | Nationhood, 1947–99 |

Sections are **interrelated** — questions can draw on more than one section at once.

## 1.1 — key names and causes

- **Shah Waliullah** and **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** — religious reformers reviving Islam in the subcontinent.
- **Decline of the Mughal Empire** — internal factors (Aurangzeb's policies, weak successors) **combined with** external factors (foreign invasions, East India Company expansion). Always explain both together, not as two separate lists.

## 1.2 — the emergence of Pakistan

- **1906** — All-India Muslim League founded.
- Political events, organisations and key figures from 1906 through to **independence in 1947**.

## 1.3 — nationhood

- Political, social and economic development of Pakistan in the decades **after** independence.

## Answering "how important/successful" questions

Key Questions consistently ask **judgement**, not just recall: "How successful was X?" "How
important was Y?" Structure: state what happened → explain its significance/impact → weigh it
against other factors → reach a conclusion.

## Exam traps

- Listing internal and external causes of Mughal decline separately instead of explaining how they combined.
- Treating the three sections as unconnected — cross-section questions are a deliberate, tested feature.
- Naming a reformer or event without stating *why* they mattered (significance, not just identification).
- Using the post-2028 4-topic syllabus structure by mistake — this content is for the 2026 series (Version 1).

## Self-test

1. Name the three sections of Paper 1.
2. Give one internal and one external factor in the Mughal Empire's decline.
3. What kind of question word signals that a judgement, not just recall, is required?
4. Why is building a timeline across all three sections useful?

**Answers:** 1. 1.1 Cultural/Historical Background to the Pakistan Movement; 1.2 The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47; 1.3 Nationhood, 1947–99. 2. Internal: Aurangzeb's policies or weak successors. External: foreign invasions or the East India Company's expansion. 3. Words like "how important" or "how successful". 4. Because exam questions can draw on more than one section, and a timeline helps place events from all three sections in relation to one another, supporting the cross-section connections examiners test.
