## Paper 1's three sections

| Section | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | Cultural/historical background — reform movements, decline of the Mughal Empire |
| 1.2 | The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47 |
| 1.3 | Nationhood, 1947–99 |

Sections are **interrelated** — questions can draw on more than one section at once. Building a
single timeline that runs across all three sections, rather than three separate timelines, is one
of the most effective ways to prepare for this, since it makes the cross-section links visible
before they show up in an exam question.

## 1.1 — key names and causes

- **Shah Waliullah** and **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** — religious reformers reviving Islam in the subcontinent. Shah Waliullah's Persian translation of the Qur'an made scripture more accessible and laid groundwork later reformers built on; Syed Ahmad Barelvi extended this revival into an organised movement.
- **Decline of the Mughal Empire** — internal factors (Aurangzeb's policies, weak successors) **combined with** external factors (foreign invasions, East India Company expansion). Always explain both together, not as two separate lists — internal weakness is what allowed external pressure to succeed, so the strongest answers treat the two as interdependent rather than as two unconnected causes running in parallel.

## 1.2 — the emergence of Pakistan

- **1906** — All-India Muslim League founded.
- Political events, organisations and key figures from 1906 through to **independence in 1947**, including the deepening divide between the Muslim League and the Indian National Congress over the following decades.
- Know the significance of key turning points in this period, not just their dates — examiners consistently reward explaining *why* an event mattered to the growing demand for a separate Muslim state, not just identifying that it happened.

## 1.3 — nationhood

- Political, social and economic development of Pakistan in the decades **after** independence — establishing institutions, managing the economy, and navigating social change.

## Answering "how important/successful" questions

Key Questions consistently ask **judgement**, not just recall: "How successful was X?" "How
important was Y?" Structure: state what happened → explain its significance/impact → weigh it
against other factors → reach a conclusion.

## 1.2 in more depth — the emergence of Pakistan

Beyond the 1906 founding of the All-India Muslim League, Section 1.2 traces the political route to
independence: the deepening divide between the Muslim League and Indian National Congress, the
significance of key events and turning points on the path to Partition, and the roles played by
major political figures in shaping the demand for a separate state. Because 1.2 sits between the
earlier cultural background (1.1) and later nation-building (1.3), strong answers on this section
often need to draw explicitly on 1.1's reform movements to explain *why* a separate Muslim political
identity had already begun forming well before 1906.

## 1.3 in more depth — nationhood after 1947

Section 1.3 covers the challenges facing the new state after independence: establishing political
institutions, economic development, and social change across the following decades. This section
rewards being able to place events in the correct chronological sequence and explain cause and
effect between them, rather than treating each development as an isolated fact — examiners
consistently credit answers that link an economic or political challenge to its specific causes
rooted in the circumstances of independence itself.

## Exam traps

- Listing internal and external causes of Mughal decline separately instead of explaining how they combined.
- Treating the three sections as unconnected — cross-section questions are a deliberate, tested feature.
- Naming a reformer or event without stating *why* they mattered (significance, not just identification).
- Using the post-2028 4-topic syllabus structure by mistake — this content is for the 2026 series (Version 1).
- Answering a "how important" question about a person or event with description alone and never actually stating a judgement on its importance.
- Missing the chronological or causal link between events in different sections when a question spans more than one section.
