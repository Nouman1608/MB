## Questions

**1.** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of each line.

**(a)** y = 5 − 2x **[2]**
**(b)** 4x + 2y = 7 **[3]**

**2.** A tank is filling with water. The graph of volume V litres against time t minutes is a straight line through (0, 12) and (5, 42).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what the gradient and the y-intercept mean for the tank. **[2]**

**3.** Sketch the line 5x + 2y = 10. Mark the points where it crosses each axis. **[4]**

**4.** A line passes through (−2, 9) and (4, −3).

**(a)** Find its equation. **[4]**
**(b)** Does the point (10, −15) lie on the line? Show your working. **[1]**

**5.** Find the equation of the line with gradient 2/3 that passes through (6, −1). **[3]**

**6.** Three lines have these equations.

```
A: y = 4 − 3x
B: 3x + y = 8
C: 6x − 2y = 1
```

**(a)** Which two lines are parallel? Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** A line parallel to 2y = 6x + 1 passes through (−3, 4). Find its equation. **[3]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) The region R satisfies y > 1, x + 2y ≤ 10 and y ≤ 2x.

**(a)** State whether each boundary line is dashed or solid. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the number of points with integer coordinates inside R, including any on a solid boundary. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) A triangle has corners (1, 1), (5, 1) and (5, 5). Its edge from (1, 1) to (5, 1) is dashed. The other two edges are solid. The region R is the inside of the triangle, with points on the solid edges but not on the dashed edge. Write three inequalities that define R. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Line L has equation 2x + 5y = 10. A second line goes through (4, −3) at right angles to L. Find its equation. **[4]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) The points are A(−1, 2), B(3, 4) and C(4, 2).

**(a)** Show that angle ABC is a right angle. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line through C that is parallel to AB. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A circle has equation x² + y² = 65.

**(a)** Show that the point (4, 7) lies on the circle. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the exact radius of the circle. **[1]**
**(c)** Is the point (6, 5) inside, on or outside the circle? Show your working. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the y-coordinates of the points on the circle where x = 1. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) The point P(3, −5) is on the circle x² + y² = 34.

**(a)** Show that P lies on the circle. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the gradient of the radius from the origin to P. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. **[4]**
**(d)** The tangent crosses the x-axis at T. Find the x-coordinate of T. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Write as y = −2x + 5. [1] Gradient **−2**, y-intercept **5**. [1]
**(b)** 2y = −4x + 7 [1], so y = −2x + 7/2. [1] Gradient **−2**, y-intercept **7/2** (3.5). [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers are easier to earn when you rearrange visibly into y = mx + c. A common loss is to read the gradient as 4 straight from 4x + 2y = 7 without dividing by 2.

**2. (a)** Gradient = (42 − 12) ÷ (5 − 0) [1] = 6. [1] The y-intercept is 12, so **V = 6t + 12**. [1]
**(b)** The gradient: **6 litres are added every minute**. [1] The intercept: **there were 12 litres in the tank at the start**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* An interpretation needs the units and the context. "The gradient is 6" alone does not say what changes by 6 each minute.

**3.** When x = 0: 2y = 10, so y = 5, giving the point (0, 5). [1] When y = 0: 5x = 10, so x = 2, giving the point (2, 0). [1] Draw a straight line through (0, 5) and (2, 0), extended past both axes. [1] The line slopes down to the right, which matches the gradient of −5/2 from y = −(5/2)x + 5. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A sketch should show the line crossing the axes at labelled values. A line with no marked intercepts leaves the examiner nothing to check, and a line sloping the wrong way is a clear error.

**4. (a)** m = (−3 − 9) ÷ (4 − (−2)) [1] = −12 ÷ 6 = −2. [1] Substitute (−2, 9): 9 = −2 × (−2) + c, so c = 5. [1] **y = −2x + 5** [1]
**(b)** When x = 10: −2 × 10 + 5 = −15, which equals the y-coordinate, so **yes, (10, −15) lies on the line**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the coordinates in the same order on the top and the bottom. Checking the second given point in your equation is a quick way to catch a sign slip.

**5.** y − (−1) = (2/3)(x − 6) [1], so y + 1 = (2/3)x − 4. [1] **y = (2/3)x − 5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep brackets around a negative y-value when you substitute. Many errors in this kind of question start with y − (−1) written as y − 1.

**6. (a)** A: gradient −3. B: y = 8 − 3x, gradient −3. C: y = 3x − 1/2, gradient 3. [1] **A and B are parallel** because they have the same gradient, −3. [1]
**(b)** 2y = 6x + 1 gives y = 3x + 1/2, so m = 3. [1] Then 4 = 3 × (−3) + c, so c = 13. [1] **y = 3x + 13** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Lines B and C are a trap, because the numbers 3 and 6 look similar. State the gradients you found, since a reason with no gradients named is weak.

**7. (a)** y > 1 is **dashed**. [1] x + 2y ≤ 10 is **solid**. [1] y ≤ 2x is **solid**. [1]
**(b)** Corners of R: (1/2, 1), (8, 1) and (2, 4). Take each whole-number y in turn. [1] For y = 2: x ≥ 1 and x ≤ 6, so 6 points. For y = 3: x ≥ 1.5 and x ≤ 4, so x = 2, 3, 4, which is 3 points. [1] For y = 4: x ≥ 2 and x ≤ 2, so 1 point (2, 4). [1] For y = 1 none are allowed, since y > 1. Total 6 + 3 + 1 = **10 points**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Work along each row of the grid and write down what you find. Counting points from a rough picture is where most slips occur, especially on the boundaries.

**8.** The bottom edge is y = 1 and is dashed, so **y > 1**. [1] The right edge is x = 5 and is solid, so **x ≤ 5**. [1] The diagonal edge is y = x and is solid. Test (4, 2) inside the triangle: 2 ≤ 4, so **y ≤ x**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Test a point that is clearly inside the region against each inequality. Choosing the sign by guessing which side looks right often reverses it.

**9.** 5y = −2x + 10, so y = −(2/5)x + 2 and the gradient of L is −2/5. [1] The perpendicular gradient is 5/2. [1] Substitute (4, −3): −3 = (5/2) × 4 + c, so c = −13. [1] **y = (5/2)x − 13** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange L to y = mx + c before taking the negative reciprocal. Taking the reciprocal of the coefficient of x in 2x + 5y = 10 gives the wrong gradient.

**10. (a)** Gradient of AB = (4 − 2) ÷ (3 − (−1)) = 2/4 = 1/2. [1] Gradient of BC = (2 − 4) ÷ (4 − 3) = −2. [1] (1/2) × (−2) = −1, so AB and BC are perpendicular and **angle ABC = 90°**. [1]
**(b)** The gradient is 1/2. [1] Substitute (4, 2): 2 = 2 + c, so c = 0. **y = x/2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that" question, work out both gradients, write their product and state the conclusion. Saying the lines "look perpendicular" does not prove anything.

**11. (a)** 4² + 7² = 16 + 49 [1] = 65, so the point lies on the circle. [1]
**(b)** r = **√65** [1]
**(c)** 6² + 5² = 36 + 25 = 61, which is less than 65, so (6, 5) is **inside** the circle. **[1] [1]**
**(d)** 1 + y² = 65, so y² = 64. [1] y = **8 or −8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give both y-values in part (d). Taking only the positive root loses the second point. In part (a), show the sum and the total, not only the word "yes".

**12. (a)** 3² + (−5)² = 9 + 25 = 34, so P is on the circle. [1]
**(b)** Gradient of the radius = −5/3. [1]
**(c)** The tangent is perpendicular to the radius, so its gradient is **3/5**. [1] y − (−5) = (3/5)(x − 3) [1], so y + 5 = (3/5)x − 9/5. [1] **y = (3/5)x − 34/5** (or 3x − 5y = 34) [1]
**(d)** At T, y = 0: 0 = (3/5)x − 34/5, so **x = 34/3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Tangent questions reward a clear chain: radius gradient, negative reciprocal, then the line through the point. An exact fraction such as 34/3 is safer than a rounded decimal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing only some of the terms when rearranging to y = mx + c, so the gradient is wrong.
- Mixing up the order of subtraction in the gradient, with one difference reversed.
- Losing a negative sign when substituting a point with a negative coordinate.
- Giving interpretation answers with no units or no context.
- Sketching a line with no marked intercepts or the wrong direction of slope.
- Choosing solid or dashed lines from habit rather than from the sign in the inequality.
- Taking the reciprocal but not changing the sign for a perpendicular gradient, or the reverse.
- Writing x² + y² = r instead of r², or giving only the positive root when a circle is cut at two points.
- Using the gradient of the radius as the gradient of the tangent.
