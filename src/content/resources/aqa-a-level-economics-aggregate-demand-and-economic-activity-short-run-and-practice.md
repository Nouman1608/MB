## Questions

**1.** In Country D national income rises from 2,000 to 2,400 and consumption from 1,560 to 1,880 (bn cu). Calculate the marginal propensity to consume and the multiplier. **[3]**

**2.** Country D's government spends an extra 12 bn cu on road repairs. The MPC is 0.7.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum rise in national income. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons why the actual rise in real output may be smaller. **[3]**

**3.** A new warehouse pays 60 m cu in extra wages in Region R. Residents spend 50% of any extra income on goods made in the region.

**(a)** Calculate the extra income created in the first four rounds. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the final total change in regional income. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the regional multiplier is smaller than a national multiplier for the same households. **[2]**

**4.** Economy P has an MPC of 0.9 and economy Q has an MPC of 0.6. Each receives an injection of 25 bn cu. Calculate the total rise in income in each economy and explain why the results differ. **[5]**

**5.** In Country E actual output is 1,150 and normal capacity output is 1,250 (bn cu). The MPC is 0.8.

**(a)** Calculate the output gap as a percentage of normal capacity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the injection needed to close the gap if prices stay constant. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a larger injection would be needed if the SRAS curve slopes upward. **[2]**

**6.** In Country F, money wage rates rise 6% and productivity rises 4%.

**(a)** Calculate by how much, in percentage terms, unit labour cost changes. **[2]**
**(b)** Raw materials are 25% of firms' costs and their price rises 16%. Calculate the rise in total costs, other costs unchanged. **[2]**
**(c)** Employer payroll tax rises from 8% to 11% of a wage bill of 200. Calculate the percentage rise in labour cost and state what happens to SRAS. **[3]**

**7.** Country F has 15 million workers at normal employment and output per worker of 52,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate normal capacity output in bn cu. **[1]**
**(b)** Productivity rises 2.5% and employment 0.8%. Calculate the new capacity. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how better factor mobility and a stronger enterprise culture can shift LRAS. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why LRAS is drawn vertical at the normal capacity level. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a banking system that lends to firms affects LRAS. **[2]**
**(c)** Actual output is 780 and normal capacity is 800. Calculate the output gap as a percentage. **[2]**

**9.** On a Keynesian AS curve for Country G the price index is 100 at every output up to 800, 108 at 900, 120 at 950 and 140 at 980 (the maximum).

**(a)** AD rises so that equilibrium moves from 800 to 900. Calculate the percentage change in output and in prices. **[2]**
**(b)** AD rises again so that equilibrium moves from 950 to 980. Calculate the same changes to 1 d.p. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the price effect differs between (a) and (b). **[2]**

**10.** Country H has AD: Y = 1800 - 6P and SRAS: Y = 1000 + 2P, with Y in bn cu and P an index. Normal capacity is 1,260. Government spending rises 8 bn cu and the MPC is 0.75.

**(a)** Show that equilibrium is P = 100 and Y = 1,200 before the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the size of the AD shift and the new equilibrium. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the rise in output is much smaller than the AD shift. **[3]**
**(d)** Evaluate whether this policy is an effective way to raise output in Country H. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Change in consumption 320 and change in income 400 [1], so MPC = 320/400 = 0.8 [1]; multiplier = 1/(1 - 0.8) = **5** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the MPC uses changes, not totals. Dividing 1,880 by 2,400 gives the average propensity to consume and does not answer the question.

**2. (a)** Multiplier = 1/(1 - 0.7) = 3.33 [1]; 12 x 3.33 = **40 bn cu** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Spare capacity may be limited, so prices rise and real output rises by less [1]; the MPC may be lower than 0.7, for example if confidence falls or some spending leaks abroad [1]; each point is developed by saying how it reduces the later rounds [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the reason and then follow it through to the size of later rounds. A bare list of "leakages" shows less than one developed chain.

**3. (a)** 60 + 30 + 15 + 7.5 [1] = **112.5 m cu** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 60/(1 - 0.5) = **120 m cu** [1]
**(c)** A region has a larger share of spending on goods from outside it, so less is passed on locally at each round [1], giving a smaller chain of income than in a whole nation, where more of the spending stays inside [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the question says "for the same households", so answers should compare the region and the nation, not just describe leakage.

**4.** Economy P: multiplier 1/0.1 = 10 [1]; Economy Q: 1/0.4 = 2.5 [1]; rises in income **250 bn cu** and **62.5 bn cu** [1]; a higher MPC means more of each round of income is spent again [1], so less is lost at each stage and the chain is longer [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* explain the mechanism round by round. Saying "a bigger MPC means a bigger multiplier" only repeats the question.

**5. (a)** (1,150 - 1,250)/1,250 [1] = **-8%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Multiplier = 5 [1]; 100/5 = **20 bn cu** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** On an upward-sloping SRAS curve some of the AD rise raises prices instead of output [1], so AD must shift further to get real output up by 100 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* give the gap as a percentage of normal capacity, with the sign. A value of 8% with no sign hides that output is below capacity.

**6. (a)** 1.06/1.04 = 1.0192 [1], a rise of **1.92%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 0.25 x 16 [1] = **4%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Labour cost rises from 216 to 222 [1], which is 6/216 = **2.78%** [1]; higher costs shift SRAS to the left [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* ratios of wage to productivity indices are the usual route to unit cost. A cost rise shifts the curve and should not be described as a movement along it.

**7. (a)** 15 m x 52,000 = **780 bn cu** [1]
**(b)** 1.025 x 1.008 = 1.0332 [1]; 780 x 1.0332 = **805.9 bn cu** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Better factor mobility lets resources move to where they are most productive, so more output from the same total of resources [1]; an enterprise culture brings new firms and ideas, raising productivity and capacity [1]; both raise normal capacity, so LRAS shifts right [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* end by linking each determinant to normal capacity. A named factor without that link stays descriptive.

**8. (a)** In the long run output is limited by the quantity and quality of resources [1], and not by the price level, so the curve does not slope [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Banks turn savings into loans for investment [1], which raises the capital stock and normal capacity, shifting LRAS right [1] **[2]**
**(c)** (780 - 800)/800 [1] = **-2.5%** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* keep short-run and long-run effects apart. Credit that raises spending this year is an AD effect, while the capacity effect comes through the extra capital.

**9. (a)** Output: 900/800 = +12.5% [1]; prices: 108/100 = **+8%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Output: 980/950 = +3.2% [1]; prices: 140/120 = **+16.7%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** In (a) the economy still has spare resources, so costs rise slowly [1]; in (b) it is nearly at full capacity, so bottlenecks make extra demand raise prices strongly [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* use the figures in the explanation. Comparing 8% with 16.7% shows you have read the table.

**10. (a)** 1800 - 6 x 100 = 1200 and 1000 + 2 x 100 = 1200 [1], so AD = SRAS at P = 100, Y = **1,200** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Multiplier = 4 [1]; AD shift = 8 x 4 = **32** [1]; new AD Y = 1832 - 6P, so 832 = 8P, P = **104**, Y = **1,208** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** The AD shift of 32 raises demand at each price but firms supply more only if prices rise [1]; here prices rise 4% [1], and that rise cuts demand along the new AD curve, so output rises only 8 [1] **[3]**
**(d)** Output rises, so it is effective in a limited sense [1]; but only a quarter of the shift (8 of 32) appears as output [1]; there is 60 of spare capacity, so a Keynesian flat section would give a bigger rise, though the SRAS used here is upward-sloping [1]; judgement: a larger injection, or supply-side change, would be needed to close the gap, and cost and timing matter [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* for evaluation, reach a stated judgement about this economy using its numbers. Listing general limits of fiscal policy does not earn the same credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using totals rather than changes to find the MPC.
- Writing the multiplier as 1/MPC.
- Quoting a multiplier answer as a change in real output when prices also rise.
- Giving percentage gaps without a sign or without dividing by normal capacity.
- Treating a rise in costs as a movement along SRAS.
- Naming a determinant of LRAS but not linking it to normal capacity.
- Mixing up AD effects of credit with the capacity effect of investment.
- Evaluating in general terms with no use of the data given.
