## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by homeostasis. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain what is meant by saying that homeostasis is an automatic control system. **[1]**

**2.** Name the **three** parts of a control system in the body. **[3]**

**3.** Name the stimulus that each of these organs contains receptors for. **[4]**

**(a)** The eyes.
**(b)** The ears (give **one**).
**(c)** The skin (give **one**).
**(d)** The pancreas.

**4.** Name **two** coordination centres and **two** types of effector. **[4]**

## Section B

**5.** A person walks out of a warm room into cold air. Her skin temperature falls. Describe the control system that responds, from the stimulus to a response. Name the receptor, the coordination centre, the effector and one response, in order. **[5]**

**6.** A person's core temperature was measured six times at 30-minute intervals while resting.

| Reading | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature (°C) | 36.5 | 36.9 | 37.2 | 36.7 | 37.0 | 36.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean core temperature. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the range of the readings. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what the readings show about the control of body temperature. **[2]**

**7.** A runner's core temperature was measured during exercise.

| Time (min) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature (°C) | 37.0 | 37.4 | 38.0 | 38.6 | 38.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of temperature rise in the first 20 minutes, in °C per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total rise in temperature during the 40 minutes. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why the temperature stopped rising between 30 and 40 minutes. **[2]**

**8.** List the **four** conditions inside the body that are controlled. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why it is important that conditions inside the body are kept relatively constant. **[2]**

**10.** A student says: "The pancreas is only a receptor, and the brain is the only coordination centre." Explain why this statement is wrong. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the receptors in the skin with the receptors in the brain that are sensitive to temperature. **[2]**

**12.** Describe how an automatic control system brings about a response to a rise in the temperature of the blood. Include the stimulus, receptor, coordination centre, effector and response. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Keeping conditions inside the body relatively constant [1]
**(b)** It happens without conscious control (the body does it by itself) [1]

**2.** Receptors [1]; coordination centres [1]; effectors [1]

**3. (a)** Light [1]
**(b)** Sound (or changes in position/balance) [1]
**(c)** Any one of: touch; pressure; pain; temperature changes [1]
**(d)** The concentration of glucose in the blood [1]

**4.** Any two coordination centres: brain; spinal cord; pancreas [2]; muscles [1] and glands [1]

**5.** Receptor: temperature receptors in the skin [1]; coordination centre: the brain (thermoregulatory centre) [1]; effector: muscles (or blood vessels in the skin) [1]; a response: shivering (or constriction of blood vessels in the skin) [1]; the chain is given in the correct order from stimulus to response [1]

**6. (a)** 36.5 + 36.9 + 37.2 + 36.7 + 37.0 + 36.9 = 221.2; 221.2 ÷ 6 [1] = 36.87, so **36.9 °C** [1]
**(b)** 37.2 − 36.5 = **0.7 °C** [1]
**(c)** The temperature varies only slightly around the mean [1]; this shows that the body temperature is kept relatively constant by a control system [1]

**7. (a)** rise = 38.0 − 37.0 = 1.0 °C; 1.0 ÷ 20 [1] = **0.05 °C per minute** [1]
**(b)** 38.6 − 37.0 = **1.6 °C** [1]
**(c)** Responses to the high temperature, such as sweating (and more blood flow to the skin), were increasing heat loss [1]; heat loss became equal to the energy transferred by the muscles, so the temperature stopped rising [1]

**8.** Temperature [1]; water content of the body [1]; ion content of the body [1]; blood glucose levels [1]

**9.** Chemical reactions in cells are controlled by enzymes [1]; enzymes (and cells) work best within a narrow range of conditions, so large changes would stop cells working properly [1]

**10.** The pancreas is also a coordination centre, not only a receptor site [1]; the spinal cord is also a coordination centre, as well as the brain [1]

**11.** Skin receptors detect the temperature at the surface of the body (skin temperature) [1]; the receptors in the brain detect the temperature of the blood flowing through the brain (core temperature) [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- stimulus: a rise in the temperature of the blood
- receptors in the brain are sensitive to blood temperature (and skin receptors detect skin temperature)
- the brain is the coordination centre that receives and processes the information
- effectors: sweat glands (and the muscle in the walls of blood vessels in the skin)
- sweat glands release more sweat, which cools the body as it evaporates
- the response reduces the temperature back towards normal; the system is automatic

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that homeostasis keeps conditions "the same" rather than "relatively constant".
- Saying that receptors cause the response.
- Naming muscles or glands as coordination centres.
- Leaving out the pancreas from lists of coordination centres.
- Giving only two or three controlled conditions.
- Describing how the eye or ear works, which is not required.
