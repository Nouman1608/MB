## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing plan | A document setting out marketing objectives, target market, actions, budget and timetable |
| Segmentation | Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar needs or characteristics |
| Target market | The segment or segments a business chooses to serve |
| Positioning | How a business wants customers to see its product compared with rivals |
| Position map | A diagram plotting brands on two axes that buyers use to choose |
| Niche market | A small, specialised segment with distinct needs |
| Mass market | A large market of customers with broadly similar needs |
| USP | A unique selling point/proposition: the feature or benefit that sets a product apart in a way customers value |
| Differentiation | Making a business or product different from rivals in ways customers notice |

## The role of marketing planning

* Turns business objectives into marketing objectives and actions.
* Answers: where are we now, where do we want to be, whom do we serve, how will we get there, how will we know?
* Coordinates departments and allocates a limited budget.
* Sets targets that results can be measured against.
* Limitation: based on assumptions and forecasts, so must be reviewed when the market changes.
* Inputs: SWOT and STEEPLE analyses, market research.

## Segmentation bases

* Demographic: age, income, gender, family, occupation.
* Geographic: region, country, urban or rural, climate.
* Psychographic: lifestyle, values, interests.
* Behavioural: usage, occasion, benefits sought, loyalty.

## Segment tests

Measurable; substantial; accessible; distinct; growing or stable. Value of a segment = number of buyers × average spend.

## Targeting choices

| Choice | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrated (one niche) | Expertise; loyalty; focused budget | Dependence on one segment |
| Differentiated (several segments) | Wider reach; spreads risk | Higher cost of several mixes |
| Undifferentiated (mass) | Economies of scale | Weak fit with any group; price competition |

## Position maps

* Choose axes buyers actually use (confirm with research).
* Look for gaps, crowding and the brand's own position.
* A gap is only an opportunity if demand exists and it can be served profitably.
* Repositioning moves a brand to a new place on the map, for example through new features or a new image.

## Niche versus mass: the trade-off

* Niche: higher margins and loyalty; small base; rivals may enter if it grows.
* Mass: volume and economies of scale; low margins; intense competition.

## USP tests

1. Unique.
2. Valued by the target market.
3. Credible (provable).
4. Defensible (hard to copy).

## Differentiation routes

Product features and design; quality and reliability; service and after-sales support; brand image; distribution and convenience; ethical or sustainable practices; clear price positioning. Each costs money, so it must be matched to what the target segment values.

## Worked mini-example

Ferncastle Pet Foods (invented) compares two segments:

* Owners of working dogs: 20,000 buyers × 300 a year = 6,000,000.
* Owners of small pet dogs: 250,000 buyers × 60 a year = 15,000,000.

The small-dog segment is worth 2.5 times as much, but large brands dominate it. The working-dog segment is smaller, spends five times as much per buyer, and values high-protein recipes, which Ferncastle already makes. Concentrated targeting on working-dog owners uses its strength; the risk is dependence on one segment.

## Exam traps

* Segmentation described with no target chosen.
* The largest segment assumed to be the best.
* Position map axes that buyers do not use.
* Every gap treated as an opportunity.
* A USP that is a vague claim.
* Differentiation described without its cost.
* A plan treated as fixed once written.

## Quick self-test

1. State two roles of a marketing plan.
2. Name the four main bases of segmentation.
3. Give an example of behavioural segmentation.
4. A segment has 8,000 buyers spending 125 each. What is its value?
5. What does "accessible" mean as a test of a segment?
6. Distinguish between a niche market and a mass market.
7. What is a position map used for?
8. Why might a gap on a position map not be an opportunity?
9. Give the four tests of a strong USP.
10. Why is "excellent customer service" often a weak USP?
11. Give one risk of concentrated targeting.
12. Give two ways a business can differentiate itself.

### Answers

1. Any two: sets marketing objectives; coordinates departments; allocates budget; provides targets to measure results against.
2. Demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural.
3. Any one: frequent versus occasional buyers; buyers on a particular occasion; buyers seeking a specific benefit.
4. 8,000 × 125 = 1,000,000.
5. The business can reach the segment through suitable media and distribution channels.
6. A niche market is a small, specialised segment with distinct needs; a mass market is large, with broadly similar needs served by a standard offer.
7. To compare brands on two features buyers care about, and to spot gaps, crowding and the business's position.
8. There may be no demand for that combination, or it cannot be supplied profitably.
9. Unique, valued, credible, defensible.
10. Many rivals claim it, so it is not unique, and it is hard for buyers to verify before purchase.
11. Dependence on one segment: if its demand falls or larger rivals enter, the business has nothing to fall back on.
12. Any two: product features, design, service, brand image, distribution, ethical practices, price positioning.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a USP and differentiation?**
Differentiation is the overall strategy of standing apart from rivals in ways customers notice. A USP is the single, clearest feature or benefit that makes the product unique and valued.

**What should a position map's axes show?**
Two features that customers actually use when choosing between brands, such as price and quality or protection and style. Research should confirm the choice of axes.
