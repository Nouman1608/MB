## 5.1.3(a) Receptors and transducers

- A sensory receptor is specific to one type of stimulus.
- A transducer converts one form of energy into another. Receptors convert the energy of the stimulus into electrical energy, as a change in membrane potential.
- The change in potential across a receptor is the generator potential. It is graded: a bigger stimulus gives a bigger generator potential.
- A nerve impulse starts only if the generator potential reaches threshold.

### Pacinian corpuscle in steps

1. Pressure deforms the lamellae (connective tissue rings separated by viscous gel).
2. The neurone ending membrane stretches and stretch-mediated sodium channels widen.
3. Sodium ions diffuse in, giving a generator potential.
4. At threshold, voltage-gated sodium channels open and an action potential passes along the sensory neurone.

| Receptor | Detects | Converts |
|---|---|---|
| Pacinian corpuscle | Pressure, vibration | Mechanical to electrical |
| Thermoreceptor | Temperature | Thermal to electrical |
| Chemoreceptor | Chemicals | Chemical to electrical |
| Photoreceptor | Light | Light to electrical |

## 5.1.3(b) Neurone types

| | Sensory | Relay | Motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | Receptor to central nervous system | Within central nervous system | Central nervous system to effector |
| Cell body | Side branch off the fibre | In central nervous system | In central nervous system |
| Dendron or dendrites | One long dendron | Many short dendrites | Many dendrites |
| Axon | Short | Short | Long |

### Myelinated and non-myelinated

| | Myelinated | Non-myelinated |
|---|---|---|
| Covering | Schwann cells wrapped around the axon, forming a lipid-rich insulating sheath | Not wrapped in layers of myelin |
| Gaps | Nodes of Ranvier, where channels are concentrated | None |
| Conduction | Saltatory, from node to node | Continuous along the whole membrane |
| Speed | Fast | Slow |

## 5.1.3(c) Impulses

### Resting potential

| Feature | Role |
|---|---|
| Sodium-potassium pump | Uses ATP to move 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in |
| Open K⁺ channels | K⁺ diffuses out down its concentration gradient |
| Few open Na⁺ channels | Na⁺ cannot diffuse back in quickly |
| Result | Inside about −70 mV relative to outside |

### Action potential

| Stage | Channels and ions | Potential |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | Some voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open | Rises from −70 mV |
| Threshold | Many more Na⁺ channels open (positive feedback) | About −55 mV |
| Depolarisation | Na⁺ floods in | Up to about +40 mV |
| Repolarisation | Na⁺ channels close, voltage-gated K⁺ channels open, K⁺ leaves | Falls |
| Hyperpolarisation | K⁺ channels slow to close | Below −70 mV |
| Recovery | Pump and leak channels | Back to −70 mV |

### Method in steps: explaining speed in a myelinated axon

1. Myelin insulates, so no current leaks out through the membrane beneath it.
2. Depolarisation happens only at the nodes of Ranvier.
3. Local circuits carry the current from one node to the next.
4. The action potential appears to jump between nodes, so fewer points depolarise per unit length.
5. Speed = distance / time, so fewer depolarisations means a shorter time.

### Must-know distinctions

- **Generator potential or action potential.** The generator potential is graded and local. The action potential is all-or-nothing and travels.
- **Depolarisation or repolarisation.** Depolarisation is Na⁺ entering. Repolarisation is K⁺ leaving.
- **Refractory period.** Na⁺ channels cannot reopen. It makes the impulse one-way, separates impulses and limits frequency.
- **Frequency.** All action potentials in a neurone are the same size. Stimulus strength is coded by the frequency of impulses.

## 5.1.3(d) Synapses

### Cholinergic synapse in order

| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | Action potential depolarises the presynaptic knob |
| 2 | Calcium channels (voltage-gated) open and Ca²⁺ flows into the knob |
| 3 | Vesicles merge with the membrane and discharge acetylcholine (exocytosis) |
| 4 | Acetylcholine crosses the cleft and attaches to receptors |
| 5 | Sodium channels open, Na⁺ enters and the postsynaptic membrane depolarises |
| 6 | Acetylcholinesterase breaks down acetylcholine into choline and ethanoic acid |
| 7 | Products re-enter the knob and are resynthesised using ATP from mitochondria |

### Roles of synapses

- One-way transmission, because only the presynaptic side releases the transmitter and only the postsynaptic side holds receptors.
- Spatial summation: several presynaptic neurones act together.
- Temporal summation: repeated impulses from one neurone add up.
- Excitatory synapse: depolarises the postsynaptic membrane. Inhibitory synapse: hyperpolarises it (for example by opening chloride ion channels), so threshold is harder to reach.
- Summation filters out weak signals and combines many inputs.

### Worked reminders

```
speed = distance / time        0.45 m in 6.0 ms: 0.45 / 0.0060 = 75 m per s
frequency = impulses / time    45 impulses in 1.5 s: 45 / 1.5 = 30 per s
% change = change / original × 100
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transducer | A receptor that converts stimulus energy into electrical energy |
| Generator potential | Graded depolarisation of a receptor membrane |
| Threshold | The membrane potential (about −55 mV) at which positive feedback begins |
| Depolarisation | The inside of the membrane becomes less negative or positive |
| Repolarisation | The inside returns towards the resting value |
| Hyperpolarisation | The inside becomes more negative than the resting potential |
| Node of Ranvier | Gap in the myelin where the axon membrane is exposed |
| Saltatory conduction | Action potentials appearing at successive nodes only |
| Synaptic cleft | The gap between presynaptic and postsynaptic membranes |
| Exocytosis | Release of vesicle contents by fusion of the vesicle with the cell surface membrane |

## Summation and threshold in numbers

A postsynaptic neurone starts at −70 mV and fires at −55 mV, so it needs a net 15 mV of depolarisation.

```
excitatory input of 3 mV, all arriving together:
  4 inputs give 12 mV  (−58 mV, no impulse)
  5 inputs give 15 mV  (−55 mV, threshold reached)
add one inhibitory input of −3 mV at the same time:
  5 excitatory inputs now give 12 mV net, so a sixth is needed
```

Spatial summation uses many presynaptic neurones at once. Temporal summation uses one neurone firing again before the first effect has faded. Both are needed in the explanation if a question gives you a table of inputs.

## Factors that change conduction speed

| Factor | Effect on speed | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Myelination | Faster | Depolarisation only at nodes |
| Larger axon diameter | Faster | Less resistance to local currents |
| Higher temperature (within limits) | Faster | Ions diffuse faster and more ATP is available for the pump |

Synaptic delay is separate. Each synapse adds a delay because diffusion of the transmitter takes time. Pathways with fewer synapses respond faster.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the type of energy a Pacinian corpuscle converts, and the type it produces.
2. What does the sodium-potassium pump move, and in what ratio?
3. An impulse crosses 0.08 m of non-myelinated axon in 40 ms. Find the speed.
4. Each excitatory impulse depolarises a postsynaptic membrane by 4 mV. The membrane starts at −70 mV and threshold is −55 mV. How many simultaneous impulses are needed?
5. State the role of positive feedback in generating an action potential.
6. Why can an action potential not travel backwards?
7. A neurone fires 45 impulses in 1.5 s. What is the frequency, and what does the maximum possible frequency depend on?
8. Frequency rises from 50 to 80 impulses per second. Find the percentage increase.
9. Name the enzyme in the cleft of a cholinergic synapse and say what it does.
10. How does an inhibitory synapse make an action potential less likely?

### Answers

1. Mechanical energy (pressure) to electrical energy (a generator potential).
2. Three sodium ions out and two potassium ions in, using ATP.
3. 0.08 / 0.040 = 2.0 m per s.
4. 15 / 4 = 3.75, so 4 impulses (−70 + 16 = −54 mV; three give −58 mV).
5. Depolarisation opens more voltage-gated sodium channels, which causes further depolarisation, so the potential rises rapidly to its peak.
6. The region just behind is refractory because sodium channels cannot reopen yet.
7. 45 / 1.5 = 30 per s. The maximum depends on the length of the refractory period.
8. (80 − 50) / 50 × 100 = 60%.
9. Acetylcholinesterase. It hydrolyses acetylcholine into choline and ethanoic acid, so the receptors are cleared.
10. It hyperpolarises the postsynaptic membrane, for example by opening chloride channels, so more depolarisation is needed to reach threshold.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the nerve impulse jumps" without saying it is the depolarisation, at the nodes of Ranvier.
- Leaving out the units or the millisecond conversion in speed calculations.
- Saying sodium ions "pump" into the axon during depolarisation. They diffuse through channels.
- Naming only one of the two ions and one of the two mechanisms in the resting potential.
- Missing the word threshold when explaining all-or-nothing.
- Describing a bigger action potential instead of a higher frequency.
- Writing "neurotransmitter" where the question asks for the ion that triggers release: calcium.
- Forgetting that acetylcholinesterase is needed to end the response.
- Confusing the pump with channels, or confusing hyperpolarisation with repolarisation.
