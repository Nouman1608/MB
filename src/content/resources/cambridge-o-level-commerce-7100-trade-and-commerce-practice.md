## Section A

**1.** Define the term *trade*. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** differences between trade and commerce. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the **two** branches of home trade and state what each involves. **[4]**

**4.** A coffee grower in Kenya sells coffee beans to a roasting company in the United Kingdom. State whether this is an export or an import for each country, and explain which way the payment flows. **[3]**

**5.** Define *logistics*. **[2]**

**6.** State **three** tasks a freight forwarder may carry out for an exporter. **[3]**

**7.** Match each case to the type of commerce it shows: (a) a customer buys vegetables at a street market; (b) a customer orders a book on a retailer's website using a desktop computer; (c) a customer taps "buy" inside a supermarket's smartphone app; (d) a customer buys earrings by replying to a post on a social media channel. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain how **three** aids to trade help a small furniture maker to sell tables to customers in other towns. **[6]**

**9.** Aziz runs a business importing kitchen equipment. He is considering using a freight forwarder instead of arranging each shipment himself. Explain **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage to Aziz of using a freight forwarder. **[6]**

**10.** Using an example, explain how production, trade and commerce are interdependent. **[6]**

**11.** Noor sells hand-made soap. At present she sells only from a stall at a weekly market. Discuss whether she should add scommerce as a way of selling. **[8]**

**12.** "Warehousing is both an aid to trade and an element of logistics." Explain this statement. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The buying and selling of goods and services [1].

**2.** Trade is only the buying and selling of goods and services, whereas commerce includes trade **and** the aids to trade [1]; services such as banking or insurance are part of commerce but are not trade [1].

**3.** **Wholesale trade** [1]: buying goods in large quantities from producers and selling them in smaller quantities, usually to retailers [1]. **Retail trade** [1]: selling goods in small quantities to the final consumer [1].

**4.** It is an **export for Kenya** [1] and an **import for the United Kingdom** [1]. Payment flows from the United Kingdom roasting company to the Kenyan grower, so money comes into Kenya and leaves the United Kingdom [1].

**5.** The planning and management of the movement **and** storage of goods [1] through a supply chain, so that goods arrive at the right place and time [1].

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: booking space with shipping lines, airlines or hauliers; combining small consignments into larger loads; preparing shipping documents; arranging customs clearance; advising on the best route or method of transport [1] [1] [1].

**7.** (a) Traditional commerce [1]; (b) ecommerce [1]; (c) mcommerce [1]; (d) scommerce [1].

**8.** Any three aids; for each, 1 mark for naming the aid and 1 for an explanation applied to the furniture maker. **Transport** carries heavy tables from the workshop to customers in other towns, which the maker could not do by hand; **advertising**, for example online adverts or leaflets, tells customers in other towns that the tables exist; **communications** let customers send orders and the maker confirm delivery dates by telephone or email; **banking** gives customers a safe way to pay at a distance, such as a bank transfer; **insurance** covers the tables against damage in transit; **warehousing** lets finished tables be stored until orders arrive [1] [1] [1] [1] [1] [1].

**9.** **Advantages:** the forwarder has **expertise** in documents and customs rules [1], so Aziz's shipments are less likely to be delayed by mistakes [1]; the forwarder can **combine loads** from several clients [1], which may lower Aziz's transport cost per item [1]. **Disadvantage:** Aziz must pay the forwarder's **fee** [1], which reduces his profit on each shipment, and he has less direct control over the journey [1].

**10.** Example chosen, for instance wheat and bread [1]. **Production needs trade**: a wheat farmer grows far more than the family can use, so it must be sold to millers [1]. **Trade needs production**: millers and bakers have nothing to buy or sell without the harvest [1]. **Trade needs aids to trade**: the wheat must be transported, stored and paid for through a bank [1]. **Commerce supports production**: sales revenue from trade pays the farmer to plant next season [1]. A break at one point (for example a poor harvest or a transport strike) affects all the others [1].

**11.** Knowledge of scommerce: buying and selling via social media channels [1]. **For:** she can reach many more customers than visit the market [1]; she can sell on days when the market is closed [1]; posting photos and videos of the soap is cheap promotion [1]. **Against:** she must pack and send orders, adding delivery and packaging costs [1]; she needs time and skill to manage posts, messages and payments [1]; customers cannot smell or test the soap before buying [1]. **Judgement**, for example: she should add scommerce while keeping the stall, because the extra sales are likely to outweigh delivery costs and the stall still serves customers who want to test the product [1].

**12.** As an **aid to trade**, warehousing lets goods produced at one time be stored and sold when they are wanted [1], for example seasonal crops sold throughout the year [1]. As an **element of logistics**, warehousing is one of the stages managed in the supply chain [1], where goods are stored, sorted, packed and dispatched alongside transport and freight forwarding [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: writing "commerce is bigger" without saying what it adds (the aids to trade).
- Question 4: naming the export correctly but reversing the flow of money.
- Question 7: calling the website order "mcommerce" when the device is a desktop computer.
- Question 8: naming three aids without applying them to tables and customers in other towns.
- Question 11: giving only advantages, or ending without a judgement linked to Noor's soap business.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does Question 11 give a mark for knowledge?** A discuss answer is stronger when it shows the meaning of the key term before applying it, and this mark scheme credits that.

**Can I use any product in Question 10?** Yes. Any example works if each link (production, trade and aids to trade) is shown for that product.
