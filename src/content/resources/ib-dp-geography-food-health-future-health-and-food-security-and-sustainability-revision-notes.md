Condensed recall for **6.4 Future health and food security and sustainability** (IB DP Geography, Option F: Food and health). Fuller explanation is in the [Food and health study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-food-health-study-guide/).

## Pressures ahead

| Food | Health |
|---|---|
| Population growth | Emerging and re-emerging disease |
| Rising demand for animal products | Antimicrobial resistance |
| Climate change affecting yields and water | Ageing populations |
| Soil degradation | Burden of non-communicable disease |
| Biofuel competition for land | |
| Conflict and price volatility | |

## Sustainable options

- Sustainable intensification
- Agroecology
- Reducing food waste
- Changing diets
- Protecting soils and water
- Improving crop resilience
- Strengthening local and regional food systems

## Resilience and governance

- **Resilience:** can systems absorb shocks such as drought or a pandemic?
- **Governance:** international agreements, trade rules and investment priorities shape outcomes.

## Evaluating futures

1. State the pressure and the response.
2. Weigh effectiveness, cost, equity and sustainability.
3. Recognise uncertainty: solutions differ by context.
4. Conclude with a justified judgement.

## Link to earlier subtopics

Use measures from 6.1 to judge progress, diffusion ideas from 6.2 to explain risk, and stakeholders from 6.3 to say who must act.

## Exam traps

- Offering a single solution as if it fits every place.
- Ignoring uncertainty in climate and disease projections.
- Treating food and health futures separately.
- Quoting projected figures from memory.

## Self-test

1. Give three pressures on future food security.
2. Name two health challenges.
3. List three sustainable food options.
4. What does resilience mean for a food system?
5. Why do solutions depend on context?

**Answers:** 1. For example population growth, climate change, soil degradation. 2. For example antimicrobial resistance and non-communicable disease. 3. For example reducing food waste, agroecology, changing diets. 4. Its ability to absorb shocks such as drought or a pandemic. 5. Places differ in resources, climate, governance and culture.
