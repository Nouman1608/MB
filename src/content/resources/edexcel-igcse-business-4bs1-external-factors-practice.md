Use the [external factors study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors/) and the [external factors revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Show your workings.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define an external factor. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one example of a technological factor. **[1]**

**2.** State the external factor shown in each statement.
**(a)** "A large share of the town's population is now over 65." **[1]**
**(b)** "The new government has promised to raise taxes on imported cars." **[1]**
**(c)** "Customers have complained about the fumes from our delivery vans." **[1]**

**3.** A shop can fit low-energy lighting for 7,200. It will save 1,600 a year on electricity. Calculate how long it takes to recover the cost. **[2]**

**4.** A drinks maker's sales of sugary drinks fell from 250,000 bottles to 205,000 as customers cut down on sugar. Calculate the percentage change in sales. **[2]**

**5.** Sunward Holidays sells beach holidays mainly to young families. Outline one way an ageing population could affect Sunward Holidays. **[2]**

**6.** Give two decisions a business could make in response to environmental concerns. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one way technological change can reduce a business's costs. **[3]**

**8.** Wexford Bottling can buy a machine for 90,000 that would replace three workers, each paid 21,000 a year. The machine costs 9,000 a year to maintain.
**(a)** Calculate the yearly saving. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how long it would take to recover the cost of the machine, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse one drawback to Wexford Bottling of buying the machine. **[4]**

**9.** Solano Mining plc plans to invest 200 million in a new mine in Country Z. An election will be held there in three months, and the main opposition party has promised higher taxes on mining profits. Analyse how this political factor may affect Solano Mining's decision. **[6]**

**10.** Greenway Coffee runs five cafés and sells 300,000 takeaway drinks a year. Customers have asked it to replace plastic-lined cups, which cost 0.05 each, with compostable cups at 0.12 each.
**(a)** Calculate the extra yearly cost of switching. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Greenway Coffee should switch to compostable cups. **[8]**

**11.** Pageturn Books is a small independent bookshop. Sales are falling as more people buy books online, but surveys show local people value places to meet and relax. The owner can afford one of two projects. Option A: a website to sell books online. Option B: a small café area with author events. Justify which option Pageturn Books should choose. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate which external factor is likely to be most significant for a high-street travel agent. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Something outside the business's control that affects its decisions [1].
**(b)** Any one: new machinery or automation, online selling, new software, video calling [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Social [1]. **(b)** Political [1]. **(c)** Environmental [1]. **[3]**

**3.** 7,200 ÷ 1,600 [1] = **4.5 years** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** (205,000 − 250,000) ÷ 250,000 × 100 [1] = **−18%** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Fewer of its customers may be young families, so demand for beach holidays could fall [1]; Sunward could respond by offering holidays designed for older travellers, such as quieter resorts or tours [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two of: switch to recyclable or less packaging [1], invest in energy saving such as solar panels [1], use lower-emission vehicles, choose suppliers with better environmental records, reduce waste. **[2]**

**7.** New technology such as automated machinery can do tasks faster than people [1]. This means fewer workers are needed for the same output [1], so wage costs per unit fall (or: fewer mistakes are made, so waste falls) [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Wages saved 3 × 21,000 = 63,000 [1]; yearly saving 63,000 − 9,000 = **54,000** [1].
**(b)** 90,000 ÷ 54,000 [1] = **1.7 years** [1].
**(c)** Three workers lose their jobs [1], so remaining staff may fear they are next, and their morale and productivity may fall [1]. If the machine breaks down, production stops until it is repaired, because there are no longer people to do the work [1], which could delay orders and damage relationships with customers [1]. **[8]**

**9.** If the opposition wins, higher taxes on mining profits would reduce Solano's profit from the mine [1], so the 200 million investment would take longer to pay back [1]. Because the result is uncertain [1], Solano may delay its decision until after the election, when the tax policy is known [1]. Delay has a cost: a rival might secure the site, or the project could be held up by a year [1]. Solano might instead invest a smaller amount first, or compare mines in more stable countries [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Extra cost per cup 0.12 − 0.05 = 0.07 [1]; 300,000 × 0.07 = **21,000** [1].
**(b)** For switching: customers have asked for the change, so it may increase loyalty [1] and attract customers who care about the environment [1]. It could improve Greenway's reputation compared with rivals [1]. Against: 21,000 a year is a significant cost for a business with five cafés [1], which reduces profit unless prices rise [1]. A price rise of 0.07 a drink might lose price-sensitive customers [1]. Judgement: for example, switch and add a small charge or a discount for customers who bring their own cup, because customers asked for the change and the cost per drink is small [1]; this protects both reputation and profit [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Option B is the better choice [1]. Large online sellers already dominate internet book sales [1], so a small shop's website would struggle to compete on price or range [1]. The surveys show local people value places to meet and relax (a social factor) [1], so a café and author events give them a reason to visit that online sellers cannot offer [1]. Café sales add a new source of revenue, and visitors are likely to buy books while they are there [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Technological: customers can book flights and hotels online themselves [1], so fewer visit the agent and its sales fall [1]. Social: some customers, often older ones, still prefer advice in person for complex trips [1], which gives the agent a market to target [1]. Political: changes in travel rules or unrest in a destination can stop bookings suddenly [1]. Environmental: concern about flying could reduce demand for some holidays over time [1]. Judgement: technology is most significant, because it has already moved many bookings online and affects the agent's core service now [1]; the agent should focus on customers who value advice, which turns the social factor into an opportunity [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a factor without the decision it leads to.
- Calling internal problems external factors.
- Forgetting non-financial drawbacks of new technology, such as staff morale.
- Writing about political factors as if they meant only new laws.
- Ending "evaluate" or "justify" answers without a decision.
