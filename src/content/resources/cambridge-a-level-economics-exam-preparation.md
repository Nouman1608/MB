Cambridge A-Level Economics 9708 has four papers: Paper 1 AS Level Multiple Choice (1 hour, 30 marks, 17% of the A Level), Paper 2 AS Level Data Response and Essays (2 hours, 60 marks, 33%), Paper 3 A Level Multiple Choice (1 hour 15 minutes, 30 marks, 17%) and Paper 4 A Level Data Response and Essays (2 hours, 60 marks, 33%). The weightings are percentages of the full A Level and sum to 100. Revise with our pages on [basic economic ideas and resource allocation](/resources/a-level-economics-basic-economic-ideas-and-resource-allocation/), [the price system and the microeconomy](/resources/a-level-economics-price-system-and-the-microeconomy/) and [government microeconomic intervention](/resources/a-level-economics-government-microeconomic-intervention/).

## AS Level and A Level routes

Papers 1 and 2 alone certificate as a standalone AS Level, weighted 33% and 67% of the AS Level in that route. Alternatively a candidate continues to Papers 3 and 4 for the full A Level. Confirm which route your centre has entered you for.

## Papers 1 and 3: the multiple-choice papers

Paper 1 is 60 minutes and 30 marks (17%), and Paper 3 is 75 minutes and 30 marks (17%). **How to spend the time:** Paper 1 gives 60 / 30 = 2 minutes per question, and Paper 3 gives 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per question. Work through the paper once answering what you are sure of, mark the doubtful items, and return to them. Eliminate wrong options deliberately; for calculation items, work the arithmetic rather than guessing.

## Papers 2 and 4: data response and essays

Each of these is 120 minutes and 60 marks (33%). Paper 2 is the AS Level paper, and Paper 4 is the A Level paper. **How to spend the time:** 120 / 60 = 2 minutes per mark. Because each paper combines data response with essays, decide your plan before you begin: allocate time to each question in proportion to its marks, and leave a few minutes to check that every part has been attempted. For data-response parts, use the figures and extract given; for essays, aim for structured argument with evaluation.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Paper 1: 60 / 30 = 2.0 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 120 / 60 = 2.0 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 120 / 60 = 2.0 minutes per mark.
- Weightings: 17 + 33 + 17 + 33 = 100. Total marks: 30 + 60 + 30 + 60 = 180.

## Command words and question styles

Economics answers build from knowledge to judgement. "Define" and "State" need a precise term. "Calculate" needs working shown. "Explain" needs a cause and effect, ideally supported by a diagram described or drawn. "Analyse" develops a chain of reasoning. "Assess", "Discuss" and "Evaluate" require arguments on both sides and a reasoned conclusion that considers factors such as time period, size of effect and the assumptions behind the model.

## Worked practice scenario: price elasticity and revenue

**Question:** A café raises the price of a lunch set from 20 dollars to 22 dollars, and weekly sales fall from 500 to 450. (a) Calculate the price elasticity of demand. (b) Calculate the change in total revenue. (c) Comment on the pricing decision.

**Model answer:**

```
(a) % change in price    = (22 - 20) / 20 x 100 = +10%
    % change in quantity = (450 - 500) / 500 x 100 = -10%
    PED = -10 / +10 = -1.0 (unit elastic, ignoring sign: 1.0)

(b) Revenue before = 20 x 500 = 10,000
    Revenue after  = 22 x 450 = 9,900
    Change = 9,900 - 10,000 = -100 dollars (a fall of 1%)
```

(c) Because demand is unit elastic, the price rise leaves revenue almost unchanged, and revenue falls slightly. The café has no revenue reason to raise the price, but if variable costs per meal are high, selling fewer meals at a higher price could still raise profit. The figures also come from a single week, so a firmer decision would test whether the response is stable over a longer period and how rival cafés price.

## Planning your final weeks

Treat the two multiple-choice papers and the two data-response papers as different skills. For multiple choice, do short daily sets of questions and keep a log of the concepts you miss, then revisit them. For data response and essays, build a bank of real-world examples for each major topic, and practise drawing the key diagrams from memory: supply and demand shifts, market failure, aggregate demand and supply, and trade effects. Write one essay a week under timed conditions, and always finish with an evaluative conclusion that considers time periods and assumptions. Rehearse formulae such as elasticity and percentage change so that they are automatic.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know whether you are entered for the AS route or the full A Level.
- Practise a full timed multiple-choice paper.
- Keep a formula sheet: elasticities, multipliers, percentage changes.
- Draw clear, labelled diagrams and refer to them in the text.
- Use data-response extracts as evidence in every answer.
- Plan essays with arguments on both sides.
- Conclude with a judgement that answers the question set.
- Check units and signs in calculations.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A-Level Economics 9708?**
Four: AS Level Multiple Choice, AS Level Data Response and Essays, A Level Multiple Choice, and A Level Data Response and Essays.

**How long is each paper?**
Paper 1 is 1 hour, Paper 2 is 2 hours, Paper 3 is 1 hour 15 minutes and Paper 4 is 2 hours.

**What is the weighting of each paper?**
17%, 33%, 17% and 33% of the full A Level respectively.

**Can I take only the AS Level?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 can be certificated on their own, weighted 33% and 67% of the AS Level.

**How many marks are there in total?**
180: 30 + 60 + 30 + 60.
