## 1905 compared with February 1917

| | 1905 | February 1917 |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Bloody Sunday; war with Japan | Bread shortages and strikes in wartime Petrograd |
| Army | Mostly loyal; crushed revolts | Petrograd garrison mutinied |
| Opposition | Split by the October Manifesto | Duma and Soviet both turned against the Tsar |
| Outcome | Nicholas survived | Nicholas abdicated |

## Reds v Whites

| Reds | Whites |
|---|---|
| Central area, industry, railways | Scattered on the edges of Russia |
| United leadership (Lenin, Trotsky) | Rival generals with different aims |
| Disciplined Red Army; ex-tsarist officers watched by commissars | Brutal, feared by peasants as landlords' allies |
| War Communism, Cheka, propaganda | Limited, inconsistent foreign help |

## War Communism v NEP

| | War Communism | NEP |
|---|---|---|
| Grain | Requisitioned by force | Tax in kind; surplus sold |
| Trade | Private trade banned | Small private trade allowed |
| Industry | All nationalised | State kept heavy industry; small firms private |
| Effects | Army supplied; famine and revolts | Recovery; ideological opposition from the Left |

## Worked example: describe two features

*Describe two features of the Kornilov Revolt (1917).* (6 marks)

1. General Kornilov, the army commander, sent troops towards Petrograd in August 1917, apparently to
   restore order and crush the Soviet.
2. Kerensky armed the Bolshevik Red Guards to defend the city; railway workers and agitators stopped
   Kornilov's troops, and the episode boosted Bolshevik support.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing calendars.** Stick to one system, and know why "October" was November in the West.
- **Merging the two 1917 revolutions.** February was largely spontaneous; October was planned.
- **Assuming the Constituent Assembly was Bolshevik.** The SRs won the most seats.
- **Ignoring provenance.** Soviet sources after 1917 glorify Lenin; White or émigré sources do the
  opposite — use this in cross-referencing.
- **Treating the NEP as a success for everyone.** It revived the economy but divided the party.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was Rasputin's significance?**
He symbolised the corruption of the court and damaged the
monarchy's reputation while Nicholas was at the front.

**Why did the Provisional Government fail?**
It continued the war, delayed land reform and elections,
shared power with the Soviet and lost the army's support after Kornilov.

**What did Brest-Litovsk cost Russia?**
Ukraine, Poland, Finland and the Baltic lands, with much of its
population, farmland and industry.
