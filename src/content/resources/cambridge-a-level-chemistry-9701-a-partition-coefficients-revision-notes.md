**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level**
subtopic 25.2: state what Kpc means; calculate and use Kpc where the solute is in the same
physical state in both solvents; explain how polarities of solute and solvents affect Kpc.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partition coefficient, Kpc | ratio of the concentrations of a solute in two immiscible solvents at equilibrium, at a stated temperature |
| Immiscible | do not mix; form two layers (e.g. hexane and water) |
| Solvent extraction | removing a solute from one solvent by shaking with a second, immiscible solvent |
| Same physical state | the solute exists as the same species in both layers (no association or dissociation) |
| Polarity | uneven charge distribution in a molecule; determines which intermolecular forces it forms |

## The expression

    X(aq) ⇌ X(org)          Kpc(org/water) = [X(org)] ÷ [X(aq)]

- No units (ratio of like quantities).
- State the order: Kpc(org/water) = 1 ÷ Kpc(water/org).
- Concentrations may be in mol dm⁻³ or g cm⁻³, as long as both use the same unit.
- Depends on temperature, like any equilibrium constant.
- Kpc greater than 1: solute favours the solvent on top of the ratio.

## Finding Kpc from data

**From masses and volumes** *(invented data)*: at equilibrium, 0.84 g of solute is in 20 cm³
of organic layer and 0.12 g in 80 cm³ of water.

    [org] = 0.84 ÷ 20 = 0.042 g cm⁻³ ;  [aq] = 0.12 ÷ 80 = 0.0015 g cm⁻³
    Kpc(org/water) = 0.042 ÷ 0.0015 = 28

**From titrations:** titrate a measured sample of each layer; convert titre to moles of solute,
divide by the sample volume, then take the ratio. For iodine, remember
I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ (1 : 2).

## Extraction calculations

**One extraction** *(equal volumes, Kpc(org/water) = 3.0)*: 1.50 g in 50.0 cm³ water, shaken
with 50.0 cm³ organic solvent.

    3.0 = x ÷ (1.50 − x)  →  x = 1.125 g extracted (75%)

**Two portions of 25.0 cm³** *(same total solvent)*:

    3.0 = (x ÷ 25.0) ÷ ((m − x) ÷ 50.0)  →  x = 0.60m each time
    first: 0.90 g ; second: 0.60 × 0.60 = 0.36 g ; total 1.26 g (84%)

Several small portions extract more than one large portion of the same total volume.

**Shortcut (derived from the Kpc expression):** for one extraction, the fraction of solute left
in water is

    fraction left = V(aq) ÷ (V(aq) + Kpc × V(org))

and after n identical extractions it is that fraction raised to the power n. Show the full
Kpc working in an exam; use the shortcut to check.

## Polarity and Kpc

| Solute type | Example | Main forces | Kpc(hexane/water) |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-polar | I₂, alkanes | instantaneous dipole–induced dipole | large |
| weakly polar, long chain | hexan-1-ol | mainly instantaneous dipole–induced dipole, some hydrogen bonding | greater than 1 |
| polar, small, hydrogen bonding | ethanol, ammonia | hydrogen bonds with water | small |
| ionic | NaCl | ion–dipole with water | almost zero |

- The closer the solute's polarity to a solvent's, the more of it is found in that solvent.
- A more polar organic solvent raises Kpc(organic/water) for a polar solute.
- Within a homologous series, longer chains raise Kpc(organic/water).

## Why "same physical state" matters

If a solute associates (for example, carboxylic acids forming hydrogen-bonded dimers in a
non-polar solvent) or ionises in one layer, the species in the two layers differ and the simple
concentration ratio is not constant. 9701 calculations use only systems where the solute is
the same species in both solvents.

## Exam traps

- Definition needs **immiscible solvents**, **concentration ratio**, **equilibrium** and
  constant temperature.
- No units for Kpc.
- Always write the solvent order.
- Include **both volumes** when they differ.
- In repeated extractions, use the mass **remaining** as the starting mass each time.
- Polarity answers must name forces (hydrogen bonding, instantaneous dipole–induced dipole,
  ion–dipole), not just "like dissolves like".
- Do not use moles and masses mixed in the same ratio.

## Self-test

1. State what is meant by a partition coefficient.
2. Why does Kpc have no units?
3. Kpc(water/ether) = 0.25 for a solute. What is Kpc(ether/water)?
4. At equilibrium a solute has concentration 0.150 mol dm⁻³ in an organic layer and
   0.0120 mol dm⁻³ in water. Calculate Kpc(org/water).
5. 0.60 g of a solute in 40 cm³ of water is shaken with 10 cm³ of hexane.
   Kpc(hexane/water) = 20. Calculate the mass extracted.
6. Use the shortcut to find the fraction left in 100 cm³ of water after three extractions with
   10 cm³ portions when Kpc(org/water) = 5.0.
7. What volume of organic solvent extracts 80% of a solute from 50 cm³ of water in one step if
   Kpc(org/water) = 8.0?
8. Why do two small portions extract more than one large one?
9. Predict whether Kpc(hexane/water) for propan-1-ol is greater or smaller than for
   pentan-1-ol. Explain.
10. Why is ammonia's Kpc(hexane/water) very small?
11. State one condition for a simple Kpc to apply.
12. How does changing from hexane to a more polar organic solvent affect Kpc(organic/water) for
    a polar solute?

**Answers**

1. The ratio of the concentrations of a solute in two immiscible solvents at equilibrium, at a
   stated temperature.
2. It is a ratio of two concentrations in the same units, so the units cancel.
3. **4.0**.
4. 0.150 ÷ 0.0120 = **12.5**.
5. Fraction left = 40 ÷ (40 + 20 × 10) = 1/6; mass extracted = 0.60 × 5/6 = **0.50 g**.
6. Each time 100 ÷ (100 + 50) = 2/3 remains; (2/3)³ = **0.30** (30% left, 70% extracted).
7. 8.0 = (0.80 ÷ V) ÷ (0.20 ÷ 50), so V = **25 cm³**.
8. Each fresh portion contains no solute, so it removes a fixed fraction of what is left; the
   fractions left multiply, leaving less overall.
9. **Smaller** for propan-1-ol: its shorter hydrocarbon chain makes it relatively more polar,
   and its –OH group hydrogen bonds with water, so more stays in water.
10. Ammonia forms strong hydrogen bonds with water but only weak forces with hexane.
11. The solute must be in the same physical state (same species) in both solvents; or the
    system must be at equilibrium at constant temperature.
12. Kpc(organic/water) increases.
