## Section A

**1.** Which equation represents the second ionisation energy of magnesium?

A: Mg(g) → Mg²⁺(g) + 2e⁻

B: Mg⁺(g) → Mg²⁺(g) + e⁻

C: Mg(s) → Mg²⁺(g) + 2e⁻

D: Mg⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + e⁻

**[1]**

**2.** Write the equation, with state symbols, for the first ionisation energy of chlorine. **[1]**

**3.** State and explain the trend in first ionisation energy down Group 2 (Be 900, Mg 738, Ca 590, Sr 549). **[3]**

**4.** State the four factors that influence the size of an ionisation energy. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the successive ionisation energies of an element always increase. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. One electron is removed from a gaseous 1+ ion. A is the sum of the first two; C and D have wrong states. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** Cl(g) → Cl⁺(g) + e⁻ [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**3.** Ionisation energy decreases down the group [1]. Each element has an extra shell, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus [1] and more shielded by inner shells, which outweighs the increase in nuclear charge [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**4.** Nuclear charge; atomic or ionic radius (distance of the electron from the nucleus); shielding by inner shells and sub-shells; spin-pair repulsion. Two marks for all four; one mark for two or three [2]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**5.** Each electron is removed from an ion with a greater positive charge [1], so the remaining electrons are held more strongly by the nucleus and the ion is smaller [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**6. (a)** Nuclear charge increases from 3 to 10 [1]. Electrons are removed from the same (second) shell with the same inner shielding (1s²) [1], so the attraction for the outer electron increases and the radius decreases [1].

**(b)** Boron's electron is removed from 2p, beryllium's from 2s [1]. The 2p electron is higher in energy (further from the nucleus) and shielded by the 2s electrons, so less energy is needed [1].

**(c)** Oxygen's electron is removed from a 2p orbital containing two electrons [1]; spin-pair repulsion between them makes it easier to remove [1]. *(Total: 7 marks)*

**7. (a)** There is a very large jump between the 4th and 5th values (4356 to 16 091) [1], so four electrons are in the outer shell and the fifth comes from an inner shell, giving Group 14 [1].

**(b)** The first two electrons come from 3p and the 3rd from 3s [1]; the 3s electron is lower in energy and less shielded, so an extra rise occurs at that change of sub-shell, while the 3rd and 4th both come from 3s [1].

**(c)** Si⁴⁺(g) → Si⁵⁺(g) + e⁻ [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**8. (a)** Na⁺: 1s²2s²2p⁶ [1]. Mg⁺: 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹ [1].

**(b)** For Na⁺ the electron is removed from the 2p sub-shell, in the second shell [1]; for Mg⁺ it is removed from 3s, in the third shell [1]. The 2p electron is much closer to the nucleus and less shielded, so it is attracted far more strongly [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**9. (a)** The largest jump is between the 3rd and 4th values (2745 to 11 577) [1], so there are three outer electrons: Group 13 [1].

**(b)** Aluminium [1]; 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p¹ [1].

**(c)** Al₂O₃ [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**10. (a)** Potassium [1] (19 electrons; 2, 8, 8, 1).

**(b)** Each steep step marks the first electron removed from a shell closer to the nucleus [1]. The first point is the single 4s electron; the next eight come from the third shell (3s²3p⁶), then eight from the second shell, then two from the first shell [1]. Electrons in inner shells are closer to the nucleus and less shielded, so they need much more energy to remove [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**11. (a)** First: 1251 − 1000 = 251; second: 2298 − 2252 = 46 [1].

**(b)** For second ionisation, S⁺ (3p³) loses an electron from a singly occupied orbital, while Cl⁺ (3p⁴) loses one from a doubly occupied orbital [1]. Spin-pair repulsion in Cl⁺ makes its electron easier to remove [1], which largely cancels the effect of the extra proton, so the rise is small [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**12. (a)** K(g) → K⁺(g) + e⁻ [1].

**(b)** Calcium has one more proton [1], and both electrons are in 4s with the same inner shielding, so calcium's outer electron is attracted more strongly [1].

**(c)** Larger [1]: K⁺ has the configuration of argon, so its second electron comes from the 3p sub-shell, closer to the nucleus, whereas Ca⁺ loses its remaining 4s electron [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing two electrons in a second ionisation equation, or using (s) or (aq).
- Stating a trend without explaining it through nuclear charge, distance and shielding.
- Using spin-pair repulsion for the Be/B or Mg/Al dip.
- Not quoting the values used to find the large jump.
- Forgetting that the second ionisation pattern is shifted by one element.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find the group from successive ionisation energies?**
Find the largest jump between consecutive values. If it comes after the nth value, the element has n outer electrons: Group 1 or 2 for n = 1 or 2, and Group n + 10 for p-block elements.

**Why does boron have a lower first ionisation energy than beryllium?**
Boron loses a 2p electron, which is higher in energy than beryllium's 2s electron and is shielded by the 2s pair.
