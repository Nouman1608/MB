## Dynamic equilibrium

Three conditions, all required:

1. Forward and reverse **rates are equal**.
2. Concentrations of reactants and products remain **constant**.
3. The system is **closed**.

Both reactions continue — nothing stops. "The reaction has stopped" is the classic wrong answer.

## Le Chatelier's principle

> If a change is applied to a system at equilibrium, the position shifts to **oppose** that change.

| Change | Equilibrium shifts | Effect on K |
|---|---|---|
| ↑ concentration of a reactant | Towards products | **Unchanged** |
| ↑ pressure | Towards the side with **fewer gas moles** | **Unchanged** |
| ↑ temperature | Towards the **endothermic** direction | **CHANGES** |
| Catalyst added | **No shift** | Unchanged |

**Only temperature changes the value of K.** Concentration and pressure shift the position but leave K the same — the single most examined idea in this topic.

## Equilibrium constants

```
For  aA + bB  <=>  cC + dD

Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)        concentrations, mol dm^-3
Kp = (pC^c x pD^d) / (pA^a x pB^b)      partial pressures
```

**Partial pressure:** `p_X = mole fraction of X × total pressure`, where mole fraction = moles of X ÷ total moles.

Units must be **worked out each time** from the expression — they are not fixed. If moles of gas are equal on both sides, K has no units.

Solids and pure liquids are **omitted** from K expressions.

## Interpreting K

```
K >> 1   ->  products favoured
K ~ 1    ->  comparable amounts
K << 1   ->  reactants favoured
```

K depends **only on temperature**.

## The two industrial processes

```
HABER    N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)      forward EXOTHERMIC
         450 C, 200 atm, iron catalyst
         4 moles gas -> 2 moles, so HIGH pressure favours ammonia

CONTACT  2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)     forward EXOTHERMIC
         450 C, 1-2 atm, V2O5 catalyst
         3 moles gas -> 2 moles
```

Both use a **compromise temperature**: a lower temperature would raise the yield (the forward reaction is exothermic) but the rate would be uneconomically slow.

The Contact process uses low pressure because the yield is already high — high pressure would add cost for little gain.

## Exam traps

- Saying reactions "stop" at equilibrium.
- Claiming a catalyst increases yield — it only shortens the time to reach equilibrium.
- Stating K changes with pressure or concentration. Only temperature.
- Including solids or pure liquids in a K expression.
- Quoting units for K without deriving them.
- Explaining the compromise temperature without mentioning **both** yield and rate.

## Self-test

1. State the three conditions for dynamic equilibrium.
2. Which single factor changes the value of K?
3. Write Kp for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.
4. Why is 450 °C used in the Haber process?
5. What happens to the position of equilibrium if pressure is increased in the Contact process?

**Answers:** 1. Forward and reverse rates are equal; concentrations remain constant; the system is closed. 2. Temperature. 3. Kp = (p_NH₃)² / (p_N₂ × (p_H₂)³). 4. A compromise — the forward reaction is exothermic so a lower temperature would give a higher yield, but the rate would be too slow to be economic. 5. It shifts towards SO₃, the side with fewer moles of gas (3 → 2), increasing yield — though in practice low pressure is used because yield is already high.
