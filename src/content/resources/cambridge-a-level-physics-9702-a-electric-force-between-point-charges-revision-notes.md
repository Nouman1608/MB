Subtopic 18.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers Coulomb's law for two point charges in free space and the treatment of a charged spherical conductor as a point charge at its centre. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Electric force between point charges study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-force-between-point-charges/); to apply the ideas, try the [Electric force between point charges practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-force-between-point-charges-practice/).

## Coulomb's law

```
F = Q₁Q₂ / (4πε₀r²)
1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ m F⁻¹  (data sheet)
ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹      (data sheet)
```

- Recall the law; the constants are given.
- Point charges, in free space (air ≈ free space).
- Force along the line joining the charges.
- Equal and opposite forces on the two charges.
- Q₁Q₂ > 0: repulsive. Q₁Q₂ < 0: attractive.
- Units of ε₀: F m⁻¹ ≡ C² N⁻¹ m⁻².

## Scaling

| Change | F becomes |
|---|---|
| Q₁ × 2 | 2F |
| Q₁ × 2 and Q₂ × 2 | 4F |
| r × 2 | F/4 |
| r × 3 | F/9 |
| r × ½ | 4F |
| Q₁ × 2, r × 3 | 2F/9 |

## Spheres

- Outside a charged conducting sphere: treat the charge as a **point at the centre**.
- Use **centre-to-centre** distance.
- Reason: charge spreads uniformly over the surface of an isolated sphere; by symmetry the external field is radial, as from a point.
- Approximation breaks down when two conductors are close: charges redistribute.

## Charge sharing

Identical conducting spheres in contact share charge equally:

```
each final charge = (Q₁ + Q₂) / 2     (algebraic sum)
```

Example: +8 nC and −4 nC → each +2 nC. Force changes from attractive to repulsive and from 32 to 4 units (× 1/8).

## Several charges

1. Draw a diagram, choose a positive direction.
2. Find each force magnitude separately.
3. Assign each direction (attraction or repulsion).
4. Add as vectors: signs on a line; Pythagoras at right angles.

**Zero-force point between two like charges Q_A and Q_B a distance d apart:** Q_A/x² = Q_B/(d − x)², so √Q_A/x = √Q_B/(d − x). It lies closer to the smaller charge. Example: +2 μC and +8 μC, 0.60 m apart → x = 0.20 m from the +2 μC charge.

## Electric versus gravitational

| Feature | Electric | Gravitational |
|---|---|---|
| Property | Charge | Mass |
| Law | 1/r² | 1/r² |
| Direction | Attract or repel | Attract only |
| Constant | 8.99 × 10⁹ | 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ |
| Electron–proton ratio F_E/F_G | about 2 × 10³⁹ | |
| Proton–proton ratio F_E/F_G | about 1.2 × 10³⁶ | |

The ratios do not depend on r, because both forces are inverse-square.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point charge | A charge whose size is negligible compared with the distances involved |
| Coulomb's law | Force between point charges ∝ product of charges, ∝ 1/(separation)² |
| Permittivity of free space ε₀ | Constant in Coulomb's law, 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹ |
| Free space | A vacuum; air is treated as free space |
| Charge sharing | Equal division of total charge between identical conductors placed in contact |

## Exam traps

- Surface gap instead of centre distance.
- r not squared.
- nC and μC not converted.
- Scalar addition of forces in different directions.
- Saying the larger charge feels the larger force.
- Sharing charge by magnitudes rather than the algebraic sum.
- Calculating a "negative force" without saying attractive.

## Self-test

1. State Coulomb's law in words.
2. Calculate the force between +3.0 μC and −5.0 μC 0.20 m apart. Is it attractive or repulsive?
3. The separation of two charges is halved. By what factor does the force change?
4. Two spheres of radius 1.0 cm have centres 8.0 cm apart. What value of r is used?
5. Why can a charged sphere be treated as a point charge?
6. Identical spheres carry +10 nC and −2 nC and touch. What charge does each carry afterwards?
7. Show that the units of ε₀ can be written C² N⁻¹ m⁻².
8. Two charges +1.0 nC and +9.0 nC are 0.40 m apart. Where between them is the force on a third charge zero?
9. Two forces of 3.0 μN and 4.0 μN act at right angles on a charge. Find the resultant.
10. Why does the ratio of electric to gravitational force between two protons not depend on their separation?
11. A +2.0 nC charge feels 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ N from a charge 0.10 m away. Find the other charge's magnitude.
12. Does a +1 nC charge exert a smaller force on a +5 nC charge than the +5 nC charge exerts on it?
