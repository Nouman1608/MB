These notes condense sub-topic **3.1 Measuring economic activity and illustrating its variations** from the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022). It is SL and HL content. For full explanations and a step-by-step data set read the [3.1 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-1/); then test yourself with the [3.1 practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-1-practice/). Countries and figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gross domestic product (GDP) | Total value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a period |
| Gross national income (GNI) | GDP plus net income from abroad: income earned by a country's residents wherever they produce |
| Nominal value | Measured at current prices |
| Real value | Adjusted for inflation, at constant (base-year) prices |
| GDP deflator | Price index used to convert nominal GDP into real GDP |
| Per capita | Divided by population |
| Purchasing power parity (PPP) | Exchange rate that equalises the cost of a basket of goods between countries |
| Value added | Value of output minus the cost of bought-in inputs |
| Business cycle | Short-term fluctuations of real GDP around its long-term growth trend |
| Long-term growth trend | The path of potential output |
| Recession | A period of falling real GDP; often defined as two consecutive quarters of decline |
| Output gap | Difference between actual and potential output |

## Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| GDP (expenditure) | C + I + G + (X - M) |
| GNI | GDP + net income from abroad |
| Real GDP | Nominal GDP ÷ deflator × 100 |
| Per capita | Total ÷ population |
| Growth rate | (new - old) ÷ old × 100 |

**Worked reminder (Losk, billions of losks).** C = 240, I = 60, G = 80, X = 70, M = 90.

```
GDP = 240 + 60 + 80 + (70 - 90) = 360
Net income from abroad = +15, so GNI = 375
Next year nominal GDP = 396 with deflator 110:
Real GDP = 396 / 110 x 100 = 360
Nominal growth = (396 - 360) / 360 x 100 = 10%; real growth = 0%
```

All of the rise was inflation. With a population of 12 million, real GDP per capita is 360 bn ÷ 12 m = **30,000 losks** in both years.

## Three approaches

- **Output:** sum of value added (avoids double counting).
- **Income:** wages + rent + interest + profit.
- **Expenditure:** C + I + G + (X - M).
- Equal in principle, because spending on output becomes income; they differ in practice through measurement error.

## PPP in one line

GDP per capita at PPP = GDP per capita at market rates × (price of the basket in the reference country ÷ price of the same basket in the country, converted at market rates). Use PPP to compare living standards.

## Business cycle checklist

| Phase | What real GDP does | Typical unemployment and inflation |
|---|---|---|
| Expansion | Rises | Unemployment falls; inflation may rise |
| Peak | Highest point | Low unemployment; inflationary pressure |
| Contraction | Falls | Unemployment rises; inflation eases |
| Trough | Lowest point | High unemployment; spare capacity |

## GDP and well-being

| Weakness | One-line reason |
|---|---|
| Inaccurate data | Surveys and estimates, later revised |
| Informal and home production omitted | Understates output |
| Leisure and quality of life ignored | More hours can raise GDP but cut well-being |
| Composition of output | Defence or disaster repair counts the same as health care |
| Environmental costs omitted | Depletion and pollution not subtracted |
| Distribution ignored | Average hides inequality |

**Alternatives:** OECD Better Life Index (many weighted dimensions); happiness measures based on life-satisfaction surveys; Happy Planet Index (well-being and life expectancy relative to ecological footprint). They add breadth but involve choices about weights and rely on survey responses.

## Exam traps

- Calling a fall in the **growth rate** a recession; output must actually fall.
- Comparing **nominal** figures across years.
- Forgetting "× 100" in the deflator formula.
- Swapping GDP and GNI: GDP is about **location**, GNI about **ownership**.
- Comparing total GDP instead of **per capita** figures between countries.
- Listing limitations of GDP without saying why each matters.

## Quick self-test

1. Define GDP.
2. How does GNI differ from GDP?
3. C = 500, I = 120, G = 150, X = 200, M = 230. Calculate GDP.
4. If net income from abroad is -25, calculate GNI for question 3.
5. Nominal GDP is 880 and the deflator is 110. Calculate real GDP.
6. Real GDP rose from 750 to the figure in question 5. Calculate real growth.
7. Why do economists use real GDP per capita to compare living standards over time?
8. A mine sells ore for 50; a smelter sells metal for 90; a manufacturer sells tools for 160. Calculate total value added.
9. Why is a PPP figure better than a market exchange rate figure for comparing living standards?
10. Real GDP grew by 3% then by 1%. Is this a recession?
11. Give two reasons why GDP may understate well-being and one reason why it may overstate it.
12. Name one alternative measure of well-being and one limitation of it.

### Answers

1. The total value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period.
2. GNI adds net income from abroad, so it measures income of residents rather than output produced inside the borders.
3. 500 + 120 + 150 + (200 - 230) = **740**.
4. 740 - 25 = **715**.
5. 880 ÷ 110 × 100 = **800**.
6. (800 - 750) ÷ 750 × 100 ≈ **6.7%**.
7. Real removes inflation; per capita allows for population change.
8. 50 + (90 - 50) + (160 - 90) = **160**, equal to the final sale.
9. It adjusts for differences in price levels, so it shows what incomes actually buy.
10. No: output is still rising; only growth has slowed.
11. Understates: home production and informal work are omitted; leisure is ignored. Overstates: pollution and resource depletion are not subtracted (or defensive spending is counted).
12. Happy Planet Index; it depends on survey answers and chosen weights (any valid pairing earns credit).
