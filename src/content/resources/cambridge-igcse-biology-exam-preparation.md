Cambridge IGCSE Biology (0610, for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028) is tiered into Core and Extended. A Core candidate sits Paper 1 Multiple Choice (45 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 3 Theory (1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). An Extended candidate sits Paper 2 Multiple Choice (45 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 4 Theory (1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). Both tiers then take the same practical component, either Paper 5 Practical Test (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) or Paper 6 Alternative to Practical (1 hour, 40 marks, 20%). Build your knowledge with topic pages such as [Biological molecules](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-biological-molecules/), [Diseases and immunity](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-diseases-and-immunity/) and [Coordination and response](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-coordination-and-response/).

## Choosing and understanding your route

Each tier has a multiple-choice paper and a theory paper: Papers 1 and 3 for Core, Papers 2 and 4 for Extended. Within a tier, the weightings are 30% + 50% + 20% = 100% with the practical component. The practical component is a single alternative choice common to both tiers: your school enters you for Paper 5 or Paper 6, not both. Ask your teacher which tier and which practical paper you are entered for before you build a timetable.

## Papers 1 and 2: Multiple Choice

Both multiple-choice papers are 45 minutes and 40 marks, which is 45 / 40 = 1.125 minutes per mark. That is quick, so practise reading each stem carefully, predicting the answer before looking at the options, and crossing out wrong choices. Practise across the full range of topic areas, because any of them can appear. Data interpretation, from graphs and tables to diagrams, is a regular feature of any biology multiple-choice practice.

## Papers 3 and 4: Theory

Core Paper 3 and Extended Paper 4 are each 1 hour 15 minutes and 80 marks, so 75 / 80 = 0.94 minutes per mark. This is the fastest pace in the course, and it means answers must be short and exact. Learn the key terms from every topic area, from cells and enzymes to ecosystems and biotechnology, and practise writing one precise sentence for each mark available. Revision notes such as [Drugs](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-drugs-revision-notes/) are best used by turning each into a short list of tested facts and testing yourself on it.

## Papers 5 and 6: the practical component

Paper 5 Practical Test (75 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) is assessed as a practical paper. Paper 6 Alternative to Practical (60 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) is a written paper that assesses practical skills. For Paper 5 the rate is 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark, and for Paper 6 it is 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark. Whichever you sit, rehearse the same skills: following a method, recording results in a table, plotting a graph, calculating a result, identifying sources of error and suggesting improvements. Confirm the precise skills in the syllabus.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Papers 1 and 2: 45 / 40 = 1.125 minutes per mark.
- Papers 3 and 4: 75 / 80 = 0.94 minutes per mark.
- Paper 5: 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark.
- Paper 6: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Core candidate with Paper 5: 45 + 75 + 75 = 195 minutes for 40 + 80 + 40 = 160 marks.
- Extended candidate with Paper 6: 45 + 75 + 60 = 180 minutes for 160 marks.

## Command words and question styles

"State" and "name" want a short fact. "Describe" wants features or a pattern, for graphs including quoted values. "Explain" wants a reason using biological terms. "Suggest" asks you to apply knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. "Calculate" wants working and units. "Compare" wants similarities and differences, not two separate descriptions. Check the syllabus for the full definitions.

## Worked practice scenario

Original question: "Potato cylinders were left in a concentrated sugar solution. One cylinder had an initial mass of 5.00 g and a final mass of 4.35 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass and explain the result."

```
change in mass = final - initial = 4.35 - 5.00 = -0.65 g
percentage change = change / initial x 100
                  = -0.65 / 5.00 x 100
                  = -13.0 %
```

Explanation: the mass decreased by 13.0%. The sugar solution has a lower water potential than the cytoplasm of the potato cells, so water moved out of the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable membrane, and the cylinder lost mass. Always divide by the initial mass, and always include the sign: a negative value shows a loss. A reasonable follow-up is to blot each cylinder dry before weighing, so that surface liquid does not add false mass.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm your tier (Core or Extended) and your practical paper (5 or 6).
- Practise multiple-choice papers at 1.125 minutes per mark.
- Learn precise definitions for each topic and write answers one point per mark.
- Practise percentage change, magnification and rate calculations with units.
- Rehearse tables, graph plotting and error evaluation for the practical component.
- Read the whole stem of each multiple-choice question before looking at options.
- Use biological terms, not everyday language, in explanations.
- Check every answer has the units and sign it needs.

## Self-test

1. Which papers does an Extended candidate sit?
2. What is the weighting of the practical component?
3. What is the pace of Papers 3 and 4 in minutes per mark?
4. Why do you divide by the initial mass in a percentage change?
5. Can a candidate sit both Paper 5 and Paper 6?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 2, Paper 4 and either Paper 5 or Paper 6. 2. 20%. 3. 75 / 80 = 0.94 minutes per mark. 4. Because percentage change is measured relative to the starting value. 5. No; they are alternatives in a single practical group.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610?**
There are six papers, but each candidate sits three: two for their tier and one practical component.

**What is the difference between Core and Extended?**
Core candidates sit Papers 1 and 3, and Extended candidates sit Papers 2 and 4. The multiple-choice papers are 40 marks and 30%, and the theory papers are 80 marks and 50%.

**What is the difference between Paper 5 and Paper 6?**
Paper 5 is a Practical Test of 1 hour 15 minutes, and Paper 6 is an Alternative to Practical of 1 hour. Both are 40 marks and 20%.

**Do both tiers take the same practical component?**
Yes. Paper 5 or Paper 6 is a single alternative choice common to both tiers.
