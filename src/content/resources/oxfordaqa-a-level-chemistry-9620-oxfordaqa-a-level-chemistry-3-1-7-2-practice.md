These questions cover **3.1.7.2 Equilibrium constant Kc for homogeneous systems** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2. Values of Kc are given "at a certain temperature"; some are invented for practice. Give answers to 3 significant figures.

## Section A

**1.** For CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g), write an expression for Kc and deduce its units. **[2]**

**2.** Write an expression for Kc for 4NH₃(g) + 5O₂(g) ⇌ 4NO(g) + 6H₂O(g). **[1]**

**3.** State **two** changes to an equilibrium mixture at constant temperature that do not change the value of Kc. **[2]**

**4.** State the units, if any, of Kc for 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g). **[1]**

**5.** For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), the equilibrium constant is K. Write the equilibrium constant for 2SO₃(g) ⇌ 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) in terms of K. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** At equilibrium, a mixture for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) contains [SO₂] = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³, [O₂] = 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ and [SO₃] = 0.400 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kc and give its units. **[3]**

**7.** 0.0800 mol of N₂O₄ is sealed in a 2.00 dm³ flask and heated. At equilibrium, 0.0500 mol of N₂O₄ remains.

N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g)

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium amount, in mol, of NO₂. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Kc and give its units. **[3]**

**8.** 0.400 mol of ethanoic acid and 0.600 mol of ethanol react with an acid catalyst:

CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

At equilibrium, 0.300 mol of ethyl ethanoate is present. No water was present at the start.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium amounts of ethanoic acid, ethanol and water. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Kc. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the volume of the mixture is not needed. **[1]**

**9.** For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), Kc = 36.0 at a certain temperature. 0.200 mol of H₂ and 0.200 mol of I₂ are sealed in a 1.00 dm³ flask at this temperature. Calculate the amount of each substance at equilibrium. **[4]**

**10.** For PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), Kc = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ at a certain temperature. In a 2.00 dm³ flask at equilibrium, [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the amount, in mol, of PCl₅ in the flask. **[3]**

**11.** This question is about the effect of temperature on Kc.

**(a)** For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict and explain the effect of raising the temperature on the value of Kc. **[2]**

**(b)** For a reaction D(g) + 2E(g) ⇌ F(g), Kc is 0.45 mol⁻² dm⁶ at 300 K and 1.8 mol⁻² dm⁶ at 400 K (invented values). Deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic, explaining your answer. **[2]**

**12.** For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), Kc = 36.0 at a certain temperature. An equilibrium mixture at this temperature contains 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ H₂, 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ I₂ and 0.300 mol dm⁻³ HI. Extra iodine is injected so that [I₂] instantly becomes 0.150 mol dm⁻³, at the same temperature.

**(a)** Calculate the value of [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂]) immediately after the injection. **[1]**

**(b)** Compare your answer with Kc and explain how the mixture responds. **[2]**

**(c)** State the value of Kc once equilibrium is re-established. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Kc = [CH₃OH] / ([CO][H₂]²) [1]; units: (mol dm⁻³) / (mol dm⁻³)³ = **mol⁻² dm⁶** [1]

**2.** Kc = [NO]⁴[H₂O]⁶ / ([NH₃]⁴[O₂]⁵) [1]

**3.** Any two from: changing the concentration of a reactant or product [1]; adding a catalyst [1]; changing the pressure

**4.** **No units** (powers of 2 on top and underneath cancel) [1]

**5.** **1/K** [1]

**6.** Kc = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]²[O₂]) [1]; = 0.400² / (0.0500² × 0.0300) = 0.160 / 7.50 × 10⁻⁵ = **2.13 × 10³** [1]; units **mol⁻¹ dm³** [1]

**7. (a)** N₂O₄ reacted = 0.0800 − 0.0500 = 0.0300 mol [1]; NO₂ formed = 2 × 0.0300 = **0.0600 mol** [1]

**(b)** [N₂O₄] = 0.0500 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0250 mol dm⁻³ and [NO₂] = 0.0600 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ [1]; Kc = 0.0300² / 0.0250 = **0.0360** [1]; units **mol dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Here the powers do not cancel, so using moles would give 0.072, a wrong answer. Always convert to concentrations unless n = 0.

**8. (a)** 0.300 mol of each reactant has reacted: ethanoic acid = **0.100 mol**, ethanol = **0.300 mol** [1]; water = **0.300 mol** (formed 1 : 1 with the ester) [1]

**(b)** Kc = (0.300 × 0.300) / (0.100 × 0.300) [1]; = **3.00** (no units) [1]

**(c)** There are equal numbers of concentration terms (equal powers) on top and underneath, so the volume cancels [1]

**9.** Let x mol of H₂ react: H₂ = I₂ = 0.200 − x, HI = 2x [1]; (2x)² / (0.200 − x)² = 36.0, so 2x / (0.200 − x) = 6.00 [1]; 2x = 1.20 − 6.00x, so x = 0.150 [1]; equilibrium: H₂ = I₂ = **0.0500 mol**, HI = **0.300 mol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the square root of both sides first; it turns the problem into a simple linear equation.

**10.** Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅], so [PCl₅] = (0.0500 × 0.0500) / 0.0200 [1]; = 0.125 mol dm⁻³ [1]; amount = 0.125 × 2.00 = **0.250 mol** [1]

**11. (a)** Kc decreases [1]; the forward reaction is exothermic, so raising the temperature shifts the position to the left (the endothermic direction), lowering the product concentrations relative to the reactants [1]

**(b)** Kc increases as the temperature rises, so the position moves right on heating [1]; heating favours the endothermic direction, so the forward reaction is **endothermic** [1]

**12. (a)** 0.300² / (0.0500 × 0.150) = 0.0900 / 0.00750 = **12.0** [1]

**(b)** 12.0 is less than Kc (36.0) [1]; so H₂ and I₂ react to form more HI, shifting the position right until the ratio returns to 36.0 [1]

**(c)** **36.0** (unchanged, because the temperature is unchanged) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Questions like this test that you separate the position of equilibrium, which moves, from Kc, which does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using moles when the volume does not cancel.
- Forgetting the mole ratio (the change for NO₂ is twice that for N₂O₄).
- Missing powers in the expression, especially for SO₃ and SO₂.
- Units guessed instead of cancelled.
- Saying Kc changes when a concentration changes.
