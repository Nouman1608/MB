## Questions

**1.** Zephmoor Print makes wedding stationery. Identify the Ansoff quadrant for each of these strategies. **(a)** Selling the same stationery to customers in Ireland. **(b)** Adding printed gift wrap, sold to its existing customers. **(c)** Offering existing customers a discount on repeat orders. **(d)** Buying a firm that makes garden furniture and selling it to garden centres. **[4]**

**2.** Explain the nature and purpose of Ansoff's matrix. **[4]**

**3.** Marnoch Dairy sells milk to supermarkets. Distinguish between market development and product development, using an example from Marnoch Dairy for each. **[4]**

**4.** Nessbury Fabrication makes steel parts for car makers and employs 120 people. It is considering diversifying into house building. Analyse the risk of this strategy for Nessbury's stakeholders. **[6]**

**5.** Vossler Audio is choosing between two options, figures in £000.

Launch a new speaker: cost 60, a 0.55 chance of a payoff of 210 and a 0.45 chance of a payoff of 30.
Upgrade the current speaker: cost 10, a certain payoff of 55.

**(a)** Calculate the expected value and the net gain of each option. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain one qualitative factor that could lead managers to choose the upgrade even if the launch has the higher net gain. **[2]**

**6.** A business must choose one of two projects, each costing £120,000. Net cash inflows are:

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project P | £50,000 | £50,000 | £40,000 | £30,000 |
| Project Q | £20,000 | £30,000 | £50,000 | £90,000 |

Discount factors at 10%: 0.909, 0.826, 0.751, 0.683.

**(a)** Calculate for each project the payback period, the average rate of return and the net present value. **[6]**
**(b)** Explain why the payback results conflict with the other two. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two strengths and one limitation of using a decision tree when making a business decision. **[6]**

**8.** Ulverly Tiles makes ceramic floor tiles. Sales have been flat at £14m for three years and the profit margin is 6%. The owner wants sales 20% higher within four years. Cash in the bank is £1.1m. Three options are proposed.

Option 1: a discount scheme for builders' accounts it already supplies, costing £150,000 a year.
Option 2: selling its present tiles to builders' merchants in Germany, costing £1.5m.
Option 3: a new range of waterproof wall panels for its existing customers, costing £900,000.

Recommend and justify an option for Ulverly. **[9]**

**9.** Assess the usefulness of Ansoff's matrix to a business choosing a growth strategy. **[8]**

**10.** Dovrane Logistics delivers goods for retailers. It plans a refrigerated delivery service for its existing clients at a cost of £450,000. Forecast net cash inflows are £110,000, £120,000, £130,000, £130,000 and £120,000 in years 1 to 5. Discount factors at 8%: 0.926, 0.857, 0.794, 0.735, 0.681. Targets: payback within 3.5 years and ARR of at least 9%. A decision tree gives a 0.5 chance of a payoff of £900,000 and a 0.5 chance of £250,000. The two drivers who know refrigeration will soon retire, and the bank will lend only £300,000. Evaluate whether Dovrane should go ahead. **[12]**

**11.** Strathby Clinic opened three new physiotherapy clinics in a neighbouring county in 18 months. The business forecast 40 patients a day at each. Actual numbers average 22 a day. Staff turnover is high, patient satisfaction has fallen and the overdraft is at its limit. Evaluate the main reasons for the failure of the expansion so far. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Market development [1]. **(b)** Product development [1]. **(c)** Market penetration [1]. **(d)** Diversification [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-word quadrant answers an "identify" question; extra explanation is unlikely to add anything, while a vague label such as "expansion" identifies nothing.

**2.** Nature: a model that sorts growth strategies into four quadrants [1] by whether the products and the markets are new or existing [1]. Purpose: to show managers the range of growth options [1] and compare the risk of each, supporting strategic decisions [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate what it is from what it is for; a list of quadrants describes the model but does not explain its purpose.

**3.** Market development: selling existing products in a new market [1], for example selling Marnoch's milk in another region or to cafes [1]. Product development: selling new products to the existing market [1], for example launching flavoured milk drinks for its supermarkets [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both items contrasted. State what is new in each, and make the example fit that definition.

**4.** Diversification is high risk because Nessbury has neither house building skills nor knowledge of that market [1]. Employees may lose jobs if costly mistakes force cuts, or may gain work if it succeeds [1][1]. Shareholders could lose dividends and share value if the project fails and drains cash from the steel parts business [1][1]. Car makers may see slower deliveries if management attention moves elsewhere [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each stakeholder effect to the strategy and its cause; a list of stakeholders with no consequence reads as description.

**5.** **(a)** Launch: expected value = 0.55 × 210 + 0.45 × 30 = 115.5 + 13.5 = 129 [1]; net gain = 129 − 60 = **69, that is £69,000** [1]. Upgrade: expected value = 55 [1]; net gain = 55 − 10 = **45, that is £45,000** [1]. **(b)** The launch could damage the brand if the new speaker is unreliable [1], and the upgrade protects the reputation and a known customer base, which matters beyond the figures [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication and subtract the cost, since an expected value is not a net gain; link any qualitative factor to the choice made.

**6.** **(a)** Payback: P is 2 years for £100,000, then £20,000 of £40,000, so 2.5 years [1]; Q is £100,000 after 3 years, then £20,000 of £90,000, so 3.22 years [1]. ARR: P profit = 170 − 120 = £50,000, average £12,500, so 10.4% [1]; Q profit = 190 − 120 = £70,000, average £17,500, so 14.6% [1]. NPV: P present value = 45,450 + 41,300 + 30,040 + 20,490 = £137,280, so NPV **£17,280** [1]; Q present value = 18,180 + 24,780 + 37,550 + 61,470 = £141,980, so NPV **£21,980** [1]. **(b)** Payback favours P because it returns cash sooner [1]. ARR and NPV favour Q because its larger later inflows give more total profit and value [1]. Payback ignores cash after the cost is recovered, while ARR and NPV use the whole project [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* State units and round consistently. In an explanation, name what each method measures; saying the answers differ only restates the question.

**7.** Strength: it weighs each outcome by its probability [1], so a risky option is compared fairly with a safer one [1]. Strength: it sets out the choices and their costs visually [1], which aids discussion [1]. Limitation: the probabilities and payoffs are estimates and may be biased [1], so small errors can change the best option, and the expected value may never occur [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a reason as well as a label.

**8.** Option 1 is market penetration [1], Option 2 market development [1], Option 3 product development [1]. Target: sales of 14 × 1.2 = £16.8m, so £2.8m more [1]. Option 1 is cheap and low risk but may squeeze a 6% margin and not lift sales by £2.8m [1]. Option 2 costs £1.5m, more than the £1.1m cash in the bank, so needs borrowing, with unfamiliar customers [1]. Option 3 costs £900,000, within cash, and uses existing customers, but needs new production skills [1]. Recommendation: Option 3, because it is affordable and builds sales with known customers [1], subject to testing whether builders want panels [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Rank the options against the objective and the stated cash, and say why the rejected options are weaker, not only why the chosen one is good.

**9.** Strength: it presents every growth option in one simple framework [1], allowing quick comparison [1]. Strength: it links strategy to risk, showing that moving away from current products and markets raises risk [1]. Limitation: it has no financial figures, so it cannot show which option is profitable [1]. Limitation: it ignores resources and the reactions of rivals [1], and the risk ranking is only a guide [1]. Judgement: useful as a first screen but not enough alone [1], so add investment appraisal and qualitative judgement [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An "assess" answer is stronger when it ends in a judgement about how far the matrix helps, not just two lists.

**10.** Payback: £110,000 + £120,000 + £130,000 = £360,000 after 3 years, so a further £90,000 of £130,000 gives 3.69 years, which misses the 3.5 year target [1][1]. ARR: total inflows £610,000, profit £160,000, average £32,000, which is 7.1%, below the 9% target [1][1]. NPV: present values 101,860 + 102,840 + 103,220 + 95,550 + 81,720 = £485,190, so NPV = **£35,190** [1][1]. Decision tree: expected value = 0.5 × 900,000 + 0.5 × 250,000 = £575,000, so net gain £125,000 [1]. The tools conflict: NPV and the tree support going ahead, but payback and ARR miss their targets [1]. Qualitative: serving existing clients (product development) is a moderate risk [1], but the retiring drivers and a £300,000 loan limit against £450,000 are serious constraints [1]. Judgement: proceed only if the funding gap is solved and replacement staff are secured, because NPV is slightly positive and the probability is a guess [1][1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain a conflict by the tools' different measures, and show how the case constraints change the decision.

**11.** Over-optimistic forecasts: actual patients are 22 against 40, which is 55% of the forecast [1], so revenue falls well short. Poor handling of the new market: high staff turnover and lower satisfaction suggest staff and customers there were misjudged [1][1]. Financial strain: three clinics at once left the overdraft at its limit, so there was no margin for error [1]. Judgement: the forecast was the main cause because the other problems follow from lower demand and cash pressure [1], though the pace of expansion made the failure worse [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the case figures and decide which cause came first; unranked problems are only partly evaluative.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a quadrant without showing what is new in the strategy.
- Saying diversification is risky without explaining why.
- Listing stakeholders without a consequence for each.
- Calculating payback, ARR or NPV without comparing the result to a target.
- Treating a conflict between tools as an error, not explaining it.
- Recommending a strategy without dismissing the others.
- Finishing an evaluation without a judgement.
