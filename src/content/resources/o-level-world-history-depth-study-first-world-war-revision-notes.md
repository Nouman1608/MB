**These notes condense Depth Study A of Cambridge O Level History 2147, The First World War, 1914-18, for last-minute recall.** They follow the syllabus's four key questions -- stalemate, a world war, other fronts, and the 1918 armistice -- and are built for Paper 1 Section B, where you answer one three-part question (4 + 6 + 10 marks). For full explanations see the [Depth Study A study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-first-world-war/); for exam practice see [Depth Study A practice questions](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-first-world-war-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL HISTORY (2147), 2024-2026 -- DEPTH STUDY A**

1. Why was there stalemate on the Western Front?
2. To what extent was it a world war?
3. How important were other fronts?
4. Why did Germany ask for an armistice in 1918?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Schlieffen Plan | German plan to defeat France quickly through Belgium before facing Russia |
| BEF | British Expeditionary Force, Britain's small professional army of 1914 |
| Race to the sea | Attempts to outflank each other northwards in autumn 1914 |
| Attrition | Wearing down the enemy's men and resources |
| No man's land | Ground between opposing trench lines |
| Creeping barrage | Artillery fire moving forward just ahead of advancing infantry |
| Convoy | Merchant ships sailing together under naval escort |
| Unrestricted submarine warfare | Sinking any ship in a war zone without warning |
| Armistice | Agreement to stop fighting (not the peace treaty) |

## Key question 1: stalemate -- four causes to learn

1. **Schlieffen Plan failed:** Belgian resistance; BEF at Mons; Russia's fast mobilisation; the Marne.
2. **Defence stronger than attack:** machine guns, barbed wire, artillery, railways bringing reserves.
3. **Trench conditions** made attacks costly and men exhausted: mud, rats, lice, trench foot, shell shock.
4. **New weapons did not at first break the deadlock:** gas (1915), tanks (1916), aircraft -- only combined tactics worked by 1918.

**Verdun vs the Somme:** Verdun was a German attack to bleed France white; the Somme was an Allied attack partly to relieve Verdun. Haig debate -- "butcher" (57,000 British casualties on day one) or a commander learning lessons used in 1918?

## Key question 2: a world war -- case studies

| Case study | Remember |
|---|---|
| Indian troops, Western Front | Arrived 1914; Ypres, Neuve Chapelle; first Indian VC (Khudadad Khan) |
| Canadian troops, Western Front | Gas at Second Ypres 1915; Vimy Ridge April 1917 |
| South West Africa | South African forces under Botha; German surrender July 1915 |
| German East Africa | Indian troops at Tanga 1914; Smuts's campaign 1916; Lettow-Vorbeck unbeaten until after the Armistice |
| Japan in Asia | Tsingtao 1914; Pacific islands; naval escorts protecting sea lanes |
| Arab Revolt | Sharif Hussein, 1916; T. E. Lawrence; Aqaba 1917; Damascus 1918 |

## Key question 3: other fronts

- **Sea:** Jutland -- British losses higher, but the German fleet stayed in port and the blockade went on. U-boats -- the main German threat; convoys from 1917 cut losses.
- **Gallipoli:** poor planning, failed naval attack, Turkish defence, terrain, disease -- evacuation by January 1916.
- **Eastern Front:** drew German troops east until Russia's collapse; Brest-Litovsk freed troops for 1918.
- **Civilians:** conscription, DORA, rationing, women's work, air raids; Germany's "turnip winter" 1916-17.

## Key question 4: why the armistice -- weigh these

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| US entry | Fresh troops and supplies; Allied morale |
| Spring Offensive failure | Losses of best troops; overstretched supply lines |
| Allied counter-attack | Amiens, the Hundred Days, combined-arms tactics |
| Blockade | Food shortages, strikes, falling morale |
| Allies collapse | Bulgaria, Ottomans, Austria-Hungary sue for peace |
| Revolution | Kiel Mutiny; Kaiser abdicates 9 November |

## Worked example: a (b) answer in three steps

*Explain why the Gallipoli campaign failed.* **[6]**

- **Reason 1 + evidence + link:** the naval attack of March 1915 failed against mines, which warned the Turks and gave them time to strengthen defences.
- **Reason 2 + evidence + link:** landings were badly planned -- troops landed on narrow beaches below cliffs and could not advance inland.
- **Reason 3 + evidence + link:** Turkish defenders, well led by commanders such as Mustafa Kemal, held the heights, while heat and disease weakened the Allies.

## Exam traps

- Writing about the causes of the war (not in this Depth Study).
- Calling the Armistice the Treaty of Versailles.
- Describing trenches without explaining why they caused stalemate.
- Forgetting the African and Asian case studies in key question 2.
- Giving one-sided (c) answers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who won Jutland?**
Tactically, Germany sank more ships; strategically, Britain kept control of the North Sea and the blockade.

**How many marks are the three parts?**
(a) 4, (b) 6, (c) 10 -- 20 marks for the Section B question.

**What should a (c) conclusion do?**
Give a clear judgement about the factor in the question compared with at least one other factor, with a reason.

## Self-test

1. Where was the BEF's first battle? *Mons.*
2. When were tanks first used? *The Somme, September 1916.*
3. Who led South African forces in South West Africa? *Louis Botha.*
4. Which Japanese action captured a German base in China? *The siege of Tsingtao, 1914.*
5. Why was 8 August 1918 a turning point? *The Allied attack at Amiens broke German morale.*
