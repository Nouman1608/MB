Cambridge O Level Computer Science (2210) combines two kinds of learning. One is understanding computer systems: what happens to data inside a machine and across a network, and what hardware, software and the internet actually do. The other is problem-solving: designing algorithms, writing programs, organising data in databases and reasoning with logic. For examinations in 2026 to 2028 the course is assessed by two written papers that every candidate takes. Paper 1, Computer Systems, and Paper 2, Algorithms, Programming and Logic, are each 105 minutes long, each marked out of 75 and each worth 50% of the final grade.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus content is split into ten topics, and the split between the papers is clean: the first six belong to Paper 1 and the last four to Paper 2.

### Content for Paper 1: Computer Systems

| Topic | Name | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data representation | How numbers, characters and media are held as data |
| 2 | Data transmission | Types and methods of data transmission; methods of error detection; encryption |
| 3 | Hardware | Computer architecture; input and output devices; data storage; network hardware |
| 4 | Software | The programs that make hardware useful |
| 5 | The internet and its uses | How the internet operates and what it is used for |
| 6 | Automated and emerging technologies | Automated systems and newer technologies |

These topics reward clear explanation. Much of the challenge is describing a process or a component accurately and in the right terms.

### Content for Paper 2: Algorithms, Programming and Logic

| Topic | Name |
|---|---|
| 7 | Algorithm design and problem-solving |
| 8 | Programming |
| 9 | Databases |
| 10 | Boolean logic |

These topics reward method. You are expected to produce and interpret solutions, not only to describe them.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Length | Marks | Share of grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Computer Systems | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Algorithms, Programming and Logic | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% |

What this means in practice:

- There is a single route through the qualification. Both papers are compulsory and externally assessed, and there is no coursework.
- The course is not tiered. All candidates take the same papers and grades are reported on the A* to E scale.
- Assessment is linear, with both papers sat at the end of the course.
- Paper 1 is made up of short-answer and structured questions on Topics 1 to 6.
- Paper 2 uses short-answer, structured and scenario-based questions on Topics 7 to 10, so expect to meet a described situation and work out a solution for it.
- Calculators are not allowed in either paper.

This O Level shares its ten-topic content and two-paper structure with Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science (0478). The difference in the qualification itself is the grading scale, which for 2210 runs from A* to E.

## How the parts fit together

It is tempting to treat Paper 1 as "the theory paper" and Paper 2 as "the coding paper", but the content overlaps in useful ways.

- **Binary links the papers.** Data representation in Topic 1 is the foundation for Boolean logic in Topic 10, where every input and output is a one or a zero. Students who are fluent in binary usually find logic circuits far less abstract.
- **Logic links to programming.** Every condition in an IF statement or a loop is a Boolean expression, so Topic 10 and Topic 8 support one another.
- **Algorithms come before code.** Topic 7 is about planning and representing a solution, and Topic 8 is about implementing it. Being able to read and correct someone else's algorithm is as important as writing your own.
- **Systems knowledge informs scenarios.** A scenario-based question in Paper 2 may describe a real system; understanding how hardware, networks and data storage behave helps you make sensible design choices.

Running through all ten topics are the same habits: precise vocabulary, careful reading of what a question actually asks, and patient, step-by-step working.

## Planning your study

The course works best when the two papers' content is studied in parallel rather than in sequence.

- **Theory strand.** Work through Topics 1 to 6 in order. Data representation comes first because later topics assume it; transmission and hardware follow; software, the internet and emerging technologies close the strand.
- **Problem-solving strand.** From the first weeks, set aside regular time for Topic 7 and Topic 8. Short, frequent programming sessions build fluency far better than a late, intensive block.
- **Joining the strands.** Once binary is secure, study Boolean logic. Once you can write conditions and loops confidently, move on to databases.

A workable year plan has three phases. In the opening phase, cover Topics 1 to 3 and the basics of algorithms and programming. In the middle phase, finish Topics 4 to 6, extend your programming and add Topic 9. In the final phase, complete Topic 10, revisit every topic, and move to full past papers under timed conditions. The course's exam-preparation page sets out how to approach each paper, so use it once you are ready for paper-level practice.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is assessed in Cambridge O Level Computer Science 2210?**
Two compulsory written papers: Paper 1 Computer Systems on Topics 1 to 6, and Paper 2 Algorithms, Programming and Logic on Topics 7 to 10. Each is 105 minutes, 75 marks and 50% of the grade.

**Is there coursework in O Level Computer Science 2210?**
No. The two externally assessed papers make up the whole qualification.

**How is O Level 2210 related to IGCSE 0478?**
They share the same ten topics and the same two-paper structure. O Level 2210 is graded A* to E.

**Are calculators allowed?**
No. Neither paper permits a calculator, so practise conversions and calculations by hand.

**When does this syllabus apply?**
This page describes the 2026-2028 syllabus, first assessed in 2026.
