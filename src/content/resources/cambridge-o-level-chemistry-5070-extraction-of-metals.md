This guide covers subtopic **9.6 Extraction of metals** of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028 series). [Alloys and Extraction of Metals](/resources/alloys-and-extraction-of-metals/) lists the blast furnace equations and the aluminium half-equations; this guide adds the redox analysis of each step, the reasoning examiners ask for (why each raw material is used, why cryolite, why the anodes wear away) and reacting-mass calculations for both extractions.

## What the syllabus asks

1. **Describe** the ease of obtaining metals from their ores, related to the position of the metal in the **reactivity series**.
2. **Describe** the extraction of iron from **hematite** in the **blast furnace**, including **symbol equations for each step**, limited to: the burning of carbon (coke) to provide heat and produce carbon dioxide; the reduction of carbon dioxide to carbon monoxide; the reduction of iron(III) oxide by carbon monoxide; the thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate (limestone) to produce calcium oxide; the formation of slag.
3. **Describe** the extraction of aluminium from purified **bauxite / aluminium oxide**, including the **role of cryolite**, why the **carbon anodes need to be regularly replaced**, and the **reactions at the electrodes, including ionic half-equations**. Details of purifying bauxite are not required.

## Ease of extraction and the reactivity series

An **ore** is a rock containing a metal compound from which the metal can be extracted. Extraction is **reduction**: the metal compound loses oxygen (or the metal ions gain electrons). The more reactive a metal, the more strongly it holds on to oxygen in its compounds and the harder it is to reduce.

| Position in series | Examples | Method | Ease |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above carbon | K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al | Electrolysis of a molten compound | Hardest; needs large amounts of electricity |
| Below carbon | Zn, Fe, Cu | Heating the oxide with carbon (reduction) | Easier and cheaper |
| Very unreactive | Au (and some Ag) | Found uncombined as the metal | Easiest; only separation needed |

Carbon's position is the dividing line: carbon can remove oxygen only from the oxide of a metal **less** reactive than itself.

## Iron: the blast furnace

### Raw materials and their jobs

| Raw material | Main substance | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hematite | Iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃ | Source of iron |
| Coke | Carbon, C | Fuel (heat) and source of the reducing agent |
| Limestone | Calcium carbonate, CaCO₃ | Removes the sandy impurity (silicon dioxide) as slag |
| Hot air | Oxygen, O₂ | Burns the coke |

### The five steps, with equations

```
(a) C + O₂ → CO₂                     coke burns: exothermic, heats the furnace
(b) CO₂ + C → 2CO                    carbon dioxide reduced to carbon monoxide
(c) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂         iron(III) oxide reduced by carbon monoxide
(d) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂                limestone thermally decomposed
(e) CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃              slag (calcium silicate) formed
```

### Redox in each step

- **Step (b):** carbon dioxide **loses** oxygen, so it is reduced; carbon gains oxygen, so it is oxidised.
- **Step (c):** iron(III) oxide **loses** oxygen and is **reduced** to iron. Carbon monoxide **gains** oxygen and is **oxidised** to carbon dioxide. Carbon monoxide is therefore the **reducing agent**. In terms of oxidation numbers, iron falls from +3 to 0.
- **Step (e)** is not a redox reaction: it is a reaction between a basic oxide (calcium oxide) and an acidic oxide (silicon dioxide) to form a salt, which links to the oxide classification in subtopic 7.2.

The molten iron sinks to the bottom of the furnace. The molten slag is less dense, floats on top of the iron, and is run off separately. The iron produced still contains impurities, including carbon.

### Worked example: reacting masses

*Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be obtained from 1000 kg of iron(III) oxide. (Relative atomic masses: Fe = 56, O = 16.)*

- Mr of Fe₂O₃ = (2 × 56) + (3 × 16) = 160.
- 160 kg of Fe₂O₃ contains 2 × 56 = 112 kg of iron.
- Mass of iron = 1000 × 112 ÷ 160 = **700 kg**.

The same answer comes from the equation in step (c): one mole of Fe₂O₃ gives two moles of Fe.

## Aluminium: electrolysis

Aluminium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so heating its oxide with carbon cannot reduce it. Aluminium is extracted by **electrolysis** of aluminium oxide obtained from purified bauxite.

### The role of cryolite

Aluminium oxide has a melting point of over 2000 °C. Heating it to that temperature would need enormous amounts of energy. Instead, the aluminium oxide is **dissolved in molten cryolite**. The mixture is molten at a much lower temperature, so the cell runs at a lower temperature and uses less energy, which reduces cost. The molten mixture also conducts electricity well. Cryolite is **not a catalyst** and is not a source of aluminium for the process.

### Reactions at the electrodes

Both electrodes are made of carbon (graphite). In the molten electrolyte the ions are free to move: Al³⁺ ions move to the cathode and O²⁻ ions to the anode.

```
cathode (−):  Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al          reduction: aluminium ions gain electrons
anode (+):    2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻          oxidation: oxide ions lose electrons
overall:      2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂
```

Molten aluminium collects at the bottom of the cell and is tapped off.

### Why the carbon anodes must be replaced

The oxygen produced at the anode is at a very high temperature, and it **reacts with the carbon anodes**, forming carbon dioxide:

```
C + O₂ → CO₂
```

The anodes gradually burn away, so they must be replaced regularly. The cathode is not attacked in this way because aluminium, not oxygen, forms there.

### Worked example: reacting masses for aluminium

*Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium from 1000 kg of aluminium oxide. (Al = 27, O = 16.)*

- Mr of Al₂O₃ = (2 × 27) + (3 × 16) = 102.
- Mass of aluminium = 1000 × 54 ÷ 102 = **529 kg** (to 3 significant figures).

## Common mistakes

- **"Carbon monoxide is the fuel."** Coke is the fuel; carbon monoxide is the reducing agent.
- **"Limestone reduces the iron oxide."** Limestone decomposes to calcium oxide, which removes silicon dioxide as slag.
- **Unbalanced equations**: step (c) needs 3CO and 3CO₂; the anode half-equation needs 2O²⁻ and 4e⁻.
- **"Cryolite is a catalyst"** or "cryolite lowers the melting point of aluminium". It lowers the temperature needed to melt the **electrolyte** (aluminium oxide).
- **Swapping electrodes**: aluminium forms at the **cathode**, oxygen at the **anode**.
- **"The anodes dissolve."** They react with oxygen to form carbon dioxide.
- **Saying aluminium is extracted by carbon reduction** because it is a common metal.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** the blast furnace: give each step with its symbol equation, in order (a) to (e).
- **Explain** the method of extraction: compare the metal's position with carbon's.
- **State** the role of cryolite in one sentence about temperature and energy.
- **Explain** anode replacement: oxygen formed, reacts with carbon, carbon dioxide, anode used up.
- **Calculate**: show Mr, the ratio, then the answer with units.
