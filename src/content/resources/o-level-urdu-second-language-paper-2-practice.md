## Section A

**1.** State the total time, total marks, and weighting of Paper 2
within the qualification. **[3]**

**2.** Name the four exercises on Paper 2, and state the marks each
carries. **[4]**

**3.** State which exercises use Urdu-only questions and answers, and
which exercise uses an English question. **[2]**

## Section B

**4.** A candidate transforms a sentence from active to passive voice
as instructed in Exercise 1, but in doing so changes the sentence's
original meaning.

**(a)** Explain why this answer is unlikely to gain the mark. **[2]**
**(b)** State what Exercise 1 always requires a transformation to
preserve. **[1]**

**5.** In Exercise 2's cloze passage, a candidate selects an option
that is grammatically correct on its own but does not fit the meaning
of the surrounding sentence.

**(a)** Explain why this option is still likely to be marked wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what the candidate should do differently when
approaching each gap. **[2]**

**6.** A candidate writes a fluent, grammatically accurate 200-word
response for Exercise 3, but ignores the specified audience and format
given on the question paper.

**(a)** Explain why this response is likely to be capped regardless of
its language accuracy. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what the candidate should check before starting to
write. **[1]**

**7.** A candidate translates an English idiom word-for-word into
Urdu for Exercise 4, producing a literal but unnatural-sounding
sentence.

**(a)** Explain why this is marked down compared with a natural Urdu
equivalent. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest a technique for checking whether a translation sounds
natural. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why Exercises 3 and 4 should receive more revision time,
proportionally, than Exercises 1 and 2. **[2]**

**9.** A candidate assumes their revision notes for 3247 First Language
Urdu's Paper 2 will help them prepare for 3248 Second Language Urdu's
Paper 2.

**(a)** Explain why this assumption is mistaken. **[2]**
**(b)** State what each specification's Paper 2 actually tests. **[2]**

**10.** Name three named grammar points a candidate should build into
a personal checklist to prepare systematically for Exercise 2. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks, worth 50% of the qualification [3].

**2.** Exercise 1, Sentence transformation, 5 marks; Exercise 2,
Multiple-choice cloze passage, 10 marks; Exercise 3, Extended writing,
20 marks; Exercise 4, Translation, 15 marks [4].

**3.** Exercises 1 to 3 use Urdu-only questions and answers; Exercise
4's question is presented in English, with the answer given in Urdu
[2].

**4. (a)** This answer is unlikely to gain the mark because the
instruction in Exercise 1 is always to preserve the original meaning
while changing the required structure, not simply to alter the form of
the sentence [2].
**(b)** The original sentence's meaning [1].

**5. (a)** Because the cloze exercise tests grammatical usage in
context, not grammar as an abstract, isolated rule; an option that is
correct on its own but does not fit the surrounding sentence's meaning
does not satisfy what the exercise is actually testing [2].
**(b)** The candidate should read a full sentence around each gap
before choosing an option, rather than judging each option only on
whether it is grammatically valid in isolation [2].

**6. (a)** Because Exercise 3 specifies a purpose, format and audience
on the question paper, and ignoring these caps the achievable mark
regardless of how accurate the language itself is, since matching the
specified task is part of what is being assessed [2].
**(b)** The candidate should check the specified purpose, format and
intended audience before starting to write, and plan the response
around all three [1].

**7. (a)** Because a literal, word-for-word translation that produces
unnatural Urdu is marked down compared with an accurate translation
that also reads naturally in the target language; precision alone is
not enough if the result does not read as genuine Urdu [2].
**(b)** Reading the translation aloud as if it were a standalone Urdu
sentence, separate from the English original, to check whether it
sounds natural [1].

**8.** Because Exercises 3 and 4 together carry 35 of the paper's 50
marks, so revision time weighted toward extended writing and
translation practice reflects where most of the marks are actually
concentrated, rather than splitting effort evenly across all four
exercises [2].

**9. (a)** This assumption is mistaken because 3247 and 3248 are two
separate specifications with genuinely different Paper 2 content, even
though both share a similar Paper 1 [2].
**(b)** 3247's Paper 2 (Texts) tests literary set texts through an
unseen passage plus prescribed poetry and prose; 3248's Paper 2
(Grammar, Writing and Translation) tests applied language skills —
grammar in context, extended writing, and translation from English
into Urdu [2].

**10.** Any three of: verb conjugation, subject/verb agreement,
comparatives, superlatives, parts of speech, nouns, prepositions,
postpositions, articles, context-appropriate vocabulary [3].

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Because Exercises 3 and 4 carry 35 of the paper's 50 marks between
them, the most efficient use of revision time is clearing Exercises 1
and 2's grammar points quickly and confidently, then dedicating
sustained, repeated timed practice to extended writing and
translation. Build a personal checklist from the syllabus's own named
grammar points for Exercise 2, since every cloze gap is designed to
probe one of them specifically. For both Exercise 3 and Exercise 4,
the recurring theme across common mistakes is matching the task
exactly — the specified purpose, format and audience in Exercise 3,
and natural rather than literal Urdu in Exercise 4 — since these
requirements cap achievable marks independently of raw language
accuracy.
