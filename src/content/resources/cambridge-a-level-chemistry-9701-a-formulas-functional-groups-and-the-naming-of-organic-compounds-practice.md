## Section A

**1.** Give the systematic name of CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COCl. **[1]**

**2.** Give the structural formula of N-ethylpropanamide. **[1]**

**3.** State the molecular formula of 2,4,6-tribromophenol. **[1]**

**4.** Name the compound C₆H₅COOCH₂CH₃. **[1]**

**5.** Classify each amide as primary, secondary or tertiary: (i) CH₃CON(CH₂CH₃)₂; (ii) CH₃CH₂CONH₂; (iii) HCONHCH₃. **[2]**

**6.** Give the systematic name of the cyclic compound in which a six-membered carbon ring carries an –OH group and, on the adjacent ring carbon, a –CH₃ group. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Compound A has the structural formula CH₃CH(NH₂)COOH.

**(a)** Give the systematic name of A. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the two functional groups in A. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that A fits the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO₂. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest one chemical property of A that results from each functional group. **[2]**

**8.** A benzene ring carries three substituents: –COOH, –OH and –NO₂. The –OH is on the carbon next to the –COOH, and the –NO₂ is on the carbon opposite the –COOH.

**(a)** Explain which group determines the parent name. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the systematic name of the compound. **[2]**

**(c)** Give its molecular formula. **[1]**

**9.** Methylbenzene reacts with chlorine under two different sets of conditions to give compounds B and C, both of formula C₇H₇Cl. In B the chlorine is on the ring at the position next to the methyl group; in C the chlorine is on the side-chain.

**(a)** Name B and C. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the brackets in the name of C are important. **[1]**

**10.** Draw the skeletal formula of each compound, and give its molecular formula.

**(a)** butanoyl chloride **[2]**

**(b)** cyclopentene **[2]**

**11.** The compound CH₃CONHC₆H₅ is formed when phenylamine reacts with ethanoyl chloride.

**(a)** Name the compound. **[1]**

**(b)** State the type of amide it is and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Give its molecular formula. **[1]**

**12.** For each name below, decide whether it is correct. If it is not, give the correct name.

**(a)** 3-chloro-4-methylbenzoic acid, for a benzoic acid with –CH₃ at C4 and –Cl at C3 **[1]**

**(b)** 5-nitrophenol, for a phenol with –NO₂ on the carbon next-but-one to the –OH **[1]**

**(c)** methyl propanoate, for CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ **[1]**

**13.** Ethanoyl chloride and ethanamide have similar molecular sizes, but ethanamide is a solid at room temperature while ethanoyl chloride is a liquid that fumes in moist air. Explain both observations in terms of the functional groups. **[3]**

**14.** Three esters with the molecular formula C₄H₈O₂ have straight chains on both sides of the ester link.

**(a)** Give the structural formula and name of each. **[3]**

**(b)** For one of your esters, name the acyl chloride and the alcohol that would react to make it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2-methylbutanoyl chloride [1].

**2.** CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ [1].

**3.** C₆H₃Br₃O [1]. Three ring carbons carry Br and one carries OH, so only two ring hydrogens remain; the third H is on the O.

**4.** Ethyl benzoate [1].

**5.** (i) tertiary; (ii) primary; (iii) secondary [2: all three correct; 1 for two correct].

**6.** 2-methylcyclohexanol [1]. The carbon carrying –OH is C1, and numbering towards the methyl group gives it locant 2 rather than 6.

**7. (a)** 2-aminopropanoic acid [1].

**(b)** Amine (amino) group and carboxylic acid (carboxyl) group [1].

**(c)** Molecular formula C₃H₇NO₂; with n = 3, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO₂ = C₃H₇NO₂ [1].

**(d)** –COOH: acidic, e.g. reacts with NaOH or with carbonates to give CO₂ [1]; –NH₂: basic, e.g. reacts with acids such as HCl [1].

**8. (a)** –COOH has the highest priority, so the compound is named as a benzoic acid, with that carbon as C1 [1].

**(b)** –OH at C2 and –NO₂ at C4 [1]; 2-hydroxy-4-nitrobenzoic acid (prefixes in alphabetical order) [1].

**(c)** C₇H₅NO₅ [1].

**9. (a)** B: 2-chloromethylbenzene (1-chloro-2-methylbenzene) [1]; C: (chloromethyl)benzene [1].

**(b)** They show that "chloromethyl" is one substituent, –CH₂Cl, on the ring, so the Cl is on the side-chain and not on the ring [1].

**10. (a)** Zigzag of three carbons, then a carbon with a double-bonded O and a Cl [1]; C₄H₇OCl [1].

**(b)** A pentagon with one double bond in the ring [1]; C₅H₈ [1].

**11. (a)** N-phenylethanamide [1].

**(b)** Secondary amide [1]: the nitrogen carries one carbon group (the phenyl group) and one hydrogen [1].

**(c)** C₈H₉NO [1].

**12. (a)** Correct: substituents at 3 and 4, listed alphabetically (chloro before methyl) [1].

**(b)** Incorrect: numbering must give the lowest locant, so it is 3-nitrophenol [1].

**(c)** Incorrect: the acid part has four carbons, so it is methyl butanoate [1].

**13.** Ethanamide (primary amide) has N–H bonds, so its molecules form many hydrogen bonds with each other [1], which need more energy to overcome, so it has a higher melting point and is solid [1]. Ethanoyl chloride has no N–H or O–H, so no hydrogen bonding between its molecules; its –COCl group is rapidly hydrolysed by water vapour, giving fumes of HCl [1].

**14. (a)** CH₃CH₂COOCH₃, methyl propanoate [1]; CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, ethyl ethanoate [1]; HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃, propyl methanoate [1].

**(b)** Any one matching pair, both named correctly: methyl propanoate from propanoyl chloride and methanol; ethyl ethanoate from ethanoyl chloride and ethanol; propyl methanoate from methanoyl chloride and propan-1-ol [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming acyl chlorides as "ethanoic chloride" or similar.
- Numbering an aromatic ring from a carbon other than the one carrying the parent group.
- Putting prefixes in numerical rather than alphabetical order.
- Naming esters with the acid part first.
- Miscounting hydrogens on substituted ring carbons when giving a molecular formula.
- Confusing (chloromethyl)benzene with 2-chloromethylbenzene.
