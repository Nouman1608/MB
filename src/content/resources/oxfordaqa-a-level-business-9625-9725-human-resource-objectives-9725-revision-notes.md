These notes condense **3.2.2.1 Human resource objectives** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [human resource objectives study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725/) has the full workforce plan example; the [human resource objectives practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725-practice/) test it. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **HR objectives:** number and skill level of employees; employee engagement; employee retention.
- **HR planning actions:** recruitment, training, redeployment, retention, redundancies.
- **Recruitment stages:** job analysis → job description → person specification.
- **Recruitment sources:** internal or external.
- **Selection methods:** CV, application form, interview, references, testing, assessment centre.
- **Contracts:** full time, part time, temporary, permanent, zero hours.
- **Training:** induction, off the job, on the job.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Human resource objective | A target for the workforce that supports corporate objectives |
| Employee engagement | How committed employees are to the business and its goals |
| Employee retention | Keeping the employees the business wants to keep |
| Human resource planning | Forecasting the workforce needed and planning how to provide it |
| Redeployment | Moving existing employees to different jobs or locations |
| Redundancy | Dismissal because the job is no longer needed |
| Job analysis | Studying the tasks and skills a job involves |
| Job description | Document setting out a job's title, duties and responsibilities |
| Person specification | Document setting out the qualifications, skills and qualities the job-holder needs |
| Internal recruitment | Filling a vacancy from existing employees |
| External recruitment | Filling a vacancy from outside the business |
| Assessment centre | A day or more of tasks, tests and interviews used to compare candidates |
| Zero hours contract | No guaranteed hours; staff work only when offered and accepting work |
| Induction | Training that introduces new employees to the business |

## Workforce plan method

```
Expected leavers  = current staff × expected labour turnover
Staff remaining   = current staff − expected leavers
Recruitment need  = staff required − staff remaining
Skill gap         = skilled staff required − (skilled staff now − skilled leavers)
```

Close the gap by recruiting, training internal staff, redeploying, improving retention, or (if overstaffed) redundancies.

**Quick example:** 60 staff, 20% turnover expected, 70 needed next year. Leavers 12; remaining 48; recruit 22.

## Value and limits of HR planning

- **Value:** staff ready on time; avoids rushed hiring and overstaffing; time to train internally; links HR to the corporate plan.
- **Limits:** relies on forecasts of sales and turnover; unexpected resignations; plans need updating.

## Internal vs external: when each fits

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Skills exist inside | New skills needed |
| Speed and low cost matter | Wider choice matters |
| Continuity and motivation through promotion | Fresh ideas or culture change wanted |
| Leaves another vacancy | Costs more; candidate less well known |

## Selection methods: what each tests

- **CV / application form**: qualifications and experience on paper; forms make comparison easier.
- **Interview**: communication and fit; open to bias.
- **References**: past reliability; may be vague.
- **Testing**: specific skills objectively.
- **Assessment centre**: a rounded view over several tasks; expensive.

## Contracts and HR objectives

- Permanent and full time support **engagement** and **retention**, but raise fixed labour costs.
- Part time, temporary and zero hours give **flexibility**, but can weaken engagement and retention.

## Training types

| Type | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|
| Induction | Quicker settling in; fewer early leavers | Time before new staff are productive |
| On the job | Cheap; relevant | Can pass on bad habits; slows the trainer |
| Off the job | Wider expertise; qualifications | Costly; output lost while away |

## HR objectives and corporate objectives

| Corporate objective | Likely HR objective | HR planning action |
|---|---|---|
| Growth into new markets | Raise numbers and new skills | External recruitment; off-the-job training |
| Cost reduction | Lower numbers; keep key skills | Redeployment; natural wastage; redundancies as a last resort |
| Better service quality | Raise engagement and retention | Induction; on-the-job training; permanent contracts |

A plan that recruits heavily while the corporate objective is cost reduction shows the functions are not aligned: examiners reward spotting this.

## Exam traps

- Job description = **job**; person specification = **person**.
- HR planning is wider than recruitment: name the five actions.
- Use the case's turnover figure when working out recruitment need.
- Link every method to one of the three HR objectives.
- Do not recommend an expensive method (assessment centre) for a short, low-skill role without justification.

## Self-test

1. State the three HR objectives named in the specification.
2. A firm has 200 staff, expects 12% labour turnover and needs 220 staff next year. How many must it recruit?
3. What is redeployment?
4. Distinguish a job description from a person specification.
5. Give one advantage of internal recruitment.
6. Give one disadvantage of external recruitment.
7. Which selection method gives the most rounded view of graduate candidates?
8. Give one reason a business might use references.
9. State one disadvantage of zero hours contracts for the business.
10. What is induction training?
11. Give one disadvantage of off-the-job training.
12. Give one limitation of HR planning.

### Answers

1. Number and skill level of employees; employee engagement; employee retention.
2. Leavers 200 × 12% = 24; remaining 176; recruit 220 − 176 = **44**.
3. Moving existing employees to different jobs or locations.
4. The job description sets out the job's duties; the person specification sets out the qualities of the person needed to do it.
5. Quicker and cheaper; candidate already knows the business; motivates through promotion (any one).
6. More costly and slower; candidate less well known (any one).
7. An **assessment centre**.
8. To get an independent view of the candidate's past reliability and performance.
9. Lower commitment and engagement, leading to higher labour turnover (any valid point).
10. Training that introduces new employees to the business, its rules and colleagues.
11. Costly, and output is lost while staff are away (any one).
12. It depends on forecasts that may be wrong (any valid point).
