These questions practise section 5.1.1, How fast?, of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. They test every outcome from 5.1.1(a) to (k), including the PAG9 and PAG10 techniques. H432 is the full A level. Questions 4 and 10 are longer problems.

Stuck? Use the [How fast? study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-how-fast/) or the [How fast? revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-how-fast-revision-notes/). Use R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by each term.

**(a)** The order of reaction with respect to a reactant. **[1]**
**(b)** The half-life of a reactant. **[1]**
**(c)** The rate-determining step. **[1]**

**2.** For each rate equation, state the overall order and deduce the units of k.

**(a)** rate = k[R][S] **[2]**
**(b)** rate = k[T]²[V] **[2]**

**3.** Substances M, N and Z react in solution. Initial rates at a fixed temperature:

| Run | [M] / mol dm⁻³ | [N] / mol dm⁻³ | [Z] / mol dm⁻³ | Rate, t = 0 / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.100 | 0.200 | 0.050 | 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.300 | 0.200 | 0.050 | 2.7 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 3 | 0.100 | 0.400 | 0.050 | 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 4 | 0.100 | 0.200 | 0.150 | 8.1 × 10⁻⁴ |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to M, N and Z. Explain your reasoning. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the rate equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate k, with units. **[2]**
**(d)** Work out the starting rate when [M] = 0.250, [N] = 0.100 and [Z] = 0.080 mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**4.** Purple crystal violet, CV⁺, fades as it reacts with excess hydroxide ions. A colorimeter follows the reaction. Starting [CV⁺] = 2.00 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³, absorbance 0.880.

| t / s | 0 | 80 | 160 | 240 | 320 | 480 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.880 | 0.622 | 0.440 | 0.311 | 0.220 | 0.110 |

**(a)** Describe how absorbance readings can be converted into concentrations of CV⁺. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the data to show that the reaction is first order with respect to CV⁺. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the rate constant from the half-life. **[2]**
**(d)** A tangent to the curve at t = 160 s passes through (0 s, 0.745) and (391 s, 0.000). Calculate the rate at 160 s in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. **[3]**

**5.** Compound W decomposes on a hot metal surface. [W] falls in a straight line from 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ at t = 0 to 0.0180 mol dm⁻³ at t = 160 s.

**(a)** State the order with respect to W and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate constant and give its units. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate when W would be used up. **[1]**

**6.** Initial rates for 2N₂O₅(g) → 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g) at one temperature:

| [N₂O₅] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.30 | 0.60 | 0.90 | 1.20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ | 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ |

**(a)** Describe the shape of the rate–concentration graph and deduce the order. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine k from the gradient. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the half-life of N₂O₅. **[2]**

**7.** A gas-phase reaction has the overall equation A + 2B → AB₂ and the rate equation rate = k[B]².

**(a)** Suggest a two-step mechanism for this reaction, showing which step is rate-determining. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why A does not appear in the rate equation. **[1]**

**8.** A clock reaction measures the time, t, for a fixed small amount of product to form at different [Y].

| [Y] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.040 | 0.080 | 0.100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| t / s | 60 | 30 | 24 |

**(a)** Explain why the reciprocal of t is proportional to the initial rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the order with respect to Y, showing your working. **[2]**
**(c)** State two variables to keep constant. **[2]**

**9.** A first-order reaction has Ea = 96.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and A = 2.5 × 10¹² s⁻¹.

**(a)** Use k = Ae^(–Ea/RT) to calculate k at 300 K. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate k at 310 K. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the factor by which k increases between 300 K and 310 K. **[1]**
**(d)** Use the Boltzmann distribution to explain why heating increases k. **[3]**

**10.** Rate constants for a second-order reaction:

| T / K | 293 | 303 | 313 | 323 | 333 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k / dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ | 0.0595 | 0.131 | 0.276 | 0.554 | 1.07 |
| (1/T) / 10⁻³ K⁻¹ | 3.413 | 3.300 | | 3.096 | |
| ln k | –2.82 | –2.03 | | –0.59 | |

**(a)** Complete the two missing columns. **[2]**
**(b)** Show how the Arrhenius equation predicts a linear plot of ln k versus 1/T. **[1]**
**(c)** All five points fit one line. From the end points, calculate Ea in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the pre-exponential factor, A, with units. **[3]**


## Answers

**1. (a)** The index n in [A]^n within the rate equation, for reactant A [1].
**(b)** The time taken for its concentration to fall to half of its starting value [1].
**(c)** The slowest step in a mechanism, which controls the overall rate [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Time for half the reaction" is too vague; the definition needs concentration and half.

**2. (a)** Overall order 2 [1]; k = rate ÷ [R][S] gives **dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Overall order 3 [1]; k = rate ÷ [T]²[V] gives **dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the units cancelling; inverted powers, such as mol² dm⁻⁶ s⁻¹, lose the mark.

**3. (a)** Runs 1 and 2: [M] × 3, rate × 3, so **first order in M** [1]. Runs 1 and 3: [N] × 2, rate unchanged, so **zero order in N** [1]. Runs 1 and 4: [Z] × 3, rate × 9 = 3², so **second order in Z** [1].
**(b)** **rate = k[M][Z]²** [1]
**(c)** k = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.100 × 0.050²) = **0.36** [1] **dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** rate = 0.36 × 0.250 × 0.080² = **5.8 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the runs you compare; bare orders with no reasoning earn little if one is wrong.

**4. (a)** Measure the absorbance of standard CV⁺ solutions of known concentration, using the same filter [1]. Plot a calibration graph and read concentrations from it [1].
**(b)** 0.880 → 0.440 takes 160 s; 0.440 → 0.220 takes 160 s; 0.220 → 0.110 takes 160 s [1]. The half-life is constant, so the reaction is first order in CV⁺ [1].
**(c)** k = 0.693 ÷ 160 [1] = **4.33 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Gradient = 0.745 ÷ 391 = 1.91 × 10⁻³ absorbance units s⁻¹ [1]. Each unit of absorbance is 2.00 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 0.880 = 2.27 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [1]. Rate = 1.91 × 10⁻³ × 2.27 × 10⁻⁵ = **4.33 × 10⁻⁸ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote at least two successive half-lives with values; "the half-life is constant" alone is not evidence.

**5. (a)** **Zero order** [1]: a straight line means the rate stays constant as [W] falls [1].
**(b)** rate = (0.0500 − 0.0180) ÷ 160 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴; rate = k, so k = **2.0 × 10⁻⁴** [1] **mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** t = 0.0500 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **250 s** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For zero order, k has the units of rate; s⁻¹ here loses the units mark.

**6. (a)** A straight line through the origin [1], so the reaction is **first order** in N₂O₅ [1].
**(b)** Gradient = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 1.20 [1] = k = **5.0 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** t½ = 0.693 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **1.39 × 10³ s** (about 23 minutes) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the gradient from the whole line, not one point, and give k with units.

**7. (a)** Step 1 (slow, rate-determining): B + B → B₂ [1]. Step 2 (fast): A + B₂ → AB₂ [1]. The steps add up to A + 2B → AB₂ and the intermediate B₂ cancels [1].
**(b)** A reacts only in a fast step after the rate-determining step, so changing [A] does not change the rate (zero order) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that your steps add up to the overall equation; a correct slow step alone may not earn full credit.

**8. (a)** So little product forms in time t that the rate is almost constant [1]. Rate ≈ fixed amount ÷ t, so rate ∝ 1/t [1].
**(b)** 1/t = 0.0167, 0.0333 and 0.0417 s⁻¹, a ratio of 1 : 2 : 2.5 [1]. [Y] is in the ratio 1 : 2 : 2.5, so the rate is proportional to [Y]: **first order** [1].
**(c)** Any two of: temperature [1]; concentrations of the other reactants [1]; total volume of the mixture. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Keep the amounts the same" is too vague; name each variable precisely.

**9. (a)** Ea/RT = 96 000 ÷ (8.314 × 300) = 38.49 [1]. k = 2.5 × 10¹² × e^(–38.49) = **4.81 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Ea/RT = 37.25; k = **1.67 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Factor = 1.67 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 4.81 × 10⁻⁵ = **3.46** (3.47 from the rounded values) [1]
**(d)** The Boltzmann distribution shifts to higher energies [1]. More molecules have energy ≥ Ea [1]. More collisions per second succeed; concentrations are unchanged, so k must increase [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert Ea to J mol⁻¹ before using R; 96.0 in the exponent gives k close to A.

**10. (a)** 313 K: 1/T = 3.195 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = –1.29 [1]. 333 K: 1/T = 3.003 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = 0.07 [1].
**(b)** ln k = –Ea/RT + ln A has the form y = mx + c, with –Ea/R as the constant gradient and ln A as the intercept [1].
**(c)** Gradient = (0.07 − (–2.82)) ÷ (3.003 × 10⁻³ − 3.413 × 10⁻³) = –7.05 × 10³ K [1]. Ea = –gradient × R = 7.05 × 10³ × 8.314 [1] = **58.6 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** ln A = ln k + (Ea/R)(1/T) = –2.82 + 7.05 × 10³ × 3.413 × 10⁻³ [1] = 21.24 [1]. A = e^21.24 = **1.67 × 10⁹ dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded gradient into (d); small rounding in ln A changes A noticeably.


## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking orders from equation coefficients instead of from the data.
- Treating a factor of 3 or 2.5 as a doubling.
- Units of k with the powers of mol and dm inverted.
- Forgetting to convert absorbance to concentration.
- Claiming first order from a single half-life.
- A slow step containing a zero-order reactant.
- Using T in °C, or kJ inside Ea/RT.
