These notes condense sub-topic **5(b) Density and pressure** (statements 5.3 to 5.7) of Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE Physics 4PH1, Issue 4. Full explanations and five worked examples are in the
[Density and pressure study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-density-and-pressure/); exam-style questions
are in the [Density and pressure practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-density-and-pressure-practice/).

## Equations (all must be recalled)

```
ρ = m / V            m = ρ × V            V = m / ρ
p = F / A            F = p × A            A = F / p
p = h × ρ × g        h = p / (ρ × g)      (p = pressure DIFFERENCE over vertical height h)
```

Units: ρ in kg/m³, m in kg, V in m³, p in Pa (N/m²), F in N, A in m², h in m, g in N/kg.

## Density

- Density belongs to the **material**: cutting a block in half halves mass and volume, density unchanged.
- 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water: 1000 kg/m³.

## Density practical (5.4)

| Sample | Mass | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Regular solid | Top-pan balance | Measure length, width, height (ruler); multiply |
| Irregular solid | Top-pan balance | Displacement: rise in water level in a measuring cylinder, or overflow from a displacement can into a cylinder |
| Liquid | Balance: (cylinder + liquid) − (empty cylinder), or tare the balance | Measuring cylinder, read at the bottom of the meniscus, eye level |

**Better results:** larger samples; repeat and average; read the meniscus at eye level; remove air bubbles from a
submerged object; measure each dimension in several places.

## Pressure on surfaces

- Same force, smaller area → bigger pressure (knife edge, needle, nail point, narrow heel).
- Same force, bigger area → smaller pressure (snowshoes, tractor tyres, wide foundations, skis).
- Force must be the component at right angles to the surface; for an object resting on the floor, that is its weight.

## Pressure in fluids

- At a point in a liquid or gas at rest, pressure acts **equally in all directions** (5.6), because particles move
  randomly and hit surfaces from every direction.
- Pressure increases with **depth** and with **density** of the fluid.
- It does **not** depend on the shape or width of the container.
- Total pressure under a liquid = pressure at the surface (usually atmospheric) + h × ρ × g.

**Evidence:** holes at the same depth round a bottle spurt equally; lower holes spurt further.

## Two-step pattern

```
step 1:  p = h × ρ × g        (pressure from depth)
step 2:  F = p × A            (force on a window, hatch or wall)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Density | Mass per unit volume of a material, ρ = m/V |
| Pressure | Force acting at right angles to a surface, per unit area, p = F/A |
| Pascal (Pa) | Unit of pressure; 1 Pa = 1 N/m² |
| Pressure difference | Difference in pressure between two points in a fluid, h × ρ × g |
| Displacement | Using the rise in water level to find the volume of an object put into it |
| Meniscus | Curved liquid surface in a cylinder; read the bottom of it at eye level |
| Fluid | A liquid or a gas: a substance that can flow |

## Exam traps

- cm² not converted: 1 cm² = 1 × 10⁻⁴ m².
- Using total depth from the bottom of a tank instead of depth below the surface.
- Using a slanted length rather than the vertical height in h × ρ × g.
- Adding atmospheric pressure when the question asks for the pressure "due to the water" alone, or leaving it out when
  it asks for the total.
- Saying liquid pressure acts only downwards.
- Using the final cylinder reading, not the rise, as the volume of a stone.
- Forgetting to subtract the container's mass from a liquid's mass.

## Self-test

Take g = 10 N/kg.

1. A 0.60 kg object has a volume of 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Calculate its density.
2. Calculate the mass of 0.50 m³ of water.
3. Iron has a density of 7800 kg/m³. Calculate the volume of a 3.9 kg iron block.
4. Describe how to measure the volume of a small irregular stone.
5. Calculate the pressure when a 900 N force acts on 0.030 m².
6. What area is needed to keep the pressure from a 1200 N load down to 4000 Pa?
7. Calculate the pressure difference between the surface and a depth of 12 m in water.
8. Atmospheric pressure is 100 kPa. Calculate the total pressure at 12 m depth in water.
9. Why does water spurt further from a hole near the bottom of a bottle than from one near the top?
10. In which directions does pressure act at a point in a liquid at rest?
11. At what depth in an oil of density 850 kg/m³ is the pressure difference 17 000 Pa?
12. Explain why camels' wide feet help them to walk on soft sand.

**Answers:** 1. ρ = 0.60 ÷ 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ = 2500 kg/m³. 2. m = 1000 × 0.50 = 500 kg. 3. V = 3.9 ÷ 7800 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³.
4. Part-fill a measuring cylinder with water and record the volume; lower the stone in so it is fully under water;
record the new volume; the stone's volume is the difference. 5. p = 900 ÷ 0.030 = 30 000 Pa. 6. A = 1200 ÷ 4000 =
0.30 m². 7. p = 12 × 1000 × 10 = 120 000 Pa. 8. 100 + 120 = 220 kPa. 9. The pressure is greater at greater depth,
because there is more water above pushing down. 10. Equally in all directions. 11. h = 17 000 ÷ (850 × 10) = 2.0 m.
12. A wide foot spreads the camel's weight over a large area, so the pressure on the sand is smaller and the feet do
not sink in as far.
