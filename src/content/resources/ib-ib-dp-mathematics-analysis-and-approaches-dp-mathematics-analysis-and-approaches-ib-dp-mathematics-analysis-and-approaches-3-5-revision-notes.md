These notes condense **section 3.5** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: the unit circle definitions of cos θ and sin θ, tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, exact values of 0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2 and their multiples, and the ambiguous case of the sine rule. It is SL content, examined at SL and HL. Explanations are in the [unit circle study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-5/); exam-style questions are in the [unit circle practice set](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-5-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Definitions**

- Rotate from (1, 0) through θ (anticlockwise if θ > 0, clockwise if θ < 0).
- The point reached on the unit circle is (cos θ, sin θ).
- tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, undefined where cos θ = 0 (θ = π/2 + kπ).
- tan θ is the gradient of the radius OP.
- On a circle of radius r: (r cos θ, r sin θ).
- Ratios repeat every 2π: θ and θ + 2kπ give the same point.

**Signs by quadrant**

- First: all positive. Second: sin only. Third: tan only. Fourth: cos only.
- On the axes: cos 0 = 1, sin(π/2) = 1, cos π = −1, sin(3π/2) = −1; the other ratio is 0.

**Exact values (from the two special triangles)**

- Half an equilateral triangle of side 2: sides 1, √3, 2.
  - sin(π/6) = 1/2, cos(π/6) = √3/2, tan(π/6) = √3/3.
  - sin(π/3) = √3/2, cos(π/3) = 1/2, tan(π/3) = √3.
- Isosceles right-angled triangle with sides 1, 1, √2:
  - sin(π/4) = cos(π/4) = √2/2, tan(π/4) = 1.

**Any multiple: three steps**

1. Locate the point (reduce by 2π; note the quadrant).
2. Find the reference angle to the x-axis.
3. Apply the sign of the quadrant.

**Line through the origin**

- Angle θ with the positive x-axis: y = x tan θ.
- Gradient m: angle θ with tan θ = m.

**Ambiguous case of the sine rule (A acute, a opposite A, b adjacent)**

- a < b sin A: no triangle.
- a = b sin A: one right-angled triangle.
- b sin A < a < b: two triangles (B and 180° − B).
- a ≥ b: one triangle.
- Check every obtuse candidate with the angle sum.
- Cosine-rule check: the third side solves a quadratic; two positive roots means two triangles.

**Method: an ambiguous-case question**

1. Label the given angle A, the side opposite it a, and the other given side b.
2. Work out b sin A, the shortest possible length for a.
3. Compare a with b sin A and with b to decide how many triangles there are.
4. If two, find the acute angle from the sine rule, then 180° minus it.
5. Check each against the angle sum, and complete each triangle separately.

**Two-line reminder**

- AB = 10, BC = 7.5, A = 35°: 10 sin 35° = 5.74 < 7.5 < 10, so two triangles.
- sin C = 0.7648 gives C = 49.9° or 130.1°, and AC = 13.0 cm or 3.36 cm.

**Multiples you should place instantly**

- Multiples of π/6: 5π/6, 7π/6, 11π/6 have reference angle π/6.
- Multiples of π/4: 3π/4, 5π/4, 7π/4 have reference angle π/4.
- Multiples of π/3: 2π/3, 4π/3, 5π/3 have reference angle π/3.
- Large or negative angles: add or subtract 2π until the angle lies between 0 and 2π.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unit circle | Circle of radius 1 centred at the origin |
| Reference angle | Acute angle between OP and the x-axis |
| Quadrant | One of the four regions cut out by the axes |
| Exact value | A value written with integers, fractions and surds |
| Ambiguous case | Two sides and a non-included angle that allow two triangles |
| Coterminal angles | Angles differing by a multiple of 2π, which give the same point |

## Exam traps

- tan(π/2) is undefined, not 0 or 1.
- Negative angles rotate clockwise.
- The reference angle is to the x-axis, never the y-axis.
- sin(π/6) = 1/2, not √3/2; draw the triangle if unsure.
- In the ambiguous case, quoting only the calculator's acute angle.
- Keeping an obtuse angle whose sum with the given angle exceeds 180°.
- Decimal exact values on Paper 1 lose the accuracy mark.

## Self-test

1. Write down the coordinates of the point at angle 2π/3 on the unit circle.
2. Find the exact value of sin(5π/4).
3. Find the exact value of cos(−π/3).
4. Find the exact value of tan(17π/6).
5. Write down sin 3π.
6. For which θ in 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π is tan θ undefined?
7. Find the equation of the line through the origin at angle 3π/4 to the positive x-axis.
8. Find the angle the line y = (√3/3)x makes with the positive x-axis.
9. P(5/13, −12/13) is on the unit circle at angle θ. Find tan θ and the quadrant.
10. Find the point at angle π/6 on the circle of radius 4 centred at the origin.
11. A = 30°, b = 10 and a = 6. How many triangles are possible?
12. A = 30°, b = 10 and a = 4. How many triangles are possible?

### Answers

1. (−1/2, √3/2).
2. Third quadrant, reference π/4: −√2/2.
3. cos is unchanged by the reflection in the x-axis: 1/2.
4. 17π/6 − 2π = 5π/6, second quadrant: −√3/3.
5. 3π is the same point as π, (−1, 0): sin 3π = 0.
6. θ = π/2 and θ = 3π/2.
7. tan(3π/4) = −1, so y = −x.
8. tan θ = √3/3, so θ = π/6.
9. tan θ = −12/5; fourth quadrant.
10. (4 cos(π/6), 4 sin(π/6)) = (2√3, 2).
11. b sin A = 5 < 6 < 10: two triangles.
12. 4 < 5 = b sin A: no triangle.
