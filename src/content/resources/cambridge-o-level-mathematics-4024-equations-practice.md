## Section B

**7.** Solve x² + 4x − 6 = 0 by completing the square, giving your answers in surd form. **[3]**

**8.** Solve 5x² − 2x − 9 = 0, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Show that the equation 4/x + 3/(x + 2) = 2 can be written as 2x² − 3x − 8 = 0. **[3]**

**(b)** Solve 2x² − 3x − 8 = 0, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**10.** Make r the subject of V = (4/3)πr³, and find r when V = 500. **[4]**

**11.** Make x the subject of a(x − b) = c(x + b). **[3]**

**12.** Make x the subject of s = √((x + t)/3). **[2]**

**13.** A rectangular garden is (x + 2) m long and (x + 1) m wide. Its area is 56 m².

**(a)** Show that x² + 3x − 54 = 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Solve the equation and find the length of the garden. **[3]**

**14.** At a café, a sandwich costs $4 and a drink costs $2.50. A group bought 18 items for $60.

**(a)** Write down two equations in s and d, where s is the number of sandwiches and d the number of drinks. **[2]**

**(b)** Solve your equations. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 3x − 12 − 10 + 2x = 8 [1]; 5x − 22 = 8 [1]; **x = 6** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* −2(5 − x) = −10 + 2x. Writing −10 − 2x gives x − 22 = 8, so x = 30.

**2.** Multiply by 12: 3(3x + 2) − 4(x − 1) = 24 [1]; 9x + 6 − 4x + 4 = 24, so 5x + 10 = 24 [1]; **x = 14/5** (or 2.8) [1] **[3]**

**3.** 6(x − 1) = 4(x + 3) [1]; 6x − 6 = 4x + 12 [1]; **x = 9** [1] **[3]**

**4.** First × 5: 15x − 10y = 95; second × 2: 4x + 10y = 0 [1]. Add: 19x = 95, **x = 5** [1]. Then 2(5) + 5y = 0, **y = −2** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Substitute: 2x + (3x − 7) = 8 [1]; 5x = 15, **x = 3** [1]; y = 9 − 7, **y = 2** [1] **[3]**

**6.** ac = −12, sum −1 → −4 and 3: 6x² − 4x + 3x − 2 = 2x(3x − 2) + (3x − 2) [1] = (2x + 1)(3x − 2) = 0 [1]; **x = −1/2 or x = 2/3** [1] **[3]**

**7.** (x + 2)² − 4 − 6 = 0 [1]; (x + 2)² = 10, x + 2 = ±√10 [1]; **x = −2 ± √10** [1] **[3]**

**8.** a = 5, b = −2, c = −9: b² − 4ac = 4 + 180 = 184 [1]; x = (2 ± √184)/10 [1]; **x = 1.56 or x = −1.16** [1] **[3]**

**9. (a)** Multiply by x(x + 2): 4(x + 2) + 3x = 2x(x + 2) [1]; 7x + 8 = 2x² + 4x [1]; so 2x² − 3x − 8 = 0 [1]

**(b)** b² − 4ac = 9 + 64 = 73 [1]; x = (3 ± √73)/4 [1]; **x = 2.89 or x = −1.39** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that", every step must be visible, because the answer is printed. Do not skip from the first line to the final equation.

**10.** r³ = 3V/(4π) [1]; **r = ∛(3V/(4π))** [1]. When V = 500: r³ = 1500/(4π) = 119.36... [1]; **r = 4.92** (3 s.f.) [1] **[4]**

**11.** ax − ab = cx + cb [1]; ax − cx = ab + cb, so x(a − c) = b(a + c) [1]; **x = b(a + c)/(a − c)** [1] **[3]**

**12.** s² = (x + t)/3 [1]; **x = 3s² − t** [1] **[2]**

**13. (a)** (x + 2)(x + 1) = 56 [1]; x² + 3x + 2 = 56, so x² + 3x − 54 = 0 [1]

**(b)** (x + 9)(x − 6) = 0 [1]; x = 6 (x = −9 is rejected because lengths must be positive) [1]; length = 6 + 2 = **8 m** [1] **[5]**

**14. (a)** **s + d = 18** [1]; **4s + 2.5d = 60** [1]

**(b)** 4s + 4d = 72, subtract: 1.5d = 12, **d = 8** [1]; **s = 10** [1] **[4]**

*Check:* 10 × $4 + 8 × $2.50 = $40 + $20 = $60. ✓

## Where marks are usually lost

- Sign errors when a bracket follows a minus (Questions 1 and 2).
- Multiplying only the fractions, not the right-hand side, by the common denominator.
- Forgetting the ± in Question 7, so one solution is lost.
- Writing −b as −2 instead of +2 in Question 8.
- Rounding √73 or 119.36 before the end of the calculation.
- Leaving the subject on both sides in Question 11.
- Giving x = 6 as the length in Question 13; the length is x + 2.
- Not rejecting the negative root with a reason.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the quadratic formula given in 4024?**
Yes. It is in the List of formulas on the examination paper, but you must substitute a, b and c correctly, including their signs.

**When should I give answers in surd form?**
When the question asks for exact answers or surd form. Otherwise, on Paper 2, give answers to the accuracy stated, or 3 significant figures.

**Which method should I use for simultaneous equations?**
Elimination when both equations are in the form ax + by = c; substitution when one is already arranged as y = … or x = ….
