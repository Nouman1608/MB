## Questions

**1.** Identify two marketing objectives a business might set. **[2]**

**2.** Tavernby Footwear sold £12.6m of shoes in a market worth £150m. A year later the market was worth £165m and Tavernby sold £14.85m. Calculate the market growth, the sales growth and Tavernby's market share in each year. **[5]**

**3.** Explain one advantage and one limitation of secondary research for a business entering a new market. **[4]**

**4.** Hexbridge Gyms asked 80 members whether they would pay for an evening yoga class. 45% said yes. Explain why the managers should be cautious about using this result to forecast demand. **[4]**

**5.** Fentonby Print recorded the price of a photo mug and the units sold in five weeks.

| Price (£) | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units sold | 90 | 82 | 75 | 61 | 55 |

Describe the correlation, then explain why extending the trend to a price of £15 would be unwise. **[4]**

**6.** Salterbeck Helmets sells 4,000 helmets a month at £60. Its research suggests price elasticity of demand of −0.5. It plans a 10% price rise. Analyse the effect on monthly revenue. **[5]**

**7.** Incomes are forecast to rise 4%. A tinned food maker has income elasticity of demand of −0.8; a cruise company has +2.5. State the change in demand for each firm and the type of good. **[4]**

**8.** Hexbridge Gyms is considering targeting people over 60 with daytime classes rather than mass marketing to all adults. Explain two segmentation methods it could use and evaluate the niche approach. **[6]**

**9.** Fentonby Print's products are below. Treat relative share above 1 and market growth above 5% as high.

| Product | Own share | Largest rival | Market growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photobooks | 22% | 11% | 14% |
| Calendars | 28% | 14% | 1% |
| Greeting cards | 4% | 20% | 9% |
| Posters | 3% | 12% | −2% |

Calculate each relative share, classify each product on the Boston Matrix and recommend an action for the portfolio. **[7]**

**10.** Craigholt Audio will launch a smart speaker costing £45 a unit to make. It can use price skimming at £140 or penetration pricing at £85. Calculate the profit margin per unit under each, and justify which it should choose. **[8]**

**11.** Tavernby Footwear sells through twelve shops and wants to add a website. Its target market is shoppers aged 25 to 40 who compare brands on quality. Its brand is positioned as mid-priced and dependable. Evaluate how it should adjust its marketing mix, including people, process and physical environment, for multi-channel selling. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: sales volume, sales value, market size, market growth, sales growth, market share, brand loyalty [2] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A short identify question rewards precise, listed terms; vague phrases such as "sell more" are weaker than a named objective.

**2.** Market growth = 15 ÷ 150 × 100 = **10%** [1]; sales growth = 2.25 ÷ 12.6 × 100 = **17.9%** [1]; share before = 12.6 ÷ 150 = **8.4%** [1]; share after = 14.85 ÷ 165 = **9.0%** [1]; Tavernby grew faster than the market, so it gained share [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each formula with figures; a bare final number gives the marker little to reward if an arithmetic slip occurs, and the comparison of the two growth rates shows interpretation.

**3.** Advantage: cheaper and quicker than gathering new data [1], because the data already exists (for example, published industry reports) [1]. Limitation: may be out of date or not designed for this firm's question [1], so the firm may base decisions on irrelevant figures [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" calls for a point plus a developed reason; naming an advantage alone usually leaves marks unclaimed.

**4.** The sample is small (80), so sampling error is large [1]; the 45% is only an estimate, and a confidence interval would be wide (roughly 34% to 56%) [1]; members may not represent non-members or future customers [1]; saying yes is not the same as paying, so demand could be lower [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers link caution to sample size and representativeness and state what the result cannot show, not just that it is "unreliable".

**5.** Strong negative correlation: as price rises, units fall, and the points lie close to a downward line [2]; extrapolating the trend (about 9 fewer units per £1) gives a negative figure of sales at £15, which is impossible [1]; the data cover only £5 to £9, and demand behaviour outside that range is unknown [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe both direction and strength; marks for the limitation come from applying it to the stated £15, not from a generic comment.

**6.** Quantity change = −0.5 × 10 = −5%, so 3,800 helmets [1]; new price £66 [1]; old revenue = 4,000 × 60 = £240,000 [1]; new revenue = 3,800 × 66 = £250,800 [1]; demand is inelastic, so the rise increases revenue by 4.5% (£10,800) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" needs a chain from elasticity to quantity to revenue; stating only that inelastic demand means revenue rises, without figures, is thinner.

**7.** Tinned food: −0.8 × 4 = **−3.2%** [1], an inferior good [1]. Cruise: 2.5 × 4 = **+10%** [1], a luxury-type normal good (YED above 1) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair the number with its classification; the sign of the value is what separates inferior from normal goods.

**8.** Demographic: age (over 60) defines the segment [1]; behavioural: daytime use, since many may be retired and visit off-peak [1]. Niche benefit: less direct competition and a tailored mix may build loyalty [1]. Limit: a smaller market and risk if the segment changes or rivals copy [1]. Judgement: worthwhile if the segment is large enough locally to cover costs and uses off-peak capacity [2] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the method and tie it to the case; a judgement needs a condition or reason, not just "it depends".

**9.** Relative shares: photobooks 22 ÷ 11 = **2.0**; calendars 28 ÷ 14 = **2.0**; cards 4 ÷ 20 = **0.2**; posters 3 ÷ 12 = **0.25** [2]. Photobooks: star; calendars: cash cow [1]; greeting cards: question mark; posters: dog [1]. Recommendation: use calendar cash to invest in photobooks [1]; build or drop greeting cards depending on cost of lifting share, and consider withdrawing posters [1], giving a balance of cash generators and future earners [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the relative share working, then use the classifications to make a portfolio decision rather than only labelling.

**10.** Skimming margin = 140 − 45 = **£95** (67.9%) [1]; penetration margin = 85 − 45 = **£40** (47.1%) [1]. Skimming: high margin recovers development costs and suits a new product with few rivals [2], but may limit sales and attract competitors [1]. Penetration: builds share and brand loyalty quickly, but low margin and difficult to raise later [2]. Judgement: for a distinctive product, skimming then lowering; if rivals are strong, penetration [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A justified choice weighs both options and ties the decision to the product, rivals and objectives.

**11.** Product: keep the quality focus and make the online range consistent with the shops [1], so the dependable image carries across channels [1]. Price: align shop and web prices or explain differences, as comparing shoppers will notice [1]; dynamic pricing risks harming a dependable image [1]. Promotion: social media and search aimed at 25 to 40s [1]. Place: multi-channel adds reach, but stock systems must link shops and web [2]. People: shop staff trained to handle web orders and returns [1]. Process: easy ordering, delivery and returns [1]. Physical environment: website design matching the brand and shops consistent [1]. Judgement: success depends on integrating the mix and funding the systems, since poor process would damage a dependable brand more than a missing channel [2] **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* A long evaluation needs a reasoned conclusion that refers to the target market and positioning in the stem, not a list of the 7Ps.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the new figure instead of the original when finding growth.
- Mixing units, such as £m with £.
- Giving a result without saying whether the firm gained or lost share.
- Saying correlation proves cause.
- Explaining a limitation of a sample without linking it to the case.
- Using absolute share on the Boston Matrix.
- Recommending without linking to objectives, target market or positioning.
- Evaluating with a conclusion that does not follow from the points made.
