All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Fenwick Foods.

> **Case study: Fenwick Foods**
>
> Fenwick Foods makes packaged soups in one factory. It has a managing
> director, 3 department managers (production, sales and finance),
> 12 supervisors and 60 factory workers. Each supervisor is in charge of
> 5 workers. Messages from the managing director normally pass through
> every level and take one day to go from each level to the next. Staff
> have complained that decisions are slow and that supervisors keep
> asking managers about small matters. The managing director is
> considering delayering by removing all 12 supervisors. Workers would
> then report directly to the 3 department managers. Each supervisor is
> paid $30,000 a year and redundancy would cost $8,000 for each person.
> The managing director would also like to delegate more decisions
> to department managers.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following describes span of control? **[1]**
- A. The number of levels in a business
- B. The number of people who report directly to a manager
- C. The distance between two sites
- D. The total number of staff in a business

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of a flat structure? **[1]**
- A. Many levels of management
- B. Narrow spans of control
- C. Few levels of management
- D. A long chain of command

**3.** Define the term "delegation". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** advantages of a flat organisational structure. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** disadvantage of a centralised organisational structure. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** State the number of levels in Fenwick Foods' structure at present and the number after the supervisors are removed. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the span of control of each department manager if the supervisors are removed and the 60 workers are shared equally between the 3 managers. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the annual saving in wages if the 12 supervisors are removed. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the redundancy cost, and the net saving in the first year. **[3]**

**10.** Calculate the time it takes a message from the managing director to reach the workers at present, and after delayering. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse **two** effects on the department managers of delayering Fenwick Foods. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Fenwick Foods should delayer. Use your answers to Questions 7 to 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Passing authority to carry out a task or make a decision [1] to someone at a lower level, while the manager remains responsible for the result [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: faster communication [1]; lower costs, as there are fewer managers [1]; more responsibility for staff, which can motivate them [1]; senior managers can hear workers' ideas more easily [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Decisions are made at the top, so local managers must wait for approval [1], which slows decisions and may fail to meet local needs [1]. Accept senior managers may be overloaded [1] so make poorer decisions [1]. **[2]**

**6.** At present there are 4 levels (managing director, managers, supervisors, workers) [1]; after delayering there would be 3 levels [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 60 workers / 3 managers [1] = a span of control of **20** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** 12 x $30,000 [1] = **$360,000** a year [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Redundancy cost = 12 x $8,000 = **$96,000** [1]; net saving in the first year = $360,000 - $96,000 [1] = **$264,000** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** At present the message passes through 3 steps: director to managers, managers to supervisors, supervisors to workers = **3 days** [1]; after delayering it takes 2 steps = **2 days** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **Workload:** each manager's span of control rises from 4 (supervisors) or 5 (workers) to 20 workers [1], so managers have less time for each person and may become overloaded [1].
**Responsibility:** managers will take over the supervisors' tasks, such as organising shifts and answering questions [1], which can be motivating for some managers but stressful for others, and may need training [1].
**Communication:** managers will deal with the workers directly [1], so they can hear problems sooner, but they have more people to talk to [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For delayering:** it saves $360,000 a year in wages and $264,000 in the first year after redundancy costs [1]; messages reach workers in 2 days instead of 3, which speeds decisions [1]; staff complaints about slow decisions would be reduced [1]; workers would have more responsibility [1].
**Against:** a span of control of 20 is wide for managers who also have other tasks [1]; supervisors' jobs would be lost, which could lower morale among the remaining staff [1]; workers lose the support and promotion route that supervisors provided [1].
**Judgement:** delayering is probably worth doing because the savings are large, but Fenwick should delegate some decisions to experienced workers, or keep a few team leaders, so that managers are not overloaded [1]. The result should be reviewed after a year to check productivity and quality have not fallen [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing span of control with the number of levels.
- Forgetting to subtract the redundancy costs when asked for the first-year saving.
- Counting the managing director as a level when working out the time for a message, or counting the number of levels instead of the steps between them.
- Describing a tall or flat structure in general terms without using the case details.
- Not giving a judgement at the end of an evaluation answer.
