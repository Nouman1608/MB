## Questions

**1.** Nine pupils each send this many emails in a day: 8, 15, 3, 11, 19, 6, 13, 10, 16. Find the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[3]**

**2.** The lengths in centimetres of ten leaves are 41, 38, 45, 52, 36, 47, 40, 44, 49, 43.

**(a)** Find the range. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the lower quartile and the upper quartile. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the interquartile range. **[1]**

**3.** The table shows the number of faults found in each of 48 items.

| Faults | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 11 | 9 | 10 | 4 | 2 |

**(a)** Write down the range. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the lower quartile. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the upper quartile. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the interquartile range. **[1]**

**4.** A game is played 35 times. The score each time is recorded.

| Score | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 7 | 9 | 8 | 5 | 2 |

Find the interquartile range. **[4]**

**5.** The journey times to school of 100 pupils are summarised. The table gives the number of pupils with a journey time of t minutes or less.

| t (up to) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 10 | 35 | 65 | 85 | 95 | 100 |

Using linear interpolation, estimate:

**(a)** the median **[2]**
**(b)** the lower quartile **[2]**
**(c)** the upper quartile **[2]**
**(d)** the interquartile range. **[1]**

**6.** Use the data in question 5.

**(a)** Estimate the percentile corresponding to a journey time of 45 minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate how many pupils had a journey time of more than 12 minutes and up to 35 minutes. **[3]**

**7.** A cumulative frequency polygon for the heights of 200 plants passes through these points (height in cm, cumulative frequency): (30, 0), (40, 18), (50, 70), (60, 140), (70, 185), (80, 200).

**(a)** Estimate how many plants are shorter than 55 cm. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate how many plants are between 45 cm and 64 cm tall. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the 90th percentile of the heights. **[3]**

**8.** Seven whole numbers in ascending order are 4, 7, a, 12, 15, b, 22. The interquartile range is 11 and the sum of the numbers is 88. Find a and b. **[5]**

**9.** Scores in a test for two classes:

Class A: 46, 52, 38, 61, 49, 55, 43
Class B: 58, 44, 50, 47, 53, 49, 51, 56

**(a)** Find the interquartile range for class A. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the interquartile range for class B. **[3]**
**(c)** Which class has the smaller spread in the middle half of its scores? **[1]**

**10.** A pupil finds the quartiles of 2, 5, 7, 8, 11, 14, 15 and writes: "Lower half is 2, 5, 7, 8 so Q1 = 6. Upper half is 8, 11, 14, 15 so Q3 = 12.5. IQR = 6.5."

**(a)** Identify the mistake. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the correct lower quartile, upper quartile and interquartile range. **[3]**

**11.** The time, t minutes, that 80 people spent online one evening:

| Time t | 0 < t ≤ 20 | 20 < t ≤ 40 | 40 < t ≤ 60 | 60 < t ≤ 90 | 90 < t ≤ 120 | 120 < t ≤ 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 20 | 24 | 20 | 6 | 2 |

**(a)** Write the cumulative frequencies. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the lower quartile. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the upper quartile. **[2]**
**(d)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[1]**
**(e)** Estimate the percentile for 100 minutes. **[2]**
**(f)** Estimate how many people spent between 25 and 75 minutes online. **[3]**

**12.** The marks of 200 candidates in an assessment are summarised. The table gives the number scoring up to and including each mark.

| Mark (up to) | 20 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 10 | 44 | 84 | 130 | 164 | 188 | 200 |

The lowest 25% of candidates fail. The top 15% earn a distinction.

**(a)** Estimate the highest mark that fails. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the lowest mark that earns a distinction. **[2]**
**(c)** Find how many candidates scored between these two marks, as a percentage of all candidates. **[2]**
**(d)** Estimate the percentile for a mark of 65. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Ordered: 3, 6, 8, 10, 11, 13, 15, 16, 19 [1]. Median 11 is left out. Q1 = (6 + 8)/2 = **7** [1]. Q3 = (15 + 16)/2 = **15.5**, so IQR = 15.5 − 7 = **8.5** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* With odd n, leaving out the median and then using the two halves is the standard route. Show the ordered list so a slip can be traced.

**2. (a)** 52 − 36 = **16** cm [1].
**(b)** Ordered: 36, 38, 40, 41, 43, 44, 45, 47, 49, 52 [1]. Lower half 36, 38, 40, 41, 43 gives Q1 = **40** [1]. Upper half 44, 45, 47, 49, 52 gives Q3 = **47** [1].
**(c)** IQR = 47 − 40 = **7** cm [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* With n = 10 each half has five values, so the quartile is a single data value. Give the units if the question has them.

**3. (a)** 5 − 0 = **5** [1].
**(b)** Cumulative: 12, 23, 32, 42, 46, 48. Each half has 24 values, so Q1 is the mean of the 12th and 13th values [1]. The 12th is 0 and the 13th is 1, so Q1 = **0.5** [1].
**(c)** Q3 is the mean of the 36th and 37th values [1]. Both lie in the row ending at 42 (positions 33 to 42), so Q3 = **3** [1].
**(d)** IQR = 3 − 0.5 = **2.5** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* When the two middle positions fall in different rows, the quartile is the mean of two values, so it need not be a possible number of faults. Write both positions.

**4.** Cumulative: 4, 11, 20, 28, 33, 35 [1]. The median is the 18th value, left out. Each half has 17 values. Q1 is the 9th value: it lies in the row ending at 11, so Q1 = **2** [1]. Q3 is the 18 + 9 = 27th value: it lies in the row ending at 28, so Q3 = **4** [1]. IQR = 4 − 2 = **2** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out the positions before looking at the table, and state which method you use.

**5. (a)** Position 50, between 35 (t = 20) and 65 (t = 30): 20 + (50 − 35)/(65 − 35) × 10 [1] = **25** minutes [1].
**(b)** Position 25, between 10 (t = 10) and 35 (t = 20): 10 + (25 − 10)/(35 − 10) × 10 [1] = **16** minutes [1].
**(c)** Position 75, between 65 (t = 30) and 85 (t = 40): 30 + (75 − 65)/(85 − 65) × 10 [1] = **35** minutes [1].
**(d)** IQR = 35 − 16 = **19** minutes [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The positions 50, 25 and 75 are cumulative frequencies, not times. Show the pair of points you interpolate between.

**6. (a)** At t = 45 the cumulative frequency is 85 + (45 − 40)/(50 − 40) × (95 − 85) = 90 [1]. Percentile = 90/100 × 100 = **90th** [1].
**(b)** At 35: 65 + (35 − 30)/(40 − 30) × 20 = 75 [1]. At 12: 10 + (12 − 10)/(20 − 10) × 25 = 15 [1]. Number = 75 − 15 = **60** pupils [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "More than 12 and up to 35" needs two cumulative frequencies and a subtraction. Quoting only the cumulative frequency at 35 answers a different question.

**7. (a)** Between (50, 70) and (60, 140): 70 + (55 − 50)/(60 − 50) × 70 [1] = **105** plants [1].
**(b)** At 64: 140 + (64 − 60)/(70 − 60) × 45 = 158 [1]. At 45: 18 + (45 − 40)/(50 − 40) × 52 = 44 [1]. Number = 158 − 44 = **114** plants [1].
**(c)** Position 0.9 × 200 = 180 [1]. It lies between (60, 140) and (70, 185): 60 + (180 − 140)/(185 − 140) × 10 [1] = **68.9** cm (3 s.f.) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The 90th percentile is a height, not a count. Check the answer sits inside the class you interpolated in, here 60 to 70 cm.

**8.** The lower half is 4, 7, a, so Q1 = 7 [1]. The upper half is 15, b, 22, so Q3 = b [1]. IQR: b − 7 = 11, so **b = 18** [1]. Sum: 4 + 7 + a + 12 + 15 + 18 + 22 = 78 + a = 88 [1], so **a = 10** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the ordering given to identify which values are the quartiles. Check your final answers against the order, here 7 ≤ 10 ≤ 12.

**9. (a)** Ordered A: 38, 43, 46, 49, 52, 55, 61 [1]. Q1 = 43 and Q3 = 55 [1]. IQR = 55 − 43 = **12** [1].
**(b)** Ordered B: 44, 47, 49, 50, 51, 53, 56, 58 [1]. Q1 = (47 + 49)/2 = 48 and Q3 = (53 + 56)/2 = 54.5 [1]. IQR = 54.5 − 48 = **6.5** [1].
**(c)** **Class B**, since 6.5 is less than 12 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both IQRs when you compare.

**10. (a)** The median 8 was included in both halves, but with n = 7 it should be left out [1].
**(b)** Lower half 2, 5, 7 gives Q1 = **5** [1]. Upper half 11, 14, 15 gives Q3 = **14** [1]. IQR = 14 − 5 = **9** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good error-spotting answer names the specific step that went wrong. Redoing the question without naming the error may not answer part (a).

**11. (a)** 8, 28, 52, 72, 78, 80 [1].
**(b)** Position 20, between 8 (t = 20) and 28 (t = 40): 20 + (20 − 8)/(28 − 8) × 20 [1] = **32** minutes [1].
**(c)** Position 60, between 52 (t = 60) and 72 (t = 90): 60 + (60 − 52)/(72 − 52) × 30 [1] = **72** minutes [1].
**(d)** IQR = 72 − 32 = **40** minutes [1].
**(e)** At 100: 72 + (100 − 90)/(120 − 90) × 6 = 74 [1]. 74/80 × 100 = **92.5th** percentile [1].
**(f)** At 75: 52 + (75 − 60)/(90 − 60) × 20 = 62 [1]. At 25: 8 + (25 − 20)/(40 − 20) × 20 = 13 [1]. 62 − 13 = **49** people [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* The class widths differ, so use the width of the class you are in (20 or 30 here). Label estimates as estimates.

**12. (a)** 25% of 200 = 50th value, between (40, 44) and (50, 84): 40 + (50 − 44)/(84 − 44) × 10 [1] = **41.5** [1].
**(b)** Top 15% starts above the 85th percentile, position 170, between (70, 164) and (80, 188): 70 + (170 − 164)/(188 − 164) × 10 [1] = **72.5** [1].
**(c)** 170 − 50 = 120 candidates [1], so 120/200 × 100 = **60%** [1].
**(d)** At 65: 130 + (65 − 60)/(70 − 60) × (164 − 130) = 147 [1]. 147/200 × 100 [1] = **73.5th** percentile [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage-of-all question needs a division by n at the end. Keep the cumulative frequency visible so each step can be credited.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not ordering the data first.
- Including the median in both halves when n is odd.
- Forgetting the mean of two values when a quartile falls between rows.
- Mixing up positions and values: n/4 is a cumulative frequency.
- Using the wrong class width when classes are unequal.
- Not subtracting two cumulative frequencies for a part of the distribution.
- Dividing by the wrong total for a percentile.
- Writing the IQR as an interval instead of one number.
- Giving a grouped answer as exact when it is an estimate.
