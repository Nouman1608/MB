Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **9.3 Alloys and their properties**. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Alloys and their properties study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alloys-and-their-properties/).

## Four outcomes, four things to know

1. **Definition:** an alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements.
2. **Explanation:** different sized atoms disrupt the layers, so they cannot slide; the alloy is harder and stronger.
3. **Use:** stainless steel cutlery, because it is hard and resists rusting.
4. **Diagrams:** recognise an alloy from a particle diagram.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alloy | A mixture of a metal with other elements |
| Mixture | Substances combined physically, not chemically; proportions can vary |
| Brass | Copper + zinc |
| Stainless steel | Iron + other elements such as chromium, nickel and carbon |
| Layers | Rows of identical metal ions in a pure metal, which can slide over each other |
| Hardness | Resistance to being scratched or dented |
| Rusting | Corrosion of iron and steel to form hydrated iron(III) oxide |

## Alloy versus compound

| Feature | Alloy (mixture) | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed formula | No | Yes |
| Proportions | Can vary | Fixed |
| Elements chemically combined | No | Yes |
| Example | Brass | Copper(II) oxide |

Alloys still conduct electricity and heat: they keep metallic bonding.

## The hardness explanation (three marks)

```
pure metal: atoms all the same size → regular layers → layers slide easily → soft
alloy:      atoms of different sizes → layers distorted → layers cannot slide easily
            → harder and stronger
```

Key phrases: **different sized atoms**; **layers can no longer slide over each other**.

## The two named alloys

| Alloy | Made of | Why better than the pure metal |
|---|---|---|
| Brass | Copper and zinc | Harder and stronger than copper; resists corrosion |
| Stainless steel | Iron with chromium, nickel and carbon | Hard and does not rust like pure iron |

## Stainless steel cutlery

- **Hard**: blades and prongs keep their shape.
- **Resistant to rusting**: cutlery is wet every time it is washed.
- Both properties are needed for full credit.

## Diagram checklist

An alloy diagram shows:

- closely packed particles, as in any solid metal
- mostly one kind of particle, with some of a **different size**
- the different particles **scattered through** the main lattice

Not an alloy:

- all particles the same size (pure metal)
- alternating + and − ions (ionic compound)
- separate small molecules with gaps (simple molecular substance)
- a block of one metal next to a separate block of another (two pieces of metal side by side, not mixed)

## Model answers

**"Describe what is meant by an alloy, using brass as an example." [2]**
An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements [1]. Brass is a mixture of copper and zinc [1].

**"Explain why stainless steel is used for cutlery." [2]**
Stainless steel is hard, so the cutlery does not bend or blunt [1]. It is resistant to rusting, so it does not corrode when washed [1].

**"Explain why an alloy is harder than the pure metal." [3]**
In the pure metal the atoms are all the same size, in layers that slide easily [1]. In the alloy, atoms of a different size disrupt the layers [1]. The layers can no longer slide over each other easily [1].

## Describing diagrams in words

When a question asks you to explain your choice of diagram, refer to what is drawn, not what you know:

- "The particles are all the same size" → pure metal.
- "There are particles of two different sizes, and the smaller number are scattered among the others" → alloy.
- "The particles are charged, alternating + and −" → ionic compound.

A choice with no reference to the diagram scores only the identification mark.

## Exam traps

- An alloy is a **mixture**, never a compound.
- Brass is copper and **zinc** (not tin).
- Stainless steel contains **carbon**, a non-metal: alloys are metals mixed with other **elements**.
- Write **different sized**, not "bigger": carbon atoms in steel are smaller than iron atoms.
- Layers "cannot slide easily", not "cannot move".
- The hardness explanation must describe the pure metal **and** the alloy.
- Stainless steel cutlery: **two** properties, hardness and resistance to rusting.
- Steel is not an element.

## Self-test

1. Define an alloy.
2. Name the elements in brass.
3. Name **three** elements, other than iron, that can be in stainless steel.
4. Is an alloy a mixture or a compound? Give a reason.
5. Explain why pure metals are malleable.
6. Explain why an alloy is harder than the pure metal.
7. Give the two properties that make stainless steel suitable for cutlery.
8. Carbon atoms are smaller than iron atoms. Do they still make steel harder? Explain.
9. A diagram shows all circles the same size in rows. What does it represent?
10. A diagram shows alternating + and − ions. Is it an alloy?
11. Do alloys conduct electricity? Explain.
12. Why would pure iron be a poor material for cutlery?

**Answers:** 1. A mixture of a metal with other elements. 2. Copper and zinc. 3. Chromium, nickel, carbon. 4. A mixture: the elements are not chemically combined and the proportions can vary (no fixed formula). 5. The atoms are the same size in regular layers, which can slide over each other. 6. Atoms of a different size disrupt the layers, so they can no longer slide over each other easily. 7. Hardness and resistance to rusting. 8. Yes; any difference in size distorts the layers so they cannot slide easily. 9. A pure metal. 10. No; it is an ionic compound. 11. Yes; they are still metals with delocalised electrons. 12. It rusts and is relatively soft.
