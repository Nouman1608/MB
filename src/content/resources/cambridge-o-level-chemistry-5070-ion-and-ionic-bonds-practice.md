These questions cover section 2.4, Ion and ionic bonds, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Use a Periodic Table. Where a question asks for a dot-and-cross diagram, the answer describes what a correct diagram shows. Revise first with the [ion and ionic bonds study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-ion-and-ionic-bonds/) and the [ion and ionic bonds revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-ion-and-ionic-bonds-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an *anion*. **[1]**

**2.** State the charge on the ion formed by each element.

**(a)** Barium (Group II) **[1]**

**(b)** Nitrogen (Group V) **[1]**

**(c)** Iodine (Group VII) **[1]**

**3.** Give the formula of each ionic compound.

**(a)** Lithium bromide **[1]**

**(b)** Calcium nitride **[1]**

**(c)** Potassium sulfide **[1]**

**4.** Describe the formation of an aluminium ion from an aluminium atom. Include the electronic configurations of the atom and the ion. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** conditions under which sodium fluoride conducts electricity. **[2]**

**6.** A student says: "An ionic bond is when a metal gives electrons to a non-metal." Give the correct definition of an ionic bond. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Potassium reacts with chlorine to form potassium chloride.

**(a)** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for potassium chloride, showing outer-shell electrons only. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the structure of solid potassium chloride. **[2]**

**8.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for magnesium fluoride, MgF₂. Show the outer-shell electrons only. **[3]**

**9.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why sodium oxide has a high melting point. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows some properties of four substances.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Conducts when solid? | Conducts when molten? |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | 1418 | no | yes |
| G | −7 | no | no |
| H | 1085 | yes | yes |
| K | 714 | no | yes |

**(a)** Identify the **two** substances that are ionic compounds. Explain your choice. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why substance K does not conduct when solid but does when molten. **[2]**

**11.** Lithium reacts with nitrogen to form lithium nitride, Li₃N.

**(a)** Explain why three lithium atoms react with each nitrogen atom. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe what a dot-and-cross diagram of lithium nitride shows. **[3]**

**12.** Calcium oxide and potassium bromide are both ionic compounds. Calcium oxide melts at a much higher temperature. Suggest an explanation. **[3]**

**13.** Element X is in Group II and element Y is in Group VI of the Periodic Table. X and Y react to form an ionic compound.

**(a)** Give the formula of the compound, using X and Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the ions form when X reacts with Y. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict **two** physical properties of the compound. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A **negative ion** (formed when an atom gains electrons) [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** 2+ [1]

**(b)** 3− [1]

**(c)** 1− [1]

**[3]**

**3. (a)** LiBr [1]

**(b)** Ca₃N₂ [1]

**(c)** K₂S [1]

**[3]**

**4.** The aluminium atom (**2,8,3**) [1] **loses its three outer-shell electrons** [1], forming **Al³⁺** with configuration **2,8** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** When **molten** [1]; when **dissolved in water** (aqueous) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A **strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions** [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** [K]⁺ with no outer electrons (or a full shell of 8 crosses) and [Cl]⁻ with **8 outer electrons: 7 of one symbol and 1 of the other** [1]; square brackets and charges **+** and **−** shown correctly [1]

**(b)** A **giant lattice** [1] of **alternating K⁺ and Cl⁻ ions** in a regular arrangement, held by strong electrostatic attraction [1]

**[4]**

**8.** **One** [Mg]²⁺ ion and **two** [F]⁻ ions [1]; each F⁻ has **8 outer electrons: 7 crosses and 1 dot** (one electron from Mg to each F) [1]; Mg²⁺ shown with no outer electrons (or full inner shell), brackets and charges **2+** and **−** correct [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Sodium oxide has a **giant ionic lattice** [1] with **many strong electrostatic attractions** between Na⁺ and O²⁻ ions [1]; a **large amount of energy** is needed to overcome these attractions [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** **F and K** [1]: both have **high** melting points [1] and conduct when **molten but not when solid** [1]

**(b)** In the solid the ions are in **fixed positions** and cannot move [1]; when molten the ions are **free to move** and carry the charge [1]

**[5]**

*Examiner insight:* H conducts when solid, so it is a metal (copper melts at 1085 °C). G has a low melting point and never conducts, so it is a simple molecular substance.

**11. (a)** Each lithium atom can **lose only one** electron (Group I) [1]; nitrogen needs to **gain three** electrons (Group V) to fill its outer shell, so three lithium atoms are needed [1]

**(b)** **Three** [Li]⁺ ions, each with no outer electrons [1]; **one** [N]³⁻ ion with **8 outer electrons: 5 of nitrogen's own and 3 from lithium** (shown with the other symbol) [1]; brackets with charges **+** and **3−** [1]

**[5]**

**12.** Calcium oxide contains ions with **larger charges** (Ca²⁺ and O²⁻) than potassium bromide (K⁺ and Br⁻) [1]; so the **electrostatic attractions** between the ions in the lattice are **stronger** [1]; and **more energy** is needed to overcome them [1]. **[3]**

**13. (a)** XY [1]

**(b)** Each X atom **loses two** outer electrons [1], forming **X²⁺** [1]; each Y atom **gains** those **two** electrons, forming **Y²⁻**, so both ions have a full outer shell [1]

**(c)** Any two [1] [1]: high melting point / high boiling point; solid at room temperature; does not conduct when solid; conducts when molten (or in aqueous solution, if soluble)

**[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying ionic compounds conduct because electrons move.
- Defining the ionic bond as electron transfer.
- Drawing only one fluoride ion for MgF₂, or one lithium ion for Li₃N.
- Leaving out square brackets or charges in dot-and-cross diagrams.
- Explaining a high melting point with "strong bonds" but no mention of the giant lattice.
