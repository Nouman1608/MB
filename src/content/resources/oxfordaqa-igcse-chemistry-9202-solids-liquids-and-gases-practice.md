## Section A

**1.** Name the change of state when:

**(a)** a solid turns into a liquid **[1]**

**(b)** a gas turns into a liquid **[1]**

**(c)** a solid turns directly into a gas **[1]**

**2.** Which change of state gives out energy?

A: boiling

B: evaporating

C: freezing

D: melting

**[1]**

**3.** Describe the arrangement and movement of the particles in a gas. **[2]**

**4.** State why a liquid takes the shape of its container but keeps a fixed volume. **[2]**

**5.** Define **diffusion**. **[2]**

**6.** Ice at −10 °C is heated steadily until it is water at 20 °C. State what happens to the temperature while the ice is melting. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Use the data (values rounded) to answer the questions.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorine | −101 | −34 |
| Mercury | −39 | 357 |
| Sulfur | 115 | 445 |
| Methane | −182 | −162 |

**(a)** Give the state of each substance at 25 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the substance that is a liquid over the widest range of temperature. **[1]**

**(c)** State which substance has the weakest forces between its particles. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**8.** A sample of stearic acid is heated from 40 °C to 90 °C. It melts at 69 °C.

**(a)** Sketch or describe the shape of the graph of temperature against time. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy and particles, why part of the graph is flat. **[2]**

**9.** Explain, in terms of particles and energy, what happens when water boils. **[3]**

**10.** A crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is placed at the bottom of a beaker of still water. After an hour the purple colour has spread through most of the water.

**(a)** Explain this observation. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest how the result would differ if the water were warmer. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**11.** Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution is placed at one end of a long glass tube, and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other end. After a few minutes a white ring forms inside the tube.

**(a)** Name the white solid. **[1]**

**(b)** State where the ring forms, and explain why. **[3]**

**12.** A gas jar of air is placed upside down on a gas jar of brown bromine vapour, with a cover between them. The cover is removed.

**(a)** Describe what is seen after some time. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what this shows about particles. **[2]**

**13.** A student says: "When ice melts, the water particles get bigger and start to melt." Identify two errors in this statement and correct them. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Melting [1]

**(b)** Condensing [1]

**(c)** Sublimation [1]

**[3]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Far apart, randomly arranged [1]; moving quickly in all directions [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The particles can move past each other, so the liquid flows to fill the shape [1]; they stay close together (touching), so the volume does not change [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The spreading of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration [1], caused by their random movement [1]. **[2]**

**6.** It stays constant (at 0 °C) until all the ice has melted [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Chlorine and methane: gas; mercury: liquid; sulfur: solid [2] (all four correct for two marks; two or three correct for one mark).

**(b)** Mercury (liquid from −39 °C to 357 °C, a range of 396 °C) [1].

**(c)** Methane [1]: it has the lowest boiling point, so the least energy is needed to separate its particles [1].

**[5]**

**8. (a)** Temperature rises from 40 °C [1], is flat at 69 °C while it melts, then rises again to 90 °C [1].

**(b)** Energy taken in is used to overcome forces between particles (to change the solid to a liquid) [1], not to increase the movement / kinetic energy of the particles, so the temperature does not rise [1].

**[4]**

**9.** The water particles take in energy and move faster [1]. They overcome the forces between them [1] and move far apart, escaping as a gas (steam) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The particles of potassium manganate(VII) and water move randomly [1], so the potassium manganate(VII) particles spread from where they are concentrated to where they are less concentrated (diffusion) [1].

**(b)** The colour would spread faster [1], because the particles have more energy and move faster [1].

**[4]**

**11. (a)** Ammonium chloride [1]

**(b)** Nearer the hydrochloric acid end [1]. Ammonia particles have a lower mass (Mr 17) than hydrogen chloride particles (Mr 36.5) [1], so they move faster and diffuse further in the same time [1].

**[4]**

**12. (a)** The brown colour spreads evenly through both jars [1].

**(b)** The bromine and air particles move randomly [1] and mix by diffusion, without any stirring [1].

**[3]**

**13.** Particles do not get bigger; they gain energy and vibrate / move more [1]. Particles do not melt; the forces between particles are partly overcome so they can move past one another [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining a change of state without mentioning energy.
- Saying particles expand or melt.
- Claiming the temperature rises during melting or boiling.
- Saying the ammonium chloride ring forms nearer the ammonia end.
- Giving "particles move" without the word "random" in a diffusion answer.
- Not quoting data values when predicting a state.
