These notes condense subtopic **14.1 Mammalian nervous system** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the CNS and PNS, the three types of neurone, the reflex arc and the structure and action of a synapse. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [14.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-nervous-system/). Then try the [14.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-nervous-system-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Nervous system | Brain, spinal cord and nerves; coordinates and regulates body functions |
| Impulse | An electrical signal that travels along neurones |
| CNS | Brain and spinal cord |
| PNS | Nerves outside the brain and spinal cord |
| Reflex action | A rapid and automatic response to a stimulus |
| Synapse | A junction between two neurones |

## Neurone types

| Neurone | Direction of impulse | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Receptor to CNS | Mostly PNS |
| Relay | Between sensory and motor | Within CNS |
| Motor | CNS to effector | Mostly PNS |

An **effector** is a muscle (which contracts) or a gland (which secretes). A **receptor** detects a stimulus.

## The reflex arc

**Stimulus, receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone (in the CNS), motor neurone, effector, response.**

- Reflexes are fast because the pathway is short with few synapses.
- They are automatic, so no decision by the brain is needed.
- They protect the body, for example by pulling the hand away from heat.

Example: hot object; heat receptors in skin; sensory neurone to the spinal cord; relay neurone; motor neurone; arm muscle contracts; hand moves away.

## Synapse

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Vesicles | Hold neurotransmitter molecules in the end of the first neurone |
| Synaptic gap | The space between neurones |
| Receptor proteins | On the next neurone; neurotransmitter binds to them |

**Events:**

1. An impulse arrives and vesicles release neurotransmitter into the gap.
2. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap.
3. It binds to receptor proteins on the next neurone.
4. An impulse is stimulated in the next neurone.

**One direction only:** vesicles with neurotransmitter are only on one side and receptor proteins are only on the other.

## Worked calculation

*An impulse travels 1.2 m to the spinal cord and 1.2 m back along neurones at 80 m/s. Find the travel time.*

1. Distance = 1.2 + 1.2 = 2.4 m.
2. Time = 2.4 divided by 80 = 0.03 s.

The synapses add to this time. Always write the working: distance, then division by speed, then the unit.

## Reflex compared with voluntary action

| Feature | Reflex | Voluntary |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| Brain decision | Not needed | Needed |
| Example | Withdrawing a hand from a hot object | Catching a dropped ruler |

## Stimulus, receptor and effector examples

| Situation | Receptor | Effector | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand touches a hot pan | Heat receptors in the skin | Arm muscle | Hand pulled away |
| Dust enters the eye | Touch receptors in the eye surface | Eyelid muscles | Eye closes |
| Smell of food | Chemical receptors in the nose | Salivary glands | Saliva released |
| Bright light on the eye | Light receptors in the retina | Muscles in the iris | Pupil gets smaller |

## Identifying neurones on a diagram

- Follow the direction of the impulse from the receptor.
- The first neurone, bringing the impulse into the spinal cord, is **sensory**.
- The short neurone linking the two, fully inside the spinal cord, is **relay**.
- The neurone leaving the spinal cord towards a muscle or gland is **motor**.
- A gap drawn between two neurones is a **synapse**.
- A reflex arc has at least two synapses: sensory to relay and relay to motor.

## Short model answers

**Describe a reflex action.** A rapid and automatic response to a stimulus.

**Describe the path of a simple reflex arc.** Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector.

**Explain why an impulse can only travel one way across a synapse.** Neurotransmitter is released from vesicles on one side only and the receptor proteins that detect it are on the other side only.

**Describe what happens at a synapse.** An impulse causes vesicles to release neurotransmitter into the gap. It diffuses across, binds to receptor proteins on the next neurone and stimulates a new impulse.

**State the difference between the CNS and PNS.** The CNS is the brain and spinal cord. The PNS is the nerves outside them.

## Exam traps

- Impulses travel in neurones. They do **not** travel in the blood.
- Neurotransmitter **diffuses** across the gap. It is not an impulse.
- Neurones do not touch. There is a gap.
- Put the relay neurone **inside** the CNS.
- Name the effector as a muscle or a gland.
- Say "rapid and automatic" for a reflex. "Quick" alone is not enough.
- The brain and spinal cord are CNS; do not list nerves as CNS.
- In a time calculation, give a unit with the answer.

6. Name the two kinds of effector.
7. Define a reflex action.
8. List the parts of a reflex arc in order.
9. What is a synapse?
10. What do vesicles at a synapse contain?
11. What happens to neurotransmitter after it is released?
12. Why do synapses ensure one-way transmission?

### Answers

1. It coordinates and regulates body functions.
2. The brain and the spinal cord.
3. The nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.
4. The sensory neurone.
5. Inside the CNS (brain or spinal cord).
6. Muscles and glands.
7. A rapid and automatic response to a stimulus.
8. Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector.
9. A junction between two neurones.
10. Neurotransmitter molecules.
11. It diffuses across the synaptic gap and binds to receptor proteins on the next neurone.
12. Neurotransmitter is released from only one side and the receptor proteins are only on the other side.
