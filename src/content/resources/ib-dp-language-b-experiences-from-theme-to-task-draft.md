# Experiences: from theme to task

The model texts are in English and every example is only an illustration of a skill; do the language-producing tasks in the target language of your school course and ask your school teacher to check them. The brief's recommended topics are optional and its possible questions are only possible, so your teacher chooses what to teach.

[Language B course hub](/boards/ib/ib-dp/language-b/) · [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-dp/language-b/). Applies to SL and HL, first assessment 2020, examined until the new syllabus is first assessed in May 2029. This page is a task-planning companion to the reviewed [Experiences study guide](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-experiences/).

## The theme in the brief

- Guiding principle: "Explore and tell the stories of the events, experiences and journeys that shape our lives."
- Optional recommended topics: leisure activities; holidays and travel; life stories; rites of passage; customs and traditions; migration.
- Possible questions: "How does our past shape our present and our future?" and "How and why do different cultures mark important moments in life?"

## Where the theme can appear

The brief does not tie any component to one theme. Paper 1 (25%, one writing task from three, 30 marks; SL 1 hour 15 minutes, 250 to 400 words; HL 1 hour 30 minutes, 450 to 600 words), Paper 2 (50%: listening 25 marks, reading 40 marks) and the individual oral (25%, 30 marks) can all use it.

## A four-step planning routine for any Experiences prompt

1. **Read the prompt for context, audience and purpose.** These are among the conceptual understandings in the Language B course: context, audience, purpose, meaning, variation.
2. **Pick the topic and the question it answers.** A task about a village festival fits customs and traditions and the second possible question.
3. **Choose a text type that fits the audience.** Text types are set by your school's guide; typical choices include a blog post, an article or a speech.
4. **Plan a before, during, after shape** so the reader sees how an event changed someone.

## Worked example: planning a Paper 1 response

Prompt (original, English): *Write a blog post for other students about a journey that changed how you see your own town.*

- Context: a student blog. Audience: peers. Purpose: to share and reflect.
- Topic: holidays and travel, or migration. Question: how does our past shape our present and our future?
- Plan: (a) hook with a moment from the journey; (b) what happened, in order; (c) what you noticed on your return; (d) a closing thought that links then and now.
- Language checklist: two ways to express past time, time markers (at first, afterwards, since then), an opinion phrase, one conditional sentence.

At HL the brief says tasks require more complex language and structures and a higher word range, so aim to control a wider range of structures and plan for 450 to 600 words.

## Worked example: preparing the SL oral stimulus

At SL the oral starts from a visual image clearly relevant to one or more themes. Image (describe in English): a family at a station platform with suitcases. Steps: say what is shown in one or two sentences; interpret (migration, a rite of passage or a journey); link to a possible question; give a personal opinion. Do not spend your whole preparation on description.

## Common errors

- Telling a list of events with no reflection.
- Not matching text type to audience.
- Ignoring the second possible question: culture, not only personal experience.
