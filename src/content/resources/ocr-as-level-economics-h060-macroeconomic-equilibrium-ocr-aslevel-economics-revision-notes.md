## What you must be able to do

| Specification wording | In one line |
|---|---|
| Explain equilibrium in the macroeconomy | The price level and real output where AD = AS, with no pressure to change |
| Evaluate effects of changes in AD and AS on macroeconomic indicators | Translate P and Y into growth, unemployment, inflation and the current account, then judge with context |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Macroeconomic equilibrium | The price level and real output at which AD equals AS |
| Short-run equilibrium | AD = SRAS; can be at any output level |
| Long-run equilibrium | AD = SRAS = LRAS; at potential output in the neo-classical model |
| Potential output | Output when all resources are fully employed |
| Excess demand | Planned spending exceeds output at the current price level |
| Excess supply | Output exceeds planned spending at the current price level |
| Unplanned stock change | Stocks rising or falling unexpectedly; the signal for firms to adjust |
| Equilibrium with unemployment | A Keynesian short-run equilibrium below full capacity that can persist |
| Self-correction | The neo-classical idea that falling wages shift SRAS right and restore full capacity |

## How adjustment works

- **Excess supply** at a high price level: unsold stocks build up, firms cut prices and output, the price level falls.
- **Excess demand** at a low price level: stocks run down, firms raise prices and output, the price level rises.
- The movement stops where AD = AS.

### Worked reminder: finding equilibrium

| Price level | AD | SRAS |
|---|---|---|
| 95 | 470 | 430 |
| 100 | 450 | 450 |
| 105 | 430 | 470 |

Equilibrium: **P = 100, Y = 450**. At P = 105 there is excess supply of 40; at P = 95 excess demand of 40.

### Worked reminder: a rise in AD

AD rises by 40 at every price level (510, 490, 470 at P = 95, 100, 105). New equilibrium: **P = 105, Y = 470**. Growth = 20 / 450 = **4.4%**; the price level rises **5%**.

### Worked reminder: below potential

Potential output is 500 but equilibrium is 450, a shortfall of 50, or **10%** of potential. Expect high unemployment and low inflation.

## Two views of what happens below potential

| | Neo-classical | Keynesian |
|---|---|---|
| Wages | Flexible; fall when unemployment is high | Sticky downwards |
| Adjustment | SRAS shifts right, output returns to potential | Can stay below potential for a long time |
| Policy implication | Little need for demand management | Raise AD with fiscal or monetary policy |

## Effects on indicators

| Change | Growth | Unemployment | Inflation | Current account |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD right | Up | Down | Up | Worse |
| AD left | Down | Up | Down | Better |
| SRAS right | Up | Down | Down | Better (more competitive) |
| SRAS left | Down | Up | Up | Worse (less competitive) |
| LRAS right | Sustainable growth up | Down in long run | Less pressure | May improve |

Current account effects are tendencies, not certainties: they depend on how income and competitiveness change.

## Evaluation checklist

1. **Starting point:** spare capacity or near full capacity?
2. **Size** of the shift.
3. **Time period:** short-run demand effects, long-run capacity effects.
4. **Other shifts** at the same time.
5. **Policy responses and expectations.**

## Exam traps

- Equilibrium is not the same as full employment.
- Name both the price level and real output when stating an equilibrium.
- Short-run equilibrium is AD = SRAS; long-run needs LRAS too.
- A supply shock can push inflation up and output down, creating a policy dilemma.
- Demand-led and supply-led growth have opposite effects on inflation.
- Do not stop at P and Y: the question usually asks about named indicators.
- Use "likely" for current account effects; they are not automatic.

## Self-test

1. Define macroeconomic equilibrium.
2. At the current price level, planned spending exceeds output. What happens to stocks, output and prices?
3. State the condition for long-run equilibrium.
4. Can short-run equilibrium occur below full capacity? Explain.
5. Equilibrium real output rises from 640 to 656 and the price level from 100 to 102. Calculate growth and inflation.
6. Which shift raises growth and lowers inflation together?
7. How does a rise in AD affect the current account, and why?
8. In the neo-classical view, what restores full capacity after a fall in AD?
9. Why might the Keynesian economy stay below potential?
10. Why does a cost shock create a conflict between objectives?
11. Potential output is 900 and equilibrium output is 846. Calculate the shortfall as a percentage of potential.
12. Give two factors that determine how far a rise in AD raises output rather than prices.

### Answers

1. The price level and real output at which aggregate demand equals aggregate supply.
2. Stocks fall, firms raise output and prices until AD = AS.
3. AD = SRAS = LRAS.
4. Yes: AD can meet SRAS at an output below potential, leaving unemployment.
5. Growth = 16 / 640 = **2.5%**; inflation = **2.0%**.
6. A rightward shift of SRAS (or LRAS).
7. It usually worsens, because higher incomes raise imports and higher prices make exports less competitive.
8. Falling money wages lower costs, shifting SRAS right.
9. Wages are sticky downwards, and falling prices may make households delay spending.
10. Output falls and inflation rises, so raising AD worsens inflation and cutting AD worsens unemployment.
11. 54 / 900 = **6.0%**.
12. Any two: the amount of spare capacity; the slope of AS; the size of the shift; policy responses; expectations.
