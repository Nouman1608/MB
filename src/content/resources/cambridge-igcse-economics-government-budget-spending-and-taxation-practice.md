## Questions

**1.** Define the government budget. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Identify three main areas of government spending. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why a government spends on one of these areas. **[2]**

**3.** Country B's budget (invented) expects these revenues: income tax 90, sales tax 70, tax on company profits 35, import duties 5, fees and other income 20 (all in millions of units).

**(a)** Calculate total tax revenue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate tax revenue as a percentage of total revenue, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why governments levy taxes. **[4]**

**4.** Classify each tax as direct or indirect: income tax, sales tax, tax on company profits, import duty. **[4]**

**5.** Country C (invented) charges no income tax on the first 5,000 of income, 20% on income from 5,001 to 25,000, and 40% on income above 25,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid and the average tax rate for a person earning 15,000 and a person earning 45,000. **[4]**
**(b)** State whether this tax is progressive, regressive or proportional, and justify your answer. **[2]**

**6.** In Country C a sales tax of 15% applies to taxed goods. Household P has an income of 9,000 and spends 6,000 on taxed goods. Household R has an income of 45,000 and spends 20,000 on taxed goods.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid by each household as a percentage of its income. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the sales tax is regressive. **[2]**

**7.** A tax raises 250 million units and costs 20 million units to collect.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of collection as a percentage of revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** State which quality of a good tax this measures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a tax with complicated and unclear rules fails the test of certainty. **[2]**

**8.** A tax of 3 per unit is placed on a good. Before the tax the price was 15 and 2,000 units were sold. The whole tax is passed on to consumers and 1,600 units are now sold.

**(a)** Calculate government revenue from the tax. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in price, the percentage change in quantity and the price elasticity of demand. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the producers' revenue, excluding the tax, before and after the tax, and the change. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain one other effect of the tax on producers. **[1]**

**9.** Country D (invented) plans to spend 30 of a 150 budget on education and then raises this to 36.

**(a)** Calculate the share of the 150 budget spent on education. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in education spending. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one effect of more education spending on the economy and one opportunity cost. **[4]**

**10.** Country A (invented) is considering raising its sales tax from 10% to 14% to pay for health. Household L has an income of 15,000 and spends 12,000 on taxed goods. Household H has an income of 75,000 and spends 30,000 on taxed goods.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid as a percentage of income by each household at 10% and at 14%. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether raising the sales tax is a fair way to pay for health. **[4]**

**11.** Country E (invented) places a tax of 4 per bottle on a sugary drink, a demerit good. Before the tax the price was 12 and 5,000 bottles were sold. After the tax the price is 15 and 3,500 bottles are sold.

**(a)** Calculate government revenue from the tax. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the share of the tax paid by consumers and by producers. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate producers' revenue, excluding the tax, before and after the tax. **[3]**
**(d)** Analyse the effects of the tax on consumers, producers, the government and the economy. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A plan of expected government revenue and planned spending [1] for a period, usually one year [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that mentions only taxes, or only spending, describes half of the budget; naming both sides is the safest way to give a precise meaning.

**2. (a)** Any three areas, such as health [1], education [1] and defence [1]. Social security, infrastructure and debt interest also count. **[3]**
**(b)** Education, for example: gives workers skills [1] which raise productivity and incomes, benefiting the whole country [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" asks for a reason and a link. Naming an area alone, or saying it is important, gives you nothing to build on.

**3. (a)** 90 + 70 + 35 + 5 = **200** million units [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Total revenue = 200 + 20 = 220 [1]. 200 / 220 × 100 = **90.9%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two reasons, each with a development. For example: to raise revenue [1] so that health and schools can be paid for [1]; to redistribute income [1] by taxing high earners more and giving benefits to low earners [1]. Other valid reasons: to discourage demerit goods, or to protect domestic firms with import duties. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In percentage questions the denominator is the whole revenue, not the tax total; choosing the wrong base is the usual way to lose the answer.

**4.** Income tax: direct [1]. Sales tax: indirect [1]. Tax on company profits: direct [1]. Import duty: indirect [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Decide by what is taxed, income or spending, not by who hands over the money.

**5. (a)** 15,000: (15,000 − 5,000) × 20% = **2,000** [1]; average rate 2,000 / 15,000 = **13.3%** [1]. 45,000: 20,000 × 20% + 20,000 × 40% = 4,000 + 8,000 = **12,000** [1]; average rate 12,000 / 45,000 = **26.7%** [1].
**(b)** Progressive [1], because the average rate rises from 13.3% to 26.7% as income rises [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the band-by-band working so a reader can follow your method, and quote the two average rates in the justification.

**6. (a)** P: 15% × 6,000 = 900 [1]; 900 / 9,000 = **10%** [1]. R: 15% × 20,000 = 3,000 [1]; 3,000 / 45,000 = **6.7%** [1].
**(b)** R pays more money but a smaller share of income [1], because richer households spend a smaller share of income on taxed goods [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Regressive refers to the share of income. An answer that says "rich pay less" without the percentage comparison is not accurate.

**7. (a)** 20 / 250 × 100 [1] = **8%** [1].
**(b)** Economical (cheap to collect) [1].
**(c)** If rules are unclear, taxpayers cannot know how much or when to pay [1], which leads to mistakes, disputes and avoidance [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the quality and then apply it to the case given. A list of qualities without reference to the tax described is rarely enough.

**8. (a)** 3 × 1,600 = **4,800** [1].
**(b)** Price: 3 / 15 × 100 = **20%** [1]. Quantity: −400 / 2,000 × 100 = **−20%** [1]. Elasticity = −20 / 20 = **−1** [1].
**(c)** Before: 15 × 2,000 = 30,000 [1]. After: producers receive 15 per unit, so 15 × 1,600 = 24,000 [1]. Change = **−6,000** [1].
**(d)** Any one of: lower profit; a cut in output or jobs; less money to invest [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Producers' revenue excludes the tax. Using the consumer price of 18 gives a figure that includes money passed on to the government.

**9. (a)** 30 / 150 × 100 [1] = **20%** [1].
**(b)** 6 / 30 × 100 = **20%** [1].
**(c)** Effect: more educated, skilled workers [1] raise productivity and output over time [1]. Opportunity cost: the extra 6 units cannot be used for another area such as health [1], or must come from higher taxes or borrowing [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Opportunity cost should be tied to the figures, here the extra 6 units, rather than stated as a general idea.

**10. (a)** L at 10%: 1,200 / 15,000 = **8.0%**; at 14%: 1,680 / 15,000 = **11.2%** [2]. H at 10%: 3,000 / 75,000 = **4.0%**; at 14%: 4,200 / 75,000 = **5.6%** [2].
**(b)** The tax is regressive: L pays 11.2% of income against 5.6% for H [1], so it is not fair in terms of ability to pay [1]. However, the money could pay for health, which helps low-income households most [1]. A judgement, for example that it is fair only if the health spending outweighs the higher burden on L, with a reason [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation uses the calculated percentages, offers a counter-argument and reaches a judgement, rather than giving two unconnected lists.

**11. (a)** 4 × 3,500 = **14,000** [1].
**(b)** Consumers pay 3 of the 4, so **75%** [1]; producers 1 of 4, so **25%** [1].
**(c)** Before: 12 × 5,000 = 60,000 [1]. After: producers keep 15 − 4 = 11 per bottle, so 11 × 3,500 = 38,500 [1]. Fall = **21,500** [1].
**(d)** Consumers: price up 25%, so they buy 30% fewer and may switch to healthier drinks [1]. Producers: lower revenue and profit, may cut output [1]. Government: 14,000 of extra revenue [1]. Economy: lower consumption of a demerit good, so better health, though spending on other goods may rise [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover each named group separately and give a reason with each. Four effects on one group will not show the range the question asks for.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using total tax instead of total revenue as the denominator.
- Describing a tax as regressive because the rich pay less money.
- Classifying taxes by who delivers the payment instead of by what is taxed.
- Including the tax in producers' revenue.
- Giving an effect with no link, such as "price rises" with no result for quantity.
- Stating opportunity cost without saying what is given up.
- Writing a list for an evaluation question with no judgement.
- Leaving out units or the percentage sign.
