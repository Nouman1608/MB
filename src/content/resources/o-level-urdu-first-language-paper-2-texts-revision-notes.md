## Three parts, one shared assessment objective

| Part | Marks | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Unseen Passage | 20 | Extract not seen before (poetry or prose) |
| 2 Poetry | 15 | 10 prescribed ghazals + 3 prescribed nazms — answer 1 of 2 |
| 3 Prose | 15 | 8 prescribed prose articles — answer 1 of 2 |

**All three parts test AO3 (Literature)**: first-hand textual
knowledge, literal and deeper meaning, how writers create effect
through language, and an informed personal response.

## Part 1: Unseen Passage — a skill, not memorised content

Cannot be prepared by memorising a text. Practise reading unfamiliar
extracts and identifying **how** the writer creates effect (word
choice, imagery, structure) — not just summarising **what** the
extract says. Use past-question prompts as a model for the kind of
observation expected.

## Part 2 Poetry — the prescribed list

- **10 ghazals**: Khwaja Haider Ali Ahtish, Parveen Shakir, Momin
Khan Momin, Allama Iqbal, Ahmed Faraz.
- **3 nazms**: Hali's *Musadas Hali* (17 selected stanzas), Zameer
Jafree's *"Admee"*, Mahmood Sarhadi's *"Qitaat"*.

Choose **1 of 2** essay questions.

## Part 3 Prose — the prescribed list

**8 articles**, including: Muhammad Hussain Azad, Mirza Farhatullah
Baig, Ghulam Abbas, Ahmad Nadeem Qasmi, Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, Mushtaq
Ahmed Yousafi, Syed Imtiaz Ali Taj, and two of Ghalib's letters
(*Khatoot-e-Ghalib*). Choose **1 of 2**.

## What the mark schemes actually reward

Increasing depth of the same four qualities, across all three parts:

1. A **personal, informed response** to the text.
2. Understanding beyond literal meaning to **deeper implications**.
3. Explicit engagement with **how** the writer achieves effects
(word choice, imagery, structure, tone).
4. **Close, well-selected textual reference** — not paraphrase alone.

**The highest mark-scheme levels require sustained, perceptive
engagement with the writer's craft**, not just accurate comprehension
— this is the single biggest gap between a mid-band and top-band
response.

## Worked example: a strong Part 2 answer structure

A question asks how a named poet conveys a particular emotion in a
prescribed ghazal.

```
Weak answer:    Summarises the ghazal's general subject matter
                without quoting specific lines.
Strong answer:  Identifies specific sher (couplets), explains the
                imagery/word choice used, and connects that
                language choice explicitly to the named emotion --
                using precise quotation, not paraphrase, as evidence.
```

This identify → explain → connect structure applies equally to Part
3 Prose essay answers.

## Why this differs from 3248 Second Language Urdu

Cambridge O Level First Language Urdu (3247) is a distinct
specification from Second Language Urdu (3248), even though both are
offered by the same board for candidates studying the same language.
3247's Paper 2 is built entirely around literature -- an unseen
extract plus prescribed poetry and prose -- while 3248's equivalent
paper (Grammar, Writing and Translation) tests a different skill set
altogether. Candidates and teachers should confirm which specific
syllabus code their centre has entered them for before using any
revision material, since the two Urdu specifications are not
interchangeable despite the shared subject name.

## Building depth on a manageable subset

Given the choice of 1 from 2 questions in both Part 2 and Part 3, it
is more efficient to know roughly six of the ten prescribed ghazals,
two of the three nazms, and five of the eight prose articles in close
textual depth -- with specific quotable lines ready -- than to have a
shallow, general familiarity with the entire prescribed list. This
still leaves a genuine fallback option if one particular question
doesn't suit the texts you know best.

## Time management across the three parts

Paper 2 combines a shorter comprehension-based part with two
essay-based parts, and time pressure affects each differently.
Because Part 1 has a fixed, bounded answer (responding to what is
actually on the page), it can be completed efficiently once the
underlying skill is secure. Parts 2 and 3, by contrast, reward depth
of textual reference that only comes from a fuller, well-planned
essay response — spending disproportionate time perfecting Part 1
at the expense of either essay is a common way strong preparation
still produces a mid-band overall result.

## Worked example: selecting between two essay options under time pressure

A Part 3 Prose question offers a choice between two questions on
different prescribed articles. A candidate has strong textual
knowledge of one article and only moderate knowledge of the other.

```
Weaker approach:  Choosing based on which question "sounds easier"
                   at a glance, without checking which article it
                   actually draws on.
Stronger approach: Quickly identifying which prescribed article each
                   question requires, then choosing the one matching
                   the article known in greater textual depth --
                   since depth of reference, not the phrasing of the
                   question itself, is what the mark scheme rewards.
```

This same selection logic applies equally to the Part 2 Poetry
choice between a ghazal-based and nazm-based question.

## Exam traps

- Answering the Unseen Passage as a comprehension exercise, without
engaging with *how* the writer creates effect.
- Writing about a prescribed text from general memory of its "story"
without quoting or closely referencing actual wording.
- Choosing a Part 2/3 question based on which text is easiest to
summarise, rather than which you know in the most depth.
- Spending too long on Part 1, leaving insufficient time for the two
essay-based parts.
- Preparing only enough prescribed texts to have exactly one usable
option — leaving no fallback if that specific question doesn't suit
you on the day.

## Self-test

1. What assessment objective do all three parts of Paper 2 test?
2. Why can't Part 1 be prepared by memorising a specific text?
3. Name the four qualities the mark schemes reward with increasing
depth across all three parts.
4. What distinguishes a top-band Part 2/3 response from a mid-band
one?
5. How many ghazals, nazms and prose articles are prescribed, and
how many questions must you answer from each in Parts 2 and 3?

**Answers:** 1. AO3, Literature (texts). 2. Because the passage is
genuinely unseen — preparation must build the transferable skill of
identifying how a writer creates effect, not memorise any specific
text. 3. A personal/informed response; understanding beyond literal
meaning; explicit engagement with how effects are created; close,
well-selected textual reference. 4. Sustained, perceptive engagement
with the writer's craft, rather than merely accurate comprehension.
5. 10 ghazals and 3 nazms for Part 2, 8 prose articles for Part 3;
answer 1 of 2 essay questions in each of Part 2 and Part 3.
