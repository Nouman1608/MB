These questions are for **section 14.8 Mobile communication systems** (Topic 14, A Level) of Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology (9626), 2025–2027. Use 300,000 kilometres per second for the speed of radio waves unless told otherwise, and 1 megabyte = 8 megabits. All organisations and figures are invented.

## Section A

**1.** Describe what is meant by a cell in a cellular network. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by handover. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** improvements of 5G compared with 4G. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the role of a transponder in a communications satellite. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** uses of global mapping systems. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A mobile operator has 60 channels. It uses clusters of 4 cells, each cell with a different group of channels, and the clusters are repeated across a region of 36 cells.

**(a)** Calculate the number of channels in each cell. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total number of channels available across the region at one time. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the operator reuses channels rather than using different channels in every cell. **[2]**

**7.** A GPS receiver measures that a signal from one satellite took 0.075 seconds to arrive.

**(a)** Calculate the distance to the satellite. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why signals from at least four satellites are needed to work out a position. **[3]**

**8.** A geostationary satellite is 36,000 km above the ground. Radio waves travel at 300,000 km per second.

**(a)** Calculate the delay for a signal sent from a ground station to the satellite and back to the ground. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain the effect of this delay on a telephone conversation. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a low Earth orbit satellite has a shorter delay. **[2]**

**9.** A 600 MB video is downloaded on a phone. Download speeds are 25 megabits per second on 4G and 400 megabits per second on 5G.

**(a)** Calculate the download time for each, in seconds. **[4]**

**(b)** State **two** reasons why the actual speeds may be lower than these figures. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how data is prepared, sent and received by a satellite communication system. **[6]**

**11.** A rural area has no mobile mast and no cable. Evaluate satellite communication as a way of providing telephone and internet access to its homes. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why 5G networks that use high frequencies need many more base stations than earlier systems. **[4]**

**13.** Discuss the benefits and concerns of using satellites for surveillance. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** A cell is the area covered by one base station [1]. The network is divided into many cells, and the devices in a cell communicate by radio with that cell's base station [1]. **[2]**

**2.** When a user moves from one cell to another [1], the network transfers the call or data session to the new base station and channel without a break [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: faster data speeds [1]; lower delay (latency) [1]; support for many more devices in an area [1]; better for Internet of Things and remote control of machines [1]. **[2]**

**4.** It receives the weak signal from the ground [1], amplifies it and changes its frequency, then sends it back to Earth [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: route planning and navigation [1]; urban planning [1]; monitoring changes in forests or coastlines [1]; finding places [1]; responding to disasters [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 60 ÷ 4 = 15 [1].

**(b)** Number of clusters = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 [1]; 9 × 60 [1] = 540 channels [1].

**(c)** The radio spectrum is limited [1]; reusing the channels in cells far apart, without interference, serves many more users with the same spectrum [1].

**7. (a)** Distance = speed × time [1] = 300,000 × 0.075 = 22,500 km [1].

**(b)** Each satellite signal gives a distance, so the receiver lies on a sphere around that satellite [1]. Signals from three satellites give a position in latitude, longitude and height [1]. A fourth is needed because the receiver's clock is not accurate, so it is used to correct the time [1].

**8. (a)** Distance = 2 × 36,000 = 72,000 km [1]; time = 72,000 ÷ 300,000 [1] = 0.24 seconds [1].

**(b)** The reply takes as long again, so there is a gap of about half a second between a person speaking and hearing the answer [1], which makes conversation awkward, and people talk over each other [1].

**(c)** It orbits much closer to the Earth [1], so the signal travels a shorter distance [1].

**9. (a)** 600 × 8 = 4,800 megabits [1]. 4G: 4,800 ÷ 25 [1] = 192 seconds [1]. 5G: 4,800 ÷ 400 = 12 seconds [1].

**(b)** Any two from: weak signal or distance from the mast [1]; many users sharing the cell [1]; walls and buildings blocking the signal [1]; the server being slow [1]. **[6]**

**10.** The data is converted to digital form, compressed and coded with error-correction bits [1]. It is modulated onto a carrier wave [1]. The ground station sends it as a microwave signal from a dish aimed at the satellite (uplink) [1]. The satellite's transponder receives, amplifies and changes the frequency [1] and sends it back to Earth (downlink) [1]. The receiving dish picks it up, and the receiver demodulates, corrects errors, decodes and decompresses the data to produce sound, pictures or data [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Advantages: covers remote areas where cable and masts are too costly [1]; quick to install with a dish [1]; can serve homes over a very wide area [1]. Disadvantages: delay from the long distance makes calls and online gaming awkward [1]; weather, such as heavy rain, can reduce the signal [1]; equipment and subscriptions cost more than other services [1]; limited capacity is shared between users [1]. Conclusion: suitable when no alternative exists, with low earth orbit systems giving the lower delay [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**12.** Higher frequencies carry more data [1], but they travel shorter distances [1] and are blocked easily by walls and buildings [1]. So each base station covers a small area, and many more are needed to give continuous coverage [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Benefits: covers large and remote areas [1]; useful for weather forecasting and tracking storms [1]; monitors crops, forests and ships [1]; can help in disasters [1]. Concerns: invasion of privacy [1]; images could be misused by governments or companies [1]; high cost [1]. Maximum 5 marks. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not mentioning handover or frequency reuse when asked about cell structure.
- Using one-way instead of the full signal path for satellite delay.
- Leaving out the conversion from megabytes to megabits.
- Saying GPS satellites "track" the user.
- Describing 5G as only "faster" without a reason linked to cell size or latency.
