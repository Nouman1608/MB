## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rhodopsin | Visual pigment of rods: opsin (protein) bound to retinal |
| Opsin | Protein part of rhodopsin; remains after bleaching |
| Retinal | Light-absorbing part of rhodopsin |
| Hyperpolarisation | Inside of the cell becomes more negative than at rest |
| Habituation | Reduced response to a repeated stimulus that is neither harmful nor rewarding |
| CNS / PNS | Brain and spinal cord / neurones outside them |
| Phytochrome | Plant pigment with Pr and Pfr forms |
| IAA | Indoleacetic acid, the main auxin |
| Gibberellin | Plant hormone that switches on amylase genes in cereal grains |
| Aleurone layer | Outer layer of a cereal endosperm that makes amylase |

## 8.8 Rods: the sequence

In the dark: cation channels open, sodium ions enter, rod partly depolarised, neurotransmitter released continuously, bipolar neurone inhibited.

In the light, in steps:
1. Rhodopsin absorbs light and splits into opsin and retinal.
2. Cation channels close.
3. Sodium ions stop entering; pumping out continues.
4. Rod hyperpolarised.
5. Less neurotransmitter released.
6. Bipolar neurone depolarised and releases neurotransmitter.
7. Optic neurone reaches threshold and an action potential passes to the brain.

Remember: the light closes the channels. A rod is hyperpolarised in the light, not depolarised.

Rods: many connect to one bipolar neurone, giving high sensitivity and low detail.

## 8.9 Habituation

- Definition: a decrease in response to a stimulus repeated many times without a consequence.
- Value: saves energy and attention for new or dangerous stimuli.
- Not the same as: fatigue (a new stimulus gives a full response) or damage to a receptor.
- Usual mechanism: fewer calcium ions enter the presynaptic neurone, so less neurotransmitter is released.
- Practical: repeat the same stimulus at fixed intervals, record the response each time, and check that a new stimulus restores it.

## 8.10 Nervous system

CNS = brain and spinal cord. PNS = all neurones outside, including sensory neurones and motor neurones.

## 8.11 Plant hormones

| Hormone | Made or acts | Effect | Transcription link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phytochrome | Pr (red, about 660 nm) to Pfr (far-red, about 730 nm) | Light-sensitive germination, etiolation, flowering | Pfr enters the nucleus and affects transcription factors |
| Auxin (IAA) | Shoot tip, moves down | Elongation of shoot cells; inhibits elongation in roots at higher concentration; apical dominance | Repressor removed, transcription factor switches on elongation genes |
| Gibberellin | Embryo of seed | Stem elongation; amylase production in cereal grains | Repressor protein broken down, amylase gene transcribed |

Method in steps for phototropism:
1. Light from one side.
2. IAA moves to the shaded side.
3. Wall pH falls on that side as proton pumps act.
4. Cells there elongate more.
5. Shoot bends towards the light.

Gravitropism of a root: IAA gathers on the lower side, inhibits elongation there, so the root grows downwards.

Flowering: Pfr promotes flowering in long-day plants and inhibits flowering in short-day plants.

## 8.12 Core Practical 18 summary

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Aim | Investigate amylase production in germinating cereal grains |
| Method | Grain halves (with and without embryo) on starch agar, with or without gibberellic acid |
| Detection | Remove grains, flood with iodine solution; the clear zone shows starch digestion |
| Measure | Clear zone diameter; area = πr² |
| Controls | Boiled grain halves; same temperature, time, grain type, agar |
| Hazards | Iodine staining; microorganism contamination of plates |
| Reliability | Repeat on several plates and calculate the mean |

**Worked reminder.** A clear zone has a diameter of 14 mm.
- Radius = 7 mm.
- Area = π × 7² = 153.9 = **154 mm²** (3 significant figures).
- A zone of diameter 7 mm has area **38.5 mm²**, a quarter of the area when the diameter is halved.

## 8.13 Nervous against hormonal

| | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Pathway | Neurones | Blood |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| Effect | Short-lived, localised | Longer-lasting, widespread |
| Target | Effector cells | Cells with the right receptor |

## 8.14 Brain regions

| Region | Functions |
|---|---|
| Cerebral hemispheres | Conscious thought, memory, language, voluntary movement, interpreting senses |
| Hypothalamus | Temperature and water balance, hunger, thirst, controls pituitary |
| Pituitary gland | Hormone release, such as ADH, and control of other glands |
| Cerebellum | Coordinates movement and balance, posture |
| Medulla oblongata | Heart rate, ventilation rate, reflexes such as coughing and swallowing |

## 8.15 Scans

| Scan | Principle | Shows | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRI | Magnetic field and radio waves, hydrogen nuclei | Structure of soft tissue | No ionising radiation; cannot be used with some metal implants |
| fMRI | Blood oxygen changes | Active regions | Activity uses more oxygen |
| PET | Radioactive tracer, gamma rays | Metabolic activity | Tracer has a short half-life |
| CT | Rotating X-ray source | Cross-sectional structure | Ionising radiation; quick |

**Half-life reminder.** Remaining fraction = (1/2) to the power of (time ÷ half-life).
- Tracer with a half-life of 110 minutes, after 220 minutes: (1/2)² = 1/4.
- Starting at 600 MBq, the activity is 600 ÷ 4 = **150 MBq**.

## 8.16 Brain chemicals

| Condition | Imbalance | Treatment idea |
|---|---|---|
| Parkinson's disease | Loss of dopamine-releasing neurones, so less dopamine | L-DOPA, converted to dopamine in the brain |
| Depression | Low serotonin activity at synapses | SSRIs block uptake of serotonin from the synapse |

Drug development: computer modelling and laboratory tests, animal tests, then clinical trials. Phase I: safety in healthy volunteers. Phase II: dose and effect in patients. Phase III: compared with a placebo or existing treatment, randomised and double-blind.

## Interpreting data: a checklist

1. Read the axis titles and units before describing anything.
2. Describe the trend with figures from the table or graph, including the start and end values.
3. Calculate a percentage change as (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
4. Compare with the control, so that the effect can be attributed to the variable.
5. Explain using the biology: name the ion, hormone, structure or region.
6. Comment on reliability: repeats, means, and whether the ranges overlap.

**Worked reminder: habituation.** A habituating response falls steeply at first and then levels off. If a snail takes 40 s to re-extend its tentacles on trial 1 and 10 s on trial 12, the decrease is 30 s, which is 75%. A graph that levels off at a low value shows that the response has been largely lost, not that the animal has stopped being able to respond: a new stimulus should restore it.

**Worked reminder: gibberellin and amylase.** If amylase activity rises from 4 units to 20 units in the presence of gibberellin, the increase is 16 units, which is (20 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 = **400%**. Explain with transcription of the amylase gene in the aleurone layer, then hydrolysis of starch in the endosperm.

## Must-know distinctions

- Depolarisation (inside less negative) and hyperpolarisation (inside more negative).
- Habituation and fatigue.
- Structural scans (MRI, CT) and functional scans (fMRI, PET).
- Hypothalamus (controls) and pituitary gland (releases hormones).
- Cerebellum (movement and balance) and medulla oblongata (heart rate and ventilation).
- IAA in shoots (promotes elongation) and in roots at a high concentration (inhibits it).
- Phytochrome (a pigment that senses light) and auxin (a hormone that moves).

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two parts of rhodopsin.
2. In the dark, which ion enters the outer segment of a rod through open cation channels?
3. State what happens to the cation channels when rhodopsin splits.
4. A mouse is given 30 puffs of air and its startle response falls from 30 movements to 12 movements per minute. Calculate the percentage decrease.
5. Which form of phytochrome absorbs far-red light?
6. Give one response of a shoot to IAA and one response of a root to a high concentration of IAA.
7. Which tissue of a cereal grain makes amylase after gibberellin acts on it?
8. A clear zone on a starch plate has a diameter of 12 mm. Calculate its area to 3 significant figures.
9. State one structural and one functional scan technique.
10. A coleoptile grows from 18 mm to 27 mm. Calculate the percentage increase.
11. Name the brain region that controls the rate of ventilation.
12. Give the term for the process by which SSRIs increase serotonin in the synapse.

### Answers

1. Opsin and retinal.
2. Sodium ions.
3. They close.
4. (30 − 12) ÷ 30 × 100 = **60%**.
5. Pfr.
6. Shoot: cell elongation (bending towards light). Root: elongation is inhibited.
7. The aleurone layer.
8. Radius 6 mm; π × 36 = **113 mm²**.
9. Structural: MRI or CT. Functional: fMRI or PET.
10. (27 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 = **50%**.
11. The medulla oblongata.
12. Blocking reuptake (they inhibit serotonin uptake by the presynaptic neurone).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying light opens the sodium channels in a rod.
- Leaving out the sodium ions when explaining hyperpolarisation.
- Describing habituation as a muscle getting tired.
- Giving only the shoot effect of IAA and omitting the root.
- Saying gibberellin "produces amylase" without mentioning the gene being transcribed.
- Calling the clear zone the part where starch is present.
- Forgetting a boiled-grain control, or not describing how contamination is prevented.
- Writing "the brain" instead of the named region.
- Saying fMRI uses radiation, or that CT shows function.
