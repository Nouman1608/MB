These notes condense content item 1.4.4, Business plans, in Topic 1.4 (Making the business effective) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples are in the [business plans study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-plans/); test yourself with the [business plans practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-plans-practice/). ## What the specification lists

**The role and importance of a business plan**, to identify:

- the business idea
- business aims and objectives
- target market (market research)
- forecast revenue, cost and profit
- cash-flow forecast
- sources of finance
- location
- marketing mix.

**The purpose of planning business activity:** the role and importance of a business plan in minimising risk and obtaining finance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business plan | A written document setting out what a business will do, how, and the finances involved |
| Target market | The group of customers the business aims to sell to |
| Market research | Gathering information about customers, competitors and the market |
| Forecast | An estimate of future figures, such as sales, costs or profit |
| Cash-flow forecast | An estimate of cash coming in and going out each month, with opening and closing balances |
| Source of finance | Where money for the business comes from, such as savings, a loan or an overdraft |
| Risk | The chance of failure or of losing money |
| Margin of safety | Actual or budgeted sales minus break even sales |

## Why each section is there

| Section | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Business idea | What will we sell, and what need does it meet? |
| Aims and objectives | What are we trying to achieve, and by when? |
| Target market (market research) | Who will buy, and is there evidence of demand? |
| Forecast revenue, cost and profit | Will the business make a profit? |
| Cash-flow forecast | Will we have enough cash each month? |
| Sources of finance | Where will the money come from? |
| Location | Where will we operate, and why there? |
| Marketing mix | What price, product, promotion and place will we use? |

## The chain inside a plan

Research → sales estimate → revenue forecast → profit and cash-flow forecasts → finance needed.

Weak research weakens every forecast that follows.

## Minimising risk

- Tests the idea on paper before money is committed.
- Cash-flow forecast warns of shortages so finance can be arranged in advance.
- "What if?" testing shows how far sales could fall before a loss.
- Gives targets to compare with actual results once trading.

## Obtaining finance

- Lenders see evidence of demand and realistic forecasts.
- The cash-flow forecast shows whether repayments can be met.
- The plan shows how much the owner is investing.
- A clear plan shows the owner understands the business, building confidence.

## Limits of a plan

- Forecasts may be wrong, especially with no trading history.
- Conditions change: new rivals, cost rises, new tastes.
- Owners may be over-optimistic.
- Writing a plan takes time.

So: base the plan on research, test it, update it.

## Formulae often used in a plan

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue | price × quantity |
| Total cost | TFC + TVC |
| Break even (units) | fixed cost ÷ (sales price − variable cost) |
| Margin of safety | actual or budgeted sales − break even sales |
| Net cash-flow | cash inflows − cash outflows |
| Closing balance | opening balance + net cash-flow |

## Exam traps

- A plan reduces risk; it does not remove it.
- A plan improves the chance of finance; it does not guarantee it.
- "Importance" questions need judgement, not a list of contents.
- Profit forecast and cash-flow forecast are different sections answering different questions.
- Use the business in the source; say which section of the plan helps it and how.

## Self-test

1. Define a business plan.
2. List four items the specification says a business plan should identify.
3. Why does a business plan include market research on the target market?
4. Give two ways a business plan can reduce risk.
5. Why might a bank refuse a loan to an owner with no business plan?
6. Give one limitation of a business plan.
7. A plan forecasts 250 sales at £40 each. Calculate forecast revenue.
8. Fixed costs are £4,500 and each unit sells for £40 with a variable cost of £25. Calculate break even output.
9. Using questions 7 and 8, calculate the margin of safety.
10. Opening balance is £1,200 and net cash-flow is −£1,750. Calculate the closing balance.
11. Why is a profit forecast not enough on its own to show a bank that a loan can be repaid?
12. Explain why weak market research reduces the value of a business plan.

### Answers

1. A written document setting out what a business will do, how it will do it and the finances involved.
2. Any four from: business idea; aims and objectives; target market (market research); forecast revenue, cost and profit; cash-flow forecast; sources of finance; location; marketing mix.
3. To show there is evidence of demand and who the customers are, which supports the sales forecast.
4. Any two: tests the idea before money is spent; cash-flow forecast warns of shortages; "what if?" testing; targets to check progress.
5. The bank has no evidence that there is demand or that repayments can be made, so the loan looks too risky.
6. Any one: forecasts may be wrong; conditions change; owner optimism; takes time.
7. 250 × £40 = £10,000.
8. £4,500 ÷ (£40 − £25) = £4,500 ÷ £15 = 300 units.
9. 250 − 300 = −50, so there is no margin of safety: forecast sales are 50 units below break even.
10. £1,200 + (−£1,750) = −£550 (an overdrawn balance).
11. Profit does not show when cash arrives; the business could be profitable over a year but short of cash in the months repayments are due.
12. The sales estimate comes from the research, so if the research is weak, the revenue, profit and cash-flow forecasts built on it are unreliable.
