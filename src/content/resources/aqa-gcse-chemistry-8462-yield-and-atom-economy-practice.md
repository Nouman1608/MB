These questions cover section **4.3.3 Yield and atom economy of chemical reactions** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The topic is assessed on Paper 1.
Questions 6 and 9, and parts (a) and (b) of Question 7, test content the specification marks (HT only) and are labelled **Higher tier only**.
Everything else is for both tiers. Relative atomic masses: H 1, C 12, O 16, Mg 24, Fe 56, Cu 63.5, Ca 40.
Show your working.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-yield-and-atom-economy/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-yield-and-atom-economy-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A reaction has a maximum theoretical yield of 24.0 g of product. A student obtains 18.0 g. Calculate the
percentage yield. **[2]**

**2.** Give **two** reasons why the actual yield of a reaction can be less than the maximum theoretical yield. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the atom economy for making calcium oxide, CaO, in the reaction CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ **[2]**

**4.** Ethene reacts with hydrogen: C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ Show that the atom economy for making ethane is 100%. **[2]**

**5.** Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ Calculate the atom economy for making oxygen. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** (Higher tier only) 6.0 g of magnesium is burned in excess oxygen: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

**(a)** Calculate the maximum theoretical mass of magnesium oxide. **[3]**
**(b)** A student collects 8.4 g of magnesium oxide. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**7.** Iron is made from iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ A furnace is charged with
40.0 g of Fe₂O₃. It produces 25.2 g of iron.

**(a)** (Higher tier only) Calculate the maximum theoretical mass of iron. **[3]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the atom economy of the reaction for making iron. **[3]**

**8.** Copper can be made by heating copper(II) oxide in hydrogen: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy for making copper. **[3]**
**(b)** The only other product is water. Suggest why this reaction might be considered a good one. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A company can make a chemical by two routes.

| | Route 1 | Route 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Atom economy | 100% | 52% |
| Percentage yield | 55% | 92% |
| Rate | Slow | Fast |
| By-product | None | Can be sold for use in fertilisers |

Evaluate the two routes and recommend which the company should use. **[6]**

**10.** Explain why using reactions with a high atom economy is important for sustainable development. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 18.0 ÷ 24.0 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: the reaction is **reversible** and does not go to completion; some product is **lost when it
is separated** from the mixture; some reactants react in **different ways** (side reactions). **[2]**

**3.** Mr CaCO₃ = 100; Mr CaO = 56 [1]; 56 ÷ 100 × 100 = **56%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Mr of reactants = 28 + 2 = 30 [1]; Mr of ethane = 30, so 30 ÷ 30 × 100 = **100%** [1] (all atoms end up in
the one product). **[2]**

**5.** Mr H₂O₂ = 34, so 2H₂O₂ = 68 [1]; Mr O₂ = 32 [1]; 32 ÷ 68 × 100 = **47.1%** [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Moles Mg = 6.0 ÷ 24 = 0.25 mol [1]; ratio Mg : MgO = 1 : 1, so 0.25 mol MgO [1]; mass = 0.25 × 40 = **10.0 g** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 8.4 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = **84%** [1]

**7. (a)** Moles Fe₂O₃ = 40.0 ÷ 160 = 0.250 mol [1]; ratio 1 : 2, so 0.500 mol Fe [1]; mass = 0.500 × 56 = **28.0 g**
[1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 25.2 ÷ 28.0 × 100 = **90%** [1]
**(c)** Reactants: 160 + 3 × 28 = 244 [1]; desired product 2 × 56 = 112 [1]; 112 ÷ 244 × 100 = **45.9%** [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Mr CuO = 63.5 + 16 = 79.5; Mr H₂ = 2; total reactants = 81.5 [1]; Mr Cu = 63.5 [1]; 63.5 ÷ 81.5 × 100 = **77.9%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Water is a harmless by-product (or the atom economy is relatively high) [1]

**9.** Marking points, up to 6:
- Route 1 has the higher atom economy, so it produces less waste [1].
- Route 1 has a much lower yield (55% against 92%), so less product is obtained from the same materials [1].
- Route 1 is slow, so less product is made per hour [1].
- Route 2 has a lower atom economy, but its by-product can be sold, which offsets the waste [1].
- Route 2 has a high yield and a fast rate, so it is more economic [1].
- Conclusion: Route 2 recommended, with a reason that refers to the data [1]. **[6]**

**10.** High atom economy means more of the reactants end up as useful products [1], so there is less waste and
the raw materials are used more efficiently / resources last longer [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the wrong value on top in a percentage calculation.
- Missing balancing numbers in the Mr totals.
- Using the experimental masses instead of equation Mr values for atom economy.
- Forgetting to calculate moles before the ratio in a theoretical-mass question.
- Evaluating a pathway without using all the data given.
