**These notes condense optional topic 3.2.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236,
Globalisation, Transport and Communication in Pakistan, examined in Paper 2 Section C.** Learn the
definitions, a balanced list of impacts for globalisation and TNCs, the factors behind transport
patterns, the main projects including CPEC, and how communication and financial networks support
development.

## Syllabus coverage

**9236 topic 3.2.3 (Version 1.9)**
- **3.2.3.1** Globalisation impacts; TNC characteristics and impacts; consumer cultures
- **3.2.3.2** Distribution of road, rail, air, river, sea; road and rail development; dry ports, seaports, airports; transport and development, CPEC
- **3.2.3.3** Communication networks: factors, opportunities, challenges; financial systems

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | Growing interconnection of countries through trade, investment, people, technology and culture |
| TNC | Transnational corporation operating in more than one country |
| Joint venture | Partnership between a foreign company and a local firm |
| Digital consumer | Someone who researches, buys and pays for goods online |
| Dry port | Inland customs and container terminal linked to a seaport |
| CPEC | China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, launched 2015 |
| Deep-water port | Port able to take very large ships, e.g. Gwadar |
| Financial inclusion | Access to banking and payments for everyone |
| Mobile wallet | Phone-based account for payments and transfers |

## Globalisation: impacts

- **Economy +:** export markets, FDI, outsourcing jobs. **−:** competition, global shocks.
- **People +:** remittances, information, choice. **−:** urban–rural digital divide, cultural tension.
- **Environment +:** access to clean technology. **−:** pollution from export industries, more waste.

## TNCs: impacts

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Jobs and training | Profits sent abroad |
| Tax revenue | Local firms out-competed |
| Technology and skills | Pressure on labour standards |
| Exports and investment in suppliers | Pollution and water use |
| Better products for consumers | Decisions made abroad |

Examples: Unilever, Nestlé, car joint ventures (Pak Suzuki; Indus Motor assembling Toyota),
soft-drink and fast-food brands, technology and telecoms firms.

## Consumer change

- Smartphones and cheap data → online shopping (e.g. Daraz) and mobile payments.
- Global brands popular with young urban consumers; local labels compete.
- Shopping malls in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad.

## Transport: distribution and projects

- **Dense:** Indus Plain (flat, populous). **Sparse:** Balochistan and mountains (relief, hazards, low demand).
- **Air:** links big cities and remote north (Gilgit, Skardu).
- **River:** very limited — seasonal flow and barrages.
- **Sea:** Karachi Port, Port Qasim, Gwadar.
- **Projects:** M-2 and CPEC motorways; Karakoram Highway; ML-1 upgrade (planned); Lahore Orange Line (2020); Islamabad International Airport (2018); dry ports (Lahore, Faisalabad, Sialkot).

## CPEC: for and against

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Fills gaps in roads, power and ports | Large debts to repay |
| Jobs in construction and power | Many contracts and skilled jobs go to Chinese firms |
| Links Gwadar to China and Central Asia | Security risks; local benefits in Balochistan questioned |
| Special economic zones for industry | Environmental and land impacts |

## Communication and finance

- **Growth:** private TV channels; mobile phones; 3G and 4G licences auctioned in 2014.
- **Opportunities:** e-commerce, online learning, telemedicine, freelancing and IT exports, farm prices.
- **Challenges:** terrain and cost; power cuts; low digital literacy; gender gap; cyber-security.
- **Finance:** State Bank of Pakistan; Raast instant payments (2021); Islamic banking; microfinance; JazzCash and Easypaisa mobile wallets; stock exchange.

## Transport modes compared

| Mode | Strength in Pakistan | Weakness in Pakistan |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Flexible, door-to-door, carries most freight and passengers | Congestion, accidents, damage from overloaded trucks and floods |
| Rail | Cheap for bulky goods such as coal, cement and containers | Old track and engines; slow; financial losses |
| Air | Fast between cities and to remote northern towns | Expensive; flights cancelled in mountain weather |
| Sea | Carries almost all international freight | Congestion at Karachi; Gwadar not yet fully used |
| River | Potential for cheap bulk transport | Seasonal flow, barrages and silt prevent regular use |

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why river transport is little used in Pakistan. **[3]**

**Model answer:** River flow varies greatly between seasons, very high during the monsoon and
snowmelt and low in winter, so channels are unreliable [1]. Barrages and canal headworks block
navigation along the Indus and its tributaries [1]. Shifting sandbanks and silt make channels
shallow, and road and rail offer faster, more reliable routes [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Describing globalisation only as "TNCs".** It also includes migration, culture, technology and trade.
- **One-sided CPEC answers.** Always balance benefits against debt, local benefit and security.
- **Forgetting river and sea transport.** The specification lists five modes.
- **Ignoring finance.** Financial systems are named in the specification; give examples such as mobile wallets.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are mobile wallets important for development?** They let people without bank accounts receive
wages, remittances and government payments safely, which supports small businesses and women's
financial independence.

**What is the difference between a seaport and a dry port?** A seaport handles ships; a dry port is
inland, linked to the seaport, and handles customs and containers.
