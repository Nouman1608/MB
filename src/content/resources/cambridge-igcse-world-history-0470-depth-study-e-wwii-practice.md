These questions cover all four key questions of Depth Study E: The Second World War in Europe and the Asia-Pacific,
1939-c.1945.

## Section A

**1.** Give **three** features of *Blitzkrieg*. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why the Vichy government collaborated with Germany. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** reasons why the RAF was able to win the Battle of Britain. **[3]**

**4.** Give **two** American actions in 1941 that worsened relations with Japan. **[2]**

**5.** State **three** effects of Allied bombing on German civilians. **[3]**

**6.** Identify **three** stages in the Nazi treatment of Jews in occupied Poland. **[3]**

**7.** Give **two** ways in which the Allies consolidated their victory over Japan. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the evacuation from Dunkirk in 1940. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the impact of Japanese control on the people of Malaya and Singapore. **[4]**

**10.** Why did Hitler invade the Soviet Union in 1941? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Why was the Battle of Midway a turning point? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** "Resistance movements in France were more effective than resistance in Malaya." How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

**13.** "The Allied advance through Italy was of little importance in the defeat of Nazi Germany." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: surprise attacks (1); dive-bombers and aircraft destroying defences and communications
(1); tanks breaking through at weak points (1); motorised infantry following quickly (1); bypassing strongpoints and
encircling the enemy (1); speed, so the enemy could not organise a defence (1); radio communication between units (1).
**[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: France had been defeated and Pétain believed further resistance was useless (1); to
protect French people from harsher German rule (1); many in the government shared Nazi hostility to communism and
Jews (1); to keep control of the French empire and fleet (1); the hope of better terms after a German victory (1).
**[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: radar gave early warning of raids (1); Fighter Command's control system directed
fighters where they were needed (1); Spitfires and Hurricanes were effective fighters (1); British pilots fought over
home territory, so those shot down could fly again (1); British aircraft production outpaced losses (1); German
bombers lacked fighter protection at the limit of their range (1); the Luftwaffe switched from airfields to London in
September 1940 (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: Japanese assets in the USA were frozen (1); oil exports to Japan were stopped (1); the USA
demanded that Japan withdraw from China and Indochina (1); aid was sent to China (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: hundreds of thousands killed (1); firestorms in cities such as Hamburg (1943) and
Dresden (1945) (1); millions made homeless (1); children evacuated to the countryside (1); damage to transport and
supplies of food and fuel (1); nights spent in shelters and bunkers (1); morale weakened but did not collapse (1).
**[3]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Jews were forced to wear the Star of David and lost property and jobs (1); they were
moved into sealed ghettos such as Warsaw and Łódź (1); starvation and disease killed many in the ghettos (1); mass
shootings by the Einsatzgruppen after 1941 (1); deportation to death camps such as Treblinka and Auschwitz-Birkenau
(1); systematic murder in gas chambers (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: Japan was occupied by American forces under General MacArthur (1); a new democratic
constitution was introduced, renouncing war (1); the army and navy were disbanded (1); Japanese leaders were tried at
the Tokyo war crimes trials (1); the emperor was kept as a symbol but lost his political power (1). **[2]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: the German breakthrough in May 1940 trapped British and French troops on the Channel
coast (1); the operation, codenamed Dynamo, ran from late May to early June (1); over 300,000 troops were taken off (1);
Royal Navy ships and hundreds of small civilian boats were used (1); the RAF tried to protect the beaches from the
Luftwaffe (1); German tanks had halted before reaching the town (1); almost all heavy equipment was abandoned (1); it
saved the core of the British army but France fell weeks later (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: thousands of Chinese men were killed in the Sook Ching massacres (1); the Kempeitai used
arrest, torture and executions (1); food shortages and malnutrition (1); forced labour, including on the
Burma-Thailand railway (1); the Japanese printed occupation money that became worthless (1); Japanese language and
customs were imposed in schools (1); different ethnic groups were treated differently, increasing tensions (1); women
were forced into sexual slavery (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **Lebensraum**, because Hitler had long planned to take land in the east for German settlement, as set out in
*Mein Kampf*; **ideology**, because he hated communism and saw Slavs and Jews as racial enemies; **resources**, because
Ukraine's grain and the Caucasus oil would let Germany fight a long war; **belief in a quick victory**, because the
Purges had weakened the Red Army and its poor performance against Finland suggested it would collapse; **Britain**,
because defeating the USSR would remove Britain's last hope of an ally in Europe. **[6]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content: **loss of carriers**, because Japan lost four aircraft carriers,
the core of its striking power, so it could no longer attack at will; **loss of pilots**, because experienced aircrew
could not be replaced quickly; **American industrial strength**, because the USA could build new carriers far faster
than Japan, so the balance of naval power shifted permanently; **the initiative**, because Japan's expansion in the
Pacific was halted and the USA could begin its counter-offensive at Guadalcanal; **morale**, because the victory came
only six months after Pearl Harbor and showed Japan could be beaten. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** French resistance sent intelligence to the Allies before D-Day; it sabotaged railways and communications,
slowing German reinforcements to Normandy; it helped Allied airmen escape; Jean Moulin united groups under de Gaulle;
the resistance helped liberate Paris in August 1944. **Disagree:** the French resistance was divided between
communist and non-communist groups for much of the war; German reprisals such as Oradour-sur-Glane were very costly;
the MPAJA in Malaya tied down Japanese forces in difficult jungle terrain and, with Force 136, gathered intelligence;
Malayan resistance faced a much harsher occupation with less outside help; neither movement could defeat the occupier
alone. **Judgement:** for example, French resistance was more effective because it was closely linked to a major Allied
invasion, while Malayan resistance never had that opportunity. **[10]**

**13.** Levels as for Question 12. Indicative content. **Agree:** progress was slow after Salerno, with heavy losses at
Monte Cassino; the mountains favoured the defenders; the war in Italy lasted until May 1945; the decisive blows came
from the Red Army in the East and from D-Day; Italy drew Allied resources away from France. **Disagree:** the
invasion of Sicily led to Mussolini's fall and Italy's surrender, removing Germany's main ally; German divisions had
to be sent to occupy Italy and hold the line; it gave the Allies airfields to bomb southern Germany and its allies;
the Mediterranean was opened to Allied shipping; it gave experience in amphibious landings used on D-Day. **Judgement:**
for example, Italy was not the decisive front, but it weakened Germany by tying down troops and removing an ally, so
it was more than of little importance. **[10]**
