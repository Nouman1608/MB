## The two statements

1. **Identify** in diagrams and images the alimentary canal (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine: duodenum and ileum; large intestine: colon, rectum, anus) and the associated organs (salivary glands, pancreas, liver, gall bladder).
2. **Describe** their functions in relation to ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion.

## Order of the alimentary canal

```
mouth → oesophagus → stomach → duodenum → ileum → colon → rectum → anus
```

Memory line: "**M**y **O**ld **S**ister **D**rives **I**n **C**old **R**ainy **A**utumns."

- Small intestine = duodenum + ileum.
- Large intestine = colon + rectum + anus.
- Associated organs (food does **not** pass through): salivary glands, pancreas, liver, gall bladder.

## Key terms

| Term | Syllabus definition or recall |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | taking substances, e.g. food and drink, into the body |
| Digestion | the breakdown of food |
| Absorption | movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood |
| Assimilation | uptake and use of nutrients by cells |
| Egestion | removal of undigested food from the body as faeces |
| Alimentary canal | the tube from mouth to anus through which food passes |
| Associated organ | organ that secretes into the canal through a duct but does not carry food |
| Bile | made by the liver, stored in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum |
| Pancreatic juice | made by the pancreas; contains amylase, protease and lipase; enters the duodenum |

## Diagram clues

- The person faces you: body's left = your right.
- Stomach: J-shaped bag on your right, under the diaphragm.
- Liver: largest organ, on your left, over part of the stomach; gall bladder underneath it.
- Pancreas: below the stomach, in the loop of the duodenum.
- Duodenum: where the bile and pancreatic ducts join.
- Ileum: coiled tube in the middle.
- Colon: wide tube around the outside of the ileum.

## Organ → process → what happens

| Organ | Process | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | ingestion, digestion | food enters; teeth break it up; salivary amylase starts on starch |
| Salivary glands | digestion | secrete saliva with amylase |
| Oesophagus | moves food | carries food to the stomach |
| Stomach | digestion | churns food; protease and hydrochloric acid in gastric juice |
| Duodenum | digestion | receives bile and pancreatic juice |
| Ileum | digestion, absorption | digestion completed; nutrients and most water absorbed into blood |
| Pancreas | digestion | secretes amylase, protease and lipase into the duodenum |
| Liver | digestion, assimilation | makes bile; cells use absorbed nutrients |
| Gall bladder | digestion | stores and releases bile |
| Colon | absorption | some water absorbed |
| Rectum | egestion | stores faeces |
| Anus | egestion | faeces leave the body |

## Method in steps: labelling an unfamiliar diagram

1. Find the mouth and the anus to fix the two ends of the canal.
2. Find the stomach (the widest bag below the diaphragm) and the liver (the largest organ beside it).
3. Find the ducts: where they join the gut is the duodenum; the sac on one duct under the liver is the gall bladder; the gland on the other duct is the pancreas.
4. The long coiled tube after the duodenum is the ileum; the wider tube framing it is the colon; the short straight end is the rectum.
5. Check each label against the order mouth → anus before you write it.

## Answer patterns

- **"Describe the function of organ X"**: name the process (ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation or egestion), then say what happens there.
- **"Follow the food"**: organs in order, with one process or secretion for each.
- **"Suggest the effect of removing organ X"**: say which process is lost or reduced, then the consequence for the person.

## Pairs to keep apart

- **Absorption vs assimilation:** into the blood vs used by cells.
- **Egestion vs excretion:** undigested food (never absorbed) vs waste made by metabolism.
- **Liver vs gall bladder:** makes bile vs stores bile.
- **Duodenum vs ileum:** first part receiving secretions vs long part where most absorption happens.
- **Colon vs rectum:** some water absorbed vs faeces stored.

## Exam traps

- The gall bladder does not make bile.
- The pancreas and liver are not part of the alimentary canal.
- Absorption of nutrients happens in the small intestine, not the stomach.
- Most water is absorbed in the small intestine; only some is absorbed in the colon.
- Use syllabus names: "oesophagus" not "gullet"; "colon" not "bowel".
- Definitions must match the syllabus: absorption is "from the intestines into the blood".

## Self-test

1. Name the two parts of the small intestine, in order.
2. Name the three parts of the large intestine, in order.
3. Define ingestion.
4. Define assimilation.
5. Which organ makes bile, and which stores it?
6. Into which part of the alimentary canal do bile and pancreatic juice flow?
7. Name the organ that secretes amylase, protease and lipase into the small intestine.
8. Which region absorbs most of the water from food and drink?
9. State the function of the rectum.
10. Explain why the pancreas is an associated organ and not part of the alimentary canal.
11. Explain why egestion is not an example of excretion.
12. On a diagram of the abdomen, a large organ lies on the viewer's left, just under the diaphragm. Name it.

### Answers

1. Duodenum, then ileum.
2. Colon, rectum, anus.
3. The taking of substances, such as food and drink, into the body.
4. The uptake and use of nutrients by cells.
5. The liver makes bile; the gall bladder stores it.
6. The duodenum.
7. The pancreas.
8. The small intestine.
9. It stores faeces before egestion.
10. Food does not pass through it; it secretes pancreatic juice into the duodenum through a duct.
11. Faeces are mainly undigested food that was never absorbed, so they are not waste made by the body's metabolism.
12. The liver.
