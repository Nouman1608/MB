These notes condense sub-topic **4.9 Barriers to economic growth and/or economic development** from the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022). It is SL and HL content. For full explanations and a worked country profile read the [4.9 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-4-9/); then test yourself with the [4.9 practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-4-9-practice/). Countries and figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic growth | An increase in real GDP (or real GDP per capita) over time |
| Economic development | Improvement in living standards, health, education, freedom and opportunity for the population |
| Poverty cycle (trap) | Linked factors that keep income low: low income → low saving and tax → low investment → low productivity → low income |
| Appropriate technology | Technology suited to local skills, costs and conditions |
| Informal economy | Activity not registered or taxed by the state |
| Capital flight | Large outflows of savings and investment funds from a country |
| Indebtedness | High debt whose servicing absorbs export earnings and government revenue |
| Debt service ratio | Debt service ÷ export earnings × 100 |
| Property rights | Legally secure ownership of land and assets |
| Good governance | Accountable, effective and honest government |

## Each barrier in one line

**Economic**

- **Rising inequality:** the poor cannot afford education or start businesses; gains go to few.
- **Infrastructure and appropriate technology:** poor roads, power and telecoms raise costs; unsuitable technology goes unused.
- **Low human capital:** poor health and schooling reduce productivity.
- **Primary sector dependence:** volatile prices, low value added, weak long-run demand growth.
- **Lack of access to international markets:** barriers abroad, poor transport and standards limit exports.
- **Informal economy:** narrow tax base; workers lack protection; firms stay small without credit.
- **Capital flight:** less domestic investment; weaker currency.
- **Indebtedness:** debt service crowds out imports and public spending.
- **Geography, including landlocked countries:** higher transport costs; reliance on neighbours.

**Political and social**

- **Weak institutional framework:** unreliable courts, ineffective tax collection, weak banks, insecure property rights.
- **Gender inequality:** talent wasted; lower returns from family health and education.
- **Lack of good governance and corruption:** funds diverted; investors deterred.
- **Unequal political power and status:** policy serves a narrow group.

## Growth or development?

| Mainly growth | Mainly development | Both |
|---|---|---|
| Capital flight, indebtedness, market access | Inequality, unequal political power | Human capital, gender inequality, institutions, corruption |

## How barriers reinforce each other

- No property rights → no collateral → no bank credit → household stays in the poverty trap.
- Primary dependence + debt → a price fall raises the debt service ratio.
- Informal economy → narrow tax base → more borrowing and less spending on schools.
- Corruption → capital flight → less investment → lower growth.
- Landlocked → higher transport costs → weaker access to international markets.

**Worked reminder.** Exports are 8.0 billion, with tea 70% (5.6 billion). Debt service is 2.0 billion, a ratio of 2.0 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = **25%**. Tea prices fall 30%: tea earnings 5.6 × 0.7 = 3.92, so exports fall to 6.32 billion and the debt service ratio rises to 2.0 ÷ 6.32 × 100 ≈ **31.6%**.

## Significance criteria

1. **Scale:** how many people or how much output?
2. **Root or symptom:** does it cause other barriers?
3. **Reversibility:** can policy change it?
4. **Vulnerability:** does it expose the economy to shocks?
5. **Time horizon:** urgent now or important over decades?

A strong judgement often separates the **root** barrier (usually institutional) from the most **urgent** one (often debt or price exposure).

**Sentence frame:** "The most significant barrier in [country] is [X], because it [causes Y and Z / affects most people]; however, [W] is more urgent in the short term because [data point]."

## Exam traps

- Listing barriers without applying them to the country given.
- Treating all barriers as equal.
- Confusing growth with development.
- Calling geography the main barrier without noting it cannot be changed.
- Forgetting that barriers interact.
- Quoting a ratio without saying what it means for the economy.

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish economic growth from economic development.
2. Draw the four links of a poverty cycle in words.
3. Why does dependence on primary products make an economy vulnerable?
4. Debt service is 1.2 billion and exports 5.0 billion. Calculate the debt service ratio.
5. Explain how a large informal economy can weaken public services.
6. Why does capital flight reduce growth?
7. How can missing property rights keep a household in poverty?
8. Give one reason gender inequality reduces growth.
9. Name the four parts of a weak institutional framework listed in the guide.
10. Why might corruption act like a tax on business?
11. Give one barrier that mainly affects development rather than growth.
12. Give two criteria for judging the significance of a barrier.

### Answers

1. Growth is a rise in real GDP; development is wider improvement in living standards, health, education and opportunity.
2. Low income → low saving and tax revenue → low investment in capital, health and education → low productivity → low income.
3. Prices are volatile, so export earnings and tax revenue swing sharply; value added is low.
4. 1.2 ÷ 5.0 × 100 = **24%**.
5. Informal activity is untaxed, so revenue is low and the state cannot fund schools, clinics and infrastructure.
6. Savings and investment funds leave, so less is available for domestic capital formation; the currency may also weaken.
7. Without title to land, people cannot offer collateral, so they cannot borrow to invest in productive assets.
8. Women's skills are under-used, so the labour force and productivity are lower than they could be.
9. Legal system, taxation structures, banking system, property rights.
10. Bribes raise the cost of doing business unpredictably, like an arbitrary tax, deterring investment.
11. Rising inequality, or unequal political power and status.
12. Any two: scale; root or symptom; reversibility; vulnerability to shocks; time horizon.
