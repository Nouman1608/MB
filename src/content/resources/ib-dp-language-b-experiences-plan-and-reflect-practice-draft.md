# Experiences: plan and reflect

The model texts are in English and every example is only an illustration of a skill; do the language-producing tasks in the target language of your school course and ask your school teacher to check them. The brief's recommended topics are optional and its possible questions are only possible, so your teacher chooses what to teach. The IB holds copyright in its papers, so these questions do not copy their format or marks.

[Language B course hub](/boards/ib/ib-dp/language-b/) · [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-dp/language-b/). Applies to SL and HL, first assessment 2020, examined until the new syllabus is first assessed in May 2029.

## Questions

**1.** Name the guiding principle of Experiences as the brief states it, and two optional recommended topics. **[3]**

### Passage A (original text written for Marlbridge)

> **The first day of the market.** Every October my aunt opens a stall that her mother ran before her. This year she let me stand beside her. "Do not shout," she whispered. "Let them smell the bread first." By noon we had sold out, and my aunt, who rarely smiles, hummed all the way home.

**2.** (a) When does the aunt open the stall? **[1]** (b) What advice does she give? **[1]** (c) What does the humming show? **[1]**

**3.** Explain how the passage links past, present and future. **[3]**

**4.** Which recommended topic fits Passage A best, and which possible question? Justify. **[3]**

**5.** Write a plan (not the full text) for a Paper 1 task: *Write a letter to a grandparent asking about a childhood custom.* Name context, audience, purpose, three paragraphs and two structures you would use. **[5]**

**6.** Rewrite this flat sentence as a reflective one: "I moved to a new city. It was hard. Now it is fine." **[3]**

## Model answers

**1.** "Explore and tell the stories of the events, experiences and journeys that shape our lives." [1]; any two topics from: leisure activities, holidays and travel, life stories, rites of passage, customs and traditions, migration [2].

**2.** (a) Every October [1]. (b) Do not shout; let them smell the bread first [1]. (c) Pride or happiness, because they sold out [1].

**3.** Past: her mother ran the stall [1]. Present: the narrator stands beside her this year [1]. Future: the narrator is learning the work and may continue it (inference accepted) [1].

**4.** Customs and traditions, or life stories [1]. Second possible question or first (past shaping present) [1]. Reason from the text [1].

**5.** Context: a family letter [1]; audience: a grandparent [1]; purpose: to ask and show interest [1]; paragraphs: greeting and reason, questions about the custom, closing with a personal link [1]; structures such as a conditional or a past-habit form [1].

**6.** Accept reflection with a bridge, for example "When I moved to a new city I found it hard, but looking back I realise it taught me to make friends quickly." [1 past, 1 contrast, 1 reflection].
