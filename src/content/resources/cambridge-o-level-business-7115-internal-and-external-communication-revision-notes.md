Condensed recall for subtopic **2.4 Internal and external communication**. For worked recommendations and examples, use the [Internal and external communication study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-internal-and-external-communication/).

## The process

**Sender → message → medium → receiver → feedback.**

- **Effective communication**: the message is received, understood and acted on.
- **One-way**: no feedback (notices, most letters).
- **Two-way**: feedback given (meetings, phone calls, video calls).
- **Internal**: within the business (managers, employees, departments).
- **External**: with people outside (customers, suppliers, banks, government, the public).

## Why it matters

Correct instructions → fewer mistakes. Departments coordinate → right stock, deadlines met. Staff informed → better motivation. Accurate information → better decisions. Customers and suppliers well informed → good reputation, smooth orders. Safety rules understood → fewer accidents.

## Methods

**Verbal**: face-to-face, meetings, telephone, video calls. Fast feedback; little or no record.

**Written**: letters, memos, reports, notices, emails. Permanent record; feedback slower.

**Visual**: charts, diagrams, posters, presentations. Make data and instructions easier to grasp; can be misread without explanation.

**IT-based**: email, text and instant messaging, video conferencing, intranet, website, social media.

| IT method | Main benefit | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Email | Fast, cheap, to many people, record kept | Overload; may be ignored or misread |
| Text / instant message | Instant for short, urgent messages | Unsuitable for detail |
| Video conferencing | Face-to-face across distance; saves travel | Needs equipment and reliable internet |
| Intranet | Information always available to staff | Staff may not check it |
| Website / social media | Reaches many customers cheaply | Public criticism; must be kept updated |

## Choosing the method: seven factors

1. Urgency
2. Need for a written record
3. Complexity of the message
4. Number and location of receivers
5. Confidentiality
6. Cost
7. Sensitivity of the news

**Quick matches:** sensitive news to one person → private meeting, then letter. Urgent change to many staff → briefing plus notice or email. Formal agreement with a supplier or bank → letter or email for a record. Routine update to distant managers → video call.

## Barriers and remedies

| Barrier | Cause | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Jargon | Sender uses technical words | Plain language suited to the receiver |
| Unclear message | Poor planning, too long | Short, structured message; key points first |
| Wrong medium | Method does not suit message | Use the seven factors |
| Long chain of command | Message passes through many people | Communicate directly with those affected |
| Noise or technical failure | Environment or equipment | Quieter location; back-up method |
| Receiver not listening or distrustful | Attitude, poor relationships | Build trust; involve staff |
| Language or cultural differences | Diverse workforce or customers | Translation, visual aids, check understanding |
| No feedback | One-way method | Two-way method; ask for confirmation |

## Problems of ineffective communication

Mistakes and waste; low morale and rumours; poor coordination; missed deadlines; lost customers; accidents; poor decisions.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Communication | Transferring a message from a sender to a receiver |
| Effective communication | The message is received, understood and acted on |
| Medium | The method used to send a message |
| Feedback | The receiver's response to a message |
| Internal communication | Communication between people within a business |
| External communication | Communication between a business and people outside it |
| One-way communication | Communication with no feedback |
| Two-way communication | Communication in which the receiver responds |
| Communication barrier | Anything that stops a message being received or understood |
| Information overload | Receiving too many messages to deal with properly |

## Exam traps

- **Effective means understood**, not just sent.
- **Email is not always best**: sensitive or complex messages need two-way methods.
- **Give benefits and limitations in context**: who receives the message and why it matters.
- **A barrier answer needs cause and remedy.**
- **External communication includes the government and the public**, not only customers.
- **Recommend one method** (or a main method plus a back-up) and justify with factors.

## Self-test

1. Name the five parts of the communication process.
2. What makes communication effective?
3. Give one example each of internal and external communication.
4. Give one benefit and one limitation of a notice board.
5. Why might a business use video conferencing instead of travel to a meeting?
6. Give one limitation of email.
7. What is meant by two-way communication?
8. Name three factors that affect the choice of communication method.
9. Which method suits telling a customer that their order will be late, and why?
10. Explain how a long chain of command can be a barrier.
11. Suggest one way to reduce a language barrier.
12. Give two problems caused by ineffective communication.

## Answers

1. Sender, message, medium, receiver, feedback.
2. The receiver gets the message, understands it as intended and acts on it.
3. Internal: a manager emailing staff about new shift times. External: a business writing to a supplier about an order.
4. Benefit: cheap and reaches many people. Limitation: may not be read, and gives no feedback.
5. It allows face-to-face discussion while saving travel time and cost.
6. Any one: information overload; can be ignored; tone may be misread; not suitable for sensitive news.
7. The receiver responds, so the sender can check the message was understood.
8. Any three: urgency, need for a record, complexity, number and location of receivers, confidentiality, cost, sensitivity.
9. A phone call (or personal email) so the customer is told quickly and can respond, followed by written confirmation of the new date.
10. The message passes through many people, so it may be delayed, changed or lost before it reaches the receiver.
11. Any one: translate the message; use visual aids or pictures; check understanding with feedback.
12. Any two: mistakes and waste; low morale; poor coordination; lost customers; accidents.
