## Questions

**1.** (a) Define the resistance of a component. **[1]**
**(b)** A p.d. of 9.0 V produces a current of 15 mA in a resistor. Calculate its resistance. **[2]**

**2.** (a) State Ohm's law. **[2]**
**(b)** A filament lamp passes 0.20 A when the p.d. is 2.0 V and 0.50 A when the p.d. is 10.0 V. Calculate the resistance at each p.d. and state whether the lamp is ohmic. **[3]**

**3.** Describe the I-V graph of a filament lamp for p.d. values of both signs, and explain its shape. **[4]**

**4.** The table gives data for an LED.

| p.d. / V | 0 | 1.0 | 1.6 | 1.8 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| current / mA | 0 | 0 | 0.1 | 5 | 20 |

**(a)** Estimate the p.d. at which the LED starts to conduct appreciably. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance of the LED at 2.0 V. **[2]**
**(c)** The LED is to run at 20 mA from a 9.0 V supply with a series resistor. Calculate the resistor's value. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how you would obtain the I-V characteristic of a semiconductor diode in the laboratory. Include the circuit and any precautions. **[5]**

**6.** (a) State and explain how the resistance of a light-dependent resistor changes as the light intensity increases. **[2]**
**(b)** An NTC thermistor has resistance 4.7 kΩ at 20 °C and 1.2 kΩ at 50 °C. A 6.0 V cell pack is placed across it at each temperature. Calculate the current each time. **[2]**

**7.** A constantan wire has length 2.40 m and diameter 0.20 mm. Its resistance is 37.4 Ω.
**(a)** Calculate the resistivity of constantan. **[3]**
**(b)** A second wire of the same material and length has diameter 0.40 mm. Calculate its resistance. **[2]**

**8.** A student determines the resistivity of a metal wire. She measures the diameter with a micrometer and the resistance of different lengths of wire. The graph of resistance against length is a straight line with gradient 10.2 Ω m⁻¹. The mean diameter is 0.25 mm.
**(a)** Explain why she measures the diameter at several places and in perpendicular directions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistivity. **[3]**
**(c)** The diameter has an uncertainty of ±0.01 mm. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the area. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why she uses a small current. **[1]**

**9.** Compare and explain how the resistivity of a metal and of a semiconductor change when the temperature rises. **[4]**

**10.** A heated rear window is rated 90 W at 12 V.
**(a)** Calculate the current. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its resistance. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in 10 minutes. **[2]**

**11.** An electric shower is rated 7.5 kW. It runs for 6.0 minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred in kW h. **[2]**
**(b)** Electricity costs 28 p per kW h. Calculate the cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Convert the energy to joules. **[1]**
**(d)** The water gains 2.43 MJ of thermal energy. Calculate the efficiency. **[2]**

**12.** A heating element is made from nichrome wire of diameter 0.40 mm. It is to dissipate 1.2 kW from a 230 V supply. Take the resistivity of nichrome as 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m.
**(a)** Calculate the current. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance needed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the cross-sectional area of the wire. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the length of wire needed. **[2]**
**(e)** The element runs for 40 minutes at 30 p per kW h. Calculate the cost. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Resistance is the p.d. across a component divided by the current through it (R = V/I). [1]
**(b)** R = V/I = 9.0 / 0.015 [1] = **600 Ω** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A definition scores best when it says "p.d. across" and "current through" the same component. Convert milliamperes to amperes before dividing.

**2.** (a) The current through a conductor is directly proportional to the p.d. across it [1], provided the temperature (and other physical conditions) stay constant. [1]
**(b)** R = 2.0 / 0.20 = 10 Ω [1]; R = 10.0 / 0.50 = 20 Ω [1]. The resistance changes, so I is not proportional to V and the lamp is **non-ohmic**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Leaving out the constant-temperature condition loses the second point. A conclusion needs to refer to the calculated values.

**3.** The graph passes through the origin and is symmetrical, so the curve for negative p.d. is a rotation of the positive one. [1] The gradient decreases (the line flattens) as the magnitude of p.d. increases. [1] The current heats the filament, so the metal ions vibrate with larger amplitude. [1] There are more collisions with conduction electrons, so resistance (V/I) rises. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A good answer links the shape to temperature and collisions. "The resistance increases" with no cause is only a restatement of the graph.

**4.** (a) About **1.6 V** to 1.8 V (current becomes appreciable there). [1]
**(b)** R = V/I = 2.0 / 0.020 [1] = **100 Ω** [1]
**(c)** p.d. across the resistor = 9.0 - 2.0 = 7.0 V [1]; R = 7.0 / 0.020 = **350 Ω** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a series-resistor question, subtract the LED's p.d. from the supply before using V/I. Using the full supply p.d. gives 450 Ω.

**5.** Circuit: the supply connected across the whole track of a variable resistor so the p.d. can start from zero (potential divider). [1] The diode in series with an ammeter, the voltmeter connected across the diode only. [1] A protective resistor in series so the forward current stays below the rated maximum. [1] Increase the p.d. in equal steps, recording V and I at each, and take extra readings near the threshold. [1] Reverse the diode (or the supply) and repeat to get the reverse current; plot I against V. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A reason for each feature is rewarded more than a list of apparatus. Mention that the diode is damaged by a large forward current if you name the protective resistor.

**6.** (a) The resistance **decreases**. [1] More light releases more charge carriers in the semiconductor. [1]
**(b)** At 20 °C: I = 6.0 / 4700 = 1.3 mA. [1] At 50 °C: I = 6.0 / 1200 = 5.0 mA. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert kΩ to Ω before dividing. Give the explanation as more charge carriers, not faster electrons.

**7.** (a) A = π(0.10 × 10⁻³)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁸ m² [1]; ρ = RA/L = 37.4 × 3.14 × 10⁻⁸ / 2.40 [1] = **4.9 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m** [1]
**(b)** Doubling the diameter makes A four times larger, so R is a quarter: [1] 37.4 / 4 = **9.35 Ω**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Diameter must be halved to a radius, and mm to m, before squaring. Scaling arguments like part (b) are quicker and show understanding.

**8.** (a) The wire may not have a uniform diameter or a circular cross-section. [1] Repeating at several places and orientations and taking a mean reduces the effect of this. [1]
**(b)** A = π(0.125 × 10⁻³)² = 4.91 × 10⁻⁸ m² [1]; ρ = gradient × A = 10.2 × 4.91 × 10⁻⁸ [1] = **5.0 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m** [1]
**(c)** % uncertainty in d = 0.01 / 0.25 × 100 = 4%, so in A it is **8%**. [1]
**(d)** A large current heats the wire, which changes its resistivity. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The gradient of an R-L graph is ρ/A, so multiply by A and not by L. The doubling for a squared quantity is a common check-point.

**9.** Metal: resistivity **increases**. [1] The ions vibrate more, so conduction electrons collide with them more often (the number density of electrons hardly changes). [1] Semiconductor: resistivity **decreases**. [1] More charge carriers are released as thermal energy rises, which outweighs the extra collisions. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each material needs both a direction and a reason. Writing "electrons move faster" for the semiconductor does not explain the fall in resistivity.

**10.** (a) I = P/V = 90 / 12 [1] = **7.5 A** [1]
**(b)** R = V/I = 12 / 7.5 = **1.6 Ω** (or V²/P). [1]
**(c)** t = 600 s; W = Pt = 90 × 600 [1] = **5.4 × 10⁴ J** (54 kJ) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert minutes to seconds when the answer is in joules. Check part (c) with W = VIt = 12 × 7.5 × 600.

**11.** (a) t = 0.10 h; E = 7.5 × 0.10 [1] = **0.75 kW h** [1]
**(b)** Cost = 0.75 × 28 = **21 p** [1]
**(c)** 0.75 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = **2.7 × 10⁶ J** [1]
**(d)** efficiency = 2.43 / 2.70 × 100% [1] = **90%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use input energy in the denominator. Work in kW and hours throughout part (a) and only convert to joules at the end.

**12.** (a) I = P/V = 1200 / 230 = **5.2 A** [1]
**(b)** R = V²/P = 230² / 1200 [1] = **44 Ω** [1]
**(c)** A = π(0.20 × 10⁻³)² = **1.26 × 10⁻⁷ m²** [1]
**(d)** L = RA/ρ = 44.1 × 1.26 × 10⁻⁷ / 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ [1] = **5.0 m** [1]
**(e)** Energy = 1.2 × (40/60) = 0.80 kW h [1]; cost = 0.80 × 30 = **24 p** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values until the end: using 44 Ω rather than 44.08 Ω shifts the length slightly. Rearrange R = ρL/A before substituting.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating Ohm's law without the constant-temperature condition.
- Calling a lamp "ohmic" or "not ohmic" without calculated values of V/I.
- Taking the slope of a curved graph as resistance.
- Using diameter as radius, or not converting mm and mm² to metres.
- Subtracting nothing for the LED's p.d. in a series-resistor question.
- Explaining thermistors and LDRs with "electrons move faster" instead of "more charge carriers".
- Mixing kW with seconds or W with hours, so kW h and joules get confused.
- Using output energy rather than input energy when finding the cost or efficiency.
- Omitting units or giving too many significant figures.
