This study guide covers **section 1.6, Percentages**, in topic 1 (Numbers and the number system) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017). Statements 1.6 A to G are Foundation Tier content needed on both tiers. The Higher Tier adds **repeated percentage change** and **solving compound interest problems**, marked **(Higher tier only)** below.

Percentages are also taught with ratio in the [percentages, ratio and proportion study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-percentages-ratio-and-proportion/). This page concentrates on percentages alone, built around one idea: a percentage change is a multiplication. Afterwards, use the [section 1.6 revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-percentages-revision-notes/) and the [section 1.6 practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-percentages-practice/).

## Parts per hundred (1.6 A, 1.6 C)

"Per cent" means "out of 100", so 6.5% = 6.5/100 = 0.065 = 13/200. To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by 100; to get a fraction, write over 100 and simplify. Percentages above 100 are allowed: 120% = 1.2, which is the multiplier for a 20% increase.

## One number as a percentage of another (1.6 B)

Write the first number as a fraction of the second, in the same units, then multiply by 100.

- 18 out of 40 = 18/40 × 100 = 45%.
- 450 ml as a percentage of 1.2 litres: 1.2 litres = 1200 ml, so 450/1200 × 100 = 37.5%.

## Percentages as operators (1.6 D)

The specification's example is "15% of 120 = 15/100 × 120". Finding a percentage of an amount is a multiplication by a decimal: 35% of 260 = 0.35 × 260 = 91.

The same idea handles increases and decreases in one step. The **multiplier** is (100 + p)/100 for an increase of p%, and (100 − p)/100 for a decrease.

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| increase by 15% | 1.15 |
| increase by 2.5% | 1.025 |
| decrease by 8% | 0.92 |
| decrease by 40% | 0.6 |

So £340 increased by 15% is 340 × 1.15 = £391, and £85 decreased by 8% is 85 × 0.92 = £78.20. One multiplication replaces "find the percentage, then add or subtract", and it extends directly to repeated changes.

## Percentage increase and decrease (1.6 E)

**Percentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100.**

**Worked example 1.** A sapling grows from 1.6 m to 1.84 m. A trader buys a bike for £45 and sells it for £54. Find each percentage change.

```
sapling:  change = 1.84 − 1.6 = 0.24 m
          0.24 ÷ 1.6 × 100 = 15% increase
trader:   profit = 54 − 45 = £9
          9 ÷ 45 × 100 = 20% profit
```

Always divide by the **original** value. A useful check: 1.6 × 1.15 should give 1.84, and it does.

## Reverse percentages (1.6 F)

When you know the value **after** a percentage change, divide by the multiplier to get back to the original. Never take the percentage of the new value.

**Worked example 2.** After a 35% reduction, a coat costs £52. Find the original price.

```
multiplier for a 35% decrease = 0.65
original × 0.65 = 52
original = 52 ÷ 0.65 = £80
```

Check: 35% of £80 is £28, and £80 − £28 = £52. Taking 35% of £52 and adding it gives £70.20, which is wrong, because the 35% was a percentage of the original price, not of the sale price.

## Compound interest and depreciation (1.6 G)

**Simple interest** adds the same amount each year: £1200 at 3.5% simple interest earns £42 every year. **Compound interest** adds interest to the balance, so next year's interest is calculated on a bigger amount.

**Value after n years = original × (multiplier)ⁿ.**

**Worked example 3.** (a) £1200 is invested at 3.5% per year compound interest. Find its value after 4 years. (b) A phone bought for £850 depreciates by 22% each year. Find its value after 3 years.

```
(a) 1200 × 1.035⁴ = 1377.027… = £1377.03
    (simple interest would give 1200 + 4 × 42 = £1368)
(b) 850 × 0.78³ = 403.369… = £403.37
```

Depreciation is a compound decrease: the multiplier is less than 1. Round only the final money answer to the nearest penny.

## Repeated percentage change (Higher tier only)

Successive changes multiply. A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease gives 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99, an overall **1% decrease**, not zero change, because the second change acts on a different amount.

To find an overall percentage change, multiply the multipliers, then compare with 1. A population that falls 4% a year for 5 years has multiplier 0.96⁵ = 0.8154 (4 s.f.), an overall decrease of about **18.5%**, not 5 × 4% = 20%.

## Solving compound interest problems (Higher tier only)

Higher questions may ask for the original amount, the rate or the number of years.

**Worked example 4.**

(a) After 3 years of 6% compound growth, an investment is worth £5955.08. Find the amount invested.

```
original × 1.06³ = 5955.08
original = 5955.08 ÷ 1.191016 = £5000
```

(b) £6000 grows to £6489.60 in 2 years at a fixed compound rate. Find the rate.

```
multiplier² = 6489.60 ÷ 6000 = 1.0816
multiplier  = √1.0816 = 1.04    ->  4% per year
```

(c) A car worth £16 000 depreciates by 12% a year. After how many whole years is it first worth less than £8000?

```
n = 5: 16 000 × 0.88⁵ = £8443.71   (still above £8000)
n = 6: 16 000 × 0.88⁶ = £7430.47   (below)
answer: 6 years
```

Show the values either side of the target, as in (c). That is the evidence the method mark needs.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by the new value when finding a percentage change.
- Using 0.22 instead of 0.78 as a depreciation multiplier.
- Finding the percentage of the new price in a reverse-percentage question.
- Using simple interest when the question says "compound", or the reverse.
- Adding percentages in repeated change: +20% then +10% is +32%, not +30%.
- Rounding a multiplier or an intermediate value, then raising it to a power.

## Exam technique

- **Work out** or **Calculate**: write the multiplier you are using, such as "× 0.85", so the method is clear even if the arithmetic slips.
- **Give your answer to the nearest penny** or **to 3 significant figures**: round once, at the end.
- **Show that** a final value is correct: work forwards from the information given and reach the printed value exactly.
- **Explain** whether two changes cancel: use a calculation with a convenient starting value, such as £100, and conclude in words.
- Read the time carefully: "after 3 years" uses the power 3, and "during the third year" needs the difference between year 2 and year 3 values.
