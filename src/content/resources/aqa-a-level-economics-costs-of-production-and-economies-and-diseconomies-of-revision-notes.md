## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost | Does not vary with output in the short run (rent, insurance) |
| Variable cost | Varies with output (materials, piece-rate labour) |
| Total cost | Fixed cost plus variable cost |
| Average cost | Total cost per unit |
| Marginal cost | Cost of producing one more unit |
| Short run | Period when one or more inputs cannot be changed |
| Long run | Period when every input can be changed, so no fixed costs |
| Internal economy of scale | Lower unit cost from the firm's own growth |
| External economy of scale | Lower unit cost from growth of the industry or area |
| Diseconomy of scale | Unit cost rises as scale rises |
| LRAC | Lowest unit cost of each output when plant size can be chosen |
| Minimum efficient scale (MES) | Smallest output reaching the lowest LRAC |

## Formulas

```
TC  = FC + VC
AFC = FC / Q       AVC = VC / Q       AC = TC / Q = AFC + AVC
MC  = ΔTC / ΔQ   (also ΔVC / ΔQ)
Unit labour cost = wage per worker / output per worker
```

## Method: completing a cost table

1. Find FC. It is the total cost at zero output, or TC minus VC.
2. VC = TC minus FC at each output.
3. MC = this TC minus the previous TC.
4. AVC = VC / output. AC = TC / output. Check AC = AFC + AVC.
5. Mark where AVC and AC are lowest and check MC is below them before and above them after.

### Worked reminder: a print shop (invented)

Fixed costs are 80. Total cost is given.

| Output | TC | VC | MC | AFC | AVC | AC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 | 40 | 40 | 80.00 | 40.00 | 120.00 |
| 2 | 150 | 70 | 30 | 40.00 | 35.00 | 75.00 |
| 3 | 170 | 90 | 20 | 26.67 | 30.00 | 56.67 |
| 4 | 200 | 120 | 30 | 20.00 | 30.00 | 50.00 |
| 5 | 245 | 165 | 45 | 16.00 | 33.00 | 49.00 |
| 6 | 310 | 230 | 65 | 13.33 | 38.33 | 51.67 |

The fifth unit costs 45, below the previous average of 50, so average cost drops to (200 + 45) / 5 = 49. The sixth costs 65, above 49, so AC rises to 310 / 6 = 51.67. The lowest AC sits where MC crosses it, between outputs 5 and 6.

## Shapes of the curves: reasons in a line each

| Curve | Shape | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| AFC | Falls throughout | Fixed cost spread over more units |
| MC | Falls then rises | Gains from specialisation, then diminishing returns |
| AVC | U-shaped | Follows MC but changes more slowly |
| AC | U-shaped | AFC falls fast at first; AVC rises later |

MC meets AVC and AC at their minimum points. **Shifts:** higher fixed costs lift AC and TC only. Lower factor prices or higher productivity lower MC, AVC and AC. A change in output is a movement along the curves.

## Factor prices, productivity and input choice

- A rise in the price of a factor raises costs. If firms can substitute, they use less of it and more of the cheaper factor.
- Compare methods by total cost for the same output, then cost per unit.
- Higher productivity lowers unit cost: unit labour cost = wage / output per worker.
- Limits: capital is fixed in the short run, retraining takes time, and some tasks cannot be automated.

### Worked reminder: two methods (invented)

Output is 300 units. Method A uses 6 workers and 2 machines; method B uses 2 workers and 5 machines. A machine costs 90 a day.

| Wage | Cost of A | Cost of B | Per unit A | Per unit B | Cheaper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 6 × 100 + 180 = 780 | 2 × 100 + 450 = 650 | 2.60 | 2.17 | B |
| 60 | 6 × 60 + 180 = 540 | 2 × 60 + 450 = 570 | 1.80 | 1.90 | A |

A fall in the wage from 100 to 60 makes the labour-intensive method cheaper.

Productivity example: a wage of 120 a day with 40 units per worker gives unit labour cost 3.00. If output per worker reaches 50, it falls to 2.40, a fall of 20%.

## Economies and diseconomies of scale

| Internal economies | Examples |
|---|---|
| Technical | Large machines, indivisibilities, increased dimensions |
| Purchasing | Bulk-buying discounts |
| Financial | Cheaper borrowing |
| Marketing | Advertising spread over more output |
| Managerial | Affordable specialist managers |
| Risk-bearing | Spread across products or markets |

**External economies:** skilled labour pool, supplier cluster, shared infrastructure, local training college.

**Internal diseconomies:** communication, coordination and control problems; weaker motivation; duplication of tasks. **External diseconomies:** congestion, rising rents and wages, strained infrastructure.

| Cause | Effect on LRAC |
|---|---|
| Internal economy or diseconomy | Movement down or up along the curve |
| External economy | Whole curve shifts down |
| External diseconomy | Whole curve shifts up |

## Returns to scale and costs

Double all inputs, with input prices unchanged:

- Output more than doubles (increasing returns): AC falls, an economy of scale.
- Output exactly doubles (constant returns): AC unchanged.
- Output less than doubles (decreasing returns): AC rises, a diseconomy of scale.

Worked reminder: 15 workers and 5 machines cost 900 and produce 400 units, so AC = 900 / 400 = 2.25. Tripling inputs costs 2,700 and produces 1,000 units, 2.5 times as much. AC = 2,700 / 1,000 = 2.70, up 20%. Decreasing returns, so a diseconomy.

Careful: lower input prices from bulk buying are an economy of scale without any physical gain in output per input.

## LRAC, MES and industry structure

- LRAC is the lower boundary of the short-run average cost curves, one for each plant size.
- U-shaped: economies then diseconomies. L-shaped: economies, then flat. The flat part suggests diseconomies are weak or absent.
- Cost disadvantage of a small firm = its LRAC minus the LRAC at MES.
- Number of firms that fit at MES = market size / MES.
- If MES is a small share of the market: many firms, low barriers. If MES is a large share: few firms, high barriers, possibly natural monopoly.

Example: MES is 30,000 units and market demand is 120,000. 120 / 30 = 4 firms can operate at MES. If MES were 90,000, it would be 75% of the market and only one firm fits efficiently.

## Chains of reasoning to rehearse

- **Wage rise leads to a change of method.** Wage rises, labour-intensive method costs more per unit, firm substitutes machines, labour demand falls. Evaluate: capital is fixed in the short run, so the response is bigger in the long run.
- **Growth leads to lower unit costs.** More output, fixed and overhead costs spread, bulk discounts, better machines, AC falls. Evaluate: only until MES; beyond it the curve may be flat or may turn up.
- **High MES leads to few firms.** Large minimum scale, new entrants face a big cost gap or heavy start-up spending, fewer rivals, less competition. Evaluate: how large MES is relative to the market, and whether external economies let small firms keep costs down.
- **L-shaped LRAC.** Past MES there is no clear penalty for size, so large firms hold no cost advantage over firms at MES, and many firms near MES can survive together if the market is big enough.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Fixed v variable | Responds to output or not |
| AC v MC | Per unit overall v cost of the next unit |
| Short run v long run | Some factor fixed v all variable |
| Economy of scale v increasing returns | Fall in cost per unit v rise in output per unit of input |
| Internal v external | The firm's own size v the industry's size |

## Quick self-test

1. A firm pays 150 a day in fixed costs and produces 25 units. Calculate AFC.
2. Total cost rises from 350 to 380 when output rises by one unit. State marginal cost.
3. AVC is 28 at output 15. Calculate VC.
4. TC is 480 at output 60. Calculate AC.
5. Total cost for 4 units is 200 and the fifth unit adds 45. Calculate the new AC.
6. State why AFC never rises as output rises.
7. Classify: a local training college supplies skilled labour to an industrial cluster.
8. Classify: delays occur because decisions pass through seven management layers.
9. Inputs triple, cost triples from 900 to 2,700, and output rises 2.5 times. State the returns to scale and the effect on AC.
10. MES is 40,000 units and market demand is 160,000. How many firms can operate at MES?

### Answers

1. AFC = 150 / 25 = 6.
2. MC = 380 minus 350 = 30.
3. VC = 28 × 15 = 420.
4. AC = 480 / 60 = 8.
5. New AC = (200 + 45) / 5 = 49.
6. FC is constant, so FC divided by a larger output is always smaller.
7. External economy of scale.
8. Internal diseconomy of scale (communication).
9. Decreasing returns; AC rises from 2.25 to 2.70.
10. 160,000 / 40,000 = 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that cost curves "shift" when output changes. Output changes move you along the curve.
- Calling a rise in total cost a diseconomy of scale. Check average cost.
- Giving only a label such as "bulk buying" without saying it lowers average cost.
- Mixing up internal and external. Ask who gains: one firm (internal) or all firms in the area (external).
- Using AC where the question asks for MC, or dividing by the wrong output.
- Forgetting that fixed costs vanish in the long run.
- Stating that MES means the biggest output; it is the lowest output reaching minimum LRAC.
- Describing the L-shaped LRAC without saying what it implies for small firms and entry.
