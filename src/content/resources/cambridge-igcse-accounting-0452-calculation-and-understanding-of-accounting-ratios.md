## What 6.1 covers

Candidates should understand and calculate ten ratios: gross profit margin;
mark-up; profit margin; return on capital employed (ROCE); current (working
capital) ratio; acid test (liquid) ratio; rate of inventory turnover (times);
inventory turnover (days); trade receivables turnover (days); trade payables
turnover (days). The syllabus states that candidates must use the formulas in its
list of accounting ratios, and that these are the only formulas accepted.

## The ten formulas and what they measure

| Ratio | Formula | Answer as | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | % | gross profit earned on each $100 of sales |
| Mark-up | gross profit ÷ cost of sales × 100 | % | gross profit added to each $100 of cost |
| Profit margin | profit for the year ÷ revenue × 100 | % | profit left from each $100 of sales after all expenses |
| ROCE | profit for the year before interest ÷ capital employed × 100 | % | return earned on the long-term finance used |
| Current (working capital) ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities | ratio, x : 1 | ability to pay short-term debts |
| Acid test (liquid) ratio | (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities | ratio, x : 1 | ability to pay short-term debts without selling inventory |
| Rate of inventory turnover | cost of sales ÷ average inventory | times | how many times inventory is sold and replaced in a year |
| Inventory turnover | average inventory ÷ cost of sales × 365 | days | average days goods are held before sale |
| Trade receivables turnover | trade receivables ÷ credit sales × 365 | days | average days credit customers take to pay |
| Trade payables turnover | trade payables × 365 ÷ credit purchases | days | average days taken to pay suppliers |

**Capital employed** = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.

The groups: the first four are **profitability** ratios, the current and acid
test ratios are **liquidity** ratios, and the four turnover ratios are
**efficiency** ratios.

## Choosing the right figures

- **Profit for the year before interest** (ROCE) is profit for the year with the
  interest on non-current liabilities (such as debenture interest) added back. The
  lenders' money is in capital employed, so their return is included in the profit.
- **Average inventory** is (opening inventory + closing inventory) ÷ 2. Both
  inventory ratios use it. Closing inventory alone is a common error.
- **Credit sales** and **credit purchases** are used for the receivables and
  payables ratios, not totals. If a question gives total revenue and cash sales,
  subtract.
- **Trade** receivables and **trade** payables only: leave out other receivables
  (prepayments, accrued income) and other payables (accruals).
- **Inventory** in the acid test is closing inventory, as shown in current assets.
- Give percentages and days to **two decimal places** unless the question says
  otherwise, and show ratios as x : 1.

## Worked example: Elmfield Supplies Ltd

Figures for the year ended 31 December 2028:

```
Revenue (credit sales 300,000; cash sales 120,000)          420,000
Opening inventory                              38,000
Purchases (credit purchases 252,000)          270,000
Closing inventory                             (46,000)
Cost of sales                                              262,000
Gross profit                                               158,000
Expenses                                                  (101,500)
Debenture interest (8% on 75,000)                           (6,000)
Profit for the year                                         50,500

Current assets: inventory 46,000; trade receivables 37,500;
   other receivables 2,500; bank 9,000                       95,000
Current liabilities: trade payables 33,600; other payables 6,500   40,100
Equity: ordinary shares 200,000; general reserve 20,000;
   retained earnings 61,000                                 281,000
Non-current liabilities: 8% debentures                       75,000
```

```
Gross profit margin   158,000 ÷ 420,000 × 100              = 37.62%
Mark-up               158,000 ÷ 262,000 × 100              = 60.31%
Profit margin          50,500 ÷ 420,000 × 100              = 12.02%
Capital employed      200,000 + 20,000 + 61,000 + 75,000   = 356,000
ROCE                  (50,500 + 6,000) ÷ 356,000 × 100     = 15.87%
Current ratio          95,000 : 40,100                     = 2.37 : 1
Acid test             (95,000 − 46,000) : 40,100           = 1.22 : 1
Average inventory     (38,000 + 46,000) ÷ 2                = 42,000
Rate of inventory turnover  262,000 ÷ 42,000               = 6.24 times
Inventory turnover     42,000 ÷ 262,000 × 365              = 58.51 days
Trade receivables turnover  37,500 ÷ 300,000 × 365         = 45.63 days
Trade payables turnover     33,600 × 365 ÷ 252,000         = 48.67 days
```

Two checks you can always make: mark-up is larger than gross profit margin
(because cost of sales is smaller than revenue), and inventory turnover in days
equals 365 ÷ rate of inventory turnover (365 ÷ 6.24 is about 58.5).

## What the numbers mean

- A gross profit margin of 37.62% means 37.62 cents of every dollar of sales is
  left after paying for the goods sold.
- A profit margin of 12.02% means 12.02 cents of every dollar of sales is left
  after all expenses, including interest.
- ROCE of 15.87% means each $100 of long-term finance earned 15.87 before interest.
- A current ratio of 2.37 : 1 means there is 2.37 of current assets for every 1 of
  current liabilities.
- Receivables of 45.63 days against payables of 48.67 days means Elmfield collects
  from customers a little faster than it pays suppliers, which helps cash flow.

## Rearranging to find missing figures

**From liquidity ratios.** Current ratio 2.2 : 1 and current liabilities 15,000:
current assets = 2.2 × 15,000 = 33,000. Acid test 1.4 : 1: liquid assets = 1.4 ×
15,000 = 21,000, so inventory = 33,000 − 21,000 = **12,000**.

**From receivables days.** Credit sales 146,000 and receivables turnover 40 days:
trade receivables = 146,000 × 40 ÷ 365 = **16,000**.

**From margin to mark-up.** Gross profit margin 30% on revenue of 80,000: gross
profit 24,000, cost of sales 56,000, mark-up = 24,000 ÷ 56,000 × 100 = **42.86%**.

## Common mistakes

- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory.
- Using total sales or total purchases in the days ratios.
- Including other receivables or other payables in the days ratios.
- Forgetting to add back debenture interest in ROCE, or using total assets as
  capital employed.
- Writing a current ratio as a percentage, or without ": 1".
- Rounding too early: keep full figures until the final answer.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate:** write the formula, substitute, then give the answer with its unit.
- **State** the meaning of a ratio in one sentence using the business's figures.
- Lay out multiple ratios in a table so each working is visible.
