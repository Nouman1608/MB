## Specification in brief

- Concurrent substitution and elimination of a halogenoalkane; the named example is **2-bromopropane with potassium hydroxide**.
- Explain the role of the reagent as **both nucleophile and base**.
- Outline the **mechanisms** of both reactions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Elimination | Removal of a small molecule (here HX) from adjacent carbon atoms, forming C=C |
| Base | Proton (H⁺) acceptor; here OH⁻ uses a lone pair to remove H⁺ |
| Nucleophile | Electron pair donor that attacks an electron-deficient carbon |
| Concurrent reactions | Two reactions of the same reactant happening at the same time in the same mixture |
| Adjacent carbon | A carbon bonded directly to the C–X carbon (also called the β-carbon) |
| Positional isomers | Alkenes that differ only in the position of C=C |
| E/Z isomers | Stereoisomers caused by restricted rotation about C=C when each alkene carbon has two different groups |

## Recall: the two pathways from 2-bromopropane

| | Substitution | Elimination |
|---|---|---|
| Role of OH⁻ | nucleophile | base |
| Lone pair goes to | δ+ carbon (C2) | H on C1 or C3 |
| Number of arrows | 2 | 3 |
| Favoured by | water as solvent, warm | ethanol as solvent, hot |
| Organic product | propan-2-ol | propene |
| Equation | CH₃CHBrCH₃ + OH⁻ → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + Br⁻ | CH₃CHBrCH₃ + OH⁻ → CH₂=CHCH₃ + H₂O + Br⁻ |

### The three elimination arrows

1. Lone pair on O of OH⁻ → H on an **adjacent** carbon.
2. That C–H bond → the C–C bond (becomes the second bond of C=C).
3. C–X bond → X (halide ion leaves).

Products: alkene + H₂O + X⁻.

## Recall: counting alkenes

1. Find the C–X carbon.
2. List adjacent carbons with at least one H.
3. Non-equivalent neighbours → different positional isomers.
4. Check each alkene for E/Z isomers.

| Halogenoalkane | Neighbours with H | Alkenes formed |
|---|---|---|
| 2-bromopropane | C1, C3 (equivalent) | propene only |
| 1-bromobutane | C2 only | but-1-ene only |
| 2-bromobutane | C1, C3 (different) | but-1-ene, (E)-but-2-ene, (Z)-but-2-ene |
| bromocyclohexane | two equivalent ring CH₂ | cyclohexene only |
| 2-bromo-2-methylpropane | three equivalent CH₃ | 2-methylpropene only |
| 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane | none with H | no elimination possible |

### Worked reminders

- **2-Chloro-2-methylbutane, (CH₃)₂CClCH₂CH₃.** Neighbours of the C–Cl carbon: two equivalent CH₃ groups and one CH₂. Loss of H from a CH₃ gives **2-methylbut-1-ene**; loss from the CH₂ gives **2-methylbut-2-ene**. Neither has E/Z isomers (one carbon of each C=C carries two identical groups). Two alkenes.
- **3-Bromohexane, CH₃CH₂CHBrCH₂CH₂CH₃.** Neighbours C2 and C4 differ. Hex-2-ene and hex-3-ene form, each as E and Z isomers. Four alkenes.
- **Bromoethane.** Only one neighbour: **ethene**, CH₂=CH₂. With aqueous hydroxide the main product is ethanol.

## Recall: two eliminations compared

| | Halogenoalkane (3.3.3.2) | Alcohol (3.3.5.2) |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | KOH (base) | concentrated H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄ (acid catalyst) |
| Solvent / conditions | ethanol, hot | heat |
| Small molecule removed | HX | H₂O |
| First arrow | OH⁻ lone pair → adjacent H | O lone pair → H⁺ |
| Intermediate | none (one step) | carbocation |

Both reactions give alkenes, and the same product-counting method applies to each.

## Exam traps

- In elimination, the hydroxide arrow goes to **H**, never to carbon.
- The H removed is on a carbon **next to** the C–X carbon.
- Always write the solvent: "aqueous" or "ethanolic".
- Don't forget **water** and **X⁻** as products of elimination.
- OH⁻ is a **base**, not an acid or electrophile.
- Name stereoisomers fully: (E)-but-2-ene and (Z)-but-2-ene count as two products.
- Acid-catalysed elimination is for **alcohols** (3.3.5.2); halogenoalkanes use hydroxide.

## Self-test

1. What is meant by "concurrent" substitution and elimination?
2. In substitution, to which atom does OH⁻ donate its lone pair?
3. In elimination, to which atom does OH⁻ donate its lone pair?
4. State the role of OH⁻ in elimination.
5. Give the conditions that favour elimination over substitution.
6. Write an equation for the elimination of HBr from 2-bromopropane using KOH.
7. Describe the second arrow in the elimination mechanism.
8. Name every alkene formed from 2-bromobutane with ethanolic KOH.
9. Why does 1-bromobutane give only one alkene?
10. Name the substitution product when 2-chloro-2-methylpropane reacts with aqueous KOH.
11. Explain why 1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane gives no alkene with hot ethanolic KOH.
12. 8.15 g of bromocyclohexane (Mr 162.9) gives 3.28 g of cyclohexene (Mr 82.0). Calculate the percentage yield.

### Answers

1. **Both reactions happen at the same time** in the same reaction mixture, using the same reagent.
2. The **δ+ carbon** bonded to the halogen.
3. A **hydrogen atom on a carbon adjacent** to the C–X carbon.
4. **Base** (proton acceptor).
5. **KOH dissolved in ethanol**, **heated** (reflux).
6. **CH₃CHBrCH₃ + KOH → CH₂=CHCH₃ + H₂O + KBr**
7. From the **C–H bond** on the adjacent carbon to the **C–C bond**, forming C=C.
8. **But-1-ene, (E)-but-2-ene and (Z)-but-2-ene.**
9. The C–Br carbon (C1) has **only one adjacent carbon** (C2), so C=C can form only between C1 and C2.
10. **2-Methylpropan-2-ol**, (CH₃)₃COH.
11. The only adjacent carbon is **quaternary and has no H**, so there is no proton for the base to remove.
12. n(bromocyclohexane) = 0.0500 mol; n(cyclohexene) = 0.0400 mol; yield = **80.0 %**.
