These questions cover sub-topic **14.4 Homeostasis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Most of this sub-topic is Supplement content, so most questions are marked **(Extended)**; Core candidates should answer questions 1 to 3. Data in the questions are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes homeostasis?

A: the removal of waste products from the body

B: the maintenance of a constant internal environment

C: the release of energy from glucose

D: the response of the body to a stimulus

**[1]**

**2.** State the effect of insulin on blood glucose concentration. **[1]**

**3.** Name the gland that secretes insulin. **[1]**

**4.** *(Extended)* Name the organ that stores glycogen and releases glucose when glucagon is secreted. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Name the process in which arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries become wider. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name **two** structures in the skin that provide insulation. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** *(Extended)* Explain what is meant by homeostatic control by negative feedback, with reference to a set point. Use body temperature as your example. **[4]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A student eats a bowl of rice. The table shows her blood glucose concentration afterwards.

| Time after meal / minutes | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood glucose / arbitrary units | 5.0 | 7.5 | 6.4 | 5.4 | 5.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in blood glucose between 0 and 30 minutes. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how blood glucose is returned to its set point between 30 and 120 minutes. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why this is an example of negative feedback. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A woman walks for three hours without eating. Her blood glucose concentration begins to fall.

**(a)** Name the hormone secreted in response, and the gland that secretes it. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how this hormone raises blood glucose concentration. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A teenager is diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes.

**(a)** State the cause of the high blood glucose concentration in Type 1 diabetes. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline the treatment of Type 1 diabetes. **[3]**

**(c)** The teenager plays football after school. Suggest why she should check her blood glucose concentration before and after playing. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A person sits in a hot bath for 15 minutes and their face becomes red.

**(a)** Explain why the face becomes red. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the role of the brain in detecting the rise in body temperature. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how sweating helps to reduce body temperature. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A hiker is caught in freezing wind on a mountain.

**(a)** Explain how shivering helps to keep her body temperature constant. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the hair erector muscles and hairs respond, and how this reduces heat loss. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe vasoconstriction and explain how it reduces heat loss. **[3]**

**13.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "When we are hot, the capillaries move closer to the surface of the skin, and the sweat cools us because it is cold."

Identify **two** errors and correct each one. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** It decreases blood glucose concentration [1].

**3.** Pancreas [1].

**4.** Liver [1].

**5.** Vasodilation [1].

**6.** Fatty tissue [1]; hairs (raised, trapping air) [1].

**7.** Body temperature has a set point of about 37 °C [1]. If the temperature rises above or falls below the set point, the change is detected (by the brain) [1]. Responses are triggered that reverse the change, for example sweating and vasodilation when too hot, or shivering and vasoconstriction when too cold [1]. The temperature returns towards the set point, and the response then stops [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Negative" must be explained by the idea that the response opposes or reverses the change.

**8. (a)** (7.5 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100 [1] = **50%** [1].

**(b)** The pancreas secretes insulin [1]; insulin stimulates the liver [1] to convert glucose into glycogen, which is stored, so blood glucose falls back to 5.0 [1].

**(c)** The rise in blood glucose triggered a response that reversed the change, bringing it back to the set point [1].

**9. (a)** Glucagon [1]; pancreas [1].

**(b)** Glucagon stimulates the liver [1] to convert glycogen into glucose, which is released into the blood [1].

*Examiner insight:* Spell glucagon and glycogen carefully; examiners do not accept a word that could be either.

**10. (a)** The pancreas produces little or no insulin [1].

**(b)** Any three of: insulin injections (or pump); regular monitoring of blood glucose; controlling the diet / carbohydrate intake; adjusting for exercise [1] [1] [1].

**(c)** Exercise uses glucose in respiration, so blood glucose may fall too low [1]; checking shows whether she needs to eat carbohydrate or change her insulin dose [1].

**11. (a)** Vasodilation: arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries widen [1], so more blood flows through the surface capillaries, making the skin look red and losing more heat [1].

**(b)** The brain monitors the temperature of the blood flowing through it [1] and receives impulses from temperature receptors in the skin via sensory neurones [1].

**(c)** Sweat glands secrete sweat onto the skin [1]; the water evaporates, transferring heat away from the skin [1].

**12. (a)** Muscles contract rapidly [1]; the increased respiration in muscle cells releases heat [1].

**(b)** Hair erector muscles contract, raising the hairs [1]; the raised hairs trap a layer of air, which insulates the skin [1].

**(c)** Arterioles supplying the skin surface capillaries narrow [1]; less blood flows through the surface capillaries [1], so less heat is lost from the skin [1].

*Examiner insight:* Say "arterioles narrow", not "capillaries constrict". The syllabus wording names the arterioles.

**13.** Error 1: capillaries do not move [1]; the arterioles supplying the skin surface capillaries widen (vasodilation), so more blood flows near the surface [1]. Error 2: sweat does not cool because it is cold [1]; the water in sweat evaporates, transferring heat away from the skin [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing glucagon and glycogen.
- Saying the pancreas stores glycogen.
- Treatment of Type 1 diabetes without insulin.
- Saying capillaries move or constrict.
- Explaining sweating without evaporation.
- Explaining shivering without respiration releasing heat.
- Negative feedback answers without a set point.
