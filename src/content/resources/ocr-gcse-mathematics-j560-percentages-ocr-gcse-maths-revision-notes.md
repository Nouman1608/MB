These notes condense section 2.03, Percentages, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026): 2.03a percentage conversions, 2.03b percentage calculations and 2.03c percentage change. All of it is content for **both tiers**. Repeated percentage change over several years (compound interest, depreciation) is in section 5.03.

The [2.03 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-percentages-ocr-gcse-maths/) gives full explanations and worked examples; the [2.03 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-percentages-ocr-gcse-maths-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes. For percentages with ordering, see the [percentages and ordering revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-percentages-and-ordering-fractions-decimals-and-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage | Number of parts per hundred | 37.5% = 37.5/100 |
| Multiplier | The single number that applies a percentage change | 1.12 for +12% |
| Percentage change | Change as a percentage of the original value | £75 → £84 is +12% |
| Original value | The amount before a change | £850 before a 30% discount |
| Simple interest | The same interest every year, on the original sum | 2.4% of £1 250 = £30 a year |
| Principal | The sum invested or borrowed | £1 250 |
| Rate | The yearly percentage | 2.4% per year |

## Conversions

```
% → decimal:    ÷ 100            0.4%  → 0.004
decimal → %:    × 100            2.75  → 275%
% → fraction:   over 100, simplify     0.4% = 0.4/100 = 4/1000 = 1/250
fraction → %:   to decimal, × 100      3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
```

Over 100% means more than the whole; under 1% means less than one hundredth.

## Percentage of a quantity

| Without a calculator | With a calculator |
|---|---|
| Build from 10%, 5%, 1%, halves: 35% of £260 = 3 × 26 + 13 = £91 | Multiply by the decimal: 0.35 × 260 = 91 |
| 2.5% = half of 5%: 2.5% of 840 = 21 | 0.025 × 840 = 21 |
| 95% = 100% − 5% | 0.95 × amount |

## One quantity as a percentage of another

```
part ÷ whole × 100      (same units first)
18 out of 24:  18/24 × 100 = 75%
```

The "whole" is the reference value: the quantity after "of" or "compared with".

## Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| + r% | 1 + r/100 |
| − r% | 1 − r/100 |
| + 1/6 | 7/6 |
| − 1/6 | 5/6 |

From a multiplier back to a change: 1.12 → 12% increase; 0.93 → 7% decrease; 0.5 → 50% decrease; 1.5 → 50% increase.

## The three question types

```
Find the new value:       new = original × multiplier
Find the % change:        multiplier = new ÷ original
                          (or change ÷ original × 100)
Find the original value:  original = new ÷ multiplier
```

Decide which value you know before you start. "After", "now", "sale price" and "including VAT" usually mean you know the new value.

## Simple interest

```
interest = principal × rate/100 × years
per year = principal × rate/100
years = total interest ÷ interest per year
principal: find 1% from the yearly interest, then × 100
```

## Worked reminders

```
Increase £64 by 17.5% (by hand):   10% 6.40, 5% 3.20, 2.5% 1.60
                                   64 + 11.20 = £75.20
Percentage change £240 → £204:     204 ÷ 240 = 0.85, a 15% decrease
Original value, VAT 20% included:  £150 ÷ 1.2 = £125
Simple interest time:              £70 a year on £2 000; £350 ÷ 70 = 5 years
```

**Which value is the original?** In a percentage change it is the "from" value; in profit it is the buying price; in a sale it is the full price before the discount; in VAT it is the price before VAT.

## Exam traps

- **Discount as multiplier:** 0.3 finds the discount; 0.7 finds the sale price after 30% off.
- **0.4% ≠ 0.4.** It is 0.004.
- **Wrong base for percentage change:** always divide by the original value.
- **Reverse percentage by adding back:** 30% of £595 added to £595 does not give £850.
- **Missing direction:** write "increase" or "decrease".
- **Units mismatch** in "as a percentage of".
- **Simple vs compound:** "simple interest" is the same each year; a growing balance is compound (section 5.03).
- **Rounding money:** to 2 decimal places, at the end.

## Two-line checks

- Original value: multiply your answer by the multiplier; you should get the given value.
- Percentage change: apply the change to the original; you should get the new value.
- Simple interest: interest per year × years should equal the total interest.

## Quick self-test

1. Write 3/8 as a percentage.
2. Write 0.4% as a decimal and as a fraction in its simplest form.
3. Write 2 3/4 as a percentage.
4. Find 35% of £260 without a calculator.
5. Find 2.5% of 840 without a calculator.
6. Write 18 out of 24 as a percentage.
7. Increase £45 by 12%.
8. Decrease 360 kg by 1/6 using a fractional multiplier.
9. A price rises from £75 to £84. Find the percentage increase.
10. After a 30% discount a sofa costs £595. Find the original price.
11. Find the simple interest on £1 250 at 2.4% per year for 3 years.
12. What percentage change does a multiplier of 0.93 represent?

### Answers

1. 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375, so **37.5%**.
2. **0.004** and **1/250**.
3. 2.75 × 100 = **275%**.
4. 10% = 26, so 30% = 78; 5% = 13; total **£91**.
5. 5% = 42, so 2.5% = **21**.
6. 18/24 = 0.75 = **75%**.
7. 45 × 1.12 = **£50.40**.
8. 360 × 5/6 = **300 kg**.
9. 84 ÷ 75 = 1.12, so a **12% increase**.
10. 595 ÷ 0.7 = **£850**. Check: 850 × 0.7 = 595.
11. 1 250 × 0.024 = £30 a year; × 3 = **£90**.
12. 0.93 = 93%, so a **7% decrease**.
