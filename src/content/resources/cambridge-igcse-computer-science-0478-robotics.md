Section 6.2 of the Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478 syllabus has three content statements. You must **understand what is meant by robotics**, **describe the characteristics of a robot**, and **understand the roles that robots can perform and describe the advantages and disadvantages of their use**. The syllabus areas are industry, transport, agriculture, medicine, domestic settings and entertainment.

## What is robotics?

**Robotics** is a branch of computer science that incorporates the **design, construction and operation of robots**. Examples named in the syllabus are factory equipment, domestic robots and drones.

Robotics draws on computer science for the programs, on electronics for the sensors and circuits, and on engineering for the moving parts. A **robot** is a machine that can carry out tasks automatically, controlled by a program. It senses its surroundings and acts on them.

A robot is a more specific idea than an automated system. Section 6.1 covers any system built from sensors, microprocessors and actuators. A robot is one that has a mechanical body and carries out physical tasks.

## Characteristics of a robot

The syllabus gives three characteristics.

| Characteristic | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A mechanical structure or framework | The physical body, made of metal or plastic, with joints, arms, legs, wheels or rotors | The arm of a factory robot; the frame and rotors of a drone |
| Electrical components, such as sensors, microprocessors and actuators | Sensors collect data, the microprocessor processes it and actuators produce movement | Cameras and pressure sensors; a processor; motors that turn joints |
| Programmable | The robot follows a set of instructions that can be written, changed and re-run | A welding robot reprogrammed to weld a new car model |

### How the three work together

The mechanical structure gives the robot a body that can move. The electrical components give it the ability to sense and move. The program tells it what to do. If any one is missing, it is not a working robot: a metal arm with no program just sits there; a program with no body cannot affect the physical world.

### Worked example: a robot vacuum cleaner

| Characteristic | In the vacuum cleaner |
|---|---|
| Mechanical structure | A flat round body with wheels, a brush and a dust container |
| Electrical components | Bump and cliff sensors detect walls and stairs; a microprocessor decides where to go; motors (actuators) drive the wheels and brush |
| Programmable | The user sets cleaning times and the robot follows its cleaning program |

The vacuum cleaner senses a wall, the microprocessor decides to turn, and the wheel motors carry out the turn. The loop of sense, process and act from section 6.1 is running inside the robot.

## Roles of robots

| Area | Roles | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Industry | Assembling, welding, painting, packing and moving heavy loads | Car factory arms; warehouse robots that carry shelves |
| Transport | Driving and delivery | Driverless vehicles; delivery drones |
| Agriculture | Planting, harvesting, spraying and monitoring crops | Fruit-picking robots; drones that survey fields |
| Medicine | Surgery, delivering items in hospitals, and helping with rehabilitation | Surgical robots that hold instruments steady; robots that carry medicines to wards |
| Domestic settings | Cleaning, cutting grass, security and helping with tasks | Robot vacuum cleaners; lawn-mowing robots |
| Entertainment | Moving models, toys, theme-park figures and filming | Animatronic characters; camera drones |

## Advantages and disadvantages

### Advantages

- **Works without rest.** A robot does not need breaks, sleep or holidays, so production can continue all day.
- **Accuracy and consistency.** A robot repeats a task in exactly the same way, which reduces mistakes and waste.
- **Works in dangerous places.** A robot can handle toxic chemicals, high temperatures or explosives, so human workers are not put at risk.
- **Strength and speed.** A robot can lift heavy objects and do repetitive tasks faster than a person.
- **Lower labour costs** in the long term, because one robot can replace several workers.

### Disadvantages

- **High cost.** Buying, installing, programming and maintaining a robot is expensive.
- **Job losses.** Workers whose tasks are replaced may become unemployed unless they are retrained.
- **Limited adaptability.** A robot can only do what it is programmed and equipped to do. It can struggle with unexpected situations.
- **Breakdown and repair.** Faults can stop production, and repairs need skilled technicians.
- **Safety and security risks.** A robot may injure someone if a sensor fails, and robots linked to a network can be hacked.
- **Lack of judgement.** A robot cannot use common sense or deal with exceptions the way a person can.

### Apply them to the scenario

In an exam, the marks come from linking points to the context.

| Scenario | Advantage in context | Disadvantage in context |
|---|---|---|
| Car factory | Welding robots join the same seams with identical accuracy all day, so fewer faults occur | The company must pay for expensive machines, and some welders may lose their jobs |
| Fruit farm | Harvesting robots work at night when it is cool, so crops can be picked quickly when ripe | The robot may not tell a ripe fruit from an unripe one in unusual light, and may damage the crop |
| Hospital | A delivery robot carries medicine safely along corridors, leaving nurses time to care for patients | The robot cannot cope if a corridor is blocked unexpectedly |
| Home | A lawn-mowing robot cuts grass at set times while the owner is at work | It may be stolen or may get stuck, and it costs more than a manual mower |

## Robots compared with other automated systems

| | Automated system (6.1) | Robot (6.2) |
|---|---|---|
| Main idea | Sensors, microprocessor and actuators control a process | A programmable machine with a mechanical structure that carries out physical tasks |
| Example | A thermostat that switches heating on and off | A robot arm that welds car panels |
| Must it have a mechanical frame? | Not necessarily | Yes |

Robots are always automated systems at heart, but not every automated system is a robot. A greenhouse controller is an automated system but not a robot, because it has no robotic body.

## Worked example: a warehouse robot

A warehouse uses robots to fetch shelves of goods and bring them to packers.

1. **Mechanical structure:** a low, flat body with wheels that slides under a shelf unit and lifts it.
2. **Electrical components:** sensors read position markers on the floor and detect other robots; a microprocessor plans the route; motors drive the wheels and the lift.
3. **Program:** the robot receives an order, works out the route, avoids other robots, and returns the shelf when the packer has finished.

**Advantages in this context:** the robots can run all night, they move accurately without bumping into each other, and packers do not walk long distances. **Disadvantages:** the warehouse must pay for the robots and the control system, and if the control system fails, every robot stops.

## Why robots are used in dangerous places

Some jobs are risky for people: defusing explosives, inspecting inside a damaged nuclear plant, spraying chemicals on crops or cleaning inside a large furnace. A robot can be sent instead. If it is damaged, it can be replaced, but a person who is hurt cannot. This is one of the strongest reasons for using robots and is a good point to develop in an answer about industry or agriculture.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** Describe **three** characteristics of a robot. [3]

> A robot has a mechanical structure or framework, such as a body, arms, wheels or rotors [1]. It has electrical components, such as sensors, a microprocessor and actuators [1]. It is programmable, so it follows a set of instructions that can be changed [1].

**Why it scores 3:** each characteristic is one of the three in the syllabus, with a short description. Answers that say "it can think" or "it looks like a person" earn nothing: a robot does not need to look like a human, and it only follows its program.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a robot as humanoid. Most robots, such as factory arms, drones and vacuum cleaners, do not look like people.
- Saying a robot "thinks for itself". It follows a program (unless it also uses artificial intelligence, which is section 6.3).
- Leaving out the mechanical structure when listing characteristics.
- Writing "it is cheaper" without noticing that the purchase cost is high.
- Giving advantages in general ("it is faster") that are not linked to the area in the question.
- Confusing a drone with a robot. A drone is an example of a robot when it has sensors, a processor and actuators and is programmable.

## Exam technique

- **Understand what is meant by robotics**: design, construction and operation of robots.
- **Describe the characteristics**: list all three and add an example for each.
- **Roles**: name the area, the task and the benefit.
- **Advantages and disadvantages**: give a reason and link it to the scenario named in the question.
