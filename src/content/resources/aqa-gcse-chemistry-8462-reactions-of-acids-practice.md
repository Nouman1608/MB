These questions cover section **4.4.2 Reactions of acids** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for
teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 1. Questions 7, 8, 9 and 10 test content the
specification marks (HT only) and are labelled **Higher tier only**. Question 6 is on titration, which is
chemistry only.

Learn the content first in the [reactions of acids study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-acids/)
and the [reactions of acids revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-acids-revision-notes/).

Relative formula masses: HNO₃ = 63, H₂SO₄ = 98.

## Questions

**1.** Magnesium ribbon is added to dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Name the two products. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the test for the gas that is given off. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **two** observations a student would make during the reaction. **[2]**

**2.** Name the salt formed in each reaction.

**(a)** Zinc oxide with dilute sulfuric acid **[1]**
**(b)** Sodium hydroxide solution with dilute nitric acid **[1]**
**(c)** Magnesium carbonate with dilute hydrochloric acid **[1]**

**3.** Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction of calcium carbonate with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**4.** A student wants to make pure, dry crystals of copper(II) sulfate from insoluble copper(II) oxide and dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Describe how the student should make the crystals. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why the copper(II) oxide is added until some is left over. **[2]**

**5.** A solution turns universal indicator purple.

**(a)** State whether the solution is acidic, neutral or alkaline. **[1]**
**(b)** Name an ion present in all aqueous alkaline solutions. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the ionic equation, including state symbols, for the reaction that happens when an acid neutralises an alkali. **[2]**
**(d)** Give **one** advantage of using a pH probe rather than universal indicator. **[1]**

**6.** A student finds the volume of sulfuric acid needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution by titration.

**(a)** Name the piece of apparatus used to measure exactly 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the student should carry out the titration. **[4]**
**(c)** The student's titres were 24.80, 24.10, 24.15 and 24.20 cm³. State which result should be ignored and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide solution with nitric acid. The mean titre is 20.0 cm³.
KOH + HNO₃ → KNO₃ + H₂O
Calculate the concentration of the nitric acid in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) 20.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution is neutralised by 12.5 cm³ of sulfuric acid.
2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³. **[4]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid: Zn + 2H⁺ → Zn²⁺ + H₂

**(a)** Explain, in terms of electrons, why this is a redox reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the spectator ion and explain why it is left out of the equation. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A student has two acids at the same concentration, 0.1 mol/dm³. Hydrochloric acid has pH 1 and ethanoic acid has pH 3.

**(a)** Explain why the two acids have different pH values. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the hydrogen ion concentration is in the hydrochloric acid. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between a **concentrated** acid and a **strong** acid. **[2]**

**11.** A student adds excess magnesium oxide to warm dilute hydrochloric acid, filters the mixture and evaporates part of the filtrate.

**(a)** Name the salt in the filtrate. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the student does not use sodium oxide for this method. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Magnesium chloride** [1] and **hydrogen** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Hold a **lit splint** at the mouth of the test tube [1]; a **squeaky pop** shows hydrogen [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two [1] [1]: bubbles/fizzing; the magnesium gets smaller or disappears; the test tube gets warm. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Zinc sulfate [1]
**(b)** Sodium nitrate [1]
**(c)** Magnesium chloride [1]
*Examiner insight:* The first word comes from the metal compound, the second from the acid.

**3.** CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [formulae correct 1; balanced 1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Any five points [1] each: **warm** the dilute sulfuric acid; add copper(II) oxide in small amounts, **stirring**; continue until no more dissolves; **filter** to remove the excess solid; **evaporate** (heat gently) to concentrate the filtrate / until crystals start to form; leave to **cool and crystallise**; filter off the crystals and **dry** them between filter papers. **[5]**
**(b)** All the **acid has been used up** / reacted [1], so the copper sulfate is not contaminated with acid; the excess is **insoluble** and so can be filtered off [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Alkaline [1]
**(b)** **Hydroxide ion**, OH⁻ [1]
**(c)** H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) [species 1; state symbols 1] **[2]**
**(d)** It gives a **numerical** (more accurate) pH value rather than a colour match [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** A **pipette** (with a pipette filler) [1]
**(b)** Add the 25.0 cm³ of alkali to a conical flask and add a few drops of **indicator** [1]; fill the **burette** with acid and record the start reading [1]; add the acid slowly, **swirling** the flask, until the indicator **just changes colour** (end point), going drop by drop near the end [1]; record the final reading and subtract; **repeat** until results are concordant [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Ignore **24.80 cm³** (anomalous / not concordant) [1]. Mean = (24.10 + 24.15 + 24.20) ÷ 3 = **24.15 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Moles KOH = 0.0250 × 0.120 = **0.00300 mol** [1]. Ratio 1 : 1, so moles HNO₃ = 0.00300 [1]. Concentration = 0.00300 ÷ 0.0200 = **0.150 mol/dm³** [1]. Mass concentration = 0.150 × 63 = **9.45 g/dm³** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert cm³ to dm³ before dividing; 25.0 cm³ is 0.0250 dm³.

**8.** Moles NaOH = 0.0200 × 0.250 = **0.00500 mol** [1]. Ratio 2 : 1, so moles H₂SO₄ = 0.00500 ÷ 2 = **0.00250 mol** [1]. Concentration = 0.00250 ÷ 0.0125 = **0.200 mol/dm³** [1]. Mass concentration = 0.200 × 98 = **19.6 g/dm³** [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Zinc atoms **lose electrons** (are oxidised) [1] and hydrogen ions **gain electrons** (are reduced); oxidation and reduction occur together [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The **chloride ion** [1]; it is **unchanged** in the reaction (does not gain or lose electrons) [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Hydrochloric acid is **strong**: it is **completely ionised**, giving a high H⁺ concentration [1]. Ethanoic acid is **weak**: only **partially ionised**, so its H⁺ concentration is lower and its pH higher [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** pH 1 to pH 3 is 2 units, so 10 × 10 = **100 times** greater [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** **Concentrated** describes a large amount of acid per unit volume of solution [1]; **strong** describes the acid being fully ionised in water, regardless of concentration [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Magnesium chloride [1]
**(b)** MgO + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O [formulae 1; balanced 1] **[2]**
**(c)** Sodium oxide reacts with water / is **soluble** (reacts to form soluble sodium hydroxide), so no insoluble solid is left to filter off [1]; the method relies on an **insoluble** solid being in excess [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the formulae of salts without balancing them.
- Using cm³ instead of dm³ in titration calculations.
- Forgetting the 2 : 1 ratio in the sulfuric acid titration.
- Mixing up concentrated, dilute, strong and weak.
- Describing salt making without the filtering step or the reason for excess solid.
