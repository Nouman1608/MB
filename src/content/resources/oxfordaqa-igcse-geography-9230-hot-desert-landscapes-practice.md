## Section A

**1.** Define the term deflation. **[1]**

**2.** Name the process by which sand grains are moved along the ground in a series of bounces. **[1]**

**3.** What is a wadi? **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** landforms in hot deserts that are formed by deposition. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the shape of a barchan dune. **[3]**

| Process | Share of sand moved (%) |
|---|---|

**(a)** Which process moved the most sand? **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The removal of loose, fine material (dust and sand) from a surface by the wind [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Saltation [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A dry valley in a desert that carries water only after heavy rain [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: dunes; alluvial fans; salt lakes (salt flats or playas) [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Any one from: oil and mineral extraction; solar energy; farming using irrigation; tourism [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Crescent shaped [1], with a gently sloping windward side and a steep slip face on the sheltered side [1], and the two horns pointing downwind [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Saltation [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 20 + 10 = 30% [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Sand grains are too heavy to be held in the air by the wind for long [1], so they are lifted a short distance, fall back and strike the surface, bouncing along close to the ground; only very fine dust is light enough for suspension [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Wind carries sand grains close to the ground [1]. These grains strike rock surfaces like sandpaper and wear them away [1]. The base of the rock is worn faster than the top, because most sand is concentrated near the ground, so rocks may be undercut or become narrow at the base [1]. **[3]**

**9.** A zeugen forms where a layer of hard rock lies over softer rock [1]. Weathering, such as temperature changes, opens cracks and joints in the hard cap rock [1]. Wind-blown sand abrades the softer rock beneath more quickly, cutting into the base [1]. Eventually, a table-shaped block of cap rock stands on a pedestal of softer rock [1]. **[4]**

**10.** A flash flood carries a heavy load of boulders, sand and silt down a wadi [1]. When the water leaves the narrow valley and spreads out over the open plain, it slows down and loses energy [1]. Water also sinks into the dry ground and evaporates, which reduces volume [1]. The load is deposited, with the heaviest material first and the finest material furthest out, building a fan-shaped landform over many floods [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Two opportunities named, each with development (2 marks each). For example: oil and mineral extraction, where large reserves bring export earnings and jobs [2]; solar energy, where clear skies and large areas of empty land suit solar farms producing electricity [2]; farming, where irrigation from groundwater allows crops such as wheat or dates to be grown [2]; tourism, where winter sun, desert safaris and resorts attract visitors and create jobs [2]. A named place or fact supports the explanation. **[4]**

**12.** Two challenges named, each with development (2 marks each). For example: extreme temperatures, which make outdoor work dangerous and cooling expensive [2]; water supply, as there is little rain, so people depend on desalination or groundwater that is being used faster than it recharges [2]; inaccessibility, because sand and long distances make roads, pipelines and power lines costly to build and maintain [2]. **[4]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: flash floods cut wadis, carry large loads and form alluvial fans, badlands and salt lakes [1].
- Agree: a single flood can change the landscape in hours, much more than daily wind [1].
- Disagree: wind forms yardangs, zeugen and dunes, and removes fine material by deflation, producing vast sand seas [1].
- Disagree: wind acts all the time, whereas floods are rare [1].
- Disagree: in the very driest deserts, wind is the main agent [1].
- Judgement: both are important; the balance depends on the desert, for example, areas with mountains and occasional storms have strong water features, while sandy interiors are dominated by wind; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a landform without explaining how it formed.
- Confusing abrasion (erosion) with saltation (transport).
- Forgetting loss of energy in questions on deposition.
- Opportunities and challenges that are listed with no development.
- Examples that are not named or located.
- Judgements without a reasoned conclusion.
