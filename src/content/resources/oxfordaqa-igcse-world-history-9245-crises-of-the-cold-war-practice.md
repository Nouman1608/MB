Content tested: "Threats to peace in the 1960s" (the U-2 Crisis, Berlin, the space and arms races, the Cuban Missile Crisis, Czechoslovakia) and "Conflict in Vietnam" (the US escalation of the conflict and the ending of the conflict).

## Section A: knowledge checks

**1.** Give three features of the Berlin Wall in 1961. **[3]**

**2.** Give three reasons why Khrushchev placed nuclear missiles in Cuba in 1962. **[3]**

**3.** Give three features of the Prague Spring of 1968. **[3]**

**4.** Give three features of the guerrilla tactics used by the Vietcong. **[3]**

## Section B: source-based questions

**Source A** *(pre-release, invented)* -- From a television address imagined as given by an American president to the nation, October 1962.
"Our ships will halt and search every vessel bound for Cuba. We seek no war, but these weapons must be removed. I have told the Soviet leader that any missile launched from Cuba will be answered by a full response against the Soviet Union."

**Source B** *(pre-release, invented)* -- A description of a cartoon imagined as published in a western European newspaper, August 1968. A tank marked "Warsaw Pact" rolls over a small flower labelled "Prague Spring". A Soviet officer on the tank says: "We have come to protect you from yourselves."

**Source C** *(pre-release, invented)* -- From a letter imagined as written by an American soldier in Vietnam to his family, 1967.
"We burned another village today. The captain says the villagers are helping the Vietcong. Maybe so, but the old women and children don't look like soldiers. The more we do this, the more of them seem to hate us. I don't see how we win like this."

**Source D** *(unseen, invented)* -- From a report imagined as written by an American television journalist in Saigon, February 1968.
"The Vietcong attacked more than a hundred towns and cities in a single week, and reached the grounds of the American embassy. They were driven back with heavy losses. But the generals had told us the enemy was nearly beaten. Americans at home have seen the fighting on their screens and will ask how that can be true."

**Source E** *(unseen, invented)* -- From a speech imagined as given by a student leader at a protest meeting in the USA, May 1970.
"The President says he is bringing the boys home, and in the same week he sends them into Cambodia. Four of our friends have been shot by the National Guard for demonstrating. We will not be silent until the war ends."

**5.** Use Source D and your contextual knowledge to support the claim that the Tet Offensive weakened American support for the war in Vietnam. **[4]**

**6.** Sources B and C give different impressions of the actions of the superpowers. Explain one difference between them, using the content of each source and your own knowledge. **[6]**

**7.** How useful is Source A to a historian studying the Cuban Missile Crisis? Use the content and provenance of Source A and your contextual knowledge. **[8]**

**8.** "Opposition within the USA was the main reason for the end of American involvement in Vietnam." Using Sources C, D and E and your own knowledge, how far do you agree? **[12]**

**9.** "In the 1960s the superpowers avoided war because both feared the consequences of nuclear conflict." Using Sources A and B and your own knowledge, how far do you agree? **[12]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** 3-mark checks: [1] per relevant point. Support a claim (4): source detail [1], explained as support [1], contextual knowledge [1], linked to the claim [1]. Difference (6): difference stated [2], support from both sources [2], explanation using knowledge [2]. Usefulness (8): content useful [2], knowledge supporting content [2], provenance useful [2], knowledge on provenance [2]; limitations are not required. Range of sources (12): sources used and linked [4], provenance used to weigh sources [2], own knowledge [4], judgement [2].

**1.** Any three, [1] each: it was begun in the early hours of 13 August 1961 with barbed wire, then replaced by concrete; it divided East and West Berlin; it stopped East Germans fleeing to the West; it was guarded by East German troops, and some people who tried to cross it were killed; the western powers protested but did not use force; it became a symbol of the division of Europe.

**2.** Any three, [1] each: to protect Cuba, a communist ally, from an American invasion after the Bay of Pigs; to match the American missiles in Turkey and Italy; to improve the Soviet position in the arms race, as the USSR had fewer ICBMs; to test the young and inexperienced Kennedy; to put American cities within range of Soviet missiles.

**3.** Any three, [1] each: Alexander Dubček became leader of the Communist Party in January 1968; "socialism with a human face"; censorship was relaxed and there was greater freedom of speech; the Czechoslovak government stayed in the Warsaw Pact; the reforms alarmed the USSR; it ended with the Warsaw Pact invasion in August 1968.

**4.** Any three, [1] each: ambushes and booby traps; hiding among the peasant population; tunnels and hidden bases; supplies carried along the Ho Chi Minh Trail; avoiding large battles and attacking by surprise; winning the support of villagers.

**5.** Source detail [1]: the Vietcong attacked more than a hundred towns and the generals had said the enemy was nearly beaten. Explained as support [1]: this shows that the war was far from being won, which would make Americans doubt what they were told. Contextual knowledge [1]: the Tet Offensive of January 1968 was a surprise attack on cities across South Vietnam; although it ended in heavy communist losses, it had a huge effect on public opinion because it had been shown on television. Linked to the claim [1]: after Tet, many Americans turned against the war and Johnson announced he would not stand for re-election, so Tet weakened American support.

**6.** Difference stated [2]: Source B shows the USSR using force to crush a reform movement in its sphere of influence, while Source C shows the USA using force against a population in a country outside its borders and doubting the policy. Support from both [2]: B shows a tank rolling over the "Prague Spring"; C describes burning a village where the villagers "don't look like soldiers". Explanation using knowledge [2]: the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia in August 1968 was justified by the Brezhnev Doctrine, which gave the USSR the right to intervene to protect socialism; American Search and Destroy missions in Vietnam in the 1960s burned villages suspected of helping the Vietcong, and they turned many peasants against the USA.

**7.** Content useful [2]: it shows Kennedy's policy of a naval blockade ("halt and search"), his claim that he did not want war, and a nuclear warning that any missile from Cuba would be answered with a response against the USSR. Knowledge supporting content [2]: the blockade, called a quarantine, was announced on 22 October 1962; the missile sites had been found by U-2 photographs; the warning was a real threat in a crisis that brought the world close to nuclear war. Provenance useful [2]: a presidential television address to the nation is an official statement of the American position at the height of the crisis, and it shows what the US government wanted the world, and the USSR, to hear. Knowledge on provenance [2]: Kennedy was addressing the public and Khrushchev at the same time; behind the scenes, the two leaders were negotiating, and the USA later agreed in secret to remove its missiles from Turkey, which the speech does not mention, so the source shows the public stance and not the whole story.

**8.** Sources used and linked [4]: D shows that the Tet Offensive was shown on television and raised doubts about whether the war could be won; E shows student protests, a response to the widening of the war into Cambodia and the Kent State shootings; C shows the problem of fighting a guerrilla war in which the Americans' own actions turned villagers against them. Provenance [2]: D is from a television journalist who reached a mass audience, so it reflects and shapes opinion; E is from a protest leader and so is likely to stress the strength of opposition; C is a private letter, which is likely to be honest but shows only one soldier's view. Own knowledge [4]: the protest movement and the media; My Lai (1968) and Kent State (1970); Vietnamisation and the gradual withdrawal of American troops; Nixon's bombing and the Paris peace talks of 1973; the cost in American lives; the strength of the Vietcong and North Vietnamese. Judgement [2]: for example, opposition at home was important because it limited what presidents could do, but it was linked to the military reality that the USA could not defeat guerrillas supported by the USSR and China, so the main reason was a combination of both.

**9.** Sources used and linked [4]: A shows Kennedy threatening a "full response" if a missile was launched, which supports the idea that nuclear fear mattered, but also shows the use of a blockade, a limited step, to avoid war; B shows the USSR using force in its sphere of influence, but the West did not intervene, which suggests avoiding conflict with the other superpower. Provenance [2]: A is a speech, so it states official policy and wants to sound firm and also peaceful; B is a western cartoon, so it is hostile to the USSR and tells us about western reactions but not about Soviet reasoning. Own knowledge [4]: in the Cuban Missile Crisis both leaders chose to compromise on 28 October 1962; afterwards the hotline and the Test Ban Treaty were set up; in 1961 tanks at Checkpoint Charlie withdrew; in 1968 the West protested but did not act over Czechoslovakia; the Soviet sphere of influence was accepted. Judgement [2]: for example, fear of nuclear war was the main factor, because it persuaded both leaders to step back in 1961 and 1962, but respect for the other's sphere of influence also mattered, as shown in 1968.
