## The three mechanisms

| | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Solids mainly | Liquids and gases | Anywhere, including a vacuum |
| Needs a medium? | Yes | Yes | No |
| How | Lattice vibrations; free electrons in metals | Warmer fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises | Infrared waves emitted and absorbed |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Good conductor** | Transfers thermal energy quickly by conduction, e.g. metals |
| **Insulator** | Poor conductor, e.g. plastic, wood, trapped air |
| **Lattice vibrations** | Vibrating particles passing energy to bonded neighbours |
| **Free (delocalised) electrons** | Electrons moving through a metal, carrying energy rapidly |
| **Convection current** | Circulation of fluid caused by density differences |
| **Infrared radiation** | Electromagnetic radiation emitted by all objects; no medium needed |
| **Emitter / absorber** | Surface that gives out / takes in infrared radiation |

## Conduction

- All solids: lattice vibrations pass energy from particle to neighbour.
- Metals: free electrons gain energy and move quickly to cooler parts → metals conduct best.
- Liquids poor, gases very poor → trapped air insulates.
- **Experiment:** rods of different materials, **same length and diameter**, heated equally at one
  end; pins stuck with wax at the far ends; first pin to fall = best conductor.

## Convection

- Heated fluid expands → density falls → rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks → current forms.
- No convection in solids: particles cannot move from place to place.
- **Experiment (water):** crystal of potassium manganate(VII) at the bottom of a beaker, heated
  gently beneath; coloured stream rises, crosses, sinks and returns.
- **Experiment (air):** smoke or paper rising above a candle or heater.

## Radiation

- Dull black: best emitter and absorber, poor reflector.
- Shiny white or silver: poorest emitter and absorber, good reflector.
- Rate of emission increases with **higher surface temperature** and **larger surface area**.
- **Emitters experiment:** Leslie cube filled with hot water; infrared detector at the same
  distance from each face; dull black reads highest.
- **Absorbers experiment:** dull black and shiny plates equidistant from a radiant heater, pins
  fixed with wax; black plate's pin falls first.

## Applications (syllabus list)

- **Pans:** metal base conducts energy quickly to food; plastic or wooden handle insulates.
- **Room heating:** heater placed low; warm air rises, cool air sinks back to it; convection
  current spreads warmth.
- **Infrared thermometer:** detects infrared emitted by a surface; hotter surface emits more, so
  the reading gives its temperature without contact.
- **Liquids:** vacuum flask: vacuum stops conduction and convection; silvering reflects
  radiation; stopper stops convection and evaporation.
- **Buildings:** loft and cavity insulation trap air; double glazing; reflective foil.

## Fair-test checklist for the named experiments

- **Conductors:** same length and diameter of rod; same heating; same amount of wax; same start
  temperature.
- **Emitters:** same water temperature for every face; detector at the same distance; same
  surroundings.
- **Absorbers:** identical plates; equal distances from the heater; same amount of wax.
- **Cooling cans:** same size and volume of water; same start temperature; lids to limit
  evaporation.

## Linking to other subtopics

- **2.1:** particle spacing explains why gases conduct poorly.
- **2.2:** expansion lowers density, which drives convection.
- **3.3:** infrared is part of the electromagnetic spectrum.

## Exam traps

- "Heat rises" loses the mark; say the warmer, less dense fluid rises.
- Convection needs a fluid: never in solids.
- Metals: always mention free electrons as well as vibrations.
- A vacuum stops conduction and convection, **not** radiation.
- Name **infrared** radiation, not just "heat".
- Fair tests: same distance, same size, same starting temperature, same heating.
- Good absorbers are good emitters; good reflectors are poor absorbers.

## One-line explanations to learn

- **Why metals feel cold:** they conduct energy away from your hand quickly.
- **Why trapped air insulates:** air is a poor conductor, and small pockets stop convection.
- **Why fins are dull black:** large area and a good emitting surface increase radiation.

## Self-test

1. Name the two mechanisms of conduction in a metal.
2. Why are metals better conductors than plastics?
3. Describe how to compare four rods as conductors.
4. Explain how a convection current forms in a heated liquid.
5. Why can convection not occur in a solid?
6. Why can infrared radiation travel through space?
7. Which surface is the best emitter of infrared radiation?
8. State two factors that increase the rate of emission from an object.
9. Describe how a Leslie cube shows which surface is the best emitter.
10. Why is a room heater placed near the floor?
11. How does an infrared thermometer measure temperature?
12. Explain two features of a vacuum flask.

### Answers

1. Lattice vibrations and free (delocalised) electrons.
2. Metals have free electrons that carry energy quickly; plastics have only lattice vibrations.
3. Same length and diameter; heat one end of each equally; wax-fixed pins at the far ends; the
   first pin to fall marks the best conductor.
4. Heated liquid expands and becomes less dense, so it rises; cooler, denser liquid sinks to
   replace it, setting up a circulation.
5. Its particles cannot move from place to place.
6. It does not need a medium.
7. Dull black.
8. Higher surface temperature; larger surface area.
9. Fill with hot water so all faces are at the same temperature; place a detector the same
   distance from each face; the dull black face gives the highest reading.
10. Warm air rises from it and cooler air sinks back towards it, so a convection current
    circulates through the whole room.
11. It detects the infrared radiation emitted by a surface; a hotter surface emits more, which
    the thermometer converts into a temperature.
12. Any two: vacuum stops conduction and convection; silvered surfaces reflect infrared; stopper
    reduces conduction, convection and evaporation.
