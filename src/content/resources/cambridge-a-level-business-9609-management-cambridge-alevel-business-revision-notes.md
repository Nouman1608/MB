Condensed notes for **2.3 Management** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, 2026–2028). For applied examples and a model answer, see the [Management study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-management-cambridge-alevel-business/); for exam-style questions, see the [Management practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-management-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/).

## What 2.3 asks you to handle

Section 2.3.1: the four traditional functions; the role of managers (Fayol, Mintzberg); managers' contribution to performance; four management styles; McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Manager | Someone responsible for achieving objectives through other people and resources |
| Planning | Setting objectives and deciding how to reach them |
| Organising | Arranging people, tasks and resources |
| Directing | Guiding, instructing and supporting staff |
| Controlling | Comparing results with plans and correcting differences |
| Management style | The way a manager makes decisions and treats staff |
| Autocratic | Manager decides alone and instructs |
| Democratic | Staff are consulted and influence decisions |
| Laissez-faire | Staff are left to decide how to work |
| Paternalistic | Manager decides but in staff's interests, and explains why |
| Theory X | Manager assumes staff dislike work and need close control |
| Theory Y | Manager assumes staff can enjoy work and seek responsibility |

## The four functions as a cycle

Plan → organise → direct → control → (results feed the next plan). Always give an example from the case for each function you use.

## Fayol against Mintzberg

- **Fayol**: what management *should* involve; orderly elements: forecast and plan, organise, command, coordinate, control. Useful checklist; underplays daily reality.
- **Mintzberg**: what managers *actually* do; fragmented, interrupted, much talking. Ten roles in three groups. Realistic; less guidance on doing the roles well.
- They are compatible: Fayol = purpose; Mintzberg = daily activity.

## Mintzberg's roles: quick classifier

| Group | Roles | Clue words in a case |
|---|---|---|
| Interpersonal | Figurehead, leader, liaison | Ceremony, motivating staff, networking outside the team |
| Informational | Monitor, disseminator, spokesperson | Reading reports, passing information inside, speaking to outsiders |
| Decisional | Entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator | New ideas, emergencies, budgets, deals |

## Evidence of managers' contribution

Late deliveries, costs, labour turnover, productivity, customer complaints. Before crediting the manager, check: demand changes, new equipment, competitor changes, seasonal effects.

## How managers raise performance

| Contribution | Effect on the business |
|---|---|
| Clear direction and targets | Staff know priorities, so less effort is wasted |
| Allocating resources well | Money and people go where returns are highest |
| Motivating staff | Higher productivity, lower labour turnover |
| Solving problems quickly | Less disruption to output and customers |
| Controlling cost and quality | Higher margins, fewer complaints |

## Applying functions to a case: template

For each function used, write one sentence naming **what this manager does** in the case, then one sentence on **the effect**. Example for a café opening a second site: *organising* means recruiting baristas and setting the rota, so the new site can open fully staffed on time.

## Choosing a style: five factors

1. **Urgency** → autocratic in crises.
2. **Staff skill** → experts suit democratic or laissez-faire; new staff need direction.
3. **Complexity** → complex decisions benefit from many views.
4. **Culture and expectations** → some staff expect consultation; others value security (paternalistic).
5. **Manager's beliefs** → Theory X or Y.

## Styles: where each goes wrong

- Autocratic → ideas lost, skilled staff leave.
- Democratic → delay when time is short.
- Laissez-faire → drift with inexperienced staff.
- Paternalistic → professionals feel patronised.

## McGregor

- Theory X → close supervision, autocratic style, pay linked to output (Taylor link).
- Theory Y → delegation, empowerment, democratic style (Herzberg and Maslow links).
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy**: treating staff as X can make them act like X.
- Limitation: managers mix assumptions; some staff do not want more responsibility.

## Exam traps

- Theory X and Theory Y describe **managers' assumptions**, not types of worker.
- No style is always best; justify by the situation.
- Paternalistic is **not** democratic: the manager still decides.
- Laissez-faire is **not** the same as no management; objectives are still set.
- Fayol's elements and Mintzberg's roles are different lists; do not mix them.
- One improvement figure is not proof of good management; name other possible causes.

## Self-test

1. Name the four traditional functions of a manager.
2. Give one example of controlling in a retail store.
3. What is the main difference in method between Fayol and Mintzberg?
4. A manager signs a sponsorship deal at a charity event. Which Mintzberg role is this?
5. A manager reads weekly sales reports from each branch. Which role?
6. Which management style best suits an emergency evacuation, and why?
7. Give one disadvantage of a laissez-faire style.
8. How does a paternalistic style differ from an autocratic one?
9. Describe the self-fulfilling prophecy in McGregor's theory.
10. Which motivation theorist's ideas fit best with Theory X?
11. Costs fell from $50 000 to $46 000 a month after a new manager arrived. Calculate the percentage fall.
12. Give one reason why the fall in question 11 may not be due to the manager.

**Answers**

1. Planning, organising, directing, controlling.
2. For example, comparing weekly sales with the target and changing staffing if sales fall short.
3. Fayol described what management should involve from experience; Mintzberg observed what managers actually do.
4. Negotiator (signing the deal) or figurehead (representing the business at the event); credit either if justified.
5. Monitor (informational).
6. Autocratic, because quick, clear instructions are needed and there is no time to consult.
7. Lack of direction, especially with inexperienced staff, so deadlines may be missed.
8. Both keep decisions with the manager, but a paternalistic manager acts out of concern for staff welfare and explains decisions.
9. A manager who assumes staff are lazy controls them closely, which gives staff no chance to show initiative, so they behave as expected.
10. Taylor.
11. (50 000 − 46 000) ÷ 50 000 × 100 = 8%.
12. For example, lower prices for materials, falling output or new equipment.
