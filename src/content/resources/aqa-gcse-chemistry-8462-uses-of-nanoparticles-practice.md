These questions cover section **4.2.4.2 Uses of nanoparticles** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification,
for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at Foundation and
Higher Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content. The questions supply the information you need,
as the specification requires.

Learn the content first in the [uses of nanoparticles study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Give **two** areas in which nanoparticles have applications. **[2]**

**2.** State what is meant by a nanoparticle in terms of its size. **[1]**

**3.** **(a)** State **one** possible risk of using nanoparticles in cosmetics. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the risk in part (a) is described as "possible". **[1]**

**4.** A scientist says that new uses of nanoparticles are an important area of research. Give **one** reason why
this research is useful. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A deodorant contains nanoparticles. A company says that a small mass of nanoparticles is as effective as a
larger mass of ordinary particles of the same material. Explain why. **[3]**

**6.** A chemical company compares two catalysts for the same reaction.

| Catalyst | Mass needed per batch / kg | Cost per kg / £ |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary pieces | 40 | 35 |
| Nanoparticles | 4 | 220 |

**(a)** Calculate the cost of catalyst per batch for each. **[2]**
**(b)** State which is cheaper per batch and by how much. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** other factor the company should consider before changing to nanoparticles. **[1]**

**7.** A sun cream contains nanoparticles that block ultraviolet light. Because they are so small, the cream looks
clear on the skin. A consumer group is concerned that the nanoparticles may pass through the skin.

**(a)** Give **one** advantage of using nanoparticles in the sun cream. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the consumer group's concern. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what information would help to decide whether the nanoparticles are safe. **[2]**

**8.** A hospital is testing a medicine in which a drug is attached to nanoparticles. The nanoparticles carry the
drug to cells in the body.

**(a)** Suggest **one** advantage of using nanoparticles in this way. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** possible risk. **[1]**

**9.** A school is told that a cleaning product contains silver nanoparticles that kill bacteria. The product is
more expensive than the normal product. Evaluate whether the school should buy it, using the information given
and your knowledge of nanoparticles. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two [1] [1]: **medicine**; **electronics**; **cosmetics and sun creams**; **deodorants**; **catalysts**.
**[2]**

**2.** A particle with a diameter of **1 to 100 nm** [1]

**3. (a)** Any one: they may **enter the body** (through the skin or lungs); their **long-term effects on health are
unknown**; they may **harm the environment** [1]
**(b)** The risks are **not proven** / effects are not fully understood [1]

**4.** Any one: **new uses** may be found; the **risks** can be understood; the materials may have **new
properties** [1]

**5.** Nanoparticles have a very high surface area to volume ratio [1]; for the same mass, more of the material is
exposed at the surface [1]; so a smaller mass can be as effective [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Ordinary: 40 × 35 = **£1400** [1]; nanoparticles: 4 × 220 = **£880** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The **nanoparticle** catalyst [1], by 1400 - 880 = **£520** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: the cost and difficulty of **making** nanoparticles reliably; **possible risks** to health or the
environment; whether the catalyst can be **recovered** and reused [1]

**7. (a)** Any one: the cream is **clear / spreads evenly**; less material is needed; blocks ultraviolet light
effectively [1]
**(b)** Nanoparticles are **very small** [1] so they might pass through the skin and enter the body, where their
effects are not known [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two [1] [1]: results of **long-term studies** on health; evidence of whether the particles **pass
through the skin**; their **effect on the environment** when washed off. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Any one: the drug can be **delivered to specific cells**; a **smaller amount of drug** may be needed [1]
**(b)** Any one: the nanoparticles might **harm healthy cells**; the **long-term effects** are not known [1]

**9.** Marking points, up to 6:
- Advantage: silver nanoparticles are said to kill bacteria, so the product may clean better [1].
- Nanoparticles have a high surface area to volume ratio, so a small quantity may be effective [1].
- The product costs more, which is a disadvantage for the school's budget [1].
- Possible risk: nanoparticles could be washed into drains and the environment and their effects are not fully
  known [1].
- Possible risk: they may be breathed in or touched and their effects on health are not fully known [1].
- Conclusion with justification, for example: the school should wait for further evidence / buy only if the
  extra cost is justified by a clear benefit and safety information is available [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the conclusion earns a mark only if it follows from reasons that you have given.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only advantages or only risks in an evaluation.
- Not using the figures from the question in a cost calculation.
- Writing that nanoparticles "are dangerous" instead of "may be harmful".
- Leaving out the conclusion.
- Not linking the effectiveness of small amounts to the high surface area to volume ratio.
