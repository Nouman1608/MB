## Answers

**1.** HCF of 56 and 84 is 28: **2/3** [1]

**2. (a)** 375/1000 = **3/8** [1]

**(b)** **37.5%** [1]

**3. (a)** 17 ÷ 4 = 4 remainder 1: **4 1/4** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 9 + 5 = 23: **23/9** [1]

**4. (a)** 145 ÷ 100 = **1.45** [1]

**(b)** 145/100 = **29/20** (or 1 9/20) [1]

**5.** 0.6/100 = 6/1000 [1]; **3/500** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 0.6% is less than one hundredth; 6/100 or 3/50 is ten times too large.

**6.** 7 ÷ 12 = 0.583 33…, so **0.583** [1]

*Examiner insight:* at Core, a decimal that does not stop is rounded to the accuracy asked; recurring notation is not required.

**7. (a)** **0.12** [1]

**(b)** **12%** [1]

**(c)** **9/20** [1]

**(d)** **45%** [1]

**(e)** **7/8** [1]

**(f)** **0.875** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 87.5% = 87.5/100 = 875/1000; cancelling by 125 gives 7/8. Knowing that 1/8 = 0.125 makes (e) and (f) quick.

**8. (a)** 25% = 1/4 = 2/8, so 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8 [1]; bus = 1 − 5/8 = **3/8** [1]

**(b)** **37.5%** [1]

**(c)** 3/8 × 32 = **12** [1]

*Examiner insight:* converting 25% to a fraction first lets you add it to 3/8; mixing a fraction and a percentage in one calculation usually goes wrong.

**9. (a)** 40 = 2³ × 5, which has only the prime factors 2 and 5, so it **terminates** (0.225) [1]

**(b)** 24 = 2³ × 3 and 5/24 cannot be simplified; the factor 3 means it **does not terminate** [1]

**10.** x = 0.6363…, 100x = 63.6363… [1]; 99x = 63 [1]; x = 63/99 = **7/11** [1]

**11.** x = 0.1333…; 10x = 1.333… and 100x = 13.333… [1]; 100x − 10x: 90x = 12 [1]; x = 12/90 = 2/15 [1]

*Examiner insight:* this is a "show that", so the subtraction must be seen. 100x − x leaves 99x = 13.2, which still has a decimal; choose multipliers whose tails match.

**12.** **0.5̇71428̇** (dots over the first 5 and the last 8) [1]

**13.** x = 1.2424…, 100x = 124.2424… [1]; 99x = 123 [1]; x = 123/99 = 41/33 = **1 8/33** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the whole number part can be kept separate: 0.2̇4̇ = 24/99 = 8/33, so 1.2̇4̇ = 1 8/33. Both routes earn full marks.

**14. (a)** To change a decimal to a percentage you multiply by 100; Ravi has multiplied by 10 (4% is 0.04) [1]

**(b)** **40%** [1]; 4/10 = **2/5** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping before a fraction is fully simplified (for example 28/42 instead of 2/3).
- Place-value errors with percentages below 1% or above 100%.
- Truncating instead of rounding (0.583 is right; 0.584 and 0.58 are not, for 3 d.p.).
- (Extended) Dots placed on the wrong digits, which changes the value.
- (Extended) Presenting a calculator check instead of algebra in a "show that" question.

## Approaching conversion questions

Decide the target form first, then use the shortest route: a denominator that divides into 10, 100 or 1000 converts by scaling, and anything else by division. Write the intermediate fraction (such as 375/1000) before simplifying, because it earns the method mark if the cancelling goes wrong. For recurring decimals, write x, choose the two multiples, subtract, then simplify, in that order every time.
