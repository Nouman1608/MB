These notes summarise section 5.3, Circles, arcs and sectors, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [circles, arcs and sectors study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors/), and exam-style questions are in the [circles, arcs and sectors practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors-practice/).

## What is examined

- Calculations with the circumference and area of a circle. The formulas are in the List of formulas.
- Arc length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area, including minor and major sectors.
- Answers may be asked for in terms of π.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radius r | centre to circumference |
| Diameter d | across the circle through the centre, d = 2r |
| Circumference | distance around the circle |
| Arc | part of the circumference |
| Sector | region between two radii and an arc |
| Minor sector | angle less than 180° |
| Major sector | angle greater than 180° |
| In terms of π | an exact answer with π left as a symbol |

## Formulas

```
C = 2πr = πd
A = πr²
arc length = θ/360 × 2πr
sector area = θ/360 × πr²
perimeter of a sector = arc length + 2r
perimeter of a semicircle = πr + 2r
```

The first two are given in the examination. The arc and sector formulas come from them and the fraction θ/360.

## Method box: sector problems

1. Write the fraction of the circle: θ/360.
2. Multiply by the circumference for arc length, or by the area for sector area.
3. For a perimeter, add the radii.
4. For a missing angle, form an equation and solve for θ.
5. For a missing radius, form an equation in r or r².

### Examples

```
r = 10, θ = 72°:   arc = 1/5 × 20π = 4π,  area = 1/5 × 100π = 20π
r = 6, θ = 250°:   arc = 25π/3,  area = 25π
sector area 12π, θ = 120°:  r² = 36, r = 6
```

## Rings and borders

```
ring area = π R² − π r²
```

Subtract areas, not radii. A path of width w around a circle of radius r has outer radius r + w.

## Rounding and π

- Use the π key on your calculator, or 3.142.
- Do not round until the end of the working.
- Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.
- In terms of π: keep the symbol, for example 14π.

## Context problems

| Situation | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Wheel making n turns | distance = n × circumference |
| Clock hand | tip travels a fraction of the circumference: minutes ÷ 60 |
| Running track | two semicircles make one full circle |
| Pendulum swing | arc length with θ the swing angle |
| Pie chart | angle = percentage × 360° |
| Path around a pond | outer area − inner area |

### Example: running track

A track has straights of 100 m and two semicircular ends, and each lap is 400 m. The semicircles together have circumference 2πr = 400 − 200 = 200 m, so r = 100 ÷ π = 31.8 m.

### Example: pie chart

A sector for 15% of the data has angle 0.15 × 360 = 54°. If the pie chart has radius 10 cm, the area of that sector is 54/360 × 100π = 15π cm², which is 47.1 cm².

### Example: wheel

A wheel of radius 35 cm has circumference 70π = 220 cm. For a distance of 100 m = 10 000 cm, it makes 10 000 ÷ 220 = 45.5 turns.

## Estimating with π

For a quick check, use π ≈ 3. A circle of radius 7 has circumference about 42 (actual 44) and area about 147 (actual 154). If your answer is far from the estimate, look for a slip, such as using the diameter as the radius.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Circumference | a length, in cm |
| Area | in square units, cm² |
| Radius vs diameter | halve the diameter before using πr² |
| Arc | part of the curve |
| Sector | a slice including the radii |

## Exam traps

- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Forgetting the two radii for the perimeter of a sector.
- Writing the fraction as 360/θ.
- Not using the exact value of π, so the third figure is off.
- Forgetting that the major sector has the larger angle.
- Subtracting radii in a ring problem.

## Self-test

1. Find the circumference of a circle of diameter 10 cm, in terms of π.
2. Find the area of a circle of radius 5 cm, in terms of π.
3. Find the arc length of a sector with radius 4 cm and angle 90°, in terms of π.
4. Find the area of a sector with radius 6 cm and angle 60°, in terms of π.
5. Find the perimeter of a semicircle of radius 5 cm, to 3 significant figures.
6. The circumference of a circle is 20π cm. Find its area in terms of π.
7. A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 120°. Find its arc length in terms of π.
8. A circle has area 30 cm². Find its radius to 3 significant figures.
9. A ring has outer radius 10 cm and inner radius 8 cm. Find its area in terms of π.
10. A sector has angle 45° and radius 8 cm. Find its area in terms of π.

### Answers

1. **10π cm**.
2. **25π cm²**.
3. 1/4 × 2π × 4 = **2π cm**.
4. 1/6 × π × 36 = **6π cm²**.
5. 5π + 10 = **25.7 cm**.
6. r = 10, so the area is **100π cm²**.
7. 1/3 × 2π × 9 = **6π cm**.
8. r² = 30 ÷ π = 9.549, so **r = 3.09 cm**.
9. π(100 − 64) = **36π cm²**.
10. 1/8 × π × 64 = **8π cm²**.
