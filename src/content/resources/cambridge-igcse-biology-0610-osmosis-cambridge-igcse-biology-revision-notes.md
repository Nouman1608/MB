## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Solvent | A liquid in which substances dissolve; in organisms, water |
| Partially permeable membrane | Lets small molecules such as water through, but not larger ones such as sucrose |
| Dilute solution | Contains a lot of water and little solute |
| Concentrated solution | Contains less water and more solute |
| Dialysis tubing | Partially permeable tubing used as a model membrane |
| Water potential (Extended) | The tendency of water to move out of a solution; highest in pure water |
| Turgid (Extended) | Swollen and firm, after taking in water |
| Turgor pressure (Extended) | Pressure of cell contents pushing outwards on the cell wall |
| Flaccid (Extended) | Soft, after losing water |
| Plasmolysis (Extended) | Membrane and cytoplasm pulled away from the cell wall after heavy water loss |

## Core: water as a solvent

- **Digestion:** soluble products of digestion dissolve in water and are absorbed.
- **Excretion:** urea dissolved in water leaves as urine.
- **Transport:** substances dissolved in plasma; ions in xylem; sucrose in phloem.

## Core: the statements

- Water diffuses through **partially permeable membranes** by osmosis.
- Water moves **into and out of cells** by osmosis through the **cell membrane**.
- Plants are supported by the **pressure of water inside the cells pressing outwards on the cell wall**.
- Core direction: from **dilute** to **more concentrated** solution, across the membrane.

## Core: dialysis tubing practical

| Set-up | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrose solution inside, water outside | Gains mass; liquid rises in capillary | Water enters by osmosis; sucrose cannot leave |
| Water inside, sucrose solution outside | Loses mass | Water leaves by osmosis |
| Water inside, water outside (control) | Little change | No concentration difference |

## Core: plant tissue practical

- Equal-sized potato or other tissue pieces; blot; weigh.
- Range of sucrose concentrations, same volume, same time, same temperature.
- Blot; reweigh; **percentage change = change ÷ start mass × 100**.
- Gain in dilute solutions, loss in concentrated ones.
- Where the line crosses 0%: no net water movement; estimates the concentration inside the cells.

**Worked reminder:** start 3.20 g, end 2.88 g → change −0.32 g → −0.32 ÷ 3.20 × 100 = **−10%**.

## Extended: the definition

**Net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.**

Pure water = highest water potential. More solute = lower water potential.

## Extended: plant cells in three solutions

| Outside solution | Water potential compared with cell sap | Water moves | Cell becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure water or very dilute | Higher | In | **Turgid**; high **turgor pressure** |
| Same | Equal | No net movement | No change |
| Concentrated | Lower | Out | **Flaccid**, then **plasmolysed** |

## Extended: importance for organisms

- Root hair cells absorb water by osmosis from soil of higher water potential.
- Wilting: water loss → flaccid cells → loss of support.
- Animal cells (no wall): burst in water; shrink in concentrated solutions. Plasma water potential must stay steady.

## Exam traps

- Sucrose does **not** cross dialysis tubing or the cell membrane in these explanations.
- Plant cells do **not** burst; the wall resists.
- **Plasmolysis** is for plant cells only.
- Say "water potential" (Extended) or "dilute/concentrated" (Core), not just "concentration".
- Use **percentage** change, and blot before every weighing.
- Always name the **partially permeable membrane**.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three roles of water as a solvent named in the syllabus.
2. Through what type of membrane does osmosis occur?
3. Why does sucrose solution in dialysis tubing in water cause the tubing to gain mass?
4. State how water supports plants.
5. A potato cylinder goes from 4.00 g to 4.36 g. Calculate the percentage change.
6. Why are cylinders blotted before weighing?
7. What does it mean if a potato cylinder shows 0% change?
8. (Extended) Give the Extended definition of osmosis.
9. (Extended) Which has the higher water potential: pure water or a sugar solution?
10. (Extended) Describe a plasmolysed cell.
11. (Extended) Explain why a red blood cell bursts in pure water but a plant cell does not.
12. (Extended) Explain how a root hair cell takes up water.

### Answers

1. Digestion, excretion and transport.
2. A partially permeable membrane.
3. Water moves into the tubing by osmosis, from the dilute water outside to the concentrated solution inside; sucrose cannot leave.
4. Water inside the cells presses outwards on the cell wall.
5. 0.36 ÷ 4.00 × 100 = **+9%**.
6. To remove surface water that would change the mass without having entered by osmosis.
7. No net movement of water: the solution matches the concentration inside the cells.
8. The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.
9. Pure water.
10. The membrane and cytoplasm have pulled away from the cell wall because the cell has lost a lot of water.
11. Water enters both by osmosis; the red blood cell has no cell wall, so it bursts, but the plant cell wall resists the turgor pressure.
12. Soil water has a higher water potential than the cell sap, so water moves into the root hair cell by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membrane.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is "turgid" a Core term?**
No. Turgid, turgor pressure, flaccid and plasmolysis are Extended terms in 0610, though Core candidates describe the effects on plant tissue.

**Can osmosis happen without a membrane?**
No. Osmosis is defined as movement of water across a partially permeable membrane.
