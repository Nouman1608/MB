This page is a guide to AQA GCSE English Language (8700) past papers for the June 2026, November 2025, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it with the mark scheme, which shows what each level of response needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still stayed in a lower level. The notes for each series below are a quick way into those reports. Because the source texts change every series, a report is most helpful when read next to the paper it describes.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 8700 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2015 and first examined in June 2017. It has two written papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks and 50 per cent of the GCSE: Paper 1 (Explorations in creative reading and writing) and Paper 2 (Writers' viewpoints and perspectives). Each paper is split evenly between a reading section and a writing section. Every candidate also completes a separate Spoken Language endorsement, reported as Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified; it does not count towards the GCSE grade, so the thresholds below cover the two written papers only. All the series on this page were set on this specification.

## November 2025

The November 2025 reports record rising attainment on Paper 1 and a stable result on Paper 2, with entries for Paper 2 up from about 48,000 in November 2024 to about 59,000. AQA gives the Paper 2 mean as 34.3 marks, slightly above the 32.8 of a year earlier.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8700/1): imagery taken at a very literal level in the language question, structural shifts spotted but not explained, and some drift into wider social issues in the evaluation question instead of staying with the extract. In the writing task, some candidates rewrote the extract almost word for word, changing little more than a detail.
- Paper 2 (8700/2): inferences were easier for candidates than linking ideas across the two texts. In the language question, candidates struggled to pick the best examples from the given lines and often explained an adjective with a synonym. In the comparison question, perspectives were compared but the methods behind them were left out. In the writing task, ideas were hard to develop concretely, and sentence demarcation and agreement caused most of the technical errors.

**What stronger answers did:** read metaphorical language for what it implied, showed how the structure changed across the extract and what each change did for the reader, argued clearly for or against the statement with linked evidence, and wrote a developed episode rather than a rushed whole story.

**Spoken language:** the verifiers noted precise use of the criteria and a marked fall in candidates reading from a script, along with longer talks and better questioning.

## June 2025

Both reports for June 2025 were positive. The Paper 2 report gives a mean mark that rose from 36.6 in June 2024 to 40.5, with gains in reading and in both writing assessment objectives.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8700/1): answers that went outside the lines the question named, a glossary meaning copied out as if it were the comment on effect, and writing that tried to cover a whole story and so came out thin and rushed. Some responses copied or lightly altered the extract.
- Paper 2 (8700/2): misreadings of idiomatic or figurative phrases in the opening true-or-false task, a focus on the subject of the text instead of what the writer said about it, language examples chosen because they were unfamiliar rather than because they were useful, comparison of viewpoints without comment on methods, and writing that drifted from the set topic. Punctuation was sometimes used as a checklist instead of for effect.
- Spoken language: over-reliance on scripts, no clear purpose for the talk, ideas that stayed at headline level, and questioning that was pre-prepared or too easy to allow higher marks.

**What stronger answers did:** identified explicit information without over-complicating it, explained the effect of structure (for example why a story opens as it does), and kept to a developed episode in creative writing.

## June 2024

For June 2024 the Paper 1 report found the extract accessible and the paper well answered, while the Paper 2 report records a modest rise in reading marks and steady writing marks.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8700/1): structure was the weak point. Many candidates struggled to see structural features, and those who did often did not say what they achieved. Few drew out wider themes in the evaluation question. In descriptive writing, a setting with nothing in it limited the piece, and very rare, showy words damaged clarity.
- Paper 2 (8700/2): candidates wrote about the writers' feelings rather than the subject, did not explain the how and why of language choices, assumed things about class, race or gender that the text did not support, and gave general ideas in the writing task without examples. Ambitious vocabulary was often imprecise or misspelt.

**What stronger answers did:** engaged closely with the people and situations in the texts, gave sustained evaluation, and built atmosphere by showing rather than telling in creative writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA 8700 past papers?**
AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment-resources page for 8700, and its past-paper finder lists papers by series.

**Do the thresholds include the Spoken Language endorsement?**
No. The endorsement is reported separately as Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified and does not count towards the GCSE grade, so the thresholds apply to the two written papers.

**Which papers should I sit when practising a series?**
Sit both written papers, Paper 1 and Paper 2, for the same series, then mark them with the official mark scheme. Each paper has the same split between reading and writing.

**What do examiners report most often?**
Across these series they describe structure that is spotted but not explained, language examples that are explained with a synonym, comparison that leaves out methods, and creative writing that tries to tell a whole story instead of one episode.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
