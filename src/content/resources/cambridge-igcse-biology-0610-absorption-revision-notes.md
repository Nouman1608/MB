These notes condense sub-topic **7.5 Absorption** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 and 2 are Core; statements 3 to 5 are **Extended only**. Full teaching: [absorption study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-absorption/). ## Core in three lines

1. **Absorption** = the movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood.
2. Nutrients are absorbed in the **small intestine**.
3. **Most water** is absorbed from the small intestine; **some** is absorbed from the **colon**.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Absorption | movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood |
| Small intestine | duodenum + ileum; where digestion is completed and nutrients are absorbed |
| Colon | first part of the large intestine; absorbs some water |
| Villus (Extended only) | finger-like projection of the small intestine wall |
| Microvilli (Extended only) | tiny folds of the cell membrane on the surface of each epithelial cell of a villus |
| Epithelium (Extended only) | single layer of cells covering the villus |
| Capillaries (Extended only) | blood vessels in the villus; absorb glucose and amino acids |
| Lacteal (Extended only) | lymph vessel in the centre of the villus; absorbs fatty acids and glycerol |

## Water sources and where it goes

- Water comes from drinks, food and digestive juices (saliva, gastric juice, pancreatic juice, bile).
- Small intestine: most absorbed.
- Colon: some absorbed, so faeces become semi-solid.
- Fast passage through the colon → less water absorbed → watery faeces.

## Villi and microvilli (Extended only)

```
wall of small intestine → covered in VILLI → each villus covered in epithelial cells → each cell has MICROVILLI
                     each step increases the internal surface area
```

Significance: much larger internal surface area → more molecules absorbed at once → faster, more complete absorption.

## Villus structure (Extended only)

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| finger-like projection | more surface in contact with digested food |
| epithelium one cell thick | short distance to the blood |
| microvilli on epithelial cells | even more surface area |
| capillary network under the epithelium | absorbs sugars and amino acids; flowing blood keeps a concentration gradient |
| central lacteal | absorbs fatty acids and glycerol |

Draw it as: a finger shape, a single outer layer of cells, a mesh of capillaries inside, one lacteal running up the middle.

## Who absorbs what (Extended only)

| Product of digestion | Absorbed into |
|---|---|
| Glucose | blood capillaries |
| Amino acids | blood capillaries |
| Fatty acids and glycerol | lacteal |
| Water and mineral ions | blood capillaries |

From the capillaries, blood goes to the liver in the hepatic portal vein. Lymph from the lacteals later drains into the blood.

## From food to blood: the full chain

| Nutrient eaten | Digested to | Absorbed in | Into (Extended only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starch | simple reducing sugars, then glucose | small intestine | blood capillaries |
| Protein | amino acids | small intestine | blood capillaries |
| Fats and oils | fatty acids and glycerol | small intestine | lacteal |
| Mineral ions, vitamins | not digested (already small) | small intestine | blood capillaries |
| Fibre | not digested | not absorbed | egested in faeces |

Fibre is the useful contrast: it is never absorbed, which is why it adds bulk all the way to the rectum.

## Method in steps: drawing a villus (Extended only)

1. Draw a tall finger shape rising from a baseline (the intestine wall).
2. Draw a single row of cells around its edge and label it "epithelium, one cell thick".
3. Add a fringe on the outer edge of those cells and label it "microvilli".
4. Draw a branching mesh just inside the epithelium and label it "blood capillaries".
5. Draw one tube up the centre and label it "lacteal".
6. Use ruled label lines that touch the structure and do not cross each other.

## Answer patterns

- **"Where are nutrients absorbed?"** Small intestine (not "intestine").
- **"Explain the significance of villi"** (Extended): surface area → rate.
- **"Describe a villus"** (Extended): shape, epithelium with microvilli, capillaries, lacteal, each with position.
- **"Suggest the effect of damaged villi"** (Extended): smaller surface area → less absorbed → effect on the body.

## Exam traps

- Nutrients are not absorbed in the stomach or the colon.
- The colon absorbs **some** water, not most.
- (Extended) Villi ≠ microvilli: villi are projections of the gut wall; microvilli are on cells.
- (Extended) Lacteals take fat products; capillaries take sugars and amino acids. Do not swap.
- (Extended) "Thin wall" needs "one cell thick" or "short distance".
- (Extended) The lacteal is a lymph vessel, not a blood vessel.

## Self-test

1. Define absorption.
2. Name the region where nutrients are absorbed.
3. Where is most water absorbed?
4. Name one other region where some water is absorbed.
5. Give **two** sources of the water that enters the gut, apart from drinks.
6. Why are faeces semi-solid rather than liquid?
7. (Extended) What is a villus?
8. (Extended) Where are microvilli found?
9. (Extended) Explain the significance of villi and microvilli.
10. (Extended) Name the vessel in a villus that absorbs fatty acids and glycerol.
11. (Extended) State **two** products of digestion absorbed by the blood capillaries.
12. (Extended) Explain how blood flowing through the capillaries helps absorption.

### Answers

1. The movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood.
2. The small intestine.
3. The small intestine.
4. The colon.
5. Any two: food; saliva; gastric juice; pancreatic juice; bile.
6. Some water is absorbed from the remains as they pass along the colon.
7. A finger-like projection of the wall of the small intestine.
8. On the surface of the epithelial cells covering each villus.
9. They greatly increase the internal surface area, so more molecules are absorbed at once and absorption is faster.
10. The lacteal.
11. Glucose and amino acids.
12. It carries absorbed molecules away, keeping their concentration in the blood low, so a concentration gradient is maintained.
