## Section A

**1.** How many metres are there in 3.2 km? **[1]**

**2.** On a map with a scale of 1:25 000, how many metres on the ground does 1 cm represent? **[1]**

**3.** Convert 3.5 km² to hectares. **[1]**

**4.** Simplify the ratio 600 : 150. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by the reliability of data. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A map has a scale of 1:50 000.

**(a)** A road measures 7.2 cm on the map. Calculate its real length in kilometres. **[2]**

**(b)** A footpath is 4.5 km long on the ground. Calculate its length on a 1:25 000 map, in centimetres. **[2]**

**7.** A student uses a 1:50 000 map on which each grid square represents 1 km². An urban area covers 7 whole squares and 8 part-squares.

**(a)** Estimate the area of the urban area, in km². **[2]**

**(b)** The urban area has a population of 6 600. Calculate the population density, in people per km². **[1]**

**8.** A student wants a sample of 32 from a list of 800 households.

**(a)** Calculate the interval for a systematic sample. **[1]**

**(b)** The 800 households are in three zones with 500, 200 and 100 households. A stratified sample of 40 is needed. Calculate the number to sample in each zone. **[3]**

**(c)** Give **one** advantage of a stratified sample over a random sample. **[1]**

**9.** A magnitude 8 earthquake is compared with a magnitude 6 earthquake. Calculate how many times greater the ground motion of the larger earthquake is, assuming each whole number is 10 times greater. **[2]**

**10.** The population of a region is 4.5 million urban and 1.5 million rural. Calculate the ratio of urban to rural people, and the proportion of the population that is urban, as a percentage. **[3]**

**11.** A student times a float travelling 10 m along a river. The times in three trials are 12 s, 14 s and 13 s. The river is 5.0 m wide and the mean depth is 0.4 m.

**(a)** Calculate the mean time. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the velocity, in m/s, to two decimal places. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the cross-sectional area and the discharge, giving the discharge to one decimal place. **[3]**

**12.** Design a data collection sheet for recording pebble sizes at sites along a beach. Describe **four** features of a well-designed sheet. **[4]**

**13.** Pebble size at four sites along a beach was recorded as a mean of 20 pebbles at each site: 62 mm, 48 mm, 35 mm and 21 mm, from the west end to the east end.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in pebble size from the first site to the last. **[2]**

**(b)** The hypothesis was "pebble size decreases along the beach from west to east". Write a conclusion supported by the data. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** ways in which the reliability of the investigation could be improved. **[2]**

**14.** A student collected 10 questionnaires from people in one street to find out how people travel to work in a town of 80 000 people. Evaluate the sampling method. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 3.2 × 1 000 = 3 200 m [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 250 m [1]. **[1]**

**3.** 3.5 × 100 = 350 ha [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 4 : 1 (divide both by 150) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Whether repeating the method gives the same or similar results [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 7.2 × 0.5 km [1] = 3.6 km [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 4.5 km at 1:25 000 means 1 km = 4 cm, so 4.5 × 4 [1] = 18 cm [1]. (Accept 4.5 ÷ 0.25 = 18.) **[2]**

**7. (a)** 7 + (8 × 0.5) [1] = 11 km² [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 6 600 ÷ 11 = 600 people per km² [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** 800 ÷ 32 = every 25th household [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 500 ÷ 800 × 40 = 25 [1]; 200 ÷ 800 × 40 = 10 [1]; 100 ÷ 800 × 40 = 5 [1]. (Check: 25 + 10 + 5 = 40.) **[3]**

**(c)** The sample reflects the proportions of the groups, so all zones are fairly represented [1]. **[1]**

**9.** The difference is 2 whole numbers [1]. 10 × 10 = 100 times greater [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Ratio = 4.5 : 1.5 = 3 : 1 [1]. Proportion = 4.5 ÷ (4.5 + 1.5) = 0.75 [1] = 75% [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** (12 + 14 + 13) ÷ 3 = 13 s [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Velocity = 10 ÷ 13 [1] = 0.77 m/s [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Area = 5.0 × 0.4 = 2.0 m² [1]. Discharge = 2.0 × 0.77 [1] = 1.5 m³/s (1.54 before rounding) [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Any four features, 1 mark each: a clear title and space for the date, time, weather and location [1]; a row for each site or each pebble, with columns for each variable [1]; units shown in the column headings (mm) [1]; space for repeat measurements and a mean [1]; space for notes on anomalies or conditions [1]; a column for site number or distance along the beach [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**13. (a)** (62 - 21) ÷ 62 × 100 [1] = 66% (66.1%) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Pebble size decreases at every site, from 62 mm in the west to 21 mm in the east [1]. This is a decrease of 66%, so the data support the hypothesis [1]. Possible explanation, such as longshore drift sorting pebbles by size, or attrition making pebbles smaller as they travel [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Any two from: measure more pebbles at each site; add more sites; repeat on different days; use random sampling to choose the pebbles; use the same method and equipment every time [1 each]. **[2]**

**14.** Indicative content:
- The sample of 10 is far too small for a town of 80 000 and is unlikely to be representative [1].
- Using one street may be biased: it may be near a bus route, or have a particular type of household, so the results do not represent the whole town [1].
- No stratification: it ignores different parts of the town, such as the centre and suburbs, and different age groups [1].
- Improvements: a much larger sample, for example 100 or more [1]; random or systematic sampling across several areas [1]; stratified sampling in proportion to the population of each district [1].
- Judgement: the method is weak, so the results could not be used to draw a reliable conclusion about the town, although it might give some ideas for a pilot survey [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the scale in the wrong direction.
- Mixing units, such as km with m.
- Counting part-squares as whole squares.
- Not showing working, so a wrong final answer earns nothing.
- Writing a ratio in the wrong order.
- Writing a conclusion with no figures.
