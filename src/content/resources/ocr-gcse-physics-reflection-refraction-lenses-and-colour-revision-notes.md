## P5.3a: four outcomes at a boundary (Higher Tier only)

| Outcome | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Absorbed | Energy passes to the material | Water absorbs microwaves; skin absorbs infrared |
| Transmitted | Wave passes through | Window glass transmits visible light |
| Reflected | Wave bounces off | Metal reflects radio waves and microwaves |
| Refracted | Direction changes as speed changes | Light entering a glass block |

What happens depends on the **substance** and the **wavelength**. Always name both in an answer.

## P5.3b: velocity and refraction (Higher Tier only)

Key facts:

- Speed in a vacuum is about 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. It is lower in glass, perspex and water.
- Crossing a boundary: **frequency constant**, speed changes, wavelength changes.
- Equation to use: v = f λ, with v in m/s, f in Hz and λ in m.
- Entering a medium where the wave is slower, the ray turns **towards** the normal; entering one where it is faster, it turns **away from** it.
- Along the normal: no change of direction.

Explaining the bend in steps:

1. One end of the wavefront enters the new substance first.
2. That end changes speed while the other end is still moving at the old speed.
3. The wavefront turns, so the direction changes.

Method for a wavelength problem:

1. Convert nm to m (× 10⁻⁹).
2. Write v = f λ and rearrange.
3. Keep the frequency fixed when the substance changes.
4. Give units and a sensible number of significant figures.

Worked reminder: infrared with frequency 3.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz has wavelength 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m in air (3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.0 × 10¹⁴). In water, with speed 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s, it is 7.5 × 10⁻⁷ m.

## P5.3d: ray diagram rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Reflection | The two angles are equal, each taken between the ray and the normal |
| Into a slower medium | Ray bends towards the normal |
| Into a faster medium | Ray bends away from the normal |
| Through a parallel-sided block | Emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray, shifted sideways |
| Along the normal | No bend |

Checklist for any ray diagram: straight lines with a ruler, arrows on rays, dashed normal at every boundary, angles labelled, nothing measured from the surface.

Equations are not needed for this statement.

## P5.3c: lenses

| Feature | Convex | Concave |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Thicker in the middle | Thinner in the middle |
| Effect on parallel rays | Converge to the principal focus | Diverge |
| Real image possible? | Yes | No |
| Typical image | Depends on object distance | Virtual, upright, smaller |
| Vision use | Long sight | Short sight |

Three construction rays for a convex lens:

1. Parallel to the axis, then through the principal focus.
2. Through the centre of the lens, undeviated.
3. Through the principal focus, then parallel to the axis.

Convex lens images:

| Object distance | Image |
|---|---|
| Beyond 2F | Real, inverted, smaller |
| Between F and 2F | Real, inverted, larger |
| Inside F | Virtual, upright, larger |

Similarities between lenses: both refract at two surfaces, both have principal foci, and a centre ray goes straight on. Qualitative work only.

## P5.3e: colour

- Primary colours of light: red, green, blue.
- Secondary: red + green = yellow; green + blue = cyan; red + blue = magenta.
- A filter **transmits** its own colour and **absorbs** the others. It does not add colour.
- An opaque object shows the colour it **reflects**. It absorbs the rest.
- White reflects all visible colours; black absorbs nearly all.
- Specular reflection: smooth surface, parallel rays stay parallel, you see an image.
- Scattering: rough surface, rays leave in many directions, so you can see the surface from any angle.

Method for a colour question:

1. List the colours in the incoming light.
2. For a filter, list what it transmits.
3. For an object, list what it reflects.
4. Only colours present in the light and also reflected or transmitted reach the eye.
5. If none are left, the answer is black (no light).

## Worked reminders

**Wavelength change (Higher Tier only).** Green light of frequency 5.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz is in air (3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) and then in water (2.25 × 10⁸ m/s).

```
Air:    λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.5 × 10¹⁴ = 5.45 × 10⁻⁷ m  (545 nm)
Water:  λ = 2.25 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.5 × 10¹⁴ = 4.09 × 10⁻⁷ m  (409 nm)
```

The frequency is the same in both. The wavelength is shorter in water because the speed is lower.

**Reading a speed change (Higher Tier only).** If light's speed falls from 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s to 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s, it has lost one third of its speed. Its wavelength falls by the same fraction.

**Colour chain.** White light passes through a cyan filter, then a yellow filter.

1. Cyan transmits green and blue, and absorbs red.
2. Yellow transmits red and green, and absorbs blue.
3. Only green is transmitted by both. The light that emerges is green.

**Colour of an object.** A green leaf in white light reflects green and absorbs red and blue. In red light it has nothing to reflect, so it looks black.

**Choosing the lens.** Rays from a distant object come to a focus in front of the retina. The eye is too strong for its length. Fit a concave lens so the rays diverge slightly before they enter the eye.

## Practical links

The specification suggests two lens investigations and a filter investigation. Know what each one shows:

- Lenses of both types and rays of light: concave lenses spread the rays and convex lenses bring them together.
- A convex lens and a bulb: move the bulb nearer to or further from the lens and record where a sharp image forms on a screen. The image size and position change, and inside the focal length no image forms on the screen.
- Coloured filters with light sources: record which colours pass through each filter, alone and in pairs. Filters take colours away. They do not add any.

For a method or evaluation question, say what you change, what you measure, and one control, such as keeping the room dark so stray light does not blur the image.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Normal** (90° to the surface) versus the surface itself.
- **Frequency** (fixed by the source) versus **wavelength** (depends on the substance).
- **Convex** (converges) versus **concave** (diverges).
- **Real image** (rays meet, can be on a screen) versus **virtual image** (rays only appear to come from it).
- **Specular** reflection versus **scattering**.
- **Filter** (removes colours) versus **source** (supplies colours).

## Quick self-test

1. (Higher Tier only) Name the four things that can happen to an electromagnetic wave at a boundary.
2. (Higher Tier only) Which quantity stays the same when light enters glass from air?
3. A ray goes from air into water. Does it bend towards or away from the normal?
4. (Higher Tier only) A wave has frequency 9.0 × 10⁷ Hz. Find its wavelength in air, using 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s.
5. (Higher Tier only) Light of frequency 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz has wavelength 380 nm in a plastic. Find its speed there.
6. A ray hits a plane mirror at 40° to the normal. What is the angle between the reflected ray and the mirror?
7. Which lens can form a real image on a screen?
8. Describe the image of an object placed inside the focal length of a convex lens.
9. What colour does a yellow filter transmit from white light, in terms of primary colours?
10. A blue shirt is lit by pure red light. What colour does it look, and why?
11. Why can you see a sheet of paper from every angle but only see your face in a mirror from some positions?

### Answers

1. Absorbed, transmitted, reflected, refracted.
2. Frequency.
3. Towards the normal, because the light slows down.
4. λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.0 × 10⁷ = **3.3 m** (3.33 m before rounding).
5. v = 5.0 × 10¹⁴ × 380 × 10⁻⁹ = **1.9 × 10⁸ m/s**.
6. The reflected ray is at 40° to the normal, so 90° − 40° = **50°** to the mirror.
7. A convex lens.
8. Virtual, upright and larger. The lens acts as a magnifying glass.
9. Red and green, which combine to look yellow. It absorbs blue.
10. Black or very dark. The shirt reflects blue but absorbs red, and there is no blue in the light.
11. Paper scatters light in many directions. A mirror gives specular reflection, so parallel rays stay parallel and only reach certain positions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking an angle from the surface line when it should be taken from the normal.
- Saying "frequency changes" in refraction.
- Describing refraction without saying the wave speed changes.
- Giving the wavelength in nm into an equation that needs m.
- Writing that a filter "gives" colour rather than absorbing the others.
- Drawing a ray diagram freehand, with unclear angles or no arrows.
- Calling any image "real" without checking whether the rays actually meet.
- Writing "the glass blocks light" instead of naming the wave type and the outcome.
