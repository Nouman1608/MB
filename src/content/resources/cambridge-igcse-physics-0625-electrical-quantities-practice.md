## Section A

**1.** State the unit of:

**(a)** potential difference **[1]**
**(b)** resistance. **[1]**

**2.** An acetate rod becomes positively charged when rubbed with a cloth. Explain how it becomes charged. **[2]**

**3.** Name the meter used to measure current, and state how it is connected in a circuit. **[2]**

**4.** State the difference between direct current and alternating current. **[1]**

**5.** A resistor has a p.d. of 4.5 V across it and a current of 0.15 A through it. Calculate its resistance. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student tests whether materials are electrical conductors or insulators.

**(a)** Describe an experiment she could do. **[3]**
**(b)** Copper is a conductor and rubber is an insulator. Explain this difference using a simple electron model. **[2]**

**7.** A student finds the resistance of a length of wire. She records these readings.

| p.d. / V | 1.2 | 2.4 | 3.6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current / A | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 |

**(a)** State how the ammeter and the voltmeter are connected in her circuit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance of the wire. **[2]**
**(c)** She repeats the experiment with a longer piece of the same wire. State how the resistance changes. **[1]**

**8.** An 800 W microwave oven is connected to a 230 V supply and used for 6.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the oven. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred, in joules. **[2]**
**(c)** Electricity costs 25 cents per kW h. Calculate the cost of this use. **[2]**

**9.** A cell is connected to a lamp, and the lamp lights.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfers that take place. **[2]**
**(b)** Define the e.m.f. of the cell. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A phone is charged with a current of 0.80 A for 2.0 hours. The p.d. across the phone is 5.0 V.

**(a)** Define electric current. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred to the phone. **[2]**
**(d)** State the direction of flow of the free electrons compared with the direction of conventional current. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* This question is about electric fields.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by an electric field. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the electric field around an isolated positive point charge, including its direction. **[2]**
**(c)** A small negative charge is placed between two parallel plates; the upper plate is positive and the lower plate negative. State the direction of the force on the charge. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A piece of resistance wire 0.80 m long has a resistance of 2.0 Ω. Calculate the resistance of a piece of the same metal that is:

**(a)** 2.0 m long with the same cross-sectional area **[1]**
**(b)** 0.80 m long with half the cross-sectional area **[1]**
**(c)** 2.0 m long with twice the cross-sectional area. **[2]**

**13.** *(Extended)* This question is about current–voltage graphs.

**(a)** Describe the shape of the I–V graph for a filament lamp, and explain it. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the I–V graph for a diode. **[2]**
**(c)** A lamp takes 0.40 A at 2.0 V and 0.75 A at 6.0 V. Calculate its resistance at each p.d. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Volt** (V) [1].
**(b)** **Ohm** (Ω) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Electrons are transferred [1] from the rod to the cloth, so the rod has fewer electrons than protons [1]. **[2]**

*Common mistake:* "positive charges move onto the rod". In charging by friction only electrons move.

**3.** An **ammeter** [1], connected **in series** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Direct current flows in one direction only; alternating current repeatedly reverses direction [1]. **[1]**

**5.** R = V / I = 4.5 ÷ 0.15 [1] = **30 Ω** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Connect a cell, a lamp (or an ammeter) and two crocodile clips in series [1]. Clip the sample between the crocodile clips [1]. If the lamp lights (or the ammeter shows a current) the sample is a conductor; if not, it is an insulator [1].
**(b)** Copper has free electrons that can move through the metal and carry charge [1]; in rubber the electrons are held in their atoms and cannot move through it [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** Ammeter **in series** with the wire [1]; voltmeter **in parallel** across the wire [1].
**(b)** R = 1.2 ÷ 0.10 = 12 Ω (each pair gives the same value) [1]; resistance = **12 Ω** [1].
**(c)** It **increases** [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** I = P / V = 800 ÷ 230 [1] = **3.5 A** (3.48 A) [1].
**(b)** E = Pt (or IVt) = 800 × (6.0 × 60) [1] = **288 000 J** (2.9 × 10⁵ J) [1].
**(c)** energy = 0.80 kW × 0.10 h = 0.080 kW h [1]; cost = 0.080 × 25 = **2.0 cents** [1]. **[6]**

*Common mistake:* multiplying 0.80 kW by 6.0 minutes, giving 4.8. The time must be in hours.

**9. (a)** Energy is transferred from the cell (chemical store) to the lamp by the current [1]; the lamp transfers it to the surroundings by light and by heating [1].
**(b)** The electrical work done by the cell in moving a unit charge around the complete circuit [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The charge passing a point per unit time [1].
**(b)** Q = It = 0.80 × (2.0 × 3600) [1] = **5760 C** (5.8 × 10³ C) [1].
**(c)** W = QV = 5760 × 5.0 [1] = **28 800 J** (2.9 × 10⁴ J) [1].
**(d)** Electrons flow from negative to positive, the **opposite** way to conventional current [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** A region in which an electric charge experiences a force [1].
**(b)** Straight lines radiating outwards from the charge [1], with arrows pointing away from the charge [1].
**(c)** **Upwards**, towards the positive plate (opposite to the field direction) [1]. **[4]**

*Common mistake:* in (c), giving the field direction (downwards). The field direction is the force on a positive charge; a negative charge is pushed the opposite way.

**12. (a)** 2.0 × (2.0 ÷ 0.80) = **5.0 Ω** [1].
**(b)** Half the area doubles R: **4.0 Ω** [1].
**(c)** Length factor 2.5 gives 5.0 Ω [1]; twice the area halves it: **2.5 Ω** [1]. **[4]**

**13. (a)** A curve through the origin that becomes less steep as the p.d. increases (same shape for negative p.d.) [1]. As the current increases, the filament gets hotter [1], so its resistance increases [1].
**(b)** Almost no current for reverse p.d. [1]; in the forward direction, current starts at a small p.d. and then rises steeply [1].
**(c)** At 2.0 V: R = 2.0 ÷ 0.40 = **5.0 Ω**; at 6.0 V: R = 6.0 ÷ 0.75 = **8.0 Ω** [1 each]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying positive charges move when a rod is rubbed.
- Leaving time in minutes or hours in E = IVt, or in seconds for kW h.
- Treating cross-sectional area as directly proportional to resistance.
- Explaining the lamp curve without both "hotter" and "resistance increases".
- Giving the field direction as the force on a negative charge.
