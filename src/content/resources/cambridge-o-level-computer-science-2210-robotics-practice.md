## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by robotics. **[2]**

**2.** Give **two** examples of robots. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by a robot being programmable. **[1]**

**4.** Name the part of a robot that produces movement. **[1]**

**5.** Give **one** role of a robot in agriculture. **[1]**

**6.** Give **one** role of a robot in entertainment. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Describe **three** characteristics of a robot. **[3]**

**8.** A robot vacuum cleaner cleans a house on its own.

**(a)** Identify **one** sensor the robot would need and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the part of the robot that carries out the instructions in its program. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the robot vacuum cleaner shows that it has a mechanical structure. **[1]**

**9.** A student says: "My computer is a robot because it has a program, sensors and a processor." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** A hospital uses a robotic arm to help a surgeon during an operation.

**(a)** Describe **one** advantage of using the robotic arm. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** disadvantage for the hospital. **[2]**

**11.** The pseudocode below controls a robot arm in a factory. The robot picks up boxes from a conveyor and puts them on a pallet.

```
FOR Box ← 1 TO 10
   CALL MoveArm("Conveyor")
   CALL GripOn
   CALL MoveArm("Pallet")
   CALL GripOff
NEXT Box
```

**(a)** State how many boxes are moved to the pallet. **[1]**

**(b)** The factory now needs the robot to move 25 boxes. State the change that the programmer makes. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how this shows that a robot is programmable. **[2]**

**12.** A farm uses a drone fitted with a camera to check its fields.

**(a)** Describe **one** way in which the drone helps the farmer. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** problem the farmer might have with the drone. **[2]**

**13.** A car company uses robots for welding on its production line.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages of using robots instead of people for welding. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages of using the robots. **[4]**

**14.** Discuss whether a family should buy a domestic robot lawn mower. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A branch of computer science [1] that incorporates the design, construction and operation of robots [1].

**2.** Any two: factory equipment (such as a welding arm); a domestic robot (such as a robot vacuum cleaner or lawn mower); a drone [1 each].

**3.** Its instructions can be written or changed, so that the same robot can do different tasks / its actions are controlled by a program [1].

**4.** The actuator (for example a motor) [1].

**5.** Any one: picking fruit; planting seeds; spraying crops; milking cows; surveying fields [1].

**6.** Any one: animatronic figures; robot toys; filming drones; drones in light shows [1].

**7.** A mechanical structure or framework (a physical body) [1]; electrical components, such as sensors, microprocessors and actuators [1]; it is programmable [1].

**8. (a)** One mark for a sensor, for example an infrared / proximity / bump sensor [1], and one for a reason, for example to detect furniture and walls so that it does not crash into them [1].
**(b)** The microprocessor [1].
**(c)** It has a body, wheels and brushes / physical parts that hold the components and allow it to move [1].

**9.** A robot also needs a mechanical structure / framework that allows it to move or act [1]. A computer has no body that carries out physical tasks according to its program [1].

**10. (a)** The arm can make very precise, steady movements [1], which can reduce the risk of mistakes caused by tiredness or a shaking hand [1]. (Accept: the surgeon can work through smaller cuts, so patients recover faster.)
**(b)** The robot is very expensive to buy and maintain [1], and staff need training to use it [1].

**11. (a)** 10 [1].
**(b)** Change the 10 to 25 in the FOR loop [1].
**(c)** The same robot can do a different job when its program is changed [1], without any change to the robot's mechanical structure [1].

**12. (a)** The drone can fly over the whole field quickly, so the farmer can see crop health or damage without walking across the land [1], which saves time and labour [1].
**(b)** Any one with a development: the drone may be expensive to buy and maintain [1], so a small farm may not afford it [1]; it relies on battery power, so it can only fly for a short time [1], so a large farm may need several flights [1]; bad weather could stop it flying [1], so the check would be delayed [1].

**13. (a)** Any two with development: robots work for 24 hours a day without rest [1], so more cars are produced [1]; the welds are the same every time [1], so quality is consistent [1]; they can work in conditions with heat, sparks and fumes [1], which keeps workers safe [1]. Maximum 4.
**(b)** Any two with development: the cost of buying and installing robots is high [1], so it takes a long time before the company saves money [1]; workers may lose their jobs [1], so the company may have to retrain or lay off staff [1]; when a robot breaks down, the production line may stop [1], so production is lost until it is repaired [1]. Maximum 4.

**14.** One mark for each of up to four points, such as: the mower saves the family time on a regular chore [1]; it can work while the family is out [1]; it is expensive to buy [1]; it can only do what it is programmed to do, so it may miss areas or get stuck on obstacles [1]; it may be a safety risk to children or pets if it fails to detect them [1]. A balanced answer needs at least one advantage and one disadvantage.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: only saying "the study of robots" without design, construction and operation.
- Question 4: answering "the motor" is fine, but "the engine" or "the processor" is not.
- Question 7: giving "sensors, microprocessor, actuators" as three separate characteristics. They are one characteristic (electrical components).
- Question 8: naming a sensor without saying what it detects.
- Question 9: saying only "a computer is not a robot" with no reason.
- Question 10 and Question 13: making the point but not developing it.
- Question 11 (c): saying "you can change the program" without saying that the same robot does a new job.
- Question 14: writing advantages only.

## Checking a robot definition

Test any machine with three questions. Does it have a physical body? Does it have electrical parts such as sensors and actuators? Can its program be changed? If any answer is no, it does not meet the syllabus characteristics of a robot.
