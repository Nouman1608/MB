Cambridge O Level Physics (5054) is a single-route physics course that
takes students from measurement and motion to nuclear and space
physics. Unlike some physics qualifications, it has no tiers: every
candidate studies the same content and sits the same written papers.
Assessment consists of a 60-minute multiple-choice paper (30%), a
105-minute theory paper (50%) and a practical component (20%) taken
either as a practical test or as an alternative to practical. This
overview follows the 2026-2028 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

The content falls into six areas.

**Motion, forces and energy** is the foundation. It covers physical
quantities and measurement techniques, motion, mass and weight,
density, forces, momentum, energy, work and power, and pressure.

**Thermal physics** deals with the kinetic particle model of matter,
thermal properties and temperature, and the transfer of thermal energy.

**Waves** brings together the general properties of waves, light, the
electromagnetic spectrum and sound.

**Electricity and magnetism** is the most varied area, covering simple
magnetism and magnetic fields, electrical quantities, electric
circuits, practical electricity, electromagnetic effects and the uses
of an oscilloscope.

**Nuclear physics** covers the nuclear model of the atom and
radioactivity.

**Space physics** covers Earth and the Solar System, and stars and the
Universe.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Theory | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 3 | Practical Test | 90 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Practical | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

Everyone takes Papers 1 and 2. For the practical component, candidates
take **either** Paper 3, a hands-on practical test, **or** Paper 4, a
written alternative to practical. Both carry 40 marks and 20%, and your
school will tell you which one you are entered for. All papers are
externally assessed, and the syllabus is examined in the June and
November series.

## How the parts fit together

Papers 1 and 2 are not divided by topic: either can include questions
from any of the six areas. The course therefore works best when the
areas are understood as connected, rather than revised in isolation.

Useful connections to notice include:

- **Energy** links motion and forces with thermal physics, electricity
  and nuclear physics.
- **Forces and fields** connect the forces area with magnetism and
  electromagnetic effects.
- **Waves** link light and sound with the electromagnetic spectrum,
  which reappears in radioactivity and astronomy.
- **The oscilloscope**, a distinctive part of the 5054 electricity and
  magnetism area, connects electrical signals with the wave ideas of
  frequency and amplitude.

Practical ability matters across the whole qualification, not only in
the 20% practical component. Theory questions often describe
experiments, ask you to interpret data or graphs, or require you to
explain how a measurement could be made more accurately. Regular class
practicals, with careful recording of results, are the best
preparation for both the practical paper and the experimental side of
the theory paper.

The multiple-choice paper rewards breadth and precision. With 40 marks
across the whole syllabus, it tests whether you know definitions, units
and relationships securely. The theory paper, with 80 marks over 105
minutes, gives space for calculations, explanations and longer answers.

## Planning your study

A clear path through the content follows the syllabus order:

1. Start with measurement techniques, then work through motion, mass and
   weight, density, forces, momentum, energy, work and power, and
   pressure.
2. Move on to thermal physics, which uses the energy ideas just learned.
3. Study waves, including light and sound.
4. Take electricity and magnetism next, leaving time for the
   oscilloscope and practical electricity.
5. Finish with nuclear physics and space physics.

Alongside the content, keep a running sheet of the quantities you meet,
with their symbols, SI units and the equations that link them. The
same relationships turn up in every area, and having them in one place
makes it far easier to spot connections, check the units in a
calculation and revise efficiently later. Add a short note on how each
quantity is measured in the laboratory, since that supports the
practical component as well as the theory paper.

Practise multiple-choice questions little and often from early in the
course; they are a quick way to check recall. Begin theory questions on
each area once it is taught, and work through past-paper questions on
completed areas from the middle of the course onwards. Full exam
practice for each paper is covered in the separate exam preparation
guide and belongs to the final run-up to the series you are entered
for.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Cambridge O Level Physics 5054 tiered?**
No. There is a single route, so all candidates sit the same papers.

**Which papers do I sit for O Level Physics?**
Paper 1 Multiple Choice and Paper 2 Theory, plus either Paper 3 Practical Test or Paper 4 Alternative to Practical.

**How long is the theory paper?**
Paper 2 Theory lasts 105 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50% of the qualification.

**What is the difference between Paper 3 and Paper 4?**
Paper 3 is a 90-minute practical test using apparatus; Paper 4 is a 60-minute written Alternative to Practical. Both are worth 40 marks and 20%, and you take only one.

**What is the oscilloscope topic?**
The uses of an oscilloscope form part of the electricity and magnetism area of the 5054 syllabus.
