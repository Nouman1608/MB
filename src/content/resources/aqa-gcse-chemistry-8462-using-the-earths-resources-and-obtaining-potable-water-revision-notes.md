These notes condense **4.10.1 Using the Earth's resources and obtaining potable water** of the AQA GCSE
Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 2 content. Higher tier only: phytomining
and bioleaching (4.10.1.4). For explanations use the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-using-the-earths-resources-and-obtaining-potable-water/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-using-the-earths-resources-and-obtaining-potable-water-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Finite** resources: will run out (crude oil, coal, metal ores). **Renewable**: replaced as fast as used.
- **Sustainable development**: meets the needs of **current generations** without compromising the ability of
  **future generations** to meet their own needs.
- Natural products can be supplemented or replaced by **agricultural** and **synthetic** products.
- **Potable water**: safe to drink; low dissolved salts and microbes. **Not pure water**.

## Resources compared

| | Finite | Renewable |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Crude oil, coal, metal ores | Timber (if replanted), crops, wind |
| Replacement | Millions of years or never | Quickly enough to keep up with use |
| Use | Processed for energy and materials | Food, timber, clothing, fuels |

## Producing potable water in the UK

| Step | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Choose a **fresh water** source | Low in dissolved salts |
| **Filter beds** | Remove insoluble solids |
| **Sterilise** (chlorine, ozone, UV) | Kill microbes |

## Salty water

- **Desalination** is needed when fresh water is limited.
- Methods: **distillation** and **reverse osmosis** (membrane).
- Both need **large amounts of energy**.

## Required practical 8

| Test | Method |
|---|---|
| pH | Universal indicator or pH meter |
| Dissolved solids | Evaporate a measured volume; weigh the residue |
| Purification | Distillation: heat, condense, collect |

Dissolved solids (g/dm³) = mass of residue ÷ volume in dm³.
Example: 0.0125 g from 25.0 cm³ → 0.0125 ÷ 0.0250 = **0.50 g/dm³**.

## Sewage treatment

1. **Screening** and **grit removal**.
2. **Sedimentation** → **sludge** and **effluent**.
3. **Anaerobic digestion** of sludge (no oxygen; biogas).
4. **Aerobic biological treatment** of effluent (air bubbled in).

Industrial waste water may also need harmful chemicals removed.

## Ease of obtaining potable water

| Source | Ease |
|---|---|
| Ground water | Easiest |
| Waste water | Harder |
| Salt water | Hardest (energy needed) |

## Higher tier only: alternative metal extraction

| Method | Process |
|---|---|
| **Phytomining** | Plants absorb metal compounds; harvest and **burn**; metal compounds in the **ash** |
| **Bioleaching** | **Bacteria** produce a **leachate** solution containing metal compounds |

Copper is then obtained by **displacement with scrap iron** (Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu) or **electrolysis**.
These methods suit **low-grade ores** and avoid digging, moving and disposing of rock. Disadvantages: slow, and
need land (phytomining).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Finite resource | Cannot be replaced at the rate it is used |
| Renewable resource | Replaced as fast as it is used |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without harming future generations |
| Potable water | Water safe to drink |
| Desalination | Removing salt from salty water |
| Effluent | The liquid left after sludge settles |
| Sludge | Solids that settle in sewage |

## Exam traps

- **Potable = pure.** It is not; it contains dissolved substances.
- **Filtering kills microbes.** Sterilising does.
- **Anaerobic with air.** Anaerobic means without oxygen.
- **Desalination cheap.** It uses large amounts of energy.
- **Phytomining and bioleaching reversed.** Plants versus bacteria.
- **Order of sewage steps.** Screening, sedimentation, anaerobic digestion (sludge), aerobic treatment (effluent).
- **Units.** Convert cm³ to dm³ for g/dm³.

## Quick self-test

1. Give one finite and one renewable resource.
2. Define sustainable development.
3. Why is potable water not pure water?
4. State the three main steps in treating fresh water in the UK.
5. Name three sterilising agents.
6. Name two ways of desalinating sea water.
7. Why are these methods expensive?
8. Put these sewage stages in order: aerobic treatment, screening, sedimentation, anaerobic digestion.
9. A 50.0 cm³ sample leaves 0.025 g of residue. Calculate g/dm³.
10. Which is easier to make potable: ground water or sea water? Explain.
11. (Higher tier only) What is phytomining?
12. (Higher tier only) How can copper be obtained from a copper sulfate leachate?

### Answers

1. Finite: crude oil. Renewable: timber (replanted).
2. Development that meets the needs of current generations without compromising future generations.
3. It contains dissolved substances (such as salts).
4. Choose a fresh source, filter, sterilise.
5. Chlorine, ozone, ultraviolet light.
6. Distillation and reverse osmosis.
7. They need large amounts of energy.
8. Screening, sedimentation, anaerobic digestion (of sludge), aerobic treatment (of effluent).
9. 0.025 ÷ 0.0500 = 0.50 g/dm³.
10. Ground water; it has low levels of dissolved salts, so only filtering and sterilising are needed, while sea water needs desalination.
11. Using plants to absorb metal compounds, then burning the plants to give ash containing the metal compounds.
12. Displacement using scrap iron, or electrolysis.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain why potable water is not the same as pure water.**
"Potable water is safe to drink, but it contains dissolved substances such as salts. Pure water contains only H₂O."

**Give reasons for the steps used to produce potable water.**
"A fresh water source has low levels of dissolved salts. Filter beds remove insoluble solids. Sterilising with
chlorine, ozone or ultraviolet light kills microbes."

**Compare getting potable water from ground water and from sea water.**
"Ground water only needs filtering and sterilising. Sea water needs desalination by distillation or reverse
osmosis, which uses a lot of energy, so it is more difficult and expensive."

## Calculation pattern for dissolved solids

1. Convert the volume to dm³ (cm³ ÷ 1000).
2. Find the mass of residue (mass of dish with residue minus mass of empty dish).
3. Divide mass by volume.

Example: dish 24.512 g empty and 24.537 g with residue from 50.0 cm³. Residue = 0.025 g; volume = 0.0500 dm³;
0.025 ÷ 0.0500 = **0.50 g/dm³**.
