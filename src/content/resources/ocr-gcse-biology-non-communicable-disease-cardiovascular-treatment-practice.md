## Questions

**1.** Type 2 diabetes is described as a multifactorial disease. Explain what this means and give one example of a factor that can contribute. **[2]**

**2.** Region X has a population of 250 000 and recorded 190 new cases of type 2 diabetes in one year. Region Y has a population of 1.2 million and recorded 840 new cases.

**(a)** Calculate the number of new cases per 100 000 people in each region. **[2]**
**(b)** State which region has the higher rate. **[1]**

**3.** Compare the use of a stent with a coronary bypass operation to treat coronary heart disease. **[4]**

**4.** The table shows invented data on lung cancer deaths among men of the same age.

| Cigarettes smoked per day | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deaths per 100 000 men per year | 14 | 80 | 150 | 240 |

**(a)** Describe what happens to the death rate as smoking increases. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times higher the death rate is for men smoking 20 cigarettes per day than for non-smokers. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how substances in tobacco smoke can lead to lung cancer. **[2]**

**5.** Describe two differences between a benign tumour and a malignant tumour, and state what is meant by a secondary tumour. **[4]**

**6.** A tumour starts from one cell. Every cell divides by mitosis once every 16 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 18 divisions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many days 18 divisions take. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**7.** In a country, deaths from coronary heart disease fell from 310 per 100 000 people to 205 per 100 000 people over twenty years.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons for the change, one lifestyle-related and one medical. **[2]**

**8.** Two researchers want to find out whether regular exercise lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes. Researcher A asks 40 members of one gym about their health. Researcher B collects health and activity records from 1200 adults chosen at random from a national register. Evaluate the two plans. **[4]**

**9.** The table shows invented data from seven countries.

| Sugary drink intake (mL per person per day) | 20 | 45 | 60 | 85 | 110 | 140 | 170 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adults with type 2 diabetes (%) | 4.1 | 5.0 | 6.2 | 7.4 | 9.0 | 10.1 | 12.3 |

**(a)** Describe how the two variables are related. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why these data alone do not prove that sugary drinks cause type 2 diabetes. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what further evidence would make the conclusion stronger. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** State what happens to the liver in cirrhosis. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how long-term heavy alcohol use can lead to cirrhosis. **[2]**
**(c)** A country's alcohol use rises sharply in one year, but cirrhosis cases do not rise until many years later. Suggest an explanation. **[2]**

**11.** A trial gave 800 patients a statin and 800 patients a placebo for five years. The table shows the results.

| | Statin group | Placebo group |
|---|---|---|
| Mean blood LDL cholesterol at start (mmol/dm³) | 4.4 | 4.4 |
| Mean blood LDL cholesterol at end (mmol/dm³) | 2.6 | 4.3 |
| Patients with a heart attack | 31 | 52 |
| Patients reporting muscle pain | 36 | 12 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mean LDL cholesterol in the statin group. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of patients having a heart attack in each group. Give your answers to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate the use of statins for patients with coronary heart disease, using the data and your own knowledge. **[4]**

**12.** Town C has a population of 30 000 and 45 new cases of type 2 diabetes in a year. Town D has a population of 20 000 and 24 new cases. The whole country has a population of 5 million and 9500 new cases.

**(a)** Calculate the rate per 100 000 for Town C and for Town D. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two lifestyle-related reasons why Town C could have a higher rate than Town D. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the national rate per 100 000 and state how the two towns compare with it. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one reason why comparing disease rates between countries is useful. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** It is caused by an interaction of several factors, not a single cause [1]. Example: a diet high in sugar, being overweight, lack of exercise, or inherited gene variants (any one) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Many causes" alone is often too vague. Naming one specific factor shows you know what the causes are.

**2. (a)** Region X: 190 ÷ 250 000 × 100 000 = **76.0** [1]. Region Y: 840 ÷ 1 200 000 × 100 000 = **70.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Region X** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Region Y has more cases in total, which tempts a quick answer. Show the division for each region.

**3.** A stent is a mesh tube that holds the artery open from inside, with a smaller procedure and quicker recovery [1]. A clot can form on a stent or the artery can narrow again [1]. A bypass joins a vessel around the blockage and can deal with several blockages [1]. A bypass is major surgery with a longer recovery [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison answer usually needs both treatments in the same sentence or paired points.

**4. (a)** As the number of cigarettes per day increases, the death rate rises [1], from 14 to 240 per 100 000 (quoting data) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 150 ÷ 14 [1] = **10.7** times [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Chemicals in tobacco smoke damage the DNA of lung cells [1]. A mutation in genes controlling division can make cells divide uncontrollably, forming a tumour [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In "describe" questions, quoting figures from the table alongside the trend is good practice. In part (c), link smoke to DNA damage, then to uncontrolled division.

**5.** Benign tumours stay in one place and do not invade neighbouring tissue [1]; malignant tumours invade neighbouring tissue [1]. Malignant tumour cells can spread in the blood to other parts of the body but benign tumour cells do not [1]. A secondary tumour is a new tumour formed in another part of the body by cells that spread from the first tumour [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Make each difference a pair, one point for each tumour type. Saying only "malignant is worse" is too vague.

**6. (a)** Cells = 2¹⁸ [1] = **262 144** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 18 × 16 = 288 hours [1]; 288 ÷ 24 = **12.0 days** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is multiplying 20 by 2 instead of using a power of 2 in part (a). In part (b) the question asks for days, not hours.

**7. (a)** Change = 205 − 310 = −105 [1]. (−105 ÷ 310) × 100 = **−33.9%** (a fall of 33.9%) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Lifestyle: fewer people smoke, or healthier diets, or more exercise (any one) [1]. Medical: use of statins, stents or bypass surgery, or drugs to lower blood pressure (any one) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value, not the new one. Stating "fall" or a minus sign shows you have read the direction correctly.

**8.** Researcher B's sample is much larger [1] and chosen at random, so it is more representative of the population [1]. Researcher A's sample of 40 gym members is small and biased towards people who are already active, so it cannot show the effect of exercise on the whole population [1]. Researcher B's plan is better, though neither design controls for diet, age or inherited risk [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement. State which plan is better and why, then add one remaining limitation.

**9. (a)** Positive correlation: as sugary drink intake rises, the percentage with type 2 diabetes rises [1], from 4.1% at 20 mL to 12.3% at 170 mL (quoting data) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A correlation does not show cause [1]. Other factors, such as total diet, activity levels or wealth, may differ between the countries and explain the pattern [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** A biological mechanism for how excess sugar leads to the disease [1]. Studies that follow individuals over years and control other factors such as exercise and total energy intake [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name a specific alternative factor rather than writing "other variables".

**10. (a)** Healthy liver tissue is replaced by scar tissue [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Alcohol is broken down in the liver, so heavy drinking exposes liver cells to damage [1]. Over a long time the damaged cells are replaced by scar tissue [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The damage builds up over many years [1], so cirrhosis appears only after long-term heavy drinking [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mentioning the time delay is the key idea in part (c).

**11. (a)** Change = 2.6 − 4.4 = −1.8 [1]. (−1.8 ÷ 4.4) × 100 = **−40.9%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Placebo: 52 ÷ 800 × 100 = **6.5%** [1]. Statin: 31 ÷ 800 × 100 = **3.9%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Statin lowered cholesterol by 40.9% while placebo group cholesterol hardly changed [1]. Heart attacks fell from 6.5% to 3.9% of patients [1]. Muscle pain was more common with statins (36 compared with 12 patients) [1]. Judgement: benefits seem to outweigh the side effects for most patients, but the trial lasted five years and patients should be monitored, and lifestyle change is still needed [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from the table on both sides of the argument. A conclusion that depends on the patient reads as a stronger evaluation than a flat yes or no.

**12. (a)** Town C: 45 ÷ 30 000 × 100 000 = **150.0** [1]. Town D: 24 ÷ 20 000 × 100 000 = **120.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** More people eat a high-sugar or high-fat diet [1]. Less regular exercise (accept more people who are overweight) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** National: 9500 ÷ 5 000 000 × 100 000 = **190.0** per 100 000 [1]. Both towns have a lower rate than the national rate [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Countries with lower rates may show which lifestyle or health policies reduce disease [1], and rising rates in one country can warn others to act early [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert to a rate before comparing places of different sizes. In part (b), tie each factor to the disease for full effect.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing totals of cases instead of rates per 100 000.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new value rather than the original value.
- Writing "it causes" from a correlation with no further evidence.
- Evaluating a treatment with advantages only, or with no judgement at the end.
- Calling a statin or stent a cure, when they manage the condition.
- Mixing up benign and malignant tumours, or forgetting secondary tumours.
- Writing that cancer is infectious or always inherited.
