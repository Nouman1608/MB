## Questions

**1.** (HL only) Define market power, and distinguish a price taker from a price maker. **[3]**

**2.** (HL only, calculator-free) A made-up grain firm sells at a fixed market price of 11 per unit. Its total cost (TC) is: 0 units 12; 1 unit 22; 2 units 30; 3 units 37; 4 units 45; 5 units 55; 6 units 68.

**(a)** Calculate the marginal cost of the fifth unit. **[1]**
**(b)** Determine the profit-maximising output, giving the reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out the profit and classify it as abnormal, normal or a loss. **[2]**

**3.** (HL only, calculator-free) In a made-up bus market the seven firms have market shares of 34, 22, 14, 11, 8, 6 and 5 per cent.

**(a)** Find the CR3 and CR4 concentration ratios. **[2]**
**(b)** The third and fourth firms merge. Calculate the new three-firm ratio. **[1]**
**(c)** Comment on what the change suggests about the market. **[1]**

**4.** (HL only, calculator allowed) A made-up monopolist faces demand P = 84 - 4Q. Its total cost is TC = 150 + 12Q + 2Q², so MC = 12 + 4Q.

**(a)** Determine the profit-maximising output and the price the monopolist charges. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare loss compared with perfect competition, assuming MC would be the competitive supply curve. **[3]**

**5.** (HL only) Sketch a diagram of a natural monopoly and use it to explain why a regulator might set the price where P = AC rather than P = MC. **[4]**

**6.** (HL only) Two made-up ferry firms, Seaward and Tidemark, each choose to Hold or Cut their fare. Payoffs are profits (Seaward, Tidemark): both Hold 30, 30; Seaward Hold and Tidemark Cut 10, 50; Seaward Cut and Tidemark Hold 50, 10; both Cut 20, 20.

**(a)** Explain what each firm will choose if they cannot agree. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the incentive to cheat if they agree to Hold. **[2]**
**(c)** State the risk this matrix shows for firms that do not collude. **[1]**

**7.** (HL only) Explain, with reference to a diagram, why a monopolistically competitive firm earning abnormal profit in the short run earns only normal profit in the long run, and comment on its efficiency. **[6]**

**8.** (HL only) Explain two advantages of a market dominated by one very large firm. **[4]**

**9.** (HL only, essay) A made-up country, Veldmark, has a single firm supplying nearly all its electricity. Evaluate the use of government intervention to respond to abuse of its market power. **[10]**

**10.** (HL only, calculator allowed) A made-up firm in a perfectly competitive market has TC = 64 + Q², so MC = 2Q and AC = 64/Q + Q.

**(a)** Calculate the profit when the market price is 26. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what happens in the long run and calculate the long-run price. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that the firm is allocatively efficient in the long run. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the result if the price is instead 10. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain what then happens in the market. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Market power is a firm's ability to influence the price it charges [1]. A price taker has none, so it accepts the market price and faces a horizontal demand curve [1]. A price maker faces a downward-sloping demand curve and chooses a price and quantity along it [1].
*Examiner insight:* Define the term in your own words before contrasting; a contrast alone does not show you know what the term means.

**2. (a)** MC = 55 - 45 = **10** [1].
**(b)** Each unit up to the fifth costs no more than 11 (fifth: 10), but the sixth costs 68 - 55 = 13, above the price [1]. Output = **5 units** [1].
**(c)** TR = 11 × 5 = 55 and TC = 55, so profit = **0** [1]. AR = AC, so **normal profit** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each difference in TC; write the MC against MR comparison on both sides of the answer so the reasoning is visible.

**3. (a)** CR3 = 34 + 22 + 14 = **70** per cent [1]; CR4 = 70 + 11 = **81** per cent [1].
**(b)** Merged firm 25, so the largest three are 34, 25 and 22: CR3 = **81** per cent [1].
**(c)** Concentration rises, so fewer firms dominate; the market is more oligopolistic, though the ratio alone does not show behaviour [1].
*Examiner insight:* Re-rank after a merger before adding; adding the old top three is a common slip.

**4. (a)** MR = 84 - 8Q [1]. MC = MR: 12 + 4Q = 84 - 8Q gives Q = **6** [1]. P = 84 - 24 = **60** [1].
**(b)** TC = 150 + 72 + 72 = 294 [1]. TR = 360, profit = **66** [1].
**(c)** Perfect competition: P = MC, 84 - 4Q = 12 + 4Q, so Q = 9 [1]. At Q = 6, MC = 36 and P - MC = 60 - 36 = 24 [1]. Welfare loss = ½ × 3 × 24 = **36** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Calculate asks for working, so state MR before equating it to MC; the price comes from AR, not MR.

**5.** Diagram: downward AR with MR below it; AC falling across the whole range of demand; MC below AC [1]. Unregulated output is at MC = MR, with price on AR above AC [1]. At P = MC, the firm makes a loss because MC is below AC, so it would not stay [1]. P = AC gives normal profit and more output than the unregulated case, a workable compromise [1].
*Examiner insight:* Label MC = MR, P = MC and P = AC as three separate points; unlabelled intersections lose the explanation.

**6. (a)** If Tidemark holds, Seaward earns 50 by cutting against 30 by holding [1]. If Tidemark cuts, Seaward earns 20 against 10 [1]. So Seaward cuts either way, as does Tidemark: both **Cut**, earning 20 each [1].
**(b)** If both hold, each earns 30, but a firm that secretly cuts earns 50 [1], while its rival falls to 10 [1].
**(c)** A **price war**, leaving both on 20 rather than 30 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote payoffs from the matrix in the explanation; stating "they cut" without numbers is an assertion, not analysis.

**7.** Short run: at MC = MR the price (AR) is above AC, giving abnormal profit [1]. Entry is free, so profit attracts new firms [1]. Their close substitutes take sales: this firm's demand shifts left and becomes more elastic [1]. Entry continues until AR is tangent to AC [1]. At the MC = MR output, AR = AC, so normal profit [1]. P is above MC, so allocative inefficiency, but less than a monopoly, with greater product variety [1].
*Examiner insight:* The diagram should show the tangency point and the MC = MR quantity below it; describing the shift without drawing it leaves the key step implicit.

**8.** Economies of scale: spreading fixed cost over more output lowers average cost [1], which can be passed on in lower prices or kept as profit [1]. R&D: abnormal profit can fund research [1], leading to innovation and new or better products [1].
*Examiner insight:* Explain means give reasons, so each advantage needs a link from cause to effect, not just a name.

**9.** Plan (indicative, not an official scheme): legislation and regulation, such as a price cap or rules against abuse, explained [1] and linked to price towards AC and higher output [1]; limit, regulator has less cost information than the firm [1]. Government ownership explained [1], with strength, prices can follow social goals [1]; limit, weaker profit incentive, cost to taxpayers [1]. Fines explained [1]; strength, deterrence, limit, hard to prove and may be passed to consumers [1]. Judgement on which suits a natural monopoly, with justification [1] and a labelled diagram or applied reference to Veldmark [1].
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate invites a weighed judgement, so end with a reasoned conclusion about the case, not a restatement of the list.

**10. (a)** MC = P: 2Q = 26, Q = 13 [1]. TR = 338 and TC = 64 + 169 = 233 [1]. Profit = **105**, abnormal [1].
**(b)** Abnormal profit attracts entry, market supply increases and the price drops [1]. Entry stops at the lowest AC, where AC = MC: 64/Q + Q = 2Q gives Q = 8 [1]. Price = AC = 64/8 + 8 = **16** [1].
**(c)** At Q = 8, MC = 16 [1], equal to the price, so P = MC and the firm is allocatively efficient [1].
**(d)** Q = 5, TR = 50, TC = 64 + 25 = 89, so a loss of **39** [1], since AR is below AC [1].
**(e)** Firms leave, supply shifts left and price rises [1] until it reaches 16 and firms earn normal profit [1].
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-step calculation, carry your own earlier values forward and show each substitution so one slip does not hide the rest.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the price from MR or MC instead of AR.
- Calling any positive profit "normal".
- Drawing the welfare loss with MR as one boundary.
- Giving a payoff-matrix answer with no numbers from the matrix.
- Adding the old top firms after a merger.
- Leaving diagram curves or equilibrium points unlabelled.
- Listing government policies without judging their limits.
- Skipping the working in a Calculate question.
