**(a)** Explain why sulfur dioxide is classified as an acidic oxide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Acidic [1].

**(b)** Basic [1].

**(c)** Basic [1].

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** A non-metal [1].

**4.** An oxide that reacts with acids and with bases [1] to produce a salt and water [1].

**5.** Aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) [1]; zinc oxide (ZnO) [1].

**6. (a)** Sulfur is a non-metal [1]; sulfur dioxide reacts with bases/alkalis to form a salt and water / dissolves in water to give an acidic solution [1].

**(b)** SO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + H₂O: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** It dissolves in rainwater, forming an acidic solution [1].

**7. (a)** CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ [1].

**(b)** Blue / purple [1]; calcium hydroxide is an alkali (calcium oxide is a basic oxide), so the solution has a pH above 7 [1].

**8. (a)** CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃ [1].

**(b)** Calcium oxide is a basic oxide (metal oxide) [1]; sulfur dioxide is an acidic oxide, so they neutralise each other to form a salt [1].

**9. (a)** The black solid dissolves / disappears [1]; the solution turns blue [1].

**(b)** CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O [1].

**(c)** Copper(II) oxide is insoluble in water, so it does not form an alkaline solution [1].

**10. (a)** ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(b)** A salt and water [1].

**(c)** Metal oxides are usually basic and react only with acids; zinc oxide also reacts with a base [1].

**11. (a)** R: basic [1]; S: amphoteric [1]; T: acidic [1].

**(b)** Zinc oxide or aluminium oxide [1].

**12.** Sodium oxide is basic and sulfur dioxide is acidic, with aluminium oxide amphoteric in between [1]; across the period the elements change from metals to non-metals [1]; metal oxides are basic and non-metal oxides are acidic, so the oxide type follows the element's character [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying an acidic oxide reacts with an acid.
- Giving only half the amphoteric definition: "reacts with acids and bases" without "to produce a salt and water", or the reverse.
- Using "neutral" or "unreactive" to describe amphoteric oxides.
- Writing CaOH or Ca(OH) instead of Ca(OH)₂.
- Testing an insoluble oxide such as copper(II) oxide with universal indicator and water.
- Forgetting to link oxide type to the metallic or non-metallic character of the element when the question asks for an explanation.
