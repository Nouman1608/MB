Condensed notes on the **Leadership** content (5.11 on this site) in the Area of Study **Management**, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and a worked example are in the [leadership study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-leadership/); test yourself with the [leadership practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-leadership-practice/). The whole topic is in the [human resources revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-human-resources-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

- Meaning of leadership.
- Leadership theory: trait (Carlyle and Galton, McGregor's Theory X and Y), behavioural (Blake and Mouton's managerial grid), contingency (Lewin, Tannenbaum and Schmidt's behavioural continuum) and functional (Adair's Three Circles).
- Styles: autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, paternalistic.
- Evaluate the usefulness of theories and styles; the impact and importance of leadership; recommend and justify methods of leadership.

## Core recall

### Meaning

- **Leadership:** influencing others to work willingly towards a goal.
- **Management:** wider; planning, organising, directing and controlling resources.
- A manager is not always a leader.

### The theories at a glance

| Family | Name | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Trait | Carlyle and Galton | Leaders are born with qualities |
| Trait (beliefs) | McGregor | Theory X: people dislike work, need control. Theory Y: people seek responsibility |
| Behavioural | Blake and Mouton grid | Score concern for production and for people (1 to 9 each); 9, 9 is the aim |
| Contingency | Lewin | Autocratic, democratic or laissez-faire, depending on the situation |
| Contingency | Tannenbaum and Schmidt | Continuum from boss-centred to subordinate-centred; choose by manager, subordinates and situation |
| Functional | Adair | Balance task, team and individual |

### Grid positions (production, people)

| Position | Name |
|---|---|
| 9, 1 | Authority-compliance |
| 1, 9 | Country club |
| 1, 1 | Impoverished |
| 5, 5 | Middle of the road |
| 9, 9 | Team management |

### The four styles

| Style | Who decides | Best when | Main problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Leader alone | Crisis, unskilled staff, safety | Low commitment, high turnover |
| Democratic | Leader after consulting | Skilled staff, creative work, change | Slow |
| Laissez-faire | Staff | Experts, self-motivated teams | Drift and confusion |
| Paternalistic | Leader, for the staff's good | Loyalty and family culture | Can feel patronising |

### Usefulness of each theory

- Trait: simple for selection; little evidence leaders are born.
- Theory X and Y: shows how beliefs shape behaviour; only two extremes.
- Grid: trainable target; assumes 9, 9 is always best.
- Contingency: realistic; needs a flexible leader.
- Adair: practical; does not weight the circles.

### Impact and importance

- Motivation, retention and cost of turnover.
- Productivity and quality.
- Culture and standards.
- Success of change.
- Matters most when staff are skilled and mobile, or the business is changing.

### Recommend and justify: a four-step pattern

1. Choose a style (or a mix over time).
2. Give two or three reasons from the case (staff skill, urgency, task type).
3. State a risk of your choice.
4. Say what would change your mind.

## Quick worked reminder

Ferncliffe Care Group (invented): three homes, 20 staff each. Autocratic North lost 9 staff (45% turnover); democratic Central lost 4 (20%); laissez-faire South lost 6 (30%). At £3,200 per leaver, North's replacement cost of £28,800 is £16,000 more than Central's £12,800. Style probably explains part of the gap, but one year of data from three homes is not conclusive.

## Exam prompts and how to approach them

- **"Explain Adair's Three Circles":** name the task, team and individual circles, say what each covers, and give one example of a leader neglecting a circle.
- **"Evaluate trait theory":** give one reason it helps (simple selection) and one reason it fails (little evidence leaders are born), then judge its value to the case.
- **"Recommend a style for this business":** link the style to staff skill, urgency and task type, give a risk, and say what would change your choice.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Leadership | Influencing others to work towards a goal |
| Trait theory | Leaders are born with certain qualities |
| Theory X | The belief that workers dislike work and need control |
| Theory Y | The belief that workers want responsibility and enjoy work |
| Managerial grid | A model scoring concern for production and for people |
| Behavioural continuum | A line from boss-centred to subordinate-centred leadership |
| Action-centred leadership | Adair's balance of task, team and individual |
| Autocratic | The leader makes decisions alone |
| Democratic | The leader consults and often follows the group |
| Laissez-faire | The leader leaves decisions to staff |
| Paternalistic | The leader decides in what they believe are the staff's interests |

## Exam traps

- Styles describe what leaders do; theories explain which to use.
- Theory X and Y are beliefs held by managers, not types of worker.
- Do not say one style is always best; contingency theory says it depends.
- Apply the theory to the case; do not only define it.
- Evaluate and recommend answers need a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Define leadership.
2. Give one difference between leadership and management.
3. State the main claim of trait theory.
4. What do Theory X and Theory Y assume?
5. What are the two axes of the managerial grid?
6. What does a 1, 9 score mean?
7. Name the two ends of Tannenbaum and Schmidt's continuum.
8. Name the three circles in Adair's model.
9. Distinguish between autocratic and paternalistic leadership.
10. Give one situation where an autocratic style is suitable.
11. Give one limitation of the managerial grid.
12. A home has 25 staff and 10 leave in a year. Calculate its staff turnover.

### Answers

1. The ability to influence others so that they work willingly towards a goal.
2. Management covers planning, organising and controlling resources; leadership is about influencing people. A manager may not be a leader.
3. Leaders are born with qualities such as confidence and intelligence; they cannot easily be trained.
4. Theory X: people dislike work and need control. Theory Y: people seek responsibility and are self-motivated.
5. Concern for production and concern for people.
6. Low concern for production, high concern for people: the country club style, with a friendly team but weak results.
7. Boss-centred leadership and subordinate-centred leadership.
8. Task, team and individual.
9. Autocratic leaders decide and command without explaining or considering staff views; paternalistic leaders decide too, but explain and act in what they see as the staff's interests.
10. A crisis, for example a fire evacuation, or work involving unskilled staff or safety.
11. It assumes 9, 9 is best in every situation, ignoring context.
12. 10 ÷ 25 × 100 = **40%**.
