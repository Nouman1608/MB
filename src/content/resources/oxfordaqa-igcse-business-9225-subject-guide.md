OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225) introduces students to how businesses start, operate and
make decisions in a changing world. It suits anyone curious about enterprise, management and the
economy, and it provides a foundation for later study of business, economics or accounting. The
qualification is linear with no coursework. It is assessed by two written papers at the end of the
course, each lasting 120 minutes, worth 90 marks and carrying 50% of the grade. The specification
was first taught in September 2020 and first examined in May/June 2022.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged in six topic areas.

**Business in the real world** sets the scene: what businesses are and the context in which they
operate.

**Influences on business** examines the outside forces that shape business decisions: technology,
ethical and environmental considerations, the effect of the economic climate on business,
globalisation, legislation and the competitive environment.

The remaining four areas are the main **functional areas** of a business:

- **Business operations**, how goods and services are produced and delivered.
- **Human resources**, how a business organises, recruits, manages and motivates its people.
- **Marketing**, how a business understands and reaches its customers.
- **Finance**, how a business raises money and judges its financial performance.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Influences of Operations and Human Resources on Business Activity | 120 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Influences of Marketing and Finance on Business Activity | 120 minutes | 90 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed written exams taken at the end of the course. The qualification
is untiered and has no coursework, so the whole grade rests on these two equally weighted papers.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles show how the course is designed. Each paper pairs two of the functional areas,
operations with human resources on Paper 1 and marketing with finance on Paper 2, and frames them as
*influences on business activity*. That framing is a reminder that the first two topic areas are not
introductory material to be left behind. The ideas in Business in the real world and Influences on
business provide the context for questions on either paper.

The functional areas also depend on each other. A decision about production methods affects staffing
and costs; a marketing campaign needs funding and must be delivered by operations; changes in the
economic climate or in legislation can affect every function at once. Questions often reward
students who can trace these knock-on effects rather than discussing one area in isolation.

A single scenario shows how far one decision can reach. Imagine a small manufacturer deciding
whether to move part of its production overseas. Globalisation and the economic climate shape the
opportunity; legislation and ethical and environmental considerations limit what is acceptable; the
choice changes business operations and the supply of goods; it affects existing staff and creates
new recruitment and management needs in human resources; it may allow a change in price or brand
message in marketing; and it has to be funded and then judged on its financial results. A student
who can follow a case like this across all six topic areas is well placed for either paper, because
each paper's two functional areas are always set against the wider influences on business.

It also helps to notice that some ideas recur under different headings. Competition, for example,
appears as part of the competitive environment, but it also drives pricing decisions in marketing,
efficiency targets in operations and pay and retention decisions in human resources.

Across the whole course, the key skills are applying business ideas to a specific situation,
analysing causes and consequences, and making a supported judgement. Calculations appear too,
particularly in finance, so numerical accuracy matters alongside written argument.

## Planning your study

A sensible order starts with Business in the real world and Influences on business, because they
give the vocabulary and context for everything that follows. Then work through the functional areas
in pairs that match the papers: business operations and human resources first, followed by marketing
and finance. This gives you a full set of content for each paper as you go.

Throughout the course, collect short real-world examples of businesses facing the influences you
study, such as new technology, changes in the law or competition. These make application easier in
the exam. Practise financial calculations regularly rather than leaving them until the end.

At the end of each topic area, write a one-page summary that lists its key terms, any formulas, and
two or three ways it connects to the other areas. By the end of the course these summaries form a
compact map of the whole specification, which is far more useful for revision than a stack of
separate notes.

Once all six areas have been taught, start full timed papers and review where marks were lost. The
exam-preparation page explains how to approach each paper in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE Business assessed?**
By two written papers. Paper 1 covers the influences of operations and human resources on business activity, and Paper 2 covers the influences of marketing and finance. Each is 120 minutes, 90 marks and 50% of the grade.

**Is there coursework in 9225?**
No. The qualification is linear and assessed entirely by the two written papers.

**Is IGCSE Business tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same two papers.

**When was 9225 first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2020 and first examined in May/June 2022.

**Which external influences does the course cover?**
Technology, ethical and environmental considerations, the economic climate, globalisation, legislation and the competitive environment.
