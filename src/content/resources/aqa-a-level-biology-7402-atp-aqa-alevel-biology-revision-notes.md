## Structure

ATP = **ribose + adenine + three phosphate groups**. It is a **nucleotide derivative**: an adenine RNA nucleotide (one phosphate) with two more phosphates added.

| Molecule | Phosphate groups |
|---|---|
| Adenine RNA nucleotide | 1 |
| ADP | 2 |
| ATP | 3 |

## Reactions

| Reaction | Equation | Enzyme | Type | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrolysis | ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi | ATP hydrolase | Hydrolysis | Released |
| Resynthesis | ADP + Pi → ATP + H₂O | ATP synthase | Condensation | Required |

Resynthesis happens during **respiration** (all cells) and **photosynthesis** (chloroplasts).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATP | Adenosine triphosphate, the immediate energy source of cells |
| ADP | Adenosine diphosphate |
| Pi | Inorganic phosphate group |
| ATP hydrolase | Enzyme catalysing ATP hydrolysis |
| ATP synthase | Enzyme catalysing condensation of ADP and Pi |
| Coupling | Using energy from ATP hydrolysis to drive an energy-requiring reaction |
| Phosphorylation | Adding a phosphate group to a molecule, often making it more reactive |
| Adenosine | Adenine joined to ribose |

## Why ATP, not glucose, is the immediate energy source

- Hydrolysis is a **single reaction** with **one enzyme**: energy released quickly.
- Releases energy in **small, manageable amounts**: less wasted as heat.
- **Rapidly resynthesised** from ADP + Pi: molecules recycled.
- Pi can **phosphorylate** other molecules, making them more reactive.

ATP is **not** a long-term store; cells hold little and recycle it fast. Long-term stores: glycogen, starch, triglycerides.

## Model sentences for applied questions

When a question describes an unfamiliar energy-requiring process, these sentences carry the usual marking points:

- "ATP is hydrolysed to ADP and Pi by ATP hydrolase, releasing energy."
- "The hydrolysis is coupled to the process, so the energy released drives it."
- "The Pi can phosphorylate the substrate or carrier protein, making it more reactive or changing its shape."
- "ADP and Pi are then recombined by ATP synthase, using energy from respiration."

A cell with a high demand for ATP, such as a muscle cell or a cell carrying out a lot of active transport, usually has many mitochondria, because that is where most ATP is resynthesised in aerobic respiration.

## Calculations with given data

- Moles of ATP needed = energy required ÷ energy per mole of ATP, rounded **up** to a whole number.
- Turnover time = ATP in pool ÷ rate of use.

Mini reminder with new numbers: a reaction needs 75 kJ per mole; ATP gives 30.5 kJ per mole (data supplied). 75 ÷ 30.5 = 2.46, so 3 moles of ATP are needed, supplying 91.5 kJ; 16.5 kJ is released as heat.

## Exam traps

- "High-energy bonds release energy when broken." Say hydrolysis of ATP releases energy.
- "ATP is an energy store." It is an immediate energy source.
- "Respiration makes energy." It releases energy, used to make ATP.
- ATP synthase and ATP hydrolase swapped.
- Deoxyribose instead of ribose.
- Forgetting water: hydrolysis uses it, resynthesis releases it.
- Giving a fraction of an ATP molecule as an answer.
- Saying phosphorylation "adds energy" instead of "adds a phosphate group".

## Self-test

1. Name the three components of ATP.
2. Why is ATP called a nucleotide derivative?
3. Name the enzyme that catalyses ATP hydrolysis.
4. Write the equation for the resynthesis of ATP.
5. Name the two processes during which ATP synthase resynthesises ATP.
6. What type of reaction is the resynthesis of ATP?
7. Give two reasons ATP is a better immediate energy source than glucose.
8. What is meant by coupling?
9. What is phosphorylation, and what is its usual effect?
10. A reaction needs 40 kJ per mole. ATP releases 30.5 kJ per mole. How many moles of ATP are needed?
11. A cell holds 6.0 × 10⁸ ATP molecules and uses 3.0 × 10⁶ per second. How long does it take to use the whole pool once?
12. Why do cells not keep large amounts of ATP?

### Answers

1. Ribose, adenine and three phosphate groups.
2. It is built on an adenine nucleotide (ribose, adenine, phosphate), with two extra phosphate groups.
3. ATP hydrolase.
4. ADP + Pi → ATP + H₂O.
5. Respiration and photosynthesis.
6. Condensation.
7. Any two: single-step hydrolysis releases energy quickly; small, manageable amounts of energy released; rapidly resynthesised; Pi can phosphorylate other molecules.
8. Linking the energy released by ATP hydrolysis to an energy-requiring reaction so that the reaction can happen.
9. Adding a phosphate group to a molecule; it often makes the molecule more reactive.
10. 40 ÷ 30.5 = 1.31, so 2 moles.
11. 6.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁶ = 200 seconds.
12. It is used and resynthesised so quickly that a small pool is enough; it is an immediate energy source, not a store.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between ATP hydrolase and ATP synthase?**
ATP hydrolase catalyses hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and Pi, releasing energy. ATP synthase catalyses condensation of ADP and Pi to make ATP.

**Is ATP an energy store?**
No. It is an immediate source of energy that is constantly recycled. Long-term stores are molecules such as glycogen, starch and triglycerides.

**What sugar does ATP contain?**
Ribose, the same sugar as in RNA nucleotides.
