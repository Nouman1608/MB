This guide covers content item 2.5.2, Effective recruitment, in Theme 2 Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0), Issue 2, July 2022. The page on [organisational structures and job roles](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-organisational-structures-and-job-roles/) gives the basic definitions. This page shows how the roles and documents are used in a real recruitment decision and how to choose a method for a particular business. Every business and figure is invented.

## What 2.5.2 contains

| Heading in the specification | Points listed |
|---|---|
| Different job roles and responsibilities | key job roles and their responsibilities: directors, senior managers, supervisors/team leaders, operational and support staff |
| How businesses recruit people | documents: person specification and job description, application form, CV; recruitment methods used to meet different business needs (internal and external recruitment) |

## Job roles: recognising them from what people do

Exam cases rarely label a role. They describe what someone does, and you must identify the role or explain why it matters. Use the scope of each person's responsibilities as the clue.

| Role | Scope of responsibility | Clue words in a case |
|---|---|---|
| Directors | The whole business over the long term | "decided to expand overseas", "set the company's objectives", "answer to shareholders" |
| Senior managers | One function or area, such as marketing, operations or a region | "head of", "responsible for the marketing budget", "regional manager" |
| Supervisors/team leaders | A small team's daily work | "organises the shift", "checks the team's work", "first person staff go to with a problem" |
| Operational staff | Producing the good or providing the service | "picks orders", "serves customers", "drives deliveries" |
| Support staff | Helping others do their jobs | "IT help desk", "cleaning", "reception", "payroll" |

### How roles change as a business grows

In a start-up the owner is director, manager, supervisor and often operational worker at the same time. As the business grows, the owner can no longer do everything, so roles are separated. A common sequence is:

1. The owner hires operational staff and supervises them directly.
2. A supervisor or team leader is appointed so that the owner can spend time on planning.
3. Senior managers are appointed to run functions such as sales or finance.
4. Support staff are added when tasks like payroll or IT become too large for others to handle alongside their jobs.

Each new role is a recruitment decision, which is why the specification places roles and recruitment together.

## Recruitment documents and how they are used

The specification names four documents. Two are written by the business before advertising, and two are produced by candidates.

| Document | Written by | Contains | Used to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job description | Business | Job title, duties, who the job holder reports to, hours, place of work | Tell candidates what the job involves |
| Person specification | Business | Skills, qualifications, experience and qualities the right person needs | Set the standard candidates are judged against |
| Application form | Business designs it; candidate completes it | The same questions for every candidate | Compare candidates fairly, point by point |
| CV | Candidate | Education, work history, skills, in the candidate's own layout | Let candidates present themselves in their own way |

A person specification often splits requirements into **essential** criteria (a candidate without them cannot do the job) and **desirable** criteria (useful extras). This split is standard practice rather than wording from the specification, but it makes the document's purpose clear: it is the measuring stick for shortlisting.

### Worked example: shortlisting at Hadley Logistics

Hadley Logistics needs a warehouse team leader. Its person specification states:

- Essential: at least two years' warehouse experience; able to organise a team's shift; forklift licence.
- Desirable: experience of training new staff.

Three applicants' forms show:

| Candidate | Two years' experience | Organised a shift | Forklift licence | Trained staff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ayo | Yes (4 years) | Yes | Yes | No |
| Bea | Yes (3 years) | Yes | No | Yes |
| Cal | No (1 year) | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Only Ayo meets every essential criterion, so only Ayo is shortlisted on the essentials. Bea and Cal each meet the desirable criterion, but that cannot make up for a missing essential one. If Hadley had asked for CVs, it would have needed to search each layout for the same facts, which takes longer and risks missing information. This is the main reason a business with many applicants uses an application form.

## Internal and external recruitment: matching method to need

The specification phrase is "recruitment methods used to meet different business needs". The skill is to read the need from the case and pick the method that meets it.

**Internal recruitment** fills a vacancy from existing employees, for example through a staff noticeboard, an email to staff or the business's internal website.

**External recruitment** looks outside the business, for example through job websites, recruitment agencies, local newspapers, social media or a job centre.

| Business need | Method that usually meets it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fill the post quickly and cheaply | Internal | No advertising cost; candidates already known |
| Reward loyal staff and motivate others | Internal | Shows that promotion is possible |
| Bring in skills the business does not have | External | Existing staff lack them |
| New ideas or a change of culture | External | A newcomer sees the business differently |
| Many identical posts to fill, such as a new branch | External | The internal pool is too small |
| Hard-to-find specialist | External, often through an agency | The agency searches and screens for the business |

### Worked example: comparing methods at Hadley Logistics

The team leader post pays £26,500 a year. Hadley has three options.

- **Internal.** An email to staff costs nothing and four employees apply. But promoting an operational worker leaves their job empty, and filling it externally needs a £300 advert. The real cost of internal recruitment is therefore about £300, not zero.
- **Job website.** An advert costs £350 and attracts 70 applicants, of whom 7 meet the essential criteria. Cost per suitable applicant = £350 ÷ 7 = **£50**.
- **Recruitment agency.** The fee is 15% of the first year's salary: 0.15 × £26,500 = **£3,975**. The agency supplies 5 screened candidates, a cost of £3,975 ÷ 5 = **£795** per candidate.

The website is the cheapest external option per suitable applicant, but someone at Hadley must read 70 applications. The agency is expensive but saves managers' time. Internal recruitment is cheapest and quick, and an existing employee already knows the warehouse, but only if one of the four internal applicants meets the essential criteria.

## Exam technique

- **Explain** (no context): fact plus two expansion points. "Internal recruitment fills a vacancy from existing staff. The business already knows the candidate's ability, so there is less risk of appointing someone unsuitable."
- **Outline**: two linked points about the business in the question, such as the cost figures above.
- **Justify**: recommend internal or external for the business, using evidence about both.
- **Evaluate**: decide which method or document is best and say what the answer depends on, such as whether a suitable internal candidate exists.

### Annotated model answer

*Question (Justify, extract):* Should Hadley use the job website or the agency?

*Strong answer extract:* "The website costs £350 against £3,975 for the agency, a saving of £3,625, and it still produced 7 applicants meeting the essential criteria, more than the agency's 5. Although managers must read 70 applications, the essential criteria make this quicker. I recommend the website, unless managers are too busy with the warehouse to shortlist, in which case the agency's screening would justify its fee."

*Why it works:* it compares both options with figures, gives the drawback of the choice and states the condition that would change the decision.

## Common mistakes

- Swapping the job description (the job) and person specification (the person).
- Saying an application form is written by the candidate. The business designs it.
- Treating internal recruitment as free. It usually creates another vacancy.
- Naming "external recruitment" as a single method. Give the method, such as an agency or job website.
- Describing directors as "the bosses" without saying they set long-term direction for the whole business.
- Confusing support staff with supervisors. Support staff help others work; supervisors direct a team.
