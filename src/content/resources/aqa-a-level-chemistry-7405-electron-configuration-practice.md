These questions cover section **3.1.1.3 Electron configuration** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the ideas in the [Electron configuration study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electron-configuration/) and the [Electron configuration revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electron-configuration-revision-notes/) first. Ionisation energies are in kJ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Write the full electron configuration of each species.

**(a)** a sulfur atom **[1]**

**(b)** a sulfide ion, S²⁻ **[1]**

**2.** Titanium has atomic number 22. Using noble-gas shorthand, write the electron configuration of:

**(a)** a titanium atom **[1]**

**(b)** a Ti²⁺ ion **[1]**

**3.** State the number of unpaired electrons in an atom of each element.

**(a)** nitrogen **[1]**

**(b)** oxygen **[1]**

**(c)** iron **[1]**

**4.** An atom has the electron configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³.

**(a)** Identify the element. **[1]**

**(b)** State the block of the Periodic Table it belongs to and give a reason. **[1]**

**5.** From the list K⁺, Na⁺, Cl⁻, O²⁻, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, select all the ions that are isoelectronic with an argon atom. **[2]**

**6.** A student writes the configuration of the Co²⁺ ion (cobalt, Z = 27) as [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s².

**(a)** Give the correct configuration. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the student's error. **[1]**

**7.** Magnesium is in Group 2.

**(a)** Define the term *first ionisation energy*. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the third ionisation energy of magnesium. **[1]**

**8.** The first ionisation energies of the Group 2 elements beryllium to barium are 900, 738, 590, 550 and 503 kJ mol⁻¹. Explain this trend. **[3]**

**9.** The first ionisation energy of argon is 1521 kJ mol⁻¹ and that of potassium is 419 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Explain why the first ionisation energy of argon is greater than that of chlorine (1251 kJ mol⁻¹). **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the value for potassium is so much lower than the value for argon. **[2]**

**10.** An element in Period 3 has the following first five ionisation energies: 787, 1577, 3232, 4356, 16 091.

**(a)** Deduce the group of the element and identify it. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why each successive ionisation energy is greater than the one before. **[1]**

**11.** The first four ionisation energies of aluminium are 578, 1817, 2745 and 11 577. Explain how these values provide evidence that aluminium's outer electrons occupy two different sub-shells. **[3]**

**12.** Describe and explain the pattern you would see in a graph of log(ionisation energy) against the number of the electron removed, for all eleven electrons of sodium. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how the first ionisation energies of the elements sodium to argon give evidence for the existence of sub-shells and of orbitals holding pairs of electrons. Refer to specific elements. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴** [1]

**(b)** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶** [1]

**2. (a)** **[Ar] 3d² 4s²** [1]

**(b)** **[Ar] 3d²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Ti²⁺ loses both 4s electrons; [Ar] 4s² would mean 3d electrons had been removed first.

**3. (a)** 3 [1]

**(b)** 2 [1]

**(c)** 4 [1]

*Examiner insight:* Electrons occupy each orbital in a sub-shell singly before pairing, so Fe's 3d⁶ has one pair and four unpaired electrons.

**4. (a)** **Arsenic** [1]

**(b)** p block, because its highest-energy electrons are in a p sub-shell (4p) [1]

**5.** K⁺, Cl⁻ and Ca²⁺ (all 18 electrons) for both marks; any two correct with no incorrect ion for one mark. Na⁺, O²⁻ and Mg²⁺ have 10 electrons. **[2]**

**6. (a)** **[Ar] 3d⁷** [1]

**(b)** The 4s electrons are removed before the 3d electrons, but the student removed two 3d electrons and kept 4s² [1]

**7. (a)** The energy needed to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms [1] to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions [1]

**(b)** **Mg²⁺(g) → Mg³⁺(g) + e⁻** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The third ionisation starts from Mg²⁺, not from Mg; one electron is removed per step.

**8.** First ionisation energy decreases down the group [1]. The outer electrons are in a higher shell, so further from the nucleus, and there is more shielding by inner shells [1]. These effects outweigh the increase in nuclear charge, so the attraction for the outer electron is weaker [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Argon has one more proton, so a greater nuclear charge [1]; its outer electron is in the same sub-shell (3p) with similar shielding, so it is attracted more strongly [1]

**(b)** Potassium's outer electron is in a new shell (4s), further from the nucleus [1] and shielded by an extra inner shell, so it is attracted much less strongly despite the higher nuclear charge [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** The big jump is between the fourth and fifth values, so there are four outer electrons: **Group 4** [1]; the element is **silicon** [1]

**(b)** Each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion, so it is held more strongly [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The first rise (578 to 1817) is much larger, proportionally, than the second (1817 to 2745) [1]. The first electron is removed from the higher-energy 3p sub-shell [1], and the second and third from the lower-energy 3s sub-shell, which is closer to the nucleus [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The answer must link the uneven rise to two named sub-shells; describing the big jump after the third electron is evidence for shells, not sub-shells.

**12.** One low value, then eight values rising gradually, then two very high values [1]. A big jump after the first electron because the next comes from shell 2, closer to the nucleus with less shielding [1]; another big jump after the ninth because the last two come from shell 1 [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Indicative points; award one mark for each, up to 6:

- General increase across the period because nuclear charge increases while electrons enter the same shell with similar shielding [1]
- Magnesium (3s²) to aluminium (3s² 3p¹): ionisation energy falls [1]
- Aluminium's outer electron is in the 3p sub-shell, which is higher in energy than 3s (and partly shielded by 3s), so is easier to remove [1]
- This shows that a shell is divided into sub-shells of different energy [1]
- Phosphorus (3p³) to sulfur (3p⁴): ionisation energy falls [1]
- In sulfur two electrons share one 3p orbital and repel, so one is easier to remove; this shows orbitals hold pairs of electrons [1]

Maximum **[6]**.

*Examiner insight:* Each drop needs the element named, the configuration involved and the reason; "sulfur has a full orbital" without the repulsion idea does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Removing 3d before 4s for transition-metal ions.
- Writing the second or third ionisation equation from the neutral atom.
- Leaving out state symbols.
- Explaining the Group 2 trend without saying that distance and shielding outweigh nuclear charge.
- Confusing evidence for shells (big jumps) with evidence for sub-shells (the Al and S drops, uneven rises within a shell).
