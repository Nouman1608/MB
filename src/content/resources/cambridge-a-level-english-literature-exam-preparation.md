Cambridge A-Level English Literature (9695) is assessed by four written papers, each 2 hours long, worth 50 marks, and each 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1, Drama and Poetry; Paper 2, Prose and Unseen; Paper 3, Shakespeare and Drama; and Paper 4, Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose. Papers 1 and 2 can be certificated alone as an AS Level, in which case each is weighted 50% of the AS. This page sits beside the site's pages on [Paper 1 Drama and Poetry](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-1-drama-and-poetry/), [Paper 2 Prose and Unseen](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-2-prose-and-unseen/) and [Paper 3 Shakespeare and Drama](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-3-shakespeare-and-drama/).

## AS Level or full A Level

The syllabus is staged. You can take Papers 1 and 2 and stop with an AS Level, or continue to Papers 3 and 4 for the full A Level. The percentages given for each paper are shares of the full A Level, which is why they add up to 100% across four papers. If you are sitting only the AS, remember that each of your two papers then counts for half of that AS.

## Paper 1: Drama and Poetry (2 hours, 50 marks, 25%)

This is an AS paper. It combines drama and poetry in one sitting, so you need to be able to move between the two forms and to manage the time for each answer. Revise your set plays and poems with the same priorities: methods, effects, context where relevant, and a clear argument. The [Paper 1 revision notes](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-1-drama-and-poetry-revision-notes/) support this.

## Paper 2: Prose and Unseen (2 hours, 50 marks, 25%)

Also an AS paper. The title tells you that it tests studied prose and an unseen element. For the unseen part you cannot prepare the text, only the method: read slowly, identify form, voice and structure, then build an argument about how the passage works. Practise on unfamiliar poems and extracts regularly. See [the Paper 2 revision notes](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-2-prose-unseen-revision-notes/).

## Paper 3: Shakespeare and Drama (2 hours, 50 marks, 25%)

The first A2 paper. It combines a Shakespeare text and other drama. Treat plays as performed art: think about staging, audience and dramatic structure as well as language. Detailed notes are on [Paper 3 Shakespeare and Drama](/resources/a-level-english-literature-paper-3-shakespeare-and-drama-revision-notes/).

## Paper 4: Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose (2 hours, 50 marks, 25%)

The second A2 paper. It spans writing before and after 1900, so build a sense of how conventions and contexts differ across the two periods and be ready to compare where a question invites it. A [Paper 4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-paper-4-pre-and-post-1900-poetry-and-prose-study-guide/) is available.

The record does not set out the question choices or the number of questions on each paper, so check the specification and specimen papers for that rather than relying on this page.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Each paper: 120 minutes / 50 marks = 2.4 minutes per mark.
- Two-paper AS total: 2 x 120 = 240 minutes for 2 x 50 = 100 marks.
- Four-paper A Level total: 4 x 120 = 480 minutes for 4 x 50 = 200 marks.
- Each mark is worth 25 / 50 = 0.5% of the full A Level.

At 2.4 minutes per mark, a paper does not simply reward speed. Build in planning time: if you set aside about 10 minutes at the start and 10 minutes for a final read-through, that leaves about 100 minutes of writing on each paper.

## Command words and question styles

Literature essays commonly use "Discuss", "Explore", "Compare" and "How far". Each calls for a sustained argument supported by evidence. "Compare" wants both texts handled together, not in two separate halves. "How far" requires you to weigh the claim and reach a judgement, so a one-sided answer will fall short. Avoid plot retelling; every paragraph should be about what the writer does and why it matters.

## Worked practice scenario

An original Paper 2 style practice using an invented prose extract.

> By the third winter the shop had learned to do without her. Customers asked after her in the voice people use for a clock that has stopped, and Mr Anwar, who had never once been late, began to arrive a little earlier each morning, as though he could shorten the absence.

Question: "How does the writer create a sense of loss in this passage?"

Plan: (1) Personification of the shop ("learned to do without"). (2) The clock simile, linking time and absence. (3) Mr Anwar's changing routine: grief shown through behaviour, not statement. (4) Overall: loss is quiet and domestic.

Model paragraph: "The writer presents loss indirectly, by showing how an ordinary place adapts to a missing person. The shop is personified as having 'learned to do without her', a phrase that sounds practical and even cold, yet it implies an effort of adjustment that was neither easy nor chosen. The simile of 'a clock that has stopped' deepens this: customers lower their voices as if marking a pause in time itself, and the comparison turns an absence into something audible. By ending on Mr Anwar arriving earlier, the writer converts grief into a small, futile action, and the phrase 'as though he could shorten the absence' leaves the sadness unspoken but unmistakable."

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know which of your papers are AS and which are A2.
- Keep a bank of short, flexible quotations from every set text.
- Practise unseen analysis on unfamiliar passages each week.
- Plan every essay for several minutes before writing.
- Use the 2.4 minutes per mark figure to set a time for each question.
- Argue a line; do not narrate.
- Compare throughout when the question asks for comparison.
- Reserve time to read through and correct slips.

## Self-test

1. How many papers make up the full A Level and what is each worth?
2. Which papers form the standalone AS Level?
3. How many minutes per mark is allowed on each paper?
4. What is the total examination time for the full A Level?
5. How much of the AS Level does each AS paper count for?

**Answers:** 1. Four papers, each 25%. 2. Papers 1 and 2. 3. 2.4 minutes per mark, since 120 / 50 = 2.4. 4. 480 minutes, since 4 x 120 = 480. 5. 50% each.

There are four written papers, each 2 hours and 50 marks. Each is worth 25% of the full A Level.

**What are the paper titles?**
Paper 1 is Drama and Poetry, Paper 2 is Prose and Unseen, Paper 3 is Shakespeare and Drama, and Paper 4 is Pre- and Post-1900 Poetry and Prose.
