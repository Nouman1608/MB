## 1.3.4 Managing people

### Approaches to staffing

| Term | Meaning | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Staff as an asset | People are a source of advantage worth investing in | Higher training and pay bills |
| Staff as a cost | People are an expense to minimise | Low morale, high turnover |
| Multi-skilling | Staff trained for several jobs | Training time |
| Part-time and temporary | Fewer hours or fixed period | Less commitment |
| Zero-hour contract | No guaranteed hours | Insecure income for staff |
| Flexible hours, home working | Choice over when or where | Harder to supervise |
| Outsourcing | An outside firm does the task | Less control |
| Dismissal | Contract ended for a reason about the employee | Fault-based |
| Redundancy | The job itself no longer exists | Not the worker's fault |
| Individual approach | Terms agreed one to one | Slower across many staff |
| Collective bargaining | Union or group negotiates for many | Stronger employee voice |

**Must-know distinction:** dismissal concerns the individual worker; redundancy concerns the post.

### Recruitment, selection and training

- **Recruitment** attracts applicants; **selection** picks one.
- **Internal:** cheaper, faster, known person, but narrow choice and a gap elsewhere.
- **External:** wider pool and new ideas, but dearer and slower to settle.
- **Costs to list:** advertising, agency fees, interview time, tests, induction, trainer time, lost output while learning.
- **Worked reminder:** advert £900, agency fee 10% of a £25,000 salary (£2,500) and two managers interviewing for 3 hours each at £25 an hour (£150) give a hiring cost of £3,550, before any induction or training.
- **Induction:** introduction to the firm. **On-the-job:** learning at the workstation. **Off-the-job:** away from work, such as a course.

### Organisational design

| Term | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy | Levels of authority |
| Chain of command | Route for instructions down and reports up |
| Span of control | Number of people reporting to one manager |
| Centralised | Key decisions at the top |
| Decentralised | Decisions pushed down |
| Tall | Many levels, narrow spans |
| Flat | Few levels, wide spans |
| Matrix | Project teams drawing people from several departments |

**Quick calculation.** Number of managers needed is staff divided by span. For 120 staff: span 8 gives 15 managers; span 15 gives 8. Wider span means fewer managers and lower cost, but each manager watches over more people.

**Impact table.**

| Structure | Efficiency | Motivation |
|---|---|---|
| Tall | Close control but slow, costly | Clear promotion steps; may feel restrictive |
| Flat | Quick, cheaper | More responsibility; fewer promotion steps, risk of overload |
| Matrix | Shares skills across projects | Variety; two bosses can conflict |

### Motivation

**Why it matters:** better output and quality, lower absence and turnover, so lower recruitment cost.

| Theorist | Key idea | Practical use |
|---|---|---|
| Taylor | Simple tasks, close supervision, pay by output | Piecework |
| Mayo | Social needs, attention and teamwork | Team working, consultation |
| Maslow | Hierarchy: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation | Meet the lowest unmet need |
| Herzberg | Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction | Fix pay, then enrich the job |

| Financial method | Idea |
|---|---|
| Piecework | Paid per unit |
| Commission | Percentage of sales |
| Bonus | Extra for hitting a target |
| Profit share | Employees share profit |
| Performance-related pay | Pay linked to assessed performance |

| Non-financial method | Idea |
|---|---|
| Delegation | Authority passed down |
| Consultation | Ask before deciding |
| Empowerment | Staff decide about their own work |
| Team working | Shared goals |
| Flexible working | Choice of hours or place |
| Job enrichment | More responsibility and challenge |
| Job rotation | Switching between tasks |
| Job enlargement | More tasks at the same level |

**Method in steps for a motivation question.**

1. Name the problem in the case (turnover, errors, absence).
2. Pick a theory that explains it.
3. Pick a method that fits that theory and the workforce.
4. Say what it costs and how long it takes to work.
5. Judge it against one alternative.

**Quick calculations.**

- Commission: 3% of £80,000 sales is £2,400.
- Piecework: 480 units at 75p is £360.
- Profit share: 5% of £260,000 is £13,000; shared among 26 staff, £500 each.

### Leadership

- **Management:** plans, organises, coordinates and controls. **Leadership:** sets direction and inspires.
- **Autocratic:** leader decides alone. **Paternalistic:** leader decides but explains and cares. **Democratic:** involves staff. **Laissez-faire:** broad aims, staff decide how.
- **Entrepreneur to leader:** the founder must delegate, trust managers and put systems in place, which is hard for someone used to controlling everything.

## 1.3.5 Entrepreneurs and leaders

### Role of an entrepreneur

| Role | Points |
|---|---|
| Creating and setting up | Spot an opportunity, plan, raise finance |
| Running and expanding/developing | Manage cash and people, then grow |
| Intrapreneurship | Employees innovate inside an existing business |
| Barriers | Finance, experience, red tape, competition, fear of failure |
| Risk and uncertainty | Risk can be estimated; uncertainty cannot |

### Motives and characteristics

- **Characteristics and skills:** risk-taking, creativity, determination, self-confidence, organisation, communication, opportunity spotting.
- **Financial motives:** profit maximisation (highest possible) and profit satisficing (enough to be content).
- **Non-financial motives:** ethical stance, social entrepreneurship, independence, home working.

### Business objectives

| Objective | Short note |
|---|---|
| Survival | New or struggling firms; cash flow |
| Profit maximisation | Highest profit; hard to know |
| Sales maximisation | Highest sales; margins may fall |
| Market share | Firm sales divided by market sales, times 100 |
| Cost efficiency | Lower unit cost |
| Employee welfare | Pay, safety, conditions |
| Customer satisfaction | Loyalty and repeat sales |
| Social objectives | Benefit to community or environment |

**Quick calculation.** Market share: £2.4m of a £30m market is 8%.

### Business choices

- **Opportunity cost:** the next best alternative given up. Two uses of £20,000 earn £3,000 and £2,600 a year. Choosing the first gives a net gain of £400 and an opportunity cost of £2,600.
- **Trade-off:** more of one aim means less of another, for example growth against short-term profit.

## Method in steps for a judgement question

1. State your answer in the first sentence.
2. Give one developed reason for it, using case figures.
3. Give one developed reason against it.
4. Say which matters more for this business, and why.
5. Name what the judgement depends on, such as firm size, staff skill, time frame or cash available.

## Must-know distinctions

- Asset versus cost view of staff.
- Dismissal versus redundancy.
- Individual approach versus collective bargaining.
- Span of control versus chain of command.
- Job enrichment (depth) versus enlargement (breadth).
- Hygiene factors versus motivators.
- Risk versus uncertainty.
- Profit maximisation versus profit satisficing.
- Management versus leadership.

## Quick self-test

1. State two ways a business can create a flexible workforce.
2. Define redundancy.
3. A manager supervises 9 staff. Which term describes the 9?
4. Give one benefit and one cost of internal recruitment.
5. Which motivation theorist favoured pay by output?
6. According to Herzberg, what do hygiene factors do?
7. Name two financial methods of improving performance.
8. What is the difference between job enrichment and job enlargement?
9. Define profit satisficing.
10. A firm chooses project A (net return £7,000) over project B (£5,500). What is the opportunity cost?

### Answers

1. Any two from multi-skilling, part-time and temporary staff, zero-hour contracts, flexible hours and home working, outsourcing.
2. The job no longer exists, so the employee is let go through no fault of their own.
3. Span of control.
4. Benefit: cheaper or quicker, and the person knows the firm. Cost: the choice is narrow, and it leaves another vacancy.
5. Taylor.
6. They prevent dissatisfaction but do not by themselves motivate.
7. Any two from piecework, commission, bonus, profit share, performance-related pay.
8. Enrichment adds responsibility and challenge; enlargement adds more tasks at the same level.
9. Aiming for a satisfactory profit rather than the highest possible.
10. £5,500 a year, the value of the next best alternative.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a term but never applying it to the case.
- Calling redundancy dismissal, or the reverse.
- Saying a flat structure is always better, with no mention of managers' workload.
- Writing that pay always motivates, and not using Herzberg.
- Leaving out the cost of a motivation method.
- Treating risk and uncertainty as one idea.
- Quoting a leadership style without saying why it suits the task or the people.
- Giving the whole cost, not the next best option, as an opportunity cost.
- Ending an evaluation with no judgement and no condition on which it depends.
