These questions cover subtopic 4.5 Energy, work and power of Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 (Paper 4). Take g = 10 m s⁻² and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Where a question says "use an energy method", a solution by Newton's second law and constant-acceleration formulae is not acceptable.

## Section A

**1.** A trolley is pulled 12 m along level ground in 8 s by a constant force of 50 N acting at 35° above the horizontal. Find the work done by the force and its average power. **[3]**

**2.** A particle of mass 0.8 kg is released from rest at a point on a smooth curved track 3.2 m above the lowest point of the track.

**(a)** Find the speed of the particle at the lowest point.

**(b)** Find its speed when it is 1.4 m above the lowest point. **[3]**

**3.** A car of mass 1200 kg moves at a constant speed of 30 m s⁻¹ along a straight level road. The power of its engine is 18 kW. Find the resistance to motion. **[2]**

**4.** A particle of mass 3 kg moves on a smooth horizontal surface with speed 2 m s⁻¹. A horizontal force then does 60 J of work on it. Find its new speed. **[2]**

**5.** A pump raises 200 kg of water each minute through a vertical height of 15 m and delivers it at a speed of 8 m s⁻¹. Find the power of the pump, assuming no energy is wasted. **[4]**

**6.** A ball of mass 0.4 kg is projected vertically upwards at 15 m s⁻¹. Air resistance can be ignored. Use an energy method to find the greatest height reached and the speed of the ball when it is 5 m above the point of projection. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A sledge of mass 20 kg is pulled from rest across rough horizontal ground by a rope inclined at 25° above the horizontal. The tension in the rope is 60 N and the coefficient of friction is 0.15. Use an energy method to find the speed of the sledge after it has moved 10 m. **[6]**

**8.** A car of mass 1200 kg has an engine with a constant power of 24 kW. The resistance to motion is a constant 600 N.

**(a)** Find the maximum speed of the car on a level road.

**(b)** Find the acceleration of the car on a level road at the instant when its speed is 15 m s⁻¹.

**(c)** The car moves up a hill inclined at θ to the horizontal, where sin θ = 1/20. Find its maximum speed up the hill. **[7]**

**9.** A particle of mass 4 kg is released from rest at the top of a rough plane inclined at 35° to the horizontal. It slides 6 m down a line of greatest slope and reaches the bottom with speed 7 m s⁻¹. Find the work done against friction and the coefficient of friction between the particle and the plane. **[5]**

**10.** A cyclist and bicycle have total mass 90 kg. The cyclist rides up a straight hill inclined at θ to the horizontal, where sin θ = 0.05, against a constant resistance of 25 N.

**(a)** Find the cyclist's power when moving up the hill at a constant 4 m s⁻¹.

**(b)** The cyclist increases the power to 420 W. Find the acceleration at the instant the speed is 4 m s⁻¹. **[5]**

**11.** A child of mass 30 kg slides from rest down a slide. The top of the slide is 4 m above the bottom, and the child reaches the bottom at 6 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the work done against resistance on the slide.

**(b)** At the bottom the child moves onto a horizontal mat, where a constant resistance of 90 N acts. Find the distance the child slides on the mat before stopping. **[5]**

**12.** A car of mass 900 kg accelerates from 10 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹ while travelling 300 m along a level road, taking 20 s. The resistance to motion is a constant 500 N. The driving force is not constant.

**(a)** Find the work done by the car's engine.

**(b)** Find the average power of the engine. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** W = 50 cos 35° × 12 [1] = **491 J** [1]; average power = 491.5 ÷ 8 = **61.4 W** [1] **[3]**

**2. (a)** ½v² = 10 × 3.2 [1]; **v = 8 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** ½v² = 10 × (3.2 − 1.4) = 18, so **v = 6 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the mass cancels in both parts. The track's shape is not needed because only height changes affect potential energy.

**3.** At constant speed, driving force = resistance; D = 18 000 ÷ 30 [1] = **600 N** [1] **[2]**

**4.** ½ × 3 × 2² + 60 = ½ × 3 × v² [1]; v² = 44, **v = 6.63 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**

**5.** Mass per second = 200 ÷ 60 = 10/3 kg [1]; PE per second = (10/3) × 10 × 15 = 500 W [1]; KE per second = ½ × (10/3) × 8² = 106.7 W [1]; power = **607 W** [1] **[4]**

**6.** ½ × 0.4 × 15² = 0.4 × 10 × h, so **h = 11.25 m** [1]; ½v² = ½ × 15² − 10 × 5 = 62.5 [1]; v² = 125, **v = 11.2 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**

**7.** R = 200 − 60 sin 25° = 174.6 N [1]; F = 0.15 × 174.6 = 26.20 N [1]; work by tension = 60 cos 25° × 10 = 543.8 J [1]; work against friction = 262.0 J [1]; ½ × 20 × v² = 543.8 − 262.0 = 281.8 [1]; **v = 5.31 m s⁻¹** [1] **[6]**

**8. (a)** At maximum speed D = 600 N [1]; v = 24 000 ÷ 600 = **40 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** D = 24 000 ÷ 15 = 1600 N [1]; 1600 − 600 = 1200a [1]; **a = 0.833 m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** Weight component = 1200 × 10 × 1/20 = 600 N, so total resistance 1200 N [1]; v = 24 000 ÷ 1200 = **20 m s⁻¹** [1] **[7]**

**9.** PE lost = 4 × 10 × 6 sin 35° = 137.7 J [1]; KE gained = ½ × 4 × 49 = 98 J [1]; work against friction = **39.7 J** [1]; F = 39.66 ÷ 6 = 6.61 N and R = 40 cos 35° = 32.77 N [1]; μ = 6.61 ÷ 32.77 = **0.202** [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** Driving force = 90 × 10 × 0.05 + 25 = 70 N [1]; P = 70 × 4 = **280 W** [1]

**(b)** D = 420 ÷ 4 = 105 N [1]; 105 − 45 − 25 = 90a [1]; **a = 0.389 m s⁻²** [1] **[5]**

**11. (a)** PE lost = 30 × 10 × 4 = 1200 J [1]; KE at bottom = ½ × 30 × 36 = 540 J [1]; work against resistance = **660 J** [1]

**(b)** 540 = 90d [1]; **d = 6 m** [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** KE gained = ½ × 900 × (20² − 10²) = 135 000 J [1]; work against resistance = 500 × 300 = 150 000 J [1]; work by engine = **285 000 J** [1]

**(b)** 285 000 ÷ 20 [1] = **14 250 W (14.3 kW)** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* because the driving force varies, the constant-acceleration formulae cannot be used. The work–energy principle is the only route.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Work done calculated with the full force instead of its component along the motion.
- Distance along a slope used in place of vertical height in mgh.
- The normal reaction taken as mg when a pull has an upward component.
- Power and resultant force combined in P = Fv instead of power and driving force.
- Kilowatts left unconverted in a calculation in newtons and metres per second.
