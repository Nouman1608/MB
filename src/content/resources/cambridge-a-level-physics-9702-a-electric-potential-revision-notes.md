Subtopic 18.5 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the definition of electric potential, the link between field strength and potential gradient, the potential of a point charge and the electric potential energy of two point charges. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Electric potential study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-potential/); to apply the ideas, try the [Electric potential practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-potential-practice/).

## Definition

> Electric potential at a point: the **work done per unit positive charge** in bringing a **small test charge** from **infinity** to the point.

- Unit: volt, V = J C⁻¹.
- Zero at infinity.
- **Scalar**: add with signs, no directions.
- Positive near +Q (work done against repulsion); negative near −Q.

## Equations

```
V = Q / (4πε₀r)                 (data sheet)
E_P = Qq / (4πε₀r) = qV         (data sheet)
E = −ΔV/Δx  (= −dV/dr)          (recall)
W = qΔV                         (work to move q between two points)
point charge only:  E = V/r
```

## Potential versus field strength (point charge)

| | Potential V | Field strength E |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Scalar | Vector |
| Formula | Q/(4πε₀r) | Q/(4πε₀r²) |
| Distance law | 1/r | 1/r² |
| Unit | V (J C⁻¹) | V m⁻¹ (N C⁻¹) |
| Combining charges | Add with signs | Add as vectors |
| Doubling r | Halves | Quarters |

## Gradient relationship

- |E| = gradient of the V–x (or V–r) graph; on a curve, use a **tangent**.
- Minus sign: E points towards **decreasing** potential.
- Positive charges move from high to low V; negative charges from low to high V.
- Uniform field: straight-line V–x graph, constant gradient.
- **Equipotentials:** perpendicular to field lines. Point charge: concentric spheres, wider spacing further out for equal ΔV.
- No work is done moving along an equipotential.

## Zeros are different

| Situation | V = 0? | E = 0? |
|---|---|---|
| Midway between +Q and −Q | Yes | No (fields add towards −Q) |
| Midway between +Q and +Q | No (2 × kQ/r) | Yes |
| At infinity | Yes | Yes |

## Electric potential energy of two charges

| Pair | E_P | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Like charges | Positive | Work done pushing them together; released if they separate |
| Unlike charges | Negative | Bound: work needed to separate them |

**Energy conservation:** ΔE_K = −ΔE_P.

- Closest approach (head-on, target fixed): E_K = Qq/(4πε₀r_min).
- Two equal particles released from rest repel and share the energy equally (equal masses).
- Charge accelerated through ΔV: gain in E_K = |qΔV|.

## Scalar addition in action

Charges of +2.0 nC, +2.0 nC, +2.0 nC and −2.0 nC sit at the corners of a square of side 0.10 m. Each is 0.10/√2 = 0.0707 m from the centre, so:

```
V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (2.0 + 2.0 + 2.0 − 2.0) × 10⁻⁹ / 0.0707 = 510 V
```

No angles or components are needed: the net charge (+4.0 nC) at one distance does the job. Finding E at the same point would need vector addition.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electric potential | Work done per unit positive charge bringing a small test charge from infinity to the point |
| Potential difference | Work done per unit charge moving a charge between two points |
| Potential gradient | Rate of change of potential with distance, ΔV/Δx |
| Equipotential | Surface on which the potential is the same everywhere |
| Electric potential energy | Work done bringing a charge from infinity to its position in the field of another charge |

## Exam traps

- Using r² in V or E_P.
- Treating potentials as vectors.
- Ignoring the sign of a negative charge.
- Saying E = 0 where V = 0.
- Forgetting the minus in E = −dV/dx when stating a direction.
- Leaving "from infinity" or "positive" out of the definition.
- Using E = V/r for anything other than a single point charge.

## Self-test

1. Define electric potential at a point.
2. Why is the potential near a negative charge negative?
3. Calculate V at 0.25 m from +5.0 nC.
4. For Question 3, calculate E at the same point.
5. Equipotentials 2.0 mm apart differ by 5.0 V. Find E.
6. Find E_P for +2.0 μC and −3.0 μC 0.15 m apart.
7. How much work is needed to bring +1.0 nC from infinity to a point where V = 450 V?
8. Where between +6.0 nC and −2.0 nC, 0.40 m apart, is V zero?
9. A V–r graph has tangent gradient −1.2 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ at a point. What is E there?
10. In which direction does an electron move when released in a field: towards higher or lower potential?
11. An electron moves from a point at −200 V to a point at +300 V. Find its gain in kinetic energy.
12. Why is no work done moving a charge along an equipotential?

**Answers:** 1. The work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to the point. 2. The negative charge attracts the positive test charge, so negative work is done by the agent bringing it in. 3. 8.99 × 10⁹ × 5.0 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 0.25 = 180 V. 4. E = V/r = 180 ÷ 0.25 = 720 V m⁻¹. 5. 5.0 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 2.5 × 10³ V m⁻¹. 6. 8.99 × 10⁹ × (−6.0 × 10⁻¹²) ÷ 0.15 = −0.36 J. 7. qV = 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ J. 8. 6.0/x = 2.0/(0.40 − x) → x = 0.30 m from the +6.0 nC charge. 9. 1.2 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹, in the direction of decreasing potential. 10. Towards higher potential. 11. |q|ΔV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 500 = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ J. 12. ΔV = 0, so W = qΔV = 0 (the field is perpendicular to the motion).
