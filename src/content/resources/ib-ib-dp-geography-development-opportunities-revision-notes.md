## Measuring development

**Development** is improvement in people's economic, social and political well-being, not only
growth in income.

| Measure | Strength / limit |
|---|---|
| GNI per capita | Simple, but ignores distribution and non-monetary well-being |
| Human Development Index (HDI) | Combines life expectancy, knowledge (years of schooling) and a decent standard of living (income); dimensions combined with a geometric mean; hides inequality, environmental cost and gender gaps unless adjusted |
| Gini coefficient | Shows how evenly income is shared within a country |
| Other approaches | Gender equality, multidimensional poverty, happiness or well-being |

A country can rise on average income while inequality widens.

## HDI method in outline

Each dimension is converted to an index between 0 and 1 using fixed minimum and maximum values,
and the three indices are combined with a geometric mean. A single figure says nothing about which
regions or groups sit far below it.

## Routes to development, and their costs

| Route | Cost or risk |
|---|---|
| Trade | Exposure to price changes and competition |
| Foreign direct investment | Jobs, but profit outflows |
| Aid | Can help, but may create dependence |
| Remittances | Support households, but may signal skilled emigration |
| Education and skills | Benefit depends on jobs being available |
| Technology transfer | Depends on local capacity |
| Governance | Quality of institutions shapes every other route |

**Spatial inequality:** opportunity is often concentrated in cities and coastal regions. Gender,
ethnicity and age also affect who can take opportunities.

## Exam traps

- Equating development with income growth.
- Quoting HDI as if it shows inequality within a country.
- Listing routes without costs.

## Self-test

1. Name the three HDI dimensions.
2. Why does GNI per capita limit understanding of development?
3. What does the Gini coefficient show?
4. Give one benefit and one cost of foreign direct investment.
5. What is spatial inequality?

**Answers:** 1. Life expectancy, years of schooling and income. 2. It ignores distribution and
non-monetary well-being. 3. How evenly income is shared within a country. 4. Jobs; profit outflows.
5. Opportunity concentrated in some places, such as cities and coasts, leaving others behind.
