These questions cover section 10.1 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: understanding of trend, including the calculation of moving averages, with centring where appropriate. Recall the methods from the [trend study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-understanding-of-trend/) or the [trend revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-understanding-of-trend-revision-notes/). All data are invented. Give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** State the order of moving average you would use for each series.

**(a)** Monthly sales of a shop with a pattern that repeats each year. **[1]**

**(b)** Daily takings of a shop that is open six days a week, with a pattern that repeats each week. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the 3-point moving averages of the series 8, 14, 11, 9, 17, 13, 12, 20, 16. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why 4-point moving averages of quarterly data need to be centred. **[2]**

**4.** The first 4-point moving total of a quarterly series covers Year 1 Q1 to Q4 and is 212. The next is 220.

**(a)** Find the first centred moving average. **[1]**

**(b)** State the quarter against which it is plotted. **[1]**

**5.** Explain the difference between the trend and the seasonal variation of a time series. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A school canteen records the number of meals served each weekday for two weeks.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 210 | 180 | 195 | 200 | 240 |
| Week 2 | 215 | 186 | 200 | 207 | 247 |

**(a)** Explain why 5-point moving averages are appropriate. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the 5-point moving averages. **[4]**

**(c)** Describe the trend. **[2]**

**7.** A company's quarterly profits ($ thousand) for three years are shown.

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 25 | 34 | 40 | 21 |
| Year 2 | 29 | 38 | 43 | 26 |
| Year 3 | 32 | 41 | 48 | 29 |

**(a)** Calculate the 4-point moving totals and moving averages. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the centred moving averages and state the quarter each belongs to. **[3]**

**(c)** Estimate the average increase in the trend per quarter. **[2]**

**8.** A college's enrolments for six consecutive terms (three terms a year) are 50, 35, 41, 53, 38, 44.

**(a)** A student calculates 2-point moving averages. Find the first two, and explain why 2-point averages are not suitable for these data. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the appropriate moving averages. **[2]**

**9.** A trend line is drawn through the centred moving averages of a quarterly series. It passes through 60 at Year 1 Q3 and 74 at Year 3 Q2.

**(a)** Find the increase in the trend per quarter. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate the trend value for Year 4 Q1. **[2]**

**(c)** Give one reason why trend values far beyond the data may be unreliable. **[1]**

**10.** The first four values of a quarterly series are 20, 30, 26 and 20. The next two values, p and q, are missing. The 4-point moving totals are 96, 100 and 104.

**(a)** Find p and q. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the first two centred moving averages. **[2]**

**11.** The centred moving averages of a shop's quarterly sales are 120, 118, 116.5, 115 and 113. A student writes: "Sales go up and down every quarter, so there is no trend." Comment on the student's statement and describe the trend. **[3]**

**12.** A series has 36 monthly values with a yearly seasonal pattern.

**(a)** State how many 12-point moving averages can be calculated, and how many centred values these give. **[2]**

**(b)** State the month against which the first centred value is plotted. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **12-point** [1].

**(b)** **6-point** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** 11.0, 11.3, 12.3, 13.0, 14.0, 15.0, 16.0 [3] (2 marks for five or six correct, 1 mark for at least three correct). **[3]**

**3.** A 4-point average is the middle of four quarters, which falls halfway between the 2nd and 3rd quarters [1]. Averaging neighbouring pairs places each value on an actual quarter so it can be compared with the data [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** (212 + 220) ÷ 8 = **54** [1].

**(b)** **Year 1 Q3** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The trend is the long-term underlying movement of the series [1]. Seasonal variation is the short-term pattern that repeats over each cycle, such as every year [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** The pattern repeats every five days, so each average contains one value for each day of the week and the daily effect cancels [1].

**(b)** Totals 1025, 1030, 1036, 1041, 1048, 1055 [1] [1]. Averages **205, 206, 207.2, 208.2, 209.6, 211** [1], plotted at Week 1 Wednesday to Week 2 Wednesday [1].

**(c)** A steady upward trend [1] of about 1.2 meals per day, from 205 to 211 over five days [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Using running totals (subtract the Monday leaving, add the Monday joining) cuts the arithmetic in half and makes slips visible.

**7. (a)** Totals 120, 124, 128, 131, 136, 139, 142, 147, 150 [1] [1]. Averages 30, 31, 32, 32.75, 34, 34.75, 35.5, 36.75, 37.5 [1].

**(b)** Centred: 30.5, 31.5, 32.375, 33.375, 34.375, 35.125, 36.125, 37.125 [1] [1], from Year 1 Q3 to Year 3 Q2 [1].

**(c)** (37.125 − 30.5) ÷ 7 [1] = about **$950 per quarter** (0.946 thousand) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Eight centred values span seven quarterly steps; dividing by 8 understates the rate.

**8. (a)** (50 + 35) ÷ 2 = **42.5** and (35 + 41) ÷ 2 = **38** [1]. The cycle is three terms [1], so a 2-point average does not contain one of each term and the seasonal pattern remains in the averages, which still zigzag [1].

**(b)** 3-point: **42, 43, 44, 45** [2] (1 mark for two correct). **[5]**

**9. (a)** Year 1 Q3 to Year 3 Q2 is 7 quarters [1]. (74 − 60) ÷ 7 = **2 per quarter** [1].

**(b)** Year 4 Q1 is 3 quarters after Year 3 Q2 [1], so the trend is 74 + 3 × 2 = **80** [1].

**(c)** The trend may change direction or rate in the future, so extending the line assumes the past pattern continues [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** 30 + 26 + 20 + p = 100, so **p = 24** [1]. 26 + 20 + 24 + q = 104 [1], so **q = 34** [1].

**(b)** (96 + 100) ÷ 8 = **24.5** [1] and (100 + 104) ÷ 8 = **25.5** [1]. **[5]**

**11.** The ups and downs in the data are seasonal variation, which the moving averages remove, so the student is wrong [1]. The centred moving averages fall steadily [1], by about 1.75 per quarter, from 120 to 113 over four quarters, so sales show a downward trend [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** 36 − 12 + 1 = **25** moving averages [1], giving **24** centred values [1].

**(b)** **Month 7** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The first 12-point average lies between months 6 and 7 and the second between months 7 and 8, so centring places the first value at month 7.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing an order that does not match the cycle.
- Plotting moving averages at the end rather than the middle of each block.
- Not centring even-order moving averages.
- Dividing by the number of moving averages instead of the number of steps between them when finding a rate.
- Describing the trend from the raw data.
- Giving a direction with no rate or context.
