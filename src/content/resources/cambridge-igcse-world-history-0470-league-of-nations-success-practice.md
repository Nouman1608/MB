These questions cover key question 2.2 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "To what extent was the League of Nations a
success?"

## Section A

**1.** Describe the organisation of the League of Nations. **[4]**

**2.** Name **three** major powers that were not members of the League in 1925. **[3]**

**3.** Describe the League's handling of the dispute over the Aaland Islands. **[4]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which the League helped refugees. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **three** reasons why sanctions against Italy failed in 1935-36. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the events of the Corfu crisis of 1923. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why was the League able to solve some disputes in the 1920s but not others? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did the League fail to stop Japan in Manchuria? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Weaknesses in its organisation and membership made the failure of the League inevitable." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The League's humanitarian work was more important than its peacekeeping." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a speech by a delegate of a small European state to the League Assembly, 1936. "When
Bulgaria was invaded, the League acted within days. When Abyssinia was invaded, the League debated for months, and
oil flowed to the invader. We small nations have learned that the Covenant protects us only from each other."

What is the delegate's message in Source A? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary cartoon of 1932, written for this page. A Japanese soldier
marches into a building labelled "Manchuria" while a League official, holding a thick report, taps him politely on
the shoulder. In the background, Britain and France look at their watches.

How far does Source B show the reasons for the League's failure over Manchuria? Use the source and your knowledge to
explain your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: the Assembly of all members met once a year (1); decisions had to be unanimous (1); the
Council met more often, with permanent members such as Britain and France (1); the Council could impose sanctions (1);
the Secretariat ran the administration (1); the Permanent Court of International Justice heard legal disputes (1);
the ILO dealt with working conditions (1); commissions dealt with mandates, refugees, health and slavery (1).
**[4]**

**2.** 1 mark each: the USA (1); Germany, which joined only in 1926 (1); the USSR (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: Sweden and Finland both claimed the islands (1); most islanders spoke Swedish (1); the
dispute was referred to the League (1); the League investigated (1); it ruled that the islands should belong to
Finland (1); they were to be demilitarised (1); the islanders' Swedish language and self-government were protected
(1); Sweden accepted the decision, avoiding war (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: helped return hundreds of thousands of prisoners of war after the First World War (1);
Nansen passports gave identity papers to stateless refugees (1); organised camps and aid for refugees, for example
after the war between Greece and Turkey (1); helped refugees find work and homes (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: oil was not included (1); the Suez Canal was not closed to Italian ships (1); the USA was
not a member and kept trading (1); Britain and France feared war with Italy (1); they wanted Mussolini as an ally
against Hitler (1); the Hoare-Laval Pact undermined the League's position (1); sanctions were applied slowly (1).
**[3]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: an Italian general, Tellini, was killed in August 1923 while mapping the Greek-Albanian
border (1); Mussolini blamed Greece (1); he demanded compensation and an apology (1); Italy bombarded and occupied
Corfu (1); Greece appealed to the League (1); the Conference of Ambassadors decided the case (1); Greece had to
apologise and pay compensation to Italy (1); Italy then withdrew from Corfu (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **small states complied**: Sweden accepted the Aaland ruling and Greece withdrew from Bulgaria in 1925
because they could not resist great-power pressure; **great powers could defy it**: Mussolini used force at Corfu and
gained compensation; **Britain and France's interests**: France would not act against its ally Poland over Vilna;
**no army**, so the League relied on persuasion, which worked only when the parties were willing. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **distance**: Manchuria was far from Europe, and Britain and France
were unwilling to send forces; **Japan was a great power** and permanent Council member; **the Depression** made members
unwilling to impose trade sanctions; **no US membership**, so sanctions without America, Japan's major trading partner,
would be weak; **slowness**: the Lytton Commission took a year to report, by which time Manchukuo existed; Japan simply
left the League in 1933. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content. **Agree:**
no army; unanimity made decisions slow; no USA, so sanctions were weak; Germany and the USSR absent for long periods;
reliance on Britain and France. **Disagree:** the same structure solved the Aaland and Bulgaria disputes; failure came
in the 1930s because of the Depression and aggressive powers, not organisation alone; Britain and France chose not to
act in Abyssinia, for example by excluding oil; humanitarian work succeeded. **Judgement:** for example, weaknesses made
failure likely against great powers, but it was not inevitable until circumstances changed after 1929. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** refugee work, Nansen passports and the return of
prisoners of war; the fight against epidemics; the Slavery Convention; ILO standards; much of this work continued
through the 1930s and passed to the United Nations. **Disagree:** the League's main purpose was to prevent war; it
succeeded in the Aaland Islands and Bulgaria, preventing small wars; its failure in Manchuria and Abyssinia
contributed to the collapse of peace; humanitarian work was limited where members refused to adopt ILO standards.
**Judgement:** for example, judged by lives improved, humanitarian work mattered more; judged by its own main aim,
peacekeeping was what counted. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the message in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4):
explains the message using the source only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative content: the message is
that **the League treated small and great powers differently**: it acted quickly against Greece in 1925 ("within
days") but slowly and weakly against Italy ("debated for months", "oil flowed"); so collective security protected
small nations only "from each other", not from great powers. Knowledge: Greece withdrew from Bulgaria in 1925; Italy
invaded Abyssinia in October 1935; sanctions excluded oil; Italy conquered Abyssinia and sanctions were dropped in
1936. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains how far, using the source's details and knowledge of other reasons. Level 2
(3-4): explains reasons shown by the source only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative content: **shown**:
the League's **weakness and politeness** (the tap on the shoulder) against Japanese force; **slowness** (the thick
report, the Lytton Commission); **Britain and France's lack of commitment** (looking at their watches). **Not shown**:
the absence of the USA; the impact of the Depression on members' willingness to impose sanctions; distance; Japan's
status as a permanent Council member; Japan's departure in 1933. **Judgement:** it shows several important reasons but
not all of them. **[6]**
