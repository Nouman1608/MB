Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **8.4 Transition elements**. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Transition elements study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-transition-elements/).

## The five properties (learn as a list)

Transition elements are metals that:

1. have **high densities**
2. have **high melting points**
3. have **variable oxidation numbers**
4. form **coloured compounds**
5. often act as **catalysts**, both as elements and in compounds

Mnemonic: **D**ense, **M**elt high, **V**ariable, **C**oloured, **C**atalyst.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transition element | A metal in the central block, between Group II and Group III |
| Oxidation number | The charge an atom would have if its compound were fully ionic; shown by a Roman numeral in names |
| Variable oxidation number | The ability to form compounds with the metal in more than one oxidation state |
| Catalyst | Increases the rate of a reaction by lowering the activation energy; not used up |
| Coloured compound | A compound (or its solution) that is not white or colourless |

## Transition element vs Group I metal

| Property | Transition element (e.g. iron) | Group I metal (e.g. sodium) |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | High (iron 1538 °C) | Low (sodium 98 °C) |
| Density | High (iron 7.87 g cm⁻³) | Low (sodium 0.97 g cm⁻³) |
| Oxidation numbers | More than one (+2, +3 for iron) | Only +1 |
| Colour of compounds | Usually coloured | White / colourless solutions |
| Catalytic activity | Often catalysts | Not used as catalysts |
| Reaction with cold water | Very slow (iron rusts) | Vigorous |

## Oxidation numbers: the method

- Oxygen in compounds = −2; the total for a compound = 0; for an ion = its charge.
- FeO: Fe = +2 → iron(II) oxide. Fe₂O₃: 2Fe = +6 → Fe = +3 → iron(III) oxide.
- Cu₂O: 2Cu = +2 → Cu = +1 → copper(I) oxide. CuO → copper(II) oxide.
- MnO₂: Mn = +4. MnO₄⁻: Mn + (−8) = −1 → Mn = +7 → manganate(VII).
- V₂O₅: 2V = +10 → V = +5 → vanadium(V) oxide.
- Cr₂O₃: Cr = +3 → chromium(III) oxide.

## Colours to recall

| Substance | Colour |
|---|---|
| Hydrated copper(II) sulfate | Blue |
| Fe²⁺(aq) | Pale green |
| Fe³⁺(aq) | Yellow-brown |
| KMnO₄(aq) | Purple |
| CuO | Black |
| Cu²⁺ + NaOH(aq) | Light blue precipitate |
| Fe²⁺ + NaOH(aq) | Green precipitate (turns brown near surface on standing) |
| Fe³⁺ + NaOH(aq) | Red-brown precipitate |
| Cr³⁺ + NaOH(aq) | Green precipitate |

Zinc, aluminium and calcium ions give **white** precipitates: no transition-element colour.

## Catalyst examples from 5070

| Process | Catalyst | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Haber process (ammonia) | Iron | Element |
| Hydrogenation of alkenes | Nickel | Element |
| Contact process (SO₂ → SO₃) | Vanadium(V) oxide | Compound |

The syllabus wording "as elements and in compounds" means you should be able to give one example of each.

## Model answer: "Describe the transition elements" [4]

"Transition elements are metals with high densities [1] and high melting points [1]. They have variable oxidation numbers, for example iron(II) and iron(III) [1], and they form coloured compounds, such as blue copper(II) sulfate [1]. They often act as catalysts, for example iron in the Haber process."

Five properties are offered for four marks: the extra one protects against a slip. Each property is stated in the syllabus wording, and two are backed by an example.

## Where else these properties are tested

- **Subtopic 6.4 Redox**: Roman numerals in names and calculating oxidation numbers.
- **Subtopics 6.2 and 6.3 (rate of reaction; reversible reactions and equilibrium)**: iron and vanadium(V) oxide as catalysts in the Haber and Contact processes.
- **Topic 11 Organic chemistry**: nickel as the catalyst for adding hydrogen to alkenes.
- **Topic 12 Identification of ions**: coloured precipitates of Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ and Cr³⁺ with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

## Exam traps

- Say "high density" and "high melting point", not "strong" or "hard".
- It is the **compounds** that are coloured, not the metals.
- "Often" act as catalysts: not every reaction of a transition metal is catalysed.
- The Roman numeral is the oxidation number, not the number of atoms: iron(III) oxide is Fe₂O₃.
- Do not choose zinc to illustrate colour or variable oxidation number.
- In a comparison question, use comparatives against a named metal ("higher than sodium").
- Vanadium(V) oxide is a compound catalyst; iron in the Haber process is an element catalyst.

## Self-test

1. List the five properties of transition elements in the 5070 syllabus.
2. State two properties in which iron differs from sodium.
3. Deduce the oxidation number of iron in Fe₂O₃.
4. Name the compound Cu₂O.
5. Calculate the oxidation number of manganese in MnO₄⁻.
6. Give the colour of the precipitate when aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to a solution of iron(III) ions.
7. Name a transition element that acts as a catalyst as the element, and the process.
8. Name a transition-element compound that acts as a catalyst, and the process.
9. A solution is pale green. Which transition-metal ion might it contain?
10. Explain how iron(II) sulfate and iron(III) sulfate show that iron has variable oxidation numbers.
11. Why is zinc a poor example of a typical transition element at this level?
12. State what a catalyst does to the activation energy of a reaction.
