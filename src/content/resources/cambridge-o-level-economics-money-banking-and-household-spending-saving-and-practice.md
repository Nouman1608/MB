## Questions

**1.** Define money. **[2]**

**2.** Explain two functions of money, giving an example of each. **[4]**

**3.** A village with 8 goods uses barter.

**(a)** Calculate how many exchange rates it needs if every pair of goods has its own rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one other problem of barter that money solves. **[2]**

**4.** A community proposes using rare seashells as money. Assess the idea using two characteristics of good money. **[4]**

**5.** In Country A the price index rises from 100 to 160.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of its original purchasing power that money keeps. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what this means for money as a store of value. **[2]**

**6.** Explain two roles of a central bank. **[4]**

**7.** Explain why commercial banks are important to:
**(a)** producers **[2]**
**(b)** consumers **[2]**
**(c)** the government **[2]**

**8.** A small bank has deposits of 500,000 units. It lends 60 per cent at 10 per cent interest and pays depositors 4 per cent on all deposits.
**(a)** Calculate its gross interest margin. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why it does not lend all its deposits. **[1]**

**9.** Household A has a disposable income of 5,000 a month and spends 4,000. Household B has 1,500 and spends 1,440.
**(a)** Calculate the saving ratio of each household. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons why their saving ratios may differ. **[4]**

**10.** Ali borrows 7,200 units on a flat-rate loan repaid over 3 years in 36 equal monthly instalments.
**(a)** At 9 per cent a year, calculate the total repaid and the monthly instalment. **[3]**
**(b)** The rate is 12 per cent instead. Calculate the extra paid each month and in total. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how a higher rate may affect a household's decision to borrow. **[3]**

**11.** A household saves 4,000 units for 2 years at 3 per cent a year.
**(a)** Calculate the interest earned with simple interest and with compound interest. **[3]**
**(b)** If the rate is 6 per cent and inflation 2 per cent, estimate the real rate. **[2]**

**12.** In Country B a household's disposable income falls from 3,000 to 2,400 a month and its spending from 2,700 to 2,280. The central bank then cuts interest rates.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in income and in spending, the new saving, and the saving ratios before and after. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether the rate cut will increase this household's spending. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Anything generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services [1] and in settlement of debts [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns more when it states acceptance as payment; "cash" or "notes and coins" alone leaves out bank deposits.

**2.** Medium of exchange: accepted in payment [1], e.g. a worker is paid wages in money and buys food [1]. Unit of account: prices and wages are measured in it [1], e.g. a shirt priced at 20 units is easy to compare with a haircut at 8 [1]. (Store of value or standard of deferred payment also gain credit.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For each function, a one-line meaning plus a short example is the safest way to show you understand it, not just name it.

**3. (a)** Pairs = 8 × 7 ÷ 2 [1] = **28** exchange rates [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Barter needs a double coincidence of wants [1]: a seller must want what the buyer offers at the same time, but with money the seller accepts it and spends it later [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula or the pairs working, since a bare number gives the marker nothing to reward if it is wrong. State the barter problem and then say how money solves it.

**4.** Scarcity: they are limited in supply, so they keep value [1], but if shells can be gathered freely the supply may rise and value fall [1]. Divisibility or portability: small shells are light and easy to carry [1], but they cannot be split to give exact change and are not uniform in size, so some are worth more [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An assessment answer gives a point in favour and a point against for each characteristic; one-sided answers show only part of the thinking.

**5. (a)** 100 ÷ 160 × 100 [1] = **62.5 per cent** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Money now buys only 62.5 per cent as much, a 37.5 per cent fall [1], so savings lose purchasing power and it works less well as a store of value [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mixing up the 60 per cent price rise with the loss of purchasing power is common; the second is found by dividing, not by repeating the 60.

**6.** Banker to the government: holds its accounts and handles its borrowing and payments [1], so the state can pay for its spending [1]. Lender of last resort: lends to a sound bank short of cash [1], preventing panic that would hurt depositors and the economy [1]. (Issuing notes and coins, carrying out monetary policy and supervising banks also gain credit.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a role earns little by itself; add a reason it matters, because the question asks for explanation.

**7. (a)** Banks give loans and overdrafts [1], so firms can buy equipment or cover a gap before customers pay [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Banks provide safe places to save and a way to pay [1] and lend through mortgages and loans so households can spend ahead of income [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Banks lend to the government by buying its debt [1] and help collect and move tax and benefit payments [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each part needs a point specific to that group; a general sentence about "providing loans" used three times will not fit all three.

**8. (a)** Lent: 500,000 × 0.60 = 300,000 [1]. Interest received: 300,000 × 0.10 = 30,000 [1]. Interest paid: 500,000 × 0.04 = 20,000 [1]. Margin: 30,000 − 20,000 = **10,000 units** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** It must keep part back to meet withdrawals [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The bank pays interest on all deposits but earns it only on the part lent; applying 4 per cent to only the lent part is a common slip.

**9. (a)** A: saving 5,000 − 4,000 = 1,000 [1]; 1,000 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = **20 per cent** [1]. B: 1,500 − 1,440 = 60 [1]; 60 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = **4 per cent** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Income: A's income is higher, so after essentials more is left to save [1], while B must spend nearly all its income on necessities [1]. Confidence or debts: B may be repaying debt or fear job loss, which affects its choice [1], while A, secure about future income, may save for a goal [1]. (Other valid factors: dependants, age, attitude to risk.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison answer links the factor to both households; saying "A earns more" gains credit only when you explain why that changes saving.

**10. (a)** Interest 7,200 × 0.09 × 3 = 1,944 [1]. Total 7,200 + 1,944 = **9,144** [1]. Instalment 9,144 ÷ 36 = **254** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** At 12 per cent: interest 7,200 × 0.12 × 3 = 2,592 and total 9,792 [1]. Instalment 9,792 ÷ 36 = 272, so **18 extra a month** [1]. Total extra 9,792 − 9,144 = **648** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** A higher rate raises the cost of borrowing [1], so repayments take more of income [1], and households may borrow less or delay purchases; the effect is smaller if the purchase is a necessity or confidence is high [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An extension or evaluative remark, such as how strongly the change affects a household, helps lift a developed answer beyond a simple cost statement.

**11. (a)** Simple: 4,000 × 0.03 × 2 = **240** [1]. Compound: 4,000 × 1.03² = 4,243.60 [1], so interest **243.60** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Real rate is about nominal minus inflation [1] = 6 − 2 = **4 per cent** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Compound questions need the final amount minus the original sum; giving 4,243.60 as the "interest" is the usual error.

**12. (a)** Income: (2,400 − 3,000) ÷ 3,000 = **−20 per cent**; spending: (2,280 − 2,700) ÷ 2,700 = **−15.6 per cent** [1]. Saving before 3,000 − 2,700 = 300 [1]; after 2,400 − 2,280 = 120 [1]. Saving ratio **10 per cent before, 5 per cent after** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** A lower rate makes borrowing cheaper and saving less rewarding [1], so spending may rise [1]. But income has fallen 20 per cent and the household is saving less [1], so it may borrow to keep its spending up or cut it further [1]. Confidence could be low after the fall in income, so it may not borrow [1]. Judgement: the rate cut helps only if confidence and income recover; with income falling, I expect the effect to be small, but it would be larger if lenders are keen [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation reaches a judgement and gives the reason behind it, such as which factor is likely to outweigh the others for this household.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing only "cash" for money and leaving out bank deposits.
- Naming a function or role with no explanation or example.
- Confusing the central bank with a commercial bank.
- Not showing working for percentages, ratios and interest.
- Mixing up a price rise of 60 per cent with the loss of purchasing power it causes.
- Giving only one side in a question that says assess or evaluate.
- Stating that higher interest rates always reduce spending, without mentioning confidence, debt or income.
- Giving general points on banks without tying them to the group named.
