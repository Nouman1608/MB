## Section A

**1.** Name **two** anhydrous compounds that can be used in a chemical test for water. **[2]**

**2.** Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride paper is dipped into a liquid.

**(a)** State the colour of the paper before it is dipped. **[1]**

**(b)** State the colour of the paper if the liquid contains water. **[1]**

**3.** Which statement about anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is correct?

A: It turns blue only with pure water.

B: It turns blue with any liquid that contains water.

C: It turns white when water is added.

D: It shows the boiling point of water.

**[1]**

**4.** State **one** substance in natural water that is beneficial and give the reason. **[2]**

**5.** Name the stage in the treatment of the domestic water supply that:

**(a)** kills harmful microbes **[1]**

**(b)** removes tastes and odours **[1]**

**(c)** allows large insoluble particles to settle out. **[1]**

**6.** Phosphates in rivers come from two sources. Name **both** sources. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Three liquids, X, Y and Z, were tested. The results are shown.

| Liquid | Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| X | turns blue | 100 |
| Y | turns blue | 102 |
| Z | stays white | 78 |

**(a)** State which liquid contains no water. **[1]**

**(b)** State which liquid is pure water. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what liquid Y could be and explain its boiling point. **[2]**

**8.** A student uses tap water instead of distilled water to dissolve a sample when testing it for chloride ions with acidified aqueous silver nitrate. A white precipitate forms.

**(a)** Explain why the student cannot conclude that the sample contains chloride ions. **[2]**

**(b)** State why distilled water would give a more reliable result. **[1]**

**9.** Water from a river contains sand, mud, harmful microbes and dissolved metal compounds.

**(a)** Describe how sedimentation and filtration treat this water. **[2]**

**(b)** State which of the substances listed are **not** removed by sedimentation and filtration. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why chlorine is added to the water. **[1]**

**10.** Nitrates enter a lake from nearby farmland.

**(a)** State where the nitrates come from. **[1]**

**(b)** State the effect of large amounts of nitrates on the water and on aquatic life. **[2]**

**11.** Some substances found in natural water can be both beneficial and harmful. Use metal compounds as an example to explain this statement. **[2]**

**12.** A sample of seawater is frozen slowly. It begins to freeze at −2 °C.

**(a)** Explain what this freezing point shows about the seawater. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the boiling point of the seawater would compare with that of pure water. **[1]**

**13.** Describe the treatment of the domestic water supply, naming each stage and what it removes or does. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate [1]; anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Blue [1]

**(b)** Pink [1]

**[2]**

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A is the most common wrong choice. The test detects water, whatever else is dissolved in it.

**4.** Dissolved oxygen [1], needed by fish and other aquatic life (for respiration) [1]. **or** Some metal compounds [1], which provide essential minerals for life [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Chlorination [1]

**(b)** Treatment with carbon [1]

**(c)** Sedimentation [1]

**[3]**

**6.** Fertilisers [1]; detergents [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Z [1]

**(b)** X [1]. It contains water and boils at exactly 100 °C, the boiling point of pure water [1].

**(c)** A solution of a substance in water, such as salt water [1]; the dissolved substance raises the boiling point above 100 °C [1].

**[5]**

**8. (a)** Tap water contains dissolved chemical impurities, which can include chloride ions [1]; these could form the white precipitate with silver nitrate, so the precipitate may not come from the sample [1].

**(b)** Distilled water contains fewer chemical impurities, so any precipitate comes from the sample [1].

**[3]**

**9. (a)** Sedimentation: the water stands so that heavy solids such as sand settle to the bottom [1]. Filtration: the water passes through sand and gravel beds, which trap smaller insoluble particles such as mud [1].

**(b)** Harmful microbes [1]; dissolved metal compounds [1].

**(c)** To kill harmful microbes [1].

**[5]**

**10. (a)** Fertilisers (washed off the land) [1]

**(b)** They lead to deoxygenation of the water (loss of dissolved oxygen) [1], which damages or kills aquatic life [1].

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the syllabus does not require the stages of eutrophication. Credit is for the effect: deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life.

**11.** Some metal compounds provide essential minerals for life, so they are beneficial [1]; other metal compounds are toxic, so they are harmful [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** Pure water freezes at 0 °C [1]; a lower freezing point shows the seawater is impure / contains dissolved substances [1].

**(b)** Higher than 100 °C [1].

**[3]**

**13.** One mark per stage matched to its purpose, up to 4:

- sedimentation: large insoluble solids settle out [1]
- filtration (through sand and gravel): removes smaller insoluble solids [1]
- carbon: removes tastes and odours [1]
- chlorination: kills harmful microbes [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* a stage named without its purpose scores nothing. "Filtered to make it clean" does not say what is removed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that cobalt(II) chloride turns blue in water.
- Treating a positive copper(II) sulfate test as proof of purity.
- Saying impurities lower the boiling point.
- Claiming that filtration removes microbes or dissolved substances.
- Giving "tap water is dirty" instead of naming dissolved impurities and their effect on a result.
