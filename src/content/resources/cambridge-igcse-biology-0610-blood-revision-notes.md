These notes condense sub-topic **9.4 Blood** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core; outcomes 5 to 7 are **Extended only**.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [blood study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood/). Then use the [blood practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-practice/). The [transport in animals revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-animals-revision-notes/) cover the whole topic.

## Components (Core)

Four, by name: **red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, plasma**.

## Functions (Core)

| Component | Function in syllabus words |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | transport oxygen; contain **haemoglobin**, which combines with oxygen in the lungs and releases it in respiring tissues |
| White blood cells | **phagocytosis** and **antibody production** |
| Platelets | clotting (details not required at Core) |
| Plasma | transport of blood cells, ions, nutrients, urea, hormones and carbon dioxide |

Plasma list memory aid: **C**ells, **I**ons, **N**utrients, **U**rea, **H**ormones, **C**arbon dioxide. Oxygen is not on it.

## Plasma: from and to

| Substance | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose, amino acids | small intestine | all cells |
| Urea | liver | kidneys |
| Carbon dioxide | respiring cells | lungs |
| Hormones | endocrine glands | target organs |

## Identifying cells

| | Red blood cell | White blood cell | Platelet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | none | yes | none (fragment) |
| Size | medium | larger | very small |
| Number | very many | few | scattered |

**Extended only:**

| | Lymphocyte | Phagocyte |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | large, round, fills most of cell | lobed |
| Cytoplasm | thin rim | plenty |
| Function | antibody production | engulfing pathogens by phagocytosis |

Routine: nucleus? → no = red cell; yes = white cell → round and filling cell = lymphocyte; lobed = phagocyte.

## Clotting

**Roles (Core):**

1. Prevents blood loss.
2. Prevents entry of pathogens.

**Process (Extended only):**

```
vessel damaged → fibrinogen (soluble) → fibrin (insoluble) → mesh traps blood cells → clot
```

## Applying the functions

- Fewer red cells → less haemoglobin → less oxygen to tissues → less aerobic respiration → tiredness.
- Fewer white cells → less phagocytosis and fewer antibodies → more infections.
- Fewer platelets → slower clotting → more blood loss and more chance of pathogens entering.

## Worked reminders

**Cell count ratio.** One field of view shows 360 red cells and 3 white cells. Ratio = 360 ÷ 3 = 120, so **120 : 1**. Count several fields and take a mean, because white cells are few and vary by chance between fields.

**Describe clotting in three marks (Extended).**

1. Soluble fibrinogen in the plasma...
2. ...is converted to insoluble fibrin...
3. ...which forms a mesh that traps blood cells to make a clot.

**State a red blood cell function in full.** "Transports oxygen" earns one mark; add "using haemoglobin, which combines with oxygen in the lungs and releases it in respiring tissues" for the explanation marks.

**Suggest frame.** Component changed → its function reduced → effect on cells or tissues → symptom. Every arrow is usually a mark.

## Command words

- **List**: the four names only.
- **Identify**: name the cell, then the feature you used, for example "no nucleus".
- **State**: one precise function per component.
- **Describe** (Extended clotting): the two proteins and the mesh, in order.
- **Suggest**: apply a function to an unfamiliar situation; more than one answer can be credited.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Plasma | Liquid part of blood that transports cells and dissolved substances |
| Haemoglobin | Protein in red blood cells that combines with oxygen |
| Phagocytosis | Engulfing and digesting pathogens |
| Antibody | Protein made by lymphocytes that acts against a specific pathogen |
| Platelet | Cell fragment involved in clotting |
| Lymphocyte | White cell with a large round nucleus; produces antibodies (Extended) |
| Phagocyte | White cell with a lobed nucleus; engulfs pathogens (Extended) |
| Fibrinogen | Soluble plasma protein converted to fibrin during clotting (Extended) |
| Fibrin | Insoluble protein that forms the mesh of a clot (Extended) |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Red vs white cells:** no nucleus and very many, compared with a nucleus and few.
- **Haemoglobin vs plasma:** haemoglobin carries oxygen; plasma carries carbon dioxide, urea, nutrients, ions and hormones.
- **Lymphocyte vs phagocyte (Extended):** antibodies compared with engulfing; round nucleus compared with lobed nucleus.
- **Fibrinogen vs fibrin (Extended):** soluble starting protein compared with insoluble mesh-forming product.
- **Roles vs process of clotting:** roles (Core) say why clotting matters; process (Extended) says how it happens.

## Exam traps

- Listing "water" or "serum" as a component; give the four syllabus names.
- Plasma said to carry oxygen.
- Red blood cells given a nucleus.
- "White cells fight disease" with no named process.
- Lymphocyte and phagocyte functions swapped.
- Fibrin and fibrinogen in the wrong order.
- Only one role of clotting given when two are asked for.

## Self-test

1. List the four components of blood.
2. Name the substance in red blood cells that combines with oxygen.
3. State the function of platelets.
4. Name four substances, other than blood cells, transported in plasma.
5. Where is urea carried from and to in the plasma?
6. How can you tell a white blood cell from a red blood cell in a photomicrograph?
7. State the two roles of blood clotting.
8. Suggest one effect of having fewer red blood cells than normal.
9. (Extended) Describe the nucleus of a lymphocyte.
10. (Extended) State the function of a phagocyte.
11. (Extended) Name the soluble protein that is converted during clotting, and the product.
12. (Extended) What does fibrin form, and what does it trap?

### Answers

1. Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets and plasma.
2. Haemoglobin.
3. Clotting.
4. Any four of: ions, nutrients (such as glucose), urea, hormones, carbon dioxide.
5. From the liver to the kidneys.
6. White blood cells have a nucleus (and are larger and fewer); red blood cells have no nucleus.
7. Preventing blood loss and preventing the entry of pathogens.
8. Less oxygen carried to tissues, so less aerobic respiration and tiredness.
9. Large and round, filling most of the cell.
10. Engulfing pathogens by phagocytosis.
11. Fibrinogen is converted to fibrin.
12. A mesh; it traps blood cells to form a clot.
