## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Where is interest on a loan made by a partner to the partnership recorded?

A: deducted in the appropriation account

B: as an expense in the statement of profit or loss

C: in the partner's capital account only

D: added in the appropriation account

**[1]**

**2.** A partnership's profit for the year is 40,000. Interest on drawings is 600,
partners' salaries 8,000 and interest on capital 4,000. What is the balance of
profit to be shared?

A: 27,400

B: 28,600

C: 29,800

D: 32,000

**[1]**

**3.** State three items usually included in a partnership agreement. **[3]**

**4.** Explain one difference between a partner's capital account and current
account. **[2]**

**5.** On 1 April 2028 a partner lent the partnership 8,000 at 5% a year. The year
end is 31 December 2028. Calculate the interest for 2028 and state where it is
recorded. **[2]**

**6.** State two disadvantages of forming a partnership. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Lim and Okoro are surveyors in partnership. Their agreement provides for
interest on capital at 4% a year, a salary of 12,000 to Okoro, and the balance of
profit to be shared equally. Fixed capitals are Lim 50,000 and Okoro 30,000.
Interest on drawings for 2028 is Lim 450 and Okoro 300. Profit for the year ended
31 December 2028 was 64,000. On 1 January 2028 the current accounts had credit
balances of Lim 1,800 and Okoro 950. Drawings were Lim 26,000 and Okoro 30,400.

**(a)** Prepare the appropriation account for the year ended 31 December 2028. **[6]**

**(b)** Prepare the partners' current accounts in columnar form. **[6]**

**8.** Patel and Quinn share profits and losses 2:1. Interest on capital is
Patel 4,500 and Quinn 2,500, and Quinn receives a salary of 10,000. Profit for
the year was 14,600.

**(a)** Prepare the appropriation account. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the total amount credited, net of any share of loss, to each
partner's current account. **[2]**

**9.** A partnership's bookkeeper calculated profit for the year as 38,400 and
then deducted interest of 700 on a partner's loan in the appropriation account.

**(a)** Calculate the correct profit for the year. **[1]**

**(b)** State the effect of the correction on the balance of profit shared in the
profit-sharing ratio. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why interest on a partner's loan is treated as an expense. **[2]**

**10.** Ng and Ford's balances at 31 December 2028 are: capital accounts Ng 45,000
and Ford 35,000; current accounts Ng 3,600 credit and Ford 1,150 debit; loan from
Ng, repayable in 2032, 12,000. Prepare the capital section of the statement of
financial position and show how the loan is presented. **[4]**

**11.** Mei runs a web design business as a sole trader. Jon has offered to join
her as a partner, bringing 30,000 of capital and his skills in app development.
Advise Mei whether she should form the partnership. **[4]**

**12.** Explain the purpose of the appropriation account and why a partner's salary
is shown there rather than with wages. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. The partner is acting as a lender. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. 40,000 + 600 − 8,000 − 4,000 = 28,600. **[1]**

**3.** Any three from: capital to be contributed; profit- and loss-sharing ratio;
interest on capital; interest on drawings; salaries; interest on partners' loans;
limits on drawings [1] [1] [1]. **[3]**

**4.** The capital account records the partner's long-term investment and is
usually fixed [1], whereas the current account records the partner's share of
profit, salary and interest less drawings, and changes every year [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 8,000 × 5% × 9/12 = **300** [1], recorded as an expense in the statement of
profit or loss and credited to the partner's current (or loan) account [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two from: unlimited liability; profits must be shared; decisions
take longer; disagreements between partners; each partner is bound by the
others' business decisions [1] [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

```
Appropriation account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Profit for the year                                         64,000
Add interest on drawings:  Lim                   450
                           Okoro                 300           750
                                                            64,750
Less salary:               Okoro                            12,000
Less interest on capital:  Lim                 2,000
                           Okoro               1,200         3,200
                                                            49,550
Balance of profit shared:  Lim (1/2)          24,775
                           Okoro (1/2)        24,775        49,550
```

Interest on drawings added, 750 [1]; salary 12,000 [1]; interest on capital
2,000 and 1,200 [1]; balance 49,550 [1]; shares 24,775 each [1]; heading and
separate partner figures [1].

**(b)**

```
Current accounts ($)
                        Lim     Okoro                          Lim    Okoro
Drawings             26,000    30,400  Balance b/d           1,800      950
Interest on drawings    450       300  Salary                    –   12,000
Balance c/d           2,125     8,225  Interest on capital   2,000    1,200
                                       Share of profit      24,775   24,775
                     ------    ------                       ------   ------
                     28,575    38,925                       28,575   38,925
                     ======    ======                       ======   ======
                                       Balance b/d           2,125    8,225
```

Opening balances [1]; drawings [1]; interest on drawings on the debit side [1];
salary and interest on capital [1]; share of profit [1]; closing balances
2,125 and 8,225 [1]. **[12]**

**8. (a)**

```
Profit for the year                                  14,600
Less salary:              Quinn                     10,000
Less interest on capital: Patel           4,500
                          Quinn           2,500      7,000
Balance: loss                                       (2,400)
Shared:                   Patel (2/3)    (1,600)
                          Quinn (1/3)      (800)    (2,400)
```

Salary and interest on capital deducted [1]; residual loss 2,400 [1]; Patel
1,600 [1]; Quinn 800 [1].

**(b)** Patel: 4,500 − 1,600 = **2,900** [1]. Quinn: 10,000 + 2,500 − 800
= **11,700** [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** 38,400 − 700 = **37,700** [1].

**(b)** No effect: the 700 is now deducted before profit for the year instead of
in the appropriation account, so the balance shared is the same [1].

**(c)** The partner has acted as a lender, not as an owner investing capital [1];
the business would have to pay interest to any lender, so it is a cost of running
the business and must be deducted to find profit [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

```
Capital accounts:    Ng        45,000
                     Ford      35,000          80,000
Current accounts:    Ng         3,600
                     Ford      (1,150)          2,450
                                               82,450
Non-current liabilities
   Loan from Ng                                12,000
```

Capital accounts 80,000 [1]; Ford's debit balance shown as a deduction [1];
total 82,450 [1]; loan shown as a non-current liability, not in capital [1]. **[4]**

**11.** In favour: Jon's 30,000 gives more capital without borrowing [1], and
his app skills let the business offer new services and share the workload [1].
Against: Mei would share profits and decisions, and with unlimited liability she
could be responsible for business debts Jon incurs [1]. Conclusion: she should
go ahead if a written partnership agreement sets out capital, profit sharing and
rewards, because the extra capital and skills are likely to raise profit [1]. **[4]**

**12.** The appropriation account shows how profit for the year is divided among
the partners under the partnership agreement [1]. A partner is an owner, not an
employee [1], so a salary is a share of profit, and including it in wages would
understate the profit made by the business [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating interest on drawings as a deduction.
- Putting interest on a partner's loan in the appropriation account.
- Placing a debit current account balance on the wrong side.
- Including a partner's loan in the capital section.
- Sharing a residual loss in a different ratio from profit.
