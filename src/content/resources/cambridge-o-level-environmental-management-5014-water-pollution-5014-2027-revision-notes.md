Condensed notes for sub-topic 3.2 Water pollution in the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus for 2027 to 2029. For full explanations and worked data examples, see the [water pollution study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-water-pollution-5014-2027/); to apply the content, try the [water pollution practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-water-pollution-5014-2027-practice/).

## The sub-topic in three lists

**3.2.1 Sources (5)**: domestic waste; sewage; plastic waste; industrial processes; agricultural practices.

**3.2.2 Impacts (6)**: cholera risk; accumulation of industrial toxins; bioaccumulation; biomagnification; acid rain effects on organisms in rivers and lakes; leaching and nutrient enrichment leading to eutrophication.

**3.2.3 Strategies (3)**: improved sanitation; treatment of sewage; pollution control and legislation.

## Sources: pollutant and pathway

- **Domestic waste**: food scraps, detergents, oils, household chemicals; dumped or washed from tips.
- **Sewage**: organic matter, nutrients, bacteria; untreated outfalls and overflowing latrines.
- **Plastic waste**: litter carried by wind, drains and rivers.
- **Industrial processes**: heavy metals, toxic chemicals, warm water; pipes, spills, leaks.
- **Agricultural practices**: nitrate and phosphate fertilisers, animal waste, pesticides; run-off and leaching.

Point source = one identifiable outlet, easier to regulate. Diffuse source = spread across the land, such as farm run-off, harder to control.

## Impacts: the chains to learn

**Cholera risk**
Untreated sewage → bacteria in drinking water sources → infection risk, highest after floods.

**Bioaccumulation (one organism, over time)**
Toxin absorbed faster than excreted → concentration in that organism rises with age.

**Biomagnification (up a food chain)**
Each consumer eats many prey → keeps the toxin from all of them → concentration multiplies at each level → top predators and people who eat them are most at risk.

**Acid rain in water**
Lower pH → fish eggs and young fish fail → sensitive invertebrates lost → food web disrupted.

**Eutrophication**
Fertiliser leaching or sewage → nutrient enrichment → algal bloom → light blocked → plants die → algae die → bacteria decompose dead matter, using oxygen → fish die.

**Organic shortcut**
Sewage organic matter is decomposed directly by bacteria → oxygen falls close to the outfall even without a bloom.

## Strategies with one benefit and one limitation

| Strategy | How it works | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Improved sanitation | Toilets, latrines, sewers keep waste from water sources | Cuts disease risk at source | Costly to extend to fast-growing or remote settlements |
| Treatment of sewage | Screening and settling, then bacteria break down organic matter with air | Removes most organic matter and many microorganisms | Works expensive to build and run; can be overwhelmed by floods |
| Pollution control and legislation | Discharge limits, permits, monitoring, fines, fertiliser rules | Applies to every polluter; deters dumping | Needs enforcement; diffuse sources hard to trace |

## Data skills

- Dissolved oxygen falls just below a sewage outfall and recovers downstream as organic matter is broken down and the water is re-oxygenated.
- Concentration factor = higher concentration ÷ lower concentration. For example, 6 ppm in a seabird ÷ 0.003 ppm in water = 2,000 times.
- Always quote two figures with units and the change between them.
- Bioaccumulation evidence looks different: the same kind of organism tested over time, or older individuals compared with younger ones, shows rising concentration with age.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pollutant | A substance that harms living things or makes water unsafe to use |
| Point source | Pollution from a single identifiable outlet |
| Diffuse source | Pollution spread across a wide area |
| Leaching | Dissolved ions washed through the soil into water |
| Nutrient enrichment | Increase in nutrients in water from organic content or inorganic ions |
| Eutrophication | The chain from nutrient enrichment to oxygen loss and death of aquatic life |
| Algal bloom | Rapid growth of algae covering the water surface |
| Bioaccumulation | Build-up of a toxin in a single organism over time |
| Biomagnification | Increase in toxin concentration up a food chain |
| Sanitation | Safe disposal of human waste |

## Exam traps

- Fish in eutrophic water die of oxygen shortage, not poisoning.
- Oxygen is used by bacteria (decomposers), not by fish eating algae.
- Bioaccumulation is in **one** organism; biomagnification is **along a chain**.
- Plastic waste is a named source in 3.2 as well as having its own sub-topic, 3.6.
- Acid rain impacts in 3.2 are about organisms in rivers and lakes; formation is Topic 4.
- "Pollution control" needs detail: a limit, a permit, monitoring or a fine.

## Self-test

1. List the five sources of water pollution named in the syllabus.
2. Give two inorganic ions that cause nutrient enrichment.
3. What is leaching?
4. Define bioaccumulation.
5. Why are top predators most affected by biomagnification?
6. What blocks the light in a eutrophic lake?
7. Which organisms use up the oxygen during eutrophication?
8. Name the bacterial disease the syllabus links to water polluted by sewage.
9. Give one effect of acid rain on organisms in a lake.
10. Why is diffuse pollution harder to control than point-source pollution?
11. State the three strategies for improving water quality.
12. A toxin is 0.05 ppm in small fish and 1.5 ppm in seals. How many times more concentrated is it in the seals?

## Answers

1. Domestic waste, sewage, plastic waste, industrial processes, agricultural practices.
2. Nitrate and phosphate.
3. The washing of dissolved ions, such as fertiliser nutrients, through the soil into ground water and rivers.
4. The build-up of a toxic substance in a single organism over time.
5. Each eats many organisms from the level below and retains the toxin from all of them, so concentration is highest at the top.
6. The algal bloom on the surface.
7. Bacteria decomposing dead plants and algae.
8. Cholera.
9. Fish eggs and young fish fail to develop; sensitive invertebrates disappear; food webs are disrupted (any one).
10. It comes from a wide area with many contributors, so it cannot be traced to one outlet or controlled by a single permit.
11. Improved sanitation; treatment of sewage; pollution control and legislation.
12. 1.5 ÷ 0.05 = 30 times.
