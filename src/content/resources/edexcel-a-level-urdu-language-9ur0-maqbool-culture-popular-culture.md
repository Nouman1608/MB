This guide covers **sub-theme 2.2, popular culture (مقبول کلچر)**, of Theme 2 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). The specification frames popular culture mainly through screen media, in three aspects:

- **سینما سے متعلق کلیدی تحریکیں اور اصناف**: key movements and genres in cinema
- **سینما میں آنے والی تبدیلیاں، کیا کیا دکھایا جا سکتا ہے**: changes in cinema, and what can be shown
- **ٹیلیویژن کلچر کے اثرات**: the effects of television culture

The [popular culture revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-popular-culture-revision-notes/) range widely over drama, music, cricket and fashion. This page narrows the focus to cinema and television, which is where the specification's wording points. The context is the Urdu-speaking world: Pakistan and the areas of India where Urdu is an official language. Practise with the [popular culture practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-maqbool-culture-popular-culture-practice/).

## Aspect 1: key movements and genres in cinema

**Genre (صنف, plural اصناف)** means a type of film with recognisable conventions. **Movement (تحریک)** means a period or group of film-makers with a shared approach. Learn a few of each, with one example you are sure of.

**Genres you can name:**

- **رومانوی فلم** (romance) and **سماجی فلم** (social drama about family, class or injustice).
- **ایکشن** and, in Punjabi cinema, the violent rural revenge film. *Maula Jatt* (1979) is the best-known example, and *The Legend of Maula Jatt* (2022) reworked it for a new generation with great commercial success.
- **مزاحیہ فلم** (comedy) and **تاریخی فلم** (historical film). In Indian cinema, *Mughal-e-Azam* (1960) is celebrated for its Urdu dialogue and grandeur.
- **مسلم سوشل** (the "Muslim social"), an Indian film genre set in Muslim households and rich in Urdu poetry and etiquette; *Pakeezah* (1972) is often cited.

**Movements and periods:**

- **The Lahore film industry** (often called Lollywood) produced many Urdu and Punjabi films in the 1950s and 1960s, a period often described as a golden age.
- **Decline**: from the late 1970s and 1980s, audiences fell; explanations commonly given include home video and piracy, the closure of cinema halls, and the rise of television.
- **Revival**: from the late 2000s, new film-makers and multiplex cinemas in large cities brought audiences back. Shoaib Mansoor's *Khuda Kay Liye* (2007) is widely seen as a turning point, and later films such as *Waar* (2013) showed growing ambition.
- **Parallel cinema in India**: realist films concerned with social questions. *Garm Hava* (1973), about a Muslim family in Agra after Partition, is a key example and links to Theme 4.

When you write about genre, explain what the conventions **do**: a social drama raises questions about a society; a romance often tests love against family expectations; an action film may express anger at injustice.

## Aspect 2: changes in cinema, and what can be shown

The phrase **کیا کیا دکھایا جا سکتا ہے** (what can be shown) points to **censorship and limits**. Organise this aspect in three strands.

**Technology and audiences.** Single-screen halls have given way, in big cities, to multiplexes with higher ticket prices, so cinema-going has become more of a middle-class activity. Streaming platforms now let people watch at home, and some film-makers release directly online.

**Content.** Pakistani film-makers increasingly treat subjects such as extremism, women's rights and corruption. Each new subject raises the question of what society accepts on screen.

**Regulation.** In Pakistan and in India, a film needs a certificate from a censor board before public release. Boards can cut scenes, set age ratings or refuse certification, typically on grounds such as religion, morality, national security or relations with other countries. Political tension has also affected what audiences can see: Indian films were not shown in Pakistani cinemas for long periods, including after 1965 and again after 2019.

**The debate.** Supporters of censorship argue that it protects social values, religious feeling and children. Critics argue that it limits **آزادیٔ اظہار** (freedom of expression), favours safe stories over honest ones, and is applied inconsistently. A strong answer distinguishes between age classification, which most people accept, and banning ideas, which is more contested.

Useful terms: **سنسر بورڈ** (censor board), **نمائش** (screening), **پابندی** (ban), **کاٹ چھانٹ** (cuts), **فلم بینی** (cinema-going), **سینما گھر** (cinema hall).

## Aspect 3: the effects of television culture

**ٹیلیویژن کلچر** means the habits, tastes and attitudes that grow around television. Effects to discuss:

- **On language**: television spread a standard spoken Urdu into homes across Pakistan, including homes where another language is spoken; today it also spreads a mixed Urdu–English style.
- **On the family**: watching a drama together at a fixed hour was once a shared family ritual. With phones and on-demand viewing, family members increasingly watch separately.
- **On attitudes**: dramas shape views on marriage, women's roles and class; some open debate on issues such as domestic violence, while critics say others normalise it by repetition.
- **On consumption**: advertising and fashion seen on screen influence what people buy and wear, especially at weddings and Eid.
- **On cinema**: for many years television drama overshadowed Pakistani cinema; now many film actors and directors come from television, and films often borrow its style.

Link this aspect to the media research subject (2.3) only where it helps: television news and talk shows belong mainly there, while drama and entertainment belong here.

## Worked example: reading for inference

Original review, written for this guide:

> فلم کی کہانی نئی نہیں، مگر ہدایت کار نے اسے اس سلیقے سے پیش کیا ہے کہ ناظرین آخر تک نشستوں سے بندھے رہتے ہیں۔ البتہ گانوں کی تعداد ضرورت سے زیادہ ہے اور دوسرا حصہ کچھ طویل محسوس ہوتا ہے۔

A Section B question might ask: **مبصر کی فلم کے بارے میں مجموعی رائے کیا ہے؟** (What is the reviewer's overall opinion of the film?)

**Model answer:** مبصر کی رائے مجموعی طور پر مثبت ہے، کیونکہ وہ ہدایت کار کے انداز کی تعریف کرتا ہے، اگرچہ وہ گانوں کی زیادتی اور دوسرے حصے کی طوالت پر تنقید بھی کرتا ہے۔

**How to get there:** **مگر** after a criticism introduces praise, so the first sentence is positive overall; **نشستوں سے بندھے رہتے ہیں** ("stay tied to their seats") is an idiom for being gripped. **البتہ** ("however, admittedly") signals the reservations. The word **مجموعی** (overall) in the question tells you to weigh both, not to list only one.

## Worked example: a sentence into Urdu

"Films that discuss social problems are sometimes banned, but they often start an important debate."

**Model:** سماجی مسائل پر بات کرنے والی فلموں پر کبھی کبھی پابندی لگا دی جاتی ہے، لیکن اکثر یہی فلمیں ایک اہم بحث کا آغاز کرتی ہیں۔

Note the participial phrase **بات کرنے والی فلموں** for "films that discuss", the passive **پابندی لگا دی جاتی ہے** agreeing with پابندی (feminine), and **یہی** for emphasis.

## Common mistakes

- Writing only about television drama and cricket and ignoring cinema, which two of the three aspects name.
- Confusing **صنف** (genre) with **تحریک** (movement).
- Giving release dates or box-office figures you have not checked.
- Treating censorship as simply right or wrong instead of distinguishing classification from banning.
- Writing **فلم** with masculine agreement; it is feminine (**فلم ریلیز ہوئی**).

## Exam technique

- Reading and listening texts on cinema often contain the vocabulary of reviews: **ہدایت کار** (director), **اداکاری** (acting), **منظر نامہ** (screenplay), **عکاسی** (cinematography), **مبصر** (reviewer). Recognise it quickly.
- In Paper 3 Section B, a recording might defend censorship and a text attack it; summarise each fairly before you judge.
- Back every evaluation with one concrete example: a named genre, a period, or a type of regulation.
