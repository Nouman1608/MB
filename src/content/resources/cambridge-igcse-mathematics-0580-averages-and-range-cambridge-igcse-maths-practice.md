These questions cover section 9.3 of Topic 9 Statistics in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use Core content (C9.3, Averages and range) and suit every candidate. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use E9.3 content (quartiles, interquartile range, grouped means and modal classes), examined only on Papers 2 and 4. Each question is also labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [averages and range study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-averages-and-range-cambridge-igcse-maths/) and the [averages and range revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-averages-and-range-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Here are nine numbers.

```
6  2  8  4  2  9  4  2  8
```

**(a)** Write down the mode. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the median. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the range. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the mean. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) The mean of eight numbers is 7. One number is removed, and the mean of the remaining seven numbers is 6. Find the number that was removed. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Here are twelve values in order.

```
3  5  5  6  8  9  11  12  12  14  15  20
```

**(a)** Find the median. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the range. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) State which average, mean, median or mode, is most suitable for each data set. Give a reason each time.

**(a)** The colours of cars in a car park. **[1]**

**(b)** The finishing times in a race, where one injured runner walked to the finish. **[1]**

**(c)** The heights of 30 plants that are all between 40 cm and 52 cm. **[1]**

**5.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range of these seven values.

```
13  18  21  25  26  30  34
```

**[2]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The numbers of emails received by 20 people one day are grouped as 1–10 (frequency 6), 11–20 (frequency 9) and 21–30 (frequency 5).

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the midpoint of the class 11–20. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** (calculator) The table shows the number of books read last month by 30 students.

```
Books read (x)   0    1    2    3    4    5
Frequency (f)    2    7   10    8    2    1
```

**(a)** Write down the mode. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the median. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean. **[2]**

**(d)** Find the range. **[1]**

**8.** (calculator) The table shows the number of children in some families.

```
Number of children (x)   1    2    3    4
Frequency (f)            6    k    8    4
```

**(a)** The mean number of children per family is 2.5. Find k. **[3]**

**(b)** A second group of 30 families has a mean of 3.1 children per family. Find the mean number of children per family for all the families in both groups. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator) A basketball team scored a mean of 68 points in its first 10 games. The lowest score was 55 and the range was 25.

**(a)** In the 11th game the team scored 90 points. Find the new mean. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the range of all 11 scores. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator) The annual salaries, in dollars, of the eight people who work at a small firm are:

```
21 000   23 000   23 000   25 000   26 000   28 000   30 000   95 000
```

**(a)** Find the mean, the median and the mode. **[4]**

**(b)** The firm's advert says "Average salary over $33 000". Explain why this is misleading. **[1]**

**(c)** The person paid $95 000 leaves and is not replaced. Find the new mean salary. **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (calculator) The masses of 50 parcels are shown.

```
Mass m (kg)   0 < m ≤ 2   2 < m ≤ 4   4 < m ≤ 6   6 < m ≤ 8   8 < m ≤ 10
Frequency         8           17          14           7           4
```

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate an estimate of the mean mass. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why your answer to part (b) is an estimate. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The table shows the scores of 15 players on a quiz.

```
Score (x)       1    2    3    4    5
Frequency (f)   2    4    5    3    1
```

**(a)** Find the median score. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the lower quartile and the upper quartile. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the interquartile range. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **2** [1]

**(b)** In order: 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 6, 8, 8, 9; the 5th value is **4** [1]

**(c)** 9 − 2 = **7** [1]

**(d)** 45 ÷ 9 = **5** [1] **[4]**

**2.** Total of eight = 8 × 7 = 56 and total of seven = 7 × 6 = 42 [1]; removed number = 56 − 42 = **14** [1]

**3. (a)** Position 6.5: (9 + 11) ÷ 2 = **10** [1]

**(b)** 20 − 3 = **17** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** **Mode**: the data are qualitative (colours), so no other average is possible [1]

**(b)** **Median**: the walking runner's time is an extreme value that would pull the mean up [1]

**(c)** **Mean**: there are no extreme values, so the mean uses every height fairly [1] **[3]**

**5.** LQ = 2nd value = **18** and UQ = 6th value = **30** [1]; IQR = 30 − 18 = **12** [1]

**6. (a)** **11–20** [1]

**(b)** (11 + 20) ÷ 2 = **15.5** [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** **2** [1]

**(b)** Position 15.5, between the 15th and 16th values; running totals 2, 9, 19 [1]; both values are 2, so median = **2** [1]

**(c)** Σfx = 0 + 7 + 20 + 24 + 8 + 5 = 64 [1]; mean = 64 ÷ 30 = **2.13** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(d)** 5 − 0 = **5** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* 10 is the frequency of the mode, not the mode; and the mean is divided by 30 students, not by the 6 columns.

**8. (a)** Σfx = 6 + 2k + 24 + 16 = 46 + 2k and Σf = 18 + k [1]; (46 + 2k) ÷ (18 + k) = 2.5, so 46 + 2k = 45 + 2.5k [1]; **k = 2** [1]

**(b)** First group: 20 families with total 50 children; second group: 30 × 3.1 = 93 [1]; mean = (50 + 93) ÷ 50 = **2.86** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* (2.5 + 3.1) ÷ 2 = 2.8 is wrong because the groups have different numbers of families.

**9. (a)** Total = 10 × 68 + 90 = 770 [1]; new mean = 770 ÷ 11 = **70 points** [1]

**(b)** Highest of the first 10 = 55 + 25 = 80, so 90 is the new highest [1]; range = 90 − 55 = **35** [1] **[4]**

**10. (a)** Total = 271 000 [1]; mean = 271 000 ÷ 8 = **$33 875** [1]; median = (25 000 + 26 000) ÷ 2 = **$25 500** [1]; mode = **$23 000** [1]

**(b)** The mean is pulled up by the one salary of $95 000; seven of the eight people earn $30 000 or less, so a typical salary is well under $33 000 [1]

**(c)** 176 000 ÷ 7 = **$25 142.86** (or $25 100 to 3 s.f.) [1] **[6]**

**11. (a)** **2 < m ≤ 4** [1]

**(b)** Midpoints 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 [1]; Σf × midpoint = 8 + 51 + 70 + 49 + 36 = 214 [1]; 214 ÷ 50 = **4.28 kg** [1]

**(c)** The actual masses are not known; each is assumed to be at the midpoint of its class [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** Running totals 2, 6, 11, 14, 15; the median is the 8th value = **3** [1]

**(b)** LQ = 4th value = **2** [1]; UQ = 12th value = **4** [1]

**(c)** 4 − 2 = **2** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* with n = 15, the positions are (15 + 1) ÷ 4 = 4, (15 + 1) ÷ 2 = 8 and 3 × 16 ÷ 4 = 12; use the running totals to find which score is in each position.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a frequency or a position instead of a data value.
- Dividing by the number of columns in a frequency table.
- Averaging two means from groups of different sizes.
- Forgetting to order a list before finding the median or quartiles.
- (Extended) Using class end points instead of midpoints, or calling an estimate exact.
