All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Carston Cars.

> **Case study: Carston Cars**
>
> Carston Cars is a car dealership with 80 employees. Its showroom
> salespeople are paid a basic $1,500 a month plus 3% commission on
> their sales. The 20 mechanics are paid $12 an hour for a 40-hour week.
> Staff turnover is high among the mechanics, who say they are bored and
> have no chance to use their ideas. The owner is thinking of two
> changes: a 5% rise in the mechanics' hourly rate, or a profit-sharing
> scheme in which 8% of the company's annual profit of $200,000 would
> be shared equally among all 80 employees. The owner has also thought
> about letting mechanics decide how to organise their own repair work.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a financial method of motivation? **[1]**
- A. Giving staff more responsibility
- B. Commission on sales
- C. Training
- D. A democratic management style

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a fringe benefit? **[1]**
- A. A fixed annual salary
- B. A staff discount on the firm's products
- C. Overtime pay
- D. A profit-related bonus

**3.** Define the term "commission". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** benefits to a business of a motivated workforce. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** disadvantage of paying employees a salary. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A salesperson sells cars worth $90,000 in a month. Calculate the salesperson's total pay for the month. **[3]**

**7.** Calculate the weekly pay of a mechanic at present, and the weekly pay after a 5% rise. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate the extra yearly cost to Carston of the 5% rise for all 20 mechanics, assuming 52 weeks. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the amount each employee would receive from the profit-sharing scheme. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** possible disadvantages of the profit-sharing scheme for Carston Cars. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** ways in which giving mechanics more responsibility could motivate them. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate which of the owner's proposals is likely to be the best way to reduce staff turnover among the mechanics. Use your answers to Questions 8 and 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Extra pay based on sales [1], usually a percentage of the value of the sales a person makes [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: higher productivity [1]; staff retention [1]; better quality [1]; better customer service [1]; lower absence [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A salary is fixed whatever the output [1], so it does not reward extra effort and some staff may do the minimum [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Commission = 3% x $90,000 = $2,700 [1]; total pay = $1,500 + $2,700 [1] = **$4,200** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Present weekly pay = 40 x $12 = $480 [1]; new hourly rate = $12 x 1.05 = $12.60 [1]; new weekly pay = 40 x $12.60 = **$504** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Extra per mechanic = $504 - $480 = $24 a week; for 20 mechanics over 52 weeks = $24 x 20 x 52 [1] = **$24,960** a year [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Profit-sharing pool = 8% x $200,000 = $16,000 [1]; shared among 80 employees = $16,000 / 80 [1] = **$200** each [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Depends on profit:** in a year with low profit employees would receive little or nothing [1], so the scheme would not motivate them or might upset them [1].
**Weak link to effort:** with 80 employees, one mechanic's work has little effect on the total [1], and the payment is made once a year, so some staff may not work harder [1]. Accept the cost to the company: $16,000 less profit for owners [1] with a consequence [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Variety and interest:** mechanics said they were bored [1], so deciding how to organise their own work gives them more variety and makes the job more interesting [1].
**Use of their ideas:** mechanics said they had no chance to use their ideas [1], and being allowed to organise their work shows trust and makes them feel valued [1].
**Business benefit:** more motivated mechanics are less likely to leave [1], reducing the costs of recruiting and training replacements and improving the quality of repairs [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Pay rise:** costs $24,960 a year [1] and gives each mechanic an extra $24 a week [1], but the mechanics said they were bored and had no chance to use their ideas, so pay alone may not stop them leaving [1], since pay rises are soon taken for granted.
**Profit sharing:** the $200 per employee is smaller than the pay rise for a mechanic ($1,248 a year) [1], and it varies with profit [1]; but it costs Carston $16,000, less than the pay rise [1].
**Responsibility:** it costs little and tackles the cause the mechanics gave [1].
**Judgement:** giving mechanics more responsibility is likely to be the best way, because it addresses the reasons they gave for leaving [1], though it works best combined with a modest pay rise or profit share, depending on what Carston can afford [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the full commission to the sales instead of multiplying the sales by 3%.
- Forgetting to multiply the weekly extra by both 20 mechanics and 52 weeks.
- Dividing the profit-sharing pool by 20 instead of 80 employees.
- Writing that money is the best motivator without using the mechanics' reasons for leaving.
- Describing a method without saying how it affects the business, such as productivity or retention.
