Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Give percentages as stated, rounded to 1 decimal place if needed.

## Questions

**1.** A new cafe is about to open. Identify two purposes of market research for the owner. **[2]**

**2.** A garden centre uses (i) a questionnaire given to its customers and (ii) a government report on household incomes.

**(a)** State whether each is primary or secondary research. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage of secondary research. **[2]**

**3.** Brindle Garden Centre sold 27,600 of plants in a town market worth 184,000 last year. This year the market is worth 208,000 and Brindle sold 31,200.

**(a)** Calculate Brindle's market share last year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Brindle's market share this year. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse what the figures show about Brindle's performance. **[3]**

**4.** Fenwick Gym questioned 500 people outside its own entrance. 125 said they would join a new yoga class, 210 said no and 165 were undecided.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage who said they would join. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the owner should be cautious about relying on this result. **[3]**

**5.** A bakery asked 600 local people what they cannot find nearby. 150 wanted late-night delivery, 90 wanted vegan cakes, 60 wanted gluten-free bread and the rest wanted nothing new.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage who wanted late-night delivery. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the biggest gap in the market and explain how this helps the owner's decision. **[3]**

**6.** Explain one benefit and one limitation of using social media to collect market research data. **[4]**

**7.** Average weekly household spending on restaurant meals in Quarnby Vale fell from 520 to 468.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change. **[2]**
**(b)** Recommend one way a local restaurant could respond to this change, giving a reason. **[4]**

**8.** Hadleigh Pianos builds each instrument to its owner's own design and then looks for buyers.

**(a)** Define product orientation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain one risk of Hadleigh's approach. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one advantage of niche marketing for Hadleigh. **[2]**

**9.** Ostrava Shoes has sales of 63,000 in a market worth 840,000. The market is not expected to change.

**(a)** Calculate its market share. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra sales needed to reach a 10% share. **[2]**

**10.** Zephyr Sportswear sells running clothing. Its owner wants to divide the market into segments.

**(a)** Identify two bases for segmenting a market. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how one of them could help Zephyr. **[3]**

**11.** Marlow Cycles is a small business with 30 employees. Last year its sales were 600,000 in a market worth 5.0 million. This year the market is worth 5.2 million and Marlow's sales are 650,000. A large rival has just opened nearby. The owner can either carry out test marketing of a new electric bike in one shop, or rely on a market report and government reports. Test marketing would cost more and take longer.

**(a)** Calculate Marlow's market share in each year. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate which research approach Marlow should use. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of the following, one mark each: to identify and understand customer needs [1]; to identify gaps in the market; to reduce risk; to inform business decisions [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "state" or "identify" answer gains credit from the purpose itself. Adding a cafe example does not earn more here.

**2. (a)** (i) Primary, because the business collects it first-hand [1]. (ii) Secondary, because someone else collected it [1].
**(b)** One advantage, such as lower cost [1], developed: the report already exists, so the garden centre does not pay to design and run its own research [1]. Accept speed with a development.
*Examiner insight:* Advantages gain credit when they are explained. The single word "cheap" is usually too thin for a two-mark "explain".

**3. (a)** 27,600 / 184,000 [1] x 100 = **15%** [1]
**(b)** 31,200 / 208,000 [1] x 100 = **15%** [1]
**(c)** Sales rose by about 13.0% (3,600 on 27,600) [1], but so did the market (24,000 on 184,000) [1], so Brindle only kept its share and did not gain on rivals [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" answers tend to gain credit by connecting two figures, here sales growth and market growth, instead of describing one.

**4. (a)** 125 / 500 [1] x 100 = **25%** [1]
**(b)** The sample was taken outside the gym [1], so respondents are mostly people who already exercise and may not represent the wider market [1]. Many (165) were undecided, so the true figure could differ from 25% in either direction [1].
*Examiner insight:* Reliability points are stronger when tied to the scenario detail, such as where people were questioned.

**5. (a)** 150 / 600 x 100 = **25%** [1]
**(b)** Late-night delivery is the biggest gap (25%, against 15% and 10%) [1]. It shows demand for a service no one nearby provides [1]. The owner can use this to decide where to spend, lowering the risk of launching a product few want [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using the figures from the data to identify the largest gap is what separates the answer from a general one.

**6.** Benefit: it is cheap and quick [1], because a poll or comments reach many customers without printing or interviewing [1]. Limitation: respondents may not represent all customers [1], so the results could be biased towards younger or more active users [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each side needs a reason attached. A list of two bare words does not show the reasoning that "explain" asks for.

**7. (a)** (468 - 520) / 520 [1] x 100 = **-10%** (a fall of 10%) [1]
**(b)** Recommendation: introduce a lower-priced set menu [1]. Reason: customers are spending less, so a cheaper option keeps them eating there [1]. Support from the data: a 10% fall is large enough to affect sales [1]. A drawback or condition: profit per meal falls, so costs should be checked first [1].
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation is stronger with a reason from the data and a limit, because it shows judgement and not only a suggestion.

**8. (a)** Making the product first and then looking for buyers, instead of starting from what customers want [1].
**(b)** Risk: the pianos may not match what buyers want [1], so instruments could stay unsold and money would be tied up [1].
**(c)** Advantage: a niche has fewer competitors [1], so buyers who value custom design may pay higher prices [1].
*Examiner insight:* Definitions that contrast with the opposite (market orientation) are clear. Advantages should be linked to the stated business.

**9. (a)** 63,000 / 840,000 [1] x 100 = **7.5%** [1]
**(b)** 10% of 840,000 = 84,000 [1]. Extra sales = 84,000 - 63,000 = **21,000** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Showing the target sales figure first helps an answer that contains a slip, since the method is visible.

**10. (a)** Any two of the following, one mark each: location [1]; demographics; lifestyle; income; age [1].
**(b)** For example, age: Zephyr could target runners aged 18 to 30 [1] with bright, low-priced clothing sold online [1], which reduces wasted advertising on people unlikely to buy [1]. Accept any other basis explained in the same way.
*Examiner insight:* Naming a basis is only the first step. Credit comes from showing what the business would then do differently.

**11. (a)** Last year: 600,000 / 5,000,000 x 100 = **12%** [1]. This year: 650,000 / 5,200,000 x 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Share has risen by 0.5 percentage points [1].
**(b)** Points for test marketing: it gives real buying evidence for a costly new product [1]; it reduces the risk of launching an electric bike that does not sell [1]; and it is primary data suited to Marlow's own customers, so the rival cannot use it [1]. Points against: it costs more and takes longer [1]; the rival can see the product early and respond [1]; one shop may not represent all customers [1]. Points for secondary research: cheaper and faster, and shows how the 5.2 million market is growing [1], but it is not specific to Marlow's customers [1]. Judgement [1], for example: use the reports first, because they are cheap and the market is growing, then test market in one shop if they show demand, since the electric bike is a large investment and the new rival makes a wrong choice more costly. Accept a justified alternative.
*Examiner insight:* High-level "evaluate" answers weigh both approaches using the scenario data and end with a decision that follows from the weighing, not a repeat of the points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a purpose or method and not applying it to the business.
- Writing "cheap" or "quick" with no reason.
- Calling government or market reports primary research.
- Using the firm's sales as the divisor in market share.
- Treating a rise in sales as a rise in market share.
- Ignoring where and who a sample came from when asked about reliability.
- Giving a response to a market change with no drawback.
- Naming a segmentation basis and not saying how it helps.
- Ending an evaluation without a decision tied to the scenario.
