IB Diploma Programme Economics is an individuals and societies course that
studies how individuals, firms and governments make choices about scarce resources, and
how those choices play out in markets, national economies and the global economy. It is
offered at Standard Level and Higher Level, and the current syllabus was first assessed
in 2022. Students at both levels sit two written papers and complete an internally
assessed portfolio of three commentaries; HL students also sit a third paper. At SL the
weightings are 30% for Paper 1, 40% for Paper 2 and 30% for the portfolio, while at HL
they are 20%, 30%, 30% for Paper 3 and 20% for the portfolio. This page sets out how the
syllabus is arranged and how the parts of the assessment relate to it.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has four units, and the IB does not attach any one unit to a single paper.

**Unit 1: Introduction to economics.** A short opening unit on what economics is and
how economists approach the world. It sets up the vocabulary and ways of thinking used
throughout the rest of the course.

**Unit 2: Microeconomics.** Demand, supply and competitive market equilibrium, followed
by elasticity of demand and supply, the role of government in markets, and market
failure through externalities, common pool resources and public goods. HL adds a
critique of the maximising behaviour of consumers and producers, market failure through
asymmetric information and market power, and the market's inability to achieve equity.

**Unit 3: Macroeconomics.** Measuring economic activity, aggregate demand and aggregate
supply, macroeconomic objectives, inequality and poverty, and the three families of
policy: monetary policy, fiscal policy and supply-side policies.

**Unit 4: The global economy.** International trade and protection, economic
integration, exchange rates and the balance of payments, followed by sustainable
development, measuring development, barriers to growth and development, and strategies
for achieving them.

Several sub-topics in every unit after the first are marked as including HL-only
material, often a calculation, so an HL student meets extra quantitative work across
microeconomics, macroeconomics and the global economy rather than in one block.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Format | Time | Marks | SL weighting | HL weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Extended response, one question from three | 75 minutes | 25 | 30% | 20% |
| Paper 2 | Data response, one question from two | 105 minutes | 40 | 40% | 30% |
| Paper 3 | Policy paper (HL extension) | 105 minutes | 60 | not taken | 30% |
| Internal assessment | Portfolio of three commentaries | no fixed sitting | 45 | 30% | 20% |

Papers 1 and 2 are the same papers at both levels, set on any part of the syllabus.
Paper 1 is taken without a calculator; a calculator is permitted for Papers 2 and 3.
Paper 3 is a structured, data-based policy paper that draws on the HL extension topics.

The internal assessment is a portfolio of three commentaries, each on a published news
extract. It is assessed by the teacher and externally moderated by the IB. Because the
portfolio carries 30% at SL and 20% at HL, it matters more to the final grade at SL,
though it is significant at both levels.

## How the parts fit together

The four units are taught in order, but the assessment treats them as one body of
knowledge. Paper 1 asks for a sustained, evaluative answer to one question, which might
come from any unit. Paper 2 starts from data and text and asks you to apply economic
theory to a real situation. Paper 3, at HL, asks you to work with data to reach and
justify a policy recommendation.

The same skills therefore run through every unit: defining terms precisely, drawing and
explaining diagrams, applying theory to real-world examples, using data, and evaluating
policies by weighing their costs, benefits and side effects. The commentaries in the
portfolio use those skills on current news, which is why it helps to keep a habit of
reading economic news from the start of the course.

Connections between units are worth tracing explicitly. Elasticity in Unit 2 returns in
the analysis of tax incidence, trade protection and exchange rate effects; market
failure in Unit 2 links to sustainable development in Unit 4; and fiscal and monetary
policy in Unit 3 return when Unit 4 asks how countries pursue growth and development.

## Planning your study

Most schools follow the syllabus order: the introductory unit, then microeconomics, then
macroeconomics, then the global economy. That order works because each unit builds on
diagrams and concepts from the one before.

A sensible plan across the course is:

- **First year:** the introduction and microeconomics, then much of macroeconomics. Begin
  building a diagram bank and a file of real-world examples for each sub-topic.
- **Portfolio:** commentaries are usually written at points spread across the course, each
  once the relevant unit has been taught. Your school will set the deadlines.
- **Second year:** finish macroeconomics and teach the global economy, then move into
  revision and full past papers.

HL students should track the HL-only sub-topics and calculations separately, since they
are easy to under-revise if notes are organised only by the shared content. Start timed
practice on Paper 1 and Paper 2 questions as each unit finishes rather than waiting for
the final term, and use the exam preparation page for paper-by-paper technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is IB DP Economics assessed at SL?**
SL students sit Paper 1 (75 minutes, 25 marks, 30%) and Paper 2 (105 minutes, 40 marks, 40%), and complete the internal assessment portfolio (45 marks, 30%).

**What extra assessment does HL Economics have?**
HL adds Paper 3, a policy paper on the HL extension topics lasting 105 minutes for 60 marks and 30%. Papers 1 and 2 and the portfolio are the same as at SL but are weighted 20%, 30% and 20%.

**What is the internal assessment in IB Economics?**
It is a portfolio of three commentaries on published news extracts, worth 45 marks. It is assessed by the teacher and externally moderated by the IB.

**Can you use a calculator in IB Economics exams?**
A calculator is not allowed in Paper 1. It is permitted in Paper 2 and in the HL Paper 3.

**What are the four units of DP Economics?**
Introduction to economics, Microeconomics, Macroeconomics and The global economy.
