## Section A

**1.** Describe the piezo-electric effect. **[2]**

**2.** Define the specific acoustic impedance of a medium. **[1]**

**3.** Muscle has a density of 1075 kg m⁻³ and the speed of sound in it is 1590 m s⁻¹. Calculate its specific acoustic impedance. **[1]**

**4.** An ultrasound echo from a boundary inside the body is received 97 μs after the pulse was transmitted. Calculate the depth of the boundary. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a gel is applied between an ultrasound transducer and the patient's skin. **[2]**

**6.** The gel has Z = 1.53 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ and skin has Z = 1.63 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹. Calculate the fraction of the incident intensity that is transmitted into the skin. **[2]**

---

## Section B

**7.** A piezo-electric transducer is used both to produce and to receive ultrasound.

**(a)** Explain how the transducer produces ultrasound and how it detects the returning echoes. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the ultrasound is emitted as short pulses rather than continuously. **[2]**

**8.** Soft tissue has a density of 1060 kg m⁻³. Bone has Z = 7.75 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate Z for soft tissue. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the intensity reflection coefficient at a soft tissue-bone boundary. **[2]**

**(c)** State the fraction of the incident intensity that crosses the boundary. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why organs behind bone show up poorly in ultrasound images. **[1]**

**9.** The tissue-bone boundary in Question 8 lies 3.0 cm below the skin. The attenuation coefficient of the soft tissue is 23 m⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the factor by which the intensity of the pulse is reduced by attenuation on its journey to the boundary and back. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the intensity of the echo arriving back at the skin as a fraction of the intensity entering the tissue. **[1]**

**10.** A pulse produces two echoes, one from the front surface and one from the back surface of an organ. They arrive 40 μs and 92 μs after the pulse is transmitted.

**(a)** Calculate the depth of the front surface of the organ. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the thickness of the organ. **[2]**

**11.** Ultrasound passes through a tissue. After travelling 4.0 cm, its intensity has fallen to 40 % of its initial value.

**(a)** Calculate the attenuation coefficient of the tissue. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance the ultrasound must travel through this tissue for its intensity to fall to 10 % of its initial value. **[2]**

**12.** Ultrasound is rarely used to image the lungs. Air has Z = 4.3 × 10² kg m⁻² s⁻¹. Explain why ultrasound gives little useful information about tissue inside the lungs. **[2]**

**13.** Explain how an ultrasound scanner can obtain diagnostic information from the echoes it receives. Refer to both the timing and the intensity of the echoes. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** When a p.d. is applied across a piezo-electric crystal, the crystal changes shape [1]; when the crystal's shape is changed (it is compressed or stretched), an e.m.f. is generated across it [1].

**2.** The product of the density of the medium and the speed of sound in it, Z = ρc [1].

**3.** Z = 1075 × 1590 = 1.71 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ [1].

**4.** The pulse travels to the boundary and back, so d = ct/2 [1] = 1540 × 97 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 2 = 0.075 m (7.5 cm) [1].

**5.** Without gel there would be air between transducer and skin; the acoustic impedances of air and skin are very different, so almost all the ultrasound would be reflected [1]. The gel has an impedance close to that of skin, so almost all the ultrasound is transmitted into the body [1].

**6.** I_R/I₀ = (1.63 − 1.53)² ÷ (1.63 + 1.53)² = 0.0100 ÷ 9.99 = 0.0010 [1]; fraction transmitted = 1 − 0.0010 = 0.999 [1].

**7. (a)** An alternating p.d. is applied across the crystal, making it change shape (expand and contract) at the same frequency [1]; the vibrations produce longitudinal ultrasound waves, with the largest amplitude when the frequency equals the crystal's natural frequency (resonance) [1]. A returning echo exerts varying pressure that changes the shape of the crystal [1], so an alternating e.m.f. is generated across it, which is amplified and processed [1].

**(b)** The same crystal must stop transmitting so that it can receive the echoes [1]; a pulse has a definite start, so the time taken for each echo to return can be measured and used to find the depth [1].

**8. (a)** Z = ρc = 1060 × 1540 = 1.63 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** I_R/I₀ = (7.75 − 1.63)² ÷ (7.75 + 1.63)² [1] = 37.5 ÷ 88.0 = 0.43 [1].

**(c)** 1 − 0.43 = 0.57 [1].

**(d)** A large fraction is reflected at the bone surface and most of the rest is absorbed (attenuated) in the bone, so very little ultrasound reaches the organ and returns [1].

**9. (a)** Total path = 2 × 0.030 = 0.060 m [1]; factor = e^(−23 × 0.060) = 0.25 [1].

**(b)** 0.43 × 0.25 = 0.11 [1].

**10. (a)** d = 1540 × 40 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 2 = 0.031 m (3.1 cm) [1].

**(b)** Extra time for the round trip across the organ = 92 − 40 = 52 μs [1]; thickness = 1540 × 52 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 2 = 0.040 m (4.0 cm) [1].

**11. (a)** 0.40 = e^(−μ × 0.040) [1]; μ = −ln 0.40 ÷ 0.040 = 23 m⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 0.10 = e^(−23x) [1]; x = ln 10 ÷ 23 = 0.10 m (10 cm) [1].

**12.** The impedance of air is very much smaller than that of soft tissue, so at the boundary with air in the lungs almost all the intensity is reflected (reflection coefficient close to 1) [1]; very little ultrasound enters the air-filled lung, so no echoes come back from structures inside it [1].

**13.** The time delay between transmitting a pulse and receiving an echo gives the depth of the boundary, using d = ct/2 [1]. The intensity of the echo depends on the difference in acoustic impedance between the two tissues at the boundary, so it gives information about what the boundary is [1]. Echoes from many directions or positions are combined to build an image of the internal structures [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the piezo-electric effect in one direction only.
- Using d = ct instead of d = ct/2 for an echo.
- Attenuating over the one-way distance when the pulse travels there and back.
- Explaining the gel as a lubricant rather than as impedance matching.
- Forgetting that a reflection coefficient and an attenuation factor are multiplied.
- Giving Z without its unit, kg m⁻² s⁻¹.
