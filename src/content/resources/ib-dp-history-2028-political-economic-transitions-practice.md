## Part 1: a paper 1 style set (24 marks)

**Inquiry question: How was the transition achieved?**

**Source A** (constructed for practice). From a government notice in Japan, 1872.

> "The land shall be measured and a certificate issued to each owner. The tax will be paid in money and not in rice. Every man shall serve in the army when he comes of age. The old distinctions among the classes are ended."

**Source B** (constructed for practice). From a speech by a Russian reformer, 1992.

> "Prices must be freed now. If we wait, the shelves will stay empty and the state will go bankrupt. Citizens will receive vouchers so that the property of the state becomes the property of the people."

**Source C** (constructed for practice). From a letter written in 1993 by a retired factory worker in a provincial Russian town to a newspaper.

> "My pension buys less each month. They tell us the market will make us richer, but the directors of the factory seem to be the ones who have become rich."

*Context for source C:* The letter was one of many received by the newspaper, which printed a selection under the heading "Readers write".

**1.** Explain how the **content** of both source A and source B can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**2.** Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**3.** Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[12]**

## Part 2: short knowledge questions

**4.** Explain one way in which the Meiji government used land reform to strengthen the state. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one way in which events in Eastern Europe affected the Soviet Union. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6):
- Source A: land measurement and certificates, and a cash tax, show land reform as a way of achieving the transition **[1]**; conscription for every man shows the creation of a new national army **[1]**; the ending of class distinctions shows the dismantling of the old social order **[1]**.
- Source B: freeing prices shows market liberalisation as the means of change **[1]**; vouchers show privatisation, intended to spread ownership **[1]**; the argument that delay would bring bankruptcy shows the urgency felt by reformers **[1]**.
Markbands: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial explanation (maximum 3 for one source); 5 to 6 for a range of specific content from both sources, developed.

**2.** Marking points (6):
- Author: an ordinary pensioner, so the source gives the experience of a group affected by the reforms, not a policy-maker **[1 to 2]**.
- Selection: the newspaper printed a selection, so the letter may reflect the paper's editorial choices and may not be typical **[1 to 2]**.
- Date: 1993, in the middle of rapid change, so it shows early reactions rather than the long-term outcome **[1 to 2]**.
- Link to the question: it is useful for the human cost of how the transition was achieved, but cannot show the policy decisions or how widely the view was held **[1]**.
Reward references to context that are linked to use.

**3.** Marking points (12):
- Source A: the perspective of an official of the Meiji state, which is confident, top-down and focused on state strength **[2]**.
- Source B: a reformer's perspective, urgent and committed to the market and to rapid change **[2]**.
- Source C: a pensioner's perspective, sceptical and focused on who gained from the reforms **[2]**.
- Similarities: A and B both come from reformers who present the changes as necessary and beneficial; both emphasise the redistribution of property or obligations **[2]**.
- Differences: A and B are official and optimistic, while C is critical; A concerns obligations to the state, B concerns market freedom, and C concerns the result for ordinary people **[2]**.
- Combined: the sources show that the transitions were achieved by state-led policies that were promoted as necessary, but the experience of those affected (C) shows that the methods were disputed, so a full answer needs both official aims and popular reactions **[2]**.
Markbands: 1 to 3 minimal; 4 to 6 partial; 7 to 9 at least two sources (maximum 9 for two sources); 10 to 12 insightful understanding of all three sources.

**4.** For example: the 1873 land tax reform gave owners certificates of ownership **[1]**; the tax was paid in cash at a set rate instead of in rice that varied with harvests **[1]**; this gave the government a reliable revenue to pay for the army, industry and administration **[1]**.

**5.** For example: communist governments in Eastern Europe fell in 1989 without Soviet military intervention **[1]**; this showed that the Soviet Union would not force allied states to remain communist, and encouraged opposition within the USSR **[1]**; nationalist movements in the Soviet republics then grew stronger, weakening the Communist Party's control **[1]**.
