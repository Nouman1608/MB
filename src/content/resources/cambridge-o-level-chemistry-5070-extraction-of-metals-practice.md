These questions cover subtopic 9.6 Extraction of metals of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028). Revise first with the [Extraction of metals study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-extraction-of-metals/) and the [Extraction of metals revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-extraction-of-metals-revision-notes/).

Relative atomic masses: Fe = 56; Al = 27; O = 16; C = 12; Ca = 40.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain why gold is found in the Earth's crust as the uncombined metal. **[1]**

**2.** Which metal must be extracted by electrolysis rather than by heating its oxide with carbon?

A: copper

B: iron

C: magnesium

D: zinc

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** uses of coke in the blast furnace. **[2]**

**4.** Write the symbol equation for the thermal decomposition of limestone. **[1]**

**5.** Name the substance that is oxidised when iron(III) oxide is reduced in the blast furnace. **[1]**

**6.** State the material used for both electrodes in the extraction of aluminium. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Iron is extracted from hematite in the blast furnace.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction in which carbon dioxide is converted to carbon monoxide. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide by carbon monoxide. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of oxygen, why this is a redox reaction. **[2]**

**8.** Limestone is added to the blast furnace to remove silicon dioxide.

**(a)** Write the equation for the formation of slag. **[1]**

**(b)** Classify calcium oxide and silicon dioxide as acidic or basic oxides. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the slag is separated from the iron. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be produced from 4000 kg of iron(III) oxide. Show your working. **[3]**

**10.** Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite.

**(a)** Explain why aluminium is not extracted using carbon. **[1]**

**(b)** State the role of cryolite. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the ionic half-equations at the cathode and at the anode. **[2]**

**(d)** State which half-equation shows oxidation, and give a reason. **[1]**

**11.** The carbon anodes in an aluminium cell must be replaced regularly.

**(a)** Explain why. Include an equation in your answer. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the cathode does not need replacing for the same reason. **[1]**

**12.** Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium that can be obtained from 510 kg of aluminium oxide. **[2]**

**13.** Zinc is below carbon in the reactivity series and potassium is above it.

**(a)** Suggest how zinc could be extracted from zinc oxide, and write a balanced equation using carbon. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why potassium is extracted by electrolysis of a molten compound and not from an aqueous solution of its salt. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Gold is very unreactive / at the bottom of the reactivity series, so it does not form compounds [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** Fuel / burns to provide heat [1]; produces carbon monoxide, the reducing agent [1].

**4.** CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ [1].

**5.** Carbon monoxide [1].

**6.** Carbon / graphite [1].

**7. (a)** CO₂ + C → 2CO [1].

**(b)** Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Iron(III) oxide loses oxygen, so it is reduced [1]; carbon monoxide gains oxygen, so it is oxidised [1].

**8. (a)** CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ [1].

**(b)** Calcium oxide basic and silicon dioxide acidic (both needed) [1].

**(c)** The slag is molten and less dense than molten iron, so it floats on top [1]; it is run off / tapped separately [1].

**9.** Mr of Fe₂O₃ = 160 [1]; mass of iron in 160 kg = 112 kg [1]; 4000 × 112 ÷ 160 = 2800 kg [1].

**10. (a)** Aluminium is more reactive than carbon [1].

**(b)** Aluminium oxide dissolves in it, so the electrolyte is molten at a lower temperature [1]; this saves energy / reduces cost [1].

**(c)** Cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al [1]. Anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ [1].

**(d)** The anode equation, because oxide ions lose electrons [1].

**11. (a)** Oxygen is produced at the anode [1]; it reacts with the hot carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂ [1]; so the anode is gradually used up / burns away [1].

**(b)** Aluminium, not oxygen, forms at the cathode, so the carbon there does not react [1].

**12.** Mr of Al₂O₃ = 102; mass of aluminium in 102 kg = 54 kg [1]; 510 × 54 ÷ 102 = 270 kg [1].

**13. (a)** Heat zinc oxide with carbon [1]; ZnO + C → Zn + CO (or 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂) [1].

**(b)** Potassium reacts vigorously with water, so it could not be obtained from an aqueous solution / it is too reactive [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling carbon monoxide the fuel.
- Unbalanced Fe₂O₃ + 3CO equation.
- Writing O²⁻ → O₂ + 2e⁻ instead of 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻.
- Calling cryolite a catalyst.
- Leaving out units or the ratio step in reacting-mass calculations.
- Saying the anodes are replaced because aluminium attacks them.
