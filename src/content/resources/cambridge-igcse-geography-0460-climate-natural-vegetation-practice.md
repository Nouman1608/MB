These questions cover topic 2.5 Climate and natural vegetation in Theme 2 (The natural environment) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. The weather station in Question 7 is fictional and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Climate and Natural Vegetation study guide](/resources/igcse-geography-climate-and-natural-vegetation/) and the [Climate and Natural Vegetation revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-climate-natural-vegetation-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the difference between weather and climate. **[2]**

**2.** Give two characteristics of an equatorial climate. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why hot deserts have a large difference between day and night temperatures. **[2]**

**4.** Describe two ways in which plants are adapted to survive in hot deserts. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the soils of tropical rainforests are often infertile, even though the vegetation is dense. **[2]**

**6.** Give two causes of deforestation in tropical rainforests. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows climate data for a fictional weather station.

| Month | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | 13 | 15 | 19 | 24 | 29 | 33 | 34 | 33 | 30 | 24 | 18 | 14 |
| Rainfall (mm) | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

**(a)** State the mean temperature of the hottest month. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual temperature range. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total annual rainfall. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why places with this type of climate receive so little rainfall. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why places with an equatorial climate receive high rainfall throughout the year. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the structure of the tropical rainforest and explain how plants are adapted to the conditions. **[5]**

**10.** Explain the relationship between the climate, soil, vegetation and wildlife of a hot desert ecosystem. **[5]**

**11.** Describe the effects of tropical rainforest deforestation on the natural environment, both locally and globally. **[5]**

**12.** For a named area of tropical rainforest you have studied, describe the causes of deforestation and explain its effects on people. **[7]**

**13.** "The deforestation of tropical rainforests brings more benefits than costs." To what extent do you agree? Use examples. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Weather is the day-to-day condition of the atmosphere, such as temperature, rainfall and wind [1]. Climate is the average weather of a place measured over a long period, usually 30 years [1]. **[2]**

**2.** High temperatures all year, around 26 to 28°C, with a very small annual range [1]. High rainfall, usually over 2,000 mm a year, falling in every month [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Clear skies with little cloud let strong sunshine heat the ground during the day [1]. At night there is no cloud to trap heat, so heat is lost quickly and temperatures fall sharply [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is cloud cover; "it is hot in the day and cold at night" restates the question.

**4.** Long roots spread widely or reach deep underground to find water [1]. Small, waxy or spiny leaves reduce water loss by transpiration [1]. Also credit stems that store water, such as in cacti, or seeds that lie dormant until rain falls. **[2]**

**5.** Heavy rainfall leaches nutrients down through the soil, out of reach of plant roots [1]. Nutrients from decomposed leaf litter are taken up very quickly by plants, so few are stored in the soil [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Clearing land for cattle ranching or commercial farming, such as oil palm or soya [1]. Logging for timber [1]. Also credit mining, road building, hydroelectric dams or settlement. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 34°C (July) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 34 − 13 = 21°C [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 10 mm [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Hot deserts lie at around 30° north and south of the equator, under subtropical high pressure [1]. Air sinks and warms as it descends, so water vapour does not condense into cloud [1]. Prevailing winds often blow from the land, so they carry little moisture; cold ocean currents and distance from the sea also reduce rainfall in some deserts [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The data show a hot desert; the explanation must be about why air does not rise and cool, not just "it is hot".

**8.** The sun is overhead or nearly overhead all year, so the ground is heated strongly every day [1]. Warm air rises by convection, carrying moisture evaporated from the forest and nearby oceans [1]. As it rises it cools, water vapour condenses and tall cumulonimbus clouds form [1]. Heavy convectional rain, often with thunderstorms, falls in the afternoon; the equatorial region is also an area of low pressure where trade winds converge, so air rises all year [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The rainforest has distinct layers: emergents rising above a dense canopy, an understorey and a dim forest floor [1]. Emergent trees have buttress roots to support their great height in shallow soils [1]. Canopy trees have drip-tip leaves so heavy rain runs off quickly [1]. Lianas and epiphytes climb or grow on other plants to reach the light [1]. Trees have thin, smooth bark because there is no cold season to protect against, and plants on the forest floor tolerate low light [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Very low and unreliable rainfall limits plant growth, so vegetation is sparse [1]. Plants are adapted to drought, with long roots, water storage and small leaves, and many grow only after rain [1]. Sparse vegetation means little organic matter, so soils are thin, sandy or stony and low in humus [1]. High evaporation can draw salts to the surface, making soils saline [1]. Animals are adapted to heat and lack of water, for example by being active at night and obtaining water from food, and their numbers are limited by the scarce plant food [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Local: soil erosion increases because roots no longer bind the soil and heavy rain washes it away [1]. Local: loss of habitats reduces biodiversity, and some species may become extinct [1]. Local: rivers become choked with eroded sediment, and flooding increases as less rain is intercepted [1]. Global: less carbon dioxide is absorbed, and burning trees releases carbon dioxide, adding to climate change [1]. Global: less water is transpired into the atmosphere, which can reduce rainfall over the region and beyond [1]. **[5]**

**12.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content:

Named area of rainforest, such as a named country or region of the Amazon [1]. Cause: cattle ranching or commercial farming [1]. Cause: logging, mining, road building or dams, with a specific example [1]. Effect on indigenous people: loss of land, food sources and way of life [1]. Effect: conflict between groups over land [1]. Effect: jobs and income from farming, logging and mining [1]. Explanation of why effects differ between groups, for example landowners and companies gain while forest communities lose [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe causes and explain effects" needs both parts; specific place names and activities lift an answer out of the lowest level.

**13.** Indicative content:

Benefits: jobs and income from logging, farming and mining [1]. Benefits: export earnings from crops such as soya and oil palm, and from timber and minerals [1]. Benefits: land for settlement, roads and hydroelectric power [1]. Costs: loss of biodiversity and of possible medicines [1]. Costs: soil erosion, so cleared land may soon become unproductive [1]. Costs: release of carbon dioxide, contributing to climate change [1]. Costs: loss of land and culture for indigenous peoples [1]. Judgement: short-term economic benefits are often outweighed by long-term environmental and social costs, especially where clearance is unplanned, although sustainable management can reduce the costs [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the desert climate with no figures from the data.
- Explaining desert dryness with "it is hot", rather than sinking air and high pressure.
- Listing rainforest layers without linking them to plant adaptations.
- Giving deforestation effects only on the environment when the question asks about people.
- Case studies with no named area.
