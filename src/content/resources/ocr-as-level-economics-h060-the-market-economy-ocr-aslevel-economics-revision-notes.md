## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Market economy | Most resources privately owned; decisions made by households and firms, coordinated by prices |
| Planned economy | Most resources state-owned; a central authority decides what, how and for whom |
| Mixed economy | Markets and government both allocate resources, in varying proportions |
| Price mechanism | The way changes in prices, driven by demand and supply, allocate resources |
| Profit motive | Firms' aim of earning profit, which steers them toward goods consumers will pay for |
| Consumer sovereignty | The idea that consumers' spending decides what is produced |
| Property rights | Legal rights to own, use and sell resources |
| The margin | The point of deciding about one more or one less unit |
| Rationality | Acting consistently to achieve an aim, given the information available |

## How a market economy answers the three questions

| Question | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| What to produce | Consumer spending and the prices it creates; firms follow profit |
| How to produce | Cost-minimising methods, because lower costs mean higher profit |
| For whom | Those able to pay, which depends on income and wealth |

## Strengths: point and chain

- **Information:** prices summarise the wants of millions, so resources move without a planner.
- **Incentives:** profit rewards efficiency and innovation, so costs fall and new products appear.
- **Choice:** competition gives consumers variety and the power to switch.
- **Responsiveness:** prices change quickly when tastes or technology change.

## Weaknesses: point and chain

- **Missing goods:** where non-payers cannot be excluded, firms cannot charge, so nothing is supplied.
- **Externalities:** third-party costs and benefits are ignored, so some goods are over- or under-produced.
- **Information:** consumers may misjudge the benefits or harms of goods.
- **Inequality:** output goes to those who can pay, not to those in greatest need.
- **Market power:** dominant firms restrict output and raise prices.
- **Instability:** recessions leave labour and capital unused.

## Role of government in a market economy

- Legal framework: property rights and contracts.
- Provision of goods the market will not supply.
- Taxes, subsidies and regulation for external effects.
- Consumer protection and competition policy.
- Redistribution through taxes and benefits.
- Stabilising the economy.

Every intervention also has costs, which is why the scale of government is debated.

## The margin and rationality

- A rational agent does something one more time only if the extra benefit is at least the extra cost.
- Café example: an extra hour with extra revenue £85 and extra cost £60 is worth it (+£25); the next, with £50 against £60, is not (-£10).
- Totals do not decide marginal choices: a profitable day does not justify an unprofitable extra hour.
- Assumed aims: consumers maximise satisfaction, firms maximise profit, government maximises the welfare of society.
- Limits of rationality: incomplete information, habit, and the way choices are presented.
- Version 3.0 lists rationality in topic 1.1 The economic problem; it does not name the margin.

## Exam traps

- "A market economy has no government": wrong; every market needs law and property rights.
- "Planned economies have no prices": prices may exist but are set by the state.
- Listing strengths or weaknesses without the mechanism.
- Treating "unfair" as a positive statement.
- Using totals rather than extra values for a marginal decision.
- Calling the UK a market economy without noting it is mixed.

## Self-test

1. Define a market economy.
2. How does a market economy decide what to produce?

11. State the objective usually assumed for firms.
12. Give one reason why people may not behave rationally.

### Answers

2. Through consumer spending: goods people will pay for earn profit, so firms produce them.
3. By ability to pay, which depends on income and wealth.
4. An economy in which both markets and government allocate resources.
5. A rising price signals that a good is in greater demand relative to supply.
6. It rewards firms that cut costs or offer what consumers want, so resources move to valued uses.

11. Profit maximisation.
12. Incomplete information, habit, or being swayed by how a choice is presented.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mixed economy just a compromise?**
It is the form every real economy takes. The question is the balance: which goods markets should allocate and which the state should provide or regulate.

**Why does the margin matter in a market economy?**
Prices guide decisions at the margin: a small rise in price makes it worthwhile for firms to supply one more unit and for some consumers to buy one fewer.
