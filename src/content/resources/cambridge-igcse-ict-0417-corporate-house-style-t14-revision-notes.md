These notes cover one statement from Topic 14 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417: the **purpose and uses of a corporate house style**. Use them after the [corporate house style study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-corporate-house-style-t14/), and then try the [corporate house style practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-corporate-house-style-t14-practice/). The wider topic, including how to create and modify styles, is in the [Styles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-styles-revision-notes/).

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus statement | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Corporate house style: "Purpose and uses of a corporate house style" | Explain why organisations have one; name the documents and media it is applied to; follow given house-style rules in practical tasks |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corporate house style | An organisation's agreed rules for the look and wording of everything it produces |
| Corporate identity | How the organisation is recognised by the public, built up by repeating the house style |
| Style guide | The written document that sets out the house-style rules |
| Template | A file containing saved styles, page setup, logo and standard text, used as the starting point for new documents |
| Master slide | The slide that holds the shared layout and formatting for every slide in a presentation |
| Stylesheet | A file of formatting rules applied to every page of a website |
| Style | One saved, named set of formatting in the software; a house style is put into practice using many styles |
| Direct formatting | Formatting applied by hand to selected text, which can drift away from the house style |

## Purposes: point plus reason

Exam marks usually need the reason as well as the point.

| Purpose | Reason it matters |
|---|---|
| Consistency | Every document looks the same whichever member of staff makes it |
| Recognition | Customers identify the organisation from its logo, colours and fonts |
| Professional image | Well-presented, matching documents build trust |
| Saves time | Staff start from templates and do not design each document |
| Fewer errors | Standard wording (address, legal notice) is stored once, not retyped |
| Easier to read | Information such as a reference number is always in the same place |
| Stands out | The organisation's material does not look like a competitor's |

## Uses: where a house style applies

- **Stationery**: letterheads, compliment slips, envelopes, business cards.
- **Business documents**: letters, invoices, receipts, order forms, reports, memos.
- **Marketing**: leaflets, posters, adverts, newsletters, social media posts.
- **On screen**: presentations, websites, emails and email signatures.
- **Identity items**: staff badges and printed signs.

Linking each use to where the rules are stored is useful in longer answers:

- Word processing: template plus named styles.
- Presentations: master slide.
- Websites: external stylesheet.
- Database reports: a saved report layout reused for every report.

## Typical contents of a house style

- Logo: which version, its size, its position and the space kept clear around it.
- Colour scheme: the exact colours, and which is used for what.
- Fonts: heading and body fonts, point sizes, and serif or sans-serif.
- Layout: margins, alignment, line and paragraph spacing, column use.
- Headers and footers: what appears and where it is aligned.
- Wording: the organisation's name, slogan, date format, standard sign-off.
- Images and charts: borders, captions, chart colours.

## Drawbacks

- Initial cost and time to design the style and build templates.
- Staff need training and someone must check that the rules are followed.
- Less creative freedom for individual documents.
- A rebrand means updating every template, master slide, stylesheet and printed stock.

## Exam traps

- **House style versus style.** A style is one named format in the software. A house style is the whole set of organisation rules. Do not use the terms as if they mean the same.
- **"It looks good"** earns nothing on its own. Say *why* the appearance matters: recognition, trust or readability.
- **Forgetting on-screen uses.** Websites, presentations and emails are uses as well as printed letters.
- **Practical slips.** Missing one rule, such as the size of footer text, the logo position or the date format, loses the mark for that rule even if everything else is right.
- **Hand formatting.** If a template or styles are supplied, apply them; do not recreate the look manually.
- **Repeating one idea.** "Consistent" and "all documents look the same" are the same point. Make different points for different marks.

## Self-test

1. Define *corporate house style*.
2. What is the name of the document in which house-style rules are written down?
3. State two purposes of a house style and give a reason for each.
4. Name four different documents that a house style would be applied to.
5. Where would a house style be stored for a presentation?
6. Where would it be stored for a website?
7. Explain how a template reduces errors.
8. Give two elements of a house style other than the logo.
9. Why is a house style useful to a customer reading a letter?
10. Give two drawbacks of a house style.
11. A member of staff formats a heading by hand to look like the house style. Why is this a poor method?
12. A company changes its logo. What has to happen to its documents and files?

### Answers

1. The agreed set of rules for the appearance and wording of all of an organisation's documents and media.
2. A style guide (brand guidelines).
3. Any two with reasons: recognition, because customers identify the organisation from its logo and colours; professional image, because matching documents build trust; saves time, because staff start from a template; fewer errors, because standard text is stored once.
4. Any four, for example letter, invoice, business card, report, leaflet, presentation, website, email signature.
5. On the master slide.
6. In an external stylesheet.
7. Standard content such as the address, logo and legal notice is already in the template, so it is not retyped and cannot be mistyped.
8. Any two: colour scheme, fonts and sizes, margins and layout, header and footer content, date format, slogan.
9. Information such as the reference number or contact details is always in the same place, so it is quick to find, and the reader can trust the letter is genuine.
10. Any two: cost and time to set up, staff training, limits creativity, every template must be updated after a rebrand.
11. It is slow, may not match exactly, and will not update if the heading style is later changed.
12. Every template, master slide, stylesheet and stock of printed stationery must be updated to the new logo.
