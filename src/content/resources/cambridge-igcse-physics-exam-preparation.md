Cambridge IGCSE Physics (0625) is tiered and has a practical component. Core candidates take Paper 1 (Multiple Choice, 45 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 3 (Theory, 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). Extended candidates take Paper 2 (Multiple Choice, 45 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 4 (Theory, 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). Every candidate then sits one practical route: Paper 5 Practical Test (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) or Paper 6 Alternative to Practical (1 hour, 40 marks, 20%). Revise alongside the pages on [motion, forces and energy](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy/), [electricity and magnetism](/resources/igcse-physics-0625-electricity-and-magnetism/) and [nuclear physics](/resources/igcse-physics-0625-nuclear-physics/).

## Core or Extended: the tier decision

The Core papers (1 and 3) and Extended papers (2 and 4) have identical durations, marks and weightings, but different questions. According to the verified record, Core candidates are eligible for grades C to G and Extended candidates for A* to G. The practical component is separate from the tier route. Agree your tier with your teacher after timed mock papers, rather than on impressions alone.

## The multiple choice paper: Paper 1 or Paper 2

Whichever tier you take, the multiple choice paper is 45 minutes for 40 marks and carries 30%. That is 45 / 40 = 1.125 minutes per question. Read each stem fully, cover the options, predict the answer, and then compare. Where a calculation is needed, do it on the paper rather than estimating by sight. Do not leave blanks, as there is no reason to skip a choice question; but flag long calculations and return to them if time allows.

Cover the whole syllabus when preparing: motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics. Multiple choice questions can sample any of the six areas, so patchy revision is exposed quickly.

## The theory paper: Paper 3 or Paper 4

Theory is 1 hour 15 minutes for 80 marks and is worth 50%, the largest single share. That is 75 / 80 = 0.9375 minutes per mark, or roughly 56 seconds, which is the tightest pace of any component. Practise writing short, accurate sentences, and use the mark allocation as a guide to how many distinct points are wanted. Calculation questions reward a clear layout: write the equation, substitute with units, then state the answer with its unit.

Use the [space physics](/resources/igcse-physics-0625-space-physics-revision-notes/) and [thermal physics](/resources/igcse-physics-thermal-physics-revision-notes/) revision notes for content where candidates often rely on vague recall.

## The practical route: Paper 5 or Paper 6

The record shows that a candidate sits one of Paper 5 or Paper 6, so only one counts towards the total. Paper 5 is a Practical Test of 1 hour 15 minutes, worth 40 marks (20%). Paper 6 is a written Alternative to Practical of 1 hour, worth 40 marks (20%). Both are externally assessed. Your school will tell you which one you are entered for.

Whichever you take, the underlying skills are the same: following a method, recording readings in a clear table with units, plotting and interpreting graphs, calculating from results, identifying sources of error and suggesting improvements. If you are entered for Paper 6, practise by writing about experiments you have actually performed, not just memorising generic descriptions.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Papers 1 and 2: 45 / 40 = 1.125 minutes per mark.
- Papers 3 and 4: 75 / 80 = 0.9375 minutes per mark.
- Paper 5: 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark.
- Paper 6: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.

The weightings add to 30% + 50% + 20% = 100% for the papers you sit. Theory is the longest-weighted component, so it should receive the largest share of revision time, but the 20% practical is easy to under-prepare.

## Command words and question styles

Define needs a precise statement of meaning. State needs a brief fact. Describe asks what happens, often with a sequence. Explain asks why, and usually needs a physical principle. Calculate needs a number and a unit. Suggest means applying knowledge to an unfamiliar context. Sketch asks for a diagram or graph showing shape, and Plot asks for accuracy. Always check which verb is used before you answer.

## Worked practice scenario

A van of mass 1200 kg accelerates on a straight road from a speed of 8 m/s to 20 m/s in 12 s. Ignore resistive forces. Calculate (a) the increase in kinetic energy and (b) the average power developed.

**Model answer.**

(a) Kinetic energy is one half of mass times speed squared.
Initial: 0.5 x 1200 x 8 squared = 600 x 64 = 38 400 J.
Final: 0.5 x 1200 x 20 squared = 600 x 400 = 240 000 J.
Increase = 240 000 - 38 400 = 201 600 J.

(b) Power is energy transferred divided by time.
P = 201 600 / 12 = 16 800 W, which is 16.8 kW.

The answer is a sensible magnitude for a small van's engine. Present the units at every step and state the final answer with the unit.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm your tier and your practical route (Paper 5 or Paper 6).
- Memorise the key equations you are expected to recall and check which are provided.
- Time yourself at 1.125 minutes per mark on multiple choice.
- Practise concise theory answers at under one minute per mark.
- Revise graph skills: axes, scales, plotting, line of best fit and gradient.
- Bring a calculator, ruler and sharp pencil.
- In calculations, write the equation, the substitution and the unit.
- Check significant figures and units before leaving a question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625?**
There are six papers in total, but a candidate sits three: two papers for their tier (multiple choice and theory) plus one practical route, either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

**What are the durations and marks of the papers?**
Papers 1 and 2 are 45 minutes and 40 marks, Papers 3 and 4 are 1 hour 15 minutes and 80 marks, Paper 5 is 1 hour 15 minutes and 40 marks, and Paper 6 is 1 hour and 40 marks.

**What is the weighting of each component?**
The multiple choice paper is 30%, the theory paper is 50% and the practical component is 20%.

**Do both Paper 5 and Paper 6 count?**
No. A candidate sits one of them, so only one counts towards the total.

**Which examination series does this syllabus cover?**
It covers examinations in 2026, 2027 and 2028, in the June and November series, and Cambridge does not publish a separate first-teaching date.
