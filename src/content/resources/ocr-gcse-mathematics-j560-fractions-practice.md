These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 2.01, Fractions (2.01a to 2.01c). All of the content is for both tiers. Work without a calculator and show every step. Give fractions in their simplest form. Section A has short questions; Section B has multi-step and context questions.

Revise first with the [2.01 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-fractions/) or the [2.01 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-fractions-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** Write 24/40 in its simplest form. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 4 2/7 as an improper fraction. **[1]**

**(c)** Which one of 10/16, 15/24 and 20/30 is **not** equivalent to 5/8? **[1]**

**2.** Work out

**(a)** 3/4 + 2/5 **[2]**

**(b)** 7/9 − 5/6 **[2]**

**3.** Work out

**(a)** 5/12 × 9/10 **[2]**

**(b)** 4/15 ÷ 8/25 **[2]**

**4.** Find 3/5 of £4.75. **[2]**

**5.** Write 48 minutes as a fraction of 2 hours. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**6.** Find 11/8 of 56 m. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Show that 2 1/2 + 1 5/6 = 4 1/3. **[3]**

**8.** Work out 1 2/5 − 3 1/4. Give your answer as a mixed number. **[3]**

**9.** Work out

**(a)** 2 2/3 × (−1 1/8) **[2]**

**(b)** 4 1/2 ÷ 1 4/5 **[2]**

**10.** Work out 5/6 + 2/3 ÷ 4/9. Give your answer as a mixed number. **[3]**

**11.** A ribbon 7 1/2 m long is cut into pieces that are each 5/8 m long. How many pieces are there? **[3]**

**12.** Nadia earns £540 one month. She saves 2/9 of it. She spends 3/7 of the rest on rent.

**(a)** How much does she spend on rent? **[3]**

**(b)** What fraction of the £540 is left after saving and paying rent? **[2]**

**13.** 5/12 of the students in a year group walk to school. 85 students walk. How many students are in the year group? **[2]**

**14.** A recipe for 4 people uses 3/4 kg of flour. Ben makes the recipe for 7 people. How much flour does he need? Give your answer as a mixed number of kilograms. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **3/5** [1]

**(b)** **30/7** [1]

**(c)** **20/30** (it equals 2/3) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Cross products give a fast check: 20 × 8 = 160 but 30 × 5 = 150, so 20/30 ≠ 5/8.

**2. (a)** 15/20 + 8/20 [1]; **23/20** or 1 3/20 [1]

**(b)** 14/18 − 15/18 [1]; **−1/18** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A negative answer in part (b) is correct, because 5/6 is slightly larger than 7/9.

**3. (a)** Cancelling, for example to 1/4 × 3/2, or 45/120 [1]; **3/8** [1]

**(b)** 4/15 × 25/8 [1]; **5/6** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Only the second fraction is inverted. Cancelling 4 with 8 and 25 with 15 before multiplying gives 1/3 × 5/2 directly.

**4.** £4.75 ÷ 5 = £0.95 [1]; × 3 = **£2.85** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Write money to 2 decimal places: £2.85, not £2.850 or 2.85p.

**5.** 2 hours = 120 minutes, so 48/120 [1]; **2/5** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 48/2 would compare minutes with hours. Convert first.

**6.** 56 ÷ 8 = 7 [1]; 7 × 11 = **77 m** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A fraction greater than 1 gives an answer greater than 56 m, which is a quick check.

**7.** 5/2 + 11/6 [1]; 15/6 + 11/6 = 26/6 [1]; 26/6 = 13/3 = 4 1/3 [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question every conversion must be written, including the final simplification to 4 1/3.

**8.** 7/5 − 13/4 [1]; 28/20 − 65/20 = −37/20 [1]; **−1 17/20** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Converting to improper fractions keeps the sign under control. Subtracting 1 − 3 and 2/5 − 1/4 separately gives −2 + 3/20, which is easy to misread.

**9. (a)** 8/3 × (−9/8) [1]; **−3** [1]

**(b)** 9/2 × 5/9 [1]; **5/2** = 2 1/2 [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Different signs give a negative product in part (a). Cancelling the 8s and 3 with 9 leaves −3/1.

**10.** Division first: 2/3 × 9/4 = 3/2 [1]; 5/6 + 9/6 = 14/6 [1]; **2 1/3** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Adding first, (5/6 + 2/3) ÷ 4/9, gives 3 3/8, which ignores the order of operations.

**11.** 7 1/2 ÷ 5/8 [1]; 15/2 × 8/5 = 120/10 [1]; **12 pieces** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "How many pieces" in a division by a fraction gives a number larger than 7 1/2, because each piece is less than 1 m.

**12. (a)** Saves 540 ÷ 9 × 2 = £120 [1]; rest £420 [1]; rent 420 ÷ 7 × 3 = **£180** [1]

**(b)** Left 540 − 120 − 180 = £240 [1]; 240/540 = **4/9** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The 3/7 applies to £420, not £540. A check: she keeps 7/9, then 4/7 of that, and 7/9 × 4/7 = 4/9.

**13.** 85 ÷ 5 = 17, so 1/12 of the year group is 17 [1]; 17 × 12 = **204 students** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* This is 85 ÷ 5/12. Finding 5/12 of 85 instead gives a non-whole number of students, a sign that the method is wrong.

**14.** Scale factor 7/4 [1]; 3/4 × 7/4 = 21/16 [1]; **1 5/16 kg** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Finding the flour for one person first, 3/16 kg, then multiplying by 7, is an equally good method.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding or subtracting the denominators.
- Inverting the first fraction in a division.
- Multiplying the whole-number parts and the fraction parts of mixed numbers separately.
- Losing the negative sign when the second mixed number is larger.
- Ignoring the order of operations when a calculation mixes + with × or ÷.
- Applying a second fraction to the original amount instead of the remainder.
- Not converting units before writing one quantity as a fraction of another.
- Not simplifying the final fraction.
