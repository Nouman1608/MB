**These notes condense content area 3.2.2.4 of 9237: the categories of Hadith (Sahih, Hasan, Daeef and Maudu), the methods used to test reliability (Isnad and Matn), and the role of the main compilers, including the features of Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.** The full explanation is in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-compilation-and-categorisation-of-hadith/).

## Why Hadith are tested

- The Prophet (pbuh) warned that whoever lies about him intentionally will take his place in the Fire (Bukhari 3461).
- Reports were fabricated for political and sectarian reasons, and by storytellers.
- Honest narrators sometimes forget or mix up details.
- The great collections were made about two centuries after his death.

## How Hadith were compiled: stages

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| Lifetime of the Prophet (pbuh) | Companions memorised; some wrote (Abdullah ibn Amr's al-Sadiqa) |
| First and second centuries AH | Encouragement to write down Hadith (Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, al-Zuhri); Malik's al-Muwatta |
| Musnads | Arranged by companion, for example Ahmad ibn Hanbal's Musnad |
| Third century AH | Sahih collections arranged by subject |
| Six major collections | Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah |

## Testing reliability

| Test | What is checked |
|---|---|
| **Isnad** (chain) | Each narrator: met the one before? upright? good memory? chain complete? |
| **Matn** (text) | Does it contradict the Qur'an, stronger Hadith, sense or history? Unusual wording? Hidden defects? |
| **Rijal** (narrator biographies) | Dates, teachers, students and reliability of each narrator |

Quote: Ibn al-Mubarak is reported to have said that the Isnad is part of the religion.

## The four categories

| Category | Meaning | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| Sahih | Sound | Complete chain; upright narrators with excellent memory; no hidden defect |
| Hasan | Good | As Sahih, but a narrator has a slightly weaker memory |
| Da'if | Weak | A condition is missing: broken chain, doubtful narrator, defect |
| Maudu' | Fabricated | Invented, or a known liar in the chain |

**Remember:** Sahih and Hasan can be used for rulings; Da'if is not used for belief or halal and haram; Maudu' is rejected.

## Compilers: Bukhari and Muslim

| Feature | Sahih al-Bukhari | Sahih Muslim |
|---|---|---|
| Compiler | Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (194-256 AH) | Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (about 206-261 AH) |
| Place | Bukhara | Nishapur |
| Link | Teacher | Student of al-Bukhari |
| Count (traditional) | About 7,000 with repeats; about 2,600 without | Several thousand without repeats |
| Condition | Narrators must have met | Narrators must have lived at the same time |
| Arrangement | By subject; chapter headings with legal comments | By subject; versions of one Hadith grouped together |
| Extra | Took about sixteen years | Long introduction on method |

**Sahihayn:** the two books together; a Hadith in both is held in the highest esteem.

**Other compilers:** Abu Dawud (law), al-Tirmidhi (grading), al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Malik (al-Muwatta), Ahmad ibn Hanbal (al-Musnad).

## The prescribed passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 3461

| Part of the Hadith | Point |
|---|---|
| "Convey (my teachings) to the people even if it were a single sentence" | Muslims have a duty to pass on the Prophet's teaching |
| "tell others the stories of Bani Israel ... it is not sinful" | Accounts of earlier peoples may be told |
| "whoever tells a lie on me intentionally will surely take his place in the Fire" | A grave warning that explains the testing of Hadith |

**Link to the topic:** the duty to pass on and the warning against lying create the need for the Isnad and Matn.

## Model answer: a 4-mark question

**Question:** Explain **two** differences between a Sahih and a Da'if Hadith. **[4]**

> A Sahih Hadith has a complete chain of narrators [1], each upright and with an excellent memory, while a Da'if Hadith has a broken chain or a doubtful narrator [1]. A Sahih Hadith can be used as a basis for law and belief [1], but a Da'if Hadith cannot be used to establish rulings on halal and haram [1].

## Grading practice

| Chain description | Likely grade |
|---|---|
| All narrators upright, excellent memory, met in sequence | Sahih |
| All upright and met, one slightly weaker in memory | Hasan |
| One link is missing or unknown | Da'if |
| A narrator is known to have invented reports | Maudu' |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Isnad | The chain of narrators |
| Matn | The text of the Hadith |
| Rijal | Narrators; the science of their biographies |
| Sahihayn | The two Sahih books of Bukhari and Muslim |
| Muwatta | Malik's collection of Hadith and the practice of Madinah |
| Musnad | A collection arranged by the narrating companion |
| Mutawatir | Reported by so many that agreed falsehood is impossible |

## Exam traps

- Isnad is the chain; Matn is the text.
- Bukhari: narrators met. Muslim: narrators were contemporaries.
- Da'if is weak; Maudu' is fabricated.
- Do not claim that Hadith were only written long after the Prophet (pbuh).
- Give conditions for each category, not just the name.

## Self-test

1. What is the Isnad?
2. What is the Matn?
3. Give two things scholars checked about each narrator.
4. What is a Hasan Hadith?
5. What is a Maudu' Hadith?
6. In which city was Imam Bukhari born?
7. Whose student was Imam Muslim?
8. What is the difference between their conditions for accepting narrators?
9. What are the Sahihayn?
10. What warning in Bukhari 3461 explains why Hadith are tested?

**Answers:** 1. The chain of narrators from the collector back to the Prophet (pbuh). 2. The text of the report. 3. Any two: honesty or character; memory; whether they met the previous narrator; whether the chain is complete. 4. A good Hadith: it meets the conditions of Sahih except that a narrator has a slightly weaker memory. 5. A fabricated Hadith falsely attributed to the Prophet (pbuh). 6. Bukhara. 7. Imam al-Bukhari's. 8. Bukhari required that narrators had met; Muslim accepted that they were contemporaries and could have met. 9. The Hadith collections of Bukhari and Muslim. 10. That whoever lies about the Prophet (pbuh) intentionally will take his place in the Fire.
