These notes condense subtopic **6.3 Mineral nutrition** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the importance of nitrate ions for making amino acids and proteins, and of magnesium ions for making chlorophyll. The full explanations and worked data are in the [6.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mineral-nutrition/). Then try the [6.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mineral-nutrition-practice/).

## The two ions

| Ion | Used to make | Why needed | Effect of shortage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrate | Amino acids, then proteins | Nitrogen is needed; carbohydrates have none. Proteins form enzymes, cytoplasm and membranes, and are needed for growth | Stunted growth; yellow leaves, especially older ones |
| Magnesium | Chlorophyll | Each chlorophyll molecule contains magnesium | Yellow leaves (chlorosis); less light absorbed, so less photosynthesis |

## Key chains of reasoning

**Nitrate**

1. Nitrate ions contain nitrogen.
2. Nitrogen is needed to make **amino acids**.
3. Amino acids are joined to make **proteins**.
4. Proteins are needed for growth and for **enzymes**.
5. No nitrate leads to stunted growth.

**Magnesium**

1. Magnesium is needed to make **chlorophyll**.
2. Chlorophyll absorbs light for **photosynthesis**.
3. No magnesium means leaves are yellow, less light is absorbed and less glucose is made.

## Important points

- Mineral ions are **not** food and do not provide energy.
- Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen come from carbon dioxide and water. Nitrogen and magnesium come from the soil.
- Ions are absorbed by **active transport** in root hair cells and move upwards in the **xylem**.
- Fertilisers add nitrate (and other ions) to soil where crops have used them up.

## Water culture experiment

| Set | Solution | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| A | Complete (all ions) | Control |
| B | Without nitrate | Shows the effect of lacking nitrate |
| C | Without magnesium | Shows the effect of lacking magnesium |

Keep the same seedlings, volume of solution, light and temperature. Aerate the solutions so that roots have oxygen for respiration. Compare height, leaf colour and mass after several weeks.

### Worked data

Complete solution 24.0 cm; without nitrate 9.0 cm; without magnesium 17.0 cm.

- Without nitrate: (24.0 - 9.0) divided by 24.0 x 100 = 62.5 per cent shorter.
- Without magnesium: (24.0 - 17.0) divided by 24.0 x 100 = 29.2 per cent shorter.

Percentage reduction = (control - test) divided by control x 100.

## Deficiency symptoms side by side

| Feature | Nitrate deficiency | Magnesium deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Strongly stunted | Reduced |
| Leaves | Pale green or yellow, older leaves first | Yellow between the veins, older leaves first |
| Cannot be made | Amino acids and proteins | Chlorophyll |
| Main process affected | Growth and enzyme production | Photosynthesis |

## Working with data: a method

1. Identify the **control** (the complete solution or the normal plant).
2. Find the **difference** between the control and each test.
3. Divide the difference by the **control value** and multiply by 100.
4. State the result with a unit and one decimal place if needed.
5. **Explain** with the chain: ion, substance not made, effect on the plant.

## Where each element comes from

| Element | Source | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon, oxygen | Carbon dioxide | Carbohydrates |
| Hydrogen, oxygen | Water | Carbohydrates |
| Nitrogen | Nitrate ions in soil water | Amino acids and proteins |
| Magnesium | Magnesium ions in soil water | Chlorophyll |

## Fertilisers

- Crops are harvested, so minerals are removed from the field.
- Fertiliser replaces nitrate, which raises protein production and yield.
- Too much fertiliser can wash into water and harm water life.

## Quick check of the chain

Ask yourself three questions for each ion: What is it used to make? What does that substance do? What happens if it is missing? If you can answer all three in one sentence each, you have the full-mark pattern for any question on this subtopic.

## Short model answers

**Explain why plants need nitrate ions.** Nitrate contains nitrogen, which is needed to make amino acids. Amino acids are joined to make proteins, which are needed for enzymes and growth.

**Explain why a plant lacking magnesium has yellow leaves.** Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll. Without it the leaves lack the green pigment, so they look yellow and absorb less light.

**Explain why a plant lacking magnesium grows poorly.** Less chlorophyll means less light is absorbed, so photosynthesis is slower, less glucose is made and growth is reduced.

**State how the ions enter the root.** By active transport in the root hair cells, using energy from respiration.

## Exam traps

- Nitrate is not "plant food" and it does not give energy.
- Include the middle step: nitrate gives **amino acids**, which make proteins.
- Magnesium makes chlorophyll; it does not "make leaves green" by itself.
- Do not swap the uses: nitrate is for amino acids and proteins, magnesium is for chlorophyll.
- Yellow leaves occur with both shortages; stunted growth is the key sign of nitrate shortage.
- The control is the complete solution.

## Self-test

1. Name the ion used to make amino acids.
2. Why can carbohydrates not be made into amino acids without nitrate?
3. What are amino acids joined to make?
4. Give two uses of protein in a plant.
5. State two effects of nitrate deficiency.
6. Name the ion at the centre of chlorophyll.
7. Why does magnesium deficiency reduce photosynthesis?
8. Give one symptom of magnesium deficiency in leaves.
9. How do root hair cells absorb these ions?
10. What is the control in a water culture experiment?
11. A control seedling is 20.0 cm tall and a test seedling is 14.0 cm tall. What is the percentage reduction?
12. Why should the culture solutions be aerated?

### Answers

1. Nitrate.
2. Carbohydrates contain no nitrogen; nitrate supplies it.
3. Proteins.
4. Any two: enzymes; cytoplasm; membranes; growth.
5. Stunted growth; yellow leaves.
6. Magnesium.
7. Less chlorophyll is made, so less light is absorbed.
8. Yellow leaves (chlorosis).
9. By active transport.
10. The solution with all the ions.
11. (20.0 - 14.0) divided by 20.0 x 100 = 30 per cent.
12. So that the roots can respire aerobically and so release energy for active transport.
