The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** State **two** purposes of a trial balance. **[2]**

**2.** Which balance appears on the credit side of a trial balance?

A: carriage outwards

B: discount allowed

C: purchases returns

D: drawings **[1]**

**3.** A credit sale of 450 to T Grant was debited to the account of T Grantham. Which type of error is this?

A: commission

B: principle

C: omission

D: original entry **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the agreement of a trial balance does not prove that the accounting records are correct. **[2]**

**5.** A trial balance has debit total 39,280 and credit total 38,060. One credit balance has been placed on the debit side. Calculate the value of that balance. **[2]**

**6.** Name the type of error in each case.

(i) Cash received for a sale of 95 was not recorded.

(ii) Fuel for the van was debited to the motor vehicles account.

(iii) Rent received of 600 was debited to rent received and credited to bank. **[3]**

## Section B

His balances at 31 December 2027 were: machinery 24,000; provision for depreciation of machinery 9,600; van 12,500; inventory at 1 January 2027, 1,840; trade receivables 3,260; trade payables 2,470; bank overdraft 1,390; cash 95; sales 58,700; sales returns 410; purchases 19,300; purchases returns 640; wages 16,800; rent 7,200; carriage outwards 290; discount allowed 185; discount received 240; rent received 1,800; drawings 11,900; loan 6,000; capital, to be calculated.

**9.** Name the type of error in each case, which does not affect the trial balance.

(i) A purchase invoice for 2,160 was entered as 2,610 in the purchases journal and posted.

(ii) A payment of 300 to J Kerr, a supplier, was debited to J Carr, another supplier.

(iii) The purchases account was overcast by 250 and the commission received account was overcast by 250.

(iv) A cheque payment of 420 for insurance was debited to bank and credited to insurance.

(v) A new printer was debited to the office expenses account.

(vi) A credit note for 75 sent to a customer was lost and never recorded. **[6]**

**10.** For each error, state whether the trial balance totals will still agree. If they will not, state which total will be greater and by how much.

(i) A payment of 480 to a supplier was credited to bank but not debited to the supplier.

(ii) Sales of 1,300 on credit were not recorded.

(iii) The sales account was undercast by 100.

(iv) Discount allowed of 45 was debited to the customer's account and debited to discount allowed.

(v) Cash drawings of 200 were debited to wages.

(vi) The balance of the carriage outwards account, 310, was left out of the trial balance. **[6]**

**11.** Repairs of 900 to Hawthorn Repairs' machinery were debited to the machinery account.

**(a)** Name the type of error.

**(b)** Explain why the trial balance still agrees.

**(c)** State the effect of the error on the profit for the year and on non-current assets. Ignore depreciation.

**(d)** Kamal says that, because his trial balance agrees, his financial statements will be correct. Evaluate his view. **[7]**

**12.** A trial balance shows debit total 84,642 and credit total 85,430. These errors are found:

1. Carriage inwards of 340 was entered on the credit side.
2. Discount allowed of 175 was copied into the trial balance as 157.
3. The sales journal total of 6,450 was posted to the sales account as 6,540. Customers' accounts were posted correctly.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected trial balance totals, showing your workings.

**(b)** Name **one** type of error that could still exist after the totals agree. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two [2]: to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry; to show that errors exist if the totals differ; to provide a summary of balances for preparing the financial statements. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **A** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Some errors do not affect the agreement of the totals [1], for example an error of omission, where nothing is recorded on either side, so the totals still agree although the records are wrong [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Difference 39,280 - 38,060 = 1,220 [1]; balance = 1,220 / 2 = **610** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** (i) Omission [1]; (ii) principle [1]; (iii) complete reversal [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**11. (a)** Error of principle [1].

**(b)** A debit of 900 and a credit of 900 were both recorded, so debits still equal credits [1].

**(c)** Profit for the year is overstated by 900 [1]; non-current assets are overstated by 900 [1].

**(d)** Agreement shows only that debits equal credits [1]. Errors such as this one, and omissions, leave the totals equal while profit and assets are wrong [1]. Kamal is therefore wrong: his statements could contain errors even though his trial balance agrees [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**12. (a)** Debit: 84,642 + 340 + 18 = **85,000** [1] [1]. Credit: 85,430 - 340 - 90 = **85,000** [1] [1].

**(b)** Any one [1]: commission; principle; omission; original entry; complete reversal; compensating. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Error 3 overstated the sales account by 90, so only the credit total changes; error 2 understated discount allowed by 18, so only the debit total changes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing carriage outwards or discount allowed on the credit side.
- Forgetting the provision for depreciation is a credit balance.
- Moving a misplaced balance in only one column when amending totals.
- Confusing commission (same type of account) with principle (wrong type of account).
- Assuming that every error is hidden by an agreed trial balance, or that none is.
