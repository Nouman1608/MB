## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Work done | Energy transferred when a force causes an object to move through a distance |
| Displacement | Distance moved in a straight line, with direction |
| Line of action | The direction in which a force acts |
| Joule (J) | Work done when a force of 1 N causes a displacement of 1 m |
| Newton-metre (N m) | Force × distance; 1 N m = 1 J |
| Friction | A contact force that opposes motion between surfaces |
| Thermal energy store | The store that increases when an object's temperature rises |

## The equation

```
W = F s           recall
F = W / s         s = W / F
W in J,  F in N,  s in m (along the line of action of the force)
```

### Prefix conversions to do first

| Given | Convert to | Multiply by |
|---|---|---|
| kN | N | 1000 |
| kJ | J | 1000 |
| MJ | J | 1 000 000 |
| cm | m | 0.01 |
| mm | m | 0.001 |
| km | m | 1000 |

*Worked reminder.* A tractor pulls a trailer 1.5 km with a force of 2.0 kN.
W = 2000 × 1500 = 3 000 000 J = **3.0 MJ**.

## Is work being done? Three checks

1. Is there a force on the object?
2. Does the object move?
3. Does it move along the force's line of action (at least partly)?

All three "yes" means work is done by that force. Any "no" means it is not.

| Example | Work done by the force named? |
|---|---|
| Weightlifter holding a bar still above her head (her upward push) | No: no displacement |
| Weightlifter raising the bar (her upward push) | Yes: the bar moves upwards |
| Waiter carrying a tray across a level room (his upward push) | No: the motion is at right angles to the force |
| Gravity on a ball falling from a shelf | Yes: the ball moves downwards, along the force |

## Energy transfer when work is done

**Work done = energy transferred.** Always name the stores.

- Lifting: chemical store (person or motor's fuel) → gravitational potential energy store of the object.
- Accelerating: chemical store → kinetic energy store.
- Braking: kinetic energy store → thermal energy store of brakes.
- Moving at a steady speed against friction: chemical store → thermal energy store of the object and
  surroundings.
- Stretching or compressing a spring: → elastic potential energy store.

### Connecting equations

| If the work done... | then the same number of joules equals |
|---|---|
| lifts an object at steady speed | Eₚ = m g h |
| speeds up an object (no friction) | the gain in Eₖ = ½ m v² |
| stops a moving object | the loss of Eₖ, transferred to thermal stores |
| happens in a time t | P = W / t gives the power |

*Worked reminder.* A motor lifts a 40 kg crate 3.0 m in 6.0 s, g = 9.8 N/kg.
F = 40 × 9.8 = 392 N; W = 392 × 3.0 = 1176 J; P = 1176 / 6.0 = **196 W**.

## Work done against friction

- Friction opposes motion; work done against it transfers energy to thermal stores.
- The temperature of the object (and its surroundings) **rises**.
- Examples: brakes, rubbing hands, a drill bit, a sliding box on a rough floor.
- In a machine, this transfer is usually wasted energy, so lubrication is used to reduce friction.

## Method in steps: a two-stage work done question

```
Step 1  find the force (often a weight: W = m g, or a resultant)
Step 2  find the distance along that force's line of action, in metres
Step 3  W = F s, with the unit
Step 4  if asked, describe the stores or find the power
```

## Exam traps

- A horizontal distance used with a vertical force (or the other way round).
- Leaving cm, kN or km unconverted.
- Answering "energy is lost" instead of naming a store.
- Writing "heat is produced by friction" without the idea of work done against friction and a temperature
  rise.
- Assuming work is done because a person feels tired; ask whether the object moved along the force.
- Giving the answer to a "kJ" question in J, or the other way round.
- Confusing work done (J) with force (N).

## Quick self-test

1. State the equation linking work done, force and distance, with units.
2. Define one joule.
3. Convert 640 N m into joules.
4. A force of 25 N pushes a trolley 8.0 m. Calculate the work done.
5. A crane does 54 kJ of work lifting a load 12 m. Calculate the lifting force.
6. A force of 1.2 kN moves an object 45 cm along its line of action. Calculate the work done.
7. A student holds a 20 N bag still for 30 s. How much work does she do on the bag? Why?
8. Name the energy store that increases when a box is pushed at a steady speed across a rough floor.
9. Explain why a car's brakes get hot when the car stops.
10. A 60 kg climber climbs 25 m vertically. Take g = 9.8 N/kg. Calculate the work done against gravity.
11. The climber in question 10 takes 150 s. Calculate the useful power.
12. A sledge is pulled 30 m at a steady speed by a horizontal force of 70 N. Describe what happens to the
    work done.

### Answers

1. W = F s; W in joules, F in newtons, s in metres.
2. The work done when a force of 1 N causes a displacement of 1 m.
3. **640 J**.
4. 25 × 8.0 = **200 J**.
5. 54 000 ÷ 12 = **4500 N** (4.5 kN).
6. 1200 × 0.45 = **540 J**.
7. **0 J**: the bag does not move, so there is no displacement.
8. The thermal energy store (of the box and floor).
9. Friction between the brakes and the wheel does work, transferring energy from the car's kinetic store to
   the thermal store of the brakes, so their temperature rises.
10. F = 60 × 9.8 = 588 N; W = 588 × 25 = **14 700 J** (14.7 kJ).
11. 14 700 ÷ 150 = **98 W**.
12. W = 70 × 30 = 2100 J is done against friction; at steady speed the kinetic store does not change, so the
    2100 J goes to the thermal stores of the sledge and the ground.
