## Section A

**1.** Describe the aims of **two** of the "Big Three" at the Paris Peace Conference. **[6]**

**2.** Describe the structure and membership of the League of Nations. **[6]**

## Section B

**3.** "Hitler's foreign policy was entirely to blame for the outbreak of war in Europe in 1939." How
far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**4.** Explain why the wartime alliance between the USA and the USSR broke down between 1945 and 1948.
**[10]**

**5.** Explain **two** reasons for the League of Nations' failure to resolve the Manchurian and
Abyssinian crises of the 1930s. **[8]**

**6.** Explain why the Berlin Blockade (1948–49) was a turning point in relations between the USA and
the USSR. **[8]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any two: **Wilson** aimed for a settlement based on his **Fourteen Points and
self-determination**, seeking a lasting peace rather than punitive terms [1] [1] [1]. **Clemenceau**
wanted Germany **permanently weakened** to protect French security, given France's direct experience of
invasion [1] [1] [1]. **Lloyd George** sought a settlement **firm enough to satisfy British public
opinion** but not so harsh that it would destabilise Europe and damage post-war trade [1] [1] [1].

**2.** The League was created as part of the peace settlement with the aim of **preventing future
war** through **collective security** [1] [1]. Its structure included an **Assembly** (all member
states), a **Council** (permanent and rotating members with greater power), and a **Secretariat**
[1] [1]. **Membership was limited** — the USA never joined despite Wilson's role in founding it, and
Germany and the USSR were excluded or joined only later, which weakened its authority from the start
[1] [1].

**3.** Award marks for a **balanced answer** that weighs Hitler's foreign policy against other
factors. **Hitler's responsibility**: rearmament in breach of Versailles, remilitarisation of the
Rhineland, the Anschluss with Austria, the Sudeten crisis and Munich Agreement, and the invasion of
Poland show a **consistent pattern of aggressive expansion** [1] [1] [1]. **Other factors**: the
**failure of appeasement** by Britain and France emboldened Hitler to take greater risks [1] [1]; the
**weakness of the League of Nations**, exposed by Manchuria and Abyssinia, removed a check on
aggression [1] [1]; the terms of **Versailles itself** created German resentment that Hitler
exploited [1] [1]. A strong answer reaches a **supported judgement** rather than simply listing causes
on both sides.

**4.** The alliance was based on the **shared aim of defeating Nazi Germany**, which disappeared once
that goal was achieved in 1945 [1] [1]. Disagreements emerged over the **post-war occupation of
Germany** and reparations [1] [1]. **Soviet expansion into Eastern Europe** by 1948, installing
communist governments in states liberated by the Red Army, alarmed the USA and Britain, who saw this as
a breach of wartime promises of free elections [1] [1]. Ideological differences — **capitalism versus
communism** — that had been set aside for the war resurfaced once the common enemy was gone, hardening
into mutual suspicion by 1948 [1] [1].

**5.** Any two: the League had **no standing army**, so it could not enforce its decisions with force
against Japan in Manchuria or Italy in Abyssinia [1] [1] [1]. **Key powers pursued self-interest over
collective security** — Britain and France were unwilling to risk war with Italy over Abyssinia given
the wider international climate of the 1930s [1] [1] [1]. The League's **economic sanctions were
incomplete and inconsistently applied** (e.g. oil sanctions against Italy were not imposed), reducing
their effectiveness [1] [1].

**6.** The USSR's blockade of West Berlin was intended to **force the Western powers out of the
city**, but the **Berlin Airlift** successfully supplied the city by air for nearly a year, which was
seen as a clear Western victory and a Soviet failure [1] [1] [1]. It **hardened the division of
Germany and Europe** into opposing blocs, directly contributing to the formation of **NATO (1949)** and
the separate West and East German states [1] [1] [1]. It demonstrated that **neither side was willing
to risk direct military conflict**, establishing a pattern of confrontation through proxy actions
rather than direct war that characterised much of the rest of the Cold War [1] [1].

## Section C

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why the Solidarity movement in Poland was significant for the decline
of Soviet control over Eastern Europe. **[8]**

**Answer:** Solidarity was significant because it was the **first independent trade union movement**
to emerge and survive within the Soviet bloc, demonstrating that organised opposition to communist rule
could exist and gain mass popular support rather than being immediately suppressed [1] [1] [1] [1]. It
also showed that the **Soviet Union was no longer willing or able to intervene militarily** to crush
internal dissent in a satellite state as it had in Hungary (1956) and Czechoslovakia (1968), signalling
a real shift in Soviet policy under Gorbachev toward tolerating internal reform rather than enforcing
uniform control by force [1] [1] [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing Hitler's actions in isolation for question 3 without weighing appeasement and the
  League's weakness as contributing factors — the question explicitly asks "how far," which requires
  balance.
- Naming League of Nations weaknesses without linking them to the specific outcome in Manchuria or
  Abyssinia.
- Treating the breakdown of the US-Soviet alliance as caused only by ideology, missing the concrete
  disputes over occupied Germany and Eastern Europe.
- Describing the Berlin Blockade's events without explaining its significance as a turning point.
