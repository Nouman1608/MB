These questions cover section **3.2.3.1 Trends in properties** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the ideas first in the [Trends in properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-trends-in-properties/) and the [Trends in properties revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-trends-in-properties-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values Ag = 107.9, I = 126.9.

## Questions

**1.** Define electronegativity and explain why it decreases down Group 7(17) from fluorine to iodine. **[4]**

**2.** Explain why the boiling points of the halogens increase down the group. **[3]**

**3.** State the appearance of bromine and iodine at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

**4.** Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution.

**(a)** State the colour change observed. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** State what is oxidised and what is reduced. **[1]**

**5.** Bromine water is added to sodium chloride solution. State what is observed and explain your answer. **[2]**

**6.** Write two half-equations, and the overall ionic equation, for the reaction between bromine and iodide ions. **[3]**

**7.** Solid sodium chloride is warmed with concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State what is observed. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this is not a redox reaction. **[1]**

**8.** Solid sodium bromide is warmed with concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** State two observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the redox reaction in which bromine is formed. **[1]**

**(c)** State the role of sulfuric acid in this reaction and the change in oxidation state of sulfur. **[2]**

**9.** Solid sodium iodide reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid to form several sulfur-containing products.

**(a)** Name three of the products, and state the oxidation state of sulfur in each. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of ionic size, why iodide ions are better reducing agents than bromide ions. **[2]**

**10.** In the test for halide ions, the solution is first acidified with dilute nitric acid before silver nitrate solution is added.

**(a)** Explain why silver nitrate solution is used. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the solution is acidified. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why dilute sulfuric acid is not used. **[1]**

**11.** Three unlabelled solutions contain sodium chloride, sodium bromide and sodium iodide. Describe how you would identify each, using acidified silver nitrate solution and ammonia solution, and give the observations. **[5]**

**12.** 20.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sodium iodide solution is added to an excess of acidified silver nitrate solution. Calculate the mass of silver iodide formed. **[3]**

**13.** Explain why chloride ions do not reduce concentrated sulfuric acid, whereas iodide ions do. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Electronegativity is the ability of an atom to attract the bonding pair of electrons in a covalent bond [1]; atomic radius increases down the group (more shells) [1]; there is more shielding between the nucleus and the bonding pair [1]; so the bonding pair is held less strongly [1]. **[4]**

**2.** The halogens are simple molecules (X₂) held together by London forces [1]; down the group the molecules have more electrons, so the London forces are stronger [1]; more energy is needed to overcome them, so the boiling point rises [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Bromine is a red-brown liquid [1]; iodine is a grey-black (shiny) solid [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Colourless to brown [1]

**(b)** Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂ [1]

**(c)** Iodide is oxidised and chlorine is reduced [1]. **[3]**

**5.** No reaction (the solution stays orange or no change) [1]; bromine is a weaker oxidising agent than chlorine, so it cannot oxidise chloride ions [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Br₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Br⁻ [1]; 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻ [1]; Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂ [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl [1]

**(b)** Steamy (misty) fumes of hydrogen chloride [1]

**(c)** No oxidation states change; chloride ions are not oxidised to chlorine [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Any two from: steamy fumes of HBr, orange-brown fumes or vapour of bromine, colourless choking gas (SO₂) [2]

**(b)** 2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O [1]

**(c)** Sulfuric acid is the oxidising agent [1]; sulfur changes from +6 to +4 [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Sulfur dioxide with sulfur +4 [1], sulfur with oxidation state 0 [1], hydrogen sulfide with sulfur –2 [1]

**(b)** Iodide ions are larger than bromide ions, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus [1] and more shielded, so it is lost more easily [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** It supplies Ag⁺ ions, which form precipitates with halide ions [1]

**(b)** Carbonate and hydroxide ions would also form precipitates with silver ions [1]; the nitric acid reacts with them and removes them, so they do not give a false positive [1]

**(c)** It would form a precipitate of silver sulfate (with silver ions) [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Add dilute nitric acid and then silver nitrate solution to a sample of each [1]; chloride gives a white precipitate, bromide gives a cream precipitate and iodide gives a yellow precipitate [1]. To confirm, add dilute ammonia solution [1]: the white precipitate dissolves [1]; the cream precipitate dissolves only in concentrated ammonia and the yellow precipitate stays undissolved in concentrated ammonia [1]. **[5]**

**12.** n(NaI) = 0.0500 × 0.0200 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(AgI) = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol, Mᵣ(AgI) = 107.9 + 126.9 = 234.8 [1]; mass = 1.00 × 10⁻³ × 234.8 = **0.235 g** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Chloride ions are smaller than iodide ions, so the outer electron is closer to the nucleus and less shielded, and is not easily lost [1]; chloride ions are therefore weak reducing agents and cannot reduce sulfuric acid, whereas iodide ions are strong reducing agents [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the bromide precipitate as white or yellow.
- Writing that covalent bonds break when halogens boil.
- Stating that chloride ions reduce sulfuric acid.
- Omitting the reason why the silver nitrate test is acidified.
- Mixing up the dilute and concentrated ammonia results.
