OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Computer Science (9645) is a
modular course for students who want to learn to program properly and to
understand how computers, networks and databases work underneath the
code. It is a recently revised specification, first taught from September
2024, with first International AS exams in May/June 2025 and first
International A-level exams in May/June 2026. The full A-level is
assessed through four externally set papers: two AS papers worth 20% of
the A-level each and two A-level papers worth 30% each, with one paper at
each stage sat on screen in C#, Python or VB.Net.

## How the course is organised

The specification lists sixteen topics, split evenly between the AS and
the A-level stage.

**AS topics (1 to 8).** The first four are about writing and reasoning
about programs: procedural programming; fundamental data structures
(arrays and lists, records, queues and stacks); program design; and
searching and sorting algorithms. The second four are about the machine:
representing data, computer systems, computer organisation and
architecture, and machine code and assembly language.

**A-level topics (9 to 16).** The programming strand continues with
object-oriented and additional programming, advanced data structures and
advanced algorithms. The theory strand broadens into functional
programming, theory of computation, networking and cyber security,
databases, and artificial intelligence.

The split is not accidental. At both stages, roughly half the content is
"how to build software" and half is "how computing works", and the papers
follow that line exactly.

## How it's assessed

Every component is an externally assessed exam. There is no tiering.

| Paper | Content | Format | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Procedural programming, fundamental data structures, program design, searching and sorting | On-screen exam (C#, Python or VB.Net) | 120 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Representing data, computer systems, organisation and architecture, machine code and assembly | Written exam | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Procedural and object-oriented programming, data structures and algorithms | On-screen exam (C#, Python or VB.Net) | 150 minutes | 90 | 30% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Functional programming, theory of computation, networking and cyber security, databases, artificial intelligence | Written exam | 90 minutes | 75 | 30% |

Because the qualification is modular, the AS papers can be sat at the end
of the first year and stand as an International AS in their own right,
where each AS paper is worth 50% of the AS. Students continuing to the
full A-level then sit the two A-level papers, which together carry more
weight than the AS stage.

## How the parts fit together

The most useful way to picture 9645 is as two parallel strands running
through both years.

The **programming strand** runs AS Paper 1 into A-level Paper 1. Arrays,
records, queues and stacks from topic 2 are the foundation for the
advanced data structures of topic 10; the searching and sorting
algorithms of topic 4 lead into the advanced algorithms of topic 11; and
procedural programming in topic 1 is extended, not replaced, by the
object-oriented work in topic 9. The A-level on-screen paper names
procedural and object-oriented programming together, so the habits you
form in the first year are still being tested at the end of the second.

The **theory strand** runs AS Paper 2 into A-level Paper 2. Representing
data and machine code give you the low-level vocabulary that makes
networking, databases and theory of computation easier to follow. Some
second-year theory topics also feed back into programming: functional
programming is a different way of thinking about the same problems you
solve procedurally, and it pays to compare the two paradigms explicitly.

Two skills cross both strands: tracing an algorithm by hand, and
explaining precisely what a piece of code or hardware does. Practise both
from the start.

## Planning your study

A sensible first year teaches topics 1 to 4 alongside 5 to 8 rather than
one block after the other, so that programming practice continues every
week while the theory builds. Get a working development environment in
your chosen language early and write small programs constantly; the
on-screen papers reward fluency that only comes from regular coding.

In the second year, start with object-oriented programming and advanced
data structures, since both depend on first-year material that is still
fresh, then move through advanced algorithms. Spread the theory topics
(functional programming, theory of computation, networking and cyber
security, databases, artificial intelligence) across the year rather than
leaving them until the spring.

Begin timed questions on each topic as soon as it is taught, and move to
full past papers once a whole paper's content has been covered. The
course's exam-preparation page explains how to approach the on-screen and
written papers differently, so use that for paper technique rather than
inventing your own routine.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA A-level Computer Science 9645?**
There are four: AS Paper 1 and AS Paper 2, each worth 20% of the A-level, and A-level Paper 1 and A-level Paper 2, each worth 30%. All four are externally assessed exams.

**Which programming languages can I use in the on-screen exams?**
AS Paper 1 and A-level Paper 1 are on-screen exams available in C#, Python or VB.Net. Your school will normally choose one language for the class.

**Is there coursework in 9645?**
No. The assessment record lists four exams only: two on-screen programming papers and two written theory papers.

**How long are the 9645 papers?**
AS Paper 1 is 120 minutes, AS Paper 2 is 90 minutes, A-level Paper 1 is 150 minutes and A-level Paper 2 is 90 minutes.

**When was this specification first examined?**
It was first taught from September 2024, with first International AS exams in May/June 2025 and first International A-level exams in May/June 2026.
