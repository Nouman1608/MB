It tests every learning outcome in sections 2.1 Concept and use of a classification system and 2.2 Features of organisms. The 5090 syllabus is not tiered, so every question applies to all candidates. ## Questions

**1.** Frogs from two islands look slightly different. Scientists breed them together in a laboratory. The tadpoles grow into adults that mate and produce tadpoles of their own.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a species. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain whether the frogs from the two islands belong to the same species. **[2]**

**2.** Cabbage is *Brassica oleracea*, turnip is *Brassica rapa* and onion is *Allium cepa*.

**(a)** State which two of these plants are most closely related. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** A student writes the name of the cabbage by hand as "Brassica Oleracea". State **two** corrections needed. **[2]**

**3.**

**(a)** State one feature present in all plant cells but absent from all fungal cells. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the kingdom whose members have cells with no nucleus. **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** State **two** differences between reptiles and amphibians. **[2]**
**(b)** State **one** feature of fish that is not found in adult amphibians. **[1]**

**5.** The table describes four arthropods.

| Arthropod | Body sections | Pairs of legs | Pairs of antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| X | 2 | 4 | 0 |
| Y | 2 | 5 | 2 |
| Z | many similar segments | 2 on each segment | 1 |

**(a)** Name the arthropod group of each of W, X, Y and Z. **[4]**
**(b)** State **two** features shared by all arthropods that are not shown in the table. **[2]**

**6.** Plant P has seeds with one cotyledon and leaves with parallel veins. Plant Q has no flowers and has leaves called fronds.

**(a)** State whether P is a monocotyledon or a dicotyledon. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the number of petals in a flower of P. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the plant group of Q and state how Q reproduces. **[2]**
**(d)** State one feature that places both P and Q in the plant kingdom. **[1]**

**7.**

**(a)** State the **two** main features of a virus. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why viruses can only replicate inside living cells. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says that a virus is a type of prokaryote. Explain why this is incorrect. **[2]**

**8.** Use this dichotomous key to identify specimens A to D.

```
1 a  Has legs ................................ go to 2
  b  Has no legs ............................. go to 5
2 a  Three pairs of legs ..................... ant
  b  More than three pairs of legs ........... go to 3
3 a  Four pairs of legs ...................... mite
  b  More than four pairs of legs ............ go to 4
4 a  Two pairs of legs on most segments ...... millipede
  b  One pair of legs on each segment ........ woodlouse
5 a  Has a coiled shell ...................... snail
  b  No shell ................................ earthworm
```

Specimen A: no legs, no shell, body of ring-like segments. Specimen B: seven pairs of legs, one pair on each segment. Specimen C: four pairs of legs. Specimen D: three pairs of legs.

**(a)** Identify specimens A, B, C and D. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the arthropod group that specimen C belongs to. **[1]**

**9.** The table describes four leaves.

| Leaf | Divided into leaflets? | Veins | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | No | Parallel | Smooth |
| Q | Yes | Branching network | Toothed |
| R | No | Branching network | Toothed |
| S | No | Branching network | Smooth |

**(a)** Construct a dichotomous key to identify the four leaves. **[5]**
**(b)** State which leaf most likely comes from a monocotyledon. **[1]**

**10.** Three organisms are described.

- J is made of thread-like hyphae. Its cell walls are not made of cellulose and it has no chloroplasts. It secretes enzymes onto rotting fruit and absorbs the products.
- K is a single cell with a nucleus and chloroplasts. It swims using a flagellum.
- L is a single cell with a cell wall. It has circular DNA free in the cytoplasm and also has plasmids.

**(a)** Name the kingdom of each organism. For each, give one feature from the description that supports your answer. **[6]**
**(b)** A student suggests placing J and L in the same group because both can feed on rotting fruit. Explain why this is not a useful basis for classification. **[2]**

**11.** The table describes five vertebrates.

| Animal | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Fur on the body; external ears; feeds young on milk; front limbs are wings |
| B | Dry skin with scales; lays leathery-shelled eggs on land |
| C | Feathers; beak; cannot fly |
| D | Wet scales; fins; gills; lateral line |
| E | Moist skin with no scales; young live in water and have gills |

**(a)** Classify each animal into its vertebrate group. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why animal A is classified as a mammal and not a bird, even though it has wings. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A group of organisms that can reproduce [1] to produce fertile offspring [1].
**(b)** The island frogs produced offspring that were themselves able to reproduce [1], so the offspring are fertile and the frogs are **the same species** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Fertile" is the creditworthy word in both parts; an answer that only says the frogs "can breed" scores the first mark at most.

**2. (a)** **Cabbage and turnip** [1]; they share the same genus, *Brassica* [1].
**(b)** The species name should start with a lower-case letter: oleracea [1]. The handwritten name should be underlined [1].
*Examiner insight:* The reason in (a) must name the genus; "they have the same first name" without the term genus may not be credited.

**3. (a)** A cellulose cell wall [1].
**(b)** **Prokaryote** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Chloroplasts are not accepted in (a) because many plant cells, such as root cells, have none; "all plant cells" rules them out.

**4. (a)** Reptiles have dry, scaly skin; amphibians have moist skin with no scales [1]. Reptiles lay eggs with rubbery or leathery shells on land; amphibians lay soft eggs without shells in water [1].
**(b)** Any one: fins / scales / lateral line [1].
*Examiner insight:* A difference needs both sides stated; "reptiles have scales" alone does not show the contrast and may not earn the mark.

**5. (a)** W **insect** [1]; X **arachnid** [1]; Y **crustacean** [1]; Z **myriapod** [1].
**(b)** Jointed legs [1]; an exoskeleton [1]. (Also accept: a segmented body.)
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the question says "not shown in the table", so repeating numbers of legs or antennae earns nothing; read the stem carefully.

**6. (a)** **Monocotyledon** [1].
**(b)** Three, or a multiple of three (such as six) [1].
**(c)** **Fern** [1]; it reproduces by spores [1].
**(d)** Cellulose cell walls / chloroplasts so it can photosynthesise [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Q reproduces asexually" is not enough for (c); the syllabus feature is spores, and the answer must name them.

**7. (a)** A protein coat [1]; genetic material (DNA or RNA) [1].
**(b)** A virus is not a cell and has no structures to make copies of itself [1]; it must enter a living host cell and use the cell's materials to make new viruses [1].
**(c)** A prokaryote is a cell, with cytoplasm, a cell membrane, ribosomes and a cell wall [1]; a virus has only a protein coat around genetic material, so it is not a cell and belongs to no kingdom [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (c), two separate points are needed for two marks: what a prokaryote has, and what a virus lacks.

**8. (a)** A **earthworm** [1]; B **woodlouse** [1]; C **mite** [1]; D **ant** [1].
**(b)** **Arachnids** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Spelling of names taken from a key is usually tolerated, but a name not in the key, such as "spider" for C, scores zero.

**9. (a)** One correct key:

```
1 a  Leaf divided into leaflets ............ Q
  b  Leaf not divided ...................... go to 2
2 a  Parallel veins ........................ P
  b  Branching network of veins ............ go to 3
3 a  Toothed edge .......................... R
  b  Smooth edge ........................... S
```

Couplet 1 uses one feature with two opposite statements [1]. Couplet 2 uses one feature with two opposite statements [1]. Couplet 3 uses one feature with two opposite statements [1]. Three couplets in total, one fewer than the number of leaves [1]. Each leaf ends at its own letter and none appears twice [1].
**(b)** **P** [1], because it has parallel veins.
*Examiner insight:* Other valid keys score full marks; marks are lost when a couplet mixes features, such as "has leaflets" against "smooth edge".

**10. (a)** J **Fungus** [1]; hyphae / cell walls not made of cellulose / no chloroplasts with enzymes secreted and products absorbed [1].
K **Protoctist** [1]; single-celled but has a nucleus (and chloroplasts) [1].
L **Prokaryote** [1]; no nucleus, with circular DNA free in the cytoplasm / plasmids [1].
**(b)** Organisms are classified by features they share, such as cell structure [1]; J has a nucleus and L does not, so they share few features even though they feed in the same place [1].
*Examiner insight:* The supporting feature must come from the description and match the kingdom; "K is green" or "L is small" earns no credit.

**11. (a)** A **mammal** [1]; B **reptile** [1]; C **bird** [1]; D **fish** [1]; E **amphibian** [1].
**(b)** A has fur and external ears / feeds its young on milk, which are mammal features [1]; it has no feathers or beak, which every bird has [1].
*Examiner insight:* Wings are not a defining feature on their own; credit goes to the features the syllabus lists for each group.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "can interbreed" for a species with no mention of fertile offspring.
- Writing binomial names with a capital letter on the species name, or not underlining them by hand.
- Building a key with a couplet that compares two different features, or leaving an organism without its own end point.
- Using colour, size or where an organism lives as a key feature.
- Giving chloroplasts as a feature of all plant cells, when root cells have none.
- Saying prokaryotes lack DNA; they lack a nucleus.
- Counting arthropod legs as individual legs when the table gives pairs, or the reverse.
- Giving one-sided differences ("reptiles have scales") instead of a clear contrast.
- Describing a virus as a cell or as a bacterium.
- Naming an organism not listed in the key you were given.
