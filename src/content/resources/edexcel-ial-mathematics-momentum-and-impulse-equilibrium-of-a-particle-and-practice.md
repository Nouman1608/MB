## Questions

**1.** A particle of mass 0.6 kg moves at 9 m s⁻¹ along a smooth horizontal surface. It receives an impulse of magnitude 7.2 N s in the direction opposite to its motion. Find the speed and direction of the particle afterwards. **[3]**

**2.** A ball of mass 0.45 kg hits a fixed vertical wall at 14 m s⁻¹, perpendicular to the wall, and rebounds at 9 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the impulse the wall exerts on the ball. **[2]**
**(b)** The contact lasts 0.015 s. Find the average force on the ball. **[2]**

**3.** Particle A of mass 2.5 kg moves at 7 m s⁻¹ on a smooth horizontal line. It collides directly with particle B of mass 1.5 kg moving towards it at 3 m s⁻¹. After impact A keeps moving the same way, at 1 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Determine B's velocity just after impact. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude and direction of the impulse on B. **[2]**

**4.** A particle of mass 3 kg moving at 8 m s⁻¹ collides directly with a stationary particle B of mass m kg. The particles join and move at 2 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find m. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse on B. **[2]**

**5.** A block of weight 90 N lies on rough horizontal ground, with μ = 0.3 between the block and the ground. A horizontal force P acts on it and it does not move.

**(a)** When P = 20 N, find the friction force and the normal reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the largest P that keeps the block at rest. **[2]**

**6.** Three forces (3i − 2j) N, (pi + 5j) N and (−7i + qj) N act on a particle in equilibrium.

**(a)** Find p and q. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant of the first and third forces. **[2]**

**7.** A particle of mass 7 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string makes 25° with the horizontal and the other 55°, on opposite sides of the particle. Find the tension in each string. **[6]**

**8.** A 15 kg crate stands on a rough floor where the coefficient of friction is 0.25. A force P pushes it, directed 35° below the horizontal.

**(a)** Find the value of P at which the crate is just about to slip. **[5]**
**(b)** Show that if P = 40 N the crate stays at rest, and state the friction force. **[3]**

**9.** A uniform rod AB of length 4.8 m and mass 36 kg rests horizontally on supports at C and D. C is 0.6 m from A and D is 1.2 m from B.

**(a)** Calculate the reaction at each support. **[4]**
**(b)** State where the weight acts, and why. **[1]**

**10.** A uniform plank AB of length 6 m and mass 24 kg rests horizontally on supports at C and D. C is 1 m from A and D is 4 m from A. A child of mass 18 kg stands on the plank 4.5 m from A.

**(a)** Calculate the reaction at each support. **[5]**
**(b)** The child walks towards B. Find the greatest distance from A at which the plank stays in equilibrium on both supports. **[3]**

**11.** A uniform plank AB of length 4 m and mass 30 kg hangs horizontally from two vertical ropes attached at C and D, each 0.5 m from the nearer end. A load of mass 20 kg is placed at E, x m from A. Rope C can bear a tension of at most 330 N and rope D at most 350 N.

**(a)** For x = 3.5, find the tension in each rope. **[4]**
**(b)** Find every x for which neither rope is overloaded. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Take the original direction as positive. −7.2 = 0.6v − 0.6 × 9 [1]. 0.6v = 5.4 − 7.2 = −1.8 [1]. v = −3, so the particle moves at **3 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State a positive direction and put the sign of the impulse into the equation. A bare "3" omits the direction.

**2. (a)** Take away from the wall as positive. I = 0.45 × 9 − 0.45 × (−14) [1], so I = **10.35 N s** (10.4 N s to 3 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** Force = I / t = 10.35 / 0.015 [1] = **690 N** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the initial velocity as negative is the step people skip. Showing the substitution line makes a sign error easy to spot.

**3. (a)** Take A's direction as positive. 2.5 × 7 + 1.5 × (−3) [1] = 2.5 × 1 + 1.5v [1]. 13 = 2.5 + 1.5v, so v = 7. B moves at **7 m s⁻¹ in A's original direction** [1].
**(b)** I = 1.5 × 7 − 1.5 × (−3) [1] = **15 N s in A's original direction** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction of B's velocity in words. The impulse on A is −15 N s, so quote one of the two and check it against the other.

**4. (a)** 3 × 8 = (3 + m) × 2 [1], so 3 + m = 12 and **m = 9** [1].
**(b)** I = 9 × 2 − 9 × 0 [1] = **18 N s** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat joined particles as one common velocity. Check with the 3 kg particle: 3 × 2 − 3 × 8 = −18 N s.

**5. (a)** Vertical: R = 90 N [1]. Horizontal: friction balances P, so F = 20 N [1].
**(b)** F ≤ 0.3 × 90 = 27 [1], so the greatest P is **27 N** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (a) friction is 20 N, not μR. Writing F = μR when the block is only at rest gives 27 N.

**6. (a)** i: 3 + p − 7 = 0 [1], so **p = 4** [1]. j: −2 + 5 + q = 0, so **q = −3** [1].
**(b)** (3i − 2j) + (−7i − 3j) = (−4i − 5j) [1]. Magnitude = √(16 + 25) = **√41 = 6.40 N** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Resolve each component separately and show both equations. In part (b) the answer equals the magnitude of the second force, which is a quick consistency check.

**7.** Weight = 7 × 9.8 = 68.6 N [1]. Horizontal: T₁ cos 25° = T₂ cos 55° [1]. Vertical: T₁ sin 25° + T₂ sin 55° = 68.6 [1]. Substituting T₁ = T₂ cos 55° / cos 25°: T₂(cos 55° tan 25° + sin 55°) = 68.6 [1]. **T₂ = 63.1 N** [1], **T₁ = 40.0 N** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Mark the angles on a force diagram. Keep full values until the last line.

**8. (a)** Vertical: R = 15 × 9.8 + P sin 35° = 147 + P sin 35° [1]. Horizontal: F = P cos 35° [1]. Limiting: F = 0.25R [1]. P cos 35° = 0.25(147 + P sin 35°), so P(cos 35° − 0.25 sin 35°) = 36.75 [1]. **P = 54.4 N** [1].
**(b)** R = 147 + 40 sin 35° = 169.9 [1]. Greatest friction = 0.25 × 169.9 = 42.5 N [1]. Friction needed = 40 cos 35° = 32.8 N, which is less than 42.5 N, so the crate stays at rest and the **friction force is 32.8 N** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A downward push raises R. Say in words that the friction needed is less than the greatest available, since "show that" wants the reasoning.

**9. (a)** CD = 4.8 − 0.6 − 1.2 = 3 m and the weight acts 2.4 m from A, which is 1.8 m from C. Weight = 36 × 9.8 = 352.8 N. Moments about C: R_D × 3 = 352.8 × 1.8 [1], with both lever arms correct [1]. **R_D = 212 N** (211.68 N) [1]. R_C = 352.8 − 211.68 = **141 N** [1].
**(b)** At the midpoint, 2.4 m from each end, because the rod is uniform [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking moments about C removes the unknown R_C. "Uniform" is the modelling assumption that justifies the weight's position, so quote it.

**10. (a)** Weights: plank 24 × 9.8 = 235.2 N at 3 m from A, child 18 × 9.8 = 176.4 N at 4.5 m from A. Distances from C: 2 m and 3.5 m. Moments about C: R_D × 3 = 235.2 × 2 + 176.4 × 3.5 [1], with correct distances [1]. R_D × 3 = 1087.8, so **R_D = 362.6 N** [1]. Vertical: R_C + R_D = 411.6 [1], so **R_C = 49 N** [1].
**(b)** About to tilt about D, so R_C = 0. Let the child be y m beyond D. Moments about D: 235.2 × 1 = 176.4y [1]. y = 4/3 [1]. Distance from A = 4 + 4/3 = **5.33 m** (3 s.f.) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Greatest distance" signals a limiting condition, so set the reaction at the other support to zero. Check that your answer lies on the plank, here 5.33 m is less than 6 m.

**11. (a)** Weights: plank 294 N, load 196 N, total 490 N. E is above D. Moments about C: T_D × 3 = 294 × 1.5 + 196 × 3 [1], with correct distances [1]. **T_D = 343 N** [1]. **T_C = 490 − 343 = 147 N** [1].
**(b)** Moments about C with signed distance x − 0.5: 3T_D = 441 + 196(x − 0.5) [1]. T_D ≤ 350 gives 441 + 196(x − 0.5) ≤ 1050, so x ≤ 3.607 [1]. T_C = 490 − T_D, and T_C ≤ 330 needs T_D ≥ 160 [1]. Then 441 + 196(x − 0.5) ≥ 480, so x ≥ 0.699 [1]. Both limits needed: **0.699 ≤ x ≤ 3.61** (3 s.f.) [1]. Check that neither rope goes slack: both tensions stay positive in this range [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert each limit to a condition on x before solving, and give a two-sided range. An upper limit alone answers half the question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a speed where a signed velocity is needed in an impulse or collision equation.
- Leaving out the direction of an impulse or a final velocity.
- Writing F = μR for a block that is only at rest.
- Treating R as the weight when the applied force has a vertical part.
- Taking a moment with the distance along a rod rather than the perpendicular distance.
- Applying "about to tilt" without setting the correct reaction to zero.
- Leaving out the final comparison in a "show that" question.
