These questions cover sections 3.2.3 (Structure and bonding of carbon) and 3.2.4 (Nanoparticles) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. There are no tiers, so all of these questions are relevant to you. Short recall questions come first; the last few combine ideas and include calculations.

Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-structure-and-bonding-of-carbon-and-nanoparticles/) or the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-structure-and-bonding-of-carbon-and-nanoparticles-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ of C = 12 and Avogadro's constant = 6.02 × 10²³ per mole where needed.

## Questions

**1.** State the number of covalent bonds that a carbon atom can form. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why diamond is very hard. **[2]**

**3.** This question is about graphite.

**(a)** State the number of other carbon atoms that each carbon atom is bonded to in graphite. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why graphite is soft and slippery. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why graphite conducts electricity, and state one way in which graphite is similar to a metal. **[3]**

**5.** Fullerenes are another form of carbon.

**(a)** State what the structure of fullerenes is based on. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two uses of fullerenes. **[2]**

**6.** Three particles have these diameters: particle P, 0.07 µm; particle R, 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ m; particle S, 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ m.

**(a)** State the size range of structures studied in nanoscience. **[1]**
**(b)** Convert each diameter to nanometres and identify which particles are nanoparticles. **[3]**

**7.** A catalyst can be made as cube-shaped nanoparticles of side 15 nm or as cube-shaped grains of side 1.5 µm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of one nanoparticle. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio for one grain, and how many times larger the nanoparticle ratio is. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the same mass of catalyst works faster as nanoparticles. **[1]**

**8.** Read this information.

*Compound W is a white solid that absorbs ultraviolet light. As a powder of 30 µm particles it leaves a visible white layer on skin. As 30 nm particles it absorbs ultraviolet light just as well but cannot be seen on the skin.*

**(a)** Treating the particles as cubes, show that the 30 nm particles have a surface area to volume ratio 1000 times that of the 30 µm particles. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the information to suggest why a sun cream maker chooses the 30 nm form. **[2]**
**(c)** Apart from suntan creams, name one other type of cosmetic that the specification gives as a possible use of nanoparticles. **[1]**

**9.** Substance J is an element made only of carbon atoms. It has a very high melting point, conducts electricity as a solid, and leaves a dark mark when rubbed on paper because it rubs off easily.

**(a)** Identify J. Explain each of its three properties in terms of its structure and bonding. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why diamond, rather than J, is used to coat the tips of drill bits. **[2]**

**10.** A graphite electrode has a mass of 0.36 g.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of carbon atoms in the electrode. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of delocalised electrons in the electrode. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why 0.36 g of diamond does not conduct electricity. **[2]**
**(d)** Graphite also conducts heat. State what is responsible for this. **[1]**

**11.** A chemist compares 1.0 cm³ of a catalyst as one cube with the same volume divided into cubes of side 10 nm.

**(a)** Convert 1.0 cm to nanometres. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of 10 nm cubes that 1.0 cm³ makes. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the total surface area of these cubes, first in nm² and then in cm². **[3]**
**(d)** The single 1.0 cm cube has a surface area of 6.0 cm². Compare this with your answer to (c) and explain why nanoparticle catalysts can save metal. **[2]**

**12.** Read this information.

*Fullerene nanotubes are tubes made of hexagonal rings of carbon atoms. They are very strong but have a low density. Some other fullerenes form hollow cages large enough to hold a small molecule.*

**(a)** Suggest why nanotubes are added to the frames of tennis rackets. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest how cage-shaped fullerenes could be used to deliver a drug into the body. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **Four** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "State" needs only the number; an added reason that is wrong can contradict an otherwise correct answer.

**2.** Each carbon atom forms **four covalent bonds** in a **giant covalent structure** [1]. Many **strong** bonds must be broken, needing a lot of energy [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Strong bonds" alone is too thin for two marks; name the four bonds per atom or the giant structure as well.

**3. (a)** **Three** [1]
**(b)** There are **no covalent bonds between the layers**, only weak forces [1], so the **layers slide over each other** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing that the covalent bonds in graphite are weak is chemically wrong and loses the first mark.

**4.** One electron from each carbon atom is **delocalised** [1]. These electrons **move through the structure** and carry the current [1]. Like a metal, graphite **has delocalised electrons** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mention of ions or atoms moving cancels the explanation; only electrons move in graphite.

**5. (a)** **Hexagonal rings** of carbon atoms [1]
**(b)** Any two of: drug delivery into the body; lubricants; catalysts; nanotubes for reinforcing materials [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* Vague answers such as "medicine" or "sport" are weaker than the specification wording; write "drug delivery" and "reinforcing tennis rackets".

**6. (a)** **1–100 nm** [1]
**(b)** P: 0.07 × 1000 = 70 nm; R: 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 1 × 10⁻⁹ = 250 nm; S: 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 1 × 10⁻⁹ = 8 nm [1]. P is a nanoparticle [1]. S is a nanoparticle; R is not [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each converted value; a bare list of letters gives the examiner nothing to credit if one conversion slips.

**7. (a)** Surface area = 6 × 15² = 1350 nm²; volume = 15³ = 3375 nm³ [1]. Ratio = 1350 ÷ 3375 = **0.4 nm⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** 1.5 µm = 1500 nm, so ratio = 6 ÷ 1500 = 0.004 nm⁻¹ [1]. 0.4 ÷ 0.004 = **100 times** larger [1]
**(c)** More of the catalyst is exposed at the surface, so more reacting particles can make contact with it [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert both lengths to the same unit before dividing; using 1.5 instead of 1500 gives a factor that is badly wrong.

**8. (a)** 30 nm: 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 nm⁻¹; 30 µm = 30 000 nm: 6 ÷ 30 000 = 0.0002 nm⁻¹ [1]. 0.2 ÷ 0.0002 = 1000 [1]
**(b)** The 30 nm form absorbs ultraviolet light as well as the powder [1] but cannot be seen, so it does not leave a white layer on the skin [1].
**(c)** **Deodorants** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" the final value is given, so every step of the working must appear to earn the marks.

**9. (a)** J is **graphite** [1]. Very high melting point: many **strong covalent bonds** must break [1]. Conducts: one electron per carbon atom is **delocalised** and moves through the structure [1]. Rubs off: each atom bonds to three others in **layers** [1] with **only weak forces between the layers**, so layers slide off [1].
**(b)** Diamond has **four covalent bonds per carbon atom**, giving a rigid giant structure [1], so diamond is **very hard** and is not worn away like graphite [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each property needs its own structural reason; one general sentence about "strong bonds" cannot explain all three.

**10. (a)** n = 0.36 ÷ 12 = **0.030 mol** [1]
**(b)** Atoms = 0.030 × 6.02 × 10²³ [1] = **1.81 × 10²²**, one delocalised electron per atom [1]
**(c)** In diamond, **all four outer electrons** of every carbon atom are used in covalent bonds [1], so there are **no delocalised electrons** to move [1].
**(d)** The **delocalised electrons** [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the one-electron-per-atom link in (b); a number without it does not show why atoms equal electrons.

**11. (a)** 1 cm = 1 × 10⁻² m ÷ 1 × 10⁻⁹ m = **1.0 × 10⁷ nm** [1]
**(b)** Volume = (1.0 × 10⁷)³ = 1.0 × 10²¹ nm³ [1]. Number = 1.0 × 10²¹ ÷ 10³ = **1.0 × 10¹⁸** [1]
**(c)** Area of one cube = 6 × 10² = 600 nm² [1]. Total = 1.0 × 10¹⁸ × 600 = 6.0 × 10²⁰ nm² [1]. 1 nm² = 1 × 10⁻¹⁴ cm², so total = **6.0 × 10⁶ cm²** [1]
**(d)** The nanoparticles have **1 000 000 (10⁶) times** the surface area [1]. A much smaller mass of catalyst gives the same surface for reactions, so less metal is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* Area units square the conversion factor; using 10⁻⁷ instead of 10⁻¹⁴ for nm² to cm² is an easy slip to make.

**12. (a)** Nanotubes are very strong [1], and their low density means they reinforce the frame without adding much mass [1].
**(b)** The drug molecule is held **inside the hollow cage** [1], which carries it through the body to where it is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions reward answers built from the information given; quote the property, then link it to the use.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying graphite is soft because covalent bonds are weak, instead of weak forces between layers.
- Saying ions, atoms or "particles" carry the current in graphite.
- Explaining diamond's hardness with "strong bonds" but not four bonds per atom or a giant structure.
- Missing the lower limit when quoting the nanoscale range.
- Dividing lengths in different units in a surface area to volume calculation.
- Converting area with the length factor (10⁻⁷) instead of its square (10⁻¹⁴).
- Ignoring the information given in "suggest" questions and writing a memorised answer.
- Giving one structural reason for several properties.
