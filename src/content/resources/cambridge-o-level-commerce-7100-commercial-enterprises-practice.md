## Section A

**1.** State the difference between a private sector enterprise and a public sector enterprise. **[2]**

**2.** Define *limited liability*. **[1]**

**3.** State **three** features of a sole trader. **[3]**

**4.** Identify **two** features of a public corporation. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** reasons why multinational companies exist in the global economy. **[2]**

**6.** Distinguish between a *franchisor* and a *franchisee*. **[2]**

**7.** Name **four** of the physical location factors listed in the syllabus. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Zara and Imran run a successful printing business as a partnership. They want to buy new machinery costing $250,000 and are considering forming a private limited company. Explain **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of this change. **[6]**

**9.** Thirty small dairy farmers each sell their milk separately to a large processor at low prices. Explain why forming a cooperative would be appropriate for them. **[4]**

**10.** A government owns the national railway as a public corporation. Explain **three** reasons why it might keep the railway in the public sector. **[6]**

**11.** A multinational clothing company plans to open a large factory in a host country. Assess whether the host country will benefit. **[8]**

**12.** Hamid wants to open a fast-food restaurant. He can either start his own independent business or buy a franchise from a well-known chain. Discuss which option he should choose. **[8]**

**13.** TechNest is an online retailer of electronics that does not have a shop. Explain which **two** ecommerce location factors are most important for TechNest when choosing where to locate. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A private sector enterprise is owned by private individuals or investors [1]; a public sector enterprise is owned and controlled by the government on behalf of the public [1].

**2.** The owners can lose only the money they have invested in the business, not their personal possessions [1].

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: owned and controlled by one person; unlimited liability; owner keeps all the profit; capital limited to the owner's savings and loans; easy and quick to set up; the business and the owner are legally the same [1] [1] [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: owned by the state; run by a board appointed by the government; financed by government funds and customers' payments; aims to provide a service; accountable to the government [1] [1].

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: to reach new markets; to use cheaper labour, land or raw materials; to avoid trade barriers such as tariffs; to reduce transport costs by producing near customers; to spread risk; to gain economies of scale; to benefit from host government incentives [1] [1].

**6.** The franchisor owns the brand and sells the right to use its name, products and methods [1]; the franchisee buys that right and runs an outlet under the franchisor's rules [1].

**7.** Any four, 1 mark each: labour; markets; transport; power; raw materials; competition; suppliers; communication; government influence [1] [1] [1] [1].

**8.** **Advantages:** limited liability [1], so if the loan for the $250,000 machinery cannot be repaid, Zara and Imran would not lose their personal possessions [1]; they can raise capital by selling shares privately, for example to family or employees [1], reducing the amount they must borrow for the machinery [1]. **Disadvantage:** the company must be registered and file accounts [1], which adds cost and means some financial information is no longer private [1].

**9.** In a cooperative the farmers would own the business together as members [1]. Selling their milk jointly gives them bargaining power [1], so they could obtain higher prices from the processor [1]. They could also share costs such as a chilling tank or transport, with each member having one vote, so no single farmer dominates [1].

**10.** Any three, each with development, 2 marks per reason. **Essential service**: many people depend on the railway to reach work, so the state keeps fares affordable. **Unprofitable routes**: rural lines may make a loss, so a private firm would close them, but the state can keep them open. **Strategic importance**: rail moves freight and could be needed in an emergency, so the government wants control. **Avoiding a private monopoly**: a single private owner could raise fares without competition. **Very large investment**: track and trains need more capital than private investors may risk [1] [1] [1] [1] [1] [1].

**11.** **Benefits:** jobs for local workers [1], and the factory may train them in new skills [1]; tax revenue for the government from the factory's profits and workers' incomes [1]; exports of clothing earn foreign currency [1]. **Drawbacks:** profits may be sent back to the MNC's home country [1]; local clothing makers may be unable to compete and close [1]; workers may be paid low wages or the factory may pollute rivers [1]. **Judgement**: the host country is likely to benefit overall if the jobs and skills are long-term and the government enforces labour and environmental rules [1].

**12.** **Franchise for:** an established brand brings customers from day one [1]; the franchisor gives training, supplies and national advertising, lowering the risk of failure [1]. **Franchise against:** Hamid pays an initial fee and royalties, reducing his profit [1]; he must follow the franchisor's menu, prices and decor, so he has little freedom [1]. **Independent for:** Hamid keeps all profits [1] and can design his own menu for local tastes [1]. **Independent against:** he must build a reputation from nothing and has no support, so the risk of failure is higher [1]. **Judgement**: for example, a franchise suits Hamid if he lacks restaurant experience, because the lower risk outweighs the cost of royalties [1].

**13.** **Logistics** [1]: TechNest depends on fast delivery to customers' homes, so it should locate near parcel carriers and main roads [1]. **Warehousing** [1]: it needs a large, secure building to store electronics before dispatch, so space and rent matter more than passing trade [1]. (Wi-fi or technology specialists, with a reason linked to running the website, are acceptable alternatives.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: saying public sector means "owned by shareholders".
- Question 8: giving advantages of limited companies in general without linking them to the machinery purchase.
- Question 10: listing reasons with no explanation linked to a railway.
- Question 11: missing the judgement, which an assess question requires.
- Question 13: choosing physical factors such as "markets" or "competition" for a business that has no shop.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I recommend either option in Question 12?** Yes. Either is credited if the judgement follows from the points made and refers to Hamid's situation.

**Why does Question 10 give 2 marks per reason?** An explain question wants each reason developed, here by linking it to a railway.
