Subtopic 8.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers interference, coherence, two-source demonstrations, the conditions for fringes and the double-slit equation. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Interference study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-interference/); to apply the ideas, try the [Interference practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-interference-practice/).

## Core definitions

- **Interference**: the superposition of waves from coherent sources, producing a stable pattern of maxima and minima.
- **Coherent sources**: sources with a **constant phase difference** (so they must have the same frequency).
- **Path difference**: the difference in distance from the two sources to a point.
- **Phase difference**: how far one oscillation is ahead of the other, as a fraction of a cycle, in degrees or radians.

## Path difference and what you get

For sources **in phase**:

```
maximum:  path difference = nλ            (phase difference 0, 2π, 4π ...)
minimum:  path difference = (n + ½)λ      (phase difference π, 3π, 5π ...)
```

Convert: phase difference = 2π × (path difference ÷ λ).

For sources **in antiphase**, swap the two lines: nλ gives a minimum, (n + ½)λ gives a maximum. The centre line becomes a minimum.

## Resultant amplitude and intensity

| Point | Resultant amplitude (two waves of amplitude A) | Intensity (relative to one source alone) |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum | 2A | 4 times |
| Minimum | 0 | 0 |
| Unequal amplitudes A₁ > A₂ | max A₁ + A₂, min A₁ − A₂ | minima not fully dark |

Intensity ∝ amplitude². Energy is redistributed, not destroyed.

## Conditions for observable two-source fringes

1. Sources **coherent** (constant phase difference).
2. Waves **overlap**: each slit narrow enough to diffract light into the shared region.
3. Amplitudes **similar**, so minima are close to zero and contrast is good.
4. For light: **a small and D large**, so x is big enough to see and measure.
5. Transverse waves must oscillate in the same plane to cancel fully.

## The four demonstrations

| Wave | Two coherent sources | Detector |
|---|---|---|
| Water (ripple tank) | Two dippers on one vibrating bar | Eye or shadow on a screen: calm lines and rough regions |
| Sound | Two speakers on one signal generator | Microphone and oscilloscope, or ear |
| Light | Laser on a double slit | Fringes on a screen |
| Microwaves | One transmitter, metal sheet with two gaps | Receiver probe and meter |

Moving a detector from one maximum to the **next** maximum changes the path difference by **one wavelength**; from a maximum to the adjacent minimum, by **half a wavelength**.

## The double-slit equation

```
λ = ax / D
a = slit separation (centre to centre)
x = fringe spacing (bright to bright)
D = slit-to-screen distance
```

- Valid when D ≫ a (small angles, evenly spaced fringes).
- x ∝ λ, x ∝ D, x ∝ 1/a.
- Measure across many spacings: from bright fringe 1 to bright fringe 11 is **10 spacings**.
- Convert millimetres to metres before substituting.

**Quick check example.** a = 0.60 mm, D = 1.80 m, λ = 480 nm gives x = λD/a = (480 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.80) ÷ (0.60 × 10⁻³) = 1.44 × 10⁻³ m, about 1.4 mm.

**Changing two variables at once.** If D is multiplied by 1.5 and a is halved, x is multiplied by 1.5 × 2 = 3.

## Special cases to recall

- **White light**: central fringe white (zero path difference for every colour); other fringes coloured, blue edge on the inside, red on the outside; the pattern blurs after a few fringes.
- **One slit covered**: no interference; only a single-slit diffraction patch remains.
- **Two separate lamps**: no stable fringes, because their phase difference changes randomly.
- **Microwaves**: wavelength a few centimetres, so a and D are comparable and λ = ax/D is only approximate; use path differences instead.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Superposition | Resultant displacement is the vector sum of individual displacements |
| Constructive interference | Waves arrive in phase; resultant amplitude is a maximum |
| Destructive interference | Waves arrive in antiphase; resultant amplitude is a minimum |
| Coherent | Constant phase difference |
| Antiphase | Phase difference of π rad (180°) |
| Fringe spacing x | Distance between centres of adjacent bright (or dark) fringes |
| Slit separation a | Distance between the centres of the two slits |

## Exam traps

- Writing "same frequency" or "in phase" as the definition of coherent. The mark is for *constant phase difference*.
- Counting fringes rather than spacings.
- Leaving a or x in millimetres.
- Assuming sources are in phase when the question says one is reversed or in antiphase.
- Claiming the central fringe is a minimum for in-phase sources (it is a maximum).
- Quoting x ∝ a instead of x ∝ 1/a.
- Saying waves "cancel and lose energy" at minima.

## Self-test

1. Define coherence.
2. Two in-phase sources give a path difference of 2.5λ at a point. State the phase difference and the type of interference.
3. Repeat question 2 for sources in antiphase.
4. State what happens to the fringe spacing if the slit separation is doubled.
5. Why is the central fringe white when white light is used?
6. A detector moves from a maximum to the adjacent minimum. By how much has the path difference changed?
7. Give two reasons why a "minimum" in a sound interference experiment may not be silent.
8. Fringes are measured from bright fringe 1 to bright fringe 9 as 12.0 mm. What is x?
9. Why do two dippers on the same bar act as coherent sources?
10. Each of two waves has amplitude A at a maximum. How does the intensity compare with one wave alone?
11. Why must each slit be narrow?
12. Calculate λ when a = 0.50 mm, x = 2.4 mm and D = 2.0 m.

**Answers:** 1. Sources with a constant phase difference. 2. 5π rad, equivalent to π rad: destructive (minimum). 3. Constructive (maximum). 4. It halves, since x ∝ 1/a. 5. Path difference is zero for every wavelength at the centre, so every colour has a maximum there. 6. Half a wavelength. 7. The two amplitudes at that point are unequal; reflections from walls and background noise also reach it. 8. 12.0 ÷ 8 = 1.5 mm. 9. Both are driven by the same motor, so they have the same frequency and a fixed phase difference. 10. Amplitude 2A, so four times the intensity. 11. So that diffraction spreads the light from each slit into the region where it overlaps light from the other slit. 12. λ = (0.50 × 10⁻³ × 2.4 × 10⁻³) ÷ 2.0 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m.
