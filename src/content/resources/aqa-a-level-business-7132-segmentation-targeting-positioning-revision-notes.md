Condensed notes on section **3.3.3 Making marketing decisions: segmentation, targeting, positioning** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and the Fenwick Fitness worked example are in the [segmentation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-segmentation-targeting-positioning/); exam-style questions are in the [segmentation practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-segmentation-targeting-positioning-practice/).

## Specification content

- The process and value of segmentation, targeting and positioning.
- Segmentation methods: demographic, geographic, income, behavioural.
- Influences on choosing a target market and positioning.
- Targeting may include niche and mass marketing.
- Positioning should include market mapping.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market segment | A group of customers sharing characteristics that affect how they buy |
| Segmentation | Dividing a market into such groups |
| Targeting | Choosing which segment or segments to serve |
| Positioning | How customers see the product compared with rivals |
| Repositioning | Changing that image |
| Niche marketing | Focusing on a small, specialised segment |
| Mass marketing | Selling a standard product to a large part of the market |
| Market map | Diagram placing products on two axes that matter to customers |

## The process

1. **Segment**: divide the market.
2. **Target**: choose segment(s).
3. **Position**: decide the image and build the marketing mix to deliver it.

## Segmentation methods

| Method | Based on | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Demographic | Age, gender, family, occupation | Toys for under-fives |
| Geographic | Region, urban or rural, climate | Waterproof clothing for wet regions |
| Income | Income bands, spending power | Premium and budget ranges |
| Behavioural | Usage, loyalty, benefits sought, occasion | Loyalty schemes, gift ranges |

**A good segment is** identifiable and measurable; large enough to be profitable; reachable; responsive.

**Value:** focused spending; products matched to needs; spots gaps; different prices for different segments; builds loyalty.

**Costs and risks:** research cost; several mixes may be needed; segment may be small; behaviour changes; may ignore customers outside the segment.

## Niche versus mass

| | Mass | Niche |
|---|---|---|
| Sales volume | High | Low |
| Competition | Heavy | Often less |
| Price | Often competitive | Can be higher |
| Marketing cost | High in total | Low in total, more targeted |
| Risk | Product suits nobody exactly | Dependence on one segment; larger rival may enter |
| Typical firm | Large | Small or specialist |

A firm may also use **differentiated marketing**, with a different mix for each of several segments.

## Influences on choosing a target market

Size and growth of segment; profit potential; competition; the business's resources and strengths; marketing objectives; ethical issues (for example marketing to children); technology (data and social media make small segments easier to reach).

## Positioning and market maps

- Choose a position (cheapest, most reliable, most stylish) and make the marketing mix consistent with it.
- **Market map:** two axes, for example price and quality. Shows competitors' positions and **gaps**.
- A gap is not proof of an opportunity: there may be no demand, or it may not be possible to supply at a profit.
- Limits: axes are a judgement; a snapshot in time; reflects the firm's view rather than customers'.
- **Repositioning:** changing the image (for example moving upmarket) needs changes across the whole mix.

## Revenue potential of a segment

Revenue potential = size of segment × proportion expected to buy × average spend.

## Worked example to remember

A wearable tracker firm studies three segments.

| Segment | Size | Expected buyers | Average spend | Revenue potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 120,000 | 10% | £60 | 120,000 × 0.10 × £60 = £720,000 |
| Young professionals | 80,000 | 15% | £150 | 80,000 × 0.15 × £150 = £1,800,000 |
| Retired people | 50,000 | 8% | £120 | 50,000 × 0.08 × £120 = £480,000 |

The largest segment (students) is not the most valuable. Young professionals give 60% of the £3 million total because they buy more often and spend more. Before choosing, check cost of reaching each segment, competition and fit with the firm's strengths. The figures are estimates, so state that they carry uncertainty.

## How to write a good answer

- Name the method from the evidence in the case (age is demographic, location is geographic, income band is income, buying habits are behavioural).
- Say who the target market is, then say how the product, price, promotion and place suit them.
- For niche or mass, weigh the firm's size, resources and objectives, and make a judgement.

## Exam traps

- Segmentation divides, targeting chooses, positioning creates the image.
- Match the method to the case: age is demographic; location is geographic.
- "Niche" means a specific small segment, not just a small firm.
- A gap on a market map needs checking before action.
- A luxury position is undone by constant discounting.

## Self-test

1. Name the three steps of the segmentation, targeting and positioning process.
2. Which method of segmentation is used by a retailer that opens stores only in northern towns?
3. A gym sells a cheaper membership for students. Which method is this?
4. Give two features of a good market segment.
5. Give two benefits of segmentation.
6. State one advantage and one disadvantage of niche marketing.
7. Why might a large manufacturer prefer mass marketing?
8. A segment has 60,000 people, 5% are expected to buy and they spend £80. Calculate revenue potential.
9. What is a market map?
10. A market map shows no product at high quality and low price. Why should the business not assume it can launch one at a profit?
11. Give two influences on the choice of target market.
12. Why might a brand sold at a premium damage its positioning by frequent discount offers?

### Answers

1. Segmentation, targeting, positioning.
2. Geographic.
3. Demographic (age or occupation); it may also be described as income segmentation if based on spending power.
4. Any two: identifiable and measurable; large enough to be profitable; reachable; responsive.
5. Any two: focused spending; products matched to needs; spots gaps; supports different prices; builds loyalty.
6. Advantage (any one): less competition; higher prices; strong loyalty. Disadvantage (any one): limited growth; dependence on one segment; larger rival may enter.
7. It can use economies of scale and its large capacity to sell to many customers at a low unit cost.
8. 60,000 × 0.05 × £80 = 3,000 buyers × £80 = **£240,000**.
9. A diagram that places products or brands on two axes that matter to customers, showing competitors' positions and gaps.
10. The gap may exist because it costs too much to make such a product, or because there is too little demand; more research is needed.
11. Any two: size and growth of the segment; profit potential; competition; the firm's resources; marketing objectives; ethical issues.
12. Discounting signals lower quality and sends a message that conflicts with the premium image, confusing customers.
