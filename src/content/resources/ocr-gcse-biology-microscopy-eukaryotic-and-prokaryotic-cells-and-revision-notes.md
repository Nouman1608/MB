## B1.1a: Light microscope and staining

### Parts and jobs

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Lamp | Lights the specimen from below |
| Stage | Holds the slide |
| Objective lens | First magnification; rotating turret, several powers |
| Eyepiece lens | Second magnification; the lens you look through |
| Coarse and fine focus | Sharpen the image |
| Slide and cover slip | Hold a thin specimen flat; cover slip keeps it flat, stops it drying and protects the lens |

**Total magnification = eyepiece × objective.** For example ×10 × ×40 = ×400.

### Stains

- Cells are mostly colourless, so little contrast shows without a stain.
- A stain either makes a colourless specimen visible, or colours different structures or tissues differently so they can be told apart.
- Typical pairings: methylene blue with cheek cells, iodine solution with onion epidermis.

### Method in steps: preparing and viewing a slide

1. Place a thin layer of cells in a drop of liquid on a slide.
2. Add a drop of stain.
3. Lower a cover slip at an angle with a needle so no air bubbles are trapped.
4. Clip on the stage; start with the lowest-power objective.
5. Focus with coarse then fine focus; change to a higher-power objective only when sharp.
6. Draw with a pencil: clear lines, no shading, label lines not crossing, title with the magnification.

### Magnification formula triangle

| Want | Method |
|---|---|
| Magnification | divide image by actual |
| Actual size | divide image by magnification |
| Image size | multiply actual by magnification |

**Unit conversions:** ×1000 going from mm to µm, and again from µm to nm (so ×1 000 000 from mm to nm); divide to go back.

**Standard form:** a number between 1 and 10 times a power of 10. 0.0000032 m = 3.2 × 10⁻⁶ m. 4500 nm = 4.5 × 10³ nm = 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ m.

### Worked reminders

- Image 24 mm, ×400: actual = 24 ÷ 400 = 0.06 mm = 60 µm.
- Scale bar 25 mm labelled 10 µm: magnification = 25 000 µm ÷ 10 µm = ×2500.
- Actual 1.5 µm, ×8000: image = 12 000 µm = 12 mm.
- Drawing 50 mm, real length 0.25 mm: magnification = 50 ÷ 0.25 = ×200.

**Estimation:** a rough value from a quick count or round numbers, used to check a calculated answer. If 5 cells fit across a 2.0 mm field, each is about 0.4 mm (400 µm). A calculated answer of 4 mm for one cell must be wrong.

## B1.1b: Sub-cellular structures

| Structure | Key fact to state | Cells |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Contains the genetic material (chromosomes); controls the cell | Animal, plant |
| Genetic material | DNA; genes carry instructions for proteins | All |
| Chromosomes | Long DNA molecules carrying many genes | Linear in nucleus; one circular chromosome in bacteria |
| Plasmids | Small rings of extra DNA; genes such as antibiotic resistance | Bacteria |
| Mitochondria | Contain enzymes for cellular respiration | Animal, plant |
| Chloroplasts | Contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis | Green plant cells |
| Cell membrane | Selective barrier; receptor molecules | All |
| Ribosomes | Site of protein synthesis | All |

Other structures that appear on diagrams: cytoplasm (where many reactions occur), plant cell wall (cellulose), permanent vacuole (plants), bacterial cell wall (not cellulose).

### Eukaryote against prokaryote

| | Eukaryotic | Prokaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Yes | No; DNA free in cytoplasm |
| DNA | Several linear chromosomes | One main circular chromosome plus plasmids |
| Mitochondria | Yes | No |
| Chloroplasts | In some plant cells | No |
| Ribosomes | Yes | Yes |
| Size | Larger | Much smaller (around a few µm) |

### Must-know distinctions

- **Genetic material or chromosome?** Genetic material is the DNA; a chromosome is one long coiled DNA molecule.
- **Chromosome or plasmid?** Plasmids are small extra rings, not the main chromosome.
- **Mitochondria or chloroplasts?** Mitochondria are for respiration (release energy) and are in nearly all eukaryotic cells; chloroplasts are for photosynthesis (use light) and only in some plant cells.
- **Cell wall or cell membrane?** The membrane controls what crosses; the wall supports.

### Structure and function pairings

- Many mitochondria: cell uses lots of energy (muscle).
- Many chloroplasts: cell photosynthesises (palisade layer of a leaf).
- Many ribosomes: cell makes lots of protein.
- Receptors on the membrane: only cells with the matching receptor respond to a given chemical signal.

## B1.1c: Electron microscopy

- TEM sends electrons through a very thin specimen.
- Higher **resolution** than a light microscope: closer points are seen as separate.
- Light microscope resolution is limited to roughly 200 nm.
- Revealed fine detail: internal structure of mitochondria and chloroplasts, nuclear envelope, ribosomes (about 25 nm, invisible by light microscopy).
- Increased understanding: we could link detailed structure to function, and see differences between prokaryotes and eukaryotes.
- Limits: dead, treated specimens (possible artefacts), monochrome image, expensive equipment, vacuum.
- Light microscope: living cells possible, colour, cheap, quick.

**Magnification and resolution are different.** Magnification enlarges the image; resolution decides how much fine detail is visible.

### Worked reminder (TEM)

Image 36 mm, ×30 000: actual = 36 ÷ 30 000 = 0.0012 mm = 1.2 µm = 1200 nm = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ m.

### Light microscope and TEM compared

| | Light microscope | TEM |
|---|---|---|
| Radiation | Visible light | Beam of electrons |
| Resolution | Lower (about 200 nm) | Much higher |
| Specimen | Can be living | Dead, very thin, treated |
| Image | Colour (often stained) | Black, white and grey |
| Cost and size | Cheap, portable | Expensive, large |
| Shows | Nucleus, cells, large organelles | Internal detail of organelles, ribosomes |

### Method in steps: any magnification question

1. Write the formula: magnification = image size ÷ actual size.
2. Underline what you are asked for and what is given.
3. Put image and actual size into the same unit (usually µm). Convert mm to µm by multiplying by 1000.
4. Rearrange before substituting if the unknown is not the magnification.
5. Calculate, then give the unit.
6. Check by estimation: is the size sensible for this kind of cell or organelle? Animal cells are tens of µm across, bacteria a few µm, mitochondria around 1 µm.

### Second worked reminder (scale bar)

A scale bar is 20 mm long and labelled 2 µm. A structure measures 30 mm on the same image.

1. Magnification = 20 000 µm ÷ 2 µm = ×10 000.
2. Actual length = 30 000 µm ÷ 10 000 = 3 µm.
3. In standard form: 3 × 10⁻⁶ m.

Notice that you can skip the magnification: 30 ÷ 20 × 2 = 3 µm.

### Command words in this topic

- **Describe** (a microscope method): give the steps in order, in words.
- **Explain** (a structure and its function): state what the structure contains or is, then link it to what the cell does.
- **Calculate**: show the formula, the conversion and the unit.
- **Suggest** (why a stain is used, or why a TEM image shows more): apply the idea of contrast or resolution to the situation given.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the part of the microscope that holds the slide.
2. A ×10 eyepiece and ×10 objective are used. What is the total magnification?
3. A cell is 0.08 mm long. Give this in µm.
4. An image is 45 mm and the real length is 15 µm. Calculate the magnification.
5. Write 0.0000048 m in standard form.
6. Name the molecules in the cell membrane that detect chemical signals.
7. State the function of ribosomes.
8. Where is the genetic material of a bacterium?
9. State one reason a TEM shows more detail than a light microscope.
10. A TEM image has magnification ×20 000. A structure is 40 mm long on the image. Find its real length in µm.

### Answers

1. The stage.
2. 10 × 10 = ×100.
3. 0.08 × 1000 = 80 µm.
4. 45 mm = 45 000 µm; 45 000 ÷ 15 = ×3000.
5. 4.8 × 10⁻⁶ m.
6. Receptor molecules.
7. Protein synthesis.
8. Free in the cytoplasm, as a circular chromosome, with plasmids; there is no nucleus.
9. It has higher resolution, so structures closer together are seen as separate.
10. 40 ÷ 20 000 = 0.002 mm = 2 µm.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing actual by image instead of image by actual.
- Forgetting to convert mm to µm before dividing.
- A positive power on a tiny length, such as 3.2 × 10⁶ m when 3.2 × 10⁻⁶ m was meant.
- Writing "magnifies more" instead of "has higher resolution".
- Saying a bacterium has a nucleus, or that plasmids are its only DNA.
- Describing mitochondria as "making energy" rather than releasing it in respiration.
- Giving the function of the membrane as "keeps everything in" rather than a selective barrier.
- Putting chloroplasts in root cells or animal cells.
- Using a stain without saying why: state that it makes structures visible or distinguishes them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are stains used on microscope slides?**
Most cells are almost colourless, so their parts show little contrast. A stain either makes a colourless specimen visible or colours different structures differently, for example methylene blue on cheek cells or iodine solution on onion epidermis.

**What is the difference between a chromosome and a plasmid?**
A chromosome is one long coiled DNA molecule, while plasmids are small extra rings of DNA found in bacteria, separate from the main chromosome. Plasmids can be copied and passed between bacteria.

**What are the disadvantages of a transmission electron microscope?**
TEM specimens must be dead, thin and treated, so living processes cannot be watched and preparation can create artefacts. The image is monochrome, the specimen is in a vacuum, and the equipment is expensive.

**How many micrometres are in a millimetre?**
There are 1000 µm in 1 mm and 1000 nm in 1 µm. Convert to the same unit before dividing in a magnification calculation.
