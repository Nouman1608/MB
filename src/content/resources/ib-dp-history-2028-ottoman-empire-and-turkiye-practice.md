They are not reproduced exam questions, and the real Paper 3 has essay questions only. Source extracts are constructed for practice. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-ottoman-empire-and-turkiye/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-ottoman-empire-and-turkiye-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short answers

**1.** Explain why Muhammad Ali was a threat to the Ottoman sultan. **[3]**

**2.** Give two reasons why the Congress of Berlin in 1878 revised the Treaty of San Stefano. **[2]**

**3.** Describe two features of the Tanzimat reforms. **[2]**

## Section B: source and argument

*Constructed for practice, not a real source:* "The Tanzimat gave the empire a new civil service, new schools and new courts. What it did not give it was money, and without money a modern state is only a building with a flag." (a modern historian's summary)

**4.** Explain the argument made in this extract. **[3]**

**5.** Suggest how a historian who admired the Tanzimat might respond to this view. **[3]**

**6.** Examine the extent to which the actions of Mustafa Kemal were the main reason for the success of the Turkish War of Independence. **[6]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** To what extent was the Committee of Union and Progress a force for reform in the Ottoman Empire? **[15]**

**8.** Evaluate the claim that the reforms of Atatürk were more revolutionary than they were a continuation of earlier change. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark for the identification of his power (governor of Egypt from 1805, modern army, administration). One for his challenge to the sultan: war on the sultan's forces in the 1830s, Ibrahim's conquest of Syria. One for the consequence: the sultan needed foreign help, and Egypt stayed autonomous with hereditary rule after 1840. **[3]**

**2.** One mark each for two of: San Stefano created a large Bulgaria that Britain and Austria-Hungary feared would be a Russian satellite; other powers wanted to prevent Russia from becoming dominant in the Balkans and near the Straits; Bismarck hosted the Congress to settle disputes through compromise among the powers. **[2]**

**3.** One mark each for two of: security of life and property and regular taxation (1839); conscription and a modern army; legal equality for non-Muslims (1856); new schools, ministries and courts; the 1876 constitution. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that the Tanzimat built institutions; one for the claim that it lacked finance; one for the conclusion that without funds the modern state was only an appearance of strength, linking to debt and foreign control of revenue. **[3]**

**5.** Up to 3 marks for developed points, for example: the institutions survived the empire and were used by the CUP and the republic, so the funding problem did not make them worthless; debt resulted from borrowing for the war and palace costs, not from reform itself; reform helped Ottoman survival until 1914; the Crimean War and the Public Debt Administration show external pressures were the problem. **[3]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Credit his leadership: Gallipoli in 1915, the 1919 organisation at Samsun, Erzurum and Sivas, the Ankara assembly in 1920, and the military success at Sakarya and the 1922 offensive. Credit other factors: Greek overreach and the Smyrna landing which stirred nationalism, divisions among the Allies, Soviet help, Ottoman officer networks, and popular resistance. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial examination; 5 to 6 for a balanced examination and a judgement, for example that his leadership was central but not sufficient on its own. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and defining "reform" (constitution, press, parties, modernisation) against "force for reform";
- up to 4 for evidence in favour (1908 revolution, the restored constitution, elections, new laws and education);
- up to 4 for evidence against or complicating it (the 1913 coup, authoritarian rule, Turkification, treatment of minorities including the 1915 Armenian deportations and killings, which most historians term genocide and which the Turkish state disputes, wartime government);
- up to 4 for evaluation of perspectives (liberal modernisers against authoritarian nationalists) and a reasoned judgement, for example separating the 1908 to 1913 period from the later one. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the argument and criteria for "revolutionary" (speed, scope, break with the past);
- up to 4 for revolutionary features (abolition of sultanate and caliphate, secular law, Latin alphabet, women's political rights, the republic);
- up to 4 for continuity (Tanzimat schools and law, CUP nationalism and centralisation, the officer corps, authoritarian methods, many reforms that had been proposed earlier);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, which may conclude that the scale and pace were new while many ideas and institutions came from the late empire. **[15]**
