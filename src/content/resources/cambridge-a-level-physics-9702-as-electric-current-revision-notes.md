Subtopic 9.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers current as a flow of charge carriers, the quantisation of charge, Q = It and I = Anvq. These notes are for quick recall. For the derivation of I = Anvq and annotated worked examples, read the [Electric current study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-electric-current/); for exam-style questions, use the [Electric current practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-electric-current-practice/).

## Current and charge carriers

- **Electric current** = rate of flow of charge.
- **Charge carriers**: free electrons in metals; positive and negative ions in electrolytes; electrons and positive ions in ionised gases.
- **Conventional current** flows from + to − round the external circuit; electrons in a metal drift from − to +.
- In an electrolyte, positive ions moving one way and negative ions moving the other both add to the current.

## Quantised charge

```
Q = Ne       e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C,  N = whole number
```

- Any charge must be a whole-number multiple of e.
- Test a value: Q ÷ e must be a whole number.
- 1 C ≈ 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons' worth of charge.
- An ion with charge +2e (such as Cu²⁺) carries 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

## Q = It

```
Q = It         1 C = 1 A s
number of carriers passing = It / q
```

- The **coulomb** is the charge passing a point when 1 A flows for 1 s.
- Convert: mA × 10⁻³, µA × 10⁻⁶, minutes × 60, hours × 3600.
- **Varying current:** charge = **area under the I–t graph**.

| I–t graph shape | Charge |
|---|---|
| Horizontal line at I for time t | It |
| Straight line from 0 to I₀ over time t | ½ I₀ t |
| Straight line from I₀ down to 0 | ½ I₀ t |
| Curve | Count squares × charge per square |

**Example:** current falls steadily from 40 mA to zero in 8.0 s: Q = ½ × 0.040 × 8.0 = 0.16 C, which is 1.0 × 10¹⁸ electrons.

## I = Anvq

```
I = Anvq
A = cross-sectional area (m²),  n = carriers per m³
v = mean drift speed (m s⁻¹),   q = charge per carrier (C)
```

**Derivation skeleton:** in time t carriers move vt → volume swept Avt → number of carriers nAvt → charge nAvtq → I = Q/t = nAvq.

**Copper check:** n ≈ 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³, so a 0.50 mm² wire carrying 3.0 A has v = 3.0 ÷ (0.50 × 10⁻⁶ × 8.5 × 10²⁸ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 4.4 × 10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹.

## Proportional reasoning with v = I/(Anq)

| Situation | What is fixed | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Thin and thick wire of same metal in series | I, n, q | v ∝ 1/A; half the diameter gives 4 × drift speed |
| Same wire, current doubled | A, n, q | v doubles |
| Metal vs semiconductor, same I and A | I, A, q | Semiconductor: much smaller n, much larger v |
| Diameter doubled, same I | I, n, q | A × 4, v ÷ 4 |

**Why lamps light instantly:** the field is set up round the circuit almost immediately, and free electrons already present throughout the wire start drifting together; no electron has to travel from the switch to the lamp.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Charge carrier | A charged particle free to move and so carry current |
| Elementary charge e | Smallest free charge, 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C |
| Quantised | Exists only in whole-number multiples of a basic unit |
| Coulomb | Charge passing when 1 A flows for 1 s |
| Number density n | Number of charge carriers per unit volume |
| Drift speed | Mean speed of carriers along the conductor |
| Conventional current | Direction of flow of positive charge |

## Exam traps

- Defining current as "the amount of charge" or "the flow of electrons" instead of "rate of flow of charge".
- Using Q = It with the final value of a changing current.
- Using diameter in πr², or converting mm to m after squaring.
- Thinking a thin section of a series circuit carries less current.
- Forgetting q = 2e for a doubly charged ion.
- Confusing drift speed with random thermal speed.
- Giving n in "electrons" with no "per cubic metre".

## Self-test

1. Define electric current.
2. Name the charge carriers in a metal and in an electrolyte.
3. Is a charge of 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C possible? Explain.
4. Define the coulomb in terms of base units.
5. A current of 0.25 A flows for 2.0 minutes. Find the charge.
6. How many electrons pass a point each second when the current is 3.2 mA?
7. How is charge found from an I–t graph?
8. State the meaning of n in I = Anvq, with its unit.
9. Two wires of the same metal are in series; wire P has half the diameter of wire Q. Compare the drift speeds.
10. Why does a lamp light almost instantly although drift speed is tiny?
11. A current of 0.096 A in an electrolyte is carried entirely by ions of charge +2e. How many such ions reach the electrode in 10 s?
12. What happens to the drift speed in a wire if the current is halved?

**Answers:** 1. The rate of flow of charge. 2. Free electrons; positive and negative ions. 3. No: 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.25, not a whole number. 4. 1 C = 1 A s. 5. Q = 0.25 × 120 = 30 C. 6. 3.2 × 10⁻³ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0 × 10¹⁶. 7. Area under the graph. 8. The number of charge carriers per unit volume, in m⁻³. 9. P has a quarter of the area, so its drift speed is four times that in Q. 10. An electric field is set up throughout the circuit almost immediately, so free electrons all round the circuit start to drift at once. 11. Q = 0.96 C; number = 0.96 ÷ (2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 3.0 × 10¹⁸. 12. It halves.
