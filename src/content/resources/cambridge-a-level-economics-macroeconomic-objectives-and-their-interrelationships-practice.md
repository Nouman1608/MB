## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between the internal value of money and the external value of money. **[2]**

**2.** Country A had goods exports of 420, goods imports of 455, net services of +30, net primary income of −12 and net secondary income of −8 (currency units). GDP was 1,250. Calculate the current account balance and express it as a percentage of GDP. **[3]**

**3.** A consumer price index in Country G rises from 108.0 to 112.32. Nominal wages rise by 6%.

**(a)** Calculate the inflation rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate real wage growth. **[2]**

**4.** In the short run, Country G's economy offers these combinations of unemployment and inflation: unemployment 4% with inflation 6%; unemployment 6% with inflation 4%; unemployment 8% with inflation 3%.

**(a)** Calculate the extra inflation from cutting unemployment from 6% to 4%. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why raising unemployment from 6% to 8% lowers inflation by less. **[3]**

**5.** Country H's nominal GDP rises from 480 to 513.6 while its price deflator rises from 100 to 104. Calculate real GDP growth to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**6.** One unit of Country D's currency buys 4.00 units of Country E's currency. A basket costs 50 in D and 200 in E. A year later prices in D are 6% higher and prices in E are 2% higher. The exchange rate has not changed.

**(a)** Show that the basket cost the same in both countries at the start. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how much dearer D's basket is in E's currency than E's basket. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the exchange rate that restores the original relationship, and the percentage depreciation needed. **[2]**

**7.** Country J has imports of 500 and exports of 480. Income elasticity of demand for imports is 2.2. Real income grows 4% and exports grow 3%.

**(a)** Calculate the new value of imports. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the new current account balance on goods. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why the balance might improve in later years. **[1]**

**8.** In Country K, inflation = expected inflation − 0.8 × (unemployment − 4), with both rates in percent. Expected inflation is 2.5% and unemployment is 4%. The government holds unemployment at 3% for three years. Each year, expected inflation equals the previous year's inflation.

**(a)** Calculate inflation in each of the three years. **[3]**
**(b)** The government then lets unemployment return to 4%. Calculate inflation in year 4 and explain the result. **[2]**
**(c)** State the shape of the long-run Phillips curve. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why a government cannot permanently keep unemployment below the natural rate by raising aggregate demand. **[6]**

**10.** Country L's currency falls 8% against its trading partners. Imports have a weight of 25% in its price index.

**(a)** Calculate the rise in import prices in L's currency and the direct effect on the price index. **[3]**
**(b)** Give one reason the effect on the price index may be smaller and one reason it may be larger over time. **[2]**

**11.** Country M's real GDP grows 4% and its population grows 1.5%.

**(a)** Calculate growth in real GDP per head to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two ways in which an objective of faster growth may conflict with other objectives. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether a government can achieve both low inflation and low unemployment. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The internal value of money is its purchasing power over goods and services at home, which falls when the price level rises [1]. The external value is what it buys abroad, shown by the exchange rate [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition question rewards a precise contrast. Say where each value is measured, not just that they are "different".

**2.** Goods balance: 420 − 455 = −35 [1]. Current account: −35 + 30 − 12 − 8 = **−25** [1]. As a share of GDP: −25 / 1,250 × 100 = **−2.0%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the sign. A deficit given as a positive number tells the reader you have not interpreted your own result.

**3. (a)** Change = 112.32 − 108.0 = 4.32 [1]. Inflation = 4.32 / 108.0 × 100 = **4.0%** [1].
**(b)** Real growth = 1.06 / 1.04 − 1 [1] = **1.92%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtracting 4 from 6 gives about 2%, which is close but not the figure asked for. Show the division when a precise answer is wanted.

**4. (a)** 6 − 4 = **2 percentage points** more inflation [1].
**(b)** Raising unemployment from 6% to 8% lowers inflation by 1 point only [1]. The curve is convex, flattening as unemployment rises [1]. At high unemployment wage pressure is already weak, so extra jobless workers lower wage growth and prices very little [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers from the table in your explanation. A general statement about "diminishing returns" without 2 and 1 does not use the data you were given.

**5.** Nominal growth = 513.6 / 480 − 1 = 7.0% [1]. Real growth = 1.070 / 1.04 − 1 [1] = 2.885% = **2.9%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Follow the rounding instruction. Keep extra digits until the last step and round once at the end.

**6. (a)** 50 × 4.00 = 200, equal to E's price [1].
**(b)** D's basket is 50 × 1.06 = 53, which is 53 × 4.00 = 212 in E's currency [1]. E's basket is 200 × 1.02 = 204, so D's is 212 / 204 − 1 = **3.9% dearer** [1].
**(c)** Parity rate = 204 / 53 = **3.85** units of E per unit of D [1]. Depreciation = (4.00 − 3.849) / 4.00 = **3.8%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Express both prices in the same currency before comparing. Part (c) depends on the prices you found in part (b), so state which figures you are using.

**7. (a)** Import growth = 2.2 × 4% = 8.8% [1]. New imports = 500 × 1.088 = **544** [1].
**(b)** New exports = 480 × 1.03 = 494.4 [1]. Balance = 494.4 − 544 = **−49.6**, compared with −20 at the start [1].
**(c)** Investment during the growth period raises export capacity or productivity, so export volumes rise [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the deficit widened, not just what the number is. Linking growth to the balance in a sentence earns credit that bare calculations do not.

**8. (a)** Year 1: 2.5 − 0.8 × (3 − 4) = **3.3%** [1]. Year 2: expected inflation 3.3, so 3.3 + 0.8 = **4.1%** [1]. Year 3: 4.1 + 0.8 = **4.9%** [1].
**(b)** Year 4: unemployment is at the natural rate, so inflation = expected inflation = **4.9%** [1]. Unemployment returns to the natural rate but inflation stays high until expectations fall [1].
**(c)** **Vertical**, at the natural rate of unemployment [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the model's own rule each year and show the expectation you used. In part (b), say that the short-run gain has gone while the higher inflation remains.

**9.** Higher aggregate demand raises output, and firms hire more workers [1], so unemployment falls below the natural rate [1]. Labour markets tighten, wages and prices rise [1]. Workers see that real wages have fallen and revise their expectations of inflation upwards [1]. The short-run Phillips curve shifts up, and the real wage is restored so firms stop expanding [1]. Unemployment returns to the natural rate with higher inflation, so the long-run Phillips curve is vertical [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain of linked steps scores better than a list of terms. Show what workers expect and why real wages matter.

**10. (a)** Import price rise = 1 / 0.92 − 1 = **8.7%** [1]. Direct effect = 0.25 × 8.7 [1] = **2.2 percentage points** on the price index [1].
**(b)** Smaller: importers may absorb part of the rise in their profit margins, or domestic competition limits price rises [1]. Larger: dearer imported inputs raise costs for firms, and workers may demand higher wages (second-round effects) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A currency fall of 8% does not mean import prices rise by 8%. The reciprocal calculation matters, and the evaluation points need a reason, not just "it depends".

**11. (a)** 1.04 / 1.015 − 1 [1] = **2.5%** [1].
**(b)** Growth vs sustainability: faster growth may use up resources or raise pollution [1], which lowers the living standards future generations can reach [1]. Growth vs redistribution: gains from growth may go mostly to higher incomes, widening inequality, or policies to share income may reduce incentives and slow growth [1], so the objectives must be traded off by value judgement [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For "two ways" answers, label each conflict and develop it separately. A single sentence naming two objectives is a statement, not an explanation.

**12.** Traditional Phillips curve analysis suggests a trade-off, so lower unemployment appears to cost higher inflation [1] because a tight labour market raises wages and prices [1]. In the short run, for given expected inflation, this trade-off exists on the short-run curve [1]. In the long run it disappears: expectations adjust and the long-run curve is vertical at the natural rate [1], so demand management alone cannot hold unemployment below it [1]. Both goals can be met together only if the natural rate falls, through supply-side policies such as training or better labour-market information [1], or through supply-led growth that raises potential output with low price pressure [1]. Credible inflation targets lower expected inflation, which makes low inflation cheaper to keep [1]. Supply shocks and wage pressure can shift the curve and break the pattern [1]. Judgement: low inflation with low unemployment is possible in the long run, when supply-side improvement and credible policy combine, but not by raising aggregate demand alone, and the timescale is long [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation means a reasoned conclusion that weighs time, causes and policy credibility. Keep the final judgement tied to the argument you built.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping the minus sign on a deficit, or omitting units.
- Subtracting percentages where a ratio is needed (real wage growth, real GDP growth).
- Rounding early and then giving a final figure that differs in the last digit.
- Treating a fall in the exchange rate as a 1-for-1 rise in import prices.
- Moving the Phillips curve without saying what expectations or costs changed.
- Drawing a sloping long-run Phillips curve or placing it at zero unemployment.
- Describing a conflict between objectives without showing the mechanism.
- Finishing an essay with a summary and no judgement.
