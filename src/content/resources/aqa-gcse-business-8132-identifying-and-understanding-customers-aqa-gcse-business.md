It opens Topic 5, Marketing, and the rest of the topic builds on it: segmentation, market research and the marketing mix are all ways of finding and meeting customer needs. ## What the specification asks for

Students should be able to explain the importance of identifying and satisfying customer needs. The specification gives five reasons:

| Reason | Covered below |
|---|---|
| To provide a product or service that customers will buy | Reason 1 |
| To increase sales | Reason 2 |
| To select the correct marketing mix | Reason 3 |
| To avoid costly mistakes | Reason 4 |
| To be competitive | Reason 5 |

This is a short section of the specification, but it is examined through application. You will usually be given a business and asked why understanding its customers matters, or what happens when it fails to.

## What are customer needs?

A customer need is what a customer wants from a product or service before they will buy it. Needs are wider than the product itself. Customers commonly look for:

- **Price or value for money:** a price they think is fair for what they get.
- **Quality:** the product works, lasts and meets the standard they expect.
- **Design and features:** how it looks and what it does.
- **Convenience:** it is easy to find and buy, at a time and place that suits them.
- **Choice:** enough options to suit their taste.
- **Service:** helpful staff, quick delivery, easy returns.

Different customers weigh these differently. A commuter buying lunch may care most about speed and convenience. A family buying a sofa may care most about quality and design and be willing to wait. **Identifying** customers means knowing who they are; **understanding** them means knowing which needs matter most to them and why.

Needs also change. Rising concern about the environment, changes in income, or new technology all shift what customers want. A business that understood its customers five years ago may not understand them now.

## Reason 1: to provide a product or service that customers will buy

A product only earns revenue if someone buys it. Building the product around what customers actually need, rather than what the owner assumes they need, raises the chance that it sells.

Example: Penhallow Pets plans a dog-walking service. Talking to local dog owners reveals that what they need most is a walker who can come at lunchtime on weekdays while they are at work. Penhallow designs the service around a midday slot, rather than the early-morning walks the owner had planned.

## Reason 2: to increase sales

When a product meets needs, more people buy it and existing customers come back. Satisfied customers also recommend it, which brings in new customers at no extra cost.

### Worked example: meeting a need raises sales

Tolver Bakery sells 1,200 sandwiches a week at £3.50. Customer comments show that many office workers walk past because the queue is too long at lunchtime. Tolver adds a pre-order service by phone. Sales rise to 1,450 sandwiches a week.

- Revenue before: 1,200 × £3.50 = £4,200 a week
- Revenue after: 1,450 × £3.50 = £5,075 a week
- Increase: £5,075 − £4,200 = **£875 a week**, or (£875 ÷ £4,200) × 100 = about **20.8%**

The product did not change. Tolver met a need for **convenience** that it had been missing.

## Reason 3: to select the correct marketing mix

The marketing mix is the combination of price, product, promotion and place. Every element should be chosen to fit the target customer, so a business that does not understand its customers is guessing on four decisions at once.

| If the business learns that customers... | It can set... |
|---|---|
| are price-sensitive students | a low price and promotion on social media |
| are busy professionals who value time | delivery or online ordering (place) |
| want a premium, gift-quality product | a higher price, quality packaging and upmarket stockists |
| live in one area | local promotion rather than national advertising |

A mismatch wastes money: an expensive television advertising campaign for a product bought only by a small local group reaches millions of people who will never buy.

## Reason 4: to avoid costly mistakes

Developing, making and promoting a product costs money before any revenue arrives. If the product does not meet customer needs, that money is lost. Finding out first is much cheaper than finding out after launch.

### Worked example: the cost of a mistake avoided

Wendron Drinks plans a new range of energy drinks. It would spend £18,000 on product development, £12,000 on a first production run and £9,000 on promotion, a total of £18,000 + £12,000 + £9,000 = **£39,000**. Before committing, it spends £2,500 asking 300 of its regular customers what they want. Most say they are cutting down on sugar and caffeine and would prefer a flavoured sparkling water.

By switching the plan, Wendron has avoided risking £39,000 on a product its own customers say they would not buy. The £2,500 of research is about 6.4% of that sum (£2,500 ÷ £39,000 × 100). Customers may not do exactly what they say, so the research reduces the risk rather than removing it.

## Reason 5: to be competitive

In most markets, customers can choose a rival. A business that understands its customers better than its competitors can offer something they value more: better quality, a more convenient location, a design that suits them. A business that ignores changing needs loses customers to rivals who notice them first.

Example: two hairdressers on the same street charge similar prices. One finds that many customers work late and starts opening until 20:00 on Thursdays. It wins customers who cannot reach the other salon before it closes. Understanding a need has created a competitive advantage without cutting price.

## How businesses find out about customers

Section 3.5.1 asks why identifying needs matters. The methods are covered in the next sections:

- **Segmentation** (3.5.2) groups customers by gender, age, location and income so that needs can be identified for each group.
- **Market research** (3.5.3) gathers information on demand, competition and the target market, using primary and secondary methods.

In a 3.5.1 answer you can mention these as the way a business identifies needs, but keep the focus on why it matters.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain one reason why it is important for Hartlow Cycles, a new bike shop, to identify the needs of its customers.* (The case says the town has many families and a large university.)

"Identifying needs helps Hartlow Cycles select the correct marketing mix. *(reason)* If research shows that most potential customers are students with little money, it can stock cheaper second-hand bikes and promote them on social media. *(applied to the case)* This makes it more likely that students buy from Hartlow rather than elsewhere, increasing its sales." *(consequence)*

The answer chooses one reason, uses the students from the case, and finishes with a consequence for the business.

## Common mistakes

- Listing all five reasons with no explanation when the question asks for one, explained.
- Confusing customer needs with the product's features: a need is what the customer wants, such as convenience; a feature is what the product has.
- Assuming all customers have the same needs.
- Forgetting that needs change over time.
- Writing "to make a profit" as a reason. Sales and avoiding costly mistakes lead to profit, but they are the reasons the specification names.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State or identify:** one of the five reasons, briefly.
- **Explain:** a reason, applied to the business, with a consequence such as higher sales or avoided costs.
- **Analyse:** a longer chain, for example need identified → mix chosen to fit → more customers buy → revenue rises.
- **Evaluate:** usually combined with other marketing sections, for example whether research into needs is worth its cost for a small business.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is it important for a business to identify customer needs?**
The specification gives five reasons: to provide a product or service customers will buy, to increase sales, to select the correct marketing mix, to avoid costly mistakes and to be competitive.

**What is the difference between identifying and understanding customers?**
Identifying customers means knowing who they are. Understanding them means knowing which needs, such as price, quality or convenience, matter most to them and why.

**How does understanding customers help a business compete?**
It can offer something customers value that rivals do not, such as longer opening hours or a better-suited design, which wins customers without necessarily cutting price.
