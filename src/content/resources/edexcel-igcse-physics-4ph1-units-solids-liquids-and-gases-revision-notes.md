## Units by equation

| Equation | Units to substitute | Answer unit |
|---|---|---|
| ρ = m / V | kg, m³ | kg/m³ |
| p = F / A | N, m² | Pa |
| p = h × ρ × g | m, kg/m³, N/kg (or m/s²) | Pa |
| ΔQ = m × c × ΔT (P) | kg, J/kg °C, °C (or K: same size) | J |
| p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ | any pressure unit, any volume unit, used consistently | same units as given |
| p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂ | any pressure unit; **T in kelvin only** | same pressure unit as given |

## Building the units

```
1 Pa = 1 N/m²
m × kg/m³ × N/kg = N/m² = Pa        (check for p = h ρ g)
J ÷ (kg × °C) = J/kg °C             (unit of c)
1 kPa = 1000 Pa
```

## Conversion factors

| From | To | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| cm | m | ÷ 100 |
| mm | m | ÷ 1000 |
| cm² | m² | ÷ 10 000 (10⁴) |
| mm² | m² | ÷ 1 000 000 (10⁶) |
| cm³ | m³ | ÷ 1 000 000 (10⁶) |
| litre | m³ | ÷ 1000 (1 litre = 1000 cm³) |
| g | kg | ÷ 1000 |
| g/cm³ | kg/m³ | × 1000 |
| kPa | Pa | × 1000 |
| °C | K | + 273 |
| K | °C | − 273 |

**Why area and volume factors are bigger:** 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100 × 100 cm² and 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 cm³.

## Temperature facts

- Absolute zero: 0 K = −273 °C. Nothing can be colder.
- Freezing point of water 0 °C = 273 K; boiling point 100 °C = 373 K.
- **Changes** are equal on both scales: a 25 °C rise is a 25 K rise.
- Use kelvin whenever a temperature is in a ratio or proportion (p ∝ T); a temperature change can stay in °C.
- The symbol is K, not °K.

## Sense checks

- Water: 1000 kg/m³. Most metals: thousands of kg/m³. Air: about 1 kg/m³.
- Atmospheric pressure: about 100 000 Pa (100 kPa).
- Specific heat capacity of water: 4200 J/kg °C; metals are usually a few hundred.
- A density answer of 2.7 kg/m³ for a metal means a factor of 1000 has been lost.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pascal (Pa) | Unit of pressure; one newton per square metre |
| Kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³) | Unit of density: mass in kilograms of each cubic metre |
| Kelvin (K) | Unit of the absolute temperature scale, starting at absolute zero; same size as 1 °C |
| Degree Celsius (°C) | Temperature unit with 0 °C at the freezing point of water |
| J/kg °C | Unit of specific heat capacity: joules to warm 1 kg by 1 °C |
| Square metre (m²) | Unit of area, equal to 10 000 cm² |
| Cubic metre (m³) | Unit of volume, equal to 1 000 000 cm³ |

## Exam traps

- cm² ÷ 100 or cm³ ÷ 100: the factor must be squared or cubed.
- g/cm³ to kg/m³ by dividing: it is × 1000.
- Celsius in p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂.
- Converting a temperature **change** by adding 273. A rise of 20 °C is a rise of 20 K, not 293 K.
- Grams in ΔQ = m × c × ΔT.
- Mixing kPa and Pa, or cm³ and m³, on the two sides of p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
- Writing "°K".

## Self-test

1. Express the pascal in terms of newtons and metres.
2. Convert 35 cm² to m².
3. Convert 250 cm³ to m³ in standard form.
4. Convert 0.92 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
5. Convert 27 °C to kelvin.
6. Convert 77 K to °C.
7. A liquid warms from 15 °C to 40 °C. State the temperature rise in kelvin.
8. A 60 N force acts on an area of 15 cm². Calculate the pressure in kPa.
9. Calculate the pressure difference 30 cm below the surface of oil of density 800 kg/m³ (g = 10 N/kg).
10. Calculate the energy needed to warm 250 g of water by 12 °C (c = 4200 J/kg °C).
11. Show that the unit of h × ρ × g is the pascal.
12. Explain why kelvin must be used in p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂ but a temperature change in °C can be used in ΔQ = m × c × ΔT.

**Answers:** 1. 1 Pa = 1 N/m². 2. 35 ÷ 10 000 = 0.0035 m². 3. 250 ÷ 1 000 000 = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³. 4. 920 kg/m³.
5. 300 K. 6. −196 °C. 7. 25 K. 8. A = 0.0015 m²; p = 60 ÷ 0.0015 = 40 000 Pa = 40 kPa. 9. p = 0.30 × 800 × 10 =
2400 Pa. 10. ΔQ = 0.250 × 4200 × 12 = 12 600 J. 11. m × kg/m³ × N/kg = (m × kg × N) ÷ (m³ × kg) = N/m² = Pa.
12. Pressure is proportional to temperature only when temperature is measured from absolute zero, so the ratio needs
kelvin; a temperature change is the same number in °C and K because the units are the same size.
