These questions cover section 12.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: expectation, including expected profit and loss in simple games and the idea of a fair game. Recall the methods from the [expectation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-expectation/) or the [expectation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-expectation-revision-notes/). Amounts are in rupees (Rs). Give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** X has the distribution shown. Find E(X).

| x | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(X = x) | 0.1 | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.2 |

**[2]**

**2.** A fair spinner has four equal sections numbered 1, 1, 2 and 3. Find the expected score. **[2]**

**3.** A game costs Rs 3 to play. The player wins Rs 10 with probability 0.25 and nothing otherwise. Find the player's expected profit per game. **[2]**

**4.** A player rolls a fair six-sided die and receives twice the score in rupees. Find the cost to play that would make the game fair. **[2]**

**5.** The number of goals scored by a team in a match has expectation 1.7. Explain what this means. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A player pays Rs 4 and rolls two fair dice. A double six wins Rs 50, any other double wins Rs 10, and anything else wins nothing.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of the winnings. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the player's expected profit per game. **[2]**

**(c)** The prize for any other double stays at Rs 10. Find the prize for a double six that would make the game fair. **[2]**

**7.** X takes the values 0, 1, 2 and 3 with probabilities 0.15, a, b and 0.25. Given that E(X) = 1.6, find a and b. **[4]**

**8.** At a charity stall a player pays Rs 20 and takes one card at random from 20 cards. One card wins Rs 100, three cards win Rs 40 each, and the rest win nothing.

**(a)** Find the expected winnings per game. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the charity's expected profit from 150 games. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the charity's actual profit may differ from your answer to (b). **[1]**

**9.** A bag holds 3 red and 2 blue balls. A player pays Rs 8 and takes two balls at random without replacement. The player wins Rs 20 if both balls are the same colour, and nothing otherwise.

**(a)** Show that the probability that both balls are the same colour is 2/5. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that the game is fair. **[2]**

**10.** A student may sit a practical assessment up to three times and stops after the first pass. The probability of passing at each attempt is 0.5, independently. Each attempt costs Rs 40. X is the number of attempts.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[2]**

**(b)** Find E(X). **[1]**

**(c)** Find the expected total cost. **[1]**

**11.** A player pays Rs 5 to spin a spinner with three outcomes. With probability 0.5 the player receives nothing. With probability 0.3 the player's Rs 5 is returned. With probability 0.2 the player's Rs 5 is returned together with a further Rs 15.

**(a)** Draw up a table of the player's profit and its probability. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the player's expected profit per game. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on the game from the organiser's point of view. **[1]**

**12.** Game A costs Rs 2 and pays Rs 10 with probability 0.15. Game B costs Rs 5 and pays Rs 30 with probability 0.1 or Rs 10 with probability 0.15; otherwise it pays nothing.

**(a)** Find the expected profit per game for a player of each game. **[4]**

**(b)** A player has a fixed amount of money to spend on one game repeatedly. Explain which game is better value. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** E(X) = 0.1 + 1.2 + 1.5 + 1.4 [1] = **4.2** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** (1 × 2/4) + (2 × 1/4) + (3 × 1/4) [1] = 7/4 = **1.75** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** E(winnings) = 10 × 0.25 = Rs 2.50 [1]. Expected profit = 2.50 − 3 = **−Rs 0.50** per game [1]. **[2]**

**4.** E(winnings) = 2 × (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) ÷ 6 [1] = **Rs 7**, which is the fair cost [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Over a large number of matches the mean number of goals per match would be close to 1.7 [1]. It is not a number of goals the team can score in one match, which must be a whole number [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** P(Rs 50) = **1/36** [1]. P(Rs 10) = **5/36** [1]. P(Rs 0) = **30/36** [1].

**(b)** E(winnings) = 50/36 + 50/36 = 100/36 = Rs 2.78 [1]. Expected profit = 2.78 − 4 = **−Rs 1.22** per game [1].

**(c)** w/36 + 50/36 = 4 [1], so w = 144 − 50 = **Rs 94** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Any other double" excludes double six, so there are 5 such outcomes, not 6.

**7.** 0.15 + a + b + 0.25 = 1, so a + b = 0.6 [1]. a + 2b + 0.75 = 1.6, so a + 2b = 0.85 [1]. Subtracting, **b = 0.25** [1] and **a = 0.35** [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** (100 × 1 + 40 × 3) ÷ 20 [1] = **Rs 11** [1].

**(b)** Charity's expected profit per game = 20 − 11 = Rs 9 [1]. Over 150 games: **Rs 1350** [1].

**(c)** Expectation is a long-run average; the cards actually drawn vary, so the number of prizes won in 150 games will not exactly match the probabilities [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** P(red, red) = 3/5 × 2/4 = 6/20 [1]. P(blue, blue) = 2/5 × 1/4 = 2/20 [1]. Total 8/20 = **2/5** [1].

**(b)** E(winnings) = 20 × 2/5 = Rs 8 [1], which equals the cost, so the expected profit is zero and the game is fair [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** P(X = 1) = 0.5, P(X = 2) = 0.25, P(X = 3) = 0.25 [2] (1 mark for two correct).

**(b)** E(X) = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.75 = **1.75** [1].

**(c)** 1.75 × 40 = **Rs 70** [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)**

| Profit (Rs) | −5 | 0 | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 0.5 | 0.3 | 0.2 |

Profit values [1]; probabilities matched [1].

**(b)** −2.5 + 0 + 3 [1] = **Rs 0.50** per game [1].

**(c)** The player expects to gain, so the organiser expects to lose about Rs 0.50 per game and should lower the top prize or raise the cost [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A returned stake is a profit of zero; counting it as Rs 5 profit is the usual error.

**12. (a)** Game A: E(winnings) = 10 × 0.15 = 1.50 [1], expected profit **−Rs 0.50** [1]. Game B: E(winnings) = 3 + 1.50 = 4.50 [1], expected profit **−Rs 0.50** [1].

**(b)** Both lose Rs 0.50 per game on average, but Game A loses 0.50 ÷ 2 = 25% of each stake and Game B loses 0.50 ÷ 5 = 10% [1]. With a fixed amount to spend, **Game B** loses less of it in the long run [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to subtract the cost to play from expected winnings.
- Treating a returned stake as a profit.
- Giving the player's expected profit when the organiser's is asked for.
- Counting "other doubles" or similar outcomes wrongly.
- Writing E(X) as if it were the result of a single game.
- Rounding probabilities before multiplying.
