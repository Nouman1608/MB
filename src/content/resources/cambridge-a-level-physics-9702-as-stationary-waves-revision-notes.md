Condensed notes on subtopic 8.1 Stationary waves (Cambridge 9702, AS Topic 8).
For full explanations and worked examples, use the
[stationary waves study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-stationary-waves/);
for the whole topic, see the [Superposition revision notes](/resources/as-physics-superposition-revision-notes/).

## What 8.1 asks you to do

- Explain and use the principle of superposition
- Understand experiments showing stationary waves with **microwaves**, **stretched strings** and **air columns** (end corrections negligible)
- Explain formation **graphically** and identify nodes and antinodes
- Determine wavelength from node or antinode positions

## Superposition

> When two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.

Signs matter: +4 mm and −1 mm give +3 mm.

## Formation

- Two progressive waves, **same frequency** (and amplitude), travelling in **opposite directions**, superpose.
- Usually an incident wave and its reflection.
- Graphical method: draw both waves and the resultant at t = 0, T/4, T/2, 3T/4.
  - In step → resultant amplitude doubled.
  - A quarter-period later → waves in antiphase → resultant zero everywhere.
- **Node**: point of zero displacement at all times.
- **Antinode**: point of maximum amplitude.

## Distances

```
node - node          = lambda / 2
antinode - antinode  = lambda / 2
node - antinode      = lambda / 4
n gaps between nodes = n lambda / 2
```

## Stationary vs progressive: the two exam favourites

| | Stationary | Progressive |
|---|---|---|
| Amplitude | Varies with position (0 at nodes) | Same for all points |
| Phase | In phase within a segment; antiphase across a node | Varies continuously along the wave |
| Energy | No net transfer along the wave | Transferred in direction of travel |

## The three experiments

| Experiment | Set-up | Ends / boundaries | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microwaves | Transmitter facing a metal reflector; probe moved along the line between | Reflector is a node of the electric field | Distance across several minima → λ/2 per gap; f = c/λ |
| Stretched string | Vibration generator, string over pulley with masses | Both ends nodes: L = nλ/2 | Node separation → λ; v = fλ |
| Air column | Sound source at open end of a tube | Closed end node; open end antinode (particle displacement) | Resonance lengths |

## Air column patterns (end corrections neglected)

| Tube | Allowed lengths |
|---|---|
| Closed one end | L = λ/4, 3λ/4, 5λ/4 … (odd quarter-wavelengths) |
| Open both ends | L = λ/2, λ, 3λ/2 … (whole half-wavelengths) |

- Successive resonance lengths, at fixed frequency, differ by **λ/2**.
- Closed tube: resonant frequencies f₁, 3f₁, 5f₁ …
- Open tube: f₁, 2f₁, 3f₁ …

**Example.** A 0.85 m tube open at both ends; v = 340 m s⁻¹. Lowest frequency: L = λ/2, λ = 1.70 m, f = 340 ÷ 1.70 = **200 Hz**; next: **400 Hz**.

**Example — speed of sound from resonance lengths.** A 680 Hz source above a tube closed by a movable piston gives resonances at 0.125 m and 0.375 m.

```
lambda / 2 = 0.375 - 0.125 = 0.250 m     lambda = 0.500 m
v = f lambda = 680 x 0.500 = 340 m/s
```

**Example — microwaves.** A probe finds nodes at 3.0 cm, 4.5 cm, 6.0 cm and 7.5 cm from a reference mark: node spacing 1.5 cm = λ/2, so λ = 3.0 cm and f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 0.030 = 1.0 × 10¹⁰ Hz. Measuring 4.5 cm across the three gaps, rather than one gap, gives the smaller percentage uncertainty.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to recall |
|---|---|
| Principle of superposition | Resultant displacement = sum of individual displacements |
| Stationary wave | Pattern from two waves of equal frequency travelling in opposite directions |
| Node | Point of zero displacement at all times |
| Antinode | Point of maximum amplitude |
| Segment (loop) | Region between adjacent nodes; all points in phase |
| Resonance (air column) | Large-amplitude stationary wave when the tube length fits the pattern |

## Exam traps

- Node spacing taken as λ, not λ/2.
- Counting minima instead of gaps between them.
- Antinode placed at a closed end.
- Saying stationary waves carry energy along the medium.
- Stating that all points vibrate in phase, or that amplitude is constant.
- Forgetting the waves must travel in **opposite** directions with the **same frequency**.

## Self-test

1. State the principle of superposition.
2. State the conditions for a stationary wave to form.
3. How far apart are adjacent nodes?
4. How far is a node from the nearest antinode?
5. What is the phase difference between two points in adjacent segments?
6. A microwave probe finds the 1st and 4th minima 4.5 cm apart. Find λ.
7. Find the frequency of these microwaves.
8. A string 0.90 m long, fixed at both ends, shows three segments at 50 Hz. Find the wave speed.
9. A tube 0.25 m long is closed at one end; v = 340 m s⁻¹. Find the lowest resonant frequency.
10. A tube 0.50 m long is open at both ends; v = 340 m s⁻¹. Find the lowest resonant frequency.
11. At 1000 Hz, successive resonance lengths of a closed tube differ by 0.17 m. Find the speed of sound.
12. Why does a stationary wave not transfer energy along its length?

**Answers:**
1. When waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.
2. Two waves of the same frequency (and similar amplitude) travelling in opposite directions through the same region.
3. **λ/2**.
4. **λ/4**.
5. **180°** (antiphase).
6. Three gaps: λ/2 = 1.5 cm, λ = **3.0 cm**.
7. f = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 0.030 = **1.0 × 10¹⁰ Hz**.
8. 3λ/2 = 0.90 m, λ = 0.60 m; v = 50 × 0.60 = **30 m s⁻¹**.
9. λ = 4 × 0.25 = 1.0 m; f = **340 Hz**.
10. λ = 2 × 0.50 = 1.0 m; f = **340 Hz**.
11. λ/2 = 0.17 m, λ = 0.34 m; v = 1000 × 0.34 = **340 m s⁻¹**.
12. It is formed by two equal waves carrying energy in opposite directions, so there is no net transfer.
