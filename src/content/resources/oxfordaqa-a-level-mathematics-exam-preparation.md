OxfordAQA International A-Level Mathematics (9660) is a modular qualification of four units, three of
them compulsory and one chosen. Unit P1 (Pure Maths, AS) and Unit PSM1 (Pure Maths, Statistics and
Mechanics, AS) are each 1.5 hours, 80 marks, 20% of the full A-level. Unit P2 (Pure Maths, A-level) is
compulsory and by far the largest single unit: 2.5 hours, 120 marks, 37.5%. The final unit is a choice
between Unit S2 (Statistics) or Unit M2 (Mechanics), each 1.5 hours, 80 marks, 22.5%. These notes
complement the site's guides to [Unit P1: Pure Maths](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-mathematics-unit-p1/)
and [Bernoulli and Binomial Distributions](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-mathematics-bernoulli-and-binomial-distributions/).

## Unit P2 alone is worth more than a third of the whole A-level

At 37.5%, Unit P2 outweighs any other single unit by a wide margin -- more than either AS unit and more
than the S2/M2 option. **Exam-preparation priority**: allocate revision time proportionally, meaning P2
should receive noticeably more time than any other single unit, not an equal share across all four.

## Confirm which option unit your school teaches, early

Unit S2 and Unit M2 share no content -- one is pure statistics, the other pure mechanics -- and most
schools teach only one of the two. **Exam-preparation priority**: confirm which option applies to you
before revision begins, since preparing both "just in case" wastes time better spent deepening the
option you will actually sit.

## PSM1 is a genuine taster for whichever option comes next

Unit PSM1 combines pure maths with an introduction to both statistics and mechanics at AS level, meaning
its content directly feeds whichever of S2 or M2 a candidate goes on to take at A-level.
**Exam-preparation priority**: revisit PSM1's statistics or mechanics sections specifically once the
A-level option is confirmed, rather than treating AS and A2 option content as unconnected.

## Method marks reward shown working, not just final answers

Across all four units, partial credit is available for correct method even where a final numerical
answer is wrong. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise showing every step of a calculation in full,
including intermediate substitutions, rather than jumping to a final answer -- an unsupported correct
answer can still lose marks, and a wrong answer with correct method can still gain most of them.

## Worked routine: showing full working for method marks

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - write the formula or method being used, before
substituting:
Establishes the method mark independently of the arithmetic.

Step 2 - substitute values one line at a time:
Not combined into one dense line -- each substitution should be
checkable on its own.

Step 3 - simplify in clearly separated stages:
A marker should be able to follow every algebraic step without
guessing an intermediate move.

Step 4 - box or underline the final answer:
Makes it unambiguous which line is being offered as the answer.

Step 5 - sanity-check the answer against the question's context:
A probability above 1, a negative length, or an angle over 360
degrees signals an error worth catching before moving on.
```

Step 5 catches a wide range of arithmetic slips cheaply -- it takes seconds and does not require
re-deriving the whole solution, only checking whether the final number is plausible for what the
question actually asked.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: weight revision time towards Unit P2, which alone is 37.5% of the qualification;
  confirm your S2/M2 option early and revisit the matching PSM1 content once confirmed; drill showing
  full working, not just final answers.
- **During any paper**: substitute and simplify one step at a time, in full, for every calculation.
- **After every answer**: sanity-check the result against the question's real-world context before
  moving on.

## Self-test

1. Why does Unit P2 deserve more revision time than any other single unit?
2. Why is it important to confirm your S2/M2 option early rather than preparing both?
3. How does Unit PSM1 connect to the A-level option units?
4. Why should working be shown in full, even for straightforward calculations?

**Answers:** 1. Because it is worth 37.5% of the full A-level, more than any other single unit,
including either AS unit or the S2/M2 option. 2. Because S2 and M2 share no content, and preparing both
"just in case" wastes revision time better spent deepening the one option actually sat. 3. PSM1
introduces both statistics and mechanics at AS level, so it directly feeds whichever of S2 or M2 is
taken at A-level, and its relevant sections should be revisited once the option is confirmed. 4. Because
method marks are available even when the final answer is wrong, so full working captures partial credit
that an answer alone would not.
