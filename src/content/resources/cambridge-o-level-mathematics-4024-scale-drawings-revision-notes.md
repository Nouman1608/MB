These notes summarise section 4.3, Scale drawings, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [scale drawings study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-scale-drawings/), and exam-style questions are in the [scale drawings practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-scale-drawings-practice/).

## What is examined

- Draw and interpret scale drawings, using a ruler for all straight edges.
- Use and interpret three-figure bearings measured clockwise from north (000° to 360°).
- Understand north, east, south and west, for example "D is due east of C".
- Find the bearing of A from B when the bearing of B from A is given.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scale | the ratio of a drawn length to the real length |
| Scale factor | the number you multiply by to go from drawing to reality |
| Bearing | an angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures |
| Back bearing | the reverse bearing, from the other point to the first |
| Due east | exactly east, on a bearing of 090° |
| Elevation | the angle above the horizontal |
| North line | a vertical line drawn at each point to measure from |

## Scales

```
real length = drawn length × scale number
drawn length = real length ÷ scale number
```

| Map scale | 1 cm on the map is |
|---|---|
| 1 : 1000 | 10 m |
| 1 : 25 000 | 250 m |
| 1 : 50 000 | 500 m |
| 1 : 100 000 | 1 km |
| 1 : 200 000 | 2 km |

Unit conversions to remember:

- 100 cm = 1 m, 1000 m = 1 km, so 100 000 cm = 1 km.
- 10 000 m² = 1 hectare.

### Area scale

Areas use the square of the length scale.

```
1 cm on map = 2 km  →  1 cm² on map = 4 km²
```

## Compass points and bearings

| Direction | Bearing | Direction | Bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| N | 000° | S | 180° |
| NE | 045° | SW | 225° |
| E | 090° | W | 270° |
| SE | 135° | NW | 315° |

Three rules for any bearing:

1. Start at the point you are at, and face north.
2. Turn clockwise to the target.
3. Write three figures, with zeros in front if needed.

## Back bearings

```
bearing less than 180°:    add 180°
bearing 180° or more:      subtract 180°
```

| Bearing of B from A | Bearing of A from B |
|---|---|
| 072° | 252° |
| 135° | 315° |
| 248° | 068° |
| 305° | 125° |

The reason is that north lines are parallel, so the two angles at A and B are co-interior and add up to 180°.

## Method box: a bearings problem

1. Draw a sketch and put a north line at each point.
2. Mark every distance and bearing given.
3. Work out the angles inside the triangle using back bearings and the fact that angles in a triangle sum to 180°.
4. Use Pythagoras, the sine rule, the cosine rule or trigonometry as needed.
5. Convert the final angle into a bearing, adding to or subtracting from the original bearing.
6. Give the bearing with three figures.

### Example

A ship sails 12 km on 040° and then 9 km on 130°.

```
angle at the turning point = (040° + 180°) − 130° = 90°
distance from start = √(12² + 9²) = 15 km
bearing of finish from start = 040° + tan⁻¹(9/12) = 040° + 36.9° = 076.9°
```

## Scale drawings

1. Choose a scale that fits the page.
2. Use a ruler for all straight lines and a protractor for bearings.
3. Label all points and write the scale on the drawing.
4. Measure the required length or angle.
5. Convert lengths using the scale.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Bearing of B from A | measure at A |
| Bearing of A from B | measure at B |
| Length scale | applies to lengths |
| Area scale | is the length scale squared |
| Writing 45° | a bearing must be written 045° |

## Exam traps

- Measuring from the wrong point.
- Writing two-figure bearings.
- Adding 180° to a large bearing and not subtracting 360° afterwards.
- Applying a length scale to an area.
- Mixing units: cm, m and km.
- Not using a ruler for straight edges in a drawing.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Draw | produce an accurate drawing, with a ruler |
| Measure | read a value from a diagram |
| Calculate | work out from given information |
| Find | calculate or measure as the question indicates |
| Write down | give the answer without working |

## Self-test

1. A map scale is 1 : 20 000. A path is 7.5 km long. Find its length on the map in centimetres.
2. A plan has scale 1 : 500. A hall is 36 m long. Find its drawn length.
3. The bearing of B from A is 252°. Find the bearing of A from B.
4. The bearing of Y from X is 037°. Find the bearing of X from Y.
5. On a map, 1 cm represents 2 km. A forest has area 5 cm². Find its real area.
6. Write down the bearing of south-east.
7. A walker goes 6 km on a bearing of 090° and then 8 km on a bearing of 000°. Find the distance from the start and the bearing of the finish from the start (to the nearest degree).
8. P is 5 km due south of Q. R is 12 km due east of P. Find QR and the bearing of R from Q (to the nearest degree).
9. The bearing of C from D is 310°. Find the bearing of D from C.
10. A scale drawing has 1 cm for 5 m. A drawn line is 8.6 cm. Find its real length.

### Answers

1. 7.5 km = 750 000 cm; 750 000 ÷ 20 000 = **37.5 cm**.
2. 3600 ÷ 500 = **7.2 cm**.
3. 252° − 180° = **072°**.
4. 037° + 180° = **217°**.
5. 1 cm² represents 4 km², so 5 × 4 = **20 km²**.
6. **135°**.
7. Distance = √(36 + 64) = **10 km**. Angle from north: tan⁻¹(6/8) = 36.9°, so the bearing is **037°**.
8. QR = √(25 + 144) = **13 km**. The angle at Q measured from the south line is tan⁻¹(12/5) = 67.4°, so the bearing is 180° − 67.4° = 112.6°, which is **113°** to the nearest degree.
9. 310° − 180° = **130°**.
10. 8.6 × 5 = **43 m**.
