These questions cover section 3.2.2.2, Challenges of rapid economic development, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Related: [rapid economic development study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-challenges-rapid-economic-development/) and [rapid economic development revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-challenges-rapid-economic-development-revision-notes/). Questions 8 to 13 ask about an LIC or NEE case study: use the country you have studied.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a transnational corporation. **[1]**

**2.** Name the sector of the economy that includes call centres and banking. **[1]**

**3.** Give **one** example of what bilateral aid might include. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** reasons why a government might create a special economic zone. **[2]**

**5.** Name **one** environmental problem caused by rapid industrial growth. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the share of workers in each sector in an invented NEE.

| Sector | 1980 (%) | 2020 (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 68 | 24 |
| Secondary | 18 | 30 |
| Tertiary | 14 | 46 |

**(a)** Calculate the fall in the share of workers in the primary sector between 1980 and 2020. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the changes in the structure of employment shown by the table. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **two** reasons for the change in the share of tertiary workers. **[4]**

**7.** The invented NEE's exports were worth US$ 20 billion in 1990 and US$ 260 billion in 2020. Calculate how many times greater exports were in 2020 than in 1990. **[1]**

**8.** For a named LIC or NEE, explain the importance of the country regionally and globally. **[3]**

**9.** For a named LIC or NEE, explain how manufacturing industry has stimulated economic development. **[4]**

**10.** For a named LIC or NEE, explain the advantages and disadvantages of TNCs to the country. **[6]**

**11.** Explain how international aid can have both positive and negative impacts on the receiving country. **[4]**

**12.** For a named LIC or NEE, explain **two** environmental impacts of economic development. **[4]**

**13.** For a named LIC or NEE, assess the effects of economic development on the quality of life of its people. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** A company that operates in more than one country (with headquarters in one and factories or offices in others) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Tertiary (accept: service sector) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any one of: money, food, equipment or expertise given by one government directly to another government [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: to attract foreign investment; to create jobs; to encourage exports; to bring in technology and skills; to test economic reforms in a small area [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Any one from: air pollution; water pollution; loss of farmland; deforestation; waste; carbon emissions [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 68 - 24 = 44 percentage points [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The primary sector fell sharply, from 68% to 24% [1]. The secondary sector increased from 18% to 30% [1]. The tertiary sector grew the most, from 14% to 46%, and became the largest sector by 2020 [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Reason 1, such as rising incomes create demand for services like shops, banks, education and health [2]. Reason 2, such as industrial growth and urbanisation need transport, finance and trade services [2]; or technology and foreign investment, such as call centres and IT firms, create service jobs [2]; or a growing tourism industry [2]. **[4]**

**7.** 260 ÷ 20 = 13 times [1]. **[1]**

**8.** Named country [1]. Regional importance, such as it is the largest economy in its region or a major trading partner of its neighbours [1]. Global importance, such as it is a major producer or exporter of a product, a member of an international organisation, or has a large population [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Factories create jobs, raising incomes [1]. Exports of manufactured goods earn foreign currency [1]. This pays for imports of machinery and technology, and allows investment [1]. The multiplier effect: workers spend money and other industries such as transport, services and construction expand [1]. Tax revenue funds roads, power and schools. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**10.** Named country and TNC [1]. Advantages, such as jobs, investment, transfer of technology and skills, exports, tax revenue (up to 2 marks for developed advantages) [2]. Disadvantages, such as low wages, profits going abroad, factories relocating when costs rise, pollution, loss of local firms (up to 2 marks for developed disadvantages) [2]. A concluding judgement on the balance of advantages and disadvantages, with a reason [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Positive: aid can fund schools, clinics, roads and clean water, improving health and education [1]. Emergency aid saves lives after disasters [1]. Negative: it can create dependency, so the country relies on others [1]. It may be tied to the donor's conditions or goods, or lost through corruption, so it may not reach the poorest people [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Two impacts, each with development (2 marks each). For example: air pollution from coal-fired power stations and factories causes smog and health problems [2]; water pollution from industrial waste and fertiliser harms rivers and fish [2]; loss of farmland and forests to cities and factories reduces habitats [2]; greenhouse gas emissions increase and contribute to climate change [2]. **[4]**

**13.** Named country [1]. Positive effects with detail, such as higher incomes, less poverty, higher life expectancy, wider education [2]. Negative effects with detail, such as inequality between regions, poor conditions for some workers, pollution that harms health [2]. Judgement on the overall effect and who gains most, with a reason [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only advantages (or only disadvantages) of TNCs.
- Writing "pollution" with no cause or effect.
- Case-study answers with no named country or facts.
- Describing a table without quoting figures.
- Confusing the sectors of the economy.
- Judgements without a reasoned conclusion.
