## Section A

**1.** Using exercise as an example, explain why the activities of different organs must be coordinated. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why a hormone carried to every part of the body in the blood affects only some cells. **[2]**

**3.** On a cold day, temperature receptors in the skin detect a fall in temperature, the hypothalamus is stimulated, and skeletal muscles begin to shiver. Name the receptor, the coordinator and the effector in this response. **[3]**

**4.** State whether each of the following is an example of negative feedback or positive feedback.

**(a)** A rise in blood glucose leads to insulin secretion, which lowers blood glucose. **[1]**

**(b)** Platelets at a wound release chemicals that attract more platelets. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why having separate mechanisms to raise and to lower body temperature gives more precise control than having only one. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A volunteer moved from a warm room into a cold room. Her skin temperature fell from 33.0 °C to 27.0 °C in 20 minutes, while her core temperature fell only from 37.0 °C to 36.8 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of fall of skin temperature in °C per minute. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the skin temperature fell much more than the core temperature. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the importance of the peripheral temperature receptors in this situation. **[1]**

**7.** A lizard spends the early morning lying on a sunlit rock with its side facing the sun. By midday it moves into the shade of a bush, and in the late afternoon, as the air cools, it lies on the rock again with its body pressed flat against the warm surface. Describe and explain these behaviours. **[4]**

**8.** Describe how the hypothalamus brings about a fall in body temperature when core temperature rises above the set point. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain why an endotherm needs much more food than an ectotherm of the same mass. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why very small mammals have particularly high metabolic rates. **[2]**

**10.** If the core temperature of a person falls well below normal, it may continue to fall even without further cooling. Explain why this is an example of positive feedback. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a factor controlled by negative feedback fluctuates slightly about its set point instead of staying exactly constant. **[2]**

**12.** Plan an investigation into the effect of light exercise on the skin temperature of a volunteer's forehead. Include the independent and dependent variables, two controlled variables and one ethical consideration. **[4]**

**13.** Desert foxes have very large ears with many blood vessels near the surface. Suggest how this helps them to keep their core temperature down. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** During exercise muscles need more oxygen and glucose and produce more carbon dioxide and heat [1]. Breathing rate, heart rate and blood flow to muscles must all increase together, and heat must be lost through the skin, so the organs involved must act in a coordinated way [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Only target cells have receptors on their membranes [1] with a shape complementary to the hormone, so the hormone binds only to these cells and they alone respond [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Receptor: **peripheral temperature receptors in the skin** [1]. Coordinator: **the hypothalamus** [1]. Effector: **skeletal muscles** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** **Negative** feedback [1].

**(b)** **Positive** feedback [1]. **[2]**

**5.** With one mechanism, the factor could only be brought back by switching that mechanism on or off [1]. With separate mechanisms the body can actively raise or actively lower the factor, so it can correct a change in either direction faster and keep it closer to the set point [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** (33.0 − 27.0) ÷ 20 = **0.30 °C per minute** [1].

**(b)** Arterioles supplying the skin constricted, so less warm blood flowed near the surface and the skin cooled [1]. This reduced heat loss from the blood, so the core temperature was kept close to its set point by negative feedback [1].

**(c)** They detect the fall in external (skin) temperature before the core temperature changes, so responses can begin early [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The skin temperature falling is part of the response, not a failure of control: it is the core temperature that is kept within narrow limits.

**7.** In the morning the lizard's body temperature is low, so it basks to absorb heat from the sun and from the warm rock by conduction [1]. Turning its side to the sun maximises the surface area exposed to radiation [1]. At midday it moves into shade to avoid overheating [1]. In the late afternoon, pressing against the warm rock gains heat by conduction as the air cools; all of these are behavioural responses, because an ectotherm relies on heat from its surroundings [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Temperature receptors in the hypothalamus detect the rise in blood temperature (and skin receptors may detect a rise in skin temperature) [1]. The hypothalamus sends nerve impulses to effectors [1]. Arterioles supplying skin capillaries dilate, so more blood flows near the surface and more heat is lost by radiation [1]. Sweat glands secrete more sweat, and evaporation of water removes heat; hair erector muscles relax so hairs lie flat (also accept reduced metabolic rate) [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** An endotherm releases much of the energy from its food as heat to keep its core temperature constant [1]. An ectotherm gains most of its heat from the surroundings, so it uses far less energy and needs less food [1].

**(b)** Small mammals have a large surface area to volume ratio [1], so they lose heat quickly to the surroundings and must release more heat by respiration per unit mass [1]. **[4]**

**10.** A low body temperature slows enzyme-controlled reactions, including respiration [1]. Less heat is released by metabolism, so body temperature falls further [1]. The change causes a response that makes the change larger, which is positive feedback [1]. **[3]**

**11.** There is a short delay between a change being detected and the effectors correcting it [1]. The factor overshoots the set point slightly before the opposite correction starts, so it oscillates about the set point within narrow limits [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Independent variable: exercise (for example time spent stepping, or none) [1]. Dependent variable: forehead temperature measured with a digital thermometer or data logger probe at set times [1]. Controlled variables, any two for [1]: room temperature; the same volunteer or similar volunteers; time of day; clothing; same position of the probe. Ethical consideration, one for [1]: volunteers give informed consent and can stop at any time; volunteers should be healthy; exercise should be light. **[4]**

**13.** The large surface area of the ears gives more area for heat loss [1]. Vasodilation of arterioles in the ears allows a large volume of warm blood to flow near the surface, so more heat is lost by radiation [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing blood vessels as moving towards or away from the skin.
- Writing "sweat cools the body" without evaporation removing heat.
- Missing the coordinator, or calling the hypothalamus an effector.
- Giving positive feedback examples that are really negative feedback.
- Saying ectotherms have no control over body temperature.
- Plans with no units, no named measuring instrument or no ethical point.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does skin temperature change so much if mammals are endotherms?**
Endotherms keep their core temperature constant. Changing blood flow to the skin is one of the ways they do this, so the skin temperature itself varies widely.

**How do I recognise positive feedback in an unfamiliar example?**
Ask whether the response makes the original change bigger. If it does, it is positive feedback; if it brings the factor back towards its starting value, it is negative feedback.

**Do I need named hormones for temperature control?**
For 5.1.1(d) the specification focuses on peripheral receptors, the hypothalamus, effectors in skin and muscles and behavioural responses. Describing the nerve-based responses fully is the priority.
