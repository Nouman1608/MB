Condensed recall for **3.2.3 Brain and neuropsychology** (AQA GCSE Psychology 8182, Paper 2). For
full explanations and evaluation, read the
[Brain and Neuropsychology study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-psychology-8182-brain-and-neuropsychology/).

## Nervous system map

- **CNS** = brain + spinal cord.
- **PNS** = everything else, split into:
  - **somatic** -- senses and voluntary movement of skeletal muscles;
  - **autonomic** -- involuntary functions; **sympathetic** (fight-or-flight) and
    **parasympathetic** (rest and digest).

## Fight-or-flight, in order

Threat perceived → sympathetic branch activated → adrenal glands release **adrenaline** → heart
rate and breathing up, pupils dilate, blood to muscles, digestion slows → threat passes →
parasympathetic branch restores rest.

## James-Lange theory of emotion

Event → **bodily change** → brain interprets the change → **emotion**. "We feel afraid because our
heart races." *Against:* many emotions share the same arousal; people with spinal cord injuries
still report emotions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sensory neuron | Receptors → CNS |
| Relay neuron | Connects neurons within the CNS |
| Motor neuron | CNS → muscles and glands |
| Synapse | Gap between two neurons |
| Neurotransmitter | Chemical messenger released into the synapse |
| Excitation | Makes the next neuron more likely to fire |
| Inhibition | Makes the next neuron less likely to fire |
| Cell assembly | Group of neurons strengthened by firing together (Hebb) |
| Localisation of function | Particular brain areas do particular jobs |
| Cognitive neuroscience | Study of how brain structure and function relate to thought and behaviour |

## Synaptic transmission in four steps

1. Impulse reaches the axon terminal.
2. Neurotransmitter released into the synapse.
3. Neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the next neuron.
4. Next neuron excited or inhibited.

**Hebb (1949):** repeated co-activation strengthens connections → learning physically changes the
brain ("fire together, wire together").

## Brain areas

| Area | Function |
|---|---|
| Frontal lobe | Thinking, planning; **motor area**; **Broca's area** (speech production) |
| Parietal lobe | **Somatosensory area** (touch, temperature, pain) |
| Occipital lobe | **Visual area** |
| Temporal lobe | **Auditory area**, memory; **Wernicke's area** (understanding language) |
| Cerebellum | Balance and co-ordination |

Each hemisphere controls the **opposite** side of the body.

## Studies at a glance

**Penfield -- interpretive cortex.** Awake epilepsy patients; temporal lobes stimulated with weak
current; some reported vivid experiences or memories.
*Evaluation:* direct evidence in living people; but few patients reported this, all had epilepsy,
experiences may not be real memories.

**Tulving (1989) -- 'gold' memory study.** Radioactive gold injected; tiny sample including Tulving;
thinking about episodic memories linked to **frontal** activity, semantic to **back** of cortex.
*Evaluation:* biological support for different memory types; but tiny, non-naive sample,
inconsistent results.

## Scans

| Scan | Shows | Method |
|---|---|---|
| CT | Structure | X-rays |
| PET | Activity | Radioactive tracer |
| fMRI | Activity | Blood-oxygen changes; no radiation |

**Neurological damage:** stroke or injury → effects depend on location; motor area damage → weakness
on the opposite side; Broca's area damage → difficulty producing speech; frontal damage → changes in
planning, self-control and personality.

## Self-test

1. Which branch of the autonomic nervous system produces fight-or-flight?
2. State the James-Lange order of events.
3. Where are relay neurons found?
4. What is the difference between excitation and inhibition?
5. Which lobe contains the visual area?
6. Which scan shows structure rather than activity?
7. What did Tulving find about episodic memory?
8. Why might a stroke in the left hemisphere weaken the right arm?

**Answers:** 1. The sympathetic branch. 2. Event, then bodily change, then interpretation of that
change as an emotion. 3. Within the CNS. 4. Excitation makes the next neuron more likely to fire;
inhibition makes it less likely. 5. The occipital lobe. 6. CT. 7. Thinking about episodic memories
was associated with more activity in frontal areas. 8. Each hemisphere's motor area controls the
opposite side of the body.
