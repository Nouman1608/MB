This guide covers **Paper 1 The History and Culture of Pakistan**, one
of two papers in OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies (9236),
for International GCSE exams May/June 2026 onwards. Paper 1 has two
compulsory topics plus a choice of one of two optional topics,
examined across the paper's sections.

## Where this fits in 9236

Paper 1 covers the modern political history of Pakistan -- from the
movement for independence through to the 21st century -- alongside a
choice of deeper historical background, either the arrival and
influence of Islam in the subcontinent or the Mughal Empire. It pairs
with Paper 2, which covers the human and physical geography of
Pakistan.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE PAKISTAN STUDIES (9236) — PAPER 1 THE
HISTORY AND CULTURE OF PAKISTAN**

- 3.1.1 The Pakistan Movement (1857–1947) — compulsory topic covering
the political and social developments leading to the creation of
Pakistan
- 3.1.2 Pakistan into the 21st Century: From Independence to 2022 —
compulsory topic covering Pakistan's political and social development
since independence
- 3.1.3 Islam in the Subcontinent (c700–c1831) — optional topic
- 3.1.4 The Mughal Empire (c1526–c1850s) — optional topic

## How to approach it

The two compulsory topics (3.1.1 and 3.1.2) form a continuous historical
narrative from the movement for independence through to recent
decades, so revise them as a connected timeline rather than two
isolated blocks -- exam questions often ask students to explain how
earlier developments shaped later ones. Whichever optional topic a
course follows (3.1.3 or 3.1.4), build a clear sense of its own internal
chronology and key turning points, since this depth of knowledge is
what distinguishes strong optional-topic answers from general
awareness. Because Paper 1 draws on named dates, individuals and
events throughout, keep a running timeline as part of revision rather
than relying on prose summaries alone.

## Reform movements before 1857

Muslim revival in the subcontinent began before British rule was consolidated. **Shah Waliullah** (1703-62) translated the Qur'an into Persian and sought to unify Muslims around scripture. **Syed Ahmad Barelvi** led the Jihad movement in the north-west. (The Faraizi movement in Bengal under Haji Shariatullah is required content on Cambridge's Pakistan Studies syllabuses, not on this OxfordAQA specification, and is not examinable here.)

These movements matter because they establish that Muslim political consciousness predated, rather than resulted from, British policy.

## 1857 and its consequences

The **War of Independence (1857)** — described by the British as a mutiny — arose from grievances including the Doctrine of Lapse, annexations, the greased cartridges, resentment at missionary activity and economic distress.

Its failure had consequences that shaped everything after: the East India Company was replaced by direct Crown rule, the Mughal emperor was deposed, and the British held Muslims disproportionately responsible, excluding them from government service and education.

## Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement

Sir Syed (1817-98) concluded that Muslim recovery required reconciliation with the British and the acquisition of modern education. He founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh** in 1875, wrote *The Causes of the Indian Revolt* and *Loyal Muhammadans of India*, and promoted scientific education through the Scientific Society.

His **Two Nation Theory** — that Hindus and Muslims were distinct nations in religion, culture, law and history — became the intellectual foundation of the Pakistan Movement. He opposed Muslim participation in the Indian National Congress on the grounds that Muslims would be permanently outvoted.

## The path to Partition

Key stages, which examiners expect in sequence with dates:

- **1906** — Simla Deputation and the founding of the **All India Muslim League**
- **1909** — Morley-Minto Reforms grant separate electorates
- **1930** — Allama Iqbal's Allahabad Address proposing a north-western Muslim state
- **1933** — Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coins the name "Pakistan"
- **1940** — **Lahore Resolution**
- **1947** — Independence, and the Radcliffe Award

**Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah** moved from "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity" to leader of the demand for a separate state, the shift driven by Congress rule in the provinces (1937-39) and the failure of constitutional guarantees.

## Worked example

Why was Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's educational work so important? Explain your answer.

```
Context   after 1857 Muslims were excluded from government service and
          had rejected English education, falling behind Hindus

Action    Aligarh College taught modern subjects alongside Islamic values
          reconciled Muslims to English education without abandoning faith

Result    produced an educated Muslim professional class
          Aligarh graduates later led the Muslim League and the
          Pakistan Movement itself

Judgement his greatest contribution, because political organisation
          required an educated leadership that did not previously exist
```

## Common mistakes

Calling 1857 a "mutiny" without noting that this is the British characterisation. Dating the Muslim League to 1905 rather than 1906. Attributing the name "Pakistan" to Iqbal rather than Rahmat Ali. Saying Jinnah always wanted partition, which ignores his earlier unity work. Listing events without explaining causation, when "explain why" questions require it.

## Quick revision checklist

- Outline the pre-1857 reform movements and why they matter.
- Explain the causes and consequences of 1857, especially for Muslims.
- Describe Sir Syed's work and assess its importance.
- State the Two Nation Theory and trace its development to 1940.
- Sequence the stages from 1906 to 1947 with accurate dates.
- Explain Jinnah's changing position and what caused the change.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are the 9236 papers structured?**
Each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 77 marks: Section A (28 marks) and Section B (28 marks) are compulsory topics, and Section C (21 marks) is one of two optional topics.

**Which Paper 1 topics are compulsory?**
3.1.1 The Pakistan Movement (1857–1947) and 3.1.2 Pakistan into the 21st century: from independence to 2022.

**How should the compulsory topics be revised?**
As one continuous narrative from the reform movements to the present, with dates, people and turning points.
