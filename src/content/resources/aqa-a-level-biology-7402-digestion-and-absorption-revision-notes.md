Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.3.3 Digestion and absorption**. Fuller explanations and a worked inhibitor experiment are in the [digestion and absorption study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-digestion-and-absorption/). Test yourself with the [digestion and absorption practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-digestion-and-absorption-practice/).

## One-line summary

Digestion is **hydrolysis** of large molecules into small ones; absorption across the ileum epithelium is by **co-transport** (monosaccharides, amino acids) or **diffusion from micelles** (monoglycerides, fatty acids).

## Enzyme table

| Enzyme | Made by / located | Substrate | Bond | Products |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Salivary glands, pancreas | Starch | Glycosidic | Maltose |
| Maltase | Membrane-bound, ileum epithelium | Maltose | Glycosidic | Glucose |
| Sucrase | Membrane-bound | Sucrose | Glycosidic | Glucose, fructose |
| Lactase | Membrane-bound | Lactose | Glycosidic | Glucose, galactose |
| Lipase | Pancreas | Triglyceride | Ester | Monoglycerides, fatty acids |
| Endopeptidase | Stomach, pancreas | Polypeptide (middle) | Peptide | Shorter polypeptides |
| Exopeptidase | Pancreas, ileum | Polypeptide (ends) | Peptide | Amino acids, dipeptides |
| Dipeptidase | Membrane-bound | Dipeptide | Peptide | Amino acids |

## Bile salts

- Made in the liver.
- **Emulsify** lipids: large droplets → many small droplets.
- Larger total surface area for lipase → faster hydrolysis.
- Physical change only; no bonds hydrolysed.
- Also form **micelles** with monoglycerides and fatty acids.

## Why three protein-digesting enzymes

- Endopeptidases make many shorter chains.
- More chain ends → more sites for exopeptidases.
- Dipeptidases finish at the membrane, next to absorption sites.
- Faster overall production of amino acids.

## Co-transport of glucose (and galactose, amino acids)

1. Na⁺ actively pumped out of epithelial cell into blood (Na⁺/K⁺ pump, uses ATP).
2. Na⁺ concentration in cell kept lower than in the ileum lumen.
3. Na⁺ diffuses into cell through a co-transporter protein.
4. Glucose carried in with it, against its own gradient.
5. Glucose leaves the cell into the blood by facilitated diffusion.

ATP is used indirectly: it maintains the Na⁺ gradient.

## Micelles and lipid absorption

- Micelle = bile salts + monoglycerides + fatty acids; water-soluble.
- Carry lipid products to the epithelial cell surface.
- Break up, releasing monoglycerides and fatty acids next to the membrane.
- These are non-polar, so diffuse through the phospholipid bilayer.
- Micelles maintain a concentration gradient at the membrane.

## Adaptations of the ileum epithelium to link

- Villi and microvilli: large surface area.
- Thin epithelium: short diffusion distance.
- Many carrier and co-transporter proteins.
- Membrane-bound enzymes next to transport proteins.
- Many mitochondria: ATP for the sodium–potassium pump.

## Data-question patterns

| What the data show | What to write |
|---|---|
| Respiratory inhibitor or no oxygen cuts glucose uptake | No ATP → Na⁺/K⁺ pump stops → no Na⁺ gradient → no co-transport |
| Uptake rate levels off at high glucose concentration | All co-transporter proteins in use; carrier number is limiting |
| No sodium ions in the lumen solution | Nothing to diffuse in through the co-transporter, so glucose uptake falls |
| Bile duct blocked | No emulsification or micelles → slower lipid digestion and absorption; more lipid in faeces |
| Enzyme missing (for example lactase) | That disaccharide is not hydrolysed and cannot be absorbed |

For percentage change, divide the change by the original value and multiply by 100.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydrolysis | Breaking a chemical bond by adding water |
| Disaccharidase | Enzyme that hydrolyses a disaccharide into monosaccharides |
| Emulsification | Breaking lipid into small droplets, increasing surface area |
| Endopeptidase | Hydrolyses peptide bonds within a polypeptide |
| Exopeptidase | Hydrolyses peptide bonds at the ends of a polypeptide |
| Co-transport | Two substances carried together through one protein; one moves down its gradient, driving the other against its gradient |
| Micelle | Water-soluble structure of bile salts, monoglycerides and fatty acids that carries lipid products to the epithelium |

## Exam traps

- Amylase → maltose, not glucose.
- Bile salts do not contain enzymes and do not hydrolyse lipids.
- "Glucose is actively transported" is too vague; describe the Na⁺ gradient and co-transport.
- Na⁺ is pumped out of the cell on the **blood** side, not the lumen side.
- Glucose leaves the epithelial cell by facilitated diffusion.
- Endo = inside the chain; exo = at the ends.
- Fatty acids and monoglycerides cross the membrane by diffusion, not co-transport.

## Quick self-test

1. What type of reaction is digestion?
2. Name the product of starch digestion by amylase.
3. Give the products when lactose is hydrolysed.
4. Where are disaccharidases found?
5. How do bile salts increase the rate of lipid digestion?
6. What are the products of lipase action?
7. Explain the difference between an endopeptidase and an exopeptidase.
8. Why does producing more chain ends speed up protein digestion?
9. Describe the role of the sodium–potassium pump in glucose absorption.
10. How does glucose leave the epithelial cell into the blood?
11. What is a micelle made of?
12. Why can monoglycerides cross the epithelial cell membrane by diffusion?

### Answers

1. Hydrolysis.
2. Maltose.
3. Glucose and galactose.
4. Attached to the cell-surface membranes of epithelial cells lining the ileum.
5. They emulsify lipids into smaller droplets, giving a larger surface area for lipase to act on.
6. Monoglycerides and fatty acids.
7. Endopeptidases hydrolyse peptide bonds within the chain; exopeptidases hydrolyse bonds at the ends, removing terminal amino acids.
8. Exopeptidases act at the ends of chains, so more ends gives more sites for them to act at once.
9. It actively transports Na⁺ out of the epithelial cell into the blood, keeping the Na⁺ concentration in the cell lower than in the lumen, so Na⁺ diffuses in through the co-transporter, bringing glucose.
10. By facilitated diffusion.
11. Bile salts, monoglycerides and fatty acids.
12. They are non-polar (lipid-soluble), so they dissolve in and pass through the phospholipid bilayer.
