Condensed notes for section 1.1, Types of business organisation, Topic 1 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1), Issue 1, October 2016. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign.

## The specification points

- **1.1 a** Explain the characteristics of public sector organisations, private sector organisations, sole traders and partnerships.
- **1.1 b** Explain the connection between stakeholders and a business, identifying the stakeholders' use of the business's financial statements.

## Public sector versus private sector

| | Public sector | Private sector |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Government (national or local) | Individuals or groups of individuals |
| Main aim | Provide a service to the public | Usually make a profit |
| Finance | Mainly taxation; some charges | Owners' capital, retained profit, loans |
| Accountable to | Government and taxpayers | The owners |
| Examples | State school, public hospital, council refuse service | Sole traders, partnerships |

## Sole trader versus partnership

| Feature | Sole trader | Partnership |
|---|---|---|
| Number of owners | One | Two or more |
| Liability | Unlimited | Partners personally responsible for the firm's debts |
| Profit | All to the owner | Shared in the agreed ratio |
| Capital | One person's savings and borrowing | Contributions from every partner |
| Decisions | Fast, one person | Shared; more ideas, possible disagreement |
| Skills | One person's | Partners can specialise |
| Continuity | Ends if the owner stops | Must be re-formed when a partner leaves or dies |
| Key document | None required | Partnership agreement |

**Partnership agreement usually covers:** profit-sharing ratio; capital each partner contributes; rules on drawings; what happens when a partner joins or leaves.

**Business entity:** for both forms, the accounting records treat the business as separate from its owners, even though the law does not. Owner's money in = capital; money or goods out for private use = drawings.

## Quick comparison with figures

- Sole trader profit 30,000: owner keeps 30,000.
- Partnership profit 45,000 shared 3 : 2: partners receive 27,000 and 18,000.
- Debts of 60,000 with business assets of 42,000: shortfall 18,000 falls on the owner (sole trader) or the partners (partnership), from their personal assets.

## Stakeholders (1.1 b)

| Stakeholder | Internal or external | Main statement | What they want to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner or partners | Internal | Statement of profit or loss | Profit and their share; growth of capital |
| Managers | Internal | Both | Costs, profit, performance against plans |
| Employees | Internal | Statement of profit or loss | Job security, ability to pay wages |
| Bank or lender | External | Both | Ability to repay; assets available as security |
| Suppliers | External | Statement of financial position | Ability to pay what is owed on time |
| Customers | External | Both | Whether the business will survive to supply them |
| Government or tax authority | External | Statement of profit or loss | Profit on which tax is calculated |
| Local community | External | Both | Whether the business will grow, stay or close |
| Taxpayers (public sector) | External | Organisation's statements | Whether public money is used well |

**Conflicts:** owners may want high drawings; lenders and employees may prefer profit kept in the business.

## Method: a stakeholder answer in three parts

1. Name the stakeholder.
2. Name the statement and the figure they use.
3. Say what decision that figure helps them make.

Example: "The bank would look at profit for the year in the statement of profit or loss, to judge whether the business could afford loan repayments."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public sector | Organisations owned and controlled by government |
| Private sector | Organisations owned by individuals, not government |
| Sole trader | A business owned by one person |
| Partnership | A business owned by two or more people sharing profits |
| Unlimited liability | Owners are personally responsible for all business debts |
| Partnership agreement | A document setting out the terms between partners |
| Capital | The owner's investment in the business |
| Drawings | Money or goods taken by the owner for private use |
| Stakeholder | A person or group with an interest in the business |

## Exam traps

- Writing "limited liability" for a sole trader or partnership.
- Defining the private sector as "businesses that make profit" without the ownership point.
- Listing stakeholders without saying how they use the statements.
- Forgetting that continuity is a disadvantage for both forms.
- Treating drawings as an expense.
- Giving a recommendation with no reasons on both sides.

## Self-test

**1.** Who owns a public sector organisation?
**2.** Give the main aim of most private sector organisations.
**3.** What is meant by unlimited liability?
**4.** Give **two** items usually found in a partnership agreement.
**5.** Profit of 48,000 is shared 3 : 1. What does each partner receive?
**6.** Give **one** advantage of a partnership over a sole trader.
**7.** Give **one** disadvantage of a partnership compared with a sole trader.
**8.** Which stakeholder uses profit to calculate tax due?
**9.** Why does a supplier look at the statement of financial position?
**10.** Name an internal and an external stakeholder.
**11.** Why might a lender and the owner disagree about drawings?
**12.** How does the business entity concept apply to a sole trader?

### Answers

**1.** National or local government, on behalf of the public.
**2.** To make a profit for the owners.
**3.** The owner is personally responsible for all the business's debts, so personal assets can be taken to pay them.
**4.** Any two: profit-sharing ratio; capital contributions; drawings rules; arrangements when a partner joins or leaves.
**5.** 36,000 and 12,000.
**6.** More capital, or shared workload and skills.
**7.** Profits are shared, or decisions must be agreed, or partners are responsible for each other's business decisions.
**8.** The government or tax authority.
**9.** To see whether the business has enough bank and receivables to pay what it owes.
**10.** Internal: owner, manager or employee. External: bank, supplier, customer, government or community.
**11.** Drawings take capital and cash out of the business, reducing what is available to repay the loan.
**12.** The records treat the business as separate from the owner: capital in, drawings out, and no private items in expenses.
