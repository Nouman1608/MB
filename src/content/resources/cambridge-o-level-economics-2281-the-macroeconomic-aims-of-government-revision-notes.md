Quick recall for section 4.2 **The macroeconomic aims of government** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: the five aims, the "reasons behind the choice of aims and the criteria that governments set for each aim", and the three named "possible conflicts between aims". Full explanations are in the [macroeconomic aims study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-macroeconomic-aims-of-government/); test yourself with the [macroeconomic aims practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-macroeconomic-aims-of-government-practice/). Figures are invented unless stated.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Macroeconomic aim | A goal for the economy as a whole |
| Criterion | The measurable standard used to judge whether an aim is met |
| Economic growth | A sustained rise in real GDP |
| Real GDP | Output measured at constant prices, removing the effect of inflation |
| Full employment | Nearly everyone willing and able to work has a job (not zero unemployment) |
| Unemployment rate | Unemployed ÷ labour force × 100 |
| Inflation | A sustained rise in the general price level |
| Stable prices | A low, steady rate of inflation (not zero) |
| Current account | The record of a country's trade in goods and services, plus income flows, with the rest of the world |
| Redistribution of income | Narrowing the gap between high and low incomes |

## The five aims: meaning, reason, criterion

| Aim | Why chosen (cost of missing it) | Typical criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | No rise in living standards; fewer jobs; less tax revenue | Real GDP growth within a target range |
| Low unemployment | Lost output; lost tax; higher benefit spending; poverty and lost skills | Unemployment rate below a stated level |
| Stable prices | Savers and fixed incomes hurt; uncertainty for firms; exports less competitive | Inflation within a target range; many central banks, including the Bank of England and the European Central Bank, aim for 2% |
| Balance of payments stability | Deficits must be financed by borrowing or selling assets; pressure on the currency | Current account deficit or surplus within a stated percentage of GDP |
| Redistribution | Poverty, poor health, lost opportunity, social tension | Gap between top and bottom income groups narrowing |

## Why criteria differ between countries

- Stage of development (a developing economy may target faster growth)
- Size of current problems (start with a realistic target when unemployment is high)
- Political promises and public opinion
- Agreements with international lenders

## Reading a data table

For each indicator, state the figure, the criterion and whether it is met. Then look for patterns: did one aim improve while another worsened? That is evidence of a conflict.

Example: growth up from 1.0% to 4.5% and unemployment down from 7.5% to 4.8%, but inflation up from 1.5% to 4.2% and the current account deficit up from 1.0% to 3.6% of GDP.

## The three named conflicts

**Full employment versus stable prices**
More spending → more jobs → labour becomes scarce → wages and costs rise → prices rise.
Likely near full capacity; less likely with spare capacity or when jobs come from higher productivity.

**Economic growth versus balance of payments stability**
Higher incomes → more imports → deficit widens; rising prices may hurt exports.
Likely when growth is led by consumer spending; less likely when growth is export-led.

**Full employment versus balance of payments stability**
More people in work → more spending → more imports → deficit widens.
Likely when much extra income is spent on imports; less likely when new jobs are in export industries.

## Aims that support each other

- Growth and employment: more output needs more workers.
- Low inflation and the balance of payments: stable prices keep exports competitive.

## How governments set priorities

Biggest problem first; public opinion; stage of development; promises and agreements.

## Exam traps

- Zero unemployment or zero inflation as the definition of the aim.
- An aim with no reason, or a criterion with no number.
- Conflicts stated without a chain.
- Treating conflicts as certain rather than possible.
- Using nominal instead of real GDP for growth.
- Dividing the unemployed by people in work instead of by the labour force.

## Quick self-test

1. List the five macroeconomic aims named in the syllabus.
2. What is a criterion for a macroeconomic aim?
3. A labour force is 8 million and 0.36 million are unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate.
4. A price index rises from 150 to 154.5. Calculate the inflation rate.
5. Nominal GDP rises from 800 to 864 while prices rise by 5%. Calculate real growth to one decimal place.
6. A country's current account deficit is 30 and its GDP is 600. Express the deficit as a percentage of GDP.
7. Give two reasons why governments aim for low unemployment.
8. Why is stable prices not the same as zero inflation?
9. Explain the chain behind the conflict between full employment and stable prices.
10. When is growth least likely to worsen the balance of payments?
11. Name two aims that usually support each other.
12. Give one reason why criteria differ between countries.

### Answers

1. Economic growth; full employment/low unemployment; stable prices/low inflation; balance of payments stability; redistribution of income.
2. The measurable standard, such as a target range for a rate, used to judge whether the aim is met.
3. 0.36 ÷ 8 × 100 = 4.5%.
4. 4.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = 3%.
5. Real GDP = 864 ÷ 1.05 = 822.9; growth = 22.9 ÷ 800 × 100 = 2.9%.
6. 30 ÷ 600 × 100 = 5%.
7. Any two: lost output; lost tax revenue; higher benefit spending; poverty; loss of skills.
8. The aim is a low, steady rate of price increase that people can plan around, not a complete absence of price rises.
9. More spending creates jobs; as spare workers run out, firms compete for staff and wages rise; higher costs and strong demand push prices up.
10. When growth is led by exports (or when imports are machinery that will raise future exports).
11. Economic growth and low unemployment (or low inflation and balance of payments stability).
12. Different stages of development, different current problems, political promises or agreements with lenders (any one).
