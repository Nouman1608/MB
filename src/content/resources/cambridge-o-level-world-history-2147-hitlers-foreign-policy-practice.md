These questions cover key question 2.3 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, which was the Paper 2 prescribed topic for
November 2025.

## Section A

**1.** Describe how Germany rearmed between 1933 and 1936. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** members of the Anti-Comintern Pact by the end of 1937. **[2]**

**3.** Describe German and Italian involvement in the Spanish Civil War. **[4]**

**4.** Describe the crisis over the Sudetenland in September 1938. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** leaders who signed the Munich Agreement. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the terms of the Nazi–Soviet Pact. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did Chamberlain follow a policy of appeasement in 1938? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Stalin sign the Nazi–Soviet Pact in August 1939? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Appeasement was the main reason why war broke out in 1939." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The Munich Agreement was a success for Britain." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined letter by a Czech teacher to a friend in London, October 1938. "Your Prime
Minister is cheered in the streets for bringing peace. Here we have lost our mountains, our fortresses and three
million citizens, and no one asked us. If this is peace, it is a peace that someone else has paid for."

What can you learn from Source A about the effects of the Munich Agreement? Use the source and your knowledge to
explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study Sources B and C, which are invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from an imagined British newspaper editorial, March 1936. "German soldiers have marched into
German towns. It is hard to see why Britain should go to war to keep Germans out of their own back garden."

*Source C (invented)*: from an imagined British newspaper editorial, March 1939. "The pretence is over. Prague is not a
German city. Every promise Herr Hitler has made is now worthless, and those who trusted him must say so."

Why do Sources B and C take such different views of Hitler? Use the sources and your knowledge to explain your answer.
**[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: Germany left the Disarmament Conference and the League in 1933 (1); rearmament began in
secret at first (1); in March 1935 Hitler announced conscription (1) and an air force, the Luftwaffe (1); the army was
expanded far beyond 100,000 men (1); the Anglo-German Naval Agreement (1935) allowed a navy up to 35% of Britain's (1);
arms spending rose and reduced unemployment (1); German troops entered the Rhineland in March 1936 (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: Germany (1); Japan (1); Italy, which joined in 1937 (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: the war began in July 1936 when Franco's Nationalists rebelled against the Republic (1);
Germany and Italy supported Franco (1); German aircraft flew Nationalist troops from Morocco (1); the Condor Legion
fought in Spain (1) and bombed Guernica in 1937 (1); Italy sent tens of thousands of troops (1); both ignored the
Non-Intervention Committee (1); Germany tested weapons and tactics (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: Sudeten Germans led by Henlein demanded union with Germany (1); Hitler threatened war (1);
Chamberlain flew to meet Hitler at Berchtesgaden (1) and agreed in principle to transfer areas with a German majority
(1); at Bad Godesberg Hitler demanded more and faster (1); war seemed close and Britain prepared defences (1); at
Munich on 29–30 September Hitler, Chamberlain, Daladier and Mussolini agreed the transfer of the Sudetenland (1);
Czechoslovakia was not invited (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Hitler (1); Chamberlain (1); Daladier (1); Mussolini (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: signed on 23 August 1939 (1); by Ribbentrop and Molotov (1); Germany and the USSR agreed
not to attack each other (1); for ten years (1); a secret protocol divided Poland between them (1); the Baltic states
were placed in the Soviet sphere (1); Germany would not face a two-front war if it attacked Poland (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **Britain was not
ready for war**, especially in the air, so Chamberlain wanted time to rearm; **public opinion** remembered 1914–18 and
feared bombing of cities; **sympathy with German claims**: the Sudetenland was German-speaking and many thought
Versailles unfair; **lack of allies**: the Dominions and the USA would not support a war, and Chamberlain distrusted
the USSR; **belief that Hitler could be trusted** to stop once German speakers were united. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **distrust of Britain and France** after they excluded the USSR from
Munich and gave way to Hitler; **the weak Anglo-French mission** sent to Moscow in August 1939 suggested they were not
serious; **time**: the Red Army had been weakened by Stalin's purges and needed time to rebuild; **territory**: the
secret protocol gave him eastern Poland and influence over the Baltic states as a buffer; **he hoped Germany and the
West would weaken each other** in a war. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:**
failure to act over the Rhineland, Anschluss and Munich let Germany grow stronger and convinced Hitler that Britain
and France would always give way, so he expected no war over Poland. **Disagree:** Hitler's aims, especially Lebensraum,
made war likely whatever others did; Versailles gave him grievances to exploit; the League's failures removed collective
security; the Nazi–Soviet Pact removed the risk of a two-front war; Britain did stand firm over Poland, and war still
came. **Judgement:** for example, Hitler's aims were the main cause; appeasement explains why he grew strong enough and
confident enough to risk war in 1939. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** it avoided war in 1938 when Britain was unprepared; it bought a year for
rearmament, including fighters and radar; it was popular, and Chamberlain was cheered; it showed the Dominions and
public that Britain had tried every peaceful option, so war in 1939 had wider support. **Disagree:** Czechoslovakia,
with its fortifications, army and Škoda works, was lost to the Allied side; Hitler took the rest of the country in
March 1939; the USSR was alienated, contributing to the Nazi–Soviet Pact; Germany also used the year to rearm.
**Judgement:** a short-term success, but a long-term failure because it rested on trusting Hitler. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5–6): developed inferences supported from the source and context. Level 2 (2–4): inferences
or details explained. Level 1 (1): copies or paraphrases. Indicative content: Czechoslovakia lost territory, its
mountain border and its defensive fortifications ("our fortresses"), leaving it open to attack; around three million
German-speaking people were transferred; Czechoslovakia was not consulted ("no one asked us"); there was a sharp contrast
between relief in Britain and bitterness in Czechoslovakia; the writer implies peace was bought at Czechoslovakia's
expense. Context: Czechoslovakia was not invited to Munich; Poland and Hungary also took land. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (7–8): explains the difference through the changed context of 1936 and 1939, using both
sources. Level 3 (5–6): explains the difference from content or context. Level 2 (2–4): describes the difference.
Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **Source B**, March 1936: Hitler had only moved troops into German
territory, which many British people saw as reasonable revision of an unfair treaty; appeasement had wide support.
**Source C**, March 1939: Hitler had just occupied Bohemia and Moravia, non-German land, breaking the Munich Agreement,
so his claim to want only German territory was disproved. **Explanation:** the difference is caused by events between
the two dates, not simply by different newspapers; British opinion shifted after Prague, leading to the guarantee to
Poland. **[8]**
