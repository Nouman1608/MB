## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What provides the energy for diffusion? **[1]**

A: Respiration in the cell

B: The kinetic energy of random movement of molecules and ions

C: Light absorbed by chlorophyll

D: Protein carriers in the membrane

**2.** State what is meant by "net movement" in the definition of diffusion. **[1]**

**3.** State the direction in which carbon dioxide diffuses between respiring muscle cells and the blood, and give the reason. **[2]**

**4.** A block of agar measures 1 cm × 1 cm × 4 cm. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why diffusion is important for a single-celled organism living in a pond. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student placed pink agar cubes, each with 1 cm sides, in dilute acid at four temperatures and timed how long each cube took to turn colourless.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean time to turn colourless / s | 420 | 300 | 210 | 150 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of temperature on the time taken. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain this effect. **[2]**

**(c)** State **two** variables the student should keep constant. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the rate at 40 °C as 1000 ÷ time. **[1]**

**7.** Plan an investigation into the effect of concentration gradient on the rate of diffusion, using agar cubes and acid. **[6]**

**8.** Explain why a very small organism can obtain enough oxygen by diffusion across its surface, but a large animal cannot. **[3]**

**9.** A crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is placed at the bottom of a beaker of still water. After an hour the purple colour has spread through the water, although nobody stirred it. Explain this observation in terms of particles. **[3]**

**10.** Blood flows continuously past the alveoli. Explain why this helps oxygen to keep diffusing into the blood. **[3]**

**11.** In one condition, four repeat cubes took 180 s, 175 s, 260 s and 185 s to turn colourless.

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean time, leaving out the anomalous result. **[2]**

**12.** Damp red litmus papers were placed at 5 cm, 10 cm, 15 cm and 20 cm along a glass tube. Cotton wool soaked in ammonia solution was placed at one end. The papers turned blue after 20 s, 45 s, 75 s and 110 s.

**(a)** Describe the relationship between distance and the time taken for a paper to turn blue. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain this relationship. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean speed of the ammonia over the first 10 cm, in cm per second, to two decimal places. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Diffusion is passive; it does not use energy from respiration. **[1]**

**2.** The overall movement once movement in both directions has been taken into account [1]. **[1]**

**3.** From the muscle cells into the blood [1], because respiring cells make carbon dioxide, so its concentration is higher in the cells than in the blood [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Surface area = 2 × (1 × 1) + 4 × (1 × 4) = 18 cm²; volume = 4 cm³ [1]. Ratio = 18 ÷ 4 = **4.5 : 1** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Oxygen diffuses from the water, where its concentration is higher, through the cell membrane into the cell [1], where it is used in respiration, and carbon dioxide diffuses out; no special organs are needed because the distance to all parts of the cell is short [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** As the temperature increases, the time taken decreases [1], from 420 s at 10 °C to 150 s at 40 °C [1].

**(b)** At higher temperatures the particles have more kinetic energy and move faster [1], so the acid diffuses into the cube and reaches the centre more quickly [1].

**(c)** Any two: cube size [1]; acid concentration [1]; volume of acid; the same agar mixture.

**(d)** 1000 ÷ 150 = **6.67** (arbitrary units) [1]. **[7]**

**7.** Any six points:

- Make up acid at several different concentrations, for example by diluting with water [1].
- Cut agar cubes of the same size, for example 1 cm sides, from the same block of pink agar [1].
- Place one cube in the same volume of each acid concentration [1].
- Start a timer and record the time for each cube to turn completely colourless [1].
- Keep the temperature the same, for example with a water bath [1].
- Repeat each concentration at least three times and calculate a mean [1].
- Wear eye protection, because acid is an irritant (a further creditworthy point). **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A plan must say what is changed, what is measured and what is kept the same. Repeats and a mean are a common missing mark.

**8.** A small organism has a large surface area compared with its volume [1], and the distance from its surface to every cell is short, so diffusion supplies oxygen fast enough [1]. A large animal has a small surface area to volume ratio and the distance to inner cells is too great, so it needs gas exchange organs and a transport system [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The particles of the substance move randomly [1]. There is a higher concentration of them near the crystal, so more particles move away from the crystal than towards it [1]. This net movement down the concentration gradient continues until the colour is evenly spread [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Blood arriving at the alveoli has a low oxygen concentration [1]. Oxygen that diffuses in is carried away as the blood flows on [1], so the oxygen concentration in the blood next to the alveoli stays lower than in the air, keeping a steep concentration gradient [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** **260 s** [1].

**(b)** (180 + 175 + 185) ÷ 3 [1] = **180 s** [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** The greater the distance, the longer the time taken [1].

**(b)** Ammonia particles move randomly and spread down the concentration gradient [1]; they take longer to travel a greater distance, so papers further along change later [1].

**(c)** 10 ÷ 45 = **0.22 cm/s** [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "particles spread out" without "net" and the direction.
- Saying diffusion needs energy from respiration.
- Describing a trend without quoting data and units.
- Plans that change two variables, or that leave out repeats.
- Forgetting to leave out the anomaly before calculating a mean.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which factors might a diffusion investigation test?**
Surface area, temperature, concentration gradient or distance, the four the syllabus names.

**How do I describe a trend in a table?**
Say what happens to the dependent variable as the independent variable increases, and quote the first and last values with units.
