## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which blood vessel carries blood out of the right ventricle?

A: aorta

B: pulmonary vein

C: pulmonary artery

D: vena cava

**[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by an artery. **[1]**

**3.** A heart diagram shows a chamber with a much thicker muscular wall than any other chamber. Name this chamber. **[1]**

**4.** An ECG trace shows 6 peaks in exactly 5 seconds, with each peak marking one heartbeat. Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute. **[2]**

**5.** State the function of the coronary arteries. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the valves that prevent blood flowing back from the aorta into the left ventricle. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A doctor uses a stethoscope to listen to a patient's chest.

**(a)** State what causes the sounds the doctor hears. **[1]**

**(b)** State **two** other ways the activity of the heart can be monitored. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* The first sound happens as the ventricles begin to contract. Name the valves that close at this moment and explain why they close. **[2]**

**8.** A student investigates the effect of the intensity of exercise on heart rate. She does step-ups for two minutes at three different rates: 15, 25 and 35 steps per minute.

**(a)** Name the independent variable and the dependent variable. **[2]**

**(b)** State **three** variables she should keep the same. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why she should rest between each trial until her pulse returns to its resting rate. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows a person's heart rate during and after a period of running.

| Time / minutes | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Activity | rest | running | running | resting | resting | resting | resting |
| Heart rate / beats per minute | 66 | 140 | 152 | 120 | 94 | 74 | 66 |

**(a)** Describe the changes in heart rate shown in the table. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate between 0 and 4 minutes. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain why the heart rate increased during running. **[3]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A baby is born with a small hole in the septum between the left and right ventricles.

**(a)** Explain the importance of the septum in a healthy heart. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the baby may become tired easily. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Describe the functioning of the heart from the moment the ventricles start to contract until they have relaxed, referring to the action of the valves. **[4]**

**12.** One branch of a coronary artery becomes blocked.

**(a)** Explain why the part of the heart muscle supplied by this branch may die. **[3]**

**(b)** The syllabus lists seven risk factors for coronary heart disease. Name **three** that a person cannot change. **[3]**

**(c)** Discuss how diet and exercise could reduce this person's risk of a further blockage. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The right ventricle sends deoxygenated blood to the lungs, so the vessel is an artery (away from the heart) carrying deoxygenated blood.

**2.** A blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Carries oxygenated blood" scores zero, because the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.

**3.** The left ventricle [1].

**4.** 6 beats in 5 s, so 6 ÷ 5 × 60 [1] = **72 beats per minute** [1].

**5.** To supply the heart muscle (with blood) [1] carrying oxygen and glucose (for respiration) [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Supply the heart with blood" is not enough; the heart muscle is the tissue being supplied, and naming oxygen or glucose earns the second mark.

**6.** Semilunar valves [1].

**7. (a)** Valves closing [1].

**(b)** ECG [1]; pulse rate [1].

**(c)** The atrioventricular valves [1]; pressure in the ventricles becomes higher than in the atria, so the valves close to prevent blood flowing back into the atria [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c) the reason must mention backflow into the atria or the pressure change; "to stop blood going the wrong way" is accepted only if the atria are named.

**8. (a)** Independent: rate of stepping / intensity of exercise [1]. Dependent: heart rate [1].

**(b)** Any three of: duration of exercise (two minutes); height of the step; the same person; time between stopping and counting the pulse; length of time the pulse is counted for; rest period before each trial [1] [1] [1].

**(c)** So that each trial starts from the same heart rate / the effect of the previous trial does not affect the next result [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Keep it fair" is not a variable. Name the quantity, such as "the height of the step".

**9. (a)** Heart rate increased from 66 to 152 beats per minute during running [1]; it fell after running stopped [1]; it returned to the resting rate of 66 by 12 minutes [1].

**(b)** (152 − 66) ÷ 66 × 100 [1] = 130.30...% = **130%** [1].

**(c)** Muscle cells respire faster during running [1]; they need more oxygen and glucose and must remove more carbon dioxide [1]; a faster heart rate pumps blood to the muscles faster, delivering oxygen and glucose and removing carbon dioxide at a greater rate [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), quote figures from the table with units. In (c), "the body needs more energy" does not score: respiration releases energy, and the answer must say what the blood delivers.

**10. (a)** It separates oxygenated blood on the left from deoxygenated blood on the right [1], so blood pumped to the body carries as much oxygen as possible [1].

**(b)** Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix, so blood leaving in the aorta carries less oxygen [1]; less oxygen reaches respiring cells / less aerobic respiration, so less energy is released [1].

*Examiner insight:* For "suggest", apply the function you gave in (a) to the new situation; answers that only repeat (a) score in (a) but not in (b).

**11.** The ventricles contract and the pressure in them rises [1]. The atrioventricular valves close, preventing backflow into the atria [1]. The semilunar valves open and blood is pushed into the aorta and the pulmonary artery [1]. When the ventricles relax, the semilunar valves close, preventing backflow from the arteries into the ventricles [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each mark pairs a valve with its action and its effect. Naming the valves in the wrong positions loses every valve mark, so learn where each kind sits.

**12. (a)** Less blood / no blood flows to that part of the heart muscle [1]; it receives less oxygen and glucose [1]; so the cells cannot respire (aerobically) and release enough energy [1].

**(b)** Genetic predisposition [1]; age [1]; sex [1].

**(c)** A diet low in saturated fat and cholesterol reduces the build-up of fatty deposits in the arteries [1]; less salt in the diet helps to lower blood pressure [1]; regular exercise helps control body mass, lowers blood pressure and strengthens the heart [1]; but diet and exercise cannot change genetic predisposition, age or sex, so the risk is reduced, not removed [1].

*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer needs a balancing point; four benefits with no limitation usually caps the mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining arteries by oxygen content instead of direction.
- Saying the heart sounds come from muscle contracting.
- Converting pulse or ECG counts to beats per minute with the wrong multiplier.
- Vague controlled variables such as "same conditions".
- Explaining activity and heart rate with "more energy needed" and no mention of oxygen, glucose or carbon dioxide.
- Placing the semilunar valves between atria and ventricles.
