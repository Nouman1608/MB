## Questions

**1.** Skerra Foods sells £96,000 of pies a year and buys in £61,500 of ingredients and packaging.

**(a)** Define added value. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Skerra Foods' added value. **[2]**

**2.** Kestlecombe Joinery makes 4,500 units a month with 15 workers. After training and a new machine it makes 5,400 units with 16 workers. Calculate labour productivity before and after, and the percentage change. **[4]**

**3.** A Kestlecombe workshop can make a maximum of 4,800 units a month and currently makes 3,600.

**(a)** Calculate capacity utilisation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate it if output rises to 4,200. **[1]**
**(c)** Analyse one risk of running at the higher level. **[2]**

**4.** Skerra Foods holds flour with a buffer stock of 150 bags, usage of 40 bags a day, a lead time of 3 days and a reorder quantity of 500 bags.

**(a)** Calculate the reorder level. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum stock level. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one drawback of moving to just in time (JIT). **[2]**

**5.** A project has activities P (2 days, no predecessor), Q (5, after P), R (3, after P), S (4, after Q) and T (2, after R and S).

**(a)** Identify the critical path and the project duration. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total float of R. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the free float of R. **[1]**

**6.** An activity has an optimistic time of 2 days, a most likely time of 6 days and a pessimistic time of 16 days.

**(a)** Calculate the expected time. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one use and one limit of PERT. **[2]**

**7.** Kestlecombe Joinery scraps 90 of 1,800 units input. After introducing total quality management (TQM) it scraps 54 of 1,800.

**(a)** Distinguish between quality control and quality assurance. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wastage rate before and after. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how training can improve quality. **[2]**

**8.** Kestlecombe Joinery makes made-to-order kitchens by job production. It is considering batch production of standard cabinets. Evaluate whether it should change. **[6]**

**9.** Skerra Foods packs 500,000 pies a year at £1.10 a pack in the UK. An overseas supplier quotes £0.80.

**(a)** Define offshoring and re-shoring. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual saving. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one non-financial risk of offshoring. **[2]**

**10.** Oxley Pike Cycles has high stock costs, frequent late orders and a skilled, stable workforce. Its suppliers are within 20 miles. Recommend and justify a lean production method. **[6]**

**11.** Skerra Foods is choosing a site for a distribution centre. Evaluate the importance of location to the business and its stakeholders. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Added value is selling price minus the cost of bought-in materials and services [1]; it is before overheads, so it is not profit [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 96,000 - 61,500 [1] = **£34,500** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit for stating that overheads are still to be paid; equating added value with profit is a common slip.

**2.** Before: 4,500 / 15 = 300 units per worker [1]. After: 5,400 / 16 = 337.5 [1]. Change 37.5 [1]; 37.5 / 300 x 100 = **12.5%** increase [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both productivity figures before the percentage; a bare percentage leaves nothing to reward if it is wrong.

**3. (a)** 3,600 / 4,800 [1] x 100 = **75%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 4,200 / 4,800 x 100 = **87.5%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** At 87.5% there is little spare capacity [1], so a breakdown or demand surge could cause late orders and lost customers [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" asks for a consequence, not just a statement that capacity is nearly full.

**4. (a)** 150 + (40 x 3) [1] = **270 bags** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 270 + 500 - 120 [1] = **650 bags** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** JIT holds almost no stock [1], so one late delivery from a supplier halts production [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A drawback gains more credit when linked to the consequence for the business, not only named.

**5. (a)** Forward pass gives ESTs P 0, Q 2, R 2, S 7, T 11 [1]; critical path P, Q, S, T [1]; duration 11 + 2 = **13 days** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** LFT of R = 13 - 2 = 11 [1]; total float = 11 - 2 - 3 = **6 days** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Free float = 11 - 2 - 3 = **6 days** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the forward and backward passes; a wrong final figure can still earn credit for correct working shown.

**6. (a)** (2 + 4 x 6 + 16) / 6 [1] = **7 days** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Use: it allows for uncertainty by weighting three estimates [1]. Limit: the estimates may be inaccurate or biased [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State one use and one limit separately; two uses would earn no credit for the limit.

**7. (a)** Quality control inspects output to find defects [1]; quality assurance prevents defects by managing the whole process [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Before 90 / 1,800 x 100 = **5%** [1]; after 54 / 1,800 x 100 = **3%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Trained staff understand the standards and make fewer errors [1], reducing waste and rework [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link training to a result for the business, such as lower waste, to move beyond a simple assertion.

**8.** Job production gives flexibility and high prices but high unit cost [1]. Batch production for standard cabinets spreads set-up time and lowers unit cost [1], and customers may pay less and receive faster delivery [1]. But the firm needs stock and storage space, and risks unsold cabinets if demand is weak [1]; skilled workers may find repeated tasks less motivating [1]. Judgement: a mixed approach, batch for standard lines and job for bespoke kitchens, is likely best if enough standard demand exists, because it keeps the premium brand while cutting cost [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong evaluations weigh unit cost against flexibility for named stakeholders and then commit to a conditional judgement.

**9. (a)** Offshoring moves activities to another country [1]; re-shoring brings them back to the home country [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 500,000 x (1.10 - 0.80) [1] = **£150,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Longer supply lines risk late delivery or poorer quality control [1], which could damage reputation with UK customers [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Non-financial" means do not answer with cost; name a risk and its effect.

**10.** Recommend just in time (JIT) [1], because stock costs are high and JIT cuts stock held [1]. Suppliers within 20 miles make fast, reliable deliveries likely [1]. A skilled, stable workforce also suits kaizen, but JIT addresses the stated problem more directly [1]. Risk: any supplier failure stops production, so agree delivery terms and keep a small buffer [1]. Overall JIT is justified because its main condition, reliable nearby supply, is met [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Justification gains credit when the choice is tied to the case facts and the main alternative is considered.

**11.** Location affects cost, speed and customer service [1]. Near motorways and customers cuts delivery time and fuel cost [1]; cheaper land away from cities lowers rent, though staff may be harder to recruit [1]. Stakeholders: customers gain faster delivery, employees gain or lose commuting convenience, the local community gains jobs but may face traffic [1]. A weakness is that modern digital tracking reduces some location needs [1]. Judgement: location is very important for a distribution centre because transport is its main cost, so access should outweigh rent [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover both the business and at least one other stakeholder, then reach a clear judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating added value as profit.
- Omitting x 100 when asked for a percentage.
- Forgetting to subtract usage during lead time for maximum stock.
- Calculating float without subtracting duration.
- Writing a one-sided evaluation with no judgement.
- Giving generic benefits with no use of case data.
- Confusing quality control with quality assurance.
- Naming a risk without explaining its effect.
