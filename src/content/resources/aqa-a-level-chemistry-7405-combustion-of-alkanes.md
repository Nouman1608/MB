## Alkanes as fuels

Alkanes burn in oxygen with a large release of energy, so combustion is strongly exothermic. They are easily transported, and their products when burned completely are carbon dioxide and water. Alkanes from the lower-boiling fractions are volatile and easy to ignite, which suits engines.

## Complete combustion

In a plentiful supply of oxygen, a hydrocarbon burns completely to carbon dioxide and water. General equation:

```
CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂ → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O
```

### Balancing method

1. Balance carbon first: one CO₂ for each carbon.
2. Balance hydrogen next: one H₂O for each two hydrogens.
3. Count oxygen atoms on the right and halve the total to find the O₂ needed. Use a fraction, then double everything if you want whole numbers.

| Fuel | Equation (whole numbers) |
|---|---|
| Methane | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| Propane | C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| Butane | 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O |
| Octane | 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O |

## Incomplete combustion

When the supply of oxygen is limited, carbon monoxide (CO) and/or carbon (soot) forms instead of carbon dioxide. Water is still produced.

```
CH₄ + 1½O₂ → CO + 2H₂O          (carbon monoxide)
CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O             (carbon, soot)
2C₈H₁₈ + 17O₂ → 16CO + 18H₂O
```

Incomplete combustion releases less energy than complete combustion. Carbon monoxide is poisonous: it binds to haemoglobin in blood, stopping it from carrying oxygen. It is colourless and odourless, so faulty gas appliances are dangerous. Soot appears as a yellow, smoky flame and deposits carbon on surfaces.

### Worked example 1: write the equation

Write the equation for the incomplete combustion of propane to carbon monoxide and water.

- C₃H₈ + ?O₂ → 3CO + 4H₂O.
- Oxygen atoms on the right: 3 + 4 = 7, so 3½O₂ are needed.
- C₃H₈ + 3½O₂ → 3CO + 4H₂O, or 2C₃H₈ + 7O₂ → 6CO + 8H₂O.

## Pollutants from internal combustion engines

| Pollutant | How it forms | Harm |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen oxides, NOₓ (NO, NO₂) | Nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperature and spark in the engine: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO, then 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ | Cause acid rain, photochemical smog and respiratory problems |
| Carbon monoxide, CO | Incomplete combustion | Toxic |
| Carbon (particulates, soot) | Incomplete combustion | Respiratory problems, dirt, global dimming |
| Unburned hydrocarbons | Fuel that does not burn fully | Contribute to smog; some are carcinogenic |

Note that the nitrogen oxides do **not** come from the fuel. They come from the nitrogen in the air, which reacts only at very high temperature.

## Catalytic converters

A catalytic converter in a car's exhaust contains a **honeycomb** of ceramic coated with thin layers of platinum, palladium and rhodium (a support medium, which gives a large surface area). Hot exhaust gases pass over the surface, where reactions take place:

```
2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂
2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂
C₈H₁₈ + 25NO → 8CO₂ + 9H₂O + 12½N₂
```

The first and third reactions remove NO and CO (or unburned hydrocarbons) together, producing harmless nitrogen, carbon dioxide and water. They work at high temperature, so they are inefficient when the engine is cold. Leaded petrol must not be used, because lead poisons the catalyst.

### Worked example 2: a catalytic converter equation

Write an equation for the removal of carbon monoxide and nitrogen monoxide.

- CO + NO → CO₂ + N₂ needs balancing.
- Carbon monoxide is oxidised and nitrogen monoxide is reduced.
- 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ (oxygen: 2 + 2 = 4 on each side; nitrogen: 2 = 2).

## Sulfur dioxide

Fuels such as coal and some crude oil fractions contain sulfur compounds. When they burn, the sulfur forms sulfur dioxide:

```
S + O₂ → SO₂
```

Sulfur dioxide is a toxic, acidic gas. It dissolves in rain water to give acidic solutions (sulfurous acid, then sulfuric acid after oxidation), which cause **acid rain**: damage to buildings, trees and aquatic life.

### Removing SO₂ from flue gases

Power stations remove SO₂ from their waste (flue) gases before releasing them, using calcium oxide or calcium carbonate, often as a wet slurry in water. Both react with the acidic gas.

```
CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃
CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂
```

The calcium sulfite can be oxidised by air to calcium sulfate (gypsum), which is sold for plasterboard.

```
2CaSO₃ + O₂ → 2CaSO₄
```

**Why it works:** sulfur dioxide is an acidic oxide. Calcium oxide is a basic oxide, and calcium carbonate is a base that reacts with acids, so they neutralise the SO₂ and trap it as a solid. Both are cheap, and limestone (calcium carbonate) is abundant.

## Calculations

### Worked example 3: volume of gases

20.0 g of propane (Mᵣ = 44.0) burns completely. Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed and the volume of oxygen needed at room temperature and pressure (molar volume 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹).

```
n(C₃H₈) = 20.0 ÷ 44.0 = 0.4545 mol
C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
V(CO₂) = 3 × 0.4545 × 24.0 = 32.7 dm³
V(O₂) = 5 × 0.4545 × 24.0 = 54.5 dm³
```

### Worked example 4: mass of carbon dioxide

Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide produced by burning 1.00 kg of octane (Mᵣ = 114.0).

```
n(C₈H₁₈) = 1000 ÷ 114.0 = 8.77 mol
n(CO₂) = 8 × 8.77 = 70.2 mol
mass = 70.2 × 44.0 = 3090 g = 3.09 kg
```

### Worked example 5: limestone for desulfurisation

A power station produces 3.20 kg of SO₂ (Mᵣ = 64.1). Calculate the mass of calcium carbonate (Mᵣ = 100.1) needed to remove it.

```
n(SO₂) = 3200 ÷ 64.1 = 49.9 mol
n(CaCO₃) = 49.9 mol (1 : 1)
mass = 49.9 × 100.1 = 4997 g = 5.00 kg
```

## Common mistakes

- Missing water from the products of incomplete combustion.
- Balancing O₂ without checking oxygen atoms on both sides.
- Saying NOₓ comes from the fuel. It comes from nitrogen in the air.
- Describing carbon monoxide as harmless. It is toxic because it binds to haemoglobin.
- Forgetting that the catalytic converter uses precious metals on a support, and that lead poisons it.
- Saying calcium oxide removes SO₂ because it is a catalyst. It reacts with it, as a base.
- Writing CaSO₄ in the first reaction. The first product is calcium sulfite, CaSO₃.
- Mixing up "global warming" (CO₂) with "acid rain" (SO₂, NOₓ).

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Write an equation**: balance carbon, then hydrogen, then oxygen; include state symbols if asked.
- **Explain how NOₓ forms**: high temperature and spark let nitrogen and oxygen from the air react.
- **Explain why SO₂ can be removed with calcium carbonate**: acid–base reaction.
- **Discuss pollutants**: for each, give the cause and the harm.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does incomplete combustion occur?**
Because there is not enough oxygen for the carbon to be fully oxidised to carbon dioxide, such as in a poorly adjusted gas fire or a crowded engine cylinder.

**Does a catalytic converter remove carbon dioxide?**
No. It converts CO and NO into CO₂ and N₂, so carbon dioxide is produced. It does not reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

**Why is limestone used rather than calcium oxide?**
Limestone is cheaper and easily mined. Calcium oxide has to be made from limestone by heating, which costs energy.
