These questions cover sub-topic (g) Acids, bases and salt preparations (points 2.34 to 2.43C) of Topic 2 in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). Questions marked **(Paper 2 only)** test points 2.40C, 2.41C or 2.43C. A calculator may be used. Revise first with the [acids, bases and salt preparations study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-bases-and-salt-preparations/) or the [acids, bases and salt preparations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-bases-and-salt-preparations-revision-notes/). Questions on indicators and titres are in the [Groups 1 and 7, acids, salts and tests practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-inorganic-groups-acids-salts-tests-practice/).

Relative atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Cl = 35.5, Zn = 65, Ba = 137, S = 32. One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ at rtp.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name **one** base that is an alkali and **one** base that is not an alkali. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each compound as soluble or insoluble in water: calcium sulfate, sodium hydroxide, lead(II) chloride, iron(III) nitrate. **[2]**

**3.** Give the formula of the salt formed when:

**(a)** magnesium hydroxide reacts with dilute nitric acid **[1]**
**(b)** ammonia reacts with dilute sulfuric acid **[1]**

**4.** The equation shows the reaction of carbonate ions with an acid.

`CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂`

Identify the base in this reaction and explain your answer in terms of protons. **[2]**

**5.** A student plans to make sodium chloride crystals by adding excess sodium hydroxide to dilute hydrochloric acid and filtering off the excess. Explain why this plan will not work. **[2]**

**6.** Magnesium carbonate precipitates when magnesium chloride solution is mixed with sodium carbonate solution. Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for this reaction. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**7.** A student makes zinc chloride from zinc carbonate and dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation, with state symbols. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** observations that show all the acid has reacted. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the mixture is filtered before the solution is heated. **[1]**
**(d)** The student uses 2.50 g of zinc carbonate, which all reacts. Calculate the maximum mass of zinc chloride formed and the volume of carbon dioxide given off at rtp, in cm³. **[3]**

**8.** **(Paper 2 only)** Ammonium nitrate is made from ammonia solution and dilute nitric acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why ammonium nitrate cannot be made by adding an excess of a solid reactant to nitric acid and filtering. **[2]**
**(c)** A titration with indicator shows the volume of acid needed for 25.0 cm³ of ammonia solution. Describe the steps that follow to obtain pure, dry crystals of ammonium nitrate. **[4]**

**9.** **(Paper 2 only)** A student wants a pure, dry sample of copper(II) carbonate.

**(a)** Suggest **two** solutions the student could mix. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation, with state symbols, for your chosen reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the student obtains a pure, dry sample from the mixture. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why distilled water, not tap water, is used in this method. **[1]**

**10.** For each salt, state the method of preparation (excess insoluble solid, titration, or precipitation) and name suitable reactants.

**(a)** silver chloride **[1]**
**(b)** magnesium sulfate **[1]**
**(c)** sodium sulfate **[1]**
**(d)** calcium carbonate **[1]**

**11.** A student makes hydrated copper(II) sulfate from copper(II) oxide and dilute sulfuric acid. The student heats the blue solution until all the water has gone and obtains a white powder with some black specks in it.

**(a)** Explain why the product is a white powder, not blue crystals. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the black specks and state how the method should be changed to avoid them. **[2]**

**12.** **(Paper 2 only)** A student dissolves 2.08 g of barium chloride, BaCl₂, in water and adds excess sodium sulfate solution. The barium sulfate precipitate is filtered, washed and dried. Its mass is 2.10 g.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of barium sulfate, BaSO₄. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of barium sulfate that could form. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest **one** reason why the yield is less than 100%. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Alkali: sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide or ammonia solution [1]. Not an alkali: an insoluble base such as copper(II) oxide or magnesium hydroxide [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A metal such as zinc is not a base, so it scores nothing for the second mark.

**2.** Calcium sulfate insoluble; sodium hydroxide soluble; lead(II) chloride insoluble; iron(III) nitrate soluble. Any two correct [1]; all four correct [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Calcium sulfate and lead(II) chloride are both listed exceptions. "Calcium hydroxide is slightly soluble" does not make calcium sulfate soluble.

**3. (a)** Mg(NO₃)₂ [1].
**(b)** (NH₄)₂SO₄ [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Brackets are needed whenever there is more than one polyatomic ion. MgNO₃ has unbalanced charges.

**4.** The carbonate ion is the base [1] because it accepts protons (H⁺ ions) from the acid [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "It accepts hydrogen" is not accepted; the particle accepted is a proton, or H⁺.

**5.** Sodium hydroxide is soluble, so the excess dissolves and cannot be filtered off [1]. The salt would be contaminated with sodium hydroxide; the exact volumes must be found by titration instead [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The key word is "soluble". Answers that only say "it is an alkali" do not explain why filtering fails.

**6.** `Mg²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → MgCO₃(s)`: correct formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Sodium and chloride ions are spectators and must not appear in an ionic equation.

**7. (a)** `ZnCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)`: formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1].
**(b)** Fizzing stops [1]; solid zinc carbonate remains undissolved [1].
**(c)** To remove the unreacted (excess) zinc carbonate [1].
**(d)** Moles ZnCO₃ = 2.50 ÷ 125 = 0.0200 mol [1]. Mass ZnCl₂ = 0.0200 × 136 = **2.72 g** [1]. Volume CO₂ = 0.0200 × 24 000 = **480 cm³** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) the answer asks for cm³. Writing 0.48 with no unit, or 0.48 cm³, loses the final mark.

**8. (a)** `NH₃(aq) + HNO₃(aq) → NH₄NO₃(aq)` [1].
**(b)** Both reactants are soluble [1], so there is no insoluble reactant whose excess could be filtered off; any excess would contaminate the salt [1].
**(c)** Mix 25.0 cm³ of ammonia solution with exactly the volume of acid found [1] without any indicator [1]. Heat the solution to evaporate some water until it is saturated, then leave it to cool and crystallise [1]. Filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The repeat without indicator is the step most often left out. Heating to dryness instead of crystallising also loses the third mark.

**9. (a)** A soluble copper(II) salt, such as copper(II) sulfate or copper(II) nitrate solution [1], and a soluble carbonate, such as sodium carbonate or potassium carbonate solution [1].
**(b)** For example `CuSO₄(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) → CuCO₃(s) + Na₂SO₄(aq)`: formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols, with (s) for the precipitate [1].
**(c)** Filter to collect the precipitate [1]. Wash it on the filter paper with distilled water [1]. Dry it in a warm oven or between filter papers [1].
**(d)** Tap water contains dissolved ions (salts) that would remain on the solid and make it impure [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Copper(II) oxide or copper metal cannot be used: neither dissolves in water, so neither can supply copper ions to a precipitation.

**10. (a)** Precipitation, e.g. silver nitrate solution and sodium chloride solution [1].
**(b)** Excess insoluble solid, e.g. magnesium oxide or magnesium carbonate added to dilute sulfuric acid [1].
**(c)** Titration of sodium hydroxide solution with dilute sulfuric acid [1].
**(d)** Precipitation, e.g. calcium nitrate (or calcium chloride) solution and sodium carbonate solution [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs both the method and workable reactants. Magnesium metal is also acceptable in (b), since magnesium reacts with dilute sulfuric acid.

**11. (a)** Heating to dryness drove off the water of crystallisation [1], leaving white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate instead of blue hydrated crystals [1].
**(b)** The black specks are unreacted copper(II) oxide that was not removed [1]. Filter the mixture to remove the excess oxide before heating the solution [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), say what was lost (water of crystallisation), not just "it was overheated".

**12. (a)** 137 + 32 + (4 × 16) = **233** [1].
**(b)** Moles BaCl₂ = 2.08 ÷ 208 = 0.0100 mol [1]. 1 : 1 ratio, so mass BaSO₄ = 0.0100 × 233 = **2.33 g** [1].
**(c)** 2.10 ÷ 2.33 × 100 = **90.1%** [1].
**(d)** Some precipitate stayed on the filter paper or beaker, or passed through the filter paper, or was lost during washing [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Mr of BaCl₂ is 137 + 71 = 208. A follow-through mark is usually given in (c) for a correct percentage from a wrong (b).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "hydrogen" instead of "proton" or "H⁺" in proton-transfer answers.
- Naming an insoluble reactant for a precipitation.
- Leaving out filtering of the excess solid, or the no-indicator repeat in a titration preparation.
- Keeping the wrong part after filtering: the filtrate holds a soluble salt, the residue is the precipitate.
- Unbalanced charges in salt formulae.
- Missing units or wrong units in gas-volume answers.
