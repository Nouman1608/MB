## Questions

**1.** State Hooke's law in full, including the necessary qualifier. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between the limit of proportionality and the elastic limit, and state which comes first. **[3]**

**3.** Two springs each of spring constant 30 N m⁻¹ are joined.

**(a)** Calculate the effective spring constant in **series**. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate it in **parallel**. **[2]**

**4.** A wire of length 3.20 m and diameter 0.50 mm stretches 4.8 mm under a load of 68 N.

**(a)** Calculate the cross-sectional area. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the stress. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the strain. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the Young modulus. **[2]**
**(e)** State, with a reason, the Young modulus of a wire of the same material but half the length. **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the elastic strain energy stored in the wire in question 4. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the energy stored is the **area under** the force–extension graph, and state when the formula ½Fx cannot be used. **[3]**

**7.** Classify each as brittle, ductile or polymeric, giving a reason: glass, copper, rubber. **[3]**

---

## Answers

**1.** The **extension is directly proportional to the applied force** [1], **up to the limit of proportionality** [1].

**2.** The **limit of proportionality** is where the force–extension graph **ceases to be a straight line** [1]. The **elastic limit** is where the material **no longer returns to its original shape** when unloaded [1]. The limit of proportionality comes **first** [1].

**3. (a)** In series: 1/k = 1/30 + 1/30 = 1/15 [1], so k = **15 N m⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** In parallel: k = 30 + 30 = **60 N m⁻¹** [1] [1].

**4. (a)** r = 0.25 mm = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m [1]
A = πr² = **1.963 × 10⁻⁷ m²** [1].
**(b)** σ = 68 ÷ 1.963 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = **3.46 × 10⁸ Pa** [1].
**(c)** ε = 4.8 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3.20 [1] = **1.50 × 10⁻³** [1].
**(d)** E = 3.46 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.50 × 10⁻³ [1] = **2.31 × 10¹¹ Pa** [1].
**(e)** **The same** [1], because the Young modulus is a property of the **material**, not of the specimen's dimensions [1].

**5.** E = ½Fx = 0.5 × 68 × 4.8 × 10⁻³ [1] = **0.163 J** [1].

**6.** Work done = force × distance, and since the force **varies as the extension increases** [1], the total work is the **sum of many small contributions** — the area under the graph [1]. The formula ½Fx applies only where the graph is a **straight line**; for a non-linear graph the area must be found by counting squares or by the trapezium rule [1].

**7. Glass** — **brittle**; it fractures at the elastic limit with **no plastic deformation** [1].
**Copper** — **ductile**; it undergoes **large plastic deformation** and can be drawn into wire [1].
**Rubber** — **polymeric**; it shows very large extensions and its **loading and unloading curves differ** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting "up to the limit of proportionality".
- Putting the elastic limit before the limit of proportionality.
- Combining springs like resistors the wrong way round.
- Using the diameter rather than the radius for area.
