These questions cover section **3.1.6.1 Chemical equilibria and Le Chatelier's principle** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Chemical equilibria study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chemical-equilibria-and-le-chateliers-principle/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chemical-equilibria-and-le-chateliers-principle-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by a dynamic equilibrium. **[3]**

**2.** State Le Chatelier's principle. **[1]**

**3.** Sulfur trioxide is made by the reaction 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −98 kJ mol⁻¹. State and explain the effect on the position of equilibrium of:

**(a)** increasing the pressure **[2]**

**(b)** increasing the temperature **[2]**

**(c)** adding a catalyst **[2]**

**(d)** removing SO₃ as it forms **[1]**

**4.** For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH = +58 kJ mol⁻¹, N₂O₄ is colourless and NO₂ is brown. State the colour change when the temperature of an equilibrium mixture is increased. Explain. **[3]**

**5.** For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), explain why changing the pressure does not change the position of equilibrium. **[2]**

**6.** Methanol is made by CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g), ΔH = −91 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict the conditions of temperature and pressure that give the highest equilibrium yield, with reasons. **[4]**

**7.** Explain why the Haber process is operated at a compromise temperature of about 450 °C rather than at a much lower temperature. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why very high pressures are not used in industrial processes such as the Haber process even though they increase the yield. **[2]**

**9.** In the cobalt chloride equilibrium [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 4Cl⁻(aq) ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻(aq) + 6H₂O(l), the pink complex becomes blue on adding concentrated hydrochloric acid. Explain the colour change. **[2]**

**10.** A student states that "at equilibrium the reaction has stopped". Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

**11.** A student says that a catalyst allows a greater yield of product in a reversible reaction. Explain why this is incorrect, and state the benefit of a catalyst. **[3]**

**12.** The equilibrium 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g) is exothermic in the forward direction. An equilibrium mixture in a syringe is compressed to half its volume at constant temperature. State and explain the effect on the amount of N₂O₄. **[3]**

**13.** Hydrogen is made by CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g), ΔH = +206 kJ mol⁻¹. State and explain the effect on the equilibrium yield of hydrogen of:

**(a)** increasing the temperature **[2]**

**(b)** increasing the pressure **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The forward and reverse reactions are both occurring [1]; at the same rate [1]; so the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant in a closed system [1]. **[3]**

**2.** When a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** The position shifts to the right [1]; there are 2 moles of gas on the right and 3 on the left, so the shift reduces the pressure [1]

**(b)** The position shifts to the left [1]; the forward reaction is exothermic, so the reverse (endothermic) reaction absorbs the added heat [1]

**(c)** No effect on the position [1]; the catalyst increases the rates of the forward and reverse reactions equally [1]

**(d)** The position shifts to the right to replace the SO₃ removed [1]. **[7]**

**4.** The mixture becomes browner (darker) [1]; the forward reaction is endothermic, so the position shifts to the right to absorb the added heat [1]; more NO₂, which is brown, is formed [1]. **[3]**

**5.** There are 2 moles of gas on each side of the equation [1]; a shift in either direction would not change the pressure, so the position does not shift [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Low temperature [1], because the forward reaction is exothermic [1]; high pressure [1], because there are fewer moles of gas on the right (1 against 3) [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The forward reaction is exothermic, so a low temperature gives a higher yield [1]; but at a low temperature the rate of reaction is too slow [1]; 450 °C is a compromise that gives an acceptable rate and an acceptable yield [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Very high pressure needs thicker, stronger and more expensive equipment, and more energy to compress the gas [1]; there is also a greater safety risk [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Adding concentrated HCl increases the concentration of chloride ions [1]; the position shifts to the right (opposing the change), forming the blue [CoCl₄]²⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The statement is incorrect [1]; the forward and reverse reactions are still occurring at equal rates (dynamic equilibrium), so there is no net change in concentrations [1]. **[2]**

**11.** A catalyst increases the rates of the forward and reverse reactions equally [1]; so the position of equilibrium and the equilibrium yield are unchanged [1]; the benefit is that equilibrium is reached sooner, or that a lower temperature can be used for the same rate [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The amount of N₂O₄ increases [1]; there are fewer moles of gas on the right (1 against 2) [1]; so the position shifts to the right to reduce the pressure [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Immediately after compression the mixture looks darker because the concentration of NO₂ is higher. It then lightens as the position shifts to the right.

**13. (a)** The yield increases [1]; the forward reaction is endothermic, so the position shifts to the right to absorb the added heat [1]

**(b)** The yield decreases [1]; there are 4 moles of gas on the right and 2 on the left, so the position shifts to the left to reduce the pressure [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that reactions stop at equilibrium.
- Leaving out "closed system" or "equal rates".
- Saying that a catalyst changes the yield.
- Giving the effect of pressure without counting moles of gas.
- Ignoring the rate when discussing the compromise.
