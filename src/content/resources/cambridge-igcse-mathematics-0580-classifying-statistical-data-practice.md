These questions cover section 9.1, Classifying statistical data, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. C9.1 and E9.1 are identical, so every question suits both Core and Extended candidates. All of them can be done without a calculator. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [classifying statistical data study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-classifying-statistical-data/) and the [classifying data revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-classifying-statistical-data-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State the type of data for each variable. Use qualitative, discrete or continuous.

**(a)** The number of goals scored in a hockey match. **[1]**

**(b)** The mass of an apple. **[1]**

**(c)** A student's favourite subject. **[1]**

**(d)** The length of a song. **[1]**

**2.** Which one of these variables is continuous?

A: the number of pets in a house

B: the time taken to boil a kettle

C: a person's shoe size

D: the amount of pocket money a child receives

**[1]**

**3.** Ana records the heights of some seedlings, in centimetres, using the classes 0–5, 5–10 and 10–15.

**(a)** Explain the problem with these classes for a seedling of height 5 cm. **[1]**

**(b)** Rewrite the three classes so that the problem is removed. **[1]**

**4.** A tally table shows the favourite snack of some children.

```
Snack      Tally
Fruit      ||||/ ||
Crisps     ||||/ ||||/ |
Biscuits   ||||
```

**(a)** Write down the frequency for each snack. **[2]**

**(b)** How many children are in the survey? **[1]**

**5.** Whole-number data are grouped in the classes 1–9, 10–19 and 20–29. Write down the class that contains each of the values 9, 10, 19 and 20. **[2]**

**6.** A form asks for a person's age in completed years, such as 15. Explain why age is still continuous data. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** The number of goals scored in 25 football matches is:

```
2  0  1  3  1  4  2  2  0  5  1  3  2
1  0  2  3  1  4  2  1  0  2  3  1
```

**(a)** Draw a tally table for the data and find the frequency for each number of goals. **[3]**

**(b)** What fraction of the matches had no goals? **[1]**

**(c)** How many matches had more than 2 goals? **[1]**

**8.** The masses, in kilograms, of 16 parcels are:

```
0.8  2.4  1.0  3.6  2.0  0.5  1.9  3.0
2.7  1.2  0.9  2.2  3.9  1.5  2.0  1.0
```

**(a)** Complete a frequency table using the classes 0 ≤ m < 1, 1 ≤ m < 2, 2 ≤ m < 3 and 3 ≤ m < 4. **[3]**

**(b)** How many parcels have a mass of at least 2 kg? **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the frequency table cannot tell you how many parcels have a mass of exactly 2.0 kg. **[1]**

**9.** At a holiday club, each of 16 children chose one activity. Each record shows the session, morning (M) or afternoon (A), and the activity, painting (P) or drama (D).

```
MP  MD  AP  AD  MP  MP  AD  AP
MD  MP  AP  AD  AD  MP  AP  MD
```

**(a)** Complete a two-way table showing session and activity, including all totals. **[3]**

**(b)** What fraction of the children who chose painting came in the morning? **[1]**

**(c)** What percentage of the afternoon children chose drama? **[1]**

**10.** 200 students in Years 10 and 11 each study one language: French, Spanish or Mandarin.

- 110 of the students are in Year 10.
- 30% of the Year 10 students study French.
- 1/3 of the Year 11 students study Mandarin.
- 25 Year 11 students study French.
- 52 students in total study Mandarin.

**(a)** Complete a two-way table for this information, including all totals. **[4]**

**(b)** What fraction of the students who study Spanish are in Year 11? Give your answer in its simplest form. **[1]**

**(c)** What percentage of all 200 students are Year 10 students who study Mandarin? **[1]**

**11.** A car park holds 90 cars. Each car is petrol or electric, and each is white, black or another colour.

- The ratio of electric cars to petrol cars is 1 : 5.
- 2/5 of the electric cars are white.
- 30 cars are black, and 4 of these are electric.
- 33 cars are white.

**(a)** Complete a two-way table with fuel type in the rows and colour in the columns, including all totals. **[4]**

**(b)** What fraction of the black cars are electric? Give your answer in its simplest form. **[1]**

**12.** The masses of 40 dogs range from 3.2 kg to 27.8 kg.

**(a)** Write down five classes of equal width that could be used to group the masses. Use inequalities. **[2]**

**(b)** A student uses "20 kg or more" as the last class. Give one disadvantage of this class. **[1]**

**(c)** After tallying, the student's frequencies add up to 38. Explain what this shows. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Discrete** (goals are counted) [1]

**(b)** **Continuous** (mass is measured) [1]

**(c)** **Qualitative** [1]

**(d)** **Continuous** (time is measured) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "quantitative" alone is not enough when the question offers discrete and continuous as choices.

**2.** **B** [1]

*Examiner insight:* shoe size and money both change in fixed steps, so they are discrete even though they are not whole numbers of "things".

**3. (a)** 5 is the end of the first class **and** the start of the second, so it could go in either class (the classes overlap) [1]

**(b)** **0 ≤ h < 5, 5 ≤ h < 10, 10 ≤ h < 15** (or 0 < h ≤ 5, 5 < h ≤ 10, 10 < h ≤ 15) [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** Fruit **7**, crisps **11** [1]; biscuits **4** [1]

**(b)** 7 + 11 + 4 = **22** children [1] **[3]**

**5.** 9 is in **1–9** and 10 is in **10–19** [1]; 19 is in **10–19** and 20 is in **20–29** [1]

**6.** Age is time since birth, which is **measured**; giving it in completed years only rounds it down, so it can take any value in a range [1]

**7. (a)** Tallies drawn for each value [1]; frequencies 0 goals: **4**, 1 goal: **7**, 2 goals: **7** [1]; 3 goals: **4**, 4 goals: **2**, 5 goals: **1** [1]

**(b)** **4/25** [1]

**(c)** 4 + 2 + 1 = **7** matches [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the frequencies must add to 25; if your total is 24 or 26, go through the list again before moving on.

**8. (a)** 0 ≤ m < 1: **3** [1]; 1 ≤ m < 2: **5** and 2 ≤ m < 3: **5** [1]; 3 ≤ m < 4: **3** [1]

**(b)** 5 + 3 = **8** [1]

**(c)** Grouping puts 2.0 kg in the class 2 ≤ m < 3 with the other masses in that class, so the individual values are lost [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the values 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 sit on boundaries; each goes in the class that starts at that value, not the one that ends there.

**9. (a)**

```
             Painting   Drama   Total
Morning          5        3       8
Afternoon        4        4       8
Total            9        7      16
```

Morning row correct [1]; afternoon row correct [1]; all totals correct [1]

**(b)** **5/9** [1]

**(c)** 4/8 × 100 = **50%** [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** Year 11 = 200 − 110 = 90; Year 10 French = 30% of 110 = 33; Year 11 Mandarin = 1/3 of 90 = 30 [1]

Year 10 Mandarin = 52 − 30 = 22 [1]

Year 10 Spanish = 110 − 33 − 22 = 55; Year 11 Spanish = 90 − 25 − 30 = 35 [1]

```
            French   Spanish   Mandarin   Total
Year 10       33        55        22       110
Year 11       25        35        30        90
Total         58        90        52       200
```

All totals correct [1]

**(b)** 35/90 = **7/18** [1]

**(c)** 22/200 × 100 = **11%** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "30% of the Year 10 students" means 30% of 110, not of 200; using 200 gives 60, which cannot fit in a row of 110 with the other values.

**11. (a)** Electric = 90 ÷ 6 = 15, petrol = 75 [1]; white electric = 2/5 of 15 = 6 [1]

Electric other = 15 − 6 − 4 = 5; petrol white = 33 − 6 = 27; petrol black = 30 − 4 = 26 [1]

```
            White   Black   Other   Total
Electric       6       4       5      15
Petrol        27      26      22      75
Total         33      30      27      90
```

Petrol other = 75 − 27 − 26 = 22 and all totals correct [1]

**(b)** 4/30 = **2/15** [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** For example **0 ≤ m < 6, 6 ≤ m < 12, 12 ≤ m < 18, 18 ≤ m < 24, 24 ≤ m < 30**. Five classes of equal width with no gaps or overlaps [1]; the classes include both 3.2 and 27.8 [1]

**(b)** You cannot tell how heavy the dogs in that class are, because it has no upper limit; it also groups very different masses, such as 21 kg and 27 kg, together [1]

**(c)** **Two masses have been missed** (40 dogs but only 38 tallies), so the tally must be checked [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "numerical" or "quantitative" without saying discrete or continuous.
- Classes for continuous data that overlap at the ends, or leave gaps.
- Tally tables whose frequencies do not add up to the number of data items.
- Taking a percentage or fraction of the grand total instead of the group named.
- Leaving total cells blank when asked to complete a two-way table.
