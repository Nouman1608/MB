## Questions

**1.** State, with a reason, whether each compound shows cis-trans isomerism.

**(a)** 1,1-dichloroprop-1-ene, CCl₂=CHCH₃ **[1]**
**(b)** 4,4-dimethylpent-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHC(CH₃)₃ **[1]**
**(c)** 1,2-dibromocyclobutane **[1]**

**2.** 1-bromo-1-chloroethane, CH₃CHBrCl, exists as a pair of enantiomers.

**(a)** Identify the chiral carbon and explain why it is chiral. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw stereochemical formulas of the two enantiomers using wedge-dash bonds. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the two enantiomers differ in their effect on plane-polarized light. **[2]**
**(d)** A sample of 1-bromo-1-chloroethane has no effect on plane-polarized light. Explain this. **[2]**

**3.** Methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃, and ethyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₃, are isomers. Fragment data: CH₃⁺ 15, C₂H₅⁺ 29, CH₃O⁺ 31, CH₃CO⁺ 43, HCOO⁺ 45. Explain how their mass spectra would allow you to tell them apart. **[4]**

**4.** The mass spectrum of 1-phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃, has peaks at m/z 120, 105, 77 and 43. Identify the ions at 105, 77 and 43. **[3]**

**5.** Use these IR data: O–H (alcohol) 3200–3600 cm⁻¹; O–H (carboxylic acid) 2500–3000 cm⁻¹; C=O 1700–1750 cm⁻¹; C=C 1620–1680 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Compound A absorbs very broadly across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and strongly at 1720 cm⁻¹. Identify both bonds and the functional group. **[2]**
**(b)** Compound B absorbs broadly at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ and at 1650 cm⁻¹, with nothing at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹. Deduce two features of its structure. **[2]**

**6.** These parts are about greenhouse gases.

**(a)** State which vibrations of a CO₂ molecule absorb infrared radiation, and explain why the symmetric stretch does not. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why CH₄ is a greenhouse gas but N₂ is not. **[2]**

**7.** Predict the number of ¹H NMR signals, their area ratio and each signal's splitting.

**(a)** 2,2-dichlorobutane, CH₃CCl₂CH₂CH₃ **[3]**
**(b)** ethyl 2,2-dimethylpropanoate, (CH₃)₃CCOOCH₂CH₃ **[3]**

**8.** Ester X, C₅H₁₀O₂, gives four ¹H NMR signals: 1.1 ppm (triplet), 1.3 ppm (triplet), 2.3 ppm (quartet) and 4.1 ppm (quartet), with integration steps of 9.6, 9.6, 6.4 and 6.4 mm. Shifts: H on carbon next to C=O 2.0–2.7 ppm; H on carbon bonded to O 3.3–4.3 ppm.

**(a)** Deduce the ratio of hydrogen atoms in each environment. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify X. Explain how the signals support your answer and why X cannot be propyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃. **[4]**

**9.** Compound W contains 46.1% carbon, 7.8% hydrogen and 46.1% oxygen by mass. Relative atomic masses: C 12.01, H 1.01, O 16.00.

- MS: M⁺ at m/z 104; other peaks at 73 and 45.
- IR: very broad absorption across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and strong absorption at 1710 cm⁻¹.
- ¹H NMR: four signals: 2.6 ppm (triplet), 3.4 ppm (singlet), 3.7 ppm (triplet) and 11.5 ppm (singlet), with integration steps of 6.0, 9.0, 6.0 and 3.0 mm.

**(a)** Determine the empirical formula of W. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of W. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the bonds behind the two IR absorptions and the functional group they show. **[2]**
**(d)** Deduce the number of hydrogen atoms in each environment and explain the splitting. **[3]**
**(e)** Deduce the structure of W and identify the ions at m/z 73 and 45. **[2]**

**10.** An optically active liquid, V, has the molecular formula C₅H₁₀O.

**(a)** IR: strong absorption at 1730 cm⁻¹; no broad O–H band. State what this shows. **[2]**
**(b)** MS: M⁺ at m/z 86; large peaks at 57 and 29. Suggest an ion for each peak. **[2]**
**(c)** ¹H NMR includes a doublet of area 1 at 9.6 ppm. State what the shift and the splitting show. **[2]**
**(d)** V is one of: pentanal, 2-methylbutanal, 3-methylbutanal and 2,2-dimethylpropanal. Identify V, using (c) and its optical activity. **[2]**
**(e)** A sample of V made in the laboratory from non-chiral starting materials has no optical activity. Suggest why. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** No: carbon 1 carries two Cl atoms [1].
**(b)** Yes: carbon 2 has H and CH₃; carbon 3 has H and C(CH₃)₃ [1].
**(c)** Yes: carbons 1 and 2 each carry H and Br, and the ring stops rotation [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the groups on each carbon; a bare "yes" or "no" gives no reason.

**2. (a)** Carbon 1: it is bonded to four different groups, H, Br, Cl and CH₃ [1].
**(b)** The four groups arranged tetrahedrally round carbon 1, using plain lines in the page plus one tapered wedge and one hashed bond [1]; the reflection of that drawing beside it [1].
**(c)** Each rotates the plane of plane-polarized light [1] by the same angle but in opposite directions [1].
**(d)** It is a racemic mixture, with equal amounts of the two enantiomers [1]; their equal and opposite rotations cancel, so there is no net rotation [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), make the second drawing a true reflection; a turned copy of the first is the same enantiomer.

**3.** Both show M⁺ at m/z 74 [1]. Methyl ethanoate gives 43, CH₃CO⁺, by loss of OCH₃ (74 − 31) [1]. Ethyl methanoate gives 45, HCOO⁺, by loss of C₂H₅ (74 − 29) [1], and 29 from C₂H₅⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pair each m/z value with a positively charged formula; numbers alone explain nothing.

**4.** 105: C₆H₅CO⁺, from loss of CH₃ (120 − 15) [1]. 77: C₆H₅⁺ [1]. 43: CH₃CO⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write each fragment as a cation; C₆H₅CO without the + sign is incomplete.

**5. (a)** 2500–3000 cm⁻¹: O–H of a carboxylic acid; 1720 cm⁻¹: C=O [1]. Together they show a carboxyl group, so A is a carboxylic acid [1].
**(b)** Broad 3200–3600 cm⁻¹: an alcohol O–H group [1]. 1650 cm⁻¹ with nothing at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹: a C=C bond and no C=O, so B is an unsaturated alcohol [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bond (O–H) is not a functional group (carboxyl); give both when asked.

**6. (a)** The asymmetric stretch and the bends absorb [1]. In the symmetric stretch, the two C=O bond dipoles change equally and cancel, so the dipole moment does not change [1].
**(b)** CH₄ vibrations change its dipole moment, so it absorbs infrared emitted by Earth's surface and re-emits some back down [1]. N₂'s stretch never creates a dipole, so N₂ cannot absorb infrared [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link absorption to a change in dipole moment; CO₂ itself is non-polar.

**7. (a)** Three signals [1]; ratio 3 : 2 : 3 (CH₃ on carbon 2, CH₂, end CH₃) [1]; singlet (carbon 2 has no H), quartet, triplet respectively [1].
**(b)** Three signals [1]; ratio 9 : 2 : 3 ((CH₃)₃C, OCH₂, end CH₃) [1]; singlet, quartet, triplet respectively [1].
*Examiner insight:* A carbon with no hydrogen blocks splitting; counting hydrogens across it is a common slip.

**8. (a)** Dividing by 3.2: 9.6 : 9.6 : 6.4 : 6.4 = **3 : 3 : 2 : 2** [1].
**(b)** X is **ethyl propanoate**, CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ [1]. Two quartet (2H) plus triplet (3H) pairs show two ethyl groups [1]. The 2.3 ppm CH₂ is next to C=O and the 4.1 ppm CH₂ is bonded to O, so an ethyl sits on each side of the ester link [1]. Propyl ethanoate would show a 3H singlet near 2.0 ppm, and its middle CH₂ (five neighbouring H) would be neither quartet nor triplet [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say why the rejected isomer fails, not only why your choice fits.

**9. (a)** Moles in 100 g: C 46.1/12.01 = 3.84; H 7.8/1.01 = 7.72; O 46.1/16.00 = 2.88 [1]. Dividing by 2.88 gives 1.33 : 2.68 : 1; multiplying by 3 gives 4 : 8 : 3, so **C₄H₈O₃** [1].
**(b)** M(C₄H₈O₃) = 104, equal to M⁺, so the molecular formula is also **C₄H₈O₃** [1].
**(c)** 2500–3000 cm⁻¹: O–H; 1710 cm⁻¹: C=O [1]. Together they show a carboxyl group, so W is a carboxylic acid [1].
**(d)** Dividing by 3.0 gives 2 : 3 : 2 : 1, matching 8 H [1]. The 3.4 ppm singlet (3H) is CH₃ bonded to O with no neighbouring H; the 11.5 ppm singlet (1H) is –COOH [1]. The two 2H triplets are adjacent CH₂ groups splitting each other; the 3.7 ppm one is bonded to O, the 2.6 ppm one is next to C=O [1].
**(e)** W is **3-methoxypropanoic acid**, CH₃OCH₂CH₂COOH [1]. 73 is CH₂CH₂COOH⁺, from loss of OCH₃ (104 − 31); 45 is CH₃OCH₂⁺ (or COOH⁺) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), scale 1.33 : 2.68 : 1 to whole numbers; rounding 1.33 down to 1 gives a wrong formula.

**10. (a)** A C=O group is present [1]; there is no O–H, so V is not an alcohol or a carboxylic acid [1].
**(b)** 57: C₄H₉⁺, from loss of CHO (86 − 29) [1]. 29: CHO⁺ (or C₂H₅⁺) [1].
**(c)** 9.6 ppm: an aldehyde –CHO hydrogen [1]; doublet: the next carbon carries exactly one H [1].
**(d)** **2-methylbutanal**, CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO [1]: only it has a CH next to the CHO (the others would give a triplet or a singlet), and its carbon 2 carries H, CH₃, C₂H₅ and CHO, so it is chiral [1].
**(e)** The synthesis produces equal amounts of both enantiomers, a racemic mixture, so the rotations cancel [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d), name the four groups on the chiral carbon rather than just saying "it is chiral".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "rotates light" instead of "rotates the plane of plane-polarized light".
- Explaining a racemic mixture as containing non-chiral molecules.
- Fragment ions written without a positive charge, or masses lost confused with fragment masses.
- Confusing the alcohol O–H band with the much broader acid O–H band.
- Counting hydrogens on the same carbon, or across a carbon with no H, when applying the n + 1 rule.
- Treating 1.33 in a mole ratio as 1 instead of multiplying through by 3.
