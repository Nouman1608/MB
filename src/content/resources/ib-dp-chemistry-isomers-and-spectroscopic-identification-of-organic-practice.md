## Questions

**1.** State, with a reason, whether each compound shows cis-trans isomerism.

**(a)** pent-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(b)** 3-methylpent-2-ene, CH₃CH=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(c)** 1,2-dibromocyclobutane **[1]**

**2.** 2-chlorobutane, CH₃CHClCH₂CH₃, exists as a pair of enantiomers.

**(a)** Identify the chiral carbon and explain why it is chiral. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw stereochemical formulas of the two enantiomers using wedge-dash bonds. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the two enantiomers differ in their effect on plane-polarized light. **[2]**
**(d)** A laboratory sample of 2-chlorobutane has no effect on plane-polarized light. Explain this. **[2]**

**3.** Methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃, and ethyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₃, are isomers. Fragment data: CH₃⁺ 15, C₂H₅⁺ 29, CH₃O⁺ 31, CH₃CO⁺ 43, HCOO⁺ 45. Explain how their mass spectra would allow you to tell them apart. **[4]**

**4.** The mass spectrum of 1-phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃, has peaks at m/z 120, 105, 77 and 43. Identify the species responsible for each of the peaks at 105, 77 and 43. **[3]**

**5.** Use these IR data: O–H (alcohol) 3200–3600 cm⁻¹; C=O 1700–1750 cm⁻¹; C=C 1620–1680 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** One absorption of a compound is reported at a wavelength of 5.88 μm. Calculate its wavenumber and identify the bond responsible. **[2]**
**(b)** A different compound absorbs broadly at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ and at 1650 cm⁻¹, with nothing at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹. Deduce two features of its structure. **[2]**

**6.** This question is about greenhouse gases.

**(a)** State which vibrations of a CO₂ molecule absorb infrared radiation, and explain why the symmetric stretch does not. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why CH₄ is a greenhouse gas but N₂ is not. **[2]**

**7.** For each compound, predict the number of ¹H NMR signals, the ratio of their areas and the splitting of each signal.

**(a)** 1,1,2-trichloroethane, CHCl₂CH₂Cl **[3]**
**(b)** 2-chloro-2-methylbutane, (CH₃)₂CClCH₂CH₃ **[3]**

**8.** Three isomers of C₃H₆O₂ are propanoic acid, methyl ethanoate and ethyl methanoate. One of them gives this ¹H NMR spectrum:

| δ / ppm | Splitting | Integration step / mm |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 | triplet | 12.6 |
| 2.4 | quartet | 8.4 |
| 11.7 | singlet | 4.2 |

Use these approximate shifts: H on carbon next to C=O 2.0–2.7; H on carbon bonded to O 3.3–4.3; –COOH 10–13 ppm.

**(a)** Deduce the ratio of hydrogen atoms in each environment. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the compound. Explain how the signals support your answer and rule out the other two isomers. **[4]**

**9.** Compound W contains 62.0% carbon, 10.4% hydrogen and 27.6% oxygen by mass. Use Ar values C = 12.01, H = 1.01, O = 16.00.

- MS: M⁺ at m/z 116; other peaks at 101, 59 and 43.
- IR: broad absorption at 3450 cm⁻¹ and strong absorption at 1710 cm⁻¹.
- ¹H NMR: four signals, all singlets, at 1.2, 2.2, 2.6 and 3.8 ppm, with integration steps of 21.0, 10.5, 7.0 and 3.5 mm.

**(a)** Determine the empirical formula of W. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of W. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the two functional groups shown by the IR spectrum. **[2]**
**(d)** Deduce the number of hydrogen atoms in each environment and explain why every signal is a singlet. **[3]**
**(e)** Deduce the structure of W and identify the ions at m/z 59 and 43. **[2]**

**10.** An optically active liquid, V, has the molecular formula C₆H₁₂O.

**(a)** Its IR spectrum shows a strong absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ and no broad absorption at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹. State what this shows. **[2]**
**(b)** Its mass spectrum has M⁺ at m/z 100 and large peaks at 57 and 43. Identify a possible ion for each of these two peaks. **[2]**
**(c)** Its ¹H NMR spectrum includes a singlet of area 3 at 2.1 ppm. State what this shows. **[1]**
**(d)** V is one of: hexan-2-one, 4-methylpentan-2-one, 3-methylpentan-2-one and 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one. Identify V, using its optical activity. **[2]**
**(e)** A sample of V made in the laboratory from non-chiral starting materials has no optical activity. Suggest why. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** No: carbon 1 carries two H atoms, so only one arrangement exists [1].
**(b)** Yes: carbon 2 has H and CH₃, and carbon 3 has CH₃ and C₂H₅, so both carbons carry two different groups [1].
**(c)** Yes: carbons 1 and 2 each have H and Br, and the ring prevents rotation, so the Br atoms can share a face or not [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give what is attached to each carbon; a bare "yes" or "no" earns little.

**2. (a)** Carbon 2: it is bonded to four different groups, H, Cl, CH₃ and C₂H₅ [1].
**(b)** A tetrahedral carbon with two bonds in the plane, one wedge and one dash, carrying the four groups [1]; a second drawing that is the mirror image of the first [1].
**(c)** Each rotates the plane of plane-polarized light [1] by the same angle but in opposite directions [1].
**(d)** It is a racemic mixture, with equal amounts of the two enantiomers [1]; their equal and opposite rotations cancel, so there is no net rotation [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), make the second drawing a true reflection; a rotated copy of the first is the same enantiomer.

**3.** Both have M⁺ at m/z 74 [1]. Methyl ethanoate gives a peak at 43, CH₃CO⁺, formed by loss of OCH₃ (74 − 31) [1]. Ethyl methanoate gives a peak at 45, HCOO⁺, formed by loss of C₂H₅ (74 − 29) [1], and a peak at 29 from C₂H₅⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pair each m/z value with a positively charged formula; numbers alone explain nothing.

**4.** 105: C₆H₅CO⁺, from loss of CH₃ (120 − 15) [1]. 77: C₆H₅⁺ [1]. 43: CH₃CO⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write each fragment as a cation; C₆H₅CO without the + sign is incomplete.

**5. (a)** Wavenumber = 1/(5.88 × 10⁻⁴ cm) = **1.70 × 10³ cm⁻¹** (1701 cm⁻¹) [1]; this is C=O [1].
**(b)** Broad 3200–3600 cm⁻¹: an alcohol O–H group [1]. 1650 cm⁻¹ with nothing at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹: a C=C bond and no C=O, so it is an unsaturated alcohol [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert μm to cm before taking the reciprocal.

**6. (a)** The asymmetric stretch and the bending vibrations absorb [1]. In the symmetric stretch, both C=O bond dipoles change equally and cancel, so the molecule's dipole moment does not change [1].
**(b)** CH₄ has vibrations that change its dipole moment, so it absorbs infrared emitted by Earth's surface and re-emits some back down [1]. The stretch of N₂ never creates a dipole, so N₂ cannot absorb infrared radiation [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link absorption to a change in dipole moment; CO₂ itself is non-polar.

**7. (a)** Two signals [1]; ratio CH : CH₂ = 1 : 2 [1]; CH triplet (two neighbours) and CH₂ doublet (one neighbour) [1].
**(b)** Three signals [1]; ratio 6 : 2 : 3 for the two CH₃ on carbon 2, the CH₂ and the end CH₃ [1]; the 6H signal is a singlet, CH₂ a quartet and the end CH₃ a triplet [1].
*Examiner insight:* Carbon 2 in (b) has no hydrogen, so the 6H signal cannot be split; counting across it is a common slip.

**8. (a)** 12.6 : 8.4 : 4.2 = **3 : 2 : 1** [1].
**(b)** Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH [1]. The 11.7 ppm singlet (1H) is the –COOH proton [1]. The quartet (2H) at 2.4 ppm is a CH₂ next to C=O and next to a CH₃, and the triplet (3H) is that CH₃, so an ethyl group is joined to C=O [1]. Methyl ethanoate would give only two singlets, and ethyl methanoate would put its CH₂ quartet at 3.3–4.3 ppm with no signal above 10 ppm [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say why each rejected isomer fails, not only why your choice fits.

**9. (a)** Moles in 100 g: C 62.0/12.01 = 5.16; H 10.4/1.01 = 10.3; O 27.6/16.00 = 1.73 [1]. Dividing by 1.73 gives 3 : 6 : 1, so **C₃H₆O** [1].
**(b)** M(C₃H₆O) = 58; 116/58 = 2, so **C₆H₁₂O₂** [1].
**(c)** Broad 3450 cm⁻¹: O–H of an alcohol [1]. 1710 cm⁻¹: C=O, a carbonyl group (ketone) [1].
**(d)** Dividing by 3.5 gives 6 : 3 : 2 : 1, matching 12 H [1]. Assignments: 1.2 ppm is two equivalent CH₃ groups (6H); 2.2 ppm is CH₃ next to C=O (3H); 2.6 ppm is CH₂ next to C=O (2H); 3.8 ppm is O–H (1H) [1]. All are singlets because no carbon bearing H is bonded to another carbon bearing H [1].
**(e)** W is **4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one**, CH₃COCH₂C(OH)(CH₃)₂ [1]. 59 is (CH₃)₂COH⁺ and 43 is CH₃CO⁺ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the division in (a) and the factor of 2 in (b); a bare formula is hard to credit.

**10. (a)** A C=O group is present [1]; there is no O–H, so it is not an alcohol [1].
**(b)** 43: CH₃CO⁺ [1]. 57: C₄H₉⁺, from loss of CH₃CO (100 − 43) [1].
**(c)** A CH₃ group bonded to the C=O carbon, with no hydrogen on the neighbouring carbon, so V is a methyl ketone [1].
**(d)** **3-methylpentan-2-one**, CH₃COCH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ [1]: carbon 3 is chiral (H, CH₃, C₂H₅, COCH₃), while none of the other three isomers has a carbon with four different groups [1].
**(e)** The synthesis produces equal amounts of both enantiomers, a racemic mixture, so the rotations cancel [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d), name the four groups on the chiral carbon, not just "it is chiral".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing the "mirror image" enantiomer as a rotated copy of the first.
- Writing "rotates light" instead of "rotates the plane of plane-polarized light".
- Explaining a racemic mixture as containing non-chiral molecules.
- Fragment ions written without a positive charge, or masses lost confused with fragment masses.
- Forgetting to convert μm to cm when finding a wavenumber.
- Saying CO₂ absorbs IR because it is polar.
- Counting the hydrogens on the same carbon when applying the n + 1 rule.
