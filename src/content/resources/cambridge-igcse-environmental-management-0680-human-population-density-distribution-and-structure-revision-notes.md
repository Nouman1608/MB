## The four statements

1. **7.1.1** Density and distribution: sparse and dense places; density in people per km²; distribution as spread.
2. **7.1.2** Interpret pyramids: young and elderly dependants, working population, female and male.
3. **7.1.3** World Bank income groups: LICs, MICs, HICs.
4. **7.1.4** Compare an LIC and an HIC pyramid.

## Density versus distribution

| | Density | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Number of people living in an area | How people are spread over an area |
| Form | A number | A pattern |
| Unit | People per km² | None (described in words or on a map) |
| Formula | Population ÷ area | Not calculated |
| Example statement | "120 people per km²" | "Clustered along the coast and river valleys" |

**Sparsely populated** = few people. **Densely populated** = many people.

Densely populated areas are usually lowland river valleys and coastal plains with water, fertile soil, moderate climate and jobs. Sparsely populated areas are usually deserts, high mountains, polar regions and dense forest.

## Describing a distribution map: PNDA

- **P**attern: even or uneven; clustered or dispersed.
- **N**amed places where dense and where sparse.
- **D**ata: quote values from the key.
- **A**nomaly: anything that breaks the pattern.

## Pyramid basics

- Bars = age groups (usually 5 years); males left, females right.
- Check whether bars show **numbers** or **percentages**.
- **Young dependants**: 0 to 14. **Working population**: 15 to 64. **Elderly dependants**: 65 and over.
- **Dependants per 100 workers** = (young + elderly) ÷ working × 100.

| Feature | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Wide base | High birth rate; many children |
| Sides narrowing quickly | Many deaths at each age; shorter life expectancy |
| Straight sides | Most people survive into old age |
| Wide top | Long life expectancy; ageing population |
| Narrow base under a wider middle | Falling birth rate |
| Bulge in working ages | Past baby boom or in-migration of workers |
| Notch in one age group | War, epidemic or period of low births |
| More women at the top | Women tend to live longer than men |

## World Bank income groups

- Classified by **income per person** (gross national income per capita).
- **LICs**: lowest; **MICs**: middle (split by the World Bank into lower-middle and upper-middle); **HICs**: highest.
- Limits are revised every year, so learn the groups, not the figures.
- Averages hide inequality within a country.

## LIC versus HIC structure

| | LIC | HIC |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Wide | Narrow |
| Top | Narrow | Wide |
| Shape | Triangle | Column |
| Main dependants | Young | Elderly |
| Pressure on | Schools, jobs, food, housing | Healthcare, pensions, care homes |
| Gender | Fairly even | More elderly women than men |

## Worked density comparison

Region P: 1,200,000 people in 4,000 km² = 300 people per km². Region Q: 1,200,000 people in 48,000 km² = 25 people per km². Same population, but P is twelve times as densely populated, which is why density, not total population, is used to compare crowding.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population density | Number of people living in an area, usually per km² |
| Population distribution | How people are spread over an area |
| Sparsely populated | Containing few people |
| Densely populated | Containing many people |
| Population structure | Make-up of a population by age and sex |
| Population pyramid | Back-to-back bar chart of age groups by sex |
| Young dependent population | People aged 0 to 14 |
| Working population | People aged 15 to 64 |
| Elderly dependent population | People aged 65 and over |
| LIC, MIC, HIC | Low-, middle- and high-income countries in the World Bank classification |

## Exam traps

- Density given without units, or calculated as area ÷ population.
- Treating density as even across a country.
- "The pyramid is a triangle" with no figures quoted.
- Using "developed" or "poor" when the syllabus uses LIC and HIC.
- Reading one side of a percentage pyramid as the whole population; add male and female bars.
- Comparing in two separate paragraphs; compare in the same sentence.

## Self-test

1. Define population density.
2. Distinguish density from distribution.
3. A district has 750,000 people in 2,500 km². Calculate its density.
4. Name two types of place that are usually sparsely populated.
5. Which age group makes up the working population?
6. On a pyramid, what does a wide base suggest?
7. What might a bulge in the 25 to 39 age groups show?
8. Why are there often more women than men in the oldest bars?
9. What measure does the World Bank use to group countries?
10. Name the three income groups in the syllabus.
11. Give two differences between typical LIC and HIC pyramids.
12. A country has 30% aged 0 to 14, 60% aged 15 to 64 and 10% aged 65+. How many dependants are there per 100 working-age people?

**Answers:** 1. The number of people living in an area, usually per km². 2. Density is a number per unit area; distribution is the pattern of how people are spread. 3. 750,000 ÷ 2,500 = 300 people per km². 4. Any two: deserts; high mountains; polar regions; dense forest. 5. 15 to 64. 6. A high birth rate, with many children. 7. Past baby boom or in-migration of working-age adults. 8. Women tend to live longer than men. 9. Income per person (gross national income per capita). 10. Low-income, middle-income, high-income countries. 11. Any two: LIC wide base, HIC narrow base; LIC narrow top, HIC wide top; LIC triangular, HIC column-shaped. 12. (30 + 10) ÷ 60 × 100 = 66.7.
