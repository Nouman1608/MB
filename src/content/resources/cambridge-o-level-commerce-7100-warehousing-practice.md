## Section A

**1.** State **two** functions of warehousing. **[2]**

**2.** Identify the type of warehouse described: "customs-approved storage where imported goods are kept without paying duty until they are released". **[1]**

**3.** State **one** type of goods that is stored in cold storage. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** uses of automation in warehouses. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** benefit of warehouse automation. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** An importer brings in 600 cases of wine worth $80 each. The import duty is 25%. The cases are stored in a bonded warehouse and 200 cases are released for sale in the first month.

**(a)** Calculate the total value of the wine. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the duty payable in the first month. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why using a bonded warehouse helps the importer. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how a warehouse helps to provide price stability for farmers' crops. **[3]**

**8.** Describe the difference between a cash and carry warehouse and a regional distribution centre. **[4]**

**9.** A warehouse processes 5,000 orders a month. Human error affects 2% of orders. After automation the error rate is 0.4%. Each error costs $15 to correct.

**(a)** Calculate the number of errors before and after automation. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly saving from fewer errors. **[2]**

**10.** Explain what is meant by **logistics postponement** and give an example. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **two** benefits to a retailer of using automation in its warehouse. **[4]**

**12.** A company sells frozen fish. Explain why it uses cold storage. **[2]**

**13.** Evaluate whether a small business should invest in warehouse automation. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: link with seasonal production; link with demand; protecting supply; price stability; logistics postponement [1 each].

**2.** Bonded warehouse [1].

**3.** Any one from: fresh food, frozen food, medicines, flowers [1].

**4.** Any two from: robotics; automated sorting; voice picking [1 each].

**5.** Any one from: lower labour costs; less human error; improved picking accuracy; improved inventory control; improved customer service [1].

**6.** **(a)** 600 × 80 = $48,000 [1].

**(b)** Value of 200 cases = 200 × 80 = $16,000 [1]. Duty = 25% × 16,000 = $4,000 [1].

**(c)** Duty is not paid until the goods are released for sale [1], so the importer does not need to pay duty on unsold cases, which helps cash flow [1].

**7.** Crops are harvested at one time, so there is a large supply and prices may fall [1]. A warehouse stores some of the crop [1] and releases it later when supply is lower, so prices do not rise sharply [1].

**8.** A cash and carry warehouse sells to trade customers such as small retailers and caterers [1], who choose the goods, pay at once and carry them away themselves [1]. A regional distribution centre receives goods from many suppliers [1] and delivers them to shops in its region [1].

**9.** **(a)** Before: 2% × 5,000 = 100 errors [1]. After: 0.4% × 5,000 = 20 errors [1].

**(b)** Fewer errors = 100 − 20 = 80 [1]. Saving = 80 × 15 = $1,200 [1].

**10.** Delaying final packaging, labelling or customising until the customer's order is known [1], so goods are stored in a general form [1]. Example: a warehouse holds unlabelled products and adds the correct language label after an order is received [1].

**11.** Any two developed: improved picking accuracy [1], so customers get the right goods and fewer are returned [1]; lower labour costs [1], because fewer workers are needed to move and sort goods [1]; improved inventory control [1], because the system records stock so shortages are spotted early [1]; improved customer service [1], because orders are sent faster [1]. Maximum 4.

**12.** Frozen fish must be kept at a low temperature [1] or it will go bad before it can be sold [1].

**13.** Any five from: benefits: fewer errors and more accurate picking [1]; lower labour costs over time [1]; better stock records [1]; drawbacks: high cost of equipment may be too much for a small business [1]; needs skilled staff to maintain [1]; the saving may be small if the number of orders is low [1]; a justified conclusion, for example that a small business may do better to wait until order volumes are high enough [1]. Maximum 5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 6(b): calculating duty on all 600 cases instead of the 200 released.
- Question 8: describing only one of the two warehouses.
- Question 9: working out 2% of the wrong number, or forgetting to multiply by $15.
- Question 10: no example given.
- Question 12: saying only "to keep it cool" without the reason (it would spoil).
- Question 13: describing automation without a decision for a small business.

|---|---|

| Automation | Robotics, automated sorting, voice picking |

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a bonded warehouse the same as a normal warehouse?** No. It is approved by customs and stores imported goods so that duty is not paid until they are released for sale in the country.

**Do I need to name an example in every answer?** An example helps to show understanding and earns marks when the question asks for it, but always give the explanation first.
