This guide covers section 5.4, Surface area and volume, in the Mensuration topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to carry out calculations and solve problems involving the surface area and volume of a cuboid, prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone. Answers may be asked for in terms of π. The List of formulas gives the curved surface area of a cylinder and of a cone, the surface area of a sphere, and the volumes of a prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone and sphere. The term prism refers to any solid with a uniform cross-section, for example a cylindrical sector. After this guide, use the [surface area and volume revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume-revision-notes/) and the [surface area and volume practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume-practice/).

## What section 5.4 asks for

1. Use the given formulas for volume and curved surface area.
2. Build the total surface area from the pieces, including flat faces that are not in the list.
3. Rearrange to find a missing length.
4. Use Pythagoras' theorem to find a slant height or a vertical height.

## Formulas

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid l × w × h | lwh | 2(lw + lh + wh) |
| Prism, cross-section area A, length l | Al | 2A + perimeter × l |
| Cylinder, radius r, height h | πr²h | curved 2πrh, plus 2πr² for both ends |
| Cone, radius r, height h, slant height l | 1/3 πr²h | curved πrl, plus πr² for the base |
| Sphere, radius r | 4/3 πr³ | 4πr² |
| Pyramid, base area A, height h | 1/3 Ah | base plus the triangular faces |

The ones in the List of formulas are: curved surface areas of the cylinder and cone, the surface area of the sphere, and the volumes of the prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone and sphere. The cuboid formulas and the flat end faces you add up for yourself.

For a cone, the slant height l is related to the vertical height h and radius r by Pythagoras: l² = r² + h².

## Cuboids and prisms

### Worked example 1

A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
volume = 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³
surface area = 2(8 × 5) + 2(8 × 3) + 2(5 × 3) = 80 + 48 + 30 = 158 cm²
```

### Worked example 2

A prism of length 12 cm has a trapezium as its cross-section, with parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height 4 cm and two equal sloping sides. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
cross-section area = 1/2 × (6 + 10) × 4 = 32 cm²
volume = 32 × 12 = 384 cm³
sloping side = √(2² + 4²) = √20 = 4.47 cm
perimeter of the cross-section = 6 + 10 + 2 × 4.47 = 24.94 cm
surface area = 2 × 32 + 24.94 × 12 = 64 + 299.3 = 363 cm²
```

The overhang on each side is (10 − 6) ÷ 2 = 2 cm. The sides together form a rectangle whose width is the perimeter of the cross-section.

### A sector prism

### Worked example 3

A prism has a cross-section that is a quarter circle of radius 6 cm. It is 10 cm long. Find its volume.

```
cross-section area = 1/4 × π × 6² = 9π
volume = 9π × 10 = 90π = 283 cm³
```

## Cylinders

### Worked example 4

A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume, curved surface area and total surface area, in terms of π and to 3 significant figures.

```
volume = π × 4² × 10 = 160π = 503 cm³
curved surface area = 2π × 4 × 10 = 80π
two circular ends = 2 × π × 4² = 32π
total surface area = 112π = 352 cm²
```

### Finding a missing dimension

### Worked example 5

A cylinder has volume 300 cm³ and height 7 cm. Find its radius.

```
π × r² × 7 = 300
r² = 300 ÷ (7π) = 13.64
r = 3.69 cm
```

## Cones

### Worked example 6

A cone has base radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Find the slant height, the curved surface area, the total surface area and the volume.

```
slant height l = √(6² + 8²) = 10 cm
curved surface area = π × 6 × 10 = 60π = 188 cm²
base = π × 36 = 36π
total surface area = 96π = 302 cm²
volume = 1/3 × π × 36 × 8 = 96π = 302 cm³
```

### Worked example 7

The curved surface area of a cone is 40π cm² and its slant height is 8 cm. Find the radius.

```
π × r × 8 = 40π
r = 5 cm
```

## Spheres and hemispheres

### Worked example 8

A sphere has radius 5 cm. Find its surface area and volume.

```
surface area = 4 × π × 25 = 100π = 314 cm²
volume = 4/3 × π × 125 = 500π/3 = 524 cm³
```

### Worked example 9

A solid hemisphere has radius 6 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
volume = 1/2 × 4/3 × π × 6³ = 144π = 452 cm³
curved surface = 1/2 × 4π × 36 = 72π
flat circular face = π × 36 = 36π
total surface area = 108π = 339 cm²
```

### Worked example 10

The volume of a sphere is 36π cm³. Find its radius.

```
4/3 × π × r³ = 36π
r³ = 36 × 3 ÷ 4 = 27
r = 3 cm
```

## Pyramids

For a pyramid, the volume is 1/3 × base area × vertical height.

### Worked example 11

A pyramid has a square base of side 10 cm and a vertical height of 12 cm. The apex is directly above the centre of the base. Find the volume, the slant height of a triangular face, and the total surface area.

```
volume = 1/3 × 100 × 12 = 400 cm³
slant height: l² = 12² + 5² = 169, so l = 13 cm
each triangle = 1/2 × 10 × 13 = 65 cm²
surface area = 100 + 4 × 65 = 360 cm²
```

The 5 in Pythagoras comes from half of the base side, the distance from the centre of the base to the midpoint of an edge.

## Problems involving two solids

Questions often connect two solids through volume: a solid is melted and recast, or a solid is lowered into a liquid. The volume stays the same, so set the two volumes equal.

### Worked example 12

A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is lowered into a cylindrical tank of radius 20 cm that contains water. The sphere is completely covered. By how much does the water level rise?

```
volume of the sphere = 4/3 × π × 6³ = 288π cm³
the rise is a cylinder of radius 20 cm and height h
π × 20² × h = 288π
400h = 288
h = 0.72 cm
```

### Worked example 13

A solid metal cube of side 6 cm is melted and recast as small spheres of radius 1.5 cm. How many whole spheres can be made?

```
volume of the cube = 6³ = 216 cm³
volume of one sphere = 4/3 × π × 1.5³ = 4.5π = 14.14 cm³
number of spheres = 216 ÷ 14.14 = 15.3
```

So 15 whole spheres can be made. Notice that the answer is rounded down, because part of a sphere cannot be used.

### Worked example 14

A cone and a cylinder have the same base radius of 10 cm and the same height of 12 cm. Compare their volumes.

```
cylinder: π × 10² × 12 = 1200π
cone: 1/3 × π × 10² × 12 = 400π
```

The cone's volume is exactly one third of the cylinder's. The same relationship holds for a pyramid and a prism with the same base and height.

## Choosing the right height

A frequent source of error is the height:

- **vertical (perpendicular) height** goes in volume formulas,
- **slant height** goes in curved surface area formulas for a cone and in triangular face areas for a pyramid.

If only one is given, find the other with Pythagoras' theorem.

## Common mistakes

- Using the slant height in a volume formula.
- Forgetting the base or the ends when asked for the total surface area.
- Mixing up πr²h (cylinder) with 1/3 πr²h (cone).
- Cubing 2r instead of r, or squaring the wrong quantity.
- Using the diameter in place of the radius.
- Giving volume in square units, or surface area in cubic units.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** a volume: write the formula, substitute and then give the unit cm³.
- **Total** surface area: list each part, then add.
- **In terms of π**: do not turn π into a decimal.
- Draw and label the solid, and mark the right angle for Pythagoras.
