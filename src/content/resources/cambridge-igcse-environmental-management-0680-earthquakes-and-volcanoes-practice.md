## Section A

**1.** Describe the core of the Earth. **[2]**

**2.** State what is meant by a *hotspot*. **[2]**

**3.** Name the type of plate boundary where plates slide past each other. **[1]**

**4.** State what a *volcanic bomb* is. **[1]**

**5.** Name **two** continents that border the Pacific Ocean. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain how sulfur dioxide released in an eruption can damage vegetation many kilometres from the volcano. **[3]**

**7.** Describe what happens at a divergent (constructive) plate boundary and explain why volcanic activity occurs there. **[3]**

**8.** Source: *An earthquake of magnitude 7.2 struck 15 km beneath the coastal city of Port Velan at 03:10. Gas pipes burst in the old town, and the main water pipe from the reservoir was broken. Thirty minutes later, waves flooded the harbour.*
(a) State the term for the point 15 km beneath the city where the earthquake began. **[1]**
(b) Using the source, explain **two** secondary impacts of the earthquake. **[4]**
(c) A second earthquake of magnitude 6.2 struck a month later. Calculate approximately how many times more energy the first earthquake released. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why many people continue to live close to active volcanoes. **[4]**

**10.** Describe **three** ways buildings can be designed to reduce damage in an earthquake. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why monitoring is more useful for managing volcanic eruptions than earthquakes. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of strategies used during and after a tectonic event to reduce its impacts. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The very hot, dense centre of the Earth **[1]**, made mainly of iron and nickel, which provides the heat that drives mantle convection **[1]**.

**2.** A place where a plume of unusually hot mantle rock rises beneath a plate **[1]**, away from a plate boundary, melting through the crust to form volcanoes **[1]**.

**3.** Conservative **[1]**.

**4.** A large lump of lava thrown out during an eruption that cools and hardens as it travels through the air **[1]**.

**5.** Any two: Asia; North America; South America; Oceania; Antarctica. **[2]**

**6.** Sulfur dioxide is carried by the wind away from the volcano **[1]**; it dissolves in water in clouds to form acid rain **[1]**; the acid rain damages leaves and makes soil acidic, harming or killing plants **[1]**.

**7.** Two plates move apart **[1]**; the gap reduces pressure on the mantle beneath, so magma rises to fill it **[1]**, erupting as lava and forming new crust, so volcanic activity and small earthquakes occur **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Focus **[1]**.
(b) Any two explained, 2 marks each: burst gas pipes in the old town **[1]** could ignite, causing fires that spread through damaged buildings **[1]**; the broken water pipe **[1]** cuts off or contaminates the drinking-water supply, raising the risk of water-related disease **[1]**; waves flooding the harbour show a tsunami **[1]**, damaging boats, port buildings and infrastructure **[1]**. **[max 4]**
(c) One whole-number step, so about 32 times more energy **[1]**.

**9.** Any four: fertile volcanic soils give high crop yields **[1]**; jobs and income from tourism **[1]**; minerals and building materials can be extracted **[1]**; geothermal power provides energy **[1]**; eruptions may be rare and monitored, so people feel the risk is low **[1]**; family and land ties or lack of money to move elsewhere **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**10.** Any three: cross-bracing of walls and frames **[1]**; deep or flexible foundations, or base isolators and shock absorbers that let the building move **[1]**; lightweight roofs that cause less harm if they fall **[1]**; reinforced concrete or steel frames that bend without breaking **[1]**; windows that do not shatter. **[max 3]**

**11.** Volcanoes usually give warning signs before erupting, such as small earthquakes, swelling of the ground and changes in gas emissions **[1]**, which instruments can detect, so warnings and evacuation can take place **[1]**; earthquakes cannot be predicted precisely, so monitoring cannot give a reliable advance warning of when one will strike **[1]**.

**12.** Award up to 6 for a balanced discussion of strategies used during and after: **Evacuation**: moves people away from lava, pyroclastic flows or tsunami zones, saving lives **[1]**; but roads may be blocked and people may refuse to leave homes or livestock **[1]**. **Shelters**: provide safety, food and water for the homeless **[1]**; but overcrowding and poor sanitation can spread disease **[1]**. **Rebuilding**: restores homes and services and gives a chance to build to safer standards **[1]**; but it is slow and expensive **[1]**. **International aid**: brings rescue teams, medical care and money when local resources are overwhelmed **[1]**; but it can arrive late, miss remote areas or create dependency **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing focus and epicentre.
- Saying "pollution" for sulfur dioxide without the link to acid rain.
- Using the source only for the hazard itself, ignoring the pipes and waves that show secondary impacts.
- Putting before-event strategies such as zoning into an answer about during and after.
- Discussing only benefits.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Earthquakes and volcanoes questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Which magnitude scale should I name?**
The syllabus names the Moment Magnitude Scale. Each whole-number increase means about 32 times more energy released.

**Do I need to know a real example for each boundary?**
The syllabus does not name examples, but a correct one, such as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge for a divergent boundary, makes a description clearer.
