## Questions

**1.** Describe what happens to chlorophyll during photoionisation and state where its replacement electrons come from. **[3]**

**2.** In a chloroplast the stroma is pH 8.0 and the thylakoid space is pH 5.0.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the hydrogen ion concentration is in the thylakoid space. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the difference is formed and how it leads to the production of ATP. **[4]**

**3.** Photolysis of water produces three products. Name them and give one fate of each. **[3]**

**4.** Nine carbon dioxide molecules are fixed in the Calvin cycle.

**(a)** State how many molecules of GP are formed. **[1]**
**(b)** Three molecules of triose phosphate leave the cycle. Calculate how many RuBP molecules the rest can regenerate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the number of carbon atoms leaving the cycle must equal the number entering. **[1]**

**5.** A herbicide blocks the electron transfer chain in chloroplasts. Explain the effect on the concentrations of GP, triose phosphate and RuBP shortly after treatment. **[4]**

**6.** The table shows the rate of oxygen release (arbitrary units) by a plant under four sets of conditions.

| Treatment | Light | CO₂ | Temperature | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | low | 0.04% | 20 °C | 5 |
| X | high | 0.04% | 20 °C | 5 |
| Y | high | 0.20% | 20 °C | 12 |
| Z | high | 0.20% | 30 °C | 24 |

**(a)** Identify the limiting factor in W and in X, with evidence. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why raising the temperature from 20 °C to 30 °C increased the rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the rate would not keep rising as temperature is raised further. **[1]**

**7.** Pigments from a leaf were separated by chromatography. The solvent front moved 9.2 cm. Four spots had centres 8.7, 6.4, 5.9 and 4.1 cm from the origin.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf of the second and fourth spots. **[2]**
**(b)** An extract from a shade-tolerant plant gave a larger spot than a sun plant at the fourth position. Suggest what this shows. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two precautions for getting reliable measurements. **[2]**

**8.** Chloroplast extract was mixed with DCPIP and absorbance was read at the start and after 10 minutes.

| Tube | Start | After 10 min |
|---|---|---|
| 20 °C | 0.62 | 0.38 |
| 30 °C | 0.62 | 0.20 |
| 45 °C | 0.62 | 0.55 |
| Boiled extract, 30 °C | 0.62 | 0.62 |
| 30 °C in foil | 0.62 | 0.61 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate at 20 °C and at 30 °C, with units. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the result at 45 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the boiled and foil tubes show. **[2]**

**9.** In a 300 m² polytunnel, 1200 kg of strawberries were grown in ambient conditions. Carbon dioxide enrichment (cost £240) gave 1380 kg. Adding artificial light as well (extra cost £520) gave 1470 kg. Strawberries sell at £3.20 per kg.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield from carbon dioxide enrichment. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net gain from carbon dioxide enrichment. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate the addition of artificial light. **[2]**
**(d)** State one limitation of the data. **[1]**

**10.** A lamp was placed 10 cm and then 20 cm from pondweed. The pondweed released 1.8 cm³ and 0.72 cm³ of gas in 12 minutes respectively.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at each distance. **[2]**
**(b)** Light intensity is roughly proportional to 1 ÷ distance². State the expected ratio of rates, 10 cm to 20 cm, if light were the only limiting factor. **[1]**
**(c)** The measured ratio is lower. Suggest two reasons. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why a tank of water between lamp and plant improves the investigation. **[1]**

**11.** Describe and explain how light energy is conserved in the light-dependent reaction and used to make a simple sugar in the light-independent reaction. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Chlorophyll absorbs light [1], which excites electrons so that they leave the chlorophyll (it is ionised) [1]. The electrons are replaced by electrons from the photolysis of water [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that says electrons are "lost" but not that light caused it tends to miss the idea that makes it photoionisation.

**2. (a)** The difference is 8.0 − 5.0 = 3.0 pH units [1], so 10³ = **1000 times** greater [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Energy released as electrons move along the electron transfer chain is used to move protons from the stroma into the thylakoid space [1]. The membrane is impermeable to protons, so a gradient builds up [1]. Protons flow back down the gradient through ATP synthase [1], which makes ATP from ADP and phosphate (chemiosmosis) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculations of pH differences gain credit for converting units to a tenfold factor; stating only "3" does not answer "how many times".

**3.** Electrons, which replace those lost from chlorophyll [1]. Protons, which add to the gradient or reduce NADP [1]. Oxygen, which is released or used in respiration [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming three products with no fate for each is a common way to lose credit when the question asks for both.

**4. (a)** **18** GP [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 18 − 3 = 15 triose phosphate remain, which is 45 carbons [1]. 45 ÷ 5 = **9 RuBP** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** If more carbon left than entered, the cycle could not regenerate enough RuBP to keep fixing carbon dioxide [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the carbon count makes it clear how the answer was reached; a bare number leaves nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**5.** No electrons move along the chain, so less ATP is made [1] and NADP is not reduced [1]. GP cannot be reduced, so GP rises and triose phosphate falls [1]. Less TP and ATP means RuBP is not regenerated but is still used by rubisco, so RuBP falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that give the direction of change for each compound, with a reason tied to the reaction that stopped, are easier to credit than a list of "decreases".

**6. (a)** Raising light from W to X gave no change in rate [1]. Raising carbon dioxide from X to Y raised the rate [1]. Carbon dioxide is therefore the limiting factor in both W and X [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Higher temperature increases kinetic energy and collisions between enzymes (such as rubisco) and substrate [1], so more reactions occur per second [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Enzymes denature at high temperature [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparisons that change only one variable between treatments are what support a conclusion about limiting factors.

**7. (a)** Second: 6.4 ÷ 9.2 = **0.70** [1]. Fourth: 4.1 ÷ 9.2 = **0.45** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The shade plant has more of that pigment [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Mark the solvent front as soon as the paper is removed [1]. Measure to the centre of each spot, and use pencil for the origin [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Rf has no unit; writing one, or quoting it to more decimal places than the measurements allow, can lose credit.

**8. (a)** 20 °C: (0.62 − 0.38) ÷ 10 = **0.024** absorbance units per minute [1]. 30 °C: (0.62 − 0.20) ÷ 10 = **0.042** absorbance units per minute [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The rate is low because enzymes have begun to denature [1], so fewer electrons reach DCPIP [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Boiled: no colour change shows enzymes need to be active [1]. Foil: very little change shows light is required for the reaction [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Rates need the unit; "per minute" is part of the answer.

**9. (a)** (1380 − 1200) ÷ 1200 × 100 = **15%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Extra income = 180 × 3.20 = £576 [1]. Net gain = 576 − 240 = **£336** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Extra income from light is 90 × 3.20 = £288 [1], which is less than the cost of £520, a net loss of **£232**, so it is not worthwhile [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** One season, one crop, no repeats, or prices vary [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation needs a judgement supported by figures; a percentage alone, with no cost, is unlikely to complete it.

**10. (a)** 1.8 ÷ 12 = **0.15** cm³ per minute [1]; 0.72 ÷ 12 = **0.060** cm³ per minute [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (20 ÷ 10)² = **4** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The measured ratio is 2.5. Another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, is limiting [1]. The lamp is not a point source, or the gas collected includes oxygen used in respiration or dissolving [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** It absorbs heat from the lamp, so temperature stays constant [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Suggestions that name a specific variable score better than "errors" or "mistakes".

**11.** Chlorophyll absorbs light, which excites and releases electrons (photoionisation) [1]. Electrons pass along the electron transfer chain, losing energy [1]. Energy is used to move protons into the thylakoid space [1]. Protons flow through ATP synthase, driving ATP formation (chemiosmosis) [1]. Electrons and protons reduce NADP [1]. Photolysis replaces the electrons [1]. In the stroma, carbon dioxide reacts with RuBP, catalysed by rubisco, to form GP [1], reduced to triose phosphate using ATP and reduced NADP, then TP is converted to sugars and used to regenerate RuBP [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Extended answers earn credit for linking each stage to the next, such as ATP and reduced NADP being used in the reduction of GP.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that energy is made, rather than transferred.
- Forgetting that photolysis supplies electrons for chlorophyll.
- Saying that ATP synthase pumps protons.
- Not giving the direction of change for GP, triose phosphate and RuBP.
- Describing the light-independent reaction as needing darkness.
- Calling the x-axis factor limiting on a plateau.
- Giving a percentage without dividing by the original.
- Leaving out units, or giving Rf with units.
- Evaluating agricultural data with no mention of cost.
