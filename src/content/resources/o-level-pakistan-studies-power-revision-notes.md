**These notes compress Section 3 Power of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059 Paper 2 into
tables and checklists for the last weeks before the exam.** You must be able to draw the extraction
methods, name where each fuel comes from and how it is moved, and judge which power sources can
best close Pakistan's energy gap. For full explanations, use the
[Power study guide](/resources/o-level-pakistan-studies-power/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Overburden | Soil and rock lying above a coal seam, removed in open cast mining |
| Adit | A horizontal or gently sloping tunnel into a hillside to reach a seam |
| Anticline | An upfold of rock layers that can trap oil and gas beneath cap rock |
| Lignite | Low-grade "brown" coal with high moisture and ash; most Thar coal |
| LPG | Liquefied petroleum gas, sold in cylinders where there is no pipeline |
| LNG | Liquefied natural gas, imported by ship and regasified at Port Qasim |
| Thermal power | Electricity from burning fuel (coal, oil, gas, waste) to make steam |
| Run-of-river | Hydel scheme using river flow without a large storage reservoir |
| Load-shedding | Planned power cuts when demand exceeds supply |
| Circular debt | Unpaid bills passed along the power chain, so fuel cannot be bought |

| Fuel | Where | Moved by | Headline fact |

## Generation mix in one line each

- **Thermal:** imported oil, gas and LNG, imported coal (Sahiwal, Port Qasim) and Thar coal
- **Nuclear:** Chashma units (Punjab) and K-2, K-3 (Karachi)
- **Hydel:** Tarbela, Mangla, Warsak; run-of-river Ghazi–Barotha, Neelum–Jhelum
- **Wind:** Jhimpir–Gharo corridor, Thatta district, Sindh
- **Solar:** solar parks and, since about 2022, a boom in rooftop panels

## Renewables: one advantage, one drawback, one Pakistani fact

| Source | Advantage | Drawback | Pakistan link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydel | No fuel bill; stores irrigation water | Silting; low winter flow; people resettled | Upper Indus schemes under construction |
| Wind | No emissions; steady sea breezes | Variable; needs new grid lines | Jhimpir–Gharo corridor |
| Solar | Sunshine most of the year | No output at night; dust on panels | Rooftop growth in cities and farms |
| Wave/tidal | Long coastline | Untested, costly technology | Not yet developed |

## Worked example: a part (d) style judgement

*"Pakistan should spend on solar power rather than on new large dams." Do you agree? Give reasons.*

> Solar can be installed quickly in small units, needs no fuel and suits scattered villages in
> Balochistan and Thar far from the grid. However, it produces nothing at night, and batteries
> are costly. Large dams such as Tarbela give cheap power for decades and also store irrigation
> water and reduce floods, but they take many years to build, silt up and displace people.
> **Judgement:** solar is the better short-term choice for remote areas, but dams remain
> necessary for irrigation, so Pakistan needs both rather than one instead of the other.

The shape: argument for, argument against, Pakistani example on each side, a clear judgement.

## Exam traps

- **Drawing an adit as a vertical hole.** An adit goes in sideways from a hillside; a shaft goes
  down.
- **Saying Pakistan "has no coal".** It has huge reserves; the problem is quality and location.
- **Forgetting the limits of LPG.** Higher cost, winter shortages and safety risk must be stated
  when asked how gas reaches areas off the network.
- **Treating hydel as reliable all year.** River flow and output drop sharply in winter.
- **Quoting undated statistics.** Reserve and output figures change each year.
- **Listing renewables without judging them.** "Evaluate" needs a conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Which mining method suits a thick seam near the surface?
2. Name two Balochistan coal areas.
3. Why is most Thar coal burned close to the mine?
4. Give two limits of supplying LPG instead of piped gas.
5. Name the pipeline carrying refined fuel inland from Port Qasim.
6. Give two physical conditions for a multi-purpose hydel scheme.
7. Why does hydel output fall in winter?

**Answers:** 1. Open cast. 2. Any two of Duki, Sor Range, Chamalang. 3. It is low-grade lignite
with high moisture, so it is uneconomic to transport. 4. Any two of higher cost, irregular supply
in winter, safety risk. 5. The White Oil Pipeline (to Mahmood Kot). 6. Any two of narrow gorge,
strong impermeable rock, reliable river flow, large storage valley. 7. Snowmelt stops and rainfall
is low, so river flow is reduced.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the quickest way to revise the three coal-mining methods?**
Draw each from memory in under a minute, with four labels, then check the labels against the
study guide.

**Is nuclear power renewable?**
No. Nuclear stations use uranium, which is a finite resource, even though they emit little carbon
dioxide while running.

**Why is power linked to development?**
Industry, irrigation tubewells, hospitals, schools and telecommunications all need reliable,
affordable energy, so shortages slow growth and cost jobs.
