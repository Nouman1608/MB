## Food chains

```
producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer
(arrow = "is eaten by" = direction biomass moves)
```

- Photosynthetic organisms are the **producers of biomass** for life on Earth.
- A producer synthesises molecules: usually a green plant or alga making glucose by photosynthesis.
- **Predators** kill and eat other animals; **prey** are eaten. One animal can be both.

## Required practical 9: sampling

| | Random quadrats | Transect |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Abundance (population size) | Distribution (change across a habitat) |
| Placing | Random coordinates from a random number generator | Regular intervals along a tape |
| Also record | Count in each quadrat | Count or percentage cover, plus an abiotic factor at each point |
| Result | Mean per quadrat → per m² → × area | Pattern linked to the factor |

**Estimating population size**

```
population = (mean number per quadrat ÷ quadrat area) × total area
```

Worked reminder: mean 4.5 per 0.5 m² quadrat in a 600 m² field → 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = 9 per m² → 9 × 600 =
**5400**.

**Averages**: mean (add and divide), median (middle value in order), mode (most common value).

**Improving the investigation**: more quadrats; random positions; repeat transects at several places; same
person counting; clear rule for plants on the edge of the quadrat.

## Predator–prey cycles

```
prey rise → predators rise (later) → prey fall → predators fall (later) → repeat
```

- Prey peak comes **before** predator peak (time lag).
- Predator numbers are usually lower than prey numbers.
- Interpret graphs: give peak years and explain each change by food supply or predation.

## Cycling materials

| Cycle | Key points |
|---|---|
| Carbon | Photosynthesis removes CO₂; respiration by plants, animals and microorganisms returns it; combustion returns it; feeding passes carbon compounds along food chains |
| Water | Evaporation and transpiration → condensation → precipitation; provides fresh water on land before draining to the sea |

- **Microorganisms** return **carbon to the atmosphere as CO₂** and **mineral ions to the soil**.
- The **nitrogen cycle is not required**.

## Decomposition (biology only)

| Condition | Effect on rate of decay |
|---|---|
| Warm (to an optimum) | Faster: decomposer enzymes work faster; too hot denatures them |
| Moist | Faster: microorganisms need water |
| Oxygen available | Faster: aerobic respiration by decomposers |

- **Compost**: decayed material used as a natural fertiliser.
- **Anaerobic decay** → **methane**; **biogas generators** make methane fuel.
- **Rate of decay** = change in mass ÷ time (for example g per week).

### Required practical 10 (biology only)

- Effect of temperature on decay of fresh milk, by **measuring pH change**.
- Milk made alkaline; lipase breaks down fat into fatty acids, so pH falls.
- Follow with a pH probe or an indicator such as phenolphthalein (pink → colourless).
- Same volumes, same concentrations; temperature set with a water bath.
- Rate = 1 ÷ time taken.

## Environmental change (biology only, Higher tier only)

- Changes in **temperature**, **availability of water** and **composition of atmospheric gases** affect where
  species are found.
- Changes may be **seasonal**, **geographic** or **caused by human interaction**.
- Evaluate: what moved, the link to the change, limitations (correlation, one site, other factors), judgement.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Producer | Organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis; starts every food chain |
| Primary consumer | Eats producers |
| Predator | Consumer that kills and eats other animals |
| Quadrat | Square frame used to sample a known area |
| Transect | Line across a habitat along which samples are taken |
| Abundance | How many individuals of a species are present |
| Distribution | Where a species is found |
| Percentage cover | Fraction of a quadrat covered by a species |
| Decomposer | Microorganism that breaks down dead material and waste |
| Biogas | Gas, mainly methane, from anaerobic decay |

## Exam traps

- Arrows point **towards the eater**.
- Quadrats for numbers; transects for distribution.
- Decay speeds up with temperature only **up to an optimum**.
- Microorganisms release CO₂ by **respiration**, not by "breathing it out".
- In RP10 the pH **falls** because fatty acids are made.
- Higher tier evaluate answers need a **limitation**.

## Self-test

1. What do all food chains begin with?
2. In "grass → rabbit → stoat", name the secondary consumer.
3. Which sampling technique shows how a species' distribution changes across a habitat?
4. Why must quadrats be placed at random to estimate abundance?
5. Ten quadrats give a total of 64 plants. Each quadrat is 0.25 m². Estimate the number of plants in 200 m².
6. Find the median of 3, 8, 2, 6, 5.
7. In a predator–prey cycle, which population peaks first?
8. Name two processes that return carbon dioxide to the air.
9. Give the two things microorganisms return to the environment when they decay material.
10. Give three conditions that speed up decay.
11. Why does the pH of milk fall in Required practical 10?
12. (Higher tier) Name the three environmental changes in 4.7.2.4.

### Answers

1. A producer (usually a green plant or alga).
2. The stoat.
3. A transect.
4. So the sample is representative and not biased by where the student chooses to look.
5. Mean = 6.4 per quadrat; 6.4 ÷ 0.25 = 25.6 per m²; 25.6 × 200 = **5120**.
6. In order 2, 3, 5, 6, 8; median = **5**.
7. The prey.
8. Any two: respiration; decay by microorganisms; combustion.
9. Carbon dioxide to the atmosphere; mineral ions to the soil.
10. Warmth (up to an optimum), moisture, oxygen.
11. Lipase breaks down fats in the milk into fatty acids.
12. Temperature; availability of water; composition of atmospheric gases.
