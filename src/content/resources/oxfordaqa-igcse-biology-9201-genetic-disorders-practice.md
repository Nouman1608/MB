These questions cover section **3.5.4 Genetic disorders** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201)
specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All data are invented
for practice. The specification asks for sensitivity when teaching about inherited disorders. A calculator is useful for
questions 8 to 10.

Learn the content first in the [Genetic disorders study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-genetic-disorders/)
and the [Genetic disorders revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-genetic-disorders-revision-notes/).
For mixed questions on inheritance, use the [Genetic variation, monohybrid inheritance, DNA and genetic disorders practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-genetic-variation-monohybrid-inheritance-dna-and-practice/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State whether polydactyly is caused by a dominant or a recessive allele. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether cystic fibrosis is caused by a dominant or a recessive allele. **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** Explain what is meant by a carrier of a recessive disorder. **[1]**
**(b)** Using C for the normal allele and c for the cystic fibrosis allele, write the genotype of a carrier. **[1]**

**3.** State what causes Down's syndrome. **[1]**

## Section B

**4.** Two parents are both carriers of the cystic fibrosis allele (Cc).

**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram (Punnett square) to show the possible genotypes of their children. **[3]**
**(b)** State the probability that a child has cystic fibrosis. **[1]**
**(c)** State the probability that a child is a carrier. **[1]**

**5.** A family tree for a disorder shows two unaffected parents who have one affected child and one unaffected child.

**(a)** State whether the allele for the disorder is dominant or recessive. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer to part (a). **[2]**

**6.** Polydactyly is caused by a dominant allele, D. A man with polydactyly (Dd) and a woman without polydactyly (dd) have a child.

**(a)** State the probability that the child has polydactyly. **[1]**
**(b)** The couple's first child has polydactyly. State the probability that their second child has polydactyly. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** Sickle cell anaemia is caused by a recessive allele, h. Normal is H. One parent is a carrier (Hh) and the other has sickle cell anaemia (hh).

**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram to show the possible genotypes of their children. **[3]**
**(b)** State the probability that a child is a carrier but not affected. **[1]**

**8.** Cystic fibrosis affects about 1 in 2 500 babies in a country. In one year, 48 000 babies are born. Calculate the number of babies expected to have cystic fibrosis. **[2]**

**9.** Two carriers of the cystic fibrosis allele plan to have three children. Each child has a 1 in 4 chance of being affected.

**(a)** Calculate the probability that none of the three children is affected. Give your answer as a decimal to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the probability that at least one of the three children is affected. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the answer to (b) does not mean one child will definitely be affected. **[1]**

**10.** The table shows the number of babies born with Down's syndrome per 10 000 births, for five groups of mothers.

| Age of mother | 20-24 | 25-29 | 30-34 | 35-39 | 40-44 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cases per 10 000 births | 6 | 7 | 12 | 38 | 115 |

**(a)** Describe the trend shown in the table. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the number of cases is for mothers aged 40 to 44 than for mothers aged 20 to 24. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** State **one** conclusion that the table does not allow you to make. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why Down's syndrome is described differently from cystic fibrosis in the specification. **[2]**

**12.** Two unaffected parents have a child with cystic fibrosis. Explain how this can happen, using a genetic diagram or description, and give the probability that their next child will also have cystic fibrosis. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Dominant [1]
**(b)** Recessive [1]

**2. (a)** A person with one copy of a recessive allele who is not affected but can pass the allele on [1]
**(b)** Cc [1]

**3.** An extra chromosome (inheriting an abnormal number of chromosomes) [1]

**4. (a)** Gametes C and c from each parent [1]; offspring CC, Cc, Cc, cc [1]; shown clearly in a Punnett square [1]
**(b)** 1/4 (25%) [1]
**(c)** 2/4 = 1/2 (50%) [1]

**5. (a)** Recessive [1]
**(b)** The parents are unaffected but have an affected child [1]; so each parent carries a recessive allele without showing the disorder, and the child inherited one from each [1]

**6. (a)** 1/2 (50%) [1]
**(b)** 1/2 [1]; each child's chance is independent of the earlier children (the first child does not change the probability) [1]

**7. (a)** Gametes H and h from one parent, h and h from the other [1]; offspring Hh, Hh, hh, hh [1]; shown clearly in a Punnett square [1]
**(b)** 2/4 = 1/2 (50%) [1]

**8.** 48 000 ÷ 2 500 [1] = **19.2**, so about 19 babies [1]

**9. (a)** (3/4) × (3/4) × (3/4) [1] = 27/64 = **0.42** [1]
**(b)** 1 − 0.42 = **0.58** (37/64) [1]
**(c)** It is a probability, not a certainty; each birth is an independent chance, so the number of affected children could be 0, 1, 2 or 3 [1]

**10. (a)** The number of cases per 10 000 births increases as the age of the mother increases [1]; with a much steeper increase after the age of 34 (from 12 to 38 to 115) [1]
**(b)** 115 ÷ 6 [1] = 19.2, so about **19 times** as many [1]
**(c)** Any one of the following [1]: that the age of the mother causes the extra chromosome; that the pattern applies in every country or that an individual mother's chance can be predicted exactly; that the father's age has no effect

**11.** Down's syndrome is caused by an extra chromosome [1]; cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele of a gene (not by an abnormal number of chromosomes) [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele
- the unaffected parents must both be carriers (Cc)
- each parent produces gametes with C or c
- the child inherited c from each parent (genotype cc)
- the genetic diagram gives offspring CC, Cc, Cc, cc
- the probability that the next child has cystic fibrosis is 1/4 (25%), because each birth is independent of the previous one

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "1 in 4 children will be affected" instead of "each child has a 1 in 4 chance".
- Calling a carrier "affected".
- Using a lower-case letter for a dominant allele.
- Saying that Down's syndrome is caused by a faulty allele.
- Adding probabilities for independent events instead of multiplying.
- Drawing conclusions about cause from a table of numbers alone.
