## Questions

**1.** Triangle ABC has A(0, 1), B(2, 1) and C(2, 4). It is translated by the vector (−3, −2). Write down the coordinates of A′, B′ and C′. **[2]**

**2.** The triangle with vertices A(2, 3), B(5, 3) and C(5, 1) is reflected.

**(a)** Reflect it in the line y = −1 and write down the image coordinates. **[2]**
**(b)** A different reflection maps A to (−4, 3). Find the equation of its mirror line. **[2]**

**3.** The triangle with vertices (3, 2), (6, 2) and (6, 4) is called T.

**(a)** T is rotated through 180° about (1, 1). Write down the image coordinates. **[2]**
**(b)** A rotation maps P(4, 3) to P′(3, −2) and Q(4, 1) to Q′(1, −2). Describe the rotation fully. **[4]**

**4.** Triangle R has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (3, 2).

**(a)** R is enlarged by scale factor 2, centre (0, −1). Find the image coordinates. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the area of R and the area of the image. **[2]**

**5.** A triangle has area 9.5 cm² and perimeter 21 cm. It is enlarged by scale factor 4.

**(a)** Find the perimeter of the image. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the area of the image. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the two triangles are congruent or similar. **[1]**

**6.** Triangle T has vertices (0, 2), (2, 2) and (2, 3). Triangle U has vertices (−2, 0), (−2, −2) and (−3, −2). Triangle V has vertices (4, −1), (6, −1) and (6, 0). Triangle W has vertices (2, 0), (0, 0) and (0, −1). The vertices are listed in matching order.

**(a)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps T to U. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps T to V. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps T to W. **[3]**

**7.** **(Extension only)** The triangle with vertices (3, 1), (4, 1) and (4, 3) is enlarged.

**(a)** Use scale factor −2 and centre (2, 0). Find the image coordinates. **[3]**
**(b)** Triangle H has vertices (2, 5), (8, 5) and (8, −1). Enlarge H by scale factor ⅓, centre (−1, 2). Find the image coordinates. **[2]**

**8.** **(Extension only)** Triangle J has vertices (4, 1), (8, 1) and (8, 3). Triangle K has vertices (1, 1), (−1, 1) and (−1, 0). The vertices are listed in matching order. Describe fully the single transformation that maps J to K. **[4]**

**9.** **(Extension only)** Triangle M has vertices (1, 2), (3, 2) and (3, 5).

**(a)** M is rotated 90° anticlockwise about the origin and the image is then reflected in the line y = x. Find the final vertices and describe the single transformation that maps M to them. **[4]**
**(b)** A point is reflected in x = 1 and then in x = 5. Describe the single transformation that has the same effect. **[2]**

**10.** Triangle A has vertices (3, 1), (5, 1) and (5, 4).

**(a)** A is rotated through 90° anticlockwise about (2, 0) to give B. Find the vertices of B. **[3]**
**(b)** B is enlarged by scale factor 2, centre (1, 1), to give C. Find the vertices of C. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the area of A and the area of C. **[2]**
**(d)** State which two of the triangles A, B and C are congruent. **[1]**

**11.** **(Extension only)** Triangle A has vertices (2, 0), (3, 0) and (3, 2). It is enlarged by scale factor 3, centre (1, −1), to give B. Then B is enlarged by scale factor −⅓, centre (1, −1), to give C.

**(a)** Find the vertices of B. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the vertices of C. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the single transformation that maps A to C. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Add −3 to each x and −2 to each y: (x, y) → (x − 3, y − 2). [1] A′(−3, −1), B′(−1, −1), C′(−1, 2). [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each image point with its own letter and give full coordinates. A list of numbers with no labels makes it hard to see which vertex went where.

**2. (a)** For y = −1 the rule is (x, y) → (x, −2 − y). [1] A′(2, −5), B′(5, −5), C′(5, −3). [1]
**(b)** The mirror line is halfway between A and its image. Midpoint of x-values = (2 + (−4))/2. [1] The mirror line is **x = −1**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a mirror line as an equation such as x = −1, not in words.

**3. (a)** Relative to (1, 1) the points are (2, 1), (5, 1), (5, 3). Turn 180°: (−2, −1), (−5, −1), (−5, −3). [1] Add (1, 1): **(−1, 0), (−4, 0), (−4, −2)**. [1]
**(b)** PP′ has midpoint (3.5, 0.5), and the perpendicular bisector is x + 5y = 6. [1] QQ′ has midpoint (2.5, −0.5), and the perpendicular bisector is x + y = 2. [1] Subtracting gives 4y = 4, so y = 1 and x = 1. The centre is (1, 1). [1] P is (3, 2) from the centre and P′ is (2, −3) from it, so the turn is 90° clockwise. [1] The rotation is **90° clockwise about (1, 1)**. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A description of a rotation has three parts: angle, direction and centre. Check your final answer by rotating one point, so that you catch a clockwise and anticlockwise mix-up.

**4. (a)** Image = centre + 2 × (point − centre), so (1, 1) gives (0, −1) + 2(1, 2). [1] The image is **(2, 3), (6, 3), (6, 5)**. [1]
**(b)** Area of R = ½ × 2 × 1 = **1** square unit. [1] The image has base 4 and height 2, so its area is ½ × 4 × 2 = **4** square units. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Measure from the centre of enlargement and not from a corner of the shape. Using the area scale factor 2² = 4 as a check shows you the answer to part (b) is consistent.

**5. (a)** Lengths scale by 4: 21 × 4 = **84 cm**. [1]
**(b)** Areas scale by 4² = 16. [1] 9.5 × 16 = **152 cm²**. [1]
**(c)** The angles are equal but the sides are different sizes, so the triangles are **similar** and not congruent. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to multiply the area by 4. Write the area scale factor as 4² first, and include units.

**6. (a)** It is a **reflection**. [1] The mirror line is **y = −x**, since (0, 2) maps to (−2, 0), using (x, y) → (−y, −x). [1]
**(b)** It is a **translation**. [1] The vector is **(4, −3)**, since (0, 2) maps to (4, −1). [1]
**(c)** It is a **rotation**. [1] The angle is **180°**, which needs no direction. [1] The centre is the midpoint of (0, 2) and (2, 0), which is **(1, 1)**. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the transformation first, then give each detail it needs. If you name two transformations in one description, the answer is not a single transformation.

**7. (a)** Relative to (2, 0): (1, 1), (2, 1), (2, 3). [1] Multiply by −2: (−2, −2), (−4, −2), (−4, −6). [1] Add (2, 0): **(0, −2), (−2, −2), (−2, −6)**. [1]
**(b)** Relative to (−1, 2): (3, 3), (9, 3), (9, −3). Multiply by ⅓: (1, 1), (3, 1), (3, −1). [1] Add (−1, 2): **(0, 3), (2, 3), (2, 1)**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* With a negative scale factor the image lands across the centre from the object. A quick sketch shows at once if your answer is on the wrong side.

**8.** The base of J has length 4 and the base of K has length 2, so the size factor is ½. [1] K lies across the centre from J, so the scale factor is **−½**. [1] The line through (4, 1) and (1, 1) is y = 1. The line through (8, 3) and (−1, 0) is y = x/3 + 1/3. They meet where x/3 + 1/3 = 1, so x = 2. [1] The centre is (2, 1). It is an **enlargement, scale factor −½, centre (2, 1)**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A scale factor of ½ with the wrong sign describes a different image. Say which side of the centre the image is on before you decide on the sign.

**9. (a)** After the rotation, (x, y) → (−y, x): (−2, 1), (−2, 3), (−5, 3). [1] After the reflection in y = x, swap coordinates: **(1, −2), (3, −2), (3, −5)**. [1] Comparing with M, only the y-values change sign. [1] The single transformation is a **reflection in the x-axis (y = 0)**. [1]
**(b)** x → 2 − x → 10 − (2 − x). [1] This equals x + 8, so it is a **translation by (8, 0)**. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The order of transformations is part of the question. Follow each vertex through both steps in the order given, then compare the final positions with the starting positions.

**10. (a)** Relative to (2, 0): (1, 1), (3, 1), (3, 4). [1] Turn 90° anticlockwise, (x, y) → (−y, x): (−1, 1), (−1, 3), (−4, 3). [1] Add (2, 0): **B(1, 1), (1, 3), (−2, 3)**. [1]
**(b)** Relative to (1, 1): (0, 0), (0, 2), (−3, 2). [1] Multiply by 2: (0, 0), (0, 4), (−6, 4). [1] Add (1, 1): **C(1, 1), (1, 5), (−5, 5)**. [1]
**(c)** Area of A = ½ × 2 × 3 = **3** square units. [1] Area of C = 3 × 2² = **12** square units. [1]
**(d)** **A and B** are congruent, because a rotation preserves size and shape. [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-step question, show each image clearly so that your method can be followed and you can spot slips early.

**11. (a)** Relative to (1, −1): (1, 1), (2, 1), (2, 3). [1] Multiply by 3 and add the centre: **B(4, 2), (7, 2), (7, 8)**. [1]
**(b)** Relative to (1, −1) the points of B are (3, 3), (6, 3), (6, 9). Multiply by −⅓: (−1, −1), (−2, −1), (−2, −3). [1] Add the centre: **C(0, −2), (−1, −2), (−1, −4)**. [1]
**(c)** The combined scale factor is 3 × (−⅓) = −1. [1] A scale factor of −1 about (1, −1) is a **rotation of 180° about (1, −1)**. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying the scale factors only works because both enlargements use the same centre. Check the result with one vertex, for example (2, 0) mapping to (0, −2).

## Where marks are usually lost

* Describing a transformation in one word, with no mirror line, centre, angle, direction or vector.
* Leaving the direction out of a rotation that is not 180°.
* Measuring a reflection from the wrong line, or using the shape's edge instead of the mirror line.
* Writing the translation vector with the numbers swapped or the signs reversed.
* Measuring an enlargement from a corner instead of from the centre.
* Using k instead of k² for the area scale factor.
* Putting a negative enlargement on the same side of the centre as the object.
* Combining transformations in the wrong order.
