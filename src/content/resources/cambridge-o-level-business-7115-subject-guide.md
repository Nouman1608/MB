Cambridge O Level Business Studies (7115) is a course in how businesses
work: why they exist, how they are organised, how they treat their
employees and customers, how they produce and sell, how they are
financed, and how the economy and society affect them. The syllabus
edition covered here is a single-year edition for 2026. It is assessed
through two externally assessed papers of equal weight, each lasting 90
minutes, carrying 80 marks and worth 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus is divided into six topics, which can be thought of as a
framing topic, four business functions and a wider-context topic.

**Framing the subject (Topic 1: Understanding business activity).**
Business activity, the classification of businesses, enterprise, business
growth and size, types of business organisation, and the objectives of
businesses and their stakeholders.

**The four functions (Topics 2 to 5).**
- *People in business:* motivating employees, organisation and
  management, recruitment, selection and training, and internal and
  external communication.
- *Marketing:* marketing, competition and the customer, market research,
  the marketing mix and marketing strategy.
- *Operations management:* production of goods and services; costs, scale
  of production and break-even analysis; achieving quality production;
  and location decisions.
- *Financial information and decisions:* business finance needs and
  sources, cash-flow forecasting and working capital, income statements,
  the statement of financial position and the analysis of accounts.

**The wider context (Topic 6: External influences on business
activity).** Economic issues, environmental and ethical issues, and
business and the international economy.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Short Answer and Data Response | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Case Study | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed and the course is not tiered;
grades run from A* to E. Paper 1 contains four questions that combine
short answers with structured data responses. Paper 2 contains four
questions based on a case study supplied as an insert. The assessment is
linear, so both papers are taken in the same series.

This page describes the 2026 edition of the syllabus, for which 2026 is
both the first and the final examination year. If your exams fall in a
later year, check the syllabus Cambridge publishes for that year before
relying on the details above.

## How the parts fit together

Because both papers can draw on any topic, 7115 rewards students who see
the syllabus as one connected picture. Break-even analysis sits in
operations but depends on cost and revenue ideas used again in finance.
Recruitment and training in people in business affect quality
production in operations. Marketing strategy depends on market research
and on what the business can afford, which comes back to finance.
External influences in Topic 6, such as economic, environmental and
ethical issues, can change decisions in every one of the four functions.

The two papers ask you to use the same knowledge in different ways.
Paper 1's data-response questions require you to read and interpret
information quickly, often with some calculation. Paper 2 asks you to
apply your understanding to a single business described in the case
study and to make reasoned recommendations. Practising both styles on
each topic as you study it builds the skills gradually.

Stakeholders are a good example of an idea that runs through the whole
syllabus. They are introduced in Topic 1, but every later decision
affects them: employees in people in business, customers in marketing,
the local community in location decisions, lenders and owners in
finance, and society at large in environmental and ethical issues.
Asking "who gains and who loses?" for any business decision is a simple
habit that helps you write balanced answers, and it links naturally back
to business objectives, which also appear in the opening topic.

## Planning your study

A general route through the course:

- **Start:** Topic 1, so that terms such as stakeholders, sectors and
  types of organisation are secure from the outset.
- **Middle:** Topics 2 to 5 in order, with short, regular practice on
  cash-flow forecasts, break-even and the analysis of accounts.
- **Later:** Topic 6, linking each external influence back to the four
  functions, followed by whole-syllabus revision.

Keep a notebook of real business examples throughout, since applying
ideas to a specific organisation is central to both papers. Start using
past-paper questions on each topic once it is complete, and leave full
timed papers and detailed technique for the revision period, when the
exam preparation page is the place to go.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115?**
There are two: Paper 1, Short Answer and Data Response, and Paper 2, Case Study. Each lasts 90 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50%.

**Is O Level Business 7115 tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same two papers, and grades run from A* to E.

**What does the Paper 2 case study involve?**
Paper 2 has four questions based on a case study provided as an insert with the paper, so you apply your knowledge to one business.

**Which years does this syllabus edition cover?**
The edition described here is a single-year edition for 2026. Check the syllabus for your own examination year if you are sitting later.

**What are the six topics in 7115?**
Understanding business activity, People in business, Marketing, Operations management, Financial information and decisions, and External influences on business activity.
