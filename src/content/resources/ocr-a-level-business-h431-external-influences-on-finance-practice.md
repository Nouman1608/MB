## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain **one** way in which a rise in interest rates could affect a business's finance decisions. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a legal and an ethical influence on accounting and finance. **[3]**

**3.** A company pays corporation tax at 20% on a profit before tax of £180,000. The rate rises to 25%. Calculate the fall in profit after tax. **[3]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following is a technological influence on accounting and finance?

A: A rise in the rate of corporation tax.

B: The development of cloud accounting software.

C: A fall in the pound against the dollar.

D: A new law on late payment of invoices. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business might invest in energy-saving equipment even though it is expensive. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Hadley Fabrications has a variable-rate loan of £400,000. The interest rate rises from 4% to 6.5%. Its operating profit is £160,000.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in annual interest. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate interest cover before and after the rise. **[4]**

**(c)** Evaluate how Hadley Fabrications could respond to the rise in interest rates. **[8]**

**7.** A UK exporter is paid $300,000 for a contract. When the contract was agreed, £1 was worth $1.20. By the time of payment, £1 is worth $1.35.

**(a)** Calculate the sterling value of the payment at each rate. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the loss and express it as a percentage of the original value. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **two** ways in which the exporter could reduce this exchange rate risk in future. **[4]**

**8.** In a recession, a wholesaler with credit sales of £1,500,000 finds that its bad debts rise from 0.4% of sales to 1.6%.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in bad debts. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate the other effects of the recession on the wholesaler's finances and the actions it should take. **[8]**

**9.** Evaluate the significance of ethical influences on the way a business reports its financial results. **[8]**

**10.** A retailer is considering whether to move its accounting to cloud-based software with automated bank feeds. Evaluate the impact of this technological change on the business and its stakeholders. **[8]**

**11.** Evaluate which **one** of political, economic or international influences is likely to be the most important for a UK business that imports most of its products from Asia and sells in the UK market. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Higher interest rates raise the cost of borrowing [1], so a business may delay investment, repay debt or prefer retained profit to loans [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A legal influence is a rule set by law that must be followed, such as the requirement to file accounts [1]. An ethical influence concerns what is right beyond the law, such as not window dressing the accounts [1]. Breaking the law brings penalties, whereas ethical failures harm trust and reputation [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Tax at 20% = £36,000, profit after tax £144,000 [1]. Tax at 25% = £45,000, profit after tax £135,000 [1]. Fall = **£9,000** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** It may reduce running costs, for example electricity, and so pay for itself over time [1]. It may also improve the business's image with customers and lenders who care about the environment [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Before: 4% × £400,000 = £16,000. After: 6.5% × £400,000 = £26,000 [1]. Increase = **£10,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Before: 160,000 ÷ 16,000 = **10.0 times** [1] [1]. After: 160,000 ÷ 26,000 = **6.15 times** (6.2) [1] [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Repay part of the loan from retained profit, which cuts the interest bill, but uses cash [1] [1]. Fix the rate on the loan (if the lender allows) to give certainty, though a fixed rate may be higher and would miss falls in rates [1] [1]. Delay or scale back investment, since new projects now have a higher cost of finance [1]. Raise prices or cut costs to protect profit, though this may reduce sales [1].
Assessment: interest cover of 6.15 times is still comfortable, so the rise reduces profit by £10,000 but does not threaten survival [1].
Judgement: Hadley should avoid drastic action; it should repay some debt if it has surplus cash and fix the rate if it expects further rises [1]. (Maximum 8 marks.) **[8]**

**7. (a)** At $1.20: $300,000 ÷ 1.20 = **£250,000** [1]. At $1.35: $300,000 ÷ 1.35 = **£222,222** [1] [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Loss = £250,000 − £222,222 = **£27,778** [1]. Percentage = 27,778 ÷ 250,000 × 100 [1] = **11.1%** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Agree a forward exchange rate with the bank, which fixes the sterling value at the time of the contract [1], giving certainty although the exporter would not gain if the pound fell [1]. Invoice in sterling, so the buyer bears the risk [1], but the buyer may then prefer a competitor who invoices in dollars [1]. (Other sensible methods may be credited, such as buying supplies in dollars.) **[4]**

**8. (a)** Before: 0.4% × £1,500,000 = £6,000. After: 1.6% × £1,500,000 = £24,000 [1] [1]. Increase = **£18,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Other effects: customers pay more slowly, so cash is tighter [1]; demand falls, so revenue falls [1]; banks may be less willing to lend when the wholesaler most needs an overdraft [1]; interest rates may have changed [1].
Actions: tighter credit checks and shorter credit terms [1], though this may lose sales [1]; credit insurance or factoring, which cost money but protect cash [1]; reduce stock and spending to preserve cash [1].
Judgement: the most urgent action is to protect cash, because the combined effect of late payment and a harder funding market can threaten survival even if the business remains profitable [1]. (Maximum 8 marks.) **[8]**

**9.** Ethical standards affect how honestly a business reports results, going beyond what the law requires [1]. Window dressing, for example delaying supplier payments or counting revenue early to show a better position, may mislead lenders and investors [1]. A business that reports honestly builds trust, which can lower the cost of borrowing [1] and reduces the risk of legal action if it is found out [1].
Against: honest reporting of poor results may make it harder to raise funds or may cause the share price to fall in the short term [1]; managers with bonuses linked to profit are tempted to take shortcuts [1].
Judgement: ethical influences are highly significant, because trust is what gives accounts value, and the short-term gain from misleading users is outweighed by the long-term cost if the business is found out [1]. They are reinforced by law and by auditors [1]. **[8]**

**10.** Business: cloud software with bank feeds cuts the time spent on bookkeeping and the chance of errors [1]; managers get up-to-date figures on sales and cash [1]; the cost is the subscription and training [1]; risks include cyber attacks and system failure [1].
Stakeholders: owners gain from lower admin costs and better decisions [1]; accountants and finance staff may need new skills, and some routine roles may go [1]; the tax authority and lenders can get information more easily [1].
Judgement: the change is likely to be worthwhile for most retailers, provided that data is protected and staff are trained [1]. **[8]**

**11.** International: a fall in the pound would raise the sterling cost of imports, directly reducing gross profit [1] [1]; the business is exposed on most of its purchases [1].
Economic: interest rates and consumer spending affect borrowing costs and demand [1], but the effect is less direct on the main cost base than exchange rates [1].
Political: trade tariffs or import rules could add costs [1], but are less frequent [1].
Judgement: international influences, especially exchange rates, are probably the most important for an importer, because they affect most costs immediately and can be large, but the business can reduce the risk by fixing rates in advance [1]. This would change if a recession cut demand sharply [1]. (Maximum 9 marks.) **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an influence without linking it to a finance decision.
- No figure to show the size of the effect.
- Treating every influence as a threat.
- Dividing instead of multiplying (or the reverse) when converting currency.
- Not choosing one influence as most important when asked.
