## The four groups at a glance

| PAG | Example activity | Living material | Measured quantity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Photosynthesis | Cabomba under a lamp | Plant | Bubbles or gas volume per minute |
| 6 Physiology | Exercise and pulse or ventilation | Human volunteers | Beats or breaths per minute; recovery time |
| 7 Microbiology | Antimicrobial discs on a bacterial lawn | Bacteria | Diameter then area of clear zone |
| 8 Transport | Potato chips in solutions | Plant tissue | Change in mass or length |

Techniques that appear across the groups: measuring length, area, mass, time, temperature, volume of liquids and gases and pH; safe use of a Bunsen burner and a water bath or electric heater; measuring rates (gas produced, water taken up, colour change of an indicator); microscopes, magnification and labelled drawings; safe and ethical use of living organisms.

## Formulas and conversions

| Quantity | Formula | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | amount / time | 4.5 cm³ in 15 min = 0.30 cm³ per min |
| Percentage change | (final - initial) / initial x 100 | 3.10 g to 2.79 g = -10.0% |
| Area of a circle | πr², r = diameter / 2 | d = 14 mm gives 154 mm² |
| Magnification | image size / actual size | 45 mm drawing of 30 µm cell = x1500 |
| Light intensity | proportional to 1 / distance² | 15 cm to 45 cm gives 1/9 |
| Mean | total / number of values | ignore a clear anomaly |

Conversions: 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 mm = 1000 µm. For magnification, convert both lengths to the same unit first: 45 mm = 45 000 µm, and 45 000 / 30 = 1500.

## Method boxes

**PAG 5: rate of photosynthesis (Cabomba)**
1. Cut the stem under water; invert it in water with sodium hydrogencarbonate added.
2. Put the tube in a water-filled beaker with a thermometer, to hold temperature steady.
3. Set the lamp at a measured distance; wait two minutes.
4. Count bubbles for a fixed time, three repeats, find the mean, divide by time.
5. Change one variable only: distance (light), temperature or hydrogencarbonate (carbon dioxide).

**PAG 6: exercise and pulse**
1. Rest three minutes; take a pulse at the wrist with two fingers.
2. Count for 30 s and double (21 beats in 15 s means x4 = 84 per min); repeat and average.
3. Same standard exercise for all, then measure straight away and each minute.
4. Recovery time is the time to return to the resting rate.

**PAG 7: bacterial lawn**
1. Sterilise dishes and agar (autoclave); wipe bench with disinfectant or alcohol.
2. Work near a Bunsen flame; flame the loop and cool it; lid open as little as possible.
3. Spread a lawn; place discs with flamed forceps, including a sterile water control.
4. Tape the lid on with short strips, invert, label the base, incubate at no more than about 25 °C.
5. Measure the clear zone diameter (two directions, mean), then calculate area.
6. Microscope work: view a prepared, stained bacteria slide, draw it in labelled single lines and state the magnification.

**PAG 8: potato chips**
1. Cut identical chips with a cork borer; same length, no skin.
2. Make a dilution series; 100 cm³ of 0.3 M from 1.0 M stock = 30 cm³ stock + 70 cm³ water.
3. Blot, weigh, soak for a set time at constant temperature, blot, reweigh.
4. Calculate percentage change; plot against concentration; read off where the line crosses zero.

## Reading graphs and evaluating

- A rate curve that rises then levels off means another factor has become limiting; name it.
- A line of best fit that crosses the horizontal axis (percentage change = 0) gives the concentration with no net water movement.
- A point far from the pattern is an anomaly: check for a recording error, repeat it, and say whether you excluded it from the mean.
- To improve reliability, repeat more times, use more volunteers or more plates, and control the variables better.
- To improve accuracy, use a smaller measuring scale (for example a syringe graduated in 0.1 cm³ rather than 1 cm³), a balance that reads to 0.01 g, and a timer, not a guess.
- Suggest a follow-up: a narrower range of concentrations, another agent, a different plant or a longer soak.

## Worked reminders

**Gas volume rate.** A gas syringe collects 5.4 cm³ in 18 minutes. Rate = 5.4 / 18 = 0.30 cm³ per minute.

**Pulse recovery.** A resting rate of 66 per minute becomes 110 after exercise. Increase = (110 - 66) / 66 x 100 = 66.7%.

**Antimicrobial zones.** Zone diameters 14 mm, 16 mm and 15 mm for one agent have a mean of 15 mm. Radius 7.5 mm, area = π x 7.5² = 177 mm².

**Chip length.** A chip goes from 30.0 mm to 31.5 mm. Change = (31.5 - 30.0) / 30.0 x 100 = +5.0%. It has gained water, so the solution had a higher water potential than the chip.

**Reading the zero.** If a chip gains 4% in 0.1 M and loses 6% in 0.3 M, the straight line crosses zero at 0.1 + 0.2 x 4 / (4 + 6) = 0.18 M. Roughly, that is the concentration with about the same water potential as the cells.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Independent, dependent, control variable:** change it, measure it, keep it the same.
- **Accuracy and precision:** nearness to the true value versus tightness of the spread of repeats.
- **Repeatable and reproducible:** same method again versus a different person or equipment.
- **Random and systematic error:** scatter versus a consistent shift in one direction.
- **Sterilise and disinfect:** autoclaving kills all organisms on glassware and media; a disinfectant or alcohol wipe cleans surfaces.
- **Clear zone:** shows growth was prevented near the disc, not that every cell died.
- **Water potential direction:** net water movement is down the water potential gradient, across a partially permeable membrane. No calculation with water potential is needed.

## Safe and ethical practice

- Volunteers: explain the task, get agreement, allow anyone to stop, exclude anyone with a relevant condition, and stop if someone feels unwell.
- Animals: gentle handling, short time, no extremes, return to the habitat.
- Plants: take only what is needed.
- Microorganisms: cultures are not opened after sealing; dishes are autoclaved or disinfected for disposal.
- Heat and electricity: tie back hair, wear eye protection, use a heatproof mat, turn the Bunsen to the safety flame when not heating, and keep mains equipment dry.
- Diagrams: label the lamp, ruler, thermometer, plant, tube and water level, and show where each measurement is taken.

## Quick self-test

1. A volunteer counts 21 beats in 15 s. What is the pulse rate per minute?
2. A lamp is moved from 15 cm to 45 cm. By what fraction does the light intensity change?
3. A chip changes from 3.10 g to 2.79 g. Calculate the percentage change.
4. A clear zone is 14 mm across. Calculate its area (3 s.f.).
5. How would you make 100 cm³ of 0.3 M sucrose from 1.0 M stock?
6. A drawing of a cell is 45 mm long. The cell is 30 µm long. What is the magnification?
7. Why is a bacterial dish inverted?
8. Why include a disc soaked in sterile water?
9. Why is percentage change better than change in mass for chips?
10. Give two ways to make a Cabomba investigation safe and ethical.

### Answers

1. 21 x 4 = **84 beats per minute**.
2. Intensity = (15/45)² = 1/9, so it falls to **one ninth**.
3. (2.79 - 3.10) / 3.10 x 100 = **-10.0%**.
4. r = 7 mm, area = π x 49 = **154 mm²**.
5. **30 cm³ of stock plus 70 cm³ of distilled water**.
6. 45 mm = 45 000 µm; 45 000 / 30 = **x1500**.
7. **So condensation collects in the lid and cannot drip on to the agar.**
8. **It is a negative control: no clear zone shows the disc and agar do not stop growth.**
9. **Chips begin with different masses, so percentage change allows a fair comparison.**
10. **Keep mains equipment away from water and dry; handle the plant gently and take only the material needed (any two sensible answers).**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating the time with a rate (beats, bubbles or cm³ need "per minute").
- Writing "fair test" without naming the variable kept the same.
- Using mass change, not percentage change, when starting masses differ.
- Using the diameter in the πr² formula instead of the radius.
- Mixing mm and µm in a magnification calculation.
- Saying the lamp "heats the plant" with no mention of controlling temperature in a water bath.
- Describing osmosis as movement of solute instead of water.
- Saying bacteria were killed when the data show only a clear zone.
- Drawing diagrams with shading, crossing label lines or no labels on the water level and measuring device.
