## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the normal in a ray diagram. **[1]**

**2.** State the range of frequencies that a human can hear. **[1]**

**3.** State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection to happen. **[2]**

**4.** A ray of light strikes a plane mirror. The angle between the ray and the mirror surface is 34°. State the angle of reflection, and explain how you worked it out. **[2]**

**5.** Light passes from air into a glass block, meeting the surface at an angle of incidence of 30°. State what happens to the speed of the light and the direction in which it bends. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student investigates the refractive index of glass using a rectangular glass block, a ray box and a protractor.

**(a)** Describe how the student should take a set of readings of angle of incidence and angle of refraction. **[4]**
**(b)** The student plots sin *i* on the y-axis against sin *r* on the x-axis. The line of best fit passes through the origin and through the point (0.600, 0.882). Determine the refractive index of the glass. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way the student could make the angle readings more accurate. **[1]**

**7.** A ray of light in air strikes a glass surface at an angle of incidence of 52°. The angle of refraction is 31°.

**(a)** Calculate the refractive index of the glass. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the critical angle for this glass. **[2]**
**(c)** Inside the same glass, a ray meets the glass-to-air surface at an angle of incidence of 35°. Describe what happens to this ray. **[2]**

**8.** Optical fibres are used to carry information.

**(a)** Explain how light travels along an optical fibre, even when the fibre is gently curved. **[3]**
**(b)** State the form in which the information travels along the fibre. **[1]**

**9.** A right-angled glass prism has angles of 90°, 45° and 45°. The glass has a refractive index of 1.50.

**(a)** Show that the critical angle of the glass is less than 45°. **[2]**
**(b)** A ray enters the long face of the prism at 90° to it. Describe the path of the ray through the prism and explain why it leaves travelling in the opposite direction. **[3]**
**(c)** A prism of the same shape is made from a plastic with a refractive index of 1.30. Explain why it would not turn the ray in the same way. **[2]**

**10.** Two students measure the speed of sound in air. They stand 150 m from a large flat wall. One student claps so that each clap coincides with the echo of the previous clap. The other times 20 clap-to-echo intervals as 17.6 s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of sound in air from these results. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the students time 20 intervals instead of one. **[2]**
**(c)** In a second method, two microphones 1.20 m apart are connected to an oscilloscope, and the sound reaches the second microphone 3.5 ms after the first. Calculate the speed of sound given by this method. **[2]**

**11.** A microphone connected to an oscilloscope picks up the sound from a tuning fork.

**(a)** Explain how the microphone and oscilloscope together display the sound wave. **[2]**
**(b)** The timebase is set to 0.40 ms per division, and one complete wave covers 2.5 divisions. Calculate the frequency of the sound. **[3]**
**(c)** State, with a reason, whether a person with normal hearing could hear this sound. **[1]**
**(d)** A second tuning fork produces a sound of half this frequency at the same loudness. Describe how its trace differs from the first, with the same oscilloscope settings. **[2]**

**12.** Diamond has a refractive index of 2.42.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle of diamond. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why light that enters a cut diamond is often reflected many times inside it before it leaves. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A line drawn at 90° (perpendicular) to the surface at the point where the ray meets it [1].

**2.** 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz [1].

**3.** The light is travelling from a material with a higher refractive index towards one with a lower refractive index, for example glass to air [1]. The angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle [1].

**4.** The angle of incidence is 90° − 34° = 56°, because angles are measured from the normal, not the surface [1]. By the law of reflection, the angle of reflection is also 56° [1].

**5.** The light slows down [1] and bends towards the normal [1].

**6. (a)** Place the block on paper and draw round it [1]. Shine a narrow ray from the ray box into one face and mark the incident and emerging rays with crosses, then join them through the outline to show the refracted ray [1]. Draw the normal at the point of entry and measure the angles of incidence and refraction from it with the protractor [1]. Repeat for several different angles of incidence, for example 20° to 70° [1].
**(b)** n = gradient = 0.882 ÷ 0.600 [1] = 1.47 [1].
**(c)** Any one: use a narrower ray (a narrow slit in the ray box); mark the crosses further apart along each ray; draw the normal with a protractor so it is exactly 90° to the surface; repeat each angle and take a mean [1].

**7. (a)** n = sin *i* ÷ sin *r* = sin 52° ÷ sin 31° = 0.788 ÷ 0.515 [1] = 1.53 [1].
**(b)** sin *c* = 1 ÷ *n* = 1 ÷ 1.53 = 0.654 [1], so *c* = 40.8° [1].
**(c)** 35° is less than the critical angle, so the ray refracts out into the air (some weak partial reflection also occurs) [1], bending away from the normal [1].

**8. (a)** Light enters the fibre and strikes the side wall at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle [1], so it is totally internally reflected [1]. This repeats many times along the fibre, so the light stays inside the glass and follows the fibre round gentle bends [1].
**(b)** As pulses of light [1].

**9. (a)** sin *c* = 1 ÷ 1.50 = 0.667 [1], so *c* = 41.8°, which is less than 45° [1].
**(b)** The ray enters along the normal, so it does not bend, and meets one short face at an angle of incidence of 45° [1]. Since 45° is greater than the critical angle, it is totally internally reflected through 90° onto the other short face, where the same happens again [1]. The two 90° turns send the ray back out through the long face, travelling parallel to its original path but in the opposite direction [1].
**(c)** sin *c* = 1 ÷ 1.30, so *c* = 50.3° [1]. The ray meets the short face at 45°, which is less than 50.3°, so it refracts out of the prism instead of being totally internally reflected [1].

**10. (a)** Time for one interval = 17.6 ÷ 20 = 0.88 s [1]. Distance travelled = 2 × 150 = 300 m [1]. Speed = 300 ÷ 0.88 = 340 m/s (2 s.f.; 341 m/s accepted) [1].
**(b)** Human reaction time when starting and stopping the stopwatch is large compared with one 0.88 s interval [1]. Timing 20 intervals makes the reaction-time error a much smaller fraction of the measured time, so the result is more accurate [1].
**(c)** Speed = 1.20 ÷ 0.0035 [1] = 340 m/s (2 s.f.; 343 m/s accepted) [1].

**11. (a)** The microphone converts the pressure variations of the sound into a varying voltage (electrical signal) [1]. The oscilloscope displays this voltage against time as a trace on the screen [1].
**(b)** Period T = 2.5 × 0.40 ms = 1.0 ms = 0.0010 s [1]. f = 1 ÷ T [1] = 1 ÷ 0.0010 = 1000 Hz [1].
**(c)** Yes, because 1000 Hz lies within the human hearing range of 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz [1].
**(d)** The trace shows the same height (same amplitude, same loudness) [1] but each wave is twice as wide, covering 5.0 divisions, so half as many waves fit on the screen [1].

**12. (a)** sin *c* = 1 ÷ 2.42 = 0.413 [1], so *c* = 24.4° [1].
**(b)** The critical angle of diamond is very small [1], so light inside it meets most faces at angles greater than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected again and again before it meets a face at a small enough angle to leave [1].
