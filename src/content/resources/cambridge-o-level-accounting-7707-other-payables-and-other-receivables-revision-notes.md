## What the syllabus asks

1. The importance of matching costs and revenues.
2. Applying the matching / accruals concepts using accruals and
prepayments.
3. Journal entries and ledger accounts for accrued and prepaid expenses.
4. Journal entries and ledger accounts for accrued and prepaid incomes.

## Matching in one line

The statement of profit or loss includes the incomes **earned** and the
expenses **incurred** in the period, not the cash received or paid.

**Why it matters:** profit becomes a fair measure of each period, years can
be compared, and the statement of financial position shows what is owed by
and to the business at the year end.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Where shown |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense incurred but unpaid at the year end | Other payables |
| Prepaid expense (prepayment) | Expense paid for a later period | Other receivables |
| Accrued income | Income earned but not received at the year end | Other receivables |
| Income received in advance (prepaid income) | Income received for a later period | Other payables |
| Other payables | Current liabilities other than trade payables | Current liabilities |
| Other receivables | Current assets other than trade receivables | Current assets |

## Journal entries at the year end

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense account | Other payables |
| Prepaid expense | Other receivables | Expense account |
| Accrued income | Other receivables | Income account |
| Income received in advance | Income account | Other payables |
| Transfer of expense for the year | Statement of profit or loss | Expense account |
| Transfer of income for the year | Income account | Statement of profit or loss |

## Balance brought down: which side?

| Account | Debit b/d | Credit b/d |
|---|---|---|
| Expense account | Prepaid expense | Accrued expense |
| Income account | Accrued income | Income received in advance |

## Calculation frame

```
Expense = paid − owing at start + prepaid at start + owing at end − prepaid at end
Income  = received − owed to us at start + in advance at start
          + owed to us at end − in advance at end
```

**Quick example (expense).** Rates paid $3,300; $250 prepaid at the start;
$400 prepaid at the end. Expense = $3,300 + $250 − $400 = $3,150.

**Quick example (income).** Commission received $2,800; $180 received in
advance at the start; $90 owed to the business at the end. Income = $2,800
+ $180 + $90 = $3,070.

## Time apportionment

- Find the monthly amount first.
- Count the months of the period paid for that fall **after** the year end
(prepaid) or the months used but **not yet paid** (accrued).
- Example: year end 31 December; $1,800 paid on 1 October for 12 months →
9 months prepaid = $1,350; expense for this year from this payment = $450.

## Effect of omitting an adjustment

| Omitted | Profit | Current assets | Current liabilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Overstated | No effect | Understated |
| Prepaid expense | Understated | Understated | No effect |
| Accrued income | Understated | Understated | No effect |
| Income received in advance | Overstated | No effect | Understated |

## One account, two adjustments

An account can open with one kind of balance and close with the other.
Example: rent payable of 00 owing at the start; ,200 paid; 00
prepaid at the end. Expense = ,200 − 00 − 00 = ,500. The opening
balance is a credit (accrual); the closing balance brought down is a debit
(prepayment).

## Exam traps

- Income received in advance is a **liability**, even though the cash is in
the bank.
- An accrued expense brought down sits on the **credit** side.
- The opening balance belongs to the previous year and reverses this year.
- Count months carefully: 1 October to 31 December is three months.
- The statement of profit or loss shows the figure for the year, not the
cash.
- Other payables and other receivables are separate from trade payables and
trade receivables.

## Quick self-test

1. Explain the matching concept.
2. Electricity paid $2,600; $300 owing at the end of the year. Calculate the
expense.
3. Is a prepaid expense an asset or a liability?
4. Rent of $6,000 for 12 months was paid on 1 September. Year end 31
December. How much is prepaid?
5. Give the journal entry for wages accrued of $420.
6. Give the journal entry for commission of $150 owed to the business at
the year end.
7. On which side is income received in advance brought down?
8. Rent received $4,800, including $400 for next year. What is the income?
9. A business forgot to record an accrued expense of $500. Effect on profit?
10. Insurance account: debit balance b/d $200 on 1 January. What does it
represent?
11. Interest received $660; $110 owed at the start; $160 owed at the end.
Calculate the income.
12. Where does income received in advance appear in the statement of
financial position?

### Answers

1. Expenses and incomes are recorded in the period to which they relate,
not when cash is paid or received.
2. $2,600 + $300 = $2,900.
3. An asset (other receivable).
4. 8 months × $500 = $4,000.
5. Dr Wages $420, Cr Other payables $420.
6. Dr Other receivables $150, Cr Commission receivable $150.
7. Credit.
8. $4,800 − $400 = $4,400.
9. Profit overstated by $500.
10. Insurance prepaid at the end of the previous year (an asset).
11. $660 − $110 + $160 = $710.
12. Current liabilities, as other payables.
