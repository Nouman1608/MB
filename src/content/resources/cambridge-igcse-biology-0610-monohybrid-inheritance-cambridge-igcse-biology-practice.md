## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which genotype is homozygous? **[1]**

A: Aa

B: Bb

C: cc

D: Dd

**2.** In a plant, the allele H is dominant to the allele h. What is the expected phenotype ratio when a plant with genotype Hh is crossed with a plant with genotype hh? **[1]**

A: 1 : 1

B: 1 : 2 : 1

C: 3 : 1

D: all show the dominant phenotype

**3.** Describe a recessive allele. **[1]**

**4.** State the alleles carried by the gametes of an organism with the genotype Ff. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Describe codominance. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the blood group of a person with the genotype IᴬIᵒ. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** In tomato plants, the allele for red fruit (R) is dominant to the allele for yellow fruit (r). A heterozygous red-fruited plant is crossed with a yellow-fruited plant.

**(a)** Draw a genetic diagram to show the possible genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring. **[4]**

**(b)** State the percentage of offspring expected to have yellow fruit. **[1]**

**(c)** The cross produces 60 plants. Calculate the number expected to have red fruit. **[1]**

**8.** Two black mice were bred together several times. They produced 31 black offspring and 11 white offspring.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of black to white offspring. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the genotypes of the parents, using B for the dominant allele. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the ratio is not exactly the expected ratio. **[1]**

**9.** Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele, f. The dominant allele is F. A family is described below.

- Persons 1 (male) and 2 (female) do not have cystic fibrosis. Their children are person 3 (a son without cystic fibrosis), person 4 (a daughter with cystic fibrosis) and person 5 (a daughter without cystic fibrosis).
- Person 4 has two children with person 6, a man without cystic fibrosis. Their children, persons 7 and 8, do not have cystic fibrosis.

**(a)** Explain how persons 1, 2 and 4 show that the allele for cystic fibrosis is recessive. **[2]**

**(b)** State the genotypes of person 4 and person 1. **[2]**

**(c)** State the possible genotypes of person 3. **[1]**

**(d)** State the genotype of person 7 and give a reason. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* In rabbits, the allele for black fur (B) is dominant to the allele for white fur (b). A breeder has a black rabbit of unknown genotype.

**(a)** Describe how a test cross could be used to find its genotype, and how the results would be interpreted. **[3]**

**(b)** The test cross produces 8 black and 7 white offspring. State the genotype of the black rabbit. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A mother has blood group A and a father has blood group B. Their first child has blood group O.

**(a)** State the genotypes of the mother, the father and the child. **[3]**

**(b)** State the probability that their next child has blood group AB. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A woman has normal colour vision, but her father was red-green colour-blind. She has children with a man who has normal colour vision. Xᴮ is the allele for normal vision and Xᵇ is the allele for colour blindness.

**(a)** State the woman's genotype and explain how you know it. **[2]**

**(b)** Use a genetic diagram to find the probability that a son of this couple is colour-blind. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why none of their daughters will be colour-blind. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* In a species of plant (an invented example), the alleles Fᴿ for red flowers and Fᵂ for white flowers are codominant. Plants with the genotype FᴿFᵂ have flowers with red and white patches. Two patched plants are crossed.

**(a)** State the expected phenotype ratio of the offspring. **[2]**

**(b)** The cross produces 80 offspring. Calculate the expected number with patched flowers. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]

**2.** **A** [1]

**3.** An allele that is **only expressed when there is no dominant allele** of the gene present in the genotype [1].

**4.** **F or f**: each gamete carries one of them [1].

**5.** Both alleles in a **heterozygous** organism **contribute to the phenotype** [1].

**6.** **Group A** [1]

**7. (a)** Parents' genotypes **Rr × rr** [1]. Gametes **R and r** from the red parent; **r and r** from the yellow parent [1]. Offspring genotypes **Rr and rr**, from a correct Punnett square [1]. Phenotypes: **Rr red, rr yellow**, ratio **1 red : 1 yellow** [1].

**(b)** **50%** [1]

**(c)** 60 × 1/2 = **30** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Gametes must show one allele each; writing "Rr" as one gamete loses the gamete mark and usually the Punnett square mark as well.

**8. (a)** 31 ÷ 11 = 2.8, so about **3 : 1** [1].

**(b)** Both parents are **Bb** [1]. Two black parents produced **white offspring**, so each must carry the recessive allele b; the **3 : 1 ratio** fits Bb × Bb [1].

**(c)** **Fertilisation is random**, so which gametes fuse is a matter of chance; the expected ratio is a probability [1].

**9. (a)** Persons 1 and 2 **do not have** cystic fibrosis, but their daughter, person 4, **does** [1]. She must have received the allele from parents who **carry it without showing it**, so it is recessive [1].

**(b)** Person 4: **ff** [1]. Person 1: **Ff** [1].

**(c)** **FF or Ff** [1]

**(d)** **Ff**: person 7 must have received an **f from the mother, person 4**, who is ff, but does not have cystic fibrosis [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c) both genotypes are needed; nothing in the family shows whether person 3 carries the allele.

**10. (a)** Cross the black rabbit with a **white (homozygous recessive, bb) rabbit** [1]. If **all the offspring are black**, the black rabbit is probably **BB** [1]. If **any offspring are white**, the black rabbit must be **Bb** [1].

**(b)** **Bb** [1]

**11. (a)** Mother **IᴬIᵒ** [1]; father **IᴮIᵒ** [1]; child **IᵒIᵒ** [1].

**(b)** **1/4 / 0.25 / 25%** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The group O child is the evidence: each parent must have passed on Iᵒ, so neither can be homozygous.

**12. (a)** **XᴮXᵇ** [1]. She received **Xᵇ from her colour-blind father**, who has only one X chromosome, but she has normal vision, so her other X carries Xᴮ [1].

**(b)** Gametes: **Xᴮ and Xᵇ** from the woman; **Xᴮ and Y** from the man [1]. Offspring: **XᴮXᴮ, XᴮXᵇ, XᴮY, XᵇY** [1]. Sons are XᴮY or XᵇY, so the probability that a son is colour-blind is **1/2** [1].

**(c)** Every daughter receives **Xᴮ from her father**, so she has at least one allele for normal vision [1].

*Examiner insight:* The question asks about a son, so the answer is 1/2, not the 1/4 chance that any child is a colour-blind son.

**13. (a)** 1 red : 2 patched : 1 white, with the colours correctly matched to the numbers [2].

**(b)** 80 × 2/4 = **40** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a gamete with two alleles.
- Giving a genotype ratio when a phenotype ratio is asked for.
- Stating a pedigree genotype without using the evidence of parents or children.
- (Extended) Using the wrong individual for a test cross.
- (Extended) Writing blood groups or sex-linked alleles without the correct symbols.
- (Extended) Confusing "probability a son is affected" with "probability a child is an affected son".
