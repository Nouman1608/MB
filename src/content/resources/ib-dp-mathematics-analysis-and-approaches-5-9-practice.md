These questions cover **section 5.9 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches: kinematic problems involving displacement, velocity, acceleration and total distance travelled**. It is SL content, so the set suits SL and HL students. In every question a particle moves in a straight line, t is in seconds, s in metres, v in m s⁻¹ and a in m s⁻². Give answers exactly or to 3 significant figures.

Learn the methods first in the [section 5.9 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-9/) and the [section 5.9 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-9-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) The displacement of a particle is s(t) = 2t³ − 3t² − 12t + 5, for t ≥ 0.

**(a)** Find expressions for v(t) and a(t). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time at which the particle is at rest. **[2]**

**2.** (calculator-free) A particle has velocity v(t) = 2e^(−t) − 1, for t ≥ 0. Find the exact time when the particle is at rest, and its acceleration at that time. **[4]**

**3.** (calculator-free) A particle has velocity v(t) = 3 − 2t. When t = 1, s = 5. Find s(t). **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free) A particle has acceleration a(t) = 4 cos 2t, and v(0) = 1. Find v(t), and hence find v(π/4). **[3]**

**5.** (calculator-free) For a particle, at t = 2 the velocity is 5 and the acceleration is −3; at t = 3 the velocity is −2 and the acceleration is −1. State whether the particle is speeding up or slowing down at each time, with a reason. **[2]**

**6.** (calculator-free) A particle has velocity v(t) = 2t − 6, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5. Find the displacement and the total distance travelled over this time. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator-free) A particle starts at the origin O with velocity 12 m s⁻¹. Its acceleration is a(t) = 2t − 8, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 8.

**(a)** Find an expression for v(t). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the times when the particle is at rest. **[2]**
**(c)** Find an expression for s(t). **[2]**
**(d)** Show that the particle is at O when t = 6. **[1]**
**(e)** Find the total distance travelled from t = 0 to t = 8. **[4]**

**8.** (calculator-free) A particle has displacement s(t) = t² e^(−t), for t ≥ 0.

**(a)** Show that v(t) = t(2 − t)e^(−t). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time when the particle changes direction, and its displacement then. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the exact times when the acceleration is zero. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the exact total distance travelled from t = 0 to t = 4. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator-free) A particle has velocity v(t) = 6 sin 3t, for 0 ≤ t ≤ π/2.

**(a)** Find the time when the particle changes direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the displacement from t = 0 to t = π/2. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the total distance travelled from t = 0 to t = π/2. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) A particle has velocity v(t) = 3 cos 2t + t, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 4.

**(a)** Find the first time at which the particle is at rest. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the acceleration at t = 1, and determine whether the particle is speeding up at that time. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the displacement from t = 0 to t = 4. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the total distance travelled from t = 0 to t = 4. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A particle has velocity v(t) = t² e^(−0.5t) − 1, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10.

**(a)** Find the times when the particle changes direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the greatest speed of the particle, justifying your answer. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the total distance travelled. **[2]**

**12.** (calculator-free) The velocity–time graph of a particle, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10, is made of three straight line segments: from (0, 0) to (2, 6), from (2, 6) to (6, −2), and from (6, −2) to (10, −2). The particle starts at O.

**(a)** Write down the acceleration at t = 4. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the time when the particle changes direction. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the displacement of the particle from O when t = 10. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the total distance travelled. **[2]**
**(e)** Find the greatest distance of the particle from O, and the time at which it occurs. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **v(t) = 6t² − 6t − 12** [1]; **a(t) = 12t − 6** [1]
**(b)** 6(t − 2)(t + 1) = 0 [1]; t ≥ 0, so **t = 2** [1] **[4]**

**2.** 2e^(−t) = 1, so **t = ln 2** [1] [1]; a(t) = −2e^(−t) [1]; a(ln 2) = −2 × 1/2 = **−1 m s⁻²** [1] **[4]**

**3.** s(t) = 3t − t² + c [1]; 5 = 3 − 1 + c, so c = 3 [1]; **s(t) = 3t − t² + 3** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The condition is at t = 1, not t = 0, so c is not simply 5.

**4.** v(t) = 2 sin 2t + c [1]; v(0) = 1 gives c = 1, so **v(t) = 2 sin 2t + 1** [1]; v(π/4) = 2 sin(π/2) + 1 = **3** [1] **[3]**

**5.** At t = 2, v > 0 and a < 0 (opposite signs), so **slowing down** [1]; at t = 3, v < 0 and a < 0 (same sign), so **speeding up** [1] **[2]**

**6.** Displacement = ∫₀⁵ (2t − 6) dt = [t² − 6t]₀⁵ = **−5 m** [1]; v = 0 at t = 3 [1]; ∫₀³ v dt = −9 and ∫₃⁵ v dt = 4 [1]; distance = 9 + 4 = **13 m** [1] **[4]**

**7. (a)** v(t) = t² − 8t + c [1]; v(0) = 12 [1], so **v(t) = t² − 8t + 12** [1]
**(b)** (t − 2)(t − 6) = 0 [1]; **t = 2 and t = 6** [1]
**(c)** s(t) = t³/3 − 4t² + 12t + k, and s(0) = 0 so k = 0 [1]; **s(t) = t³/3 − 4t² + 12t** [1]
**(d)** s(6) = 72 − 144 + 72 = 0 [1]
**(e)** s(2) = 32/3, s(6) = 0, s(8) = 512/3 − 256 + 96 = 32/3 [1] [1]; distance = 32/3 + 32/3 + 32/3 [1] = **32 m** [1] **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* The displacement over 0 ≤ t ≤ 8 is only 32/3 m; integrating v without splitting at t = 2 and t = 6 gives this value and loses most of the marks in (e).

**8. (a)** Product rule: v(t) = 2t e^(−t) − t² e^(−t) [1] = t(2 − t)e^(−t) [1]
**(b)** v changes from + to − at **t = 2** [1] [1]; s(2) = **4e⁻² m** [1]
**(c)** a(t) = (2 − 2t)e^(−t) − (2t − t²)e^(−t) = (t² − 4t + 2)e^(−t) [1]; t² − 4t + 2 = 0 [1]; **t = 2 ± √2** [1]
**(d)** s(0) = 0, s(2) = 4e⁻², s(4) = 16e⁻⁴ [1]; distance = 4e⁻² + (4e⁻² − 16e⁻⁴) [1] = **8e⁻² − 16e⁻⁴ m** [1] **[11]**

**9. (a)** sin 3t = 0 with sign change [1]: **t = π/3** [1]
**(b)** ∫₀^(π/2) 6 sin 3t dt = [−2 cos 3t]₀^(π/2) [1] = −2 cos(3π/2) + 2 cos 0 [1] = **2 m** [1]
**(c)** ∫₀^(π/3) v dt = 4 and ∫ from π/3 to π/2 of v dt = −2 [1]; distance = 4 + 2 = **6 m** [1] **[7]**

**10. (a)** Using the GDC, v(t) = 0 [1] first when **t = 0.946 s** (0.94576…) [1]
**(b)** a(t) = −6 sin 2t + 1 [1]; a(1) = **−4.46 m s⁻²** [1]; v(1) = −0.248 < 0, so v and a have the same sign and the particle is **speeding up** [1]
**(c)** ∫₀⁴ (3 cos 2t + t) dt [1] = **9.48 m** (9.4840…) [1]
**(d)** ∫₀⁴ |3 cos 2t + t| dt [1] = **11.5 m** (11.496…) [1] **[9]**

**11. (a)** Using the GDC, v = 0 with a sign change at **t = 1.43 s and t = 8.61 s** [1] [1]
**(b)** a(t) = (2t − 0.5t²)e^(−0.5t) = 0 at t = 4 (t = 0 also, but v(0) = −1) [1]; v(4) = 16e⁻² − 1 = 1.165… [1]; end points: |v(0)| = 1, |v(10)| = 0.326 [1]; greatest speed **1.17 m s⁻¹** at t = 4 [1]
**(c)** ∫₀¹⁰ |v(t)| dt [1] = **6.19 m** (6.1873…) [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Greatest speed means the largest |v|, so the end points and any negative minimum of v must be compared, not just the maximum of v.

**12. (a)** Gradient of the middle segment: (−2 − 6)/(6 − 2) = **−2 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** The middle segment crosses v = 0 at **t = 5** [1]
**(c)** Signed areas: 0 to 2: 6; 2 to 5: 9; 5 to 6: −1; 6 to 10: −8 [1] [1]; displacement = 6 + 9 − 1 − 8 = **6 m** [1]
**(d)** 6 + 9 + 1 + 8 [1] = **24 m** [1]
**(e)** The particle moves in the positive direction until t = 5, then back [1]; greatest distance **15 m, at t = 5** (at t = 10 it is only 6 m from O) [1] **[9]**
