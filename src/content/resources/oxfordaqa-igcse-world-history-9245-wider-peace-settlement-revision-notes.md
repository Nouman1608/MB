## Treaty by treaty

**Saint-Germain (Austria):** recognised Czechoslovakia, Poland, Hungary and Yugoslavia; South Tyrol and Trieste to Italy; Bohemia and Moravia to Czechoslovakia; *union with Germany forbidden*; army 30,000. Result: small landlocked state; economic crisis; League loan by 1922.

**Neuilly (Bulgaria):** Western Thrace to Greece (lost Aegean coast); land to Yugoslavia; army 20,000; reparations. Result: refugees, hostility to neighbours.

**Trianon (Hungary):** about two-thirds of territory lost; Transylvania to Romania; Slovakia and Ruthenia to Czechoslovakia; Croatia to Yugoslavia; army 35,000; over three million Hungarians outside the new borders. Result: deep resentment ("No, no, never!"); later drift towards Germany and Italy.

**Sèvres (Ottoman Empire):** Arab lands as mandates (Syria and Lebanon to France; Iraq and Palestine to Britain); Smyrna under Greek administration; eastern Thrace to Greece; Straits internationalised; Armenia independent. Result: rejected by Kemal's nationalists; never enforced.

**Lausanne (Turkey):** Turkey kept Anatolia, Smyrna and eastern Thrace; no reparations; foreign privileges ended; Straits demilitarised but open; Greek-Turkish population exchange; mandates unchanged. Significance: the only treaty negotiated rather than imposed; showed a treaty could be overturned by force.

## Strengths and difficulties

| Strengths | Difficulties |
|---|---|
| New nations for Poles, Czechs, Slovaks, Romanians, South Slavs | Minorities in every new state, e.g. about three million German speakers in Czechoslovakia |
| Mostly democratic new states; minority treaties | Old trading area broken by borders and tariffs |
| League supervision of mandates and minorities | Austria and Hungary too weak to stand alone |
| Lasting Greek-Turkish border after Lausanne | Italy's "mutilated victory"; Fiume seized |
| | Arab hopes of independence replaced by mandates |
| | Revisionism in Hungary and Bulgaria |

## Turning knowledge into source answers

Section A is entirely source-based, so learn facts as **tests** for sources:

- A source praising self-determination: test it with the German speakers in Czechoslovakia or the Hungarians in Romania.
- A source calling Austria doomed: support it with the ban on union and the League loan; qualify it with Austria's survival through the 1920s.
- A source on Turkey: check the date. Before September 1922 Sèvres stood; after it, the nationalists had the upper hand.
- Provenance shortcuts: Czech, Polish or Romanian sources tend to defend the borders; Austrian, Hungarian, Bulgarian and Turkish nationalist sources tend to attack them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Self-determination | The right of peoples to govern themselves |
| Successor states | States formed from the collapsed empires |
| Mandate | Territory governed by a League member on the League's behalf |
| Minority treaty | Agreement by a new state to protect minorities |
| Revisionism | The aim of overturning a treaty |
| Landlocked | Having no coastline |
| Population exchange | Agreed transfer of peoples between states |
| "Mutilated victory" | Italian nationalist complaint about the settlement |
| The Straits | The Dardanelles and Bosphorus linking the Black Sea and Mediterranean |

## Exam traps

- Confusing Trianon (Hungary) and Saint-Germain (Austria).
- Saying Sèvres was enforced: it was replaced by Lausanne.
- Assuming the victors were all satisfied: Italy was not.
- Forgetting the strengths of the treaties.
- In source questions, describing the treaty instead of using knowledge to test the source.
- Ignoring provenance: a Hungarian and a Czech source on the same border will disagree for clear reasons.

## Self-test

1. Which treaty dealt with Austria?
2. What did that treaty forbid Austria to do?
3. Which treaty dealt with Hungary, and roughly how much territory did Hungary lose?
4. Which state gained Transylvania?
5. What did Bulgaria lose to Greece under the Treaty of Neuilly?
6. What was a mandate?
7. Which Arab lands became French mandates?
8. Who led the Turkish nationalists who rejected Sèvres?
9. Give two terms of the Treaty of Lausanne.
10. Why is Lausanne different from the other peace treaties?
11. Why did Italian nationalists call the settlement a "mutilated victory"?
12. Give one strength and one difficulty of the wider settlement.

## Answers

1. Saint-Germain (1919).
2. Unite with Germany.
3. Trianon (1920); about two-thirds.
4. Romania.
5. Western Thrace and its Aegean coastline.
6. A territory governed by a League member on the League's behalf.
7. Syria and Lebanon.
8. Mustafa Kemal.
9. Any two: Turkey kept Anatolia, Smyrna and eastern Thrace; no reparations; foreign privileges ended; Straits demilitarised but open; Greek-Turkish population exchange.
10. It was negotiated with the defeated state rather than imposed, after Turkey overturned Sèvres by force.
11. Italy did not receive all the land, such as Dalmatia, promised in 1915.
12. Strength: new nations for peoples such as Czechs and Poles, or minority treaties. Difficulty: minorities in new states, economic disruption, weak states, revisionism or Arab disappointment.
