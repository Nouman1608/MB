## Questions

**1.** Four angles meet at a point. They are 5a°, (2a + 10)°, (3a − 20)° and 100°. Find the value of a and the size of the smallest angle. **[3]**

**2.** Lines PQ and RS are parallel. A line crosses PQ at X and RS at Y. Angle QXY is (3t + 14)° and angle XYR is (5t − 30)°. These two angles are alternate.

**(a)** Find the value of t, and the size of angle QXY. **[3]**
**(b)** Angle XYS lies on the line RS, next to angle XYR. Find angle XYS, giving a reason. **[2]**

**3.** A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 15°.

**(a)** Work out the number of sides. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the sum of its interior angles. **[2]**

**4.** A pentagon has angles 2x°, (x + 30)°, 115°, (3x − 25)° and 90°. Find x and the largest angle. **[4]**

**5.** **(a)** A right-angled triangle has its two acute angles equal to (2z + 5)° and 3z°. Find z. **[2]**
**(b)** Triangle ABC has AB = AC. Angle ABC is (3p + 8)° and angle BAC is (4p + 14)°. Find p and angle BAC. **[3]**

**6.** **(a)** ABCD is a rhombus with perimeter 36 cm. The diagonals meet at M. Angle BAC is 29°. Find the length AB, angle ABC and angle AMB. **[4]**
**(b)** In trapezium PQRS, PQ is parallel to SR. Angle SPQ is (3x + 20)° and angle PSR is (2x + 10)°. Find x, then find angle PQR when angle QRS is 120°. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** For each shape give the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotation symmetry: a rectangle, a kite, a regular octagon. **[3]**
**(b)** Name a quadrilateral with rotation symmetry of order 2 but no line of symmetry. **[1]**
**(c)** A regular polygon has interior angle 140°. State its number of lines of symmetry. **[2]**

**8.** A regular hexagon, a square and a regular polygon with n sides fit together exactly around a point, one corner of each meeting there with no gaps. Find n. **[4]**

**9.** ABCD is a parallelogram, with AB parallel to DC and AD parallel to BC. Prove that angle ABC equals angle ADC. **[4]**

**10.** Kite ABCD has AB = AD and CB = CD. Prove that angle ABC = angle ADC by considering the diagonal BD. **[5]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** A, B and C are points on a circle with centre O. O lies inside the acute angle ABC. Prove that angle AOC = 2 × angle ABC. **[5]**

**12.** ABCDE is a regular pentagon. An equilateral triangle CDF is drawn outside the pentagon on side CD.

**(a)** Work out the interior angle of the pentagon. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out angle BCF. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out angle CBF, giving a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 5a + (2a + 10) + (3a − 20) + 100 = 360 (angles at a point) [1]; 10a + 90 = 360, so a = 27 [1]. The angles are 135°, 64°, 61°, 100°, so the smallest is **61°** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the fact you use to form the equation, and check your answer by adding all the angles back to 360°.

**2. (a)** Alternate angles are equal: 3t + 14 = 5t − 30 [1]; 44 = 2t, so **t = 22** [1]; angle QXY = 3 × 22 + 14 = **80°** [1].
**(b)** Angle XYS = 180 − 80 [1] = **100°** (angles on a line) [1].
*Examiner insight:* a reason is part of the answer on a "give a reason" part, so quote the named fact rather than only the number.

**3. (a)** n = 360 ÷ 15 [1] = **24** [1].
**(b)** (24 − 2) × 180 [1] = **3960°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the (n − 2) × 180 substitution. A bare number is harder to credit if it is wrong.

**4.** Sum of interior angles = (5 − 2) × 180 = 540° [1]; 2x + x + 30 + 115 + 3x − 25 + 90 = 540, so 6x + 210 = 540 [1]; x = 55 [1]. Angles: 110°, 85°, 115°, 140°, 90°. Largest = **140°** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the question asks for the largest angle, not x. Reread the question and answer what it asks.

**5. (a)** (2z + 5) + 3z = 90 (the acute angles of a right-angled triangle sum to 90°) [1]; 5z = 85, so **z = 17** [1].
**(b)** Base angles equal and angles sum to 180°: 2(3p + 8) + 4p + 14 = 180 [1]; 10p + 30 = 180, **p = 15** [1]; angle BAC = 4 × 15 + 14 = **74°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in an isosceles triangle, say which two angles are equal and why, before you write the equation.

**6. (a)** All sides of a rhombus are equal, so AB = 36 ÷ 4 = **9 cm** [1]. Triangle ABC is isosceles (AB = BC), so angle BCA = 29° [1]; angle ABC = 180 − 29 − 29 = **122°** [1]. The diagonals of a rhombus meet at right angles, so angle AMB = **90°** [1].
**(b)** Co-interior angles between parallel lines: 3x + 20 + 2x + 10 = 180 [1]; **x = 30**, so angle SPQ = 110° [1]. Angle PQR + angle QRS = 180° (co-interior), so angle PQR = 180 − 120 = **60°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* quote the property by name (diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular) rather than relying on how the diagram looks.

**7. (a)** Rectangle: 2 lines, order 2 [1]. Kite: 1 line, order 1 [1]. Regular octagon: 8 lines, order 8 [1].
**(b)** **Parallelogram** [1].
**(c)** Exterior angle = 180 − 140 = 40° [1], so n = 360 ÷ 40 = 9 and there are **9 lines of symmetry** [1].
*Examiner insight:* give both numbers for each shape. Order 1 is the correct way to say a shape has no rotation symmetry.

**8.** The interior angle of a regular hexagon is 120° [1] and of a square is 90° [1]. The third angle is 360 − 120 − 90 = 150° [1]. Exterior angle = 30°, so n = 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the angle at the point is an interior angle of the new polygon. Convert it to an exterior angle before dividing.

**9.** Let angle ABC = b. AD ∥ BC, so angle DAB + angle ABC = 180° (co-interior angles) [1]; so angle DAB = 180 − b [1]. AB ∥ DC, so angle DAB + angle ADC = 180° (co-interior angles) [1]; so angle ADC = 180 − (180 − b) = b. Hence angle ABC = angle ADC [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* a proof needs a reason on every line and a closing statement of what has been shown. Do not use numbers from the diagram.

**10.** In triangle ABD, AB = AD, so angle ABD = angle ADB (base angles of an isosceles triangle) [1]. In triangle CBD, CB = CD, so angle CBD = angle CDB (base angles of an isosceles triangle) [1]. Angle ABC = angle ABD + angle CBD [1] and angle ADC = angle ADB + angle CDB [1]. Adding equal angles gives angle ABC = angle ADC [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* write which sides are equal and name the triangle each time. A proof needs both the statement and its reason to be visible.

**11.** (Higher tier only) OA = OB = OC (radii) [1]. Let angle ABO = u and angle CBO = v, so angle ABC = u + v. Triangle OAB is isosceles, so angle OAB = u (base angles) [1]. Extend BO to meet the circle at D. Angle AOD = u + u = 2u (exterior angle of triangle OAB) [1]. Similarly angle OCB = v, so angle COD = 2v [1]. Angle AOC = 2u + 2v = 2 × angle ABC [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* introduce your own letters for the base angles and carry them through. Each line needs its reason, and the last line must state the result you were asked to prove.

**12. (a)** (5 − 2) × 180 ÷ 5 [1] = **108°** [1].
**(b)** Angle BCF = 108 + 60 [1] = **168°** (angles at C, with angle DCF = 60° in an equilateral triangle) [1].
**(c)** CF = CD = CB (sides of the equilateral triangle and the regular pentagon), so triangle BCF is isosceles [1]; angle CBF = (180 − 168) ÷ 2 = **6°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* look for equal lengths first. Spotting CB = CF turns a hard-looking angle into a base-angle calculation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving an angle with no reason, or a vague reason such as "parallel lines".
- Using the wrong parallel-line fact (alternate and corresponding mixed up).
- Using 360° for a straight line or 180° for angles at a point.
- Finding an exterior angle and stopping before the interior angle is asked for.
- Answering for x when the question asks for an angle.
- Treating a diagram as accurate when it is not.
- Writing "no symmetry" when the shape has rotation symmetry of order 2, as a parallelogram does.
- Ending a proof without stating what has been proved, or using measured values.
