## Section A

**1.** A compass is placed under a long straight wire. State what happens to the compass needle when a current is switched on in the wire, and what happens when the current is then reversed. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the construction of a simple electromagnet. **[3]**

**3.** Sketch, in words, the magnetic field pattern around a flat circular coil carrying a current, saying where the field is strongest. **[2]**

**4.** A beam of charged particles passes through a magnetic field without being deflected. Suggest why. **[1]**

**5.** A wire carrying a current lies between the poles of a magnet. The magnetic field runs from left to right across the page. The current flows into the page. Use the left-hand rule to state the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**

**6.** State two changes, each made on its own, that would increase the force on a current-carrying wire placed at right angles to a magnetic field. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student winds 50 turns of insulated copper wire round a soft iron nail and connects it to a cell and a switch. She records how many paper clips the nail can lift.

**(a)** Predict what happens to the number of paper clips lifted when she winds on 50 more turns, keeping the current the same. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** State why the wire must be insulated. **[1]**

**(c)** When the switch is opened, almost all the paper clips drop off. Explain why. **[2]**

**(d)** Describe the magnetic field pattern inside the coil when the current flows. **[2]**

**8.** A beam of electrons travels from left to right across the page through a magnetic field directed into the page.

**(a)** State the direction of the conventional current represented by the electron beam. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the left-hand rule to find the direction in which the beam is deflected. **[2]**

**(c)** The magnetic field is now turned so that it points from left to right, along the beam. State and explain what happens to the beam. **[2]**

**9.** A simple d.c. motor has a rectangular coil that rotates between the poles of a permanent magnet.

**(a)** Explain why the coil experiences a turning effect when a current flows. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the motor needs a split-ring commutator. **[3]**

**(c)** Give two changes that would make the coil rotate faster. **[2]**

**(d)** State one change that would make the coil rotate in the opposite direction. **[1]**

**10.** A loudspeaker contains a coil attached to a cone, placed in the field of a permanent magnet.

**(a)** The current in the coil is alternating with a frequency of 440 Hz. State the frequency of the sound produced and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** The amplitude of the alternating current is increased. Describe and explain the effect on the sound. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why the loudspeaker would not produce a continuous sound if it were connected to a steady direct current. **[2]**

**11.** A wire is placed between two magnets that produce a uniform field. The wire can be rotated so that it lies at different angles to the field. Describe how the force on the wire changes as the wire is turned from lying at right angles to the field to lying parallel to the field. **[2]**

**12.** A student says: "In a motor, reversing the current and reversing the magnet together makes the motor run backwards twice as fast." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The needle is deflected (turns to line up with the circular field round the wire) [1]; when the current is reversed, the needle deflects the opposite way [1].

**2.** A coil of insulated wire [1] wound round a soft iron core [1], connected to a supply (with a switch) so that a current flows through the coil [1].

**3.** Circular field lines round each side of the coil, in opposite directions on the two sides [1]; the lines pass almost straight through the centre of the coil, where the field is strongest [1].

**4.** The particles are moving parallel to the magnetic field (so no force acts on them) [1].

**5.** Towards the bottom of the page (downwards) [1].

**6.** Increase the current in the wire [1]; use a stronger magnetic field [1].

**7. (a)** More paper clips are lifted [1], because more turns make the electromagnet's field stronger [1].
**(b)** To stop the current taking a short cut between turns that touch (or through the iron nail), so it flows all the way round the coil [1].
**(c)** With no current, the coil produces no field [1]; iron is magnetically soft, so it loses almost all its induced magnetism and can no longer hold the clips [1].
**(d)** Straight, parallel field lines [1], equally spaced, showing a strong, nearly uniform field [1].

**8. (a)** From right to left (opposite to the electron motion) [1].
**(b)** First finger into the page, second finger pointing left [1]; the thumb points towards the bottom of the page, so the beam is deflected downwards [1].
**(c)** The beam is not deflected [1], because the electrons now move parallel to the field, so there is no force on them [1].

**9. (a)** The two sides of the coil at right angles to the field carry current in opposite directions [1]; each side experiences a force (motor effect) [1]; the forces are in opposite directions, one up and one down, producing a turning effect about the axle [1].
**(b)** Without it, the forces would reverse their turning effect after the coil passes the vertical, so the coil would rock back and forth rather than rotate [1]. The commutator reverses the current in the coil every half turn [1], so the force on each side reverses and the coil keeps turning in the same direction [1].
**(c)** Any two from: increase the current [1]; use a stronger magnet [1]; add more turns to the coil [1]; maximum 2.
**(d)** Reverse the current (swap the supply connections) or reverse the magnetic field (swap the poles) [1].

**10. (a)** 440 Hz [1], because the force on the coil reverses each time the current reverses, so the cone vibrates at the same frequency as the current [1].
**(b)** The sound is louder [1]; a larger current gives a larger force on the coil [1], so the cone vibrates with a larger amplitude, producing a sound wave of larger amplitude [1].
**(c)** A steady current gives a constant force in one direction [1], so the cone would move once to a new position and stay there instead of vibrating [1].

**11.** The force is largest when the wire is at right angles to the field [1] and decreases to zero when the wire lies parallel to the field [1].

**12.** The statement is wrong [1]. Reversing the current reverses the force, and reversing the field reverses it again, so the force is back in its original direction and the motor turns the same way as before [1]. The size of the force depends on the size of the current and the strength of the field, which have not changed, so the speed does not double [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using electron flow instead of conventional current in the left-hand rule (Question 8).
- Saying the commutator "reverses the coil" instead of the current.
- Describing the turning effect without stating that the forces on the two sides are in opposite directions.
- For the loudspeaker, missing the step "force reverses when the current reverses".
- Forgetting that motion parallel to a field gives no force.
