Condensed recall for section **6.2 Stars and the Universe**, Cambridge O Level Physics 5054, 2026–2028 series. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Stars and the Universe study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-physics-5054-stars-and-the-universe/); exam-style questions are in the [Stars and the Universe practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-physics-5054-stars-and-the-universe-practice/).

## The Sun as a star (6.2.1)

- **Size:** medium.
- **Composition:** mostly **hydrogen and helium**.
- **Radiation:** most energy in the **infrared, visible and ultraviolet** regions.
- **Power source:** nuclear reactions that release energy; in stable stars, **fusion of hydrogen into helium**.
- **Fusion** (section 5.2.4): two smaller nuclei combine to form a larger nucleus, releasing energy.

Not fission, not chemical burning.

## Galaxies and distances (6.2.2)

- A **galaxy** is made up of many billions of stars.
- The Sun is a star in the **Milky Way**.
- Other Milky Way stars are **much further** from the Earth than the Sun is.
- **Light-year:** the **distance** travelled in a vacuum by light in one year.

```
1 year         = 365 × 24 × 3600  = 3.15 × 10⁷ s
1 light-year   = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 3.15 × 10⁷ = 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m

distance in light-years = years that light spends travelling
```

Sense of scale: Sun–Earth ≈ 500 light-seconds (1.5 × 10¹¹ m); one light-year is about 63 000 times further.

## Life cycle of a star (6.2.2)

```
interstellar cloud of gas and dust (contains hydrogen)
        │  internal gravitational attraction → collapse, temperature rises
        ▼
protostar
        │  inward gravity balanced by outward force from high central temperature
        ▼
stable star (hydrogen → helium fusion)
        │  most central hydrogen converted to helium
        ├───────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                               ▼
   most stars:                    more massive stars:
   RED GIANT                      RED SUPERGIANT
        │                               │
        ▼                               ▼
   planetary nebula               supernova → nebula of hydrogen
   + WHITE DWARF at centre          + new heavier elements
                                  + NEUTRON STAR or BLACK HOLE at centre
                                        │
                                        ▼
                                  nebula may form new stars with orbiting planets
```

**Stability in one sentence:** inward gravitational attraction is balanced by an outward force due to the high temperature at the centre.

**Why the protostar heats up:** it is collapsing under its own internal gravitational attraction.

## The Universe (6.2.3)

- The Milky Way is one of **many billions of galaxies** in the Universe.
- Milky Way diameter ≈ **100 000 light-years** (≈ 9.5 × 10²⁰ m).
- **Redshift:** an **increase in the observed wavelength** of electromagnetic radiation emitted from **receding** stars and galaxies.
- Light from distant galaxies shows redshift. **Further away → greater redshift → faster speed away from the Earth.**

**Big Bang evidence, four links:**

1. Distant galaxies show redshift, so they are moving away.
2. Greater distance goes with greater redshift and faster recession.
3. So the Universe is expanding.
4. Traced back, everything began together in a very small region: the Big Bang.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nuclear fusion | Two smaller nuclei combine into a larger nucleus, releasing energy |
| Galaxy | A system of many billions of stars |
| Milky Way | The galaxy containing the Sun |
| Light-year | Distance travelled by light in a vacuum in one year |
| Interstellar cloud | Cloud of gas and dust, containing hydrogen, from which stars form |
| Protostar | Interstellar cloud collapsing and heating under its internal gravitational attraction |
| Stable star | Star in which inward gravity balances the outward force from its high central temperature |
| Red giant / red supergiant | Expanded star after most central hydrogen is converted to helium; supergiants come from more massive stars |
| Planetary nebula | Material ejected by a red giant of a less massive star, with a white dwarf at its centre |
| Supernova | Explosion of a red supergiant |
| Redshift | Increase in observed wavelength of radiation from a receding source |

## Exam traps

- **A light-year is a distance**, never a time.
- **"Medium-sized"**, not "large" or "small", for the Sun.
- **Infrared, visible, ultraviolet**: do not add radio waves, X-rays or gamma rays.
- **Fusion of hydrogen into helium**, not "fusion of helium", not fission.
- **Red giant vs red supergiant**: the more massive star forms the supergiant.
- **White dwarf** comes from the less massive star; **neutron star or black hole** from the supernova.
- **Supernova nebula** contains hydrogen **and** new heavier elements.
- **Redshift** is a longer observed wavelength, not light "turning red".
- **Receding** must appear in a redshift definition.
- **Milky Way ≠ Universe.**

## Self-test

1. State the size and the two main elements of the Sun.
2. Name the three regions of the electromagnetic spectrum in which the Sun radiates most of its energy.
3. What nuclear reaction powers stable stars?
4. Define a light-year.
5. A star is 12 light-years away. Calculate its distance in metres.
6. What is a protostar, and why does its temperature rise?
7. State the condition for a star to be stable.
8. What happens to a star when most of the hydrogen at its centre has been converted to helium?
9. Name what is left at the centre after (a) a red giant from a less massive star and (b) a supernova.
10. Define redshift.
11. Galaxy F shows a greater redshift than galaxy G. What can you say about their speeds and distances?
12. State the approximate diameter of the Milky Way.

**Answers**

1. Medium-sized; hydrogen and helium.
2. Infrared, visible and ultraviolet.
3. Fusion of hydrogen into helium.
4. The distance travelled in a vacuum by light in one year.
5. 12 × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = 1.1 × 10¹⁷ m.
6. An interstellar cloud collapsing under its own internal gravitational attraction; the collapse makes its temperature increase.
7. The inward force of gravitational attraction is balanced by an outward force due to the high temperature at the centre.
8. It expands: most stars form a red giant, more massive stars form a red supergiant.
9. (a) A white dwarf, within a planetary nebula. (b) A neutron star or a black hole.
10. An increase in the observed wavelength of electromagnetic radiation emitted from receding stars and galaxies.
11. F is moving away faster than G and is further away.
12. About 100 000 light-years.
