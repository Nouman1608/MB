## Section A

**1.** Match each quantity to its unit: charge, current, power, resistance. Units: ampere, coulomb, ohm, watt. **[2]**

**2.** State the unit of voltage and express it in terms of the joule and the coulomb. **[2]**

**3.** Convert:

**(a)** 45 mA to amperes **[1]**
**(b)** 6.8 kΩ to ohms **[1]**
**(c)** 0.25 hours to seconds **[1]**

**4.** A student writes "a current of 3 A means 3 C of charge". Explain what is missing from this statement. **[2]**

**5.** Show that the unit A × V is the same as the watt. **[2]**

**6.** Which of these is a unit of energy: A, C, J, W? **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A phone charger supplies a current of 1.5 A at 5.0 V for 40 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred, in kJ. **[3]**

**8.** A light-emitting diode in a circuit has a current of 15 mA through it and a voltage of 2.1 V across it.

**(a)** Calculate the power of the LED, in W. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance of the LED at this current. **[2]**

**9.** A student uses E = Q × V and rearranges it to find the charge, writing Q = E × V.

**(a)** Use units to show that this rearrangement is incorrect. **[2]**
**(b)** A 9.0 V battery transfers 540 J. Use the correct rearrangement to find the charge that passes. **[2]**

**10.** A 2.4 kW electric shower runs from 240 V for 8.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the current through the shower. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred, giving your answer in MJ. **[3]**

**11.** A resistor of 1.2 kΩ is connected to a 6.0 V supply.

**(a)** Calculate the current through the resistor in mA. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge passing through it in 5.0 minutes. **[2]**

**12.** A cell transfers 360 J of energy to a circuit while 240 C of charge passes through it.

**(a)** Calculate the voltage of the cell. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy and charge, what this voltage means. **[1]**
**(c)** The same charge passes in 4.0 minutes. Calculate the current. **[2]**

**13.** A data sheet for a sensor lists: "supply 3.3 V, current 120 μA".

**(a)** Convert the current to amperes in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the power of the sensor. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy it uses in one day (24 hours). **[3]**

**14.** Four students each give an answer to a different calculation. For each one, state whether the unit is correct for the quantity, and give the correct unit where it is not.

**(a)** "The charge that flowed was 36 A." **[1]**
**(b)** "The resistance of the lamp is 12 Ω." **[1]**
**(c)** "The power of the motor is 450 J." **[1]**
**(d)** "The energy transferred to each coulomb is 6.0 J/C." **[1]**

---

## Answers

**1.** charge: coulomb; current: ampere; power: watt; resistance: ohm. All four correct [2]; two or three correct [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The volt (V) [1]; 1 V = 1 J/C (a joule per coulomb) [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 0.045 A [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 6800 Ω [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 0.25 × 3600 = 900 s [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Current is the rate of flow of charge, so it must refer to time [1]: 3 A means 3 C of charge passing a point **each second** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 1 A = 1 C/s and 1 V = 1 J/C [1]; A × V = (C/s) × (J/C) = J/s, which is the watt [1]. **[2]**

**6.** J (joule) [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** t = 40 × 60 = 2400 s [1]; Q = I × t = 1.5 × 2400 [1] = 3600 C [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** E = I × V × t = 1.5 × 5.0 × 2400 [1] = 18 000 J [1] = 18 kJ [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** I = 0.015 A; P = I × V = 0.015 × 2.1 [1] = 0.0315 W, so 0.032 W [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** R = V ÷ I = 2.1 ÷ 0.015 [1] = 140 Ω [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** E × V has units J × J/C = J²/C [1], which is not the coulomb, the unit of charge, so the rearrangement is wrong [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Q = E ÷ V = 540 ÷ 9.0 [1] = 60 C [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** I = P ÷ V = 2400 ÷ 240 [1] = 10 A [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** t = 8.0 × 60 = 480 s [1]; E = I × V × t = 10 × 240 × 480 = 1 152 000 J [1] = 1.2 MJ (1.15 MJ) [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** R = 1200 Ω [1]; I = V ÷ R = 6.0 ÷ 1200 = 0.0050 A [1] = 5.0 mA [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Q = I × t = 0.0050 × 300 [1] = 1.5 C [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** V = E ÷ Q = 360 ÷ 240 [1] = 1.5 V [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The cell transfers 1.5 J of energy to each coulomb of charge passing through it [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** t = 240 s; I = Q ÷ t = 240 ÷ 240 [1] = 1.0 A [1]. **[2]**

**13. (a)** 120 μA = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ A [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** P = I × V = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ × 3.3 [1] = 3.96 × 10⁻⁴ W, about 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ W [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** t = 24 × 3600 = 86 400 s [1]; E = I × V × t = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ × 3.3 × 86 400 [1] = 34 J (34.2 J) [1]. **[3]**

**14. (a)** Incorrect: charge is measured in coulombs, C; the ampere is the unit of current [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Correct: resistance is measured in ohms [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Incorrect: power is measured in watts, W (joules per second); the joule is the unit of energy [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Correct: energy per coulomb is a voltage, and 6.0 J/C is 6.0 V [1]. **[1]**
