## Section A

**1.** Identify three weaknesses of Trotsky as a candidate to succeed Lenin. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two ways in which the post of General Secretary helped Stalin. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish between Socialism in One Country and Permanent Revolution. **[2]**

**4.** State three reasons Stalin gave, or had, for launching the Great Turn. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two consequences of the murder of Kirov in December 1934. **[4]**

**6.** Identify three methods the regime used to discipline industrial workers in the 1930s. **[3]**

**7.** Give three examples of the "Great Retreat" in social policy. **[3]**

**8.** Identify three steps in Soviet foreign policy towards collective security, 1934-36. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Ideological arguments were the most important factor in Stalin's victory in the power struggle." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the effectiveness of propaganda and the cult of personality in securing Stalin's control, 1929-41. **[30]**

**11.** "The Five Year Plans achieved their aims." Assess this view. **[30]**

**12.** Assess the extent to which Stalin's policies changed the lives of Soviet women, 1928-41. **[30]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: he had been a Menshevik until 1917 and was seen as an outsider by Old Bolsheviks (1); his arrogance and poor relations with colleagues (1); fear that as Commissar for War he could become a "Bonaparte" using the Red Army (1); his absence from Lenin's funeral (1); his association with War Communism and militarising labour (1); his failure to organise a following in the party apparatus (1). **[3]**

**2.** Two ways, 2 marks each: control of appointments through the Secretariat and Orgburo let him place loyal local secretaries, who chose delegates to party congresses, so he could win votes (2); access to party files and information gave him knowledge of members' records and weaknesses (2); the Lenin enrolment of 1924 brought in new members who owed their positions to the apparatus he ran (2). **[4]**

**3.** Socialism in One Country held that the USSR could build socialism by its own efforts without waiting for revolution abroad (1); Permanent Revolution, Trotsky's view, held that socialism in backward Russia could only survive if revolution spread to the advanced industrial countries (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: security against invasion by capitalist powers (1); self-sufficiency in industry and food (1); creating a fully socialist economy and ending private trade and peasant farming under NEP (1); ending the grain procurement crisis by securing state control of grain (1); weakening the Right and enhancing his own control (1). **[3]**

**5.** Two consequences, 2 marks each: the decree of 1 December 1934 allowed rapid trials without defence or appeal, giving the NKVD a legal tool for mass repression (2); Zinoviev and Kamenev were arrested and blamed, leading to the first show trial in August 1936 (2); the murder was used to justify a wider hunt for "enemies" in the Leningrad party and beyond (2). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: labour books recording employment history (1938) (1); laws of 1940 making lateness and unauthorised job-changing criminal offences (1); internal passports restricting movement (1932) (1); piece-rates and higher norms after the Stakhanovite movement (1); accusation of "wrecking" for failures (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: the 1936 ban on abortion (1); harder and costlier divorce (1); rewards for large families (1); return of formal teaching, exams and teacher authority in schools (1); fees for upper secondary and higher education (1940) (1); revival of Russian national heroes in history teaching and film (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: joining the League of Nations (September 1934) (1); the Franco-Soviet mutual assistance pact (May 1935) (1); the Soviet-Czechoslovak pact (May 1935) (1); the Comintern's switch to Popular Fronts against fascism (1935) (1); aid to the Spanish Republic from 1936 (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For ideology:** Socialism in One Country offered a patriotic, optimistic programme that suited party members; Stalin's centrist position on NEP let him ally first with the Right against the Left and then adopt the Left's industrialisation policy against the Right; Trotsky's Permanent Revolution seemed risky. **Against:** Stalin's offices -- General Secretary, Orgburo -- and control of delegates allowed him to win votes regardless of argument; rivals' errors (Zinoviev and Kamenev's protection of Stalin over the Testament, Trotsky's isolation); the ban on factions turned opponents into "splitters"; skilful timing of alliances; the cult of "Lenin's disciple". **Criterion:** what converted support into victory at congresses? **Judgement:** ideology supplied the issues on which each opponent was isolated, but organisational power decided outcomes; many answers will conclude that ideas were used tactically, as Stalin's reversal on industrialisation in 1928 shows. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Effectiveness:** the cult presented Stalin as Lenin's heir, wise leader and father of the peoples; the *Short Course* (1938) rewrote party history; posters, film, newspapers and schoolbooks spread the image; Stakhanovite campaigns and celebrations of industrial achievement generated real enthusiasm, especially among the young and the newly promoted. **Limits:** peasants who suffered collectivisation and famine were hard to persuade; private jokes and complaints are recorded in NKVD reports; control rested heavily on the Terror, the NKVD and the Gulag; material incentives for the new elite bought loyalty. **Criterion:** compare propaganda with terror and patronage as sources of control, and distinguish town and countryside. **Judgement:** propaganda helped legitimise the regime and the Terror itself, but on its own could not explain the absence of challenge after 1934. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Aims:** rapid growth of heavy industry, military security, self-sufficiency, a socialist economy and an industrial working class. **Achieved:** large increases in coal, steel, oil and electricity; new industrial centres in the Urals and Siberia, such as Magnitogorsk; major projects such as the Dnieper dam and Moscow Metro; private industry and trade eliminated; armaments expanded under the third Plan, providing the base that later sustained the war effort. **Not achieved:** many targets were unrealistic and missed or met by falsification; quality was poor and waste high; consumer goods and housing were neglected; living standards fell in the early 1930s; agriculture remained weak after collectivisation; the Terror removed managers and engineers, disrupting production in 1937-38. **Judgement:** measured against Stalin's priority of heavy industry and defence, the Plans were substantially successful; measured against balanced growth or living standards, they were not. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Change:** millions of women entered industrial and professional work, including heavy industry, medicine and teaching; literacy among women rose sharply; propaganda celebrated women tractor drivers and Stakhanovites; collectivisation changed peasant women's work. **Limits and reversals:** the 1936 law banned abortion and made divorce harder; women carried the double burden of paid work and housework, with few consumer goods or childcare places; few women reached senior party or state posts; the Zhenotdel (women's department) was closed in 1930; many peasant women's lives were dominated by famine and the collective farm. **Criterion:** distinguish opportunity from equality and legal from lived change. **Judgement:** women's working lives changed greatly, but the regime's family policy moved them back towards traditional roles, so change was large in scale but limited in liberation. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
