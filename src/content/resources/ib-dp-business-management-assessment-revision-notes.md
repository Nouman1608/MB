## Why weightings shift so much between SL and HL

Paper 1 drops from 35% at SL to 25% at HL, and the IA drops from 30% to 20%, with the difference absorbed almost entirely by HL Paper 3 (25%), a component SL students do not sit at all. This is a larger structural shift than in some other DP subjects, where SL and HL differ mainly in content depth rather than in which components exist -- HL Business Management students are assessed on a genuinely different paper structure, not just a harder version of the same three components.

## SL vs HL weightings

| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (pre-seen case study) | 35% | 25% |
| Paper 2 (stimulus material + extended response) | 35% | 30% |
| Paper 3 (social-enterprise stimulus) | — | 25% |
| Internal assessment | 30% | 20% |

## Paper 1's distinctive feature

A short statement is **released to schools three months in advance**, containing a small
number of research topics plus approximately the first 200 words of the case study. The case
study itself (800–1,000 words, describing a **fictitious** organisation) remains unseen until the
exam, a different skill from Paper 2 (and HL Paper 3), which respond to stimulus material that is
entirely **unseen** under timed conditions.

## Internal assessment: the same task, weighted differently

Both SL and HL students complete the same **business research project** (maximum 1,800 words,
worth 25 marks) on a real organisation, against the same assessment criteria at both levels. The
requirements do not differ by level — only the weighting does: 30% of the final grade at SL and
20% at HL.

Both are marked by your own teacher first, then externally moderated by IB examiners.

## The four interdisciplinary concepts

Change, creativity, ethics, sustainability — these run through the **whole**
course and both papers and the IA, rather than being confined to one unit.

## The internal assessment in more depth

Both the SL commentary and the HL research project require applying business tools and theory to a **real organisation** the student has genuinely researched, not a hypothetical or textbook example -- assessment criteria reward the appropriateness of the tools selected for the specific issue investigated, the quality and range of research evidence used, and the depth of analysis and evaluation reached, rather than simply describing the organisation's situation. A common weakness at both levels is applying a business tool mechanically (filling in a SWOT or Ansoff matrix template) without using it to generate genuine insight or a reasoned recommendation -- examiners consistently reward analysis that goes beyond the template to a specific, justified conclusion.

## Command terms across the papers

Command terms are grouped by assessment objective. **AO1** (knowledge and understanding) covers **define**, **describe**, **identify**, **list**, **outline** and **state**. **AO2** (application and analysis) covers **analyse**, **apply**, **comment**, **demonstrate**, **distinguish**, **explain** and **suggest**. **AO3** (synthesis and evaluation) covers **compare**, **contrast**, **discuss**, **evaluate**, **examine**, **justify**, **recommend** and **to what extent** -- these require a reasoned judgement supported by business tools and evidence from the stimulus material. **AO4** (use and application of appropriate skills) is separate from this cognitive-demand ladder and covers skills-specific terms such as **annotate**, **calculate**, **complete**, **construct**, **determine**, **draw**, **label**, **plot** and **prepare**. An examination question can use a command term from the assessment objective level set for that topic, or a less demanding term from a lower level, but not a more demanding term from a higher level. Because Paper 1's case study is released in advance, AO3 command terms there can be prepared for in more depth than on Paper 2 or HL Paper 3, where the same command terms must be answered against largely unseen stimulus material under full exam-time pressure -- a genuinely different skill worth practising separately.

## Why the four concepts matter for every paper

Change, creativity, ethics and sustainability are not confined to a single unit -- they are the lens through which the guide expects every business scenario to be analysed, on every paper and in the IA. A strong answer at any level typically identifies which of the four concepts is most relevant to the scenario given (for example, recognising that a question about a firm entering a new international market is fundamentally about change) and uses that concept explicitly to frame the analysis, rather than answering purely from unit-specific content in isolation.

## HL Paper 3 and the social-enterprise focus

Paper 3, sat only by HL students and worth 25% of the HL grade, is built around a social-enterprise stimulus -- distinct from the profit-focused organisations more typical of Papers 1 and 2. This requires HL students to be comfortable applying standard business tools and concepts to an organisation whose primary objective may be social or environmental impact rather than profit maximisation, which changes how success, strategy and stakeholder interests are evaluated. Underpreparing for this shift in context, rather than the underlying business tools themselves, is a common source of lost marks on Paper 3.
