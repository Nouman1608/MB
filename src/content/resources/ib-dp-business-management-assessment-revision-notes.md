Condensed for quick recall of how the course is assessed. For the full subject overview, use the
[IB DP Business Management subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-subject-guide/).

## Why weightings shift so much between SL and HL

Paper 1 drops from 35% at SL to 25% at HL, and the IA drops from 30% to 20%, with the difference absorbed almost entirely by HL Paper 3 (25%), a component SL students do not sit at all. This is a larger structural shift than in some other DP subjects, where SL and HL differ mainly in content depth rather than in which components exist -- HL Business Management students are assessed on a genuinely different paper structure, not just a harder version of the same three components.

## SL vs HL weightings

| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (pre-seen case study) | 35% | 25% |
| Paper 2 (stimulus material + extended response) | 35% | 30% |
| Paper 3 (social-enterprise stimulus) | — | 25% |
| Internal assessment | 30% | 20% |

## Paper 1's distinctive feature

The Paper 1 case study is **released in advance** — this tests research and preparation on a
known organisation, a different skill from Paper 2 (and HL Paper 3), which respond to **unseen**
stimulus material under timed conditions.

## Internal assessment differs by level, not just weighting

- **SL:** a written commentary applying course concepts to a real business.
- **HL:** a full research project, again on a real organisation — a different task, not just a
  longer version of the SL commentary.

Both are marked by your own teacher first, then externally moderated by IB examiners.

## The six underpinning concepts

Change, culture, ethics, globalization, innovation, strategy — these run through the **whole**
course and both papers and the IA, rather than being confined to one unit.

## The internal assessment in more depth

Both the SL commentary and the HL research project require applying business tools and theory to a **real organisation** the student has genuinely researched, not a hypothetical or textbook example -- assessment criteria reward the appropriateness of the tools selected for the specific issue investigated, the quality and range of research evidence used, and the depth of analysis and evaluation reached, rather than simply describing the organisation's situation. A common weakness at both levels is applying a business tool mechanically (filling in a SWOT or Ansoff matrix template) without using it to generate genuine insight or a reasoned recommendation -- examiners consistently reward analysis that goes beyond the template to a specific, justified conclusion.

## Command terms across the papers

Business Management uses a command-term hierarchy consistent with other individuals-and-societies DP subjects: **define**, **identify** and **describe** at the lower end; **explain**, **analyse** and **examine** in the middle; **evaluate**, **discuss**, **recommend** and **to what extent** at the top, where a reasoned judgement supported by business tools and evidence from the stimulus material is required. Because Paper 1's case study is released in advance, top-tier command terms there can be prepared for in more depth than on Paper 2 or HL Paper 3, where the same command terms must be answered against completely unseen stimulus material under full exam-time pressure -- a genuinely different skill worth practising separately.

## Why the six concepts matter for every paper

Change, culture, ethics, globalization, innovation and strategy are not confined to a single unit -- they are the lens through which the specification expects every business scenario to be analysed, on every paper and in the IA. A strong answer at any level typically identifies which of the six concepts is most relevant to the scenario given (for example, recognising that a question about a firm entering a new international market is fundamentally about globalization and strategy) and uses that concept explicitly to frame the analysis, rather than answering purely from unit-specific content in isolation.

## HL Paper 3 and the social-enterprise focus

Paper 3, sat only by HL students and worth 25% of the HL grade, is built around a social-enterprise stimulus -- distinct from the profit-focused organisations more typical of Papers 1 and 2. This requires HL students to be comfortable applying standard business tools and concepts to an organisation whose primary objective may be social or environmental impact rather than profit maximisation, which changes how success, strategy and stakeholder interests are evaluated. Underpreparing for this shift in context, rather than the underlying business tools themselves, is a common source of lost marks on Paper 3.

## Self-test

1. Why does Paper 1 test a different skill from Paper 2/Paper 3?
2. Name the internal assessment task at HL, and how it differs from the SL task.
3. List the six underpinning concepts.
4. How is the internal assessment marked?
5. Why is applying a business tool "mechanically" a common weakness in the IA?
6. What distinguishes the organisation type typically used in HL Paper 3 from Papers 1 and 2?

**Answers:** 1. Because the Paper 1 case study is released in advance, testing research and prepared analysis, while Paper 2 (and HL Paper 3) use unseen stimulus material under timed exam conditions. 2. A research project on a real organisation, differing in kind (not just length) from the SL written commentary. 3. Change, culture, ethics, globalization, innovation, strategy. 4. First by the student's own teacher, then externally moderated by IB examiners. 5. Because assessment criteria reward genuine insight and a reasoned, justified conclusion from the tool used, not just correctly filling in a template such as SWOT or Ansoff's matrix. 6. Paper 3 is built around a social-enterprise stimulus, where success and strategy must be evaluated against social or environmental objectives rather than profit maximisation alone.
