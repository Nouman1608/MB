## Short-answer questions

Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** State **two** factors that may affect the ratios of two businesses in the same trade. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** problems of inter-business comparison. **[2]**

**3.** Which factor is the most likely reason that one business has a much higher trade receivables turnover (days) than another business in the same trade?

A: It rents its premises

B: It sells mainly on credit to trade customers

C: It buys its goods in bulk

D: It is financed by a long-term loan

**[1]**

**4.** Two shops each have revenue of $300,000 and identical trading. Shop A owns its premises. Shop B pays rent of $24,000 a year. Calculate how many percentage points lower Shop B's profit margin is because of the rent. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why different year-end dates can make an inter-business comparison misleading. **[2]**

**6.** Two businesses each buy equipment for $32,000 at the start of the year. One uses reducing balance depreciation at 25%; the other uses straight line depreciation at 10% of cost.

**(a)** Calculate the first-year depreciation charge for each business. **[2]**

**(b)** State the difference this makes to the two businesses' profit for the year. **[1]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Brightloaf Bakery Limited and Crumbwell Bakery Limited provide these figures for the same year.

| | Brightloaf | Crumbwell |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 250,000 | 400,000 |
| Cost of sales | 150,000 | 260,000 |
| Expenses | 60,000 | 92,000 |
| Loan interest | 4,000 | 0 |
| Issued shares | 100,000 | 250,000 |
| Reserves | 60,000 | 150,000 |
| Loan | 50,000 | 0 |

Crumbwell owns its bakery premises. Brightloaf rents its premises.

**(a)** Calculate for each business the gross profit margin, the profit margin and the return on capital employed. **[6]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why Brightloaf's ROCE is higher than Crumbwell's. **[2]**

**8.** Fernhill Limited and Greystone Limited are in the same trade.

| | Fernhill | Greystone |
|---|---|---|
| Current assets | 84,000 | 63,000 |
| of which inventory | 54,000 | 12,000 |
| Current liabilities | 42,000 | 34,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio and the acid test ratio for each business. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why comparing only the current ratios would give a misleading view of the two businesses' liquidity. **[2]**

**9.** Hollis Traders and Jarvis Traders sell the same goods. Each allows customers 30 days' credit and receives 60 days' credit from suppliers.

| | Hollis | Jarvis |
|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | 18,000 | 9,000 |
| Credit sales | 146,000 | 219,000 |
| Trade payables | 15,000 | 30,000 |
| Credit purchases | 109,500 | 182,500 |

**(a)** Calculate the trade receivables turnover (days) and trade payables turnover (days) for each business. **[4]**

**(b)** Compare the two businesses' management of credit, using your answers to (a). **[3]**

**10.** Ayesha wants to compare her clothing shop, which has a year end of 31 January, with a rival's shop, which has a year end of 31 August. The rival is a sole trader and gives Ayesha only its profit for the year and revenue. Explain **three** problems Ayesha faces in making the comparison. **[6]**

**11.** Delphine owns a florist and has found that her profit margin is 8.00%, while a rival florist in the same town has a profit margin of 13.00%. Delphine rents a shop on the main street for $21,000 a year. The rival trades from premises it has owned for twenty years. Delphine depreciates her new delivery van using the reducing balance method; the rival uses the straight line method on a van bought eight years ago. Evaluate Delphine's view that her business is managed less well than the rival's. **[6]**

**12.** A bank is considering a five-year loan to one of two businesses. It provides these ratios.

| Ratio | Business M | Business N |
|---|---|---|
| Profit margin | 15.00% | 9.00% |
| ROCE | 22.00% | 14.00% |
| Current ratio | 1.20 : 1 | 2.10 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.55 : 1 | 1.30 : 1 |
| Trade payables turnover | 75 days | 32 days |

Both businesses receive 30 days' credit from suppliers. Advise the bank which business it should lend to. Justify your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, one mark each: type of goods or service [1]; pricing policy [1]; size and buying power; location; cash or credit sales; method of finance; owned or rented premises; age of assets; accounting policies. **[2]**

**2.** Any two, one mark each: information may not be available [1]; different accounting policies [1]; different year-end dates; businesses not truly alike; historic cost; an untypical year; non-financial aspects ignored. **[2]**

**3.** B [1]. Credit sales to trade customers create trade receivables; a cash retailer has few. **[1]**

**4.** 24,000 ÷ 300,000 × 100 [1] = **8.00 percentage points** lower [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Inventory, trade receivables and trade payables change through the year, especially in seasonal trades [1], so figures taken at different dates are not like for like and liquidity and efficiency ratios can differ for timing reasons alone [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Reducing balance: 32,000 × 25% = **8,000** [1]. Straight line: 32,000 × 10% = **3,200** [1].

**(b)** The reducing balance business shows profit **4,800** lower [1], with no difference in trading. **[3]**

**7. (a)**

| | Brightloaf | Crumbwell |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit | 100,000 | 140,000 |
| Gross profit margin | 100,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **40.00%** [1] | 140,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **35.00%** [1] |
| Profit for the year | 100,000 − 60,000 − 4,000 = 36,000 | 140,000 − 92,000 = 48,000 |
| Profit margin | 36,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **14.40%** [1] | 48,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **12.00%** [1] |
| Capital employed | 100,000 + 60,000 + 50,000 = 210,000 | 250,000 + 150,000 = 400,000 |
| ROCE | 40,000 ÷ 210,000 × 100 = **19.05%** [1] | 48,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **12.00%** [1] |

**(b)** Crumbwell owns its premises, so its capital employed (400,000) includes them [1], while Brightloaf rents, so its smaller capital employed (210,000) gives a higher return on the profit it makes [1]. Also accept, for the same two marks: Brightloaf earns a higher gross profit margin, perhaps from its pricing policy or type of products, so it makes more profit from each dollar of revenue. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Fernhill current ratio 84,000 : 42,000 = **2.00 : 1** [1]; acid test (84,000 − 54,000) : 42,000 = **0.71 : 1** [1]. Greystone current ratio 63,000 : 34,000 = **1.85 : 1** [1]; acid test (63,000 − 12,000) : 34,000 = **1.50 : 1** [1].

**(b)** Fernhill's current ratio is higher, which suggests it is more liquid [1], but most of its current assets are inventory, which must be sold before it becomes cash, so its acid test of 0.71 : 1 shows it may struggle to pay current liabilities, while Greystone at 1.50 : 1 can [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** Hollis: receivables 18,000 ÷ 146,000 × 365 = **45.00 days** [1]; payables 15,000 ÷ 109,500 × 365 = **50.00 days** [1]. Jarvis: receivables 9,000 ÷ 219,000 × 365 = **15.00 days** [1]; payables 30,000 ÷ 182,500 × 365 = **60.00 days** [1].

**(b)** Hollis's customers take 45.00 days against 30 allowed, so its credit control is weaker; Jarvis collects in 15.00 days, well inside its terms [1]. Jarvis uses the full 60 days' credit from suppliers, while Hollis pays in 50.00 days [1]. Overall Jarvis manages working capital better, collecting cash quickly and paying slowly within its terms, but the comparison assumes both have similar customers [1]. **[7]**

**10.** One mark for each problem, one for applying it, up to three problems.

- Different year-end dates [1]: January follows the busy season and August precedes it, so inventory and receivables at the two dates are not comparable [1].
- Information not available [1]: as a sole trader the rival does not publish accounts and has given only two figures, so only the profit margin can be calculated, not liquidity or efficiency ratios [1].
- Accounting policies may differ [1]: for example the depreciation method, so profit figures are not on the same basis [1].
- Also creditable: the shops may not be alike in size or location; one year may be untypical; non-financial aspects are ignored. **[6]**

**11.** Points for Delphine's view: the rival earns 5 percentage points more profit on each $100 of revenue [1], which could mean it controls expenses better [1].

Points against: Delphine pays rent of $21,000 that the rival does not pay because it owns its premises [1]; the reducing balance method gives a high charge on her new van, while the rival's old van carries a small charge [1]; so much of the gap comes from premises and accounting policy, not management [1].

Judgement: the comparison does not show that Delphine's business is managed less well; she should adjust for rent and depreciation, or compare with a florist that also rents, before deciding [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Business N's current ratio of 2.10 : 1 and acid test of 1.30 : 1 show it can meet its current liabilities without selling inventory, while M's acid test of 0.55 : 1 suggests it may not [1]. M takes 75 days to pay suppliers against 30 allowed, which suggests it is short of cash, while N pays in 32 days, close to its terms [1]. M is more profitable, with a ROCE of 22.00% against 14.00% [1], and profit is needed to pay interest over five years [1]. Advice: lend to N, because a bank's first concern is that interest and repayments are made on time, and N's liquidity is much stronger [1]. Limitation: the bank should also check the businesses' year-end dates, accounting policies and security for the loan, because the ratios alone may not be comparable [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a verdict from one ratio without explaining what causes the difference.
- Forgetting to add loan interest back when calculating ROCE.
- Stating the number of days without comparing it with the credit terms given.
- Writing general problems instead of applying them to the businesses in the question.
- Leaving out a final judgement in Evaluate and Advise answers.
