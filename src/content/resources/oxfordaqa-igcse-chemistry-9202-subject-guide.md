OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) is a separate-science chemistry qualification taken
over two years. It starts with atoms, the periodic table and bonding, develops the quantitative and
practical side of the subject through analysis, acids and the mole, and goes on to rates,
equilibria, energy changes and organic chemistry. The qualification is linear and examined by two
written papers at the end of the course. Each lasts 90 minutes, carries 90 marks and is worth 50%
of the grade, and any part of the specification may be assessed on either paper.

## How the course is organised

The specification is divided into ten content areas. Grouping them by theme makes the course easier
to navigate.

**Particles, structure and bonding**
1. *Atomic structure and the periodic table*: solids, liquids and gases; a simple model of the atom;
   and the periodic table.
2. *Structure, bonding and the properties of matter*: ionic, covalent and metallic bonds; how
   bonding and structure relate to properties; the structure and bonding of carbon; and
   nanoparticles.

**Reactions and analysis**
3. *Chemical changes*: metals and electrolysis.
4. *Chemical analysis*: purity and chromatography, and identifying common gases and ions.
5. *Acids, bases and salts*: the properties of acids and bases, and preparing salts.

**Quantities and patterns**
6. *Quantitative chemistry*: conservation of mass and the quantitative meaning of equations, amount
   of substance and masses of pure substances, the mole concept, and molar concentrations.
7. *Trends within the periodic table*: group properties and transition metals.

**Rates, energy and carbon chemistry**
8. *The rate and extent of chemical change*: rate of reaction, factors affecting equilibrium, the
   production of ammonia and sulfuric acid, and redox reactions.
9. *Energy changes*: exothermic and endothermic reactions, calculating and explaining energy change,
   and chemical cells and fuel cells.
10. *Organic chemistry*: carbon compounds as fuels, synthetic and naturally occurring polymers, and
    the structure and reactions of organic compounds.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Coverage | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |

The qualification is linear and untiered: every candidate sits both papers at the end of the course.
There is no separate practical exam. Both papers are externally assessed written exams. First
teaching was in September 2016, and the specification was first examined in May/June 2018.

OxfordAQA's Prohibited Combinations rule applies to all four of its International GCSE Science
specifications, so a centre cannot enter a student for a separate science such as Chemistry and for
Combined Science in the same series.

## How the parts fit together

With no paper split, the ten areas have to be held together as one body of chemistry, and the
specification is built for that. Atomic structure explains the arrangement of the periodic table,
which in turn explains bonding, and bonding explains properties. Those ideas return in group trends
and transition metals in area 7, in the behaviour of metals and electrolysis in area 3, and in
the polymers of area 10.

Quantitative chemistry is the other main thread. Balanced equations, the mole and concentrations
are needed for salt preparation, for energy change calculations, for yields in industrial processes
such as ammonia and sulfuric acid production, and for interpreting analysis results. Redox appears
in more than one place too, linking metals, electrolysis and chemical cells.

Practical chemistry ties the course together. Chromatography, gas and ion tests, salt preparation,
rate experiments and energy change measurements all generate the methods, data and evaluation
questions that can appear on either paper.

## Planning your study

In the first year, cover the particle and bonding areas first, then chemical changes, analysis and
acids, bases and salts. Introduce quantitative chemistry early and keep practising it alongside the
other topics rather than treating it as a separate block, because confidence with the mole grows
with repetition.

In the second year, study trends within the periodic table, rates and equilibrium, energy changes
and organic chemistry. Return to earlier areas regularly, since both papers can test anything, and
keep a running list of tests, observations and equations.

When the whole specification has been taught, sit full timed papers and treat each as a check on
the entire course. The exam-preparation page covers handling equations, moles and a specification
with no paper split, and past papers are useful for testing readiness.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry?**
Two. Paper 1 and Paper 2 each last 90 minutes, carry 90 marks and are worth 50% of the grade.

**Are topics split between the 9202 papers?**
No. Any part of the specification may be assessed on either paper, so both papers draw on the whole course.

**Is there a practical exam in IGCSE Chemistry?**
No. There is no separate practical exam; both components are written papers, so practical methods and data are best learned alongside the theory.

**Can I take Chemistry and Combined Science in the same series?**
No. OxfordAQA's Prohibited Combinations rule prevents a centre entering a student for both a separate science and Combined Science in the same series.

**When was 9202 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2016, and the first exams were in May/June 2018.
