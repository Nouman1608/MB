These notes condense subtopic **11.1, Physical properties of the Group 17 elements** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Full explanations are in the [11.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-physical-properties-of-the-group-17-elements/). The [Group 17 revision notes](/resources/as-chem-group17-revision-notes/) summarise the whole topic.

## The three outcomes

- **Describe** the colours and the volatility trend of Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂.
- **Describe and explain** the bond strength trend of the halogen molecules.
- **Interpret** volatility using **instantaneous dipole–induced dipole** forces.

## Colours and states

| | Cl₂ | Br₂ | I₂ |
|---|---|---|---|
| State (room temperature) | Gas | Liquid | Solid |
| Colour | Pale yellow-green | Dark red-brown | Shiny grey-black |
| Vapour | Pale yellow-green | Orange-brown | Purple |
| In water | Very pale green | Yellow to orange | Yellow-brown to brown |
| In cyclohexane | Very pale green | Orange to red-brown | Purple |

Colour darkens down the group. Iodine sublimes on gentle warming.

## Volatility

```
           electrons   m.p./°C   b.p./°C
Cl2          34         -101       -34      gas
Br2          70           -7        59      liquid
I2          106          114       184      solid
```

Volatility **decreases** down the group.

**Explanation chain:**

1. Simple molecular: melting and boiling overcome forces **between** molecules only.
2. Forces are **id-id**: an instantaneous dipole in one molecule induces a dipole in a neighbour, and the two attract.
3. More electrons → larger, more **polarisable** electron cloud → stronger id-id forces.
4. More energy needed to separate molecules → higher melting and boiling points → lower volatility.

The X–X covalent bonds are **not broken** when a halogen melts or boils.

## Bond strength

```
F-F  158    Cl-Cl  242    Br-Br  193    I-I  151    (kJ mol^-1)
```

Cl–Cl → I–I: bond strength **decreases** because:

- atoms get larger, so the bond is longer;
- the bonding pair is further from the nuclei and more shielded;
- so the nuclei attract the bonding pair less strongly.

**F–F is weaker than expected**: fluorine atoms are very small, so the lone pairs on the two bonded atoms are close together and repel, weakening the bond.

## Two trends, two reasons

| | Volatility | Bond strength |
|---|---|---|
| About | Between molecules | Within one molecule |
| Cl₂ → I₂ | Decreases | Decreases |
| Reason | More electrons, stronger id-id | Longer bond, bonding pair further from nuclei |

Never use one to explain the other.

## Predicting astatine (below iodine)

- Expected to be a dark (black) solid.
- Higher melting and boiling points than iodine (more electrons, stronger id-id forces).
- At–At bond expected to be weaker than I–I (larger atoms).

## How 11.1 links to the rest of Topic 11

- **Colours** are the evidence in displacement reactions (11.2): orange-brown bromine or purple iodine appearing in a cyclohexane layer shows which halogen has formed.
- **Bond strength** of X–X is not the reason reactivity with hydrogen falls down the group. The X–X bond actually gets weaker from Cl₂ to I₂, which on its own would make reaction easier. The falling strength of the **H–X** bond formed is what matters (11.2).
- **Volatility** explains why chlorine is handled as a gas, bromine as a fuming liquid and iodine as a solid in practical work.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Volatility | How readily a substance vaporises; high volatility means a low boiling point |
| Instantaneous dipole | A temporary uneven distribution of electrons in a molecule at one moment |
| Induced dipole | A dipole created in a molecule by a nearby dipole distorting its electron cloud |
| id-id forces | Attractions between instantaneous and induced dipoles in neighbouring molecules |
| Polarisability | How easily an electron cloud is distorted |
| Bond energy | Energy needed to break one mole of a given covalent bond in gaseous molecules |
| Sublimation | Change directly from solid to gas |

## Exam traps

- Explaining iodine's higher boiling point with I–I bond strength.
- "Van der Waals" with no explanation, when the syllabus asks you to interpret using id-id forces.
- "Bigger molecules" without linking to the number of electrons and polarisability.
- Wrong colours: bromine liquid is red-brown, iodine vapour is purple.
- Writing Cl, Br, I as single atoms instead of diatomic molecules.
- Saying bond strength decreases "because nuclear charge decreases": nuclear charge increases down the group.
- Forgetting that fluorine breaks the bond strength pattern.

## Self-test

1. Give the colour and state of bromine at room temperature.
2. What colour is iodine in cyclohexane?
3. State the trend in volatility from chlorine to iodine.
4. Name the forces between halogen molecules.
5. Explain how an instantaneous dipole leads to an attraction between two molecules.
6. Why are the id-id forces between I₂ molecules stronger than between Cl₂ molecules?
7. Are covalent bonds broken when bromine boils?
8. State and explain the trend in bond strength from Cl₂ to I₂.
9. Why is the F–F bond weaker than the Cl–Cl bond?
10. Predict the state of astatine at room temperature and give a reason.
11. A student says: "Iodine has the weakest bond, so it should be the easiest to boil." Explain the error.

**Answers:**

1. Dark red-brown liquid.
2. Purple (violet).
3. Volatility decreases (boiling point increases).
4. Instantaneous dipole–induced dipole (id-id) forces.
5. Uneven electron distribution in one molecule gives an instantaneous dipole; this distorts the electron cloud of a neighbour, inducing a dipole; the two dipoles attract.
6. I₂ has more electrons (106 compared with 34), so its electron cloud is more polarisable and larger instantaneous and induced dipoles form.
7. No. Only the id-id forces between Br₂ molecules are overcome.
8. Decreases. The atoms are larger, so the bond is longer and the bonding pair is further from the nuclei and more shielded, so it is attracted less strongly.
9. Fluorine atoms are very small, so the lone pairs on the two atoms are close together and repel each other, weakening the bond.
10. Solid: At₂ would have more electrons than I₂, so stronger id-id forces and a higher melting point.
11. Boiling does not break the I–I bond. It overcomes the forces between molecules, which are strongest for iodine because I₂ has the most electrons.
