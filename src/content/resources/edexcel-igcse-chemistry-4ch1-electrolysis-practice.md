These questions cover sub-topic 1(i) Electrolysis of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): statements 1.55C–1.60C. The qualification is untiered, and every statement in this sub-topic has a C reference, so all of it is **Paper 2 only**. A calculator may be used in both 4CH1 papers. Course links: [4CH1 Chemistry hub](/boards/edexcel/igcse/chemistry/), [printable checklist](/checklists/edexcel/igcse/chemistry/), the [electrolysis study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-electrolysis/) and the [electrolysis revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-electrolysis-revision-notes/). The topic-level [Principles of chemistry practice](/resources/igcse-edexcel-chemistry-principles-practice/) has further questions on concentrated sodium chloride solution.

Where needed: molar volume of a gas = 24 000 cm³ at rtp.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by:

**(a)** a cation **[1]**
**(b)** an anion **[1]**

**2.** Tetrachloromethane, CCl₄, is a covalent liquid. Explain why it does not conduct electricity. **[2]**

**3.** A student tests four substances, W, X, Y and Z.

| Substance | Solid conducts? | Molten conducts? | Dissolves in water? | Solution conducts? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| X | Yes | Yes | No | -- |
| Y | No | No | Yes | No |
| Z | No | Yes | No | -- |

**(a)** Identify the substance that is a metal. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the two ionic compounds and explain your choice. **[3]**

**4.** Solid lead(II) bromide is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. A bulb is connected in series. The solid is heated until it melts.

**(a)** Explain why the bulb lights only after the lead(II) bromide has melted. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is seen at the anode. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the half-equation for the reaction at the cathode and explain why it is a reduction. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the experiment is carried out in a fume cupboard. **[1]**

**5.** Dilute sulfuric acid is electrolysed with inert electrodes. The gases are collected separately.

**(a)** Name the gas formed at each electrode. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. **[1]**
**(c)** 30 cm³ of oxygen is collected. Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed at the same time and the amount, in moles, of hydrogen at rtp. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the sulfuric acid becomes more concentrated. **[1]**

**6.** Copper(II) sulfate solution is electrolysed with inert electrodes. (A_r Cu = 63.5)

**(a)** Describe two observations, other than bubbles at the anode. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the half-equations for the reactions at the cathode and at the anode. **[2]**
**(c)** 0.508 g of copper is deposited. Calculate the volume of oxygen formed at rtp. **[3]**

**7.** Predict the product at the cathode and at the anode when each solution is electrolysed with inert electrodes.

**(a)** copper(II) nitrate solution **[2]**
**(b)** potassium sulfate solution **[2]**
**(c)** concentrated magnesium bromide solution **[2]**

**8.** A student electrolyses sodium chloride solution with graphite electrodes. Universal indicator is added.

**(a)** The solution near the cathode turns purple. Explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the test for the gas formed at the anode, and give the result. **[2]**
**(c)** With a very dilute solution, a second gas is also formed at the anode. Name it and write the half-equation for its formation. **[2]**

**9.** Describe an experiment to find the products at each electrode when concentrated potassium chloride solution and copper(II) sulfate solution are electrolysed. Include how you would identify each product. **[6]**

**10.** Molten sodium chloride is electrolysed. 2.30 g of sodium forms at the cathode. (A_r Na = 23, Cl = 35.5)

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of electrons transferred. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of chlorine formed at rtp, in dm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of sodium chloride decomposed. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain, in terms of electrons, why the reaction at the anode is oxidation. **[1]**

**11.** Concentrated copper(II) chloride solution is electrolysed with inert electrodes. (A_r Cu = 63.5)

**(a)** Predict the product at each electrode. Explain each prediction. **[4]**
**(b)** Write the two half-equations. **[2]**
**(c)** 0.381 g of copper forms. Calculate the volume of chlorine expected at rtp. **[3]**
**(d)** The volume of chlorine measured is less than expected. Suggest one reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A positive ion [1].
**(b)** A negative ion [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Positive particle" or "positive atom" is not enough; the word **ion** is needed for each mark.

**2.** It is made of molecules, so there are no ions [1]. Its electrons are held in covalent bonds, so there are no charged particles free to move [1].
*Examiner insight:* Two separate points are needed for both marks: no ions, and no mobile electrons. Saying only "it is covalent" restates the question and scores nothing.

**3. (a)** **X** [1].
**(b)** **W and Z** [1]. Neither conducts as a solid [1], but both conduct when molten, because the ions become free to move [1].
*Examiner insight:* Z is insoluble, so you can only use its molten result. Y conducts in no state, so it is covalent; don't pick it as ionic just because it dissolves.

**4. (a)** In the solid, the ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice [1]. When molten, the ions are free to move and carry the charge [1].
**(b)** Red-brown fumes (of bromine) [1].
**(c)** `Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb` [1]. Lead ions gain electrons [1].
**(d)** Bromine vapour is toxic [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "electrons move when molten" is a contradiction and loses the mark; the mobile particles in a molten salt are ions.

**5. (a)** Cathode: **hydrogen** [1]. Anode: **oxygen** [1].
**(b)** `2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂` [1].
**(c)** Volume of H₂ = 2 × 30 = **60 cm³** [1]. Moles = 60 ÷ 24 000 = **0.0025 mol** [1].
**(d)** Hydrogen and oxygen come from water, so water is removed while the acid stays in the solution [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the second mark can be awarded as a follow-through from a wrong volume, provided you divide by 24 000 for cm³.

**6. (a)** A pink-brown solid forms on the cathode [1]. The blue colour of the solution fades [1].
**(b)** Cathode: `Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu` [1]. Anode: `4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻` [1].
**(c)** n(Cu) = 0.508 ÷ 63.5 = 0.00800 mol [1]. 4e⁻ give 2Cu and 1O₂, so n(O₂) = 0.00800 ÷ 2 = 0.00400 mol [1]. Volume = 0.00400 × 24 000 = **96 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The middle mark is for the 2 : 1 ratio from balanced electrons. Using a 1 : 1 ratio gives 192 cm³ and loses that mark and the final one.

**7. (a)** Cathode: **copper** [1]. Anode: **oxygen** [1].
**(b)** Cathode: **hydrogen** [1]. Anode: **oxygen** [1].
**(c)** Cathode: **hydrogen** [1]. Anode: **bromine** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each mark is for one named product at the correct electrode. Naming a product without its electrode, or giving "potassium" or "magnesium" for an aqueous solution, scores zero for that mark.

**8. (a)** Hydrogen ions are discharged at the cathode [1], leaving hydroxide ions (sodium hydroxide) in solution, which is alkaline [1].
**(b)** Hold damp blue litmus paper in the gas [1]. It turns red, then white (bleached) [1].
**(c)** **Oxygen** [1]. `4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻` [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Turns red" on its own is the test for an acid, not for chlorine; the bleaching is what earns the result mark.

**9.** One mark per point:
- Graphite (inert) electrodes in the solution, connected to a low-voltage d.c. supply [1].
- Small test tubes filled with the solution and inverted over each electrode to collect gas [1].
- Watch the cathode with copper(II) sulfate: a pink-brown coating shows copper [1].
- Test a cathode gas with a lighted splint: squeaky pop shows hydrogen (from potassium chloride solution) [1].
- Test the anode gas from copper(II) sulfate with a glowing splint: it relights, showing oxygen [1].
- Test the anode gas from potassium chloride with damp blue litmus paper: it is bleached, showing chlorine [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each test must be paired with its result and the product it shows. Rinsing the electrodes between solutions is good practice but would not replace a missing test.

**10. (a)** n(Na) = 2.30 ÷ 23 = 0.100 mol [1]. `Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na`, so n(e⁻) = **0.100 mol** [1].
**(b)** `2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻`, so n(Cl₂) = 0.100 ÷ 2 = 0.0500 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0500 × 24 = **1.20 dm³** [1].
**(c)** 0.100 × 58.5 = **5.85 g** [1].
**(d)** Chloride ions lose electrons [1].
*Examiner insight:* Check the units asked for: an answer of 1200 with no unit, when dm³ was requested, does not score the final mark in (b).

**11. (a)** Cathode: **copper** [1], because copper is less reactive than hydrogen [1]. Anode: **chlorine** [1], because chloride ions are discharged in preference to hydroxide ions in a concentrated solution [1].
**(b)** `Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu` [1]. `2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻` [1].
**(c)** n(Cu) = 0.381 ÷ 63.5 = 0.00600 mol [1]. Both half-equations use 2e⁻, so n(Cl₂) = 0.00600 mol [1]. Volume = 0.00600 × 24 000 = **144 cm³** [1].
**(d)** Some chlorine dissolves in the water of the solution [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), each explanation mark depends on the correct product; a wrong product with a correct-sounding reason does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying "electrons move" in a molten or dissolved electrolyte instead of "ions move".
- Writing that a solid ionic compound "has no ions".
- Forgetting H⁺ and OH⁻ from water, so giving the metal for a reactive-metal solution.
- Writing Cl or Br for the anode product instead of Cl₂ or Br₂.
- Putting electrons on the wrong side of an anode half-equation, or leaving charges unbalanced.
- Using a 1 : 1 ratio for copper and oxygen, or hydrogen and oxygen, instead of balancing electrons.
- Dividing a cm³ volume by 24 instead of 24 000.
- Giving a gas test without its result, or "litmus turns red" for chlorine.
