These notes condense **4.2.4.2 Uses of nanoparticles** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for
teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only statements
and no required practical. For full explanations, see the
[uses of nanoparticles study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles/); then practise with
the [uses of nanoparticles practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-uses-of-nanoparticles-practice/).

## Core facts

- Nanoparticles have many applications in **medicine**, **electronics**, **cosmetics and sun creams**, as
  **deodorants**, and as **catalysts**.
- New applications for nanoparticulate materials are an important **area of research**.
- You should consider the **advantages and disadvantages** of the applications, but you do **not** need to know
  specific examples or properties beyond those specified.
- You should be able to **evaluate** the use of nanoparticles for a specified purpose, given appropriate
  information.
- You should be able to **explain that there are possible risks** associated with the use of nanoparticles.

## Why they are useful

| Property | Effect |
|---|---|
| Very small size | Can spread thinly, enter small spaces, build tiny devices |
| High surface area to volume ratio | More of the material is exposed, so smaller quantities may be needed |
| Different properties from bulk material | New uses become possible |

## Advantages and risks

| Advantages | Possible risks and drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Smaller amounts of material needed | Very small particles may enter the body through skin or lungs |
| New medical treatments and electronics | Long-term health effects may be unknown |
| Better cosmetics and catalysts | Might spread in the environment |
| Active area of research | Production can be costly; testing is incomplete |

Always use the word **possible** with risks. They are not all proven.

## Evaluating: a four-step plan

1. **Benefit:** state what is better and link it to a property.
2. **Risk or drawback:** state at least one.
3. **Evidence:** use the figures or statements in the question.
4. **Conclusion:** state a judgement, or say what more information is needed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nanoparticle | A particle 1 to 100 nm across |
| Catalyst | A substance that speeds up a reaction without being used up |
| Evaluate | Weigh up advantages and disadvantages and reach a judgement |
| Risk | A possible harm |
| Cosmetic | A product for appearance, such as a sun cream |
| Research | Investigation to find out new information |

## Worked examples in brief

**Example 1.** "Evaluate nanoparticles in sun cream." Benefit: spread evenly and thinly, less material needed.
Risk: may pass into the body; long-term effects unknown. Conclusion: more testing needed.

**Example 2.** "Why is a nanoparticle catalyst effective?" High surface area to volume ratio, so more reactant
particles meet the surface.

**Example 3.** Cost comparison: normal catalyst 20 kg at £30 per kg = £600; nanoparticle catalyst 2 kg at
£150 per kg = £300. The nanoparticle catalyst is £300 cheaper per batch.

## Exam traps

- **One-sided answers.** An evaluation needs both sides.
- **"Nanoparticles are harmful."** Say they may be, and that effects are not fully known.
- **Learning examples instead of using the data.** The question will supply the information.
- **Missing the conclusion.** Finish with a judgement.
- **No link to a property.** Mention size or the surface area to volume ratio.
- **Ignoring cost.** Cost may be given as data.

## Quick self-test

1. Name three areas where nanoparticles are used.
2. Why may a smaller quantity of a nanoparticle catalyst be needed?
3. Why is research into nanoparticles important?
4. Give one possible risk of using nanoparticles.
5. Why is the word "possible" used about the risks?
6. A normal catalyst: 10 kg at £25 per kg. Nanoparticle catalyst: 1 kg at £120 per kg. Calculate each cost.
7. Which of the two costs less in question 6?
8. What four things should an evaluation include?
9. Do you need to learn specific examples of nanoparticle uses for the exam?
10. Which property of nanoparticles helps deodorants and catalysts work with small amounts?

### Answers

1. Any three of medicine, electronics, cosmetics and sun creams, deodorants, catalysts.
2. They have a high surface area to volume ratio, so more of the material is exposed.
3. New applications may be found and the risks need to be understood.
4. They may enter the body through skin or lungs; long-term effects unknown; may spread in the environment.
5. The risks are not all proven and some are not yet known.
6. Normal: 10 × 25 = £250. Nanoparticle: 1 × 120 = £120.
7. The nanoparticle catalyst.
8. A benefit, a risk, use of the data, and a conclusion.
9. No. The question gives the information.
10. A high surface area to volume ratio.
