Cambridge IGCSE Literature in English (0475) introduces the close study of poetry, prose and drama, and the skill of writing a clear, well-supported response to literature. It is a flexible qualification: every candidate takes the same first paper, but the second half of the grade can be earned in one of three ways, through an examination, through a combination of examinations, or through an examination and coursework. In the 2026 syllabus, Paper 1 Poetry and Prose (90 minutes, 50 marks) is compulsory and worth 50%, and the remaining 50% comes from one of three routes made up of Paper 2 Drama, Paper 3 Drama (Open Text), Paper 4 Unseen and Component 5 Coursework.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus content is organised around the components themselves.

- **Paper 1, Poetry and Prose.** Section A is Poetry and Section B is Prose. You answer two questions, each on a different text. Every candidate studies this content.
- **Paper 2, Drama.** Two questions on two drama texts.
- **Paper 3, Drama (Open Text).** One question on one drama text.
- **Paper 4, Unseen.** One question asking for a critical commentary on writing you have not studied in advance.
- **Component 5, Coursework.** A portfolio of two assignments, each on a different text.

Which of Papers 2 to 4 and Component 5 you study depends on the route your centre enters you for. Poetry and prose are common to everyone; drama is part of every route too, either through Paper 2 or through Paper 3.

## The set texts

Set texts rotate, so use the list for the year your candidates sit. The lists below are from the Cambridge International syllabuses for 2026 and 2027 (Section 3, Subject content).

**Paper 1 Section A, Poetry (2026 and 2027, the same list):** answer on one of three set texts: 15 poems from *Songs of Ourselves* Volume 1 (from Aphra Behn's "Song: Love Armed" to Judith Wright's "Hunting Snake"); 15 poems from *Songs of Ourselves* Volume 2, Part 3 (from Nancy Fotheringham Cato's "The Road" to George Szirtes's "Song"); or 15 poems from *Kumukanda* by Kayo Chingonyi.

**Paper 1 Section B, Prose, one text:**

- *2026:* Chinua Achebe, *Things Fall Apart*; Jane Austen, *Pride and Prejudice*; Anita Desai, *Fire on the Mountain*; Kiran Desai, *Hullaballoo in the Guava Orchard*; Susan Hill, *I'm the King of the Castle*; Harper Lee, *To Kill a Mockingbird*; H G Wells, *The War of the Worlds*; or ten stories from *Stories of Ourselves* Volume 2.
- *2027:* the same, except that *The War of the Worlds* is replaced by Lesley Nneka Arimah, *What it Means When a Man Falls from the Sky*.
- The ten *Stories of Ourselves* Volume 2 stories are the same in both years: Rossetti, "Nick"; Schreiner, "The Woman's Rose"; Ellison, "The Black Ball"; Anand, "The Gold Watch"; Atwood, "When It Happens"; Ballard, "The Man Who Walked on the Moon"; Kincaid, "A Walk to the Jetty"; Gardam, "Showing the Flag"; Forna, "Haywards Heath"; and Gunesekera, "Fluke".

**Paper 2 Drama (two different texts) and Paper 3 Drama, Open Text (one text):**

- *2026:* Pearl Cleage, *Blues for an Alabama Sky*; Shelagh Delaney, *A Taste of Honey*; Shakespeare, *A Midsummer Night's Dream*; Shakespeare, *Antony and Cleopatra*; Tennessee Williams, *A Streetcar Named Desire*.
- *2027:* the same, except that *A Streetcar Named Desire* is replaced by Chinonyerem Odimba, *Princess & the Hustler*.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Poetry and Prose | 90 minutes | 50 | 50% | Externally assessed |
| Paper 2 | Drama | 90 minutes | 50 | 50% | Externally assessed |
| Paper 3 | Drama (Open Text) | 45 minutes | 25 | 25% | Externally assessed |
| Paper 4 | Unseen | 75 minutes | 25 | 25% | Externally assessed |
| Component 5 | Coursework | No timed paper | 25 | 25% | Internally assessed, externally moderated |

Not every candidate takes every component. All candidates take Paper 1, plus exactly one of these routes:

| Route | Components | Share of grade |
|---|---|---|
| Route A | Paper 2 Drama | 50% |
| Route B | Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) and Paper 4 Unseen | 25% + 25% |
| Route C | Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) and Component 5 Coursework | 25% + 25% |

Paper 3 is the same paper whether you take it on Route B or Route C. The coursework in Route C is marked by your teachers and then moderated by Cambridge. The qualification is not tiered, and grades run from A* to G.

This is a single-year syllabus edition, so 2026 is both its first and its final examination year. Candidates sitting in later years should check the syllabus for their own examination year. Ask your teacher which route you are entered for, since it decides which components you prepare.

## How the parts fit together

Whatever the route, the core skills are the same: knowing a text well, selecting relevant detail, analysing how a writer's choices of language, structure and form create meaning, and organising those ideas into a focused response.

The routes test those skills in different conditions.

- **Route A** asks for two drama answers in one paper, so it rewards thorough knowledge of the set drama texts and the ability to write two strong responses in a single sitting.
- **Route B** combines a single drama answer with unseen commentary. The unseen paper draws directly on the close-reading habits you build for Paper 1's poetry and prose sections.
- **Route C** combines a single drama answer with coursework, where you have time to draft, refine and develop an extended argument about two texts.

Paper 1 sits at the centre of all three. The way you learn to analyse a poem or a prose extract for Paper 1 is the same method you apply to drama, to unseen writing and to coursework.

## Planning your study

The order of study depends partly on your route, but the overall shape is similar for everyone.

**Early in the course**
- Read your Paper 1 poetry and prose texts in full, and start your drama text early too.
- Build analytical vocabulary: terms for imagery, sound, structure, narrative voice and dramatic technique.

**Middle of the course**
- Study texts in detail, collecting key quotations and noting how each writer develops themes and characters.
- If you are on Route B, begin regular practice with short unseen poems and prose extracts.
- If you are on Route C, plan your coursework assignments with your teacher and allow time for drafting.

**Later in the course**
- Write timed responses for each examined component on your route.
- Revisit Paper 1 texts throughout, since that paper carries half the grade for every candidate.

Move on to complete papers once all the texts for your route have been studied. For paper-by-paper advice on approaching each examination, use the course's exam-preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which paper is compulsory in Cambridge IGCSE Literature in English 0475?**
Paper 1 Poetry and Prose is compulsory for every candidate. It lasts 90 minutes, is marked out of 50 and is worth 50%.

**What are the three routes in 0475?**
Route A is Paper 2 Drama. Route B is Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) with Paper 4 Unseen. Route C is Paper 3 with Component 5 Coursework. Each route is worth the remaining 50%.

**How is the coursework assessed?**
Component 5 is a portfolio of two assignments, each on a different text. It is worth 25 marks and 25%, and is internally assessed and externally moderated.

**Is IGCSE English Literature 0475 tiered?**
No. All candidates are graded on the same scale, from A* to G.

**Which exam year does this page cover?**
It describes the 2026 syllabus, which is a single-year edition.
