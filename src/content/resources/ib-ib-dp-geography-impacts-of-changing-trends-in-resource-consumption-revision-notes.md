Condensed recall for **10.2 Impacts of changing trends in resource consumption** (Unit 3: Global
resource consumption and security). For the full explanation, read the
[Global resource consumption study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-global-resource-consumption-study-guide/).

## Benefits and pressures

Rising consumption brings improved nutrition, health, comfort, industrial growth and development,
but also pressures.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Environmental | Land use change, deforestation, soil degradation, aquifer depletion, pollution, biodiversity loss, greenhouse gas emissions |
| Economic | Price volatility, import dependence, stranded assets as energy systems change, costs of environmental damage |
| Social and political | Unequal access, energy poverty, food insecurity, displacement, resource conflict, geopolitics of exporters and importers |

## Resource security

**Resource security** means reliable access to adequate, affordable and safe supplies. Profiles
differ: **resource-rich exporters**, **import-dependent** countries and countries facing **shortages**
because of environment, infrastructure or conflict.

Pressure in one place can affect another through trade and shared systems. A country can reduce
domestic water stress by importing water-intensive food, effectively importing **virtual water**.

## Causes of supply disruption

- Concentrated production.
- Transport chokepoints.
- Political instability.
- Extreme weather.

## Reserves and the R/P ratio

- **Reserves** are the portion of a resource that can be extracted economically with present
  technology; they change with price and technology.
- **R/P ratio** = reserves / annual production, the number of years reserves would last at current
  output.
- Example (invented figures): 360 / 12 = 30 years; with 60 more of reserves, 420 / 12 = 35 years; if
  production rises to 14, 360 / 14 = 25.7 years.
- It is a **snapshot, not a forecast**, because reserves and consumption change.

## Exam traps

- Treating reserves as the total amount of a resource in the ground.
- Treating the R/P ratio as a prediction of when a resource will run out.
- Listing impacts with no located example or no sense of who is affected.

## Self-test

1. Define resource security.
2. Name two causes of supply disruption.
3. Calculate the R/P ratio for reserves of 90 units and production of 6 units a year.
4. What is virtual water?
5. Why is the R/P ratio a simplification?

**Answers:** 1. Reliable access to adequate, affordable and safe supplies. 2. For example concentrated
production and political instability. 3. 90 / 6 = 15 years. 4. Water embedded in traded goods, which
countries can import instead of using their own. 5. Reserves and consumption change over time.
