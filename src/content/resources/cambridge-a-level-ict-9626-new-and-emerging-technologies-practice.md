## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between augmented reality and virtual reality. **[2]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by the Internet of Things. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** uses of 3D printing in medicine. **[2]**

**4.** Give **two** disadvantages of autonomous transport systems. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by computer-assisted translation and why the results still need checking. **[2]**

**6.** State **one** way in which a new technology can increase e-waste. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A 3D printer builds a part that is 45 mm tall in layers 0.15 mm thick. Each layer takes 30 seconds and the part uses 80 g of material costing £30 per kilogram.

**(a)** Calculate the number of layers. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the printing time in hours. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the cost of the material. **[2]**

**8.** A hospital uses wearable sensors on patients. 500 sensors each send a 50-byte reading every 5 seconds.

**(a)** Calculate the number of readings from one sensor in an hour. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the data produced by all the sensors in one hour, in bytes. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **two** benefits of this monitoring for patients and staff. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how robotics and 3D printing are used in the development of prosthetics. **[4]**

**10.** Discuss the impact of new and emerging technologies on the environment. **[6]**

**11.** A flight training school plans to use virtual reality for pilot training. Evaluate this plan. **[6]**

**12.** Describe how blockchain technology could be used to track food from a farm to a shop, and give **one** disadvantage. **[4]**

**13.** A bank is considering using artificial intelligence to decide whether customers receive loans. Assess the impact on the bank and on its customers. **[5]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Augmented reality overlays digital information on a live view of the real world [1]; virtual reality replaces the real world with a fully computer-generated environment viewed in a headset [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Everyday objects fitted with sensors and network connections [1] that collect and exchange data without human input, such as smart thermostats [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: custom prosthetics [1]; models of organs for planning surgery [1]; surgical guides and tools [1]; tissue engineering using living cells [1]; artificial blood vessels [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two from: sensors can fail in poor weather [1]; it is unclear who is legally responsible after an accident [1]; the system could be hacked [1]; drivers could lose their jobs [1]; high cost [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Software that translates text or speech from one language to another [1]. The results need checking because idioms and context can be translated wrongly [1]. **[2]**

**6.** For example: devices are replaced quickly, or large numbers of connected devices and sensors are thrown away [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 45 ÷ 0.15 = 300 layers [1].

**(b)** 300 × 30 = 9,000 seconds [1]; 9,000 ÷ 3,600 = 2.5 hours [1].

**(c)** 80 g = 0.08 kg [1]; 0.08 × 30 = £2.40 [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 3,600 ÷ 5 = 720 readings [1].

**(b)** 720 × 50 = 36,000 bytes per sensor [1]; 36,000 × 500 = 18,000,000 bytes [1].

**(c)** Any two benefits, each with a reason: staff are alerted at once if a reading goes out of range [1], so treatment can begin sooner [1]; readings are recorded continuously, giving a full history [1] that helps diagnosis [1]; patients can move around or be at home [1] because no nurse must be present to take readings [1]; nurses can monitor more patients at once [1], freeing time for care [1]. **[7]**

**9.** 3D printing can produce a limb to fit the exact measurements of one person [1], quickly and at a lower cost than a traditional limb [1]. Robotics allows a prosthetic limb to move [1], controlled by signals from muscles or nerves, so the user can grip and walk [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Positive: 3D printing uses only the material needed, so less waste [1]; recycled plastic can be used [1]; robots reduce errors and waste in manufacturing [1]; IoT sensors can reduce energy use in buildings and farming [1]; devices can harvest energy from wireless signals, reducing batteries [1]. Negative: short device lifetimes and many connected devices produce e-waste with toxic materials [1]; data centres for AI and blockchain mining use large amounts of electricity [1]; extracting materials for devices harms the environment [1]. Judgement [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**11.** Advantages: pilots can practise emergencies safely [1]; lower cost than using a real aircraft, with no fuel [1]; scenarios and weather can be repeated [1]; training can take place at any time [1]. Disadvantages: headsets and software are expensive to buy [1]; a simulation does not give the real feel and risk of flight, and motion sickness may occur [1]; software needs updating [1]. Conclusion, for example that it is suitable as part of training, with some real flying [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**12.** Each stage (harvest, packing, transport, delivery) adds a record to a block [1], which is linked to the previous block and copied across many computers [1], so records cannot be altered and the origin of the food can be checked [1]. Disadvantage, for example: every business must use the system and enter data correctly, or false information may still be recorded; or it takes much computing power [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Bank: decisions are faster and cheaper [1]; consistent across all applicants [1]; but if the training data is biased, the system will repeat that bias [1]. Customers: quicker answers [1]; but they may be refused without an explanation they can understand [1], and may be treated unfairly [1]. Judgement: only acceptable if decisions are explainable and can be reviewed by a person [1]. Maximum 5 marks. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a technology by its name only ("VR is virtual reality").
- Mixing up AR and VR.
- Not showing the unit conversions in the 3D printing and data calculations.
- Giving environmental points on one side only in a discuss question.
- Saying "it is more secure" for blockchain without linking blocks and copies.
