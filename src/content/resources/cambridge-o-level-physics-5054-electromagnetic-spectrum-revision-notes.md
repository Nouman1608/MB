## Order of the regions

| Increasing frequency → | | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radio | Microwaves | Infrared | Visible | Ultraviolet | X-rays | Gamma rays |

Increasing wavelength: the same table read from right to left.

Memory hook: infrared sits just below red, ultraviolet just beyond violet.

## Speed

- In a vacuum: **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s** for every region.
- In air: approximately the same as in a vacuum.
- Wave equation: **v = f λ**, so at fixed speed, higher f means shorter λ.

Prefixes to convert before calculating:

| Prefix | Factor |
|---|---|
| G (giga) | 10⁹ |
| M (mega) | 10⁶ |
| k (kilo) | 10³ |
| n (nano) | 10⁻⁹ |

Example: a 100 MHz radio broadcast has λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁸ = 3.0 m.
A 25 GHz microwave link has λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.5 × 10¹⁰ = 0.012 m.

## Uses and the reason for each

| Region | Use named in the syllabus | Property it relies on |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | Radio and TV communications | Travels through the atmosphere; long wavelength diffracts round hills and buildings |
| Radio | Astronomy | Stars and galaxies emit radio waves, collected by radio telescopes |
| Microwaves | Satellite TV | Passes through the atmosphere to and from satellites; dishes give narrow beams |
| Microwaves | Mobile phones, Bluetooth | Carries signals between devices |
| Microwaves | Microwave ovens | Absorbed by water molecules in food, heating it |
| Infrared | Household appliances (grills, toasters, heaters) | Emitted by hot elements and absorbed by food or people |
| Infrared | Remote controllers | Coded pulses picked up by a sensor on the device |
| Infrared | Intruder alarms, thermal imaging | Warm bodies emit more infrared than cooler surroundings |
| Infrared | Optical fibres | Carries signals along the fibre |
| Visible | Photography, vision | Detected by eyes and camera sensors |
| Ultraviolet | Security marking, counterfeit notes | Makes special inks fluoresce (glow) |
| Ultraviolet | Sterilising water | Kills bacteria and other microorganisms |
| X-rays | Medical imaging, security scanners | Pass through soft tissue, absorbed by bone and dense materials |
| X-rays | Killing cancerous cells | Concentrated beams kill cells |
| X-rays and gamma | Detecting cracks in metal | Penetrate metal; flaws change how much gets through |
| Gamma | Detecting and killing cancerous cells | Highly penetrating; kills cells |
| Gamma | Sterilising food and medical equipment | Penetrates packaging and kills bacteria |

## Damage

| Type of harm | Regions | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Heating | Excessive exposure, especially microwaves and infrared | Heating of soft tissues; burns |
| Ionising | Ultraviolet | Skin cancer; cataracts |
| Ionising | X-rays, gamma rays | Cell mutation; cancer |

Ionising = able to remove electrons from atoms, damaging molecules in
cells. Risk increases towards the high-frequency end.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic spectrum | The family of transverse waves that all travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum, ordered by frequency |
| Frequency | Number of waves passing a point per second, in hertz (Hz) |
| Wavelength | Distance between consecutive identical points on a wave, in metres |
| Fluorescence | Glowing (giving out visible light) when ultraviolet falls on a substance |
| Ionising radiation | Radiation that can knock electrons out of atoms |
| Sterilising | Killing bacteria and other microorganisms |

## Common properties of all electromagnetic waves

- Transverse.
- Transfer energy, not matter.
- Same speed in a vacuum.
- Can be reflected, refracted and diffracted.

## Exam traps

- "Which travels fastest in a vacuum?" — none; they all travel at the same
  speed.
- The order by wavelength is the reverse of the order by frequency. Read
  which one the question asks for.
- Microwaves and infrared cause **heating and burns**; they are not
  ionising.
- Ultraviolet effects are **skin cancer and cataracts**; X-ray and gamma
  effects are **cell mutation and cancer**. Keep the pairs together.
- Visible light sits between infrared and ultraviolet, with red next to
  infrared.
- "Explain why X-rays are used to image bones" needs the contrast:
  absorbed by bone, pass through soft tissue.
- Gamma sterilisation works through sealed packaging; ultraviolet is used
  for water.
- Convert MHz, GHz and nm to base units before substituting.

## Self-test

1. List the regions in order of increasing wavelength.
2. State the speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum.
3. Which region lies between visible light and X-rays?
4. Find the wavelength of a 200 MHz radio wave.
5. Find the frequency of light of wavelength 600 nm.
6. Why are microwaves used for satellite television?
7. Explain how a thermal-imaging camera can detect a person in the dark.
8. How does ultraviolet help detect a counterfeit bank note?
9. Why do X-ray images show bones clearly?
10. Name the region used to sterilise sealed medical equipment and explain
    why it works through the packaging.
11. State the effect of excessive exposure to infrared on the body.
12. Name two ionising effects of ultraviolet.

**Answers**

1. Gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio
   waves.
2. **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s**.
3. **Ultraviolet**.
4. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.0 × 10⁸ = **1.5 m**.
5. f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ = **5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
6. They pass through the atmosphere to and from the satellite, and dishes
   can send and receive them as narrow beams.
7. A warm body emits more infrared than its cooler surroundings; the camera
   forms an image from the infrared, so no visible light is needed.
8. Genuine notes carry features that fluoresce under ultraviolet; a forgery
   lacks them or glows in the wrong places.
9. X-rays pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bone, so bone shows
   as a shadow.
10. **Gamma rays**: they are highly penetrating, so they pass through the
    packaging and kill the bacteria inside.
11. Heating of soft tissue and burns.
12. Skin cancer and cataracts.
