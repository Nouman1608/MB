Condensed recall for **2.4 Ions and ionic bonds**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620. For
full explanations and worked diagrams, use the
[Ions and Ionic Bonds study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-ions-and-ionic-bonds/).

> **Tier note:** items marked *(Supplement)* are for Extended candidates only.

## The essentials in six lines

- **Metals lose** outer electrons → **cations** (positive).
- **Non-metals gain** electrons → **anions** (negative).
- Each ion has a **noble gas electronic configuration**.
- **Ionic bond** = strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
- *(Supplement)* **Giant lattice** = regular arrangement of alternating positive and
  negative ions.
- Properties: **high melting and boiling points**; conducts when **molten or aqueous**, not
  when **solid**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Ion | A charged particle formed when an atom loses or gains electrons |
| Cation | A positive ion; formed by metals losing electrons |
| Anion | A negative ion; formed by non-metals gaining electrons |
| Ionic bond | Strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions |
| Giant lattice *(Supplement)* | Regular, repeating arrangement of alternating positive and negative ions extending throughout the solid |
| Electrostatic attraction | Force between opposite charges |
| Dot-and-cross diagram | Diagram of outer-shell electrons, dots for one atom and crosses for the other |
| Electrolyte | A molten or dissolved ionic substance that conducts because its ions can move |

## Ion charges from group number

```
Group I    → 1+      Group V   → 3−
Group II   → 2+      Group VI  → 2−
Group III  → 3+      Group VII → 1−
```

Groups IV and VIII (0) do not normally form simple ions at this level.

## Electron changes to recall

| Atom | Configuration | Ion | Ion configuration | Same as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Na | 2,8,1 | Na⁺ | 2,8 | Neon |
| K | 2,8,8,1 | K⁺ | 2,8,8 | Argon |
| Mg | 2,8,2 | Mg²⁺ | 2,8 | Neon |
| O | 2,6 | O²⁻ | 2,8 | Neon |
| Cl | 2,8,7 | Cl⁻ | 2,8,8 | Argon |
| S | 2,8,6 | S²⁻ | 2,8,8 | Argon |

Note that ions can share a configuration and still be different: Na⁺, Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ are all
2,8, but they have different numbers of protons, so different charges.

## Dot-and-cross rules

- Outer shell only, unless the question asks for all shells.
- Electrons lost must equal electrons gained: this fixes the ratio of ions.
- Each ion in **square brackets**, charge at the **top right outside** the bracket.
- Metal ion: no outer electrons shown (its old outer shell is empty).
- Non-metal ion: **eight** electrons — its own as crosses, the transferred ones as dots.
- Core diagrams: Group I with Group VII (NaCl, KBr, LiF).
- *(Supplement)* any metal with any non-metal: MgO (1 : 1), CaCl₂ (1 : 2), Na₂O (2 : 1),
  Al₂O₃ (2 : 3).

## Property explanations (Supplement wording)

**High melting point:** giant lattice → many strong electrostatic attractions between
oppositely charged ions → much energy needed to overcome them.

**Conductivity:** solid → ions in fixed positions, cannot move → no conduction. Molten or
aqueous → lattice broken down, ions free to move → conducts.

Core candidates describe the same two properties without the reasons.

## Exam traps

- "Ionic bond = transfer of electrons" loses the definition mark. Transfer forms the ions;
  attraction is the bond.
- Conduction in ionic compounds is by **ions**, never "free electrons" or "delocalised
  electrons".
- Ionic compounds conduct when **molten or in aqueous solution**: give both conditions.
- Do not write "intermolecular forces" for an ionic compound — there are no molecules.
- Check brackets: [Cl]⁻ is one ion; writing Cl₂⁻ for two chloride ions is wrong.
- A formula shows no charges: write CaCl₂, not Ca²⁺Cl⁻₂.
- In a diagram, the metal ion's charge equals the number of dots that moved to the
  non-metal(s).

## Self-test

1. What type of ion does a Group II metal form, and why?
2. Define an ionic bond.
3. Give the electronic configuration of a potassium ion and name the noble gas it matches.
4. How many dots and crosses appear around the bromide ion in a dot-and-cross diagram of
   potassium bromide?
5. Name the ion S²⁻.
6. *(Supplement)* Deduce the formula of the compound formed between calcium and oxygen,
   giving the electron reasoning.
7. *(Supplement)* Deduce the formula of sodium nitride from the ion charges.
8. *(Supplement)* Describe the structure of solid potassium chloride in syllabus wording.
9. Why does molten sodium chloride conduct electricity?
10. *(Supplement)* Explain why sodium chloride has a high melting point.
11. A student draws a shared pair of electrons between Na and Cl. What is wrong?
12. Give one ion with the configuration 2,8 that is a cation and one that is an anion.

**Answers**

1. A 2+ ion (a cation); it loses its two outer electrons to gain a noble gas configuration.
2. A strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
3. 2,8,8; argon.
4. Seven of one symbol (bromine's own outer electrons) and one of the other (the
   electron from potassium): eight in total.
5. Sulfide ion.
6. Ca loses two electrons and O gains two, so the ratio is 1 : 1: CaO.
7. Na⁺ and N³⁻: three Na⁺ balance one N³⁻, so Na₃N.
8. A giant lattice: a regular arrangement of alternating positive (K⁺) and negative (Cl⁻)
   ions.
9. In the molten state the ions are free to move and carry charge.
10. It has a giant lattice with strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely
    charged ions in all directions, so a large amount of energy is needed to overcome them.
11. Sharing electrons is covalent bonding; in sodium chloride an electron is transferred
    and the ions are drawn separately in brackets.
12. Cation: Na⁺, Mg²⁺ or Al³⁺. Anion: O²⁻ or F⁻ (or N³⁻).
