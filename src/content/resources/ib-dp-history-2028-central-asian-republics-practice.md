This **higher level (HL) only** set covers the regional study of the emergence of Central Asian republics (1917 to 2020) from the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Every Paper 3 question on this study requires you to refer to one republic; the answers below use Kazakhstan, and you may substitute your own. ## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why local elites and intellectuals in one Central Asian republic resisted Bolshevik control between 1917 and 1924. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "They counted our sheep and our goats, then took them for the state. We were told to settle by the new well and farm the land. By winter there was nothing left to eat, and the road to the border was full of families." (An oral testimony, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the effects of collectivisation. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of oral testimony as evidence of the effects of collectivisation. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the environmental impact of Soviet policies in Central Asia. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent did Soviet policies between 1920 and 1940 transform one Central Asian republic? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the reasons for the establishment of an independent republic in 1991. **[15]**

**6.** Explain the significance of the Virgin Lands Campaign for Kazakhstan. **[6]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which the Second World War affected one Central Asian republic. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). Kazakh nationalists in the Alash Orda wanted autonomy for Kazakhs and feared rule by a party dominated by Russians; reformist Muslim intellectuals (Jadids) were suspicious of Bolshevik attacks on Islam and customs; requisitioning of grain and livestock during the Civil War angered herders and farmers; many elites had little say in the new Soviet institutions, and resented Russian settlers who gained from Soviet land policy. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests that herds were seized and counted by the state (1), that the policy forced nomads to settle and farm (1), and that it led to famine and flight (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. Oral testimony is recalled long after the event, so memory can change or be shaped by later stories (1); one account is individual and may not be typical of the whole republic, where experience varied by region (1); it cannot tell the historian the total number of deaths or the state's reasons, which need official records and demographic data (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): the diversion of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya for cotton irrigation shrank the Aral Sea (up to 2); fishing collapsed and dust containing salts and chemicals harmed health (up to 1); the Semipalatinsk nuclear tests, 456 between 1949 and 1989, caused radiation illness and contamination (up to 2); the Virgin Lands Campaign led to soil erosion (up to 1). **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "transform" (up to 2).
- Political change: creation of the republic, 1936 constitution, one-party rule, the Great Terror (up to 3).
- Economic change: collectivisation, forced settlement, the famine, the Turksib railway, industry (up to 4).
- Social and cultural change: the hujum, attack on religion, literacy, script changes, Russification (up to 3).
- Continuities: clan networks, Islam in private life, local identity (up to 2).
- A judgement consistent with the evidence, with a view of historians (up to 1). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis that ranks reasons (up to 2).
- Gorbachev's policies: glasnost and perestroika, the economic crisis, the loosening of party control (up to 3).
- Protest and resistance: Jeltoqsan (1986), anti-nuclear and environmental movements, ethnic violence elsewhere (up to 3).
- The collapse of the USSR: the August 1991 coup attempt, the declarations of Russia, Ukraine and others, and the dissolution on 26 December (up to 3).
- The role of leaders: Nazarbayev and others, who accepted independence once the union failed (up to 2).
- A judgement, for example that independence was the result of the Soviet collapse rather than a mass independence movement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): the campaign from 1954 ploughed up large areas of northern grassland for grain (up to 1); it produced large early harvests and eased Soviet food shortages (up to 1); it brought about a million settlers, mainly Slavs, so that Kazakhs became about 30 per cent of the population in 1959 (up to 2); it caused soil erosion and falling yields, and shaped later debates over identity and language (up to 2). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the effect, 1 for explanation). Evacuated factories and workers from the west boosted industry and mixed the population; deported peoples such as Koreans, Germans and Chechens were settled there, and many died; large numbers of men served in the Red Army and many were killed; the government eased controls on religion and created an official Muslim board in 1943. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need dates for the declarations of independence?**
Yes, in outline. Know that Kazakhstan was last, on 16 December 1991, and the USSR ended on 26 December.

**How should I handle the word genocide for the famine?**
Present it as a debate. Explain the evidence for and against, and state which view you find better supported.
