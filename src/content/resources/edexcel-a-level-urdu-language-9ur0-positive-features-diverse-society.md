This guide covers **sub-theme 3.1, the positive features of a diverse society (ایک متنوع اور گوناگوں معاشرے کے مثبت پہلو)**, of Theme 3 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). The specification names three aspects:

- **پاکستان میں کمیونٹی سے پیوستگی**: community cohesion in Pakistan
- **ایک متنوع معاشرے کے کلچر پر اثرات**: the effects of a diverse society on culture
- **معاشی ترقی میں مہاجرین کا کردار**: the role of migrants in economic development

Theme 3 is set in the context of **Pakistan**. That matters here: the sub-theme asks you to look at diversity inside Pakistan, a country shaped by migration from its first day. The [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-positive-features-of-a-diverse-society-revision-notes/) introduce the general benefits of diversity; this page develops each aspect with Pakistani examples. See the [Theme 3 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-theme-3-study-guide/) for the whole theme, and practise with the [diverse society practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-positive-features-diverse-society-practice/).

The sub-theme is not a research subject, so it reaches you through translation, reading and Paper 3 listening and evaluation.

## Pakistan as a diverse society

Before the aspects, fix the picture of diversity you will draw on:

- **Languages**: Urdu as the national language and link language, alongside Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Saraiki, Brahui and many others.
- **Religion and sect**: a Muslim majority with Sunni and Shia communities and other groups, and religious minorities including Christians, Hindus and Sikhs.
- **Migration**: the millions who arrived from India in 1947, known as **مہاجرین** (migrants), Afghan refugees since 1979, and large internal migration from villages and smaller provinces to cities, above all Karachi.

Karachi is the strongest single example for this sub-theme: a port city whose population includes people from every province and many communities, so that it is often described as a small version of Pakistan.

## Aspect 1: community cohesion in Pakistan (کمیونٹی سے پیوستگی)

**پیوستگی** means "being joined, bonded". Cohesion is what holds different groups together. Organise examples by **who** builds it:

- **Neighbourhoods**: in a **محلہ**, families of different origins share weddings, funerals and Eid greetings; neighbours help in emergencies. Use these everyday examples rather than only national ones.
- **Welfare and charity**: the Edhi Foundation, founded by Abdul Sattar Edhi, became famous for serving people regardless of religion, ethnicity or origin. **لنگر** (free food) at shrines feeds anyone who comes.
- **Shared places and occasions**: Sufi shrines attract visitors from different communities; national days and cricket matches bring people together across regions.
- **The state**: Pakistan marks **قومی یومِ اقلیت** (National Minorities Day) on 11 August, recalling Jinnah's speech of 11 August 1947 to the Constituent Assembly about equal citizenship. The Kartarpur Corridor, opened in 2019, allows Sikh pilgrims from India to visit the gurdwara at Kartarpur.

Because this is a sub-theme about **positive** features, your examples should mainly show cohesion working. But a strong answer names its conditions: tolerance (**رواداری**), equal treatment under the law and contact between groups. Where these are weak, tension follows, and an honest answer can say so briefly.

## Aspect 2: the effects of a diverse society on culture

Here diversity is a source of cultural richness. Develop three strands:

**Language.** Urdu developed through contact and continues to absorb words from regional languages and English. In Pakistani cities, people often switch between Urdu and their regional language within a single conversation. Urdu's role as a shared language allows Sindhi, Pashtun, Punjabi and Baloch Pakistanis to understand one another.

**Food.** Karachi's food culture shows layers of migration: dishes brought by families from Delhi, Lucknow and Hyderabad Deccan after 1947 sit beside Sindhi, Pashtun, Baloch and Punjabi cooking. A single street can offer many regional cuisines.

**Arts and heritage.** Music and poetry move across language boundaries: Sufi poetry in Punjabi and Sindhi is sung throughout the country, and Pashto and Balochi music reach national audiences through television. Religious minorities have also shaped institutions: Christian missionary schools and colleges, such as Forman Christian College in Lahore, have educated generations of Pakistanis of all faiths, and Karachi's small Parsi community played a notable part in the city's civic life and institutions.

The analytical question is whether diversity produces **mixing** (a shared new culture), **coexistence** (separate cultures side by side), or both. Most evidence suggests both, and saying so is more convincing than claiming complete harmony.

## Aspect 3: the role of migrants in economic development

**مہاجرین** here can include the migrants of 1947, later refugees and internal migrants. Points to develop:

- **The migrants of 1947**: many were traders, artisans, civil servants and professionals who helped build the administration, trade and industry of the new state, particularly in Karachi, which became the first capital and the main commercial centre.
- **Internal migrants**: workers from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and elsewhere power transport, construction, ports and factories in Karachi and other cities; their remittances support families back home.
- **Afghan refugees**: many have worked in sectors such as transport, construction, carpet-weaving and small trade. Their presence is also debated in terms of pressure on resources, which links to sub-theme 3.2.

For each group, say **what** they contributed (skills, labour, capital, trade links, enterprise) and **why** migrants often contribute disproportionately: people who leave home are frequently ambitious, willing to take risks and connected to networks in several places.

## Worked example: translating into English

Original passage, written for this guide:

> کراچی کی کسی بھی مصروف سڑک پر کھڑے ہو جائیں تو چند ہی منٹوں میں آپ کے کانوں میں پشتو، سندھی، پنجابی اور اردو کی آوازیں پڑیں گی۔ یہ شہر اُن لوگوں نے آباد کیا جو روزگار کی تلاش میں ملک کے کونے کونے سے یہاں آئے، اور آج اس کی رونق انھی کی محنت کا نتیجہ ہے۔

**Model translation:** Stand on any busy road in Karachi and within a few minutes you will hear Pashto, Sindhi, Punjabi and Urdu. This city was built up by people who came here from every corner of the country in search of work, and its vibrancy today is the result of their hard work.

**Points to notice:**

- **کھڑے ہو جائیں تو** is a conditional with an implied "if"; English handles it naturally with an imperative ("Stand ... and").
- **آپ کے کانوں میں ... آوازیں پڑیں گی** is an idiom ("sounds will fall into your ears"): render it simply as "you will hear". The verb is feminine plural because it agrees with آوازیں.
- **آباد کیا** means "populated, built up, settled", not "made rich".
- **ملک کے کونے کونے سے**: the doubled noun gives "from every corner".
- **انھی کی محنت**: انھی with emphatic ی means "their very own" hard work; "their" is enough in English.

## Worked example: building a positive argument with a condition

A Paper 3 evaluation might ask whether diversity strengthens Pakistan. A model sentence that makes a positive case but keeps balance:

> پاکستان کا تنوع اس کی کمزوری نہیں بلکہ طاقت ہے، بشرطیکہ تمام برادریوں کو قانون کی نظر میں برابر سمجھا جائے اور انھیں ایک دوسرے سے ملنے جلنے کے مواقع میسر ہوں۔

(Pakistan's diversity is not its weakness but its strength, provided that all communities are treated as equal in the eyes of the law and have opportunities to mix with one another.)

**بشرطیکہ** (provided that) introduces the condition and takes a subjunctive (**سمجھا جائے**, **میسر ہوں**). It is an efficient way to show complex language while keeping the argument balanced.

## Common mistakes

- Writing only about Pakistanis in Britain: the specification places this sub-theme in Pakistan, and the first aspect names Pakistan explicitly.
- Confusing **مہاجر** (migrant who arrives) with **تارکِ وطن** (one who leaves the homeland).
- Listing cultures without explaining the **effect** of diversity on culture.
- Presenting cohesion as automatic, with no conditions.
- Writing **برادری** with masculine agreement; it is feminine (**ہر برادری اپنی روایات رکھتی ہے**).

## Exam technique

- In reading texts on this sub-theme, look for words of unity (**یکجہتی**, **بھائی چارہ**, **ہم آہنگی**) and words of division (**تعصب**, **فرقہ واریت**, **امتیاز**) to identify a writer's stance quickly.
- When asked **وضاحت کیجیے**, give the mechanism: not only that migrants built businesses, but why migrants tend to be enterprising.
- Use **بشرطیکہ**, **اگرچہ** and **جس کے نتیجے میں** to make your argument both positive and nuanced.
