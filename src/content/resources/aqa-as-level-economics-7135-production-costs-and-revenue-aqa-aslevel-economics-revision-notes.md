These notes condense **3.1.3 Production, costs and revenue** of AQA AS Economics (7135), specification version 1.3, parts 3.1.3.1 to 3.1.3.5. The [3.1.3 study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-production-costs-and-revenue-aqa-aslevel-economics/) explains each idea with a full cost and revenue table, and the [3.1.3 practice questions](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-production-costs-and-revenue-aqa-aslevel-economics-practice/) test it. All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Production | Converting inputs (factor services) into final output |
| Productivity | Output per unit of input in a period |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker or per worker-hour |
| Specialisation | Concentrating on a limited range of tasks or products |
| Division of labour | Splitting production into separate tasks, each done by different workers |
| Medium of exchange | Something generally accepted in payment for goods and services |
| Short run | Period in which at least one factor of production is fixed |
| Long run | Period in which all factors of production are variable |
| Fixed cost | Cost that does not vary with output in the short run |
| Variable cost | Cost that varies directly with output |
| Average cost | Total cost divided by output |
| Total revenue (TR) | Price x quantity sold |
| Average revenue (AR) | TR / quantity, which equals price |
| Profit | TR - TC |
| Internal economies of scale | Falls in long-run average cost from the firm's own growth |
| External economies of scale | Falls in long-run average cost from the growth of the industry |
| Diseconomies of scale | Rises in long-run average cost as the firm grows too large |

## Formulae

```
Labour productivity = total output / number of workers
TC  = TFC + TVC
ATC = TC / Q        AFC = TFC / Q        AVC = TVC / Q
ATC = AFC + AVC
TR  = P x Q         AR = TR / Q = P
Profit = TR - TC    (negative = loss)
```

**Quick check.** A firm makes 250 units. TFC = 1,000, TVC = 2,750. TC = 3,750, ATC = 15, AFC = 4, AVC = 11. Price is 18, so TR = 4,500 and profit = 750.

## Recall by part

**3.1.3.1** Productivity can rise through better capital, training, organisation or motivation. Higher productivity at the same wage cuts labour cost per unit.

**3.1.3.2** Benefits of the division of labour: skill through repetition, no time lost switching tasks, workers matched to tasks, specialist machinery becomes worthwhile. Costs: monotony, dependence on every stage, narrow skills. Specialists must trade; barter needs a double coincidence of wants, so money as a medium of exchange makes specialisation workable.

**3.1.3.3** Short-run ATC is likely to be U-shaped (no formal diminishing-returns link needed at AS). AFC always falls as output rises. The long-run average cost (LRAC) curve's shape is set by economies and diseconomies of scale.

**3.1.3.4** Internal economies: technical, purchasing, managerial, financial, marketing, risk-bearing. External economies: skilled labour pool, nearby specialist suppliers, local training and shared infrastructure. Diseconomies: poor communication, coordination problems, low motivation, weak control. LRAC falls, may flatten, then rises.

**3.1.3.5** AR is the firm's demand curve because AR = price, and the demand curve shows the price at which each quantity can be sold. The most profitable output is where TR - TC is largest.

## AS limits

- No marginal cost or marginal revenue: find the best output by comparing profit at each level.
- No formal link between the U-shaped short-run curve and diminishing returns.
- Technological change belongs to the A-level content, not 3.1.3.

## Exam traps

- Comparing total cost across firm sizes instead of average cost.
- Using price minus AVC as "profit per unit"; profit per unit is price minus ATC.
- Listing types of economy of scale without saying why size lowers average cost.
- Treating external economies as something the firm does; they come from the industry or area.
- Saying the firm should choose the output with the highest revenue.
- Describing money's role vaguely ("it makes trade easier") without the double coincidence of wants.

## Self-test

1. A workshop's 12 workers make 1,800 chairs a month. Calculate labour productivity.
2. Productivity rises to 165 chairs per worker. Calculate the percentage rise.
3. Give two benefits of the division of labour.
4. Why does specialisation require an efficient means of exchange?
5. Classify as fixed or variable: rent of a factory; flour used by a bakery; insurance; electricity used by machines.
6. TFC = 600 and TVC = 900 at an output of 150. Calculate ATC and AFC.
7. At output 200, ATC = 9.50. Calculate total cost.
8. A firm sells 40 units for a TR of 1,400. Calculate AR.
9. Explain why the AR curve is the firm's demand curve.
10. Give one internal and one external economy of scale for a car manufacturer.
11. State two causes of diseconomies of scale.
12. TR is 5,200 and TC is 5,450. State the profit or loss.

### Answers

1. 1,800 / 12 = 150 chairs per worker.
2. (165 - 150) / 150 = 10%.
3. Any two: skills improve with repetition; no time lost switching tasks; workers matched to tasks; specialist machinery can be used.
4. Specialists produce a surplus of one good and must trade for everything else; money avoids the need for a double coincidence of wants.
5. Fixed, variable, fixed, variable.
6. TC = 1,500; ATC = 10; AFC = 4.
7. 200 x 9.50 = 1,900.
8. 1,400 / 40 = 35, which is the price.
9. AR equals the price, and the demand curve shows the price at which each quantity can be sold, so the two curves are the same.
10. Internal: technical, such as large robotic production lines, or purchasing discounts on steel. External: a cluster of component suppliers nearby, or a local pool of trained engineers.
11. Any two: communication problems, coordination of many sites, low worker motivation, weaker control of costs.
12. A loss of 250.
