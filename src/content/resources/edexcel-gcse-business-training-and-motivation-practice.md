These questions cover Theme 2, Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions, items 2.5.3 (Effective training and development) and 2.5.4 (Motivation), from the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. ## Questions

**1.** Define the term "performance review". **[2]**

**2.** Which one of the following is a non-financial method of motivating employees? **[1]**
A Commission
B Job rotation
C Bonus
D Promotion

**3.** Give two examples of fringe benefits. **[2]**

**4.** Ostler Interiors pays a sales adviser a basic wage of £1,450 a month plus commission of 3% of sales. Sales in March were £52,000. Calculate the adviser's total pay for March. **[3]**

**5.** Tarrant Parcels sorted 2,520 parcels in one month using 9 sorters. After the owner gave sorters more responsibility for planning their own shifts, the firm sorted 3,150 parcels the next month using 10 sorters.
**(a)** Calculate the number of parcels per sorter in the first month. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in parcels per sorter between the first and second months. **[3]**

**6.** Explain one way in which self-learning can help an employee to develop. **[3]**

**7.** Hollis Cafes employs 56 staff. Last year 14 of them left. The owner wants to train staff in barista skills.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of staff who left last year. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline one way in which barista training could reduce the number of staff leaving Hollis Cafes. **[2]**

**8.** Larkfield Printers has 20 operators who have used the same printing presses for many years. The owner is replacing them with digital presses and the supplier offers a training course on the new machines. Analyse how retraining the operators could benefit Larkfield Printers. **[6]**

**9.** Brackley Optical employs 12 staff. The owner is considering sending all of them on a formal lens-fitting course. Each course costs £380. Each employee will also lose £150 of output while away from work. The owner expects each trained employee to bring in extra gross profit of £25 a week for 30 weeks.
**(a)** Calculate the total cost of training all 12 employees. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the net benefit of the training over the 30 weeks. **[3]**
**(c)** Give one reason why the real net benefit might be lower than your answer to (b). **[1]**

**10.** Discuss the importance of motivation in the workplace. **[6]**

**11.** Fennel and Co sells kitchen equipment and has 5 sales employees. Each employee currently sells £36,000 a month. The gross profit margin is 30%. The owner is choosing between two options.

Option A: pay each employee commission of 2% of all the sales they make. The owner forecasts that this would raise sales by 10%.
Option B: give each employee a fringe benefit costing £100 a month. No change in sales is forecast. Two of the five employees left last year and each replacement cost £2,400. The owner thinks the benefit might prevent one employee leaving each year.

Justify which option the owner should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Ruddock Plumbing employs 12 plumbers. The owner wants to improve how well they use a new job-booking app and to keep experienced plumbers. The owner is considering target setting and performance reviews twice a year for every plumber. Each review would take 90 minutes of the owner's time, which the owner values at £40 an hour. Evaluate whether target setting and performance reviews are the best way for Ruddock Plumbing to achieve its aims. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A performance review is a meeting between a manager and an employee [1] where work is assessed against targets, feedback is given and next steps are agreed [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition tends to earn more when it says who meets and what is done. "Checking staff" is too vague to show the idea.

**2.** **B** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Test each option against the specification lists. Commission, bonus and promotion are financial methods, and job rotation is non-financial.

**3.** Any two from: staff discount, free meals, health cover, company car, gym membership [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Give" asks for recall, so no development is needed. Two different benefits are needed, and "more pay" would not be a fringe benefit.

**4.** Commission = 3% of £52,000 = 0.03 × 52,000 [1] = £1,560 [1]. Total pay = 1,450 + 1,560 = **£3,010** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution. An answer of £1,560 alone shows only part of the pay, because the basic wage must be added.

**5. (a)** Productivity = output ÷ number of employees [1] = 2,520 ÷ 9 = **280 parcels per sorter** [1]
**(b)** Second month: 3,150 ÷ 10 = 315 [1]. Percentage change = (315 − 280) ÷ 280 × 100 [1] = **12.5% increase** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The firm used more sorters in the second month, so comparing total parcels would mislead. Divide by the starting figure and say whether it is a rise or a fall.

**6.** Self-learning lets the employee choose when and how to learn, for example through an online module [1]. This means the employee can fit learning around work without needing time away from the job [1]. They can therefore build skills that prepare them for more responsibility [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" asks for a fact and two expansion points. A bare statement such as "it saves money" would be one point only.

**7. (a)** 14 ÷ 56 × 100 [1] = **25%** [1]
**(b)** Training in barista skills can make staff more confident [1], so they enjoy their work more and are less likely to look for another job [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" asks for two linked points placed in the case. Name the training in the question, then say what follows from it.

**8.** Retraining teaches operators how to use the digital presses [1]. They can then run the machines correctly, with fewer mistakes and less wasted paper [1]. Output per operator is likely to rise [1], so Larkfield gets the return it hoped for from the investment in the new presses [1]. Operators who have used the old presses for years may fear for their jobs [1]. Being retrained should make them more confident and motivated, so they are more likely to stay, which saves recruitment costs [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" places the answer in the case. Mentioning the long-serving operators and the digital presses scores better than general points about technology.

**9. (a)** Cost per employee = 380 + 150 = £530 [1]. Total = 12 × 530 [1] = **£6,360** [1]
**(b)** Benefit per employee = 25 × 30 = £750 [1]. Total benefit = 12 × 750 = £9,000 [1]. Net benefit = 9,000 − 6,360 = **£2,640** [1]
**(c)** Any one: a trained employee leaves for a rival; the extra gross profit is lower than the owner expects; the course takes longer than planned [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not forget the lost output: the course fee alone gives a different total. Labelling each step (cost, benefit, net) makes the working easy to follow.

**10.** Motivation helps a business attract employees [1]. A business known for good pay and interesting work receives more applications and can choose better candidates [1]. It helps retain employees [1], so recruitment and training costs fall and experience stays in the business [1]. It also raises productivity [1], because motivated employees concentrate better and make fewer errors, so output per worker rises [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" has no context, but each reason needs expanding. The three reasons the specification names (attracting, retaining, productivity) make a clear structure.

**11.** Option A. Commission if sales stayed at £180,000 (5 × £36,000) would be 2% × 180,000 = £3,600 a month [1]. A 10% rise gives sales of £198,000 [1]. Extra gross profit = 30% × 18,000 = £5,400 [1]. Commission on the new sales = 2% × 198,000 = £3,960 [1]. Net gain = 5,400 − 3,960 = £1,440 a month [1].
Option B costs 5 × £100 = £500 a month, or £6,000 a year [1]. It has no forecast sales effect, and preventing one leaver would save only £2,400 a year [1], less than the cost.
The risk with A is that sales must rise by about 7.1% for it to pay for itself; a smaller rise would give a net loss, and pay becomes uncertain for staff [1].
I recommend Option A [1], because on the owner's forecast it adds £1,440 a month while B costs more than it is expected to save. A sales rise of at least 7.1% is needed, so the owner should review results after a few months. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* "Justify" asks for one option, backed by the figures given. Naming a condition (the sales rise needed) and the weakness of the rejected option strengthens the case.

**12.** Cost: 12 plumbers × 2 reviews × 1.5 hours = 36 hours, and 36 × £40 = £1,440 a year [1]. Targets would show plumbers what is expected in using the app [1]. Reviews give feedback and show who needs more training [1]. Feeling valued and clear about the job can raise motivation and help keep experienced plumbers [1]. However, a review changes nothing unless training follows [1], and unrealistic targets could cause stress for plumbers who dislike new technology [1]. Alternatives are informal coaching from a plumber who already uses the app [1], or a bonus for keeping experienced plumbers, though the latter adds to cost [1]. Judgement: reviews and targets are a good start but are not enough by themselves [1], because they identify needs without solving them. They work best when combined with coaching and with fair targets. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer tends to do better when it weighs advantages against drawbacks, uses figures from the case, and ends with a supported conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "profit" or "pay" when asked for a fringe benefit.
- Giving total pay as the commission alone and leaving out the basic wage.
- Comparing total output when the number of employees has changed, instead of output per employee.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure instead of the original.
- Leaving out lost output when working out the cost of training.
- Giving one point with no expansion on an "explain" question.
- Writing in general terms when the question names the business and its situation.
- Giving only the benefits of a method and ignoring its cost or drawback.
- Stopping an "evaluate" or "justify" answer before reaching a clear decision.
