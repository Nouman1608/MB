## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** What is the name for the minimum amount of wealth that a Muslim must hold before Zakah is due? **[1]**

**2.** What fraction or percentage of qualifying savings is paid as Zakah? **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** of the groups of people who may receive Zakah according to Qur'an 9:60. **[2]**

**4.** Hassan has held savings of 24,000 for a full lunar year. He owes no debts, and the Nisaab in his country is 5,000. Calculate the Zakah he must pay. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the difference between Zakah and sadaqah. **[4]**

**6.** A farmer harvests 4,000 kg of dates. Explain how much Ushr is due if the trees were watered by rain, and how much if they were watered by a costly pump, and why the two rates differ. **[4]**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark questions give one mark for each point and one for explaining it, or one mark for each
accurate detail. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a
general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question.
The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** The Nisaab [1].

**2.** 2.5 per cent, or one-fortieth [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: the poor; the needy; those employed to collect Zakah; bringing hearts together; freeing captives or slaves; those in debt; the cause of Allah; the stranded traveller.

**4.** 24,000 is above the Nisaab, so Zakah is due [1]. 24,000 x 2.5% = 24,000 x 0.025 = 600 [1].

**5.** Any four, [1] each, with a mark for development where marked:

- Zakah is compulsory, a Pillar of Islam [1]; sadaqah is voluntary [1].
- Zakah is a fixed rate (2.5 per cent) on qualifying wealth above the Nisaab [1]; sadaqah can be any amount at any time [1].
- Zakah is paid once the wealth has been held for a lunar year [1]; sadaqah has no such condition [1].
- Zakah goes to the eight groups in Qur'an 9:60 [1]; sadaqah may be given to anyone in need, or to a good cause [1].

**6.** Rain: 10 per cent of 4,000 = 400 kg [1]. Costly pump: 5 per cent of 4,000 = 200 kg [1]. The rates differ because irrigation by pump needs extra effort and cost [1], so the farmer pays less, which keeps the system fair [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two benefits, the benefit identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Benefits (AO1):

- **Reduces poverty** [1]: money reaches those who cannot meet their needs, such as the poor and the indebted [1].
- **Narrows the gap between rich and poor** [1]: wealth is spread more fairly so that it does not stay with a few [1].
- **Builds unity** [1]: the giver and receiver feel part of one community, caring for each other [1].
- **Supports the community's needs** [1]: funds help those in debt, stranded travellers and collectors who run the system [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "so that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich among you" (Qur'an 59:7): wealth must circulate through the whole community [2].
- Qur'an 9:60 lists the groups who receive Zakah: it is a system that is designed to meet real social needs [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the poor and the needy are named first in the list; Zakah removes hunger and hardship, and without the poor receiving help the community cannot be just.
- **For the statement:** the system also frees captives and helps those in debt, which also helps the weakest in society.
- **Against the statement:** the passage names eight groups, so the purpose is wider than the poor alone; collectors, new Muslims, travellers and those serving the faith are all included.
- **Against the statement:** Zakah is also a duty to Allah and a means of purification for the giver; this is why it is an "obligation [imposed] by Allah".
- **Balanced view:** helping the poor is a main purpose, but obedience, purification and unity are equally important to Muslims.

*Use of the passage*

- "for the poor and for the needy" supports the statement directly.
- "for those in debt", "freeing captives" and "the [stranded] traveller" show care for different kinds of hardship.
- "an obligation [imposed] by Allah" shows the first purpose is worship.
- "Knowing and Wise" shows that Allah designed the system with a purpose and wisdom beyond a single group.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is a command from Allah** [1]: Zakah is a Pillar, and Muslims obey even when it is hard [1].
- **Wealth is a trust** [1]: Muslims believe their money belongs to Allah, so giving part of it returns a share to those who have a right to it [1].
- **It purifies the giver** [1]: giving removes the pull of greed and keeps the heart focused on Allah [1].
- **It protects the community** [1]: the rich today may need the help of others in future [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them" (Qur'an 9:103) [2].
- "establish prayer and give Zakah" (Qur'an 2:43): Zakah is placed next to prayer as a pillar of the faith [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** giving builds generosity, humility and gratitude; the giver learns that wealth is a trust.
- **For the statement:** paying Zakah in line with the rules shows honesty and discipline.
- **For the statement:** it brings the giver closer to the community and teaches compassion for those in need.
- **Against the statement:** a person can pay Zakah and still behave badly in other ways; the benefit depends on sincerity, not only on payment.
- **Against the statement:** being a better person also needs prayer, honesty and good character, so Zakah alone does not make a person better.
- **Balanced view:** Zakah is a strong tool for building character when it is given sincerely and together with the other Pillars.

*Use of the passage*

- "an obligation [imposed] by Allah" shows that obedience is part of being a good Muslim.
- "Knowing and Wise" suggests Allah knows how giving shapes the person.
- The list of recipients, such as "the poor and the needy" and "those in debt", shows who the giver learns to care for.
- The word "only" at the start of the verse shows that Zakah must be spent on the right groups, which teaches responsibility and honesty.
