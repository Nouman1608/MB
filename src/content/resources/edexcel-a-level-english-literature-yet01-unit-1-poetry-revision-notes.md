Condensed notes for the poetry section of Unit 1 (WET01) in Pearson Edexcel International A Level
English Literature (YET01). For the full method and a worked comparison, use the
[Unit 1 Post-2000 Poetry study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-english-literature-yet01-unit-1-poetry/).

## The task in one box

| Detail | Section A |
|---|---|
| Question | One from a choice of two; comparative |
| Poems | One named poem plus your own choice of a second from the prescribed list |
| Format | Open book; list of prescribed poems printed in a Source Booklet |
| Marks | 25 of the paper's 50 |
| AOs | AO1, AO2, AO4 (no AO3) |
| Weighting | Unit 1 is 50% of the International AS and 25% of the International A Level |

## Key features of poetry (specification list)

Form and structure · language choice · imagery, symbols and motifs · rhyme and rhythm · theme · tone and
mood · voice.

## AO checklist

| AO | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Is my thesis comparative, personal and sustained? Is my terminology accurate and useful? |
| AO2 | Have I analysed form, structure and language, and explained effects? |
| AO4 | Is there a connection in every paragraph, in methods as well as ideas? |

## Comparison paragraph frame

1. Comparative point (both poems named).
2. Poem A: method → reference → effect.
3. Poem B: method → reference → effect.
4. Connection: what the difference reveals.

**Useful connectives:** *whereas*, *by contrast*, *similarly*, *both poets*, *while A... B instead...*,
*this difference suggests...*

## Key terms to use with discrimination

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dramatic monologue | A poem spoken by a character, often revealing more than they intend |
| Volta | A turn in argument or mood |
| Enjambment | A sentence running over a line break |
| Caesura | A pause within a line |
| Chiasmus | A reversed repetition (A B / B A) |
| Free verse | Verse without regular metre or rhyme |
| Apostrophe | Direct address to an absent person, thing or idea |
| Intertextuality | Deliberate echo of another text |
| Persona | The speaker as distinct from the poet |
| Semantic field | A group of words from one area of meaning |
| Tone | The attitude a poem conveys |
| Motif | A recurring image or idea |

## Choosing the second poem in two minutes

- Does it share the **focus**?
- Does it treat it **differently**?
- Does it offer **formal contrast**?
- Do you know where its **key lines** are?

## Traps

- Two separate essays and a closing comparison.
- Context paragraphs (AO3 is not assessed here).
- Picking a second poem you know well but which barely fits.
- Paraphrasing instead of analysing.
- Ignoring how each poem ends.
- Using a term incorrectly to sound sophisticated.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Section A worth, and which AOs are assessed?
2. Which AO is not assessed in Section A?
3. What is the structure of the question?
4. Where is the list of prescribed poems printed in the exam?
5. Name the seven key features of poetry in the specification.
6. What does AO4 require?
7. Give three tests for choosing a second poem.
8. Why organise by point of comparison?
9. What is a dramatic monologue?
10. What does "terminology with discrimination" mean?
11. Name two prescribed poems that could be compared on memory or childhood.
12. What proportion of the International AS is Unit 1 worth?

**Answers:** 1. 25 marks; AO1, AO2, AO4. 2. AO3. 3. One named poem compared with a second poem of your
choice from the prescribed list. 4. In a Source Booklet. 5. Form and structure; language choice;
imagery, symbols and motifs; rhyme and rhythm; theme; tone and mood; voice. 6. Exploring connections
across literary texts. 7. Shares the focus; treats it differently; offers formal contrast (also: you
can find its key lines). 8. It keeps both poems in every paragraph, making AO4 visible throughout.
9. A poem spoken by a character, often revealing more than they intend. 10. Choosing the right term and
using it to reach an insight, not as decoration. 11. Any two of *To My Nine-Year-Old Self*, *Material*,
*Out of the Bag*, *Genetics* (or other defensible pairs). 12. 50%.
