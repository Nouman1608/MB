These questions cover section 5.5, Compound shapes and parts of shapes, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [compound shapes and parts of shapes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes/), and the [compound shapes and parts of shapes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-revision-notes/) summarise them. Use the π key on your calculator. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** An L-shape is made by removing a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle from the corner of a 14 cm by 9 cm rectangle. Find its area and its perimeter. **[3]**

**2.** A circle of radius 6 cm is drawn inside a square of side 12 cm, touching all four sides. Find the area of the square that is outside the circle. **[2]**

**3.** A chord subtends an angle of 90° at the centre of a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the area of the minor segment. **[3]**

**4.** A solid is half of a cylinder of radius 4 cm and length 9 cm, cut along its length through the axis. Find its volume in terms of π. **[2]**

**5.** A pipe has outer radius 6 cm, inner radius 5 cm and length 30 cm. Find the volume of material in the pipe. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A shape is made from a rectangle 10 cm by 5 cm with a semicircle on one of the 5 cm sides. Find its area and its perimeter. **[4]**

**7.** A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 8 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Find the volume and the total surface area, in terms of π. **[5]**

**8.** An ice-cream is modelled as a cone of height 8 cm and base radius 3 cm, on top of a hemisphere of radius 3 cm. The flat face of the hemisphere is joined to the base of the cone.

**(a)** Find the volume.

**(b)** Find the slant height of the cone, and the total outer surface area. **[5]**

**9.** A cone has base radius 10 cm and height 20 cm. A smaller cone, with base radius 5 cm and height 10 cm, is cut off the top, leaving a frustum.

**(a)** Find the volume of the frustum.

**(b)** Find the total surface area of the frustum. **[6]**

**10.** A shape is made from a rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm with an isosceles triangle on one of the 8 cm sides. The triangle has base 8 cm and height 3 cm.

**(a)** Find the area of the shape.

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the shape. **[4]**

**11.** A cuboid measures 10 cm by 6 cm by 4 cm. A cylindrical hole of radius 1 cm is drilled through the 4 cm dimension. Find the volume of the remaining solid. **[3]**

**12.** The volume of a cone is 800 cm³. A small cone, similar to it with a scale factor of 1/2, is cut off the top. Find the volume of the frustum. **[2]**

**13.** A running track is a rectangle 90 m by 60 m with a semicircle at each end of the 60 m sides. Find the total area enclosed, and the length around the outside, to the nearest metre. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Area = 14 × 9 − 6 × 4 = 126 − 24 = **102 cm²** [1][1]. Perimeter = 2(14 + 9) = **46 cm** [1].

**2.** Square = 144 cm² and circle = 36π [1]. Area outside = 144 − 36π = **30.9 cm²** [1].

**3.** Sector = 1/4 × π × 100 = 25π [1]. Triangle = 1/2 × 10 × 10 = 50 [1]. Segment = 25π − 50 = **28.5 cm²** [1].

**4.** Volume = 1/2 × π × 4² × 9 [1] = **72π cm³** [1].

**5.** Cross-section = π × 36 − π × 25 = 11π [1]. Volume = 11π × 30 [1] = 330π = **1040 cm³** [1].

**6.** Semicircle radius 2.5. Area = 50 + 1/2 × π × 6.25 = 50 + 3.125π [1] = **59.8 cm²** [1]. Perimeter = 10 + 10 + 5 + π × 2.5 [1] = **32.9 cm** [1].

**7.** Volume = π × 9 × 8 + 2/3 × π × 27 = 72π + 18π = **90π cm³** [1][1]. Surface area = base 9π + cylinder curved 2π × 3 × 8 = 48π + hemisphere curved 2π × 9 = 18π [1][1], total **75π cm²** [1].

**8. (a)** Cone = 1/3 × π × 9 × 8 = 24π and hemisphere = 2/3 × π × 27 = 18π [1]. Volume = 42π = **132 cm³** [1].

**(b)** Slant height = √(9 + 64) = √73 = **8.54 cm** [1]. Cone curved surface = π × 3 × 8.544 = 80.5 [1]. Hemisphere curved surface = 2π × 9 = 56.5. Total = **137 cm²** [1].

**9. (a)** Big cone = 1/3 × π × 100 × 20 = 2000π/3 and small cone = 1/3 × π × 25 × 10 = 250π/3 [1]. Frustum = 1750π/3 = **1830 cm³** [1].

**(b)** Slant heights: √500 = 22.36 and √125 = 11.18 [1]. Curved surface = π(10 × 22.36 − 5 × 11.18) = 526.9 [1][1]. Circles: 25π + 100π = 392.7. Total = 526.9 + 392.7 = **920 cm²** [1].

**10. (a)** Area = 40 + 1/2 × 8 × 3 = **52 cm²** [1][1].

**(b)** Sloping sides of the triangle = √(4² + 3²) = 5 cm [1]. Perimeter = 5 + 5 + 8 + 5 + 5 = **28 cm** [1].

**11.** Cuboid = 240 cm³ [1]. Hole = π × 1² × 4 = 4π [1]. Remaining = 240 − 4π = **227 cm³** [1].

**12.** The small cone's volume is (1/2)³ = 1/8 of the big cone's, so 100 cm³ [1]. Frustum = 800 − 100 = **700 cm³** [1].

**13.** The two semicircles form a circle of diameter 60 m, so radius 30 m [1]. Area = 90 × 60 + π × 30² = 5400 + 900π [1] = 8227.4, so **8230 m²** [1]. Perimeter = 2 × 90 + 2π × 30 = 180 + 60π [1] = 368.5, so **369 m** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including the joining line in a perimeter, or leaving out the curved edge.
- Counting the faces that are joined together in a compound solid.
- Using the vertical height in the curved area of a cone.
- Forgetting to subtract the small cone from the big cone for a frustum.
- Subtracting radii instead of areas for a pipe.
