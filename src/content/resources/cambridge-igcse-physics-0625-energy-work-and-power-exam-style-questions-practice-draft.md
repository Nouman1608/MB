## Questions

**1.** A force of 45 N moves a box 6.0 m along the floor, in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done on the box. **[2]**

**2.** A motor does 4 800 J of work in 12 s. Calculate its power. **[2]**

**3.** State the energy store that holds the energy in each of the following.

**(a)** A stretched spring. **[1]**

**(b)** A tank of petrol. **[1]**

**(c)** A hot cup of tea. **[1]**

**4.** *(Supplement)* A car of mass 1 200 kg travels at 15 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the car. **[2]**

**(b)** The car now brakes to a stop. The braking force is a constant 6 750 N. Calculate the distance the car travels while it stops. **[2]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* A student of mass 65.0 kg climbs a flight of stairs with a vertical height of 3.20 m, in a time of 8.00 s.

**(a)** Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy of the student. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the average useful power of the student. **[2]**

**6.** *(Supplement)* A ball of mass 0.50 kg is dropped from a height of 4.0 m above the ground. Assume that there is no air resistance. Calculate the speed of the ball just before it hits the ground. **[3]**

**7.** *(Supplement)* A crane motor takes 5 000 J of electrical energy to lift a load of mass 200 kg through a vertical height of 1.5 m.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of the crane. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate how much of the 5 000 J is not usefully transferred. **[1]**

**8.** *(Supplement)* A lift motor has an input power of 4.0 kW and an efficiency of 75%.

**(a)** Calculate the useful output power of the motor. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken for the lift to raise a load of mass 600 kg through a vertical height of 12 m at this useful power. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** State one advantage of generating electrical power using solar cells. **[1]**

**(b)** State one disadvantage of generating electrical power using solar cells. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Supplement)* State the nuclear process that releases energy in the Sun. **[1]**

**10.** A constant force of 80 N pushes a trolley through 5.0 m along a horizontal track. The trolley starts from rest and friction can be ignored.

**(a)** Calculate the work done on the trolley. **[2]**

**(b)** State the energy transferred to the kinetic energy store of the trolley. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Supplement)* The mass of the trolley is 25 kg. Calculate the speed of the trolley at the end of the 5.0 m. **[3]**

## Answers

Marking key: **M** = method mark, **A** = answer mark, **B** = independent mark; a bare **[1]** is one point. **ecf** = error carried forward: a later mark can still be awarded for correct working that uses an earlier wrong value. A correct final answer with no working scores full marks unless the question says "show". Units are required: a missing or wrong unit loses the answer mark. These conventions are to be confirmed by the teacher.

**1.** W = Fd = 45 × 6.0 [M1]; = **270 J** [A1].

**2.** P = W ÷ t = 4 800 ÷ 12 [M1]; = **400 W** [A1].

**3.**

**(a)** **Elastic (strain)** energy store [1].

**(b)** **Chemical** energy store [1].

**(c)** **Internal (thermal)** energy store [1].

**4.**

**(a)** Ek = ½mv² = ½ × 1 200 × 15² [M1]; = 135 000 J, which is **1.35 × 10⁵ J** [A1].

**(b)** The work done by the brakes equals the kinetic energy removed, so W = Fd = 135 000 J [M1]; d = 135 000 ÷ 6 750 = **20.0 m** [A1] (ecf from (a)).

**5.**

**(a)** ΔEp = mgΔh = 65.0 × 9.8 × 3.20 [M1]; = 2 038.4 J, so **2 040 J** [A1] (accept 2 038 J or 2.04 × 10³ J).

**(b)** P = E ÷ t = 2 038.4 ÷ 8.00 [M1]; = 254.8 W, so **255 W** [A1] (ecf from (a)).

**6.** Gain in kinetic energy = loss of gravitational potential energy, so ½mv² = mgΔh [M1]; the mass cancels: v² = 2gΔh = 2 × 9.8 × 4.0 = 78.4 [M1]; v = **8.85 m/s** [A1]. (The same answer follows if the energies are calculated with m = 0.50 kg first: mgΔh = 19.6 J and v = √(2 × 19.6 ÷ 0.50).)

**7.**

**(a)** Useful energy = gain in gravitational potential energy = mgΔh = 200 × 9.8 × 1.5 [M1]; = 2 940 J [A1]; efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input × 100 = 2 940 ÷ 5 000 × 100 [M1]; = **58.8%** [A1] (ecf from the first A mark).

**(b)** 5 000 − 2 940 = **2 060 J** [1] (ecf; accept: transferred to the surroundings as internal (thermal) energy).

**8.**

**(a)** Useful power = efficiency × input power = 0.75 × 4 000 [M1]; = **3 000 W (3.0 kW)** [A1].

**(b)** Work done = mgΔh = 600 × 9.8 × 12 = 70 560 J [M1]; t = W ÷ P = 70 560 ÷ 3 000 [M1]; = **23.5 s** [A1] (ecf from (a)).

**9.**

**(a)** Any one from: it is renewable (the Sun will not run out); no fuel is burned in use, so no carbon dioxide is released; it works in remote places with no grid [1].

**(b)** Any one from: it works only when the Sun is shining (not at night, less in cloud), so it is not reliable; a large area is needed for a large output; the cells are costly to make [1].

**(c)** **Nuclear fusion** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** W = Fd = 80 × 5.0 [M1]; = **400 J** [A1].

**(b)** The work done is equal to the energy transferred (W = ΔE), so **400 J** [1] (ecf from (a)).

**(c)** Ek = ½mv² = 400 J, so ½ × 25 × v² = 400 [M1]; v² = 800 ÷ 25 = 32 [M1]; v = **5.66 m/s** [A1] (ecf from (b)).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to write the unit with the answer, or confusing J with W (energy with power) (questions 1, 2, 7, 8).
- Squaring only the mass, or not squaring the speed, in Ek = ½mv² (questions 4, 6, 10).
- Using the mass instead of mgΔh as the "useful energy" in an efficiency calculation (question 7).
- Writing efficiency as a decimal when the question asks for a percentage, or putting the input energy on the top of the fraction.
- Mixing kW and W: 4.0 kW is 4 000 W (question 8).
- Writing "heat" as an energy store, and "energy is used up" instead of "transferred" or "stored" (question 3).
