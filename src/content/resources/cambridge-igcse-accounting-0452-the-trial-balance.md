This guide covers sub-topic **3.1 The trial balance**. The syllabus lists "the purposes and limitations of a trial balance", "how to prepare a trial balance from a given list of balances and amend a trial balance which contains errors", and "the types of errors which do not affect the trial balance: commission, compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry, principle".

The examples follow Marigold Florists, owned by Esther Lund, with a year ending 30 June 2027. Amounts are in dollars. Use this guide with the [3.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-trial-balance-revision-notes/) and [3.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-trial-balance-practice/). What happens when a trial balance does *not* agree is the subject of [correction of errors](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-correction-of-errors/).

## 1. What a trial balance is for

A **trial balance** is a list of the balances on every ledger account, including the cash book, at a given date, with debit balances in one column and credit balances in the other. Because every transaction has a debit and a credit of equal value, the two totals should agree.

**Purposes**

- To check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry: equal totals show that, for every debit recorded, a credit of the same amount appears to have been recorded.
- To help find errors: if the totals disagree, at least one error exists and must be traced.
- To provide a summary of all balances from which the financial statements are prepared.

**Limitations**

- Agreement does not prove the records are correct, because six types of error leave the totals equal (section 5).
- When the totals disagree, the trial balance shows that an error exists but not where it is.
- It is only a list at one date; it is not a financial statement and does not show profit.

## 2. Which side does each balance go on?

The rule is simple: an account's balance goes on the side on which the account usually has more entries. Assets, expenses and drawings have debit balances; liabilities, capital and income have credit balances. The awkward items are below.

| Balance | Side | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening inventory | Debit | An asset at the start of the year; closing inventory is not in the trial balance |
| Sales returns | Debit | Reduces income, so it is the opposite side to sales |
| Purchases returns | Credit | Reduces an expense, so it is the opposite side to purchases |
| Carriage inwards and carriage outwards | Debit | Both are expenses |
| Discount allowed | Debit | An expense |
| Discount received | Credit | Income |
| Bank overdraft | Credit | Money owed to the bank |
| Provision for depreciation | Credit | Reduces an asset |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Credit | Reduces trade receivables |
| Irrecoverable debts | Debit | An expense |
| Drawings | Debit | Reduces capital |

## 4. Worked example 2: amending a trial balance that contains errors

A trainee drafted Marigold's trial balance and got totals of **debit 174,070** and **credit 170,650**. Four balances were on the wrong side: discount received (530) and the bank overdraft (2,180) were shown as debits; sales returns (620) and carriage inwards (380) were shown as credits.

Moving a balance from one column to the other changes **both** totals by the amount:

| Correction | Debit total | Credit total |
|---|---|---|
| Trainee's totals | 174,070 | 170,650 |
| Move discount received to credit | -530 | +530 |
| Move bank overdraft to credit | -2,180 | +2,180 |
| Move sales returns to debit | +620 | -620 |
| Move carriage inwards to debit | +380 | -380 |
| **Corrected totals** | **172,360** | **172,360** |

The difference in the trainee's totals was 174,070 - 170,650 = 3,420. A balance on the wrong side causes a difference of **twice** its value: 2 x (530 + 2,180) - 2 x (620 + 380) = 5,420 - 2,000 = 3,420. Halving a difference is a useful first test when looking for one misplaced balance.

When an exam asks you to *amend* a trial balance, redraft it in full with the items on the correct sides and show the new totals, unless the question asks only for corrected totals.

## 5. Errors that do not affect the trial balance

Each of these errors leaves debits equal to credits, so the totals still agree.

| Error | Meaning | Marigold example | Why the totals still agree |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commission | Correct amount posted to the correct side of the wrong account of the same type | A 340 credit sale to Petal Hotel debited to Petals Café's account | Both are customer accounts on the debit side |
| Compensating | Two or more errors of equal value cancel out | Wages overcast by 100 and commission received overcast by 100 | Both totals are too high by 100 |
| Complete reversal | Correct accounts and amount, but debit and credit swapped | Rent of 900 paid: bank debited, rent credited | A debit and a credit of 900 are still made |
| Omission | Transaction not recorded at all | An invoice for 260 from a supplier is lost and never entered | Nothing is recorded on either side |
| Original entry | Wrong amount used for both entries, usually copied wrongly from the document | A sale of 1,580 recorded as 1,850 in the sales journal and the ledger | Equal debit and credit of the wrong amount |
| Principle | Entry made in the wrong type of account | Van repairs of 750 debited to the motor vehicle account | A debit and a credit of 750 are still made |

**Telling commission from principle** is the commonest difficulty. Ask whether the two accounts are the *same type*. Two customers: commission. An expense and a non-current asset: principle. Errors of principle often also break the capital or revenue rules of [sub-topic 4.1](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-capital-and-revenue-expenditure-and-receipts/), and they affect profit.

**Errors that do affect the totals** include a debit with no matching credit, entries of different amounts on each side, both entries on the same side, an addition error in one account, and a balance left out of the trial balance. These are corrected through a suspense account in sub-topic 3.2.

## Common mistakes

- Putting closing inventory in the trial balance alongside opening inventory.
- Listing the bank overdraft, provision for depreciation or discount received as debits.
- Saying a trial balance "proves the accounts are correct". It proves only that totals agree.
- Describing an error of commission as "the wrong account" without saying the account is of the same type.
- Calling an incorrect amount copied from an invoice an error of commission rather than original entry.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** a trial balance with a heading that includes the business name and date, two columns, and totals underlined. Use the exact account names given.
- **Identify** the type of error from a description: check whether entries are missing, swapped, the wrong amount or the wrong account, then whether the wrong account is the same type.
- **Explain** a limitation by naming an error type and saying why the totals still agree.
- For multiple-choice error questions, ask "is the debit still equal to the credit?" for each option before naming the error.
