## Equations to recall and apply

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | distance = speed × time | m |
| Speed | speed = distance ÷ time | m/s |
| Acceleration | a = (v − u) ÷ t | m/s² |
| Uniform acceleration | v² − u² = 2as | m²/s² |
| Average speed | total distance ÷ total time | m/s |

Symbols: u initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration, s distance, t time.

## Measuring (P2.1a, P2.1b)

- Short, fast motion: light gates or video, not a hand stopwatch.
- Walking or running: tape measure or trundle wheel, plus a stopwatch over the longest practical distance.
- Repeat and take a mean. Start and stop timing on the same signal.
- Ramp investigation (PAG P3): vary the angle, measure the speed, control the trolley, release point and distance.

## Units and rates (P2.1c)

| Convert | Do this |
|---|---|
| km to m | × 1000 |
| min to s | × 60 |
| h to s | × 3600 |
| km/h to m/s | ÷ 3.6 |
| m/s to km/h | × 3.6 |
| mph to m/s | roughly × 0.447 (1 mile is about 1609 m) |

**Method in steps**
1. Write the quantity with its old unit.
2. Multiply by the ratio that equals 1 (for example 1000 m per 1 km).
3. Check the size: a larger unit gives a smaller number.

**Worked reminder.** A runner covers 4.5 km in 25 min. Distance 4500 m, time 1500 s, speed 4500 ÷ 1500 = 3.0 m/s, which is 10.8 km/h.

**Rates by proportion.** 540 m in 3 min 20 s: time 200 s, rate 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7 m/s.

## Scalar and vector (P2.1d)

| Scalar | Vector |
|---|---|
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |

- Vectors need a direction; in one dimension, give opposite directions opposite signs.
- Displacement is the straight-line change in position from start to finish.
- Reversing direction makes velocity change sign.
- Constant speed round a circle means changing velocity.

## Graph rules (P2.1e, P2.1f)

| Graph | Gradient | Area under line | Flat line means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance-time | speed | no meaning needed | stationary |
| Velocity-time | acceleration | distance travelled | constant velocity |

**Distance-time:** straight slope is constant speed, steeper is faster, curve steepening is speeding up, curve flattening is slowing down. On a curve, use the gradient of a tangent at that point.

**Velocity-time:** upward straight slope is uniform acceleration, downward straight slope is uniform deceleration, a curve is changing acceleration. Below the time axis means motion the other way.

**Method for area:** split into rectangles and triangles, work out each, add. Triangle ½ × base × height. Trapezium ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × width.

**Worked reminder.** Velocity rises from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s, then stays at 12 m/s for 6 s. Distance = ½ × 4 × 12 + 6 × 12 = 24 + 72 = 96 m.

## Average speed for non-uniform motion (P2.1g)

- Use total distance ÷ total time for the whole journey.
- Include stops in the time.
- Never average the separate speeds.

## Uniform acceleration (P2.1h)

**Method in steps for v² − u² = 2as**
1. List u, v, a, s and mark the unknown.
2. Write the equation, then substitute with units.
3. Rearrange, for example v² = u² + 2as.
4. Square root last.
5. Check the sign: braking gives negative a.

**Worked reminders**
- Start at rest, a = 3.2 m/s², s = 40 m: v² = 2 × 3.2 × 40 = 256, so v = 16 m/s.
- Brakes to rest with a = −5.0 m/s² over 22.5 m: u² = 2 × 5.0 × 22.5 = 225, so u = 15 m/s.
- Velocity 6 m/s to 14 m/s in 5 s: a = 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 m/s², and distance = ½ × (6 + 14) × 5 = 50 m (area of a trapezium).

## Describing a journey from a graph

Use a fixed pattern for each section of the graph: say what the line does, say what that means for the motion, then give a number.

**Worked reminder (distance-time).** Points (0 s, 0 m), (10 s, 25 m), (25 s, 25 m), (35 s, 5 m).
- 0 to 10 s: straight upward slope, constant speed 25 ÷ 10 = 2.5 m/s.
- 10 to 25 s: horizontal, stationary for 15 s.
- 25 to 35 s: straight downward slope, returning towards the start at 20 ÷ 10 = 2.0 m/s, so velocity is −2.0 m/s.
- Distance travelled = 25 + 20 = 45 m. Displacement = 5 m from the start. Average speed = 45 ÷ 35 = 1.3 m/s (2 s.f.).

**Worked reminder (velocity-time).** A lift speeds up from 0 to 2.4 m/s in 3 s, moves at 2.4 m/s for 5 s, then slows to rest in 2 s.
- Accelerations: 2.4 ÷ 3 = 0.80 m/s², then 0, then −2.4 ÷ 2 = −1.2 m/s².
- Distance = ½ × 3 × 2.4 + 5 × 2.4 + ½ × 2 × 2.4 = 3.6 + 12 + 2.4 = 18 m.

## Choosing the right equation

| You are given | You want | Use |
|---|---|---|
| distance and time | speed | speed = distance ÷ time |
| speed and time | distance | distance = speed × time |
| two velocities and a time | acceleration | a = (v − u) ÷ t |
| u, v and a, no time | distance | v² − u² = 2as |
| u, a and distance, no time | v | v² = u² + 2as |
| a graph with a sloping line | speed or acceleration | gradient |
| a velocity-time graph | distance | area |

Rearranging: t = s ÷ v, v = s ÷ t, v = u + at, and a = (v² − u²) ÷ 2s. Practise cover-up triangles only if you can also rearrange without them.

## Quick checks on your answer

- A walking speed of about 1 to 2 m/s is sensible; 20 m/s on foot is not.
- Distances from a velocity-time graph must have units of metres when the axes are m/s and s.
- If the object slows down, the final speed must be smaller than the initial speed.
- A positive and a negative answer from a square root: pick the one in the direction of travel.

## Must-know distinctions

- Speed has size only (scalar); velocity also has direction (vector).
- Gradient of distance-time is speed; gradient of velocity-time is acceleration.
- Area of velocity-time is distance; area of distance-time has no use here.
- Constant speed does not mean constant velocity if direction changes.
- Deceleration means a negative acceleration when forward is positive.

## Quick self-test

1. Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
2. Convert 0.25 m/s to km/h.
3. How long does it take to travel 2.4 km at 8 m/s?
4. State the difference between distance and displacement.
5. What does the gradient of a velocity-time graph give?
6. A car at rest accelerates at 3.2 m/s² over 40 m. State its final speed.
7. A velocity-time graph is a horizontal line at 5 m/s. Describe the motion.
8. A cyclist goes 300 m in 40 s, rests for 20 s, then goes 200 m in 40 s. Find the average speed.
9. A trolley's velocity rises uniformly from 6 m/s to 14 m/s in 5 s. Find the acceleration.
10. What does a downward-sloping straight line on a displacement-time graph show?

### Answers

1. 72 ÷ 3.6 = **20 m/s**.
2. 0.25 × 3.6 = **0.90 km/h**.
3. Time = 2400 ÷ 8 = **300 s** (5 min).
4. Distance is the total path length (scalar). Displacement is the straight-line change in position with direction (vector).
5. **Acceleration.**
6. v² = 2 × 3.2 × 40 = 256, so **16 m/s**.
7. **Constant velocity of 5 m/s**, zero acceleration.
8. Total distance 500 m, total time 100 s, so **5.0 m/s**.
9. (14 − 6) ÷ 5 = **1.6 m/s²**.
10. **Moving back towards the start** at constant speed, which is negative velocity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a speed in km/h or a time in minutes when the unit asked for is m/s.
- Reading a gradient from a tiny triangle, which makes it inaccurate.
- Quoting a size only where velocity or displacement is asked for.
- Treating a flat line on a velocity-time graph as stationary.
- Counting squares for area without checking what one square represents.
- Dividing the distance by only the moving time and missing the rest period.
- Forgetting that a negative acceleration is a slowing down, and quoting a deceleration with the wrong sign.
- Taking the square root too early in a v² − u² = 2as question.
