## Questions

**1.** Define an epigenetic change, and explain why increased methylation of DNA is not classed as a gene mutation. **[3]**

**2.** Explain how increased methylation of a promoter and decreased acetylation of histones can each reduce the expression of a gene. **[4]**

**3.** Gene R is a tumour suppressor gene. Promoter methylation and mRNA from gene R were measured in four tissue samples.

| Sample | Promoter sites methylated (%) | Gene R mRNA (arbitrary units) |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy tissue | 5 | 80 |
| Benign growth | 9 | 76 |
| Early tumour | 68 | 9 |
| Advanced tumour | 74 | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times higher methylation is in the advanced tumour than in healthy tissue, and the percentage fall in mRNA between healthy tissue and advanced tumour. **[3]**
**(b)** State two conclusions the data support. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe an experiment to test whether methylation causes the fall in mRNA. **[2]**

**4.** A drug stops acetyl groups being removed from histones. Cells were treated with it. Histone acetylation near gene W rose from 1.0 to 2.8 relative units and mRNA from gene W rose from 120 to 330 copies per cell.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mRNA copies. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the rise in mRNA. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason this drug might cause side effects. **[1]**

**5.** Explain how a mutation in a proto-oncogene and a mutation in a tumour suppressor gene can each lead to the formation of a tumour. **[4]**

**6.** In one cancer, the promoter of tumour suppressor gene P is hypermethylated and the promoter of oncogene Q is hypomethylated. Explain how each change contributes to uncontrolled cell division. **[4]**

**7.** In a long-term study, 126 of 840 people with a harmful variant of a tumour suppressor gene developed a cancer. In a group of 5,600 people without the variant, 224 developed the cancer.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group that developed the cancer, and the risk ratio. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest two ways a person found to carry the variant could reduce their risk. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one limitation of using this result to predict an individual's future health. **[1]**

**8.** Mice with a tumour driven by an overactive oncogene were given a drug that blocks its protein. The tumour was 0.20 cm³ at the start. After 21 days the mean volume was 1.45 cm³ in the control group and 0.62 cm³ in the treated group.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in tumour volume in each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much smaller the treated tumours were than the controls after 21 days, as a percentage of the control volume. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how blocking the oncogene's protein slows tumour growth. **[2]**

**9.** A section of mRNA reads 5′ GGCUAAGCUUACCGAUUCGA 3′. A researcher designs an siRNA to bind to it. Cells without siRNA contained 3,200 copies of this mRNA. Cells with it contained 480 copies.

**(a)** Write the sequence of the part of the siRNA that pairs with this mRNA, in the same left-to-right order as the mRNA. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the siRNA reduces the amount of the protein made. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage decrease in mRNA copies and explain why the gene is not fully switched off. **[2]**

**10.** A miRNA of 22 nucleotides pairs with 17 bases of a target mRNA and has 5 mismatches.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of its bases that pair. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare how a miRNA and an siRNA bind to mRNA and the effect each can have. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why one miRNA can affect the activity of several different genes. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Distinguish between totipotent and pluripotent cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Name one example of a multipotent cell and one example of a unipotent cell, stating what each forms. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why specialised cells translate only part of their DNA. **[2]**

**12.** A stem cell treatment was tested for a degenerative disorder. 150 patients received the treatment and 150 received a standard treatment. After a year, 96 treated patients and 54 controls had improved.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group that improved and the difference. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate stem cells as a treatment for human disorders, using this trial and other knowledge. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** An epigenetic change alters gene function [1] without altering the DNA base sequence, and it is preserved when cells divide [1]. Methylation adds methyl groups but no base is lost, added or replaced, so the sequence is unchanged [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare statement that methylation "switches genes off" does not explain why it is not a mutation. Name what stays the same: the base sequence.

**2.** Methylation: a methylated promoter is harder for transcription factors and RNA polymerase to bind [1], so less mRNA is made [1]. Acetylation: with fewer acetyl groups, histones attract DNA more strongly [1], so DNA is tightly packed and the gene cannot be transcribed easily [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention histone charge and attraction to DNA; answers that stop at "DNA is tighter" are weaker.

**3. (a)** 74 / 5 = **14.8 times** [1]. Fall in mRNA = (80 − 5) / 80 × 100 [1] = **93.75%, about 93.8%** [1].
**(b)** Higher methylation of the promoter is associated with lower mRNA from gene R [1]. The tumours have higher methylation and lower expression than healthy tissue [1].
**(c)** Treat tumour cells with a drug that removes methylation (and keep untreated cells as a control) [1]. Measure mRNA from gene R in both; a rise in mRNA after treatment supports methylation as the cause [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum. In (b), keep to what the data show: association, not proof of cause, which is why (c) is asked.

**4. (a)** (330 − 120) / 120 × 100 = **175%** [1].
**(b)** More acetyl groups remain on the histones [1]. The attraction between histones and DNA is weaker, so DNA is looser [1]. RNA polymerase and transcription factors reach the promoter more easily, so more transcription occurs [1].
**(c)** The drug acts on histones throughout the body, so it may increase expression of genes that should stay off in healthy cells [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write a chain of cause and effect, not a list of separate facts.

**5.** Proto-oncogene: a mutation makes the protein overactive or too abundant [1], so the cell is signalled to divide when it should not, giving uncontrolled division [1]. Tumour suppressor gene: a mutation makes its protein non-functional [1], so division is not slowed, damaged DNA is not repaired and damaged cells do not undergo apoptosis [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State each gene's normal role before describing the effect of its mutation, and keep the two cases separate.

**6.** Gene P: hypermethylation lowers transcription [1], so less tumour suppressor protein is made and the brakes on division are lost [1]. Gene Q: hypomethylation increases transcription of the oncogene [1], so too much of the protein stimulating division is made [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The changes act in opposite directions on the genes but give the same result for the cell. Say "increased" or "decreased" transcription for each gene.

**7. (a)** 126 / 840 × 100 = **15.0%** [1]. 224 / 5,600 × 100 = **4.0%** [1]. Risk ratio = 15.0 / 4.0 = **3.75** [1].
**(b)** Two from: more frequent screening so cancer is found early; preventive surgery; lifestyle changes to reduce other risk factors [1] [1].
**(c)** Most carriers (85%) did not develop the cancer, so a result shows risk and not certainty [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each suggestion to the higher risk given in the data.

**8. (a)** Control: (1.45 − 0.20) / 0.20 × 100 = **625%** [1]. Treated: (0.62 − 0.20) / 0.20 × 100 = **210%** [1].
**(b)** (1.45 − 0.62) / 1.45 × 100 = **57.2%** [1].
**(c)** The oncogene protein signals cells to divide [1]. Blocking it lowers the division rate, so the tumour grows more slowly [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the question says a percentage of the control, so the control volume is the divisor.

**9. (a)** Complement of each base (A with U, G with C): **CCGAUUCGAAUGGCUAAGCU** [2].
**(b)** The siRNA joins a protein complex [1]. It binds by complementary base pairing to the mRNA [1]. The mRNA is broken down, so less is translated and less protein is made [1].
**(c)** (3,200 − 480) / 3,200 × 100 = **85%** [1]. Some mRNA remains, because not every molecule is destroyed, so the gene is knocked down, not deleted [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Write U not T and check the length matches the target. State that siRNA acts on mRNA, not DNA.

**10. (a)** 17 / 22 × 100 = **77.3%** (77%) [1].
**(b)** siRNA is fully complementary to its target mRNA, while miRNA is often only partly complementary [1]. siRNA typically leads to breakdown of the mRNA [1]. miRNA can block translation or lead to breakdown, so gene activity can change [1].
**(c)** Pairing need not be perfect [1], so one miRNA can bind to several mRNAs with similar sequences [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Put both molecules in each comparison point, using "whereas" or "but".

**11. (a)** Totipotent cells can form every cell type, including a whole organism [1]. Pluripotent cells form many cell types, but not a whole organism [1].
**(b)** Multipotent: a bone marrow cell that forms blood cells [1]. Unipotent: a cell that forms only one type, such as a muscle satellite cell, which forms muscle fibres [1].
**(c)** Different genes are expressed in different cell types [1]. Only the genes needed for that cell's function are transcribed and translated, and the rest of the DNA is not translated [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe potency by what each cell can form, and make examples match the named type.

**12. (a)** Treated: 96 / 150 × 100 = **64.0%**; control: 54 / 150 × 100 = **36.0%** [1]. Difference = **28.0 percentage points** [1].
**(b)** For: a large difference between groups, with equal group sizes [1]; stem cells can replace damaged tissue and may avoid donor shortages [1]; the patient's own cells reduce rejection [1]. Against or limits: 36% of controls also improved, so other factors may contribute [1]; long-term safety, such as tumour risk, is not shown by a one-year trial [1]. Ethical objections apply if embryos are used, and cost may limit access [1]. Judgement: a real benefit is suggested, but longer follow-up is needed. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate means weighing evidence both ways, then reaching a judgement. Quote figures from the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling methylation a mutation, or saying it changes the base sequence.
- Mixing up which gene is switched off (tumour suppressor) and which is over-activated (oncogene).
- Writing T instead of U when pairing bases with mRNA.
- Saying siRNA acts on DNA, or removes the gene.
- Using the wrong baseline in a percentage change.
- Treating correlation in methylation data as proof of cause.
- Giving only advantages of stem cell therapy, or no judgement.
