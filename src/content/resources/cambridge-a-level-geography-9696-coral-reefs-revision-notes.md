Condensed recall for **8.3 Coral reefs** (Cambridge 9696, Paper 3, Coastal environments option). Full explanations are in the [8.3 coral reefs study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-coral-reefs/); test yourself with the [8.3 coral reefs practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-coral-reefs-practice/). ## What the syllabus lists

- Characteristics, distribution and formation of fringing reefs, barrier reefs and atolls.
- Conditions required for coral growth.
- Threats (global warming, sea-level rise, pollution, physical damage) and possible management strategies.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Polyp | Small coral animal that secretes a calcium carbonate skeleton |
| Hermatypic coral | Reef-building coral containing zooxanthellae |
| Zooxanthellae | Symbiotic algae in polyp tissue that photosynthesise and feed the coral |
| Coralline algae | Algae that deposit calcium carbonate and cement the reef crest |
| Fore-reef slope | Seaward face; most diverse; often has spurs and grooves |
| Reef crest | Highest part, where waves break; robust corals and algae only |
| Reef flat | Shallow platform behind the crest; rubble, sand, scattered corals |
| Fringing reef | Grows from the shore with little or no lagoon |
| Barrier reef | Separated from the shore by a wide, deep lagoon |
| Atoll | Ring of reef around a lagoon with no central island |
| Bleaching | Loss of zooxanthellae under heat stress, leaving white skeleton visible |
| Ocean acidification | Fall in sea water pH as it absorbs carbon dioxide; slows skeleton growth |
| Keep-up / catch-up / give-up | Possible reef responses to sea-level rise |
| No-take zone | Area where all fishing and collecting is banned |

## Conditions and why

- **Warm:** about 23-29°C, winter minimum above about 18°C. Narrow tolerance; too hot causes bleaching.
- **Light:** mostly top 30 m. Zooxanthellae photosynthesise.
- **Clear:** sediment blocks light and smothers polyps.
- **Saline:** normal sea water; reefs absent at large river mouths.
- **Moderate waves:** oxygen and food in, sediment out.
- **Brief exposure only** at low tide.
- **Firm base** for larvae to settle.

## Distribution recall

- Mostly 30°N-30°S. Highest diversity in the Coral Triangle (Indonesia, Philippines, Malaysia, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Timor-Leste).
- Caribbean: fewer species; Mesoamerican Barrier Reef (Belize, Mexico).
- Exceptions: Bermuda (about 32°N) warmed by the Gulf Stream.
- Absent: tropical west coasts with cold currents and upwelling; large river mouths such as the Amazon.

## Formation theories

| Theory | Idea | Best evidence / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Subsidence (Darwin) | Fringing reef round a sinking island becomes barrier reef then atoll | Thick reef limestone over volcanic rock on drilled atolls / does not explain similar lagoon depths |
| Glacial control (Daly) | Waves planed platforms at low glacial sea level; reefs grew on rims as sea rose | Explains uniform lagoon depths / does not explain thick reef rock |
| Antecedent platform | Reefs grow on any shallow surface; lagoons partly from solution at low sea level | Fits continental-shelf barrier reefs / varies by site |

## Threats and management at a glance

| Threat | Mechanism | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Global warming | Bleaching; acidification; stronger storms | Emissions cuts (global); resilience through local protection |
| Sea-level rise | Reefs drown if growth slows | Reduce local stress so growth keeps up |
| Pollution | Nutrients feed algae and starfish outbreaks; sediment blocks light | Catchment management, sewage treatment |
| Physical damage | Anchors, trampling, blast fishing, coral mining, reclamation | Mooring buoys, diver education, fishing laws, no-take zones |

Examples to name: Apo Island community sanctuary (Philippines); Great Barrier Reef zoning and the Reef 2050 Plan (Australia).

## Exam traps

- Conditions listed without linking to zooxanthellae.
- Only Darwin's theory given, with no mention of sea level change.
- Saying bleached coral is dead.
- Every threat answered with "global warming".
- Management strategies not matched to the threat they reduce.
- No scale distinction between local and global action in evaluation.

## Self-test

1. What is the relationship between polyps and zooxanthellae?
2. Why do reefs grow mainly in the top 30 m?
3. What role do coralline algae play?
4. Describe the reef crest.
5. Why does Bermuda have reefs at about 32°N?
6. Why are reefs absent off tropical west coasts?
7. How does a barrier reef differ from a fringing reef?
8. State the glacial control theory in one sentence.
9. What is coral bleaching?
10. How does nutrient pollution damage reefs?
11. Distinguish keep-up and give-up reefs.
12. Why can local management not fully protect a reef?

### Answers

1. Symbiosis: the algae photosynthesise and give the polyp sugars; the polyp gives shelter, carbon dioxide and nutrients.
2. Zooxanthellae need light for photosynthesis, and light falls off quickly with depth.
3. They deposit calcium carbonate, cementing fragments and forming a wave-resistant crust on the crest.
4. The highest part of the reef, where waves break; only robust corals and coralline algae survive; may be exposed at low tide.
5. The warm Gulf Stream raises sea temperature above what the latitude alone would give.
6. Cold currents and upwelling keep sea temperature too low.
7. A barrier reef is separated from the shore by a wide, deep lagoon; a fringing reef grows from the shore.
8. Waves cut platforms when sea level fell in glacial periods, and corals grew up on their rims as sea level rose.
9. The expulsion of zooxanthellae under heat stress, leaving the coral white and starved; it can recover if stress ends soon.
10. Nutrients favour algae that outcompete and smother coral, and are linked to crown-of-thorns starfish outbreaks.
11. Keep-up reefs grow as fast as sea level rises; give-up reefs grow too slowly and drown.
12. Marine heatwaves and acidification come from global emissions, which local rules cannot control.
