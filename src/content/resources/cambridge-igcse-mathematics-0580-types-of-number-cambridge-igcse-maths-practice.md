These questions cover subtopic 1.1 Types of number in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C1.1) and Extended (E1.1) content is identical, so every question suits both tiers. All of them are non-calculator, because this content is regularly tested on Papers 1 and 2.

Learn the methods first in the [types of number study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-types-of-number-cambridge-igcse-maths/) and the [types of number revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-types-of-number-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 40 709 in words. **[1]**

**(b)** Write three and a quarter million in figures. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) From this list: 1, 15, 17, 25, 33, 36, 41, 64

**(a)** write down the prime numbers **[1]**

**(b)** write down the square numbers **[1]**

**(c)** write down a number that is both a square number and a cube number. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Write 420 as a product of its prime factors, using index notation. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Find the highest common factor (HCF) of 84 and 120. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Write down the reciprocal of 2⅔. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Write down the irrational numbers in this list: √36, √40, 0.45, 5π, ∛8 **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 2016 as a product of its prime factors. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence find the smallest positive integer k such that 2016k is a square number. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 150 and 105 as products of their prime factors. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the HCF of 150 and 105. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of 150 and 105. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A florist has 84 roses and 126 tulips. She uses all of them to make identical bunches, each with the same number of roses and the same number of tulips.

**(a)** Find the greatest number of bunches she can make. **[2]**

**(b)** How many roses and how many tulips are in each bunch? **[1]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Two lighthouse lamps flash together at exactly 9 pm. One flashes every 20 seconds and the other every 28 seconds. Find the time at which they next flash together. **[3]**

**11.** (non-calculator) N = 2ᵃ × 3ᵇ, where a and b are positive integers. The HCF of N and 360 is 72, and the LCM of N and 360 is 1080.

Find a and b, and hence find N. **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down an irrational number between 5 and 6. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the reciprocal of 0.625, giving your answer as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why 0 has no reciprocal. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Forty thousand, seven hundred and nine [1]

**(b)** 3 250 000 [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (a) the zeros in the thousands and tens places are placeholders; "forty thousand and seventy-nine" would be 40 079, a different number.

**2. (a)** 17, 41 [1]

**(b)** 1, 25, 36, 64 [1]

**(c)** 1 or 64 [1]

*Examiner insight:* 33 = 3 × 11 and 15 = 3 × 5, so neither is prime; check each odd number for small factors.

**3.** 420 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 × 7 seen [1]; **2² × 3 × 5 × 7** [1]

**4.** 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5 [1]; HCF = 2² × 3 = **12** [1]

**5.** 2⅔ = 8/3, so the reciprocal is **3/8** [1]

**6.** **√40** [1], **5π** [1]. (√36 = 6, 0.45 = 9/20 and ∛8 = 2 are rational.) A list with an extra rational number loses one mark.

**7. (a)** Correct division or factor tree [1]; **2⁵ × 3² × 7** [1]

**(b)** 2⁵ needs one more 2 and 7 needs one more 7 [1]; **k = 14** [1]. (2016 × 14 = 28 224 = 168².)

*Examiner insight:* "hence" means use part (a); listing multiples of 2016 until a square appears is very slow and rarely finished.

**8. (a)** 150 = 2 × 3 × 5² [1]; 105 = 3 × 5 × 7 [1]

**(b)** **15** [1]

**(c)** 2 × 3 × 5² × 7 = **1050** [1]

**9. (a)** HCF of 84 and 126 needed [1]; **42 bunches** [1]

**(b)** 84 ÷ 42 = **2 roses** and 126 ÷ 42 = **3 tulips** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "greatest number of identical groups using everything" signals the HCF; using the LCM gives a number larger than the flowers available.

**10.** LCM needed: 20 = 2² × 5, 28 = 2² × 7 [1]; LCM = 2² × 5 × 7 = 140 seconds [1]; 140 s = 2 minutes 20 seconds, so they flash together at **2 minutes 20 seconds past 9 pm** [1]

**11.** 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5 and 72 = 2³ × 3², 1080 = 2³ × 3³ × 5 [1]. From the LCM, the highest power of 2 is 2³, so a = 3, and the highest power of 3 is 3³, so b = 3 [1]. N = 2³ × 3³ = **216** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check your answer: HCF(216, 360) = 72 and LCM(216, 360) = 1080. The HCF uses the lowest powers, so it confirms a ≥ 3 and b ≥ 2.

**12. (a)** Any correct answer, such as **√30** (√25 = 5 and √36 = 6) or π + 2 [1]

**(b)** 0.625 = 5/8 [1]; reciprocal **8/5** [1]

**(c)** The reciprocal of 0 would be 1 ÷ 0, and division by zero is undefined; no number multiplied by 0 gives 1 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including 1 or an odd composite such as 15 or 33 in a list of primes.
- Leaving a prime factorisation as a list, or not finishing it (2³ × 75).
- Using the HCF for a "next time together" problem, or the LCM for a "greatest number of groups" problem.
- Giving the reciprocal of a mixed number as 1 over the whole-number part.
- Giving a decimal approximation, such as 5.48, when asked for an irrational number: a rounded decimal is rational.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these from Cambridge IGCSE past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for revision of 0580 subtopic 1.1. Use them alongside official past papers.

**Is 5.48 an acceptable irrational number between 5 and 6?**
No. 5.48 is a terminating decimal, so it is rational. Give an exact irrational value such as √30.
