This set covers the opening content points of key development 3.1.1.4: "the Second World War and
its effects on the Indian subcontinent, including the 'Day of Deliverance'" and "the Quit India
movement – aims and impact; the 1942 Cripps mission". The tariffs follow the pattern of the
[Pakistan movement practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-pakistan-movement-practice/),
whose Lahore Resolution and Direct Action Day questions are not repeated here. Learn the content
from the [Cripps Mission and Quit India study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-cripps-mission-and-quit-india/).

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** Who was Viceroy when India was declared at war in 1939? **[1]**

A: Lord Irwin &nbsp; B: Lord Linlithgow &nbsp; C: Lord Wavell &nbsp; D: Lord Mountbatten

**2.** In which month and year did the Cripps Mission visit India? **[1]**

A: August 1940 &nbsp; B: March 1942 &nbsp; C: August 1942 &nbsp; D: March 1946

**3.** In which city did the All-India Congress Committee pass the Quit India resolution? **[1]**

A: Delhi &nbsp; B: Calcutta &nbsp; C: Lahore &nbsp; D: Bombay

**4.** Which slogan did the Muslim League use in reply to Quit India? **[1]**

A: "Do or die" &nbsp; B: "Divide and Quit" &nbsp; C: "Pakistan Zindabad" &nbsp; D: "Swaraj"

**5.** State **two** reasons why the Congress provincial ministries resigned in 1939. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** proposals made by the Cripps Mission. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** aims of the Quit India movement. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why Britain sent the Cripps Mission to India in 1942.
**[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why the Day of Deliverance (1939) was important for the
Muslim League. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** reasons why Congress rejected the Cripps proposals. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** ways in which the Quit India movement strengthened the position of the Muslim
League. **[6]**

**12.** "The Second World War did more than anything else to strengthen the Muslim League between
1939 and 1945."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer using the statement above and your
contextual knowledge. **[9]**

**13.** Which was more important for the Pakistan movement?

- the Cripps Mission (1942)
- the Quit India movement (1942)

Explain your answer with reference to both bullet points. **[9]**

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 mark for each point identified and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and
9-mark questions are marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other
accurate points.

**1.** **B: Lord Linlithgow** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B: March 1942** [1]. March 1946 is the Cabinet Mission, which Cripps also joined. **[1]**

**3.** **D: Bombay** [1], on 8 August 1942. **[1]**

**4.** **B: "Divide and Quit"** [1]. "Do or die" was Gandhi's call to Congress supporters. **[1]**

**5.** Two reasons, 1 mark each: the Viceroy declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders
[1]; Britain refused to promise independence or give immediate power in return for support [1].
Also credit: Congress wanted Britain to state its war aims for India. **[2]**

**6.** Two proposals, 1 mark each: dominion status after the war [1]; provinces could stay out of the
new Indian Union [1]. Also credit: a constitution-making body after the war; Britain to keep control
of defence during the war; Indian leaders to join the Viceroy's Council. **[2]**

**7.** Two aims, 1 mark each: an immediate end to British rule [1]; an Indian government to decide
India's part in the war [1]. Also credit: mass non-violent struggle if Britain refused. **[2]**

**8.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Japan had captured Singapore and was advancing through Burma [1], so Britain needed Indian
  co-operation to defend India's eastern border [1];
- the United States pressed Britain to settle with Indian leaders [1], because it wanted India's full
  support for the Allied war effort [1].

Also credit: Labour members of the War Cabinet favoured a new offer. **[4]**

**9.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- it showed the depth of Muslim grievance at Congress rule from 1937 to 1939 [1], uniting Muslims
  behind the League's account of discrimination [1];
- it showed the League acting as a national organisation [1], since meetings and prayers were held
  across India on one day, strengthening its claim to speak for all Muslims [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *No real power now:* Congress wanted an Indian national government with control over the war
  effort, including defence; Cripps offered self-government only after the war, which Gandhi is
  often quoted as calling a "post-dated cheque".
- *The provincial opt-out:* allowing provinces to stay out of the Union threatened the unity of
  India and seemed to open the way to Pakistan.
- *Princely states:* their members of the constitution-making body would be nominated rather than
  elected, which Congress saw as undemocratic.
- *Distrust after 1939:* Congress believed Britain had made decisions about India without
  consultation and doubted post-war promises. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Congress removed from politics:* its leaders were in prison for most of the rest of the war, so
  the League faced little organised opposition and could recruit and campaign freely.
- *Provincial power:* the League formed ministries in several Muslim-majority provinces, gaining
  experience of government and patronage.
- *British trust:* by refusing to join a rising in wartime, the League appeared co-operative, and
  British officials gave more weight to its demands.
- *Clear contrast:* "Divide and Quit" presented Pakistan as the condition for any British departure.
  **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the war led to the resignation of Congress ministries, the August Offer's minority pledge,
  Britain's need for League co-operation, and Quit India, which removed Congress leaders; all of these
  strengthened the League.
- *Disagree:* the League's own actions mattered: Jinnah's leadership, the Day of Deliverance, the
  Lahore Resolution's clear demand, and organisation and recruitment; Muslim grievances from Congress
  rule (1937-39) came before the war.
- *Judgement:* for example, the war created the opportunity, but the League's leadership and its
  clear demand for Pakistan turned that opportunity into lasting strength. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Answers must address both bullet points. Indicative
content:
- *Cripps:* the first British plan to let provinces stay out of an Indian Union, which suggested the
  principle of Pakistan could be accepted; its failure showed Congress and League demands could not
  be reconciled easily.
- *Quit India:* removed Congress leaders, let the League grow and form provincial governments, and
  improved the League's standing with Britain.
- *Judgement:* for example, Quit India had the greater practical effect on the League's strength,
  while Cripps mattered more as a principle that later negotiations built on. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the Cripps Mission with the Cabinet Mission.
- Describing Quit India without explaining its effect on the League.
- Explaining only one party's rejection of Cripps when asked about both.
- In 9-mark answers, giving no clear comparison in the conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need the names of the Viceroys?**
Yes: Linlithgow (1936-43) declared war and made the August Offer; Wavell followed in 1943.

**How should the Cripps and Cabinet Missions be kept apart?**
Cripps came in 1942, during the war, and offered dominion status after it; the Cabinet Mission came
in 1946 and proposed a three-tier federation.
