These questions cover key question 2.1 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470, the Paper 2 prescribed topic for June 2026.

## Section A

**1.** Describe Clemenceau's aims at the Paris Peace Conference. **[4]**

**2.** State **three** of Wilson's Fourteen Points. **[3]**

**3.** Describe the military terms of the Treaty of Versailles. **[4]**

**4.** Identify **three** territories Germany lost in Europe under the Treaty. **[3]**

**5.** What was the "stab in the back" myth? **[2]**

**6.** Describe the occupation of the Ruhr in 1923. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did Lloyd George want a less harsh treaty than Clemenceau? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Germans resent the Treaty of Versailles? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "The Treaty of Versailles was the main cause of Germany's problems between 1919 and 1923." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "Clemenceau got more of what he wanted from the Treaty of Versailles than Wilson did." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter by a French schoolteacher in Reims to her sister, July 1919. "They say in Berlin
that the peace is cruel. Let them come and see our cathedral, our schools, our fields full of shell holes. Germany
keeps its factories and its mines; we keep our graves. If this peace is harsh, it is not harsh enough."

How useful is Source A as evidence about opinions of the Treaty of Versailles? Use the source and your knowledge to
explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary German poster of 1923, written for this page. A thin German
worker, chained to a huge iron weight labelled "Versailles", tries to lift a sack of banknotes while a French soldier
with a bayonet stands on the sack.

What is the message of the poster described in Source B? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your
answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: security for France against future attack (1); to weaken Germany permanently (1); a very
small German army (1); the Rhineland separated from Germany or demilitarised (1); large reparations to rebuild
devastated France (1); the return of Alsace-Lorraine (1); punishment, as French public opinion demanded (1); control
of the Saar's coal (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: open diplomacy without secret treaties (1); freedom of the seas (1); free trade (1);
disarmament (1); self-determination for national groups (1); an independent Poland with access to the sea (1); a
League of Nations (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: army limited to 100,000 men (1); no conscription (1); no tanks (1); no military aircraft
(1); navy limited to six battleships (1); no submarines (1); Rhineland demilitarised (1); Allied troops to occupy the
Rhineland for 15 years (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: Alsace-Lorraine (1); Eupen and Malmédy (1); North Schleswig (1); West Prussia and Posen
(1); Danzig (1); Memel (1); the Saar, placed under the League (1); part of Upper Silesia after a plebiscite (1).
**[3]**

**5.** 1 mark each: the claim that the German army had not been defeated in 1918 (1); but had been betrayed by
politicians, socialists or others at home who agreed the armistice and the Treaty (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: Germany fell behind with reparations deliveries in late 1922 (1); French and Belgian
troops entered the Ruhr in January 1923 (1); they aimed to take coal and goods directly (1); the German government
ordered passive resistance (1); workers went on strike (1); some Germans were killed or expelled by the occupiers
(1); the government printed money to pay strikers (1); this worsened hyperinflation (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **trade**, because Germany had been one of Britain's biggest trading partners and a ruined Germany could not
buy British goods; **fear of communism**, because a desperate Germany might follow Russia into revolution; **fear of
revenge**, because, as his Fontainebleau Memorandum argued, an unjust peace would lead to another war; **Britain had
suffered less damage at home** than France and had already gained its main aims, the German navy and colonies;
**balance of power**, because Britain did not want France to dominate Europe. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **Diktat**, because Germany was not allowed to negotiate;
**war guilt**, because Article 231 seemed to blame Germany alone; **reparations** seemed a crushing burden;
**territorial losses** placed Germans under Polish rule and split East Prussia from Germany; **self-determination**
was denied to Germans, as Anschluss was banned; **military limits** left Germany defenceless; **national pride**,
because many believed the army had not been defeated. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content. **Agree:**
reparations led to the default and the Ruhr occupation of 1923; army cuts triggered the Kapp Putsch; the Treaty's
signatories were attacked and murdered (Erzberger, Rathenau); the Diktat fuelled extremist parties. **Disagree:**
Germany's war debts and money-printing caused inflation; the revolution of 1918-19 and the Spartacist rising happened
before the Treaty was signed; the Weimar constitution's proportional representation produced weak coalitions;
political violence came from both left and right for many reasons. **Judgement:** for example, the Treaty made
existing problems worse and gave extremists a cause, but did not create them all. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** France gained Alsace-Lorraine, reparations, a tiny
German army, a demilitarised and occupied Rhineland and the Saar's coal; Wilson's self-determination was ignored for
Germans, and the USA did not join his League. **Disagree:** Clemenceau did not get an independent Rhineland, and the
guarantees he accepted collapsed when the US Senate rejected the Treaty; Germany stayed united and kept its industry,
so France was not secure; Wilson got the League written into the Treaty and new states based on self-determination,
such as Poland and Czechoslovakia. **Judgement:** for example, Clemenceau gained more terms, but neither achieved his
real goal: lasting security or a lasting peace. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): evaluates usefulness using content and provenance, supported by knowledge. Level 2
(3-4): evaluates using content or provenance only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or asserts usefulness.
Indicative content: **content**: it shows a French view that the Treaty was "not harsh enough" because of war damage
("cathedral", "fields full of shell holes") and because Germany kept its "factories and its mines"; it shows awareness
that Germans called it cruel. **Provenance**: written in July 1919, just after signing, by a civilian from Reims, a
city badly damaged in the war, in a private letter, so likely a sincere view. **Limits**: one person's opinion; it
does not show British, American or German views. Knowledge: many French people and Foch thought the Treaty too
lenient; Germans called it a Diktat. **Judgement:** useful for French public feeling, less for opinion overall.
**[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the message in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4):
explains the message using the source only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative content: the message is
that **the Treaty and France were crushing ordinary Germans**: the chain and weight show Versailles as a burden that
keeps Germany down; the **sack of banknotes** stands for reparations and worthless money; the **French soldier**
represents the Ruhr occupation, preventing recovery. Knowledge: French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr in January
1923; hyperinflation destroyed the mark; nationalists blamed Versailles. **[6]**
