These questions cover subtopic 8.5 Noble gases of Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028: the Group VIII noble gases as unreactive, monatomic gases, explained by electronic configuration, with links to bonding. The subtopic is Core; Question 11 uses the Extended trend skill from 8.1. Data given are real values.

## Section A

**1.** State the group of the Periodic Table that contains the noble gases. **[1]**

**2.** Give the electronic configuration of neon. **[1]**

**3.** Which formula correctly represents argon gas?

A: Ar

B: Ar₂

C: Ar⁺

D: Ar₂⁺ **[1]**

**4.** State the meaning of the term *monatomic*. **[1]**

**5.** Name the noble gas that has only two electrons in its outer shell. **[1]**

**6.** State **three** properties of the noble gases given in the syllabus description of Group VIII. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain, in terms of electronic configuration, why argon is unreactive. **[2]**

**8.** Chlorine gas consists of Cl₂ molecules, but argon gas consists of single Ar atoms.

**(a)** Give the electronic configurations of a chlorine atom and an argon atom. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why chlorine atoms bond together in pairs but argon atoms do not. **[3]**

**9.** An atom of element Q has two occupied electron shells, with two electrons in the first shell and eight in the second. (Q is not the element's symbol.)

**(a)** Identify element Q. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict whether Q is reactive or unreactive. Explain. **[2]**

**10.** Sodium forms Na⁺ ions and fluorine forms F⁻ ions.

**(a)** Give the electronic configuration of each ion. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the noble gas with the same electronic configuration as these ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a neon atom does not form an ion. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* The table shows the boiling points of some noble gases.

| Noble gas | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|
| Helium | −269 |
| Neon | −246 |
| Argon | −186 |
| Krypton | −153 |
| Xenon | −108 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in boiling point down the group. **[1]**

**(b)** Radon is below xenon. Predict whether radon is a gas, liquid or solid at 25 °C. Explain your answer using the data. **[2]**

**12.** A student writes: "Noble gases are unreactive because they are gases." Explain why this is incorrect. **[2]**

**13.** Describe how particles of argon gas should be drawn in a particle diagram, and explain why. **[2]**

**14.** Hydrogen (proton number 1) and helium (proton number 2) are both gases at room temperature.

**(a)** Give the electronic configuration of a helium atom. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why hydrogen exists as H₂ molecules but helium exists as single atoms. **[3]**

**(c)** Clean, dry air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. State what makes up the remainder. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Group VIII [1].

**2.** 2,8 [1].

**3.** **A** [1].

**4.** Exists as single (separate) atoms [1].

**5.** Helium [1].

**6.** Unreactive [1]; monatomic [1]; gases (at room temperature) [1].

**7.** Argon has a full outer shell (2,8,8) [1]; so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons [1].

**8. (a)** Chlorine: 2,8,7 [1]; argon: 2,8,8 [1].

**(b)** Each chlorine atom has seven outer electrons and needs one more for a full outer shell [1]; two chlorine atoms share a pair of electrons (covalent bond), so each gets a full outer shell [1]; argon already has a full outer shell, so its atoms do not need to share electrons / bond [1].

**9. (a)** Neon [1].

**(b)** Unreactive [1]; its outer shell is full (eight electrons), so it does not need to lose, gain or share electrons [1].

**10. (a)** Na⁺: 2,8 [1]; F⁻: 2,8 [1].

**(b)** Neon [1].

**(c)** Its outer shell is already full, so it has no tendency to lose or gain electrons [1].

**11. (a)** Boiling point increases down the group [1].

**(b)** Gas [1]; its boiling point is predicted to be above −108 °C but still far below 25 °C, continuing the trend [1].

**12.** Being a gas does not explain reactivity: other gases, such as chlorine or oxygen, are very reactive [1]; noble gases are unreactive because they have a full outer shell of electrons [1].

**13.** As separate single circles (not joined in pairs), spread far apart and randomly arranged [1]; because argon is monatomic and a gas, its atoms are not bonded and are far apart [1].

**14. (a)** 2 [1].

**(b)** A hydrogen atom has one electron and needs two for a full first shell [1]; two hydrogen atoms share a pair of electrons, so each has a full shell like helium [1]; a helium atom already has a full outer shell of two electrons, so it does not bond and stays as a single atom [1].

**(c)** Noble gases [1]; carbon dioxide [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying every noble gas has eight outer electrons; helium has two.
- Writing "they are stable" without any reference to a full outer shell or electrons.
- Writing Ar₂, or calling the particles molecules.
- Explaining unreactivity by the fact that they are gases.
- Giving the explanation without the consequence ("so they do not lose, gain or share electrons").
- Predicting a value that goes against the trend in a data question.
- Naming argon as the answer whenever a configuration of 2,8,8 appears: if the particle has a charge, it is an ion such as Cl⁻ or K⁺, not an argon atom.
- Drawing noble gas particles joined in pairs in a particle diagram.
- Forgetting carbon dioxide when stating what makes up the remainder of clean, dry air.
