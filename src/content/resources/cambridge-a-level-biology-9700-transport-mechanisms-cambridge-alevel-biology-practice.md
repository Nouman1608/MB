These questions cover sub-topic **7.2 Transport mechanisms** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AS Level, Paper 1 and Paper 2 style). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [transport mechanisms study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-transport-mechanisms-cambridge-alevel-biology/) and the [transport mechanisms revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-transport-mechanisms-cambridge-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which substance forms the Casparian strip?

A: cellulose

B: lignin

C: suberin

D: sucrose **[1]**

**2.** Which process in the transport of sucrose into a companion cell uses ATP directly?

A: diffusion of H⁺ through cotransporter proteins

B: movement of sucrose through plasmodesmata

C: pumping of H⁺ out of the companion cell

D: mass flow of sap in the sieve tube **[1]**

**3.** Define transpiration. **[2]**

**4.** State the type of bond responsible for both cohesion and adhesion in the xylem, and name the substance in the vessel wall involved in adhesion. **[2]**

**5.** A potato tuber stores starch in summer and supplies sucrose to new shoots in spring. Explain why the tuber is described as both a sink and a source. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how water moves from the soil into the xylem of a root by the apoplast and symplast pathways, including the role of the endodermis. **[6]**

**7.** Explain how water moves up the xylem from root to leaf. **[5]**

**8.** A dendrometer measured the diameter of a branch every four hours on a sunny day.

| Time | 02:00 | 06:00 | 10:00 | 14:00 | 18:00 | 22:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diameter / mm | 48.62 | 48.62 | 48.55 | 48.49 | 48.54 | 48.60 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in diameter between 06:00 and 14:00. Give your answer to 2 significant figures.

**(b)** Explain the change in diameter between 06:00 and 14:00.

**(c)** Suggest why the diameter at 02:00 and 06:00 is the same. **[6]**

**9.** Marram grass grows on sand dunes. A transverse section of its leaf shows a rolled leaf, stomata only on the inner surface in deep grooves, many hairs on the inner surface and a thick cuticle on the outer surface.

**(a)** Explain how **three** of these features reduce water loss by transpiration.

**(b)** Suggest why the outer surface has no stomata. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how sucrose is loaded into a phloem sieve tube at a source, with reference to proton pumps and cotransporter proteins. **[5]**

**11.** Explain how sap moves through a sieve tube from a leaf to a developing fruit. **[5]**

**12.** A scientist measured the rate of water uptake by a leafy shoot in still air and in moving air, at the same temperature.

| Condition | Rate of water uptake / mm³ min⁻¹ |
|---|---|
| Still air | 12.0 |
| Moving air | 19.8 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in moving air.

**(b)** Explain the difference in rate.

**(c)** Explain why water uptake by a shoot is not exactly equal to the rate of transpiration. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Evaporation of water from the internal surfaces of leaves (mesophyll cell walls) [1]; followed by diffusion of water vapour (through stomata) to the atmosphere [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Hydrogen bonds [1]; cellulose [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** In summer it removes sucrose from the phloem and stores it as starch, so it is a sink [1]; in spring it converts starch to sucrose and releases it into the phloem, so it is a source [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Any six from: water enters root hair cells by osmosis, down a water potential gradient; apoplast: water moves through cellulose cell walls and intercellular spaces; without crossing membranes, drawn along by cohesion; symplast: water moves through cytoplasm from cell to cell via plasmodesmata; at the endodermis, the Casparian strip of suberin blocks the apoplast; suberin is waterproof; so water must enter the cytoplasm of endodermal cells (cross a membrane); water enters xylem through pits in the lignified walls [6]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* the syllabus names lignin, cellulose, endodermis, Casparian strip and suberin; answers that use all five usually score highly.

**7.** Any five from: water evaporates from mesophyll cell walls (transpiration); water moves out of leaf xylem to replace it; this lowers pressure at the top of the xylem, creating tension; hydrogen bonds between water molecules give cohesion; so the column is pulled up as a continuous unit (transpiration pull); adhesion of water to cellulose in the walls supports the column; lignin prevents vessels collapsing [5]. Total: 5 marks.

**8. (a)** 48.62 − 48.49 = 0.13 mm [1]; 0.13 ÷ 48.62 × 100 = **0.27 %** [1].

**(b)** Light and temperature increase, so stomata open and transpiration increases [1]; greater tension in the xylem [1]; pulls the vessel walls inwards, so the branch narrows [1].

**(c)** At night stomata are closed or transpiration is very low, so there is little tension in the xylem [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**9. (a)** Any three, each with its explanation: rolled leaf traps humid air, reducing the water vapour gradient; stomata in grooves have still, humid air above them, so less diffusion; hairs trap a layer of still, moist air, increasing the diffusion distance; thick cuticle reduces evaporation through the outer epidermis [3].

**(b)** The outer surface is exposed to dry, moving air, so stomata there would lose water rapidly [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**10.** Proton pumps in the companion cell membrane use ATP [1]; to actively transport H⁺ out of the cell [1]; creating a high H⁺ concentration outside [1]; H⁺ diffuse back in through cotransporter proteins, carrying sucrose with them [1]; sucrose passes into the sieve tube element through plasmodesmata [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**11.** Sucrose loaded at the source lowers the water potential in the sieve tube [1]; water enters by osmosis (from xylem) [1]; raising hydrostatic pressure at the source [1]; at the fruit (sink), sucrose is removed and used or stored, so water leaves and pressure is lower [1]; sap moves by mass flow down the hydrostatic pressure gradient [1]. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "down a concentration gradient" earns nothing for the last point; the gradient is one of hydrostatic pressure.

**12. (a)** (19.8 − 12.0) ÷ 12.0 × 100 [1] = **65 %** [1].

**(b)** Moving air removes the humid air near the stomata [1]; so the water vapour gradient is steeper and water vapour diffuses out faster, increasing tension and uptake [1].

**(c)** Some water taken up is used by cells, for example in photosynthesis or to keep cells turgid [1]. Total: 5 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "water evaporates out of the stomata".
- Omitting hydrogen bonds from cohesion and adhesion.
- Calling the Casparian strip lignin.
- Saying sucrose is pumped rather than cotransported.
- Explaining mass flow with concentration gradients only.
