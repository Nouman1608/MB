These notes condense **1.2.1 Practical skills** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026). The statement lists ten skills, (a) to (j), developed through practical work for the Practical Endorsement and also useful for the written papers. They sit under four headings: independent thinking; use and application of scientific methods and practices; research and referencing; and instruments and equipment.

Worked examples, including a risk assessment and a spreadsheet layout, are in the [1.2.1 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-1/). Exam-style questions are in the [1.2.1 practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-1-practice/). For how the endorsement is run, use the [Practical Endorsement revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Investigative approach | Working out and justifying your own method to answer a question |
| Hazard | Something with the potential to cause harm |
| Risk | The chance of harm from the way the work is done |
| Control measure | A specific action that reduces a risk |
| Risk assessment | A record linking each hazard to its risk and control measure |
| Contemporaneous record | A record made at the time of the activity, not afterwards |
| CPAC | Common Practical Assessment Criteria: the five competencies for the Practical Endorsement |
| Data logger | A device that records readings from a probe at set intervals |
| Citation | A reference that lets a reader find the exact source used |
| Peer review | Checking of research by other experts before publication |

## The ten skills in one table

| Skill | One-line meaning | CPAC |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Investigative approaches | Plan and justify your own method; solve problems in a practical context | 2 |
| (b) Safe, correct use of equipment and materials | Identify hazards and minimise risks | 3 |
| (c) Follow written instructions | Carry out a method accurately, in order | 1 |
| (d) Make and record observations and measurements | Accurate, precise, sufficient, with units | 4 |
| (e) Keep appropriate records | A dated, contemporaneous record of each activity | 4 |
| (f) Present information and data scientifically | Conventions for tables, graphs, units and names | 5 |
| (g) Use software and tools | Spreadsheets, graphing, data loggers, reporting | 5 |
| (h) Online and offline research | Websites, textbooks, printed scientific sources | 5 |
| (i) Cite sources correctly | Full references linked to where they were used | 5 |
| (j) Use a wide range of instruments and techniques | The apparatus and techniques in 1.2.2 | 2 |

The specification expects (b), (c), (d), (e), (f) and (j) to be developed across all activities.

## Safety: the risk assessment checklist

1. List each substance and each step.
2. For each, name the **hazard**: corrosive, irritant, flammable, toxic (the categories named in 1.2.2(k)), or a physical hazard such as pressure or heat.
3. State the **risk**: how harm could happen in this method.
4. Give a **specific control**: what you will do, and why it reduces this risk.

**Typical controls.**

| Hazard | Control |
|---|---|
| Corrosive liquid | Eye protection; gloves; small volumes |
| Irritant or toxic vapour | Fume cupboard; good ventilation |
| Flammable liquid | No naked flames; heat with a water bath or electric heater |
| Pressure in a separating funnel | Invert and open the tap often, pointing away from people |
| Hot apparatus | Allow to cool; use tongs |

## Records and presentation

**A lab record contains:** date; aim; method or reference to it, with changes noted; raw data in tables (quantity / unit headings); observations as they happen; processing; conclusion; evaluation; sources used.

**Presentation conventions:** SI units; quantity / unit headings; consistent decimal places; labelled axes; correct names and formulae; a logical order from aim to evaluation.

## Software and tools

- Spreadsheet formulas process data: for example temperature in K as =A2+273, and 1/time as =1/B2.
- Graphs with trendlines give gradients and intercepts quickly.
- Data loggers with temperature or pH probes record many readings for continuous monitoring.
- Always check one row by hand: a single formula error repeats down a whole column.

## Research and citation

**Judging a source.**

- Who wrote it, and what is their expertise?
- When was it published, and is it current?
- Has it been checked by others, for example by peer review?
- Does it agree with other reliable sources?

**What a citation needs.**

| Source | Elements |
|---|---|
| Book | Author, year, title, edition, publisher, page |
| Journal article | Authors, year, title, journal, volume, pages |
| Website | Author or organisation, year, page title, URL, date accessed |

Cite in the text where the information is used, with the full list at the end. Follow the style your teacher sets; the specification points to the Practical Skills Handbook for guidance.

## Exam traps

- **Hazard and risk confused.** Concentrated acid is the hazard; splashing it into your eyes while pouring is the risk.
- **Generic safety.** "Wear goggles" alone does not link to a hazard.
- **Records written later.** The CPAC evidence is the contemporaneous record.
- **Unrecorded changes** to a method.
- **Citations without the detail** needed to find the source: no URL, no access date, no page.
- **Unchecked spreadsheets.**
- **Sources used without judging reliability.**

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four headings under which the 1.2.1 skills are listed.
2. Explain the difference between a hazard and a risk.
3. Which CPAC competency does correctly citing sources contribute to?
4. Give a specific control measure for heating a flammable organic liquid.
5. What is meant by a "contemporaneous record"?
6. List four things a good lab record contains.
7. Why must the tap of a separating funnel be opened frequently while shaking?
8. Write a spreadsheet formula to convert a temperature in °C in cell A2 into kelvin.
9. List the elements of a citation for a website.
10. Give two questions to ask when judging how reliable a source is.
11. Why should one row of a spreadsheet be checked by hand?
12. Which 1.2.1 skills does the specification expect to be developed across all activities?

### Answers

1. **Independent thinking; use and application of scientific methods and practices; research and referencing; instruments and equipment.**
2. A **hazard** is something that could cause harm; a **risk** is the chance of that harm happening from the way the work is done.
3. **CPAC 5**, researches, references and reports.
4. Heat with a **water bath or electric heater**, with no naked flames nearby, because the vapour could ignite.
5. A record **made at the time** the activity is carried out.
6. Any four: date, aim, method with changes, raw data in tables, observations, processing, conclusion, evaluation, sources.
7. Shaking can build up **pressure** (especially if a gas forms), which could force out the stopper and spray the contents.
8. **=A2+273**.
9. Author or organisation, year, page title, **URL** and **date accessed**.
10. Any two: who wrote it and with what expertise; when it was published; whether it has been peer reviewed or checked; whether other reliable sources agree.
11. One wrong formula is copied down the whole column, so checking a row by hand catches the error.
12. **(b), (c), (d), (e), (f) and (j).**
