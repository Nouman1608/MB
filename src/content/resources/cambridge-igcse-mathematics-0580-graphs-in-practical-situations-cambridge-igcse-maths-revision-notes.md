These notes condense subtopic **2.9 Graphs in practical situations** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). Core (C2.9) and Extended (E2.9) share two statements: use and interpret graphs in practical situations, including travel and conversion graphs, and draw graphs from given data. Lines marked *(Extended)* are E2.9 only: the gradient of a tangent, rate of change in kinematics (distance–time and speed–time graphs, acceleration and deceleration), and distance as the area under a speed–time graph, using linear sections only.

For worked examples, read the [graphs in practical situations study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-graphs-in-practical-situations/); for marked exam-style questions, use the [graphs in practical situations practice questions](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-graphs-in-practical-situations-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rate of change | How fast the vertical quantity changes per unit of the horizontal one; the gradient |
| Intercept | The value on the vertical axis when the horizontal quantity is zero |
| Conversion graph | A straight line changing one unit or currency into another |
| Distance–time (travel) graph | Distance from a fixed point plotted against time |
| Average speed | Total distance ÷ total time, stops included |
| Tangent *(Extended)* | A straight line touching a curve at one point, showing the rate of change there |
| Speed–time graph *(Extended)* | Speed plotted against time |
| Acceleration / deceleration *(Extended)* | Rate of increase / decrease of speed; the gradient of a speed–time graph |

## Condensed recall

### Gradient as a rate

```
gradient = change in vertical quantity / change in horizontal quantity
units    = (vertical unit) per (horizontal unit)
```

Always give the units and say what the rate means in context: "the depth falls by 5 cm every minute".

### Conversion graphs

- Through the origin: the quantities are in direct proportion, so you may scale a reading (if 20 units ↔ 34 units, then 60 ↔ 102).
- Not through the origin (for example Celsius to Fahrenheit, which passes through (0, 32) and (100, 212)): **do not scale**. Read the graph or use the equation. Here the gradient is 180/100 = 1.8 and the equation is F = 1.8C + 32, so 25 °C is 77 °F.

### Travel graphs

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Steeper line | faster |
| Horizontal line | stationary |
| Line sloping down | travelling back towards the start |
| Lines crossing (two travellers) | they are at the same place at the same time |

Convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60 before finding a speed in km/h.

### Drawing from given data

Time across, distance up; label both axes with units; use a ruler for constant-rate sections; a smooth curve only when the rate changes smoothly.

### Tangents *(Extended)*

Draw a long ruled tangent touching the curve at the point; choose two points **on the tangent** far apart; gradient = rise ÷ run. A chord between two points on the curve gives an average rate, not the rate at a point.

### Speed–time graphs *(Extended)*

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | acceleration (negative gradient = deceleration, quoted as a positive value) |
| Horizontal line | constant speed (not stopped) |
| Graph on the time axis | stopped |
| Area under the graph | distance travelled |

Areas: rectangle; triangle ½ × base × height; trapezium ½(a + b)h. Unit change: km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s.

## Comparing the two travel graphs

| | Distance–time | Speed–time *(Extended)* |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical axis | distance | speed |
| Gradient | speed | acceleration |
| Horizontal section | stopped | constant speed |
| Area under graph | no meaning | distance |

## Exam traps

- **Scale misread.** Check how much one small square represents on each axis before reading anything.
- **Minutes as hours.** 15 km in 20 minutes is 15 ÷ (1/3) = 45 km/h, not 0.75.
- **Average speed without the stop.** Include time spent stationary in the total time.
- **Scaling a non-proportional graph.** Only lines through the origin can be scaled.
- **Gradient sign.** A falling depth or a return journey gives a negative gradient; state the rate in words.
- **Tangent points from the curve** *(Extended)*. Use points on your ruled tangent.
- **Triangle instead of trapezium** *(Extended)*. If the speed does not start at zero, the first section is a trapezium.
- **Mixed units** *(Extended)*. Speed in km/h and time in seconds must be converted before finding an area.

## Self-test

1. A conversion graph is a straight line through (0, 32) and (100, 212), with °C across and °F up. Find the gradient, explain the intercept and convert 25 °C.
2. A cyclist covers 15 km in 20 minutes at a constant speed. Find the speed in km/h.
3. What does a horizontal section on a distance–time graph show?
4. A car drives 30 km in 40 minutes, stops for 20 minutes, then returns home in 60 minutes. Find the average speed for the whole trip.
5. A graph of water depth against time is a straight line from (0 min, 80 cm) to (16 min, 0 cm). Find and interpret the gradient.
6. A walker sets off at 09 00 and walks at 5 km/h for 1.5 hours. Write down the two points you would plot for this part of the journey.
7. *(Extended)* A speed–time graph rises in a straight line from 0 to 18 m/s in 6 s. Find the acceleration.
8. *(Extended)* A train slows steadily from 24 m/s to rest in 8 s. Find the deceleration.
9. *(Extended)* Speed rises steadily from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 6 s. Find the distance travelled.
10. *(Extended)* Convert 54 km/h to m/s.
11. *(Extended)* A tangent to a distance–time curve passes through (1, 2) and (6, 22), with time in seconds and distance in metres. Estimate the speed at the point of contact.
12. *(Extended)* A vehicle accelerates from rest to v m/s in 10 s, travels at v m/s for 30 s, then decelerates to rest in 10 s. It travels 800 m. Find v.

### Answers

1. Gradient 180/100 = **1.8** (°F rise per 1 °C); intercept **32 °F at 0 °C**; 1.8 × 25 + 32 = **77 °F**.
2. 20 minutes = 1/3 hour; 15 ÷ 1/3 = **45 km/h**.
3. The object is **stationary**: distance from the start is not changing.
4. Total distance 60 km, total time 2 hours: **30 km/h**.
5. −80/16 = **−5 cm per minute**: the depth falls by 5 cm every minute.
6. **(09 00, 0 km)** and **(10 30, 7.5 km)**.
7. 18 ÷ 6 = **3 m/s²**.
8. 24 ÷ 8 = **3 m/s²**.
9. Trapezium: ½(4 + 10) × 6 = **42 m**.
10. 54 ÷ 3.6 = **15 m/s**.
11. (22 − 2) ÷ (6 − 1) = **4 m/s**.
12. Trapezium area ½(30 + 50)v = 40v = 800, so **v = 20 m/s**.
