The skills column suggests producing ethanol by fermentation, followed by purification by fractional distillation.

## Hydration of alkenes beyond ethene

The specification says *alcohols*, not only ethanol, are made by hydration. Any alkene adds water across its C=C bond:

```
alkene + H₂O  →  alcohol        (acid catalyst, H⁺)
```

The H⁺ ion comes from the acid (phosphoric acid industrially). It is used in step 1 and released in step 3, so it is a catalyst.

### The mechanism in words a marker can credit

1. **Arrow 1** starts at the C=C bond and goes to H⁺. A carbocation forms on the carbon that did not receive the H.
2. **Arrow 2** starts at a lone pair on the oxygen of H₂O and goes to the positive carbon. The product is a protonated alcohol, R–OH₂⁺.
3. **Arrow 3** starts at an O–H bond in R–OH₂⁺ and goes to the oxygen. H⁺ leaves and the alcohol forms.

Marks are lost for arrows that start at a positive charge or at an atom rather than a bond or lone pair, and for not showing the + charge on the carbocation and on the oxonium oxygen.

### Unsymmetrical alkenes: predicting both products

When the two carbons of the C=C carry different groups, two carbocations are possible. The more stable one (tertiary > secondary > primary, because alkyl groups release electrons and stabilise the positive charge) forms faster and gives the **major** product.

**Worked example 1.** 2-methylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, reacts with steam and an acid catalyst.

```
H⁺ adds to C3  →  carbocation on C2, (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃    tertiary
H⁺ adds to C2  →  carbocation on C3, (CH₃)₂CH–C⁺H–CH₃   secondary

major product:  2-methylbutan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₃
minor product:  3-methylbutan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH(OH)CH₃
```

**Worked example 2.** Cyclohexene gives one product only, cyclohexanol, because both carbons of its C=C are equivalent. Symmetrical alkenes such as but-2-ene and cyclohexene never give a major/minor pair.

## Two industrial routes compared

| Factor | Hydration of ethene | Fermentation of glucose |
|---|---|---|
| Raw material | Ethene from crude oil (finite) | Sugars from crops (renewable) |
| Conditions | Steam, phosphoric acid on silica, about 300 °C, high pressure | Yeast, about 35 °C, aqueous, no air |
| Type of process | Continuous; unreacted ethene recycled | Batch; vessel emptied and restarted |
| Rate | Fast | Slow |
| Product | Fairly pure ethanol | Dilute aqueous ethanol needing fractional distillation |
| Atom economy | 100% (one product) | 51.1% (CO₂ is a by-product) |
| Energy | High temperature and pressure need much energy | Low temperature, but distillation needs energy |

The atom economy for fermentation is 2 × 46.0 ÷ 180.0 × 100 = 51.1%. A question that asks you to compare the two routes is best answered with paired points from this table: "hydration is continuous **whereas** fermentation is a batch process".

## Justifying the fermentation conditions

"Justify" means give the condition **and** the reason for it. A list of conditions without reasons scores little.

| Condition | Justification |
|---|---|
| Yeast | Its enzymes catalyse the conversion of glucose to ethanol |
| About 35 °C | Fast enough rate without denaturing the enzymes: below this the rate is slow, above it the enzymes lose their shape and stop working |
| Air excluded (anaerobic) | With oxygen, yeast respires aerobically to CO₂ and water, and ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid |
| Aqueous solution | Glucose and the enzymes must be in solution to react |
| Fractional distillation afterwards | Ethanol (about 78 °C) boils below water, so it can be separated and concentrated for use as a fuel |

The fermentation stops long before all the sugar is used if the ethanol concentration becomes high enough to kill the yeast, which is another reason the product is dilute.

## Worked example 3: a fermentation calculation with a yield

A distillery ferments 500 kg of glucose. The overall yield of ethanol is 80.0%. Calculate the mass of ethanol obtained. (Mᵣ glucose 180.0, ethanol 46.0)

```
n(glucose)          = 500 000 ÷ 180.0 = 2778 mol
n(ethanol), maximum = 2 × 2778 = 5556 mol
mass, maximum       = 5556 × 46.0 = 255 600 g = 255.6 kg
actual mass         = 255.6 × 0.800 = 204 kg
```

Converting the mass to grams first keeps the mole step familiar; convert back to kilograms at the end.

## The biofuel argument, built properly

**Biofuel:** a fuel produced from biological material (biomass) that has been grown recently and can be regrown.

**Carbon neutral, by the equations alone:** photosynthesis absorbs 6 CO₂ per glucose; fermentation releases 2 CO₂; burning the 2 C₂H₅OH releases 4 CO₂. Six in, six out.

**Not valid in practice:** fossil fuels are burned to plant, fertilise, harvest and transport the crop, to distil the ethanol and to deliver the fuel. Each releases CO₂ that was not absorbed by the crop.

### Planning a "discuss" answer on biofuel use

Extended "discuss" answers are credited for balance and specific points. A reliable structure:

1. **Define** the fuel as a biofuel and state the carbon-neutral claim with its equations.
2. **Environmental advantages:** renewable source; less crude oil used; lower net CO₂ than petrol if fossil-fuel inputs are small.
3. **Environmental disadvantages:** fossil-fuel energy in farming, fertiliser making, distillation and transport; forest clearance releasing stored carbon and destroying habitats; fertiliser run-off polluting water.
4. **Ethical issues:** land and water used for fuel instead of food, which can raise food prices for people who can least afford it.
5. **Conclude** with a judgement linked to the evidence: for example, a biofuel is most beneficial when grown on land not needed for food with low fossil-fuel inputs.

## Common mistakes

- Starting the first curly arrow at H⁺ instead of at the C=C bond.
- Naming the minor product as the major one, or giving only one product for an unsymmetrical alkene.
- "High temperature" for fermentation; it is warm, about 35 °C.
- Giving "no air" without saying what oxygen would do.
- Claiming hydration uses a renewable feedstock.
- Saying bioethanol "produces no CO₂". It does; the claim is about *net* emission.
- A one-sided "discuss" answer.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Outline the mechanism**: curly arrows, both intermediates with charges, and H⁺ regenerated.
- **Justify**: condition plus reason, for each condition.
- **Explain the meaning of**: a definition in one sentence, with "recently grown" or "renewable biological material".
- **Discuss**: points for and against, an ethical point, and a reasoned conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is phosphoric acid a catalyst in hydration and not a reactant?**
Its H⁺ ion is used in the first step of the mechanism and released in the last, so it is regenerated and not used up.

**Why is fermentation a batch process?**
The yeast works slowly and stops when the ethanol concentration rises, so the vessel is emptied, the ethanol separated and a new batch started.

**Which route has the better atom economy?**
Hydration of ethene has an atom economy of 100% because ethanol is the only product. Fermentation is 51.1% because carbon dioxide is also made.
