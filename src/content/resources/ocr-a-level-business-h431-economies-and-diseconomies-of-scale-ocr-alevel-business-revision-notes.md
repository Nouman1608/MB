## What the specification lists

## Core recall

### Definitions

- **Economies of scale:** average (unit) cost falls as output rises.
- **Diseconomies of scale:** average cost rises when output grows too large to manage well.
- **Average cost** = total cost ÷ output.
- **Internal:** arises from the size of the business itself; the business has some control.
- **External:** arises from the size of the industry or area; no single firm controls it.

### Internal economies

| Type | Why unit cost falls |
|---|---|
| Purchasing | Bulk orders earn supplier discounts |
| Technical | Bigger, specialised machines used fully |
| Managerial | Specialist managers do jobs more efficiently |
| Financial | Lower interest rates; can raise capital from shares |
| Marketing | Advertising cost spread over more units |
| Risk-bearing | Many products or markets absorb a failure |

### Internal diseconomies

| Type | Why unit cost rises |
|---|---|
| Communication | More layers and sites, slow or distorted messages |
| Coordination and control | Harder to supervise; waste and errors |
| Lack of motivation | Workers feel remote; absence, turnover, poor quality |
| Slow decisions | Many people to consult |

### External economies and diseconomies

| Economies | Diseconomies |
|---|---|
| Pool of skilled labour | Congestion and slow deliveries |
| Specialist suppliers nearby | Rising rents and wages |
| Better infrastructure | Shortage of suitable workers |
| Reputation of the area | Strain on local services; planning limits |

### The shape of average cost

- Falls while economies dominate, reaches a minimum, then rises as diseconomies dominate (a U shape in the long run).
- Fixed costs spread over more units is a large part of the early fall.
- Average cost can rise while total profit still rises, because extra volume earns a margin.

## Worked reminder

Brackwell Biscuits (invented): fixed costs £150,000; variable cost £0.50 a pack, falling to £0.46 above 600,000 packs.

| Output | Total cost | Average cost |
|---|---|---|
| 300,000 | £150,000 + £150,000 = £300,000 | £1.00 |
| 600,000 | £150,000 + £276,000 = £426,000 | £0.71 |

The fall of £0.29 is 29% of £1.00. Adding a second site (extra £90,000 fixed, variable cost back to £0.50) at 1,800,000 packs gives total cost £1,140,000 and average cost £0.633, above the £0.585 at 1,200,000 packs.

## Evaluating costs and benefits: a checklist

1. Name the type and its cause.
2. Quantify: unit cost change, total saving, cost of achieving it.
3. Stakeholders: customers (lower prices?), owners (profit), employees (jobs, morale), suppliers (bigger orders, less bargaining power), community (traffic, noise).
4. Costs come first: machines, premises, finance, training, redundancy.
5. Risks: demand too low to use the capacity, diseconomies, external diseconomies.
6. Judgement and what it depends on (certainty of savings, time to pay back, size of market, objectives).

## Payback style calculation

A merger saves £90,000 a year but needs extra managers costing £75,000. Net benefit £15,000 a year. One-off costs of £120,000 are repaid in 120,000 ÷ 15,000 = 8 years. Long payback means a risky case for a small firm.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economy of scale | A fall in average cost as output rises |
| Diseconomy of scale | A rise in average cost as output rises |
| Average cost | Total cost divided by output |
| Bulk-buying | Buying large quantities for a lower price per unit |
| Cluster | A concentration of related firms in one area |
| Minimum efficient scale | The output at which average cost is lowest |
| Infrastructure | Roads, rail, broadband and utilities that firms share |

## Exam traps

- Do not say that growth always lowers cost; explain when it raises it.
- Do not mix up internal and external. A local skills pool is external.
- Name the cause of each economy, not just the label.
- Remember the cost of getting the economy: finance, training, redundancy.
- A lower average cost helps only if demand is high enough to sell the output.
- In a case question, tie the answer to the sector and size of the business.

## Self-test

1. Define economies of scale.
2. Give one internal and one external economy of scale.
3. Why does a large firm often get lower interest rates?
4. A firm has fixed costs of £90,000 and variable costs of £2 a unit. Calculate average cost at 30,000 units.
5. Its variable cost falls to £1.80 a unit at 60,000 units. Calculate average cost at 60,000 units.
6. Explain why a very large firm may suffer from poor communication.
7. State two external diseconomies of scale.
8. Why can average cost rise while profit still rises?
9. Give two stakeholders and say how each may be affected by economies of scale.
10. Why must costs be counted when evaluating a merger?

### Answers

1. Falls in the average cost of production as output increases.
2. Internal: bulk purchasing discounts. External: a pool of skilled labour in the area. Other correct examples are accepted.
3. Lenders see it as lower risk, with more assets and more stable income.
4. Total cost £90,000 + (£2 × 30,000) = £150,000; ÷ 30,000 = **£5.00**.
5. Total cost £90,000 + (£1.80 × 60,000) = £198,000; ÷ 60,000 = **£3.30**.
6. More layers of management and more sites mean messages pass through more people, so they are slow or distorted and decisions are delayed.
7. Any two: congestion; rising rents; rising wages; shortage of workers; strain on local services.
8. Each extra unit still sells above its marginal cost, so total profit rises, even though extra complexity pushes the average cost per unit up.
9. Customers may gain from lower prices; employees may lose jobs if economies come from fewer staff or gain security if the firm is stronger.
10. Savings often come after one-off costs such as redundancy and new machines, and new management layers add cost, so net benefit may be small or slow.
