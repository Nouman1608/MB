These questions cover subtopic 3.3 Gradient of linear graphs of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core candidates find gradients from a grid only, so questions that describe a line drawn on a grid suit both tiers. Questions marked **(Extended)** ask you to calculate a gradient from coordinates with no grid, which is Extended content (E3.3). Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A straight line drawn on a grid with 1 unit per square on both axes passes through the grid points (0, 1) and (3, 7). Find the gradient of the line. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) For each line, state whether its gradient is positive, negative, zero or not defined.

**(a)** The line y = 4 **[1]**

**(b)** The line x = –2 **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A straight line falls 5 units for every 2 units it moves to the right.

**(a)** Write down its gradient. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the change in y when the line moves 6 units to the right. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) On a printed grid, 1 small square on the x-axis represents 0.2 units and 1 small square on the y-axis represents 5 units. A straight line rises 4 small squares while moving 10 small squares to the right.

Find the gradient of the line. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Four lines have gradients ½, –3, 2 and –¼. List the gradients in order from the steepest line to the least steep. **[1]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Calculate the gradient of the line through (–4, –7) and (2, 5). **[2]**

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Calculate the gradient of the line through (3, –2) and (–5, 4). **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator) A straight line drawn on a grid with 1 unit per square passes through the grid points A(–3, 4) and B(5, 0).

**(a)** Find the gradient of the line. **[2]**

**(b)** The line is extended to the right. Find the value of y on the line when x = 9. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how you can tell from the diagram that the gradient is negative. **[1]**

**9.** (calculator) A straight-line graph shows the cost, C dollars, of buying w kilograms of rice. It passes through the grid points (2, 7.5) and (10, 27.5).

**(a)** Find the gradient of the line. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the gradient represents. **[1]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The line through (2, k) and (8, –1) has gradient –2/3. Find the value of k. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) P is (–2, 7), Q is (1, 1) and R is (4, –5). Show that P, Q and R lie on one straight line. **[3]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A triangle has vertices A(0, 0), B(6, 2) and C(2, 6).

**(a)** Calculate the gradients of AB, BC and CA. **[3]**

**(b)** State which side of the triangle is steepest. **[1]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The line through (a, 1) and (5, a) has gradient 3. Find the value of a. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Rise 7 – 1 = 6 over run 3: gradient = **2** [1]

**2. (a)** **Zero** (horizontal line) [1]

**(b)** **Not defined** (vertical line) [1]

**3. (a)** **–5/2** (or –2.5) [1]

**(b)** 6 × (–2.5) = **–15** (y falls by 15) [1]

**4.** Run = 10 × 0.2 = 2 units and rise = 4 × 5 = 20 units [1]; gradient = 20 ÷ 2 = **10** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4 comes from counting squares and scores nothing; the method mark is for converting both sides into axis units.

**5.** **–3, 2, ½, –¼** [1]

*Examiner insight:* steepness depends on the size of the gradient, not its sign, so –3 is steeper than 2.

**6.** (5 – (–7)) / (2 – (–4)) [1] = 12/6 = **2** [1]

**7.** (4 – (–2)) / (–5 – 3) [1] = 6/(–8) = **–3/4** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the denominator is –8, so the gradient is negative; writing 6/8 loses the accuracy mark.

**8. (a)** Run = 5 – (–3) = 8 and fall = 4 – 0 = 4 [1]; gradient = –4/8 = **–½** [1]

**(b)** From B, 4 more to the right means a fall of 2: y = 0 – 2 = **–2** [1]

**(c)** The line goes **down** from left to right, so y decreases as x increases [1]

**9. (a)** (27.5 – 7.5) / (10 – 2) = 20/8 [1] = **2.5** [1]

**(b)** The cost **per kilogram**: rice costs **$2.50 per kg** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "the slope of the line" does not answer "what it represents"; the mark needs the meaning and the units in context.

**10.** (–1 – k) / (8 – 2) = –2/3 [1]; –1 – k = –4 [1]; **k = 3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check by substituting back: (–1 – 3)/6 = –4/6 = –2/3.

**11.** Gradient PQ = (1 – 7)/(1 – (–2)) = –6/3 = –2 [1]; gradient QR = (–5 – 1)/(4 – 1) = –6/3 = –2 [1]; equal gradients and the common point Q, so P, Q and R are collinear [1]

*Examiner insight:* without the statement about the shared point, equal gradients only prove the lines are parallel, so the final mark is lost.

**12. (a)** AB: 2/6 = **1/3** [1]; BC: (6 – 2)/(2 – 6) = 4/(–4) = **–1** [1]; CA: (6 – 0)/(2 – 0) = **3** [1]

**(b)** **CA** (gradient 3 has the largest size) [1]

**13.** (a – 1)/(5 – a) = 3 [1]; a – 1 = 15 – 3a, so 4a = 16 [1]; **a = 4** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the check (4 – 1)/(5 – 4) = 3 takes seconds and confirms the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting squares without converting to axis units.
- Writing run over rise.
- Losing the negative sign for a falling line.
- (Extended) Subtracting coordinates in different orders on top and bottom.
- Giving the meaning of a gradient without units.
- (Extended) Proving collinear points without mentioning the shared point.
