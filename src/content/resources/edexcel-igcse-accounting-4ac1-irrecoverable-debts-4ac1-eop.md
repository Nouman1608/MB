This guide covers section 5.3, Irrecoverable debts, in Topic 5 (Accounting for end of period adjustments) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. The three outcomes are: (a) explain why it is necessary to provide a provision for irrecoverable debts; (b) distinguish between an irrecoverable debt and a provision for an irrecoverable debt; (c) calculate and record irrecoverable debts and the provision in the books of account. They repeat section 2.6, but Topic 5 is examined in Paper 2, which assesses making "appropriate adjustments to entries at the end of an accounting period". Here, then, the starting point is a trial balance with notes, and the finishing point is the income statement and the statement of financial position.

All businesses and figures are invented. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign. For the first teaching of write-offs and the provision account, see the [Topic 2 irrecoverable debts and other receivables and payables guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-irrecoverable-debts-and-other-receivables-payables/) and the [end-of-period adjustments overview](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-end-of-period-adjustments/). Companion pages: the [irrecoverable debts revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-irrecoverable-debts-4ac1-eop-revision-notes/) and the [irrecoverable debts practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-irrecoverable-debts-4ac1-eop-practice/).

## 1. What a trial balance tells you

Three lines matter:

| Trial balance line | Side | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | Debit | Amounts owed by credit customers, before any year-end write-off in the notes |
| Irrecoverable debts | Debit | Debts already written off during the year |
| Provision for irrecoverable debts | Credit | The provision made at the end of last year (the opening balance) |

The notes then add one or both of: a further debt to write off, and the rate for this year's provision. Work in a fixed order:

1. Write off any further debt: reduce trade receivables and add to irrecoverable debts.
2. Calculate the provision required on the **reduced** trade receivables.
3. Compare it with the trial balance provision. Only the difference goes to the income statement.
4. Show trade receivables less the new provision in current assets.

### Worked example 1: a decrease in the provision

Larchmont Supplies has a year end of 31 March 2028. Its trial balance includes trade receivables 52,800, irrecoverable debts 760 and provision for irrecoverable debts 1,900. Notes:

- A debt of 800 owed by P. Mendes is irrecoverable and must be written off.
- The provision is to be 2.5% of trade receivables.

Workings:

- Trade receivables after the write-off: 52,800 − 800 = **52,000**.
- Irrecoverable debts for the year: 760 + 800 = **1,560**.
- Provision required: 2.5% × 52,000 = **1,300**.
- Change: 1,300 − 1,900 = a **decrease of 600**.

**Irrecoverable debts**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance (trial balance) | 760 | 31 Mar Income statement | 1,560 |
| 31 Mar P. Mendes | 800 | | |
| | 1,560 | | 1,560 |

**Provision for irrecoverable debts**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 31 Mar Income statement (decrease) | 600 | 1 Apr 2027 Balance b/d | 1,900 |
| 31 Mar Balance c/d | 1,300 | | |
| | 1,900 | | 1,900 |

**In the statements.** The income statement shows irrecoverable debts of 1,560 as an expense. The decrease in the provision, 600, is shown as income (added after gross profit) or deducted from expenses, depending on the layout the question uses. Current assets show:

| | |
|---|---:|
| Trade receivables | 52,000 |
| Less provision for irrecoverable debts | (1,300) |
| | 50,700 |

Compared with statements that ignored both notes, profit falls by 800 for the write-off and rises by 600 for the decrease, a net fall of 200. Net trade receivables were 52,800 − 1,900 = 50,900 and are now 50,700: also a fall of 200. That match is a quick check on any provision question.

## 2. Following the provision over several years

Examiners like to test whether you charge the change, not the balance. A schedule keeps it clear.

### Worked example 2: three years at 5%

Ridgeway Paints starts a provision of 5% of trade receivables (after write-offs) in Year 1.

| Year | Trade receivables | Provision required | Opening provision | Change | Effect on profit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 40,000 | 2,000 | 0 | +2,000 | Down 2,000 |
| 2 | 46,000 | 2,300 | 2,000 | +300 | Down 300 |
| 3 | 38,000 | 1,900 | 2,300 | −400 | Up 400 |

Only in Year 1, with no opening balance, is the whole provision charged. In Year 3 the provision falls even though the rate is unchanged, because there are fewer receivables to provide against. Each year's closing provision is the next year's opening figure in the trial balance.

## 3. Adjusting a draft profit

Some Paper 2 parts give a profit already calculated and ask you to correct it.

### Worked example 3

Corran Electrical has a draft profit of 34,500. The following were not dealt with: a debt of 1,200 is irrecoverable; trade receivables before this write-off are 61,200; the provision is to be 4% of trade receivables; the existing provision is 2,100.

- Trade receivables after write-off: 61,200 − 1,200 = 60,000.
- Provision required: 4% × 60,000 = 2,400. Increase: 2,400 − 2,100 = 300.
- Adjusted profit: 34,500 − 1,200 − 300 = **33,000**.
- Current assets: 60,000 − 2,400 = **57,600**.

Check: net receivables were 61,200 − 2,100 = 59,100, so they fall by 1,500, the same as profit (1,200 + 300).

## 4. Written parts: explain and distinguish

**Explain why a provision is necessary (5.3 a).** Build each point into a chain, using the business in the question.

Model answer: *Some of Corran Electrical's credit customers are likely not to pay, but it cannot yet say which ones. Under prudence, trade receivables should not be shown at more than the business expects to collect, so the provision reduces them to a realistic figure. Under accruals, the sales that caused these debts were made this year, so the expected loss is charged against this year's profit rather than a later one.*

**Distinguish between an irrecoverable debt and a provision (5.3 b).** Contrast them on the same features: certainty, which customers, and the ledger.

Model answer: *An irrecoverable debt is a known loss on a named customer, such as P. Mendes, whose account is closed by the write-off. A provision is an estimate of future losses on receivables in general; no customer is named and customers' accounts are not changed.*

## 5. Errors and their effects

| Error | Effect on profit | Effect on current assets |
|---|---|---|
| Whole new provision charged instead of the increase | Understated by the opening provision | None, if the balance carried down is correct |
| Provision calculated before a write-off | Understated by the rate × the write-off | Understated by the same amount |
| Write-off omitted | Overstated by the debt less the rate × the debt | Overstated by the same amount |
| Decrease in provision treated as an expense | Understated by twice the decrease | None |

At Corran Electrical, charging the full 2,400 instead of 300 understates profit by 2,100. Calculating 4% on 61,200 gives 2,448, which is 48 too high (4% × 1,200).

## Common mistakes

- Reading the trial balance provision as this year's figure.
- Applying the rate to trade receivables before the year-end write-off.
- Leaving the 760 already written off out of the year's irrecoverable debts total.
- Showing the provision under current liabilities instead of deducting it from trade receivables.
- Writing off a debt by debiting the provision account.

## Exam technique

"Calculate" needs each step on its own line: write-off, receivables, provision, change. "Record" means ledger accounts with dates and the name of the other account, balanced and carried down. In a full statement, label the change "increase in provision" or "decrease in provision" so its direction is clear. "Explain" needs reasons tied to the business named, not a definition alone.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the provision in a trial balance the new provision?**
No. It is last year's closing provision. Calculate the new one from the notes and charge or credit only the difference.

**Where is a decrease in the provision shown?**
In the income statement as income, or as a reduction of expenses. It increases profit.
