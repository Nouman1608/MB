All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Harbourside Hotel.

> **Case study: Harbourside Hotel**
>
> Harbourside Hotel employs 150 staff. Last year 27 of them left, mostly
> receptionists and waiters, who said they were unsuited to the jobs or
> that the work was not what the advert described. Each time someone
> leaves, the hotel spends an average of $3,200 on recruiting, selecting
> and training a replacement. The manager wants to cut the number of
> leavers to 10% of staff by improving the way the hotel recruits.
> The hotel also needs a new head chef. The deputy chef, who has worked
> there for six years, could be promoted, which would cost $600 in
> notice and interview time. Alternatively the hotel could advertise
> externally for $1,800 and spend 40 hours of manager time on
> interviews, valued at $25 an hour.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following lists the duties and responsibilities of a job? **[1]**
- A. A CV
- B. A job description
- C. A reference
- D. An induction programme

**2.** Which **one** of the following is an example of internal recruitment? **[1]**
- A. Advertising a post in a newspaper
- B. Using a recruitment agency
- C. Promoting a current employee
- D. Interviewing people from another business

**3.** Define the term "person specification". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** methods a business can use to select between shortlisted candidates. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business may need to recruit new staff. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the percentage of staff who left Harbourside Hotel last year. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the cost of replacing the 27 leavers. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the number of leavers if the manager's target of 10% is reached, and the saving in replacement costs. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the extra cost of recruiting the head chef externally compared with promoting the deputy chef. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why Harbourside Hotel's staff may have left. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** benefits to Harbourside Hotel of recruiting and selecting its staff more effectively. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Harbourside Hotel should appoint its new head chef by internal or external recruitment. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A document that lists the skills, qualifications and experience [1] wanted in the person who will do the job [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: interview [1]; aptitude or skills test [1]; presentation [1]; work trial [1]; group exercise [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Staff may have left and need replacing [1], so the work can continue and customers are served [1]. Accept: growth needing more staff, new skills required or seasonal demand, with a reason [1] each. **[2]**

**6.** 27 / 150 [1] x 100 = **18%** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 27 x $3,200 [1] = **$86,400** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Target leavers = 10% x 150 = 15 [1]; cost = 15 x $3,200 = $48,000 [1]; saving = $86,400 - $48,000 = **$38,400** a year [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Management time = 40 x $25 = $1,000 [1]; external cost = $1,800 + $1,000 = $2,800 [1]; extra cost = $2,800 - $600 = **$2,200** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Unsuited to the job:** some staff may have been chosen without proper interviews or tests [1], so their skills or attitude did not match the post and they left [1].
**Job not as advertised:** if the advert did not describe the work accurately, staff would find the work different from what they expected [1], so they became dissatisfied and left [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Staff retention:** a better match between staff and jobs should cut the number of leavers [1]; if turnover fell from 18% to 10%, the hotel would save $38,400 a year in replacement costs [1].
**Customer service:** receptionists and waiters with the right skills and attitude deal with guests well [1], so guests are more satisfied, write better reviews and return [1].
**Productivity:** employees who are well suited to their jobs need less supervision and make fewer mistakes [1], which allows the hotel to serve more guests efficiently [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For internal:** the deputy chef is cheaper by $2,200 [1]; the hotel knows the deputy's skills and the deputy knows the hotel [1]; promotion would motivate other staff [1]; the deputy needs little induction [1].
**For external:** a wider choice of candidates, with a better chance of finding the best chef [1]; new ideas for menus may attract more guests [1]; promoting the deputy would leave a vacancy to be filled [1].
**Judgement:** the deputy chef should be promoted if they meet the person specification, because the cost is low and the hotel already has a high turnover, so rewarding loyal staff helps retention [1]. If the hotel wants to improve its menu significantly, or the deputy has little experience, the extra $2,200 for external recruitment would be a small price for the right chef [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the wrong number when calculating turnover. Use the total staff of 150.
- Forgetting to include the manager's time in the cost of external recruitment.
- Describing recruitment and selection in general terms, without using Harbourside's problems.
- Stating that internal recruitment is always better without giving a reason.
- Not giving a final judgement in the evaluation question.
