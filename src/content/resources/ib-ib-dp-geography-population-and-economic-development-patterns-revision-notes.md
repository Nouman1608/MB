Condensed recall for **8.1 Population and economic development patterns** (Unit 1: Changing
population). For the full explanation, read the
[Population Distribution study guide](/resources/ib-dp-geography-population-distribution/).

## Why population is unevenly distributed

| Physical factors | Human factors |
|---|---|
| Climate | Economic opportunity |
| Relief (terrain) | Historical patterns of settlement |
| Soil fertility | Political stability |
| Availability of fresh water | Infrastructure |
| Resource access | |

A strong answer applies **named** factors to a **named** place rather than listing categories.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population density | People per unit area, usually per square kilometre |
| Population distribution | The overall pattern of where people are located |
| Carrying capacity | Maximum population an environment can sustainably support given its resources |
| Natural change | Births minus deaths |
| Net migration | Immigration minus emigration |

The same density can hide very different distributions: two areas with 18 people over nine squares
both average 2 per square, but one may be evenly spread and the other clustered.

## Demographic transition model

The demographic transition model describes how birth and death rates change as a country develops.

- Both rates start **high**, with death rates falling first.
- Births exceed deaths by the widest gap in the middle stages, so growth is **fastest** then.
- Both rates end **low**, and growth slows.

Each stage has a distinct effect on overall growth rate. The model links population change to
economic development, but it is a generalisation, so note where real countries differ.

## Exam traps

- Confusing density with distribution.
- Blending natural change and migration into one figure instead of stating them separately.
- Listing physical factors with no named place.
- Treating carrying capacity as fixed; resources and technology change it.

## Self-test

1. Name three physical and three human factors affecting distribution.
2. Distinguish density from distribution.
3. Define carrying capacity.
4. Why is growth fastest in the middle stages of the demographic transition model?
5. What are the two components of population change?

**Answers:** 1. Physical: climate, relief, soil fertility, fresh water, resource access; human: economic
opportunity, historical settlement, political stability, infrastructure. 2. Density is people per unit
area; distribution is the pattern of where they are. 3. The maximum population an environment can
sustainably support. 4. Death rates have fallen while birth rates are still high, so the gap is
widest. 5. Natural change and net migration.
