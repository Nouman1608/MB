## Money: definition and forms

**Money:** anything generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for settling debts.

| Form | Key point |
|---|---|
| Coins and notes (cash) | Issued by or for the central bank; worth more than their material, so they depend on trust |
| Bank deposits | Spent by card, transfer or cheque; usually the largest part of the money people use |
| Electronic and mobile transfers | Methods of moving deposit money |
| Commodity money (historic) | Items with their own value, such as precious metals; shows why money developed |

**Barter** needs a double coincidence of wants. Money removes this and gives every good a single price.

## Four functions

| Function | One-line meaning | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| Medium of exchange | Accepted in payment | Swap |
| Unit of account / measure of value | Common measure to compare prices | Sum up |
| Store of value | Keeps purchasing power for the future | Save |
| Standard of deferred payment | Unit for debts repaid later | Settle later |

## Characteristics and the function each supports

| Characteristic | Supports |
|---|---|
| Acceptability | Medium of exchange |
| Durability | Store of value |
| Portability | Medium of exchange |
| Divisibility | Medium of exchange and unit of account |
| Uniformity (homogeneity) | Unit of account |
| Limited supply (scarcity) | Store of value and deferred payment |
| Security against forgery | Acceptability, so every function |

**Method for "would X make good money?":**

1. Check each characteristic for the item.
2. Say which functions fail as a result.
3. Judge overall: good for small trades only, or suitable as general money.

## Rising prices weaken money

```
Real value = money value / (1 + inflation rate)
3,000 held for one year with 20% inflation = 3,000 / 1.20 = 2,500
Loss = 500, one sixth of purchasing power
```

Store of value suffers first; very rapid price rises also damage the unit of account and deferred payment, and in extreme cases people abandon the currency.

## Central bank

| Role | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Issues notes and coins | Controls the currency's supply, keeping trust |
| Banker to the government | Holds its accounts and manages its borrowing |
| Banker to commercial banks | Holds their reserves; settles payments between banks |
| Lender of last resort | Stops one bank's cash shortage turning into a run on many banks |
| Monetary policy | Sets a key interest rate; influences money supply and exchange rate |
| Holds foreign currency reserves | Can be used to influence the exchange rate |
| Supervises banks | Limits risky lending, protecting depositors |

## Commercial banks

| Role | Main beneficiaries |
|---|---|
| Accept deposits; pay interest | Consumers and producers |
| Lend: loans, mortgages, overdrafts | Consumers (houses, cars) and producers (machines, stock) |
| Payment services: cards, transfers | All three groups |
| Foreign exchange | Producers that trade abroad; travellers |
| Buy government debt | Government |

**Aims:** profit, while keeping enough cash (liquidity) for withdrawals and lending safely (security).

Worked reminder: deposits 20 million, reserves kept 15%, loans at 7%, savers paid 2%.

```
Loans = 20 x 0.85 = 17 million
Interest received = 17 x 0.07 = 1.19 million
Interest paid = 20 x 0.02 = 0.40 million
Margin = 0.79 million before running costs
```

## Importance in one chain each

- **Consumers:** bank loan → buy a house now and repay over time → higher living standards sooner.
- **Producers:** loan → invest in capital → more output, lower unit cost, more jobs.
- **Government:** central bank manages borrowing and interest rates → government can finance spending and pursue its macroeconomic aims.
- **Whole economy:** banks turn many small savings into large loans → investment and growth.

## Exam traps

- **Function versus characteristic.** "Durability" is a characteristic; "store of value" is a function.
- **Central bank customers.** Its customers are the government and commercial banks, not ordinary households.
- **Lender of last resort** is about protecting the system, not rescuing every badly run firm.
- **Banks' margin** comes from the gap between interest charged and interest paid; do not add the two.
- **Answer for the named group.** A question on producers wants loans, overdrafts and foreign exchange, not mortgages.
- **Deposits are money.** Do not limit money to notes and coins.

## Self-test

1. Define money.
2. State the four functions of money.
3. Explain why durability matters for money.
4. Which function is most damaged when prices rise quickly?
5. A household holds 4,800 in cash and prices rise by 20% in a year. Calculate the real value at the end of the year.
6. Give two reasons why fish would make poor money.
7. State two roles of a central bank.
8. What is meant by lender of last resort?
9. State two ways commercial banks help producers.
10. A bank lends 12 million at 9% and pays 1% on 15 million of deposits. Calculate its interest margin.
11. Why might a commercial bank not lend out all its deposits?
12. Explain one way a central bank is important to the government.

### Answers

1. Anything generally accepted as payment for goods and services and for settling debts.
2. Medium of exchange; unit of account (measure of value); store of value; standard of deferred payment.
3. Money that wears out or rots cannot keep its value over time, so it cannot act as a store of value.
4. Store of value.
5. 4,800 / 1.20 = 4,000.
6. Not durable (it rots); not uniform (sizes differ); also accept not easily divisible or portable.
7. Any two: issues notes and coins; banker to government; banker to commercial banks; lender of last resort; monetary policy; holds reserves; supervises banks.
8. The central bank lends to a commercial bank that cannot meet withdrawals, to protect confidence in the banking system.
9. Any two: loans for investment; overdrafts for cash-flow gaps; foreign exchange for trade; payment services.
10. Received 12 x 0.09 = 1.08 million; paid 15 x 0.01 = 0.15 million; margin 0.93 million.
11. It must keep cash for depositors' withdrawals; lending everything risks being unable to pay and losing confidence.
12. It holds the government's account and manages its borrowing, so the government can pay for spending when tax revenue arrives later.
