These questions cover **3.2.3 Energy resources**, statements a to d, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. All fuel data and site descriptions are invented for practice. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Learn the content first from the [Energy resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-resources/) and the [Energy resources revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-resources-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a renewable energy resource. **[1]**

**2.** Name **three** renewable energy resources. **[3]**

**3.** Give **two** factors that are considered when choosing a fuel for a particular use. **[2]**

**4.** A generator burns 4.0 kg of fuel with an energy content of 45 MJ/kg. Calculate the energy released. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why nuclear fuel is classed as non-renewable. **[1]**

**6.** Name the gas produced by burning fossil fuels that is linked to global warming. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table gives invented data for three fuels.

| Fuel | Energy content (MJ/kg) | State | Safety note |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 48 | Liquid | Vapour catches fire easily |
| Q | 15 | Solid | Low risk |
| R | 55 | Gas | Must be stored under high pressure |

**(a)** Calculate the mass of fuel Q needed to release the same energy as 1.0 kg of fuel R. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest which fuel is best for a small passenger aircraft, giving **two** reasons. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest which fuel is most suitable for a school science laboratory demonstration, and explain why. **[2]**

**8.** Two water heaters each burn 0.80 kg of a fuel with an energy content of 40 MJ/kg. Heater X has an efficiency of 0.85. Heater Y has an efficiency of 0.60.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released by the fuel. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the useful energy from each heater. **[2]**

**(c)** State what happens to the rest of the energy in heater Y. **[1]**

**(d)** Give **one** advantage to a household of choosing heater X. **[1]**

**9.** A small hydroelectric scheme lets 3.0 × 10⁵ kg of water fall through 40 m each hour.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy transferred by the water each hour. **[2]**

**(b)** The scheme is 0.80 efficient. Calculate the electrical energy produced each hour. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **one** advantage and **one** drawback of hydroelectric power. **[2]**

**10.** For coal-fired power stations, describe **one** environmental impact of each of the following stages: extraction, use and disposal. **[3]**

**11.** A remote mountain village has fast-flowing streams, strong sunshine for most of the year, and no connection to the national electricity grid. It is far inland, with no hot rocks near the surface.

**(a)** Explain why wave power and geothermal power are not suitable. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate the use of solar power and small-scale hydroelectric power for the village, and recommend a choice. **[5]**

**12.** A country plans to replace some coal-fired power stations with nuclear power stations.

**(a)** Give **two** advantages of this change. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **two** drawbacks of this change. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A resource that is replenished as it is used, so it will not run out [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any three of: wind, wave, solar, geothermal, hydroelectric, tidal, biofuel [3]. **[3]**

**3.** Any two of: ease of storage, energy content, safety (also accept cost or availability) [2]. **[2]**

**4.** E = 4.0 × 45 [1] = **180 MJ** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Its fuel (uranium) is mined from a limited supply and is not replaced as it is used [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Carbon dioxide [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 1.0 kg of R releases 55 MJ [1]; mass of Q = 55 ÷ 15 = **3.7 kg** (3.67 kg) [1].

**(b)** Fuel P [1]: it has a high energy content, so less mass is carried for the same energy [1]; it is a liquid, so it can be stored in ordinary tanks rather than heavy high-pressure containers [1].

**(c)** Fuel Q [1]: it is low risk (a solid that does not give off flammable vapour), so it is safest in a room full of students, and its low energy content does not matter for a short demonstration [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** 0.80 × 40 = **32 MJ** [1].

**(b)** X: 0.85 × 32 = **27.2 MJ** [1]; Y: 0.60 × 32 = **19.2 MJ** [1].

**(c)** It is transferred to the surroundings by heating (for example, hot exhaust gases and the heater body) [1].

**(d)** It needs less fuel for the same heating, so it is cheaper to run (or releases fewer combustion products) [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Ep = 3.0 × 10⁵ × 9.8 × 40 [1] = **1.176 × 10⁸ J** (about 1.2 × 10⁸ J) [1].

**(b)** 0.80 × 1.176 × 10⁸ [1] = **9.4 × 10⁷ J** (9.408 × 10⁷ J) [1].

**(c)** Advantage, any one [1]: renewable; reliable; can start producing electricity quickly; no carbon dioxide during operation. Drawback, any one [1]: building the dam floods a valley, destroying habitats or land; only suitable in places with the right landscape. **[6]**

**10.** Extraction: mining damages the landscape (or is dangerous for miners) [1]. Use: burning coal releases carbon dioxide (global warming) or sulfur dioxide (acid rain) [1]. Disposal: large amounts of ash must be dumped or stored [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Wave power needs a coastline with waves, and the village is far inland [1]; geothermal power needs hot rocks near the surface, which the area does not have [1].

**(b)** Solar: strong sunshine for most of the year gives a good output [1], but there is no output at night, so storage or a back-up is needed [1]. Hydroelectric: fast-flowing streams can turn small turbines day and night, so the supply is more reliable [1], but building it may disturb the streams and their wildlife [1]. Recommendation with reason, for example: small-scale hydroelectric as the main supply because it works continuously, with solar panels to add to it during the day [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* the recommendation mark needs a reason linked to the village's features; any sensible, justified choice is credited.

**12. (a)** Any two [2]: no carbon dioxide released during generation; no sulfur dioxide; reliable supply that does not depend on the weather; large output from a small amount of fuel.

**(b)** Any two [2]: radioactive waste must be stored safely for a very long time; nuclear fuel is non-renewable; there is a risk of radioactive material being released in an accident; uranium must be mined. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling nuclear power renewable.
- Vague environmental points with no named gas or habitat.
- Evaluations with no conclusion, or a conclusion with no reason.
- Forgetting to multiply energy content by mass.
