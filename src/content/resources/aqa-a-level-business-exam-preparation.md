AQA A-Level Business 7132 is a linear qualification, not tiered, assessed by three compulsory written papers of 2 hours each: Paper 1 (Business 1), Paper 2 (Business 2) and Paper 3 (Business 3), each worth 100 marks and one third of the A-level, 300 marks in total. Each paper draws on all ten content sections. The notes below turn that structure into a plan, alongside our topic pages on [financial management](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-financial-management/), [human resource management](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-human-resource-management/) and [analysing the strategic position of a business](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-analysing-the-strategic-position-of-a-business/).

## Which specification are you sitting?

Two codes appear in this subject. 7132 was first taught in September 2023, with the first A-level assessment in June 2025 and the final A-level assessment in June 2027. A replacement, 7138, has been taught since September 2026, with first A-level exams in June 2028. If your course began before September 2026 you are on 7132; if it began in or after September 2026 you are on 7138. Check with your teacher, because the papers differ.

## Paper 1 (Business 1): four sections, one paper

Paper 1 is 2 hours and 100 marks, one third of the A-level. Section A has 15 multiple-choice questions (15 marks), Section B is short-answer (35 marks), and Sections C and D each ask for one essay from a choice of two (25 marks each). **How to spend the time:** the whole paper works out at 1.2 minutes per mark, so the two essays, at 25 marks each, deserve about 30 minutes apiece, which leaves roughly 60 minutes for Sections A and B together. Choose each essay question in the first minute and write a short plan before starting.

## Paper 2 (Business 2): three data-response questions

Paper 2 is 2 hours, 100 marks and one third of the A-level. It has three compulsory data-response questions, each approximately 33 marks, each with three or four parts. Every question is compulsory, so there is no choice to make. **How to spend the time:** 120 minutes divided by three questions gives about 40 minutes each. Read the stimulus material first, then pick out the figures and facts you will quote, because the short parts reward application to the context rather than general definitions.

## Paper 3 (Business 3): case study

Paper 3 is 2 hours, 100 marks and one third of the A-level (recorded as 33.34% so that the three weightings sum to exactly 100). It has one compulsory case study followed by approximately six questions. **How to spend the time:** allow 10 to 15 minutes to read and annotate the case study, then spread the remaining time across the questions in proportion to their marks. Because every paper draws on all ten content sections, keep revision of the whole course alive until the end.

## Replacement specification 7138 at a glance

For candidates on 7138, the three papers are also 2 hours each, but with 90 marks each (270 marks in total) and a different split of content: Paper 1 covers What is business? and managing marketing and finance, Paper 2 covers managing people and operations (with links to Paper 1 content), and Paper 3 covers business and society, the external environment and business strategy. Papers 1 and 2 each use two case studies followed by five compulsory questions worth 45 marks per case study. The marks-per-minute figure is 120 / 90 = 1.33 minutes per mark.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- 7132, each paper: 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark.
- Paper 1 Section A: 15 marks at 1.2 = 18 minutes for the multiple-choice questions at most; aim for less and bank the time.
- Paper 1 Section B: 35 marks x 1.2 = 42 minutes.
- Paper 1 each essay: 25 x 1.2 = 30 minutes. Total check: 18 + 42 + 30 + 30 = 120.
- Paper 2 each question: about 33 marks x 1.2 = about 40 minutes.
- 7138, each paper: 120 / 90 = 1.33 minutes per mark; a 45-mark case-study block is 60 minutes.

## Command words and question styles

Business papers reward a chain of reasoning, so match your depth to the command word. "Identify" and "State" need a short answer only. "Explain" needs a point and a developed consequence. "Analyse" needs a chain of cause and effect tied to the context. "Evaluate" and "Justify" need a judgement that weighs both sides and reaches a supported conclusion. Always use the business named in the stimulus rather than a generic firm.

## Worked practice scenario: break-even and a judgement

**Question:** Greenfield Loaves bakes and sells loaves at a price of 6.00 pounds each. Variable cost per loaf is 2.40 pounds and fixed costs are 48,000 pounds a year. It forecasts sales of 18,000 loaves. (a) Calculate break-even output. (b) Calculate the margin of safety. (c) Evaluate whether the owner should open a second site.

**Model answer:**

```
(a) Contribution per unit = 6.00 - 2.40 = 3.60
    Break-even output = fixed costs / contribution = 48,000 / 3.60 = 13,333.33
    So break-even is 13,334 loaves (round up: a part loaf cannot be sold).

(b) Margin of safety = forecast sales - break-even output
                     = 18,000 - 13,334 = 4,666 loaves
    As a percentage of forecast: 4,666 / 18,000 x 100 = 25.9% (1 d.p.)
```

(c) A judgement paragraph: the margin of safety of about 26% suggests the existing site is profitable and can absorb a fall in demand of roughly a quarter before making a loss. However, a second site would add fixed costs and raise the break-even point, and the forecast of 18,000 loaves is a forecast, not a certainty. The decision depends on whether demand at the new location is proven, how the site would be financed and whether cash flow can cope in the first year. On balance, the owner should proceed only if market research supports demand there; otherwise improving the first site's margin carries less risk.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are sitting 7132 or 7138 and which paper is on which day.
- Practise one full two-hour paper in timed conditions for each of the three papers.
- Keep a one-page formula sheet for break-even, margins, ratios and investment appraisal.
- Read the stimulus or case study fully before answering any question.
- Underline the command word and the number of marks, then plan the length of the response.
- Quote at least one fact or figure from the stimulus in every application answer.
- Show every step of a calculation, with units.
- End extended answers with a reasoned judgement, not a restatement of the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA A-Level Business 7132?**
There are three compulsory written papers, each 2 hours and 100 marks, each worth one third of the A-level (300 marks in total). The qualification is linear and not tiered.

**Do the three papers cover different content?**
No. Each paper draws on all ten content sections of the specification, so revise the whole course rather than allocating topics to single papers.

**What is the structure of Paper 1?**
Section A has 15 multiple-choice questions for 15 marks, Section B has short-answer questions for 35 marks, and Sections C and D each require one essay from a choice of two, worth 25 marks each.

**Is 7132 still examined?**
Yes, it continues to be examined through summer 2027 for candidates already partway through, with the final A-level assessment in June 2027. Candidates starting in September 2026 take the replacement, 7138.

**How is 7138 different from 7132?**
Its three papers are each 2 hours but worth 90 marks, 270 in total. Paper 1 and Paper 2 each use two case studies followed by five compulsory questions, and first A-level exams are in June 2028.
