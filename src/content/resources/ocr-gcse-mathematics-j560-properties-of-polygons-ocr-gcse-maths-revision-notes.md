## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base angles | The two equal angles of an isosceles triangle, opposite the equal sides |
| Hypotenuse | The side opposite the right angle; the longest side |
| Diagonal | A line segment joining two vertices that are not next to each other |
| Bisect | Cut into two equal parts |
| Perpendicular | At 90° |
| Line of symmetry | A mirror line that maps the shape onto itself |
| Order of rotation symmetry | The number of times a shape fits onto itself in one full turn |

## Triangles

```
Equilateral:     3 equal sides; 3 angles of 60°; 3 lines; rotation order 3
Isosceles:       2 equal sides; base angles equal; 1 line; order 1
Right-angled:    one 90° angle; the other two add up to 90°
Right isosceles: 90°, 45°, 45°; 1 line of symmetry
```

## Quadrilaterals

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals | Lines | Order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal; 2 pairs parallel | all 90° | equal; bisect each other at 90°; bisect the angles | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | opposite equal and parallel | all 90° | equal; bisect each other | 2 | 2 |
| Rhombus | 4 equal; opposite parallel | opposite equal | bisect each other at 90°; bisect the angles | 2 | 2 |
| Parallelogram | opposite equal and parallel | opposite equal | bisect each other | 0 | 2 |
| Kite | 2 pairs of equal adjacent sides | one pair equal (between the unequal sides) | perpendicular; one bisects the other | 1 | 1 |
| Trapezium | one pair parallel | co-interior pairs add to 180° | (isosceles trapezium: equal) | 0 (isosceles: 1) | 1 |

Regular n-sided polygon: n lines of symmetry and rotation symmetry of order n.

## Reasons to quote

- "Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal."
- "Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal."
- "Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal and parallel."
- "The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles."
- "The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other."
- "In a kite, the angles between the unequal sides are equal."
- "The diagonal through the vertices between the equal sides of a kite is a line of symmetry."

## Why some properties are true

- Parallelogram opposite angles: each pair of neighbouring angles is co-interior (180°), so opposite angles equal 180° minus the same angle.
- Equilateral angles: three equal angles total 180°, so 60° each.
- Right-angled triangle: 180° − 90° leaves 90° for the other two.
- Isosceles base angles: reflection in the line of symmetry swaps them.

## Methods

- **Rhombus diagonals to side:** halve both diagonals, then Pythagoras. Diagonals 16 and 12 give half-diagonals 8 and 6, so side 10.
- **Rectangle diagonals with algebra:** the four half-diagonals are all equal; set two expressions equal.
- **Kite angles:** the two equal angles are at the ends of the shorter diagonal; the four angles add to 360°.
- **Identify from diagonals:** equal → rectangle family; perpendicular → rhombus or kite family; both bisect → parallelogram family.

Reminder: in rhombus ABCD, angle ABC = 124°, so angle BAD = 56° (co-interior) and the diagonal AC bisects it: angle BAC = 28°.

## Formal proof (Higher tier only)

Angle in a semicircle: draw OC to the point on the circle; OA = OB = OC (radii) gives two isosceles triangles with base angles x and y; the triangle sum gives 2x + 2y = 180, so angle ACB = x + y = 90°.

## Exam traps

- Rectangle diagonals are not perpendicular and are not lines of symmetry.
- A parallelogram has no lines of symmetry but rotation symmetry of order 2.
- Only one diagonal of a kite is bisected.
- A kite's equal angles are between the unequal sides.
- Order of rotation symmetry is never 0; "no rotation symmetry" is order 1.
- Pythagoras with whole diagonals instead of half-diagonals.
- Properties stated without a reason when "justify" or "give a reason" is asked.

## Quick self-test

1. Write down the angles of an equilateral triangle.
2. An isosceles triangle has a top angle of 34°. Find each base angle.
3. A right-angled triangle has one angle of 37°. Find the third angle.
4. Which quadrilaterals have diagonals that bisect each other at right angles?
5. Which quadrilateral has equal diagonals that are not perpendicular?
6. How many lines of symmetry has a rhombus that is not a square?
7. Write down the order of rotation symmetry of a parallelogram.
8. A rhombus has diagonals 16 cm and 12 cm. Find the length of a side.
9. In rhombus ABCD, angle ABC = 124°. Find angle BAC.
10. A kite has angles 80° and 130° and 130°. Find the fourth angle.
11. Write down the number of lines of symmetry and order of rotation symmetry of a regular octagon.
12. In rectangle WXYZ, the diagonals meet at M. WM = (3x − 2) cm and XM = (x + 6) cm. Find x.

### Answers

1. 60°, 60°, 60°
2. (180 − 34) ÷ 2 = 73°
3. 90 − 37 = 53°
4. Square and rhombus
5. Rectangle (that is not a square)
6. 2
7. 2
8. √(8² + 6²) = 10 cm
9. Angle BAD = 56°, so angle BAC = 28°
10. 360 − 80 − 130 − 130 = 20°
11. 8 lines; order 8
12. 3x − 2 = x + 6, so x = 4
