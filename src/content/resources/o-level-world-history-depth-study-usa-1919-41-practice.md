Depth Study D of Cambridge O Level History 2147 is assessed in **Paper 1, Section B**, which offers two questions on each Depth Study; you answer one, worth 20 marks -- (a) 4, (b) 6, (c) 10. The four questions below cover every key question.

## Question 1: The boom (key question 1)

**(a)** Describe how mass production changed the car industry in the 1920s. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why agriculture did not share in the prosperity of the 1920s. **[6]**

**(c)** "Government policies were the main reason for the economic boom of the 1920s." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Henry Ford's moving assembly line; each worker did one task; car-building time fell dramatically; the price of the Model T fell, so ordinary families could afford cars; car ownership rose rapidly; Ford paid higher wages ($5 a day) to keep workers; other firms such as General Motors copied the methods.

**(b)** European demand for US food fell after the war as European farming recovered; tractors and fertilisers increased output, causing over-production; prices fell, so farm incomes dropped; many farmers had borrowed to expand and could not repay loans; foreign countries put tariffs on US goods in retaliation for US tariffs, cutting exports.

**(c)** *Government policies:* Republican laissez-faire, low income tax for the wealthy, few regulations, and the Fordney-McCumber Tariff (1922), which protected US industry from imports. *Other factors:* mass production and new technology; electricity creating demand for consumer goods; credit and hire purchase; advertising; the USA's wealth and resources after the war; confidence and the stock market. *Judgement:* government created favourable conditions, but industry's new methods and consumer demand drove the boom.

## Question 2: Society in the 1920s (key question 2)

**(a)** What was the Ku Klux Klan's aim in the 1920s? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Prohibition was introduced in 1920. **[6]**

**(c)** "The lives of women changed a great deal in the 1920s." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** To keep white Protestant Americans dominant; it targeted Black Americans, Catholics, Jews and immigrants; it used intimidation, violence and lynching; it claimed to defend "100% Americanism"; it gained political influence in some states; membership reached millions in the mid-1920s.

**(b)** The temperance movement and the Anti-Saloon League had campaigned for decades; many Protestant churches, especially in rural areas, saw drink as sinful; alcohol was blamed for crime, poverty and violence in families; during the war, drinking seemed unpatriotic because many brewers were German-Americans and grain was needed for food; the 18th Amendment was ratified in 1919 and came into force in January 1920.

**(c)** *Change:* women gained the vote nationally in 1920; more women worked as typists, telephonists and shop assistants; flappers challenged conventions in dress and behaviour; labour-saving devices reduced housework for some; birth-control campaigns grew. *Continuity:* most women still married young and stayed at home; women were paid less than men; few held political office; rural, Black and immigrant women saw little change; many Americans disapproved of flappers. *Judgement:* change was real for young, urban, middle-class women, but limited for most.

## Question 3: The Wall Street Crash (key question 3)

**(a)** Describe what happened on the New York stock market in October 1929. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Roosevelt won the presidential election of 1932. **[6]**

**(c)** "Speculation was the main cause of the Wall Street Crash." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Share prices had begun to fall; on Black Thursday (24 October) nearly 13 million shares were sold in panic; leading bankers bought shares to restore confidence, but this failed; on Black Tuesday (29 October) over 16 million shares were sold; prices collapsed; many investors who had bought on the margin were ruined.

**(b)** The Depression had worsened under Hoover, with mass unemployment and bank failures; Hoover's belief in limited government action looked uncaring, especially after troops cleared the Bonus Army; Roosevelt promised a New Deal of direct government help; he had acted energetically as Governor of New York; he campaigned widely and seemed confident; he promised to end Prohibition.

**(c)** *Speculation:* rising prices encouraged buying on the margin with borrowed money; share prices rose far above company values; when confidence fell, investors had to sell to repay loans, causing a collapse. *Other causes:* over-production as demand fell by 1929; unequal distribution of wealth limited spending; weak, unregulated banks had lent to speculators; farm problems; tariffs reducing trade. *Judgement:* speculation explains the sudden crash in October 1929, but the economy's underlying weaknesses explain why it turned into a long Depression.

## Question 4: The New Deal (key question 4)

**(a)** Describe the work of the Tennessee Valley Authority. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Supreme Court opposed the New Deal. **[6]**

**(c)** "The New Deal failed because unemployment remained high throughout the 1930s." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Set up in 1933; covered a poor region across several states; built dams on the Tennessee River; produced cheap hydroelectric power; controlled flooding; created jobs; improved farming and reduced soil erosion.

**(b)** Most judges were conservative and had been appointed by Republican presidents; they believed the federal government was taking powers that belonged to the states or to private business; in 1935 they ruled the NRA unconstitutional and in 1936 the AAA; they saw some measures as interfering with free enterprise; Roosevelt's plan in 1937 to appoint extra judges made the conflict worse.

**(c)** *Agree:* unemployment fell from about 13 million (1933) but stayed high; the 1937-38 recession pushed it back up; full employment came only with war production. *Disagree:* millions gained work through the CCC, PWA and WPA; the banking system was saved; Social Security gave lasting protection; the Wagner Act strengthened workers; the TVA transformed a region; democracy survived. *Judgement:* measured by unemployment alone it fell short, but its aims included relief and reform, where it achieved a great deal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- (a): giving reasons instead of describing.
- (b): one reason only, or reasons without explanation.
- (c): no definition of what "success" or "main cause" means.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many questions should I practise?**
All four here: in the exam you pick one of two, and you cannot predict which key questions will appear.

**What figures are worth learning?**
Unemployment of about 13 million by 1933, the dates of Prohibition (1920-33), and the price of a Model T falling below $300 are enough; explanation earns more marks than extra statistics.
