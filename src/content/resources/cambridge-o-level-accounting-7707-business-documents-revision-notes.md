Condensed recall for **2.2 Business documents**, Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707, 2027-2029 syllabus: the eight documents (invoice, debit note, credit note, cheque counterfoil, paying-in slip, receipt, bank statement, statement of account); how they are used as sources of information; producing and recording them manually or digitally. Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## Key terms: the eight documents

| Document | From → to | What it shows | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invoice | Seller → buyer | Goods, quantities, prices, trade discount, total due, terms | Seller: sales journal. Buyer: purchases journal |
| Debit note | Buyer → seller (returns), or seller → buyer (undercharge) | Goods returned and credit requested; or an extra charge | Leads to a credit note; or recorded like an extra invoice |
| Credit note | Seller → buyer | Reduction in amount owed (returns, overcharge) | Seller: sales returns journal. Buyer: purchases returns journal |
| Cheque counterfoil | Kept by the payer | Date, payee, amount of cheque written | Payer: cash book payments (bank column) |
| Paying-in slip | Business → bank | Cash and cheques deposited | Cash book receipts (bank column) |
| Receipt | Receiver → payer | Proof of payment: amount, date, method | Evidence; cash book for cash sales |
| Statement of account | Seller → buyer | Opening balance, invoices, credit notes, payments, balance due | Checking against the supplier's ledger account; reminder to pay; **no entry** |
| Bank statement | Bank → business | All deposits, withdrawals, charges, transfers | Entering missing items (charges, interest, direct debits); checking the cash book |

## Calculations to master

**Invoice total.** List price × quantity, less trade discount.
12 lamps at $24.50 = 294.00; less 10% trade discount 29.40; **total $264.60**.

**Credit note.** Value returns at the price **charged**, after trade discount.
3 lamps returned: 3 × 24.50 × 90% = **$66.15**.

**Statement balance.** Opening balance + invoices − credit notes − payments.
500.00 + 264.60 − 66.15 − 500.00 = **$198.45**.

## How documents are used as sources

- **Record**: the first entry in a book of prime entry is made from the document.
- **Check**: compare invoice with order and goods received; statement of account with the ledger; bank statement with the cash book.
- **Prove**: receipts and counterfoils show payment was made.
- **Trace**: invoice and credit note numbers link the ledger back to the transaction.

## Manual or digital

| | Manual | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | No equipment needed; simple; works without power | Fast; arithmetic automatic; copies stored and searchable; can feed the books directly; easy to email |
| Limitations | Slow; arithmetic and copying errors; paper storage; easily lost or damaged | Cost and training; security risks; one wrong price or customer detail repeated on many documents |

Online transfers and card payments have replaced many cheques, so digital records often take the place of counterfoils and paying-in slips.

## Document flow for one credit sale

1. **Invoice** from seller: seller's sales journal, buyer's purchases journal.
2. **Debit note** from buyer if goods are faulty: a request for credit.
3. **Credit note** from seller: seller's sales returns journal, buyer's purchases returns journal.
4. **Statement of account** at the month end: buyer checks it against its purchases ledger.
5. **Cheque** written by buyer: buyer's **counterfoil** is the source for its cash book payment.
6. **Receipt** from seller: proof of payment for the buyer.
7. **Paying-in slip** when the seller banks the cheque: seller's cash book receipt.
8. **Bank statement** to each business: confirms the money moved and shows any charges.

## Exam traps

- **Credit note comes from the seller**, never the buyer.
- **Trade discount is never recorded**; only the net figure is.
- **Cash discount on an invoice is only an offer**; it is recorded only if payment is made in time.
- **Statement of account**: a summary for checking, not a source for a new entry.
- **Counterfoil vs receipt**: the payer keeps the counterfoil; the receiver issues the receipt.
- **Returns value**: use the price after trade discount.
- **Debit note has two uses**: read whether it is about returns or an undercharge.
