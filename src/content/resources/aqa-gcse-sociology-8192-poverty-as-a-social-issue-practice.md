## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which measure of poverty did Townsend prefer?

A. The state standard of poverty
B. The relative income standard
C. Relative deprivation
D. Absolute poverty

**[1]**

**2.** A benefit paid to everyone in a group, whatever their income, is called:

A. A selective benefit
B. A universal benefit
C. A means-tested benefit
D. A conditional benefit

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term "absolute poverty". **[2]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by the culture of poverty. **[2]**

**5.** Identify and explain **one** reason why Townsend criticised the state standard of poverty. **[3]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by a poverty trap. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**7.** Study the table. The data are fictional.

> **Northvale (a fictional region): households counted as poor under three measures, 2025 (survey of 1,200 households)**

| Measure | Households counted as poor |
|---|---|
| Benefit entitlement line | 96 |
| Income below the average for similar households | 132 |
| Deprivation index | 252 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of households counted as poor on the deprivation index. **[1]**
**(b)** Using the table, explain why sociologists say the amount of poverty depends on how it is defined. **[3]**

**8.** Read the item.

> Hollins Moor (a fictional town) lost its two largest factories when production moved overseas. Ten years later, unemployment is high. A local councillor says: "People here have lost the habit of work. Benefits are too easy to get."

**(a)** Explain how Murray would interpret the councillor's view. **[2]**
**(b)** Using the item, explain **one** way a material deprivation explanation would challenge the councillor. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which UK governments have tried to reduce poverty or unemployment. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** functionalist and **one** feminist view of poverty. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that poverty in the UK is caused mainly by the values of poor people. **[12]**

**12.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that globalisation has increased poverty in the UK. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** C. Relative deprivation [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* relative income was one of the measures Townsend criticised; do not confuse the two "relative" options.

**2.** B. A universal benefit [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "selective" and "means-tested" mean the same thing here, which is a clue that neither is the answer.

**3.** Lacking the basic necessities needed for survival [1], such as enough food, clean water, shelter and clothing [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the examples show what "basic" means; "very poor" is not a definition.

**4.** The idea that poor people develop distinct values, such as fatalism or living for the present [1], which are passed from parents to children and keep the family in poverty [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the second mark needs the idea of transmission between generations.

**5.** Townsend argued the state standard was arbitrary [1] because it was based on entitlement to benefits [1], which was decided by the government of the day rather than by what people actually need, so it could change for political reasons and miss people who were deprived [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "arbitrary" is the word the specification's summary uses; explain what made it arbitrary.

**6.** A poverty trap occurs when a person on means-tested benefits earns more [1] but loses benefits as their income rises [1], so they gain little or nothing from working extra hours and may stay on a low income [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* link it to means testing; that is the mechanism.

**7. (a)** 252 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 21% [1]
**(b)** On the benefit entitlement line only 8% of households are poor (96 ÷ 1,200), but on the deprivation index 21% are [1]. This mirrors Townsend's finding that his relative deprivation measure found far more poverty than the state standard [1]. The amount of poverty depends on the definition because each measure draws the line in a different place, based on benefits, average income or lack of what is widely available [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* calculate at least one comparison figure in (b); describing "bigger" and "smaller" alone limits marks.

**8. (a)** Murray would see the town as having an emerging underclass [1] whose dependence on benefits has replaced the value of hard work, supporting the New Right view that the benefits system creates a culture of dependency [1].
**(b)** Unemployment followed the factory closures when production moved overseas [1], so the cause is a lack of jobs and income, not a change in values; people may want work but there is none available locally [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the item is built so that the closure date comes before the unemployment; use that order of events.

**9.** First, the national minimum wage, introduced in 1999, set a legal floor for pay [1], aiming to reduce in-work poverty among low-paid workers [1]. Second, Universal Credit, introduced from 2013, combined several means-tested benefits [1] and was designed to make work pay by reducing the sudden loss of benefits when people take a job [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the welfare state after the Beveridge Report, job search support and training schemes are also creditworthy.

**10.** Functionalist: Gans argued that poverty has functions for the better-off [1], for example ensuring that low-paid and unpleasant jobs are done [1]. Feminist: women face a higher risk of poverty [1] because of lower pay, part-time work, unpaid caring and lone parenthood [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* name the perspective before each point so the examiner can see the contrast.

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- The claim matches the culture of poverty explanation [1].
- Lewis's idea of values such as fatalism passed between generations supports it [1].
- Murray argues benefits created an underclass with values that reject work [1].
- Murray pointed to rising illegitimacy, crime and youth unwillingness to work in Britain [1].
- New Right theories blame welfare for a culture of dependency [1].
- Against: material deprivation explains poverty by low pay, unemployment and lack of resources [1].
- Townsend's relative deprivation measure found far more poverty than the state standard, showing poverty as lack of resources [1].
- Murray's cultural definition largely ignores economic reasons and has a poor evidence base [1].
- Many labelled "underclass" hold conventional attitudes and want paid work [1].
- Globalisation removed industrial jobs, causing structural unemployment [1].
- Marxist: poverty is built into capitalism; feminist: women's caring roles and lower pay increase risk [1].
- Judgement: values may matter for some individuals, but structural causes explain why whole areas and groups are poor, so the claim is mostly rejected [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* "mainly" invites you to weigh causes against each other; say which carries more weight and why.

**12.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Globalisation is the growing interconnection of economies worldwide [1].
- Manufacturing jobs moved to countries with lower wages [1].
- Former industrial areas faced unemployment and long-term decline [1].
- Competition from abroad can hold down wages and make work less secure [1].
- This supports a material deprivation explanation of poverty [1].
- Marxists see this as capitalism moving production to maximise profit [1].
- Against: cheaper imported goods lower the cost of living for poor households [1].
- New jobs in services and technology have been created [1].
- Government policy, such as the minimum wage and benefits, affects how far job loss becomes poverty [1].
- New Right: poverty reflects dependency and values, not global change [1].
- Feminist: women's poverty is linked more to caring and low pay than to factory closures [1].
- Judgement: globalisation has increased poverty in some areas and groups, but its effects depend on policy and on other factors such as gender, so it is a major but not sole cause [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* globalisation answers need balance; examiners expect at least one benefit as well as harms.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up Townsend's three measures.
- Calling Murray Marxist.
- Describing a policy without explaining how it was meant to reduce poverty.
- Using Townsend's 1968–69 figures as if they describe today.
- One-sided globalisation answers.
