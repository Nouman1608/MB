## Questions

**1.** Sketch the curve y = (x + 2)(x − 1)(x − 5), labelling every point where it meets the axes. **[3]**

**2.** Sketch y = (x − 1)²(x + 2)², labelling the points where it meets the axes and the coordinates of its maximum point. **[4]**

**3.** A cubic curve touches the x-axis at x = −1, crosses it at x = 3 and passes through (0, −9). Find its equation in the form y = ax³ + bx² + cx + d. **[4]**

**4.** Sketch y = 6x² − x³, finding the coordinates of its stationary points and stating their nature. **[5]**

**5.** On one set of axes, sketch y = −8/x and y = 8/x², showing the asymptotes. **[3]**
Find the coordinates of the point where the two curves meet. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Solve x³ − 9x = 3x. **[3]**
(b) Explain what the solutions to part (a) represent on the graphs of y = x³ − 9x and y = 3x. **[1]**
(c) Hence solve x³ − 9x < 3x. **[2]**

**7.** (a) Sketch y = eˣ and y = 4 − x on one set of axes and explain why eˣ = 4 − x has exactly one real solution. **[2]**
(b) Show that the solution lies between x = 1 and x = 1.5. **[3]**

**8.** By sketching y = sin x and y = x/3 for x in radians, find how many solutions sin x = x/3 has. **[3]**

**9.** y is proportional to √x. When x = 16, y = 12. Find y when x = 25, and find x when y = 21. **[4]**

**10.** The table shows values of x and y.

| x | 1 | 2 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 24 | 12 | 6 | 4.8 |

(a) Show that y is inversely proportional to x. **[2]**
(b) State what to plot against y to obtain a straight line through the origin, and give the gradient. **[2]**
(c) Find y when x = 8. **[1]**

**11.** The curve C has equation y = x²(x + 3) and the line L has equation y = 4x.
(a) Sketch C and L on one set of axes, labelling the intercepts of C and the coordinates of its maximum point. **[3]**
(b) Find the coordinates of all the points where C meets L. **[3]**
(c) Hence solve x²(x + 3) ≤ 4x. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Roots at x = −2, 1 and 5, so the curve crosses the x-axis at (−2, 0), (1, 0) and (5, 0). [1] Put x = 0: (2)(−1)(−5) = 10, so the y-intercept is **(0, 10)**. [1] Positive cubic: the curve comes from bottom left, crosses at −2, peaks above the axis, crosses at 1, dips below, and crosses at 5 heading up to the top right. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch is easier to credit when each axis crossing carries its coordinates and the curve clearly continues beyond the last root instead of stopping at it.

**2.** Both roots are repeated, so the curve touches the x-axis at (1, 0) and (−2, 0) without crossing. [1] At x = 0, y = (1)(4) = **4**, so the y-intercept is (0, 4). [1] dy/dx = 2(x − 1)(x + 2)² + 2(x − 1)²(x + 2) = 2(x − 1)(x + 2)(2x + 1). [1] The middle stationary point is x = −1/2, where y = (−3/2)²(3/2)² = 81/16, so the maximum is **(−1/2, 81/16)**. The curve is W-shaped, with both ends rising. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A product-rule derivative is easiest to check when you factorise it fully. Naming the point as a maximum, not just giving its coordinates, shows you have read the shape.

**3.** The curve touches at −1 and crosses at 3, so y = k(x + 1)²(x − 3). [1] At (0, −9): k(1)(−3) = −9. [1] k = 3. [1] Expanding: 3(x² + 2x + 1)(x − 3) = **y = 3x³ − 3x² − 15x − 9**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Begin with the factorised form, since the roots are given. Leaving out k, or squaring the wrong factor, loses the whole structure of the answer.

**4.** y = x²(6 − x). The curve touches the x-axis at (0, 0) and crosses at (6, 0). [1] The leading term is −x³, so it comes from top left and finishes at bottom right. [1] dy/dx = 12x − 3x² = 3x(4 − x), zero at x = 0 and x = 4. [1] x = 0: y = 0, and d²y/dx² = 12 − 6x = 12 > 0, so **minimum at (0, 0)**. [1] x = 4: y = 96 − 64 = 32, and d²y/dx² = −12 < 0, so **maximum at (4, 32)**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A stationary point question usually asks for its nature as well as its position. A short reason, such as the sign of the second derivative, supports the label you give.

**5.** y = −8/x: two branches in quadrants 2 and 4. [1] y = 8/x²: two branches above the x-axis, symmetric about the y-axis. [1] Both have the asymptotes x = 0 and y = 0 drawn and the curves approach them without meeting. [1]
Equate: −8/x = 8/x², so −8x = 8 and x = −1. [1] y = 8/1 = 8, so **(−1, 8)**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* On the same axes the two curves overlap in quadrant 2. Check that your sketch shows the intersection there, matching the algebra.

**6. (a)** x³ − 12x = 0. [1] x(x² − 12) = 0. [1] **x = 0, x = 2√3, x = −2√3.** [1]
**(b)** They are the x-coordinates of the points where the curve y = x³ − 9x meets the line y = 3x. [1]
**(c)** The curve is below the line when x³ − 12x < 0. For x > 2√3 the cubic is positive, so it is negative between 0 and 2√3, positive between −2√3 and 0, and negative below −2√3. [1] **x < −2√3 or 0 < x < 2√3.** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing the first equation by x would lose the solution x = 0. Factorise instead and use the sketch to read off the intervals.

**7. (a)** eˣ is increasing and always positive, passing through (0, 1). The line 4 − x is decreasing. [1] An increasing curve and a decreasing line can meet only once. [1]
**(b)** Let f(x) = eˣ + x − 4. f(1) = e − 3 ≈ −0.28, which is negative. [1] f(1.5) = e^1.5 + 1.5 − 4 ≈ 1.98, which is positive. [1] The sign changes, so the intersection (the solution) lies between **1 and 1.5**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the two values and the sign of each. Stating only that "it changes sign" without the numbers leaves your claim without evidence.

**8.** Sketch y = sin x, which oscillates between −1 and 1, and the line y = x/3 through the origin with gradient 1/3. [1] Since |sin x| ≤ 1, any intersection has |x/3| ≤ 1, so |x| ≤ 3. At x = π/2 the sine is 1 and the line is about 0.52; at x = π the sine is 0 and the line is about 1.05, so they cross once in this range. [1] By symmetry the same happens for negative x, and they also meet at the origin: **3 solutions**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A convincing count uses a stated bound, such as |sin x| ≤ 1, to rule out any intersection beyond x = 3.

**9.** y = k√x. [1] 12 = k × 4, so k = 3. [1] x = 25: y = 3 × 5 = **15**. [1] y = 21: √x = 7, so x = **49**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Find k from the given pair, write it into the equation, and only then use it. Squaring the wrong side when finding x is a common slip.

**10. (a)** xy = 24, 24, 24 and 24 for the four pairs. [1] The product is constant, so y = 24/x and y is inversely proportional to x. [1]
**(b)** Plot y against 1/x. [1] The graph is a straight line through the origin with gradient **24**. [1]
**(c)** y = 24/8 = **3**. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the product for every pair. A single check does not demonstrate that a relationship holds for all the data.

**11. (a)** C touches the x-axis at (0, 0) and crosses it at (−3, 0). [1] dy/dx = 3x² + 6x = 3x(x + 2), so the maximum is at (−2, 4). [1] L is a straight line through the origin with gradient 4, shown on the same axes. [1]
**(b)** x²(x + 3) = 4x gives x(x² + 3x − 4) = 0. [1] x(x + 4)(x − 1) = 0, so x = −4, 0, 1. [1] The points are **(−4, −16), (0, 0) and (1, 4)**. [1]
**(c)** C is below or on L when x(x + 4)(x − 1) ≤ 0. [1] **x ≤ −4 or 0 ≤ x ≤ 1.** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) follows from the sketch. Use the strict or non-strict inequality that matches the question, and include the intersections when the sign is "less than or equal".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing a repeated root as a crossing, or a single root as a touch.
- Leaving out the y-intercept or giving an x-value without the corresponding y-value.
- Getting the ends of a cubic or quartic the wrong way round for a negative leading coefficient.
- Dividing by x, or by another factor containing x, and losing a solution.
- Giving stationary points without saying whether each is a maximum or a minimum.
- Drawing reciprocal curves that cross an axis or bend back from an asymptote.
- Reading an inequality from a graph without testing a value in each interval.
- Writing only x-values when the question asks for coordinates.
- Treating a linear graph with a non-zero intercept as a proportional relationship.
