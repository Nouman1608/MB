These questions cover section 3.8.2 strategic positioning of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-positioning-how-to-compete/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-positioning-how-to-compete-revision-notes/).

Questions 6 to 9 use this data on three cycle helmet makers.

| | Ardway | Quill | Marsden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price per helmet | £25 | £45 | £32 |
| Cost per helmet | £21 | £36 | £29 |
| Helmets sold a year | 80,000 | 30,000 | 40,000 |
| Description | Standard design, huge factory, few frills | Lightweight, award-winning design, strong brand | Mid-range, no strong features |

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a differentiation strategy? **[1]**

A: Cutting costs through bulk buying to charge the lowest price

B: Offering a unique design that customers will pay more for

C: Selling only to left-handed customers at the lowest price

D: Selling the same product as rivals at the same price

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a risk of a cost leadership strategy? **[1]**

A: A rival finds a way to produce at even lower cost

B: The firm's prices are too high

C: The brand is too exclusive

D: The firm's profit margins are too high

**3.** Define "competitive advantage". **[2]**

**4.** A business sells 50,000 units at £30 with a unit cost of £24. Calculate its annual profit and its profit margin. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why focus strategies can be attractive to a small business. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**6.** Calculate the annual profit and profit margin for each of the three helmet makers. **[6]**

**7.** Identify the strategic position of each of the three firms, using Porter's strategies. **[3]**

**8.** Analyse **one** reason why Marsden earns a much lower profit margin than the other firms. **[4]**

**9.** Ardway's costs rise by 5% because of higher energy prices, and it keeps its price unchanged. Calculate the new annual profit (assuming sales stay the same) and the percentage fall. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** difficulties that Quill might have in maintaining its competitive advantage. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse why a differentiation strategy may be less successful during a recession. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether a small independent bookshop should follow a low cost or a differentiation focus strategy. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Differentiation relies on a unique offer that customers value and pay a premium for. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. The cost leader's advantage disappears if a rival's costs fall below its own. **[1]**

**3.** Something that gives a business a lasting edge over its rivals [1], such as lower costs, a stronger brand or technology, so it wins more customers or profit [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Profit = (£30 − £24) × 50,000 = **£300,000** [1]; profit per unit £6 shown or implied [1]; margin = 6 ÷ 30 × 100 = **20%** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A focus strategy targets a narrow group with specific needs [1], so the small business can learn their needs better than large rivals [1] and avoid direct competition with large firms that find the niche too small to be worth serving [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Ardway: (25 − 21) × 80,000 = **£320,000** [1]; margin 4 ÷ 25 = **16.0%** [1]. Quill: (45 − 36) × 30,000 = **£270,000** [1]; margin 9 ÷ 45 = **20.0%** [1]. Marsden: (32 − 29) × 40,000 = **£120,000** [1]; margin 3 ÷ 32 = **9.4%** [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Ardway: cost leadership (low cost) [1]. Quill: differentiation [1]. Marsden: no clear position, "stuck in the middle" [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Marsden has neither the lowest cost nor a distinctive product [1]. It cannot match Ardway's price because its costs are higher (£29 against £21) [1], and it cannot charge Quill's premium because it offers no strong features or brand [1]. It therefore earns a small gap between price and cost on each helmet and has a low volume [1]. **[4]**

**9.** New cost = £21 × 1.05 = £22.05 [1]; profit per helmet = £25 − £22.05 = £2.95 [1]; annual profit = £2.95 × 80,000 = **£236,000** [1]; fall = (320,000 − 236,000) ÷ 320,000 = **26.25%** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Difficulty 1: imitation [1]. Rivals can copy lightweight designs or launch similar products at lower prices, so the premium disappears [1]. Difficulty 2: changing customer tastes or a recession [1]. If customers decide a cheaper helmet is good enough, they will not pay £45, and sales fall [1]. (Credit also: the cost of continuing to invest in design; damage to brand from a safety scandal.) **[4]**

**11.** Differentiation depends on customers paying a premium [1]. In a recession incomes fall and customers become more price-conscious [1], so they trade down to cheaper alternatives [1]. Demand for premium goods is usually income-elastic, so sales fall by a larger percentage than income [1]. The differentiated firm has higher costs and may need volume to cover them [1], so profit can fall sharply [1]. However, loyal customers and a strong brand can protect some of the firm's sales. **[6]**

**12.** Low cost: for a small shop this is difficult, because large online retailers have economies of scale and bulk-buying power [1]. A small shop would struggle to match the price and could be undercut [1]. Differentiation focus: for the independent shop, it can offer expert advice, author events, local focus and a community role [1], which large rivals find hard to copy [1]. Customers may pay more for the experience and personal service [1], and loyal local customers reduce price sensitivity [1]. Risks: the niche may be small, and online sales can still undercut on price [1]; the shop must keep investing in events and knowledge to hold its edge [1]. Factors: the size of the local customer base [1], the shop's skills, and how many customers value the extra service enough to pay [1]. Judgement: a differentiation focus is likely to be better, because it plays to the shop's strengths [1], but it needs a clear niche such as children's books, specialist subjects or second-hand rarities [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers use the numbers to compare margins and volumes, then judge the strategy against the firm's resources and customers.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing low cost with low price.
- Assuming differentiation always brings a premium.
- Treating focus as simply "being small".
- Forgetting to explain how an advantage might be lost.
- Giving a recommendation with no link to the business's resources.
