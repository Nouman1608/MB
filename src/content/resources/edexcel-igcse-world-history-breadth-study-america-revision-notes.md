## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Federalist | Supporter of a strong central government and the Constitution |
| Strict / loose construction | Reading the Constitution narrowly / allowing implied powers |
| States' rights | Belief that states hold powers the federal government cannot override |
| Manifest Destiny | Belief that the USA was destined to spread across the continent |
| Popular sovereignty | Letting settlers vote on slavery in a territory |
| Secession | A state leaving the Union |
| Emancipation | Freeing enslaved people |
| Reconstruction | Rebuilding and reorganising the South after 1865 |
| Black Codes | Southern laws restricting freed people |

## Worked example: explain two ways different

*Explain two ways in which the position of enslaved people in 1850 was different from their position in
1870.* (6 marks)

1. **Legal status:** in 1850 slavery was legal in the South and protected by the Fugitive Slave Act; by
   1870 the 13th Amendment had abolished it.
2. **Rights:** in 1850 black people had no citizenship (confirmed by Dred Scott in 1857); by 1870 the 14th
   and 15th Amendments gave citizenship and banned racial discrimination in voting.

## Essay plan in outline

*"Westward expansion was the main cause of conflict between North and South in the years 1803–61."
How far do you agree?*

- **For:** each new territory reopened the slavery question — Missouri (1820), the Mexican lands and the
  Compromise of 1850, Kansas–Nebraska (1854) and Bleeding Kansas.
- **Against:** slavery itself and the moral campaign against it; states' rights; economic differences
  between the industrial North and the cotton South; Dred Scott and John Brown; Lincoln's election.
- **Judgement:** expansion was the arena in which the slavery conflict was fought; slavery was the issue.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Narrating instead of tracing change.** The (c) question covers at least 25 years; show change and
  continuity.
- **Confusing the compromises.** 1787 (Connecticut), 1820 (Missouri), 1850.
- **Treating emancipation as complete equality.** Black Codes and later violence limited freedom.
- **Leaving out Native Americans.** They appear in topics 2 and 5.
- **Inventing numbers.** Use approximate figures only when certain.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was the Missouri Compromise important?**
It balanced slave and free states and drew a line that kept
the peace until 1854.

**What made 1863 a turning point?**
Gettysburg, Vicksburg and the Emancipation Proclamation.

**Why did Reconstruction end in 1877?**
Northern commitment faded, white Southern resistance grew, and
federal troops were withdrawn.
