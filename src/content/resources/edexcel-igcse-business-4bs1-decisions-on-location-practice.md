Use the [decisions on location study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-decisions-on-location/) and the [decisions on location revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-decisions-on-location-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Show your workings.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define relocation. **[1]**
**(b)** Define trade bloc. **[1]**

**2.** Give two factors that influence the location of a factory. **[2]**

**3.** Sana runs a nail salon. She says: "Most of my customers book after walking past the window." State the location factor that matters most to Sana. **[1]**

**4.** A business uses 6 tonnes of materials to make 1 tonne of product. Its supplier and its market are 150 km apart. Transport costs 0.30 per tonne per kilometre. Calculate how much it saves per tonne of product by locating near its supplier rather than near its market. **[2]**

**5.** A business imports parts worth 800,000 into a country that charges an 8% tariff. Calculate the total cost of the parts including the tariff. **[2]**

**6.** Outline one benefit to a small clothes shop of adding e-commerce to its fixed premises. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one disadvantage to a business of locating near its competitors. **[3]**

**8.** Ashby Print pays rent of 3,000 a month in the city centre. A unit on an industrial estate costs 1,800 a month. Moving would raise delivery costs by 4,200 a year and cost 18,000 once.
**(a)** Calculate the yearly rent saving. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net yearly saving. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how long it would take to recover the cost of moving, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(d)** Analyse one risk to Ashby Print of relocating. **[4]**

**9.** Nova Electronics plc needs a factory with 1,200 workers to supply customers inside a trade bloc. Output will be worth 80 million a year. Country P is inside the bloc, with average wages of 14,000 a year. Country Q is outside it, with average wages of 10,000 a year; goods from Country Q pay a 5% tariff on entering the bloc. Country P has a larger pool of trained engineers.
**(a)** Calculate the total yearly wages in each country. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the yearly tariff if the factory is in Country Q. **[1]**
**(c)** State which country has lower wages plus tariff, and by how much. **[2]**
**(d)** Evaluate where Nova Electronics should locate its factory. **[8]**

**10.** Rosa's Bakes makes celebration cakes. Option A is a shop on the high street at 2,200 a month. Option B is a kitchen unit on the edge of town at 900 a month, selling only online with local delivery. Most of Rosa's orders come from people who want to see designs and taste samples before ordering. Justify which option Rosa should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse why a software company usually has more freedom in choosing its location than a cement maker. **[6]**

**12.** Explain how a new legal control could cause a business to relocate. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Moving an existing business to a new site [1].
**(b)** A group of countries that agree to reduce barriers to trade between themselves [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: proximity to market [1], to labour [1], to materials, to competitors; legal controls; trade blocs. **[2]**

**3.** Proximity to market (passing customers) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Near the supplier: 1 × 150 × 0.30 = 45; near the market: 6 × 150 × 0.30 = 270 [1]; saving **225** per tonne [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Tariff 8% × 800,000 = 64,000 [1]; total **864,000** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The shop can sell to customers beyond its town [1], so it can increase sales without paying for a second shop in another place [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Customers can easily compare prices with nearby rivals [1], so the business may have to cut its prices to keep them [1], which reduces its profit margin (or: rivals compete for the same skilled staff, raising wages) [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** (3,000 − 1,800) × 12 = **14,400** [1].
**(b)** 14,400 − 4,200 = **10,200** [1].
**(c)** 18,000 ÷ 10,200 [1] = **1.8 years** [1].
**(d)** Some workers may not be able to travel to the industrial estate [1], so Ashby could lose experienced staff [1]. It would then pay to recruit and train replacements [1], and work could be slower or contain more errors in the meantime, which may upset customers and cut into the saving [1]. Losing city-centre customers who walk in is also accepted. **[8]**

**9. (a)** Country P: 1,200 × 14,000 = **16.8 million** [1]. Country Q: 1,200 × 10,000 = **12.0 million** [1].
**(b)** 5% × 80 million = **4.0 million** [1].
**(c)** Country Q [1]: 12.0 + 4.0 = 16.0 million against 16.8 million, so **0.8 million** lower [1].
**(d)** For Country Q: it is 0.8 million a year cheaper on wages plus tariff [1], which raises profit for shareholders [1]. For Country P: being inside the bloc means no tariff, so if the tariff rose, Country Q would lose its advantage [1]; P has more trained engineers, so productivity and quality may be higher [1], which could more than make up for the 0.8 million [1]. Goods from P also reach bloc customers without border delays [1]. Other factors: transport links and legal controls in each country [1]. Judgement: for example, Country P, because the cost gap is small (1% of output) and the skilled workforce and secure access to the bloc reduce risk [1]. **[13]**

**10.** Option A is the better choice [1]. Rosa's customers want to see designs and taste samples before ordering [1], which an online-only business cannot offer [1], so Option B could lose most of her orders [1]. The high street gives passing trade and a place to meet customers [1]. Option B saves 1,300 a month, but the saving is worth little if sales fall sharply; she could still add online ordering to the shop [1]. **[6]**

**11.** A cement maker uses heavy, bulky materials such as limestone [1], so it locates near its quarry to keep transport costs down [1], and it needs large sites and legal approval for dust and noise [1]. A software company's product is delivered over the internet [1], so it has no materials to transport and no need for passing trade [1]. Its main need is skilled labour, so it can locate anywhere it can attract programmers, or let staff work remotely [1]. **[6]**

**12.** A government may introduce stricter limits on noise or pollution near housing [1]. A factory in a residential area may then be unable to meet the limits without very expensive equipment [1], so it may move to an industrial area where its activity is allowed [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing factors without deciding which one matters most in the case.
- Forgetting the one-off moving cost, or dividing it the wrong way.
- Assuming lower wages always mean lower costs.
- Ignoring customers' need to visit when recommending e-commerce only.
- Naming a specific law instead of describing a legal control.
