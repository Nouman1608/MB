**The Mughal Empire (c1526–c1850s) is topic 3.1.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies
9236, one of two optional topics in Paper 1, Section C.** It covers the founding of the empire by
Babur, its growth under Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan, the reign of Aurangzeb, and the
decline that followed, ending with British expansion. Section C is worth 21 marks of short and
extended answers, so be ready to describe each ruler's achievements and to explain causes of
growth and decline.

## What was the context in 1526?

North India was ruled by the **Lodi Sultanate** of Delhi, weakened by quarrels among Afghan nobles,
while Rajput kingdoms such as Mewar were powerful. In Central Asia, Timurid princes competed with
the Uzbeks and with Safavid Persia. Religiously, India had a Hindu majority, Muslim rulers and a
strong Sufi tradition, together with the *bhakti* devotional movements.

## How did Babur found the empire?

**Babur** (ruled in India 1526–30) descended from Timur on his father's side and from Genghis Khan
on his mother's. He lost his homeland of Fergana, took Kabul in 1504 and then turned to India. At the
**First Battle of Panipat (1526)** his smaller army, using field artillery and cavalry tactics,
defeated Sultan Ibrahim Lodi. He then defeated the Rajput confederacy under Rana Sanga at **Khanwa
(1527)**. His memoir, the *Baburnama*, shows a ruler who was a skilled soldier, poet and lover of
gardens.

## How did the empire grow to 1658?

- **Humayun (1530–40, 1555–56):** lost the empire to the Afghan Sher Shah Suri and spent years in exile in Persia before regaining Delhi shortly before his death.
- **Akbar (1556–1605):** won the Second Battle of Panipat (1556); expanded the empire across north India, Gujarat, Bengal and Kashmir; built alliances with Rajput rulers; organised nobles through the **mansabdari** system; reformed land revenue with his finance minister **Raja Todar Mal**; abolished the jizya; held religious debates in the *Ibadat Khana*; built **Fatehpur Sikri**.
- **Jahangir (1605–27):** continued Akbar's system; his wife **Nur Jahan** was influential; Mughal painting flourished; he received the English ambassador **Sir Thomas Roe**, who won trading rights for the East India Company.
- **Shah Jahan (1628–58):** the high point of Mughal architecture, including the **Taj Mahal** at Agra, the **Red Fort** and **Jama Masjid** in Delhi, and the **Shalimar Gardens** in Lahore.

**Economy and trade:** a unified empire, good roads, a stable silver currency and secure trade
routes supported agriculture, textile production and overseas trade with Persia, Central Asia and
European companies.

**Culture:** Persian was the language of government and literature; Mughal painting, music and
architecture blended Persian, Central Asian and Indian styles; madrasas and libraries spread
learning.

## How did Aurangzeb rule, and what state did he leave the empire in?

**Aurangzeb (1658–1707)** was Shah Jahan's third son, known for his piety, discipline and simple
lifestyle. He defeated his brothers, including Dara Shikoh, in the war of succession and confined
his father in Agra fort.

**Reforms:** he governed according to the Sharia, sponsored the compilation of the legal code
**Fatawa-e-Alamgiri**, appointed *muhtasibs* to supervise public morality, reimposed the **jizya** in
1679, removed taxes he regarded as un-Islamic, and reduced court spending on music and display. He
also built the **Badshahi Mosque** in Lahore.

**Expansion:** he conquered the Deccan sultanates of **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)**,
taking the empire to its greatest size, but fought the Marathas, first under Shivaji, for more than
twenty years.

**At his death (1707):** *strengths*: the largest territory of any Mughal ruler, a functioning
administration and army. *Weaknesses*: the long Deccan wars had drained the treasury; Rajput,
Jat, Sikh and Maratha revolts continued; there were too few *jagirs* (land grants) for the growing
number of nobles; and no clear rule of succession.

## Why did the Mughal Empire decline after 1707?

- **Weak leadership:** frequent wars of succession and short reigns; powerful nobles, such as the Sayyid brothers, controlled emperors.
- **Nadir Shah's invasion:** the Persian ruler defeated Emperor Muhammad Shah at the **Battle of Karnal (1739)** and **sacked Delhi**, carrying off vast treasure, including the Peacock Throne. Mughal prestige never recovered.
- **Marathas and Sikhs:** the Marathas expanded across central and western India, and Sikh chiefs took control of Punjab, where **Ranjit Singh** later built a kingdom with its capital at Lahore.
- **The East India Company:** at **Plassey (1757)**, Robert Clive defeated Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula of Bengal after securing the defection of Mir Jafar. The Company gained control of Bengal's wealth and in 1765 the right to collect its revenue.
- **British expansion to the 1850s:** wars against Mysore and the Marathas, the annexation of **Sindh (1843)** and **Punjab (1849)**, and of Awadh (1856). After the War of Independence of 1857, the last emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was exiled in 1858.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was the mansabdari system?** Akbar's system of ranking nobles and officials by a *mansab*
(rank) that set their pay and the number of troops they had to provide.

**Why was Plassey so important?** It gave the East India Company control of Bengal, India's richest
province, and the money to expand further.

**Was Aurangzeb responsible for the decline?** Partly: his long wars and costly campaigns weakened
the empire, but weak successors, invasions and the rise of other powers after 1707 were also
decisive.
