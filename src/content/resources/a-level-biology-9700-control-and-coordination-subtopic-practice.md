These questions work through Topic 15, Control and coordination, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027, one subtopic at a time: section 15.1 (Control and coordination in mammals, outcomes 1–12) and section 15.2 (Control and coordination in plants, outcomes 1–3). ## Questions

**1.** *(15.1, outcomes 1 and 2)* After a long run without drinking, a person's blood becomes more concentrated and ADH is released.

**(a)** Using ADH, insulin and glucagon as examples, describe three features of the endocrine system. **[3]**
**(b)** Compare the nervous system and the endocrine system. **[3]**

**2.** *(15.1, outcomes 3–5)* Taste-bud chemoreceptor cells detect sodium ions in salty food.

**(a)** State two differences in structure and one difference in function between the sensory neurone that carries this information and a motor neurone. **[3]**
**(b)** State the role of intermediate neurones. **[1]**
**(c)** Outline the role of a sensory receptor cell. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe the events that lead from the arrival of sodium ions to an action potential in the sensory neurone. **[5]**

**3.** *(15.1, outcome 6)* An axon's resting potential is −65 mV. During an action potential it rises to +30 mV, then falls to −80 mV before returning to −65 mV.

**(a)** Calculate the change in membrane potential from rest to the peak. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the resting potential is maintained. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the changes in membrane potential from the start of the action potential until it reaches −80 mV. **[4]**

**4.** *(15.1, outcomes 6 and 8)* The refractory period of a neurone lasts 1.6 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum frequency of impulses this neurone can carry. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the resting potential is restored during the refractory period, and why this period limits impulse frequency. **[3]**

**5.** *(15.1, outcome 7)* In a myelinated axon the nodes of Ranvier are 1.2 mm apart, and impulses travel at 60 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken for an impulse to pass from one node to the next, in ms. **[2]**
**(b)** In some diseases the myelin sheath is damaged. Suggest and explain the effect on impulse transmission. **[3]**

**6.** *(15.1, outcome 9)* **(a)** Describe the structure of a cholinergic synapse. **[4]**
**(b)** A toxin blocks the voltage-gated calcium ion channels in the presynaptic membrane. Explain why no action potentials are produced in the postsynaptic neurone. **[3]**

**7.** *(15.1, outcome 10)* Describe how an action potential in a motor neurone leads to the release of calcium ions inside a striated muscle fibre. **[5]**

**8.** *(15.1, outcome 11)* An electron micrograph of relaxed striated muscle shows sarcomeres 2.6 µm long, with an A band of 1.6 µm and an H zone of 0.6 µm.

**(a)** Describe how you would identify one sarcomere, the A band and the I band on the micrograph. **[3]**
**(b)** The sarcomere contracts to 2.2 µm. Calculate the new length of the H zone. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the length of one thin (actin) filament, from the Z line to its free end. **[2]**

**9.** *(15.1, outcome 12)* **(a)** Explain the sliding filament model of muscle contraction, including the roles of calcium ions, troponin, tropomyosin and ATP. **[6]**
**(b)** Suggest why muscles stay contracted if ATP runs out. **[2]**

**10.** *(15.2, outcome 1)* An insect bends the sensitive hairs on one lobe of an open Venus fly trap.

**(a)** Describe how stimulating the hairs leads to the trap closing. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how closure of the trap is achieved. **[3]**

**11.** *(15.2, outcomes 2 and 3)* **(a)** Explain how auxin causes elongation growth in cells behind a shoot tip. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe the role of gibberellin in the germination of a barley seed. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Hormones are **secreted into the blood** by ductless glands: ADH by the posterior pituitary, insulin and glucagon by the islets of Langerhans [1]; they are **carried in the blood** around the body [1]; each acts only on **target cells with specific receptors**: ADH on collecting duct cells, insulin and glucagon on liver cells [1].
**(b)** Nervous: **electrical impulses** along neurones; endocrine: **hormones** carried in the blood [1]. Nervous responses are **faster** and usually **short-lived**; hormonal responses are **slower** and often **longer-lasting** [1]. Nervous signals reach **specific effectors**; hormones can affect **widespread target cells** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both systems in each point; "the nervous system is fast" alone is not credited.

**2. (a)** The sensory neurone's **cell body is outside the CNS**, part-way along; the motor neurone's **cell body is inside the CNS**, at one end [1]. The sensory neurone has a **long dendron** to its cell body; the motor neurone has **many short dendrites** [1]. The sensory neurone carries impulses **from a receptor to the CNS**; the motor neurone carries them **from the CNS to an effector** [1].
**(b)** They **connect sensory neurones to motor neurones** in the CNS [1].
**(c)** It **detects a stimulus** and converts it into a **receptor potential** (a change in membrane potential) [1]; if this reaches **threshold** it **stimulates an action potential** in the sensory neurone [1].
**(d)** **Na⁺ diffuse into the chemoreceptor cell** through sodium ion channels [1]; the cell **depolarises** [1]; **voltage-gated calcium ion channels open** and Ca²⁺ diffuse in [1]; Ca²⁺ cause **vesicles to release neurotransmitter** onto the sensory neurone [1]; the neurone depolarises and, if **threshold** is reached, an **action potential** passes to the brain [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each step in (d) is a mark; skipping the Ca²⁺ step loses it.

**3. (a)** 30 − (−65) = **95 mV** [1].
**(b)** **Na⁺–K⁺ pumps** use ATP to move **3 Na⁺ out per 2 K⁺ in** [1]; the membrane is **more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺**, so K⁺ diffuse out faster than Na⁺ leak in [1]; **large negatively charged proteins** stay inside [1].
**(c)** **Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open** and Na⁺ diffuse in: **depolarisation** [1]; past threshold, **more Na⁺ channels open** and the inside becomes **positive**, up to +30 mV [1]; Na⁺ channels close and **voltage-gated K⁺ channels open**, so K⁺ diffuse out: **repolarisation** [1]; K⁺ channels close slowly, so too many K⁺ leave and the membrane is **hyperpolarised** to −80 mV [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the channel type and the ion's direction; in (a), 30 − 65 = −35 scores nothing.

**4. (a)** 1.6 ms = 0.0016 s; frequency = 1 ÷ 0.0016 [1] = **625 impulses per second (625 Hz)** [1].
**(b)** K⁺ channels close and the **Na⁺–K⁺ pump** restores the resting distribution of ions [1]; meanwhile the **Na⁺ channels cannot open**, so a new action potential cannot start [1]; this sets a **minimum time between impulses**, which limits the **maximum frequency** and keeps action potentials **separate** [1].
*Examiner insight:* 1 ÷ 1.6 = 0.625 (ms not converted) loses the accuracy mark; the method mark can stand.

**5. (a)** 1.2 mm = 0.0012 m; time = 0.0012 ÷ 60 [1] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ s = **0.020 ms** [1].
**(b)** The membrane is **no longer insulated** between nodes, so ions leak across it [1]; **local circuits cannot reach the next node** with enough current to bring it to threshold [1]; **saltatory conduction is lost**, so impulses are **slower or fail** altogether [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Impulses go slower" alone earns one mark at most.

**6. (a)** A **presynaptic knob** with **many mitochondria** and **vesicles of acetylcholine** [1]; **voltage-gated calcium ion channels** in the presynaptic membrane [1]; a **synaptic cleft** between the membranes [1]; **acetylcholine receptors (ligand-gated Na⁺ channels)** on the **postsynaptic membrane** [1]. Acetylcholinesterase in the cleft can replace any one point.
**(b)** Ca²⁺ **cannot enter the presynaptic knob** when an action potential arrives [1]; **vesicles do not fuse** with the presynaptic membrane, so **no acetylcholine is released** [1]; no ACh binds to postsynaptic receptors, Na⁺ channels stay closed and **threshold is not reached** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Always say presynaptic or postsynaptic; "channels in the membrane" is too vague for credit.

**7.** **Acetylcholine is released** at the **neuromuscular junction** and crosses the cleft [1]; it binds to receptors and **depolarises the sarcolemma** [1]; the depolarisation **spreads along the sarcolemma** [1] and **down the T-tubules** into the fibre [1]; the **sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca²⁺** into the sarcoplasm [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write "sarcolemma" and "sarcoplasmic reticulum", not "cell membrane" and "endoplasmic reticulum".

**8. (a)** A sarcomere runs **from one Z line to the next** [1]; the **A band is dark**, the length of the thick (myosin) filaments [1]; the **I band is light**, either side of a Z line, with thin (actin) filaments only [1].
**(b)** The sarcomere shortens by 2.6 − 2.2 = 0.4 µm, and the H zone by the same amount [1]; 0.6 − 0.4 = **0.2 µm** [1].
**(c)** Half the I band = (2.6 − 1.6) ÷ 2 = 0.5 µm; overlap each side = (1.6 − 0.6) ÷ 2 = 0.5 µm [1]; thin filament = **1.0 µm** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each subtraction so a method mark survives an error; give units.

**9. (a)** At rest **tropomyosin covers the myosin-binding sites** on actin [1]; **Ca²⁺ bind to troponin**, which changes shape and **moves tropomyosin** aside [1]; **myosin heads bind to actin**, forming **cross-bridges** [1]; the heads tilt (**power stroke**), releasing ADP and Pi and **pulling actin towards the M line** [1]; **ATP binds** to the myosin head, which **detaches** [1]; the head **hydrolyses ATP** and returns upright, ready to bind again [1].
**(b)** Myosin heads **cannot detach** from actin [1], so **cross-bridges stay attached** [1].
*Examiner insight:* ATP binding and ATP hydrolysis earn separate marks; "ATP provides energy" earns neither.

**10. (a)** Bending a hair opens **ion channels** in its cells, giving a **receptor potential** [1]; a **second stimulation within a short time** is needed [1]; then an **action potential spreads across the lobes** [1].
**(b)** Cells in the **outer layers of the lobes and midrib pump H⁺ into their cell walls**, loosening them [1]; **water enters by osmosis** and these cells expand rapidly [1]; the lobes **flip from convex to concave**, shutting the trap [1].
*Examiner insight:* Repeating the action potential in (b) earns nothing.

**11. (a)** Auxin binds to **receptors** in cells of the elongation zone [1]; it stimulates **proton pumps** to **pump H⁺ into the cell wall**, lowering its pH [1]; at low pH **expansins loosen the links between cellulose microfibrils** [1]; water enters by **osmosis** and **turgor stretches the loosened wall**, so the cell elongates [1].
**(b)** The seed **absorbs water** and the **embryo releases gibberellin** [1]; gibberellin reaches the **aleurone layer** [1]; it causes **breakdown of DELLA proteins**, which normally inhibit a factor that promotes transcription [1]; the **amylase gene is transcribed** and amylase is **secreted into the endosperm** [1]; amylase **hydrolyses starch to maltose**, used by the embryo for **respiration and growth** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Auxin makes cells divide" scores nothing; in (b), gibberellin does not "activate amylase" directly.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the inside of the axon becomes "negative" during an action potential, instead of positive at the peak.
- Leaving out voltage-gated calcium ion channels in the taste-bud cell or the presynaptic knob.
- Saying the A band or the filaments shorten.
- Giving ATP only an "energy" role instead of detachment and re-cocking.
