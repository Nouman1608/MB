## Questions

**1.** A sequence has nth term x(n) = 3n² − 2. (a) Write down the first three terms. (b) Show that 145 is a term of the sequence and state which term it is. **[3]**

**2.** A sequence is defined by x(n+1) = 1/(1 − x(n)), with x(1) = 3. (a) Find x(2), x(3) and x(4). (b) Hence write down x(100), giving a reason. **[4]**

**3.** A sequence is defined by x(n+1) = (x(n)² + 6)/5, with x(1) = 0. (a) Find x(2) and x(3) as decimals. (b) Suppose the sequence converges to a limit L. Show that L satisfies L² − 5L + 6 = 0 and find the possible values of L. **[5]**

**4.** An arithmetic sequence has 4th term 19 and the sum of its first 12 terms is 348. (a) Find the first term and the common difference. **[4]** (b) Find the smallest term of the sequence that exceeds 200. **[2]**

**5.** (a) Evaluate Σ from r = 1 to 40 of (5 − 2r). **[2]** (b) Use the formula for the sum of the first n natural numbers to show that Σ from r = 1 to n of (2r − 1) = n². **[3]**

**6.** A geometric sequence has 3rd term 18 and 6th term 486. (a) Find the common ratio and the first term. **[3]** (b) Find the sum of the first 9 terms. **[2]** (c) Explain why the series does not have a sum to infinity. **[1]**

**7.** Maya pays £800 into a savings account at the start of each year for 10 years. The account grows by 4% each year, and nothing is withdrawn. (a) Show that the value at the end of the 10th year of the first payment is 800 × 1.04¹⁰. **[1]** (b) Show that the total value at the end of the 10th year is 800 × (1.04 + 1.04² + ... + 1.04¹⁰), and hence find it to the nearest pound. **[4]**

**8.** A geometric series has first term 20 and sum to infinity 50. (a) Find the common ratio. **[2]** (b) Find the sum of all the terms from the 4th term onwards. **[3]**

**9.** By writing it as a geometric series, show that 0.2777... (with the 7 recurring) equals 5/18. **[4]**

**10.** A geometric series has first term 4 and common ratio (3x − 1)/2. (a) Find the set of values of x for which the series converges. **[3]** (b) Find the value of x for which the sum to infinity is 8. **[2]**

**11.** (a) Evaluate 8C3. **[1]** (b) Expand (3 + 2x)⁴, simplifying each coefficient. **[4]** (c) Hence find the coefficient of x² in the expansion of (2 + x)(3 + 2x)⁴. **[2]**

**12.** The coefficient of x³ in the expansion of (1 + kx)⁷ is 5 times the coefficient of x², where k is a non-zero constant. (a) Find k. **[4]** (b) Use the first three terms of the expansion of (1 + x)¹⁰, with x = 0.01, to estimate 1.01¹⁰. Given that 1.01¹⁰ = 1.104622 to 6 decimal places, find the error in your estimate. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) x(1) = 3 − 2 = 1, x(2) = 12 − 2 = 10, x(3) = 27 − 2 = 25. [1]
(b) 3n² − 2 = 145, so 3n² = 147 and n² = 49. [1] n = 7 (n = −7 is rejected because n is positive), so 145 is the **7th term**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" part, set the formula equal to 145 and solve, then comment that n is a positive whole number. Checking only that 3 × 49 − 2 = 145 leaves the position of the term unexplained.

**2.** (a) x(2) = 1/(1 − 3) = −1/2. [1] x(3) = 1/(1 + 1/2) = 2/3. [1] x(4) = 1/(1 − 2/3) = 3. [1]
(b) x(4) = x(1), so the sequence repeats every 3 terms. 100 = 3 × 33 + 1, so x(100) = x(1) = **3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep fractions exact rather than using decimals, so that x(4) visibly returns to 3. A bare answer of 3 with no mention of the period is unlikely to earn the reason.

**3.** (a) x(2) = (0 + 6)/5 = **1.2**. [1] x(3) = (1.44 + 6)/5 = **1.488**. [1]
(b) At the limit, x(n+1) and x(n) both equal L, so L = (L² + 6)/5. [1] Then 5L = L² + 6, so L² − 5L + 6 = 0. [1] (L − 2)(L − 3) = 0, so **L = 2 or L = 3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the step where you replace both terms by L before rearranging. Give both roots unless the question tells you to choose one, because the equation alone does not decide which limit the sequence reaches.

**4.** (a) x(4) = a + 3d = 19. [1] S(12) = 6(2a + 11d) = 348, so 2a + 11d = 58. [1] Substituting a = 19 − 3d: 38 − 6d + 11d = 58, so d = 4. [1] Then **a = 7, d = 4**. [1]
(b) 7 + 4(n − 1) > 200 gives n > 49.25, so n = 50. [1] The term is 7 + 4 × 49 = **203**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two unknowns need two equations, so write both clearly before eliminating. In part (b), test the whole number either side: the 49th term is 199, which does not exceed 200.

**5.** (a) The terms form an arithmetic series with first term 3, last term −75 and 40 terms. [1] S = 40/2 × (3 + (−75)) = 20 × (−72) = **−1440**. [1]
(b) Σ(2r − 1) = 2Σr − Σ1 = 2 × n(n + 1)/2 − n. [1] = n(n + 1) − n = n² + n − n. [1] = **n²**, as required. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The last term of part (a) is 5 − 80 = −75, and many errors come from sign slips here. For "show that", every line of the algebra should be visible, ending on the stated result.

**6.** (a) ar² = 18 and ar⁵ = 486. Dividing gives r³ = 27. [1] So **r = 3** (the only real cube root). [1] a = 18/9 = **2**. [1]
(b) S(9) = 2(3⁹ − 1)/(3 − 1) = 3⁹ − 1. [1] = 19 683 − 1 = **19 682**. [1]
(c) The common ratio 3 does not satisfy |r| < 1, so the terms grow and the series does not converge. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing the two terms removes a and is much faster than guessing. State the condition |r| < 1 explicitly in part (c) and say that r = 3 breaks it.

**7.** (a) The payment is multiplied by 1.04 once for each of the 10 years, giving **800 × 1.04¹⁰**. [1]
(b) The first payment grows for 10 years, the second for 9, and so on, down to the last payment, which grows for 1 year. [1] The total is 800(1.04¹⁰ + 1.04⁹ + ... + 1.04), which is the same sum written in reverse order. [1] This is geometric with first term 1.04 and ratio 1.04, so the total is 800 × 1.04(1.04¹⁰ − 1)/(1.04 − 1). [1] This equals 800 × 1.04 × 12.0061... = 9989.08, so **£9989**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say how many years each payment grows, because that justifies the first term 1.04 rather than 1. Round only at the end: rounding 1.04¹⁰ early can shift the final pound.

**8.** (a) 20/(1 − r) = 50, so 1 − r = 0.4. [1] **r = 0.6**. [1]
(b) The 4th term is 20 × 0.6³ = 4.32. [1] The sum from the 4th term onwards has first term 4.32 and ratio 0.6, so it equals 4.32/(1 − 0.6). [1] = **10.8**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A second method is S∞ minus S(3) = 50 − 39.2 = 10.8. Either route is fine, but you need to state which terms you are summing. Check that 0.6 satisfies |r| < 1 before using the formula.

**9.** 0.2777... = 0.2 + 0.07 + 0.007 + 0.0007 + ... [1] The terms after 0.2 form a geometric series with first term 0.07 and ratio 0.1. [1] Its sum to infinity is 0.07/(1 − 0.1) = 0.07/0.9 = 7/90. [1] Total: 2/10 + 7/90 = 18/90 + 7/90 = 25/90 = **5/18**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Separate the non-repeating part (0.2) before forming the series. Starting the geometric series at 0.27 would give the wrong first term.

**10.** (a) Convergence needs |(3x − 1)/2| < 1, so |3x − 1| < 2. [1] −2 < 3x − 1 < 2. [1] −1 < 3x < 3, so **−1/3 < x < 1**. [1]
(b) 4/(1 − r) = 8, so 1 − r = 1/2 and r = 1/2. [1] (3x − 1)/2 = 1/2 gives 3x − 1 = 1, so **x = 2/3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check that the x from part (b) lies in the interval from part (a). Here 2/3 does, which confirms the series really converges. A value outside the interval would be rejected.

**11.** (a) 8C3 = 8!/(3! 5!) = 8 × 7 × 6/6 = **56**. [1]
(b) Coefficients 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 with a = 3 and b = 2x. [1] Terms: 81, 4 × 27 × 2x = 216x, 6 × 9 × 4x² = 216x², 4 × 3 × 8x³ = 96x³, 16x⁴. [2] So (3 + 2x)⁴ = **81 + 216x + 216x² + 96x³ + 16x⁴**. [1]
(c) The x² terms come from 2 × 216x² and x × 216x. [1] 432 + 216 = **648**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Numerical coefficients such as 2⁴ and 3² must be applied to every term. For part (c), list the two ways of reaching x² before adding, so a slip is easy to trace.

**12.** (a) The x² coefficient is 7C2 k² = 21k². [1] The x³ coefficient is 7C3 k³ = 35k³. [1] So 35k³ = 5 × 21k², giving 35k³ − 105k² = 0, i.e. 35k²(k − 3) = 0. [1] Since k is non-zero, **k = 3**. [1]
(b) Estimate: 1 + 10(0.01) + 45(0.01)² = 1 + 0.1 + 0.0045 = **1.1045**. [1] Error = 1.104622 − 1.1045 = **0.000122** (3 s.f.), so the estimate is slightly low. [2]
*Examiner insight:* Do not divide through by k² without noting k is non-zero, as this is the condition that rules out the root k = 0. In part (b), state that the estimate is smaller than the true value.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using n instead of n − 1 as the power of r in the nth term of a geometric sequence.
- Not rejecting a root that cannot be a term number or limit, such as a negative n.
- Stating a sum to infinity without checking |r| < 1.
- Rounding n down in "least n" questions.
- Sign slips with negative terms, especially in (−3x)² and 5 − 2r.
- Forgetting to apply numerical coefficients to every term of a binomial expansion.
- Leaving out a way of reaching a power when expanding a product of brackets.
- Giving only one limit when L = f(L) has two roots and the question does not choose between them.
