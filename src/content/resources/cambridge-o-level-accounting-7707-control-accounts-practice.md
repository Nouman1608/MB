## Answers

**1.** Checks the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger [1]. Locates errors to one ledger [1]. Also accept: gives total trade receivables or payables quickly; helps prevent and detect fraud; supports credit control. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Purchases returns journal [1]

**(b)** General journal [1]

**(c)** Cash book [1]

**(d)** Purchases journal [1] **[4]**

**3.** **(a)** Credit [1]

**(b)** Debit [1]

**(c)** Debit [1]

**(d)** Credit [1] **[4]**

**4.** A customer pays more than the amount owed, or returns goods after paying for them [1], so the business owes the customer money and the customer's account has a credit balance [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The other business is both a customer and a supplier [1]. Setting the amounts off against each other means only the net amount needs to be paid, which saves making two payments [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Each transaction is posted once and the software updates the individual account and the control total together [1], so the control account is produced automatically and arithmetical errors in totalling are unlikely [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** The control account is produced automatically from the same postings that update customers' and suppliers' accounts [1], so time is saved and arithmetical errors in totals become rare [1]. Totals of trade receivables and trade payables, and lists of overdue accounts, are available at any time [1], which helps Mariam to chase late payers and plan payments to suppliers [1]. Also accept: input errors are not revealed by the control account, because both records change together.

**(b)** Disagree [1]. The software cannot detect an invoice entered with the wrong amount or a receipt posted to the wrong customer, so totals and overdue lists should still be reviewed by someone other than the person who enters the data [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including cash sales or cash purchases.
- Putting dishonoured cheques, refunds or contra entries on the wrong side.
- Netting opening debit and credit balances.
- Naming a vague source such as "the journal" instead of "general journal" or "sales returns journal".
- Forgetting to bring the balances down at the start of the next period.
