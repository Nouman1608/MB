These notes condense section 10.2, Fertilisers, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Both outcomes are required for every candidate, and questions usually link them to formulae, calculations, salts and ion tests. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [fertilisers study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-fertilisers/); then try the [fertilisers practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-fertilisers-practice/).

## The two outcomes

1. **Ammonium salts and nitrates** are used as fertilisers.
2. **NPK fertilisers** provide nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium for improved plant growth.

## Core facts

- Plants need N, P and K in large amounts; harvesting removes them from the soil.
- Plants absorb these elements as dissolved ions through their roots.
- Plants cannot use N₂ gas from the air directly.
- All ammonium salts and all nitrates are soluble (section 7.3 solubility rules), so their ions reach roots in soil water.
- Solubility also means excess fertiliser is washed into rivers, where nitrates and phosphates lead to deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life (section 10.1).

## Common fertiliser compounds

| Name | Formula | Mr | Supplies | % N by mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonium nitrate | NH₄NO₃ | 80 | N | 35.0 |
| Ammonium sulfate | (NH₄)₂SO₄ | 132 | N | 21.2 |
| Ammonium chloride | NH₄Cl | 53.5 | N | 26.2 |
| Ammonium phosphate | (NH₄)₃PO₄ | 149 | N, P | 28.2 |
| Potassium nitrate | KNO₃ | 101 | K, N | 13.9 |

Ar values used: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, P = 31, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39.

## Percentage composition method

```
% element = (number of atoms × Ar) ÷ Mr × 100
```

1. Write the formula; expand brackets (NH₄)₂ means 2 N and 8 H.
2. Add up Mr.
3. Multiply Ar of the element by the number of its atoms.
4. Divide by Mr and multiply by 100.

Reverse use: mass of fertiliser needed = mass of N wanted × Mr ÷ (mass of N in one formula unit).

Quick example: potassium nitrate, KNO₃. Mr = 39 + 14 + (3 × 16) = 101. % K = 39 ÷ 101 × 100 = 38.6%; % N = 14 ÷ 101 × 100 = 13.9%. A 50 kg sack therefore supplies about 19.3 kg of potassium and 6.9 kg of nitrogen. Comparing compounds by percentage, not by Mr, tells you which supplies more of an element per kilogram.

## Making ammonium salts

| Acid | Equation | Salt |
|---|---|---|
| Nitric acid | NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ | ammonium nitrate |
| Sulfuric acid | 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ | ammonium sulfate |
| Hydrochloric acid | NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl | ammonium chloride |

- Ammonia is made by the Haber process: N₂ from air, H₂ from methane.
- A soluble salt is prepared by titration, then crystallised (section 7.3).

## Ammonium salts with bases

```
ammonium salt + base → salt + water + ammonia
```

- A base such as calcium hydroxide releases ammonia gas from an ammonium fertiliser, so nitrogen is lost to the air.
- Same reaction is the test for NH₄⁺: warm with NaOH(aq); ammonia turns damp red litmus blue.

## Identifying ions in a fertiliser

| Ion | Test | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ | warm with NaOH(aq) | ammonia; damp red litmus turns blue |
| NO₃⁻ | warm with NaOH(aq) and aluminium foil | ammonia; damp red litmus turns blue |
| K⁺ | flame test | lilac flame |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fertiliser | substance added to soil to supply elements needed for plant growth |
| NPK fertiliser | mixture of compounds supplying nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium |
| Ammonium salt | salt containing the NH₄⁺ ion, made from ammonia and an acid |
| Nitrate | salt containing the NO₃⁻ ion |
| Percentage composition by mass | the percentage of a compound's mass that comes from one element |
| Neutralisation | reaction between an acid and a base |

## Exam traps

- **Ammonia** (NH₃, gas) is not **ammonium** (NH₄⁺, ion).
- NH₄NO₃ contains **two** N atoms: one in each ion.
- (NH₄)₂SO₄: the 2 multiplies N **and** H, giving 2 N and 8 H.
- P is phosphorus; K is potassium. "Phosphate" supplies P, not K.
- Ammonium salts release ammonia with **bases**, not with acids.
- Do not describe eutrophication stages: the syllabus wants only "deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life".
- Round percentages only at the end; give 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Self-test

1. Name the **two** types of compound used as nitrogen fertilisers.
2. State the three elements supplied by an NPK fertiliser.
3. Why can plants not use the nitrogen in the air directly?
4. Which element(s) does ammonium phosphate supply?
5. Calculate the Mr of ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄.
6. Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl.
7. Write the balanced equation for making ammonium nitrate from ammonia.
8. Why should lime not be added to soil at the same time as ammonium sulfate?
9. Describe the test for the ammonium ion.
10. A fertiliser gives a lilac flame. Which element does it supply?
11. Why do fertilisers end up in rivers?
12. How many kilograms of nitrogen are in 40 kg of ammonium nitrate?

## Answers

1. Ammonium salts and nitrates.
2. Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.
3. N₂ gas is unreactive and is not absorbed by plants; nitrogen must be in soluble compounds such as nitrates or ammonium salts.
4. Nitrogen and phosphorus.
5. 2 × (14 + 4) + 32 + (4 × 16) = 132.
6. Mr = 14 + 4 + 35.5 = 53.5; % N = 14 ÷ 53.5 × 100 = 26.2%.
7. NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃.
8. Lime is a base; it reacts with the ammonium salt to release ammonia gas, so nitrogen is lost from the soil.
9. Warm with aqueous sodium hydroxide; ammonia is given off, which turns damp red litmus paper blue.
10. Potassium.
11. They are soluble, so rain dissolves excess fertiliser and washes it off fields.
12. 35% of 40 kg = 14 kg (or 40 × 28 ÷ 80 = 14 kg).
