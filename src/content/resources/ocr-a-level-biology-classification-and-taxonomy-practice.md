## Questions

**1.** The taxonomic hierarchy runs: domain, kingdom, ____, class, ____, family, ____, species. Write the three missing ranks in order. **[3]**

**2.** Pedunculate oak is *Quercus robur* and sessile oak is *Quercus petraea*.

**(a)** State two conventions for writing the binomial name of pedunculate oak correctly in a printed report. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the two names tell you about the relationship between the two oaks. **[1]**

**3.** In Britain, "buzzard" refers to *Buteo buteo*, but in North America the word is often used for vultures. Explain why scientists prefer the name *Buteo buteo*. **[3]**

**4.** The table shows part of the classification of four insects. All four are in the domain Eukarya, kingdom Animalia, phylum Arthropoda and class Insecta.

| | Honey bee | Bumblebee | Common wasp | Seven-spot ladybird |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Order | Hymenoptera | Hymenoptera | Hymenoptera | Coleoptera |
| Family | Apidae | Apidae | Vespidae | Coccinellidae |
| Genus | *Apis* | *Bombus* | *Vespula* | *Coccinella* |

**(a)** Name the lowest taxon shared by the honey bee and the bumblebee. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the lowest taxon shared by the honey bee and the seven-spot ladybird. **[1]**
**(c)** The common wasp shares a lower taxon with the honey bee than the ladybird does. State what this suggests about the common ancestry of the three insects. **[2]**

**5.** Two organisms were collected and examined.

- Organism J is multicellular. It consists of branching threads (hyphae) with chitin in the walls. It grows on rotting wood, secretes enzymes onto it and absorbs the products. It has no chloroplasts.
- Organism K consists of single cells about 2 µm across. It has no nucleus, its DNA is circular and lies free in the cytoplasm, and it has a peptidoglycan wall.

**(a)** Name the kingdom of organism J and give two features from the description that support your answer. **[3]**
**(b)** Name the kingdom of organism K and give one feature that supports your answer. **[2]**

**6.** Compare the kingdoms Animalia and Fungi. Give two similarities and two differences. **[4]**

**7.** Explain how molecular evidence led to a new classification system with three domains. **[4]**

**8.** The table describes three organisms.

| | Organism X | Organism Y | Organism Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Absent | Absent | Present |
| Cell wall | No peptidoglycan | Peptidoglycan | Cellulose |
| Membrane lipids | Ether-linked, branched | Ester-linked | Ester-linked |
| DNA and histones | Circular DNA with histones | Circular DNA, no histones | Linear DNA with histones |

**(a)** Name the domain of each organism. **[3]**
**(b)** State two features that show organism X is not in the same domain as organism Y. **[2]**

**9.** Compare the five-kingdom and three-domain classification systems. **[4]**

**10.** A protein of 180 amino acids was sequenced in four species, A, B, C and D. The table shows the number of amino acid differences between each pair.

| Pair | Differences |
|---|---|
| A and B | 9 |
| A and C | 27 |
| A and D | 54 |
| B and C | 26 |
| B and D | 55 |
| C and D | 52 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of amino acids that are identical in A and C. **[2]**
**(b)** State which two species are most closely related and explain your choice. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the phylogenetic tree that the data support. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** Define phylogeny. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the taxonomic hierarchy reflects phylogeny. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why comparing DNA sequences can give a more reliable picture of relationships than comparing appearance. **[2]**

**12.** A 400-base section of a gene was compared in four species of a plant group, W, X, Y and Z. The numbers of base differences are shown.

| Pair | Differences |
|---|---|
| W and X | 8 |
| Y and Z | 12 |
| W and Y | 60 |
| W and Z | 62 |
| X and Y | 58 |
| X and Z | 64 |

An older classification, based on observable features, placed X and Y in one genus and W and Z in another.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of bases that differ between X and Y. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the arrangement of the four species on a phylogenetic tree based on these data. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the data support the older classification. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Phylum [1], order [1], genus [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Ranks must be in the correct positions; giving all three names in the wrong order does not show you know the hierarchy.

**2. (a)** Genus name starts with a capital letter and species name with a lower-case letter, giving *Quercus robur* [1]. The name is in italics (or underlined if handwritten) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** They share the same genus, *Quercus*, so they are relatively closely related (both are oaks) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) asks for two separate conventions, so give capitalisation and italics or underlining as two distinct points rather than merging them.

**3.** The same common name can refer to different organisms in different regions or countries [1]. One species may have several common names, in different places or languages [1]. The binomial name is universal: each species has one name used by scientists worldwide, which avoids confusion [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that says only "Latin is the scientific language" does not give a reason; the marks come from saying the name is the same everywhere and refers to one species.

**4. (a)** Family Apidae [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Class Insecta [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The wasp and honey bee share the same order, Hymenoptera, while the ladybird is in a different order [1]. This suggests that the wasp and honey bee share a more recent common ancestor than either does with the ladybird [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quoting the lowest taxon shared is not enough in an explain or suggest question; you need to link it to how recently the ancestor lived.

**5. (a)** Fungi [1]. Any two from: chitin in the walls; hyphae; absorbs nutrients after secreting enzymes (extracellular digestion); no chloroplasts [2]. **[3]**
**(b)** Prokaryotae [1]. Any one from: no nucleus; DNA circular and free in cytoplasm; peptidoglycan wall; very small cells [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The features must come from the description of the organism; a general statement about the kingdom that the description does not support is weaker evidence.

**6.** Similarities, any two: both are eukaryotic with a nucleus; both are heterotrophic; neither has chloroplasts; both store glycogen [2]. Differences, any two: fungi have cell walls of chitin, animals have no cell wall; fungi absorb nutrients after external digestion, animals ingest food and digest it internally; fungi have hyphae or are single cells, animals are multicellular with tissues and organs [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both organisms covered for each difference; writing only "fungi have chitin walls" without saying what animals have gains less.

**7.** Scientists compared the base sequences of ribosomal RNA (and other molecular evidence) from different organisms [1]. The prokaryotes fell into two distinct groups, Bacteria and Archaea [1], that differed from each other as much as each does from eukaryotes [1]. This showed that the old kingdom Prokaryotae did not reflect relationships, so a rank above kingdom was introduced with three domains: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks how the evidence led to the change, so state what was compared, what was found and what classification followed, not just the names of the domains.

**8. (a)** X: Archaea [1]. Y: Bacteria [1]. Z: Eukarya [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** X has no peptidoglycan whereas Y has peptidoglycan [1]. X has histones with its DNA whereas Y has no histones; or X has ether-linked branched membrane lipids whereas Y has ester-linked lipids [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Absence of a nucleus does not separate X from Y, because both lack one; choose a feature from the table where the two organisms differ.

**9.** Any four from: domain is a higher rank than kingdom [1]; the kingdom system puts all prokaryotes in one kingdom, Prokaryotae, whereas the domain system splits them into Bacteria and Archaea [1]; the four eukaryotic kingdoms are all placed within the single domain Eukarya [1]; the kingdoms were based mainly on observable features, while the domains were based on molecular evidence such as rRNA sequences [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparison answers should point to a difference in each system, not describe the two separately, and the domains do not replace the eukaryotic kingdoms.

**10. (a)** 180 − 27 = 153 identical [1]; 153 ÷ 180 × 100 = **85.0 %** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A and B [1], because they have the fewest differences (9), so they share the most recent common ancestor [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** A and B join at the most recent branch point [1]. C joins next, branching from the line leading to A and B [1]. D branches off first and is the least closely related to the others (it differs from all three by about 30 %) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction before the percentage, and in part (c) describe the order of branching rather than only naming the closest pair.

**11. (a)** Phylogeny is the evolutionary history of a group of organisms, showing how species are related by descent from common ancestors [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Groups are nested because organisms are related by branching descent from common ancestors [1]. Organisms in the same low-rank taxon, such as a genus, share a more recent common ancestor than organisms that are only in the same higher-rank taxon [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Sequences can be compared objectively and counted [1]. Appearance can be similar in distantly related species because they live in similar ways, whereas sequence differences build up over time since the common ancestor [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "More accurate" needs a reason; say what is wrong with appearance as evidence, not just that DNA is better.

**12. (a)** 58 ÷ 400 × 100 = **14.5 %** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** W and X are the closest pair and join at one branch point [1]. Y and Z form a second pair at another branch point [1]. The two pairs join at an older common ancestor [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** X and Y are 14.5 % different, much more than X and W (2.0 %) or Y and Z (3.0 %) [1]. So the data do not support placing X and Y together in a genus [1]. X is more closely related to W, and Y to Z, so the older classification may not reflect phylogeny and may need revising [1]. This is only one gene section, so more genes or other evidence should be compared before changing it, and the similar appearance of X and Y may not come from recent common ancestry [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation gains credit for using figures from the table and for a limitation of the evidence, not only for a conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up the order of the ranks, or leaving out domain.
- Giving both genus and species a capital letter, or forgetting to italicise or underline.
- Giving "Latin" as the advantage of the binomial system instead of "universal".
- Calling Archaea bacteria, or saying domains replace the eukaryotic kingdoms.
- Forgetting to subtract the differences from the total when asked for percentage identity.
- Naming the closest pair without saying that fewer differences mean a more recent common ancestor.
