**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
34.1: four production routes, amide formation with acyl chlorides, and "the basicity of aqueous
solutions of amines".

## Classification

- **Primary** RNH₂: one carbon group on N (ethylamine, CH₃CH₂NH₂).
- **Secondary** R₂NH: two carbon groups on N (diethylamine, (CH₃CH₂)₂NH).
- **Tertiary** R₃N: three carbon groups on N, no N–H (trimethylamine).
- Count groups on **nitrogen**, not on the carbon.

## The four routes

| Route | Reagents and conditions | Example | Product class |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | halogenoalkane + NH₃ in ethanol, heat under pressure | CH₃CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br | primary |
| (b) | halogenoalkane + primary amine in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube / under pressure | CH₃CH₂Br + CH₃CH₂NH₂ → (CH₃CH₂)₂NH + HBr | secondary |
| (c) | amide + LiAlH₄ | CH₃CONHCH₃ + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂NHCH₃ + H₂O | same class as the N substituents allow |
| (d) | nitrile + LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni | CH₃CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂NH₂ | primary, one carbon more than the halogenoalkane used to make the nitrile |

## Controlling substitution in routes (a) and (b)

- The amine formed is itself a nucleophile, often a better one than NH₃.
- Further substitution gives secondary, tertiary amines and a quaternary ammonium salt, R₄N⁺X⁻.
- **Excess NH₃** favours the primary amine; **excess primary amine** favours the secondary amine.
- Routes (c) and (d) give a **single product**.

## Route (c) in detail

- LiAlH₄ changes **C=O → CH₂**; nothing on N changes.
- RCONH₂ → RCH₂NH₂ (primary). RCONHR′ → RCH₂NHR′ (secondary).
- Two-step pure secondary amine: acyl chloride + primary amine → N-substituted amide → LiAlH₄.

## Route (d) and chain length

- Halogenoalkane + KCN in ethanol, heat → nitrile (AS 19.2): **adds one carbon**.
- Bromoethane → propanenitrile → **propylamine**, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
- Bromoethane + NH₃ → **ethylamine** (no extra carbon).

## Amides from acyl chlorides (outcome 2)

    RCOCl + NH₃   → RCONH₂  + HCl
    RCOCl + R′NH₂ → RCONHR′ + HCl
    RCOCl + R′₂NH → RCONR′₂ + HCl

- **Room temperature**; called a **condensation** (HCl lost).
- Excess amine: HCl → R′NH₃⁺Cl⁻.

## Basicity of aqueous amines (outcome 3)

    RNH₂ + H₂O ⇌ RNH₃⁺ + OH⁻

- Lone pair on N accepts H⁺, so amines are **Brønsted–Lowry bases**.
- Weak bases: equilibrium lies to the left, but the solution is **alkaline** (pH > 7, red litmus
  turns blue).
- Salt formation: RNH₂ + HCl → RNH₃⁺Cl⁻.
- **Order in water:** diethylamine > ethylamine > ammonia.
- Reason: alkyl groups are **electron-donating** (positive inductive effect), so electron density on
  N rises and the lone pair is **more available**; the alkylammonium ion is also stabilised.

## Nucleophile or base? One lone pair, two jobs

| The amine's lone pair attacks... | Role | Example | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| H⁺ (from water or an acid) | Brønsted–Lowry base | CH₃NH₂ + HCl | CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻ |
| δ+ carbon of a halogenoalkane | nucleophile (substitution) | CH₃NH₂ + CH₃CH₂Br | CH₃NHCH₂CH₃ + HBr |
| δ+ carbonyl carbon of an acyl chloride | nucleophile (addition–elimination) | CH₃NH₂ + CH₃COCl | CH₃CONHCH₃ + HCl |

Whenever HCl or HBr is formed alongside an amine, some amine is used up as a base, which is why
these reactions are often run with the amine in excess.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| primary amine | nitrogen bonded to one alkyl or aryl group, RNH₂ |
| secondary amine | nitrogen bonded to two carbon groups, R₂NH |
| quaternary ammonium salt | R₄N⁺X⁻, formed by complete substitution |
| nucleophilic substitution | lone pair attacks δ+ carbon, halide leaves |
| reduction | gain of hydrogen, here by LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni |
| Brønsted–Lowry base | proton acceptor |
| inductive effect | electron donation or withdrawal through σ bonds |
| condensation | two molecules join and a small molecule is lost |

## Exam traps

- Routes (a) and (b) need **ethanol** as solvent and **pressure / a sealed tube**.
- LiAlH₄ is the only syllabus reagent for **amides**; **nitriles** can use LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni.
- The nitrile route **adds a carbon**; check the carbon count of the target.
- (CH₃)₂CHNH₂ is a **primary** amine.
- "More lone pairs" is wrong: every amine has **one** lone pair on N; the point is its
  **availability**.
- Amide formation is at **room temperature**; halogenoalkane substitution needs **heat**.

## Self-test

1. Classify CH₃NHCH₂CH₃.
2. Give the reagents and conditions to make ethylamine from bromoethane in one step.
3. Why is excess ammonia used in question 2?
4. Write the equation for 1-chloropropane with ethylamine.
5. Name the amine formed when propanamide is reduced with LiAlH₄.
6. Give the product of reducing N-ethylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃, with LiAlH₄.
7. Give two reagents that reduce ethanenitrile to ethylamine.
8. Give a two-step route from bromomethane to ethylamine.
9. Write the equation for ethylamine with water and state the pH range of the solution.
10. Explain why diethylamine is a stronger base than ammonia.
11. Write the equation for the reaction of methylamine with hydrochloric acid and name the product.
12. Name the product of ethanoyl chloride with diethylamine.

**Answers:**

1. Secondary amine (N-methylethylamine).
2. Excess NH₃ in ethanol, heated under pressure (sealed vessel).
3. To make it more likely that each bromoethane reacts with NH₃ rather than with ethylamine, reducing
   further substitution.
4. CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl + CH₃CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NHCH₂CH₃ + HCl.
5. Propylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
6. Diethylamine, (CH₃CH₂)₂NH.
7. LiAlH₄; H₂ with a Ni catalyst.
8. KCN in ethanol, heat, to give ethanenitrile; then LiAlH₄ (or H₂/Ni).
9. CH₃CH₂NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ + OH⁻; pH above 7.
10. Two electron-donating ethyl groups increase electron density on N, making the lone pair more
    available to accept H⁺ (and stabilising the cation).
11. CH₃NH₂ + HCl → CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻; methylammonium chloride.
12. N,N-diethylethanamide, CH₃CON(CH₂CH₃)₂.
