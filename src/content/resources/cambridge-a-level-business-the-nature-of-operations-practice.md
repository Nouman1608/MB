## Questions

**1.** Hexdale Candles makes scented candles. State which factor of production each item below is: (i) a wax-melting machine, (ii) the founder, who risked her own savings to start the firm, (iii) the plot of land the workshop stands on, (iv) a packing assistant. **[4]**

**2.** Define *added value*. A sofa sells for £480. Bought-in fabric costs £190 and bought-in frames and fittings cost £90. Calculate the added value per sofa. **[3]**

**3.** Hexdale Candles made 9,600 candles in March with 16 employees and 10,350 candles in April with 15 employees. Calculate labour productivity for each month and the percentage change. **[4]**

**4.** Explain the difference between *efficiency* and *effectiveness*, using an example. **[3]**

**5.** A restaurant plans to reduce its food waste and fit low-energy kitchen equipment. Explain **two** impacts of these measures on the restaurant. **[4]**

**6.** Wharfdale Bottlers can fill bottles on a capital intensive line (fixed costs £90,000 a year, variable cost £0.50 a bottle) or with a labour intensive set-up (fixed costs £24,000, variable cost £1.30 a bottle).

**(a)** Calculate the annual output at which total costs are the same. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total cost of each set-up at 100,000 bottles. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend which set-up Wharfdale should use if it expects 100,000 bottles a year, and state one other factor it should consider. **[2]**

**7.** Identify the operations method (job, batch, flow or mass customisation) in each case: (i) a custom-built racing yacht, (ii) a continuous line filling identical drink cans, (iii) a bakery making 500 rolls, then 300 cakes, on the same ovens, (iv) a website where customers choose the colours and engraving on a pen assembled from standard parts. **[4]**

**8.** A small workshop restores antique chairs by hand. Analyse **one** benefit and **one** limitation of its labour intensive operation. **[4]**

**9.** Define *mass customisation*. Explain **one** benefit and **one** limitation of this method for a business. **[5]**

**10.** Dentonby Pottery has 12 craft potters. Each piece is made to order. Commissions bring 45% of sales revenue. The owner plans to move to batches of 200 identical items for retailers. This needs a £45,000 machine and three weeks of retraining. Analyse **two** problems the pottery may face in changing its operations method. **[6]**

**11.** Cragfield Cycles builds bicycles in batches. It makes 400 a month with 30 employees. It is considering a £180,000 flexible assembly line. The line would let customers choose frame size and colour online, and would reduce wasted material. Output is forecast at 520 a month with 22 employees. For two months while the line is installed, output would fall to 280 a month. Eight employees would move to roles in the sales team or leave.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity before and after the change and the percentage change. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Cragfield Cycles should adopt the new line. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) Capital [1]. (ii) Enterprise [1]. (iii) Land [1]. (iv) Labour [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* each part needs the factor named, not a description. Learn the four terms exactly as the syllabus lists them.

**2.** Added value is the selling price minus the cost of bought-in materials and components [1]. Added value = 480 - 190 - 90 [1] = **£200 per sofa** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a definition that says "profit" or "revenue minus all costs" describes something different. Mention bought-in inputs.

**3.** March: 9,600 / 16 = 600 candles per employee [1]. April: 10,350 / 15 = 690 per employee [1]. Change = (690 - 600) / 600 [1] = **+15%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* show the division for each month and use the old figure as the base for percentage change. Include units such as candles per employee.

**4.** Efficiency means producing output with little waste of resources such as time, materials or energy [1]. Effectiveness means achieving what the business set out to do, such as meeting customer needs [1]. For example, a factory can use materials efficiently but be ineffective if it makes goods customers do not want [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* answers that treat the two words as synonyms earn little. The example is what shows you can tell them apart.

**5.** Impact 1: less food waste lowers the cost of ingredients bought, so running costs fall and profit margins can improve [2]. Impact 2: low-energy equipment costs money to buy, so there is an outlay at first, but energy bills fall later, so the restaurant needs to weigh the savings against the cost [2]. (Other valid impacts include an improved image with customers who value sustainability.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* name the measure's effect on the business (costs, image, cash), not just what the measure is. Each impact needs a reason.

**6.** (a) Difference in fixed costs = 90,000 - 24,000 = 66,000 [1]. Difference in variable cost = 1.30 - 0.50 = 0.80 per bottle [1]. Output = 66,000 / 0.80 = **82,500 bottles** [1].
**(b)** Capital intensive: 90,000 + 0.50 x 100,000 = **£140,000** [1]. Labour intensive: 24,000 + 1.30 x 100,000 = **£154,000** [1].
**(c)** Recommend the capital intensive line, because 100,000 is above 82,500 and it is £14,000 cheaper [1]. Another factor: demand may be uncertain, and the line has high fixed costs and less flexibility (or the firm may not have the cash for the outlay) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* show the equation or the subtraction so the method is visible. For part (c), a recommendation tied to the figures is stronger than a general statement.

**7.** (i) Job [1]. (ii) Flow [1]. (iii) Batch [1]. (iv) Mass customisation [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* look for clues in the stem: one-off, continuous, switching between products, and customer choice from standard parts.

**8.** Benefit: the workshop can treat each chair differently, so it offers a personal service and can charge a higher price [1]. Developed: customers with valuable antiques pay for care and skill that machines would not give, which supports revenue [1]. Limitation: output is limited by the number of skilled restorers and the work is slow [1]. Developed: cost per chair is high, and the workshop cannot easily grow without hiring and training more staff [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "analyse" asks for a benefit or limitation developed with its consequence for this workshop. A list of one-word labels would not show that development.

**9.** Mass customisation is producing in volume from standard parts or processes while letting each customer choose a personalised product [1]. Benefit: customers get personal choice, so the firm can charge a higher price than for a standard product [1], while still gaining some of the cost savings of volume production [1]. Limitation: it needs flexible systems and technology, which are costly to install [1], and managing many combinations is complex, so customers may wait longer than for a stocked item [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* a definition that only says "customised" misses the mass-production element. Write both parts.

**10.** Problem 1, cost: the £45,000 machine and three weeks of retraining are an outlay and lost working time [1]. Twelve potters will not be producing for three weeks, so output and revenue fall in the short term, which strains cash [1], and the cost may take time to recover if retail prices per item are low [1]. Problem 2, customers and staff: commissions are 45% of revenue, so moving to batches may lose customers who value unique pieces [1]. Craft potters may dislike repetitive batch work and resist or leave, which hurts morale and skill [1], reducing the firm's brand as a maker of individual pieces [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* use the figures in the stem (45%, £45,000, three weeks, 12 potters). Problems about the method change in general, not about this pottery, are weaker.

**11. (a)** Before: 400 / 30 = 13.3 bicycles per employee [1]. After: 520 / 22 = 23.6 bicycles per employee [1]. Change = (23.6 - 13.3) / 13.3 = **+77%** (rounded) [1].
**(b)** For: productivity rises about 77%, so labour cost per bicycle should fall [1], and output rises 30% from 400 to 520 (520 / 400 = 1.3) [1]. Customer choice of size and colour can raise the price Cragfield can charge, and less wasted material supports sustainability and lower costs [1]. Against: £180,000 outlay is large and may strain cash [1]; output falls by 120 a month for two months, a loss of 240 bicycles in total [1]; eight employees move or leave, which may hurt morale and require redundancy or retraining costs [1]. Judgement: the new line is likely to be worthwhile if demand for personalised bicycles is strong enough to sell 520 a month, as the gain in productivity is large and the disruption lasts only two months. Cragfield should first check demand and its ability to fund the outlay, or phase the change [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* for "evaluate", reasons on both sides are not enough on their own. A final judgement that depends on a condition (demand, cash, timing) and uses the case data tends to carry the most credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining added value as profit or as all revenue.
- Naming a factor of production with no link to the business.
- Using the new figure as the base in a percentage change.
- Treating efficiency and effectiveness as the same idea.
- Giving the benefit of a method with no explanation of what it does to cost, quality or price.
- Saying capital intensive is always cheaper without checking the output level.
- Describing the problems of changing method without using the numbers in the case.
- Stopping an evaluation at a list of pros and cons with no judgement.
