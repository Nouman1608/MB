## Section A

**1.** Name the water cycle transfer in which rain is caught by leaves and branches before it reaches the ground. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** forms of precipitation that the syllabus names as fresh water sources from the atmosphere. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the ocean that lies between Africa and Australia. **[1]**

**4.** Name the stage of water treatment that uses beds of sand and gravel. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why chlorine is added after filtration rather than at the start of water treatment. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** uses of a multipurpose dam, other than hydro-electric power, that can earn money for local people. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The water budget for a rural river catchment over one year is shown. The figures are invented for practice.

| Transfer | mm per year |
|---|---|
| Precipitation | 900 |
| Evaporation and transpiration | 450 |
| Surface run-off | 270 |
| Infiltration that reaches ground water | the remainder |

(a) Calculate the amount of water, in mm, that reaches ground water. **[1]**
(b) Calculate surface run-off as a percentage of precipitation. **[1]**
(c) Half the catchment is later built on. Suggest how the surface run-off and ground water figures would change, and explain why. **[2]**

**8.** A village relies on wells dug into an aquifer. Over ten years the water level in the wells has fallen.
(a) Explain why the water level has fallen. **[2]**
(b) Suggest **two** ways the village could reduce the fall. **[2]**

**9.** The table compares two countries. The figures are invented for practice.

| | Country X | Country Y |
|---|---|---|
| Mean annual rainfall | 1,800 mm | 90 mm |
| Income level | Low | High |
| Population with potable water | 60% | 99% |
| Sewage that is treated | 25% | 95% |

(a) Use the data to explain why a water-poor country can have better access to potable water than a water-rich one. **[3]**
(b) Explain how the low level of sewage treatment in Country X reduces the availability of potable water. **[2]**

**10.** Compare distillation and reverse osmosis as methods of desalination. **[4]**

**11.** An island resort has 3,500 guests and staff. Each person uses 220 litres of water per day. The resort's reverse osmosis plant produces 600 m³ of fresh water per day.
(a) Calculate the resort's daily water demand in m³. Show your working. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the shortfall in m³, and the percentage of demand that the plant meets, to one decimal place. **[2]**
(c) Suggest **two** ways the resort could close the gap without building a second plant. **[2]**

**12.** A country has a large river in its interior and a long, dry coastline where most people live. Discuss whether a multipurpose dam or desalination plants would be the better way to increase its water supply. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Interception **[1]**.

**2.** Rain **[1]**; snow **[1]**.

**3.** Indian Ocean **[1]**.

**4.** Filtration **[1]**.

**5.** Filtration and sedimentation first remove particles that could shelter microorganisms from the chlorine **[1]**; chlorine added last kills the remaining microorganisms and stays in the water to keep it safe in the pipes **[1]**.

**6.** Tourism **[1]**; fish farming **[1]**. Also accept recreation or transport.

**7.** (a) 900 − 450 − 270 = 180 mm **[1]**.
(b) 270 ÷ 900 × 100 = 30% **[1]**.
(c) Surface run-off would increase and ground water would decrease **[1]**, because concrete and tarmac are impermeable, so less water infiltrates and more flows over the surface into drains **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Water is being pumped or drawn from the aquifer faster than it is refilled **[1]**, because recharge depends on infiltration of rainfall, which is slow and may be reduced in dry years **[1]**.
(b) Repair leaks and reduce waste, for example with drip irrigation on farms **[1]**; collect rainwater from roofs so that less is taken from the wells **[1]**. Also accept limits on pumping, or planting vegetation to increase infiltration.

**9.** (a) Country Y has only 90 mm of rain, yet 99% of people have potable water, compared with 60% in Country X, which has 1,800 mm **[1]**. Country Y has a high income, so it can pay for desalination, treatment works and pipes **[1]**. Country X has plenty of water but cannot afford to treat and pipe it to everyone, so access depends on wealth as well as rainfall **[1]**.
(b) Only 25% of sewage is treated, so untreated sewage enters rivers and wells **[1]**; these sources become contaminated with microorganisms and are unsafe to drink without treatment **[1]**.

**10.** Both turn sea water into potable water and leave a salty waste **[1]**. Distillation heats water until it boils and condenses the steam, so salt is left behind in the liquid **[1]**, whereas reverse osmosis forces water under high pressure through a membrane that stops most ions **[1]**. Both need large amounts of energy: heat for distillation and pumping for reverse osmosis **[1]**.

**11.** (a) 3,500 × 220 = 770,000 litres **[1]**; 770,000 ÷ 1,000 = 770 m³ per day **[1]**.
(b) Shortfall = 770 − 600 = 170 m³ per day **[1]**; 600 ÷ 770 × 100 = 77.9% **[1]**.
(c) Reduce demand with low-flow showers, dual-flush toilets or towel-reuse schemes **[1]**; collect rainwater, or recycle treated waste water for watering gardens **[1]**.

**12.** Levels-style answer; one mark for each point, with at least one benefit and one limitation of each option and a judgement for full marks.
- Dam benefit: stores large volumes of river water for the dry season and also provides hydro-electric power and flood control **[1]**.
- Dam limitation: the river is in the interior, but most people live on the coast, so long pipelines are needed **[1]**; the reservoir floods land and homes and traps sediment **[1]**.
- Desalination benefit: plants can be built where people live, using an unlimited, drought-proof source **[1]**.
- Desalination limitation: high energy use and cost per cubic metre, and brine damages coastal marine life **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, desalination suits the coastal population best if powered by renewable energy, while a dam suits irrigation inland; a combination may be most reliable **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 5**: saying chlorine "cleans" the water without saying it kills microorganisms.
- **Question 7(c)**: stating the change without the reason (impermeable surfaces).
- **Question 9(a)**: not quoting figures from the table, or ignoring income.
- **Question 11(a)**: forgetting to divide by 1,000 to convert litres to m³.
- **Question 12**: describing each option separately with no judgement about which suits this country.
