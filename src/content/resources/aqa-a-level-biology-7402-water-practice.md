## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain why water molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other. **[3]**

**2.** For each situation, name the property of water that is most important: (a) a lizard's body temperature changes only slowly when it moves into the shade; (b) a dog pants on a hot day; (c) water rises as a continuous column in a tall plant. **[3]**

**3.** State whether water is used or produced in each reaction: (a) formation of a dipeptide; (b) breakdown of ATP to ADP and Pi; (c) digestion of starch to maltose. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why glucose dissolves in water. **[2]**

**5.** The specification says latent heat of vaporisation provides "a cooling effect with little loss of water". Explain what this means. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how surface tension makes the surface of a pond a habitat for some organisms. **[2]**

**7.** Use the data supplied. 500 g of water absorbs 10.45 kJ of energy. The specific heat capacity of water is 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹. Calculate the temperature rise. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** Temperatures were recorded at a coastal rock pool and in the air above it over 24 hours.

| | Minimum (°C) | Maximum (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| Air | 8 | 30 |
| Rock pool | 14 | 19 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the air temperature range is than the rock pool range. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the difference in ranges. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest one advantage to a small animal of living in the rock pool rather than on the dry rock. **[1]**

**9.** The table compares water with an imaginary liquid, Liquid Z. The values are invented.

| Property | Water | Liquid Z |
|---|---|---|
| Specific heat capacity (J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹) | 4.2 | 1.4 |
| Latent heat of vaporisation (kJ g⁻¹) | 2.4 | 0.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the mass of each liquid that must evaporate to remove 120 kJ of energy. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to explain why an organism based on Liquid Z would have more difficulty cooling itself. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why an organism whose cells were mostly Liquid Z would have more difficulty keeping its enzymes working at a steady rate. **[2]**

**10.** A scientist suggests that a plant species has xylem sap in which the water molecules are less strongly attracted to each other than normal. Suggest and explain the likely effect on water transport. **[3]**

**11.** The search for life elsewhere in the universe includes a search for liquid water. Use the properties of water to explain why. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen [1], so oxygen has a slight negative charge and each hydrogen a slight positive charge; water is polar [1]. The δ+ hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the δ− oxygen of a neighbouring molecule, forming a hydrogen bond [1]. **[3]**

**2.** (a) High heat capacity [1]. (b) Large latent heat of vaporisation [1]. (c) Cohesion [1]. **[3]**

**3.** (a) Produced [1]. (b) Used [1]. (c) Used [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Glucose is a polar molecule [1], so it forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules, which surround and separate the glucose molecules [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Evaporating each gram of water removes a large amount of energy, because many hydrogen bonds must be broken [1], so a large cooling effect needs only a small mass of water to evaporate, and the organism does not lose much water [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Hydrogen bonds pull surface water molecules together, so the surface acts like an elastic film [1]; small organisms such as pond skaters can rest or move on it without breaking through [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 10.45 kJ = 10,450 J [1]; temperature rise = 10,450 ÷ (500 × 4.18) = 10,450 ÷ 2,090 = **5.0 °C** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Convert kJ to J before dividing, because the specific heat capacity is given in joules.

**8. (a)** Air range = 30 − 8 = 22 °C; pool range = 19 − 14 = 5 °C [1]; 22 ÷ 5 = **4.4 times** [1].

**(b)** Water has a relatively high heat capacity [1]: much energy is used to break hydrogen bonds before the temperature rises, so the pool gains and loses heat with only a small change in temperature [1].

**(c)** It avoids extreme temperatures (or drying out), so its enzymes work at a more constant rate [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Water: 120 ÷ 2.4 = **50 g** [1]. Liquid Z: 120 ÷ 0.6 = **200 g** [1].

**(b)** To remove the same energy, four times as much Liquid Z must evaporate [1], so cooling by evaporation would cause much greater loss of body fluid and risk dehydration [1].

**(c)** Liquid Z has a lower heat capacity, so a given energy input would raise its temperature more (three times as much) [1]; cell temperature would fluctuate more, so enzyme activity would vary or enzymes could denature [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Weaker attraction between water molecules means less cohesion [1]. As water evaporates from the leaves, the column of water in the xylem is more likely to break [1], so water would not be pulled up as a continuous column and transport to the leaves would be slower or would fail in tall plants [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Any five, each linked to life. Water is a solvent in which metabolic reactions occur [1]. It is a metabolite, taking part in reactions such as hydrolysis and condensation [1]. Its high heat capacity buffers temperature change, giving stable conditions for enzymes [1]. Its large latent heat of vaporisation allows cooling with little water loss [1]. Cohesion allows transport in columns (as in plants) and surface tension creates habitats [1]. Also accept: it transports dissolved substances; it is the most common component of cells on Earth. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Each property earns credit only when linked to why it matters for life. A list of five property names without their importance is unlikely to gain full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that hydrogen bonds are strong.
- Confusing heat capacity with latent heat.
- Saying water is released in hydrolysis.
- Not converting kJ to J in heat capacity calculations.
- Describing data without calculating ranges or ratios.
- Naming properties without linking them to organisms.
