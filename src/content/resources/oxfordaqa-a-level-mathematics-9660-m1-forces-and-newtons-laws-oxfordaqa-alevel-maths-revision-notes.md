These notes condense **M1.3 Forces and Newton's Laws** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660), version 5.2, Unit PSM1. All motion is in a straight line, horizontal or vertical, and resolution of forces is not required. Worked examples are in the [M1.3 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-forces-and-newtons-laws-oxfordaqa-alevel-maths/); the [M1.3 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-forces-and-newtons-laws-oxfordaqa-alevel-maths-practice/) test each method. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻².

## Formulae to learn (not in the formulae booklet)

```
Weight:                 W = mg
Newton's second law:    F = ma   (resultant force = mass × acceleration)
Dynamic friction:       F = μR
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Weight | Force of gravity on a body, mg, acting vertically down |
| Normal reaction R | Force from a surface, perpendicular to it |
| Tension | Pulling force in a string or rod |
| Thrust | Pushing force in a rod; strings cannot provide one |
| Resistive force | A force opposing motion, such as air resistance or friction |
| Dynamic friction | Friction on a sliding body, F = μR, opposing the sliding |
| Light | Massless, so the tension is the same throughout a string |
| Inextensible | Does not stretch, so connected bodies share the size of acceleration |
| Smooth | No friction |
| Particle | Body treated as a point mass |
| Equilibrium | Resultant force zero; at rest or constant velocity |

## Newton's laws in one line each

1. No resultant force means no change in velocity.
2. Resultant force = mass × acceleration, in the direction of the acceleration.
3. If A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A; the pair act on different bodies.

## Methods

**Rough horizontal surface.** Find R from the vertical balance first, then F = μR, then F = ma horizontally. If a vertical pull P acts upwards, R = mg − P; a vertical push P down gives R = mg + P.

**Coefficient from motion.** Find a from suvat, then F from F = ma, then μ = F ÷ R. With friction alone on a level surface, deceleration = μg.

**Lift or hanging body.** Tension (or reaction, or balance reading) T: T − mg = ma with up positive. Upward acceleration gives T > mg; downward acceleration (including slowing while rising) gives T < mg.

**Whole system first.** For a train or car and trailer, internal tensions cancel: total driving force − total resistance = total mass × a. Then use one body, starting from the end with one unknown tension.

**Rope or rod?** Calculate the force in the link. If it comes out as a thrust and the link is a rope, the rope is slack and each body moves separately.

**Pulley stages.** Stage 1: two equations, add them for a. When the hanging body lands, the string goes slack: on a smooth table the other body continues at constant speed; on a rough table it decelerates at μg.

## Worked checks

- 8 kg box, rough floor μ = 0.3, horizontal pull 40 N: R = 78.4, F = 23.52, a = 2.06 m s⁻².
- Same box with a 20 N vertical upward pull added: R = 58.4, F = 17.52, a = 2.81 m s⁻².
- Lift decelerating at 1.2 m s⁻² while rising, passenger 70 kg: R = 70 × 8.6 = 602 N.
- 5 kg and 3 kg over a smooth pulley: a = 2g ÷ 8 = 2.45 m s⁻², T = 3 × 12.25 = 36.75 N.

## Exam traps

- R = mg used in a lift or with an extra vertical force.
- Treating tension as equal to the hanging weight while the system accelerates.
- Whole-system mass used in a single-body equation.
- Friction drawn in the direction of motion.
- A rope given a thrust.
- The acceleration from stage 1 kept after the string goes slack.
- Mass in grams or weight written as m instead of mg.

## Self-test

1. Find the weight of a 2.5 kg mass.
2. A 60 kg skydiver falls at a constant speed. Find the air resistance.
3. A sledge slides to rest from 5 m s⁻¹ in 10 m on level snow. Find μ.
4. A 12 kg block is pulled across a rough floor (μ = 0.25) by a horizontal force of 50 N. Find its acceleration.
5. A 900 kg lift accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the cable tension.
6. A 75 kg person stands in a lift accelerating downwards at 1.8 m s⁻². Find the normal reaction.
7. A car (1000 kg) tows a trailer (500 kg) with a tow bar. Driving force 2400 N; resistances 300 N on the car, 150 N on the trailer. Find a.
8. Find the tension in the tow bar in question 7.
9. Particles of 6 kg and 4 kg hang over a smooth pulley. Find a and T.
10. A 2 kg block on a smooth table is joined over a smooth pulley at the edge to a hanging 0.5 kg mass. Find a.
11. In question 10 the hanging mass lands. Describe the block's motion afterwards.
12. Why can a string not be in thrust?

### Answers

1. 24.5 N.
2. 60 × 9.8 = 588 N.
3. a = −25 ÷ 20 = −1.25, μ = 1.25 ÷ 9.8 = 0.128.
4. R = 117.6, F = 29.4, a = 20.6 ÷ 12 = 1.72 m s⁻².
5. 900 × 10.3 = 9270 N.
6. 75 × 8 = 600 N.
7. (2400 − 450) ÷ 1500 = 1.3 m s⁻².
8. T − 150 = 500 × 1.3, T = 800 N.
9. a = 2g ÷ 10 = 1.96 m s⁻²; T = 4 × 11.76 = 47.04 N (47.0 N).
10. a = 0.5g ÷ 2.5 = 1.96 m s⁻².
11. The string is slack and the table is smooth, so it moves at constant speed (Newton's first law).
12. A string can only pull along its length; pushed, it simply goes slack.
