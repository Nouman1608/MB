These questions cover sub-topic **14.5 Tropic responses** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions marked **(Extended)** use Supplement outcomes 14.5.4 and 14.5.5 on auxin; a Core candidate can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes gravitropism? **[1]**

A: growth of a plant towards the direction of a light source

B: growth of parts of a plant towards or away from gravity

C: movement of water through a plant caused by gravity

D: the fall of leaves and fruits from a plant to the ground

**2.** A young root is growing in damp soil with light reaching it from one side.

**(a)** State the direction in which the root grows in relation to the light. **[1]**

**(b)** State the direction in which the root grows in relation to gravity. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why phototropism is described as a growth response and not a movement. **[2]**

**4.** Suggest one advantage to a seedling of its shoot growing towards light from one side. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State where auxin is made in a shoot, and the effect auxin has on the cells of the shoot. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "A shoot bends towards the light because cells on the lit side shrink." Identify the error and write a correct statement. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student pins four germinating broad bean seeds to damp cotton wool inside a closed dish. Their young roots point down, up, left and right. She leaves the dish in a dark cupboard for three days.

**(a)** Explain why the dish is kept in the dark. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe a suitable control for this investigation and explain its purpose. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict how the root that started pointing up, and its shoot, will have grown after three days. **[2]**

**(d)** State **two** variables that should be kept the same for the seeds in the control and the test. **[2]**

**8.** Ten cress seedlings were grown in a box lit through one slit. Ten more were grown in the same conditions but lit equally from all sides. After two days the angle of each shoot from vertical was measured.

| Group | Angles from vertical / ° |
|---|---|
| One-sided light | 40, 44, 38, 45, 39, 42, 41, 37, 43, 41 |
| Light from all sides | mean = 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean angle for the one-sided light group. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the group lit from all sides was included. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why ten seedlings were used in each group rather than one. **[1]**

**(d)** Describe what these results show. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Two young shoots were lit from the left. A thin sheet that auxin cannot pass through was pushed into each shoot just below the tip, on one side only.

**(a)** In shoot X the sheet was on the shaded side. Predict what happens to shoot X and explain your prediction. **[3]**

**(b)** In shoot Y the sheet was on the lit side. Predict what happens to shoot Y and explain your prediction. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Shoot tips were cut off and left standing on small agar blocks for three hours. Each agar block was then placed on the **right** half of the cut surface of a shoot whose own tip had been removed. The shoots were kept in the dark.

**(a)** Predict the direction in which these shoots will curve. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your prediction. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe a control for this experiment. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A potted plant is knocked over by the wind and lies on its side for several days. The end of its stem curves until it is growing vertically upwards. Explain how auxin causes this response. **[4]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Ink marks 5.0 mm apart were drawn along the upper and lower sides of a shoot lying horizontally. After one day, a marked interval on the lower side measured 7.4 mm and the matching interval on the upper side measured 5.6 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in length of the interval on each side. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times more the lower side grew than the upper side. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how these results support the role of auxin in gravitropism of a shoot. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

*Examiner insight:* A is phototropism, and only towards light. C and D are not growth responses at all.

**2. (a)** **Away from** the light [1]

**(b)** **Towards** gravity, downwards [1]

*Examiner insight:* Roots respond the opposite way to shoots for both stimuli; "down" alone is accepted for (b), but "away from gravity" is the shoot response.

**3.** The shoot curves because **one side grows faster / more than the other** [1]; the plant does not move as a whole, so the change happens slowly over **hours or days** [1].

**4.** The leaves receive **more light** [1], so the seedling can carry out **more photosynthesis** and make more glucose for growth [1].

**5.** Auxin is made in the **shoot tip** [1]. It **stimulates cell elongation** [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Makes cells grow" is accepted only if it means longer cells; "makes cells divide" scores zero for the second mark.

**6.** The error: cells on the lit side do not shrink; the curvature comes from **unequal elongation** [1]. Correct statement: **more auxin is on the shaded side, so cells on the shaded side elongate more** than cells on the lit side, and the shoot curves towards the light [1].

**7. (a)** So that **light is not a stimulus**; any change in direction is caused only by gravity [1].

**(b)** Seeds fixed on a **clinostat that rotates slowly** [1], so gravity acts **equally on all sides** and shows that curvature in the test is caused by one-sided gravity [1].

**(c)** The root curves round to grow **downwards** [1]; the shoot curves to grow **upwards** [1].

**(d)** Any two of: type / species of seed; age or size of seed; temperature; amount of water; time left [2].

*Examiner insight:* "The same conditions" scores nothing in (d); each variable must be named.

**8. (a)** 410 ÷ 10 = **41°** [1]

**(b)** It is a **control**: it shows the bending was caused by light coming from **one side**, not by other conditions in the box [1].

**(c)** One seedling might be unusual; ten allow a **mean** to be calculated, so the result is more reliable [1].

**(d)** Shoots grow **towards the light** when it comes from one side, bending a mean of 41° [1], while shoots lit from all sides grow almost straight up, a mean of 2° [1].

**9. (a)** Shoot X does **not bend** / grows straight [1]. Auxin made in the tip **cannot diffuse down the shaded side** [1], so cells on the shaded side do not elongate more than cells on the lit side [1].

**(b)** Shoot Y **bends towards the light** [1]. Auxin can still diffuse down the **shaded side**, where it stimulates more cell elongation [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each prediction needs its reason; "it bends" with no reference to auxin reaching the shaded side earns only one mark in (b).

**10. (a)** They curve **to the left** [1].

**(b)** **Auxin diffused from the tips into the agar** [1]. It then diffuses from the block into the **right side** of the shoot [1], where it **stimulates cell elongation**, so the right side grows longer and the shoot curves to the left [1].

**(c)** Place a **plain agar block** that has not had a tip on it on the right side of a cut shoot, in the dark [1].

*Examiner insight:* The shoots are in the dark, so light cannot explain the curvature; examiners reward answers that use the block position as the cause of unequal auxin.

**11.** Auxin is **made in the tip** of the stem [1] and **diffuses** back through the stem [1]. In response to gravity, **more auxin collects on the lower side** [1]. Auxin **stimulates cell elongation**, so the lower side grows longer and the stem curves upwards [1].

**12. (a)** Lower side: (7.4 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100 = **48%** [1]. Upper side: (5.6 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100 = **12%** [1].

**(b)** 2.4 ÷ 0.6 = **4 times** [1]

**(c)** The **lower side grew more**, which fits **more auxin on the lower side** [1], because auxin **stimulates cell elongation**, making the shoot curve upwards [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a) the subtraction comes first; dividing 7.4 by 5.0 gives a ratio, not a percentage increase.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "moves" instead of "grows".
- Giving root directions the same as shoot directions.
- Leaving light on during a gravitropism investigation, or giving no control.
- (Extended) Putting more auxin on the lit side or the upper side.
- (Extended) Saying auxin causes cell division rather than cell elongation.
- (Extended) Predicting a result without explaining it through auxin distribution.
