Condensed notes for sub-topic **6(b) Magnetism** (statements 6.2 to 6.7)
of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1). All of it is core
content, examinable on both papers. For full explanations and worked
answers, use the [Magnetism study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-magnetism/).

## Core facts in one place

**Poles and forces**

- Two poles: north-seeking (N) and south-seeking (S).
- Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.
- A magnet attracts magnetic substances: iron, steel, nickel, cobalt.
- Non-magnetic: copper, aluminium, brass, plastics, wood, glass.
- **Only repulsion proves an object is a magnet.**

**Hard versus soft**

- Magnetically **hard** (steel): difficult to magnetise, keeps its magnetism. Used for permanent magnets.
- Magnetically **soft** (iron): easy to magnetise, loses magnetism when the field is removed. Used in electromagnet and transformer cores.
- Always link the property to the job: a crane's electromagnet core must release the load, so it must be soft.

**Field lines**

- Direction = direction of force on a N pole; shown by a compass needle's N end.
- Outside a magnet: N to S.
- Never cross.
- Closer lines = stronger field (crowded at the poles).

**Induced magnetism**

- A magnetic material in a field becomes a magnet.
- The end nearest the magnet becomes the unlike pole, so the result is always attraction.
- Soft iron loses induced magnetism when removed from the field; steel keeps some.

## The field-pattern practical (6.6)

| Step | Plotting compass | Iron filings |
|---|---|---|
| Set-up | Magnet on paper, outline drawn | Card over magnet |
| Method | Mark where the needle points, move the compass tail to that dot, repeat pole to pole | Sprinkle thinly, tap the card |
| Shows | Shape **and direction** | Shape only |
| Weakness | Slow; each line drawn separately | No direction; filings clump near the poles |

**Two magnets**

- N facing S: lines cross the gap; attraction.
- N facing N (or S facing S): lines curve away; **neutral point** midway where the fields cancel; repulsion.

## Uniform field (6.7)

- Two flat magnets, parallel, **unlike poles facing**, small gap.
- Drawn as straight, parallel, equally spaced lines from N to S.
- Uniform only in the middle of the gap; lines bulge at the edges.
- Same strength and direction everywhere in that region.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetic substance | A material attracted by a magnet (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) |
| Magnetic field | Region where a magnet or magnetic substance feels a force |
| Magnetic field line | Line showing the direction of force on a N pole at each point |
| Magnetically hard | Hard to magnetise, keeps magnetism (steel) |
| Magnetically soft | Easy to magnetise, loses magnetism (iron) |
| Induced magnetism | Magnetism a material gains while it is in a magnetic field |
| Neutral point | Point where two fields cancel, so there is no resultant field |
| Uniform field | Field of constant strength and direction; parallel, equally spaced lines |
| Plotting compass | Small compass used to trace field direction point by point |

## Exam traps

- **"It is attracted, so it is a magnet."** Wrong: unmagnetised iron is attracted too. Look for repulsion.
- **One-sided descriptions of hard and soft.** State ease of magnetising and whether the magnetism is kept.
- **Arrows the wrong way.** Outside the magnet the arrows go N to S, never S to N.
- **Crossing lines.** A point cannot have two field directions.
- **Uniform field drawn with like poles facing.** It needs unlike poles facing.
- **Iron filings "show direction".** They show only the pattern.
- **Missing neutral point** between two like poles.
- **Writing "magnetic" for every metal.** Aluminium and copper are not attracted.

## Self-test

1. State the only test that proves an object is a permanent magnet.
2. Name four magnetic metals.
3. Why is steel used for a permanent magnet?
4. Why is iron, not steel, used for the core of a lifting electromagnet?
5. State what the direction of a field line represents.
6. What does a region of closely spaced field lines tell you?
7. An iron nail is placed near the S pole of a magnet. Which pole is induced in the nearer end of the nail?
8. Give one advantage of the plotting compass method over iron filings.
9. What is a neutral point, and where is it found between two N poles facing each other?
10. Describe the arrangement of two magnets that produces a uniform field.
11. How is a uniform field shown on a diagram?
12. Iron paper clips hang in a chain from a magnet. Explain what happens when the magnet is removed.

## Answers

1. Repulsion: bring each end near each pole of a known magnet; if any end is repelled, the object is a magnet.
2. Iron, steel, nickel, cobalt.
3. Steel is magnetically hard, so once magnetised it keeps its magnetism.
4. Iron is magnetically soft, so it loses its magnetism when the current is switched off and the load is released.
5. The direction of the force on a north pole placed at that point.
6. The magnetic field is strong there.
7. A north pole, because the induced pole nearest the magnet is the unlike pole (so the nail is attracted).
8. It shows the direction of the field as well as the pattern.
9. A point where the two fields cancel so there is no resultant field; it is midway between the two N poles.
10. Two flat magnets placed parallel to each other with unlike poles facing across a small gap.
11. Straight, parallel, equally spaced lines with arrows from N to S.
12. Each clip was held by induced magnetism; iron is magnetically soft, so the clips lose their magnetism and the chain falls apart.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is any of 4PH1 sub-topic 6b Physics-only content?**
No. Statements 6.2 to 6.7 have no "P" reference, so this sub-topic can appear on Paper 1P and Paper 2P.

**Do I need to know Earth's magnetic field for 4PH1?**
The specification does not list Earth's field as a statement. It is useful only to explain why a compass needle points along a field line.

**Which practical is required in this sub-topic?**
Statement 6.6: investigating the magnetic field pattern for a permanent bar magnet and between two bar magnets.
