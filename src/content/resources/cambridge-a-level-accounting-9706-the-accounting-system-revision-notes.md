## Debit and credit in one table

| Account type | Increase | Decrease | Normal balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assets | Dr | Cr | Debit |
| Expenses and purchases | Dr | Cr | Debit |
| Drawings | Dr | Cr | Debit |
| Liabilities | Cr | Dr | Credit |
| Capital | Cr | Dr | Credit |
| Income and sales | Cr | Dr | Credit |

Memory aid: debit the account that **receives** value, credit the account that **gives** value.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Double entry | Recording each transaction as an equal debit and credit |
| Accounting equation | Assets = liabilities + capital |
| Book of prime entry | First place a transaction is recorded before posting to the ledger |
| Ledger | Collection of accounts: sales (receivables), purchases (payables) and general (nominal) |
| Posting | Transferring entries or totals from books of prime entry to ledger accounts |
| Balance c/d and b/d | Balance carried down to make totals agree, then brought down on the opposite side |
| Trial balance | List of all ledger balances to check that total debits equal total credits |
| Full accounting records | Complete double-entry records of every transaction |
| Computerised accounting system | Software that records transactions and posts the double entry automatically |
| Audit trail | Record of who entered or changed each item and when |

## Books of prime entry

| Book | Records | Not for |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Credit sales of goods | Cash sales; sale of non-current assets |
| Sales returns journal | Returns from credit customers | Returns of cash sales |
| Purchases journal | Credit purchases of goods for resale | Non-current assets bought on credit |
| Purchases returns journal | Returns to credit suppliers | — |
| Cash book | All cash and bank receipts and payments | Credit transactions |
| General journal | Opening entries, credit purchases or sales of non-current assets, corrections, year-end adjustments | Routine trading |

## Common double entries

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays money into bank | Bank | Capital |
| Credit purchase of goods | Purchases | Supplier (payable) |
| Credit sale of goods | Customer (receivable) | Sales |
| Cash sale banked | Bank | Sales |
| Return of goods to supplier | Supplier | Purchases returns |
| Return of goods by customer | Sales returns | Customer |
| Payment to supplier | Supplier | Bank |
| Owner takes cash | Drawings | Cash or bank |
| Equipment bought on credit | Equipment | Supplier of equipment |

## Trial balance

**Purpose:** checks the arithmetic of the double entry; summarises balances for preparing financial statements.

**Limitation:** agreement does not prove there are no errors (covered in 1.4).

## Accounting concepts

| Concept | One-line test |
|---|---|
| Business entity | Is it the business's item, not the owner's? |
| Historic cost | Is it recorded at the original cost? |
| Money measurement | Can it be measured reliably in money? |
| Going concern | Is the business assumed to continue trading? |
| Consistency | Is the same method used each period? |
| Prudence | Are losses recognised and gains not anticipated? |
| Realisation | Has the revenue been earned (goods passed and customer liable)? |
| Duality | Are both effects recorded? |
| Materiality | Is the amount large enough to matter? |
| Objectivity | Is it based on verifiable evidence? |
| Matching/accruals | Is income or expense in the period it relates to? |
| Substance over form | Does the record reflect economic reality, not just legal form? |

## Full records and computerisation

- Full records: accurate profit; control of receivables and payables; easier detection of errors and fraud; evidence for lenders and tax. Cost: time, skill or a bookkeeper.
- Computerised: faster, fewer arithmetic errors, instant reports. Drawbacks: cost, training, system failure, data loss and unauthorised access.
- Security: passwords; access levels; regular backups kept separately; firewalls and anti-virus software; audit trails.

## Exam traps

- Cash sales in the sales journal.
- Credit purchase of a non-current asset in the purchases journal.
- Reversing debit and credit for suppliers and customers.
- Carrying a balance down on the wrong side.
- Treating drawings as an expense.
- Claiming a balanced trial balance proves no errors.
- Naming a concept without applying it.

## Self-test

1. State the accounting equation.
2. Which book of prime entry records goods returned by a credit customer?
3. In which book is the credit purchase of a delivery van recorded?
4. Give the double entry for a credit sale of $600 to Jay.
5. Give the double entry for the owner withdrawing $300 from the bank for personal use.
6. A supplier's account has credits of $5 000 and debits of $3 800. State the balance and which side it is brought down on.
7. State two purposes of a trial balance.
8. Which concept explains recording a vehicle bought on instalments as an asset before ownership passes?
9. Which concept is applied when a $12 calculator is treated as an expense?
10. A customer orders goods in December; they are delivered in January. In which month is the revenue realised?
11. State two advantages of keeping full accounting records.
12. State three ways of keeping data secure in a computerised system.

### Answers

1. Assets = liabilities + capital.
2. The sales returns journal.
3. The general journal.
4. Debit Jay (receivable) $600; credit sales $600.
5. Debit drawings $300; credit bank $300.
6. $1 200 credit balance: carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side.
7. To check that total debits equal total credits (arithmetic accuracy) and to provide a summary for preparing financial statements.
8. Substance over form.
9. Materiality.
10. January, when the goods are delivered and the customer becomes liable to pay.
11. Any two: accurate profit calculation; knowing amounts owed by and to the business; detecting errors and fraud; evidence for lenders and tax authorities; information for decisions.
12. Any three: passwords; access levels; regular backups kept separately; firewalls and anti-virus software; audit trails.
