## Questions

**1.** Glucose exists in two ring forms, α-glucose and β-glucose.

**(a)** State the number of carbon atoms in the ring of a glucose molecule. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the structure of β-glucose differs from that of α-glucose. **[2]**

**2.**

**(a)** Define the term *monomer*. **[1]**
**(b)** Define the term *polysaccharide*. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a triglyceride is not described as a polymer. **[1]**

**3.** State which of these sugars are reducing sugars and which are non-reducing: glucose, sucrose, maltose, fructose. **[2]**

**4.** Maltose is formed from two molecules of α-glucose.

**(a)** Describe how the two α-glucose molecules are joined. **[3]**
**(b)** Glucose has Mr 180 and water has Mr 18. Calculate the Mr of maltose. **[1]**

**5.** A sports drink contains sucrose as its only sugar. Describe how you would show that the drink contains a non-reducing sugar. Give the result you would expect at each stage. **[5]**

**6.** In a model, an amylopectin molecule and a glycogen molecule each contain 50 000 α-glucose units. The amylopectin molecule has one branch point for every 25 glucose units. The glycogen molecule has one branch point for every 10 glucose units.

**(a)** Calculate the number of α-1,6 glycosidic bonds in each molecule. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the difference you calculated suits glycogen to its role in animals. **[3]**

**7.** Describe the molecular structure of cellulose and explain how the arrangement of cellulose molecules makes plant cell walls strong. **[5]**

**8.** Tristearin is a triglyceride made from glycerol and three molecules of stearic acid.

**(a)** Name the bond that forms between glycerol and each stearic acid molecule, and the type of reaction that forms it. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many molecules of water are released when one molecule of tristearin forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Glycerol has Mr 92, stearic acid has Mr 284 and water has Mr 18. Calculate the Mr of tristearin. **[2]**

**9.** The formulae of three fatty acids, each with 18 carbon atoms, are: A C₁₈H₃₆O₂; B C₁₈H₃₄O₂; C C₁₈H₃₂O₂.

**(a)** Identify the saturated fatty acid. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of C=C double bonds in B and in C. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how a C=C double bond affects the shape of a fatty acid chain. **[1]**
**(d)** State the term used for a fatty acid such as C. **[1]**

**10.** A small migratory bird stores 30 g of triglyceride before its journey. Use these values: triglyceride releases 37 kJ g⁻¹ and carbohydrate releases 17 kJ g⁻¹ when respired.

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in the bird's triglyceride. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of glycogen that would store the same energy. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** With reference to molecular structure, explain why triglyceride is a better energy store than glycogen for a bird that flies long distances. **[3]**
**(d)** State one other function of triglycerides in animals. **[1]**

**11.**

**(a)** Describe the structure of a phospholipid molecule, with reference to the polarity of its parts. **[3]**
**(b)** A few drops of phospholipid are added to the surface of water in a dish. Predict how the molecules arrange themselves at the surface, and explain your prediction. **[2]**

**12.** A germinating seed hydrolyses its food stores of amylose, amylopectin and triglyceride.

**(a)** Name the product of complete hydrolysis of amylose, and the products of complete hydrolysis of a triglyceride. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of water molecules needed to completely hydrolyse: (i) one unbranched amylose chain of 600 glucose units; (ii) one triglyceride molecule. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why amylopectin can be hydrolysed to glucose faster than the same mass of amylose. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one advantage to a small, wind-dispersed seed of storing its energy as triglyceride rather than starch. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Five** (the sixth ring atom is oxygen) [1]
**(b)** The two forms differ only at **carbon 1** [1]. In β-glucose the OH group on carbon 1 is **above** the ring (H below), while in α-glucose it is **below** the ring [1].
*Examiner insight:* "The OH is in a different position" is too vague for the second mark; you must say above/below the ring and name carbon 1.

**2. (a)** A relatively small molecule that can join with other similar molecules to form a polymer [1]
**(b)** A polymer of many monosaccharides joined by glycosidic bonds [1]
**(c)** It is made of one glycerol and three fatty acids, not a long chain of repeating monomer units [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "many sugars" without "joined by glycosidic bonds" or "monosaccharides" is usually not enough for a definition mark.

**3.** Reducing: **glucose, maltose, fructose**; non-reducing: **sucrose**. All four correct [2] (two or three correct scores 1 mark).
*Examiner insight:* Maltose is a disaccharide but still reducing; listing every disaccharide as non-reducing loses the mark.

**4. (a)** The OH on carbon 1 of one α-glucose reacts with the OH on carbon 4 of the other [1]. A molecule of water is removed in a **condensation** reaction [1]. An **α-1,4 glycosidic bond** forms [1].
**(b)** (2 × 180) − 18 = **342** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Water is produced" earns the condensation mark only if the reaction is named or clearly described; "hydrolysis" here scores zero for that point.

**5.** Do a Benedict's test on a sample and heat: it **stays blue**, so no reducing sugar is present [1]. Heat a fresh sample with **dilute hydrochloric acid** in a boiling water bath [1]. This **hydrolyses the glycosidic bond** in sucrose, giving glucose and fructose [1]. Cool and **neutralise** with sodium hydrogencarbonate [1]. Add Benedict's solution and heat: an **orange-red / brick-red precipitate** forms, showing a non-reducing sugar was present [1].
*Examiner insight:* A colour change must name the colour; "it changes colour" or "turns positive" does not earn the result marks.

**6. (a)** Amylopectin: 50 000 ÷ 25 = **2000** [1]. Glycogen: 50 000 ÷ 10 = **5000** [1].
**(b)** Glycogen has more branches, so it has **more chain ends** [1]. Enzymes hydrolyse glucose from the ends, so **many glucose molecules can be released at the same time / quickly** [1]. Animals are active and can have a high, sudden demand for glucose for respiration [1].
*Examiner insight:* "More branches so it is more compact" is true but does not answer the question; the marks are for ends, enzyme access and rapid release.

**7.** Cellulose is made of **β-glucose** joined by **β-1,4 glycosidic bonds** [1]. Every other glucose is rotated 180°, so the chain is **straight and unbranched** [1]. Neighbouring chains lie parallel and are held together by many **hydrogen bonds** [1]. Groups of chains form **microfibrils**, which group into fibres laid in layers [1]. This gives **high tensile strength**, so the wall resists the pressure of water entering by osmosis and the cell does not burst [1].
*Examiner insight:* Reject "glycosidic bonds between chains"; the bonds between neighbouring chains are hydrogen bonds, and mixing them up costs the mark.

**8. (a)** **Ester bond** [1]; formed by **condensation** [1].
**(b)** **Three** [1]
**(c)** 92 + (3 × 284) = 944 [1]; 944 − (3 × 18) = **890** [1]
*Examiner insight:* If (b) is wrong, the method mark in (c) can still be earned for subtracting water correctly from your own count (error carried forward).

**9. (a)** **A** [1]
**(b)** B: **one**; C: **two** (both needed) [1]
**(c)** It puts a **kink / bend** in the hydrocarbon chain [1]
**(d)** **Polyunsaturated** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers in (b) are marked together; one correct value alone does not score.

**10. (a)** 30 × 37 = **1110 kJ** [1]
**(b)** 1110 ÷ 17 [1] = **65.3 g** [1]
**(c)** Triglycerides have **many C–H bonds** and little oxygen [1], so they release **more energy per gram** when respired [1]. The bird carries a **smaller mass** for the same energy, which reduces the energy cost of flight [1].
**(d)** Any one: thermal insulation; buoyancy; source of metabolic water; protection of organs [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the answer needs the unit and 3 significant figures; 65.29 or 65 loses the accuracy mark.

**11. (a)** **Glycerol** joined by ester bonds to **two fatty acids** [1]. A **phosphate group** is attached to the third carbon of glycerol [1]. The phosphate head is **polar / hydrophilic** and the fatty acid tails are **non-polar / hydrophobic** [1].
**(b)** A **single layer** forms, with phosphate heads in the water and tails pointing up into the air [1]. The heads are hydrophilic and attracted to water; the tails are hydrophobic and repelled by water [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bilayer is the wrong prediction for a surface; credit goes to a reasoned arrangement that matches the conditions given.

**12. (a)** Amylose: **α-glucose** [1]. Triglyceride: **glycerol and fatty acids** [1].
**(b)** (i) 600 − 1 = **599** [1]; (ii) **3** [1]
**(c)** Amylopectin is **branched**, so it has **more ends** than unbranched amylose [1]. Enzymes act at the ends, so more glucose is released at once [1].
**(d)** Triglyceride releases **more energy per gram** [1], so the seed can be **lighter** for the same energy store and be carried further by wind [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b)(i), answering 600 shows the n − 1 rule was missed; one water is used per glycosidic bond, not per glucose unit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing α/β glucose without naming carbon 1 or saying above/below the ring.
- Calling maltose non-reducing, or sucrose reducing.
- Mixing up condensation and hydrolysis, or which one releases water.
- Leaving out neutralisation, or not stating colours, in the non-reducing sugar test.
- Writing "glucose" where the question needs α-glucose or β-glucose.
- Saying cellulose chains are joined by glycosidic bonds to each other.
- Using n instead of n − 1 when counting bonds or water molecules in a chain.
- Forgetting to subtract the water when calculating the Mr of a condensation product.
- Linking fat to energy storage without the reason: more C–H bonds, more energy per gram.
- Calling a phospholipid tail hydrophilic, or a triglyceride polar.
