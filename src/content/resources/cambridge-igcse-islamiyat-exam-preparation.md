Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat 0493 is assessed by two compulsory written papers of exactly equal weight:
each lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, carries 50 marks, and contributes 50% of the qualification. Paper 1
covers the Qur'an, the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) and the first Islamic community; Paper 2 covers the
Hadiths, the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam. All answers are
written in English. These notes turn that structure into a preparation plan and complement the
site's topic material for
[Paper 1](/resources/igcse-islamiyat-paper-1/) and
[Paper 2](/resources/igcse-cambridge-islamiyat-paper-2/).

## Equal weighting removes the option of a "strong paper" strategy

In subjects where one paper dominates, a candidate can partly compensate for a weak component. Here
the two papers are exactly 50% each, so a strong Paper 1 cannot rescue a neglected Paper 2. **Exam-
preparation priority**: audit both papers' content honestly early in the revision period and give the
weaker one *more* time, not less. The instinct to revise the material that already feels comfortable
is particularly costly under a 50/50 split.

## Ninety minutes for fifty marks is a tighter budget than it looks

Each paper allows 1 hour 30 minutes for 50 marks -- a little under two minutes per mark once reading
and planning are included. Islamiyat answers reward accurate detail, and accurate detail takes time
to write. **Exam-preparation priority**: rehearse under timed conditions rather than open-ended
practice. Most candidates who run short of time do so because they wrote a very full answer to the
first question and then compressed the rest; practising to a per-question time allowance is what
breaks that habit.

## Precise reference to the source material is what separates answers

Both papers are grounded in specific source material -- Qur'anic passages on Paper 1, Hadiths on
Paper 2 -- and answers that refer to the specific passage or Hadith relevant to the question read very
differently from answers that gesture at a general theme. **Exam-preparation priority**: when
revising each set passage or Hadith, record alongside it (a) what it says, (b) the context in which it
was revealed or narrated, and (c) what teaching or principle it establishes. That three-part note is
directly reusable in an exam answer; a summary of the passage alone is not.

## Description and significance are different tasks and need different practice

A large share of marks across both papers depends on being able to move from *what happened* or *what
was said* to *why it matters* to Muslim belief and practice. These are separate skills, and the second
one is rarely improved by re-reading notes. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the significance
step explicitly. For each event, passage or figure you revise, write two sentences of significance
from memory before checking your notes. Candidates who only rehearse narrative detail tend to produce
answers that are accurate but which do not address the question actually asked.

## Paper 2's breadth needs a deliberate coverage plan

Paper 2 spans three distinct bodies of material -- Hadith literature, the period of the Rightly Guided
Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam -- which are historically and conceptually
quite different from one another. **Exam-preparation priority**: plan Paper 2 revision by these three
strands rather than by working linearly through a set of notes, and check near the exam that none of
the three has been left materially thinner than the others.

## Worked structure: turning an event into a full answer

The structure below is an original worked model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any
official mark scheme or past paper answer. It shows how a narrative fact becomes a complete response.

```
Prompt type: "Explain the importance of [an event or teaching]."

Step 1 - state the fact accurately and specifically:
Name the people, the place and the point in the chronology. Vague
placing ("early in Islamic history") is not creditable detail.

Step 2 - give the immediate consequence:
What changed as a direct result -- for the community, for a practice,
or for how a principle was understood.

Step 3 - connect to belief or practice:
Explain what the event or teaching establishes about Muslim belief or
about how Muslims act, since this is the "importance" the question is
asking about.

Step 4 - support from the source material:
Refer to the specific passage or Hadith that bears on the point,
rather than to the general topic it belongs to.
```

Steps 3 and 4 are where weaker answers stop short. An answer that completes steps 1 and 2 only has
described the event; it has not yet explained its importance.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: give the weaker of the two papers more revision time, not less; build
  three-part notes (content, context, teaching) for each set passage and Hadith; practise writing
  two sentences of significance from memory for every item revised; plan Paper 2 revision by its
  three distinct strands and check none has been left thin.
- **During either paper**: set a per-question time allowance before starting and hold to it -- a
  complete answer to every question scores better than an outstanding answer to the first and a
  rushed final one.
- **On every question**: check whether the question asks *what* or *why*, and make sure the answer
  actually completes the "why" step rather than describing at greater length.
- **When referring to sources**: name the specific passage or Hadith the question bears on rather
  than the general theme.

## Self-test

1. Why does the 50/50 weighting change how revision time should be allocated?
2. What three things should a note on a set passage or Hadith record?
3. Which two steps do weaker answers to an "importance" question typically omit?
4. Why should Paper 2 revision be planned by strand rather than worked through linearly?

**Answers:** 1. Because neither paper can compensate for the other -- each is exactly 50% -- so the
weaker paper should receive more time rather than the more comfortable one. 2. What the passage says,
the context in which it was revealed or narrated, and the teaching or principle it establishes.
3. Connecting the event or teaching to Muslim belief or practice, and supporting the point from the
specific relevant source material -- weaker answers complete only the accurate statement of fact and
its immediate consequence. 4. Because Paper 2 spans three conceptually distinct bodies of material
(Hadith literature, the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam), and
linear revision tends to leave whichever strand comes last materially thinner than the others.
