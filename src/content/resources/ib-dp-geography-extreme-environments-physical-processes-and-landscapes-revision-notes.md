## Weathering and mass movement

| Environment | Key weathering |
|---|---|
| Hot desert | Thermal stress; salt weathering (evaporating groundwater leaves salts in rock pores) |
| Cold | Freeze-thaw: water enters cracks, freezes and expands, repeated cycles widen cracks and produce scree |

Chemical weathering is usually slow in dry and cold settings because it needs liquid water and warmth.

How each works in more detail:

- **Thermal stress:** rock surfaces heat and expand by day and cool and contract at night. Different minerals expand by different amounts, so repeated cycles weaken the rock. Most researchers think a little moisture makes this far more effective, so do not present it as a purely dry process.
- **Salt weathering:** salty water seeps into pores, evaporates and leaves crystals that grow and exert pressure, prising grains apart. It is common where groundwater is close to the surface or near salt flats.
- **Freeze-thaw:** works best where temperatures cross 0 degrees Celsius many times and water is available, so it is most active in periglacial and high mountain zones, not in the coldest continuously frozen places.

Mass movement is the downslope movement of material under gravity. In cold environments **solifluction** is the slow flow of saturated active-layer soil over frozen ground; in mountains, **rockfalls** and **avalanches** (loose snow or slab) occur on steep slopes, often triggered by loading, warming or vibration.

## Cold environments

- **Glacial:** glaciers erode by **plucking** and **abrasion**. Landforms: corries (cirques), arretes, pyramidal peaks, glacial troughs, hanging valleys, moraines. Think of the glacier as a system: **accumulation**, **ablation**, **mass balance**.
- **Periglacial** (frozen ground without ice cover): **permafrost**, the **active layer** that thaws in summer, **frost heave**, **solifluction**; landforms such as patterned ground and pingos.

### The glacier as a system

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accumulation zone | Upper area where snowfall exceeds melting, so mass is added |
| Ablation zone | Lower area where melting, sublimation and calving remove mass |
| Equilibrium line | Boundary where accumulation equals ablation over a year |
| Mass balance | Accumulation minus ablation; positive means the glacier thickens or advances, negative means it thins or retreats |

Ice always flows downhill, even when the snout retreats: retreat means ablation at the snout is faster than ice supply.

**Warm-based** glaciers have meltwater at their base, slide over the bed and erode strongly. **Cold-based** glaciers are frozen to their bed, move mainly by internal deformation and erode little.

### Deposition

- **Till** is unsorted material dropped directly by ice: it forms moraines (terminal, lateral, medial) and drumlins. Large transported boulders are **erratics**.
- **Fluvioglacial** (meltwater) deposits are sorted and rounded: outwash plains, eskers and kames.

### Two periglacial landforms in sequence

- **Pingo:** a lake drains or fills with sediment; permafrost advances into the saturated ground beneath; water is squeezed upward and freezes into an ice core; the ground domes up. The Tuktoyaktuk Peninsula in Arctic Canada is a well-known pingo area.
- **Patterned ground:** repeated freezing and thawing sorts stones from fine material, moving larger stones outward into circles or polygons on flat ground and stripes on slopes.

## Arid environments

- Wind transport: saltation, suspension, surface creep. Wind erosion: deflation, abrasion.
- Dunes form where sand supply is large and winds consistent.
- Water is rare but powerful: infrequent heavy rain causes flash floods in **wadis** and builds **alluvial fans** and **playas**.
- Many desert landscapes are **inherited**, shaped partly under wetter past climates.

### Landforms to know

| Agent | Landform | How it forms |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | Barchan dune | Crescent shape; sand moves fastest round the lower edges, so the horns point downwind |
| Wind | Seif (linear) dune | Long ridges where winds blow from two main directions |
| Wind | Yardang | Streamlined ridge of softer rock carved by sand-blasting, aligned with the wind |
| Wind | Ventifact | Stone faceted and polished by abrasion |
| Wind | Deflation hollow | Loose fine material removed until the surface is lowered |
| Water | Alluvial fan | Flood water leaves a confined wadi, spreads out, slows and deposits sediment in a fan |
| Water | Playa | Temporary lake in a basin of inland drainage; evaporation leaves salt flats |
| Weathering and water | Mesa and butte | Flat-topped hills where a resistant caprock protects softer rock below; a butte is a smaller remnant (Monument Valley in the south-western USA is a classic example) |

**Desert pavement** (reg) forms where deflation removes fine particles, leaving a protective layer of stones.

## Mountain environments

Steep slopes, cold, thin air and intense radiation. Vegetation zones change with height; avalanches and rockfalls reflect slope instability.

**Aspect** matters: in the northern hemisphere, south-facing slopes receive more sunshine and are warmer and drier, so snowlines and tree lines sit higher on them. Lowering temperature with altitude means a single mountain can pass from forest through alpine grassland to bare rock and permanent snow.

## Explaining a landform

Give a numbered sequence, for example for a corrie: snow accumulates in a hollow, becomes ice, rotates and plucks the back wall, abrades the floor, leaving an armchair-shaped hollow.

A fuller version picks up extra credit by naming conditions: snow collects in a north- or east-facing hollow in the northern hemisphere (shaded, so it lasts); it compacts into firn and then glacier ice; rotational movement and freeze-thaw on the back wall supply debris; abrasion deepens the floor; a rock lip forms where erosion is weaker; after the ice melts, a tarn may fill the hollow. Two corries back to back leave an **arete**; three or more leave a **pyramidal peak**.

## Model paragraph

*Explain the formation of an alluvial fan.* "Alluvial fans form at a mountain front in arid areas when rare but intense storms generate flash floods in narrow wadis. While confined, the water flows fast and carries coarse sediment. On reaching the open basin floor it spreads out, loses depth and velocity and deposits its load, coarsest material first near the apex and finer material further out. Repeated floods, with channels switching across the surface, build a fan-shaped cone. Where several fans merge along a range they form a bajada."

## Exam traps

- Mixing up glacial and periglacial processes.
- Naming a landform without a process sequence.
- Forgetting that desert landforms may be inherited from wetter climates.
- Giving chemical weathering as the main process in dry or cold settings.
- Saying a retreating glacier is flowing backwards.
- Calling till sorted: only meltwater deposits are sorted.
- Drawing a barchan with the horns pointing into the wind.
