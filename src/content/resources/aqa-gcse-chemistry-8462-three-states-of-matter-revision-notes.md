These notes condense **4.2.2.1 The three states of matter** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for
teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content. All of it is for both tiers except the
limitations of the simple particle model, which the specification marks (HT only). For full explanations, see
the [three states of matter study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-three-states-of-matter/); then
practise with the [three states of matter practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-three-states-of-matter-practice/).

## Core facts

- The three states of matter are **solid**, **liquid** and **gas**.
- **Melting** and **freezing** happen at the **melting point**.
- **Boiling** and **condensing** happen at the **boiling point**.
- In the simple model, particles are small **solid spheres**.
- The energy needed to change state depends on the **strength of the forces between the particles**.
- **Stronger forces mean a higher melting point and boiling point.**
- **Atoms themselves do not have the bulk properties** of materials.

## Changes of state

| Change | From | To | Energy | At |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melting | solid | liquid | taken in | melting point |
| Freezing | liquid | solid | given out | melting point |
| Boiling | liquid | gas | taken in | boiling point |
| Condensing | gas | liquid | given out | boiling point |

During a change of state the temperature stays constant. The energy supplied overcomes the forces between the
particles. It does not make the particles move faster.

## Particle model summary

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | Regular, touching | Touching, random | Far apart, random |
| Movement | Vibrate in place | Slide past each other | Fast, in all directions |
| Shape | Fixed | Takes shape of container | Fills container |
| Volume | Fixed | Fixed | Not fixed |

## Bonding decides the melting point and boiling point

| Type | What is overcome | Melting and boiling points |
|---|---|---|
| Simple molecular | Weak intermolecular forces | Low |
| Ionic | Many strong ionic bonds | High |
| Metallic | Many strong metallic bonds | Usually high |
| Giant covalent | Many strong covalent bonds | Very high |

Remember: for small molecules the **covalent bonds are not broken** when the substance melts or boils.

## Predicting a state from data

1. Below the melting point: **solid**.
2. At or above the melting point and below the boiling point: **liquid**.
3. Above the boiling point: **gas**.

Quote the comparison. Example: a substance melts at 98 °C and boils at 883 °C. At 25 °C it is a solid because 25 °C
is below 98 °C.

## Writing a good explanation

1. Name the **particles** (molecules, ions or atoms).
2. Name the **forces** that must be overcome.
3. Say how **strong** the forces are and how much **energy** is needed.
4. Link to the **melting point or boiling point** in the question.

Model sentence: "Sodium chloride has strong electrostatic forces between oppositely charged ions in a giant
lattice, so a lot of energy is needed to melt it. Methane has only weak intermolecular forces, so little energy
is needed."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| State of matter | Solid, liquid or gas |
| Melting point | Temperature at which a solid melts and a liquid freezes |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which a liquid boils and a gas condenses |
| Particle | An atom, molecule or ion |
| Intermolecular forces | Weak forces between molecules |
| Bulk property | A property of a large number of particles together, such as hardness |

## Higher tier only: limitations of the simple model

The model shows particles as solid spheres with no forces between them. The limitations are:

- **No forces.** Real particles attract each other. The model cannot show how strong the forces are, or explain
  why melting points differ.
- **All particles are spheres.** Real atoms, molecules and ions differ in size and shape.
- **The spheres are solid.** Real atoms have a nucleus and electrons and are mostly empty space.

## Exam traps

- **"The bonds break when water boils."** The covalent bonds in H₂O stay. The intermolecular forces are overcome.
- **"The particles expand."** Heating makes them move or vibrate more. It does not make them larger.
- **"Atoms of diamond are hard."** Hardness is a bulk property of the whole structure.
- **No comparison in a "predict" answer.** Always quote the temperature comparison.
- **Mixing up the two points.** Melting is solid to liquid. Boiling is liquid to gas.
- **Forgetting a flat section on a heating curve.** The temperature does not rise during a change of state.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the change of state from liquid to solid.
2. At which temperature do boiling and condensing happen?
3. Why does the temperature stay constant while a pure solid melts?
4. How are particles arranged in a gas?
5. Which has the higher boiling point: a substance with strong forces between particles or one with weak forces?
6. A substance melts at -39 °C and boils at 357 °C. What state is it in at 20 °C?
7. A substance melts at 114 °C and boils at 184 °C. What state is it in at 150 °C?
8. Which forces are overcome when chlorine boils?
9. Why is it wrong to say a single copper atom conducts electricity?
10. (HT only) State one limitation of representing a particle as a solid sphere.
11. (HT only) Why can the simple model not explain why one solid melts at a higher temperature than another?

### Answers

1. Freezing.
2. At the boiling point.
3. The energy supplied is used to overcome the forces between the particles, not to raise their kinetic energy.
4. Far apart and moving randomly and quickly.
5. The one with strong forces between its particles.
6. A liquid, because 20 °C is above -39 °C and below 357 °C.
7. A liquid, because 150 °C is above 114 °C and below 184 °C.
8. The weak intermolecular forces between the chlorine molecules.
9. Conductivity is a bulk property of many particles in a structure, not of a single atom.
10. A real atom is not a solid sphere; it has a nucleus and electrons and is mostly empty space.
11. The model has no forces, so it cannot show how strong the forces between particles are.
