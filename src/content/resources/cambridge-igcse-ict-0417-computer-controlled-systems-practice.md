## Section A: short answers

**1.** In a computer controlled system, which **one** of the following compares a sensor reading with a preset value? **[1]**

A: Sensor

B: Actuator

C: Microprocessor

D: Conveyor belt

**2.** State the purpose of an actuator. **[1]**

**3.** Name **two** sensors used by an autonomous car. **[2]**

**4.** Give **two** tasks carried out by robots in manufacturing. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is a disadvantage of computer control rather than human workers? **[1]**

A: The system can work without breaks

B: Quality is consistent

C: The system cannot easily deal with situations it was not programmed for

D: The system can work in dangerous conditions

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A biscuit factory uses a computer controlled production line to fill boxes with biscuits.

**(a)** Describe how the computer makes sure each box contains the correct weight of biscuits. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain what the system should do if a box is found to be underweight. **[2]**

**7.** An electronics company uses robots to place components onto circuit boards.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages of using robots for this task. **[4]**

**(b)** Describe **one** effect of the robots on the company's workforce. **[2]**

**8.** An autonomous car is travelling along a road when a vehicle ahead stops suddenly. Describe how the car's control system brings it safely to a stop. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why a computer controlled system may need an analogue-to-digital converter. **[2]**

**10.** A warehouse uses robots to lift and move heavy boxes. Give **two** reasons why this is safer than using people. **[2]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** A drinks company is replacing the workers on its bottling line with a computer controlled system. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this change. **[6]**

**12.** A city plans to introduce driverless trains on its metro line. Evaluate the use of computer control rather than human train drivers. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** To carry out a physical action, such as moving a motor or opening a valve, when signalled by the computer [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Radar [1]; camera [1] (also credit: lidar, ultrasonic sensor). **[2]**

**4.** Welding [1]; spray painting [1] (also credit: assembling parts, packing, lifting heavy items, inspecting products). **[2]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** The box sits on a weight (pressure) sensor as biscuits are added [1]; the reading is converted to digital and sent to the computer [1]; the computer compares the reading with the preset weight stored in its program [1]; when the preset weight is reached, the computer signals the actuator to stop adding biscuits and restarts the conveyor [1].

**(b)** The computer signals an actuator to push the box off the line [1] and records the fault, so that it can be refilled or a jam in the feeder can be investigated [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** Robots place components with consistent precision [1], so fewer boards are faulty and less material is wasted [1]. Robots work continuously without breaks [1], so more boards are produced each day [1].

**(b)** Some workers who placed components by hand may lose their jobs [1], while new jobs are created for technicians to program and maintain the robots, which need training [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Radar or lidar sensors detect the stopped vehicle and measure its distance and the closing speed [1]; the on-board computer compares the distance with a preset safe stopping distance [1]; it signals the actuators to release the accelerator and apply the brakes [1]; the sensors keep measuring and the computer adjusts the braking until the car stops safely (feedback) [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Many sensors produce analogue signals that vary continuously [1]; the computer can only process digital data, so the ADC converts the signal before it is processed [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Workers are not at risk of back injuries from lifting heavy loads [1]; people are kept away from moving loads that could fall on them [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Advantages: the system can run 24 hours a day without breaks, increasing output [1]; every bottle is filled to the same level, so quality is consistent and less drink is wasted [1]; wage costs fall over time [1]. Disadvantages: the system is expensive to buy and install [1]; if a sensor or conveyor fails, the whole line may stop until a technician fixes it [1]; workers lose their jobs or must retrain for technical roles [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: computer control reacts faster than a human to signals and obstacles [1]; trains can run closer together and more frequently, carrying more passengers [1]; there is no risk of driver tiredness or distraction [1]. Against: the system is very expensive to install on an existing line [1]; it may not cope with unexpected events, such as a person on the track, as well as a trained driver could [1]. Judgement: driverless trains are suitable for a busy metro if staff remain on board or in a control room to deal with emergencies [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing control without the comparison with a preset value.
- Saying the sensor stops the conveyor; the computer decides and an actuator acts.
- Giving general advantages without linking them to the product in the question.
- Leaving out a conclusion in an evaluate question.
- Naming a sensor without saying what it detects in the scenario, such as "a light sensor detects the box breaking the beam".
- Treating job losses as the only effect on workers; retraining and new technical roles also count.
- In questions on autonomous vehicles, describing their social effects (5.1) when the question asks how the control system works (6.3).
