## Section A

**1.** Name **one** igneous, **one** sedimentary and **one** metamorphic rock. **[3]**

**2.** Define permeability. **[1]**

**3.** Define a finite resource. **[1]**

**4.** Define fracking. **[2]**

**5.** State what blue hydrogen and green hydrogen are produced from. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how sedimentary rock forms and how it can become metamorphic rock, using terms from the rock cycle. **[5]**

**7.** Explain why limestone is permeable but shale is impermeable, and why this matters for fracking. **[3]**

**8.** Describe **three** sustainable management strategies for rocks, ores and minerals and discuss **one** limitation of recycling. **[7]**

**9.** A family wants to cut its home energy use. Describe **three** strategies from the syllabus that it could use. **[6]**

**10.** Describe how a ground source heat pump heats a building and give **one** benefit and **one** limitation. **[4]**

**11.** A government is deciding whether to allow fracking under farmland near a town. Discuss the benefits and limitations of fracking. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Igneous: granite or basalt [1]. Sedimentary: limestone, sandstone or shale [1]. Metamorphic: marble or slate [1].

**2.** The ability of water to pass through the pore spaces of rock and soil [1].

**3.** A natural resource that is used up at a faster rate than it is replaced [1].

**4.** The extraction of natural gas or petroleum [1] from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing [1].

**5.** Blue hydrogen: natural gas [1]. Green hydrogen: renewable resources (renewable electricity used to split water) [1].

**6.** Existing rock is broken down by **weathering** and removed by **erosion** [1]; particles are carried by **transportation** and laid down by **deposition / sedimentation** in layers [1]; layers are squeezed by **compaction** and stuck together by **cementation**, forming sedimentary rock such as limestone or shale [1]. If buried deeply and exposed to heat and pressure without melting, the rock is altered into metamorphic rock [1], e.g. limestone into marble or shale into slate [1].

**7.** Limestone, like most sedimentary rocks, has pore spaces that allow water to pass through [1]; shale's pores are too small or poorly connected, so it is impermeable [1]. Gas trapped in impermeable shale cannot flow to a well, so the rock must be fractured to release it [1].

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each: **reduce and reuse** — using less material and reusing items such as building stone or scrap metal parts slows extraction [1] [1]; **recycling** — recovering metals such as aluminium and copper from waste reduces the need to mine new ore [1] [1]; **increased efficiency of extraction** — recovering more mineral from each tonne of ore means less waste and land disturbance [1] [1]; **legislation and enforcement** — laws limiting extraction or requiring restoration, backed by inspection and fines [1] [1]; **use of alternative materials** — substituting a less scarce material [1] [1]. Limitation of recycling: it depends on accessibility and ease of collection and on education, so recycling rates may be low; some materials are costly or energy-intensive to separate [1].

**9.** Any three, 2 marks each: **reducing consumption** — switching off lights and appliances, lowering the thermostat [1] [1]; **insulation** — loft or cavity wall insulation or double glazing reduces heat loss, so less fuel is needed [1] [1]; **energy-efficient devices** — LED bulbs, efficient appliances, or an electric vehicle [1] [1]; **using new energy resources** — fitting an air or ground source heat pump [1] [1]; **battery storage** with solar panels [1] [1].

**10.** Pipes buried in the ground absorb heat, which the pump transfers into the building for space or water heating [1] [1]. Benefit: steady output because ground temperature changes little / no on-site emissions [1]. Limitation: high installation cost or needs land to dig trenches or boreholes [1].

**11.** Levels-based. **Benefits:** increases domestic gas supply and energy security, reducing imports [1]; creates jobs and tax revenue for the area [1]; gas burned instead of coal releases less carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide [1]. **Limitations:** fracking can trigger small earthquakes, a risk near a town [1]; fracking fluid or leaking wells could contaminate groundwater used by farms [1]; uses large volumes of water and produces wastewater [1]; methane leaks and continued fossil fuel use add to the enhanced greenhouse effect [1]. **Judgement** linked to the farmland and town in the scenario, e.g. allow only with strict monitoring, or invest instead in energy management strategies [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Is shale permeable?**
No. The syllabus classifies shale as one of the impermeable sedimentary rocks, while most sedimentary rocks such as limestone and sandstone are permeable.

**What is the difference between a finite resource and sustainable management?**
A finite resource is used up faster than it is replaced. Sustainable management uses strategies that meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming rocks outside the syllabus list instead of the named examples.
- Leaving "shale rock" or "hydraulic fracturing" out of the fracking definition.
- Describing a strategy without explaining how it saves energy.
- Giving only one side in a "discuss the benefits and limitations" question.
