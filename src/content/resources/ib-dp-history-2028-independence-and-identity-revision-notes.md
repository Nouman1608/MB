Revision notes for the focused study **Independence and identity** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Two examples, both required: **the Haitian Revolution (about 1780 to 1825)** and **Kenyan independence (1945 to 1978)**. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-and-identity/) for detail and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-and-identity-practice/) for a paper 1 style set.

## Paper 1 in one box

| Question | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| First | Explain how the content of sources A and B can be used to answer the inquiry question | 6 |
| Second | Analyse how the context of source C influences how it can be used | 6 |
| Third | Examine how the perspectives in all the sources can be used | 12 |

Total 24 marks in 1 hour 15 minutes. The grounding content below gives the context to read sources with understanding.

## The three inquiry questions

1. What prompted the emergence of the independence movement?
2. How was independence achieved?
3. How, and with what challenges, was a new identity formed?

## Haiti (about 1780 to 1825)

**1. Emergence**

- French colonial rule: Saint-Domingue, the richest French colony, built on sugar and coffee, with a great majority of enslaved people and a rigid hierarchy of planters, poorer whites and free people of colour.
- Slave resistance, maroonage (escape and the formation of communities of escaped people) and Vodou (a religion that gave a shared identity and organisation).
- The French Revolution (1789): rights language, divisions among colonists, weakened authority.

**2. Achievement**

- War for freedom: the rising of August 1791 in the north; abolition of slavery proclaimed locally in 1793 and decreed by France in 1794.
- Independence struggle (1794 to 1804): Toussaint Louverture rose to command; Leclerc's French expedition of 1802; Toussaint seized and deported; resistance under Dessalines; victory at Vertières in November 1803.
- Roles: Toussaint (military and political leadership, governor for life under the 1801 constitution); Dessalines (declared independence on 1 January 1804).

**3. New identity and challenges**

- The 1801 constitution (slavery abolished for ever, but still part of France) and the 1804 Declaration of Independence (break with France, name Haiti).
- Continuing inequalities from the colonial period: plantation economy, divisions of colour and class.
- The independence debt of 1825: 150 million francs demanded by France as the price of recognition, reduced to 90 million in 1838.

## Kenya (1945 to 1978)

**1. Emergence**

- British colonial rule: land taken by settlers (the White Highlands), taxes, restrictions on African political activity.
- The Kenya African Union (KAU), formed 1944, led by Kenyatta from 1947.
- The Second World War: African soldiers' experience, post-war frustration, global decolonisation.

**2. Achievement**

- The Mau Mau Uprising (1952 to 1960): guerrilla war, mainly Kikuyu, Embu and Meru; state of emergency from October 1952; detention camps and villagisation; many deaths.
- Lancaster House Conferences (1960 and 1962), elections, self-government in June 1963 and independence on 12 December 1963; competition between KANU and KADU.
- Kenyatta: arrested in 1952, tried at Kapenguria, released in 1961, prime minister in 1963 and president in 1964.

**3. New identity and challenges**

- Independence constitution (1963) with regional features, soon centralised; one-party politics grew after 1966 to 1969, and was made legal in 1982.
- Education and cultural diversity: expanding schools and the harambee movement; Swahili promoted alongside English.
- Land reform: transfer of former settler land, with disputes over who benefited.

## Comparing the two examples

| Point | Haiti | Kenya |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial power | France | Britain |
| Nature of resistance | Slave rebellion and war, ending in independence by arms | Guerrilla war plus negotiation and constitutional politics |
| Date of independence | 1804 | 1963 |
| Leaders | Toussaint, Dessalines | Kenyatta |
| Post-independence problem | Debt and inequality | Land, centralisation, one-party rule |

## Skills checklist

**Question 1.** Use both sources. For each, pick specific points and say how they help the inquiry question. One source only gives a maximum of 3.

**Question 2.** Use the origin, date, purpose and audience of source C, and link each to how the source can be used.

**Question 3.** State each source's perspective. Compare similarities and differences. Use all three sources for the top markband. Two sources caps the mark at 9.

## Worked example: a question 3 opening

Inquiry question: How was independence achieved?

Source A (constructed for practice) is a French colonial official's report calling the rising a "criminal revolt". Source B (constructed for practice) is a proclamation by a Haitian general calling on citizens to "defend liberty".

"The perspectives differ sharply. Source A, written by an official of the colonial power, presents the rising as criminal, so it values order and French authority. Source B, written by a general of the rebel side, presents it as a defence of liberty, so it values freedom and legitimacy. Both can be used to answer the question: A shows how the colonial power understood and tried to suppress the movement, while B shows how the leaders mobilised support. Together they show that independence was won through armed struggle and through a struggle over how the war was described."

## Linking to concepts

- **Cause and consequence:** conditions (slavery, land theft) and actors (Toussaint, Kenyatta).
- **Continuity and change:** political change with economic continuity.
- **Perspectives:** colonial officials, settlers, enslaved people, Mau Mau fighters, loyalists.
- **Significance:** Haiti as the only successful revolt of enslaved people to create a state; Kenya within African decolonisation.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the 1801 constitution (Toussaint) and the 1804 declaration (Dessalines).
- Saying Haiti paid the debt to Britain (it was to France).
- Treating Mau Mau as the whole of the independence movement.
- Describing context without linking it to use.

## Self-test

1. Who were the leaders of the Haitian independence struggle named in the syllabus?
2. What does maroonage mean?
3. When was the Declaration of Independence of Haiti?
4. Name one cause of the Mau Mau Uprising.
5. What were the Lancaster House Conferences?
6. Who was the first president of independent Kenya?
7. What was the independence debt?

**Answers.**

1. Toussaint Louverture and Jean-Jacques Dessalines.
2. Flight from slavery and the formation of communities of escaped people.
3. 1 January 1804.
4. Any one: loss of land to settlers; taxation and restrictions; post-war frustration; failure of the KAU's peaceful demands.
5. Conferences in London (1960 and 1962) that set the constitutional path to majority rule and independence.
6. Jomo Kenyatta (prime minister from 1963 and president from 1964).
7. The sum Haiti agreed in 1825 to pay France in return for recognition, 150 million francs reduced to 90 million in 1838.
