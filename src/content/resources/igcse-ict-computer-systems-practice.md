## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between hardware and software, giving an example of each. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between system software and application software, giving an example of each. **[4]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain the difference between RAM and ROM, giving **three** points of comparison. **[6]**

**4.** Explain the purpose of each and give a suitable use:

**(a)** magnetic hard disk **[2]**
**(b)** solid state drive **[2]**
**(c)** optical disc **[2]**

**5.** A school is choosing input devices.

**(a)** Recommend a suitable device for registering student attendance and justify your choice. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend a suitable device for a student with limited hand mobility and justify it. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **four** functions of an operating system. **[8]**

**7.** Explain the difference between a LAN and a WAN, and give **two** benefits of networking computers in a school. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. Hardware** is the **physical components of a computer system**, e.g. a keyboard [1]. **Software** is the **set of instructions and programs that tell the hardware what to do**, e.g. a word processor [1].

**2. System software** **controls and manages the computer and its hardware**, e.g. the operating system or a device driver [1] [1]. **Application software** allows the **user to carry out a specific task**, e.g. a spreadsheet or web browser [1] [1].

**3. Volatility** — RAM is **volatile and loses its contents when power is removed**; ROM is **non-volatile and retains its contents** [1] [1]. **Read/write** — RAM can be **read from and written to**; ROM is **read-only** in normal operation [1] [1]. **Purpose** — RAM holds the **programs and data currently in use**, so it changes constantly; ROM holds the **start-up instructions (the bootstrap or BIOS)**, which never change [1] [1]. (Also accept: RAM is much larger.)

**4. (a)** Uses **magnetised platters**; provides **large capacity at low cost per gigabyte** [1], suited to **bulk storage of files and backups** where speed is less critical [1].
**(b)** Uses **flash memory with no moving parts**; **much faster, silent and shock-resistant** [1], suited to **laptops and the boot drive of a machine that needs to start quickly** [1].
**(c)** Uses a **laser to read pits and lands** on a disc [1]; suited to **distributing software, films or music**, since discs are cheap to mass-produce and easy to transport [1].

**5. (a)** An **RFID card reader or fingerprint scanner** [1]; it registers each student **automatically and in seconds** without a teacher entering data manually [1], which **saves lesson time and avoids transcription errors** — a biometric reader also prevents students registering for each other [1].
**(b)** A **touch screen, eye-tracking system or voice recognition (microphone)** [1]; it removes the need for **fine motor control of a mouse or keyboard** [1], allowing the student to work **independently at the same pace as the class** [1].

**6.** Any four, 2 marks each: **memory management** — allocating RAM to programs and ensuring they do not interfere with one another [1] [1]; **file management** — organising files into directories and controlling saving, opening and deletion [1] [1]; **process/CPU management** — scheduling which process uses the processor and when [1] [1]; **peripheral and device management** — communicating with hardware through drivers [1] [1]; **user interface** — providing the means by which the user interacts with the machine [1] [1]; **security** — managing user accounts, passwords and access rights [1] [1].

**7.** A **LAN covers a small geographical area such as one site**, using cabling or Wi-Fi owned by the organisation [1]; a **WAN covers a wide area, often between cities or countries**, and usually uses third-party communication links [1].
Benefits: **files and printers can be shared**, avoiding duplicated hardware and letting students access their work from any machine [1] [1]; **software and updates can be installed and managed centrally**, and **backups taken from one place**, which saves technician time and improves security [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying ROM is "permanent memory" without explaining what it stores.
- Recommending an input device without justifying it for the specific user.
- Listing operating system functions without explaining them.
- Confusing a LAN with the internet.
