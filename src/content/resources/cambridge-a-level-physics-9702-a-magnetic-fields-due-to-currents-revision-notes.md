Subtopic 20.4 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) asks you to sketch the field patterns of a long straight wire, a flat circular coil and a long solenoid, to understand that a ferrous core increases the field of a solenoid, and to explain the origin and direction of the forces between current-carrying conductors. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Magnetic fields due to currents study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-magnetic-fields-due-to-currents/); to apply the ideas, try the [Magnetic fields due to currents practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-magnetic-fields-due-to-currents-practice/).

## Grip rules (right hand)

- **Straight wire**: thumb along current → fingers curl in the field direction.
- **Coil or solenoid**: fingers curl with the current → thumb points along the field inside, towards the N end.
- **End check**: current anticlockwise as you look at an end → N pole; clockwise → S pole.
- Out of the page (•) → anticlockwise circles; into the page (×) → clockwise circles.

## The three patterns

| Conductor | Pattern | Sketch must show |
|---|---|---|
| Long straight wire | Concentric circles in planes ⊥ wire | Arrows, same sense; spacing increasing outwards |
| Flat circular coil | Loops round each side; straight along the axis near the centre | Dot on one side, cross on the other; symmetry; straight central line |
| Long solenoid | Uniform inside (parallel, equally spaced); bar-magnet-like outside | Straight parallel lines inside; loops outside; N and S ends consistent with current |

What makes each field stronger:

- Wire: larger current; closer to the wire.
- Solenoid: larger current; more turns per unit length; a ferrous core.

## Ferrous core

- Ferrous = containing iron.
- The solenoid's field magnetises the core; the core's field adds to it → B greatly increased for the same current.
- Soft iron: strongly magnetised while current flows, loses magnetism when it stops → electromagnets, relays.
- Copper and aluminium cores: no increase.

## Forces between conductors

**Origin**: each conductor lies in the magnetic field of the other, and a current in a magnetic field feels a force (F = BIL).

| Currents | Force | Field between the wires |
|---|---|---|
| Same direction | Attract | Fields oppose → weak (neutral line if currents equal) |
| Opposite directions | Repel | Fields add → strong |

- Forces are **equal and opposite** (Newton's third law), even for unequal currents.
- Force per unit length on wire 2 = (field of wire 1 at wire 2) × I₂.
- Turns of a solenoid carry current in the same direction → they attract → a loose coil shortens.

## Direction method

1. Right-hand grip on wire 1 → field direction at wire 2.
2. Left-hand rule on wire 2 → force on wire 2.
3. Force on wire 1 is opposite.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right-hand grip rule | Relates current direction to the circulation of its magnetic field |
| Flat circular coil | Single loop or short coil whose length is small compared with its radius |
| Solenoid | Long coil of many turns; uniform field inside |
| Ferrous core | Iron-based core that increases the flux density in a solenoid |
| Soft iron | Iron that magnetises and demagnetises easily |
| Catapult field | Combined field pattern explaining which way a conductor is pushed |

## Exam traps

- Using the left hand for the field pattern.
- Equally spaced circles around a wire.
- Curved or unevenly spaced lines inside a solenoid.
- Circular field lines at the centre of a flat coil.
- Saying any metal core strengthens the field.
- Saying the wire with the larger current feels the larger force.
- Explaining attraction without stating that each wire is in the field of the other.

## Self-test

1. Current flows into the page in a straight wire. Are the field lines clockwise or anticlockwise as seen on the page?
2. Describe the spacing of field lines around a long straight wire.
3. Describe the field inside a long solenoid.
4. Looking at one end of a solenoid, the current flows clockwise. Is that end N or S?
5. Describe the field lines near the centre of a flat circular coil.
6. State the effect of putting a ferrous core in a solenoid.
7. Why is soft iron used for the core of an electromagnet in a relay?
8. Two parallel wires carry currents in opposite directions. Do they attract or repel?
9. Explain the origin of the force between two parallel current-carrying wires.
10. Wire X carries 6.0 A and wire Y carries 2.0 A, parallel. How do the forces on them compare?
11. The flux density due to wire X at wire Y is 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ T and Y carries 2.0 A. Find the force per metre on Y.
12. Why does a loosely wound spring carrying a large current shorten?

**Answers:** 1. Clockwise. 2. Further apart with increasing distance from the wire (field weaker). 3. Strong, uniform: straight, parallel, equally spaced lines along the axis. 4. S. 5. Nearly straight and parallel, along the axis, at right angles to the plane of the coil. 6. The flux density increases greatly for the same current. 7. It is strongly magnetised while the current flows and loses its magnetism when the current is switched off. 8. Repel. 9. Each wire produces a magnetic field, and each wire carries a current in the field of the other, so each experiences a force (F = BIL). 10. Equal in size and opposite in direction. 11. 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 2.0 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ N m⁻¹. 12. Adjacent turns carry current in the same direction, so they attract each other.
