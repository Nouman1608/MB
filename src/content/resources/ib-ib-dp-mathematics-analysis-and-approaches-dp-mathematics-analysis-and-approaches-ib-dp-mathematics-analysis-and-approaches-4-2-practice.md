This set practises syllabus section 4.2 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (SL and HL):
frequency tables, histograms, cumulative frequency and box and whisker diagrams. Learn the methods first
with the [4.2 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-2/)
and the [4.2 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-2-revision-notes/).
All data are invented. Where a question would normally be answered from a graph, the answers give the
values found by linear interpolation; readings from a smooth curve may differ slightly.

## Section A

**1.** Lengths of leaves are measured to the nearest centimetre. One class is printed as 20–24.

**(a)** Write down the class boundaries. **[1]**
**(b)** Write down the class width and the mid-interval value. **[2]**

**2.** A histogram with equal class widths shows the following frequencies.

| x | 0 ≤ x < 10 | 10 ≤ x < 20 | 20 ≤ x < 30 | 30 ≤ x < 40 | 40 ≤ x < 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | 8 | 12 | 5 | 2 |

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the number of values that are at least 30. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the exact range of the data cannot be found. **[1]**

**3.** A cumulative frequency graph represents the scores of 240 students. State the cumulative frequency
at which you would read

**(a)** the upper quartile; **[1]**
**(b)** the 85th percentile. **[1]**

**4.** A data set has minimum 3, Q₁ = 10, median 14, Q₃ = 18 and maximum 31. The two largest values are
27 and 31.

**(a)** Show that 31 is an outlier. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the upper whisker of the box plot should end. **[1]**

**5.** A box plot has minimum 41, Q₁ = 50, median 54, Q₃ = 58 and maximum 66.

**(a)** Find the range and the interquartile range. **[2]**
**(b)** Comment on whether the data may be normally distributed. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The times, t minutes, taken by 120 students to complete a puzzle are shown.

| Time t (min) | 0 ≤ t < 5 | 5 ≤ t < 10 | 10 ≤ t < 15 | 15 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 25 | 25 ≤ t < 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 22 | 41 | 30 | 13 | 6 |

**(a)** Construct a cumulative frequency table. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the points you would plot for a cumulative frequency graph. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the median and the interquartile range. **[4]**
**(d)** Estimate the number of students who took more than 22 minutes. **[2]**
**(e)** Estimate the 90th percentile. **[2]**

**7.** The lifetimes, in hours, of batteries from two factories are summarised.

| | Minimum | Q₁ | Median | Q₃ | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Factory P | 31 | 38 | 42 | 46 | 52 |
| Factory Q | 25 | 40 | 47 | 50 | 54 |

Compare the lifetimes of the batteries from the two factories. **[4]**

**8.** The numbers of goals scored in 25 football matches are shown.

| Goals | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 7 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

**(a)** Find the median and the quartiles. **[3]**
**(b)** Determine whether there are any outliers. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the five values that the box plot shows, including the end of each whisker. **[2]**

**9.** The masses of 100 parcels are measured to the nearest kg.

| Mass (kg) | 1–5 | 6–10 | 11–15 | 16–20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 34 | 41 | 18 | 7 |

**(a)** Write down the class boundaries you would use to draw a histogram of these data. **[1]**
**(b)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(c)** State the largest possible range of the masses. **[1]**

**10.** The cumulative frequencies of the masses, w kg, of 100 dogs are:

| w | < 50 | < 60 | < 70 | < 80 | < 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 12 | 35 | 70 | 92 | 100 |

All the dogs have masses of at least 40 kg.

**(a)** Construct the frequency table. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the number of dogs with masses between 55 kg and 75 kg. **[3]**

**11.** A teacher draws box plots of two sets of test scores and says: "Both sets have the same range, so the
students performed equally consistently." Explain why this conclusion may be wrong. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 19.5 and 24.5 [1].
**(b)** Width 5 [1]; mid-interval value 22 [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** 20 ≤ x < 30 [1].
**(b)** 5 + 2 = 7 [1].
**(c)** Individual values are hidden inside the classes, so the exact minimum and maximum are unknown [1].
**[3]**

**3. (a)** 180 [1].
**(b)** 0.85 × 240 = 204 [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** IQR = 8 and 1.5 × 8 = 12 [1]. Q₃ + 12 = 30, and 31 > 30 [1].
**(b)** At 27, the largest value that is not an outlier [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Range = 25 [1]; IQR = 8 [1].
**(b)** The median is in the centre of the box (4 from each quartile) and the whiskers are of similar
length (9 and 8) [1], so the data are roughly symmetric and may be normally distributed [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** 8, 30, 71, 101, 114, 120, each paired with its class [2].
**(b)** (0, 0), (5, 8), (10, 30), (15, 71), (20, 101), (25, 114), (30, 120) [2]: upper boundaries, with
the starting point.
**(c)** Median at 60: 10 + (60 − 30)/41 × 5 ≈ 13.7 min [1]. Q₁ at 30: 10 min [1]. Q₃ at 90:
15 + (90 − 71)/30 × 5 ≈ 18.2 min [1]. IQR ≈ 8.2 min [1].
**(d)** CF at 22 ≈ 101 + 13 × 2/5 = 106.2 [1], so about 120 − 106 = 14 students [1].
**(e)** 0.9 × 120 = 108 [1]; 20 + (108 − 101)/13 × 5 ≈ 22.7 min [1]. **[12]**

**7.** Centre: the median for Q (47 h) is higher than for P (42 h), so Q's batteries typically last longer
[1] [1]. Spread: the IQRs are P 8 h and Q 10 h, so Q's lifetimes are slightly more variable in the middle
half [1]; Q's range (29 h) is also larger than P's (21 h), mostly because of a long lower whisker, so a few
of Q's batteries fail early [1]. **[4]**
*Marking note:* each mark needs a value or comparison and its meaning in context.

**8. (a)** Cumulative frequencies 4, 11, 17, 21, 23, 24, 25. Median = 13th value = 2 [1]. Q₁ = mean of 6th
and 7th values = 1 [1]. Q₃ = mean of 19th and 20th values = 3 [1].
**(b)** IQR = 2, so boundaries are 1 − 3 = −2 and 3 + 3 = 6 [1]. 7 > 6, so 7 is an outlier; there are no
low outliers [1].
**(c)** Whisker from 0, Q₁ = 1, median 2, Q₃ = 3, whisker to 5 [1], with 7 marked separately as an outlier
[1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** 0.5, 5.5, 10.5, 15.5, 20.5 [1].
**(b)** 6–10 kg [1].
**(c)** 20.5 − 0.5 = 20 kg [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 40 ≤ w < 50: 12; 50 ≤ w < 60: 23; 60 ≤ w < 70: 35 [1]; 70 ≤ w < 80: 22; 80 ≤ w < 90: 8 [1].
**(b)** CF at 55 ≈ 12 + 23/2 = 23.5 [1]; CF at 75 ≈ 70 + 22/2 = 81 [1]; about 81 − 23.5 ≈ 58 dogs [1].
**[5]**

**11.** The range depends only on the two extreme scores, which may be outliers [1]. The middle halves of the
two sets could have very different IQRs, so consistency should be judged using the IQR [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting the cumulative frequency points at mid-interval values in question 6(b).
- Answering question 6(d) with the "less than 22" reading.
- Using printed limits instead of boundaries in questions 1 and 9.
- Extending the whisker to the outlier in questions 4 and 8.
- Listing values in question 7 without saying what they show about the batteries.
