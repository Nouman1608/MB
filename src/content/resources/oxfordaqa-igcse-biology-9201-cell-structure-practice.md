These questions cover section **3.1.1 Cell structure** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201)
specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All data are
invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 7 to 9.

Learn the content first in the [Cell structure study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-cell-structure/)
and the [Organisation revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation-revision-notes/).
For mixed questions on the whole of Topic 1, use the [Organisation practice questions](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-biology-organisation-practice/).

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Name the part of an animal cell that controls the activities of the cell. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the part of an animal cell where protein synthesis occurs. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the part of an animal cell where most energy is released in respiration. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** parts found in plant cells that are not found in animal cells. **[2]**

**3.**

**(a)** Describe **one** way in which the genes of a bacterial cell are arranged differently from those of an animal cell. **[1]**
**(b)** What name is given to the circular structures that carry some bacterial genes? **[1]**

**4.** A student writes: "The cell wall controls what goes into and out of a plant cell." Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

**5.** A root cell from a potato plant growing underground has no chloroplasts. Explain why this does not mean the cell is an animal cell. **[2]**

**6.** Cell Y has a nucleus, cytoplasm, a cell membrane, a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a permanent vacuole.

**(a)** State whether cell Y is from an animal, a plant or a bacterium. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence from the description. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A photograph of a plant cell is 55 mm wide. The magnification is ×1100.

**(a)** Calculate the real width of the cell in millimetres. **[2]**
**(b)** Give your answer to part (a) in micrometres (μm). 1 mm = 1000 μm. **[1]**

**8.** A student draws a cell from a leaf. The drawing is 72 mm long. The real cell is 0.09 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows the number of mitochondria counted in four cells of each of two types: a flight muscle cell from a bat (cell M) and a skin cell (cell S).

| Cell | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell M | 310 | 290 | 350 | 330 |
| Cell S | 95 | 110 | 100 | 105 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of mitochondria in each cell type. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two means. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** way to make the comparison more reliable. **[1]**

**10.** A cell from a gland that releases digestive enzymes has a very large number of ribosomes and many mitochondria. Explain how each of these features helps the cell to do its job. **[3]**

**11.** Compare a plant cell and a bacterial cell. **[4]**

**12.** Explain how the structure of each of these cells is suited to its function. **[6]**

**(a)** A palisade cell in a leaf. **[2]**
**(b)** A root hair cell. **[2]**
**(c)** A muscle cell in the wall of the heart. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Nucleus [1]
**(b)** Ribosomes [1]
**(c)** Mitochondria [1]

**2.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: cell wall (made of cellulose); chloroplasts; permanent vacuole (filled with cell sap)

**3. (a)** The genes are not in a distinct nucleus (they are in the cytoplasm) [1]
**(b)** Plasmids [1]

**4.** The cell wall is made of cellulose and strengthens the cell [1]; it is the cell membrane that controls the passage of substances into and out of the cell [1]

**5.** Chloroplasts are only "often" present in plant cells; a root cell is underground where there is no light for photosynthesis [1]; the cell still has other plant features such as a cellulose cell wall and a permanent vacuole, so it is a plant cell [1]

**6. (a)** Plant [1]
**(b)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: it has a cellulose cell wall; it has chloroplasts; it has a permanent vacuole; it has a nucleus (so it is not a bacterium)

**7. (a)** real width = image size ÷ magnification = 55 ÷ 1100 [1] = **0.05 mm** [1]
**(b)** 0.05 × 1000 = **50 μm** [1]

**8.** magnification = image size ÷ real size = 72 ÷ 0.09 [1] = **×800** [1]

**9. (a)** Cell M: (310 + 290 + 350 + 330) ÷ 4 = 1280 ÷ 4 = **320** [1]; cell S: (95 + 110 + 100 + 105) ÷ 4 = 410 ÷ 4 = **102.5** [1]
**(b)** Mitochondria are where most energy is released in respiration [1]; flight muscle cells need much more energy for contraction than skin cells, so they have more mitochondria [1]
**(c)** Count more cells of each type (or count cells from more than one bat or more than one skin sample) [1]

**10.** Ribosomes are where protein synthesis occurs, and enzymes are proteins, so many ribosomes allow the cell to make a lot of enzyme [1]; mitochondria are where most energy is released in respiration [1]; making and releasing a lot of protein needs energy, so many mitochondria supply it [1]

**11.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: both have a cell wall; plant cells have a nucleus, whereas bacterial cells have no distinct nucleus; bacterial genes may be in plasmids, whereas plant cells do not have plasmids; plant cells have a permanent vacuole, whereas the bacterial cell described in the specification does not; plant cells often have chloroplasts, whereas bacterial cells do not; both have cytoplasm and a cell membrane; the plant cell wall is made of cellulose; plant cells are eukaryotic, whereas bacterial cells are prokaryotic

**12. (a)** Many chloroplasts [1]; to absorb light energy to make food (photosynthesis) [1]
**(b)** A long, thin extension from the cell gives a large surface area [1]; for absorbing water and ions from the soil [1]
**(c)** Many mitochondria [1]; so a lot of energy is released in respiration for contraction [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying mitochondria "produce" energy instead of "release" or "transfer" it.
- Giving the cell wall the job of controlling what enters the cell.
- Writing that all plant cells have chloroplasts.
- Giving bacterial cells a nucleus.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to micrometres, or dividing real size by image size.
- Naming a feature of a specialised cell without giving the reason it helps.
