These notes condense section 3.01, Quantities and Units in Mechanics, of OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240), specification Version 3.1 (August 2026): references 3.01a and 3.01b (Stage 1, shared with AS Level) and 3.01c (Stage 2, A Level only). For explanations and worked examples, read the [quantities and units study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-mathematics-h240-quantities-and-units-in-mechanics-ocr-alevel-maths/), then try the [quantities and units practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-mathematics-h240-quantities-and-units-in-mechanics-ocr-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Core facts

- The three S.I. base quantities in H240 mechanics are **length (m)**, **time (s)** and **mass (kg)**.
- They are **mutually independent**: none can be expressed using the other two.
- Every other mechanics unit is a product of powers of m, s and kg.
- Derived units named in the specification: velocity **m s⁻¹**, acceleration **m s⁻²**, force **N**, weight **N**, and (A Level only) moment **N m**.
- m/s and m s⁻¹ are both acceptable; so are m/s² and m s⁻².
- Weight W = mg is a force, in newtons. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless the question gives another value; g varies with location.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base quantity | A quantity chosen as fundamental and not defined from others: length, time, mass |
| Derived quantity | A quantity defined by an equation from base quantities, such as velocity or force |
| Mutually independent | None of the base quantities can be written in terms of the others |
| Newton (N) | The force giving 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻²; 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻² |
| Mass | The amount of matter in a body, in kg; the same everywhere |
| Weight | The gravitational force on a body, W = mg, in N; depends on g |
| Moment | Force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action, in N m (A Level only) |
| Unit check | Comparing the units of every term in an equation, which must all match |

## Units in base form

| Quantity | Unit | Base units |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement, distance | m | m |
| Velocity, speed | m s⁻¹ | m s⁻¹ |
| Acceleration | m s⁻² | m s⁻² |
| Force, weight, tension, friction, reaction | N | kg m s⁻² |
| Moment (A Level only) | N m | kg m² s⁻² |

## Conversions to know

| Change | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ | 1/3.6 |
| m s⁻¹ to km h⁻¹ | 3.6 |
| cm to m | 0.01 |
| mm to m | 0.001 |
| cm² to m² | 0.0001 |
| g to kg | 0.001 |
| tonnes to kg | 1000 |
| minutes to s | 60 |
| hours to s | 3600 |

Squared and cubed units take the square or cube of the factor: 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³.

## Method: any calculation with units

1. Write each given quantity in S.I. units on its own line.
2. Substitute into the formula.
3. Keep full calculator values until the end.
4. Round as the paper asks (normally 3 significant figures for non-exact answers).
5. Attach the S.I. unit to the final answer.

## Method: checking a formula

1. Replace each symbol with its unit.
2. Simplify each term to powers of m, s and kg.
3. Every term that is added, subtracted or equated must give the same result.
4. Pure numbers (2, ½, π) have no unit and cannot be checked this way.

## Worked reminders

**Speed.** 72 km h⁻¹ = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m s⁻¹.

**Weight.** A 2.5 tonne van: m = 2500 kg, W = 2500 × 9.8 = 24 500 N.

**Moment (A Level only).** 15 N at 60 cm from a pivot, at right angles: 15 × 0.6 = 9 N m.

**Unit of a constant.** In R = kv², k = R/v² has unit N ÷ m² s⁻² = kg m s⁻² ÷ m² s⁻² = kg m⁻¹.

**Formula check.** v² = u² + 2as: every term has unit m² s⁻², so it is consistent.

## Exam traps

- Weight given in kg. Weight is in N; mass is in kg.
- Grams left as grams in F = ma: a 200 g particle is 0.2 kg.
- km h⁻¹ substituted directly into a suvat formula.
- Squaring 40 cm as 0.4² = 0.16 m² is fine; writing 40 cm² as 0.4 m² is not (it is 0.004 m²).
- Moment written in N or in J; the moment unit is N m.
- A final answer with no unit, or the unit of a different quantity.
- Rounding a converted speed early (13.9 instead of 13.888...) and carrying the error.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three S.I. base quantities used in H240 mechanics and their units.
2. What does "mutually independent" mean for the base quantities?
3. Write the newton in base units.
4. Convert 90 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
5. Find the weight of a 750 g mass, using g = 9.8 m s⁻².
6. A body has weight 196 N on Earth. Find its mass.
7. Write the moment unit in base units.
8. Convert 35 cm s⁻² to m s⁻².
9. In a model, a resistance is R = cv, where v is a speed. Find the S.I. unit of c.
10. Show that s = ut + ½at² is consistent in units.
11. A force of 12 N acts at right angles to a spanner, 250 mm from the nut. Find its moment about the nut.
12. Explain why a unit check cannot show that s = ut + at² is wrong.

### Answers

1. Length in metres (m), time in seconds (s), mass in kilograms (kg).
2. None of them can be expressed in terms of the other two.
3. **1 N = 1 kg m s⁻²**, from F = ma.
4. 90 ÷ 3.6 = **25 m s⁻¹**.
5. 0.75 × 9.8 = **7.35 N**.
6. m = W ÷ g = 196 ÷ 9.8 = **20 kg**.
7. N m = kg m s⁻² × m = **kg m² s⁻²**.
8. 35 ÷ 100 = **0.35 m s⁻²**.
9. c = R/v: N ÷ m s⁻¹ = **N s m⁻¹**, which is **kg s⁻¹** in base units.
10. ut: m s⁻¹ × s = m. ½at²: m s⁻² × s² = m. s is in m. All terms are lengths, so it is consistent.
11. 250 mm = 0.25 m, so the moment is 12 × 0.25 = **3 N m**.
12. The only difference is the factor ½, which has no unit. Both versions pass the check, so the check cannot find the error.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the kilogram or the gram the base unit of mass?**
The kilogram. It is the S.I. base unit even though its name contains a prefix, so convert grams and tonnes to kilograms before using F = ma or W = mg.

**Can I write m/s instead of m s⁻¹?**
Yes. The H240 specification lists both forms for velocity and acceleration. Keep to one form within an answer.

**Is the unit for moment on the AS paper?**
No. Reference 3.01c, the N m unit for moment, is Stage 2 content and so belongs to the full A Level (H240) only.
