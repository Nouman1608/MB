H432 is the full A level, and the longer problems, Questions 8 to 10, combine elemental analysis, mass spectra and IR spectra.

**Data for all questions.** Relative atomic masses: carbon 12.0, hydrogen 1.0, oxygen 16.0. Use these IR absorptions:

| Bond | Wavenumber / cm⁻¹ |
|---|---|
| O–H (alcohol) | 3200–3600, broad |
| O–H (carboxylic acid) | 2500–3300, very broad |
| C–H | 2850–3100 |
| C=O | 1630–1820 |
| C=C | 1620–1680 |
| C–O | 1000–1300 |

## Questions

**1.** Infrared spectroscopy.

**(a)** State what happens to a covalent bond when it absorbs infrared radiation of a matching frequency. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why almost every organic compound absorbs near 3000 cm⁻¹, and why this is not used to identify a functional group. **[2]**

**2.** Carbon dioxide, water vapour and methane in the atmosphere all absorb infrared radiation.

**(a)** For each gas, name the bond responsible. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how this absorption is linked to global warming. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe one resulting change in energy use. **[1]**

**3.** IR absorption is used in breathalysers and roadside monitors.

**(a)** Explain how an IR breathalyser measures the concentration of ethanol in a breath sample. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why such an instrument measures absorption at more than one wavenumber. **[1]**
**(c)** Name two gases from car emissions that can be monitored by IR absorption. **[1]**

**4.** A colourless liquid is cyclopentanol, cyclopentanone or cyclopentanecarboxylic acid. Its IR spectrum has absorptions at 2880–2970 cm⁻¹ and a strong absorption at 1745 cm⁻¹. There is no broad absorption anywhere between 2500 and 3600 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the liquid. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two absorptions that together would identify cyclopentanecarboxylic acid. **[2]**

**5.** A student oxidises 2-methylbutan-1-ol using acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Explain what the product's IR spectrum would show to confirm oxidation, and how it would tell 2-methylbutanal from 2-methylbutanoic acid. **[3]**

**6.** Predict the IR spectrum of pent-3-en-2-one, CH₃COCH=CHCH₃. Give two expected absorptions with ranges, and one you would not expect. **[3]**

**7.** A student writes: "The molecular ion peak is always the last peak on the right of a mass spectrum, and its m/z value is the relative molecular mass." Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

**8.** Hydrocarbon H contains 83.7% carbon and 16.3% hydrogen by mass. Its mass spectrum has peaks at m/z 86 (with a very small peak at 87), 71, 43 (the tallest peak) and 15. There are no peaks at m/z 29 or 57.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of H. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of H. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the ions responsible for the peaks at m/z 71 and 43. **[2]**
**(d)** Write an equation showing how the ion at m/z 43 forms from the molecular ion. **[1]**
**(e)** Use the missing peaks to suggest the structure and name of H. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**9.** Alcohol D has the molecular formula C₅H₁₂O. Its IR spectrum shows a broad absorption at 3350 cm⁻¹, absorptions at 2870–2960 cm⁻¹ and 1040 cm⁻¹, and nothing between 1630 and 1820 cm⁻¹. Its mass spectrum has peaks at m/z 88 (very small), 73, 57 (the tallest), 31 and 15, with no peaks at 29 or 43.

**(a)** Explain how the IR spectrum shows that D is an alcohol and not a carbonyl compound. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the ions at m/z 31 and 57, and the radical lost when each forms from the molecular ion. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest the structure and name of D. Explain how the missing peaks help. **[2]**
**(d)** Refluxing D with an excess of acidified potassium dichromate(VI) gives E. Name E and give the m/z of its molecular ion peak. **[2]**
**(e)** Describe how the IR spectrum of E differs from that of D. **[1]**

**10.** Compound K contains 55.8% carbon, 7.0% hydrogen and 37.2% oxygen by mass. Its mass spectrum has peaks at m/z 86 (with a very small peak at 87), 71, 45 and 41. In its IR spectrum there is a very broad band across 2500–3300 cm⁻¹, a strong absorption at 1700 cm⁻¹ and a medium one at 1650 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of K. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of K. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the functional groups in K from the IR spectrum. **[3]**
**(d)** Identify the ions at m/z 45 and 41. **[2]**
**(e)** Use the peak at m/z 71 to explain why K is not but-3-enoic acid, CH₂=CHCH₂COOH, and suggest two structures for K that fit all the data. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The bond absorbs the energy and vibrates (stretches or bends) more [1].
**(b)** C–H bonds absorb at 2850–3100 cm⁻¹ [1]. Almost all organic compounds contain C–H bonds, so the peak does not tell one functional group from another [1].
*Examiner insight:* "The bond breaks" gains nothing; IR makes the bond vibrate more.

**2. (a)** C=O in CO₂ and O–H in H₂O [1]; C–H in CH₄ [1].
**(b)** The bonds absorb IR given out by the Earth's surface and vibrate more [1]. The energy is re-emitted in all directions, some back towards the surface, so less escapes to space and the lower atmosphere warms [1].
**(c)** Policies to generate more energy from renewable sources such as wind or solar instead of fossil fuels [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Trap heat" alone misses the bond absorption being tested.

**3. (a)** IR passes through the breath and bonds in ethanol, such as C–H and C–O, absorb at their own wavenumbers [1]. The greater the absorption, the greater the ethanol concentration [1].
**(b)** Other substances in breath may absorb at one wavenumber, so a second reading helps tell ethanol apart [1].
**(c)** Carbon monoxide, CO, and nitrogen monoxide, NO [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), link the size of the absorption to concentration.

**4. (a)** **Cyclopentanone** [1]. The strong absorption at 1745 cm⁻¹ is C=O, and the lack of any broad O–H absorption rules out the alcohol and the acid [1].
**(b)** A strong C=O absorption in 1630–1820 cm⁻¹ [1] together with a very broad O–H absorption across 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say what is absent; the missing O–H rules out two options.

**5.** A C=O absorption appears at 1630–1820 cm⁻¹ in either product, showing oxidation [1]. For 2-methylbutanal, the broad alcohol O–H at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ has gone and no O–H absorption remains [1]. For 2-methylbutanoic acid, a very broad O–H absorption across 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ is present with the C=O [1].
*Examiner insight:* "The O–H peak changes" is too vague; give range and shape for each.

**6.** A strong C=O absorption at 1630–1820 cm⁻¹ [1]. A C=C absorption at 1620–1680 cm⁻¹ [1]. No broad O–H absorption in 2500–3600 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* A C–H peak is true but not specific, so choose other bonds.

**7.** It is not always the last peak: a very small M+1 peak, one unit higher, comes from molecules that contain a carbon-13 atom [1]. The molecular ion peak is the tall peak at highest m/z apart from M+1, and its m/z equals Mr because the ions are singly charged [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement on each part of the statement.

**8. (a)** C: 83.7 ÷ 12.0 = 6.98; H: 16.3 ÷ 1.0 = 16.3 [1]. Ratio 1 : 2.34; multiply by 3 to give 3 : 7, so the empirical formula is **C₃H₇** [1].
**(b)** C₃H₇ has mass 43.0; 86 ÷ 43.0 = 2, so the molecular formula is **C₆H₁₄** [1].
**(c)** m/z 71 is C₅H₁₁⁺, formed by loss of •CH₃ [1]. m/z 43 is C₃H₇⁺ [1].
**(d)** C₆H₁₄⁺ → C₃H₇⁺ + •C₃H₇ [1]
**(e)** No peak at 29 (C₂H₅⁺) or at 57 (loss of •C₂H₅) suggests H has no ethyl group [1]. H is **2,3-dimethylbutane**, (CH₃)₂CHCH(CH₃)₂; breaking the central C–C bond gives two C₃H₇ pieces, which fits the tall peak at 43 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 2.34 to 2 gives CH₂, which is wrong; multiply by 3.

**9. (a)** Broad O–H absorption at 3350 cm⁻¹ with no C=O absorption in 1630–1820 cm⁻¹ [1].
**(b)** m/z 31 is CH₂OH⁺, formed by loss of •C₄H₉ [1]. m/z 57 is C₄H₉⁺, formed by loss of •CH₂OH [1].
**(c)** CH₂OH⁺ shows a primary alcohol, and no peaks at 29 or 43 suggest no ethyl or propyl chain, so the C₄H₉ group is (CH₃)₃C– [1]. D is **2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol**, (CH₃)₃CCH₂OH [1].
**(d)** E is **2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid** [1]; molecular ion peak at m/z **102** [1].
**(e)** E shows a strong C=O absorption at about 1700 cm⁻¹, and its O–H becomes a very broad absorption across 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show fragment ions with a + charge and lost pieces as neutral radicals.

**10. (a)** C: 55.8 ÷ 12.0 = 4.65; H: 7.0 ÷ 1.0 = 7.0; O: 37.2 ÷ 16.0 = 2.325 [1]. Divide by 2.325: C 2.00, H 3.01, O 1.00 [1]. Empirical formula **C₂H₃O** [1].
**(b)** C₂H₃O has mass 43.0; 86 ÷ 43.0 = 2, so **C₄H₆O₂** [1].
**(c)** Strong C=O at 1700 cm⁻¹ [1] with a very broad O–H at 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ shows a carboxylic acid group [1]. The absorption at 1650 cm⁻¹ shows a C=C bond [1].
**(d)** m/z 45 is COOH⁺ [1]. m/z 41 is C₃H₅⁺, formed by loss of •COOH (86 − 45 = 41) [1].
**(e)** 86 − 71 = 15, so a CH₃ group is lost; but-3-enoic acid has no CH₃ group [1]. K is **but-2-enoic acid, CH₃CH=CHCOOH, or 2-methylpropenoic acid, CH₂=C(CH₃)COOH**; both fit all the data given [1].
*Examiner insight:* If two isomers fit, name both with a reason rather than guessing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the C–H absorption near 3000 cm⁻¹ as evidence for a functional group.
- Quoting an O–H absorption without saying whether it is the alcohol range or the very broad acid range.
- Saying greenhouse gases "trap heat" without naming the C=O, O–H or C–H bond that absorbs IR.
- Not linking breathalyser absorption to ethanol concentration.
- Taking the M+1 peak as the molecular ion.
- Missing the positive charge on fragment ions.
- Rounding a ratio of 2.33 or 2.34 instead of multiplying by 3.
- Ignoring missing peaks when choosing between isomers.
