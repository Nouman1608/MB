## 14.1.1 to 14.1.3 The nervous system

Information travels in the nervous system as **electrical impulses** along **neurones** (nerve cells). A **nerve** is a bundle of many neurones.

| Part | Made of | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Central nervous system (CNS) | brain and spinal cord | receives impulses, processes information, sends impulses out |
| Peripheral nervous system (PNS) | nerves outside the brain and spinal cord | carries impulses between the CNS and the rest of the body |

The role of the nervous system is the **coordination and regulation of body functions**. "Coordination" means linking stimuli to suitable responses so that the body acts as a whole. "Regulation" means keeping body functions under control.

## 14.1.4 Identifying the three neurones

| Neurone | Direction of impulse | Where its cell body is | Clue in a diagram |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | receptor → CNS | on a short side branch part-way along the fibre | long fibre either side of the cell body |
| Relay | sensory neurone → motor neurone, inside the CNS | in the middle of a short neurone | short, many branches, drawn inside the spinal cord |
| Motor | CNS → effector | at one end, inside the CNS | many branches on the cell body; one long fibre ending on a muscle or gland |

**Identification routine:** first find which end is connected to a receptor or an effector. Connected to a receptor: sensory. Connected to a muscle or gland: motor. Entirely inside the spinal cord between the two: relay.

## 14.1.5 and 14.1.6 Reflex arcs and reflex actions

A **simple reflex arc** always has the same five parts in the same order:

```
stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone → motor neurone → effector → response
```

The stimulus and response are not parts of the arc itself, but writing them in helps you apply the arc to a new example.

A **reflex action** is "a means of **automatically** and **rapidly** integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of **effectors** (muscles and glands)". Learn the four words in bold:

- **automatically**: no conscious decision is needed
- **rapidly**: the pathway is short, with few synapses
- **integrating and coordinating**: the stimulus is linked to a suitable response
- **effectors (muscles and glands)**: effectors can be either

### Worked example 1: a muscle as the effector

*A person touches a very hot pan and pulls their hand away. Describe the reflex arc.*

1. **Stimulus:** heat.
2. **Receptor:** temperature (pain) receptors in the skin of the fingers.
3. **Sensory neurone:** carries impulses from the receptor to the spinal cord.
4. **Relay neurone:** in the spinal cord, passes impulses to the motor neurone.
5. **Motor neurone:** carries impulses from the spinal cord to the arm.
6. **Effector:** a muscle in the arm contracts.
7. **Response:** the hand is pulled away.

### Worked example 2: a gland as the effector

*When food enters the mouth, saliva is released without any thought. Identify the receptor and the effector.*

- **Receptor:** chemical receptors in the tongue (taste).
- **Effector:** the salivary glands, which secrete saliva.

This example matters because many candidates assume an effector must be a muscle. The syllabus definition says **muscles and glands**.

### Reflex or voluntary?

| | Reflex action | Voluntary action |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | automatic, no conscious thought | conscious decision in the brain |
| Speed | rapid | slower |
| Response to same stimulus | the same every time | can vary |
| Example | blinking when dust hits the eye | choosing to pick up a pen |

Reflexes protect the body because the response happens before damage builds up. You may become aware of the stimulus afterwards, but awareness is not part of the reflex arc.

## 14.1.7 to 14.1.10 Synapses

A **synapse** is a **junction between two neurones** (Core). The neurones do not touch: there is a tiny gap between them.

### Structure (Extended only)

| Structure | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Vesicles containing neurotransmitter molecules | inside the end of the first neurone | store the neurotransmitter until an impulse arrives |
| Synaptic gap | between the two neurones | the space the neurotransmitter crosses |
| Receptor proteins | on the membrane of the next neurone | bind neurotransmitter molecules |

### Events (Extended only)

1. An impulse arrives and stimulates the **release of neurotransmitter molecules from vesicles** into the synaptic gap.
2. The neurotransmitter molecules **diffuse across the gap**.
3. Neurotransmitter molecules **bind with receptor proteins** on the next neurone.
4. An **impulse is then stimulated** in the next neurone.

### Why impulses go one way (Extended only)

Synapses ensure impulses travel in **one direction only**. The reason follows from the structure: only the first neurone has vesicles of neurotransmitter, and only the next neurone has receptor proteins. Neurotransmitter can be released on one side and detected on the other, never the reverse.

### Model answer: events at a synapse

**Question:** Describe the events at a synapse. [4]

**Answer:** An impulse arriving at the end of the neurone causes vesicles to release neurotransmitter molecules into the synaptic gap (1). The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap (1). It binds to receptor proteins on the membrane of the next neurone (1). This stimulates an impulse in the next neurone (1).

The verb for crossing the gap is **diffuses**. "Travels" or "flows" may not earn the mark, and saying the impulse itself "jumps" the gap is wrong.

## Common mistakes

- Saying impulses travel along "nerves" when the question asks about neurones; impulses travel along neurones, and a nerve is a bundle of them.
- Leaving the spinal cord out of the CNS.
- Writing the reflex arc in the wrong order, or missing the relay neurone.
- Giving only muscles as effectors.
- Saying the brain must decide in a reflex.
- Describing a synapse as neurones "touching" or "joined".
- (Extended) Saying the electrical impulse crosses the gap. A chemical, the neurotransmitter, crosses it.
- (Extended) Mixing up where vesicles and receptor proteins are.

## Exam technique for 14.1 command words

- **State**: short facts, such as "electrical impulses travel along neurones".
- **Identify**: name the neurone and, if asked, the feature you used.
- **Describe** a reflex arc: all five parts in order, applied to the example given.
- **Describe** a reflex action: use the syllabus words "automatically", "rapidly" and "effectors".
- **Describe** synapse events (Extended): four steps, in order, using "diffuses" and "binds".
