This practice set covers syllabus section **1.4** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first
assessment 2021), common to SL and HL: compound interest and annual depreciation. Section A is
calculator-free, like Paper 1; Section B expects technology, like Paper 2, including the GDC financial
package. Give money to the nearest penny and other answers to 3 significant figures. All amounts are
invented for practice.

Learn the methods first in the [section 1.4 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-4/)

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) An account pays 4.8% per year, compounded quarterly, for 6 years. State the rate
per period and the number of periods. **[2]**

**2.** (calculator-free) £800 is invested at 10% per year.

**(a)** Find the value after 2 years if interest is compounded yearly. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how much more this is than with simple interest at the same rate. **[1]**

**3.** (calculator-free) £5000 grows to £6050 in 2 years with interest compounded yearly. Find the annual
interest rate. **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free) A machine costs £15 000 and depreciates by 10% each year. Find its value after
3 years. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Write an expression for the value of £P after n years at 6% per year compounded monthly. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why monthly compounding gives a larger value than yearly compounding at the same nominal
rate. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** (calculator allowed) £12 000 is invested at 3.6% per year, compounded half-yearly.

**(a)** Find the value after 9 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how long it takes for the value to first exceed £25 000. **[3]**
**(c)** Write down the TVM entries you would use for part (a). **[1]**

**7.** (calculator allowed) £8000 is invested for 5 years at 3% per year. Find the difference between the
final values with yearly compound interest and with simple interest. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) A car costs £32 000 and depreciates by 15% each year.

**(a)** Find its value after 4 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of complete years after which its value is first below £10 000. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) A boat bought for £15 000 is worth £9720 three years later. Find the annual rate
of depreciation. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) £25 000 is invested for 10 years at 4.2% per year, compounded quarterly. Inflation
is 2.5% per year throughout.

**(a)** Find the value of the investment after 10 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its real value in today's money. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) Account X pays 3.5% per year compounded yearly. Account Y pays 3.4% per year
compounded monthly. Amina invests £50 000 for 5 years. Determine which account gives the larger final value,
and by how much. **[4]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) An investment earns 6.25% per year, compounded yearly.

**(a)** Find the number of complete years needed for the investment to at least triple. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why your answer is a whole number of years. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Rate per quarter 4.8/4 = **1.2%** [1]; periods 4 × 6 = **24** [1] **[2]**

**2. (a)** 800 × 1.1² [1] = 800 × 1.21 = **£968** [1]
**(b)** Simple: 800 + 2 × 80 = £960, so **£8 more** [1]

**3.** 5000(1 + r/100)² = 6050 [1]; (1 + r/100)² = 1.21, so 1 + r/100 = 1.1 [1]; **r = 10%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Reject the negative square root, −1.1, which would give a negative growth factor.

**4.** 15 000 × 0.9³ [1] = 15 000 × 0.729 = **£10 935** [1]

**5. (a)** **P(1 + 6/1200)^(12n)**, which is P × 1.005^(12n) [1]
**(b)** Interest is added more often, so later interest is earned on interest already added [1]

**6. (a)** 12 000 × 1.018¹⁸ [1] = **£16 544.03** [1]
**(b)** 12 000 × 1.018^m > 25 000 [1]; m > 41.14 half-years, so m = 42 [1]; **21 years** [1]
**(c)** N = 18, I% = 3.6, PV = −12 000, PMT = 0, P/Y = C/Y = 2 (solve for FV) [1]
*Examiner insight:* 41.14 ÷ 2 = 20.6 years is not a valid answer, because interest is only added every half
year.

**7.** Compound: 8000 × 1.03⁵ = £9274.19 [1]; simple: 8000 + 5 × 240 = £9200 [1]; difference **£74.19** [1]
**[3]**

**8. (a)** 32 000 × 0.85⁴ [1] = **£16 704.20** [1]
**(b)** 32 000 × 0.85ⁿ < 10 000 [1]; n > 7.16 (after 7 years £10 258.47, after 8 years £8719.70) [1];
**8 years** [1]

**9.** 15 000(1 − r/100)³ = 9720 [1]; 1 − r/100 = 0.648^(1/3) = 0.8653 [1]; **r = 13.5%** [1] **[3]**

**10. (a)** 25 000 × 1.0105⁴⁰ [1] = **£37 965.81** [1]
**(b)** 37 965.81… ÷ 1.025¹⁰ [1] = **£29 658.83** [1]

**11.** X: 50 000 × 1.035⁵ = £59 384.32 [1]; Y: 50 000 × (1 + 0.034/12)⁶⁰ = £59 251.00 [1]; **account X**
gives more [1], by **£133.32** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* More frequent compounding does not always win; a higher nominal rate can outweigh it.

**12. (a)** 1.0625ⁿ ≥ 3, so n ≥ ln 3/ln 1.0625 = 18.12 [1]; **19 years** [1]
**(b)** Interest is added only once a year, so the value only changes at the end of each whole year [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the annual rate as the rate per period, or years as the number of periods.
- Giving a fractional number of periods as a time.
- Rounding a final value before dividing by an inflation factor.
- Using 1.15 instead of 0.85 for 15% depreciation.
- Writing only an answer, with no formula or TVM entries.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use the TVM solver for depreciation?**
Yes: enter the rate as a negative I%. You can also use the formula with a factor below 1.

**Will I have to derive the compound interest formula?**
No. The guide states that exam questions will not ask for the derivation.
