These notes condense **3.1.2.2 The mole and the Avogadro constant** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1, drawn on by Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. The [mole and Avogadro constant study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-2/) explains each method with worked examples; the [mole and Avogadro constant practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-2-practice/) test them. The value of the Avogadro constant is given in exams; these notes use L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Core ideas

- One mole of any particle contains L = 6.02 × 10²³ of those particles.
- One mole of a substance has a mass in grams equal to its Mr.
- The mole can count electrons, atoms, molecules, ions, formula units or "equations".
- Always name the particle: 1 mol of MgCl₂ contains 2 mol of Cl⁻ ions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Amount of substance, n | Quantity measured in moles (unit: mol) |
| Mole | Amount containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro constant, L | Number of particles in one mole, 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ |
| Molar mass | Mass of one mole, g mol⁻¹, numerically equal to Mr |
| Concentration | Amount of solute per dm³ of solution, mol dm⁻³ |
| Mass concentration | Mass of solute per dm³ of solution, g dm⁻³ |
| Standard solution | Solution of accurately known concentration |

## Equations

| Use | Equation |
|---|---|
| Particles | N = n × L |
| Mass | n = m ÷ Mr, so m = n × Mr |
| Solution | n = c × V (V in dm³) |
| Mass concentration | g dm⁻³ = mol dm⁻³ × Mr |
| Dilution | c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ |
| Mass of one particle | Mr ÷ L (in g) |

Unit conversion: V (dm³) = V (cm³) ÷ 1000.

## Method boxes

**Number of a particular atom or ion**

1. Find moles of the substance.
2. Multiply by the number of those atoms or ions per formula.
3. Multiply by L.

Example: 7.10 g Cl₂ (Mr 71.0) = 0.100 mol Cl₂ = 0.200 mol Cl atoms = 1.20 × 10²³ atoms.

**Mass needed for a solution**

1. n = c × V (V in dm³).
2. Check whether the solid is hydrated; use its Mr.
3. m = n × Mr.

Example: 500 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄ (Mr 158.0) needs 0.0200 × 0.500 × 158.0 = 1.58 g.

**Ion concentration**

Multiply the solute concentration by the number of those ions per formula unit. 0.150 mol dm⁻³ Al₂(SO₄)₃ has [Al³⁺] = 0.300 mol dm⁻³ and [SO₄²⁻] = 0.450 mol dm⁻³.

## Choosing the right equation

- Given a mass of solid: n = m ÷ Mr.
- Given a number of particles: n = N ÷ L.
- Given a solution: n = c × V, with V in dm³.
- Asked for a number of a particular atom, ion or electron: find moles of the substance first, then multiply by the count per formula, then by L.
- Asked to make a solution: work out n from c and V, then m from n and Mr.
- Two solutions of the same substance, before and after adding water: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂.

If a question gives a gas volume, pressure and temperature instead, use the ideal gas equation from subtopic 3.1.2.3.

## Exam traps

- cm³ must be converted to dm³ before using n = c × V.
- Number of atoms ≠ number of molecules for anything other than a monatomic substance.
- Hydrated and anhydrous salts have different Mr values.
- Concentration of a salt is not automatically the concentration of each ion.
- Dilution keeps moles constant; the concentration falls by the same factor the volume rises.
- Do not round intermediate values; give final answers to three significant figures unless told otherwise.
- The unit of amount is mol; the unit of L is mol⁻¹.

## Self-test

1. How many molecules are in 0.250 mol of CO₂?
2. Calculate the amount, in mol, in 3.01 × 10²⁴ atoms of helium.
3. How many atoms in total are in 0.36 g of water (Mr 18.0)?
4. How many electrons are in 1.00 mol of OH⁻ ions?
5. Calculate the mass of 9.03 × 10²² molecules of CO₂ (Mr 44.0).
6. Calculate the concentration of a solution containing 2.65 g of Na₂CO₃ (Mr 106.0) in 250 cm³.
7. What is [Na⁺] in the solution in Question 6?
8. Convert 0.150 mol dm⁻³ NaOH (Mr 40.0) into g dm⁻³.
9. How many Cl⁻ ions are in 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ MgCl₂?
10. 10.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ HCl is diluted to 250 cm³. Find the new concentration.
11. Calculate the mass, in g, of one molecule of glucose (Mr 180.0).
12. One molecule of a gas has a mass of 4.65 × 10⁻²³ g. Calculate its Mr.

### Answers

1. 0.250 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.51 × 10²³
2. 3.01 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 5.00 mol
3. 0.0200 mol × 3 atoms × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.61 × 10²²
4. 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 electrons per ion, so 10.0 mol, which is 6.02 × 10²⁴ electrons
5. 9.03 × 10²² ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.150 mol; 0.150 × 44.0 = 6.60 g
6. 0.0250 mol ÷ 0.250 dm³ = 0.100 mol dm⁻³
7. 0.200 mol dm⁻³
8. 0.150 × 40.0 = 6.00 g dm⁻³
9. 0.100 × 0.0250 × 2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²¹
10. 0.500 × 10.0 ÷ 250 = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³
11. 180.0 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.99 × 10⁻²² g
12. 4.65 × 10⁻²³ × 6.02 × 10²³ = 28.0
