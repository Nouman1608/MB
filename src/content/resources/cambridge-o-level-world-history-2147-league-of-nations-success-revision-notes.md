**These notes condense key question 2.2 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: "To what extent was the League of Nations a
success?"** Learn the four named disputes of the 1920s, the four named areas of humanitarian work and the two crises of
the 1930s, each with a reason for its outcome. The full explanation is in the
[League of Nations study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-league-of-nations-success/); exam
practice is in the [League of Nations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-league-of-nations-success-practice/).

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Organisation and membership: was failure inevitable?**
- No army; sanctions in three stages (moral, economic, military).
- Unanimity needed in Assembly and Council; permanent members had a veto.
- USA never joined; Germany in 1926–33; USSR in 1934–39; Japan left 1933; Italy left 1937.
- Britain and France led, but both were weakened and had their own interests.
- Not inevitable: the same structure produced successes in the 1920s.

**Peacekeeping in the 1920s: how successful?**
- Successes: Aaland Islands (1920–21), Bulgaria (1925).
- Failures: Vilna (1920), Corfu (1923).
- Rule of thumb: small states obeyed; powerful states, or those protected by them, did not.

**Humanitarian work: how important?**
- Refugees and prisoners of war: Nansen; Nansen passports.
- Health: action against leprosy, malaria and typhus; epidemic information.
- Working conditions: ILO under Albert Thomas; standards on hours and child labour.
- Slavery: Slavery Commission; Slavery Convention (1926).
- Important for millions, and its agencies outlived the League, but it did not prevent war.

**The Depression and the 1930s**
- Trade collapse, unemployment, economic nationalism, aggressive regimes.
- Sanctions became harder; Britain and France reluctant to spend on enforcement.
- Manchuria (1931–33): Lytton Report condemned Japan; Japan left; no sanctions.
- Abyssinia (1935–36): limited sanctions without oil; Suez left open; Hoare–Laval Plan; Italy conquered Abyssinia.

## The 1920s disputes compared

| Dispute | Parties | League action | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vilna 1920 | Poland and Lithuania | Asked Poland to withdraw | Poland kept Vilna | Failure |
| Aaland Islands 1920–21 | Sweden and Finland | Commission investigated; ruling | To Finland, demilitarised, Swedish culture protected | Success |
| Corfu 1923 | Italy and Greece | Proposed a settlement; overruled by the Conference of Ambassadors | Greece paid Italy compensation; Italy withdrew | Failure |
| Bulgaria 1925 | Greece and Bulgaria | Ordered Greek withdrawal | Greece withdrew and paid compensation | Success |

## The 1930s crises compared

| | Manchuria | Abyssinia |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressor | Japan | Italy |
| Start | Mukden Incident, September 1931 | Wal Wal clash (December 1934); invasion, October 1935 |
| League response | Lytton Commission; report adopted February 1933 | Condemnation; limited economic sanctions, November 1935 |
| Why weak | Far away; the USA, Japan's trading partner, not a member; powers unwilling to act | Oil excluded; Suez Canal not closed; Hoare–Laval Plan |
| Outcome | Manchukuo; Japan left League; attack on Jehol followed | Addis Ababa captured, May 1936; sanctions dropped, July 1936 |
| Consequence | Showed aggression by a great power would go unpunished | Destroyed the League's credibility; Italy moved towards Germany |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Covenant | The League's founding rules, part of the peace treaties |
| Collective security | Members acting together against an aggressor |
| Sanctions | Penalties to force a state to change its behaviour |
| Unanimity | All members must agree before a decision is taken |
| Veto | The power of one member to block a decision |
| Conference of Ambassadors | Body of Allied ambassadors handling peace-treaty matters, which overrode the League over Corfu |
| Nansen passport | Identity document for stateless refugees |
| ILO | International Labour Organisation |
| Lytton Commission | League inquiry into the Manchurian crisis |
| Hoare–Laval Plan | Secret 1935 Anglo-French plan to give Italy much of Abyssinia |

## Exam traps

- **Saying Germany was a founder member.** It joined in 1926.
- **Dating Manchuria to 1933 only.** The invasion was 1931; 1933 was the report and Japan's exit.
- **Assuming "sanctions failed" in Abyssinia means none were imposed.** They were imposed but limited.
- **Listing humanitarian work** without weighing its importance against peacekeeping.
- **Ignoring the Depression** when explaining the 1930s; it is a named focus point.

## Self-test

1. Name the three stages of League sanctions.
2. Who were the permanent members of the Council when the League began?
3. Why was the absence of the USA a weakness?
4. Why did the League fail over Vilna?
5. Why was the Aaland Islands decision accepted?
6. Which body overruled the League over Corfu, and what did Greece have to do?
7. What happened in the Bulgarian dispute of 1925?
8. What were Nansen passports?
9. What did the Slavery Convention of 1926 commit states to do?
10. How did the Depression make the League's work harder?
11. What was the Lytton Commission's conclusion, and how did Japan respond?
12. Give **two** reasons why the sanctions against Italy failed.

**Answers:** 1. Moral condemnation, economic sanctions, military action. 2. Britain, France, Italy and Japan. 3. It was
the richest power; its absence weakened economic sanctions and left Britain and France to lead. 4. Britain and France
would not act against Poland, which they saw as a useful ally. 5. Sweden and Finland were small states willing to
accept a ruling. 6. The Conference of Ambassadors; Greece had to apologise and pay compensation to Italy. 7. Greece
invaded after a border incident; the League ordered it to withdraw and pay compensation, and it did. 8. Identity
documents allowing stateless refugees to travel and work. 9. To suppress the slave trade and slavery. 10. It encouraged
aggressive nationalist regimes, made economic sanctions painful and made Britain and France reluctant to act.
11. Japan was in the wrong and Manchuria should be a self-governing region under Chinese sovereignty; Japan left the
League. 12. Any two: oil was not
included; the Suez Canal stayed open; the Hoare–Laval Plan undermined them; the USA and Germany were not members and
kept trading.
