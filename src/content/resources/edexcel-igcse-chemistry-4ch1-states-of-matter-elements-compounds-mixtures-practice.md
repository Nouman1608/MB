These questions cover sub-topics 1(a) **States of matter** and 1(b) **Elements, compounds and mixtures** (points 1.1 to 1.13) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). The qualification is untiered. Points 1.5C to 1.7C carry a C reference, so they are Paper 2 only; questions 8 and 9 are labelled. A calculator may be used on both papers.

## Questions

**1.** Name each change of state.

**(a)** Liquid wax turning into solid wax. **[1]**
**(b)** Steam turning into water on a cold window. **[1]**
**(c)** Solid carbon dioxide turning directly into a gas. **[1]**

**2.** A pure solid is heated steadily. Its temperature stays at 54 °C until it has all melted, then rises again.

**(a)** Explain why the temperature stays at 54 °C during melting. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the changes in the arrangement, movement and energy of the particles as the solid melts. **[3]**

**3.** A student dissolves a few crystals of purple potassium manganate(VII) in 10 cm³ of water. She takes 1 cm³ of this solution and makes it up to 10 cm³ with water. She repeats this with each new solution.

**(a)** After four dilutions the solution is still pale purple. Explain what this suggests about the particles in the crystals. **[2]**
**(b)** State what fraction of the original concentration is left after four dilutions. **[1]**
**(c)** A crystal is dropped into a beaker of still water. Explain why, after an hour, purple colour has spread through the water without stirring. **[2]**

**4.** A gas jar of air is placed upside down on top of a gas jar of brown bromine vapour. The glass cover between them is removed.

**(a)** Describe what you would see after 30 minutes. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain this observation in terms of particles. **[2]**
**(c)** The experiment is repeated at a higher temperature. State and explain the effect on the time taken. **[2]**

**5.** For each substance, state whether it is an element, a compound or a mixture.

**(a)** Nitrogen, N₂ **[1]**
**(b)** Ammonia, NH₃ **[1]**
**(c)** Sea water **[1]**
**(d)** Brass, made of copper and zinc not chemically combined **[1]**
**(e)** Iron and sulfur can be heated together to form iron(II) sulfide. Give **two** ways in which iron(II) sulfide differs from a mixture of iron and sulfur. **[2]**

**6.** Two white solids are tested. Solid A melts sharply at 122 °C. Solid B melts gradually between 114 °C and 119 °C. A sample of water boils at 102 °C at normal atmospheric pressure.

**(a)** State which solid is pure and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what the boiling point tells you about the water sample. **[1]**

**7.** Name the best method to obtain:

**(a)** sand from a mixture of sand and water **[1]**
**(b)** pure water from copper(II) sulfate solution **[1]**
**(c)** propanone from a mixture of propanone and water, which mix completely **[1]**
**(d)** crystals of potassium nitrate from potassium nitrate solution **[1]**
**(e)** the separate dyes in a black ink **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) A student finds the solubility of a salt in water at 40 °C. Her results:

| Measurement | Mass / g |
|---|---|
| Empty evaporating basin | 41.20 |
| Basin + saturated solution | 66.70 |
| Basin + solid after first heating | 46.42 |
| Basin + solid after second heating | 46.30 |
| Basin + solid after third heating | 46.30 |

**(a)** Explain why she heated the basin three times. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the solubility of the salt at 40 °C in g per 100 g of water. **[3]**
**(c)** State how she made sure the solution was saturated. **[1]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) The solubility of salt Y is shown.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solubility / g per 100 g water | 22 | 37 | 58 | 85 | 118 |

**(a)** State the quantity you would plot on each axis to draw a solubility curve. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the solubility of Y changes with temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** A saturated solution of Y in 40 g of water at 70 °C is cooled to 10 °C. Calculate the mass of crystals that forms. **[3]**
**(d)** 20 g of Y is stirred into 50 g of water at 30 °C. Show whether all of it dissolves. **[2]**

**10.** A student has a mixture of sand and copper(II) sulfate. Copper(II) sulfate dissolves in water; sand does not.

**(a)** Describe how she can obtain dry crystals of copper(II) sulfate from the mixture. **[5]**
**(b)** She now wants pure water from some copper(II) sulfate solution. Name the method and explain how the water is separated. **[2]**

**11.** A food colouring E is tested by paper chromatography with water as the solvent. The solvent front moves 7.5 cm. E gives three spots: one stays on the baseline, one moves 1.8 cm and one moves 4.5 cm. Reference dyes have these Rf values: P 0.24, Q 0.60, R 0.80.

**(a)** Explain why the baseline is drawn in pencil. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the solvent must start below the baseline. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the Rf value of each moving spot. **[2]**
**(d)** Identify the dyes present in E. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain the spot on the baseline. **[1]**
**(f)** State the minimum number of substances in E. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Freezing [1]
**(b)** Condensing [1]
**(c)** Sublimation [1]
*Examiner insight:* one-word answers score here, but "evaporation" for (b) or "melting" for (c) gets no credit because each mark needs the exact named change.

**2. (a)** The energy supplied is used to overcome the forces between the particles [1], not to increase their kinetic energy, so temperature does not rise [1].
**(b)** Arrangement changes from regular to irregular, particles still close [1]; movement changes from vibrating about fixed positions to moving around each other [1]; particles gain energy [1].
*Examiner insight:* (b) needs three separate points; "the particles move faster" alone covers only one of them.

**3. (a)** The crystals contain a very large number of particles [1] which are very small, so they still colour the water after spreading out [1].
**(b)** 1/10 × 1/10 × 1/10 × 1/10 = **1/10 000** [1]
**(c)** The particles move randomly [1] and spread from high concentration to low concentration by diffusion [1].
*Examiner insight:* "particles spread out" without random movement or a concentration gradient is too vague for credit.

**4. (a)** Both jars are the same pale brown colour [1].
**(b)** Bromine and air particles move randomly [1], so they mix until evenly spread (diffusion) [1].
**(c)** The time is shorter [1] because the particles have more energy and move faster [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c) the effect and the reason are separate marks; "it is faster" alone scores one, and the reason must mention particles having more energy or moving faster.

**5. (a)** Element [1]
**(b)** Compound [1]
**(c)** Mixture [1]
**(d)** Mixture [1]
**(e)** Iron(II) sulfide has fixed proportions of iron and sulfur [1]; it has different properties from iron and sulfur, e.g. it is not magnetic [1]. (Also accept: it can only be split up by a chemical reaction, not by a magnet.)
*Examiner insight:* "it has two elements" earns nothing in (e), because a mixture of iron and sulfur also contains two elements.

**6. (a)** Solid A [1]: a pure substance has a fixed (sharp) melting point; B melts over a range so it is a mixture [1].
**(b)** It is impure (contains dissolved substances) [1].
*Examiner insight:* naming A without a reason earns only one mark; the reason must refer to a fixed melting point or to B melting over a range.

**7. (a)** Filtration [1]
**(b)** Simple distillation [1]
**(c)** Fractional distillation [1]
**(d)** Crystallisation [1]
**(e)** Paper chromatography [1]
*Examiner insight:* "distillation" without "simple" or "fractional" may not be accepted in (b) and (c), where the type of distillation is the point being tested.

**8. (a)** To make sure all the water had evaporated [1]; the mass stopped changing (heated to constant mass) [1].
**(b)** Mass of solid = 46.30 − 41.20 = 5.10 g [1]
Mass of water = 66.70 − 46.30 = 20.40 g [1]
Solubility = 5.10 ÷ 20.40 × 100 = **25.0 g per 100 g of water** [1]
**(c)** Undissolved solid was still present (excess solid added and stirred at 40 °C) [1].
*Examiner insight:* using the first-heating mass (46.42 g) loses the accuracy mark, but method marks can still be awarded.

**9. (a)** Solubility (g per 100 g water) on the y-axis and temperature (°C) on the x-axis [1].
**(b)** Solubility increases as temperature increases [1].
**(c)** Difference per 100 g = 85 − 22 = 63 g [1]
Scale to 40 g of water: 63 × 40 ÷ 100 [1]
= **25.2 g** [1]
**(d)** 50 g of water at 30 °C dissolves at most 37 × 50 ÷ 100 = 18.5 g [1]; 18.5 g < 20 g, so **not all dissolves** (1.5 g remains) [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c), 63 g without scaling to 40 g of water scores the first mark only; the final answer needs the scaling step shown.

**10. (a)** Add water and stir (warm) so the copper(II) sulfate dissolves [1].
Filter; sand stays on the filter paper [1].
Heat the filtrate to evaporate some water until it is saturated (crystals form on a glass rod) [1].
Leave to cool so crystals form [1].
Filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers [1].
**(b)** Simple distillation [1]; the water boils off as steam, is cooled and condensed in the condenser and collected, while the copper(II) sulfate stays in the flask [1].
*Examiner insight:* evaporating to dryness instead of crystallising usually loses the cooling mark, because the question asks for crystals.

**11. (a)** Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent, so it will not run [1].
**(b)** Otherwise the spots would dissolve into the solvent instead of moving up the paper [1].
**(c)** 1.8 ÷ 7.5 = **0.24** [1]; 4.5 ÷ 7.5 = **0.60** [1]
**(d)** P and Q [1]
**(e)** That substance is insoluble in water (the solvent) [1].
**(f)** **3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* an Rf above 1 means the division is upside down and loses the calculation marks; measure both distances from the baseline.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out energy when describing a change of state.
- Saying temperature rises during melting.
- Explaining diffusion without random movement of particles.
- Giving "evaporation" when the change is condensing, or "melting" for sublimation.
- (Paper 2 only) Dividing by the mass of the solution, not the water, or using a mass before constant mass was reached.
- (Paper 2 only) Forgetting to scale crystal masses to the actual mass of water.
- Saying a compound "contains two elements" as the difference from a mixture.
- Writing "distillation" without saying simple or fractional.
- Measuring Rf from the bottom of the paper, not the baseline.
- Not counting a spot left on the baseline as a substance.
