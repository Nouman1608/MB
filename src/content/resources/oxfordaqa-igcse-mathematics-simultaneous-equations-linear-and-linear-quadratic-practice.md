These questions cover statement A21 in section 3.2.3 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) specification, Version 5.1 (for exams May/June 2018 onwards). Questions 1 to 6 are Core content, which is also assessable at Extension. Questions 7 to 11 are marked (Extension only) because they involve a linear and a quadratic equation together.

## Questions

**1.** Solve the simultaneous equations by adding the equations first.

4x + y = 19
2x − y = 5
**[3]**

**2.** Solve the simultaneous equations. Show your working.

3x + 4y = 1
5x + 3y = 9
**[4]**

**3.** Solve the simultaneous equations using substitution.

y = 5 − 2x
4x − 3y = 25
**[3]**

**4.** In a stationery shop, n is the cost in dollars of one notebook and p is the cost in dollars of one pen. The prices satisfy these equations.

4n + 3p = 16.5
2n + 5p = 13.5

Work out the total cost of one notebook and one pen. **[4]**

**5.** Here are two equations.

5x + 2y = 10
y = x + 1

**(a)** Copy and complete the table of values for 5x + 2y = 10.

| x | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| y | | | |

**[2]**

**(b)** On a grid, draw the line 5x + 2y = 10 using your table, and draw the line y = x + 1. Use your graph to estimate the solution of both equations together. **[2]**

**(c)** Solve the equations algebraically, giving your answers as fractions. **[3]**

**6.** **(a)** Explain why 3x + 6y = 10 and x + 2y = 5 have no solution. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the value of k for which 2x + 3y = 7 and 4x + 6y = k have infinitely many solutions. **[1]**

**7.** (Extension only) Solve y = 3x − 2 and y = x² − 2x + 2. **[5]**

**8.** (Extension only) Solve 3x + y = 5 and y = x² − 2x − 7. **[5]**

**9.** (Extension only) Solve 2x − y = 1 and y = 2x² − 3x − 2. Give the x-values exactly, and all values of x and y to 3 significant figures. **[6]**

**10.** (Extension only) The curve y = x² − 4x + 7 and the line y = 2x + c are drawn, where c is a constant.

**(a)** Show that the x-coordinates of any intersection satisfy x² − 6x + (7 − c) = 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the value of c for which the line touches the curve at exactly one point. **[2]**

**(c)** Show that the line does not meet the curve when c = −5. **[2]**

**11.** (Extension only) The curve y = x² − 4x + 3 meets the line y = x − 1 at points A and B.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of A and B. **[5]**

**(b)** Show that the line y = x − 2 meets the curve at two points. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the line y = x − 4 does not meet the curve. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Add the equations: 6x = 24 [1]. So x = 4 [1]. Substitute: 16 + y = 19, so y = 3 [1]. Check in the second equation: 8 − 3 = 5 ✓.
**x = 4, y = 3** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A substitution check in the equation you did not use takes a few seconds and catches a slip in the last step before you move on.

**2.** Multiply the first equation by 5 and the second by 3: 15x + 20y = 5 and 15x + 9y = 27 [1]. Subtract: 11y = −22 [1]. So y = −2 [1]. Substitute: 3x − 8 = 1, so x = 3 [1]. Check: 15 − 6 = 9 ✓.
**x = 3, y = −2** **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtracting 9y from 20y gives 11y, but a common slip is to subtract a negative result wrongly. Writing the subtraction as its own line makes the signs visible and gives the examiner something to credit.

**3.** Substitute: 4x − 3(5 − 2x) = 25 [1]. Expand and collect: 4x − 15 + 6x = 25, so 10x = 40 [1]. So x = 4 and y = 5 − 8 = −3 [1].
**x = 4, y = −3** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The bracket around 5 − 2x matters. Without it, the −3 multiplies only the 5, and the whole solution goes wrong from the first line.

**4.** Multiply the second equation by 2: 4n + 10p = 27 [1]. Subtract the first equation: 7p = 10.5, so p = 1.5 [1]. Substitute: 4n + 4.5 = 16.5, so n = 3 [1]. Total for one notebook and one pen: 3 + 1.5 = 4.50 dollars [1].
**4.50 dollars** **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for a total, not for n and p. Read the last line of a context question again before you write the final answer.

**5. (a)** At x = 0: 2y = 10, so y = 5 [1]. At x = 2: y = 0, and at x = 4: y = −5 [1].
| x | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5 | 0 | −5 |

**(b)** Plot (0, 5), (2, 0), (4, −5) and join them with a ruled line. The second line passes through (0, 1), (1, 2) and (2, 3) [1]. The lines cross at about x = 1.1, y = 2.1 [1].
**x ≈ 1.1, y ≈ 2.1** **[2]**

**(c)** Substitute y = x + 1: 5x + 2(x + 1) = 10 [1]. So 7x = 8 and x = 8/7 [1]. Then y = 8/7 + 1 = 15/7 [1].
**x = 8/7, y = 15/7** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Your graph reading of 1.1 and 2.1 is a useful check on the algebra, since 8/7 is 1.14 and 15/7 is 2.14. A graph reading that is far from the algebra usually signals a plotting slip.

**6. (a)** Multiplying the second equation by 3 gives 3x + 6y = 15 [1]. This cannot be true at the same time as 3x + 6y = 10, so no pair (x, y) satisfies both. The lines are parallel [1].
**(b)** Doubling 2x + 3y = 7 gives 4x + 6y = 14, so **k = 14** [1].
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why there is no solution. The statement "they are parallel" is stronger when it is supported by the contradiction you found.

**7.** Equate the expressions for y: x² − 2x + 2 = 3x − 2 [1]. Rearrange: x² − 5x + 4 = 0 [1]. Factorise: (x − 1)(x − 4) = 0, so x = 1 or x = 4 [1]. Use the linear equation: y = 3(1) − 2 = 1 [1] and y = 3(4) − 2 = 10 [1].
**(1, 1) and (4, 10)** **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Finding y from the linear equation keeps each pair matched. Quote the pairs as coordinates, so it is clear which y belongs to which x.

**8.** Rearrange the line: y = 5 − 3x [1]. Equate: x² − 2x − 7 = 5 − 3x [1]. Rearrange: x² + x − 12 = 0 [1]. Factorise: (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0, so x = −4 or x = 3 [1]. Use the linear equation: y = 17 and y = −4 [1].
**(−4, 17) and (3, −4)** **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign change when you rearrange 3x + y = 5 is the easy place to slip. Write y = 5 − 3x on its own line before substituting.

**9.** Rearrange the line: y = 2x − 1 [1]. Equate: 2x² − 3x − 2 = 2x − 1 [1]. Rearrange: 2x² − 5x − 1 = 0 [1]. Formula: x = (5 ± √(25 + 8))/4 = (5 ± √33)/4 [1]. So x = 2.69 or x = −0.186 [1]. Use y = 2x − 1: y = 4.37 and y = −1.37 [1].
**x = (5 ± √33)/4: (2.69, 4.37) and (−0.186, −1.37)** **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the surd form through the working and round only at the end. Work out each y-value from the unrounded x-value so that rounding errors do not build up.

**10. (a)** Set the equations equal: x² − 4x + 7 = 2x + c [1]. Rearrange: x² − 6x + 7 − c = 0 [1].
**(b)** One point of contact means b² − 4ac = 0 [1]: 36 − 4(7 − c) = 8 + 4c = 0, so **c = −2** [1].
**(c)** With c = −5 the equation is x² − 6x + 12 = 0, so b² − 4ac = 36 − 48 = −12 [1]. This is negative, so there are no real solutions and the line does not meet the curve [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, finish with the sentence that links your calculation to the claim. The number −12 alone does not say what it means.

**11. (a)** Equate: x² − 4x + 3 = x − 1 [1]. Rearrange: x² − 5x + 4 = 0 [1]. Factorise: (x − 1)(x − 4) = 0, so x = 1 or x = 4 [1]. Use y = x − 1: y = 0 and y = 3 [1]. So A(1, 0) and B(4, 3) [1].
**(b)** Equate: x² − 4x + 3 = x − 2, so x² − 5x + 5 = 0 [1]. Then b² − 4ac = 25 − 20 = 5, which is positive, so there are two intersection points [1].
**(c)** Equate: x² − 4x + 3 = x − 4, so x² − 5x + 7 = 0 [1]. Then b² − 4ac = 25 − 28 = −3, which is negative, so there are no real solutions and the line does not meet the curve [1].
**[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Parts (b) and (c) need only the discriminant, not the solutions. State the sign and what it means, rather than solving a quadratic you do not need.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Forgetting to multiply the right-hand side when you scale an equation.
* Subtracting one equation from another but changing the sign of only one term.
* Substituting an expression without brackets, as in question 3.
* Stopping after finding x, then not finding y.
* Reading values from a graph without a ruler, or quoting more decimal places than the scale allows.
* Giving a graph reading when the question asks for an algebraic solution.
* Moving a term across an equals sign without changing its sign when rearranging a linear equation.
* Finding y from the quadratic rather than the line, and pairing values wrongly.
* Giving separate lists of x-values and y-values instead of matched coordinates.
* Giving a decimal for an answer that should stay as a surd, or the other way round.
