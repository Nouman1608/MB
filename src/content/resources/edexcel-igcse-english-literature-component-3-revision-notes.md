Component 3 is the coursework alternative to Component 2 in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English
Literature (4ET1). You write **two essays, each out of 30**: Assignment A on a modern drama set text
(AO1 15, AO2 15) and Assignment B on a literary heritage set text (AO1 10, AO2 10, AO4 10). The two
marks combine to 60, worth 40% of the qualification, and the advisory length is 650–800 words per
assignment. For the full explanation, use the
[Component 3 study guide](/resources/igcse-edexcel-english-literature-component-3/).

## Route check

Every learner takes Component 1, Poetry and Modern Prose (2-hour exam, 90 marks, 60%). Learners then take
**either** Component 2 (the open-book exam on the same two kinds of text) **or** Component 3. The
qualification is linear: all components are taken at the end of the course.

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Assignment A: Modern Drama | Assignment B: Literary Heritage Texts |
|---|---|---|
| Marks | 30 | 30 |
| AOs | AO1 15, AO2 15 | AO1 10, AO2 10, AO4 10 |
| Text | One modern drama set text | One literary heritage set text |
| Task | Teacher-devised or self-generated | Teacher-devised or self-generated |
| Advisory length | 650–800 words | 650–800 words |
| Must include | Language, form and structure | Language, form and structure **and context** |

The word count is advisory: there is no prescribed length and no penalty for exceeding it.

## The assessment objectives

| AO | Wording | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Demonstrate a close knowledge and understanding of texts, maintaining a critical style and presenting an informed personal engagement | Knowing the whole text; an argued, personal response in an analytical register |
| AO2 | Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects | Dramatic methods (stage directions, dialogue, entrances, dramatic irony) and narrative methods |
| AO4 | Show understanding of the relationships between texts and the contexts in which they were written | Assignment B only: how context shapes the text and its reception |

### Which contexts count for AO4?

The specification lists the kinds of context teaching should cover:

- the writer's own life and situation, **only where these relate to the text**
- the historical setting, time and location of the text
- social and cultural contexts, such as attitudes in society
- the literary context, such as movements or genres
- how texts are received by different audiences at different times, for example a 21st-century reading

## The levels (both assignments)

| Level | Marks | Key words from the grids |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–6 | "Limited" knowledge; "minimal identification" of language, form and structure |
| 2 | 7–12 | "Some" knowledge; response "may be largely narrative"; "some comment" |
| 3 | 13–18 | "Sound" knowledge; "appropriate critical style"; "clearly relevant examples" |
| 4 | 19–24 | "Thorough" knowledge; "sustained critical style"; "sustained analysis"; for B, "detailed awareness" of context |
| 5 | 25–30 | "Assured"; "perceptive critical style"; "cohesive evaluation"; for B, context "integrated convincingly" |

Marks are awarded on a best-fit basis, balancing strengths and weaknesses. An essay with a Level 4
passage but mostly Level 3 qualities might receive a high Level 3.

## Setting a task: the specification's models

- Assignment A: "Explore the ways in which Priestley presents women in *An Inspector Calls*. You must
  consider language, form and structure."
- Assignment B: "How does Dickens portray city life in *Great Expectations*? You must consider language,
  form and structure and refer to the context of the novel."

A good task names one focus, invites argument ("explore the ways", "to what extent") and includes the
required AOs in its wording.

## Worked example: integrating context (Assignment B)

**Bolt-on context (Level 2–3):** "In the 1600s people believed in witches. In *Macbeth* there are
witches."

**Integrated context (Level 5 direction):** "Shakespeare makes the witches speak in a chanting rhythm
that sets them apart from the play's human characters, and for an audience that believed witchcraft
was real and dangerous this difference would have marked them as a threat, not a curiosity. A modern
audience may instead read them as an outward picture of Macbeth's own ambition, which shows how the
play's meaning shifts with its reception."

The second version links context to a method (AO2) and an argument (AO1), and it uses two kinds of context
from the specification's list: social and cultural belief, and reception by different audiences.

## Drama-specific methods for Assignment A

| Method | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Stage directions | Lighting (Priestley's change from "pink and intimate" to "brighter and harder"), entrances, exits |
| Dramatic irony | Birling's confident predictions in *An Inspector Calls* |
| Structure | Act divisions, cliffhangers, the narrator-chorus Alfieri in *A View from the Bridge* |
| Form | Tragedy, the "well-made play", non-naturalistic staging in *Curious Incident* |
| Dialogue | Who interrupts, who stays silent, shifts in register |

## Rules you must follow

- Work independently; you and your teacher sign the authentication sheet.
- Teachers may suggest general improvements at draft stage; corrections are your responsibility.
- Work must not be rewritten after marking.
- Acknowledge sources; teachers watch for essays derived from the internet or AI.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Retelling the play.** "Largely narrative" is a Level 2 descriptor.
- **Forgetting form in drama.** Write about the text as a play: staging, audience and performance.
- **Context in Assignment A only.** AO4 is assessed only in Assignment B, so put context where it scores.
- **A biographical paragraph.** The writer's life counts only where it relates to the text.
- **Two essays on the same kind of text.** One must be modern drama, one literary heritage.
- **Ignoring the task wording.** Both model tasks require language, form and structure.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should each Component 3 essay be?**
The advisory length is 650–800 words per assignment. There is no penalty for exceeding it, but a focused
essay usually scores better than a long one.

**Is context assessed in the drama assignment?**
No. Assignment A assesses AO1 and AO2 only. Context (AO4) carries 10 marks in Assignment B.

**Can our whole class write on the same play?**
Yes. The specification says learners may all write on the same text.

**Is Component 3 marked by Pearson?**
It is marked by your teacher and moderated by Pearson, which receives a digital sample of work.
