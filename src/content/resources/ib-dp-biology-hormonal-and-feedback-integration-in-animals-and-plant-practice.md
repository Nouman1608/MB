Questions 7 to 10 test additional higher level content, so they are **HL only**. ## Questions

**1.** The table shows the concentration of melatonin in the blood plasma of one adult over a day.

| Time | 18:00 | 20:00 | 22:00 | 00:00 | 02:00 | 04:00 | 06:00 | 08:00 | 12:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melatonin (pg cm⁻³) | 5 | 12 | 45 | 78 | 95 | 60 | 15 | 6 | 4 |

**(a)** State the time at which melatonin concentration was highest. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the peak concentration was than the concentration at 12:00. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how these data relate to the sleep-wake cycle. **[2]**

**2.** At rest, a person has a heart rate of 70 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 72 cm³. During vigorous exercise the heart rate is 160 beats per minute and the stroke volume is 120 cm³. At rest, 20% of cardiac output goes to skeletal muscle and 25% to the gut. During exercise these shares are 80% and 3%.

**(a)** Calculate cardiac output at rest and during exercise. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the blood flow to skeletal muscle during exercise. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the blood flow to the gut at rest and during exercise. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how epinephrine contributes to these changes. **[2]**

**3.** Outline how the hypothalamus and pituitary gland control the secretion of thyroxine, including the feedback involved. **[4]**

**4.** An ECG showed an interval of 0.75 s between successive R waves in a person lying down. Immediately after standing up it was 0.60 s.
**(a)** Calculate the heart rate lying down, the heart rate after standing and the percentage increase. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how baroreceptors bring about this change. **[2]**

**5.** A volunteer breathed air containing different carbon dioxide concentrations. Minute ventilation was recorded.

| Carbon dioxide in inhaled air (%) | 0.04 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minute ventilation (dm³ min⁻¹) | 6.5 | 12.0 | 26.0 | 58.0 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater minute ventilation was at 6% than at 0.04%. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the trend shown. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the control of ventilation rate that produces this trend. **[4]**

**6.** Outline how the central nervous system and the enteric nervous system control the movement of food through the digestive system. **[3]**

**7.** **(HL only)** Seedlings were exposed to light from one side. The angle of curvature of six seedlings per treatment was measured.

| Treatment | Angles of curvature (degrees) |
|---|---|
| Control | 39, 44, 41, 37, 46, 43 |
| Foil over the base | 40, 42, 38, 45, 41, 40 |
| Foil over the tip | 2, 0, 3, 1, 2, 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean angle for the control and for the tip covered with foil. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce what the data show about the site where light is detected. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two ways of making the results more reliable. **[2]**

**8.** **(HL only)** Segments of stem, 12.0 mm long, were incubated for six hours. Final lengths were: pH 7 buffer, 12.9 mm; pH 4.5 buffer, 15.0 mm; pH 7 buffer with auxin, 14.7 mm; pH 7 buffer with auxin and a chemical that blocks proton pumps, 12.9 mm.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in length in the pH 7 buffer and in the pH 4.5 buffer. **[2]**
**(b)** Using all the data, evaluate the hypothesis that auxin promotes growth by acidifying the cell wall. **[3]**

**9.** **(HL only)**
**(a)** When the shoot tip of a young plant is cut off, lateral buds begin to grow. Explain this using auxin and cytokinin. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline how the exchange of these two hormones helps coordinate root and shoot growth. **[2]**

**10.** **(HL only)** Green bananas kept alone took 7 days to ripen. Bananas kept in a closed bag with one ripe apple took 4 days.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in ripening time, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the advantage to a plant of controlling ripening by positive feedback. **[2]**

**11.** A person runs hard up a hill. Explain how epinephrine and the monitoring of blood chemistry help the body meet the demands of the muscles. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** **02:00** [1]
**(b)** 95 / 4 = **23.8 times** [1]
**(c)** Melatonin is high during the night, when sleep normally occurs, and low in the day [1]. It begins to rise in the evening and falls by morning, so it signals the cycle of sleeping and waking; light suppresses secretion, which resets the cycle each day [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "calculate" answer is best given with the working shown and the requested number of significant figures; 23.75 left unrounded would not match the instruction.

**2.** **(a)** Rest: 70 × 72 = **5040 cm³ min⁻¹** [1]. Exercise: 160 × 120 = **19 200 cm³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 0.80 × 19 200 = **15 360 cm³ min⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Rest 0.25 × 5040 = 1260; exercise 0.03 × 19 200 = 576; **1260 and 576 cm³ min⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Epinephrine increases heart rate and stroke volume, so cardiac output rises [1]. It widens blood vessels in skeletal muscle and narrows those to the gut, so a larger share of blood goes to muscle [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) asks about epinephrine, so naming the hormone's effect on both the heart and the vessels is what the question rewards; a general statement that "blood flows to muscle" is thin.

**3.** The hypothalamus releases a releasing hormone that acts on the pituitary [1]. The pituitary secretes thyroid-stimulating hormone, which stimulates the thyroid to secrete thyroxine [1]. Rising thyroxine in the blood inhibits the hypothalamus and pituitary [1], so less stimulation occurs and thyroxine returns towards its set level (negative feedback) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when the sequence is complete and the inhibition step is stated; listing the glands alone leaves the feedback unexplained.

**4.** **(a)** Lying: 60 / 0.75 = **80 beats per minute** [1]. Standing: 60 / 0.60 = **100 beats per minute** [1]. Increase: (100 − 80) / 80 × 100 = **25%** [1].
**(b)** Standing lowers blood pressure in the neck arteries, and baroreceptors in the carotid arteries and aortic arch send fewer impulses to the medulla [1]. The medulla sends more impulses along sympathetic nerves to the heart, raising heart rate and stroke volume, which restores pressure [1].
*Examiner insight:* A heart rate from an interval needs the conversion 60 divided by the interval in seconds; 1 / 0.75 gives beats per second, so state the unit.

**5.** **(a)** 58.0 / 6.5 = **8.9 times** [1]
**(b)** **Minute ventilation increases as the carbon dioxide concentration rises, and the increase becomes steeper** (positive correlation) [1]
**(c)** More carbon dioxide in the blood forms carbonic acid, which lowers blood pH [1]. Chemoreceptors in the brainstem detect the pH fall [1]. The medulla sends impulses to the diaphragm and intercostal muscles, so ventilation rate and depth increase [1]. This removes carbon dioxide, pH rises back towards normal and the stimulus declines (negative feedback) [1].
*Examiner insight:* The chain from carbon dioxide to pH to receptor to medulla to muscles is usually expected in order; naming the receptor type without saying what it detects loses the link.

**6.** Swallowing is started voluntarily, under control of the CNS [1]. After this, peristalsis in the gut is involuntary and controlled by the ENS in the gut wall, which coordinates waves of contraction so material moves in one direction [1]. Egestion of faeces is again under voluntary control from the CNS [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" with three marks suggests three distinct points; naming the two systems with their regions of control is clearer than describing the muscles alone.

**7.** **(a)** Control: 250 / 6 = **41.7 degrees** [1]. Tip covered: 12 / 6 = **2.0 degrees** [1].
**(b)** Covering the tip almost abolished curvature [1], while covering the base made no difference (mean 41.0 against 41.7), so light is detected at the tip, not the lower region where bending occurs [1].
**(c)** Any two: use more seedlings; use seedlings of the same species and age; keep light intensity and temperature the same; repeat the experiment [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* When "deduce" is used, quoting the comparison between treatments in the data is generally needed, not just the conclusion.

**8.** **(a)** pH 7: (12.9 − 12.0) / 12.0 × 100 = **7.5%** [1]. pH 4.5: (15.0 − 12.0) / 12.0 × 100 = **25.0%** [1].
**(b)** Auxin at pH 7 gave a 22.5% increase, far above the 7.5% of the buffer alone [1], similar to the effect of acid alone (25.0%) [1]. When proton pumps were blocked, auxin gave only 7.5%, so auxin needs proton pumping to act, which supports the hypothesis [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation is stronger when it uses the inhibitor treatment and the acid treatment together with figures from the table; a single comparison supports less.

**9.** **(a)** The shoot tip makes auxin, which inhibits lateral buds [1]. With the tip removed, auxin falls, so cytokinin from the root tips promotes the growth of lateral buds [1].
**(b)** Root tips make cytokinin, which is transported to the shoots, and shoot tips make auxin, which is transported to the roots [1]. Each organ's growth is regulated by a signal from the other, so root and shoot growth stay in step [1].
*Examiner insight:* State where each hormone is made and which way it is transported, as the explanation depends on this.

**10.** **(a)** (7 − 4) / 7 × 100 = **43%** [1]
**(b)** Ripe apples release ethylene gas, which in the closed bag reaches the bananas and stimulates ripening [1]. The bananas also make more ethylene as they ripen, so ripening speeds up (positive feedback) [1].
**(c)** Positive feedback makes ripening rapid [1] and synchronised across the fruits, so seeds are available to animal dispersers at the same time [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers on advantage gain more by naming rapidity and synchrony than by saying only that fruit "ripens faster".

**11.** Exercise increases respiration, so more carbon dioxide enters the blood and blood pH falls [1]. Chemoreceptors detect the pH change and signal the medulla [1]. The medulla increases ventilation through the diaphragm and intercostal muscles [1] and increases heart rate and stroke volume [1], so more oxygen is delivered and carbon dioxide removed. Epinephrine from the adrenal glands adds to the heart effects and widens muscle blood vessels, diverting blood to muscle [1]. It also stimulates the liver to convert glycogen to glucose for respiration [1].
*Examiner insight:* A six-mark explanation needs several distinct linked points; rewording one effect adds nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost
- Giving a rate calculation without a unit or without the working.
- Naming baroreceptors for chemical changes, or chemoreceptors for pressure.
- Locating baroreceptors "in the heart".
- Missing the pH step in ventilation answers.
- Forgetting to state that the feedback is negative.
- Leaving out the inhibition step in hypothalamus and pituitary answers.
- Saying that auxin "makes cells grow" with no mechanism.
- Mixing up where auxin and cytokinin are made.
- Quoting one result instead of comparing treatments.
