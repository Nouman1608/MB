## What the syllabus lists

- Vegetation: biomass productivity (biodiversity, limited nutrient cycling, fragility); adaptation to extreme temperatures; physical and physiological drought.
- Soil processes: upward capillary movement of water and minerals (salinisation).
- Desertification (natural and human factors) degrading soils and vegetation in semi-arid environments.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Net primary productivity | Rate of new biomass stored by plants after respiration |
| Endemic species | Found naturally only in one area |
| Biological soil crust | Living surface layer of cyanobacteria, lichens and mosses that binds soil |
| Ephemeral | Plant completing its life cycle in weeks after rain |
| Phreatophyte | Deep-rooted plant reaching groundwater |
| Succulent | Plant storing water in stems or leaves |
| Xerophyte | Plant tolerating drought through water-saving features |
| CAM photosynthesis | Stomata open at night; carbon stored and used by day |
| Halophyte | Salt-tolerant plant |
| Physical drought | Too little water in the soil |
| Physiological drought | Water present but cannot be taken up, e.g. because of salt |
| Capillary action | Upward movement of water through fine soil pores |
| Calcrete / gypcrete | Hard layers of calcium carbonate / gypsum in desert soils |
| Desertification | Land degradation in drylands from climatic variation and human activity |

## Productivity and cycling recall

- **NPP very low:** water limits photosynthesis (stomata closed), sparse cover, pulsed growth, high respiration.
- **Biodiversity:** low overall but many endemics; concentrated at wadis, oases, playa margins.
- **Nutrient stores:** small biomass, very small litter, relatively large soil store.
- **Flows:** slow decomposition; nutrients released in bursts after rain.
- **Fragility:** slow regrowth; soil crusts crushed by trampling and vehicles.

## Adaptations recall

| Problem | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Little water | Ephemerals; deep roots; shallow spreading roots; water storage |
| Water loss | Small or spiny leaves; waxy cuticle; sunken stomata with hairs; leaf shedding; CAM |
| Heat | Pale or hairy surfaces; vertical leaves; shading spines; small leaves |
| Salt | Halophytes store or excrete salt |

## Salinisation in four steps

1. Evaporation exceeds precipitation, so water moves upward.
2. Capillary rise carries dissolved salts.
3. Water evaporates at the surface, leaving salts.
4. Salt crusts form; calcrete and gypcrete form lower down.

Irrigation without drainage raises the water table and speeds this up (Indus basin; Aral Sea region). Remedies: drainage, leaching, drip irrigation, salt-tolerant crops, lined canals.

## Desertification recall

- **Natural:** drought, rainfall variability, climate change, wind and storm erosion.
- **Human:** overgrazing, overcultivation, fuelwood cutting, poor irrigation.
- **Pressures:** population growth, poverty, insecure tenure, settled herders.
- **Feedback:** cover lost, crusting, more runoff, less moisture, fewer plants, higher albedo.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Drought escapers** avoid the dry season as seeds; **drought avoiders** reach or store water; **drought tolerators** survive water loss.
- **Salinisation** is salt build-up near the surface; **waterlogging** is saturation from a high water table; irrigation can cause both.
- **Desertification** is land degradation; **desert expansion** is a change in climate zone. They are not the same.

## Mini essay plan

*"Desert vegetation is too fragile to recover from human disturbance." How far do you agree?*

1. **Define** fragility: slow regrowth and easily damaged soils.
2. **For:** low NPP and slow growth; biological soil crusts crushed by trampling; topsoil lost to wind and flash floods.
3. **Against:** ephemerals and seed banks respond rapidly to rain; regreening in parts of the Sahel; farmer-managed regeneration from living roots.
4. **Conditions:** recovery depends on rainfall, grazing pressure and whether soil remains.
5. **Judgement:** recovery is possible but slow, and fails once feedbacks remove soil.

## Exam traps

- "Deserts have no nutrients."
- Physiological drought defined as no water.
- Salinisation without capillary rise and evaporation.
- Desertification described as moving dunes.
- Adaptations listed without the problem each solves.
- Desertification blamed only on people or only on climate.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why NPP is low in hot deserts.
2. Which nutrient store is relatively large in a desert, and why?
3. Why is decomposition slow?
4. What are biological soil crusts and why are they fragile?
5. How do ephemerals survive drought?
6. Explain CAM photosynthesis.
7. How do sunken stomata reduce water loss?
8. Distinguish physical and physiological drought.
9. Why does water move upward in desert soils?
10. How does irrigation cause salinisation?
11. Give two remedies for salinisation.
12. Describe one positive feedback in desertification.

### Answers

1. Any two: water shortage closes stomata, sparse cover, pulsed growth, high respiration.
2. The soil store, because nutrients from weathering are not leached away by rain.
3. Decomposers need moisture, which is absent most of the time.
4. Thin living layers of cyanobacteria, lichens and mosses binding the surface; trampling crushes them and they regrow slowly.
5. They complete their life cycle quickly after rain and survive dry periods as dormant seeds.
6. Stomata open at night to take in carbon dioxide, stored as an acid and used for photosynthesis by day while stomata stay shut.
7. Humid air is trapped in the pit and among hairs, reducing the gradient for water vapour to escape.
8. Physical: too little soil water. Physiological: water is present but salty, so it cannot be taken up.
9. Evaporation at the surface exceeds rainfall, drawing water up by capillary action.
10. Excess water without drainage raises the water table, so capillary rise and evaporation leave salts at the surface.
11. Any two: drainage, leaching, drip irrigation, salt-tolerant crops, lined canals.
12. Losing plant cover lets soil crust, so infiltration falls, less moisture is stored and even fewer plants can grow.
