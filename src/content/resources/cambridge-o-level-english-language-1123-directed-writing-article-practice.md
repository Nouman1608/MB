> 400–450 words. Question 12 is a full task at the syllabus tariff of 25 marks (15 writing, 10 reading);
> the other questions practise its separate skills with practice marks.

## Section A

**1.** Write two headlines for a school magazine article about a proposal to replace the school's grass field with an artificial pitch: one as a question, one using alliteration. **[2]**

**2.** Write a scene hook (one or two sentences) for an article about a town's last independent bookshop closing. **[2]**

**3.** Rewrite this opening so that it suits an article rather than a speech: "Good morning, readers! Today I'm going to tell you about the new bus lane." **[2]**

**4.** Rewrite this sentence so that the source is attributed and the quotation is brief: "Text 1 says that the new skate park will be a safe, supervised space where young people can meet friends and develop their skills." **[2]**

**5.** Explain why "In conclusion, there are many points on both sides" is a weak ending for an article. **[1]**

**6.** Rewrite for a local newspaper: "Loads of kids are well angry about the library closing." **[1]**

## Section B

Read the two texts (original, written for this practice set).

> **Text 1: from the festival organiser's press release**
>
> Riverside Sounds, a two-day music festival, wants to come to Victoria Park next August. We expect
> fifteen thousand visitors, who will spend money in local cafés, shops and hotels. Twenty local young
> people will be offered paid work experience in event management. All litter will be cleared within
> forty-eight hours, and music will end at 10.30 p.m. each night.
>
> **Text 2: from a statement by the Victoria Park Residents' Association**
>
> Victoria Park is the only green space for thousands of families. Fencing it off for a week to set up
> and take down the stage would shut out children and dog walkers in the middle of the summer holidays.
> Festivals in other towns have left grass damaged for months. Residents near the park are worried
> about noise and crowds late at night. We are not against music; we are against losing our park.

**7.** Give one point you would use from each text and one claim you would evaluate, for an article for the youth page of the local newspaper. **[3]**

**8.** Write a headline and an opening paragraph (about 50 words) for the article. **[3]**

**9.** Write a paragraph (about 80 words) that evaluates the organiser's claims about benefits to the town. **[4]**

**10.** Write a paragraph (about 60 words) that develops the residents' concern for young people's readers. **[3]**

**11.** Write a final paragraph (about 40 words) that returns to your opening. **[2]**

**12.** Write an article for the youth page of the local newspaper giving your views on whether the festival should be held in Victoria Park. Use and evaluate ideas from both texts. Write about 250–350 words. **[25]**

## Answers

**1.** For example: question, "Is plastic grass the future of school sport?" **[1]**; alliteration, "Pitch Perfect or Pitch Problem?" **[1]**

**2.** For example: "The bell above the door still rings when you walk into Page and Pen, but in a month's time, nobody will hear it." **[2]** One mark for a vivid scene, one for a clear link to the issue.

**3.** For example: "The new bus lane on Hill Road has divided drivers, cyclists and shopkeepers. So who is right?" **[2]** One mark for removing the greeting and spoken announcement, one for a hook that introduces the issue.

**4.** For example: "The council describes the skate park as 'a safe, supervised space' for young people." **[2]** One mark for attribution, one for a brief quotation.

**5.** It is an essay formula that gives no judgement and nothing memorable for the reader **[1]**.

**6.** For example: "Many young people are angry about the library's closure." **[1]**

**7.** One mark each:
- Use from Text 1: twenty local young people would be offered paid work experience **[1]**.
- Use from Text 2: fencing off the park would shut out children during the summer holidays **[1]**.
- Evaluate: the organiser's forecast of fifteen thousand visitors spending locally is an estimate from the company that would profit, not evidence **[1]**.

**8.** Model:

> **Sounds good? The battle for Victoria Park**
>
> Every summer, Victoria Park belongs to us: football at lunchtime, picnics in the evening, the
> younger ones on the swings. Next August, a music festival wants to borrow it. Young people stand to
> gain, but we may also stand to lose.

Marking: clear, engaging headline **[1]**; hook suited to young readers **[1]**; the issue and a viewpoint introduced **[1]**.

**9.** Model:

> The organisers promise a boost for local cafés, shops and hotels from fifteen thousand visitors. That
> may be true, but it is their own estimate, and they are hardly neutral. Visitors who camp inside the
> festival may spend little outside it. The offer of paid work experience for twenty young people is
> more concrete, and genuinely valuable, but twenty places in a town of this size is a small number to
> set against a park closed for a week.

Marking: uses the organiser's claims **[1]**; evaluates the visitor estimate and its source **[1]**; weighs the work experience fairly **[1]**; register suited to the newspaper's youth page **[1]**.

**10.** Model:

> For many of us, the park is not a luxury. Families in the flats near Victoria Park have no gardens,
> and the summer holidays are exactly when younger brothers and sisters need somewhere to play. A
> fence around the park for a week would hit them hardest, and damaged grass could spoil the rest of
> the summer too.

Marking: uses the residents' concern **[1]**; develops it for young readers **[1]**; extends it with a consequence **[1]**.

**11.** Model:

> Music and green space should not have to compete. If the festival can use the field by the river
> instead, and give the grass back as it found it, then next summer the park can still belong to us.

Marking: returns to the opening idea of the park belonging to young people **[1]**; memorable judgement **[1]**.

**12.** Marked out of 25: reading (R3, R5) and writing (W1–W5).
- **Reading:** uses points from both texts; develops them (summer-holiday use, families without gardens, the value of work experience); evaluates them (the organiser's forecast and interest, the size of the work-experience offer, evidence of damage from other towns, whether 10.30 p.m. answers residents' noise concerns) **[10]**.
- **Writing:** article conventions (headline, hook, developed paragraphs, a viewpoint, a memorable ending); register suited to the youth page of a local newspaper; varied vocabulary and sentences; accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar **[15]**.

Support, opposition or a compromise can score fully if it is argued from the sources. Lower-level responses have no headline, open with a greeting, retell the texts, or end with "In conclusion".

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4:** keeping the whole sentence in quotation marks.
- **Question 9:** accepting the visitor figure without questioning who provided it.
- **Question 10:** describing the park instead of developing the concern.
- **Question 12:** forgetting the youth-page readers and writing as if to the council.
