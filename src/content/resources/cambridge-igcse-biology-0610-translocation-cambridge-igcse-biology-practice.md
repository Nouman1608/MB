These questions cover sub-topic **8.4 Translocation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The whole of 8.4 is Supplement content, so every question here is **(Extended)**; a Core candidate needs only the 8.1 fact that phloem carries sucrose and amino acids. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Revise first with the [translocation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-translocation-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [translocation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-translocation-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** *(Extended)* Describe translocation. **[2]**

**2.** *(Extended)* Which is a sink in a flowering plant in summer?

A: a mature leaf in bright light

B: a developing seed

C: a sprouting potato tuber

D: the food store of a germinating seed **[1]**

**3.** *(Extended)* State what is meant by a source. **[1]**

**4.** *(Extended)* State **two** ways in which a sink may deal with the sucrose it receives. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Name the sugar transported in phloem and the substance it is made from in the leaf. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why roots act as sinks. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Extended)* A bean plant has mature leaves halfway up its stem, a growing shoot tip at the top, developing pods with seeds below the leaves, and roots in the soil.

**(a)** Identify the source.

**(b)** Name **three** sinks and state whether sucrose travels up or down to reach each.

**(c)** Explain why this shows that translocation is not the same as water transport. **[6]**

**8.** *(Extended)* Compare transport in xylem with translocation in phloem. Refer to the substances moved, the direction and what causes the movement. **[4]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A seed is a sink while it develops on the parent plant but its food store is a source when it germinates. Explain this change. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A carrot plant grows for two years. In its first year it produces leaves and a large swollen root. In its second year it uses the root's store to grow a tall flowering shoot.

**(a)** State the role of the carrot root in the first year and explain your answer.

**(b)** State the role of the carrot root in the second year and explain your answer.

**(c)** Suggest what the flowers do with some of the sugar they receive. **[5]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Amino acids are translocated as well as sucrose.

**(a)** State the mineral ion a plant needs to absorb to make amino acids.

**(b)** Name **two** sinks that would need large amounts of amino acids, and explain why. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "Leaves are always sources, roots are always sinks, and phloem carries food down the plant." Evaluate this statement. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem [1] from sources to sinks [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Movement of food in the plant" scores nothing; name the substances, the tissue and the direction from source to sink.

**2.** **B** (a developing seed) [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** A part of a plant that releases sucrose or amino acids [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Any two from: uses it in respiration; converts it to cellulose for new cell walls; stores it as starch; uses it to make nectar [2]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Sucrose [1]; glucose (made in photosynthesis) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Roots cannot photosynthesise because they receive no light [1], so they use sucrose sent from the leaves for respiration and growth [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7. (a)** The mature leaves [1].

**(b)** Shoot tip: up [1]; developing pods or seeds: down [1]; roots: down [1].

**(c)** Sucrose moves both up and down from the leaves, whereas water in xylem moves only upwards [1]; translocation happens in phloem, water transport in xylem [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**8.** Xylem carries water and mineral ions; phloem carries sucrose and amino acids [1]. Xylem transport is upwards only; translocation is up or down [1]. Movement in xylem is caused by the transpiration pull from the leaves [1]; translocation goes from sources to sinks, wherever they are in the plant [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**9.** While developing, the seed receives sucrose and amino acids through the phloem from the parent plant [1] and stores them as food, so it is a sink [1]. During germination the stored food is broken down and released to the growing root and shoot [1], so the store releases sugar and amino acids and is a source until the seedling's leaves photosynthesise [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**10. (a)** A sink [1]; it receives sucrose from the photosynthesising leaves and stores it as starch [1].

**(b)** A source [1]; its stored starch is broken down to sugar, which is released and sent to the growing flowering shoot [1].

**(c)** Uses it to make nectar, which attracts insects for pollination / uses it in respiration for growth [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**11. (a)** Nitrate ions [1].

**(b)** Any two growing regions, such as shoot tips, root tips or developing seeds [1]; they make large amounts of protein for new cells or for storage, and proteins are made from amino acids [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**12.** Partly correct: mature leaves are usually sources because they photosynthesise and release sucrose [1]. But young, growing leaves are sinks, because they receive and use sucrose [1]. Roots are usually sinks for sugar [1], but a storage root, such as a carrot in its second year, can be a source [1]. Phloem carries substances up as well as down, for example from leaves to a growing shoot tip [1]. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement on each part of the statement, with an example that supports or contradicts it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "food" or "glucose" instead of sucrose and amino acids.
- Placing translocation in xylem.
- Defining a sink as a store only.
- Saying phloem moves substances only downwards.
- Treating every leaf as a source.
- Explaining a role switch without saying what the part does at each stage.
