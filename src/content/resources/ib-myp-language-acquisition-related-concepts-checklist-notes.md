# Related concepts: checklist notes

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use.

Companion to the reviewed [key and related concepts revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-key-and-related-concepts-revision-notes/). Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/).

## From the IB brief

- Related concepts are narrower than key concepts and are grounded in the subject.
- The brief's examples for language acquisition: **word choice, conventions, idiom**.
- Your school chooses which related concepts its units use.

## Three-question checklist

| Concept | Ask | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Word choice | Does each word suit the audience and meaning? | Swap vague or wrong-register words |
| Conventions | Does it look and sound like this text type? | Add or correct opening, closing, layout |
| Idiom | Am I sure of the expression? | Keep it, replace it with a plain phrase, or explain it |

## Quick examples (English)

- Register: "Could you please..." (formal) against "Can you..." (informal).
- Convention: a formal letter opens with a greeting and closes with a sign-off.
- Idiom: "break the ice" means to start a conversation, not to break anything.

## Traps

- Over-using idioms to look advanced.
- Mixing informal and formal in one text.

## Self-test

1. Name the three related-concept examples. 2. Rewrite "Give me a pen" for a teacher. 3. What does "break the ice" mean?

**Answers:** 1. Word choice, conventions, idiom. 2. "Could I borrow a pen, please?" 3. To start a conversation in an awkward situation.
