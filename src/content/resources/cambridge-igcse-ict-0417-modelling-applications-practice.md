## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following is **not** one of the modelling applications named in the syllabus? **[1]**

A: Weather forecasting

B: Traffic management

C: Stock control at a supermarket

D: Flood water management

**2.** State what is meant by a *variable* in a computer model. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** types of data used as inputs to a weather forecasting model. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by a *what-if* question. **[1]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is a disadvantage of computer modelling? **[1]**

A: Dangerous situations can be tested safely

B: The results depend on the accuracy of the data and formulas

C: Many scenarios can be tested quickly

D: No real prototype has to be built

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student models his monthly budget in a spreadsheet. His income is £1,250. His outgoings are rent £500, food £220, phone £35 and travel £95.

**(a)** Calculate his total monthly outgoings. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how much he saves each month. **[1]**

**(c)** His travel costs rise by 20%. Calculate his new monthly saving, showing your working. **[2]**

**(d)** At the new saving, how many months will it take him to save £2,286? **[1]**

**(e)** Explain **one** reason why a spreadsheet model is useful for this task. **[2]**

**7.** Engineers are designing a footbridge across a river.

**(a)** Describe how a computer model would be used to test the design. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage of relying on the model. **[2]**

**8.** A council is deciding whether to replace a set of traffic lights with a roundabout.

**(a)** Give **two** inputs the traffic model would need. **[2]**

**(b)** State **one** output the council would use to compare the two layouts. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the council should use a model rather than trying the roundabout on the real road. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why computers are needed to produce weather forecasts. **[3]**

**10.** A town uses a flood model.

**(a)** Explain why the model's predictions may not match a real flood. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** ways the town could use the model's outputs. **[2]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using computer modelling rather than humans when designing a tall office building. **[6]**

**12.** Personal finance advisers often use computer models with their clients. Evaluate the use of a computer model for planning a family's savings. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Stock control belongs to computers in the retail industry. **[1]**

**2.** An input value in the model that can be changed to see its effect on the results [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Air pressure [1]; temperature [1] (also credit: humidity, wind speed, wind direction, rainfall). **[2]**

**4.** Changing one or more inputs in a model to see how the outputs change [1]. **[1]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 500 + 220 + 35 + 95 = £850 [1].

**(b)** 1,250 − 850 = £400 [1].

**(c)** 20% of £95 = £19, so travel becomes £114 and outgoings become £869 [1]; new saving = 1,250 − 869 = £381 [1].

**(d)** 2,286 ÷ 381 = 6 months [1].

**(e)** Formulas recalculate automatically when a value is changed [1], so he can test many what-if changes quickly without recalculating by hand or making arithmetic errors [1]. **[7]**

**7. (a)** The design, materials and dimensions are entered into the model [1]; loads such as the weight of crowds and the force of wind or floodwater are applied as variables [1]; the engineers see where stress is greatest and change the design until it is safe [1].

**(b)** The model is only as accurate as its data and rules [1], so if a factor is missing, such as an unusually strong storm, the real bridge could behave differently from the prediction [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Number of vehicles arriving per minute on each road [1]; the current light timings or the proposed layout of the roundabout [1] (also credit: number of lanes, pedestrian crossings, time of day).

**(b)** Queue lengths at peak times [1] (also credit: average journey time through the junction).

**(c)** Building a roundabout is expensive and causes disruption [1]; the model lets the council compare both layouts and many traffic levels at no risk before spending money [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Forecasting uses huge amounts of data from weather stations, satellites, balloons and buoys [1]; the model must carry out a vast number of calculations [1], which must be done quickly enough for the forecast to be useful before the weather arrives [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The model simplifies reality and may not include every factor, such as a blocked drain [1], and the rainfall or river data entered may be inaccurate [1].

**(b)** Decide where to build flood barriers [1]; identify which homes need flood warnings and plan evacuation routes [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Advantages: cheaper, because no physical prototype has to be built [1]; safer, because the building can be tested against earthquakes and high winds without risk to anyone [1]; faster, because many designs can be compared quickly [1]; variables such as materials can be changed and the model rerun [1]. Disadvantages: the model may not include every real-life condition, so the real building could behave differently [1]; specialist software and expert staff are expensive [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: the adviser can test many what-if scenarios, such as a pay rise or an increase in interest rates [1]; results are calculated quickly and accurately [1]; charts make the results easy for the family to understand [1]. Against: the predictions depend on assumptions such as future interest rates, which may be wrong [1]; unexpected events, such as job loss, may not be included [1]. Judgement: the model is a useful planning tool, but the family should review it regularly and not treat it as a guarantee [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out working in calculation questions, so a wrong final figure earns nothing.
- Writing "it is accurate" as an advantage; accuracy depends on the data.
- Describing a model without naming any inputs or outputs.
- Confusing modelling with computer control.
