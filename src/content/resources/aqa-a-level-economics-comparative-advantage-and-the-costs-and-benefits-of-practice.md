## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between absolute advantage and comparative advantage. **[3]**

**2.** Countries G and H make only wheat and cloth. The hours of labour needed per unit are: G needs 3 for wheat and 9 for cloth; H needs 4 for wheat and 6 for cloth.

**(a)** Calculate each country's opportunity cost of one unit of cloth, in wheat. **[2]**
**(b)** State which country has the comparative advantage in each good. **[2]**

**3.** Using question 2, G and H agree to exchange one unit of cloth for two units of wheat. Show that both countries gain from this price. **[3]**

**4.** G has 3,600 labour hours and H has 4,800. Before trade, half of each country's hours go to each good.

**(a)** Calculate world output of wheat and of cloth before trade. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate world output if each country specialises fully in the good in which it has the comparative advantage. **[2]**
**(c)** At the price in question 3, H sells 300 units of cloth to G. Show that both countries consume more cloth than before trade, with no less wheat. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two assumptions of the model of comparative advantage and how each, if untrue, weakens the case for specialisation. **[4]**

**6.** A small ceramics firm has fixed costs of 90,000 a year and variable costs of 6 per unit. It sells 5,000 units a year at home. Access to export markets lets it sell 15,000 units.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in average cost. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why exporting reduces average cost, and give one reason why the gain might not last. **[3]**

**7.** Exports are 30% of Country M's national income. Metal ore is 50% of its exports. The world price of the ore falls by 12%, and the volume sold does not change.

**(a)** Calculate the fall in national income as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this shows a cost of over-specialisation, and suggest one way a government could reduce it. **[3]**

**8.** In one year, imports cause 9,200 job losses in Country N, while export industries create 5,700 jobs. The labour force is 1,400,000.

**(a)** Calculate the net job loss and express it as a percentage of the labour force. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why unemployment among the displaced workers might last longer than the year. **[3]**

**9.** Countries S and T make steel and software. S needs 5 hours per unit of steel and 10 per unit of software. T needs 8 and 12.

**(a)** Identify which country has the comparative advantage in each good. **[2]**
**(b)** After a training programme, S needs only 6 hours per unit of software. Show how the comparative advantage changes. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how changes like this help to account for changes in the UK pattern of trade. **[3]**

**10.** Explain two reasons why the pattern of UK trade with the rest of the world might change over time. **[6]**

**11.** "Free trade benefits every country and everyone within it." Evaluate this view. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Absolute advantage means a country is more productive: it uses fewer inputs, or gets more output from the same inputs [1]. Comparative advantage means a country sacrifices less of another good to make it, so its opportunity cost is smaller [1]. A country can have an absolute advantage in both goods but a comparative advantage in only one [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that mentions only "better at" earns little for comparative advantage. Opportunity cost must be named.

**2. (a)** G: 9 ÷ 3 = 3 wheat [1]. H: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 wheat [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** H has the lower opportunity cost of cloth (1.5 against 3), so H has the comparative advantage in cloth [1]. G's opportunity cost of wheat is 3 ÷ 9 = 0.33 cloth, against H's 4 ÷ 6 = 0.67, so G has it in wheat [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Always show the division. A correct country with no working may not show that you used opportunity cost.

**3.** For G, one cloth costs 3 wheat to make, but buying it costs 2 wheat, so G saves 1 wheat per cloth [1]. For H, making one cloth costs 1.5 wheat of lost output, but it receives 2 wheat, so H gains 0.5 wheat per cloth [1]. The price of 2 lies between 1.5 and 3, so both gain [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The point that the price must lie between the two opportunity costs is what completes this answer.

**4. (a)** G: 1,800 hours on wheat gives 1,800 ÷ 3 = 600 wheat; 1,800 hours on cloth gives 1,800 ÷ 9 = 200 cloth. H: 2,400 ÷ 4 = 600 wheat; 2,400 ÷ 6 = 400 cloth [1]. World: 1,200 wheat and 600 cloth [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** G makes 3,600 ÷ 3 = 1,200 wheat; H makes 4,800 ÷ 6 = 800 cloth [1]. World output is 1,200 wheat and 800 cloth, so 200 more cloth and the same wheat [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** G gets 300 cloth for 600 wheat, so G has 1,200 − 600 = 600 wheat and 300 cloth [1]. H has 800 − 300 = 500 cloth and 600 wheat [1]. Before trade G had 600 wheat and 200 cloth, and H had 600 wheat and 400 cloth, so each has 100 more cloth and the same wheat [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare each country with its own pre-trade position, not with the world total.

**5.** Constant opportunity costs [1]: if costs rise as output of one good grows, full specialisation is less beneficial and the gain is smaller [1]. Free movement of resources [1]: if workers cannot move to the growing industry, unemployment results and the gain is reduced or lost [1]. (Other valid assumptions, such as no transport costs, are also creditworthy.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each assumption needs its own consequence. Two assumptions listed with one shared comment is a weaker answer.

**6. (a)** Total cost at 5,000 units is 90,000 + 6 × 5,000 = 120,000, so average cost is 24 [1]. At 15,000 units it is 90,000 + 6 × 15,000 = 180,000, so average cost is 12 [1]. Change = (12 − 24) ÷ 24 × 100 = **−50%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Fixed costs are spread over more units [1], and a larger scale may allow specialised machinery or bulk buying [1]. The gain might not last if export demand falls or rival firms respond with lower prices, so output drops back [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that fixed costs are spread, not just that costs are lower. The economies of scale idea must be explained.

**7. (a)** Loss = 0.30 × 0.50 × 12% [1] = **1.8%** of national income [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Reliance on one export means a price change in one market has a large effect on total income [1]. The country has no other exports to offset the fall [1]. A government could encourage diversification, for example through investment in education or support for new industries [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figure from part (a) in part (b) to link the calculation to the argument.

**8. (a)** Net loss = 9,200 − 5,700 = 3,500 jobs [1]. 3,500 ÷ 1,400,000 [1] = **0.25%** of the labour force [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The skills of workers who lost jobs may not match the new jobs [1]. They may live far from where the jobs are created, and moving is costly [1]. Retraining takes time, so unemployment persists, which is structural [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the word structural and say why. A general claim that "there are no jobs" gains little.

**9. (a)** S: software costs 10 ÷ 5 = 2 steel; T: 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 steel [1]. T has the lower cost of software, so T has the comparative advantage in software and S in steel [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** S's new cost of software is 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2 steel [1], which is below T's 1.5, so S now has the comparative advantage in software [1]. T's cost of steel (8 ÷ 12 = 0.67 software) is now below S's (5 ÷ 6 = 0.83), so T moves to steel [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Investment in skills and technology changes productivity and so opportunity costs [1]. When the UK, or its trading partners, improve in some industries, the goods that are traded change [1]. Imports and exports shift towards sectors where the UK now has the lower opportunity cost [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate both countries' new costs. The comparison between them is the evidence.

**10.** Reason 1, for example growth of emerging economies [1]: faster-growing countries become new markets for UK exports and new sources of imports [1], so more trade shifts towards them [1]. Reason 2, for example changing comparative advantage towards services [1]: investment in skills and technology lowers the UK's opportunity cost of services relative to goods [1], so exports and imports shift towards services [1]. (Other valid reasons include exchange rates, trade agreements and relative costs.) **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give each reason a developed chain. Two reasons named with one line each stay thin.

**11.** Gains: specialisation according to comparative advantage raises total output [1]. Larger markets allow economies of scale [1]. Competition lowers prices and improves efficiency [1]. Consumers gain choice [1]. Costs: structural unemployment where industries close [1]. Over-specialisation exposes a country to price shocks [1]. Gains and losses are spread unequally across groups and regions [1]. Evaluation: the gains depend on assumptions such as mobile resources and constant costs, which often fail [1]. Time matters, since costs arrive quickly while gains take longer [1]. Judgement: trade usually raises national income, but "everyone" is too strong, because some workers lose unless policies such as retraining reduce the harm [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Take a position on the quotation. A list of benefits and costs with no final judgement stays descriptive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using hours per unit directly instead of dividing to get opportunity cost.
- Inverting the ratio and naming the wrong country.
- Giving a price outside the range between the two opportunity costs.
- Comparing a country's post-trade position with the world total.
- Listing benefits or costs with no chain of reasoning.
- Not using the data in the question.
- Forgetting to say who bears a cost.
- Concluding without a judgement.
