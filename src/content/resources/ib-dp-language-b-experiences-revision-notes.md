B subject brief for first assessment 2020, which remains the examined course until the new syllabus
is first assessed in May 2029, so they apply to the 2026, 2027 and 2028 sessions.

## The theme in one box

```
Guiding principle   "Explore and tell the stories of the events, experiences
                     and journeys that shape our lives."
Optional topics      leisure activities · holidays and travel · life stories ·
                     rites of passage · customs and traditions · migration
Possible questions   1. How does our past shape our present and our future?
                     2. How and why do different cultures mark important
                        moments in life?
```

- The **theme** is prescribed. The **topics** are optional and recommended, so your class may have
  studied some of them or all of them.
- Key verb: **tell**. Experiences is the narrative theme. Expect to follow and produce stories that
  move through time.
- One-line contrast: Identities = who we are; Experiences = what happened to us and what it means
  now; Sharing the planet = challenges communities face.

## Vocabulary map

| Topic | Nouns | Verbs | Evaluative language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leisure activities | club, pastime, match, rehearsal | to take up, to give up, to train | rewarding, relaxing, a waste of time |
| Holidays and travel | itinerary, delay, souvenir, hostel | to set off, to book, to explore | breathtaking, exhausting, overrated |
| Life stories | turning point, childhood, milestone | to grow up, to overcome, to look back | proud, grateful, a defining moment |
| Rites of passage | ceremony, graduation, coming of age | to mark, to celebrate, to leave home | emotional, symbolic, long-awaited |
| Customs and traditions | festival, ritual, heritage, costume | to pass down, to revive, to observe | age-old, meaningful, fading |
| Migration | border, arrival, community, homesickness | to emigrate, to settle, to adapt | uprooted, welcomed, torn between |

Make the same table in your target language. The evaluative column is the one most often missing: it shows what the experience meant.

## Narrative toolkit

| Tool | English model | What to find in your target language |
|---|---|---|
| Completed past | We *missed* the last bus. | Your completed-past form (e.g. French *passé composé*, Spanish *indefinido*) |
| Background or habit | It *was* late; we *used to* walk home. | Your background/habitual form (e.g. French *imparfait*, Spanish *imperfecto*) |
| Earlier past | She *had* already *left*. | Pluperfect or equivalent |
| Bridge to now | *Since then* I *have* always… | Markers and tenses that link past to present |
| Hypothesis | *If* I *had stayed*, I *would have*… | Past conditional structures |
| Reported speech | He said that he *had* arrived alone. | Tense and pronoun shifts after "said that" |
| Future hope | One day I *hope to* go back. | Future and wish structures |

## Method in steps: telling an experience

```
1. Set the scene     when, where, who — background past forms
2. Trigger           the moment things changed — completed past
3. Sequence          2–3 events with time markers (then, the next day, weeks later)
4. Feeling           evaluative language, not "it was good"
5. Bridge            since then / now / these days — link to the present
6. Meaning           one sentence answering a possible question
```

## Method in steps: reading a narrative text

```
1. Skim for text type, writer and purpose
2. Underline every time marker; number the events in order
3. Mark who is speaking at each point (narrator or someone quoted)
4. Separate what happened from how people felt about it
5. Find then/now contrast words: still, no longer, used to, since
6. Answer with the text's words where a short answer is asked
```

## Must-know distinctions

- **Leisure activities vs holidays and travel.** Leisure is regular free time at home (a weekly
  club); holidays and travel is time away. A text can touch both, but know which angle a question
  wants.
- **Rites of passage vs customs and traditions.** A rite of passage marks a person moving from one
  stage of life to the next (coming of age, graduation). A custom or tradition is a shared practice
  a community repeats (a festival, a seasonal dish). Many rites of passage are also customs; the
  difference is the focus on the individual's change.
- **Life stories vs migration.** A life story follows one person over time. Migration is about
  movement from one place to another and settling. A migrant's life story belongs to both.
- **Describing vs narrating.** Describing says what something is like. Narrating says what happened,
  in order. Experiences tasks usually need both, with narration as the spine.
- **Completed vs background past.** One action finished at a point in time, against the scene,
  the weather, feelings or a repeated habit.
- **SL vs HL oral stimulus.** SL: a visual image clearly relevant to one or more themes. HL: an
  excerpt from one of the two literary works studied.
- **SL vs HL Paper 1.** Same outline; HL tasks demand more complex language and structures,
  higher-order thinking, and have a higher word range.

## Assessment facts from the brief

| Component | What it involves | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Productive: one writing task from a choice of three | 30 | 25% |
| Paper 2 listening | Receptive | 25 | 25% |
| Paper 2 reading | Receptive | 40 | 25% |
| Individual oral (internal) | SL: visual image stimulus; HL: literary excerpt | 30 | 25% |

External assessment is 75% and internal 25%. The assessment outlines for SL and HL are identical;
the nature of the tasks differs. Criteria are not printed in the brief, so your teacher will share
them.

## Worked reminder: one paragraph, six checks

Original text written for Marlbridge:

> The first winter after we moved, my mother kept the heating low and the radio high, tuned to a
> station from home. By spring she had found a job and a choir. She still sings those songs today,
> but now she sings them in two languages.

- Background past: *kept*, used here for a repeated state across the winter.
- Earlier past: *had found*.
- Time markers: *the first winter*, *by spring*, *today*, *now*.
- Evaluative detail: the radio *high* shows longing without naming it.
- Bridge to now: *still… today*.
- Meaning: *two languages* answers how the past shapes the present.

## Quick self-test

1. Quote the guiding principle of Experiences.
2. Name the six optional recommended topics.
3. State the two possible questions for this theme in the brief.
4. Is a graduation best described as a rite of passage or as a leisure activity? Why?
5. Give two English time markers that "bridge" past and present.
6. What is the difference in function between "We walked to the station" and "We used to walk to the
   station"?
7. At SL, what kind of stimulus is used for the individual oral?
8. At HL, what is the oral stimulus taken from?
9. How many writing tasks do you complete on Paper 1, and from how many options?
10. What percentage of the final grade does Paper 2 carry in total?
11. Name three of the five conceptual understandings the brief lists.
12. Rewrite "It was a good trip" with evaluative language and a bridge to the present.

### Answers

1. "Explore and tell the stories of the events, experiences and journeys that shape our lives."
2. Leisure activities, holidays and travel, life stories, rites of passage, customs and traditions,
   migration.
3. "How does our past shape our present and our future?" and "How and why do different cultures
   mark important moments in life?"
4. A rite of passage: it marks a person moving from one stage of life (school) to the next.
5. Any two of: since then, ever since, these days, still, no longer, from that day on.
6. The first is one completed event; the second is a repeated habit in the past.
7. A visual image clearly relevant to one or more of the themes.
8. An excerpt from one of the two literary works studied, originally written in the target language.
9. One task, chosen from three.
10. 50% (listening 25% and reading 25%).
11. Any three of: context, audience, purpose, meaning, variation.
12. Model: "The trip was exhausting but unforgettable, and since then I have planned every holiday
    around walking." Any version with evaluative words and a past-to-present link is acceptable.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a chain of "then… then…" events with no feeling or reflection, so the text never shows
  what the experience meant.
- Using the completed-past form for background description (weather, feelings, habits), or the
  background form for single actions, in languages that separate the two.
- Drifting from past to present tense in the middle of a story without a reason.
- Ending an Experiences text in the past with no bridge to the present or future.
- Choosing a text type but ignoring its conventions: a diary with no date or first-person reflection,
  an article with no headline, an interview with no introduction of the person.
- In reading and listening, crediting the narrator with a view that belongs to someone they quote.
- Answering "in what order" or "when" questions from memory of the gist instead of the time markers
  in the text.
- Generalising about a whole culture ("everyone there…") instead of using a specific example from a
  text.
- Revising only holiday vocabulary and neglecting rites of passage, customs and migration.
