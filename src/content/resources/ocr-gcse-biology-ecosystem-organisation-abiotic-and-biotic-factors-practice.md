## Questions

**1.** A reed bed beside a river contains 200 reed warblers, many other bird, insect and plant species, microorganisms, water and mud. State the level of organisation of each of the following. **(a)** The 200 reed warblers. **[1]** **(b)** All the living things in the reed bed. **[1]** **(c)** The living things together with the water, mud and light. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each factor as abiotic or biotic. **(a)** Soil pH. **(b)** A hawk hunting in a field. **(c)** The amount of rain. **(d)** The number of aphids on a rose bush. **[4]**

**3.** A student measured the mean number of sorrel plants per square metre on four soils.

| Soil pH | 4.5 | 5.5 | 6.5 | 7.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean plants per m² | 14.2 | 9.6 | 4.1 | 1.2 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern shown by the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why soil pH can affect where plants grow. **[1]**
**(c)** State one other abiotic factor that could differ between the soils and affect the results. **[1]**

**4.** Name the type of relationship in each case and state who benefits. **(a)** Bacteria live in the root nodules of clover and fix nitrogen. **[2]** **(b)** A tick feeds on blood from a deer. **[2]** **(c)** A kestrel catches a field vole. **[1]**

**5.** Bean seedlings were sown very close together in a tray. After four weeks they were shorter and had fewer leaves than seedlings sown far apart. **(a)** Name two resources the dense seedlings competed for. **[2]** **(b)** Explain why the dense seedlings grew less. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows counts of two species in a hedge over six months.

| Month | Weevils per plant | Birds per 100 m |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 55 | 3 |
| 2 | 150 | 4 |
| 3 | 240 | 8 |
| 4 | 120 | 15 |
| 5 | 45 | 7 |
| 6 | 30 | 4 |

**(a)** Identify the predator and give the evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why weevil numbers fell between months 3 and 5. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why bird numbers also fell after month 4. **[1]**

**7.** Food chain: seaweed → limpet → crab → gull. **(a)** Name the producer. **[1]** **(b)** Name the secondary consumer. **[1]** **(c)** Explain why seaweed is called a producer. **[1]** **(d)** State what the arrows show. **[1]**

**8.** A sheep eats 80 kg dry mass of grass. 24 kg is egested, 40 kg is lost through respiration, 4 kg is lost as excretory waste and the rest becomes sheep tissue.
**(a)** Calculate the mass that becomes sheep tissue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the grass lost through respiration. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how egestion, excretion and respiration each cause biomass loss. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why a pyramid of biomass is always narrower at the top. **[1]**

**9.** Dry mass per square metre of a meadow: grass 1200 g, grasshoppers 150 g, shrews 12.6 g.
**(a)** Calculate the grasshopper biomass as a percentage of the grass biomass. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the shrew biomass as a percentage of the grasshopper biomass. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how you would draw the pyramid of biomass. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why dry mass is used. **[1]**

**10.** In a marine food chain the biomass produced in one year (kg per km²) is phytoplankton 5400, zooplankton 594, herring 62.5 and tuna 5.0.
**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of transfer from zooplankton to herring. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of transfer from herring to tuna. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the overall efficiency from phytoplankton to tuna. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the overall efficiency is much lower than each separate transfer. **[1]**

**11.** A farmer grows 7500 kg of grain on a field. Fed to cattle, 9% of the grain biomass becomes beef.
**(a)** Calculate the mass of beef produced. **[1]**
**(b)** A food chain starts with 80 000 kg of producer biomass and 10% is transferred at each step. Calculate the biomass at the fourth trophic level. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answer to explain why food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why more people can be fed from a field of grain if the grain is eaten directly. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Population [1]. **(b)** Community [1]. **(c)** Ecosystem [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A community contains living things only, so answers that include the water and mud under "community" lose the mark.

**2.** **(a)** Abiotic [1]. **(b)** Biotic [1]. **(c)** Abiotic [1]. **(d)** Biotic [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Rain is a non-living factor even though it affects living things, and the aphid count is biotic because it comes from living organisms.

**3.** **(a)** As soil pH rises from 4.5 to 7.5 the mean number of sorrel plants falls [1], from 14.2 to 1.2 per m² [1].
**(b)** pH affects the availability of minerals or damages roots, so some plants cannot grow [1].
**(c)** Moisture, or temperature, or light intensity [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quoting figures from the table is what turns "it decreases" into a complete description of a trend.

**4.** **(a)** Mutualism [1]; both the bacteria and the clover benefit [1].
**(b)** Parasitism [1]; the tick benefits and the deer is harmed [1].
**(c)** Predation [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the relationship is rarely enough on its own; state who gains and who is harmed.

**5.** **(a)** Light [1] and water or mineral ions or space [1].
**(b)** The seedlings shaded each other and shared limited water and minerals [1], so each made less food by photosynthesis and grew less [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the lack of a resource to its use in the plant, here photosynthesis, rather than writing that the plants "did not get enough".

**6.** **(a)** The birds are the predator [1] because their peak in month 4 follows the weevil peak in month 3 [1].
**(b)** More birds were eating the weevils [1] so fewer survived [1].
**(c)** Fewer weevils meant less food, so some birds starved or left [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The delay between the two peaks is the evidence for which species is the predator, and a stated time lag strengthens the answer.

**7.** **(a)** Seaweed [1]. **(b)** Crab [1]. **(c)** It makes its own food by photosynthesis [1]. **(d)** The direction in which biomass is transferred, from the food to the feeder [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An arrow does not mean "eats"; say it shows the transfer of biomass towards the eater.

**8.** **(a)** 80 - 24 - 40 - 4 = **12 kg** [1].
**(b)** 40 / 80 × 100 = **50%** [1].
**(c)** Egestion: undigested material such as cellulose leaves in faeces [1]. Excretion: waste such as urea is removed [1]. Respiration: glucose is broken down so carbon is lost as carbon dioxide [1].
**(d)** Biomass is lost at each transfer, so less is available at each higher level [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A calculation answer with no unit or percent sign is incomplete, and each loss route needs its own mechanism.

**9.** **(a)** 150 / 1200 × 100 = **12.5%** [1].
**(b)** 12.6 / 150 × 100 = **8.4%** [1].
**(c)** Draw horizontal bars to a chosen scale, with grass at the base [1], each bar centred on a vertical line and labelled with the organism [1].
**(d)** Water content varies, so dry mass shows the actual mass of tissue [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "To scale" and "grass at the bottom" are the two details a description of a pyramid must contain.

**10.** **(a)** 62.5 / 594 × 100 [1] = **10.5%** [1].
**(b)** 5.0 / 62.5 × 100 = **8.0%** [1].
**(c)** 5.0 / 5400 × 100 [1] = **0.093%** [1].
**(d)** Biomass is lost at every step, so the losses add up across the chain [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The producer level is the denominator in an overall efficiency, and answers that divide by the tuna value are a common slip.

**11.** **(a)** 7500 × 0.09 = **675 kg** [1].
**(b)** Level 2: 8000 kg, level 3: 800 kg [1], level 4: **80 kg** [1].
**(c)** By level 5 only 8 kg remains, and by level 6 only 0.8 kg [1], which is too little to support a population large enough to survive [1].
**(d)** Eating grain directly removes one trophic level [1], so the losses from egestion, excretion and respiration in cattle are avoided [1], and more of the grain biomass reaches humans [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the losses and say that there is one fewer transfer, rather than only stating that plants are "more efficient".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up population and community, or leaving abiotic parts out of the ecosystem.
- Describing a trend without figures from the table.
- Naming a relationship without saying who benefits.
- Writing that a resource "ran out" without linking it to photosynthesis or growth.
- Drawing arrows from predator to prey.
- Forgetting a % sign or unit.
- Dividing by the wrong trophic level in an efficiency calculation.
- Calling egestion excretion.
- Giving "energy is lost" with no named route of biomass loss.
