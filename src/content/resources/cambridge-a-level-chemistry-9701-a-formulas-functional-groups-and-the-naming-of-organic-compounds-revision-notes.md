**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** content:
subtopic 29.1 Formulas, functional groups and the naming of organic compounds.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Functional group** | the atom or group of atoms that gives a homologous series its characteristic reactions |
| **General formula** | the formula shared by every member of a homologous series, in terms of n |
| **Structural formula** | the minimum detail that shows the arrangement of atoms unambiguously |
| **Displayed formula** | every atom and every bond shown (not expected for a benzene ring) |
| **Skeletal formula** | carbon skeleton as lines; H on C omitted; functional groups drawn in full |
| **Primary / secondary / tertiary amide** | 0, 1 or 2 carbon groups on the amide nitrogen |

## New A Level classes

| Class | Group | Example and name |
|---|---|---|
| arene | benzene ring | C₆H₆, benzene |
| halogenoarene | X on the ring | C₆H₅Cl, chlorobenzene |
| phenol | –OH on the ring | C₆H₅OH, phenol |
| acyl chloride | –COCl | CH₃CH₂COCl, propanoyl chloride |
| secondary / tertiary amine | –NHR / –NR₂ | (CH₃)₂NH; **naming not required** |
| amide | –CONH₂, –CONHR, –CONR₂ | CH₃CH₂CONH₂, propanamide |
| amino acid | –NH₂ and –COOH | H₂NCH₂COOH, 2-aminoethanoic acid |

## General formulas worth knowing

| Series (saturated, one group) | General formula | Check |
|---|---|---|
| acyl chlorides | CₙH₂ₙ₋₁OCl | n = 2: C₂H₃OCl, ethanoyl chloride |
| primary amides | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO | n = 3: C₃H₇NO, propanamide |
| 2-amino acids | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NO₂ | n = 3: C₃H₇NO₂, 2-aminopropanoic acid |

## Suffixes and prefixes

| Group | As suffix | As prefix |
|---|---|---|
| –COOH | -oic acid | carboxy |
| –COCl | -oyl chloride | — |
| –CONH₂ | -amide | — |
| –CN | -nitrile (C counted in chain) | — |
| –OH | -ol | hydroxy |
| –NH₂ | -amine | amino |
| –NO₂ | — | nitro |
| –Cl, –Br, –I | — | chloro, bromo, iodo |
| –CH₃ | — | methyl |
| –C₆H₅ | — | phenyl |

**Priority for the suffix (highest first):** carboxylic acid > acyl chloride > amide > ketone >
alcohol / phenol > amine.

## Scope limits

- Aliphatic chains up to **six carbons**.
- Esters and amides: up to **six plus six** carbons.
- Esters and nitriles: **straight chains only**.
- Cyclic: **one ring of up to six carbons**.
- Aromatic: **one benzene ring** with one or more simple substituents.

## Aromatic naming routine

1. Is there a group with its own parent name (phenol, benzoic acid, phenylamine, benzoyl chloride)? If so,
   its ring carbon is **C1**.
2. Otherwise the parent is **benzene** and the substituents are prefixes (lowest locants overall).
3. Number round the ring to give the **lowest set** of locants.
4. List prefixes **alphabetically**; use di-, tri- for repeats (ignored for alphabetical order).

| Example | Name |
|---|---|
| NO₂ at 1 and 3 | 1,3-dinitrobenzene |
| OH at 1, Br at 2, 4, 6 | 2,4,6-tribromophenol |
| COOH at 1, NO₂ at 3 | 3-nitrobenzoic acid |
| OH at 1, CH₃ at 4 | 4-methylphenol |
| COOH at 1, OH at 4 | 4-hydroxybenzoic acid |
| NH₂ at 1, Cl at 4 | 4-chlorophenylamine |

**Ring attached to a chain:** phenylethanone C₆H₅COCH₃; methyl benzoate C₆H₅COOCH₃; phenyl ethanoate
CH₃COOC₆H₅; N-phenylethanamide CH₃CONHC₆H₅.

## Isomers: same molecular formula, different names

C₃H₆O₂ esters and acid: CH₃COOCH₃ (methyl ethanoate), HCOOCH₂CH₃ (ethyl methanoate) and the acid
CH₃CH₂COOH (propanoic acid). C₃H₇NO amides: CH₃CH₂CONH₂ (propanamide, primary), CH₃CONHCH₃
(N-methylethanamide, secondary) and HCON(CH₃)₂ (N,N-dimethylmethanamide, tertiary).

## Cyclic naming

- Carbon carrying the principal group = C1; number to give lowest locants: 2-methylcyclohexanol.
- Cycloalkenes: the C=C carbons are C1 and C2.

## Exam traps

- **Ester names**: the alkyl group from the alcohol comes **first**: CH₃COOC₆H₅ is phenyl ethanoate, while
  C₆H₅COOCH₃ is methyl benzoate.
- **(chloromethyl)benzene ≠ 2-chloromethylbenzene**: side-chain Cl versus ring Cl.
- **N locants** for groups on amide nitrogen: N-methylethanamide, N,N-dimethylethanamide.
- **No displayed formula** for a benzene ring; use the hexagon with a circle.
- **Ring corner with a substituent has no hydrogen** when counting atoms.
- **Nitrile carbon counts**: CH₃CN is ethanenitrile.
- **Secondary and tertiary amines**: recognise and draw; naming not examined.

## Self-test

1. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COCl.
2. Give the structural formula of N,N-dimethylpropanamide.
3. Classify CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ as a primary, secondary or tertiary amide, and name it.
4. Name a benzene ring with –OH at C1 and –NO₂ at C2 and C4.
5. Explain why HOC₆H₄COOH is named as a benzoic acid rather than as a phenol.
6. Give the molecular formula of benzoyl chloride.
7. Name CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₂COOH.
8. Name the ester formed from phenol and ethanoic acid's acyl chloride.
9. Draw (describe) the skeletal formula of cyclohexanone.
10. Write the general formula for acyl chlorides and use it to give the molecular formula of hexanoyl chloride.
11. Name C₆H₅CH₂Cl and state whether the chlorine is on the ring.
12. Why is "2-methylbutanenitrile" outside the naming scope, while butanenitrile is within it?

**Answers:**

1. Pentanoyl chloride.
2. CH₃CH₂CON(CH₃)₂.
3. Secondary (one ethyl group on N); N-ethylethanamide.
4. 2,4-dinitrophenol.
5. –COOH has higher priority than –OH, so it gives the suffix: 4-hydroxybenzoic acid (or 2- or 3-, depending on
   position).
6. C₇H₅OCl.
7. 3-aminobutanoic acid.
8. Phenyl ethanoate, CH₃COOC₆H₅.
9. A hexagon with a double-bonded O on one corner.
10. CₙH₂ₙ₋₁OCl; n = 6 gives C₆H₁₁OCl.
11. (Chloromethyl)benzene; no, the chlorine is on the side-chain carbon.
12. The syllabus limits nitriles to straight chains; 2-methylbutanenitrile is branched.
