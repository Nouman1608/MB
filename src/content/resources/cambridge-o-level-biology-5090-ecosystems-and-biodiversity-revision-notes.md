These notes condense subtopic **19.3 Ecosystems and biodiversity** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definitions of population, community, ecosystem and biodiversity, the factors affecting population growth, and the effect of human population growth on global resources. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [19.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-ecosystems-and-biodiversity/). Then try the [19.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-ecosystems-and-biodiversity-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Population | A group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time |
| Community | All of the populations of different species in an ecosystem |
| Ecosystem | A unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together |
| Biodiversity | The number of different species that live in an area |

**Order from smallest to largest:** population, community, ecosystem.

## Example: a pond

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Population | All the frogs in the pond |
| Community | All the frogs, fish, insects, snails, algae and plants |
| Ecosystem | The community plus the water, light, temperature and mud |
| Biodiversity | The number of different species in the pond |

## Factors affecting population growth

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Food supply | More food: faster growth. Less food: slower growth or decline |
| Competition | More individuals: more competition: slower growth |
| Predation | Predators reduce prey numbers |
| Disease | Reduces numbers, especially in crowded populations |

Also: births and immigration increase a population; deaths and emigration decrease it.

## Population growth curve

1. Slow start, with few individuals.
2. Rapid increase, with plentiful resources.
3. Levelling off, as resources become limiting.

The levelling occurs because of shortage of food, competition, predation and disease.

## Predator and prey

- Prey increase, so predators have more food and increase later.
- More predators eat more prey, so prey fall.
- Predators then have less food and fall.
- The pattern repeats, with the predator peak following the prey peak.

## The human population

- The human population is increasing quickly, because death rates have fallen (better food, medicine, sanitation).
- More people means a greater demand for food, land, water, energy, materials and space.
- Consequences include deforestation, more pollution and waste, more carbon dioxide from fossil fuels and loss of habitats and biodiversity.

## Worked calculations

**Biodiversity comparison.** Habitat A has 6 species and habitat B has 24. Habitat B has 24 divided by 6 = 4 times as many species. The difference is 18 species.

**Population growth.** A population rose from 200 to 260. Increase = 60. Percentage = 60 divided by 200 x 100 = 30 per cent.

**Rate of growth.** A culture increased from 10 000 cells to 34 000 cells in 4 hours. Rate = (34 000 - 10 000) divided by 4 = 6 000 cells per hour.

## Sampling with quadrats

| Step | Reason |
|---|---|
| Place quadrats at random | Avoids bias |
| Use many quadrats | More reliable; reduces the effect of unusual patches |
| Count the species and individuals in each | Gives biodiversity and abundance |
| Calculate a mean per quadrat | Allows comparison between areas |
| Multiply by area ratio | Estimates the whole population |

**Worked estimate.** Mean of 6 plants per m2 in a field of 500 m2 gives 6 x 500 = 3 000 plants.

## Short model answers

**Define a population.** A group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time.

**State the difference between a community and an ecosystem.** A community is all the populations of different species in an area. An ecosystem is the community and its environment interacting together.

**Define biodiversity.** The number of different species that live in an area.

**Explain why a population stops growing.** Resources such as food and space become limited, so competition increases, and predation and disease may increase. Deaths then equal births.

**Describe the effect of human population growth.** It increases the demand for resources such as food, land, water and energy, and increases pollution and loss of habitats.

## Exam traps

- A population is **one species**. A community is **many species**.
- An ecosystem includes the **non-living** environment.
- Biodiversity counts **species**, not individuals.
- Name the factor and the **effect**, not only the factor.
- A population levels off because of **limiting factors**, not because it stops reproducing.
- Do not say that predators always cause prey to go extinct.
- Show the divisor in a percentage calculation.
- Link the human population to the **demand for resources**.

### Answers

1. A group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time.
2. All of the populations of different species in an ecosystem.
3. A unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together.
4. The number of different species that live in an area.
5. Any two of: food supply; competition; predation; disease.
6. It reduces the rate of growth, because individuals compete for limited resources.
7. It reduces the numbers of the prey.
8. Individuals are closer together, so the disease passes between them more easily.
9. A slow start, then rapid growth, then levelling off.
10. Death rates have fallen because of better food, medicine and sanitation.
11. Any two of: food; land; water; energy.
12. Habitat B.
