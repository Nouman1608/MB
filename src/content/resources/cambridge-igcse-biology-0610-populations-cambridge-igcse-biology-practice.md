> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **19.4 Populations** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (explaining each phase of the sigmoid curve using limiting factors); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [populations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-populations-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [populations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-populations-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of these is a population?

A: all the insects in a meadow

B: all the daisies of one species in a lawn this summer

C: all the plants and animals in a pond

D: a lake, its organisms and the water they live in

**[1]**

**2.** In which phase of the sigmoid growth curve does the number of organisms stay roughly constant?

A: death

B: exponential

C: lag

D: stationary

**[1]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by a community. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** factors, other than food supply, that can reduce the rate of growth of a rabbit population. **[2]**

**5.** A rock pool contains seaweeds, limpets, crabs, sea water and rocks. Explain why the rock pool can be described as an ecosystem. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why the exponential phase of population growth cannot continue indefinitely in an environment with limited resources. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student grew duckweed, a small floating plant, in a beaker of pond water on a windowsill. She counted the plants each week.

| Week | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of plants | 10 | 14 | 30 | 70 | 150 | 230 | 260 | 265 | 264 |

**(a)** Identify the phase of growth between week 0 and week 1, between week 2 and week 4, and between week 6 and week 8. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of population growth between week 3 and week 4. Give the unit. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain why the number of plants stopped increasing after week 6. **[3]**

**(d)** Suggest **one** change to the conditions that could allow the population to reach a larger size. **[1]**

**8.** The table shows the numbers of aphids and of ladybirds, which eat aphids, on a crop over six months.

| Month | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aphids | 200 | 800 | 1 500 | 900 | 300 | 250 |
| Ladybirds | 5 | 8 | 20 | 45 | 40 | 15 |

**(a)** Describe how the numbers of aphids and ladybirds change over the six months. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the ladybird population reached its peak later than the aphid population. **[2]**

**9.** Twenty sheep were released on a small island with no predators. The population grew and then stayed at about 2 000 for many years. A disease then killed 40% of the sheep.

**(a)** Calculate the number of sheep that survived the disease. **[1]**

**(b)** State **two** factors that could have stopped the population from growing above about 2 000. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why the population grew quickly again after the disease. **[2]**

**10.** State whether each of these is a population, a community or an ecosystem.

**(a)** a forest, including its trees, animals, fungi, soil and climate **[1]**

**(b)** all the grey squirrels in a park in 2025 **[1]**

**(c)** all the living organisms in a coral reef **[1]**

**(d)** all the bluebells of one species in a wood in April **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Yeast was grown in two flasks of sugar solution at the same temperature.

- Flask P was sealed and left alone.
- In flask Q, fresh sugar solution was added each day and the same volume of liquid was removed, taking away ethanol.

The population in flask P reached a stationary phase after 2 days and then fell. The population in flask Q kept growing for longer and reached a higher number.

Explain the difference between the two flasks. **[4]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A small number of bacteria were added to a flask of fresh nutrient broth. Explain why the population showed:

**(a)** a lag phase **[2]**

**(b)** an exponential phase. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A includes many insect species; C is a community; D is an ecosystem.

**2.** **D** [1].

**3.** All of the populations of different species in an ecosystem [1].

**4.** Any two from: competition; predation; disease [2].

**5.** It contains a community of organisms (seaweeds, limpets, crabs) [1] and their environment (sea water, rocks), interacting together [1].

**6.** Resources such as food or space become limiting / run short [1]; competition increases, so the birth rate falls / death rate rises [1].

**7. (a)** Week 0–1: lag [1]; week 2–4: exponential (log) [1]; week 6–8: stationary [1].

**(b)** (150 − 70) ÷ (4 − 3) = 80 [1] plants per week [1].

**(c)** Any three from:

- limiting factors such as light, space on the water surface or mineral ions ran short [1]
- competition between plants increased [1]
- fewer new plants were produced / the birth rate fell [1]
- birth rate became equal to death rate [1]

**(d)** Any one: a larger container / larger surface area; adding mineral ions / fertiliser; more light [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c), a named limiting factor relevant to a floating plant (light, space, mineral ions) is needed for full marks. "Food runs out" is less suitable for a producer.

**8. (a)** Aphids increase to a peak of 1 500 in month 3, then fall [1]. Ladybirds increase to a peak of 45 in month 4, then fall [1]. The ladybird peak comes one month after the aphid peak / ladybirds fall after the aphids fall [1].

**(b)** When aphids are plentiful, ladybirds have more food, so more ladybirds survive and reproduce [1]; it takes time for ladybirds to breed and for their young to grow, so their numbers rise after the aphids' [1].

*Examiner insight:* Quote the figures and months from the table. "Both go up and down" scores nothing.

**9. (a)** 2 000 × 0.60 = 1 200 sheep [1].

**(b)** Any two from: food (grass) supply; competition for food or space; disease [2].

**(c)** There was more food per sheep / less competition after the deaths [1], so more sheep survived and reproduced / birth rate exceeded death rate [1].

**10. (a)** Ecosystem [1].

**(b)** Population [1].

**(c)** Community [1].

**(d)** Population [1].

**11.** Any four from:

- in flask P, sugar ran out / became a limiting factor [1]
- in flask P, ethanol built up to toxic levels [1]
- so in P, the death rate rose to equal and then exceed the birth rate [1]
- in flask Q, fresh sugar meant food did not become limiting so quickly [1]
- in flask Q, removing liquid kept ethanol below toxic levels [1]
- so in Q, the birth rate stayed above the death rate for longer [1]

*Examiner insight:* This question asks you to explain a difference, so each point about P should be matched with a point about Q.

**12. (a)** Few bacteria are present, so few are dividing [1]; the bacteria are adjusting to the new conditions / making the enzymes needed to use the nutrients [1].

**(b)** Nutrients and space are plentiful, with little competition [1]; the birth rate is much higher than the death rate, and each new bacterium soon divides, so numbers double regularly [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing "all the insects" or "all the frogs" as a population when several species are included.
- Confusing community and ecosystem.
- Leaving the unit off a rate.
- Describing trends without quoting figures or months.
- (Extended) Explaining a phase without referring to birth and death rates.
