Sub-topic 2.1 links what is in the soil to what a crop needs: **water, air, nutrients, anchorage and the
right pH**, plus the weather and seasons above ground. Most answers follow one chain: **particle size →
pore spaces → water and air → crop growth**.

## Four components

1. **Mineral particles**: sand (largest), silt, clay (smallest).
2. **Organic content**: living organisms (plants, animals, fungi, bacteria) and organic matter from
   decomposition.
3. **Gases**: air in pore spaces.
4. **Water**: in pore spaces and around particles.

## Seven features that matter for crops

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Mineral particles | Size controls pores, drainage and nutrient holding |
| Nutrient content | Organic content and inorganic ions feed the crop |
| pH | Most crops grow best near neutral; extremes reduce nutrient uptake |
| Pore spaces | Hold the air and water roots need |
| Gas content | Oxygen for root respiration |
| Water content and drainage | Water for photosynthesis; but waterlogging cuts oxygen |
| Ease of cultivation | Easier ploughing and planting |

## The three ions

| Ion | Formula | Element | Background use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrate | NO₃⁻ | Nitrogen | Proteins; leaf growth |
| Phosphate | PO₄³⁻ | Phosphorus | Roots; healthy development |
| Potassium | K⁺ | Potassium | Flowers, fruit; disease resistance |

## Sand v clay v loam

| | Sand | Clay | Loam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Particles | Large | Tiny | Mixed |
| Pores | Large | Tiny | Mixed |
| Drainage | Fast | Slow | Good |
| Holds water | Poorly | Well | Well |
| Air | Plenty | Little when wet | Good |
| Nutrients | Leached | Held | Held |
| Cultivation | Easy | Heavy, sticky | Easy |

## Loam: five reasons (learn in syllabus order)

1. Ideal combination of mineral particles and pore spaces.
2. Retains moisture.
3. Contains organic content and inorganic ions.
4. Allows excess water to drain.
5. Easy to cultivate.

## Weather and seasons

- **Weather**: the day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location.
- **Climate** (Topic 4): based on the weather over many years. Do not mix them up.
- **Wet and dry seasons**: crops are planted at the start of the wet season; in the dry season they need
  irrigation or the land is left. A late or short wet season lowers yields.

## Growing conditions

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Length of growing season | Longer season → bigger yields or more harvests |
| Optimum weather | Warm, enough water, sunny; frost, drought, extreme heat and storms reduce growth |
| Daylight hours | More hours of light → more photosynthesis |

**Interpreting data:** check every month against **every** condition; quote values with units; describe
patterns using the highest, lowest and when changes happen.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mineral particles | Fragments of weathered rock: sand, silt, clay |
| Organic matter | Material from the decomposition of dead organisms and waste |
| Decomposer | Organism, such as a bacterium or fungus, that breaks down dead material and releases nutrients |
| Pore space | Gap between soil particles, holding air or water |
| Drainage | Movement of excess water down through the soil |
| Waterlogging | Pores filled with water, leaving little air |
| Leaching | Nutrients washed down out of reach of roots |
| Loam | Soil with a balanced mix of sand, silt and clay and organic matter |
| pH | Measure of how acidic or alkaline a soil is |
| Growing season | Period of the year when conditions allow crops to grow |

## Exam traps

- Sand drains **fast** and holds **little** water; clay drains **slowly** and holds **a lot**.
- Gases are a soil component: roots need **oxygen** for respiration.
- Name the ions with their formulas when asked: NO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻, K⁺.
- Loam answers need **reasons**, not just "it is a mixture".
- Weather is **day-to-day**; climate is **long-term**.
- Data answers need **figures and units**.

## Self-test

1. Name the **four** components of soil.
2. Which mineral particle is the smallest?
3. Name **two** groups of living organisms found in soil.
4. Why do roots need air in the soil?
5. Give the formula of the ion that supplies phosphorus.
6. Explain why sandy soils are often low in nutrients.
7. Give **two** problems of clay soil for a farmer.
8. Give **three** reasons why loam is good for crop growth.
9. Define weather.
10. Why do farmers in wet-and-dry-season regions plant at the start of the wet season?
11. How do longer daylight hours affect crop growth?
12. Rainfall is 30 mm in March, 140 mm in April and 220 mm in May. A crop needs at least 100 mm a month.
    Which of these months are suitable?

**Answers:** 1. Mineral particles, organic content, gases, water. 2. Clay. 3. Any two: plants, animals,
fungi, bacteria. 4. For respiration by roots (and soil organisms). 5. PO₄³⁻. 6. Water drains through the
large pores quickly, washing nutrients away (leaching). 7. Any two: drains slowly and waterlogs; little air
when wet; heavy and sticky to cultivate; sets hard when dry. 8. Any three: ideal mix of particles and pore
spaces; retains moisture; contains organic content and inorganic ions; lets excess water drain; easy to
cultivate. 9. The day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location. 10. So the crop grows while rain
is available, without irrigation. 11. More hours of light allow more photosynthesis, so faster growth.
12. April and May.
