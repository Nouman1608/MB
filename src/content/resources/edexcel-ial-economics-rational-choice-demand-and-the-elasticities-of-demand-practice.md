## Questions

**1.** State what economists assume about the aims of consumers and of firms. **[2]**

**2.** Cinema A advertises tickets at 9.00 plus a 1.00 booking fee. Cinema B charges 10.00 with no fee. Many people choose Cinema A. Explain why this suggests consumers may not maximise utility. **[4]**

**3.** A shop sells a large pack of cereal (900 g for 4.32) and a medium pack (600 g for 2.76). Many shoppers assume the large pack is cheaper per gram.
**(a)** Calculate the price per 100 g of each pack. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which reason for non-rational choice this shows. **[2]**

**4.** A buyer's total utility from 1 to 5 cups of coffee in a morning is 36, 60, 78, 90 and 96 utils. One util is worth 0.25.
**(a)** Calculate the marginal utility of each cup. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how many cups the buyer takes at a price of 4.50 and at a price of 3.00. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this supports a downward-sloping demand curve. **[2]**

**5.** In the market for electric scooters (a normal good), for each event, say whether demand moves along its curve or the curve itself shifts, and give the direction.
**(i)** The price of scooters falls. **(ii)** The price of helmets, a complement, falls. **(iii)** Real incomes fall. **[3]**

**6.** A hardware store raises the price of a tin of paint from 20 to 22. Weekly sales fall from 300 to 279 tins. Calculate PED, describe the demand and calculate the change in revenue. **[4]**

**7.** Real incomes rise by 5%. Demand for good X rises from 2,000 to 2,160 units. Demand for good Y falls from 3,000 to 2,910 units. Calculate YED for each good and classify each. **[4]**

**8.** The price of good M rises from 5.00 to 5.60. Demand for good N falls from 2,400 to 2,184 units. Calculate XED and state the likely relationship between M and N. **[3]**

**9.** A straight-line demand curve is P = 80 - 4Q.
**(a)** Calculate PED for a price fall from 64 (quantity 4) to 60 (quantity 5). **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate PED for a fall from 16 (quantity 16) to 12 (quantity 17). **[2]**
**(c)** At a price of 40 and quantity 10, revenue is 400. State, with revenue figures at 64 and 16, the price that maximises revenue and PED there. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why PED for table salt is likely to be lower than PED for a premium brand of sports shoes. **[4]**

**11.** A theme park has 50,000 visitors at a ticket price of 30. Its manager estimates PED at -1.8 and plans an 8% price rise.
**(a)** Calculate the new price, visitor numbers and revenue. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether the park should raise the price. **[4]**

**12.** A government plans a per-unit tax of 1.00 on a good priced at 8.00 with sales of 60,000 units and PED of -0.3. Assume the tax is passed on in full.
**(a)** Calculate the new quantity and tax revenue. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate the tax as a way to raise revenue and cut consumption. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Consumers aim to maximise utility [1]. Firms aim to maximise profit [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit by naming the aim for each group; "make decisions logically" does not say what is being maximised.

**2.** Both tickets cost 10.00 in total [1]. The 1.00 fee is a separate, smaller add-on, so the 9.00 headline price looks cheaper [1]. The choice is driven by how the price is presented (framing), not by the utility from the film [1]. A utility maximiser would treat equal total prices as equal [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the arithmetic that the totals match; an explanation that never proves the options are identical is harder to credit.

**3. (a)** Large: 4.32 / 9 = **0.48** per 100 g [1]. Medium: 2.76 / 6 = **0.46** per 100 g [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Poor computational skills, because shoppers do not work out unit prices [1]. The assumption that bigger means cheaper is a bias, so buyers pay more per gram than necessary, failing to maximise utility [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the reason from the specification list, then link it to the figures.

**4. (a)** Marginal utility is the change in total utility: first cup 36 [1], then **24, 18, 12 and 6** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Money values at 0.25 per util are 9.00, 6.00, 4.50, 3.00 and 1.50 [1]. The buyer takes a cup while its value is at least the price: **3 cups** at 4.50 and **4 cups** at 3.00 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Each extra cup adds less utility (diminishing marginal utility) [1]. So a lower price is needed to make another cup worth buying, giving more quantity at lower prices [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert utils to money before comparing with price; an answer that compares utils with a price directly cannot reach a quantity.

**5.** (i) Movement along the curve (extension), because only the good's own price changed [1]. (ii) A shift to the right, because cheaper complements raise demand at every scooter price [1]. (iii) A shift to the left, because a normal good is bought less as real income falls [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State which way the change goes as well as its kind. "Shift" alone leaves the second half of each answer unmade.

**6.** %P = 2 / 20 = +10% [1]. %Q = -21 / 300 = -7%, so PED = -7 / 10 = **-0.7** [1]. This is price inelastic [1]. Revenue rises from 300 x 20 = 6,000 to 279 x 22 = 6,138, an increase of **138** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking the revenue rise to the inelastic value shows you understand why, not just the arithmetic.

**7.** X: %Q = 160 / 2,000 = +8%, so YED = 8 / 5 = **+1.6** [1]. Y: %Q = -90 / 3,000 = -3%, so YED = -3 / 5 = **-0.6** [1]. X is a normal, income-elastic good [1]. Y is an inferior good [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the sign. The negative sign alone is what identifies Y as inferior, so a bare "0.6" loses that point.

**8.** %P = 0.60 / 5.00 = +12% [1]. %Q = -216 / 2,400 = -9%, so XED = -9 / 12 = **-0.75** [1]. The negative value shows M and N are complements [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* After the number, state what it says about the relationship; the value alone is incomplete.

**9. (a)** %P = -4 / 64 = -6.25% [1]. %Q = +1 / 4 = +25% [1]. PED = 25 / -6.25 = **-4** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** %P = -4 / 16 = -25% and %Q = +1 / 16 = +6.25% [1]. PED = **-0.25** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Revenue is 4 x 64 = 256 at the top, 400 at the middle and 16 x 16 = 256 at the bottom, so revenue is highest at a price of **40** [1]. PED there is **-1** (unitary) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Work from the original point each time. The point of this question is that one straight line gives elastic and inelastic values.

**10.** Salt takes a tiny share of spending, so a price rise is hardly noticed and buyers do not change quantity much [1]. Shoes cost a large share of income, so buyers compare prices and delay or cut purchases [1]. Salt has few real alternatives, so switching gains little [1]. Premium shoes face many close substitutes, even with branding, so some buyers switch if the price rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each determinant needs its mechanism; "salt is cheap" with no link to buyer response stays at description.

**11. (a)** New price = 30 x 1.08 = **32.40** [1]. %Q = -1.8 x 8 = -14.4% [1]. Visitors = 50,000 x 0.856 = **42,800** [1]. Revenue = 42,800 x 32.40 = **1,386,720**, down from 1,500,000 [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Revenue falls by 113,280 (about 7.6%) because demand is elastic [1]. So the rise is not sensible if revenue is the aim [1]. However, serving 7,200 fewer visitors may cut costs, so profit could differ from revenue [1]. The PED estimate may not hold for this price range, and the response may differ in busy and quiet seasons [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation that rests on the number and then names one limit on it usually reads as evaluation; a one-sided verdict does not.

**12. (a)** %P = 1 / 8 = +12.5% [1]. %Q = -0.3 x 12.5 = -3.75% [1]. New quantity = 60,000 x 0.9625 = **57,750** [1]. Tax revenue = 57,750 x 1.00 = **57,750** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Revenue: with demand this inelastic the quantity base stays large and the tax raises 57,750 [1]. If PED were -1.6, quantity would fall 20% to 48,000 and revenue would be lower, so inelastic demand favours the revenue aim [1]. Consumption: it falls by only 2,250 units, so the tax does little to cut use [1]. Consumers: spending rises from 480,000 to 519,750, which weighs on low-income buyers [1]. Limits: PED may rise over time as substitutes appear, and may differ at other prices [1]. Judgement: the tax suits a revenue goal better than a consumption goal, and a supporting policy would be needed for the second [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate the two aims and judge each one; blending them into a single verdict usually leaves one aim unevaluated.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the new figure rather than the original in a percentage change.
- Dropping the sign of an elasticity, then misclassifying inferior goods or complements.
- Giving a PED value with no interpretation or no revenue consequence.
- Treating a fall or rise in the good's own price as a shift of demand.
- Converting utility into money values incorrectly, or not at all, before reading off quantity.
- Calling a whole straight-line demand curve elastic or inelastic.
- Naming a determinant of PED without explaining the mechanism.
- Writing evaluation as a list of caveats with no verdict.
