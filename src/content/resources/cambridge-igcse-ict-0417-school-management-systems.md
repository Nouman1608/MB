Subtopic 6.4 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers school management systems. The syllabus says these "systems are used to manage learner registration and attendance" and "to record learner performance including computer aided learning". The [ICT Applications study guide](/resources/igcse-ict-applications-systems/) gives a short overview. This guide compares the methods of taking registers, follows the data through a typical school day, and sets out what computer aided learning can and cannot do.

## What a school management system is

A **school management system** is software built around a **database** of learner records. Each learner has a unique ID number, which links together:

- personal details: name, date of birth, address, parent or carer contact details
- timetable and class groups
- attendance records: date, time of arrival, and present, absent or late
- assessment marks, grades and targets
- notes such as behaviour records and medical needs

Staff log in with a username and password. **Access levels** control what each person can see: a class teacher sees their own groups, while office staff can see contact details for the whole school. This matters because the system holds personal data about children, which must be kept secure and private.

## Registration and attendance

### Methods of recording attendance

| Method | How it works | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electronic register on a computer or tablet | Teacher clicks present, absent or late for each learner | Data goes straight into the database; no paper to collect | Takes lesson time; teacher can click the wrong name |
| OMR register sheet | Teacher shades boxes on a printed form, which is scanned | Fast to read many sheets; teachers need no device | Marks must be in the right place; sheets can be lost or damaged before scanning |
| Magnetic stripe or smart card | Learner swipes or inserts an ID card at the entrance | Automatic and time-stamped; card can also pay for lunch | Cards are lost, forgotten or lent to a friend |
| RFID card or tag | Learner taps or passes a tag near a reader | Very fast; no physical contact; can track movement between buildings | Tags can be lent or lost; readers are costly to install |
| Biometric (fingerprint or face) | Learner's fingerprint or face is scanned at the entrance | Cannot be lost, forgotten or lent | Expensive; privacy concerns; parents may need to give consent; injuries or poor lighting can cause failed scans |

The OMR and RFID methods are explained further in the [recognition systems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-recognition-systems/).

### What the system does with attendance data

- **Time-stamping**: each arrival is stored with the exact time, so late arrivals are identified automatically.
- **Alerts**: if a learner has not registered by a set time, the system can send a text message or email to the parent or carer.
- **Fire and emergency registers**: the office can print or display a list of everyone recorded as on site.
- **Patterns**: reports show attendance over weeks or terms, so staff can spot repeated absence, such as a learner who is often absent on Mondays.
- **Official returns**: attendance figures can be compiled for the school's records and for education authorities.

### Worked example: one learner's morning

The times are invented for illustration. Registration closes at 08:45.

1. At **08:52** Amir taps his RFID card at the gate. The reader sends his ID number and the time to the server.
2. The system finds Amir's record and creates an attendance entry: ID, date, 08:52.
3. It compares 08:52 with the preset time of 08:45. Because the arrival is later, the entry is marked **late**.
4. His form tutor's electronic register shows him as late, with the time, and no manual entry is needed.
5. Amir's classmate Sara has no entry by 09:30. The system marks her **absent** and sends an automatic text to her parent asking them to contact the school.
6. At the end of the week, a report lists all late and absent marks for the tutor group.

This is the same pattern as a control system: input, comparison with a stored value, then an automatic action.

## Recording learner performance

Teachers enter marks from tests, homework and coursework into the database. Because every mark is linked to the learner's ID, the system can:

- calculate averages and grades automatically
- compare each learner's results with their **targets** and highlight those falling behind
- produce **reports** for parents, often through an online portal
- let teachers analyse a whole class's results to find topics that need reteaching
- keep a record of progress across several years

**Advantages:** less time spent on calculations and report writing; fewer errors in totals; information is up to date and available to all teachers of a learner; problems are spotted early so support can be given. **Disadvantages:** staff need training; data entry errors still occur; the system must be kept secure because it holds personal data; a server failure can make records unavailable unless backups are kept.

## Computer aided learning (CAL)

**Computer aided learning** is the use of computers and software to help learners learn and to test their understanding. Examples include interactive lessons, simulations, video tutorials, and online quizzes that are marked automatically.

CAL links to the management system because quiz and test results can be saved straight into the learner's record, so the teacher sees progress without marking by hand.

| Advantages of CAL | Disadvantages of CAL |
|---|---|
| Learners work at their own pace and can repeat material | Devices, software and internet access are costly |
| Instant feedback on answers | Cannot answer unexpected questions the way a teacher can |
| Some software adapts the difficulty to the learner | Learners may be distracted by other content on the device |
| Multimedia can make topics more engaging | Less discussion and social interaction with others |
| Can be used at home and outside school hours | Technical faults interrupt learning |
| Results are recorded automatically | Some learners need more support than software gives |

CAL supports teachers but does not replace them: the teacher still plans learning, explains difficult ideas and supports learners who are struggling.

## Common mistakes

- Saying biometric registration "cannot go wrong". Scans can fail, and the method raises privacy issues.
- Describing a swipe card without saying what is recorded (ID, date, time).
- Treating CAL as only "using computers in lessons". Explain what the software does: delivers content, tests, gives feedback, records results.
- Forgetting the data security point: the system holds personal data about children.
- Mixing up registration (attendance) with recording performance (marks and grades).

## Exam technique

- **Describe** how attendance is recorded: name the method, what is read, what is stored and what the system does with it.
- **Explain** an advantage: point plus consequence, such as "parents are texted automatically, so truancy is noticed on the same day".
- **Compare** methods: say which is better for a stated reason, for example "biometrics, because a fingerprint cannot be lent to a friend".
- **Discuss** CAL: balance its benefits against its limits and say how it works with, not instead of, a teacher.
