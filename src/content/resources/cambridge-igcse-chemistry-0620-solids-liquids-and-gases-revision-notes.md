For full explanations and worked examples, read the [solids, liquids and gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-solids-liquids-and-gases/); then test yourself with the [solids, liquids and gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-solids-liquids-and-gases-practice/).

## The three states at a glance

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | Fixed | Shape of container | Fills container |
| Volume | Fixed | Fixed | Fills container |
| Flows? | No | Yes | Yes |
| Compressible? | No | Hardly | Yes, easily |
| Particle separation | Touching | Mostly touching | Far apart |
| Arrangement | Regular | Random | Random |
| Motion | Vibrate in place | Slide past each other | Fast, random, all directions |

Link every property to a particle reason:

- **Fixed shape** → particles held in fixed positions.
- **Flows** → particles can move past one another.
- **Compressible** → large spaces between particles.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Melting point | Temperature at which a solid turns to liquid; the same as the freezing point for a pure substance |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which a liquid boils, forming gas throughout the liquid |
| Evaporation | Liquid turning to gas at the surface, below the boiling point |
| Condensation | Gas turning to liquid |
| Kinetic particle theory | The idea that all matter is made of tiny particles in constant motion |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of movement; particles gain it when heated |
| Forces of attraction | The forces between particles that hold solids and liquids together |

## Changes of state

```
           melting           boiling / evaporating
  SOLID  ----------> LIQUID ----------------------> GAS
         <----------        <----------------------
           freezing              condensing
```

Melting, boiling and evaporating **take in** energy. Freezing and condensing **give out** energy.

## Deducing the state

At temperature T:

- T below melting point → **solid**
- T between melting and boiling points → **liquid**
- T above boiling point → **gas**

Example: melting point −7 °C, boiling point 59 °C. At 25 °C it is liquid; at 70 °C it is gas; at −20 °C it is solid.

## Heating and cooling curves *(Extended only)*

- Sloping section: one state, temperature changing.
- Flat section: a change of state; two states present.
- First flat section on heating: melting point. Second: boiling point.
- Temperature is constant because energy is used to **overcome forces of attraction**, not to increase kinetic energy.
- The boiling plateau is usually longer than the melting plateau: separating particles completely needs more energy.
- On a cooling curve, flat sections show condensing and freezing; energy is released as forces of attraction form.

## Gas volume

**Core: describe**

- Higher temperature, constant pressure → **larger volume**.
- Higher pressure, constant temperature → **smaller volume**.

**Extended: explain**

- Heating → particles gain kinetic energy → move faster → collide with walls **more often** and **with more force** → gas expands if it can.
- Pressure increased → particles pushed **closer together** → same particles fit in a smaller volume.

## Describe or explain: model sentences

| Command | Question | Answer that earns the marks |
|---|---|---|
| Describe | Describe the arrangement of particles in a solid | Particles are close together, touching, in a regular arrangement |
| Describe | Describe what happens to the volume of a gas when it is heated at constant pressure | The volume increases |
| Explain *(Extended only)* | Explain why the volume increases | Particles gain kinetic energy, move faster, and hit the walls more often and with more force, so the gas expands |
| Explain *(Extended only)* | Explain why boiling needs energy | Energy is needed to overcome the forces of attraction between particles so they can separate completely |

A describe answer with no reasons is complete. An explain answer with no particles, energy or forces is not.

## Reading data quickly

- Write the temperature asked about next to each melting and boiling point.
- Compare: lower than the melting point, between the two, or higher than the boiling point.
- For "liquid range", subtract: boiling point − melting point. With negative values, −39 to 357 is a range of 396 °C.

## Exam traps

- Solid particles vibrate; they do not stay perfectly still.
- Particles never change size; spacing and motion change.
- Liquid particles are close together, not spread out.
- Evaporation happens below the boiling point, at the surface only.
- A flat section of a heating curve contains two states.
- "Describe" needs what happens; "explain" needs particles, energy and forces.
- Negative temperatures: −39 °C is lower than −7 °C.

## Self-test

1. Give two properties of a liquid.
2. Describe the motion of particles in a gas.
3. Why can a gas be compressed?
4. Name the change of state from gas to liquid.
5. State one difference between evaporation and boiling.
6. A substance melts at 80 °C and boils at 218 °C. What is its state at 100 °C?
7. Which change of state gives out energy: melting or freezing?
8. Describe the effect of increasing temperature on the volume of a gas at constant pressure.
9. *(Extended only)* Why does the temperature stay constant while a solid melts?
10. *(Extended only)* Explain why increasing the pressure decreases the volume of a gas.
11. *(Extended only)* On a heating curve, why is the boiling plateau usually longer than the melting plateau?
12. Why does a solid have a fixed shape?

**Answers**

1. Any two: fixed volume; takes the shape of its container; can flow; hardly compressible.
2. Fast, random, in straight lines in all directions until they collide.
3. Its particles are far apart, with large spaces between them.
4. Condensing.
5. Evaporation happens at the surface and below the boiling point; boiling happens throughout the liquid at the boiling point.
6. Liquid.
7. Freezing.
8. The volume increases.
9. The energy supplied is used to overcome forces of attraction between particles, not to increase their kinetic energy.
10. The particles are pushed closer together, and there is space between gas particles to allow this.
11. Boiling separates particles completely, which needs more energy than melting.
12. Its particles are held in fixed positions by strong forces of attraction and can only vibrate.
