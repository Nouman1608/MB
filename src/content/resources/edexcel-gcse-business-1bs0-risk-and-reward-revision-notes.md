These notes condense content item 1.1.2, Risk and reward, from Topic 1.1 Enterprise and entrepreneurship in the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022 (Theme 1, Paper 1). Work through the [1.1.2 study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-risk-and-reward/) first, and practise with the [1.1.2 practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-risk-and-reward-practice/). The [Enterprise and entrepreneurship revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-enterprise-and-entrepreneurship-revision-notes/) cover the rest of Topic 1.1.

## The specification in two lines

- **Risk:** business failure, financial loss, lack of security.
- **Reward:** business success, profit, independence.

The heading is "the impact of risk and reward on business activity", so be ready to explain how the balance affects decisions, not just to define the words.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Tie it to the case by asking |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | The chance that things turn out worse than planned | What could go wrong for this owner? |
| Reward | The gain the owner hopes for in return for the risk | What does this owner hope to get? |
| Business failure | The business closes because it cannot continue | How long could it survive losses? |
| Financial loss | Costs exceed revenue, or money put in is lost | How much has the owner invested? |
| Lack of security | No guaranteed income, sick pay or future | What job or wage is being given up? |
| Business success | Achieving the owner's goals: surviving, growing, a good name | What does success mean for this owner? |
| Profit | Revenue minus total costs, kept by the owner | Is profit likely, and how large? |
| Independence | Being your own boss and making your own decisions | Does the owner value control? |

## Condensed recall

### How the risks link
Lack of security (no fixed wage) → a poor period brings financial loss → repeated losses or no cash → business failure.

### How the rewards link
The business meets customers' needs → business success → profit for the owner, who also enjoys independence.

### Risk and reward move together
- More money invested, a secure job given up, or an untested idea = more risk, and usually a bigger possible reward.
- Starting small, keeping a part-time job, a proven product = less risk, and usually a smaller or slower reward.

### Ways an entrepreneur can reduce risk
These follow from other parts of the specification and are standard points:
- carry out market research before spending (1.2.2 lists "to reduce risk" as a purpose);
- start small and grow with demand;
- choose a source of finance they can afford to lose or repay (1.3.4);
- plan cash with a cash-flow forecast (1.3.3).

### Why people still take the risk
- The hoped-for profit may be larger than a salary.
- Independence and the satisfaction of building something can matter more than money.
- Some see a gap in the market and want to be first to fill it.

## Mini case for recall: Kilnhouse Ceramics

Tobias leaves a job paying £21,000 a year. He puts £12,000 of savings into a pottery studio. His first-year profit is forecast at £15,000, with a poor-year forecast of a £3,000 loss.

- Expected year: £15,000 profit is £6,000 less than his old salary.
- Poor year: a loss of £3,000 and no income.
- Reward: independence and the chance of higher profit as the studio becomes known.
- Risk: lack of security; financial loss of some or all of the £12,000; failure if poor years repeat.

## Exam traps

- Listing "losing money" three times as three risks. Use the three different specification terms.
- Calling profit "money the business makes" without subtracting costs.
- Thinking a loss in one year means the business has failed.
- Saying market research or planning removes risk. They reduce it.
- Forgetting non-financial rewards. Independence is in the specification.
- Ending an evaluation with "it depends" and no decision.

## Self-test

1. List the three risks named in the specification.
2. List the three rewards named in the specification.
3. Define lack of security.
4. What is the difference between financial loss and business failure?
5. Why is profit described as the entrepreneur's reward?
6. Give one non-financial reward of running a business.
7. Why do risk and reward usually rise together?
8. Give two ways an entrepreneur could reduce the risk of starting up.
9. Using Kilnhouse Ceramics, how much less than his old salary is Tobias's expected profit?
10. Using Kilnhouse Ceramics, identify the risk shown by the poor-year forecast.
11. A business makes a loss in its first year but survives. Has it failed? Explain.
12. Explain one reason why someone might start a business even though the expected profit is below their current wage.

### Answers

1. Business failure, financial loss, lack of security.
2. Business success, profit, independence.
3. Having no guaranteed income, sick pay, holiday pay or certainty that the business will continue.
4. Financial loss means costs exceed revenue or money invested is lost; business failure means the business closes.
5. The owner keeps the profit, which is the payment for risking their money and time.
6. Independence (or business success, such as building a good reputation).
7. Actions that could bring a larger gain, such as investing more or launching a new idea, also put more at stake if they go wrong.
8. Any two: carry out market research; start small; keep a part-time job; use a cash-flow forecast; borrow only what can be repaid.
9. £21,000 − £15,000 = £6,000.
10. Financial loss (a £3,000 loss) together with lack of security (no income that year).
11. No. A loss means costs were higher than revenue for the year. The business has only failed if it closes; it can survive if it has enough cash or savings to carry on.
12. They may value independence, the chance of higher profit in later years, or the satisfaction of building their own business more than the extra wage.
