These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The People's Republic of China (1949 to 2020) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-peoples-republic-of-china/) first, then use the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-peoples-republic-of-china-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PLA | People's Liberation Army, the Party's army |
| Three-Antis, Five-Antis | 1951 to 1952 campaigns against corruption and against business crimes |
| Hundred Flowers | 1956 to 1957 invitation to criticise the Party |
| Anti-Rightist Campaign | 1957 repression of critics |
| Great Leap Forward | 1958 to 1962 drive for rapid industrialisation and collectivisation |
| Commune | Large collective unit combining farming, industry, schools and government |
| Red Guards | Young Cultural Revolution activists |
| Gang of Four | Jiang Qing, Zhang Chunqiao, Yao Wenyuan and Wang Hongwen |
| Four Modernizations | Agriculture, industry, defence, science and technology |
| Household responsibility system | Farm households contract land and keep surplus |
| Special Economic Zone | Area open to foreign investment with special rules |
| Hukou | Household registration system limiting migration rights |
| Three Represents | Jiang Zemin's theory allowing business people into the Party |

## Mao's power: methods

1. **Party and army:** PLA subordinate to the Party; Mao's prestige.
2. **Campaigns:** to remove enemies and teach loyalty.
3. **Intellectuals:** Hundred Flowers then Anti-Rightists.
4. **Mass mobilisation:** Red Guards and the Cultural Revolution.
5. **Cult of personality:** the Little Red Book, portraits, slogans.
6. **Purges:** Peng Dehuai (1959), Liu Shaoqi, Deng Xiaoping, Lin Biao.

## Economic and social change under Mao

| Policy | Aim | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Land reform | Give land to peasants | Peasant support; landlords killed |
| First Five-Year Plan | Heavy industry, Soviet help | Strong growth |
| Great Leap Forward | Rapid growth through communes | Famine; tens of millions died |
| Marriage Law 1950 | Equal rights for women | Arranged marriage banned; limits in practice |
| Education and health | Literacy; barefoot doctors | Rise in literacy and life expectancy |
| Religion | State control | Temples and churches attacked |

## Foreign policy under Mao

- **Successes:** prestige from the Korean War; Bandung; atomic bomb (1964); opening to the USA (1971 to 1972); UN seat.
- **Failures:** dependence on the USSR then split; isolation; Sino-Indian War cost goodwill; Taiwan not recovered.

## Deng and after: political developments

| Leader | Key points |
|---|---|
| Hua Guofeng | Arrested Gang of Four; "Two Whatevers"; sidelined by 1978 |
| Deng Xiaoping | Paramount leader; economic reform without political liberalisation |
| Tiananmen 1989 | Student-led protests; martial law; army clears the square on 3 to 4 June |
| Jiang Zemin | Stability and growth; Three Represents |
| Hu Jintao | "Harmonious society"; continued growth |
| Xi Jinping | Anti-corruption campaign; stronger Party control; term limit abolished |

## Economic and social change after 1976

- **Reform:** communes ended; household responsibility system; SEZs from 1980.
- **Growth:** average near 10 per cent a year; WTO entry in 2001; second-largest economy about 2010.
- **Costs:** pollution, inequality, corruption.
- **Rural–urban divide:** hukou; over 200 million migrants.
- **Population:** one-child policy; falling birth rate; imbalance of the sexes; ageing; two children from 2016.

## Foreign policy after 1976

| Area | Developments |
|---|---|
| Region | Vietnam war (1979); Hong Kong (1997); South China Sea; Taiwan |
| USSR/Russia | Normalisation (1989); borders settled; 2001 treaty |
| USA | Relations (1979); trade; tensions after 2018 |
| Belt and Road | Infrastructure across Eurasia and Africa; debt concerns |

## Perspectives

- Dikötter, Yang Jisheng: Mao's responsibility for famine.
- Jung Chang and Halliday: Mao as tyrant; criticised for evidence.
- Party resolution (1981): Mao mainly right, with mistakes.
- Vogel on Deng; critics stress repression.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the success of Deng Xiaoping's economic policies."

1. Thesis: highly successful in growth, less so in equality and political freedom.
2. Policies: household responsibility system, SEZs, Opening Up.
3. Successes: growth, poverty reduction, exports, WTO.
4. Failures: inequality, corruption, pollution, Tiananmen.
5. Compare with Mao's record.
6. Judgement and perspectives.

## Common mistakes

- Dating the Cultural Revolution to the Great Leap Forward.
- Forgetting that Deng limited political reform.
- Quoting casualty figures as exact.
- Writing about Xi Jinping without dates.

## Self-test

1. What did the Marriage Law of 1950 do?
2. What caused the Great Leap Forward famine?
3. Who were the Gang of Four?
4. What was ping-pong diplomacy?
5. What was the household responsibility system?
6. When did China join the WTO?
7. What was the one-child policy's main side effect?
8. What did Jiang Zemin's Three Represents allow?

**Answers:** 1. Banned arranged marriage and concubinage and allowed divorce. 2. Communes, unrealistic targets, inflated reports and grain requisitioning. 3. Jiang Qing, Zhang Chunqiao, Yao Wenyuan and Wang Hongwen. 4. A 1971 table tennis visit that opened US–China contact. 5. Households contracted land and kept the surplus. 6. December 2001. 7. An imbalance between boys and girls, and an ageing population. 8. Business people to join the Party.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Mao judged only by the famine?**
No. A balanced essay weighs the unification of the country, gains in health and literacy and the Marriage Law, as well as the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution.

**How should I treat Tiananmen?**
State the facts carefully, give estimates as ranges, and link to the Party's decision to combine economic reform with political control.
