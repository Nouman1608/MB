It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [shapes of complex ions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-3/); then try the [shapes of complex ions practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-3-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. Small ligands (H₂O, NH₃) usually give six-co-ordinate **octahedral** complexes.
2. Larger ligands (Cl⁻) usually give four-co-ordinate **tetrahedral** complexes.
3. Some four-co-ordinate complexes are **square planar**; cisplatin is one, and it is the **cis** isomer.
4. Ag⁺ forms **linear** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, the complex in Tollens' reagent.
5. Octahedral complexes show **cis–trans** isomerism with monodentate ligands and **optical** isomerism with bidentate ligands; square planar complexes show cis–trans isomerism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Co-ordination number | Number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion |
| Monodentate ligand | Forms one co-ordinate bond (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻) |
| Bidentate ligand | Forms two co-ordinate bonds (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂, C₂O₄²⁻) |
| Octahedral | Six ligands; 90° between adjacent ligands |
| Tetrahedral | Four ligands; 109.5° |
| Square planar | Four ligands in one plane with the metal; 90° |
| Linear | Two ligands; 180° |
| Cis isomer | Two identical ligands adjacent (90°) |
| Trans isomer | Two identical ligands opposite (180°) |
| Optical isomers | Non-superimposable mirror images |

## Shape decision in four steps

1. Count donor atoms: monodentate 1, bidentate 2, EDTA⁴⁻ 6.
2. Total = co-ordination number.
3. Six → octahedral. Two → linear. Four → tetrahedral (typical for Cl⁻ complexes) or square planar (platinum(II) examples such as cisplatin, or when told).
4. Work out the metal's oxidation state from the overall charge and the ligand charges (H₂O and NH₃ are 0; Cl⁻ is −1; C₂O₄²⁻ is −2; EDTA⁴⁻ is −4).

## Examples worth knowing

| Complex | Co-ordination number | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | 6 | octahedral |
| [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ | 6 | octahedral |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | 4 | tetrahedral |
| [FeCl₄]⁻ | 4 | tetrahedral |
| [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | 4 | square planar |
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | 2 | linear |
| [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ | 6 | octahedral |
| [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ | 6 | octahedral |

## Isomer counting

| Type of complex | Stereoisomers | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Octahedral [MA₄B₂] | 2 (cis, trans) | B ligands at 90° or 180° |
| Square planar [MA₂B₂] | 2 (cis, trans) | B ligands at 90° or 180° |
| Tetrahedral [MA₂B₂] | 1 | every position is adjacent to every other |
| Octahedral [M(bidentate)₃] | 2 (optical pair) | propeller twists either way |
| Octahedral [M(bidentate)₂B₂] | 3 (trans + optical pair of cis) | trans has a plane of symmetry; cis does not |
| Linear [MA₂] | 1 | no isomers possible |

**Two rules that do most of the work.**

- A short bidentate ligand (en, C₂O₄²⁻) can only span **adjacent** positions.
- Any complex with a plane of symmetry is **not** chiral. A square planar complex always has one (the molecular plane), so it never shows optical isomerism.

## Cisplatin

- Formula [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]; platinum is +2; square planar.
- **Cis**: Cl–Pt–Cl angle 90° and H₃N–Pt–NH₃ angle 90°.
- The trans isomer has Cl–Pt–Cl at 180°.
- Used as an anticancer drug (its action is in section 3.3.13.3).

## Exam traps

- Co-ordination number is bonds, not ligands: [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ is 6, not 3.
- Tetrahedral is 109.5°, not 90°; square planar is 90°, not 109.5°.
- No cis–trans isomers for tetrahedral complexes.
- "Cis" in cisplatin refers to both pairs: the two Cl⁻ are adjacent and so are the two NH₃.
- The cis form of [M(en)₂X₂] is chiral; the trans form is not.
- [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is linear, not bent.
- Cl⁻ is the larger ligand; its complexes are four-co-ordinate because fewer fit, not because Cl⁻ is charged.
- When drawing octahedral isomers, show 3D (wedges and dashes) and keep bidentate ligands on adjacent positions.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. State the shape and bond angle of [CoCl₄]²⁻.
2. Give the co-ordination number of [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ and the oxidation state of chromium.
3. Why do H₂O ligands give a six-co-ordinate complex with Cu²⁺ but Cl⁻ ligands give a four-co-ordinate one?
4. What is the shape of the complex in Tollens' reagent?
5. Define cis isomer, using a bond angle.
6. Which isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is cisplatin, and what is the Cl–Pt–Cl angle in it?
7. Explain why a tetrahedral complex [MA₂B₂] has no cis–trans isomers.
8. How many stereoisomers does [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ have?
9. How many stereoisomers does [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ have? Explain.
10. Why can a square planar complex never be optically active?
11. A four-co-ordinate nickel complex [NiA₂B₂] exists as two isomers. Deduce its shape.
12. State the overall charge of a complex containing Fe³⁺, one EDTA⁴⁻ ligand and no other ligands, and its shape.

### Answers

1. Tetrahedral; 109.5°.
2. 6 (three bidentate ligands); +3, since Cr − 6 = −3.
3. Cl⁻ is larger than H₂O, so only four fit round the Cu²⁺ ion.
4. Linear, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, 180°.
5. The two identical ligands are adjacent, at 90° to each other.
6. The cis isomer; 90°.
7. All four positions in a tetrahedron are adjacent (all angles 109.5°), so every arrangement is the same.
8. Two: cis and trans.
9. Three: one trans isomer (with a plane of symmetry) and two cis isomers that are non-superimposable mirror images.
10. The metal and all four donor atoms lie in one plane, which is a plane of symmetry.
11. Square planar, because a tetrahedral [MA₂B₂] complex has only one form.
12. [Fe(EDTA)]⁻, charge −1; octahedral (six donor atoms).
