## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *capital expenditure*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure. **[2]**

**3.** Classify each item as capital or revenue expenditure for a taxi company: (i) a new taxi, (ii) cleaning the taxis each week, (iii) fitting a card-payment terminal that will be used for five years, (iv) the drivers' wages. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why training staff to use new equipment is revenue expenditure. **[2]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why a newly formed business needs finance. **[4]**

### Section B: structured questions

Questions 6 to 9 use the case below.

Kittiwake Kayaks hires kayaks on a lake. In its third year it spends:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| 10 new kayaks | 12,000 |
| Delivery of the new kayaks | 400 |
| Repairs to cracked hulls on old kayaks | 1,100 |
| A new boathouse | 30,000 |
| Wages of seasonal instructors | 21,000 |
| Safety training for instructors | 1,500 |
| Advertising | 2,600 |
| Annual lake access licence | 4,000 |

**6.** Calculate:

**(a)** total capital expenditure. **[2]**

**(b)** total revenue expenditure. **[2]**

**(c)** capital expenditure as a percentage of total spending, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why the delivery of the new kayaks is treated as capital expenditure. **[2]**

**8.** Kittiwake's bookkeeper recorded the 30,000 boathouse as an expense in the statement of profit or loss. Explain the effect of this mistake on the final accounts. **[4]**

**9.** Analyse why Kittiwake is unlikely to fund its capital expenditure in the same way as its revenue expenditure. **[4]**

**10.** Halloway Engineering is a mature, profitable manufacturer. Ressa Foods is a start-up opening its first café. Analyse how the role of finance differs between the two businesses. **[6]**

**11.** Tamberlin Print must choose between buying a new press for 80,000 or paying 2,500 a month to use a press under a rental agreement. Explain how each option would be classified and how each would affect the first year's reported profit. **[4]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Spending on non-current assets that will be used in the business for more than one year [1], or on improving such assets, for example buying machinery or a building [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Capital expenditure buys or improves assets that benefit the business for more than one year, while revenue expenditure covers day-to-day running costs whose benefit is used up within the year [1]; capital spending is recorded as an asset, whereas revenue spending is charged as an expense against the year's profit [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (i) Capital [1]. (ii) Revenue [1]. (iii) Capital [1]. (iv) Revenue [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Training develops the skills of staff rather than adding to the value or life of the equipment [1], and the business does not own those skills as an asset, so the cost is charged as an expense in the year [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Reason 1: to buy assets such as premises and equipment before any sales revenue arrives [1], because without them the business cannot start trading [1]. Reason 2: to pay day-to-day costs such as wages and suppliers in the early months [1], when sales are low and customers may pay late [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct reasons, each with a consequence, are needed. Two forms of the same reason earn less.

### Section B

**6. (a)** 12,000 + 400 + 30,000 [1] = **42,400** [1]

**(b)** 1,100 + 21,000 + 1,500 + 2,600 + 4,000 [1] = **30,200** [1]

**(c)** 42,400 ÷ 72,600 × 100 = **58.4%** [1] **[5]**

**7.** Delivery is a cost needed to bring the kayaks into use [1], so it forms part of the cost of the non-current assets rather than a running cost [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Expenses are overstated by 30,000 [1], so profit for the year is understated by 30,000 [1]. Non-current assets on the statement of financial position are understated by 30,000 [1], so the business appears smaller and less profitable than it is, which could mislead owners or lenders [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Capital expenditure here is 42,400, large and one-off [1], so it is unlikely to be covered by a single season's trading cash [1]. The boathouse and kayaks will earn revenue for many years, so longer-term finance such as a loan matches their life [1]. Revenue expenditure such as wages recurs each season and can be paid from the cash that hire fees bring in [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Ressa Foods needs finance mainly to buy kitchen equipment and fit out the café before it earns revenue [1], and to cover running costs while customer numbers build [1]; its role is about survival, and it has no retained profit to draw on [1]. Halloway is profitable, so it can fund day-to-day costs from trading cash [1]; its finance role is mainly replacing assets and funding expansion [1], and it has more choice of sources because of its record [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Buying: the 80,000 press is capital expenditure, recorded as a non-current asset [1], so only a share of its cost reduces first-year profit through depreciation, not the full 80,000 [1]. Renting: 2,500 × 12 = 30,000 is revenue expenditure [1], charged in full against first-year profit as an expense [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Depreciation methods are HL only in section 3.4, but recognising that capital spending is spread over time is part of understanding the classification.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Treating delivery or installation as revenue expenditure.
* Treating training as capital expenditure.
* Adding totals without showing which items were placed in each group.
* Describing the effect of misclassification on profit but not on assets.
* Explaining the role of finance without reference to the business's stage.
