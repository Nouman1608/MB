## The levels

| Level | Organisms | Name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plants and algae (make their own food) | Producers |
| 2 | Herbivores | Primary consumers |
| 3 | Carnivores that eat herbivores | Secondary consumers |
| 4 | Carnivores that eat other carnivores | Tertiary consumers |

- **Apex predator**: a carnivore with no predators.
- An organism's level depends on **what it eats in that chain**; an omnivore can be at more than one level.

## Decomposers

```
secrete enzymes into the environment → enzymes digest dead material outside the cell
→ small soluble food molecules diffuse into the microorganism
```

## Pyramids of biomass

- Show the **relative amount of biomass** at each level of a food chain.
- **Level 1 at the bottom.**
- Bar **width proportional to biomass**, to a stated scale.
- Each level is smaller than the one below, because biomass is lost at each transfer.

**Drawing checklist**

- Choose a scale that fits the largest bar on the page.
- Calculate every bar width with the same scale.
- Label each bar with its level or organism and its biomass.
- Do not enlarge small bars to make them visible.

## Transfer of biomass: the two numbers

| Figure | What it describes |
|---|---|
| About **1%** | Fraction of incident light energy transferred by producers for photosynthesis |
| About **10%** | Fraction of biomass at one trophic level transferred to the level above |

Both are approximate; real values vary.

## Where the biomass goes

| Loss | What happens |
|---|---|
| Egestion | Material that is eaten but not absorbed leaves as faeces |
| Respiration | Absorbed glucose is broken down; carbon dioxide and water are lost |
| Excretion | Water and urea lost in urine |
| Glucose for energy | Large amounts of glucose are used in respiration for movement and keeping warm, so they do not become new biomass |
| Parts not eaten | Roots, bones and other parts may not be eaten |

## Efficiency

```
efficiency (%) = biomass at higher level ÷ biomass at lower level × 100
```

Worked reminders:

```
2500 g → 200 g:  200 ÷ 2500 × 100 = 8%      fraction 200/2500 = 2/25
200 g → 15 g:    15 ÷ 200 × 100 = 7.5%      fraction 15/200 = 3/40
estimate with 10%: 6000 kg producers → about 600 kg primary → about 60 kg secondary
```

Check: an efficiency above 100% means the division is upside down.

## Effect on numbers of organisms

- Less biomass at each higher level → **fewer organisms** can be supported.
- Few levels in a chain: after four or five transfers too little biomass is left.

Model answer to learn (3 marks): "Only about 10% of the biomass at one level passes to the next. The rest is
lost as faeces, in respiration and in urine. So each higher level has less biomass and can support fewer
organisms."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trophic level | The position of an organism in a food chain |
| Producer | Plant or alga that makes its own food; level 1 |
| Herbivore | Animal that eats plants; level 2 |
| Carnivore | Animal that eats other animals |
| Apex predator | Carnivore with no predators |
| Decomposer | Microorganism that secretes enzymes onto dead matter and absorbs the soluble products |
| Biomass | Mass of living material |
| Pyramid of biomass | Diagram of bars showing relative biomass at each trophic level |
| Egestion | Removal of undigested food as faeces |
| Efficiency of transfer | Percentage (or fraction) of biomass passed from one level to the next |

## Exam traps

- Herbivores are level **2**, not level 1.
- Egestion (faeces) is not excretion (urea in urine).
- Energy and biomass are not "used up"; say they are lost or transferred to the surroundings.
- Give fractions in their **simplest form** if asked.
- Pyramids must be to scale, level 1 at the bottom.
- Decomposers do not ingest food; digestion happens outside them.

## Self-test

1. Which trophic level do herbivores belong to?
2. What is an apex predator?
3. Describe how decomposers obtain food.
4. Which trophic level goes at the bottom of a pyramid of biomass?
5. About what percentage of incident light energy do producers transfer for photosynthesis?
6. About what percentage of biomass passes from one trophic level to the next?
7. Give three ways biomass is lost between trophic levels.
8. A level has 3200 g of biomass and the level above has 256 g. Calculate the efficiency.
9. Write the efficiency in question 8 as a fraction in its simplest form.
10. Using the 10% estimate, how much biomass reaches level 3 from 9000 kg of producers?
11. Explain why there are fewer organisms at higher trophic levels.
12. A student calculates an efficiency of 1250%. What mistake did they make?

### Answers

1. Level 2 (primary consumers).
2. A carnivore with no predators.
3. They secrete enzymes into the environment to break down dead material; small soluble molecules then diffuse
   into the microorganism.
4. Level 1 (producers).
5. About 1%.
6. About 10%.
7. Any three: egested as faeces; lost as carbon dioxide and water in respiration; lost as water and urea in
   urine; glucose used in respiration; parts not eaten.
8. 256 ÷ 3200 × 100 = **8%**.
9. 256/3200 = **2/25**.
10. 9000 → 900 → **90 kg**.
11. Biomass is lost at each transfer, so less biomass is available at higher levels to support organisms.
12. They divided the lower level's biomass by the higher level's biomass (the division was upside down).
