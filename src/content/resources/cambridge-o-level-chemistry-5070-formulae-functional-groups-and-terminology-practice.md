These questions cover section 11.1, Formulae, functional groups and terminology, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Learn the content first with the [formulae, functional groups and terminology study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae-functional-groups-and-terminology/) and the [formulae, functional groups and terminology revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae-functional-groups-and-terminology-revision-notes/).

Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16.

## Section A

**1.** Define the term *functional group*. **[1]**

**2.** Which formula belongs to the same homologous series as C₃H₆?

A: C₃H₈

B: C₅H₁₀

C: C₄H₉OH

D: C₂H₆

**[1]**

**3.** State the general formula of the alcohols. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a *saturated* compound. **[1]**

**5.** Write the molecular formula of each compound.

**(a)** The alkane with 6 carbon atoms. **[1]**

**(b)** The carboxylic acid CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH. **[1]**

**6.** Name the functional group present in CH₃CH₂CH₂OH. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The structural formula of a compound is CH₃COOCH₃.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of this compound. **[2]**

**(b)** State the molecular formula of the compound. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a structural formula is more useful than a molecular formula for this compound. **[1]**

**8.** Butane, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃, has a structural isomer.

**(a)** Define *structural isomers*. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the structural formula of the isomer of butane. **[1]**

**(c)** A student draws butane with the chain bent into an L-shape and says this is a third isomer. Explain why the student is wrong. **[1]**

**9.** The table gives information about the first four members of a homologous series.

| Compound | Formula | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| Methanol | CH₃OH | 65 |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH | 78 |
| Propan-1-ol | C₃H₇OH | 97 |
| Butan-1-ol | C₄H₉OH | 118 |

**(a)** State **two** features of the data that show these compounds form a homologous series. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict, with a reason, whether the boiling point of the next member, C₅H₁₁OH, is higher or lower than 118 °C. **[1]**

**(c)** State why all four compounds react in similar ways. **[1]**

**10.** An alkane has a relative molecular mass of 86.

**(a)** Show that its formula is C₆H₁₄. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether the alkane is saturated or unsaturated. Give a reason. **[1]**

**11.** Two compounds both have the molecular formula C₄H₈. Their structural formulae are CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ and CH₃CH=CHCH₃.

**(a)** State the term used to describe this pair of compounds. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the structural difference between them. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why both compounds are described as unsaturated. **[1]**

**12.** Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, contains a C=O double bond. A student says it is unsaturated. Explain whether the student is right. **[2]**

**13.** Draw the displayed formula of CH₃CH₂COOH. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** An atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a homologous series [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* C₃H₆ and C₅H₁₀ both fit CₙH₂ₙ (alkenes). C₃H₈ has the same number of carbons but is an alkane.

**3.** CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A compound whose molecules have only single carbon–carbon bonds [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** C₆H₁₄ [1]

**(b)** C₄H₈O₂ [1]

**[2]**

**6.** Hydroxyl group / –OH (alcohol) [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** CH₃ carbon with three H atoms bonded to a carbon that has C=O and a single bond to O [1]; that O bonded to a CH₃ group with three H atoms; all bonds shown and every carbon with four bonds [1]:

```
      H   O       H
      |   ‖       |
  H - C - C - O - C - H
      |           |
      H           H
```

**(b)** C₃H₆O₂ [1]

**(c)** The molecular formula does not show how the atoms are arranged / several different compounds could have the same molecular formula; the structural formula is unambiguous [1].

**[4]**

**8. (a)** Compounds with the same molecular formula [1] but different structural formulae [1].

**(b)** CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ [1]

**(c)** Bending the chain does not change how the atoms are joined; the longest chain is still four carbons with no branch, so it is the same molecule [1].

**[4]**

**9. (a)** Any two [1] [1]: each formula differs from the next by CH₂; all fit the same general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH; all have the same functional group, –OH; the boiling point increases steadily (trend in physical properties).

**(b)** Higher, because boiling point increases as the chain gets longer [1].

**(c)** They have the same functional group, –OH [1].

**[4]**

**10. (a)** For CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, Mr = 14n + 2 = 86 [1]; n = 6, so C₆H₁₄ (or 6 × 12 + 14 × 1 = 86) [1].

**(b)** Saturated: alkanes have only single carbon–carbon bonds [1].

**[3]**

**11. (a)** Structural isomers [1]

**(b)** The position of the carbon–carbon double bond is different (end of the chain in one, middle of the chain in the other) [1]

**(c)** Each contains a carbon–carbon double bond, so not all carbon–carbon bonds are single [1]

**[3]**

**12.** The student is wrong [1]. Saturation depends only on carbon–carbon bonds; the C=O bond is between carbon and oxygen, and the only carbon–carbon bond in ethanoic acid is single [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Chain of three carbons with all H atoms and the C–C bonds shown [1]; –COOH drawn with C=O and a separate C–O–H, including the O–H bond [1]:

```
      H   H   O
      |   |   ‖
  H - C - C - C - O - H
      |   |
      H   H
```

**[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "same formula" instead of "same molecular formula, different structural formulae".
- Leaving out hydrogens, or the O–H bond, in displayed formulae.
- Joining the two oxygen atoms in an ester or acid.
- Treating a C=O bond as making a compound unsaturated.
- Saying members of a homologous series have the same physical properties.
