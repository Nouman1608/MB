These questions practise the three Breadth Study question types of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE
History 4HI1 Paper 2, Section B — two differences (6), two causes (8) and a 16-mark change-over-time
essay — across the five key topics and themes of "Japan in transformation, 1853–1945".

---

## Set 1

**1(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the government of Japan in 1860 was different from the government
of Japan in 1890. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** causes of the fall of the Tokugawa shogunate. **[8]**

**1(c)** "Victory in war was the main reason Japan became a world power in the years 1868–1919." How far
do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05)
- the industrial revolution of the Meiji period

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 2

**2(a)** Explain **two** ways in which life in Japanese cities in the 1920s was different from life in
Japanese cities in the 1870s. **[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** causes of the return to military rule in Japan in the 1930s. **[8]**

**2(c)** "The Depression was the main reason for the decline of democracy in Japan in the years
1919–41." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Public Security Preservation Law (1925)
- Manchukuo and the League of Nations

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 3 — extra practice

**3(a)** Explain **two** ways in which Japan's relations with China in 1890 were different from its
relations with China in 1937. **[6]**

**3(b)** Explain **two** causes of the breakup of the Restoration coalition in 1873. **[8]**

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

- **Who ruled:** in 1860 the Tokugawa shogun governed in the emperor's name through feudal domains; in
  1890 the emperor was sovereign under the Meiji Constitution, with ministers drawn from the Satsuma and
  Choshu oligarchy.
- **Representation:** in 1860 there was no parliament; in 1890 the first elected Diet met, though only a
  small number of wealthy men could vote.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Humiliation by the West:** Perry's arrival and the unequal treaties showed the shogunate could not
  defend Japan, angering samurai who turned to "revere the emperor, expel the barbarians".
- **The rise of Choshu and Satsuma:** these domains modernised their forces after clashes with Western
  navies and allied in 1866, giving opponents of the shogunate the military strength to force change.

### 1(c) [16]

**War:** victory over China (1894–95) brought Taiwan and prestige; the Boxer intervention (1900); defeat
of Russia (1904–05) made Japan the first Asian state to beat a European power; Korea (1910); German
possessions gained in 1914–19 and kept at Versailles.
**Other factors:** Meiji reforms — conscription, the land tax, the Constitution; industrialisation through
the zaibatsu, steel and shipbuilding, which made the victories possible; diplomacy, especially the
Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902); Western knowledge brought by missions such as Iwakura's.
**Judgement:** war demonstrated Japan's power, but modernisation created it. Top answers range across
1868–1919.

### 2(a) [6]

- **Culture:** in the 1870s Western fashions were new and limited to a few; by the 1920s cinemas, cafés,
  department stores and the "modern girl" were common in Tokyo and Osaka.
- **Work and politics:** by the 1920s many city dwellers were factory or office workers, unions were
  active and men could vote from 1925; in the 1870s samurai had only just lost their privileges and there
  was no parliament.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Economic crisis:** the Depression ruined farmers and silk producers, and people blamed party
  politicians linked to the zaibatsu.
- **Military success and violence:** the conquest of Manchuria (1931–32) was popular, and assassinations
  such as Inukai's (1932) and the February 1936 coup attempt intimidated civilian leaders.

### 2(c) [16]

**Depression:** collapse of silk and rice prices, rural poverty, distrust of parties and business, support
for military expansion as an answer to economic problems.
**Other factors:** limits of Taisho Democracy — the emperor's sovereignty and the army's independence under
the Constitution; the Public Security Preservation Law (1925) used against the left; Tenko; the Kanto
Earthquake and 1920s recession; nationalism and the success of Manchukuo; anger at Western racism at
Versailles and at League condemnation in 1933; Konoe's New Order and the National Mobilisation Law (1938).
**Judgement:** the Depression was the trigger, but weaknesses in the political system existed earlier.

### 3(a) [6]

- In 1890 Japan was a smaller power that had not yet fought China; by 1937 it controlled Taiwan and
  Manchukuo and was at full-scale war with China.
- In 1890 China was still the leading power in Korea; by 1937 Japan had annexed Korea (1910).

### 3(b) [8]

- **The Korea debate:** Saigo Takamori and others wanted to force Korea to open; leaders returning from the
  Iwakura Mission insisted domestic reform came first, so Saigo resigned.
- **Samurai discontent:** the loss of samurai privileges and the new conscript army angered many former
  samurai, whose leaders split from the government and later rebelled in 1877.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), describing only one of the two dates.
- In (b), describing events without saying how they caused the outcome.
- In (c), writing about one decade instead of the whole span of the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the Paper 2 breadth study question structured?**
Part (a) asks you to explain two ways something was different at two dates (6 marks), part (b) to explain two causes (8 marks) and part (c) is a 16-mark essay on change, continuity or significance across the period.

**How did Japan's government change between 1860 and 1890?**
In 1860 the Tokugawa shogun governed in the emperor's name through feudal domains; by 1890 the emperor was sovereign under the Meiji Constitution, with an elected Diet.

**What loses marks in part (c)?**
Writing about one decade instead of tracing change and continuity across the whole period.
