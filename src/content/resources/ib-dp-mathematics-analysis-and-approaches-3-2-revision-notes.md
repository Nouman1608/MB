These notes condense **section 3.2** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: right-angled trigonometry, the sine rule, the cosine rule and the area of a triangle. It is SL content, examined at SL and HL. The full explanations are in the [triangle trigonometry study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-2/); exam-style questions are in the [triangle trigonometry practice set](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-2-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Right-angled triangles**

- sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse; cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse; tan θ = opposite/adjacent.
- Angles from sides: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹.
- Pythagoras: hypotenuse² = sum of the squares of the other two sides.
- Isosceles triangles split into two right-angled triangles along the line of symmetry.

**Sine rule**

- a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, or sin A/a = sin B/b = sin C/c for angles.
- Needs one complete pair: a side and its opposite angle.
- Finding an angle: the calculator gives the acute value only.
- Safe when the angle found is opposite a side shorter than the known side of the complete pair.
- The ambiguous case belongs to section 3.5, not 3.2.

**Cosine rule**

- c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C (two sides and the included angle).
- cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab) (three sides).
- Negative cos C means C is obtuse; the rule handles it automatically.
- Use it for the largest angle, opposite the longest side.

**Area**

- Area = ½ab sin C, where C is the angle between sides a and b.
- Reverse to find a side: a = 2 × Area/(b sin C).
- Reverse to find the angle: sin C = 2 × Area/(ab); two angles, C and 180° − C, may both be possible.

**Exact values for Paper 1**

- sin 30° = ½, cos 30° = √3/2, tan 30° = √3/3.
- sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2, tan 45° = 1.
- sin 60° = √3/2, cos 60° = ½, tan 60° = √3.
- cos 120° = −½, sin 120° = √3/2, sin 150° = ½.

**Accuracy**

- Store unrounded results between steps.
- Final lengths to 3 s.f.; angles in degrees when the question uses degrees.
- Check the calculator mode (degrees or radians) before starting.

**Method: a two-step triangle problem**

1. Sketch and label the triangle with every given value.
2. Ask: is there a complete pair? If yes, the sine rule is available; if no, start with the cosine rule.
3. Find the missing piece and store it unrounded.
4. For a second angle, prefer the one opposite the shorter side, or use the cosine rule again.
5. Finish with the angle sum or the area formula if asked.

**Sense checks**

- The longest side is opposite the largest angle.
- Angles add to 180°.
- If cos C > 1 or sin A > 1 appears, a value has been paired or typed wrongly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Opposite side | The side facing a given angle; side a faces angle A |
| Included angle | The angle between two given sides |
| Complete pair | A side together with its opposite angle |
| Hypotenuse | The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle |
| Obtuse angle | Between 90° and 180°; its cosine is negative |
| Supplement | 180° minus an angle; it has the same sine |

## Exam traps

- In triangle PQR, side p is QR, not PQ.
- Sine rule for an obtuse angle returns the acute supplement.
- Minus times a negative cosine: c² = a² + b² + 2ab|cos C| when C is obtuse.
- ½ab sin C needs the angle between a and b.
- Decimal answers when "exact" is asked.
- Radian mode for a degree question gives nonsense values.
- Area questions can have two valid included angles.

## Self-test

1. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 20 cm and an angle of 40°. Find the side opposite this angle.
2. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 7 cm and 24 cm. Find the hypotenuse and the smallest angle.
3. You know a, b and the angle C between them. Which rule finds c?
4. In triangle ABC, A = 50°, B = 60° and a = 10 cm. Find b.
5. Two sides are 4 cm and 6 cm with included angle 120°. Find the exact third side.
6. Find the exact area of the triangle in question 5.
7. A triangle has sides 5, 6 and 9 cm. Find its largest angle.
8. Two sides are 5 cm and 12 cm and the area is 15 cm². Find the possible included angles.
9. Why is the sine rule unsafe for finding the largest angle of a triangle?
10. Find the area of an isosceles triangle with sides 10, 10 and 16 cm, without using trigonometry.
11. A triangle has sides x cm and 8 cm, included angle 30° and area 24 cm². Find x.
12. In triangle PQR, which side is q?

### Answers

1. 20 sin 40° = 12.9 cm.
2. 25 cm; tan⁻¹(7/24) = 16.3°.
3. The cosine rule.
4. b = 10 sin 60°/sin 50° = 11.3 cm.
5. c² = 16 + 36 − 48 cos 120° = 52 + 24 = 76, so c = 2√19 cm.
6. ½ × 4 × 6 × √3/2 = 6√3 cm².
7. cos C = (25 + 36 − 81)/60 = −1/3, so C = 109.5°.
8. sin C = 30/60 = ½, so 30° or 150°.
9. It may be obtuse, and the sine rule only returns the acute angle with that sine.
10. Height √(100 − 64) = 6, area ½ × 16 × 6 = 48 cm².
11. ½ × x × 8 × ½ = 24, so 2x = 24 and x = 12.
12. PR, the side opposite angle Q.
