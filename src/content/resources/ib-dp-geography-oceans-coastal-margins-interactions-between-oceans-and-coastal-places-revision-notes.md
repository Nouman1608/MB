Condensed recall for **2.2 Interactions between oceans and coastal places** (IB DP Geography, Option B: Oceans and coastal margins). See the [Oceans and coastal margins study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-oceans-coastal-margins-study-guide/) for the full account.

## What shapes coasts

Waves, tides, currents, geology, sea level and human activity.

## Wave processes

- **Erosion:** hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, corrosion.
- **Constructive waves** tend to build beaches; **destructive waves** remove sediment.
- **Longshore drift** moves sediment along the coast in the direction of the prevailing wave approach.

## Landforms

| Erosion | Deposition |
|---|---|
| Cliffs, wave-cut platforms | Beaches |
| Caves, arches, stacks | Spits, bars, tombolos |

Explain each as a sequence of steps, not just a name.

## Sediment cells

A **sediment cell** treats a stretch of coast as a system with inputs, transfers, stores and outputs. Interrupting the flow, for example with a groyne or harbour wall, can starve beaches downdrift.

## Sea level change

- **Eustatic:** change in the global volume of water.
- **Isostatic:** land rising or sinking.
- Present-day **relative** sea level rise reflects both.
- Low-lying deltas, atolls and reef islands are especially exposed.

## Coastal ecosystems

Coral reefs, mangroves and salt marshes:

- dissipate wave energy (natural protection);
- support fisheries;
- store carbon.

Threats: warming, acidification, pollution, coastal development, destructive fishing. Losing the ecosystem raises coastal vulnerability.

## Worked check

A cliff top retreats 3.6 m in 8 years: 3.6 / 8 = 0.45 m per year. Retreat is episodic (most happens in storms), so extrapolating a mean rate is risky.

## Exam traps

- Describing a landform without the process that formed it.
- Forgetting that coasts are systems: action in one place has knock-on effects elsewhere.
- Mixing eustatic and isostatic change.
- Treating mean erosion rates as steady.

## Self-test

1. Name the four erosion processes of waves.
2. How do constructive and destructive waves differ?
3. What is a sediment cell?
4. Distinguish eustatic from isostatic change.
5. Explain how a mangrove forest reduces coastal risk.

**Answers:** 1. Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, corrosion. 2. Constructive waves build beaches; destructive waves remove sediment. 3. A stretch of coast treated as a system of inputs, transfers, stores and outputs of sediment. 4. Eustatic: global water volume changes; isostatic: land rises or sinks. 5. It dissipates wave energy, so less energy reaches the land.
