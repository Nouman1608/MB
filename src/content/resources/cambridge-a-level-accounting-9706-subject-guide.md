Cambridge International AS & A Level Accounting (9706) teaches how
businesses record, prepare and interpret financial information, and
how managers use cost information to plan and control. This overview
follows the 2026-2028 syllabus. The course is taken in two stages: the
AS Level (Papers 1 and 2) can stand alone or form the first half of
the full A Level, which is completed by adding Papers 3 and 4. All four
papers are externally assessed written examinations.

## How the course is organised

Each stage has a financial accounting strand and a cost and management
accounting strand, so the same two halves of the subject run through
the whole course at increasing depth.

**AS Level content**

- *Financial accounting:* types of business entity; the accounting
  system; accounting for non-current assets; reconciliation and
  verification; preparation of financial statements; and analysis and
  communication of accounting information.
- *Cost and management accounting:* costs and cost behaviour; and
  traditional costing methods.

**A Level content**

- *Financial accounting:* preparation of financial statements;
  regulatory and ethical considerations; business acquisition and
  merger; computerised accounting systems; and analysis and
  communication of accounting information.
- *Cost and management accounting:* activity based costing (ABC);
  standard costing; budgeting and budgetary control; and investment
  appraisal.

Two topic names, preparation of financial statements and analysis and
communication of accounting information, appear at both stages. At A
Level they return with more demanding content that builds on the AS
treatment.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Stage | Time | Marks | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | AS | 1 hour | 30 | 14% |
| Paper 2 | Fundamentals of Accounting | AS | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 36% |
| Paper 3 | Financial Accounting | A Level only | 1 hour 30 minutes | 75 | 30% |
| Paper 4 | Cost and Management Accounting | A Level only | 1 hour | 50 | 20% |

The weightings in the table are the shares of the full A Level, and
they total 100%. If you take the AS Level as a qualification on its
own, the AS papers carry different weightings: Paper 1 is 28% and
Paper 2 is 72% of the AS.

The assessment is staged rather than strictly linear. Papers 1 and 2
can lead to the AS Level as a qualification in its own right, or serve
as the first half of the A Level, which is completed by adding Papers
3 and 4 in a later series. Ask your centre which route you are
following. The 2026-2028 syllabus is first examined in 2026.

Paper 2 carries the largest single share of the A Level, so the AS
content it assesses deserves particular care even if you are aiming
for the full qualification.

## How the parts fit together

The two strands answer different questions. Financial accounting asks
how a business records what has happened and reports it to owners and
other users; cost and management accounting asks how managers use
cost information to make decisions about the future. Papers 3 and 4
follow that division at A Level, with Financial Accounting and Cost
and Management Accounting examined separately.

Within each strand, the A Level builds directly on the AS. Preparation
of financial statements and analysis and communication of accounting
information are revisited with more complex material, and
business acquisition and merger, regulatory and ethical
considerations and computerised accounting systems widen the
financial picture. In cost and management accounting, activity based
costing and standard costing extend the traditional costing methods
met at AS, while budgeting and investment appraisal move from
measuring costs to planning with them.

The accounting system, non-current assets and reconciliation at AS
are the skills everything else depends on. Accurate double-entry
records, correct treatment of assets and careful verification all
feed into the financial statements you prepare and the ratios and
commentary you produce when analysing them.

## Planning your study

1. **Start with entities and the accounting system.** Types of business
   entity and the accounting system give you the vocabulary and the
   recording methods for the rest of the course.
2. **Complete the AS financial strand.** Non-current assets,
   reconciliation and verification, then preparation and analysis of
   financial statements.
3. **Add AS cost and management accounting.** Costs, cost behaviour and
   traditional costing methods can run alongside the later financial
   topics.
4. **Move to the A Level content.** Extend financial statements and
   analysis, then cover regulatory and ethical considerations,
   acquisition and merger and computerised systems; in parallel, study
   ABC, standard costing, budgeting and investment appraisal.
5. **Keep AS skills sharp.** Even at A Level, regular practice of
   double-entry and adjustments keeps the foundations reliable.

Begin topic questions as soon as each topic is taught. Full papers
and the exam preparation page are most useful once a stage's content
is complete: before the AS papers if you sit them first, and again
before Papers 3 and 4.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Accounting 9706?**
There are four. Papers 1 and 2 make up the AS Level, and Papers 3 and 4 complete the full A Level.

**How much is each 9706 paper worth?**
As shares of the full A Level, Paper 1 is 14%, Paper 2 is 36%, Paper 3 is 30% and Paper 4 is 20%. For the AS Level on its own, Paper 1 is 28% and Paper 2 is 72%.

**Can I take AS Accounting first and finish the A Level later?**
Yes. The AS Level can be a standalone qualification or the first half of the A Level, completed by adding Papers 3 and 4 in a later series.

**Is Paper 1 multiple choice?**
Yes. Paper 1 is a 1-hour multiple-choice paper worth 30 marks.

**Which syllabus does this overview follow?**
It follows the Cambridge 2026-2028 syllabus for 9706, first examined in 2026.
