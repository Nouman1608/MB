These questions cover section 2.9 (market economic system) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All countries and figures are invented. Revise first with the [market economic system study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-market-economic-system/) and the [market economic system revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-market-economic-system-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which feature is most typical of a market economic system? **[1]**

A: The government decides what each factory produces

B: Prices are set by demand and supply

C: All land is owned by the state

D: Goods are shared equally between households

**2.** In a market economic system, how is the question "for whom to produce?" mainly answered? **[1]**

A: By government officials according to need

B: By lottery

C: By the ability of consumers to pay

D: By the number of people in each household

**3.** Define a market economic system. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** roles that a government still carries out in a market economy. **[2]**

**5.** In Country Pell (invented), the price of a smartwatch rises from 120 to 150 while the cost of making one stays at 100.

**(a)** Calculate the profit per watch before and after the price rise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in profit per watch. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a pure market economic system might not provide street lighting. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** In Country Pell, total household income is 1,200 billion. The richest 10% of households earn 42% of it and the poorest 10% earn 1.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the income of each group. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the richest group's share is than the poorest group's. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how this distribution affects what is produced in a market economy. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** advantages of a market economic system for consumers. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how competition in a market economic system can lead to an efficient use of resources. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** disadvantages of a market economic system for people on low incomes. **[4]**

**11.** Country Rask (invented) has low taxes and little state provision. Country Mova (invented) has high taxes and provides free health care and education. Analyse how the allocation of health care is likely to differ between the two countries. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether a market economic system is the best way to allocate resources. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A and C describe planned features; D is not how any market allocates goods. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. Output goes to those willing and able to pay the market price. **[1]**

**3.** An economic system in which resources are allocated through the price mechanism [1], with mainly private ownership of resources and little government intervention [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: maintaining law and order [1]; defence, enforcing contracts, protecting property rights [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Before: 120 − 100 = **20** [1]; after: 150 − 100 = **50** [1].

**(b)** (50 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 = **150%** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Firms cannot stop people who do not pay from benefiting from street lighting [1], so they cannot charge for it and it is not profitable to provide [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Richest: 0.42 × 1,200 = **504 billion** [1]; poorest: 0.015 × 1,200 = **18 billion** [1].

**(b)** 42 ÷ 1.5 = **28 times** [1].

**(c)** Spending power follows income, so firms earn more profit by producing goods the richest households want [1], such as luxury goods, while goods that poorer households need may be produced in smaller quantities [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Choice: many competing firms offer different products and prices [1], so consumers can find goods that match their tastes and budgets [1]. Responsiveness: when consumers' wants change, prices and profits change [1], so firms quickly switch resources to the goods consumers want more of [1]. Other acceptable: lower prices through competition; new products through innovation. **[4]**

**9.** Firms compete to attract customers [1]. A firm with higher costs must charge higher prices or earn lower profits, so it loses customers or may close [1]. Firms therefore have an incentive to keep costs low and avoid wasting resources [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Inequality: output goes to those who can pay [1], so people on low incomes may be unable to afford essentials such as good housing [1]. Under-provision of merit goods: education and health care must be bought [1], so low-income families may receive too little of them, harming their future earnings [1]. **[4]**

**11.** In Rask, health care is allocated mainly by price [1]: people with high incomes or private insurance receive treatment quickly, while those on low incomes may go without [1]. Private providers have a profit incentive to offer new treatments and short waiting times to those who pay [1]. In Mova, health care is allocated mainly by need and free at the point of use [1], so access is more equal, but high taxes are needed to pay for it and waiting times may be longer if demand exceeds what the state provides [1]. **[5]**

**12.** For: competition encourages efficient use of resources [1]; the price mechanism responds quickly to changing wants without a costly planning system [1]; the profit motive encourages innovation and new products [1]; consumers have wide choice [1]. Against: the output goes to those with income, so inequality can be wide [1]; goods such as street lighting may not be provided, and merit goods may be under-consumed [1]; pollution and other harm to third parties are ignored [1]. Conclusion: the market system is likely to be the best way to allocate most everyday goods, but not for public goods, merit goods or activities with large external costs; this is why most countries use a mixed system [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest conclusions say where markets work best and where they do not, rather than "it depends" with no condition.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining the market system as one with no government at all.
- Giving advantages without explaining the chain from feature to result.
- Saying output goes to those in greatest need.
- In Question 7, using 42 and 1.5 as amounts rather than percentages of total income.
- Writing only advantages or only disadvantages in a "Discuss" answer.
