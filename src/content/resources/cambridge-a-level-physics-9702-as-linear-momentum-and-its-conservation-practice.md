## Section A

**1.** Two objects collide. Which quantity is always conserved, provided no resultant external force acts?

A: total kinetic energy

B: total momentum

C: the speed of each object

D: the momentum of each object **[1]**

**2.** In an elastic collision, the relative speed of approach is 5.0 m s⁻¹. What is the relative speed of separation?

A: 0

B: 2.5 m s⁻¹

C: 5.0 m s⁻¹

D: 10 m s⁻¹ **[1]**

**3.** State the principle of conservation of momentum. **[2]**

**4.** A 0.30 kg trolley moving at 2.0 m s⁻¹ collides with a stationary 0.20 kg trolley, and they stick together. Calculate their common velocity. **[2]**

**5.** A stationary gun of mass 3.0 kg fires a bullet of mass 0.010 kg at 360 m s⁻¹. Calculate the recoil velocity of the gun. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A 0.50 kg ball moving at 6.0 m s⁻¹ collides elastically head-on with a stationary 1.5 kg ball.

**(a)** Write down an equation for conservation of momentum, using v₁ and v₂ for the final velocities of the 0.50 kg and 1.5 kg balls. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down a second equation using the relative speeds of approach and separation. **[1]**

**(c)** Solve the equations to find v₁ and v₂. **[2]**

**(d)** Show that total kinetic energy is conserved. **[2]**

**7.** A 1.5 kg trolley moving at 4.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 0.50 kg trolley. After the collision the 1.5 kg trolley moves at 2.4 m s⁻¹ in its original direction.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of the 0.50 kg trolley after the collision. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine whether the collision is elastic. **[3]**

**8.** Two gliders on an air track move towards each other. Glider P has mass 0.40 kg and speed 0.50 m s⁻¹; glider Q has mass 0.60 kg and speed 0.30 m s⁻¹. They stick together.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of the gliders after the collision, stating its direction. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the loss of kinetic energy. **[2]**

**9.** A firework of mass 3.0 kg is at rest at the top of its flight when it explodes into two pieces of mass 1.2 kg and 1.8 kg, which move horizontally. The 1.2 kg piece moves at 18 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of the 1.8 kg piece. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total kinetic energy of the pieces just after the explosion and state where it came from. **[2]**

**10.** A 2.0 kg puck moving east at 3.0 m s⁻¹ collides with a 1.0 kg puck moving north at 4.0 m s⁻¹. The pucks stick together.

**(a)** Calculate the east and north components of the total momentum. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed and direction of the combined pucks. **[3]**

**(c)** Show that the collision is inelastic. **[2]**

**11.** A student says: "In an inelastic collision, momentum is lost as heat and sound."

**(a)** Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, using Newton's third law, why the total momentum of two colliding objects is unchanged. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The total momentum of a system of interacting objects remains constant [1], provided no resultant external force acts on the system [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 0.30 × 2.0 = (0.30 + 0.20)v [1]; v = 0.60 ÷ 0.50 = **1.2 m s⁻¹** in the original direction [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 0 = 0.010 × 360 + 3.0v [1]; v = −3.6 ÷ 3.0 = **−1.2 m s⁻¹**, that is 1.2 m s⁻¹ opposite to the bullet [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 0.50 × 6.0 = 0.50v₁ + 1.5v₂ [1].

**(b)** 6.0 − 0 = v₂ − v₁ [1].

**(c)** From (b), v₂ = v₁ + 6.0; substitute: 3.0 = 0.50v₁ + 1.5v₁ + 9.0, so v₁ = **−3.0 m s⁻¹** (rebounds) [1]; v₂ = **3.0 m s⁻¹** forwards [1].

**(d)** Before: ½ × 0.50 × 6.0² = 9.0 J [1]. After: ½ × 0.50 × 3.0² + ½ × 1.5 × 3.0² = 2.25 + 6.75 = 9.0 J, so kinetic energy is conserved [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** 1.5 × 4.0 = 1.5 × 2.4 + 0.50v [1]; v = 2.4 ÷ 0.50 = **4.8 m s⁻¹** forwards [1].

**(b)** KE before = ½ × 1.5 × 4.0² = 12 J [1]. KE after = ½ × 1.5 × 2.4² + ½ × 0.50 × 4.8² = 4.32 + 5.76 = 10.1 J [1]. KE decreases, so the collision is **inelastic** (also, separation speed 2.4 m s⁻¹ is less than approach speed 4.0 m s⁻¹) [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Taking P's direction as positive: 0.40 × 0.50 + 0.60 × (−0.30) = 0.20 − 0.18 = 0.020 kg m s⁻¹ [1]; v = 0.020 ÷ 1.00 [1] = **0.020 m s⁻¹** in P's original direction [1].

**(b)** KE before = ½ × 0.40 × 0.50² + ½ × 0.60 × 0.30² = 0.050 + 0.027 = 0.077 J; KE after = ½ × 1.00 × 0.020² = 0.0002 J [1]; loss = **0.077 J** (0.0768 J) [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** 0 = 1.2 × 18 + 1.8v [1]; v = **−12 m s⁻¹**, that is 12 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction [1].

**(b)** KE = ½ × 1.2 × 18² + ½ × 1.8 × 12² = 194.4 + 129.6 = **324 J** [1]; from chemical energy stored in the explosive [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** East: 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 kg m s⁻¹; north: 1.0 × 4.0 = 4.0 kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** Total momentum = √(6.0² + 4.0²) = 7.21 kg m s⁻¹ [1]; v = 7.21 ÷ 3.0 = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1]; direction tan⁻¹(4.0 ÷ 6.0) = **34° north of east** [1].

**(c)** KE before = ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² + ½ × 1.0 × 4.0² = 9.0 + 8.0 = 17 J [1]; KE after = ½ × 3.0 × 2.4² = 8.7 J, which is less, so the collision is inelastic [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Momentum is never lost: it is conserved in every collision with no resultant external force [1]. It is kinetic energy that is partly transferred to internal energy and sound [1].

**(b)** During the collision each object exerts a force on the other that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction (third law) [1]. The forces act for the same time, so the changes in momentum (force × time) are equal and opposite [1]. The changes therefore cancel, and the total momentum is unchanged [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to give opposite velocities opposite signs.
- Leaving out "no resultant external force" from the principle.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved because momentum is.
- Using the relative-speed rule for a collision not stated to be elastic.
- In two dimensions, adding the two speeds instead of combining momentum components.
