## Questions

**1.** Define the term "conglomerate". **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a merger and a takeover, explaining each. **[4]**

**3.** Oxbarrow Joinery received 64, 70, 67, 76, 73 and 82 orders in six consecutive months.

**(a)** Calculate the three-period moving averages. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of using them to forecast next month's orders. **[2]**

**4.** Harbledown Print is considering a press costing 150,000. Net cash flows in years 1 to 4 are 40,000, 60,000, 70,000 and 50,000. Calculate the payback period, the average (accounting) rate of return, and the net present value using discount factors of 0.926, 0.857, 0.794 and 0.735. **[7]**

**5.** Pilmoor Dairy can launch a cheese line (cost 90,000; 0.5 chance of a 300,000 return, 0.5 chance of 40,000) or upgrade its existing plant (cost 30,000; 0.8 chance of 120,000, 0.2 chance of 60,000). Returns are before the cost shown.

**(a)** Calculate the net expected value of each option. **[4]**
**(b)** Recommend an option and state one limitation of the technique. **[3]**

**6.** Faversby Packaging plans a line change. Activity A (2 days) and B (4 days) can start at once. C (3 days) follows A. D (5 days) follows B. E (6 days) follows both C and D. Use nodes 1 to 5, with A from 1 to 2, B from 1 to 3, C from 2 to 4, D from 3 to 4 and E from 4 to 5.

**(a)** Calculate the earliest start time at nodes 4 and 5 and the latest finish time at nodes 2 and 3. **[4]**
**(b)** Identify the critical path and calculate the total float of A. **[3]**
**(c)** State one limitation of critical path analysis. **[1]**

**7.** Kinsdale Fabrics sells a bolt of cloth at 40 with variable cost of 26. Fixed costs are 84,000 a year and it sells 7,000 bolts, with capacity for 9,000. A buyer offers 1,500 bolts at 31 each.

**(a)** Calculate the contribution per bolt and the current annual profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Justify whether to accept the offer. **[3]**

**8.** Meldreth Toys has revenue of 800,000, cost of sales of 520,000, other operating expenses of 170,000 and interest of 20,000. Current assets are 210,000 (inventory 120,000), current liabilities 90,000, non-current liabilities 150,000 and total equity 350,000. Calculate the gross profit margin, profit for the year margin, current ratio, acid test ratio, gearing ratio and return on capital employed. **[6]**

**9.** Tumby Retail averaged 250 employees. During the year 40 left. Employees lost 1,500 days out of 60,000 possible working days.

**(a)** Calculate labour turnover and the absenteeism rate. **[4]**
**(b)** Analyse how employee share ownership might reduce labour turnover. **[4]**

**10.** Radwell Logistics' board proposes freezing driver pay for two years and delaying replacement of old diesel vans, which would raise the dividend by 15%. Drivers' representatives object, and a major customer wants lower emissions from suppliers. Evaluate the proposal using the stakeholder and shareholder models. **[8]**

**11.** Drayfield Cafes has 12 cafes and a power culture. It can take over Eastlow Bakeries, its main supplier, which has four sites and a role culture. Drayfield has non-current liabilities of 1.8m and capital employed of 4.0m. It would borrow 1.2m, and capital employed would rise by 1.2m.

**(a)** Calculate gearing before and after the takeover. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend whether Drayfield should proceed. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that operates in several unrelated industries [1], formed by merging with or taking over firms outside its original market [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the key idea is "unrelated"; simply saying "a large firm" does not distinguish a conglomerate from other growth.

**2.** In a merger two firms agree together to join [1] as one organisation, usually on similar terms [1]. A takeover is where one firm buys enough shares to control another [1], which may be opposed by the target's board [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give both definitions and make the contrast explicit; naming one only caps the explanation.

**3. (a)** Method: add three consecutive values and divide by 3 [1]. (64+70+67)/3 = 67 and (70+67+76)/3 = 71 [1]. 72 and 77 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The averages assume the past trend continues [1], but a rival's launch or a change in demand could break the pattern, so next month's orders could differ sharply [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* show the working so a slip in one figure still reveals correct method; a limitation scores when it is explained for this business.

**4.** Payback: 100,000 back after 2 years, 50,000 left of 70,000 [1], so 2.71 years [1]. ARR: (220,000 - 150,000) / 4 = 17,500 [1]; 17,500 / 150,000 x 100 = 11.7% [1]. NPV: 37,040 + 51,420 + 55,580 + 36,750 = 180,790 [1]; less 150,000 [1] gives **+30,790** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* the NPV must show the initial cost subtracted; stopping at the total present value is a common loss.

**5. (a)** Cheese line: 0.5 x 300,000 + 0.5 x 40,000 = 170,000 [1]; less 90,000 = **80,000** [1]. Upgrade: 0.8 x 120,000 + 0.2 x 60,000 = 108,000 [1]; less 30,000 = **78,000** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The cheese line has the higher net value [1], but only by 2,000, so the upgrade's lower outlay and narrower range of outcomes may suit a cautious firm [1]. Limitation: the probabilities are subjective estimates [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a recommendation needs a reason tied to the figures; when values are close, saying so shows judgement.

**6. (a)** Node 4: A then C is 5, B then D is 9, so EST is the larger, 9 [1]. Node 5: 9 + 6 = 15 [1]. LFT at node 4 = 15 - 6 = 9 [1]; node 3 = 9 - 5 = 4 [1]. Node 2 = 9 - 3 = 6. **[4]**
**(b)** Critical path B, D, E [1], 15 days [1]. Float of A = LFT 6 - EST 0 - duration 2 = **4 days** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Durations are only estimates, so the critical path may change [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* write the sums for float; the answer is the gap between available and needed time, not either figure alone.

**7. (a)** Contribution = 40 - 26 = **14** [1]. Total contribution 7,000 x 14 = 98,000 [1]; profit 98,000 - 84,000 = **14,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The offer gives 31 - 26 = 5 contribution a bolt, 7,500 extra profit [1]. Capacity allows 2,000 more bolts, so no existing sales are displaced [1]. Risk: regular customers may demand the lower price, so accept only if the order is kept separate [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* justify by comparing price with variable cost, not with average cost; then qualify with a non-numerical risk.

**8.** Gross profit 280,000: **35%** [1]. Operating profit 110,000, profit for the year 90,000: **11.25%** [1]. Current ratio 210/90 = **2.33** [1]. Acid test (210 - 120)/90 = **1.0** [1]. Capital employed 500,000; gearing 150/500 = **30%** [1]. ROCE 110/500 = **22%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* gearing and ROCE both use capital employed, not total assets; state it so a wrong base is clear.

**9. (a)** Turnover = 40 / 250 x 100 = **16%** [1][1]. Absenteeism = 1,500 / 60,000 x 100 = **2.5%** [1][1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Employees become part-owners [1], so they gain from the firm's success through dividends or share value [1]. This can raise commitment and make leaving costly, since shares may be forfeited [1]. Turnover may then fall, though the effect is weak if pay is otherwise low [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* analysis is a chain (share ownership, then incentive, then staying); a list of benefits without links earns less.

**10.** Shareholder model: the freeze and delay cut costs and lift dividends by 15%, which meets the aim of returns [2]. Stakeholder model: drivers face falling real pay, so turnover and absence may rise and service fall [2]; the customer's emissions demand and the environment are also affected, and the contract could be lost [2]. Judgement: a short-term gain may cost long-term revenue and reputation, so the proposal is weak unless the pay freeze is limited and van replacement is only phased [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* for evaluate, weigh both sides and reach a conclusion that depends on stated factors such as the contract's value.

**11. (a)** Before: 1.8 / 4.0 x 100 = **45%** [1]. After: non-current liabilities 3.0m and capital employed 5.2m [1]; 3.0 / 5.2 x 100 = **57.7%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** For: backward vertical integration secures supply and may cut costs, and Drayfield gains control over quality [2]. Against: gearing rises from 45% to 57.7%, raising interest and risk if sales fall [2]; a power culture and a role culture may clash, causing resistance and a slower start [2]. Judgement: proceed only if the supply savings clearly exceed the extra interest and if managers plan the culture change, otherwise grow organically or use a long-term supply deal [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* use the figures in the case, then reach a recommendation with a condition; "it depends" without reasons gains little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping at the present value total instead of subtracting the initial cost to find NPV.
- Using total rather than average annual profit in ARR.
- Taking the smaller figure forward where paths merge in a critical path network.
- Using the wrong base for gearing or ROCE.
- Forgetting to subtract the option's cost on a decision tree.
- Giving a limitation of a technique with no link to the business in the case.
- Describing stakeholder and shareholder views without weighing them.
- Ending an evaluate answer with no judgement, or with a judgement that states no condition.
