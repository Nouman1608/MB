This guide covers section 1.04, Inverse operations, of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The statement is in the first column, so it is content for **both tiers**. The same idea, undoing an operation, is the basis of rearranging formulae and solving equations later in the course.

## What the specification asks

Section 1.04a asks you to "know that addition and subtraction, multiplication and division, and powers and roots, are inverse operations and use this to simplify and check calculations, for example, in reversing arithmetic in 'I'm thinking of a number' or 'missing digit' problems". It gives two examples of simplifying: 223 − 98 = 223 + 2 − 100 = 125 and 25 × 12 = 50 × 6 = 100 × 3 = 300. It also cross-refers to the calculation of powers and roots in 3.01b.

| Operation | Inverse | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add 9 | Subtract 9 | 17 + 9 = 26, so 26 − 9 = 17 |
| Multiply by 6 | Divide by 6 | 14 × 6 = 84, so 84 ÷ 6 = 14 |
| Square (positive numbers) | Square root | 17² = 289, so √289 = 17 |
| Cube | Cube root | 6³ = 216, so ∛216 = 6 |

An operation followed by its inverse leaves the number unchanged. That single fact drives everything below.

## Simplifying calculations

### Addition and subtraction: compensate

Change one number to a friendly one, then use the inverse to undo the change.

```
467 + 199 = 467 + 200 − 1 = 666        (added 1 too many, so take 1 off)
223 − 98  = 223 − 100 + 2 = 125        (took 2 too many, so give 2 back)
```

The specification writes the second as 223 + 2 − 100. Both versions add 2 and subtract 100. For subtraction there is a second route: add the same amount to **both** numbers, which keeps the difference the same. 223 − 98 becomes 225 − 100 = 125.

### Multiplication and division: balance

In a product, multiplying one factor by 2 and dividing the other by 2 is an operation and its inverse, so the product is unchanged.

```
25 × 12  = 50 × 6 = 100 × 3 = 300      (the specification's example)
125 × 24 = 250 × 12 = 500 × 6 = 1 000 × 3 = 3 000
35 × 18  = 70 × 9 = 630
```

In a division, multiply **both** numbers by the same amount. The quotient is unchanged, because the scaling cancels.

```
1 800 ÷ 25 = 7 200 ÷ 100 = 72          (both × 4)
```

Choose the change that makes one number a power of 10 or a small whole number.

## Checking answers

Every calculation can be checked with its inverse. The check should be a different calculation from the one you did, so that the same slip is not repeated.

| You worked out | Check with |
|---|---|
| 5 000 − 1 784 = 3 216 | 3 216 + 1 784 = 5 000 |
| 384 × 27 = 10 368 | 10 368 ÷ 27 = 384 (or 10 368 ÷ 384 = 27) |
| 4 284 ÷ 34 = 126 | 126 × 34 = 4 284 |
| √729 = 27 | 27² = 729 |

On a calculator paper, checking with the inverse also catches keying errors.

## Number puzzles: undo in reverse order

Write the steps as a flowchart. To go back, reverse the order **and** replace each step with its inverse.

**Worked example 1.** I think of a number. I subtract 7, multiply by 3, then take the square root. The answer is 6. Find my number.

```
forward:  n → −7 → ×3 → √ → 6
reverse:  6 → square → ÷3 → +7
          6² = 36,  36 ÷ 3 = 12,  12 + 7 = 19
check:    19 − 7 = 12,  12 × 3 = 36,  √36 = 6
```

The number is **19**.

**Worked example 2.** I think of a number. I cube it, add 10, then halve the result. The answer is 37.

```
reverse:  37 × 2 = 74,  74 − 10 = 64,  ∛64 = 4
check:    4³ = 64,  64 + 10 = 74,  74 ÷ 2 = 37
```

The number is **4**.

### The "subtract from" trap

When the unknown is subtracted **from** a number, or a number is divided **by** the unknown, the inverse is not the obvious one.

**Worked example 3.** I think of a number and subtract it from 50. I double the result and get 36.

```
reverse the doubling:  36 ÷ 2 = 18, so 50 − n = 18
50 minus what is 18?   n = 50 − 18 = 32
check:  50 − 32 = 18,  18 × 2 = 36
```

The number is **32**, not 18 + 50 = 68. "Subtract from 50" is undone by subtracting from 50 again. In the same way, "divide 60 by the number" is undone by dividing 60 by the result.

## Missing numbers

Use the inverse of the operation shown, but read the position of the box carefully.

```
□ × 23 = 1 541      □ = 1 541 ÷ 23 = 67
□ − 368 = 529       □ = 529 + 368 = 897
4 284 ÷ □ = 34      □ = 4 284 ÷ 34 = 126     (not 4 284 × 34)
800 − □ = 345       □ = 800 − 345 = 455      (not 800 + 345)
```

The last two are the "from" and "by" cases again. A quick substitution confirms each answer.

## Missing digits: work column by column

**Worked example 4.** Find the missing digits a and b.

```
   8 a 3
 − 2 7 b
 -------
   5 6 8
```

```
units:     3 − b ends in 8; 3 is too small, so exchange: 13 − b = 8, b = 5
tens:      a − 1 (after the exchange) − 7 must end in 6
           a − 8 = 6 is impossible, so exchange again: 10 + a − 8 = 6, a = 4
hundreds:  8 − 1 − 2 = 5, which matches
843 − 275 = 568.  Check by the inverse: 568 + 275 = 843.
```

**Worked example 5.** Find the digits a and b in 3a6 × 7 = 2b52.

```
units:  6 × 7 = 42: units digit 2 matches, carry 4
tens:   7 × a + 4 must end in 5, so 7a ends in 1: 7 × 3 = 21, a = 3
        (no other digit works: 7 × 0 to 7 × 9 end in 0, 7, 4, 1, 8, 5, 2, 9, 6, 3)
result: 336 × 7 = 2 352, so b = 3
check:  2 352 ÷ 7 = 336
```

Write the carries down. Most wrong answers in missing-digit questions come from a forgotten carry.

## Common mistakes

- Undoing the steps of a puzzle in the original order instead of the reverse order.
- Using × as the inverse of "divide 60 by the number", or + as the inverse of "subtract it from 50".
- Compensating in the wrong direction: 467 + 199 is 467 + 200 − 1, not + 1.
- In a division, multiplying only one number by 4 when simplifying.
- Checking with the same calculation again, which repeats the same slip.
- Forgetting the exchange or the carry in a missing-digit column.
- Taking the square root as the inverse of squaring when the original number could be negative and the question asks for all possibilities.

## Exam technique

- **"Show how you could check"** or **"Use inverse operations to check"**: write the inverse calculation in full and state that it gives back the original number.
- **"Show that"** a simplified calculation is correct: write each equivalent line, as in 25 × 12 = 50 × 6 = 100 × 3 = 300.
- **Number puzzles**: write the reverse flowchart, then a forward check. The check often earns the final mark.
- **Missing digits**: explain each column briefly. A correct pair of digits with no reasoning may not score full marks when the question asks you to show your method.
