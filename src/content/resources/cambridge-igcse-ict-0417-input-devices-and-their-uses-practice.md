**1.** Name the pointing device built into most laptops. **[1]**

**2.** Name the type of sensor that would detect an intruder moving across a room in the dark. **[1]**

**3.** Which input device would be most suitable for entering a four-digit code to open a door?

A: trackerball

B: numeric keypad

C: light pen

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A driving school uses a simulator to train learner drivers before they go on the road.

**(a)** Name **one** input device that would make the simulator realistic. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **two** advantages of this device compared with a keyboard for the simulator. **[2]**

**8.** A historian wants to store a collection of old paper photographs on a computer.

**(a)** Name the input device the historian should use. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **two** advantages of storing the photographs digitally. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **one** disadvantage of using this device. **[1]**

**9.** A museum is designing an information point for visitors. The designers are choosing between a touch screen and a keyboard with a mouse.

**(a)** Give **two** reasons why a touch screen is more suitable. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **one** disadvantage of the touch screen for the museum. **[1]**

**10.** A writer with a wrist injury wants to produce a novel without typing.

**(a)** Name a suitable input device. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how it would be used to produce the text. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **two** problems the writer might have. **[2]**

**11.** A weather station on a remote mountain records conditions every five minutes.

**(a)** Name **three** sensors the station could use and what each measures. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **two** reasons why sensors are better than a person taking the readings. **[2]**

**12.** A graphic designer is choosing between a mouse and a light pen for detailed drawing on screen.

Compare the two devices for this task. **[4]**

**13.** A sound engineer uses a remote control to operate a multimedia projector from the back of a hall. Sometimes the projector does not respond.

**(a)** Explain why this might happen. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **one** advantage of the remote control for the engineer. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Touchpad. **[1]**

**2.** Infrared sensor (credit motion sensor). **[1]**

**3.** B: numeric keypad. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: video-conferencing or video calls (1); online lessons or meetings (1); taking photos for profiles or identity checks (1); monitoring a room (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any two: a ball in a fixed holder (1); the ball is rolled by the fingers, thumb or palm to move the pointer (1); has buttons like a mouse (1); the device itself does not move (1). **[2]**

**6.** A microphone produces an analogue signal that varies continuously (1); the computer can only process digital data, so it must be converted by an analogue-to-digital converter or sound card (1). **[2]**

**7. (a)** Driving wheel (credit pedals or joystick). **[1]**

**(b)** Any two: turning a wheel is like real driving, so skills transfer to a real car (1); gives smooth, continuous control of steering rather than on/off key presses (1); more engaging for learners (1). **[2]**

**8. (a)** Scanner (flatbed scanner). **[1]**

**(b)** Any two: copies can be backed up so the collection is not lost if the originals are damaged (1); images can be edited or restored (1); can be shared by email or online (1); takes up no physical space (1). **[2]**

**(c)** Any one: quality depends on the condition of the original and the resolution (1); high-resolution files are large (1); scanning a large collection takes a long time (1). **[1]**

**9. (a)** Any two: intuitive for visitors of any age, so no training is needed (1); no separate keyboard or mouse to be damaged or stolen (1); visitors select options by touching what they see, which is quick (1); takes up less space (1). **[2]**

**(b)** Any one: fingerprints and dirt on the screen (1); spreads germs between visitors (1); only a limited number of options fit on screen (1); screen can be damaged (1). **[1]**

**10. (a)** Microphone (with speech-recognition software). **[1]**

**(b)** The writer speaks the text into the microphone (1) and speech-recognition software converts the spoken words into text in a word processor (1). **[2]**

**(c)** Any two: background noise causes errors (1); words may be misrecognised, especially names or with an accent, so editing is needed (1); punctuation and formatting must be spoken as commands (1); others nearby can hear (1). **[2]**

**11. (a)** Any three, each with what it measures: temperature sensor, how hot or cold the air is (1); humidity sensor, moisture in the air (1); light sensor, sunlight levels (1); pressure sensor, air pressure (1); moisture sensor, rainfall or soil moisture (1). **[3]**

**(b)** Any two: readings can be taken continuously, day and night, and in bad weather (1); no person has to travel to or stay at the remote, possibly dangerous, site (1); readings are more accurate and there are no recording errors (1); readings are sent to the computer automatically (1). **[2]**

**12.** Any four, with points about both devices: a light pen is used directly on the screen, so drawing feels like using a real pen (1), whereas a mouse is moved on the desk and the line appears elsewhere, which is less natural (1); a light pen allows more precise placement on screen than a mouse for freehand lines (1); a light pen works only with suitable screens, whereas a mouse works with any computer (1); holding a light pen against an upright screen is tiring and the hand can block the view, which is not a problem with a mouse (1). **[4]**

**13. (a)** Many remote controls use infrared, which needs a clear line of sight to the projector (1); people or objects between the engineer and the projector may block the signal, or the engineer may be out of range, or the batteries may be low (1). **[2]**

**(b)** The engineer can change slides or settings without walking to the projector (1). **[1]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a webcam a camera in the 0417 list?**
Yes. Cameras include digital cameras, video cameras and webcams. Use whichever the scenario describes.

**Do I need to know how a sensor works inside?**
No. Know what each sensor measures, where it is used, and that its readings usually need converting from analogue to digital.
