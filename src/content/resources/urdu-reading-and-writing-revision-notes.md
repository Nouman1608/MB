## The paper's six exercises

Paper 1 is worth 60 marks, 2 hours, all in Urdu (no dictionaries):

| Exercise | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short answer questions — skim/gist-read a short text | 8 |
| 2 | Multiple matching — sentences to short paragraphs | 9 |
| 3 | Note-making under supplied headings | 9 |
| 4 | Summary, up to 100 words, own words as far as possible | 10 |
| 5 | Short functional writing (e.g. email), ~120 words | 8 |
| 6 | Extended writing, ~200 words | 16 |

Exercises 1–3 test reading skill of increasing depth; exercises 5 and 6
carry the most marks (24 of 60) and both require the specified
**purpose, format and audience** to be clearly reflected in the answer.

## Comprehension — the method

1. Read the **questions first**.
2. Read the passage once for overall sense.
3. Locate the relevant section and read **around** it — inference answers are rarely in one sentence.
4. Answer **in your own words** where instructed.
5. Match answer length to the **marks available**.

## Question types — treat them differently

| Type | Wants | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| *What / who / when* | Located detail | Quote-and-move-on is fine |
| *Why* | Inference | Copying the text scores nothing |
| Writer's attitude | Tone from word choice | Look for evaluative language |
| Meaning in context | Surrounding sentences | The clue is always nearby |

## Register — heavily credited

| Task | Register | Address |
|---|---|---|
| Letter to an official | Formal | **aap** throughout |
| Report, article | Formal / neutral | Impersonal |
| Message to a friend | Informal | **tum** acceptable |
| Speech | Formal but direct | Audience-aware |

Drifting between registers mid-piece costs marks even when every sentence is individually correct — examiners read for a single, consistent level of formality across the whole response, not just correctness sentence by sentence.

## The accuracy points that decide the grade

- **Gender agreement** — noun, adjective **and** verb must all agree.
- **Verb forms** — tense, person, and consistent respect level (tu / tum / aap).
- **Postpositions** — ne, ko, se, mein, par. The **ergative *ne*** is required with transitive perfective verbs.
- **Izafat** and compound constructions in formal writing.
- **Script accuracy** — dots and diacritics. A misplaced dot changes the word entirely.
- **Punctuation** to Urdu convention.

## A worked comprehension example

Question: *Why did the writer decide to leave the village?* — the
passage states he found no work and his children's school had closed.

```
WEAK    Copies the two sentences verbatim
        -> shows location, not understanding

STRONG  Gives the reason in your own Urdu: economic necessity (no
        employment available) combined with concern for his
        children's education

CHECK   Does the answer address WHY, not WHAT?
        Is it in your own words?
        Is the length right for the marks available?
```

## Writing structure

Plan before writing — five minutes saves the organisation marks.

```
Introduction   state the purpose / set the scene
Body           3-4 paragraphs, ONE main idea each
Conclusion     resolve, summarise, or state the request
```

For discursive writing, decide the line of argument first. For narrative, decide the **ending** first.

## Exam traps

- Copying the passage where own words are required.
- Long answers to one-mark questions and short answers to six-mark ones.
- Mixing respect levels within one piece.
- Omitting the ergative *ne*.
- Ambitious vocabulary used wrongly — a simpler correct word scores higher.
- Leaving no time to check: a five-minute reread catches most agreement errors.
- Writing a functional piece (Exercise 5) that ignores the specified purpose, format or audience — appropriateness is credited separately from accuracy, so an accurate but wrongly-pitched answer still loses marks.
- Answering "what happened" when the question asks "why" — this is the single most common way comprehension marks are lost, since a located quotation cannot substitute for an inference.

## Self-test

1. What should you do before reading the passage?
2. Which address form suits a letter to a head teacher?
3. When is the postposition *ne* required?
4. Why does a misplaced dot matter in Urdu script?
5. What is the fastest way to lose organisation marks?
6. How many marks does Paper 1 carry in total, and which two exercises carry the most?
7. What is the target length for the extended writing exercise (Exercise 6)?

**Answers:** 1. Read the questions, so you know what to look for while reading. 2. **aap** — formal register throughout, with respectful opening and closing. 3. With transitive verbs in the perfective (completed past) — the ergative construction. 4. Urdu letters are distinguished by the number and position of dots; changing them produces an entirely different letter and therefore a different word. 5. Writing without a plan, so the piece drifts and paragraphs carry no single clear idea. 6. 60 marks; Exercises 5 and 6 (the two writing tasks) carry 24 of the 60 marks between them. 7. Approximately 200 words.
