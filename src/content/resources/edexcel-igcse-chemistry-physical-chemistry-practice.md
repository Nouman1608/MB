## Questions

**1.** A student burns 0.46 g of ethanol, C₂H₅OH, to heat 100 g of water. The temperature of the water rises by 25.0 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy transferred to the water, in J. (*c* = 4.2 J/g/°C) **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the molar enthalpy change of combustion of ethanol, in kJ/mol. (*M*ᵣ of ethanol = 46) **[2]**

**2.** Manganese(IV) oxide is a catalyst for the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide. Explain how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction. **[2]**

**3.** Explain, in terms of particles, why powdered calcium carbonate reacts faster with dilute hydrochloric acid than the same mass of large lumps. **[2]**

**4.** Ammonia is made in a reversible reaction: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** State what is meant by a *dynamic equilibrium*. **[2]**

**(b)** State and explain the effect on the yield of ammonia of increasing the temperature. **[2]**

**5.** Use the bond energies below to calculate the enthalpy change for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl.

Bond energies in kJ/mol: H–H 436, Cl–Cl 242, H–Cl 431 **[3]**

**6.** A student mixes 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid with 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution. The temperature rises by 6.8 °C. Assume the solution has a density of 1.0 g/cm³ and *c* = 4.2 J/g/°C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released, in J. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation, in kJ/mol, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**7.** Sketch the reaction profile (energy level diagram) for an exothermic reaction. Label the reactants, products, activation energy and enthalpy change. Then state how the profile changes when a catalyst is added. **[4]**

**8.** A student reacts excess magnesium with 50 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid and measures the total volume of hydrogen collected.

| Time (s) | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of H₂ (cm³) | 0 | 30 | 48 | 57 | 60 | 60 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of reaction between 0 and 20 s, with units. **[2]**

**(b)** State the earliest time, from the table, at which the reaction had finished and give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the rate is lower between 40 s and 60 s than between 0 and 20 s. **[2]**

**9.** Explain, using collision theory, why increasing the temperature increases the rate of a reaction. **[3]**

**10.** Blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated gently in a test tube. A white solid forms and the tube has droplets on its cooler walls. Water is then added to the cooled white solid.

**(a)** State the colour change when the white solid reacts with water. **[1]**

**(b)** State one observation, other than colour, that shows the reaction with water is exothermic. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain what the heating step tells you about the energy change in the forward reaction. **[1]**

**11.** In the Haber process, nitrogen and hydrogen react at about 450 °C and 200 atm using an iron catalyst. The table shows illustrative equilibrium yields of ammonia at 450 °C.

| Pressure (atm) | 100 | 200 | 300 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonia in mixture (%) | 10 | 18 | 25 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in the table and explain it using the equation N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). **[3]**

**(b)** Give one reason why a plant does not use a pressure of 1000 atm. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a temperature of 450 °C is chosen, although a lower temperature gives a higher equilibrium yield. **[3]**

**(d)** A reactor uses 56 kg of nitrogen. Calculate the maximum mass of ammonia that could form, then the percentage yield if 17 kg is made. (*A*ᵣ: N = 14, H = 1) **[3]**

**12.** Plan an experiment to find how the concentration of sodium thiosulfate solution affects its rate of reaction with dilute hydrochloric acid, using the time for a cross under the flask to disappear. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** *Q* = *mc*Δ*T* = 100 × 4.2 × 25.0 = **10 500 J** [1].
**(b)** Moles of ethanol = 0.46 ÷ 46 = 0.0100 mol [1]; Δ*H* = −10.5 kJ ÷ 0.0100 mol = **−1050 kJ/mol** [1].

*Examiner insight:* use the mass of the water heated, not the mass of fuel, and keep the negative sign, because the final mark is lost without it.

**2.** A catalyst provides an **alternative pathway** (route) for the reaction [1] with a **lower activation energy**, so more collisions have enough energy to react [1].

*Examiner insight:* saying only that a catalyst "speeds up the reaction" repeats the question; the marks are for the new pathway and the lower activation energy.

**3.** Powder has a **larger surface area** (to volume ratio), so more particles of calcium carbonate are exposed to the acid [1], giving **more frequent collisions** between acid particles and calcium carbonate particles [1].

*Examiner insight:* "more collisions" needs a reason (more surface exposed) and "frequent" or "per second", since the same number of collisions happens eventually.

**4. (a)** The forward and backward reactions happen at the **same rate** [1], so the **concentrations** of reactants and products stay constant (in a closed system) [1].
**(b)** The yield **decreases** [1], because the equilibrium shifts in the **endothermic** (backward) direction to oppose the rise in temperature [1].

*Examiner insight:* "the reaction has stopped" loses the first part; both reactions continue at equal rates.

**5.** Bonds broken: H–H + Cl–Cl = 436 + 242 = 678 kJ/mol [1]. Bonds made: 2 × H–Cl = 862 kJ/mol [1]. Δ*H* = 678 − 862 = **−184 kJ/mol** [1].

*Examiner insight:* count every bond made (2 × H–Cl); forgetting the 2 gives +247 and loses marks, and reversing the subtraction gives the wrong sign.

**6. (a)** Mass of solution = 50 + 50 = 100 g [1]; *Q* = 100 × 4.2 × 6.8 = **2856 J** [1].
**(b)** Moles of HCl = 0.050 dm³ × 1.0 = 0.050 mol [1]; Δ*H* = −2.856 kJ ÷ 0.050 mol = −57.12, so **−57 kJ/mol** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the mass is that of both solutions added together, and the moles come from one reagent only, not the total volume.

**7.** Reactants drawn at a higher energy than products [1]; a curve rising above the reactants to a peak, with activation energy labelled as the arrow from reactants to the peak [1]; Δ*H* labelled as the arrow from reactants down to products [1]; with a catalyst the peak is lower, but the reactants and products stay at the same levels, so Δ*H* is unchanged [1].

*Examiner insight:* the activation energy arrow starts at the reactants, not at the bottom of the axis, and the catalyst never moves the product level.

**8. (a)** Rate = 30 ÷ 20 [1] = **1.5 cm³/s** [1].
**(b)** By **80 s**, because the volume has reached 60 cm³ and stays constant (the same at 80 s and 100 s) [1].
**(c)** The concentration of the acid has fallen [1], so there are fewer collisions per second between acid and magnesium particles [1].

*Examiner insight:* a rate needs a unit; cm³/s is not optional, and "the acid is used up" is too strong for a rate that is merely slowing.

**9.** The particles move faster, so they collide more often [1]. A greater proportion of the particles have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy [1], so a greater proportion of collisions are successful [1].

*Examiner insight:* the second and third points carry the most weight; "more collisions" alone earns one mark at best.

**10. (a)** White to **blue** [1].
**(b)** The test tube feels **hot** (or the temperature rises) [1].
**(c)** The forward reaction is **endothermic**, because heat must be supplied for it to happen [1].

*Examiner insight:* name the direction when linking energy and reversibility: if the reverse is exothermic, the forward reaction is endothermic by the same amount.

**11. (a)** The percentage of ammonia **increases** as the pressure increases [1]. The forward reaction forms 2 mol of gas from 4 mol of gas [1], so higher pressure shifts the equilibrium towards the side with fewer gas molecules [1].
**(b)** Very high pressure needs strong, expensive equipment and more energy to compress the gas, and it is a safety risk [1].
**(c)** The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature would give a higher yield [1], but the rate would be too slow [1]; 450 °C (with an iron catalyst) is a compromise that gives an acceptable yield at a fast enough rate [1].
**(d)** Moles of N₂ = 56 000 ÷ 28 = 2000 mol, so 4000 mol of NH₃ [1]; mass = 4000 × 17 = 68 000 g = **68 kg** [1]; percentage yield = 17 ÷ 68 × 100 = **25%** [1].

*Examiner insight:* in the "compromise" answer, give both sides (yield falls, rate rises) and not just one; and remember the 2 : 1 mole ratio before multiplying by 17.

**12.** This is a plan, so the real exam would mark it by quality of method. Indicative points:
Use a fixed total volume of liquid, mixing different volumes of thiosulfate solution with water, so only the concentration changes [1]. Keep the temperature and the volume and concentration of the acid the same each time [1]. Place the flask on a cross drawn on paper [1]. Add the acid, start a stopwatch and stop it when the cross can no longer be seen from above [1]. Use the same observer and the same flask each time [1]. Work out rate as 1 ÷ time and plot it against concentration, repeating the experiment to check reliability [1].

*Examiner insight:* the independent variable is changed by diluting with water to a constant total volume; changing the volume of solution as well would mean two variables change together.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mass of fuel instead of the mass of water in *Q* = *mc*Δ*T*.
- Leaving out the negative sign for an exothermic enthalpy change.
- Writing kJ when the calculation gave J, or dividing by the wrong number of moles.
- Saying a catalyst "gives particles more energy" instead of lowering the activation energy.
- Describing equilibrium as "the reaction has stopped".
- Explaining a rate change with "more collisions" and not "more collisions per second".
- Leaving the units off a rate, or giving the wrong ones.
- Not linking the equilibrium shift to the number of gas moles or to the energy change.
