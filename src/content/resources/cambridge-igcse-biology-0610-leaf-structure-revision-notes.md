These notes condense sub-topic **6.2 Leaf structure** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All of 6.2 is Core. Learn it with the [leaf structure study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-leaf-structure/), then test yourself with the [leaf structure practice questions](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-leaf-structure-subtopic-practice/). The whole topic is summarised in the [plant nutrition revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-plant-nutrition-revision-notes/).

## The three statements

1. Most leaves have a **large surface area** and are **thin**: explain how these are adaptations for photosynthesis.
2. **Identify** in diagrams and images: chloroplasts, cuticle, guard cells and stomata, upper and lower epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, air spaces, vascular bundles, xylem and phloem.
3. **Explain** how each of those structures adapts the leaf for photosynthesis.

## Leaf shape in two lines

- Large surface area → more light absorbed and more surface for carbon dioxide to enter.
- Thin → short diffusion distance for carbon dioxide to the mesophyll; light reaches the lower cells.

## Layer order, top to bottom

```
cuticle
upper epidermis
palisade mesophyll
spongy mesophyll + air spaces   (vascular bundle sits in this region, in veins and midrib)
lower epidermis with guard cells and stomata
(cuticle on the lower surface too, usually thinner)
```

Inside a vascular bundle: **xylem above, phloem below**.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Cuticle | Waxy, transparent, non-cellular layer on the epidermis |
| Epidermis | Single layer of cells on each surface; no chloroplasts except in guard cells |
| Palisade mesophyll | Tall, closely packed cells under the upper epidermis, with the most chloroplasts |
| Spongy mesophyll | Loosely arranged, rounded cells with fewer chloroplasts |
| Air spaces | Interconnecting gaps between mesophyll cells, linked to the stomata |
| Stoma (plural stomata) | Pore in the epidermis, mostly in the lower surface |
| Guard cells | Pair of cells around each stoma that open and close it; they contain chloroplasts |
| Vascular bundle | Group of xylem and phloem in a vein or the midrib |
| Xylem | Brings water and mineral ions into the leaf |
| Phloem | Carries sucrose (and amino acids) out of the leaf |
| Chloroplast | Organelle containing chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis |

## Recognition clues for images

- Upright, tightly packed green cells = palisade, and that side is the **top**.
- Rounded cells with white gaps = spongy mesophyll with air spaces.
- Thin clear line with no cells = cuticle.
- Wide, thick-walled, empty-looking cells in a vein = xylem; smaller cells below them = phloem.
- Curved pair of cells around a gap = guard cells and stoma.
- In a surface peel: the only epidermal cells with chloroplasts are guard cells.

## Structure, feature, reason

| Structure | Feature | Reason (photosynthesis link) |
|---|---|---|
| Cuticle | waxy, transparent | light passes through; less water lost |
| Upper epidermis | transparent, no chloroplasts | light reaches palisade cells |
| Palisade | many chloroplasts, near top, packed upright | absorbs most light |
| Spongy | loose, with air spaces | gases reach many cells |
| Air spaces | interconnecting | large internal surface for carbon dioxide to diffuse in |
| Stomata | pores opened and closed by guard cells | carbon dioxide in, oxygen out |
| Xylem | in veins | supplies water, a raw material |
| Phloem | in veins | removes sucrose to other parts of the plant |
| Chloroplasts | contain chlorophyll | absorb light; energy transferred to make carbohydrate |

One mark = one complete structure–feature–reason link.

## Routes through the leaf

- **Light:** cuticle → upper epidermis → palisade → spongy.
- **Carbon dioxide:** stoma → air spaces → surface of mesophyll cell → chloroplast (by diffusion, down a concentration gradient).
- **Oxygen:** chloroplast → air spaces → stoma → air.
- **Water:** xylem → mesophyll cells.
- **Sucrose:** mesophyll cells → phloem → rest of plant.

## Exam traps

- The stoma is the pore; guard cells are the cells. Do not swap them.
- The cuticle is not made of cells.
- Air spaces belong mainly to the spongy layer, not the palisade layer.
- Stomata let gases diffuse through; they do not "absorb" anything.
- Upper epidermis answers must mention light passing through, not just "protection".
- "Thin for efficiency" scores nothing; say "short diffusion distance".
- Use exact names: "palisade mesophyll", "vascular bundle", "lower epidermis".

## Self-test

1. State two features of leaf shape that adapt most leaves for photosynthesis.
2. Explain why being thin helps a leaf photosynthesise.
3. Name the layer directly below the upper epidermis.
4. Which cells of the lower epidermis contain chloroplasts?
5. What is the difference between a stoma and a guard cell?
6. In a vascular bundle in a leaf, which tissue is nearer the upper surface?
7. Explain why the upper epidermis has no chloroplasts.
8. Give the route of carbon dioxide from the air to a palisade cell.
9. Name the structure that supplies the leaf with one raw material for photosynthesis, and name the raw material.
10. A section is shown with the tightly packed upright cells at the bottom of the image. What does this tell you?
11. Explain how the air spaces adapt the leaf for photosynthesis.
12. Why does the cuticle need to be transparent?

### Answers

1. Large surface area; thin.
2. Carbon dioxide has a short distance to diffuse to the mesophyll cells, and light can reach all the cell layers.
3. Palisade mesophyll.
4. Guard cells.
5. A stoma is the pore; guard cells are the pair of cells around it that open and close it.
6. Xylem.
7. So light passes straight through to the palisade mesophyll cells below.
8. Stoma → air spaces → surface of spongy and palisade mesophyll cells → into the cell by diffusion.
9. Xylem (in the vascular bundle) supplies water.
10. The section is upside down: the palisade side is the upper surface.
11. They interconnect and give a large internal surface area, so carbon dioxide can diffuse quickly to many mesophyll cells.
12. So that light can pass through it to reach the chloroplasts in the mesophyll.
