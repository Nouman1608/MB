These notes condense data analysis and interpretation for the IB Psychology course (first assessment 2027). The strand is studied at SL and HL, but it is **not assessed at SL**. At HL it is tested in Paper 3 (four source-based questions, 30 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes). Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-hl-extension-data-analysis-and-interpretation/) for worked examples and then use the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-hl-extension-data-analysis-and-interpretation-practice/).

## Terms printed in the guide

- Bar graph, box and whisker plot, frequency table, histogram, line graph, scatterplot
- Distributions (normal, skewness), outliers
- Descriptive statistics, inferential statistics, correlation coefficient
- Thematic analysis

## Learning objectives

1. Data are represented and analysed in different forms depending on the design and the nature of the data.
2. Analyse and interpret tables, graphs and results.
3. Analyse and interpret descriptive and inferential statistics.
4. Understand the stages of thematic analysis.

Also expected: the research methodology terms (reliability, validity, generalisability, bias, reflexivity, transferability, credibility) are used to argue about research.

## Choosing a display

| Data | Display |
|---|---|
| Counts of categories or group means | Bar graph |
| One continuous variable's shape | Histogram |
| Change over time | Line graph |
| Two variables, one per participant | Scatterplot |
| Median, quartiles, spread, outliers | Box and whisker plot |
| Exact counts | Frequency table |

## Descriptive statistics

| Measure | How | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | Sum divided by n | Uses all values | Pulled by outliers |
| Median | Middle value | Resistant to outliers | Ignores most values |
| Mode | Most frequent | Works for categories | May be several or none |
| Range | Highest minus lowest | Simple | Based on two values |
| Standard deviation | Typical distance from the mean | Uses all values | Needs interpretation |

**Quick example (constructed).** Hours of sleep: 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 9, 12. The sum is 67 for 9 values, so the mean is 7.4 (to one decimal place). The median is 7, the mode is 7 and the range is 7. Remove 12 and the mean falls to 6.9, the median stays 7 and the range falls to 4: the median is resistant to the outlier.

## Distributions

- **Normal:** symmetrical bell; mean, median and mode close.
- **Positive skew:** tail to the right; mean above median.
- **Negative skew:** tail to the left; mean below median.
- **Outlier:** very different value. Investigate, do not delete automatically.
- Skewed data: report the median.

## Reading a box plot

- Box = middle 50 per cent (lower quartile to upper quartile); line inside = median.
- Whiskers = the lowest and highest values that are not outliers.
- Points beyond the whiskers = outliers.
- Compare medians first, then the size of the boxes (spread), then overlap.

## Correlation coefficient

| r | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| +1 | Perfect positive |
| about +0.6 to +0.9 | Strong positive |
| about +0.3 to +0.6 | Moderate positive |
| near 0 | Little or no linear relationship |
| negative values | Same strengths, opposite direction |
| -1 | Perfect negative |

- r squared = shared variation. r = -0.30 gives 0.09, that is 9 per cent.
- r says nothing about cause. Think bidirectional ambiguity and third variables.
- Check the scatterplot for outliers and curved patterns.

**Quick example (constructed).** Eight students: hours online 2 to 9 and well-being 55 to 40. r = -0.94, so r squared = 0.88. Strong negative association, but only eight students, and no cause.

## Inferential statistics

- Null hypothesis: no difference or relationship in the population.
- **p-value:** probability of a result this extreme if the null is true. p < 0.05 is the usual cut-off for statistical significance.
- **Type I error:** reject a true null (false positive). **Type II error:** keep a false null (false negative).
- Significant is not the same as important: check the effect size, sample size and design.
- Small samples may miss real effects (type II); many tests raise the chance of a type I error.

## Thematic analysis (six stages)

1. Familiarise with the data.
2. Code relevant segments.
3. Search for themes.
4. Review themes.
5. Define and name themes.
6. Report with extracts.

Credibility: reflexivity, triangulation, second coder, member checking, audit trail. Bias: researcher expectations, confirmation bias. Transferability: describe context in detail.

## The Paper 3 routine

| Question | Skill | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain one issue that limits interpretation of a source | 3 |
| 2 | Analyse findings from one source and state a conclusion | 6 |
| 3 | Research consideration: credibility, bias or transferability | 6 |
| 4 | Synthesis of at least three sources and discuss the validity of a claim | 15 |

Routine: read the source; describe with numbers; compare including spread; conclude no further than the design allows; evaluate one limitation tied to the source; link to the extension and concepts.

## Common mistakes

- Not checking axes.
- Ignoring spread.
- Saying "significant" when meaning "large".
- Using correlation as cause.
- Treating thematic analysis as word counting.

## Self-test

1. Which measure of central tendency is least affected by an outlier?
2. What does a negative skew look like?
3. A study reports r = +0.20. How much shared variation is this?
4. What does p = 0.03 mean in words?
5. Define a type II error.
6. List the first two stages of thematic analysis.
7. Name two ways to improve the credibility of a thematic analysis.
8. At SL, is data analysis and interpretation assessed?

**Answers:** 1. The median. 2. A long tail to the left, with the mean below the median. 3. 0.20 squared is 0.04, so 4 per cent. 4. If the null hypothesis were true, a result this extreme would occur about 3 per cent of the time, which is below the usual 5 per cent threshold. 5. Failing to reject a null hypothesis that is false. 6. Familiarisation with the data, then coding. 7. For example reflexivity and a second coder (also triangulation or member checking). 8. No. It is studied at SL but not assessed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to calculate statistics by hand in Paper 3?**
The guide emphasises analysing and interpreting data and results. Be ready to read values and statistics and to explain them. Practise simple calculations (mean, median, range) so that you can check a source.

**Does the guide name a statistical test?**
No. It lists descriptive statistics, inferential statistics and the correlation coefficient. Learn what the results mean, whichever test was used.

**What about qualitative data?**
Know thematic analysis, and how credibility, bias and transferability are judged for qualitative research.
