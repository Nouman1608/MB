**Consumer protection** means the rules, organisations and practices that protect buyers from unsafe goods, unfair treatment and dishonest selling. Section 6.3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715) syllabus has two statements: the **reasons for consumer protection**, and the **actions taken by buyers and sellers to resolve consumer protection complaints**. This guide covers both, with two worked examples and an annotated answer. Because laws differ between countries, the guide describes general principles and does not rely on one country's rules.

## Why do consumers need protection?

A consumer is at a disadvantage in many sales. The seller usually knows more about the product, and the consumer cannot always check quality before buying. Protection is needed for several reasons.

| Reason | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety** | Goods and services should not harm people | A toy with small parts that a child can swallow |
| **Quality and fitness** | Goods should work properly and last a reasonable time | A phone that fails in a week |
| **Honest information** | Descriptions, labels and prices must be true and clear | Food labelled "sugar free" that contains sugar |
| **Fair prices and practices** | Consumers should not be tricked or pressured | Hidden fees; fake "closing down" sales |
| **Unequal power** | Large firms are far stronger than one buyer | A single customer cannot easily challenge a big retailer |
| **Vulnerable groups** | Children, older people and people with little information are easily misled | A high-pressure salesperson at the door |
| **Spread of online and distance selling** | Buyers cannot see or test goods | An item that looks different from its photograph |
| **Fair competition** | Honest sellers should not lose out to dishonest ones | A rival making false claims about its product |

Protection also benefits **sellers**: customers who trust the market buy more, and honest firms are not undercut by cheats.

## What kinds of complaints arise?

- The product is **faulty**, damaged or unsafe.
- It is **not as described** in the advert, label or website.
- It **arrives late** or not at all.
- The price charged is **different** from the price advertised.
- A **service** is poorly done.
- The seller refuses a **refund** or **repair** that was promised.

## What actions can buyers take to resolve a complaint?

A sensible order is shown below.

1. **Keep evidence.** Keep receipts, order numbers, packaging, photographs and messages.
2. **Contact the seller.** Explain the problem politely and say what you want: a refund, a repair or a replacement.
3. **Complain in writing.** A letter or email gives a record and often a reply deadline.
4. **Give the seller time to put it right**, but state a reasonable deadline.
5. **Contact a consumer organisation or government agency.** These give advice and may take up the case.
6. **Use a payment protection route.** A credit card company or bank may refund a payment if goods are not delivered or are not as described.
7. **Use mediation or an ombudsman.** A neutral person helps both sides to agree.
8. **Take legal action**, such as a small claims court, as a last step.
9. **Share the experience** in an honest review, where this is allowed, to warn other buyers.

Buyers can also **avoid problems** by checking reviews, reading labels, comparing prices and keeping proof of purchase.

## What actions can sellers take to resolve a complaint?

| Action | What it involves |
|---|---|
| **Listen and apologise** | Treat the customer politely; take the complaint seriously |
| **Investigate** | Check the facts, such as the fault, the delivery record or the advert |
| **Offer a remedy** | Refund, repair, replacement, credit note or compensation |
| **Act quickly** | A fast reply reduces anger and cost |
| **Have a clear complaints policy** | Staff know what to do; customers know how to claim |
| **Recall unsafe goods** | Take back products that could harm people |
| **Correct the cause** | Fix a fault in the product, a wrong description or a poor process |
| **Train staff** | Staff handle complaints calmly and fairly |
| **Co-operate with agencies** | Work with an ombudsman, mediator or regulator |

A seller who handles complaints well can **keep the customer**, avoid legal action and protect its reputation.

### Comparing remedies

| Remedy | Good for | Cost to seller |
|---|---|---|
| Refund | Faulty item the buyer no longer wants | Lost sale plus returned item |
| Repair | Item that can be fixed easily | Repair cost; customer keeps item |
| Replacement | Customer still wants the product | Cost of a new item |
| Compensation | Loss or injury caused by the product | Can be large |
| Credit note | Customer will buy again from the seller | Seller keeps the money inside the business |

## Worked example 1: which remedy costs the seller least? (invented figures)

*A customer returns an $85 blender that stopped working after five months. The seller can refund $85, repair it for $22, or replace it with a new blender that costs the seller $52 to buy.*

1. Refund: lose the sale value of **$85** (and the blender returned may have little resale value).
2. Repair: cost is **$22**.
3. Replacement: cost is **$52**.
4. Cheapest for the seller: repair, saving 52 - 22 = **$30** compared with replacement.

**Judgement:** repair costs least and keeps the customer. If the blender is repeatedly faulty, a replacement or refund is fairer, and the seller should also ask the supplier why the fault occurred.

## Worked example 2: the cost of a recall (invented figures)

*A company sells 1,200 children's lamps and finds that the wiring is unsafe. A recall costs $14 per lamp for collection and handling, and a safe replacement costs $30 per lamp.*

1. Cost per lamp: 14 + 30 = **$44**.
2. Total cost: 1,200 x 44 = **$52,800**.
3. If the company did not recall and three children were injured, with compensation of $20,000 each, the cost would be 3 x 20,000 = **$60,000**, plus damage to its reputation and possible legal action.

**Judgement:** the recall costs less than the likely compensation, and it protects children and the company's name. Safety makes the recall the right decision, whatever the figures.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain two actions a buyer could take if an online shop delivers a damaged television.* **[4]**

> The buyer should **contact the seller in writing** [1], giving the order number and photographs of the damage and asking for a replacement or refund, which creates a record and gives the shop a chance to put things right [1]. If the shop refuses, the buyer could **ask the bank or card company for a refund** [1], because payments for goods that are not as described can often be reversed, so the buyer does not lose the money [1].

Why it scores: each action is specific to the case and followed by the reason it helps.

## What are the common mistakes in 6.3?

- Saying consumers need protection "because sellers are bad". Give a reason such as safety, honest information or unequal power.
- Listing buyer actions with no order or purpose. Start with evidence and contact with the seller; legal action comes last.
- Forgetting **sellers' actions**, such as recalls, complaint policies and training.
- Ignoring the fact that protection also benefits honest sellers.
- Quoting one country's laws as if they apply everywhere.
- Writing "complain" without saying to whom, how, and what outcome is sought.

## Exam technique

- **State** a reason: one clear point, such as safety.
- **Explain** a reason: link it to a risk to the consumer in the case.
- **Describe** an action: say who acts, what they do and what it achieves.
- **Evaluate** a response: compare cost, speed and fairness, then decide.
- In calculations, show the cost of each option and compare.
