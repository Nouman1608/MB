This guide covers **Exercise 2 of Paper 2 Listening**, the gap-fill sentences, in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu
as a Second Language (0539). The site's
[Paper 2 Listening guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening/) sets out all four
listening exercises. This page concentrates on the second one: how the printed sentences relate to the
recording, how Urdu grammar lets you predict what goes in each gap, and how to use the two hearings of a
longer text.

## What the syllabus says

- Candidates hear **a longer spoken text**; the syllabus's examples are a **conversation, interview,
  monologue or formal talk**.
- They **complete gaps in notes or sentences printed in the question paper**.
- The exercise is worth **8 marks** and assesses **L1**, identify and select relevant information, and
  **L2**, understand ideas, opinions and attitudes.
- The text is heard **twice**, with pauses to read the questions and write. Everything is in Urdu, and
  dictionaries are not allowed.

Exercise 2 tests the same objectives as Exercise 1 but in a different shape. Instead of several short
texts with a question each, there is one longer text, and the questions are a set of incomplete
statements that run through it in order.

## What the printed page looks like

The gaps are set in either **notes** (short headings with a gap after each) or **sentences** (full
statements with a word or phrase missing). Both follow the order of the recording, so if you lose your
place you can find it again by listening for the topic of the next sentence.

The gap is usually a key piece of content, such as a number, a time, a day, a place, a name, an object
or a short reason. It is normally one to three words. The words *around* the gap, however, are often not
the speaker's words. The question paper rewords what was said, so you cannot wait for the exact printed
sentence to be read out.

```
Heard:    ہم نے یہ کلب دو سال پہلے شروع کیا تھا۔
Printed:  کلب ______ پہلے شروع ہوا۔
Answer:   دو سال
```

The printed sentence changes the verb from active (شروع کیا تھا) to intransitive (شروع ہوا), but the
missing content, دو سال, is what the speaker said.

## Using Urdu grammar to predict the gap

Urdu is very helpful here. Gender, number and postpositions in the printed sentence often tell you what
kind of word is missing, before you hear anything.

| Clue in the printed sentence | What it tells you | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a feminine verb: ہوتی ہے، جاتی ہیں | the missing subject is feminine; ہیں means plural | میلے میں ______ بیچی جاتی ہیں۔ → a feminine plural noun, such as چوڑیاں |
| a masculine plural verb: خریدے جاتے ہیں | a masculine plural noun | نئے ______ خریدے جاتے ہیں۔ → بیج، پودے |
| ہر ______ کو | a day or time unit | ہر ______ کو ملاقات ہوتی ہے۔ → منگل |
| کی تعداد ______ ہے | a number | ارکان کی تعداد ______ ہے۔ → پچیس |
| ______ کی وجہ سے | a reason, a noun | ______ کی وجہ سے میچ ملتوی ہوا۔ → بارش |
| کے ... پر / کے ... میں around the gap | a noun in the oblique form | شادی کے ______ پر → دعوت ناموں |
| ایک مشہور ______ ... سنائیں گی | a feminine person | ایک مشہور ______ نظمیں سنائیں گی۔ → شاعرہ |

This prediction does two jobs. It tells you what to listen for, and afterwards it lets you **check** that
your answer fits. If the verb is feminine plural and you have written a masculine singular noun, you have
probably caught the wrong word.

  <figcaption>Gender, number and postpositions tell you what kind of word is missing.</figcaption>
</figure>

## Notes versus sentences

In **notes**, the gap often follows a heading and a colon: کتابوں کی تعداد: ______. Write just the
content: a number, a noun or a short list. In **sentences**, your answer has to read as part of the
sentence, so use the form that fits: the oblique plural after a postposition, or the feminine form when
the sentence needs it. Do not rewrite the whole sentence; write only what goes in the gap.

## When the text gives two possible answers

Longer texts deliberately mention more than one number, time or item close together. Typical patterns:

- **Then and now:** شروع میں چھ طالب علم تھے، اب ہم پچیس ہیں۔ A gap about "now" needs پچیس.
- **A list and an exception:** ہم سبزیاں اگاتے ہیں ... سردیوں میں گاجریں بھی۔ A gap about winter needs
  گاجریں.
- **A reason and a result:** the gap after کی وجہ سے wants the cause, not the effect.

The words in the printed sentence (اب، شروع میں، سردیوں میں، اصل میں) tell you which of the two details is
being asked for. Underline them in the pause.

## A routine for the two hearings

```
In the pause:   read every gap; underline time words (اب / پہلے / شروع میں)
                and grammar clues (verb gender, postpositions);
                pencil the KIND of answer beside each gap: number? day? fem. pl. noun?
First hearing:  follow the sentences in order; jot what you catch lightly;
                if you miss one, move on: the next topic will come
Second hearing: fill the gaps you missed; confirm then-and-now details
After:          read each completed sentence: does the grammar fit?
```

## Worked example

The extract below is original, written for this guide. Have it read to you twice before reading the
analysis.

> "میں نے خطاطی بارہ سال کی عمر میں اپنے دادا سے سیکھی۔ شروع میں روزانہ صرف آدھا گھنٹہ مشق کرتا
> تھا، اب تقریباً دو گھنٹے کرتا ہوں۔ میرا پسندیدہ کام شادی کے دعوت ناموں پر نام لکھنا ہے، کیونکہ اس
> سے لوگوں کی خوشی میں شریک ہونے کا احساس ہوتا ہے۔"

**(a)** اس نے خطاطی اپنے ______ سے سیکھی۔ **[1]**

- *Prediction:* after اپنے and before سے, a person, probably a relative.
- *Answer:* **دادا**. "بارہ سال کی عمر میں" is also in that sentence, but it does not fit a gap followed by
  سے سیکھی.

**(b)** آج کل وہ روزانہ تقریباً ______ مشق کرتا ہے۔ **[1]**

- *Prediction:* an amount of time. آج کل signals "now".
- *Answer:* **دو گھنٹے**. آدھا گھنٹہ is the trap: it is what he did شروع میں.

**(c)** اسے شادی کے ______ پر نام لکھنا سب سے زیادہ پسند ہے۔ **[1]**

- *Prediction:* کے before and پر after: a noun in the oblique form.
- *Answer:* **دعوت ناموں** (accept دعوت نامے if the meaning is clear). Notice that the printed sentence
  has reworded "میرا پسندیدہ کام" as "سب سے زیادہ پسند ہے". The gap content did not change.

The reason he gives (لوگوں کی خوشی میں شریک ہونے کا احساس) is an L2 detail, an attitude, and could
equally be tested with a gap: ______ میں شریک ہونے کا احساس. That is why the exercise covers L2 as well
as L1: some gaps are about how a speaker feels or why they think something, not only about facts.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for the printed words.** The paper rewords the speaker, so a candidate who listens for the
  exact sentence misses the answer.
- **Taking the "then" detail when the gap asks for "now"**, or the reverse.
- **Writing too much**, such as the whole clause from the recording, which may include a wrong detail and
  cost the mark.
- **Ignoring the grammar clue**, for instance putting a masculine singular noun into a gap before جاتی
  ہیں.
- **Leaving a gap empty.** If you caught part of it, such as the number but not the unit, write what you
  have.
- **Losing your place** and abandoning the rest of the exercise. The gaps run in order, so listen for the
  next sentence's topic and rejoin there.

## Exam technique summary

Use the pause to predict each gap from the Urdu grammar around it and to underline time words. Follow the
order of the text on the first hearing; fill and confirm on the second. Write only the gap, in a form
that fits the sentence, and check it against the grammar clue before moving on. For an overview of the
whole paper, see the
[Paper 2 Listening revision notes](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening-revision-notes/) and
the full [Paper 2 listening practice set](/resources/urdu-paper-2-listening-practice/).
