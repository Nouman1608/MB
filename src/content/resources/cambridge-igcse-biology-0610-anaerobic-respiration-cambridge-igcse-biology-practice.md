## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is the product of anaerobic respiration in human muscles?

A: alcohol

B: carbon dioxide

C: lactic acid

D: water

**[1]**

**2.** Describe anaerobic respiration. **[2]**

**3.** State how the energy released by anaerobic respiration compares with aerobic respiration. **[1]**

**4.** State the word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the balanced chemical equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State what builds up in the muscles and blood during vigorous exercise, and what this causes. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student investigates anaerobic respiration in yeast. She boils a glucose solution, cools it to 35 °C, adds yeast and covers the surface with a layer of oil. A delivery tube leads from the tube into limewater.

**(a)** Explain why the glucose solution is boiled. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the solution is cooled before the yeast is added. **[1]**

**(c)** State the purpose of the oil layer. **[1]**

**(d)** Describe and explain what happens to the limewater. **[2]**

**8.** Compare anaerobic respiration in yeast with anaerobic respiration in human muscles. Give **two** similarities and **two** differences. **[4]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Use the balanced equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast.

**(a)** Calculate the number of carbon dioxide molecules released from 18 glucose molecules. **[1]**

**(b)** A yeast culture produces 40 molecules of ethanol. Calculate the number of glucose molecules used. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate how many more carbon dioxide molecules would be released if the 18 glucose molecules in (a) were respired aerobically instead. **[2]**

**10.** A sprinter runs 200 m as fast as possible.

**(a)** Explain why her leg muscles respire anaerobically during the race. **[2]**

**(b)** State the word equation for this anaerobic respiration. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why anaerobic respiration cannot supply her energy for a long time. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* The table shows the concentration of lactic acid in a cyclist's blood and her breathing rate during recovery from a hard sprint.

| Time after sprint / minutes | 0 | 5 | 15 | 30 | 45 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lactic acid / arbitrary units | 10 | 8 | 5 | 2 | 1 |
| Breathing rate / breaths per minute | 42 | 30 | 22 | 16 | 15 |

**(a)** Describe how the lactic acid concentration changes after the sprint. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the breathing rate stays above its resting value of 15 breaths per minute for some time after the sprint. **[2]**

**(c)** Outline the role of the heart and the liver in removing the oxygen debt. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "During exercise the muscles stop respiring aerobically and switch to anaerobic respiration. The lactic acid is then breathed out as carbon dioxide."

Identify **two** errors in this statement and correct each one. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** Chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy [1] without using oxygen [1].

**3.** It releases much less energy per glucose molecule [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Less energy" without "much" and "per glucose molecule" may not be accepted; "no energy" is wrong.

**4.** Glucose → [1] alcohol + carbon dioxide [1].

**5.** Correct formulae: C₆H₁₂O₆, C₂H₅OH, CO₂ [1]; correct balancing: 1, 2, 2 [1]. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.

**6.** Lactic acid [1]; an oxygen debt [1].

**7. (a)** To remove dissolved oxygen, so respiration must be anaerobic [1].

**(b)** So that the high temperature does not denature the yeast's enzymes / kill the yeast [1].

**(c)** To stop oxygen from the air dissolving back into the solution [1].

**(d)** The limewater turns cloudy / milky [1], because carbon dioxide is released by anaerobic respiration of the yeast [1].

**8.** Similarities, any two: both break down glucose; both release energy; both happen without using oxygen; both release much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration [1] [1]. Differences, any two: yeast produces alcohol, muscles produce lactic acid; yeast produces carbon dioxide, muscles do not; muscles respire anaerobically only during vigorous exercise [1] [1].

*Examiner insight:* A difference must say what each organism does; "yeast makes alcohol" alone is half a comparison.

**9. (a)** 18 × 2 = **36** [1].

**(b)** 40 ÷ 2 = **20** [1].

**(c)** Aerobic: 18 × 6 = 108 [1]; 108 − 36 = **72** more [1].

**10. (a)** During vigorous exercise oxygen cannot be supplied to the muscles fast enough [1], so aerobic respiration alone cannot release all the energy needed and the muscles also respire anaerobically [1].

**(b)** Glucose → lactic acid [1].

**(c)** Lactic acid builds up in the muscles / it releases much less energy per glucose molecule [1].

**11. (a)** Lactic acid concentration falls from 10 to 1 arbitrary units over 45 minutes [1]; it falls quickly at first and then more slowly [1].

**(b)** Deeper and faster breathing supplies extra oxygen [1] for the aerobic respiration of lactic acid (in the liver), repaying the oxygen debt [1].

**(c)** A fast heart rate continues, transporting lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver [1]; in the liver, the lactic acid is respired aerobically [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each organ needs its own job. "The heart and liver remove lactic acid" scores nothing because it does not say how.

**12.** Error 1: muscles do not stop aerobic respiration [1]; aerobic respiration continues and anaerobic respiration is added when oxygen cannot be supplied fast enough [1]. Error 2: lactic acid is not breathed out [1]; it is carried in the blood to the liver and respired aerobically there [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the yeast and muscle equations.
- Writing "no energy" for anaerobic respiration.
- (Extended) Writing 6CO₂ or adding oxygen to the yeast balanced equation.
- (Extended) Oxygen-debt answers that leave out the liver.
- Explaining the oil layer as "keeping the yeast warm" instead of keeping oxygen out.
