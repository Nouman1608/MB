## Section A

**1.** Define the term *job enrichment*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** of Herzberg's hygiene factors. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** difference between Maslow's theory and McClelland's theory. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why motivation is important to a business that wants to reduce its unit costs. **[3]**

**5.** A warehouse operative is paid $14.50 an hour for a 40-hour week plus a fringe benefit of a staff discount worth $25 a week. Calculate the total weekly value of the operative's pay and benefits. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how a temporary contract may fail to satisfy an employee's human needs. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Brenwick Kitchens pays fitters a salary of $34 000 a year. It is considering paying them a bonus of $150 for each kitchen fitted with no customer complaint. A fitter completes 110 kitchens a year, and 90% are complaint-free.

**(a)** Calculate the fitter's total annual pay under the new scheme.

**(b)** Using Vroom's theory, explain **one** reason why the bonus might not raise effort. **[5]**

**8.** Analyse how Mayo's ideas could be applied to improve motivation in a supermarket. **[4]**

**9.** Selcombe Software's programmers are well paid but labour turnover is high. Using Herzberg's theory, analyse why this might be and what the business could do. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **two** ways in which employees at a manufacturing company could participate in the management and control of the business. **[4]**

**11.** Rowan Garage pays mechanics a time rate. The owner wants to switch to a piece rate per car serviced. Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of this change for the garage. **[4]**

**12.** Fairholm Hotels has high absence among its housekeeping staff, who are paid an hourly rate close to the legal minimum. The manager plans to introduce team working and empowerment. Evaluate whether non-financial methods are the best way to improve the motivation of these staff. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Giving employees more responsibility and decision-making in their job [1], so the work itself becomes more challenging and satisfying [1].

**2.** Any two: pay [1]; working conditions [1]; job security; supervision; company policy.

**3.** Maslow suggests everyone has the same levels of need, met in order [1], whereas McClelland suggests individuals differ in the strength of their needs for achievement, affiliation and power [1].

**4.** Motivated staff tend to work harder and more carefully [1], so output per worker rises and fewer units are faulty [1]. Labour cost is spread over more units, so unit costs fall [1].

**5.** Pay = 14.50 × 40 = $580 [1]; total = 580 + 25 = $605 a week [1].

**6.** A temporary contract gives no job security [1], so the employee's safety need (Maslow) is unmet and they may worry about income rather than focus on their work [1].

**7.** **(a)** Complaint-free kitchens = 90% × 110 = 99 [1]. Bonus = 99 × 150 = $14 850 [1]. Total = 34 000 + 14 850 = $48 850 [1].

**(b)** Any one, explained. Low instrumentality: complaints may come from faults the fitter cannot control, such as damaged units [1], so fitters may doubt that good work will earn the bonus, reducing effort [1]. Allow low valence if the fitters already earn enough that $150 is not valued.

**8.** Mayo found that attention and belonging raise output [1]. The supermarket could organise staff into small teams for each section with shared goals [1], and managers could hold regular meetings where staff are listened to [1]. Staff who feel part of a group and valued may work harder and stay longer [1].

**9.** Pay is a hygiene factor, so good pay prevents dissatisfaction but does not create lasting motivation [1]. Programmers may leave because they lack motivators such as recognition, responsibility or interesting work [1]. For example, if they only fix minor faults, the work itself is not satisfying [1]. Selcombe could enrich jobs by giving programmers ownership of whole projects [1], recognise achievements publicly [1], and offer training or a clear promotion route [1].

**10.** Any two, explained. Works council: elected employee representatives meet managers to discuss issues such as shift patterns [1], so staff influence decisions that affect them [1]. Quality circles: small groups of workers meet to solve production problems [1], so their practical knowledge improves processes [1]. Allow suggestion schemes, worker directors or employee share ownership.

**11.** Advantage: mechanics earn more by servicing more cars [1], so output per mechanic may rise and labour cost per car becomes predictable [1]. Disadvantage: mechanics may rush to complete more services [1], so safety checks could be missed, causing complaints or accidents that damage the garage's reputation [1].

**12.** For non-financial methods: team working may meet social needs (Mayo), reducing isolation in a job often done alone [1]; empowerment, such as letting staff organise their own room schedules, adds responsibility, a Herzberg motivator [1]; these methods cost relatively little compared with a pay rise [1].
Against relying on them: pay near the legal minimum may leave basic needs unmet, so, using Maslow, higher-level motivators may have little effect until pay improves [1]; Herzberg would treat low pay as a source of dissatisfaction that must be removed first [1]; absence may also be caused by poor conditions or heavy workloads, which team working does not fix [1]; empowerment needs training and trust, which takes time [1].
Judgement: non-financial methods are unlikely to work on their own [1]; the hotel should first check whether pay and conditions are causing dissatisfaction, then add team working and empowerment [1]. The best approach depends on the main reason for absence, which the manager should find by talking to staff [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a theory without applying it to the business in the question.
- Calculating a bonus on all units instead of only those that qualify.
- Explaining Vroom's terms without showing which belief is weak.
- Recommending non-financial methods to low-paid staff without discussing basic needs.
- Ending an "evaluate" answer without a judgement or a condition.
