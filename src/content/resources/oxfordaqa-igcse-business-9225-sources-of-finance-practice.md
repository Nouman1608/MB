All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Pinewood Pottery.

> **Case study: Pinewood Pottery**
>
> Ines is starting Pinewood Pottery, a sole trader business making
> hand-painted tableware. She needs $45,000 for a kiln and a workshop.
> She has $10,000 of savings. A bank has offered a $20,000 loan at 7%
> simple interest a year over 4 years. That leaves $15,000 to find.
> She is considering crowdfunding, where she expects backers to give an
> average of $50 each, or asking family and friends. Ines also expects
> to use an overdraft of $6,000 at 14% a year for 3 months to cover the
> first quarter's wages while customers start to pay. Her supplier of clay
> has offered 30 days' trade credit.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an internal source of finance? **[1]**
- A. A bank loan
- B. Retained profit
- C. An overdraft
- D. Trade credit

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a source of short-term finance? **[1]**
- A. A mortgage
- B. A new share issue
- C. An overdraft
- D. A 10-year loan

**3.** Define the term "retained profit". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** sources of finance that could be suitable for a new sole trader business. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** disadvantage of a bank loan for a new business. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the total interest Ines would pay on the bank loan. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the total amount Ines would repay on the loan, and the average monthly repayment over the 48 months. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate the amount of money Ines still needs to find after the savings and the bank loan. **[1]**

**9.** Calculate the number of backers needed to raise the remaining amount by crowdfunding. **[2]**

**10.** Calculate the interest Ines would pay on the overdraft. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** reasons why Ines might prefer trade credit to an overdraft to pay her clay supplier. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Ines should raise the remaining amount from crowdfunding or from family and friends. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Profit that the owners keep in the business [1] instead of taking it out or paying it as dividends [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: owner's savings [1]; family and friends [1]; crowdfunding [1]; a government grant or loan [1]; a bank loan [1]; trade credit [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Interest must be paid on top of the loan [1], and repayments must be made even if sales are low, which could create cash flow problems [1]. Accept: a new business has no track record so the bank may refuse [1] or require security [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Interest per year = 7% x $20,000 = $1,400 [1]; total interest = 4 x $1,400 = **$5,600** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Total repaid = $20,000 + $5,600 = **$25,600** [1]; monthly repayment = $25,600 / 48 [1] = **$533.33** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** $45,000 - $10,000 - $20,000 = **$15,000** [1]. **[1]**

**9.** $15,000 / $50 [1] = **300** backers [1]. **[2]**

**10.** $6,000 x 14% x 3/12 [1] = **$210** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **Cost:** trade credit is free if paid on time, whereas the overdraft would cost $210 in interest [1], which is a saving for a new business with little cash [1].
**Security and flexibility:** the supplier has offered 30 days' credit, so there is no need to arrange anything with the bank [1], and the bank could withdraw an overdraft at short notice, whereas the credit terms are agreed [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **Crowdfunding:** no interest to pay if rewards are products [1]; it tests demand and gives publicity, as backers may become customers [1]; but it needs 300 backers and the target may not be reached, so Ines might get nothing [1]; the campaign takes time and effort [1].
**Family and friends:** quick and easy, with flexible terms and perhaps low or no interest [1]; she needs only one or a few people [1]; but relatives may be unable to lend $15,000 and a business failure could strain relationships [1].
**Judgement:** Ines should consider family and friends first for the $15,000 if they can afford it, because it is quicker and certain, but with written terms [1]. If they cannot afford it, she could use crowdfunding, which also tests demand for her tableware before she spends the money [1]. **[9]**

## Tips for the longer questions

- In Question 11, compare cost first ($210 of interest against nothing), then control and risk (the bank can withdraw an overdraft).
- In Question 12, state the amount needed ($15,000) and use it: 300 backers at $50 is a large number for a new business to attract.
- Give each source a benefit and a drawback, then make a judgement that depends on whether family and friends can afford to lend.
- Remember that a sole trader cannot issue shares.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to multiply the yearly interest by the number of years.
- Using 12 months instead of 48 months to find the monthly repayment.
- Taking the interest on the overdraft for a whole year instead of 3/12 of a year.
- Suggesting a share issue for a sole trader.
- Making a recommendation without explaining why the other source is less suitable.
