These are condensed recall notes for **4.2.1 Principles of organisation** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, Version 1.0, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. It is Paper 1 content at both Foundation and Higher Tier; nothing here is marked (HT only). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [principles of organisation study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-principles-of-organisation-aqa-gcse-biology/); exam-style questions are in the [principles of organisation practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-principles-of-organisation-aqa-gcse-biology-practice/).

## The four specification sentences

Learn these word for word; definition marks follow the wording closely.

- Cells are the basic building blocks of all living organisms.
- A tissue is a group of cells with a similar structure and function.
- Organs are aggregations of tissues performing specific functions.
- Organs are organised into organ systems, which work together to form organisms.

Skill link: MS 1c (ratios, fractions and percentages) and an understanding of **size and scale** across cells, tissues, organs and systems.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Cell | Basic building block of living organisms |
| Specialised cell | A cell that has differentiated to carry out one particular function |
| Tissue | Group of cells with a similar structure and function |
| Organ | Aggregation (collection) of different tissues performing a specific function |
| Organ system | Group of organs working together |
| Organism | A living thing; its organ systems work together |
| Aggregation | A collection of things grouped together |
| Micrometre (µm) | One thousandth of a millimetre |
| Order of magnitude | A factor of 10; two orders of magnitude is a factor of 100 |

## The levels in order

```
cell -> tissue -> organ -> organ system -> organism
```

| Level | Animal example | Plant example |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | Muscle cell, red blood cell | Palisade cell, root hair cell |
| Tissue | Muscular, glandular, epithelial tissue; blood | Palisade mesophyll, xylem, phloem, epidermis |
| Organ | Stomach, heart, lungs, skin | Leaf, root, stem |
| Organ system | Digestive, circulatory | Roots, stem and leaves (transport system) |
| Organism | Human | Sunflower |

## The stomach as a model organ

- Muscular tissue: contracts to churn food.
- Glandular tissue: produces digestive juices (enzymes and acid).
- Epithelial tissue: covers the inside and outside.
- Together: one specific function, digesting food, so the stomach is an organ.
- With other organs it forms the digestive system, "an example of an organ system in which several organs work together to digest and absorb food."

## Sorting tests

1. One cell or many? One cell → cell (or a single-celled organism).
2. Many similar cells with one function → tissue.
3. More than one tissue working together → organ.
4. Several organs working together → organ system.

Tricky items: blood = tissue; xylem = tissue; skin = organ; leaf = organ; bacterium = single-celled organism with no tissues.

## Why organisation is needed

- Large organisms have a small surface area compared with their volume, so diffusion through the surface cannot supply inner cells.
- Specialised cells grouped into tissues and organs carry out exchange (lungs, small intestine, roots, leaves) and transport (circulatory system, xylem and phloem).
- A problem at one level spreads upwards: blocked coronary arteries → heart muscle tissue short of oxygen → heart pumps poorly → whole organism affected.

## Size and scale

```
1 m  = 1000 mm = 1 000 000 um
1 cm = 10 mm   = 10 000 um
1 mm = 1000 um
```

Method: convert to one unit, divide, round sensibly.

Mini example: a cell 20 µm long in a tissue layer 2 mm long. 2 mm = 2000 µm; 2000 ÷ 20 = 100 cells end to end.

Mini example: an organ 5 cm long, a cell 50 µm long. 5 cm = 50 000 µm; 50 000 ÷ 50 = 1000 = 10³, three orders of magnitude.

## Exam traps

- "Tissues", plural, in the organ definition: one tissue is not an organ.
- Blood is a tissue, not an organ.
- Organ systems contain organs, not just tissues.
- A bacterium is an organism without tissues.
- Convert units before dividing; mm and µm differ by 1000.
- "Similar structure **and** function": both are needed for the tissue mark.
- When explaining why something is an organ, name two of its tissues and its function.

## Self-test

1. Complete: a tissue is a group of cells with a similar ___ and ___.
2. Put in order, smallest first: organ system, cell, organ, tissue.
3. What does "aggregation" mean in the definition of an organ?
4. Name two tissues found in the stomach.
5. Is blood a cell, a tissue or an organ?
6. Name a plant organ system and the organs in it.
7. Why is the leaf an organ and not a tissue?
8. Why does a bacterium have no tissues?
9. How many micrometres are in 3 mm?
10. A cell is 25 µm long and a tissue layer is 1 mm long. How many cells fit end to end?
11. An organism is 1 m long and one of its cells is 10 µm long. How many orders of magnitude larger is the organism?
12. Give one reason why a large multicellular organism needs organ systems.

### Answers

1. Structure, function.
2. Cell, tissue, organ, organ system.
3. A collection: an organ is a collection of tissues.
4. Any two: muscular, glandular, epithelial tissue.
5. A tissue.
6. The transport system: roots, stem and leaves.
7. It contains several different tissues (such as epidermis, palisade mesophyll, xylem and phloem) working together.
8. It is made of a single cell, and a tissue needs a group of cells.
9. 3 × 1000 = 3000 µm.
10. 1 mm = 1000 µm; 1000 ÷ 25 = 40 cells.
11. 1 m = 1 000 000 µm; 1 000 000 ÷ 10 = 100 000 = 10⁵, five orders of magnitude.
12. Its surface area is small compared with its volume, so diffusion alone cannot supply inner cells; exchange and transport systems are needed.
