These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 7.03, Transformations of curves and their equations. Every question is **Higher tier only**, because the whole section is Higher tier content. As in the specification, no function notation is used. Where a question says "sketch", describe the key features of your sketch in words if you are working without paper. Column vectors are written as (a over b): a across and b up. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the rules first in the [transformations of curves study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-transformations-of-curves-and-their-equations/) or the [transformations of curves revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-transformations-of-curves-and-their-equations-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe fully the single transformation that maps the graph of y = x² onto the graph of

**(a)** y = x² + 7 **[1]**

**(b)** y = (x + 7)² **[1]**

**2.** For the curve y = (x − 4)² − 9:

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the turning point. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where the curve crosses the y-axis. **[1]**

**3.** The point (−1, 3) lies on the graph of an equation y = (expression in x). Write down the coordinates of its image when the equation is changed as follows.

**(a)** 5 is subtracted from the whole right-hand side. **[1]**

**(b)** Every x is replaced by (x − 2). **[1]**

**(c)** The whole right-hand side is made negative. **[1]**

**4.** The graph of y = x³ is translated by the vector (−3 over 2). Write down the equation of the new graph. **[2]**

**5.** Write down the equations of the asymptotes of the graph of y = 1/(x − 5) + 3. **[2]**

**6.** The graph of y = 5ˣ is reflected in the x-axis. Write down the equation of the new graph and the coordinates of the point where it crosses the y-axis. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** **(a)** Write x² − 6x + 5 in the form (x − a)² − b. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe fully the transformation that maps y = x² onto y = x² − 6x + 5. **[1]**

**(c)** Sketch y = x² − 6x + 5, labelling the turning point and the points where the curve crosses both axes. **[3]**

**8.** The curve y = (x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 5) crosses the x-axis at −1, 2 and 5.

**(a)** Every x is replaced by (x + 3). Write down where the new curve crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**(b)** Find where the curve y = (x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 5) − 10 crosses the y-axis. **[1]**

**(c)** The original curve is reflected in the x-axis. Write down where the reflected curve crosses the x-axis. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Describe fully the transformation that maps y = sin x onto y = sin x − 3. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the greatest and least values of sin x − 3. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe fully the transformation that maps y = sin x onto y = sin(x − 180°). **[1]**

**(d)** Use the points (90°, 1) and (270°, −1) on y = sin x to show that y = sin(x − 180°) is the same graph as y = −sin x. **[2]**

**10.** The graph of y = 1/x is transformed to give y = 1/(x + 1) + 2.

**(a)** Write down the equations of the asymptotes of the new graph. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points where the new graph crosses the axes. **[2]**

**11.** Ben reflects the graph of y = x² in the x-axis and then translates the result by (0 over 5). Amy translates the graph of y = x² by (0 over 5) and then reflects the result in the x-axis.

**(a)** Write down the equation of Ben's graph. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the equation of Amy's graph. **[1]**

**(c)** Only one of the graphs crosses the x-axis. Say which, and find exactly where it crosses. **[2]**

**12.** **(a)** The graph of y = x² + 8x + 10 is a translation of y = x². Find the vector of the translation. **[3]**

**(b)** The graph of y = x² + 8x + 10 is reflected in the y-axis. Write down the equation of the new graph and the coordinates of its turning point. **[2]**

**13.** The graph of y = cos x for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360° is transformed to give y = 1 − cos x. Describe the two transformations involved, and give the coordinates of the maximum point and of both minimum points of the new graph. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Translation by vector (0 over 7)** [1]

**(b)** **Translation by vector (−7 over 0)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "Describe fully" needs the word translation and the vector. "Moves left 7" is not a full description.

**2. (a)** **(4, −9)** [1]

**(b)** (0 − 4)² − 9 = 7, so **(0, 7)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The turning point of (x − a)² + b is (a, b). The sign of a changes; the sign of b does not.

**3. (a)** **(−1, −2)** [1]

**(b)** **(1, 3)** [1]

**(c)** **(−1, −3)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Replacing x by (x − 2) moves the graph 2 to the right, so add 2 to the x-coordinate.

**4.** Replace x by (x + 3) and add 2 [1]; **y = (x + 3)³ + 2** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A translation of −3 in the x-direction appears as +3 inside the bracket.

**5.** **x = 5** [1]; **y = 3** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Give equations of lines, not just the numbers. The vertical asymptote is where the denominator x − 5 equals 0.

**6.** **y = −5ˣ** [1]; crosses the y-axis at **(0, −1)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The asymptote y = 0 is unchanged by a reflection in the x-axis, but the curve now lies below it.

**7. (a)** (x − 3)² − 9 + 5 [1]; **(x − 3)² − 4** [1]

**(b)** **Translation by vector (3 over −4)** [1]

**(c)** U-shaped curve with minimum **(3, −4)** [1]; crosses the x-axis at **(1, 0) and (5, 0)** [1]; crosses the y-axis at **(0, 5)** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The x-intercepts come from (x − 1)(x − 5) = 0, or from (x − 3)² = 4. The minimum lies halfway between them, at x = 3.

**8. (a)** Each root moves 3 to the left [1]; **x = −4, −1 and 2** [1]

**(b)** At x = 0 the original gives (1)(−2)(−5) = 10, so the new curve gives 10 − 10 = 0: **(0, 0)** [1]

**(c)** Reflection in the x-axis leaves points on the x-axis unchanged: **x = −1, 2 and 5** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Points on the mirror line are invariant under a reflection, so the roots stay where they are.

**9. (a)** **Translation by vector (0 over −3)** [1]

**(b)** Greatest **−2**, least **−4** [1]

**(c)** **Translation by vector (180 over 0)**, 180° to the right [1]

**(d)** (90°, 1) moves to (270°, 1), and −sin 270° = −(−1) = 1 [1]; (270°, −1) moves to (450°, −1), which matches (90°, −1) after one 360° period, and −sin 90° = −1 [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* For a repeating graph, moving a point by a whole period of 360° lands on the same graph, so you can bring points back into 0° to 360° to compare.

**10. (a)** **x = −1** [1]; **y = 2** [1]

**(b)** x = 0 gives 1 + 2 = 3, so **(0, 3)** [1]; y = 0 gives 1/(x + 1) = −2, so x + 1 = −0.5, giving **(−1.5, 0)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Find intercepts from the new equation, not by moving the old intercepts: y = 1/x has no intercepts at all.

**11. (a)** **y = −x² + 5** [1]

**(b)** **y = −(x² + 5)**, that is y = −x² − 5 [1]

**(c)** **Ben's graph** (maximum (0, 5), above the axis) [1]; −x² + 5 = 0 gives x² = 5, so it crosses at **(−√5, 0) and (√5, 0)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Amy's maximum is (0, −5), below the axis, so it never crosses. The order of the two transformations changed the answer.

**12. (a)** x² + 8x + 10 = (x + 4)² − 16 + 10 [1]; = (x + 4)² − 6 [1]; **vector (−4 over −6)** [1]

**(b)** Replace x by −x: y = (−x + 4)² − 6 = **(x − 4)² − 6**, or y = x² − 8x + 10 [1]; turning point **(4, −6)** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Reflecting in the y-axis changes the sign of the x-coordinate of every point, so (−4, −6) goes to (4, −6). That is a quick check on the algebra.

**13.** **Reflection in the x-axis** (y = −cos x) followed by a **translation by (0 over 1)** [1]; maximum at **(180°, 2)** [1]; minimum points at **(0°, 0) and (360°, 0)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Rewrite 1 − cos x as −cos x + 1 to see the order: the reflection applies to cos x before 1 is added.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Moving a graph right instead of left for (x + a), or the other way round.
- Describing a transformation without the vector or the mirror line.
- Forgetting that asymptotes move with the graph.
- Finding intercepts by moving the old intercepts instead of using the new equation.
- Doing a reflection and a translation in the wrong order.
- Sketching without labelling the coordinates of the key points.
