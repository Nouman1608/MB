Condensed recall for section 4.3.1 of Unit 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Globalisation study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-globalisation/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-globalisation-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
GDP per capita          = GDP / population
Growth of GDP per capita = (1 + GDP growth) / (1 + population growth) - 1
Trade balance           = exports - imports
Price after a tariff    = import price x (1 + tariff rate)
Tariff revenue          = tariff per unit x units imported
```

## Types of economy and indicators

| Type | Features | Business view |
|---|---|---|
| Developed | High income, service-based, mature markets | Large but saturated; high costs |
| Developing | Low income, agriculture, weak infrastructure | Low cost, small market, higher risk |
| Emerging | Fast growth, rising middle class, industrialising | Growing demand and production base |

- **GDP:** total value of output in a year. **GDP per capita:** average income per head.
- **HDI:** index from 0 to 1 combining life expectancy, education and income; broader than GDP.
- GDP per capita grows more slowly than GDP when the population is rising.
- **Implications of growth:** more trade opportunities and jobs, but inequality, pollution, migration to cities and pressure on services.
- Employment moves from agriculture to manufacturing and then services.

## Trade, specialisation and FDI

| Term | Meaning | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Export | Sale of goods or services abroad | Earns foreign currency |
| Import | Purchase from abroad | Cheaper inputs, more choice |
| Specialisation | Focus on what is made most efficiently | Lower cost; risk of dependence |
| FDI | Investment in productive assets abroad | Market access and growth for the investor; jobs and technology for the host |

## Causes of globalisation

| Factor | How it drives globalisation |
|---|---|
| Trade liberalisation and the WTO | Lower tariffs and quotas; rules and dispute settlement |
| Political change | Closed economies opened to trade |
| Cheaper transport and communication | Easier global supply chains and sales |
| Growth of MNCs | Spread of brands and production networks |
| FDI flows | Capital moves to the highest returns |
| Migration | Fills skill gaps; creates new markets |
| Growth of the global labour force | More workers join the global economy |
| Structural change | Shift to manufacturing and services |

**Impact on business:** bigger markets, cheaper inputs, flexible location; but more competition, exchange rate and supply-chain risk, and ethical responsibility across the chain.

## Protectionism

| Barrier | Effect |
|---|---|
| Tariff | Raises import price; raises government revenue |
| Quota | Limits quantity; raises price |
| Legislation | Technical or safety rules make imports harder |
| Domestic subsidy | Lowers local firms' costs |

- **Reasons:** protect infant industries, jobs and strategic industries; stop dumping; correct a trade deficit.
- **Winners:** local producers, government (tariffs). **Losers:** consumers, importers, exporters facing retaliation.
- **Business responses:** produce inside the market (FDI), use other markets, lobby.

## Trading blocs

| Bloc | Notes |
|---|---|
| EU and the single market | Free movement of goods, services, capital and people inside; common external tariff; shared rules |
| ASEAN | Southeast Asian group reducing trade barriers |
| NAFTA | Free trade area of Canada, Mexico and the United States (replaced by the USMCA in 2020, but named in the specification) |

- **Inside the bloc:** bigger market, economies of scale, easier supply chains, more investment.
- **Outside the bloc:** faces a common tariff; may lose sales or invest inside.
- **Drawbacks:** more competition, trade diverted from cheaper outsiders, compliance with common rules.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | Increasing integration of national economies |
| Tariff | Tax on imports |
| Quota | Limit on the quantity of imports |
| WTO | Body that sets trade rules and settles disputes |
| MNC | Multinational corporation operating in several countries |
| Trading bloc | Group of countries with reduced trade barriers between them |
| Protectionism | Policies that shield domestic producers from imports |

## Exam traps

- GDP is not the same as living standards. Use HDI or GDP per capita to refine the picture.
- FDI is investment in assets abroad. Exporting is not FDI.
- A tariff helps local producers and government but hurts consumers.
- A trading bloc cuts barriers inside it but often keeps a common barrier outside.
- Say who is affected: a large MNC, a small exporter and an importer feel globalisation differently.
- For **assess**, weigh benefits against risks for the particular business and give a judgement based on its size and flexibility.

## Answer frames

- **Cause of globalisation:** name the factor, explain the mechanism and finish with the effect on a business (for example, lower costs of selling abroad).
- **Protectionism:** state the barrier, who pays, who gains, and the likely response from a firm in the source.
- **Trading bloc:** say whether the firm is inside or outside, then describe the effect on market size or costs.
- **Judgement:** show what your view depends on, such as the firm's ability to relocate or the strength of its brand.

## Self-test

1. A country has GDP of $300bn and 60 million people. Calculate GDP per capita.
2. GDP grows by 5% and population by 2%. Calculate the growth of GDP per capita.
3. A product costs $50 to import. A 30% tariff is introduced. Calculate the new price.
4. 10,000 units are imported. Calculate tariff revenue.
5. Exports are $80bn and imports $65bn. Calculate the trade balance.
6. What does HDI combine?
7. Give one reason for protectionism.
8. State one effect on a business of falling transport costs.
9. Name the trading bloc that has a single market in Europe.
10. What is FDI?

### Answers

1. 300bn / 60m = **$5,000**.
2. 1.05 / 1.02 - 1 = **2.9%**.
3. 50 x 1.30 = **$65**.
4. 0.30 x 50 x 10,000 = **$150,000**.
5. 80 - 65 = **+$15bn surplus**.
6. Life expectancy, education and income per head.
7. Any one: protect infant industries; protect jobs; protect strategic industries; prevent dumping; correct a trade deficit.
8. Any one: cheaper to export or import; easier global supply chains; more competition from distant rivals.
9. The EU.
10. Investment by a business in productive assets (such as a factory or a local firm) in another country.
