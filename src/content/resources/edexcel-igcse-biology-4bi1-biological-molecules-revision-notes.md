Condensed notes for sub-topic 2(c) **Biological molecules**, points **2.7–2.14B**, of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1), Issue 3. 2.14B is Paper 2 only; everything else is on both papers.

## Elements and units (2.7–2.8)

| Molecule | Elements | Small units | Large molecules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | C, H, O | Simple sugars (glucose) | Starch (plant store), glycogen (animal store) |
| Protein | C, H, O, N (some S) | Amino acids | Enzymes, haemoglobin, antibodies |
| Lipid | C, H, O (little O) | Fatty acids and glycerol | Fats and oils |

- Nitrogen present → protein.
- Starch and glycogen: both chains of glucose.
- Large molecules are good stores: insoluble or less soluble, so they do not draw water into cells by osmosis.

## Food tests (2.9)

| Food | Reagent | Method point | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose | Benedict's solution | Heat in a water bath, about 80 °C | Green → yellow → orange → brick-red | Blue |
| Starch | Iodine solution | No heating | Blue-black | Orange-brown |
| Protein | Biuret solution | No heating | Purple | Blue |
| Fat | Ethanol, then water | Shake with ethanol, pour into water | Cloudy white emulsion | Clear |

Fair comparison between samples: same volume of sample, same volume of reagent, same heating temperature and time. A control tube of water shows the reagent's negative colour.

Safety: ethanol is flammable (no flames); heat Benedict's in a water bath; eye protection for biuret (contains alkali).

## Enzymes (2.10)

- **Catalyst**: speeds up a reaction without being used up.
- **Enzyme**: a protein that acts as a biological catalyst in metabolic reactions.
- **Active site**: region with a specific shape where the substrate binds.
- **Specific**: only a substrate with a complementary shape fits.
- Enzyme + substrate → enzyme–substrate complex → enzyme + products.
- Enzymes catalyse breakdown (digestion, respiration) and synthesis (starch from glucose, proteins from amino acids).

## Temperature (2.11–2.12)

| Region of the graph | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rising part | Rate increases | More kinetic energy, more collisions, more enzyme–substrate complexes |
| Peak | Optimum temperature | Fastest rate |
| Falling part | Rate decreases | Active site changes shape; substrate no longer fits; enzyme denatured |

**Temperature practical in steps** (2.12):

1. Set water baths at a range of temperatures, for example 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C.
2. Place enzyme and substrate in separate tubes in each bath to reach temperature.
3. Mix and start timing.
4. Measure the time for the end point (starch gone with iodine on a spotting tile, or milk going clear with a protease).
5. Calculate rate = 1 ÷ time.
6. Repeat each temperature three times and find the mean.
7. Control: concentration and volume of enzyme and substrate, pH (buffer).

## pH (2.13 and 2.14B)

- Each enzyme has an **optimum pH**.
- pH away from the optimum alters the **shape of the active site**; the substrate fits less well; rate falls.
- Extreme pH denatures the enzyme.
- Stomach protease: optimum in acid. Trypsin (small intestine): optimum slightly alkaline.
- **pH practical** (Paper 2 only): buffer solutions set the pH; temperature is controlled with a water bath, for example 40 °C; all other method points as for temperature.

## Rate calculations

| Time to end point / s | Rate = 1 ÷ time / s⁻¹ | Rate × 1000 |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 0.020 | 20 |
| 80 | 0.0125 | 12.5 |
| 200 | 0.005 | 5 |

The shortest time is the fastest rate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metabolic reaction | A chemical reaction in a living organism |
| Catalyst | Speeds up a reaction and is not used up |
| Substrate | Molecule an enzyme acts on |
| Active site | Part of the enzyme the substrate fits into |
| Complementary | Shapes that fit together |
| Optimum | Condition giving the fastest rate |
| Denatured | Active site shape permanently changed so the enzyme no longer works |
| Buffer | Solution that keeps pH constant |
| Emulsion | Tiny droplets of one liquid spread through another |

## Exam traps

- Enzymes are **denatured**, not killed.
- The **active site** changes shape, not "the enzyme dies" or "the substrate is denatured".
- Quote figures with units when describing a graph.
- Rate is 1 ÷ time: a longer time is a slower rate.
- Benedict's needs heating; iodine and biuret do not.
- Biuret positive is **purple**, not blue (blue is the reagent's own colour).
- In the emulsion test the sample goes into ethanol first, then water.
- 2.14B (pH practical) is Paper 2 only, but the theory of pH (2.13) is on both papers.

## Self-test

**1.** Which element is in proteins but not in carbohydrates?
**2.** What are the small units that make up lipids?
**3.** Name the storage carbohydrate in animals.
**4.** Describe the test for protein and its positive result.
**5.** A sample turns blue-black with iodine. What does it contain?
**6.** Why is the emulsion test done away from flames?
**7.** Define an enzyme.
**8.** Explain why raising the temperature from 20 °C to 35 °C increases the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction.
**9.** What happens to the active site above the optimum temperature?
**10.** A reaction takes 125 s. Calculate the rate in s⁻¹.
**11.** Why are buffer solutions used in the pH practical?
**12.** Why are enzyme and substrate placed in the water bath separately before mixing?

### Answers

**1.** Nitrogen.
**2.** Fatty acids and glycerol.
**3.** Glycogen.
**4.** Add biuret solution; a purple colour shows protein.
**5.** Starch.
**6.** Ethanol is flammable.
**7.** A protein that acts as a biological catalyst, speeding up metabolic reactions without being used up.
**8.** Molecules gain kinetic energy, collide more often, and more enzyme–substrate complexes form per second.
**9.** It changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits; the enzyme is denatured.
**10.** 1 ÷ 125 = 0.008 s⁻¹.
**11.** To keep each tube at a fixed, known pH.
**12.** So both are at the test temperature when the reaction starts.
