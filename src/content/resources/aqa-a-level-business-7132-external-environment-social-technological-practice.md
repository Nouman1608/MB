These questions cover section 3.7.6 social and technological change of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-social-technological/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-social-technological-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** In Carroll's CSR pyramid, which layer is at the base? **[1]**

A: Philanthropic

B: Ethical

C: Legal

D: Economic

**2.** Which **one** of the following reflects the stakeholder concept rather than the shareholder concept? **[1]**

A: Maximising dividends in every year

B: Judging success only by share price

C: Balancing the interests of employees, customers and the community

D: Cutting staff to raise profit

**3.** Define "corporate social responsibility". **[2]**

**4.** The population of a town of 45,000 people has 27% aged over 65. The share rises to 31%. Calculate the increase in the number of people over 65. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which the growth of online shopping could threaten a high-street clothes shop. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**6.** Halloran Books has annual sales of £12m. Online sales were 20% of sales last year and are 35% this year, with total sales unchanged.

**(a)** Calculate online sales in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the increase in online sales, and state what this might mean for the number of physical stores. **[2]**

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why a business might act in a socially responsible way. **[4]**

**8.** A fast-fashion retailer announces that it will pay all of its factory workers a living wage, which will raise costs by 4%. Using Carroll's CSR pyramid, analyse the decision. **[6]**

**9.** Brae Foods makes sweet breakfast cereals. Consumers are increasingly interested in healthy eating. Analyse **two** ways in which this social change might affect its decisions. **[6]**

**10.** Wentford Dairy buys a robotic milking system for £360,000. It saves 5 workers at £26,000 each, but running costs are £22,000 a year. Calculate the annual net saving and the payback period in years, to two decimal places. **[4]**

**11.** Assess whether Wentford Dairy should replace its workers with the robotic system. **[9]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that a business should focus on maximising profit rather than being socially responsible. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]. The economic layer (profitability) is the foundation. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. The stakeholder concept balances the interests of all affected groups. **[1]**

**3.** The idea that a business has responsibilities beyond making profit [1], to society, the community and the environment, for example employees, customers and the environment [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Before: 45,000 × 0.27 = 12,150 [1]; after: 45,000 × 0.31 = 13,950, so an increase of **1,800** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Customers can compare prices instantly online [1], and online retailers have lower overheads such as rent and shop staff, so can offer lower prices [1]. The high-street shop may lose sales, and fixed costs such as rent are spread over fewer customers, reducing profit [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Last year: 20% × £12m = **£2.4m** [1]; this year: 35% × £12m = **£4.2m** [1]. **(b)** Increase = **£1.8m** [1]. Since the total is unchanged, sales through stores have fallen by £1.8m, so Halloran may need fewer or smaller stores, or fewer staff in them [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Reason 1: reputation [1]. Customers increasingly prefer ethical businesses, so socially responsible behaviour can raise sales and loyalty [1]. Reason 2: recruitment and retention [1]. Staff prefer employers with good values, which reduces turnover and training costs [1]. (Credit also: avoiding fines and boycotts; investor interest.) **[4]**

**8.** Economic: the retailer must still make a profit, and a 4% rise in costs reduces margins unless prices rise [1]. Legal: paying a living wage goes beyond the legal minimum, so it is not simply compliance [1]. Ethical: the decision shows fairness to workers in the supply chain, who are otherwise poorly paid [1], and responds to criticism of working conditions [1]. Philanthropic: it is not charity in the usual sense, but a commitment that benefits the communities where workers live [1]. Overall, the decision is mainly in the ethical layer, and may protect the brand and reduce the risk of boycotts [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Marketing: it may reformulate its products with less sugar [1], and launch new healthier lines, using the health message in advertising [1]. Operations: new recipes need new ingredients and processes, and testing [1], which may increase costs or change suppliers [1]. Finance: reformulation requires investment, but healthier products could sustain sales as the market changes [1]. Strategy: if it does nothing, sales of sugary cereals could decline [1]. (Credit any two functions or strategic effects, with explanation.) **[6]**

**10.** Labour saved = 5 × £26,000 = £130,000 [1]; net annual saving = £130,000 − £22,000 = **£108,000** [1]. Payback = £360,000 ÷ £108,000 [1] = **3.33 years** [1]. **[4]**

**11.** For: the net saving of £108,000 a year repays the cost in about 3.3 years [1], after which the saving is clear profit [1]. Milking robots can be more consistent and give data on each cow's health and yield. Against: £360,000 is a large investment, and may need borrowing, with interest [1]. Five workers lose their jobs, which may affect morale, local reputation and redundancy costs [1]. Breakdowns could halt milking, and the machine may become outdated [1]. The payback ignores what happens after the payback period and the time value of money [1]. Factors: how secure the farm's contracts are, and whether the workers can be redeployed or retrained [1]. Judgement: the investment is probably worthwhile if demand is stable and the herd is large enough to use the system [1], but the business should check financing costs and plan for staff [1]. **[9]**

**12.** For profit maximisation: managers are agents of the shareholders, who own the business, so should seek their best return [1]. Spending on CSR is a cost and can put a business at a disadvantage against rivals who do not [1]. Without profit a business cannot survive to provide jobs [1], and CSR claims may be greenwashing [1]. Against: CSR can raise profit in the long run through reputation, loyalty and recruitment [1]. Ignoring it can bring fines, boycotts and bad publicity [1]. The stakeholder concept argues that success depends on good relations with all groups [1]. Investors and customers increasingly expect it [1]. Factors: the industry and its visibility (a clothing brand is exposed to supply-chain scrutiny) [1], the size of the cost against the benefit [1], and the time horizon (short term favours profit, long term favours CSR). Judgement: the two are not simply opposed [1]; the most defensible position is to pursue profit within sensible limits, using CSR where it protects long-term value [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers avoid treating CSR as all good or all bad, and decide using the business's visibility, its costs and its time horizon.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a social trend without linking it to this business's decisions.
- Mixing up the shareholder and stakeholder concepts.
- Listing Carroll's layers without applying them.
- Ignoring costs and disruption when discussing technology.
- Assuming CSR always costs profit, or always gains it.
