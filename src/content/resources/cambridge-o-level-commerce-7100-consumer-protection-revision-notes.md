Condensed notes for sub-topic 6.3, Consumer protection: the reasons and the actions taken to resolve complaints.

## Four facts to start with

1. **Consumer protection** protects buyers from unsafe, unfair or misleading treatment.
2. Reasons include safety, quality, information, fair prices and contracts, unequal power, vulnerable groups, honest advertising and trust.
3. **Buyers** keep proof, contact the seller, complain in writing, use guarantees, seek help and, if needed, take legal action.
4. **Sellers** listen, investigate, and offer a refund, repair, replacement or credit note.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Consumer | Person who buys goods or services for own use |
| Consumer protection | Laws, rules and actions that protect buyers |
| Guarantee (warranty) | Promise to repair or replace a product within a set time |
| Refund | Money returned to the buyer |
| Replacement | A new item instead of the faulty one |
| Credit note | Document that can be used to buy other goods |
| Recall | Removing an unsafe product from sale and asking buyers to return it |
| Proof of purchase | Receipt or invoice showing what was bought |

## Reasons for consumer protection

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| Safety | Toys that can harm children |
| Quality | Goods fit for purpose |
| Information | Honest labels and prices |
| Fair prices and contracts | No hidden charges |
| Unequal knowledge and power | Buyers know less than sellers |
| Vulnerable groups | Children, older people |
| Honest advertising | No false claims |
| Trust | More trade takes place |

## Buyer actions (in order)

1. Keep proof of purchase.
2. Contact the seller.
3. Make a formal written complaint.
4. Use the guarantee or contact the manufacturer.
5. Contact a consumer organisation.
6. Ask the card provider for a refund.
7. Take legal action (last resort).

## Seller actions

1. Listen and apologise.
2. Investigate.
3. Offer a refund, repair, replacement or credit note.
4. Compensate for extra loss.
5. Clear returns and warranty policy.
6. Record complaints and fix causes.
7. Train staff.
8. Recall unsafe products.

## Choosing a remedy

| Situation | Remedy |
|---|---|
| Faulty item, new | Refund or replacement |
| Minor fault | Repair or partial refund |
| Out of stock | Credit note |
| Damaged on arrival | Replacement and postage refund |
| Dangerous product | Recall and full refund |

## Worked examples

**Refund.** $60 headset + $5 postage + $4 return postage = $69 refunded.

**Complaint rate.** 45 ÷ 3,000 × 100 = 1.5%; cost 45 × $20 = $900.

**Recall.** 5,000 toys × $35 = $175,000 maximum refund cost.

## Model answers to learn

**Why do consumers need protection?** Sellers usually know more about a product than buyers, and large businesses are more powerful than individuals. Protection stops unsafe or misleading goods being sold and gives buyers a way to put things right.

**How can a seller resolve a complaint?** Listen to the customer and check the fault, then offer a refund, repair or replacement quickly, and use the complaint to improve the product.

## Developing a consumer protection point

| Weak | Developed |
|---|---|
| "To protect customers" | "Buyers cannot test goods bought online, so protection ensures faulty goods can be returned for a refund" |
| "Contact the shop" | "Contact the shop with the receipt as proof and ask for a repair or refund, which gives the seller a chance to put things right" |
| "Give a refund" | "Refund the price and postage quickly so the customer keeps trust in the shop" |

## Escalation ladder for a buyer

| Step | If the problem is not solved, go to |
|---|---|
| Contact the seller with proof | Formal written complaint |
| Formal written complaint | Guarantee or manufacturer |
| Guarantee or manufacturer | Card provider or consumer organisation |
| Card provider or consumer organisation | Legal action (last resort) |

## Exam traps

- **Protection only for businesses.**
- **Court as the first step.**
- **No proof of purchase.**
- **Only the buyer's actions.**
- **Remedies confused.**
- **Percentage worked on the wrong amount.**

## Self-test

1. Define consumer protection.
2. Give two reasons for consumer protection.
3. Why do vulnerable groups need protection?
4. Why should a buyer keep a receipt?
5. What is a guarantee?
6. Name three remedies a seller can offer.
7. What is a credit note?
8. What is a product recall?
9. Give one benefit to an honest seller of consumer protection.
10. Calculate the refund for a $45 item with $3 postage.

**Answers**

1. Laws, rules and actions that protect buyers from unsafe, unfair or misleading treatment.
2. Any two: safety, quality, accurate information, fair prices and contracts, unequal power, vulnerable groups, honest advertising, trust.
3. They can be easily misled or pressured.
4. It is proof of what was bought, when and from whom.
5. A promise to repair or replace a product within a set time.
6. Any three: refund, repair, replacement, credit note, compensation.
7. A document that can be used to buy other goods.
8. Removing an unsafe product from sale and asking buyers to return it.
9. Dishonest rivals cannot win customers by cheating, and customers trust the market.
10. $48.
