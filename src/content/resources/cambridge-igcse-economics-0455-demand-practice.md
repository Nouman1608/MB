## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which of the following would cause an extension in demand for cinema tickets? **[1]**

A: A rise in consumers' incomes

B: A fall in the price of cinema tickets

C: A fall in the price of streaming subscriptions

D: A successful advertising campaign for a new film

**2.** Printers and printer ink are complements. The price of printers falls. What happens in the market for printer ink? **[1]**

A: Demand decreases and the demand curve shifts left

B: Quantity demanded contracts along the demand curve

C: Demand increases and the demand curve shifts right

D: Nothing, because the price of ink has not changed

**3.** Define demand. **[2]**

**4.** Distinguish between an extension in demand and an increase in demand. **[2]**

**5.** The table shows the weekly demand for melons of three buyers.

| Price | Farah | Ben | Chen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| 2 | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| 1 | 4 | 5 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the market demand at each price. **[2]**

**(b)** State what would happen to the market demand curve if Chen stopped buying melons. **[1]**

**6.** The price of a bus ticket rises from 1.20 to 1.50 and the quantity demanded falls from 4,000 to 3,400 a day. Calculate the percentage fall in quantity demanded. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** A shop's demand schedule for headphones is: price 50, 60 units; price 40, 90 units; price 30, 130 units (per month).

**(a)** Calculate the shop's revenue at a price of 50 and at a price of 40. **[2]**

**(b)** Using the correct term, describe what happens to demand when the price falls from 50 to 40. **[2]**

**(c)** After a popular review, demand rises by 30% at every price. Calculate the new quantity demanded at a price of 40. **[1]**

**8.** Explain, using a demand diagram, the effect of a rise in consumers' incomes on the demand for restaurant meals. **[5]**

**9.** Explain **two** reasons why a demand curve slopes downward from left to right. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how each of the following affects the demand for new houses: **(a)** a fall in interest rates; **(b)** a rise in the number of young adults in the population. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse the effect of a large rise in the price of electricity on the demand for electric cars. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether advertising is the most important influence on the demand for a new brand of smartphone. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Only a change in the ticket's own price causes a movement; A, C and D shift the demand curve. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. Cheaper printers mean more printers bought, so more ink is needed at every price of ink. **[1]**

**3.** The willingness and ability to buy a product [1] at a given price over a given period of time [1]. **[2]**

**4.** An extension is a rise in quantity demanded caused by a fall in the product's own price, a movement down the curve [1]. An increase is a rise in demand at every price caused by a change in a condition of demand, a shift of the curve to the right [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Quantities are added across at each price, for example 1 + 2 + 0 at a price of 3 [1]; market demand is **3** at a price of 3, **6** at 2 and **12** at 1 [1].

**(b)** It would shift to the left [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Fall = 4,000 − 3,400 = 600 [1]; 600 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **15%** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 50 × 60 = **3,000** [1]; 40 × 90 = **3,600** [1].

**(b)** Quantity demanded extends [1], from 60 to 90 units, a movement down the existing demand curve [1].

**(c)** 90 × 1.3 = **117 units** [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Diagram with price on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis, original demand curve D1 [1] and a new curve D2 to the right [1]. Higher incomes mean households can afford more meals out [1], so more meals are demanded at every price [1]; at the original price, quantity rises from Q1 to Q2, shown on the diagram [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Any two developed reasons. At a lower price the product is cheaper than its substitutes [1], so some buyers switch to it and quantity demanded rises [1]. At a lower price buyers' incomes go further [1], so existing buyers can afford more units and new buyers enter the market [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Lower interest rates reduce the cost of a mortgage [1], so more households can afford to borrow to buy, and demand increases (shifts right) [1].

**(b)** Young adults are the main group forming new households [1], so more of them means more buyers at every price, and demand increases [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Electricity is a complement to electric cars [1]. A rise in its price raises the cost of running an electric car [1], so fewer people want one at every price [1]. Demand for electric cars decreases and the demand curve shifts left; some buyers may switch to petrol cars, a substitute [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For advertising: a new brand is unknown, so advertising creates awareness [1] and can change tastes in its favour, shifting demand right [1]. Against: the prices of rival smartphones (substitutes) matter, because a cheaper rival may draw buyers away [1]; consumers' incomes limit how many can afford it [1]; reviews and fashion, which the firm cannot fully control, influence tastes [1]. Conclusion: advertising matters most at launch, when awareness is low, but over time price compared with rivals and incomes are likely to be at least as important [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In "Discuss", weigh the named factor against at least two other conditions of demand and decide when it matters most.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "increase in demand" for a change caused by the product's own price.
- Diagrams with unlabelled axes, or with the shift drawn the wrong way.
- Reversing the effects of substitutes and complements.
- Adding prices rather than quantities in a market demand table.
- Giving a factor without the reason it changes willingness or ability to buy.
