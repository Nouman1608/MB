These questions cover sub-topic 4(d), **Human influences on the environment** (specification points 4.12 to 4.18B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Points 4.12 to 4.17 can be examined on either paper. Questions labelled **(Paper 2 only)** test the bold **B** point 4.18B. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

Learn the content first with the [human influences on the environment study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-human-influences-on-the-environment/). The course hub is [Edexcel IGCSE Biology](/boards/edexcel/igcse/biology/).

## Questions

**1.** Sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide are air pollutants.

**(a)** Name a human activity that releases sulfur dioxide. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe **two** harmful effects of acid rain on living things. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how carbon monoxide reduces the amount of oxygen delivered to body cells. **[2]**

**2.** Methane, carbon dioxide and nitrous oxide are greenhouse gases.

**(a)** Name **two** other greenhouse gases in the specification. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** human activity that increases the concentration of methane. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how an increase in the concentration of greenhouse gases leads to global warming. **[3]**

**3.** The concentration of carbon dioxide in a sample of air rose from 280 ppm to 350 ppm.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** consequences of global warming. **[2]**

**4.** Untreated sewage enters a river. The dissolved oxygen concentration is 10.0 mg per dm³ upstream and 3.0 mg per dm³ downstream.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in oxygen concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the oxygen concentration falls downstream. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why fish are not found downstream of the sewage outlet. **[1]**

**5.** Sewage can contain pathogens. Explain why untreated sewage is a risk to people. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how excess fertiliser used on a farm can cause the death of fish in a nearby lake. **[6]**

**7.** A farmer applies 120 kg of fertiliser per hectare. A new plan reduces this to 80 kg per hectare.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** other way the farmer could reduce the risk of fertiliser entering the lake. **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) A hillside forest is cut down and not replanted.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by leaching and why it increases after deforestation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how deforestation can lead to soil erosion. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how deforestation affects the carbon cycle. **[3]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Deforestation can change the local climate. Explain how the removal of trees can make an area drier. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Burning fossil fuels such as coal (accept power stations) [1]
**(b)** Any two of: damages leaves/roots of trees, so trees die [1]; makes lakes acidic so fish die / biodiversity falls [1]; harms the lungs of animals [1]
**(c)** Carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin in red blood cells [1]; so haemoglobin carries less oxygen [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Carbon monoxide takes up the space of oxygen" is acceptable. "It blocks the lungs" is not.

**2.** **(a)** Water vapour [1]; CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) [1]
**(b)** Any one of: cattle farming, rice growing, landfill sites [1]
**(c)** Greenhouse gases absorb heat radiated from the Earth's surface [1]; they re-radiate some of it back to the Earth [1]; more gas means more heat is trapped, so the average temperature rises [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write "traps" or "absorbs" heat, not that the gases "reflect the Sun's rays". The mark is for the idea of heat being kept in.

**3.** **(a)** (350 − 280) ÷ 280 × 100 [1] = **25%** [1]
**(b)** Any two of: sea level rises, polar ice melts, changes in climate/extreme weather, change in distribution of species, loss of habitat [2]
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value (280). Dividing by 350 gives 20%, which is wrong.

**4.** **(a)** (10.0 − 3.0) ÷ 10.0 × 100 [1] = **70%** [1]
**(b)** Bacteria feed on the organic matter in the sewage and multiply [1]; the bacteria use oxygen for aerobic respiration [1]; so the oxygen concentration falls [1]
**(c)** The oxygen concentration is too low for fish to survive [1]
*Examiner insight:* The oxygen is used by the bacteria. "The sewage takes the oxygen" is not an explanation.

**5.** Sewage can carry pathogens (bacteria or viruses) [1]; if people drink the water, they can become infected with diseases such as cholera or typhoid [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name a disease or the route of infection to secure the second mark.

**6.** Fertiliser containing nitrates and phosphates is leached from the soil by rain into the lake [1]; the extra nutrients make algae grow rapidly (algal bloom) [1]; the bloom blocks light, so plants below cannot photosynthesise and die [1]; algae and plants die [1]; decomposers (bacteria) feed on the dead material and multiply [1]; they use up the oxygen in respiration, so fish die from lack of oxygen [1]
*Examiner insight:* The chain order is the mark scheme. Skipping the step about decomposers is the most common loss.

**7.** **(a)** (120 − 80) ÷ 120 × 100 [1] = **33.3%** (accept 33%) [1]
**(b)** Any one of: leave an uncultivated strip beside the stream, do not apply before heavy rain, use organic fertiliser in smaller amounts [1]
*Examiner insight:* Round only at the end. A rounded intermediate figure can cost the accuracy mark.

**8.** **(a)** Leaching is the washing of minerals (such as nitrates) out of the soil by rain [1]; with no roots to absorb the minerals, more are washed away [1]
**(b)** Tree roots hold the soil together and leaves reduce the force of falling rain [1]; without them the loose soil is washed away by rain / wind [1]
**(c)** Fewer trees means less photosynthesis, so less carbon dioxide is removed from the air [1]; burning or decay of the felled trees releases carbon dioxide [1]; so the carbon dioxide concentration in the air rises (accept: contributes to global warming) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (c), give both effects: less removal and more release.

**9.** Trees take up water through roots and release water vapour from their leaves (evapotranspiration) [1]; with fewer trees, less water vapour enters the air [1]; so there is less cloud formation and rainfall, and the climate becomes drier [1]
*Examiner insight:* The link from water vapour to clouds to rainfall must be stated. "Trees make rain" alone earns one mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying carbon dioxide causes acid rain.
- Saying algae use up the oxygen in eutrophication, rather than decomposers.
- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change.
- Writing "pollution" without naming the pollutant and its effect.
- Leaving out the step of leaching in a fertiliser answer.
- Confusing leaching with soil erosion.
