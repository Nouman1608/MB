**These notes condense Section 3 Power of Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448 Paper 2.** You need
to know how fossil fuels are extracted and transported, how electricity is generated, the case for
and against each renewable source, and why many parts of Pakistan still lack a reliable supply.
Use the tables for recall, then test yourself with the practice page.

## Syllabus coverage

**0448 Paper 2, Section 3 Power (2026 syllabus)**
- **3(a) Sources:** extraction of coal (open cast, adit, shaft) and of oil and gas (exploration, drilling); renewable versus non-renewable; how renewables generate electricity; power and development
- **3(b) Non-renewables:** coal quality, reserves, imports and transport; gas production, reserves, pipelines and supply beyond the pipelines; oil production, imports, pipelines and other transport; thermal and nuclear generation; rising prices and depletion
- **3(c) Renewables:** advantages and disadvantages of water, wind, wave and solar power; conditions for multi-purpose hydel schemes; factors promoting or hindering supply, including small-scale schemes; why supply is insufficient and unreliable

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-renewable | A source that cannot be replaced once used (coal, oil, natural gas) |
| Renewable | A source that is naturally replenished (water, wind, sun, waves, tides) |
| Hydel | Hydro-electricity: power from falling or flowing water turning turbines |
| Thermal power station | Burns fuel (coal, oil, gas, waste) to make steam that drives turbines |
| Open cast mining | Removing the overburden to dig a seam from the surface |
| Adit | Horizontal tunnel into a hillside to reach a coal seam |
| Shaft mine | Vertical shaft sunk to reach a deep seam |
| LPG | Liquefied petroleum gas, carried in cylinders to places without a gas pipeline |
| LNG | Liquefied natural gas, imported by sea and regasified at a terminal |
| Multi-purpose scheme | Dam that provides hydel power, irrigation storage and flood control |
| Load-shedding | Planned power cuts when demand exceeds supply |
| Transmission losses | Electricity lost on the wires between power station and user, including theft |

## Key facts to learn

**Coal**
- Largest reserves: Thar coalfield, Sindh, mined open cast.
- Quality: mostly lignite, high in ash, moisture and sulphur, with low heat value.
- Best used in power stations at the mine; costly to transport far.
- Coking coal for steel making is imported by sea to Karachi, then moved by rail and road.

**Natural gas**
- First major field: Sui, Balochistan (1952); other fields in Sindh and Balochistan.
- Pipelines carry gas to the main cities of Sindh, Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- Off-pipeline areas use LPG cylinders, which cost more, depend on road transport and run short in winter.
- Falling domestic output has led to LNG imports through Port Qasim.

**Oil**
- Domestic fields: Potwar Plateau and Sindh, which meet only a small share of demand.
- Most crude oil and products are imported, mainly from the Gulf states.
- Transport: tanker to Karachi, then pipeline inland to Mahmood Kot, plus rail and road tankers.
- Imports put pressure on the balance of trade when world oil prices rise.

**Electricity**
- Thermal: oil, gas, coal; nuclear at Chashma and near Karachi.
- Hydel: Tarbela (Indus), Mangla (Jhelum), Warsak (Kabul).
- Wind: Gharo–Jhimpir corridor, Sindh. Solar: Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park near Bahawalpur.

## Renewables: advantages and disadvantages

| Source | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Hydel | No fuel cost; irrigation and flood control | High capital cost; silting; low winter flow; resettlement |
| Wind | Free fuel; no emissions | Variable output; remote sites need new transmission lines |
| Solar | High sunshine; suits off-grid villages | Night-time gap; battery cost; dust on panels |
| Wave/tidal | Long coastline | Undeveloped, expensive, needs expertise |

## Conditions for a multi-purpose hydel scheme

- **Physical:** narrow, deep valley in strong rock; large, reliable river flow; space to store a large reservoir; low earthquake risk where possible.
- **Human:** capital (often foreign loans); engineering skills; demand for irrigation water and power downstream; transmission lines to markets; few people to resettle.

## Why is supply unreliable?

1. Demand growing faster than new generating capacity.
2. Dependence on imported, expensive fuel.
3. Hydel output falls in winter as rivers run low.
4. High transmission and distribution losses, including theft.
5. Remote areas (Balochistan, mountain north, Thar) are far from the grid.

**Solutions to quote:** small-scale micro-hydel in mountain valleys; village solar; more wind farms
in Sindh; reducing line losses; using local Thar coal instead of imported fuel (but note the
pollution trade-off).

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why natural gas is not available to everyone in Pakistan. **[4]**

**Model answer:** The pipeline network mainly links gas fields in Sindh and Balochistan with large
cities, so many villages and hill areas are not connected [1]. Building pipelines across mountains
and deserts to scattered settlements costs too much for the small demand [1]. These areas rely on
LPG cylinders, which must be carried by road and cost more [1]. Supply also runs short in winter
when household demand for heating rises and domestic production is falling [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Muddling adit and shaft.** Adit = horizontal into a hillside; shaft = vertical.
- **Calling hydel "free".** The fuel is free, but building costs and silting are major drawbacks.
- **Generic answers.** "Solar is good because it is clean" scores little. Say why it suits Pakistan (high sunshine, scattered villages) and give a limitation.
- **Forgetting the evaluation.** The final part of a question often asks you to assess. Weigh both sides and give a conclusion.
- **Quoting figures without a year.** Production and reserve figures change; always give the year.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which three dams must I know?** Tarbela on the Indus, Mangla on the Jhelum and Warsak on the Kabul
River. These appear in Section 2 (water) and again as hydel sites in Section 3.

**Is nuclear renewable?** No. Nuclear power uses uranium fuel, so it is non-renewable, but it
produces little carbon dioxide.

**What is the difference between LPG and LNG?** LPG comes in cylinders for homes away from
pipelines. LNG is natural gas cooled to a liquid, imported by ship and turned back into gas at a
terminal.
