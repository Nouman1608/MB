## Section A

**1.** State whether each item belongs in cost of sales, among the expenses, or as income added after gross profit: **(a)** carriage inwards, **(b)** carriage outwards, **(c)** discount received, **(d)** discount allowed. **[4]**

**2.** Revenue is 64,000 and returns inwards are 1,500. Opening inventory is 5,000, purchases are 40,000, returns outwards are 600 and closing inventory is 6,400. Calculate **(a)** net revenue and **(b)** cost of sales. **[3]**

**3.** Where is income received in advance shown in the statement of financial position?

A: Non-current assets

B: Current assets

C: Current liabilities

D: Capital

**[1]**

**4.** A trader has a loan of 12,000 at 5% interest a year. Interest of 350 has been paid during the year. Calculate the loan interest for the income statement and the amount owing. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why closing inventory appears in both the income statement and the statement of financial position. **[2]**

**6.** A driving instructor's trial balance shows fees of 48,000 and total expenses of 31,200. Fees of 1,500 for lessons given are still to be received. Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**

## Section B

## Answers

**1. (a)** Cost of sales [1]. **(b)** Expense [1]. **(c)** Income [1]. **(d)** Expense [1].

**2. (a)** 64,000 − 1,500 = **62,500** [1].

**(b)** 5,000 + (40,000 − 600) − 6,400 [1] = **38,000** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** C [1].

**4.** Interest for the year 5% × 12,000 = **600** [1]; owing 600 − 350 = **250** [1].

**5.** It is deducted in cost of sales because those goods have not yet been sold, so their cost is matched against next year's revenue [1]. It is still owned at the year end, so it is a current asset [1].

**6.** 48,000 + 1,500 − 31,200 [1] = **18,300** [1].

**7.** Insurance account: debit bank 3,600; credit income statement **2,700** [1], credit balance c/d (prepaid) 900 [1].

Electricity account: debit bank 2,100 and balance c/d (owing) 280 [1]; credit income statement **2,380** [1].

**8. (a)** Income statement of Tate Garden Supplies for the year ended 31 December 2025

| Carriage inwards | 700 | |

**9.** Gross margin is 37,300 ÷ 94,800 × 100 = about 39.3% and profit is about 18.1% of revenue, so a good share of gross profit is kept after expenses [1]. Drawings of 10,800 are well below profit, so capital rose by 6,350, leaving resources in the business [1]. Current assets cover current liabilities about 3.6 times [1]; even without inventory, receivables and bank (8,050) cover them about 1.8 times, so Bryony can meet short-term debts, though the bank balance itself is small [1].

**10. (a)** Gross profit is understated by 1,800 [1].

**(b)** No effect on profit for the year, because the 1,800 is deducted either way [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Drawings are value taken out of the business by the owner for personal use [1]. Under the business entity concept they are not a cost of earning revenue, so they reduce capital rather than profit [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing headings or dates on the statements.
- Deducting returns inwards from purchases.
- Using only the amount paid for loan interest or electricity.
- Leaving the other payable out of current liabilities after adding it to the expense.
- Comments that repeat figures without saying what they mean for the owner.
