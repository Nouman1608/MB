## Section A

**1.** Which of the following best describes Article 5? A: an absolute right. B: a limited right. C: a qualified right. D: a right that cannot be derogated from. **[1]**

**2.** Which paragraph of Article 5 permits arrest on reasonable suspicion of having committed an offence? A: 5.1(a). B: 5.1(b). C: 5.1(c). D: 5.2. **[1]**

**3.** State the test the European Court applied in Guzzardi v Italy (1980) to distinguish a deprivation of liberty from a restriction on movement. **[2]**

**4.** State the "acid test" for a deprivation of liberty set out in P v Cheshire West and Chester Council (2014). **[2]**

**5.** Identify the requirement in Article 5.2 and the requirement in Article 5.5. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain what is meant by "reasonable suspicion" under Article 5.1(c), referring to one decision of the European Court and one decision of an English court. **[4]**

**7.** Explain the difference between the safeguard in Article 5.3 and the safeguard in Article 5.4. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why the House of Lords in Austin v Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis (2009) held that a seven-hour containment of a crowd was not a deprivation of liberty. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the decision of the House of Lords in A v Secretary of State for the Home Department (2004) and its consequences for the law on detention of terrorist suspects. **[5]**

**10.** *Marcus, who has a learning disability and lacks capacity to decide where to live, is placed by a local council in a care home. The door is locked, staff decide when he may go out and he is always accompanied. He seems content. No court or other body has authorised the arrangement.* Suggest why Marcus may be deprived of his liberty in breach of Article 5. **[5]**

**11.** *Officers are told at a briefing to arrest Dev for a burglary. They are given no information about why he is suspected. Dev is arrested at his home and is not told the reason until he asks at the police station four hours later. He is held for 30 hours and then released without charge.* Advise Dev whether his rights under Article 5 have been infringed. **[8]**

**12.** Discuss whether Article 5, as applied by the European Court and by English courts, strikes an appropriate balance between personal liberty and public safety. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** B: a limited right. **[1]**

**2.** C: 5.1(c). **[1]**

**3.** The difference between deprivation and restriction is one of **degree or intensity, not nature or substance** [1]; the Court considers the **type, duration, effects and manner of implementation** of the measure [1].

**4.** A person is deprived of liberty if under **continuous supervision and control** [1] and **not free to leave** [1], even if the person is content or the arrangement is in their interests.

**5.** Article 5.2: a person arrested must be **told promptly, in a language they understand, the reasons for arrest and any charge** [1]. Article 5.5: a victim of arrest or detention in breach of Article 5 has an **enforceable right to compensation** [1].

**6.** Reasonable suspicion requires **facts or information that would satisfy an objective observer** that the person may have committed the offence [1], as stated in **Fox, Campbell and Hartley v UK (1990)** [1], where past convictions and undisclosed intelligence were not enough. In English law, **O'Hara v Chief Constable of the RUC (1997)** requires that the officer **actually suspects** and that there are **reasonable grounds** [1]; the grounds may come from a briefing by a superior, but a bare instruction to arrest is not sufficient [1].

**7.** Article 5.3 applies to a person **arrested or detained on suspicion under 5.1(c)** and requires them to be **brought promptly before a judge** or judicial officer, with trial within a reasonable time or release pending trial [1] [1]. Article 5.4 applies to **anyone deprived of liberty** on any ground [1] and gives them the right to **take proceedings in which a court decides speedily** whether the detention is lawful and orders release if it is not [1].

**8.** The House of Lords held that the **purpose** of the measure was relevant to whether it amounted to a deprivation of liberty [1]. The containment was imposed **in good faith** to deal with a **real risk of serious disorder and violence** [1]. It was **proportionate** to that risk [1] and was maintained **no longer than reasonably necessary** [1], so Article 5 was not engaged. (The European Court reached the same conclusion in Austin v UK, 2012.)

**9.** After the attacks of 11 September 2001 the UK **derogated from Article 5 under Article 15** and allowed indefinite detention without trial of foreign nationals suspected of terrorism [1]. The House of Lords accepted there was a **public emergency threatening the life of the nation** [1] but held that the measure was **disproportionate**, as it was not strictly required [1], and **discriminatory**, as it applied only to foreign nationals and not to British suspects posing the same risk [1]. It quashed the derogation order and made a **declaration of incompatibility** with Articles 5 and 14; Parliament replaced the power with **control orders** under the Prevention of Terrorism Act 2005 [1].

**10.** The facts meet the **Cheshire West acid test**: Marcus is under **continuous supervision and control** (locked door, staff decide when he goes out, always accompanied) [1] and is **not free to leave** [1]. His contentment is **irrelevant** to whether there is a deprivation [1]. As in **HL v UK (2004)**, a deprivation of a person lacking capacity must follow a **procedure prescribed by law** [1]; with no authorisation and no means of review, there is a likely breach of **Article 5.1 and 5.4** [1].

**11.** Arrest and 30 hours in custody are a **deprivation of liberty** [1]. The relevant ground is **Article 5.1(c)**, reasonable suspicion of burglary [1]. Under **O'Hara** the arresting officers must themselves suspect on reasonable grounds; a **bare instruction at a briefing with no information is not enough** [1], so the arrest may be unlawful from the start [1]. Under **Fox, Campbell and Hartley**, suspicion must satisfy an objective observer, which also appears unmet [1]. **Article 5.2**: reasons must be given promptly; telling Dev only when he asks, four hours later, is likely a breach [1]. **Article 5.3** is not engaged because Dev was released without charge without the need to bring him before a judge [1]. Conclusion: likely breaches of 5.1 and 5.2, with **compensation under Article 5.5**, through section 8 HRA damages or false imprisonment [1].

**12.** Award one mark per developed point, to a maximum of 8:
- Article 5 is **limited, not qualified**, so liberty can be removed only on listed grounds, which gives clear protection [1].
- **Fox, Campbell and Hartley** and **Brogan** show the Court insisting on objective suspicion and prompt judicial control **even in terrorism cases** [1].
- **A v SSHD (2004)** shows domestic courts scrutinising derogation for proportionality and discrimination [1].
- However, **Austin** allows long containments of peaceful people to fall outside Article 5 by looking at purpose and context, which may weaken protection for protesters [1].
- The UK response to A v SSHD was **control orders**, and JJ shows these could still be a deprivation; the rights balance shifted from detention to other restrictions [1].
- **Cheshire West** extends protection to vulnerable adults even where care is benevolent, though it increases the burden on public bodies [1].
- The **remedy** in Article 5.5 is compensation after the event; it does not prevent the detention [1].
- A **reasoned conclusion**, for example that the balance is broadly appropriate because grounds are exhaustive and safeguards strict, though crowd control cases show some flexibility [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Applying a proportionality test to the grounds in Article 5.1 as if the right were qualified.
- Skipping the question of whether there is a deprivation of liberty at all.
- Treating a briefing instruction as automatically giving reasonable grounds; O'Hara requires information.
- Confusing Article 5.3 with Article 5.4, or claiming Brogan set a precise time limit.
- Forgetting to state the remedy in Article 5.5.
