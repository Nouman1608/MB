Recall notes for **4.3.3 Particle model and pressure** (4.3.3.1 to 4.3.3.3) in AQA GCSE Physics (8463),
Paper 1. 4.3.3.2 and 4.3.3.3 are physics only; 4.3.3.3 is **Higher tier only**.

The full explanations and three worked examples are in the [particle model and pressure study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-particle-model-and-pressure/).
Practise with the [particle model and pressure practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-particle-model-and-pressure-practice/).

## The equation (on the equation sheet)

| Equation | Condition | Units |
|---|---|---|
| p V = constant, so p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ | Fixed mass of gas at constant temperature | Pa, m³ (any matching units work for a ratio) |

Pressure and volume are inversely proportional: V × 2 → p ÷ 2; V ÷ 3 → p × 3.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Random motion | Gas molecules move in all directions at a range of speeds |
| Temperature of a gas | Related to the average kinetic energy of its molecules |
| Gas pressure | Total force of molecular collisions on each unit area of a surface |
| Net force at right angles | The direction of the force a gas exerts on any surface |
| Fixed mass | No gas enters or leaves |
| Work | The transfer of energy by a force |
| Internal energy | Total kinetic and potential energy of all the particles |

## Chains of reasoning to learn

**Heating at constant volume → pressure rises**

1. Temperature up → average kinetic energy of molecules up.
2. Molecules move faster.
3. More frequent collisions with the walls, and each one harder.
4. Larger force per unit area → higher pressure.

**Increasing volume at constant temperature → pressure falls**

1. Same temperature → same average speed.
2. More space → each unit area of wall is hit less often.
3. Smaller force per unit area → lower pressure.

**Doing work on a gas (Higher tier only)**

1. Pushing a piston in transfers energy to the gas by a force: work is done on the gas.
2. Internal energy of the gas increases.
3. Average kinetic energy of the molecules rises → temperature rises.
4. Example: a bicycle pump warms as it is used.

## Worked reminders

- 200 kPa, 30 cm³ compressed to 12 cm³: p₂ = 6000 ÷ 12 = **500 kPa**.
- 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa, 0.50 m³ expanded until p = 8.0 × 10⁴ Pa: V₂ = 60 000 ÷ 80 000 = **0.75 m³**.
- Volume halved at constant temperature: pressure **doubles**.
- Data check: if p × V is the same for every row, the data fit pV = constant.

## What changes, and what does not

Match the explanation to the situation. Each row says what happens to the molecules' average speed, how often they
hit each unit area of wall, and how hard each collision is.

| Situation | Average speed | Collisions per unit area each second | Force per collision | Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heated at constant volume | Increases | Increases | Increases | Increases |
| Cooled at constant volume | Decreases | Decreases | Decreases | Decreases |
| Volume increased at constant temperature | Unchanged | Decreases | Unchanged | Decreases |
| Volume decreased at constant temperature | Unchanged | Increases | Unchanged | Increases |
| Compressed quickly (Higher tier) | Increases, because work is done on the gas | Increases | Increases | Increases more than pV = constant predicts |

## Exam traps

- **Speed trap:** at constant temperature, the molecules do **not** slow down when the volume increases. Only the
  collision rate per unit area falls.
- **Half-answer trap:** "more collisions" alone; add "more frequent" **and** "harder" for a temperature rise.
- **Condition trap:** pV = constant needs constant temperature and fixed mass.
- **Unit trap:** kPa with Pa, or cm³ with m³, in the same equation.
- **Direction trap:** gas pressure acts at right angles to the surface, not along it.
- **Particle trap:** heated molecules do not get bigger.
- **Higher-tier trap:** the pump warms because work is done on the gas, not because of "friction" alone.

## Self-test

1. Describe the motion of gas molecules.
2. What is the temperature of a gas related to?
3. Explain how gas molecules produce a pressure on a container wall.
4. A sealed rigid can of gas is heated. What happens to the pressure, and why?
5. In which direction does gas pressure act on a surface?
6. State the two conditions needed for pV = constant.
7. A gas at 150 kPa occupies 40 cm³. It is compressed slowly to 20 cm³. Find the new pressure.
8. A gas at 90 kPa in 0.60 m³ expands at constant temperature to 0.90 m³. Find the new pressure.
9. Why does increasing the volume at constant temperature reduce the pressure?
10. A volume is reduced to one quarter at constant temperature. By what factor does the pressure change?
11. (Higher tier) Define work.
12. (Higher tier) Explain why a bicycle pump gets warm when used.

### Answers

1. Constant random motion, in all directions at a range of speeds.
2. The average kinetic energy of its molecules.
3. Molecules collide with the wall and exert a force on it; the total force on each unit area is the pressure.
4. It increases: the molecules move faster, hitting the walls more often and harder.
5. At right angles to the surface.
6. Fixed mass of gas and constant temperature.
7. p₂ = (150 × 40) ÷ 20 = **300 kPa**.
8. p₂ = (90 × 0.60) ÷ 0.90 = **60 kPa**.
9. The molecules have more space, so they hit each unit area of wall less often; their speed is unchanged, so the
   force per unit area falls.
10. It increases four times.
11. The transfer of energy by a force.
12. Work is done on the gas as the piston is pushed in, increasing its internal energy, so the molecules' average
    kinetic energy and the temperature rise.
