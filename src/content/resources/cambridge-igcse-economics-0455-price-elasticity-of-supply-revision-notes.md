## 2.8.1 and 2.8.2 Definition and calculation

- **PES:** the responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price.
- **Formula:** PES = % change in quantity supplied / % change in price.
- PES is **positive**, because price and quantity supplied move together.

| PES | Type | Diagram |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Perfectly inelastic | Vertical line |
| 0 to 1 | Inelastic | Straight line meeting the quantity axis |
| 1 | Unitary | Straight line through the origin |
| Above 1 | Elastic | Straight line meeting the price axis |
| Infinite | Perfectly elastic | Horizontal line |

### Calculations

```
Price 10 -> 12 (+20%); quantity supplied 800 -> 1,040 (+30%)
PES = 30 / 20 = 1.5 (elastic)

PES = 0.6, price rises 15%: quantity supplied changes by 0.6 x 15 = +9%
From 3,000 units: 3,000 x 1.09 = 3,270 units
```

Method for full marks:

1. Formula with quantity on top.
2. Percentage changes from the original values.
3. Divide.
4. State elastic, inelastic or unitary, comparing the two percentages.

### Straight-line check

```
Q = 20 x P (through origin): P 5 -> 6 is +20%; Q 100 -> 120 is +20%; PES = 1
Q = 60 + 8 x P (meets quantity axis): P 5 -> 6 is +20%; Q 100 -> 108 is +8%; PES = 0.4
```

## 2.8.3 Determinants

| Factor | Elastic supply | Inelastic supply |
|---|---|---|
| Spare capacity | Idle machines and workers | Working at full capacity |
| Stocks | Large stocks | Few stocks |
| Storage | Easy to store | Perishable |
| Time period | Long run | Very short period |
| Production time | Quick to make | Slow (crops, buildings) |
| Mobility of factors | Factors switch easily | Specialised, immobile factors |

### Time

- Very short period: supply fixed, PES near 0.
- Short run: more variable factors (overtime, shifts), PES rises.
- Long run: new capacity and new firms, PES highest.

## 2.8.4 Significance

| Decision maker | Implication |
|---|---|
| Consumers | Inelastic supply: a rise in demand raises prices sharply (housing, festival flowers); prices unstable after supply shocks |
| Producers | Elastic supply lets firms expand and gain revenue when demand rises; stocks, spare capacity and flexible staff raise PES but cost money |
| Government | Subsidies raise output most when supply is elastic; training, quicker planning approval and storage can raise PES; inelastic public services need planning years ahead |

**Same demand rise, different PES:**

```
Price rises 10% in both markets
PES 0.3: quantity supplied +3% (mostly a price effect)
PES 3: quantity supplied +30% (mostly an output effect)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PES | Responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price |
| Elastic supply | PES greater than 1 |
| Inelastic supply | PES between 0 and 1 |
| Spare capacity | Unused ability to produce with existing resources |
| Stocks | Finished goods held ready for sale |
| Factor mobility | Ease with which factors move between uses |

## PES and PED side by side

| Feature | PED | PES |
|---|---|---|
| Measures the response of | Buyers (quantity demanded) | Producers (quantity supplied) |
| Sign | Negative | Positive |
| Main determinants | Substitutes, necessity, share of income, time, habit | Spare capacity, stocks, storage, production time, factor mobility, time |
| Vertical curve means | Perfectly inelastic demand | Perfectly inelastic supply |
| Key decision use | Pricing and revenue; which products to tax | Speed of output response; whether subsidies raise output |

## Exam traps

- A negative PES. Supply elasticity is positive.
- Price on top of the formula.
- Judging a straight supply line by steepness alone: check where it meets the axes.
- Using PED determinants (substitutes, necessity) to explain PES.
- Saying "supply is inelastic because it is a necessity". Necessity affects demand, not supply.
- Ignoring the time period: the same product can have inelastic supply this week and elastic supply in five years.

## Quick self-test

1. Define PES.
2. Price rises from 40 to 50 and quantity supplied rises from 600 to 660. Calculate PES.
3. Is supply in question 2 elastic or inelastic?
4. PES is 2. Price falls by 5%. What happens to quantity supplied?
5. What does a straight supply line through the origin show?
6. A straight supply line meets the price axis above zero. Is supply elastic or inelastic?
7. Give two reasons why supply of wheat is inelastic in the short run.
8. Why do stocks make supply more elastic?
9. Why is PES usually higher in the long run?
10. Demand for houses rises in a city where supply is inelastic. What happens to prices?
11. Why might a subsidy have little effect on output when supply is inelastic?
12. State one way a firm can make its supply more elastic.

### Answers

1. The responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price.
2. +10% / +25% = 0.4.
3. Inelastic.
4. It falls by 10%.
5. Unitary elasticity (PES = 1) at every point.
6. Elastic.
7. Any two: crops take a season to grow; land is fixed in the short run; output depends on weather.
8. Goods already made can be released for sale quickly when the price rises.
9. Firms have time to build capacity and new firms can enter.
10. Prices rise sharply, because extra homes cannot be built quickly.
11. Producers cannot quickly raise output, so the subsidy mainly lowers price or raises profit.
12. Any one: hold stocks, keep spare capacity, train multi-skilled staff, use flexible machinery.
