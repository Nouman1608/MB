## The six syllabus points

1. Receipts and payments accounts and income and expenditure accounts.
2. Preparing receipts and payments accounts.
3. Accounts for revenue-generating activities, such as refreshments and subscriptions.
4. Preparing income and expenditure accounts and statements of financial position.
5. Adjustments as in 5.1.
6. Defining and calculating the accumulated fund.

## Business words and club words

| Business | Club or society |
|---|---|
| Statement of profit or loss | Income and expenditure account |
| Profit / loss | Surplus / deficit |
| Capital | Accumulated fund |
| Cash book summary | Receipts and payments account |
| Trading account | Refreshments (bar, café) account |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Receipts and payments account | Summary of all cash and bank receipts and payments, with opening and closing balances |
| Income and expenditure account | Revenue income and expenditure for the year, on the matching basis |
| Accumulated fund | Total assets less total liabilities of the club |
| Subscriptions in arrears | Members' subscriptions for the year not yet received: a current asset |
| Subscriptions in advance | Subscriptions received for a later year: a current liability |
| Revenue-generating activity | An activity run to raise funds, such as a café, bar or event |

## Subscriptions account pattern

```
Debit                                   Credit
Opening arrears (b/d)                   Opening in advance (b/d)
Income and expenditure (balancing)      Bank (received)
Closing in advance (c/d)                Written off
                                        Closing arrears (c/d)
```

Shortcut: income = received + amounts written off − opening arrears + closing
arrears + opening in advance − closing in advance. The amount written off is added
back because it was part of the opening arrears but was never received; it is then
shown separately as expenditure.

## Receipts and payments layout

```
Receipts                          Payments
Balance b/d (cash and bank)       Revenue payments (rent, wages, suppliers)
Subscriptions received            Capital payments (equipment)
Takings from activities           Balance c/d
Donations, sale of equipment
```

If the opening balance is an overdraft, it starts on the payments side. The
closing balance is the figure for bank in the statement of financial position.

## Refreshments account pattern

```
Purchases = paid − opening suppliers owed + closing suppliers owed
Sales − (opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory) = gross profit
Gross profit − direct costs (for example bar wages) = profit (or loss) on refreshments
```

Transfer only the profit or loss to the income and expenditure account.

## Events and shows

Show the **net** result: ticket sales less the costs of the event, as a single line
of income (or expenditure, if a loss).

## Accumulated fund

```
Opening accumulated fund = opening assets − opening liabilities
Closing accumulated fund = opening fund + surplus (or − deficit)
```

Include **every** opening asset (equipment, inventory, arrears, prepayments, bank)
and **every** liability (advance subscriptions, accruals, suppliers owed, overdraft).

## Statement of financial position items

| Current assets | Current liabilities |
|---|---|
| Refreshment inventory | Refreshment suppliers owed |
| Subscriptions in arrears | Subscriptions in advance |
| Prepaid expenses (other receivables) | Accrued expenses (other payables) |
| Bank and cash | Bank overdraft |

Non-current assets (equipment, buildings) are shown at cost less depreciation or at
valuation, as for a business.

## Worked reminder

Received 14,200; arrears at start 300; in advance at start 450; arrears at end 380;
in advance at end 500. Income = 14,200 − 300 + 380 + 450 − 500 = **14,230**.

## Exam traps

- **Capital items** (equipment bought or sold) appear in receipts and payments, not
  in income and expenditure.
- **Opening bank balance** is never income.
- **Subscriptions received ≠ income for the year.**
- **Arrears written off** are expenditure, not a reduction of next year's income.
- **Café takings** are not income: transfer the café profit only.
- **Depreciation** belongs in income and expenditure (straight line, reducing
  balance or revaluation, as in 5.1).
- **Correct vocabulary** earns marks: surplus, deficit, accumulated fund.
- **Opening fund checklist:** an opening overdraft and opening subscriptions in
  advance are liabilities; opening arrears and prepaid expenses are assets.
- **Subscriptions written off** reduce the arrears asset in the statement of
  financial position.

## Self-test

1. What is the club equivalent of capital?
2. State one difference between receipts and payments and income and expenditure.
3. Where are subscriptions in advance at the year end shown?
4. Received 8,000; arrears at start 250; arrears at end 300. Calculate income.
5. Why is new equipment not in the income and expenditure account?
6. Bar purchases paid 3,600; suppliers owed 200 at start and 350 at end. Calculate
   purchases.
7. Bar sales 6,000; cost of sales 3,750; bar wages 1,500. Calculate the bar profit.
8. Opening assets 18,400; opening liabilities 900. Calculate the accumulated fund.
9. Accumulated fund 17,500 at start; deficit 1,200. Calculate the closing fund.
10. How are arrears that will never be received treated?
11. A dance raised ticket income of 1,100 and cost 1,350. How is it shown?
12. Name the account that summarises the club's cash book.

### Answers

1. The accumulated fund.
2. For example, receipts and payments includes capital items; income and expenditure
   does not.
3. Current liabilities.
4. 8,000 − 250 + 300 = **8,050**.
5. It is capital expenditure; only its depreciation is revenue expenditure.
6. 3,600 − 200 + 350 = **3,750**.
7. 6,000 − 3,750 − 1,500 = **750**.
8. **17,500**.
9. 17,500 − 1,200 = **16,300**.
10. Written off as expenditure in the income and expenditure account.
11. As a loss on the dance of **250**, under expenditure.
12. The receipts and payments account.
