## Formation in four lines

1. **Sulfur dioxide** from burning fossil fuels that contain sulfur compounds (coal, oil).
2. **Sulfur dioxide** from volcanic gases (natural source).
3. **Oxides of nitrogen** when nitrogen from the air reacts with oxygen at the high temperature of vehicle engines.
4. Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen **react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere** to form acid rain.

Extra understanding:
- Sulfur dioxide → sulfuric acid; oxides of nitrogen → nitric acid.
- Winds carry the gases far, so acid rain can fall in another country.
- Normal rain is slightly acidic; acid rain is more acidic.
- pH scale: one unit lower = ten times more acidic; two units = one hundred times.

## Impacts: what and why

- **Acidification of bodies of water**: rain falls directly and drains in from the land.
- **Reduced fish populations**: eggs and young fail; aluminium released from soils damages gills.
- **Aquatic food webs**: acid-sensitive insects, snails and plankton die → less food for fish and birds.
- **Acidification of soils**: nutrients such as calcium and magnesium washed out; aluminium released.
- **Crops and vegetation**: leaf damage, defoliation, weakened plants, reduced yields.
- **Buildings**: limestone and marble react with acid; metals corrode.

Link to remember: acid soil → aluminium → drains into lakes → harms fish.

## Strategies: what each removes

| Strategy | Removes | How |
|---|---|---|
| Flue-gas desulfurisation | Sulfur dioxide | Waste gases react with calcium carbonate or calcium oxide before leaving the chimney |
| Catalytic converters | Oxides of nitrogen | Catalyst in the exhaust converts them to nitrogen |
| Transport policies | Oxides of nitrogen | Fewer vehicle engines running: public transport, cycle lanes, driving charges, cleaner-vehicle rules |

## Discussion pairs

- **Desulfurisation**: removes most sulfur dioxide from big sources; useful by-product / expensive; limestone quarried; releases carbon dioxide; no effect on oxides of nitrogen.
- **Catalytic converters**: every fitted vehicle emits less / extra cost; older vehicles lack them; rising vehicle numbers offset gains; no effect on sulfur dioxide.
- **Transport policies**: also cut congestion and carbon dioxide / need investment and habit change; may be unpopular.
- **All**: acid rain crosses borders, so international cooperation is needed; damaged lakes and soils recover slowly.

## Calculations

- Remaining emissions = original × (1 − proportion removed). 8,000 tonnes with 95% removed leaves 400 tonnes.
- pH difference of 2 units = 10 × 10 = 100 times more acidic.
- Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100.

## Links to other sub-topics

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acid rain | Rain, snow or mist made acidic by sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen reacting in the atmosphere |
| Sulfur dioxide | Gas formed when sulfur in fuels burns; also released by volcanoes |
| Oxides of nitrogen | Gases formed when nitrogen and oxygen react at high temperature in engines |
| pH | Scale of acidity; lower numbers are more acidic |
| Acidification | A fall in pH of water or soil |
| Defoliation | Loss of leaves from plants |
| Flue | A chimney or duct that carries waste gases |
| Flue-gas desulfurisation | Removing sulfur dioxide from waste gases using calcium carbonate or calcium oxide |
| Catalytic converter | Exhaust device that removes oxides of nitrogen |
| Transport policy | A government plan to change how people travel |

## Exam traps

- Carbon dioxide is **not** a cause of acid rain in this syllabus.
- The nitrogen in oxides of nitrogen comes from the **air**, not the fuel.
- Always finish formation with "react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere".
- Desulfurisation removes **sulfur dioxide**; catalytic converters remove **oxides of nitrogen**.
- Volcanoes are a natural source of sulfur dioxide; do not leave them out of "sources" questions.
- Do not confuse acid rain with ozone depletion or the enhanced greenhouse effect.

## Self-test

1. Name the two pollutant gases that form acid rain.
2. Give the natural source of sulfur dioxide named in the syllabus.
3. Where does the nitrogen in oxides of nitrogen come from?
4. Why do vehicle engines produce oxides of nitrogen?
5. What do the pollutant gases react with to form acid rain?
6. Why can acid rain fall in a country that produces little pollution?
7. Explain one way acid rain reduces fish populations.
8. Give two effects of acid rain on soils.
9. What chemicals are used in flue-gas desulfurisation?
10. Which gas does a catalytic converter remove?
11. Give one limitation of flue-gas desulfurisation.
12. Rain at pH 4.0 is how many times more acidic than rain at pH 6.0?

## Answers

1. Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen.
2. Volcanic gases.
3. The atmosphere (air).
4. The high temperature makes nitrogen react with oxygen.
5. Oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere.
6. Winds carry the gases from other countries before they fall as acid rain.
7. Fish eggs and young fail to develop in acidic water, or aluminium released from soils damages gills.
8. Nutrients such as calcium and magnesium are washed out; aluminium is released, damaging roots.
9. Calcium carbonate or calcium oxide.
10. Oxides of nitrogen.
11. Expensive; needs quarried limestone; releases carbon dioxide; does not remove oxides of nitrogen (any one).
12. 100 times.
