## Questions

**1.** Tarnbright Tools makes hand tools. In year 1 its sales revenue was 52.0 million dollars and its R&D spending was 3.64 million. In year 2 sales revenue was 60.0 million and R&D spending was 3.9 million.

**(a)** Define research and development. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate R&D spending as a percentage of sales revenue in each year, and the percentage change in R&D spending between the years. **[4]**

**2.** Tarnbright has developed a new hinge mechanism, a logo for its premium range and a written instruction manual. State which form of intellectual property (copyright, patent or trademark) would normally protect each item. **[3]**

**3.** Explain one benefit and one limitation to Tarnbright of protecting its hinge mechanism with a patent. **[4]**

**4.** Nautilene Marine builds small boats. It is developing a quiet electric ferry for harbour towns, which operators have never asked for.

**(a)** Distinguish between incremental and disruptive innovation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the ferry may address an unmet need of which customers are not aware. **[3]**

**5.** Halmerick Garden Centres has a loyalty programme with 12,000 members. Each member spends an extra 9 dollars a month since joining, at a gross margin of 30%. Rewards cost 1.50 dollars per member a month, plus 8,000 dollars of fixed costs a month.

**(a)** Calculate the net monthly benefit of the programme. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest one reason why the figure in (a) may overstate the benefit. **[2]**

**6.** A supermarket examines 4,000 baskets. In 800 baskets there is barbecue charcoal; in 520 of these there are also sausages. Sausages appear in 1,000 baskets in total.

**(a)** Calculate the support for the pair (charcoal and sausages), and the confidence that a charcoal basket also contains sausages. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way the supermarket might use data mining to inform a decision, and one limit of the result. **[3]**
**(c)** Outline one difference between a database and big data. **[2]**

**7.** Cravenholt Media runs its own data centre at a yearly cost of 240,000 dollars. A cloud supplier would charge 6,500 dollars a month.

**(a)** Outline what is meant by cloud computing and by a data centre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the yearly saving from moving to the cloud, as a dollar amount and as a percentage of the current cost. **[3]**

**8.** Kindlewick Clinics suffered a cyber attack that exposed 18,000 patient records. Costs: 14 dollars per record to notify and support patients, 85,000 dollars for system recovery and 120,000 dollars in lost appointments.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one cybersecurity measure Kindlewick could adopt. **[2]**

**9.** Outline the internet of things and virtual reality, giving one business use of each. **[4]**

**10.** Kindlewick Clinics plans to use artificial intelligence to rank patient appointment requests.

**(a)** Outline what an artificial neural network is. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit and one ethical concern of the plan for patients. **[4]**

**11.** Thornfold Couriers equips each driver with a handheld device that records deliveries, location and time. The target is 90 parcels an hour. One driver delivered 612 parcels in 8 hours; 21 deliveries failed.

**(a)** Calculate the driver's delivery rate per hour and the rate as a percentage of the target. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the use of data to manage and monitor Thornfold's employees, including the idea of Digital Taylorism. **[8]**

**12.** Nautilene Marine has 12.0 million dollars in cash. It must choose between two projects.

Project A (disruptive design): cost 9.0 million, probability of success 0.4, total contribution 30.0 million over six years if successful, nothing if not.

Project B (incremental upgrade): cost 1.8 million, probability of success 0.8, total contribution 5.5 million if successful, nothing if not.

**(a)** Calculate the expected value of each project, allowing for its cost. **[4]**
**(b)** Recommend which project Nautilene should choose. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** R&D is work to discover new knowledge [1] and turn it into new or improved goods, services or processes [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Year 1: 3.64 ÷ 52.0 × 100 = **7.0%** [1]. Year 2: 3.9 ÷ 60.0 × 100 = **6.5%** [1]. Change in spending: (3.9 − 3.64) ÷ 3.64 × 100 [1] = **7.1%** (to 1 d.p.) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A calculate answer is easier to credit when the formula and substitution are visible. R&D as a share of sales revenue is a standard ratio, so say what you are measuring.

**2.** Hinge mechanism: **patent** [1]. Logo: **trademark** [1]. Instruction manual: **copyright** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each item needs its own match.

**3.** Benefit: it stops rivals copying the hinge without permission [1], so Tarnbright can recover its R&D costs through its own sales or by licensing the design for a fee [1]. Limitation: obtaining and enforcing a patent costs money and time [1], and enforcement may be hard in other countries, so rivals might still copy the hinge [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain answers usually need the point plus a consequence for the business. Saying a patent "protects the product" only describes.

**4. (a)** Incremental innovation improves an existing product or process in small steps [1]. Disruptive innovation creates a product or model that changes how the market works [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Operators have never asked for the ferry, so they may not realize their noise or fuel costs could fall [1]. A survey would not reveal it [1]. A prototype or trial could show the benefit and create demand [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For distinguish, write a clear difference. In (b), tie the answer to the harbour operators.

**5. (a)** Extra sales revenue = 12,000 × 9 = 108,000 dollars [1]. Extra gross profit = 108,000 × 30% = 32,400 [1]. Costs = 12,000 × 1.50 + 8,000 = 26,000 [1]. Net benefit = 32,400 − 26,000 = **6,400 dollars a month** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Members may already have been the best customers [1], so some of the extra spending would have happened anyway [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A suggest answer needs a reason that fits the data. A difference in spending is not proof of cause.

**6. (a)** Support = 520 ÷ 4,000 = **13%** [1]. Confidence = 520 ÷ 800 = **65%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Use: place sausages near the charcoal [1]; sausages are in 25% of all baskets, so 65% is stronger than chance [1]. Limit: the link does not prove a cause, so test the offer first [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** A database is an organized, searchable collection of data, usually held for a business purpose [1]. Big data is data so large, fast or varied that ordinary tools struggle with it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Support and confidence are standard data-mining measures, not guide content. Applying the result to a decision is where the AO3 credit lies.

**7. (a)** Cloud computing is storage and processing supplied over the internet, usually paid for by use [1]. A data centre is a facility holding the servers and storage that run IT systems [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Cloud cost = 6,500 × 12 = 78,000 [1]. Saving = 240,000 − 78,000 = **162,000 dollars** [1]. Percentage = 162,000 ÷ 240,000 × 100 = **67.5%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert monthly figures to years first.

**8. (a)** Notification and support = 18,000 × 14 = 252,000 [1]. Add recovery and lost appointments: 252,000 + 85,000 + 120,000 [1] = **457,000 dollars** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Measure: train staff to spot phishing, or encrypt patient records [1]. Explanation: many attacks rely on a person clicking a bad link, or encryption makes stolen files unreadable [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name one measure and say how it works.

**9.** IoT: a network of physical objects with sensors that send and receive data [1]; use: machines report wear so maintenance can be planned [1]. VR: an immersive computer-generated environment [1]; use: training staff in a safe setting, or a virtual showroom [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An outline is a brief account, so a short accurate point and a use for each is enough.

**10. (a)** A computing system loosely modelled on the brain [1] that learns patterns from examples [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Benefit: requests can be ranked quickly [1], so urgent patients may be seen sooner [1]. Concern: if the training data reflects past bias, some groups may be ranked lower [1], and patients may not be able to see how the ranking was reached [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Ethical points are credited when linked to a stakeholder, here the patients.

**11. (a)** Rate = 612 ÷ 8 = **76.5 parcels an hour** [1]. Percentage of target = 76.5 ÷ 90 × 100 [1] = **85.0%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** For: clear targets, and quick spotting of drivers who need help [1]; evidence for decisions about pay or training [1]; digital tools apply Taylor's ideas of measurable tasks and standard times, which can raise output [1]. Against: constant monitoring can cause stress and reduce trust [1]; a rate of 85.0% does not show causes such as traffic, so it may be unfair [1]; a speed target may push drivers to rush, and 21 of 612 deliveries (3.4%) failed [1]; location tracking raises privacy concerns [1]. Judgement: Thornfold gains most if it uses the data for support, counts quality as well as speed and tells drivers what is recorded [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers tend to need both sides tied to the business, and a conclusion. Using the figures from (a) shows application.

**12. (a)** Project A: 0.4 × 30.0 = 12.0 [1]; 12.0 − 9.0 = **3.0 million** [1]. Project B: 0.8 × 5.5 = 4.4 [1]; 4.4 − 1.8 = **2.6 million** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** For A: higher expected value (3.0 against 2.6 million) [1]; it could open a new market and give lasting advantage [1]. Against A: it uses 75% of the 12.0 million cash [1], and failure would lose 9.0 million, with no income to recover it [1]. For B: lower risk, using 15% of cash, with an 80% chance of success [1]. Against B: a small gain that may not protect against a rival. Recommendation: choose B, or a staged version of A, because the expected value gap is only 0.4 million while A's downside threatens the business, and the probabilities are only estimates [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Expected value does not decide the question alone. Better answers weigh it against risk and cash.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a technology without linking it to the business in the stem.
- Comparing monthly and yearly figures without converting.
- Treating a data-mining pattern, or a loyalty-programme gain, as proof of cause.
- Choosing the project with the highest expected value without weighing risk and cash.
- Giving a one-sided answer to "evaluate" or "recommend" with no conclusion.
