This guide covers **Theme 1 Population and Settlement**, for Cambridge
O Level Geography 2217, 2026 (Version 3). 2217 shares an identical
three-theme, 19-sub-topic structure with Cambridge IGCSE Geography
0460, independently confirmed against 2217's own specification PDF.
Exams for 2217 are available in the November series only.

## Where this fits in 2217

Population and Settlement is the first of three themes, alongside The
Natural Environment and Economic Development. It is the theme most
directly about people rather than physical processes, and — as with
its IGCSE sibling — every sub-topic is examined with reference to
named, real-world case studies rather than abstract theory alone.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL GEOGRAPHY 2217 — THEME 1 POPULATION AND
SETTLEMENT**

- 1.1 Population Dynamics — the causes of rapid population growth and
change, including birth and death rates
- 1.2 Migration — the causes and consequences of national and
international migration, both voluntary and forced
- 1.3 Population Structure — the causes and consequences of different
and changing population structures, read through population pyramids
- 1.4 Population Density and Distribution — the physical and human
factors influencing where people live and at what density
- 1.5 Settlements (Rural and Urban) and Service Provision — the
factors influencing the sites, growth and functions of settlements,
and the pattern and provision of services within them
- 1.6 Urban Settlements — land use patterns within urban areas, and the
problems associated with rapid urban growth, particularly in developing
countries
- 1.7 Urbanisation — the causes and consequences of urbanisation, and
strategies for managing its effects

## How to approach it

As with the IGCSE variant of this syllabus, every sub-topic here is
assessed with reference to specific named case studies, so revision
should be organised case-study first: know two or three concrete,
detailed examples per sub-topic — a real city, a real migration flow, a
real country's population structure — rather than only the abstract
definitions, since questions consistently ask candidates to "describe"
or "explain, using examples." Population pyramids deserve dedicated
practice, both as a stand-alone reading skill and as a bridge to other
sub-topics: a youthful pyramid connects directly to high birth rates in
1.1 and to pressure on services in 1.5, so practise tracing those links
across sub-topics. Because 2217 is only examined in the November
series, plan a revision timetable that works backwards from that single
sitting rather than assuming the multiple entry points some other
qualifications offer.
