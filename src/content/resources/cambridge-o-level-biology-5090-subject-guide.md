Cambridge O Level Biology (5090) is a single-tier course in the
biological sciences, covering cells, the human body, plants, inheritance
and the relationships between organisms and their environment. The
syllabus covered here applies to the 2026-2028 examination series. All
candidates sit the same papers: a multiple-choice paper worth 30%, a
theory paper worth 50%, and one practical component worth 20%, chosen
from a practical test or an alternative to practical.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus lists 19 topics. They can be read as five broad groups.

**Cells and molecules (Topics 1 to 5).** Cells (cell structure and
function, and specialised cells, tissues and organs), Classification,
Movement into and out of cells, Biological molecules, and Enzymes,
including enzyme action and the effects of temperature and pH.

**Plants (Topics 6 and 7).** Plant nutrition and Transport in flowering
plants.

**The human body (Topics 8 to 14).** Human nutrition, Human gas
exchange, Respiration, Transport in humans, Disease and immunity,
Excretion, and Coordination and control.

**Response, development and inheritance (Topics 15 to 18).** Coordination
and response in plants, Development of organisms and continuity of life,
Inheritance, and Biotechnology and genetic modification.

**Ecology (Topic 19).** Relationships of organisms with one another and
with the environment.

## How it's assessed

5090 is not tiered, so everyone takes the same papers. The assessment is
component-based, and all components are externally assessed.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Theory | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 3 | Practical Test | 90 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Practical | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

Papers 1 and 2 are taken by every candidate. Papers 3 and 4 are
alternatives: you take one of them, not both, and either one supplies
the 20% practical share of the qualification. The examination series run
in June and November. Check with your school which practical option you
are entered for, because it affects how you prepare for that 20%.

## How the parts fit together

The O Level topics are closely linked, and many exam questions draw on
more than one of them. Movement into and out of cells explains how
substances reach and leave the tissues described in the plant transport,
gas exchange, nutrition and excretion topics. Enzymes connect to human
nutrition, respiration and biotechnology. Coordination and control in
humans sits alongside Coordination and response in plants, so the two
are worth comparing directly. Development of organisms and continuity of
life leads straight into Inheritance, and both feed the final ecology
topic, where populations and environments are shaped by reproduction and
variation.

The practical component is linked to the content in the same way. Many
classic experiments, such as tests for biological molecules, enzyme
investigations at different temperatures and pH values, and work on
osmosis, come from the early topics. Whether you sit the Practical Test
or the Alternative to Practical, regular experimental work during the
course is the best preparation for it.

Two habits help across all three papers. The first is precise
terminology: biology answers often turn on the difference between words
such as diffusion and osmosis, tissue and organ, or gene and allele, and
the multiple-choice paper in particular tests these distinctions across
the whole syllabus. The second is reading data. Graphs of enzyme
activity, tables of results from transport or respiration experiments
and population data in the ecology topic all ask you to describe a
pattern and explain it with biological knowledge. Practising both habits
as each topic is taught, rather than only in revision, makes the theory
paper and the practical component far less daunting.

## Planning your study

The syllabus order is a good guide, because the first five topics are
used throughout. A general plan for a two-year course:

- **Opening months:** Topics 1 to 5, with practical work on microscopy,
  food tests, diffusion and enzymes.
- **Rest of the first year:** Topics 6 to 11, covering plants and the
  main human body systems.
- **First half of the second year:** Topics 12 to 18, from disease and
  immunity through coordination, development and inheritance to
  biotechnology.
- **Second half of the second year:** Topic 19, followed by revision
  across the whole syllabus.

Use multiple-choice and theory questions from past papers for each group
of topics as you finish it. Full timed papers belong in the final months
of revision, and the exam preparation page covers how to approach each
paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Cambridge O Level Biology 5090 tiered?**
No. 5090 is not tiered, so every candidate takes the same multiple-choice and theory papers.

**What is the difference between Paper 3 and Paper 4?**
Paper 3 is a Practical Test lasting 90 minutes, and Paper 4 is an Alternative to Practical lasting 60 minutes. Each carries 40 marks and 20% of the qualification, and you take only one.

**How long is the O Level Biology theory paper?**
Paper 2, Theory, lasts 105 minutes and carries 80 marks, making up 50% of the qualification.

**How much is the multiple-choice paper worth?**
Paper 1, Multiple Choice, lasts 60 minutes, carries 40 marks and is worth 30% of the qualification.

**How many topics does 5090 have?**
There are 19 topics, starting with Cells and ending with Relationships of organisms with one another and with the environment.
