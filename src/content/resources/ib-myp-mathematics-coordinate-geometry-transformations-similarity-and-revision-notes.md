## Key formulae

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Line | y = mx + c; m is the gradient, c the y-intercept |
| Gradient | (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) |
| Parallel lines | Same gradient |
| Perpendicular lines (extended) | m₁ × m₂ = −1, so the partner of m is −1/m |
| Distance | √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²] |
| Midpoint | ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) |
| Enlargement (extended), centre C, factor k | P′ = C + k(P − C) |
| Reflection in x = k | (x, y) → (2k − x, y) |
| Reflection in y = k | (x, y) → (x, 2k − y) |

## Methods in steps

**Equation of a line through two points**

1. Work out m from the two points.
2. Put one point into y = mx + c to get c.
3. Test the other point.

**Perpendicular bisector (extended)**

1. Find the midpoint of the segment.
2. Find its gradient, then flip and negate it.
3. Use the midpoint and the new gradient to find c.

**Rotation about a centre C**

1. Subtract C from the point.
2. Rotate about the origin.
3. Add C back.

**Finding an enlargement centre (extended)**

1. Join two object points to their images; the lines meet at the centre.
2. Scale factor = image length ÷ object length, negative if the image is on the other side of the centre.

**Proving triangles congruent**

1. List each equal pair with the reason: given, common side, vertically opposite, alternate, corresponding.
2. Name the condition: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS or RHS.
3. State the conclusion and, if needed, the consequence (for example, corresponding angles are equal).

## Rotation rules about the origin

| Turn | Mapping |
|---|---|
| 90° anticlockwise | (x, y) → (−y, x) |
| 180° | (x, y) → (−x, −y) |
| 90° clockwise | (x, y) → (y, −x) |

## Congruence and similarity conditions

| Test | Needs | Proves |
|---|---|---|
| SSS | Three pairs of equal sides | Congruent |
| SAS | Two sides and the angle they enclose | Congruent |
| ASA or AAS | Two angles and a side in matching position | Congruent |
| RHS | A right angle, equal hypotenuses, one more equal side | Congruent |
| AA | Two pairs of equal angles | Similar |
| Ratio | All three pairs of sides in the same ratio | Similar |

**Short proof.** Triangle XYZ has XY = XZ, and W is the midpoint of YZ. Given XY = XZ and YW = ZW, with XW common, triangles XYW and XZW are congruent by SSS. So angle XYW = angle XZW: the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

## Small worked reminders

**Gradient and equation.** Through (1, 7) and (5, −1): m = −8/4 = −2. Then 7 = −2 + c, so c = 9 and y = −2x + 9.

**Distance and midpoint.** (−2, 1) and (4, 9): differences 6 and 8, distance 10, midpoint (1, 5).

**Rotation.** (4, 1) turned 90° anticlockwise about (2, 2): relative (2, −1) becomes (1, 2); add (2, 2) to get (3, 4).

**Perpendicular line (extended).** Perpendicular to y = −3x + 2 through (6, 5): m = 1/3, then 5 = 2 + c, so c = 3 and y = x/3 + 3.

**Rational factor (extended).** Centre (4, −2), k = 3/4, point (8, 10): the offset (4, 12) becomes (3, 9), so the image is (7, 7).

**Similar triangles.** In triangle PQR, S is on PQ and ST is parallel to QR, with T on PR. PS = 4, SQ = 2, ST = 6. Then PQ = 6, k = 6/4 = 1.5 and QR = 1.5 × 6 = 9.

**Symmetry.** A non-square rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry and order 2. An equilateral triangle has 3 lines and order 3. A parallelogram that is not a rhombus has no lines of symmetry but order 2.

**Similarity.** Two triangles with sides 5, 7, 9 and 10, 14, 18 have the same ratio 2, so they are similar but not congruent.

**Tessellation test.** A regular polygon tessellates alone if 360 ÷ (interior angle) is a whole number. Triangle 6, square 4, hexagon 3. Pentagon: 360/108 is not whole.

**Mixed tessellations.** Angles at a point must total 360°. Three triangles and two squares give 3 × 60 + 2 × 90 = 360. Two hexagons and two triangles give 2 × 120 + 2 × 60 = 360.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Congruent or similar.** Congruent shapes match exactly. Similar shapes have the same form and a constant scale factor. All congruent shapes are similar, with k = 1.
- **Isometric or not.** Reflection, rotation and translation keep length and angle. Enlargement changes length unless k is 1 or −1.
- **Parallel or perpendicular.** Equal gradients are parallel. A product of −1 is perpendicular (extended).
- **Enlargement factor.** Between 0 and 1 gives a smaller image. Negative puts the image on the far side of the centre (extended).
- **Gradient of a vertical line.** It has no gradient; its equation is x = k. A horizontal line has gradient 0.

## Describing a transformation fully

| Type | Say |
|---|---|
| Reflection | The equation of the mirror line |
| Rotation | The centre, the angle and the direction |
| Translation | The column vector |
| Enlargement | The centre and the scale factor |

Isometric transformations give a congruent image. Check one vertex and one side length to confirm your description before you write it down. For criterion C, name the transformation first and give the details second.

## Enrichment in brief

- Dilation: the enlargement idea about a fixed centre.
- Polar coordinates: (r, θ) converts to x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ.
- Vector notation: AB is the vector from A to B, found as B − A; its magnitude is the distance AB.
- Vector spaces: a set closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the gradient and y-intercept of 2y = 8x − 10.
2. Find the line parallel to that one through (2, 9).
3. Find the distance and midpoint for (−1, −2) and (4, 10).
4. A line has gradient 2/3. What is the gradient of a perpendicular line? (extended)
5. Reflect (−3, 6) in y = x, then reflect (−3, 6) in x = 2, then reflect (−3, 6) in y = −2.
6. Rotate (2, −5) by 90° anticlockwise and by 180°, both about the origin.
7. Are triangles with sides 5, 7, 9 and 10, 14, 18 congruent, similar, both or neither?
8. Enlarge (3, 1) with centre (2, 2) and scale factor −2. (extended)
9. Explain why regular octagons cannot tessellate on their own.
10. Reflections in y = 1 and then y = 5 equal which single transformation? (extended)
11. A(−1, 3) and B(2, −1). Write AB as a column and find its magnitude. (enrichment)
12. Give the Cartesian coordinates of the polar point (8, 30°), to 3 significant figures. (enrichment)

### Answers

1. Halving gives y = 4x − 5: gradient 4, y-intercept −5.
2. m = 4, so 9 = 8 + c, giving c = 1 and y = 4x + 1.
3. Differences 5 and 12 give distance 13. Midpoint (1.5, 4).
4. −3/2.
5. (6, −3); (7, 6); (−3, −10).
6. (5, 2) and (−2, 5).
7. Similar only. The sides are in ratio 2, so they are not the same size.
8. (3, 1) − (2, 2) = (1, −1). Times −2 gives (−2, 2). Add the centre: (0, 4).
9. The interior angle is 135°. 360 ÷ 135 is about 2.67, not a whole number, so the angles cannot fit round a point.
10. A translation of 8 up, since 2 × 4 = 8. Test: y = 0 goes to 2, then to 8.
11. AB = (3, −4) as a column; magnitude √(9 + 16) = 5.
12. x = 8 cos 30° = 4√3 ≈ 6.93 and y = 8 sin 30° = 4, so (6.93, 4).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using (y₁ − y₂)/(x₂ − x₁), mixing the order of subtraction.
- Finding c but leaving the equation as y = mx or giving only m.
- Calling a quadrilateral a square or rectangle after checking sides only. Check right angles with gradients.
- Forgetting that a perpendicular gradient needs both a flip and a sign change.
- Rotating about the origin when the centre is elsewhere.
- Describing a transformation without all its details, such as a rotation with no direction.
- Naming congruence without listing the equal pairs and the reason for each.
- Matching the wrong sides when working out a scale factor; put matching sides in the same order.
- Using AAA or SSA to claim congruence.
- Claiming a shape tessellates because it "looks" like it fits, without the 360° angle argument.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the conditions for congruent triangles?**
Any one of SSS (three pairs of equal sides), SAS (two sides and the included angle), ASA or AAS (two angles and a matching side) or RHS (right angle, equal hypotenuses and one other side) proves congruence. Equal angles alone prove similarity, not congruence.

**How do you find the gradient of a perpendicular line?**
Flip the gradient and change its sign, so the perpendicular gradient to m is −1/m; the two gradients multiply to −1. In IB MYP Mathematics perpendicular gradients are extended content.

**What is an isometric transformation?**
A reflection, rotation or translation is isometric: lengths and angles do not change, so the image is congruent to the object. An enlargement changes lengths, so it is not isometric.

**Why do some shapes tessellate?**
A tessellation covers the plane with no gaps and no overlaps, so the angles at each meeting point must add to 360°. For example, six equilateral triangles meet at a point because 6 × 60° = 360°.
