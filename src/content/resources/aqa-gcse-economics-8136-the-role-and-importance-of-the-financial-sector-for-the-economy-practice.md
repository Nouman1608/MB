## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of the following sets Bank Rate in the UK? **[1]**

A: HM Treasury

B: The largest building society

C: The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee

D: The high street banks acting together

**2.** Define a financial intermediary. **[1]**

**3.** State **two** services a high street bank provides for borrowers. **[2]**

**4.** A firm has a loan of £150,000.

**(a)** The interest rate on the loan is 5% a year. Calculate the annual interest. **[1]**

**(b)** After a rise in Bank Rate, the interest rate on the loan rises to 7%. Calculate the increase in the firm's annual interest payment. **[2]**

**5.** Tom saves £8,000 in an account paying 2.5% interest a year.

**(a)** Calculate the interest he earns in one year. **[1]**

**(b)** Inflation over the year is 3%. State whether the purchasing power of his savings has risen or fallen. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** Explain why a bank run could cause a bank to fail, even if the bank's loans are likely to be repaid. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how the Bank of England helps to ensure the stability of the financial system. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how a cut in Bank Rate could lead firms to increase investment. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **one** reason why a saver might choose a building society rather than a commercial bank. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why the government relies on the financial sector. **[3]**

**11.** Read the extract.

*Greenway Cycles, a small manufacturer, asked its bank for a £40,000 loan to buy a new welding machine. The bank refused, saying the firm's sales were too uncertain.*

**(a)** Explain **one** likely effect of the refusal on Greenway Cycles. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **one** effect on the wider economy if many banks stopped lending to firms at the same time. **[3]**

**12.** "Raising Bank Rate helps savers but harms the economy." Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. HM Treasury sets the inflation target, but the Monetary Policy Committee sets the rate. **[1]**

**2.** An institution that channels money from savers to borrowers (for example, a bank lending out deposits) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: mortgages [1]; personal or business loans [1]; overdrafts; credit cards. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 0.05 × 150,000 = **£7,500** [1].

**(b)** 0.07 × 150,000 = £10,500 [1]; increase = 10,500 − 7,500 = **£3,000** [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** 0.025 × 8,000 = **£200** [1].

**(b)** **Fallen**, because prices rose by more (3%) than the interest earned (2.5%) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A bank lends out most of the deposits it holds, so it keeps only a small part as cash [1]. If many depositors try to withdraw at once, the bank cannot get its money back from long-term loans such as mortgages quickly enough [1], so it runs out of cash and cannot repay depositors, and may fail even though the loans would be repaid over time [1]. **[3]**

**7.** It regulates and supervises banks [1], for example requiring them to hold enough capital to absorb losses so they are less likely to fail [1]. It acts as lender of last resort [1], lending to banks that run short of cash in a crisis so that the failure of one bank does not spread to others [1]. **[4]**

**8.** A cut in Bank Rate leads commercial banks to lower the interest rates on business loans [1]. Borrowing to buy capital becomes cheaper [1], so more investment projects earn more than the cost of the interest [1]. Firms therefore borrow and invest more in machinery and buildings; a cut may also raise consumer spending, so firms expect higher sales [1]. **[4]**

**9.** A building society is a mutual owned by its members, so it has no shareholders to pay dividends to [1]. Profits can instead be returned to members [1], for example as higher interest rates on savings, so the saver may earn more [1]. **[3]**

**10.** The government borrows when its spending is greater than its tax revenue [1]. It does this by selling bonds (gilts), which financial institutions such as pension funds and insurers buy [1]. It also uses the banking system, including its account at the Bank of England, to pay benefits, wages and suppliers [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Without the loan the firm cannot buy the welding machine [1], so its productivity or capacity does not rise [1]; it may lose sales to rivals with better equipment and may not take on extra workers [1].

**(b)** Firms would not be able to finance investment [1], so investment, a component of demand, would fall [1]. Output would grow more slowly or fall, and firms might cut jobs, raising unemployment [1]. **[6]**

**12.** *For:*

- Higher Bank Rate means higher interest on savings accounts [1], so savers' incomes rise [1].
- Borrowing becomes dearer, so consumer spending and firms' investment fall [1], which slows growth and may raise unemployment [1].
- Homeowners with variable-rate mortgages pay more, cutting their spending power [1].

*Against:*

- A rise in Bank Rate is used to bring down high inflation towards the 2% target [1], and lower, stable inflation helps the whole economy plan and protects real incomes [1].
- Savers spend part of their extra interest, which offsets some of the fall in demand [1].

*Judgement:* For example, the view is partly right in the short run, because higher rates do reduce spending and investment, but if inflation is well above target, a rise prevents greater harm later, so whether it harms the economy depends on how high inflation is and how large the rise is [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A strong answer separates the short-run cost (slower growth) from the purpose of the policy (lower inflation), and judges which matters more in the situation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the government sets Bank Rate.
- Saying the Bank of England lends directly to households or firms.
- Explaining "people borrow less" without the chain to spending, investment and output.
- Forgetting to subtract in a "calculate the increase" question.
- Describing a building society without saying who owns it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the names of the Bank of England's committees?**
Knowing that the Monetary Policy Committee sets Bank Rate helps; the key marks come from explaining what the Bank does and why.

**Is "the financial sector" the same as "the banks"?**
Banks are the largest part, but building societies, insurers and pension funds are also in the financial sector.
