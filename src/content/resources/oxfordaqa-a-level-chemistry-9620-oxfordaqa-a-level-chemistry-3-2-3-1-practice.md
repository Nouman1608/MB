These questions cover **3.2.3.1 Trends in properties** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: electronegativity and boiling point, oxidising ability and displacement, reducing ability of halide ions with concentrated sulfuric acid, and the acidified silver nitrate test with ammonia. This is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Relative atomic masses: Na 23.0, Cl 35.5, Ag 107.9.

## Section A

**1.** Define the term *electronegativity*. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why iodine has a higher boiling point than chlorine. **[2]**

**3.** Bromine water is added to sodium iodide solution. Write an ionic equation for the reaction and state what is seen. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why chlorine is a stronger oxidising agent than bromine. **[2]**

**5.** Before adding silver nitrate to test for halide ions, the solution is acidified with dilute nitric acid. Explain why it is acidified, and why hydrochloric acid is not used. **[2]**

**6.** A solution gives a yellow precipitate with acidified silver nitrate. The precipitate does not dissolve in concentrated ammonia. Identify the halide ion. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Concentrated sulfuric acid is added to solid sodium bromide.

**(a)** Write an equation for the first (acid–base) reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the redox reaction that follows. **[1]**

**(c)** State the oxidation state of sulfur before and after the redox reaction. **[1]**

**(d)** Give two observations. **[2]**

**(e)** State the role of sulfuric acid in the reaction in (b). **[1]**

**8.** Concentrated sulfuric acid is added to solid sodium iodide.

**(a)** Name three sulfur-containing reduction products and give the oxidation state of sulfur in each. **[3]**

**(b)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of H₂SO₄ to H₂S. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why iodide ions reduce sulfuric acid further than bromide ions do. **[2]**

**9.** Write an overall equation for solid sodium iodide reacting with concentrated sulfuric acid to form sodium hydrogensulfate, iodine, sulfur dioxide and water. **[2]**

**10.** Three unlabelled solutions contain sodium chloride, sodium bromide and sodium iodide. Describe how acidified silver nitrate and ammonia solution can be used to identify each, giving the results. **[4]**

**11.** A 0.500 g mixture of sodium chloride and sodium nitrate is dissolved in water. Excess acidified silver nitrate gives 0.718 g of dry silver chloride. Calculate the percentage by mass of sodium chloride in the mixture. **[3]**

**12.** Astatine is below iodine in Group 7. Use the trends in this subtopic to predict:

**(a)** the state of astatine at room temperature **[1]**

**(b)** whether iodine would displace astatine from a solution of astatide ions, with a reason **[1]**

**(c)** how silver astatide would behave with concentrated ammonia. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The power of an atom to attract the pair of electrons in a covalent bond [1]

**2.** I₂ molecules have more electrons than Cl₂ molecules [1]; so the van der Waals forces between I₂ molecules are stronger and need more energy to overcome [1]

**3.** **Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂** [1]; the solution turns brown (from orange) [1]

**4.** Chlorine atoms are smaller, with fewer shells and less shielding [1]; so an incoming electron is attracted more strongly by the nucleus and chlorine gains electrons more readily [1]

**5.** To remove carbonate (or hydroxide) ions, which would also give a precipitate with silver ions [1]; hydrochloric acid would add chloride ions, giving a white precipitate whatever the sample contained [1]

**6.** **Iodide, I⁻** [1]

**7. (a)** **NaBr + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HBr** [1]

**(b)** **2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O** [1]

**(c)** **+6** in H₂SO₄ and **+4** in SO₂ [1]

**(d)** Any two: misty (steamy) fumes [1]; orange-brown vapour [1]; (also accept a colourless gas with a choking smell)

**(e)** **Oxidising agent** [1]

**8. (a)** **Sulfur dioxide, +4** [1]; **sulfur, 0** [1]; **hydrogen sulfide, −2** [1]

**(b)** **H₂SO₄ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ → H₂S + 4H₂O** [1]

**(c)** Iodide ions are larger, so the electron lost is further from the nucleus and more shielded [1]; it is lost more easily, so I⁻ is a stronger reducing agent and can reduce sulfur to lower oxidation states [1]

**9.** Correct formulae of all species [1]; **2NaI + 3H₂SO₄ → 2NaHSO₄ + I₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O** balanced [1]

**10.** Add dilute nitric acid then silver nitrate to a sample of each [1]; chloride gives a white precipitate, bromide cream and iodide yellow [1]; add dilute ammonia: only the white precipitate (AgCl) dissolves [1]; add concentrated ammonia: the cream precipitate (AgBr) dissolves but the yellow one (AgI) does not [1]

**11.** Mr(AgCl) = 143.4; n(AgCl) = 0.718 / 143.4 = 5.01 × 10⁻³ mol = n(NaCl) [1]; mass of NaCl = 5.01 × 10⁻³ × 58.5 = 0.293 g [1]; percentage = 0.293 / 0.500 × 100 = **58.6 %** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Sodium nitrate gives no precipitate, so all the silver chloride comes from sodium chloride.

**12. (a)** **Solid** (boiling point continues to rise down the group) [1]

**(b)** **Yes**: iodine is above astatine, so it is the stronger oxidising agent and would oxidise astatide ions to astatine (I₂ + 2At⁻ → 2I⁻ + At₂) [1]

**(c)** It would not dissolve: solubility of silver halides in ammonia decreases down the group [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving bond strength as the reason for the boiling point trend.
- Writing displacement equations with spectator ions.
- Leaving out sulfur (0) or hydrogen sulfide (−2) from the iodide products.
- Saying hydrochloric acid can be used to acidify the sample.
