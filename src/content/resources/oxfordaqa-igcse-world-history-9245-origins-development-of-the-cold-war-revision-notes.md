## The end of the war

- Different **ideologies**: capitalism and democracy against communism.
- New leaders: Truman, Attlee.
- **Yalta:** zones in Germany; free elections; UN.
- **Potsdam:** disagreements over reparations and Poland.
- **Atomic bombs:** ended the war; unsettled Stalin; started an arms race.
- Germany and Berlin divided into **four zones**.

## East-West tension, 1945-48

| Event | What happened | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Soviet expansion | Communist governments in eastern Europe | Iron Curtain |
| Czechoslovakia, 1948 | Communists seize power | Alarm in the West |
| Truman Doctrine | Aid to Greece and Turkey; containment | USA commits to resisting communism |
| Marshall Plan | Aid to rebuild Europe; 16 states accept | USSR refuses; Europe divided |
| Cominform and Comecon | Soviet organisations to control the bloc | Hardened the division |
| Tito | Expelled from Cominform in 1948 | Communist bloc not united |

## Germany

- 1948: western zones merge; new currency.
- **Blockade** (June 1948): Stalin cuts land routes into West Berlin.
- **Airlift:** supplies by air until May 1949.
- 1949: Federal Republic (May); GDR (October).
- **NATO** (April 1949): twelve states; collective defence.
- USSR's atomic bomb (1949).

## 1949-60

- **H-bomb,** 1952-53; **Warsaw Pact,** 1955; **early ICBMs;** **Sputnik 1,** 1957.
- **China:** Mao wins; USSR supports him.
- **Vietnam:** French defeat; Geneva Agreement divides the country at the 17th parallel.
- **Korea:** North invades the South; UN force under MacArthur; China enters; stalemate; armistice, 1953; Truman sacks MacArthur.
- **The Thaw:** Stalin dies; Khrushchev's peaceful co-existence; Austria neutral (1955).
- **Hungary, 1956:** Nagy; tanks on 4 November; West does not intervene.

## Writing good answers

- **3 marks:** three separate, precise points with names, dates or numbers.
- **6 marks:** one way, with knowledge, an explanation of the link and a consequence.
- **9 marks:** two separate ways, each explained; do not blend them.
- **12 marks:** the given factor, at least one other, a comparison and a supported judgement.
- Use the question words: "affected", "reasons", "how far do you agree".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideology | A set of political and economic beliefs |
| Iron Curtain | The division of Europe between the communist east and the west |
| Containment | The policy of stopping the spread of communism |
| Satellite state | A country under the control of a stronger one |
| Cominform | The 1947 organisation linking communist parties |
| Comecon | The 1949 organisation coordinating eastern bloc economies |
| NATO | The 1949 western defensive alliance |
| Warsaw Pact | The 1955 communist military alliance |
| Peaceful co-existence | Khrushchev's idea that the two systems could compete without war |
| Thaw | The easing of Cold War tension after Stalin's death |

## Exam traps

- Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan are 1947, not 1949.
- NATO (1949) came before the Warsaw Pact (1955).
- The Blockade was a response to the new currency.
- In the Korean War a UN force and South Korea fought North Korea, helped by China; the USSR supported North Korea.
- The Thaw ended in Hungary in 1956 for eastern Europe.
- In the 12-mark question, compare factors.

## Self-test

1. What did Yalta agree about Germany?
2. Why did the atomic bomb increase tension?
3. What was containment?
4. What did the Marshall Plan offer?
5. What was Comecon?
6. Why was Tito expelled from Cominform?
7. What was the Berlin Airlift?
8. Which countries were in NATO at the start?
9. Why was the Warsaw Pact created?
10. Which events led to the Korean War?
11. What was peaceful co-existence?
12. What happened in Hungary in November 1956?

## Answers

1. Germany would be divided into four zones, with Berlin also divided.
2. It showed the USA had a weapon that the USSR did not, increasing Stalin's suspicion and starting an arms race.
3. The American policy of stopping the spread of communism.
4. Economic aid to rebuild European economies.
5. The 1949 organisation that coordinated the economies of the eastern bloc.
6. He refused to follow Stalin's orders and followed an independent policy.
7. The flying of food and fuel into West Berlin during the Blockade.
8. Twelve, including the USA, Canada, Britain and France.
9. In response to West Germany's joining NATO in 1955.
10. North Korea's invasion of South Korea in June 1950.
11. The idea that communism and capitalism could compete peacefully.
12. Soviet tanks crushed the Hungarian rising.
