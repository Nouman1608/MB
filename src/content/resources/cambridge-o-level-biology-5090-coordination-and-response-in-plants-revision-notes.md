These notes condense subtopic **15.1 Coordination and response in plants** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: gravitropism, phototropism, the role of auxin in controlling shoot growth, and the investigation of tropisms. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [15.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-coordination-and-response-in-plants/). Then try the [15.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-coordination-and-response-in-plants-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Tropism | A directional growth response to a stimulus |
| Positive | Growth towards the stimulus |
| Negative | Growth away from the stimulus |
| Gravitropism | Parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity |
| Phototropism | Parts of a plant grow towards or away from light |
| Auxin | Plant growth substance made in the shoot tip that stimulates cell elongation |

## Responses of shoots and roots

| Part | Light | Gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot | Positive phototropism (towards light) | Negative gravitropism (upwards) |
| Root | Grows away or little response | Positive gravitropism (downwards) |

**Why useful:** shoots grow towards light for photosynthesis; roots grow down to anchor the plant and reach water and ions.

## Auxin: four facts for shoots

1. Made in the shoot tip.
2. Spreads through the plant from the tip.
3. Unequally distributed in response to light and gravity.
4. Stimulates cell elongation.

## Explaining the bending

| Stimulus | Where auxin collects | What happens | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light from one side | Shaded side of the shoot | Cells on the shaded side elongate more | Shoot bends towards the light |
| Shoot on its side (gravity) | Lower side of the shoot | Cells on the lower side elongate more | Shoot bends upwards |

**Pattern for any answer:** stimulus, unequal auxin, unequal elongation, bending.

## The role of the tip

- Remove the tip: no bending and little growth, because the auxin source is gone.
- Cover the tip with foil: no bending towards the light, because the tip detects the light.
- Cover the part below the tip only: the shoot still bends towards the light.

## Investigating tropisms

| Investigation | Method | Control | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phototropism in shoots | Seedlings in a box with a slit for light from one side | Seedlings with light from all sides or on a clinostat | Test shoots bend towards the slit |
| Gravitropism in shoots and roots | Germinating seeds on damp paper in a dish, placed on its side in the dark | Seedlings on a clinostat | Roots curve down; shoots curve up |

Keep constant: type and age of seedling, temperature, water supply and time. Measure the angle of curvature with a protractor, repeat and calculate a mean.

## Interpreting shoot diagrams

| Diagram shows | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shoot bent towards the lit side | Auxin was on the shaded side; positive phototropism |
| Shoot with a foil cap on the tip, grows straight up | The tip detects light; no unequal auxin |
| Shoot with the tip removed, no growth | Auxin source removed |
| Shoot with an agar block of auxin on one side, bent away | Cells on the block side elongated more |
| Shoot on a clinostat, grows straight | Stimulus equal on all sides |

## Comparing plant and animal responses

| Feature | Plant tropism | Reflex in an animal |
|---|---|---|
| Control by | Chemicals (auxin) | Nerve impulses |
| Speed | Slow (hours or days) | Rapid |
| Response | Directional growth | Muscle contraction or gland secretion |

## Planning an investigation: checklist

1. State what is changed (the direction of the light, or the position of the seed).
2. State what is measured (the angle of curvature, or the direction of growth).
3. Set up a control (clinostat, or light from all sides).
4. Keep constant: seedling type and age, temperature, water, time.
5. Repeat with several seedlings and calculate a mean.
6. Record the results in a table.

## Worked calculation

*Four shoots bent towards the light by 28, 34, 30 and 36 degrees. Find the mean angle.*

1. Total = 28 + 34 + 30 + 36 = 128 degrees.
2. Mean = 128 divided by 4 = 32 degrees.

A control group on a clinostat bending by a mean of 2 degrees gives a difference of 30 degrees.

## Short model answers

**Define phototropism.** A response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from light.

**Explain why a shoot grows towards light.** Auxin made in the tip moves to the shaded side. The cells on the shaded side elongate more, so the shoot bends towards the light.

**Explain why a shoot placed on its side grows upwards.** Auxin collects on the lower side. The cells there elongate more, so the shoot bends upwards.

**State the role of auxin.** It stimulates cell elongation in the shoot.

**Suggest why roots grow downwards.** Positive gravitropism takes the roots into the soil, where they anchor the plant and absorb water and ions.

## Exam traps

- Auxin moves to the **shaded** side, not the lit side.
- Auxin stimulates **elongation**, not cell division.
- Say that the shoot **grows** towards the light; it does not move.
- Shoots are negatively gravitropic; roots are positively gravitropic.
- The tip makes auxin; removing it stops the response.
- A good investigation has a control, and keeps other variables constant.
- Include the word "unequal" when describing auxin distribution.
- The experiment on gravitropism is done in the dark to remove light as a stimulus.

## Self-test

1. What is a tropism?
2. Define gravitropism.
3. Define phototropism.
4. How do shoots respond to light?
5. How do roots respond to gravity?
6. Where is auxin made?
7. What does auxin do to cells in a shoot?
8. Where does auxin collect when light shines from one side?
9. Why does a shoot placed on its side bend upwards?
10. What is the result of removing the shoot tip?
11. Why is a gravitropism test kept in the dark?
12. What is the purpose of a clinostat?

### Answers

1. A directional growth response to a stimulus.
2. A response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity.
3. A response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from light.
4. They grow towards it (positive phototropism).
5. They grow towards gravity, downwards (positive gravitropism).
6. In the shoot tip.
7. It stimulates cell elongation.
8. On the shaded side.
9. Auxin collects on the lower side, so those cells elongate more and the shoot bends up.
10. There is little growth and no bending, because the auxin source is removed.
11. To prevent light from acting as a stimulus.
12. It rotates the plant so gravity acts equally on all sides, acting as a control.
