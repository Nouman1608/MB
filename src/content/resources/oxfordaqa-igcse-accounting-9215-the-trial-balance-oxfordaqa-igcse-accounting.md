This study guide covers the second content point of section 3.2 Verification of accounting records in the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification (version 1.2): "Prepare and understand the use of a trial balance". The specification includes "the preparation of a trial balance from a list of balances and the correction of a trial balance", and "explaining the use and limitations of a trial balance". All amounts on this page are in dollars.

The [double entry verification and trial balance guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-verification-of-double-entry-records-and-the-trial-bala/) introduces the layout and the ten named error types. This page goes further on the preparation skills that cost marks: balancing ledger accounts to get the balances, placing the balances that students often misplace, finding a missing capital figure, and redrafting a trial balance that has items in the wrong column. Practise afterwards with the [trial balance revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/) and the [trial balance practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/).

## Step 1: getting the balances

A trial balance lists **balances**, so each ledger account must first be balanced off at the end of the period.

```
1. Add both sides of the account.
2. Write the difference on the SMALLER side as "Balance c/d", so both sides total the same.
3. Rule off and write the equal totals on both sides.
4. Bring the difference down on the OTHER side as "Balance b/d" on the first day of the next period.
```

The side on which the balance b/d appears is the side it goes in the trial balance.

### Worked example 1

The bank columns of a cash book for March:

```
Dr                         Bank                          Cr
 1 Mar  Balance b/d     2,400  |  4 Mar  Rent             900
 8 Mar  Sales           1,850  | 15 Mar  Purchases      2,720
22 Mar  K Osei            960  | 28 Mar  Wages          1,100
                               | 31 Mar  Balance c/d      490
                        5,210  |                        5,210
 1 Apr  Balance b/d       490  |
```

Debits total 5,210 and credits 4,720. The difference, 490, is written on the credit side as balance c/d and brought down as a **debit** balance. The bank is in funds, so 490 goes in the debit column of the trial balance. If the credit side had been larger, the balance b/d would be a credit: an overdraft, listed as a credit.

## Step 2: placing every balance

The rule comes from the accounting equation and the double entry: **assets, expenses and drawings have debit balances; liabilities, capital and income have credit balances**. Most items are obvious. These are the ones examiners use to separate candidates.

| Balance | Column | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Discount allowed | Debit | An expense: income given up to encourage prompt payment |
| Discount received | Credit | Income: a reduction in what the business pays |
| Carriage inwards | Debit | An expense, added to the cost of purchases |
| Carriage outwards | Debit | An expense of selling and delivering goods |
| Sales returns (returns inwards) | Debit | Reduces sales income |
| Purchases returns (returns outwards) | Credit | Reduces the cost of purchases |
| Rent received, commission received | Credit | Income |
| Irrecoverable debts | Debit | An expense |
| Accumulated depreciation | Credit | Reduces the asset it relates to; kept as a separate credit balance |
| Loan interest | Debit | An expense |
| Loan | Credit | A liability |
| Bank overdraft | Credit | A liability: money owed to the bank |
| Inventory at the start of the year | Debit | An asset brought forward from the previous year |

Both carriage accounts are debits. The difference between them matters later, in the income statement, not in the trial balance.

## Step 3: building the trial balance

### Worked example 2

The balances of Halvorsen Bikes at 31 December are listed in no order: sales 98,750; premises 36,000; trade payables 6,940; purchases 52,600; discount received 820; carriage outwards 950; equipment 14,000; accumulated depreciation of equipment 5,600; inventory at 1 January 6,200; bank overdraft 2,380; wages 18,300; sales returns 1,240; commission received 1,500; trade receivables 7,350; loan (repayable in five years) 15,000; carriage inwards 680; loan interest 900; purchases returns 1,460; discount allowed 610; insurance 2,150; irrecoverable debts 330; cash 420; drawings 9,600; capital (not given).

```
Total debits                          151,330
Credits other than capital            132,450
Capital = 151,330 − 132,450 =          18,880
```

Using capital as the balancing figure is acceptable only when the question says capital is missing and that the records are correct. It assumes the trial balance would otherwise agree.

Group the accounts in a sensible order (non-current assets, current assets, liabilities, trading items, other income and expenses, drawings and capital). It makes adding easier and makes the trial balance ready to use for the financial statements.

## Step 4: correcting a trial balance with items in the wrong column

The specification includes "the correction of a trial balance". A common question gives a draft prepared by someone else and asks you to redraft it.

### Worked example 3

A trainee prepared the trial balance of Petrova Florists. The debit column totals 66,090 and the credit column 61,910. On checking, four balances were in the wrong column:

- discount received 540 was in the debit column;
- accumulated depreciation 3,200 was in the debit column;
- carriage outwards 760 was in the credit column;
- sales returns 890 was in the credit column.

```
Difference in the draft = 66,090 − 61,910 = 4,180

Moving a balance across changes the difference by TWICE its value.
Net amount wrongly in the debit column = 540 + 3,200 − 760 − 890 = 2,090
2 × 2,090 = 4,180, which explains the whole difference.

Corrected debit total  = 66,090 − 540 − 3,200 + 760 + 890 = 64,000
Corrected credit total = 61,910 + 540 + 3,200 − 760 − 890 = 64,000
```

When you redraft, rewrite every balance in its correct column and add both columns again; do not only adjust the totals. The redrafted trial balance is what the mark scheme credits.

## Use and limitations

| Use | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Checks the arithmetic of the double entry: total debits = total credits | Only compares totals, so errors that keep the totals equal (commission, complete reversal, compensating, omission, original entry, principle) are not revealed |
| Lists every balance in one place, ready for preparing the income statement and statement of financial position | Does not show where an error is, only that one exists |
| A difference warns that an error exists before the financial statements are prepared | Is correct only at the date it is drawn up |

## Common mistakes

- Putting the balance c/d on the larger side, so the account fails to balance.
- Listing carriage outwards or discount allowed as credits.
- Listing accumulated depreciation as a debit, or subtracting it from the asset inside the trial balance.
- Treating the bank overdraft as a debit because "bank" is usually an asset.
- Including closing inventory as well as opening inventory. At this stage the ledger holds the opening inventory.
- Using capital as a balancing figure when the question does not say it is missing.
- Correcting only the totals of a draft instead of redrafting the whole trial balance.

## Exam technique

- **"Prepare a trial balance"**: give a heading with the business name and date, two labelled columns, and both totals; marks are usually given for groups of correctly placed balances.
- **"Calculate the capital"**: show total debits, total credits without capital, and the subtraction.
- **"Explain the use"** or **"limitations"**: give a use with its benefit, and a limitation with a named error type as an example.
