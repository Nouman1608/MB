## Core recall

### What the specification lists

- Internal organisational structures, span of control, chain of command, delayering and delegation.
- Why businesses have structures, including different job roles and responsibilities.
- The impact of a tall or flat structure on how a business is managed.
- How structure affects the different ways of communication.
- Appropriateness of structures; centralisation and decentralisation.

### Why have a structure

Clear roles and responsibilities; known reporting lines; no missed or duplicated tasks; managers know who they are responsible for; visible promotion routes; easier to plan growth.

### Job roles, top to bottom

| Role | Focus |
|---|---|
| Directors | Strategy and long-term aims |
| Senior managers | Plans and budgets for a function |
| Middle managers, supervisors | Day-to-day organisation of a department or team |
| Team leaders | Leading a small group while doing the work |
| Operational staff | Day-to-day tasks |
| Support staff | IT, administration, cleaning and other services |

### Four key terms

- Chain of command: line of authority from top to bottom.
- Span of control: number of people reporting directly to one manager.
- Delegation: passing authority for a task to someone lower down; the manager stays accountable.
- Delayering: removing management levels; shorter chain, wider spans.

### Tall against flat

| | Tall | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Levels | Many | Few |
| Chain of command | Long | Short |
| Span of control | Narrow | Wide |
| Supervision | Close | Light |
| Decisions | Slow | Fast |
| Management cost | High | Low |
| Promotion steps | Many | Few |
| Communication | Slower, risk of distortion | Faster, more direct |

### Ways of communication

- Vertical (up and down the chain) and horizontal (same level).
- Formal (official channels) and informal (unofficial contact).
- Tall: vertical messages pass many levels, causing delay and distortion; senior managers can lose touch.
- Flat: fewer links, faster messages; but managers have less time per person.

### Centralised against decentralised

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | Senior managers, head office | Local or department managers |
| Benefits | Consistency, control, whole-business view | Speed, local knowledge, motivation |
| Drawbacks | Slow, out of touch locally, staff less trusted | Inconsistency, harder to control, training needed |

### Appropriateness checklist

Size; type of work; skills of staff; speed of change in the market; need for consistency.

### Quick worked reminder

Figures invented. A business has an owner, 3 managers and 24 staff split equally. The owner's span is 3; each manager's span is 8; there are 3 levels. If the owner removes the managers, the owner's span becomes 24 and there are 2 levels.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational structure | The way jobs, authority and communication are arranged in a business |
| Organisation chart | A diagram showing roles and reporting lines |
| Hierarchy | The levels of authority in a business |
| Chain of command | The line of authority from the top to the bottom |
| Span of control | The number of people reporting directly to one manager |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to someone lower in the structure |
| Delayering | Removing levels of management |
| Tall structure | Many levels, narrow spans, long chain of command |
| Flat structure | Few levels, wide spans, short chain of command |
| Centralisation | Keeping decision-making with senior managers |
| Decentralisation | Passing decision-making to lower or local managers |

## Exam traps

- **Span vs levels.** Span is direct reports; levels are layers of hierarchy.
- **Delayering as sacking workers.** It removes management layers.
- **Flat is always best.** Wide spans can overload managers.
- **Delegation vs decentralisation.** Individual tasks versus the whole pattern of decision-making.
- **Accountability.** Delegating does not remove the manager's accountability.
- **Communication without a direction.** Say vertical or horizontal, formal or informal.

## Self-test

1. Define span of control.
2. Define chain of command.
3. What is delegation, and who remains accountable?
4. Give two features of a tall structure.
5. Give one benefit and one drawback of delayering.
6. Why might communication be slower in a tall structure?
7. Give one benefit of a centralised structure.
8. Give one benefit of decentralisation for a chain of shops.
9. A manager has 6 supervisors, each with 7 workers. What is the manager's span of control, and how many workers are there in total?
10. What is the difference between formal and informal communication?
11. Name two job roles and describe their responsibilities.
12. Why might a fast-growing technology start-up choose a flat structure?

### Answers

1. The number of people who report directly to one manager.
2. The line of authority from the top of the business to the bottom.
3. Passing authority for a task and its decisions to someone lower in the structure; the manager remains accountable.
4. Any two: many levels, narrow spans, long chain of command, close supervision.
5. Benefit: lower salary costs or faster communication. Drawback: wider spans overload managers, or fewer promotion routes.
6. Messages pass through many levels, causing delay and the risk of distortion.
7. Consistent decisions, or tight control of costs and brand.
8. Local managers decide quickly, using knowledge of their own customers.
9. Span of 6; 6 × 7 = 42 workers.
10. Formal uses official channels such as meetings and reports; informal is unofficial contact such as conversations.
11. For example: directors set strategy; supervisors organise day-to-day work of a team.
12. It needs quick decisions, has skilled staff who can work independently, and keeps management costs low.
