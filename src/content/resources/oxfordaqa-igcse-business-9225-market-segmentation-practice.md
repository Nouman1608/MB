All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Cinder Fitness.

> **Case study: Cinder Fitness**
>
> Cinder Fitness is a gym in a town of 60,000 people. The owner has
> divided the town into three age segments and estimated the proportion
> likely to join in each:
>
> | Age group | People in town | Percentage likely to join |
> |---|---|---|
> | 18 to 30 | 15,000 | 10% |
> | 31 to 50 | 20,000 | 6% |
> | 51 and over | 25,000 | 2% |
>
> The monthly membership fee is $35. The owner has $18,000 to spend on
> a promotion campaign. She is considering a social media campaign aimed
> at 18 to 30-year-olds or newspaper adverts aimed at the over 51s.
> About 55% of members are women, and the gym's classes are mainly
> in the evening.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of segmentation by income? **[1]**
- A. Selling luxury watches to high earners
- B. Selling raincoats in a wet region
- C. Selling toys to children
- D. Selling razors to men

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a niche market? **[1]**
- A. A product sold to everyone in a country
- B. A small, specialised group of customers
- C. A national supermarket chain
- D. All customers in a region

**3.** Define the term "market segmentation". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** types of market segmentation. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business might segment its market by location. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the number of people likely to join in each age segment, and the total. **[3]**

**7.** Calculate the 18 to 30 segment's share of all likely members, as a percentage to one decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the annual fee income from the likely members in the 18 to 30 segment. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the cost per member if the social media campaign wins the 18 to 30 segment's likely members. **[2]**

**10.** Recommend **one** marketing method for the 51 and over segment and explain why it is suitable. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways in which segmenting by gender could help Cinder Fitness. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Cinder Fitness should aim its campaign at the 18 to 30 segment. Use your answers to Questions 6 to 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Dividing a market [1] into groups of customers who have similar characteristics or needs [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: age [1]; gender [1]; location [1]; income [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Customers in different places have different needs because of climate or culture [1], so the business can offer products and promotion that suit each area, such as raincoats in a wet region [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 18 to 30: 10% x 15,000 = 1,500 [1]; 31 to 50: 6% x 20,000 = 1,200 [1]; 51 and over: 2% x 25,000 = 500; total = **3,200** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** 1,500 / 3,200 x 100 [1] = **46.9%** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Annual fee = $35 x 12 = $420 [1]; income = 1,500 x $420 = **$630,000** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** $18,000 / 1,500 [1] = **$12** per member [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Newspaper adverts, or direct mail, or leaflets in local clinics [1], because older people are less likely than young adults to find information on social media [1], and local papers reach them in their own area [1]. Accept another suitable method with a reason. **[3]**

**11.** **Targeting promotion:** with 55% of members women, Cinder could advertise classes and facilities that appeal to women, in magazines or online communities they use [1], so the advertising reaches more likely members [1].
**Product and timing:** it could create women-only sessions or evening classes [1], which would meet the needs of that segment and increase membership [1]. Accept other valid points about avoiding stereotypes with a reason [1] [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **For the 18 to 30 segment:** it has the most likely members (1,500, or 46.9% of the total) [1]; the cost per member is only $12 [1]; fee income from the segment would be $630,000 a year, much more than the cost of the campaign [1]; young adults can be reached easily by social media [1].
**Against:** a campaign aimed at one segment may miss the 31 to 50 segment (1,200 likely members), who might be better long-term members [1]; the percentages are only estimates [1]; members may leave after a short time, so the income may be lower than $630,000 [1].
**Judgement:** the campaign should target the 18 to 30 segment, because it has the most likely members at a low cost per member [1]. Cinder should check the results and use some of the budget later for the 31 to 50 segment, since it is also large [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole town's population of 60,000 instead of the people likely to join.
- Forgetting to multiply the monthly fee by 12 to find the annual fee.
- Dividing the campaign cost by the segment's population (15,000) instead of the likely members (1,500).
- Naming a marketing method without saying why it suits the segment.
- Describing segmentation without using the case study figures.
