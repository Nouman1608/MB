This set tests section 4.1 **The role of government** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: the role of government locally, nationally and internationally. Countries and towns are invented. Teaching is in the [role of government study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-role-of-government/) and the [role of government revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-role-of-government-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is most likely to be a role of local government?

A: Setting the national rate of income tax

B: Granting planning permission for a new shop

C: Negotiating a trade agreement with another country

D: Running the armed forces

**[1]**

**2.** Which problem most clearly needs international action?

A: Litter in a town centre

B: Long waiting times at a national hospital

C: Overfishing of a stock shared by several countries

D: A shortage of teachers in one region

**[1]**

**3.** State two roles of national government in an economy. **[2]**

**4.** Identify the role of government shown in each case.

**(a)** A government pays pensions funded by a progressive income tax. **[1]**

**(b)** A government sets maximum levels of chemicals allowed in drinking water. **[1]**

**(c)** A government owns and runs the national railway company. **[1]**

**5.** State two ways in which local government may be funded. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why defence is provided by national government rather than by private firms. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Read the passage (original, written for this practice set).

*Town M's council collects waste and runs a weekly market. Last year a new road built by the national government brought more visitors, and the market grew. The country has also signed an agreement with its neighbours to cut tariffs on farm goods, and Town M's farmers now sell more abroad.*

**(a)** Identify one role of local government and one role of national government from the passage. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the international agreement affects Town M's farmers. **[2]**

**8.** Explain two advantages of making some decisions at local rather than national level. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why a national government, rather than local government, is responsible for managing the economy as a whole. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why countries may join international organisations such as the World Trade Organization. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse how local, national and international government could each help to reduce air pollution from factories near a border. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether the problem of climate change is best dealt with nationally or internationally. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. A and D are national roles; C is an international role. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. A shared stock can only be protected if all the countries fishing it agree limits. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: making laws and running courts; defence; providing national health and education systems; regulating firms (for example competition or safety law); redistributing income through taxes and benefits; managing the economy [1 each]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Redistributor. [1]

**(b)** Regulator. [1]

**(c)** Producer (state-owned enterprise). [1]

**5.** Any two: local taxes, such as property taxes; charges for services; grants from national government [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.** Defence is a public good: once a country is protected, non-payers cannot be excluded [1], so no private firm could charge for it and make a profit; national government provides it, funded by taxes [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Local: collecting waste or running the market [1]. National: building the road [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Lower tariffs make Town M's farm goods cheaper for buyers in neighbouring countries [1], so demand for their exports rises and farmers' sales and incomes increase [1]. **[2]**

**8.** First: local officials know local needs and conditions [1], so spending can go where it is most needed, such as the most congested road [1]. Second: decisions can be made more quickly [1], and residents can hold local councillors to account if services are poor [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Problems such as recession, unemployment and inflation affect the whole country [1]. Only national government has the power to set national taxes and the budget and to borrow on a large scale [1], and policy must be consistent everywhere; one local council acting alone could not change total spending in the economy [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Trade crosses borders, so each country benefits from agreed rules that apply to all members [1]. Membership can reduce barriers to its exports in other countries [1], and gives a way to settle trade disputes without each side raising barriers against the other [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Local: the council can inspect factories, refuse permission for polluting expansions and monitor air quality [1]. National: the government can set legal limits on emissions for all factories and fine firms that break them [1], so firms cannot avoid rules by moving to another district [1]. International: pollution blows across the border, so neighbouring countries need to agree common limits [1]; otherwise one country's strict rules could be undermined by factories just across the border [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Nationally: each government can set its own taxes on pollution, subsidise clean energy and regulate its own firms [1]; it can act quickly without waiting for agreement [1]. But climate change is caused by emissions from all countries and affects all of them [1], so one country cutting emissions alone bears the cost while others share the benefit, and firms may move production to countries with weaker rules [1]. Internationally: agreement shares the cost and makes it harder for firms to escape rules [1]. Judgement: international action is essential because the problem crosses every border, but it only works if national governments carry out what they agree, so both levels are needed [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers use the idea that one country acting alone pays the cost while others share the benefit. That is the economic reason for international action.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing only about national government when the question names another level.
- Giving a role without an example or a reason.
- Confusing the role of government with the policy tools it uses.
- Not using the passage when asked to identify from it.
- Saying international organisations "control" countries.
- Ending a discussion without a judgement.
