## The outcome in one line

Describe the characteristics of living organisms: movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition, each by its syllabus definition.

## The definitions, with the words that score

| Characteristic | Syllabus definition | Key words to include |
|---|---|---|
| Movement | An action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place | "part of an organism"; "change of position or place" |
| Respiration | The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism | "chemical reactions"; "in cells"; "release energy" |
| Sensitivity | The ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment | "detect **and** respond"; "internal or external" |
| Growth | A permanent increase in size and dry mass | "permanent"; "dry mass" |
| Reproduction | The processes that make more of the same kind of organism | "same kind of organism" |
| Excretion | The removal of the waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements | "waste products of metabolism"; "in excess of requirements" |
| Nutrition | The taking in of materials for energy, growth and development | "materials"; "energy, growth and development" |

**Memory hook:** MRS GREN gives the names; the definitions earn the marks.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metabolism | All the chemical reactions that take place in an organism |
| Dry mass | The mass of an organism or tissue after all its water has been removed |
| Nutrient molecules | Substances such as glucose that cells break down in respiration |
| Egestion | Removal of undigested food as faeces; not excretion |
| Breathing | Movement of air into and out of the lungs; not respiration |
| Internal environment | Conditions inside the body, such as temperature and blood glucose concentration |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Respiration vs breathing:** respiration is chemical and happens in every cell; breathing is a physical movement of air.
- **Excretion vs egestion:** excretion removes waste **made by metabolism** (carbon dioxide, urea); egestion removes undigested food that was never part of the body's cells.
- **Growth vs swelling:** growth is permanent and increases dry mass; soaking up water is neither.
- **Release vs make:** respiration **releases** energy; energy is never "made".
- **All seven:** a living thing shows every characteristic; showing one or two is not enough.

## The characteristics in plants

| Characteristic | Example in a plant |
|---|---|
| Movement | Leaves turn towards light; flowers open and close; roots grow down |
| Respiration | Every living cell, day and night |
| Sensitivity | Shoots respond to light; roots respond to gravity |
| Growth | New cells at root and shoot tips, increasing dry mass |
| Reproduction | Seeds from flowers; many plants also reproduce asexually |
| Excretion | Oxygen from photosynthesis; carbon dioxide from respiration |
| Nutrition | Takes in carbon dioxide, water and mineral ions; makes food by photosynthesis |

## Testing "is it alive?"

1. List the characteristics the object seems to show.
2. Find at least one it clearly lacks, using the definition (for example, no cells, so no respiration; no permanent increase in dry mass; cannot make more of the same kind).
3. Conclude: living things show all seven.

Common examples: a car, fire, a crystal growing in solution, a robot, a cloud.

## Exam traps

- "Respiration produces energy": write **releases**.
- "Growth is getting bigger": add **permanent** and **dry mass**.
- "Sensitivity is reacting": add **detect** and name the change.
- "Plants do not move": parts of plants do.
- "Faeces are excreted": they are **egested**.
- A water flea swimming towards light shows movement and sensitivity; choose the one the wording stresses.
- **Describe** means give the definition, not just the name.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the syllabus definition of growth.
2. Why is dry mass used instead of total mass?
3. State where respiration takes place.
4. Correct this sentence: "Respiration makes energy for the body."
5. Name two waste products of metabolism excreted by humans.
6. Why is passing faeces not excretion?
7. Give one example of movement in a plant.
8. A plant shoot grows towards a window. Which characteristic is shown?
9. A sponge soaked in water gets bigger. Why is this not growth?
10. Name two substances a plant takes in for nutrition.
11. Give one characteristic of life that fire does not show, and explain why.
12. What two things must an organism do to show sensitivity?

### Answers

1. A permanent increase in size and dry mass.
2. Water content changes from hour to hour, so only an increase in dry mass shows new material has been made.
3. In cells.
4. Respiration is the chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and **release** energy for metabolism.
5. Carbon dioxide and urea.
6. Faeces are mainly undigested food that was never made by the body's metabolism, so their removal is egestion.
7. Any one: leaves turning towards light; flowers opening or closing; roots growing downwards.
8. Sensitivity (it detects light and responds by growing towards it).
9. The increase is temporary and is only water, so there is no permanent increase in dry mass.
10. Any two: carbon dioxide, water, mineral ions.
11. For example, reproduction: fire cannot make more of the same kind of organism because it is not an organism; or respiration: fire has no cells.
12. Detect a change in the environment and respond to it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the characteristic without its definition when asked to describe.
- Missing "permanent" or "dry mass" from growth.
- Confusing respiration with breathing, or excretion with egestion.
- Saying energy is produced.
