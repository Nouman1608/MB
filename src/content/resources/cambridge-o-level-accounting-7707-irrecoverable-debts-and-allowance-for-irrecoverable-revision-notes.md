## What the syllabus asks

1. Meaning of irrecoverable debts and irrecoverable debts recovered.
2. Journal entries and ledger accounts for irrecoverable debts.
3. Journal entries and ledger accounts for irrecoverable debts recovered.
4. The need for an allowance for irrecoverable debts.
5. Journal entries and ledger accounts for creating and adjusting the
allowance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Irrecoverable debt | Amount owed by a credit customer that will not be paid and is written off |
| Writing off | Removing the debt from the customer's account and charging it as an expense |
| Irrecoverable debt recovered | Payment received for a debt written off in an earlier period |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Estimate of the part of remaining trade receivables that may not be collected |
| Increase in allowance | New allowance − old allowance, charged as an expense |
| Decrease in allowance | Old allowance − new allowance, credited as income |

## Entries at a glance

| Event | Debit | Credit | Statement of profit or loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Write off a debt | Irrecoverable debts | Customer | Expense |
| Year-end transfer of write-offs | Statement of profit or loss | Irrecoverable debts | Expense |
| Reinstate a recovered debt | Customer | Irrecoverable debts recovered | Income |
| Receive the recovered amount | Bank | Customer | (none: cash entry) |
| Create allowance | Statement of profit or loss | Allowance | Expense (full amount) |
| Increase allowance | Statement of profit or loss | Allowance | Expense (increase only) |
| Decrease allowance | Allowance | Statement of profit or loss | Income (decrease only) |

## Why keep an allowance

- **Prudence**: receivables shown at the amount expected to be collected.
- **Matching**: the probable loss is charged in the year of the credit
sales.
- **Realistic information** for users of the statement of financial
position.

## Three-line method

```
Trade receivables after write-offs          X
Allowance required (X × %)                  A
Change = A − last year's allowance          +/− (expense if +, income if −)
```

**Quick example.** Receivables $54,000 before writing off $1,500. Allowance
4%; last year $1,950.
Receivables after write-off: $52,500. Allowance: $2,100. Increase: $150
(expense). Irrecoverable debts expense: $1,500.

## Statement of financial position

```
Trade receivables (after write-offs)        52,500
Less Allowance for irrecoverable debts       2,100
                                            ------
                                            50,400
```

The whole allowance is deducted; only its change goes to the statement of
profit or loss.

## Allowance account shape

- Credit balance brought down = last year's allowance.
- Increase: credit side, "Profit or loss".
- Decrease: debit side, "Profit or loss".
- Balance carried down (debit side) = this year's allowance.

## Two-year mini-case

Year 1: receivables after write-offs 8,000; allowance created at 5% =
00 (expense 00). Year 2: irrecoverable debts 00 written off; remaining
receivables 6,000; allowance 00, a decrease of 00 (income). Year 2
profit is reduced by 00 − 00 = 00 overall.

## Effect of mistakes on the statements

| Mistake | Profit | Net trade receivables |
|---|---|---|
| Debt not written off | Overstated by the debt, less the extra allowance charged on it | Overstated by the same amount |
| Full allowance charged instead of an increase | Understated by last year's allowance | No effect |
| Increase in allowance not recorded | Overstated by the increase | Overstated by the increase |
| Recovered debt not recorded | Understated | No effect (bank understated instead) |

## Exam traps

- Change only, not the full allowance (except when first created).
- Write off irrecoverable debts **before** calculating the percentage.
- Reinstate before recording the receipt of a recovered debt.
- Recovered debts are income of the year received.
- The allowance is never shown as a liability.
- Irrecoverable debts appear in the sales ledger control account; the
allowance does not.

## Quick self-test

1. Define "irrecoverable debt".
2. Give the journal entry to write off $380 owed by J Sim.
3. A debt of $500 written off two years ago is paid. Give the two journal
entries.
4. Is irrecoverable debts recovered an income or an expense?
5. Explain **one** reason for keeping an allowance for irrecoverable debts.
6. Receivables $40,000; allowance 3%; no previous allowance. Give the
journal entry.
7. Next year receivables are $45,000; allowance 3%. Calculate the change and
state its effect on profit.
8. The following year receivables are $38,000; allowance 3%. Calculate the
change and state its effect on profit.
9. Show the statement of financial position extract for question 8.
10. Receivables $26,800 include a debt of $800 now irrecoverable. Allowance
5%. Calculate the allowance.
11. Which side of the allowance account records a decrease?
12. Where is the irrecoverable debts account balance transferred at the year
end?

### Answers

1. An amount owed by a credit customer that the business decides will not
be paid.
2. Dr Irrecoverable debts $380, Cr J Sim $380.
3. Dr Customer $500, Cr Irrecoverable debts recovered $500; Dr Bank $500,
Cr Customer $500.
4. Income.
5. Any one: prudence, so trade receivables are not overstated; matching,
so the expected loss is charged in the year of the sales; realistic figures
for users.
6. Dr Statement of profit or loss $1,200, Cr Allowance for irrecoverable
debts $1,200.
7. $1,350 − $1,200 = $150 increase; profit reduced by $150.
8. $1,140 − $1,350 = $210 decrease; profit increased by $210.
9. Trade receivables $38,000, less allowance $1,140 = $36,860.
10. ($26,800 − $800) × 5% = $1,300.
11. The debit side.
12. To the statement of profit or loss, as an expense.
