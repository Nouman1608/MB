## Questions

**1.** In Country A (illustrative), nominal GDP rises from 6,300 to 6,741 and the price index rises from 100 to 104. Calculate the growth in real GDP, to one decimal place. **[3]**

**2.** Country B has potential output of 4,000 and actual real GDP of 4,148.
**(a)** Work out the output gap, expressed as a percentage of potential. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one likely consequence of this gap. **[2]**

**3.** Country C's real GDP rises from 500 to 600 over five years. Calculate the average annual (compound) growth rate, to one decimal place. **[3]**

**4.** Distinguish between short-run and long-run economic growth, referring to an AD/AS diagram or a production possibility diagram. **[4]**

**5.** The table shows Country N, which has a trend growth rate of 2.5%.

| Year | Real GDP growth | Inflation | Unemployment | Investment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +3.6% | 3.9% | 4.6% | +10% |
| 2 | -1.1% | 3.1% | 5.8% | -12% |
| 3 | +0.9% | 1.8% | 6.9% | -3% |
| 4 | +3.2% | 1.6% | 6.5% | +8% |

Identify the phase of the economic cycle in each year, using the indicators to support each choice. **[5]**

**6.** Country D's real GDP index (base year = 100) is 100, 104.0, 96.0 and 103.2 in years 1 to 4.
**(a)** Find the percentage change in real GDP between year 2 and year 3. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the percentage change between year 3 and year 4. **[1]**
**(c)** State whether real GDP in year 4 has returned to its year 2 level, with a reason. **[1]**

**7.** Explain one demand-side factor that can raise short-run growth and one supply-side factor that can raise the long-run trend rate of growth. **[6]**

**8.** Using an AD/AS diagram, explain the likely effect on output and the price level of a sharp rise in the price of an imported raw material. **[5]**

**9.** Country P's exports are 25% of its GDP of 2,000. A recession among its trading partners cuts its exports by 8%. The multiplier is 1.6.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in GDP, in value and as a percentage of GDP. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two other ways a global event could affect domestic economic activity. **[4]**

**10.** Country E's potential output grows 3% a year. Actual real GDP rises from 1,000, which equals potential, to 1,020 in the next year.
**(a)** Calculate the growth of actual real GDP. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate potential output and the output gap after one year, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the gap can widen when real GDP is rising. **[1]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that a global demand-side shock causes only temporary harm to a domestic economy. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Real GDP = 6,741 / 104 x 100 = 6,481.7 [1]. Growth = (6,481.7 - 6,300) / 6,300 [1] = **2.9%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the deflating step. Writing 7.0% - 4.0% = 3.0% is a different, approximate method and gives a different answer.

**2. (a)** (4,148 - 4,000) / 4,000 [1] = **+3.7%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The gap is positive, so demand is above normal capacity [1], which creates inflationary pressure as firms compete for scarce labour and materials [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A consequence needs a reason that links to the gap. Stating "inflation" alone is a bare assertion.

**3.** 600 / 500 = 1.2 [1]. 1.2^(1/5) = 1.0371 [1]. Growth = **3.7%** a year [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing 20% by 5 gives 4.0%, which ignores compounding. State the method you use.

**4.** Short-run growth is a rise in actual real GDP [1], shown by AD moving right along SRAS, or a point moving towards the boundary [1]. Long-run growth is an increase in productive capacity [1], shown by LRAS shifting right or the boundary moving outwards [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the curve that moves in each case. A rightward AD shift alone does not show long-run growth.

**5.** Year 1: boom, because growth is above trend, inflation is highest and unemployment lowest [1]. Year 2: recession, because output falls and investment drops sharply [1]. Year 3: trough or early recovery, because output grows again but only 0.9%, below trend [1]. Year 4: recovery, because growth is above trend and investment rises [1]. Unemployment peaks in year 3 and falls only in year 4, so it lags real GDP [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Support every phase with at least one number, and use the trend figure when growth is positive but weak.

**6. (a)** (96.0 - 104.0) / 104.0 [1] = **-7.7%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (103.2 - 96.0) / 96.0 = **+7.5%** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** **No**, because 103.2 is below 104.0 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the previous year's index, not by 100. The 7.2-point rise is not the 7.5% growth.

**7.** Demand-side: lower interest rates [1] make borrowing cheaper, so consumption and investment rise and AD shifts right [1]. If there is spare capacity, real GDP rises [1]. Supply-side: investment in new technology [1] raises productivity and potential output [1], so LRAS moves right and the long-run trend climbs [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each answer needs a full chain of reasoning, ending in output, not just a named factor.

**8.** The cost of production rises [1], so SRAS shifts left [1]. At the new equilibrium, real GDP is lower [1] and the price level is higher [1]. This may also open a negative output gap and raise unemployment [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A diagram or an exact description of the shift helps. The direction of both output and prices must be stated.

**9. (a)** Exports = 25% x 2,000 = 500; fall = 8% x 500 = 40 [1]. Fall in GDP = 40 x 1.6 = **64** [1], which is 64 / 2,000 = **3.2%** of GDP [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Imported input prices: a global rise in commodity prices lifts costs and shifts SRAS left [1][1]. Exchange rate: a global event can change the currency's value, altering export and import prices and net exports [1][1]. Confidence and finance are further routes. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the figures in your explanation where you can. Describe each route as a chain from event to GDP.

**10. (a)** 1,020 / 1,000 - 1 = **2.0%** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Potential = 1,000 x 1.03 = **1,030** [1]. Gap = (1,020 - 1,030) / 1,030 = **-1.0%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Actual growth (2.0%) was below the trend growth of potential (3.0%), so output fell further behind capacity [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two growth rates directly. Saying "growth is positive, so the gap closes" is the common error.

**11.** Indicative points, one mark each:
- AD shifts left, output falls and a negative gap opens [1].
- Exports fall, and the multiplier amplifies the loss, so investment and unemployment are hit [1].
- The harm is temporary if wages and prices adjust, since SRAS shifts right and output returns to potential [1].
- Keynesian view: an economy can stay below capacity for years without self-correction [1].
- Lasting harm: firms close and skills are lost, so LRAS may shift left [1].
- The size and duration of the shock matter [1].
- An open or undiversified economy suffers more [1].
- Policy response may shorten the downturn [1].
- Spare reserves or flexible labour markets help recovery [1].
- A judgement is made that depends on a stated condition, such as depth of the recession or how fast the economy adjusts [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh the temporary and lasting arguments against each other and end with a conditional conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Working with money values when the question is about real growth.
- Dividing the output gap by actual output instead of potential output.
- Calculating a simple average where compound growth is needed.
- Treating a rise in growth as an end to a negative output gap.
- Calling a slowdown a recession.
- Identifying a phase without citing the data.
- Describing shocks without the AD or SRAS shift and both of its effects.
- Giving evaluation as a list, with no judgement and no condition.
