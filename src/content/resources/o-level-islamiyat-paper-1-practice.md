This set follows the real Paper 1 structure: answer **Question 1**,
**Question 2**, and **two** of Questions 3–5, exactly as in the exam.
The paper is 1 hour 30 minutes and carries **50 marks**. Every question
has a knowledge part **(a)** and an understanding part **(b)**:
Question 1 is marked 4 + 4, and Questions 2–5 are marked 10 + 4
(8 + 14 + 14 + 14 = 50). Question 1 prints three of the Qur'an passages
from the syllabus appendix, in Arabic and English, and you choose two.

## Question 1 — compulsory (1.1 Major themes of the Qur'an) [8]

**(a)** Briefly explain the main theme(s) in each passage. **[4]**
**(b)** Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim's
life today. **[4]**

> وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ اللَّيْلُ وَالنَّهَارُ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ الَّذِي خَلَقَهُنَّ إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

**Sura 41.37**

> فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ

> وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ

**Sura 99**

1\. When the earth is shaken to her utmost convulsion, 2. And the earth throws up her burdens, 3. And man cries out: ‘What is the matter with her?’, 4. On that day will she declare her tidings: 5. ## Answers

**How this paper is marked.** Cambridge marks each part by levels of
response (syllabus section 4.2). Part (a) tests knowledge (AO1): in
Question 1, Level 4 = 4, Level 3 = 3, Level 2 = 2, Level 1 = 1; in
Questions 2–5, Level 4 = 8–10, Level 3 = 5–7, Level 2 = 3–4,
Level 1 = 1–2. Part (b) tests understanding (AO2): Level 2 = 3–4,
Level 1 = 1–2. In Question 1 the two chosen passages are read together
and given one mark for (a) and one for (b). The bracketed credits below
show one way to reach each part's tariff, and each part's credits add
up exactly to that tariff; other valid points of the same quality earn
the same credit.

**Question 1. (a) [4]** — for **each** of the two chosen passages, the
main theme identified [1] and explained from the wording of the
passage [1]; whichever two passages are chosen, the total is 4.
**Sura 41.37** — **God in Himself**: He alone deserves worship [1];
the night, day, sun and moon are only His "signs" and His creations, so
bowing to the sun or moon is forbidden and worship belongs to the one
"who created them" [1]. **Sura 99** — **God's relationship with the
created world**: the Day of Judgement [1]; the earth will be shaken and
will "declare her tidings", people will be "shown their deeds", and
even "an atom's weight" of good or evil will be seen, so every person
is accountable [1]. **Sura 6.75–79** — **God's Messengers**: God guided
the Prophet Ibrahim to certainty [1]; Ibrahim saw the star, moon and sun
set and reasoned that nothing that sets can be God, then turned "towards
Him who created the heavens and the earth" and rejected giving partners
to God [1].
**(b) [4]** — for **each** of the two chosen passages, one reasoned
point about its importance today [2]. **Sura 41.37** — Muslims
prostrate only to God in prayer and reject anything that treats created
things, such as horoscopes or lucky charms, as having power over their
lives; the wonders of nature should lead them to worship the Creator
[2]. **Sura 99** — because no deed is too small to count, Muslims do
small kindnesses such as a smile or removing litter, and avoid "small"
wrongs such as rudeness or minor dishonesty, preparing for judgement
[2]. **Sura 6.75–79** — Muslims can use reason and reflection on
creation to strengthen their faith, and, like Ibrahim, stand firm for
tawhid even when the people around them hold other beliefs [2].

**Question 2. (a)** After the Prophet's death, many who had memorised
the Qur'an were killed at the **Battle of Yamama** during the Ridda
wars, prompting concern that parts of the text could be lost [2].
**Abu Bakr**, on Umar's advice, commissioned **Zayd ibn Thabit** to
collect the written and memorised Qur'an into a single copy [2],
which was kept by Abu Bakr and later **Hafsah** [1]. As Islam spread,
Muslims in different regions began reciting in different dialects,
and disputes arose during campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan [2].
**Uthman** had Hafsah's copy reproduced by a committee under Zayd,
using the dialect of the Quraysh where there was disagreement [2];
**standard copies were sent to the main centres** of the Muslim world
and other versions were destroyed [1].
**(b)** It **preserved a single, uniform text**, preventing the
division and doctrinal dispute that competing versions would have
caused [2]; the text used today is the one established then, which
Muslims regard as evidence of the Qur'an's careful preservation [2].

**Question 3. (a)** Persecution in Makkah intensified, particularly
after the deaths of Khadijah and Abu Talib [1]. Delegations from
Yathrib pledged support at the two **pledges of Aqabah** [1]. The
Quraysh plotted to kill the Prophet; **Ali slept in his bed** while
the Prophet left with Abu Bakr [2]. They sheltered in the **cave of
Thawr** for three days to evade pursuers [2] before completing the
journey to Yathrib, which became **Madinah** [2]; Muslims migrated in
small groups to avoid detection [2].
**(b)** It marks the **start of the Islamic calendar**, showing its
significance to later generations [1]. It transformed the Muslims from
a persecuted minority into a **community with its own territory and
authority** [1]; it enabled the **Constitution of Madinah** and the
first mosque, and established the principle that migration to
preserve one's faith is legitimate [2].

**Question 4. (a)** On arrival, the Prophet paired each **Muhajir**
(migrant from Makkah) with an **Ansari** (helper of Madinah) [2],
who shared home, property and livelihood with the newcomer [2]. This
went beyond hospitality — some Ansar offered to divide their wealth
and even their households with their paired Muhajir [2]; the bond was
treated as a form of **brotherhood**, later formalised within the
wider Constitution of Madinah [2]. At first the pairing even carried
rights of mutual inheritance, until later revelation (Sura 8.75)
returned inheritance to blood relatives while the brotherhood itself
continued [2].
**(b)** The Muhajirun had arrived with **little or no property**, so
the pairing prevented **destitution** and social breakdown at a
fragile moment [2]; it also demonstrated a working **model of
solidarity** across tribal lines that helped unify the new community
[2].

**Question 5. (a)** The Farewell Sermon, delivered on the Prophet's
final pilgrimage, set out **equality regardless of race** — "no Arab
has superiority over a non-Arab" [2]; the **sanctity of life and
property** [2]; the **rights of women**, including fair treatment
[2]; the **abolition of usury** and of pre-Islamic **blood feuds**
[2]; and instructed Muslims to hold fast to the Qur'an [2].
**(b)** It is regarded as a **summary of Islamic ethics**, still cited
today on equality and human rights [2]; it confirmed principles that
shaped later Islamic law and social practice, and remains a reference
point for Muslims discussing justice and conduct [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a narrative-only answer to a part (b) question, which asks
  specifically for **significance**, not more description.
- In Question 1, writing about only one of the printed passages, or
  about all three: the rubric asks for **two**, and each carries half
  the marks of each part — the theme(s) in part (a) and their
  importance in a Muslim's life today in part (b).
- Confusing the **compilation** (Abu Bakr) with the
  **standardisation** (Uthman) of the Qur'an.
- Giving a generic answer to a 10-mark part without anchoring points
  to specific events, named individuals or quotations.
- Spending too long on Question 1's 8 marks and running short of time
  for the 14-mark questions that follow.
