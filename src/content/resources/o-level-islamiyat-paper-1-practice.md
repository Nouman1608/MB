This set follows the real Paper 1 structure: answer **Question 1**,
**Question 2**, and **two** of Questions 3–5, exactly as in the exam.

## Question 1 — compulsory (1.1 Major themes of the Qur'an) [8]

Choose **two** of the following themes and, for each, explain how it
is reflected in the Qur'an's teaching:

- Tawhid, the oneness of God
- The Day of Judgement and accountability
- Guidance for social justice and the treatment of the poor

## Question 2 — compulsory (1.2 History and importance of the Qur'an) [14]

**(a)** Describe how the Qur'an was compiled after the death of the
Prophet (pbuh) and later standardised. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain why the standardisation under Uthman was important
for the later history of Islam. **[4]**

## Answer any TWO of Questions 3–5

### Question 3 (1.3 Life and importance of the Prophet) [14]

**(a)** Describe the main events of the Hijra from Makkah to Madinah. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain why the Hijra is regarded as a turning point for the
Muslim community. **[4]**

### Question 4 (1.4 The first Islamic community) [14]

**(a)** Describe the relationship established between the Muhajirun
and the Ansar in Madinah. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain why this relationship mattered for the survival of
the early Muslim community. **[4]**

### Question 5 (1.3 / 1.4 combined) [14]

**(a)** Describe the main provisions of the Farewell Sermon. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain the importance of the Farewell Sermon for Muslims
today. **[4]**

## Answers

**Question 1.** Any two themes, 4 marks each. **Tawhid** — the Qur'an
repeatedly affirms that God is one, without partner or equal, most
directly in surahs such as Al-Ikhlas; this underlies every other
teaching, since worship, law and morality all flow from the
acknowledgement of one God [2] [2]. **The Day of Judgement** — the
Qur'an describes every person as accountable for their deeds,
rewarded or punished in the hereafter, which the Qur'an presents as a
motivation for righteous conduct in this life [2] [2]. **Social
justice** — the Qur'an commands care for orphans, the poor and
travellers, and forbids exploitation such as usury, framing generosity
as an act of faith rather than optional charity [2] [2].

**Question 2. (a)** After the Prophet's death, many who had memorised
the Qur'an were killed at the **Battle of Yamama** during the Ridda
wars, prompting concern that parts of the text could be lost [2].
**Abu Bakr**, on Umar's advice, commissioned **Zayd ibn Thabit** to
collect the written and memorised Qur'an into a single copy [2],
which was kept by Abu Bakr and later **Hafsah** [1]. As Islam spread,
Muslims in different regions began reciting in different dialects,
and disputes arose during campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan [2].
**Uthman** had Hafsah's copy reproduced by a committee under Zayd,
using the dialect of the Quraysh where there was disagreement [2];
**standard copies were sent to the main centres** of the Muslim world
and other versions were destroyed [1].
**(b)** It **preserved a single, uniform text**, preventing the
division and doctrinal dispute that competing versions would have
caused [2]; the text used today is the one established then, which
Muslims regard as evidence of the Qur'an's careful preservation [2].

**Question 3. (a)** Persecution in Makkah intensified, particularly
after the deaths of Khadijah and Abu Talib [1]. Delegations from
Yathrib pledged support at the two **pledges of Aqabah** [1]. The
Quraysh plotted to kill the Prophet; **Ali slept in his bed** while
the Prophet left with Abu Bakr [2]. They sheltered in the **cave of
Thawr** for three days to evade pursuers [2] before completing the
journey to Yathrib, which became **Madinah** [2]; Muslims migrated in
small groups to avoid detection [2].
**(b)** It marks the **start of the Islamic calendar**, showing its
significance to later generations [1]. It transformed the Muslims from
a persecuted minority into a **community with its own territory and
authority** [1]; it enabled the **Constitution of Madinah** and the
first mosque, and established the principle that migration to
preserve one's faith is legitimate [2].

**Question 4. (a)** On arrival, the Prophet paired each **Muhajir**
(migrant from Makkah) with an **Ansari** (helper of Madinah) [2],
who shared home, property and livelihood with the newcomer [2]. This
went beyond hospitality — some Ansar offered to divide their wealth
and even their households with their paired Muhajir [2]; the bond was
treated as a form of **brotherhood**, later formalised within the
wider Constitution of Madinah [2].
**(b)** The Muhajirun had arrived with **little or no property**, so
the pairing prevented **destitution** and social breakdown at a
fragile moment [2]; it also demonstrated a working **model of
solidarity** across tribal lines that helped unify the new community
[2].

**Question 5. (a)** The Farewell Sermon, delivered on the Prophet's
final pilgrimage, set out **equality regardless of race** — "no Arab
has superiority over a non-Arab" [2]; the **sanctity of life and
property** [2]; the **rights of women**, including fair treatment
[2]; the **abolition of usury** and of pre-Islamic **blood feuds**
[2]; and instructed Muslims to hold fast to the Qur'an [2].
**(b)** It is regarded as a **summary of Islamic ethics**, still cited
today on equality and human rights [2]; it confirmed principles that
shaped later Islamic law and social practice, and remains a reference
point for Muslims discussing justice and conduct [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a narrative-only answer to a part (b) question, which asks
  specifically for **significance**, not more description.
- Treating Question 1 as if only one passage may be discussed — the
  syllabus rewards two, each with its own supporting detail.
- Confusing the **compilation** (Abu Bakr) with the
  **standardisation** (Uthman) of the Qur'an.
- Giving a generic answer to Question 5-style combined questions
  without anchoring points to specific events or named individuals.
- Spending too long on Question 1's 8 marks and running short of time
  for the 14-mark questions that follow.
