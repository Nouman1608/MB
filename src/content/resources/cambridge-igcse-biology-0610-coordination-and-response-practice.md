These questions cover sub-topic **14.1 Coordination and response** (the nervous system) of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content on synapses; Core candidates can skip them.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which structures make up the central nervous system?

A: brain and nerves

B: brain and spinal cord

C: spinal cord and nerves

D: brain, spinal cord and nerves

**[1]**

**2.** State the role of the nervous system. **[1]**

**3.** Name the type of neurone that carries impulses from a receptor to the central nervous system. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a synapse. **[1]**

**5.** Name the **two** types of effector. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the function of synapses in controlling the direction of impulses. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A diagram of a reflex arc shows the skin of a finger, the spinal cord and a muscle in the upper arm. Neurone X runs from the skin to the spinal cord. Neurone Y is short and lies inside the spinal cord. Neurone Z runs from the spinal cord to the muscle.

**(a)** Identify neurones X, Y and Z. **[3]**

**(b)** Name the receptor and the effector in this reflex arc. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe a reflex action, using the syllabus description. **[3]**

**8.** A speck of dust blows towards a cyclist's eye and she blinks before she is aware of it.

**(a)** Explain why this is a reflex action and not a voluntary action. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest the effector in this response. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** advantage of this reflex. **[1]**

**9.** When food enters the mouth, the salivary glands release saliva.

**(a)** State the stimulus and the receptors in this response. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the salivary glands are described as effectors. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A diagram of a synapse shows structures P, Q and R. P is a small sac in the end of the first neurone. Q is the space between the two neurones. R is on the membrane of the second neurone.

**(a)** Identify P, Q and R. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the events at the synapse that result in an impulse in the second neurone. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Explain why an impulse can pass from neurone A to neurone B across a synapse, but not from B to A. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system. **[3]**

**13.** A student writes: "In a reflex action the brain decides how to respond, and the impulse then travels along a single nerve cell from the receptor straight to the effector."

Identify **two** errors in the student's statement and correct each one. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Coordination and regulation of body functions [1].

**3.** Sensory neurone [1].

**4.** A junction between two neurones [1].

**5.** Muscles [1]; glands [1].

**6.** Synapses ensure that impulses travel in one direction only [1].

**7. (a)** X is a sensory neurone [1]; Y is a relay neurone [1]; Z is a motor neurone [1].

**(b)** Receptor: temperature / pain / touch receptors in the skin of the finger [1]. Effector: the muscle in the upper arm [1].

**(c)** An automatic response (no conscious thought) [1] that is rapid [1] and integrates and coordinates a stimulus with the response of an effector (muscle or gland) [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), "the skin" alone is often not accepted as the receptor; name the receptors in the skin.

**8. (a)** It happens automatically, without a conscious decision [1]; it happens before she is aware of the stimulus / very rapidly [1].

**(b)** Eyelid muscles [1].

**(c)** It protects the eye from damage by the dust [1].

**9. (a)** Stimulus: (chemicals in) food in the mouth [1]. Receptors: chemical receptors (taste receptors) in the tongue [1].

**(b)** They carry out a response (secreting saliva) when stimulated by impulses from motor neurones [1].

*Examiner insight:* This question checks that you know glands are effectors as well as muscles; many candidates name a muscle here.

**10. (a)** P is a vesicle (containing neurotransmitter) [1]; Q is the synaptic gap [1]; R is a receptor protein [1].

**(b)** An impulse arrives and stimulates the release of neurotransmitter molecules from the vesicles into the gap [1]. The neurotransmitter molecules diffuse across the gap [1]. They bind with receptor proteins on the second neurone [1]. An impulse is stimulated in the second neurone [1].

*Examiner insight:* Keep the events in order. "The impulse jumps across the gap" is incorrect and scores nothing.

**11.** Neurotransmitter is stored in vesicles only in neurone A, so it can only be released from A [1]; receptor proteins are only on neurone B, so only B can be stimulated [1].

**12.** The CNS consists of the brain and spinal cord, whereas the PNS consists of the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord [1]. The CNS processes information and coordinates responses [1], whereas the PNS carries impulses between the CNS and receptors or effectors [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both systems in each point; describing one system and then the other separately can lose marks.

**13.** Error 1: the brain does not decide in a reflex action [1]; the response is automatic, with no conscious decision, and the arc can pass through the spinal cord [1]. Error 2: the impulse does not travel along a single neurone from receptor to effector [1]; it passes along a sensory neurone, a relay neurone and a motor neurone, crossing synapses between them [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each error earns one mark for spotting it and one for the correction. A correction that only says "this is wrong" scores the first mark only.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the spinal cord out of the CNS.
- Naming only muscles as effectors.
- Calling the skin the receptor instead of the receptors in the skin.
- Writing reflex descriptions without "automatic" and "rapid".
- (Extended) Saying the electrical impulse crosses the synaptic gap.
- (Extended) Putting receptor proteins on the first neurone.
