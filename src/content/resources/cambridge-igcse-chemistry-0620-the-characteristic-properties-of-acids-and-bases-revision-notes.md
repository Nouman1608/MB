Condensed notes for subtopic **7.1 The characteristic properties of acids and bases**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. Items marked *(Extended)* are Supplement content. For explanations with worked comparisons, read the [acids and bases study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-the-characteristic-properties-of-acids-and-bases/); for exam-style questions, use the [acids and bases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-the-characteristic-properties-of-acids-and-bases-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acid (Core) | Substance whose aqueous solution contains H⁺ ions |
| Alkali | A soluble base; its aqueous solution contains OH⁻ ions |
| Base | An oxide or hydroxide of a metal |
| Neutralisation | H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) |
| pH | Scale 0–14 showing acidity or alkalinity; 7 is neutral |
| Acid *(Extended)* | Proton donor |
| Base *(Extended)* | Proton acceptor |
| Strong acid *(Extended)* | Completely dissociated in aqueous solution |
| Weak acid *(Extended)* | Partially dissociated in aqueous solution |

## Reactions of acids

| Reaction | Products | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| Acid + metal | Salt + hydrogen | Fizzing; metal disappears; warms; H₂ gives squeaky pop |
| Acid + base (oxide/hydroxide) | Salt + water | Solid base dissolves; e.g. CuO: black solid → blue solution |
| Acid + carbonate | Salt + water + carbon dioxide | Fizzing; solid dissolves; CO₂ turns limewater milky |

Example equations:

```
Fe + 2HCl → FeCl2 + H2
MgO + 2HNO3 → Mg(NO3)2 + H2O
K2CO3 + 2HCl → 2KCl + H2O + CO2
```

Salt names: hydrochloric → chloride; sulfuric → sulfate; nitric → nitrate.

## Reactions of bases

| Reaction | Products |
|---|---|
| Base + acid | Salt + water |
| Base + ammonium salt (warm) | Salt + water + ammonia |

```
KOH + NH4NO3 → KNO3 + H2O + NH3
```

Ammonia test: damp red litmus turns blue.

## Indicator colours

| Indicator | Acid | Alkali |
|---|---|---|
| Litmus | Red | Blue |
| Thymolphthalein | Colourless | Blue |
| Methyl orange | Red | Yellow |

## Universal indicator and pH

| Colour | pH (approx.) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Red | 0–2 | Strongly acidic |
| Orange / yellow | 3–6 | Weakly acidic |
| Green | 7 | Neutral |
| Blue | 8–10 | Weakly alkaline |
| Purple | 11–14 | Strongly alkaline |

- Lower pH → higher H⁺ concentration → more acidic.
- Higher pH (above 7) → higher OH⁻ concentration → more alkaline.
- Universal indicator gives a pH value; litmus, methyl orange and thymolphthalein only show acid or alkali.

## Neutralisation

```
H+(aq) + OH-(aq) → H2O(l)
```

Adding alkali to acid: pH rises to 7 at neutralisation, then above 7 if more alkali is added. Adding acid to alkali: pH falls.

## Proton transfer *(Extended)*

- H⁺ = a proton.
- In HCl + NH₃ → NH₄Cl: HCl donates a proton (acid); NH₃ accepts it (base).
- In neutralisation, OH⁻ accepts a proton from H⁺.

## Strong and weak acids *(Extended)*

```
HCl(aq) → H+(aq) + Cl-(aq)
CH3COOH(aq) ⇌ H+(aq) + CH3COO-(aq)
```

Same concentration, HCl compared with CH₃COOH:

- HCl has a lower pH (higher H⁺ concentration).
- HCl reacts faster with metals and carbonates.
- Same volume and concentration of each gives the **same total** volume of gas with excess metal or carbonate.

## Identifying an unknown solution: a quick routine

1. Universal indicator paper: gives the pH, so acid, neutral or alkali and how strongly.
2. If acidic: add a small piece of magnesium ribbon (hydrogen, squeaky pop) or a little sodium carbonate (carbon dioxide, limewater milky).
3. If alkaline: warm with a little ammonium salt; damp red litmus turning blue confirms ammonia, showing the solution contains a base.
4. Report each test with its **result**, not just its name.

## Writing a "describe the properties of acids" answer

A full answer gives three reactions with their products (metal → salt + hydrogen; base → salt + water; carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide) and the three indicator colours (litmus red, thymolphthalein colourless, methyl orange red). Add "contain H⁺ ions in aqueous solution" and "pH below 7" if the mark allocation allows.

## Exam traps

- Acid + carbonate makes **three** products; carbon dioxide is the one most often missed.
- Methyl orange in alkali is **yellow**; thymolphthalein in acid is **colourless**.
- Not every base is an alkali (CuO, MgO, Fe(OH)₃ are insoluble).
- Higher pH means **lower** H⁺ concentration.
- Weak ≠ dilute; strong ≠ concentrated.
- Use ⇌ for a weak acid and → for a strong acid in dissociation equations.
- Ammonia comes from **base + ammonium salt**, not acid + ammonium salt.

## Self-test

1. Name the products of zinc and dilute hydrochloric acid.
2. State what you would see when copper(II) oxide is warmed with dilute sulfuric acid.
3. Write a balanced equation for calcium carbonate reacting with nitric acid.
4. Give the colour of thymolphthalein in an alkali.
5. Give the colour of methyl orange in an acid.
6. What is an alkali?
7. Name the gas formed when sodium hydroxide is warmed with ammonium sulfate, and give a test for it.
8. Solution A has pH 2 and solution B has pH 5. Which has the higher hydrogen ion concentration?
9. Write the ionic equation for neutralisation, with state symbols.
10. *(Extended)* Define an acid and a base in terms of protons.
11. *(Extended)* Write the equation showing ethanoic acid acting as a weak acid.
12. *(Extended)* Equal volumes of 1 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid and 1 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid react with excess magnesium. Compare the rate and the total volume of hydrogen.

**Answers**

1. Zinc chloride and hydrogen.
2. The black solid dissolves and the solution turns blue.
3. CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂.
4. Blue.
5. Red.
6. A soluble base.
7. Ammonia; turns damp red litmus paper blue.
8. A (lower pH).
9. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
10. An acid is a proton donor; a base is a proton acceptor.
11. CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq).
12. Hydrochloric acid reacts faster; both give the same total volume of hydrogen.
