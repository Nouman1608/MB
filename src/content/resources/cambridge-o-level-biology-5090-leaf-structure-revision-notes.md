These notes condense subtopic **6.2 Leaf structure** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: why leaves are large and thin, and the structure and adaptations of the cuticle, epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, air spaces, stomata and guard cells, and vascular bundles of a dicotyledonous leaf. The full explanations are in the [6.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-leaf-structure/). Then try the [6.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-leaf-structure-practice/).

## Why a large, thin leaf?

- **Large surface area**: catches more light and has more stomata for carbon dioxide to enter.
- **Thin**: light reaches all the cells, and the diffusion distance for gases is short.

## Layers from top to bottom

| Structure | Key features | Function or adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Cuticle | Waxy, waterproof, transparent | Reduces water loss; lets light through |
| Upper epidermis | One layer, transparent, no chloroplasts | Lets light reach the palisade cells; protects |
| Palisade mesophyll | Tall, narrow, packed close, many chloroplasts | Main site of photosynthesis, near the light |
| Spongy mesophyll | Rounded, loosely packed, fewer chloroplasts, large air spaces | Photosynthesis, and gas movement through the air spaces |
| Air spaces | Between spongy cells | Carbon dioxide diffuses in and oxygen out; gas exchange surface |
| Lower epidermis | Contains stomata and guard cells | Gas exchange |
| Stomata | Pores in the lower epidermis | Let carbon dioxide in, oxygen and water vapour out |
| Guard cells | Pair around each stoma; contain chloroplasts | Open and close the stoma |
| Vascular bundle | Xylem and phloem | Bring water and ions in; take sucrose away |

## Guard cells

- Take in water: turgid, the stoma **opens**.
- Lose water: flaccid, the stoma **closes** and water loss is reduced.
- Most stomata are on the **lower** surface, where it is cooler and out of the direct sun, so less water is lost.

## Vascular bundle

| Tissue | Carries | Extra function |
|---|---|---|
| Xylem | Water and mineral ions into the leaf | Support |
| Phloem | Sucrose and amino acids away from the leaf | None required |

## Chloroplasts: where?

- Many in **palisade** cells.
- Fewer in **spongy** cells.
- Present in **guard cells**.
- **None** in the other epidermal cells, which are transparent.

## Gas exchange route

Air outside → stomata → air spaces → water film on mesophyll cells → carbon dioxide dissolves and diffuses into cells. Oxygen leaves by the reverse route.

## Magnification (from the formula)

actual size = image size divided by magnification.

Example: image 60 mm at x400 gives 60 divided by 400 = 0.15 mm = 150 micrometres.

## Feature and function pairs

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| Large surface area | Absorbs more light and has more stomata |
| Thin blade | Short diffusion distance; light reaches all cells |
| Waxy cuticle | Reduces water loss |
| Transparent epidermis | Lets light through to the mesophyll |
| Many chloroplasts in palisade | Absorb light energy |
| Air spaces | Allow quick diffusion of gases |
| Stomata | Let carbon dioxide in and oxygen out |
| Veins | Bring water in and take sucrose away |

## Recognising layers on a diagram

- **Tall, neat columns near the top**: palisade mesophyll.
- **Round cells with big white gaps**: spongy mesophyll with air spaces.
- **Pores with two bean-shaped cells in the lower layer**: stomata with guard cells.
- **Group of tubes in the middle**: vascular bundle, with xylem towards the top and phloem towards the bottom.
- **Thin line on the outside**: cuticle.
- **Single layer of clear cells**: epidermis.

## Exam-style full-mark pattern

For every "explain how X is adapted" question, write: **feature**, then **because**, then **effect on photosynthesis or gas exchange**. Example: "The palisade cells contain many chloroplasts, so they absorb a large amount of light, which increases the rate of photosynthesis."

## Short model answers

**State two adaptations of a leaf for photosynthesis.** Large surface area to absorb light; thin, so light reaches all cells and gases diffuse quickly.

**Explain why the palisade cells are near the top of the leaf.** That is where the light is brightest, so the many chloroplasts in these cells absorb the maximum light.

**Explain the function of the air spaces in the spongy mesophyll.** Carbon dioxide diffuses through them to reach the cells that photosynthesise, and oxygen diffuses out through them.

**Explain why the upper epidermis has no chloroplasts.** The cells are transparent so that light passes through to the palisade mesophyll underneath.

**State the functions of xylem and phloem in a leaf.** Xylem brings water and mineral ions to the leaf cells and supports the leaf. Phloem carries sucrose and amino acids away.

## Gas exchange and water loss: the trade-off

Open stomata let carbon dioxide in for photosynthesis, but they also let water vapour out. The cuticle, the position of most stomata on the lower surface and the closing of guard cells all reduce water loss without stopping gas exchange completely.

## Exam traps

- The epidermis does not photosynthesise, apart from the guard cells.
- Palisade cells are **under the upper epidermis**; spongy cells are lower down.
- Air spaces lie **between** cells.
- Phloem carries sucrose, not xylem.
- Guard cells open the stoma when **turgid**, not when flaccid.
- A feature must be paired with its function.
- Use "diffusion", not "breathing", for gas movement in a leaf.

6. Carbon dioxide.
