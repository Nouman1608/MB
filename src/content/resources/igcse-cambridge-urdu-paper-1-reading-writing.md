This guide covers **Paper 1 Reading and Writing**, worth 67% of
Cambridge IGCSE Urdu (0539) and taken by all candidates, alongside
Paper 2 Listening (33%). Centres can additionally enter candidates for
the optional, separately-endorsed Component 5 Speaking, which is not
counted in the overall qualification grade. Paper 1 is a 2-hour
written paper worth 60 marks, and all questions and responses are in
Urdu; dictionaries are not permitted.

## Where this fits in 0539

Paper 1 tests reading comprehension and written production together
across six exercises, moving from short, targeted comprehension
questions through to extended, purpose-driven writing -- giving a full
picture of a candidate's reading and writing ability within a single
paper.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE URDU (0539) — PAPER 1 READING AND WRITING**

- Exercise 1: Short answer questions (8 marks) — skimming and
gist-reading a short text (such as an advertisement, brochure or
newspaper article) and answering with single words or short phrases
- Exercise 2: Multiple matching (9 marks) — matching sentences to a
series of short paragraphs
- Exercise 3: Note-making (9 marks) — reading a longer text and making
brief notes under supplied headings
- Exercise 4: Summary (10 marks) — writing a paragraph-length summary
of up to 100 words, using candidates' own words as far as possible
- Exercise 5: Writing exercise (8 marks) — writing a short piece of
functional prose, such as an email, of approximately 120 words, for a
specified purpose, format and audience
- Exercise 6: Extended writing exercise (16 marks) — writing a longer
piece of continuous prose, of approximately 200 words, for a specified
purpose, format and audience

## How to approach it

Exercises 1-3 test reading skills of increasing depth -- from simple
gist-reading through to detailed note-making -- so practise each
skill in isolation before combining them under timed exam conditions.
Exercise 4's summary explicitly rewards using your own words rather
than copying from the source text, so practise paraphrasing regularly
as a distinct skill from comprehension. Exercises 5 and 6 carry the
most marks (24 of the paper's 60) and both require close attention to
the specified purpose, format and audience, so before writing, check
these three elements are clearly reflected in your response, since
marks are awarded for appropriateness as well as accuracy.
