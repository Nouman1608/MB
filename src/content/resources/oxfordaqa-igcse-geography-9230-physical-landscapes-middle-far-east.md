This guide covers section 3.1.3.1, Physical landscapes in Middle and Far East, the first theme of Section C (Physical landscapes: Coastal landscapes) in Unit 1 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. The key idea is: "The Middle and Far East have a range of diverse landscapes." The specification content is "an overview of the location of major upland/lowland areas and river systems." That means this is a location-knowledge theme. You are expected to know where the main mountains, plateaus, plains and rivers are, and to describe them clearly using an atlas or a map. For the whole unit, start with the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

The later themes on [coastal landscapes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-coastal-processes-landforms/), [hot desert landscapes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-hot-desert-landscapes/) and [river landscapes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-river-landscapes/) say that examples should come "from the region". This theme gives you the geographical frame for those examples.

## What the specification lists

- An overview of the **location of major upland and lowland areas**.
- An overview of the **location of major river systems** in the Middle and Far East.

The specification does not define the region precisely. In this guide the **Middle East** means south-west Asia, from Turkey and the eastern Mediterranean coast to Iran and the Arabian Peninsula. The **Far East** means East Asia (China, Mongolia, Japan and the Koreas) and, for this purpose, South-East Asia, and the edge of South Asia where the Himalayas and the great rivers begin. Check which countries your teacher includes, and use an atlas to confirm every name.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Upland | High ground: mountains, plateaus and hills |
| Lowland | Low-lying, flat or gently sloping land, often a plain or basin |
| Plateau | A large, flat-topped area of high land |
| Basin | A low-lying area surrounded by higher land |
| Delta | A fan of land built where a river deposits sediment at its mouth |
| River system | A main river with its tributaries and the basin it drains |
| Source | Where a river begins |
| Mouth | Where a river meets the sea |
| Relief | The shape of the land surface, including height and slope |

## Why the region has such varied landscapes

The region sits across several plate margins. The Himalayas and the high plateaus of Tibet were raised where the Indian Plate collided with the Eurasian Plate. The Zagros and the Elburz mountains of Iran, and the ranges of Turkey, are part of the Alpine mountain belt formed by the closing of an ancient ocean. Japan, Taiwan and the Philippines lie on the edge of the Pacific "Ring of Fire", where plates sink beneath each other and volcanoes and earthquakes are common. Between the upland areas, large basins and plains have been filled by sediment carried by rivers. At the same time the climate varies from desert (Arabia, the Gobi) to monsoon and tropical forests (South-East Asia).

## Major uplands

### Middle East

- **Anatolian Plateau and the Taurus and Pontic mountains (Turkey):** a high plateau ringed by mountain chains.
- **Iranian Plateau, with the Zagros Mountains and the Elburz Mountains:** the Zagros run north-west to south-east along the western side of Iran. The Elburz lie along the south of the Caspian Sea and include Mount Damavand, about 5 600 m, the highest peak in Iran.
- **Mountains of the Arabian Peninsula:** the Hejaz and Asir mountains lie along the Red Sea coast, and the Hajar mountains lie in Oman and the United Arab Emirates. The interior of Arabia is mostly a lower plateau.

### Far East

- **Himalayas, Karakoram, Hindu Kush and Pamirs:** a great arc of the highest mountains in the world, along the northern edge of South Asia and the southern edge of the Tibetan Plateau. Mount Everest, about 8 849 m, is on the border of Nepal and China.
- **Tibetan Plateau:** the largest and highest plateau on Earth, at an average of over 4 000 m. The sources of several of Asia's great rivers are here.
- **Kunlun, Tian Shan and Altai mountains:** ranges across northern Tibet and Central Asia, and into Mongolia.
- **Mongolian Plateau and Gobi Desert:** high, dry steppe and desert in the north.
- **Qinling Mountains:** a range in central China separating the Yellow River and Yangtze basins.
- **Mountains of Japan:** a mountainous spine, including the Japanese Alps and Mount Fuji, about 3 776 m. Most of Japan is hilly or mountainous, so settlement is concentrated on small coastal plains.
- **Korean, Taiwanese and South-East Asian uplands:** the Taebaek range in Korea, the Central Range in Taiwan and the Annamite range between Vietnam and Laos.

## Major lowlands

### Middle East

- **Mesopotamia (Tigris-Euphrates plain):** a wide alluvial plain in Iraq, the heart of one of the world's oldest farming regions.
- **Persian Gulf coastal plains:** low, flat and often desert or salt marsh.
- **Interior desert basins:** such as the Rub' al Khali ("Empty Quarter") in southern Arabia, a vast sand desert.
- **Jordan Rift Valley:** a deep valley running from north to south, including the Dead Sea, whose shore is the lowest land on Earth's surface, more than 400 m below sea level.

### Far East

- **North China Plain:** a vast, flat, densely populated plain built by silt from the Yellow River.
- **Yangtze lowlands and the Sichuan Basin:** the Sichuan Basin is a fertile basin in the west, while the middle and lower Yangtze have wide floodplains and lakes.
- **North-East (Manchurian) Plain:** a large agricultural lowland.
- **Tarim Basin:** a dry basin in north-west China, containing the Taklamakan Desert.
- **Kanto Plain in Japan:** the lowland around Tokyo, the largest plain in Japan.
- **Deltas of South-East Asia:** the Mekong Delta in Vietnam, the Red River Delta, the Irrawaddy Delta, and the Chao Phraya basin in Thailand. These are flat and fertile, and densely populated.

## Major river systems

| River | Source | Mouth | Notes (lengths are approximate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tigris and Euphrates | Mountains of eastern Turkey | Persian Gulf, joining as the Shatt al-Arab | About 1 850 km and 2 800 km; water supply and irrigation for Syria and Iraq; shaped Mesopotamia |
| Jordan | Mountains on the border of Lebanon, Syria and Israel | Dead Sea | A short river in the rift valley; scarce water |
| Yangtze (Chang Jiang) | Tibetan Plateau | East China Sea, near Shanghai | About 6 300 km; the longest river in Asia; Three Gorges Dam |
| Yellow River (Huang He) | Tibetan Plateau | Bohai Sea | About 5 460 km; carries huge loads of silt from the Loess Plateau; history of floods |
| Pearl River | Yunnan Plateau | South China Sea, near Guangzhou and Hong Kong | Forms a large delta, one of China's biggest industrial regions |
| Mekong | Tibetan Plateau | South China Sea in Vietnam | About 4 350 km; flows through six countries; fertile delta |
| Irrawaddy | Northern Myanmar | Andaman Sea | Main river of Myanmar; delta for rice |
| Ganges and Brahmaputra | Himalayas | Bay of Bengal | On the edge of the region; form the world's largest delta |

Notice a pattern: many great Asian rivers rise on the Tibetan Plateau and flow outwards in different directions. This is why Tibet is sometimes called the "water tower" of Asia. Rivers in the Middle East, by contrast, rise in the mountains of Turkey and Iran, and flow through dry regions where water is scarce.

## How to describe a landscape from a map

Use a simple method.

1. **Give the location** with compass directions and a landmark ("in the north-west of China").
2. **Describe the relief** (high, low, flat, steep) and name features.
3. **Describe the drainage**: where rivers rise, where they flow, where they end.
4. **Link features**: for example, "the river rises on the plateau, flows through the mountains and ends in a delta".
5. **Use evidence** from the map: heights, place names, scales.

### Worked example

**Question:** Describe the location of the Yangtze river and its basin. [4 marks]

**Model answer:** "The Yangtze rises on the high Tibetan Plateau in the west of China. It flows eastwards, first through deep valleys in the mountains and the Sichuan Basin, then across the lowlands of central China. It reaches the East China Sea near Shanghai in the east, where it forms a wide estuary and delta. It is about 6 300 km long, the longest river in Asia."

This answer gives a source, direction, features along the route, the mouth, and a figure.

## Learning the map

Sketch a rough outline of the region and add:

- three plateaus: Tibetan, Iranian, Anatolian;
- four mountain ranges: Himalayas, Zagros, Elburz, the Japanese Alps;
- three lowlands: Mesopotamia, North China Plain, Mekong Delta;
- five rivers: Tigris, Euphrates, Yangtze, Yellow River, Mekong.

Repeat the sketch until you can draw it from memory. Use colour for height (brown for uplands, green for lowlands) and blue for rivers.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the Tigris and Euphrates or the Yangtze and Yellow River.** Learn one fact about each.
- **Putting the Himalayas in the wrong place.** They lie between the Indian subcontinent and the Tibetan Plateau.
- **Calling every high area a mountain.** Plateaus have a flat top.
- **Describing a river without a source and mouth.**
- **Missing the link between lowlands and population.** Plains and deltas are fertile and densely populated.
- **Using exact figures without checking.** Use "about" for lengths and heights, and use an atlas to check.
