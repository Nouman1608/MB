AQA A-Level Computer Science (7517) combines practical programming with the theory of how computers, data and
networks work. It suits students who enjoy solving problems logically and want to go on to computer science,
software engineering, mathematics, engineering or data-focused courses. The qualification is linear and not
tiered, and it has three components: an on-screen exam (Paper 1) and a written exam (Paper 2), each lasting
2 hours 30 minutes and worth 100 raw marks and 40%, plus a non-exam assessment, the computing practical project,
worth 75 marks and 20%.

## How the course is organised

The content is set out in 13 areas, which group naturally into programming and problem solving, computer systems,
and data and society.

**Programming, algorithms and computation.** Fundamentals of programming covers programming itself and
programming paradigms. Fundamentals of data structures covers arrays, fields, records and files, abstract data
types, queues, stacks, graphs, trees, hash tables, dictionaries and vectors. These are followed by fundamentals of
algorithms and theory of computation. The final area, systematic approach to problem solving, describes the
disciplined method that the project and the on-screen paper both rely on.

**How computers work.** Fundamentals of data representation, fundamentals of computer systems, and fundamentals
of computer organisation and architecture explain how data is stored and processed, from number systems to
hardware and software.

**Data, networks and their impact.** Consequences of uses of computing, fundamentals of communication and
networking, fundamentals of databases, Big Data and fundamentals of functional programming widen the course to
how systems connect, how large data sets are handled, and the legal, ethical and social questions computing
raises.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Format | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7517/1 Paper 1 | On-screen exam | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 (raw) | 40% |
| 7517/2 Paper 2 | Written exam | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 (raw) | 40% |
| 7517/NEA | Computing practical project | No exam duration | 75 | 20% |

**Paper 1** is taken on screen. It tests programming ability together with theoretical knowledge from the
programming, data structures, algorithms and theory of computation content, and the systematic problem-solving
skills described in the specification.

**Paper 2** is a written exam testing the remaining content: data representation, computer systems, organisation
and architecture, consequences of computing, networking, databases, Big Data and functional programming.

**The non-exam assessment** is the computing practical project. It assesses a student's ability to solve or
investigate a practical problem using a systematic approach. It is internally assessed and externally moderated
by AQA.

The raw marks for Papers 1 and 2 are each scaled by 1.5, to 150, so the final total is 375 scaled marks. The
specification was first taught in September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in June 2017.

## How the parts fit together

The three components are closely linked. Programming and the systematic approach to problem solving connect
Paper 1 directly to the project: the same skills of analysing a problem, designing a solution, writing and testing
code, and evaluating the result are used in both. Work on the project therefore strengthens Paper 1 performance,
and practice for Paper 1 makes the project more manageable.

Paper 2 might look like a separate set of theory topics, but it connects to programming throughout. Data
representation explains what a program's variables actually hold. Computer organisation and architecture explains
how instructions run. Databases and functional programming are both ways of structuring and processing data,
and they can feed directly into a project. Consequences of uses of computing asks students to reason about the
impact of the systems they build.

Data structures and algorithms are the bridge between theory and practice: they are examined in theory, used in
the on-screen paper and often central to a successful project.

## Planning your study

A sensible route through the course is:

1. **Start programming from the first week and keep going.** Programming fluency underpins Paper 1 and the
   project, and it is built through regular practice, not a single unit.
2. **Teach data structures and algorithms alongside programming**, so each structure is implemented as well as
   described.
3. **Cover data representation, computer systems and architecture in the first year**, giving context to the
   code being written.
4. **Introduce theory of computation, networking, databases, Big Data and functional programming** across the
   second year, linking each back to programming where possible.
5. **Plan the project early.** Choosing a problem with enough depth and scoping it realistically matters more
   than an ambitious idea. Your teacher will set the internal deadlines.

Keep a personal library of small programs that implement key algorithms and data structures; it doubles as
revision for both papers. Short theory questions can be used as each topic is taught, and on-screen programming
practice should be regular throughout. Full past papers are best attempted once their content has been covered.
How to prepare for the on-screen paper, the written paper and the project is set out on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is AQA A-Level Computer Science assessed?**
By an on-screen Paper 1 and a written Paper 2, each 2 hours 30 minutes, 100 raw marks and 40%, and a non-exam assessment worth 75 marks and 20%.

**Is Paper 1 taken on a computer?**
Yes. Paper 1 is an on-screen exam testing programming ability and theoretical knowledge, together with systematic problem-solving skills.

**What is the NEA in AQA A-Level Computer Science?**
It is the computing practical project, in which students solve or investigate a practical problem using a systematic approach. It is internally assessed and externally moderated by AQA.

**Why do the marks add up to 375?**
The raw marks for Papers 1 and 2 are each scaled by 1.5, to 150, and added to the 75-mark project, giving 375 scaled marks.

**When was the 7517 specification first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in June 2017.
