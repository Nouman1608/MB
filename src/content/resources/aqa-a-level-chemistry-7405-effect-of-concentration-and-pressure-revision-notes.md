These notes condense section **3.1.5.4 Effect of concentration and pressure** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 (and Paper 3). The [Effect of concentration and pressure study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-effect-of-concentration-and-pressure/) explains each idea with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-effect-of-concentration-and-pressure-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- The **qualitative effect of changes in concentration** on collision frequency.
- The **qualitative effect of a change in the pressure of a gas** on collision frequency.
- **Explain** how a change in concentration or pressure influences the rate of a reaction.
- Practical opportunity: continuous monitoring, for example calcium carbonate with hydrochloric acid.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration | Amount of solute per unit volume, mol dm⁻³ |
| Collision frequency | Number of collisions per unit time |
| Successful collision | Collision with energy at least equal to Eₐ |
| Continuous monitoring | Following a reaction at intervals while it proceeds |
| Initial rate | Rate at the start of the reaction |
| Tangent | Straight line touching a curve at one point; its gradient gives the rate at that point |

## The explanation for concentration

1. Higher concentration means **more particles in the same volume**.
2. Particles are closer together, so they **collide more frequently**.
3. The proportion of collisions with energy at least Eₐ is **unchanged**, because the temperature is unchanged.
4. The frequency of **successful collisions** increases.
5. The rate increases.

## The explanation for pressure

1. A higher pressure of gas means the molecules are squeezed into a smaller volume.
2. This is a higher **concentration** of gas, since n ÷ V = p ÷ RT at constant temperature.
3. The molecules collide more frequently.
4. The frequency of successful collisions increases, so the rate increases.

Applies only if at least one reactant is a **gas**. Pressure has very little effect on reactions of solids, liquids and solutions.

## Comparison with temperature

| Factor | Collision frequency | Proportion with E ≥ Eₐ | Eₐ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concentration or pressure up | Increases | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Temperature up | Increases slightly | Increases greatly | Unchanged |
| Catalyst | Unchanged | Increases | Lowered |

## Rate changes with time

- Reactants are used up, so concentration falls and collisions become less frequent.
- Therefore the rate **decreases** as the reaction proceeds.
- On a graph of product against time, the gradient is steepest at the start and falls to zero when the reaction ends.
- Rate at any time = gradient of the tangent at that time.

## Worked reminders

**Dilution.** 50.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ acid made up to 250 cm³: c = 2.00 × 50.0 ÷ 250 = **0.400 mol dm⁻³**, one fifth of the original.

**Gas concentration.** At 298 K, n ÷ V = p ÷ RT. At 100 kPa it is 1.00 × 10⁵ ÷ (8.31 × 298) = 40.4 mol m⁻³; at 200 kPa it is 80.8 mol m⁻³, so doubling the pressure doubles the concentration.

**Marble chips and acid.** Gas volumes at 30 s intervals: 0, 38, 62, 78, 88, 94 cm³.
Mean rates: 1.27, 0.80, 0.53, 0.33, 0.20 cm³ s⁻¹. The rate falls as the acid is used.

**Doubling acid concentration (acid in excess).** Steeper initial gradient, same final volume, because the marble is limiting.

**Which reactions respond to pressure?** 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g) does; NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) does not.

## Monitoring methods

| Method | What is measured | When used |
|---|---|---|
| Gas syringe | Volume of gas | A gas is produced |
| Balance | Mass lost as gas escapes | Dense gas such as CO₂ |
| Colorimeter | Absorbance | Coloured species |
| Titration of samples | Concentration | Reaction can be stopped (quenched) |

For a balance, put cotton wool in the neck of the flask to stop spray escaping.

## Answer pattern

"A higher concentration means more particles per unit volume, so the particles collide more frequently. The proportion of collisions with energy at least equal to the activation energy is unchanged, so the frequency of successful collisions increases and the rate increases."

## Common mistakes

- Saying the particles have more energy at a higher concentration.
- Saying the proportion of successful collisions rises.
- Writing "more collisions" without "per unit time" or "frequency".
- Saying pressure speeds up reactions of solutions.
- Forgetting that the rate falls with time.
- Believing a higher concentration always gives more product (it depends on the limiting reagent).
- Changing the activation energy.

## Self-test

1. State the effect of increasing concentration on collision frequency.
2. Explain why increasing concentration increases the rate.
3. Why does concentration not change the proportion of collisions with E ≥ Eₐ?
4. Explain why increasing the pressure of a gas increases the rate of a gas-phase reaction.
5. Why does pressure not affect the rate of reaction between aqueous sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid?
6. Calculate the new concentration when 25.0 cm³ of 1.20 mol dm⁻³ acid is diluted to 100 cm³.
7. Explain why the rate of reaction decreases as time passes.
8. How do you find the rate at a time from a volume–time graph?
9. State two methods of following a reaction that produces a gas.
10. Marble chips react with excess acid; the acid concentration is doubled. State two changes to the volume–time graph, or say what stays the same.
11. State why cotton wool is placed in the neck of the flask in the mass-loss method.
12. Calculate the mean rate if 48 cm³ of gas forms in 80 s.

### Answers

1. Collision frequency increases.
2. There are more particles per unit volume, so more frequent collisions; the proportion with enough energy is unchanged, so more successful collisions per second.
3. The temperature is unchanged, so the distribution of energies is unchanged.
4. A higher pressure puts the molecules closer together (higher concentration), so they collide more often and there are more successful collisions per second.
5. The reactants are in solution, which is not significantly compressible, so the concentrations do not change.
6. 1.20 × 25.0 ÷ 100 = **0.300 mol dm⁻³**.
7. The reactants are used up, so their concentration decreases and collisions become less frequent.
8. Draw a tangent to the curve at that time and calculate its gradient.
9. Any two of: gas syringe, mass loss on a balance, colorimeter (if there is a colour change), titration of samples.
10. The initial gradient is steeper; the final volume is the same, because the marble is the limiting reagent.
11. To stop acid spray from leaving the flask while allowing the gas to escape.
12. 48 ÷ 80 = **0.60 cm³ s⁻¹**.
