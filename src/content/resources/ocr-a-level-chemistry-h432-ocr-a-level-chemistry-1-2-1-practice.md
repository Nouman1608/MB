The questions here practise **1.2.1 Practical skills** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017: investigative approaches, working safely, following instructions, observations and records, presenting data, software and tools, research and citation, and the range of instruments. ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Using concentrated sulfuric acid as an example, explain the difference between a hazard and a risk. **[2]**

**2.** State which CPAC competency each action provides evidence for.

**(a)** Following a method sheet to make up a standard solution. **[1]**

**(b)** Choosing to use a fume cupboard for a reaction that gives off an irritant gas. **[1]**

**3.** List the information needed to cite a web page correctly. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a lab record should be written at the time of the experiment. **[1]**

**5.** In a spreadsheet, cell B2 holds a time in seconds and cell C2 holds a rate.

**(a)** Write a formula for 1/time, using B2. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a formula for the natural logarithm of the rate, using C2. **[1]**

**6.** State two features that suggest a source of scientific information is reliable. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A student will oxidise ethanol to ethanal by adding it dropwise to a warm mixture of potassium dichromate(VI) and concentrated sulfuric acid, distilling off the ethanal as it forms. Write a risk assessment covering three hazards, giving the risk and a specific control measure for each. **[6]**

**8.** A student's lab record for a titration reads:

"Did the titration. Got about 24. Used the same method as last week."

Suggest four ways this record should be improved. **[4]**

**9.** A student wants to investigate how the mass of a solid catalyst affects the rate of decomposition of hydrogen peroxide solution. Outline an investigative approach, including the variables, how the rate will be measured, and why a trial run is useful. **[5]**

**10.** A student's report contains the sentence: "Ethanol boils at 78 °C (internet)."

**(a)** Explain why this citation is inadequate. **[1]**

**(b)** State what a full citation for a web source should include. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why CPAC 5 links citations to planning and conclusions. **[1]**

**11.** A student follows the titration of ethanoic acid with sodium hydroxide using a pH probe connected to a data logger.

**(a)** Give two advantages of the data logger over reading pH with indicator paper by hand. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the pH probe is calibrated with buffer solutions before use. **[1]**

**12.** A method sheet says "add 25.0 cm³ of the acid to the conical flask". A student uses a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder. Explain why the student has not followed the instruction correctly, and what should be used. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The **hazard** is that concentrated sulfuric acid is corrosive: it can cause burns [1]. The **risk** is the chance of harm in the way it is used, for example splashing it onto skin or into eyes while pouring it [1]

Total: 2

**2. (a)** **CPAC 1**, follows written procedures [1]

**(b)** **CPAC 3**, safely uses a range of practical equipment and materials [1]

Total: 2

**3.** Author or organisation, year and page title [1]; full URL and the date it was accessed [1]

Total: 2

**4.** Recording at the time avoids readings being lost, misremembered or changed, so the record is a true account of what was done; the CPAC evidence is a contemporaneous record [1]

Total: 1

**5. (a)** **=1/B2** [1]

**(b)** **=LN(C2)** [1]

Total: 2

**6.** Any two: written by people with relevant expertise, such as a university or learned society [1]; peer reviewed or checked before publication [1]; recently published or updated; agrees with other reliable sources.

Total: 2

**7.** Any three hazards; for each, one mark for the hazard with its risk and one for a specific control:

- Concentrated sulfuric acid is **corrosive**; it could splash onto skin or eyes when the mixture is made [1]. Wear eye protection and gloves, and add the acid slowly to the aqueous dichromate with cooling [1].
- Potassium dichromate(VI) is **toxic** (and an oxidising agent); it could be absorbed through skin or swallowed [1]. Wear gloves, use small amounts and wash hands after use [1].
- Ethanol and ethanal are **flammable**, and ethanal is very volatile; the vapour could ignite [1]. Heat with a water bath or electric heater, with no naked flames, and cool the receiver in ice [1].
- Ethanal vapour is an **irritant**; it could be breathed in. Work in a fume cupboard or well-ventilated area.

Total: 6

*Examiner insight:* Each control must match its hazard. "Wear goggles" for a flammable vapour does not reduce the fire risk.

**8.** Any four: add the date and the aim of the titration [1]; record each burette reading (initial and final) to two decimal places in a table with quantity / unit headings [1]; give each titre exactly, not "about 24", and show which are concordant [1]; write out the method or give a precise reference to it, noting any changes [1]; name the solutions and their concentrations, and the indicator and its colour change.

Total: 4

**9.** Independent variable: mass of catalyst, for example five masses from 0.10 g to 0.50 g weighed on a balance [1]. Dependent variable: rate, measured as the volume of oxygen collected in a gas syringe over a fixed time, or the initial gradient of a volume–time graph [1]. Controlled variables: volume and concentration of hydrogen peroxide, temperature (water bath), particle size of the catalyst from the same supply [1]. Repeat each mass and take a mean [1]. A trial run shows whether the volumes and times chosen give readings that fit the syringe and are large enough to measure well, so quantities can be adjusted [1]

Total: 5

**10. (a)** "Internet" does not let a reader find the source, so the value cannot be checked [1]

**(b)** The author or organisation, year and page title [1]; the URL and the date accessed [1]

**(c)** Citations show that research has been done and that the method and conclusions are supported by checkable evidence [1]

Total: 4

**11. (a)** Any two: it records pH continuously or at short intervals, giving many more readings [1]; it gives a numerical value to a greater resolution than indicator paper colours [1]; readings are not affected by judging colours by eye; the data can be plotted directly as a pH curve.

**(b)** Calibration against buffers of known pH corrects the probe's readings, so the measured pH values are accurate [1]

Total: 3

**12.** The instruction gives 25.0 cm³, which needs a measuring instrument precise to 0.1 cm³ or better; a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder is not precise enough [1]. A 25.0 cm³ pipette (or a burette) should be used [1]

Total: 2

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "hazard" and "risk" as if they meant the same thing.
- Control measures that do not match the hazard.
- Lab records missing the date, aim, units or exact readings.
- Citations without a URL, access date or page.
- Investigations with no named controlled variables or measurement method.
- Choosing apparatus that cannot give the precision the instruction implies.
