## Questions

**1.** A cafe records the number of cakes sold each day for seven days: 6, 9, 4, 9, 12, 7, 9. Find the mean, the median and the mode. **[3]**

**2.** The marks of eight students in a test are 53, 48, 61, 48, 57, 66, 52 and 59.

**(a)** Find the median mark. **[2]**
**(b)** A ninth student scores 80. Find the new mean mark. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the new median. **[1]**

**3.** A team's goals in 40 matches are shown.

| Goals | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matches | 3 | 7 | 10 | 9 | 6 | 3 | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of goals. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the median. **[2]**
**(c)** State the mode. **[1]**

**4.** The times, in seconds, taken by 80 runners to complete a course are grouped below.

| Time t (seconds) | 15 ≤ t < 25 | 25 ≤ t < 35 | 35 ≤ t < 45 | 45 ≤ t < 65 | 65 ≤ t < 95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 15 | 22 | 30 | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate the frequency density of each class. **[2]**
**(b)** State the modal class and explain your choice. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the mean time. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why your answer to (c) is an estimate. **[1]**

**5.** Say which average (mean, median or mode) is most suitable in each case.

**(a)** The favourite sport of each of 30 pupils. **[1]**
**(b)** The salaries of 12 staff, one of whom is a director paid far more than everyone else. Give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Five numbers have a mean of 12.4. Four of them are 9, 15, 11 and 14.

**(a)** Find the fifth number. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the median of the five numbers. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the number of rooms, x, in some flats. The frequency of 3 rooms is k and the mean number of rooms is 3.1.

| Rooms (x) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flats | 2 | 7 | k | 6 | 4 |

**(a)** Find the value of k. **[4]**
**(b)** State the mode. **[1]**

**8.** The number of texts sent in a day by 60 people is grouped below.

| Texts | 0-4 | 5-9 | 10-14 | 15-19 | 20-24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| People | 6 | 14 | 21 | 12 | 7 |

**(a)** State the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the mean number of texts. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the median, using the class boundaries. **[2]**

**9.** The delivery times, in minutes, of two couriers are recorded for ten deliveries each.

Courier A: 22, 24, 25, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 64
Courier B: 23, 25, 26, 27, 27, 27, 28, 30, 31, 33

**(a)** Find the mean of each courier. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the median of each courier. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the mode of each courier. **[1]**
**(d)** A customer wants the courier who is usually quicker. Choose an average, justify it and state which courier you recommend. **[3]**

**10.** Twelve scores are 3, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 21, 22, 24 and 28.

**(a)** Find the exact mean. **[1]**
**(b)** The scores are grouped as 0-9 (3 scores), 10-19 (5 scores) and 20-29 (4 scores). Estimate the mean from the groups. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the two means differ. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Sum = 6 + 9 + 4 + 9 + 12 + 7 + 9 = 56, so mean = 56 ÷ 7 = **8** [1]. Ordered: 4, 6, 7, 9, 9, 9, 12, so the 4th value gives median **9** [1]. Mode **9** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the ordered list is the safest way to show where the median came from, because it lets a reader see where the middle value came from.

**2. (a)** Ordered: 48, 48, 52, 53, 57, 59, 61, 66 [1]. Median = (53 + 57) ÷ 2 = **55** [1].
**(b)** Sum = 444, new sum = 524 [1]. New mean = 524 ÷ 9 = **58.2** [1].
**(c)** Nine values; the 5th is **57** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* With an even number of values the median lies between two values, so quote the halfway value rather than either middle one.

**3. (a)** Σfx = 0 + 7 + 20 + 27 + 24 + 15 + 12 = 105 [1], Σf = 40 [1], mean = 105 ÷ 40 = 2.625 = **2.63** [1].
**(b)** Cumulative: 3, 10, 20, 29, 35, 38, 40. The 20th value is 2 and the 21st is 3 [1], so the median is **2.5** [1].
**(c)** Mode = **2** goals [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Here the middle falls exactly between two groups, so say which two values you averaged; a median of 2 or 3 alone misses the point.

**4. (a)** Widths 10, 10, 10, 20, 30 [1]. Densities: 0.8, 1.5, 2.2, 1.5, 0.167 [1].
**(b)** Modal class **35 ≤ t < 45** [1], because it has the highest frequency density (2.2), and the widths are unequal [1].
**(c)** Midpoints 20, 30, 40, 55, 80 [1]. Σfx = 160 + 450 + 880 + 1650 + 400 = 3540 [1]. Mean ≈ 3540 ÷ 80 = **44.25 s (44.3 s)** [1].
**(d)** The exact times are not known, so every runner is assumed to be at the class midpoint [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The class 45 ≤ t < 65 has the largest frequency (30), but comparing frequency alone ignores its greater width; showing the densities lets a reader see why you chose otherwise.

**5. (a)** The **mode**, because the data is categories [1].
**(b)** The **median** [1], because the director's salary is an extreme value that would inflate the mean [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the average earns little without a reason tied to the data given in the question.

**6. (a)** Total = 5 × 12.4 = 62 [1]. Fifth number = 62 − 49 = **13** [1].
**(b)** Ordered: 9, 11, 13, 14, 15 [1]. Median = **13** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the total of 62 before subtracting, so a slip in the subtraction is still visible to a reader.

**7. (a)** Σf = 19 + k [1]. Σfx = 2 + 14 + 3k + 24 + 20 = 60 + 3k [1]. Equation: 60 + 3k = 3.1(19 + k) [1]. 60 + 3k = 58.9 + 3.1k, so 1.1 = 0.1k and **k = 11** [1].
**(b)** The highest frequency, 11, is at x = 3, so the mode is **3 rooms** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to divide the total by 5 (the number of rows); the denominator is Σf, which includes k.

**8. (a)** **10-14** (frequency 21) [1].
**(b)** Midpoints 2, 7, 12, 17, 22 [1]. Σfx = 12 + 98 + 252 + 204 + 154 = 720 [1]. Mean ≈ 720 ÷ 60 = **12 texts** [1].
**(c)** The 30th value lies in 10-14, which has boundaries 9.5 and 14.5, with 20 people below [1]. Median ≈ 9.5 + (10 ÷ 21) × 5 = **11.9** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Starting interpolation from the boundary 9.5, not the stated limit 10, is the choice that fits whole-number classes.

**9. (a)** A: 300 ÷ 10 = **30.0** [1]; B: 277 ÷ 10 = **27.7** [1].
**(b)** A: (26 + 27) ÷ 2 = **26.5** [1]; B: (27 + 27) ÷ 2 = **27** [1].
**(c)** A: **25**; B: **27** [1].
**(d)** Use the **median** [1], because courier A's 64 minutes is an extreme value that pushes the mean up to 30.0 [1]. A's median of 26.5 is lower than B's 27, so recommend **courier A** for usually being quicker [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation is stronger when it names the figures compared and admits that the mean points the other way.

**10. (a)** 185 ÷ 12 = **15.4** [1].
**(b)** Midpoints 4.5, 14.5, 24.5: 13.5 + 72.5 + 98 = 184 [1]. 184 ÷ 12 = **15.3** [1].
**(c)** The grouped estimate assumes every score sits at its class midpoint, but the actual scores are spread differently [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good comparison states that the estimate is close but not equal, and gives the assumption as the reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Finding the median without ordering the data.
- Quoting a frequency as the mode.
- Using the number of rows as the divisor instead of Σf.
- Using class limits rather than midpoints for an estimated mean.
- Picking the modal class from the largest frequency when the classes are not equally wide.
- Calling an estimate exact, or not saying why it is an estimate.
- Giving an average without a reason that refers to the data.
- Rounding part-way through, then losing accuracy in the final line.
