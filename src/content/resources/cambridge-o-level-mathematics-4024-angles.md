This guide covers section 4.6, Angles, in the Geometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to calculate unknown angles and give simple explanations using the standard angle facts, to do the same for angles formed within parallel lines, and to know and use the angle properties of regular and irregular polygons, including exterior and interior angles and the angle sum. It also requires three-letter angle notation, for example angle ABC, and the correct geometrical terminology in reasons. After this guide, use the [angles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles-revision-notes/) and the [angles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles-practice/).

## What section 4.6 asks for

1. Use the basic facts: angles at a point, on a straight line, vertically opposite, in a triangle and in a quadrilateral.
2. Use the facts about parallel lines: corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles.
3. Use the angle properties of polygons: interior angles, exterior angles and the angle sum.
4. Write each reason in correct words.

## Three-letter notation

Angle ABC means the angle at B between the lines BA and BC. The middle letter is the vertex. Angle ABC and angle CBA are the same angle.

## The basic angle facts

| Fact | Reason to write |
|---|---|
| Angles at a point add up to 360° | angles at a point |
| Angles on a straight line add up to 180° | angles on a straight line |
| Vertically opposite angles are equal | vertically opposite angles |
| The angles of a triangle add up to 180° | angle sum of a triangle |
| The angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360° | angle sum of a quadrilateral |
| Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal | isosceles triangle |
| Each angle of an equilateral triangle is 60° | equilateral triangle |
| An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles | exterior angle of a triangle |

### Worked example 1

Three angles on a straight line are x, 2x + 15 and 45. Find x.

```
x + (2x + 15) + 45 = 180
3x + 60 = 180
x = 40
```

The angles are 40°, 95° and 45°. They add up to 180°.

### Worked example 2

Four angles at a point are 2x, 3x, 90° and (x + 30)°. Find x.

```
2x + 3x + 90 + x + 30 = 360
6x + 120 = 360
x = 40
```

### Worked example 3

Two vertically opposite angles are (3x + 10)° and (5x − 30)°. Find x and the angle.

```
3x + 10 = 5x − 30
40 = 2x
x = 20, so each angle is 3(20) + 10 = 70°
```

### Worked example 4

In triangle ABC, AB = AC. The side BC is extended to D, and angle ACD = 130°. Find the angles of the triangle.

```
angle ACB = 180° − 130° = 50°     (angles on a straight line)
angle ABC = 50°                    (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
angle BAC = 180° − 50° − 50° = 80° (angle sum of a triangle)
```

## Angles in parallel lines

When a straight line (a transversal) crosses two parallel lines, eight angles are formed, and only two different sizes appear, which add to 180°.

| Name | Shape | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Corresponding angles | F | equal |
| Alternate angles | Z | equal |
| Co-interior angles | C or U | add up to 180° (supplementary) |

These facts only apply when the lines are parallel. Parallel lines are marked with matching arrows. If a question asks you to show lines are parallel, you can use the facts in reverse: equal corresponding or alternate angles, or co-interior angles that sum to 180°.

### Worked example 5

AB and CD are parallel. A transversal meets AB at P and CD at Q, with B and D on the same side of PQ. Angle BPQ = 112°. Find angle PQD and angle PQC.

```
angle PQD = 180° − 112° = 68°   (co-interior angles, AB parallel to CD)
angle PQC = 112°                (alternate angles, AB parallel to CD)
```

Check: angle PQC and angle PQD lie on the straight line CD, so 112° + 68° = 180°. Always name each angle with three letters, so the reason is clear.

### Worked example 6

Through vertex A of triangle ABC, a line DE is drawn parallel to BC. Angle DAB = 52° and angle EAC = 61°. Find angle BAC and show that the angle sum of the triangle is 180°.

```
angle ABC = 52°         (alternate angles, DE parallel to BC)
angle ACB = 61°         (alternate angles, DE parallel to BC)
angle BAC = 180° − 52° − 61° = 67°   (angles on a straight line)
52° + 61° + 67° = 180°
```

This is also the standard proof that the angles of a triangle sum to 180°.

## Angle problems with algebra

Many questions give angles as expressions. Write an equation from the angle fact, solve it, and substitute back.

### Worked example 7

Two co-interior angles between parallel lines are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 5)°. Find x and both angles.

```
(2x + 10) + (3x − 5) = 180      (co-interior angles)
5x + 5 = 180
x = 35
angles: 2(35) + 10 = 80° and 3(35) − 5 = 100°
```

### Worked example 8

An exterior angle of a triangle is (3x − 10)°. The two opposite interior angles are (x + 50)° and 40°. Find x.

```
3x − 10 = (x + 50) + 40         (exterior angle of a triangle)
2x = 100
x = 50
```

The exterior angle is 140°. The interior angles are 100°, 40° and 40°, which add up to 180°.

## Polygons

### Angle sum

A polygon with n sides can be split into n − 2 triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex. So

```
sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°
```

| Polygon | n | Interior angle sum |
|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° |

### Exterior angles

The exterior angles of any polygon, one at each vertex, add up to 360°. At each vertex, interior angle + exterior angle = 180°.

For a **regular** polygon with n sides:

```
exterior angle = 360° ÷ n
interior angle = 180° − exterior angle
```

### Worked example 9

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 160°. How many sides has it?

```
exterior angle = 180° − 160° = 20°
n = 360 ÷ 20 = 18
```

### Worked example 10

Four angles of a pentagon are 100°, 110°, 120° and 95°. Find the fifth.

```
sum of interior angles = (5 − 2) × 180 = 540°
fifth angle = 540° − (100° + 110° + 120° + 95°) = 540° − 425° = 115°
```

### Worked example 11

The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°. Find the number of sides and the sum of the interior angles.

```
n = 360 ÷ 30 = 12
sum of interior angles = (12 − 2) × 180 = 1800°
check: 12 × 150° = 1800°
```

## Writing reasons

Examiners expect precise language. Write the reason after each statement, in brackets, using words from the table above. "Z angles" or "F angles" are not accepted as reasons. Say "alternate angles" and "corresponding angles", and mention the parallel lines.

A good layout is:

```
angle ABC = 70°   (alternate angles, AB parallel to CD)
```

Use three letters to name each angle, as in angle ABC, so there is no doubt which angle you mean.

## Common mistakes

- Using parallel-line facts when the lines are not parallel.
- Mixing up alternate and co-interior angles, so that equal angles are added.
- Giving "Z angles" as a reason instead of "alternate angles".
- Using 360° for the angle sum of a triangle, or 180° for a quadrilateral.
- Forgetting that exterior angles sum to 360° for any polygon.
- Dividing by n − 2 instead of n for a regular polygon's interior angle.
- Giving no reason when asked to explain.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the angle: show each step with its reason.
- **Give a reason** or **explain**: quote the fact in words and name the angle.
- **Show that**: finish with the stated angle.
- Mark angles you find on the diagram as you go.
