Condensed notes on subtopic 5.2 Gravitational potential energy and kinetic
energy (Cambridge 9702, AS Topic 5). For full explanations, use the
[GPE and kinetic energy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-gravitational-potential-energy-and-kinetic-energy/);
for the whole topic, see the [Work, Energy and Power revision notes](/resources/as-physics-work-energy-revision-notes/).

## What 5.2 asks you to do

- Derive ∆Eₚ = mg∆h using W = Fs, for a uniform gravitational field
- Recall and use ∆Eₚ = mg∆h
- Derive Eₖ = ½mv² using the equations of motion
- Recall and use Eₖ = ½mv²

## The two formulae

```
dEp = m g dh        joules; dh = change in VERTICAL height; uniform field only
Ek  = (1/2) m v^2   joules; scalar, never negative
```

## Derivation 1: ∆Eₚ = mg∆h

1. Lift mass m at constant speed, so the lifting force F = mg.
2. Work done W = Fs, with s = ∆h (force and displacement both vertical).
3. W = mg∆h, and this work is stored as gravitational potential energy: ∆Eₚ = mg∆h.

**Condition:** g constant over ∆h (uniform field), so F is constant.

## Derivation 2: Eₖ = ½mv²

1. Constant resultant force F on mass m from rest, over displacement s.
2. F = ma.
3. v² = u² + 2as with u = 0, so s = v²/2a.
4. W = Fs = ma × v²/2a = ½mv², and the work becomes kinetic energy.

**General form:** work done by resultant force = ½mv² − ½mu².

## Using the formulae

| Situation | Equation to start from |
|---|---|
| Falling or sliding, no friction | mg∆h = ½mv² − ½mu² (m cancels) |
| Falling or sliding with resistance | mg∆h = ∆Eₖ + F_resistive × path length |
| Braking to rest | F_brake × s = ½mv² |
| Thrown upwards, maximum height | ½mu² = mg∆h, so ∆h = u²/2g |
| Speed at a lower level, any launch angle | v² = u² + 2g∆h |

**Example — pendulum.** A bob is released from rest 0.45 m above its lowest point. Speed at the bottom:

```
v = sqrt(2 g dh) = sqrt(2 x 9.81 x 0.45) = 2.97 m/s
```

The string tension is perpendicular to the motion at every instant, so it does no work and does not appear in the energy equation.

**Example — roller-coaster car.** A 500 kg car passes over the top of a 25 m drop at 4.0 m s⁻¹. With no friction, the speed at the bottom is:

```
v^2 = u^2 + 2 g dh = 16 + 2 x 9.81 x 25 = 506.5      v = 22.5 m/s
```

The car's mass is not needed.

## Scaling rules

- Eₖ ∝ v²: double the speed, ×4 the kinetic energy; triple it, ×9.
- Eₖ ∝ m at a fixed speed.
- Braking distance ∝ v² for a constant braking force.
- Free-fall speed ∝ √∆h: four times the height gives twice the speed.
- ∆Eₚ ∝ ∆h, regardless of the route taken.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to recall |
|---|---|
| Gravitational potential energy | Energy of a mass due to its position in a gravitational field |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of a mass due to its motion |
| Uniform gravitational field | Field in which g has the same magnitude and direction everywhere in the region considered |
| ∆h | Change in vertical height, not distance along a path |
| Resultant force | Vector sum of all forces; its work equals the change in kinetic energy |

## Exam traps

- Slope length used in mg∆h: always resolve to the vertical height first.
- ½mv² with v not squared, or ½m²v.
- Using mg∆h for an orbit or a rocket far from the Earth: g is not uniform there.
- Missing the starting kinetic energy when an object is already moving at the top.
- In the Eₖ derivation, skipping the equation of motion: the syllabus requires it.
- Writing "kinetic energy is lost" when it is transferred to GPE or dissipated.
- Taking energies as negative because the object moves downwards: Eₖ is never negative, and ∆Eₚ is negative only as a change.

## Self-test

1. Derive ∆Eₚ = mg∆h, stating the condition required.
2. Derive Eₖ = ½mv² using the equations of motion.
3. Find the kinetic energy of a 0.15 kg ball moving at 20 m s⁻¹.
4. Find the gain in GPE when 5.0 kg is lifted through 3.0 m.
5. A stone falls 5.0 m from rest. Find its speed, ignoring air resistance.
6. The speed of a car triples. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?
7. A 2.0 kg trolley has 100 J of kinetic energy. Find its speed.
8. Find the work done by the resultant force when a 900 kg car accelerates from 10 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹.
9. A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 12 m s⁻¹. Find its maximum height, ignoring air resistance.
10. Why does a 2 kg mass and a 4 kg mass dropped from the same height reach the ground at the same speed (no air resistance)?
11. A box is pushed up a ramp 3.0 m long to a platform 1.2 m high. Which distance is used to find its gain in GPE?
12. With a constant braking force, a car stops in 20 m from 12 m s⁻¹. Find the stopping distance from 24 m s⁻¹.

**Answers:**
1. Lift m at constant speed: F = mg; W = Fs = mg∆h, stored as ∆Eₚ. Condition: uniform field (g constant over ∆h).
2. F = ma; v² = 2as from rest, so s = v²/2a; W = Fs = ma(v²/2a) = ½mv².
3. ½ × 0.15 × 20² = **30 J**.
4. 5.0 × 9.81 × 3.0 = **147 J**.
5. v = √(2 × 9.81 × 5.0) = **9.9 m s⁻¹**.
6. **×9**, since Eₖ ∝ v².
7. v = √(2 × 100 ÷ 2.0) = **10 m s⁻¹**.
8. ½ × 900 × (20² − 10²) = **1.35 × 10⁵ J**.
9. ∆h = 12² ÷ (2 × 9.81) = **7.3 m**.
10. mg∆h = ½mv², so m cancels and v = √(2g∆h) for both.
11. The **vertical height, 1.2 m**.
12. Distance ∝ v², so doubling the speed gives 4 × 20 = **80 m**.
