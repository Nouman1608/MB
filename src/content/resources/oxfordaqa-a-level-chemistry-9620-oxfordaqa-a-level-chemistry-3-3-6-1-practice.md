This set practises **3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test tube reactions** and Required practical 5, from section 3.3.6 Organic analysis of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the practical and synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [functional group tests study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-1/) or the [functional group tests revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-1-revision-notes/). Take Ar values as H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which reagent gives a silver mirror when warmed with an aldehyde?

A: Fehling's solution

B: Tollens' reagent

C: bromine water

D: acidified potassium dichromate(VI) **[1]**

**2.** Describe the test for a C=C bond. Give the result for a compound with a C=C bond and for one without. **[2]**

**3.** Describe a test for a carboxylic acid, including how the gas is identified. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why 2-methylbutan-2-ol gives a negative result with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). **[1]**

**5.** Which compound gives a positive result with **both** sodium carbonate solution and acidified potassium dichromate(VI)?

A: propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH

B: propanone, CH₃COCH₃

C: 2-hydroxypropanoic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH

D: propan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ **[1]**

**6.** Predict what is seen when butanal is warmed separately with Fehling's solution and with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Four unlabelled bottles contain hex-1-ene, hexan-2-ol, hexan-2-one and hexanoic acid. Describe a sequence of test tube reactions that identifies each liquid. For each test, give the reagent and the observation that identifies a liquid. **[5]**

**8.** Compound Z, C₄H₈O, is not cyclic. Z gives no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent, leaves acidified potassium dichromate(VI) orange and leaves bromine water orange. Deduce the functional group in Z and give its name. **[3]**

**9.** A student tries to distinguish butanal from butan-1-ol using acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

**(a)** Explain why this test cannot distinguish them. **[2]**

**(b)** Give a test that does distinguish them, with the result for each compound. **[2]**

**10.** Compound W, C₃H₄O₂, fizzes with sodium carbonate solution and turns bromine water from orange to colourless. Identify the two functional groups and give the structure and name of W. **[3]**

**11.** 0.740 g of propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH, is added to excess sodium carbonate solution.

2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + CO₂ + H₂O

Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide released. **[3]**

**12.** A student's notes on Required practical 5 say: "Heated the sample with Fehling's solution over a Bunsen flame. Result: went clear." Give **two** improvements to the method or the recording, with a reason for each. **[4]**

**13.** Predict the observations when 2-methylpropan-2-ol and 2-methylpropanal are each warmed with Tollens' reagent and, separately, with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** Shake with bromine water [1]; C=C present: orange to colourless; no C=C: stays orange [1]

**3.** Add sodium carbonate (or sodium hydrogencarbonate) solution: effervescence [1]; bubble the gas through limewater, which turns milky (carbon dioxide) [1]

**4.** The C–OH carbon in a tertiary alcohol has no hydrogen atom, so the alcohol is not easily oxidised and the dichromate stays orange [1]

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** Fehling's: blue solution gives a red precipitate [1]; dichromate: orange to green [1]

**7.** Add sodium carbonate solution: effervescence identifies hexanoic acid [1]; shake the other three with bromine water: orange to colourless identifies hex-1-ene [1]; warm the remaining two with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]; orange to green identifies hexan-2-ol (a secondary alcohol) [1]; stays orange identifies hexan-2-one (a ketone) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* No aldehyde is present, so dichromate can be used safely here; say why the order you choose works.

**8.** No silver mirror, so Z is not an aldehyde, and orange dichromate rules out primary or secondary alcohols [1]; bromine water stays orange, so there is no C=C; a non-cyclic C₄H₈O compound must then contain C=O [1]; Z is a ketone: **butanone**, CH₃COCH₂CH₃ [1] **[3]**

**9. (a)** Butanal is an aldehyde and is oxidised by acidified dichromate(VI) [1]; butan-1-ol is a primary alcohol and is also oxidised, so both turn the solution from orange to green [1]

**(b)** Warm with Tollens' reagent (or Fehling's solution) [1]; butanal gives a silver mirror (or red precipitate); butan-1-ol gives no silver mirror (or stays blue) [1]

**10.** Carboxylic acid (–COOH) [1]; alkene (C=C) [1]; **CH₂=CHCOOH, propenoic acid** [1] **[3]**

**11.** n(propanoic acid) = 0.740 ÷ 74.0 = 0.0100 mol [1]; n(CO₂) = 0.0100 ÷ 2 = 0.00500 mol [1]; mass = 0.00500 × 44.0 = **0.220 g** [1] **[3]**

**12.** Warm in a hot water bath instead of over a Bunsen flame [1]; because the organic liquid is flammable [1]; record colours before and after, using "colourless" or the actual colour and precipitate seen, not "clear" [1]; because "clear" means transparent and does not describe a colour change, so the result cannot be interpreted [1] **[4]**

**13.** 2-Methylpropan-2-ol with Tollens': no silver mirror [1]; with dichromate: stays orange (tertiary alcohol) [1]; 2-methylpropanal with Tollens': silver mirror [1]; with dichromate: orange to green [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- "Clear" instead of "colourless".
- No observation given for the compound that does not react.
- Dichromate used to identify alcohols while aldehydes are still possible.
- Tertiary alcohols assumed to turn dichromate green.
- Fehling's and Tollens' results swapped.
