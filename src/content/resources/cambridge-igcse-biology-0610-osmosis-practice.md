## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes a partially permeable membrane? **[1]**

A: It lets all molecules pass through it

B: It lets no molecules pass through it

C: It lets small molecules such as water through, but not larger molecules such as sucrose

D: It lets sucrose through, but not water

**2.** State **one** way in which water acts as a solvent in:

**(a)** excretion **[1]**

**(b)** transport in plants. **[1]**

**3.** State how water helps to support a plant. **[1]**

**4.** A potato cylinder has a mass of 2.80 g. After 30 minutes in a sucrose solution its mass is 2.52 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Give the term for each description.

**(a)** The pressure of the cell contents pushing outwards on the cell wall. **[1]**

**(b)** The cell membrane and cytoplasm pulling away from the cell wall. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student placed potato cylinders in sucrose solutions for 40 minutes.

| Sucrose concentration / mol/dm³ | Start mass / g | End mass / g | Percentage change / % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 3.00 | 3.30 | +10.0 |
| 0.1 | 3.20 | 3.36 | ? |
| 0.3 | 3.10 | 3.07 | −1.0 |
| 0.5 | 3.00 | 2.76 | ? |
| 0.7 | 3.25 | 2.86 | −12.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the two missing percentage changes. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the trend in the results. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the sucrose concentration at which the potato would show no change in mass. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the cylinder in 0.7 mol/dm³ lost mass. **[2]**

**(e)** State **two** variables, other than concentration, that should be kept the same. **[2]**

**7.** A length of dialysis tubing contains a dilute sucrose solution. It is placed in a beaker of concentrated sucrose solution. Predict and explain what happens to the mass of the tubing. **[3]**

**8.** Explain the role of water as a solvent in digestion. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Onion epidermis cells are placed in a concentrated salt solution and viewed under a microscope. Explain, using water potential, what happens to the cells. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Explain why a red blood cell placed in distilled water bursts. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A gardener adds far too much fertiliser to the soil around a plant, and the plant wilts. Explain why. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why the student in question 6 blotted each cylinder before weighing it and calculated percentage change rather than change in mass. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Urea (or other waste) is dissolved in water and removed in urine [1].

**(b)** Mineral ions are carried dissolved in water in the xylem, or sucrose is carried dissolved in the phloem [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The pressure of water inside the cells presses outwards on the cell wall, keeping the cells firm [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Change = 2.52 − 2.80 = −0.28 g; −0.28 ÷ 2.80 × 100 [1] = **−10%** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Turgor pressure [1].

**(b)** Plasmolysis [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 0.1 mol/dm³: 0.16 ÷ 3.20 × 100 = **+5.0%** [1]. 0.5 mol/dm³: −0.24 ÷ 3.00 × 100 = **−8.0%** [1].

**(b)** As sucrose concentration increases, the percentage change in mass decreases [1], from a gain of 10.0% in water to a loss of 12.0% at 0.7 mol/dm³ [1].

**(c)** About 0.27 mol/dm³ (accept 0.25 to 0.29) [1].

**(d)** The solution was more concentrated than the cell contents [1], so water moved out of the potato cells by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membranes [1].

**(e)** Any two: size of cylinders [1]; volume of solution [1]; time in solution; temperature; type or source of potato. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c), the answer comes from where the change crosses zero, between 0.1 and 0.3 mol/dm³ and much closer to 0.3. Any value in the accepted range scores.

**7.** The tubing loses mass [1]. The solution outside is more concentrated than the solution inside [1], so water moves out of the tubing by osmosis through the partially permeable tubing, and sucrose cannot pass through [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Enzymes digest food in solution [1], and the small soluble products, such as glucose and amino acids, dissolve in water so they can be absorbed into the blood [1]. **[2]**

**9.** The salt solution has a lower water potential than the cell sap [1]. Water moves out of the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membrane [1]. The vacuoles shrink and the cells become flaccid [1]. As more water leaves, the membrane and cytoplasm pull away from the cell wall: the cells are plasmolysed [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Distilled water has a higher water potential than the cytoplasm of the red blood cell [1]. Water enters the cell by osmosis through its partially permeable membrane [1]. Animal cells have no cell wall to resist the pressure, so the cell swells and bursts [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The fertiliser dissolves in soil water and lowers its water potential [1]. The soil water now has a lower water potential than the cell sap of the root hair cells [1], so water cannot enter by osmosis and may even leave the roots [1]. The plant loses water from its leaves faster than it takes it up, so its cells become flaccid and it wilts [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Blotting removes surface water, which would add mass that had not entered the cells by osmosis [1]. The cylinders started with different masses, so percentage change allows a fair comparison between them [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying sucrose or salt moves in or out of the cells in these explanations.
- Writing "high to low concentration" without saying of what.
- Describing plasmolysis in animal cells, or saying plant cells burst.
- (Extended) Not comparing water potential **inside** and **outside** the cell.
- Describing a trend without the change from gain to loss and the values.

## Frequently asked questions

**What do Core candidates write instead of water potential?**
Describe the solutions as dilute or concentrated, and say water moves from the dilute to the more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane.

**Why does the mass change cross zero?**
At one concentration the solution matches the cell contents, so there is no net movement of water.
