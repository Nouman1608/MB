These questions cover subtopic 1.8 Standard form in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 9 and 14 suit both tiers: conversions and ordering are non-calculator, and calculations are on calculator questions, because Core candidates calculate with standard form only on Paper 3. Questions 10 to 13 are labelled **(Extended)**: they are non-calculator calculations of the kind Extended candidates can meet on Paper 2. Figures in context questions are invented or rounded for practice.

Learn the methods first in the [standard form study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-standard-form/) and the [standard form revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-standard-form-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write in standard form

**(a)** 4 500 000 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.000 702. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Write as an ordinary number

**(a)** 3.9 × 10⁴ **[1]**

**(b)** 6.05 × 10⁻³. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Write in standard form

**(a)** 0.56 × 10⁻³ **[1]**

**(b)** 720 × 10⁵. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order, largest first: 2.1 × 10⁵, 98 000, 1.95 × 10⁵, 2.01 × 10⁵ **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Explain why 12.5 × 10³ is not written in standard form. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator) Work out, giving each answer in standard form,

**(a)** (7.4 × 10⁵) × (3.1 × 10⁻²), correct to 3 significant figures **[2]**

**(b)** (2.88 × 10⁴) ÷ (6.4 × 10⁻⁵). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) The world population is taken as 8.1 × 10⁹ and the world's land area as 1.49 × 10⁸ km². Find the mean number of people per km², correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator) Light travels at 3 × 10⁵ km per second. Find the time it takes light to travel 5.8 × 10⁷ km. Give your answer in minutes and seconds, to the nearest second. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator) A grain of sand has a mass of 6.7 × 10⁻⁴ g. Find the number of grains in 2.5 kg of sand, giving your answer in standard form correct to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Work out, giving each answer in standard form,

**(a)** (6 × 10⁸) × (5 × 10⁻³) **[2]**

**(b)** (2.4 × 10⁻²) ÷ (8 × 10³). **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Work out 4.6 × 10⁵ + 7.3 × 10⁴, giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Work out, giving each answer in standard form,

**(a)** (3 × 10⁻⁴)² **[1]**

**(b)** √(4.9 × 10⁹). **[2]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) x = 4 × 10ⁿ, where n is an integer. Write x² in standard form, giving the power of 10 in terms of n. **[2]**

**14.** (calculator) A country uses 3.6 × 10¹¹ kWh of electricity a year and has a population of 4.5 × 10⁷. Find the mean use per person, as an ordinary number. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **4.5 × 10⁶** [1]

**(b)** **7.02 × 10⁻⁴** [1]

**2. (a)** **39 000** [1]

**(b)** **0.006 05** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b), moving the point three places left puts two zeros after the decimal point before the 6.

**3. (a)** 0.56 = 5.6 × 10⁻¹, so **5.6 × 10⁻⁴** [1]

**(b)** 720 = 7.2 × 10², so **7.2 × 10⁷** [1]

**4.** 98 000 = 9.8 × 10⁴ [1]; **2.1 × 10⁵, 2.01 × 10⁵, 1.95 × 10⁵, 98 000** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 2.1 is greater than 2.01 (2.10 against 2.01); compare A digit by digit when the powers are equal.

**5.** The number in front, 12.5, is not less than 10; standard form needs 1 ⩽ A < 10 (it would be 1.25 × 10⁴) [1]

**6. (a)** 22 940 [1]; **2.29 × 10⁴** [1]

**(b)** 450 000 000 [1]; **4.5 × 10⁸** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the answer to (b) is exact, so it is not rounded; giving 4.50 × 10⁸ is also accepted.

**7.** (8.1 × 10⁹) ÷ (1.49 × 10⁸) = 54.362… [1]; **54.4** people per km² [1]

**8.** (5.8 × 10⁷) ÷ (3 × 10⁵) = 193.33… s [1]; 193 s = 3 × 60 + 13 [1]; **3 minutes 13 seconds** [1]

**9.** 2.5 kg = 2500 g [1]; 2500 ÷ (6.7 × 10⁻⁴) = 3 731 343.2… [1]; **3.73 × 10⁶** [1]

*Examiner insight:* without the unit conversion the answer is 1000 times too small; the conversion mark is available even if the division goes wrong.

**10. (a)** 30 × 10⁵ [1] = **3 × 10⁶** [1]

**(b)** 0.3 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **3 × 10⁻⁶** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b), the power is −2 − 3 = −5, then A = 0.3 needs fixing, which lowers the power to −6.

**11.** 7.3 × 10⁴ = 0.73 × 10⁵ [1]; 4.6 + 0.73 = 5.33, so **5.33 × 10⁵** [1]

**12. (a)** **9 × 10⁻⁸** [1]

**(b)** 4.9 × 10⁹ = 49 × 10⁸ [1]; √49 × √10⁸ = **7 × 10⁴** [1]

**13.** x² = 16 × 10²ⁿ [1] = **1.6 × 10²ⁿ⁺¹** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (10ⁿ)² = 10²ⁿ; the extra 1 in the power comes from writing 16 as 1.6 × 10.

**14.** (3.6 × 10¹¹) ÷ (4.5 × 10⁷) [1] = **8000 kWh** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving A outside 1 ⩽ A < 10 after a multiplication or division.
- Positive powers for small numbers, or the reverse.
- Adding the powers when adding two standard form numbers.
- Forgetting to convert kg to g or km to m before dividing.
- Copying the calculator's display format instead of writing "× 10".

## Approaching standard form questions

Work out A and the power of 10 separately, then tidy. On calculator questions, enter each number with the ×10ˣ key and write down the full display before rounding A. In context questions, convert units first, decide whether the answer should be large or small, and check the power of 10 against that expectation.

**Why are Core calculation questions on this page labelled calculator?**
The 0580 syllabus says Core candidates are expected to calculate with standard form only on Paper 3, which is a calculator paper.
