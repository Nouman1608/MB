These notes condense section 3.1.2 (Cells and cell structure) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) specification, Version 5.1, in Unit 1. For explanations and worked examples, use the [cells and cell structure study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-cells-and-cell-structure/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-cells-and-cell-structure-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnification | Size of image divided by size of object |
| Resolution | The smallest distance between two points that can be seen as separate |
| Artefact | Something on an image caused by specimen preparation, not present in the living cell |
| Ultrastructure | Fine detail of a cell seen only with an electron microscope |
| Homogenisation | Breaking up cells to release organelles |
| Ultracentrifugation | Spinning a homogenate at increasing speeds to separate organelles by density |
| Tissue | A group of similar cells working together |
| Organ | Several tissues working together |
| Prokaryote | A cell with no nucleus and no membrane-bounded organelles |
| Plasmid | A small circular piece of DNA in many prokaryotes |

## The magnification formula

magnification = size of image / size of object

| To find | Rearrange to | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Magnification | image / object | Same units top and bottom |
| Real size | image / magnification | Convert the answer to µm |
| Image size | object x magnification | Convert the answer to mm |

Conversions: 1 mm = 1,000 µm; 1 µm = 1,000 nm.

Quick examples: an image of 36 mm at x12,000 is 36 / 12,000 = 0.003 mm = 3 µm. An image of 60 mm at x40,000 is 1.5 µm. A 2.4 mm image of an 8 µm object is x300.

## Microscopes in one table

| | Optical | TEM | SEM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiation | Light | Electron beam through specimen | Electron beam over surface |
| Resolution | Lowest | Highest | High (below TEM) |
| Gives | Colour 2D image | 2D internal detail | 3D surface detail |
| Alive? | Possible | No (vacuum) | No (vacuum) |
| Main limit | Cannot resolve small organelles | Thin, dead specimens; artefacts | No internal structure |

**Reason for difference:** electrons have a shorter wavelength than light, so they resolve smaller structures.

## Cell fractionation

1. **Homogenise** in a cold, isotonic, buffered solution.
   - Cold: slows enzymes (stops digestion by released lysosome enzymes).
   - Isotonic: no osmosis, so organelles stay intact.
   - Buffered: constant pH, so proteins are not denatured.
2. **Filter** to remove debris.
3. **Centrifuge** at low speed; the pellet is the heaviest organelles (nuclei). Pour off the supernatant and spin faster.
4. Order of pellets: nuclei, chloroplasts or mitochondria, lysosomes, then endoplasmic reticulum fragments and ribosomes.

## Eukaryotic structures: quick recall

| Structure | Identify by | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Double membrane with pores; dark chromatin | Holds DNA; controls the cell |
| Mitochondrion | Double membrane; inner folds (cristae) | Aerobic respiration; ATP |
| Chloroplast | Double membrane; grana of thylakoids; stroma | Photosynthesis |
| Golgi apparatus | Stack of flattened sacs and vesicles | Modifies and packages; makes lysosomes |
| Lysosome | Small sac with one membrane | Digests with hydrolytic enzymes |
| Ribosome | Tiny dots | Protein synthesis |
| Rough ER | Folded membranes with ribosomes | Makes and transports protein |
| Smooth ER | Folded membranes without ribosomes | Makes and transports lipids |
| Vacuole | Large sac with tonoplast | Store; keeps cell turgid |
| Cell wall | Outside the membrane (plants: cellulose) | Support; prevents bursting |
| Plasma membrane | Thin bilayer | Controls entry and exit |

**Structure to function pairs that score:** cristae give a large surface area for ATP production; nuclear pores let mRNA leave; thylakoid membranes hold the pigments for the light-dependent reactions; rough ER with ribosomes is abundant in protein-secreting cells.

## Levels of organisation

Cell, then tissue, then organ, then system, then organism. A specialised cell has the organelles its job needs.

## Prokaryotes against eukaryotes

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Smaller | Larger |
| DNA | Circular, no proteins, free in cytoplasm | Linear, with histones, inside a nucleus |
| Organelles | No membrane-bounded organelles | Present |
| Ribosomes | Small | Larger |
| Cell wall | Murein (a glycoprotein) | Cellulose in plants; none in animals |
| Extras | Plasmids, capsule, flagella (many) | Not typical features |

## Exam traps

- Magnification and resolution are not the same thing.
- Unit conversion first, calculation second.
- "Electron microscopes show living cells" is false.
- Prokaryotes do have DNA. It is not in a nucleus.
- "Isotonic" needs a reason: no net osmosis, so organelles do not burst or shrink.
- Chloroplast and mitochondrion both have double membranes. Tell them apart by cristae and grana.

## Quick self-test

1. Define resolution.
2. A cell is 25 µm wide. How wide is its image at x800, in mm?
3. Why is an electron microscope able to resolve more detail than an optical microscope?
4. Why must the homogenising solution be cold?
5. Which organelles pellet first in ultracentrifugation?
6. Name two structures with a double membrane.
7. What is the function of the Golgi apparatus?
8. State the function of lysosomes.
9. What is the cell wall of a bacterium made of?
10. Name two features that some bacteria have but animal and plant cells do not.
11. Why is the SEM unsuitable for studying the inside of an organelle?
12. A scale bar of 15 mm is labelled 5 µm. What is the magnification?

### Answers

1. The smallest distance between two points that can still be seen as separate.
2. 25 µm x 800 = 20,000 µm = 20 mm.
3. Electrons have a shorter wavelength than light.
4. To slow enzymes, including those released from lysosomes, so they do not digest the organelles.
5. Nuclei, the densest and heaviest.
6. Any two from nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast.
7. It modifies and packages proteins and lipids, and makes lysosomes.
8. They contain hydrolytic enzymes that digest worn-out organelles and engulfed material.
9. Murein, a glycoprotein.
10. Any two from plasmids, a capsule, murein in the cell wall, circular DNA free in the cytoplasm.
11. It scans the surface only, so it does not show internal structure.
12. 5 µm = 0.005 mm; 15 / 0.005 = x3,000.
