## The Haber process

- Makes **ammonia** for **nitrogen-based fertilisers**.
- **N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)** (reversible, exothermic forwards).

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen source | **Air** |
| Hydrogen source | **Natural gas** (methane) |
| Catalyst | **Iron** |
| Temperature | About **450 °C** |
| Pressure | About **200 atmospheres** |
| Ammonia removed by | **Cooling** (it liquefies) |
| Unreacted N₂ and H₂ | **Recycled** |

Volume ratio: N₂ : H₂ : NH₃ = **1 : 3 : 2**.

## Higher tier only: conditions and the trade-off

| Condition | Effect on yield | Effect on rate | Why a compromise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher pressure | **Increases** (4 molecules to 2) | Increases | High pressure needs strong, costly equipment and a lot of energy |
| Higher temperature | **Decreases** (exothermic forwards) | Increases | Lower temperature gives a better yield but is too slow |
| Catalyst | No effect | **Increases** | Lets the reaction be fast at a lower temperature |
| Removing NH₃ and recycling | Increases overall yield | - | Needs cooling equipment |

- The **trade-off** is between **rate** and **position of equilibrium**.
- Commercial conditions relate to **availability and cost** of raw materials and energy, **control of
  equilibrium** and **rate**.
- Graph reading: yield falls as temperature rises; yield rises as pressure rises.

## NPK fertilisers

- **Compounds of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium** improve agricultural productivity.
- **NPK fertilisers** contain all three; they are **formulations** of salts in set percentages.
- **Ammonia** makes **ammonium salts** and **nitric acid**.
- NH₃ + HNO₃ → **NH₄NO₃** (ammonium nitrate); 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → **(NH₄)₂SO₄** (ammonium sulfate).
- **Potassium chloride, potassium sulfate and phosphate rock** are **mined**.
- **Phosphate rock** is **insoluble**, so it is treated with acid to make **soluble salts**.

| Rock treated with | Products |
|---|---|
| Nitric acid | Phosphoric acid and calcium nitrate |
| Sulfuric acid | Calcium phosphate and calcium sulfate |
| Phosphoric acid | Calcium phosphate |

## Calculation: percentage nitrogen

%N = (mass of N in the formula ÷ Mr) × 100.

- NH₄NO₃: Mr 80; N = 28; %N = **35%**.
- (NH₄)₂SO₄: Mr 132; N = 28; %N = **21.2%**.

## Industry and laboratory compared

| | Industry | Laboratory |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Very large | Small |
| Process | Continuous, several integrated processes | Batch: titration, evaporate, crystallise |
| Raw materials | Made on site from ammonia | Bought chemicals |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Haber process | Industrial process that makes ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen |
| Reversible reaction | Products can turn back into reactants |
| Compromise | Conditions chosen to balance rate, yield and cost |
| NPK fertiliser | Formulation containing nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium compounds |
| Phosphate rock | Mined, insoluble source of phosphorus |
| Recycled | Unreacted gases returned to the reactor |

## Exam traps

- **Catalyst raises yield.** It raises rate only.
- **High temperature raises yield.** It lowers it.
- **No recycling mentioned.** Unreacted gases are recycled.
- **Hydrogen from water or air.** It is from natural gas.
- **Fertiliser = ammonia.** Fertilisers are ammonium salts and others.
- **Phosphate rock used directly.** It is insoluble.
- **Pressure as a free choice.** High pressure is costly.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the raw materials of the Haber process and a source for each.
2. State the catalyst, temperature and pressure used.
3. Write the equation for the reaction.
4. How is ammonia separated from the gas mixture?
5. What happens to unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen?
6. What do the letters NPK stand for?
7. Why must phosphate rock be treated with acid?
8. Name the salts formed when phosphate rock reacts with sulfuric acid.
9. (Higher tier only) Why is a pressure of about 200 atmospheres used rather than a much higher one?
10. (Higher tier only) Explain why 450 °C is a compromise.
11. (Higher tier only) State the effect of the iron catalyst on the position of equilibrium.
12. Calculate the percentage of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate (Mr 80).

### Answers

1. Nitrogen from the air; hydrogen from natural gas.
2. Iron; about 450 °C; about 200 atmospheres.
3. N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.
4. By cooling, so the ammonia liquefies and is removed.
5. They are recycled back to the reactor.
6. Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium.
7. It is insoluble, so plants cannot absorb it; acid makes soluble salts.
8. Calcium phosphate and calcium sulfate.
9. Higher pressure would give a higher yield, but needs stronger, more expensive equipment and more energy.
10. A lower temperature gives a better yield but the reaction is too slow; a higher temperature is fast but gives a lower yield.
11. None; it only increases the rate.
12. 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35%.

## Worked answers to model

**Describe the Haber process.** "Nitrogen from the air and hydrogen from natural gas are purified and passed over
an iron catalyst at about 450 °C and 200 atmospheres. Some of the gases react to make ammonia. The mixture is
cooled so that the ammonia liquefies and is removed. Unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled."

**Explain why the temperature is a compromise (Higher tier only).** "The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower
temperature gives a higher yield, but the rate would be too slow. A higher temperature is faster but lowers the
yield. 450 °C gives an acceptable rate and yield."

## Why three elements in NPK

| Element | Use in the plant |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | Making proteins and leaf growth |
| Phosphorus | Roots and energy transfer |
| Potassium | Flowers, fruit and disease resistance |

## Reading an NPK label

A bag labelled 20 : 10 : 10 contains 20 % nitrogen, 10 % phosphorus and 10 % potassium compounds by the
manufacturer's convention. In a 50 kg bag, 20 % is **10 kg** of nitrogen compound in terms of that label.
