## Section A

**1.** A fair six-sided dice is rolled once. Write down the probability that it shows

**(a)** a 7, **[1]**

**(b)** a number less than 7. **[1]**

**2.** A bag contains 3 red, 5 blue and 2 white counters. One counter is taken at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that it is blue. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that it is not white. **[1]**

**3.** A spinner can land on red, blue, green or yellow. P(red) = 0.15 and P(blue) = 0.3. Green and yellow are equally likely.

**(a)** Work out P(green). **[2]**

**(b)** The spinner is spun 400 times. Work out the expected number of times it lands on green. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the probability that it lands on red or blue. **[1]**

**4.** Two fair dice are rolled and their scores are added.

**(a)** Work out the probability that the total is 9. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the probability of rolling a double. **[1]**

**5.** Two of four people, A, B, C and D, are chosen at random to be captains.

**(a)** List all the possible pairs. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that A is one of the captains. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** In a group of 40 students, 22 like tea, 18 like coffee and 7 like neither.

**(a)** Work out how many students like both. **[2]**

**(b)** A student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student likes exactly one of the drinks. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the probability that the student likes coffee but not tea. **[1]**

**7.** Priya spins a spinner 50 times and it lands on red 14 times. Sam spins the same spinner 500 times and it lands on red 162 times.

**(a)** Work out the relative frequency of red for each person. **[2]**

**(b)** Whose estimate of P(red) is more reliable? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Use the better estimate to predict how many times the spinner lands on red in 2000 spins. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why P(even) + P(greater than 3) is **not** the probability of getting an even number or a number greater than 3 on one roll of a fair dice, and find the correct probability. **[2]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** The probability that Lena's train is on time is 0.9. The probability that her bus is on time is 0.7. The events are independent.

**(a)** Draw a tree diagram to show this information. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the probability that exactly one of them is on time. **[2]**

**(c)** Work out the probability that at least one of them is on time. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** A bag contains 3 red and 5 green counters. Two counters are taken at random, one after the other, without replacement.

**(a)** Work out the probability that both are red. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the probability that they are different colours. **[2]**

**(c)** Work out the probability that at least one is green. **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** An archer hits a target with probability 0.6 on each shot, independently. She takes three shots.

**(a)** Work out the probability that she hits with all three. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the probability that she hits with exactly one shot. **[2]**

**(c)** Work out the probability that she hits at least once. **[1]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** A bag holds 4 red and 6 green counters. Three counters are taken at random without replacement. Work out the probability that all three are the same colour. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **0** [1].

**(b)** **1** [1].

**2. (a)** 5/10 = **1/2** [1].

**(b)** 8/10 = **4/5** [1].

**3. (a)** 1 − 0.15 − 0.3 = 0.55 [1]; 0.55 ÷ 2 = **0.275** [1].

**(b)** 0.275 × 400 = **110** [1].

**(c)** 0.15 + 0.3 = **0.45** [1].

**4. (a)** (3, 6), (4, 5), (5, 4), (6, 3): 4 outcomes out of 36 [1]; **1/9** [1].

**(b)** 6/36 = **1/6** [1].

**5. (a)** AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD [2] (1 mark for at least four correct with no repeats).

**(b)** 3/6 = **1/2** [1].

**6. (a)** 40 − 7 = 33 like at least one [1]; 22 + 18 − 33 = **7** [1].

**(b)** Tea only 15 and coffee only 11; (15 + 11)/40 = **13/20** [1].

**(c)** **11/40** [1].

**7. (a)** Priya 14/50 = 0.28 [1]; Sam 162/500 = 0.324 [1].

**(b)** Sam's, because it is based on more trials [1].

**(c)** 0.324 × 2000 = **648** [1].

**8.** The events are not mutually exclusive: 4 and 6 are both even and greater than 3, so adding counts them twice [1]. The outcomes are 2, 4, 5, 6, so the probability is **4/6 = 2/3** [1].

**9. (a)** Train branches 0.9 (on time) and 0.1 (late) [1]; from each, bus branches 0.7 (on time) and 0.3 (late) [1].

**(b)** 0.9 × 0.3 + 0.1 × 0.7 [1] = **0.34** [1].

**(c)** 1 − 0.1 × 0.3 = **0.97** [1].

**10. (a)** 3/8 × 2/7 [1] = **6/56 = 3/28** [1].

**(b)** 3/8 × 5/7 + 5/8 × 3/7 [1] = 30/56 = **15/28** [1].

**(c)** 1 − 3/28 = **25/28** [1].

**11. (a)** 0.6³ = **0.216** [1].

**(b)** One route is 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.096 [1]; there are three positions for the hit: 3 × 0.096 = **0.288** [1].

**(c)** 1 − 0.4³ = 1 − 0.064 = **0.936** [1].

**12.** P(all red) = 4/10 × 3/9 × 2/8 = 24/720 [1]; P(all green) = 6/10 × 5/9 × 4/8 = 120/720 [1]; total = 144/720 = **1/5** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 4(a): counting (3, 6) and (6, 3) as one outcome, giving 2/36.
- Question 6(a): adding 22 and 18 and forgetting the overlap is counted twice.
- Question 8: adding 1/2 and 1/2 to get 1, which cannot be right because a 1 or a 3 is neither.
- Question 10: using 3/8 × 3/8 as if the first counter were replaced.
- Question 11(b): finding one route (0.096) but not multiplying by the three positions.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do you add probabilities and when do you multiply?**
Add for mutually exclusive outcomes of one event ("this or that"); multiply along a tree diagram for one event followed by another ("this and then that").

**How does "without replacement" change a tree diagram?**
The second set of branches uses a total one smaller than the first, and the count of the colour already taken is also one smaller.
