These questions cover section **3.3.9.2 Acylation** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Acylation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-acylation/) and the [Acylation revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-acylation-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** Draw or write the structural formula of each compound.

**(a)** ethanoyl chloride **[1]**

**(b)** ethanoic anhydride **[1]**

**(c)** ethanamide **[1]**

**2.** Explain why the carbon atom of the C=O group in ethanoyl chloride is more susceptible to attack by nucleophiles than the carbon atom in ethanoic acid. **[2]**

**3.** Ethanoyl chloride is added to water.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State two observations. **[2]**

**4.** Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with ethanol and name the organic product. **[2]**

**5.** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with ammonia. Show the intermediate and the products. **[5]**

**6.** Ethanoyl chloride reacts with methylamine, CH₃NH₂.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why two moles of methylamine are needed for each mole of ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**7.** Write equations for the reactions of ethanoic anhydride with:

**(a)** water **[1]**

**(b)** ammonia **[1]**

**8.** Aspirin is made from 2-hydroxybenzoic acid and ethanoic anhydride.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. Use the formulae C₆H₄(OH)COOH and C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH. **[1]**

**(b)** Give two reasons why ethanoic anhydride is preferred to ethanoyl chloride in industry. **[2]**

**9.** A student prepares aspirin from 2.76 g of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (Mᵣ = 138.0) and excess ethanoic anhydride. The yield of purified aspirin (Mᵣ = 180.0) is 2.52 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**10.** 3.925 g of ethanoyl chloride (Mᵣ = 78.5) reacts with excess ammonia. Calculate the maximum mass of ethanamide (Mᵣ = 59.0) that could be formed. **[3]**

**11.** The crude aspirin is purified by recrystallisation.

**(a)** Describe how recrystallisation is carried out. **[4]**

**(b)** Describe how the purity of the final product is tested and what result shows it is pure. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the reactivity of ethanoyl chloride and ethanoic anhydride towards water, and explain the difference. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** CH₃COCl [1]

**(b)** (CH₃CO)₂O, with two CH₃C(=O)– groups joined through one oxygen atom [1]

**(c)** CH₃CONH₂ [1]. **[3]**

**2.** In ethanoyl chloride the carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, oxygen and chlorine [1]; both withdraw electron density, so the carbon has a larger partial positive charge than in ethanoic acid (and chloride is a good leaving group) [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl [1]

**(b)** Steamy white fumes of hydrogen chloride [1]; vigorous reaction (heat released) [1]. **[3]**

**4.** CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl [1]; ethyl ethanoate [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Lone pair on N of NH₃ attacks the δ+ carbon of C=O, with an arrow from the lone pair to carbon [1]; the C=O pi bond moves onto oxygen, giving O⁻ in the intermediate [1]; the intermediate is shown with N⁺H₃, O⁻ and Cl on carbon [1]; the lone pair on O⁻ reforms C=O and the C–Cl bond breaks, giving Cl⁻ [1]; loss of H⁺ (to a second NH₃) gives CH₃CONH₂ and NH₄⁺ [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The two stages are addition (nucleophile joins) and elimination (chloride leaves). Show both, with arrows that start at lone pairs or bonds.

**6. (a)** CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃Cl [1]

**(b)** N-methylethanamide [1]

**(c)** One molecule acts as the nucleophile and the second removes the proton (H⁺) from the nitrogen, forming the salt CH₃NH₃Cl [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** (CH₃CO)₂O + H₂O → 2CH₃COOH [1]

**(b)** (CH₃CO)₂O + NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + CH₃COOH [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** C₆H₄(OH)COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH + CH₃COOH [1]

**(b)** Any two from: cheaper; less corrosive; reacts less violently with water (easier and safer to handle); the by-product is ethanoic acid, not corrosive hydrogen chloride gas [2]. **[3]**

**9.** n(2-hydroxybenzoic acid) = 2.76 ÷ 138.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; theoretical mass of aspirin = 0.0200 × 180.0 = 3.60 g [1]; percentage yield = 2.52 ÷ 3.60 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** n(CH₃COCl) = 3.925 ÷ 78.5 = **0.0500 mol** [1]; n(ethanamide) = 0.0500 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.0500 × 59.0 = **2.95 g** [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Dissolve the crude solid in the minimum volume of hot solvent [1]; filter hot to remove insoluble impurities [1]; cool slowly so crystals form [1]; filter off the crystals, wash with a little ice-cold solvent and dry [1]

**(b)** Measure the melting point [1]; a pure sample melts sharply at the data-book value, whereas an impure sample melts over a wider range at a lower temperature [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Ethanoyl chloride reacts vigorously with cold water, whereas ethanoic anhydride reacts much more slowly [1]; the carbonyl carbon in the acyl chloride is more δ+ because it is bonded to O and Cl [1]; and chloride is a better leaving group than the ethanoate ion [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- A curly arrow that does not start at a lone pair or a bond.
- A missing O⁻ or charge in the intermediate.
- Giving water as a product of an acyl chloride reacting with an alcohol.
- Using one mole of ammonia or amine per mole of acyl chloride.
- Omitting the N- prefix in the name of the product with a primary amine.
- Listing "cheaper" only, with no second reason, for the industrial advantage.
