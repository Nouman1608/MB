# Choosing your form and designing for the audience

The endorsement has no marks and is reported separately (Not Classified, Pass, Merit or Distinction); it does not change your written grade. Check your centre's exact arrangements with your teacher.

Companion to the reviewed [Spoken Language Endorsement study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-english-language-spoken-language-endorsement-study-guide/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-english-language-spoken-language-endorsement-revision-notes/).

## What the specification allows

- A prepared presentation of no longer than 10 minutes, followed by questions and feedback.
- Two forms: (a) a speech or talk followed by audience questions; (b) a formal debate or dialogue, such as an interview with prepared extended responses to prompts shared in advance, followed by audience questions.
- The audience must always include your teacher. A teacher-only audience is allowed, but the presentation should be designed for a potentially wider audience, for example replicating a television interview.
- Assessment is live or from recordings.

## Which form suits you?

| If you... | Consider |
|---|---|
| Like to build one argument from start to finish | A speech or talk |
| Prefer to think in answer to a question | A formal interview or dialogue with prepared prompts |
| Get nervous alone at the front | A dialogue, but prepare extended responses |

Both forms are assessed on AO6: presentation skills in a formal setting, listening and responding, and spoken Standard English.

## Worked example: adapting a talk for a wider audience

Topic (original): *Should school start later?* Your audience is only your teacher.
1. Imagine a wider audience, for example a school assembly.
2. Define terms the assembly might not know.
3. Open with a question for everyone.
4. Speak to the whole room, not only to the teacher.
5. Plan answers for questions from a student, a parent and a teacher.

## Worked example: preparing prompts for an interview form

Prompts shared in advance: "Why did you choose this topic?", "What is the strongest argument against your view?", "What would you change in your school?" For each, write a two-part response: answer first, then reason and example. Rehearse each to about 60 seconds and leave time within 10 minutes for questions.

## Common errors

- Choosing a form because it looks easier, not because it suits your strengths.
- Speaking only to the teacher.
- Planning a talk that fills 10 minutes with no time for questions.

## Check your understanding

1. State the two forms. 2. Can the audience be only your teacher? *Yes, but design it for a potentially wider audience.* 3. What is the maximum length? *10 minutes.*
