## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between the balance of trade and the terms of trade. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by tariff escalation and how it affects exporters of raw materials. **[3]**

**3.** The table shows price indices for Morundi, a fictional coffee-exporting country (2010 = 100).

| Year | Export price index | Import price index |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 100 | 100 |
| 2015 | 92 | 104 |
| 2020 | 78 | 110 |
| 2025 | 101 | 118 |

**(a)** Calculate Morundi's terms of trade index for 2020. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the changes in Morundi's terms of trade between 2010 and 2025. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why a fall in the terms of trade causes problems for Morundi. **[3]**

**4.** Explain the difference between trade creation and trade diversion when a trade bloc is formed. **[4]**

**5.** In one year Calvera, a fictional island country, exported goods worth US$18 billion and services worth US$9 billion, of which tourism was US$7 billion. It imported goods worth US$25 billion and services worth US$3 billion.

**(a)** Calculate Calvera's visible balance, invisible balance and overall balance on goods and services. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why Calvera has a surplus on invisible trade. **[2]**

**6.** Outline how locational advantage and changes in the global market can each affect a country's trade. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain why a high concentration of exports in one primary commodity is a risk for a country. **[5]**

**8.** Explain the role of the WTO in promoting free trade, and why some developing countries criticise it. **[6]**

**9.** Read the information about a fictional co-operative.

> The Kessa Cocoa Co-operative has 1 200 members. It sells 35% of its cocoa on Fairtrade terms and the rest at the market price. It has used the Fairtrade Premium to build a fermenting shed and a primary school. Members say the minimum price protected them when world prices fell. Some farmers nearby cannot afford to join. All the cocoa is exported as beans; chocolate is made and sold in Europe.

Evaluate the role of Fairtrade for the members of the Kessa Co-operative and their region. **[6]**

**10.** "Global trade increases inequality between countries more than it reduces it." How far do you agree? **[20]**

## Answers

**1.** The balance of trade is the value of exports minus the value of imports [1]; the terms of trade compare the price of a country's exports with the price of its imports, showing how many imports a unit of exports can buy [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Tariff escalation means importing countries set higher tariffs on processed goods than on raw materials [1]. Exporters can sell raw materials cheaply but face higher barriers if they process them [1], so they are discouraged from adding value and stay dependent on low-value commodities [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** 78 ÷ 110 × 100 [1] = 70.9 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The terms of trade fell from 100 in 2010 to 88.5 in 2015 and to a low of 70.9 in 2020 [1]. They recovered to 85.6 by 2025 [1], but remained below the 2010 level because import prices kept rising [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Each tonne of coffee buys fewer imports such as fuel, machinery or medicines [1]. Morundi must export more just to buy the same imports, or cut imports [1]. Export earnings and government revenue fall, making debt harder to repay [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Trade creation occurs when removing tariffs between members lets them buy from a lower-cost producer within the bloc instead of a higher-cost domestic producer [1], increasing trade and efficiency [1]. Trade diversion occurs when the common external tariff makes imports from efficient non-members more expensive [1], so members switch to higher-cost suppliers inside the bloc [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** Visible: 18 − 25 = −US$7 billion [1]; invisible: 9 − 3 = +US$6 billion [1]; overall: −7 + 6 = −US$1 billion [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Tourism earns US$7 billion, most of its service exports, because visitors spend money in Calvera [1], while as a small island it buys few services from abroad [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Locational advantage: a country on a major shipping route or close to a large market has lower transport costs [1], so it can become a trading hub or attract export industries [1]. Changes in the global market: new demand, for example from fast-growing economies, can raise exports of commodities [1], while new tariffs or falling prices can reduce a country's trade [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Earnings depend on one world price [1], so a fall in that price sharply cuts export revenue [1]. Government revenue, often based on that commodity, falls too, so spending must be cut [1]. The exchange rate can swing with the commodity price, making imports suddenly costly [1]. Investment is concentrated in one sector, so other industries develop slowly [1]. **[5]**

**8.** The WTO sets rules for trade between members, based on treating all members equally [1]. It hosts negotiations that have reduced tariffs [1] and settles disputes between members [1]. Developing countries criticise it because rich countries kept large farm subsidies while poorer countries were pressed to open markets [1]. Negotiations on agriculture in the Doha Round stalled [1], and smaller countries have limited resources to bring and win disputes [1]. **[6]**

**9.** The minimum price gave members income security when world prices fell [1]. The premium funded a fermenting shed, which may improve quality [1], and a school, a wider community benefit [1]. However, only 35% of the cocoa is sold on Fairtrade terms, so most income still follows the market price [1]. Farmers who cannot afford to join do not benefit [1]. Because beans are exported unprocessed, most value is still added in Europe, so Fairtrade helps members but does not change the region's place in the trade system [1]. **[6]**

**10.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. The points below are indicative creditworthy content.

*Introduction:* define inequality between countries [1] and outline how trade links them [1].

*Agree:* many LICs export primary commodities with low value added [1], while HICs capture value through processing, brands and services [1]. Commodity prices are volatile, so earnings collapse in slumps [1]. Tariff escalation discourages processing in exporting countries [1]. Rich-country farm subsidies undercut producers in poorer countries [1]. Trade is concentrated among a few regions, so marginalised countries fall further behind [1].

*Disagree:* export-led growth through trade helped East Asian economies close the gap with HICs [1]. Global value chains spread manufacturing jobs to MICs [1]. Commodity booms raised incomes in exporting countries [1]. Importing countries gain cheap goods and inputs [1]. Comparative advantage suggests all trading partners can gain [1].

*Analysis:* the effect depends on what a country exports: manufactured and service exporters have gained more than commodity exporters [1]. Inequality between some countries has fallen while gaps for the poorest have persisted [1]. Domestic policy and institutions decide whether trade income is invested [1].

*Use of examples:* a named country that gained through trade [1]; a named commodity-dependent country [1].

*Judgement:* a reasoned conclusion on "how far", for example that trade reduced inequality for countries able to upgrade exports but widened it for commodity-dependent LICs [1]. A clear final view [1]. **[20]**

*Examiner insight:* the strongest answers distinguish between types of exporting country rather than treating "trade" as one process with one effect.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the indices the wrong way round in terms of trade calculations.
- Leaving out the sign or units in trade balances.
- Defining trade diversion as any change in trade.
- Fairtrade answers that do not use the information given.
- Essays with no named countries.
