## Questions

**1.** This question is about electronic structure.

**(a)** Write the electronic configurations of an iron atom (Z = 26) and of the Fe³⁺ ion, using [Ar] shorthand. **[2]**
**(b)** Scandium is a d-block element. Its only stable ion is Sc³⁺. Explain why scandium is not classed as a transition metal. **[2]**

**2.** Give the electronic configuration, in [Ar] shorthand, of each species: Cr (Z = 24), Cr³⁺, Cu (Z = 29), Ni²⁺ (Z = 28) and V²⁺ (Z = 23). **[5]**

**3.** Chromium forms CrO₃ and CrCl₂.

**(a)** Deduce the oxidation number of chromium in each compound. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why transition metals can show several oxidation numbers. **[2]**

**4.** Nickel(II) ions form the complex ion [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺.

**(a)** State what is meant by the term ligand. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the bonding between each NH₃ and the nickel ion. **[2]**
**(c)** State the coordination number and the shape of this complex. **[2]**

**5.** Deduce the oxidation number of the metal and the coordination number in each complex.

**(a)** [Fe(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ **[2]**
**(b)** [NiCl₄]²⁻ **[2]**
**(c)** [Co(NH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]³⁺ **[2]**

**6.** Aqueous nickel(II) sulfate is green; aqueous zinc sulfate is colourless.

**(a)** Explain why [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is coloured. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why [Zn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is colourless. **[2]**

**7.** A student makes three observations. For each, identify the factor or factors from outcome 17.11 that cause the colour change.

**(a)** A pale green solution of iron(II) sulfate left open to the air slowly turns yellow-brown. **[1]**
**(b)** Excess aqueous ammonia turns pale blue aqueous copper(II) sulfate deep blue, forming [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺. **[1]**
**(c)** Concentrated hydrochloric acid turns a pink solution of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ blue, forming [CoCl₄]²⁻. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain, in terms of the d-orbitals, why changing a ligand changes the colour. **[1]**

**8.** This question is about the shapes of complexes.

**(a)** Explain why [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is octahedral, and give its bond angle. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why chloride ions form [CoCl₄]²⁻ rather than a six-coordinate complex with Co²⁺. **[1]**
**(c)** State the Cl–Co–Cl bond angle in [CoCl₄]²⁻. **[1]**

**9.** Cis-platin, [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], is a square planar complex used in cancer treatment.

**(a)** Deduce the oxidation number of platinum. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the cis and trans isomers differ, giving the Cl–Pt–Cl angle in each. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the drug is supplied as a single isomer. **[1]**
**(d)** A dose contains 75.0 mg of cis-platin. Calculate the amount of cis-platin in moles and the mass of platinum, in mg, in the dose. **[2]**

**10.** NH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ and EDTA⁴⁻ are both ligands.

**(a)** Explain why NH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ is described as bidentate. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number and type of donor atoms in EDTA⁴⁻. **[1]**
**(c)** Fe²⁺ bonds to one EDTA⁴⁻ ion. Give the formula, with charge, of the complex and its coordination number. **[2]**

**11.** Haemoglobin carries oxygen in the blood.

**(a)** State the oxidation number of iron in haemoglobin. **[1]**
**(b)** Haemoglobin contains a polydentate ligand. State what polydentate means. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how carbon monoxide reduces the oxygen carried by blood. **[3]**
**(d)** A patient's blood contains 9.0 g of haemoglobin per 100 cm³ and the total blood volume is 5.0 dm³. Each haemoglobin molecule (Mr = 64 500) has four iron(II) sites, each able to bind one O₂. 20% of the sites hold CO. Calculate the largest volume of O₂, in dm³, the blood can carry. **[4]**

**12.** Element X is in Period 4. Its ion X³⁺ has three 3d electrons.

**(a)** Identify X and give the electronic configuration of its atom. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the electronic configuration of X²⁺. **[1]**
**(c)** [X(NH₃)₆]³⁺ and [X(H₂O)₆]³⁺ have different colours. Explain why each complex is coloured, and why the colours differ. **[3]**
**(d)** State the shape of these two complexes. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Fe: **[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²** [1]. Fe³⁺: **[Ar] 3d⁵** [1].
**(b)** Sc³⁺ is [Ar], with no 3d electrons [1]. A transition metal must form a stable ion with an incompletely filled d sub-shell, and scandium's only ion has an empty one [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), name the ion and its configuration; a bare "it has no d electrons" is about the atom, which does have one, and is unlikely to earn both marks.

**2.** Cr **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** [1]; Cr³⁺ **[Ar] 3d³** [1]; Cu **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** [1]; Ni²⁺ **[Ar] 3d⁸** [1]; V²⁺ **[Ar] 3d³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Cr and Cu atoms are the exceptions; their ions follow the normal rule of losing 4s first, so do not carry the exception into Cr³⁺.

**3. (a)** CrO₃: x + 3(−2) = 0, so **+6** [1]. CrCl₂: x + 2(−1) = 0, so **+2** [1].
**(b)** The 4s and 3d sub-shells are close in energy [1], so different numbers of electrons can be lost or used in bonding without a very large jump in ionisation energy [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Similar energy" alone is only half the answer; link it to electrons being removed from both sub-shells.

**4. (a)** A species (molecule or anion) with a lone pair it can donate to the metal ion at the centre of a complex [1].
**(b)** Dative (coordinate) covalent bond [1]; the nitrogen of NH₃ donates its lone pair to the Ni²⁺ ion [1].
**(c)** **6** [1]; **octahedral** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the donor atom (N); "ammonia shares electrons" does not show which species supplies both electrons.

**5. (a)** x + 5(0) + (−1) = +2, so Fe is **+3** [1]; coordination number **6** [1].
**(b)** x + 4(−1) = −2, so Ni is **+2** [1]; coordination number **4** [1].
**(c)** The ligand is neutral, so Co is **+3** [1]; three bidentate ligands give 3 × 2 = **6** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "3" is the number of ligands, not the coordination number; count dative bonds.

**6. (a)** The water ligands split the 3d orbitals into two energy levels [1]. A d electron absorbs visible light of energy ΔE (ΔE = hf) and is promoted to the higher level [1]. The light not absorbed is transmitted, and this is the green colour seen [1].
**(b)** Zn²⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰, a full d sub-shell [1]. No electron can move to the upper d level, so no visible light is absorbed [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers that describe electrons "falling back and giving out light" describe emission, which is the wrong model for colour in complexes.

**7. (a)** Oxidation number of iron changes, from +2 to +3 [1].
**(b)** The ligand changes, from H₂O to NH₃ [1].
**(c)** The ligand changes, H₂O to Cl⁻ [1], and the coordination number changes, from 6 to 4 [1].
**(d)** A different ligand splits the d-orbitals by a different amount, so ΔE and the frequency of light absorbed change [1].
*Examiner insight:* Where two factors change, as in (c), naming only one leaves a mark behind.

**8. (a)** There are six bonding pairs around the chromium ion [1]. They repel each other equally and get as far apart as possible [1], giving an octahedral shape with bond angles of **90°** [1].
**(b)** A chloride ion is bigger than an H₂O molecule, so there is room for just four round Co²⁺ [1].
**(c)** **109.5°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Refer to bonding pairs repelling; "six ligands make it octahedral" restates the question without explaining it.

**9. (a)** x + 2(0) + 2(−1) = 0, so **+2** [1].
**(b)** Cis: the two Cl ligands are adjacent, at **90°**; trans: they are opposite, at **180°** [1]. Both are flat, with Pt and the four donor atoms in one plane [1].
**(c)** The two isomers are different compounds with different effects in the body, and only the cis form is the intended drug [1].
**(d)** Mr = 195.1 + 2(17.0) + 2(35.5) = 300.1; n = 0.0750 ÷ 300.1 = **2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol** [1]. Mass of Pt = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ × 195.1 = 0.0488 g = **48.8 mg** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert mg to g before dividing by Mr, and convert back at the end; unit slips are the usual source of a factor of 1000 error.

**10. (a)** Each molecule has two donor atoms, the nitrogen atoms [1], so it forms two dative bonds to the same metal ion [1].
**(b)** Six: **two nitrogen and four oxygen** atoms [1].
**(c)** **[Fe(EDTA)]²⁻** (+2 − 4 = −2) [1]; coordination number **6** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Bidentate means it has two lone pairs" is not enough; the two pairs must bond to the same metal ion.

**11. (a)** **+2** [1].
**(b)** A ligand that forms several dative bonds to the same metal ion [1].
**(c)** CO replaces O₂ on the iron(II) by ligand exchange [1]. CO bonds more strongly than O₂ [1], so the change is not easily reversed and those sites cannot carry oxygen to tissues [1].
**(d)** Mass of haemoglobin = 9.0 × (5000 ÷ 100) = 450 g [1]. Amount = 450 ÷ 64 500 = 6.98 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Sites free for O₂ = 4 × 0.80 × 6.98 × 10⁻³ = 2.23 × 10⁻² mol [1]. Volume = 2.23 × 10⁻² × 24.0 = **0.54 dm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage of a multi-step calculation; a wrong final value with clear working can still show the method, while a bare wrong number shows nothing.

**12. (a)** X is **chromium** [1]; atom **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** [1].
**(b)** **[Ar] 3d⁴** [1].
**(c)** In each, ligands split the 3d orbitals and d electrons absorb visible light to move to the higher level [1]. NH₃ and H₂O split the d-orbitals by different amounts, so ΔE differs [1]. Different frequencies are absorbed, so the light transmitted, and the colour seen, differs [1].
**(d)** Both **octahedral** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Working back from the d-electron count to the element needs the configuration of the ion, then the atom; check the Cr exception at the last step.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking electrons out of 3d ahead of 4s in an ion's configuration.
- Writing Cr as [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² or Cu as [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s².
- Leaving out "ion" or "incompletely filled" from the transition metal definition.
- Not stating that the ligand donates a **lone pair** in a **dative** bond.
- Counting ligands, not dative bonds, when a ligand is bidentate or hexadentate.
- Saying the colour seen is the colour absorbed.
- Missing one of two factors when the ligand and coordination number both change.
- Giving 90° for tetrahedral complexes, or confusing cis (90°) and trans (180°).
- Describing CO poisoning without ligand exchange or stronger bonding.
