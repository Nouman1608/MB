**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The call to Prophethood (Nabuat): "The details of the call to Prophethood in Cave of Hira, including his encounter with Jibril (AS)" and "Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) reaction and the role of Hazrat Khadija (RA) and Waraqa."** It tells the story in the order it happened, explains the meaning of each part and shows how to use the first revelation, Qur'an 96:1-5, in an answer.

## Before the call

By the age of forty, Muhammad (pbuh) was known in Makkah as al-Amin, the trustworthy. He was troubled by the idol worship, cheating and injustice around him. He began to spend time alone in thought, especially during the month of Ramadhan, in a cave called **Hira** on a hill north-east of Makkah, called Jabal al-Nur (the Mountain of Light). Khadija (RA) prepared food for him. He used the time to pray and reflect in the way of Ibrahim (AS). Before the revelation, he also began to have true dreams that came true "like the light of dawn", according to the account of Aisha (RA).

## The first revelation

The year was about 610 CE. According to the hadith narrated by Aisha (RA) in Sahih al-Bukhari, the angel **Jibril (AS)** appeared to him in the cave. The main events are:

1. Jibril (AS) said, "**Iqra**" ("Read" or "Recite").
2. The Prophet (pbuh) answered, "I am not one who can read" (*ma ana bi-qari*).
3. Jibril (AS) held him tightly until he was exhausted, then let him go, and again said "Iqra".
4. The Prophet (pbuh) gave the same answer, and the angel held him a second time and a third time.
5. After the third time, Jibril (AS) recited the first verses of Surah al-Alaq (96:1-5).

The Prophet (pbuh) was unable to read or write. The answer shows that the revelation did not come from his own mind. It came from Allah, and the Qur'an later calls him "the unlettered prophet" (7:157). Muslims also see in the embrace a sign of the seriousness of the message and of the strength Allah gave him to bear it.

## The first verses: Qur'an 96:1-5

The prescribed passage reads: "Recite in the name of your Lord who created... Created man from a clinging substance. Recite, and your Lord is the most Generous Who taught by the pen. Taught man that which he knew not."

| Phrase | Meaning and lesson |
|---|---|
| "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" | The first command is to read, and to do so in the name of Allah, the Creator; faith and knowledge go together |
| "Created man from a clinging substance" | Allah created human beings from the humblest origin; humility and the wonder of creation |
| "your Lord is the most Generous" | Allah gives freely, including knowledge and guidance |
| "Who taught by the pen" | Writing is a gift of Allah; learning is encouraged |
| "Taught man that which he knew not" | Revelation is knowledge given by Allah that humans could not reach by themselves |

Note that the verses do not begin with a law or a ritual. They begin with **reading, knowledge and the Creator**. Muslims draw from this the high value of education.

## The reaction of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh)

The Prophet (pbuh) was terrified. He came down the mountain and went home to Khadija (RA), shaking and said, "Cover me, cover me" (*zammiluni*). When the fear had left him, he told her what had happened and said, "I fear for myself." His reaction is important for three reasons:

- **It shows that he did not expect to be a prophet.** He had not sought the role.
- **It shows honesty.** He did not hide his fear and confusion.
- **It shows his humanity.** The Prophet was a man, who felt fear, and who needed comfort.

## The role of Hazrat Khadija (RA)

Khadija (RA) was the first person to support him. She did three things.

1. **She comforted him.** She said, "Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep good relations with your kin, you bear the burdens of the weak, you help the poor and the needy, you honour your guests and you help those in calamity." She judged his character, which she knew better than anyone, and concluded that Allah would not abandon such a person.
2. **She believed him.** She became the first person to accept Islam, and so the first Muslim.
3. **She sought advice.** She took him to her cousin **Waraqa ibn Nawfal**.

Her response shows the importance of a spouse's support and of knowing a person's character.

## The role of Waraqa ibn Nawfal

Waraqa was an old and blind man who had left idol worship, become a Christian and studied the scriptures. When the Prophet (pbuh) told him the story, Waraqa said, "This is the *Namus* (the angel of revelation) that Allah sent to Musa. I wish I were a young man and could be alive when your people drive you out." The Prophet (pbuh) asked, "Will they drive me out?" Waraqa replied, "Yes. No man has ever come with anything like what you have brought but was treated with hostility. If I live to see that day, I will support you strongly." Waraqa died soon after, and revelation paused for a time.

Waraqa's role was to:

- **Confirm the experience** as genuine revelation, connecting it with the earlier prophets.
- **Warn** that opposition would follow.
- **Promise support** if he lived.

## The pause and the continuing revelation

After the first revelation, there was a pause (the *fatrah*). The Prophet (pbuh) was anxious. Revelation then resumed with the command in Surah al-Muddaththir (74:1-2), "O you who covers himself [with a garment], arise and warn," which began his mission. The Qur'an was revealed in parts over about twenty-three years.

## The first believers

- **Hazrat Khadija (RA)**, the first adult believer.
- **Hazrat Ali (RA)**, a boy of about ten, living in the Prophet's household.
- **Hazrat Zayd ibn Harithah (RA)**, his freed servant and adopted son.
- **Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA)**, his closest friend, who accepted without hesitation.

## Why the event matters

- **It is the start of Islam** and of the final revelation, the Qur'an.
- **It confirms Muhammad (pbuh) as the Prophet and Messenger.**
- **It shows the value of knowledge** in the first word, "Recite".
- **It shows the link between prophets**, as Waraqa connected the event with Musa (AS).
- **It started the night known as Laylat ul-Qadr**, which Muslims honour in Ramadhan.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which Hazrat Khadija (RA) supported the Prophet (pbuh) after the first revelation. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

> First, she comforted him when he came home frightened [1]. She told him that Allah would never disgrace him because of his kindness to relatives and the poor, which calmed him and gave him confidence [1]. Second, she took him to her cousin Waraqa, who knew the earlier scriptures [1], and Waraqa confirmed that the angel was the same one who came to Musa, which reassured the Prophet that the revelation was real [1]. The first words of the Qur'an, "Recite in the name of your Lord who created", show that the message was a revelation from the Creator, and Khadija's belief in it made her the first Muslim [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Writing that the Prophet (pbuh) was afraid because he doubted Allah.** He was overwhelmed by the experience.
- **Saying "read" means he could read.** He said he could not.
- **Confusing the Cave of Hira with the Cave of Thawr.** Thawr was used in the Hijrah.
- **Leaving out Waraqa's warning.** He told the Prophet (pbuh) he would face opposition.
- **Forgetting that Khadija (RA) was the first Muslim.**

## Exam technique

**Give** questions need a name or a place (Hira, Jibril, Waraqa). **Describe** questions need the order of events. **Explain** questions need a person's role and its effect on the Prophet (pbuh). In the 12-mark question on 96:1-5, use "Recite" for the command, "created" for the Creator, "the pen" for knowledge, and "that which he knew not" for revelation, and explain why the verses open with learning rather than law.
