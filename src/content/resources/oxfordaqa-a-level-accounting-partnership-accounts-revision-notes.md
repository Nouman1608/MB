## 3.2.5 in one table (International A-level only)

| Content | Skills |
|---|---|
| Prepare and comment on the financial statements of partnerships | Three statements: income statement, appropriation account, statement of financial position. Calculate salaries, interest on capital, loan interest, interest on drawings and profit or loss shares; handle revaluation and goodwill |
| Prepare capital and current accounts of partners | Fixed or fluctuating capitals; the Partnership Act 1890 when partners have not agreed terms |
| Account for changes in partnership | A new partner joining; a partner retiring |

Dissolution of partnerships will not be examined.

## Vocabulary

- **Appropriation account**: the statement after the income statement that divides profit for the year among the partners.
- **Interest on capital**: a reward for the capital each partner has invested; an appropriation, never an expense.
- **Interest on drawings**: a charge to partners for taking resources out during the year; it adds to the profit to be shared.
- **Partnership salary**: an appropriation rewarding a partner's work; not part of wages.
- **Residual profit or loss**: what is left after salaries and interest; split in the profit-sharing ratio.
- **Current account**: under fixed capitals, the account that collects every appropriation and all drawings for a partner.
- **Revaluation account**: a temporary account recording value changes in assets and liabilities when partners change.
- **Goodwill**: the value of the business as a whole above the fair value of its identifiable net assets.

## Partnership Act 1890 defaults

Use these only where partners operate without agreement (section 24 of the Act):

| Matter | Default |
|---|---|
| Profits and losses | Shared equally |
| Interest on capital | Not allowed |
| Partnership salaries | Not allowed |
| Interest on drawings | Not charged (the Act has no such term) |
| Partner's advance beyond agreed capital | Interest at 5% a year from the date of the advance |

**Reminder.** No agreement; Ruskin advances 6,000 on 1 July and the year ends 31 December. Interest = 6,000 × 5% × 6/12 = **150**, charged as an expense before profit is shared equally.

## Which statement each item belongs in

| Item | Where it goes | Partner's current account |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on a partner's loan | Income statement, as an expense | Credited only if not paid in cash |
| The loan itself | Statement of financial position, liabilities | -- |
| Interest on drawings | Appropriation account, added | Debit |
| Interest on capital | Appropriation account, deducted | Credit |
| Partnership salary | Appropriation account, deducted | Credit |
| Share of residual profit / loss | Appropriation account | Credit / debit |
| Drawings | Not in any statement of profit | Debit |

## Method in steps

**Appropriation account**

```
  Profit for the year (loan interest already charged)
+ Interest on drawings
- Interest on capital
- Partnership salaries
= Residual profit or loss -> split in the profit-sharing ratio
```

Check: salaries + interest on capital + residual = profit for the year + interest on drawings.

**Interest on drawings**: amount × rate × (months from the drawing to the year end ÷ 12), for each drawing.

**Interest on capital**: rate × capital for the months it was held; split the year if capital changes.

**Current account (fixed capitals)**

```
Credit: opening credit balance, interest on capital,
        salary, profit share, loan interest not paid out
Debit:  opening debit balance, interest on drawings,
        drawings, loss share
Closing debit balance = partner has taken out more than credited
```

**Equity section**: show capital and current accounts for each partner, with debit current balances in brackets and deducted.

## Changes in partnership: ratio rules

| Step | Ratio | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Revaluation profit | Old | Dr Revaluation, Cr old partners' capitals |
| Revaluation loss | Old | Dr old partners' capitals, Cr Revaluation |
| Goodwill created | Old | Dr Goodwill, Cr old partners' capitals |
| Goodwill written off (not kept) | New | Dr remaining partners' capitals, Cr Goodwill |
| New partner's cash | -- | Dr Bank, Cr new partner's capital |
| Retiring partner's current account (credit) | -- | Dr Current account, Cr Capital |
| Retiring partner settled | -- | Dr Capital, Cr Bank / Loan / asset taken |

Revaluation entries: an asset that rises is debited to the asset and credited to revaluation; an asset that falls, or a liability or provision that rises, is debited to revaluation.

**Goodwill reminder.** Kilbride and Mottram share 2 : 1. Aspinall joins and all three then share equally. Goodwill of 27,000 is not kept.

```
                 Kilbride   Mottram   Aspinall
Created (2:1)     +18,000    +9,000         --
Written off       -9,000    -9,000     -9,000
Net effect        +9,000         0     -9,000
```

Kilbride gives up a third of the profits and is compensated; Mottram's share stays at one third, so nothing changes for him.

**Retiring partner's amount due** = capital ± current account balance + revaluation share + goodwill share. Pay by cash, an asset taken over, or a loan; a loan is a liability of the firm afterwards.

## Commenting: points that earn credit

- Compare each partner's total reward with the capital and time they put in.
- Spot falling or debit current accounts: drawings above what was credited reduce the firm's cash.
- A partner's loan adds an interest expense and must be repaid; without an agreement it earns 5% a year.
- Without an agreement, equal sharing may be unfair where capital or work differs.
- On a change, ask whether the goodwill value is fair and whether the firm can fund payments to a retiring partner.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Loan interest vs interest on capital | Loan interest is an expense in the income statement; interest on capital is an appropriation |
| Salary to a partner vs wages to staff | Appropriation vs expense |
| Fixed vs fluctuating capitals | Two accounts per partner vs one account that changes every year |
| Old vs new ratio | Revaluation and goodwill created use the old ratio; goodwill removed uses the new one |
| Goodwill left in the books vs removed | Left in: a non-current asset. Removed: created, then written off to nil |
| Admission vs retirement | New partner brings capital; retiring partner is paid out |

## Quick self-test

1. Profit before loan interest is 42,700. A partner's loan of 9,000 carries interest at 7% a year for the whole year. Find the profit for the year.
2. A partner draws 7,200 on 30 September. The year ends 31 December; interest on drawings is 5% a year. Calculate the interest.
3. Calculate a year's interest on capital of 80,000 at 4.5%.
4. Profit for the year 55,000; interest on drawings 900; interest on capital 7,500; salaries 11,000. Find the residual profit and split it 3 : 2.
5. Two partners have no agreement and a profit for the year of 33,000. How much does each receive?
6. A current account opens with a debit balance of 1,200. Interest on capital 2,000, profit share 18,500, interest on drawings 300, drawings 17,600. Find the closing balance.
7. On admission, premises rise by 16,000, vehicles fall by 2,600 and the provision for doubtful debts rises by 400. Old partners share 3 : 2. Find each share.
8. A retiring partner has capital 28,000, a current account debit balance of 1,100, a revaluation share of 2,400 and a goodwill share of 6,000. How much is due?
9. Where is a partnership salary recorded?
10. Goodwill of 40,000 is not kept. Old partners shared 1 : 1; the new ratio is 2 : 2 : 1. How much is debited to the new partner's capital?

### Answers

1. Interest 630; profit for the year **42,070**.
2. 7,200 × 5% × 3/12 = **90**.
3. 80,000 × 4.5% = **3,600**.
4. 55,000 + 900 − 7,500 − 11,000 = **37,400**; **22,440** and **14,960**.
5. **16,500** each.
6. −1,200 + 2,000 + 18,500 − 300 − 17,600 = **1,400 credit**.
7. Profit 13,000: **7,800** and **5,200**.
8. 28,000 − 1,100 + 2,400 + 6,000 = **35,300**.
9. Appropriation account (deducted), credited to the partner's current account.
10. 40,000 × 1/5 = **8,000**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting loan interest in the appropriation account, so profit for the year is overstated.
- Deducting interest on drawings instead of adding it.
- Counting months from the start of the year, not from the drawing date to the year end.
- Using the Act's 5% for a loan where the agreement sets a different rate, or ignoring the Act where there is no agreement.
- Scaling salaries down when profit is low instead of creating a residual loss.
- Sharing revaluation or goodwill created in the new ratio, or giving the incoming partner a share.
- Treating a retiring partner's debit current account balance as an addition.
- Showing the retiring partner's loan as capital rather than a liability.
