## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following checks is carried out by the bank's computer during an ATM withdrawal? **[1]**

A: Whether the customer is the right age

B: Whether there are enough funds in the account

C: Whether the notes are genuine

D: Whether the receipt printer has paper

**2.** State what is meant by *Electronic Funds Transfer*. **[1]**

**3.** Give **one** difference between a debit card and a credit card. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by *phishing*. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** reasons why fewer people now use cheques. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Describe the steps involved when a customer uses an ATM to check her balance and then withdraw cash. **[5]**

**7.** A company pays its 200 employees by Electronic Funds Transfer.

**(a)** Describe how the money reaches the employees' accounts. **[3]**

**(b)** Give **two** advantages to the company of paying wages this way rather than in cash. **[2]**

**8.** A student uses a banking app on his phone.

**(a)** Give **three** tasks he can carry out with the app. **[3]**

**(b)** The app asks for his fingerprint as well as a PIN. Explain why. **[2]**

**9.** A shopkeeper notices that a customer's card has been copied after using a cash machine outside her shop.

**(a)** Name and describe the method the criminal is likely to have used. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** way customers can protect their PIN at an ATM. **[1]**

**10.** An elderly woman still pays her gardener by cheque.

**(a)** Give **one** advantage of the cheque for her. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage of the cheque for the gardener. **[2]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** A bank is closing several branches and encouraging customers to use internet banking instead. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this for the bank's customers. **[6]**

**12.** Compare paying for a new laptop online with a credit card and with a debit card. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Moving money from one bank account to another electronically, without cash or paper [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A debit card takes money from the customer's own account straight away, whereas a credit card borrows money that is paid back later [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Sending fake emails or using fake websites that trick people into giving their login or bank details [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Cheques take several days to clear [1]; electronic methods such as internet banking and cards are faster and more convenient [1] (also credit: many shops no longer accept them; they can be lost or bounce). **[2]**

**6.** She inserts her card and the ATM reads the chip [1]; she enters her PIN, which is checked [1]; she selects "balance" and the bank's computer sends the balance to the screen [1]; she selects "withdraw cash" and enters an amount, and the bank's computer checks funds and the daily limit, then debits her account [1]; the card is returned and then the cash is dispensed [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** The company's computer sends the payment details, with each employee's account details and pay, to its bank [1]; the bank's computer debits the company's account with the total [1]; each employee's account is credited, even if it is at a different bank [1].

**(b)** No large amounts of cash need to be collected, counted or kept on the premises, reducing the risk of theft [1]; it is faster and cheaper than preparing 200 pay packets or cheques [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Check his balance [1]; transfer money to a friend [1]; view recent transactions or statements [1] (also credit: pay bills, set up regular payments, freeze a lost card).

**(b)** A fingerprint is a second check that only he can provide [1], so even if someone discovers his PIN they cannot access his account [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Card skimming [1]: a device fitted over the card slot copies the details from the card when it is inserted [1].

**(b)** Cover the keypad with the other hand while typing the PIN [1] (also credit: check the machine for anything loose or unusual fitted to it). **[3]**

**10. (a)** She does not need internet banking or a card to pay him [1] (also credit: she has a written record of the payment).

**(b)** He must take it to a bank or photograph it, and wait for it to clear [1], and if there are not enough funds it may bounce, so he is not paid [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Advantages: customers can bank at any time from home [1]; they save the time and cost of travelling to a branch, which helps people with mobility problems [1]; they can check balances and spending instantly [1]. Disadvantages: customers without internet access or confidence with technology, often older people, may find it hard to manage their money [1]; there is a risk of phishing and hacking [1]; there is no face-to-face advice for complicated problems [1]. **[6]**

**12.** With a credit card, the cost can be paid later or spread over several months [1], whereas with a debit card the money leaves the customer's account immediately [1]. Credit card interest is charged if the balance is not paid in full, so the laptop could cost more [1], whereas a debit card only allows spending up to the money available, which helps avoid debt [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the ATM checks the balance rather than the bank's computer.
- Describing EFT only as a card payment in a shop.
- Writing "it is safer" without saying why, such as no cash kept on the premises.
- Making a point about only one card in a comparison question.
