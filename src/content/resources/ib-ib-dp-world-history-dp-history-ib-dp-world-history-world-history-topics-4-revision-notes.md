Societies in transition (1400-1700) is world history topic 4 for Paper 2. Revise four themes (religious change, intellectual and cultural change, exploration and contact, social and economic change) in at least two societies from different regions, always weighing change against continuity.

Related: [Societies in transition study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-4/) and [Societies in transition practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-4-practice/).

## Change and continuity: German lands, c.1500-1600

| Area | Change | Continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Religion | Lutheran churches; end of papal authority in Protestant states | Catholicism survived in the south and in Habsburg lands |
| Church property | Monasteries dissolved in Protestant territories | Bishoprics and abbeys survived in Catholic areas |
| Politics | Princes controlled their territory's church (*cuius regio, eius religio*) | The Holy Roman Empire and its institutions remained |
| Society | Clergy could marry; new schools and catechisms | Social hierarchy intact; the peasants' demands defeated in 1525 |
| Culture | Vernacular Bible; pamphlets and print | Latin remained the language of learning |

## Change and continuity: Ming China, c.1400-1644

| Area | Change | Continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Silver economy; commercial crops; growth of market towns | Agriculture the base; the state still taxed land |
| Foreign contact | Zheng He's voyages; lifting of the maritime ban (1567); Jesuits at court (Ricci in Beijing from 1601) | Tribute system and suspicion of foreign influence |
| Society | Merchant wealth bought land and education | Scholar-officials at the top; examination system unchanged in principle |
| Ideas | Wang Yangming's challenge to orthodox Neo-Confucianism | Confucian classics remained the basis of education |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Humanism | Study of classical texts to promote eloquence and civic virtue |
| Indulgence | Remission of punishment for sin granted by the Church, sometimes in return for payment |
| *Cuius regio, eius religio* | "Whose realm, his religion": the principle of the Peace of Augsburg |
| Counter-Reformation | The Catholic Church's response to Protestantism, including Trent and the Jesuits |
| Heliocentrism | Theory that the earth orbits the sun |
| Columbian exchange | The transfer of plants, animals, people and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia |
| Price revolution | Sustained rise in European prices in the sixteenth century |
| Single Whip reform | Ming consolidation of taxes and labour services into silver payments |
| *Sulh-i kul* | "Peace with all": Akbar's policy of religious toleration |
| Enclosure | Turning open fields or common land into private holdings |

## Causes of change: linking factors

- **Print and Reformation**: print made Luther's ideas available far faster than manuscript copying could; reform movements created demand for print.
- **Silver and state power**: American silver funded Spanish wars and fed China's monetised economy.
- **Contact and disease**: epidemics made conquest easier and transformed American societies; new crops later supported population growth in Asia and Europe.
- **Religion and politics**: princes adopted reform partly for control over church lands and appointments; rulers such as Akbar used toleration to bind diverse elites.

## Exam traps

- **Everything changed.** Always give continuity, especially in social hierarchy and rural life.
- **Europe-only answers.** Ready a second region: Ming China, Mughal India, Tokugawa Japan or the Ottoman empire.
- **"The Renaissance caused the Reformation."** Show the link (humanist study of scripture, print) without making it automatic.
- **Inventions without effects.** Explain who used a development and what it changed.
- **Wrong end date.** The topic ends in 1700, before the Enlightenment's peak.

## Self-test

1. What did Luther attack in the Ninety-five Theses?
   *The sale of indulgences and the abuses associated with them.*
2. What principle did the Peace of Augsburg establish?
   *Each prince could choose Lutheranism or Catholicism for his territory.*
3. Name two features of the Catholic response to the Reformation.
   *The Council of Trent and the Jesuit order.*
4. What was Akbar's policy of *sulh-i kul*?
   *A policy of "peace with all", tolerating different religions.*
5. Between which years did Zheng He's voyages take place?
   *1405 and 1433.*
6. Which historian coined the term "Columbian exchange"?
   *Alfred Crosby.*
7. Give one crop that moved from the Americas to Afro-Eurasia.
   *Maize, potatoes, tomatoes or chillies.*
8. Where was the great silver mine opened in 1545?
   *Potosí.*
9. What did the Single Whip reform do?
   *It consolidated taxes and labour services into payments in silver.*
10. Why was Galileo tried in 1633?
    *For supporting the heliocentric theory against Church teaching.*
11. Give one way printing changed European society.
    *It reduced the cost of books, standardised texts and spread religious and political ideas quickly.*
12. Give one continuity in German society after the Reformation.
    *Social hierarchy and princely power remained, or Catholicism survived in many areas.*
