## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by the terms totipotent and pluripotent. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the structure of a blastocyst and identify the part that is the source of embryonic stem cells. **[3]**

**3.** A culture starts with 2000 pluripotent cells. They divide every 12 hours and none die. Calculate the number of cells after 60 hours. **[2]**

**4.** Discuss the use of embryonic stem cells compared with adult stem cells in medical therapies. **[6]**

**5.** A beta cell in the pancreas makes insulin. A muscle cell from the same person does not. Explain why. **[4]**

**6.** The table shows the CpG sites methylated at the promoter of one gene (out of 40 sites) and the relative mRNA from the gene.

| Tissue | Sites methylated | Relative mRNA |
|---|---|---|
| Liver | 6 | 100 |
| Brain | 20 | 41 |
| Skin | 34 | 9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of sites methylated in skin. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship shown. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how methylation could produce this relationship. **[2]**

**7.** A gene has four exons of 63, 48, 75 and 54 nucleotides, with the stop codon in exon 4. Exons 1 and 4 are always kept. Exons 2 and 3 are each kept or removed independently.
**(a)** State the number of different mRNAs possible. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of amino acids in the shortest and the longest polypeptide. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how one gene can give different polypeptides. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how histone acetylation increases the expression of a gene, and how a DNA methylation pattern can be kept by both daughter cells after mitosis. **[5]**

**9.** Genetically identical seedlings were grown with low or high nitrogen supply. Dry masses (g) after six weeks: low nitrogen 3.2, 3.6, 3.1, 3.5; high nitrogen 5.4, 5.9, 5.6, 5.5.
**(a)** Calculate the mean mass at low nitrogen. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean mass at high nitrogen, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between the groups and why the masses within one group still vary. **[2]**

**10.** A woman with blood group A and a man with blood group B have a child with group O.
**(a)** State the genotypes of both parents. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the probability that their next child has group AB. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why ABO inheritance is an example of multiple alleles. **[1]**

**11.** A plant trait is controlled by three unlinked genes, each with an additive allele (capital) and a non-additive allele (small). Two plants of genotype AaBbCc are crossed.
**(a)** State the number of genetically different phenotype classes in the offspring. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the probability of an offspring with all six capital alleles. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the probability of an offspring with five or six capital alleles. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the trait in a population shows continuous variation. **[4]**

**12.** Coat colour in rabbits is controlled by one gene with the dominance series C (full colour) > cch (chinchilla) > ch (Himalayan) > c (albino). A Himalayan rabbit has dark fur only on its ears, nose, feet and tail.
**(a)** Two chinchilla rabbits of genotype cch ch are crossed. Give the probability that an offspring is Himalayan. **[2]**
**(b)** The enzyme that makes dark pigment is active only at lower temperatures. Explain the pattern of dark fur. **[2]**
**(c)** A Himalayan rabbit is kept in a warm room and its fur regrows. Predict and explain the change. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Totipotent: can differentiate into any cell type, including extra-embryonic tissue such as the placenta, so can form a whole organism [1]. Pluripotent: can differentiate into any body cell type but not the extra-embryonic tissue [1].
*Examiner insight:* the placenta or extra-embryonic point is what separates the two terms, so a definition that only says "can become many cells" tends to gain little.

**2.** A hollow ball of cells with a fluid-filled cavity [1]. An outer layer, the trophoblast, which goes on to form part of the placenta [1]. An inner cell mass at one side of the cavity, which is the source of embryonic stem cells [1].
*Examiner insight:* name the layer and say what it becomes. Naming the inner cell mass but not stating it is the stem cell source leaves the question unanswered.

**3.** 60 ÷ 12 = 5 doublings [1]. 2000 × 2⁵ = **64 000 cells** [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the number of doublings. Multiplying by 5 instead of 2⁵ is the common slip, and working lets the marker see where it went wrong.

**4.** Any six from: embryonic cells are pluripotent so can form a wide range of tissues [1]; they can be grown in large numbers in culture [1]; obtaining them destroys an embryo, which some people consider unacceptable [1]; adult cells do not need an embryo, so there are fewer ethical objections [1]; adult cells give a narrower range of cell types and are harder to culture [1]; a patient's own adult cells reduce the risk of rejection, whereas embryonic cells may be rejected or form tumours [1].
*Examiner insight:* "discuss" needs both sources compared with reasons. A list of ethical views without a benefit or risk from the biology limits the credit available.

**5.** Both cells contain the same genes (all arise from one zygote by mitosis) [1]. In the beta cell the insulin gene is expressed, being transcribed [1]. The mRNA is translated to give insulin [1]. In the muscle cell the insulin gene is not transcribed, so no insulin mRNA or protein is made [1].
*Examiner insight:* write "expressed" or "transcribed". Saying that muscle cells "do not have" the insulin gene is a factual error that costs the whole explanation.

**6. (a)** 34 ÷ 40 × 100 = **85%** [1]
**(b)** As the number of sites methylated increases, the relative mRNA decreases [1], from 100 at 6 sites to 9 at 34 sites [1].
**(c)** Methylated cytosine bases at the promoter reduce binding of transcription factors [1], so less transcription and less mRNA [1].
*Examiner insight:* quote paired values from the table in (b). Describing a trend with no figures is usually treated as incomplete.

**7. (a)** 2 × 2 = **4 mRNAs** [1]
**(b)** Shortest has exons 1 and 4: 63 + 54 = 117 nucleotides = 39 codons, minus the stop codon = **38 amino acids** [1]. Longest has all exons: 63 + 48 + 75 + 54 = 240 nucleotides = 80 codons [1], giving **79 amino acids** [1].
**(c)** Different combinations of exons are kept in the mature mRNA [1], so the amino acid sequence differs and a different polypeptide is made [1].
*Examiner insight:* remember the stop codon. Dividing by 3 alone gives 39 and 80, which are one too many in each case.

**8.** Acetyl groups are added to histone proteins [1]. This reduces the attraction between histones and DNA, so the chromatin loosens [1]. Transcription factors and RNA polymerase reach the promoter, so transcription rises [1]. After DNA replication the new strand is unmethylated while the parent strand is methylated [1]. Enzymes methylate the new strand to match the parent, so both daughter cells have the same pattern [1].
*Examiner insight:* link the structural change (loosened chromatin) to access for transcription. Stating only "the gene is switched on" is a result, not an explanation.

**9. (a)** (3.2 + 3.6 + 3.1 + 3.5) ÷ 4 = **3.35 g** [1]
**(b)** High mean = 22.4 ÷ 4 = 5.6 g [1]. (5.6 - 3.35) ÷ 3.35 × 100 = **67.2%** [1]
**(c)** The genotype is the same, so the difference is due to the environment (nitrogen supply, which gives more materials for growth) [1]. Variation within a group also arises from environment, such as small differences in light or water, because there is no genetic difference [1].
*Examiner insight:* give the final answer to the requested significant figures. Percentage change is a divide by the original (low-nitrogen) value, and dividing by the new value gives a different answer.

**10. (a)** Mother IA IO [1] and father IB IO [1]
**(b)** Both parents are heterozygous. Gametes IA, IO and IB, IO give IA IB, IA IO, IB IO, IO IO [1]. Probability of AB = **1/4** [1].
**(c)** There are three alleles (IA, IB, IO) of one gene in the population, though each person has two [1].
*Examiner insight:* the group O child proves that each parent carries IO, so justify the genotypes from that fact. Writing IA IA for the mother ignores the evidence.

**11. (a)** 0 to 6 capital alleles gives **7 classes** [1]
**(b)** (1/2)⁶ = **1/64** [1]
**(c)** Six capital alleles = 1/64 and five = 6/64 [1], so the total is **7/64** [1]
**(d)** Many genes each have a small additive effect [1], so there are many genotypes and many phenotype classes [1]. The environment alters the phenotype within each class, so values overlap [1]. This gives a smooth, bell-shaped distribution rather than distinct categories [1].
*Examiner insight:* answers that mention only the number of genes, without the additive effect and the environment, usually miss part of the explanation of continuous variation.

**12. (a)** Gametes cch and ch from each parent [1]. Offspring: cch cch, cch ch, cch ch, ch ch, so Himalayan = **1/4** [1]
**(b)** The extremities are cooler than the body core, so the enzyme is active there and makes dark pigment [1]. In the warmer body the enzyme is inactive, so the fur is pale [1].
**(c)** The new fur grows paler or lacks dark patches [1], because the enzyme is less active at the higher temperature [1].
*Examiner insight:* name the variable (temperature) and the enzyme activity in (b) and (c). "Because of the environment" alone does not explain the mechanism.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Saying specialised cells contain different genes instead of expressing different genes.
* Describing a blastocyst as solid, which is the morula, or calling inner cell mass cells totipotent.
* Writing a one-sided answer to a "discuss" question about stem cells.
* Dividing exon length by 3 and forgetting the stop codon.
* Describing alternative splicing as removal of exons from the DNA.
* Claiming that methylation or histone modification changes the base sequence.
* Giving a trend from a table without quoting values.
* Calculating percentage change against the wrong value.
* Not stating both parental genotypes in a multiple alleles cross.
