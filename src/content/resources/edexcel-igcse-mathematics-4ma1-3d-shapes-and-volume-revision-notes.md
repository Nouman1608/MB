Condensed recall for section 4.10 (3D shapes and volume) of the 4MA1 specification. Naming solids, faces, edges and vertices, surface area, the volume of prisms and cylinders and the conversion of volume units are Foundation Tier content for both tiers. The surface area and volume of a sphere and a right circular cone are **Higher tier only**. Worked examples are in the [3D shapes and volume study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-3d-shapes-and-volume/), and exam-style questions are in the [3D shapes and volume practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-3d-shapes-and-volume-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Face | A flat or curved surface of a solid |
| Edge | A line where two faces meet |
| Vertex | A corner where edges meet |
| Prism | Solid with the same cross-section along its whole length |
| Pyramid | Polygon base and triangular faces meeting at an apex |
| Net | A flat pattern that folds to make a solid |
| Surface area | Total area of all the faces |
| Volume | Space inside a solid, in cubic units |
| Slant height | Distance from the apex of a cone to the edge of its base |

## Properties of common solids

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube, cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Cylinder | 3 | 2 | 0 |
| Cone | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Sphere | 1 | 0 | 0 |

## Formulae on the sheet and off it

| Formula | Foundation sheet | Higher sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Volume of prism = cross-section area × length | Yes | Yes |
| Volume of cylinder = πr²h | Yes | Yes |
| Curved surface area of cylinder = 2πrh | Yes | Yes |
| Volume of cone = ⅓πr²h | Not needed | Yes |
| Curved surface area of cone = πrl | Not needed | Yes |
| Volume of sphere = 4/3 πr³ | Not needed | Yes |
| Surface area of sphere = 4πr² | Not needed | Yes |
| Area of circle = πr², cuboid volume, triangle area | No | No |

Learn the ones marked No. The area of a circle is needed for the ends of a cylinder and the base of a cone.

## Surface area (C and D)

- **Cuboid:** 2(lw + lh + wh). A cuboid 8 by 5 by 3 has surface area 2(40 + 24 + 15) = 158.
- **Any prism:** 2 × cross-section area + cross-section perimeter × length.
- **Cylinder:** curved 2πrh plus two ends 2πr², total 2πrh + 2πr².
- **Open cylinder:** 2πrh + πr².
- Draw the net or list the faces, then tick each one off.

## Volume of prisms (E)

- Volume = area of cross-section × length.
- Cuboid: length × width × height. Cylinder: πr²h.
- Triangular prism with ½ × 3 × 4 cross-section, length 10: 60.
- Trapezium cross-section ½(6 + 10) × 4 = 32, length 15: 480.
- Work backwards: h = V ÷ (πr²). V = 500, r = 5 gives h = 6.37.

## Units of volume (F)

| Length | Volume |
|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |

- 1 litre = 1000 cm³, so 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
- 4500 cm³ = 4.5 litres.
- 2.5 m³ = 2 500 000 cm³.
- Tank 50 cm by 40 cm by 30 cm = 60 000 cm³ = 60 litres.
- Melted or recast solids keep the same volume.

## Cones (Higher tier only)

- Slant height: l² = r² + h².
- Volume = ⅓πr²h.
- Curved surface area = πrl. Total surface area = πrl + πr².
- Worked values for r = 5, h = 12: l = 13, V = 100π = 314 cm³, curved area = 65π = 204 cm², total area = 90π = 283 cm².

## Spheres and hemispheres (Higher tier only)

| Quantity | Sphere | Hemisphere |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | 4/3 πr³ | 2/3 πr³ |
| Curved surface area | 4πr² | 2πr² |
| Total surface area | 4πr² | 3πr² (including the flat face) |

For r = 6: sphere volume 288π = 905 cm³, sphere surface area 144π = 452 cm². The hemisphere formulae are not on the sheet; halve the sphere and add the flat circle when it is present.

## Compound solids

1. Identify the simple solids: cylinder, hemisphere, cone, cuboid.
2. Volume: add the volumes.
3. Surface area: add the exposed surfaces only. Joined faces are inside the solid and are left out.

Cylinder r = 3, h = 10 with a hemisphere r = 3 on top: volume = 90π + 18π = 108π = 339 cm³.

## Exam traps

- Cubing the length factor: cm³ to m³ divides by 1 000 000, not 100.
- Using the diameter in a volume formula.
- Using vertical height h where the question needs slant height l.
- Leaving out the circular ends of a closed cylinder.
- Counting a joined face in the surface area of a compound solid.
- Writing cm² for a volume or cm³ for an area.
- Forgetting that litres are 1000 cm³, not 100 cm³.

## Self-test

1. How many edges does a triangular prism have?
2. How many vertices does a square-based pyramid have?
3. Find the surface area of a cube of side 5 cm.
4. A prism has a cross-section area of 18 cm² and a length of 7 cm. Find its volume.
5. Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 8 cm, in terms of π.
6. Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, in terms of π.
7. Convert 7.5 litres to cm³.
8. Convert 0.4 m³ to cm³.
9. A cuboid has a volume of 360 cm³ and a base of 12 cm by 6 cm. Find its height.
10. (Higher) Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and vertical height 7 cm, in terms of π.
11. (Higher) Find the surface area of a sphere with radius 5 cm, in terms of π.
12. (Higher) A cone has radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Find its curved surface area, in terms of π.

### Answers

1. 9 edges.
2. 5 vertices.
3. 6 × 5² = 150 cm².
4. 18 × 7 = 126 cm³.
5. π × 9 × 8 = 72π cm³.
6. 2π × 2 × 5 + 2π × 4 = 20π + 8π = 28π cm².
7. 7.5 × 1000 = 7500 cm³.
8. 0.4 × 1 000 000 = 400 000 cm³.
9. 360 ÷ (12 × 6) = 5 cm.
10. ⅓ × π × 9 × 7 = 21π cm³.
11. 4π × 25 = 100π cm².
12. Slant height = √(36 + 64) = 10 cm, so curved surface area = π × 6 × 10 = 60π cm².

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need the volume of a pyramid for 4MA1?**
The specification asks you to recognise pyramids and name their parts, and to find surface area from the area formulae for triangles and rectangles. It does not list a pyramid volume formula, whereas the Higher Tier sheet does give the volume of a cone.

**Is the formulae sheet the same for both tiers?**
No. The Foundation sheet has the trapezium area and the prism and cylinder formulae. The Higher sheet adds cone and sphere formulae and others from elsewhere in the specification.

**What does "right circular cone" mean?**
It means the base is a circle and the apex is directly above the centre of the base, so the vertical height is perpendicular to the base.
