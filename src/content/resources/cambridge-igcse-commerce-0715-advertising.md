**Advertising** is a paid form of communication that tells people about a product, service or business and encourages them to buy. It is one of the **aids to trade**: services that help goods and services move from producer to consumer. Section 5.1 of the Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715) syllabus has four parts: **types of advertising** (5.1.1), **traditional and digital media** (5.1.2), **methods of appeal** (5.1.3) and **sales promotion** (5.1.4). This guide takes them in order, with two worked examples and an annotated answer.

## What are the purposes and types of advertising? (5.1.1)

### Purposes of advertising

Businesses advertise to:

- **inform** customers about a new product, a change in price or where to buy;
- **persuade** customers to choose this product instead of another;
- **remind** customers about a product they already know;
- **increase sales** and market share;
- **build or protect** the image of the brand.

### Four types of advertising

| Type | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Informative** | Gives facts such as features, price, opening times or where to buy | A notice of a new bus timetable |
| **Persuasive** | Uses attractive images and strong words to change opinions and encourage purchase | An advert showing a famous sports star wearing a brand of trainers |
| **Collective (generic)** | Paid for by a group of producers to promote a whole product type, not one brand | "Drink more milk" campaign by dairy farmers |
| **Competitive** | Compares or challenges rival products to win customers from them | A mobile network claiming faster speeds than its rivals |

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Informative | Helps customers make informed choices; builds awareness; useful for new products | Does not always persuade; competitors can copy the information |
| Persuasive | Can change attitudes; builds brand loyalty and image | Costly; may exaggerate; customers may feel pressured |
| Collective | Shared cost between many producers; expands the whole market | Benefits rivals as much as the payer; no single brand is promoted |
| Competitive | Can win customers from rivals; highlights strengths | May provoke a response from rivals; can break the law if claims are untrue; costly |

## Which traditional and digital media can a business use? (5.1.2)

### Traditional media named in the syllabus

Television, radio, cinema, newspapers (local and national), magazines and trade journals, leaflets and flyers, packaging, direct mail, sponsorship, catalogues and brochures, posters, transport vehicles, window displays, point of sale displays, signboards and word of mouth.

### Digital media named in the syllabus

Social media advertising, other internet advertising (including websites and search engines), display advertisements, video advertisements, email marketing, digital billboards and social media influencers.

### Advantages and disadvantages of selected media

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Television | Very large audience; sight, sound and movement | Very costly; short messages; viewers may skip adverts |
| Radio | Cheaper than television; local or national reach | Sound only; listeners may be doing something else |
| Local newspaper | Targets local readers; low cost; can be changed daily | Short life; poor picture quality; limited audience |
| Direct mail | Aimed at chosen people; personal | Often thrown away; cost of printing and postage |
| Posters and billboards | Seen again and again; low cost per viewer | Limited space for words; hard to target |
| Social media advertising | Targets by age, location and interests; low cost; quick results | Can be ignored; negative comments can spread |
| Search engine adverts | Reaches people already looking for the product | Cost per click can add up; competitors bid for the same words |
| Email marketing | Cheap; personal; trackable | Can be seen as spam; limited to people with an address on file |
| Social media influencers | Trusted by followers; reaches a specific audience | Followers may doubt paid opinions; reputation risk if the influencer misbehaves |

### Factors affecting choice of advertising

The syllabus lists **audience, cost, cultural and legal considerations, flexibility to change, local and international market coverage, multi-channel advertising, timing and type of product**.

| Factor | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who are the customers, and which media do they use? |
| Cost | Can the budget cover the medium, and is the cost per person reached reasonable? |
| Cultural and legal | Does the advert respect local beliefs and language? Are there rules (for example on advertising to children or on health claims)? |
| Flexibility to change | Can the advert be altered quickly if prices or stock change? |
| Local and international coverage | Is the market a town or the world? |
| Multi-channel | Would using several media together, such as television and social media, give a stronger effect? |
| Timing | Is there a sale, a festival or a launch date? |
| Type of product | Does it need to be demonstrated (television, video) or is it a simple local service (flyer, local radio)? |

## What methods of appeal are used in advertising? (5.1.3)

An **appeal** is the emotional or rational reason an advert gives for buying.

| Appeal | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion | Makes the viewer feel happy, sad or caring | A charity advert showing a child in need |
| Fear | Shows what could go wrong if the product is not used | An advert about the risk of a house fire without a smoke alarm |
| Humour | Makes people laugh so they remember the brand | A funny advert for a snack |
| Rational | Gives facts and logic such as price, quality or savings | A comparison of fuel use of two cars |
| Ambition | Suggests the product helps people succeed | A watch linked to business success |
| Social acceptance | Suggests the product helps people fit in | A drink shown being enjoyed by a popular group |
| Romantic | Links the product to love and relationships | A perfume advert showing a couple |
| Adventure | Links the product to excitement and risk | A jeep driving through a desert |
| Music | Uses a tune or jingle to make the advert memorable | A catchy tune for a mobile service |
| Endorsement | A celebrity or influencer says they use the product | A footballer promoting boots |

To choose the **appropriate** appeal, think about the product and the audience. A smoke alarm suits fear and rational appeals; a teenage snack suits humour and music; a medical product suits rational facts rather than humour.

## What is sales promotion? (5.1.4)

**Sales promotion** is a short-term activity that encourages customers to buy now. Unlike advertising, which builds awareness over time, sales promotion works at the point of purchase or for a limited period.

### Methods in the syllabus

- **Point of sale:** displays and materials near the till or on shelves.
- **Special offers:** buy one get one free, or a lower price for a limited time.
- **Sponsorship:** paying to have the name linked with an event or team.
- **Displays:** eye-catching arrangements in shops.
- **Competitions:** customers enter for a chance to win a prize.
- **Coupons:** vouchers that give money off.
- **Sales periods:** end-of-season or seasonal sales with reduced prices.
- **Loss leaders:** a product sold at or below cost to attract customers who then buy other goods.
- **Free gifts:** a small item given with a purchase.
- **Free samples:** a trial amount of a product given away.

### Factors affecting the choice of method

The syllabus lists **business type, competitors, cost, nature of product, new or old product and timing**. A new product is often launched with free samples; an old product with surplus stock may be sold in a sales period; a supermarket may use loss leaders because its customers buy many items in one visit.

## Worked example 1: comparing media on cost (invented figures)

*A new juice brand wants to reach as many people as possible. Television costs $12,000 and reaches 400,000 people. Radio costs $2,400 and reaches 120,000. Social media adverts cost $1,500 and reach 75,000.*

Cost per 1,000 people reached = cost divided by reach, multiplied by 1,000.

- Television: 12,000 / 400,000 x 1,000 = **$30**.
- Radio: 2,400 / 120,000 x 1,000 = **$20**.
- Social media: 1,500 / 75,000 x 1,000 = **$20**.

Radio and social media are equally cheap per person. Television reaches more people overall but costs more per person. If the juice is aimed at young adults, social media is the better choice because it can be targeted; if the budget is large and mass awareness is needed, television may be justified.

## Worked example 2: does a special offer pay? (invented figures)

*A shop sells a bag of rice for $8.00. It costs $4.50. Normally 400 bags a week are sold. A 25% discount is offered.*

1. Normal profit per bag: 8.00 - 4.50 = **$3.50**. Weekly profit: 400 x 3.50 = **$1,400**.
2. Discounted price: 8.00 x 0.75 = **$6.00**. Profit per bag: 6.00 - 4.50 = **$1.50**.
3. Bags needed to match the old profit: 1,400 / 1.50 = 933.3, so the shop must sell at least **934 bags**.

**Judgement:** sales must rise from 400 to at least 934 bags (more than double) for the offer to pay on its own. It may still be worthwhile if the offer brings new customers who buy other goods.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain two reasons why a toothpaste manufacturer might use television advertising rather than a local newspaper.* **[4]**

> Television reaches a **very large audience** across the whole country [1], which suits a toothpaste sold in every supermarket, whereas a local newspaper only reaches readers in one area [1]. Television also uses **sight, sound and movement** [1], so the manufacturer can show the product working and keep the brand in people's minds [1].

Why it scores: each reason is named, then linked to the product and compared with the newspaper.

## What are the common mistakes in 5.1?

- Mixing up **advertising** (paid message to a wide audience) with **sales promotion** (short-term incentive to buy).
- Describing **collective** advertising as a single firm's advert; it is paid for by a group.
- Listing advantages of a medium without naming it.
- Choosing a medium with no link to the audience, cost or product.
- Writing "emotion" for every appeal. Name the exact one, such as fear or humour.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** or **state**: give the name only.
- **Describe** an appeal: say what the advert does to persuade.
- **Explain** a choice: link the medium or method to the audience, cost or product.
- **Assess**: weigh pros and cons for this business, then decide.
