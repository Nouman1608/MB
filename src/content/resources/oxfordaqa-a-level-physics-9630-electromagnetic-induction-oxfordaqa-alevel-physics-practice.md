These questions cover section 3.10.4, Electromagnetic induction, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [electromagnetic induction study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electromagnetic-induction-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics/) or the [electromagnetic induction revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electromagnetic-induction-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-revision-notes/) first.

All fields are uniform unless stated.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** **(a)** State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction. **[1]**

**(b)** State Lenz's law. **[2]**

**2.** A bar magnet is held stationary inside a coil connected to a sensitive centre-zero meter. What does the meter show?

A: a steady deflection

B: zero

C: a deflection that slowly falls to zero

D: an alternating deflection

**[1]**

**3.** A coil of 200 turns and area 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² is perpendicular to a field that rises uniformly from zero to 0.30 T in 0.025 s. Calculate the emf induced. **[2]**

**4.** A metal rod 0.60 m long moves at 5.0 m s⁻¹ at right angles to a field of 0.080 T. Calculate the emf between its ends. **[1]**

**5.** A coil rotates uniformly in a field. Explain why the emf is greatest when the plane of the coil lies along the field. **[2]**

**6.** A copper plate swings on a pivot between the poles of a strong magnet. Explain why its swings die away quickly. **[3]**

### Section B

**7.** A bar magnet is dropped through a vertical coil connected to a data logger, which records the emf.

**(a)** Explain why the trace shows two pulses of opposite sign. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the second pulse is taller than the first. **[1]**

**(c)** State how the areas under the two pulses compare, and why. **[1]**

**8.** A metal rod 0.30 m long is pulled at a steady 4.0 m s⁻¹ along horizontal rails in a vertical field of 0.20 T. The circuit has a total resistance of 0.50 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the induced emf. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the current. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the force needed to keep the rod moving at a steady speed. **[1]**

**(d)** Show that the mechanical power supplied equals the electrical power dissipated. **[2]**

**9.** The flux linkage of a coil rises uniformly from zero to 0.060 Wb in 0.20 s, stays constant for 0.30 s, then falls uniformly to zero in 0.10 s.

**(a)** Calculate the emf during each of the three stages. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the shape of the emf–time graph. **[1]**

**10.** A generator coil of 250 turns and area 2.4 × 10⁻³ m² rotates at 20 revolutions per second in a field of 0.15 T. At t = 0 the normal to the coil lies along the field.

**(a)** Calculate the peak emf. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the emf at t = 5.0 ms. **[2]**

**(c)** The rotation rate is tripled. State the new peak emf. **[1]**

**11.** The north pole of a bar magnet is pulled away from one end of a coil connected in a closed circuit.

**(a)** State the polarity of that end of the coil while the magnet moves away, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy, why the polarity could not be the other way. **[2]**

**12.** A car has a vertical metal aerial 1.5 m long. The car travels east at 30 m s⁻¹ where the horizontal component of the Earth's field is taken as 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ T, pointing north.

**(a)** Calculate the emf between the ends of the aerial. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why no current flows round a loop of wire that is fixed to the car and moves with it, even though an emf is induced in each side. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The direction of the induced emf (or current) is such that [1] it opposes the change that produces it [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** ε = NΔΦ/Δt = 200 × 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.30 / 0.025 [1] = **1.2 V** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** ε = BLv = 0.080 × 0.60 × 5.0 = **0.24 V** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The emf equals the rate of change of flux linkage [1]; with the plane along the field the flux linkage is zero but changing fastest (the sides cut field lines at right angles) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The plate moves through the field, so emfs are induced and eddy currents circulate in it [1]. By Lenz's law, the currents produce forces that oppose the plate's motion [1]. The plate's kinetic energy is transferred to thermal energy by the currents [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** As the magnet enters, the flux linkage increases; as it leaves, the flux linkage decreases [1]. The rate of change has opposite signs, so the emf reverses [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The magnet is moving faster as it leaves, so the flux linkage changes in a shorter time and the emf is larger [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Equal in magnitude: each area equals the total change of flux linkage, which is the same entering and leaving [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** ε = 0.20 × 0.30 × 4.0 = **0.24 V** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** I = 0.24 / 0.50 = **0.48 A** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** F = BIL = 0.20 × 0.48 × 0.30 = **0.029 N** [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** Mechanical power = Fv = 0.0288 × 4.0 = 0.115 W [1]; electrical power = I²R = 0.48² × 0.50 = 0.115 W, the same [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Rising: 0.060 / 0.20 = **0.30 V** [1]. Constant: **zero** [1]. Falling: 0.060 / 0.10 = **0.60 V**, in the opposite direction [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** A constant 0.30 V for 0.20 s, zero for 0.30 s, then a constant 0.60 V of opposite sign for 0.10 s (rectangular blocks) [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** ω = 2π × 20 = 126 rad s⁻¹ [1]; ε₀ = BANω = 0.15 × 2.4 × 10⁻³ × 250 × 126 = **11 V** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** ωt = 126 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.63 rad [1]; ε = 11.3 × sin(0.63 rad) = **6.6 V** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** ω triples, so ε₀ = **34 V** [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** A **south** pole [1]: the induced current opposes the decrease in flux by attracting the retreating north pole [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** If it were a north pole it would repel the magnet and push it away faster [1]; kinetic energy and electrical energy would both increase with no work done, which breaks conservation of energy [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** ε = BLv = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 1.5 × 30 [1] = **9.0 × 10⁻⁴ V** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Both sides of the loop have equal emfs that act in opposite senses round the loop, so they cancel; equivalently, the flux linkage of the loop does not change [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "opposes the field" for Lenz's law.
- Forgetting N in Faraday's law.
- Putting the peak emf where the coil faces the field.
- Explaining eddy-current braking without naming Lenz's law or the energy transfer.
- Using ωt in degrees in a calculator set to radians, or the reverse.
