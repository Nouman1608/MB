## Core recall: structure

The syllabus limits the description to three features.

| | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | thick | thin | one cell thick |
| Lumen | narrow | wide | very narrow |
| Valves | no | yes | no |

Always use comparisons: "thicker wall than a vein", "wider lumen than an artery".

### Cross-section clues

- **Round, thick wall, small lumen** → artery.
- **Flattened or irregular, thin wall, large lumen** → vein.
- **Tiny, single layer of cells** → capillary.

## Core recall: capillary functions

- Carry blood close to every cell.
- **Exchange of substances** between blood and tissue cells.
- Oxygen and glucose out to cells; carbon dioxide and urea into blood.
- Link the smallest arteries to the smallest veins.

## Core recall: vessels to name

| Organ | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | vena cava, pulmonary vein | aorta, pulmonary artery |
| Lungs | pulmonary artery | pulmonary vein |
| Kidney | renal artery | renal vein |

**Extended only:**

| Organ | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Liver | hepatic artery; hepatic portal vein (from gut) | hepatic veins |

The hepatic portal vein carries nutrient-rich blood **from the gut to the liver**, not to the heart.

## Extended recall: structure and pressure

Pressure falls along the route: **arteries > capillaries > veins**.

- **Artery:** high, surging pressure → thick, muscular, elastic wall withstands it and does not burst; elastic tissue stretches and recoils to keep blood flowing; narrow lumen helps maintain pressure.
- **Vein:** low pressure → thin wall is enough; wide lumen gives little resistance; valves prevent backflow.

## Extended recall: capillary structure and function

- Wall **one cell thick** → short diffusion distance.
- **Narrow lumen** → red blood cells pass close to the wall.
- **Many, branched** → large surface area; every cell near a capillary.

## Two routes to practise

**Kidney (Core):**

```
left ventricle → aorta → renal artery → kidney → renal vein → vena cava → right atrium
```

**Gut to liver to heart (Extended):**

```
small intestine capillaries → hepatic portal vein → liver → hepatic veins → vena cava → right atrium
```

## Worked reminders

**Identifying from measurements.** Vessel M: wall 0.9 mm, lumen 3.0 mm. Vessel N: wall 0.3 mm, lumen 7.5 mm. M has the thicker wall and narrower lumen, so M is an artery; N is a vein. Quote both features and both sets of numbers.

**Ratio check.** Wall : lumen for M = 0.9 : 3.0 = 1 : 3.3; for N = 0.3 : 7.5 = 1 : 25. The smaller second number means a relatively thicker wall, which fits higher pressure.

**Building an explain answer (Extended).** Use three links in each sentence:

1. Name the pressure ("blood in arteries is at high pressure").
2. Name the feature ("so the wall is thick, muscular and elastic").
3. Give the consequence ("so it does not burst and keeps blood flowing").

**Capillary answer frame (Extended).** Feature → effect → process: "one cell thick → short diffusion distance → oxygen diffuses quickly into cells".

## Command words

- **Describe**: three syllabus features, compared.
- **State**: short and precise, for example "exchange of substances".
- **Identify**: full vessel name.
- **Explain**: feature linked to pressure or diffusion with "so".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lumen | Space inside a vessel through which blood flows |
| Artery | Vessel carrying blood away from the heart; thick wall, narrow lumen |
| Vein | Vessel carrying blood to the heart; thin wall, wide lumen, valves |
| Capillary | Vessel with a wall one cell thick where substances are exchanged |
| Valve (in a vein) | Flap that prevents backflow of blood |
| Renal artery / vein | Vessels to / from the kidney |
| Hepatic portal vein | Vessel carrying blood from the gut to the liver (Extended) |
| Hepatic artery / hepatic veins | Vessel to / vessels from the liver (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Non-comparative descriptions ("thick wall" with no "than").
- Giving valves to arteries or capillaries.
- "Thin" for capillaries instead of "one cell thick".
- Saying exchange happens through vein walls.
- Hepatic portal vein sent to the heart.
- Renal artery and renal vein reversed.
- Explaining artery walls as thick "to pump blood": arteries do not pump; the heart does.
- Saying veins have a wide lumen "to hold more blood" without linking it to low pressure or resistance.

## Self-test

1. Define the lumen of a blood vessel.
2. Give two structural differences between an artery and a vein.
3. Which vessel type contains valves?
4. State the function of capillaries.
5. Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the left ventricle.
6. Name the vessel that carries blood to the kidney.
7. A vessel in a cross-section is flattened, with a thin wall and a large lumen. Identify it.
8. (Extended) Why does an artery need a thick wall?
9. (Extended) Why do veins need valves?
10. (Extended) Give two features of capillaries that help exchange, with a reason for each.
11. (Extended) Name the vessel that carries blood from the gut to the liver.
12. (Extended) Name the vessels that carry blood away from the liver.

### Answers

1. The space inside the vessel through which blood flows.
2. Arteries have a thicker wall than veins; arteries have a narrower lumen than veins (or: veins have valves, arteries do not).
3. Veins.
4. Exchange of substances, such as oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide, between blood and tissue cells.
5. The aorta.
6. The renal artery.
7. A vein.
8. It carries blood at high pressure, so a thick wall stops it bursting; elastic tissue stretches and recoils.
9. Blood in veins is at low pressure, so valves prevent it flowing backwards.
10. Wall one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance; many and branched, giving a large surface area (or narrow lumen, bringing red cells close to the wall).
11. The hepatic portal vein.
12. The hepatic veins.
