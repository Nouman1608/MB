**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.8 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The personal impact of sources of authority on the character of a Muslim: "The importance of generosity and sacrifice, including sincerity (Ikhlas), piety, honesty, hiding the faults of others and the importance of physical and mental health."** The prescribed passage is Sahih Muslim 2564c.

The Qur'an and Sunnah are not only a list of rules. They aim to shape a person: the inner qualities that make up character (*akhlaq*), and the outward actions that follow. This content area looks at the personal side, qualities a Muslim works on in himself or herself. The next content area looks at the social side.

## Generosity

**Meaning.** Generosity is giving freely from what one has, whether it is wealth, time, knowledge or help. In Islam it takes two forms: obligatory giving (*Zakah*) and voluntary giving (*sadaqah*).

**Teaching from the Qur'an.**

- "Never will you attain the good [reward] until you spend [in the way of Allah] from that which you love" (3:92). Real generosity costs something.
- The parable of a grain that grows seven ears, each with a hundred grains, shows the multiplied reward of giving (2:261).
- The faithful are those who feed the poor, the orphan and the captive for the sake of Allah alone, without wanting any reward or thanks from them (76:8-9).

**Teaching from the Hadith.** The upper hand (that gives) is better than the lower hand (that receives) (Sahih al-Bukhari). Charity does not decrease wealth (Sahih Muslim).

**Example.** The Prophet (pbuh) gave so freely that he was most generous in Ramadhan, and he never turned away a person who asked. In the Hadith studied for supporters of the Prophet (pbuh), Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) brought all of his wealth when the Prophet (pbuh) asked for charity, and when asked what he had left for his family he answered, "Allah and His Messenger" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3675).

**Impact on character.** Generosity removes greed and selfishness, builds gratitude, and makes a person aware that wealth is a trust from Allah.

## Sacrifice

**Meaning.** Sacrifice is giving up something valued, such as wealth, comfort, time or even safety, for Allah or for others.

**Examples.**

- **Prophet Ibraheem (AS)** was ready to sacrifice his son at Allah's command, and Muslims remember this at Eid al-Adha.
- **The Muhajirun** left their homes and property in Makkah for the sake of their faith.
- **The Ansar** shared their homes and wealth, and the Qur'an praises them: they "give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation" (59:9).
- **Daily sacrifice.** Getting up for Fajr, fasting in Ramadhan, giving Zakah and saving for Hajj all require self-denial.

**Impact on character.** Sacrifice builds self-control, courage and trust in Allah, and it puts the needs of others before one's own comfort.

## Sincerity (Ikhlas)

**Meaning.** Ikhlas is doing a good deed purely for Allah, with no wish for praise.

**Evidence.** "They were not commanded except to worship Allah, [being] sincere to Him in religion" (98:5). The famous Hadith says that actions are judged by intentions (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim). A deed done for show (*riya'*) loses its reward.

**Why it matters.** Generosity and sacrifice mean little if they are done to impress. Sincerity is the spirit that makes every other quality count.

**Impact on character.** It reduces pride and the need for approval, and it brings inner peace.

## Piety (Taqwa)

**Meaning.** Taqwa is awareness of Allah and care to avoid His displeasure. It is often translated as piety, God-consciousness or fear of Allah in the sense of respect and care.

**Evidence.** "Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you" (49:13). The Prophet (pbuh) said, "Fear Allah wherever you are" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi).

**Impact on character.** Taqwa acts as an inner guard. A person behaves well even when no one is watching, because he knows Allah sees him.

## Honesty

**Meaning.** Telling the truth, keeping promises, being fair in dealings and returning trusts.

**Evidence.** The Prophet (pbuh) said that truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness leads to Paradise, while lying leads to wrongdoing (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim). He also said that whoever cheats is not one of us (Sahih Muslim). He himself was known as al-Amin.

**Impact on character.** An honest person is trusted and has a clear conscience.

## Hiding the faults of others

**Meaning.** Not exposing or spreading the private mistakes of other people, and treating them with discretion.

**Evidence.** The Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever hides the faults of a Muslim, Allah will hide his faults in this world and the next (Sahih Muslim). The Qur'an warns against those who love that indecency should spread among the believers (24:19).

**Limit.** Hiding faults does not mean hiding injustice. A person may report a crime to those who can stop it, and may advise a person privately.

**Impact on character.** It builds mercy, humility and the habit of thinking well of others, and it protects people's dignity.

## Physical and mental health

Islam treats the body and the mind as trusts from Allah.

**Physical health.**

- **Rights of the body.** In the Hadith of Salman and Abu Darda, Salman told his friend that his Lord has a right over him, his soul has a right over him and his family has a right over him, so give each its due. The Prophet (pbuh) said that Salman had spoken the truth (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- **Strength.** The strong believer is better and more beloved to Allah than the weak believer, though there is good in both (Sahih Muslim).
- **Cleanliness.** Cleanliness is half of faith (Sahih Muslim), and wudu and washing are part of daily worship.
- **Moderation in food.** The Prophet (pbuh) advised eating in moderation, and the Qur'an commands "eat and drink, but be not excessive" (7:31).
- **Avoiding harm.** Alcohol, drugs and other harmful substances are forbidden. A Muslim should seek medical treatment, because Allah has sent down a cure for every disease (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- **Ease in worship.** The sick may break the fast and make it up later (2:185).

**Mental health.**

- **Peace through remembrance.** The Qur'an says that hearts find assurance in the remembrance of Allah (13:28).
- **Hope in hardship.** "With hardship [will be] ease" (94:5-6), and Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity (2:286).
- **Seeking refuge.** The Prophet (pbuh) taught a prayer for protection from worry and grief (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- **Patience and support.** Patience, prayer and the support of family and community help during trouble.
- **No stigma.** Seeking help for sadness or anxiety is not a sign of weak faith, in the same way that visiting a doctor for illness is not.

**Impact on character.** A healthy body and mind allow a person to worship, work and serve others.

## The prescribed passage: Sahih Muslim 2564c

The Hadith says: "Verily Allah does not look to your faces and your wealth but He looks to your heart and to your deeds."

| Phrase | Link to the topic |
|---|---|
| "does not look to your faces and your wealth" | Appearance and money do not decide a person's worth: a warning against pride and showing off |
| "He looks to your heart" | Ikhlas and taqwa: the inner state matters most |
| "and to your deeds" | Generosity, sacrifice, honesty and care of others must show in action |

Use it to link inner qualities to outer behaviour: a sincere heart produces good deeds, and good deeds without a sincere heart lose their value.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which the teachings of the Qur'an and Hadith shape the personal character of a Muslim. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> They teach generosity [1]: the Qur'an says that people will not attain righteousness until they spend from what they love, so Muslims learn to give and not to be greedy [1]. They also teach sincerity [1], so Muslims learn to do good deeds for Allah and not to show off, which gives them inner peace and humility [1]. The Hadith says that Allah looks at your hearts and deeds, not your faces and wealth, which shows that character matters more than appearance [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the qualities as a list of words.** Define each and show its effect on character.
- **Mixing up Ikhlas and Taqwa.** Ikhlas is sincerity of purpose; taqwa is God-consciousness.
- **Forgetting health.** Physical and mental health are named in the specification.
- **Saying hiding faults means ignoring wrongdoing.** It means protecting privacy and dignity, not allowing injustice.
- **Giving no source.** Each quality should have a verse or Hadith.
