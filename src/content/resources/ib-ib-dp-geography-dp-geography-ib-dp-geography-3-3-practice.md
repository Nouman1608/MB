## Section A

**1.** Define virtual water. **[1]**

**2.** Outline how lithium is extracted from brine beneath salt flats. **[2]**

**3.** State two rules used to manage tourist landings in Antarctica. **[2]**

**4.** Outline why drip irrigation is better suited to deserts than flood irrigation. **[2]**

**5.** State the year in which Nunavut was created as a territory of Canada. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A remote community needs 250 tonnes of supplies a year. Sealift costs 1,000 per tonne and air freight 7,500 per tonne (invented figures).

**(a)** Calculate the annual cost of moving all supplies by sealift and by air. **[2]**

**(b)** Sea ice prevents the sealift from carrying 40 per cent of the load, which must go by air. Calculate the extra cost compared with sending everything by sea. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two ways the community could reduce its dependence on imported supplies. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why Saudi Arabia phased out domestic wheat production. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two pressures created by expedition tourism on Mount Everest. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why mining in a hot desert can create conflict over water. **[3]**

## Section C

**10.** Evaluate the effectiveness of one strategy for managing tourism in an extreme environment. **[10]**

**11.** "The benefits of economic activity in extreme environments rarely reach the people who live there." Discuss this statement. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The water used to produce a good, which is effectively traded when the good is exported or imported [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Brine is pumped from beneath the salt flat into large shallow ponds [1], where the sun evaporates the water and concentrates the lithium salts, which are then processed [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two [1] each: ships carrying more than 500 passengers may not land visitors; no more than 100 visitors ashore at one site at a time; boots and clothing must be cleaned to prevent introducing non-native species; visitors must keep their distance from wildlife. **[2]**

**4.** Drip irrigation delivers small amounts of water directly to plant roots [1], so far less is lost to evaporation and run-off, and less salt is drawn to the surface, reducing the risk of salinisation [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 1999 [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Sealift: 250 x 1,000 = **250,000** [1]. Air: 250 x 7,500 = **1,875,000** [1].

**(b)** 40 per cent of 250 = 100 tonnes [1]. Extra cost = 100 x (7,500 - 1,000) = **650,000** [1].

**(c)** Any two [1] each: local renewable energy such as wind or solar to reduce fuel imports; greenhouses or hydroponics for fresh food; support for hunting, fishing and traditional food; better storage so that more can be brought in when cheaper routes are open. **[6]**

**7.** Wheat was irrigated with deep groundwater that was largely non-renewable [1]. Pumping far exceeded recharge, so aquifers were being depleted [1]; the government decided it was better to conserve the remaining water and import wheat, effectively importing virtual water [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Waste, including human waste and abandoned equipment, accumulates on the mountain [1] because cold slows decomposition and removal from high altitude is difficult [1]. Crowding on summit routes at peak times [1] increases the risk of exhaustion, frostbite and deaths while climbers wait at high altitude [1]. (Accept: risks to Sherpa workers; erosion of trails at lower altitude.) **[4]**

**9.** Mines need large volumes of water to process ore [1], but in a desert water is scarce and already used by towns, farmers, herders and wetlands [1]. Pumping groundwater for mining can lower water tables and dry up springs and lagoons, harming other users, so communities and mining companies compete for the same limited supply [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Indicative content (for example, IAATO rules in Antarctica, or the Everest waste deposit):

- **Description of the strategy and its aims:** for example, IAATO limits on ship size, numbers ashore and biosecurity [1] [1].
- **Strengths:** landings kept small and supervised [1]; biosecurity reduces the risk of non-native species [1]; operators have an interest in protecting the environment that attracts visitors [1].
- **Limitations:** self-regulation depends on voluntary membership [1]; rising visitor numbers increase overall pressure, fuel use and spill risk even if each landing is controlled [1]; enforcement in a remote area is difficult [1].
- **Comparison or context:** the strategy works better where access is limited to a few routes, as in Antarctica, than where it is open to many operators [1].
- **Conclusion:** a supported judgement, for example that the strategy has been effective at limiting local impacts but cannot by itself control the growth of tourism [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Indicative content:

- **For the statement:** profits from mining and energy often go to outside companies and shareholders [1]; fly-in fly-out workforces spend their wages elsewhere [1]; local people bear the environmental costs, such as falling water tables around Atacama salt flats [1]; on Everest, foreign operators capture much of the revenue while local workers take the greatest risks [1].
- **Against:** tourism and expedition work provide jobs and income for Sherpa communities [1]; land claims agreements, such as the one behind Nunavut, give indigenous people a share in decisions and revenue [1]; royalties and community agreements can fund local services [1].
- **Depends on:** governance, ownership of land and resources, and whether local people have political power [1] [1].
- **Conclusion:** benefits often do not reach local people by default, but strong rights and governance can change this, so the statement is true in many cases but not inevitable [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers distinguish who gains (companies, governments, local workers, indigenous communities) rather than writing about "the area" as a single unit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating extra cost using the air price alone, instead of the difference between routes.
- Describing tourism damage without any management response.
- Treating "local people" as one group with the same interests.
- Ignoring water when discussing mining in deserts.
