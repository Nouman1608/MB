## Definitions to learn

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Trading bloc | A group of countries that agree to reduce or remove trade barriers between themselves |
| Free-trade area | Bloc with no barriers between members; each member sets its own barriers on outsiders |
| Customs union | Free trade between members plus one common tariff on outsiders |
| Common market | Customs union plus free movement of labour and capital |
| Trade creation | Buying from a lower-cost partner instead of a higher-cost home producer |
| Trade diversion | Buying from a higher-cost partner instead of a lower-cost outsider |
| Non-member | A country outside the bloc, facing the external barriers |
| WTO | International organisation, set up in 1995, that sets rules for trade and settles disputes between members |
| Primary product | A farm good, mineral or fuel, sold with little processing |
| Trade pattern | What a country exports and imports, and with whom |

## Examples of blocs to quote

| Bloc | Type to attach |
|---|---|
| European Union | Customs union and single market (some members share a currency) |
| USMCA | Free-trade agreement |
| ASEAN | Group with a free-trade area |
| Mercosur | Customs union |

## Impact of a bloc in one table

| | Members | Non-members |
|---|---|---|
| Prices | Fall on partners' goods | Their goods become dearer inside the bloc |
| Sales | Rise for efficient exporters; fall for weak home firms | Fall, if partners now win the business |
| Jobs | Gained in competitive firms; lost in weak ones | Lost in export firms that lose sales |
| Costs | Larger market allows lower average cost | Smaller market, higher average cost |
| Investment | Outside firms may build inside the bloc | Less investment attracted |
| Policy | Less freedom to set own barriers | May retaliate or join another bloc |

## Method in steps: trade creation or diversion

1. Work out each supplier's price with tariff before the bloc.
2. Find the cheapest source before the bloc.
3. Remove the tariff on partners and recompute.
4. If a partner replaces a home producer, it is creation.
5. If a partner replaces a cheaper outsider, it is diversion.
6. Quantity of units times the price gap gives the saving to buyers.

## Worked reminders

**Creation.** Home price 80, partner price 62, tariff 30%. Before: 62 × 1.30 = 80.6, so buy at home. After: 62. Saving per unit = 18, which is 22.5% of 80. On 5,000 units: **90,000**.

**Diversion.** Tariff 20%. Partner 50, outsider 46. Before: outsider 46 × 1.20 = 55.2. After: partner 50. Buyers save 5.2 per unit (9.4%). Real cost rises 4 per unit. On 10,000 units, buyers save 52,000 but the government loses 92,000 of revenue, so the net change is **−40,000**.

**Outsider matching.** Highest price outsider can charge before tariff = 50 ÷ 1.20 = 41.67, a cut of 9.4% from 46.

**Economies of scale.** Average cost = fixed cost ÷ output + variable cost. With 600,000 fixed and 30 variable: 20,000 units gives 60; 50,000 units gives 42, a fall of **30%**.

## The WTO: roles and actions

- **Negotiates** lower trade barriers; deals become binding rules.
- **Promotes equal treatment** of members (blocs are an exception).
- **Settles disputes**: a panel rules on a complaint.
- **Allows retaliation** of equal value if a loser does not comply.
- **Monitors** members' policies through regular reviews.
- **Supports developing countries** with training and longer time to adjust.
- **Allows duties on dumped goods** where dumping is shown.

Evaluation points: slow talks, costly cases for poor countries, weak enforcement against large economies, tension with blocs.

**Dispute arithmetic.** Exports fall from 800 to 680 million. Loss = 120 million. Percentage fall = 120 ÷ 800 = **15%**. Authorised retaliation is limited to about the same value.

## Trade patterns

| | Developed | Developing |
|---|---|---|
| Exports | Manufactures, machinery, services | Primary products, some low-skill manufactures |
| Imports | Manufactures, raw materials, fuel | Machinery, vehicles, technology, fuel |
| Spread | Wide range | Often few products |
| Partners | Mainly other developed countries | Often few partners |
| Earnings | Steadier | Volatile |

Many developing countries now export more manufactures and trade more with each other.

**Dependence.** Exports 500 million, 62% coffee: 310 million. Price falls 20%, PED −0.5, so quantity rises 10%. Revenue = 0.80 × 1.10 = 0.88, a fall of 12%, or 37.2 million. Total exports fall 7.4%.

**Income elasticity.** Primary product YED 0.3, machinery YED 1.5, income up 10%: demand up 3% against 15%.

## Chains of reasoning to learn

**Why a bloc can help a member.** Tariffs on partners removed, so partners' prices fall, so buyers pay less and firms using imported inputs cut costs, so prices fall or profits rise, so living standards may rise. A bigger market also lets firms spread fixed costs, so average cost falls.

**Why a bloc can harm a member.** Weak home firms face cheaper partner goods, so sales fall, so some firms close and workers lose jobs. If the cheapest world supplier is outside, diversion raises the real cost of buying.

**Why a bloc can harm a non-member.** The external tariff stays on its goods while partners pay none, so its goods are dearer in the bloc, so it sells less, so its output and jobs fall. It may cut price, find new markets, join another bloc or retaliate, and retaliation can hurt its own consumers.

**Why developing countries can struggle in trade.** Few exports, mostly primary products, so earnings follow world prices. Demand for primary products grows slowly as incomes rise (low income elasticity), so export earnings grow slowly. Imports of machinery and technology cost more, so deficits can open up.

**Why the WTO matters.** Agreed rules make trade policy more predictable, so firms invest and export with more confidence. A dispute system means a smaller country can challenge a larger one without relying on force. The limit is that a ruling needs the loser to cooperate.

## Must-know distinctions

- Creation versus diversion.
- Free-trade area versus customs union: only the second has a common external tariff.
- Member versus non-member effects.
- WTO rules versus WTO enforcement.
- Developed exports (manufactures, services) versus developing exports (primary products).

## Quick self-test

1. Define a trading bloc.
2. Name the extra feature a customs union has over a free-trade area.
3. State two examples of trading blocs.
4. Home price 90, partner price 70, tariff 40%. Buying from the partner before the bloc costs what? Is trade created after the bloc?
5. A firm has fixed costs of 300,000 and variable cost of 20 per unit. Find average cost at 10,000 and at 40,000 units.
6. Give two ways a non-member can lose from a bloc.
7. State two actions of the WTO.
8. Exports of 250 fall to 210 after a trade dispute. Find the percentage fall.
9. State two features of the exports of many developing countries.
10. Primary product YED is 0.4 and income rises 4%. Find the change in demand.
11. Give one reason why export earnings from primary products can be unstable.

### Answers

1. A group of countries that reduce or remove trade barriers between themselves.
2. A common external tariff.
3. Any two, such as the European Union and Mercosur.
4. 70 × 1.40 = 98, above 90, so home buying before. After the bloc, 70 is below 90, so trade is created. Saving = 20 per unit.
5. At 10,000: 300,000 ÷ 10,000 + 20 = 50. At 40,000: 7.50 + 20 = 27.50.
6. Higher prices inside the bloc for their goods; lost sales and jobs; they may be tempted to retaliate.
7. Any two: negotiate trade deals, settle disputes, monitor policies, authorise retaliation, support developing countries.
8. (250 − 210) ÷ 250 = 16%.
9. Reliance on primary products; a narrow range of products or few buyers.
10. 0.4 × 4 = 1.6% rise.
11. Supply and demand for primary products shift with weather and world demand, and demand is price inelastic, so prices swing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a customs union as "no tariffs", missing the common external tariff.
- Treating trade diversion as only a gain because prices fall for buyers.
- Writing about tariff diagrams when the question is about blocs.
- Calling the WTO a bloc or saying it "bans" all tariffs.
- Quoting a bloc without saying what type it is.
- Using real-world claims from memory rather than the data given.
- Missing the working in a calculation.
- Ending an evaluation with no decision.
