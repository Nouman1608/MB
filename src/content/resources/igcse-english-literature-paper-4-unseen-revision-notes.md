## Who actually sits this paper

Paper 4 only exists for candidates on the **Paper 3 + Paper 4 route**
— it is not an option alongside Paper 2. If your centre enters you for
Paper 2 Drama instead, this paper does not apply to you; confirm your
route with your teacher before revising it.

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration | 1 hour 15 minutes |
| Marks | 25 |
| Weighting | 25% of the qualification (route-dependent) |
| Choice | 1 question from 2 (poem or prose extract) |
| Prior knowledge required | **None** — genuinely unseen |
| Command word | "Explore" |

Both questions carry equal marks, so choose based on which text form
plays to your strengths, not on perceived difficulty.

## What's tested — no content, only skill

Unlike Papers 1-3, there is **no set-text list** for Paper 4. The
skill rewarded is close reading and analysis applied to an unfamiliar
poem or prose extract, printed in full on the paper itself — nothing
needs to be memorised or brought into the exam.

## The four AOs, equally weighted (25% each)

| AO | Focus |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge of content via detailed reference and quotation |
| AO2 | Understanding of meanings, characters, relationships, themes |
| AO3 | Understanding of the writer's methods (language, structure, form) |
| AO4 | A sensitive, informed **personal response** |

AO4 is credited explicitly and equally alongside the others — a
commentary that identifies a technique but never states its effect,
or what the candidate makes of it, is under-performing even when the
technical identification is accurate.

## The 20-minute planning window

Cambridge's own guidance: spend around 20 of the 75 minutes reading
both questions and planning your answer before writing. Use it to:

1. Read the extract **twice**.
2. Annotate for language, structure, form, tone and shifts in mood.
3. Decide poem vs prose — then commit. Switching part-way through
wastes time neither the paper nor the candidate can afford.

## Comparing the poem and prose options

Both options test the same four AOs, but the raw material differs in
useful ways:

| | Poem option | Prose option |
|---|---|---|
| Length | Usually shorter, denser | A longer extract, more incident |
| Form focus | Line breaks, rhyme/rhythm, stanza structure | Paragraphing, narrative viewpoint, pacing |
| Risk | Over-quoting single words without unpacking effect | Retelling plot instead of analysing method |
| Strength if practised | Close, word-level analysis comes naturally | Tracking how tension/character builds across the extract |

Neither option is inherently "easier" — the mark scheme rewards the
same analytical skills either way. Choosing well on the day depends on
which extract you can say more about in 55 minutes of writing, not on
a fixed preference decided in advance.

## Building a personal response (AO4) that actually scores

AO4 is where candidates most often under-perform relative to their
AO2/AO3 marks, because it requires more than noticing a technique — it
requires saying what the technique does to a reader and to you
specifically. A weak AO4 sentence names a device ("the writer uses a
metaphor here"); a strong one connects the device to an effect and a
response ("the metaphor makes the character's fear feel physical and
overwhelming, which unsettled me as a reader because it withholds any
reassurance that the danger will pass"). Building this habit during
practice — always finishing an analytical point with "...and this
makes me feel/think..." — is the single most reliable way to convert
AO2/AO3 observations into AO4 marks without needing separate revision
time.

## Exam traps

- Writing a running summary of what happens, instead of selective,
well-supported analysis of how specific words/images/structural
choices create effects.
- Only ever practising unseen poetry (or only prose) during the
course, then being caught out when the day's better option turns out
to be the other form.
- Identifying a technique (AO3) without stating its effect or your
own response to it (AO4) — both are needed for full marks.
- Under-using the 20-minute planning window, leading to an
unstructured commentary written under increased time pressure.
- Assuming Paper 4 is "lower stakes" because there's no set text to
learn — for candidates on this route it carries the same 25%
weighting as an entire studied set text on Paper 2.

## Self-test

1. Which candidates actually sit Paper 4, and why doesn't it apply
to everyone doing 0475?
2. What is fundamentally different about Paper 4 compared with
Papers 1-3 in terms of preparation?
3. Name the four assessment objectives and their equal weighting.
4. How long should planning take, and what three things should
happen during it?
5. What is the most common weakness in a Paper 4 response, and how
is it avoided?

**Answers:** 1. Only candidates on the Paper 3 + Paper 4 route; those
entered for Paper 2 Drama instead do not sit Paper 4. 2. No content
can be pre-learned — preparation means rehearsing a repeatable
close-reading process on unfamiliar texts, not memorising material.
3. AO1 (knowledge via reference/quotation), AO2 (understanding of
meanings), AO3 (understanding of methods), AO4 (personal response) —
each weighted 25%. 4. Around 20 minutes; read the extract twice,
annotate it, and decide which option (poem or prose) to commit to.
5. Writing an unanalytical running summary instead of selective,
well-supported analysis of specific techniques and their effects —
avoided by annotating for language/structure/form/tone during
planning and building the commentary directly from those annotations.
