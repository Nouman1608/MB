Quick recall for section 2.3, Nature of quantities, of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017: learning outcomes 2.3.1(a)-(d), Scalars and vectors. For full worked examples, read the [nature of quantities study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-h556-nature-of-quantities-ocr-alevel/), then test yourself with the [nature of quantities practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-h556-nature-of-quantities-ocr-alevel-practice/).

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scalar | A quantity with magnitude only |
| Vector | A quantity with magnitude and direction |
| Resultant | The single vector with the same effect as two or more vectors combined |
| Coplanar | Lying in the same plane |
| Component | One of two perpendicular vectors that together replace the original |
| Resolving | Splitting a vector into perpendicular components |
| Vector triangle | Two vectors drawn tip to tail, closed by their resultant |

## Examples to quote

| Scalar | Matching vector |
|---|---|
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| mass | weight |
| — | acceleration, force, momentum, field strength |
| energy, power, pressure, temperature, time, charge, current, volume, density | — |

Remember: pressure, energy and current are scalars.

## Adding two vectors

| Case | Method |
|---|---|
| Same line, same direction | add magnitudes |
| Same line, opposite directions | subtract; direction of the larger |
| Perpendicular | R = √(a² + b²); θ = tan⁻¹(b/a) |
| Any angle | cosine rule for R; sine rule for direction |
| Any angle, no calculator method | scale drawing with stated scale |

- Range of the resultant of a and b: from |a − b| to a + b.
- Interior angle in the triangle = 180° − angle between the vectors drawn from one point.

## Subtracting vectors

- a − b = a + (−b): reverse b, then add tip to tail.
- Change in velocity: Δv = v − u. A change of direction at constant speed still gives a non-zero Δv.
- Two equal speeds at angle φ apart: |Δv| = 2v sin(φ/2).

## Resolving

| Angle measured from | Horizontal component | Vertical component |
|---|---|---|
| the horizontal | F cos θ | F sin θ |
| the vertical | F sin θ | F cos θ |

- On a slope at θ: W sin θ down the slope, W cos θ into the slope.
- Components of perpendicular directions are independent of each other.

## Adding several vectors

1. Resolve each into x and y, with signs.
2. Sum each direction.
3. R = √(Σx² + Σy²); direction from tan⁻¹(Σy/Σx), checking the quadrant.
4. Both sums zero means equilibrium.

## Worked reminders

- 12 N east + 5.0 N north: R = 13 N at 22.6° north of east.
- 8.0 N and 8.0 N at 90° to each other: R = 11.3 N, bisecting the angle.
- 50 N at 35° above the horizontal: 41.0 N horizontal, 28.7 N vertical.
- Weight 600 N on a 20° slope: 205 N down the slope, 564 N into it.
- Velocity 15 m s⁻¹ north to 15 m s⁻¹ east: Δv = 21.2 m s⁻¹ towards the south-east.

## Exam traps

- Arithmetic addition of non-parallel vectors.
- Using the angle between vectors drawn from a point in the cosine rule, rather than the triangle's interior angle.
- No direction on a resultant, or a direction with no reference ("at 30°" to what?).
- sin and cos swapped when the angle is from the vertical.
- Adding u to v instead of subtracting for a change in velocity.
- Scale drawings with no scale written down or too small to measure accurately.
- Using the wrong quadrant after tan⁻¹ when the x-sum is negative.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a vector quantity.
2. Name two scalars and two vectors.
3. Why is current treated as a scalar?
4. Forces of 7.0 N and 4.0 N act at a point. State the largest and smallest possible resultants.
5. Find the resultant of 9.0 N east and 12 N north.
6. Resolve 30 N acting at 40° to the horizontal.
7. Resolve 30 N acting at 40° to the vertical.
8. A box of weight 250 N rests on a slope at 30°. Find the component of its weight down the slope.
9. Two forces of 5.0 N and 8.0 N act at 60° to each other. Find the resultant.
10. A ball moving at 6.0 m s⁻¹ east bounces back at 4.0 m s⁻¹ west. Find the change in velocity.
11. State how to subtract vector b from vector a in a diagram.
12. A runner completes exactly two laps of a 400 m track. Give the distance and displacement.

### Answers

1. A quantity with both magnitude and direction.
2. Scalars: speed, mass (or energy, time). Vectors: velocity, force (or displacement, acceleration).
3. Currents at a junction combine by simple addition (Kirchhoff's first law), not by vector rules.
4. Largest 11.0 N (same direction); smallest 3.0 N (opposite directions).
5. √(81 + 144) = **15 N** at tan⁻¹(12/9.0) = **53.1° north of east**.
6. Horizontal 30 cos 40° = **23.0 N**; vertical 30 sin 40° = **19.3 N**.
7. Horizontal 30 sin 40° = **19.3 N**; vertical 30 cos 40° = **23.0 N**.
8. 250 sin 30° = **125 N**.
9. Interior angle 120°: R² = 25 + 64 − 80 cos 120° = 129, so **R = 11.4 N**.
10. Taking east as positive: Δv = −4.0 − 6.0 = **−10 m s⁻¹**, so 10 m s⁻¹ west.
11. Reverse b to give −b, then add it to a tip to tail; the resultant is a − b.
12. Distance **800 m**; displacement **0** (back at the start).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a scale drawing instead of calculation in OCR A Level Physics A?**
Yes. Learning outcome 2.3.1(c) allows vector triangles to be solved by calculation or by scale drawing. A question may specify one method; otherwise either is accepted if done carefully.

**Which angle goes in the cosine rule?**
The interior angle of the vector triangle, between the two sides you know. When the vectors are drawn from a point with angle θ between them, the interior angle is 180° − θ.

**Is weight a vector?**
Yes. Weight is a force, so it has a direction, vertically downwards. Mass is the matching scalar.
