Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539) is a language
qualification for students who are learning Urdu in addition to
another first language. Rather than studying a list of topics, students
build practical skills in reading, writing and listening, with an
optional speaking component. The grade comes from two papers: Paper 1
Reading and Writing, worth 67%, and Paper 2 Listening, worth 33%. The
speaking test, Component 5, is optional and reported separately. This
overview follows the 2025-2027 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

Because this is a skills-based language course, the syllabus is
organised by paper and exercise rather than by content topic.

### Paper 1 – Reading and Writing

Paper 1 contains six exercises, ending with two writing tasks:

- **Exercise 1: Short answer questions**
- **Exercise 2: Multiple matching**
- **Exercise 3: Note-making**
- **Exercise 4: Summary**
- **Exercise 5: Writing exercise**
- **Exercise 6: Extended writing exercise**

### Paper 2 – Listening

Paper 2 contains four exercises based on recorded material:

- **Exercise 1: Short answer questions**
- **Exercise 2: Gap-fill sentences**
- **Exercise 3: Multiple matching**
- **Exercise 4: Multiple-choice questions**

### Component 5 – Speaking

The optional speaking test is a separately endorsed component. It is
assessed by the school and externally moderated.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Reading and Writing | 120 minutes | 60 | 67% |
| Paper 2 | Listening | approximately 35–45 minutes | 30 | 33% |
| Component 5 | Speaking (optional, separately endorsed) | approximately 10–12 minutes | 60 | 0% |

Papers 1 and 2 together make up the whole IGCSE grade. The course is a
single second-language route with no tiers, and assessment is linear.

Component 5 does **not** count towards the IGCSE grade, which is why
its weighting is shown as 0%. If you take it, a separate speaking grade
from 1 (high) to 5 (low) is recorded on your certificate alongside the
main grade. Check with your school whether you will be entered for it.

## How the parts fit together

Although Paper 1 and Paper 2 are separate exams, they draw on the same
underlying language knowledge: vocabulary, grammar and an understanding
of how texts are organised.

Several exercise types appear in both papers. Short answer questions
and multiple matching are tested in reading and again in listening, so
the strategies you develop for one, such as identifying key words in
the question before you read or listen, carry over to the other.

Within Paper 1 there is a clear progression. The early exercises test
understanding, the middle exercises (note-making and summary) test your
ability to select and reorganise information, and the final two
exercises test your own writing. Note-making and summary are a natural
bridge: the selection skills from note-making are exactly what a good
summary needs, before you rephrase the ideas in continuous Urdu.

Speaking, even if you do not take Component 5, supports everything
else. Regular conversation practice builds the vocabulary and fluency
that make listening easier and writing more natural.

## Planning your study

Language skills develop gradually, so this course benefits from steady
practice across all skills throughout, rather than studying one paper
and then the other. A general plan might look like this:

- **Early in the course:** build core vocabulary and grammar, and
  practise the receptive exercises: short answers, multiple matching,
  gap-fill and multiple-choice listening tasks.
- **Middle of the course:** introduce note-making and summary, and
  begin shorter writing tasks for different purposes. Increase the
  length and variety of listening material.
- **Later in the course:** concentrate on extended writing, accuracy
  and range, and on timed reading and listening practice.

Outside lessons, read and listen to Urdu as often as you can, choosing
material at a level you can mostly follow. Keep a vocabulary log
organised by theme, adding useful phrases as well as single words, and
reuse new language in your own short pieces of writing. Reviewing the
log regularly helps with every exercise, from recognising answers in
the listening paper to showing range in the extended writing task.

If you are taking Component 5, rehearse speaking regularly rather than
only in the final weeks. Begin practising full exercises in exam
conditions once you are comfortable with each format, and leave
component-by-component exam preparation for the final stretch before
your exam series.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Cambridge IGCSE Urdu 0539 assess?**
Reading and writing in Paper 1 (120 minutes, 60 marks, 67%) and listening in Paper 2 (approximately 35–45 minutes, 30 marks, 33%). There is also an optional speaking component.

**Does the speaking test count towards my IGCSE grade?**
No. Component 5 Speaking is optional and separately endorsed. Its marks do not count towards the IGCSE grade; instead a separate speaking grade from 1 (high) to 5 (low) appears on the certificate.

**How long is the speaking test?**
Component 5 lasts approximately 10–12 minutes and is marked out of 60.

**How many exercises are in Paper 1?**
Six: short answer questions, multiple matching, note-making, summary, a writing exercise and an extended writing exercise.

**What kinds of question are in the listening paper?**
Paper 2 has four exercises: short answer questions, gap-fill sentences, multiple matching and multiple-choice questions.
