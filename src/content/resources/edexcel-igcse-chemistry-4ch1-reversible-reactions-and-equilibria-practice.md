These questions cover sub-topic 3(c) Reversible reactions and equilibria of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 3.17 to 3.22. Points 3.19C to 3.22C are Paper 2 only, and questions on them are marked **(Paper 2 only)**. Le Chatelier's principle is not required: answer using the endothermic and exothermic directions and the numbers of moles of gas. Revise with the [reversible reactions and equilibria study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-reversible-reactions-and-equilibria/) and the [physical chemistry revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-physical-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the symbol used in an equation to show that a reaction is reversible. **[1]**

**2.** State the colour change when water is added to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. **[1]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) State what is meant by the position of equilibrium lying to the right. **[1]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) State two characteristics of a reversible reaction at dynamic equilibrium. **[2]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) Explain why dynamic equilibrium can be reached only in a sealed container. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated in a test tube.

**(a)** Describe what you would see. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a word equation using the reversible symbol. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe what happens when the cooled solid is mixed with a few drops of water, and state what this shows about the reaction. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of anhydrous copper(II) sulfate left when 10.0 g of CuSO₄.5H₂O (Mr = 249.5) is fully dehydrated. **[3]**

**7.** Solid ammonium chloride is heated in a test tube with a piece of damp litmus paper at the mouth.

**(a)** Name the two gases formed. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a white solid forms near the top of the tube. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this is a reversible reaction. **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) The Haber process makes ammonia: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** State the effect of increasing the pressure on the position of equilibrium and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect on the position of equilibrium of increasing the temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** State and explain the effect of an iron catalyst on the position of equilibrium and on the rate of reaching it. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why a moderate temperature is used even though a low temperature gives a higher yield. **[1]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Dinitrogen tetroxide is colourless and nitrogen dioxide is brown: N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g). The forward reaction is endothermic.

**(a)** A sealed tube of the equilibrium mixture is heated. Describe and explain the colour change. **[2]**
**(b)** The pressure on the tube is then increased at constant temperature. Describe and explain the colour change. **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) Sulfur trioxide is made: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** Predict the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of sulfur trioxide. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the effect of decreasing the temperature on the yield. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why increasing the pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium in the reaction H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). **[1]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) A student writes: "At equilibrium the reaction has stopped, and the concentrations of reactants and products are equal." Identify and correct the two mistakes. **[2]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) Sketch a graph of the rate of the forward and reverse reactions against time for a reversible reaction that starts with only reactants in a sealed container. Describe the shape of each line. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** ⇌ [1].

**2.** White to blue [1].

**3.** There is more product than reactant at equilibrium (the equilibrium mixture contains mostly products) [1].

**4.** The forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate [1]. The concentrations of reactants and products remain constant [1].

**5.** In an open container, a gas could escape, so products (or reactants) would be lost and the forward and reverse rates could not balance [1].

**6. (a)** The blue crystals turn white [1]. Steam (water droplets) is seen at the cooler part of the tube [1].
**(b)** hydrated copper(II) sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water [1].
**(c)** The white solid turns blue and gets warm [1]. This shows the reaction is reversible, and that the reverse reaction is exothermic [1].
**(d)** Fraction of water = 90 / 249.5 = 0.361 [1]. Mass of water = 0.361 x 10.0 = 3.61 g [1]. Mass of anhydrous solid = 10.0 - 3.61 = 6.39 g [1].

**7. (a)** Ammonia and hydrogen chloride [1].
**(b)** Ammonium chloride breaks down into ammonia and hydrogen chloride on heating [1]. The two gases move to the cooler part of the tube and recombine to form solid ammonium chloride [1].
**(c)** The products can react to re-form the reactant [1].

**8. (a)** The position moves to the right, so more ammonia forms [1], because there are 4 moles of gas on the left and 2 on the right, and increased pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas [1].
**(b)** The position moves to the left, so the yield of ammonia falls (the reverse reaction is endothermic) [1].
**(c)** No effect on the position of equilibrium, because it speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally [1]; it makes equilibrium be reached faster [1].
**(d)** At a low temperature the rate is too slow; a moderate temperature gives an acceptable rate and yield [1].

**9. (a)** The mixture becomes darker brown [1], because the position moves to the right (the endothermic direction), producing more NO₂ [1].
**(b)** The mixture becomes paler (lighter brown) [1], because the position moves to the left, to the side with fewer moles of gas (1 compared with 2), producing more colourless N₂O₄ [1].

**10. (a)** The yield increases (3 moles of gas on the left, 2 on the right) [1].
**(b)** The yield increases (the forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature favours it) [1].
**(c)** There are 2 moles of gas on each side, so a change in pressure affects both directions equally [1].

**11.** Mistake 1: the reaction has not stopped; the forward and reverse reactions continue at the same rate [1]. Mistake 2: the concentrations are constant, not necessarily equal [1].

**12.** Axes labelled rate (vertical) and time (horizontal) [1]. The forward rate starts high and falls [1]. The reverse rate starts at zero and rises, and the two lines meet and stay level at the same rate, from the time that equilibrium is reached [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the reaction has stopped" at equilibrium.
- Saying the concentrations are equal.
- Saying a catalyst increases the yield.
- Reversing the temperature rule and favouring the exothermic reaction when heating.
- Counting a solid or liquid when comparing moles of gas.
- Mixing up the colour changes in the NO₂ and N₂O₄ system.
