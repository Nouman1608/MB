Condensed notes on the Redundancy and dismissal content (5.9 on this site) in the area of study The workforce, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and two costed examples are in the [redundancy and dismissal study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-redundancy-and-dismissal/). Test yourself with the [redundancy and dismissal practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-redundancy-and-dismissal-practice/). All figures are invented, and employment law changes, so use the figures for understanding only.

## What the specification lists

- Redundancy; dismissal.
- Fair dismissal: capacity, conduct, redundancy, other substantial reason.
- Unfair dismissal and its reasons: pregnancy, trade union membership, discrimination, whistle blowing.
- Procedure for dismissal; procedure for a grievance.
- Impact and importance of redundancy and dismissal; effectiveness of procedures.
- Employment tribunals: meaning, purpose, significance.

## Core recall

### Key distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dismissal | Employer ends the contract |
| Redundancy | Dismissal because the job is no longer needed; not about the person's conduct or ability |
| Resignation | Employee ends the contract |
| Constructive dismissal | Employee resigns because of the employer's serious breach of contract |
| Unfair dismissal | No fair reason, or no fair procedure, or an automatically unfair reason |

### Fair reasons

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| Capacity | Cannot do the job after support and training; long-term ill health |
| Conduct | Theft, violence, repeated lateness, breaking safety rules |
| Redundancy | Post no longer needed after consultation and fair selection |
| Some other substantial reason | Genuine reorganisation; refusal of a necessary contract change |

### Unfair reasons named in the specification

Pregnancy; trade union membership; discrimination; whistle blowing. Also: asserting a legal right, or unfair selection for redundancy.

### Dismissal procedure

1. Investigate.
2. Write to the employee with the concern, evidence and hearing date.
3. Hold a hearing; the employee may be accompanied.
4. Decide in writing with reasons (warnings first for ordinary misconduct or poor performance).
5. Offer an appeal to a more senior person.

### Grievance procedure

1. Informal discussion with the line manager.
2. Formal written grievance.
3. Meeting; employee may be accompanied.
4. Written decision.
5. Appeal.

### Redundancy procedure

Plan; consult; fair and objective selection; look for alternatives (redeployment, voluntary options); give notice; pay notice and redundancy pay.

### Employment tribunal

- An independent legal body hearing disputes between employers and employees (unfair dismissal, discrimination, unpaid wages).
- Purpose: resolve disputes more informally and cheaply than a court.
- Can order compensation, reinstatement or re-engagement.
- Significance: a way to justice for employees; a cost and reputational risk for employers; a reason for fair procedures and records.

### Costs of redundancy

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| Redundancy pay | Lower morale of survivors |
| Notice pay | Loss of skills and experience |
| Consultation and admin | Fall in output |
| | Reputation as an employer |
| | Cost of recruiting again if demand returns |

### Evaluating procedures

- Effective when clear, consistent, fair and used early.
- Limits: slow; need management time; may be treated as box-ticking; employees may distrust a process run by their manager.

## Worked reminder

Pelham Plastics (invented), 10 redundancies, £520 a week, 6 years' service, enhanced scheme of 2 weeks per year, 4 weeks' notice.

- Redundancy pay 2 × 6 × £520 = £6,240 each = **£62,400**. Notice 4 × £520 = £2,080 each = **£20,800**. Total **£83,200**.
- Annual saving 10 × £520 × 52 = **£270,400**; payback **3.7 months**.
- If the 90 survivors' output falls 4% on a contribution of £4.5 million, the loss is **£180,000**; net first-year benefit **£7,200**.
- Unfair dismissal claim (illustrative): £6,500 + £1,600 + £12,000 = **£20,100**, against a fair process costing about **£1,480**.

## Evaluating redundancy: a checklist

1. Is the fall in demand permanent?
2. Direct costs against annual saving.
3. Effect on survivors and output.
4. Loss of skills; cost of rehiring.
5. Alternatives (freeze, voluntary leaving, reduced hours, redeployment).
6. Effect on employees, community and reputation.
7. Judgement and its conditions.

## Quick case practice

A firm (invented) makes 6 staff redundant. Each receives £3,000 redundancy pay and £1,200 notice pay, and each earned £22,000 a year.

- Cost = 6 × (£3,000 + £1,200) = **£25,200**.
- Annual wage saving = 6 × £22,000 = **£132,000**.
- Payback = £25,200 ÷ £132,000 × 12 = **2.3 months**.

Model response outline: the payback is fast, but the figures omit lost output, the morale of survivors and the cost of rehiring if demand returns. Check that selection was fair and that alternatives were considered. Judgement: redundancy is justified only if the fall in demand is permanent.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gross misconduct | Serious misconduct that may justify immediate dismissal after a fair process |
| Whistle blowing | Reporting wrongdoing at work |
| Notice | The period between telling an employee and the contract ending |
| Voluntary redundancy | Employees choose to leave for a payment |
| Grievance | A complaint by an employee about treatment at work |
| Reinstatement | Giving an employee their job back |
| Conciliation | Attempt to settle a dispute before a tribunal |

## Exam traps

- Redundancy is about the job, not the person.
- Dismissal needs a fair reason and a fair procedure.
- A grievance is the employee's complaint; discipline is the employer's concern.
- Count indirect costs, not only redundancy pay.
- Procedures do not guarantee fairness.
- Quote the case when judging the effect of redundancy.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between redundancy and dismissal for conduct.
2. Name the four fair reasons for dismissal in the specification.
3. Name two reasons for unfair dismissal.
4. Give two steps in a dismissal procedure.
5. Give two steps in a grievance procedure.
6. 5 staff each receive £4,000 redundancy pay and £1,500 notice pay. Calculate the total.
7. They each earned £450 a week. Calculate the annual wage saving.
8. Calculate the payback period in months, to one decimal place.
9. What is the purpose of an employment tribunal?
10. Give one indirect cost of redundancy.

### Answers

1. Redundancy happens because the job is no longer needed; dismissal for conduct happens because of what the employee did.
2. Capacity, conduct, redundancy and some other substantial reason.
3. Any two: pregnancy; trade union membership; discrimination; whistle blowing.
4. Any two: investigate; write to the employee; hold a hearing; decide in writing; offer appeal.
5. Any two: informal talk; formal written grievance; meeting; written decision; appeal.
6. 5 × (£4,000 + £1,500) = **£27,500**.
7. 5 × £450 × 52 = **£117,000**.
8. £27,500 ÷ £117,000 × 12 = **2.8 months**.
9. To resolve disputes between employers and employees more informally and cheaply than a court.
10. Any one: lower morale; loss of skills; fall in output; reputation.
