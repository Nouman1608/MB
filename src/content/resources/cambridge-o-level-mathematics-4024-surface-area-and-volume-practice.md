These questions cover section 5.4, Surface area and volume, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [surface area and volume study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume/), and the [surface area and volume revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume-revision-notes/) list the formulas. Use the π key on your calculator. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** A cuboid measures 9 cm by 4 cm by 2.5 cm. Find its volume and total surface area. **[3]**

**2.** A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 8 cm. Find its volume and curved surface area. **[3]**

**3.** A sphere has radius 7 cm. Find its surface area and volume. **[3]**

**4.** A cone has base radius 5 cm and vertical height 12 cm. Find the slant height, the curved surface area and the volume. **[4]**

**5.** A hemisphere has radius 3 cm. Find its volume and its total surface area, in terms of π. **[3]**

**6.** A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and radius 4 cm. Find its height. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A prism has a right-angled triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm as its cross-section. It is 15 cm long. Find its volume and its total surface area. **[4]**

**8.** A pyramid has a square base of side 6 cm and a vertical height of 4 cm. The apex is directly above the centre of the base.

**(a)** Find the volume.

**(b)** Find the slant height of a triangular face.

**(c)** Find the total surface area. **[4]**

**9.** The volume of a sphere is 288π cm³. Find its radius. **[2]**

**10.** The curved surface area of a cone is 75π cm² and its slant height is 15 cm. Find its base radius. **[2]**

**11.** A solid metal sphere of radius 3 cm is lowered into a cylindrical tank of radius 10 cm that holds water. The sphere is completely covered. Find the rise in the water level. **[3]**

**12.** A solid metal cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 18 cm is melted and recast into spheres of radius 3 cm. How many spheres can be made? **[3]**

**13.** A tin of beans is a cylinder with diameter 8 cm and height 11 cm.

**(a)** Find the area of the label that goes round the curved surface.

**(b)** Find the capacity of the tin in millilitres. **[3]**

**14.** A prism has a cross-section that is a sector of angle 120° and radius 9 cm. The prism is 20 cm long. Find its volume to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**15.** A cone has base radius 4 cm and a vertical height of 9 cm. A cylinder has the same base radius and the same height. Find the ratio of the volume of the cone to the volume of the cylinder. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Volume = 9 × 4 × 2.5 = **90 cm³** [1]. Surface area = 2(36 + 22.5 + 10) [1] = **137 cm²** [1].

**2.** Volume = π × 3² × 8 = 72π = **226 cm³** [1][1]. Curved surface area = 2π × 3 × 8 = 48π = **151 cm²** [1].

**3.** Surface area = 4π × 49 = 196π = **616 cm²** [1][1]. Volume = 4/3 × π × 343 = **1440 cm³** (1437 to 4 significant figures) [1].

**4.** Slant height = √(25 + 144) = **13 cm** [1]. Curved surface area = π × 5 × 13 = 65π = **204 cm²** [1][1]. Volume = 1/3 × π × 25 × 12 = 100π = **314 cm³** [1].

**5.** Volume = 1/2 × 4/3 × π × 27 = **18π cm³** [1]. Curved surface = 1/2 × 4π × 9 = 18π and flat face = 9π [1]. Total = **27π cm²** [1].

**6.** π × 16 × h = 500 [1], so h = 500 ÷ 16π = **9.95 cm** [1].

**7.** Cross-section area = 1/2 × 6 × 8 = 24 cm² [1]. Volume = 24 × 15 = **360 cm³** [1]. Surface area = 2 × 24 + (6 + 8 + 10) × 15 [1] = 48 + 360 = **408 cm²** [1].

**8. (a)** Volume = 1/3 × 36 × 4 = **48 cm³** [1].

**(b)** l² = 4² + 3² = 25, so **l = 5 cm** [1].

**(c)** Each triangle = 1/2 × 6 × 5 = 15 cm² [1]. Surface area = 36 + 4 × 15 = **96 cm²** [1].

**9.** 4/3 × π × r³ = 288π [1], so r³ = 216 and **r = 6 cm** [1].

**10.** π × r × 15 = 75π [1], so **r = 5 cm** [1].

**11.** Volume of the sphere = 4/3 × π × 27 = 36π cm³ [1]. π × 10² × h = 36π [1]. **h = 0.36 cm** [1].

**12.** Volume of the cylinder = π × 4 × 18 = 72π [1]. Volume of one sphere = 4/3 × π × 27 = 36π [1]. 72π ÷ 36π = **2 spheres** [1].

**13. (a)** Radius 4 cm. Label area = π × 8 × 11 = 88π = **276 cm²** [1][1]. **(b)** Volume = π × 16 × 11 = 552.9 cm³, so capacity = **553 ml** [1].

**14.** Cross-section area = 120/360 × π × 81 = 27π [1]. Volume = 27π × 20 = 540π [1] = **1700 cm³** [1].

**15.** Cone volume = 1/3 × π × 16 × 9 = 48π and cylinder volume = π × 16 × 9 = 144π [1]. The ratio is **1 : 3** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the slant height in a volume formula.
- Leaving out the circular ends of a cylinder, or the base of a cone, in a total surface area.
- Mixing up the volume formulas of a cone and a cylinder.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Rounding in the middle of the working, or giving a number of spheres that is not a whole number.
