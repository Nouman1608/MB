Topic 4 of Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) is about three ways human activity changes
the atmosphere: climate change, acid rain and ozone depletion. For each one you need the same chain -
**cause → process → impacts → strategies → benefits and limitations** - plus some basic facts about the
atmosphere itself. Learning the chain once and reusing it for each problem is the fastest way to revise.

## 4.1 Atmosphere quick facts

- Layers, lowest first: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere.
- Ozone layer: lower stratosphere.
- Clean air: 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen; carbon dioxide, argon and water vapour make up the rest.
- Climate: weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years.
- Natural greenhouse effect: solar radiation passes through the atmosphere → some is absorbed by land and
  oceans → some is reflected to space → some is absorbed and re-emitted to the surface by greenhouse gases
  and clouds → the Earth stays warm enough for life.

## 4.2 Climate change chain

**Cause.** Greenhouse gas = absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal energy. Named gases: CO2, H2O,
CH4.

| Source | Greenhouse gas |
|---|---|
| Fossil fuel combustion | CO2 |
| Cattle digestion, manure, rice fields | CH4 |
| Deforestation (less photosynthesis) | CO2 stays in the air |
| Draining wetlands | CH4 |
| Cement: limestone heated to lime | CO2 |
| Population growth and energy use | CO2 |

**Process.** More greenhouse gases absorb more radiation → **enhanced greenhouse effect** → **global warming**
→ **climate change**.

**Impacts - remember three groups.**
- *Physical*: hotter land and ocean surface; melting ice sheets, glaciers and permafrost; sea-level rise;
  ocean acidification; more extreme weather (floods, loss of land, drought, wildfires).
- *Living things*: disrupted food chains; biodiversity change; habitat loss; more pests and invasive
  species; forced migration of animals.
- *People*: forced migration; changed crop yields; food shortages.

**Strategies to reduce carbon footprints - remember three groups.**
- *Energy and transport*: less fossil fuel; energy efficiency; transport policies; carbon sequestration
  (capture, remove, store CO2).
- *Food and land*: plant-based, seasonal and local food; less livestock; reforestation and afforestation;
  fewer children per woman.
- *Government and science*: international agreements and policies; taxation; research and climate models;
  artificial intelligence to predict impacts and develop strategies.

**Adaptation** = altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to climate change impacts:
resistant crops; flood defences; laws on building near coasts and floodplains; building design and
materials.

**Benefits vs limitations (4.2.9) - a general pattern you can apply**
- Global strategies (agreements, taxation) can cut emissions at scale but are hard to agree and enforce.
- Individual strategies (diet, energy saving) are cheap and immediate but small unless many people act.
- Technology (sequestration, AI, models) can be powerful but is costly and depends on data and
  investment.
- Adaptation protects people now but does not reduce the cause.

## 4.3 Acid rain chain

- **Cause**: SO2 from burning sulfur-containing fossil fuels and from volcanoes; oxides of nitrogen from
  nitrogen and oxygen reacting in hot vehicle engines.
- **Process**: these gases react with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere → acid rain.
- **Impacts**: acid lakes and rivers; fewer fish; damaged aquatic food webs; acid soils; defoliation and
  lower crop yields; damaged buildings.
- **Strategies**: flue-gas desulfurisation (SO2 reacts with calcium carbonate or calcium oxide); catalytic
  converters (remove NOx); transport policies.
- **Limitations**: desulfurisation is expensive and needs limestone; converters do nothing about SO2 from
  power stations; acid rain can fall in a different country from the source, so cooperation is needed.

## 4.4 Ozone depletion chain

- **Cause**: CFCs from aerosols and refrigerants destroy ozone in the ozone layer.
- **Impacts**: more solar radiation at the surface; more cancer and cataracts; damaged vegetation and lower
  crop yields.
- **Strategies**: international agreement and policies (Montreal Protocol, 1987); CFC ban and alternatives;
  safe disposal of CFCs.
- **Benefit**: global action reduced CFC production sharply. **Limitation**: CFCs remain in the atmosphere
  for decades, and old equipment still contains them.

## Worked example: comparing two strategies

A government must choose between a carbon tax and a tree-planting scheme.

| Criterion | Carbon tax | Afforestation |
|---|---|---|
| Speed of effect | Fast - prices change behaviour at once | Slow - trees take years to grow |
| Cost to government | Raises money | Costs money and land |
| Fairness | Can hit low-income households | Can create rural jobs |
| Extra benefits | Funds renewable energy | Habitats, soil protection, flood control |

Judgement: the tax works faster; afforestation gives wider environmental benefits. Using both is strongest.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Saying "the greenhouse effect is bad": the natural effect supports life; the problem is the enhanced
  effect.
- Linking CFCs to global warming in answers about ozone depletion.
- Saying acid rain comes from carbon dioxide: the syllabus names sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen.
- Treating adaptation as a way to reduce emissions.
- Ignoring the command word: "describe" for strategies, "discuss" for benefits and limitations.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the three greenhouse gases named in 5014?** Carbon dioxide, water vapour and methane.

**How does cement manufacture cause climate change?** Limestone (calcium carbonate) is heated to make lime
(calcium oxide), which releases carbon dioxide.

**Why is the Montreal Protocol seen as a success?** Countries agreed to phase out CFCs and alternatives
were available, so production fell sharply, although the ozone layer takes decades to recover.
