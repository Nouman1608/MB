## Questions

**1.** A force of 25 N acts at the end A of a horizontal rod OA, where OA = 0.4 m. The force makes an angle of 50° with the rod.

**(a)** Find the moment of the force about O. **[2]**
**(b)** How far is the line of action of the force from O? **[1]**

**2.** A rectangular lamina PQRS has PQ = 1.2 m horizontal and QR = 0.7 m, with P bottom left, Q bottom right, R top right and S top left. These forces act in its plane: 8 N vertically downwards at R; 6 N from R towards S; 7 N vertically upwards at Q; 20 N along RP, from R towards P. Find the total moment about P, giving its sense. **[4]**

**3.** A light rod AB has length 1.5 m. It rests horizontally on a pivot at C, where AC = 0.5 m. Particles of mass 5 kg and 2 kg are attached at A and at D, where AD = 0.9 m. A particle of mass m kg hangs from B, and the rod stays horizontal.

**(a)** Find m. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the force exerted by the pivot. **[1]**

**4.** A uniform beam AB has length 4.5 m and mass 30 kg. It is supported horizontally at C and D, with AC = 0.5 m and DB = 0.5 m. A person of mass 70 kg, modelled as a particle, stands 2 m from A. Find the reactions at C and D. **[5]**

**5.** A uniform beam AB has length 6 m and is supported horizontally at C and D, with AC = 1.0 m and DB = 1.2 m. When a particle of mass 12 kg is placed at B, the beam is about to tilt.

**(a)** Find the mass of the beam. **[3]**
**(b)** The particle is removed. Find the greatest mass of a particle that can be placed at A without the beam tilting. **[3]**

**6.** A non-uniform rod AB has length 3 m and mass M kg. Its centre of mass G is x m from A. With a particle of mass 1.5 kg attached at A, the rod rests horizontally on a pivot 1.2 m from A. With a particle of mass 3 kg attached at A instead, it rests horizontally on a pivot 1.0 m from A. Find M and x, assuming that G is further from A than each pivot. **[6]**

**7.** A uniform rod AB has length 2.4 m and mass 9 kg. It is hinged at A to a vertical wall and rests in equilibrium with B above A, the rod making 35° with the horizontal. A light horizontal string joins B to the wall.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the force exerted on the rod by the hinge, giving its magnitude and its angle to the horizontal. **[4]**

**8.** A uniform ladder of length 4.8 m and mass 15 kg rests with its foot on rough horizontal ground and its top against a smooth vertical wall. The ladder makes 65° with the horizontal.

**(a)** Show that the ladder cannot rest in equilibrium unless the coefficient of friction is at least 0.233. **[4]**
**(b)** The coefficient of friction is 0.4. A person of mass 60 kg climbs the ladder. Find how far along the ladder from its foot the person can climb before it starts to slip. **[4]**

**9.** A uniform rectangular lamina of mass 3 kg and length 0.9 m rests horizontally on a table, long sides perpendicular to the edge, with 0.3 m overhanging. A particle of mass m kg is placed at the end of the overhang.

**(a)** Find the greatest value of m for which the lamina does not tilt. **[3]**
**(b)** For this value of m, state the magnitude of the force exerted by the table on the lamina. **[1]**

**10.** A uniform plank AB has length 8 m and mass 30 kg. It hangs horizontally from vertical ropes at C and D, where AC = DB = 1 m. A person of mass 60 kg, modelled as a particle, stands x m from A (0 ≤ x ≤ 8). Each rope can bear a tension of at most 735 N.

**(a)** Show that the tensions in the ropes at C and D are 9.8(85 − 10x) N and 9.8(5 + 10x) N. **[5]**
**(b)** Find the set of values of x for which neither rope breaks. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Moment = force × perpendicular distance = 25 × 0.4 × sin 50° [1] = **7.66 N m** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** d = 0.4 sin 50° = **0.306 m** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing the product before evaluating it makes your method easy to credit. Part (a) needs the unit N m.

**2.** Take anticlockwise as positive and moments about P.
8 N down at R: perpendicular distance 1.2 m, clockwise, −9.6 [1]
6 N from R towards S: its line of action is 0.7 m above P, and it acts leftwards, so anticlockwise, +4.2 [1]
7 N up at Q: perpendicular distance 1.2 m, anticlockwise, +8.4 [1]
20 N along RP: the line of action passes through P, so the moment is zero.
Total = −9.6 + 4.2 + 8.4 = **3.0 N m anticlockwise** [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the sense of the resultant as well as its size, and say why the 20 N force contributes nothing.

**3. (a)** Moments about C, with D 0.4 m and B 1.0 m on the other side.
Clockwise: 2g × 0.4 + mg × 1.0 [1]
Anticlockwise: 5g × 0.5 [1]
2.5 = 0.8 + m, so **m = 1.7** [1]
**(b)** The rod is light, so the pivot force is the total weight: (5 + 2 + 1.7) × 9.8 = **85.3 N** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A light rod has no weight, so list the forces first and check you have not added one.

**4.** Distances from C (0.5 m from A): centre of beam 1.75 m, person 1.5 m, D 3.5 m. [1]
Moments about C: R_D × 3.5 = 30g × 1.75 + 70g × 1.5 [1]
R_D = 157.5g ÷ 3.5 = 45g = **441 N** [1]
Vertical: R_C + R_D = 100g = 980 [1]
R_C = 980 − 441 = **539 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Moments about one support avoid simultaneous equations, and a check about the other support catches a wrong distance.

**5. (a)** Tilting about D, so R_C = 0. The centre is 1.8 m from D (D is 4.8 m from A, the centre 3 m). [1]
Moments about D: 12g × 1.2 = Mg × 1.8 [1]
**M = 8 kg** [1]
**(b)** The beam tilts about C when the particle is at A. The centre of the beam is 2.0 m from C and A is 1.0 m from C. [1]
Moments about C: mg × 1.0 = 8g × 2.0 [1]
**m = 16 kg** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say which support the beam tilts about, and that the other reaction is zero, before taking moments.

**6.** Moments about the first pivot: 1.5g × 1.2 = Mg(x − 1.2), so 1.8 = M(x − 1.2) [2]
Moments about the second pivot: 3g × 1.0 = Mg(x − 1.0), so 3 = M(x − 1) [2]
Subtracting gives 1.2 = 0.2M, so **M = 6** [1]
Then 3 = 6(x − 1), so **x = 1.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Label which pivot each of your two equations uses.

**7. (a)** Moments about A; B is 2.4 sin 35° above A. [1]
T × 2.4 sin 35° = 9g × 1.2 cos 35° [1]
T = 4.5g ÷ tan 35° = **63.0 N** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** Horizontal: H = T = 62.98 N, directed away from the wall. [1]
Vertical: V = 9g = 88.2 N, upwards. [1]
Magnitude = √(62.98² + 88.2²) = **108 N** (3 s.f.) [1]
Direction = tan⁻¹(88.2 ÷ 62.98) = **54.5° above the horizontal**, pointing away from the wall. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take moments about the hinge so its unknown force drops out, and describe the direction in words as well as the angle.

**8. (a)** Resolve vertically: R = 15g. [1]
Moments about the foot: S × 4.8 sin 65° = 15g × 2.4 cos 65° [1]
Resolve horizontally: F = S = 7.5g ÷ tan 65° [1]
F ≤ μR gives μ ≥ 0.5 ÷ tan 65° = 0.2331..., so μ ≥ **0.233** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** R = 75g, so greatest friction = 0.4 × 75g = 30g, and S = 30g at slipping. [1]
Person d m up the ladder; moments about the foot: 30g × 4.8 sin 65° = 15g × 2.4 cos 65° + 60g × d cos 65° [1]
144 tan 65° = 36 + 60d [1]
**d = 4.55 m** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that", include every step from the equilibrium equations to the inequality, and state the unrounded value before the given one.

**9. (a)** Tilting about the table edge. The centre is 0.45 m from the free end, so 0.15 m inside the edge. [1]
Moments about the edge: mg × 0.3 = 3g × 0.15 [1]
**m = 1.5 kg** [1]
**(b)** The table supports all the weight: (3 + 1.5) × 9.8 = **44.1 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Identify the table edge as the pivot before writing any distances.

**10. (a)** Moments about D. Distances from D: centre of plank 3 m, C 6 m, person 7 − x. [1]
T_C × 6 = 30g × 3 + 60g(7 − x) [1]
T_C = g(90 + 420 − 60x) ÷ 6 = **9.8(85 − 10x) N** [1]
Vertical: T_C + T_D = 90g [1]
T_D = 90g − g(85 − 10x) = **9.8(5 + 10x) N** [1]
**(b)** T_C ≤ 735 = 75g gives 85 − 10x ≤ 75, so x ≥ 1. [1]
T_D ≤ 75g gives 5 + 10x ≤ 75, so x ≤ 7. [1]
Both tensions are positive here, so **1 ≤ x ≤ 7**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A range needs both ends: find each rope's condition separately, then combine them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring along the rod when the force is angled.
- Leaving out the weight of the beam or rod, or putting it at an end instead of the midpoint.
- Taking moments about the wrong support in a tilting problem.
- Giving a hinge force as one component, or a magnitude without a direction.
- Using F = μR when the ladder is not about to slip.
- Rounding part-way through and losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Omitting the unit N m or the sense of a resultant moment.
