**Key question 1.4, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "Why was there a civil war in the United States and what were its
results?" The content runs from 1820 to 1877, and the topic is prescribed for **Paper 2 in November 2025 and June
2026**. These notes concentrate on the political breakdown, the war's turning points and Reconstruction; for the
sectional differences and the crises of the 1850s, also use the
[US Civil War revision notes](/resources/cambridge-world-history-us-civil-war-revision-notes/). Explanations are in the
[0470 Civil War study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-us-civil-war/), and questions in the
[0470 Civil War practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-us-civil-war-practice/).

## Why compromise broke down after 1850

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Compromises created new grievances | Fugitive Slave Act (1850) angered Northerners |
| National parties collapsed | Whigs disappeared in the 1850s; Republicans (1854) were a Northern party; Democrats split at Charleston, April 1860 |
| The courts closed the middle ground | Dred Scott (1857): Congress could not ban slavery in the territories |
| Leaders lost cross-sectional trust | Douglas's Freeport argument (1858) cost him Southern support |
| Last deal rejected | Crittenden Compromise (December 1860) failed; Lincoln opposed slavery's extension |

## Lincoln's election: significance in four points

1. **Sectional victory:** no Southern electoral votes; not on the ballot in most of the South.
2. **Known views:** "House Divided" speech (1858); opposed the spread of slavery.
3. **Immediate secession:** South Carolina, 20 December 1860; Confederacy formed February 1861, Jefferson Davis
   president, before Lincoln's inauguration (4 March 1861).
4. **His response to Fort Sumter** (12 April 1861): call for 75,000 volunteers; Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee and
   Arkansas seceded.

**Trigger versus cause:** the election was the trigger; slavery's expansion was the underlying cause.

## Turning points of the war

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| July 1861 | First Bull Run | No short war |
| September 1862 | Antietam | Lee's invasion halted; preliminary emancipation announced |
| 1 January 1863 | Emancipation Proclamation | Freedom in rebel areas; Black enlistment |
| July 1863 | Gettysburg; Vicksburg | South on the defensive; Confederacy split along the Mississippi |
| November 1863 | Gettysburg Address | War for "a new birth of freedom" |
| September 1864 | Atlanta taken | Lincoln re-elected in November |
| November-December 1864 | Sherman's March to the Sea | Destruction of Southern resources |
| 9 April 1865 | Appomattox | Lee surrenders |
| 14 April 1865 | Lincoln shot by John Wilkes Booth | Dies next day; Andrew Johnson president |

## Northern strengths and Southern weaknesses

| North | South |
|---|---|
| Industry, railways, food supply | Few factories; railways broke down |
| Blockade (Anaconda Plan) | Cotton diplomacy failed: Britain used stocks and other sources |
| Around 180,000 Black soldiers in the army | Enlisted enslaved men only in March 1865 |
| Grant and Sherman used superior numbers | Lee won battles but could not replace losses |
| Lincoln's political skill | Davis clashed with state governors (Joseph Brown) |
| Stable finance | Inflation; Richmond bread riots (1863); "rich man's war" |

## Reconstruction's three phases

| Phase | Key features |
|---|---|
| Lincoln, 1863-65 | Ten Per Cent Plan: a state could return when 10% of voters swore loyalty |
| Johnson, 1865-67 | Pardons for Confederates; Black Codes; vetoes of the Freedmen's Bureau extension and Civil Rights Act |
| Radical (Congress), 1867-77 | Reconstruction Acts (1867): five military districts; Black male suffrage; 14th and 15th Amendments; Johnson impeached (1868) and acquitted by one vote |
| End | Disputed election of 1876; Compromise of 1877; troops withdrawn |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Secession | A state leaving the Union |
| Confederacy | The Confederate States of America, 1861-65 |
| Border states | Slave states that stayed in the Union: Delaware, Maryland, Kentucky, Missouri |
| Anaconda Plan | Northern strategy of blockade and control of the Mississippi |
| Emancipation Proclamation | Lincoln's order freeing enslaved people in rebel-held areas |
| Black Codes | Southern laws of 1865-66 restricting freedpeople's rights |
| Freedmen's Bureau | Federal agency helping formerly enslaved people with food, schools and contracts |
| Radical Republicans | Republicans, such as Thaddeus Stevens, who wanted full rights for freedpeople |
| Sharecropping | Farming land for a share of the crop, often trapping families in debt |
| Compromise of 1877 | Deal ending Reconstruction: Hayes became president and troops left the South |

## Exam traps

- **The Proclamation did not end slavery everywhere.** The 13th Amendment did (ratified December 1865).
- **Lincoln was not elected to abolish slavery.** He promised not to touch it where it existed.
- **Eleven states seceded, not all slave states.** Four border states stayed.
- **Reconstruction is part of the question.** "Results" questions expect 1865-77.
- **Use a criterion for "success".** Legal change and everyday life give different answers.

## Self-test

1. Why did the Fugitive Slave Act increase tension? *It forced Northerners to help return people who had escaped
   slavery.*
2. What happened to the Democratic Party in 1860? *It split into Northern and Southern wings.*
3. What did the Crittenden Compromise propose? *Extending the 36°30′ line to the Pacific.*
4. Which state seceded first, and when? *South Carolina, 20 December 1860.*
5. Who was president of the Confederacy? *Jefferson Davis.*
6. Why was Vicksburg important? *Its fall gave the Union control of the Mississippi and split the Confederacy.*
7. Why did the capture of Atlanta matter politically? *It helped Lincoln win re-election in November 1864.*
8. Give one reason "King Cotton" diplomacy failed. *Britain had large cotton stocks and found other sources in India and
   Egypt.*
9. What were the Black Codes? *Laws passed by Southern states in 1865-66 restricting freedpeople's rights.*
10. What did the Reconstruction Acts of 1867 do? *Divided the South into military districts and required Black male
    suffrage for readmission.*
11. What did the 15th Amendment (1870) do? *Stopped states denying the vote on grounds of race.*
12. How did Reconstruction end? *Through the Compromise of 1877, when federal troops were withdrawn after the disputed
    election of 1876.*
