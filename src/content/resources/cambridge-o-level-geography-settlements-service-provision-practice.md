## Section A

**1.** Describe a nucleated settlement pattern and give **one** reason why it may develop. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term function of a settlement. **[1]**

**3.** A village was built on a small hill surrounded by marshland beside a river. Identify **two**
site factors that may have attracted the first settlers. **[2]**

**4.** State the difference between a low-order and a high-order service, with an example of each.
**[3]**

**5.** Define sphere of influence. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the settlement hierarchy in one region.

| Level | Number of settlements | Average population | Average number of services |
|---|---|---|---|
| City | 1 | 450,000 | 320 |
| Town | 6 | 25,000 | 85 |
| Village | 35 | 1,200 | 9 |
| Hamlet | 140 | 60 | 1 |

**(a)** Calculate the total population of the region. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the region's population living in the city. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the relationship between the size of settlements and the number of settlements and
services. **[3]**
**(d)** Using the terms threshold population and range, explain why the city has many more services
than the villages. **[4]**

**7.** Two shops in a town each surveyed ten customers about the distance they had travelled
(km).

| Shop | Distances travelled (km) |
|---|---|
| Corner shop | 0.5, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 1.5, 2, 3 |
| Electrical goods store | 5, 12, 30, 8, 45, 20, 15, 10, 25, 30 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean distance travelled to each shop. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two results. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** ways the survey could be improved to give more reliable results. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why some settlements grow into large towns while others remain small villages.
**[5]**

**9.** For a named area you have studied, describe the provision of services in settlements of
different sizes and explain how service provision has changed. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Buildings are **clustered closely together** around a central point such as a crossroads,
well or market [1]. Reason, any one: a shared water supply; defence; to share farmland or labour;
limited flat land; a central meeting place such as a place of worship [1].

**2.** The main economic or social purpose of a settlement, such as a port, market town or resort
[1].

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: a **dry point** site above the marsh, safe from flooding; **defence**,
since the marsh and river make it difficult to attack; **water supply** from the river (wet point);
a possible river crossing point; fishing or reeds from the marsh.

**4.** A low-order service is **used frequently, sells cheap everyday goods and has a low threshold
and short range** [1], for example a bakery or newsagent [1]. A high-order service is **used rarely,
is expensive and has a high threshold and long range**, for example a jewellery shop or specialist
hospital [1].

**5.** The area served by a settlement or service, from which its customers come [1].

**6. (a)** 450,000 + 150,000 + 42,000 + 8,400 = **650,400** [1].
**(b)** 450,000 ÷ 650,400 × 100 = **69%** [1].
**(c)** Up to 3 marks: as settlements get **larger there are fewer of them** (140 hamlets but one
city) [1]; **larger settlements have more services** (1 in a hamlet, 320 in the city) [1]; the
relationship is not proportional, for example the city has about 18 times the population of a town
but under 4 times the services [1]. Credit use of figures.
**(d)** Up to 4 marks: **threshold population** is the minimum number of people needed to support
a service [1]; the city has 450,000 people, enough to meet the high thresholds of high-order
services such as department stores or specialist hospitals, while a village of 1,200 cannot [1];
**range** is how far people will travel to a service [1]; people from the whole region travel to the
city for high-order services because they are used rarely and are worth the journey, so the city's
sphere of influence includes the villages' populations too [1].

**7. (a)** Corner shop: 20 ÷ 10 = **2 km** [1]. Electrical goods store: 200 ÷ 10 = **20 km** [1].
**(b)** The corner shop sells **low-order** convenience goods bought often, so people use the
nearest one and travel short distances (a short range) [1]. The electrical store sells
**high-order** goods bought rarely and expensive enough to compare, so people travel further (a
long range and larger sphere of influence) [1].
**(c)** Any two, 1 mark each: survey **more customers**; survey on **different days and times**,
including weekdays and weekends; survey at more than one shop of each type; use a sampling method
such as systematic sampling (every fifth customer) to reduce bias; record customers' home
locations to map desire lines.

**8.** Up to 5 marks for developed points:
- Growth depends on **situation** more than site [1]: a settlement at a **route centre** or
  bridging point is accessible from a wide area, so it attracts trade and becomes a market centre
  [1].
- A **natural harbour** or coastal location may allow growth as a port [1].
- Nearby **resources** such as minerals or energy attract industry and workers [1].
- Being chosen as an **administrative centre** or capital brings jobs and investment, as with
  Islamabad [1].
- Once a settlement grows, it reaches the **threshold** for more services, which attracts more people
  (a cumulative effect) [1].
- Small villages may be **isolated**, have limited flat land or water, or lose people to larger
  places [1].

**9.** Mark in levels.
- **Level 1 (1–3):** general points about services and settlement size, with no named area.
- **Level 2 (4–5):** named area with settlements at more than one level and some specific
  services, plus a simple statement of change.
- **Level 3 (6–7):** detailed, named settlements at several levels, specific services at each, and a
  developed explanation of change with causes.

Indicative content: name the area and settlements at two or three levels of its hierarchy;
describe the services in each (for example a village with a general store, mosque and primary
school; a town with a secondary school, banks, a market and a hospital; a city with specialist
hospitals, universities and department stores). Changes: closure of village shops, banks or post
offices because of car ownership, online services and population decline or ageing; growth of
out-of-town retail; responses such as mobile clinics, multi-purpose shops and community shops; or,
in fast-growing areas, new schools and markets opening to meet demand.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "threshold" and "range" without explaining what they mean in context.
- Describing a table with no figures.
- Case studies with no named settlements.
