> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **18.1 Variation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (gene mutation and the sources of genetic variation); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [variation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-variation/) and the [variation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-variation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which feature shows discontinuous variation?

A: the body mass of adult goats

B: the length of leaves on an oak tree

C: the ABO blood group of students in a school

D: the body length of trout in a river

**[1]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by variation. **[1]**

**3.** State the type of variation shown by each feature.

**(a)** the wing length of houseflies **[1]**

**(b)** the seed colour of peas (yellow or green). **[1]**

**4.** Which process is the way in which new alleles are formed?

A: fertilisation

B: meiosis

C: mutation

D: random mating

**[1]**

**5.** Radiographers who take X-ray images of patients stand behind a protective screen. Suggest why, with reference to mutation. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "The bacteria mutated so that they could survive the antibiotic." Use the definition of a gene mutation to explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student measured the length of 48 runner bean pods picked from plants in one garden. The table shows the results.

| Pod length / cm | 10.0–11.9 | 12.0–13.9 | 14.0–15.9 | 16.0–17.9 | 18.0–19.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of pods | 3 | 9 | 18 | 12 | 6 |

**(a)** State the type of variation shown by pod length. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of pods in the modal class. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how the student should present these data as a graph. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest **two** environmental factors that could cause the pods to differ in length. **[2]**

**8.** Two identical twins were separated as babies and brought up in different homes. As adults, both twins have blood group A, but one twin has a body mass 9 kg greater than the other.

**(a)** Explain why both twins have the same blood group. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the twins have different body masses. **[2]**

**9.** A packet of dried peas contains a mixture of yellow and green seeds. Describe how a student could investigate the variation in seed colour in this packet and present the results. **[4]**

**10.** A group of fruit flies was exposed to X-rays. A second group, kept under the same conditions, was not. More of the offspring of the exposed group had curled wings instead of straight wings.

**(a)** Suggest an explanation for this result. **[2]**

**(b)** State the type of variation shown by wing shape in these flies. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the second group of flies was needed. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Two parent plants of the same species are crossed and produce many seeds. The seedlings that grow are genetically different from each other.

**(a)** Explain how meiosis and random fertilisation lead to this genetic variation. **[3]**

**(b)** A population of bacteria reproduces only asexually. Explain how genetic variation can still arise in this population. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A gene codes for an enzyme. A gene mutation occurs in this gene in one cell.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a gene mutation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how this mutation could stop the enzyme from working. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A, B and D can all take any value within a range, so they are continuous. Blood group has four categories with no intermediates.

**2.** Differences between individuals of the same species [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Differences between organisms" is not enough; the mark depends on "of the same species".

**3. (a)** Continuous [1].

**(b)** Discontinuous [1].

**4.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Meiosis, fertilisation and random mating produce new combinations of alleles that already exist; only mutation forms new ones.

**5.** X-rays are ionising radiation [1], which increases the rate of mutation / could cause mutations in the radiographer's cells; they are exposed many times each day [1].

*Examiner insight:* The link between ionising radiation and an increased rate of mutation is the key idea. "X-rays are dangerous" alone earns nothing.

**6.** A gene mutation is a random change in the base sequence of DNA [1]; the mutation was not caused by the antibiotic / did not happen in order to help the bacteria survive; it was already present before the antibiotic was used [1].

*Examiner insight:* The word "random" is the heart of this answer. Mutations are not directed towards a need.

**7. (a)** Continuous [1]; pod length can take any value in a range between two extremes [1].

**(b)** 18 ÷ 48 × 100 [1] = 37.5% [1].

**(c)** A histogram [1] with pod length on the x-axis and number of pods on the y-axis, with bars touching / no gaps between bars [1].

**(d)** Any two from: amount of light, amount of water, temperature, mineral ions / fertiliser in the soil, damage by pests [2].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), the modal class is 14.0–15.9 cm. Using the total of the table, 48, as the denominator is essential; dividing by the number of classes is a common slip.

**8. (a)** Identical twins have the same genes / alleles [1]; blood group is discontinuous variation caused by genes only, so the environment cannot change it [1].

**(b)** Body mass is affected by the environment as well as genes [1]; the twins may have had different diets / different amounts of exercise [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs a named environmental factor. Because the twins' genes are identical, a genetic explanation for the difference would not score.

**9.** Any four from:

- take a large sample / count all the peas in the packet [1]
- sort the peas into two groups, yellow and green [1]
- count the number in each group / record in a table [1]
- draw a bar chart [1] with separate bars for yellow and green [1]
- calculate the percentage of each colour [1]

*Examiner insight:* This is discontinuous variation, so there are no class intervals and no histogram. Answers that describe measuring each pea earn little.

**10. (a)** X-rays are ionising radiation and increase the rate of mutation [1]; a mutation produced a new allele for curled wings that was passed to the offspring [1].

**(b)** Discontinuous [1]; there are only two phenotypes, curled and straight, with no intermediates [1].

**(c)** As a control / to show that the curled wings were caused by the X-rays and not by some other factor [1].

**11. (a)** Meiosis produces gametes that are genetically different from each other [1]. Any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete (random fertilisation) [1], so each zygote / seed has a different combination of alleles [1].

**(b)** Mutation [1] forms new alleles in some bacteria, which are then passed to their offspring when they divide [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), the third mark is for the outcome: a new combination of alleles in each offspring. In (b), meiosis and fertilisation do not happen in asexual reproduction, so they cannot be the answer.

**12. (a)** A random change [1] in the base sequence of DNA [1].

**(b)** The change in base sequence may change the sequence of amino acids in the enzyme [1]; this changes the shape of the enzyme / active site [1], so the substrate no longer fits / is no longer complementary [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is a chain of three linked steps: bases, then amino acids, then shape of the active site. Missing the middle step loses a mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining variation without "of the same species".
- Choosing a histogram for categories such as seed colour.
- Writing that radiation "causes" mutation instead of increasing its rate.
- Explaining a difference between identical twins using genes.
- Leaving out the amino acid step when linking a mutation to protein shape.
