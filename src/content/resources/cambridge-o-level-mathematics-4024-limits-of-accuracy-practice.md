These questions cover subtopic 1.10 Limits of accuracy of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. Questions 1 to 9 can be done without a calculator. Questions 10 to 14 need a calculator. Show the bounds you use.

## Section A

**1.** A length is 7 m, correct to the nearest metre. Write down the lower bound and the upper bound of the length. **[2]**

**2.** A mass is 4.3 kg, correct to 1 decimal place. Write down the error interval for the mass, using inequalities. **[2]**

**3.** The number 3200 is correct to 2 significant figures. Write down the lower bound and the upper bound. **[2]**

**4.** A student says that a length given as 12.5 cm to 1 decimal place could be 12.55 cm. Explain whether she is correct. **[2]**

**5.** A rectangle measures 12 cm by 8 cm, each correct to the nearest centimetre. Find the upper bound of the perimeter. **[2]**

**6.** For the same rectangle, find the lower bound of the area. **[2]**

**7.** Each of three parcels has mass 2.4 kg correct to 1 decimal place. Find the lower bound of the total mass of the three parcels. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** a = 9.6 and b = 3.9, both correct to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Find the upper bound of a - b. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the lower bound of a - b. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answers to give the value of a - b correct to 1 significant figure, and explain your reasoning. **[2]**

**9.** An equilateral triangle has sides of length 6.4 cm, correct to the nearest 0.1 cm. Write down the lower bound and the upper bound of its perimeter. **[2]**

**10.** *Calculator.* A cyclist travels 45 km, correct to the nearest kilometre, in 3.8 hours, correct to 1 decimal place. Calculate the lower bound of the average speed. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**11.** *Calculator.* For the same journey, calculate the upper bound of the average speed, correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** *Calculator.* p = 18.4 and q = 2.3, both correct to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Calculate the upper bound of p ÷ q. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the lower bound of p ÷ q, correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the value of p ÷ q to a suitable degree of accuracy. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**13.** *Calculator.* A cube has side 5.0 cm, correct to 1 decimal place. Find the lower bound and the upper bound of the volume. Give each answer correct to 4 significant figures. **[3]**

**14.** *Calculator.* A runner completes 100 m, correct to the nearest metre, in 11.2 seconds, correct to 1 decimal place. Calculate the upper bound of the speed in m/s, correct to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Lower bound **6.5 m** **[1]**; upper bound **7.5 m** **[1]** **[2]**

**2.** lower bound 4.25 **[1]**; upper bound, giving **4.25 ⩽ m < 4.35** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The half unit for 1 decimal place is 0.05, not 0.1.

**3.** The last significant digit is in the hundreds place, so the half unit is 50 **[1]**; lower **3150** and upper **3250** **[1]** **[2]**

**4.** The interval is 12.45 ⩽ L < 12.55 **[1]**; 12.55 is the upper bound and is not included, so she is **not correct** **[1]** **[2]**

**5.** Upper bounds 12.5 and 8.5 **[1]**; 2(12.5 + 8.5) = **42 cm** **[1]** **[2]**

**6.** Lower bounds 11.5 and 7.5 **[1]**; 11.5 × 7.5 = **86.25 cm²** **[1]** **[2]**

**7.** Lower bound of one parcel is 2.35 **[1]**; 3 × 2.35 = **7.05 kg** **[1]** **[2]**

**8. (a)** upper a = 9.65 and lower b = 3.85 **[1]**; 9.65 - 3.85 = **5.8** **[1]**

**(b)** lower a = 9.55 and upper b = 3.95 **[1]**; 9.55 - 3.95 = **5.6** **[1]**

**(c)** Both 5.8 and 5.6 round to 6 to 1 significant figure **[1]**; so a - b = **6** to 1 significant figure **[1]** **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* For a subtraction, the upper bound pairs the biggest first number with the smallest second number.

**9.** Side bounds 6.35 and 6.45 **[1]**; perimeter bounds 3 × 6.35 = **19.05 cm** and 3 × 6.45 = **19.35 cm** **[1]** **[2]**

**10.** Lower distance 44.5 and upper time 3.85 **[1]**; 44.5 ÷ 3.85 = 11.558... **[1]**; **11.6 km/h** **[1]** **[3]**

**11.** Upper distance 45.5 and lower time 3.75 **[1]**; 45.5 ÷ 3.75 = 12.133..., so **12.1 km/h** **[1]** **[2]**

**12. (a)** 18.45 ÷ 2.25 **[1]** = **8.2** **[1]**

**(b)** 18.35 ÷ 2.35 **[1]** = 7.8085..., so **7.81** **[1]**

**(c)** Both bounds round to 8 to 1 significant figure **[1]**, but to 2 significant figures they are 7.8 and 8.2, which differ; so the value is **8** to 1 significant figure **[1]** **[6]**

**13.** Lower side 4.95: 4.95³ = 121.287... **[1]**; upper side 5.05: 5.05³ = 128.787... **[1]**; **121.3 cm³** and **128.8 cm³** **[1]** **[3]**

**14.** Upper distance 100.5 and lower time 11.15 **[1]**; 100.5 ÷ 11.15 = 9.0134... **[1]**; **9.01 m/s** **[1]** **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 0.1 as the half unit in Question 2.
- Using 5 or 500 instead of 50 in Question 3.
- Using the upper bound of the second value for the upper bound of a difference in Question 8.
- Using the upper time for the upper speed in Question 11.
- Rounding the bounds before dividing in Question 12.
- Writing a bound with the wrong number of significant figures in Question 13.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the upper bound part of the interval?**
No. A value exactly on the upper bound would round up. We still use it as the upper bound in calculations.

**Why is the lower bound of a speed found with the upper time?**
Speed is distance divided by time. A bigger time gives a smaller speed.
