# Key concepts: quick recall

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use.

Use with the [reviewed key and related concepts revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-key-and-related-concepts-revision-notes/). Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/).

## Facts to know cold

- Four key concepts for language acquisition (IB brief): **communication, connections, creativity, culture**.
- Key concepts frame the whole MYP curriculum. Related concepts are narrower and grounded in the subject (word choice, conventions and idiom are the brief's examples).
- Six global contexts: identities and relationships; orientation in space and time; personal and cultural expression; scientific and technical innovation; globalization and sustainability; fairness and development.
- Three question types: factual, conceptual, debatable.
- Four criteria: A Listening, B Reading, C Speaking, D Writing.
- Three proficiency levels: emergent, capable, proficient.

## One-line meanings (Marlbridge wording)

| Concept | In one line | Question to ask of a task |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Meaning passed between people | Who, why, which mode? |
| Connections | Links between languages, subjects and people | What does this link to? |
| Creativity | New use of what you know | What can I make that is mine? |
| Culture | Shared beliefs, habits and products shaping language | What does this community expect? |

## Question types with examples

- Factual: "How do you write a date in your course language?"
- Conceptual: "Why do communities have different levels of politeness?"
- Debatable: "Should slang be allowed in a job interview?"

## Exam and task traps

- Mixing up key and related concepts.
- Writing a statement of inquiry as a question.
- Confusing a topic with a concept.

## Self-test

1. List the four key concepts. 2. Is "idiom" a key concept or a related-concept example? 3. Name three of the six global contexts. 4. Give one example of a debatable question.

**Answers:** 1. Communication, connections, creativity, culture. 2. A related-concept example. 3. Any three from the list above. 4. Any open question people could argue either way, such as the slang one above.
