The IB Middle Years Programme (MYP) is designed for students aged 11 to
16, providing a framework of learning that emphasizes intellectual
challenge and connects traditional subjects to the real world. The MYP
focuses on "learning how to learn" through approaches to learning
(ATL) skills, and fosters intercultural understanding and global
engagement. Language Acquisition is one of the subject groups where
this intercultural aim is most directly realised, since learning a new
language necessarily brings a student into contact with a different
cultural framework for expression.

## What this subject covers

The ability to communicate in more than one language is central to an
international education that promotes intercultural understanding.
MYP Language Acquisition gives students the opportunity to develop
insight into the features, processes and craft of language and the
concept of culture, and to realize there are diverse ways of living,
behaving and viewing the world. Language and culture are treated as
genuinely inseparable throughout the course -- studying vocabulary and
grammar in isolation from the cultural context they operate within is
not the model MYP Language Acquisition uses.

## Aims

The course encourages and enables students to:

- gain proficiency in an additional language while supporting
  maintenance of their mother tongue and cultural heritage -- the MYP
  deliberately frames additional-language learning as complementary to,
  not a replacement for, a student's existing language and identity
- develop a respect for, and understanding of, diverse linguistic and
  cultural heritages
- develop the communication skills necessary for further language
  learning, and for study, work and leisure in a range of contexts, laying
  a foundation for continuing that language into DP Language B or ab initio.

## How it's assessed

MYP Language Acquisition is assessed against four equally weighted
criteria, each scored on an eight-point scale (1-8): Criterion A,
Listening; Criterion B, Reading; Criterion C, Speaking; and Criterion D,
Writing. Rather than being tied to a fixed year group, teaching and
learning is organized into six proficiency phases forming a developmental
continuum -- students can begin at any phase depending on prior experience,
and progress at their own pace rather than moving up with an age cohort.
This design deliberately separates "how far a student has progressed in
the language" from "how many years they have been enrolled in the MYP,"
which is a genuinely different model from most school language courses,
where progression is set by year group regardless of a student's actual
starting proficiency.

Where schools opt into MYP eAssessment, students are assessed at one of
three overall proficiency levels -- emergent, capable or proficient --
through an on-screen exam covering listening, reading and writing (each
worth 24 marks, against criteria A, B and D respectively), plus a separate,
internally assessed speaking exam against criterion C, which is externally
moderated by the IB rather than marked solely by the student's own teacher.
Which of the three eAssessment levels a student sits is agreed with their
teacher in advance, based on their working proficiency phase, rather than
being fixed by year group -- this keeps the external assessment aligned
with the same phase-based model used in ordinary classroom teaching.

The six proficiency phases sit under four key concepts that frame the
wider MYP language acquisition curriculum -- communication, connections,
creativity and culture -- explored through related concepts such as word
choice, convention and idiom. Because phases track a student's actual
language proficiency rather than their MYP year group, two students in the
same class can legitimately be working, and being assessed, in different
phases of the same continuum -- and it is entirely normal for a student
studying two different languages under MYP Language Acquisition (or one
language plus their strongest language under Language and Literature) to
sit in different phases in each.

## Prior experience and starting phase

Because placement is proficiency-based, a student's starting phase is normally determined through
an assessment or discussion early in their enrolment at a school, factoring in prior study of the
language, exposure at home, and any previous school's own placement. This matters for families
moving between IB schools, or into the MYP from a non-IB system, since the receiving school needs
enough information about a student's actual language proficiency -- not simply their age or
previous year group -- to place them in the correct phase from day one.

## Choosing a phase versus choosing a level

Because progress is tracked by proficiency phase rather than by MYP year, a student's placement in
Language Acquisition should not be assumed from their age or year group alone -- a student who
joined an IB school partway through the programme, or who has significant prior exposure to the
target language at home, may be working in a phase well ahead of (or behind) their classroom peers
in other subjects. This differs from how most other MYP subjects, and most non-IB language
qualifications, structure progression around fixed year-group cohorts, and it is worth a family
confirming directly with a school's Language Acquisition coordinator which of the six phases a
student is actually working in, since phase placement affects both classroom teaching and, where a
school uses eAssessment, which proficiency level (emergent, capable or proficient) a student is
prepared to sit.

## How this connects to further language study

The MYP's four key concepts -- communication, connections, creativity and culture -- are designed
to transfer directly into DP Language B or Language ab initio, which continue to build proficiency
through the same skills (listening, reading, speaking, writing) rather than introducing an entirely
new assessment framework. A student who has progressed well through the MYP's proficiency phases
typically enters the DP's language courses with a clear sense of their own working level, which
helps in choosing between DP Language B (for students with some existing proficiency) and Language
ab initio (for genuine beginners) at the point subject choices are made.
