## Questions

**1.** Define the term offshoring. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between outsourcing and offshoring. **[3]**

**3.** State two factors a manufacturer may consider when choosing where to locate production. **[2]**

**4.** Kelmara Textiles will make 90,000 garments a year at one site. It is comparing two sites. At site P, fixed costs are 600,000 a year, variable costs are 8.20 per garment and delivery to customers is 0.90 per garment. At site Q, fixed costs are 450,000 a year, variable costs are 7.10 per garment and delivery is 2.30 per garment.

**(a)** Calculate the total annual cost at each site. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost per garment at each site, to two decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why Kelmara might choose the site with the higher cost. **[2]**

**5.** Explain one way in which insourcing could benefit a business. **[3]**

**6.** Calderwyn Print makes 40,000 display units a year at home at a cost of 52.00 each. It is considering offshoring. The overseas plant would produce at 36.00 per unit. Shipping is 6.50 per unit, import duty is 12% of the production cost and extra stock holding adds 1.10 per unit.

**(a)** Calculate the landed cost per unit of offshore production. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual saving from offshoring. **[2]**
**(c)** The overseas currency strengthens, so the production cost rises by 15% to 41.40, with duty still 12% of production cost. Calculate the annual effect on Calderwyn compared with producing at home. **[3]**

**7.** Isenholt Components assembles parts in its own workshop. In-house fixed costs are 360,000 a year and variable costs are 5.20 per part. A subcontractor offers to assemble at 7.40 per part.

**(a)** Calculate the annual cost of each option at 140,000 parts. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the output at which the two options cost the same, to the nearest part. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why Isenholt should not decide using cost alone. **[3]**

**8.** Explain one ethical issue that may arise when a business offshores production. **[3]**

**9.** Analyse the effect of reshoring on two stakeholders of a business. **[4]**

**10.** Ridgemoor Furniture sells 20,000 desks a year. It makes them overseas at a landed cost of 49 each. Home production would cost 62 each. Overseas delivery takes 14 weeks and home production would take 3 weeks. Of the overseas desks, 9% are returned, against an expected 3% from home production, and each return costs 30 to handle. Ridgemoor is considering reshoring.

**(a)** Calculate the net extra annual cost of reshoring after allowing for return handling. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Ridgemoor should reshore. **[7]**

**11.** Oldencroft Dairy pays a contractor 0.46 per litre to bottle 2,500,000 litres of milk a year. It could bottle in-house with fixed costs of 520,000 a year and variable costs of 0.21 per litre. In a poor year, volume could fall to 1,800,000 litres. Recommend whether Oldencroft should insource bottling. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Moving production, or part of production, to another country [1], whether to the business's own plant or to an overseas contractor [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit for a definition rests on a change of country; saying only "cheaper production" describes a motive, not the term.

**2.** Outsourcing means paying an outside firm to do the work [1]. Offshoring means doing the work in another country [1]. They can occur separately: a firm can use a local contractor (outsourcing, not offshoring) or open its own overseas plant (offshoring, not outsourcing) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both terms addressed. Defining only one, or writing that they are "the same but abroad", leaves the contrast incomplete.

**3.** Any two from: labor costs and skills; cost of land or rent; closeness to customers; closeness to suppliers; transport links; tax and government incentives; political stability. First valid factor [1]; second valid factor [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct factors are needed. Answers that give "labor cost" and "wages" are one factor stated twice.

**4. (a)** Site P: 90,000 x (8.20 + 0.90) = 90,000 x 9.10 = 819,000 [1]; add fixed costs: 1,419,000 [1]. Site Q: 90,000 x (7.10 + 2.30) = 90,000 x 9.40 = 846,000 [1]; add fixed costs: **1,296,000** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** P: 1,419,000 / 90,000 = **15.77** [1]. Q: 1,296,000 / 90,000 = **14.40** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Site Q's higher delivery cost suggests it is further from customers, so site P might be chosen for faster delivery [1] and a quicker response to changes in demand, which could protect sales worth more than the 123,000 a year cost difference [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the cost per unit before multiplying and keep fixed costs separate from the per-unit costs; mixing them is the usual way to lose accuracy on this kind of calculation.

**5.** Insourcing can give the business greater control over quality [1]. Because the work is done in its own plant, it can inspect and correct problems directly rather than through a contract [1]. This can reduce defects and returns and protect the brand [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A benefit must be explained as a chain (cause, effect, result). A bare list such as "control, quality, cost" is unlikely to earn the later steps.

**6. (a)** Duty = 0.12 x 36.00 = 4.32 [1]. Landed cost = 36.00 + 6.50 + 4.32 + 1.10 [1] = **47.92** per unit [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Saving per unit = 52.00 - 47.92 = 4.08 [1]. Annual saving = 40,000 x 4.08 = **163,200** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** New duty = 0.12 x 41.40 = 4.968 and landed cost = 41.40 + 6.50 + 4.968 + 1.10 = 53.968 [1]. Compared with 52.00 the extra cost is 1.968 per unit [1]. Annual effect = 40,000 x 1.968 = **78,720 worse than producing at home** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Leave out no cost in a landed-cost total, and avoid rounding to two decimal places before multiplying by output; rounding early shifts the final answer.

**7. (a)** In-house: 360,000 + 140,000 x 5.20 = 360,000 + 728,000 [1] = **1,088,000** [1]. Subcontractor: 140,000 x 7.40 = **1,036,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 360,000 + 5.20q = 7.40q, so 2.20q = 360,000 [1]; q = 163,636.4, so **163,636 parts** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** At 140,000 parts the output is close to the point where the costs are equal, so a small rise in demand would make in-house production cheaper [1]. Expected demand is therefore important, not just today's cost [1]. Non-financial factors also matter, such as the control over quality and delivery dates that a subcontractor gives up [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Where a question asks why cost alone is not enough, name a specific non-cost factor and link it to the business; a general "other factors matter" is unlikely to gain credit.

**8.** An ethical issue is poor working conditions or low pay at the overseas site [1]. A business may choose the location because wages are low, and the supplier may not meet fair standards [1]. This can harm the brand and the trust of customers and other stakeholders if it becomes public [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the issue, then show why it is ethical for this decision. Describing only the financial effect of bad publicity addresses reputation, not the ethical point.

**9.** Home employees: reshoring may create jobs and secure income for the community [1], though the business may need to recruit and train staff [1]. Customers: delivery should be faster [1], but higher home costs could lead to higher prices [1]. (Other valid stakeholders include overseas workers, owners and suppliers.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each stakeholder needs an effect that follows from reshoring. A two-word label with no effect is a naming point only.

**10. (a)** Extra production cost = (62 - 49) x 20,000 = 260,000 [1]. Return handling: overseas 9% x 20,000 x 30 = 54,000; home 3% x 20,000 x 30 = 18,000, a saving of 36,000 [1]. Net extra cost = 260,000 - 36,000 = **224,000** a year (11.20 per desk) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** For reshoring: delivery in 3 weeks instead of 14 means less stock held in transit and a faster response to demand [1]. Lower returns save 36,000 a year and suggest better quality [1]. A home-made label may help the brand [1]. Against: unit cost rises by 13, a net 224,000 a year (about 11.20 per desk) [1], and a one-off cost to set up home production is likely [1]. The decision depends on whether faster delivery and quality are worth more than 224,000 a year, which turns on how much sales and price could rise [1]. Judgement: reshoring is justified only if the business can show it will raise sales or price by enough to cover the cost. If not, a better option may be to improve quality control overseas [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluate answer is stronger when it weighs both sides using the figures and ends with a conclusion that names what the decision depends on.

**11.** Outsourced cost = 0.46 x 2,500,000 = 1,150,000 [1]. In-house cost = 520,000 + 0.21 x 2,500,000 = 1,045,000, so insourcing saves 105,000 [1]. The costs are equal at 520,000 / (0.46 - 0.21) = 2,080,000 litres [1]. In a poor year (1,800,000 litres), the contractor costs 828,000 and in-house costs 898,000, so insourcing would cost 70,000 more [1]. Insourcing also gives control over quality, but it fixes costs and so adds risk if demand falls [1]. Recommendation: insource if volumes stay above 2,080,000 litres and the business values control, but the saving is modest, so if poor years are likely, keep the contract [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation should use the figures and name the condition under which it would change; a recommendation with no figures cannot show the reasoning.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the definitions of offshoring and outsourcing.
- Listing location factors without applying them to the business in the stem.
- Leaving out shipping, duty or stock holding when calculating landed cost.
- Adding fixed costs per unit instead of once in a total cost comparison.
- Rounding too early in multi-step calculations.
- Giving advantages only, in a question that asks you to evaluate.
- Naming a stakeholder without stating how the decision affects it.
- Ending an evaluation with a repeat of the points and no judgement.
- Forgetting that outsourcing and insourcing can be national as well as international.
