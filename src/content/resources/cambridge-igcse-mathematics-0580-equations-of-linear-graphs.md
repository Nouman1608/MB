This guide covers subtopic **3.5 Equations of linear graphs** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The two tiers are worded slightly differently:

| Tier | Statement | What the notes add |
|---|---|---|
| Core (C3.5) | Interpret and obtain the equation of a straight-line graph in the form y = mx + c. | Lines in the forms y = mx + c and x = k; finding the equation when the graph is given; finding the gradient or y-intercept from an equation such as y = 6x + 3 |
| Extended (E3.5) | Interpret and obtain the equation of a straight-line graph. | Lines in different forms, e.g. ax + by = c, y = mx + c and x = k; gradient and y-intercept from an equation such as 5x + 4y = 8 |

Both tiers are told that equations must be given "in a fully simplified form". A one-line summary of y = mx + c is in the [Coordinate geometry revision notes](/resources/igcse-mathematics-coordinate-geometry-revision-notes/). This page builds the full skill set: reading m and c, writing equations from graphs and from points, converting between forms, and saying what m and c mean in a real context. Afterwards, use the [equations of linear graphs revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-equations-of-linear-graphs-revision-notes/) and the [equations of linear graphs practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-equations-of-linear-graphs-practice/).

## y = mx + c: what each letter tells you

- **m** is the **gradient**: the number multiplying x when y is the subject.
- **c** is the **y-intercept**: the value of y where the line crosses the y-axis, at the point (0, c).

| Equation | m | c | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| y = 6x + 3 | 6 | 3 | standard form |
| y = –x + 7 | –1 | 7 | "–x" means –1x |
| y = ½x | ½ | 0 | passes through the origin |
| y = 5 – 2x | –2 | 5 | the x-term can come second |
| y = –4 | 0 | –4 | horizontal line |
| x = 3 | not defined | none | vertical line; it never crosses the y-axis |

Vertical lines cannot be written as y = mx + c, which is why the syllabus lists x = k separately.

## Core: finding the equation from a graph

1. Read **c** where the line crosses the y-axis.
2. Find **m** from a right-angled triangle under the line (change in y ÷ change in x, in axis units).
3. Write y = mx + c with the numbers substituted, and simplify.

### Worked example 1: a fractional gradient

A straight line crosses the y-axis at (0, –3) and passes through (4, 3). Find its equation.

```
c = -3
m = (3 - (-3)) / (4 - 0) = 6/4 = 3/2
y = (3/2)x - 3
```

"Fully simplified" means 6/4 must become 3/2 (or 1.5). Writing y = 6/4 x – 3 risks losing the final mark. A useful check: at x = 2, y = 3 – 3 = 0, so the line should cross the x-axis at (2, 0). Look at the graph to confirm.

## Interpreting an equation

"Interpret" covers reading information from the equation:

- Where does the line cross the y-axis? At (0, c).
- Where does it cross the x-axis? Set y = 0 and solve.
- Does a point lie on the line? Substitute its x-coordinate and compare.
- Which of two lines is steeper? Compare the sizes of the gradients.

### Worked example 2: meaning in context

A taxi company charges C dollars for a journey of d kilometres, where C = 2.5d + 4.

```
Gradient 2.5:   each extra kilometre costs $2.50
Intercept 4:    the fixed charge, $4, paid even for a 0 km journey
Cost for 12 km: C = 2.5(12) + 4 = 30 + 4 = $34
```

When asked what m or c represents, give the meaning in words with units. "The gradient" or "where it crosses the axis" scores nothing.

## Extended: finding the equation from two points

1. Calculate m from the two points.
2. Substitute m and **either** point into y = mx + c and solve for c.
3. Write the equation; check it with the other point.

### Worked example 3: two points, then another form

Find the equation of the line through (–2, 11) and (3, 1). Give it in the form ax + by = c.

```
m = (1 - 11) / (3 - (-2)) = -10/5 = -2
Using (3, 1):   1 = -2(3) + c,   so c = 7
y = -2x + 7
Check (-2, 11): -2(-2) + 7 = 4 + 7 = 11
In the form ax + by = c:   2x + y = 7
```

### Worked example 4: a fractional gradient with no fractions in the answer

Find the equation of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 4), giving your answer in the form ax + by = c where a, b and c are integers.

```
m = (4 - 2) / (4 - 1) = 2/3
Using (1, 2):  2 = (2/3)(1) + c,  so c = 2 - 2/3 = 4/3
y = (2/3)x + 4/3
Multiply every term by 3:  3y = 2x + 4
Rearrange:                 2x - 3y = -4
```

Multiplying through by the common denominator is how you clear fractions; every term must be multiplied, including y. Equivalent answers such as –2x + 3y = 4 are also fully simplified.

## Extended: gradient and intercept from ax + by = c

Rearrange to make y the subject, dividing **every** term by the coefficient of y.

### Worked example 5

Find the gradient and y-intercept of 3x – 4y = 12.

```
-4y = 12 - 3x
   y = -3 + (3/4)x
   y = (3/4)x - 3
gradient = 3/4,  y-intercept = -3
```

The common slip is to read the gradient as 3, the coefficient of x in the original equation. The gradient is only the coefficient of x **after** y is the subject.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading c from the x-axis crossing.** c is where the line meets the **y**-axis.
- **Forgetting the sign of m** for a line sloping downwards.
- **Taking m from 2y = 6x + 4 as 6.** Divide first: y = 3x + 2, so m = 3 and c = 2.
- **Leaving the answer unsimplified**, such as y = 4/2 x + 1.
- **Writing the point's coordinates into the final answer**: after finding c, the equation contains only x, y and numbers for m and c.
- **Writing y = 3 for a vertical line** through (3, 0). Vertical lines are x = k.
- **Dividing only some terms** when making y the subject.

## Exam technique

- **Find the equation**: the answer is an equation, "y = …", not just the values of m and c. A bare "m = 2, c = 7" may lose the final mark.
- **Write down the gradient** or **y-intercept**: if the equation is already y = mx + c, read the value off with no working; otherwise rearrange first.
- **Show that** a point lies on the line: substitute, simplify and state the conclusion.
- If a form is requested (for example ax + by = c with integer values), give it in that form.
- Check your equation with a second point every time; it takes seconds.
