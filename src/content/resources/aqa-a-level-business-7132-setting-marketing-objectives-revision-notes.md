Condensed notes on section **3.3.1 Setting marketing objectives** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and the Tidewell Bikes worked example are in the [marketing objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-marketing-objectives/); exam-style questions are in the [marketing objectives practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-setting-marketing-objectives-practice/).

## Specification content

- The value of setting marketing objectives.
- Marketing objectives include: sales volume and sales value; market size; market and sales growth; market share; brand loyalty.
- Throughout section 3.3: technology (digital marketing, social media, relationship marketing, dynamic pricing); ethical and environmental influences; market conditions and competition; competitiveness; links with other functions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing objective | A specific, measurable target for marketing in a stated period |
| Marketing strategy | The long-term plan for reaching the objective |
| Marketing tactic | A short-term action that carries out the strategy |
| Sales volume | Number of units sold |
| Sales value (revenue) | Volume × average selling price |
| Market size | Total sales of all firms in the market, by value or volume |
| Market growth | Percentage change in market size over a period |
| Sales growth | Percentage change in a firm's sales over a period |
| Market share | A firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Brand loyalty | How far customers keep buying a brand instead of switching |

## Formulas

- **Market growth (%)** = change in market size ÷ original market size × 100
- **Sales growth (%)** = change in sales ÷ original sales × 100
- **Market share (%)** = firm's sales ÷ total market sales × 100
- **Sales value** = sales volume × price
- Use the same basis (value or volume) for the firm and the market.

## Why set marketing objectives

- Gives **direction** to marketing and other functions.
- Allows results to be **measured** and decisions judged.
- Supports **motivation and accountability**.
- **Coordinates** functions: operations (output), finance (budget), HR (staffing).
- Focuses limited **resources**.

**Limits:** unrealistic if research is poor; narrow targets distort behaviour (volume chasing can cut profit); short-term focus; external shocks can make targets unachievable.

**SMART:** specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound.

## The objectives compared

| Objective | Suits a business that... | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Sales volume | needs to fill capacity or build scale | Discounting may cut sales value |
| Sales value | wants revenue growth or sells premium goods | Fewer units may mean less market presence |
| Market size | already has a large share | Rivals gain from the bigger market too |
| Market and sales growth | wants to expand | Growth may be only in line with the market |
| Market share | wants buying power and brand strength | Costly promotion can reduce profit |
| Brand loyalty | wants stable revenue and pricing freedom | Takes years to build |

## Reading growth and share together

- Sales growth **above** market growth: share is rising (winning customers from rivals).
- Sales growth **equal** to market growth: share is steady.
- Sales growth **below** market growth: sales rise but share falls.
- Say "percentage points" for the difference between two shares.

## Influences on the choice of objective

Corporate objectives; product life cycle stage; market conditions; competition; capacity and finance; ethical and environmental influences; technology (data that can measure loyalty and conversion).

## Exam traps

- An objective is not a strategy or tactic.
- Share must use matching bases (value with value, volume with volume).
- A rise in market share from 15% to 16% is **one percentage point**, not 1%.
- Higher volume does not always mean higher revenue.
- Sales can grow while market share falls.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between a marketing objective and a marketing strategy.
2. Give two benefits to a business of setting marketing objectives.
3. A market was worth £50m and is now worth £56m. Calculate market growth.
4. A firm's sales rose from £8m to £9.2m in the market in question 3. Calculate its sales growth.
5. Calculate the firm's market share before and after, given its sales in the earlier year were £8m.
6. Did the firm's market share rise or fall? Explain using your answers to questions 3 and 4.
7. A firm sells 40,000 units at £12.50. Calculate its sales value.
8. The firm cuts the price to £10 and sells 46,000 units. Calculate the new sales value and say whether volume or value improved.
9. Give two ways a business might measure brand loyalty.
10. Give one reason a business might choose a sales volume objective rather than a sales value objective.
11. State one way two marketing objectives might conflict.
12. Why might a business with a very large market share aim to increase market size?

### Answers

1. An objective is a target (where the business wants to be); a strategy is the long-term plan for how to get there.
2. Any two: direction; a way to measure results; motivation and accountability; coordination with other functions; focus for limited resources.
3. (56 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = **12%**.
4. (9.2 − 8) ÷ 8 × 100 = **15%**.
5. Earlier: 8 ÷ 50 × 100 = **16%**. Later: 9.2 ÷ 56 × 100 = **16.4%** (to one decimal place).
6. It rose (by about 0.4 percentage points), because sales growth of 15% was higher than market growth of 12%.
7. 40,000 × £12.50 = **£500,000**.
8. 46,000 × £10 = **£460,000**. Volume improved (up 6,000 units) but value fell by £40,000.
9. Any two: repeat purchase rate; membership of a loyalty scheme; survey of customers who would recommend the brand; share of customers who switch away.
10. Any one: to fill spare capacity; to gain economies of scale; to build a customer base quickly.
11. For example, a market share target may need price cuts that reduce a profit or brand image objective; a volume target may reduce sales value.
12. Because it holds most of the market, it gains most of the benefit when the whole market grows.
