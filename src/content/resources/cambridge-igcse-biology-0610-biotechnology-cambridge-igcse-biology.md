This guide covers section 21.2, Biotechnology, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 21.2.1 to 21.2.4 are Core and examined on all papers. Statements 21.2.5 to 21.2.7 are Supplement, examined only on the Extended papers (Papers 2 and 4), and are labelled "Extended only". The section is heavy on application: expect data tables, practical planning and "explain the result" questions rather than simple recall.

## Yeast: one reaction, two industries (Core)

Yeast is a single-celled fungus. Without oxygen it respires anaerobically:

```
glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide
```

The same reaction is used in two ways, and the mark depends on naming the right product.

### Ethanol for biofuels (21.2.1)

1. Plant material rich in sugar, or starch that has been broken down into sugar, is used as the food source for yeast.
2. The yeast is kept in large tanks **without oxygen**, so it respires anaerobically.
3. Anaerobic respiration converts the sugar into **ethanol** (the alcohol) and carbon dioxide.
4. The ethanol is separated from the mixture and used as a fuel.

The *role* of anaerobic respiration is to produce the ethanol. If oxygen were present, yeast would respire aerobically, producing carbon dioxide and water instead, so little or no ethanol would form. That is why the tanks are kept free of oxygen.

### Bread-making (21.2.2)

1. Yeast is mixed with flour, water and sugar to make a dough.
2. The dough is left in a warm place. Oxygen in the dough is soon used up, and the yeast respires anaerobically.
3. Anaerobic respiration releases **carbon dioxide**, which forms bubbles trapped in the dough, so the dough rises.
4. In the oven, the heat kills the yeast and the small amount of alcohol evaporates.

Warmth matters because respiration is controlled by enzymes. Too cold, and the reactions are slow; too hot, and the enzymes are denatured and the yeast stops producing gas.

### Worked example: dough and temperature

*Invented data.* Equal masses of dough were placed in measuring cylinders in water baths. The rise in volume after 40 minutes was recorded.

| Temperature / °C | 15 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rise in volume / cm³ | 8 | 22 | 36 | 18 | 2 |

**Mean rate of rise at 35 °C** = 36 ÷ 40 = **0.9 cm³ per minute**.

**Describe:** the rise increases from 15 °C to a peak of 36 cm³ at 35 °C, then falls to 2 cm³ at 55 °C. **Explain:** as the temperature rises towards the optimum, the yeast enzymes and substrate gain kinetic energy and collide more often, so anaerobic respiration, and carbon dioxide production, is faster. Above the optimum the enzymes are denatured, the active sites change shape, and respiration almost stops. Quote figures from the table when you describe; the examiner wants the data, not just "it goes up then down".

## Pectinase and fruit juice (Core, 21.2.3)

**Pectin** helps to hold plant cells together and is found in plant cell walls. **Pectinase** breaks down pectin. When pectinase is added to crushed fruit, the cell walls break down more easily, so more juice is released and the juice is clearer.

### Planning a pectinase investigation

Typical question: *plan an investigation into the effect of pectinase concentration on the volume of juice from apple pulp.*

- **Independent variable:** pectinase concentration (for example 0%, 1%, 2%, 3%, 4%).
- **Dependent variable:** volume of juice collected after filtering for a fixed time.
- **Control variables:** mass of apple pulp, type of apple, volume of enzyme solution added, temperature (water bath), time left before filtering, type of filter paper.
- **Control:** pulp with water (or boiled pectinase) instead of active enzyme, so any increase can be linked to the enzyme.
- **Reliability:** repeat each concentration and calculate a mean; identify and exclude anomalous results.

### Worked example: means and anomalies

*Invented data.* Three repeats at 2% pectinase gave 21.0, 22.0 and 14.5 cm³ of juice. The 14.5 cm³ result is far from the other two, so it is anomalous and is left out.

```
mean = (21.0 + 22.0) ÷ 2 = 21.5 cm³
```

If the control gave a mean of 12.5 cm³, the percentage increase is (21.5 − 12.5) ÷ 12.5 × 100 = **72%**.

## Biological washing powders (Core, 21.2.4)

Biological washing powders contain enzymes that digest stains into small, soluble molecules that wash away. Match each enzyme to the stain:

| Stain | Main substance | Enzyme | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood, egg, grass | Protein | Protease | Amino acids |
| Butter, oil, gravy | Fat or oil | Lipase | Fatty acids and glycerol |
| Pasta, potato, custard | Starch | Amylase | Simpler sugars |

The syllabus says "investigate", so you may be asked to interpret results. In a comparison of a biological and a non-biological powder at several temperatures, look for three things:

1. At low to moderate temperatures, the biological powder removes protein stains faster, because the enzymes digest the stain.
2. At high temperatures, the two powders perform similarly, because the enzymes are denatured, leaving only the detergent.
3. The advantage of a biological powder is that clothes can be washed at **lower temperatures**, using less energy, and delicate fabrics are less likely to be damaged.

A trap: a stain that is mostly fat will not be removed faster by a powder containing only protease. Read which enzymes the powder contains.

## Lactase and lactose-free milk (Extended only, 21.2.5)

"Explain" means give the reasons, not only the process.

- **What happens:** lactase is added to milk. It breaks down **lactose**, the sugar in milk, into **glucose and galactose**.
- **Why it is done:** some people do not produce enough lactase in their digestive system, so they cannot digest lactose properly. Milk in which the lactose has already been broken down can be drunk by these people.

Name both products. "Simple sugars" alone is unlikely to gain the mark.

## Fermenters (Extended only, 21.2.6 and 21.2.7)

A **fermenter** is a large vessel in which microorganisms are grown in controlled conditions so that they make a useful product on a large scale.

| Product | Organism | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin | Genetically modified bacteria carrying the human insulin gene | Bacterium |
| Penicillin | *Penicillium* | Fungus |
| Mycoprotein | *Fusarium* | Fungus |

### Why each condition is controlled

- **Temperature:** respiration by the microorganisms releases heat. A water jacket with cold water flowing through removes it. Too hot and enzymes denature; too cold and growth is slow.
- **pH:** enzymes have an optimum pH. Waste carbon dioxide can lower the pH, so a probe monitors it and acid or alkali is added.
- **Oxygen:** sterile air is bubbled in so the microorganisms can respire aerobically, releasing energy for growth. Paddles mix it through.
- **Nutrient supply:** glucose provides energy through respiration; a source of nitrogen is needed to make amino acids and proteins for growth.
- **Waste products:** carbon dioxide and other wastes are removed, because they can change the pH or become toxic and slow growth.

The vessel and nutrients are sterilised first. Any other microorganism would compete for nutrients, could contaminate the product and might be harmful.

### Worked example: reading fermenter data

*Invented data.* A fermenter making penicillin held 2.0 g of penicillin per dm³ at day 2 and 9.2 g per dm³ at day 6.

```
mean rate = (9.2 − 2.0) ÷ (6 − 2) = 7.2 ÷ 4 = 1.8 g per dm³ per day
```

If the oxygen supply failed on day 6, a good answer would predict that production slows, because the fungus can no longer respire aerobically, so less energy is released for growth and making the product.

## Common mistakes

- Naming alcohol as the reason dough rises, or carbon dioxide as the biofuel.
- Saying yeast in biofuel production needs oxygen. The tanks are kept without oxygen.
- Writing that pectinase breaks down cell membranes rather than pectin in cell walls.
- Describing a temperature trend without quoting figures.
- Missing the control (water or boiled enzyme) in an enzyme investigation.
- Giving penicillin or mycoprotein as bacterial products, or insulin as a fungal one.
- Saying the fermenter is heated. It is mainly cooled.

## Exam technique

- **Describe:** state the steps or the trend, using data from the question.
- **Explain:** add the reason, such as enzyme kinetic energy, denaturation or the need for aerobic respiration.
- **Investigate** questions: identify independent, dependent and control variables, a control, and repeats.
- **Calculate:** show working and units, for example cm³ per minute or g per dm³ per day.
