These notes condense section 2.2, Atomic structure and the Periodic Table, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). For the method and worked examples, see the [atomic structure and the Periodic Table study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-atomic-structure-and-the-periodic-table/); for exam-style questions, use the [atomic structure and the Periodic Table practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-atomic-structure-and-the-periodic-table-practice/).

## The atom in four lines

- Central **nucleus** containing **protons** and **neutrons**.
- **Electrons** in **shells** around the nucleus.
- Nucleus: almost all the mass, tiny volume.
- Atom: neutral, because protons = electrons.

## Particle data (learn exactly)

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| proton | +1 | 1 | nucleus |
| neutron | 0 | 1 | nucleus |
| electron | −1 | 1/1840 | shells |

## Definitions

- **Proton number / atomic number**: the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Mass number / nucleon number**: the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Neutrons** = mass number − proton number.
- **Electrons in an ion** = proton number − charge (so a 2+ ion has two fewer electrons than protons; a 2− ion has two more).

## Shell capacities (Z = 1 to 20)

| Shell | Maximum electrons |
|---|---|
| 1st | 2 |
| 2nd | 8 |
| 3rd | 8 (then the 4th shell begins at K) |

Quick builds: Z = 7 → 2,5 · Z = 13 → 2,8,3 · Z = 18 → 2,8,8 · Z = 20 → 2,8,8,2.

## Ions with noble-gas configurations

| Configuration | Same as | Ions |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | He | Li⁺, Be²⁺ |
| 2,8 | Ne | N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ |
| 2,8,8 | Ar | P³⁻, S²⁻, Cl⁻, K⁺, Ca²⁺ |

Positive ions: metal atoms **lose** outer electrons. Negative ions: non-metal atoms **gain** electrons to fill the outer shell.

## Periodic Table links

- Group VIII (noble gases): **full outer shell**.
- Groups I to VII: **outer-shell electrons = group number**.
- **Number of occupied shells = period number.**
- Helium (2) is in Group VIII with only two outer electrons: the group rule is for Groups I to VII only.
- Use the **atom's** configuration, not an ion's, to find group and period.

Reverse method: from group and period, write the shells. Period 3, Group V → three shells, five outer electrons → 2,8,5 (phosphorus).

## Particle counts: quick drill

Work each row as protons → neutrons → electrons.

| Particle | Z | A | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons | Configuration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⁷Li atom | 3 | 7 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2,1 |
| ²⁴Mg²⁺ ion | 12 | 24 | 12 | 12 | 10 | 2,8 |
| ³²S²⁻ ion | 16 | 32 | 16 | 16 | 18 | 2,8,8 |
| ³⁹K⁺ ion | 19 | 39 | 19 | 20 | 18 | 2,8,8 |
| ⁴⁰Ca atom | 20 | 40 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 2,8,8,2 |

Two particles can have the same configuration but be different elements (S²⁻ and K⁺ above): only the **proton number** identifies the element.

## Command words in this section

- **Describe** the structure of the atom: name the nucleus, what it contains, and where the electrons are.
- **State** the relative charge or mass: a value only.
- **Define** proton number or mass number: include "in the nucleus of an atom".
- **Determine** a configuration: count electrons first, show the count, then fill shells 2, 8, 8.
- **Deduce** group or period: say which feature of the configuration you used.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | dense centre of the atom, containing protons and neutrons |
| Nucleon | a proton or a neutron (a particle in the nucleus) |
| Shell | energy level occupied by electrons around the nucleus |
| Electronic configuration | arrangement of electrons in shells, e.g. 2,8,4 |
| Outer shell | the occupied shell furthest from the nucleus |
| Group | vertical column; same number of outer electrons |
| Period | horizontal row; same number of occupied shells |
| Cation / anion | positive ion / negative ion |

## Exam traps

- Electron mass is not zero: write 1/1840 or "negligible".
- The mass number is a count of particles, not a mass in grams.
- Ions change electrons only; protons and neutrons stay the same.
- Third shell holds 8 at this level: potassium is 2,8,8,1, not 2,8,9.
- Period of Na is 3 even though Na⁺ (2,8) has only two shells.
- Don't swap group and period: group from outer electrons, period from number of shells.

## Self-test

1. State the relative charge and relative mass of a neutron.
2. Define mass number.
3. How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in an atom of ⁴⁸Ti, proton number 22?
4. Write the electronic configuration of silicon (Z = 14).
5. Write the electronic configuration of the oxide ion, O²⁻.
6. How many electrons are in a Ca²⁺ ion?
7. An element has configuration 2,8,8,2. State its group and period.
8. Write the configuration of the element in Period 2, Group VII, and name it.
9. Why is the group-number rule not applied to helium?
10. Name **two** positive ions and **two** negative ions with the configuration 2,8.
11. Why is an atom electrically neutral?
12. Where is almost all of the mass of an atom found, and why?

## Answers

1. Charge 0; mass 1.
2. The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
3. 22 protons, 48 − 22 = 26 neutrons, 22 electrons.
4. 2,8,4.
5. 8 + 2 = 10 electrons: 2,8.
6. 20 − 2 = 18.
7. Group II, Period 4 (calcium).
8. 2,7: fluorine.
9. It is in Group VIII but has only two outer electrons; its first shell is full with two.
10. Any two of Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺; any two of N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻.
11. It has equal numbers of protons (+1 each) and electrons (−1 each).
12. In the nucleus: protons and neutrons each have relative mass 1, while electrons have negligible mass.
