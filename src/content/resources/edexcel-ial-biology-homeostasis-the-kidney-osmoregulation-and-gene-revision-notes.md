## Key definitions (7.16 and 7.17)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homeostasis | Maintaining a stable internal environment, as a dynamic equilibrium around a set point |
| Negative feedback | A change is detected and the response reverses it, so the variable stays within narrow limits |
| Positive feedback | A change is detected and the response increases it, so the system moves further from the start |
| Dynamic equilibrium | Values fluctuate a little either side of the set point as corrections switch on and off |

Control sequence: stimulus, receptor, coordinator, effector, response, then the response reduces the stimulus.

Positive feedback examples: oxytocin and contractions in labour; sodium ion channels opening during depolarisation of a neurone.

## Thermoregulation in steps

1. Central thermoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect blood temperature, and skin thermoreceptors send impulses to it.
2. The hypothalamus coordinates the response.
3. Effectors act: skin arterioles, sweat glands, skeletal muscle.
4. Temperature returns to the set point and the stimulus fades.

| Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|
| Vasodilation of skin arterioles | Vasoconstriction of skin arterioles |
| Sweating, with heat taken for evaporation | Shivering releases heat |
| More heat lost by radiation | Less sweat and a raised metabolic rate |

During exercise, extra heat from muscle respiration is removed by these responses, so core temperature rises only slightly.

## Kidney structure (7.18)

| Level | Structures |
|---|---|
| Gross | Fibrous capsule, cortex, medulla with pyramids, renal pelvis, ureter, renal artery, renal vein |
| Microscopic | Bowman's capsule and glomerulus, then proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule, collecting duct |

Where things are: Bowman's capsule, glomerulus and both convoluted tubules are in the **cortex**. Loops of Henle and collecting ducts run through the **medulla**.

Blood flow: afferent arteriole (wide) to glomerulus to efferent arteriole (narrow) to vasa recta.

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size. Convert both to the same unit before dividing.

## Urea and ultrafiltration (7.19)

- Excess amino acids are deaminated in the liver. The amino group becomes ammonia, which is combined with carbon dioxide to form urea. Ornithine cycle details are not required.
- Urea is carried in plasma to the kidneys.
- High hydrostatic pressure in the glomerulus is due to the afferent arteriole being wider than the efferent arteriole.
- Filtration barrier: capillary endothelium with fenestrations, basement membrane, podocytes with slits.
- Passes through: water, urea, glucose, amino acids, ions. Stays in blood: blood cells and large plasma proteins.
- Net filtration pressure = capillary hydrostatic pressure − capsule hydrostatic pressure − plasma opposing pressure.

## Reabsorption and the loop of Henle (7.20)

**Proximal tubule** features: microvilli, many mitochondria, sodium–potassium pumps in the basal membrane.

1. Sodium ions are pumped out of the cell into the blood.
2. Sodium ions enter from the filtrate with glucose or amino acids on co-transporters.
3. Glucose and amino acids leave to the blood by facilitated diffusion.
4. Water follows by osmosis.

**Loop of Henle** summary:

| Part | Permeability | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Descending limb | Permeable to water | Water leaves by osmosis, filtrate becomes more concentrated |
| Ascending limb | Impermeable to water | Ions leave (active transport in thick part), filtrate becomes more dilute, medulla becomes more concentrated |
| Collecting duct | Controlled by ADH | Water leaves into the concentrated medulla if permeable |

Countercurrent: the two limbs carry fluid in opposite directions, so a small difference at each level adds up to a large gradient. Vasa recta remove water without destroying the gradient. Longer loops give a steeper gradient and more concentrated urine.

## ADH feedback loop (7.21)

| Plasma too concentrated | Plasma too dilute |
|---|---|
| Osmoreceptors in hypothalamus shrink and stimulate neurosecretory cells | Osmoreceptors are not stimulated |
| ADH released from posterior pituitary | Less ADH released |
| Aquaporins inserted in collecting duct membrane | Aquaporins removed |
| More water reabsorbed, small volume concentrated urine | Less water reabsorbed, large volume dilute urine |
| Plasma and blood volume return towards normal | Plasma and blood volume return towards normal |

## Gene switching (7.22)

| | Steroid hormone | Peptide hormone |
|---|---|---|
| Solubility | Lipid-soluble | Water-soluble |
| Crosses the membrane | Yes, by diffusion | No |
| Receptor | Inside the cell | On the cell surface membrane |
| Route to the gene | Hormone–receptor complex is itself a transcription factor | Second messenger cascade activates transcription factors |

A transcription factor binds a specific DNA sequence. An activator helps RNA polymerase begin transcription, and a repressor prevents it.

## Method boxes for calculations

**Magnification.** Write the formula, convert the actual size to the unit of the image (1 mm = 1000 µm), divide, and give the answer as ×number. To find a real size, rearrange to actual size = image size ÷ magnification.

**Percentage reabsorbed.** (filtrate volume − urine volume) ÷ filtrate volume × 100. Both volumes must be per the same time and in the same unit.

**Percentage change.** (new − old) ÷ old × 100. A fall gives a negative answer, and you should state whether the value rose or fell.

**Rate.** Change in quantity ÷ time. For a rate of temperature rise, divide the temperature change by the minutes taken.

**Concentration factor.** Urine concentration ÷ plasma concentration of the same substance. It shows how much water has been removed relative to the solute.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Afferent and efferent:** afferent arteriole is wider and carries blood in. Efferent arteriole is narrower and carries blood away, which raises pressure in the glomerulus.
- **Filtration and reabsorption:** filtration is by size in the glomerulus, while reabsorption is selective and uses carrier proteins in the tubule.
- **Descending and ascending limbs:** the descending limb loses water, and the ascending limb loses ions.
- **Hypothalamus and pituitary:** the hypothalamus contains the osmoreceptors and makes ADH. The posterior pituitary releases it.
- **Coordinator and effector:** the hypothalamus coordinates. Sweat glands, skin arterioles and muscles are effectors.
- **Negative and positive feedback:** negative reverses a change, and positive amplifies it.
- **Vasoconstriction and vasodilation:** narrowing of arterioles reduces blood flow to the skin, and widening increases it.

## Command words in this topic

- **Describe** means give the structure or the order of events, without needing reasons.
- **Explain** needs a cause linked to a consequence, such as "the efferent arteriole is narrow, so the pressure in the glomerulus is high".
- **Suggest** is used when you apply the same ideas to an unfamiliar situation, for example a person who cannot make ADH.
- **Calculate** needs working. Show the numbers substituted into the formula.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is meant by dynamic equilibrium.
2. Give one example of positive feedback and explain why it is not used to keep a system steady.
3. Name the three structures that form the filtration barrier in the nephron.
4. A micrograph of a glomerulus is 36 mm wide and the real width is 240 µm. Calculate the magnification.
5. Glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure is 8.0 kPa, capsule pressure is 2.0 kPa and the opposing plasma protein pressure is 3.0 kPa. Calculate the net filtration pressure.
6. In a day, 180 dm³ of filtrate forms and 1.5 dm³ of urine is produced. Calculate the percentage of the filtrate reabsorbed to one decimal place.
7. Explain why urine contains no glucose in a healthy person.
8. State the role of the ascending limb of the loop of Henle.
9. Explain what happens to urine volume when plasma is too dilute.
10. Plasma urea is 6.0 mmol dm⁻³ and urine urea is 270 mmol dm⁻³. State the factor by which urea is more concentrated in urine than in plasma.
11. Explain why a steroid hormone can switch on a gene without a cell surface receptor.

### Answers

1. Values fluctuate slightly either side of a set point as corrections are constantly switched on and off.
2. Oxytocin and contractions in labour, or depolarisation. The change increases, so the system moves further from the set point.
3. Capillary endothelium (with fenestrations), basement membrane, podocytes.
4. 36 ÷ 0.240 = **×150**.
5. 8.0 − 2.0 − 3.0 = **3.0 kPa**.
6. (180 − 1.5) ÷ 180 × 100 = **99.2%**.
7. All the glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule by co-transport and facilitated diffusion.
8. Ions are transported out into the medulla while water stays in, which lowers the water potential of the medulla.
9. Less ADH is released, collecting duct walls are less permeable to water, so a large volume of dilute urine is produced.
10. 270 ÷ 6.0 = **45 times**.
11. It is lipid-soluble, so it diffuses through the membrane and binds a receptor inside the cell. The complex acts as a transcription factor.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the hypothalamus as the effector instead of the coordinator.
- Writing that blood vessels "move" closer to the skin.
- Saying ultrafiltration is selective for useful substances.
- Giving the wrong limb as water-permeable.
- Not mentioning aquaporins, or saying ADH enters the cell.
- Forgetting that ADH is released from the posterior pituitary, not made there.
- Using different units in magnification.
- Saying peptide hormones "enter the nucleus".
