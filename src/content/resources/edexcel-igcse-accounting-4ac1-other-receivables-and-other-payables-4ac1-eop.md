This guide covers section 5.4, Other receivables and other payables, in Topic 5 (Accounting for end of period adjustments) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. The outcomes are: (a) explain why it is necessary to account for other receivables and payables; (b) distinguish between them; (c) calculate and record them in the books of account. Section 2.7 has the same wording. Topic 5 is examined in Paper 2, where the specification says you make "appropriate adjustments to entries at the end of an accounting period", so this page starts from trial balance notes and ends with the income statement and the statement of financial position.

The specification's glossary links the names: other receivables replace "prepayments" and other payables replace "accruals". All businesses and figures here are invented, and amounts are in dollars without a currency sign. The first teaching of the four situations is in the [Topic 2 irrecoverable debts and other receivables and payables guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-irrecoverable-debts-and-other-receivables-payables/) and the [end-of-period adjustments overview](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-end-of-period-adjustments/). Companion pages: the [other receivables and other payables revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-other-receivables-and-other-payables-4ac1-eop-revision-notes/) and the [other receivables and other payables practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-other-receivables-and-other-payables-4ac1-eop-practice/).

## 1. Translating the notes

Trial balance figures for expenses and incomes are the cash paid or received, adjusted only for last year's balances. The notes tell you what is still owed or what was paid ahead. Turn each note into an adjustment before touching the statements.

| Wording in a note | Situation | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Wages of 900 are owing" | Expense accrued | Add 900 to wages | Other payable 900 |
| "Insurance includes 480 for next year" | Expense prepaid | Take 480 from insurance | Other receivable 480 |
| "Commission of 300 is due but not received" | Income accrued | Add 300 to commission | Other receivable 300 |
| "Rent received includes 500 for next month" | Income received in advance | Take 500 from rent received | Other payable 500 |

A quick test: if the business **owes** something (money or a service it has been paid for), it is an other payable. If it is **owed** something (money or a service it has paid for), it is an other receivable.

## 2. Adjusting a trial balance

### Worked example 1: five notes

Hollins Garden Centre has a year end of 30 June 2028. Its trial balance includes wages 48,300, electricity 5,460, insurance 3,840, rent received 6,500 and commission received 1,850. Notes:

1. Wages of 1,120 are owing.
2. The electricity bill for June, 640, has not been paid.
3. Insurance includes 2,880 paid on 1 March 2028 for twelve months.
4. Rent received includes 500 for July 2028.
5. Commission of 400 is due from a supplier and has not been received.

The insurance needs time apportionment. The policy costs 2,880 ÷ 12 = 240 a month. March to June is 4 months used; July to February is 8 months prepaid: 8 × 240 = **1,920**.

| Item | Trial balance | Adjustment | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Wages | 48,300 | + 1,120 | 49,420 | Other payable 1,120 |
| Electricity | 5,460 | + 640 | 6,100 | Other payable 640 |
| Insurance | 3,840 | − 1,920 | 1,920 | Other receivable 1,920 |
| Rent received | 6,500 | − 500 | 6,000 | Other payable 500 |
| Commission received | 1,850 | + 400 | 2,250 | Other receivable 400 |

In the statement of financial position the items are grouped:

- Current assets: **other receivables** 1,920 + 400 = **2,320**.
- Current liabilities: **other payables** 1,120 + 640 + 500 = **2,260**.

The effect on profit, compared with using the trial balance figures unchanged: −1,120 − 640 + 1,920 − 500 + 400 = **+60**. Small net figures like this hide large separate adjustments, which is why each one is worked on its own line.

## 3. Ledger accounts when the balance changes side

Paper 2 can give an opening balance of one kind and a closing balance of the other. The ledger account handles both if you remember which side each balance sits on.

| Balance | Is it | Brought down on |
|---|---|---|
| Expense prepaid | Asset | Debit |
| Expense accrued | Liability | Credit |
| Income accrued | Asset | Debit |
| Income received in advance | Liability | Credit |

### Worked example 2: an expense, prepaid at the start, owing at the end

Thistle Print has a year end of 31 December 2027. At 1 January rent of 900 had been paid in advance. During 2027 it paid 10,800. At 31 December rent of 1,000 is owing.

Expense = 900 + 10,800 + 1,000 = **12,700**.

**Rent payable**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d (prepaid) | 900 | 31 Dec Income statement | 12,700 |
| Bank | 10,800 | | |
| 31 Dec Balance c/d (owing) | 1,000 | | |
| | 12,700 | | 12,700 |
| | | 2028 1 Jan Balance b/d (owing) | 1,000 |

### Worked example 3: an income, owed at the start, paid in advance at the end

At 1 January Thistle Print was owed commission of 250. It received 3,400 during 2027, of which 300 relates to 2028.

Income = 3,400 − 250 − 300 = **2,850**. The 250 received this year belonged to last year, and the 300 belongs to next year.

**Commission receivable**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d (owed) | 250 | Bank | 3,400 |
| 31 Dec Income statement | 2,850 | | |
| 31 Dec Balance c/d (in advance) | 300 | | |
| | 3,400 | | 3,400 |
| | | 2028 1 Jan Balance b/d (in advance) | 300 |

The 1,000 and the 300 are both other payables at 31 December 2027.

## 4. Time apportionment

Many notes give a payment and a period, not the adjustment. Find the monthly amount, then count months on each side of the year end.

Rates of 4,500 paid on 1 October 2027 for the 15 months to 31 December 2028, year end 31 December 2027: 4,500 ÷ 15 = 300 a month. October to December 2027 is 3 months, so the expense is **900** and the other receivable is 12 × 300 = **3,600**.

## 5. Written parts: explain and distinguish

**Explain why it is necessary (5.4 a).** Model answer for Hollins Garden Centre: *The accruals concept says expenses and income belong to the period in which they are used or earned, not the period of payment. Without the adjustments, wages and electricity would be too low and profit too high, and the business would leave out liabilities of 2,260 it really owes. The statement of financial position would then not give a true picture of what the business owns and owes.*

**Distinguish (5.4 b).** Model answer: *Other receivables are amounts owed to the business, or paid by it in advance, other than by trade customers, such as insurance paid ahead; they are current assets. Other payables are amounts the business owes other than to trade suppliers, such as wages owing; they are current liabilities.*

## 6. Effects on working capital and errors

The adjustments change current assets and current liabilities, so they change the current ratio (Topic 4). If Hollins had current assets of 18,000 and current liabilities of 9,000 before adjusting, the ratio was 2.00 : 1. After adding 2,320 and 2,260 it becomes 20,320 : 11,260, which is **1.80 : 1**.

Errors to trace:

| Error | Effect on profit |
|---|---|
| Accrued expense omitted | Overstated by the accrual |
| Prepaid expense omitted | Understated by the prepayment |
| Prepayment added to the expense instead of deducted | Understated by twice the prepayment |
| Income received in advance left in income | Overstated by the amount in advance |

If Hollins added the insurance prepayment instead of deducting it, the expense would be 3,840 + 1,920 = 5,760 instead of 1,920, and profit would be understated by **3,840**, twice the 1,920.

## Common mistakes

- Adding a prepayment to the expense because the note says "includes".
- Grouping income received in advance with other receivables.
- Using the whole payment instead of apportioning months.
- Forgetting to include last year's balances in the ledger account.
- Labelling a figure "accrual" in the statement of financial position instead of using the specification's terms, other receivables and other payables.

## Exam technique

"Calculate" needs a line for each item, showing the trial balance figure, the adjustment with its sign, and the result. "Record" means ledger accounts with dates, the other account named, balances carried down and brought down. "Explain" needs the accruals concept applied to the business, with the effect on profit and on assets or liabilities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is income received in advance an other receivable?**
No. The business owes the customer a service, so it is a liability: an other payable.

**Why does an opening prepayment add to this year's expense?**
It was paid last year for a benefit used this year, so it belongs to this year's cost.
