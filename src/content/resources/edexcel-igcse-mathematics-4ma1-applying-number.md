## A method for everyday problems (1.10 A)

Everyday questions rarely tell you which operation to use. A reliable routine:

1. **Find the target.** What is the final answer, and in what unit?
2. **Make units consistent** before calculating.
3. **Label each step**, such as "area of walls =" or "tins needed =".
4. **Round from the context**: up when you must have enough (tins, buses, packs); down when you can only use whole items that fit (biscuits, crates, pieces of ribbon).
5. **Answer the question asked**, in a sentence if it asks "how many", "which" or "is it enough".

## Metric units in context (1.10 B)

Quick reference: 1 tonne = 1000 kg; 1 litre = 100 cl = 1000 ml; 1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1 m³ = 1000 litres; 1 m² = 10 000 cm²; 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³.

- 0.75 tonnes = 750 kg.
- A 0.2 litre bottle of medicine gives 200 ÷ 5 = 40 doses of 5 ml.
- 4500 cm³ = 4.5 litres.

**Worked example 1 (area, capacity and cost).** A room has a rectangular floor 4.2 m by 3.0 m and walls 2.6 m high. The door covers 1.8 m², and there are no windows. The walls need two coats of paint. One litre of paint covers 12 m², and paint is sold in 2.5 litre tins at £18.99. Find the cost of the paint.

```
perimeter of floor  = 2 × (4.2 + 3.0) = 14.4 m
area of walls       = 14.4 × 2.6 − 1.8 = 35.64 m²
two coats           = 2 × 35.64 = 71.28 m²
paint needed        = 71.28 ÷ 12 = 5.94 litres
tins needed         = 5.94 ÷ 2.5 = 2.376 -> 3 tins
cost                = 3 × 18.99 = £56.97
```

Using the perimeter times the height finds all four walls in one step. The tin count rounds **up**: 2 tins would hold only 5 litres.

## Time (1.10 C)

### Reading a timetable

| Stop | Bus A | Bus B | Bus C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park Road | 07:48 | 08:13 | 08:38 |
| Station | 08:05 | 08:30 | 08:55 |
| Hospital | 08:22 | 08:47 | 09:12 |

**Worked example 2.** Mina arrives at Park Road at 08:00. She must reach the hospital by 09:00. Which bus should she catch, how long does she wait, and how long is her journey?

```
Bus A has already left (07:48).
Bus B leaves 08:13 and arrives 08:47 (before 09:00).
wait     = 08:00 to 08:13 = 13 minutes
journey  = 08:13 to 08:47 = 34 minutes
```

Read across a row for one stop and down a column for one bus. Check the last bus that still meets the deadline: Bus C arrives 09:12, too late.

### Working backwards from a deadline

**Worked example 3.** A turkey needs 40 minutes per kilogram plus an extra 20 minutes. A 4.5 kg turkey must be ready at 18:30. When must it go in the oven?

```
cooking time = 40 × 4.5 + 20 = 200 minutes = 3 h 20 min
18:30 − 3 h  = 15:30
15:30 − 20 min = 15:10
```

Subtract hours first, then minutes. Converting 200 minutes to "2.00 hours" or "3.2 hours" is a common slip; there are 60 minutes in an hour.

### Dates

Count nights or days carefully. A holiday from 26 July to 9 August: July has 31 days, so 26 July to 31 July is 5 nights, and 31 July to 9 August is 9 more, giving **14 nights**. Know the lengths of the months; February has 28 days, or 29 in a leap year.

## Money (1.10 C)

### Wages and overtime

"Time and a half" means 1.5 times the basic hourly rate; "double time" means twice the rate. Work out basic pay and overtime separately, then add.

### Sharing a bill

**Worked example 4.** A restaurant bill is £186.40. A 10% service charge is added, and six friends share the total equally. How much does each pay?

```
total     = 186.40 × 1.1 = £205.04
each      = 205.04 ÷ 6 = 34.1733…
```

Each pays **£34.18**. Rounding down to £34.17 would leave the bill 2p short, so the context says round up.

### Currency with buying and selling rates

A bureau may use one rate when you buy foreign money and a different rate when you sell it back.

**Worked example 5.** A bureau sells euros at £1 = €1.14 and buys euros back at £1 = €1.21. Ana changes £500 into euros, spends €430 on holiday, and changes the rest back into pounds. How many pounds does she get back?

```
euros bought     = 500 × 1.14 = €570
euros left       = 570 − 430 = €140
pounds back      = 140 ÷ 1.21 = 115.702… = £115.70
```

At each conversion, ask which currency should give the bigger number. Pounds to euros at these rates gives more euros, so multiply; euros to pounds gives fewer pounds, so divide.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing units, such as multiplying 4.2 m by 260 cm.
- Rounding tins, packs or buses down when the context needs enough.
- Reading the wrong row or column of a timetable, or using a bus that has already left.
- Treating minutes as decimals: 3 h 20 min is 3.333… hours, not 3.2 hours.
- Counting days inclusively when the question asks for nights, or the reverse.
- Multiplying when you should divide in a currency conversion.
- Writing money as £34.1733 or £56.9; give two decimal places.

## Exam technique

- **Show your working** in multi-step problems, with a label on each line, so method marks can be given even if one step goes wrong.
- **Is it enough?** or **Can she afford it?** questions need a comparison and a conclusion: "She needs £56.97 and has £50, so no."
- **Give your answer to the nearest penny** or **in hours and minutes**: follow the format exactly.
- **Explain** why you rounded up or down, in context.
- A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, so the marks are for choosing the right steps and units; estimate first to catch a misplaced decimal point.
