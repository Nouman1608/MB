## Equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| w = λD/s | Fringe spacing in Young's double slit (D much larger than s) |
| d sin θ = nλ | Grating maxima, light at normal incidence |
| d = 1/N | Slit spacing from N lines per metre |
| n = c/cₛ | Refractive index |
| n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ | Snell's law |
| sin θc = n₂/n₁ | Critical angle (n₁ > n₂) |

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Path difference | Difference in distance travelled by two waves to a point |
| Coherent | Same frequency and constant phase difference |
| Monochromatic | Single wavelength |
| Constructive interference | Waves arrive in phase; path difference nλ (in-phase sources) |
| Destructive interference | Waves arrive in antiphase; path difference (n + ½)λ |
| Diffraction | Spreading of waves through a gap or around an obstacle |
| Central maximum | Brightest, widest band of a single-slit pattern |
| Refractive index | Ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to the speed in the material |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence giving a refraction angle of 90° |
| Total internal reflection | All light reflected at a boundary when θ > θc, going into a less dense medium |
| Modal dispersion | Pulse spreading because rays take different paths |
| Material dispersion | Pulse spreading because different wavelengths travel at different speeds |

## Interference

| Path difference (in-phase sources) | Result |
|---|---|
| 0, λ, 2λ … | Maximum |
| λ/2, 3λ/2 … | Minimum |

If the sources are in antiphase, the two rows swap.

Worked reminder: path difference 7.5 cm with λ = 3.0 cm is 2.5λ, so a **minimum**.

**Young's double slit**: equally spaced fringes; w ∝ λ and w ∝ D, w ∝ 1/s. White light: white central fringe, coloured fringes with blue nearer the centre.

**Interference with sound and microwaves**: two loudspeakers on one signal generator, or two gaps in a metal sheet in front of a microwave source, give maxima and minima explained by path difference.

**Laser safety**: do not look along the beam; avoid reflections; keep the beam below eye level.

## Diffraction

**Single slit (monochromatic)**: central maximum twice the width of the others and much brighter.

| Change | Central maximum |
|---|---|
| Narrower slit | Wider |
| Longer wavelength | Wider |
| White light | White centre, coloured edges; side maxima are spectra, violet nearest the centre |

**Grating method in steps**
1. Convert lines per mm to lines per m, then d = 1/N.
2. sin θ = nλ/d for each order.
3. Highest order: largest whole n ≤ d/λ.
4. To find λ from a pattern, measure the angle between orders n either side and halve it.

Worked reminder: 600 lines per mm, λ = 656 nm: d = 1.67 × 10⁻⁶ m, θ₁ = **23.2°**, highest order **2**.

Applications: identifying elements from spectra (including starlight); measuring wavelengths.

**Required practical 2**: interference by Young's slits and by a diffraction grating. Measure across many fringes; measure D with a tape; for a grating find θ from tan θ = x/D or measure the angle directly.

## Refraction and fibres

**Method for a refraction problem**
1. Draw the normal; measure angles from it.
2. Apply n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
3. If going into a lower n, find θc; if θ₁ > θc, total internal reflection.

Worked reminder: water (n = 1.33) to air: θc = **48.8°**.

**Step-index fibre**: core with higher n, cladding with lower n.

| Problem | Cause | Reduce it by |
|---|---|---|
| Modal dispersion | Different ray paths, different times | Narrower (monomode) core |
| Material dispersion | Different wavelengths, different speeds | Monochromatic source |
| Absorption | Energy absorbed by the glass | Amplifiers or regenerators along the fibre |

Cladding: keeps total internal reflection at the core boundary, protects the core from scratches, prevents signals crossing between fibres.

## Exam traps

- Calling two lamps coherent because they have the same colour.
- Mixing mm and m in w = λD/s.
- Saying a narrower slit narrows the central maximum.
- Giving a grating order that needs sin θ > 1.
- Using lines per mm directly as d.
- Writing sin θc = n₁/n₂, which is greater than 1.
- Measuring angles from the surface.
- Mixing up modal and material dispersion.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Double slit vs grating**: broad, closely spaced fringes vs sharp, bright, widely spaced maxima.
- **Diffraction vs interference**: spreading at a gap vs superposition of coherent waves (a diffraction pattern is itself an interference effect).
- **Pulse broadening vs absorption**: the pulse spreads in time vs its amplitude falls.

## Quick self-test

1. State the conditions for two sources to be coherent.
2. Two in-phase sources give a path difference of 3λ at a point. What is observed?
3. Light of 650 nm passes through slits 0.40 mm apart onto a screen 3.0 m away. Find w.
4. In question 3, the screen is moved to 1.5 m. What is the new fringe spacing?
5. Describe the central fringe when white light is used in a double-slit experiment.
6. What happens to the single-slit central maximum when blue light replaces red?
7. A grating has 300 lines per mm. Find d.
8. Light of 500 nm falls on the grating in question 7. Find θ₁ and the highest order.
9. Light enters glass (n = 1.50) from air at 30°. Find the angle of refraction.
10. Find the critical angle between a core of n = 1.52 and cladding of n = 1.48.
11. Name the type of dispersion reduced by using a monochromatic source.
12. Give two functions of the cladding.

### Answers

1. Same frequency and a constant phase difference.
2. A **maximum** (constructive interference).
3. w = 650 × 10⁻⁹ × 3.0 / 0.40 × 10⁻³ = **4.9 mm**.
4. w ∝ D, so it halves to about **2.4 mm**.
5. **White**, because all wavelengths have zero path difference there.
6. It becomes **narrower**, because blue has a shorter wavelength.
7. d = 1/(300 × 10³) = **3.33 × 10⁻⁶ m**.
8. sin θ₁ = 0.150, θ₁ = **8.6°**; d/λ = 6.7, so the highest order is **6**.
9. sin θ₂ = sin 30°/1.50 = 0.333, θ₂ = **19.5°**.
10. sin θc = 1.48/1.52, θc = **76.8°**.
11. **Material** dispersion.
12. Any two: keeps total internal reflection at the core boundary; protects the core from scratches; stops signals crossing between fibres.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the central fringe white in a white-light double-slit pattern?**
At the centre the path difference is zero for every wavelength, so all colours interfere constructively there and combine to give white.

**Why do gratings give sharper maxima than two slits?**
With many slits, light from all of them must arrive in phase for a maximum, which only happens at precise angles. Elsewhere the many waves cancel, so the maxima are sharp and bright.
