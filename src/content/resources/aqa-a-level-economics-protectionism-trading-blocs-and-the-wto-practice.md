These questions cover tariffs, quotas, export subsidies, the causes and consequences of protectionism, customs unions, the Single European Market and the WTO for AQA A-level Economics (7136), specification version 1.3, section 4.2.6.2 (3.2.6.2 on the AQA website). Every country and figure is invented and illustrative. Read the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-economics-protectionism-trading-blocs-and-the-wto/) and [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-economics-protectionism-trading-blocs-and-the-wto-revision-notes/) first. Course pages: [AQA A-level Economics hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/economics/), [checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/economics/) and [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/). Show your working. Quantities are in thousands and prices in c, an invented currency.

## Questions

**1.** Country Ash puts a 20% ad valorem tariff on imported lamps. The import price before the tariff is 45 c.

**(a)** Calculate the price paid by buyers after the tariff. **[2]**
**(b)** Imports after the tariff are 80 thousand. Calculate the tariff revenue. **[2]**

**2.** Country Elm is a small economy. In its market for blankets, Qd = 160 − 2P and Qs = 2P − 20. The world price is 30 c.

**(a)** Calculate imports under free trade. **[2]**
**(b)** A tariff of 5 c is imposed. Calculate the new price, imports and tariff revenue. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the deadweight loss. **[3]**

**3.** In Elm's blanket market (question 2), a quota of 40 thousand units on imports is imposed instead of the tariff.

**(a)** Show that the domestic price rises to 35 c. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the quota rent. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a quota could leave Elm worse off than the tariff. **[2]**

**4.** Country Fenn exports dried apricots. Qd = 100 − P and Qs = 2P − 20. The world price is 50 c.

**(a)** Calculate exports with no subsidy. **[2]**
**(b)** An export subsidy of 6 c per unit is introduced. Calculate the new domestic price, exports and cost to the government. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the net change in Fenn's economic welfare. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why a government might adopt protectionist policies. **[4]**

**6.** Country Hale has a tariff of 8 c per unit on all imports of a good and buys 50 thousand units. Outsider Ivor sells at 15 c. Partner Jorn sells at 19 c. Hale and Jorn form a customs union.

**(a)** State where Hale buys before the union, and the price paid. **[2]**
**(b)** State where Hale buys after the union and name the effect. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the net effect on Hale, assuming it still buys 50 thousand units. **[3]**

**7.** Distinguish between a customs union and the Single European Market. **[4]**

**8.** Explain two roles of the World Trade Organisation (WTO). **[4]**

**9.** In question 2, each thousand blankets made at home needs 10 workers.

**(a)** Calculate the jobs protected by the 5 c tariff. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the consumer loss per job protected. **[3]**

**10.** Two countries choose between free trade and a tariff. Payoffs (A, B) are: both free trade 10, 10; A tariff and B free trade 14, 4; B tariff and A free trade 4, 14; both tariff 6, 6.

**(a)** Show that each country's best choice is a tariff whatever the other does. **[2]**
**(b)** State the outcome and compare combined payoffs with free trade. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the WTO might help the countries reach a better outcome. **[3]**

**11.** Using your answer to question 6 and other knowledge, evaluate whether Hale's government should join a customs union with Jorn. **[12]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Tariff = 0.20 × 45 = 9 c [1]. Price = 45 + 9 = **54 c [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Revenue = 9 × 80 [1] = **720 thousand c [1]**. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert a percentage tariff to a sum per unit first. Revenue uses imports after the tariff, not before.

**2. (a)** Qd = 160 − 60 = 100 and Qs = 60 − 20 = 40 [1]. Imports = **60 thousand [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Price = 30 + 5 = 35 c [1]. Qd = 90, Qs = 50, imports = **40 thousand [1]**. Revenue = 5 × 40 = **200 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
**(c)** Production triangle = 1/2 × 5 × (50 − 40) = 25 [1]. Consumption triangle = 1/2 × 5 × (100 − 90) = 25 [1]. Deadweight loss = **50 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the base of each triangle from the change in quantity along a curve, and keep the revenue rectangle out of the loss. A check against the consumer loss of 475 (= 225 + 200 + 50) shows the answer is consistent.

**3. (a)** Set Qd − Qs = 40: (160 − 2P) − (2P − 20) = 40 [1]. 180 − 4P = 40, so **P = 35 c [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Rent = (35 − 30) × 40 [1] = **200 thousand c [1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** If licences go to foreign exporters or importers, the 200 leaves Elm [1]; under the tariff the government collects the same 200 as revenue [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A quota answer gains from saying who holds the licences. Stating that quota and tariff give the same price is not enough on its own.

**4. (a)** Qd = 100 − 50 = 50 and Qs = 100 − 20 = 80 [1]. Exports = **30 thousand [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Domestic price = 56 c [1]. Qd = 44, Qs = 92, exports = **48 thousand [1]**. Cost = 6 × 48 = **288 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
**(c)** Producer gain = (80 + 92)/2 × 6 = 516 [1]. Consumer loss = (50 + 44)/2 × 6 = 282 [1]. Net = 516 − 282 − 288 = **−54 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The subsidy cost is paid on all exports after the subsidy. Say the net result is a loss and name where it comes from.

**5.** Any two developed reasons, for example: infant industries: a new industry has high unit costs while small [1], so a tariff gives time to reach scale and cut costs [1]. Anti-dumping: foreign firms may sell below cost to win share [1], and a tariff raises the price to a fairer level, though consumers lose cheap goods [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason becomes a developed point when you link it to a mechanism and a limit. A bare list of reasons with no chain of reasoning gains less.

**6. (a)** Ivor: 15 + 8 = 23; Jorn: 19 + 8 = 27 [1]. Hale buys from **Ivor at 23 c [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** After the union Jorn's price is **19 c [1]**, below Ivor's 23, so this is **trade diversion [1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** Consumers save (23 − 19) × 50 = 200 [1]. Tariff revenue lost = 8 × 50 = 400 [1]. Net effect = **−200 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the comparison of landed prices before you name the effect. The result can be cross-checked: the extra world resource cost is (19 − 15) × 50 = 200.

**7.** Customs union: no tariffs on trade between members [1] and a common external tariff on non-members [1]. SEM: free movement of goods and services, capital and labour [1] with common rules or standards so that barriers other than tariffs fall [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Distinction answers do best when each side gets its own feature. Stating both are "trading blocs" earns little.

**8.** Any two, each explained: negotiating agreements that lower trade barriers [1], which widens access to markets [1]; setting rules such as non-discrimination [1], so members treat each other's goods equally [1]. Settling disputes or monitoring policies are also valid. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair a role with its effect on trade. Saying the WTO "stops protectionism" overstates it, because some exceptions exist.

**9. (a)** Output rises from 40 to 50 thousand [1]. Jobs = 10 × 10 = **100 [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Consumer loss = (100 + 90)/2 × 5 = 475 [1]. Per job = 475 ÷ 100 [1] = **4.75 thousand c [1]**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Be clear about which loss you use. The net loss of 50 gives 0.5 thousand c per job. Either figure can be used if you label it.

**10. (a)** If B plays free trade, A gets 14 from a tariff against 10 [1]. If B tariffs, A gets 6 against 4, so a tariff is best either way (the same holds for B) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Both tariff, payoffs **6, 6 [1]**. Combined 12 against 20 under free trade, so **8 lower [1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** Members commit to cut tariffs through agreements [1]. Monitoring and dispute settlement make cheating costly [1]. Repeated talks build trust so both choose free trade and gain 10 each [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both comparisons for each country. Explain why the rules change incentives, not just that they exist.

**11.** A strong answer could combine these points. Trade creation: cheaper member output replaces dearer home output [1], with an example such as a lower price for buyers [1]. Trade diversion: in question 6, buying switches from Ivor to Jorn [1], for a net loss of 200 [1]. The common external tariff keeps prices of outside goods high [1]. A larger tariff-free market allows scale economies [1] and more competition [1]. Hale gives up an independent trade policy [1]. The result depends on how efficient Jorn is relative to outsiders [1] and on how much trade shifts [1]. Judgement on size or time [1], ending in a justified conclusion [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation uses the numbers from question 6 and reaches a decision with reasons, for instance that Hale gains only if trade creation or scale benefits outweigh the 200 loss.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Finding revenue from pre-tariff import volumes.
- Taking the whole consumer loss as deadweight loss.
- Drawing or describing the tariff line at the wrong price.
- Not saying who holds quota licences.
- Writing "trade diversion" without comparing landed prices.
- Treating a customs union and the SEM as the same.
- Giving WTO roles with no effect on trade.
- Ending evaluation with "it depends" and no reason.
