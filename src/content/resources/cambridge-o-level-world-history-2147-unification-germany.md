**Key question 3 of Core Content Option A in Cambridge O Level History 2147 asks "How was Germany unified?"** In 1850
the German states were back under Austrian leadership after a failed revolution. Twenty-one years later a German
Empire led by Prussia was proclaimed in the palace of Versailles, after three short wars. The question asks you to
explain that change and to judge how much of it was the work of Otto von Bismarck. It was the Paper 2 prescribed
topic for June 2025 and remains available for Paper 1 throughout 2024–2026.

Short recall is in the [Germany revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-unification-germany-revision-notes/),
and exam-style questions with mark schemes are in the
[Germany practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-unification-germany-practice/). The
[O Level nineteenth century study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-nineteenth-century-1848-1914/) places the
question within Option A.

## What the specification sets

Focus points:

1. Why was Germany not unified in 1848–50?
2. How did Bismarck bring about Austria's defeat of 1866?
3. How did Bismarck bring about France's defeat of 1870?
4. How far was Bismarck responsible for the unification of Germany?

Specified content: German nationalism; the Zollverein; the 1848 revolution in Prussia; the setting up and eventual
failure of the Frankfurt Parliament; the re-establishment of Austrian influence by 1850; Bismarck as Prussian
Minister-President; and Bismarck's foreign policy to 1871 (Schleswig-Holstein, the Austro-Prussian War and its
consequences, relations with France, the Spanish succession and the Franco-Prussian War, and the creation of the
German Empire).

## Germany before 1848: two forces pulling apart

The Congress of Vienna (1815) created a **German Confederation** of 39 states with a Diet at Frankfurt chaired by
Austria. It was designed to keep order, not to build a nation.

Two forces worked against that design. **Nationalism** spread among students, professors and writers, who argued that
Germans shared a language and culture and should share a state. Austria's chancellor Metternich suppressed it through
the Carlsbad Decrees (1819), which censored universities and the press. **Economic integration** moved faster. The
Prussian-led **Zollverein**, a customs union formed in 1834, removed tariffs between most German states but excluded
Austria. By the 1850s railways, coal and iron tied northern and western Germany to Prussia's economy. When historians
say Germany was united "by coal and iron" as much as by "blood and iron", this is what they mean.

## Why was Germany not unified in 1848–50?

**The revolution in Prussia.** Street fighting in Berlin in March 1848 forced King Frederick William IV to withdraw his
troops, promise a constitution and appear to support German unity. By November the army was back in the capital; the
Prussian assembly was closed and the king issued his own constitution in December 1848, which left real power with
the crown.

**The Frankfurt Parliament.** Elected across the Confederation, it met in St Paul's Church in May 1848. Its members
were mostly lawyers, officials and professors, and it suffered from three weaknesses:

- **No army or money of its own.** In the Schleswig-Holstein dispute of 1848, Prussia fought Denmark on the
  Parliament's behalf and then made an armistice at Malmö without asking it. The Parliament could do nothing.
- **Division over borders.** Supporters of a "greater Germany" wanted to include Austria's German lands; supporters of
  a "smaller Germany" wanted Prussian leadership without Austria. The smaller solution won only after Austria insisted
  on keeping its whole multinational empire together.
- **Dependence on princes.** In April 1849 the Parliament offered the crown of a united Germany to Frederick William
  IV. He refused a crown offered by an elected assembly. The larger states withdrew their delegates, and the rump
  parliament was dispersed by troops in June 1849.

**Austria's return.** Prussia then tried to lead a union of princes, the Erfurt Union. Austria, with Russian support,
forced Prussia to abandon it at **Olmütz (November 1850)**, and the old Confederation was restored. Prussian officers
and conservatives remembered Olmütz as a humiliation.

**The lesson:** unity needed military power and the defeat of Austria, which liberal speeches could not supply.

## Bismarck as Minister-President

In 1862 King William I faced a deadlock: the Prussian parliament, dominated by liberals, refused to pay for army
reforms planned by the war minister Roon. William appointed Bismarck Minister-President in September 1862. In his
"blood and iron" speech to a budget committee, Bismarck argued that great questions would be decided by force rather
than by speeches and majority votes. He collected taxes without parliament's approval and carried out the reforms. The
enlarged and modernised army, with Moltke as Chief of the General Staff, was the tool he would use.

## How did Bismarck bring about Austria's defeat of 1866?

1. **Schleswig-Holstein, 1864.** When the new Danish king, Christian IX, accepted a constitution binding Schleswig more
   closely to Denmark, Bismarck persuaded Austria to join Prussia in war. Denmark was defeated in 1864 and gave up both
   duchies to joint Austro-Prussian control.
2. **The Gastein Convention, 1865.** Prussia administered Schleswig and Austria administered Holstein, which lay inside
   Prussian-dominated north Germany. The arrangement created endless opportunities for quarrels.
3. **Isolating Austria.** Russia was grateful for Prussian help against the Polish rising of 1863. Napoleon III, met at
   Biarritz in 1865, appeared willing to stay neutral in the hope of gains for France. Italy signed an alliance in
   April 1866, promised Venetia. Britain stayed out of continental affairs.
4. **War, June 1866.** Bismarck accused Austria of breaking the Gastein Convention and proposed reforms of the
   Confederation that Austria could not accept. Most German states sided with Austria. Prussian railways, the
   breech-loading needle gun and Moltke's planning brought victory at **Königgrätz (Sadowa) on 3 July 1866**.

**Consequences.** The **Treaty of Prague** excluded Austria from German affairs and dissolved the Confederation.
Prussia annexed Schleswig-Holstein, Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau and Frankfurt and formed the **North German
Confederation** (1867). Bismarck insisted on lenient terms for Austria, so it would not seek revenge, and the Prussian
parliament passed an Indemnity Bill approving his earlier unauthorised spending, splitting the liberals. The southern
states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden and part of Hesse) stayed outside but signed secret military alliances with
Prussia.

## How did Bismarck bring about France's defeat of 1870?

- **Relations with France sour.** Napoleon III expected compensation for his neutrality in 1866 and was refused. His
  attempt to buy Luxembourg in 1867 was blocked, which humiliated him.
- **The Spanish succession.** In 1870 Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, a relative of the Prussian king, was
  offered the vacant Spanish throne. France feared encirclement, and the candidature was withdrawn on 12 July.
- **Ems.** France demanded a promise that it would never be renewed. William I refused politely at Ems and telegraphed
  an account to Bismarck, who edited and published it so that the exchange read as an insult to both sides.
- **War.** France declared war on 19 July 1870. The southern states joined Prussia under their alliances, as Bismarck
  had expected. At **Sedan (1–2 September 1870)** Napoleon III and his army were captured; Paris was besieged and
  surrendered in January 1871.
- **The Empire.** William I was proclaimed German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles on **18 January 1871**.
  The Treaty of Frankfurt (May 1871) gave Germany Alsace and much of Lorraine and imposed an indemnity on France.

## How far was Bismarck responsible?

| Bismarck's role | Other factors |
|---|---|
| Chose the moments for war in 1864, 1866 and 1870 | Prussia's economic strength, built through the Zollverein, coal, iron and railways |
| Isolated Austria and France diplomatically | Army reforms by Roon and Moltke's planning won the battles |
| Lenient peace with Austria; Indemnity Bill won over liberals | Mistakes by Austria and Napoleon III, who walked into wars without allies |
| Edited the Ems telegram to bring France into war | Nationalism in the south made Bavaria and others join a war against France |
| Negotiated the southern states into the Empire | Russia and Britain chose not to intervene |

Historians disagree whether Bismarck followed a long-term plan or exploited opportunities as they arose. The evidence
of 1864–66, when he worked with Austria before turning on it, and of 1870, when the Spanish candidature only became a
crisis by chance, suits the second view better: he had a clear aim, Prussian dominance of north Germany, and great
skill in using events.

## Annotated model answer: a (b) question

*Why was the Frankfurt Parliament unable to unify Germany? Explain your answer.*

> One reason is that the Parliament had no army of its own. **[Reason identified.]** When Denmark tried to absorb
> Schleswig in 1848, it had to rely on Prussian troops, and when Prussia signed the Malmö armistice without consulting
> it the Parliament was powerless. This showed that it could pass resolutions but not enforce them. **[Explained with
> specific evidence.]** A second reason is that it depended on the princes. When Frederick William IV refused the
> crown in April 1849, there was no other candidate strong enough, and the larger states recalled their members.
> **[Second reason, developed and linked to the outcome.]**

Two developed reasons are enough for the top level. Listing the "greater Germany" debate, the middle-class membership
and the king's refusal without explaining any of them would stay in the middle levels.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Zollverein as political unification. It was a customs union, not a state.
- Saying Austria lost territory to Prussia in 1866. It lost Venetia to Italy and its place in Germany, not Prussian
  annexations.
- Claiming Bismarck forged the Ems telegram. He edited a real report to change its tone.
- Forgetting that the southern states joined only in 1870–71.
- Writing "Bismarck unified Germany" as a narrative with no weighing of other factors in a (c) answer.

## Exam technique for 2147

- **Describe** (a): name specific events, such as the terms of the Gastein Convention or the Treaty of Prague.
- **Explain** (b): link each cause to the result. "This meant that..." is a useful bridge.
- **How far** (c): set Bismarck's diplomacy against Prussian strength and others' mistakes, and decide which mattered
  most with a reason.
- Paper 2 sources on Bismarck often come from his own memoirs, written after 1890. Ask whether he had reasons to
  present himself as a master planner.
