This set covers parts 15C (Carboxylic acids) and 15D (Carboxylic acid derivatives) of Topic 15 in the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry specification (YCH11), Issue 1, September 2017, outcomes 15.9 to 15.16. The content is International A2 (IA2); Pearson assesses it in Unit 4: Rates, Equilibria and Further Organic Chemistry. When stuck, return to the [carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-their-derivatives/) or the shorter [carboxylic acids revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-their-derivatives-revision-notes/). Use these values throughout. Relative atomic masses: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Cl = 35.5, Na = 23.0. Molar gas volume at RTP: 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Name each compound.

**(a)** (CH₃)₃CCOOH **[1]**
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COCl **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**2.** Use these values. Butanoic acid boils at 164 °C, pentan-1-ol at 138 °C and methyl propanoate at 80 °C. All three have Mr = 88.0.

**(a)** Explain, by comparing the forces between molecules, why the three compounds boil in this order. **[3]**
**(b)** Butanoic acid is miscible with water, but decanoic acid, C₉H₁₉COOH, is almost insoluble. Explain this difference. **[2]**

**3.** 2-methylpropanoic acid, (CH₃)₂CHCOOH, can be prepared in two ways.

**(a)** It can be made from 2-methylpropan-1-ol. Give the reagents and conditions, write an equation using [O], and state the colour change seen. **[4]**
**(b)** It can also be made by hydrolysing a nitrile. Name the nitrile and give the reagent and condition for the hydrolysis. **[2]**

**4.** 2.22 g of propanoic acid is added to excess sodium carbonate solution.

**(a)** Give a balanced equation for what happens. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of carbon dioxide produced at room temperature and pressure. **[3]**

**5.** This question is about 2-methylbutanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH.

**(a)** Name the organic product formed with lithium tetrahydridoaluminate(III), and state why water must be kept out of the reaction mixture. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction in (a), using [H] for the reducing agent. **[1]**
**(c)** Write an equation for the reaction with phosphorus(V) chloride and state what you would see. **[2]**

**6.** A student warms 3.08 g of 3-methylbutan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH, with excess ethanoic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid. 3.64 g of ester is collected.

**(a)** Name the ester and give its structural formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the yield is below 100% even when no product is lost. **[1]**

**7.** For each reaction of ethanoyl chloride, write an equation and name the organic product.

**(a)** with water **[2]**
**(b)** with 2-methylpropan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH **[2]**
**(c)** with concentrated ammonia **[2]**
**(d)** with methylamine, CH₃NH₂ **[2]**

**8.** Ethanoyl chloride is much more reactive towards nucleophiles than ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Explain this difference in reactivity. **[2]**
**(b)** State one advantage of making an ester from an acyl chloride rather than from a carboxylic acid. **[1]**

**9.** Methyl 2-methylpropanoate, (CH₃)₂CHCOOCH₃, is hydrolysed.

**(a)** Name the products when it is heated under reflux with dilute sulfuric acid. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for its hydrolysis by sodium hydroxide solution. **[1]**
**(c)** Hydrolysis with sodium hydroxide goes to completion. Explain why. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of 1.50 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide that reacts with 3.06 g of the ester. **[2]**

**10.** Terylene is a polyester used for clothing fibres.

**(a)** Name the type of polymerisation that forms Terylene and the small molecule lost when the monomers are a diol and a dicarboxylic acid. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the two monomers. **[2]**
**(c)** The repeat unit is –OC–C₆H₄–COO–CH₂CH₂–O–. Calculate its Mr, and the number of repeat units in a chain of Mr 57 600. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why Terylene is broken down by hot sodium hydroxide solution, but poly(ethene) is not. **[1]**

**11.** A chemist has a pineapple-scented ester, X, with molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₂. She refluxes X with excess sodium hydroxide solution and distils off an alcohol, B. Acidifying the residue releases a monoprotic carboxylic acid, A. In a titration, 0.352 g of A is neutralised by 16.00 cm³ of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of A and identify A. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the identity of B and name X. **[2]**
**(c)** X can be made from A in two steps through an acyl chloride. Give the reagent for each step and write an equation for the second step. **[3]**
**(d)** Give a simple chemical test that gives a positive result with A but not with X. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** **Propanoyl chloride** [1]
**(c)** **Propyl ethanoate** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (c) the alkyl word comes from the alcohol and is written first; "ethyl propanoate" is a different compound, so check which side of the ester oxygen each chain is on.

**2. (a)** Methyl propanoate has no O–H, so it has only London forces and permanent dipole–dipole forces between molecules [1]. Pentan-1-ol forms hydrogen bonds between molecules through its O–H group [1]. Butanoic acid forms hydrogen-bonded dimers using O–H and the C=O oxygen, so the most energy is needed to separate its molecules [1].
**(b)** The –COOH group forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules [1]. In decanoic acid the long hydrocarbon chain cannot hydrogen bond with water, so it does not mix [1].
*Examiner insight:* with equal Mr values, an answer that only says "bigger London forces" misses the point; name the force each substance has and say which needs most energy to overcome.

**3. (a)** Excess potassium dichromate(VI) acidified with dilute sulfuric acid [1]; heat under reflux [1]. (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH + 2[O] → (CH₃)₂CHCOOH + H₂O [1]. Orange to green [1].
**(b)** **2-methylpropanenitrile**, (CH₃)₂CHCN [1]; dilute hydrochloric acid, heated under reflux [1].
*Examiner insight:* "distil" in place of "reflux" describes how to collect the aldehyde, so it is unlikely to earn credit for the condition when the acid is the target.

**4. (a)** 2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂ [1]
**(b)** Mr(propanoic acid) = 74.0; n = 2.22 ÷ 74.0 = 0.0300 mol [1]. n(CO₂) = 0.0300 ÷ 2 = 0.0150 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0150 × 24 000 = **360 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the 2 : 1 ratio from the balanced equation is where most answers go wrong; check your equation before you use it, and give the unit asked for.

**5. (a)** **2-methylbutan-1-ol** [1]. LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water, so water must be excluded [1].
**(b)** CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH + H₂O [1]
**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH + PCl₅ → CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COCl + POCl₃ + HCl [1]. Steamy (misty) fumes [1].
*Examiner insight:* "white fumes" or "white smoke" alone describes a different observation; say steamy or misty fumes for HCl.

**6. (a)** **3-methylbutyl ethanoate** [1]; CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂ [1]
**(b)** Mr(C₅H₁₂O) = 88.0, n = 3.08 ÷ 88.0 = 0.0350 mol [1]. Mr(C₇H₁₄O₂) = 130.0, maximum mass = 0.0350 × 130.0 = 4.55 g [1]. Yield = 3.64 ÷ 4.55 × 100 = **80.0%** [1].
**(c)** The reaction is reversible, so an equilibrium mixture forms and not all the alcohol is converted [1].
*Examiner insight:* base the yield on the reagent not in excess (the alcohol), and show the maximum mass as a separate step.

**7. (a)** CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl [1]; **ethanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** CH₃COCl + (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH(CH₃)₂ + HCl [1]; **2-methylpropyl ethanoate** [1]
**(c)** CH₃COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + NH₄Cl [1]; **ethanamide** [1]
**(d)** CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]; **N-methylethanamide** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (c) and (d), use two molecules of ammonia or amine; an equation that releases HCl does not show the ammonium or alkylammonium salt that actually forms.

**8. (a)** In ethanoyl chloride, both the oxygen and the chlorine withdraw electrons from the carbonyl carbon, so it carries a larger δ+ charge and attracts nucleophiles more strongly [1]. Chloride is a good leaving group, so the C–Cl bond breaks readily [1].
**(b)** Any one: the reaction is not reversible, so the yield is higher; it is fast at room temperature; no acid catalyst is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* a vague "acyl chlorides are more reactive" only repeats the question; give the reasons, the size of the δ+ charge on the carbon and how easily Cl⁻ leaves.

**9. (a)** **2-methylpropanoic acid** [1] and **methanol** [1]
**(b)** (CH₃)₂CHCOOCH₃ + NaOH → (CH₃)₂CHCOONa + CH₃OH [1]
**(c)** Excess hydroxide converts the acid into its carboxylate ion, and that ion cannot react with methanol to re-form the ester [1].
**(d)** Mr(C₅H₁₀O₂) = 102.0; n = 3.06 ÷ 102.0 = 0.0300 mol, ratio 1 : 1 [1]. Volume = 0.0300 ÷ 1.50 = 0.0200 dm³ = **20.0 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* giving 2-methylpropanoic acid as a product of the alkaline reaction in (b) is wrong; with excess hydroxide the product is the sodium salt.

**10. (a)** Condensation polymerisation [1]; water [1]
**(b)** **Ethane-1,2-diol** [1] and **benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid** [1]
**(c)** C₁₀H₈O₄: Mr = 120.0 + 8.0 + 64.0 = **192.0** [1]. Number of repeat units = 57 600 ÷ 192.0 = **300** [1].
**(d)** The ester links in Terylene are hydrolysed by hydroxide ions, but the poly(ethene) chain contains only C–C and C–H bonds, which hydroxide ions do not attack [1].
*Examiner insight:* when a question says "name", give names rather than formulae, and include the positions 1,2 and 1,4 in full.

**11. (a)** n(NaOH) = 16.00 ÷ 1000 × 0.250 = 0.00400 mol = n(A) [1]. Mr = 0.352 ÷ 0.00400 = **88.0** [1]. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH: 14n + 46 = 88, n = 3, so A is **butanoic acid**, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH [1].
**(b)** X has six carbons and A has four, so B has two: B is **ethanol** [1]. X is **ethyl butanoate**, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ [1].
**(c)** Step 1: phosphorus(V) chloride, PCl₅ [1]. Step 2: ethanol at room temperature [1]. CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl [1].
**(d)** Add sodium hydrogencarbonate (or carbonate) solution: A gives effervescence of CO₂, X gives none [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b), link the carbon count to the evidence rather than just naming the alcohol.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the two halves of an ester name, as in "ethyl propanoate" for CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.
- Explaining boiling temperatures with London forces alone when the Mr values are the same.
- Writing "distil" when the product needed is the carboxylic acid.
- Writing 2[H] instead of 4[H] for reducing a carboxylic acid with LiAlH₄.
- Describing HCl as "white smoke" rather than steamy fumes.
- Showing HCl as a product with ammonia or an amine instead of the ammonium or alkylammonium salt.
- Giving the free acid when sodium hydroxide is the reagent; with excess alkali you get the sodium salt.
