> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the real exam's structure or mark tariffs. Answers use Germany as the model country after the Cold War.

**HL only.** These questions practise Regional study 12 in the Europe option (HL Paper 3). Revise first with the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-europe-cold-war-revision-notes/) and the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-europe-cold-war/).

## Questions

**1.** State two aims of the Marshall Plan. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the Berlin Blockade began in 1948. **[4]**

**3.** Read this short extract, constructed for practice.

> "We were told the wall was built to keep the fascists out. Yet the guns point inward, and my cousin in the West sends me letters that arrive opened. In school we learn that we are free; at home we whisper." (Constructed for practice, in the voice of an East Berliner in 1962.)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about life in East Germany. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse one limitation of the extract as evidence about East German society as a whole. **[3]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why the Soviet Union dominated Central and Eastern Europe after 1945. **[4]**

**5.** Analyse the reasons for the failure of the Prague Spring. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the reasons for the reunification of Germany in 1990. **[6]**

**7.** "The creation of the EEC was mainly the result of the Cold War." To what extent do you agree? Plan an answer: state your argument, three themes and a counter-argument. **[15]**

**8.** Compare the opposition to Soviet control in Yugoslavia and in Hungary. **[4]**

**9.** Discuss how historians disagree about responsibility for the origins of the Cold War in Europe. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: rebuild European economies; restore trade and stability; reduce the appeal of communism; create markets for American goods. One mark each. **[2]**

**2.** Up to 4. The Western powers merged their zones and introduced the Deutschmark in June 1948 (1); Stalin saw this as moving toward a separate West German state against Soviet interests (1); he hoped to force the Allies out of Berlin, or to block the new state, by cutting land access (1); the Soviets also wanted reparations and influence over the whole of Germany (1). **[4]**

**3. (a)** Up to 3: the official explanation, fascists, is doubted since the guns point inward (1); there is surveillance of mail (1); a gap between public claims of freedom and private whispers, which shows lack of free speech (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Up to 3: it is one person's view, and others, including committed party members, may have viewed the Wall differently (1); constructed to show a critical voice, but critical voices were less likely to speak openly (1); it does not mention benefits such as jobs, childcare and cheap housing (1). **[3]**

**4.** Up to 4, two reasons explained. The Red Army was present in eastern Europe at the end of the war and could support local communists (2); the USSR used rigged elections, secret police, show trials, COMECON and the Warsaw Pact to control politics, economics and defence (2). Accept: Stalin's wish for a buffer. **[4]**

**5.** Up to 6 using a markband approach. Soviet leaders feared that reform would spread and weaken the bloc (1); Warsaw Pact troops invaded in August 1968 (1); Dubček's reforms were seen as threatening party control (1); the Brezhnev Doctrine asserted the right to intervene (1); the West did not intervene, and Czechoslovak resistance was largely non-violent (1); a judgement on the main reason, such as the Soviet willingness to use force (1). **[6]**

**6.** Up to 6: the East German economy was weak and its government lost legitimacy (1); Gorbachev refused to use force and let reform spread (1); Hungary opened its border, and mass emigration and Leipzig protests followed (1); the fall of the Wall made reunification hard to resist (1); Kohl pushed quick unity, with US support, and the Two Plus Four Treaty settled external questions (1); a judgement on the most important factor (1). **[6]**

**7.** Credit a plan with these features, 3 marks each, 15 in all. Understanding of the claim: the EEC's founding in 1957 and the six states (3). Cold War factors: US support through the Marshall Plan, fear of the Soviet threat, desire to bind West Germany into Western Europe (3). Other factors: reconciliation between France and Germany, the Schuman Plan and ECSC, economic recovery and the wish to prevent future war (3). Perspectives: historians who stress the Cold War or Atlantic context against those who stress European initiatives and national interests (3). Counter-argument and judgement: for example that the Cold War provided the conditions, while European leaders made the choices (3). **[15]**

**8.** Up to 4. Both challenged Moscow's control (1); Tito, as a leader with his own partisan base, broke with Stalin in 1948 and kept power (1); Hungary's revolt in 1956 under Nagy tried to leave the Warsaw Pact and was crushed by Soviet troops (1); a judgement on why Yugoslavia succeeded, such as geography, lack of Soviet troops and Western support (1). **[4]**

**9.** Up to 6: the traditional view blames Soviet expansion and Stalin's control of eastern Europe (2); the revisionist view stresses American economic aims and the Marshall Plan (2); post-revisionist views stress mutual misunderstanding and security fears (1); a reasoned judgement or explanation of why historians differ, for example access to Soviet archives (1). **[6]**
