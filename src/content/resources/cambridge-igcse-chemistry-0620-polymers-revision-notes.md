Condensed recall for **11.8 Polymers**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). Worked examples are in the [polymers study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-polymers/); exam-style questions are in the [polymers practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-polymers-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcomes 1–5 (definition, poly(ethene), plastics, disposal, environmental challenges) are Core. Outcomes 6–13 (repeat units, condensation polymers, nylon, PET, proteins) are Supplement, marked *(Extended)*.

## Core facts

- **Polymer:** a large molecule built up from many smaller molecules called **monomers**.
- **Poly(ethene)** is made by **addition polymerisation** of **ethene** monomers.
- **Plastics** are made from polymers.
- Plastics are **non-biodegradable**, which creates disposal problems.
- Environmental challenges: **landfill**, **accumulation in oceans**, **toxic gases from burning**.

## Addition polymerisation

```
n CH₂=CH₂ → —[CH₂—CH₂]ₙ—
```

- Monomer must have a **C=C**.
- The C=C opens; monomers join in a chain.
- The **polymer is the only product**.

## Plastics: properties and problems

| Challenge | Cause | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Landfill | non-biodegradable | plastics stay buried for a very long time; landfill space fills up |
| Oceans | durable, light, not broken down | waste builds up; animals swallow it or get trapped |
| Burning | carbon-containing polymers | toxic gases form, such as carbon monoxide when oxygen is limited |

## Repeat units of addition polymers *(Extended)*

Method: take the two C=C carbons → make the bond single → add a bond extending out from each → brackets and *n*. Other groups become **side branches**.

| Monomer | Repeat unit |
|---|---|
| ethene, CH₂=CH₂ | —[CH₂—CH₂]— |
| propene, CH₂=CHCH₃ | —[CH₂—CH(CH₃)]— |
| but-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | —[CH₂—CH(C₂H₅)]— |
| but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | —[CH(CH₃)—CH(CH₃)]— |

Reverse method: two main-chain carbons of the repeat unit → put back C=C → remove extending bonds.

## Condensation polymerisation *(Extended)*

- Each monomer has **two functional groups**.
- Each link formed releases **one water molecule**.

| Polymer | Monomers | Linkage | Named example |
|---|---|---|---|
| polyamide | dicarboxylic acid + **diamine** | **amide** –CO–NH– | **nylon** |
| polyester | dicarboxylic acid + **diol** | **ester** –CO–O– | **PET** |

Structures (□ = carbon chain block):

```
nylon:  —[CO—□—CO—NH—□—NH]—
PET:    —[CO—□—CO—O—□—O]—
```

**PET** can be **converted back into monomers and re-polymerised**. The full name of PET is not required.

## Addition vs condensation *(Extended)*

| Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|
| monomers have C=C | monomers have two functional groups |
| one product only | polymer + water |
| repeat unit has the same atoms as the monomer | repeat unit has fewer atoms than the monomers (water lost) |

## Proteins *(Extended)*

- **Natural polyamides**.
- Monomers: **amino acids**, H₂N–CH(R)–COOH, where **R** is a side-chain that varies.
- Linkage: **amide**, –CO–NH–, formed with loss of water.

```
—NH—CH(R)—CO—NH—CH(R′)—CO—NH—CH(R″)—CO—
```

## Working back to monomers *(Extended)*

| Given | Do this | Result |
|---|---|---|
| addition repeat unit | restore C=C between the two main-chain carbons; remove extending bonds | the alkene monomer |
| polyamide chain | cut each –CO–NH–; add –OH to CO ends and H to NH ends | HOOC–□–COOH and H₂N–■–NH₂ |
| polyester chain | cut each –CO–O–; add –OH to CO ends and H to O ends | HOOC–□–COOH and HO–■–OH |
| protein chain | cut each –CO–NH–; add –OH and H as above | amino acids, H₂N–CH(R)–COOH |

## Answer frames

- **Describe the formation of poly(ethene):** "Many ethene molecules join together; the C=C double bond in each breaks and the molecules link into a long chain. This is addition polymerisation and poly(ethene) is the only product."
- **Describe an environmental challenge:** name the challenge, then the property that causes it: "Plastics accumulate in oceans because they are non-biodegradable."
- **Compare polymerisation types** *(Extended)*: one sentence per difference, mentioning both types each time.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monomer | small molecule that joins with others to form a polymer |
| Addition polymerisation | monomers with C=C join; polymer is the only product |
| Non-biodegradable | not broken down by microorganisms |
| Repeat unit *(Extended)* | smallest section of the polymer chain that repeats |
| Condensation polymerisation *(Extended)* | monomers join with the loss of a small molecule such as water |
| Linkage *(Extended)* | the group of atoms joining monomer units: amide or ester |

## Exam traps

- A repeat unit has **no C=C** and **two extending bonds**.
- Side groups (CH₃, C₂H₅) **branch off** the chain; they are not part of it.
- Condensation always produces **water** too.
- Amide = **–CO–NH–** (nylon, proteins); ester = **–CO–O–** (PET).
- "Pollution" alone is not a named environmental challenge.
- Plastics do not "rot" or "dissolve" in landfill.

## Self-test

1. Define a polymer.
2. Name the monomer used to make poly(ethene).
3. State the type of polymerisation used to make poly(ethene).
4. Explain why plastics cause problems in landfill sites.
5. Name a toxic gas that can form when plastics burn.
6. *(Extended)* Draw the repeat unit of poly(propene).
7. *(Extended)* A polymer has the repeat unit —[CH(CH₃)—CH(CH₃)]—. Name the monomer.
8. *(Extended)* Name the two types of monomer used to make a polyester.
9. *(Extended)* Name the linkage in nylon and give its structure.
10. *(Extended)* State one difference between addition and condensation polymerisation.
11. *(Extended)* Name the monomers that make up proteins.
12. *(Extended)* State what can be done with PET that allows it to be recycled into new PET.

**Answers:**

1. A large molecule built up from many smaller molecules called monomers.
2. Ethene.
3. Addition polymerisation.
4. They are non-biodegradable, so they do not decompose and take up space for a very long time.
5. Carbon monoxide.
6. —[CH₂—CH(CH₃)]—, with the CH₃ as a side branch and bonds extending from each main-chain carbon.
7. But-2-ene.
8. A dicarboxylic acid and a diol.
9. Amide linkage, –CO–NH–.
10. Addition gives only the polymer; condensation also gives a small molecule such as water. (Or: addition monomers have C=C; condensation monomers have two functional groups.)
11. Amino acids.
12. It can be converted back into its monomers and re-polymerised.
