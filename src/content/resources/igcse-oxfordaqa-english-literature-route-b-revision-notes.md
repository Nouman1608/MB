Route B of OxfordAQA International GCSE English Literature (9275) replaces Route A's unseen-prose section
with coursework. After Paper 1 (Prose and drama, 40%), Route B students sit **Paper 2b: Poetry** (1 hour
30 minutes, open book, 60 marks, 40%), with one 30-mark question on the *People and Places* anthology and
one 30-mark question on an unseen poem, and complete a **non-exam assessment** (30 marks, 20%): one
extended response to a whole prose fiction text that is **not** on the set-text list. For the full
explanation, use the
[Route B study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-literature-route-b-poetry-and-non-exam-assessment-study-guide/).

## Route A and Route B compared

| | Route A (entry 9275 A) | Route B (entry 9275 B) |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Prose and drama | 1 h 30, closed book, 60 marks, 40% | Same |
| Second exam | Paper 2a: Poetry and unseen texts, 2 h 15, open book, 90 marks, 60% | **Paper 2b: Poetry, 1 h 30, open book, 60 marks, 40%** |
| Unseen prose | Section C of Paper 2a (30 marks) | **Not taken** |
| Coursework | None | **NEA, 30 marks, 20%** |
| Total scaled mark | 150 | 150 |

## 3.3.1 Poetry: Paper 2b Section A (30 marks)

- Study the OxfordAQA Exams poetry anthology ***People and Places***: 20 poems, thematically linked, from
  different times and contexts.
- Answer **one question from a choice of two**.
- The paper is **open book**, so you can refer to the anthology, but you need to know the poems well
  enough to select evidence quickly.
- For the detailed anthology method, see the
  [Poetry and Unseen Texts revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-english-literature-poetry-unseen-revision-notes/),
  which cover the same anthology for Route A.

## 3.3.2 Unseen poetry: Paper 2b Section B (30 marks)

- Answer **one question on a previously unseen poem**.
- The specification asks you to experience a wide range of poetry so you can "closely analyse poems" and
  "analyse and compare key features such as their content, theme, structure and use of language".

Quick method: situation and speaker → shifts and structure → language and imagery → themes and ideas →
your personal interpretation.

## 3.3.3 Non-exam assessment (30 marks)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Task | One extended response to a prose fiction text of your choice |
| Text | A **substantial whole prose fiction text** |
| Exclusion | Must **not** come from any prescribed set text for 9275 |
| Range | Any literary period, from the literary canon of the British Isles or seminal world literature |
| Standard | Similar in quality, scope and challenge to the Paper 1 set texts |
| Marking | Internally assessed, using the same assessment objectives |

The Paper 1 prose set texts, which you **cannot** use for the NEA, are *Great Expectations*, *To Kill a
Mockingbird*, *Purple Hibiscus*, *Never Let Me Go*, *The Book Thief* and the OxfordAQA Exams Short Stories
Anthology.

## Assessment objectives

| AO | Wording | Paper 2b | NEA | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Understanding of, and engagement with, themes, ideas and contexts | 16% | 8% | 40% |
| AO2 | Analysis of how writers create meanings and effects | 16% | 8% | 40% |
| AO3 | Informed, personal responses, using appropriate terminology and coherent, accurate writing | 8% | 4% | 20% |

## NEA levels (30 marks)

| Level | Marks | Descriptor | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 26–30 | Convincing, critical analysis and exploration | Judicious, precise references; analysis of methods; "critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text" |
| 5 | 21–25 | Thoughtful, developed consideration | Apt references integrated; examination of methods; possibly alternative interpretations |
| 4 | 16–20 | Clear understanding | Effective references; clear explanation of methods; focused response |
| 3 | 11–15 | Explained, structured comments | Relevant references; identification of effects |
| 2 | 6–10 | Supported, relevant comments | Some awareness of implicit ideas |
| 1 | 1–5 | Simple, explicit comments | Narrative or descriptive approach |

The word **"whole text"** appears in every level from Level 3 upwards, so the essay must range across the
novel, not one chapter.

## Choosing an NEA text and task

| Good choice | Why |
|---|---|
| *Jane Eyre* (Charlotte Brontë) | Substantial, canonical, rich in themes of independence, class and religion |
| *Frankenstein* (Mary Shelley) | Layered narrative structure gives scope for AO2 |
| *Things Fall Apart* (Chinua Achebe) | Seminal world literature, strong contextual links for AO1 |

A good task asks **how** the writer presents an idea across the whole text: "How does Charlotte Brontë
present Jane's search for independence in *Jane Eyre*?"

## Worked example: a Level 5–6 NEA paragraph

"Brontë makes Jane's independence a matter of speech before it is a matter of money. At Gateshead the
child who has been silenced bursts out against Mrs Reed, and the novel's first-person narration lets the
reader feel that outburst as a victory. Later, at Thornfield, the same voice insists to Rochester that she
is his equal in soul, so the novel's structure repeats the pattern of a woman finding words before she
finds freedom. Read in the context of a period in which a governess had little social status, this
insistence becomes a challenge to the class and gender order."

AO1 (theme and context), AO2 (narrative voice, structure) and AO3 (argued, personal response) work
together, and the paragraph refers across the whole text.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Choosing a set text for the NEA**: it must not be on the prescribed list.
- **Choosing a short or slight text**: it must be substantial and similar in challenge to the set texts.
- **Writing about part of the novel**: the levels ask for a response to the "whole text".
- **Retelling**: Level 1 is "narrative and/or descriptive in approach".
- **Treating open book as no revision**: you still need to know the anthology poems.
- **Comparing unprompted in the unseen**: answer the question set on the one unseen poem.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Route B include unseen prose?**
No. Route B replaces Route A's unseen-prose section with the non-exam assessment. Route B students still
answer an unseen poetry question on Paper 2b.

**Can my NEA text be a translated novel?**
The specification allows "seminal world literature" as well as the literary canon of the British Isles,
provided the text is similar in quality, scope and challenge to the Paper 1 set texts. Check with your
teacher, who selects the text.

**How much is the NEA worth?**
30 marks and 20% of the International GCSE, with AO1 8%, AO2 8% and AO3 4%.

**Is Paper 2b open book?**
Yes. Paper 2b: Poetry is an open-book exam lasting 1 hour 30 minutes.
