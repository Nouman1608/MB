Condensed notes for **2.2 Motivation** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, 2026–2028). For worked diagnosis of a motivation problem and a model answer, use the [Motivation study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-motivation-cambridge-alevel-business/); for exam-style questions, use the [Motivation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-motivation-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/).

## What 2.2 asks you to handle

- **2.2.1**: why motivation is needed to achieve objectives.
- **2.2.2**: human needs and whether work satisfies them.
- **2.2.3**: Taylor, Mayo, Maslow, Herzberg, McClelland (content theories); Vroom (process theory).
- **2.2.4**: theories in practice; eight payment methods; nine non-financial motivators; employee participation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The desire to put effort into a task or job |
| Human need | Something a person must have, or strongly wants, to feel secure or fulfilled |
| Content theory | Explains *what* motivates people |
| Process theory | Explains *how* people decide how much effort to give |
| Hygiene factor | Herzberg: a factor that causes dissatisfaction if poor but does not motivate for long |
| Motivator | Herzberg: a factor that creates satisfaction and effort |
| Expectancy | Vroom: belief that effort leads to performance |
| Instrumentality | Vroom: belief that performance leads to a reward |
| Valence | Vroom: how much the reward is valued |
| Job enrichment | Adding responsibility and decision-making to a job |
| Empowerment | Giving staff authority to decide how to do their work |

## Symptoms of poor motivation

High labour turnover, high absence, falling quality, more complaints, low productivity, few suggestions. Use these as **evidence** in a case.

## The theories in one line each

- **Taylor**: pay linked to output; break jobs into simple timed tasks; manager plans, worker does.
- **Mayo**: attention, belonging and group norms raise output.
- **Maslow**: five levels; a lower need must be reasonably met before the next motivates.
- **Herzberg**: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction.
- **McClelland**: achievement, affiliation and power needs vary between individuals.
- **Vroom**: effort depends on expectancy × instrumentality × valence; if one is zero, so is motivation.

## How they connect

- Taylor (economic person) **vs** Mayo (social person).
- Herzberg's hygiene factors ≈ Maslow's lower levels; motivators ≈ esteem and self-actualisation.
- Maslow: satisfied need stops motivating. Herzberg: hygiene factors never truly motivate.
- McClelland explains **individual differences** Maslow ignores.
- Vroom is the **evaluation tool**: find the weak link in any scheme.

## Limitations for evaluation

- **Taylor**: ignores social needs; quality may suffer.
- **Mayo**: one workplace; output may have risen because workers knew they were being observed.
- **Maslow**: order not fixed; hard to measure; cultural differences.
- **Herzberg**: based on professional staff; pay may motivate the low-paid.
- **McClelland**: needs hard to measure.
- **Vroom**: assumes rational calculation.

## Matching pay to the job

| Job feature | Method |
|---|---|
| Countable output, checkable quality | Piece rate |
| Individual sales | Commission, bonus |
| Team output | Team bonus, profit sharing |
| Professional, hard to measure | Salary, performance-related pay |
| Scarce skills | Fringe benefits |

Avoid piece rates where quality or safety matters, and commission where customers need impartial advice.

## Non-financial motivators and their theory links

- **Training, development, promotion** → Maslow's esteem and self-actualisation; Herzberg's advancement.
- **Status** → esteem; may cause resentment.
- **Job re-design** → variety; reduces boredom.
- **Job enrichment** → Herzberg's responsibility and the work itself.
- **Team working** → Mayo; McClelland's affiliation.
- **Empowerment and participation** → responsibility, recognition; needs training and trust.

## Levels of participation

Consulted (suggestion schemes, team briefings) → share decisions (works councils, worker directors) → share ownership (employee share schemes, co-operatives). Deeper participation: more commitment, slower decisions.

## Cost check

Always weigh a motivator's cost against its benefit, such as savings from lower labour turnover (leavers saved × cost per replacement).

## Exam traps

- Theory without application to the case earns little.
- Maslow and Herzberg are **not** the same theory.
- Job enrichment adds **responsibility**, not just more tasks.
- Profit sharing rewards the **whole workforce**, not individual effort.
- Do not say money "always" or "never" motivates; it depends on the workforce.
- In Vroom, a big reward does not help if staff do not believe performance will earn it.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between a content theory and a process theory.
2. Give two symptoms of poor motivation.
3. What is the main difference between Taylor's and Mayo's view of workers?
4. Which of Maslow's levels do Herzberg's motivators most closely match?
5. Name McClelland's three needs.
6. In Vroom's theory, what happens to motivation if instrumentality is zero?
7. Give one limitation of Herzberg's theory.
8. Why might piece rates be unsuitable for a pharmacy technician?
9. What is the difference between job re-design and job enrichment?
10. Give one benefit and one drawback of worker directors.
11. Staff turnover falls from 50 to 35 leavers a year. Each replacement costs $2 500. What is the annual saving?
12. Which payment method suits a team whose output cannot be traced to individuals?

**Answers**

1. A content theory explains what motivates people; a process theory explains how people decide how much effort to give.
2. Any two: high labour turnover, high absence, falling quality, more complaints, low productivity.
3. Taylor saw workers as mainly motivated by pay; Mayo stressed social needs such as attention and belonging.
4. Esteem and self-actualisation.
5. Achievement, affiliation and power.
6. It falls to zero, because staff do not believe good performance will be rewarded.
7. For example, it was developed from interviews with professional staff, so pay may motivate low-paid workers more than it suggests.
8. Accuracy and safety matter more than speed; paying per item could encourage rushing and errors.
9. Job re-design is any change to the tasks in a job; job enrichment specifically adds responsibility and decision-making.
10. Benefit: staff views reach the board, raising commitment. Drawback: decisions may be slower, or the influence may be small in practice.
11. 15 × 2 500 = $37 500.
12. A team bonus or profit sharing.
