These questions cover section M1.4 (Momentum and impulse) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2, in International AS Unit PSM1. All motion is in a straight line. Use g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Read the [momentum and impulse study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-momentum-and-impulse/) first, or refresh the methods with the [momentum and impulse revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-momentum-and-impulse-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A lorry of mass 8000 kg moves along a straight road at 12 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of its momentum. **[1]**

**2.** A particle of mass 0.5 kg moving at 8 m s⁻¹ is brought to rest by a constant force in 0.25 s.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the impulse on the particle. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the force. **[1]**

**3.** Two trolleys, of masses 1.5 kg and 2.5 kg, are held at rest with a compressed spring between them. When the spring is released, the 2.5 kg trolley moves at 1.2 m s⁻¹. Find the speed and direction of the 1.5 kg trolley. **[3]**

**4.** A ball of mass 0.3 kg moves perpendicular to a fixed smooth wall. It hits the wall at 10 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 6 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted by the wall on the ball. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the momentum of the ball is not conserved in the impact. **[1]**

**5.** Particle A, of mass 0.2 kg, moves at 3 m s⁻¹. It collides directly with particle B, of mass 0.1 kg, moving at u m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction. The particles coalesce and are at rest immediately after the collision. Show that u = 6. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Particle A, of mass 3 kg, moves at 4 m s⁻¹. It collides directly with particle B, of mass m kg, moving at 2 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction. The particles coalesce and move at 1 m s⁻¹ in the original direction of A.

**(a)** Find m. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the impulse exerted on B, stating its direction. **[2]**

**7.** Particle A (0.4 kg) moves at 5 m s⁻¹ and particle B (0.6 kg) moves at 2 m s⁻¹ in the same direction along the same line. A catches up with B and they collide. Immediately after the collision B moves at 3.8 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity of A immediately after the collision. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that the impulses on A and B are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. **[2]**

**8.** A ball of mass 0.25 kg is dropped from rest at a height of 3.6 m above a horizontal floor. It rebounds vertically with speed 4.2 m s⁻¹. Model the ball as a particle.

**(a)** Find the speed of the ball just before it hits the floor. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the impulse exerted by the floor on the ball. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the height the ball reaches after the bounce. **[2]**

**(d)** State one modelling assumption you have made in part (a), other than that the ball is a particle. **[1]**

**9.** A particle of mass 2 kg moves in a straight line at 3 m s⁻¹. A constant force of 5 N acts on it, opposite to its direction of motion, for 2 s.

**(a)** Find the velocity of the particle at the end of the 2 s. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the time after the force starts to act at which the particle is momentarily at rest. **[2]**

**10.** On a smooth horizontal line, particle A (2 kg) moves at 5 m s⁻¹ towards particle B (1 kg), which is at rest. After the collision A continues in the same direction at 2 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity of B after the collision. **[2]**

**(b)** B then hits a fixed smooth wall at right angles and rebounds at 3 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted by the wall on B. **[2]**

**(c)** B then collides with A and the particles coalesce. Find their common velocity. **[3]**

**11.** Particle P, of mass 5 kg, moves to the right at 2 m s⁻¹. Particle Q, of mass 3 kg, moves to the left at 4 m s⁻¹ along the same line. They collide, and immediately afterwards Q moves to the right at 1 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the speed of P after the collision and state its direction. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted by P on Q. **[2]**

**12.** A tennis ball of mass 0.06 kg travels horizontally at 25 m s⁻¹. It is hit by a racket and returns along the same line at 35 m s⁻¹. The ball is in contact with the racket for 0.015 s.

**(a)** Find the magnitude of the impulse on the ball. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the average force exerted on the ball. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the greatest force on the ball during contact is larger than your answer to part (b). **[1]**

**13.** Particle A, of mass 2 kg, is projected at 7 m s⁻¹ along a horizontal surface. A constant resistance of 3 N opposes its motion. After travelling 8 m, A collides with particle B, of mass 3 kg, which is at rest. The particles coalesce.

**(a)** Find the speed of A just before the collision. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the speed of the combined particle just after the collision. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Momentum = mv = 8000 × 12 = **96 000 N s** (kg m s⁻¹) [1]. Total: 1

**2. (a)** Impulse = m(v − u) = 0.5(0 − 8) [1] = −4, so the magnitude is **4 N s** [1].

**(b)** F = 4 ÷ 0.25 = **16 N** [1]. Total: 3

**3.** The total momentum is zero before release, so it is zero after [1]. Taking the 2.5 kg trolley's direction as positive: 0 = 2.5(1.2) + 1.5v [1], so v = −2. The 1.5 kg trolley moves at **2 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction** to the other trolley [1]. Total: 3

**4. (a)** Away from the wall positive: impulse = 0.3(6) − 0.3(−10) [1] = **4.8 N s** [1].

**(b)** The wall is fixed and exerts an external impulse on the ball, so the ball's momentum changes [1]. Total: 3

**5.** A's direction positive: total momentum before = 0.2 × 3 − 0.1u, and after = 0, so 0.6 − 0.1u = 0 [1]. Hence 0.1u = 0.6 and **u = 6** [1]. Total: 2

**6. (a)** A's direction positive. Before: 3(4) + m(−2) = 12 − 2m [1]. After: (3 + m)(1) [1].
12 − 2m = 3 + m, so 3m = 9 [1], giving **m = 3** [1].

**(b)** Impulse on B = 3(1) − 3(−2) [1] = **9 N s in the original direction of A** [1]. Total: 6

**7. (a)** Before: 0.4(5) + 0.6(2) = 3.2 [1]. After: 0.4v + 0.6(3.8) = 0.4v + 2.28 [1].
0.4v = 0.92, so **v = 2.3 m s⁻¹ in the original direction** [1].

**(b)** Impulse on A = 0.4(2.3 − 5) = −1.08 N s; impulse on B = 0.6(3.8 − 2) = +1.08 N s [1]. These are equal in size, 1.08 N s, and opposite in sign, so opposite in direction [1]. Total: 5

**8. (a)** v² = 0² + 2(9.8)(3.6) = 70.56 [1], so **v = 8.4 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Up positive: impulse = 0.25(4.2) − 0.25(−8.4) [1] = **3.15 N s upwards** [1].

**(c)** 0 = 4.2² − 2(9.8)h [1], so h = 17.64 ÷ 19.6 = **0.9 m** [1].

**(d)** Any one: air resistance is negligible; the only force while the ball falls is its weight; g is constant at 9.8 m s⁻² [1]. Total: 7

**9. (a)** Original direction positive. Impulse = −5 × 2 = −10 N s [1].
2v − 2(3) = −10 [1], so v = −2: the particle moves at **2 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction** to its original motion [1].

**(b)** The impulse needed to stop it is 2 × 3 = 6 N s [1], so 5t = 6 and **t = 1.2 s** [1]. Total: 5

**10. (a)** Towards the wall positive: 2(5) + 0 = 2(2) + 1v [1], so **v = 6 m s⁻¹ towards the wall** [1].

**(b)** Impulse = 1(−3) − 1(6) = −9 [1], so the magnitude is **9 N s** [1].

**(c)** A moves at +2 and B at −3 [1]. 2(2) + 1(−3) = 3V [1], so **V = 1/3 m s⁻¹ (0.333 m s⁻¹) towards the wall** [1]. Total: 7

**11. (a)** Right positive. Before: 5(2) + 3(−4) = −2 [1]. After: 5v + 3(1) [1].
5v + 3 = −2, so v = −1 [1]. P moves at **1 m s⁻¹ to the left** [1].

**(b)** Impulse on Q = 3(1) − 3(−4) [1] = **15 N s** [1]. Total: 6

**12. (a)** Return direction positive: impulse = 0.06(35) − 0.06(−25) [1] = 0.06 × 60 = **3.6 N s** [1].

**(b)** Average force = 3.6 ÷ 0.015 [1] = **240 N** [1].

**(c)** The force varies during the contact, rising from zero to a peak and falling again, so its greatest value exceeds its average [1]. Total: 5

**13. (a)** Deceleration = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m s⁻² [1]. v² = 7² − 2(1.5)(8) = 49 − 24 = 25 [1], so **v = 5 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** 2(5) + 3(0) = 5V [1], so **V = 2 m s⁻¹** [1]. Total: 5

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a rebound velocity with the same sign as the approach velocity.
- Applying conservation of momentum to a ball and a fixed wall or floor.
- Giving an impulse or a velocity with no direction.
- Finding the impulse on the wrong particle when the question says "exerted by P on Q".
- Using a speed in a momentum equation where a signed velocity is needed.
- Forgetting the 1.5 m s⁻² deceleration before a collision in a multi-stage question.
