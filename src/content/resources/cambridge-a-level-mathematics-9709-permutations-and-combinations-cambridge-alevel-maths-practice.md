## Section A

**1.** Find the number of different arrangements of the letters of the word DECIMAL, and the number of these that begin with a vowel. **[3]**

**2.** A team of 4 is chosen from 9 people. Find the number of different teams that include one particular person. **[2]**

**3.** Find the number of different arrangements of the letters of the word BALLOON. **[2]**

**4.** Six people stand in a line. Find the number of arrangements in which two particular people, A and B, stand next to each other. **[2]**

**5.** Five boys and four girls stand in a line. Find the number of arrangements in which boys and girls alternate. **[2]**

**6.** A committee of 3 is chosen from 6 men and 4 women. Find the number of committees that contain exactly one woman. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The nine letters of the word SEVENTEEN are arranged in a line.

**(a)** Find the number of different arrangements.

**(b)** Find the number of arrangements in which the four Es are together.

**(c)** Find the number of arrangements that begin and end with E.

**(d)** Find the number of arrangements in which no two Es are next to each other. **[9]**

**8.** A committee of 5 is chosen from 7 men and 5 women.

**(a)** Find the number of committees with at least 2 women.

**(b)** One of the men, M, refuses to serve. Find the number of committees with at least 2 women that do not include M. **[6]**

**9.** Eight people sit in two rows of four seats.

**(a)** Find the number of possible seating arrangements.

**(b)** Find the number of arrangements in which two particular people, P and Q, both sit in the front row.

**(c)** Find the number of arrangements in which P and Q sit in the same row. **[5]**

**10.** A box contains 5 red, 4 blue and 3 green balls, each with a different number on it. Four balls are chosen.

**(a)** Find the number of possible selections.

**(b)** Find the number of selections that contain at least one ball of each colour.

**(c)** Find the number of selections that contain no green ball. **[6]**

**11.** Four-digit numbers are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8. No digit may be used more than once.

**(a)** Find how many of these numbers are greater than 5000.

**(b)** Find how many of these numbers are even and greater than 5000. **[5]**

**12.** Three letters are chosen from the six letters of the word BANANA and arranged in a line. Find the number of different arrangements that can be made. **[5]**

**13.** Explain why the following method for question 8(a) is wrong: "Choose 2 women in ⁵C₂ ways, then any 3 of the remaining 10 people in ¹⁰C₃ ways, giving 1200." **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 7! = **5040** [1]. Three vowels (E, I, A) for the first letter, then 6! for the rest [1]: 3 × 720 = **2160** [1] **[3]**

**2.** The particular person is included; choose 3 more from 8 [1]: ⁸C₃ = **56** [1] **[2]**

**3.** 7! ÷ (2! × 2!) [1] = **1260** [1] **[2]**

**4.** Block AB with 4 others: 5! arrangements, × 2 for BA [1]: **240** [1] **[2]**

**5.** The only pattern is B G B G B G B G B [1]; 5! × 4! = **2880** [1] **[2]**

**6.** ⁴C₁ × ⁶C₂ [1] = 4 × 15 = **60** [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** S, E × 4, V, N × 2, T: 9! ÷ (4! × 2!) [1] = **7560** [1]

**(b)** Block EEEE with S, V, N, N, T: 6 objects, 6! ÷ 2! [1] = **360** [1]

**(c)** Middle 7 letters S, V, N, N, T, E, E: 7! ÷ (2! × 2!) [1] = **1260** [1]

**(d)** Arrange S, V, N, N, T: 5! ÷ 2! = 60 [1]; choose 4 of the 6 gaps: ⁶C₄ = 15 [1]; 60 × 15 = **900** [1] **[9]**

**8. (a)** Total ¹²C₅ = 792 [1]; subtract 0 women ⁷C₅ = 21 and 1 woman 5 × ⁷C₄ = 175 [1]; **596** [1]

**(b)** Now 6 men and 5 women: ¹¹C₅ = 462 [1]; subtract ⁶C₅ = 6 and 5 × ⁶C₄ = 75 [1]; **381** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* in (b), adding the cases 2W, 3W, 4W, 5W gives 10 × 20 + 10 × 15 + 5 × 6 + 1 = 381, a useful check.

**9. (a)** 8! = **40 320** [1]

**(b)** P and Q take 2 of 4 front seats: ⁴P₂ = 12 [1]; others: 6! = 720; **8640** [1]

**(c)** Both in front or both in back: 2 × 8640 [1] = **17 280** [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** ¹²C₄ = **495** [1]

**(b)** One colour appears twice [1]: 2R: ⁵C₂ × 4 × 3 = 120 [1]; 2B: 5 × ⁴C₂ × 3 = 90 [1]; 2G: 5 × 4 × ³C₂ = 60; total **270** [1]

**(c)** Choose from the 9 non-green balls: ⁹C₄ = **126** [1] **[6]**

**11. (a)** First digit 5, 7 or 8: 3 ways [1]; remaining three places: ⁵P₃ = 60, so **180** [1]

**(b)** Last digit 8: first digit 5 or 7, middle ⁴P₂ = 12, giving 24 [1]. Last digit 2: first digit 5, 7 or 8, giving 3 × 12 = 36 [1]. Total **60** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the cases split on the last digit because 8 can be used as either the first or last digit, but not both.

**12.** Letters B, A × 3, N × 2. All different (B, A, N): 3! = 6 [1]. A pair of As with B or N: 2 × 3 = 6 [1]. A pair of Ns with B or A: 2 × 3 = 6 [1]. Three As: 1 [1]. Total **19** [1] **[5]**

**13.** A committee with three or more women is counted more than once [1], because different choices of the "first two" women lead to the same committee [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Repeated letters not divided out, or divided out when the objects are distinct.
- "No two together" answered with the complement of "all together".
- The internal order of a block of distinct people forgotten.
- At-least problems double counted by choosing the compulsory people first.
- Digit problems with overlapping cases, where a digit is allowed in two restricted places at once.
