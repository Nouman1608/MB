These questions cover sub-topics 2(a) **Level of organisation** (point 2.1) and 2(c) **Biological molecules** (points 2.7–2.14B) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3, for the June and November series examined under Issue 3. The course is untiered and a calculator may be used on both papers. Question 9 tests the bold **B** statement 2.14B and is labelled "Paper 2 only".

## Questions

**1.** State the level of organisation of each of these structures.

**(a)** A chloroplast **[1]**
**(b)** The layer of ciliated epithelium lining the trachea **[1]**
**(c)** A kidney **[1]**

**2.** A student writes: "The small intestine is a tissue because it is made of cells." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**3.** A sample of a biological molecule is analysed. It contains carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and sulfur.

**(a)** Identify the type of biological molecule. **[1]**
**(b)** Give a reason why it cannot be a lipid. **[1]**

**4.** Starch and glycogen are both storage carbohydrates.

**(a)** State the smaller units that both molecules are made from. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one difference between starch and glycogen in where they are found. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the structure of a lipid molecule in terms of its smaller units. **[1]**

**5.** A student tests four solutions, A to D.

| Solution | Benedict's test | Iodine test | Biuret test | Emulsion test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Brick-red | Orange-brown | Blue | Clear |
| B | Blue | Blue-black | Blue | Clear |
| C | Blue | Orange-brown | Purple | Cloudy white |
| D | Blue | Orange-brown | Blue | Clear |

**(a)** State the biological molecule or molecules present in A, B and C. **[3]**
**(b)** The student concludes that D contains no sugar. D is a sucrose solution. Explain why the student's conclusion is not valid. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how you would test a sample of crushed peanut for fat. Include one safety precaution. **[3]**

**7.** Amylase catalyses the breakdown of starch.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by the statement that amylase is a biological catalyst. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why amylase does not catalyse the breakdown of protein. **[2]**

**8.** A student investigates the effect of temperature on amylase. She records the time for all the starch to be digested, using iodine solution on a spotting tile. Each temperature is repeated three times.

| Temperature (°C) | 15 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean time (s) | 400 | see (a) | 80 | 100 | 500 |

**(a)** The three times at 25 °C were 196 s, 204 s and 290 s. Calculate the mean time at 25 °C, leaving out the anomalous result. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate of reaction at 35 °C. Use the formula rate = 1 / time and give the unit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times faster the reaction is at 35 °C than at 15 °C. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the rate at 55 °C is much lower than at 35 °C. **[3]**
**(e)** State one variable the student should control, other than the volume of amylase. **[1]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) A student measures the rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction at four pH values.

| pH | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (s⁻¹) | 0.002 | 0.008 | 0.020 | 0.005 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate at pH 5 as a percentage of the rate at pH 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe a method the student could use to investigate the effect of pH on the activity of amylase. **[5]**

**10.** Enzyme P has an optimum pH of 2. Enzyme Q has an optimum pH of 7. Both are found in the human digestive system.

**(a)** Suggest which enzyme works in the stomach. Give a reason. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why enzyme P has very little activity at pH 7. **[2]**

**11.** Two tubes are set up at 37 °C. Tube X contains starch solution and amylase. Tube Y contains starch solution and amylase that had been boiled and then cooled to 37 °C. After 10 minutes both tubes are tested.

| Tube | Iodine test | Benedict's test |
|---|---|---|
| X | Orange-brown | Orange |
| Y | Blue-black | Blue |

**(a)** Explain the results for tube X. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the results for tube Y. **[2]**
**(c)** In a separate experiment, the starch concentration in a tube falls from 2.0 g/dm³ to 0.5 g/dm³. Calculate the percentage of the starch that has been digested. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Organelle [1]
**(b)** Tissue [1]
**(c)** Organ [1]
*Examiner insight:* One word per part is enough; adding "a group of cells" to (b) is fine, but "cell" for (b) scores zero because epithelium is many similar cells.

**2.** The small intestine contains several different tissues, such as muscle and epithelium [1]; a group of different tissues working together is an organ, not a tissue [1]
*Examiner insight:* "It is made of cells" is true of every level above the cell, so the mark needs the word "different" or named tissues.

**3. (a)** **Protein** [1]
**(b)** Lipids contain only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, so a molecule with nitrogen cannot be a lipid [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming nitrogen as the deciding element is what earns (b); "it has more elements" is too vague.

**4. (a)** **Glucose** (simple sugar) [1]
**(b)** Starch is stored in plants; glycogen is stored in animals (and fungi) [1]
**(c)** Glycerol joined to fatty acids (usually three) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both units are needed in (c); "fatty acids" alone is incomplete and loses the mark.

**5. (a)** A: glucose [1]; B: starch [1]; C: protein and fat [1]
**(b)** Benedict's solution does not give a positive result with sucrose [1]; so a blue Benedict's result does not show that no sugar is present -- the student has not tested for sucrose [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), C needs both molecules for the mark; giving only one of protein or fat scores zero for C.

**6.** Shake the crushed peanut with ethanol, then pour the ethanol into a tube of water [1]; a cloudy white emulsion shows fat is present [1]; keep ethanol away from flames because it is flammable [1]
*Examiner insight:* The colour change must be stated as "cloudy" or "milky white emulsion"; "it changes colour" earns nothing.

**7. (a)** It speeds up the breakdown of starch [1]; without being used up or changed, and it is a protein made by living cells [1]
**(b)** The active site of amylase has a shape complementary only to starch [1]; protein molecules do not fit the active site, so no enzyme-substrate complex forms [1]
*Examiner insight:* (b) needs "active site" and "shape"; "amylase only works on starch" restates the question and gains no credit.

**8. (a)** (196 + 204) ÷ 2 [1] = **200 s** [1]
**(b)** 1 ÷ 80 [1] = **0.0125 s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** 400 ÷ 80 = **5 times faster** [1]
**(d)** At 55 °C the bonds holding the amylase in shape break [1]; the active site changes shape, so the enzyme is denatured [1]; starch no longer fits the active site, so fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form [1]
**(e)** Any one: pH (using a buffer); concentration or volume of starch solution; concentration of amylase; time between samples [1]
*Examiner insight:* Including 290 s in the mean in (a) loses the answer mark, and in (b) the unit s⁻¹ is needed for the second mark.

**9. (a)** 0.008 ÷ 0.020 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
**(b)** Add a buffer solution of a set pH to starch solution and amylase [1]; keep all tubes in the same water bath at one temperature [1]; mix and take one drop every 30 s onto iodine on a spotting tile [1]; record the time when the iodine stays orange-brown [1]; repeat for a range of pH values (for example 4 to 9), repeating each and calculating a mean [1]
*Examiner insight:* A method that varies pH but never states that temperature is kept constant caps the plan, because temperature becomes an uncontrolled variable.

**10. (a)** P, because the stomach contains acid, so its pH is low [1]
**(b)** At pH 7 the active site of P changes shape (is altered) [1]; so the substrate no longer fits and few enzyme-substrate complexes form [1]
*Examiner insight:* (a) needs the reason; naming P without linking it to the acidic stomach earns nothing.

**11. (a)** No starch remains, because the amylase has broken it down [1]; into sugar, which gives a positive Benedict's result [1]
**(b)** Boiling denatured the amylase, changing the shape of its active site [1]; so starch was not broken down and no sugar was produced [1]
**(c)** (2.0 − 0.5) ÷ 2.0 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), cooling back to 37 °C does not restore the enzyme; an answer that says the amylase "recovers" contradicts the denaturation mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling an organ "a group of cells" rather than "a group of different tissues".
- Naming a single cell when the question asks for a tissue.
- Giving only one of the two units of a lipid.
- Stating a positive food test result without the starting colour when asked to "describe the change".
- Treating a negative Benedict's test as proof that no sugar of any kind is present.
- Saying an enzyme is "killed", or saying the substrate does not fit without saying the active site changed shape.
- Including an anomalous repeat in a mean.
- Leaving off the unit (s⁻¹) on a rate, or dividing the wrong way in a percentage.
- In the pH practical (Paper 2 only), forgetting that temperature must now be controlled.
