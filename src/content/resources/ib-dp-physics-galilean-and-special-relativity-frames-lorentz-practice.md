These questions cover **sub-topic A.5, Galilean and special relativity**, from the IB *Physics guide*, first assessment 2025. All of A.5 is additional higher level content, so every question is **HL only**. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-physics-galilean-and-special-relativity-frames-lorentz/) first and condense with the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-physics-galilean-and-special-relativity-frames-lorentz-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** Frame S′ moves at 8.0 m/s along +x relative to frame S. An event has x = 120 m and t = 5.0 s in S.

**(a)** Use the Galilean transformations to find x′ and t′. **[2]**
**(b)** A runner moves at 11.0 m/s along +x in S. Find the runner's velocity in S′. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** State what is meant by Galilean relativity. **[1]**
**(b)** State the two postulates of special relativity. **[2]**
**(c)** A lamp in S emits a light pulse along +x. S′ moves at 0.50c along +x. State the speed of the pulse in S′ according to Galilean addition and according to special relativity, and say which agrees with the second postulate. **[2]**

**3.** An unstable particle has a proper mean lifetime of 26 ns and moves at 0.92c in the laboratory.

**(a)** Calculate γ. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the mean lifetime measured in the laboratory. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the distance it travels in the laboratory frame in that time. **[1]**
**(d)** State which of the two lifetimes is a proper time interval and why. **[1]**

**4.** A rocket has proper length 150 m and passes a station at 0.85c.

**(a)** Find the length measured from the station. **[2]**
**(b)** State the length of the rocket measured by the pilot. **[1]**

**5.** A star is 6.0 light-years from Earth in the Earth frame. A ship travels there at a constant 0.80c.

**(a)** Find the journey time in the Earth frame. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the journey time measured on the ship, and identify which time is the proper time interval. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the Earth-star distance in the ship frame. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that your answers to (b) and (c) are consistent. **[1]**

**6.** Frame S′ moves at 0.40c along +x relative to S. An event has x = 6.0 × 10⁸ m and t = 3.0 s in S.

**(a)** Calculate γ. **[1]**
**(b)** Find x′. **[2]**
**(c)** Find t′. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that (cΔt)² − (Δx)² takes the same value in both frames, measuring from the origin event. **[2]**

**7.** Relative to Earth, spacecraft A moves at 0.75c along +x and spacecraft B moves at 0.60c along −x.

**(a)** Find the velocity of B in the frame of A. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why Galilean addition gives an impossible answer. **[1]**

**8.** In frame S, two events are 8.0 μs apart in time and 1440 m apart in space.

**(a)** Calculate the space-time interval Δs. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the proper time between the events. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the speed of the frame in which the events happen at the same place. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why no frame exists in which these events are simultaneous. **[1]**

**9.** Two lightning strikes are simultaneous in S and 450 m apart along x. Frame S′ moves at 0.80c along +x.

**(a)** Find Δt′, the time of the larger-x strike minus the time of the smaller-x strike, giving its sign. **[3]**
**(b)** State which strike S′ observers say happened first, and explain the answer. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** A particle moves at 0.80c. Find the angle between its world line and the ct axis. **[2]**
**(b)** A world line makes 30.0° with the ct axis. Find the speed as a fraction of c. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the ct′ and x′ axes of a frame moving at 0.80c are drawn relative to ct and x. **[2]**
**(d)** State why the scales along ct′ and x′ differ from those along ct and x. **[1]**

**11.** Muons form 12 km above the ground (Earth frame) and move at 0.995c. Their proper mean lifetime is 2.2 μs.

**(a)** Find the distance a muon would travel in one proper lifetime if there were no time dilation. **[2]**
**(b)** Find γ, the lifetime in the Earth frame, and the corresponding distance. **[3]**
**(c)** In the muon frame, find the contracted thickness of the 12 km layer and the time to cross it. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why (b) and (c) support special relativity. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** x′ = x − vt = 120 − 8.0 × 5.0 [1] = **80 m**, and t′ = t = **5.0 s**. [1]
**(b)** u′ = u − v = 11.0 − 8.0 = **3.0 m/s**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both coordinates in (a); a candidate who finds only x′ has answered half the question.

**2. (a)** Newton's laws of motion hold in the same form for observers in every inertial frame. [1]
**(b)** The laws of physics take the same form in every inertial frame. [1] The speed of light in a vacuum is the same for all inertial observers, regardless of source or observer motion. [1]
**(c)** Galilean: c − 0.50c = **0.50c**. Special relativity: **c**. [1] The relativistic result agrees with the second postulate. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Postulate 2 loses credit if it omits "all inertial observers" or "vacuum"; say who measures the speed.

**3. (a)** γ = 1/√(1 − 0.92²) = **2.55**. [1]
**(b)** Δt = γΔt₀ = 2.55 × 26 = **66 ns**. [1]
**(c)** d = 0.92 × 3.00 × 10⁸ × 66.3 × 10⁻⁹ = **18 m**. [1]
**(d)** The 26 ns is proper, because it is measured by a clock at rest with the particle, so both decay events happen at one place. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Justify "proper" by the events being at the same place in that frame; naming the smaller number alone is weaker.

**4. (a)** γ = 1.898 [1], L = 150/1.898 = **79 m**. [1]
**(b)** The pilot is at rest relative to the rocket, so measures the proper length **150 m**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) rewards recognising that the proper length is read in the rest frame, not computing again.

**5. (a)** t = 6.0/0.80 = **7.5 years**. [1]
**(b)** The ship's clock is present at both events, so its time is proper: t₀ = 7.5/γ with γ = 1.667 [1], giving **4.5 years**. [1]
**(c)** L = 6.0/1.667 [1] = **3.6 light-years**. [1]
**(d)** In the ship frame, 3.6 ly at 0.80c takes 3.6/0.80 = **4.5 years**, equal to (b). [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs the check written out with numbers, not just a statement that they agree.

**6. (a)** γ = 1/√(1 − 0.16) = **1.09**. [1]
**(b)** vt = 0.40 × 3.00 × 10⁸ × 3.0 = 3.6 × 10⁸ m [1]; x′ = 1.091 × (6.0 − 3.6) × 10⁸ = **2.6 × 10⁸ m**. [1]
**(c)** vx/c² = 0.40 × 2.0 s = 0.80 s [1]; t′ = 1.091 × (3.0 − 0.80) = **2.4 s**. [1]
**(d)** In S: (9.0 × 10⁸)² − (6.0 × 10⁸)² = 4.5 × 10¹⁷ m². [1] In S′: (7.2 × 10⁸)² − (2.62 × 10⁸)² = 5.19 × 10¹⁷ − 0.69 × 10¹⁷ = 4.5 × 10¹⁷ m², the same. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values into (d); early rounding of γ can make the two intervals differ in the third figure.

**7. (a)** Take S′ as A's frame, so v = +0.75c, and u = −0.60c. [1] u′ = (−0.60 − 0.75)c/(1 − (−0.60)(0.75)) = −1.35c/1.45 [1] = **−0.93c**, so B approaches A at 0.93c. [1]
**(b)** Galilean addition gives 1.35c, which exceeds the speed of light. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the signed substitution into the equation; a wrong sign for u gives a speed that is easy to challenge, and the working shows where it went wrong.

**8. (a)** cΔt = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁶ = 2400 m. [1] Δs² = 2400² − 1440² = 3.69 × 10⁶ m². [1] Δs = **1920 m**. [1]
**(b)** Δt₀ = Δs/c = 1920/3.00 × 10⁸ = **6.4 μs**. [1]
**(c)** v = Δx/Δt = 1440/2400 = **0.60c**. [1]
**(d)** Δs² is positive and invariant. In a frame where the events were simultaneous, Δs² would equal −(Δx′)², which cannot be positive. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) needs the invariance link; saying "nothing can go faster than light" alone does not use the interval.

**9. (a)** γ = 1.667 [1]; vΔx/c² = 0.80 × 450/(3.00 × 10⁸) = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ s [1]; Δt′ = −γvΔx/c² = **−2.0 μs**. [1]
**(b)** The negative sign means the larger-x strike occurs first in S′. [1] Time depends on position in t′ = γ(t − vx/c²), so equal t in S gives different t′. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State which event is first and tie it to the sign; a number without direction is incomplete.

**10. (a)** tan θ = v/c = 0.80 [1], θ = **38.7°**. [1]
**(b)** tan θ = v/c [1], so v/c = tan 30.0° = **0.577**. [1]
**(c)** ct′ is the world line of the S′ origin, at 38.7° to ct. [1] x′ is tilted up by the same angle from x, towards the 45° light line. [1]
**(d)** The scales are fixed by lines of constant space-time interval. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Measure θ from the ct axis, not x; the speed is greater than c if tan is read from the wrong axis.

**11. (a)** d = 0.995 × 3.00 × 10⁸ × 2.2 × 10⁻⁶ [1] = **660 m**. [1]
**(b)** γ = **10.0** [1]; Δt = 22 μs [1]; d = 0.995c × 22.0 μs ≈ **6.6 km**. [1]
**(c)** L = 12 km/10.0 = **1.2 km** [1], crossed in 1.2 km/(0.995c) = **4.0 μs**. [1]
**(d)** Without relativity a muon would cover only 660 m, yet muons reach the ground; time dilation (Earth frame) and length contraction (muon frame) both explain this, so the observations support both effects. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep frames separate: dilation belongs in the Earth frame and contraction in the muon frame, not both in one argument.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Galilean addition once the speeds are a sizeable fraction of c.
- Swapping which frame gives the proper time or proper length.
- Dropping the sign of v or u in the velocity addition and Lorentz equations.
- Rounding γ early, then failing to reproduce an invariant interval.
- Writing "time slows" without naming the clock and the observer.
- Treating x′ as perpendicular to ct′ on a space-time diagram.
- Measuring the world-line angle from the x axis.
- Leaving out units or mixing light-years, metres and seconds in one line.
