## Section A (practise using any unseen poem)

**1.** Choose a short unseen poem. Explore how the poet presents their central idea or feeling, referring closely to language, form and structure. **[20]**

## Section B (practise using two poems from your own anthology)

**2.** Compare how two poems from your studied anthology present a shared theme (e.g. loss, conflict, identity, nature). **[30]**

## Section C (practise using your own studied prose text)

**3.** Explore how the writer of your studied modern prose text presents the development of a central character across the novel. **[40]**

---

## Answers (worked approach — marks depend on your chosen texts)

**1.** A top-band response identifies the central idea precisely [4], then builds a sustained analysis of **language** (imagery, word choice, sound), **form** (rhyme, meter, stanza shape) and **structure** (how the poem develops or shifts) [12], with every point rooted in a short, precisely selected quotation and a clear "effect" explained, not just named [4].

**2.** The strongest answers organise **by point of comparison**, not poem-by-poem (e.g. "both poets use natural imagery, but Poem A does X while Poem B does Y") [10], analyse language/form/structure in each poem with precision [12], and make the comparison **explicit and sustained** throughout rather than only in a closing paragraph [8].

**3.** Track the character across **at least three distinct points** in the novel (beginning, a turning point, and the ending or a late development) [16], using specific, well-chosen textual evidence at each point [12], and integrate relevant **context** — social, historical, cultural — that illuminates why the character develops as they do, rather than adding context as a disconnected final paragraph [12].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Section B answers that analyse each poem in a separate half rather than comparing directly, point by point.
- Section C answers that retell the plot instead of tracking character or theme development with a clear argument.
- Context in Section C treated as a bolt-on paragraph rather than woven into the analysis.
- Uneven time management — remember the marks are not equal across sections (20/30/40).
