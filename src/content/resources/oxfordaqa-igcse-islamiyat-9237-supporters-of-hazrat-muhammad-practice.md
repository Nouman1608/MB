## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** The helpers [1].

**2.** Uhud [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: shared their homes; shared food; paired with a Muhajir as a brother; offered a share of property or orchards; protected the Prophet (pbuh) as promised at Aqaba.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: bravery; sacrifice; generosity; early faith; loyalty to the Prophet (pbuh).

**5.** Any four, [1] each: the Prophet's camel knelt near his house [1]; he carried the Prophet's luggage inside [1]; the Prophet (pbuh) stayed with him while the mosque was built [1]; for about seven months [1]; he showed respect by offering the upper floor [1]; the Prophet (pbuh) chose the lower floor for visitors [1]; he later fought in campaigns for Islam [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Strength and protection** [1]: he was a respected man of the Quraysh, so the Quraysh knew the Muslims had a powerful defender [1].
- **Loyalty of family** [1]: the Prophet's own uncle supported him when others of his family did not [1].
- **Encouragement to others** [1]: his conversion made it easier for other people to accept Islam [1].
- **A fighter for Islam** [1]: he fought bravely at Badr and was martyred at Uhud [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two lessons, the lesson identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Lessons (AO1):

- **Generosity** [1]: they shared homes and wealth with strangers, so Muslims should give to those in need [1].
- **Putting others first** [1]: they preferred the migrants over themselves, a model of ithar [1].
- **Brotherhood in faith** [1]: they treated the migrants as family, showing that faith is stronger than tribe or race [1].
- **Keeping promises** [1]: they kept their pledge to protect the Prophet (pbuh) [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "they love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given, but give them preference over themselves" (Qur'an 59:9) [2].
- "The believers are but brothers" (Qur'an 49:10) [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the hadith shows two companions competing in giving, and Abu Bakr gave everything.
- **For the statement:** the Ansaar shared their homes and wealth with the migrants.
- **Against the statement:** courage was also vital, as with Hamza (RA) and Talha (RA) in battle, and loyalty, as with Abu Ayyub (RA).
- **Against the statement:** the passage shows trust in Allah and the Prophet (pbuh) as the deeper source of Abu Bakr's generosity.
- **Balanced view:** generosity was a main quality, but it came from faith and was matched by courage and loyalty.

*Use of the passage*

- "half of my wealth" and "everything he had" show levels of giving.
- "Allah and his messenger" shows that his trust and faith were the basis of the generosity.
- "I will never do better than Abu Bakr" shows humility and respect among the companions.
- "ordered us to give charity" shows that giving was a response to the Prophet's call.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Talha shielded the Prophet at Uhud** [1]: he used his body and hand to take blows and arrows when the Prophet (pbuh) was attacked [1].
- **Talha spent his wealth** [1]: he gave generously to the poor and the community [1].
- **Zubair was an early believer** [1]: he accepted Islam as a youth and faced hardship, including migration [1].
- **Zubair fought in many battles** [1]: he volunteered for dangerous tasks, and the Prophet (pbuh) called him his hawari [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allah" (Qur'an 33:23) [2].
- "And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger - those will be with the ones upon whom Allah has bestowed favour" (Qur'an 4:69) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Ansaar gave the Prophet (pbuh) a home, protection and a community when Makkah had rejected him.
- **For the statement:** the companions fought, gave their wealth and learned and passed on the Qur'an and the Sunnah.
- **Against the statement:** Muslims believe the mission's success came from Allah, and the Qur'an says that victory comes only from Allah.
- **Against the statement:** the Prophet (pbuh) also showed leadership, patience and trust that did not depend on people.
- **Balanced view:** Allah decreed success and the companions were the means; their support was needed for the way it unfolded.

*Use of the passage*

- "ordered us to give charity" shows the companions responding as a community to the Prophet's leadership.
- "everything he had" shows total sacrifice, which supports the idea that the mission rested on such commitment.
- "Allah and his messenger" shows that their support was driven by trust in Allah, not by a human need.
