## Formulas to learn

Do not expect to be given these in the exam.

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Total variable costs | Variable cost per unit × output |
| Total costs | Fixed costs + total variable costs |
| Revenue | Selling price × quantity sold |
| Profit (or loss if negative) | Revenue − total costs |
| Total profit from an investment | Total return − cost of investment |
| Average annual profit | Total profit ÷ number of years |
| Average rate of return | Average annual profit ÷ cost of investment × 100 |
| Margin of safety | Actual or planned output − break-even output |

The specification states that you will **not** be expected to draw break-even charts or use the break-even formula. You must interpret charts and identify break-even output and margin of safety.

## Core recall

### Fixed or variable?

| Usually fixed | Usually variable |
|---|---|
| Rent, business rates | Raw materials, ingredients |
| Insurance | Packaging |
| Salaries | Delivery charge per item |
| Loan interest | Commission per sale |

Test: if output doubles, does the cost double? Yes → variable. No → fixed.

### ARR in four steps

Example: a van costs £30,000 and returns £9,000, £11,000, £12,000 and £13,000 over four years.

1. Total return = £45,000.
2. Total profit = £45,000 − £30,000 = £15,000.
3. Average annual profit = £15,000 ÷ 4 = £3,750.
4. ARR = £3,750 ÷ £30,000 × 100 = **12.5%**.

Judge ARR against: other investments, the interest rate on borrowing, and the business's target. Remember the returns are forecasts.

### Break-even chart: what to look for

| Feature | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Flat horizontal line |
| Total costs | Starts at fixed costs, rises with output |
| Revenue | Starts at zero, rises with output |
| Break-even output | Where revenue meets total costs; read down to the output axis |
| Loss | Area left of break-even, total costs above revenue |
| Profit | Area right of break-even, revenue above total costs |
| Margin of safety | Distance on the output axis from break-even to actual output |

### Effect of changes

| Change | Break-even output |
|---|---|
| Selling price up | Falls |
| Selling price down | Rises |
| Fixed costs up | Rises |
| Variable cost per unit up | Rises |
| Costs down | Falls |

### Value of break-even

**Useful for:** setting sales targets, testing a business plan, supporting a loan application, testing "what if" changes, showing risk through the margin of safety.

**Limited because:** assumes all output is sold; assumes constant price and variable cost; relies on forecasts; ignores demand; fixed costs can change.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Costs that do not change with output in the short run |
| Variable costs | Costs that change directly with output |
| Total costs | Fixed costs plus total variable costs |
| Revenue | Money received from sales: price × quantity |
| Profit | Revenue minus total costs, when positive |
| Loss | When total costs are greater than revenue |
| Investment | Spending now on assets such as machinery, buildings or vehicles to earn returns later |
| Average rate of return | Average annual profit from an investment as a percentage of its cost |
| Break-even output | The output at which total revenue equals total costs |
| Margin of safety | The amount by which output exceeds break-even output |

## Exam traps

- Multiply variable cost per unit by output before adding fixed costs.
- In ARR, subtract the cost before averaging, and divide by the cost, not by revenue or total return.
- State margin of safety in units if output is in units.
- Read break-even where revenue meets **total** costs, not fixed costs.
- A higher price lowers break-even on paper but might reduce demand.
- Show every step; method marks are available even if a final figure is wrong.

## Self-test

1. Define fixed costs and give one example.
2. A business makes 2,000 units with variable cost £1.50 each and fixed costs of £5,000. Calculate total costs.
3. At a price of £5, what is the revenue from 2,000 units?
4. Using questions 2 and 3, calculate profit.
5. A machine costs £25,000 and returns £35,000 in total over 5 years. Calculate the ARR.
6. Why should ARR be compared with the interest rate on a loan?
7. Where is break-even output on a break-even chart?
8. Break-even output is 1,800 units and the business sells 2,300. What is the margin of safety?
9. What happens to break-even output if fixed costs rise?
10. What happens to break-even output if the selling price rises?
11. State two limitations of break-even analysis.
12. Will you be asked to draw a break-even chart in AQA GCSE Business?

### Answers

1. Costs that do not change with output in the short run, such as rent or insurance.
2. 2,000 × £1.50 = £3,000; £3,000 + £5,000 = **£8,000**.
3. 2,000 × £5 = **£10,000**.
4. £10,000 − £8,000 = **£2,000** profit.
5. (£35,000 − £25,000) ÷ 5 = £2,000; £2,000 ÷ £25,000 × 100 = **8%**.
6. If the ARR is lower than the interest rate, the investment earns less than the borrowing costs.
7. Where the revenue line crosses the total cost line.
8. 2,300 − 1,800 = **500 units**.
9. It rises.
10. It falls.
11. Any two of: assumes all output is sold; assumes price and variable cost per unit stay constant; based on forecasts; ignores demand; fixed costs may change.
12. No. You interpret charts and identify break-even and margin of safety.

## Frequently asked questions

**What investments does the AQA GCSE Business specification mention?**
Machinery, buildings and vehicles, for which you may be asked to calculate the average rate of return.

**What does a large margin of safety tell a business?**
Sales could fall a long way before the business makes a loss, so it faces less risk.
