These questions cover sub-topic 2(f) Acids, alkalis and titrations of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 2.28 to 2.33C. Questions marked **(Paper 2 only)** test the titration point 2.33C. Revise with the [acids, alkalis and titrations study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-alkalis-and-titrations/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-alkalis-and-titrations-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the colour of phenolphthalein in an alkaline solution. **[1]**

**2.** State the colour of methyl orange in an alkaline solution. **[1]**

**3.** Classify each solution using the pH scale.

**(a)** pH 11 **[1]**
**(b)** pH 4 **[1]**
**(c)** pH 7 **[1]**

**4.** Name the ion that alkalis produce in aqueous solution. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why universal indicator gives only an approximate pH value. **[1]**

**6.** Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for the reaction between an acid and an alkali. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student tests three solutions with litmus and with phenolphthalein.

| Solution | Litmus | Phenolphthalein |
|---|---|---|
| P | Red | Colourless |
| Q | Blue | Pink |
| R | Purple (no change) | Colourless |

**(a)** Identify the alkaline solution. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the acidic solution and explain why the phenolphthalein result alone would not show it is acidic. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest the pH of R. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how universal indicator paper could be used to compare the pH of vinegar and of a soap solution. Give the colours and the pH classifications you would expect if vinegar has pH 3 and the soap solution has pH 10. **[4]**

**9.** Potassium hydroxide solution is added a little at a time to dilute nitric acid containing universal indicator, until the alkali is in excess.

**(a)** Describe the colour changes seen. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of ions, why the pH increases. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the salt formed. **[1]**

**10.** **(Paper 2 only)** A student carries out a titration to find the volume of dilute sulfuric acid that neutralises 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution, using methyl orange.

**(a)** Name the piece of apparatus used to measure the 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution. **[1]**
**(b)** State the colour change at the end point. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the burette is rinsed with the acid rather than with water before it is filled. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the acid is added drop by drop near the end point. **[1]**
**(e)** The student leaves an air bubble in the tip of the burette, and it comes out during the titration. State and explain the effect on the recorded titre. **[2]**

**11.** **(Paper 2 only)** The student's burette readings are shown.

| | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading / cm³ | 23.60 | 22.95 | 45.55 | 22.65 |
| Initial reading / cm³ | 0.20 | 0.00 | 22.95 | 0.10 |

**(a)** Calculate the titre for each run. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the concordant titres and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the rough titre is not included in the mean. **[1]**

**12.** **(Paper 2 only)** Describe how to carry out an acid-alkali titration to find the volume of hydrochloric acid that exactly neutralises 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Pink [1].

**2.** Yellow [1].

**3. (a)** Strongly alkaline [1].
**(b)** Weakly acidic [1].
**(c)** Neutral [1].

**4.** Hydroxide ion, OH⁻ [1].

**5.** The colours change gradually and are matched by eye against a chart [1].

**6.** H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l): correct equation [1]; state symbols [1].

**7. (a)** Q [1].
**(b)** P [1]. Phenolphthalein is colourless in neutral solutions as well as in acids, so colourless does not prove acidity; the red litmus does [1].
**(c)** 7 (neutral) [1].

**8.** Dip a clean glass rod in each solution and touch it on a separate piece of universal indicator paper [1]. Compare each colour with the pH colour chart [1]. Vinegar: red/orange, strongly acidic [1]. Soap solution: blue, weakly alkaline [1].
*Marking note:* pH 3 is "strongly acidic" and pH 10 is "weakly alkaline" in this specification's bands; other band names lose the classification marks.

**9. (a)** Starts red (acid) [1]; turns green at neutral [1]; becomes blue then purple when alkali is in excess [1].
**(b)** Hydroxide ions from the alkali react with hydrogen ions to form water [1], so the concentration of H⁺ ions decreases (and then OH⁻ ions are in excess) [1].
**(c)** Potassium nitrate [1].

**10. (a)** Pipette (with a pipette filler) [1].
**(b)** Yellow to orange [1].
**(c)** Water left in the burette would dilute the acid, making the titre too large [1].
**(d)** So the end point is not overshot / so the exact volume is found [1].
**(e)** The titre would be too large [1], because the burette reading changes as the bubble leaves without that volume of acid entering the flask [1].

**11. (a)** Rough 23.40; run 1 22.95; run 2 22.60; run 3 22.55 cm³: all four correct [2]; two or three correct [1].
**(b)** Runs 2 and 3 (22.60 and 22.55 cm³) are concordant; run 1 is 0.35 cm³ higher and is left out [1]. Mean = (22.60 + 22.55) ÷ 2 = 22.575 = 22.58 cm³ [1].
**(c)** The rough titre is only a guide to the approximate end point and is usually overshot [1].
*Marking note:* Averaging all three runs gives 22.70 cm³ and loses both marks in (b).

**12.** One mark per point, up to 6:
- Use a pipette and filler to put 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution into a conical flask [1].
- Add a few drops of a single indicator such as phenolphthalein [1].
- Fill a burette with hydrochloric acid (rinsed with the acid first) and record the initial reading at eye level [1].
- Add acid while swirling, with the flask on a white tile, adding drop by drop near the end point [1].
- Stop when the indicator changes colour permanently (pink to colourless) and record the final reading; titre = final − initial [1].
- Repeat until concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) are obtained and calculate the mean [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "clear" instead of colourless.
- Misnaming the pH bands, especially at pH 3, 4, 10 and 11.
- Including the rough titre in the mean.
- Averaging titres that are not concordant.
- Forgetting the white tile, swirling or dropwise addition when describing the method.
