## Questions

**1.** Which function of money is shown when a student compares the prices of two laptops? **[1]**
A) Medium of exchange  B) Unit of account  C) Store of value  D) Means of deferred payment

**2.** Explain what is meant by money as a means of deferred payment, using an example. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why barter is less efficient than using money. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why money is defined as more than notes and coins. **[2]**

**5.** Prices rise by 8% over a year. Calculate the value, in today's prices, of £500 kept in cash for that year, to the nearest pound. Explain what this shows about money as a store of value. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** agents in the UK financial sector. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how a building society differs from a commercial bank. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** roles of the Bank of England. **[4]**

**9.** Bank K pays savers 2.5% and lends to firms at 6.5%. It lends 40 million units. Calculate the bank's interest income minus its interest payments on this lending. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how high street banks help firms to invest. **[3]**

**11.** "The financial sector is essential to economic growth in the UK." Evaluate this statement. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Money allows goods to be bought now and paid for later because debts are agreed in money terms [1], e.g. repaying a mortgage over 25 years [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Barter needs a double coincidence of wants [1]; each person must have what the other wants at the same time [1], so finding trading partners takes time and many trades do not happen [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Most money is held as bank deposits [1], which can be spent by card or transfer, so deposits count as money as well as cash [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 500 ÷ 1.08 = **£463** [1]. The cash buys fewer goods than before [1], so inflation weakens money as a store of value [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Any two: the Bank of England [1]; commercial banks [1]; building societies [1]; insurance companies [1]. **[2]**

**7.** A building society is owned by its members (savers and borrowers) [1], while a commercial bank is owned by shareholders [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Any two developed: sets the Bank Rate [1] to influence interest rates and meet the inflation target [1]; keeps the financial system stable [1] by supervising banks so depositors' money is safe [1]; issues banknotes [1] in England and Wales [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Income: 0.065 × 40 million = 2.6 million; payments: 0.025 × 40 million = 1.0 million [1]; difference = **1.6 million units** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Banks collect savers' deposits [1] and lend them to firms [1], so firms can buy machinery or premises before they have the full amount, raising output and jobs [1]. **[3]**

**11.** *For:*
- Banks move savings to firms that want to invest [1], raising productive capacity and growth [1].
- Loans and mortgages let households spend, raising demand [1].
- Financial services are a large UK employer and export [1].

*Against or limits:*
- Growth also depends on skills, technology and demand, not just finance [1].
- If banks lend too riskily, a financial crisis can cause a recession [1], which is why the Bank of England's role in stability matters [1].

*Judgement:* The financial sector is essential because investment depends on borrowing, but only a stable sector supports growth; an unstable one can damage it [1] [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up store of value and means of deferred payment.
- Defining money as just cash.
- Giving the Bank of England's role as "printing money" without mentioning interest rates or stability.
- Writing one-sided answers to evaluation questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four functions of money in the AQA specification?** A medium of exchange, a store of value, a unit of account and a means of deferred payment. Learn an everyday example of each, because multiple-choice questions often describe a situation and ask which function it shows.

**How does inflation affect money as a store of value?** If prices rise, each pound buys fewer goods, so money kept as cash loses real value. To find the real value of cash after inflation, divide by (1 + the inflation rate): £500 after 8% inflation is worth 500 ÷ 1.08 = £463 in the original prices.

**What is the difference between a commercial bank and a building society?** A commercial bank is a company owned by shareholders and aims to make a profit. A building society is a mutual, owned by its members (savers and borrowers), and traditionally specialises in savings and mortgages.

**How do banks make a profit from lending?** They charge borrowers a higher interest rate than they pay savers. The difference between the interest received and the interest paid covers the bank's costs and provides its profit.
