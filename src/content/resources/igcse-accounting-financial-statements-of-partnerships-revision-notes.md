## The idea in five lines

- Two or more people own a partnership and divide its profit under an agreement.
- The statement of profit or loss stops at **profit for the year**.
- The **appropriation account** divides that profit between the partners.
- Each partner has a **capital account** (long term) and a **current account** (yearly).
- Year-end adjustments from 5.1 are made before the profit is shared.

## Types of partnership business

| Type | What changes in the statements |
|---|---|
| Trading | Cost of sales and gross profit |
| Service | No cost of sales; fees less expenses |
| Manufacturing | Manufacturing account first; cost of production replaces purchases |
| Combination | Use the parts that match each activity |

## Advantages and disadvantages: recall list

- **For:** more capital; shared skills; shared workload and risk; cover for absence; ideas discussed.
- **Against:** unlimited liability; profit shared; disagreements; slower decisions; one partner's choices can bind the rest.

## Partnership agreement

**Why it matters:** it fixes the rules in advance, so the accounts can be prepared and disputes are less likely.
**Contents to list:** each partner's capital, the ratio, interest rates (capital, drawings, loans), salaries and drawings limits.

## Where each item is placed

| Item | Statement | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on a partner's loan | Business expense, before profit for the year | Credit current account if unpaid |
| Interest on capital | Appropriation account, deduct | Credit current account |
| Salary | Appropriation account, deduct | Credit current account |
| Interest on drawings | Appropriation account, add | Debit current account |
| Share of profit | Appropriation account, balance | Credit current account |
| Share of loss | Appropriation account, balance | Debit current account |
| Drawings | Not in either statement | Debit current account |

## Method in steps

1. Prepare the statement of profit or loss with all 5.1 adjustments and any loan interest. Stop at profit for the year.
2. Start the appropriation account with that profit.
3. Add interest on drawings.
4. Deduct interest on capital and salaries.
5. Share what remains in the profit-sharing ratio. A negative balance is a loss, shared in the same ratio.
6. Post to the current accounts. Bring down the balances.
7. Show capital and current accounts in the statement of financial position.

## The 5.1 adjustments in one table

| Adjustment | Effect on profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Expense owing | Expense increases | Other payable |
| Expense paid in advance | Expense decreases | Other receivable |
| Depreciation | Expense increases | Accumulated depreciation grows, carrying amount falls |
| Irrecoverable debt | Expense increases | Trade receivables fall |
| Change in allowance | Expense for the increase (income for a decrease) | Receivables shown net of allowance |
| Goods taken by owner | Reduces cost of sales or purchases | Added to that partner's drawings |

## Ledger reminders

- **Capital account:** credit the opening balance and any capital introduced. With fixed capitals nothing else is posted, and the closing balance is brought down at the same amount.
- **Capital account in ledger form (example):** a partner opens the year with a credit balance of $40,000
  and introduces a further 10,000 on 1 October.

```
Capital account: Hale
                         Balance b/d         40,000
                         Bank (1 October)    10,000
Balance c/d    50,000
               50,000                        50,000
                         Balance b/d         50,000
```

- **Current account, credit side:** opening credit balance, interest on capital, salary, interest on the partner's loan if unpaid, share of profit.
- **Current account, debit side:** opening debit balance, drawings, interest on drawings, share of loss.
- Balance each account with a total, then bring the balance down. With a debit balance, the carried-down figure sits on the credit side and the brought-down figure on the debit side.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Capital vs current account | Capital is long-term investment and normally fixed; current records yearly entitlements and withdrawals |
| Loan interest vs interest on capital | The loan is charged to profit as a business expense; interest on capital is an appropriation |
| Interest on drawings vs drawings | Interest is added in the appropriation account; drawings are never in it |
| Partner's loan vs partner's capital | Loan is a liability and earns loan interest; capital is equity |
| Profit vs balance of profit | Profit is before appropriations; the balance is what is shared in the ratio |

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using profit before loan interest to start the appropriation account.
- Subtracting interest on drawings.
- Posting drawings or the share of profit to a fixed capital account.
- Sharing a loss in the wrong ratio, or treating it as a negative profit that is simply ignored.
- Forgetting that interest on capital and salaries are still allowed in a loss year.
- Leaving a debit current account out of the statement of financial position, or adding it instead of deducting it.
- Placing a partner's loan inside capital.
- Giving advantages without relating them to the facts in the question.
