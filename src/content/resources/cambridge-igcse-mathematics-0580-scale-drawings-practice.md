These questions cover subtopic 4.3 Scale drawings of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is the same for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. You need a ruler and a protractor for the drawing questions. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Measurements from your own drawing are accepted within a small tolerance.

Related: [scale drawings study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-scale-drawings/) | [scale drawings revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-scale-drawings-revision-notes/)

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write the scale "1 cm represents 20 m" as a ratio in the form 1 : n. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A map has a scale of 1 : 25 000.

**(a)** Two villages are 6.4 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance between them in kilometres. **[2]**

**(b)** A road is 2.5 km long. Find its length on the map in centimetres. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the three-figure bearing of due south. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a bearing of 72° in three-figure form. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** The bearing of B from A is 038°. Find the bearing of A from B. **[1]**

**(b)** The bearing of D from C is 260°. Find the bearing of C from D. **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Write each direction as a three-figure bearing.

**(a)** 25° east of south **[1]**

**(b)** 15° north of west **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A room is 5.6 m long and 4.2 m wide. It is drawn on a plan with a scale of 1 : 100. Find the length and width of the room on the plan. **[2]**

**7.** (non-calculator) Point D is due east of point C.

**(a)** Write down the bearing of D from C. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the bearing of C from D. **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** (calculator) A hall is 36 m long and 22 m wide. A scale drawing of it must fit in a space 15 cm by 10 cm.

**(a)** Show that a scale of 1 cm to 3 m fits, and find the length and width of the drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** On the drawing, a doorway is 0.4 cm wide. Find its real width. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A boat sails from harbour H for 6 km on a bearing of 090° to a buoy A. It then sails 8 km on a bearing of 000° to a buoy B.

**(a)** Using a scale of 1 cm to 1 km, make a scale drawing of the journey. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your drawing to find the distance HB. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your drawing to find the bearing of B from H. **[1]**

**(d)** Find the bearing of H from B. **[1]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Town Q is 8 km due north of town P. A tower T is on a bearing of 060° from P and on a bearing of 120° from Q.

**(a)** Using a scale of 1 cm to 1 km, describe how to locate T on a scale drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the distances PT and QT. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why triangle PQT is equilateral. **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) The bearing of X from Y is three times the bearing of Y from X. Both bearings are less than 360°, and the bearing of Y from X is less than 180°. Find both bearings. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator) A garden is drawn to a scale of 1 : 250.

**(a)** A path is 9.2 cm long on the drawing. Find its real length in metres. **[1]**

**(b)** A rectangular flowerbed measures 3.6 cm by 2.4 cm on the drawing. Find its real dimensions. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the real area of the flowerbed. **[1]**

**13.** (non-calculator) A ship sails 7 km due south from port O to point A, then 7 km due east to point B.

**(a)** Find the bearing of B from O. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the bearing of O from B. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 20 m = 2000 cm, so **1 : 2000** [1]

**2. (a)** 6.4 × 25 000 = 160 000 cm [1] = **1.6 km** [1]

**(b)** 2.5 km = 250 000 cm; 250 000 ÷ 25 000 = **10 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* write the real distance in centimetres first, then convert once; most errors come from dividing by 1000 instead of 100 000.

**3. (a)** **180°** [1]

**(b)** **072°** [1]

**4. (a)** 038° + 180° = **218°** [1]

**(b)** 260° – 180° = **080°** [1]

**5. (a)** 180° – 25° = **155°** [1]

**(b)** 270° + 15° = **285°** [1]

**6.** 5.6 m = 560 cm, 560 ÷ 100 = **5.6 cm** [1]; 420 ÷ 100 = **4.2 cm** [1]

**7. (a)** **090°** [1]

**(b)** **270°** [1]

**8. (a)** 36 ÷ 3 = 12 cm, which is less than 15 cm, and 22 ÷ 3 = 7.33 cm, which is less than 10 cm, so it fits [1]; drawing **12 cm by 7.3 cm** [1]

**(b)** 0.4 × 3 = **1.2 m** [1]

**9. (a)** HA drawn 6 cm due east of H [1]; AB drawn 8 cm due north from A [1]

**(b)** **10 km** (10 cm on the drawing) [1]

**(c)** About **037°** [1]

**(d)** 037° + 180° = about **217°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the triangle HAB is right-angled at A, so Pythagoras confirms HB = 10 km. The bearing in (c) is measured at H from north, not from east.

**10. (a)** Draw PQ 8 cm long with Q due north of P [1]; draw a line from P on a bearing of 060° and a line from Q on a bearing of 120°; T is where the lines cross [1]

**(b)** **PT = QT = 8 km** [1]

**(c)** Angle QPT = 60° (the bearing from P is measured from the north line PQ), and angle PQT = 180° – 120° = 60°, so the third angle is 60° and all angles are equal [1]

**11.** Let the bearing of Y from X be b, so the bearing of X from Y is b + 180° [1]; b + 180 = 3b, so b = 90 [1]; Y from X is **090°** and X from Y is **270°** [1]

**12. (a)** 9.2 × 250 = 2300 cm = **23 m** [1]

**(b)** 3.6 × 250 = 900 cm and 2.4 × 250 = 600 cm: **9 m by 6 m** [1]

**(c)** 9 × 6 = **54 m²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* find the real lengths first and then multiply. Multiplying the drawing area by 250 does not give the real area.

**13. (a)** Triangle OAB is right-angled at A with OA = AB, so angle AOB = 45° [1]; bearing = 180° – 45° = **135°** [1]

**(b)** 135° + 180° = **315°** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting kilometres to centimetres incorrectly.
- Measuring a bearing anticlockwise or from the wrong point.
- Leaving out the leading zero in a bearing.
- Adding 180° to a bearing that is already more than 180°.
- Scaling an area by the length scale.
- Drawing north lines that are not parallel.
