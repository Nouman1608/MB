## Questions

**1.** State three non-monetary indicators that can be used to compare living standards between countries. **[3]**

**2.** Country A has nominal GDP of 150 cu bn, a price index of 125 (base year 100) and a population of 5 million. Calculate real GDP per capita. **[3]**

**3.** Country B has GDP of 90 cu bn, net property income from abroad of plus 6 cu bn, capital consumption of 9 cu bn and a population of 3 million. Calculate (a) GNI and (b) net national income (NNI) per capita. **[4]**

**4.** A basket of goods costs 40 cu in Country C and 2,600 units in Country D. The market exchange rate is 50 units per cu. GDP per capita in Country D is 195,000 units.
**(a)** Calculate Country D's GDP per capita in cu at the market rate and at purchasing power parity (PPP). **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the two figures differ. **[2]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why GDP per capita may be a poor guide to living standards. **[4]**

**6.** **(a)** A person is deprived in sanitation, drinking water, electricity, cooking fuel and housing, and in no other indicator. Each living-standards indicator has weight 1/18. Decide whether the person is poor on the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI), where the poverty threshold is a weighted score of 1/3. **[2]**
**(b)** In a country 24% of people are poor and their average deprivation is 50%. Calculate the MPI. **[2]**

**7.** Country E has life expectancy 66 years, expected schooling 12 years, mean schooling 6 years and income of 4,000 cu PPP. Assume these limits: life expectancy 25 to 85, expected schooling 0 to 18, mean schooling 0 to 15, income 500 to 60,000 cu.
**(a)** Calculate the Human Development Index (HDI) for Country E, using the geometric mean of the three dimension indices and a logarithm of income. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why the income index uses a logarithm. **[2]**

**8.** Country A has GDP of 120 cu bn and a population of 4 million. Household production is valued at 14 cu bn and leisure at 10 cu bn. Regrettable necessities cost 9 cu bn and pollution costs 7 cu bn.
**(a)** Calculate the Measure of Economic Welfare (MEW). **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate MEW per capita and GDP per capita. **[2]**
**(c)** State one limitation of MEW. **[1]**

**9.** A study of four invented countries shows the Gini coefficient by income band: low income 0.32, lower-middle 0.41, upper-middle 0.47, high income 0.39.
**(a)** Describe the pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why inequality might fall at higher income levels. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why the pattern may not hold for every country. **[2]**

**10.** Country F has real GDP per capita of 4,000 cu growing at 5% a year. Country G has 24,000 cu growing at 1.5% a year.
**(a)** Calculate real GDP per capita in each country after 20 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in the absolute gap and the ratio of G to F. **[2]**
**(c)** Comment on what this shows about comparing growth rates. **[2]**

**11.** Explain the difference between classifying economies by national income and classifying them by level of development. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether a rise in real GDP per capita shows that living standards have improved. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark each for any three, for example life expectancy [1], infant mortality [1] and adult literacy [1]. Other acceptable indicators include school enrolment, access to clean water or sanitation, doctors per thousand people and electricity access. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name measurable outcomes, not general ideas. "Health" alone is a dimension, while "life expectancy at birth" is an indicator.

**2.** Real GDP = 150/1.25 [1] = 120 cu bn [1]. Per capita = 120 bn/5 m = **24,000 cu** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* writing the deflation step down matters, because dividing by 125 instead of 1.25 or skipping the population step makes it hard to give credit for the rest.

**3. (a)** GNI = 90 + 6 = **96 cu bn** [1].
**(b)** NNI = 96 - 9 = 87 cu bn [1]. Per capita = 87 bn/3 m [1] = **29,000 cu** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* NNI is built from GNI, not GDP, and a candidate who subtracts depreciation from GDP only has missed the net income term.

**4. (a)** PPP rate = 2,600/40 = 65 units per cu [1]. Market-rate income = 195,000/50 = 3,900 cu [1]. PPP income = 195,000/65 = **3,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** The basket costs 52 cu at the market rate in Country D, above 40 cu in Country C, so prices are higher in D [1]. Converting at the market rate therefore overstates what D's income buys, and PPP income is lower [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the direction matters. A comparison that says only "they differ because of prices" does not show which country is dearer or why PPP is lower here.

**5.** Any two reasons, each worth a point and its development. Example one: GDP per capita is an average, so a rise can go to a few people while most gain nothing [1]; this can overstate the living standards of typical households [1]. Example two: GDP omits unpaid household work and the informal economy [1], so the real level of output and welfare is understated, especially in poorer countries [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* name a limitation, then say which way it distorts the comparison. A list of omitted items with no consequence earns less than two developed points.

**6. (a)** Score = 5 x 1/18 = 5/18 = 0.278 [1]. This is below 1/3, so the person is not MPI-poor, despite five deprivations [1].
**(b)** MPI = H x A [1] = 0.24 x 0.50 = **0.12** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* compare the score with 1/3 explicitly. Five deprivations looks like poverty, and the conclusion has to follow from the weights.

**7. (a)** Health = (66 - 25)/60 = 0.683 [1]. Education = (12/18 + 6/15)/2 = (0.667 + 0.4)/2 = 0.533 [1]. Income = ln(4,000/500)/ln(60,000/500) = 2.079/4.787 = 0.434 [1]. HDI = (0.683 x 0.533 x 0.434)^(1/3) [1] = **0.541** [1].
**(b)** Extra income adds less welfare at high income [1], so the logarithm gives smaller index gains for each extra cu as income rises [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* keep unrounded values until the final step. Rounding each index early can shift the third decimal of the HDI.

**8. (a)** MEW = 120 + 14 + 10 - 9 - 7 [1] = **128 cu bn** [1].
**(b)** MEW per capita = 128 bn/4 m = 32,000 cu [1]. GDP per capita = 120 bn/4 m = 30,000 cu [1].
**(c)** Valuing leisure, household work or pollution is subjective, so MEW is hard to compare across countries [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "it is hard to measure" is too thin. Say what has to be valued and why that valuation is contestable.

**9. (a)** Inequality rises from 0.32 to 0.47 as income moves from low to upper-middle [1], then falls to 0.39 at high income, an inverted U [1].
**(b)** Higher income allows more tax revenue and welfare spending [1], and education spreads, narrowing wage differences [1].
**(c)** The pattern is from a few cases and does not set out a rule [1]. Policy choices, such as land reform or progressive taxes, can change inequality at any income level [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* quote values from your data in the description. Naming an inverted U with no values may not show that you read it.

**10. (a)** F = 4,000 x 1.05^20 = 10,613 cu [1]. G = 24,000 x 1.015^20 = 32,325 cu [1].
**(b)** Gap rises from 20,000 to 21,711 cu (unrounded values) [1]. Ratio falls from 6.0 to 3.05 [1].
**(c)** Higher growth in F narrows the relative gap but the absolute gap still widens [1], so catching up depends on starting level and takes decades [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* give both the absolute and the relative gap. One measure alone can support the opposite conclusion.

**11.** Classification by national income uses one measure, usually GNI per capita, to place countries in bands [1]. It is simple and comparable [1]. Classification by development uses several features, such as health, education and economic structure [1], so two countries in one income band can fall into different development groups [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the contrast has to be explicit. A paragraph describing each in turn without comparison is weaker than one that contrasts them directly, for example with the word "whereas".

**12.** Indicative points, one mark each. For: real GDP per capita removes price rises and population change [1]; higher income allows spending on food, health and education [1]; it usually correlates with life expectancy and schooling [1]. Against: it is an average, so distribution may be unchanged or worse [1]; output may come with pollution or resource depletion [1]; unpaid work and informal activity are missed [1]; the composition of output matters [1]. Evaluation: compare it with HDI, MEW or MPI, which adjust for these gaps [1]; the importance of each limit depends on the country, so judge with data on inequality and health [1]; a justified conclusion, such as that it is a necessary but not sufficient indicator [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* answers that list limits without weighing them stay descriptive. Reach a conclusion and say what evidence would change it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by 125 instead of 1.25 when deflating nominal values.
- Using total GDP instead of a per capita figure for living standards.
- Applying the PPP rate the wrong way round.
- Treating five MPI deprivations as automatic poverty without weighting.
- Rounding the HDI dimension indices too early.
- Naming a limitation of GDP without saying which way it distorts.
- Presenting the Kuznets pattern as a law.
- Comparing growth rates without the starting levels or the absolute gap.
