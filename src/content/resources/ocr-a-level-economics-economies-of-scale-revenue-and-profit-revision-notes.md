## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SRAC | Average cost with at least one fixed factor |
| LRAC | Cheapest cost per unit at each output once every factor can change; the envelope of the SRAC curves |
| Internal economy of scale | Fall in LRAC as the firm itself grows; movement along LRAC |
| External economy of scale | Fall in every firm's costs as the industry grows; LRAC shifts down |
| Diseconomies | Rise in LRAC, internal (movement along) or external (shift up) |
| MES | Smallest output at which LRAC reaches its floor |
| TR, AR, MR | Money from all sales; revenue per unit (= price); extra TR from one more unit |
| Accounting profit | TR minus explicit costs |
| Economic profit | TR minus explicit and implicit costs |
| Normal profit | Least return that keeps resources in their present use; counted within TC |
| Supernormal profit | Profit above normal profit: TR > TC |
| Loss | TR < TC |

## Formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit | TR − TC |
| Profit per unit | AR − AC |
| Total supernormal profit or loss | (AR − AC) × output |
| AR | TR/Q, which equals P |
| MR | Change in TR/change in Q |
| Straight-line demand P = a − bQ | MR = a − 2bQ |
| Point PED | (change in Q/change in P) × (P/Q) |
| MR and PED | MR = P(1 + 1/PED) |
| Percentage change | (new − old)/old × 100 |
| Firms at MES | Market size/MES |

## SRAC, LRAC and scale

- Each plant size has its own SRAC. The LRAC touches the lowest point of each choice and never lies above any SRAC.
- Falling LRAC means economies of scale, flat means constant returns and rising LRAC means diseconomies.
- Internal economies: technical, purchasing, managerial, financial, marketing, risk-bearing.
- Internal diseconomies: control and coordination, communication, motivation.
- External economies: skilled labour pool, specialist suppliers, shared infrastructure.
- External diseconomies: congestion, higher rents, wages bid up.

**Method for an economies-of-scale calculation.**

1. Find total cost at each output.
2. Divide by output to get AC.
3. Compare percentage changes: AC falls if cost rises by less than output.

**Reminder.** A firm raises output from 10,000 to 20,000 units and LRTC goes from £90,000 to £138,000. AC moves from £9.00 to £6.90, a fall of 23.3%. Output rose 100% but cost only 53.3%.

## MES

- MES is the lowest output reaching minimum LRAC.
- Compare it with market size. A market of 90,000 units and an MES of 15,000 gives 90,000/15,000 = 6 firms.
- A small MES relative to the market means many firms and low barriers. A large MES means few firms, perhaps one, and high barriers.
- A firm below MES has higher costs. If AC is £11.00 against £8.80 at MES, it is (11.00 − 8.80)/8.80 × 100 = 25% above the minimum.

## Evaluating scale

Evaluate the causes by asking how large each effect is, how soon it appears, and what limits it. Evaluate significance for each agent.

| Agent | Possible effect |
|---|---|
| Firm | Lower costs, price flexibility, barrier to entry, risk of diseconomies |
| Consumers | Lower prices if the saving is passed on |
| Workers | Jobs from growth, or redundancies after mergers |
| Government | Competition concerns where scale limits rivals |

Useful evaluation points:

- Economies may take years to appear, while the costs of expansion arrive immediately.
- Diseconomies can be postponed by delegation, divisions and better information systems.
- The more concentrated the market, the less likely it is that the saving reaches consumers.
- External economies help every firm in the area, so they are less likely to become a source of market power.

Conclude with a judgement tied to the case: for example, a large MES makes the benefits more significant but also makes market power more likely.

## Revenue and elasticity

- On a falling demand curve, AR is the demand curve and MR lies below it.
- For P = a − bQ, MR has the same intercept and twice the slope.
- Elastic demand (PED beyond −1): MR positive, a price fall raises TR.
- Unit elastic (PED = −1): MR = 0, TR at its maximum.
- Inelastic (PED between 0 and −1): MR negative, a price fall lowers TR.

**Reminder.** Demand P = 50 − 2Q. At 10 units, P = 30 and TR = 300. MR = 50 − 40 = 10. PED = −(1/2) × 30/10 = −1.5. Check: 30 × (1 − 1/1.5) = 10. TR peaks where MR = 0, at 12.5 units.

## Profit

- Accounting profit exceeds economic profit by the implicit costs.
- Zero economic profit still means the owner is earning normal profit.
- On an AR and AC diagram, profit is the rectangle between AR and AC at the chosen output.
- A firm can be loss-making and still produce in the short run if AR covers AVC.

**Method for MC = MR.**

1. Find MR from the demand curve and MC from the cost function.
2. Set MR = MC and solve for output.
3. Read price from AR at that output.
4. Compute TR, TC and profit (or loss), and check against the neighbouring outputs.

**Reminder.** A firm sells 120 units at AR £25 with AC £22.50: TR is £3,000, TC £2,700, so supernormal profit is £300.

## Worked reminders

**Price change and TR.** With P = 50 − 2Q, cutting price from 30 to 27 raises quantity from 10 to 11.5. That is +15% for a 10% price fall, so demand is elastic. TR rises from 300 to 310.5.

**Profit maximised.** With TC = 20 + 14Q + Q², MC = 14 + 2Q. Setting MR = MC: 50 − 4Q = 14 + 2Q, so Q = 6.

| Output | Price | TR | TC | Profit | MR | MC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 40 | 200 | 115 | 85 | 32 | 23 |
| 6 | 38 | 228 | 140 | 88 | 28 | 25 |
| 7 | 36 | 252 | 167 | 85 | 24 | 27 |

The sixth unit has MR 28 above MC 25, so it adds 3 to profit. The seventh has MR 24 below MC 27, so it lowers profit by 3. Maximum profit is £88 at price £38.

**Loss minimised.** If demand weakens to P = 26 − 2Q, MR = 26 − 4Q. Setting 26 − 4Q = 14 + 2Q gives Q = 2 and price 22. TR = 44 and TC = 52, so the loss is £8. AVC = 14 + 2 = 16 is below the price of 22, so the firm still covers variable cost. Closing would lose the fixed cost of £20, which is worse.

## Must-know distinctions

- Movement along LRAC (internal) versus shift of LRAC (external).
- MES versus the output of lowest SRAC on one plant.
- Normal profit (inside costs) versus supernormal (above costs).
- Accounting versus economic profit.
- MR versus AR: for a price-setting firm, MR is below AR.

## Quick self-test

1. Define minimum efficient scale.
2. LRTC is £90,000 at 10,000 units and £138,000 at 20,000 units. State LRAC at each and the percentage change.
3. Rival firms gain from a trained workforce built up by the growth of their industry. Is this an internal or external economy?
4. A firm faces P = 50 − 2Q. Find TR and MR at 10 units.
5. Find PED at 10 units for this firm.
6. At what output is TR highest?
7. Revenue is £80,000, explicit costs £55,000 and implicit costs £22,000. Find accounting and economic profit.
8. Output is 200 units at AR £15 and AC £18. Find the profit or loss.
9. At output 40, MR is £22 and MC is £19. Should the firm change output?
10. A market takes 90,000 units and MES is 15,000. What is the largest number of firms that could each reach MES?

### Answers

1. The smallest output at which LRAC reaches its lowest value.
2. 90,000/10,000 = £9.00 and 138,000/20,000 = £6.90. Change = (6.90 − 9.00)/9.00 × 100 = −23.3%.
3. External: it arises from the industry's growth and shifts LRAC down.
4. P = 30, so TR = 300. MR = 50 − 4 × 10 = 10.
5. −(1/2) × (30/10) = −1.5, which is elastic.
6. Where MR = 0: 50 − 4Q = 0, so Q = 12.5.
7. Accounting profit 80,000 − 55,000 = £25,000. Economic profit 25,000 − 22,000 = £3,000.
8. TR = 3,000 and TC = 3,600, so a loss of £600.
9. Yes, produce more: MR exceeds MC by £3, so another unit adds to profit.
10. 90,000/15,000 = 6 firms.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating percentage change from the new value instead of the old.
- Giving a figure for MR without showing the working that leads to it.
- Mixing up a movement along LRAC with a shift of it.
- Treating economies of scale as automatic gains for consumers.
- Describing MES with no reference to market size.
- Calling accounting profit supernormal profit.
- Drawing MC = MR but reading price from the MC curve instead of AR.
- Listing causes of economies of scale with no evaluation of size or timing.
