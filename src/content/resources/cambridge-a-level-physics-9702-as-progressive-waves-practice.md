## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the amplitude and by the period of a progressive wave. **[2]**

**2.** Describe how a small cork floating in a ripple tank shows that a progressive wave transfers energy but not the water itself. **[2]**

**3.** Using the definitions of speed, frequency and wavelength, derive the wave equation v = fλ. **[3]**

**4.** A sound wave of frequency 2.0 kHz has a wavelength of 0.17 m in air. Calculate its speed and its period. **[2]**

**5.** The amplitude of a wave is reduced to 40% of its original value. Calculate the new intensity as a percentage of the original intensity. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A signal is displayed on a cathode-ray oscilloscope. The time-base is set at 0.50 ms div⁻¹ and the y-gain at 0.20 V div⁻¹. Exactly three complete cycles occupy 8.4 divisions horizontally, and the vertical distance from a peak to a trough is 5.0 divisions.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the signal. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the amplitude of the signal. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why measuring across three cycles, rather than one, gives a more reliable value of the period. **[1]**

**7.** Two microphones, M₁ and M₂, are placed in a line directly in front of a loudspeaker, with M₂ further from the loudspeaker. Their outputs are shown on a two-trace oscilloscope with the time-base at 0.25 ms div⁻¹. One complete cycle of each trace occupies 10 divisions, and the peaks of the M₂ trace occur 2.5 divisions later than the peaks of the M₁ trace.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the sound. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the phase difference between the two signals, in degrees. **[2]**

**(c)** The microphones are 0.21 m apart, which is the smallest separation that gives this phase difference. Calculate the speed of sound. **[2]**

**8.** A transverse wave of frequency 15 Hz travels along a stretched string at 4.5 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the phase difference between two points on the string 0.40 m apart. **[2]**

**(c)** The frequency of the source is doubled, and the tension in the string is unchanged. State the new wavelength and the new speed. **[2]**

**9.** A small lamp emits 3.0 W of light uniformly in all directions. Ignore any absorption.

**(a)** Calculate the intensity of the light at a distance of 2.0 m from the lamp. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance from the lamp at which the intensity is 0.015 W m⁻². **[2]**

**(c)** Determine the ratio of the amplitude of the light wave at 2.0 m to the amplitude at the distance found in **(b)**. **[2]**

**10.** A laser produces a beam of power 5.0 mW with a circular cross-section of diameter 2.0 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the intensity of the beam. **[2]**

**(b)** The beam passes through a filter that reduces its amplitude to 0.70 of its original value. Calculate the intensity of the beam leaving the filter. **[2]**

**11.** Ripples spread out in circles from a vibrating point dipper in a ripple tank. Explain why the amplitude of the ripples decreases with distance from the dipper, even if no energy is dissipated. **[2]**

**12.** A progressive wave has a period of 0.040 s. At time t = 0, a particle P on the wave is at its maximum positive displacement.

**(a)** State the displacement of P at t = 0.010 s and at t = 0.020 s. **[2]**

**(b)** State the phase difference between the oscillation of P at t = 0 and at t = 0.030 s. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Amplitude: the **maximum displacement** of a particle from its equilibrium position [1]. Period: the **time for one complete oscillation** of a particle [1].

**2.** The cork **bobs up and down about the same position** as the ripples pass, but does not travel with them [1]. The cork gains kinetic and potential energy, showing that **energy** reaches it through the wave without the water being carried along [1].

**3.** In one period T the wave advances **one wavelength** λ [1]. Speed = distance ÷ time = λ/T [1]. Since f = 1/T, **v = fλ** [1].

**4.** v = fλ = 2000 × 0.17 = **340 m s⁻¹** [1]. T = 1/f = **5.0 × 10⁻⁴ s** [1].

**5.** Intensity ∝ amplitude², so the new intensity is 0.40² of the original [1] = **16%** [1].

**6. (a)** One cycle = 8.4 ÷ 3 = 2.8 div [1]; T = 2.8 × 0.50 ms = 1.4 ms [1]; f = 1 ÷ 1.4 × 10⁻³ = **710 Hz** (714 Hz) [1].

**(b)** Amplitude = 5.0 ÷ 2 = 2.5 div [1]; 2.5 × 0.20 = **0.50 V** [1].

**(c)** The reading uncertainty in the length is spread over three cycles, so the **percentage uncertainty** in the period is smaller [1].

**7. (a)** T = 10 × 0.25 ms = 2.5 ms [1]; f = 1 ÷ 2.5 × 10⁻³ = **400 Hz** [1].

**(b)** 2.5 ÷ 10 = ¼ cycle [1]; phase difference = **90°** [1].

**(c)** A quarter-cycle phase difference corresponds to λ/4 = 0.21 m, so λ = 0.84 m [1]; v = fλ = 400 × 0.84 = **336 m s⁻¹** [1].

**8. (a)** λ = v/f = 4.5 ÷ 15 = **0.30 m** [1].

**(b)** 0.40 ÷ 0.30 = 1⅓ cycles [1]; phase difference = **120°** (equivalently 480°; 2π/3 rad) [1].

**(c)** The speed depends on the string, so it stays **4.5 m s⁻¹** [1]; the wavelength halves to **0.15 m** [1].

**9. (a)** I = P ÷ 4πr² = 3.0 ÷ (4π × 2.0²) [1] = **0.060 W m⁻²** [1].

**(b)** r² = P ÷ 4πI = 3.0 ÷ (4π × 0.015) = 15.9 m² [1]; r = **4.0 m** [1].

**(c)** The intensity at 4.0 m is a quarter of the intensity at 2.0 m [1]. Since amplitude ∝ √intensity, the amplitude ratio is **2 : 1** [1].

**10. (a)** Area = π(1.0 × 10⁻³)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ m² [1]; I = 5.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ = **1.6 × 10³ W m⁻²** [1].

**(b)** Intensity ∝ amplitude², so the factor is 0.70² = 0.49 [1]; I = 0.49 × 1590 = **780 W m⁻²** [1].

**11.** The energy is spread over **circles of increasing circumference**, so the power per unit length of wavefront (the intensity) decreases with distance [1]. Since intensity ∝ amplitude², the **amplitude decreases** too [1].

**12. (a)** At t = 0.010 s (a quarter period): **zero** displacement [1]. At t = 0.020 s (half a period): **maximum negative** displacement [1].

**(b)** 0.030 ÷ 0.040 = ¾ cycle, so **270°** (3π/2 rad) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the peak-to-trough distance as the amplitude without halving (Question 6).
- Forgetting to convert ms div⁻¹ to seconds (Questions 6 and 7).
- Expecting the wave speed to double with the frequency on the same string (Question 8).
- Using intensity ∝ amplitude instead of amplitude squared (Questions 5, 9 and 10).
- Using the beam diameter instead of the radius for the area (Question 10).
- Quoting a phase difference without units, or as a distance.
