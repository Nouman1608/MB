These questions cover section **3.1.5.2 Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-maxwell-boltzmann-distribution/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-maxwell-boltzmann-distribution-revision-notes/). Where a question asks for a sketch, describe the labelled features in words, as a mark scheme would.

## Questions

**1.** State what is plotted on each axis of a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution for a gas. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the curve:

**(a)** starts at the origin **[1]**

**(b)** does not touch the horizontal axis at high energy **[1]**

**3.** State what is represented by the area under the whole curve, and what is represented by the area to the right of the activation energy. **[2]**

**4.** Sketch a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution for a gas at a temperature T₁. Mark the activation energy, Eₐ, and shade the area that represents molecules able to react. **[3]**

**5.** On the same axes as in question 4, add the curve for a higher temperature, T₂. Describe how it differs from the T₁ curve. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why the peak of the T₂ curve is lower than that of the T₁ curve although the same number of molecules is present. **[2]**

**7.** A sample of 2.00 × 10²³ molecules is heated from 300 K to 350 K. The percentage of molecules with energy at least equal to Eₐ rises from 0.20% to 1.60%.

**(a)** Calculate the number of molecules with energy of at least Eₐ at each temperature. **[2]**

**(b)** State how many times larger the number is at 350 K. **[1]**

**(c)** State what happens to the activation energy between 300 K and 350 K. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why the most probable energy is lower than the mean energy. **[2]**

**9.** A student draws the T₂ curve so that it ends at the energy axis at a high energy. State why this is incorrect. **[1]**

**10.** A student draws a second curve that is higher than the first at all energies. Explain why this cannot be correct for the same sample of gas. **[2]**

**11.** The Maxwell–Boltzmann distributions for a gas at 298 K and 320 K are drawn on the same axes. At which energy do the curves cross, relative to the peak of the 298 K curve? **[1]**

**12.** Use a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution to explain why a small increase in temperature can cause a large increase in the number of molecules able to react. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** x-axis: energy (of the molecules) [1]; y-axis: number (or fraction) of molecules with that energy [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** No molecules have zero energy [1]

**(b)** There is no maximum energy, so a very few molecules have very high energy [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Whole area: the total number of molecules in the sample [1]; area to the right of Eₐ: the number of molecules with energy equal to or greater than Eₐ [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Axes labelled energy and number of molecules [1]; curve starting at the origin, with a single peak and a tail that approaches the horizontal axis without touching it [1]; Eₐ marked as a vertical line on the energy axis and the area to its right shaded [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The T₂ peak is lower [1]; and at a higher energy (to the right) [1]; the tail is higher than the T₁ tail, so the area to the right of Eₐ is larger (the areas under both curves are equal) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The area under the curve is fixed because the number of molecules is the same [1]; at a higher temperature the molecules are spread over a wider range of energies, so fewer have the most probable energy [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 300 K: 0.0020 × 2.00 × 10²³ = **4.0 × 10²⁰** [1]; 350 K: 0.0160 × 2.00 × 10²³ = **3.2 × 10²¹** [1]

**(b)** 3.2 × 10²¹ ÷ 4.0 × 10²⁰ = **8 times** [1]

**(c)** It does not change [1]. **[4]**

**8.** The curve has a long tail to high energy [1]; the few molecules with very high energies increase the mean above the peak, which is the most probable energy [1]. **[2]**

**9.** There is no upper limit to energy, so the curve must approach but not touch the axis [1]. **[1]**

**10.** The area under the curve represents the total number of molecules [1]; this is the same for the same sample, so a curve that is higher everywhere would have a greater area, which is impossible [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The curves cross to the right of the peak of the 298 K curve [1]. **[1]**

**12.** At a higher temperature the peak falls and moves to the right, and the tail is larger [1]; the activation energy lies in the tail, where the curve is low, so a small shift produces a large proportional increase in the area beyond Eₐ [1]; more molecules have energy of at least Eₐ, so there are many more successful collisions per second [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing a symmetrical curve or one that does not start at the origin.
- Letting the curve touch or cross the energy axis.
- Drawing two curves with different areas.
- Not lowering the peak for the higher temperature.
- Describing the shift without referring to the area beyond Eₐ.
