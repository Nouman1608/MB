---

## Section A

**1.** Define the term data. **[1]**

**2.** Explain how data becomes information. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** sources of direct data. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by indirect data. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** sources of indirect data. **[2]**

**6.** The numbers 23, 25 and 31 are stored in a file. Explain why these numbers are data and not information. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Tidewell Council wants to find out how residents travel to work.

**(a)** Describe how the council could collect this as direct data using a questionnaire. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe **two** disadvantages of using a questionnaire for this purpose. **[4]**

**8.** A marine scientist logs the temperature of a lake every ten minutes for a year using sensors.

**(a)** Name this method of collecting data. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** advantages of this method compared with a person reading a thermometer. **[4]**

**9.** Halden Hospital uses census data to plan how many beds it will need for older patients in ten years.

**(a)** Explain why this is indirect data. **[2]**

**(b)** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the hospital using census data for this purpose. **[6]**

**10.** Compare direct data with indirect data. **[4]**

**11.** An online shop buys the purchase histories of customers from another company to decide which adverts to show. Explain **one** advantage and **two** disadvantages of using this data. **[5]**

**12.** Briarwood Farm wants to decide whether to grow a new crop next spring. Evaluate whether the farm should rely on direct data, indirect data or both. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Raw facts, figures or symbols with no context or meaning [1]. **[1]**

**2.** It is given context [1] and meaning, for example by being labelled, processed or put into a situation so that a person can understand and use it [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: questionnaires [1]; interviews [1]; data logging [1]; observation [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Data that was collected for a different purpose [1] and is then reused by someone else [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: weather data [1]; census data [1]; the electoral register [1]; personal information collected by businesses and used by third parties [1]; research from textbooks, journals and websites [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The numbers have no context [1], so we cannot tell what they measure (they could be ages, prices or temperatures) or what they mean [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** The council writes a set of questions about how people travel (for example car, bus, bicycle, walking) [1] and gives them to a sample of residents on paper or online [1]. The answers are collected and counted to show how many use each method [1].

**(b)** Any two disadvantages, each worth one mark with one mark for development: many people do not return questionnaires [1], so the sample may be too small to be representative [1]; people may answer carelessly or dishonestly [1], which makes the results inaccurate [1]; closed questions cannot capture an unexpected reason or option [1], so useful detail is lost [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** Data logging [1].

**(b)** Any two from: readings are taken automatically at set intervals with no human needed [1], so none are missed overnight or in bad weather [1]; sensors are accurate and consistent [1], avoiding human reading errors [1]; the data is stored directly in a computer [1], so it can be processed straight away without typing it in [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** The census was collected by a government for the purpose of counting the population [1], not by the hospital for its own bed planning [1].

**(b)** Advantages: it covers the whole population, a scale the hospital could not survey itself [1]; it is cheap or free, so no collection cost [1]; census data is collected by an official body and is usually reliable [1]. Disadvantages: it may be several years old, so the age profile may have changed [1]; it does not include health details or planned local housing, so it may not match the hospital's exact needs [1]; it needs cleaning or reorganising to show only older residents in the hospital's area [1]. Conclusion: it is a good starting point but should be combined with the hospital's own admissions records [1]. Any six from the seven points, with at least one advantage and one disadvantage. **[8]**

**10.** Direct data is collected first-hand for the specific purpose, whereas indirect data was collected for another purpose [1]. Direct data is relevant and its source is known, whereas indirect data may be irrelevant or of unknown reliability [1]. Direct data is usually slower and more expensive to collect, whereas indirect data is quick and cheap to obtain [1]. Direct data is up to date, whereas indirect data may be out of date [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Advantage: it is quick and cheap [1], because the shop does not have to collect the histories itself [1]. Disadvantage 1: the customers did not give the shop permission, so there are privacy and legal concerns [1]. Disadvantage 2: the data may be out of date or may not match the shop's own products [1], and the shop cannot check how accurately it was gathered, so adverts may be wasted [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Direct data: surveying local buyers and running a small trial plot gives relevant, current evidence about local conditions and demand [1]; but it costs time and money, and a small trial may not represent the whole farm [1]. Indirect data: weather records and published research on yields are cheap and cover many years [1]; but they may come from different soil or climates and may be out of date [1]. Both: indirect data narrows the choices cheaply, then direct data tests the shortlisted crop on the farm itself [1]. Judgement: the farm should use both, because the cost of a failed crop is high and each type covers the weakness of the other [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining information as "processed data" with no mention of context or meaning.
- Saying direct data is always accurate or indirect data is always unreliable.
- Listing sources without saying whether they are direct or indirect.
- Disadvantages that are generic ("it takes a long time") rather than linked to the scenario.
- Leaving out the conclusion in an evaluate or discuss question.
