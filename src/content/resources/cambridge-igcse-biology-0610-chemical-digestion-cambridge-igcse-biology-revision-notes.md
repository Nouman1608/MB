## Definition and purpose

- **Chemical digestion:** the breakdown of **large insoluble** molecules into **small soluble** molecules.
- **Why:** small soluble molecules **can be absorbed** through the wall of the small intestine into the blood.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Enzyme | protein that acts as a biological catalyst |
| Substrate | the molecule an enzyme acts on |
| Product | the molecule(s) an enzyme makes |
| Amylase | starch → simple reducing sugars |
| Protease | protein → amino acids |
| Lipase | fats and oils → fatty acids and glycerol |
| Gastric juice | stomach secretion containing protease and hydrochloric acid |
| Maltase (Extended only) | maltose → glucose, on the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine |
| Pepsin (Extended only) | protease working in the acidic stomach |
| Trypsin (Extended only) | protease working in the alkaline small intestine |

## Core enzyme map

| Enzyme | Secreted by | Acts in | Substrate → products |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | salivary glands | mouth | starch → simple reducing sugars |
| Amylase | pancreas | small intestine | starch → simple reducing sugars |
| Protease | stomach | stomach | protein → amino acids |
| Protease | pancreas | small intestine | protein → amino acids |
| Lipase | pancreas | small intestine | fats and oils → fatty acids + glycerol |

Memory hook: **the pancreas makes all three**; the stomach makes protease only; the salivary glands make amylase only.

## Journey of each nutrient (Core)

| Nutrient | Mouth | Stomach | Small intestine | Absorbed as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starch | salivary amylase starts | amylase stops (acid) | pancreatic amylase continues | simple sugars |
| Protein | nothing | protease starts | pancreatic protease continues | amino acids |
| Fats and oils | nothing | churned only | pancreatic lipase | fatty acids and glycerol |

Use this table for "describe the digestion of X" questions: follow the nutrient through each region in order.

## Hydrochloric acid: exactly two functions

1. Kills harmful microorganisms in food.
2. Provides an acidic pH for optimum enzyme activity (the stomach protease).

## Extended-only chains

**Starch:**

```
starch --amylase--> maltose --maltase (on small-intestine epithelium)--> glucose
```

**Protein:**

```
stomach (acidic): pepsin          small intestine (alkaline): trypsin
             protein  -->  smaller pieces  -->  amino acids
```

**Bile and pH:**

```
acidic mixture leaves stomach → bile (alkaline) neutralises it in duodenum
→ suitable pH for intestinal enzymes such as trypsin
```

## Linking pH to enzyme shape

- Each enzyme has an **optimum pH**.
- Away from it, the active site changes shape and the substrate fits less well; at extreme pH the enzyme is **denatured**.
- Pepsin: optimum acidic (about pH 2). Trypsin: optimum alkaline.
- Salivary amylase stops working in the acidic stomach.

## Method in steps: showing amylase action with food tests

1. Mix starch solution with amylase (or saliva) at a set temperature.
2. Take a drop every minute and test with iodine on a spotting tile.
3. End point: iodine stays orange-brown, so no starch remains.
4. Test the final mixture with Benedict's (heated): positive shows reducing sugar was made.
5. Control: starch with boiled amylase or water stays blue-black.

## Answer patterns

- **"Describe the function of enzyme X"**: X breaks down substrate into product(s). Name all three parts.
- **"State where X is secreted and where it acts"**: two answers; for pancreatic enzymes they differ.
- **"Explain why enzyme X works in region Y"** (Extended): region Y's pH matches X's optimum; link to active-site shape.
- **"Interpret a pH graph"**: give the optimum with its value, then describe the rise and fall using numbers from the graph.
- **"Suggest the effect of losing organ Z"**: which enzymes are lost, which nutrients are then less digested, and the effect on absorption.

## Exam traps

- Core: amylase gives "simple reducing sugars". Extended: maltose, then maltase gives glucose.
- Lipase products: fatty acids **and** glycerol.
- The liver makes bile, not enzymes.
- Pancreatic enzymes act in the small intestine, not the pancreas.
- Acid does not digest food; it kills microorganisms and sets the pH.
- (Extended) Maltase is on the small-intestine epithelium, not in a juice from the pancreas.
- (Extended) Bile neutralises the acidic mixture entering the duodenum.

## Self-test

1. Describe chemical digestion.
2. Why must food be chemically digested before it is absorbed?
3. State the products of lipase.
4. Name **two** organs that secrete amylase.
5. Where does protease from the pancreas act?
6. State **two** functions of hydrochloric acid in gastric juice.
7. Name the organ that secretes amylase, protease and lipase.
8. Explain why salivary amylase stops working in the stomach.
9. (Extended) Name the product of amylase acting on starch.
10. (Extended) State where maltase is found.
11. (Extended) Name the protease that works in acidic conditions and where it acts.
12. (Extended) Explain why bile is needed for trypsin to work well.

### Answers

1. The breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules.
2. Only small soluble molecules can be absorbed into the blood.
3. Fatty acids and glycerol.
4. Salivary glands and pancreas.
5. In the small intestine (duodenum).
6. Kills harmful microorganisms; provides an acidic pH for optimum enzyme activity.
7. The pancreas.
8. The stomach is acidic; amylase works best near neutral, so its active site changes shape and it stops working.
9. Maltose.
10. On the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine.
11. Pepsin, in the stomach.
12. Bile is alkaline and neutralises the acidic mixture entering the duodenum, giving the alkaline pH trypsin needs.
