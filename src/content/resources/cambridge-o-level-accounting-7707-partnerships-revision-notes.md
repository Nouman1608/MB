## What the syllabus asks

1. Partnerships as trading, service, manufacturing or combined businesses.
2. Advantages and disadvantages of forming a partnership.
3. Importance and contents of a partnership agreement.
4. Purpose of an appropriation account.
5. Statements of profit or loss, appropriation accounts and statements of
financial position.
6. Interest on partners' loans, interest on capital, interest on drawings,
salaries and division of the residual profit or loss.
7. Sole trader adjustments (5.1) applied to partnerships.
8. Capital and current accounts: uses, differences, ledger form and
statement of financial position.

**Not examined:** dissolution; changes in profit-sharing ratio; limited
liability partnerships; statements on admission or departure of a partner.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partnership | Two or more people carrying on a business together to make a profit |
| Partnership agreement | Written terms on capital, profit sharing, interest, salaries and duties |
| Appropriation account | Shows how the profit for the year is divided between partners |
| Profit-sharing ratio | Agreed ratio for dividing residual profit or loss |
| Interest on capital | Return to partners on capital invested; an appropriation |
| Interest on drawings | Charge on partners' drawings; adds to profit to be shared |
| Partner's salary | Agreed amount for a partner's work; an appropriation, not wages |
| Capital account | Records a partner's long-term investment (usually fixed) |
| Current account | Records yearly appropriations and drawings |

## Advantages and disadvantages

**Advantages:** more capital; shared skills and workload; shared losses;
continuity during absence; privacy.

**Disadvantages:** shared profit; disagreements; partners bound by each
other's decisions; unlimited liability (ordinary partnerships); capital
still limited.

## Agreement contents

Capital contributed; profit-sharing ratio; interest on capital; interest on
drawings; salaries; interest on partners' loans; drawings limits; duties and
dispute procedures; joining and leaving arrangements.

## Appropriation account layout

```
Profit for the year (after loan interest)        X
Add  Interest on drawings (each partner)         X
                                                 X
Less Interest on capital (each partner)         (X)
     Salaries (each partner)                    (X)
Residual profit                                  X
Shared: Partner A (ratio)                        X
        Partner B (ratio)                        X
```

**Partner's loan interest** is an **expense** in the statement of profit or
loss, not an appropriation.

## Current account entries

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| Opening debit balance | Opening credit balance |
| Drawings | Interest on capital |
| Interest on drawings | Salary |
| Share of residual loss | Share of residual profit |
| Closing credit balance c/d | Interest on partner's loan (if unpaid) |
| | Closing debit balance c/d |

## Statement of financial position

```
Capital accounts       A  X    B  X     Total  X
Current accounts       A  X    B (X)    Total  X
                                                X
Loan from partner: non-current liability (or current, if due within a year)
```

## Quick worked check

Profit $40,000; interest on drawings A $200, B $300; interest on capital
A $2,500, B $1,500; salary B $8,000; ratio 1 : 1.
Residual = $40,000 + $500 − $12,000 = $28,500; each partner $14,250.
Total to A = $2,500 + $14,250 = $16,750; to B = $1,500 + $8,000 + $14,250
= $23,750, before interest on drawings ($200 and $300) is debited.

**If the residual is a loss:** share the loss in the profit-sharing ratio
and debit it to the current accounts. Interest on capital and salaries are
still credited, because the agreement grants them before the residual is
found.

## Exam traps

- Interest on drawings is **added**; interest on capital and salaries are
**deducted**.
- Loan interest: statement of profit or loss, not appropriation.
- Salaries to partners are not wages.
- A debit current account balance is shown in brackets and deducted.
- Use fixed capitals unless told otherwise.
- Share the **residual** profit in the ratio, not the whole profit.

## Quick self-test

1. State **two** advantages of forming a partnership.
2. State **two** items usually found in a partnership agreement.
3. What is the purpose of an appropriation account?
4. Where is interest on a partner's loan recorded?
5. Is interest on drawings added or deducted in the appropriation account?
6. Capitals: X $50,000, Y $30,000; interest on capital 4%. Calculate each
partner's interest.
7. Profit for the year $37,400; interest on capital $4,000 in total;
salaries $10,000; interest on drawings $600; ratio 2 : 1. Calculate each
share of residual profit.
8. Give **two** differences between capital and current accounts.
9. What does a debit balance on a current account mean?
10. On which side of a current account are drawings entered?
11. Name **one** topic in partnerships that is not examined.
12. How is a partner's salary different from an employee's wages in the
accounts?

### Answers

1. Any two: more capital; shared skills and workload; shared losses; cover
during absence; privacy.
2. Any two: capital to be contributed; profit-sharing ratio; interest on
capital; interest on drawings; salaries; interest on loans; drawings limits.
3. To show how the profit for the year is divided between the partners.
4. As an expense in the statement of profit or loss (credited to the
partner's current account if unpaid).
5. Added.
6. X $2,000; Y $1,200.
7. $37,400 + $600 − $4,000 − $10,000 = $24,000; partner with 2 shares
$16,000 and partner with 1 share $8,000.
8. Capital account records long-term investment, current account records
yearly appropriations and drawings; capital is usually fixed, current
changes each year; current can have a debit balance.
9. The partner has withdrawn more than his or her share of profit to date.
10. Debit.
11. Any one: dissolution; changes to the profit-sharing ratio; limited
liability partnerships; statements on admission or departure of a partner.
12. A partner's salary is an appropriation of profit; wages are an expense
in the statement of profit or loss.
