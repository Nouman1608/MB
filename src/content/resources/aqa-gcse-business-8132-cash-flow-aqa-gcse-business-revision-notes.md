## Core recall

### Cash and profit

| | Cash | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Money available to spend now | Revenue minus costs over a period |
| When it changes | When money actually moves in or out | When a sale is made or a cost is incurred |
| Can be positive while the other is negative? | Yes: a loan raises cash while the business makes a loss | Yes: credit sales record profit before any cash arrives |

Reasons they differ: sales on credit, buying on credit, loans received or repaid, owners paying in capital, large purchases of equipment.

### Why cash matters

Wages, rent and suppliers must be paid in cash on the due date.

**Consequences of cash flow problems:**

- staff and suppliers not paid on time
- suppliers refuse credit or stop deliveries
- expensive emergency borrowing
- missed discounts and opportunities
- damaged reputation with banks and suppliers
- insolvency and possible closure, even for a profitable business.

**Effects of positive cash flow:** bills paid on time, a safety margin, discounts taken, growth funded without borrowing.

### Forecast rows

| Row | Formula |
|---|---|
| Cash inflows | Sum of money coming in |
| Cash outflows | Sum of money going out |
| Net cash flow | Inflows − outflows |
| Opening balance | Previous month's closing balance |
| Closing balance | Opening balance + net cash flow |

You will complete and interpret sections of a forecast, not construct a whole one.

### Interpreting a forecast: four questions

1. **What** happens: does the closing balance go negative or get dangerously low?
2. **When:** which months?
3. **Why:** seasonal sales, a large purchase, late payers, costs too high?
4. **How serious:** how large and how long is the shortfall?

### The five solutions

| Solution | Fits | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Re-scheduling payments | Short gap, good supplier relations | Supplier may refuse or charge |
| Overdraft | Short, temporary gap | High interest; bank can withdraw it |
| Reducing cash outflow | Costs too high, spending can wait | Quality or growth may suffer |
| Increasing cash inflow | Slow payers or low sales | Discounts cut revenue; promotions cost money |
| Finding new sources of finance | Long-term shortage, large purchase | Interest, loss of control, or one-off |

Rule: match the solution to the **cause** and the **length** of the problem.

## Quick worked reminder

| £ | May | Jun | Jul |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inflows | 14,500 | 16,200 | 11,800 |
| Outflows | 15,100 | 13,900 | 14,600 |
| Net cash flow | −600 | 2,300 | −2,800 |
| Opening balance | 3,200 | 2,600 | 4,900 |
| Closing balance | 2,600 | 4,900 | 2,100 |

The balance never goes negative, but July's net outflow of £2,800 nearly halves it. If August is similar, the business would be close to an overdraft.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Cash | Money held by the business in notes, coins and bank accounts |
| Cash flow | The movement of money into and out of a business |
| Cash inflow | Money coming into the business, such as sales and loans received |
| Cash outflow | Money leaving the business, such as wages and payments to suppliers |
| Net cash flow | Cash inflows minus cash outflows for a period |
| Opening balance | The cash a business has at the start of a period |
| Closing balance | The cash a business has at the end of a period |
| Cash flow forecast | A prediction of future cash inflows and outflows |
| Insolvency | Being unable to pay debts when they are due |
| Credit sale | A sale where the customer pays later |

## Exam traps

- Cash and profit are different; never use them as the same word.
- Closing balance becomes next month's opening balance.
- Subtract a negative net cash flow; do not add it as a positive.
- A negative **net** cash flow is not the same as being overdrawn; check the **closing** balance.
- A one-month seasonal dip needs a short-term solution, not a long-term loan.
- When interpreting, use figures from the forecast and say when and why.

## Self-test

1. Define net cash flow.
2. How is the closing balance calculated?
3. Give two consequences of a cash flow problem.
4. Explain how a business can make a profit but have a cash flow problem.
5. Explain how a business can have cash but make a loss.
6. A business has an opening balance of £1,800, inflows of £9,400 and outflows of £10,600. Calculate the closing balance.
7. A business has an opening balance of −£500, inflows of £8,000 and outflows of £9,200. What is the closing balance?
8. Which solution suits a garden centre facing a shortage only in January?
9. Give one drawback of offering discounts to customers who pay early.
10. Why might reducing cash outflow harm a business?
11. Give one benefit of positive cash flow.
12. Do you need to construct a whole cash flow forecast in AQA GCSE Business?

### Answers

1. Cash inflows minus cash outflows for a period.
2. Opening balance + net cash flow.
3. Any two of: unable to pay staff or suppliers, suppliers stop credit, expensive borrowing, missed opportunities, damaged reputation, insolvency.
4. It sells on credit, so profit is recorded but cash arrives later, while it must pay its own costs now.
5. For example, it receives a loan, which adds cash but is not revenue.
6. Net: £9,400 − £10,600 = −£1,200; closing: £1,800 − £1,200 = **£600**.
7. Net: −£1,200; closing: −£500 − £1,200 = **−£1,700**.
8. An overdraft, or re-scheduling payments to suppliers, because the gap is short and temporary.
9. It reduces the revenue received from each sale.
10. For example, cutting staff or buying cheaper materials can reduce quality or service, or delaying investment can limit growth.
11. Any one of: bills paid on time, a safety margin, discounts taken, growth funded without borrowing.
12. No. You complete and interpret sections of one.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the five solutions to cash flow problems in AQA GCSE Business (8132)?**
Re-scheduling payments, overdrafts, reducing cash outflow, increasing cash inflow and finding new sources of finance.

**Why can a profitable business fail?**
If it cannot pay its debts on time because cash has not yet arrived, suppliers and lenders may force it to close, even though it is making a profit.
