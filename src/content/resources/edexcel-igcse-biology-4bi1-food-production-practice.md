These questions cover sub-topic 5(a), **Food production** (specification points 5.1 to 5.9B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Points 5.1 to 5.8 can be examined on either paper. Questions labelled **(Paper 2 only)** test the bold **B** point 5.9B. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

Learn the content first with the [food production study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-food-production/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-food-production-revision-notes/). The course hub is [Edexcel IGCSE Biology](/boards/edexcel/igcse/biology/).

## Questions

**1.** Lettuces are grown in a glasshouse.

**(a)** Give **two** ways in which a glasshouse can increase the yield of lettuces. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why adding carbon dioxide to the air in the glasshouse can increase yield. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why raising the temperature far above the optimum would reduce the yield. **[2]**

**2.** A farmer grows wheat. In one field the yield is 4.0 tonnes per hectare without fertiliser and 5.5 tonnes per hectare with fertiliser.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how nitrate in the fertiliser helps the wheat to grow. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** problem that can result if too much fertiliser is applied. **[1]**

**3.** Aphids feed on the sap of crop plants.

**(a)** Explain why a farmer wants to control the aphids. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using a pesticide. **[2]**
**(c)** Ladybirds eat aphids. Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using ladybirds to control aphids. **[2]**

**4.** Bread dough contains flour, water, sugar and yeast. The dough is left in a warm place and rises.

**(a)** Name the gas that makes the dough rise. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a word equation for the reaction that makes this gas in yeast without oxygen. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the bread is baked after the dough has risen. **[2]**

**5.** A student investigates the effect of temperature on yeast. She mixes yeast with glucose solution, covers it with oil and measures the volume of gas collected in 5 minutes.

| Temperature in °C | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of gas in cm³ | 3 | 9 | 21 | 30 | 0 |

**(a)** Explain why she added a layer of oil. **[1]**
**(b)** State **two** variables she should keep the same. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the rate of gas production at 30 °C in cm³ per minute. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe and explain the result at 60 °C. **[2]**

**6.** Yoghurt is made from milk by adding the bacterium *Lactobacillus*.

**(a)** Explain why the milk is heated before the bacteria are added. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the substance in the milk that the bacteria use and the acid they produce. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the milk thickens. **[2]**

**7.** A fermenter is used to grow microorganisms. Explain why each of these is controlled.

**(a)** Aseptic precautions **[2]**
**(b)** Temperature **[2]**
**(c)** Agitation **[2]**

**8.** A fermenter starts with 500 bacteria. The population doubles every 20 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria after 3 hours. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why the population would not keep doubling for days in a real fermenter. **[2]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) A fish farm keeps thousands of salmon in cages.

**(a)** Explain why fish of different sizes are kept in separate cages. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe **two** ways to control disease on a fish farm. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why uneaten food and fish waste must be controlled. **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) Evaluate the use of selective breeding and controlled feeding in fish farming to increase production of protein. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Any two of: warmer, so more enzyme activity; protected from wind, rain and frost; protected from pests; longer growing season [2]
**(b)** Carbon dioxide is a raw material of photosynthesis [1]; a faster rate of photosynthesis makes more glucose for growth, if light and temperature are not limiting [1]
**(c)** Enzymes involved in photosynthesis denature [1]; so the rate of photosynthesis falls and there is less growth [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Photosynthesis needs carbon dioxide" is the first mark. The second is for linking to growth and yield.

**2.** **(a)** (5.5 − 4.0) ÷ 4.0 × 100 [1] = **37.5%** [1]
**(b)** Nitrate is used to make amino acids [1]; which are used to make proteins for growth [1]
**(c)** Any one of: it is leached into streams and causes eutrophication, it wastes money [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), divide by the yield without fertiliser. Dividing by 5.5 gives 27.3%, which is wrong.

**3.** **(a)** Aphids remove sap, so the plants grow less [1]; they can spread disease between plants, so yield and quality are reduced [1]
**(b)** Advantage: kills pests quickly (accept easy to apply) [1]; disadvantage: kills useful insects or builds up in food chains or pests become resistant [1]
**(c)** Advantage: no chemical residue on the crop (accept specific to the pest) [1]; disadvantage: slow to work, or may not remove all the aphids [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give a different point for each part. "Kills pests" cannot be both an advantage and a disadvantage.

**4.** **(a)** Carbon dioxide [1]
**(b)** glucose [1] → ethanol + carbon dioxide [1]
**(c)** The heat kills the yeast / stops the process [1]; the gas bubbles expand and the dough sets, giving a light texture (accept: the ethanol evaporates) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "alcohol" is accepted for ethanol, but "lactic acid" is not.

**5.** **(a)** To exclude oxygen, so the yeast respires anaerobically [1]
**(b)** Any two of: mass of yeast, volume of glucose solution, concentration of glucose, time (5 minutes), pH [2]
**(c)** 21 ÷ 5 = **4.2 cm³ per minute** [1]
**(d)** No gas is produced at 60 °C [1]; the high temperature has denatured the enzymes in the yeast [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), divide by the 5 minutes of the experiment, not by the temperature.

**6.** **(a)** To kill unwanted microorganisms that could spoil the yoghurt [1]
**(b)** Lactose (milk sugar) [1]; lactic acid [1]
**(c)** The lactic acid lowers the pH [1]; the milk proteins clump together (coagulate), so the milk becomes thicker [1]
*Examiner insight:* A common error is to say that the bacteria make alcohol. Alcohol is made by yeast.

**7.** **(a)** To stop other microorganisms entering [1]; they would compete for nutrients or spoil the product [1]
**(b)** Enzymes work fastest at the optimum temperature [1]; respiration releases heat, so cooling is needed to keep the temperature at the optimum (too hot denatures enzymes) [1]
**(c)** Stirring mixes the contents [1]; so nutrients, oxygen and heat are spread evenly and the microorganisms are in contact with their food [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a consequence for the microorganisms. "To keep it clean" scores one mark at most.

**8.** **(a)** 3 hours = 180 minutes ÷ 20 = 9 doublings [1]; 2⁹ = 512 [1]; 500 × 512 = **256 000** [1]
**(b)** Nutrients or oxygen run out [1]; waste products build up (accept: space/pH changes) [1]
*Examiner insight:* If you multiply 500 by 9 you get 4500. Each doubling multiplies the number by 2, so the numbers grow quickly.

**9.** **(a)** Larger fish may eat smaller ones (intraspecific predation) [1]; separating them by size reduces the loss of fish [1]
**(b)** Any two of: lower stocking density, vaccines, treatments for infection, remove sick fish, good water quality [2]
**(c)** Waste produces ammonia that is toxic to fish [1]; waste decays and uses up oxygen / pollutes the water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the specific method in (b). "Stop disease spreading" restates the question.

**10.** Selective breeding produces fish that grow faster or resist disease, so more protein is produced in the same time [1]; controlled feeding gives the best growth with little waste, so food is not wasted and the water stays cleaner [1]; but selective breeding can reduce variation, so a disease could affect many fish (accept an equivalent disadvantage) [1]; a judgement, such as "overall these methods increase production if the water quality is also managed" [1]
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation marks need a balanced point and a conclusion, not just a list of benefits.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that more carbon dioxide or heat always increases yield.
- Calculating a percentage increase from the new value.
- Writing "alcohol" for the yoghurt product, or "oxygen" for the gas in dough.
- Giving a fermenter condition without the reason.
- Not converting hours to minutes in a doubling question.
- Describing biological control without a disadvantage.
