These notes condense section **3.2.5.6 Catalysts** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Transition metal catalysts study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts-transition-metals/), and exam-style questions are in the [Transition metal catalysts practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts-transition-metals-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Transition metals revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-transition-metals-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Distinguish **heterogeneous** and **homogeneous** catalysts.
- Explain **support media** and **poisoning**.
- Explain the importance of **variable oxidation states** in catalysis.
- Write equations for **V₂O₅** (Contact process), **Fe²⁺** (S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻) and **Mn²⁺** (autocatalysis).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | Speeds up a reaction by an alternative route with lower activation energy; regenerated |
| Heterogeneous | Catalyst in a different phase from the reactants |
| Homogeneous | Catalyst in the same phase as the reactants |
| Active site | Place on a catalyst surface where reactants are adsorbed |
| Support medium | Material on which a catalyst is spread to give a large surface area |
| Poisoning | Impurity blocks active sites |
| Autocatalysis | A product catalyses its own formation |

## Heterogeneous catalysts

1. Reactants adsorb at active sites.
2. Bonds weaken; molecules held close.
3. Reaction occurs on the surface.
4. Products desorb.

| Process | Catalyst |
|---|---|
| Contact process: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ | V₂O₅ |
| Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | Fe |

- **Support medium:** large surface area; minimises cost of expensive metals.
- **Poisoning:** impurities (sulfur compounds for iron in the Haber process; lead in catalytic converters) block active sites; efficiency falls; the catalyst must be replaced or the feed purified: a **cost implication**.

### V₂O₅ in the Contact process

```
SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄       V +5 to +4
2V₂O₄ + O₂ → 2V₂O₅            V +4 to +5
overall: 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃
```

## Homogeneous catalysts

The catalyst reacts to form an intermediate and is then regenerated.

### Fe²⁺ with S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻

Overall: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂.

Why slow without a catalyst: both reactants are negative ions that repel each other.

```
S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺
2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂
```

### Mn²⁺ autocatalysis

Overall: 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂.

```
4Mn²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O
2Mn³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 2CO₂
```

- Slow at first, since there is no Mn²⁺.
- Speeds up as Mn²⁺ forms.
- Slows when reactants are used up.
- Adding Mn²⁺ at the start removes the slow stage.

## Why transition metals make good catalysts

- Variable oxidation states: the metal can form intermediates by gaining or losing electrons.
- The catalyst returns to its original state, so it is regenerated.
- The alternative route has a lower activation energy.

## What a catalyst does and does not do

| Does | Does not |
|---|---|
| Lower the activation energy | Change ΔH |
| Increase the rate of the forward and reverse reactions equally | Change the equilibrium position |
| Get regenerated | Get used up |
| Allow lower temperature to be used | Make an unfeasible reaction feasible |

## Worked example

Show that Fe²⁺ catalyses S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻.

Add the two steps: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ + 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ + 2Fe²⁺ + I₂. Cancel the iron ions: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂. Fe²⁺ is regenerated, so it is a catalyst.

## Heterogeneous and homogeneous compared

| Feature | Heterogeneous | Homogeneous |
|---|---|---|
| Phase | Different from reactants | Same as reactants |
| Examples | Fe (Haber), V₂O₅ (Contact) | Fe²⁺ with S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻, Mn²⁺ |
| Where it acts | At active sites on the surface | Throughout the solution |
| Mechanism | Adsorption, reaction, desorption | Intermediate species formed and reacted |
| Separation | Easy: the catalyst is a separate solid | Harder: it is mixed with the products |
| Weakness | Poisoning, limited surface area | Needs separation from the product |

## Energy profile of a catalysed reaction

- Draw the uncatalysed reaction with one high activation energy peak.
- Draw the catalysed route with a lower activation energy; for a homogeneous catalyst there are often two smaller peaks with a dip between them for the intermediate.
- Reactants and products are at the same energy levels, because ΔH is unchanged.

## Practical: investigating Mn²⁺ autocatalysis

1. Mix acidified potassium manganate(VII) with ethanedioic acid in warm water and time the colour change from purple to colourless.
2. Repeat with a few drops of manganese(II) sulfate solution added at the start.
3. Result: the reaction with Mn²⁺ added loses its colour sooner, because the slow initial stage has gone.

## Exam traps

- "Catalysts are not involved in the reaction."
- Homogeneous and heterogeneous mixed up.
- No reason given for the slow uncatalysed reaction (repulsion of like charges).
- Oxidation states not given in the V₂O₅ equations.
- Mn³⁺ shown in the overall equation.
- Support medium described as making the catalyst stronger.
- Catalysts said to shift the position of equilibrium.
- Poison described as "reacting with the reactants" rather than blocking active sites.

## Self-test

1. Define a heterogeneous catalyst.
2. Name the catalyst in the Haber process.
3. Name the catalyst in the Contact process.
4. Why is a support medium used?
5. What is catalyst poisoning?
6. State why poisoning increases costs.
7. Why is the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ slow?
8. Write the first catalytic step for Fe²⁺ in this reaction.
9. What is autocatalysis?
10. Why does the MnO₄⁻ and C₂O₄²⁻ reaction speed up?
11. What happens to the activation energy when a catalyst is used?
12. Why are variable oxidation states important in catalysis?

### Answers

1. A catalyst in a different phase from the reactants.
2. Iron.
3. Vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅.
4. To maximise surface area and minimise cost.
5. An impurity blocks the active sites so the catalyst is less efficient.
6. The catalyst must be replaced or the feed purified.
7. Both are negative ions that repel each other.
8. S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺.
9. A product of the reaction acts as a catalyst for it.
10. Mn²⁺ forms and catalyses the reaction.
11. It decreases.
12. The metal can form intermediates and be regenerated, giving a lower activation energy route.
