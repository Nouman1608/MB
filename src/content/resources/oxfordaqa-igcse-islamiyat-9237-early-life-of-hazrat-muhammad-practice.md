## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Aminah [1].

**2.** Al-Amin (the trustworthy) and as-Sadiq (the truthful) [1]. Accept either.

**3.** Any two, [1] each: his mother Aminah; his wet-nurse Halimah; his grandfather Abdul Mutalib; his uncle Abu Talib.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: she was wealthy; she was a widow; she ran a successful trading business; she was respected in Makkah; she was about forty; she hired honest men to trade for her.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: Bahira was a Christian monk of Busra in Syria [1]; the caravan of Abu Talib stopped near him [1]; he noticed signs that the boy would be a prophet [1]; he questioned the boy and talked with Abu Talib [1]; he warned Abu Talib to protect the boy from those who might harm him [1]; Abu Talib then took him home [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Honesty about goods** [1]: he did not hide faults, so buyers could trust what they bought [1].
- **Fair prices** [1]: he gave full value to both buyers and sellers [1].
- **Keeping promises** [1]: he carried out agreements exactly, so people trusted him with their money [1].
- **Success without cheating** [1]: his trip to Syria for Khadija (RA) brought a good profit by lawful means [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Guardian** [1]: he took in the orphan after his mother's death and gave him love and care [1].
- **Status** [1]: he seated the boy beside the Ka'aba with the leaders, which gave him honour [1].
- **Example of leadership** [1]: as chief of Makkah and keeper of Zamzam, he showed the boy how to serve a community [1].
- **Link to the Ka'aba** [1]: he kept the sacred house, so the boy grew up near it [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge?" (Qur'an 93:6): Allah reminds the Prophet (pbuh) that He provided shelter through those who cared for him [2].
- The Prophet's (pbuh) later saying that the one who cares for an orphan will be near him in Paradise [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** his honesty, trustworthiness and fairness earned titles such as al-Amin, so people believed him when he later said he was a prophet.
- **For the statement:** the Qur'an praises his character directly.
- **Against the statement:** his upbringing by guardians and his marriage gave him protection and support that made his mission possible.
- **Against the statement:** his orphan years and work as a shepherd and trader shaped his care for the weak and the knowledge of trade.
- **Balanced view:** his character was central, but protection by Abu Talib, the support of Khadija (RA) and experience from his work all prepared him for his mission.

*Use of the passage*

- "great moral character" shows that Allah Himself praised his character.
- "indeed" stresses that the statement is certain and strong.
- "you O Muhammad" shows that Allah addresses him personally and honours him.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Companionship and support** [1]: Khadija (RA) comforted him and stood by him throughout hardship [1].
- **Economic security** [1]: her wealth freed him from financial worry and let him spend time in reflection [1].
- **First believer** [1]: she was the first to accept his message, which gave him confidence [1].
- **Family** [1]: their children, including Fatimah (RA), became part of the Prophet's household [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Did He not find you in need and make [you] self-sufficient?" (Qur'an 93:8): Allah reminds the Prophet (pbuh) that He made him independent of need [2].
- The Prophet's (pbuh) saying that the best of you are those who are best to their families [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** honesty, fairness and trustworthiness are needed in every period of history, especially in work and business.
- **For the statement:** his care for the poor and orphans is a model for how Muslims should treat the weak.
- **For the statement:** he earned his own living, showing that work is honourable.
- **Against the statement:** he lived in a different time and society, and Muslims today face different challenges.
- **Against the statement:** after prophethood he became a leader, teacher and judge, and his example in those roles is not shown by his early life alone.
- **Balanced view:** his early qualities are a lasting model for individual conduct, while his later life gives guidance for community and leadership.

*Use of the passage*

- "great moral character" presents his character as the model.
- "indeed" shows that this is a firm truth, not an opinion.
- The verse is general, not limited to one period, so it supports the view that his qualities are for all times.
