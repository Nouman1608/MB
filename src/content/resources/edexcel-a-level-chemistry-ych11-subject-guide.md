Pearson Edexcel International A Level Chemistry (YCH11) takes students
from atomic structure and bonding through energetics, kinetics and
equilibria to transition metals and organic synthesis. It is a natural
choice for students aiming at chemistry, medicine, pharmacy,
engineering or other science-based degrees. The qualification is
modular, with six externally assessed units: Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are
theory papers worth 20% each, and Units 3 and 6 are practical skills
papers worth 10% each. Units 1 to 3 form the International Advanced
Subsidiary (IAS) stage and Units 4 to 6 the IA2 stage. First teaching
was September 2018 and first examination January 2019.

## How the course is organised

Each unit has a clear title, and the theory units are subdivided into
five topic areas.

**IAS stage**

- **Unit 1: Structure, Bonding and Introduction to Organic Chemistry**
  covers formulae, equations and amount of substance; atomic structure
  and the periodic table; bonding and structure; introductory organic
  chemistry and alkanes; and alkenes.
- **Unit 2: Energetics, Group Chemistry, Halogenoalkanes and Alcohols**
  covers energetics; intermolecular forces; redox chemistry and Groups
  1, 2 and 7; an introduction to kinetics and equilibria; and the
  organic chemistry of halogenoalkanes, alcohols and spectra.
- **Unit 3: Practical Skills in Chemistry I** assesses the practical
  skills developed across the IAS content.

**IA2 stage**

- **Unit 4: Rates, Equilibria and Further Organic Chemistry** covers
  kinetics; entropy and energetics; chemical equilibria; acid-base
  equilibria; and carbonyls, carboxylic acids and chirality.
- **Unit 5: Transition Metals and Organic Nitrogen Chemistry** covers
  redox equilibria; transition metals and their chemistry; arenes;
  organic nitrogen compounds, including amines, amides, amino acids and
  proteins; and organic synthesis.
- **Unit 6: Practical Skills in Chemistry II** assesses practical skills
  at IA2 level.

## How it's assessed

| Unit 2 | WCH12/01 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| Unit 3 | WCH13/01 | 1 hour 20 minutes | 50 | 10% |
| Unit 4 | WCH14/01 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 5 | WCH15/01 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 6 | WCH16/01 | 1 hour 20 minutes | 50 | 10% |

Weightings are shares of the full International A Level, and every unit
is externally assessed.

- **Practical skills are tested on paper.** Units 3 and 6 are written
  papers, not a hands-on laboratory endorsement, so experimental work
  done in class needs to be turned into precise written answers about
  procedures, measurements, uncertainties and conclusions.
- **Mathematics carries real weight.** The specification states that a
  minimum of 20% of marks overall will target mathematics at Level 2 or
  above, and each written paper includes its own stated minimum; in
  Unit 1, for example, at least 18 marks do so.

As the course is modular, units can be taken in different series, and
the IAS can be certificated once Units 1 to 3 are complete.

## How the parts fit together

Chemistry at this level is strongly cumulative, and the unit titles
reveal how the IA2 content grows out of the IAS.

Physical chemistry develops in two steps. Unit 2 introduces energetics
and gives a first look at kinetics and equilibria; Unit 4 returns to
all three in depth, adding entropy, quantitative treatment of chemical
equilibria and acid-base equilibria. Redox chemistry follows the same
pattern, from redox and the groups in Unit 2 to redox equilibria and
transition metals in Unit 5.

Organic chemistry builds across all four theory units. Alkanes and
alkenes in Unit 1 lead to halogenoalkanes and alcohols in Unit 2, then
to carbonyls, carboxylic acids and chirality in Unit 4, and finally to
arenes, organic nitrogen compounds and organic synthesis in Unit 5.
Organic synthesis in particular expects you to draw on reactions from
every earlier unit.

Underneath everything is Unit 1's amount of substance content. Moles,
equations and calculations reappear in energetics, equilibria, acid-base
work and titrations, and they are central to the practical skills
papers. Becoming fluent in them early saves time throughout the course.

## Planning your study

A sensible route follows the unit order, because each unit assumes the
one before:

- **IAS stage:** begin with formulae, equations and amount of
  substance, then work through the rest of Unit 1 and on into Unit 2.
  Carry out practical work alongside each topic and practise writing it
  up in the style needed for Unit 3.
- **IA2 stage:** start Unit 4 by reviewing the Unit 2 introductions to
  kinetics and equilibria, then progress to Unit 5. Build a single
  organic reactions map that you extend unit by unit, which becomes
  invaluable for organic synthesis.
- **Throughout:** practise calculations every week rather than in
  occasional blocks.

Check the series in which each unit will be sat with your school, since
modular entry patterns vary. Begin past papers for a unit once its
content is complete, and use the exam-preparation page for advice on
the six units and the two practical skills papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Edexcel International A Level Chemistry?**
Six. Units 1 to 3 make up the IAS and Units 4 to 6 the IA2. Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are worth 20% each and Units 3 and 6 are worth 10% each.

**Are the IAL Chemistry practical units hands-on exams?**
No. Units 3 and 6, Practical Skills in Chemistry I and II, are written papers of 1 hour 20 minutes and 50 marks each.

**How much maths is in IAL Chemistry?**
The specification states that a minimum of 20% of marks overall will target mathematics at Level 2 or above. In Unit 1, for example, at least 18 marks do so.

**How long are the IAL Chemistry theory papers?**
Units 1 and 2 are 1 hour 30 minutes for 80 marks, and Units 4 and 5 are 1 hour 45 minutes for 90 marks.

**What does Unit 5 cover?**
Unit 5, Transition Metals and Organic Nitrogen Chemistry, covers redox equilibria, transition metals, arenes, organic nitrogen compounds and organic synthesis.
