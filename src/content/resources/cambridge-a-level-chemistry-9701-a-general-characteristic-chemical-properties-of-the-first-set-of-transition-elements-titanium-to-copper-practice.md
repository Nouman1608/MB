of transition elements, titanium to copper) of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701,
2025–2027 series. Titration data are invented for practice. Use E⦵ values: MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺ +1.52 V;
Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ +1.33 V; Cl₂/Cl⁻ +1.36 V; Br₂/Br⁻ +1.07 V; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77 V. Revise first with the

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the terms *ligand* and *complex*. **[2]**

**2.** Which ligand is bidentate?

A: CN⁻

B: NH₃

C: C₂O₄²⁻

D: EDTA⁴⁻

**[1]**

**3.** What is the coordination number of chromium in [Cr(en)₂Cl₂]⁺?

A: 2

B: 4

C: 6

D: 8

**[1]**

**4.** Give the shape and bond angle of each complex: (i) [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, (ii) [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂].
**[2]**

**5.** Predict the formula and charge of the complex formed when Fe²⁺ bonds to six cyanide ions.
**[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Aqueous ammonia is added dropwise to aqueous copper(II) sulfate until it is in excess.

**(a)** Describe what is observed. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the formation of the precipitate from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and hydroxide
ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the ligand exchange that occurs with excess ammonia. **[1]**

**7.** Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to a pink solution of cobalt(II) chloride.

**(a)** State the colour change and give the formula of the new complex. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the shape of the complex changes. **[2]**

**(c)** State and explain what is seen when the resulting solution is diluted with water. **[1]**

**8.** Compare the reactions of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess aqueous ammonia, giving
the formula of each product. **[2]**

**9.** Use E⦵ values to explain why acidified dichromate(VI) can be used to oxidise bromide ions,
and why a manganate(VII) titration should not be acidified with hydrochloric acid. **[3]**

**10.** 20.0 cm³ of a solution of Fe²⁺ ions, acidified with dilute sulfuric acid, needs
16.80 cm³ of 0.0180 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄ for complete reaction.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺. **[2]**

**(c)** State how the end point is detected. **[1]**

**11.** 0.268 g of sodium ethanedioate, Na₂C₂O₄ (M = 134.0 g mol⁻¹), is dissolved in dilute sulfuric
acid and titrated at about 60 °C with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of KMnO₄ needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the colour of the first few drops of KMnO₄ is slow to disappear, but later
drops are decolourised quickly. **[2]**

**12.** 25.0 cm³ of copper(II) sulfate solution is added to excess potassium iodide. The iodine
formed needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate.

**(a)** Write equations for the two reactions. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of Cu²⁺. **[2]**

**13.** Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) oxidises Fe²⁺:
Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O.
25.0 cm³ of Fe²⁺ solution needs 22.50 cm³ of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ K₂Cr₂O₇. Calculate the
concentration of Fe²⁺. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Ligand: a species with a lone pair of electrons that forms a dative covalent bond to a
central metal atom or ion [1]. Complex: a molecule or ion formed by a central metal atom or ion
surrounded by one or more ligands [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. CN⁻ and NH₃ are monodentate; EDTA⁴⁻ is polydentate.

**3.** **C** [1]. Each en forms two bonds (4) plus two Cl⁻ (2).

**4.** (i) linear, 180° [1]; (ii) square planar, 90° [1].

**5.** [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ [1].

**6. (a)** A pale blue precipitate forms [1], which dissolves in excess ammonia to give a deep blue
solution [1].

**(b)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ + 2H₂O [1].

**(c)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O [1].

**7. (a)** Pink to blue [1]; [CoCl₄]²⁻ [1].

**(b)** Chloride ions are larger than water molecules [1], so only four fit around the Co²⁺ ion:
the six-coordinate octahedral complex becomes a four-coordinate tetrahedral complex [1].

**(c)** The solution turns pink again: the equilibrium [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O
shifts to the left as water is added [1].

**8.** Copper: only four water ligands are replaced, giving [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ [1]. Cobalt: all six
are replaced, giving [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ [1].

**9.** Dichromate with bromide: E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = 1.33 − 1.07 = +0.26 V, positive, so feasible
[1]. Manganate(VII) with chloride: E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = 1.52 − 1.36 = +0.16 V, so MnO₄⁻ would
oxidise Cl⁻ from the acid [1], using up extra titrant and giving an inaccurate (too large) titre
[1].

**10. (a)** MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O [1].

**(b)** mol MnO₄⁻ = 0.01680 × 0.0180 = 3.02 × 10⁻⁴; mol Fe²⁺ = 1.51 × 10⁻³ [1]. Concentration =
1.51 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0200 = **0.0756 mol dm⁻³** [1].

**(c)** The first permanent pale pink colour (excess MnO₄⁻) [1].

**11. (a)** mol C₂O₄²⁻ = 0.268 ÷ 134.0 = 2.00 × 10⁻³; mol MnO₄⁻ = 2.00 × 10⁻³ × 2/5 = 8.00 × 10⁻⁴
[1]. Volume = 8.00 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0200 = 0.0400 dm³ = **40.0 cm³** [1].

**(b)** At first the reaction is slow because both reactants are negative ions, which repel [1].
Mn²⁺ ions produced catalyse the reaction (autocatalysis), so it speeds up [1].

**12. (a)** 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂ [1]; I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ [1].

**(b)** mol S₂O₃²⁻ = 0.02240 × 0.100 = 2.24 × 10⁻³ = mol Cu²⁺ [1]. Concentration =
2.24 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0896 mol dm⁻³** [1].

**13.** mol Cr₂O₇²⁻ = 0.02250 × 0.0150 = 3.375 × 10⁻⁴; mol Fe²⁺ = 6 × 3.375 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.025 × 10⁻³
[1]. Concentration = 2.025 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0810 mol dm⁻³** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting ligands instead of dative bonds for the coordination number.
- Leaving ligand charges out of the overall charge.
- Writing a hexaammine complex for copper, or a tetraammine for cobalt.
- Describing a precipitate without "precipitate", or a colour without saying whether it is a
  solution or a solid.
- Using the wrong mole ratio in manganate(VII), dichromate(VI) or copper–iodide calculations.
- Forgetting that hydrochloric acid is unsuitable for acidifying manganate(VII).
