These questions cover **4.2.5 Static electricity** (4.2.5.1 and 4.2.5.2) of AQA GCSE Physics (8463), a
physics-only subtopic assessed on Paper 1 at both tiers. Every explanation should start from the movement of
electrons.

Revise first with the [static electricity study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-static-electricity/)
and the [static electricity revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-static-electricity-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the charged particles that are transferred when two insulators are rubbed together. **[1]**

**2.** A balloon is rubbed on a jumper and becomes negatively charged. State the charge on the jumper and explain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** State what happens when two objects with different types of charge are brought close together. **[1]**

**4.** Complete the sentence: the force between two charged objects gets ______ as the distance between them decreases. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why static charge builds up on insulators rubbed together but not on a metal rod held in the hand. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the direction of the field lines around an isolated positively charged sphere, and where the field is strongest. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student rubs a Perspex rod with a dry cloth. The rod becomes positively charged.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of electrons, how the rod and the cloth become charged. **[3]**
**(b)** The student hangs the rod from an insulating thread and brings the cloth close to one end, without touching. Describe and explain what happens. **[3]**

**8.** Two identical rods are rubbed with the same cloth. One rod is hung from a thread. The second rod is brought near it.

**(a)** Describe what the student would observe. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how this observation is evidence that charged objects exert forces without contact. **[2]**

**9.** Explain what is meant by an electric field, and use the idea of a field to explain why the force between two charged spheres increases as they are moved closer together. **[4]**

**10.** A charged metal sphere stands on an insulating base. An earthed metal sphere is moved slowly towards it, and at a small separation a spark jumps between them.

**(a)** Explain why the charge on the first sphere does not leak away before the spark. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the spark happens only when the spheres are close together. **[3]**
**(c)** State which particles cross the gap in the spark. **[1]**

**11.** A girl slides off a plastic chair and then feels a small shock as she touches a metal radiator connected to earth. Explain how she became charged and why she feels a shock. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Electrons [1]

**2.** Positive [1]; electrons were transferred from the jumper to the balloon, so the jumper lost electrons and is left with an equal positive charge [1]

**3.** They attract each other [1]

**4.** stronger [1]

**5.** Electrons cannot move freely through an insulator, so the transferred charge stays where it is [1]; in a metal held in the hand, charge can flow through the metal and the person to earth, so it does not build up [1]

**6.** Straight radial lines with arrows pointing away from the sphere [1]; strongest close to the surface, where the lines are closest together [1]

**7. (a)** Electrons are transferred by rubbing [1]; from the rod to the cloth [1]; the rod loses electrons and becomes positive, and the cloth gains electrons and becomes negative, with an equal amount of charge [1]
**(b)** The end of the rod moves towards the cloth [1]; because the rod and cloth have different types of charge, so they attract [1]; this is a non-contact force, acting across the gap [1]

**8. (a)** The hanging rod turns away from the second rod [1]
**(b)** Both rods have the same type of charge, so they repel [1]; the hanging rod moves even though nothing touches it, so a force acts across the gap [1]

**9.** An electric field is a region around a charged object in which another charged object experiences a force [1]; each sphere is in the electric field of the other, and it is the field that exerts the force [1]; the field is strongest close to a charged object and weaker further away [1]; so as the spheres move closer, each is in a stronger part of the other's field and the force increases [1]

**10. (a)** The insulating base stops charge flowing away to earth [1]
**(b)** As the gap gets smaller the electric field between the spheres becomes stronger [1]; when the field is strong enough [1]; electrons are made to cross the air gap, producing the spark [1]
**(c)** Electrons [1]

**11.** Rubbing between her clothes and the plastic chair transfers electrons [1]; so she becomes charged (and the chair gets an equal opposite charge) [1]; her shoes or the chair are insulators, so the charge stays on her rather than flowing to earth [1]; as her hand approaches the earthed radiator, the pd and the electric field in the gap become large [1]; electrons jump across the gap as a spark, which she feels as a small shock [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that names the shock but leaves out how the charge arose, or why it did not leak away, stays in the lower marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying protons or positive charges move.
- Forgetting that the charges on the two rubbed objects are equal and opposite.
- Describing an observation without saying no contact occurred.
- Explaining a spark without the strong field in the gap.
- Drawing or describing field arrows in the wrong direction.
