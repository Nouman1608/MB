These notes condense sub-topic **18.1 Variation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 8 are Core. Outcomes 9 and 10 (gene mutation and the sources of genetic variation) are **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [variation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-variation/) first, then test yourself with the [variation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-variation-practice/). The [variation and selection revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-variation-and-selection-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 18.

## Core recall: the definition

> Variation is the **differences between individuals of the same species**.

Differences between species (a horse and a zebra) are not what this outcome means.

## Core recall: two types of variation

| | Continuous | Discontinuous |
|---|---|---|
| Phenotypes | a **range** between two extremes | a **limited number**, **no intermediates** |
| Syllabus examples | body length, body mass | ABO blood groups, seed shape in peas, seed colour in peas |
| Cause | genes **and** environment | **usually genes only** |
| Data collected by | measuring | counting individuals in each category |
| Graph | histogram, bars touching | bar chart, bars separate |

**Decision test:** can the feature take any value between the smallest and largest? Continuous. A few separate categories? Discontinuous.

## Core recall: causes

- **Genes only** (most discontinuous features): blood group is fixed by inherited alleles; no change in diet or climate alters it.
- **Genes and environment** (continuous features): alleles set the possible range; conditions decide where an individual falls.
- Environmental factors to quote for plants: light, water, temperature, mineral ions. For animals: diet, exercise, disease.
- **Evidence for an environmental effect:** compare organisms with identical genes (cuttings from one plant, identical twins). Any difference between them is environmental.

## Core recall: investigating variation

Method checklist:

- define the feature and how it is measured (same instrument, same starting point);
- take a large, random sample;
- record in a results table with units in the heading;
- continuous: group into equal class intervals, draw a histogram;
- discontinuous: tally each category, draw a bar chart;
- state the modal class or most common category.

Calculation to expect: **percentage in a class = number in class ÷ total × 100**.

## Core recall: mutation

- Mutation is **genetic change**.
- Mutation is the way **new alleles** are formed.
- **Ionising radiation** (X-rays, gamma rays, radiation from radioactive substances) and **some chemicals** (for example, some in tobacco smoke) **increase the rate** of mutation.

## Extended recall

- **Gene mutation:** a **random** change in the **base sequence** of DNA.
- Changed base sequence → may change amino acid sequence → may change protein shape and function.
- **Sources of genetic variation in populations:** mutation, meiosis, random mating, random fertilisation.
- Only **mutation** makes new alleles; the other three make **new combinations** of alleles.
- In asexual reproduction (for example bacteria), offspring are identical to the parent, so mutation is the main source of variation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Variation | Differences between individuals of the same species |
| Phenotype | The observable features of an organism |
| Continuous variation | A range of phenotypes between two extremes |
| Discontinuous variation | A limited number of phenotypes with no intermediates |
| Mutation | Genetic change |
| Allele | An alternative form of a gene; new alleles arise by mutation |
| Ionising radiation | Radiation such as X-rays and gamma rays that increases mutation rate |
| Gene mutation (Extended) | A random change in the base sequence of DNA |
| Random fertilisation (Extended) | Any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete |
| Random mating (Extended) | Any individual in a population may mate with any other |

## Exam traps

- **"Between organisms."** The definition needs "of the same species".
- **Rounded data.** Mass recorded to the nearest kilogram is still continuous.
- **Cause of discontinuous variation.** Write "usually genes only", never "environment".
- **Cause of continuous variation.** "Environment" alone is wrong; it is genes and environment.
- **Wrong graph.** Histogram for continuous; bar chart for discontinuous.
- **"Radiation causes mutation."** Say radiation **increases the rate** of mutation.
- **Mutation with a purpose.** Mutations are random; organisms do not mutate "in order to" survive.
- **Meiosis makes new alleles.** It does not; it makes new combinations. Only mutation forms new alleles.
- Giving an example of variation between two species.
- Stating the type of variation with no reason when a reason is asked for.
- Plotting continuous data as a bar chart with gaps.
- Saying the environment causes discontinuous variation.
- Forgetting "random" in the Extended definition of gene mutation.

## Command-word reminders

- **State** the type of variation: type plus reason ("range of values" or "no intermediates").
- **Describe** an investigation: feature, measurement, sample, table, graph.
- **Suggest** a cause: name a factor that fits the organism in the question.

## Self-test

1. Define variation.
2. Give the two syllabus examples of continuous variation.
3. Name the three syllabus examples of discontinuous variation.
4. Which type of variation is usually caused by genes only?
5. Which graph should be drawn for data on the length of 60 earthworms?
6. A student records the number of people in a class with each ABO blood group. Which graph should she draw?
7. Thirty cuttings from one plant grow to different heights. What causes the difference, and why?
8. Describe mutation.
9. State two factors that increase the rate of mutation.
10. What is the importance of mutation for variation?
11. (Extended) Describe a gene mutation.
12. (Extended) List the four sources of genetic variation in populations and say which one forms new alleles.

### Answers

1. Differences between individuals of the same species.
2. Body length and body mass.
3. ABO blood groups, seed shape in peas, seed colour in peas.
4. Discontinuous variation.
5. A histogram (continuous data, bars touching).
6. A bar chart with separate bars (discontinuous data).
7. The environment; cuttings from one plant are genetically identical, so genes cannot explain the difference.
8. Mutation is genetic change.
9. Ionising radiation and some chemicals.
10. It is the way in which new alleles are formed.
11. A random change in the base sequence of DNA.
12. Mutation, meiosis, random mating and random fertilisation; only mutation forms new alleles.
