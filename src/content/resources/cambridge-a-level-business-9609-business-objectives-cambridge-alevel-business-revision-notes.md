Condensed notes for **1.4 Business objectives** in Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 (2026–2028). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Business objectives study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-business-objectives-cambridge-alevel-business/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [Business objectives practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-business-objectives-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/).

## What 1.4 asks you to handle

- **1.4.1**: objectives in private, public and social enterprises; why objectives matter; CSR and the triple bottom line; mission → aims → objectives → strategy → tactics.
- **1.4.2**: decision-making stages and the role of objectives; why objectives change; targets and budgets; communicating objectives; SMART; ethics.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Mission statement | A qualitative statement of the organisation's purpose and values; no figure, no deadline |
| Aim | A broad, long-term intention that the objectives serve |
| Objective | A specific, measurable goal with a time limit |
| Strategy | The long-term plan for reaching an objective |
| Tactic | A short-term action that carries out part of a strategy |
| Target | An objective broken into figures for a department, product or period |
| Budget | A financial plan showing the money expected or allowed for a period |
| CSR | Accepting responsibilities to society and the environment beyond profit |
| Triple bottom line | Judging performance on economic (financial), social and environmental objectives |
| Ethics | Moral principles that guide decisions and behaviour |
| SMART | Specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-limited |

## Objectives by organisation (recall grid)

- **Sole trader**: survival, owner's income, independence.
- **Private limited company**: profit, steady growth, keeping control.
- **Public limited company**: shareholder returns, dividends, share price (a low share price raises takeover risk).
- **Public sector**: service for all, value for money, staying within budget, service standards.
- **Social enterprise**: social or environmental purpose first; surplus to stay financially sustainable.

Link the objective to **who sets it**: owners, shareholders, government or a social mission.

## The four jobs of an objective

1. Decision rule: ranks options.
2. Delegation: lets managers hand over a target, not a method.
3. Control: actual results compared with the objective.
4. Coordination: functional objectives come from one corporate objective.

## Objectives that conflict

| Pair | Why they clash |
|---|---|
| Profit vs growth | Expansion costs cut profit in the short run |
| Market share vs margin | Lower prices win share but earn less per sale |
| Short-term profit vs long-term survival | Cutting training or research boosts profit now, weakens the firm later |
| Financial vs social/environmental | Fair wages, clean energy and better materials raise costs |

## Hierarchy: what changes at each level

- **Tactics** change often; a failed promotion is replaced without changing the objective.
- **Strategy** changes if resources or the market shift.
- **Objectives** change when the business's situation changes.
- **Mission** should rarely change.

## Decision-making stages

Set objectives → gather information → identify options → evaluate options against the objective → choose and implement → review against the objective → (back to start).

The objective is the **test** used at the evaluation stage and the **benchmark** at review. The option with most revenue is not always best; rank by the objective.

## Why objectives change

- **Internal**: growth beyond survival, new owner or chief executive, losses that force a focus on cash.
- **External**: recession, a new competitor, new technology, new laws, stakeholder pressure.

Exam rule: name the trigger, then say which objective rises or falls in priority.

## Targets and budgets: the method

1. Find the current figure (profit = margin × revenue).
2. Find the target figure.
3. Work out the gap.
4. Turn the gap into a budget (for example a cost budget = revenue − target profit).
5. Allocate the gap between departments.

*Quick check:* revenue $500 000, margin 12%, objective 15% at the same revenue → profit must rise from $60 000 to $75 000, so costs must fall from $440 000 to $425 000, a $15 000 cut.

## Communicating objectives

- **Involvement** in setting targets raises commitment.
- **Imposed** targets without reasons cause resistance and stress.
- **Measured-target trap**: staff hit what is measured and neglect what is not.

## SMART rewrite pattern

Weak: "Grow online sales." → SMART: "Raise online sales revenue from $120 000 to $150 000 (25%) by the end of the financial year, using the current website team."

## Ethics

- Shapes **objectives** (what the firm will not do for profit) and **activities** (how it reaches its goals).
- Evaluate: a cost in a price-driven market; an investment where customers reward responsible behaviour.

## CSR: weigh it

- **For**: reputation, loyalty, recruitment, lower risk of fines or boycotts.
- **Against**: higher costs, reporting costs, less flexibility; claims not backed by action can destroy trust.
- **Depends on**: customer values in the market, financial strength, whether the commitment is genuine.

## Exam traps

- A mission statement is **not** a target.
- Not every business aims to **maximise** profit.
- "Achievable" (resources) and "realistic" (sensible in the market) are different tests; use the context to separate them.
- Public-sector objectives are not "the same as a charity's": they are set by government and funded by taxation.
- In a calculation, keep the units: dollars, percentages and the time period.
- An "evaluate" answer about CSR needs both costs and benefits and a judgement.

## Self-test

1. State the difference between an aim and an objective.
2. Give one objective typical of a public-sector organisation.
3. Why might a plc be more concerned with its share price than a private limited company?
4. Name the three elements of the triple bottom line.
5. At which two stages of decision-making is the objective used as a test or benchmark?
6. Give one internal and one external reason why objectives change.
7. A firm has revenue of $800 000 and a 5% margin. It wants a 6% margin at the same revenue. By how much must costs fall?
8. Which SMART element is missing from "Raise sales by 10%"?
9. Explain one way in which poorly communicated objectives can harm a business.
10. Give one example of ethics shaping an activity rather than an objective.
11. Why might a social enterprise still need a financial objective?
12. Which level of the hierarchy is most likely to change when a promotion fails?

**Answers**

1. An aim is a broad long-term intention; an objective is a specific, measurable goal with a deadline that serves the aim.
2. For example, providing a service to all citizens, staying within budget, or meeting a service standard such as a waiting-time target.
3. Its shares are traded publicly, so a falling share price upsets shareholders who can sell easily and makes a takeover more likely.
4. Economic (financial), social and environmental.
5. When options are evaluated (the objective ranks them) and at review (results are compared with it).
6. Internal: for example, a new chief executive or growth past the survival stage. External: for example, a recession or a new competitor.
