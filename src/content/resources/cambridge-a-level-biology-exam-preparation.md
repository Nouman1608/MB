Cambridge A-Level Biology 9700 has five externally assessed papers. Paper 1 Multiple Choice (75 minutes, 40 marks, 15.5% of the A Level), Paper 2 AS Level Structured Questions (75 minutes, 60 marks, 23%), Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills (120 minutes, 40 marks, 11.5%), Paper 4 A Level Structured Questions (120 minutes, 100 marks, 38.5%) and Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation (75 minutes, 30 marks, 11.5%). The weightings sum to 100%. Revise with our pages on [cell membranes and transport](/resources/a-level-biology-9700-cell-membranes-and-transport/), [control and coordination](/resources/a-level-biology-9700-control-and-coordination/) and [carbohydrates and lipids](/resources/a-level-biology-9700-carbohydrates-and-lipids/).

## Two routes: AS Level and A Level

The structure is staged. Papers 1 to 3 alone can be certificated as a standalone Cambridge International AS Level, weighted 31%, 46% and 23% of the AS Level in that route. Alternatively, a candidate continues to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level. Confirm with your centre which route you are on, because the percentages and the papers you sit depend on it.

## Papers 1 and 2: the AS written papers

Paper 1 is 75 minutes and 40 marks of multiple choice, 15.5% of the A Level. Paper 2 is 75 minutes and 60 marks of AS structured questions, 23%. **How to spend the time:** for Paper 1, 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per question mark, so a first pass answering everything you are sure of, then a second pass on hard items, works well. For Paper 2, 75 / 60 = 1.25 minutes per mark, so read the stem carefully and write answers matched to the marks available.

## Paper 3: Advanced Practical Skills

Paper 3 is a practical paper of 120 minutes and 40 marks, 11.5% of the A Level. It is externally assessed. **How to spend the time:** 120 / 40 = 3 minutes per mark, the most generous rate of any paper, because practical work takes time. Practise the manipulation, measurement, recording and graph work you meet in class practicals, and rehearse presenting results in clear tables with units and headings.

## Paper 4: A Level Structured Questions

Paper 4 is 120 minutes and 100 marks, 38.5% of the A Level, the largest single paper. **How to spend the time:** 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark. It is your biggest opportunity, so allow substantial revision time for the A Level content.

## Paper 5: Planning, Analysis and Evaluation

Paper 5 is 75 minutes and 30 marks, 11.5% of the A Level. **How to spend the time:** 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per mark. Practise designing investigations, identifying variables, analysing data and evaluating methods in unfamiliar contexts, since this paper rewards experimental reasoning more than recall.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

| Paper | Minutes | Marks | Minutes per mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 75 | 40 | 75 / 40 = 1.875 |
| 2 | 75 | 60 | 75 / 60 = 1.25 |
| 3 | 120 | 40 | 120 / 40 = 3.0 |
| 4 | 120 | 100 | 120 / 100 = 1.2 |
| 5 | 75 | 30 | 75 / 30 = 2.5 |

Check on weightings: 15.5 + 23 + 11.5 + 38.5 + 11.5 = 100. Total marks: 40 + 60 + 40 + 100 + 30 = 270.

## Command words and question styles

Cambridge biology uses command words such as "state", "describe", "explain", "suggest" and "calculate". "Describe" asks what happens, "explain" asks why, and "suggest" asks you to apply knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. Match your response to the word and to the marks, and give units and significant figures in calculations.

## Worked practice scenario: rate and magnification

**Question:** In an experiment on the enzyme catalase, 24 cm3 of oxygen was collected in 120 s at 20 degrees Celsius and 48 cm3 in 100 s at 35 degrees Celsius. (a) Calculate the rate of reaction at each temperature. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in rate. (c) A cell has an image length of 45 mm in a drawing and a real length of 0.15 mm. Calculate the magnification.

**Model answer:**

```
(a) Rate at 20 C = 24 / 120 = 0.20 cm3 per s
    Rate at 35 C = 48 / 100 = 0.48 cm3 per s

(b) Increase = (0.48 - 0.20) / 0.20 x 100 = 140%

(c) Magnification = image length / actual length = 45 / 0.15 = x300
```

State the units: cm3 per s for rates, and no units for magnification. A brief comment would add that the rate rises with temperature because molecules have more kinetic energy and collide more often with the active sites, up to the point where denaturation begins.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are sitting the AS route or the full A Level.
- Make a table of the five papers with times, marks and weightings.
- Practise Paper 5 style planning questions with no recall element.
- Keep a list of definitions and command words.
- Do practical write-ups to the same standard as exam answers.
- Practise unit conversions before magnification questions.
- Read each question stem twice.
- Re-check calculations, units and rounding at the end.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A-Level Biology 9700?**
There are five externally assessed papers: Multiple Choice, AS Level Structured Questions, Advanced Practical Skills, A Level Structured Questions, and Planning, Analysis and Evaluation.

**What is the biggest paper?**
Paper 4, A Level Structured Questions, with 120 minutes, 100 marks and 38.5% of the A Level.

**Can I take just the AS Level?**
Yes. Papers 1 to 3 can be certificated as a standalone AS Level, weighted 31%, 46% and 23% in that route.

**Is there a practical paper?**
Yes. Paper 3, Advanced Practical Skills, lasts 120 minutes, carries 40 marks and is 11.5% of the A Level.

**Which examination years does this syllabus cover?**
It is for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027.
