> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **20.2 Habitat destruction** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Everything in this sub-topic is Core, so all candidates should attempt every question. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [habitat destruction study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-habitat-destruction-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [habitat destruction revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-habitat-destruction-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is the description of biodiversity?

A: the number of organisms that live in an area

B: the number of different species that live in an area

C: the mass of living material in an area

D: the number of different habitats in a country

**[1]**

**2.** Which of these is an example of habitat destruction by extraction of natural resources?

A: draining a marsh to build houses

B: clearing grassland to graze cattle

C: digging an open-cast coal mine

D: releasing untreated sewage into a river

**[1]**

**3.** State the **three** reasons for habitat destruction named in the syllabus. **[3]**

**4.** A field of wheat contains millions of plants. Explain why it has low biodiversity. **[2]**

**5.** State how humans can have a negative impact on habitats through altering food webs, and give **one** example. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a fall in biodiversity in a forest and the extinction of a species. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Part of a coastal mangrove forest was cleared to make ponds for farming shrimps. Students counted the species in equal areas of each habitat.

| Group | Mangrove forest | Shrimp ponds |
|---|---|---|
| Fish species | 35 | 3 |
| Crab species | 14 | 2 |
| Bird species | 22 | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the total number of species in each habitat. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the number of species when mangrove forest is replaced by shrimp ponds. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons why the shrimp ponds support fewer species. **[2]**

**8.** A river runs through a valley. Before the forest on the valley sides was cleared, the river flooded twice in ten years. After the forest was cleared, it flooded seven times in ten years.

Explain how clearing the forest increased the number of floods. **[4]**

**9.** Scientists measured soil loss from two hillsides with the same slope and rainfall. The forested hillside lost 0.4 tonnes of soil per hectare per year. The cleared hillside lost 12 tonnes per hectare per year.

**(a)** Calculate how many times more soil was lost from the cleared hillside. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why more soil was lost from the cleared hillside. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** effect of this soil loss on a farmer who grows crops on the cleared hillside. **[1]**

**10.** A company opens a limestone quarry in an area of grassland that contains rare orchids found in only a few places.

**(a)** Describe how the quarry destroys the habitat. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the quarry could lead to the extinction of the orchid species. **[2]**

**11.** Wolves in a valley were hunted until none were left. Over the next twenty years, the number of deer increased. The deer ate most of the young trees along the river, and the number of songbird species nesting in the trees fell.

Use ideas about food chains and food webs to explain how hunting the wolves had a negative impact on the habitat. **[4]**

**12.** Explain **three** ways in which clearing a rainforest by felling and burning increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A and B are increases in area for housing and livestock; D is freshwater pollution. Mining extracts a natural resource.

**3.** Increased area for housing, crop plant production and livestock production [1]; extraction of natural resources [1]; freshwater and marine pollution [1].

**4.** Biodiversity is the number of different species [1]; the field contains mainly one species, wheat, so the number of species is low even though there are many organisms [1].

**5.** Altering food webs / food chains can damage habitats [1]; any one example, such as: hunting a predator lets its prey increase and overgraze plants; overfishing removes food for seabirds; introducing a species with no predators replaces native species [1].

**6.** A fall in biodiversity means fewer species live in that forest; the species may still survive elsewhere [1]. Extinction means every individual of a species has died, so the species is lost permanently [1].

**7. (a)** Mangrove forest: 71; shrimp ponds: 13 [1].

**(b)** (71 − 13) ÷ 71 × 100 [1] = 81.7% [1].

**(c)** Any two from: the mangrove trees that provided food and shelter have been removed; the ponds contain mainly one farmed species / are like a monoculture; fewer types of habitat (roots, mud, canopy) are present; chemicals or waste from the farm may pollute the water [2].

**8.** Any four from:

- fewer trees take up water through their roots [1]
- less water is lost by transpiration [1]
- less rain is intercepted by leaves / the canopy [1]
- more rain reaches the ground at once and runs off into the river [1]
- eroded soil washed into the river can block / raise the river bed [1]
- so the river overflows more often [1]

*Examiner insight:* "Trees soak up water" is too vague on its own; name uptake by roots and transpiration, or interception by the canopy.

**9. (a)** 12 ÷ 0.4 = 30 times [1].

**(b)** On the cleared hillside there are no tree roots to bind the soil [1]; there is no canopy or leaf litter to shield the ground, so rain hits the soil directly [1]; the soil is washed away by rain / blown away by wind [1].

**(c)** Any one: crops grow less well because the soil is thinner / less fertile; the farmer must add more fertiliser; lower yields [1].

**10. (a)** The grassland plants and soil are removed [1], so the organisms lose their food and shelter / the area can no longer support the grassland species [1].

**(b)** The orchid is found in only a few places, so the quarry removes a large part of its population / habitat [1]; if the remaining sites are also lost, there is nowhere else for it to grow and the species dies out [1].

**11.** Wolves were predators of the deer [1]. Without predators, the deer population increased [1]. More deer ate more young trees, so fewer trees grew along the river [1]. Songbirds lost their nesting sites / food, so the number of songbird species (biodiversity) fell [1].

*Examiner insight:* This is outcome 3 applied to a context. Each step must follow the feeding links: predator removed, prey increases, plants decrease, other species lose habitat.

**12.** Burning the trees releases carbon dioxide by combustion [1]. Fewer trees are left to remove carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis [1]. Wood and leaves that are left are broken down by decomposers, whose respiration releases carbon dioxide [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining biodiversity as the number of organisms.
- Explaining flooding and soil loss with "trees protect the ground" and no mechanism.
- Dividing by the new value when calculating a percentage decrease.
- Treating local loss of species as extinction.
- Giving only one way in which deforestation adds carbon dioxide.
