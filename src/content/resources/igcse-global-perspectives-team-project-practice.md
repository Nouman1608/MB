## Section A

**1.** State the total marks for the Team Project, and its percentage
weighting toward the qualification. **[2]**

**2.** Name the three separate pieces of evidence the Team Project
produces. **[3]**

**3.** State the word limit for the Explanation of Research and
Planning, and for the Reflective Paper. **[2]**

**4.** State how many marks the Reflective Paper carries out of the
component's 70 total. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A team submits a Reflective Paper of 1400 words, believing the
extra length demonstrates more thorough reflection.

**(a)** Explain what will actually happen to the marking of this
paper. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what the team should have done instead. **[1]**

**6.** A team submits, as its Evidence of Action, photographs of their
planning meetings and a copy of their research notes.

**(a)** Explain why this submission is unlikely to score well. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what the team should submit instead. **[2]**

**7.** Two candidates on the same team both contributed to a
successful project, but one communicated clearly, shared ideas, and
helped resolve a disagreement, while the other completed their
individual research but rarely engaged with the rest of the team.

**(a)** Explain why these two candidates might receive different
Collaboration marks despite being on the same successful team. **[2]**
**(b)** Name two behaviours the syllabus lists as evidence of good
collaboration. **[2]**

**8.** A candidate writes their Reflective Paper as a description of
what the team did, week by week, without discussing what they
personally learned or how effective the action was.

**(a)** Explain why this approach is likely to score poorly. **[2]**
**(b)** State the three things a strong Reflective Paper should
include. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why the Explanation of Research and Planning is
described as "built up in three stages" rather than written once at
the end of the project. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why understanding exactly which parts of Component 3
are marked identically for every team member, and which are marked
individually, matters for how a candidate should approach their own
contribution to the project. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 70 marks [1], worth 35% of the qualification [1].

**2.** The Explanation of Research and Planning, the Evidence of
Action, and the individual Reflective Paper [3].

**3.** The Explanation of Research and Planning is 300–400 words; the
Reflective Paper is 750–1000 words [2].

**4.** 45 marks [1].

**5. (a)** The paper will only be marked on its first 1000 words;
anything written beyond that point cannot be credited, regardless of
its quality [2].
**(b)** The team should have drafted the Reflective Paper to fit
within the 750–1000 word limit from the start, treating the limit as a
hard cap rather than a rough guide [1].

**6. (a)** This submission is unlikely to score well because the
syllabus is explicit that Evidence of Action must show what the team
actually did, not evidence of research or planning meetings [2].
**(b)** The team should submit material that shows the action itself
being carried out — for example, posters, leaflets, web pages, a video
(up to 10 minutes), presentations, or photographs of the event or
activity itself [2].

**7. (a)** Because Collaboration marks include an individual
component (5 of the 10 marks) assessed through teacher observation of
each candidate's actual behaviour during the project, so a candidate
who engaged well with the team can receive a higher individual
Collaboration mark than one who contributed research but did not
engage collaboratively, even on the same successful team [2].
**(b)** Any two of: clear communication between team members; sharing
and developing ideas; agreeing targets and deadlines; solving
problems; being flexible when required; dividing tasks and recognising
every member's contribution; asking for and giving support when
needed [2].

**8. (a)** This approach is likely to score poorly because a
Reflective Paper that only describes what happened, without
reflecting on personal learning or evaluating effectiveness, caps the
achievable mark well below the top band [2].
**(b)** A summary of the candidate's own research findings and how
they were used by the team; reflection on what the candidate learned
about the issue, teamwork and themselves; and an evaluation of how
effective the action taken and the candidate's own collaboration were
[3].

**9.** Because the document is a strategic, working record that
develops alongside the project itself — starting with topic and issue
identification, then a plan of action and roles, and finally a review
of any changes made — rather than a single retrospective summary
written after the project has already finished [2].

**10.** Because the Explanation of Research and Planning and Evidence
of Action are marked identically for every team member, while the
Reflective Paper and part of the Collaboration mark are assessed
individually, a candidate's own final grade depends heavily on their
personal reflection and their own observed collaborative behaviour,
not just on the team's shared output; understanding this should
motivate genuine individual engagement throughout the project, not
just contribution to the shared documents [3].

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Because the Reflective Paper carries nearly two-thirds of this
component's marks and is assessed individually, it deserves at least
as much dedicated attention as the shared team documents, even though
the project itself is a collaborative effort. Draft its three
required elements — research summary, personal learning, and
evaluation of effectiveness — throughout the project rather than
reconstructing them from memory in the final week, and treat every
word and time limit as a hard cap from the start of drafting, since
content beyond the limit cannot be credited no matter how strong it
is. Remember too that Collaboration is observed behaviour, not an
automatic team mark — genuine engagement with communication, problem
solving and shared decision-making throughout the project protects
marks that a strong final product alone cannot guarantee.
