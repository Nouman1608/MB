## 5.1 Units: convert fast

| Quantity | Facts to know |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1000 m |
| Area | multiply the length factor by itself: 1 m² = 10 000 cm², 1 cm² = 100 mm², 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² |
| Volume | cube the length factor: 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| Capacity | 1 cm³ = 1 ml, 1 l = 1000 cm³, 1 m³ = 1000 l |
| Mass | 1 kg = 1000 g |

Smaller unit means a bigger number. Larger unit means a smaller number.

Reminder: 0.06 m² = 0.06 × 10 000 = 600 cm². And 2.5 m³ = 2500 litres.

## Method boxes

**Perimeter and area of a sector**
1. Write the fraction θ/360 and simplify it.
2. Arc = fraction × 2πr. Area = fraction × πr².
3. Perimeter of the sector = arc + 2r (two straight radii).
4. If the question says "in terms of π", keep π in the answer.

**Surface area of a solid**
1. List every face: flat faces, curved faces, ends.
2. Decide if any face is missing (open top) or hidden (a join).
3. Find each area and add.
4. For a cone or pyramid, get l or the slant height from Pythagoras first.

**Volume of a compound solid**
1. Split into cones, cylinders, hemispheres, prisms or pyramids.
2. Work out any missing height by subtraction.
3. Add volumes. If a piece is removed, subtract.

**Shaded regions**
1. Area of the large shape minus area of the small shape.
2. Keep π in until the last line.

## Worked reminders

**Ring (annulus).** Outer radius 10 cm, inner radius 6 cm. Area = π(10² − 6²) = π × 64 = **64π cm²**.

**Quarter circle.** Radius 10 cm. Area = ¼ × π × 100 = **25π cm²**. Perimeter = 10 + 10 + ¼ × 2π × 10 = **20 + 5π cm**.

**Solid hemisphere, radius 3 cm.** Volume = ⅔ × π × 27 = **18π cm³**. Curved surface = 2π × 9 = 18π. Total surface area = 18π + π × 9 = **27π cm²** because the flat face counts when the solid is closed.

**Compound solid.** A cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 9 cm has a hemisphere of the same radius fixed on top. Volume = π × 16 × 9 + ⅔ × π × 64 = 144π + (128/3)π = **(560/3)π cm³**. The curved surface is 2π × 4 × 9 + 2π × 16 = 72π + 32π, plus the base 16π, giving **120π cm²** in total. The circle where the two parts meet is a join, so it is left out.

**Mixed units.** A tank 1.2 m long, 50 cm wide and 40 cm deep: change to one unit first. 120 × 50 × 40 = 240 000 cm³ = **240 litres**.

**Recasting.** When one solid is melted and made into another, the volume stays the same. Equate the two volumes and solve for the missing length.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Careful about |
|---|---|
| Height v slant height | Area and volume use the perpendicular height. Curved surface of a cone uses the sloping edge l. |
| Radius v diameter | Halve the diameter before using any circle formula. |
| Perimeter v area | Perimeter is a length (cm). Area is cm². Volume is cm³. |
| Curved v total surface | Total includes flat ends. Open containers leave off a lid. |
| Minor v major sector | Minor sector has angle below 180°, major above 180°. The same formulas work. |
| In terms of π v decimal | Leave π in if asked. Otherwise give 3 significant figures. |
| Capacity v volume | Capacity is measured in ml or litres, volume in cm³ or m³. |

The syllabus says to give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless a different accuracy is specified, and to avoid rounding before the final answer. For a calculator paper, use π from your calculator or 3.142.

## Parts of shapes and solids

- A half or quarter of a circle, cylinder or sphere is half or a quarter of the whole.
- A cut adds new flat faces. A half-cylinder has a flat rectangle as well as its two semicircular ends.
- A frustum is a cone with a smaller, similar cone removed from the top. Find both cones, then subtract volumes. Add the base, the top circle and the difference of the two curved surfaces.
- A prism can have any uniform cross-section, so find the cross-section area first.

## Quick self-test

Try these without notes. Questions 3 and 10 are best done without a calculator.

1. Convert 0.06 m² to cm².
2. Convert 4800 cm³ to litres.
3. A trapezium has parallel sides 9 cm and 15 cm and height 8 cm. Find its area.
4. A parallelogram has area 72 cm² and base 9 cm. Find its perpendicular height.
5. A sector has radius 8 cm and angle 135°. Find its arc length and area in terms of π.
6. A sector of radius 8 cm has area 40π cm². Find its angle.
7. A sphere has radius 6 cm. Find its volume and surface area in terms of π.
8. A cone has base radius 5 cm and sloping edge 13 cm. Find its perpendicular height, volume and curved surface area, in terms of π.
9. A cylinder with base radius 6 cm has volume 360π cm³. Find its height.
10. A square-based pyramid has base edge 6 cm and vertical height 4 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.
11. A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. Find its total surface area.
12. A sphere of radius 3 cm is melted and recast as a cone with base radius 3 cm. Find the cone's height.

### Answers

1. 0.06 × 10 000 = **600 cm²**.
2. 4800 ÷ 1000 = **4.8 litres**.
3. ½ × (9 + 15) × 8 = **96 cm²**.
4. 72 ÷ 9 = **8 cm**.
5. Fraction = 135/360 = 3/8. Arc = 3/8 × 16π = **6π cm**. Area = 3/8 × 64π = **24π cm²**.
6. 40π = (θ/360) × 64π, so θ/360 = 5/8, and θ = **225°**.
7. Volume = (4/3) × π × 216 = **288π cm³**. Surface area = 4 × π × 36 = **144π cm²**.
8. h² = 13² − 5² = 144, so h = **12 cm**. Volume = ⅓ × π × 25 × 12 = **100π cm³**. Curved surface = π × 5 × 13 = **65π cm²**.
9. π × 36 × h = 360π, so h = **10 cm**.
10. Volume = ⅓ × 36 × 4 = **48 cm³**. Slant height = √(4² + 3²) = 5 cm. Four faces = 4 × ½ × 6 × 5 = 60. Total = 36 + 60 = **96 cm²**.
11. 2 × (40 + 24 + 15) = **158 cm²**.
12. Sphere volume = (4/3) × π × 27 = 36π. Cone: ⅓ × π × 9 × h = 36π, so 3πh = 36π and h = **12 cm**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting between area units with the length factor instead of its square.
- Using the slant side of a trapezium or parallelogram as the height.
- Using the diameter as the radius, especially when a question gives the width of a circular shape.
- Forgetting the radii in the perimeter of a sector.
- Taking a major sector as the same angle as the minor one, instead of 360° minus it.
- Using the vertical height of a cone where the curved surface needs l, or the reverse.
- Including a lid on an open cup, or missing the flat face on a solid hemisphere.
- Counting a join between two pieces of a compound solid in the surface area.
- Rounding early, then giving a final answer that is not accurate to 3 significant figures.
- Writing cm² where cm³ is needed, or leaving units off.
