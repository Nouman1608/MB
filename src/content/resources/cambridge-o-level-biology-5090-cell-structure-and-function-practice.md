## Section A

**1.** Which structure is found in a bacterial cell but not in an animal cell?

A: nucleus

B: mitochondrion

C: plasmid

D: ribosome

**[1]**

**2.** Which structure is the site of protein synthesis?

A: chloroplast

B: nucleus

C: ribosome

D: sap vacuole

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** structures that can be seen on an electron micrograph of an animal cell but not on a photomicrograph of the same cell. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the function of the cell membrane and of the cellulose cell wall, and state one difference in their permeability. **[3]**

**5.** Describe the structure of a bacterial cell, limited to the structures named in the syllabus. **[5]**

## Section B

**6.** A student prepares a slide of onion epidermis.

**(a)** Name a suitable stain and state why a stain is used. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the student should add the cover slip, and explain why. **[2]**

**(c)** The student sees dark circles with thick black outlines. Identify these and state how to avoid them. **[2]**

**(d)** The student cannot find any chloroplasts. Explain why. **[2]**

**7.** Three cells are described.

Cell P: cell wall present; nucleus present; large sap vacuole; chloroplasts present.

Cell Q: no cell wall; nucleus present; many mitochondria.

Cell R: cell wall present; no nucleus; circular DNA and plasmids.

**(a)** Identify each cell as animal, plant or bacterial. **[3]**

**(b)** For cell R, give **two** features that show it is not a plant cell. **[2]**

**8.** Muscle cells contain many more mitochondria than skin cells.

**(a)** State the function of mitochondria. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest why muscle cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

**9.** A plant cell is placed in pure water.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the sap vacuole. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the cell does not burst. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why the nucleus is described as controlling the activities of the cell. **[2]**

**11.** A student draws a stained cheek cell seen under a light microscope. Give **five** rules the student should follow to produce a good biological drawing. **[5]**

**12.** Compare the structure of an animal cell with that of a plant cell from a leaf, using only the structures named in the syllabus. **[5]**

**13.** An electron micrograph of a leaf mesophyll cell shows the structures described below.

Structure J: a large, pale space filling most of the cell.

Structure K: oval bodies with stacks of internal membranes.

Structure L: very small dots scattered through the cytoplasm.

Structure M: small, sausage-shaped bodies with a folded inner membrane.

**(a)** Identify structures J, K, L and M. **[4]**

**(b)** State the function of structure K. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why structure L would not be visible on a photomicrograph of the same cell. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two of: ribosomes [1]; detail of mitochondria, such as the folded inner membrane, or mitochondria themselves [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Cell membrane controls which substances enter and leave the cell [1]. Cellulose cell wall supports the cell and stops it bursting [1]. The membrane is partially permeable but the wall is fully permeable [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A cell wall (not made of cellulose) [1]; a cell membrane inside it [1]; cytoplasm [1] containing ribosomes [1]; a single loop of circular DNA free in the cytoplasm, with small rings of DNA called plasmids, and no nucleus [1]. **[5]**

**6. (a)** Iodine solution [1]; the cell structures are colourless, so a stain gives contrast to make the nucleus visible [1].

**(b)** Lower it at an angle using a mounted needle [1], so that air is pushed out and no bubbles are trapped [1].

**(c)** Air bubbles [1]; lower the cover slip slowly at an angle [1].

**(d)** Onion epidermis cells grow underground in the bulb and receive no light [1], so they do not photosynthesise and have no chloroplasts [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) and (c), each practical step needs its reason; "to avoid bubbles" earns the second mark only when linked to the angled cover slip.

**7. (a)** P: plant [1]. Q: animal [1]. R: bacterial [1].

**(b)** It has no nucleus [1]; it has plasmids (or its DNA is circular and free in the cytoplasm) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A cell wall is present in both plant and bacterial cells, so it cannot be used to tell them apart.

**8. (a)** Site of aerobic respiration, releasing energy [1].

**(b)** Muscle contraction needs a lot of energy [1]; more mitochondria means more aerobic respiration, so more energy is released [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** It swells as water enters [1].

**(b)** The cytoplasm and membrane press against the cell wall [1], and the strong cellulose wall resists further expansion, so the cell becomes firm [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Animal cells have no wall, which is why a red blood cell in pure water can burst; mentioning the wall is the key to this answer.

**10.** The nucleus contains DNA [1], which carries the instructions that control which proteins, including enzymes, the cell makes, and so controls its activities [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Use a sharp pencil [1]; draw clear, single, unbroken lines [1]; no shading [1]; ruled label lines that touch the structure [1]; draw only what you see, at a sensible size and in proportion [1]. Also accept adding a title in place of any one point. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Drawing a textbook cell with structures you cannot actually see, such as mitochondria under a light microscope, breaks the "draw what you see" rule.

**12.** Both have a nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes [1]. Only the plant cell has a cellulose cell wall [1], chloroplasts [1] and a large sap vacuole [1]. The plant cell has a more regular shape because of its wall, whereas the animal cell's shape is irregular [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs similarities as well as differences; a list of plant-only structures alone caps the answer.

**13. (a)** J: sap vacuole [1]. K: chloroplast [1]. L: ribosomes [1]. M: mitochondria [1].

**(b)** Absorbs light (using chlorophyll) for photosynthesis [1].

**(c)** Ribosomes are too small to be seen with a light microscope [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use size and internal detail to tell organelles apart: chloroplasts are larger than mitochondria and have stacked membranes, while mitochondria have a folded inner membrane.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "energy is made" instead of "released".
- Calling the cell wall partially permeable.
- Labelling ribosomes on a light-microscope image.
- Using the cell wall alone to separate plant and bacterial cells.
- Forgetting the reason when describing a practical step.
