Condensed recall for section 4.5 (Construction) of the 4MA1 specification. It is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes. For the reasons behind each construction and full worked examples, see the [Construction study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-construction-edexcel-igcse-maths/); for drawing tasks with expected measurements, use the [Construction practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-construction-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The four statements

- **A** Measure and draw lines to the nearest millimetre.
- **B** Construct triangles and other 2D shapes with a ruler, protractor and compasses.
- **C** Solve problems using scale drawings.
- **D** With straight edge and compasses only: (i) perpendicular bisector of a line segment, (ii) bisector of an angle.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line segment | A straight line with two endpoints |
| Bisect | Cut into two equal parts |
| Perpendicular | At right angles (90°) |
| Perpendicular bisector | The line that cuts a segment in half at 90° |
| Angle bisector | The line that cuts an angle into two equal angles |
| Arc | Part of a circle drawn with compasses |
| Included angle | The angle between two given sides |
| Scale | The ratio of a length on a drawing to the real length |

## Measuring

- Nearest millimetre → one decimal place in cm (7.3 cm) or a whole number of mm (73 mm).
- Start from the 0 mark, not the end of the ruler.
- Angles to the nearest degree; read the protractor scale that starts at 0 on your arm.

## Which triangle method?

| You are given | Method |
|---|---|
| 3 sides (SSS) | Draw the longest side; arcs from each end with the other two lengths |
| 2 sides + included angle (SAS) | Draw one side, protractor at one end, measure the second side along the arm |
| 2 angles + included side (ASA) | Draw the side, protractor at both ends, extend the arms to meet |

- **Triangle test:** the two shorter sides must add to more than the longest. 5, 6, 12 is impossible; 5, 6, 10 is possible.
- **Check:** angles of your drawing should add to 180°; measure a side or angle you were not given.

## Shapes from equal arcs

- **Equilateral triangle:** equal arcs from both ends of a side → every angle 60°.
- **Rhombus:** equal arcs give equal sides; with a 60° angle, the short diagonal equals the side.
- **30°:** bisect 60°. **90°:** perpendicular bisector. **45°:** bisect 90°.

## Perpendicular bisector of AB (straight edge and compasses)

1. Compasses more than half of AB.
2. Arcs above and below from A.
3. Same setting, arcs from B.
4. Join the crossing points.

**Fact:** every point on it is equidistant from A and B. It works because the four points form a rhombus, whose diagonals bisect at right angles.

## Angle bisector of angle ABC

1. Arc from B crossing both arms at P and Q.
2. Equal arcs from P and from Q meeting inside the angle.
3. Join B to the meeting point.

**Fact:** it splits the angle exactly in half. It works because the shape formed is a kite, and the kite's diagonal from B is a line of symmetry.

## Scale drawings

```
real length    = drawing length × scale number
drawing length = real length ÷ scale number
(use the same unit for both)
```

| Conversion | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| m → cm | 100 |
| km → m | 1000 |
| km → cm | 100 000 |

- 1 : 500 means 1 cm represents 5 m. 1 : 100 000 means 1 cm represents 1 km.
- **Angles are not scaled.**
- **Bearings in a scale drawing:** draw a new north line at every turning point, parallel to the first.

Mini examples: 1 : 250, real length 12 m → 1200 ÷ 250 = 4.8 cm. 1 : 30 000, map length 7 cm → 210 000 cm = 2.1 km.

## Exam traps

- No visible arcs on a bisector → no marks for method.
- Changing the compass width between A and B.
- Opening the compasses to less than half the segment.
- Using a protractor where the question says compasses only.
- Scaling angles.
- Measuring a later bearing from the first north line.
- Forgetting to convert the measured length back to real units.

## Self-test

1. Write 6.8 cm in millimetres.
2. Can a triangle have sides 7 cm, 3 cm and 3 cm? Why?
3. Which construction method uses only a ruler and compasses?
4. Which tools may be used for a perpendicular bisector?
5. What is true of every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB?
6. How do you construct a 45° angle without a protractor?
7. A plan is 1 : 200. A real length is 15 m. Find the plan length.
8. A map is 1 : 50 000. A map length is 3.6 cm. Find the real distance in km.
9. On a 1 : 100 plan, an angle measures 72°. What is the real angle?
10. Why does an angle bisector construction work?
11. What must you draw at each turning point in a bearings scale drawing?
12. In a rhombus with side 4 cm and an angle of 60°, how long is the short diagonal?

### Answers

1. 68 mm.
2. No: 3 + 3 = 6 < 7.
3. Three sides (SSS).
4. A straight edge and compasses only.
5. It is the same distance from A as from B.
6. Construct a perpendicular (90°), then bisect it.
7. 1500 ÷ 200 = 7.5 cm.
8. 3.6 × 50 000 = 180 000 cm = 1.8 km.
9. 72°: angles are not scaled.
10. The arcs make a kite, and its diagonal from the vertex is a line of symmetry, so the two angles are equal.
11. A new north line, parallel to the first.
12. 4 cm (it splits into two equilateral triangles).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why must construction arcs be left on the drawing?**
The arcs show that you used straight edge and compasses, which statement 4.5 D requires. A line with no arcs could have been drawn with a protractor.

**How do you construct a 60° angle without a protractor?**
Draw a line, then draw arcs of the same radius from both ends so they cross. Joining the crossing point to an end makes an equilateral triangle, and each of its angles is 60°.
