Section 3.4 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification asks you to "prepare financial statements of sole traders from ledger accounts including adjustments from the application of accounting concepts", and to "prepare and comment on income statements and statements of financial position from a trial balance". The statements "could be for service businesses" or "trading businesses".

The [sole traders' financial statements and comparison guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-sole-traders-financial-statements-and-comparison-with-o/) works through inventory valued at net realisable value, doubtful debts and goods taken for own use. This guide covers the items it leaves out: returns, carriage outwards, discounts, loan interest owing and income due, how year-end ledger accounts are closed off, and how the capital account links the two statements. Figures are invented and in dollars.

Recap with the [sole trader statements revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-sole-trader-statements-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/) and practise with the [sole trader statements practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-sole-trader-statements-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/).

## Where each trial balance item goes

| Item | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Sales (revenue), less returns inwards | Top of the income statement |
| Opening inventory, purchases less returns outwards, carriage inwards, less closing inventory | Cost of sales |
| Discount received, rent received, commission received, profit on disposal | Added after gross profit |
| Carriage outwards, discount allowed, wages, rent, loan interest, depreciation and other running costs | Expenses |
| Non-current assets and their provisions | Non-current assets |
| Closing inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank, cash | Current assets |
| Trade payables, other payables, bank overdraft | Current liabilities |
| Loans repayable after more than a year | Non-current liabilities |
| Capital, profit, drawings | Capital section |

Two pairs cause most errors. **Carriage inwards** is a cost of buying goods, so it goes in cost of sales; **carriage outwards** is a cost of delivering to customers, so it is an expense. **Discount allowed** is an expense; **discount received** is income.

## Closing off ledger accounts

At the year end, each income and expense account is closed by transferring the amount for the year to the income statement. Any accrual or prepayment is carried down as a balance. Three examples from Lakeside Hardware, owned by Amara Osei, year ended 31 March 2026:

**Rent account**

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank | 9,600 | Income statement | 8,800 |
| | | Balance c/d (prepaid) | 800 |
| | 9,600 | | 9,600 |
| Balance b/d | 800 | | |

**Loan interest account** (8% on a 15,000 loan, only 600 paid)

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank | 600 | Income statement | 1,200 |
| Balance c/d (owing) | 600 | | |
| | 1,200 | | 1,200 |
| | | Balance b/d | 600 |

**Commission received account** (350 earned but not yet received)

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income statement | 2,450 | Bank | 2,100 |
| | | Balance c/d (due) | 350 |
| | 2,450 | | 2,450 |
| Balance b/d | 350 | | |

The debit balances brought down (rent prepaid, commission due) are other receivables. The credit balance (interest owing) is an other payable. The amounts transferred are what the income statement shows.

## Commenting on the statements

The specification says "prepare and comment". A good comment uses figures from the statements and says what they mean for this owner.

- Gross profit is 66,500 on net revenue of 184,000, a margin of about 36.1%. Profit for the year is about 9.0% of revenue, so expenses take most of the gross profit; wages alone are 28,500.
- Drawings of 16,500 almost equal profit of 16,650, so capital grew by only 150. Amara is taking out nearly everything the business earns, leaving little to fund growth or repay the loan.
- Current assets of 30,250 cover current liabilities of 9,300 more than three times, so short-term debts can be met. But over half of current assets is inventory, and the bank balance is only 3,400.
- The loan is not due until 2030, so it does not threaten short-term liquidity, but its 1,200 interest is a fixed cost every year.

## Service businesses

A service business, such as a tutor or a plumber, has no inventory of goods for resale, so the income statement has no cost of sales or gross profit. It starts with fees or revenue, adds other income and deducts expenses. The statement of financial position is prepared the same way. If the trial balance shows a small item such as "inventory of cleaning materials", treat it as a current asset and adjust the related expense.

## Common mistakes

- Putting carriage outwards in cost of sales, or discount allowed with income.
- Deducting returns inwards from purchases instead of from revenue.
- Taking loan interest from the trial balance without adding the amount owing.
- Forgetting income due when closing an income account.
- Showing drawings as an expense.
- Omitting the capital section, so there is no check that the statement balances.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare**: use full headings with the business name and the date or period, and label every subtotal.
- Show workings for every adjusted figure in brackets next to the item.
- **Comment**: quote figures, compare them, and link them to the owner's position.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is carriage inwards part of cost of sales?**
It is a cost of getting the goods the business buys to its premises, so it is part of the cost of those goods.

**Where does a bank loan repayable in five years appear?**
Under non-current liabilities, deducted after net current assets.
