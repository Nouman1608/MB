## What the syllabus lists

- Global distribution and climatic characteristics.
- Definitions and causes of aridity: pressure and wind systems, ocean currents, rain shadow.
- Key features: high wind energy; diurnal and seasonal variations in precipitation and temperature.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aridity | Long-term water deficit where potential evapotranspiration exceeds precipitation |
| P/PET | UNEP aridity index: precipitation ÷ potential evapotranspiration |
| Hyper-arid / arid / semi-arid | P/PET below 0.05 / 0.05-0.20 / 0.20-0.50 |
| BWh / BSh | Köppen hot desert / hot steppe (semi-arid) |
| Subsidence inversion | Warm layer aloft from sinking air that caps convection |
| Upwelling | Rise of cold deep water along a coast |
| Rain shadow | Dry lee side of mountains where descending air warms and dries |
| Continentality | Dryness and temperature extremes from distance from the sea |
| Coefficient of variation | Standard deviation ÷ mean × 100; measures rainfall variability |
| Virga | Rain that evaporates before reaching the ground |
| Harmattan | Dry, dusty north-easterly from the Sahara over West Africa, roughly November to March |
| Haboob | Dust storm driven by thunderstorm outflow |
| Shamal | North-westerly wind over Iraq and the Gulf that raises dust |

## Distribution recall

| Type | Examples | Main cause |
|---|---|---|
| Subtropical | Sahara, Arabian, Kalahari, Australian | Descending Hadley air |
| West-coast | Atacama, Namib | Cold currents and upwelling plus subtropical high |
| Rain shadow | Patagonian, Great Basin | Mountains block moist air |
| Monsoon margin | Thar | Dry edge of the monsoon |
| Semi-arid fringe | Sahel, Kalahari margins | Short ITCZ wet season |

East coasts at the same latitudes are wetter because onshore trades bring moist air from warm seas.

## Causes in one line each

- **Pressure and winds:** sinking air warms and dries, inversion caps convection; dry trades over land.
- **Cold currents:** cool lower air is stable; moisture condenses as fog not rain.
- **Rain shadow:** rain on the windward side; descending air warms and dries on the lee.
- **Continentality:** moisture lost before air reaches the interior.

## Temperature and rainfall variation

- **Diurnal range** often above 20°C in air, more at the surface: clear skies, little latent heat use by day, free longwave loss by night.
- **Seasonal range** grows with latitude and distance from the sea.
- **Rainfall:** low, variable between years (high coefficient of variation), patchy in space, concentrated in short seasons, intense; some evaporates as virga.

## High wind energy: why

- Strong heating gives steep pressure gradients and gusty convection.
- Sparse vegetation gives little friction.
- Dry, loose sediment is easily lifted.

## Mini essay plan

*Assess the relative importance of the causes of aridity in hot deserts.*

1. **Define** aridity as P/PET below 0.50 and name the syllabus causes.
2. **Pressure systems:** explain the subtropical belt (Sahara, Arabian, Kalahari).
3. **Ocean currents:** explain why the Atacama and Namib extend towards the equator and are among the driest places.
4. **Rain shadow and continentality:** explain deserts outside the subtropical belt (Patagonia, Great Basin, interior Australia).
5. **Combination:** the Atacama has all three main causes.
6. **Judgement:** pressure explains the global pattern; local factors explain the extremes and exceptions.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Aridity** is long-term; **drought** is a temporary shortfall against normal rainfall.
- **Hot desert (BWh)** has P/PET below 0.20; **semi-arid (BSh)** 0.20-0.50.

## Exam traps

- Defining deserts by rainfall alone.
- Saying descending air is cold.
- Cold currents linked to rain shadow rather than stability and fog.
- "Low rainfall" with no reference to variability or intensity.
- Diurnal range explained only by daytime heating.
- No figures quoted when describing a climate graph.

## Self-test

1. Calculate P/PET for a station with 150 mm rain and 2,000 mm PET, and classify it.
2. Why is rainfall alone a poor definition of aridity?
3. What do the letters BWh mean?
4. Why does sinking air in the subtropical high not produce rain?
5. How does a subsidence inversion stop rain?
6. How do cold currents produce fog rather than rain?
7. Explain a rain shadow in two sentences.
8. Why are east coasts at 25° often wetter than west coasts?
9. What does a high coefficient of variation of rainfall mean?
10. Give two reasons for the large diurnal temperature range.
11. Give two reasons why deserts are high wind energy environments.
12. What is the harmattan?

### Answers

1. 150 ÷ 2,000 = 0.075: arid.
2. The same rainfall is less effective where evaporation is higher, so supply must be compared with demand.
3. B = dry climate, W = desert, h = hot.
4. It warms by compression, so its relative humidity falls and clouds cannot form.
5. The warm layer aloft stops rising air from reaching heights where tall rain clouds could form.
6. They cool the lowest air, making it stable so it does not rise; moisture condenses near the surface as fog.
7. Moist air rises over mountains, cools and rains on the windward side. It descends the lee side, warming and drying.
8. Onshore trade winds bring moist air from warm seas on east coasts, while west coasts have cold currents and offshore winds.
9. Annual totals differ greatly from year to year, so the mean is unreliable.
10. Clear skies let strong solar heating reach the ground and allow longwave radiation to escape at night; little water is used for evaporation.
11. Strong heating creates steep pressure gradients; sparse vegetation gives little friction; loose dry sediment is easily lifted.
12. A dry, dusty north-easterly wind from the Sahara over West Africa, roughly November to March.
