Revision notes for **sub-theme 3.1** of Theme 3 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0): the positive features of a diverse society (ایک متنوع اور گوناگوں معاشرے کے مثبت پہلو). For the wider theme, read the [Theme 3 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-theme-3-study-guide/).

## Key ideas

- **Cultural variety**: languages, food, dress, music and festivals shared across communities.
- **Economic contribution**: skills, enterprise, labour and new markets brought by migrants.
- **Learning and understanding**: contact between communities can reduce prejudice and widen knowledge.
- **Shared citizenship**: balancing common values with respect for difference.

## Issues and perspectives

**Diversity is not only about other countries.** Pakistan is itself a diverse society. Alongside Urdu, the national language, people speak Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Saraiki and many other languages, and the country has religious minorities and regional cultures with their own dress, music and food. Urdu often works as the shared language (**رابطے کی زبان**, link language) between them. A strong answer can draw on Pakistan's own diversity as well as on Pakistani communities abroad.

**Urdu as an example of mixing.** The word **اردو** comes from a Turkic word for a camp or army, and the language grew through contact: its vocabulary draws on Persian, Arabic and Turkish as well as on the languages of the subcontinent. This makes a memorable example of how contact between peoples can create something new rather than destroy what was there before.

**Cultural enrichment.** Food, music, fashion and festivals cross community lines: a dish, a song or a celebration that began in one community becomes part of everyone's life. Think of how South Asian food and music have become part of everyday life in British cities.

**Economic contribution.** Newcomers fill gaps in the labour market, start businesses, bring skills and languages, and open trading links with their countries of origin. Be careful to describe this as a pattern and to support any figure with a named source.

**Social and educational benefits.** Children who grow up with classmates of different backgrounds learn to see a question from more than one point of view, and regular contact (**میل جول**) can reduce prejudice (**تعصب**). Shared projects such as charities, sport and local events build **بھائی چارہ** (fellowship) and **یکجہتی** (solidarity).

**The conditions for success.** The positive features do not appear automatically. Most writers agree they depend on **رواداری** (tolerance), **باہمی احترام** (mutual respect), a common language for public life, fair treatment under the law and opportunities to meet. Where communities live separate lives, the benefits are smaller. Mentioning these conditions shows the examiner that your argument is balanced, even when the task asks you to focus on the positives.

## Writing frame

Point about contribution, then its result, then a counter-view introduced with **تاہم**, then a reasoned conclusion. Use **کیونکہ** for reasons and **میرے خیال میں** for your opinion.

## Grammar to control

- Masculine plural subject, masculine plural verb: ہجرت کرنے والے لوگ ... لے جاتے ہیں.
- Masculine singular: معاشرہ ... بن جاتا ہے.
- Dative construction: انھیں ... فکر ہوتی ہے, which differs from English.

## Useful sentences with grammar notes

1. **مختلف ثقافتوں کے میل جول سے معاشرے میں رواداری بڑھتی ہے۔** (Contact between different cultures increases tolerance in society.) معاشرہ changes to the oblique معاشرے after میں; the verb agrees with رواداری (feminine), so بڑھتی.
2. **ہجرت کر کے آنے والوں نے برطانیہ کی معیشت میں اہم کردار ادا کیا ہے۔** (Those who came as migrants have played an important role in Britain's economy.) With **نے**, the verb agrees with the object کردار (masculine singular): ادا کیا ہے.
3. **ایک ہی محلے میں مختلف زبانیں بولی جاتی ہیں۔** (Different languages are spoken in a single neighbourhood.) Passive with جانا: بولی جاتی ہیں agrees with زبانیں (feminine plural).
4. **جب بچے مختلف پس منظر کے دوستوں کے ساتھ پڑھتے ہیں تو ان کے دل سے تعصب کم ہو جاتا ہے۔** (When children study with friends from different backgrounds, prejudice fades from their hearts.) The جب ... تو frame links a condition to a result; تعصب is masculine, so ہو جاتا ہے.
5. **اگرچہ تنوع کے کچھ چیلنج بھی ہیں، پھر بھی اس کے فوائد زیادہ ہیں۔** (Although diversity brings some challenges, its benefits are greater.) The concessive frame اگرچہ ... پھر بھی lets you admit a counter-point and still keep your position.

## Model paragraph

ایک متنوع معاشرہ اپنے شہریوں کو بہت کچھ سکھاتا ہے۔ جب مختلف زبانوں، مذاہب اور روایات کے لوگ ایک ساتھ رہتے ہیں تو ان کے کھانے، لباس اور تہوار پورے معاشرے کا حصہ بن جاتے ہیں۔ اس کے علاوہ نئے آنے والے اپنے ہنر اور محنت سے معیشت کو مضبوط کرتے ہیں۔ خود اردو زبان اس میل جول کی ایک اچھی مثال ہے، کیونکہ اس میں فارسی، عربی، ترکی اور مقامی زبانوں کے الفاظ شامل ہیں۔ تاہم یہ فوائد اسی وقت حاصل ہوتے ہیں جب لوگ ایک دوسرے کا احترام کریں اور مکالمے کا راستہ کھلا رکھیں۔ میرے خیال میں تنوع کمزوری نہیں بلکہ طاقت ہے۔

**Translation:** A diverse society teaches its citizens a great deal. When people of different languages, religions and traditions live together, their food, dress and festivals become part of the whole society. In addition, newcomers strengthen the economy with their skills and hard work. The Urdu language itself is a good example of this mixing, because it contains words from Persian, Arabic, Turkish and local languages. However, these benefits are only gained when people respect one another and keep the path of dialogue open. In my view, diversity is not a weakness but a strength.

**Why it works:** a general claim, two developed benefits, a concrete example linked to the language itself, a condition introduced with **تاہم**, and a short, memorable conclusion using **نہیں بلکہ** (not ... but rather).

## Discussion and essay prompts

- «تنوع کسی بھی معاشرے کی طاقت ہے۔» (Diversity is the strength of any society.) To what extent do you agree?
- «ایک کثیر الثقافتی معاشرے میں سب سے اہم چیز ایک مشترکہ زبان ہے۔» (In a multicultural society the most important thing is a shared language.) Discuss.
- How can schools help a diverse society to work well?
- Does diversity bring benefits automatically, or only under certain conditions?

## Exam traps

- Presenting only one view, or opinions with no reasons.
- Generalising about whole communities instead of using specific, accurate evidence.
- Careless gender agreement and tense in longer answers.
- Forgetting that تنوع and معاشرہ are masculine but رواداری, ثقافت and ہم آہنگی are feminine.
- Using economic figures without a source; describe the pattern instead.
- Listing foods and festivals without explaining why they matter to the argument.
