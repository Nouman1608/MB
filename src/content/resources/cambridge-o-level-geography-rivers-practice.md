## Section A

**1.** Define the terms *watershed* and *tributary*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between *infiltration* and *throughflow*. **[2]**

**3.** Name and describe **two** processes by which a river transports its
load. **[4]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which a river channel usually changes between its
source and its mouth. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** opportunities for people living on a delta. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Students measured a river at one site. The data below are
**illustrative**.

- Width: 6 m
- Depths at five equally spaced points across the channel: 0.2 m, 0.5 m,
  0.7 m, 0.6 m, 0.3 m
- Mean velocity: 0.5 m/s

**(a)** Calculate the mean depth. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the discharge. Show your working and give units. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why discharge at this site might be much higher after a
storm. **[2]**

**7. (a)** With the help of a labelled diagram, explain the formation of a
waterfall. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how potholes form in the bed of a river. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how an oxbow lake forms from a meander. **[4]**

**9.** A town is built on the flood plain of a large river. Its drainage basin
has recently been deforested, and the town has grown rapidly.

**(a)** Explain how deforestation and urban growth can increase flood risk.
**[4]**
**(b)** Compare hard engineering and soft engineering as ways of reducing
flood risk to the town. **[4]**

**10.** For a named river you have studied, describe the opportunities it
offers people and evaluate how its hazards have been managed. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Watershed: the boundary of a drainage basin, usually a ridge of high
land [1]. Tributary: a smaller river or stream that joins a larger river [1].

**2.** Infiltration is the downward movement of water from the surface into
the soil [1]; throughflow is the sideways movement of water through the soil
towards the river [1].

**3.** Any two, 1 mark for the name and 1 for the description: traction —
large boulders are rolled along the bed [2]; saltation — pebbles are bounced
along the bed [2]; suspension — fine particles such as silt and clay are
carried within the water [2]; solution — dissolved minerals are carried in
the water [2].

**4.** Any two of: becomes wider [1]; becomes deeper [1]; discharge
increases [1]; velocity usually increases [1]; gradient becomes gentler [1].

**5.** Any two of: fertile alluvial soil for farming [1]; flat land for
building [1]; water for irrigation [1]; fishing [1]; river transport and
trade, often with a port [1].

**6. (a)** (0.2 + 0.5 + 0.7 + 0.6 + 0.3) ÷ 5 = 2.3 ÷ 5 = **0.46 m** [1].
**(b)** Cross-sectional area = 6 × 0.46 = 2.76 m² [1]; discharge = 2.76 ×
0.5 = **1.38 m³/s** [1].
**(c)** Any two of: heavy rain adds a large volume of water to the basin [1];
saturated ground means less infiltration and more overland flow, which
reaches the river quickly [1]; tributaries upstream deliver extra water [1];
both depth and velocity increase, so discharge rises [1].

**7. (a)** Up to 4 marks; a clear labelled diagram can earn marks: a band of
hard, resistant rock lies over softer rock [1]; the softer rock is eroded more
quickly by hydraulic action and abrasion, undercutting the hard rock [1]; a
plunge pool is deepened at the base by the force of falling water and
swirling load [1]; the unsupported overhang collapses [1]; the process
repeats, so the waterfall retreats upstream and leaves a gorge [1].
**(b)** Pebbles are trapped in hollows or eddies in a rocky bed [1] and are
swirled round by the current, drilling the hollow deeper and wider by abrasion
[1].

**8.** Up to 4 marks: erosion is greatest on the outside of meander bends,
where velocity is highest [1]; continued erosion on the outer banks narrows
the neck of land between two bends [1]; during a flood, the river cuts
through the neck and takes the shorter, straighter course [1]; deposition
occurs at the ends of the old bend, sealing it off [1] to leave a
horseshoe-shaped oxbow lake, which may eventually dry out [1].

**9. (a)** Deforestation: fewer trees means less interception and less
transpiration [1], so more rainwater reaches the ground and runs off quickly
as overland flow, raising the river's peak [1]. Urban growth: concrete and
tarmac are impermeable, so infiltration is reduced [1], and drains carry water
rapidly into the river, shortening the time before the flood peak [1].
**(b)** Up to 4 marks for comparative points: hard engineering, such as dams,
embankments or flood relief channels, gives strong protection against large
floods, whereas soft engineering, such as afforestation or flood plain zoning,
gives less immediate protection [1]; hard engineering is much more expensive
to build and maintain, whereas soft approaches are cheaper [1]; hard
engineering can move the problem downstream or damage habitats, whereas soft
engineering works with natural processes and is more sustainable [1]; soft
approaches such as afforestation take years to become effective, whereas a
levée or channel works as soon as it is built [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking:
- **Level 3 (6–7 marks):** a named river with place-specific detail;
  opportunities clearly described; management described and evaluated with
  both advantages and disadvantages and a judgement.
- **Level 2 (3–5 marks):** opportunities and management described with some
  detail; limited evaluation.
- **Level 1 (1–2 marks):** generic points that could apply to any river.

Example of Level 3 content (River Nile, Egypt): water for irrigation in a
desert climate; fertile alluvium on the flood plain and delta; transport and
tourism. Before the Aswan High Dam, the annual flood was unreliable. The dam,
completed in 1970, created Lake Nasser, controls flooding, allows year-round
irrigation and generates hydro-electric power. However, silt is trapped behind
the dam, so farmers depend on artificial fertilisers and the delta coastline
is no longer replenished; overall, flood control and water security have
improved greatly, at an environmental cost.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

"Explain the formation of" questions are marked on a correct **sequence**:
number your stages mentally and name a process at each step. In calculation
questions, show every step, use the mean depth, and always give units. When
comparing hard and soft engineering, write sentences that mention **both**
approaches, using words such as "whereas" or "however".
