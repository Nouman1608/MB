## What the specification asks

- **Empirical formula:** the simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
- **Molecular formula:** the actual number of atoms of each element in a compound.
- The **relationship** between them.
- Calculate the empirical formula from **composition by mass** or **percentage by mass**.
- Calculate the molecular formula from the **empirical formula and Mᵣ**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule |
| Empirical formula mass | Sum of Aᵣ values in the empirical formula |
| Composition by mass | Mass of each element in a sample, in grams |
| Percentage by mass | Mass of each element per 100 g of compound |
| By difference | Finding a missing value by subtracting the others from the total |

## The relationship

```
molecular formula = n × empirical formula
n = Mᵣ ÷ empirical formula mass      (n is a whole number)
```

| Empirical formula | Compound | n | Molecular formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH₂ | Ethene | 2 | C₂H₄ |
| CH₂ | Cyclohexane | 6 | C₆H₁₂ |
| CH₂O | Ethanoic acid | 2 | C₂H₄O₂ |
| C₂H₅ | Butane | 2 | C₄H₁₀ |
| CH₄ | Methane | 1 | CH₄ |

Ionic and giant covalent substances have only an empirical formula (for example NaCl, SiO₂).

## Fractional ratios: multiply, never round

| Value after ÷ smallest | Multiply all by | Example result |
|---|---|---|
| ~1.5 or ~2.5 | 2 | 1 : 1.5 → 2 : 3 |
| ~1.33 or ~2.67 | 3 | 1 : 2.67 → 3 : 8 |
| ~1.25 or ~1.75 | 4 | 1 : 1.25 → 4 : 5 |
| Within 0.1 of a whole number | Round | 1 : 1.98 → 1 : 2 |

## Worked reminders

**Simple ratio.** 85.7% C, 14.3% H.
C 7.14, H 14.3; ÷ 7.14 → 1 : 2.00. **CH₂**.

**Ratio needing × 2.** 82.8% C, 17.2% H, Mᵣ 58.0.
C 6.90, H 17.2; ratio 1 : 2.49 → × 2 → C₂H₅ (29.0). n = 2 → **C₄H₁₀**.

**Ratio needing × 3.** 81.8% C, 18.2% H.
C 6.82, H 18.2; ratio 1 : 2.67 → × 3 → **C₃H₈**.

**Composition by mass.** 0.400 g Ca, 0.120 g C, 0.480 g O.
Ca 0.00998, C 0.0100, O 0.0300 → 1 : 1 : 3. **CaCO₃**.

**By difference.** 52.2% C, 13.0% H, rest O (34.8%).
C 4.35, H 13.0, O 2.175 → 2 : 6 : 1. **C₂H₆O**.

## Sources of Mᵣ

- Given in the question.
- Molecular ion peak in a mass spectrum (highest m/z for the molecule).
- Gas data: Mᵣ = mRT ÷ pV.

## Metal oxide practical: errors and direction

| Problem | Effect |
|---|---|
| Metal not fully oxidised | O mass too low; O ratio too small |
| Oxide smoke escapes | Final mass too low; O ratio too small |
| Some nitride forms (magnesium) | Mass gained too low |

Heat to constant mass to show the reaction is complete.

## Exam traps

- Dividing by 32 for oxygen (that is O₂); use Aᵣ = 16.0.
- Forgetting the element given by difference.
- Rounding 1.5 or 1.33 to a whole number.
- Multiplying only one value by the factor.
- Rounding moles before dividing by the smallest.
- Writing the empirical formula when the molecular formula is asked for.
- n not a whole number: recheck the empirical formula mass.

## Self-test

1. Define empirical formula.
2. What is the empirical formula of C₆H₁₂O₆?
3. A compound has empirical formula CH₂ and Mᵣ 70.0. What is its molecular formula?
4. Find the empirical formula: 75.0% C, 25.0% H.
5. Find the empirical formula: 40.0% S, 60.0% O (S = 32.1).
6. After dividing by the smallest, a ratio is 1 : 1.33. What do you do?
7. 0.540 g of aluminium combines with 0.480 g of oxygen. Find the empirical formula (Al = 27.0).
8. A compound has 92.3% C and 7.7% H. Its Mᵣ is 26.0. Find the molecular formula.
9. Why does sodium chloride have no molecular formula?
10. A ratio comes out as 1 : 1.96. What is the whole-number ratio?
11. 1.27 g of copper forms 1.59 g of an oxide. Find the empirical formula (Cu = 63.5).
12. Name two different compounds with the empirical formula CH.

### Answers

1. The simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
2. CH₂O.
3. 70.0 ÷ 14.0 = 5, so **C₅H₁₀**.
4. C 6.25, H 25.0 → 1 : 4: **CH₄**.
5. S 1.246, O 3.75 → 1 : 3.01: **SO₃**.
6. Multiply every value by 3.
7. Al 0.0200, O 0.0300 → 1 : 1.5 → **Al₂O₃**.
8. C 7.69, H 7.7 → CH (13.0); n = 2: **C₂H₂**.
9. It is a giant ionic lattice with no separate molecules, so only the ratio of ions can be given.
10. 1 : 2.
11. Cu 0.0200, O 0.32 ÷ 16.0 = 0.0200 → 1 : 1: **CuO**.
12. Ethyne (C₂H₂) and benzene (C₆H₆).
