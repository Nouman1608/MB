Condensed notes for sub-topic 4.2, Warehousing: functions, types and automation.

## Four facts to start with

1. A warehouse stores goods between production and sale.
2. Five functions: seasonal production, demand, protecting supply, price stability, logistics postponement.
3. Four types: bonded, cold storage, cash and carry, regional distribution centre.
4. Automation uses robotics, automated sorting and voice picking.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Warehouse | Building used to store goods |
| Stock (inventory) | Goods held ready for sale |
| Bonded warehouse | Customs-approved warehouse where imported goods are stored before duty is paid |
| Cold storage | Warehouse kept at low temperatures |
| Cash and carry | Warehouse where trade customers pay and take goods away |
| RDC | Regional distribution centre serving shops in a region |
| Logistics postponement | Delaying final packaging, labelling or customising until an order is known |
| Automation | Machines and computers doing tasks previously done by people |
| Voice picking | Workers follow spoken instructions through a headset |

## Functions of warehousing

| Function | One-line explanation |
|---|---|
| Seasonal production | Store harvests to sell all year |
| Demand | Hold stock ready for peak demand |
| Protecting supply | Reserve stock if supplies are delayed |
| Price stability | Store when plentiful, release when scarce |
| Logistics postponement | Delay labelling or packaging until the order arrives |

## Types of warehouse

| Type | Key point | Typical user |
|---|---|---|
| Bonded | Duty delayed until goods released | Importers of perfume, alcohol, tobacco |
| Cold storage | Refrigerated | Food, medicine, flowers |
| Cash and carry | Trade customers collect goods | Small shops, caterers |
| RDC | Serves a region | Supermarket chains |

## Automation

| Use | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Robotics | Robots move and lift goods |
| Automated sorting | Scanners and conveyors send items to the right place |
| Voice picking | Spoken instructions through a headset |

Benefits: lower labour costs; less human error; improved picking accuracy; improved inventory control; improved customer service.

Drawbacks: high cost; skilled maintenance; job losses; stops if systems fail.

## Worked examples

**Bonded duty.** 250 bottles at $50 with 20% duty: 250 × 50 = $12,500; 20% of 12,500 = $2,500 duty paid when released.

**Error saving.** 4,000 orders: errors fall from 1.5% (60) to 0.2% (8). 52 fewer errors at $18 each saves $936.

**Labour saving.** 12 pickers at $2,400 = $28,800; 5 pickers = $12,000; saving $16,800.

## Model answers to learn

**Why does a supermarket chain use an RDC?** It receives goods from many suppliers and sends them to shops in the region in one delivery, which lowers transport costs and lets shops re-order quickly.

**Why is a bonded warehouse useful to an importer?** The importer does not pay customs duty until the goods are released for sale, which helps cash flow.

## Developing a warehouse point

| Weak | Developed |
|---|---|
| "It stores goods" | "It stores the rice harvest so that it can be sold all year, linking seasonal production with steady demand" |
| "Automation is faster" | "Scanners and conveyors sort thousands of items an hour, so orders leave the warehouse sooner and customers receive them on time" |
| "Bonded is cheaper" | "Duty is paid only when goods are released for sale, so the importer's cash is not tied up" |

## Case in brief: a supermarket chain

An RDC combines goods from many suppliers; cold storage keeps fresh and frozen goods safe; automated sorting and voice picking prepare shop orders; one mixed delivery goes to each shop. The chain gains lower transport costs, fewer errors and fewer empty shelves.

## Exam traps

- **Bonded means duty-free forever.** It is delayed.
- **Cash and carry confused with a supermarket.**
- **Function not explained.**
- **Automation without a named use.**
- **Wrong total for a percentage.**
- **Only benefits** when asked for drawbacks too.

## Self-test

1. What is a warehouse for?
2. Name three functions of warehousing.
3. What is a bonded warehouse?
4. Name one product stored in cold storage.
5. Who uses cash and carry warehouses?
6. What is an RDC?
7. What is voice picking?
8. Name two benefits of warehouse automation.
9. Give one disadvantage of automation.
10. What is logistics postponement?

**Answers**

1. To store goods between production and sale.
2. Any three: link with seasonal production, link with demand, protecting supply, price stability, logistics postponement.
3. A customs-approved warehouse where imported goods are stored without paying duty until released.
4. Any one: fresh food, frozen food, medicines, flowers.
5. Trade customers such as small retailers and caterers.
6. A regional distribution centre: a large warehouse supplying shops in a region.
7. Workers get spoken instructions through a headset and confirm picks by voice.
8. Any two: lower labour costs, less human error, improved picking accuracy, improved inventory control, improved customer service.
9. Any one: high cost, skilled maintenance needed, job losses, stops if systems fail.
10. Delaying final packaging, labelling or customising until the order is known.
