## Core facts

| Fact | Value or statement |
|---|---|
| Wave type | Longitudinal |
| Produced by | A vibrating source |
| Human audible range | About 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz |
| Speed in air | About 330–350 m/s |
| Order of speed | Solids > liquids > gases (in general) |
| Ultrasound | Frequency higher than 20 kHz |
| In a vacuum | Cannot travel |

## Equations

```
wave equation        v = f λ
speed                v = distance / time
echo distance        d = (v × t) / 2      (t = time for there and back)
```

## Longitudinal waves

- Particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction of travel.
- **Compression**: particles close together, higher pressure.
- **Rarefaction**: particles spread out, lower pressure.
- Wavelength = distance between the centres of consecutive compressions
  (or consecutive rarefactions).
- Energy travels; the particles stay about fixed positions.

## Vacuum experiment (bell jar)

1. Electric bell inside a bell jar, hung on soft supports.
2. Pump the air out: the sound fades.
3. The hammer is still seen striking — light crosses a vacuum, sound
   cannot.
4. Let air back in: the sound returns.

Reason: sound needs particles to pass the vibration on.

## Loudness, pitch and timbre

| Property of the sound | Property of the wave | Oscilloscope trace |
|---|---|---|
| Loudness | Amplitude | Height of the trace |
| Pitch | Frequency | Number of waves across the screen |
| Timbre (quality) | Waveform shape | Shape of the trace, for the same note |

Louder → taller. Higher pitch → waves closer together on the screen.

## Echoes

- An echo is the **reflection** of sound waves.
- Hard, smooth surfaces reflect well; soft surfaces absorb.
- Reflection experiment: two tubes aimed at a hard board with a screen
  between them; sound is loudest when the tubes make equal angles with the
  normal.

## Speed of sound in air: methods

**Two observers.** Measured distance (hundreds of metres). Start the
stopwatch on seeing the sound being made, stop on hearing it.
v = distance ÷ time. Repeat, average, swap positions.

**Echo with rhythm clapping.** Measured distance d from a wall. Clap so each
clap coincides with the previous echo; time N intervals.
v = 2d ÷ (total time ÷ N).

Improve accuracy: long distances, many intervals timed, repeats.

## Ultrasound uses

| Use | How it works |
|---|---|
| Cleaning | Vibrations in a liquid bath shake dirt off delicate objects |
| Prenatal and medical scanning | Pulses partly reflect at tissue boundaries; echo times give depths for an image; not ionising |
| Sonar | Pulses reflect from the sea bed, fish or objects; depth = v × t ÷ 2 |

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Longitudinal wave | A wave in which the vibrations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Compression | Region of a longitudinal wave where particles are closest together |
| Rarefaction | Region where particles are furthest apart |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position |
| Pitch | How high or low a note sounds; set by frequency |
| Timbre | The quality of a sound that distinguishes sources playing the same note |
| Echo | Sound reflected from a surface |
| Ultrasound | Sound with a frequency higher than 20 kHz |

## Exam traps

- Halve the total echo time (or double the distance) — the sound goes
  there and back.
- Units: 20 000 Hz = 20 kHz. "20 000 kHz" is wrong.
- Loudness ↔ amplitude, pitch ↔ frequency. Never cross them.
- Timbre differences are about **shape**, not height or spacing.
- "Sound travels fastest in gases" is wrong; it is generally slowest in
  gases.
- In the two-observer method, timing starts when the action is **seen**,
  not heard.
- Ultrasound is still sound: longitudinal, needs a medium, cannot cross a
  vacuum.
- Converting milliseconds and microseconds: 1 ms = 10⁻³ s, 1 µs = 10⁻⁶ s.

## Self-test

1. State the approximate range of human hearing.
2. Describe the motion of air particles as a sound wave passes.
3. What is a rarefaction?
4. Why can astronauts on a spacewalk not hear each other directly?
5. A note gets louder but stays at the same pitch. What happens to the
   oscilloscope trace?
6. Two instruments play the same note equally loudly. What is different
   about their traces?
7. Sound of frequency 850 Hz travels at 340 m/s. Find its wavelength.
8. A student 99 m from a wall hears the echo of a clap 0.60 s later. Find
   the speed of sound.
9. Put steel, water and air in order of increasing speed of sound.
10. Define ultrasound.
11. A sonar echo returns after 0.50 s; the speed in sea water is
    1500 m/s. Find the depth.
12. Give one reason ultrasound, rather than X-rays, is used for prenatal
    scanning.

**Answers**

1. About 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz.
2. They vibrate backwards and forwards about fixed positions, parallel to
   the direction the wave travels.
3. A region where the particles are spread out, at lower pressure.
4. Space is a vacuum; there are no particles to carry the vibrations.
5. The trace becomes taller (larger amplitude); the number of waves across
   the screen stays the same.
6. The shape of the waveform.
7. λ = 340 ÷ 850 = **0.40 m**.
8. v = 2 × 99 ÷ 0.60 = **330 m/s**.
9. Air, water, steel.
10. Sound with a frequency higher than 20 kHz.
11. d = 1500 × 0.50 ÷ 2 = **375 m**.
12. Ultrasound is not ionising, so it does not damage the cells of the
    developing baby.
