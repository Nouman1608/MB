## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a non-financial method of motivation? Select one answer.

A: Commission

B: Promotion

C: Job enrichment

D: Fringe benefits

**[1]**

**2.** Define the term "commission". **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why motivation is important in the workplace. **[2]**

**4.** A salesperson earns a basic wage of £1,650 a month plus 2.5% commission on sales. In March she sold £36,000 of goods. Calculate her total pay for March. **[2]**

**5.** A factory has 20 workers who produce 7,200 units a week. Calculate the factory's productivity per worker. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of offering fringe benefits. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** disadvantage to a business of using job rotation. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain **one** reason why promotion can motivate employees. **[3]**

**9.** Riverside Cakes employs 10 cake decorators, each paid £420 a week. They decorated 1,500 cakes a week. The owner then gave decorators autonomy to design their own cakes for orders, and weekly output rose to 1,620 cakes.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in productivity per decorator. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the labour cost per cake after the change, to the nearest penny. **[2]**

**(c)** Outline **one** risk to Riverside Cakes of giving decorators autonomy. **[2]**

**10.** Morton Home Care finds it hard to keep care workers. Many say the job is the same every day and that they have no chance to take on responsibility.

Analyse how job enrichment could help Morton Home Care. **[6]**

**11.** Penrose Opticians has 16 staff with an average salary of £24,000. Several staff have left for rival opticians. The owner is considering two options.

Option 1: a 5% pay rise for all staff.

Option 2: fringe benefits of free eye care and private health cover, costing the business £600 per employee a year.

Justify which **one** of these options Penrose Opticians should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Hallam Energy runs a call centre where staff sell energy contracts by phone. Staff are paid a fixed hourly wage. Sales per worker are low, and customer complaints about service are rare. The manager is thinking of replacing part of the hourly wage with commission on each contract sold.

Evaluate whether Hallam Energy should introduce commission. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Promotion is listed among the financial methods in the specification because it usually brings higher pay.

**2.** A payment to an employee based on the value of sales they make [1], usually a percentage of those sales [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: attracting employees [1]; retaining employees [1]; increasing productivity. **[2]**

**4.** Commission = 0.025 × £36,000 = £900 [1]. Total pay = £1,650 + £900 = **£2,550** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 7,200 ÷ 20 [1] = **360 units per worker** [1] **[2]**

**6.** Fringe benefits such as staff discount add value to a job without raising basic pay [1]. Employees feel the business cares about them [1], so they are less likely to leave, reducing recruitment costs [1]. **[3]**

*Other creditable points:* benefits help attract applicants; some benefits, such as health cover, may reduce absence.

**7.** Employees must learn several different tasks [1], so they need extra training [1], and output may fall while they are slower at tasks that are new to them [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Promotion gives an employee a higher position with more responsibility and usually more pay [1]. The chance of promotion gives staff a goal to work towards [1], so they work harder to show they deserve it, raising productivity [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Before: 1,500 ÷ 10 = 150 cakes; after: 1,620 ÷ 10 = 162 cakes [1]. Increase = (162 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **8%** [1]

**(b)** £420 ÷ 162 [1] = **£2.59** per cake [1]

**(c)** Decorators may create designs that customers did not expect [1], so some customers may complain or refuse cakes, costing Riverside sales and reputation [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Job enrichment would give care workers more responsible tasks, such as planning a client's weekly care [1]. This answers their complaint that the job is the same every day [1], so they gain a sense of achievement and feel trusted [1]. More motivated workers are more likely to stay [1], so Morton saves on recruiting and training replacements and clients keep familiar carers [1]. However, extra responsibility needs training and workers may expect more pay, which would raise costs [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Option 1 costs 16 × (0.05 × £24,000) = £19,200 a year [1]. Option 2 costs 16 × £600 = £9,600 a year [1], half the cost of Option 1 [1]. A pay rise directly matches what rivals may be offering [1], so it may be more effective in stopping staff leaving for higher pay [1], but it is a permanent cost increase [1]. Fringe benefits linked to the business, such as free eye care, are cheap for an optician to provide and show the business values staff [1]. However, staff who left for more money may not value health cover as highly as cash [1]. Judgement: Option 2, because it costs £9,600 less and may retain staff who value security; but if exit reasons show pay is the main cause, Option 1 would be needed [1]. **[9]**

**12.** Commission links pay to contracts sold [1], so staff have a direct reason to sell more, which could raise the low sales per worker [1]. The cost to Hallam rises only when sales rise, so wage costs fall in a weak month [1]. However, staff pay becomes less certain [1], which may make some staff leave, raising recruitment costs [1]. Commission can encourage pushy selling [1], so the rare complaints may increase and harm Hallam's reputation [1]. Staff may also ignore tasks that earn no commission, such as helping existing customers [1]. Judgement: Hallam should introduce a small commission alongside most of the hourly wage, because sales are the problem and complaints are low; it should monitor complaints, since success depends on keeping service quality [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* In Evaluate, use the case evidence on both sides: low sales favour commission, while good service is something commission could put at risk.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing Promotion in Question 1: it is a financial method in the specification.
- Forgetting to add basic pay to commission in Question 4.
- Rounding the labour cost per cake before dividing.
- Explaining a method without linking it to attracting, retaining or productivity.
- In Justify, comparing costs but not effectiveness, or the reverse.
