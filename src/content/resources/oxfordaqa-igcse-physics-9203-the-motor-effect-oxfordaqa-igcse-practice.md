These questions cover section 3.6.4 The motor effect, statements a to d, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Diagrams are described in words: "into the page" and "out of the page" refer to a wire seen end-on, and field lines always run from north to south. Current means conventional current. For help, see the [motor effect study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-the-motor-effect-oxfordaqa-igcse/) and the [motor effect revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-the-motor-effect-oxfordaqa-igcse-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what each of the following represents in Fleming's left-hand rule: first finger, second finger, thumb. **[3]**

**2.** A wire carrying a current lies between the poles of a magnet, parallel to the field lines. What force acts on it?

A: a force along the wire

B: a force at 90° to the wire

C: no force

D: a force that keeps reversing

**[1]**

**3.** State three changes that would increase the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field. **[3]**

**4.** A magnetic field points from left to right across the page. A wire carries a current out of the page. State the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**

**5.** A magnetic field points up the page. A wire carries a current out of the page. State the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**

**6.** The current in question 5 is reversed and the magnet is also turned round. State the direction of the force now, and explain your answer. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A wire is held between the poles of a magnet, at 90° to the field. When a current flows, the wire is pushed downwards.

**(a)** Explain why there is a force on the wire. **[2]**

**(b)** The magnet sits on a top-pan balance. State and explain what happens to the balance reading when the current is switched on. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what happens to the size of the force if the wire is turned so that it lies parallel to the field. **[1]**

**8.** A copper rod rests across two horizontal metal rails. A magnet below the rod produces a field pointing vertically upwards through it. Seen from above, the current flows along the rod away from the observer (towards the top of the page in a plan view), and the field points out of the page.

**(a)** Use Fleming's left-hand rule to find the direction in which the rod rolls, as seen in the plan view. **[2]**

**(b)** State two ways to make the rod roll in the opposite direction. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two ways to make the rod accelerate more quickly. **[2]**

**9.** A rectangular coil is placed between a north pole on the left and a south pole on the right. The plane of the coil is parallel to the field. Current flows into the page along the left side of the coil and out of the page along the right side.

**(a)** State the direction of the force on each side. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the coil rotates, and state the direction of rotation. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why there is no force on the two short sides of the coil in this position. **[1]**

**10.** The coil in question 9 turns until its plane is perpendicular to the field.

**(a)** Explain why there is no turning effect in this position. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what must happen to the current in the coil if the coil is to keep rotating in the same direction. **[2]**

**11.** A toy motor turns too slowly. A student suggests three changes: (i) use a coil with more turns, (ii) use thicker plastic on the wire, (iii) use a stronger magnet.

**(a)** For each suggestion, state and explain whether it would make the motor turn faster. **[3]**

**(b)** Give one further change that would make the motor turn faster. **[1]**

**12.** A magnetic field points from right to left across the page.

**(a)** A wire carries current down the page. State the direction of the force on it. **[1]**

**(b)** Another wire carries current out of the page. State the direction of the force on it. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a student who used his right hand gave the opposite answers. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** First finger: magnetic field (N to S) [1]. Second finger: current (conventional) [1]. Thumb: force or motion [1]. **[3]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Use a stronger magnetic field [1]; increase the current [1]; increase the length of wire in the field [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Upwards (up the page) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** To the left [1]. **[1]**

**6.** To the left (unchanged) [1], because reversing the current reverses the force and reversing the field reverses it again, so the two changes cancel [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** The current produces a magnetic field around the wire [1], which interacts with the field of the magnet, producing a force (the motor effect) [1].

**(b)** The reading decreases [1]. The wire and the magnet exert forces on each other: the wire is pushed down, so the magnet is pushed **up** by an equal and opposite force, and presses less on the balance [1].

**(c)** The force becomes zero [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Using the left-hand rule with the field out of the page and the current up the page [1], the force is **to the right** [1].

**(b)** Reverse the current [1]; reverse the magnet (field) [1].

**(c)** Any two: increase the current; use a stronger magnet; use a rod with a greater length in the field (wider magnet) [1] [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** Left side: downwards [1]. Right side: upwards [1].

**(b)** The forces on the two sides are in opposite directions [1] and act on opposite sides of the axle, so they produce a turning effect [1]. The coil rotates anticlockwise as seen in this view [1].

**(c)** The short sides are parallel to the field lines, so they do not cut them [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** The forces on the long sides still act up and down [1], but now act along the plane of the coil through the axle, so they have no turning effect [1].

**(b)** The current must be reversed [1] every half turn, so that the forces keep turning the coil the same way [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** (i) Yes: more turns put a greater length of wire in the field, increasing the force [1]. (ii) No: the insulation does not change the current, the field or the length of wire in the field [1]. (iii) Yes: a stronger field gives a larger force on each side [1].

**(b)** Increase the current (for example, use a higher pd supply) [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Into the page [1].

**(b)** Downwards [1].

**(c)** The right hand gives the opposite direction for the same finger positions; the left-hand rule must be used for the force on a current [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the right hand, or using electron flow for the current.
- Forgetting that the force on a wire parallel to the field is zero.
- Saying that reversing both current and field reverses the force.
- Not explaining that opposite currents in the two coil sides give opposite forces.
- Forgetting the equal and opposite force on the magnet.
- Vague suggestions such as "make it more powerful".
