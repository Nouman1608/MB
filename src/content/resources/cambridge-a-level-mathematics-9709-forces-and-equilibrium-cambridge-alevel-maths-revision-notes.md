These notes condense section **4.1 Forces and equilibrium** of Mechanics (Paper 4) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. Use g = 10 m s⁻². For worked examples see the [4.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-forces-and-equilibrium-cambridge-alevel-maths/); for marked questions use the [4.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-forces-and-equilibrium-cambridge-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Core ideas

- Every body is modelled as a particle: all forces act at one point.
- A force is a vector: it has magnitude and direction, and it can be split into components.
- **Equilibrium**: resultant force zero, so the components in any two perpendicular directions each sum to zero.
- A **contact force** has a normal component R and a frictional component F; its magnitude is √(R² + F²).
- **Smooth** contact: F = 0. A model that ignores friction; it cannot explain a body staying still under a sideways push.
- **Rough** contact: F ≤ μR. **Limiting equilibrium** ("about to slip"): F = μR.
- **Newton's third law**: the force of A on B is equal and opposite to the force of B on A.

## Resolving

| Force | Along the direction | Perpendicular |
|---|---|---|
| P at angle θ to the direction | P cos θ | P sin θ |
| Weight on a plane inclined at α (along the slope) | mg sin α, down the slope | mg cos α, into the slope |
| Horizontal force H on a plane at α | H cos α along the slope | H sin α into the slope |

Resultant of forces with components X and Y: magnitude √(X² + Y²), direction tan⁻¹(Y/X) from the X direction (check the quadrant).

## Friction decision

1. Work out which way the particle would move without friction.
2. Friction acts the opposite way.
3. Find R by resolving perpendicular to the surface.
4. If the question says "limiting", "about to slip" or "on the point of moving", use F = μR.
5. Otherwise find F from equilibrium and check F ≤ μR.

To decide whether a particle on a slope with no other force stays at rest: it does if μ ≥ tan α, because then μmg cos α ≥ mg sin α.

## Range of a force on a slope

| Case | Friction acts | Equation along the plane (P up the plane) |
|---|---|---|
| About to slide down | up the plane | P + μR = mg sin α |
| About to slide up | down the plane | P = mg sin α + μR |

Equilibrium for all P between the two values. If the "slide down" value is negative, the particle stays at rest even with P = 0.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resultant | The single force with the same effect as several forces |
| Component | The part of a force in a given direction |
| Normal reaction | Contact force perpendicular to the surface |
| Friction | Contact force along the surface, opposing relative motion |
| Coefficient of friction μ | Ratio F/R at limiting friction |
| Limiting equilibrium | At rest with friction at its maximum, μR |
| Smooth | No friction acts |
| Tension | Pulling force in a string, acting along the string away from the body |

## Exam traps

- R is not always mg: a slope or an angled force changes it.
- A pull upwards at an angle reduces R; a push downwards at an angle increases it.
- F = μR only at limiting equilibrium or during sliding.
- Friction reverses between "about to slide up" and "about to slide down".
- Scale drawings get no credit: always calculate.
- A third-law pair acts on two different bodies; do not cancel them in one body's equation.
- Use g = 10 m s⁻²; using 9.8 changes every answer.

## Self-test

1. Find the components of a 12 N force at 35° to the horizontal.
2. Forces 5 N east and 12 N north act on a particle. Find the resultant.
3. A 4 kg box is pulled along rough horizontal ground by a horizontal force but stays at rest with μ = 0.6. What is the greatest force it can withstand?
4. A particle rests on a rough plane at 30° on the point of slipping. Find μ.
5. Find R for a 3 kg particle on a plane at 40°.
6. A 2 kg particle hangs from a string. Find the tension.
7. A crate of 10 kg is on the floor. State the force the crate exerts on the floor.
8. On a smooth slope at 30°, what force parallel to the slope holds a 6 kg particle at rest?
9. A force of 40 N pulls a 10 kg box at 30° above the horizontal. Find R.
10. R = 24 N and F = 7 N. Find the magnitude of the contact force.
11. μ = 0.5 and the plane is at 20°. Will a particle placed on it slip?
12. Three forces in equilibrium: 6 N east, 8 N north and P. Find P.

**Answers**

1. 12 cos 35° = **9.83 N** horizontally, 12 sin 35° = **6.88 N** vertically.
2. √(25 + 144) = **13 N**, at tan⁻¹(12/5) = **67.4°** north of east.
3. R = 40 N, so F max = 0.6 × 40 = **24 N**.
4. μ = tan 30° = **0.577**.
5. R = 30 cos 40° = **23.0 N**.
6. **20 N**.
7. **100 N downwards** (Newton's third law).
8. 60 sin 30° = **30 N** up the slope.
9. R = 100 − 40 sin 30° = **80 N**.
10. √(576 + 49) = **25 N**.
11. tan 20° = 0.364 < 0.5, so it **does not slip**.
12. P = **10 N**, opposite to the resultant of the other two: at tan⁻¹(8/6) = **53.1° south of west**.
