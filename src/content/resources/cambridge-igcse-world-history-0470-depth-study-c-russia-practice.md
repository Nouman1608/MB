These questions cover all four key questions of Depth Study C: Russia, 1905-41.

## Section A

**1.** Give **three** methods the Tsar used to control Russia before 1905. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** reforms introduced by Stolypin. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** effects of the First World War on people in Russian cities. **[3]**

**4.** Give **two** reasons why the Bolsheviks closed the Constituent Assembly in January 1918. **[2]**

**5.** State **three** features of the New Economic Policy. **[3]**

**6.** Identify **three** methods Stalin used to control the Soviet people, other than the Purges. **[3]**

**7.** Give **two** ways in which women's lives changed under Stalin. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the events of Bloody Sunday in 1905. **[4]**

**9.** Describe War Communism. **[4]**

**10.** Why did the Bolsheviks win the Civil War? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Why did Stalin launch the Purges? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** "Trotsky's own mistakes were the main reason why Stalin, and not Trotsky, emerged as Lenin's successor." How far
do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**13.** "The New Economic Policy was a success." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Okhrana secret police (1); censorship of newspapers and books (1); exile to Siberia
of opponents (1); the army and Cossacks to crush unrest (1); the Orthodox Church teaching obedience to the Tsar (1);
Russification of national minorities (1); appointed officials such as provincial governors (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: peasants were allowed to leave the commune and own their land (1); the Peasant Land Bank
lent money to buy land (1); peasants were encouraged to settle in Siberia (1); field courts tried and executed
revolutionaries (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: food shortages and long bread queues (1); fuel shortages in winter (1); rapid inflation
(1); overcrowding as workers moved to war factories (1); long working hours (1); grief over heavy casualties (1);
strikes (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Socialist Revolutionaries won more seats than the Bolsheviks (1); Lenin claimed
soviet power was more democratic than a parliament (1); the Assembly refused to accept Bolshevik decrees (1); Lenin
feared it would become a rival centre of power (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: grain requisitioning was replaced by a tax in kind (1); peasants could sell surplus
produce for profit (1); small businesses could be privately owned (1); private trade was allowed (1); the state kept
control of heavy industry, banks and foreign trade (1); a new stable currency (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: the NKVD secret police (1); the gulag labour camps (1); the cult of personality (1);
censorship (1); rewriting history and textbooks (1); socialist realism in the arts (1); control of the Communist
Party and its appointments (1); attacks on the Orthodox Church and other religions (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: many more women worked in industry, including heavy industry (1); free childcare and
education helped some women train and work (1); divorce was made harder in 1936 (1); abortion was banned in 1936 (1);
women still did most housework, so they carried a double burden (1); mothers of large families received state payments from 1936 (1).
**[2]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: it happened in St Petersburg in January 1905 (1); Father Gapon led a march of workers and
their families (1); they carried icons and pictures of the Tsar (1); they wanted to present a petition asking for
better conditions and an elected assembly (1); troops opened fire near the Winter Palace (1); many were killed and
wounded (1); the Tsar was not in the palace (1); it destroyed many people's faith in the Tsar and set off strikes
across Russia (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: it was the Bolshevik economic policy during the Civil War (1); all large factories were
nationalised (1); private trade was banned (1); grain was requisitioned from peasants by armed squads (1); food was
rationed, with more for workers and soldiers (1); strict discipline was imposed in factories (1); money lost its value
and barter grew (1); peasants grew less, contributing to famine in 1921 (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **Trotsky's leadership**, because he built a disciplined Red Army, used former Tsarist officers watched by
commissars and travelled the fronts by armoured train; **geography**, because the Reds held the central area with
Moscow, Petrograd, the railways and most industry, so they could move troops and supplies quickly; **White
weaknesses**, because the Whites were divided in aims, had leaders far apart from each other and could not
co-ordinate attacks; **War Communism and the Cheka**, because food and resources went to the army and opposition was
crushed by terror; **propaganda**, because foreign intervention let the Bolsheviks pose as defenders of Russia, and
peasants feared that a White victory would return land to the landlords. **[6]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content: **to remove rivals**, because Old Bolsheviks such as Zinoviev,
Kamenev and Bukharin could challenge his leadership, so show trials eliminated them; **fear of opposition after
Kirov**, because Kirov was popular and his murder in 1934 gave Stalin a reason to claim there was a conspiracy;
**to control the army**, because senior officers such as Tukhachevsky were potential rivals; **to blame others for
economic failure**, because "wreckers" and "saboteurs" could be blamed for the problems of the Five-Year Plans;
**Stalin's personality**, because he was deeply suspicious and wanted total loyalty. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** Trotsky missed Lenin's funeral, which Stalin used to look like Lenin's loyal follower; he was seen as
arrogant and made few allies; he was slow to use Lenin's Testament against Stalin; he had joined the Bolsheviks late
(1917), so many distrusted him; he did not build support in the party machine. **Disagree:** Stalin's position as
General Secretary let him appoint loyal officials who voted for him; the Testament, which criticised Stalin, was not
published; "socialism in one country" was more popular than "permanent revolution"; Stalin skilfully used alliances
with Zinoviev, Kamenev and later Bukharin; others feared Trotsky as a potential military dictator. **Judgement:** for
example, Trotsky's mistakes helped, but Stalin's control of the party machine was decisive, because it decided
the votes at congresses. **[10]**

**13.** Levels as for Question 12. Indicative content. **Agree:** grain production recovered and famine ended; small
businesses and trade revived, so goods returned to the shops; the peasants were calmed after the unrest of 1920-21;
it gave the Bolsheviks time to secure their rule; the currency stabilised. **Disagree:** heavy industry grew slowly;
the "scissors crisis" of 1923, when industrial prices rose far above farm prices, discouraged peasants from selling
grain; many communists saw NEPmen and richer peasants as a return to capitalism; it split the party between Left and
Right; Stalin abandoned it in 1928 in favour of planning and collectivisation. **Judgement:** for example, it
succeeded as a short-term rescue of the economy, but it could not achieve the rapid industrialisation the
communists wanted. **[10]**
