## Section A

**1.** Define the term massive open online course (MOOC). **[2]**

**2.** State **two** advantages of computer-based training. **[2]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by video-conferencing. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by autonomy in learning. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** items stored in a virtual learning environment. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A MOOC has 12,000 enrolled learners, and 7.5% of them complete the course. The yearly cost of running it is £27,000.

**(a)** Calculate the number of learners who complete the course. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost per learner who completes it, and the cost per enrolled learner. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **two** reasons why many learners do not complete a MOOC. **[4]**

**7.** A rural school pays £210 each time a specialist teacher visits, and the teacher visits 20 times in the year. The school could instead pay £900 a year for video-conferencing.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly saving from using video-conferencing. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages for the pupils of replacing the visits with video-conferencing. **[4]**

**8.** A computer-based training test has 40 questions. The pass mark is 75%. A learner answers 27 questions correctly.

**(a)** Calculate the number of correct answers needed to pass. **[1]**

**(b)** State whether the learner passes, with a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the software may help the learner to improve. **[2]**

**9.** Compare a networked course with a MOOC. **[4]**

**10.** A company with 600 staff in six countries needs them all to learn a new safety procedure. Evaluate computer-based training and video-conferencing for this task and recommend one. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss the effect of technology enhanced learning on student motivation. **[6]**

**12.** Explain the effects of technology enhanced learning on teachers. **[4]**

**13.** Assess the impact of technology enhanced learning on students' achievement. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** An online course [1] that is open to very large numbers of learners, usually free or at low cost, delivered with recorded lectures, quizzes and forums [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: learners work at their own pace [1]; material can be repeated [1]; progress is recorded automatically [1]; content is consistent [1]; no tutor is needed at the time [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Real-time audio and video communication between people in different places [1] using cameras, microphones, screens and a network connection, so they can see and hear each other and take part in a live session [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The learner controls their own learning [1]: what to study, when, where or at what speed [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: course materials; assignments and tasks; quizzes; discussion forums; learners' marks and progress [1] [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 12,000 × 0.075 [1] = 900 learners [1].

**(b)** £27,000 ÷ 900 = £30 per completing learner [1][1]; £27,000 ÷ 12,000 = £2.25 per enrolled learner [1]. (First mark for the method, second for the answer.)

**(c)** Any two reasons with a development: little personal help from a tutor, so learners who are stuck give up [1][1]; it needs self-discipline, with nobody checking progress [1][1]; learners may have enrolled out of curiosity, with no qualification to work towards [1][1]; poor internet access interrupts study [1][1]. **[9]**

**7. (a)** 210 × 20 = £4,200 [1]; 4,200 − 900 = £3,300 [1].

**(b)** Any two, each with a reason: harder for the teacher to see each pupil's work and notice who is struggling [1][1]; faults in the connection interrupt lessons [1][1]; less personal contact and practical help [1][1]; pupils may lose focus on a screen [1][1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** 0.75 × 40 = 30 [1].

**(b)** No, because 27 is fewer than 30 [1].

**(c)** The software shows which questions were wrong and which topic each belongs to [1], and directs the learner to repeat that material until the topic is understood [1]. **[4]**

**9.** A networked course is for registered members of one school, college or company, but a MOOC is open to anyone [1]. A networked course lets the teacher set, track and mark work, whereas a MOOC has very little personal contact [1]. A networked course needs its own network and servers and has set-up costs, whereas a MOOC is hosted by the provider and can cost the learner little or nothing [1]. A MOOC has many learners but low completion, whereas a networked course has a small, known group which the teacher can support [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Computer-based training: the same content for all 600 staff [1]; they can take it at a time that suits them in any country [1]; results show who has passed [1]; but there is no live help with questions [1]. Video-conferencing: staff can ask questions and discuss [1]; but with six time zones, it is hard to find a time [1], and 600 staff would need many sessions, which is costly [1]. Recommendation, for example computer-based training, with an optional live question session [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**11.** Positive: interactive content, games and videos keep students interested [1]; instant feedback shows progress and builds confidence [1]; students can choose how to learn, which motivates many [1]; access to resources about their interests [1]. Negative: distractions such as social media and games [1]; isolation, with less contact with teachers and classmates [1]; unreliable equipment or slow connections cause frustration [1]; those without devices at home may feel left behind [1]. Conclusion linking the effect to the type of student or task [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**12.** Positive: less time spent on marking when quizzes are marked automatically [1], so more time to help individual pupils [1]. Resources can be shared with other teachers [1]. Negative: teachers need time and training to learn systems and to create digital resources [1], and some worry that technology will replace them [1]. Maximum 4 marks. **[4]**

**13.** Positive: instant feedback lets students correct mistakes and repeat material [1]; teachers can see results by topic and target weak areas [1]; wider access to teachers and resources [1]. Negative: poor quality or inaccurate online resources [1]; copying instead of learning [1]; students without devices or internet fall behind [1]. Judgement, for example that achievement improves most when technology is combined with teacher support [1]. Maximum 5 marks. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a method without saying how it is delivered or who uses it.
- Calculating the cost per learner without separating the enrolled and the completing learners.
- Forgetting the mark for a reason when one is asked for with a point.
- Writing only about students in answers that also ask about teachers.
- Evaluating without recommending one option.
