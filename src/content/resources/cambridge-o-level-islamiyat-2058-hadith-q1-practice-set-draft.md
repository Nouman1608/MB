> **DRAFT for teacher review.** Original questions written for Marlbridge, not past-paper questions. Not yet reviewed by a teacher and not for publication. Hadiths are cited by number in Appendix 2 of the 2058 syllabus; read each in the syllabus translation and Arabic first.

**How to use this set.** Paper 2 Question 1 is compulsory: four Hadiths set, two answered, 8 marks. This set covers Hadiths 1, 6, 9, 12, 14 and 20.

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Hadith 1 says religion is sincerity. List three of the five parties to whom sincerity is owed. **[3]**

**2.** Hadith 6 gives three ways to respond to an evil action. State them in order. **[3]**

**3.** Hadith 9 praises one source of food above all others. What is it, and what does it teach about work? **[3]**

**4.** In Hadith 12 the Prophet (pbuh) sent two men to govern parts of Yaman. Name them and give one instruction he gave. **[3]**

**5.** Hadith 14 asks for mercy on a certain kind of man. Describe him. **[2]**

**6.** In Hadith 20, what does God not look at, and what does He look at? **[2]**

## Section B: Exam-style

**7.** Hadith 6. (a) Explain what it teaches Muslims about believing and acting. **[4]** (b) Explain how Muslims can put this into practice today. **[4]**

**8.** Hadith 14. (a) Explain what it teaches. **[4]** (b) Explain how Muslims can put this into practice today. **[4]**

**9.** Hadith 20. (a) Explain what it teaches. **[4]** (b) Explain how Muslims can put this into practice today. **[4]**

## Section C: Evaluate

**10.** "Hadith 12 is only about two governors, so it has no lesson for Muslims today." Give two reasons why this is not a full reading. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three of: Allah [1], His Book [1], His Messenger [1], the leaders of the Muslims [1], the common people [1].

**2.** With the hand [1]; if unable, with the tongue [1]; if unable, with the heart, which is the weakest of faith [1].

**3.** Food eaten from the work of one's own hand [1-2]; honest work is honoured [1].

**4.** Abu Musa [1] and Mu'adh ibn Jabal [1]; any one of: be gentle and not hard; cause rejoicing and do not alienate [1].

**5.** He is kindly when he sells [1], when he buys, and when he demands his money back [1].

**6.** He does not look at forms and possessions [1]; He looks at hearts and deeds [1].

**7.** (a) Every Muslim shares responsibility for wrong around him [1]; response depends on ability: hand, tongue, heart [1]; the heart is the weakest level, so even then a believer must reject the wrong [1]; belief must show in action where able [1]. (b) Speak up against injustice or bullying politely [up to 2]; use lawful means or report to those in authority [up to 2]; at minimum dislike the wrong in the heart [1].

**8.** (a) Kindness is expected in trade, in buying, selling and asking for repayment [1-2]; mercy is asked for the kind person [1]; faith covers business conduct [1]. (b) Fair prices and honest dealing [up to 2]; patience and kindness with debtors [up to 2].

**9.** (a) God's judgement looks at hearts and deeds [1-2]; outward appearance and wealth are not what counts [1-2]. (b) Check intention before acts of worship [up to 2]; avoid showing off or judging by appearance and wealth [up to 2].

**10.** The Hadith gives lasting guidance for anyone who leads or teaches [1]; gentleness and not alienating people applies to any position of responsibility, such as parents, teachers and managers [1]; credit one further reasoned point [1].
