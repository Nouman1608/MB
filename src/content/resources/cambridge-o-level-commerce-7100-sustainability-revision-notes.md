Condensed notes for sub-topic 6.1, Sustainability: purpose and nine actions.

## Four facts to start with

1. Sustainability protects the environment for **future generations**.
2. It applies to every link of the supply chain.
3. The syllabus lists nine actions.
4. Actions usually cost money now but can save money and improve reputation later.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Sustainability | Meeting present needs without harming future generations |
| Eco-friendly | Causing little harm to the environment |
| Landfill | Burying waste in the ground |
| Recycling | Turning waste into new material |
| Sustainable logistics | Transport and storage planned to cut fuel use and emissions |
| Water stewardship | Responsible use and protection of water |
| Resale marketplace | Platform for selling used goods |

## The nine actions

| Action | Example |
|---|---|
| Sustainable materials in production | Recycled cotton; managed-forest wood |
| Sustainable logistics | Fuller loads; electric vehicles |
| Reducing packaging | Lighter or recyclable wrappers |
| Selling eco-friendly products | Energy-saving appliances |
| Recycling and reducing waste | Sorting plastic, paper, metal |
| Reducing returns and exchanges | Accurate size guides |
| Digital resale marketplaces | Platform for used clothes |
| Free repair services | Repairing a broken zip |
| Water stewardship | Recycling factory water |

## Purpose

To make commercial processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations. Reasons: protect resources, reduce pollution, meet customer expectations, follow laws, cut long-term costs, improve reputation.

## Costs and benefits

| Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|
| Less waste and pollution | Higher cost of materials or equipment |
| Lower running costs in time | Staff training and planning |
| Better reputation | Customers may not pay a higher price |
| Customer loyalty | Possible conflicts with convenience |

## Worked examples

**Packaging.** 12 g to 9 g per pack × 500,000 packs = 1,500,000 g = 1.5 tonnes saved.

**Recycling rate.** 28 ÷ 40 × 100 = 70%.

**Returns.** 10,000 orders: 8% (800) down to 5% (500) = 300 fewer returns × $9 = $2,700.

**Logistics.** 20 loads × 80 litres = 1,600; 14 loads × 80 = 1,120; saving 480 litres (30%).

**Water.** 2,000 litres cut to 1,400 litres is a 30% reduction.

## Model answers to learn

**Why reduce packaging?** Less packaging means less waste and lower material and transport costs. The packaging must still protect the product.

**Why offer free repairs?** Repairing extends the product's life, so fewer new goods are produced and less waste is created. It also builds customer loyalty.

## Which link in the chain?

| Action | Link |
|---|---|
| Sustainable materials | Production |
| Water stewardship | Production and sourcing |
| Reducing packaging | Packaging |
| Sustainable logistics | Transport and storage |
| Eco-friendly products | Selling |
| Reducing returns | Selling and delivery |
| Resale marketplace, free repair | After sale |
| Recycling and reducing waste | Production, packaging and after sale |

## Developing a sustainability point

| Weak | Developed |
|---|---|
| "It saves the planet" | "Using less plastic means less waste goes to landfill, so the environment is protected for future generations" |
| "Recycling is good" | "Recycling offcuts means the factory buys less new material, which cuts costs and saves resources" |
| "Free repair is nice" | "Repairing products extends their life, so fewer new products are made and customers stay loyal" |

## Case in brief

A trainer firm uses recycled plastic soles, treats its dye water, replaces plastic wrap with paper, ships by sea in full containers, runs a resale site and repairs worn soles. Costs rise at first; savings and reputation follow.

## Exam traps

- **Vague answers** such as "be green".
- **No environmental effect** stated.
- **Costs ignored.**
- **Ethics confused** with sustainability.
- **Percentage on the wrong base.**
- **Only one link** in the chain considered.

## Self-test

1. Define sustainability.
2. State the purpose of sustainability in the supply chain.
3. Name three actions to improve sustainability.
4. What is water stewardship?
5. How does a digital resale marketplace help?
6. Why might a business offer free repairs?
7. Give one cost of using sustainable materials.
8. Calculate the recycling rate: 18 tonnes recycled out of 30 tonnes of waste.
9. Give one way to reduce returns.
10. Give one benefit of reducing packaging.

**Answers**

1. Meeting present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
2. To make commercial processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations.
3. Any three of the nine actions.
4. Using water carefully and responsibly in production and sourcing, and protecting water supplies.
5. Used goods stay in use longer, so fewer new goods are made.
6. It makes products last longer, reduces waste and builds loyalty.
7. Any one: materials may cost more; may be hard to find.
8. 18 ÷ 30 × 100 = 60%.
9. Any one: accurate sizing guides, clear descriptions or photos, quality checks, good packaging.
10. Any one: less waste, lower material cost, lower transport cost.

## Command words to expect

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Identify, State, Give | A short answer with no explanation |
| Define | A precise meaning |
| Describe | The main points or features |
| Explain | A reason, linked to the situation, with a development |
| Analyse | Break the issue into parts and show how they connect |
| Assess, Consider | A reasoned judgement about importance or effect |
| Discuss | Both sides of an issue, in a structured way |
| Justify | A case backed with evidence |
| Evaluate | A judgement of value, ending with a conclusion |
