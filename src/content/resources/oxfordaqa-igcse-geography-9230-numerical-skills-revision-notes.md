These notes cover section 3.3.1.3, Numerical skills, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Geographical skills can be tested in all three written exams. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [numerical skills study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-numerical-skills/). For the wider skills picture, see the [geographical and fieldwork skills revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-geographical-and-fieldwork-skills-revision-notes/).

## Specification checklist

- **Number, area and scales;** relationships between units.
- **Design fieldwork data collection sheets;** accuracy, sample size, procedures, control groups, reliability.
- **Proportion and ratio; magnitude and frequency.**
- **Draw informed conclusions** from numerical data.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scale | Relationship between map distance and real distance |
| Ratio | Comparison of two quantities (3:1) |
| Proportion | Part compared with the whole |
| Magnitude | Size, often compared in powers of ten |
| Frequency | How often something occurs |
| Accuracy | Closeness to the true value |
| Precision | How finely something is measured |
| Reliability | Repeating gives similar results |
| Sample size | Number of items measured |
| Control | A variable kept constant |

## Units

| Quantity | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 km = 1 000 m; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 cm = 10 mm |
| Area | 1 km² = 100 ha; 1 ha = 10 000 m² |
| Time | 1 hour = 3 600 s |
| Discharge | m³ per second (cumecs) |

Convert to **one unit** before calculating. Write units on every answer.

## Map scale

| Scale | 1 cm on map is | 1 km on ground is |
|---|---|---|
| 1:25 000 | 250 m | 4 cm |
| 1:50 000 | 500 m | 2 cm |

- Map to ground: **multiply**. 6.4 cm at 1:25 000 is 1 600 m.
- Ground to map: **divide**. 2.4 km at 1:50 000 is 4.8 cm.
- Curved lines: lay string along the line, then measure the string. 38 cm at 1:50 000 is 19 km.
- Area on 1:50 000: each grid square is 1 km². Count whole squares and add half for part-squares: 5 whole + 6 part = 8 km².
- Rectangle area = length × width; population density = population ÷ area.

## Ratio and proportion

- **Ratio:** divide both numbers by the smallest to simplify. 2.4 million to 0.8 million is 3:1.
- **Proportion:** part ÷ whole. 2.4 ÷ 3.2 = 0.75 = 75%.
- **Sharing in a ratio:** add the parts, divide, multiply. 50 shared in 6:3:1 gives 30, 15, 5.
- Keep the **order** of the ratio the same as in the question.

## Magnitude and frequency

- Earthquake scale: each whole number is about 10 times the ground motion and about 32 times the energy. Two units are 100 times the motion and about 1 000 times the energy.
- **Frequency table or tally:** records how many in each category.
- **Rate:** count per base (per 1 000 people) so that places can be compared fairly.

## Data collection sheet checklist

- Title, date, time, weather, site.
- One row per site or observation.
- Columns with **units**.
- Space for **repeat readings** and a **mean**.
- Space for notes on anomalies.

## Sampling

| Method | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Random | Equal chance for each item (random numbers) | 30 pebbles from a random number table |
| Systematic | Every nth item | 1 200 households, sample 40: every 30th |
| Stratified | Proportional to sub-group size | Zones of 600, 300, 100 homes, sample 50: 30, 15, 5 |

- Larger samples are more representative but take more time.
- Stratified: (group size ÷ total) × sample size.

## Reliability and accuracy

- **Reliable:** repeat readings, calculate means, use more sites, check anomalies, same method every time.
- **Accurate:** calibrated equipment, careful reading, correct technique.
- **Precise:** appropriate level of detail (nearest cm or mm).
- Reliable does not mean accurate: a faulty instrument can give the same wrong answer each time.
- **Control:** keep conditions the same (time of day, depth, equipment).

## River calculations

- Velocity = distance ÷ mean time. 10 m in 15 s is 0.67 m/s.
- Cross-sectional area = width × mean depth. 4.0 m × 0.5 m = 2.0 m².
- Discharge = area × velocity. 2.0 × 0.67 = 1.3 m³/s.

## Drawing conclusions

1. State the pattern with **figures**.
2. Link to the **hypothesis** (supports, partly supports, does not support).
3. **Explain** using geographical ideas.
4. Give **limitations** (sample size, anomalies, one day).
5. Use cautious wording ("suggests", not "proves").

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** A student surveys 40 households chosen from a list of 1 200. Describe how a systematic sample could be chosen and give **one** disadvantage. [3 marks]

**Model answer:** "Divide 1 200 by 40 to get 30, then choose every 30th household from the list, starting from a random point between 1 and 30. A disadvantage is that if the list has a pattern, for example every 30th house is a corner plot, the sample could be biased and may not represent all the households."

How it scores: one mark for calculating the interval, one for the method of choosing, and one for a valid disadvantage.

## Quick checks for sense

- A stream velocity of 25 m/s is too fast: check units.
- 38 cm on a 1:50 000 map is 19 km, not 190 km.
- A stratified sample must add up to the planned total.
- Percentages of a whole must add to 100.
- Always show working: you can earn marks for method even if the answer is wrong.

## Formula card

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Real distance | Map distance × scale distance |
| Density | Population ÷ area |
| Velocity | Distance ÷ time |
| Cross-sectional area | Width × mean depth |
| Discharge | Area × velocity |
| Systematic interval | Population size ÷ sample size |
| Stratified sample | (Group size ÷ total) × sample size |

## Exam traps

- Check the **units** match before calculating.
- Do not use the map scale in the wrong direction.
- Use whole squares plus half of the part-squares for area.
- Give ratios in the order stated.
- Stratified samples must be in proportion to group size.
- Do not call data reliable just because the sample is large; check the method.
- Conclusions need figures and a link to the hypothesis.

## Self-test

1. How many metres are in 2.5 km?
2. At 1:50 000, how many kilometres is 6 cm on the map?
3. Convert 4 km² to hectares.
4. Simplify the ratio 600:150.
5. A systematic sample of 25 is needed from 500 homes. What is the interval?
6. What is the difference between accuracy and reliability?
7. How can a student make river velocity data more reliable?
8. A magnitude 6 earthquake compared with a magnitude 4: how many times greater is the ground motion?
9. Calculate the velocity if a float travels 10 m in 20 s.
10. Why should a conclusion mention limitations?

**Answers**

1. 2 500 m.
2. 6 × 0.5 = 3 km.
3. 400 ha.
4. 4:1.
5. 500 ÷ 25 = every 20th home.
6. Accuracy is closeness to the true value; reliability means repeating gives similar results.
7. Take repeat timings and use the mean; measure at several sites; use the same method and equipment; check anomalies.
8. 100 times (10 × 10).
9. 10 ÷ 20 = 0.5 m/s.
10. Because small samples, anomalies and one-day data limit how far the results can be trusted.
