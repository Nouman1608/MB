This guide covers **Topic 1 Prose and Drama**, assessed in Paper 1 of
OxfordAQA International GCSE English Literature (9275), which overlaps
with AQA's UK GCSE English Literature (8702). Two routes exist through
the specification: Route A (Paper 1 plus Paper 2a, with an unseen-prose
section) and Route B (Paper 1 plus Paper 2b plus non-exam assessment).

## Where this fits in 9275

Prose and Drama establishes close-reading and analytical skills
applied to whole studied texts -- a novel and a play, drawn from the
specification's prescribed text list -- that carry forward into Topic
2 (Poetry and Unseen Texts), where the same skills are applied to
shorter and unseen material.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE ENGLISH LITERATURE (9275) — TOPIC 1
PROSE AND DRAMA**

- Prose Fiction — close study of a set prose text, including its
themes, characters and the writer's methods
- Drama — close study of a set play text, including its themes,
characters and dramatic techniques

## How to approach it

Both sub-topics reward being able to write with precise textual
reference rather than general plot summary -- practise selecting and
embedding short, specific quotations that support a clear analytical
point about theme, character or technique. Because the specification
draws on a prescribed text list, know the exact edition and text your
course is following, since alternative editions can differ in
pagination and sometimes in content. Drama benefits from attention to
how meaning is created in performance -- stage directions, dialogue
and dramatic structure -- not just the written text treated as prose,
since examiners look for awareness of the text as something written to
be staged. Building a short bank of well-chosen, memorised quotations
for both the prose text and the play -- covering major characters and
themes -- makes it far easier to write with precise textual evidence
under timed exam conditions, rather than relying on paraphrase.

## Assessment at a glance

Paper 1 (Prose and Drama) is a 1 hour 30 minute closed-book exam worth
60 marks -- 40% of the International GCSE -- and is common to both
Route A and Route B. Section A (Prose Fiction, 30 marks) and Section B
(Drama, 30 marks) each require one question on the candidate's chosen
set text, with a choice between an essay-style question and a
passage-based question in both sections.

OxfordAQA has published a revised 9275 specification for first
teaching from September 2026, refreshing the set-text and anthology
lists -- affecting cohorts starting from September 2026, with first
exams in May/June 2028 -- but the Paper 1 structure described above is
unchanged, and the current (Version 4.2) specification remains in
force through the final May/June 2027 series.
