## What you must be able to do

- Explain that the characteristics of Ti to Cu arise from an **incomplete d sub-level** in atoms or ions.
- Name the **four characteristic properties**.
- Define **ligand, complex and co-ordination number**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| d-block element | Highest-energy electron is in a d sub-level (Sc to Zn in period 4) |
| Transition metal | Element (Ti to Cu) with an incomplete d sub-level in its atoms or ions |
| Ligand | Molecule or ion that donates a pair of electrons to form a co-ordinate bond with a metal |
| Co-ordinate bond | Covalent bond where both electrons come from one atom |
| Complex | Central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands |
| Co-ordination number | Number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion |

## Electron configurations

| Atom | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Ti | [Ar] 3d² 4s² |
| V | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² |
| Cr | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ |
| Mn | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² |
| Fe | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² |
| Co | [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² |
| Ni | [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² |
| Cu | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ |
| Zn | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² |

Ions: remove **4s electrons first**.

| Ion | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Fe²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁶ |
| Fe³⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁵ |
| Cu⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ |
| Cu²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁹ |
| Mn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁵ |
| Zn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ |
| Sc³⁺ | [Ar] |

## Which d-block elements are transition metals?

| Element | Reason |
|---|---|
| Sc | Sc³⁺ has no d electrons, so **not** a transition metal |
| Ti to Cu | Form ions with incomplete d sub-level (Cu through Cu²⁺), so **are** transition metals |
| Zn | Zn²⁺ has a full 3d¹⁰, so **not** a transition metal |

## Four characteristic properties

| Property | Example |
|---|---|
| Complex formation | [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ |
| Formation of coloured ions | Cu²⁺(aq) blue; Fe³⁺(aq) yellow-brown |
| Variable oxidation state | Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺; V in +2 to +5 |
| Catalytic activity | Fe in the Haber process; V₂O₅ in the Contact process |

Why variable oxidation state: the 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so different numbers of electrons can be lost.

## Ligands and complexes

- Ligands have at least one **lone pair**.
- Examples: H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, OH⁻, CN⁻.
- The ligand is the electron-pair **donor**; the metal ion is the **acceptor**.
- Co-ordination number counts **bonds**, not ligands.

| Complex | Co-ordination number | Metal oxidation state |
|---|---|---|
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | 6 | +2 |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | 4 | +2 |
| [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ | 6 | +3 |
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | 2 | +1 |
| [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ | 6 | +3 |

Oxidation state = overall charge minus the sum of ligand charges.

## Worked examples

**Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺.** Iron is [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s². Remove 4s² to give Fe²⁺, [Ar] 3d⁶. Remove one more electron from 3d to give Fe³⁺, [Ar] 3d⁵.

**Is it a transition metal?** Write the configuration of each common ion. If at least one has a d sub-level that is neither empty nor full, the element is a transition metal. Copper: Cu⁺ is 3d¹⁰ but Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, so yes. Zinc: only Zn²⁺, 3d¹⁰, so no.

**Oxidation state from a complex.** [CuCl₄]²⁻: four chloride ligands contribute –4, so the metal must be +2 to give –2 overall.

## Common ions and colours in water

| Ion | Colour of aqueous ion |
|---|---|
| Cu²⁺ | Blue |
| Fe²⁺ | Pale green |
| Fe³⁺ | Yellow-brown |
| Mn²⁺ | Very pale pink |
| Cr³⁺ | Green or violet, depending on the ligands |
| Zn²⁺ | Colourless |

These show why the definition matters: Zn²⁺ is colourless because its d sub-level is full.

## How a ligand bonds

1. The ligand has a lone pair on a donor atom (O in water, N in ammonia, Cl in chloride).
2. The metal ion has empty orbitals that can accept the pair.
3. A co-ordinate bond forms. Both electrons in the bond came from the ligand.
4. The number of such bonds is the co-ordination number.

## Transition metal or main-group metal?

| Feature | Transition metal (for example Fe) | Main-group metal (for example Ca) |
|---|---|---|
| d sub-level | Incomplete in atoms or ions | Not involved |
| Oxidation states | Several (Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺) | One (Ca²⁺) |
| Ions in solution | Usually coloured | Colourless |
| Complexes | Many | Few |
| Catalysis | Common | Rare |

## Exam traps

- Calling Sc and Zn transition metals.
- Defining a transition metal as "any d-block element".
- Removing 3d before 4s in ions.
- Copper written as 3d⁹ 4s².
- Ligand described as "accepting" electrons.
- Co-ordination number given as the number of ligands in every case.
- Ignoring ligand charges in oxidation-state questions.
- "All transition metal compounds are coloured."

## Self-test

1. Define a transition metal.
2. Why is zinc not a transition metal?
3. Why is scandium not a transition metal?
4. Write the configuration of Fe³⁺.
5. Write the configuration of a chromium atom.
6. Name the four characteristic properties.
7. Define a ligand.
8. What is the co-ordination number of [CuCl₄]²⁻?
9. What is the oxidation state of cobalt in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺?
10. Why can iron have two common oxidation states?
11. What type of bond forms between a ligand and a metal ion?
12. Which feature must a ligand have?

### Answers

1. An element with an incomplete d sub-level in its atoms or ions (Ti to Cu).
2. Zn²⁺ has a full d sub-level.
3. Sc³⁺ has no d electrons.
4. [Ar] 3d⁵.
5. [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.
6. Complex formation, coloured ions, variable oxidation state, catalytic activity.
7. A molecule or ion that donates a pair of electrons to form a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal.
8. 4.
9. +3.
10. The 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so different numbers can be lost.
11. A co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond.
12. At least one lone pair of electrons.
