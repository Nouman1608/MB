## Section A

**1.** Define *مطلع* and *مقطع*. **[2]**

**2.** In the illustrative lines, identify the *ردیف* and the *قافیہ*, and explain why the couplet
could open a ghazal. **[3]**

**3.** Explain one effect of repeating *کی طرح جلتے ہیں* at the end of both lines. **[2]**

**4.** State the usual stanza pattern of a *مسدس*, and one way a poet can use the final couplet of
each stanza. **[2]**

**5.** For the question *"مسدسِ حالی کے منتخب بندوں میں حالی نے اپنی قوم کی حالت کو کن طریقوں سے
مؤثر انداز میں پیش کیا ہے؟"*, identify the text, the focus and the method word. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** *مطلع*: the opening couplet of a ghazal, in which both lines rhyme and end with the refrain [1].
*مقطع*: the closing couplet, which usually contains the poet's pen name (*تخلص*) [1].

**2.** *ردیف*: *کی طرح جلتے ہیں* [1]. *قافیہ*: *چراغوں* / *خوابوں* [1]. It could open a ghazal because
both lines share the rhyme and refrain, which is the mark of a *مطلع* [1].

**3.** Any one effect, explained [2]: the repetition binds lamps and dreams together, so the speaker's
"burning" becomes both a quiet glow and a slow destruction; or it gives the sense of something that
happens every evening, an unending routine of pain.

**4.** Six lines: usually four lines on one rhyme, then a couplet on a new rhyme [1]. The final
couplet can sum up, sharpen or turn the idea of the stanza, often delivering its judgement or warning
[1].

**5.** Text: the selected stanzas of *Musadas Hali* [1]. Focus: the condition of his community
(*قوم کی حالت*) [1]. Method word: *کن طریقوں سے* / *مؤثر انداز میں*, so language and technique must
be discussed [1].

**6.** Any four, one mark each [4]:
- It shows only "a basic understanding of surface meaning" (love, sadness) with no deeper
  implications.
- There is no reference to the text: no quotation or close detail, so not "careful and relevant
  reference".
- Nothing is said about how the poet achieves effects: no image, word choice or form.
- "Very good and I liked it" is an opinion without reasons, not an informed personal response.
- It does not engage with the focus of the question, only the general subject.

**7.** Model paragraph [5]:

> ان مصرعوں میں بولنے والا خود کو ایک اجنبی شہر میں تنہا محسوس کرتا ہے۔ "ترے شہر" کہہ کر وہ ظاہر کرتا
> ہے کہ یہ جگہ اس کی نہیں، کسی اور کی ہے۔ شام کا ذکر تنہائی کے اس لمحے کی طرف اشارہ ہے جب دن کی
> مصروفیت ختم ہو جاتی ہے اور دل خالی رہ جاتا ہے۔ "چراغوں کی طرح جلنا" روشنی دینا بھی ہے اور خود کو
> گھلانا بھی، جبکہ "خوابوں کی طرح جلنا" بتاتا ہے کہ اس کی امیدیں بھی راکھ ہو رہی ہیں۔ ردیف کی تکرار اس
> جلنے کو ایک روزانہ عذاب بنا دیتی ہے۔

Marking: addresses the focus throughout [1]; short integrated quotations [1]; literal and deeper
meaning [1]; comments on image and its double sense [1]; comments on form (refrain) with its effect
[1].

**8.** Four points [4]:
- Choose the ghazal you know best in which a single strong feeling runs through several couplets [1].
- Select three or four couplets that show that feeling, ideally ones that show it in different ways
  or develop it [1].
- Introduction naming the feeling and the methods; one paragraph per couplet, using
  point–quotation–meaning–method–link [1].
- Conclusion with a personal judgement on which couplet makes the feeling most vivid, and why [1].

**9.** Three points [3]: a nazm develops one subject, so the essay can follow its movement from opening
to close rather than selecting independent couplets [1]; in a musaddas, the closing couplet of each
stanza can be examined for how it turns or sharpens the idea [1]; paragraphs can track stages of the
argument across the selected stanzas, linking each back to the focus [1].

**10.** For example [2]: *حالی نے ان بندوں میں اپنی قوم کی پستی اور غفلت کا ایسا نقشہ کھینچا ہے جو قاری
کو جھنجھوڑ دیتا ہے۔ وہ ماضی اور حال کے تضاد، دردمند لہجے اور ہر بند کے آخری شعر میں سمٹے ہوئے نتیجے
کے ذریعے یہ تصویر مؤثر بناتے ہیں۔* One mark for a direct answer to the focus, one for naming
methods to be explored.

**11.** Marked on the five-level scheme, best fit [15].

- **Level 5 (13–15)**: clear understanding of the poem and its deeper implications; a developed
  exploration of how the poet achieves effects (imagery, contrast, tone, form); careful, relevant,
  memorised reference; every paragraph addresses the focus.
- **Level 4 (10–12)**: understanding with some deeper implications; explores the language; some
  thoroughness of evidence; addresses the focus.
- **Level 3 (7–9)**: some understanding and some reference to language, but parts drift into
  summary; partly addresses the focus.

For (a), strong answers select stanzas that show the community's condition and explore how Hali makes
it powerful: for example, contrasts between past and present, the tone of grief or warning, and the
use of each stanza's closing couplet; quotations must be from the prescribed stanzas. For (b), strong
answers name the feelings precisely, choose couplets that show them, analyse imagery and word choice,
and give a reasoned personal view as the question asks (*آپ کی کیا رائے ہے؟*). An answer on a
ghazal that is not prescribed cannot be credited for knowledge of the text.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: giving the whole refrain as the rhyme. The *قافیہ* is the rhyming word before the
  refrain.
- **Question 7**: writing about the lines in general instead of the focus given.
- **Question 11**: summarising the poem from beginning to end, and quoting inaccurately from memory.
