This guide follows sections 10.01 and 10.02 of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026). It covers units and measurement (10.01a to 10.01c) and perimeter calculations (10.02a to 10.02c). Everything here is examined on both tiers: the specification lists no Higher tier only content in these sections. For quick recall use the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-units-compound-measures-maps-bearings-perimeter-revision-notes/), then test yourself with the [practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-units-compound-measures-maps-bearings-perimeter-practice/). The [OCR GCSE Mathematics hub](/boards/ocr/gcse/mathematics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/gcse/mathematics/) show where this unit sits. ## 10.01a Units of measurement

You need the metric links between units, plus the time and money facts the specification expects you to know.

| Quantity | Facts to know |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm |
| Area | 1 m² = 10 000 cm², 1 cm² = 100 mm² |
| Volume | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³, 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| Capacity | 1 litre = 1000 ml = 1000 cm³, 1 m³ = 1000 litres |
| Mass | 1 tonne = 1000 kg, 1 kg = 1000 g |
| Time | 1 min = 60 s, 1 h = 60 min, 1 day = 24 h, 1 year = 365 days |

**Why area and volume factors are squared and cubed.** A square 1 m by 1 m is also 100 cm by 100 cm. Its area is 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm², not 100 cm². Volume works the same way with three lengths.

**Worked example 1.** Convert 3.7 m² to cm².

```
1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm²
3.7 × 10 000 = 37 000 cm²
```

**Worked example 2.** Give 0.35 m³ in cm³ and in litres: 0.35 × 1 000 000 = 350 000 cm³, and 0.35 × 1000 = 350 litres.

**Time.** Hours and minutes are not decimals. 3 h 18 min is 3 + 18/60 = 3.3 hours, and 2.35 h is 2 h 21 min (0.35 × 60 = 21). For the 24-hour clock add 12 to afternoon times, so 6.40 pm is 18:40. From 22:50 to 03:15 is 1 h 10 min to midnight plus 3 h 15 min, which is 4 h 25 min.

**Money.** Multiply or divide by the exchange rate. If £1 = €1.17, then £85 is 85 × 1.17 = €99.45, and €234 is 234 ÷ 1.17 = £200.

**Algebraic contexts.** The same conversions work when a length is a letter. Keep the unit with the letter.

**Worked example 3.** A ribbon is x metres long. It is cut into pieces of 20 cm. How many pieces?

```
x metres = 100x cm
Number of pieces = 100x ÷ 20 = 5x
```

## 10.01b Compound units

| Quantity | Relationship | Units used |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | distance divided by time | km/h or m/s |
| Density | mass divided by volume | g/cm³ or kg/m³ |
| Pressure | force divided by area | N/m² or N/cm² |
| Rate of pay | pay divided by hours worked | £ per hour |
| Unit price | cost divided by quantity | pence per gram |

Rearrange each one as needed: distance = speed × time, time = distance ÷ speed, mass = density × volume, force = pressure × area.

**Worked example 4 (speed).** A cyclist rides 15 km in 40 minutes. Find the speed in km/h.

```
40 min = 40/60 h = 2/3 h
Speed = 15 ÷ (2/3) = 22.5 km/h
```

**Converting a compound unit.** Convert the top and the bottom separately.

**Worked example 5.** Change 54 km/h into m/s.

```
54 km = 54 000 m and 1 h = 3600 s
54 000 ÷ 3600 = 15 m/s
```

Short cut: km/h to m/s divides by 3.6.

**Pay and unit pricing.** At £9.60 per hour, 7 h 30 min earns 9.60 × 7.5 = £72.00. For best buys, find the cost of the same amount for each. A 750 g pack costs £2.10 and a 1.2 kg pack costs £3.24. That is 210 ÷ 750 = 0.28 p per gram and 324 ÷ 1200 = 0.27 p per gram, so the larger pack is the better buy.

**Worked example 6 (density).** A block has mass 2.16 kg and volume 800 cm³. Find its density in g/cm³ and in kg/m³.

```
2.16 kg = 2160 g
Density = 2160 ÷ 800 = 2.7 g/cm³
1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³, so density = 2700 kg/m³
```

**Worked example 7 (pressure).** A force of 840 N acts on an area of 0.35 m². Find the pressure in N/m², then in N/cm².

```
Pressure = 840 ÷ 0.35 = 2400 N/m²
1 m² = 10 000 cm², so 2400 N/m² = 2400 ÷ 10 000 = 0.24 N/cm²
```

**Worked example 8 (algebraic contexts).** A car travels d km in t minutes. Write its speed in km/h and in m/s.

```
km/h: distance d km, time t/60 h, so speed = d ÷ (t/60) = 60d/t
m/s: distance 1000d m, time 60t s, so speed = 1000d/(60t) = 50d/(3t)
```

## 10.01c Maps, bearings and scale drawings

With a scale of 1 : 20 000, each 1 cm measured on the map stands for 20 000 cm on the ground, which is 200 m.

**Worked example 9.** On a 1 : 20 000 map, a footpath measures 7.3 cm. A cycle route is 2.8 km long in real life. Find the real length of the footpath and the map length of the cycle route.

```
7.3 × 20 000 = 146 000 cm = 1460 m = 1.46 km
2.8 km = 280 000 cm, and 280 000 ÷ 20 000 = 14 cm
```

**Bearings.** A bearing says which way to go from one point to another. Measure the turn clockwise from the north line, and always give three figures, such as 065° or 308°. Put your pencil on the place you travel from, draw a north line there, then measure clockwise to the line you want.

The bearing of A from B starts at B. To get a back bearing, add 180° if the bearing is below 180°, otherwise subtract 180°. If B is on a bearing of 072° from A, then A is on a bearing of 252° from B, because north lines are parallel and the angles between them add to 180°.

**Worked example 10 (scale drawing).** A boat sails 12 km on a bearing of 040°, then 9 km on a bearing of 130°. Find how far it is from the start and the bearing of the start from the boat.

```
Scale 1 cm : 3 km, so the legs are 4 cm and 3 cm.
Between bearings 040° and 130° the turn is 90°, so the legs meet at a right angle.
Distance = √(12² + 9²) = √225 = 15 km (5 cm on the drawing)
Angle at the start between the first leg and the line home = tan⁻¹(9/12) = 36.9°
Bearing of the boat from the start = 040° + 36.9° = 076.9°
Bearing of the start from the boat = 076.9° + 180° = 256.9°, so about 257°
```

On a scale drawing, measure the final line and the angle with a sharp pencil, ruler and protractor, and mark each north line.

## 10.02a Perimeter of rectilinear shapes

Perimeter is the distance round the edge. A rectilinear shape has only straight sides at right angles. Find any missing lengths, then add every side.

**Worked example 11.** A 14 cm by 9 cm rectangle has a 5 cm by 4 cm notch cut from a corner. Find the perimeter left.

The notch is at the top right corner. It shortens the top edge by 5 cm and the right edge by 4 cm. It also adds a step of 5 cm across and 4 cm down. The lost length and the added length match, so the outline is as long as the outer rectangle.

```
Perimeter = 2 × (14 + 9) = 46 cm
```

This shortcut works for any notch cut from a corner. It does not work for a notch cut from the middle of an edge, where you must add the two extra sides.

## 10.02b Circumference and arc length

For a circle with radius r and diameter d:

```
circumference C = πd = 2πr
```

**Worked example 12.** Find the circumference of a circle with diameter 14 cm, as a multiple of π and as a decimal to 3 significant figures.

```
C = π × 14 = 14π = 43.98... = 44.0 cm (3 s.f.)
```

**Arc length.** A sector is a slice of a circle. Its arc is the curved edge. The arc is a fraction of the whole circumference, and the fraction is the angle over 360°.

```
arc length = (angle/360) × 2πr
```

**Worked example 13.** Find the arc length of a sector of radius 9 cm and angle 80°.

```
Arc = 80/360 × 2 × π × 9 = 2/9 × 18π = 4π cm
4π = 12.57 cm (to 2 decimal places)
```

## 10.02c Perimeter of composite shapes

Split the outline into straight pieces and arcs. Count only the outside edge.

**Worked example 14 (running track).** A track has two straight sides of 60 m and two semicircular ends. Each end has diameter 30 m.

```
Two straights: 2 × 60 = 120 m
Two semicircles make one circle: π × 30 = 94.25 m
Perimeter = 120 + 94.25 = 214.2 m (1 d.p.)
```

**Worked example 15 (window).** A window is a rectangle 1.2 m wide and 1.5 m high with a semicircle on the top edge.

```
Diameter of semicircle = 1.2 m. The top of the rectangle is inside the shape, so leave it out.
Perimeter = 1.2 (bottom) + 2 × 1.5 (sides) + ½ × π × 1.2 (arc)
          = 4.2 + 1.885... = 6.08 m (3 s.f.)
```

**Worked example 16 (sector).** Find the perimeter of a sector of radius 7 cm and angle 130°.

```
Arc = 130/360 × 2 × π × 7 = 15.88 cm
Perimeter = arc + two radii = 15.88 + 14 = 29.9 cm (3 s.f.)
```

**Worked example 17 (quarter circle removed).** A rectangle is 10 cm by 8 cm. A quarter circle of radius 4 cm is removed from one corner.

```
Remaining straight edges: 10 + 8 + (10 − 4) + (8 − 4) = 28 cm
Arc = ¼ × 2π × 4 = 2π cm
Perimeter = 28 + 2π = 34.3 cm (3 s.f.)
```

## Common errors

- Using 100 for area conversion between m² and cm², instead of 10 000.
- Treating 3 h 18 min as 3.18 hours.
- Multiplying when you should divide by an exchange rate, or the reverse.
- Giving a bearing with fewer than three figures, such as 65° instead of 065°.
- Using the diameter in 2πr, or the radius in πd.
- Giving only the arc as the perimeter of a sector, or counting the inside diameter of a semicircle.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-step answer, or leaving out units.
