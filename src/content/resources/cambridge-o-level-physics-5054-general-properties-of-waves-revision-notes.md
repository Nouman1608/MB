## Core ideas

- Waves transfer **energy without transferring matter**.
- Wave motion is shown by ropes, springs (slinky) and water waves in a ripple tank.

## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Frequency:** the number of wavelengths that pass a point per unit time (Hz).
- **Wavelength:** the distance between two consecutive, identical points, such as two consecutive
  crests (m).
- **Amplitude:** the maximum distance from the mean position (m).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wavefront** | Line joining points in step, such as along one crest |
| **Crest / trough** | Highest / lowest point of a transverse wave |
| **Wave speed** | Distance a wavefront travels per unit time |
| **Transverse wave** | Vibration at right angles to the direction of energy transfer |
| **Longitudinal wave** | Vibration parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| **Reflection** | Wave bounces off a surface |
| **Refraction** | Change of direction caused by a change of speed |
| **Diffraction** | Spreading of a wave through a gap or round an edge |

## Equation

```
v = f λ       f = v / λ       λ = v / f
```

Units: m/s, Hz, m. Convert cm → m (÷ 100) and kHz → Hz (× 1000).

**Quick examples**
- f = 50 Hz, λ = 0.040 m → v = **2.0 m/s**.
- v = 340 m/s, f = 1.7 kHz → λ = 340 ÷ 1700 = **0.20 m**.
- v = 0.30 m/s, λ = 2.0 cm → f = 0.30 ÷ 0.020 = **15 Hz**.

## Reading diagrams

- Count **wavelengths**, not crests: first to fifth crest = 4λ.
- Amplitude is **half** the crest-to-trough height.
- Frequency = waves passing ÷ time.

## Transverse versus longitudinal

| Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic radiation | Sound |
| Water surface waves | Seismic P-waves (primary) |
| Seismic S-waves (secondary) | |

## What changes?

| | Speed | Wavelength | Frequency | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reflection | Same | Same | Same | Changes (i = r) |
| Refraction | Changes | Changes | **Same** | Changes at an angle |
| Diffraction | Same | Same | Same | Spreads out |

- **Deep → shallow:** slower, shorter wavelength, same frequency, bends towards the normal.
- **Gap ≈ wavelength:** most spreading. **Gap ≫ wavelength:** little spreading.
- **Edge:** longer wavelength bends more into the region behind the barrier.

## Ripple tank checklist

- Vibrating bar → straight waves; dipper → circular waves.
- Lamp above, screen below: crests appear as bright lines.
- Reflection: straight barrier at an angle.
- Refraction: glass plate makes a shallow region.
- Diffraction by a gap: two barriers; narrow the gap to increase spreading.
- Diffraction by an edge: one barrier.

## Source and medium

- The **source** fixes the frequency.
- The **medium** (or depth) fixes the speed.
- Wavelength follows from λ = v ÷ f.
- Doubling the frequency in the same medium halves the wavelength.

## Worked calculations in exam layout

**From counts.** 30 waves pass a point in 12 s; six consecutive crests span 0.90 m.

```
f = 30 / 12 = 2.5 Hz
λ = 0.90 / 5 = 0.18 m        (six crests = five wavelengths)
v = f λ = 2.5 × 0.18 = 0.45 m/s
```

**Seismic S-wave (illustrative values).** v = 4.0 km/s, f = 0.50 Hz.

```
λ = v / f = 4000 / 0.50 = 8000 m
```

## Writing a full "describe" answer

*Describe how a ripple tank shows refraction.* Place a glass plate on the floor of the tank to
make a shallow region. Send straight waves from the vibrating bar towards it, with the edge of the
plate at an angle to the wavefronts. Over the plate the wavefronts are closer together, showing a
shorter wavelength and lower speed, and they change direction at the boundary.

## Exam traps

- Amplitude is not crest-to-trough.
- Frequency does not change in refraction.
- Diffraction does not change wavelength or speed.
- Sound is longitudinal; electromagnetic waves are transverse.
- Units: convert before using v = fλ.
- "Waves carry water along" is wrong: only energy moves.

## Linking to other subtopics

- **3.2 Light:** refraction of light is explained by the same change of speed.
- **3.3:** all electromagnetic waves are transverse and travel at the same speed in a vacuum.
- **3.4 Sound:** sound is longitudinal; echoes are reflection of sound.

## Self-test

1. State what is transferred by a wave and what is not.
2. Define frequency.
3. A wave diagram shows 4 complete waves across 2.0 m. Find the wavelength.
4. A transverse wave is 8.0 cm from crest to trough. State its amplitude.
5. Find the speed of a wave with f = 25 Hz and λ = 0.60 m.
6. A sound of 850 Hz travels at 340 m/s. Find its wavelength.
7. Give two examples of transverse waves from the syllabus.
8. Name the longitudinal seismic wave.
9. What happens to wavelength and frequency when water waves enter shallower water?
10. When is diffraction through a gap greatest?
11. How is a shallow region made in a ripple tank?
12. Which waves bend more round the end of a barrier: long or short wavelength?

### Answers

1. Energy is transferred; matter is not.
2. The number of wavelengths that pass a point per unit time.
3. 2.0 ÷ 4 = **0.50 m**.
4. **4.0 cm**.
5. 25 × 0.60 = **15 m/s**.
6. 340 ÷ 850 = **0.40 m**.
7. Any two: electromagnetic radiation, water surface waves, seismic S-waves.
8. The P-wave (primary).
9. The wavelength decreases; the frequency stays the same.
10. When the gap width is similar to the wavelength.
11. By placing a glass plate on the floor of the tank.
12. Long wavelength.
