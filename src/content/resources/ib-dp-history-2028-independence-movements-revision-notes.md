**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. Questions on this study require you to refer to **one country**. Build a full evidence set for one (for example the United States, Haiti or Venezuela) and keep short comparisons from the others. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-movements/) for detail and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-independence-movements-practice/) to test yourself.

## Key terms

- **Criollo**: American-born person of Spanish descent.
- **Peninsular**: Spanish-born official or settler.
- **Junta**: local governing council, as set up in Spanish America in 1808 to 1810.
- **Caudillo**: regional or national strongman leader.
- **Bourbon reforms**: 18th-century Spanish changes to raise revenue and tighten control.
- **Monroe Doctrine (1823)**: US warning against European intervention in the Americas.
- **Gran Colombia**: state formed 1819 and dissolved in 1830 (Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Panama).
- **Articles of Confederation**: first US framework (1781), replaced by the Constitution (1787).

## Causes of independence movements

| Factor | United States | Spanish America | Haiti |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colonial power's actions | Taxes after 1763; Coercive Acts | Bourbon reforms; crisis of 1808 | French control; racial divisions |
| Economic | Trade limits, debt | Trade monopoly, taxation | Plantation wealth, slavery |
| Social / religious | Local assemblies; Protestant networks | Criollo resentment; clergy split | Planters, free people of colour, enslaved people |
| Ideas | Locke, Paine | Enlightenment, US and French models | Rights of man, abolition |

## Leaders

- Intellectual: Jefferson, Paine, Bolívar (*Jamaica Letter*, 1815).
- Political: Continental Congress; Venezuelan congress (1811); Dessalines (1804).
- Military: Washington, Toussaint Louverture, Bolívar, Sucre, San Martín.
- Mobilising support: Bolívar and freedom for enslaved soldiers; Toussaint and former slaves; Hidalgo's call to rural poor in Mexico.

## Why the wars were won

- Strategy: avoid destruction (Washington), combine plains cavalry with an Andean crossing (Bolívar), use disease and guerrilla war (Haiti).
- Foreign help: France for the US; British volunteers and loans for Latin America; none for Haiti beyond arms trade.
- Weak opponents: Britain's long supply lines, Spain's Napoleonic and liberal crises, French losses to yellow fever.

## New states

- Constitutions: US 1787, Gran Colombia, Mexico 1824, Brazilian monarchy.
- Borders: colonial lines were unclear; disputes followed.
- Identity: symbols and histories; strong regional loyalties in Latin America.
- Role of leaders: Washington stepped down; Bolívar's strong executive; Dessalines took the imperial title.

## Challenges after independence

| Challenge | Example |
|---|---|
| Economic | War debt; Haitian indemnity (1825); loans and defaults in Latin America |
| External threats | US and Britain (1812); Mexico and Texas (1836) |
| Leadership disputes | Breakup of Gran Colombia; caudillos; party conflict in the US |
| Marginalised groups | Slavery continued in many states; Indigenous land loss; Indian Removal Act (1830) |

## US relations with Latin America

- Early support: recognition of new states in 1822.
- Monroe Doctrine: protection, or beginning of US dominance? The British navy gave it force.
- Cooperation: Bolívar's Congress of Panama, with weak results.
- Trade: growing, but Britain stayed the largest partner.

## Debates

- Ideas versus interests.
- Social revolution (Haiti) versus elite transfer of power.
- Leaders versus conditions.
- Significance of the Monroe Doctrine.

## Essay skeleton

1. Name your country and state your line.
2. Weigh two or three causes or factors and rank them.
3. Bring in a perspective (for example Bailyn on ideas, James on Haiti, Lynch on Spanish America).
4. Use one comparison with another country to test your claim.
5. Conclude with a clear judgement.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was foreign support the main reason for the success of one independence movement?"

1. Choose the country (for example the USA) and state a line: foreign help was crucial at key moments but not the main cause.
2. Foreign support: the French alliance (1778), loans, the fleet at Yorktown.
3. Other reasons: Washington's strategy and survival, local militias, Saratoga, British overstretch and the distance from Britain.
4. Compare: Bolívar's British volunteers and the Andes campaign; Haiti, which had little foreign help.
5. Perspective: historians who stress ideas and leadership against those who stress international circumstances.
6. Judgement: foreign help turned a stalemate into victory, but without American resistance it would have had nothing to build on.

## Common mistakes

- Narrating battles.
- Treating all Latin American states as the same.
- Naming several countries and giving thin evidence on each.
- Forgetting marginalised groups.

## Self-test

1. Why did the Seven Years' War help to cause the American Revolution?
2. What happened in Spain in 1808 and why did it matter?
3. Name two leaders who mobilised popular support and how.
4. Why did Gran Colombia break up?
5. What did the Monroe Doctrine state?
6. Give one economic burden on Haiti after independence.

**Answers:** 1. Britain's war debts led to new taxes and tighter control that colonists resisted. 2. Napoleon forced the king to abdicate, creating a crisis of authority that led to juntas. 3. For example, Toussaint (appeals to the formerly enslaved) and Bolívar (offers of freedom for enslaved soldiers; war decrees). 4. Regional rivalries, leadership disputes and the size of the state. 5. That European powers should not colonise or interfere in the independent Americas. 6. The indemnity demanded by France in 1825.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need Brazil?**
Not necessarily. It is a useful contrast because independence came through the ruling dynasty.

**How much detail on battles?**
Only what supports an argument about why a side won.
