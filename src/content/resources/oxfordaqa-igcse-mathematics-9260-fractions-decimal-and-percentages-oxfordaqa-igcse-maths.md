## 1. Three ways of writing one number

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are three names for the same value. Learn the most common equivalents so that you can move between them without working.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |
| 1/20 | 0.05 | 5% |
| 1/3 | 0.333... | 33.3...% |
| 2/3 | 0.666... | 66.6...% |

### Converting without a calculator

If the denominator is a factor of 10, 100 or 1,000, make an equivalent fraction with that denominator.

```
7/25  = 28/100   = 0.28  = 28%
3/40  = 75/1000  = 0.075 = 7.5%
11/20 = 55/100   = 0.55  = 55%
```

Otherwise divide the numerator by the denominator with short division.

Percentages that are not whole numbers convert the same way: 0.4% = 0.4/100 = 4/1000 = 1/250, and 12.5% = 0.125 = 1/8.

## 2. Comparing and ordering

To order a mixture of forms, convert everything to decimals, written to the same number of places.

**Worked example 1.** Write in order of size, smallest first: 0.63, 5/8, 62.6%, 7/11.

```
0.63    = 0.6300
5/8     = 0.6250
62.6%   = 0.6260
7/11    = 0.6363...
Order:  5/8, 62.6%, 0.63, 7/11
```

Write the final answer in the original forms, not as decimals. A common slip is to compare 0.63 with 0.626 by counting digits: 0.626 has more digits but is smaller.

## 3. Recurring decimals (Extension only)

### Fraction to recurring decimal

Divide. When a remainder repeats, the digits repeat.

```
4/11 = 0.363636...    (written with dots over the 3 and the 6)
7/12 = 0.58333...     (dot over the 3 only)
```

A fraction in its simplest form gives a recurring decimal when its denominator has a prime factor other than 2 or 5: 11 is prime, and 12 = 2 × 2 × 3.

### Recurring decimal to fraction

Multiply by a power of 10 so that the recurring parts line up, then subtract.

**Worked example 2.** Write 0.454545... as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
x      = 0.454545...
100x   = 45.454545...
99x    = 45
x      = 45/99 = 5/11
```

Two digits repeat, so multiply by 100.

**Worked example 3.** Show that 0.1666... = 1/6.

```
x      = 0.1666...
10x    = 1.666...
100x   = 16.666...
100x - 10x = 15
90x    = 15
x      = 15/90 = 1/6
```

Here only the 6 repeats, so use two multiples (10x and 100x) whose recurring tails are identical. In a "show that" question the answer is given, so every line of working must be visible.

## 4. Fractions, decimals and percentages as operators (N13)

An operator acts on a quantity: "of" means multiply. Choose the form that suits the calculation.

- With a calculator, decimals are quickest: 35% of 640 = 0.35 × 640 = 224.
- Without a calculator, fractions are often quickest: 12.5% of 640 = 1/8 of 640 = 80.

Operators can be applied one after another. Multiply them together to get a **single multiplier**.

**Worked example 4.** A water tank holds 1,200 litres. On Monday 35% of the water is used. On Tuesday 3/5 of what is left is used. What fraction of the original water remains, and how many litres is that?

```
After Monday:   1 - 0.35 = 0.65 of the water remains
After Tuesday:  1 - 3/5  = 2/5  of that remains
Single multiplier: 0.65 × 2/5 = 0.26 = 26/100 = 13/50
Water left:     0.26 × 1,200 = 312 litres
```

The "of what is left" wording means the second operator acts on 0.65 of the tank, not on the full tank. Adding 35% and 60% to get 95% used is the classic error.

**Worked example 5.** Find 40% of 15% of 2,000.

0.4 × 0.15 × 2,000 = 0.06 × 2,000 = 120. Note that 40% of 15% is 6%, not 55% or 25%.

## 5. One quantity as a fraction or percentage of another (N14)

Write the quantity you are describing as the numerator and the quantity after "of" as the denominator. Put both in the same units first.

**Worked example 6.** A recipe uses 450 g of flour and 75 g of sugar.

```
Sugar as a fraction of flour:   75/450 = 1/6
Flour as a percentage of sugar: 450/75 × 100 = 600%
```

Both answers are correct. The second is greater than 100% because the flour is the larger quantity. The specification explicitly includes fractions greater than 1 and percentages greater than 100.

**Worked example 7.** In a school, 3/8 of the students walk to school, 0.3 take the bus and the rest cycle. 52 students cycle. How many students are in the school, and what percentage cycle?

```
Walk + bus:  3/8 + 0.3 = 0.375 + 0.3 = 0.675
Cycle:       1 - 0.675 = 0.325 = 32.5%
Total:       52 ÷ 0.325 = 160 students
Check:       3/8 of 160 = 60, 0.3 of 160 = 48, 60 + 48 + 52 = 160
```

Converting everything to decimals first made the addition easy. The final step divides by the operator, because 52 is 0.325 of an unknown total.

## 6. Where this leads: percentage change (N15)

Percentage change is N14 applied to a change: express the change as a percentage of the original value.

percentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100

For example, a town's population rises from 24,000 to 30,000. The change is 6,000, and 6,000 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = 25%. Equivalently, the new population is 30,000 ÷ 24,000 = 125% of the old one. Multipliers, interest and reverse percentages (Extension only) are in the dedicated percentage change guide linked above.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing decimals by the number of digits instead of by place value.
- Treating "of what is left" as "of the whole".
- Adding percentages that act one after another, such as 40% then 15%, instead of multiplying.
- Using the wrong denominator in N14: the quantity after "of" goes underneath.
- Leaving a fraction unsimplified when the question says "in its simplest form".
- Multiplying by 10 in a recurring decimal when two digits repeat; you need 100.
- Mixing units, such as grams with kilograms, before writing a fraction.

## Exam technique

- **Work out** or **calculate**: show the conversion or operator you used, not just the answer.
- **Show that**: the answer is given, so the working is the whole mark. Write every line, including the subtraction step for recurring decimals.
- **Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form**: divide numerator and denominator by their highest common factor; a decimal answer will not match the requirement.
- **Write in order**: give the original numbers, not your converted decimals.
- On a non-calculator paper, look for friendly equivalents (12.5% = 1/8, 0.2 = 1/5) before doing long multiplication.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which fractions give terminating decimals?**
A fraction in its simplest form gives a terminating decimal when the only prime factors of its denominator are 2 and 5. Any other prime factor, such as 3 or 7, gives a recurring decimal.

**Is a percentage over 100 possible?**
Yes. When one quantity is larger than the one it is compared with, the percentage is over 100. The specification includes percentages greater than 100 and fractions greater than 1.
