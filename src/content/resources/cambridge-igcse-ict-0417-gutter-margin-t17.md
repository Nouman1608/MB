The last "know and understand" statement in Topic 17 Document production of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 is **gutter margin**: "the purpose of setting gutter margins". It is a small statement, but it combines a reason (why the extra space is needed), a decision (which edge it goes on) and a calculation (how much room is left for the text). This guide covers all three in more depth than the [Document production study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-document-production-study-guide/).

## What a gutter margin is

A **gutter margin** (often just "gutter") is extra blank space added to the margin on the edge of the page that will be **bound**. It is added **on top of** the normal margin on that edge. If a document has a left margin of 2 cm and a gutter of 1 cm, the text on a single-sided page starts 3 cm from the left edge of the paper.

The word *gutter* is also used in desktop publishing for the gap between columns. In the 0417 syllabus, the gutter margin means the binding space; the gap between columns is called the spacing between columns (see the [organise page layout study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-organise-page-layout-t17/)).

## The purpose of a gutter margin

A gutter margin is set so that **text is not lost or hidden by the binding**. Several things happen at the bound edge of a document:

- **The binding itself uses some of the paper.** Staples, stitching, glue, a comb or a spiral wire all sit a short distance in from the edge.
- **Holes may be punched** for a ring binder, removing paper near the edge.
- **Bound pages do not open flat.** Near the spine the page curves away from the reader, so text close to the inside edge is hard to read or disappears into the fold.

The extra space moves the text away from the binding so that it all stays visible. It also keeps the document looking **balanced** once bound: without a gutter, the visible inside margin looks much narrower than the outside margin, because part of it is taken up by the binding.

Setting the gutter in the page setup, rather than simply making one margin bigger, has a further purpose: the normal margins can stay at the values in the house style, while the gutter adds just the binding allowance on the correct edge.

## Which edge? Gutter position

### Single-sided documents

Printed on one side only, every page is bound on the same edge, normally the **left**. The gutter is added to the left margin of every page.

Some documents are bound along the **top** edge, such as a desk calendar, a flip-over notepad or a report clipped at the top. Word processors offer a **gutter position** setting of *left* or *top*; for these documents the gutter goes at the top.

### Double-sided documents and mirror margins

When a document is printed on both sides and bound like a book, the binding edge is on the **left of right-hand pages** (odd-numbered) and on the **right of left-hand pages** (even-numbered). A gutter fixed on the left would be on the wrong side for half the pages.

**Mirror margins** solve this. The left and right margins become **inside** and **outside** margins, and the gutter is added to the **inside** margin of every page, whichever side that is. In many word processors, choosing mirror margins places the gutter on the inside automatically.

| Document | Printing | Bound edge | Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report in a ring binder | Single-sided | Left | Gutter on the left |
| Stapled booklet | Double-sided | Inside (alternates) | Mirror margins with a gutter |
| Desk calendar | Single-sided | Top | Gutter position top |
| Letter sent loose | Single-sided | None | No gutter needed |

## Calculating the text area

With a gutter on the left or inside:

> text width = page width - left (or inside) margin - right (or outside) margin - gutter

**Example 1.** A4 portrait (21.0 cm wide), left and right margins 2.5 cm, gutter 1.5 cm on the left.
Text width = 21.0 - 2.5 - 2.5 - 1.5 = **14.5 cm**. The text starts 2.5 + 1.5 = **4.0 cm** from the left edge and ends 2.5 cm from the right edge.

**Example 2.** The same settings with mirror margins (inside 2.5 cm, outside 2.5 cm, gutter 1.5 cm). On every page the text starts 4.0 cm from the **spine** and stops 2.5 cm from the **outer** edge. On a right-hand page that is 4.0 cm from the left edge; on a left-hand page it is 4.0 cm from the right edge. The text width is still 14.5 cm on every page.

**Example 3.** A desk calendar on A4 landscape (21.0 cm high), top and bottom margins 2 cm, gutter 2 cm at the top.
Text height = 21.0 - 2 - 2 - 2 = **15.0 cm**, starting 4.0 cm below the top edge.

A gutter reduces the space available for text. Text that fitted on a page before the gutter was set may reflow onto the next page, so check the pagination after setting it.

## Worked example: choosing settings for three documents

*Scenario (invented):* a youth club produces three documents.

1. **A 30-page handbook, printed on both sides and stapled down the spine.** Use mirror margins with a gutter, so the binding allowance is always on the inside edge. A plain left gutter would be wrong on every even-numbered page, where the binding is on the right.
2. **A single-sided risk assessment, hole-punched for a ring binder.** Add a gutter on the left so the punched holes do not go through any text.
3. **A single-sided wall planner bound at the top with a wire.** Set the gutter position to top, so the wire does not cover the first row of the planner.

In each case the reasoning has the same three steps: how is it bound, which edge does that affect, and therefore where does the gutter go?

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain the purpose of setting a gutter margin for a printed booklet. [2]*

> A gutter margin adds extra space to the inside edge of each page, where the booklet is bound, **(what it is, applied to the booklet)** so that no text is hidden by the staples or lost in the fold and the booklet can be read easily. **(purpose)**

*Why it scores:* it says both what the gutter does and why, and refers to the booklet. Writing only "for binding" may not earn both marks.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the gutter is the space between columns.
- Saying a gutter "replaces" the margin; it is added to it.
- Setting a left gutter on a double-sided document, so even pages have the extra space on the outside edge.
- Forgetting to subtract the gutter when calculating the text width.
- Adding a gutter to a document that will not be bound, wasting space for no purpose.

## Exam technique

On the theory paper, *state* or *describe* the purpose in terms of binding and visible text, and *explain* by saying what would happen without it (text hidden in the binding or by punched holes). In the practical paper, set the exact gutter value and position given, and use the page setup dialogue to check that mirror margins are on when the task says the document is double-sided. If a calculation is asked for, write out every subtraction.

## Check your understanding

1. What is the purpose of a gutter margin? *To add space on the binding edge so text is not hidden or lost in the binding.*
2. Is the gutter added to the margin or does it replace it? *It is added to it.*
3. On which edge is the gutter for a top-bound calendar? *The top.*
4. Why are mirror margins used for a double-sided booklet? *The binding edge alternates sides, and mirror margins put the gutter on the inside of every page.*
5. A4 portrait, margins 2 cm left and right, gutter 1 cm. What is the text width? *21.0 - 2 - 2 - 1 = 16.0 cm.*
6. Why might text move to another page after a gutter is set? *The text area is narrower, so less text fits on each page.*
