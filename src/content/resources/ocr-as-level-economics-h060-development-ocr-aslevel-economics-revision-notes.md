These notes condense topic 2.2 **Development** in Component 02, Macroeconomics, of the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification, Version 3.0 (June 2026). The topic covers the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors, the policy objective of sustainable development, the relationship between growth and sustainable development, and the usefulness of GDP, the HDI and other social and cultural indicators. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [development study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-development-ocr-aslevel-economics/). Practise with the [development practice questions](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-development-ocr-aslevel-economics-practice/). Growth rates and GDP per capita are in the [economic growth revision notes](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-economic-growth-ocr-aslevel-economics-revision-notes/), and the A Level [development revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-economic-development-alternative-measures-and-revision-notes/) go further. All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary sector | Extracting or harvesting natural resources: farming, fishing, mining |
| Secondary sector | Making goods: manufacturing, construction, energy |
| Tertiary sector | Providing services: retail, finance, health, education |
| Structure of the economy | The share of output or employment in each sector |
| Industrialisation | A shift of resources from primary to secondary activity |
| Deindustrialisation | A fall in the share of manufacturing as services grow |
| Economic development | A sustained improvement in living standards and wellbeing |
| Sustainable development | Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs |
| Natural capital | The stock of natural resources, such as forests, fish and a stable climate |
| Decoupling | Output growing faster than resource use or emissions |
| HDI | Index of health, education and income per head, from 0 to 1 |

## Formulas

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sector share (%) | Sector output or employment ÷ total × 100 |
| Change in share | New share − old share, in percentage points |
| Simplified dimension index | (actual − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum) |
| Simplified HDI (practice only) | Average of the health, education and income indices |
| Emissions per unit of output | (1 + emissions growth) ÷ (1 + GDP growth) |

## Worked reminder

Country K employs 2.0 m in primary, 3.0 m in secondary and 5.0 m in tertiary activity: shares of **20%, 30% and 50%** of 10.0 m.

Simplified HDI with goalposts of 20 to 85 years, 0 to 18 years of schooling and 0 to 50,000 income per head. Life expectancy 79.3, schooling 14.4 and income 15,000 give indices of 59.3 ÷ 65 = **0.912**, 14.4 ÷ 18 = **0.800** and **0.300**. Simplified HDI = 2.012 ÷ 3 = **0.671**. The official HDI logs income and uses a geometric mean.

## Structural change

| Stage | Main sector | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Low income | Primary | Most labour needed to grow food |
| Industrialising | Secondary grows | Farm productivity releases labour; investment in factories |
| High income | Tertiary dominates | Demand for services rises with income; manufacturing moves abroad |

## Growth and sustainable development

- **Supports:** funds health, education and clean water; reduces poverty; pays for cleaner technology.
- **Conflicts:** depletes natural capital; raises pollution and emissions; gains may be unequal.
- **Depends on:** the type of growth, policy (pricing pollution), distribution, time period and stage of development.
- Relative decoupling cuts emissions per unit of output; only absolute decoupling cuts total emissions.

## Measures

| Measure | Useful because | Limited because |
|---|---|---|
| GDP per capita | Regular, comparable, shows resources | Hides inequality, unpaid work, pollution and leisure |
| HDI | Adds health and education | Leaves out inequality, environment, freedom; equal weights |
| Social indicators (infant mortality, literacy, clean water, Gini) | Show basic living conditions and fairness | Each shows one aspect; hard to combine |
| Cultural and wellbeing indicators (life satisfaction, trust, cultural participation) | Capture what money misses | Subjective; culturally dependent |

## Evaluation points

- Growth is usually necessary for development but not sufficient.
- A country with higher income per head can have a lower HDI.
- GDP can rise while natural capital falls, so measured growth can overstate sustainable gains.
- No single measure is best: use a dashboard suited to the question.
- Data quality is often weakest in the poorest countries.

## Exam traps

- A falling sector share is not always a falling number of workers.
- Sustainable development has economic and social strands as well as environmental ones.
- Changes in shares are in percentage points, not per cent.
- Do not describe the HDI without its limits.
- Sustained growth (long-lasting) is not the same as sustainable growth (not harming future generations).

## Self-test

1. Name the three sectors and give one example of each.
2. Define sustainable development.
3. Output is 40 bn primary, 60 bn secondary and 100 bn tertiary. Calculate the tertiary share.
4. The primary share of employment falls from 45% to 30%. State the change.
5. Give two reasons why the tertiary share rises as countries get richer.
6. Name the three dimensions of the HDI.
7. Using the simplified method, life expectancy is 66.8 with goalposts of 20 and 85. Calculate the health index.
8. Give two limitations of GDP per capita as a measure of development.
9. Give two limitations of the HDI.
10. Name two social or cultural indicators other than the HDI.
11. GDP rises by 25% and emissions by 5%. What happens to emissions per unit of output?
12. Give one way growth can help sustainable development and one way it can harm it.

### Answers

1. Primary (farming), secondary (manufacturing), tertiary (banking); any correct examples.
2. Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
3. 100 ÷ 200 × 100 = **50%**.
4. A fall of **15 percentage points**.
5. Any two: rising farm and factory productivity releases labour; demand for services rises faster than income; manufacturing moves to lower-cost countries.
6. Health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling), income (GNI per head).
7. 46.8 ÷ 65 = **0.720**.
8. Any two: hides inequality; leaves out unpaid work and the informal economy; counts pollution and depletion as gains; ignores leisure and wellbeing.
9. Any two: leaves out inequality, the environment or freedom; equal weights are arbitrary; schooling years ignore quality; weak data.
10. Any two: infant mortality, literacy, access to clean water, the Gini coefficient, life satisfaction, cultural participation.
11. It falls to 1.05 ÷ 1.25 = 0.84 of its old level, a **16%** fall (relative decoupling).
12. Helps: funds health, education or cleaner technology. Harms: depletes natural resources or raises pollution.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to calculate the official HDI?**
The specification does not list an HDI calculation; it asks you to evaluate the usefulness of the HDI. A simplified calculation helps you see how the three dimensions combine, but what matters most is knowing what it measures and what it leaves out.
