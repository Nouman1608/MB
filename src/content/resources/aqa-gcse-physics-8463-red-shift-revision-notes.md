## Core facts

- Light from **most distant galaxies** shows an **observed increase in wavelength**: **red-shift**.
- Red-shift shows the galaxy is **receding** (moving away).
- **Further away → faster recession → bigger increase in wavelength.**
- The change of speed with distance is evidence that **space itself (the universe) is expanding**.
- Red-shift supports the **Big Bang theory**: the universe began from a **very small region that was extremely hot
  and dense**.
- **Since 1998 onwards**, observations of **supernovae** suggest distant galaxies are **receding ever faster**.
- Not yet understood: **dark mass** and **dark energy**.

## Why receding stretches light

```
source moves away while emitting → each crest emitted further away than the last
→ crests arrive more spread out → longer observed wavelength
faster recession → more spreading → bigger red-shift
```

## Reading spectra

- Elements absorb at fixed wavelengths → dark lines; the **pattern** identifies the element.
- Galaxy spectrum: **same pattern**, every line at a **longer** wavelength.
- Size of red-shift = observed wavelength − laboratory wavelength.

**Method: comparing two galaxies**

```
Step 1  increase = observed λ − laboratory λ   (for each galaxy)
Step 2  fractional increase = increase ÷ laboratory λ
Step 3  bigger increase → receding faster → further away
```

Example (invented): lab 656 nm; galaxy X 676 nm (+20 nm); galaxy Y 696 nm (+40 nm) → Y is faster and further.

## The expanding universe

- Pattern: speed away ∝ distance (for the data you are given, check speed ÷ distance is constant).
- Balloon model: dots on an inflating balloon all move apart; twice as far → twice as fast; no centre on the
  surface.
- Meaning: **space between galaxies is stretching**; not an explosion from one point into empty space.

## The Big Bang chain (learn all five links)

```
1  distant galaxies red-shifted → receding
2  speed increases with distance → universe expanding
3  expanding now → smaller in the past
4  trace back → very small, extremely hot, dense region
5  matches the Big Bang theory → red-shift is evidence for it
```

## Observations to theories

```
observe spectra → find pattern (red-shift grows with distance) → explain (expansion)
→ theory (Big Bang) → new observations test it (1998 supernovae: expansion speeding up)
```

## Not yet understood

| Idea | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Dark mass | More gravity in galaxies than visible matter can explain; unknown what it is |
| Dark energy | Name for whatever causes the expansion to accelerate; nature unknown |

## Must-know distinctions

| Do not confuse | With |
|---|---|
| Increase in wavelength (red-shift) | A change in the colour of the galaxy |
| Space expanding | Galaxies moving through space away from one centre |
| Evidence that supports a theory | Proof of a theory |
| Dark mass: extra gravitational effect in galaxies | Dark energy: cause of accelerating expansion |
| Laboratory wavelength (reference) | Observed wavelength from the galaxy |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red-shift | Observed increase in wavelength of light from a receding galaxy |
| Receding | Moving away |
| Galaxy | Huge group of stars held together by gravity |
| Spectrum | Light spread out by wavelength |
| Absorption line | Dark line where an element absorbs a particular wavelength |
| Big Bang theory | The universe began from a very small, extremely hot and dense region |
| Expanding universe | Space itself is stretching, so galaxies move apart |

## Exam traps

- "The light turns red."
- Nearer galaxies said to have bigger red-shift.
- Galaxies "flying away from the centre of the explosion".
- "Red-shift proves the Big Bang."
- Comparing galaxy wavelengths with each other, not with the laboratory value.
- Missing "it was smaller in the past" from the Big Bang chain.
- Dark mass and dark energy swapped.

## Quick self-test

1. Define red-shift.
2. What does red-shift tell you about a galaxy's motion?
3. Galaxy A has a bigger red-shift than galaxy B. Which is further away?
4. How do astronomers know a line in a galaxy's spectrum has shifted?
5. What does the change of galaxy speed with distance show?
6. What does the Big Bang theory suggest about the start of the universe?
7. Why does an expanding universe imply it was smaller in the past?
8. What did observations of supernovae since 1998 suggest?
9. Name two things about the universe that are not yet understood.
10. A lab line is at 500 nm; it appears at 530 nm in a galaxy's light. Calculate the increase as a percentage.
11. Why is red-shift called evidence, not proof?
12. In the balloon model, why does a more distant dot move away faster?

### Answers

1. The observed increase in the wavelength of light from most distant galaxies.
2. It is moving away from us (receding).
3. Galaxy A.
4. The same pattern of lines as in a laboratory spectrum appears, but at longer wavelengths.
5. That the universe (space itself) is expanding.
6. It began from a very small region that was extremely hot and dense.
7. If galaxies are moving apart now, they were closer together earlier, so it was smaller.
8. That distant galaxies are receding ever faster (the expansion is accelerating).
9. Dark mass and dark energy.
10. 30 ÷ 500 × 100 = **6 %**.
11. It supports the theory, but a theory can be changed if new observations do not fit it.
12. There is more stretching surface between it and you, so the distance between you grows faster.
