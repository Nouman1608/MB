## What the specification says about formulae

- The specification says there is no formula sheet, and that the formulae you must know are the ones listed under 6.02d. They include the circumference and area of a circle.
- For spheres, cones and composite solids (10.04b) it says the formulae will be given. For pyramids (10.04c) it says the formula 1/3 × area of base × height will be given.
- Learn every formula below anyway. It saves time and lets you check what you are given.
- Papers 2 and 5 do not permit a calculator, so practise leaving answers in terms of π.

## Formula table

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid l × w × h | lwh | 2(lw + lh + wh) |
| Any right prism | cross-section area × length | 2 × cross-section + perimeter × length |
| Cylinder (r, h) | πr²h | 2πr² + 2πrh (curved part 2πrh) |
| Sphere (r) | (4/3)πr³ | 4πr² |
| Hemisphere (solid) | (2/3)πr³ | 3πr² (curved part 2πr²) |
| Cone (r, h, slant l) | (1/3)πr²h | πr² + πrl (curved part πrl) |
| Pyramid | (1/3) × base area × height | base + all sloping faces |

Slant height of a cone: l = √(r² + h²). Slant height of a face of a square-based pyramid: √(h² + (half the base side)²).

## Units

| Quantity | Fact |
|---|---|
| Volume units | cm³, m³ and so on |
| 1 cm³ | 1 millilitre |
| 1000 cm³ | 1 litre |
| 1 m³ | 1 000 000 cm³ |
| 1 m² | 10 000 cm² |

Convert all lengths to one unit before you multiply. Area conversions use the square of the length factor, and volume conversions use the cube: 1 m is 100 cm, so 1 m² is 100² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ is 100³ = 1 000 000 cm³.

## Method boxes

**Prism or cylinder volume**
1. Identify the cross-section (it may be a trapezium, triangle, L-shape or circle).
2. Find its area, using Pythagoras if a length is missing.
3. Multiply by the length.

**Total surface area of a prism**
1. List the faces: two ends and the rectangles around.
2. The rectangles together make perimeter × length.
3. Add the two ends.

**Cone or pyramid with a missing slant height**
1. Draw the right-angled triangle: perpendicular height, radius (or half-side), slant height.
2. Use Pythagoras.
3. Use the slant height for area, the perpendicular height for volume.

**Composite solid**
1. Sketch and label each part.
2. For volume, add the parts, or subtract a removed part.
3. For surface area, list only the outside faces. A joined face is hidden for both solids.
4. Add up and give units.

**Working backwards**
1. Write the formula with known values substituted.
2. Rearrange for the unknown.
3. For a cube root or square root, check the answer back in the formula.

## Small worked reminders

- A cylinder of radius 2.5 cm and height 8 cm: V = π × 6.25 × 8 = 50π ≈ 157.1 cm³.
- A sphere of diameter 10 cm has radius 5 cm: V = (4/3) × π × 125 = 500π/3 ≈ 523.6 cm³.
- A cone of radius 6 cm and height 8 cm: l = 10, curved area = π × 6 × 10 = 60π ≈ 188.5 cm².
- A solid hemisphere of radius 2 cm: total surface area = 3π × 4 = 12π ≈ 37.7 cm².
- A pyramid with a 6 cm by 5 cm rectangular base and height 9 cm: V = (1/3) × 30 × 9 = 90 cm³.

## Three full reminders

**A prism with a trapezium cross-section.** Parallel sides 6 cm and 12 cm, distance between them 4 cm, slanted sides 5 cm each (the horizontal overhang on each side is 3 cm, and 3² + 4² = 5²), length 15 cm.

```
Cross-section = ½ × (6 + 12) × 4 = 36 cm²
Volume = 36 × 15 = 540 cm³
Perimeter = 6 + 12 + 5 + 5 = 28 cm
Surface area = 2 × 36 + 28 × 15 = 72 + 420 = 492 cm²
```

**A capsule.** A cylinder of radius 2 cm and length 7 cm with a hemisphere of radius 2 cm on each end.

```
Cylinder: π × 4 × 7 = 28π
Two hemispheres make one sphere: (4/3) × π × 8 = 32π/3
Volume = 28π + 32π/3 = 116π/3 ≈ 121.5 cm³
Surface: curved cylinder 2π × 2 × 7 = 28π, plus a sphere's surface 4π × 4 = 16π
Surface area = 44π ≈ 138.2 cm²
```

**Litres and depth.** A cylindrical tank has radius 50 cm and holds 200 litres. Convert: 200 litres = 200 000 cm³. Then 200 000 = π × 50² × h, so h = 200 000 ÷ (2500π) = 80/π ≈ 25.5 cm.

## Writing answers in terms of π

- Multiply the numbers and write π once: 4² × 11 × π gives 176π.
- Add like terms: 28π + 16π = 44π.
- A fraction of π is fine: 500π/3.
- Convert to a decimal only on the last line, and only if the question asks for it. Give the accuracy the question states.
- Square roots can often be removed by hand before you start. A Pythagorean triple such as 3, 4, 5 or 5, 12, 13 often appears, and noticing it saves time on a paper without a calculator.

## Spotting the right formula in a question

| The question mentions | Think |
|---|---|
| "How much water / paint / metal" | volume |
| "How much material / wrapping / label" | surface area, possibly only part of it |
| "Open at the top" | leave out one end |
| "Same shape melted and recast" | volumes equal |
| "Litres", "millilitres" | convert to cm³ |
| "Slant height", "sloping side" | Pythagoras with the perpendicular height |
| "Hollow", "removed", "cut out" | subtract volumes, check which surfaces are now exposed |

## Must-know distinctions

- Perpendicular height h and slant height l. Volumes use h. Curved cone area uses l.
- Curved surface area and total surface area. Total includes every end or base that is present.
- Diameter and radius. Formulae use the radius.
- Volume (cubic units, space inside) and surface area (square units, outer covering).
- Exact and rounded answers. Give π in the answer if the question asks for it, otherwise follow the stated accuracy.
- A hemisphere as a bowl (open, one curved surface 2πr²) and as a solid (add the flat face πr²).

## Quick self-test

1. A cube has side 7 cm. Find its surface area.
2. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius 2.5 cm and height 8 cm, in terms of π.
3. Find the volume of a sphere of diameter 10 cm, in terms of π.
4. A cone has radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Find the curved surface area in terms of π.
5. Find the volume of a pyramid with a 6 cm by 5 cm base and height 9 cm.
6. Find the total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 2 cm, in terms of π.
7. A cuboid has volume 240 cm³ and a base measuring 4 cm by 6 cm. Find its height.
8. How many cm³ are there in 2 m³?
9. A cylinder has volume 250π cm³ and radius 5 cm. Find its height.
10. A cuboid of base 8 cm by 8 cm and height 5 cm has a square-based pyramid of height 3 cm and the same base on top. Find the total volume.

### Answers

1. 6 × 7² = **294 cm²**.
2. π × 2.5² × 8 = **50π cm³**.
3. Radius 5, so (4/3) × π × 125 = **500π/3 cm³** (≈ 523.6).
4. l = √(36 + 64) = 10, so π × 6 × 10 = **60π cm²**.
5. (1/3) × 30 × 9 = **90 cm³**.
6. 3 × π × 2² = **12π cm²**.
7. 240 ÷ 24 = **10 cm**.
8. 2 × 1 000 000 = **2 000 000 cm³**.
9. 250π = π × 25 × h, so **h = 10 cm**.
10. 320 + (1/3) × 64 × 3 = 320 + 64 = **384 cm³**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter in place of the radius, especially when the radius is given as "half the width".
- Putting the slant height into a volume formula.
- Leaving out the base of a cone or an end of a cylinder in a "total surface area" question, or adding one that is not there.
- Counting the hidden joining face of a composite solid.
- Missing the hypotenuse when finding the perimeter of a triangular cross-section.
- Converting units wrongly: 1 m³ is 1 000 000 cm³, not 100 cm³ or 1000 cm³.
- Rounding π to 3.14 or rounding mid-way and finishing a few units out. Examiners advise keeping exact values until the end.
- Not showing the substitution. If you write only a final decimal and it is wrong, there is little to credit.
- Writing the wrong unit, or none, on the final answer.
