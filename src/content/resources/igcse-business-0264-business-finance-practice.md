## Questions

**1.** State three reasons why a business may need finance. **[3]**

**2.** Define the term working capital. **[2]**

**3.** Identify one short-term and one long-term finance need of a business. **[2]**

**4.** Pendleford Kitchens has inventory of £21,000, trade receivables of £14,000 and cash of £2,500. It owes trade payables of £26,000 and has a bank overdraft of £18,500.

**(a)** Calculate the working capital of Pendleford Kitchens. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one problem the business may face because of its working capital. **[2]**

**5.** State two internal sources of finance. **[2]**

**6.** Rowanby Cycles buys a delivery van with a cash price of £16,000 on hire purchase. It pays a deposit of £3,200 and 30 monthly instalments of £460.

**(a)** Calculate the total amount paid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much more this is than the cash price. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one difference between hire purchase and leasing. **[2]**

**7.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of a bank overdraft as a source of finance. **[4]**

**8.** Millbrook Gate Games needs £40,000 of start-up capital. The owner provides £14,000. A crowdfunding campaign raises £16,000 from 320 backers. A government grant provides the remaining £10,000.

**(a)** Calculate the owner's share of the start-up capital as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average amount given by each backer. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one risk to Millbrook Gate Games of relying on crowdfunding. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one advantage of a government grant compared with a bank loan. **[2]**

**9.** Choose two of the factors that affect the selection of finance, and for each explain how it could change the decision. **[4]**

**10.** Aldermoor Joinery is a partnership. The partners want £42,000 for a computer-controlled cutting machine that they expect to use for eight years. They can put in £12,000 of retained profit, have no other borrowing, and have little spare cash for a large deposit.
Recommend a source of finance for the remaining £30,000 and justify your choice. **[6]**

**11.** Ludlowe Print Ltd needs £120,000 for a new printing press. It has 90,000 shares. Option A is to issue 60,000 new shares at £2 each. Option B is a bank loan of £120,000 at 7% interest a year. The business currently makes a profit of £30,000 a year, and you should assume it would stay at this level before any loan interest.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly interest on the loan. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the company owned by the new shareholders under Option A. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which option Ludlowe Print Ltd should choose. **[6]**

**12.** Give one benefit and one drawback of a business raising money by selling assets it no longer wants. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, one mark each: start-up capital [1]; capital for expansion or growth [1]; replacing existing non-current assets [1]. (Investing in new technology and working capital are also accepted.) **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" asks for the point only, so spend no time on explanation. Repeating the same reason in different words earns nothing extra.

**2.** The money a business has available to pay its everyday expenses [1], found by current assets minus current liabilities [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Money in the bank" or "cash" alone is a common weak answer. Mentioning the formula or the idea of everyday bills shows you know it is wider than cash.

**3.** Short-term: for example paying wages or suppliers, or bridging a gap before customers pay [1]. Long-term: for example buying a new building or machine, or expanding to a second site [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give one of each, not two of one kind. Linking the need to a time period shows you understand the difference.

**4. (a)** Current assets = 21,000 + 14,000 + 2,500 = £37,500 [1]. Current liabilities = 26,000 + 18,500 = £44,500 [1]. Working capital = 37,500 - 44,500 = **-£7,000** [1].
**(b)** Working capital is negative, so the business owes more in the short term than it has available [1]. It may struggle to pay suppliers or wages on time, which could damage supplier relationships or lead to insolvency [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both totals and the subtraction so your method is visible. In part (b), a problem alone is not enough: add what could happen as a result.

**5.** Owners' investment [1]; retained profit [1]. (Sale of unwanted assets and working capital are also accepted.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not offer a loan or an overdraft here. Both come from outside the business, so they are external sources.

**6. (a)** 30 x £460 = £13,800 [1]. Total = 3,200 + 13,800 = **£17,000** [1].
**(b)** 17,000 - 16,000 = **£1,000** [1].
**(c)** With hire purchase the business becomes the owner of the van after the final payment [1]. With leasing it only rents the van and never owns it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare both methods in your answer. Describing only hire purchase leaves the difference unstated.

**7.** Advantage: it is flexible, because the business borrows only what it needs and pays interest only on the amount drawn [1], so it suits a short gap in cash flow [1]. Disadvantage: interest rates can be high if the overdraft is used for a long time [1], or the bank can ask for repayment at short notice, which could leave the business short of cash [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each of the two points needs a reason or consequence to earn full credit. A bare "it is flexible" is only the start of a point.

**8. (a)** 14,000 / 40,000 x 100 [1] = **35%** [1].
**(b)** 16,000 / 320 = **£50** [1].
**(c)** The campaign might not reach its target [1], so the business would have to find the missing money elsewhere or delay opening [1]. (Accept: backers may expect rewards that add to costs.)
**(d)** A grant does not have to be repaid [1], whereas a bank loan must be repaid with interest, so the grant leaves more cash in the business [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) and (d), name Millbrook Gate Games' situation or the effect on its cash. Generic statements are weaker than ones tied to the case.

**9.** Any two factors, each with an explanation: size of business, legal form, amount required, length of time, existing loans, cost, or purpose. For example: legal form [1], because only a company can issue shares so a sole trader must use other sources [1]. Amount required [1], because a small sum may be met by trade credit but a large sum may need a long-term loan or shares [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A list of factors scores only for naming them. The reasoning, saying how each factor changes the choice, earns the rest.

**10.** Recommendation: a long-term bank loan of £30,000 [1]. The machine is a long-term need of eight years, so finance of a similar length matches the purpose [1]. A partnership cannot issue shares, so the legal form rules that source out [1]. An overdraft would be costly and could be recalled at short notice, so it is unsuitable for a machine [1]. With no existing loans, the partners can probably borrow, so repayments should be affordable [1]. Using £12,000 of retained profit first cuts the amount borrowed and the interest paid [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick one source, say why it fits this case, and say why a different source is a weaker choice. Listing every source without choosing weakens a "recommend and justify" answer.

**11. (a)** 120,000 x 7% [1] = **£8,400** a year [1].
**(b)** 60,000 / (90,000 + 60,000) [1] = **40%** [1].
**(c)** For Option A: there is no repayment and no interest, so the profit of £30,000 stays in the business [1]. Against Option A: the new shareholders own 40%, so the existing owners lose some control and share future profit [1]. For Option B: the owners keep full ownership [1]. Against Option B: interest of £8,400 leaves profit of 30,000 - 8,400 = £21,600, and the loan must be repaid even if profit falls [1]. Judgement, for example: a loan is better if the owners value control and profit is steady, because £21,600 still remains [1]. Shares are better if the owners fear repayment pressure [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs both options weighed and a decision with a reason. A one-sided answer, or a conclusion that just repeats a point, usually leaves marks unclaimed.

**12.** Advantage: it raises cash without borrowing, so there is no interest or repayment [1], and it removes an item the business no longer needs, which may have running costs [1]. Disadvantage: it is usually a one-off source and cannot be repeated each year [1], and the business may have to accept a low price if it needs the money quickly [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* This is an "unwanted" asset question. Say why the sale does not harm the business, as selling an asset it still needs would be a different problem.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "money in the bank" instead of current assets less current liabilities when defining working capital.
- Giving an external source, such as a loan, when asked for internal sources.
- Not showing working in calculations and leaving out units such as £ or %.
- Recommending a source but not applying case facts such as legal form, amount or length of time.
- Describing a source with no consequence for cost, cash flow or control.
- Mixing up leasing and hire purchase, or overdraft and bank loan.
- Giving only advantages when asked to evaluate.
- Forgetting that a loan must be repaid with interest and a grant does not.
