## Questions

**1.** A heating element carries a steady current of 2.8 A for 25 minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the charge that passes through the element. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of electrons that pass through it in this time. **[2]**

**2.** Four oil droplets are found to carry charges of 3.2, 4.8, 5.6 and 8.0 (all × 10⁻¹⁹ C). Identify the charge that cannot be correct and explain your choice. **[3]**

**3.** State Kirchhoff's first law and explain why it is a consequence of conservation of charge. Currents of 3.5 A and 1.2 A enter a junction, and 2.9 A leaves in one wire. Calculate the current in the only other wire leaving the junction. **[4]**

**4.** **(a)** A metal wire is connected across a cell. State the direction of electron flow relative to the direction of conventional current. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how charge is carried through an electrolyte. **[2]**
**(c)** In an electrolyte, 3.0 × 10¹⁸ singly charged positive ions cross a plane each second in one direction and 2.0 × 10¹⁸ singly charged negative ions cross it each second in the other direction. Calculate the current. **[2]**

**5.** Show that the current I in a conductor is given by I = Anev, defining each symbol. **[4]**

**6.** A copper wire has a diameter of 1.2 mm and carries a current of 4.5 A. The number density of conduction electrons in copper is 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³.
**(a)** Calculate the mean drift velocity of the electrons. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken for an electron to drift 1.0 m along the wire. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Two wires X and Y of the same metal are joined in series. The diameter of Y is one third of the diameter of X. Compare the mean drift velocities in the two wires. **[3]**
**(b)** A metal has n = 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³ and a semiconductor sample of the same cross-sectional area has n = 4.0 × 10²² m⁻³ (both given). The two carry the same current. Calculate the ratio of the drift velocity in the semiconductor to that in the metal. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, in terms of n, why an insulator carries almost no current. **[1]**

**8.** Draw a circuit diagram for an experiment to find how the current in a filament lamp varies with the p.d. across it. The circuit must allow the current to be changed. **[4]**

**9.** A motor has a p.d. of 9.0 V across it and a current of 0.80 A for 3.0 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred to the motor. **[3]**

**10.** A battery of e.m.f. 4.5 V supplies a steady current of 0.60 A to a lamp for 5.0 minutes. The p.d. across the lamp is 4.1 V.
**(a)** State the difference between e.m.f. and p.d. in terms of energy transfer. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge that passes and the energy the battery supplies to this charge. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in the lamp and state where the remaining energy goes. **[2]**

**11.** Electrons and protons are each released from rest and accelerated through a p.d. of 1.2 kV in a vacuum.
**(a)** Calculate the final speed of an electron. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the final speed of a proton and compare it with that of the electron. **[2]**

**12.** A beam of alpha particles (charge +2e, mass 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) is accelerated from rest through 1.5 kV. The beam current is 2.0 μA.
**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy gained by one alpha particle. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of alpha particles arriving each second. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the power carried by the beam, and check it using the beam current. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** t = 25 × 60 = 1500 s [1]. Q = It = 2.8 × 1500 = **4.2 × 10³ C** (4200 C) [1].
**(b)** N = Q/e [1] = 4200 / 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **2.6 × 10²² electrons** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the time to seconds before substituting, and write that conversion as a separate line so it is easy to check.

**2.** Divide each charge by e: 3.2/1.6 = 2.0, 4.8/1.6 = 3.0, 5.6/1.6 = 3.5, 8.0/1.6 = 5.0 [1]. Charge is quantised, so every value must be a whole-number multiple of e [1]. **5.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C** cannot be correct because it is 3.5e [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the principle (whole-number multiples of e) and show the division, rather than quoting the value alone.

**3.** Total current into a junction equals total current out of it [1]. Charge cannot be created, destroyed or stored at the junction, so the charge entering per second equals the charge leaving per second [1]. 3.5 + 1.2 = 2.9 + I [1]. I = **1.8 A** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention charge not building up at the junction; restating the law does not explain it.

**4. (a)** Electrons are repelled from the negative terminal and travel through the wire towards the positive terminal [1]. Conventional current is drawn the opposite way, out of the positive terminal [1].
**(b)** Free ions carry the charge: positive ions move towards the negative electrode and negative ions move towards the positive electrode [1]. Both contribute to the current, so the current is the sum of the two [1].
**(c)** Charge crossing per second = (3.0 × 10¹⁸ + 2.0 × 10¹⁸) × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = **0.80 A** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), check that every carrier crossing the plane is counted, including both signs of ion.

**5.** In time t, carriers drift a distance vt, so the volume of conductor passed is Avt, where A is the cross-sectional area and v the mean drift velocity [1]. The number of carriers in that volume is nAvt, with n the number density [1]. The charge passing is nAvte, with e the charge on each carrier [1]. I = Q/t = nAvte / t = **Anev** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question, give every step in order, ending on the stated result.

**6. (a)** A = π(0.60 × 10⁻³)² = 1.13 × 10⁻⁶ m² [1]. v = I / (Ane) [1] = 4.5 / (1.13 × 10⁻⁶ × 8.5 × 10²⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **2.9 × 10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** t = distance / speed = 1.0 / 2.9 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **3.4 × 10³ s** (about 57 minutes) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Substituting 1.2 mm where the radius belongs gives the wrong area and a drift velocity out by a factor of four, so write the radius on its own line first.

**7. (a)** The current is the same and n is the same, so v ∝ 1/A [1]. A ∝ d², so Y has one ninth of the area of X [1]. The drift velocity in Y is **9 times** that in X [1].
**(b)** For the same I and A, v ∝ 1/n [1]. v(semiconductor) / v(metal) = 8.5 × 10²⁸ / 4.0 × 10²² = **2.1 × 10⁶** [1].
**(c)** An insulator has almost no free charge carriers, so n is extremely small and no useful current flows even with a large p.d. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Solve comparisons by proportion, stating which quantities stay constant.

**8.** Cell (or power supply) in series with a switch, a variable resistor and the filament lamp, forming a closed loop [1]. An ammeter in series with the lamp [1]. A voltmeter connected in parallel across the lamp only [1]. Standard symbols and ruled lines [1]. **[4]**

```
   +--[cell]--[switch]--[variable resistor]--+
   |                                          |
   |       +-----(V)-----+                    |
   |       |             |                    |
   +--(A)--+----(lamp)---+--------------------+
```

*Examiner insight:* Check each meter's position against its job: ammeter in series, voltmeter across the lamp.

**9.** Q = It = 0.80 × 180 [1] = 144 C. W = VQ [1] = 9.0 × 144 = **1.3 × 10³ J** (1296 J) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the charge as an intermediate result and quote the unit.

**10. (a)** E.m.f. is the energy transferred to each coulomb of charge by the source, from chemical (or other) energy to electrical [1]. P.d. is the energy transferred from each coulomb of charge, from electrical to other forms, in a component [1].
**(b)** Q = It = 0.60 × 300 = 180 C [1]. W = EQ = 4.5 × 180 = **810 J** [1].
**(c)** W = VQ = 4.1 × 180 = **738 J** [1]. The remaining 72 J is transferred as thermal energy inside the battery itself [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Define both terms using the direction of the energy change. Saying "e.m.f. is the voltage of the battery" does not make the distinction.

**11. (a)** Electrical energy gained = kinetic energy: eV = ½mv² [1]. v = √(2eV/m) = √(2 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1200 / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **2.05 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** v = √(2 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1200 / 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = **4.8 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1]. The electron is about **43 times faster**, the square root of the mass ratio [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both speeds and the ratio. Check the mass used for each particle.

**12. (a)** KE = qV = 2 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1500 = **4.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1].
**(b)** ½mv² = 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁶, so v = √(2 × 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷) [1] = **3.8 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Rate = I / (2e) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ / (3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = **6.3 × 10¹² s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** Power = rate × KE = 6.25 × 10¹² × 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ = **3.0 × 10⁻³ W** [1]. Check: IV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 1500 = 3.0 × 10⁻³ W [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Each particle carries 2e, so the number arriving per second is half what it would be for singly charged particles at the same current.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting minutes into seconds.
- Using the diameter or radius in place of the area, or forgetting to square the radius.
- Leaving n in cm⁻³ or the area in mm² when other quantities are in SI units.
- Missing one of the two ion types when finding current in an electrolyte.
- Describing drift velocity as the speed of electrons.
- Defining p.d. and e.m.f. without energy per unit charge.
- Using the proton mass for an electron, or e instead of 2e for an alpha particle.
