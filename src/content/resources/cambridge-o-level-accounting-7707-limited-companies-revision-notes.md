These notes condense section **5.3 Limited companies** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The section covers company types, "advantages and disadvantages of operating as a limited company", limited liability, equity, the four-part capital structure, ordinary shares and debentures, "issued, called-up, and paid-up" share capital and the three statements, with the 5.1 adjustments. For full explanations and a worked statement of changes in equity, read the [limited companies study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-limited-companies/); then try the [limited companies practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-limited-companies-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limited liability | A shareholder can lose no more than the amount paid, or unpaid, on the shares held |
| Equity | Total assets less total liabilities: the shareholders' interest in the company |
| Ordinary share capital | Shares owned by the company's owners; usually carry votes; dividend varies and may be nil |
| Preference share capital | Shares with a fixed rate of dividend paid before any ordinary dividend; usually no vote |
| General reserve | Profit transferred from retained earnings and kept in the company |
| Retained earnings | Accumulated profit not yet paid out as dividends or transferred to reserves |
| Debentures (loan capital) | Long-term loans to the company at a fixed rate of interest, repaid on an agreed date |
| Issued share capital | Number of shares issued x nominal value |
| Called-up share capital | Number of shares issued x amount per share the company has asked for |
| Paid-up share capital | Called-up capital less amounts not yet received |
| Finance costs | Interest on debentures and other loans, shown in the statement of profit or loss |

## Company types in one line each

- **Trading**: revenue, then cost of sales from opening inventory, purchases and closing inventory.
- **Service**: fees or revenue, then expenses; no cost of sales.
- **Manufacturing**: a manufacturing account first; its factory cost of production replaces purchases in cost of sales.

## Advantages and disadvantages to recall

- **For**: limited liability; capital from many shareholders; continuity when shareholders change; a separate legal person that owns assets and makes contracts.
- **Against**: cost and formality of setting up; legal rules on accounts and less privacy; profit shared widely; shareholders who are not directors have little day-to-day control.

## Where each item goes

| Item | Statement of profit or loss | Statement of changes in equity | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debenture interest for the year | Finance costs | | Any unpaid part under other payables |
| Preference and ordinary dividends paid | Never | Deducted from retained earnings | Lower bank |
| Profit for the year | Final line | Added to retained earnings | Inside retained earnings |
| Transfer to general reserve | Never | Out of retained earnings, into general reserve | Both closing balances |
| New shares issued for cash | Never | Added to share capital | Higher share capital and bank |
| Debentures | Never | Never | Non-current liabilities |

## The chains to memorise

**Profit:** gross profit - expenses = profit from operations; profit from operations - finance costs = profit for the year.

**Retained earnings:** opening balance + profit for the year - transfer to general reserve - preference dividend - ordinary dividend = closing balance.

**Equity check:** opening equity + shares issued + profit for the year - dividends = closing equity. Transfers between reserves do not change the total.

## Dividend arithmetic

- Preference dividend = rate x preference share capital. A 6% rate on $80,000 gives $4,800.
- Ordinary dividend per share x number of shares. Read which date's shares the question means: shares in issue at the start, or at the date of payment.
- A dividend can be paid in a loss year from retained earnings built up in earlier years.

## Exam traps

- Dividends in the statement of profit or loss lose marks every time.
- Charge a **full year** of debenture interest: rate x debentures. Any unpaid part is accrued.
- A transfer to the general reserve is not an expense and moves no cash.
- Debentures are a liability, not part of equity.
- Issued, called-up and paid-up are three different figures; give the one asked for.
- An error in closing inventory or an accrual changes profit for the year, retained earnings and the statement of financial position by the same amount.
- Use the full heading on each statement, including the company name and date.
- In the statement of changes in equity, show deductions in brackets and total every column.

## Self-test

1. Define limited liability.
2. A company has total assets of $480,000 and total liabilities of $130,000. Calculate its equity.
3. A company issues 80,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 and calls $0.40 a share. Holders of 2,500 shares have not paid. Calculate issued, called-up and paid-up share capital.
4. A company has $45,000 of 6% debentures and has paid $1,350 interest during the year. State the finance cost for the year and the amount accrued.
5. Where does a preference dividend paid appear?
6. Retained earnings at the start were $25,000. Profit for the year was $38,000, $8,000 was transferred to general reserve and dividends of $12,000 were paid. Calculate closing retained earnings.
7. What is the effect of a transfer to general reserve on total equity?
8. State two differences between ordinary shares and debentures.
9. Profit from operations is $51,200 and debenture interest is $3,600. Calculate profit for the year.
10. Closing inventory was understated by $1,500. State the effect on profit for the year, retained earnings and current assets.
11. Explain how a company can pay a dividend in a year when it made a loss.
12. State one disadvantage of operating as a limited company.

### Answers

1. Shareholders can lose no more than the amount paid, or unpaid, on their shares; their personal assets are not used to pay the company's debts.
2. 480,000 - 130,000 = **$350,000**.
3. Issued: 80,000 x $0.50 = **$40,000**. Called-up: 80,000 x $0.40 = **$32,000**. Unpaid: 2,500 x $0.40 = $1,000, so paid-up = **$31,000**.
4. Finance cost: 6% x 45,000 = **$2,700**. Accrued: 2,700 - 1,350 = **$1,350**, shown under other payables.
5. In the statement of changes in equity, deducted from retained earnings. It does not appear in the statement of profit or loss.
6. 25,000 + 38,000 - 8,000 - 12,000 = **$43,000**.
7. None: it moves profit from retained earnings to the general reserve within equity.
8. Any two: ordinary shareholders are owners, debenture holders are lenders; dividends vary and are not compulsory, interest is fixed and must be paid; shares are equity, debentures are a liability; shareholders usually vote, debenture holders do not; debentures are repaid on an agreed date.
9. 51,200 - 3,600 = **$47,600**.
10. Each is $1,500 too low: profit for the year, retained earnings at the year end and current assets (inventory).
11. Dividends are paid from retained earnings, which include profits kept from earlier years, so a balance can remain after a loss.
12. Any one: setting-up cost and formalities; accounts may have to be made available to the public; profit shared among many shareholders; shareholders who are not directors have little control.
