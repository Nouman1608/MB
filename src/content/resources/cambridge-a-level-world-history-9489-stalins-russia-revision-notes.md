**These notes condense Stalin's Russia, 1924-41, the second topic of the Paper 4 European option in Cambridge 9489.** For fuller explanation and an annotated paragraph see the [Stalin's Russia study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-stalins-russia/); for essays and short questions see the [Stalin's Russia practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-stalins-russia-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 2.1 How did Stalin gain control of the Soviet Union?
- 2.2 How effectively did Stalin govern the Soviet Union?
- 2.3 How successful were Stalin's economic policies?
- 2.4 How far did Stalin transform Soviet society?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Collective leadership | Shared Politburo rule after Lenin's incapacity and death |
| Lenin's Testament | Lenin's notes of 1922-23 assessing his colleagues; the postscript urged Stalin's removal |
| General Secretary | Head of the party Secretariat, controlling appointments; Stalin from 1922 |
| NEP | New Economic Policy (1921): private trade and peasant markets within a socialist state |
| Socialism in One Country | Stalin's 1924 doctrine that the USSR could build socialism without world revolution |
| Permanent Revolution | Trotsky's view that socialism in Russia depended on revolution abroad |
| Great Turn | The 1928-29 shift to forced collectivisation and rapid planned industrialisation |
| Kolkhoz / sovkhoz | Collective farm / state farm with wage-paid labour |
| Dekulakisation | Expropriation, deportation or killing of peasants labelled kulaks |
| Gosplan | State planning agency drafting Five Year Plan targets |
| Stakhanovite | Worker who exceeded norms, after Alexei Stakhanov's 1935 record |
| Yezhovshchina | Peak of the Terror, 1937-38, under NKVD chief Yezhov |
| Gulag | System of forced-labour camps |
| Great Retreat | Return to traditional values in family, school and culture from the mid-1930s |
| Socialist Realism | Official artistic doctrine from 1934: art must show socialist progress |

## 2.1 Power struggle -- four reasons in brief

1. **Office:** General Secretary, Orgburo, appointments, the Lenin enrolment.
2. **Ideas:** Socialism in One Country was patriotic and hopeful; Stalin occupied the centre between Left and Right.
3. **Rivals' errors:** Trotsky's arrogance and absence from the funeral; Zinoviev and Kamenev's opposition to the October 1917 rising; Bukharin's late resistance.
4. **Alliances:** with Zinoviev-Kamenev (1923-25), with the Right (1925-27), against the Right (1928-29).

## 2.2 Governing -- tools and results

- **Terror:** Kirov's murder as trigger; NKVD; show trials 1936-38; mass operations with quotas; gulags; the 1937 purge of the high command.
- **Cult:** fiftieth birthday (1929), the *Short Course* (1938), posters and statues, "Father of the Peoples".
- **Constitution 1936:** rights on paper; one-party reality.
- **Foreign policy:** Treaty of Berlin (1926) -- collective security from 1934 -- Spain (1936-39) -- clashes with Japan (1938-39) -- Nazi-Soviet Pact (1939). Neutrality Pact with Japan, April 1941.

## 2.3 Economy -- balance sheet

| Area | Gains (regime's view) | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Collectivisation | State control of grain; labour freed for industry | Famine of 1932-33; livestock roughly halved; low productivity |
| Plans 1-2 | Heavy industry multiplied; new industrial cities | Waste, falsified figures, poor quality |
| Plan 3 | Armaments production expanded | Consumer goods squeezed |
| Labour | Stakhanovite incentives, technical training | Labour books, harsh discipline laws (1938-40), forced labour |

## 2.4 Society -- change and retreat

- **Women:** mass entry into industry; double burden; 1936 abortion ban and harder divorce.
- **Nationalities:** Russification; deportations of whole peoples; heavy famine losses in Kazakhstan and Ukraine.
- **Education:** from progressive experiment to traditional schooling; fees from 1940; literacy and technical training expanded.
- **Culture:** Cultural Revolution (1928-31), then Socialist Realism; artists under pressure.
- **Religion:** 1929 law, League of the Militant Godless, church closures and arrests; belief persisted.

## Exam traps

- **Dates of the Plans:** first 1928-32 (declared complete early), second 1933-37, third 1938-41 (cut short by war).
- **Alliances:** Stalin sided with the Right against the Left, then turned on the Right; do not reverse them.
- **Terror figures:** quote records cautiously and say what they measure.
- **"Transform":** set progress (literacy, industrial jobs for women) against reversal (the Great Retreat).
- **Foreign policy:** the Pact was a response to failed collective security as well as a deal with Hitler.

## Self-test

1. What did the postscript to Lenin's Testament recommend? *Removing Stalin as General Secretary.*
2. Which two rivals saved Stalin from the Testament in 1924? *Zinoviev and Kamenev.*
3. What was Permanent Revolution? *Trotsky's view that the Russian revolution must spread abroad to survive.*
4. Who were the leaders of the Right? *Bukharin, Rykov and Tomsky.*
5. What triggered the Great Turn in 1927-28? *The grain procurement crisis, as peasants held back grain from low state prices.*
6. What did "Dizzy with Success" do? *It blamed local officials for excesses and briefly slowed collectivisation.*
7. Whose murder in December 1934 began the escalation of terror? *Sergei Kirov's.*
8. Name two defendants at the March 1938 show trial. *Bukharin and Rykov (also Yagoda).*
9. Which senior commander was shot in 1937? *Marshal Tukhachevsky.*
10. What did the 1936 family law do? *Banned abortion and made divorce harder and costlier.*
11. Where was Socialist Realism adopted as official policy? *At the First Congress of Soviet Writers, 1934.*
12. What did the Nazi-Soviet Pact's secret protocol do? *It divided eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres, including Poland.*
