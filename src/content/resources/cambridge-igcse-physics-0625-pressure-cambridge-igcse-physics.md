This guide teaches section 1.8, Pressure, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Core statements 1 to 3 cover p = F/A, everyday examples and a qualitative description of pressure in liquids. Supplement statement 4, the equation Δp = ρgΔh, is examined only on Papers 2 and 4 and is labelled **Extended only**. The [Motion, forces and energy study guide](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy/) notes that liquid pressure does not depend on the container's shape; this page builds the calculations and explanations around that.

## What the syllabus asks for

| Statement | Tier | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core | Define pressure as force per unit area; recall and use p = F/A |
| 2 | Core | Describe how pressure varies with force and area in everyday examples |
| 3 | Core | Describe, qualitatively, how pressure beneath the surface of a liquid changes with depth and density |
| 4 | Extended only | Recall and use Δp = ρgΔh for the change in pressure beneath the surface of a liquid |

## Pressure = force ÷ area

```
p = F / A        p in pascals (Pa),  F in newtons (N),  A in m²
1 Pa = 1 N/m²
```

The force is the force acting **at right angles** (normal) to the surface, such as the weight of an object resting on the floor. You may also see N/cm² when areas are small.

### Converting areas

Areas are often given in cm² or mm², and the conversion is the step most often wrong.

- 1 m = 100 cm, so **1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm²**. Divide cm² by 10 000 to get m².
- 1 m = 1000 mm, so **1 m² = 1 000 000 mm²**.
- 25 cm² = 25 ÷ 10 000 = 0.0025 m².

**Worked example 1.** A person of weight 600 N stands in flat shoes with a total area of 300 cm² on the floor. Later, they balance all their weight on one stiletto heel of area 1.0 cm². Compare the pressures.

```
flat shoes:  A = 300 / 10 000 = 0.030 m²      p = 600 / 0.030  = 20 000 Pa
heel:        A = 1.0 / 10 000 = 0.000 10 m²   p = 600 / 0.000 10 = 6 000 000 Pa
```

The same force on an area 300 times smaller gives a pressure 300 times larger, enough to dent a wooden floor.

**Worked example 2 (choosing the face).** A brick of mass 2.4 kg measures 20 cm × 10 cm × 5.0 cm (g = 9.8 N/kg). Find the largest and smallest pressures it can exert on the ground.

```
W = 2.4 × 9.8 = 23.5 N
smallest face: 10 cm × 5.0 cm = 50 cm² = 0.0050 m²   → p = 23.5 / 0.0050 = 4700 Pa (largest)
largest face:  20 cm × 10 cm = 200 cm² = 0.020 m²    → p = 23.5 / 0.020  = 1200 Pa (smallest)
```

The weight is the same whichever face it rests on; only the area changes.

## Everyday examples

| Example | Large or small area? | Effect on pressure | Why it is useful |
|---|---|---|---|
| snowshoes and skis | large | low | the person does not sink into soft snow |
| wide tractor tyres | large | low | the tractor does not sink into soft mud |
| wide straps on a heavy bag | large | low | less painful on the shoulder |
| building foundations | large | low | the ground beneath does not sink |
| knife blade (sharp) | very small | high | cuts easily with a small force |
| drawing pin point | very small | high | pushes into a board easily |
| needle, nail, football studs | small | high | pierce material or grip the ground |

The head of a drawing pin is a neat two-sided example: the same force acts on your thumb (large head, low pressure, no pain) and on the board (tiny point, high pressure, the pin goes in).

To describe how pressure varies: **for the same area, a larger force gives a larger pressure; for the same force, a smaller area gives a larger pressure.**

## Pressure in liquids

Beneath the surface of a liquid, the pressure:

- **increases with depth**, because there is more liquid above pushing down;
- **is greater in a denser liquid** at the same depth;
- acts **in all directions** at a given depth;
- depends only on depth and density (and g), not on the shape or width of the container.

Consequences:

- A dam is built **thicker at the bottom**, where the water pressure is greatest.
- Water squirts out **faster** from a hole near the bottom of a tall container than from a hole near the top.
- A diver feels increasing pressure on the ears as they swim deeper.
- Water-storage tanks are placed high up, so the pressure in taps below is large.

## Δp = ρgΔh (Extended only)

```
Δp = ρ g Δh      Δp in Pa, ρ in kg/m³, g in N/kg, Δh in m
```

Δh is the **vertical** depth below the surface (or the vertical difference between two points in the liquid).

**Worked example 3.** A diver goes from the surface to a depth of 25 m in sea water of density 1030 kg/m³. Find the increase in pressure.

```
Δp = ρ g Δh = 1030 × 9.8 × 25 = 252 350 Pa  ≈ 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa
```

The pressure on the diver also includes the pressure of the atmosphere pushing on the surface. Δp = ρgΔh gives only the extra pressure due to the water; if a question gives atmospheric pressure and asks for the **total** pressure, add the two.

**Worked example 4 (comparing liquids).** Find the pressure due to the liquid 0.40 m below the surface of oil (density 800 kg/m³) and of water (density 1000 kg/m³).

```
oil:   Δp = 800  × 9.8 × 0.40 = 3136 Pa  ≈ 3100 Pa
water: Δp = 1000 × 9.8 × 0.40 = 3920 Pa  ≈ 3900 Pa
```

**Worked example 5 (finding a depth).** At what depth in fresh water is the pressure due to the water 49 000 Pa?

```
Δh = Δp / (ρ g) = 49 000 / (1000 × 9.8) = 5.0 m
```

## Common mistakes

- Dividing cm² by 100 instead of 10 000.
- Using mass (kg) instead of weight (N) as the force.
- Saying a sharp knife has "more force"; it has a smaller area and so a larger pressure for the same force.
- Thinking a wider container has a greater pressure at the bottom for the same depth.
- *(Extended)* Using the distance from the bottom of the container instead of the depth below the surface.
- *(Extended)* Using density in g/cm³ in Δp = ρgΔh: convert to kg/m³ first.

## Exam technique: command words

- **Define** pressure: "force per unit area". The equation alone may not earn the mark.
- **Calculate**: convert areas to m² before substituting; give the unit Pa (or N/m²).
- **Describe** how pressure varies in an everyday example: say what happens to the area, then what happens to the pressure, for the same force.
- **Explain** a design (a dam, a tractor tyre): link the shape to the pressure and the pressure to the result.
- **Describe, qualitatively**, liquid pressure: "increases with depth; greater in a denser liquid".
