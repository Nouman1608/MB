These questions cover sub-topic **1.2 The accounting equation**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define the term liability. **[1]**

**2.** A business has assets of 85,000, current liabilities of 6,200 and non-current liabilities of 20,000. What is the owner's equity?

A: 58,800

B: 65,000

C: 78,800

D: 111,200 **[1]**

**3.** Which transaction increases both assets and liabilities?

A: paying a supplier by bank transfer

B: buying a machine on credit

C: the owner withdrawing cash

D: selling goods for cash at cost price **[1]**

**4.** State whether each item is an asset or a liability: (a) trade receivables; (b) bank overdraft; (c) delivery van; (d) other payables. **[4]**

**5.** A business has assets of 46,300 and owner's equity of 31,850. Calculate its liabilities. **[1]**

**6.** Owner's equity was 24,000 on 1 January and 27,600 on 31 December. During the year the owner introduced capital of 1,500 and took drawings of 7,200. Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Orbit Phone Repairs has these transactions.

(i) Buys tools on credit, 900.

(ii) Pays electricity by bank transfer, 120.

(iii) A customer pays the 340 she owed.

(iv) The owner takes 200 cash for private use.

(v) Repairs a phone for a customer who pays 150 cash.

(vi) Pays a supplier 500 by bank transfer.

**(a)** For each transaction, state the effect (increase, decrease or no effect) on total assets, total liabilities and owner's equity.

**(b)** Explain why the accounting equation still balances after transaction (iii). **[8]**

**8.** At the start of March a business has assets of 18,000, liabilities of 4,000 and owner's equity of 14,000. During March:

1. It buys goods on credit for 2,000.
2. It sells goods that cost 1,500 for 2,400 cash.
3. It pays rent of 600 in cash.
4. It receives a long-term loan of 3,000.
5. It pays 2,500 to a supplier.

**(a)** Prepare a table showing total assets, total liabilities and owner's equity after each transaction.

**(b)** Calculate the profit for March and show that it explains the change in owner's equity. **[7]**

**9.** Sana Textiles has these balances at 31 December 2027: machinery 32,000; motor vehicles 14,500; inventory 7,800; trade receivables 5,600; cash 180; bank overdraft 2,350; trade payables 6,900; other payables 450; loan repayable in 2033, 12,000.

**(a)** Calculate total non-current assets, total current assets, total current liabilities and total non-current liabilities.

**(b)** Calculate the owner's equity. **[6]**

**10.** At 1 January 2027 a business had assets of 51,200 and liabilities of 13,700. At 31 December 2027 it had assets of 58,050 and liabilities of 16,400. During the year the owner brought his own van, valued at 6,000, into the business, took 9,800 in cash and took goods costing 650 for his family.

**(a)** Calculate the owner's equity at the start and end of the year.

**(b)** Calculate the profit for the year.

**(c)** Explain why drawings are added back when calculating profit from the change in owner's equity. **[7]**

**11.** Kofi lists his business position as follows. Assets: shop fittings 9,000; inventory 4,300; bank overdraft 1,600; his private flat 75,000. Liabilities: trade payables 3,100. He calculates owner's equity as 86,800.

**(a)** Identify **two** errors in Kofi's list.

**(b)** Calculate the correct owner's equity.

**(c)** Name the accounting concept Kofi has ignored by including the flat, and explain it. **[6]**

**12.** State the effect of each transaction on total assets, total liabilities and owner's equity, giving amounts.

**(a)** The owner brings her own van, worth 7,000, into the business.

**(b)** The business repays 2,000 of a bank loan by bank transfer.

**(c)** A customer who owes 800 becomes bankrupt and the debt is written off.

**(d)** Inventory of 1,500 is bought, half paid by bank transfer and half on credit. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** An amount owed by the business to an outsider [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **A** [1]. 85,000 - (6,200 + 20,000) = 58,800. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** (a) Asset [1]; (b) liability [1]; (c) asset [1]; (d) liability [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**5.** 46,300 - 31,850 = **14,450** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**6.** 27,600 - 24,000 - 1,500 + 7,200 [1] = **9,300** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7. (a)** One mark for each transaction with all three effects correct [6]:

(i) assets +900; liabilities +900; equity no effect.

(ii) assets -120; liabilities no effect; equity -120.

(iii) no effect on any total.

(iv) assets -200; liabilities no effect; equity -200.

(v) assets +150; liabilities no effect; equity +150.

(vi) assets -500; liabilities -500; equity no effect.

**(b)** Bank rises by 340 and trade receivables fall by 340 [1], so one asset replaces another and the totals do not change [1]. Total: 8 marks.

**8. (a)** One mark for each correct row [5]:

| After | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20,000 | 6,000 | 14,000 |
| 2 | 20,900 | 6,000 | 14,900 |
| 3 | 20,300 | 6,000 | 14,300 |
| 4 | 23,300 | 9,000 | 14,300 |
| 5 | 20,800 | 6,500 | 14,300 |

**(b)** Profit = (2,400 - 1,500) - 600 = **300** [1]. 14,000 + 300 = 14,300, the closing owner's equity [1]. Total: 7 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In transaction 2 assets rise by 900, not 2,400, because inventory of 1,500 leaves the business.

**9. (a)** Non-current assets 32,000 + 14,500 = **46,500** [1]. Current assets 7,800 + 5,600 + 180 = **13,580** [1]. Current liabilities 2,350 + 6,900 + 450 = **9,700** [1]. Non-current liabilities **12,000** [1].

**(b)** 46,500 + 13,580 - 9,700 - 12,000 [1] = **38,380** [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**10. (a)** Opening 51,200 - 13,700 = **37,500** [1]. Closing 58,050 - 16,400 = **41,650** [1].

**(b)** Drawings 9,800 + 650 = 10,450 [1]. Profit = 41,650 - 37,500 - 6,000 + 10,450 [1] = **8,600** [1].

**(c)** Drawings reduced owner's equity during the year [1], but they are not an expense of earning profit, so adding them back leaves only the change caused by trading [1]. Total: 7 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The van is capital introduced even though no cash was paid in; treat it exactly like cash capital.

**11. (a)** Any two [2]: the bank overdraft is a liability, not an asset; the private flat is not a business asset and should be left out.

**(b)** Assets 9,000 + 4,300 = 13,300 [1]. Owner's equity 13,300 - (3,100 + 1,600) = **8,600** [1].

**(c)** Business entity [1]: the business is treated as separate from its owner, so only the business's own assets and liabilities are recorded [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**12. (a)** Assets +7,000; owner's equity +7,000; liabilities no effect [2].

**(b)** Assets -2,000; liabilities -2,000; owner's equity no effect [2].

**(c)** Assets -800 (trade receivables); owner's equity -800, because the loss is an expense; liabilities no effect [2].

**(d)** Inventory +1,500 and bank -750, so assets +750; liabilities +750; owner's equity no effect [2]. Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In (d), net the two asset changes before stating the total effect.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting a bank overdraft as an asset.
- Adding the full selling price of goods to assets and equity instead of the profit.
- Forgetting goods taken for private use when totalling drawings.
- Not subtracting capital introduced when finding profit from two equity figures.
- Giving the direction of a change but not the amount when the question asks for amounts.
