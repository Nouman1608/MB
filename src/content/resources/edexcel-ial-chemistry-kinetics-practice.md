This set covers Topic 11: Kinetics of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry specification (YCH11), Issue 1, September 2017, outcomes 11.1 to 11.13, including Core Practicals 9a, 9b and 10. Topic 11 belongs to Unit 4: Rates, Equilibria and Further Organic Chemistry, an International A2 (IA2) unit. Work through the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-kinetics/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-kinetics-revision-notes/) before you start; the [checklist](/checklists/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) and [course hub](/boards/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) list other topics.

Use R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ wherever needed. The Arrhenius equation is k = Ae^(−Ea/RT).

## Questions

**1.** Define each term.

**(a)** Order of reaction with respect to a substance **[1]**
**(b)** Rate constant **[1]**
**(c)** Half-life **[1]**

**2.** For a reaction, rate = k[C][D]².

**(a)** State the overall order. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the units of k. **[1]**
**(c)** At one temperature, k = 160 in these units. Calculate the rate when [C] = 0.025 mol dm⁻³ and [D] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(d)** [C] is multiplied by 1.5 and [D] is doubled. State the factor by which the rate changes. **[1]**

**3.** Methyl methanoate is hydrolysed in aqueous acid: HCOOCH₃ + H₂O → HCOOH + CH₃OH. Water is the solvent. Use these data.

| Experiment | [HCOOCH₃] / mol dm⁻³ | [H⁺] / mol dm⁻³ | Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.40 | 0.10 | 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.80 | 0.10 | 4.4 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 3 | 0.80 | 0.25 | 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 4 | 0.20 | 0.30 | to find |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to HCOOCH₃ and to H⁺, explaining your reasoning. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the rate equation for the hydrolysis. **[1]**
**(c)** Use experiment 1 to find the value and units of k. **[2]**
**(d)** Predict the initial rate for experiment 4. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest why the order with respect to water cannot be found from these experiments. **[1]**

**4.** Hydrogen peroxide decomposes in the presence of a catalyst: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Use these data.

| t / min | 0 | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [H₂O₂] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.800 | 0.566 | 0.400 | 0.283 | 0.200 | 0.100 |

**(a)** Use two half-lives to show that the reaction is first order in H₂O₂. **[2]**
**(b)** A tangent at t = 6 min gives a rate of 0.046 mol dm⁻³ min⁻¹. Calculate k, with units. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a technique for collecting these data continuously. **[1]**

**5.** Initial rates were measured at three concentrations of reactant K.

| [K] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.35 × 10⁻³ |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to K and describe the shape of the rate-concentration graph. **[2]**
**(b)** Another reactant, L, is zero order. Describe its concentration-time graph and how its half-life changes as the reaction proceeds. **[2]**

**6.** Select and justify a technique to obtain rate data for each reaction.

**(a)** MgCO₃(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Mg(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) **[2]**
**(b)** 2MnO₄⁻(aq) + 5C₂O₄²⁻(aq) + 16H⁺(aq) → 2Mn²⁺(aq) + 10CO₂(g) + 8H₂O(l), with excess ethanedioate **[2]**
**(c)** CH₃COOC₂H₅(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → CH₃COO⁻(aq) + C₂H₅OH(aq) **[2]**

**7.** In Core Practical 9a, propanone and sulfuric acid are in large excess. 5.00 cm³ samples are run into sodium hydrogencarbonate solution and titrated with 0.00500 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate. I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻.

| Time / min | 2 | 6 | 10 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titre / cm³ | 21.60 | 19.20 | 16.80 | 14.40 |

**(a)** Explain why the samples are run into sodium hydrogencarbonate. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the titres to deduce the order with respect to iodine. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the rate of reaction of iodine in mol dm⁻³ min⁻¹. **[3]**
**(d)** Rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺]. State what this shows about the rate-determining step and explain why H⁺ is described as a catalyst. **[2]**

**8.** Two halogenoalkanes are hydrolysed by aqueous hydroxide ions. For 2-chloro-2-methylbutane, rate = k[C₅H₁₁Cl]. For 1-bromopropane, rate = k[C₃H₇Br][OH⁻].

**(a)** Name the mechanism for each and explain how each rate equation supports it. **[4]**
**(b)** State the effect on each rate of doubling [OH⁻]. **[1]**

**9.** Hydrogen reacts with iodine monochloride: H₂(g) + 2ICl(g) → I₂(g) + 2HCl(g). Rate = k[H₂][ICl].

**(a)** State which species, and how many of each, are involved up to and including the rate-determining step. **[1]**
**(b)** Propose a two-step mechanism consistent with the rate equation and the overall equation, labelling the slow step. **[3]**

**10.** In Core Practical 10, a student times a clock reaction at four temperatures, using the same mixture each time.

| Temperature / °C | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t / s | 96 | 51 | 28 | 16 |

**(a)** Explain why 1/t can be used in place of k. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate 1/T (in K⁻¹) and ln(1/t) for each run. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the gradient of a graph of ln(1/t) against 1/T to calculate Ea in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest one way to keep the temperature of each run steady. **[1]**

**11.** This question is about catalysts.

**(a)** Classify each as homogeneous or heterogeneous: H⁺(aq) in the iodination of propanone; solid iron in the reaction of nitrogen and hydrogen gases. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a solid catalyst increases the rate of a gas-phase reaction. **[3]**

**12.** In a Harcourt-Esson clock, a student varies the volume of potassium iodide solution, adding water so the total volume is 50 cm³ every time.

| Volume of KI / cm³ | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t / s | 75 | 50 | 38 | 30 |

**(a)** Explain why water is added. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the order with respect to iodide ions. **[2]**
**(c)** State the role of the sodium thiosulfate in the mixture. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The exponent on that substance's concentration term in the rate equation [1].
**(b)** The k linking rate to the concentration terms, constant at a fixed temperature [1].
**(c)** The time for a reactant's concentration to drop to 50% of a chosen value [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link order to the rate equation, not to the balanced equation.

**2. (a)** 3 [1]
**(b)** mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹ [1]
**(c)** Rate = 160 × 0.025 × 0.040² [1] = **6.4 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** 1.5 × 2² = **6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Square the factor for [D] before multiplying; adding factors is a common slip.

**3. (a)** Experiments 1 and 2: [ester] doubles, rate doubles, so first order in ester [1]; experiments 2 and 3: [H⁺] × 2.5, rate × 2.5, so first order in H⁺ [1].
**(b)** Rate = k[HCOOCH₃][H⁺] [1]
**(c)** k = 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.40 × 0.10) = 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ [1]; **mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.20 × 0.30 = **3.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(e)** Water is the solvent, in such large excess that its concentration stays effectively constant [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the experiments you compared.

**4. (a)** 0.800 → 0.400 takes 6 min [1]; 0.400 → 0.200 takes another 6 min (or 0.200 → 0.100 takes 6 min), so t½ is constant and the reaction is first order [1].
**(b)** k = rate ÷ [H₂O₂] = 0.046 ÷ 0.400 [1] = **0.115 min⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Measure the volume of oxygen given off with a gas syringe [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show two separate half-lives; one alone cannot show they are constant.

**5. (a)** [K] doubles, rate × 4; [K] triples, rate × 9, so **second order** [1]; the graph is an upward curve through the origin [1].
**(b)** A straight line with a negative gradient [1]; the half-life gets shorter as [L] falls [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the numerical factor for each change.

**6. (a)** Mass change on a balance [1]; CO₂ is dense, so the mass lost is large enough to measure [1].
**(b)** Colorimetry [1]; purple MnO₄⁻ is the only strongly coloured species, so absorbance falls as it reacts [1].
**(c)** Conductivity [1]; mobile OH⁻ ions are replaced by less conductive ethanoate ions, so conductivity falls [1]. (Titrating quenched samples with standard acid is also acceptable.)
*Examiner insight:* Each justification needs the property that changes.

**7. (a)** It neutralises the acid catalyst, stopping the reaction at that time [1].
**(b)** The titre falls by 2.40 cm³ in each 4 min interval [1]; the graph is a straight line, so the rate is constant and independent of [I₂] [1].
**(c)** Gradient = 2.40 ÷ 4 = 0.600 cm³ min⁻¹ [1]; moles of I₂ per minute = 0.600 × 10⁻³ × 0.00500 ÷ 2 = 1.50 × 10⁻⁶ mol min⁻¹ [1]; rate = 1.50 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 0.00500 dm³ = **3.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** The rate-determining step involves one propanone and one H⁺; iodine reacts after it [1]. H⁺ is used in one step and regenerated later [1].
*Examiner insight:* Remember the 1 : 2 ratio between iodine and thiosulfate, and divide by the sample volume in dm³.

**8. (a)** Tertiary chloride: **SN1** [1]; only the halogenoalkane is in the slow step, forming a carbocation [1]. 1-bromopropane: **SN2** [1]; both the halogenoalkane and OH⁻ are in the single step, via one transition state [1].
**(b)** Tertiary unchanged; primary doubles [1].
*Examiner insight:* Tie each mechanism to the species in its rate equation.

**9. (a)** One H₂ and one ICl [1].
**(b)** Step 1 (slow): H₂ + ICl → HI + HCl [1][1]; step 2 (fast): HI + ICl → I₂ + HCl [1].
*Examiner insight:* Add your steps to check they give the overall equation; other mechanisms that fit both equations are equally valid.

**10. (a)** The same amount of reaction happens in each run, so rate ∝ 1/t, and k ∝ rate at fixed concentrations [1].
**(b)** 1/T: 3.356, 3.247, 3.145, 3.049 (× 10⁻³ K⁻¹) [1]; ln(1/t): −4.56, −3.93, −3.33, −2.77 [1].
**(c)** Gradient = (−2.77 − (−4.56)) ÷ ((3.049 − 3.356) × 10⁻³) [1] = −5830 K [1]; Ea = 5830 × 8.31 = 48 400 J mol⁻¹ ≈ **48 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** Warm the solutions separately in a thermostatic water bath before mixing [1].
*Examiner insight:* Take the gradient from points far apart on your best-fit line, and convert J to kJ at the end.

**11. (a)** H⁺(aq): homogeneous [1]; iron: heterogeneous [1].
**(b)** Reactant gases bond weakly (adsorb) at active sites on the solid [1]; bonds weaken and molecules are held close together, lowering the activation energy [1]; products desorb, freeing the sites [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the words adsorb and desorb.

**12. (a)** To keep the total volume constant, so [I⁻] is proportional to the volume of KI used [1].
**(b)** 1000/t ÷ volume ≈ 1.67, 1.67, 1.64, 1.67: rate is proportional to [I⁻] [1]; **first order** in iodide [1].
**(c)** It removes the iodine formed, delaying the blue-black colour until a fixed amount of reaction has occurred [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use all four runs, not just one pair.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading orders from the balanced equation.
- Leaving out the units of k, or using units for the wrong overall order.
- Forgetting the 1 : 2 iodine to thiosulfate ratio in Core Practical 9a.
- Using °C instead of K when calculating 1/T.
- Missing the negative sign of the gradient, or quoting J mol⁻¹ as kJ mol⁻¹.
- Proposing mechanism steps that do not add up to the overall equation.
