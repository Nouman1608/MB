## Section A

**1.** State **two** characteristics of Near Field Communication (NFC). **[2]**

**2.** Describe why infrared is not suitable for connecting devices in different rooms. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why microwave links between two towers need a clear line of sight. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how inductive charging transfers power to a phone. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by war driving. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A 20 MB video is sent to a friend either by Bluetooth at 3 megabits per second or by Wi-Fi at 100 megabits per second.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken by each method, in seconds, to one decimal place. **[4]**

**(b)** State **one** reason why Bluetooth is still used for sending small items. **[1]**

**7.** A household network is secured using WEP.

**(a)** Explain why WEP is unsuitable. **[2]**

**(b)** State the security protocol the household should use, and **two** other measures to protect the network. **[3]**

**8.** A 10-character Wi-Fi password uses lower-case letters and digits (36 possible symbols for each character). An attacker can try 1,000,000,000 passwords per second.

**(a)** Calculate the number of possible passwords. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of days needed to try them all, to the nearest day. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why a 6-character password from the same symbols is much weaker. **[2]**

**9.** Compare Wi-Fi with Bluetooth. **[4]**

**10.** A farm uses hundreds of small battery-powered sensors across its fields to report soil moisture. Evaluate the use of wireless technology for this purpose and recommend a suitable method. **[6]**

**11.** A coffee shop offers free Wi-Fi to customers. Discuss the security risks to customers and suggest how the risks can be reduced. **[6]**

**12.** Explain **three** advantages and **one** disadvantage of wireless power transfer. **[4]**

**13.** Describe how NFC is used to make a contactless payment and explain why the very short range is a security benefit. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: very short range of a few centimetres [1]; uses magnetic induction [1]; very low data rate [1]; passive tags need no battery [1]; used for contactless payment and tickets [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Infrared needs a clear line of sight between the sender and the receiver [1] and the signal cannot pass through walls, so it is blocked between rooms [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Microwaves travel in straight lines [1], so hills, buildings or the curve of the Earth between the towers would block the signal [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A coil in the charging pad produces a changing magnetic field [1], which induces a current in a coil in the phone that charges the battery [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Travelling through an area [1] searching for wireless networks, especially open or poorly protected ones, in order to use or attack them [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 20 × 8 = 160 megabits [1]. Bluetooth: 160 ÷ 3 = 53.3 seconds [1][1]. Wi-Fi: 160 ÷ 100 = 1.6 seconds [1].

**(b)** Any one: low power; simple pairing; no network needed; short range makes it hard to intercept [1].

**7. (a)** WEP has serious weaknesses [1] and can be broken in minutes by freely available tools, so the traffic can be read [1].

**(b)** WPA2 or WPA3 [1]. Any two others: long random passphrase [1]; change the default router password [1]; keep firmware updated [1]; turn off WPS [1].

**8. (a)** 36¹⁰ [1] = 3,656,158,440,062,976 passwords [1].

**(b)** Seconds = 3,656,158,440,062,976 ÷ 1,000,000,000 = about 3,656,158 [1]. Days = 3,656,158 ÷ 86,400 [1] = 42 days [1].

**(c)** 36⁶ = 2,176,782,336 [1], which takes only about 2 seconds to try at the same speed [1].

**9.** Wi-Fi has a range of tens of metres, but Bluetooth has about 10 metres [1]. Wi-Fi has a much higher speed, so it suits large files and streaming, whereas Bluetooth is slower [1]. Bluetooth uses less power, so it suits headphones and small devices, but Wi-Fi uses more [1]. Wi-Fi connects devices to a network and the internet through an access point, whereas Bluetooth connects devices directly in pairs [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Advantages: no cables are needed across fields, which saves cost and avoids damage by machinery [1]; sensors can be placed anywhere and moved [1]; low power methods allow batteries to last for long periods [1]. Disadvantages: range and signal are limited by distance, crops and weather [1]; batteries must be replaced [1]; signals could be intercepted if not encrypted [1]. Recommendation: a low power radio method, or a mesh of short-range links back to a gateway, because Wi-Fi uses too much power for battery sensors and Bluetooth has too short a range [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**11.** Risks: data sent over an open network can be intercepted [1]; a criminal can set up a fake hotspot with a similar name and capture details [1]; other users on the same network may attack devices [1]; malware can be downloaded from fake sites [1]. Reduction: the shop can use WPA3 or WPA2 with a password given to customers [1]; customers can use a VPN [1], visit only HTTPS sites [1] and turn off file sharing and auto-connect [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**12.** Any three advantages: no cable or connector to wear out [1]; devices can be sealed against water and dust [1]; convenient, as devices charge when placed on a pad [1]; no risk of connecting the wrong cable [1]. One disadvantage: some energy is lost as heat, so charging is slower and less efficient [1]; or, the device must be placed correctly [1]. **[4]**

**13.** The customer holds the phone or card close to the terminal [1]. The terminal's field induces a small current in the device, which then sends the payment details wirelessly [1]. The range is only a few centimetres, so an attacker would need to be extremely close [1], which makes it much harder to intercept the data than with Wi-Fi [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that a method is "fast" or "secure" without comparison or a reason.
- Not converting megabytes to megabits in transfer calculations.
- Recommending WEP or relying on hidden network names and MAC filtering.
- Mixing up the range of Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
- Giving security measures without saying which threat each prevents.
