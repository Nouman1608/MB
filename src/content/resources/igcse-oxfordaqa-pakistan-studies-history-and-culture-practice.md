## Section A

**1.** State the year in which the All India Muslim League was founded, and the year of the Lahore Resolution. **[2]**

**2.** Who coined the name "Pakistan," and in what year? **[2]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain why the events of 1857 were significant for Muslims in the subcontinent. **[7]**

**4.** Explain the importance of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's work for the Muslim community. **[8]**

**5.** "Muhammad Ali Jinnah always wanted a separate Muslim state." How far do you agree? **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Muslim League: **1906** [1]. Lahore Resolution: **1940** [1].

**2.** **Chaudhry Rahmat Ali**, in **1933** [1] [1].

**3.** After the failure of the 1857 uprising, the East India Company was replaced by **direct Crown rule** [2], the Mughal emperor was **deposed**, formally ending Mughal authority [2], and the British held Muslims **disproportionately responsible**, excluding them from government service and education in the following decades [3] — a marginalisation that shaped the community's political trajectory for the rest of the century.

**4.** Sir Syed concluded that Muslim recovery required **reconciliation with the British and modern education** [2]; he founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh** (1875), teaching modern subjects alongside Islamic values [2]; his writings (*The Causes of the Indian Revolt*, *Loyal Muhammadans of India*) worked to rebuild British trust in Muslims [2]; and his **Two Nation Theory** — that Hindus and Muslims were distinct nations — became the intellectual foundation later Muslim League leaders built upon [2].

**5.** A strong answer **disagrees** with the statement, or at least significantly qualifies it [2]: Jinnah began his career as an advocate of **Hindu-Muslim unity**, working within the Indian National Congress and being described as an "ambassador of unity" [3]; his position shifted specifically after Congress provincial rule (1937-39) and the failure of constitutional guarantees for Muslims convinced him unity was not achievable [3]; the demand for a separate state therefore emerged from a **changing political context**, not a fixed lifelong goal [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling 1857 a "mutiny" without noting this is the British characterisation of events.
- Attributing the name "Pakistan" to Iqbal instead of Rahmat Ali.
- Treating Jinnah's demand for partition as a constant position rather than a response to changing circumstances.
- Listing events chronologically without explaining the causal link between them, when "explain why" is the actual demand of the question.
