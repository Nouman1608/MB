## Questions

**1.** Explain how pollen from a peat bog and rings in a tree trunk can each provide evidence of past climate. **[4]**

**2.** A botanist records the mean spring temperature and the day of the year of first flowering of a plant in five periods.

| Mean spring temperature (°C) | 6.1 | 6.8 | 7.4 | 8.0 | 8.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First flowering (day of year) | 118 | 112 | 109 | 101 | 98 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown. **[2]**
**(b)** The correlation coefficient is −0.99. Explain why this does not on its own prove that temperature causes the change. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how greenhouse gases cause the greenhouse effect. **[4]**

**4.** Using your knowledge of the carbon cycle, explain two ways of reducing the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[4]**

**5.** The atmospheric carbon dioxide concentration was 338 ppm in 1980 and 370 ppm in 2000 (data supplied).

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of increase per year. **[1]**
**(b)** Extrapolate to predict the concentration in 2030. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why climate models limit the reliability of such a prediction. **[2]**

**6.** Oxygen uptake by a lizard was measured (data supplied).

| Temperature (°C) | 14 | 18 | 26 | 28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen uptake (cm³ per g per hour) | 1.8 | 0.32 | 4.5 | 0.72 |

The values at 18 °C and 28 °C come from one experiment. The values at 14 °C and 26 °C come from another.

**(a)** Calculate Q₁₀ from the 18 °C and 28 °C values. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Q₁₀ from the 14 °C and 26 °C values, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the rate would fall if the lizard's body temperature reached 45 °C. **[2]**

**7.** A student investigated brine shrimp hatching. Fifty eggs were placed in each of three tubes at each temperature. The numbers hatched after 24 hours were:

| Temperature (°C) | 15 | 25 | 35 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tube 1 | 8 | 26 | 37 | 5 |
| Tube 2 | 10 | 28 | 38 | 7 |
| Tube 3 | 9 | 27 | 39 | 6 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number hatched at each temperature, and the percentage hatched at 15 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe and explain the trend. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate Q₁₀ for 15 °C to 25 °C using the means. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one control variable and one ethical precaution. **[2]**

**8.** In a beetle population, a pesticide-resistance allele was present as 36 copies among 600 alleles in generation 1 and 117 copies among 650 alleles in generation 8.

**(a)** Calculate the allele frequency in each generation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how mutation and natural selection could account for the change. **[4]**

**9.** A species of snail lived on both sides of a valley. A river then changed course and divided the population.

**(a)** Explain how this could lead to the formation of two species. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe how speciation could occur without a geographical barrier. **[2]**

**10.** A report concluded that human activity has little effect on climate. It was paid for by a coal company. Suggest two reasons for caution and two checks you would make. **[4]**

**11.** A reforestation scheme plants 250 ha. The trees store 8.0 tonnes of carbon per hectare per year (data supplied).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide that this represents each year, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate reforestation and biofuel crops as ways to balance human needs and conservation. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Pollen: peat is waterlogged and acidic, so pollen is preserved in layers [1]; the species present at each depth show the plants, and so the climate, at that time [1]. Tree rings: one ring is added each year [1]; wide rings indicate favourable (warm, wet) years and narrow rings poor years [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs a link from the record to the climate, so state what the pollen or ring shows and what it means.

**2. (a)** As temperature rises, the day of first flowering is earlier [1], from day 118 at 6.1 °C to day 98 at 8.6 °C, a negative correlation [1]
**(b)** A correlation only shows the two variables rise and fall together, which does not establish cause [1]; another factor (such as rainfall or day length) could change over the same periods, and a mechanism is needed [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table in (a), and name a possible confounding factor in (b); "it is not proven" alone earns little.

**3.** Incoming sunlight, mostly short-wavelength, reaches the surface and warms it [1]; the warmed surface emits longer-wavelength infrared [1]; greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane absorb it [1] and re-emit some towards the surface, so less heat escapes to space and the surface warms [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of radiation at each stage; "traps heat" is too vague to show the mechanism.

**4.** Reforestation (or protecting forests): photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide, which is stored as organic carbon in wood [1], so less remains in the atmosphere [1]. Burning less fossil fuel (or capturing carbon dioxide from power stations): fossil fuels release carbon that was stored for millions of years [1], so avoiding combustion cuts input to the atmosphere [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each method needs its cycle process, such as photosynthesis or combustion, to earn the explaining mark.

**5. (a)** (370 − 338) ÷ 20 = **1.6 ppm per year** [1]
**(b)** 370 + 1.6 × 30 [1] = **418 ppm** [1]
**(c)** Models depend on assumptions about future emissions [1]; many interacting variables and feedback loops, so the rate may change [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working in (b) and check the number of years from the last measured date, so the method is visible.

**6. (a)** The interval is 10 °C, so Q₁₀ = 0.72 ÷ 0.32 [1] = **2.25 (about 2.3)** [1]
**(b)** The interval is 12 °C: (4.5 ÷ 1.8)^(10/12) [1] = **2.1** [1]
**(c)** High temperature breaks hydrogen bonds, changing the tertiary structure and active site [1]; enzymes denature, so substrates no longer fit and the rate falls [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Q₁₀ is a ratio with no units; using 10 ÷ 12 as the power is the usual step to show for a non-10 °C interval.

**7. (a)** Means 9, 27, 38 and 6 [1]; at 15 °C, 9 ÷ 50 × 100 = **18%** [1]
**(b)** Hatching rises from 15 to 35 °C [1] because greater kinetic energy gives more enzyme-substrate collisions [1]; at 40 °C it falls because enzymes denature [1]
**(c)** 27 ÷ 9 = **3.0** [1]
**(d)** Control: egg number, salinity, volume, light, or time [1]; ethical: use the minimum number of eggs, and dispose of nauplii humanely [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A control variable must be something you keep the same; naming "temperature" does not gain credit.

**8. (a)** 36 ÷ 600 = **0.06** [1]; 117 ÷ 650 = **0.18** [1]
**(b)** A random mutation produced the resistance allele [1]; the pesticide is a selection pressure, so resistant beetles survive [1]; they reproduce more and pass on the allele [1]; so its frequency rises across generations [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the mutation occurred before the pesticide; marks are lost for implying beetles mutate to cope.

**9. (a)** The river is a geographical barrier that stops gene flow [1]; the two populations experience different mutations and selection pressures [1]; allele frequencies diverge [1]; eventually they cannot interbreed to produce fertile offspring, so they are separate species [1]
**(b)** Sympatric speciation: reproductive isolation within the same area [1], for example flowering at different times, a new food source, or polyploidy in plants [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The end point matters: a good answer states that interbreeding no longer gives fertile offspring.

**10.** Caution: the funder has a financial interest, so data may be selected [1]; values and interests can shape the conclusion [1]. Checks: whether the report was peer reviewed and results replicated [1]; the data, sample size and period, and whether the conclusion goes beyond them [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Point to who gains from the result; do not say it must be wrong.

**11. (a)** 250 × 8.0 = 2,000 tonnes of carbon [1]; × 44 ÷ 12 [1]; **7,330 tonnes of carbon dioxide per year** [1]
**(b)** Reforestation restores habitat, stores carbon and reduces erosion [1], but young trees store little at first and monoculture supports less biodiversity [1]. Biofuel crops give fuel that is nearly carbon neutral [1], but they compete with food or forest, and fertiliser and machinery use energy [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer needs benefit and drawback for each option.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that a correlation proves a cause, or dismissing it without a mechanism.
- Saying "traps heat" without absorption and re-emission of infrared.
- Forgetting the exponent when the temperature interval is not 10 °C.
- Giving a unit with Q₁₀.
- Naming the independent variable as a control variable.
- Missing ethical precautions in Core Practical 12 answers.
- Saying mutations happen because an organism needs them.
- Describing speciation without isolation and without loss of fertile interbreeding.
- Calling biofuels carbon neutral with no limitation.
