## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by genetic variation, and give two sources of it. **[3]**

**2.** Distinguish between natural and artificial classification, and give an example of an artificial grouping. **[3]**

**3.** A researcher released 80 pale and 120 dark ground beetles onto dark volcanic soil. One year later, 10 pale and 90 dark beetles from the release were still alive.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each colour that survived. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain these results using natural selection. **[3]**

**4.** A student writes: "The lizards grew longer legs to run away from snakes, and passed longer legs on to their young." Explain two errors in this statement, then rewrite it correctly. **[4]**

**5.** A gene of 250 bases was sequenced in three species. Species J and K differ in 5 bases, J and L differ in 30 bases, and K and L differ in 28 bases.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference for J and K, and for J and L. **[2]**
**(b)** State which two species share the most recent common ancestor. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how DNA sequencing can lead to a different classification from one based on appearance. **[2]**

**6.** A culture contains 4.0 × 10⁶ bacteria. One in every 2.0 × 10⁶ carries an allele for resistance to an antibiotic. The antibiotic is added and kills every non-resistant cell. Resistant cells divide every 30 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the number of resistant bacteria at the start. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of resistant bacteria after 4 hours, assuming none die. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a second dose of the same antibiotic is much less effective. **[2]**

**7.** A bacterial sample from a patient formed a clear zone of 24 mm diameter around a disc of an antibiotic. A sample taken after a long treatment course formed a zone of 12 mm diameter around the same type of disc.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in zone diameter. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the change suggests about the bacteria. **[2]**

**8.** The mean limb length of an extinct mammal was measured from fossils in three rock layers: 14 cm (oldest layer), 19 cm (middle layer) and 25 cm (youngest layer).

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean limb length from the oldest to the youngest layer, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what the data suggest about the species. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why the fossil record is incomplete. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Describe the contribution of Alfred Russel Wallace to the theory of evolution by natural selection. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which the theory has affected modern biology. **[2]**

**10.** A seedbank stores 2000 seeds of a rare wild plant. A test shows that 85% will germinate.

**(a)** Calculate how many of the stored seeds are expected to germinate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how seedbanks help to conserve biodiversity. **[2]**

**11.** A species of mouse lives on one landmass. Rising sea levels split it into two populations on separate islands with different climates. Explain how two new species could form. **[6]**

**12.** In a hospital, 2% of samples of a bacterium were resistant to an antibiotic in year 1. In year 8, 9% were resistant.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in the proportion of resistant samples. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how heavy use of the antibiotic could cause this increase. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to slow the rise. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Differences between individuals of a species caused by different alleles [1]. Any two sources: mutation, meiosis, random fertilisation [1][1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Differences" alone is too vague. Mention alleles or genes, or an answer could describe environmental variation.

**2.** Natural classification uses many features and shows how organisms are related by ancestry [1]. Artificial classification uses a few chosen, convenient features [1]. Example: grouping animals by whether they can fly, or by living in water [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare example with no statement of what the basis is does not show you know the difference, so state the basis for each type.

**3. (a)** Pale: 10 / 80 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Dark: 90 / 120 × 100 = **75.0%** [1]
**(b)** Dark beetles were better camouflaged on dark soil, so fewer were eaten by predators [1]. They were more likely to survive and reproduce, passing on the allele for dark colour [1]. Over many generations the allele would become more common in the population [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentages are worked out from the number released, not from the survivors. In the explanation, "survive" needs "and reproduce" and the allele being passed on to complete the chain.

**4.** Error 1: individuals do not change in order to survive or grow features because they need them [1]. Variants with different leg lengths already existed because of random mutation [1]. Error 2: it describes one generation of individuals instead of a change in a population over time [1]. Correct version: lizards with alleles for longer legs escaped snakes more often, survived and reproduced, so the allele became more common over generations [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A rewrite that still says the lizards "needed" or "wanted" longer legs repeats the misconception, even if other words are corrected.

**5. (a)** J and K: 5 / 250 × 100 = **2.0%** [1]. J and L: 30 / 250 × 100 = **12.0%** [1]
**(b)** **J and K** [1]
**(c)** Organisms can look alike because similar environments select similar features, although they are not closely related [1]. DNA sequences show the number of differences, which reflects relatedness, so the groups can be changed [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), give the pair and, where a reason is wanted, link fewer differences to a more recent common ancestor.

**6. (a)** 4.0 × 10⁶ / 2.0 × 10⁶ = **2** resistant bacteria [1]
**(b)** 4 hours = 8 divisions of 30 minutes [1]. 2 × 2⁸ = 2 × 256 = **512** [1]
**(c)** The population now consists mostly of resistant bacteria descended from the survivors [1]. The resistance allele has been passed on, so the antibiotic no longer kills most of them [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the number of divisions before the power of 2. A bare "512" is easier to credit when the working is visible.

**7. (a)** (12 - 24) / 24 × 100 = **-50%**, a decrease of 50% [1][1]
**(b)** The bacteria have become less susceptible, so more are resistant [1]. Resistant bacteria survived the treatment, reproduced and passed on the allele [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change is taken against the original value. Say the antibiotic selected resistant bacteria; saying it "made" them resistant loses the point.

**8. (a)** (25 - 14) / 14 × 100 [1] = **78.6%** [1]
**(b)** Limb length increased through the layers [1], so characteristics of the species changed over time, which supports evolution [1]
**(c)** Any two: soft-bodied organisms decay and leave no fossil; most organisms do not die where fossils form; fossils destroyed by rock movement; many fossils not yet found [1][1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For "describe the data", quote figures. For the incomplete record, two different reasons are needed, not one reason said twice.

**9. (a)** Wallace independently developed the idea of evolution by natural selection [1]. His work, from collecting specimens in south-east Asia, was presented together with Darwin's in 1858 [1]
**(b)** Any one explained: it explains how organisms are related, which underpins classification; it explains how antibiotic resistance arises; it supports conservation of variation [1] with a link, such as seedbanks storing seeds to keep genetic variation [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" asks for the effect as well as the name of the impact, so give a consequence.

**10. (a)** 85 / 100 × 2000 [1] = **1700** seeds [1]
**(b)** Seedbanks store seeds of many species, kept cold and dry, so that a species can be regrown if it dies out in the wild [1]. Storing seeds from many plants keeps genetic variation that may be needed if the environment changes [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "To protect biodiversity" restates the question. Say what is stored and what it allows later.

**11.** Sea splits the mice, so the populations no longer interbreed [1]. The climates differ, so the selection pressures differ [1]. Mice with different alleles survive and reproduce on each island [1]. Different random mutations also occur in each population [1]. Over many generations the populations become increasingly different [1]. If they met, they could no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, so they are separate species [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A six-mark explanation gains credit by following a clear sequence. The end point, no fertile offspring, is the test for separate species.

**12. (a)** (9 - 2) / 2 × 100 [1] = **350%** [1]
**(b)** A random mutation produced a resistance allele in some bacteria [1]. The antibiotic killed non-resistant bacteria but resistant ones survived [1]. The resistant bacteria reproduced and passed on the allele [1]. The proportion of resistant bacteria rose over generations [1]
**(c)** Reduce antibiotic use, for example not prescribing for viral infections [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A rise from 2% to 9% is 7 percentage points but 350% as a percentage increase; answer what the question asks. State that the mutation was random and already present.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that individuals adapt or evolve rather than that a population changes across generations.
- Saying organisms change "in order to" or "because they need to".
- Missing "reproduce" and "pass on the allele" from natural selection answers.
- Describing artificial classification without naming its basis.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original when finding percentage change.
- Describing a trend without quoting figures from the data.
- Giving one reason for an incomplete fossil record twice in different words.
- Saying antibiotics cause resistance, rather than selecting for resistant bacteria.
- Writing "biodiversity" in a seedbank answer without saying what is stored.
