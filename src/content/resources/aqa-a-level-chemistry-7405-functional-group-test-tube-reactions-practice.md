These questions cover section **3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test-tube reactions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Functional group tests study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-functional-group-test-tube-reactions/) and the [Functional group tests revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-functional-group-test-tube-reactions-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0, Ag = 107.9 and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ at room temperature and pressure.

## Questions

**1.** Describe a test that shows a hydrocarbon contains a carbon–carbon double bond. State the observation. **[2]**

**2.** Propanal and propanone are both colourless liquids of formula C₃H₆O.

**(a)** Describe a test that distinguishes them and state what is seen with each. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of oxidation, why the two compounds behave differently. **[1]**

**3.** Samples of butan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol are each warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) solution. State the observation in each case and explain the difference. **[3]**

**4.** A student has two colourless liquids, ethanoic acid and ethanol.

**(a)** Describe a test to distinguish them and state the result for each. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction that occurs with sodium carbonate. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how the gas formed can be identified. **[1]**

**5.** A student wants to find out whether an organic liquid is a chloroalkane or a bromoalkane.

**(a)** Describe the three stages of the test. **[3]**

**(b)** State the observation for each halogenoalkane. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why dilute nitric acid is added before silver nitrate. **[1]**

**6.** Fehling's solution is warmed with an aldehyde.

**(a)** State the colour change. **[2]**

**(b)** State what happens to the oxidation number of copper. **[1]**

**7.** 1.50 g of ethanoic acid (Mᵣ = 60.0) is added to excess sodium carbonate solution.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of ethanoic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

**8.** 0.0150 mol of propanal reacts completely with excess Tollens' reagent. One mole of propanal produces two moles of silver. Calculate the mass of silver formed. **[2]**

**9.** Compound X has molecular formula C₃H₆O. It does not decolourise bromine water and it gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Identify X and explain your reasoning. **[3]**

**10.** Compound Y has molecular formula C₄H₁₀O. When warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) the solution remains orange. Identify Y and state what the observation shows. **[2]**

**11.** Four unlabelled liquids are pentane, pent-1-ene, pentanal and pentanoic acid. Describe a sequence of tests, with expected observations, that identifies each liquid. **[5]**

**12.** A student adds acidified potassium dichromate(VI) to an unknown liquid and sees a colour change to green. The student concludes that the liquid is an aldehyde. Evaluate this conclusion and suggest a further test. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Add bromine water and shake [1]; the orange colour is decolourised, giving a colourless solution [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Warm each with Tollens' reagent in a water bath [1]; propanal gives a silver mirror [1]; propanone gives no change [1]

**(b)** An aldehyde is easily oxidised to a carboxylic acid, but a ketone is not easily oxidised [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Fehling's solution is an alternative; the results are a red precipitate for the aldehyde and a solution that stays blue for the ketone.

**3.** Butan-2-ol: orange to green [1]; 2-methylpropan-2-ol: stays orange [1]; butan-2-ol is a secondary alcohol that can be oxidised to a ketone, but 2-methylpropan-2-ol is tertiary and not easily oxidised [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Add sodium carbonate (or hydrogencarbonate) solution to each [1]; ethanoic acid fizzes and ethanol does not [1]

**(b)** 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ [1]

**(c)** Bubble the gas through limewater, which turns milky [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** Warm the halogenoalkane with aqueous sodium hydroxide [1]; acidify with dilute nitric acid [1]; add aqueous silver nitrate [1]

**(b)** Chloroalkane: white precipitate [1]; bromoalkane: cream precipitate [1]

**(c)** To remove excess hydroxide ions, which would form a brown precipitate of silver oxide and mask the result [1]. **[6]**

**6. (a)** Blue [1] to a red precipitate [1]

**(b)** It decreases from +2 to +1 (reduction) [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** n = 1.50 ÷ 60.0 = **0.0250 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(CO₂) = 0.0250 ÷ 2 = 0.0125 mol [1]; V = 0.0125 × 24.0 = **0.300 dm³** (300 cm³) [1]. **[3]**

**8.** n(Ag) = 2 × 0.0150 = 0.0300 mol [1]; mass = 0.0300 × 107.9 = **3.24 g** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** X is **propanal**, CH₃CH₂CHO [1]; the silver mirror shows an aldehyde group [1]; no decolourisation of bromine water shows no C=C, ruling out prop-2-en-1-ol, and propanone would not give a silver mirror [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Y is **2-methylpropan-2-ol**, (CH₃)₃COH [1]; the solution staying orange shows that Y is a tertiary alcohol, which is not easily oxidised [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Several isomers of C₄H₁₀O, such as ethers, would also not turn dichromate green, but the question implies an alcohol. State the class first, then the example.

**11.** Add sodium carbonate solution to a sample of each: only pentanoic acid fizzes [1]. Add bromine water to the other three: only pent-1-ene decolourises it [1]. Warm the remaining two with Tollens' reagent in a water bath: pentanal gives a silver mirror [1]; pentane gives no change with any of the reagents [1]. Use separate samples for each test and warm in a water bath because the liquids are flammable [1]. **[5]**

**12.** The conclusion is not justified, because primary and secondary alcohols, as well as aldehydes, turn dichromate green [1]; a further test is to warm a fresh sample with Tollens' reagent (or Fehling's solution) [1]; a silver mirror (or red precipitate) would show an aldehyde, and no change would suggest an alcohol [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "clear" instead of "colourless".
- Failing to give a result for both substances being distinguished.
- Not naming the gas in the carbonate test or the confirming test for it.
- Using a naked flame to warm test-tube mixtures.
- Using hydrochloric acid in the halide test.
- Assuming dichromate on its own identifies an aldehyde.
