These notes condense subtopic **18.1 Biotechnology** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: yeast in bread and ethanol production, why bacteria are useful, fermenters and the conditions that are controlled, and enzymes in washing powders, fruit juice and lactose-free milk. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [18.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-biotechnology/). Then try the [18.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-biotechnology-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Biotechnology | Use of living organisms, or substances from them, to make useful products |
| Yeast | A single-celled fungus; ferments sugar by anaerobic respiration |
| Fermentation | glucose gives ethanol + carbon dioxide |
| Plasmid | A small ring of DNA in a bacterium, separate from the main chromosome |
| Fermenter | A large vessel for growing microorganisms to make a product |

**Equation:** C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2

## Uses of yeast

| Product | Use | Process |
|---|---|---|
| Bread | Carbon dioxide makes the dough rise | Yeast ferments sugar in the dough; baking sets the bubbles |
| Ethanol | Alcoholic drinks and fuel | Yeast ferments sugar with no oxygen; ethanol is collected |

## Why bacteria are useful

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Rapid reproduction | Binary fission can occur every 20 minutes, so a lot of product is made quickly |
| Make complex molecules | They can make proteins such as insulin |
| No ethical concerns | There is less objection to growing and manipulating bacteria than animals |
| Plasmids | Genes can be put into plasmids, which the bacteria copy |

## Fermenter conditions

| Condition | Why it is controlled |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Optimum for enzymes; remove the heat released |
| pH | Optimum for enzymes; add acid or alkali |
| Oxygen | Needed for aerobic respiration in many organisms; supplied by air bubbles |
| Nutrient supply | Sugars, amino acids and minerals for growth and the product |
| Waste products | Can be toxic or slow growth, so they are removed |

Other features: stirrer to mix the contents; sterile conditions to prevent contamination; sampling to check the product.

## Enzymes in use

| Enzyme | Where used | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Protease, lipase | Biological washing powder | Break down protein and fat stains; work at lower temperatures |
| Pectinase | Fruit juice production | Breaks down pectin in cell walls; more juice, clearer juice |
| Lactase | Lactose-free milk | Breaks down lactose to glucose and galactose |

## Comparing the uses

| Process | Organism or enzyme | Raw material | Useful product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread making | Yeast | Sugar in dough | Carbon dioxide |
| Ethanol production | Yeast | Sugar solution | Ethanol |
| Washing powder | Protease, lipase | Protein and fat stains | Soluble products washed away |
| Fruit juice | Pectinase | Pectin in crushed fruit | More, clearer juice |
| Lactose-free milk | Lactase | Lactose | Glucose and galactose |
| Large-scale production | Bacteria or fungi in a fermenter | Nutrient medium | Medicines, proteins, food |

## Benefits and limits of using microorganisms

- **Benefits:** quick growth, cheap nutrients, controlled conditions, products can be made all year round, and genetic modification lets bacteria make human proteins.
- **Limits:** the vessel must be kept sterile, the conditions must be controlled carefully, and waste products can poison the organisms. Very high concentrations of ethanol kill the yeast.

## Worked calculation

*1.0 kg of crushed pears gives 450 cm3 of juice without pectinase and 585 cm3 with pectinase. Find the percentage increase.*

1. Increase = 585 - 450 = 135 cm3.
2. Percentage = 135 divided by 450 x 100 = 30 per cent.

Divide by the volume without the enzyme.

## Yeast investigation

| Variable | How it is controlled |
|---|---|
| Independent | Temperature (use water baths at set temperatures) |
| Dependent | Number of bubbles of carbon dioxide per minute |
| Kept constant | Mass of yeast, volume and concentration of sugar solution, time |

Expected result: the rate rises to an optimum, then falls to zero when the enzymes are denatured.

## Short model answers

**Explain the role of yeast in bread making.** Yeast ferments sugar in the dough and produces carbon dioxide. The gas forms bubbles which make the dough rise.

**State why bacteria are useful in biotechnology.** They reproduce rapidly, make complex molecules and have plasmids; there are few ethical concerns about growing them.

**Explain why a fermenter is kept at a constant temperature.** Enzymes work best at an optimum temperature, so the temperature must be controlled to get the maximum rate of growth and product.

**State the use of pectinase.** It breaks down pectin in the cell walls of fruit, which increases the volume of juice and makes it clearer.

**State the use of lactase.** It breaks down lactose in milk to make lactose-free milk.

## Exam traps

- Bread rises because of **carbon dioxide**, not ethanol.
- Ethanol production needs **no oxygen** (anaerobic).
- The fermenter needs cooling as well as heating.
- Enzymes in washing powder are **denatured** in very hot water.
- Plasmids are **not** the main chromosome.
- "No ethical concerns" refers to the fact that bacteria are single-celled.
- Pectinase breaks down **cell walls**; it does not add liquid.
- Always say **why** a condition is controlled (enzymes, respiration, toxicity).

## Self-test

1. Define biotechnology.
2. What type of organism is yeast?
3. Write the word equation for fermentation by yeast.
4. Which product of fermentation makes bread rise?
5. Why does yeast ferment in anaerobic conditions?
6. Name two reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology.
7. What is a plasmid?
8. Name three conditions controlled in a fermenter.
9. Why must a fermenter be stirred?
10. What is the use of protease in a washing powder?
11. What does pectinase do in fruit juice production?
12. What does lactase break down?

### Answers

1. The use of living organisms, or substances from them, to make useful products.
2. A single-celled fungus.
3. Glucose gives ethanol + carbon dioxide.
4. Carbon dioxide.
5. With little or no oxygen, it releases energy by fermentation.
6. Any two of: rapid reproduction; they make complex molecules; no ethical concerns; they have plasmids.
7. A small ring of DNA in a bacterium, separate from the main chromosome.
8. Any three of: temperature; pH; oxygen; nutrient supply; waste products.
9. To mix the contents so nutrients, oxygen and temperature are even.
10. It breaks down protein stains.
11. It breaks down pectin in cell walls, giving more and clearer juice.
12. Lactose, the sugar in milk.
