These notes condense **3.3.2.2 Modification of alkanes by cracking** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [cracking study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-2/); then try the [cracking practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-2-practice/).

## The subtopic in four lines

1. Cracking breaks **C–C bonds** in long-chain alkanes, giving shorter alkanes and **alkenes**.
2. **Thermal**: high pressure, high temperature; high percentage of alkenes.
3. **Catalytic**: slight pressure, high temperature, **zeolite**; mainly motor fuels and aromatic hydrocarbons.
4. Economic reason: turn surplus, low-value heavy fractions into scarce, high-value light fractions and useful alkenes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Cracking | Breaking C–C bonds in alkanes to form smaller molecules |
| Thermal cracking | High temperature and high pressure, no catalyst |
| Catalytic cracking | High temperature, slight pressure, zeolite catalyst |
| Zeolite | Porous aluminosilicate catalyst with a large surface area |
| Alkene | Unsaturated hydrocarbon with a C=C bond; CₙH₂ₙ |
| Aromatic hydrocarbon | Hydrocarbon containing a benzene ring |
| Motor fuel | Fuel for vehicle engines, such as petrol |
| Feedstock | Starting material for making other chemicals |

## Conditions and products

| | Thermal | Catalytic |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | high | slight |
| Temperature | high | high |
| Catalyst | none | zeolite |
| Mainly gives | alkenes | motor fuels, aromatic hydrocarbons |

Mechanisms are not required for either.

## Equations

**Why an alkene must form:** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ splitting into two alkanes would need two extra H atoms, so at least one product is CₙH₂ₙ.

**Balancing method:** subtract C and H separately, then check general formulas.

| Equation | Check |
|---|---|
| C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + C₄H₈ | C 12; H 26 |
| C₁₄H₃₀ → C₈H₁₈ + 3C₂H₄ | C 8 + 6; H 18 + 12 |
| C₂₀H₄₂ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₃H₆ + C₆H₁₂ | C 8 + 6 + 6; H 18 + 12 + 12 |
| C₁₀H₂₂ → C₆H₁₄ + 2C₂H₄ | C 6 + 4; H 14 + 8 |

**Mass method:** moles of alkane cracked × mole ratio × Mr of product. The percentage of product mass in one product = (its mass in the equation ÷ total) × 100.

## The economic argument in five points

1. Fractional distillation gives fixed proportions of each fraction.
2. More long-chain fractions are produced than are needed.
3. Short-chain fractions (motor fuels) are in higher demand than supply.
4. Higher demand means a higher value; cracking converts low-value surplus into high-value products.
5. Alkenes are reactive and valuable for making polymers and other chemicals.

With data: quote the surplus fraction (supply > demand) and the shortage fraction (demand > supply) by number.

## Model sentences for data questions

Use the numbers in the table, in this order:

1. "Fraction X is in surplus: supply is a % but demand is only b %."
2. "Fraction Y is in short supply: supply is c % but demand is d %."
3. "Cracking breaks the long chains of X into shorter molecules that can be used as Y."
4. "Y is in higher demand, so it is more valuable; cracking also gives alkenes for polymers."

## Linked facts

- Alkenes from cracking decolourise bromine water (orange to colourless); alkanes do not.
- The zeolite's large surface area comes from its porous structure.
- Cracking products are often separated again by fractional distillation.

## Exam traps

- Cracking is chemical (bonds break); distillation is physical.
- Catalytic: **slight** pressure. Thermal: **no** catalyst.
- Thermal → alkenes; catalytic → motor fuels and aromatics. Do not swap them.
- Every cracking equation needs at least one alkene.
- Ethene is C₂H₄, propene C₃H₆, butene C₄H₈.
- Economic answers need demand, supply and value, not just "makes petrol".
- The catalyst is a zeolite, not platinum, nickel or iron.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Which type of bond is broken in cracking?
2. State the conditions for thermal cracking.
3. State the conditions for catalytic cracking.
4. Which type of cracking would a company use to make mainly alkenes?
5. Name the two main types of product of catalytic cracking.
6. Explain why cracking an alkane must give at least one alkene.
7. Complete: C₁₈H₃₈ → C₁₀H₂₂ + 2C₂H₄ + ?
8. Nonane cracks to give propene and one alkane only, one molecule of each. Name the alkane.
9. Calculate the mass of ethene that can be made from 198 g of tetradecane by C₁₄H₃₀ → C₈H₁₈ + 3C₂H₄.
10. Why are alkenes more useful to the chemical industry than alkanes?
11. Give one reason why a refinery cannot simply produce more petrol by distilling more crude oil.
12. What is a zeolite?

### Answers

1. C–C bonds.
2. High temperature and high pressure.
3. Slight pressure, high temperature, zeolite catalyst.
4. Thermal cracking.
5. Motor fuels and aromatic hydrocarbons.
6. Two alkane products would need two more hydrogen atoms than the starting alkane has.
7. C₄H₈ (butene): C 18 − 10 − 4 = 4; H 38 − 22 − 8 = 8.
8. Hexane, C₆H₁₄ (C₉H₂₀ → C₃H₆ + C₆H₁₄).
9. n(C₁₄H₃₀) = 198 ÷ 198.0 = 1.00 mol; n(C₂H₄) = 3.00 mol; mass = 84.0 g.
10. The C=C bond makes them much more reactive, so they can be made into polymers and other chemicals.
11. Distilling more crude oil also produces more of the surplus heavy fractions, because the proportions are fixed by the crude oil.
12. A porous aluminosilicate solid with a large surface area, used as the catalyst in catalytic cracking.
