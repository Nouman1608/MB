## Questions

**1.** Name the business document described in each case.

**(a)** Sent by a bank, listing all deposits, withdrawals and charges. **[1]**
**(b)** Sent by a seller to reduce the amount a customer owes after goods are returned. **[1]**
**(c)** Kept in the cheque book by the person who wrote the cheque. **[1]**
**(d)** Given to a customer to confirm that a payment has been received. **[1]**

**2.** Rosewell Stationery sells 50 reams of paper at a list price of 6.40 each to Garrowby School. Trade discount is 15%. A cash discount of 2% is allowed if the invoice is paid within 10 days. Calculate (a) the invoice total **[3]** and (b) the amount payable if Garrowby pays in 8 days. **[1]**

**3.** State the book of prime entry in which each item is first recorded. **[5]**

- Goods bought on credit for resale, supplier's invoice received.
- A cheque received from a customer.
- A credit note issued to a customer.
- A printer bought on credit for office use.
- Stamps bought from the office float.

**4.** Rosewell Stationery's credit sales in October: 4 Oct, Garrowby School, list price 380, trade discount 10%; 11 Oct, Dovecote Cafe, list price 150, trade discount 20%; 19 Oct, Nutley Traders, list price 500, trade discount 25%.

**(a)** Prepare the sales journal, showing the total. **[3]**
**(b)** State the two postings that arise from the Dovecote Cafe invoice and the journal total. **[2]**

**5.** Fallowfield Garage keeps a cash book. On 1 October the cash balance was 90 and the bank balance was 940. During the month: 2 Oct, cash sales 45; 5 Oct, a customer paid by cheque 230 in settlement of 240 owed; 9 Oct, a supplier was paid 380 by bank transfer in settlement of 400 owed; 12 Oct, 70 cash was paid into the bank; 15 Oct, wages of 35 were paid in cash. Enter these items in the cash book (cash, bank and discount columns) and balance it at 31 October. **[6]**

**6.** A petty cashier works with an imprest float of 100. During the month vouchers were: postage 14.20, travel 18.50, cleaning 9.75, postage 7.30, refreshments 6.40.

**(a)** Calculate the total spent and the cash that should remain. **[2]**
**(b)** State the amount needed to restore the float. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the purpose of the imprest system. **[2]**

**7.** Stanbridge Ltd buys display cabinets for 2,400 on credit from Cullen & Co.

**(a)** Prepare the general journal entry, including a narrative. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the purchases journal is not used. **[1]**

**8.** A bank statement received by Prescot Toys shows: a charge of 18.50; a direct credit of 640.00 from a customer, Sedgwick Florists; and a direct debit of 95.00 for insurance. None is in the cash book. State on which side of the cash book each is entered and the other account involved. **[4]**

**9.** Sedgwick Florists receives a statement from a supplier. It shows an opening balance of 520, an invoice for 280, a credit note for 60 and a payment received of 400.

**(a)** Calculate the closing balance on the statement. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how Sedgwick uses the statement as a source of information. **[2]**

**10.** A market trader keeps cash in a tin and handwrites all records.

**(a)** Give one advantage and one drawback of holding cash in a tin at the stall. **[2]**
**(b)** The trader considers accounting software. Give two benefits and two limitations of digital methods for original entry. **[4]**

**11.** Stanbridge Ltd buys goods from Elmsworth Parts with a list price of 600 and trade discount of 15%. It later returns goods worth 80 (after trade discount). It pays the balance by bank transfer, earning a 2.5% cash discount.

**(a)** Prepare Elmsworth Parts' account in Stanbridge's purchases ledger, showing the balance. **[5]**
**(b)** State the book of prime entry for each of the three entries in the account. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the trade discount does not appear in the account. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Bank statement [1]
**(b)** Credit note [1]
**(c)** Cheque counterfoil [1]
**(d)** Receipt [1]
*Examiner insight:* Learn the issuer as well as the name; a description such as "sent by a bank" points to one document only.

**2. (a)** Gross 50 x 6.40 = 320.00 [1]. Trade discount 15% = 48.00 [1]. Invoice total **272.00** [1].
**(b)** 2% of 272.00 = 5.44, so payable **266.56** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Cash discount is worked out on the figure after trade discount; starting from the list price gives a different answer.

**3.** Purchases journal [1]; cash book [1]; sales returns journal [1]; general journal [1]; petty cash book [1].
*Examiner insight:* A printer is a non-current asset, not goods for resale, which is why a purchases journal answer is a common trap.

**4. (a)**

| Date | Customer | List | Trade discount | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 Oct | Garrowby School | 380 | 38 | 342 |
| 11 Oct | Dovecote Cafe | 150 | 30 | 120 |
| 19 Oct | Nutley Traders | 500 | 125 | 375 |
| | Total | | | 837 |

Net figures correct [1]; trade discount shown correctly [1]; total 837 [1].
**(b)** Debit Dovecote Cafe 120 in the sales ledger [1]; credit sales 837 in the nominal ledger [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the journal with a clear heading and a total; the posting answer needs both an account and a side.

**5.**

| Details | Disc | Cash | Bank | Details | Disc | Cash | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Balances b/d | | 90 | 940 | Bank (contra) | | 70 | |
| Sales | | 45 | | Wages | | 35 | |
| Customer | 10 | | 230 | Supplier | 20 | | 380 |
| Cash (contra) | | | 70 | Balances c/d | | 30 | 860 |
| Totals | 10 | 135 | 1,240 | Totals | 20 | 135 | 1,240 |

Opening balances and sales entered [1]; customer cheque 230 with discount allowed 10 [1]; supplier payment 380 with discount received 20 [1]; contra on both sides [1]; closing cash 30 [1]; closing bank 860 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Discount goes in the discount column, not in the bank figure; the cash book is easier to mark if the totals visibly agree.

**6. (a)** Total 14.20 + 18.50 + 9.75 + 7.30 + 6.40 = **56.15** [1]; cash remaining 100 - 56.15 = **43.85** [1].
**(b)** **56.15** [1].
**(c)** To control small payments [1] by restoring a fixed float each period, so all spending is checked against vouchers [1].
*Examiner insight:* Restore the amount spent, not the amount left; saying why it is controlled gains more than naming the system.

**7. (a)** Debit Display cabinets 2,400 [1]; credit Cullen & Co 2,400 [1]; narrative such as cabinets bought on credit [1].
**(b)** The purchases journal records only goods bought for resale, and cabinets are a non-current asset [1].
*Examiner insight:* A narrative is part of a journal entry; leaving it out usually costs the final mark.

**8.** Bank charges: payments side, bank column; debit bank charges [1]. Direct credit: receipts side, bank column; credit Sedgwick Florists [1]. Insurance direct debit: payments side, bank column; debit insurance [1]. The statement is the first evidence of these items, so it is the source [1].
*Examiner insight:* State the side, the column and the other account; naming only the side leaves the entry incomplete.

**9. (a)** 520 + 280 - 60 - 400 = **340** [1]; the credit note and payment are deducted [1].
**(b)** Sedgwick compares each item with its own supplier account [1]; a difference points to a missing document or an error to investigate [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Check it" is too vague; say what is checked against what.

**10. (a)** Advantage: cash is at hand for small payments or change [1]. Drawback: risk of theft or loss [1].
**(b)** Benefits, any two: faster entry; automatic totals and postings; easy backup and searching [2]. Limitations, any two: set-up and staff training costs; breakdown or lost data; one wrong input spreading automatically [2].
*Examiner insight:* If a question asks for both benefits and limitations, answers covering only one side usually leave marks unclaimed.

**11. (a)** Purchases 600 less 15% = 510 [1].

```
Elmsworth Parts account (purchases ledger)
Debit                       |  Credit
Purchases returns     80.00 |  Purchases           510.00
Bank                 419.25 |
Discount received     10.75 |
                     510.00 |                      510.00
```

Return 80 on the debit side [1]; owed 430 and discount 10.75 (2.5% of 430) [1]; bank 419.25 [1]; balance nil [1].
**(b)** Purchases journal [1]; purchases returns journal [1]; cash book [1].
**(c)** Only the net invoice figure is recorded, so trade discount is never entered [1].
*Examiner insight:* Work out cash discount on the balance actually owed after the return, and label each side of the account.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a document without matching it to its issuer or use.
- Calculating cash discount on the list price.
- Recording gross, not net, invoice values in a journal.
- Placing a cheque or transfer in the cash column.
- Forgetting that a contra appears on both sides of the cash book.
- Restoring the petty cash float with the cash left.
- Omitting a narrative from the general journal.
- Giving benefits only when a question asks for limitations too.
