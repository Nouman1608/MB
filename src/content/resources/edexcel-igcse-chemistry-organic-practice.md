These twelve questions cover Topic 4 of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1): crude oil, fuels and pollution, cracking, alkanes, alkenes, ethanol and addition polymers. Questions 1 and 10 use alcohol content that is assessed on Paper 2 only. Try each question without notes, then mark yourself point by point. Use 24 dm³ as the molar volume of a gas at room temperature and pressure. Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16.

## Questions

**1.** Ethanol can be made by the fermentation of glucose.

**(a)** State **two** conditions, other than the absence of air, used for fermentation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why air must be kept out of the fermentation mixture. **[1]**

**2.** Describe how crude oil is separated into fractions by fractional distillation. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** Write the structural formulae of the **two** isomers of C₄H₁₀. **[2]**

**(b)** State what is meant by the term *saturated*. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the molecular formula of the alkane with nine carbon atoms. **[1]**

**4.** Gasoline, kerosene, diesel and bitumen are fractions of crude oil.

**(a)** State how the boiling point and the viscosity change from gasoline to bitumen. **[2]**

**(b)** Give one use of kerosene. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why bitumen is collected at the bottom of the fractionating column. **[1]**

**5.** Butane, C₄H₁₀, is used in portable stoves.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of butane. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the incomplete combustion of butane to form carbon monoxide and water. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why carbon monoxide is harmful to people. **[1]**

**6.** A camping stove burns 3.30 g of propane, C₃H₈, in plenty of oxygen: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of propane burned. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of this carbon dioxide at room temperature and pressure. **[1]**

**7.** Hexadecane, C₁₆H₃₄, is cracked to form one molecule of octane, C₈H₁₈, and two molecules of the same alkene.

**(a)** Deduce the molecular formula of the alkene. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** State the catalyst and the temperature range used in catalytic cracking. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why oil companies crack long-chain hydrocarbons. **[1]**

**8.** **(a)** Describe a test to distinguish between hexane and hexene, giving the result for each. **[2]**

**(b)** Propene reacts with bromine. Write the equation and name the product. **[2]**

**(c)** State the type of reaction in (b). **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain how burning some fossil fuels can cause acid rain. **[2]**

**(b)** Petrol contains no nitrogen, yet oxides of nitrogen are formed in a car engine. Explain why. **[2]**

**10.** Ethanol is made industrially by the hydration of ethene.

**(a)** State **three** conditions used for this reaction. **[3]**

**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, ferments to form ethanol and carbon dioxide. Calculate the maximum mass of ethanol, C₂H₅OH, formed from 36.0 g of glucose. Ar values as above. **[4]**

**11.** Poly(chloroethene) and poly(propene) are addition polymers.

**(a)** Name and give the structural formula of the monomer of poly(chloroethene). **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the repeat unit of poly(propene), showing the extension bonds. **[2]**

**(c)** One poly(propene) molecule has 400 repeat units. Calculate its relative molecular mass. **[2]**

**(d)** Give **two** problems caused by the disposal of addition polymers. **[2]**

**12.** Explain how a refinery obtains petrol from crude oil, and how burning petrol in a car engine can harm the environment. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A temperature of about **30 °C** [1]; **yeast** (enzymes in yeast) [1].
**(b)** Oxygen in the air would **oxidise the ethanol** to **ethanoic acid** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Give the biological catalyst by name and a temperature value. "Warm" or "enzymes" on their own are too vague to score.

**2.** Crude oil is **heated so it vaporises** and the vapour enters near the bottom of the column [1]; the column has a **temperature gradient**, hot at the bottom and cooler at the top [1]; each fraction **condenses** at the height where the temperature falls below its **boiling point**, so fractions are separated by boiling point [1].

*Examiner insight:* Say that the column is hot at the bottom and cool at the top. Marks go to "separated by boiling point", not to "separated by size" on its own.

**3. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ (butane) [1]; CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ (2-methylpropane) [1].
**(b)** It contains only **single bonds** between carbon atoms (no C=C), so no more atoms can be added [1].
**(c)** C₉H₂₀ [1] (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ with n = 9: 2 × 9 + 2 = 20).

*Examiner insight:* A bent four-carbon chain is the same molecule as butane. A different isomer needs a branch.

**4. (a)** The boiling point **increases** [1]; the viscosity **increases** (the fraction is thicker) [1].
**(b)** Fuel for aircraft [1].
**(c)** Bitumen has the **largest molecules and the highest boiling point**, so it condenses (or stays liquid) in the hottest part of the column [1].

*Examiner insight:* Write "increases" for both properties. Answers that stop at "gets bigger" for the molecules do not say what happens to the property asked about.

**5. (a)** Correct formulae: C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O [1]; balanced: 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O [1].
**(b)** 2C₄H₁₀ + 9O₂ → 8CO + 10H₂O [1].
**(c)** It reduces the ability of the blood to carry oxygen (it is toxic) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Balance carbon first, then hydrogen, and leave oxygen to last. Oxygen is the only element that appears in two products, so it is the one to fix at the end.

**6. (a)** Mr of C₃H₈ = 3 × 12 + 8 × 1 = 44 [1]; amount = 3.30 ÷ 44 = **0.075 mol** [1].
**(b)** Amount of CO₂ = 3 × 0.075 = 0.225 mol [1]; mass = 0.225 × 44 = **9.90 g** [1].
**(c)** Volume = 0.225 × 24 = **5.4 dm³** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Use the 1 : 3 ratio from the equation before converting to mass. Copying the propane amount into the carbon dioxide step is the commonest slip.

**7. (a)** C₄H₈ [1] (each alkene has (16 − 8) ÷ 2 = 4 carbons and (34 − 18) ÷ 2 = 8 hydrogens).
**(b)** C₁₆H₃₄ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₄H₈ [1].
**(c)** Silica or alumina catalyst [1]; 600–700 °C [1].
**(d)** More long-chain hydrocarbons are produced than are needed, so cracking makes shorter-chain fuels, such as petrol, which are in higher demand [1].

*Examiner insight:* Check your equation by counting atoms on both sides. Here 16 C and 34 H balance exactly, and any other split of products shows an arithmetic slip.

**8. (a)** Add **bromine water** to each [1]. Hexene **decolourises** it (orange to colourless); hexane **stays orange** [1].
**(b)** C₃H₆ + Br₂ → C₃H₆Br₂ (or CH₂=CHCH₃ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCHBrCH₃) [1]; 1,2-dibromopropane [1].
**(c)** Addition [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Goes clear" is not accepted for the result. Write "colourless", and name the starting colour as orange or brown.

**9. (a)** Some fuels contain **sulfur** impurities, which burn to form **sulfur dioxide** [1]; sulfur dioxide dissolves in rainwater to form an **acidic** solution [1].
**(b)** Nitrogen and oxygen from the **air** react [1]; the temperature inside the engine is high enough for this to happen [1].

*Examiner insight:* The sulfur comes from the fuel, but the nitrogen comes from the air. Saying the fuel contains nitrogen loses the mark.

**10. (a)** One mark each for any three from: steam; phosphoric acid catalyst; about 300 °C; about 60–70 atm [1] [1] [1].
**(b)** C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH [1].
**(c)** Mr of glucose = 6 × 12 + 12 × 1 + 6 × 16 = 180 [1]; amount of glucose = 36.0 ÷ 180 = 0.200 mol [1]; amount of ethanol = 2 × 0.200 = 0.400 mol [1]; Mr of ethanol = 46, so mass = 0.400 × 46 = **18.4 g** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The equation gives two ethanol for each glucose. Forgetting the 2 gives 9.2 g, which carries only the first two marks.

**11. (a)** Chloroethene [1]; CH₂=CHCl [1].
**(b)** –CH₂–CH(CH₃)– inside brackets [1]; with a bond extending from each end of the bracket (and no C=C) [1].
**(c)** Mr of C₃H₆ = 3 × 12 + 6 × 1 = 42 [1]; 42 × 400 = **16 800** [1].
**(d)** Addition polymers do not biodegrade, so they remain in landfill for a very long time [1]; burning them can produce toxic gases [1].

*Examiner insight:* A repeat unit is drawn without a double bond. Its brackets must have open bonds on both sides, and the CH₃ stays attached where the monomer had it.

**12.** This is an extended response. The real exam marks it with levels of response, not point by point, so a clear and linked explanation matters more than the number of facts. Indicative points, worth 8 in this set:
- Crude oil is heated until it vaporises [1].
- The vapour rises up a column that is hot at the bottom and cool at the top [1].
- Gasoline (petrol) condenses where the temperature drops below its boiling point and is collected there [1].
- Demand for petrol is greater than the supply from distillation, so long-chain fractions are cracked into shorter chains, using a silica or alumina catalyst at 600–700 °C [1].
- Incomplete combustion in the engine produces carbon monoxide, which is poisonous [1].
- The high temperature in the engine makes nitrogen and oxygen from the air form oxides of nitrogen [1].
- Sulfur impurities burn to form sulfur dioxide [1].
- Oxides of nitrogen and sulfur dioxide dissolve in rain, forming acid rain [1].

*Examiner insight:* Link each harm to its cause in one sentence, such as "nitrogen and oxygen from the air react in the hot engine to form oxides of nitrogen". A list of pollutants with no cause scores low.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "warm" instead of a temperature value for fermentation, or leaving out yeast.
- Writing that bromine water "goes clear" instead of "colourless".
- Unbalanced equations: count carbon, hydrogen and oxygen on both sides before moving on.
- Blaming nitrogen in the fuel for oxides of nitrogen. They form from nitrogen in the air.
- Drawing an alkane isomer as a bent chain instead of a branched one.
- Leaving out the 1 : 3 or 1 : 2 ratio from the equation in mole calculations.
- Drawing a polymer repeat unit that still has C=C, or has no extension bonds.
- Using "separated by size" in distillation answers instead of boiling point and condensing.
- Describing cracking as "making the molecules smaller" without saying why it is done.
