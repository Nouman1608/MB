This guide covers section 13.2 of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. The syllabus lists "the causes, nature and problems of debt for countries"; "the international debt crisis and debt relief"; "different types of international aid and aid donors: relief aid, development aid, tied aid, bilateral aid and multilateral aid"; and states that candidates "should be able to critically evaluate the impacts of international aid on receiving countries". It is part of Topic 13, Global interdependence, in Paper 4.

The [Topic 13 study guide](/resources/a-level-geography-9696-global-interdependence/) tells the story of the 1980s crisis, the HIPC Initiative, Zambia's 2020 default and the Pergau Dam case. ## The nature of debt

**Who is owed?**

- **Bilateral creditors:** other governments, through loans from one state to another.
- **Multilateral creditors:** the World Bank, the IMF and regional development banks.
- **Commercial creditors:** banks and bondholders who buy government bonds.

**Measures used in data questions:**

- **Debt-to-GDP ratio** = total debt ÷ GDP × 100.
- **Debt service** = interest plus repayments of principal due in a year.
- **Debt service ratio** = debt service ÷ export earnings × 100. It shows how much of a country's foreign income goes on servicing debt.

### Worked example

*A fictional country owes US$24 billion abroad. Its GDP is US$40 billion and it earns US$8 billion a year from exports. This year it must pay US$1.2 billion in interest and US$0.8 billion in repayments.*

- Debt-to-GDP ratio = 24 ÷ 40 × 100 = **60%**.
- Debt service = 1.2 + 0.8 = **US$2.0 billion**.
- Debt service ratio = 2.0 ÷ 8 × 100 = **25%**. A quarter of export earnings goes on servicing debt before any imports are bought.

If the currency then falls in value against the US dollar, the debt and its service become more expensive in local currency, even though nothing has been borrowed.

## Causes of debt problems

Borrowing is normal; problems arise when debt grows faster than the ability to repay.

- **Borrowing for development** that does not raise export earnings or tax revenue, so the loan cannot pay for itself.
- **Trade deficits** financed by loans.
- **Falling commodity prices**, which cut export earnings while repayments stay the same.
- **Rising interest rates** on loans with variable rates, which raise debt service suddenly.
- **Currency depreciation**, because most external debt is in foreign currencies.
- **Shocks** such as droughts, conflicts or pandemics that reduce income and force more borrowing.
- **Poor governance**: corruption, capital flight and loans spent on prestige projects. The term "odious debt" is used for debt taken on by regimes for purposes that did not benefit the population.

## Problems of debt for countries

- **Crowding out:** debt service takes money from health, education and infrastructure.
- **Conditions:** rescue loans often require austerity, privatisation or trade liberalisation, which can raise prices for the poor.
- **Loss of credit:** a default cuts access to new loans and raises borrowing costs.
- **Currency crises:** pressure on reserves can trigger a sharp fall in the exchange rate and inflation.
- **Loss of policy control:** decisions may be shaped by creditors rather than voters.

## The international debt crisis and relief

The 1980s crisis followed lending of oil-exporters' deposits to developing countries, then sharp rises in interest rates and falling commodity prices. When Mexico announced in 1982 that it could not service its debt, the crisis spread.

**Forms of debt relief:**

| Approach | How it works |
|---|---|
| Rescheduling | Repayments spread over a longer period; creditor governments coordinate through the Paris Club |
| Debt reduction for bank loans | The Brady Plan (1989) exchanged bank loans for bonds worth less, reducing debts of several middle-income countries |
| Cancellation for the poorest | The HIPC Initiative (1996, enhanced 1999) and the Multilateral Debt Relief Initiative (2005) wrote off debts of qualifying countries |
| Debt-for-nature swaps | Part of a debt is cancelled in return for spending on conservation; the first was agreed for Bolivia in 1987 |
| Campaigns | The Jubilee 2000 campaign gathered millions of signatures for cancellation of the poorest countries' debts |

**Evaluating relief:** cancellation released money for social spending in many countries, but conditions were demanding and slow to meet. Relief also treats the stock of debt, not the causes, so new borrowing can rebuild it, especially as lending from commercial bondholders and new bilateral creditors has grown.

## Types of aid and donors

The five syllabus types are not alternatives; each aid flow can be described in several ways.

| Dimension | Options |
|---|---|
| Purpose and timescale | **Relief aid** (short-term, after disasters) or **development aid** (long-term improvement) |
| Conditions | **Tied aid** (must be spent on donor goods or services) or untied aid |
| Channel | **Bilateral aid** (government to government) or **multilateral aid** (pooled through international organisations) |

So a donor government paying for a road built by its own firms is bilateral, tied, development aid.

**Aid donors:**

- governments, through official development assistance (grants and concessional loans);
- multilateral organisations such as the World Bank, UN agencies and regional banks;
- non-governmental organisations (NGOs) and charities;
- private foundations;
- global health partnerships, such as Gavi, the vaccine alliance founded in 2000.

## Critically evaluating the impacts of aid

| Positive impacts | Negative impacts |
|---|---|
| Relief aid saves lives after disasters | Food aid can undercut local farmers and markets |
| Health programmes cut disease; smallpox was eradicated in 1980 through a WHO-led global campaign | Aid can create dependency and reduce pressure to raise taxes |
| Infrastructure and education raise long-term productivity | Tied aid returns money to donors and may supply unsuitable goods |
| Multilateral aid pools expertise and reduces donor bias | Conditions can override local priorities |
| Small-scale NGO projects can reach the poorest | Corruption can divert funds; loans add to debt |

**The debate.** Jeffrey Sachs, in *The End of Poverty* (2005), argued that a large increase in well-targeted aid could lift the poorest countries out of poverty traps. William Easterly, in *The White Man's Burden* (2006), criticised large, top-down aid plans and favoured smaller, accountable, feedback-driven projects. Dambisa Moyo, in *Dead Aid* (2009), argued that large government-to-government aid had harmed African development by encouraging dependency and corruption. Using these viewpoints shows evaluation of different perspectives.

**A structure for "critically evaluate":** judge impacts by **type** (relief or development, tied or untied), **channel** (bilateral, multilateral or NGO), **scale** (national or community), **timescale** (immediate or long-term) and **context** (the quality of governance in the receiving country).

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain how a fall in commodity prices can cause debt problems. [4]*

> Many LICs depend on one or two commodities for most export earnings (**export dependence**). When prices fall, foreign currency income falls (**effect on earnings**), but debt repayments, usually in US dollars, stay the same (**fixed obligations**). The debt service ratio therefore rises, so countries must cut imports or spending, or borrow more to keep up repayments (**consequence**).

## Common mistakes

- Confusing debt (the total owed) with debt service (payments due this year).
- Treating the five aid types as separate categories rather than overlapping descriptions.
- Assuming all aid is a grant; some is a loan that adds to debt.
- Writing that debt relief solved debt problems permanently.
- Evaluating aid with no distinction between types, channels or contexts.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate**: show the ratio and say what it means for the country.
- **Explain** debt: give the chain from cause to repayment difficulty.
- **Critically evaluate** aid: use named examples and the type, channel, scale, timescale and context structure, then conclude.
