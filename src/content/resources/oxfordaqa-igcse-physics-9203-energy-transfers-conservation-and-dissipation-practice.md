These questions cover **3.2.2 Energy transfers, conservation and dissipation of energy**, statements a to g, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Some questions use the energy equations from section 3.2.1. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Learn the content first from the [energy transfers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-conservation-and-dissipation/) and the [energy transfers revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-conservation-and-dissipation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a system in physics. **[1]**

**2.** State the principle of conservation of energy. **[2]**

**3.** Name **two** forces that dissipate energy by heating the surroundings. **[2]**

**4.** An electric fan is supplied with 1200 J of energy and transfers 780 J as kinetic energy of the air. Calculate its efficiency as a decimal. **[2]**

**5.** A device has an efficiency of 35%. It is supplied with 60 W. Calculate its useful power output. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the efficiency of a device can never be greater than 100%. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A child on a swing is pulled back and released from rest. The child's centre of mass is then 0.80 m above its lowest position. The child and seat together have a mass of 30 kg.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfers during one swing from the highest point on one side to the highest point on the other side. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy transferred as the child moves down to the lowest point. **[2]**

**(c)** Assuming no energy is dissipated, calculate the speed at the lowest point. **[3]**

**8.** A skateboarder of mass 55 kg starts from rest at the top of a ramp 3.2 m high. At the bottom her speed is 7.0 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy at the bottom. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy dissipated and explain where it has gone. **[3]**

**9.** A toy dart is fired upwards by a spring. The spring stores 0.36 J of elastic potential energy. The dart has a mass of 0.015 kg.

**(a)** Describe the energy changes from release until the dart reaches its highest point. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum height the dart could reach if no energy were dissipated. **[3]**

**(c)** The dart actually rises 1.8 m. Calculate the percentage of the stored energy that becomes gravitational potential energy. **[2]**

**10.** An electric hoist lifts a load of mass 250 kg through 6.0 m in 20 s. The electrical power input to the hoist is 1500 W.

**(a)** Calculate the useful energy transferred to the load. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total energy supplied in 20 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the efficiency of the hoist. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest **one** way the efficiency could be improved, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**11.** An electric motor is supplied with 800 J. It transfers 480 J as useful kinetic energy, 280 J by heating, and the rest as sound.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred as sound. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how you would draw a Sankey diagram for the motor using a scale of 1 mm to 10 J, giving the width of each arrow. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the efficiency as a percentage. **[1]**

**12.** A pendulum is set swinging in a laboratory. After several minutes it has almost stopped.

**(a)** A student says that the energy of the pendulum has been destroyed. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**(b)** State where the pendulum's energy has gone, and why it is now less useful. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** change that would make the pendulum keep swinging for longer. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** An object or group of objects being studied [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Energy cannot be created or destroyed [1]; it can only be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Friction [1]; air resistance [1]. **[2]**

**4.** efficiency = 780 ÷ 1200 [1] = **0.65** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** useful power = 0.35 × 60 [1] = **21 W** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Energy cannot be created [1], so the useful energy output cannot be more than the total energy input [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** On the way down, Ep decreases and Ek increases [1]; at the lowest point Ek is greatest [1]; on the way up, Ek decreases and Ep increases until the child is momentarily at rest [1].

**(b)** Ep = 30 × 9.8 × 0.80 [1] = **235.2 J** [1].

**(c)** ½ × 30 × v² = 235.2 [1]; v² = 15.68 [1]; v = **3.96 m/s**, about 4.0 m/s [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Ep = 55 × 9.8 × 3.2 [1] = **1724.8 J**, about 1720 J [1].

**(b)** Ek = ½ × 55 × 7.0² [1] = **1347.5 J**, about 1350 J [1].

**(c)** dissipated = 1724.8 − 1347.5 = **377.3 J**, about 380 J [1]; work done against friction (between the wheels and the ramp) and air resistance [1] has heated the surroundings [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** Elastic potential energy of the spring is transferred to kinetic energy of the dart [1]; as it rises, kinetic energy is transferred to gravitational potential energy [1].

**(b)** Ep = 0.36 J at the top, so m g h = 0.36 [1]; h = 0.36 ÷ (0.015 × 9.8) [1] = **2.45 m** [1].

**(c)** Ep = 0.015 × 9.8 × 1.8 = 0.2646 J [1]; 0.2646 ÷ 0.36 × 100 = **73.5%** [1]. **[7]**

**10. (a)** Ep = 250 × 9.8 × 6.0 [1] = **14 700 J** [1].

**(b)** E = P × t = 1500 × 20 = **30 000 J** [1].

**(c)** efficiency = 14 700 ÷ 30 000 [1] = **0.49** (49%) [1].

**(d)** Lubricate the moving parts (gears, pulleys) [1], which reduces friction, so less energy is dissipated by heating [1]. **[7]**

**11. (a)** 800 − 480 − 280 = **40 J** [1].

**(b)** Input arrow 80 mm wide [1]; useful arrow 48 mm wide continuing straight on [1]; heating arrow 28 mm and sound arrow 4 mm wide, both bending away from the main arrow [1].

**(c)** 480 ÷ 800 × 100 = **60%** [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** Energy cannot be destroyed [1]; it has been transferred out of the pendulum's kinetic and gravitational potential stores [1].

**(b)** It has been dissipated to the surroundings by air resistance and friction at the pivot, heating the air and the pivot slightly [1]. It is spread out over a large amount of material as a tiny temperature rise, so it cannot easily be used [1].

**(c)** Any one [1]: reduce friction at the pivot (lubricate it or use a smoother support); use a more streamlined bob to reduce air resistance. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "energy is lost" instead of naming the transfer.
- Efficiency answers above 1 or with units.
- Not squaring the speed in kinetic energy.
- Sankey diagrams whose outputs do not add up to the input.
