This guide teaches **section 12.4 The impact of IT** of Topic 12 IT in society in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. It is A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. The [IT in society revision notes](/resources/a-level-cambridge-ict-it-in-society-revision-notes/) cover the whole topic; this page goes deeper on the impact of IT. Use it with the [impact of IT revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-impact-of-it-revision-notes/) and the [impact of IT practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-impact-of-it-practice/).

## What section 12.4 covers

| Syllabus strand | Areas named |
|---|---|
| Impact of IT on society | Sport, manufacturing, healthcare, education, banking, e-business and finance, news, entertainment and media, family and home, government, politics, individuals and organisations |
| Impact on monitoring and surveillance | Individuals, organisations, security and policing |

Questions usually give a setting and ask for the **positive and negative effects**. The best answers name a specific technology, say what it changes, and state who benefits or loses. Good answers cover both sides.

## How to analyse an impact

Use four steps for any area:

1. **Technology**: what IT is used?
2. **Change**: what is now done differently?
3. **Benefit**: who gains, and how?
4. **Cost or risk**: who loses, or what new problem is created?

## Impact by area

### Sport

IT is used for performance analysis with sensors and video, wearable devices that record heart rate and speed, video assistant referees, goal-line and ball-tracking systems, online ticketing and streaming of events. **Benefits:** fairer decisions, better training, wider audiences and new revenue. **Drawbacks:** play is slowed by reviews, richer clubs can afford better technology, athlete data raises privacy concerns, and tracking data may be used in betting.

### Manufacturing

Robots, computer-aided design and manufacture, and automated stock control carry out work that people used to do. **Benefits:** faster and more consistent production, 24-hour operation, safer work in dangerous jobs and lower costs per item. **Drawbacks:** unemployment for unskilled workers, a need for retraining, high set-up and maintenance costs, and a stoppage if systems fail.

**Worked example 1: robot against human.** A robot completes one item every 45 seconds. A worker takes 3 minutes (180 seconds). Over an 8-hour shift (28,800 seconds):

- Robot output = 28,800 ÷ 45 = 640 items. Worker output = 28,800 ÷ 180 = 160 items. The robot makes **four times** as many.
- If 2.5% of the robot's items are faulty, 640 × 0.025 = 16 are faulty, so 624 are good. If 5% of the worker's items are faulty, 160 × 0.05 = 8 are faulty, so 152 are good.

The robot's output is higher, but it brings costs to buy, program and maintain it, and the company may need fewer manual workers.

### Healthcare

Electronic health records, expert diagnostic systems, scanners, robotic surgery, telemedicine and patient monitoring. **Benefits:** quicker, more accurate diagnosis; specialist advice for remote areas; records available wherever the patient is treated. **Drawbacks:** sensitive data is at risk of theft; over-reliance on systems; cost; and staff need training.

### Education

Virtual learning environments, interactive whiteboards, online resources, computer-based testing and online classes. **Benefits:** personalised work, access to materials at any time, quick feedback. **Drawbacks:** the digital divide, distractions, plagiarism and the cost of devices and training. (Section 12.5 covers technology enhanced learning in detail.)

### Banking

Online and mobile banking, cash machines, chip and contactless cards and automated fraud detection. **Benefits:** 24-hour service and lower costs for banks. **Drawbacks:** branches close, which is a problem for people without internet access, along with phishing, fraud and system failures.

**Worked example 2: cost of a transaction.** A bank pays £3.50 to process a transaction in a branch and £0.25 online. If 10,000 transactions a week move online, the saving is (3.50 − 0.25) × 10,000 = £32,500 per week, or £32,500 × 52 = £1,690,000 per year. The saving is real, but the bank must also spend on security, and it may lose customers who prefer a face-to-face service.

### E-business and finance

Online shopping, electronic payment, online trading and electronic funds transfer. **Benefits:** a global market; low overheads; shopping at any time; instant payments. **Drawbacks:** small shops lose trade; fraud, data theft and identity theft; delivery problems; customers cannot see goods before buying.

### News

News is published online and on social media, with live blogs and push notifications, and ordinary people can report events as they happen. **Benefits:** speed and wide reach. **Drawbacks:** false stories spread rapidly, local newspapers lose income, and news may be tailored to what users already like.

### Entertainment and media

Streaming music and video, online gaming, digital effects, e-books and user-generated content. **Benefits:** a huge choice on demand; lower distribution costs; anyone can publish. **Drawbacks:** piracy, losses for creators, less time spent on other activities, shops for physical media closing and addiction to games.

### Family and home

Smart home devices, video calls, online shopping, home working and entertainment. **Benefits:** easy contact with distant relatives; convenience; flexible working. **Drawbacks:** less face-to-face time; screen time; devices that listen may reduce privacy; and children may see unsuitable content.

### Government and politics

Governments provide online services such as tax returns and licence renewals, electronic voting trials, open data and public consultation. **Benefits:** faster, cheaper services; wider public involvement; better planning. **Drawbacks:** people without access are excluded, personal data is at risk, systems can fail or be hacked. In politics, parties use targeted online advertising and social media to reach voters. This is effective, but false information and manipulation of opinion are risks, and election systems need strong security.

### Individuals and organisations

Individuals gain convenience, information and communication, and new jobs (such as web design) arise, but some jobs disappear, people can feel overloaded with information, and there is a risk of identity theft. Organisations gain efficiency, a wider market and better data, but must spend on equipment, training and security, and must keep up with fast change.

## Monitoring and surveillance

**Monitoring and surveillance** means collecting information about people's activities, using CCTV, number plate recognition, location tracking, online tracking, card and phone records, and software that records staff computer use.

| Group | Effect |
|---|---|
| Individuals | Safer public places and quicker help in emergencies, but loss of privacy, the feeling of being watched, and risk that data is misused or stolen |
| Organisations | Can check that staff follow rules, protect property and measure productivity, but staff may feel mistrust and stress, and the organisation takes on duties under data protection law |
| Security and policing | Helps prevent and solve crime and track suspects, through CCTV, facial recognition and data analysis. Risks are wrongful identification, bias in the systems, misuse of the powers and loss of public trust |

Balanced answers say that surveillance can protect people, but only when it is proportionate, legal, open about what is collected and kept secure.

## Common mistakes

- Listing technologies without saying what changed.
- Giving only the benefits or only the drawbacks.
- Writing "it saves time" with no figure or example. Say what is faster and for whom.
- Writing about the whole of society when a particular group is named in the question.
- Forgetting that surveillance has benefits as well as privacy costs.
- Using the same example (such as online shopping) for several areas.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** an impact: technology, then the change it causes.
- **Explain**: add why the change happens.
- **Discuss**: include effects for at least two groups, plus a balanced conclusion.
- **Assess** or **evaluate**: make a judgement about which effect is more significant and why.
- Use the scenario's own details, such as the type of company or the age of the users.
