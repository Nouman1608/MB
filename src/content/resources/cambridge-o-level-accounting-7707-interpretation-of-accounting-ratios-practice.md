These questions cover section 6.2 Interpretation of accounting ratios of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. Questions 7 and 8 use the same business. Revise first with the [interpretation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-interpretation-of-accounting-ratios/) and the [interpretation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-interpretation-of-accounting-ratios-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** reasons why a business's gross profit margin may fall. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** reason why a business that made a profit may have less cash at the end of the year than at the start. **[2]**

**3.** Revenue rose from $84,000 to $96,600. Calculate the percentage change. **[1]**

**4.** Which change would increase a business's gross profit margin?

A: selling more units at the same price and cost

B: a rise in purchase prices

C: a rise in selling prices with costs unchanged

D: an increase in rent received

**[1]**

**5.** Suggest **two** ways a business could improve its working capital. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how other income affects the profit margin, giving an example of other income. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Okafor Electrical is owned by a sole trader.

| | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 240,000 | 252,000 |
| Cost of sales | 156,000 | 151,200 |
| Expenses | 54,000 | 75,600 |

**(a)** Prepare a statement comparing the results for the two years, showing gross profit and profit for the year, and the change in dollars and as a percentage for each line. **[5]**

**(b)** Calculate the gross profit margin and the profit margin for each year. **[4]**

**8.** For Okafor Electrical (Question 7), the owner changed to a cheaper supplier in 2028 and opened a second showroom with extra staff.

**(a)** Comment on the changes in the gross profit margin and the profit margin. **[4]**

**(b)** Recommend **two** actions to improve the profit margin. **[2]**

**9.** Harbour Café has the following ratios.

| Ratio | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | 2.10 : 1 | 1.40 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 1.20 : 1 | 0.60 : 1 |
| Inventory turnover | 18 days | 31 days |

**(a)** Explain what the change in the acid test ratio shows. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** possible causes of the change in liquidity. **[4]**

**(c)** Recommend **two** actions the owner could take to improve liquidity. **[2]**

**10.** Petra made a profit of $34,000 for the year, but her bank balance rose by only $1,200. During the year:

- depreciation was $6,500
- inventory increased by $4,200
- trade receivables decreased by $2,700
- trade payables decreased by $1,800
- drawings were $28,000
- a loan of $10,000 was received
- a new van was bought for $18,000.

**(a)** Prepare a statement explaining the difference between the profit and the change in the bank balance. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain why depreciation is added to profit in this statement. **[1]**

**11.** Lomond Shoes sold 4,000 pairs in 2027 at $50 a pair. Each pair cost $30.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit and gross profit margin in each case, compared with 2027:

(i) 10% more pairs are sold at the same price and cost. **[2]**

(ii) The selling price rises to $55 and the quantity and cost are unchanged. **[2]**

(iii) The cost rises to $33 a pair and the selling price and quantity are unchanged. **[2]**

**(b)** Closing inventory at the end of 2028 is overvalued by $2,000. State the effect on gross profit for 2028 and for 2029. **[2]**

**12.** The owner of Brightwater Hardware says, "My business is doing much better, because my current ratio rose from 1.60 : 1 to 2.90 : 1." During the year inventory doubled, trade receivables turnover rose from 30 days to 52 days and the bank balance fell. Evaluate the owner's claim. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: selling prices reduced [1]; purchase prices increased without a rise in selling prices [1]; closing inventory undervalued; a change in the mix towards lower-margin goods. **[2]**

**2.** Any one, explained: cash was spent on non-current assets [1], which is capital expenditure, not an expense, so it reduces cash but not profit [1]; or inventory or trade receivables increased, so cash is tied up; or drawings or loan repayments, which are not expenses. **[2]**

**3.** 12,600 / 84,000 x 100 = **15%** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A raises gross profit but not the margin; D affects profit for the year, not gross profit.

**5.** Any two: collect trade receivables faster [1]; reduce inventory levels [1]; reduce drawings; obtain a long-term loan to replace short-term borrowing; sell unused non-current assets. **[2]**

**6.** Other income, such as rent received or commission received, is added to gross profit when calculating profit for the year [1], so a rise in other income raises the profit margin even if gross profit and expenses are unchanged [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

| | 2027 | 2028 | Change $ | Change % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 240,000 | 252,000 | +12,000 | +5.00 |
| Cost of sales | 156,000 | 151,200 | -4,800 | -3.08 |
| Gross profit | 84,000 | 100,800 | +16,800 | +20.00 |
| Expenses | 54,000 | 75,600 | +21,600 | +40.00 |
| Profit for the year | 30,000 | 25,200 | -4,800 | -16.00 |

Marks: gross profit both years [1]; profit both years [1]; dollar changes [1]; percentage changes for revenue and cost of sales [1]; percentage changes for the remaining lines [1].

**(b)** Gross profit margin: 2027 84,000 / 240,000 x 100 = **35.00%** [1]; 2028 100,800 / 252,000 x 100 = **40.00%** [1]. Profit margin: 2027 30,000 / 240,000 x 100 = **12.50%** [1]; 2028 25,200 / 252,000 x 100 = **10.00%** [1]. **[9]**

**8. (a)** The gross profit margin rose from 35% to 40% [1], because the cheaper supplier cut cost of sales while revenue still rose [1]. The profit margin fell from 12.50% to 10.00% [1], because expenses rose by 40% (from 22.50% to 30.00% of revenue), mainly the second showroom's staff and running costs, which more than used up the extra gross profit [1].

**(b)** Any two: reduce showroom costs, for example by sharing staff between sites [1]; increase sales at the second showroom through advertising, so its fixed costs are spread over more revenue [1]; review other expenses for savings. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Recommending a cheaper supplier earns nothing here: the gross margin is already improving and is not the problem.

**9. (a)** In 2028 the café has only $0.60 of liquid assets for each $1 of current liabilities, down from $1.20 [1], so it may not be able to pay its current liabilities on time without selling inventory [1].

**(b)** Any two, each explained: inventory increased, shown by inventory days rising from 18 to 31 [1], so money is tied up in food and drink that may spoil [1]; cash was used to buy non-current assets such as kitchen equipment [1], reducing liquid assets [1]; current liabilities rose, for example a bank overdraft or more owed to suppliers [1], which lowers both ratios [1].

**(c)** Any two: order inventory in smaller amounts more often [1]; reduce drawings [1]; arrange a long-term loan instead of an overdraft; sell unused equipment. **[8]**

**10. (a)**

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Profit for the year | 34,000 |
| Add depreciation | 6,500 |
| Less increase in inventory | (4,200) |
| Add decrease in trade receivables | 2,700 |
| Less decrease in trade payables | (1,800) |
| Less drawings | (28,000) |
| Add loan received | 10,000 |
| Less purchase of van | (18,000) |
| Increase in bank balance | 1,200 |

Marks: depreciation added [1]; inventory and receivables correct [1]; payables correct [1]; drawings, loan and van correct [1]; result $1,200 [1].

**(b)** Depreciation reduces profit but involves no payment of cash, so it is added back [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** 2027: gross profit 4,000 x 20 = $80,000; margin 40.00%.

(i) 4,400 x 20 = **$88,000** [1]; margin unchanged at **40.00%** [1].

(ii) 4,000 x 25 = **$100,000** [1]; margin 100,000 / 220,000 x 100 = **45.45%** [1].

(iii) 4,000 x 17 = **$68,000** [1]; margin 68,000 / 200,000 x 100 = **34.00%** [1].

**(b)** 2028 gross profit is $2,000 too high [1]; 2029 gross profit is $2,000 too low, because the overvalued figure becomes opening inventory [1]. **[8]**

**12.** For the claim: a current ratio of 2.90 : 1 means there are more current assets to cover each $1 of current liabilities [1]. Against: the rise is caused by inventory doubling, which ties up cash and may become obsolete, so liquidity is not really better [1]. Receivables now take 52 days instead of 30 [1], so cash is collected more slowly and the risk of irrecoverable debts rises [1]. The falling bank balance confirms that the business has less money available to pay debts [1]. Judgement: the claim is not justified; the business is less liquid in practice, and the acid test ratio would show this [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs points on both sides and a clear judgement that follows from them.
