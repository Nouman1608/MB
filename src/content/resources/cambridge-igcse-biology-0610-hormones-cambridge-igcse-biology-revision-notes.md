## Definition (Core)

> A hormone is a **chemical substance**, **produced by a gland** and **carried by the blood**, which **alters the activity of one or more specific target organs**.

Four ideas: chemical · gland · blood · target organs.

## Glands and hormones

| Gland | Where | Hormone |
|---|---|---|
| Adrenal glands | on top of each kidney | adrenaline |
| Pancreas | below the stomach | insulin; **glucagon** (Extended only) |
| Testes | scrotum (male) | testosterone |
| Ovaries | either side of the uterus (female) | oestrogen |

## Adrenaline

**Core:** secreted in **fight or flight** situations. Effects (syllabus limit):

1. increased **breathing rate**
2. increased **heart rate**
3. increased **pupil diameter**

**Why each helps:** more oxygen in; blood with oxygen and glucose reaches muscles faster; more light enters the eye.

**Extended only: metabolic activity**

- increases **blood glucose concentration**
- increases **heart rate**
- → more glucose and oxygen to muscles → faster respiration → more energy for muscle contraction.

## Nervous vs hormonal (Core)

| | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Speed of action | very fast | slower |
| Duration of effect | short | longer lasting |

**Reason:** impulses along neurones are fast and stop quickly; hormones travel in the blood and remain there for a time.

## Worked reminders

**Heart-rate percentage.** 70 → 98 beats per minute after adrenaline: (98 − 70) ÷ 70 × 100 = **40%** increase.

**Two observations in one scenario.** A goalkeeper dives within a fraction of a second (nervous: fast). Her heart is still racing two minutes later (hormonal: adrenaline, long-lasting). One sentence for each, naming the type of control.

**Why only target organs respond.** The hormone reaches every organ in the blood, but only target organs respond to it.

**Direction of blood glucose changes (Extended).**

| Hormone | Effect on blood glucose |
|---|---|
| Insulin | decreases |
| Glucagon | increases |
| Adrenaline | increases |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hormone | Chemical produced by a gland, carried by the blood, altering target organs |
| Gland | Organ that secretes a substance; endocrine glands secrete hormones |
| Target organ | Organ whose activity is altered by a particular hormone |
| Adrenaline | Hormone from the adrenal glands in fight or flight situations |
| Insulin | Pancreatic hormone that decreases blood glucose |
| Glucagon | Pancreatic hormone that increases blood glucose (Extended) |
| Testosterone | Hormone from the testes |
| Oestrogen | Hormone from the ovaries |
| Fight or flight | Situation of danger or stress that triggers adrenaline release |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Adrenal gland vs kidney:** the small gland sits on top of the kidney.
- **Hormone vs impulse:** a chemical in the blood compared with electrical signals along neurones.
- **Speed vs duration:** nervous control wins on speed; hormonal control wins on duration.
- **Insulin vs glucagon (Extended):** both from the pancreas, opposite effects on blood glucose.

## Exam traps

- "Hormones travel along nerves."
- "Hormones affect the whole body" instead of target organs.
- Adrenal glands placed in the brain, or confused with kidneys.
- "Pupil size changes" with no direction.
- (Extended) Adrenaline said to decrease blood glucose.
- (Extended) Glucagon said to come from the liver.
- Comparisons with only one type of control in each sentence.

## Command words

- **Describe** a hormone: chemical, gland, blood, target organs.
- **Identify** a gland: use its position on the body diagram, then name its hormone.
- **State** adrenaline's effects: each starts with "increased".
- **Explain** an effect: add how it helps in fight or flight (oxygen, glucose, light).
- **Compare**: speed of action and duration of effect, both types in each sentence.
- **Describe** adrenaline's metabolic role (Extended): blood glucose up, heart rate up, then the benefit to respiring muscles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving "carried by the blood" out of the definition.
- Naming the gland but not the hormone, or the other way round, when both are asked for.
- Giving adrenaline effects that are not on the syllabus list instead of the three that are.
- Writing "makes you scared" or "gives you energy" instead of named effects.
- Comparing on features other than speed and duration.

## Self-test

1. Describe what is meant by a hormone.
2. Name the gland that secretes adrenaline and state where it is.
3. Which gland secretes insulin?
4. Name the hormone secreted by the testes.
5. Name the hormone secreted by the ovaries.
6. In what kind of situation is adrenaline secreted?
7. State three effects of adrenaline in the Core syllabus.
8. Give one reason why increased heart rate is useful in danger.
9. Compare the speed and duration of nervous and hormonal control.
10. (Extended) Name the second hormone secreted by the pancreas.
11. (Extended) State two effects of adrenaline on metabolic activity.
12. (Extended) Explain how adrenaline's effect on blood glucose helps muscles.

### Answers

1. A chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.
2. Adrenal glands; on top of the kidneys.
3. The pancreas.
4. Testosterone.
5. Oestrogen.
6. Fight or flight situations (danger, fear, stress).
7. Increased breathing rate, increased heart rate, increased pupil diameter.
8. Blood carrying oxygen and glucose reaches the muscles faster.
9. Nervous control is faster but short-lived; hormonal control is slower but longer lasting.
10. Glucagon.
11. Increases blood glucose concentration; increases heart rate.
12. More glucose is available for respiration in muscle cells, releasing more energy for contraction.
