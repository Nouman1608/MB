## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Contact force | Needs the objects to touch: friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force |
| Non-contact (field) force | Acts across a gap: gravity, electrostatic, magnetic |
| Friction | Force between two surfaces that impedes motion; may result in heating |
| Air resistance | A form of friction, for motion through air |
| Scalar | Magnitude only |
| Vector | Magnitude and direction; drawn as an arrow |
| Weight | The pull of gravity on an object |
| Elastic distortion | Object goes back to its first shape once the forces stop |
| Inelastic distortion | Object stays out of shape afterwards |
| Limit of proportionality | Point beyond which extension is no longer directly proportional to force |
| Velocity | Speed in a given direction, v = s ÷ t |

## Scalar or vector?

| Scalar | Vector |
|---|---|
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| time | acceleration |
| | force |
| | momentum |

Arrow rule: **length = magnitude, head = direction**. With a scale of 1 cm to 4 N, a 14 N force is 3.5 cm long.

## Force pairs

- When two objects interact, each exerts a force on the other.
- The two forces are vectors: equal in size, opposite in direction.
- They act on **different objects**, so they never cancel each other.
- Example: a boot pushes the ground backwards; the ground pushes the boot forwards.

## Equations

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| W = m × g | W weight (N), m mass (kg), g gravitational field strength (N/kg) |
| F = k × e | F force (N), k spring constant (N/m), e extension (m) |
| v = s ÷ t | v velocity or average speed (m/s), s displacement or distance (m), t time (s) |
| speed = gradient of distance-time graph | change in distance ÷ change in time |

The value of g is given in the question. Use that value.

## Method boxes

**Weight calculation**

1. Write W = m × g.
2. Check mass is in kg (divide grams by 1000).
3. Substitute the g value supplied.
4. Give the answer in N with a sensible number of significant figures.

**Hooke's law calculation**

1. Work out extension = new length − original length.
2. Convert cm to m.
3. Use F = k × e, or rearrange to k = F ÷ e or e = F ÷ k.
4. Check the force is within the limit of proportionality.

**Reading a distance-time graph**

1. Flat line: stationary.
2. Straight slope: constant speed. Speed = rise ÷ run, with units from the axes.
3. Curve: speed changing. Use a tangent for the speed at one instant.
4. Average speed: total distance ÷ total time, including any rests.

## Worked reminders

**Spring.** A spring of original length 20.0 cm is 26.0 cm long under 3.0 N. Extension = 6.0 cm = 0.060 m. k = 3.0 ÷ 0.060 = 50 N/m. A 5.0 N load then gives e = 5.0 ÷ 50 = 0.10 m, so the length is 30.0 cm (if the limit is not exceeded).

**Weight.** A 2.0 kg bag where g = 1.6 N/kg: W = 2.0 × 1.6 = 3.2 N. Its mass is still 2.0 kg.

**Distance-time.** A runner covers 100 m in the first 20 s, rests for 10 s, then covers 100 m in 30 s. Average speed = 200 ÷ 60 = 3.3 m/s (2 significant figures).

**Displacement.** A cat walks 6 m north then 2 m south in 8 s: distance 8 m, speed 1.0 m/s; displacement 4 m north, velocity 0.50 m/s north.

## Friction, heating and field forces

- Friction acts against the motion, or against the tendency to move.
- Rubbing surfaces warm up: brakes, hands, a rope sliding through a fist.
- Air resistance grows with speed and falls with a more streamlined shape.
- Field forces act without contact because each object sits in a field: a falling apple is in the Earth's gravitational field, a paper clip near a magnet is in its magnetic field, and a charged comb near scraps of paper is in an electric field.
- A force changes the shape of an object only when other forces also act. A spring needs a pull at each end.

## Model answers to learn

- **Stretching.** "The extension is directly proportional to the force, so the graph is a straight line through the origin, until the limit of proportionality."
- **Past the limit.** "The line curves, so extension is no longer proportional to force. The spring may not return to its original length."
- **Gradient.** "The gradient of a force-extension graph is the spring constant. The gradient of a distance-time graph is the speed."
- **Weight.** "Weight is the pull of gravity on the object. It is different on different planets because g is different, but the mass is the same."

## Force-extension graph

- A straight line from the origin that stays straight until the limit of proportionality. Gradient = k.
- Beyond the limit the line curves: extension grows faster than force.
- A stiffer spring has a larger k, so a steeper force-extension line.
- A spring stretched elastically stores elastic potential energy, and releases it when it returns to its original length.

## Required practical: force and extension

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Set up | Spring hung from a clamped stand, vertical rule alongside |
| Measure | Original length, then length after each added mass |
| Force | Weight of the masses, found with W = m × g |
| Process | Extension = length − original length; plot force against extension |
| Conclude | Straight line through the origin, so extension is proportional to force within the limit |
| Reduce error | Eye level with the pointer (parallax), repeat readings, check the zero error, small mass steps |
| Safety | Eye protection, stable stand, feet clear of falling masses |

Unloading the spring and re-measuring checks whether the distortion was elastic.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Mass and weight:** mass is a scalar in kg and does not change with place. Weight is a vector in N and depends on g.
- **Distance and displacement:** distance is the path length. Displacement is the straight-line separation with a direction.
- **Speed and velocity:** speed is a scalar. Velocity includes direction.
- **Length and extension:** extension is the increase in length.
- **Elastic and inelastic:** elastic recovers fully; inelastic does not.

## Quick self-test

1. Is tension a contact or a non-contact force?
2. Calculate the weight of a 0.25 kg object where g = 9.8 N/kg.
3. An object weighs 49 N where g = 9.8 N/kg. What is its mass?
4. A force of 1.8 N gives a spring an extension of 12 cm. Find the spring constant in N/m.
5. Using the same spring, find the extension for 4.5 N (assume the limit is not exceeded).
6. On a distance-time graph an object is at 20 m when t = 10 s and at 80 m when t = 30 s. What is its speed?
7. Convert 90 km/h to m/s.
8. A bird flies 360 m in 45 s. Find its average speed.
9. A trolley moves 120 m in 15 s, stops for 5 s, then moves 60 m in 10 s. Find its average speed.
10. Name two scalars and two vectors from this unit.
11. Explain what inelastic distortion means.

### Answers

1. Contact.
2. W = 0.25 × 9.8 = 2.45 N, so **2.5 N** (2 significant figures).
3. m = 49 ÷ 9.8 = **5.0 kg**.
4. k = 1.8 ÷ 0.12 = **15 N/m**.
5. e = 4.5 ÷ 15 = 0.30 m = **30 cm**.
6. Speed = (80 − 20) ÷ (30 − 10) = 60 ÷ 20 = **3.0 m/s**.
7. 90 ÷ 3.6 = **25 m/s**.
8. 360 ÷ 45 = **8.0 m/s**.
9. Total distance 180 m, total time 30 s: 180 ÷ 30 = **6.0 m/s**.
10. Scalars: any two of distance, speed, time. Vectors: any two of displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum.
11. Once the forces stop, the object stays out of shape.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the new length of a spring where the extension is needed.
- Forgetting to convert cm to m before using a spring constant in N/m.
- Writing a weight in kg, or a mass in N.
- Missing units, or giving too many significant figures.
- Describing a flat distance-time line as steady movement instead of stationary.
- Averaging speeds instead of using total distance over total time.
- Writing "the forces cancel" for a force pair, without noting they act on different objects.
- In the practical, forgetting to read at eye level, or saying "repeat" without saying what is then averaged.
- Calling a graph proportional when it does not pass through the origin.
