These notes condense **section 8.4 Graphs and charts** (Topic 8 Spreadsheets) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. AS Level content, tested in Paper 2 Practical and Paper 1 Theory. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [graphs and charts study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-graphs-and-charts/); test yourself with the [graphs and charts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-graphs-and-charts-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data series | A set of values plotted on a chart |
| Category labels | The names along the category axis, or on the pie sectors |
| Contiguous data | Data in adjacent cells |
| Non-contiguous data | Data in separate blocks of cells |
| Legend | The key that links colours or lines to series |
| Scale | The range and spacing of numbers on an axis |
| Interval | The step between numbers on an axis |
| Secondary axis | A second value axis, usually on the right |
| Combination chart | A chart that combines two chart types, such as columns and a line |
| Sector | One slice of a pie chart |

## Which chart?

| Purpose | Chart |
|---|---|
| Compare values for different categories | Bar (column) |
| Compare several series per category | Comparative bar (clustered or stacked) |
| Share of a whole | Pie |
| Change over time or a trend | Line |
| Compare several trends over time | Comparative line |
| Two series with different units or sizes | Combination, with a secondary axis |

Matching examples:
- Units sold in each region: **bar**.
- Percentage share of sales by region: **pie**.
- Monthly rainfall over a year: **line**.
- Rainfall (mm) and temperature (degrees Celsius) by month: **combination**.

## Selecting the data

- **Contiguous:** select the block, including the labels.
- **Non-contiguous:** select the first range, hold **Ctrl**, then select the second.
- **Specified ranges:** set the series values and the category labels exactly as the question says.
- Check that headings are used as **series names**, not plotted as data.

## Formatting checklist

| Item | Check |
|---|---|
| Title | States what the chart shows |
| Legend | Present if more than one series |
| Segment labels, values, percentages | Shown as the task specifies |
| Category axis labels and title | Present and readable |
| Series labels | Taken from the headings |
| Value axis title | Includes the unit |
| Scale: maximum and minimum | Set as required; bars start at zero |
| Data interval | Regular, giving about five to ten steps |
| Secondary axis | Used for a series with different units or size |
| Extract pie sector | Only the requested sector is pulled out |
| Field selection buttons | Shown or hidden on a pivot chart as required |

## Working out pie chart figures

Share = value divided by total × 100. Angle = value divided by total × 360.

Example: units North 102, South 43, East 30; total 175.
- North 102 / 175 = 58.29% (209.8 degrees).
- South 43 / 175 = 24.57% (88.5 degrees).
- East 30 / 175 = 17.14% (61.7 degrees).
The shares add to 100%.

## Setting axes: worked example

Rainfall values are 60, 45, 50, 38, 30 and 22, and temperatures are 5, 6, 9, 12, 15 and 19.

- Primary axis (rainfall, mm): minimum 0, maximum 70, interval 10.
- Secondary axis (temperature, degrees Celsius): minimum 0, maximum 25, interval 5.
- Rainfall as columns, temperature as a line, so the reader can tell them apart.
- Titles: chart title, "Month", "Rainfall (mm)" and "Temperature (degrees Celsius)".

## Building a chart: step order

1. Read the specification and list every required item (type, data, titles, labels, scale, position).
2. Select the correct data, including the headings.
3. Insert the chart type asked for.
4. Check the series and category labels in the chart data settings.
5. Add the title, axis titles, legend and labels.
6. Set the axis minimum, maximum and interval.
7. Apply any special formatting, such as a secondary axis or an extracted sector.
8. Check the printed view: nothing overlaps and nothing is cut off.

## Justifying a chart choice

Name the chart, state the data, and link to the purpose. For example: "A line graph, because the monthly sales are time-series data and the manager needs to see the trend over the year." For a comparison: "A comparative bar chart, because it shows sales of two products side by side for each region."

## Making a chart clear

- One idea per chart.
- Distinct colours that also work in black and white.
- No 3-D effects.
- Pie charts: few sectors, one whole.
- Labels that are big enough and do not overlap.

## Exam traps

- A line graph for unordered categories.
- A pie chart for change over time.
- A value axis that does not start at zero on a bar chart.
- No axis title or unit.
- Headings plotted as data.
- Forgetting Ctrl for a non-contiguous range.
- Same axis for series with very different sizes.
- Both legend and full segment labels repeating the same information.

## Self-test

1. Which chart shows change over time?
2. Which chart shows the share of a whole?
3. What is a comparative bar chart used for?
4. How do you select two separate blocks of data?
5. What is the purpose of a legend?
6. Why use a secondary axis?
7. A bar chart value axis starts at 90 instead of 0. What is the problem?
8. What does extracting a pie sector do?
9. Calculate the percentage for a sector of 30 out of a total of 150.
10. Why should axis titles include units?
11. Give one reason to avoid 3-D charts.
12. What are data intervals?

### Answers

1. A line graph.
2. A pie chart.
3. Comparing two or more series for each category.
4. Select the first block, hold Ctrl, then select the second.
5. It shows which colour or line represents which series.
6. A series with different units or a very different size can be read clearly.
7. Small differences look much larger than they are, which is misleading.
8. It pulls one slice out of the pie to emphasise it.
9. 30 / 150 × 100 = 20%.
10. So the reader knows what the numbers measure.
11. They distort the sizes of bars or sectors.
12. The steps between the numbers on an axis, such as every 10.
