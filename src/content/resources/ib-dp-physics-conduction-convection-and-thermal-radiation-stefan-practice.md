Use σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴ and L⊙ = 3.83 × 10²⁶ W where needed, together with the Wien constant 2.9 × 10⁻³ m K. ## Questions

**1.** Explain, in terms of the kinetic energy of particles, how thermal energy is conducted along a solid rod when one end is heated. **[3]**

**2.** A glass window pane has thermal conductivity 1.0 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, area 1.5 m² and thickness 6.0 mm. One face is at 18 °C and the other at 10 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of thermal energy transfer through the pane. **[3]**
**(b)** The pane is replaced by one of the same glass and area but twice as thick, and the temperature difference across it is halved. State the new rate. **[2]**

**3.** A small electric heater is to warm a room. Describe, with reference to density, why the heater warms the room more effectively when it is placed near the floor than near the ceiling. **[4]**

**4.** A copper rod has cross-sectional area 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² and length 0.50 m. Its sides are insulated. One end is held at 100 °C and the other at 20 °C. The thermal conductivity of copper is 385 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature gradient along the rod. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate of thermal energy transfer along the rod. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in 10 minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** Assuming the gradient is uniform, determine the temperature 0.20 m from the hot end. **[2]**

**5.** A lamp filament radiates as a black body with a luminosity of 60 W at a temperature of 2700 K.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area of the filament. **[3]**
**(b)** The temperature is raised to 3000 K with the area unchanged. Calculate the new luminosity. **[2]**

**6.** Star X has three times the radius of star Y and half its surface temperature. Both behave as black bodies. Determine L(X)/L(Y). **[3]**

**7.** The surface of human skin is at 306 K.

**(a)** Calculate the peak wavelength of its emission, treating it as a black body. **[2]**
**(b)** A hot star has a peak wavelength of 290 nm. Calculate its surface temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** State the region of the electromagnetic spectrum in which each peak lies. **[2]**

**8.** A star has luminosity 2.5 × 10²⁷ W and lies at a distance of 4.0 × 10¹⁷ m.

**(a)** Calculate its apparent brightness. **[3]**
**(b)** A second star with four times this luminosity has the same apparent brightness. Determine its distance. **[2]**
**(c)** Express the luminosity of the first star in terms of L⊙. **[1]**

**9.** A star has a spectrum with a peak at 580 nm. Its apparent brightness at Earth is 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ W m⁻² and it is 2.0 × 10¹⁷ m away.

**(a)** Determine its surface temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its luminosity. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate its radius. **[3]**
**(d)** Express its luminosity in terms of L⊙. **[1]**

**10.** Star P has surface temperature 9000 K and radius 5.6 × 10⁸ m. Star Q has surface temperature 3500 K and radius 2.8 × 10¹⁰ m. Treat both as black bodies.

**(a)** Calculate the luminosity of P. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the luminosity of Q. **[2]**
**(c)** Show, using proportionality, that L(Q)/L(P) is about 57. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the peak wavelength of each star. **[2]**

**11.** The temperature of a black body is raised from 3000 K to 4500 K.

**(a)** Describe three changes to its emission spectrum. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the new peak wavelength. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the factor by which the power radiated per unit area changes. **[2]**

**12.** The insulated panels of a cold room have total area 30 m², thickness 0.12 m and thermal conductivity 0.035 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. The inner surface is at −18 °C and the outer surface at 24 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at which thermal energy is conducted into the room. **[3]**
**(b)** The cooling unit can remove only 250 W in steady state. Determine the largest temperature difference across the panels for which the unit can cope. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy conducted in through the panels in 8.0 hours at the rate found in (a). **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Particles at the hot end have greater average kinetic energy and vibrate more [1]. They interact with neighbouring particles and pass on some of their energy [1]. Each neighbour passes it on in turn, so there is a net flow of energy towards the cold end without any bulk movement of the solid [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Heat moves from hot to cold" gives the direction but not the mechanism, so state the particle energy and the neighbour interaction.

**2. (a)** ΔT = 18 − 10 = 8 K [1]. ΔQ/Δt = kAΔT/Δx = 1.0 × 1.5 × 8 / 6.0 × 10⁻³ [1]. **= 2.0 × 10³ W** [1].
**(b)** The rate is proportional to ΔT/Δx. Half the ΔT and double the thickness gives one quarter [1]. New rate **= 500 W** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 6.0 mm to 6.0 × 10⁻³ m before substituting; a thickness left in mm gives an answer a thousand times too small, and writing the proportionality in (b) shows the reasoning even if the arithmetic slips.

**3.** Air next to the heater is warmed and its particles spread out, so its density falls [1]. The less dense warm air rises [1]. Cooler, denser air sinks or flows in to take its place, setting up a circulating convection current that carries energy through the room [1]. A heater near the ceiling warms air that is already at the top, so little circulation forms and the lower air stays cool [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit tends to follow the chain heating, lower density, rise, replacement; writing "hot air rises" with no mention of density usually leaves out a link.

**4. (a)** Gradient = ΔT/Δx = 80 / 0.50 **= 160 K m⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Rate = kA × gradient = 385 × 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 160 [1] **= 12.3 W** [1].
**(c)** E = 12.32 × 600 [1] **= 7.39 × 10³ J** [1].
**(d)** Fall in temperature = 160 × 0.20 = 32 K [1]. Temperature = 100 − 32 **= 68 °C** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded rate into part (c) so that rounding does not drift the third significant figure.

**5. (a)** Rearrange L = σAT⁴ to A = L/σT⁴ [1]. T⁴ = 2700⁴ = 5.31 × 10¹³ [1]. A = 60 / (5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 5.31 × 10¹³) **= 1.99 × 10⁻⁵ m²** [1].
**(b)** L ∝ T⁴, so L = 60 × (3000/2700)⁴ [1] **= 91.4 W** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) a ratio method avoids repeating the area calculation and reduces the chance of an arithmetic error.

**6.** L ∝ r²T⁴ [1]. Ratio = 3² × (1/2)⁴ [1] = 9/16 **= 0.563** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the proportionality before inserting numbers; a squared radius and a fourth-power temperature are the two details most often mismatched.

**7. (a)** λmax = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 306 [1] **= 9.48 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1].
**(b)** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 290 × 10⁻⁹ [1] **= 1.0 × 10⁴ K** [1].
**(c)** Skin: **infrared** [1]. Star: **ultraviolet** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert nanometres to metres before dividing; the region should be named for each peak separately, since a bare "infrared and ultraviolet" does not show which belongs to which.

**8. (a)** b = L/4πd² [1] = 2.5 × 10²⁷ / (4π × (4.0 × 10¹⁷)²) [1] **= 1.24 × 10⁻⁹ W m⁻²** [1].
**(b)** For equal b, d² ∝ L [1], so d = 4.0 × 10¹⁷ × √4 **= 8.0 × 10¹⁷ m** [1].
**(c)** 2.5 × 10²⁷ / 3.83 × 10²⁶ **= 6.53 L⊙** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), recognise that four times the luminosity needs only twice the distance.

**9. (a)** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 580 × 10⁻⁹ [1] **= 5.0 × 10³ K** [1].
**(b)** L = 4πd²b [1] = 4π × (2.0 × 10¹⁷)² × 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ [1] **= 1.51 × 10²⁷ W** [1].
**(c)** A = L/σT⁴ = 1.51 × 10²⁷ / (5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × (5000)⁴) = 4.26 × 10¹⁹ m² [1]. r = √(A/4π) [1] **= 1.84 × 10⁹ m** [1].
**(d)** 1.51 × 10²⁷ / 3.83 × 10²⁶ **= 3.94 L⊙** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a chain of steps, show each intermediate value with its unit so that a reader can follow your method.

**10. (a)** A = 4πr² = 4π × (5.6 × 10⁸)² = 3.94 × 10¹⁸ m² [1]. L = σAT⁴ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 3.94 × 10¹⁸ × 9000⁴ [1] **= 1.47 × 10²⁷ W** [1].
**(b)** A = 4π × (2.8 × 10¹⁰)² = 9.85 × 10²¹ m² [1]. L = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 9.85 × 10²¹ × 3500⁴ **= 8.38 × 10²⁸ W** [1].
**(c)** L ∝ r²T⁴, so ratio = (2.8 × 10¹⁰ / 5.6 × 10⁸)² × (3500/9000)⁴ [1] = 50² × 0.389⁴ = 2500 × 0.0229 = **57** [1].
**(d)** P: 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 9000 = **3.2 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]. Q: 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 3500 = **8.3 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" the value is given, so display the substituted ratio and an unrounded value before the result.

**11. (a)** The peak moves to a shorter wavelength [1]. The curve rises at every wavelength, so more power is emitted overall [1]. The area under the curve increases, since total power per unit area grows as T⁴ [1].
**(b)** λmax = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 4500 [1] **= 6.4 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1].
**(c)** Factor = (4500/3000)⁴ = 1.5⁴ [1] **= 5.06** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Make each spectrum feature a separate comparative statement, such as "peak at shorter wavelength", not a general "it gets brighter".

**12. (a)** ΔT = 24 − (−18) = 42 K [1]. ΔQ/Δt = 0.035 × 30 × 42 / 0.12 [1] **= 368 W** (367.5 W) [1].
**(b)** ΔT = (ΔQ/Δt)Δx / kA [1] = 250 × 0.12 / (0.035 × 30) [1] **= 28.6 K** [1].
**(c)** 8.0 h = 28 800 s [1]. E = 367.5 × 28 800 **= 1.06 × 10⁷ J** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before substituting, as in (b); a temperature difference is the same number in kelvin and in degrees Celsius.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting Celsius values into σAT⁴ or into Wien's law.
- Leaving out the 4π, or using the radius where the area is needed.
- Not converting millimetres, centimetres or nanometres to metres.
- Explaining convection as "heat rises" with no mention of density.
- Describing conduction without mentioning the kinetic energy of the particles.
- Mixing up luminosity, which belongs to the star, with apparent brightness, which depends on distance.
- Squaring the temperature, or taking the fourth power of the radius, in scaling questions.
- Giving no unit, or W where W m⁻² is wanted.
