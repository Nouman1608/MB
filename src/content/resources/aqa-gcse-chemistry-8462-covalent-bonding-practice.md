## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State what is meant by a double covalent bond. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the total number of electrons shared between the two atoms in a nitrogen molecule, N₂. **[1]**

**2.** Choose from this list: I₂, MgCl₂, diamond, C₂H₆, SiO₂, Zn.

**(a)** Give the **two** substances that consist of small molecules. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the **two** substances that have giant covalent structures. **[1]**

**3.** A dot and cross diagram of ammonia, NH₃, shows outer-shell electrons only.

**(a)** How many electrons should the diagram show in total? **[1]**
**(b)** How many lone pairs does it show? **[1]**
**(c)** How many electrons are in or touching the outer shell of the nitrogen atom? **[1]**

**4.** A ball and stick model shows two carbon atoms joined by a double bond. Each carbon atom is also
bonded to two hydrogen atoms.

**(a)** Give the molecular formula. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the formula is not written as CH₂. **[1]**

**5.** Describe the dot and cross diagram of a water molecule, showing outer electrons only. **[2]**

**6.** Give **one** limitation of a displayed formula and **one** limitation of a space-filling model for
representing a small molecule. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student draws a dot and cross diagram of chlorine, Cl₂. It shows **two** shared pairs between the
atoms and **two** lone pairs on each atom.

**(a)** Use the number of electrons to show that the diagram is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the correct diagram. **[2]**

**8.** Hydrogen sulfide has the formula H₂S. Sulfur is in Group 6.

**(a)** Predict the number of covalent bonds a sulfur atom forms. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the dot and cross diagram of H₂S, showing outer electrons only. **[2]**
**(c)** Draw the displayed formula of H₂S using lines for bonds. **[1]**

**9.** Poly(chloroethene) is a polymer. Its repeat unit contains two carbon atoms joined by a single bond.
One carbon atom is also bonded to two hydrogen atoms; the other is bonded to one hydrogen atom and one
chlorine atom.

**(a)** Describe how the repeat unit should be drawn. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why poly(chloroethene) is described as having very large molecules rather than a giant
covalent structure. **[2]**

**10.** In silicon dioxide, each silicon atom is covalently bonded to four oxygen atoms, and each oxygen atom
is covalently bonded to two silicon atoms.

**(a)** Explain why the formula is SiO₂. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** limitations of a 2D diagram of part of the silicon dioxide structure. **[2]**

**11.** A model of a methanol molecule shows one carbon atom bonded to three hydrogen atoms and to one
oxygen atom. The oxygen atom is also bonded to one hydrogen atom.

**(a)** Give the molecular formula, in the order C, H, O. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of covalent bonds formed by the carbon atom and by the oxygen atom. **[2]**
**(c)** State the number of lone pairs on the oxygen atom. **[1]**

**12.** Oxygen (O₂) contains a double bond, nitrogen (N₂) contains a triple bond and water (H₂O) contains two
single bonds. Explain, in terms of electrons, why each molecule has this bonding. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Two shared pairs** of electrons between the same two atoms [1]
**(b)** **6** electrons (three shared pairs) [1]

**2. (a)** **I₂ and C₂H₆** [1]
**(b)** **Diamond and SiO₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* MgCl₂ is ionic and Zn is metallic, so neither belongs in either list.

**3. (a)** 5 + 3 × 1 = **8** [1]
**(b)** **1** [1]
**(c)** **8** [1]

**4. (a)** **C₂H₄** [1]
**(b)** A molecular formula gives the **actual number** of atoms in one molecule, so it is not simplified [1].

**5.** **Two** overlaps between the oxygen and each hydrogen, each with **one dot and one cross** [1]; **two
lone pairs** (four non-bonding electrons) on the oxygen, giving 8 around O and 2 around each H [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Displayed formula: does not show the **3D shape** / does not show lone pairs or which atom each
electron came from [1]. Space-filling: the **bonds** between atoms cannot be seen [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Chlorine atoms have 7 outer electrons each, so the diagram should show **14** [1]; the student's
shows 2 × 2 + 2 × 2 × 2 = **12** (and two shared pairs would give each atom more than one bond) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **One** shared pair (one dot, one cross) in the overlap [1]; **three lone pairs** on each chlorine
atom, giving 8 electrons around each [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **2** (it needs 2 electrons to complete its outer shell) [1]
**(b)** Two shared pairs, one between S and each H, each with one dot and one cross [1]; two lone pairs on
the sulfur, giving 8 around S and 2 around each H [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **H–S–H** [1]

**9. (a)** Two carbon atoms joined by a single line, with H, H on one carbon and H, Cl on the other, all
joined by single lines [1]; square brackets round the unit with a bond line from each carbon passing
through the brackets, and *n* after the closing bracket [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Each polymer molecule is a **separate long chain with two ends**, made of many repeat units joined
by covalent bonds [1]; a giant covalent structure is a **continuous network** of atoms bonded in all
directions, with no separate molecules [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** It is a **giant** structure, so the formula gives the **ratio** of atoms [1]; each Si has 4 O
neighbours and each O is shared between 2 Si, so there are 4 ÷ 2 = 2 O atoms for every Si [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: it cannot show the **3D arrangement** of the four bonds around silicon; it shows
only a **small part** of the structure; it does not show the **electrons** in the bonds; the atoms are not
to scale. **[2]**

**11. (a)** **CH₄O** [1]
**(b)** Carbon: **4** [1]; oxygen: **2** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **2** [1]

**12.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- Each atom shares electrons to complete its outer shell [1].
- An oxygen atom has **6** outer electrons and needs **2** more [1].
- So two oxygen atoms share **two pairs**, a double bond [1].
- A nitrogen atom has **5** outer electrons and needs **3** more [1].
- So two nitrogen atoms share **three pairs**, a triple bond [1].
- In water, oxygen needs 2 electrons but each hydrogen atom can share only **one** electron, needing 1 more
  to reach 2 [1].
- So oxygen forms **one shared pair with each of two hydrogen atoms**: two single bonds [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Full marks need the electron numbers, not just the bond names; "because nitrogen makes
three bonds" is a restatement, not an explanation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing O₂ or N₂ with too few shared pairs.
- Putting two dots, or two crosses, in one shared pair.
- Simplifying molecular formulae such as C₂H₄.
- Treating SiO₂ as a small molecule because its formula is short.
- Missing *n* or the bond lines through the brackets on a polymer repeat unit.
