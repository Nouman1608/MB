These questions cover subtopic 5.1, Units of measure, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical, so every question suits both tiers. Section A is non-calculator; Section B questions are labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

For explanations, read the [units of measure study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-units-of-measure/); for a summary, use the [units of measure revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-units-of-measure-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Convert

**(a)** 0.85 km to metres, **[1]**

**(b)** 3600 g to kilograms, **[1]**

**(c)** 0.4 litres to millilitres. **[1]**

**2.** Convert

**(a)** 2.5 m² to cm², **[1]**

**(b)** 760 mm² to cm². **[1]**

**3.** A container has a volume of 0.024 m³.

**(a)** Write this volume in cm³. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the capacity of the container in litres. **[1]**

**4.** Ahmed says, "1 m² = 100 cm², because there are 100 cm in a metre." Explain Ahmed's mistake and give the correct value. **[2]**

**5.** Write these lengths in order, largest first: 2900 cm, 0.031 km, 30 500 mm. **[2]**

**6.** Convert 4.5 × 10⁸ mm³ to m³. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) A swimming pool is a cuboid 25 m long and 12 m wide, with a constant depth of 1.6 m.

**(a)** Work out the volume of the pool in m³. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the capacity of the pool in litres. **[1]**

**(c)** The empty pool is filled at a rate of 400 litres per minute. How many hours does it take to fill? **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A bottle holds 0.3 litres of medicine. One dose is 7.5 ml, and two doses are taken each day. For how many full days will the bottle last? **[3]**

**9.** (non-calculator) During one day, 18 mm of rain falls on a flat rectangular lawn measuring 6 m by 9 m. Work out the volume of rain that falls on the lawn, in litres. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator) A rectangular floor measures 4.2 m by 3.6 m. It is covered, with no gaps, by rectangular tiles measuring 30 cm by 20 cm, all laid the same way round with the 30 cm side along the 4.2 m wall.

**(a)** Work out the number of tiles needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Tiles are sold in boxes of 20. How many boxes must be bought? **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A metal bar is a cuboid measuring 2 m by 5 cm by 4 cm. It is melted down and made into cubes of side 2 cm, with no metal wasted. How many cubes are made? **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A rectangular field has an area of 0.036 km².

**(a)** Write this area in m². **[1]**

**(b)** The field is 240 m long. Work out its width. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.85 × 1000 = **850 m** [1]

**(b)** 3600 ÷ 1000 = **3.6 kg** [1]

**(c)** 0.4 × 1000 = **400 ml** [1]

**2. (a)** 2.5 × 10 000 = **25 000 cm²** [1]

**(b)** 760 ÷ 100 = **7.6 cm²** [1]

**3. (a)** 0.024 × 1 000 000 = **24 000 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 24 000 ÷ 1000 = **24 litres** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the direct route 0.024 × 1000 = 24 litres uses 1 m³ = 1000 litres; either route earns the mark.

**4.** A square metre is 100 cm by 100 cm, so the length factor must be squared [1]; 1 m² = 100 × 100 = **10 000 cm²** [1]

**5.** In metres: 29 m, 31 m and 30.5 m [1]; order: **0.031 km, 30 500 mm, 2900 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* give the final list in the units the question used; a list of 31, 30.5, 29 only shows the working.

**6.** 1 m³ = 1000³ mm³ = 10⁹ mm³ [1]; 4.5 × 10⁸ ÷ 10⁹ = **0.45 m³** [1]

**7. (a)** 25 × 12 × 1.6 = **480 m³** [1]

**(b)** 480 × 1000 = **480 000 litres** [1]

**(c)** 480 000 ÷ 400 = 1200 minutes [1]; 1200 ÷ 60 = **20 hours** [1]

**8.** 0.3 litres = 300 ml [1]; 300 ÷ 7.5 = 40 doses [1]; 40 ÷ 2 = **20 days** [1]

**9.** 18 mm = 0.018 m [1]; volume = 6 × 9 × 0.018 = 0.972 m³ [1]; 0.972 × 1000 = **972 litres** [1]

*Examiner insight:* working in centimetres also works: 600 × 900 × 1.8 = 972 000 cm³ = 972 litres. Mixing metres with millimetres does not.

**10. (a)** 420 ÷ 30 = 14 tiles along and 360 ÷ 20 = 18 tiles across [1]; 14 × 18 = **252 tiles** [1]

**(b)** 252 ÷ 20 = 12.6 [1]; round up: **13 boxes** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the area check gives the same count: 15.12 m² ÷ 0.06 m² = 252. Twelve boxes hold only 240 tiles, which is not enough.

**11.** Volume of the bar = 200 × 5 × 4 = 4000 cm³ [1]; volume of one cube = 2³ = 8 cm³ [1]; 4000 ÷ 8 = **500 cubes** [1]

**12. (a)** 0.036 × 1 000 000 = **36 000 m²** [1]

**(b)** 36 000 ÷ 240 [1] = **150 m** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 100 instead of 10 000 for m² ↔ cm², or 100 instead of 1 000 000 for m³ ↔ cm³.
- Treating 1 litre as 100 cm³.
- Multiplying dimensions in mixed units, such as 2 m × 5 cm × 4 cm = 40.
- Rounding the number of boxes or containers down when the question asks how many are needed.
- Leaving out the unit, or keeping the old unit, on the final answer.
- Forgetting that a rate in litres per minute gives a time in minutes, which may then need converting to hours.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the quickest way to convert m³ to litres?**
Multiply by 1000, because 1 m³ = 1000 litres. To go from litres to m³, divide by 1000.

**How do I convert mm³ to m³?**
The length factor from mm to m is 1000, so the volume factor is 1000³ = 10⁹. Divide by 10⁹; standard form makes this easier to write.
