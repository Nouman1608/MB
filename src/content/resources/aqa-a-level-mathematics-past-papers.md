This page is a guide to AQA A Level Mathematics (7357) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published one, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/a-level/mathematics-7357)
- [AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/a-level/mathematics-7357/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series
- [AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification at a glance](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/a-level/mathematics-7357/specification/specification-at-a-glance)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade-boundary archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. Every paper on this page was set on the 7357 specification, which was first taught in September 2017 and first examined in June 2018, so older and newer papers share the same content. The qualification is linear: all three papers are sat at the end of the course, each lasting two hours and worth 100 marks, and each carrying one third of the A-level. Paper 1 is pure mathematics; Paper 2 is pure mathematics plus mechanics; Paper 3 is pure mathematics plus statistics.

## June 2025

The reports describe Paper 1 as very accessible, with a mean score close to 2024, and Paper 2 as stronger on pure content than on mechanics. On Paper 3 the reports say candidates still struggle most where an answer needs reasoning or interpretation rather than calculation.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7357/1)**

- Sketches of exponential graphs lacked a labelled intercept, used wrong asymptotes (some vertical) or ignored what a base between 0 and 1 does.
- When a model was linearised with logarithms, candidates substituted values back into the original model instead of using the gradient and intercept of the straight line.
- Series questions that led from one part to the next were often not linked, so a maximum value found in one part was not used to decide a range in the next.
- Proving that a function has no inverse caused trouble: some candidates described it as the wrong kind of mapping or tried to rearrange and treated a failed rearrangement as proof.
- Sketching the graph of an inverse function lost marks for a missed fixed point and for a curve that was not symmetrical with the original.
- "Explain" answers often gave a calculation with no reason, and "show that" answers jumped over a step the question needed to see.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7357/2)**

- Moments questions improved, but the final mark was frequently lost for rounding badly or leaving out units.
- Trapezium-rule answers used the wrong number of brackets in the area expression.
- Stationary-point arguments did not state that the derivative must be zero, and "show" questions needed an explicit substitution of the given value.
- Trigonometric equations went better when written with secant and tangent than when turned into fractions, and some candidates treated an expression as an equation.
- The product rule was sometimes misremembered, and a final answer given as an expression when an equation was asked for lost the last mark.
- Mechanics errors included mixing up sine and cosine when resolving, adding a friction term where none applied, and giving only one correct modelling assumption where two were needed.
- Vector questions lost marks to the wrong order of subtraction for a displacement and to conclusions that left out the points named in the question.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 (7357/3)**

- Binomial expansion terms went wrong through sign errors and through forgetting a numerical factor taken outside the bracket.
- Integration of a fraction with a linear denominator was often given a wrong coefficient, and transformations were described with a reversed scale factor or an incorrect translation.
- In related-rates work, candidates treated a decreasing quantity as increasing.
- The proof by contradiction often began with an incomplete negation of the statement.
- Statistics answers were too vague: a binomial condition had to be stated in context, a single overall conclusion was needed for a model check, and a probability at a single point of a continuous distribution had to be explained as zero for that model.
- Venn diagram work suffered when compound set notation was not broken into its parts.
- Comparisons of two data sets were better when both the average and the spread were discussed.

## June 2024

The reports say Paper 1 was about ten per cent better on average than the previous year, with routine questions done well and "explain" and "show that" questions still difficult. Paper 2 was stronger on mechanics than on pure content, and Paper 3 showed strong calculation but weak reasoning.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7357/1)**

- Trigonometric equations lost solutions, for example by dropping the minus sign when taking a square root.
- Chain-rule work was spoiled by missing brackets, and binomial work by factors not raised to the right power or terms not simplified into ascending powers.
- Candidates who restarted a small-angle question rather than reusing an earlier result made extra work for themselves.
- The change-of-sign method for a root was a weakness, with only about 30 per cent of candidates gaining full marks.
- Domain and range answers needed set notation in the right variable, and the reason a function has no inverse was often missing.
- Differential-equation questions led to a wrong sign for a decreasing quantity and to a missing constant of integration.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7357/2)**

- Moments remained a topic candidates found hard to apply, although the idea of force multiplied by distance was better understood.
- Compound-growth questions confused months with years or started from the wrong first term.
- Inequality answers ignored strict signs, divided by a variable that could be zero, or gave two regions when the solution was one.
- Newton's second law questions went wrong when the resultant was found as a difference rather than a sum.
- Modelling assumptions had to be given separately and correctly, and combined statements earned only one mark.
- Vector questions lost the final mark through incomplete conclusions.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 (7357/3)**

- "Show that" answers did not substitute the given values explicitly, and some left the answer outside the requested form.
- A constant of integration was forgotten, which cost the later parts that depended on it.
- Completing the square and differentiation from first principles produced sign and bracket errors, and a limit was replaced by simply setting the increment to zero.
- Interpretation in context was weak: comparisons of data needed to be about the stated measures, not unrelated ones.
- Hypothesis tests lost marks for the wrong symbol in the hypotheses, for comparing with the wrong critical value, and for using one tail where two were needed.
- Normal-distribution questions needed the right z-value and the right tail, and conditional probability questions needed the correct denominator.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A Level Mathematics 7357 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 7357 assessment-resources page. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page and its archive.

**Which papers do I sit for a full practice run?**
All three: Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3. Each is two hours and 100 marks, and every candidate sits all three, so there are no options to choose between.

**Are the paper-level grade thresholds official grades?**
No. AQA states that the paper-level boundaries are notional and for illustration only. The grade a candidate receives comes from the subject boundary for the total mark out of 300.

**What do examiners say most often about 7357?**
In the 2024 and 2025 reports the same themes recur: incomplete reasoning in "show that" and "explain" questions, lost marks for rounding, units and brackets, sign errors, and statistics answers that are not written in context.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
