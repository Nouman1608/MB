Condensed recall for sub-topic 7.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029). For explanations and two worked calculations, use the [Human population size study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-0680-human-population-size/).

## The equation

**Population change = (births − deaths) + (immigrants − emigrants)**
- Births − deaths = **natural change**.
- Immigrants − emigrants = **net migration**.

## Five factors (7.2.1)

| Factor | How the syllabus measures it | Raises population when | Lowers population when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birth rates | Children per woman | Fertility above about 2.1 | Fertility below about 2.1 |
| Death rates | Life expectancy | Life expectancy rises | Famine, war, epidemics |
| Migration | Emigration, immigration; push and pull | Immigration exceeds emigration | Emigration exceeds immigration |
| Rural and urban migration | Movement within a country | Cities gain people | Rural areas lose people |
| Urbanisation | Rising urban proportion | Cities grow faster than the countryside | |

## Why fertility differs

**High fertility**: high infant mortality · children as workers or old-age support · little access to contraception · early marriage and little education for girls · traditions favouring large families.

**Low fertility**: high cost of raising children · women in education and careers · contraception available · later marriage · pensions and healthcare.

## Why life expectancy rises

Healthcare and vaccination · clean water and sanitation · reliable food · safer homes and work.

## Push and pull

| Push (from origin) | Pull (to destination) |
|---|---|
| Unemployment, low farm incomes | Jobs, higher wages |
| Poor schools and hospitals | Better services |
| War, persecution | Safety, freedom |
| Drought, natural hazards, degraded land | Reliable water and food |
| Few opportunities for young people | Family and friends already there |

## Urbanisation, precisely

**Urbanisation = increase in the proportion of people living in urban areas.** Caused by rural-urban migration plus high natural increase in cities, where migrants are young adults.

## The twelve impacts of growth (7.2.2)

1. Informal urban settlements
2. Megacities (more than 10 million people)
3. Availability of resources
4. Energy usage
5. Sanitation
6. Water supply
7. Management of waste
8. Availability of land
9. Facilities and infrastructure
10. Air, land, light, noise, visual and water pollution
11. Traffic congestion
12. Changes in employment opportunities

Memory grouping: **where people live** (1, 2, 8) · **resources and services** (3 to 7, 9) · **environment and economy** (10 to 12).

## Six pollution types, one example each

| Type | Example from growing cities |
|---|---|
| Air | Vehicle exhaust, burning waste |
| Land | Dumped rubbish, landfill |
| Light | Street and building lights at night |
| Noise | Traffic, construction, aircraft |
| Visual | Billboards, sprawling housing, waste tips |
| Water | Untreated sewage, industrial discharge |

## Calculation patterns

- **Total change**: work out natural change and net migration separately, then add. A city of 400,000 with 12,000 births, 4,000 deaths, 6,000 immigrants and 2,000 emigrants changes by +8,000 + 4,000 = +12,000, which is 12,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = 3% in the year.
- **Urban share**: urban population ÷ total population × 100. If the share rises between two dates, the country is urbanising, even if the rural population also grew.

## Chains linking growth to the environment

- More people → more vehicles → traffic congestion → air and noise pollution.
- Faster growth than sewer building → untreated sewage in rivers → water pollution and water-related disease.
- More households → more solid waste than can be collected → dumping and burning → land and air pollution.
- More demand for housing → land built over, including farmland and habitats → loss of food production and biodiversity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fertility rate | Average number of children per woman |
| Life expectancy | Average number of years a person can expect to live |
| Natural change | Births minus deaths |
| Net migration | Immigrants minus emigrants |
| Emigration | Leaving a country to live elsewhere |
| Immigration | Arriving in a country to live |
| Push factor | Something that drives people away from a place |
| Pull factor | Something that attracts people to a place |
| Urbanisation | Increase in the proportion of people living in urban areas |
| Informal settlement | Unplanned housing built without permission, usually lacking services |
| Megacity | City with more than 10 million people |

## Exam traps

- Emigration and immigration swapped.
- Push and pull factors placed at the wrong end of the move.
- Urbanisation defined as "cities getting bigger".
- "Pollution" without naming which of the six types.
- Ignoring migration in a population change calculation.
- Saying fertility falls because of "development" without a specific reason, such as contraception or girls' education.

## Self-test

1. Write the population change equation.
2. How does the syllabus measure birth rates?
3. Roughly what fertility rate replaces each generation?
4. Give two reasons fertility is high in some rural areas.
5. Give two reasons life expectancy rises.
6. Distinguish emigration from immigration.
7. Give one push and one pull factor for rural-urban migration.
8. Define urbanisation.
9. What is a megacity?
10. Why do informal settlements often lack sanitation?
11. Give an example of light pollution and of visual pollution.
12. A town of 50,000 has 1,500 births, 500 deaths, 800 immigrants and 1,300 emigrants in a year. Calculate the total change.

**Answers:** 1. Change = (births − deaths) + (immigrants − emigrants). 2. Children per woman. 3. About 2.1 children per woman. 4. Any two: children work on farms; high infant mortality; little contraception; early marriage; tradition. 5. Any two: healthcare and vaccination; clean water; sanitation; reliable food. 6. Emigration is leaving a country; immigration is arriving. 7. Push: low farm incomes or few jobs; pull: city jobs or better services. 8. An increase in the proportion of people living in urban areas. 9. A city with more than 10 million people. 10. They are built without planning or permission, faster than authorities can lay sewers. 11. Light: street lighting at night; visual: billboards or sprawling housing. 12. Natural change +1,000; net migration −500; total +500.
