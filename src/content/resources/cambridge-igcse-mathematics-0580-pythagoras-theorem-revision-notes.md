These notes condense subtopic **6.1 Pythagoras' theorem** from Topic 6 Trigonometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The statement, "Know and use Pythagoras' theorem", is the same for Core (C6.1) and Extended (E6.1). For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Pythagoras' theorem study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem/); to practise, use the [Pythagoras' theorem practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-practice/).

## The theorem

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c and shorter sides a and b:

```
a² + b² = c²
hypotenuse:     c = √(a² + b²)      ADD the squares
shorter side:   a = √(c² − b²)      SUBTRACT the squares
```

The theorem is **not** on the List of formulas, so learn it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypotenuse | The longest side, opposite the right angle |
| Shorter sides | The two sides that meet at the right angle (sometimes called the legs) |
| Pythagorean triple | Three whole numbers that fit a² + b² = c² |
| Converse | If a² + b² = c² for the sides of a triangle, then it has a right angle opposite c |
| Exact answer | A surd such as √41 or 3√5, not a rounded decimal (Extended surd work) |

## Which operation?

```
1. Find the right angle.
2. The side opposite it is the hypotenuse; label it c.
3. Want c?            add, then square root.
   Want a shorter side? subtract (c² first), then square root.
4. Sense check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side.
```

On a non-calculator paper, numbers in a Pythagoras question are usually chosen to make a triple or a perfect square.

## Hidden right-angled triangles

| Shape | Right-angled triangle |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | two sides and a diagonal |
| Isosceles triangle | half the base, the height and an equal side |
| Rhombus | half of each diagonal and a side (the diagonals cross at 90°) |
| Ladder against a wall | ladder, wall height and ground distance |
| Journey north then east | the two legs and the direct distance |
| Circle with a tangent | radius, tangent length and the line to the centre |
| Points on a grid (Extended length of a line segment) | difference in x, difference in y, and the distance |

**Worked reminder.** An isosceles triangle has equal sides 10 cm and base 12 cm. Half the base is 6 cm, so the height is √(100 − 36) = 8 cm and the area is ½ × 12 × 8 = **48 cm²**.

## Non-calculator tricks

- **Difference of two squares:** c² − b² = (c − b)(c + b). For 41² − 40², (1)(81) = 81, so the side is 9.
- **Scale down first:** 30, 40, ? is 10 × (3, 4, 5), so the answer is 50.
- **Exact answers (Extended):** √(2² + 6²) = √40 = 2√10. Simplify by taking out the largest square factor.

## "Show that" and checking

- Show at least one more significant figure than the value you are asked to show, then round to it.
- Converse check: square all three sides; if the two smaller squares add to the largest, there is a right angle opposite the longest side.

## Exam traps

- Adding the squares when finding a shorter side.
- Forgetting the square root at the end.
- Using the side opposite the right angle as a shorter side.
- Rounding a length in part (a) and reusing it in part (b).
- Squaring a negative difference of coordinates wrongly: (−5)² = 25, not −25.
- Taking the full base of an isosceles triangle instead of half of it.
- Answering with the hypotenuse when the question asks for how much further one route is.

## Self-test

1. Shorter sides 12 cm and 16 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
2. Hypotenuse 25 cm, one side 7 cm. Find the other side.
3. Shorter sides 4.6 cm and 7.3 cm. Find the hypotenuse to 3 s.f.
4. Hypotenuse 11.5 cm, one side 9.2 cm. Find the other side to 3 s.f.
5. A rectangle is 15 cm by 8 cm. Find the length of its diagonal.
6. A square has diagonal 10 cm. Find its side length to 3 s.f.
7. Is a triangle with sides 11, 60 and 61 right-angled?
8. Is a triangle with sides 6, 9 and 11 right-angled?
9. An isosceles triangle has equal sides 25 cm and base 48 cm. Find its height.
10. (Extended) Find the exact hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 3 and 9, in simplest surd form.
11. (Extended) Find the distance between (1, −2) and (7, 6).
12. A ship sails 20 km south then 21 km west. How far is it from its start?

### Answers

1. √(144 + 256) = √400 = **20 cm**
2. √(625 − 49) = √576 = **24 cm**
3. √(21.16 + 53.29) = √74.45 = **8.63 cm**
4. √(132.25 − 84.64) = √47.61 = **6.9 cm** (exactly 6.9)
5. √(225 + 64) = √289 = **17 cm**
6. s² + s² = 100, s² = 50, s = **7.07 cm**
7. 121 + 3600 = 3721 = 61², so **yes**
8. 36 + 81 = 117, but 11² = 121, so **no**
9. Half the base is 24 cm; √(625 − 576) = **7 cm**
10. √(9 + 81) = √90 = **3√10**
11. √(6² + 8²) = **10 units**
12. √(400 + 441) = √841 = **29 km**
