## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Respiration | An exothermic reaction in living cells that transfers energy from glucose |
| Exothermic | Transfers energy to the surroundings |
| Aerobic respiration | Respiration using oxygen |
| Anaerobic respiration | Respiration without oxygen |
| Fermentation | Anaerobic respiration in yeast cells |
| Incomplete oxidation | Glucose not fully broken down, so less energy is transferred |
| Lactic acid | The product of anaerobic respiration in muscles |
| Fatigue | Muscles stop contracting efficiently after long vigorous activity |
| Oxygen debt | Extra oxygen needed after exercise to react with lactic acid and remove it from cells (Higher tier only) |
| Metabolism | The sum of all the reactions in a cell or the body |

## Why organisms need energy

- Chemical reactions to build larger molecules.
- Movement.
- Keeping warm.

Respiration happens **continuously** in **all** living cells.

## Equations

| Type | Word equation |
|---|---|
| Aerobic | glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water |
| Anaerobic in muscles | glucose → lactic acid |
| Anaerobic in plants and yeast | glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide |

Recognise: C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂, CO₂, H₂O.

## The comparison, as paired sentences

- **Oxygen:** aerobic needs it, whereas anaerobic does not.
- **Products:** aerobic gives CO₂ and water, whereas anaerobic gives lactic acid (muscles) or ethanol and CO₂
  (plants, yeast).
- **Energy:** aerobic transfers much more, whereas anaerobic transfers much less, because oxidation of glucose
  is incomplete.

## Fermentation uses

| Product | Why yeast is used |
|---|---|
| Bread | Carbon dioxide makes the dough rise |
| Alcoholic drinks | Ethanol is produced |

## Exercise chain

More energy needed → heart rate, breathing rate and breath volume up → more oxygenated blood to muscles → if
oxygen is insufficient, anaerobic respiration → lactic acid builds up → oxygen debt → long vigorous activity
causes fatigue.

**Higher tier only:** blood carries lactic acid to the **liver** → converted back into **glucose**. Oxygen debt =
extra oxygen needed after exercise to react with the lactic acid and remove it from cells. This is why breathing
stays fast and deep after exercise ends.

## Calculations

**Percentage increase:** (new − original) ÷ original × 100.
Breathing rate 12 → 30 breaths per minute: (30 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 = **150%**.

**Volume breathed per minute:** breathing rate × breath volume.
20 breaths per minute × 1.5 dm³ = **30 dm³ per minute**.

**Oxygen debt (Higher tier only):** (mean uptake during recovery − resting uptake) × recovery time.
(0.8 − 0.3) dm³ per minute × 6 minutes = **3.0 dm³**.

## Metabolism

| Process | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Building carbohydrates | Glucose | Starch, glycogen, cellulose |
| Building lipids | 1 glycerol + 3 fatty acids | Lipid |
| Making amino acids | Glucose + nitrate ions | Amino acids |
| Building proteins | Amino acids | Proteins |
| Respiration | Glucose | Energy transferred |
| Breaking down excess protein | Proteins | Urea, excreted |

All are **enzyme-controlled**; the building reactions use energy from respiration.

## Reading exercise data

- During exercise: heart rate, breathing rate and breath volume all **rise**.
- After exercise: they stay **above resting** for a while, then return to resting values.
- A **faster return to resting** usually means a fitter person or a smaller oxygen debt.
- Blood lactic acid **rises** during hard exercise and **falls** during recovery as it is removed (Higher tier
  only: taken to the liver and converted to glucose).
- Always quote values and times when you describe a table or graph.

## Links to other topics

- **Cell structure (4.1.1):** aerobic respiration takes place in the **mitochondria**.
- **Photosynthesis (4.4.1):** the glucose respired by plants is made in photosynthesis.
- **Blood glucose (4.5.3):** glycogen stored in the liver and muscles can be converted back to glucose.
- **Kidneys (4.5.3, Biology only):** urea from protein breakdown is removed in the urine.

## Exam traps

- "Respiration makes energy." It transfers energy.
- "Respiration is breathing." Breathing is ventilation of the lungs.
- "Plants respire only at night." They respire all the time.
- Carbon dioxide in the muscle anaerobic equation.
- "Anaerobic respiration releases no energy." Much less, not none.
- Percentage increase divided by the new value.
- Oxygen debt calculated without subtracting resting uptake (Higher tier only).
- Glycogen confused with glucagon.

## Self-test

1. Why is respiration described as exothermic?
2. Give three uses of energy from respiration.
3. Give the word equation for anaerobic respiration in muscles.
4. What are the products of fermentation?
5. Why does anaerobic respiration transfer less energy than aerobic?
6. Why does heart rate increase during exercise?
7. When do muscles respire anaerobically?
8. Why do muscles become fatigued?
9. (Higher tier only) What happens to lactic acid in the liver?
10. (Higher tier only) Define oxygen debt.
11. What are the small units used to build a lipid?
12. What is formed when excess proteins are broken down?

### Answers

1. It transfers energy to the surroundings.
2. Building larger molecules; movement; keeping warm.
3. glucose → lactic acid.
4. Ethanol and carbon dioxide.
5. The oxidation of glucose is incomplete.
6. To supply the muscles with more oxygenated blood (and remove carbon dioxide).
7. When insufficient oxygen is supplied to meet the demand for energy.
8. During long periods of vigorous activity, lactic acid builds up and they stop contracting efficiently.
9. It is converted back into glucose.
10. The extra oxygen the body needs after exercise to react with accumulated lactic acid and remove it from cells.
11. One glycerol and three fatty acids.
12. Urea.
