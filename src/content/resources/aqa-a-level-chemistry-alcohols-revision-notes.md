## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary alcohol | The C bonded to –OH is bonded to one other carbon (methanol: none) |
| Secondary alcohol | The C bonded to –OH is bonded to two other carbons |
| Tertiary alcohol | The C bonded to –OH is bonded to three other carbons |
| Hydration | Addition of water (steam) across C=C to make an alcohol |
| Fermentation | Conversion of glucose to ethanol and CO₂ by yeast, without air |
| Biofuel | A fuel made from biological material (biomass) that has been grown recently and can be regrown |
| Carbon neutral | No net emission of CO₂ to the atmosphere |
| Elimination (dehydration) | Removal of H₂O from an alcohol to form an alkene |
| [O] | Oxygen supplied by the oxidising agent, allowed in oxidation equations |

## 3.3.5.1 Alcohol production

### Two industrial routes for ethanol

| | Hydration of ethene | Fermentation of glucose |
|---|---|---|
| Equation | CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ |
| Catalyst | Acid, e.g. phosphoric acid on a solid support | Enzymes in yeast |
| Conditions | Steam, about 300 °C, high pressure | Around 35 °C, anaerobic, aqueous |
| Raw material | Ethene from crude oil (finite) | Sugar from crops (renewable) |
| Product | Fairly pure ethanol; unreacted ethene recycled | Aqueous ethanol; needs fractional distillation |

### Justify the fermentation conditions

- **Temperature**: too cold → slow; too hot → yeast enzymes denature. About 35 °C is a compromise.
- **No air**: oxygen would oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid, and yeast would respire aerobically to CO₂ and
  water.
- **Separation**: fractional distillation (ethanol boils at about 78 °C), then the ethanol can be used as a
  biofuel.

### Hydration mechanism in three arrows (plus loss of H⁺)

```
1  C=C electrons → H⁺            gives carbocation
2  lone pair on O of H₂O → C⁺     gives R–OH₂⁺
3  O–H bond electrons → O         releases H⁺ (catalyst back)
```

With an unsymmetrical alkene, the more stable carbocation (tertiary > secondary > primary) gives the major
alcohol. Carbocation stability is explained in [alkenes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alkenes/).

**Worked reminder.** But-1-ene + steam: H⁺ adds to C1, giving the secondary carbocation on C2. Major product:
butan-2-ol. Minor product (via the primary carbocation): butan-1-ol. But-2-ene is symmetrical, so it gives
butan-2-ol only.

### Carbon-neutral equations: method in steps

1. Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (6 CO₂ in).
2. Fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (2 out).
3. Combustion: 2C₂H₅OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O (4 out).
4. Total 6 in and 6 out, so "carbon neutral" by these equations alone.
5. Not valid: fossil fuels burned for farm machinery, fertiliser production, distillation and transport.

### Biofuel decisions: points to weigh

- Renewable; saves crude oil; can cut net CO₂.
- Land diverted from food (food prices: ethical); deforestation and habitat loss; fertiliser run-off; water use.

## 3.3.5.2 Oxidation of alcohols

**Reagent:** acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (K₂Cr₂O₇ / H₂SO₄). **Colour change if oxidation happens:**
orange → green (Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺).

| Alcohol | Product | Example equation |
|---|---|---|
| Primary (distil) | Aldehyde | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O |
| Primary (reflux, excess oxidant) | Carboxylic acid | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O |
| Secondary | Ketone | CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₂CH₃ + H₂O |
| Tertiary | No reaction (stays orange) | — |

### Must-know distinctions

- **Distil vs reflux.** Distil off the aldehyde as it forms (alcohol in excess, gentle heat): the aldehyde has
  no hydrogen bonding between molecules, so it boils lowest and escapes before further oxidation. Reflux with
  excess dichromate: vapour returns to the flask until all is oxidised to the acid.
- **Why tertiary alcohols resist oxidation.** No H atom on the carbon bonded to –OH.
- **Aldehyde vs ketone.** An aldehyde has C=O at the end of the chain (–CHO); a ketone has C=O between two
  carbons. Only the aldehyde is easily oxidised further.

### Method in steps: predicting an oxidation product

1. Find the carbon bonded to –OH and count the carbons joined to it.
2. Primary: keep the chain length; –CH₂OH becomes –CHO (distil) or –COOH (reflux). Name it -al or -oic acid.
3. Secondary: –CH(OH)– becomes –CO–. Name it -one, keeping the same position number.
4. Tertiary: no product; the dichromate stays orange.
5. Balance with [O]: one [O] per step; each step to an aldehyde or ketone also makes H₂O.

### Tests for aldehydes

| Reagent | Warm with aldehyde | Warm with ketone |
|---|---|---|
| Tollens' ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺) | Silver mirror | No change |
| Fehling's (blue, Cu²⁺) | Red precipitate | Stays blue |

Warm in a water bath. The aldehyde is oxidised: RCHO + [O] → RCOOH.

## 3.3.5.3 Elimination

**Reagent and conditions:** heat with concentrated sulfuric acid or concentrated phosphoric acid.

**Mechanism:** (1) lone pair on O takes H⁺ → R–OH₂⁺; (2) C–O breaks, H₂O leaves → carbocation; (3) C–H
electrons on the adjacent carbon form C=C, H⁺ released.

**Counting products:** list every carbon next to the C–OH carbon that carries an H. Each different one gives
a different position of C=C; then check each alkene for E/Z isomers.

- Propan-1-ol or propan-2-ol: propene only.
- 2-methylbutan-2-ol: 2-methylbut-1-ene and 2-methylbut-2-ene (no E/Z, because one carbon of the double bond
  carries two methyl groups).

**Use:** ethanol from fermentation → ethene → poly(ethene), making addition polymers without crude oil.

## Required practical 5: distillation of a product

Cyclohexene from cyclohexanol (suggested in the specification):

1. Heat cyclohexanol with concentrated phosphoric acid; distil off the crude product.
2. Separating funnel: shake with sodium carbonate solution (release CO₂ pressure), discard aqueous layer, wash
   with water.
3. Dry with anhydrous CaCl₂ or MgSO₄ until clear; decant.
4. Redistil; collect the fraction near the boiling point of cyclohexene.

Apparatus points: anti-bumping granules; thermometer bulb at the side arm; water in at the bottom of the
condenser; never heat a sealed system; use an electric heater, not a flame, with flammable organic liquids.

## Quick self-test

1. Classify 2-methylpropan-1-ol.
2. Give the conditions for the industrial hydration of ethene.
3. Write the equation for fermentation of glucose.
4. Why must air be kept out of a fermentation vessel?
5. Calculate the maximum mass of ethanol from 90.0 g of glucose (Mr 180.0; ethanol 46.0).
6. Write an equation, using [O], for oxidation of propan-2-ol.
7. State the colour change when a primary alcohol is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI).
8. Name the product of warming butan-1-ol under reflux with excess acidified dichromate.
9. What is seen when propanone is warmed with Fehling's solution?
10. Name the alkenes from pentan-3-ol and concentrated sulfuric acid.
11. 6.00 g of cyclohexanol (Mr 100.0) gives 3.28 g of cyclohexene (Mr 82.0). Find the percentage yield.
12. Give one reason why bioethanol is not truly carbon neutral.

### Answers

1. Primary: the C–OH carbon is joined to one other carbon.
2. Steam with a phosphoric acid catalyst on a solid support, about 300 °C, high pressure.
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
4. Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid (and yeast would respire aerobically).
5. 90.0 ÷ 180.0 = 0.500 mol glucose → 1.00 mol ethanol → **46.0 g**.
6. CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O
7. Orange to green.
8. Butanoic acid.
9. No change: the solution stays blue.
10. (E)-pent-2-ene and (Z)-pent-2-ene.
11. 0.0600 mol in, 3.28 ÷ 82.0 = 0.0400 mol out; 0.0400 ÷ 0.0600 × 100 = **66.7 %**.
12. Fossil fuels are burned to power farm machinery, make fertiliser, distil the ethanol or transport it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "high temperature" for fermentation, or forgetting that it must be anaerobic.
- Leaving yeast or enzymes out of the conditions.
- Stating that fermented ethanol is carbon neutral without the counter-argument when asked to evaluate.
- Starting the hydration curly arrow at H⁺ instead of at the C=C bond.
- Forgetting to show H⁺ being regenerated, so the acid is not shown as a catalyst.
- Giving "reflux" for making an aldehyde.
- Writing an aldehyde group as –COH instead of –CHO.
- Saying the dichromate "turns green" with a tertiary alcohol.
- Naming only one but-2-ene or pent-2-ene when E and Z both form.
- Writing "white" or "brown" for the Fehling's precipitate.
