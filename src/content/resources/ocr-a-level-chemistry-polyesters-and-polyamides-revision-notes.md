## Key definitions

- **Condensation polymerisation:** monomers join with the loss of a small molecule, H₂O or HCl, at every link.
- **Addition polymerisation:** monomers with C=C join as the double bond opens; the polymer is the only product (4.1.3(j)).
- **Polyester:** a condensation polymer whose monomer units are joined by ester links, –COO–.
- **Polyamide:** a condensation polymer whose monomer units are joined by amide links, –CONH–. Each link is a secondary amide.
- **Repeat unit:** the smallest section of chain that, repeated, gives the whole polymer. Bonds pass out through the brackets at each end.
- **Hydrolysis:** the reverse of condensation, in which water splits each link; here it is carried out in hot aqueous acid or alkali.

The specification states you will not be expected to recall the structures of synthetic polyesters and polyamides or their monomers. Questions give you the monomers or a section of chain, so the skill being tested is applying the rules below to structures you have not seen before. Practise on unfamiliar examples rather than learning named polymers.

## 6.2.3(a): Which monomers make which link

| Monomer pair or single monomer | Link | Small molecule lost |
|---|---|---|
| Dicarboxylic acid + diol | ester | H₂O |
| Diacyl chloride + diol | ester | HCl |
| Hydroxycarboxylic acid (one monomer) | ester | H₂O |
| Dicarboxylic acid + diamine | amide | H₂O |
| Diacyl chloride + diamine | amide | HCl |
| Aminocarboxylic acid (one monomer) | amide | H₂O |

Two reminders from 6.1.3(f):

- Acyl chlorides react much more readily than carboxylic acids.
- Phenols are not readily esterified by carboxylic acids. A diol with –OH groups on a benzene ring needs a diacyl chloride.

### Method in steps: repeat unit from monomers

1. Decide the link from the groups present: –COOH or –COCl with –OH gives ester; with –NH₂ gives amide.
2. For two monomers, write one of each side by side, acid-type monomer first.
3. Remove –OH (or –Cl) from each acyl group and H from each –OH or –NH₂.
4. Join the pieces through the new link and leave an open bond at each end.
5. Put square brackets round the unit, with a bond through each bracket, and add n.
6. Count the small molecules: for n of each of two monomers, 2n − 1, often written 2n. For one monomer, n − 1.

Reminder: ethanedioic acid, HOOC–COOH, with pentane-1,5-diamine, H₂N–(CH₂)₅–NH₂, gives –[CO–CO–NH–(CH₂)₅–NH]–, losing water.

## 6.2.3(b): Hydrolysis products

Both kinds of hydrolysis are carried out by heating under reflux.

| Link | Hot aqueous acid (e.g. HCl(aq)) | Hot aqueous alkali (e.g. NaOH(aq)) |
|---|---|---|
| Ester, –COO– | –COOH + –OH | –COO⁻ Na⁺ + –OH |
| Amide, –CONH– | –COOH + –NH₃⁺ Cl⁻ | –COO⁻ Na⁺ + –NH₂ |

Logic to remember rather than memorise:

- Acid protonates the basic –NH₂, giving an ammonium salt. The –COOH stays as the acid.
- Alkali deprotonates the –COOH, giving a carboxylate salt. The –NH₂ stays as the amine; the –OH of an alcohol is not affected.
- The –OH from a diol is the same in both conditions.
- A phenol –OH freed by hydrolysis is weakly acidic, so excess alkali turns it into –O⁻ Na⁺ (6.1.1(h)).

### Method in steps: hydrolysis products

1. Find every ester or amide link in the backbone.
2. Split each link between the C=O carbon and the O or N.
3. Give –OH to every acyl end; give H to every O or N end.
4. Adjust for conditions: in acid, –NH₂ becomes –NH₃⁺ (with Cl⁻ if HCl is used); in alkali, –COOH becomes –COO⁻ Na⁺.
5. Amount of NaOH: one mole per ester or amide link, so two per repeat unit of a diacid-based polymer.

### Worked reminder

A polyester made from 3-hydroxypropanoic acid has repeat unit –[O–CH₂–CH₂–CO]–, C₃H₄O₂, Mr 72.0 (C 12.0, H 1.0, O 16.0).

- A chain of Mr 36 000 holds 36 000 ÷ 72.0 = **500** repeat units.
- Hot NaOH(aq) gives **HO–CH₂–CH₂–COO⁻ Na⁺**, sodium 3-hydroxypropanoate.
- Hot HCl(aq) gives **HO–CH₂–CH₂–COOH**, the monomer itself.

## 6.2.3(c): Predicting from monomers and chains

### 6.2.3(c)(iii): Spot the type

| Clue | Type |
|---|---|
| Monomer has C=C; chain backbone is all carbon | addition |
| Each monomer has two groups from –COOH, –COCl, –OH, –NH₂ | condensation |
| –COO– or –CONH– sits in the backbone | condensation |
| –COO– or –CONH– only in side groups | still addition |

### Method in steps: monomers from a section of chain

1. Check the backbone. All carbon means addition; go to step 5.
2. Cut every ester or amide link between C=O and O or N.
3. Collect the pieces. One kind of piece means one monomer; two alternating kinds means two monomers.
4. Add –OH (or –Cl for an acyl chloride) to each acyl end and H to each O or N end.
5. For addition: take two backbone carbons as the repeat unit, put C=C between them and remove the open bonds.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Addition vs condensation:** addition gives one product only; condensation also gives H₂O or HCl.
- **H₂O vs HCl:** water from –COOH; hydrogen chloride from –COCl.
- **Acid vs alkaline hydrolysis of a polyamide:** acid gives –COOH and –NH₃⁺; alkali gives –COO⁻ and –NH₂.
- **Acid vs alkaline hydrolysis of a polyester:** acid gives –COOH; alkali gives –COO⁻. The alcohol is the same either way.
- **Repeat unit vs monomer:** the repeat unit has lost the atoms of the small molecule; the monomer has not.
- **Two monomers vs one:** a diacid–diol or diacid–diamine polymer has one residue of each in its repeat unit; a single-monomer polymer has one residue.

## Quick self-test

1. ClOC–(CH₂)₃–COCl polymerises with a diol. Which small molecule forms?
2. Give the type of polymerisation for CH₂=C(CH₃)–CH₂OH, and its repeat unit.
3. Give the repeat unit formed from ethanedioic acid and pentane-1,5-diamine, and its Mr (C 12.0, H 1.0, O 16.0, N 14.0).
4. How many moles of water form, approximately, when 0.500 mol of the repeat unit in question 3 is made from these monomers? What mass is this (Mr H₂O 18.0)?
5. Give the hydrolysis products of the polymer in question 3 with hot dilute HCl(aq).
6. Give the products with hot NaOH(aq) for the same polymer.
7. A section reads –NH–CH₂–C₆H₄–CH₂–NH–CO–(CH₂)₂–CO–. Give the two monomers.
8. Why must benzene-1,3-diol be polymerised with a diacyl chloride rather than a dicarboxylic acid?
9. Is –CH₂–CH(CONH₂)–CH₂–CH(CONH₂)– a polyamide? Explain.
10. How many moles of NaOH hydrolyse 0.500 mol of the repeat unit in question 3?

### Answers

1. **HCl**.
2. **Addition** (it has C=C and only one other reactive group); repeat unit **–[CH₂–C(CH₃)(CH₂OH)]–**.
3. **–[CO–CO–NH–(CH₂)₅–NH]–**, C₇H₁₂N₂O₂, **Mr 156.0**.
4. Two water molecules per repeat unit: **1.00 mol**, **18.0 g**.
5. **HOOC–COOH** and **Cl⁻ ⁺H₃N–(CH₂)₅–NH₃⁺ Cl⁻**.
6. **NaOOC–COONa** (sodium ethanedioate) and **H₂N–(CH₂)₅–NH₂**.
7. **H₂N–CH₂–C₆H₄–CH₂–NH₂** and **HOOC–(CH₂)₂–COOH**, butanedioic acid (or its diacyl chloride).
8. Both –OH groups are on the ring, so they are phenolic. A carboxylic acid will **not readily esterify a phenol**; an acyl chloride will.
9. **No.** The backbone is all carbon and the amide groups are side groups. It is an addition polymer of CH₂=CH–CONH₂.
10. Two amide links per repeat unit: **1.00 mol**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing H₂O as the by-product when the monomer is a diacyl chloride.
- Drawing a repeat unit with –H or –OH still on its ends, or with no bonds through the brackets.
- Putting two residues of the same monomer in the repeat unit of a two-monomer polymer.
- Splitting an ester link between the O and the carbon chain, which gives wrong monomers.
- Giving the free diamine, not its ammonium salt, as a product of acid hydrolysis.
- Giving the carboxylic acid, not its salt, as a product of alkaline hydrolysis.
- Classifying a polymer by side groups instead of by the backbone.
- Showing an amide link without the H on the nitrogen.
- Using one mole of NaOH per repeat unit when there are two links in it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between addition and condensation polymerisation?**
In addition polymerisation, monomers with a C=C bond join as the double bond opens and the polymer is the only product. In condensation polymerisation, a small molecule such as H₂O or HCl is lost at every link formed between monomers.

**What are the products of acid and alkaline hydrolysis of a polyamide?**
Hot aqueous acid gives a carboxylic acid, –COOH, and an ammonium salt, –NH₃⁺ Cl⁻, because the acid protonates the amine group. Hot aqueous alkali gives a carboxylate salt, –COO⁻ Na⁺, and the free amine, –NH₂.

**How do you tell if a polymer is an addition or condensation polymer from its chain?**
Look at the backbone. If –COO– or –CONH– links sit in the backbone, it is a condensation polymer; if the backbone is all carbon, it is an addition polymer even when ester or amide groups appear in the side groups.

**Why does a diol with –OH groups on a benzene ring need a diacyl chloride?**
Those –OH groups behave as phenols, and phenols are not readily esterified by carboxylic acids. Acyl chlorides are far more reactive, so a diacyl chloride is used to make the polyester.
