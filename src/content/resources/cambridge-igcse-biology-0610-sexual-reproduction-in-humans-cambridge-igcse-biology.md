This guide covers sub-topic **16.4 Sexual reproduction in humans** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 8 are Core. Outcomes 9 and 10, on exchange across the placenta and substances that cross it, are Supplement content and are marked **Extended only**.

The [reproduction study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-reproduction/) gives the function tables in brief. This page follows the journey of the gametes through both systems, explains each adaptation in the depth that "explain" questions need, and builds full answers on gametes and on the placenta. Afterwards, use the [revision notes for human reproduction](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-humans-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and the [practice questions for human reproduction](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexual-reproduction-in-humans-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## The male reproductive system

Follow the sperm from where they are made to where they leave the body.

| Order | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Testes | produce sperm; also produce testosterone (sub-topic 16.5) |
| – | Scrotum | sac of skin that holds the testes outside the body, where the temperature is slightly lower, suiting sperm production |
| 2 | Sperm ducts | carry sperm from the testes towards the urethra |
| 3 | Prostate gland | adds fluid to the sperm; the fluid and sperm together form semen, in which sperm can swim |
| 4 | Urethra | carries semen out of the body through the penis; also carries urine, but not at the same time |
| 5 | Penis | becomes erect so that semen can be placed in the vagina |

On a diagram, the **urethra** runs through the penis; the **sperm ducts** loop up and over from the testes to join it. Do not confuse the urethra with the **ureters**, which carry urine from the kidneys to the bladder.

## The female reproductive system

Follow the egg cell from where it is released.

| Order | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ovaries | produce and release egg cells; also produce oestrogen and progesterone (sub-topic 16.5) |
| 2 | Oviducts | carry the egg cell from the ovary to the uterus; **fertilisation happens here** |
| 3 | Uterus | the embryo implants in its lining; the fetus develops here |
| 4 | Cervix | a ring of muscle at the neck of the uterus; holds the fetus in place in pregnancy and widens at birth |
| 5 | Vagina | receives the penis and semen during intercourse; the birth canal |

Exam diagrams are usually a front view. The **oviducts** are the two narrow tubes leading from each side of the uterus towards the ovaries.

## Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the **fusion of the nuclei** from a male gamete (**sperm**) and a female gamete (**egg cell**). It normally takes place in an **oviduct**. Semen is placed in the vagina, and sperm swim through the cervix and uterus into the oviducts, where one may meet an egg.

## Gametes and their adaptations

### Sperm

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Flagellum** | a tail that beats so the sperm can swim towards the egg |
| Many **mitochondria** | carry out aerobic respiration, releasing the energy the flagellum needs to move |
| **Enzymes in the acrosome** | the acrosome, at the tip of the head, contains enzymes that digest a way through the jelly coat into the egg |

### Egg cell

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Energy stores** in the cytoplasm | supply energy for the zygote's early cell divisions, before the embryo implants and receives nutrients from the mother |
| **Jelly coat that changes at fertilisation** | once one sperm has entered, the coat changes so that no more sperm can enter |

### Worked example 1: comparing gametes

*Compare male and female gametes in terms of size, structure, motility and numbers.* **[4]**

> Sperm are much **smaller** than egg cells [1]. A sperm has a **flagellum** and an acrosome, while an egg cell has no flagellum but has energy stores and a jelly coat [1]. Sperm are **motile** and swim; egg cells are **not motile** and are moved along the oviduct [1]. Sperm are produced in **very large numbers**, whereas usually only **one egg cell** is released in each menstrual cycle [1].

Each sentence states **both** gametes. A list of sperm features alone is a description, not a comparison, and scores poorly. The syllabus names four headings, so use all four.

## From zygote to fetus

1. The **zygote** divides repeatedly to form an **embryo**, a ball of cells.
2. The embryo moves down the oviduct to the uterus.
3. The embryo **implants** into the thick lining of the uterus.
4. The **placenta** and **umbilical cord** develop, and the embryo grows into a **fetus**.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Placenta | the organ where substances are exchanged between the mother's blood and the fetus's blood |
| Umbilical cord | joins the fetus to the placenta; contains blood vessels that carry the fetus's blood to and from the placenta |
| Amniotic sac | the membrane that encloses the fetus and holds the amniotic fluid |
| Amniotic fluid | cushions and protects the fetus from bumps and supports it |

## The placenta and umbilical cord in exchange (Extended only)

In the placenta, the **mother's blood** and the **fetus's blood** flow very close together but **do not mix**. Substances move between them, mainly by **diffusion**, across a thin barrier.

| From mother to fetus | From fetus to mother |
|---|---|
| oxygen | carbon dioxide |
| dissolved nutrients: glucose, amino acids | excretory products such as urea |
| water and mineral ions | |

The **umbilical cord** carries the fetus's blood, containing carbon dioxide and urea, to the placenta, and carries blood that has gained oxygen and dissolved nutrients back to the fetus.

### Worked example 2: an "explain" answer on exchange

*Explain how the fetus obtains oxygen and gets rid of carbon dioxide.* **[3]**

> In the placenta the mother's blood and the fetus's blood are close together but do not mix [1]. Oxygen diffuses from the mother's blood into the fetus's blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses from the fetus's blood into the mother's blood [1]. Blood vessels in the umbilical cord carry the oxygenated blood to the fetus and carry carbon dioxide from the fetus to the placenta [1].

### Substances that should not cross (Extended only)

The barrier in the placenta does not stop everything. Some **pathogens**, such as the rubella virus and HIV, and some **toxins**, such as nicotine from smoking and alcohol, can cross the placenta and affect the development of the fetus. Questions will name the pathogen or toxin; your job is to state that it can **cross the placenta into the fetus's blood**.

## Common mistakes

- Saying sperm are made in the sperm ducts or the prostate gland. They are made in the **testes**.
- Confusing **urethra** with **ureter**, or **uterus** with **ovary**.
- Placing fertilisation in the uterus. It normally happens in the **oviduct**.
- Writing that the acrosome "breaks" the egg. Its **enzymes digest** a way in.
- Saying mitochondria "give energy". They **release** energy by **respiration**.
- Describing only one gamete in a "compare" question.
- (Extended) Saying the bloods mix in the placenta, or that the umbilical cord "feeds" the fetus without mentioning blood.
- (Extended) Saying amniotic fluid supplies nutrients. It protects; the **placenta** supplies nutrients.

## Exam technique

- **Identify**: read the diagram's orientation first, then locate the urethra or uterus as a landmark.
- **State the function**: one precise phrase per part.
- **Explain** an adaptation: feature + how it helps fertilisation or early development.
- **Compare**: use comparative words (smaller, larger, more) and mention both gametes in each point.
- **Describe** the placenta's function (Extended): name substances **and** their direction of movement.
