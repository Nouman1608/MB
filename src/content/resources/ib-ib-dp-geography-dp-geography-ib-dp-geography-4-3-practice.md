## Section A

**1.** Define a composite index of vulnerability. **[1]**

**2.** Outline one reason why more women than men died in many villages affected by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami. **[2]**

**3.** A disaster causes losses of 12 billion in a country with a GDP of 300 billion. Calculate the loss as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**

**4.** Outline why renters and informal settlers may recover more slowly after a disaster. **[2]**

**5.** State one local and one distant impact of the 2010 eruption of Eyjafjallajokull. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows invented vulnerability scores (0 = least vulnerable, 10 = most vulnerable) for two districts.

| Indicator | District M | District N |
|---|---|---|
| Unreinforced housing | 9 | 4 |
| Poverty | 6 | 5 |
| Population over 70 | 2 | 7 |
| Distance to hospital | 7 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean vulnerability score for each district. **[2]**

**(b)** A planner decides housing should count twice as much as each other indicator. Calculate the new weighted mean for each district. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain one limitation of using a composite index to compare the districts. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the time of day at which an earthquake strikes can affect who is killed. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two reasons why the 2010 eruption of Merapi caused many more deaths than the 2010 eruption of Eyjafjallajokull. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why economic losses should be compared relative to GDP as well as in absolute terms. **[3]**

## Section C

**10.** With reference to two contrasting geophysical hazard events, examine why their impacts differed. **[10]**

**11.** "Poverty is the single most important cause of vulnerability to geophysical hazards." To what extent do you agree? **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A single measure that combines several indicators of vulnerability, such as housing quality, poverty and access to services, into one score [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Many women were at home or on the shore with children when the waves arrived, while many men were at sea or in fields [1], so women were more exposed and also delayed by trying to save children and older relatives (accept: fewer women had learned to swim or climb) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 12 / 300 x 100 = **4 per cent** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** They are less likely to have insurance or savings [1], and may be unable to prove ownership, so they can be excluded from reconstruction aid that is allocated to property owners [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Local: meltwater floods, ash on farmland or evacuation of residents [1]. Distant: closure of much of European airspace and cancelled flights, or disrupted trade such as air-freighted produce [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** District M: (9 + 6 + 2 + 7) / 4 = 24 / 4 = **6.0** [1]. District N: (4 + 5 + 7 + 3) / 4 = 19 / 4 = **4.75** [1].

**(b)** District M: (9 x 2 + 6 + 2 + 7) / 5 = 33 / 5 = **6.6** [1]. District N: (4 x 2 + 5 + 7 + 3) / 5 = 23 / 5 = **4.6** [1].

**(c)** The result depends on which indicators are chosen and how they are weighted, which are value judgements [1]; averaging can hide a specific weakness, such as District N's large elderly population, which matters for evacuation planning [1]. **[6]**

**7.** People are in different places at different times of day [1]. A night-time earthquake catches people asleep at home, so deaths depend on housing quality; a daytime event catches children in schools and adults at work [1], so the collapse of schools or workplaces can kill particular groups, as when many schools collapsed in the 2005 Kashmir earthquake [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Exposure: Merapi's fertile slopes are densely populated, whereas few people live near Eyjafjallajokull [1], so far more people were within reach of the pyroclastic flows [1]. Livelihoods and perception: farmers with livestock on Merapi's slopes were reluctant to leave or returned to tend animals, and cultural ties to the volcano influenced decisions [1], whereas Iceland's sparse population was evacuated efficiently by well-resourced civil protection [1]. (Accept: different hazard types: pyroclastic flows at Merapi, mainly ash and meltwater at Eyjafjallajokull.) **[4]**

**9.** Wealthy countries have more valuable assets, so their absolute losses are often larger [1]. A smaller absolute loss can be a much larger share of a poorer country's output [1], so it causes slower recovery and greater dependence on aid; relative figures therefore show the true economic impact [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Indicative content (for example, Merapi and Eyjafjallajokull in 2010, or Haiti and Chile in 2010):

- **Description of contrasting impacts:** deaths, people affected, local versus distant economic effects [1] [1].
- **Physical explanations:** type of hazard (pyroclastic flows versus ash and meltwater), magnitude, distance to population [1] [1].
- **Exposure:** population density and settlement on hazardous slopes [1].
- **Vulnerability:** wealth, governance, building quality, livelihoods, perception and culture [1] [1].
- **Response:** effectiveness of monitoring, warning and evacuation [1].
- **Examination:** interaction between factors, for example Merapi's high vulnerability partly offset by an effective warning [1].
- **Conclusion:** differences reflect human factors more than the physical events alone [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Indicative content:

- **For:** poverty limits access to safe housing, insurance and savings [1]; it pushes people onto hazardous land [1]; poor countries have less capacity to monitor, warn and respond [1]; losses are a larger share of income [1].
- **Against:** gender, age and disability create vulnerability within all income groups [1]; governance and corruption matter: weak enforcement of building codes can kill even in middle-income countries [1]; perception and culture affect decisions, as at Merapi [1]; physical exposure matters: wealthy places can still be highly exposed [1].
- **Interaction:** poverty often works through other factors, such as housing and governance [1].
- **Conclusion:** poverty is a very important root cause but not the single cause; vulnerability is multi-dimensional [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* "To what extent" questions on vulnerability reward recognising that factors overlap. Use at least one example from within a community, not only comparisons between countries.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to change the divisor when weighting an index.
- Comparing absolute losses only.
- Describing an event's impacts without explaining the differences.
- Writing that vulnerability is "about money" alone.
