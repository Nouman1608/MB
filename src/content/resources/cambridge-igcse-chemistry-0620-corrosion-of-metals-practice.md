## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which substance is rust?

A: anhydrous iron(II) oxide

B: hydrated iron(III) oxide

C: iron(III) chloride

D: iron carbonate **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** conditions needed for iron to rust. **[2]**

**3.** A student says, "My aluminium bike frame has rusted." Explain why the word "rusted" is incorrect. **[1]**

**4.** Name a suitable barrier method for each object and give a reason for each choice.

**(a)** the hinge of a garden gate **[2]**

**(b)** a steel wire fence around a playing field **[2]**

**5.** Describe how a layer of grease prevents a steel tool from rusting. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State what is meant by *galvanising*. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Four identical iron nails were set up as shown and left for one week.

| Tube | Conditions | Rust after one week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | nail half covered in tap water, tube open | heavy rust |
| 2 | nail covered in boiled water with a layer of oil, tube open | none |
| 3 | nail in dry air, with anhydrous calcium chloride, tube stoppered | none |
| 4 | nail painted, then half covered in tap water, tube open | none |

**(a)** State which substance is missing from tube 2, and which is missing from tube 3. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the water in tube 2 is boiled **and** covered with oil. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the result in tube 4. **[2]**

**(d)** What do tubes 1, 2 and 3 together show about the conditions for rusting? **[1]**

**8.** A car body is painted to prevent rusting. After a minor accident, the paint on one door is scratched down to the steel.

**(a)** Predict what will happen at the scratch over the following weeks. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your prediction. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A steel roof sheet is galvanised.

**(a)** Explain how the zinc protects the steel while the coating is undamaged. **[1]**

**(b)** The sheet is scratched through the zinc. Explain, in terms of electron loss and the reactivity series, why the exposed steel does not rust. **[3]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the change that happens to the zinc. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Blocks of metal are bolted to the steel hull of a ship below the waterline.

**(a)** Name a metal that would be suitable for the blocks. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the blocks have to be replaced from time to time. **[2]**

**(c)** A crew member suggests using copper blocks because copper does not corrode easily. Explain why this would not protect the hull. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Iron nails were each wrapped tightly in a strip of a different metal and left in water for a week.

| Metal strip | Nail rusted? |
|---|---|
| zinc | no |
| magnesium | no |
| copper | yes |
| silver | yes |

Use the reactivity series to explain the pattern in these results. **[3]**

**12.** Describe and explain how barrier methods protect iron from rusting. Refer to **two** named methods in your answer. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Water [1] and oxygen [1].

**3.** Rusting is the corrosion of iron and steel only; aluminium corrodes but does not rust [1].

**4. (a)** Greasing / oiling [1]: the hinge moves, so paint would rub off; grease stays on and lubricates [1].

**(b)** Coating with plastic (or painting) [1]: a plastic coating is a tough, flexible, waterproof layer over a large length of wire / paint covers large areas cheaply [1].

**5.** The grease forms a layer over the steel surface [1] that excludes oxygen and water [1].

**6.** Coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc [1].

**7. (a)** Tube 2: oxygen (air) [1]. Tube 3: water [1].

**(b)** Boiling removes dissolved oxygen / air from the water [1]; the oil stops air dissolving back into the water [1].

**(c)** The paint forms a barrier [1] that keeps water and oxygen away from the iron, so it does not rust [1].

**(d)** Both water and oxygen are needed for rusting [1].

**8. (a)** The steel at the scratch will rust [1].

**(b)** The paint barrier is broken [1], so water and oxygen can reach the steel surface [1].

**9. (a)** The zinc acts as a barrier that keeps water and oxygen away from the steel [1].

**(b)** Zinc is more reactive than iron [1]. Zinc loses electrons more readily than iron [1], so the zinc is oxidised instead of the iron and the iron does not rust [1].

**(c)** Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1].

**10. (a)** Zinc or magnesium [1].

**(b)** The blocks corrode / are oxidised instead of the steel [1], so they gradually wear away and stop protecting the hull once used up [1].

**(c)** Copper is less reactive than iron [1], so it would not lose electrons in preference to iron; the iron in the hull would still be oxidised and rust [1].

**11.** Zinc and magnesium are more reactive than iron [1], so they lose electrons / corrode in preference to iron, giving sacrificial protection [1]. Copper and silver are less reactive than iron, so they cannot protect it, and the nail rusts [1].

**12.** Any two named methods, painting, greasing or coating with plastic [1] [1]. Each puts a layer over the surface of the iron [1] that excludes oxygen and/or water, so rusting cannot happen [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only water, or only air, as the condition for rusting.
- Writing "iron oxide" without "hydrated" and "(III)".
- Describing barrier methods without saying that they exclude oxygen or water.
- Choosing paint for moving parts such as chains and hinges.
- Explaining scratched galvanised steel as a "barrier" effect.
- Leaving out electron loss in the sacrificial protection explanation.
- Choosing a less reactive metal, such as copper, for sacrificial protection.
