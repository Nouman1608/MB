Condensed notes for outcomes 1.2.5 to 1.2.9 in Unit 1 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11), International AS content. For full worked examples, use the [correction of errors study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-correction-of-errors/); then test yourself with the [correction of errors practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-correction-of-errors-practice/).

## The four-question routine

For every error in a list, answer in this order:

```
1. Were equal amounts posted to the debit and credit sides?
      yes -> trial balance agrees, no suspense
      no  -> trial balance disagrees, correction uses suspense
2. What journal turns "what was done" into "what should have been done"?
3. Does the correction involve an income or expense account?
      yes -> profit changes; no -> profit unchanged
4. Does it change the control account, the list of balances, or both?
```

## Errors that leave the trial balance agreeing (1.2.5)

| Error | Typical correction pattern |
|---|---|
| Omission | Make the whole entry: Dr correct account, Cr correct account |
| Commission (right type, wrong account) | Dr correct account, Cr wrong account (e.g. between two customers) |
| Principle (wrong type of account) | Dr correct class, Cr wrong class (e.g. Dr Equipment, Cr Repairs) |
| Original entry (wrong amount both sides) | Post the difference only, in the original direction |
| Complete reversal | Twice the amount, in the correct direction |
| Compensating | Correct each error separately |
| Day book overcast or undercast (both postings from the same total) | Adjust the total in the general ledger account and the control account |

## Errors that make it disagree

| Error | Size of the difference |
|---|---|
| One side omitted | The amount |
| Both postings on the same side | Twice the amount |
| Different amounts on each side | The gap between them |
| Transposition on one side | A multiple of 9 |
| Casting error in one account | The size of the error |

## Suspense account (1.2.7)

- Open it on the **short** side: debits too high → credit suspense; credits too high → debit suspense.
- Each correction posts suspense on the side that **cancels** the original error.
- It must close to **nil**. If it does not, an error is still missing or a journal is wrong.
- Errors that leave the trial balance agreeing **never** touch suspense.

**Quick check:** debit-excess errors minus credit-excess errors = the opening difference.

## Statement of revised profit (1.2.8)

| Correction | Effect on profit |
|---|---|
| Income understated, now increased | Increase |
| Income overstated, now reduced | Decrease |
| Expense understated, now increased | Decrease |
| Expense overstated, now reduced | Increase |
| Between two statement of financial position accounts | None |
| Capital expenditure taken out of an expense | Increase (less any depreciation the question asks for) |
| Drawings taken out of an expense | Increase |

Revenue, returns and purchases corrections also change **gross profit**; other expense and income corrections change **profit for the year** only.

## Control accounts (1.2.9)

| Where the error was made | Which total is wrong |
|---|---|
| A day book total (posted to the control account and a general ledger account) | Control account (and that general ledger account); list unaffected |
| One document posted to a customer or supplier account | List of balances only |
| A document missing from the day book altogether | Both |
| Listing a balance wrongly (wrong figure, wrong side, left out) | List only |
| A general ledger entry not made (irrecoverable debt, contra) | Control account (and the other general ledger account); list unaffected |
| Posting to the wrong personal account | Neither total; journal between the two personal accounts |

A balance on the **wrong side** of the list changes the total by **twice** the balance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Journal (the journal proper) | Book of prime entry for corrections and transfers, with a narrative for each |
| Narrative | Short explanation under a journal entry |
| Suspense account | Temporary account holding a trial balance difference until the errors are found |
| Statement of revised profit | Original profit adjusted for each correction that affects income or expenses |
| Casting | Adding up a column; overcast means too high, undercast means too low |
| Contra entry | Set-off between a customer's and supplier's account for the same business |

## Exam traps

- A double debit is corrected with **twice** the amount from suspense, not the original amount.
- A complete reversal needs **twice** the amount but **no** suspense.
- A day book casting error does **not** use suspense when both postings come from the same total.
- Do not adjust profit for corrections between receivables, payables, bank or suspense.
- Show increases and decreases separately, then the net change, so a reversed sign can still earn method marks.
- If shop fittings or equipment were debited to an expense, only adjust depreciation if a rate is given.

## Quick self-test

1. Insurance of 480 was debited to the insurance account but not credited to bank. Does the trial balance agree?
2. Give the journal to correct this: a payment of 250 to supplier Hollis was debited to supplier Hollins.
3. A sale of 920 to a customer was credited to the customer's account and debited to revenue. Give the journal.
4. A trial balance has credits 380 higher than debits. Which side is the suspense balance on?
5. Wages of 2,640 were debited to wages as 2,460; bank was correct. Give the journal.
6. Office equipment of 1,700 was debited to office expenses. State the effect on profit of correcting it.
7. The purchases day book was undercast by 300. Which accounts are wrong, and is suspense used?
8. A customer's balance of 410 was left out of the list. Which total is corrected?
9. Profit was 22,000. Discount allowed of 150 was never recorded. Find revised profit.
10. A suspense account opened with a debit of 560 and the only correction found is a credit of 400. What does this tell you?
11. A credit balance of 90 on a supplier's account was listed as a debit. By how much does the list total change?
12. Why does a correction between trade receivables and bank not affect profit?

### Answers

1. No: only one side was posted, so debits exceed credits by 480.
2. Dr Hollis 250, Cr Hollins 250 (error of commission; no suspense).
3. Dr Trade receivables 1,840, Cr Revenue 1,840 (complete reversal; twice the amount).
4. Debit side (the debit column was short).
5. Dr Wages 180, Cr Suspense 180.
6. Office expenses fall by 1,700, so profit increases by 1,700 (less any depreciation if a rate is given).
7. Purchases and the trade payables control account are both understated by 300; no suspense.
8. The list of balances only: add 410.
9. 22,000 − 150 = 21,850.
10. Another error worth 160 remains, or one of the journals is wrong; the account has not cleared.
11. It falls from +90 to −90 within the list, a change of 180.
12. Both are statement of financial position accounts; no income or expense is involved.
