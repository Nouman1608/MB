## Section A

**1.** State the test and positive result for each gas.

**(a)** hydrogen **[1]**

**(b)** ammonia **[1]**

**(c)** sulfur dioxide **[1]**

**2.** A solution is acidified with dilute nitric acid and aqueous silver nitrate is added. A yellow precipitate forms. Which ion is present?

A: bromide

B: chloride

C: iodide

D: sulfate

**[1]**

**3.** State the flame colour given by:

**(a)** lithium ions **[1]**

**(b)** copper(II) ions **[1]**

**4.** Name the cation that gives a red-brown precipitate with aqueous ammonia. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the litmus paper used to test for chlorine must be damp. **[1]**

**6.** A white solid gives no colour change with acidified potassium manganate(VII), but gives effervescence with dilute hydrochloric acid. The gas turns limewater milky. Name the anion present and state which anion has been ruled out by the first test. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student tests solution Y. The results are shown.

| Test | Observation |
|---|---|
| Add aqueous sodium hydroxide, then excess | green precipitate that dissolves in excess |
| Add aqueous ammonia, then excess | green precipitate that does not dissolve in excess |
| Acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous silver nitrate | white precipitate |

**(a)** Identify the cation in Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the anion in Y. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the compound in Y. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how to show that a solution contains sulfate ions. Explain why the reagents are added in the order you give. **[3]**

**9.** A student wants to test a solution for nitrate ions, but the solution may also contain ammonium ions.

**(a)** Explain why ammonium ions could give a false positive result. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the student should carry out the test so that the result is reliable. **[2]**

**10.** Two solutions contain calcium nitrate and zinc nitrate.

**(a)** Describe how aqueous sodium hydroxide could be used to tell them apart. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe a different test that would confirm the calcium ion. **[1]**

**11.** Complete the table of observations for the reactions of aqueous copper(II) sulfate.

| Reagent added | Few drops | Excess |
|---|---|---|
| aqueous sodium hydroxide | (a) | (b) |
| aqueous ammonia | (c) | (d) |

**[4]**

**12.** Solid Z gives a light green flame. A solution of Z, acidified with dilute nitric acid, gives a cream precipitate with aqueous silver nitrate.

**(a)** Name the cation in Z. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the anion in Z. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the formula of Z. **[1]**

**13.** Iron(II) ions and iron(III) ions are both present in different bottles of old laboratory solution. Describe how aqueous sodium hydroxide distinguishes them, including what happens to the iron(II) precipitate on standing. **[3]**

**14.** Ammonium carbonate is a white solid. A student carries out two tests on it.

**(a)** Describe a test that shows the presence of ammonium ions, including the test for the gas produced. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe a test that shows the presence of carbonate ions, including the test for the gas produced. **[2]**

**(c)** The student also warms the solid with aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil and finds that ammonia is produced. Explain why this does **not** show that nitrate ions are present. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Lighted splint: pops [1]

**(b)** Damp red litmus paper: turns blue [1]

**(c)** Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII): purple to colourless [1]

**[3]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Red [1]

**(b)** Blue-green [1]

**[2]**

**4.** Iron(III), Fe³⁺ [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The gas must dissolve in water on the paper to act on (bleach) the litmus [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Carbonate [1]; sulfite has been ruled out [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Chromium(III), Cr³⁺ [1]

**(b)** Chloride, Cl⁻ [1]

**(c)** Chromium(III) chloride [1]

**[3]**

**8.** Add dilute nitric acid, then aqueous barium nitrate [1]; a white precipitate shows sulfate [1]. The acid is added first to remove carbonate ions, which would also give a white precipitate with barium ions [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Ammonium ions also give ammonia when warmed with sodium hydroxide [1].

**(b)** Warm the solution with aqueous sodium hydroxide first and test for ammonia; if none (or once it is no longer given off) [1], add aluminium foil and warm again; ammonia now shows nitrate [1].

**[3]**

**10. (a)** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide a little at a time: both give a white precipitate [1]. Add excess: the zinc precipitate dissolves to give a colourless solution [1]; the calcium precipitate does not dissolve [1].

**(b)** Flame test: orange-red flame [1].

**[4]**

**11. (a)** light blue precipitate [1]

**(b)** light blue precipitate, does not dissolve / insoluble in excess [1]

**(c)** light blue precipitate [1]

**(d)** precipitate dissolves, giving a dark blue solution [1]

**[4]**

**12. (a)** Barium, Ba²⁺ [1]

**(b)** Bromide, Br⁻ [1]

**(c)** BaBr₂ [1].

**[3]**

**13.** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide: iron(II) gives a green precipitate [1]; iron(III) gives a red-brown precipitate [1]. Both are insoluble in excess; the iron(II) precipitate turns brown near the surface on standing [1]. **[3]**

**14. (a)** Warm the solid with aqueous sodium hydroxide [1]; ammonia is given off, which turns damp red litmus paper blue [1].

**(b)** Add dilute acid: effervescence [1]; the gas turns limewater milky, showing carbon dioxide [1].

**(c)** The ammonium ions in the solid give ammonia with sodium hydroxide on warming, whether or not nitrate is present [1].

**[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "white solution" for a precipitate.
- Describing "carbon dioxide given off" with no limewater result.
- Leaving out what happens in excess.
- Using hydrochloric or sulfuric acid to acidify before the halide or sulfate test.
- Mixing up flame colours for lithium (red) and calcium (orange-red).
- Forgetting that the litmus must be damp.
