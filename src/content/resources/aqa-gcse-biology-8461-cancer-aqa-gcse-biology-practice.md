## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe what cancer is. **[2]**

**2.** State whether each statement applies to benign tumours, malignant tumours or both.

**(a)** Made of abnormal cells. **[1]**
**(b)** Forms secondary tumours. **[1]**
**(c)** Usually contained within a membrane. **[1]**
**(d)** Can press on nearby organs as it grows. **[1]**

**3.** Name the transport system that carries malignant tumour cells to other parts of the body. **[1]**

**4.** A patient has a benign tumour inside the skull. Explain why doctors may still want to remove it, even though it is not cancer. **[2]**

**5.** In a model of tumour growth (invented figures), one abnormal cell divides every 4 days and every new cell keeps dividing.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 32 days. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many days it takes for the number of cells to first exceed 1000. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** Explain how a change in a single cell can lead to a tumour. **[3]**

**7.** A woman's mother and grandmother both developed breast cancer. Suggest why her doctor may advise her to have regular checks. **[2]**

**8.** A study followed 25 000 people living in homes with high levels of a radioactive gas, which gives off ionising radiation, and 25 000 people living in homes with very low levels. Over 15 years, 40 people in the first group and 10 in the second group developed lung cancer.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of lung cancer per 100 000 people in each group. **[2]**
**(b)** How many times more likely were people in the first group to develop lung cancer? **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest two variables the scientists should have made similar in both groups, and explain why. **[2]**

**9.** A scan shows tumours in a patient's lung and in one of their bones. Doctors say the bone tumour is a secondary tumour from the lung. Explain what this means and how the bone tumour formed. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why a person who has never smoked can still develop lung cancer. **[2]**

**11.** A website claims that a food additive causes stomach cancer. Its evidence is that 60 out of 100 patients with stomach cancer said they often ate foods containing the additive. Evaluate this evidence. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Changes in cells [1] that lead to uncontrolled growth and division [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Uncontrolled" is the word the marks depend on; "cells dividing fast" is not enough.

**2. (a)** Both [1]
**(b)** Malignant [1]
**(c)** Benign [1]
**(d)** Both [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) catches students who think benign tumours are always harmless.

**3.** The blood (circulatory system) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "It spreads through the body" does not name the route.

**4.** The tumour keeps growing in one area [1]; inside the skull it can press on the brain and damage its function, even though it does not spread [1]
*Examiner insight:* Benign means it does not invade or spread, not that it causes no harm.

**5. (a)** 32 ÷ 4 = 8 divisions [1]; 2⁸ = **256 cells** [1]
**(b)** 2⁹ = 512 and 2¹⁰ = 1024, so 10 divisions are needed [1]; 10 × 4 = **40 days** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each division doubles the number, so use powers of 2, not multiplication by the number of divisions.

**6.** A change in the cell (its DNA) means its division is no longer controlled [1]; the cell divides repeatedly by mitosis, and so do all the new cells it produces [1]; the cells build up into a mass of abnormal cells, a tumour [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the answer to the cell cycle: the problem is loss of control, not a different type of division.

**7.** Some cancers have genetic risk factors, so a family history may mean she has inherited a gene variant that raises her risk [1]; regular checks could find a tumour early, before it grows or spreads [1]
*Examiner insight:* A risk factor raises the chance of cancer; it does not mean she will develop it.

**8. (a)** First group: 40 ÷ 25 000 × 100 000 = **160 per 100 000** [1]; second group: 10 ÷ 25 000 × 100 000 = **40 per 100 000** [1]
**(b)** 160 ÷ 40 = **4 times** [1]
**(c)** Any two variables with a reason (1 mark each): smoking habits, because smoking is a major risk factor for lung cancer [1]; age, because older people are more likely to develop cancer [1]; also accept exposure to other carcinogens at work, or family history (genetic risk). The reason in each case: so that the difference can be linked to the radiation rather than to another factor.
*Examiner insight:* Smoking is the most important variable to match in any lung cancer study.

**9.** The bone tumour is made of cancer cells that came from the malignant tumour in the lung [1]; cells from the lung tumour invaded neighbouring tissue and some broke away into the blood [1]; the blood carried them to the bone [1]; there they kept dividing uncontrollably to form a new (secondary) tumour [1]
*Examiner insight:* A full answer gives invasion, transport in the blood and growth in a new place.

**10.** Smoking is only one risk factor for lung cancer [1]; others include exposure to carcinogens such as ionising radiation, or genetic risk factors, and many cancers result from several factors interacting [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name at least one other risk factor to earn the second mark.

**11.** Any four:
- there is no comparison group of people without stomach cancer, so we cannot tell whether 60% is unusually high [1]
- 100 patients is a small sample [1]
- a link would show only a correlation, not that the additive causes cancer [1]
- other factors, such as smoking, diet or genetic risk, were not taken into account [1]
- people may not remember accurately what they ate (also accept: no mechanism is described)
*Examiner insight:* The missing comparison group is the most important flaw; without it the 60 out of 100 figure means very little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "uncontrolled" when describing cancer.
- Saying benign tumours cannot cause harm.
- Not naming the blood as the route of spread.
- Multiplying instead of using powers of 2 for repeated division.
- Treating a correlation, or one risk factor, as proof of cause.
