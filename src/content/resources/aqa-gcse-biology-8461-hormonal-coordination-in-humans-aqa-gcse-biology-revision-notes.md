## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hormone | A chemical secreted by a gland directly into the blood, carried to a target organ |
| Endocrine system | The glands that secrete hormones |
| Target organ | The organ where a hormone produces its effect |
| Insulin | Hormone from the pancreas that causes glucose to move from the blood into cells |
| Glycogen | Storage carbohydrate made from glucose in liver and muscle cells |
| Glucagon | Hormone from the pancreas that causes glycogen to be converted to glucose (Higher tier only) |
| Deamination | Removal of the nitrogen-containing part of amino acids in the liver, forming ammonia (Higher tier only) |
| Selective reabsorption | Kidneys taking back useful substances (glucose, some ions, water) into the blood |
| ADH | Pituitary hormone that increases water reabsorption in the kidney tubules (Higher tier only) |
| Ovulation | Release of an egg, about every 28 days |
| Negative feedback | A change triggers a response that reverses the change (Higher tier only) |

## Nervous versus hormonal

| | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Message | Electrical impulse | Chemical in the blood |
| Speed | Very fast | Slower |
| Duration | Short | Longer |

## Glands and hormones

| Gland | Hormone |
|---|---|
| Pituitary ("master gland") | FSH, LH, ADH |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine (Higher tier only) |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline (Higher tier only) |
| Pancreas | Insulin; glucagon (Higher tier only) |
| Ovary | Oestrogen, progesterone |
| Testis | Testosterone |

## Blood glucose

- **High:** insulin → glucose into cells → glycogen stored in liver and muscle.
- **Low (Higher tier only):** glucagon → glycogen to glucose → glucose released into blood.

| | Type 1 | Type 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Not enough insulin produced | Cells do not respond to insulin |
| Treatment | Insulin injections | Carbohydrate-controlled diet; exercise |
| Risk factor | | Obesity |

## Water and nitrogen (Biology only)

- Lungs (breathing out) and skin (sweat): **uncontrolled** losses.
- Kidneys: **controlled** removal of excess water, ions and urea.
- **Higher tier only:** amino acids → deaminated in liver → ammonia (toxic) → urea.
- Urine is made by **filtration** then **selective reabsorption** of glucose, some ions and water.
- **Higher tier only:** blood too concentrated → pituitary releases ADH → tubules more permeable → more water
  reabsorbed → less, more concentrated urine.
- Kidney failure → dialysis (dialysis fluid has the same concentration of useful substances as blood, so only
  urea and excess ions diffuse out) or transplant (risk of rejection; donor shortage).

## Reproduction

| Hormone | Role |
|---|---|
| FSH | Egg matures |
| LH | Egg released |
| Oestrogen | Lining maintained (and built up) |
| Progesterone | Lining maintained |
| Testosterone | Sperm production |

**Higher tier only:** oestrogen inhibits FSH and stimulates LH; progesterone inhibits FSH and LH.

## Contraception

- **Hormonal:** oral pill (inhibits FSH); progesterone injection, implant or patch (months or years).
- **Non-hormonal:** condoms, diaphragms (barrier); IUD (prevents implantation; some release a hormone);
  spermicides; abstaining around ovulation; sterilisation.
- Only barrier methods protect against sexually transmitted infections.

## Infertility (Higher tier only)

- Fertility drug: FSH and LH.
- IVF: FSH and LH → several eggs mature → collected → fertilised in the lab → embryos → one or two inserted into
  the uterus.
- Drawbacks: emotionally and physically stressful; low success rates; multiple births risky for babies and mother.

## Feedback (Higher tier only)

- Adrenaline: fear or stress → heart rate up; more oxygen and glucose to brain and muscles; "flight or fight".
- Thyroxine: stimulates basal metabolic rate; growth and development; controlled by negative feedback.

## Exam traps

- Glucagon ≠ glycogen.
- Insulin does not "turn glucose into glycogen in the blood".
- Type 2: insulin **is** produced, but cells do not respond.
- ADH → **less** urine, not more.
- FSH matures; LH releases.
- Hormonal effects are **slower** but **longer-lasting**.
- Pituitary is in the **brain**; adrenal glands are on the **kidneys**.

## Self-test

1. Why is the pituitary called the "master gland"?
2. Give one way hormonal responses differ from nervous responses.
3. What does insulin cause?
4. Where is excess glucose stored, and as what?
5. Compare the causes of Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes.
6. Name two uncontrolled routes of water loss.
7. Which substances are selectively reabsorbed by the kidneys?
8. (Higher tier only) Why is ammonia converted to urea?
9. (Higher tier only) What does ADH do to the kidney tubules?
10. Which hormone causes an egg to mature?
11. How do oral contraceptives prevent pregnancy?
12. (Higher tier only) Give two drawbacks of IVF.

### Answers

1. It secretes hormones that act on other glands, stimulating them to release hormones.
2. Hormonal responses are slower (or last longer).
3. Glucose to move from the blood into cells.
4. In liver and muscle cells, as glycogen.
5. Type 1: the pancreas does not produce enough insulin; Type 2: body cells no longer respond to insulin.
6. Breathing out from the lungs; sweating from the skin.
7. Glucose, some ions and water.
8. Ammonia is toxic; urea can be excreted safely.
9. Makes them more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood.
10. FSH.
11. Hormones inhibit FSH production, so no eggs mature.
12. Any two: emotionally and physically stressful; success rates not high; multiple births risky.
