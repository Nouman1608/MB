This practice set covers the **individual oral** of IB Diploma Programme Language B, at SL and HL.
It follows the IB Language B subject brief for first assessment 2020, which remains the examined
course until the new syllabus is first assessed in May 2029, so it applies to the 2026, 2027 and 2028
sessions. The tasks use the themes **sharing the planet**, **identities** and **experiences**. Texts
are in English; say your answers aloud in your target language as well as writing them.

and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-individual-oral-revision-notes/). Course links:

## Questions

**1.** Using the subject brief, state (a) the stimulus for the individual oral at SL, (b) the
stimulus at HL, and (c) the oral's weighting and mark total. **[3]**

### Stimulus A — image described in words (original, written for Marlbridge)

> A riverbank on a grey morning. Volunteers of different ages are sorting rubbish into sacks. At the
> front, a boy of about twelve holds up a plastic bottle towards a man in a suit, who is taking a
> photo with his phone. A hand-painted banner on a fence reads "Our river, our job". Behind them, on
> the far bank, stands a factory with a tall chimney.

**2.** Name the prescribed theme Stimulus A is most clearly relevant to, and give two details from
the image that make it relevant. **[3]**

### Passage B — transcript of an SL practice oral on Stimulus A (original, written for Marlbridge)

> STUDENT: Um, so in this picture there are some people. They are next to a river. There is a boy and
> he has a bottle. There is a man with a phone. There are bags. There is a banner. There is a factory.
> I think it is about the environment.
>
> TEACHER: Why do you think the man is taking a photo?
>
> STUDENT: For the newspaper.
>
> TEACHER: Would you take part in something like this?
>
> STUDENT: Yes.
>
> TEACHER: The banner says "Our river, our job". Whose job is it really?
>
> STUDENT: I don't know the word… the people who own the… the building that makes things. The factory.
> The factory must clean it. Or maybe also the government.

**3.** Identify three weaknesses in the student's opening in Passage B. **[3]**

**4.** Rewrite the student's opening (about 80 words, in English) using the five-move shape: frame,
describe, interpret, connect, close. **[4]**

**5.** Rewrite the student's answers "For the newspaper." and "Yes." so that each one extends the
conversation. **[4]**

**6.** In the student's last answer, name the repair strategy used, explain why it helps, and
suggest one further move that would strengthen the answer. **[3]**

**7.** Choose one of the brief's possible questions for sharing the planet and explain how it could
work as a lens for Stimulus A. **[2]**

### Passage C — extract standing in for an HL literary work (original text written for Marlbridge)

> The well at the end of the village had been dry since spring. Every morning Tomas walked past it
> with two plastic cans, down to the road where the water lorry stopped. The driver never looked at
> anyone. He filled the cans, took the coins, and wrote a number in a notebook that nobody else was
> allowed to read.
>
> "It will rain in October," Tomas's grandfather said each evening, as he had said for sixty years.
>
> In October it did not rain. The lorry came twice a week, then once. The price in the notebook
> changed, and nobody asked why. Tomas began to leave earlier, before the sun reached the square, so
> that no one would see how light his cans were on the way home.

**8.** (HL) Identify two prescribed themes Passage C could be linked to, and support each with a
quotation. **[4]**

**9.** (HL) Identify two literary features of Passage C and explain the effect of each, with a
quotation. **[4]**

**10.** (SL) Plan a full presentation on Stimulus A, then write two questions your teacher might
ask and a note on how you would answer each. **[8]**

**11.** (HL) Treat Passage C as an excerpt from one of your literary works. Plan your presentation
using the four questions: where, what, how, why. **[8]**

**12.** (SL) In the conversation on Stimulus A, your teacher asks: "Can one person really make a
difference to the environment?" Write a model answer of about 60 words (in English). **[5]**

## Answers

The real oral is marked holistically against criteria your teacher will share. The `[1]` points
below are **indicative**; any valid equivalent earns the point.

**1.** (a) A **visual image** clearly relevant to one (or more) of the themes. [1] (b) An **excerpt
from one of the two literary works** studied. [1] (c) **25% of the final grade, 30 marks.** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rehearsing the other level's task (plot summary at SL, image description at HL)
means practising the wrong oral.

**2.** **Sharing the planet** (the environment). [1] Detail 1: volunteers "sorting rubbish into
sacks" beside a river. [1] Detail 2: the factory chimney on the far bank, suggesting a source of
pollution. [1] (The banner "Our river, our job" is also acceptable.)
*Examiner insight:* The theme mark depends on the details; "it is about nature" with no reference to
the image does not show why the stimulus is relevant.

**3.** Any three: it is a **list of "there is/are"** with no point [1]; the **theme link comes only
at the end** and is vague ("about the environment") [1]; there is **no interpretation** of details
such as the banner or the man in a suit [1]; no **cultural connection**; no **signposting** or
structure; a **filler** ("Um, so") opens it.
*Examiner insight:* Strong openings make a claim in the first sentence.

**4.** Model opening: *"This image shows a river clean-up, and I think it asks who is responsible for
pollution. Volunteers of all ages sort rubbish while a man in a suit photographs a boy holding up a
bottle. What interests me is the contrast: the banner says 'Our river, our job', yet the factory
behind them is untouched. This reminds me of a river campaign we studied from [target-language
country]. So I want to ask: is clean-up work a solution, or a distraction?"*
Indicative points: a **frame with a main idea** [1]; **brief description** [1]; an **interpretation**
of a specific detail [1]; a **connection** and a **closing question** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Description should be the shortest part of the opening, not half of it.

**5.** "For the newspaper": *"Probably for a local newspaper or a company website. I think he wants to
be seen supporting the clean-up — maybe he even works for the factory."* An answer [1] plus a reason
or interpretation. [1]
"Yes": *"Yes, I think I would, because it shows the problem is real. Last spring my class picked
litter in a park near school, and people stopped to ask what we were doing."* An answer [1] plus a
reason and example. [1]
*Examiner insight:* An extended answer gives the teacher something to build on; a one-word answer
forces a fresh question.

**6.** Strategy: **paraphrase / circumlocution** ("the building that makes things"). [1] It keeps
the conversation in the target language when a word is missing. [1] Further move: **give a reason**
("because the factory is on the river, it should pay for the damage") or **qualify** ("the government
should make rules, and the factory should follow them"). [1]
*Examiner insight:* Paraphrasing is a strength; switching to your own language, or stopping, breaks
the exchange.

**7.** "What environmental and social issues present challenges to the world, and how can these
challenges be overcome?" [1] Stimulus A shows one challenge (river pollution) and one response
(volunteers), so the lens lets you ask whether volunteering is enough or whether the factory must
change. [1] ("What challenges and benefits does globalization bring?" is acceptable if linked to the
factory and trade.)
*Examiner insight:* A possible question helps only when applied to the stimulus.

**8.** **Sharing the planet** (environment, or equality) [1]: "The well at the end of the village had
been dry since spring". [1] **Experiences** (life stories, or customs and traditions) [1]: "as he had
said for sixty years" shows the grandfather's lifelong expectation being broken. [1] (Identities is
acceptable: Tomas hiding "how light his cans were".)
*Examiner insight:* The quotation must prove the theme link, not just retell an action.

**9.** Feature 1: **contrast** between prediction and outcome: "It will rain in October" against "In
October it did not rain" [1], which shows that old certainties no longer hold. [1] Feature 2: the
**notebook as a symbol** of power: "a notebook that nobody else was allowed to read" [1], which shows
the driver controls price and information. [1] (Also acceptable: the light cans showing shame.)
*Examiner insight:* Naming a feature is only half the point; the effect must say what it shows about
the characters or the theme.

**10.** Model plan:

```
Frame     clean-up image: who is responsible for pollution?
Describe  volunteers, boy with bottle, man in suit, banner, factory
Interpret 1) banner vs untouched factory  2) photo = publicity?
Connect   a target-language campaign or law studied in class
Close     is volunteering a solution or a distraction?
```

Indicative points: frame [1]; brief description [1]; first interpretation [1]; second
interpretation [1]; cultural connection [1]; closing question [1]. Likely questions with answer
notes: "Should factories pay for clean-ups?" (position, reason, example) [1]; a move to identities,
"What does volunteering say about young people?" [1]
*Examiner insight:* Strong candidates prepare moves, not scripts; a memorised paragraph per heading
sounds read and cannot adapt.

**11.** Model plan:

```
Where  [your work: what comes just before / after]
What   drought; Tomas buys water; prices rise; he hides
How    contrast (rain prediction); notebook symbol; light cans
Why    theme: sharing the planet / experiences; link to whole work
```

Indicative points: places the extract in the work [1]; states the key change [1]; first feature with quotation [1] and effect [1]; second feature with quotation [1]
and effect [1]; links to a prescribed theme [1]; links to the whole work [1].
*Examiner insight:* Retelling the extract line by line leaves no time for how it is written; choose
two or three features and go deep.

**12.** Model answer: *"On their own, not very much, because much pollution comes from industry and
transport. But that doesn't mean giving up. One person can start something bigger: the clean-up in
the image probably began with a few neighbours. So one person makes a small difference directly, and
a bigger one by persuading others."*
Indicative points: **position** [1]; **reason** [1]; **qualification** [1]; **example** [1];
**conclusion** returning to the question [1].
*Examiner insight:* Weak answers stop at "yes" or "no"; strong ones show a position that develops
under questioning.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing items in an image instead of making a claim about them.
- Leaving the theme link until the end, or making it vague.
- Retelling the plot of an HL extract instead of analysing features.
- Quoting a feature without explaining its effect.
- One-word answers that close the conversation.
- Switching language when a word is missing, instead of paraphrasing.
- General comments about a culture with no specific example.
