## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Write the three-figure bearing of north-west. **[1]**
**(b)** A road runs from town A to town B on a bearing of 135°. State the compass point of B from A, and find the bearing of A from B. **[2]**

**2.** A map has a scale of 1 : 75 000.
**(a)** Two villages are 8.6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they on the ground? Give your answer in km. **[2]**
**(b)** A canal is 12 km long. Find its length on the map in cm. **[2]**
**(c)** On a second map the same 3 km is drawn as 5 cm. Write the scale of the second map as a ratio 1 : n. **[2]**

**3.** The bearing of a lighthouse L from a ship S is 251°. A buoy B has bearing 320° from S.
**(a)** Find the bearing of S from L. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the size of angle LSB. **[2]**

**4.** A boat leaves harbour H and sails 7.5 km on a bearing of 038° to point X. It then sails 10.5 km on a bearing of 112° to point Y. Use a scale of 1 cm to 1.5 km.
**(a)** Make an accurate scale drawing of the journey. **[4]**
**(b)** Measure HY and find the real distance from H to Y. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the bearing of H from Y. **[2]**

**5.** Using ruler and compasses only, construct triangle ABC with AB = 7 cm, BC = 5.5 cm and AC = 4.5 cm. Leave in your construction arcs. Measure angle ABC. **[5]**

**6.** **(a)** Draw a line PQ of length 8 cm. Using ruler and compasses only, construct an angle of 60° at P. **[3]**
**(b)** Using ruler and compasses only, bisect your angle. State the size of each part. **[2]**

**7.** Draw a line AB of length 8 cm. Point P is above AB with AP = 6 cm and BP = 5 cm.
**(a)** Construct triangle ABP using ruler and compasses. **[2]**
**(b)** Construct the perpendicular from P to AB. **[2]**
**(c)** Measure the shortest distance from P to AB. **[1]**

**8.** X and Y are points 5 m apart. A path is the set of all points exactly 3 m from the line segment XY.
**(a)** Describe the shape of the path. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the length of the path, to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**9.** ABCD is a rectangular playground with AB = 12 m and BC = 8 m. Draw it with 1 cm standing for 2 m. A prize is hidden at a point that is closer to A than to B, nearer to AB than to AD, and less than 7 m from A.
**(a)** Draw the playground and shade the region where the prize may be. **[7]**
**(b)** A point T is 5 m from AD and 4 m from AB. Is T in the region? Give a reason. **[1]**

**10.** A straight coast runs north to south through port P. A boat is 12 km from P on a bearing of 070°. Use a scale of 1 cm to 2 km to find the shortest distance from the boat to the coast. **[5]**

**11.** Port B is 18 km due east of port A. The bearing of a lighthouse L from A is 048° and the bearing of L from B is 312°. Use a scale of 1 cm to 3 km.
**(a)** Draw the line AB and the two bearing lines, and mark L. **[3]**
**(b)** Measure AL and write the real distance in km. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that LA = LB. **[2]**
**(d)** A radar at L detects points within 10 km. Is port A detected? Give a reason. **[2]**
**(e)** Find the shortest distance from L to the line AB, by calculation. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** North-west is 315° [1]. **315°**
**(b)** 135° is south-east, so B is **south-east** of A [1]. Back bearing: 135° + 180° = **315°** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write every bearing with three figures, and name the compass point when the question asks for it.

**2. (a)** 8.6 × 75 000 = 645 000 cm [1] = **6.45 km** [1].
**(b)** 12 km = 1 200 000 cm; 1 200 000 ÷ 75 000 [1] = **16 cm** [1].
**(c)** 3 km = 300 000 cm [1]. 5 : 300 000 = **1 : 60 000** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion from cm to km as a separate line, so a slip in the power of ten is visible and the method can still be seen.

**3. (a)** 251° is more than 180°, so subtract: 251° − 180° [1] = **071°** [1].
**(b)** Both lines start at S: 320° − 251° [1] = **69°** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For an angle between two bearings from the same point, subtract the bearings; do not add 180° unless you are switching to the other end of the line.

**4. (a)** Scale lengths: 7.5 ÷ 1.5 = 5 cm and 10.5 ÷ 1.5 = 7 cm [1]. Draw a north line at H, measure 038° clockwise and draw HX = 5 cm [1]. Draw a north line at X, measure 112° clockwise and draw XY = 7 cm [1]. Join H to Y [1].
**(b)** HY measures about 9.7 cm, so the distance is 9.7 × 1.5 ≈ **14.5 km** [1] (accept 14.2 km to 14.8 km).
**(c)** Bearing of Y from H is about 082° [1]. Add 180°: **262°** (accept 260° to 264°) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep your north lines on the page. A bearing of H from Y needs a north line drawn at Y.

**5.** Draw AB = 7 cm [1]. Set compasses to 4.5 cm, centre A, draw an arc [1]. Set to 5.5 cm, centre B, draw an arc crossing the first at C [1]. Join AC and BC, leaving the arcs [1]. Angle ABC measures **40°** (accept 39° to 41°) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A triangle made by trial and error gives no evidence of a construction, even if its sides are right, so keep the arcs.

**6. (a)** Draw PQ = 8 cm. Draw an arc centred at P cutting PQ at X [1]. With the same radius, draw an arc centred at X cutting the first arc at Z [1]. Join P to Z; angle ZPQ is **60°** [1].
**(b)** Draw arcs from X and from Z with equal radius so that they cross [1]. Join P to the crossing point. Each part is **30°** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State the size of the angle you have made and the reason it is that size, instead of only measuring it.

**7. (a)** Draw AB = 8 cm [1]. Arc of radius 6 cm from A and arc of radius 5 cm from B cross at P [1].
**(b)** Draw an arc centred at P cutting AB at two points [1]. Arcs of equal radius from those points cross on the other side of AB; join to P [1].
**(c)** The perpendicular measures about **3.7 cm** (accept 3.6 cm to 3.9 cm) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The shortest distance is along the perpendicular, so a measurement from P to the nearer end of AB is not the shortest distance.

**8. (a)** Two straight lines [1], each 5 m long and 3 m from XY on either side, joined at each end by a semicircle of radius 3 m [1].
**(b)** The two straight parts have length 2 × 5 = 10 m [1]. The two semicircles make one circle: 2π × 3 = 18.85 m [1]. Total 10 + 18.85 = 28.85 (more exactly 28.8496), so **28.8 m** to 1 d.p. [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw semicircles, not square corners, at the ends of the path.

**9. (a)** Draw rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm [1]. Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB (3 cm from A) [1]; the region is on the A side [1]. Construct the bisector of angle DAB, a line at 45° through A [1]; the region is below it, nearer AB [1]. Draw an arc of radius 3.5 cm centred at A [1]; the region is inside it. Shade the part that satisfies all three [1].
**(b)** T is 5 m from AD and 4 m from AB, so it is 2.5 cm across and 2 cm up: left of the perpendicular bisector, below the 45° line (4 < 5), and 6.4 m from A, which is less than 7 m. **Yes, T is in the region** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Test a point against each boundary to decide which side to shade.

**10.** The boat is 12 ÷ 2 = 6 cm from P [1]. Draw the coast as a vertical line through P and a line of 6 cm at 070° clockwise from north [1]. Construct the perpendicular from the boat to the coast line [1]. It measures about 5.6 cm [1], so the distance is 5.6 × 2 = **11.3 km** (accept 11.0 km to 11.6 km) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert from cm back to km at the end and give units.

**11. (a)** Draw AB = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 cm [1]. At A draw a north line and measure 048° clockwise [1]. At B draw a north line and measure 312° clockwise; the lines cross at L [1].
**(b)** AL measures about 4.0 cm, so AL ≈ **12 km** (calculation gives 12.1 km) [1].
**(c)** The bearing of B from A is 090°, so angle LAB = 90° − 48° = 42° [1]. At B, the bearing 312° is 48° west of north, so angle LBA = 90° − 48° = 42°. The base angles are equal, so triangle ALB is isosceles and LA = LB [1].
**(d)** AL ≈ 12 km [1], which is more than 10 km, so **A is not detected** [1].
**(e)** The perpendicular from L meets AB at its midpoint M, with AM = 9 km [1]. LM = 9 × tan 42° = **8.1 km** [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" asks for each reason: state the bearings that make each base angle 42°, not just that the triangle looks isosceles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring a bearing from the wrong north line, or anticlockwise.
- Not writing three figures for a bearing, such as 38° instead of 038°.
- Forgetting to convert km to cm, or to convert back, when using a scale.
- Using the wrong protractor scale, so an angle reads as 130° instead of 50°.
- Changing the compass width between two arcs of a construction.
- Leaving out the construction arcs, or rubbing them out.
- Measuring slanted lines as the shortest distance from a point to a line.
- Shading a region for only one or two of the loci conditions.
