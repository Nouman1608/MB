## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Colorimeter | Instrument that measures the absorbance of light by a coloured solution |
| Blank | A sample without the coloured substance, used to set zero |
| Calibration curve | Graph of known concentrations against absorbance, used to find unknowns |
| Serial dilution | A series of dilutions made by repeating the same dilution step |
| Graticule | Scale in the eyepiece of a microscope |
| Stage micrometer | Slide with a scale of known length, used to calibrate the graticule |
| Rf | Distance moved by the solute ÷ distance moved by the solvent |
| Aseptic technique | Methods that prevent contamination of cultures and surroundings |
| Quadrat | A frame of known area used for sampling |
| Transect | A line along which samples are taken |

## Measuring: (a) and (b)

- Mass: balance to 0.01 g; tare with the container.
- Volume: syringe or graduated pipette for small volumes.
- Temperature: thermostatic water bath; allow time to equilibrate.
- pH: calibrated meter.
- **Colorimeter:** complementary filter; zero with a blank; same cuvette.
- **Calibration curve:** absorbances of 0, 0.15, 0.30, 0.45, 0.60 for 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 mmol dm⁻³. Gradient 0.75. An unknown with absorbance 0.36 has a concentration of 0.36 ÷ 0.75 = 0.48 mmol dm⁻³.
- **Potometer:** cut the shoot and assemble under water; no air in the xylem; seal joints; measure the distance the bubble moves in a set time.

## Serial dilutions: (c)

| Type | Method | Result from 1.0 mol dm⁻³ |
|---|---|---|
| Ten-fold | 1 cm³ into 9 cm³ of water | 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 |
| Two-fold | 5 cm³ into 5 cm³ of water | 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 |

Use a clean pipette tip or rinse between tubes. Errors carry through every later tube.

## Microscope and graticule: (d)

1. Start on low power.
2. Coarse focus, then fine focus.
3. Centre the specimen before moving to high power.
4. Calibrate the graticule for each objective with a stage micrometer.

Example: 50 graticule divisions = 40 stage divisions of 10 μm = 400 μm. One division = 8.0 μm. A cell of 6.5 divisions = 52 μm.

## Drawing: (e)

- Sharp pencil, clear continuous lines, no shading.
- Large, accurate proportions.
- Ruled label lines, not crossing, no arrowheads, horizontal labels.
- Title and magnification or scale bar.

**magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object.** Same units. A 78 mm drawing of a 0.052 mm cell is × 1500.

## Reagents: (f)

| Test | Reagent | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| Starch | Iodine in potassium iodide | Blue-black |
| Reducing sugar | Benedict's, heat | Green to brick-red precipitate |
| Non-reducing sugar | Acid hydrolysis, neutralise, Benedict's | Brick-red after hydrolysis |
| Protein | Biuret | Purple or lilac |
| Lipid | Ethanol then water | Cloudy white emulsion |

## Chromatography and electrophoresis: (g)

- Pencil origin line above the solvent level.
- Mark the solvent front when the paper is removed.
- **Rf = distance moved by the solute ÷ distance moved by the solvent** (no unit, less than 1). A spot that moves 4.2 cm while the front moves 7.0 cm has Rf = 0.60.
- Electrophoresis separates charged molecules in a gel by charge and size.

## Organisms and aseptic technique: (h) and (i)

- Ethical: minimal numbers, avoid harm, return animals; consent for volunteers.
- Aseptic: disinfect bench, flame the loop, lid lifted slightly, flame the neck of bottles, tape the dish with short strips, incubate upside down at about 25 °C.
- Zone of inhibition area = π r². Diameter 14 mm gives π × 7² = 154 mm².

## Dissection, sampling and ICT: (j) to (l)

- Dissection: sharp tools on a board, cut away from the body, keep tissue moist.
- Quadrats: random coordinates for uniform habitats; transects for gradients.
- Density = mean per quadrat ÷ quadrat area. 54 plants in 12 quadrats of 0.25 m² gives 4.5 per quadrat, 18 per m², and 7200 in 400 m².
- Data logger: precise, automatic; computer modelling: tests ideas that cannot be tested in a laboratory.

## Quick chains

- 1 mm = 1000 μm.
- magnification × 1500 ← drawing 78 mm ÷ 0.052 mm.
- Rf 0.60 ← 4.2 ÷ 7.0.
- 18 per m² × 400 m² = 7200.
- 0.36 ÷ 0.75 = 0.48.

## Exam traps

- Ink origin line in chromatography.
- Solvent level above the origin line.
- Calibrating the graticule on one lens and using another.
- Mixing mm and μm.
- Not zeroing the colorimeter with a blank.
- Sealing a culture dish fully.
- Starting on high power.
- Saying a Benedict's result is "red" without heating or the colour sequence.

## Self-test

1. Which filter colour is chosen for a colorimeter?
2. What is a blank used for?
3. How would you make a 10⁻² dilution from a stock solution using ten-fold steps?
4. 20 graticule divisions line up with 10 stage divisions of 10 μm. What is the length of one graticule division?
5. A drawing is 60 mm long and the real object is 0.12 mm. Calculate the magnification.
6. A spot moves 3.6 cm and the solvent front 9.0 cm. Calculate Rf.
7. What is the colour of a positive biuret test?
8. Why is the origin line drawn in pencil?
9. Calculate the area of a clear zone of radius 8 mm.
10. In a quadrat survey, 45 plants are found in 15 quadrats of 0.5 m². Calculate the density per m².

### Answers

1. The colour complementary to that of the solution.
2. To set the instrument to zero, so that absorbance by the solvent and cuvette is not included.
3. Two ten-fold steps: 1 cm³ of stock into 9 cm³ of water, then 1 cm³ of that into 9 cm³ of water.
4. 10 × 10 = 100 μm for 20 divisions, so 5.0 μm per division.
5. 60 ÷ 0.12 = × 500.
6. 3.6 ÷ 9.0 = 0.40.
7. Purple or lilac.
8. Pencil does not dissolve and run in the solvent; ink would.
9. π × 8² = 201 mm².
10. 45 ÷ 15 = 3 per quadrat; 3 ÷ 0.5 = 6 plants per m².

## Frequently asked questions

**Why start with the low-power objective?**
It has a wider field of view and a longer working distance, so the specimen is easier to find and there is less risk of damaging the slide.

**Do I need to learn the Benedict's colour sequence?**
Yes: blue, green, yellow, orange, brick-red as the amount of reducing sugar increases.

**Is Rf the same for every run?**
No. Rf depends on the solvent and the paper, so it is compared with standards run under the same conditions.
