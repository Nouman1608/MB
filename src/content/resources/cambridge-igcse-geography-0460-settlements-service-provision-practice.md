These questions cover topic 1.5 Settlements (rural and urban) and service provision in Theme 1 (Population and settlement) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. All settlements and survey results are fictional and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Settlements and Service Provision study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-geography-0460-settlements-service-provision/) and the [Settlements and Service Provision revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-settlements-service-provision-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term "threshold population". **[1]**

**2.** Define the "range" of a good or service. **[1]**

**3.** Identify the settlement pattern described in each case.

- Single farmhouses spread across a wide area of farmland, each surrounded by its own fields.
- Houses along both sides of the only road through a narrow, steep-sided valley.
- Houses grouped closely around a well and a weekly market square. **[3]**

**4.** Give one example of a low-order service and one example of a high-order service. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a bridging point was an advantage for an early settlement. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by a "dry point" site and why it was chosen. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows information about four fictional settlements in one region.

| Settlement | Population | Number of services |
|---|---|---|
| Halden | 120,000 | 640 |
| Orrin | 15,000 | 95 |
| Pell | 2,200 | 18 |
| Quarry End | 400 | 3 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between population and the number of services. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of services per 1,000 people in Pell. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why Quarry End has very few services. **[2]**

**8.** Students asked 100 shoppers at a supermarket in Orrin how far they had travelled.

| Distance travelled (km) | Number of shoppers |
|---|---|
| Less than 5 | 46 |
| 5 to less than 10 | 28 |
| 10 to less than 15 | 18 |
| 15 or more | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of shoppers who had travelled 10 km or more. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the students could use a questionnaire to map the supermarket's sphere of influence. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why a department store in Halden would be likely to have a larger sphere of influence than the supermarket in Orrin. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how accessibility and resources can influence the growth of a settlement. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why services in many rural villages in high-income countries have declined, and describe one way of maintaining services. **[5]**

**11.** For an area you have studied, describe the hierarchy of settlements and services and explain the reasons for it. **[7]**

**12.** "The physical site of a settlement is less important today than its accessibility." To what extent do you agree? Use examples. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The minimum number of people needed to support a service and keep it running [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The maximum distance people are willing to travel to use the good or service [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Threshold is about people and range is about distance; swapping them is one of the most common errors in this topic.

**3.** Dispersed [1]. Linear [1]. Nucleated [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Low-order: a small food shop, newsagent or primary school [1]. High-order: a department store, specialist hospital, university or airport [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A bridging point was where a river could be crossed easily, by a ford or bridge [1], so routes met there and people came to trade, helping the settlement grow as a market centre [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A site on land raised above an area liable to flood, such as a gravel terrace or low hill [1], chosen so that buildings stayed dry and safe from flooding [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** As population increases, the number of services increases [1]. Halden, the largest settlement with 120,000 people, has 640 services, whereas Quarry End, with 400 people, has only 3 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 18 ÷ 2.2 = 8.2 services per 1,000 people [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Its population of 400 is below the threshold needed for most services [1]. Residents can travel to Pell or Orrin nearby for services, which are within their range, so few services open in Quarry End [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe the relationship" needs the direction of the relationship and data from the table; quoting figures with no statement of the pattern gains only one mark.

**8. (a)** 18 + 8 = 26 out of 100, so 26% [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Ask a sample of shoppers at different times and days where they have travelled from, such as their village or postcode [1]. Plot each origin on a map of the area [1]. Draw desire lines from each origin to the supermarket and draw a boundary around most of them to show the sphere of influence [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** A department store is a high-order service with a large threshold, so there are few of them and they are found only in large settlements [1]. People use it rarely and are willing to travel further for comparison goods, so its range, and therefore its sphere of influence, is larger [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Accessibility: settlements at a meeting of routes, a bridging point or a natural harbour can be reached easily, so people come to trade [1], which creates jobs and services and attracts more people, so the settlement grows [1]. Resources: deposits of coal, minerals or building stone attract mining and industry [1], so workers move in and the settlement grows, although it may decline when the resource runs out [1]. **[4]**

**10.** More people own cars, so they travel to larger towns where services are cheaper and there is more choice [1]. Large supermarkets and online shopping take customers from village shops, so their number of customers falls below the threshold and they close [1]. Young people move away and the remaining population is older and smaller [1]. Banks and post offices close branches as more services move online [1]. One way of maintaining services: a community-run shop, or a mobile shop or library that visits several villages on different days so each service reaches its threshold [1]. **[5]**

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content:

A named area, with settlements ranked from the largest town to villages and hamlets [1]. Examples of named high-order services found only in the largest settlement [1]. Examples of middle-order services in smaller towns [1]. Examples of low-order services found in villages [1]. Reason: high-order services have large thresholds, so they can only survive in large settlements [1]. Reason: people travel further for high-order services, so the largest settlement has the largest sphere of influence [1]. Reason: accessibility, as the main settlement is where routes meet, so it can serve the whole area [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Without named settlements and services, an answer cannot reach the top level, however well the theory is explained.

**12.** Indicative content:

Agree: modern technology reduces the importance of site factors such as water supply, because water can be piped long distances [1]. Agree: flood defences and drainage allow building on land that would once have been avoided [1]. Agree: accessibility to motorways, railways, airports and ports now decides which settlements grow, for example towns at motorway junctions or near airports [1]. Agree: settlements with poor accessibility often decline, even if their sites are good [1]. Disagree: steep relief still makes building expensive and limits growth [1]. Disagree: flood risk remains a major problem for settlements on flood plains, and coastal sites face erosion [1]. Disagree: the original site often decided the situation, so the two are linked [1]. Judgement: accessibility is usually more important for growth today, but site factors still limit where and how settlements can expand [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing threshold and range.
- Naming a settlement pattern without the evidence that shows it.
- Describing a site factor with no explanation of why it helped early settlers.
- Relationship answers with no data, or data with no stated relationship.
- Hierarchy answers that rank settlements only by population and never mention services.
