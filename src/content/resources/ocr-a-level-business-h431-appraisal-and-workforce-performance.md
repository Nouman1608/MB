## What appraisal is

**Appraisal** is a formal, regular assessment of an employee's performance against agreed standards or objectives, usually by discussion. Its purposes are to:

- review achievements and identify strengths and weaknesses;
- agree objectives for the next period;
- identify training and development needs;
- decide on pay, bonus or promotion;
- improve motivation through recognition and feedback;
- provide evidence for disciplinary or dismissal decisions where needed;
- improve communication between employee and manager.

## Methods of appraisal

| Method | Meaning | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Managerial assessment | The line manager assesses the employee | Manager knows the job and the standards; simple | Subjective; bias; the employee has little say |
| Peer assessment | Colleagues assess one another | Colleagues see daily performance; encourages teamwork | May be influenced by friendship or rivalry; can cause tension |
| Self-assessment | The employee evaluates their own performance | Involves the employee; promotes reflection | May be inflated or too modest |
| 360 degree assessment | Feedback from manager, peers, subordinates, and sometimes customers | Full picture from several views | Time-consuming; costly; anonymous comments may be hurtful |
| Target setting | Specific, measurable goals are agreed, then assessed against results | Clear and objective; links to business aims | Targets may be too easy or too hard; ignores things not measured |

Most businesses combine methods. A manager's review of the employee's self-assessment against agreed targets is common.

## Measures of workforce performance

The specification lists six measures. The formulae below are the standard forms.

- **Lateness (%)** = number of late arrivals ÷ total possible attendances × 100.
- **Absenteeism (%)** = days lost to absence ÷ total working days available × 100.
- **Labour turnover (%)** = leavers in a period ÷ average number employed × 100.
- **Productivity** = output ÷ number of employees (or hours worked).
- **Holiday entitlement:** the paid leave an employee is entitled to by law and by contract. The statutory minimum for a full-time employee is 5.6 weeks a year, and part-timers receive it pro rata (5.6 times the days they work a week).
- **Performance related pay (PRP):** pay (usually a bonus) linked to performance, such as output above a target.

### Worked example 1: calculating the measures

Penhallow Distribution (invented) has 50 staff, each expected to work 250 days a year, so 50 × 250 = **12,500** working days.

**Absenteeism.** 750 days were lost. 750 ÷ 12,500 × 100 = **6.0%**. At a cost of £95 a day for sick pay and cover, absence cost 750 × £95 = **£71,250**. A cut to 4% (500 days) would save 250 × £95 = £23,750.

**Lateness.** There were 1,200 late arrivals. 1,200 ÷ 12,500 × 100 = **9.6%**.

**Labour turnover.** 9 staff left. The average number employed was 50. 9 ÷ 50 × 100 = **18%**.

**Productivity.** This year 150,000 parcels were handled by 50 staff, so 150,000 ÷ 50 = **3,000** parcels per employee. Last year the figure was 135,000 parcels with 48 staff, 135,000 ÷ 48 = 2,812.5. The change is (3,000 − 2,812.5) ÷ 2,812.5 × 100 = **+6.7%**.

**Holiday entitlement.** A full-time employee working 5 days a week is entitled to 5.6 × 5 = **28 days** (which may include bank holidays). A part-time employee working 3 days a week is entitled to 5.6 × 3 = **16.8 days**.

**Interpretation.**

- Absence of 6.0% looks high compared with the business's target, and costs over £70,000 a year. It deserves investigation, for example whether it is concentrated in one team or on Mondays.
- A turnover of 18% is moderate for the sector but costly if each leaver costs £3,000 to replace (9 × £3,000 = £27,000).
- Productivity has risen, but it does not show whether the rise comes from better working, longer hours, new equipment or an easier mix of parcels.
- Lateness of 9.6% suggests discipline or transport problems and affects the start of shifts.

### Performance related pay

Penhallow introduces PRP: £2 for every parcel an employee handles above 2,800 a year (a target of 2,800). With every employee at 3,000, the bonus is (3,000 − 2,800) × £2 = **£400** each, or 50 × £400 = **£20,000** in total.

The extra output above target is 200 × 50 = 10,000 parcels. If each earns a contribution of £3.50, the extra contribution is 10,000 × £3.50 = **£35,000**. Net gain = £35,000 − £20,000 = **£15,000**.

Evaluation of PRP:

- It motivates staff to raise output and rewards effort fairly, and the net gain is positive here.
- It may encourage speed over quality (more parcels, more errors), and teamwork may suffer if staff compete.
- Some outputs are hard to measure, and some employees' performance depends on others.
- It adds a fixed target that must be set fairly: too low, and the business pays for output it would have had anyway; too high, and staff give up.
- Staff on PRP face uncertain income.

## Evaluating measures of workforce performance

**Useful because:**

- they give objective figures that can be tracked and compared with targets and rivals;
- they point to problems (high absence in a team) and support decisions on training, rewards and staffing;
- they link to costs (the cost of absence, the cost of turnover).

**Limits:**

- a figure tells you what is happening, but not why. High turnover may be a result of low pay, poor management or a competitor's offers;
- a single measure can mislead. Productivity may rise because staff work unsafe hours;
- people may "game" measures if they are the basis for reward;
- measures differ between sectors, so comparisons need care;
- some contributions, such as helping colleagues, are not measured.

The best practice is to use several measures together and to follow up with a conversation or survey to understand the cause.

## Evaluating appraisal

Benefits: gives feedback and recognition, identifies training needs, sets clear goals, supports pay decisions, improves communication, and gives a record for legal purposes.

Limits: it takes time; managers may be biased or avoid honest criticism; employees may dread it; a yearly event is too infrequent to be useful; and if there is no follow-up, staff lose faith.

Stakeholders: employees (feedback, fairness, pay), managers (information, time), owners (productivity and cost), customers (better service).

Which method? A 360 degree review suits managers and complex roles where many people see performance; target setting suits sales or production jobs with clear outputs; peer assessment suits teams; self-assessment works best combined with managerial review.

## Employee contribution

An employee's **contribution** is the value they add to the business, through output, quality, ideas, customer service, teamwork and reliability, less the cost of employing them. It can be estimated with measures such as output per employee, revenue per employee or profit per employee.

For example, 150,000 parcels at an average revenue of £11 gives £1,650,000 across 50 staff, or £33,000 per employee. The contribution (revenue less variable cost of £7.50 a parcel, a contribution of £3.50) is £10,500 per employee. This can be compared with employee cost, to judge whether the contribution justifies the pay.

Evaluation: numbers do not capture everything. An employee who trains new colleagues or solves customers' problems may add more than the figures show. Contribution also depends on the equipment, management and team around the employee, so low output may be the business's fault and not the employee's.

## Recommending methods of appraisal

State the method, why it suits the job, the cost and the risk. For example: "Use target setting with a manager's review twice a year for the delivery drivers, because their output is measurable; add a 360 degree review for supervisors, whose role depends on how others see them. The risk is that targets drive speed at the expense of safety, so include a safety measure."

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong base: absence is divided by days available, not by staff numbers.
- Using the number of leavers rather than the average number employed.
- Treating a measure as an explanation of the cause.
- Assuming PRP always raises motivation.
- Confusing peer assessment (colleagues) with 360 degree (many sources, including subordinates).
- Forgetting holiday entitlement is pro rata for part-time staff.

## Exam technique

Write the formula, put in the numbers, give the result with a unit, and then say what it means. For evaluation, give a strength and a limit of the measure or method and a judgement about the business in the case.
