## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Give two ways in which the nucleotides of DNA differ from those of RNA. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the two purines found in nucleic acids and say how a purine molecule differs in shape from a pyrimidine. **[2]**

**2.** A single-stranded polynucleotide contains 35 nucleotides.
**(a)** State the number of phosphodiester bonds in the strand and the number of water molecules released when it was made. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of phosphodiester bonds in a double-stranded DNA fragment of 35 base pairs. **[1]**

**3.** A DNA fragment has 25 base pairs and 69 hydrogen bonds in total. Calculate the number of A-T pairs and the number of G-C pairs, and state the number of cytosine bases. Show your working. **[4]**

**4.** **(a)** Describe the structure of an ATP molecule. **[3]**
**(b)** State the products of the hydrolysis of ATP. **[1]**

**5.** A cell process needs 183 kJ of energy per mole of product. Hydrolysis of one mole of ATP supplies 30.5 kJ (value supplied).
**(a)** Calculate the minimum number of moles of ATP needed per mole of product. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the real amount is likely to be greater. **[1]**

**6.** In a DNA purification from fruit, the tissue is mashed, then mixed with detergent, salt and a protease, and filtered. Ice-cold ethanol is then poured slowly on top of the filtrate. Explain the purpose of the detergent, the salt, the protease and the ice-cold ethanol. **[5]**

**7.** A student compared the DNA obtained from three plant tissues using the same method. The dried DNA was weighed.

| Tissue | Mass of tissue (g) | Mass of dried DNA (mg) |
|---|---|---|
| Banana | 12.0 | 3.6 |
| Kiwi fruit | 15.0 | 5.7 |
| Spinach leaf | 24.0 | 8.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the mass of DNA in mg per gram of tissue for kiwi fruit and spinach. **[2]**
**(b)** Banana gave 0.30 mg per gram. Calculate the percentage more DNA per gram that kiwi fruit gave compared with banana, to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why the student cannot be confident that kiwi fruit has the most DNA per gram. **[2]**

**8.** Describe how DNA is replicated semi-conservatively, including the roles of helicase and DNA polymerase. **[6]**

**9.** A bacterial culture was kept for many generations on a food source labelled with heavy nitrogen (¹⁵N), so all its DNA was heavy (HH). It was then moved to light nitrogen (¹⁴N) for two generations, then to ¹⁵N again for one generation. Hybrid DNA has one heavy and one light strand (HL).
**(a)** Give the percentages of HH, HL and LL DNA after the two generations in ¹⁴N. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the percentages of each type after the following generation in ¹⁵N, showing your working. **[2]**
**(c)** A conservative model of replication predicts that one wholly original and one wholly new molecule are made each time. Use the first generation in ¹⁴N to explain why the result does not support that model. **[2]**

**10.** A yeast genome has 1.2 × 10⁷ base pairs. Assume that 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ errors occur per base pair per replication (value supplied).
**(a)** Calculate the expected number of errors per replication of the genome. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the expected number of errors across 5,000 replications. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what is meant by saying that spontaneous mutations are random. **[2]**
**(d)** State one way a spontaneous mutation can arise during replication. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how the structure of DNA makes it both a stable store of genetic information and able to be copied accurately. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** DNA nucleotide has deoxyribose and RNA nucleotide has ribose [1]; DNA has thymine where RNA has uracil [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Adenine and guanine [1]; purines have two rings whereas pyrimidines have one ring [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Contains deoxyribose" earns credit only if the comparison is clear, so name the sugar in each nucleotide.

**2.** **(a)** 34 bonds, since 35 − 1 [1]; 34 water molecules, one per bond [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2 × 34 = **68** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say 35 or 70 have counted the units instead of the links between them.

**3.** Let a = A-T pairs and g = G-C pairs; a + g = 25 and 2a + 3g = 69 [1]. Substituting gives 50 − 2g + 3g = 69, so g = **19** [1]. a = 25 − 19 = **6** [1]. Cytosine pairs with guanine, so there are **19 cytosines** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing both equations first makes your method clear and lets you check the arithmetic against them.

**4.** **(a)** A ribose sugar [1]; the base adenine [1]; three inorganic phosphates [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** ADP and inorganic phosphate [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Three phosphates" is stronger than "a phosphate group", because the number is what separates ATP from ADP.

**5.** **(a)** Moles = 183 ÷ 30.5 [1] = **6.0 mol** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Some energy is lost as heat, so more ATP is needed than the minimum [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the unit with the numerical answer; a bare number leaves it unclear what has been calculated.

**6.** Detergent dissolves the phospholipids in membranes (cell surface membrane and nuclear envelope), releasing DNA [1]. Salt (sodium ions) masks the negative charge of the phosphate groups so the DNA strands clump together [1]. Protease digests proteins attached to the DNA [1]. DNA is insoluble in ethanol, so it precipitates [1]. Layering slowly lets the DNA appear as white strands at the boundary [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For each reagent, state the effect on the cell or on the DNA, not just "it helps extract DNA".

**7.** **(a)** Kiwi: 5.7 ÷ 15.0 = **0.38 mg per g** [1]; spinach: 8.4 ÷ 24.0 = **0.35 mg per g** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (0.38 − 0.30) ÷ 0.30 × 100 [1] = **27%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Only one result per tissue, so no repeats or mean [1]; the differences between 0.30, 0.35 and 0.38 are small [1]. (Equally creditable: the product is crude and contains RNA and protein, so the mass is not all DNA.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage answer should show which value is the baseline; dividing by the wrong tissue gives a different figure.

**8.** Helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs [1]. The two strands separate and the helix unwinds [1]. Each strand acts as a template [1]. Free nucleotides line up by complementary base pairing, A with T and G with C [1]. DNA polymerase joins the nucleotides by forming phosphodiester bonds [1]. Both daughter molecules are made of an original strand paired with a new strand, which is what semi-conservative means [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what each enzyme does to which bond; "helicase unzips DNA" on its own is too vague.

**9.** **(a)** After the first generation in ¹⁴N all are HL; after the second, 2 HL and 2 LL out of 4, so **50% HL and 50% LL** [1]; **0% HH** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Each of the 2 HL gives 1 HH and 1 HL; each of the 2 LL gives 2 HL, so 8 molecules: 2 HH, 6 HL [1]. **25% HH, 75% HL, 0% LL** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** A conservative model predicts one heavy and one light molecule, with no hybrid [1]; the result was all hybrid (HL), which fits only semi-conservative replication [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the working molecule by molecule, so a slip in the final percentage is traceable.

**10.** **(a)** 1.2 × 10⁷ × 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ [1] = **3.6 × 10⁻³** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 3.6 × 10⁻³ × 5,000 = **18** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** It is not possible to predict which base or which gene will change [1]; each is equally likely to occur at any replication (the change is not directed by need) [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** A wrong nucleotide is paired with the template and joined into the new strand [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Random" needs an explanation in terms of prediction or direction; repeating "it happens by chance" gains little.

**11.** The sugar-phosphate backbone is held by strong covalent phosphodiester bonds [1]. The bases are on the inside, protected by the backbone [1]. Many hydrogen bonds between the bases hold the strands together, which makes the molecule stable [1]. Each hydrogen bond is weak, so helicase can separate the strands for copying [1]. The sequence of bases carries the genetic information [1]. Complementary base pairing (A with T, G with C) means each strand can be used as a template to give the same sequence [1]. In semi-conservative replication each daughter molecule keeps one original strand, which conserves information [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer links each feature to a function; listing features without the consequence gains less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing hydrogen bonds for the link between nucleotides in the same strand.
- Giving the number of nucleotides when asked for bonds.
- Forgetting the unit or a stated baseline in a calculation.
- Describing helicase or DNA polymerase without naming the bond affected.
- Using "DNA" and "gene" or "base" interchangeably.
- Explaining DNA purification as a list of steps without saying why.
- Confusing the pyrimidine and purine families.
- Treating a single run as reliable when comparing yields.
- Mixing up conservative and semi-conservative predictions.
