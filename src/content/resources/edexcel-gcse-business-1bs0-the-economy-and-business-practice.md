## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to benefit a UK business that exports its products? **[1]**

A: A rise in the value of the pound

B: A fall in the value of the pound

C: A rise in interest rates

D: A rise in inflation

**2.** Define the term inflation. **[1]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are likely effects of rising unemployment on a business selling luxury holidays? **[2]**

A: Sales are likely to fall

B: Recruitment of staff is likely to be harder

C: Customers are likely to trade up to more expensive holidays

D: There may be more applicants for each job vacancy

E: Interest on its loans will fall

**4.** State **one** way a rise in income tax could affect a business's sales. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** impact of a rise in interest rates on a furniture shop. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** impact of rising inflation on a business's costs. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Hartfield Wines imports wine from France at €9 a bottle. It buys 6,000 bottles a year. The exchange rate changes from £1 = €1.20 to £1 = €1.125.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of one bottle in pounds before and after the change. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the increase in Hartfield Wines' annual cost of wine. **[2]**

**8.** Calder Cycles, a UK business, exports bikes to the USA at a UK price of £450. The exchange rate changes from £1 = $1.30 to £1 = $1.20.

**(a)** Calculate the dollar price of a bike before and after the change. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline **one** likely effect of this change on Calder Cycles. **[2]**

**9.** Maple Leaf Builders has a loan of £80,000 on which it pays interest each year. The interest rate rises from 6% to 7.5%. Calculate the increase in its annual interest cost. **[2]**

**10.** Ellie runs Truffle Box, which sells premium chocolates by post. Consumer incomes are falling. Analyse the impact of falling consumer incomes on Truffle Box. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** way an increase in unemployment could benefit a business. **[3]**

**12.** Westcote Coffee Roasters buys green coffee beans from Brazil, paid for in US dollars, and sells roasted coffee to UK cafés. Over the past year inflation has risen, interest rates have risen and the pound has fallen in value against the dollar. The business has a £50,000 loan with a variable interest rate. Evaluate which of these changes is likely to have the greatest impact on Westcote Coffee Roasters. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A general rise in prices over time [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. D [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Customers have less income left after tax, so they may spend less and sales may fall [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Many customers buy furniture using credit [1]. Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive [1], so customers may delay buying sofas or beds, reducing the shop's sales [1]. (Alternatively: the shop's own loan repayments rise, reducing profit.) **[3]**

**6.** Inflation means the prices of materials and energy rise [1], so the business pays more for the same inputs [1]. Unless it can raise its own prices without losing customers, its profit margin will fall [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Before: €9 ÷ 1.20 = **£7.50** [1]. After: €9 ÷ 1.125 = **£8.00** [1]

**(b)** Increase per bottle = £0.50 [1]. 6,000 × £0.50 = **£3,000** a year [1] **[4]**

**8. (a)** Before: £450 × 1.30 = **$585** [1]. After: £450 × 1.20 = **$540** [1]

**(b)** The bikes are $45 cheaper for American customers [1], so Calder Cycles is likely to sell more bikes in the USA without cutting its £450 price [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Before: 6% × £80,000 = £4,800. After: 7.5% × £80,000 = £6,000 [1]. Increase = **£1,200** a year [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Premium chocolates are a luxury that customers can easily cut back on [1], so when incomes fall Truffle Box's sales are likely to fall [1]. Customers may switch to cheaper supermarket chocolates [1], so Truffle Box may need to offer smaller, cheaper boxes or special offers to keep customers [1], which would reduce its revenue per sale [1]. However, some customers still buy gifts for birthdays and special occasions, so demand at these times may hold up better than everyday spending [1]. **[6]**

**11.** When unemployment rises, more people apply for each job [1]. The business can choose from a larger group of applicants [1], so it is more likely to find skilled staff quickly and may not need to raise wages to attract them, keeping costs down [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The weaker pound means Westcote must pay more pounds for the same dollar price of beans [1]. Beans are its main raw material, so this raises the cost of every bag of coffee it sells [1].

Inflation raises other costs such as energy for roasting and wages [1], although it may allow Westcote to raise its prices if its café customers face rising prices everywhere [1].

The rise in interest rates increases the cost of its £50,000 variable-rate loan [1]; for example, a 2 percentage point rise would add £1,000 a year (2% × £50,000) [1], and cafés may sell less if their customers have less to spend [1].

Judgement: the fall in the pound is likely to have the greatest impact, because it affects the cost of the beans in every sale, whereas the loan cost is a fixed amount that is probably small compared with the total cost of beans [1]. This depends on how much Westcote spends on beans each year and whether it can pass the higher cost on to cafés without losing them to rival roasters [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The source gives no interest rate figures, so an illustrative calculation must be labelled as an example. The best answers compare the size of each effect rather than describing all three separately.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Multiplying instead of dividing when converting euros to pounds in Question 7.
- Saying a weaker pound helps importers.
- Describing falling incomes in general in Question 10 without saying that Truffle Box sells a luxury.
- Discussing all three changes in Question 12 without deciding which matters most.
