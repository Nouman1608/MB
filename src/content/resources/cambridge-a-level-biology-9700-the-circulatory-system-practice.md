These questions cover sub-topic **8.1 The circulatory system** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AS Level, Paper 1 and Paper 2 style, with Paper 3 recognition skills). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [circulatory system study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-circulatory-system/) and the [circulatory system revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-circulatory-system-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which vessel carries oxygenated blood into the heart?

A: aorta

B: pulmonary artery

C: pulmonary vein

D: vena cava **[1]**

**2.** Which white blood cell has a lobed nucleus and granular cytoplasm?

A: lymphocyte

B: monocyte

C: neutrophil

D: red blood cell **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a *closed* circulation and give **one** advantage of it. **[2]**

**4.** Name the **two** properties of water that the syllabus links to its role in transport in mammals. **[2]**

**5.** State **three** functions of tissue fluid. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** The aorta is an elastic artery. An artery supplying the kidney is a muscular artery.

**(a)** Explain how the structure of the aorta wall is related to its function.

**(b)** Explain how the structure of a muscular artery is related to its function. **[5]**

**7.** A photomicrograph at × 25 shows an artery and a vein in transverse section.

| Measurement on image | Artery | Vein |
|---|---|---|
| Wall thickness / mm | 4.0 | 1.5 |
| Lumen diameter / mm | 10.0 | 25.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the actual wall thickness of the vein in µm.

**(b)** Calculate the wall thickness to lumen diameter ratio for each vessel.

**(c)** Explain the difference between the ratios.

**(d)** Give **one** feature, other than wall thickness, that would help you identify the vein in a longitudinal section. **[7]**

**8.** Relate the structure of capillaries to their function of exchanging substances with tissues. **[4]**

**9.** A student viewed a blood smear at × 1500. A red blood cell measured 11 mm across. A cell with a kidney-shaped nucleus measured 27 mm across.

**(a)** Calculate the actual diameter of each cell.

**(b)** Name the cell with the kidney-shaped nucleus.

**(c)** State **two** ways in which a lymphocyte would look different from the cell in (b). **[5]**

**10.** Describe how tissue fluid forms at the arterial end of a capillary and how much of it returns at the venous end. **[5]**

**11.** In one tissue, 30.0 cm³ of fluid leaves the capillaries per minute and 27.3 cm³ returns to them.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the fluid that does not return to the capillaries.

**(b)** State what happens to this fluid.

**(c)** In a person with liver disease, fewer plasma proteins are made. Suggest and explain why their ankles may swell. **[6]**

**12.** Explain how the solvent properties of water and its high specific heat capacity make it suitable as the main component of blood. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Blood remains inside vessels at all times [1]; advantage: blood pressure can be kept high, or flow can be directed to particular organs [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Solvent action [1]; high specific heat capacity [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Any three from: supplies oxygen to cells; supplies glucose, amino acids or other nutrients; removes carbon dioxide; removes other waste such as urea; provides a stable environment for cells [3]. Total: 3 marks.

**6. (a)** Thick layer of elastic fibres [1]; stretches when blood is forced in during ventricular systole and recoils between beats [1]; so pressure is smoothed and kept high, and blood keeps moving [1].

**(b)** Relatively more smooth muscle in the tunica media [1]; contracts to narrow the lumen or relaxes to widen it, controlling blood flow to the organ [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**7. (a)** 1.5 mm = 1500 µm [1]; 1500 ÷ 25 = **60 µm** [1].

**(b)** Artery: 4.0 ÷ 10.0 = **0.40** [1]; vein: 1.5 ÷ 25.0 = **0.06** [1].

**(c)** The artery carries blood at high pressure, so needs a thick wall [1]; the vein carries blood at low pressure, so a wide lumen reduces resistance and a thin wall is enough [1].

**(d)** Valves [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**8.** Any four from: wall only one endothelial cell thick, so short diffusion distance; gaps between endothelial cells let plasma leave to form tissue fluid; narrow lumen, so red blood cells are close to the wall; narrow lumen slows blood, giving time for exchange; very many capillaries give a large surface area; every cell is close to a capillary [4]. Total: 4 marks.

**9. (a)** Red blood cell: 11 000 ÷ 1500 = **7.3 µm** [1]; other cell: 27 000 ÷ 1500 = **18 µm** [1].

**(b)** Monocyte [1].

**(c)** Any two from: smaller; round nucleus; nucleus fills most of the cell; only a thin rim of cytoplasm [2]. Total: 5 marks.

**10.** At the arterial end, blood (hydrostatic) pressure is high [1]; plasma is forced out through gaps between endothelial cells [1]; red blood cells and most plasma proteins are too large to leave and stay in the blood [1]; at the venous end, hydrostatic pressure is lower and the plasma proteins give blood a lower water potential [1]; so water moves back into the capillary by osmosis [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**11. (a)** 30.0 − 27.3 = 2.7 cm³ [1]; 2.7 ÷ 30.0 × 100 = **9.0 %** [1].

**(b)** It drains into lymph vessels (becomes lymph) [1].

**(c)** Fewer plasma proteins, so the water potential of the blood is higher (less negative) [1]; less water returns to capillaries by osmosis at the venous end [1]; tissue fluid accumulates faster than it can drain, and gravity makes it collect in the ankles [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* the mark is for linking plasma proteins to water potential; "less protein so less osmosis" without water potential rarely scores.

**12.** Water is polar, so ions and polar molecules dissolve in it [1]; so substances such as glucose, amino acids, urea and hydrogencarbonate ions are carried in solution in plasma [1]; high specific heat capacity means a lot of energy is needed to change its temperature, so blood temperature stays stable [1]; blood can carry heat from active organs to other parts of the body [1]. Total: 4 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining arteries by oxygen content.
- Using the wrong unit when converting image size.
- Naming plasma proteins as a component of tissue fluid.
- Describing tissue fluid formation without the venous end.
- Swapping monocyte and neutrophil nuclei.
