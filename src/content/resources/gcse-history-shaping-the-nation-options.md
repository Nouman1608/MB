This guide covers **Paper 2: Shaping the Nation** of AQA GCSE History (8145) in more depth than a
general course overview -- specifically, the named thematic study and British depth study options AQA
offers, since the [course structure guide](/resources/gcse-history-course-structure/) covers only the
two-component shape of the qualification and does not itemise these named choices. It complements the
existing [Paper 1 options guide](/resources/gcse-history-understanding-the-modern-world-options/) and
the separate [Historic Environment study resources](/resources/gcse-history-historic-environment-revision-notes/),
which cover the shared site-study skill within this paper's depth studies rather than the named content
options themselves.

## Where this fits in 8145

Paper 2 combines one thematic study (Section A) and one British depth study including the historic
environment (Section B). Every centre teaching AQA GCSE History selects one option from each section --
so, as with Paper 1, two schools sitting the same exam paper format may be revising entirely different
historical content.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE HISTORY (8145) — PAPER 2: SHAPING THE NATION**

**Section A: Thematic studies** (choose one) --

- **AA. Britain: Health and the People, c1000 to the present day** -- medicine and public health across
  four parts: medieval medicine and the Black Death; the Renaissance's challenge to medical authority and
  inoculation; the germ theory revolution in surgery and public health reform; and modern treatment,
  wartime medical advances, and the creation of the NHS.
- **BB. Britain: Power and the People, c1170 to the present day** -- the citizen's relationship with the
  state across four parts: Magna Carta and the origins of Parliament; the English Revolution and the
  extension of royal challenge; the Great Reform Act, Chartism and workers' movements; and 20th-century
  equality and rights, including women's suffrage and minority rights.
- **AC. Britain: Migration, Empires and the People, c790 to the present day** -- Britain's interaction
  with the wider world across four parts: Viking and Norman invasions; colonisation of the Caribbean and
  North America; expansion in India and Africa; and the end of Empire and its 20th-century legacy,
  including Windrush.

**Section B: British depth studies** (choose one, each including its own historic environment component
worth roughly 10% of the whole course) --

- **BA. Norman England, c1066-c1100** -- the Conquest and its military aspects, feudal government and
  Domesday Book, and the Norman Church and monasticism.
- **BB. Medieval England: The Reign of Edward I, 1272-1307** -- government and the Model Parliament, life
  and the legal system, and Edward's military campaigns in Wales and Scotland.
- **BC. Elizabethan England, c1568-1603** -- Elizabeth's court and the problem of a female ruler, the
  Elizabethan "Golden Age" and poverty, and religious troubles including Mary Queen of Scots and the
  Spanish Armada.
- **BD. Restoration England, 1660-1685** -- the restored Crown and Catholic plots, the Great Plague and
  Fire of London, and land, trade and naval war.

## How the two sections differ in structure

Section A thematic studies span 700-1,200 years and are organised into four chronological parts each,
tracing one theme (health, power, or migration and empire) across the very long term. Section B depth
studies instead cover a single 20-40 year period in close focus, organised into three content parts plus
a fourth part devoted specifically to the historic environment. Because of this structural difference,
thematic studies reward being able to trace a single thread of change across centuries, while depth
studies reward close, detailed knowledge of a short, specific period -- confirm which kind of thinking
your revision plan is actually built around before assuming the same approach works for both halves of
Paper 2.

## How to approach the thematic study

Each thematic study is explicitly built around a small set of recurring factors -- for AA Health and the
People, these are war, religion, chance, government, communication, science and technology, and the role
of the individual; similar factor lists exist for BB and AC. A reliable revision structure is to take
each major development you have studied and ask which of these named factors explains it, and whether
more than one factor worked together -- the specification explicitly rewards showing how factors
interacted, not just naming the most obvious single cause. Because these are long-span themes, also
build a simple chronological anchor list of major turning points (for AA, this might be the Black Death,
Jenner's vaccination, the germ theory revolution, and the creation of the NHS) so you can place any
detail you are asked about within the right part of the course.

## How to approach the depth study, including the historic environment

Because each depth study's fourth part is a historic environment component tied to a specific,
annually-published site, confirm your current site with your teacher before revising -- the
[Historic Environment study resources](/resources/gcse-history-historic-environment-revision-notes/)
cover the general skill (location, function, structure, people connected with the site, design, and how
the site connects to the wider depth study) that applies whichever specific site and depth study option
your school has been assigned. For the three main content parts of your chosen depth study, practise
explaining how political, religious, social and cultural strands of the period connect to each other --
BC Elizabethan England, for instance, expects students to connect the religious threat from Catholic
plots to the political question of the succession, not to revise "religion" and "politics" as separate,
unconnected topics.

## Worked example: connecting a historic environment site to its depth study

Whichever specific site your school has been assigned, a reliable structure for a historic-environment
question is to state the site's key feature (for example, a castle's defensive design), connect that
feature to a specific event or development from the depth study's other three parts (for BA Norman
England, this might be the Harrying of the North or a specific 1067-1075 revolt), and then explain what
the site reveals about the period's values or society (for example, what the scale of castle-building
reveals about how the Normans maintained control over a conquered population). Naming the feature
without connecting it back to the wider depth study content is the single most common way marks are lost
on this component.

## Common mistakes

Revising a thematic study or depth study option your school did not actually choose -- always confirm
your exact options (for example, "AA Health and the People" rather than "BB Power and the People", and
"BC Elizabethan England" rather than "BA Norman England") before using any revision resource. Treating a
thematic study as a list of isolated events rather than a connected chain of cause and change across
centuries. Revising depth-study content and the historic environment component as entirely separate,
when the historic environment's fourth part is explicitly meant to connect back to the same period's
political, social and religious content covered in the depth study's first three parts.

## Quick revision checklist

- Confirm exactly which thematic study (AA/BB/AC) and which British depth study (BA/BB/BC/BD) your
  school teaches for Paper 2, and which specific historic environment site applies this series.
- For your thematic study, build a chronological anchor list of major turning points and practise
  explaining each through the specification's named factors (war, religion, government, and so on).
- For your depth study, practise connecting the political, religious and social strands of your specific
  period rather than revising them as separate topics.
- Use the [Historic Environment study resources](/resources/gcse-history-historic-environment-revision-notes/)
  for the shared site-study skill, and connect it explicitly back to your specific depth study's content.
- Do not confuse Paper 2's options with [Paper 1's period study and wider world depth study options](/resources/gcse-history-understanding-the-modern-world-options/)
  -- the two papers draw on entirely different named content.
