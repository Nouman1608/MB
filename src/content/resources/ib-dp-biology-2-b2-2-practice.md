## Questions

**1.** Explain why a ribosome is classed as an organelle but a plant cell wall is not. **[2]**

**2.** Leaf cells were homogenised and the homogenate was spun at increasing speeds.

**(a)** State why the homogenising solution was buffered. **[1]**

**(b)** The pellets were tested as shown. Identify the main organelle in each pellet.

| Pellet | Speed | Contains | Notable activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low | Much DNA | Little enzyme activity |
| 2 | Medium | Green pigment | Light-dependent oxygen release |
| 3 | Higher | Small amount of DNA | Krebs cycle enzymes |
| 4 | Highest | rRNA | Peptide bond formation |

**[4]**

**3.** Explain how the development of the ultracentrifuge changed the study of organelles. **[2]**

**4.** In bacteria, ribosomes can begin translating an mRNA molecule before its transcription is complete. Explain why this cannot happen in eukaryotic cells and the advantage to the eukaryotic cell. **[3]**

**5.** A white blood cell engulfs a bacterium. Outline how the bacterium is digested without damage to the white blood cell. **[3]**

**6.** Lysosomal enzymes work best at about pH 5, while the cytoplasm is about pH 7.2. Suggest why the release of enzymes from one damaged lysosome causes little harm to the cell. **[2]**

**7.** (HL only) Explain two ways in which the structure of the chloroplast is adapted for photosynthesis. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only) The same number of protons, 9.0 × 10⁴, is pumped into a space of 3.0 × 10⁻³ µm³ and into a space of 3.0 × 10⁻² µm³.

**(a)** Calculate the proton concentration in each space. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the significance of these values for mitochondrial structure. **[1]**

**9.** (HL only) Describe what happens to the nuclear envelope during mitosis and explain why. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) Distinguish between proteins made by free ribosomes and proteins made by ribosomes on the rough endoplasmic reticulum, giving an example of each. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) A pancreatic cell secretes a digestive enzyme. Outline the route of the enzyme from its synthesis to its release from the cell. **[4]**

**12.** (HL only) Outline the role of clathrin in the formation of vesicles. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A ribosome is a discrete structure inside the cell with a specific function, protein synthesis [1]; the cell wall lies outside the plasma membrane, so it is not a subunit within the cell [1] **[2]**

**2. (a)** To keep the pH constant so that proteins and enzymes are not denatured [1]

**(b)** Pellet 1: nuclei [1]. Pellet 2: chloroplasts [1]. Pellet 3: mitochondria [1]. Pellet 4: ribosomes [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Use the evidence in the table, such as Krebs cycle enzymes for mitochondria, rather than relying on settling order alone.

**3.** It allowed organelles to be separated from each other by centrifuging at different speeds [1]; each fraction could then be studied biochemically to find out what that organelle does, so progress followed the new technique [1] **[2]**

**4.** In eukaryotes, transcription happens in the nucleus and translation in the cytoplasm, separated by the nuclear envelope [1]; mRNA must leave through nuclear pores before ribosomes can reach it [1]. This allows the RNA to be modified, for example by removing introns, before it is translated [1] **[3]**

**5.** The bacterium is enclosed in a phagocytic vacuole formed by endocytosis [1]. Lysosomes fuse with the vacuole and release hydrolytic enzymes into it [1]. Digestion takes place inside the membrane-bound vacuole, so the enzymes do not reach the cytoplasm, and useful products pass out [1] **[3]**

**6.** The enzymes are diluted in the large volume of cytoplasm [1], and at pH 7.2 they are far from their optimum pH, so their activity is low [1] **[2]**

**7.** Any two adaptations, each explained. Thylakoid membranes give a large surface area [1] for photosystems and ATP synthase, so more light is absorbed and more ATP made [1]. The small thylakoid space lets pumped protons build up quickly [1], giving a steep gradient for ATP synthesis [1]. Either point may be replaced by: the stroma concentrates the enzymes and substrates of the Calvin cycle, speeding carbon fixation. **[4]**

**8. (a)** 9.0 × 10⁴ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁻³ = **3.0 × 10⁷ per µm³** [1]; 9.0 × 10⁴ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁻² = **3.0 × 10⁶ per µm³** [1]

**(b)** The narrow intermembrane space of the mitochondrion means that pumping builds a high proton concentration quickly, giving a steep gradient to drive ATP synthase [1] **[3]**

**9.** The nuclear envelope breaks down into small vesicles [1]; this allows spindle microtubules to reach and attach to the chromosomes, and the vesicles fuse later to re-form envelopes around the daughter nuclei [1] **[2]**

**10.** Free ribosomes make proteins that stay in the cytosol, such as enzymes of glycolysis [1]; ribosomes on the rER make proteins for secretion, for lysosomes or for membranes, such as digestive enzymes [1] **[2]**

**11.** The enzyme is made by ribosomes on the rER and enters the rER lumen, where it folds [1]. It is carried in a transport vesicle to the Golgi apparatus [1]. It is modified as it passes through the Golgi cisternae [1]. A secretory vesicle carries it to the plasma membrane, where it is released by exocytosis [1] **[4]**

**12.** Clathrin proteins assemble into a cage-like coat on the cytoplasmic side of a membrane [1]; the coat helps the membrane curve and pinch off as a vesicle, for example in endocytosis [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing the cell wall or cytoplasm as organelles.
- Identifying fractions by speed only and ignoring the test results.
- Giving "protection of DNA" as the main advantage of the nucleus.
- Leaving out the membrane around the phagocytic vacuole.
- Describing a structure without the function it enables.
- Skipping the Golgi apparatus in the secretory route.
