These questions cover key question 2.1 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, "Was the Treaty of Versailles fair?", the
Paper 2 prescribed topic for June 2026.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the terms of the Treaty of Versailles concerning the Rhineland and the Saar. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** of Wilson's Fourteen Points. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the territorial terms of the Treaty of Versailles in eastern Europe. **[4]**

**4.** Describe the hyperinflation in Germany in 1923. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** violent challenges to the Weimar Republic between 1919 and 1923. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the reactions in Germany to the signing of the Treaty in 1919. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why was Lloyd George not fully satisfied with the Treaty of Versailles? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did the Treaty of Versailles cause political instability in Germany up to 1923? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Clemenceau got what he wanted from the Treaty of Versailles." How far do you agree? Explain your answer.
**[10]**

**10.** "The Treaty of Versailles could be justified at the time." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined speech by a German member of the National Assembly, June 1919. "They call this
a peace. Our soldiers left France undefeated in the field, and now we are told we alone began the war, we alone must
pay, and we must stand defenceless while every neighbour arms. No German who signs this can look his children in the
eye."

How far is Source A a reliable account of the Treaty of Versailles? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your
answer. **[8]**

**12.** Study Source B, which is invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary British cartoon from 1919, written for this page. Clemenceau, as

Wilson stand behind him; Lloyd George holds a smaller knife and Wilson looks away, holding a book labelled "Fourteen

What is the message of the cartoon described in Source B? Use details of the source and your knowledge to explain

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: the Rhineland was demilitarised (1); no German troops or fortifications were allowed there
(1); Allied troops would occupy it (1) for fifteen years (1); the Saar would be run by the League of Nations (1) for
fifteen years (1); its coal mines went to France (1); a plebiscite would then decide its future (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: open diplomacy without secret treaties (1); freedom of the seas (1); free trade (1);
disarmament (1); self-determination for the peoples of Europe (1); an independent Poland with access to the sea (1); a
League of Nations (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: West Prussia and Posen were given to Poland (1); this created the Polish Corridor
separating East Prussia from the rest of Germany (1); Danzig became a free city under the League (1); Memel was taken
from Germany (1); a plebiscite was to decide Upper Silesia, part of which went to Poland in 1921 (1); union with
Austria was forbidden (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: the government printed money to pay striking workers during the Ruhr occupation (1);
prices rose at a staggering rate (1); by November 1923 the mark was almost worthless (1); workers were paid more than
once a day and spent wages at once (1); savings were wiped out, hurting the middle classes (1); people with debts or
mortgages could pay them off cheaply (1); many blamed the Republic and the Treaty (1); Stresemann introduced the
Rentenmark in November 1923 (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Spartacist rising (January 1919) (1); the Kapp Putsch (March 1920) (1); the murders of
Erzberger (1921) or Rathenau (1922) (1); the Munich Putsch (November 1923) (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: Germans were shocked by the terms (1); they called it a Diktat because they had not
negotiated (1); the government of Scheidemann resigned rather than sign (1); the National Assembly accepted it only
because the Allies threatened to resume the war (1); nationalists blamed the "November criminals" (1); war guilt was
especially resented (1); a German newspaper printed protests and calls for revenge (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **he feared the
Treaty was too harsh**: as in the Fontainebleau Memorandum, he believed German resentment could lead to another war;
**trade**: a crippled Germany could not buy British goods; **the Polish settlement** put many Germans under foreign
rule, which he saw as a future danger; **but he also won much** of what Britain wanted, such as the German navy and
colonies, so his dissatisfaction centred on reparations and Germany's frontiers. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **the new republic was blamed** for accepting the Treaty, so
nationalists saw its leaders as traitors; **the "stab in the back" myth** gave the right a reason to reject democracy,
shown by the Kapp Putsch and political murders; **the army limit** left many former soldiers bitter, and some joined
the Freikorps; **reparations and the Ruhr occupation** brought hyperinflation, which encouraged extremism such as the
Munich Putsch. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:**
Alsace-Lorraine returned; German army cut to 100,000; Rhineland demilitarised and occupied; Saar coal to France; war
guilt accepted, justifying reparations. **Disagree:** he wanted the Rhineland detached as a separate state but did not
get it; the Anglo-American guarantee lapsed when the Senate rejected the Treaty; the reparations figure was delayed and
later proved hard to collect; Germany kept its industry and remained larger than France; French critics thought him
too lenient, and he failed to become President of France in 1920. **Judgement:** he gained more than Wilson, but not the security
he most wanted. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** Germany had imposed Brest-Litovsk on Russia in 1918; it had invaded Belgium
and devastated northern France; many people in the victorious countries believed Germany started the war; the Treaty
did not break Germany up; compromises softened several terms. **Disagree:** Germany was not allowed to negotiate; it
alone was blamed; self-determination was not applied to Germans; Keynes argued in 1919 that reparations would ruin
Europe's economy; the US Senate rejected it; Foch is reported to have called it only an armistice. **Judgement:** for
example, it could be justified as a response to the war, but even at the time many observers doubted it would keep
the peace. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 4 (7–8): evaluates reliability through content checked against knowledge and through purpose,
with a judgement. Level 3 (5–6): evaluates using content or provenance. Level 2 (2–4): accepts or rejects the source
with simple reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. **Accurate:** Article 231 placed responsibility on Germany;
reparations were required; German forces were limited while the victors kept theirs. **Unreliable or one-sided:** the
claim that soldiers were "undefeated" repeats the "stab in the back" myth, since the army had asked for an armistice;
"we alone must pay" ignores that other defeated powers also faced treaties; the speaker is a German politician
appealing to national feeling at the moment of signing, so exaggeration is likely. **Judgement:** reliable as evidence
of German feeling in June 1919, not as a balanced account of the Treaty. **[8]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5–6): explains a message supported by details and context. Level 2 (3–4): explains a message
from details. Level 1 (1–2): describes details or gives a surface reading. Indicative content: **message**: the
Treaty was being driven by Clemenceau's wish to punish Germany through reparations; Lloyd George shares in it on a
smaller scale; Wilson ignores his own principles; Germany is already weak; **context**: French demands for heavy
reparations and security; Lloyd George's election promises; Wilson's compromises over self-determination; a British
cartoonist may be blaming France for a peace some in Britain thought too harsh. **[6]**
