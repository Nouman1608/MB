## Section A

**1.** Define *metallic bonding*. **[2]**

**2.** State the number of delocalised electrons contributed per atom by (a) sodium and (b) aluminium. **[2]**

**3.** Name the particles that occupy the lattice positions in a piece of solid copper. **[1]**

**4.** Which statement about solid magnesium is correct?

A: It contains Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions.

B: Its electrons are shared in pairs between neighbouring atoms.

C: It contains Mg²⁺ ions and delocalised electrons.

D: It contains Mg atoms held by intermolecular forces. **[1]**

**5.** State two factors, other than the number of delocalised electrons, that affect the strength of metallic bonding. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the bonding in a metal is described as non-directional. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The melting points of three Period 3 metals are: sodium 98 °C, magnesium 650 °C, aluminium 660 °C.

**(a)** Explain why magnesium has a much higher melting point than sodium. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the increase from magnesium to aluminium is predicted by the metallic bonding model. **[1]**

**8.** The melting points of three Group 1 metals are: lithium 181 °C, sodium 98 °C, potassium 63 °C.

**(a)** State one feature of the metallic bonding that is the same in all three metals. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the trend in melting points. **[3]**

**9.** Copper is used for electrical wiring.

**(a)** Explain how solid copper conducts electricity. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether molten copper conducts electricity, with a reason. **[1]**

**10.** Solid sodium conducts electricity but solid sodium chloride does not. Both conduct when molten.

**(a)** Identify the charge carriers in solid sodium. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why solid sodium chloride does not conduct. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify the charge carriers in molten sodium chloride. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes: "In a metal, the metal atoms are held together by ionic bonds between positive and negative ions."

**(a)** Identify two errors in this statement. **[2]**

**(b)** Rewrite the statement so that it is correct. **[1]**

**12.** Substance Y is a solid with a high melting point. It conducts electricity in the solid state and when molten. It does not dissolve in water or in hexane.

**(a)** Deduce the type of bonding in Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how each of the three observations supports your deduction. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Electrostatic attraction [1] between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons [1].

**2. (a)** One [1].

**(b)** Three [1].

**3.** Positive copper ions (metal cations) [1].

**4.** C [1].

**5.** The charge on the metal ion (higher charge, stronger bonding) [1]; the size (radius) of the metal ion (smaller ion, stronger bonding) [1].

**6.** Each positive ion attracts the delocalised electrons all around it [1], so the attraction acts in all directions rather than along a line between two particular atoms [1].

**7. (a)** Magnesium forms Mg²⁺ and contributes two delocalised electrons per atom, compared with Na⁺ and one electron for sodium [1]. Mg²⁺ is smaller than Na⁺ [1]. The electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons is stronger in magnesium, so more energy is needed to overcome it [1].

**(b)** Aluminium forms a 3+ ion (smaller still) and contributes three delocalised electrons per atom, so the attraction is stronger again [1].

**8. (a)** Each forms a 1+ ion / each contributes one delocalised electron per atom [1].

**(b)** The ions become larger down the group [1], so the delocalised electrons are further from the nucleus of each ion [1]. The attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons becomes weaker, so less energy is needed to melt the metal [1].

**9. (a)** Copper contains delocalised electrons that are free to move through the lattice [1]; when a potential difference is applied they flow towards the positive terminal, carrying charge [1].

**(b)** Yes: the delocalised electrons are still present and mobile in the liquid [1].

**10. (a)** Delocalised electrons [1].

**(b)** Its ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice, so there are no mobile charge carriers [1].

**(c)** Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions (now free to move) [1].

**11. (a)** The lattice contains positive ions, not atoms [1]; there are no negative ions and the bonding is not ionic (the negative charge is the delocalised electrons) [1].

**(b)** "In a metal, positive metal ions are held together by electrostatic attraction to delocalised electrons." [1]

**12. (a)** Metallic bonding [1].

**(b)** High melting point: strong electrostatic attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons throughout a giant lattice needs a lot of energy to overcome [1]. Conducts as solid and liquid: delocalised electrons are mobile in both states (an ionic solid would not conduct) [1]. Insoluble in water and hexane: neither solvent can overcome the metallic bonding (there are no separate molecules or ions for the solvent to surround) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "metal atoms" instead of "positive metal ions" in the definition.
- Writing "electrons" without "delocalised".
- Saying the ions move to carry current in a solid metal.
- Explaining the Group 1 trend with "more electrons", when each atom still contributes one; the change is ion size.
- Giving "stronger bonds" as the whole explanation for a higher melting point.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the jump from sodium to magnesium larger than from magnesium to aluminium?**
The model predicts an increase in both steps. The size of each step is not something the AS syllabus asks you to explain, so concentrate on the direction of the trend and its causes.
