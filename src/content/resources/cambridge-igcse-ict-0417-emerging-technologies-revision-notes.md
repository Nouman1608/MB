Section 1.5 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers the impact of emerging technologies on everyday life, naming artificial intelligence (AI) and extended reality, which is split into virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR).

Condensed for revision. For full explanations and a model discuss answer, use the [emerging technologies study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-emerging-technologies/), then try the [emerging technologies practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-emerging-technologies-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus item | What you must recall |
|---|---|
| Impact of emerging technologies | Effects on everyday life of AI and of extended reality (virtual and augmented) |

## The impact formula

**Technology + where it is used + what changes for people.**
"AI translation software lets tourists read signs and menus in another language, so they can travel more independently."

More impact points written to the formula:

- VR walk-throughs let buyers explore a home before it is built, so they can change the design before building starts.
- AR repair instructions appear over the machine itself, so a technician makes fewer mistakes.
- AI captions on video calls let people with hearing loss follow conversations in real time.

## Artificial intelligence

**Definition**: a computer system carrying out tasks that normally need human intelligence: recognising speech and images, using language, learning from data, making decisions or predictions.

**Everyday uses**: voice assistants; recommendations in shopping and streaming; translation and predictive text; chatbots; analysing medical scans; detecting card fraud; adaptive learning apps; tools that generate text and images.

| Positive impacts | Negative impacts |
|---|---|
| Automates repetitive tasks, saving time | Routine jobs reduced or replaced; retraining needed |
| Works at any time of day | Collects large amounts of personal data |
| Finds patterns in large data sets (fraud, disease) | Can be biased if trained on unfair data |
| Improves accessibility (speech, captions) | Can be wrong, and people may over-rely on it |
| Personalises learning and services | Realistic fake images, audio and text spread misinformation |
| | Less human contact with services |

## Extended reality

**Extended reality (XR)**: technologies that change how people see their surroundings with computer-generated content.

| | Virtual reality (VR) | Augmented reality (AR) |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Completely computer-generated environment replacing the real view | Digital images or information added to the real view |
| Hardware | Headset, motion sensors, hand controllers | Smartphone or tablet camera and screen, smart glasses |
| Everyday uses | Training pilots, surgeons and firefighters; virtual school trips; games; walking through unbuilt buildings | Furniture placement, virtual try-on, street navigation arrows, museum information, repair instructions, games |

| Positive impacts | Negative impacts |
|---|---|
| Safe practice of dangerous situations | Motion sickness, headaches, eye strain |
| Cheaper than some real training or travel | VR users cannot see real obstacles; AR users distracted outdoors |
| Engaging, visual learning | Expensive equipment limits access |
| Customers preview products before buying | Long use may reduce face-to-face contact |
| | Cameras may record people without permission |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence | Computer systems performing tasks that normally need human intelligence |
| Chatbot | Software that holds a text or spoken conversation with a user |
| Bias (in AI) | Unfair results caused by unfair or unrepresentative data |
| Extended reality | Umbrella term for VR and AR |
| Virtual reality | Fully computer-generated environment replacing the real world |
| Augmented reality | Computer-generated content laid over the real world |
| Headset | Wearable display with a screen for each eye and motion tracking |
| Immersive | Making the user feel present inside the experience |

## Exam traps

- **VR replaces, AR adds.** The real world is not visible in VR.
- An impact is an effect on people, not a description of the hardware.
- "Discuss" needs both positives and negatives; one-sided answers are limited.
- AI is software, not "a robot".
- Avoid repeating one idea twice ("saves time" and "is faster").
- Autonomous vehicles and smart home devices also appear in Topic 5 (microprocessor-controlled devices); if you use them here, focus on the effect of the AI on people.
- No brand names.

## Self-test

1. Define artificial intelligence.
2. Give **two** everyday uses of AI.
3. Give **two** positive impacts of AI on everyday life.
4. Explain how AI could lead to unfair decisions.
5. Give **one** way AI can make it harder to know whether information is genuine.
6. State the difference between virtual reality and augmented reality.
7. Name **two** items of hardware used for virtual reality.
8. Give **two** uses of VR for training.
9. Describe **one** use of augmented reality in shopping.
10. Give **two** health problems linked to using VR for long periods.
11. Why can AR be a safety risk when walking outdoors?
12. Give **one** privacy concern about AR glasses.

## Self-test answers

1. The ability of a computer system to carry out tasks that would normally need human intelligence.
2. Any two: voice assistants, recommendations, translation, chatbots, medical scan analysis, fraud detection, adaptive learning.
3. Any two: saves time by automating tasks; available at any time; spots patterns such as fraud; improves accessibility; personalises services.
4. If it learns from data that is unfair or unrepresentative, its decisions will repeat that unfairness.
5. It can produce realistic fake images, video, audio or text.
6. VR replaces the user's view with a computer-generated environment; AR adds digital content to the real view.
7. Any two: headset; motion sensors; hand controllers or gloves.
8. Any two: pilots, surgeons, firefighters, or any dangerous or costly real-life situation.
9. Using a phone camera to see how furniture would look in a room, or trying on glasses or clothes virtually.
10. Any two: motion sickness, headaches, eye strain.
11. The user looks at the screen instead of their surroundings, so may walk into traffic or obstacles.
12. Built-in cameras can record people nearby without their permission.
