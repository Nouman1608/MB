## 5.1.1 The atom

- Small, **positively charged nucleus** at the centre.
- **Negatively charged electrons** in orbit around it.
- Neutral atom: number of electrons = number of protons.
- **Positive ion:** atom has **lost** electrons.
- **Negative ion:** atom has **gained** electrons.
- Forming an ion never changes the nucleus.

## Alpha-particle scattering (Extended only)

| Observation | Evidence for |
|---|---|
| Most pass straight through or nearly so | Very small nucleus; atom mostly empty space |
| Some deflected through large angles | Nucleus is **positively charged** (repels positive alpha particles) |
| A very few bounce back | Nucleus contains **most of the mass** of the atom |

- Thin metal foil (for example gold) so particles pass through rather than being absorbed; vacuum so air does not stop them.

## 5.1.2 The nucleus

- Contains **protons** and **neutrons** (together called nucleons).
- Relative charges: **proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1**.
- **Proton number (atomic number) Z:** number of protons.
- **Nucleon number (mass number) A:** number of protons + neutrons.
- **Neutrons = A − Z.**
- Nuclide notation: ᴬ_Z X, with A at the top and Z at the bottom (for example ¹⁹₉F).

## Isotopes

- **Isotopes:** atoms of the same element, same proton number, **different numbers of neutrons**.
- An element may have more than one isotope (hydrogen: ¹₁H, ²₁H, ³₁H).
- Different Z means a different element, not an isotope.

## Fission and fusion (Extended only)

| | Fission | Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Splitting a large nucleus | Joining two light nuclei |
| Trigger | Usually absorbs a neutron first | Light nuclei collide and join |
| Extra products | 2 or 3 neutrons | Often a neutron |
| Mass | Products slightly less | Products slightly less |
| Energy | Released | Released |

**Method in steps: balancing a nuclide equation**

1. Add the top numbers (A) on the left.
2. Add the top numbers on the right; the totals must be equal.
3. Repeat for the bottom numbers (Z).
4. Use the difference to find a missing nucleus or the number of neutrons (¹₀n).
5. Name the missing element from its Z, using a symbol you are given.

**Examples**

```
²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁰₅₄Xe + ⁹⁴₃₈Sr + 2 ¹₀n
²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n
```

## Must-know distinctions

- **Isotope vs ion:** isotopes differ in neutrons; ions differ from their atoms in electrons.
- **Proton number vs nucleon number:** Z counts protons only; A counts protons and neutrons.
- **Atom vs nucleus:** the nucleus is tiny but holds almost all the mass; the electrons occupy the space around it.
- **Fission vs fusion (Extended only):** splitting a heavy nucleus vs joining light ones; both release energy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Small, dense, positive centre of the atom |
| Nucleon | A proton or a neutron |
| Proton number (Z) | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Nucleon number (A) | Number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus |
| Ion | Atom that has lost or gained electrons |
| Isotope | Same element, different number of neutrons |
| Fission (Extended only) | Splitting of a nucleus |
| Fusion (Extended only) | Joining of nuclei |

## Exam traps

- **A on top, Z on the bottom.**
- Isotopes differ in **neutrons**, not protons or electrons.
- Ions differ from atoms in **electrons**, not protons.
- Each alpha-scattering observation has its **own** conclusion.
- In fission and fusion the products have **less** mass; give no numbers.
- In an equation, a neutron is ¹₀n: A = 1, Z = 0.

## Self-test

1. Describe the structure of an atom.
2. State the relative charges of a proton, a neutron and an electron.
3. How many neutrons are in ⁶⁵₃₀Zn?
4. An atom of ²⁷₁₃Al loses three electrons. State the charge on the ion and its number of electrons.
5. An atom of ¹⁹₉F gains one electron. How many electrons does the ion have?
6. Define nucleon number.
7. What is meant by isotopes?
8. ¹⁴₆C and ¹⁴₇N have the same nucleon number. Are they isotopes? Explain.
9. *(Extended)* State the relative charge and relative mass of the nucleus ¹⁰⁷₄₇Ag.
10. *(Extended)* Which alpha-scattering observation shows that the nucleus is very small?
11. *(Extended)* In ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹³⁷₅₅Cs + ⁹⁶₃₇Rb + x ¹₀n, find x.
12. *(Extended)* State how the total mass changes in nuclear fusion and what this tells you about energy.

### Answers

1. A small, positive **nucleus** (protons and neutrons) with negative **electrons** orbiting it.
2. **+1, 0, −1**.
3. 65 − 30 = **35**.
4. Charge **+3**; 13 − 3 = **10 electrons**.
5. 9 + 1 = **10**.
6. The number of **protons plus neutrons** in a nucleus.
7. Atoms of the same element (same proton number) with **different numbers of neutrons**.
8. **No**: they have different proton numbers (6 and 7), so they are different elements.
9. Relative charge **+47**; relative mass **107**.
10. **Most** alpha particles pass straight through.
11. 236 − 137 − 96 = 3, so **x = 3** (bottom row: 92 = 55 + 37).
12. The mass **decreases** slightly; the lost mass is **released as energy**.
