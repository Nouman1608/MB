All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Brook Bakery.

> **Case study: Brook Bakery**
>
> Brook Bakery has 6,000 regular customers and is thinking of making a
> gluten-free loaf. It asked 400 customers, in the shop on Saturday
> mornings, whether they would buy one. 128 said yes. The bakery also
> asked the 400 customers how much they would pay for a gluten-free loaf.
>
> | Price they would pay | Number of customers |
> |---|---|
> | Under $3 | 60 |
> | $3 to $3.99 | 140 |
> | $4 to $4.99 | 120 |
> | $5 or more | 80 |
>
> The baker also read an online report which said the market for
> gluten-free bread in the region is growing. She plans to sell the
> loaf at $4.50 and expects that only a quarter of customers who
> said they would buy will actually buy a loaf once a week.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of secondary research? **[1]**
- A. A questionnaire given to customers
- B. A focus group
- C. A report from an online news site
- D. Observing customers in the shop

**2.** Which **one** of the following is quantitative data? **[1]**
- A. "I like the smell of fresh bread"
- B. 32% of customers said yes
- C. "The staff are friendly"
- D. A customer's reason for choosing a bakery

**3.** Define the term "primary research". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why a business carries out market research. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** drawback of using a questionnaire. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the percentage of the 400 customers who said they would buy a gluten-free loaf. **[2]**

**7.** Use the percentage from Question 6 to estimate the number of Brook Bakery's 6,000 customers who would buy a gluten-free loaf. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the percentage of the 400 customers who would pay $4 or more for the loaf. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the bakery's estimated annual revenue from gluten-free loaves, using the baker's expectation that only a quarter of the customers in Question 7 will buy a loaf each week for 52 weeks, at $4.50 per loaf. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why the survey results might be inaccurate. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **one** benefit and **one** drawback of the baker using the online report as part of her research. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Brook Bakery should launch the gluten-free loaf, using your answers to Questions 6 to 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** New information collected first-hand [1] by the business for its own purpose [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: to identify market opportunities [1]; to understand customers [1]; to understand competitors [1]; to reduce the risk of failure [1]; to measure market size [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Many people do not reply [1], so the results may not represent all customers; or people may not answer truthfully [1]. Accept: badly worded questions lead to misleading results [1], so the business might make the wrong decision [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 128 / 400 x 100 [1] = **32%** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 32% x 6,000 [1] = **1,920** customers [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Customers paying $4 or more = 120 + 80 = 200 [1]; 200 / 400 [1] x 100 = **50%** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Customers buying weekly = 25% x 1,920 = 480 [1]; loaves a year = 480 x 52 = 24,960 [1]; revenue = 24,960 x $4.50 = **$112,320** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Unrepresentative sample:** the survey took place in the shop on Saturday mornings [1], so it missed customers who shop at other times or who do not visit in person [1].
**Untruthful answers:** people may say they would buy a gluten-free loaf to be polite [1], but not buy it when it is on sale, so the 32% is probably too high [1]. Accept sample too small, or poorly worded questions, with a consequence [1] [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Benefit:** the report is secondary research, so it is cheap and quick to get [1], and it shows that the regional market is growing, which supports the baker's plan [1].
**Drawback:** the report was written for another purpose and may not apply to Brook's own customers [1]; it may be out of date or not specific to the town [1].
**Use:** the baker should combine the report with her own primary research for a fuller picture [1], because the report cannot show what Brook's customers will pay [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For launching:** 32% of the sample said they would buy, which suggests 1,920 customers [1]; half of the sample would pay $4 or more, so the planned price of $4.50 is acceptable to a large share [1]; the online report says the market is growing [1]; the baker's cautious estimate still gives revenue of $112,320 a year [1].
**Against:** the survey only asked customers in the shop on Saturdays, so it may be unrepresentative [1]; people often say they will buy and then do not [1]; the bakery has not included the costs of ingredients, equipment and staff, so $112,320 is revenue and not profit [1].
**Judgement:** the bakery should launch, perhaps on a small scale or as a test, because the evidence is positive and the cautious estimate is still sizeable [1]. It should do more research, such as a test batch in the shop for a month, before spending much on equipment [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 6,000 instead of 400 as the total when finding the percentage who would pay $4 or more.
- Forgetting to find 25% of 1,920 before multiplying by 52 and $4.50.
- Confusing primary/secondary with qualitative/quantitative.
- Saying that a survey is accurate without commenting on sample, honesty and wording.
- Stating revenue is profit in the evaluation.
