## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term "sustainable development". **[2]**

**2.** State the difference between a private cost and an external cost. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** ways in which business activity contributes to global warming. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how an environmental concern can be an opportunity for a business. **[3]**

**5.** Identify **two** ethical issues, other than child labour, that a business might face. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why a government uses legal controls instead of relying on businesses to protect the environment voluntarily. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Brisa Airport plans a second runway. It will double the number of flights. The airport company will pay for construction. Hotels, taxi firms and restaurants near the airport expect more customers, and 1,500 new jobs are expected. Homes under the new flight path will face more noise.

**(a)** Identify **two** external costs and **two** external benefits of the expansion. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the airport company may not take the external costs into account when making its decision. **[3]**

**8.** Pomona Laundry is considering solar panels costing $24,000. They would cut its electricity bill by $4,000 a year. The owner also expects that advertising the laundry as "solar powered" would bring extra customers, adding $2,400 a year to profit.

**(a)** Calculate how many years it would take for the electricity savings alone to cover the cost of the panels. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many years it would take if the extra profit from new customers is included. **[2]**

**(c)** Consider whether Pomona Laundry should install the solar panels. Justify your answer. **[5]**

**9.** A pressure group starts a campaign against Gully Snacks, claiming that one of its ingredient suppliers clears forest to grow crops. Explain **two** factors that will affect whether Gully Snacks changes its supplier. **[4]**

**10.** Rowan Rugs buys $200,000 of rugs a year from a supplier. A journalist reports that the supplier employs children. Rowan has two options:

- Option 1: switch to a certified supplier that charges 10% more.
- Option 2: keep the current supplier but pay $18,000 a year for independent checks, with a deadline for the supplier to stop using child labour.

**(a)** Calculate the extra annual cost of Option 1. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference in annual cost between the two options. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** benefits to Rowan Rugs of responding to the report rather than ignoring it. **[4]**

**(d)** Recommend and justify which option Rowan Rugs should choose. **[6]**

**11.** A government introduces a law limiting the waste that leather tanneries may release into rivers. Tanneries that break the law will be fined.

**(a)** Explain **two** effects of the law on a tannery that does not yet meet the limit. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain how the law could benefit a tannery that already meets the limit. **[2]**

**12.** Consider whether acting ethically always reduces a business's profit. Justify your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Meeting the needs of people today [1] without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A private cost is paid by the business itself, such as wages [1], whereas an external cost falls on third parties outside the business, such as residents affected by pollution [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two: burning fossil fuels in factories [1]; using petrol or diesel vehicles and aircraft for transport (or using electricity from fossil-fuel power stations, or clearing forests) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Customers who are worried about an issue such as plastic waste may prefer products that address it [1]. A business that offers such products, for example refillable containers, can attract those customers [1] and may be able to charge a higher price or win market share from rivals [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Any two: paying fair prices to suppliers [1]; honest advertising (or safe working conditions, or fair treatment of staff) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Reducing environmental harm usually costs money [1], so some businesses will not do it voluntarily because rivals that do nothing would have lower costs [1]. A law makes every firm meet the same standard, so the harm is reduced and responsible firms are not undercut [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** External costs, any two: noise for homes under the flight path [1]; more air pollution or more traffic congestion near the airport [1]. External benefits, any two: more customers for nearby hotels, taxis and restaurants [1]; new jobs for local people [1].

**(b)** The airport company does not pay the external costs [1], for example it does not compensate residents for noise [1], so these costs do not reduce its profit and it may ignore them unless forced by law or pressure groups [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* The construction cost is a private cost, paid by the airport company, so it is not credited as external.

**8. (a)** $24,000 ÷ $4,000 = **6 years** [1].

**(b)** Total annual gain = 4,000 + 2,400 = $6,400 [1]; $24,000 ÷ $6,400 = **3.75 years** [1].

**(c)** For: the panels pay for themselves within four years if the extra customers appear [1], and then keep saving money for many years [1]; they also cut the laundry's carbon emissions and support sustainable development [1]. Against: $24,000 is a large sum for a small business and may need a loan [1]. Judgement: install the panels if the owner can finance them, but treat the extra customers as uncertain; even on savings alone the cost is recovered in six years [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* A justified answer notes which figure is certain (the electricity saving) and which is an estimate (the extra profit).

**9.** Factor 1: how much Gully depends on its public image [1]. Snacks are sold to consumers who can easily switch brands, so bad publicity could cut sales quickly, making a change more likely [1]. Factor 2: the cost of changing supplier [1]. If other suppliers charge much more or cannot supply enough, Gully may resist, especially if the campaign attracts little support (also credit: size and support of the campaign, or whether the law is moving the same way) [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 10% × $200,000 = **$20,000** [1].

**(b)** $20,000 − $18,000 = Option 2 is **$2,000 a year cheaper** [1].

**(c)** Benefit 1: Rowan protects its reputation [1], so customers who read the report do not boycott it and sales are protected [1]. Benefit 2: retailers that buy Rowan's rugs may require ethical supply chains [1], so acting keeps those business customers (also credit: staff morale, being able to advertise ethical sourcing) [1].

**(d)** Recommendation, for example Option 1 [1]. It removes the problem immediately, which matters because the report is already public [1]. The extra cost is only $2,000 a year more than Option 2 [1]. Option 2 keeps the existing relationship and may help the children if the supplier changes, but it relies on checks that might miss abuses [1], and a second report would cause more damage [1]. Judgement: Option 1 is safer for Rowan's reputation, unless the current supplier can show quickly and independently that it has stopped using children [1]. (Credit a reasoned choice of Option 2.) **[12]**

**11. (a)** Effect 1: the tannery must buy treatment equipment or change its process [1], raising its costs and cutting profit unless it raises prices [1]. Effect 2: if it does not comply it faces fines [1], and the publicity could damage its reputation with customers [1].

**(b)** Rival tanneries that pollute will now face the same costs or fines [1], so the compliant tannery is no longer undercut on price and may win customers from rivals that struggle to comply [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: ethical choices often raise costs, for example paying fair prices to suppliers or checking for child labour [1], and if customers will not pay more, profit falls [1]. Against: ethical behaviour can attract loyal customers and allow a higher price [1]; it avoids fines, boycotts and bad publicity that would cut profit [1]; and treating staff well can raise motivation and productivity [1]. Judgement: acting ethically may reduce profit in the short run, but not always, and over time it can protect or raise profit, especially for businesses whose customers care about how products are made [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing the business's own costs as external costs.
- Treating environmental issues only as constraints.
- Naming a pressure group method without explaining why the business would respond.
- Recommending an ethical option without using the cost figures given.
- Writing about laws in general instead of their effect on the business in the question.
- Ending a "consider" answer without a conclusion.
