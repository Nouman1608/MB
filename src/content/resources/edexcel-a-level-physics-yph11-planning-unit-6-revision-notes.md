Condensed notes for section **6.3 Planning**, Unit 6: Practical Skills in Physics II, Pearson Edexcel IAL Physics (YPH11), Issue 3. Full teaching with two complete plans is in the [Unit 6 planning study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-planning-unit-6/); questions are in the [Unit 6 planning practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-planning-unit-6-practice/).

## The seven 6.3 points in one line each

1. **Apparatus with details**: the most appropriate items, with range, resolution and relevant dimensions or values.
2. **Calibration**: zero check, or check against a known value.
3. **Measurement**: the most appropriate instrument(s) and the technique for each variable.
4. **Controls**: every other variable, and how it is kept constant.
5. **Repeats**: whether appropriate, and why.
6. **Safety**: a specific hazard and how it is dealt with.
7. **Data use**: what is calculated, what is plotted, and how the answer comes from the graph.

Not on the 6.3 list (unlike Unit 3): implications and context; sources of uncertainty and systematic error (these are assessed in 6.5 Analysis).

## Details that earn the apparatus marks

| Choice | Detail to give | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Time constant for hand timing | RC of tens of seconds, for example 470 µF with 100 kΩ (47 s) | Readings every 10 s are practical |
| Time constant below about 1 s | Data logger or oscilloscope | Too fast to read by eye |
| Mass–spring period | At least 0.5 s; time 20 oscillations | Reaction-time uncertainty becomes 2% or less |
| Heating experiment | Temperature rise of at least 10 °C | Thermometer uncertainty becomes a small fraction |
| Pendulum or spring amplitude | Small, a few centimetres or under about 10° | Keeps motion simple harmonic |
| Lead absorber | Range of thicknesses up to about 20 mm, measured by micrometer | Enough change in count rate to see the pattern |
| Light gates | Card length measured with Vernier calipers | Speed = card length ÷ time |

## Unit 4 and 5 core practicals: planning points

| Core practical | Key controls | Hazard and precaution |
|---|---|---|
| 9 Force and change of momentum | Track tilted to compensate for friction; same trolley mass | Falling masses: sand tray, feet clear |
| 10 Collisions of small spheres analysed with ICT | Level surface; camera fixed and perpendicular; scale in the frame | Rolling balls: keep floor clear |
| 11 Capacitor charge and discharge | Same R and C; supply p.d. constant | Electrolytic capacitor: connect with correct polarity, do not exceed its voltage rating |
| 12 Thermistor calibrated in a potential divider as a thermostat | Same supply p.d. and fixed resistor; stir water bath; wait for steady readings | Hot water: scalding; keep electrical connections dry |
| 13 Specific latent heat | Same heater power; a control funnel without heater to measure melting from surroundings | Hot heater: let it cool before handling |
| 14 Pressure and volume of a gas | Same mass of gas; wait after each change so temperature returns to room value | Pressurised apparatus: eye protection, do not exceed the stated pressure |
| 15 Absorption of gamma radiation by lead | Fixed source-to-tube distance; same source and tube | Ionising radiation: tongs, distance, short exposure, return to lead box; wash hands after lead |
| 16 Unknown mass from oscillations | Same spring; same small amplitude; vertical motion | Falling masses or toppling stand: G-clamp, soft mat |

## Repeats

| Situation | Repeats appropriate? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hand timing of oscillations | Yes, about 3 | Reaction time causes random error |
| Counting radioactive decays | Yes, or count for longer | Decay is random |
| Data logger recording many points | Repeat runs rather than readings | Each run already contains many readings |
| Reading a steady digital meter | No | Reading does not vary |
| Correcting a zero error | No | Systematic: correct it instead |

## Turning the equation into a graph

| Relationship | Plot | Gradient | Intercept |
|---|---|---|---|
| T = 2π√(m/k) | T² against m | 4π²/k | Near zero (spring mass) |
| V = V₀e^(−t/RC) | ln V against t | −1/RC | ln V₀ |
| C = C₀e^(−μx) | ln C against x | −μ | ln C₀ |
| pV = constant | p against 1/V | The constant | Zero |
| y = kxⁿ | lg y against lg x | n | lg k |
| ΔE = mcΔθ (heater) | Δθ against t | P/mc | Zero |

Write the log form in the plan; it shows the examiner exactly what you will calculate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Most appropriate apparatus | The item whose range and resolution suit the size of the quantity |
| Relevant dimensions | Sizes of apparatus that affect the result, such as string length or card length |
| Fiducial marker | Fixed reference point used for timing oscillations |
| Background count | Count from sources other than the one being studied |
| Corrected count rate | Measured count rate minus background count rate |
| Time constant | RC: time for a discharging capacitor's p.d. to fall to 1/e of its initial value |
| Log graph | A graph of ln y or lg y used to turn a curve into a straight line |
| Random process | One whose outcome varies unpredictably, such as decay |

## Exam traps

- Naming apparatus without values: Unit 6 asks for details.
- Starting oscillation timing at the turning point instead of the equilibrium position.
- Subtracting a background **count** recorded over a different time from a count; convert both to rates first.
- Plotting V against t for a discharge and "finding RC from the curve" without a log graph.
- Ignoring capacitor polarity or voltage rating in the safety section.
- Writing a context or uncertainty paragraph instead of the data-use paragraph the marks are for.

## Self-test

**1.** Which two Unit 3 planning points are not on the 6.3 list?
**2.** Why time 20 oscillations rather than one?
**3.** Where should the fiducial marker be placed for a mass on a spring, and why?
**4.** RC = 0.22 s. How should the discharge be recorded?
**5.** What graph tests whether count rate falls exponentially with lead thickness?
**6.** Background: 270 counts in 600 s. Reading: 1,350 counts in 100 s. Find the corrected count rate.
**7.** Why are repeats important in radioactivity experiments?
**8.** State two precautions when handling a sealed gamma source.
**9.** What is plotted to find k from a mass–spring experiment?
**10.** In the gas-law practical, why wait after each change of volume?
**11.** For y = kxⁿ, what does the gradient of lg y against lg x give?
**12.** Give one hazard specific to capacitor experiments.

### Answers

**1.** Implications and context; sources of uncertainty and systematic error.
**2.** The reaction-time uncertainty becomes a small fraction of the total time.
**3.** At the equilibrium position, where the mass moves fastest, so the timing point is definite.
**4.** With a data logger or oscilloscope; it is too fast for hand timing.
**5.** ln (corrected count rate) against thickness.
**6.** Background 0.45 s⁻¹; reading 13.5 s⁻¹; corrected 13.05 s⁻¹, about 13.1 s⁻¹.
**7.** Decay is random, so counts in equal times vary.
**8.** Any two: tongs; hold at arm's length pointing away from people; minimise time; return to its lead box.
**9.** T² against m; k = 4π² ÷ gradient.
**10.** Compression warms the gas; the temperature must return to the fixed value.
**11.** n.
**12.** An electrolytic capacitor can fail if connected the wrong way round or above its voltage rating.
