Cambridge A-Level Chemistry 9701 is a staged qualification made up of five papers. Paper 1 Multiple Choice (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 15.5% of the full A Level), Paper 2 AS Level Structured Questions (1 hour 15 minutes, 60 marks, 23%) and Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills (2 hours, 40 marks, 11.5%) make up the AS Level; Paper 4 A Level Structured Questions (2 hours, 100 marks, 38.5%) and Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation (1 hour 15 minutes, 30 marks, 11.5%) complete the full A Level. Preparation draws on topic pages such as [Chemical Energetics: Lattice Energy, Entropy and Gibbs Free Energy](/resources/a-chemical-energetics-lattice-energy-entropy-and-gibbs-free-energy/), [Carboxylic Acids and Acyl Chlorides](/resources/a-carboxylic-acids-and-acyl-chlorides/) and [Amines](/resources/a-amines-aliphatic-and-aromatic/).

## Choose your route: AS Level or full A Level

The syllabus allows Papers 1 to 3 to be certificated on their own as a standalone AS Level. Alternatively a candidate can go on to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level. In the AS route the same papers are weighted 31%, 46% and 23%, which are the full-A-Level weightings of 15.5%, 23% and 11.5% doubled. Ask your school which route you are following before you plan revision. The weightings quoted in the rest of this page are percentages of the full A Level, and the five add up to 100%: 15.5 + 23 + 11.5 + 38.5 + 11.5 = 100.

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice

Paper 1 gives 75 minutes for 40 marks, or 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark. That is generous for a multiple-choice paper, and it exists because many questions need a short calculation or the interpretation of data. **Preparation priority**: practise working multi-step questions quickly, and avoid mental arithmetic you cannot check. Eliminate options by reasoning and leave a few minutes at the end to review flagged questions.

## Paper 2: AS Level Structured Questions

Paper 2 is 75 minutes for 60 marks: 75 / 60 = 1.25 minutes per mark. It is where AS content is examined in written form. **Preparation priority**: write concise answers with correct terminology, show working in calculations, and practise drawing structures, mechanisms and equations accurately. A good habit is to give each mark a point you could point to in your answer.

## Paper 3: Advanced Practical Skills

Paper 3 is a practical paper of 2 hours for 40 marks, so 120 / 40 = 3.0 minutes per mark. It assesses practical skills in the laboratory, and the generous allowance reflects the time needed to carry out the work and record results. **Preparation priority**: use every available opportunity to handle apparatus. Practise recording results clearly in tables, with units and sensible precision, then processing and evaluating them.

## Paper 4: A Level Structured Questions

Paper 4 is the largest paper, with 100 marks and 38.5% of the full A Level, in 2 hours: 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark, the fastest pace of the five papers. **Preparation priority**: because the pace is tight, your recall and your calculation routines must be fluent. Build a routine for each type of calculation, and rehearse extended written answers that link several ideas. Since this is a staged qualification, A Level work builds on AS content, so keep revising the AS topics rather than letting them lapse.

## Paper 5: Planning, Analysis and Evaluation

Paper 5 is 75 minutes for 30 marks, giving 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per mark. It tests your ability to plan investigations, analyse data and evaluate methods rather than recall content. **Preparation priority**: practise writing a plan with variables, apparatus, method and expected results, and then evaluating sources of error and suggesting improvements.

## Total time and marks

Across the five papers you have 75 + 75 + 120 + 120 + 75 = 465 minutes for 40 + 60 + 40 + 100 + 30 = 270 marks. The AS papers alone total 75 + 75 + 120 = 270 minutes for 140 marks.

## Command words

Typical command words are state, define, describe, explain, calculate, deduce, predict, suggest and evaluate. "Deduce" and "suggest" ask you to apply knowledge to unfamiliar information, so practise with data you have not seen before.

## Worked practice scenario: an equilibrium constant

Question (original): "2.00 mol of ethanoic acid is mixed with 2.00 mol of ethanol with a catalyst. At equilibrium the mixture contains 1.50 mol of ethyl ethanoate. CH3COOH + C2H5OH <=> CH3COOC2H5 + H2O. Calculate Kc."

```
Step 1  Equilibrium moles:
        ester = 1.50 mol, water = 1.50 mol (1 : 1 ratio)
        acid = 2.00 - 1.50 = 0.50 mol
        ethanol = 2.00 - 1.50 = 0.50 mol
Step 2  Kc = [ester][water] / ([acid][ethanol])
Step 3  Both sides have two terms, so volume V cancels:
        Kc = (1.50 x 1.50) / (0.50 x 0.50) = 2.25 / 0.25 = 9.0
        Kc has no units.
```

Stating why the volume cancels shows you understand the expression, not just the arithmetic.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know whether you are sitting AS (Papers 1 to 3) or the full A Level.
- Practise multiple choice with working, not guesses.
- Revise AS topics alongside A Level topics for Paper 4.
- Handle apparatus regularly for Paper 3.
- Practise planning and evaluation tasks for Paper 5.
- Give units and sensible significant figures in answers.
- Check the marks per question and match your effort.
- Use remaining time to recheck calculations.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A-Level Chemistry 9701?**
Five: Papers 1, 2 and 3 for the AS stage and Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level.

**Can I take the AS Level on its own?**
Yes. Papers 1 to 3 can be certificated as a standalone AS Level, or you can continue to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level.

**Which paper has the highest weighting?**
Paper 4, A Level Structured Questions, at 38.5% of the full A Level.

**How long is the practical paper?**
Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills is 2 hours and 40 marks, 11.5% of the full A Level.

**Is there a newer syllabus?**
A 2028-2030 edition has been published for future cohorts. This page is written for the 2025-2027 syllabus.
