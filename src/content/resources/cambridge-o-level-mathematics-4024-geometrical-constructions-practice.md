These questions cover section 4.2, Geometrical constructions, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [geometrical constructions study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-constructions/), and the [geometrical constructions revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-constructions-revision-notes/) summarise them. Where a question asks you to construct, use a ruler, compasses and a protractor on paper and keep all construction arcs. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.

## Section A

**1.** Explain why a triangle with sides 9 cm, 4 cm and 3 cm cannot be constructed. **[2]**

**2.** A triangle has sides 8 cm, 6 cm and 5 cm. Describe the steps to construct it with a ruler and compasses, saying which line is drawn first and what radius is used for each arc. **[3]**

**3.** Decide whether each of these could be the net of a cube, and give a reason.

**(a)** Six squares in a single row.

**(b)** A column of four squares, with one extra square attached to each side of the second square in the column. **[3]**

**4.** A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. Use its net to find the surface area and then the volume. **[3]**

**5.** A reflex angle is drawn by first measuring an angle of 115° on the opposite side. State the size of the reflex angle. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The cross-section of a prism is a right-angled triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. The prism is 12 cm long.

**(a)** Find the total area of the three rectangles in the net.

**(b)** Find the total surface area.

**(c)** Find the volume. **[4]**

**7.** The net of a square-based pyramid has a square of side 10 cm and four isosceles triangles, each with a height of 13 cm measured from the base of the triangle to its apex.

**(a)** Find the surface area of the pyramid.

**(b)** Find the vertical height of the pyramid.

**(c)** Find its volume. **[6]**

**8.** A rhombus ABCD has sides of 13 cm. The diagonal AC is 10 cm. The diagonals cross at M.

**(a)** Find the length of BM.

**(b)** Hence find the length of BD and the area of the rhombus. **[4]**

**9.** The net of a cylinder consists of two circles of radius 3 cm and a rectangle of height 10 cm.

**(a)** Find the length of the rectangle, giving your answer to 3 significant figures.

**(b)** Find the total surface area, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**10.** A cube has edges of 4 cm.

**(a)** How many squares are in a net of the cube?

**(b)** Find the surface area of the cube.

**(c)** Find its volume. **[3]**

**11.** A prism has an equilateral triangle of side 4 cm as its cross-section. The prism is 9 cm long.

**(a)** Find the height of the triangular end, giving your answer to 3 significant figures.

**(b)** Find the surface area, to the nearest whole number.

**(c)** Find the volume, to 3 significant figures. **[6]**

**12.** Construct triangle ABC with AB = 7 cm, BC = 5 cm and AC = 4 cm, and measure the largest angle. State what size of angle you expect, correct to the nearest degree. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** For a triangle to exist, the two shorter sides must add to more than the longest side [1]. 4 + 3 = 7, which is less than 9, so the arcs would never meet [1].

**2.** Draw the longest side, 8 cm, as a base AB [1]. Draw an arc of radius 6 cm from A and an arc of radius 5 cm from B, so that they cross at C [1]. Join AC and BC, and leave the arcs on the page [1].

*Examiner insight:* Any valid arrangement of the three radii scores, as long as the sides and arcs match.

**3. (a)** **No**: six squares in a row would overlap when folded [1]. **(b)** **Yes**: it is a cross shape, and folding the four squares of the column gives a cube's four side faces, with the two extra squares as the top and bottom [1][1].

**4.** Surface area = 2(8 × 5) + 2(8 × 3) + 2(5 × 3) = 80 + 48 + 30 = **158 cm²** [1][1]. Volume = 8 × 5 × 3 = **120 cm³** [1].

**5.** 360 − 115 = **245°** [1].

**6. (a)** The rectangles form a strip: (6 + 8 + 10) × 12 = **288 cm²** [1].

**(b)** The two triangles have area 2 × (1/2 × 6 × 8) = 48 [1], so the surface area is 288 + 48 = **336 cm²** [1].

**(c)** Volume = 24 × 12 = **288 cm³** [1].

**7. (a)** Square = 100 [1]. Triangles = 4 × (1/2 × 10 × 13) = 260 [1]. Surface area = **360 cm²** [1].

**(b)** h² = 13² − 5² = 144 [1], so **h = 12 cm** [1].

**(c)** Volume = 1/3 × 100 × 12 = **400 cm³** [1].

**8. (a)** AM = 5 and AB = 13, so BM² = 169 − 25 = 144 [1] and **BM = 12 cm** [1].

**(b)** BD = 2 × 12 = **24 cm** [1]. Area = 1/2 × 10 × 24 = **120 cm²** [1].

**9. (a)** Length = circumference = 2π × 3 = **18.8 cm** [1][1].

**(b)** Curved surface = 18.85 × 10 = 188.5 cm², and two circles = 2π × 9 = 56.5 cm² [1]. Total = **245 cm²** [1].

**10. (a)** **Six** squares [1]. **(b)** 6 × 16 = **96 cm²** [1]. **(c)** 4³ = **64 cm³** [1].

**11. (a)** h² = 16 − 4 = 12, so **h = 3.46 cm** [1][1].

**(b)** End area = 1/2 × 4 × 3.464 = 6.928 cm², so two ends give 13.86 [1]. Rectangles = 3 × 4 × 9 = 108 [1]. Total = 121.86 ≈ **122 cm²** [1].

**(c)** Volume = 6.928 × 9 = 62.35 ≈ **62.4 cm³** [1].

**12.** Draw AB = 7 cm, arc of 4 cm from A, arc of 5 cm from B, join at C [1][1]. Measure the angle at C, which is opposite the longest side [1]. Expected: about **101°** (101.5° by calculation) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rubbing out construction arcs.
- Using the wrong radius for an arc, or the wrong end of the line as the centre.
- Using the slant height as the vertical height for the volume of a pyramid.
- Forgetting the curved part of the cylinder's net is a rectangle with length 2πr.
- Not stating the units, or confusing cm² with cm³.
