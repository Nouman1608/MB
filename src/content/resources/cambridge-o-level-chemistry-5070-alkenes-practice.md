Learn the content first with the [alkenes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alkenes/) and the [alkenes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the type of bond that makes alkenes unsaturated. **[1]**

**2.** Which compound would decolourise aqueous bromine?

A: C₃H₈

B: C₄H₁₀

C: C₃H₆

D: C₂H₆

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by an *addition reaction*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the catalyst used when hydrogen is added to an alkene. **[1]**

**5.** Complete the cracking equation: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₁₀H₂₂ + ...... **[1]**

**6.** State the **two** conditions used to crack large alkane molecules. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Describe a test to show that a hydrocarbon is unsaturated. Give the result for an unsaturated and for a saturated hydrocarbon. **[3]**

**8.** Ethene reacts with bromine.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the product. **[1]**

**(c)** Draw the displayed formula of the product. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why this is described as an addition reaction. **[1]**

**9.** Propene, CH₃CH=CH₂, reacts with steam.

**(a)** State the catalyst used. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the **two** possible alcohols formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why two different alcohols can form from propene but only one from ethene. **[1]**

**10.** Tetradecane, C₁₄H₃₀, is cracked.

**(a)** In one reaction, tetradecane forms octane, C₈H₁₈, and one other hydrocarbon only. Write the balanced equation. **[1]**

**(b)** In another reaction, it forms C₁₀H₂₂ and two molecules of the same alkene. Identify the alkene and write the equation. **[2]**

**(c)** State the homologous series of the product you identified in (b). **[1]**

**11.** Explain why the cracking of fractions such as fuel oil is carried out. **[3]**

**12.** But-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃, reacts separately with bromine, hydrogen and steam.

**(a)** Write the structural formula of the product with bromine and name it. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the product with hydrogen and state the catalyst. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the product with steam. **[1]**

**13.** A student has two unlabelled gases, one an alkane and the other an alkene, both with three carbon atoms per molecule.

**(a)** Write the molecular formula of each gas. **[2]**

**(b)** The student bubbles each gas through aqueous bromine. Describe what the student observes for each. **[2]**

**(c)** The product formed from the alkene is then tested with fresh aqueous bromine. Predict the result and explain it. **[2]**

**14.** Propene reacts with hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction, using structural formulae. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the displayed formula of the product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the product is described as saturated. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Carbon–carbon double (covalent) bond / C=C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* C₃H₆ fits CₙH₂ₙ, so it is propene, an alkene. The other three fit CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

**3.** A reaction in which only one product is formed [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Nickel [1]. **[1]**

**5.** C₂H₄ [1]. **[1]**

**6.** High temperature [1]; a catalyst [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Shake (or bubble) the hydrocarbon with aqueous bromine [1]. Unsaturated: orange to colourless [1]. Saturated: stays orange / no change [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the result for the saturated hydrocarbon is part of the answer. A test is only a test if you say what a negative result looks like as well as a positive one.

**8. (a)** CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br [1]

**(b)** 1,2-dibromoethane [1]

**(c)** Two carbons joined by a single bond, one Br on each carbon, two H on each carbon, all bonds shown [1]:

```
      Br  Br
      |   |
  H - C - C - H
      |   |
      H   H
```

**(d)** Only one product is formed (the bromine adds across the double bond) [1]

**[4]**

**9. (a)** An acid catalyst [1]

**(b)** Propan-1-ol [1]; propan-2-ol [1]

**(c)** In propene the two carbons of the double bond are different (one is on the end of the chain, one in the middle), so the –OH can join either; in ethene both carbons are equivalent [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* the same idea explains why but-1-ene gives two possible alcohols but but-2-ene gives only butan-2-ol.

**10. (a)** C₁₄H₃₀ → C₈H₁₈ + C₆H₁₂ [1]

**(b)** Ethene [1]; C₁₄H₃₀ → C₁₀H₂₂ + 2C₂H₄ [1]

**(c)** Alkenes [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* in (b), 4 carbons and 8 hydrogens remain after C₁₀H₂₂. Shared between two identical molecules, that gives C₂H₄ each.

**11.** Any three [1] each:

- fractional distillation gives more of the long-chain fractions than is needed
- there is greater demand for short-chain fractions such as gasoline than is supplied
- cracking converts the surplus long-chain molecules into more useful shorter ones
- cracking produces alkenes, used to make polymers and other chemicals
- cracking produces hydrogen

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "to make smaller molecules" restates what cracking is and earns no mark. Each creditworthy point says why the products are wanted.

**12. (a)** CH₃CHBrCHBrCH₃ [1]; 2,3-dibromobutane [1]

**(b)** Butane [1]; nickel [1]

**(c)** Butan-2-ol [1]

**[5]**

**13. (a)** Alkane: C₃H₈ [1]; alkene: C₃H₆ [1]

**(b)** Alkane (propane): solution stays orange [1]; alkene (propene): orange to colourless [1]

**(c)** No change / stays orange [1]; the product, 1,2-dibromopropane, is saturated, with no C=C left to react [1]

**[6]**

**14. (a)** CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₃ [1]

**(b)** Three carbons joined by single bonds with eight hydrogens, all bonds shown [1]:

```
      H   H   H
      |   |   |
  H - C - C - C - H
      |   |   |
      H   H   H
```

**(c)** All its carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds; the double bond has been converted to a single bond by the addition of hydrogen [1]

**[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "clear" instead of "colourless" in the bromine test.
- Giving a description of cracking ("breaks large molecules") as its reason.
- Writing HBr or water as a second product of an addition reaction.
- Placing both new atoms on the same carbon.
- Forgetting to check the hydrogen balance in cracking equations.
