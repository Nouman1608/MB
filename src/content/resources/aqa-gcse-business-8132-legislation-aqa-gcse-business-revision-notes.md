## Core recall

### What the specification names

- Employment law: national minimum wage/living wage; the Equality Act (2010).
- Health and safety law: the Health and Safety at Work Act (1974).
- Consumer law, including trade descriptions.
- Assess the impact on businesses, for example cost, training needs, recruitment and the consequences of failure to follow legislation.
- Be aware of the benefits of providing a safe working environment.
- "Only brief knowledge of each law is needed."

### Each law in brief

| Law | What it requires, briefly |
|---|---|
| National minimum wage / National Living Wage | Pay at least the legal hourly minimum for the worker's age band; rates are reviewed regularly |
| Equality Act (2010) | No discrimination because of nine protected characteristics; equal pay for equal work; reasonable adjustments for disabled people |
| Health and Safety at Work Act (1974) | Employers protect employees' health, safety and welfare so far as reasonably practicable; employees take reasonable care |
| Trade descriptions | Goods must not be described falsely or misleadingly |
| Consumer Rights Act 2015 | Goods of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described |

The nine protected characteristics: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, sexual orientation.

### Impact grid

| | Cost | Training needs | Recruitment | Failure to comply |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum wage | Higher wage bill, knock-on rises for others | Little | More applicants, better retention | Back pay owed, penalties, bad publicity |
| Equality Act | Reasonable adjustments, pay reviews | Fair-practice training for managers | Fair adverts and job-related criteria; wider talent pool | Employment tribunal claims and compensation |
| Health and safety | Equipment, guards, inspections | Induction and specialist safety training | Safe reputation attracts staff | Orders to stop work, prosecution, fines, compensation |
| Consumer law | Quality checks, refunds, repairs | Sales staff describe products accurately | Little | Legal action, penalties, lost trust |

### Benefits of a safe working environment

Fewer accidents and less absence; better motivation and productivity; lower staff turnover; better reputation; no compensation or legal costs.

### Which businesses feel each law most

- Minimum wage: labour-intensive, low-margin businesses such as cafés, care homes and cleaning firms.
- Health and safety: high-risk work such as building, manufacturing and warehousing.
- Consumer law: retailers and manufacturers making claims about products.
- Equality Act: every employer, especially those recruiting often.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legislation | Laws passed by Parliament |
| National minimum wage | The legal minimum hourly pay for workers, by age band |
| National Living Wage | The highest legal minimum rate, for older workers |
| Real Living Wage | A voluntary, higher rate set by the Living Wage Foundation |
| Discrimination | Treating someone unfairly because of a characteristic such as age or race |
| Protected characteristic | A characteristic the Equality Act protects from discrimination |
| Reasonable adjustment | A change an employer makes so a disabled person is not disadvantaged |
| Employment tribunal | A body that hears claims by employees against employers |
| Risk assessment | Identifying hazards and deciding how to reduce them |
| Trade description | What a business says about a product, such as its quality, size or origin |
| Trading Standards | Local officers who enforce consumer law |

## Exam traps

- **Describing the law at length.** Brief knowledge only; effects earn the marks.
- **Learning a wage figure.** Rates change; use those in the case study.
- **Only costs.** Add the benefits and the cost of failure.
- **Same impact for all.** A care home and a software firm feel a wage rise differently.
- **Real Living Wage as compulsory.** Only the legal minimum must be paid.
- **Health and safety as employer-only.** Employees have duties too.
- **No judgement.** "Assess" needs a conclusion about how significant the impact is.

## Self-test

1. Name the two employment laws in the specification.
2. Which health and safety law does the specification name?
3. State four of the nine protected characteristics.
4. What does the Consumer Rights Act 2015 say goods must be?
5. Give two costs to a business of complying with health and safety law.
6. Give two benefits of a safe working environment.
7. Why might a rise in the minimum wage increase costs by more than the pay rise for minimum-wage staff alone?
8. What is a reasonable adjustment? Give an example.
9. Which business is likely to be more affected by a minimum wage rise: a hotel or an accountancy firm? Why?
10. A café employs 6 staff on the minimum, each working 1,200 hours a year. The rate rises by 40p. Calculate the extra yearly wage cost.
11. Give two consequences for a business that misdescribes its products.
12. What does the specification say about how much knowledge of each law is needed?

### Answers

1. National minimum wage/living wage; the Equality Act (2010).
2. The Health and Safety at Work Act (1974).
3. Any four of: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, sexual orientation.
4. Of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described.
5. Any two: protective equipment, machine guards, inspections, safety training, maintenance.
6. Any two: fewer accidents and less absence; better motivation; lower staff turnover; better reputation; no compensation costs.
7. Staff paid just above the minimum may expect rises to keep the gap that rewards experience.
8. A change so a disabled person is not disadvantaged, for example a ramp, adapted equipment or changed hours.
9. The hotel, because it employs many staff on low pay, so wages are a larger share of its costs.
10. 6 × 1,200 × £0.40 = £2,880.
11. Any two: refunds or replacements; legal action and penalties; loss of customer trust and sales.
12. Only brief knowledge is needed; more emphasis should be on the effects on businesses.
