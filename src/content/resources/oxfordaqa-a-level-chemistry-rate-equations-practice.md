These questions cover **section 3.1.11 Rate equations** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level
Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3, for International AS and A-level exams May/June 2020
onwards: 3.1.11.1, 3.1.11.2 and Required practical 8. Being International A2 material, it is
**International A-level only**. The Unit 4 paper (Organic 2 and Physical 2) tests it, and the Unit 5 paper (Practical and synoptic) may test any topic.

Learn the ideas first in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-rate-equations/) and
[revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-rate-equations-revision-notes/). See also the
[course hub](/boards/oxfordaqa/a-level/chemistry/), the
[printable checklist](/checklists/oxfordaqa/a-level/chemistry/) and the free
[10-minute diagnostics](/diagnostics/). Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Temperature is constant unless stated.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by the *order* of a reaction with respect to one reactant, and by the
*rate constant*. Explain why orders cannot be worked out from the balanced equation. **[3]**

**2.** For the reaction 2V + Z → products, Rate = k[V]²[Z].

**(a)** The rate is 3.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ when [V] = 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ and [Z] = 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³.
Calculate k, stating its units. **[2]**
**(b)** [V] is reduced to one-third of its value and [Z] is tripled. Calculate the factor by which the
rate changes. **[2]**

**3.** Initial rates were measured for the reaction B + C + 2D → products.

| Run | [B] / mol dm⁻³ | [C] / mol dm⁻³ | [D] / mol dm⁻³ | Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.040 | 0.010 | 0.15 | 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.080 | 0.010 | 0.15 | 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 3 | 0.040 | 0.030 | 0.15 | 2.16 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 4 | 0.020 | 0.020 | 0.45 | 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 5 | 0.060 | 0.025 | 0.30 | to be calculated |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to B, C and D. Explain each deduction. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the full rate equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the value of k, including units. **[2]**
**(d)** Work out the initial rate in run 5. **[1]**

**4.** The concentration of reactant Y was followed during a reaction.

| t / s | 0 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Y] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.1500 | 0.1488 | 0.1476 |

**(a)** Use these early readings to calculate the initial rate. **[1]**
**(b)** Later, when [Y] = 0.075 mol dm⁻³, the tangent to the concentration–time curve passes through
(230 s, 0.099 mol dm⁻³) and (630 s, 0.051 mol dm⁻³). Calculate the rate at that point. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the order with respect to Y. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**5.** Compound D decomposes on a hot metal surface. A plot of rate against [D] is a horizontal straight
line at a rate of 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

**(a)** State the order with respect to D. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the value of k with its units. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the time taken for [D] to fall from 0.090 mol dm⁻³ to 0.018 mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**6.** A second-order reaction has A = 8.0 × 10¹⁰ mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹ and Ea = 82 kJ mol⁻¹. Use k = Ae^(–Ea/RT).

**(a)** Calculate k at 305 K. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate k at 315 K, and the factor by which k increases between the two temperatures. **[2]**

**7.** The rate constant of a first-order reaction was measured at four temperatures.

| T / K | 300 | 315 | 330 | 345 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k / s⁻¹ | 0.00771 | 0.0317 | 0.115 | 0.370 |

**(a)** Use ln k = –Ea/RT + ln A to explain why ln k plotted against 1/T gives a straight line, and state
what its gradient represents. **[2]**
**(b)** The four points lie on a straight line. Use the points at 300 K and 345 K to calculate the
gradient, then the activation energy in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the value of A, including its units. **[2]**

**8.** The reaction G + 3H → GH₃ has the rate equation Rate = k[G][H]².

**(a)** Suggest a two-step mechanism in which the first step is the rate-determining step. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a single step, G + 3H → GH₃, is not consistent with the rate equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the intermediate in your mechanism. **[1]**

**9.** For Required practical 8, a student runs a clock reaction, mixing a 0.200 mol dm⁻³
solution of reactant V with water and fixed volumes of the other solutions (total 50.0 cm³ each run).

| Volume of V solution / cm³ | 5.0 | 10.0 | 15.0 | 20.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to colour change, t / s | 96 | 48 | 32 | 24 |

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of V in the mixture in the 5.0 cm³ run. **[1]**
**(b)** Why does 1/t give a measure of the initial rate? Explain. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the order of reaction with respect to V from these results. **[2]**
**(d)** The student can judge the colour change only to within 1 s. Calculate the percentage
uncertainty in the shortest time, and state which run is the least reliable. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest one change that would reduce this timing uncertainty. **[1]**

**10.** A continuous monitoring method was used for the reaction RCl + H₂O → ROH + H⁺ + Cl⁻, with
[RCl] = 0.100 mol dm⁻³ at the start. At intervals, 5.00 cm³ samples were removed, run into ice-cold
solvent and titrated with 0.0250 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Explain why each sample is run into ice-cold solvent. **[1]**
**(b)** The sample taken at 20 min needed 8.00 cm³ of sodium hydroxide. Calculate [RCl] at 20 min. **[3]**
**(c)** Tangents to the concentration–time curve give a rate of 2.55 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ min⁻¹ at the start
and 1.53 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ min⁻¹ at 20 min. Deduce the order with respect to RCl. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate k in s⁻¹. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Order: the exponent on that reactant's concentration in the rate equation [1]. Rate constant: the
constant linking the rate to the concentration terms, which is fixed at a given temperature [1]. The
rate depends on the mechanism (the rate-determining step), not the overall equation, so orders must be
found by experiment [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An order definition without "power" and "rate equation" is usually too vague.

**2. (a)** k = 3.0 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ ((5.0 × 10⁻³)² × 2.0 × 10⁻³) = **60** [1] **mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Factor = (⅓)² × 3 [1] = **⅓** (the rate falls to one-third) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show each reactant's factor separately so that any slip can still be followed.

**3. (a)** B: runs 1 and 2, [B] ×2 and rate ×2, so first order [1]. C: runs 1 and 3, [C] ×3 and rate ×9,
so second order [1]. D: runs 1 and 4, [B] ×0.5 and [C] ×2 give ×0.5 × 4 = ×2, which is the whole rate
change, so D is zero order even though [D] ×3 [1].
**(b)** **Rate = k[B][C]²** [1]
**(c)** k = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.040 × 0.010²) = **6.0** [1] **mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Rate = 6.0 × 0.060 × 0.025² = **2.25 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For D, show that the effects of B and C account for the whole rate change.

**4. (a)** Initial rate = (0.1500 – 0.1476) ÷ 10 = **2.4 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Rate = (0.099 – 0.051) ÷ (630 – 230) = **1.2 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** [Y] halves (0.150 → 0.075) and the rate halves [1], so **first order** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote rates as positive values.

**5. (a)** **Zero order** [1]
**(b)** Rate = k, so k = **3.6 × 10⁻⁵** [1] **mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Time = (0.090 – 0.018) ÷ 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ = **2.0 × 10³ s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Zero-order k has the units of rate, not s⁻¹.

**6. (a)** Ea = 82 000 J mol⁻¹ [1]; Ea/RT = 82 000 ÷ (8.31 × 305) = 32.35 [1];
k = 8.0 × 10¹⁰ × e^(–32.35) = **7.1 × 10⁻⁴ mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** At 315 K, k = **2.0 × 10⁻³ mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹** [1]; factor = 1.99 × 10⁻³ ÷ 7.12 × 10⁻⁴ = **2.8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded k values into the factor; early rounding shifts the answer.

**7. (a)** Comparing with y = mx + c: ln k is y, 1/T is x, and –Ea/R and ln A do not change [1]. The gradient is –Ea/R [1].
**(b)** 300 K: 1/T = 3.3333 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = –4.865; 345 K: 1/T = 2.8986 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = –0.994 [1];
gradient = 3.871 ÷ (–4.347 × 10⁻⁴) = –8.90 × 10³ K [1];
Ea = 8.90 × 10³ × 8.31 = 7.40 × 10⁴ J mol⁻¹ = **74.0 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** At 330 K: ln A = –2.163 + 74 000 ÷ (8.31 × 330) = 24.82 [1];
A = e^24.82 = **6.0 × 10¹⁰ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A negative Ea signals a sign error and is unlikely to be credited.

**8. (a)** Step 1 (slow): G + 2H → GH₂ [1]; Step 2 (fast): GH₂ + H → GH₃ [1]
**(b)** A single step would put three H particles in the rate-determining step, giving
Rate = k[G][H]³, but the order with respect to H is 2 [1].
**(c)** **GH₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The steps must also add up to the overall equation.

**9. (a)** [V] = 0.200 × 5.0 ÷ 50.0 = **0.020 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(b)** The same fixed amount of reaction has happened at each colour change, so rate ∝ 1/t [1].
**(c)** 1/t = 0.0104, 0.0208, 0.0313, 0.0417 s⁻¹, so 1/t is directly proportional to [V] [1]; the reaction
is **first order** in V [1].
**(d)** 1 ÷ 24 × 100 = **4.2 %** [1]; the 20.0 cm³ run (shortest time) is least reliable [1].
**(e)** Use more dilute solutions so that every run takes longer, or detect the colour change with a
light sensor [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Repeat the experiment" does not shrink the timing uncertainty, so it is unlikely
to gain the mark.

**10. (a)** Cooling and diluting slows the reaction almost to a stop, so [RCl] does not change before the
titration [1].
**(b)** n(NaOH) = 0.0250 × 8.00 × 10⁻³ = 2.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol = n(H⁺) [1];
[H⁺] formed = 2.00 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 5.00 × 10⁻³ = 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ [1];
[RCl] = 0.100 – 0.0400 = **0.0600 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(c)** [RCl] and the rate both fall to 0.60 of their start values (1.53 ÷ 2.55) [1], so
**first order** [1].
**(d)** k = 2.55 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.100 = 0.0255 min⁻¹ [1]; ÷ 60 = **4.25 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each H⁺ formed means one RCl used; remember to subtract from 0.100.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an order without saying which two runs were compared.
- Writing units of k that do not match the overall order.
- Using kJ mol⁻¹ or °C inside k = Ae^(–Ea/RT).
- Rounding 1/T too early, which spoils a gradient taken from small differences.
- Reporting a negative rate or a negative activation energy.
- Suggesting "repeat" when the question asks how to reduce a specific uncertainty.
