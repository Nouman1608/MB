## Core recall

- Many reactions are **reversible**.
- At **dynamic equilibrium** in a closed system: forward and reverse reactions proceed at **equal rates**; concentrations of reactants and products stay **constant**.
- **Le Chatelier's principle**: if a factor is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to **oppose** the change.
- It predicts the effect of **concentration**, **pressure** and **temperature** on the **position** of equilibrium in homogeneous reactions.
- A **catalyst** does **not** affect the position of equilibrium; it only shortens the time to reach it.
- Industry often uses a **compromise** temperature and pressure.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | A reaction that can go in both the forward and reverse directions |
| Dynamic equilibrium | Forward and reverse rates equal; concentrations constant; both reactions continuing |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave, so equilibrium can be reached |
| Position of equilibrium | The composition of the equilibrium mixture; "to the right" means more products |
| Homogeneous reaction | All reactants and products in the same phase |
| Yield | Amount of product obtained compared with the maximum possible |
| Compromise conditions | Conditions that balance yield against rate, cost and safety |

## The approach to equilibrium

- Start: forward rate high, reverse rate zero.
- Forward rate falls as reactants are used; reverse rate rises as products form.
- Rates become equal: equilibrium. Concentrations then constant, **not necessarily equal**.

## Le Chatelier predictions

| Change | Shift of position | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Add reactant / remove product | Right | Uses up added reactant or replaces removed product |
| Add product / remove reactant | Left | Uses up added product |
| Increase pressure | Towards fewer moles of **gas** | Reduces the pressure |
| Decrease pressure | Towards more moles of gas | Raises the pressure |
| Equal gas moles on both sides | No shift with pressure | Neither side reduces pressure |
| Increase temperature | Endothermic direction | Absorbs heat |
| Decrease temperature | Exothermic direction | Releases heat |
| Add catalyst | No shift | Forward and reverse rates increase equally |

**Indirect concentration changes:** if the added substance is not in the equation, first say which species it removes or produces. Example: OH⁻ removes H⁺.

## Systems with visible colour changes

**Chromate–dichromate:** 2CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O

- Add acid → more orange. Add alkali → OH⁻ removes H⁺ → more yellow.

**Dinitrogen tetroxide–nitrogen dioxide:** N₂O₄ (colourless) ⇌ 2NO₂ (brown), ΔH = +57 kJ mol⁻¹

- Heat → browner (endothermic forward). Cool → paler.
- Compress: darker at once (higher [NO₂]), then fades a little (shift to fewer gas moles), ending darker than at the start.

**Hydrogen–iodine:** H₂ + I₂ (purple) ⇌ 2HI, equal gas moles

- Compress: darker only because every concentration rises; no shift and no later fading.

## Compromise conditions: the answer pattern

For an **exothermic** forward reaction with **fewer gas moles** on the right (for example CO + 2H₂ ⇌ CH₃OH, ΔH = −91 kJ mol⁻¹):

- **Temperature**: low temperature gives higher yield, but the rate is too slow → moderate temperature.
- **Pressure**: high pressure gives higher yield and faster rate, but equipment and energy are expensive and risk is greater → moderately high pressure.
- **Catalyst**: acceptable rate at a lower temperature; no effect on yield.
- **Recycling** unreacted gases raises the overall conversion.

## Exam traps

- "Concentrations are equal at equilibrium": wrong; they are constant.
- "The reaction stops": wrong; it is dynamic.
- Direction stated without a reason linked to the equation.
- Counting liquid or solid moles when applying the pressure rule.
- Catalyst "increases yield" or "shifts the equilibrium right".
- Compromise answers that mention only yield, with no rate, cost or safety.
- Forgetting that a shift only partly opposes a change (the compressed NO₂ mixture stays darker).

## Quick self-test

1. State two features of a reaction at dynamic equilibrium.
2. Why must the system be closed?
3. State Le Chatelier's principle.
4. 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O. What colour change is seen when NaOH(aq) is added, and why?
5. For N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ (ΔH positive), predict the colour change on cooling.
6. Predict the effect of increasing pressure on the position of 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g).
7. Predict the effect of increasing pressure on the position of H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2HCl(g).
8. Why does a compressed N₂O₄/NO₂ mixture finish darker than it started?
9. State the effect of a catalyst on the position of equilibrium and on the time taken to reach it.
10. For an exothermic forward reaction, why is a low temperature not used in industry?
11. Give two reasons why an extremely high pressure is not used.
12. For CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g), ΔH = +206 kJ mol⁻¹, which conditions of temperature and pressure would give the highest equilibrium yield?

### Answers

1. Forward and reverse rates are **equal**; concentrations are **constant**.
2. So that no reactant or product can escape; otherwise the reverse reaction cannot keep pace.
3. If a factor affecting a system at equilibrium is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
4. Orange → **yellow**: OH⁻ removes H⁺, so the position shifts left to replace it.
5. The mixture becomes **paler**: the position shifts in the exothermic direction, to the left.
6. Shifts **right** (3 moles of gas → 2).
7. **No effect**: 2 moles of gas on each side.
8. The shift only partly opposes the increase in concentration caused by compression.
9. **No effect** on position; equilibrium is reached **sooner**.
10. The rate would be too slow to be economic.
11. Expensive thick-walled equipment and compressors (high energy costs); greater safety risk.
12. **High temperature** (endothermic forward) and **low pressure** (2 moles of gas → 4).
