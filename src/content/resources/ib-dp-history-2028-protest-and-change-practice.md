## Part 1: a paper 1 style set (24 marks)

**Inquiry question: What led to the emergence of protest movements?**

**Source A** (constructed for practice). From a Tunisian graduate's post on a social media page, December 2010.

> "I studied for six years and I sell vegetables from a cart. The police take my scales and my money. The officials are friends of the family at the palace. Tonight we go out into the street."

**Source B** (constructed for practice). From a statement by a government spokesperson on state television, January 2011.

> "The government has created thousands of new jobs and will create more. A small number of troublemakers, encouraged from abroad, are using the internet to damage the country's stability. The security forces will protect citizens and property."

**Source C** (constructed for practice). From a report by a foreign news correspondent published in 2011.

> "In the interior towns, young people with diplomas have been jobless for years. Videos of the protests spread rapidly on the internet, even though state television said nothing."

*Context for source C:* The correspondent visited Tunisia a few weeks after the departure of Ben Ali and wrote for a newspaper abroad.

**1.** Explain how the **content** of both source A and source B can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**2.** Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**3.** Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[12]**

## Part 2: short knowledge questions

**4.** Explain one way in which the National Organization for Women challenged authority in the United States. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one reason why unemployment contributed to protest in Tunisia. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6):
- Source A: six years of study followed by selling vegetables shows graduate unemployment **[1]**; police seizing scales and money shows official harassment and repression **[1]**; the link with the president's family shows corruption, and the call to go out into the street shows the move to protest **[1]**.
- Source B: the claim of new jobs shows that the regime recognised unemployment as an issue **[1]**; the blame placed on troublemakers encouraged from abroad shows how the regime explained the protests, and its mistrust of dissent **[1]**; the reference to the internet shows the regime's concern about new media, and the promise to protect order shows the role of security forces **[1]**.
Markbands: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial explanation (maximum 3 if one source); 5 to 6 for a range of specific content from both sources, developed.

**2.** Marking points (6):
- Author: a foreign correspondent, an outsider who observed events at a distance and relied on interviews and online material **[1 to 2]**.
- Date: written a few weeks after Ben Ali left, so it reflects the excitement of the moment and may not show later complexity **[1 to 2]**.
- Audience and purpose: written for readers abroad, so it explains the events in general terms and may stress the role of the internet **[1 to 2]**.
- Link to the question: it is useful for the causes (youth unemployment, control of state media) and for outside perceptions, but less so for the full range of causes, or for the feelings of participants **[1]**.
Reward context linked to use.

**3.** Marking points (12):
- Source A: the perspective of an unemployed young graduate, expressing economic hardship and anger at corruption and repression **[2]**.
- Source B: the regime's perspective, defensive and emphasising jobs, order and foreign interference **[2]**.
- Source C: a foreign observer's perspective, analysing causes and the role of the internet, after the regime had fallen **[2]**.
- Similarities: A and B both refer to employment, and A and C both describe jobless graduates; B and C both mention the internet **[2]**.
- Differences: A blames corruption and police behaviour while B blames outsiders and a small group; A is a participant, B is an official and C an outsider; C contradicts B by describing years of joblessness and state silence **[2]**.
- Combined: the sources show that the protest movement emerged from economic hardship and repression, and that the regime misread or denied the causes; a full answer needs participants', regime and observer views **[2]**.
Markbands: 1 to 3 minimal; 4 to 6 partial; 7 to 9 at least two sources (maximum 9 for two sources); 10 to 12 insightful understanding of all three sources.

**4.** For example: NOW lobbied for enforcement of laws against employment discrimination, challenging the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission to act **[1]**; it campaigned for an Equal Rights Amendment to the Constitution **[1]**; it organised public demonstrations and campaigns, such as the Women's Strike for Equality in 1970 in which it took part, to gain publicity and members **[1]**.

**5.** For example: many young people held university degrees but could not find jobs, especially in inland regions **[1]**; this created frustration with a government that offered no opportunity and with a ruling circle seen as corrupt **[1]**; unemployed young people therefore formed the core of the street protests that began in Sidi Bouzid **[1]**.
