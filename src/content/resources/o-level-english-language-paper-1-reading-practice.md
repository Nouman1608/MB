## Section A

**1.** State the total marks and time allowed for Paper 1, and its weighting toward the final grade. **[3]**

**2.** State the Reading/Writing objective split for Paper 1, and how it compares with Paper 2. **[2]**

**3.** Name the two source texts used on Paper 1, and roughly how long each is. **[2]**

---

## Section B

**4.** A candidate answers Question 2 (Use of Language) by correctly explaining that a quoted phrase means "the character felt trapped," but does not discuss how the writer's specific word choice creates this effect on the reader.

**(a)** State which assessment objective Question 2 is marked against. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why this answer would not score full marks despite being an accurate paraphrase. **[3]**

**5.** A candidate's summary answer for Question 3a is 175 words long and includes every relevant point from Text B.

**(a)** State the word limit for Question 3a. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain what happens to the points made after the word limit is reached. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this matters even though every point is factually relevant. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between what Question 1's explicit-meaning and implicit-meaning sub-questions each require, using the relevant assessment objective codes. **[4]**

---

## Section C

**7.** A candidate spends equal time (roughly 55 minutes each) on Section A and Section B during the 2-hour paper, but reports running out of time to check their Question 1 and 3b answers before the end. Evaluate this candidate's timing approach and suggest one specific adjustment. **[6]**

**8.** Explain why the summary task (Question 3a) is examined on the Reading paper (Paper 1) even though it is partly marked against Writing objectives. **[5]**

---

## Worked answers

**1.** 50 marks; 2 hours; worth 50% of the final grade. **[3]**

**2.** 80% Reading, 20% Writing — the mirror image of Paper 2, which is 80% Writing, 20% Reading. **[2]**

**3.** Text A, a narrative text of about 900 words, and Text B, a shorter non-fiction or discursive text of about 550–600 words. **[2]**

**4. (a)** R4 — demonstrate understanding of how writers achieve effects and influence readers. **[1]**
**(b)** Question 2 is marked specifically against R4, which requires discussion of the *effect* the writer's language choice has on the reader, not just an accurate restatement of what the word or phrase means; correctly paraphrasing the meaning alone addresses R1/R2-style understanding, not R4, so the answer is being marked against the wrong objective even where the paraphrase itself is accurate. **[3]**

**5. (a)** No more than 150 words. **[1]**
**(b)** Points made after the 150-word limit is reached are not credited at all. **[2]**
**(c)** Because the limit applies to the summary as continuous writing as a whole, a summary that runs to 175 words has effectively "spent" its available space on material beyond the credited limit — the extra 25 words carry no additional Reading marks, and the candidate has also risked losing Writing marks for insufficiently concise, organised prose (W2/W3), so exceeding the limit costs marks even when every included point is genuinely relevant to the text. **[2]**

**6.** Explicit-meaning sub-questions (R1) are answerable by locating and restating information stated directly in the text; implicit-meaning sub-questions (R2) require inferring what a character thinks, feels or implies without it being spelled out, and must be grounded in a specific detail from the text rather than a disconnected guess. **[4]**

**7.** Spending equal time on both sections is a reasonable starting split, since Section A and Section B are each worth 25 marks, but running out of time to check answers suggests too little buffer was built into the plan, particularly given that Question 3a's 150-word limit requires extra planning time to select and order points before writing. A specific adjustment would be to plan for slightly less than the full 55 minutes on each section (for example, 50–52 minutes), deliberately reserving a short buffer at the end of the paper specifically to check that Question 1 and 3b answers are grounded in specific textual detail rather than general impressions, since these are exactly the kind of answers most likely to be improved by a final check. **[6]** (Marks for identifying the timing issue specifically and proposing a concrete, workable adjustment rather than a vague "manage time better" response.)

**8.** The summary task sits on the Reading paper because its core skill is a Reading skill: correctly identifying which points in Text B are relevant to the specific summary instruction (tested via R1 and R5). However, because a summary must be produced as continuous writing in the candidate's own words rather than simply copied or listed, the task also inherently requires organising the selected material into connected, well-expressed prose — which is why W2 (organise and structure ideas for deliberate effect) and W3 (range of vocabulary and sentence structures) are also credited. Examining both skill sets through a single task reflects that, in practice, correctly selecting relevant material and expressing it well are not fully separable skills, even though they are marked as separate objectives within the same answer. **[5]**
