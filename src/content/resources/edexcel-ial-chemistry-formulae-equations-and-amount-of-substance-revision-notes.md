**Use these values throughout:** H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, S 32.1, Ca 40.1;
L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; molar volume at room temperature and pressure
24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Definitions (1.1, 1.4)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atom | Smallest particle of an element that keeps its chemical identity |
| Element | Substance whose atoms all have the same atomic number |
| Ion | Particle carrying a charge because electrons were gained or lost |
| Molecule | Discrete particle of atoms joined by covalent bonds |
| Compound | Two or more elements chemically combined |
| Empirical formula | Lowest whole-number ratio of the atoms present |
| Molecular formula | Real atom count per molecule, element by element |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | Abundance-weighted average atomic mass, on the scale where carbon-12 = 12 exactly |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | Sum of Ar values for a molecule (simple molecular substances) |
| Relative formula mass | The same sum for a compound with a giant structure, e.g. NaCl, SiO₂ |
| Molar mass, M | Mass per mole of a substance, in g mol⁻¹ |
| ppm | Parts per million: by volume for atmospheric gases, by mass for solutes |

Ar, Mr and relative formula mass are ratios, so they have no units. Molar mass has units.

## Formula sheet

| Quantity | Expression | Units to use |
|---|---|---|
| Amount from mass (1.2, 1.7) | n = m / M | g, g mol⁻¹ |
| Particles (1.2) | N = n × L | mol, mol⁻¹ |
| Concentration (1.5) | c = n / V | mol, dm³ |
| Mass concentration (1.5) | g dm⁻³ = c × M | mol dm⁻³, g mol⁻¹ |
| Gas at room conditions (1.8) | V = n × 24.0 | dm³ |
| Ideal gas (1.8) | pV = nRT | Pa, m³, K |
| Molar mass of a vapour (1.8) | M = mRT / pV | g, Pa, m³, K |
| ppm by mass (1.4) | (mass of part / total mass) × 10⁶ | same mass units |
| ppm from a percentage (1.4) | % × 10⁴ | |
| Percentage yield (1.9) | actual / theoretical × 100% | same mass units |
| Atom economy (1.9) | M(desired product) / Σ M(all products) × 100% | g mol⁻¹ |

**Unit conversions:** cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³; dm³ ÷ 1000 = m³; cm³ × 10⁻⁶ = m³; kPa × 1000 = Pa;
°C + 273 = K.

## Method boxes

**Empirical formula (1.6)**

1. Write the mass or percentage of each element. If oxygen is not given, find it by difference.
2. Divide each by its Ar.
3. Scale the results so the smallest becomes 1.
4. If you get a value such as 1.5 or 1.33, multiply all values by 2 or 3.
5. For the molecular formula, divide Mr by the empirical formula mass.

**Reacting masses (1.7)**

1. Balanced equation.
2. Moles of the substance you know.
3. Mole ratio from the coefficients.
4. Moles × M for the substance you want.

**Gas volumes (1.8)**

1. At room temperature and pressure: V = n × 24.0 dm³.
2. At any other conditions: pV = nRT, after converting to Pa, m³ and K.
3. For gas-to-gas questions, volumes are in the same ratio as the equation's coefficients, provided
   all gases are measured at the same conditions.

**Ionic equations (1.3, 1.12)**

1. Write the full equation with state symbols.
2. Split every aqueous ionic substance into its ions. Keep solids, liquids, gases and covalent
   molecules together.
3. Cross out ions that are identical on both sides.
4. Check atoms and total charge balance.

## Small worked reminders

**Concentration.** 1.06 g of sodium carbonate in 100 cm³ of solution:
n = 1.06 / 106.0 = 0.0100 mol; c = 0.0100 / 0.100 = 0.100 mol dm⁻³; mass concentration = 10.6 g dm⁻³.

**Gas ratio.** For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g), 30 cm³ of hydrogen reacts with 10 cm³ of nitrogen to give
20 cm³ of ammonia, all at the same conditions.

**Atom economy can reach 100%.** When the equation has only one product, as in an addition reaction,
every reactant atom ends up in the desired product.

## Core Practical 1 in brief (1.11)

- Aim: measure the volume occupied by one mole of gas.
- Measure: mass of solid reactant (by difference), final gas volume, room temperature and pressure.
- Calculation: moles of gas from the equation and the mass used; molar volume = gas volume / moles.
- Typical sources of low results: gas escaping before the bung is fitted, gas dissolving in water if
  collected over water, an oxide layer on a metal, leaks at joints.
- Improve: use a larger mass so the percentage uncertainty in the mass is smaller; add the solid in a
  small tube that is tipped over after the bung is fitted.
- Percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty / measured value) × 100%. For a value found by difference, add
  the uncertainties of both readings.

## Test-tube reactions to know (1.12)

| Reaction type | Observation | Ionic equation |
|---|---|---|
| Metal displacement, e.g. Mg in Cu²⁺(aq) | Pink-brown solid; blue colour fades | Mg(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) |
| Acid + metal | Fizzing; solid disappears | Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g) |
| Acid + carbonate | Fizzing; gas turns limewater cloudy | CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) |
| Acid + alkali | No visible change; mixture warms | H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) |
| Precipitation, e.g. Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ | White precipitate | Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s) |
| Precipitation, e.g. Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ | White precipitate | Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Empirical vs molecular formula:** ratio vs actual count. They can be the same (H₂O) or different
  (C₂H₄ and CH₂).
- **Relative molecular mass vs relative formula mass:** molecules vs giant structures.
- **Percentage yield vs atom economy:** yield depends on the experiment; atom economy depends only on
  the equation.
- **ppm by volume vs ppm by mass:** gases in air vs dissolved substances.
- **mol dm⁻³ vs g dm⁻³:** convert using molar mass.

## Quick self-test

1. Work out the relative formula mass of calcium hydrogencarbonate, Ca(HCO₃)₂.
2. Calculate the amount in moles in 9.45 g of nitric acid, HNO₃.
3. How many molecules are in 0.0500 mol of oxygen gas?
4. 0.0400 mol of solute is dissolved in 160 cm³ of solution. Give the concentration.
5. Give the mass concentration of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide, NaOH.
6. Find the amount of gas in 450 cm³ at 35.0 °C and 98.0 kPa.
7. By mass, a hydrocarbon contains 82.8% C and 17.2% H. Find its empirical formula.
8. A 2.0 kg soil sample contains 0.0036 g of cadmium. Give this in ppm by mass.
9. Calculate the atom economy for making calcium oxide by CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂.
10. Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for any strong acid reacting with any alkali.
11. Calculate the volume at room temperature and pressure of 0.88 g of carbon dioxide.
12. A theoretical yield is 4.00 g and 3.10 g is obtained. Give the percentage yield.

### Answers

1. 40.1 + 2 × (1.0 + 12.0 + 3 × 16.0) = **162.1**
2. M = 63.0 g mol⁻¹; n = 9.45 / 63.0 = **0.150 mol**
3. 0.0500 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **3.01 × 10²² molecules**
4. 0.0400 / 0.160 = **0.250 mol dm⁻³**
5. 0.150 × 40.0 = **6.00 g dm⁻³**
6. n = pV / RT = (98 000 × 450 × 10⁻⁶) / (8.31 × 308) = **0.0172 mol**
7. C: 82.8 / 12.0 = 6.90; H: 17.2 / 1.0 = 17.2; ratio 1 : 2.49, i.e. 2 : 5, so **C₂H₅**
8. (0.0036 / 2000) × 10⁶ = **1.8 ppm**
9. 56.1 / 100.1 × 100 = **56.0%**
10. **H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)**
11. n = 0.88 / 44.0 = 0.0200 mol; V = 0.0200 × 24.0 = **0.480 dm³**
12. 3.10 / 4.00 × 100 = **77.5%**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing Mr for an ionic compound when the question asks about relative formula mass, or giving Ar
  or Mr a unit.
- Using 25 cm³ instead of 0.025 dm³ in c = n / V.
- Leaving kPa, cm³ or °C in pV = nRT; each one gives a wrong answer.
- Rounding a mole ratio too early: 0.0499 : 0.0748 is 1 : 1.5, which means 2 : 3, not 1 : 1.
- Ionic equations with spectator ions left in, or with charges that do not balance.
- No state symbols where they are requested, especially (s) on a precipitate.
- Using the coefficient of the wrong substance when converting moles across an equation.
- Calculating atom economy with only one of the waste products in the denominator.
- Describing an observation with the name of a product ("hydrogen is made") instead of what is seen
  ("fizzing").
