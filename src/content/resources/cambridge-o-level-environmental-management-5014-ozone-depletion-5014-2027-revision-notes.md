Condensed notes for sub-topic 4.4 Ozone depletion in the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus for 2027 to 2029. For explanations, worked data and a model answer, see the [ozone depletion study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-ozone-depletion-5014-2027/); for exam-style questions, try the [ozone depletion practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-ozone-depletion-5014-2027-practice/).

## Four statements

| Statement | Command | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 4.4.1 | State | CFCs in aerosols and refrigerants cause ozone depletion in the ozone layer |
| 4.4.2 | Describe | Three impacts |
| 4.4.3 | Describe | Three strategies |
| 4.4.4 | Discuss | Benefits and limitations of the strategies |

## The cause

- **CFCs = chlorofluorocarbons.**
- Uses named in the syllabus: **aerosols** (propellant) and **refrigerants** (cooling fluid in fridges, freezers and air conditioners).
- Unreactive at ground level → not broken down → drift up to the stratosphere.
- Ultraviolet radiation breaks them apart → chlorine released → chlorine destroys ozone; one chlorine atom destroys many ozone molecules.
- Long-lived: they stay in the atmosphere for decades.

Ozone layer = lower stratosphere (from 4.1). The deepest thinning is over Antarctica in spring.

## Impact chain

CFCs → thinner ozone layer → less ultraviolet absorbed → **higher levels of solar radiation reach the surface** → then:
- **increased rates of cancer** (skin) and **cataracts** (clouding of the eye lens);
- **damage to vegetation**, so **crop yields fall**.

## Strategies

1. **International agreement and policies**: the Montreal Protocol (1987) committed countries to phase out CFCs; national laws put it into practice.
2. **CFC ban and alternatives**: production and use banned; other propellants, pump sprays and new cooling fluids used instead.
3. **Safe disposal of CFCs**: remove and collect CFCs from old fridges, freezers and air conditioners before scrapping.

## Benefits and limitations

| Strategy | + | − |
|---|---|---|
| International agreement | Global action; almost all countries joined; CFC production fell sharply | Depends on compliance; illegal production must be detected |
| Ban and alternatives | Stops new CFCs; industries keep making products | Some early alternatives still harmed ozone; some later ones are greenhouse gases; existing CFCs remain |
| Safe disposal | Captures CFCs from old appliances | Needs collection systems and trained workers; informal scrapping releases CFCs |

**Overarching limitation**: CFCs last for decades, so recovery is slow even when every strategy works.

**Typical judgement**: largely successful, because new CFC release almost stopped, but the full benefit takes many years.

## Ozone depletion vs climate change

| | Ozone depletion | Climate change |
|---|---|---|
| Main cause in syllabus | CFCs | Carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour |
| Process | Ozone destroyed in the stratosphere | Enhanced greenhouse effect |
| Main effect | More ultraviolet radiation at the surface | Global warming and changing climate |
| Response so far | Global ban with alternatives; largely successful | Many strategies; emissions still high |

Why the ozone response worked better: a small number of chemicals, made by relatively few companies, with alternatives available. Greenhouse gases come from almost every part of the economy.

## Data skills

- **Lag**: chlorine in the stratosphere keeps rising for some years after CFC production falls, because CFCs take time to rise and last for decades.
- **Percentage fall** = (old − new) ÷ old × 100. Production falling from 800 to 40 thousand tonnes is a 95% fall.
- Describe a trend with two figures and a direction; explain it with the long lifetime of CFCs.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ozone layer | Region of the lower stratosphere rich in ozone that absorbs ultraviolet radiation |
| Ozone depletion | Thinning of the ozone layer |
| CFCs | Chlorofluorocarbons; chemicals once used in aerosols and refrigerants |
| Aerosol | A can that sprays a product using a propellant gas |
| Refrigerant | The cooling fluid in fridges, freezers and air conditioners |
| Ultraviolet radiation | Part of solar radiation that damages living cells |
| Cataract | Clouding of the lens of the eye |
| Montreal Protocol | The 1987 international agreement to phase out CFCs |
| Safe disposal | Removing and collecting CFCs before an appliance is scrapped |

## Exam traps

- Ozone depletion is caused by **CFCs**, not carbon dioxide.
- Ozone depletion does **not** cause global warming; keep the two separate.
- Write "more ultraviolet (solar) radiation", not "more heat".
- Impacts include cataracts and crop damage, not only skin cancer.
- The ban worked because **alternatives** existed; mention them.
- "Discuss" answers need the slow-recovery limitation.

## Self-test

1. What do the letters CFC stand for?
2. Name the two uses of CFCs given in the syllabus.
3. In which layer of the atmosphere is the ozone layer?
4. Why do CFCs reach the stratosphere instead of breaking down near the ground?
5. What is released from CFCs that destroys ozone?
6. State the three impacts of ozone depletion.
7. What is a cataract?
8. Name the 1987 international agreement on CFCs.
9. Why was it important that alternatives to CFCs were available?
10. Why must old fridges be disposed of safely?
11. Give the main reason the ozone layer is recovering only slowly.
12. Give one way the ozone response differs from the response to climate change.

## Answers

1. Chlorofluorocarbons.
2. Aerosols and refrigerants.
3. The lower stratosphere.
4. They are unreactive, so they are not broken down in the lower atmosphere.
5. Chlorine.
6. Higher levels of solar radiation at the surface; increased rates of cancer and cataracts; damage to vegetation resulting in reduced crop yields.
7. Clouding of the lens of the eye.
8. The Montreal Protocol.
9. Industries could stop using CFCs without losing their products, so the ban could be followed.
10. They still contain CFCs, which would be released if the appliance were broken up.
11. CFCs already in the atmosphere last for decades.
12. Ozone involved a few chemicals with alternatives and was largely solved; greenhouse gases come from almost all activities and emissions remain high.
