## Section A

**1.** State the specification's central idea about how readers relate to a text. **[2]**

**2.** Name the three literary forms this component's technique vocabulary must be adapted across. **[3]**

**3.** State one form-specific consideration relevant to drama that does not apply to prose. **[1]**

## Section B

**4.** A poem's speaker addresses an absent listener throughout, without the listener ever responding.

**(a)** Name the technique being used. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain one interpretive effect this technique has on how a reader constructs meaning from the poem. **[2]**

**5.** A novel shifts between two narrators' first-person accounts of the same events, each contradicting details of the other's version.

**(a)** Explain what interpretive possibilities this dual, contradictory narration opens up for the reader, compared with a single reliable narrator. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why simply identifying "dual narration" as the technique, without further analysis, would not satisfy this component's requirements. **[2]**

**6.** A play uses dramatic irony extensively, where the audience knows information a central character does not.

**(a)** Explain how dramatic irony shapes an audience's relationship to that character differently from how a reader relates to a character in a novel using a comparable technique. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why staging-specific considerations make drama's version of this analysis distinct from prose or poetry. **[2]**

## Section C

**7.** Using a studied work, identify one distinctive technique the writer uses and explain in detail how it shapes a reader's construction of meaning across the whole text, not just at a single point. **[8]**

## Worked answers

**1.** That reading is an active process: readers construct meaning from a writer's choices, and two readers can build genuinely different, both defensible, interpretations of the same passage, rather than there being one meaning simply transmitted to a passive reader. **[2]**

**2.** Prose, poetry, and drama. **[3]**

**3.** Staging (or dialogue, or dramatic irony) -- a consideration specific to a work being performed. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Apostrophe (direct address to an absent or non-responding listener). **[1]**
**(b)** Because the listener never responds, the reader is positioned to fill the resulting silence with their own inference about what the addressed listener might think or feel, making the reader an active participant in completing the poem's implied dialogue rather than a passive observer of a two-way exchange. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Contradictory dual narration forces the reader to actively adjudicate between two accounts rather than accepting either at face value, opening up genuine ambiguity about what "really" happened; this positions the reader as an active investigator constructing their own version of events from partial, competing evidence, in contrast to a single reliable narrator, where the reader's task is closer to simply receiving an account already resolved for them. **[3]**
**(b)** Because naming the technique alone is descriptive, not analytical -- this component specifically rewards explaining the *effect* of a technique on how meaning is constructed, so an answer must go on to say what the contradiction does to the reader's interpretive experience (e.g. sustained uncertainty, active comparison between accounts) rather than stopping at identification. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Dramatic irony creates a gap in knowledge between audience and character that positions the audience as a knowing observer of the character's actions, often generating tension, anticipation or (in comedy) humour from watching the character act without full information; a comparable technique in a novel (e.g. narrative foreshadowing the reader understands but a character does not) creates a similar knowledge gap, but the audience's real-time, collective and non-pausable experience of watching a live performance intensifies the anticipatory effect in a way private, self-paced novel-reading does not replicate in the same way. **[3]**
**(b)** Drama is performed and staged, so its version of this analysis must additionally account for elements a page-based text does not have -- how physical staging, actor delivery, timing and audience visibility (all of whom can see what a character on stage cannot) combine to produce the irony's effect, considerations with no direct equivalent in prose or poetry's purely textual analysis. **[2]**

**7.** A strong answer names a specific technique from a specific studied work (e.g. a particular narrative structure, a recurring type of imagery, or a characteristic use of dialogue) and traces its effect across the whole text rather than at one isolated moment -- showing, for instance, how an unreliable narrator's credibility is gradually undermined over several chapters, or how a recurring image accumulates and shifts meaning as it reappears in different contexts. The strongest answers explicitly connect the technique to the specification's central idea that readers actively construct meaning, showing how the writer's specific craft choice shapes what interpretive possibilities are opened up or closed off for the reader at different points in the text, rather than simply cataloguing where the technique appears. **[8]** (Marks for a specific, correctly identified technique; sustained tracing of its effect across the text rather than a single instance; and explicit connection to reader-constructed meaning.)

## Why this set stays effect-focused

Every worked answer deliberately goes beyond naming a technique to explaining its effect on the
reader, because the revision notes identify exactly this gap -- description without effect -- as the
most common way answers in this component fall short. Question 6 in particular is written to test
whether a student can adapt the same underlying skill (explaining how a technique shapes meaning)
across a form-specific context (staging) that has no direct equivalent in prose or poetry, which is
the adaptation the revision notes flag as necessary rather than assuming techniques transfer unchanged
across forms.
