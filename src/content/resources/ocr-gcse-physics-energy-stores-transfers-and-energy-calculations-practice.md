## Questions

**1.** State the law of conservation of energy for a closed system, and explain what happens to the energy when a bouncing ball finally comes to rest. **[3]**

**2.** For each situation, describe which energy stores change.

**(a)** A cyclist brakes and comes to a stop. **[2]**
**(b)** An electric kettle brings water to the boil. **[2]**

**3.** A runner of mass 62 kg moves at 7.5 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the runner's kinetic energy. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** The runner speeds up to 9.0 m/s. Calculate the increase in kinetic energy, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**4.** A parcel of mass 3.5 kg is lifted from the floor onto a shelf 1.8 m high.

**(a)** Calculate how much energy is transferred to the parcel's gravitational store. **[2]**
**(b)** The parcel falls off the shelf. Ignoring air resistance, find how fast it is moving as it reaches the floor. **[3]**

**5.** A spring of spring constant 120 N/m is compressed by 0.060 m.

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in the spring. **[2]**
**(b)** The spring is released and all this energy is transferred to a dart of mass 0.020 kg. Calculate the speed of the dart. **[3]**

**6.** A block of iron of mass 0.75 kg (specific heat capacity 450 J/kg °C) is heated from 15 °C to 95 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred to the block. **[2]**
**(b)** The heater has a power of 150 W and all its energy goes to the block. Calculate the time taken. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the time in a real experiment would be longer. **[1]**

**7.** A 9.0 V supply drives a current of 0.50 A through a motor for 40 s. The motor lifts a 0.80 kg load through 5.0 m in this time.

**(a)** Find the total charge that passes through the motor. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred by the supply. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained by the load and state how much energy goes to other stores. **[3]**

**8.** An immersion heater has a power of 3.0 kW and is switched on for 40 minutes. Electricity costs 30 p per kW h.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred in kW h. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Convert your answer to (a) into joules. **[2]**

**9.** A ball of mass 0.060 kg is dropped from a height of 2.0 m and rebounds to 1.3 m.

**(a)** Calculate the energy dissipated during the bounce. **[2]**
**(b)** State where this energy goes. **[1]**
**(c)** Ignoring air resistance, find the ball's speed on reaching the floor. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one way to make the measurement of the rebound height more reliable. **[1]**

**10.** A car of mass 1200 kg travels at 25 m/s and brakes to a stop.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the car. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the changes in energy stores as the car stops. **[2]**
**(c)** The braking force is a constant 7500 N. Calculate the braking distance. **[2]**
**(d)** The car now travels at 50 m/s and brakes with the same force. Use calculation to show how the braking distance changes. **[2]**

**11.** A crate of mass 40 kg is pushed up a ramp 6.0 m long by a constant force of 120 N acting along the ramp. The crate rises 1.5 m vertically.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the pushing force. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how much the crate's gravitational potential energy increases. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred to the thermal store and explain why it is not zero. **[3]**
**(d)** The crate is released at the top and slides back down. Suppose all its gravitational potential energy became kinetic energy. Calculate its speed at the bottom. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Energy is never created or destroyed; it is only moved from one store to another [1]. In a closed system the total energy does not change [1]. The ball's kinetic and gravitational energy are transferred to the thermal stores of the ball, floor and air (and sound), so the energy is spread out, not lost [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that says energy is "lost" or "used up" contradicts the law. Naming the thermal store of the surroundings is the point that separates a full answer from a partial one.

**2. (a)** The kinetic store of the cyclist and bike decreases [1]. The thermal store of the brakes, wheels and surroundings increases [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Work is done by the current, so energy is transferred from the electrical supply [1] to the thermal store of the water (and some to the kettle and air) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a store that goes down and a store that goes up. Writing only "kinetic energy turns into heat" without saying what is heated is easy to under-credit.

**3. (a)** E = ½mv² = ½ × 62 × 7.5² [1] = 1743.75 J = **1700 J** (2 s.f.) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** E = ½ × 62 × 9.0² = 2511 J [1]. Increase = 2511 − 1744 = 767 J = **770 J** (2 s.f.) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out your substitution, not just the final value. Squaring only the 7.5 and not the whole speed is the usual slip, so write v² clearly.

**4. (a)** E = mgh = 3.5 × 9.8 × 1.8 [1] = 61.74 J = **62 J** (2 s.f.) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The gravitational potential energy becomes kinetic energy: ½mv² = 61.74 [1]. v² = 2 × 61.74 / 3.5 = 35.28 [1]. v = 5.94 = **5.9 m/s** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the energy link (gravitational potential becomes kinetic) in words. It shows your method even if a calculation slip follows.

**5. (a)** E = ½kx² = ½ × 120 × 0.060² [1] = **0.216 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** ½mv² = 0.216 [1]. v² = 2 × 0.216 / 0.020 = 21.6 [1]. v = 4.65 = **4.6 m/s** (2 s.f.) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the extension into metres before squaring. Using 6.0 for centimetres would give an energy 10 000 times too large.

**6. (a)** ΔE = mcΔθ = 0.75 × 450 × (95 − 15) [1] = **27 000 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** t = E / P = 27 000 / 150 [1] = **180 s** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Some energy heats the surroundings (or the heater itself) rather than the block [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The temperature change is a difference (80 °C), not 95 °C. For the explanation, say where the extra energy goes, not just "heat is lost".

**7. (a)** Q = It = 0.50 × 40 [1] = **20 C** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** E = QV = 20 × 9.0 [1] = **180 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** E = mgh = 0.80 × 9.8 × 5.0 [1] = **39.2 J** [1]. Energy to other stores = 180 − 39.2 = **140.8 J** (about 140 J), mostly the thermal store of the motor and surroundings [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how each part feeds the next. Using your own answer from (b) in (c) is good practice, and writing the equation each time makes your method easy to follow.

**8. (a)** t = 40 / 60 = 0.667 h [1]. E = Pt = 3.0 × 0.667 = **2.0 kW h** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Cost = 2.0 × 30 = **60 p** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 1 kW h = 3 600 000 J [1], so E = 2.0 × 3 600 000 = **7 200 000 J** (7.2 × 10⁶ J) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep kW with hours. Using 40 for the time with kW gives a figure 60 times too large, and mixing minutes into the equation is a common slip.

**9. (a)** Energy dissipated = loss in gravitational potential energy = mg × change in height = 0.060 × 9.8 × (2.0 − 1.3) [1] = 0.4116 J = **0.41 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It goes to the thermal stores of the ball, the floor and the air, with some as sound [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** mgh = ½mv², so v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 2.0) [1] = 6.26 = **6.3 m/s** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Repeat the drop several times and calculate a mean rebound height (or read the height at eye level against a fixed rule) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For (d), give a specific action that improves reliability. "Be more careful" does not say what you would do.

**10. (a)** E = ½mv² = ½ × 1200 × 25² [1] = **375 000 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The kinetic store decreases [1]. The thermal store of the brakes, tyres and road increases by the same amount [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Work done = Fs, so s = 375 000 / 7500 [1] = **50 m** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** At 50 m/s, E = ½ × 1200 × 50² = 1 500 000 J [1]. s = 1 500 000 / 7500 = **200 m**, four times as far [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show the effect" question wants figures from a calculation. Saying "it takes longer to stop" without the 200 m comparison is not a calculation.

**11. (a)** W = Fs = 120 × 6.0 [1] = **720 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** E = mgh = 40 × 9.8 × 1.5 [1] = **588 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Energy to thermal store = 720 − 588 = **132 J** [1]. It is not zero because friction acts between the crate and the ramp [1], so some of the work done heats the crate and ramp [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** ½mv² = 588 [1], v² = 2 × 588 / 40 = 29.4 [1], v = 5.42 = **5.4 m/s** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) use the vertical height of 1.5 m, not the 6.0 m slope length. Using the slope length is the most common error here.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that energy is "used up" or "lost" instead of naming the store it moves to.
- Saying "heat" or "energy" with no named store.
- Not squaring the speed or extension, or squaring only part of the product.
- Leaving extension in centimetres or mass in grams.
- Putting the length along a ramp into mgh in place of the vertical rise.
- Mixing kW and seconds, or W and hours, in E = Pt.
- Giving the final temperature rather than the temperature change in mcΔθ.
- Rounding in the middle of a calculation, or giving too many significant figures at the end.
- Leaving out units, or giving joules when kW h is asked for.
