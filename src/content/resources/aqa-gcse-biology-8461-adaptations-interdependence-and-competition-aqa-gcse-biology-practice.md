These questions cover section **4.7.1 Adaptations, interdependence and competition** of the AQA GCSE Biology
(8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. Every question suits both tiers. All data are invented for
practice. A calculator is useful for questions 8 and 11.

## Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by a population. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is meant by a community. **[1]**

**2.** A gardener sows lettuce seeds very close together in a tray. Suggest **two** factors the seedlings
compete for. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each adaptation is structural, behavioural or functional.

**(a)** Penguins huddle together in winter storms. **[1]**
**(b)** A seal has a thick layer of fat under its skin. **[1]**
**(c)** A fish in icy water makes chemicals in its blood that stop it freezing. **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** State what is meant by an extremophile. **[1]**
**(b)** Bacteria live around deep-sea vents where the water is above 80 °C. Suggest why the enzymes of most other
organisms would not work there. **[2]**

**5.** Trees along the bank of a stream are cut down. Name **two** abiotic factors in the stream that are likely
to change. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** State what is meant by a stable community. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **one** event that could make a stable community unstable. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** On a small island, seabirds nest in large numbers. Their droppings add mineral ions to the soil, and
plants on the island grow well. Rats arrive on a ship and eat seabird eggs. Explain how the arrival of rats
could affect the plants on the island. **[4]**

**8.** A student investigates a woodland flower at different distances from the edge of a wood.

| Distance into wood (m) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light intensity (% of open field) | 100 | 70 | 40 | 20 | 10 |
| Mean plants per m² | 24 | 18 | 9 | 4 | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in mean plants per m² from 0 m to 40 m. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship between light intensity and the number of plants. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why there are fewer plants further into the wood. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest **one** other abiotic factor that may change with distance into the wood. **[1]**

**9.** A species of hare lives on cold mountains. Its fur is brown in summer and white in winter, it has thick
fur and large, furry hind feet, and it shelters in hollows in the snow. Explain how **three** of these features
help the hare to survive. **[6]**

**10.** A fungal disease kills most of the ash trees in a mixed wood. Many insect species feed only on ash leaves.
Explain how the disease could affect the rest of the woodland community. **[4]**

**11.** A scientist grows two plant species, P and Q, in pots of the same soil, alone and together. The table
shows the mean dry mass of each species after eight weeks.

| Species | Grown alone (g) | Grown together (g) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 40 | 32 |
| Q | 36 | 12 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the mass of Q when grown with P. Give your answer to 2 significant
figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify which species is the better competitor and give a reason from the data. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** resources the plants were competing for in the pots. **[2]**

**12.** Hot deserts have high daytime temperatures and very little water. Explain how structural, behavioural and
functional adaptations help organisms to survive in hot deserts. Use examples in your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** All the organisms of one species living in a habitat [1]
**(b)** All the populations of different species living in a habitat [1]

**2.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: light; space; water; mineral ions (from the soil)
*Examiner insight:* "Food" scores nothing for plants.

**3. (a)** Behavioural [1]
**(b)** Structural [1]
**(c)** Functional [1]

**4. (a)** An organism that lives in an extreme environment, such as at high temperature, high pressure or high
salt concentration [1]
**(b)** At high temperatures most enzymes change shape (denature) [1]; so the substrate no longer fits the active
site and reactions such as respiration stop [1]

**5.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: light intensity (more light reaches the water); temperature
(water warms up more); moisture or water level; oxygen level (warmer water may hold less oxygen)

**6. (a)** All the species and environmental factors are in balance, so population sizes stay fairly constant [1]
**(b)** Any one: a new predator or pathogen arrives; a species is removed; a large change in an abiotic factor,
such as a flood or drought [1]

**7.** Rats eat seabird eggs, so fewer seabirds hatch and the seabird population decreases [1]; fewer seabirds
means fewer droppings [1]; so fewer mineral ions are added to the soil [1]; so plant growth decreases (plants
grow less well or fewer plants survive) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a direction word; "the rats affect the plants" scores nothing.

**8. (a)** 24 − 2 = 22; 22 ÷ 24 × 100 [1] = **92%** (allow 91.7%) [1]
**(b)** As light intensity decreases, the number of plants decreases [1]
**(c)** Less light reaches the ground further into the wood because of the tree canopy [1]; so the plants
photosynthesise less, and fewer can grow or survive [1]
**(d)** Any one: temperature; moisture level; wind intensity; soil pH or mineral content [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), the answer must link light to photosynthesis; "plants need light" alone is not enough.

**9.** Any three features, each with the feature identified and explained, 2 marks each [6]:
- white fur in winter matches the snow, so it is camouflaged [1]; it is less likely to be seen and eaten by
  predators [1]
- brown fur in summer matches the ground and plants [1]; so it is camouflaged when there is no snow [1]
- thick fur traps air, which insulates [1]; reducing heat loss in the cold [1]
- large furry hind feet spread its weight [1]; so it does not sink into snow and can escape predators (fur also
  insulates the feet) [1]
- sheltering in snow hollows is a behavioural adaptation [1]; that reduces heat loss from wind [1]

**10.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: insects that feed only on ash leaves lose their food, so
their populations decrease; animals that eat those insects, such as birds, have less food and may decrease; dead
ash trees let more light reach the ground, so ground plants increase; other tree species face less competition
for light, so they grow more; dead wood provides food for decomposers, which increase; animals that sheltered or
nested in ash trees lose shelter
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer follows at least one full chain from the disease to a consumer.

**11. (a)** 36 − 12 = 24; 24 ÷ 36 × 100 [1] = **67%** (2 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** P [1]; P's mass fell by only 8 g (20%) when grown with Q, while Q's fell by 24 g (67%) [1]
**(c)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: light; water; mineral ions; space (for roots)

**12.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer explains all three types with examples):
- structural: cacti have spines instead of leaves, reducing the surface area for water loss [1]
- structural: thick waxy cuticle, or large ears on some desert animals to transfer heat to the surroundings [1]
- behavioural: animals stay in burrows during the hottest part of the day and are active at night [1]
- behavioural: this avoids high temperatures and reduces water loss by evaporation [1]
- functional: producing very concentrated urine, so little water is lost [1]
- the adaptations are linked to the two problems: overheating and shortage of water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming adaptations without saying how each saves water or prevents overheating cannot reach
the top level.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying plants compete for food.
- Writing chains of effects without "increases" or "decreases".
- Naming an adaptation but not explaining how it helps in that habitat.
- Quoting the trend without figures when the question gives data.
- Confusing behavioural adaptations (what the organism does) with functional ones (body processes).
- Mixing up a population (one species) and a community (all species).
