Goods are made in one place and wanted in another. **Transport** moves them. Sub-topic 4.1 of Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100) covers the **three functions of transport** in the supply chain, the **types and methods** of transport with their advantages and disadvantages, the **factors that affect the choice** of method, and **containerisation**, including transshipment.

## The three functions of transport

| Function | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Gets goods to customers when they want them, in time to be useful | Fresh flowers flown overnight to arrive for a festival |
| **Place** | Moves goods from where they are produced to where they are needed | Tea from a plantation to supermarkets in another country |
| **Quantity** | Moves goods in the amounts needed, from large bulk loads to single parcels | A ship carrying thousands of tonnes of grain; a courier carrying a single parcel |

Without transport, a producer could only sell to people nearby. Transport widens the market and so supports trade.

## Types and methods of transport

| Type | Methods |
|---|---|
| Road | Delivery vans, trucks, couriers |
| Rail | Freight trains |
| Air | Freight planes |
| Sea | Ferry, tanker, container ship, cargo liner |
| Pipeline | Pipes for liquids and gases |

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Road** (vans, trucks, couriers) | Door-to-door delivery; flexible routes and times; good for short and medium distances; suits small loads | Traffic jams and delays; limited load size; higher cost per unit over long distances; pollution; drivers' hours limits |
| **Rail** (freight trains) | Carries heavy, bulky loads cheaply over long distances; less affected by road traffic; lower pollution per tonne | Fixed routes and timetables; needs another method to reach the door; not available everywhere |
| **Air** (freight planes) | Fastest method over long distances; good security; suits urgent, high-value or perishable goods | Most expensive; limits on size and weight; airports are often far from the customer; weather can delay flights |
| **Sea** (ferry, tanker, container ship, cargo liner) | Cheapest for large and heavy loads over long distances; can carry very large quantities; container ships suit manufactured goods | Slowest; needs ports and other transport to and from them; weather delays; risk of damage or loss |
| **Pipeline** | Cheap to run once built; continuous flow; very safe for liquids and gases; low labour costs | Only for liquids and gases; very expensive to build; fixed route; leaks can cause damage |

Types of ship: a **tanker** carries liquids such as oil; a **container ship** carries goods in standard containers; a **cargo liner** runs on a fixed route and timetable carrying general cargo; a **ferry** carries vehicles, passengers and goods over short sea crossings.

## Choosing a method of transport

Businesses weigh up these factors.

| Factor | What to ask | Effect on the choice |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of the goods** | Are they perishable, fragile, dangerous, heavy, liquid? | Fresh fruit needs speed or refrigeration; oil suits a pipeline or tanker |
| **Urgency** | How soon are they needed? | Urgent orders go by air or courier |
| **Cost** | What can the business afford? | Cheap, bulk goods go by sea or rail |
| **Security** | Is theft or damage likely? | High-value goods need secure transport such as air or guarded vehicles |
| **Distance** | How far? | Short distances suit road; long distances suit sea, rail or air |
| **Reliability of transporter** | Does the carrier deliver on time without loss? | A late supplier may disrupt production |

### Worked example: choosing between air and sea

A business must send 500 kg of clothing to a customer abroad. Air costs $4.20 per kg and takes 2 days. Sea costs $0.60 per kg and takes 25 days.

1. Air cost = 500 × 4.20 = **$2,100**.
2. Sea cost = 500 × 0.60 = **$300**.
3. Difference = 2,100 − 300 = **$1,800**.

If the clothes are for a fashion event in ten days, only air works despite the $1,800 extra cost. If the customer does not need them for two months, sea saves $1,800. The answer depends on **urgency** and **cost**.

### Worked example: matching goods to transport

| Goods | Likely method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh flowers for another continent | Air | Perishable and urgent |
| Crude oil to a refinery | Pipeline or tanker | Liquid in huge quantities |
| Coal from a mine to a power station | Rail | Heavy, bulky and long distance |
| A parcel to a local customer | Courier | Small and door-to-door |
| Thousands of cars | Cargo or container ship | Large quantity, long distance, not urgent |

## Containerisation

**Containerisation** is the use of standard-sized metal containers to move goods. Goods are packed once into the container at the factory. The container is then moved by lorry, train or ship without being unpacked.

### Features

- Containers have **standard sizes**, so they stack on ships and fit lorries and trains.
- They are **sealed**, so goods are protected from weather and theft.
- Special **cranes** lift them between ship, rail and road.
- **Transshipment** is the transfer of goods from one ship or vehicle to another during the journey, for example from a large ship at a hub port to smaller ships that take containers to other ports.

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Quick loading and unloading, so lower labour costs and less time in port | Expensive equipment such as cranes and special ships and lorries |
| Goods are better protected from damage and theft | Ports and roads must be large enough to handle containers |
| Easy to switch between sea, rail and road | Empty containers must be returned, which costs money |
| Standard sizes make it easy to plan loads | Not suitable for very large or unusual items, or for liquids without special containers |
| Lower insurance costs because of less damage | Containers can hide illegal goods, so inspections are needed |

### Worked example: cost per unit

A container holds 2,400 boxes of shirts. The shipping cost is $3,600 for the full container.

Cost per box = 3,600 ÷ 2,400 = **$1.50**.

If the container is only 75% full, it holds 0.75 × 2,400 = 1,800 boxes, and the cost per box rises to 3,600 ÷ 1,800 = **$2.00**. A full container is cheaper per unit, which is why businesses try to fill them.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** A company in Kenya exports cut flowers to Europe. Explain why air transport is suitable. [4]

> Flowers are perishable, so they must reach the customer quickly before they wilt [1]. Air freight is the fastest method over a long distance, so the flowers arrive fresh [1]. The flowers are valuable, and air transport has good security, so there is less chance of theft [1]. Although air is the most expensive method, the higher price of fresh flowers can cover the cost [1].

**Why it scores 4:** each point links a feature of air transport to the nature of the goods and the distance, and the last point deals with the disadvantage.

## Common mistakes

- Giving only a general advantage ("fast") without linking it to the goods.
- Confusing the function **place** with the factor **distance**.
- Forgetting the disadvantages in an evaluation.
- Suggesting a pipeline for solid goods.
- Treating containerisation as a type of transport. It is a method of packing and moving goods, using ships, trains and lorries.
- Saying sea is best because it is fast. It is cheap but slow.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** the goods, the distance and the urgency in the question first.
- **Explain** by linking the method's feature to that detail.
- **Evaluate** by giving a disadvantage and a justified choice.
