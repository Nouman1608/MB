## Questions

**1.** Define what is meant by a divorce of ownership from control. **[2]**

**2.** Market K has 2 firms selling 90% of all output. Their products differ in design, and a new firm would need a very costly plant and a licence. Market L has 300 sellers of the same product, and entry is easy. Identify the market structure nearest to each and justify each choice using all three factors that distinguish structures. **[4]**

**3.** Firm C faces the demand and cost schedule below (money in pounds, Q in units).

| Q | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | | 44 | 40 | 36 | 32 | 28 | 24 | 20 |
| Total cost | 30 | 50 | 66 | 80 | 94 | 110 | 130 | 156 |

Calculate marginal revenue and marginal cost for each unit, identify the profit-maximising output and calculate the maximum profit. **[5]**

**4.** A firm made sales of £9.6 million in a market worth £80 million. Next year its sales rise by 25% and the market grows by 10%. Calculate the firm's market share in each year, giving the second to one decimal place. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two ways in which a divorce of ownership from control may affect the objectives of a firm. **[4]**

**6.** Firm E has the demand P = 72 − 2Q and total cost TC = 36 + 6Q + Q², with Q in thousands of units and money in thousands of pounds.

**(a)** Find the output, price and profit when profit is maximised. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the output, price and profit if the firm maximises sales revenue. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why managers of a large company might satisfice rather than maximise profit, and state one effect on output. **[4]**

**8.** Firm H has the demand P = 70 − 2Q and total cost TC = 40 + 10Q + Q² (Q in hundreds, money in thousands of pounds).

**(a)** Work out the output that maximises profit, with the price and profit at that output. **[3]**
**(b)** The firm improves product quality. Demand becomes P = 72 − 2Q and cost becomes TC = 40 + 18Q + Q². Calculate the new maximum profit and state what it implies about the firm's objective if it goes ahead. **[2]**

**9.** Distinguish between the objectives of survival and growth for a firm. **[4]**

**10.** Firm G has the demand P = 100 − 2Q and total cost TC = 60 + 10Q + Q² (Q in thousands, money in thousands of pounds). Shareholders have hired managers whose pay depends on sales revenue.

**(a)** Find the output, price and profit when the firm maximises profit. **[3]**
**(b)** The managers maximise revenue but shareholders require profit of at least 600. Using the table below, identify the output chosen and its revenue. **[3]**
**(c)** State the profit given up and one effect on consumers. **[2]**

| Q | 14 | 15 | 16 | 17 | 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Profit | 612 | 615 | 612 | 603 | 588 |

**11.** "Because of the divorce of ownership from control, large firms no longer try to maximise profit." Evaluate this statement. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The owners of the firm (shareholders) are different people from those who run it [1]. Decisions are made by managers rather than by the owners [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that only say "owners and managers are different" tend to be thin. Show who controls decisions.

**2.** Market K: oligopoly [1], because there are few firms with a high combined share, differentiated products and high barriers (cost and licence) [1]. Market L: close to perfect competition [1], because there are many sellers, an identical product and easy entry [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the structure earns little unless each choice is backed by evidence from the stem on number, differentiation and entry.

**3.** Price × Q gives TR of 44, 80, 108, 128, 140, 144, 140, so MR is 44, 36, 28, 20, 12, 4, −4 [1]. MC from the total cost column is 20, 16, 14, 14, 16, 20, 26 [1]. MR is above MC up to the 4th unit (20 against 14) and below it at the 5th (12 against 16) [1]. Profit-maximising output is **4 units** [1]. Profit = 128 − 94 = **£34** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the MR and MC lines and compare them unit by unit. A correct output without comparison is hard to justify, and the profit step is easy to miss.

**4.** Year 1 share = 9.6 / 80 = **12.0%** [1]. Year 2 sales = 9.6 × 1.25 = 12.0 and the market = 80 × 1.10 = 88 [1]. Share = 12.0 / 88 = **13.6%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the growth to the market as well as the firm. Using 12.0 / 80 is a common slip.

**5.** First, managers may pursue growth or sales revenue because these raise their pay and status [1], whereas shareholders want profit, so the objective moves away from profit maximisation [1]. Second, managers may choose to protect themselves with safe, steady results or generous staff benefits [1], which raises costs and lowers profit below the maximum [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each way needs a developed link from managers' motives to the change in objective. Listing "growth, revenue" with no reason stays at one point.

**6.** **(a)** MR = 72 − 4Q and MC = 6 + 2Q [1]. Setting them equal gives Q = **11**, then P = 72 − 22 = **£50** [1]. TR = 550, TC = 36 + 66 + 121 = 223, profit = **£327 thousand** [1].
**(b)** MR = 0 gives Q = **18** [1], P = 72 − 36 = **£36** [1]. TR = 648, TC = 36 + 108 + 324 = 468, profit = **£180 thousand** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use MC = MR for profit and MR = 0 for revenue. Answers that apply MC = MR to both parts lose the contrast.

**7.** Managers lack exact knowledge of MC and MR [1], and must balance several groups such as shareholders and staff [1], so they aim for a target level of profit and stop searching once it is met [1]. Output may therefore differ from the MC = MR level, with less pressure to cut costs or find the best output [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason before the effect. A bare definition of satisficing does not answer "why".

**8.** **(a)** MR = 70 − 4Q and MC = 10 + 2Q give Q = **10** [1], P = **£50** [1], profit = 500 − 240 = **£260 thousand** [1].
**(b)** New MR = MC: 72 − 4Q = 18 + 2Q, Q = 9, P = 54, TR = 486, TC = 283, profit = **£203 thousand** [1]. Profit falls by 57, so the firm gives up profit for quality, which suggests an objective other than profit maximisation (or a longer-term view) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Finish the sentence on what the numbers imply. The question asks for the objective, not only a profit figure.

**9.** Survival means keeping the firm in business, often by covering its costs when conditions are hard [1]. Growth means increasing the firm's size, sales or capital [1]. Survival is defensive and short-run [1], while growth is expansionary, may be financed from profit and may reduce current dividends [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers to "distinguish" gain from stating the contrast between the two, not just defining each in isolation.

**10.** **(a)** MR = 100 − 4Q and MC = 10 + 2Q give Q = **15** [1], P = **£70** [1], profit = 1050 − 435 = **£615 thousand** [1].
**(b)** Output of 17 has profit 603, which is above 600, and 18 has 588, which is below [1]. The managers choose Q = **17** [1], where P = 66 and TR = 1122 [1].
**(c)** Profit given up = 615 − 603 = **£12 thousand** [1]. Consumers get more output (17 against 15) at a lower price (£66 against £70) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the table to find the highest output that still meets the profit floor. Stopping at 15, the profit-maximising output, misses that revenue still rises beyond it.

**11.** Points for the statement: shareholders are dispersed and cannot monitor managers [1], managers may seek growth, revenue or perks [1], and the evidence of higher costs shows profit may fall short of the maximum [1]. Points against: shareholders use controls such as share options and voting [1], takeover threat disciplines poor performers [1], and managers who own shares or are paid on profit gain from maximising it [1]. Further context: the answer depends on the size of the firm, the competitiveness of the market and whether the firm has a long-term plan [1]. The statement is too strong because "no longer" overstates the case, so managers' behaviour is more likely partly profit-seeking and partly satisficing [1]. A reasoned judgement with justification [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation gains when you weigh both sides and reach a justified judgement using context such as size, competition and controls, instead of listing points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a market structure with no evidence on number of firms, differentiation and entry.
- Reading MR and MC from the wrong curves or mixing total and marginal values.
- Reading price from the MR or MC curve instead of the demand curve.
- Using MC = MR for revenue maximisation instead of MR = 0.
- Forgetting to apply growth to both the firm and the market in a market share calculation.
- Describing the divorce of ownership from control without linking it to objectives, conduct and performance.
- Treating every effect as harmful, with no controls or benefits.
- Defining satisficing as "no profit" or "loss-making".
