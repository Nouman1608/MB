## Definitions to learn

| Term | Meaning (standard working definition) |
|---|---|
| Budget | A financial plan for a future period, stated in money terms |
| Cost centre | A part of a business where costs are collected but no revenue is attributed |
| Profit centre | A part of a business where both costs and revenue are identified, so profit can be found |
| Variance | The difference between the actual figure and the budgeted figure |
| Favorable variance | A variance that increases profit compared with the budget |
| Adverse variance | A variance that reduces profit compared with the budget |

## Cost centre or profit centre?

Ask one question: does this part of the business earn revenue that can be traced to it?

- Yes: profit centre. Examples: a shop, an online store, a branch, a product line with its own sales.
- No: cost centre. Examples: human resources, IT support, finance, cleaning, security.

Roles in brief:

| | Cost centre | Profit centre |
|---|---|---|
| Aim of the manager | Keep spending within the budget | Meet revenue, cost and profit targets |
| Main use to owners | Control and accountability | Judge performance, decide where to invest or review |
| Main risk | Cutting cost lowers the service others receive | Managers pursue their own profit and ignore the whole business |

## Method: constructing a budget

1. Choose the period and split it into months.
2. Forecast units and price, then work out sales revenue (units × price).
3. Calculate variable costs (cost per unit × units) and list fixed costs by month.
4. Calculate gross profit (sales revenue − cost of sales), then deduct other expenses.
5. Find profit or loss for each month and total each row.
6. Cross-check: the monthly totals across must equal the column totals down.
7. State your assumptions.

A cost centre budget is the same but stops after the cost lines.

Mini reminder with new numbers: a stall plans 450 units at a price of 14 dollars. Unit cost is 9 dollars and fixed costs are 1,200 dollars. Revenue is 6,300 and variable cost is 4,050. Profit is 6,300 − 4,050 − 1,200 = 1,050.

## Method: variances

1. Write the budget figure and the actual figure for the same item and period.
2. Variance = actual − budget.
3. Percentage = variance ÷ budget × 100. Divide by budget, not actual. This is the standard form, as the guide's formulae list does not give one.
4. Decide F or A using the rule below.
5. Suggest a reason that fits the case.

| Item | Actual above budget | Actual below budget |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | F | A |
| Profit | F | A |
| Cost | A | F |

A memory aid: ask "did this make profit bigger than planned?" If yes, favorable.

### Worked reminder: Kowhai Candles

Kowhai Candles budgets one month for its workshop profit centre. The actual figures are alongside.

| US dollars | Budget | Actual | Variance | % of budget | F or A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales revenue | 18,000 | 19,350 | +1,350 | +7.5 | F |
| Wax and materials | 6,400 | 7,040 | +640 | +10.0 | A |
| Rent | 2,500 | 2,500 | 0 | 0.0 | none |
| Wages | 3,100 | 3,380 | +280 | +9.0 | A |
| Profit | 6,000 | 6,430 | +430 | +7.2 | F |

The profit variance is favorable (+430) even though two cost variances are adverse. This is because the revenue gain of 1,350 outweighs the extra costs of 920. Check: 1,350 − 640 − 280 = 430.

Costs rose by 10.0% on materials while revenue rose by only 7.5%, so profit per unit of revenue is under pressure. A manager should ask whether the extra sales needed extra wax, or whether the wax supplier raised its price.

## Causes to quote

| Variance | Possible reasons |
|---|---|
| Revenue favorable | Higher demand, successful promotion, price increase, new customers |
| Revenue adverse | Discounts, weak demand, a competitor's action, a poor forecast |
| Cost adverse | Supplier price rise, waste, overtime, inflation, extra volume |
| Cost favorable | Efficiency gain, supplier discount, lower volume, cuts in quality or maintenance (check these) |

Tie the reason to the case. Quote a figure from the stem.

## Budgets and decision-making

For each benefit, add one limit.

| Benefit | Limit |
|---|---|
| Planning and setting targets | Forecasts can be wrong |
| Coordination between departments | Takes time and can become rigid |
| Control of spending | May stop useful, unplanned spending |
| Accountability of centre managers | Managers may pad costs or understate sales |
| Variances direct attention to problems | A variance shows what happened, not why |
| Better later forecasts | Fixing past variances may lead to short-term thinking |

Conclusion template for "evaluate" or "justify" questions: budgets are most useful when assumptions are stated and realistic, variances are investigated rather than just reported, and managers take part in setting the targets. The guide's theory of knowledge prompt asks what assumptions underlie the techniques used when budgeting, which fits that point well.

## Quick self-test

1. State what a cost centre is.
2. State what a profit centre is.
3. Is a company's IT support team normally a cost centre or a profit centre? Give a reason.
4. A budget for materials is 8,000 dollars. Actual spending is 7,600 dollars. Calculate the variance, the percentage, and say whether it is favorable or adverse.
5. Budgeted revenue is 25,000 dollars. Actual revenue is 21,750 dollars. Calculate the variance, the percentage, and label it F or A.
6. Budgeted profit is 4,000 dollars. Actual profit is 5,200 dollars. Calculate the variance and percentage.
7. In the Kowhai table, which two lines show adverse variances?
8. Give one reason why a favorable cost variance might be bad news.
9. Name two benefits of a budget for decision-making.
10. Give one limitation of using variances to judge a manager.

### Answers

1. A part of a business where costs are identified and collected, but revenue is not attributed to it.
2. A part of a business where both costs and revenue are identified, so its profit can be measured.
3. A cost centre. It supports other parts of the business and does not earn revenue on its own.
4. 7,600 − 8,000 = −400, which is 400 below budget. The percentage is −400 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = −5.0%. It is a cost, so below budget is favorable (F).
5. 21,750 − 25,000 = −3,250. The percentage is −3,250 ÷ 25,000 × 100 = −13.0%. Revenue is below budget, so it is adverse (A).
6. 5,200 − 4,000 = +1,200, which is +30.0% of budget and favorable.
7. Wax and materials (+640), and wages (+280).
8. The saving may have come from cutting quality, delaying maintenance or buying fewer inputs than the sales needed, which could damage future sales.
9. Any two from: planning, control of spending, coordination, accountability, resource allocation, motivation, appraisal.
10. The manager may not control the cause, for example a supplier price rise or a market downturn, so the variance can be unfair as a measure of effort.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "favorable" for any positive number, even when the item is a cost.
- Calculating the percentage against the actual figure rather than the budget.
- Dropping the F or A label, or the sign of the variance.
- Defining a cost centre as a department that makes a loss. It is about recording costs only.
- Giving a list of benefits of budgeting with no link to the business in the case.
- Giving a reason for a variance that contradicts the data, such as "higher demand" for a revenue shortfall.
- Stopping an "evaluate" answer after the advantages, with no limits and no judgment.
- Forgetting to state assumptions or to total rows and columns twice.
