## Equations

| Equation | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Φ = BA | Flux through area A | B perpendicular to A |
| NΦ | Flux linkage of an N-turn coil | Same flux through every turn |
| NΦ = BAN cos θ | Rotated coil | θ between B and the normal to the coil |
| NΦ = BAN sin α | Same coil, angle α given to the plane | α = 90° − θ |
| NΦ = BAN cos ωt | Coil rotating at angular speed ω | ω = 2πf |

Units: 1 Wb = 1 T m² = 1 kg m² s⁻² A⁻¹. Flux linkage: Wb (often written Wb-turns).

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Magnetic flux Φ | Product of flux density and the area perpendicular to the field, Φ = BA |
| Weber | Flux through 1 m² perpendicular to a field of 1 T |
| Flux linkage | Number of turns × flux through each turn, NΦ |
| Normal to a coil | Line at right angles to the plane of the coil |
| Flux density | Flux per unit area, so 1 T = 1 Wb m⁻² |

## Condensed recall

- Face of the coil perpendicular to the field: NΦ = BAN, maximum.
- Plane of the coil along the field: NΦ = 0.
- Turned through 180°: NΦ = −BAN, so the change is 2BAN.
- Reversing the field also gives a change of 2BAN cos θ.
- Only the area where the field exists counts (for example the core of a solenoid).
- A coil moving inside a uniform field, without turning, keeps a constant flux linkage.
- Coil entering a field at steady speed: NΦ rises linearly; inside: constant; leaving: falls linearly.
- Rotating coil: NΦ follows a cosine curve between ±BAN.

## Changes to know

| Movement | Δ(NΦ) |
|---|---|
| 0° → 90° | BAN |
| 0° → 60° | 0.5 BAN |
| 0° → 180° | 2BAN |
| Removed from field | BAN cos θ |
| Field reversed | 2BAN cos θ |

## Rotating coil at key positions

| Position of coil | θ (field to normal) | NΦ | NΦ changing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face perpendicular to field | 0° | +BAN | momentarily not (graph at a peak) |
| Plane along field | 90° | 0 | fastest |
| Face perpendicular, turned over | 180° | −BAN | momentarily not |
| Plane along field again | 270° | 0 | fastest, in the opposite sense |

These positions fix the emf in section 3.10.4: zero emf at the peaks of NΦ, largest emf where NΦ crosses zero.

## Method box: flux linkage of a tilted coil

1. Convert the area to m² (cm² × 10⁻⁴).
2. Sketch the coil and its normal; mark the field.
3. Find θ, the angle between the field and the normal.
4. NΦ = BAN cos θ. If the angle to the plane is given, use sin instead.

## Worked reminders

- 0.080 m × 0.050 m coil, 120 turns, 0.25 T perpendicular: Φ = **1.0 × 10⁻³ Wb**, NΦ = **0.12 Wb**.
- 12 cm², 300 turns, 65 mT: NΦ = 0.065 × 12 × 10⁻⁴ × 300 = **0.023 Wb**.
- 80 turns, 0.040 m × 0.060 m, 0.30 T: BAN = **0.058 Wb**; at θ = 60°, **0.029 Wb**.

## Exam traps

- Using cos of the angle to the plane.
- Missing the sign change at 180°, so getting BAN instead of 2BAN.
- Giving flux when flux linkage is asked for (or the reverse).
- Using the area of the coil rather than the area of the field.
- Leaving cm² unconverted: an error of 10⁴.
- Describing a coil moving inside a uniform field as having changing flux linkage.

## Self-test

1. Define magnetic flux.
2. Express the weber in SI base units.
3. A coil's plane lies along a magnetic field. What is its flux linkage?
4. Find the flux linkage of a 250-turn coil of area 2.0 × 10⁻³ m² perpendicular to a 0.15 T field.
5. The field makes 25° with the normal to the coil in question 4. Find the flux linkage.
6. The field makes 25° with the plane of that coil instead. Find the flux linkage.
7. A coil with BAN = 0.060 Wb is turned through 180°. What is the change in flux linkage?
8. Why does a coil wound round a solenoid have flux linkage based on the solenoid's area?
9. A coil enters a field at steady speed. Describe the NΦ–t graph as it enters.
10. Write NΦ for a coil rotating at angular speed ω, starting with its normal along the field.
11. A coil rotates at 50 revolutions per second. How long after NΦ = BAN does NΦ first reach zero?
12. A 100-turn coil of area 2.0 × 10⁻³ m² in 0.50 T has NΦ = 0.050 Wb. Find the angle between the field and the normal.

### Answers

1. The product of the flux density and the area perpendicular to the field, Φ = BA.
2. **kg m² s⁻² A⁻¹**.
3. **Zero**.
4. 0.15 × 2.0 × 10⁻³ × 250 = **0.075 Wb**.
5. 0.075 × cos 25° = **0.068 Wb**.
6. 0.075 × sin 25° = **0.032 Wb**.
7. 2 × 0.060 = **0.12 Wb**.
8. The field is confined to the inside of the solenoid; outside it there is (almost) no field to pass through the rest of the coil.
9. A straight line rising from zero to BAN.
10. NΦ = BAN cos ωt.
11. A quarter of a period: (1/50) / 4 = **5.0 ms**.
12. BAN = 0.10 Wb, so cos θ = 0.50 and θ = **60°**.
