This guide covers subtopic **8.3 Group VII properties** in Topic 8, The Periodic Table, of Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028 series. The [Periodic Table groups and trends study guide](/resources/periodic-table-groups-and-trends/) gives the appearance table and one displacement equation. This page works through every displacement combination with observations and ionic equations, explains *why* displacement happens in the way examiners credit, and shows how to predict the properties of fluorine and astatine from given information. Then use the [Group VII revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-vii-properties-revision-notes/) and the [Group VII practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-vii-properties-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks for

This subtopic is **Core only**, so every candidate needs all of it.

- Describe the Group VII halogens, chlorine, bromine and iodine, as diatomic non-metals with general trends down the group, limited to (a) increasing density and (b) decreasing reactivity.
- State the appearance of the halogens at r.t.p.: chlorine, a pale yellow-green gas; bromine, a red-brown liquid; iodine, a grey-black solid.
- Describe and explain the displacement reactions of halogens with other halide ions.
- Predict the properties of other elements in Group VII, given information about the elements.

## The halogens as diatomic non-metals

Each halogen atom has **seven outer-shell electrons**. Two atoms share a pair of electrons in a single covalent bond, giving **diatomic** molecules: Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. Always write the formula of the element with the subscript 2.

As non-metals, the halogens are poor conductors of electricity, and they form **1− ions** (halide ions) by gaining one electron: chloride Cl⁻, bromide Br⁻, iodide I⁻. Note the naming: the *element* is chlor**ine**; the *ion* is chlor**ide**.

## Appearance and the two syllabus trends

| Halogen | State at r.t.p. | Appearance (syllabus wording) | Colour in aqueous solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | Gas | Pale yellow-green gas | Very pale yellow-green |
| Bromine, Br₂ | Liquid | Red-brown liquid | Orange (yellow-orange when dilute) |
| Iodine, I₂ | Solid | Grey-black solid | Brown |

Down the group:

- **Density increases.** Chlorine is a gas, bromine a dense liquid and iodine a solid.
- **Reactivity decreases.** Chlorine is the most reactive of the three; iodine the least.

The colours also darken down the group, and the change of state from gas to liquid to solid shows that melting and boiling points increase. These are not among the two syllabus trends, but they are often given as *information* in prediction questions.

## Displacement reactions

A **more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from a solution of its halide ions.** The halide solutions (potassium chloride, potassium bromide, potassium iodide) are colourless, so a colour change shows that a new halogen has formed.

| Halogen added ↓ / Halide solution → | Potassium chloride | Potassium bromide | Potassium iodide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine water | — | Reaction: turns orange (bromine) | Reaction: turns brown (iodine) |
| Bromine water | No reaction | — | Reaction: turns brown (iodine) |
| Iodine solution | No reaction | No reaction | — |

### Equations for the three reactions

```
Cl2(aq) + 2KBr(aq) → 2KCl(aq) + Br2(aq)
Cl2(aq) + 2I-(aq)  → 2Cl-(aq) + I2(aq)       (ionic form)
Br2(aq) + 2KI(aq)  → 2KBr(aq) + I2(aq)
```

The ionic form leaves out the potassium ions, which do not change. Chlorine with bromide ions as an ionic equation is: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂.

### Explaining displacement

A halogen reacts by **gaining** an electron to form a halide ion. A more reactive halogen has a stronger tendency to gain electrons than a less reactive one. So when chlorine meets bromide ions, chlorine takes electrons from the bromide ions: chlorine becomes chloride ions and bromide ions become bromine molecules.

When bromine is added to chloride ions, nothing happens, because bromine is less reactive than chlorine and cannot take electrons from chloride ions.

A complete "describe and explain" answer has three parts:

1. **What you see** (the colour change, or no change).
2. **What forms** (name the new halogen; give an equation if asked).
3. **Why** (the added halogen is more reactive than the halogen in the halide, so it displaces it).

These are redox reactions: the added halogen is reduced and the halide ion is oxidised. That terminology is Extended content in 6.4; for this Core subtopic, "displaces" and "more reactive" are enough.

### Worked example

*Aqueous chlorine is added to aqueous sodium iodide. Describe and explain what happens.*

> The colourless solution turns brown, because iodine forms. Chlorine is more reactive than iodine, so chlorine displaces iodine from the iodide: Cl₂ + 2NaI → 2NaCl + I₂.

## Deducing a reactivity order from results

Questions sometimes give displacement results for halogens labelled with letters and ask for an order of reactivity. Count how many halides each halogen displaces: the one that displaces the most is the most reactive.

| Halogen added | Halide of X | Halide of Y | Halide of Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | — | No change | No change |
| Y | Colour change | — | Colour change |
| Z | Colour change | No change | — |

Y displaces two halides, Z displaces one and X displaces none, so the order is **Y > Z > X** (most reactive first). If the halogens are real ones, the order must also match their positions in the group: the most reactive is highest up.

## Predicting fluorine and astatine

Fluorine is above chlorine; astatine is below iodine. Continue each trend:

| Property | Fluorine (above Cl) | Astatine (below I) |
|---|---|---|
| Reactivity | More reactive than chlorine (the most reactive halogen) | Less reactive than iodine |
| Density | Lower than chlorine | Higher than iodine |
| State at r.t.p. | Gas | Solid |
| Colour | Paler than chlorine (pale yellow) | Darker than iodine (black) |
| Formula of element | F₂ | At₂ |
| Ion | F⁻ | At⁻ |
| Displacement | Would displace Cl, Br and I from their halides | Would not displace any of Cl, Br or I from their halides |

When data are given, use them. For example, given melting points of chlorine −101 °C, bromine −7 °C and iodine 114 °C, predict astatine's melting point to be above 114 °C, so it is a solid.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing "chloride" for the element** or "chlorine" for the ion.
- **Writing Cl or Br** instead of Cl₂ or Br₂ in equations.
- **Saying iodine displaces bromine.** A less reactive halogen cannot displace a more reactive one.
- **Describing bromine as a red-brown gas.** It is a liquid at r.t.p.
- **Saying iodine is purple** at r.t.p. The syllabus wording is grey-black solid.
- **Giving the colour of potassium iodide solution as brown before reaction.** Halide solutions are colourless; the brown colour shows iodine has formed.
- **Saying reactivity increases** down Group VII. That is Group I.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe** the halogens: diatomic non-metals, density increases and reactivity decreases down the group.
- **State** appearance: use the exact syllabus colours and states.
- **Describe and explain** displacement: observation, product, reason based on relative reactivity.
- **Predict**: compare with the neighbouring halogen and continue the trend in the same direction.
