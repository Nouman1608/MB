These notes condense section 12.5, Identification of ions and gases, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All four outcomes are required for every candidate. For the reasoning behind each test and worked examples, use the [identification of ions and gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-identification-of-ions-and-gases/); then try the [identification of ions and gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-identification-of-ions-and-gases-practice/).

## The four outcomes in one line each

1. **Anions**: carbonate, chloride, bromide, iodide, nitrate, sulfate, sulfite.
2. **Aqueous cations** with sodium hydroxide and ammonia: Al³⁺, NH₄⁺, Ca²⁺, Cr³⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Zn²⁺.
3. **Gases**: NH₃, CO₂, Cl₂, H₂, O₂, SO₂.
4. **Flame tests**: Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Ba²⁺, Cu²⁺.

## Anions

| Anion | Reagents | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| CO₃²⁻ | dilute acid, then limewater | fizzing; limewater milky |
| Cl⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | white ppt. |
| Br⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | cream ppt. |
| I⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | yellow ppt. |
| SO₄²⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) | white ppt. |
| NO₃⁻ | NaOH(aq), then aluminium foil, warm | ammonia (damp red litmus blue) |
| SO₃²⁻ | acidified KMnO₄(aq) | purple → colourless |

Silver halide colours: **white, cream, yellow** as you go down Group VII from chlorine to iodine.

## Cations

| Ion | NaOH(aq) | NH₃(aq) |
|---|---|---|
| Al³⁺ | white ppt., dissolves in excess | white ppt., stays in excess |
| NH₄⁺ | NH₃ on warming | — |
| Ca²⁺ | white ppt., stays in excess | none or very slight white ppt. |
| Cr³⁺ | green ppt., dissolves in excess | green ppt., stays in excess |
| Cu²⁺ | light blue ppt., stays in excess | light blue ppt., dissolves → dark blue solution |
| Fe²⁺ | green ppt., stays; browns at surface | same |
| Fe³⁺ | red-brown ppt., stays | same |
| Zn²⁺ | white ppt., dissolves in excess | white ppt., dissolves in excess |

## Look-alike pairs

| Pair | Same result | Deciding test |
|---|---|---|
| Al³⁺ / Zn²⁺ | white ppt. dissolving in excess NaOH | excess NH₃: Zn dissolves, Al does not |
| Al³⁺ / Ca²⁺ | white ppt. with NaOH | excess NaOH: Al dissolves, Ca does not |
| Cr³⁺ / Fe²⁺ | green ppt. | excess NaOH: Cr dissolves, Fe²⁺ does not |
| Cl⁻ / SO₄²⁻ | white ppt. | different reagent: AgNO₃ for Cl⁻, Ba(NO₃)₂ for SO₄²⁻ |
| SO₂ gas / SO₃²⁻ ion | KMnO₄ decolourised | one is a gas tested directly; one is an ion in the sample |

## Gases

| Gas | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| NH₃ | damp red litmus | blue |
| CO₂ | limewater | milky |
| Cl₂ | damp litmus | bleached |
| H₂ | lighted splint | pop |
| O₂ | glowing splint | relights |
| SO₂ | acidified KMnO₄(aq) | purple → colourless |

## Flame tests

| Ion | Colour |
|---|---|
| Li⁺ | red |
| Na⁺ | yellow |
| K⁺ | lilac |
| Ca²⁺ | orange-red |
| Ba²⁺ | light green |
| Cu²⁺ | blue-green |

Clean the wire first: a trace of sodium gives yellow and can mask other colours.

## Why the reagents are chosen

- **Acidify** to remove carbonate, which would also give precipitates.
- **Nitric acid**, because HCl adds Cl⁻ and H₂SO₄ adds SO₄²⁻.
- **Warm with NaOH before adding aluminium**: ammonia at that stage means NH₄⁺, not NO₃⁻.
- **Add reagent dropwise, then in excess**: two observations, not one.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Precipitate (ppt.) | an insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed |
| Excess | more reagent than is needed to react completely |
| Effervescence | fizzing caused by a gas being given off |
| Acidify | add a little acid to make the solution acidic before a test |
| Anion | negatively charged ion |
| Cation | positively charged ion |
| Damp litmus | litmus moistened with water so a gas can dissolve and act on it |

## Exam traps

- "White solution" is never right: a precipitate is a solid.
- Iron(II) is **green**; iron(III) is **red-brown**.
- Copper(II) with ammonia: the **dark blue solution** only appears in excess.
- Litmus for ammonia and chlorine must be **damp**.
- Hydrogen: **lighted** splint. Oxygen: **glowing** splint.
- Do not name the gas as the observation for carbonate: write "effervescence", then the limewater result.
- Calcium gives an orange-red flame; do not confuse it with lithium (red).

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and result for a positive test for bromide ions.
2. Why is dilute nitric acid, not hydrochloric acid, used in the halide test?
3. Describe the test for nitrate ions.
4. A solution gives a white precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide that dissolves in excess. Name two possible ions.
5. How would you decide between the two ions in question 4?
6. Which cation gives a red-brown precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide?
7. Which cation gives a light blue precipitate that dissolves in excess aqueous ammonia, and what colour is the final solution?
8. Describe the test for chlorine.
9. A gas relights a glowing splint. Name it.
10. Give the flame colour for potassium and for barium.
11. A solid gives effervescence with dilute acid and the gas turns limewater milky. Which anion is present?
12. Describe the test for sulfite ions and its positive result.

## Answers

1. Acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous silver nitrate: cream precipitate.
2. Hydrochloric acid contains chloride ions, which would give a white precipitate whatever the sample contained.
3. Add aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil and warm carefully; ammonia is given off, turning damp red litmus blue.
4. Aluminium (Al³⁺) and zinc (Zn²⁺).
5. Add aqueous ammonia in excess: the zinc precipitate dissolves; the aluminium precipitate does not.
6. Iron(III), Fe³⁺.
7. Copper(II), Cu²⁺; dark blue.
8. Hold damp litmus paper in the gas: it is bleached.
9. Oxygen.
10. Potassium lilac; barium light green.
11. Carbonate, CO₃²⁻.
12. Add a small volume of acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII): it changes from purple to colourless.
