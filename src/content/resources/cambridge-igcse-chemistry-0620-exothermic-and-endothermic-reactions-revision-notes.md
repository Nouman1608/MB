Condensed recall for **5.1 Exothermic and endothermic reactions**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry
0620. For worked diagrams and calculations, use the
[Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-exothermic-and-endothermic-reactions/).

> **Tier note:** items marked *(Supplement)* are for Extended candidates only.

## Core statements

- **Exothermic:** transfers thermal energy **to** the surroundings → temperature of the
  surroundings **increases**.
- **Endothermic:** takes in thermal energy **from** the surroundings → temperature of the
  surroundings **decreases**.

## Supplement statements

- **Enthalpy change, ΔH:** the transfer of thermal energy during a reaction.
  **Negative** for exothermic; **positive** for endothermic.
- **Activation energy, Ea:** the **minimum energy that colliding particles must have to
  react**.
- **Bond breaking** is endothermic; **bond making** is exothermic.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Surroundings | Everything outside the reacting chemicals, e.g. the water, tube and thermometer |
| Thermal energy | Energy transferred as heat |
| Reaction pathway diagram | Graph of energy against progress of reaction |
| ΔH *(Supplement)* | Enthalpy change; units kJ/mol |
| Ea *(Supplement)* | Activation energy; always positive |
| Bond energy *(Supplement)* | Energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond |

## Pathway diagram rules

```
exothermic:  products BELOW reactants   ΔH arrow DOWN   ΔH negative
endothermic: products ABOVE reactants   ΔH arrow UP     ΔH positive
both:        one hump; Ea arrow from REACTANTS up to the PEAK
```

*(Supplement)* Label: axes (energy; progress of reaction), reactants, products, ΔH, Ea.

## Bond energy method (Supplement)

```
ΔH = bonds broken (reactants) − bonds made (products)
```

1. List every bond in every reactant and product molecule.
2. Multiply by the coefficients.
3. Total broken; total made.
4. Subtract **broken − made**; give sign and kJ/mol.

**Bonds to count carefully:** O=O (one double bond in O₂) · two C=O in CO₂ · two O–H in
H₂O · three N–H in NH₃ · N≡N in N₂ · four C–H in CH₄.

**Mini-example** (data given: N≡N 945, H–H 436, N–H 391):
N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Broken: 945 + 3 × 436 = 2253. Made: 6 × 391 = 2346.
ΔH = 2253 − 2346 = **−93 kJ/mol** (exothermic).

## Setting out a calculation

| Bonds broken | Energy / kJ/mol | Bonds made | Energy / kJ/mol |
|---|---|---|---|
| list × number | ... | list × number | ... |
| **Total** | **A** | **Total** | **B** |

ΔH = A − B. If A < B, ΔH is negative: exothermic. If A > B, ΔH is positive: endothermic.

## Describe versus explain

| Question | Core-level answer | Supplement-level answer |
|---|---|---|
| Why is neutralisation exothermic? | It transfers thermal energy to the surroundings; the temperature rises | More energy is released making new bonds than is needed to break the old ones |
| What does the hump show? | Energy needed to start the reaction | Ea: minimum energy colliding particles must have to react |

## Reading temperature data

- Change = final − initial. **Positive** change → exothermic; **negative** → endothermic.
- The bigger the change for the same amounts, the more energy transferred.
- Thermal decomposition needs **continuous** heating — a sign it is endothermic.

## Exam traps

- A **rise** in temperature means **exothermic**, not "heat taken in".
- Ea is measured from the **reactants**, not the products or the axis.
- ΔH arrow goes from **reactants to products**, not to the peak.
- **Broken − made**; reversing it flips the sign.
- Forgetting **coefficients**: 2H₂O has four O–H bonds.
- A ΔH answer without a **sign** or **units** loses marks.
- Ea does **not** tell you whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
- "Breaking bonds releases energy" is wrong — breaking always **needs** energy.
- Needing a spark or flame to **start** does not make a reaction endothermic: that energy is the activation energy, and burning fuels are exothermic.

## Self-test

1. State what happens to the temperature of the surroundings in an endothermic reaction.
2. Give two examples of exothermic reactions.
3. A thermometer reading falls from 20 °C to 12 °C during a reaction. Classify it.
4. On a pathway diagram, the products are below the reactants. What type of reaction is it?
5. *(Supplement)* Define activation energy.
6. *(Supplement)* What is the sign of ΔH for an endothermic reaction?
7. *(Supplement)* Is bond making exothermic or endothermic?
8. *(Supplement)* Where does the Ea arrow start and finish?
9. *(Supplement)* How many C=O bonds are broken when 2 mol of CO₂ react?
10. *(Supplement)* Bonds broken total 1200 kJ/mol; bonds made total 1500 kJ/mol. Find ΔH.
11. *(Supplement)* Explain why the reaction in question 10 is exothermic.
12. *(Supplement)* If a reaction has ΔH = −250 kJ/mol, what is ΔH for the reverse reaction?

**Answers**

1. It decreases.
2. Any two: combustion, neutralisation, displacement, respiration.
3. Endothermic (surroundings cooled by 8 °C).
4. Exothermic.
5. The minimum energy that colliding particles must have to react.
6. Positive.
7. Exothermic.
8. From the reactants' energy level to the top of the hump.
9. Four (2 × 2).
10. 1200 − 1500 = −300 kJ/mol.
11. More energy is released making bonds than is needed to break bonds.
12. +250 kJ/mol.
