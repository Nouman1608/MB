This guide covers section **7.3 Technology and sustainability** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The syllabus lists this as new content in the 2027-2029 edition. The broader [concepts, ethics, technology and sustainability guide](/resources/igcse-accounting-accounting-concepts-ethics-technology-and-sustainabilit/) introduces all of Topic 7; this page goes further into 7.3 alone, with a new business, a worked error example and an annotated answer to an "advise" question. All businesses and figures on this page are invented. Amounts are in dollars.

## What the syllabus asks for

Candidates should have an understanding of:

| Point | What it means in an answer |
|---|---|
| Digital applications for all or part of the accounting records | Every book kept digitally, or only some |
| The need to store accounting data safely and sustainably | Why records must be protected, and why storage should limit waste and energy |
| The risks of not storing data safely and sustainably | What can go wrong and who is affected |
| The different types of storage systems | Manual, data storage devices, cloud services, other digital services |
| Their advantages and disadvantages | Balanced comparison, applied to a business |

The syllabus adds that candidates do not need knowledge of, or access to, any specific accounting software package. Describe what a digital application does; never name a product.

## 1. Digital applications for all or part of the records

**Kestrel Stationers**, owned by Farah Qureshi, sells office supplies to about 150 credit customers. Farah keeps the sales journal and sales ledger on a spreadsheet but writes the cash book and purchases ledger by hand: a digital application for **part** of the records. Using one for **all** of them would put every book of prime entry, every ledger and the trial balance in one digital system.

What a digital application does that manual books do not:

- **Posts both sides of an entry from one input.** A sales invoice entered once updates the sales journal, the customer's account in the sales ledger and the sales account, which applies the duality concept without a second handwritten posting.
- **Calculates balances and totals automatically.** Running balances on customer accounts and the trial balance are produced on request, so arithmetic slips in casting and balancing are removed.
- **Produces reports and searches quickly.** A list of overdue customers can be produced in minutes, and one invoice found by number, date or customer.

It does not remove errors of principle, omission or original entry: it copies whatever it is given, everywhere at once.

### Worked example: one wrong input repeated

Farah's spreadsheet holds a price list. On 1 March a box of printer paper is entered at 2.50 instead of 25.00. During March, 120 boxes are sold on credit before the error is noticed.

| Step | Working | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Correct sales value | 120 x 25.00 | 3,000 |
| Value invoiced | 120 x 2.50 | 300 |
| Revenue understated | 3,000 - 300 | 2,700 |

Every one of the 120 invoices, every affected customer account and the sales total is wrong by the same mistake. The trial balance would still balance, because both sides were posted from the same wrong figure, so the error is one of original entry and is not revealed by it. The lesson for an answer: digital applications reduce arithmetic errors but can multiply input errors, so input must be checked.

## 2. Why accounting data must be stored safely

Records are not finished with when the year ends. Kestrel Stationers needs them:

- to prepare the financial statements and compare years;
- to chase unpaid customers and answer suppliers' queries;
- to support a loan application;
- to answer tax authority questions about income and expenses;
- as evidence in a dispute or a suspected fraud.

Storing data **safely** means the records stay **complete** (nothing missing), **accurate** (nothing altered without authority), **available** (they can be found and read when needed) and **confidential** (only authorised people can see them). The last links to confidentiality in section 7.2.

## 3. Why accounting data must be stored sustainably

**Sustainable** storage limits the environmental damage of keeping records while keeping them usable for as long as needed. The costs are paper, ink and filing space; electricity for computers, devices and the servers behind cloud services; and electronic waste when devices are replaced.

Sustainable practice: print only what is needed, avoid duplicate copies, send documents electronically, keep formats that stay readable, and wipe and recycle old devices.

## 4. The risks of not storing data safely and sustainably

| Risk | Example at Kestrel Stationers | Who is affected |
|---|---|---|
| Physical loss | A burst pipe soaks the handwritten cash book | Owner: the statements cannot be prepared |
| Device failure or deletion | The only spreadsheet file is corrupted | Owner and customers: amounts owed are unknown |
| Unauthorised access | A former employee still knows the password | Customers: private details exposed |
| Fraud or alteration | Someone changes a customer balance to hide a theft | Owner and lenders: figures are unreliable |
| Records cannot be produced | The tax authority asks for invoices from two years ago | Owner: possible penalties |
| Waste | Old computers dumped with records still on them | Society and the environment; customers' data at risk |

### Worked example: the cost of a back-up gap

Farah backs up the spreadsheet at the close of business every Friday. The computer fails at the close of business the following Thursday. The business records about 35 credit sales a day and trades Monday to Thursday in that week.

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Days not backed up | Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday | 4 days |
| Entries to re-create | 4 x 35 | 140 invoices |

A daily back-up would have limited the loss to one day, about 35 invoices. The example shows why the frequency of back-ups, not just their existence, matters.

## 5. The four types of storage system

**Manual** storage means paper books, files and documents kept in the business, such as a bound cash book and box files of invoices.

- Advantages: needs no electricity, internet or equipment; staff need little training; cannot be hacked remotely.
- Disadvantages: destroyed by fire or flood with no copy; bulky; slow to search, total and copy; uses paper and space.

**Data storage devices** are equipment the business holds, such as a computer's internal drive, an external hard drive, a USB flash drive or a memory card.

- Advantages: fast; large capacity for little cost; the business controls its own data; no internet needed to use it.
- Disadvantages: small devices are easily lost or stolen; devices fail; a device kept in the office is lost with the office; old devices become electronic waste.

**Cloud services** store data on a provider's servers, reached over the internet.

- Advantages: access from any place with an internet connection; data is held away from the premises, so a fire at the shop does not destroy it; the provider maintains the equipment; several staff can work on the same records.
- Disadvantages: needs a reliable internet connection; usually a continuing charge; the business depends on a third party, whose service may fail or close; data held elsewhere must be protected by passwords and controlled access.

**Other digital services** are digital systems that create or hold records as a by-product of another service, such as online banking statements, invoices sent and received by email, and records kept by an online payment service.

- Advantages: records are created automatically, without retyping; less paper; useful independent evidence, such as a bank statement.
- Disadvantages: the records are spread across many systems; each service may keep them only for a limited time; the business does not control the format or availability.

Most businesses combine types. A combination survives more events than any single method, because one copy can be lost without losing the record.

## 6. Annotated model answer

**Question:** *Advise Farah whether Kestrel Stationers should move all its records to a cloud service. Justify your answer.*

**Answer:** Moving to a cloud service would let Farah reach the records from home and from the shop, and because the data is held away from the premises, a fire or flood at the shop would no longer destroy the records, as it could the handwritten cash book now. *(Advantage, applied to the business.)* However, she would pay a continuing charge, the shop would depend on its internet connection to record sales, and if the provider failed she could lose access to customer balances. *(Disadvantages, applied.)* With 150 credit customers, losing the sales ledger would be serious, so off-site safety matters more than the charge. I advise her to move the records to a cloud service but also keep a regular back-up on a data storage device in the shop, so that she can still work if the internet connection fails. *(Decision, with a reason and a safeguard.)*

Why it works: "advise" asks for a course of action, so the answer ends with a recommendation, after both sides are applied to the business.

## Common mistakes

- Naming a software product, which the syllabus does not require and examiners do not reward.
- Saying a digital system "has no errors". It removes arithmetic errors but repeats input errors.
- Treating "sustainably" as meaning "safely". Safe storage protects the data; sustainable storage limits waste and energy use.
- Writing "the cloud is unsafe" without a reason: name the risk, such as dependence on a provider.
- Giving only advantages when the question says "discuss" or "evaluate".

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State / Identify:** one precise point, such as "cloud services".
- **Explain:** the point plus why or how, for example "a USB drive is easily lost because it is small and portable, so the only copy of the records could disappear".
- **Discuss:** advantages and disadvantages, in depth and structured.
- **Evaluate:** judge the value of something; weigh both sides and reach a conclusion.
- **Advise:** a suggested course of action for the business in the question, supported by reasons.
- **Suggest:** apply knowledge to the situation; several sensible answers can earn credit.

Use the marks for each question as a guide to how many developed points to give.
