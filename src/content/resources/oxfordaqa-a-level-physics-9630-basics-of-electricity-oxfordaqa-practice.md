Take e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Treat meters as ideal.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define electric current and potential difference. **[2]**

**2.** A steady current of 0.45 A flows through a lamp for 3.5 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the charge that passes through the lamp. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of electrons that pass through it. **[1]**

**3.** A sensor has a pd of 2.4 V across it and carries a current of 160 μA. Calculate its resistance in kΩ. **[2]**

**4.** A 12 V motor draws 0.80 A for 15 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred by the motor. **[2]**

**5.** Use V = W/Q to show that the volt is equivalent to kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why an ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** The current in a component is 0.30 A for the first 2.0 s. It then falls steadily to 0.10 A over the next 4.0 s and stays at 0.10 A for a further 4.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the total charge that flows in the 10 s. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the average current over the 10 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of electrons that flow in the 10 s. **[1]**

**8.** A data logger records the total charge that has passed through a component.

| t / s | 0 | 2.0 | 4.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q / mC | 0 | 24 | 48 | 66 | 78 |

**(a)** Calculate the current during the first 4.0 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the current changes after 4.0 s, using values from the table. **[2]**

**(c)** The pd across the component is 5.0 V throughout. Calculate the energy transferred in the first 4.0 s. **[1]**

**9.** An electron gun produces a beam current of 0.25 mA. Each electron is accelerated through a pd of 4.5 kV.

**(a)** Calculate the number of electrons leaving the gun per second. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy gained by each electron. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the total energy transferred to the beam in one minute. **[2]**

**10.** In an electrolyte, positive ions with charge +2e move in one direction and negative ions with charge −e move in the opposite direction. In 10 s, 3.0 × 10¹⁸ positive ions and 6.0 × 10¹⁸ negative ions cross a plane in the liquid.

**(a)** Explain why the motion of both kinds of ion adds to the current in the same direction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the current. **[2]**

**11.** An electric heater connected to a 230 V supply transfers 1.2 MJ of energy in 25 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the charge that passes through the heater. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the current. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the resistance of the heater. **[1]**

**12.** A student says: "The resistance of a filament lamp is the gradient of its I-V graph, and current is the charge that flows through a circuit."

**(a)** Correct the statement about resistance. **[2]**

**(b)** Correct the statement about current. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Current is the rate of flow of charge (I = ΔQ/Δt) [1]; pd is the work done (energy transferred) per unit charge between two points (V = W/Q) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "Flow of charge" without "rate" does not earn the current mark.

**2. (a)** Q = It = 0.45 × 210 = 94.5 C [1]

**(b)** N = 94.5 / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 5.9 × 10²⁰ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 3.5 minutes is 210 s, not 350 s.

**3.** R = V/I = 2.4 / 160 × 10⁻⁶ [1] = 1.5 × 10⁴ Ω = 15 kΩ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The micro prefix must become 10⁻⁶ before dividing.

**4.** W = VIt = 12 × 0.80 × 900 [1] = 8.6 × 10³ J [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* This combines W = QV with Q = It in a single step.

**5.** V = W/Q, and the joule is kg m² s⁻² while the coulomb is A s [1]; so V = kg m² s⁻² / (A s) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* State the base units of both the joule and the coulomb before dividing.

**6.** So that it takes no current from the circuit and does not change the pd it is measuring [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer about the voltmeter "not affecting the circuit" needs the reason: no current passes through it.

**7. (a)** First 2.0 s: 0.30 × 2.0 = 0.60 C [1]; next 4.0 s: ½ × (0.30 + 0.10) × 4.0 = 0.80 C [1]; last 4.0 s: 0.10 × 4.0 = 0.40 C, total 1.8 C [1]

**(b)** 1.8 / 10 = 0.18 A [1]

**(c)** N = 1.8 / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.1 × 10¹⁹ [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The charge is the area under the current-time graph; the falling section is a trapezium.

**8. (a)** I = ΔQ/Δt = 48 × 10⁻³ / 4.0 [1] = 0.012 A (12 mA) [1]

**(b)** The current decreases [1]: from 4.0 to 6.0 s it is 18 mC / 2.0 s = 9 mA, and from 6.0 to 8.0 s it is 12 mC / 2.0 s = 6 mA [1]

**(c)** W = QV = 48 × 10⁻³ × 5.0 = 0.24 J [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The current is the gradient of the charge-time data; equal charge increases in equal times mean a constant current.

**9. (a)** N per second = I/e [1] = 0.25 × 10⁻³ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.6 × 10¹⁵ s⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** W = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 4500 = 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1]

**(c)** W = VIt = 4500 × 0.25 × 10⁻³ × 60 [1] = 68 J (67.5 J) [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The answer to (c) can also be found as (a) × (b) × 60; both methods should agree.

**10. (a)** Positive charge moving one way and negative charge moving the opposite way both transfer positive charge in the same direction, so their currents add [1]

**(b)** Charge = (3.0 × 10¹⁸ × 2 + 6.0 × 10¹⁸ × 1) × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.92 C [1]; I = 1.92 / 10 = 0.19 A [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Subtracting the two contributions is the common error; the currents add.

**11. (a)** Q = W/V = 1.2 × 10⁶ / 230 [1] = 5.2 × 10³ C [1]

**(b)** I = Q/t = 5217 / 1500 = 3.5 A [1]

**(c)** R = V/I = 230 / 3.48 = 66 Ω [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values from one part to the next to avoid rounding drift.

**12. (a)** Resistance is the ratio V/I at a particular point on the graph [1]; for a curved graph this is not the gradient (or its reciprocal), so the coordinates of the point must be used [1]

**(b)** Current is the rate of flow of charge, the charge passing a point per unit time [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A correction must state the right idea, not just say the original is wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving minutes or hours unconverted.
- Missing the "rate" in the definition of current.
- Treating resistance as a gradient.
- Ignoring prefixes such as μ, m and k.
- Subtracting the currents of oppositely moving charges of opposite sign.
- Using a triangle instead of a trapezium for a current that does not fall to zero.
