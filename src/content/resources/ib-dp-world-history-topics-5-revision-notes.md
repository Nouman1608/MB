Early Modern states (1450-1789) is world history topic 5 for Paper 2. Revise at least two states from different regions under four themes: emergence and expansion, legitimacy, methods of government, and challenges and limits.

Related: [Early Modern states study guide](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-5/) and [Early Modern states practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-5-practice/).

## State profiles

**Russia under Peter the Great (1682-1725): Europe**
- Legitimacy: Romanov dynasty; tsar as autocrat; title of Emperor (1721).
- Methods: conscript army and new navy; Senate (1711); colleges; Table of Ranks (1722); poll tax; Holy Synod (1721).
- Expansion: Great Northern War (1700-1721); Poltava (1709); Nystad (1721).
- Limits: dependence on serfdom; resistance from traditionalists; uncertain succession after Alexei's death (1718).

**Ottoman empire under Suleiman (1520-1566): Europe and the Middle East**
- Legitimacy: Ottoman dynasty; sultan as protector of Islam; conquest.
- Methods: *devshirme* and Janissaries; *timar* cavalry; grand vizier and imperial council; legal codes (*kanun*).
- Expansion: Belgrade (1521), Rhodes (1522), Mohács (1526), siege of Vienna (1529), Baghdad (1534).
- Limits: distance and supply; later Janissary power; dependence on continued conquest for revenue and reward.

**Mughal empire under Akbar (1556-1605): Asia and Oceania**
- Legitimacy: Timurid descent; Islamic and Persian kingship; a court ideology placing the emperor above religious divisions.
- Methods: *mansabdari* ranks; rotated *jagirs*; measured land revenue (Todar Mal); Rajput alliances.
- Religion: abolished the *jizya* (1564); debates in the house of worship at Fatehpur Sikri; *sulh-i kul*.
- Limits: the system depended on expansion and on the emperor's personal authority.

**Stuart England (1603-1689): Europe**
- Legitimacy: hereditary monarchy; claims to divine right under James I and Charles I.
- Conflict: disputes over taxation and religion; civil war (1642-1651); execution of Charles I (1649).
- Outcome: Restoration (1660); Glorious Revolution (1688); Bill of Rights (1689).
- Significance: the clearest example of limits on a ruler, through Parliament.

**Qing China under Kangxi (1661-1722): Asia and Oceania**
- Legitimacy: Manchu conquest dynasty presenting itself as holder of the Mandate of Heaven; patron of Confucian learning.
- Methods: suppressed the Revolt of the Three Feudatories (1673-1681); took Taiwan (1683); Treaty of Nerchinsk with Russia (1689).
- Limits: tension between Manchu and Han elites; scale of the empire.

## Methods of government compared

| Method | Russia | Ottoman empire | Mughal empire |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elite control | Table of Ranks; compulsory service | *Devshirme* elite loyal to the sultan | *Mansabdari* ranks; rotated *jagirs* |
| Army | Conscript standing army | Janissaries and *timar* cavalry | Cavalry provided by *mansabdars* |
| Revenue | Poll tax on male peasants | Taxes from *timars*, trade and tribute | Land revenue on measured land |
| Religion | Church under the Holy Synod | Sultan as protector of Islam; religious communities under their own leaders | Toleration and *sulh-i kul* |
| Capital | St Petersburg (1703) | Istanbul | Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Lahore |

## Challenges to Early Modern rulers

| Challenge | Example |
|---|---|
| Noble revolt | The Fronde in France (1648-1653) |
| Representative assemblies | English Parliament against Charles I and James II |
| Military revolt | The *streltsy* revolt against Peter (1698) |
| Regional power | Revolt of the Three Feudatories against Kangxi |
| Religious opposition | Old Believers in Russia; Huguenots in France |
| Financial crisis | French debts by the 1780s, leading to the calling of the Estates-General (1789) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolutism | Claim to sovereign power not shared with any assembly |
| Divine right | Belief that a king's authority came directly from God |
| Table of Ranks | Peter's 1722 hierarchy of service ranks |
| Holy Synod | State-controlled council replacing the Russian patriarchate |
| *Devshirme* | Ottoman levy of Christian boys trained for military and palace service |
| *Timar* | Ottoman revenue grant in return for cavalry service |
| *Mansabdari* | Mughal system of ranks determining status, pay and troops |
| *Jagir* | Mughal revenue assignment granted to a noble |
| Bill of Rights | 1689 English act limiting royal power |
| Enlightened absolutism | Rule by absolute monarchs influenced by Enlightenment ideas, such as Frederick II |

## Judgement lines for conclusions

- "Absolute monarchy worked through bargains with elites, not in spite of them."
- "Service ranks bound nobles to the state, but only while the ruler could reward them."
- "Limits on rulers came from money as often as from assemblies."

## Exam traps

- **"Absolute" means unlimited.** Show the bargains and limits.
- **One example for everything.** Prepare two or three states from different regions.
- **Reform lists.** Judge which reforms worked, why, and for how long.
- **Wrong region.** The Ottoman empire spans Europe and the Middle East; if a question requires two regions, pair it with a clearly different one, such as Mughal India.
- **Dates.** The topic runs to 1789; avoid the French Revolution itself.

## Self-test

1. What did the Table of Ranks link status to?
   *Service in the army, navy or civil administration.*
2. Which battle of 1709 marked Peter's victory over Sweden?
   *Poltava.*
3. What replaced the Russian patriarchate in 1721?
   *The Holy Synod.*
4. What was the *devshirme*?
   *The Ottoman levy of Christian boys, converted and trained as Janissaries or officials.*
5. Why was Suleiman called *Kanuni*?
   *For his legal codes; the name means "the Lawgiver".*
6. What determined a Mughal noble's place in the *mansabdari* system?
   *His rank, which set his status, pay and the cavalry he had to maintain.*
7. Why were *jagirs* rotated?
   *To stop nobles building local power bases.*
8. Name one way Akbar bound Hindu elites to Mughal rule.
   *Rajput alliances and marriages, or abolishing the jizya.*
9. What did the Bill of Rights (1689) require?
   *Parliamentary consent for taxation and for a standing army in peacetime.*
10. Which revolt did Kangxi suppress between 1673 and 1681?
    *The Revolt of the Three Feudatories.*
11. What was the Fronde?
    *A series of revolts against the French crown, involving nobles and the Paris parlement, in 1648-1653.*
12. Give one limit on Peter the Great's reforms.
    *They depended on serfdom and coercion, or the succession was left uncertain.*
