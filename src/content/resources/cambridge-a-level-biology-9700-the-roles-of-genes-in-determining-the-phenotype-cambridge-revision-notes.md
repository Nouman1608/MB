These notes condense section **16.2 The roles of genes in determining the phenotype** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, read the [16.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-roles-of-genes-in-determining-the-phenotype-cambridge/); for exam-style questions, use the [16.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-roles-of-genes-in-determining-the-phenotype-cambridge-practice/).

## Key terms (outcome 16.2.1)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gene | A length of DNA that codes for a polypeptide (or protein) |
| Locus | The position of a gene on a chromosome |
| Allele | An alternative form of a gene |
| Dominant | An allele expressed in the phenotype whenever it is present |
| Recessive | An allele expressed only when no dominant allele is present (homozygous) |
| Codominant | Both alleles expressed in the heterozygote |
| Linkage | Genes on the same chromosome, tending to be inherited together |
| Test cross | Cross with a homozygous recessive individual to reveal an unknown genotype |
| F1 / F2 | First filial generation / offspring of crossing F1 individuals |
| Genotype | The alleles an organism has |
| Phenotype | The observable characteristics, resulting from genotype and environment |
| Homozygous / heterozygous | Two identical alleles / two different alleles at a locus |

## Core recall

### Six-line genetic diagram

Key → parental phenotypes → parental genotypes → gametes → offspring genotypes (Punnett square) → offspring phenotypes and ratio.

### Expected ratios to recognise

| Cross | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Aa × Aa (dominance) | 3 : 1 |
| Aa × aa (test cross) | 1 : 1 |
| Codominant heterozygotes crossed | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| AaBb × AaBb (unlinked) | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 |
| AaBb × aabb (unlinked test cross) | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 |
| Linked test cross | Two large parental classes, two small recombinant classes |
| Epistasis | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 merged into fewer classes (work it out; ratios need not be recalled) |

### Sex linkage

- Genes on the non-homologous part of the X chromosome.
- Write as Xᴴ, Xʰ; Y carries no allele.
- Males have one allele, so a recessive allele always shows.
- A father passes his X allele to all daughters and none to sons.

### Multiple alleles

More than two alleles exist at one locus in the population, but each individual has only two. Example: the ABO blood group gene with Iᴬ, Iᴮ and Iᴼ (Iᴬ and Iᴮ codominant, Iᴼ recessive).

### Autosomal linkage

- Linked genes are on the same autosome; alleles on one chromosome tend to stay together.
- Recombinants form only by crossing over between the loci.
- Recombinant percentage = recombinants ÷ total offspring × 100.

### Epistasis

One gene affects the expression of another gene at a different locus. Typical cause: both genes code for enzymes in the same pathway, so a non-functional early enzyme hides the effect of later genes.

### Chi-squared

```
χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E        degrees of freedom = number of classes − 1
```

| Degrees of freedom | Critical value at p = 0.05 |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3.84 |
| 2 | 5.99 |
| 3 | 7.82 |

- χ² below the critical value → difference not significant → results fit the expected ratio.
- χ² above → significant → another explanation (such as linkage) is needed.
- Critical values will be given in the exam.

### Genes, proteins and phenotype

| Gene | Protein | Condition | Inheritance |
|---|---|---|---|
| TYR | Tyrosinase | Albinism | Autosomal recessive |
| HBB | β-globin of haemoglobin | Sickle cell anaemia | Base substitution; glutamic acid → valine |
| F8 | Factor VIII | Haemophilia | X-linked recessive |
| HTT | Huntingtin | Huntington's disease | Dominant; expanded CAG repeat |

### Le/le and gibberellin

- Le: functional enzyme in gibberellin synthesis → active gibberellin → stem elongation → tall.
- le: non-functional enzyme → little active gibberellin → dwarf (lele).

## Exam traps

- Always give a **key** for symbols.
- Each gamete carries **one** allele of each gene.
- Sex-linked alleles must be shown **on the X**.
- Degrees of freedom = classes − 1, not offspring − 1.
- "Not significant" means the data **fit** the expected ratio, not that the experiment failed.
- Codominant alleles use superscripts, not capital and lower-case letters.
- In the gene–protein–phenotype chain, name the protein and what it does.
- Females can be carriers of X-linked recessive conditions; males cannot.

## Self-test

1. Define locus.
2. Distinguish between genotype and phenotype.
3. What ratio is expected from Aa × aa?
4. Why are sons of a carrier mother and unaffected father at risk of haemophilia, but not daughters?
5. Which gametes does AaBb produce if the genes are unlinked?
6. A linked test cross gives 180 parental and 20 recombinant offspring. Calculate the recombinant percentage.
7. Give a definition of epistasis.
8. Calculate the degrees of freedom for a chi-squared test with three phenotype classes.
9. χ² = 2.10 with 1 degree of freedom. Is the difference significant at p = 0.05?
10. Which protein is non-functional in albinism?
11. What amino acid change occurs in sickle cell haemoglobin?
12. Explain why lele pea plants are dwarf.

### Answers

1. The position of a gene on a chromosome.
2. Genotype: the alleles an organism has. Phenotype: its observable characteristics.
3. 1 : 1.
4. Sons receive their only X from the mother, which may carry Xʰ; daughters also receive Xᴴ from the father, which is dominant.
5. AB, Ab, aB and ab, in equal proportions.
6. 20 ÷ 200 × 100 = **10 %**.
7. The interaction of two genes at different loci, where one gene affects the expression of the other.
8. 3 − 1 = **2**.
9. No: 2.10 is less than 3.84, so it is not significant.
10. Tyrosinase.
11. Glutamic acid is replaced by valine.
12. The le allele codes for a non-functional enzyme in gibberellin synthesis, so little active gibberellin is made and stems do not elongate.
