Condensed notes on sub-topic **2(b) Mains electricity** (statements 2.2 to

## Equations

```
P = I × V                  (know and use; also I = P / V for fuses)
E = I × V × t              (use; t in seconds)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| live wire | carries the alternating mains voltage |
| neutral wire | completes the circuit; close to 0 V |
| earth wire | connects a metal case to the ground; no current in normal use |
| insulation | plastic covering on wires preventing contact with conductors |
| double insulation | insulating case as well as insulated wires; no earth wire needed |
| fuse | thin wire in the live side that melts if current exceeds its rating |
| fuse rating | the current above which the fuse melts |
| circuit breaker | automatic switch in the live side that trips on excess current; resettable |
| a.c. | alternating current: repeatedly reverses direction (mains) |
| d.c. | direct current: one direction only (cells, batteries) |

## Safety features: fault, then result

- **Earthed metal case, live wire touches case:** large current flows
  through the low-resistance earth wire → fuse melts or breaker trips →
  live disconnected → case not live, user safe.
- **Double insulation:** no touchable metal can become live, so no
  earth wire is needed.
- **Fuse:** current above rating → wire heats and melts → circuit broken →
  cable does not overheat (fire risk reduced).
- **Circuit breaker:** current above set value → switch opens → reset
  after the fault is fixed; faster than a fuse.
- **Why the live side?** Breaking the live connection isolates the
  appliance from the high voltage.

## Electrical heating

Electrons collide with the ions in the resistor's metal lattice and
transfer energy → ions vibrate more → temperature rises. Uses: kettle,
toaster, iron, electric heater, immersion heater, hairdryer, shower. In
cables it is wasted energy, so cables have low resistance.

## Fuse selection steps

1. Convert power to watts.
2. I = P ÷ V.
3. Pick the smallest available rating above I.

| Appliance (230 V) | Current | Fuse (3 A, 5 A or 13 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 40 W lamp | 0.17 A | 3 A |
| 700 W blender | 3.0 A | 5 A |
| 1.0 kW microwave oven | 4.3 A | 5 A |
| 2.3 kW kettle | 10 A | 13 A |

## a.c. versus d.c.

| | a.c. | d.c. |
|---|---|---|
| direction | reverses repeatedly | one direction only |
| source | mains supply | cell, battery |
| voltage−time graph | wave crossing zero | horizontal line |

## Exam traps

- A fuse rated equal to the normal current blows in normal use; rated far
  above it, it does not protect the cable.
- The earth wire does not make an appliance safe on its own: the large
  fault current must then blow the fuse.
- A double-insulated appliance has no earth wire; do not say it is
  "earthed".
- Electrons do not "rub" on the wire: they collide with ions.
- E = I × V × t needs time in seconds, not minutes or hours.
- "Mains is d.c." is wrong; mains is a.c.

## Self-test

1. Name the wire that is connected to the metal case of an appliance.
2. Explain why a fuse is placed in the live wire rather than the neutral.
3. State one advantage of a circuit breaker over a fuse.
4. Why does a double-insulated appliance not need an earth wire?
5. Describe what happens when the live wire touches the earthed metal
   case of a toaster.
6. Explain, in terms of electrons, why a heating element gets hot.
7. State the difference between a.c. and d.c.
8. Calculate the current in a 920 W appliance on 230 V.
9. Choose a fuse (3 A, 5 A or 13 A) for a 2.0 kW heater on 230 V.
10. A 230 V television takes 0.50 A for 3.0 hours. Calculate the energy
    transferred.
11. Why are mains cables made of copper with a low resistance?
12. A 400 W food mixer on 230 V is fitted with a 13 A fuse. Explain why
    this is not the best choice from 3 A, 5 A and 13 A.

## Answers

1. The earth wire.
2. When the fuse melts it disconnects the appliance from the high voltage
   of the live wire; in the neutral, the appliance would stay connected
   to the live wire.
3. Any one: it can be reset; it acts faster.
4. Its case is made of an insulator, so no metal part that can be touched
   could become live.
5. A large current flows from the live wire through the case and earth
   wire to the ground; the fuse melts (or breaker trips), disconnecting the
   supply.
6. Electrons collide with the ions in the metal, transferring energy, so
   the ions vibrate more and the temperature rises.
7. a.c. repeatedly reverses direction; d.c. flows in one direction only.
8. I = P ÷ V = 920 ÷ 230 = 4.0 A.
9. I = 2000 ÷ 230 = 8.7 A; choose 13 A.
10. E = I × V × t = 0.50 × 230 × 10 800 = 1 242 000 J ≈ 1.2 × 10⁶ J.
11. Low resistance means little energy is transferred as heat in the
    cable, so less energy is wasted and the cable does not overheat.
12. I = 400 ÷ 230 = 1.7 A, so a 3 A fuse is the right choice. A 13 A fuse
    would let a fault current of up to 13 A flow without melting, so the
    mixer's thin cable could overheat.
