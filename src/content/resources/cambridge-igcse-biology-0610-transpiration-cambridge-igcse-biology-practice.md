These questions cover sub-topic **8.3 Transpiration** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 3 are Core. Questions and parts on statements 4 to 7 (stomata and air spaces, the transpiration pull, humidity and wilting) are labelled **(Extended)**. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [transpiration study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-transpiration-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [transpiration revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-transpiration-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Describe transpiration. **[1]**

**2.** Which sequence describes how water leaves a leaf?

A: diffuses from mesophyll cells into air spaces, then evaporates through stomata

B: evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces into air spaces, then diffuses out through stomata

C: evaporates through the cuticle, then diffuses into the xylem

D: diffuses out of the xylem through the stomata as liquid water **[1]**

**3.** State the effect on transpiration rate of increasing the temperature. **[1]**

**4.** A potometer measures water uptake. Explain why this is only an estimate of the rate of transpiration. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the effect of increasing humidity on the rate of transpiration. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the force that holds water molecules together as a column in the xylem. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student uses a potometer to investigate the effect of wind speed. She places a fan at different distances from a leafy shoot and records how far the air bubble moves in 4 minutes.

| Distance of fan / cm | Distance moved by bubble in 4 min / mm |
|---|---|
| no fan | 12 |
| 100 | 20 |
| 50 | 32 |
| 25 | 46 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of water uptake with the fan at 25 cm, in mm per min.

**(b)** Describe the effect of wind speed on the rate of water uptake. Use data from the table.

**(c)** State **two** variables she should keep the same.

**(d)** Describe **two** precautions she should take when setting up the potometer. **[7]**

**8.** Water vapour leaves a leaf through the stomata. Describe where the water evaporates and how the vapour leaves the leaf. **[3]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Four leaves were treated with petroleum jelly and hung up for two days.

| Leaf | Surfaces covered | Start mass / g | Mass after 2 days / g |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | none | 3.20 | 2.40 |
| Q | upper | 3.00 | 2.31 |
| R | lower | 3.10 | 2.98 |
| S | both | 3.05 | 3.02 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage loss in mass for leaf R.

**(b)** Explain why the results are compared as percentages.

**(c)** Explain the difference between the results for leaves Q and R.

**(d)** Suggest why leaf S still lost a little mass. **[7]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Explain how water moves from the roots to the top of a tall plant. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A gardener notices that her bean plants transpire much faster on a hot, dry, windy afternoon than on a cool, humid, still evening. Explain this difference. Refer to all three factors. **[6]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A potted plant is left in hot sunshine with dry soil. By midday its leaves droop. In the evening, after watering, the leaves are upright again.

**(a)** Explain why the plant wilted.

**(b)** Describe how the cells of the leaves changed to cause the drooping.

**(c)** Explain why the leaves recovered. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The loss of water vapour from leaves [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** The rate increases [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Some of the water taken up is used by the plant cells, for example in photosynthesis [1], so uptake is not exactly equal to the water lost by transpiration [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** *(Extended)* The rate decreases [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**6.** *(Extended)* Forces of attraction between water molecules [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**7. (a)** 46 ÷ 4 = **11.5 mm per min** [1].

**(b)** As wind speed increases (fan closer), the rate of uptake increases [1]; for example from 12 mm in 4 min with no fan to 46 mm in 4 min with the fan at 25 cm [1].

**(c)** Any two from: same shoot; temperature; humidity; light intensity; time of each reading [2].

**(d)** Any two from: cut the shoot under water; assemble the potometer under water; seal the joints with petroleum jelly so they are airtight; dry the leaves before starting; let the shoot settle before recording [2]. Total: 7 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In (b) one mark is for the trend and one for quoting figures with units; a trend alone scores only one mark.

**8.** Water evaporates from the surfaces of the mesophyll cells [1] into the air spaces [1], and the water vapour then diffuses out of the leaf through the stomata [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**9.** *(Extended)* **(a)** 3.10 − 2.98 = 0.12 g [1]; 0.12 ÷ 3.10 × 100 = **3.9%** [1].

**(b)** The leaves had different starting masses, so percentages allow a fair comparison [1].

**(c)** Leaf Q (upper covered) lost 23% of its mass but leaf R (lower covered) lost only 3.9% [1]; most stomata are in the lower epidermis [1], so covering the lower surface blocked most of the routes for water vapour to diffuse out [1].

**(d)** The petroleum jelly may not have completely sealed every stoma / a little water may be lost through the cuticle or the cut leaf stalk [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**10.** *(Extended)* Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and diffuses out through the stomata [1]. This creates a transpiration pull on the water in the xylem [1]. Water molecules are held together by forces of attraction between them [1], so a continuous column of water is drawn up the xylem from the roots [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**11.** *(Extended)* Higher temperature in the afternoon gives water molecules more kinetic energy [1], so evaporation from the mesophyll surfaces is faster [1]. Low humidity means less water vapour in the air outside [1], so the concentration gradient is steeper and diffusion out is faster [1]. Wind removes water vapour from around the leaf [1], keeping the concentration gradient steep [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**12.** *(Extended)* **(a)** The plant lost water by transpiration faster than it could take water up [1], because the soil was dry and the hot conditions increased transpiration [1].

**(b)** The cells lost water [1] and became flaccid instead of turgid, so they no longer supported the leaves [1].

**(c)** Watering allowed water uptake to increase, and transpiration slowed in the cooler evening [1], so the cells took in water and became turgid again [1]. Total: 6 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying water "evaporates through the stomata".
- Treating potometer uptake as exactly equal to water loss.
- Describing a trend without quoting data and units.
- Using mass loss instead of percentage loss when start masses differ.
- (Extended) Saying the roots push water up the stem.
- (Extended) Explaining wind as "blowing water off the leaf".
- (Extended) Describing wilting without the words flaccid or turgid.
