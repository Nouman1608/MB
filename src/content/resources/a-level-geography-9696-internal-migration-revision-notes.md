Condensed recall for **5.2 Internal migration (within a country)**. For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal migration | Movement within one country |
| Rural-urban migration | Countryside to town or city |
| Urban-rural migration | City to countryside (counterurbanisation) |
| Stepped migration | Moving up the settlement hierarchy in stages, such as village, town, city |
| Urban-urban migration | Between towns and cities |
| Intra-urban movement | Within a city, for example centre to suburbs |
| Remittances | Money sent home by migrants |

## Rural-urban migration: causes

- **Push:** shortage of jobs, low farm income, land shortage, mechanisation, poor services, drought or conflict.
- **Pull:** jobs and higher wages, education and health care, perceived opportunities, links with earlier migrants.
- Most common in LICs and MICs, where urban growth is rapid.

## Impacts

| Area | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Source (rural) | Remittances, less pressure on land | Loss of young adults, ageing population, labour shortages, closing services |
| Destination (urban) | Labour supply, economic growth, diversity | Pressure on housing, jobs, water and transport; informal settlements; pollution |

## Urban-rural and other flows

Counterurbanisation: people leave large cities for quality of life, lower housing costs, remote working or retirement. Effects on rural destinations are covered in 6.1. Urban-urban and intra-urban moves reflect changing life stage, housing and jobs; they shape suburbs and inner city areas.

## Exam traps

- Stepped migration is an up-the-hierarchy process, not simply "migration to a town".
- Rural-urban migration is not the same as urbanisation; it is one cause of it.
- Give impacts for both the source and the destination.
- Counterurbanisation is internal movement, not emigration.

## Self-test

1. What is stepped migration?
2. Give two rural push factors.
3. Name one effect on the source area.
4. What are remittances?
5. Give an effect on city destinations.
6. Define counterurbanisation.

**Answers:** 1. Moving up the settlement hierarchy in stages. 2. Unemployment, low farm incomes, poor services, land shortage. 3. Loss of young adults or an ageing population. 4. Money sent home by migrants. 5. Pressure on housing and services or growth of informal settlements. 6. Movement from large cities to smaller towns and rural areas.
