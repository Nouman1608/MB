These notes condense one content point from key development 3.2.1.2 of the 9236 specification:
"causes of tropical cyclones and their impacts on the environment and people of Pakistan". The full
explanation is in the [tropical cyclones study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-tropical-cyclones/);
the wider climate content is in the
[Landscape revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-landscape-revision-notes/).

## Conditions needed

- Sea surface temperature above about **26.5 °C**, to a depth of tens of metres.
- At least about **5 degrees** from the equator, so the **Coriolis effect** can start rotation.
- **Low vertical wind shear**.
- **Moist** mid-level air and an existing **disturbance**.

## Formation in six steps

1. Warm sea heats the air; rapid **evaporation**.
2. Warm, moist air **rises**; surface **low pressure**.
3. **Condensation** forms clouds and releases **latent heat**, so air rises faster.
4. Surface air spirals inward, **anticlockwise** in the northern hemisphere.
5. A calm **eye** forms; the **eye wall** has the strongest winds and rain.
6. Over land or cool water the cyclone loses heat and moisture, and **weakens**.

## The Arabian Sea pattern

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Main seasons | Pre-monsoon (May–June) and post-monsoon (October–November) |
| Why not mid-summer | Monsoon winds create high wind shear |
| Frequency | Fewer cyclones than the Bay of Bengal |
| Common tracks | Many head towards Oman, Iran or Gujarat |
| Exposed Pakistani areas | Indus delta, Sindh coast including Karachi, Makran coast |

## Examples

| Cyclone | Area | Key impacts |
|---|---|---|
| May 1999 | Southern Sindh coast, Thatta and Badin | Fishing villages destroyed; many deaths; boats, crops and livestock lost |
| Yemyin, June 2007 | Makran coast, then Balochistan and Sindh | Flash floods; homes, roads and bridges washed away; many displaced |
| Biparjoy, June 2023 | Landfall near the Sindh–Gujarat border | Large advance evacuation from coastal Sindh; few deaths in Pakistan |

## Impacts

| On people | On the environment |
|---|---|
| Deaths and injuries, especially from storm surge | Saltwater flooding of farmland |
| Homes, roads, power lines, ports damaged | Coastal and delta erosion |
| Fishing boats and crops lost | Mangrove damage |
| Displacement to relief camps | Flash-flood erosion inland |
| Water-borne disease; food shortages | Some recharge of reservoirs and groundwater |

## Responses

- **Pakistan Meteorological Department**: satellite tracking, forecasts, advisories.
- **NDMA and provincial authorities**: evacuation and relief.
- **Fishing bans and port closures** before landfall.
- **Mangrove restoration** on the Sindh coast.
- **Embankments, shelters and stronger buildings**.

## Why impacts vary

| Factor | Higher impact when | Lower impact when |
|---|---|---|
| Relief | Coast is flat and low, as in the Indus delta | Coast is higher, as on parts of the Makran coast |
| Mangroves | Belts have been cleared | Wide, healthy belts absorb waves |
| Housing | Mud and thatch homes | Strong concrete buildings and shelters |
| Warning | Little or late warning | Early forecasts and organised evacuation |
| Population | Dense settlement near the landfall, such as Karachi | Remote, thinly settled coast |
| Wealth | Poor families with few savings | Insurance, savings and government support |

**Exam phrasing:** for "explain why impacts differ", give a factor, then show how it changes the
damage, then name a place or event. For "how far" questions, weigh physical against human factors
and decide which mattered more in your example.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tropical cyclone | Intense low-pressure storm over warm tropical seas |
| Storm surge | Rise in sea level pushed onto the coast by wind and low pressure |
| Coriolis effect | Deflection of moving air caused by the Earth's rotation |
| Latent heat | Heat released when water vapour condenses |
| Wind shear | Change in wind speed or direction with height |
| Eye | Calm centre of a cyclone with sinking air |
| Eye wall | Ring of strongest winds and heaviest rain around the eye |
| Landfall | When the centre of a cyclone crosses the coast |

## Exam traps

- Saying cyclones form over land.
- Clockwise rotation in the northern hemisphere.
- Leaving out the storm surge.
- Mixing impacts on people with impacts on the environment.
- An example with no date or place.

## Self-test

1. What sea temperature is generally needed for a tropical cyclone to form?
2. Why do cyclones not form on the equator?
3. What is wind shear, and why must it be low?
4. Why does condensation strengthen a cyclone?
5. In which direction do northern-hemisphere cyclones rotate?
6. In which months do most Arabian Sea cyclones occur?
7. Why are few cyclones formed during the height of the monsoon?
8. Which parts of Pakistan are most exposed to cyclones?
9. Name one cyclone that affected Pakistan and state where.
10. Define storm surge.
11. Give two environmental impacts of cyclones on the Sindh coast.
12. Which organisation issues weather warnings in Pakistan?

## Answers

1. Above about 26.5 °C.
2. The Coriolis effect is too weak near the equator to make the air spin.
3. A change in wind speed or direction with height; high shear tears the storm's structure apart.
4. It releases latent heat, warming the air so it rises faster and pressure falls further.
5. Anticlockwise.
6. May–June and October–November.
7. Strong monsoon winds create high wind shear.
8. The Indus delta and Sindh coast, including Karachi, and the Makran coast.
9. Any one: the May 1999 cyclone (southern Sindh); Yemyin, 2007 (Makran coast and Balochistan);
   Biparjoy, 2023 (near the Sindh–Gujarat border).
10. A rise in sea level pushed onto the coast by a storm's winds and low pressure.
11. Any two: saltwater flooding of farmland; coastal erosion; damage to mangroves.
12. The Pakistan Meteorological Department.
