This guide goes deeper into **3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers**, the first subtopic of section 3.3.12 Polymers in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 (Organic 2 and Physical 2) and in the synoptic Unit 5. Most marks in this subtopic come from four drawing skills and one explanation, so this guide works through each of them with polymers the topic overview does not use.

## What the specification asks for

- Condensation polymers form from **dicarboxylic acids and diols**, **dicarboxylic acids and diamines**, and **amino acids**.
- The repeating units of **polyesters** (for example Terylene) and **polyamides** (for example nylon 6,6 and Kevlar), and the linkages between them.
- **Typical uses** of these polymers.
- Draw the repeating unit **from monomer structures** and **from a section of polymer chain**; draw the **monomers from a section of polymer**.
- Explain the nature of the **intermolecular forces** between condensation polymer molecules.

## Condensation compared with addition

| Feature | Addition polymerisation | Condensation polymerisation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer feature | A C=C double bond | Two reactive groups per molecule (–COOH, –OH, –NH₂) |
| Other product | None | A small molecule, here water, for every link made |
| Backbone | Carbon atoms only | Contains O or N in ester or amide links |
| Repeating unit vs monomer | Same atoms as the monomer | Monomers minus water |

## The linkages, drawn out

An **ester link** is –CO–O–: a carbonyl carbon bonded to an oxygen that is bonded to carbon. An **amide link** is –CO–NH–: a carbonyl carbon bonded to an N–H group. When a question asks you to draw the link, show the C=O double bond and, for the amide, the N–H bond explicitly. Most errors come from linking C to C, or O to N.

## Naming nylons

A nylon made from a diamine and a dicarboxylic acid is named with two numbers: **carbons in the diamine, then carbons in the diacid**.

- Nylon 6,6: hexane-1,6-diamine (6 C) and hexanedioic acid (6 C).
- Nylon 6,10: hexane-1,6-diamine (6 C) and decanedioic acid, HOOC–(CH₂)₈–COOH (10 C).

A nylon made from a single amino acid takes one number: **nylon 6** comes from 6-aminohexanoic acid, H₂N–(CH₂)₅–COOH. Count carbons including the C=O carbons.

## Skill 1: monomers to repeating unit

### Worked example 1: a polyester

Draw the repeating unit of the polyester from hexanedioic acid, HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH, and ethane-1,2-diol, HO–CH₂CH₂–OH, and write the equation.

1. Remove –OH from each –COOH of the acid and –H from each –OH of the diol.
2. Join the carbonyl carbon to the diol's oxygen.
3. Repeating unit: **–[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O]–**, with a bond through each bracket.
4. Equation: n HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH + n HO–CH₂CH₂–OH → –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O]ₙ– + 2n H₂O.

Two water molecules leave per repeating unit, because each unit contains one complete ester link and shares a second with its neighbour.

### Worked example 2: a single amino acid

Draw the repeating unit of nylon 6 from 6-aminohexanoic acid.

1. One monomer carries both groups, so the repeating unit contains **one** residue.
2. Remove H from –NH₂ and OH from –COOH.
3. Repeating unit: **–[NH–(CH₂)₅–CO]–**, with one water molecule lost per unit.

## Skill 2 and 3: from a chain section

### Worked example 3: nylon 6,10

A section of polymer chain is:

`–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₈–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₈–CO–`

Give the repeating unit, the monomers and the name of the polymer.

1. Every link is –CO–NH–, so it is a polyamide.
2. The shortest repeating stretch is one diamine residue plus one diacid residue: **–[NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₈–CO]–**.
3. Cut each C–N bond. Add H to each N: **hexane-1,6-diamine**, H₂N–(CH₂)₆–NH₂. Add OH to each C=O carbon: **decanedioic acid**, HOOC–(CH₂)₈–COOH.
4. Carbons: 6 in the diamine and 10 in the acid, so this is **nylon 6,10**.

The repeating unit can start at any point, as long as it contains one of each residue and its ends cut through linkages. Starting from –CO–(CH₂)₈–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH– is equally correct.

## Calculations with repeating units

For a two-monomer polymer: Mr(repeating unit) = Mr(monomer 1) + Mr(monomer 2) − 2 × 18.0.

### Worked example 4: nylon 6,10 numbers

Calculate the Mr of the nylon 6,10 repeating unit, its percentage of nitrogen by mass, and the number of repeating units in a chain of Mr 2.82 × 10⁴. (Mr: hexane-1,6-diamine 116.0, decanedioic acid 202.0)

1. Mr = 116.0 + 202.0 − 36.0 = **282.0** (formula C₁₆H₃₀N₂O₂).
2. % N = 28.0 ÷ 282.0 × 100 = **9.93%**.
3. Number of units = 2.82 × 10⁴ ÷ 282.0 = **100**.

## The explanation: forces between chains

| Polymer | Strongest force between chains | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Polyamides (nylon 6,6, nylon 6,10, Kevlar) | **Hydrogen bonds** | The δ+ H of an N–H group on one chain is attracted to a lone pair on the δ− O of a C=O on another |
| Polyesters (Terylene) | **Permanent dipole–dipole forces** | Polar C=O groups attract each other; there is no H bonded to O or N, so no hydrogen bonding |
| All polymers | **Van der Waals forces** | Present between all chains; larger for longer chains with more contact |

**Kevlar** is exceptionally strong because its benzene rings make the chains flat and rigid. The chains line up in parallel, so many N–H and C=O groups face each other and form a regular network of hydrogen bonds.

A full answer names the force, identifies the groups involved on **neighbouring chains**, and says why those groups are polar.

## Typical uses

| Polymer | Typical uses |
|---|---|
| Terylene | Clothing fibres; plastic drinks bottles |
| Nylon 6,6 | Clothing, ropes, carpets |
| Kevlar | Bulletproof vests and body armour; uses needing high strength for low mass |

## Common mistakes

- Two residues of the same monomer, or one residue only, inside a two-monomer repeating unit.
- Leaving –OH or –H on the ends of a repeating unit instead of bonds through the brackets.
- Writing n H₂O instead of 2n H₂O for a diacid with a diol or diamine.
- Linking N to O, or C to C, at the junction between residues.
- Claiming hydrogen bonds between polyester chains.
- Describing "hydrogen bonds within a chain" when the question is about forces between chains.
- Miscounting carbons in a nylon name by leaving out the C=O carbons.

## Exam technique

- **Draw** a repeating unit: brackets, bonds through the brackets, every atom of the linkage, and n if the equation is asked for.
- **Deduce** monomers: cut every linkage the same way, then add H and OH to restore the functional groups.
- **Explain** intermolecular forces: name the force, the groups and the partial charges.
- **Calculate**: subtract water for every link made, and divide the chain Mr by the repeating unit Mr.
