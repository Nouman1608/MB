These notes condense section **3.1.1.3 Electron configuration** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. The [Electron configuration study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electron-configuration/) explains each idea with worked examples, and the [Electron configuration practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electron-configuration-practice/) test them. Mass spectrometry and isotopes are in the [Mass number and isotopes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-mass-number-and-isotopes-revision-notes/).

## In scope

- Configurations of **atoms and ions up to Z = 36** using shells and s, p and d sub-shells (orbitals).
- **Define** first ionisation energy.
- **Write equations** for first and successive ionisation energies.
- **Explain** how ionisation energies in **Period 3 (Na–Ar)** and **Group 2 (Be–Ba)** give evidence for shells and sub-shells.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shell | Principal energy level, n = 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| Sub-shell | Set of orbitals of one type (s, p or d) within a shell |
| Orbital | Region holding up to two electrons of opposite spin |
| Isoelectronic | Having the same electron configuration |
| First ionisation energy | Energy to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions |
| Successive ionisation energy | Energy to remove one more mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous ions |
| Shielding | Repulsion of an outer electron by inner electrons, reducing nuclear attraction |

## Capacities and order

| Sub-shell | Orbitals | Electrons |
|---|---|---|
| s | 1 | 2 |
| p | 3 | 6 |
| d | 5 | 10 |

Filling order: **1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p**. One orbital at a time within a sub-shell (singly, then pair). Two exceptions to Z = 36: **Cr [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** and **Cu [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹**.

## Atoms: quick reference

| Atom | Configuration | Unpaired electrons |
|---|---|---|
| O | 1s² 2s² 2p⁴ | 2 |
| K | [Ar] 4s¹ | 1 |
| Ti | [Ar] 3d² 4s² | 2 |
| Fe | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² | 4 |
| Zn | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² | 0 |
| As | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³ | 3 |

Check every time: superscripts add up to the electron count.

## Ions in steps

1. Count electrons: Z − charge.
2. **Negative ion or main-group positive ion:** add to, or remove from, the outer sub-shell.
3. **Transition-metal positive ion:** remove **4s first**, then 3d.
4. Check the total.

| Ion | Configuration | Note |
|---|---|---|
| S²⁻ | 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ | Isoelectronic with Ar |
| Ca²⁺ | 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ | Isoelectronic with Ar |
| Cr³⁺ | [Ar] 3d³ | 4s¹ then two 3d removed |
| Co²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁷ | Both 4s removed |
| Ni²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁸ | Both 4s removed |
| Cu⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ | The single 4s removed |
| Zn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ | Both 4s removed |

## Ionisation equations

```
nth ionisation:  X⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾⁺(g) → Xⁿ⁺(g) + e⁻
first (K):       K(g)   → K⁺(g)   + e⁻
second (Mg):     Mg⁺(g) → Mg²⁺(g) + e⁻
third (Al):      Al²⁺(g) → Al³⁺(g) + e⁻
```

One electron per equation, every species gaseous.

## Three factors

Nuclear charge (up: harder to remove), distance from the nucleus (up: easier), shielding by inner electrons (up: easier). Every explanation should name the factor that changes and say what happens to the attraction.

## Evidence for shells and sub-shells

| Data | Pattern | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Group 2 first IE, Be to Ba (900, 738, 590, 550, 503 kJ mol⁻¹) | Decreases | Outer electron in a higher shell each time: further away and more shielded, outweighing extra nuclear charge. Evidence for **shells** |
| Period 3 first IE, general rise | Increases | Same shell, similar shielding, more protons |
| Al lower than Mg | Drop | Outer electron in **3p**, higher in energy than 3s. Evidence for **sub-shells** |
| S lower than P | Drop | Paired electrons in one 3p orbital repel. Evidence for **orbitals** |
| Ar (1521) to K (419) | Big fall | New shell starts. Evidence for **shells** |
| Successive IEs | Big jump | Next electron comes from a shell closer to the nucleus |
| Successive IEs within a shell | Smaller, uneven rises | Change of sub-shell (for example Al: 3p, then 3s) |

**Counting from a jump.** The number of electrons removed *before* the big jump equals the number of outer-shell electrons, and so the group.

**Sodium's 11 successive IEs** on a log scale fall into groups of 1, 8 and 2: one electron in shell 3, eight in shell 2, two in shell 1.

## Exam traps

- Fe²⁺ or Fe³⁺ written with 4s electrons still present.
- Ca(g) → Ca²⁺(g) + 2e⁻ given as the second ionisation equation.
- Missing (g) on any species.
- Group 2 answers that mention only distance and forget to say it outweighs nuclear charge.
- "Al has a full 3s sub-shell" given as the reason for its drop: name the 3p electron.
- Electrons paired in p orbitals before each has one.
- Using "atomic radius" without linking it to attraction.

## Self-test

1. Write the full configuration of chlorine.
2. Write the configuration of Ti²⁺ in shorthand.
3. How many unpaired electrons does a nitrogen atom have?
4. Name the element with configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁴.
5. Name two ions isoelectronic with neon.
6. Write the equation for the second ionisation energy of calcium.
7. Write the configuration of Mn³⁺.
8. Why does first IE decrease from Mg to Ca?
9. Why is the first IE of Al lower than that of Mg?
10. An element has successive IEs of 550, 1064 and 4138 kJ mol⁻¹. State its group.
11. Why does each successive ionisation energy of an atom increase?
12. Why is the first IE of K much lower than that of Ar?

### Answers

1. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵.
2. [Ar] 3d².
3. Three (one in each 2p orbital).
4. Selenium.
5. Any two of Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, F⁻, O²⁻, N³⁻.
6. Ca⁺(g) → Ca²⁺(g) + e⁻.
7. [Ar] 3d⁴ (two 4s removed, then one 3d).
8. Ca's outer electron is in shell 4, further from the nucleus and more shielded; this outweighs the greater nuclear charge.
9. Al's outer electron is in 3p, which is higher in energy than 3s, so less energy is needed to remove it.
10. Group 2 (big jump after the second electron).
11. Each electron is removed from a more positive ion, so it is attracted more strongly.
12. K's outer electron is in a new shell (4s), further from the nucleus and better shielded.
