These questions cover subtopic 3.4 Length and midpoint of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is **Extended only**, so every question is for Extended candidates (Papers 2 and 4). Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). On non-calculator questions, give exact answers.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Calculate the length of the line segment joining (–1, –2) and (5, 6). **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Find the coordinates of the midpoint of the line segment joining (–5, 8) and (7, –4). **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Find the length of the line segment joining (2, –3) and (–4, 0). Give your answer as a surd in its simplest form. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) The midpoint of AB is (3, 1). A is the point (7, –5). Find the coordinates of B. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) Calculate the length of the line segment joining (1.6, –0.4) and (–2.2, 3.1). Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) The midpoint of the segment joining (p, –2) and (10, q) is (4, 3). Find p and q. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) A triangle has vertices A(–2, 1), B(4, 3) and C(2, –3).

**(a)** Show that triangle ABC is isosceles. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of M, the midpoint of AC. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the length of BM, giving your answer as a surd in its simplest form. **[2]**

**(d)** BM is perpendicular to AC. Find the area of triangle ABC. **[1]**

**8.** (non-calculator) PQ is a diameter of a circle. P is (–3, –2) and Q is (5, 4).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the centre of the circle. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the radius of the circle. **[2]**

**(c)** Show that the point (4, 5) lies on the circle. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) The distance between (3, k) and (–2, 4) is 13 units. Find the two possible values of k. **[4]**

**10.** (calculator) A triangle has vertices (0, 0), (5, 2) and (1, 6). Calculate its perimeter, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**11.** (non-calculator) ABCD is a parallelogram with A(–1, –2), B(5, 0) and C(7, 6). The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

**(a)** Find the midpoint of AC. **[1]**

**(b)** Hence find the coordinates of D. **[2]**

**12.** (non-calculator) The point P lies on the y-axis and is the same distance from A(2, 5) as from B(6, 1). Find the coordinates of P. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** √((5 – (–1))² + (6 – (–2))²) = √(36 + 64) [1] = √100 = **10** [1]

**2.** ((–5 + 7)/2, (8 + (–4))/2) [1] = **(1, 2)** [1]

**3.** √(6² + 3²) = √45 [1] = √(9 × 5) = **3√5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* √45 is correct but not in its simplest form, so it earns the method mark only.

**4.** B = (2 × 3 – 7, 2 × 1 – (–5)) [1] = **(–1, 7)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (5, –2), the midpoint of A and M, is a common wrong answer. B must be on the far side of M from A.

**5.** √(3.8² + 3.5²) = √26.69 [1] = 5.166… = **5.17** [1]

**6.** (p + 10)/2 = 4, so **p = –2** [1]; (–2 + q)/2 = 3, so **q = 8** [1]

**7. (a)** AB² = 6² + 2² = 40 [1]; BC² = 2² + 6² = 40 and AC² = 4² + 4² = 32 [1]; AB = BC = √40, so the triangle is isosceles [1]

**(b)** **M(0, –1)** [1]

**(c)** BM² = 4² + 4² = 32 [1]; BM = √32 = **4√2** [1]

**(d)** AC = √32 = 4√2, so area = ½ × 4√2 × 4√2 = ½ × 32 = **16 square units** [1]

*Examiner insight:* working with squared lengths keeps (a) exact; a conclusion sentence ("two sides are equal, so…") is needed for the final mark.

**8. (a)** **(1, 1)** [1]

**(b)** PQ = √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10 [1]; radius = **5** [1]

**(c)** Distance from (1, 1) to (4, 5) = √(3² + 4²) [1] = 5, equal to the radius, so the point is on the circle [1]

**9.** (3 – (–2))² + (k – 4)² = 13² [1]; 25 + (k – 4)² = 169, so (k – 4)² = 144 [1]; k – 4 = 12 gives **k = 16** [1]; k – 4 = –12 gives **k = –8** [1]

*Examiner insight:* square-rooting 144 gives ±12. Giving only k = 16 loses the last mark.

**10.** √(5² + 2²) = √29 = 5.385… [1]; √(4² + 4²) = √32 = 5.656… [1]; √(1² + 6²) = √37 = 6.082… [1]; perimeter = 17.124… = **17.1** [1]

*Examiner insight:* keep each length unrounded and add at the end. Rounding each to 3 s.f. first gives 5.39 + 5.66 + 6.08 = 17.13, which happens to agree here but can differ in the last figure.

**11. (a)** ((–1 + 7)/2, (–2 + 6)/2) = **(3, 2)** [1]

**(b)** (3, 2) is also the midpoint of BD, so D = (2 × 3 – 5, 2 × 2 – 0) [1] = **(1, 4)** [1]

**12.** P = (0, y): 2² + (y – 5)² = 6² + (y – 1)² [1]; 4 + y² – 10y + 25 = 36 + y² – 2y + 1, so –8y = 8 [1]; **P = (0, –1)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the y² terms cancel, so the equation is linear. Check: both distances are √40.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the answer as a squared length.
- Mixing up the formulas: adding for length, subtracting for midpoint.
- Not simplifying a surd when the simplest form is asked for.
- Missing the second value of an unknown coordinate.
- Rounding lengths before adding them.
- Finding the midpoint of A and M instead of the far end B.
