**Key question 1.6, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "What caused the First World War?" The specification covers the
origins of the war from 1890 to 1914: the Alliance System, the 1902 Anglo-Japanese Alliance, the arms race, colonial
rivalries, the Balkans and the crisis of June-July 1914. Explanations are in the
[causes of the First World War study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-causes-first-world-war/);
practise with the [causes of the First World War practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-causes-first-world-war-practice/).

## The two blocs

| Triple Alliance | Triple Entente |
|---|---|
| Dual Alliance: Germany and Austria-Hungary (1879) | Franco-Russian Alliance (1894) |
| Italy joins (1882) | Entente Cordiale: Britain and France (1904) |
| A formal military alliance | Anglo-Russian Convention (1907): an understanding, not a binding alliance |

**Also learn:** the Reinsurance Treaty (1887) with Russia, allowed to lapse after Bismarck's dismissal (1890); the
**Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902)**, which ended British isolation and let Japan fight Russia in 1904-05.

## Timeline of rising tension

| Date | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1890 | Bismarck dismissed; Reinsurance Treaty lapses | Russia moves towards France |
| 1896 | Kruger Telegram | British anger at Germany |
| 1898, 1900 | German Navy Laws (Tirpitz) | Start of the naval race |
| 1898 | Fashoda crisis | Britain and France clash, then settle |
| 1902 | Anglo-Japanese Alliance | Britain leaves isolation |
| 1904 | Entente Cordiale | Colonial deal: Egypt for Britain, Morocco for France |
| 1904-05 | Russo-Japanese War | Russia defeated; turns to the Balkans |
| 1905-06 | First Moroccan Crisis; Algeciras | Germany isolated; Entente strengthened |
| 1906 | HMS Dreadnought launched | Naval race intensifies |
| 1907 | Anglo-Russian Convention | Triple Entente complete |
| 1908-09 | Bosnian Crisis | Russia humiliated; Serbia furious |
| 1909 | "We want eight and we won't wait" | British public demands more dreadnoughts |
| 1911 | Agadir Crisis | Britain backs France; Germany gains part of the French Congo |
| 1912-13 | Balkan Wars | Serbia nearly doubles in size |
| 1913 | French three-year service law; Russian army expansion | Land arms race |

## The July Crisis chain

1. **28 June:** Princip assassinates Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo.
2. **5-6 July:** German "blank cheque" to Austria-Hungary.
3. **23 July:** Austrian ultimatum to Serbia.
4. **25 July:** Serbia accepts most, not all, of the terms.
5. **28 July:** Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia.
6. **30 July:** Russian general mobilisation.
7. **1 August:** Germany declares war on Russia; **3 August** on France.
8. **4 August:** Germany invades Belgium; Britain declares war, citing the 1839 Treaty of London.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Splendid isolation | Britain's policy of avoiding permanent alliances, abandoned after 1902 |
| Entente | A friendly understanding rather than a binding military alliance |
| Weltpolitik | Germany's "world policy" of colonies and naval power after 1897 |
| Dreadnought | A fast, big-gun battleship type, first launched by Britain in 1906 |
| Schlieffen Plan | German plan to defeat France through Belgium before Russia mobilised |
| Mobilisation | Calling up and moving an army ready for war |
| Pan-Slavism | The idea that Slav peoples should be united, often under Russian protection |
| Black Hand | Serbian nationalist secret society, linked to the assassination |
| Blank cheque | Germany's unconditional promise of support to Austria-Hungary, July 1914 |

## Weighing the causes

| Cause | Strongest argument | Best counter-argument |
|---|---|---|
| Alliances | Turned a local war into a European one within a week | Italy stayed neutral; Britain was not bound |
| Arms race | Created fear; the Schlieffen Plan left no time for diplomacy | By 1912 Britain had a clear lead in dreadnoughts, easing that tension |
| Colonial rivalry | Moroccan crises tightened the Entente against Germany | Crises were settled; the war began in the Balkans |
| Balkans | The trigger and the direct clash of Austrian and Russian interests | Balkan crises in 1908 and 1912-13 did not cause a general war |

## Exam traps

- **Dates of the alliances.** Triple Alliance 1882, Franco-Russian 1894, Entente Cordiale 1904, Anglo-Russian 1907.
- **The ententes were not alliances.** Britain chose to fight in 1914; it was not obliged to.
- **Morocco versus Bosnia.** The Moroccan crises were colonial; the Bosnian Crisis was Balkan.
- **The war itself is not this key question.** Stop at early August 1914.
- **Japan matters.** The Anglo-Japanese Alliance is named in the specification.

## Self-test

1. Which two powers formed the Dual Alliance in 1879? *Germany and Austria-Hungary.*
2. Why did Russia ally with France in 1894? *Germany had let the Reinsurance Treaty lapse, leaving Russia without a
   German partner.*
3. What did the Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902) promise? *Neutrality if either fought one power; help if either fought
   two.*
4. What did Britain and France agree in 1904? *To settle colonial disputes: Britain in Egypt, France in Morocco.*
5. Why was HMS Dreadnought important? *It made earlier battleships obsolete, restarting the naval race.*
6. What happened at Algeciras in 1906? *Germany was isolated, supported only by Austria-Hungary, and France's position
   in Morocco was confirmed.*
7. What did Germany send to Agadir in 1911? *The gunboat Panther.*
8. Why did the Bosnian Crisis make war more likely? *Russia was humiliated and determined not to back down again;
   Serbia was angered.*
9. Who assassinated Franz Ferdinand? *Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb linked to the Black Hand.*
10. What was the blank cheque? *Germany's promise of full support for Austria-Hungary in July 1914.*
11. Why did Russian mobilisation lead to war with France? *The Schlieffen Plan required Germany to attack France
    first once Russia mobilised.*
12. Why did Britain enter the war? *Germany invaded Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed in 1839.*
