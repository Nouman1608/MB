## Microeconomics

**Microeconomics** is the study of the economic decisions of individual households, workers and firms, and of the individual markets in which they buy and sell.

Typical micro questions:

- Why has the price of tomatoes risen this month?
- How many workers should a clothing firm employ?
- Why are surgeons paid more than shop assistants?
- How will a tax on sugary drinks affect sales of those drinks?

### Decision makers in microeconomics

The syllabus content overview for Topic 3, *Microeconomic decision makers*, names them: **banks, households, workers, trade unions and firms**.

| Decision maker | Typical micro decision |
|---|---|
| Households (consumers) | What to buy, how much to save |
| Workers | Which job to take, how many hours to work |
| Firms (producers) | What to produce, how much, at what price, with which methods |
| Trade unions | What wage rise to ask for on behalf of their members |
| Banks | Whom to lend to, and at what interest rate |

A government can also act at the micro level when it intervenes in one market, for example by setting a maximum price for bread or taxing cigarettes.

## Macroeconomics

**Macroeconomics** is the study of the economy as a whole.

Instead of the price of one good, macroeconomics looks at the **general price level** (inflation). Instead of one firm's workers, it looks at **total employment and unemployment**. Instead of one market's output, it looks at **total output** and its growth. It also covers the country's trade with the rest of the world (the balance of payments).

Typical macro questions:

- Why is the country's total output growing slowly?
- Why have prices across the economy risen by more than last year?
- Why has unemployment risen across the whole country?
- What will happen to total spending if income tax is cut?

### Decision makers in macroeconomics

The main macroeconomic decision maker is the **government**, which tries to achieve macroeconomic aims such as economic growth, low unemployment and stable prices (Topic 4, *Government and the macroeconomy*). It uses policies that affect the whole economy: fiscal policy (taxes and government spending), monetary policy (interest rates and the money supply) and supply-side policy. In many countries monetary policy is carried out by the central bank.

## The difference in one table

| | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Individual households, workers, firms and markets | The whole economy |
| Price | The price of one good or service | The general price level |
| Employment | Wages and jobs in one occupation or firm | Total employment and unemployment |
| Output | Output of one firm or industry | Total output and economic growth |
| Main decision makers | Households, workers, firms, trade unions, banks | Government (and central bank) |
| Where in the syllabus | Topics 2 and 3 | Topic 4 |

Topics 5 (economic development) and 6 (international trade and globalisation) draw on both. For example, a multinational company's choice of location is a micro decision, while the effect of many such choices on a country's growth is a macro issue.

## How the two connect

Micro and macro are two views of the same economy, not separate economies.

**From micro to macro.** Macro totals are built from millions of micro decisions. If many households decide to spend less, total spending in the economy falls.

**Worked example 1 (invented).** In a recession, 1,500 firms in Country L each cut 4 jobs. That is 1,500 × 4 = 6,000 jobs lost. Each firm's decision is micro. If the labour force is 1.2 million, the 6,000 extra unemployed add 6,000 ÷ 1,200,000 × 100 = **0.5 percentage points** to the unemployment rate, which is a macro outcome.

**From macro to micro.** Macro conditions change the choices of individuals. When the central bank raises interest rates (macro), a household may postpone buying a car on credit, and a firm may delay buying a new machine (both micro).

## Classifying awkward cases

The test is the **scale of the question**, not who is acting.

**Worked example 2.** Classify each.

| Statement | Micro or macro | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The government sets a maximum rent for flats in the capital | Micro | One market (rented housing) |
| The government raises income tax to reduce total spending | Macro | Aimed at the whole economy |
| A trade union wins a 5% pay rise for bus drivers | Micro | One occupation |
| The country's average wage rises by 5% | Macro | An economy-wide average |
| A firm closes a factory because of a recession | Micro decision caused by a macro event | The decision is one firm's; the cause is economy-wide |
| The price of rice rises after a poor harvest | Micro | One market |
| Prices of most goods rise by 6% over a year | Macro | The general price level: inflation |
| A bank refuses a loan to a small business | Micro | One bank and one customer |

When a statement mixes both, as in the factory closure, say so and explain which part is which. Examiners reward the reasoning.

## Common errors

- Saying "micro is about small things, macro about big things". A very large firm is still micro; the test is one decision maker or market versus the whole economy.
- Calling every government action macro. A government setting a price in one market is acting at the micro level.
- Confusing the price of one good rising with inflation.
- Forgetting the decision makers when the question asks for them.

## Exam technique

- **Define** each branch with the key contrast: individual decision makers and markets versus the economy as a whole.
- **Identify** whether an issue is micro or macro, then give the reason in a few words.
- **Explain** the difference by comparing them on the same feature: "Micro studies the price of one good; macro studies the general price level."
- In multiple-choice questions, look for words such as "total", "general", "national" or "the economy" (macro) against "a firm", "a market" or "a household" (micro).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who are the decision makers in microeconomics?**
Households (consumers), workers, firms, trade unions and banks. The government also acts at the micro level when it intervenes in a single market.

**Is unemployment a micro or macro issue?**
Total unemployment in the economy is a macro issue. Why one firm cuts jobs, or why wages differ between occupations, is micro.
