Condensed notes for sub-topic 1.5 of Cambridge O Level Geography (2217), 2026 series.

## Patterns

- **Dispersed**: scattered farms; large farms, pastoral or plantation farming, water widely
  available.
- **Nucleated**: clustered round a crossroads, well, market or place of worship; defence, shared
  water, cooperation.
- **Linear**: a line along a road, river, coast, railway or valley floor.

## Site factors

- Wet point (water supply) and dry point (above flooding).
- Defence: hilltop, island, meander loop (Durham, River Wear).
- Bridging point or ford; gap through hills.
- Shelter, sunny aspect, fertile land, building materials, fuel.

**Site** = the land built on. **Situation** = position relative to routes, resources and other
places. **Situation decides growth.**

## Functions

- Market town, port, administrative centre, industrial or mining town, resort, commuter settlement.
- Large cities are **multi-functional**.
- Functions **change**: fishing village to resort; mining town declines; farming village becomes
  commuter village.
- Planned administrative capital: **Islamabad**, built in the 1960s.

## Hierarchy

Isolated dwelling → hamlet → village → town → city → conurbation.

Up the hierarchy: **fewer** settlements, **larger**, **further apart**, **more and higher-order
services**, **larger sphere of influence**.

| | Low-order service | High-order service |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Bread, newspaper, primary school | Jewellery, cars, specialist hospital |
| How often used | Frequently | Rarely |
| Cost | Low | High |
| Threshold | Low | High |
| Range | Short | Long |
| Found in | Villages and above | Large towns and cities |

## Changing service provision

- **Rural decline** of shops, banks and post offices: car ownership, online shopping and banking,
  ageing or falling population.
- **Responses**: mobile services, multi-purpose shops, community-run shops.
- **Rapidly growing cities**: services cannot keep pace with demand.

## Studying service provision

- Questionnaire: where do shoppers live, how often do they visit?
- **Desire lines** from the settlement to customers' homes show the **sphere of influence**.
- Evaluate: sample size, time and day, location of the survey.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Site | The land on which a settlement is built |
| Situation | Position of a settlement relative to surrounding features and places |
| Function | Main economic or social purpose of a settlement |
| Settlement hierarchy | Ranking of settlements by size and services |
| Threshold population | Minimum number of people needed to support a service |
| Range | Maximum distance people will travel to use a service |
| Sphere of influence | Area served by a settlement or service |
| Low-order service | Frequently used, cheap service with a low threshold |
| High-order service | Rarely used, costly service with a high threshold |

## Exam traps

- Site and situation mixed up.
- "Nucleated" with no centre named.
- Threshold defined as "number of users" instead of the minimum needed.
- Hierarchy explained without threshold and range.

## Self-test

1. Name the pattern of houses built along both sides of a valley road.
2. Give two defensive site factors.
3. Why does a city have a larger sphere of influence than a village?
4. Give two reasons why village shops have closed.
5. What does a long desire line on a sphere-of-influence map show?

**Answers:** 1. Linear. 2. Any two: hilltop, island, meander loop, marsh or river protection.
3. It provides high-order services with long ranges, so people travel further to use them.
4. Any two: car ownership lets people shop in towns; online shopping; ageing or declining
population falls below the threshold. 5. A customer who travelled a long way, suggesting a
high-order service or a large sphere of influence.
