These questions cover content item 1.1.3, The role of business enterprise, in Topic 1.1 Enterprise and entrepreneurship of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Calculators may be used. Added value is taken as selling price minus the cost of bought-in inputs (a standard meaning; the specification gives no formula). Every business and figure is invented and the mark splits are the writer's own.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a role of an entrepreneur named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Paying taxes

B: Organising resources

C: Setting interest rates

D: Writing laws

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a service? **[1]**

A: A bag of flour

B: A garden bench

C: A dog-walking session

D: A box of chocolates

**3.** Define the term added value. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** ways a business can add value, as listed in the specification. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a small business might try to create a unique selling point. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why meeting customer needs is a purpose of business activity. **[3]**

**7.** The table shows information about one product sold by *Cobble Yard Bags*.

| Item | Selling price (£) | Bought-in inputs per item (£) | Sold per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas tote bag | 9.50 | 2.10 | 1,200 |

**(a)** Calculate the added value per tote bag. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total added value from tote bags in a year. **[1]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**8.** *Gorsey Bank Bakery* is owned by Owain. He rents a small shop, employs one baker and has chosen to sell only sourdough bread and pastries. He used £15,000 of his savings to buy an oven. Each sourdough loaf sells for £4.20; flour, salt and packaging cost £0.95 a loaf. The bakery sells 180 loaves a week.

**(a)** Calculate the added value from sourdough loaves in one week. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline **one** way Owain has acted as an entrepreneur by taking risks. **[2]**

**(c)** Owain is considering two ways to add value.

Option 1: offer a home delivery service. A driver would cost £150 a week, and Owain expects to sell an extra 60 loaves a week at the same price.

Option 2: switch to organic flour. Inputs would rise to £1.35 a loaf and he would raise the price to £4.80. He expects weekly sales to stay at 180 loaves.

Justify which option Owain should choose. **[9]**

**9.** *Sable and Pine* makes wooden toys. Its toys are more expensive than most rivals' because they are made from sustainably sourced timber and are designed to be passed between generations. Analyse how quality and design add value for Sable and Pine. **[6]**

**10.** *Brookfield Laundry Lockers* places lockers at a railway station. Commuters leave their washing in the morning and collect it cleaned and folded in the evening. Outline **one** way Brookfield adds value through convenience. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B: Organising resources [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C: A dog-walking session [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The other options are physical goods.

**3.** The difference between the selling price of a product and the cost of the bought-in materials and services used to make it [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: convenience [1]; branding [1]; quality; design; unique selling points. **[2]**

**5.** A small business often competes with larger rivals that can charge lower prices [1]. A USP gives customers a reason to choose the small business that is not based on price [1], so it can win loyal customers and may be able to charge a higher price [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A business can only sell its product if customers want it [1]. If it meets their needs, such as price or convenience, customers will buy and come back [1], giving the revenue the business needs to cover its costs and survive [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** £9.50 − £2.10 = **£7.40** [1].

**(b)** £7.40 × 1,200 = **£8,880** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Added value per loaf = £4.20 − £0.95 = £3.25 [1]. Weekly added value = £3.25 × 180 = **£585** [1].

**(b)** Owain used £15,000 of his own savings to buy the oven [1], so if the bakery does not sell enough bread he could lose some of this money, because a used oven is worth less than he paid [1].

**(c)** Option 1: the extra 60 loaves add 60 × £3.25 = £195 of added value a week [1]. After paying the driver £150, the business gains £45 a week [1]. Delivery adds convenience, which could attract customers who cannot reach the shop and build loyalty [1]. However, the 60 extra loaves are only expected; if fewer people order, the £150 driver cost could exceed the gain [1].

Option 2: added value per loaf becomes £4.80 − £1.35 = £3.45 [1], so weekly added value is 180 × £3.45 = £621, a gain of £36 a week [1]. It adds value through quality and needs no new staff [1]. The risk is that some customers may refuse to pay 60p more and sales may fall below 180 [1].

Judgement: Owain should choose Option 1, because it brings the larger weekly gain (£45 against £36) and could reach new customers, but only if he is confident of the 60 extra loaves; if that forecast is uncertain, Option 2 is lower risk because it adds no fixed cost [1]. **[13]**
*Examiner insight:* A Justify answer can recommend either option. The judgement is supported when it compares the calculated gains and names the uncertain forecast behind each.

**9.** Sable and Pine uses sustainably sourced timber, which gives the toys a higher quality than mass-produced rivals [1]. Customers who care about the environment may be willing to pay more for this [1], so the business can charge a higher price than its rivals [1]. The toys are designed to last and be passed down between generations [1], which makes them suitable as special gifts, for example for grandparents buying for grandchildren [1]. Because the design and quality are hard to match cheaply, the higher price is accepted, increasing added value per toy even though timber costs are higher [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Commuters do not have to visit a laundrette during its opening hours, because they drop off and collect at the station they already pass through [1]. This saves them time, so they are willing to pay more than they would for a self-service laundrette, raising Brookfield's added value [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling added value "profit" in a definition.
- Forgetting to multiply by the number sold when a total is asked for.
- Naming the entrepreneur's roles without a detail from the case.
- In a Justify answer, quoting the figures for only one option.
- Saying a method of adding value "makes the product better" without saying how it changes price or sales.
