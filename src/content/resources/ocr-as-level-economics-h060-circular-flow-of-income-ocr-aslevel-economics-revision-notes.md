These notes condense the **Circular flow of income** from Component 02, Macroeconomics, of the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification. Full explanations are in the [circular flow study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-circular-flow-of-income-ocr-aslevel-economics/), and the [circular flow practice questions](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-circular-flow-of-income-ocr-aslevel-economics-practice/) give exam-style work. All figures are invented.

## What you must be able to do

| Specification wording | In one line |
|---|---|
| Explain the circular flow of income, with injections and leakages | Money flows from firms to households as income and back as spending; S, T and M leave the flow, I, G and X enter it |
| Explain the methods of measuring national income, output and expenditure | Value added, factor incomes, and C + I + G + (X - M) all measure the same total |

## The model in five lines

1. Households supply factor services; firms pay factor incomes (wages, rent, interest, profit).
2. Households spend income on firms' output, which becomes firms' revenue.
3. Output = income = expenditure, by definition.
4. Withdrawals (S, T, M) leave the flow; injections (I, G, X) enter it.
5. National income is in equilibrium when planned injections equal planned withdrawals.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Factor incomes | Payments to factors of production: wages, rent, interest and profit |
| Real flow | Factor services and goods and services moving between sectors |
| Money flow | Incomes and spending, moving the opposite way to real flows |
| Leakage (withdrawal) | Income not passed on as spending on domestic output: saving, taxation, imports |
| Injection | Spending entering the flow that does not come from households' current income: investment, government spending, exports |
| Equilibrium national income | The level at which planned injections equal planned withdrawals, so income has no tendency to change |
| Value added | A firm's output value minus the value of inputs bought from other firms |
| Transfer payment | Money paid with no output in return, such as a state pension; excluded from national income |
| GDP | The total value of output produced within a country in a period |
| Statistical discrepancy | The balancing item when the three measures do not match |
| Hidden economy | Unrecorded activity, such as cash-in-hand work |

## Rules for the arithmetic

- **Total withdrawals** W = S + T + M; **total injections** J = I + G + X.
- J > W: national income **rises**. J < W: national income **falls**. J = W: equilibrium.
- If W is a fixed share of income (W = wY), equilibrium Y = J / w.
- **Output method:** add value added at each stage, never total sales.
- **Income method:** add wages, rent, interest and profit; leave out transfers.
- **Expenditure method:** C + I + G + X - M.

### Worked reminder: equilibrium

S = 45, T = 80, M = 55, so W = 180. I = 60, G = 85, X = 50, so J = 195. J exceeds W by 15, so national income rises.

### Worked reminder: equilibrium with a share

W = 0.25Y and J = 150. Equilibrium Y = 150 / 0.25 = **600**. If investment falls by 10, J = 140 and Y = **560**: income falls by 40, four times the fall in investment.

### Worked reminder: value added

Timber 80 sold to a furniture maker, who sells a table for 300 to a shop, which sells it for 420. Value added: 80 + 220 + 120 = **420**, equal to the final sale. Total sales of 800 double count.

### Worked reminder: the three methods agree

| Method | Items | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Income | Wages 250, rent 20, interest 15, profit 65 | 350 |
| Expenditure | C 240, I 50, G 70, X 90, M 100 | 350 |
| Output | Value added in agriculture 20, manufacturing 90, services 240 | 350 |

Child benefit of 30 is excluded from the income total.

## Which sector does each flow belong to?

| Sector | Leakage | Injection | Mainly driven by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Households and financial sector | Saving | Investment | Saving: income, interest rates, confidence. Investment: expected profits, interest rates |
| Government | Taxation | Government spending | Fiscal policy decisions; tax revenue also rises with income |
| Foreign | Imports | Exports | Imports: domestic income, exchange rate. Exports: foreign incomes, exchange rate |

Withdrawals rise with domestic income; injections depend mainly on other factors. That difference is why income moves towards equilibrium.

## Limits of the model, for evaluation

- It does not show productive capacity: higher spending may raise prices instead of real output.
- Equilibrium income can be below full employment.
- Flows interact: higher saving may lower interest rates and raise investment.
- It ignores wealth and debt, which affect spending.
- Measurement is imperfect: hidden economy, different data sources and timing.

## Exam traps

- Equilibrium needs **total** J = total W, not S = I, T = G and X = M separately.
- Benefits and pensions are **transfer payments**, not G and not factor income.
- Imports are subtracted in the expenditure method because the spending leaves the domestic economy.
- Value added avoids double counting; adding sales does not.
- A rise in injections raises **nominal** income; the effect on real output depends on spare capacity.
- Leakages are not wasted: saving can fund investment and taxes fund government spending.

## Self-test

1. Name the four factor incomes.
2. State the three withdrawals and the three injections.
3. W = 300 and J = 320. In which direction will national income move?
4. Explain why national income stops rising once it has started to rise.
5. W = 0.2Y and J = 100. Calculate equilibrium income.
6. A government spends 400 on goods and services and 150 on pensions. What figure for G enters the expenditure method?
7. Ore worth 50 becomes steel worth 140, then a car worth 400. Calculate total value added.
8. C = 500, I = 120, G = 160, X = 200, M = 230. Calculate GDP by the expenditure method.
9. Give two reasons why the three measures differ in practice.
10. Can the economy be in equilibrium when the government runs a budget deficit? Explain.
11. Why is the hidden economy a problem for the income method?
12. State one limitation of the circular flow model.

### Answers

1. Wages, rent, interest and profit.
2. Withdrawals: saving, taxation, imports. Injections: investment, government spending, exports.
3. J > W, so national income **rises**.
4. Withdrawals rise with income, so W increases until it equals J.
5. Y = 100 / 0.2 = **500**.
6. **400**: pensions are transfer payments.
7. 50 + 90 + 260 = **400**, equal to the value of the car.
8. 500 + 120 + 160 + 200 - 230 = **750**.
9. Any two: hidden economy; different data sources; timing differences; changes in stocks.
10. Yes. G > T can be offset by S > I or M > X, so long as total J = total W.
11. Unrecorded incomes are not reported for tax, so measured income understates the true total.
12. Any one: ignores capacity and prices; equilibrium may be below full employment; ignores wealth and debt; treats flows as independent.
