Data for all questions: specific heat capacity of water, c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹. Assume dilute solutions have density 1.00 g cm⁻³ and the same c as water. Relative atomic masses: Mg 24.31, O 16.00, C 12.01, H 1.01, N 14.01, Cl 35.45. Round final answers to 3 s.f. unless told differently.

## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between heat and temperature. **[2]**

**2.** A sports cold pack holds solid ammonium nitrate and a sealed pouch of water. Bursting the pouch lets the solid dissolve, and the pack turns cold.

**(a)** Identify the system and the surroundings in this process. **[2]**
**(b)** State the direction of energy transfer and the sign of ΔH when the ammonium nitrate dissolves. **[2]**

**3.** Sketch a fully labelled energy profile for an exothermic reaction, showing ΔH and the activation energy, Ea. **[3]**

**4.** On an energy profile, the reactants are at 180 kJ mol⁻¹, the transition state at 245 kJ mol⁻¹ and the products at 60 kJ mol⁻¹ (relative potential energies).

**(a)** Determine ΔH. **[1]**
**(b)** Determine the activation energy. **[1]**
**(c)** State, with a reason, whether the reactants or the products are more stable. **[2]**

**5.** The temperature of 250 g of water in a beaker goes from 18.4 °C to 23.9 °C. Calculate the heat taken in by the water, in kJ. **[2]**

**6.** In an insulated cup, 55.0 cm³ of 0.900 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 55.0 cm³ of 0.900 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. The temperature rises by 6.1 K.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change of neutralisation, in kJ mol⁻¹. **[4]**
**(b)** State two assumptions made in your calculation. **[2]**

**7.** A student dissolves 6.00 g of ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl, in 60.0 g of water. The water temperature falls by 6.4 K.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change, in kJ mol⁻¹, for dissolving one mole of NH₄Cl. Assume only the water changes temperature. **[4]**
**(b)** Apart from the thermometer reading, state one observation showing that the process is endothermic. **[1]**

**8.** A 0.600 g cashew nut burns on a mounted needle beneath a metal can holding 100.0 g of water, which warms by 16.8 K. The packet gives cashews an energy content of 23.0 kJ g⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released per gram of nut in this experiment. **[3]**
**(b)** Express your answer as a percentage of the label value. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why the experimental value is lower. **[2]**

**9.** A spirit burner containing propan-1-ol, C₃H₇OH, heats 200.0 g of water in an 85.0 g copper can. The burner's mass falls by 0.420 g, and the water and can both warm by 11.2 K. A data value for the standard enthalpy change of combustion of propan-1-ol is −2021 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Using only the heat absorbed by the water, determine ΔH of combustion for propan-1-ol. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage difference between your answer and the data value. **[1]**
**(c)** Copper has a specific heat capacity of 0.385 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹. Recalculate the enthalpy change to include the heat absorbed by the can. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one change to the method that would bring the result closer to the data value, and give one reason why the result would still be less exothermic. **[2]**

**10.** Magnesium powder (0.500 g) is added to 60.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ zinc sulfate solution:

Mg(s) + ZnSO₄(aq) → MgSO₄(aq) + Zn(s)

Before mixing, the solution is steady at 19.6 °C. The highest reading recorded is 36.2 °C. Extending the cooling part of the temperature–time graph back to the time of mixing gives 37.7 °C.

**(a)** Explain why the extrapolated temperature is used rather than the highest recorded one. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that zinc sulfate is the limiting reactant. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction, in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**

**11.** At very high temperatures, nitrogen and oxygen combine: N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g). The reaction is endothermic.

**(a)** State what happens to the temperature of the surroundings as the reaction proceeds. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of the relative stability of reactants and products, why the reaction is endothermic. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline what the symbol ⦵ in ΔH⦵ tells you about the conditions. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Temperature indicates the average kinetic energy of the particles, measured in K [1]. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference, measured in J, and it depends on the amount of substance [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Make two separate points; calling temperature "the amount of heat an object holds" contradicts your own definition of heat.

**2. (a)** System: the ammonium nitrate and the water it dissolves in [1]. Surroundings: everything else, such as the casing, the air and the skin [1].
**(b)** Energy moves from surroundings to system [1]; ΔH is **positive** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The water takes part in the change here, so state what you count as the system and stay consistent.

**3.** Axes labelled reaction coordinate (horizontal) and potential energy (vertical) [1]. Product level drawn below the reactant level, with a ΔH arrow pointing down from reactants to products [1]. One peak between them, with Ea shown as an arrow rising from the reactant line to the summit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Label reactants, products, ΔH and Ea on the sketch itself; unlabelled lines are hard to credit.

**4. (a)** ΔH = 60 − 180 = **−120 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Ea = 245 − 180 = **65 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** The **products** [1], because their potential energy is lower than that of the reactants [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the sign in (a); 120 on its own does not tell the reader that energy is released.

**5.** ΔT = 23.9 − 18.4 = 5.5 K; Q = 250 × 4.18 × 5.5 = 5747.5 J [1] = **5.75 kJ** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for kJ, so 5750 J is the right energy in the wrong unit; convert before the final line.

**6. (a)** m = 55.0 + 55.0 = 110.0 g; Q = 110.0 × 4.18 × 6.1 = 2805 J = 2.805 kJ [1]
n(H₂O formed) = 0.0550 × 0.900 = 0.0495 mol (equal amounts, so neither is in excess) [1]
ΔH = −2.805 ÷ 0.0495 [1] = **−56.7 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Any two of: density of the mixture is 1.00 g cm⁻³ [1]; its specific heat capacity equals water's [1]; no energy is lost or taken up by the cup.
*Examiner insight:* Taking m as 55.0 g (one solution) halves Q; show the combined mass in your working.

**7. (a)** Q = 60.0 × 4.18 × 6.4 = 1605 J = 1.605 kJ [1]
M(NH₄Cl) = 14.01 + (4 × 1.01) + 35.45 = 53.50 g mol⁻¹; n = 6.00 ÷ 53.50 = 0.1121 mol [1]
ΔH = +1.605 ÷ 0.1121 [1] = **+14.3 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The beaker feels cold, or moisture condenses on its outside surface [1].
*Examiner insight:* The water cooled, so the answer must be positive; check your sign against what the thermometer did, not against habit.

**8. (a)** Q = 100.0 × 4.18 × 16.8 = 7022 J = 7.022 kJ [1]
Energy per gram = 7.022 ÷ 0.600 [1] = **11.7 kJ g⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** 11.7 ÷ 23.0 × 100 = **50.9 %** [1]
**(c)** Any two of: much of the flame's energy heats the air, not the water [1]; the can absorbs some energy [1]; the nut burns incompletely; energy escapes while the lit nut is moved under the can.
*Examiner insight:* "Heat loss" is one vague idea; for two marks, give two distinct routes by which energy misses the water.

**9. (a)** M(C₃H₇OH) = (3 × 12.01) + (8 × 1.01) + 16.00 = 60.11 g mol⁻¹; n = 0.420 ÷ 60.11 = 6.987 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
Q(water) = 200.0 × 4.18 × 11.2 = 9363 J = 9.363 kJ [1]
ΔH = −9.363 ÷ (6.987 × 10⁻³) [1] = **−1340 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** (2021 − 1340) ÷ 2021 × 100 = **33.7 %** [1]
**(c)** Q(can) = 85.0 × 0.385 × 11.2 = 367 J; total Q = 9363 + 367 = 9730 J [1]
ΔH = −9.730 ÷ (6.987 × 10⁻³) = **−1390 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Change: e.g. a draught shield, or a smaller gap between wick and can [1]. Remaining reason: e.g. incomplete combustion, or energy still lost to the air [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), m is the 200.0 g of water, not the 0.420 g of fuel; label each mass in your working.

**10. (a)** Energy leaks away while the reaction is still releasing it, so the recorded peak is too low [1]. Extending the cooling line back to the mixing time estimates the loss-free maximum [1].
**(b)** n(ZnSO₄) = 0.0600 × 0.250 = 0.0150 mol [1]; n(Mg) = 0.500 ÷ 24.31 = 0.02057 mol, more than the 0.0150 mol needed for 1 : 1 reaction, so ZnSO₄ is limiting [1].
**(c)** ΔT = 37.7 − 19.6 = 18.1 K; Q = 60.0 × 4.18 × 18.1 = 4539 J = 4.539 kJ [1]
ΔH = −4.539 ÷ 0.0150 [1] = **−303 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Basing ΔT on 36.2 °C gives −278 kJ mol⁻¹ and ignores the correction the question sets up; use a supplied extrapolated value.

**11. (a)** The temperature of the surroundings falls [1].
**(b)** The product, NO, is less stable (higher in potential energy) than the reactants [1]; N₂ is especially stable because of its strong triple bond, so overall energy has to be absorbed [1].
**(c)** Standard conditions: a pressure of 100 kPa, with values usually quoted at 298 K [1]; every substance in its standard state [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "it needs energy" just restates endothermic; compare the stability of reactants and products.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the wrong m: it is the liquid that warmed or cooled, never the fuel or solid.
- Dividing Q by the amount of a reagent that was left over at the end.
- Dividing a value in joules by moles and then quoting kJ mol⁻¹.
- Missing signs: an exothermic ΔH needs a minus; a falling thermometer means ΔH is positive.
- Counting only one solution's volume after mixing two.
- Ignoring an extrapolated temperature that the question supplies.
- Energy profile axes labelled "time" or "energy", or Ea measured from the product level.
