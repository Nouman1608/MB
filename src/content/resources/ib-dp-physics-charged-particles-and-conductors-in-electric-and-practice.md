See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-physics-charged-particles-and-conductors-in-electric-and/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-physics-charged-particles-and-conductors-in-electric-and-revision-notes/). Use: e = 1.60 × 10^-19 C, electron mass 9.11 × 10^-31 kg, proton mass 1.67 × 10^-27 kg, atomic mass unit u = 1.66 × 10^-27 kg, μ0 = 4π × 10^-7 T m A^-1, g = 9.81 m s^-2.

## Questions

**1.** A proton moves horizontally northwards through a uniform horizontal magnetic field that points east. State the direction of the magnetic force on the proton, and the direction it would be for an electron on the same path. **[3]**

**2.** An alpha particle (charge 3.2 × 10^-19 C, mass 6.64 × 10^-27 kg) moves at 1.8 × 10^6 m s^-1 through a uniform field of 0.50 T.

**(a)** Calculate the force on it when it moves at right angles to the field. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force when its velocity is at 40° to the field. **[2]**
**(c)** For the case in (a), calculate the radius of its circular path. **[3]**

**3.** An electron starts from rest at one of two parallel plates 2.0 cm apart, with a potential difference of 400 V between them. Treat the field as uniform.

**(a)** Calculate the electric field strength. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the electron. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate its speed on reaching the other plate. **[2]**

**4.** A charged particle moves in a circle in a uniform magnetic field. Explain why the path is circular and why the kinetic energy of the particle does not change. **[3]**

**5.** Singly charged ions of mass 20 u and 22 u, both moving at 2.0 × 10^5 m s^-1, enter a uniform field of 0.80 T at right angles and each completes a semicircle.

**(a)** Calculate the radius for the 20 u ions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the radius for the 22 u ions. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the distance between where the two kinds of ion finish their semicircles. **[2]**

**6.** Charged particles pass through a region of crossed fields, E = 1.2 × 10^4 V m^-1 and B = 0.060 T, with the electric and magnetic forces opposing.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of particles that pass straight through. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain whether a particle of twice the charge and the same speed also passes straight through. **[2]**
**(c)** A particle enters at 2.5 × 10^5 m s^-1. State, with a reason, whether it is deflected towards the direction of the electric force or the magnetic force. **[2]**

**7.** A straight, horizontal wire of mass 4.0 g and length 0.20 m lies at right angles to a horizontal uniform field of 0.080 T. A current in the wire makes the magnetic force on it balance its weight. State the condition for this and calculate the current. **[4]**

**8.** Two long straight parallel wires are 3.0 cm apart in air. They carry 15 A and 20 A in opposite directions.

**(a)** Calculate the force per unit length on each wire. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the force is attractive or repulsive. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the force on a 0.80 m length of one wire. **[1]**
**(d)** The separation is doubled and the 15 A current is doubled. Determine the new force per unit length. **[2]**

**9.** Particles pass undeflected through crossed fields E = 2.0 × 10^2 V m^-1 and B = 1.0 × 10^-3 T. The electric field is then switched off, and the particles move on a circle of radius 1.14 mm in the same magnetic field. The field is into the page and the particles move to the right.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the particles. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge-to-mass ratio. **[2]**
**(c)** The value for an electron is 1.76 × 10^11 C kg^-1. Comment on your result. **[2]**
**(d)** The particles curve downwards on the page. Deduce the sign of their charge. **[2]**

**10.** Three long parallel wires lie in one plane, 5.0 cm apart in turn. The left and middle wires carry 10 A and the right wire carries 20 A, all in the same direction.

**(a)** Calculate the force per unit length on the middle wire due to the left wire, and state its direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force per unit length on the middle wire due to the right wire, and state its direction. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the resultant force per unit length on the middle wire. **[2]**

**11.** A proton moves on a circular path of radius 0.15 m at right angles to a uniform field of 1.2 T.

**(a)** Calculate its speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its kinetic energy in joules and in electronvolts. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the time for one revolution. **[2]**


## Answers

**1.** Right-hand rule: fingers along north, curl towards east [1], thumb points down, so the force on the proton is **downward** [1]. The electron is negative, so the force is reversed: **upward** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the rule and the charge sign, so your reasoning can earn credit even if the direction is wrong.

**2. (a)** F = qvB sin 90° [1] = 3.2 × 10^-19 × 1.8 × 10^6 × 0.50 = **2.9 × 10^-13 N** [1].
**(b)** F = qvB sin 40° [1] = 2.88 × 10^-13 × 0.643 = **1.9 × 10^-13 N** [1].
**(c)** qvB = mv²/r, so r = mv/(qB) [1] = 6.64 × 10^-27 × 1.8 × 10^6 / (3.2 × 10^-19 × 0.50) [1] = **7.5 × 10^-2 m** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), use the angle between velocity and field, and show the sin θ term.

**3. (a)** E = V/d = 400 / 0.020 = **2.0 × 10^4 V m^-1** [1].
**(b)** a = eE/m [1] = 1.60 × 10^-19 × 2.0 × 10^4 / 9.11 × 10^-31 = **3.5 × 10^15 m s^-2** [1].
**(c)** v² = 2ad [1] = 2 × 3.51 × 10^15 × 0.020, so v = **1.2 × 10^7 m s^-1** [1] (1.19 × 10^7). **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values from (b) into (c).

**4.** The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity [1]. It therefore changes the direction but not the speed, so it acts as a centripetal force and the path is a circle [1]. The force does no work, so the kinetic energy is constant [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link "perpendicular to velocity" to "no work" explicitly.

**5. (a)** r = mv/(qB) [1] = 20 × 1.66 × 10^-27 × 2.0 × 10^5 / (1.60 × 10^-19 × 0.80) = **5.2 × 10^-2 m** [1].
**(b)** r = 22 × 1.66 × 10^-27 × 2.0 × 10^5 / (1.60 × 10^-19 × 0.80) = **5.7 × 10^-2 m** [1].
**(c)** Each semicircle ends a diameter from the entry point, so separation = 2(r₂ − r₁) [1] = 2 × (5.706 − 5.188) × 10^-2 = **1.0 × 10^-2 m** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use diameters, not radii.

**6. (a)** qE = qvB, so v = E/B [1] = 1.2 × 10^4 / 0.060 = **2.0 × 10^5 m s^-1** [1].
**(b)** Yes [1], because the charge cancels from qE = qvB, so the condition v = E/B does not depend on q [1].
**(c)** The magnetic force qvB is now larger than the electric force qE because v is greater than E/B [1], so the particle deflects in the direction of the **magnetic** force [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), show the cancellation; a bare "yes" gives little to reward.

**7.** The magnetic force must equal the weight, with the magnetic force upward [1]. BIL = mg [1], so I = mg/(BL) = 4.0 × 10^-3 × 9.81 / (0.080 × 0.20) [1] = **2.5 A** [1] (2.45 A). **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert grams to kilograms before substituting.

**8. (a)** F/L = μ0 I1 I2 / (2πr) [1] = 4π × 10^-7 × 15 × 20 / (2π × 0.030) = **2.0 × 10^-3 N m^-1** [1].
**(b)** **Repulsive**, because the currents are in opposite directions [1].
**(c)** F = 2.0 × 10^-3 × 0.80 = **1.6 × 10^-3 N** [1].
**(d)** The force is proportional to I1 and to 1/r, so the factor is 2 × ½ = 1 [1]. The new value is **2.0 × 10^-3 N m^-1**, unchanged [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), proportional reasoning is quicker than recalculating.

**9. (a)** v = E/B [1] = 2.0 × 10^2 / 1.0 × 10^-3 = **2.0 × 10^5 m s^-1** [1].
**(b)** q/m = v/(Br) [1] = 2.0 × 10^5 / (1.0 × 10^-3 × 1.14 × 10^-3) = **1.75 × 10^11 C kg^-1** [1].
**(c)** It agrees with the electron value to within 1% [1], so the particles are probably electrons, though a ratio match alone is not proof [1].
**(d)** For a positive charge moving right in a field into the page, fingers right and curling into the page give an upward force [1]. The particles curve downward, so the charge is **negative** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), compare with the number and qualify the conclusion. In (d), state the rule used.

**10. (a)** F/L = 4π × 10^-7 × 10 × 10 / (2π × 0.050) [1] = **4.0 × 10^-4 N m^-1**, attractive, so towards the left wire [1].
**(b)** F/L = 4π × 10^-7 × 10 × 20 / (2π × 0.050) [1] = **8.0 × 10^-4 N m^-1**, attractive, so towards the right wire [1].
**(c)** The forces are in opposite directions, so subtract: 8.0 × 10^-4 − 4.0 × 10^-4 [1] = **4.0 × 10^-4 N m^-1 towards the 20 A wire** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a direction with a vector result. Both forces are attractive but point opposite ways.

**11. (a)** qvB = mv²/r, so v = qBr/m [1] = 1.60 × 10^-19 × 1.2 × 0.15 / 1.67 × 10^-27 = **1.7 × 10^7 m s^-1** [1].
**(b)** Ek = ½mv² [1] = 0.5 × 1.67 × 10^-27 × (1.7246 × 10^7)² = **2.5 × 10^-13 J** [1]. Dividing by 1.60 × 10^-19 gives **1.6 × 10^6 eV** [1].
**(c)** T = 2πr/v [1] = 2π × 0.15 / 1.7246 × 10^7 = **5.5 × 10^-8 s** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert joules to electronvolts by dividing by e, and keep the unrounded speed.


## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a direction without naming the rule or the sign of the charge.
- Leaving out sin θ when the angle is not 90°.
- Explaining constant kinetic energy by "the field is uniform" instead of "no work is done".
- Adding the forces from two wires instead of finding the resultant with directions.
