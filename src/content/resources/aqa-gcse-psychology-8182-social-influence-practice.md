## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a dispositional factor affecting obedience? Shade one box. **[1]**

- A Proximity
- B Authoritarian Personality
- C Culture
- D Agency

**2.** Define **deindividuation**. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** social factors that affect bystander intervention. **[2]**

**4.** In Asch's study, explain why participants who wrote their answers privately conformed less. **[2]**

**5.** Outline the difference between the **autonomous state** and the **agentic state**. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Read the scenario.

*A Year 11 class is asked to estimate the number of sweets in a large jar. The first four pupils
say aloud "about 300". Priya, who thought it was about 500, also says "about 300". Later, when the
teacher asks everyone to write their estimate on paper, Priya writes 500.*

**(a)** Use your knowledge of social factors affecting conformity to explain Priya's behaviour. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how **one** dispositional factor might make a different pupil less likely to conform in this situation. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the procedure and findings of Milgram's study of obedience. **[4]**

**8.** Read the scenario.

*A security guard tells shoppers to leave a shopping centre by the side exit. Most shoppers obey.
When a teenager in a hoodie gives the same instruction, almost no one obeys.*

Use Milgram's Agency Theory to explain the difference in obedience. **[4]**

**9.** Evaluate Piliavin's subway study as evidence about bystander behaviour. **[6]**

**10.** Explain how **social loafing** might affect the behaviour of a group of six students
completing a joint project. Suggest **one** way a teacher could reduce it. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss social and dispositional explanations of obedience. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** Authoritarian Personality [1].

**2.** The loss of a sense of personal identity [1] and of normal self-restraint when a person is
anonymous in a crowd, which can lead to behaviour they would not show alone [1].

**3.** Any two: presence of others [1]; cost of helping [1]. *(Similarity to victim and expertise
are dispositional, so do not credit.)*

**4.** Answering aloud in front of the group creates pressure to fit in and avoid disapproval [1].
When answers were private, the group could not see the participant's answer, so this pressure was
removed and conformity fell [1].

**5.** In the **autonomous state** a person acts on their own conscience and feels responsible for
their actions [1]. In the **agentic state** they see themselves as acting for an authority figure,
so responsibility is passed to that person [1].

**6. (a)** Up to 4 marks: identify a relevant factor [1], link to the scenario [1], for two factors.
- **Anonymity** [1]: Priya conformed when she had to say her answer publicly but gave her own
  answer (500) when it was written privately, showing that removing public pressure reduced
  conformity [1].
- **Task difficulty** [1]: guessing sweets in a jar is hard and ambiguous, so Priya may doubt her
  own estimate and look to others [1].
- *Also creditable:* **group size** [1]: four pupils agreed before her, a large enough majority to
  create strong pressure [1].
**(b)** **Expertise** [1]: a pupil who is good at estimating quantities (for example, from maths)
would be more confident in their own answer and less influenced by the group [1]. *Or*
**personality**: a confident pupil with less need for approval would be less likely to go along with
the group [1] [1].

**7.** Up to 2 marks for procedure, 2 for findings.
**Procedure:** 40 male volunteers acted as "teacher" and were told to give a "learner" (a
confederate) electric shocks, rising in 15-volt steps to 450 volts, for each wrong answer [1]; no
real shocks were given and the experimenter used verbal prods such as "the experiment requires that
you continue" [1].
**Findings:** all participants went to at least 300 volts [1] and 65% (26 of 40) went to the
maximum 450 volts, many showing signs of distress [1].

**8.** Up to 4 marks.
- The security guard has **legitimate authority** (uniform, role in the centre) [1], so shoppers
  shift into the **agentic state** and pass responsibility for their actions to him [1].
- The teenager has no recognised authority [1], so shoppers stay in the **autonomous state**, judge
  the instruction for themselves and do not obey [1].

**9.** Level-based answer, up to 6 marks.
- *Strengths:* a field experiment in a real setting with real commuters, so **high ecological
  validity**; passengers did not know they were being studied, so their behaviour was natural (no
  demand characteristics); many trials make the findings more reliable.
- *Weaknesses:* passengers could not give **informed consent**, were deceived and were not
  **debriefed**; the researchers had little control over who was in the carriage (extraneous
  variables); the findings come from one city and one type of setting, so may not generalise.
- *Credit* explaining that the study supports the cost-reward explanation but did **not** find
  diffusion of responsibility, which limits it as evidence for the presence-of-others factor.
**5--6 marks:** two or more well-explained points on both sides with a conclusion. **3--4:**
reasonable points, some explained. **1--2:** points listed with little explanation.

**10.** Social loafing is when individuals put in less effort in a group because their own
contribution cannot be identified [1]. In a group of six, some students may do little work and rely
on the others, expecting them to finish the project [1]. To reduce it, the teacher could give each
student an identifiable task that is marked separately [1].

**11.** Level-based answer, up to 9 marks.
- **Social explanation (Agency Theory):** obedience results from the shift to the agentic state in a
  hierarchy; factors include legitimacy of authority, proximity and culture. *Evidence:* Milgram's
  variations -- obedience fell in a run-down office building, when the teacher and learner were
  close together and when orders came by telephone.
- **Dispositional explanation (Adorno):** harsh, strict upbringing produces an authoritarian
  personality, measured by the F-scale, with deference to authority. *Evidence:* obedient
  participants in Milgram-type research tended to score higher on the F-scale.
- **Discussion:** Agency Theory explains why obedience levels change when the situation changes,
  which a fixed personality cannot; Adorno explains why some people resist in the same situation;
  the authoritarian personality cannot explain obedience across a whole population; F-scale
  evidence is correlational. A strong conclusion argues that both are needed, with situation having
  the larger effect on **levels** of obedience and personality explaining **individual
  differences**.
**7--9 marks:** accurate knowledge of both explanations, effective discussion with evidence and a
reasoned conclusion. **4--6:** both explanations described, some discussion. **1--3:** limited or
one-sided.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a dispositional factor when the question asks for a social one, or the reverse.
- Not using the scenario in application questions (Questions 6, 8 and 10).
- Describing Milgram without any findings, or giving findings with no procedure.
- Evaluating with generic phrases ("it lacks validity") instead of explaining why.
