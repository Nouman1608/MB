## Questions

**1.** The ethyl ester of the amino acid phenylalanine has the condensed formula C₆H₅CH₂CH(NH₂)COOCH₂CH₃. Identify the three functional groups in this molecule. **[3]**

**2.** Hexanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH, is used to make nylon.

**(a)** State its molecular formula. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce its empirical formula. **[1]**
**(c)** State how many hydrogen atoms are not drawn in its skeletal formula. **[1]**
**(d)** State, with a reason, whether hexanedioic acid is saturated or unsaturated. **[1]**

**3.** State the homologous series to which each compound belongs. **[5]**

(a) C₇H₁₆ (b) CH₃CH₂CH₂CONH₂ (c) CH₃(CH₂)₄Br (d) CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ (e) CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂

**4.** A chloroalkane, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁Cl, has a molar mass of 106.61 g mol⁻¹. Use Ar values C = 12.01, H = 1.01, Cl = 35.45.

**(a)** Determine the value of n. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the condensed structural formula and the IUPAC name of the tertiary isomer with this molecular formula. **[2]**

**5.** Approximate boiling points of four straight-chain aldehydes are given. Use these values.

| Aldehyde | propanal | butanal | pentanal | hexanal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boiling point / K | 321 | 348 | 376 | 403 |

**(a)** Describe the trend and calculate the mean increase in boiling point per CH₂ unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the trend. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the boiling point of heptanal. **[1]**
**(d)** 2-methylbutanal is an isomer of pentanal. Predict whether its boiling point is above or below 376 K, and give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Give the IUPAC name of each compound. **[5]**

(a) (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₂CH₂CH₃ (b) CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)(OH)CH₂CH₃ (c) (CH₃)₂CHCH(CH₃)CHO (d) CH₂=CHCH₂COCH₃ (e) CH₃CCl₂CH₂CH₃

**7.** A student wrote these names. For each one, state whether it is correct and, if not, give the correct name. **[4]**

(a) 4,4-dimethylbutanal (b) 2-propylpropanoic acid (c) hex-5-en-3-ol for CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH=CH₂ (d) 2-methylbutan-3-one

**8.** For each pair, state the type of structural isomerism shown, or state that the compounds are not isomers. **[4]**

(a) pentan-2-ol and pentan-3-ol
(b) hexanoic acid and ethyl butanoate
(c) 1-chlorohexane and 1-chloro-3-methylpentane
(d) hexane and cyclohexane

**9.** Classify each compound as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH(CH₃)₂ **[1]**
**(b)** (CH₃)₃CCH₂CH₂Br **[1]**
**(c)** (CH₃CH₂)₃CCl **[1]**
**(d)** (CH₃)₂CHNHCH₃ **[1]**
**(e)** CH₃CH₂CH(NH₂)CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(f)** Explain your answer to (e). **[1]**

**10.** Several compounds have the molecular formula C₅H₁₀O₂.

**(a)** Show that C₅H₁₀O₂ fits the general formula of the carboxylic acids. **[1]**
**(b)** Pentanoic acid is the straight-chain acid. Write the condensed formulas and IUPAC names of two branched carboxylic acids with this formula. **[4]**
**(c)** State the type of structural isomerism between pentanoic acid and your answers to (b). **[1]**
**(d)** Methyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃, also has this formula. Name its functional group and state the type of isomerism it shows with pentanoic acid. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why pentanoic acid has a much higher boiling point than methyl butanoate. **[3]**

**11.** Four compounds, A to D, all have the molecular formula C₆H₁₂O.

A: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CHO
B: CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₂CH₃
C: (CH₃)₃CCH₂CHO
D: HOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH=CHCH₃

**(a)** Give the IUPAC name of each compound. **[4]**
**(b)** State the type of structural isomerism between A and B, and between A and C. **[2]**
**(c)** D is unsaturated. State what this means. **[1]**
**(d)** Predict which of A and C has the higher boiling point. Explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **Phenyl** (C₆H₅–) [1]; **amino** (–NH₂) [1]; **ester** (–COO–CH₂CH₃) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the functional group, not the class: "amino" and "ester" are what the question asks for, while "amine" or "benzene" answer a different question.

**2. (a)** **C₆H₁₀O₄** [1]
**(b)** 6 : 10 : 4 has a common factor of 2, so **C₃H₅O₂** [1]
**(c)** The four CH₂ groups carry **8** hydrogen atoms, which are not drawn; the two O–H hydrogens are shown [1]
**(d)** **Saturated**: every carbon–carbon bond is single; the C=O bonds are not carbon–carbon bonds [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), remember that hydrogen atoms bonded to oxygen stay in a skeletal formula, so counting all ten is a slip.

**3.** (a) **alkane** [1] (b) **amide** [1] (c) **halogenoalkane** [1] (d) **ester** [1] (e) **amine** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b) and (e), check whether the nitrogen is bonded to a C=O carbon: CONH₂ is an amide and NH₂ alone is an amine.

**4. (a)** 14.03n + 1.01 + 35.45 = 106.61 [1]; 14.03n = 70.15, so **n = 5** (C₅H₁₁Cl) [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂Cl** [1]; **2-chloro-2-methylbutane** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the equation from the general formula before solving it; a bare "n = 5" gives the reader no method to follow.

**5. (a)** Boiling point **increases** as the chain gets longer [1]; differences 27, 28 and 27 K, so the mean is **about 27 K** per CH₂ [1]
**(b)** Each successive member has one more CH₂, so more electrons and a larger surface area [1]. London (dispersion) forces between molecules become stronger [1]. More energy is needed to separate the molecules, so the boiling point is higher [1]
**(c)** 403 + 27 = **about 430 K** [1]
**(d)** **Below** 376 K [1]; the branched molecule is more compact, so there is less surface contact and the London forces are weaker [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the forces being overcome are between molecules; writing that covalent bonds break on boiling contradicts the explanation.

**6.** (a) **2-methylhex-2-ene** [1] (b) **3-methylpentan-3-ol** [1] (c) **2,3-dimethylbutanal** [1] (d) **pent-4-en-2-one** [1] (e) **2,2-dichlorobutane** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), the ketone suffix takes the lowest locant before the C=C does, so numbering from the C=C end gives the wrong name.

**7.** (a) Incorrect: the chain extends into a methyl group, so it is **4-methylpentanal** [1]
(b) Incorrect: the longest chain containing COOH has five carbons, so it is **2-methylpentanoic acid** [1]
(c) **Correct**: the -ol gets the lowest locant (3, not 4) before the C=C is considered [1]
(d) Incorrect: the carbonyl must have the lower number, so it is **3-methylbutan-2-one** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A name with a substituent on the last chain carbon, or an alkyl prefix longer than the chain beyond it, is a sign that the longest chain has been missed.

**8.** (a) **Position** isomers [1] (b) **Functional group** isomers, both C₆H₁₂O₂ [1] (c) **Branched and straight-chain** (chain) isomers, both C₆H₁₃Cl [1] (d) **Not isomers**: hexane is C₆H₁₄ and cyclohexane is C₆H₁₂ [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write out both molecular formulas before naming an isomer type; in (d), that check alone gives the answer.

**9. (a)** **Secondary**: the C–OH carbon is bonded to two carbons [1]
**(b)** **Primary**: the C–Br carbon is bonded to one carbon [1]
**(c)** **Tertiary**: the C–Cl carbon is bonded to three carbons [1]
**(d)** **Secondary** amine: the nitrogen is bonded to two carbons [1]
**(e)** **Primary** amine [1]
**(f)** For amines you count the carbon atoms bonded to the nitrogen, and here the N is bonded to only one carbon [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (e), counting the carbons around the C–N carbon, as you would for an alcohol, leads to "secondary", which is the wrong rule for amines.

**10. (a)** CₙH₂ₙO₂ with n = 5 gives 2 × 5 = 10 hydrogen atoms, so C₅H₁₀O₂ fits [1]
**(b)** Any two of: **CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH** [1], **2-methylbutanoic acid** [1]; **(CH₃)₂CHCH₂COOH** [1], **3-methylbutanoic acid** [1]; or (CH₃)₃CCOOH, 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid
**(c)** **Branched and straight-chain** (chain) isomerism [1]
**(d)** **Ester** [1]; **functional group** isomerism [1]
**(e)** Pentanoic acid has an O–H in its carboxyl group, so its molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other [1]. Methyl butanoate has no O–H, so only dipole–dipole and London forces act between its molecules [1]. Breaking the stronger hydrogen bonds between acid molecules takes more energy, so the acid boils higher [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), each formula and its name should match; a correct name attached to a different structure does not show the skill.

**11. (a)** A **hexanal** [1]; B **hexan-3-one** [1]; C **3,3-dimethylbutanal** [1]; D **hex-4-en-1-ol** [1]
**(b)** A and B: **functional group** isomers (aldehyde and ketone) [1]; A and C: **branched and straight-chain** (chain) isomers [1]
**(c)** The molecule contains at least one carbon–carbon multiple bond (here a C=C) [1]
**(d)** **A** [1]; its straight chain gives more surface contact than the compact branched C, so its London forces are stronger [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), aldehyde names carry no number for the CHO group, but a ketone such as B always needs one to show where the C=O sits.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the class (amine, ester compound) when the question asks for the functional group.
- Leaving a common factor in an empirical formula.
- Forgetting the hydrogen atom of –OH when counting atoms from a skeletal formula.
- Treating a C=O bond as making a compound unsaturated.
- Choosing the longest chain without checking that it contains the functional group or the C=C.
- Numbering so a C=C or methyl gets the lowest number when an -ol, -one or -oic acid is present.
- Explaining boiling points by covalent bonds breaking.
- Classifying amines by the carbon atom instead of the nitrogen atom.
- Calling compounds isomers without first checking their molecular formulas.
