## Section A

**1.** Differentiate y = 2x^(3/2) − 4x^(−1/2). **[2]**

**2.** Differentiate y = 5 sin x + 2eˣ − 3 ln x. **[2]**

**3.** Differentiate y = (1 − 4x)⁷. **[2]**

**4.** Differentiate y = ln(x³ − 2x). **[2]**

**5.** Differentiate y = e^(2x) sin x, giving your answer in factorised form. **[3]**

**6.** Differentiate y = x²/(x + 3), giving your answer as a single fraction in factorised form.
**[3]**

**7.** Differentiate y = cos(3x² + 1). **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** f(x) = x² e^(−x).

**(a)** Show that f′(x) = x e^(−x)(2 − x). **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the two stationary points of f. **[3]**

**9.** y = (ln x)/x², x > 0.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = (1 − 2 ln x)/x³. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact x-coordinate of the stationary point. **[2]**

**10.** f(x) = √(x² + 9).

**(a)** Find f′(x). **[2]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the tangent to y = f(x) at x = 4, in the form ax + by + d = 0, where
a, b, d ∈ ℤ. **[3]**

**11.** y = sin²(2x).

**(a)** Find dy/dx. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence show that dy/dx = 2 sin(4x). **[1]**

**12.** f(x) = (x − 1)/eˣ.

**(a)** Use the quotient rule to show that f′(x) = (2 − x)/eˣ. **[3]**

**(b)** Write f(x) as a product and confirm the result of (a) with the product rule. **[2]**

**13.** f(x) = eˣ/(x² + 1).

**(a)** Show that f′(x) = eˣ(x − 1)²/(x² + 1)². **[3]**

**(b)** Hence explain why f is never decreasing, and state where its gradient is zero. **[2]**

**14.** y = ln(cos x), for 0 < x < π/2.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = −tan x. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the gradient of the curve at x = π/4. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** dy/dx = 3x^(1/2) [1] + 2x^(−3/2) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** dy/dx = 5 cos x + 2eˣ − 3/x [2: 1 for any two correct terms]. **[2]**

**3.** 7(1 − 4x)⁶ × (−4) [1] = −28(1 − 4x)⁶ [1]. **[2]**

**4.** dy/dx = (3x² − 2)/(x³ − 2x) [1 for numerator, 1 for denominator]. **[2]**

**5.** u = e^(2x), u′ = 2e^(2x); v = sin x, v′ = cos x [1].
dy/dx = e^(2x) cos x + 2e^(2x) sin x [1] = e^(2x)(2 sin x + cos x) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** dy/dx = [(x + 3)(2x) − x²(1)]/(x + 3)² [1] = (x² + 6x)/(x + 3)² [1] = x(x + 6)/(x + 3)² [1].
**[3]**

**7.** dy/dx = −sin(3x² + 1) × 6x [1] = −6x sin(3x² + 1) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** u = x², u′ = 2x; v = e^(−x), v′ = −e^(−x) [1]. f′(x) = 2x e^(−x) − x² e^(−x) [1]
= x e^(−x)(2 − x) [1].

**(b)** e^(−x) ≠ 0, so x = 0 or x = 2 [1]. f(0) = 0, giving (0, 0) [1]; f(2) = 4e^(−2), giving
(2, 4e^(−2)) [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** u = ln x, u′ = 1/x; v = x², v′ = 2x [1].
dy/dx = [x²(1/x) − 2x ln x]/x⁴ [1] = (x − 2x ln x)/x⁴ = (1 − 2 ln x)/x³ [1].

**(b)** 1 − 2 ln x = 0, so ln x = 1/2 [1] and x = e^(1/2) = √e [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** f(x) = (x² + 9)^(1/2); f′(x) = (1/2)(x² + 9)^(−1/2) × 2x [1] = x/√(x² + 9) [1].

**(b)** f(4) = 5 and f′(4) = 4/5 [1]. y − 5 = (4/5)(x − 4) [1], so 5y − 25 = 4x − 16 and
4x − 5y + 9 = 0 [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** y = (sin 2x)²; outer derivative 2 sin 2x [1], times the derivative of sin 2x, which is
2 cos 2x [1]: dy/dx = 4 sin 2x cos 2x [1].

**(b)** 4 sin 2x cos 2x = 2(2 sin 2x cos 2x) = 2 sin 4x, using sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A with A = 2x [1].
**[4]**

**12. (a)** u = x − 1, u′ = 1; v = eˣ, v′ = eˣ [1]. f′(x) = [eˣ − (x − 1)eˣ]/e^(2x) [1]
= eˣ(2 − x)/e^(2x) = (2 − x)/eˣ [1].

**(b)** f(x) = (x − 1)e^(−x), so f′(x) = e^(−x) + (x − 1)(−e^(−x)) [1] = e^(−x)(2 − x), which equals
(2 − x)/eˣ [1]. **[5]**

**13. (a)** u = eˣ, u′ = eˣ; v = x² + 1, v′ = 2x [1]. f′(x) = [(x² + 1)eˣ − 2x eˣ]/(x² + 1)² [1]
= eˣ(x² − 2x + 1)/(x² + 1)² = eˣ(x − 1)²/(x² + 1)² [1].

**(b)** eˣ > 0, (x − 1)² ≥ 0 and (x² + 1)² > 0, so f′(x) ≥ 0 for all x and f is never decreasing [1].
The gradient is zero only at x = 1 [1]. **[5]**

**14. (a)** Chain rule with u = cos x: dy/dx = (1/cos x) × (−sin x) [1] = −sin x/cos x = −tan x [1].

**(b)** −tan(π/4) = −1 [1]. **[3]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 and 2 check the standard derivatives, including rational powers. Questions 3, 4 and 7
isolate the chain rule; 5 and 6 the product and quotient rules. Section B combines rules and then uses
the derivative: stationary points (8, 9), a tangent (10), a double angle identity (11), and the same
derivative found two ways (12), which is a good habit for checking work. Questions 13 and 14 use a factorised derivative to
reason about the sign of a gradient, and link ln to a trigonometric function.
