Condensed recall for section 4.11 (Similarity) of the 4MA1 specification. Similar figures, scale factors, maps and scale drawings are Foundation Tier content for both tiers. The ratios of areas and volumes of similar figures, and problems that use them, are **Higher tier only**. Worked examples are in the [Similarity study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-similarity/), and exam-style questions are in the [Similarity practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-similarity-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Similar | Same shape, different size: one figure is an enlargement of the other |
| Congruent | Identical in shape and size |
| Corresponding | Matching sides or angles in two similar figures |
| Scale factor | The number you multiply lengths by to get the new figure |
| Scale | The ratio of a length on a map or drawing to the real length |
| Ratio of areas | Area of one figure : area of the other |

## Similar figures (A)

1. Corresponding angles are equal.
2. Corresponding lengths are in the same ratio, the scale factor.
3. **Scale factor = new length ÷ old length.**
4. For triangles, two equal angles are enough to prove similarity.
5. For other shapes, check both angles and side ratios. A square and a rectangle are not similar.

**Check for rectangles:** 6 by 4 and 9 by 6 are similar (9 ÷ 6 = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5). 6 by 4 and 10 by 7 are not (1.67 against 1.75).

## Setting out a similar-triangles question

1. Mark the equal angles on a sketch.
2. List corresponding sides in matching order, such as ABC and PQR.
3. Find the scale factor from a pair where both lengths are known.
4. Multiply or divide to find the missing length.

**Example:** ABC and PQR similar, AB = 6, BC = 8, AC = 10, PQ = 9. Scale factor 1.5, so QR = 12 and PR = 15.

## Triangles in diagrams

| Situation | Similar triangles |
|---|---|
| DE parallel to BC in triangle ABC | ADE and ABC (angle A is common) |
| Two lines crossing between parallel lines | Triangles either side of the crossing point |
| Shadows at the same time of day | Object and shadow against a second object and its shadow |

**Parallel line example:** AD = 4, DB = 2, DE = 5. Then AB = 6 and AD ÷ AB = DE ÷ BC gives BC = 7.5.

Use the **whole** side of the large triangle (AB), not the part DB.

## Maps and scale drawings (B)

| Scale | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 : 50 000 | 1 cm on the map = 50 000 cm = 0.5 km |
| 1 : 25 000 | 1 cm on the map = 250 m |
| 1 cm to 60 cm | A room 480 cm long is 8 cm on the plan |

- Map to real: **multiply** by the scale number, then convert units.
- Real to map: **divide** by the scale number.
- 100 000 cm = 1 km. 100 cm = 1 m.

Example: on a 1 : 50 000 map, 3.4 cm is 170 000 cm = 1.7 km. A real distance of 4.5 km is 9 cm on the map.

## Area and volume (Higher tier only)

If the length scale factor is k:

| Quantity | Factor | Ratio form |
|---|---|---|
| Length, perimeter, height | k | 1 : k |
| Area, surface area | k² | 1 : k² |
| Volume | k³ | 1 : k³ |

**From the ratio of areas or volumes to lengths:** take the square root of the area ratio, or the cube root of the volume ratio.

| Given | Lengths | Areas | Volumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lengths 2 : 3 | 2 : 3 | 4 : 9 | 8 : 27 |
| Areas 9 : 25 | 3 : 5 | 9 : 25 | 27 : 125 |
| Volumes 8 : 27 | 2 : 3 | 4 : 9 | 8 : 27 |

**Examples**

- Lengths 2 : 5, small area 12 cm². Area factor 2.5² = 6.25, so the large area is 75 cm².
- Heights 12 cm and 18 cm, small jug 240 ml. Volume factor 1.5³ = 3.375, so the large jug holds 810 ml.
- Label areas 50 and 128, small tin 250 ml. Length factor √2.56 = 1.6, volume factor 4.096, large tin 1024 ml.
- Model at 1 : 50 with surface area 0.18 m². Real area 0.18 × 2500 = 450 m².
- Map at 1 : 50 000: 1 cm² represents 0.25 km², so 8 cm² is 2 km².

## Quick chains

- k = 3: lengths × 3, areas × 9, volumes × 27.
- k = 1/2: lengths × 1/2, areas × 1/4, volumes × 1/8.
- Volume factor 64: length factor 4, area factor 16.
- Area factor 49: length factor 7, volume factor 343.

## Exam traps

- Pairing sides that do not match.
- Using AD ÷ DB instead of AD ÷ AB.
- Applying k to an area or a volume.
- Forgetting that perimeter follows k, not k².
- Taking a square root for a volume ratio, or a cube root for an area ratio.
- Leaving map answers in cm when metres or kilometres are asked for.
- Treating a shape as similar when only one dimension has been scaled.

## Self-test

1. Triangles are similar with scale factor 2.5. A side of 8 cm in the small triangle becomes what length in the large one?
2. Rectangles 8 cm by 5 cm and 12 cm by 7.5 cm: are they similar?
3. A map has scale 1 : 20 000. A path is 7.5 cm on the map. Find the real length in metres.
4. A real road is 2.4 km long. Find its length on a 1 : 40 000 map.
5. A 1.5 m post casts a 2 m shadow. A flagpole casts a 9 m shadow at the same time. Find its height.
6. DE is parallel to BC. AD = 3 cm, AB = 9 cm and DE = 4 cm. Find BC.
7. (Higher) Two similar shapes have lengths in the ratio 1 : 4. What is the ratio of their areas?
8. (Higher) Two similar solids have lengths in the ratio 2 : 3. The smaller has volume 80 cm³. Find the larger volume.
9. (Higher) Two similar shapes have areas 20 cm² and 45 cm². Find the ratio of their lengths.
10. (Higher) Two similar bottles have volumes 125 ml and 216 ml. The smaller bottle has a surface area of 100 cm². Find the surface area of the larger.
11. (Higher) A 1 : 25 model has a volume of 0.4 litres. Find the real volume in litres.
12. (Higher) The perimeter of a shape is 24 cm. A similar shape has area four times as big. Find its perimeter.

### Answers

1. 8 × 2.5 = 20 cm.
2. 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 and 7.5 ÷ 5 = 1.5, so they are similar.
3. 7.5 × 20 000 = 150 000 cm = 1500 m.
4. 240 000 cm ÷ 40 000 = 6 cm.
5. 1.5 × 9 ÷ 2 = 6.75 m.
6. AB ÷ AD = 3, so BC = 4 × 3 = 12 cm.
7. 1 : 16.
8. 80 × (3/2)³ = 80 × 3.375 = 270 cm³.
9. Area ratio 20 : 45 = 4 : 9, so lengths 2 : 3.
10. Length ratio 5 : 6 (cube roots of 125 and 216), so areas 25 : 36, giving 100 × 36/25 = 144 cm².
11. 0.4 × 25³ = 0.4 × 15 625 = 6250 litres.
12. Area factor 4 means length factor 2, so the perimeter is 48 cm.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to prove triangles are similar in 4MA1?**
You must understand and use the properties of similar figures. When a question says "show that", give the equal angles with their reasons, for example "corresponding angles are equal".

**What is the difference between Foundation and Higher on this section?**
Both tiers use scale factors, similar triangles, maps and scale drawings. Only the Higher Tier uses the squares and cubes of the scale factor for areas and volumes.

**Is a congruent figure also similar?**
Yes, with a scale factor of 1.
