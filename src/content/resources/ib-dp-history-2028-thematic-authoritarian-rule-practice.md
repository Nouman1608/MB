These tasks follow the paper 2 structure for the thematic study of authoritarian rule: a concept question (6 marks), a short response (4 marks) and an essay (15 marks). The study prescribes no examples, so the suggested answers are one route only. Any accurate and relevant examples earn credit.

## Section A: concepts

**1.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of cause and consequence. **[6]**

**2.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of perspectives. **[6]**

## Section B: short responses

**3.** Explain how propaganda helped one authoritarian ruler or regime to maintain power. **[4]**

**4.** Explain the experiences of one marginalised group under an authoritarian regime you have studied. **[4]**

## Section B: essays

**5.** To what extent did economic crisis explain the emergence of authoritarian rule? Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

**6.** Evaluate the importance of popular resistance in challenging authoritarian rule. Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

## Section B: a further short response

**7.** Explain one way in which an external threat challenged an authoritarian regime. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a definition: the interplay of multiple reasons for, and results of, events, including actors and conditions **[1]**;
- a specific example, for example the rise of the Nazis to power in 1933, with dates **[1]**;
- analysis of causes: conditions (the Depression, the legacy of the First World War, weaknesses in the Weimar system) and actors (Hitler, conservative politicians who brought him into office in January 1933) **[2]**;
- analysis of consequences: the Enabling Act, the end of political parties, persecution of Jews and other groups, and war **[1]**;
- a judgement about whether the outcome was inevitable **[1]**.
Bands: 1 to 2 describes the concept with minimal analysis; 3 to 4 partial analysis with a relevant example; 5 to 6 clear and accurate analysis with a specific example.

**2.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a definition: diverse points of view from and about the past **[1]**;
- a specific example, for example Stalin's USSR or the Congo Free State **[1]**;
- identification of perspectives: for the Congo Free State, Leopold's administration, concession companies, African communities, missionaries and reformers such as E. D. Morel **[2]**;
- analysis of validity, corroboration and contradiction, for example how missionary and photographic evidence contradicted official claims **[1]**;
- a judgement about which perspectives are best supported **[1]**.

**3.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a relevant regime, such as Nazi Germany **[1]**;
- description of propaganda methods, such as Goebbels' control of the press, film and radio, rallies and posters **[1]**;
- explanation of how propaganda built support, for example by promoting the Hitler cult and national unity **[1]**;
- explanation of a limit or of how it worked with other methods, for example that propaganda was backed by terror and could not hide all hardships **[1]**.

**4.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a group and regime, for example Jews in Nazi Germany **[1]**;
- description of discrimination, for example the Nuremberg Laws of 1935 removing citizenship **[1]**;
- explanation of how the experience changed over time, from legal exclusion and boycotts to deportation and murder **[1]**;
- explanation of an effect on community or response, for example emigration, resistance or loss of livelihood **[1]**.

**5.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a thesis, for example that economic crisis created conditions in which authoritarian rule was attractive, but political choices and ideas decided the outcome **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions, such as Germany after 1929 (Europe) and Chile in 1973 (Americas) **[3]**;
- analyse economic factors with specific evidence such as mass unemployment in Germany and shortages and inflation in Chile **[4]**;
- connect examples and weigh other factors: ideas (fascism, anti-communism), conflict and political decisions; consider an example in which crisis was not the main factor, such as colonial rule established by conquest **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement **[2]**.

**6.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a thesis, for example that popular resistance was important when it was widespread and combined with other pressures, but alone rarely brought down a regime **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions, such as the Salt March and the wider campaigns against British rule (Asia) and the "No" campaign in Chile (Americas) **[3]**;
- analyse resistance with specific evidence, for example the mass participation and international attention of the Salt March, or the registration of voters and the 1988 plebiscite in Chile **[4]**;
- compare with other factors (internal opposition, external threats, the cost of ruling) and connect the examples **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement **[2]**.

**7.** Marking points (4). Award: identification of a relevant regime and threat, such as defeat in war for Nazi Germany **[1]**; accurate description, such as the Allied advance in 1944 and 1945 and the Soviet entry into Berlin **[1]**; explanation of how it ended the regime's control, such as the collapse of the army and state and the death of Hitler in April 1945 **[1]**; explanation of an additional factor or limit, such as that the regime had survived internal opposition and that only defeat in war removed it **[1]**.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I handle estimates of deaths?** Use "about" or give a range, and say that historians' estimates differ.

**Can I mention one regime in two regions?** No. An example represents only one region in a response.
