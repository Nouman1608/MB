## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify **two** services usually provided by local government. **[2]**

**2.** Which of the following is an example of the government acting at the international level?

A: a council granting planning permission for a supermarket

B: a national income tax rise

C: several countries agreeing limits on catches in a shared sea

D: a city council running a bus service

**[1]**

**3.** State **one** reason why governments provide public goods. **[1]**

**4.** A town council spends 60 million a year, of which 21 million goes on schools. Calculate the share spent on schools. **[1]**

**5.** Which role is the government performing when it sets a minimum wage?

A: provider

B: regulator

C: producer

D: international partner

**[1]**

**6.** State **two** ways a national government may redistribute income. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** In Country K, government spending is 84 billion, GDP is 280 billion and the government employs 2.4 million people out of a workforce of 16 million.

**(a)** Calculate government spending as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the workforce employed by the government. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** reasons why the government is a large employer in many countries. **[4]**

**8.** A district council spends 32 million a year. It raises 20 million from local taxes and receives the rest from national government.

**(a)** Calculate the grant from national government and its share of the council's spending. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage for the district of relying on this grant. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why national government, rather than local government, manages interest rates and national taxes. **[4]**

**10.** Factories in Country X release chemicals into a river that flows through Countries Y and Z. Explain why Countries Y and Z cannot solve this problem by acting alone. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** roles of government in correcting market failure. **[6]**

**12.** Discuss whether local government or national government should be responsible for providing schools. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: refuse collection [1]; local roads [1]; parks; libraries; street lighting; planning permission. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A and D are local actions; B is national.

**3.** Firms cannot charge non-payers, who cannot be excluded, so the market would not supply them [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 21 / 60 x 100 = **35%** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** B [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two: progressive income taxes [1]; welfare benefits such as unemployment pay or pensions [1]; free or subsidised public services for low-income households. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 84 / 280 x 100 = **30%** [1].

**(b)** 2.4 / 16 x 100 = **15%** [1].

**(c)** Governments provide services such as health care and education directly [1], which need large numbers of teachers, doctors and nurses [1]. Governments also run defence, police and the civil service [1], which are public goods or core state functions that only the government provides, so all their staff are public employees [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Grant = 32 - 20 = **12 million** [1]; 12 / 32 x 100 = **37.5%** [1].

**(b)** If national government cuts the grant, the council must cut services or raise local taxes [1], so local services depend on decisions made outside the district [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Interest rates and national taxes affect the whole economy, so they must be set for the whole country [1]. If each local area set different rates, money and firms would move between areas, causing confusion [1]. National government needs these tools to pursue the macroeconomic aims, such as low inflation and low unemployment [1], which are national problems that one local council cannot solve [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The pollution is an external cost created by firms in Country X [1]. Countries Y and Z have no power to tax or regulate firms in Country X [1]. Even if Y and Z tighten their own rules, the polluted water still arrives from upstream [1]. Only an agreement that includes Country X, with shared limits and monitoring, can reduce the pollution at its source [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Provider: public goods such as street lighting would not be supplied by markets because non-payers cannot be excluded [1]; the government provides them from taxation [1], so a good valued by society is supplied [1].
Regulator: firms ignore the external costs of pollution when deciding output [1]; the government sets legal limits on emissions [1], forcing firms to reduce harm to third parties and moving output closer to what is best for society [1]. (Accept merit goods, demerit goods or monopoly with a developed role.) **[6]**

**12.** Local: councils understand local needs, such as where population is growing and new schools are needed [1]; parents can hold local officials to account for the quality of schools [1].
National: education is a merit good with large external benefits for the whole economy, so national funding ensures every area can afford good schools [1]; national standards mean all pupils have similar opportunities, wherever they live [1]; poorer districts may raise too little in local taxes [1].
Judgement: a shared approach works best: national government sets standards and provides most funding, while local government plans and runs schools to meet local needs [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a service without saying why government rather than the market provides it.
- Confusing local and national roles.
- Describing the international role only as trade.
- Forgetting to explain why one country acting alone cannot solve a cross-border problem.
- Ending a "discuss" question without stating which level should act and why.
