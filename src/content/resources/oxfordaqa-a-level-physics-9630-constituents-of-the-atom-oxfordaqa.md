## The simple model and its scale

An atom is a tiny nucleus, containing protons and neutrons (together called nucleons), surrounded by electrons. The radius of an atom is of order 10⁻¹⁰ m and the radius of a nucleus of order 10⁻¹⁵ m, so the atom is about 10⁵ times wider than its nucleus. Because volume depends on radius cubed, the atom's volume is about 10¹⁵ times the nucleus's, yet nearly all the mass is in the nucleus.

**Worked example 1: picturing the scale.** If a nucleus were enlarged to the size of a bead of radius 1 mm, how far away would the edge of the atom be?

Scale factor for radius = 10⁵, so the atom's radius = 10⁵ × 1 mm = 100 m. A bead in the centre of a sports field, with electrons at the far edge and empty space between, is a fair picture.

**Worked example 2: why electron mass is usually ignored.** Find the fraction of the mass of a carbon-12 atom (6 protons, 6 neutrons, 6 electrons) that is due to its electrons.

Electron mass = 6 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 5.47 × 10⁻³⁰ kg. Nucleus mass = 12 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 2.00 × 10⁻²⁶ kg.

Fraction = 5.47 × 10⁻³⁰ / (2.00 × 10⁻²⁶ + 5.47 × 10⁻³⁰) = 2.7 × 10⁻⁴, about 0.03%. This is why the mass of an ion is usually taken as A × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, unless a question asks you to include the electrons.

## Rutherford scattering as evidence

The experiment, carried out by Geiger and Marsden under Rutherford's direction, fired alpha particles at a thin gold foil in a vacuum and counted them at different angles. It is worth knowing why each choice was made:

- **Alpha particles** are positive, so they are repelled by positive charge, and they are massive and fast, so only a strong, concentrated force can turn them back.
- **Gold** can be beaten into an extremely thin foil, so each alpha particle is likely to be deflected by at most one nucleus. Gold nuclei also carry a large positive charge.
- **A vacuum** stops the alpha particles being absorbed or scattered by air molecules before they reach the foil or detector.

The three results and what each shows:

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Almost all alpha particles pass straight through or are deflected by small angles | Most of the atom is empty space |
| A small fraction are deflected through large angles | The positive charge is concentrated in a very small volume |
| A very few are deflected by more than 90° | The concentrated region contains most of the atom's mass |

### Predicting what changes

Examiners test understanding by changing the set-up. Reason from the repulsive force each time:

- *Foil of a lower proton number (such as aluminium):* each nucleus has less charge, so the repulsion is weaker and fewer particles are deflected through large angles.
- *Slower alpha particles:* with less kinetic energy, a given force deflects them more, so more large-angle deflections.
- *Much thicker foil:* particles may be deflected several times, so the pattern no longer shows the effect of single nuclei, and more particles are absorbed.

## How the model of the atom changed

The specification asks you to appreciate that scientific knowledge changes as evidence accumulates. A clear sequence:

1. **Electron identified (Thomson, 1897).** Atoms contain small negative particles, so they are not indivisible.
2. **Plum pudding model.** Positive charge spread through the atom with electrons embedded in it. With charge spread out, the electric field inside is never strong enough to deflect an alpha particle by a large angle.
3. **Nuclear model (Rutherford, 1911).** Large-angle scattering contradicts the plum pudding prediction, so the positive charge and mass must be in a tiny nucleus.
4. **Neutron identified (Chadwick, 1932).** The mass of most nuclei is roughly twice, or more than twice, what their protons alone would give. An uncharged nuclear particle of similar mass to the proton explains this.

The pattern to describe in an answer: a model makes a prediction; an experiment tests it; if the result contradicts the prediction, the model is modified or replaced.

## Charge and mass of the particles

| Particle | Charge / C | Relative charge | Mass / kg | Relative mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | +1 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 1 |
| Neutron | 0 | 0 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 1 |
| Electron | −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | −1 | 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ | about 1/1830 |

The proton and electron carry charges of equal size and opposite sign; the proton is about 1830 times more massive.

## Specific charge

Specific charge = charge / mass, unit C kg⁻¹. It is large for particles that carry a lot of charge for little mass.

**Worked example 3: ranking.** Rank the electron, proton, alpha particle and uranium-238 nucleus (Z = 92) by the size of their specific charge.

- Electron: 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹ (negative)
- Proton: 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 9.58 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹
- Alpha particle (2 protons, 4 nucleons): 2/4 of the proton value = 4.79 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹
- Uranium-238: 92/238 of the proton value = 3.70 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹

Order of magnitude: electron > proton > alpha > uranium-238. Heavy nuclei have more neutrons per proton, so their Z/A, and hence their specific charge, is smaller.

**Worked example 4: working backwards.** An ion has 16 nucleons and specific charge −1.20 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹ (electron mass ignored). Its proton number is 8. Identify its charge and the number of electrons.

Mass = 16 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 2.67 × 10⁻²⁶ kg.

Charge = specific charge × mass = −1.20 × 10⁷ × 2.67 × 10⁻²⁶ = −3.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, which is −2e.

The ion has 2 more electrons than protons: **10 electrons**. It is the oxide ion, ¹⁶₈O²⁻.

## Nuclide notation

In ᴬ_Z X, A is the nucleon number and Z the proton number; neutrons N = A − Z. An ion's charge is written as a superscript after the symbol, for example ⁵⁶₂₆Fe³⁺ has 26 protons, 30 neutrons and 23 electrons. **Isotopes** have the same Z but different A, so they are the same element with different numbers of neutrons. Isotopes have identical charge in their nuclei but different masses, so their nuclei have different specific charges.

## Common mistakes

- Saying alpha particles are "attracted" to the nucleus.
- Giving "most of the atom is empty space" as the only conclusion.
- Claiming the plum pudding model predicted no deflection: it predicted only small deflections.
- Using the neutral atom's electron count for an ion.
- Dropping the negative sign on the specific charge of an electron or a negative ion.
- Using A protons for the charge instead of Z, or Z nucleons for the mass instead of A.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** (Rutherford): source, thin gold foil, vacuum, detector moved to different angles, then the results.
- **Explain** (conclusions): pair each observation with its own conclusion, and name the force as repulsion between positive charges.
- **Explain** (changing models): state what the old model predicted, what was observed, and why the new model fits.
- **Calculate**: write charge and mass on separate lines before dividing; state units and sign.
- **Compare**: give both values and a relationship, such as "about half".
