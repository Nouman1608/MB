These notes condense **2.1.2 Compounds, formulae and equations** from Module 2 of OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026). There are two statements. Statement (a) is writing formulae of ionic compounds from ionic charges, by predicting charges from the periodic table and recalling seven named ions. Statement (b) is constructing balanced equations, including ionic equations and state symbols, for reactions you have studied and for unfamiliar reactions when you are given information.

The full teaching is in the [compounds, formulae and equations study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-compounds-formulae-and-equations/). Test yourself with the [2.1.2 practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-2-1-2-practice/). The previous subtopic is summarised in the [2.1.1 revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-2-1-1-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ion | Atom or group of atoms with an overall charge |
| Cation / anion | Positive ion / negative ion |
| Molecular ion (polyatomic ion) | Charged group of covalently bonded atoms, such as SO₄²⁻ |
| Formula unit | Simplest ratio of ions in an ionic compound |
| Balanced equation | Same number of each type of atom, and the same total charge, on both sides |
| State symbol | (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water |
| Ionic equation | Equation showing only the species that change |
| Spectator ion | Ion present in solution that is unchanged by the reaction |

## 2.1.2(a)(i): charges from the periodic table

| Position | Ion charge | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 | 1+ | Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺ |
| Group 2 | 2+ | Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Ba²⁺ |
| Group 13 | 3+ | Al³⁺ |
| Group 15 | 3− | N³⁻, P³⁻ |
| Group 16 | 2− | O²⁻, S²⁻ |
| Group 17 | 1− | F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ |

Metals lose their outer-shell electrons; non-metals gain enough to fill the outer shell. Negative ions of single elements end in **-ide**: nitride, oxide, sulfide, chloride.

The specification says charges on ions other than these and the seven below will be **provided** in a question, for example Fe³⁺ or Cu²⁺, often shown by a Roman numeral in the name: iron(III), copper(II).

## 2.1.2(a)(ii): the seven ions to recall

| Name | Formula |
|---|---|
| Nitrate | NO₃⁻ |
| Carbonate | CO₃²⁻ |
| Sulfate | SO₄²⁻ |
| Hydroxide | OH⁻ |
| Ammonium | NH₄⁺ |
| Zinc | Zn²⁺ |
| Silver | Ag⁺ |

The specification adds that "nitrate" and "sulfate" should be assumed to be NO₃⁻ and SO₄²⁻.

## Writing an ionic formula in four steps

1. Write each ion with its charge.
2. Find the lowest whole-number ratio that makes the total charge zero.
3. Put brackets round a molecular ion when you need more than one of it.
4. Write the cation first and remove the charges.

| Compound | Ions | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium sulfate | Al³⁺, SO₄²⁻ | Al₂(SO₄)₃ |
| Ammonium carbonate | NH₄⁺, CO₃²⁻ | (NH₄)₂CO₃ |
| Zinc hydroxide | Zn²⁺, OH⁻ | Zn(OH)₂ |
| Calcium nitride | Ca²⁺, N³⁻ | Ca₃N₂ |
| Silver carbonate | Ag⁺, CO₃²⁻ | Ag₂CO₃ |

**Check:** in Ca₃N₂, 3 × (+2) + 2 × (−3) = 0.

## 2.1.2(b): balancing equations

- Write correct formulae first; never change a formula to make it balance. Change only the numbers in front.
- Balance elements that appear in one substance on each side first; leave elements that appear as the uncombined element (O₂, H₂) until last.
- Treat a molecular ion that is unchanged, such as SO₄²⁻, as one unit.
- With an odd number of O atoms needed from O₂, double every coefficient.
- Add state symbols last.

### Worked reminder: combustion of ethanol

C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l)

Carbon: 2 = 2. Hydrogen: 6 = 6. Oxygen: 1 + 6 = 4 + 3 = 7.

## Reaction types to write from memory

| Reaction | General equation |
|---|---|
| Acid + metal | acid + metal → salt + hydrogen |
| Acid + carbonate | acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide |
| Acid + metal oxide or hydroxide | acid + base → salt + water |
| Acid + ammonia | acid + ammonia → ammonium salt |
| Group 2 metal + water | metal + water → hydroxide + hydrogen |
| Precipitation | two solutions → insoluble salt + solution |

## Ionic equations in four steps

1. Write the full balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Split **aqueous** ionic compounds and strong acids into ions. Leave solids, liquids, gases and covalent molecules whole.
3. Cross out ions that appear unchanged on both sides (spectators).
4. Check that atoms **and** charges balance.

### Worked reminders

- **Neutralisation:** H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l), for any strong acid with any alkali.
- **Precipitation:** Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s).
- **Carbonate solution with acid:** CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l).
- **Insoluble oxide with acid:** CuO(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l). CuO stays whole because it is a solid.

## Unfamiliar reactions

Questions may describe a reaction you have not met. Read the names, convert each to a formula using the charges given, then balance as usual.

**Example.** Magnesium nitride reacts with water to form magnesium hydroxide and ammonia.
Mg₃N₂(s) + 6H₂O(l) → 3Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2NH₃(g)
Nitrogen: 2 = 2. Magnesium: 3 = 3. Hydrogen: 12 = 6 + 6. Oxygen: 6 = 6.

## Exam traps

- Writing NH₄SO₄ for ammonium sulfate: two NH₄⁺ ions are needed, so (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- Writing CaOH₂ without brackets.
- Forgetting the Roman numeral: iron(III) oxide is Fe₂O₃, not FeO.
- Splitting a solid or a gas into ions in an ionic equation.
- Leaving out state symbols when the question asks for them.
- Balancing atoms but not charge in an ionic equation.

## Quick self-test

1. Predict the charge on the ion formed by selenium.
2. Write the formula of silver sulfate.
3. Write the formula of ammonium phosphate, given the phosphate ion is PO₄³⁻.
4. Name Zn(NO₃)₂.
5. Write the formula of iron(III) hydroxide.
6. Balance: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃.
7. Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for magnesium with dilute hydrochloric acid.
8. Give the ionic equation for silver nitrate solution reacting with sodium chloride solution.
9. Name the spectator ions when sodium hydroxide solution neutralises nitric acid.
10. Why is solid zinc carbonate not split into ions in an ionic equation?
11. Balance: C₆H₁₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O using whole numbers.

### Answers

1. 2− (Group 16).
2. Ag₂SO₄.
3. (NH₄)₃PO₄.
4. Zinc nitrate.
5. Fe(OH)₃.
6. 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃.
7. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g).
8. Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).
9. Na⁺ and NO₃⁻.
10. It is a solid, so its ions are not free in solution.
11. 2C₆H₁₄ + 19O₂ → 12CO₂ + 14H₂O.
