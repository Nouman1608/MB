These notes condense **4.2.1.1 Chemical bonds** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for
teaching from September 2016, version 1.1). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only
statements and no required practical. For full explanations and worked examples, read the
[chemical bonds study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-bonds/); then try the
[chemical bonds practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-bonds-practice/).

## The whole subsection in five lines

1. There are **three** types of strong chemical bond: **ionic**, **covalent**, **metallic**.
2. Ionic: the particles are **oppositely charged ions**.
3. Covalent: the particles are **atoms that share pairs of electrons**.
4. Metallic: the particles are **atoms that share delocalised electrons**.
5. Every one is explained by **electrostatic attraction** plus the **transfer or sharing** of electrons.

## Where each bond occurs

| Bond | Occurs in | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Ionic | Compounds of a metal with a non-metal | NaF, MgO, K₂S, CaCl₂ |
| Covalent | Most non-metal elements; compounds of non-metals | O₂, N₂, HBr, CH₄, SO₂ |
| Metallic | Metallic elements and alloys | Fe, Al, Zn, brass, solder |
| None (no strong bonds) | Group 0 elements | He, Ne, Ar |

Shortcut: look for a metal symbol first. Metal + non-metal = ionic. Metal(s) only = metallic. Non-metals
only = covalent (unless it is a single noble gas).

## Electrostatic explanation for each bond

| Bond | Electrons | Attraction between |
|---|---|---|
| Ionic | Transferred, metal → non-metal | Positive metal ions and negative non-metal ions, in all directions |
| Covalent | One pair shared by two atoms | The shared pair and the nuclei of both atoms |
| Metallic | Outer electrons delocalised | Positive metal ions and the sea of delocalised electrons |

A full "explain" answer has two parts: **electron change** + **what attracts what**. Leaving out either part
usually drops a mark.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chemical bond | A strong attraction that holds atoms or ions together in a substance |
| Ion | An atom or group of atoms with an overall charge, from losing or gaining electrons |
| Electrostatic force | The force between charged particles; opposite charges attract |
| Shared pair | Two electrons, one usually from each atom, held between two bonded atoms |
| Delocalised electron | An outer electron not tied to one atom, free to move through the structure |
| Alloy | A mixture of a metal with one or more other elements; bonding is metallic |
| Compound | Two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions |

## Recognising bonding from a description

| If the question says… | Think… |
|---|---|
| "electrons are transferred" | Ionic |
| "a pair of electrons is shared between two atoms" | Covalent |
| "electrons are free to move through the structure" | Metallic |
| "positive and negative ions" | Ionic |
| "positive ions and free electrons", with no negative ions | Metallic |
| "a molecule" | Covalent (ionic and metallic substances do not form molecules) |

## Exam traps

- **The hidden metal.** Long formulae such as KNO₃, Na₂CO₃ or MgSO₄ contain a metal, so they count as
  compounds of a metal with non-metals.
- **Diatomic elements.** H₂, N₂, O₂ and Cl₂ are elements, not compounds, and their bonding is covalent.
- **Alloys.** A mixture of metals is not a compound and is not ionic.
- **Atoms vs ions.** Ionic bonding involves ions; covalent and metallic bonding are described in terms of
  atoms in this subsection.
- **"Sharing" in metals.** Metallic bonding is also a kind of sharing, but of delocalised electrons
  through the whole structure, not of a pair between two atoms.
- **Electron direction.** In ionic bonding, electrons go **from the metal to the non-metal**.
- **Naming the force.** "Strong bond" is not an explanation; "strong electrostatic attraction between…" is.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three types of strong chemical bond.
2. What are the particles in ionic bonding?
3. Which type of bonding occurs in alloys?
4. Name the type of bonding in calcium bromide, CaBr₂.
5. Name the type of bonding in sulfur dioxide, SO₂.
6. Why is argon not described as having covalent bonding?
7. In which direction are electrons transferred when an ionic compound forms?
8. In a covalent bond, what is the shared pair of electrons attracted to?
9. What is meant by "delocalised" electrons?
10. Sodium nitrate is NaNO₃. Explain why it counts as a compound of a metal with non-metals.
11. Give one similarity between ionic and metallic bonding.
12. Give one difference between the particles in covalent and ionic bonding.

### Answers

1. Ionic, covalent and metallic.
2. Oppositely charged ions.
3. Metallic bonding.
4. Ionic: calcium is a metal and bromine is a non-metal.
5. Covalent: sulfur and oxygen are both non-metals.
6. Argon is a Group 0 element; its atoms stay separate and do not form bonds with each other.
7. From the metal atoms to the non-metal atoms.
8. The positive nuclei of both bonded atoms.
9. Outer-shell electrons that are not held by one atom and are free to move through the whole structure.
10. It contains sodium, a metal, combined with nitrogen and oxygen, which are non-metals.
11. Both involve strong electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and negative charge (or
    both give giant structures).
12. Ionic bonding involves ions; covalent bonding involves atoms sharing a pair of electrons.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the bond type without the reason when the question says "explain how you know".
- Stating "they share electrons" without the word **pair** for covalent bonds.
- Describing metallic bonding with negative ions.
- Writing that ionic forces act "between each pair of ions"; they act in all directions.
- Listing argon or neon as covalent.
