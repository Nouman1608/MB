Quick recall for section 3.1.1 Economic foundations (3.1.1.1 Economic activity, 3.1.1.2 The factors of production, 3.1.1.3 Making choices) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics (9214) specification, for exams from May/June 2025 onwards. Worked explanations are in the [economic foundations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-economics-9214-economic-foundations-oxfordaqa-igcse-economics/); exam-style questions are in the [economic foundations practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-economics-9214-economic-foundations-oxfordaqa-igcse-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Need | Something required to survive or live at a basic level: food, water, shelter, clothing |
| Want | Something desired but not essential |
| Economic activity | Production of goods and services to satisfy needs and wants (its central purpose) |
| Key economic decisions | What to produce, how to produce, who is to benefit |
| Economic groups | Consumers, producers and government |
| Economic resource | An input that is limited in supply and has alternative uses |
| Factors of production | Land, labour, capital, enterprise |
| Basic economic problem | Unlimited wants, limited resources, so choices must be made |
| Opportunity cost | The benefit of the next best alternative given up |
| Net benefit | Benefits minus costs of an option |

## 3.1.1.1 Economic activity

**Needs and wants change over time** because of:

- rising incomes (luxuries become normal, such as refrigerators);
- changes in how society works (online banking and schooling make a phone or computer close to essential);
- place and climate (heating is a need in a cold country).

**Key decisions: spot the verb.**

| Situation | Decision |
|---|---|
| Choosing between a hospital and a stadium | What to produce |
| Choosing machines or more workers | How to produce |
| Deciding who gets cheap housing | Who is to benefit |

**Interactions between groups**

- Consumers → producers: spending and labour.
- Producers → consumers: goods, services and incomes (wages, rent, interest, profit).
- Consumers and producers → government: taxes.
- Government → consumers: services, benefits, pensions.
- Government → producers: purchases, rules and regulations.

A change for one group passes to the others. Example chain: income tax cut → consumers spend more → producers sell more and hire more → more wages → more spending; government revenue per person falls but other tax revenue may rise.

## 3.1.1.2 Factors of production

| Factor | Examples | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Soil, minerals, forests, fish, a lake | Rent |
| Labour | Physical and mental human effort: a chef, a coder | Wages or salaries |
| Capital | Man-made aids to production: machines, vans, tools, buildings, software | Interest |
| Enterprise | Organising the other factors and bearing the risk | Profit (can be a loss) |

Points that earn the mark:

- An economic resource is **scarce** and has **alternative uses**, so using it has an opportunity cost.
- **Money is not capital** in this sense; it pays for resources.
- One person can supply two factors: a self-employed plumber gives labour and enterprise, earning wages and profit.

## 3.1.1.3 Making choices

- **Why** choices are made: scarcity.
- **How** choices are made: compare benefits with costs; pick the highest net benefit; note the opportunity cost.

Steps for a cost-benefit question:

1. Work out net benefit = benefits − costs for each option.
2. Choose the highest net benefit.
3. Opportunity cost = net benefit of the next best option (not all options).
4. Evaluate: uncertain figures, risk, effects on third parties, sustainability and fairness.

Mini example: a bus company can spend 50,000 on a new route (net benefit 18,000), new ticket machines (net benefit 12,000) or staff training (net benefit 9,000). It picks the new route; the opportunity cost is the ticket machines, 12,000.

Opportunity cost exists even without money: an hour of sport costs an hour of revision.

## Analysis and evaluation prompts

- Use the case: name the person, firm or government and quote its figures (AO2).
- Chain: scarcity → choice → something given up → consequence for that group (AO3).
- Weigh: how certain are the figures? Who gains, who loses? Short run or long run? Is the option sustainable?

## Exam traps

- Highest revenue is not the best choice; highest net benefit is.
- Opportunity cost is one alternative, not the sum of all.
- Capital means capital goods; profit rewards enterprise only.
- Services count as production.
- When asked how groups interact, show a flow between at least two groups, not a description of one.
- "Needs change over time" answers need the reason for the change.

## Self-test

1. State the central purpose of economic activity.
2. Name the three key economic decisions.
3. Give one reason why a want may become a need.
4. A government decides whether new flats will be sold or rented cheaply to low-income families. Which key decision is this mainly about?
5. Name the three main economic groups.
6. Give one flow from government to producers.
7. State two features of an economic resource.
8. Identify the factor of production: a fishing boat.
9. State the reward to land.
10. Why is money in a savings account not a factor of production?
11. Options have net benefits of 6,000, 4,500 and 4,000. What is the opportunity cost of choosing the best one?
12. A student earns 10 an hour and gives up a 3-hour shift to revise. Paid work was her next best option. What is the opportunity cost?

### Answers

1. The production of goods and services to satisfy needs and wants.
2. What to produce, how to produce, who is to benefit.
3. Rising incomes, or changes in society such as services moving online (any one with a reason).
4. Who is to benefit.
5. Consumers, producers and government.
6. Government purchases from firms, or rules and regulations (either).
7. Limited in supply; has alternative uses (so using it has an opportunity cost).
8. Capital.
9. Rent.
10. It does not produce anything itself; it is a means of paying for resources such as capital.
11. 4,500 (the next best alternative only).
12. 3 × 10 = 30 currency units.
