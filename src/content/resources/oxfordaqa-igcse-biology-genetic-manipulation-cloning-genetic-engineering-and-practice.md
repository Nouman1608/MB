## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State what is meant by a clone. **[1]**
**(b)** A gardener makes new geranium plants by cuttings. Give two features of this method. **[2]**

**2.** A farmer wants a new calf that is genetically identical to her best dairy cow, using a cell taken from the cow's skin. Describe the stages by which this calf could be produced. **[5]**

**3.** A breeder splits the cells of a young cattle embryo and transplants the resulting embryos into host cows.
**(a)** Explain why the cells must be split before they become specialised. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why two calves that grew from cells of the same original embryo are genetically identical. **[1]**
**(c)** State how the genes of a calf compare with those of its host mother. **[1]**

**4.** An orchid nursery uses tissue culture. One small group of cells is divided into 5 pieces. After each growth period every group is again divided into 5 pieces. This happens 4 times.
**(a)** Calculate the number of groups of cells after the fourth division. **[2]**
**(b)** The cells are grown on a sterile nutrient medium. Explain why it must be sterile. **[2]**

**5.** A farm collects 15 embryos from 3 donor cows. Each is split into 2 and the 30 embryos are transplanted into host cows. 18 calves are born alive.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of transplanted embryos that gave a live calf. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest one reason why not every transplanted embryo gave a calf. **[1]**
**(c)** The farmer says that all 18 calves are identical to each other. Evaluate this statement. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one disadvantage of a herd made from only a few sets of identical calves. **[1]**

**6.** Describe how a human gene can be transferred into bacteria so that they make the protein it codes for. **[6]**

**7.** A scientist mixes 8000 bacteria with plasmids that carry a human gene. 56 bacteria take up a plasmid.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of bacteria that took up a plasmid. **[2]**
**(b)** A bacterium with the plasmid divides every 15 minutes. Calculate how many bacteria it produces in 2 hours, assuming none die. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why this is useful for producing a protein. **[1]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why genes are transferred into a plant at an early stage of its development, not into a fully grown plant. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the two types of vector that are usually used. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows the yield of a herbicide-resistant GM oilseed rape and a non-GM variety grown in five fields, in tonnes per hectare.

| Field | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GM | 3.9 | 4.2 | 4.0 | 4.3 | 4.1 |
| Non-GM | 3.4 | 3.6 | 3.3 | 3.5 | 3.7 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean yield of each variety. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate, to 3 significant figures, the percentage increase in yield for the GM variety. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why a herbicide-resistant crop may give a higher yield. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one way to make the conclusion more reliable. **[1]**

**10.** Explain two concerns that people have about growing GM crops. **[4]**

**11.** A farm trial compared an insect-resistant GM crop with a non-GM crop.

| | Non-GM | GM |
|---|---|---|
| Insecticide sprays per season | 7 | 2 |
| Yield in tonnes per hectare | 18.0 | 21.0 |
| Wild flower species at the field edge | 14 | 13 |

**(a)** Calculate, to 3 significant figures, the percentage increase in yield. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate, to 3 significant figures, the percentage reduction in sprays. **[2]**
**(c)** A neighbouring farmer asks whether he should grow the GM crop. Use the data and your knowledge to make a judgement. **[4]**

**12.** A laboratory used adult cell cloning on a farm animal. 150 egg cells received a nucleus from an adult body cell. 24 developed into embryos, 6 pregnancies began and 2 animals were born alive.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of egg cells that developed into embryos. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of egg cells used for each live animal. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of pregnancies that ended in a live birth. **[1]**
**(d)** State two differences between how offspring are produced by adult cell cloning and by embryo transplants. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest two reasons why a breeder might still use adult cell cloning, despite the low success rate. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** An organism that is genetically identical to its parent [1]
**(b)** Any two of (1 mark each, maximum 2): needs no special equipment; produces many identical plants from one parent; an older, simple method used by gardeners; no seeds or fertilisation needed [2] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Quick" or "cheap" on their own are weak. Tie the feature to the method, such as "no special equipment needed".

**2.** The nucleus is taken out of an unfertilised egg cell from another cow [1]; a nucleus taken from an adult body cell, for example a skin cell, is put into that egg cell [1]; an electric shock acts as the catalyst, so the egg cell begins to divide [1]; giving embryo cells with the same genetic information as the adult cell [1]; at the ball-of-cells stage the embryo is placed in the womb of an adult female, where it continues to develop [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which cell each nucleus comes from. "DNA from the adult" is less precise about what is removed and inserted.

**3.** **(a)** Before they are specialised, each cell can still develop into a whole animal [1]; after specialisation the separated cells could no longer grow into a complete calf [1]
**(b)** They came from the same embryo, so they have the same genetic information [1]
**(c)** There is no genetic link, because the host mother only carries the embryo [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) say genes, and say what the host does.

**4.** **(a)** 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 [1] = **625 groups** [1]
**(b)** Bacteria and fungi grow faster than plant cells on a nutrient medium [1]; they would compete with or kill the plant cells [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the repeated multiplication or the power 5⁴.

**5.** **(a)** 18 ÷ 30 × 100 [1] = **60%** [1]
**(b)** Any one of: the embryo did not implant in the womb; the embryo failed to develop; a pregnancy was lost [1]
**(c)** Calves from the two halves of the same original embryo are identical to each other [1]; calves from different original embryos are not identical [1]
**(d)** There is little genetic variation, so a single disease could affect the whole herd [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" asks for a judgement. Say that the statement is only partly correct and explain which calves are identical.

**6.** Any six of (1 mark each, maximum 6): enzymes isolate or cut out the required gene from a human chromosome; a plasmid (vector) is taken from a bacterium; the plasmid is cut open using an enzyme; the human gene is inserted into the plasmid; the plasmid is put into bacterial cells; the bacteria divide, making many copies of the gene; the bacteria make the protein coded for by the gene [6]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the steps in order.

**7.** **(a)** 56 ÷ 8000 × 100 [1] = **0.7%** [1]
**(b)** 2 hours = 120 minutes, 120 ÷ 15 = 8 divisions [1]; 2⁸ = **256 bacteria** [1]
**(c)** Many bacteria carry the gene, so a large amount of protein can be made [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how you found the number of divisions, and convert hours to minutes first.

**8.** **(a)** The gene is then present in every cell as the plant grows [1]; the plant develops with the desired characteristic throughout, whereas a gene added to a mature plant would reach only some cells [1]
**(b)** A bacterial plasmid and a virus [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link early transfer to cell division. "It is easier" is too vague to explain anything.

**9.** **(a)** GM: 20.5 ÷ 5 = **4.1** [1]; non-GM: 17.5 ÷ 5 = **3.5** t/ha [1]
**(b)** (4.1 − 3.5) ÷ 3.5 × 100 [1] = **17.1%** [1]
**(c)** Weeds can be killed without harming the crop [1]; so less competition for light, water and minerals [1]
**(d)** Repeat the trial in more fields, on other soils or over more years [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original (non-GM) value. Dividing by the final value gives a different percentage.

**10.** Any two concerns, each with an explanation (2 marks each, maximum 4): the effect on wild flowers, because heavy weedkiller use on a herbicide-resistant crop could kill wild flowers in and around fields; the effect on insects, because fewer weeds and pests mean less food for insects, which affects animals that feed on them; uncertainty about human health, because the effects of eating GM crops are not fully known [4]
*Examiner insight:* Give the effect and what it leads to, not only "it is not natural".

**11.** **(a)** (21.0 − 18.0) ÷ 18.0 × 100 [1] = **16.7%** [1]
**(b)** (7 − 2) ÷ 7 × 100 [1] = **71.4%** [1]
**(c)** Benefits: yield up by 16.7% and sprays down by 71.4%, so higher income and lower costs [1]; one limit: the wild flower species fell from 14 to 13, so there may be some effect on wild plants [1]; one trial only, so more fields and years are needed, and human health effects are uncertain [1]; a conclusion that follows, for example that the benefit to the farmer is clear but wider effects should be monitored [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement needs a decision backed by figures from the table, not only a list of benefits and risks.

**12.** **(a)** 24 ÷ 150 × 100 = **16%** [1]
**(b)** 150 ÷ 2 = **75** [1]
**(c)** 2 ÷ 6 × 100 = **33% (33.3%)** [1]
**(d)** Any two (1 mark each, maximum 2): adult cell cloning uses an egg with its nucleus removed plus a nucleus from an adult body cell; embryo transplants split the cells of an existing embryo; an electric shock is used in adult cell cloning; adult cell cloning copies an adult, whereas embryo transplants give animals identical to each other [2]
**(e)** Any two (1 mark each, maximum 2): it copies an adult animal whose features are already known; it can reproduce a valuable animal; it can copy an animal that has already been genetically engineered [2] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (d) state both techniques, so a difference is clear.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a clone is "similar" instead of genetically identical.
- Not saying the nucleus is removed from the egg cell in adult cell cloning.
- Saying the electric shock fertilises the egg.
- Forgetting that embryo cells must be split before they become specialised.
- Writing that plasmids come from humans. The plasmid is the bacterial vector.
- Using the final value as the denominator in a percentage change.
- Giving concerns about GM crops with no explanation, or judging without using the figures.
