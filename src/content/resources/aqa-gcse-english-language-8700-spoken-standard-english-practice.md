## Section A — Short questions

**1.** State what spoken Standard English means for the general criteria of the spoken language assessment. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the difference between **accent** and **dialect**, and say which one Standard English refers to. **[3]**

**3.** Rewrite each sentence in Standard English: (a) *"I seen the results yesterday."* (b) *"They was surprised by the vote."* (c) *"She don't agree with the plan."* **[3]**

**4.** Rewrite in Standard English and name the feature corrected: *"It don't make no difference what we do."* **[2]**

**5.** Rewrite each sentence in Standard English: (a) *"I was sat at the front when it happened."* (b) *"Them posters was put up last week."* **[2]**

**6.** Each sentence below contains a **hypercorrection**. Correct it and explain the error: (a) *"Between you and I, the plan is flawed."* (b) *"Any questions can be sent to myself."* **[4]**

**7.** Is this sentence Standard English, and is it formal? *"I'm gutted the club's closing."* Explain both answers. **[2]**

## Section B — Longer and structured questions

**8.** Read this original transcript from a student's presentation.

> *"Basically, me and my mate done some research, and, like, we found that loads of people ain't got nowhere to go in
> the evenings. The youth centre what used to be on Mill Street shut down, innit."*

Identify **four** non-standard or informal features and rewrite the transcript in spoken Standard English suitable for a formal presentation. **[8]**

**9.** A student says: *"I'm from Newcastle, so I'll have to put on a different accent to pass the spoken language endorsement."* Explain why this is a misunderstanding and what the student does need to do. **[3]**

**10.** A student delivers a well-prepared talk in Standard English, but in the question session says: *"Nah, we never done that bit, it weren't worth it."* Explain how this could affect the assessment and rewrite the reply. **[4]**

**11.** Explain what "whenever and wherever appropriate" suggests about the relationship between Standard English and other varieties of English. **[3]**

**12.** A presentation on regional dialects includes several dialect words. Explain how the speaker can use them without failing the requirement for spoken Standard English. **[3]**

**13.** List **three** practical methods for reducing non-standard forms and fillers in a live presentation. **[3]**

---

## Answers

**1.** The learner must be intelligible [1] and must generally use language appropriate to the formal setting of the presentation [1].

**2.** Accent is the way words are pronounced [1]; dialect is a variety with its own grammar and vocabulary [1]. Standard English is a dialect, so it refers to grammar and vocabulary and can be spoken in any accent [1].

**3.** (a) "I saw the results yesterday." [1] (b) "They were surprised by the vote." [1] (c) "She doesn't agree with the plan." [1]

**4.** "It doesn't make any difference what we do" (or "It makes no difference what we do") [1]. Features corrected: the double negative and the agreement of "don't" with "it" [1].

**5.** (a) "I was sitting at the front when it happened." [1] (b) "Those posters were put up last week." [1]

**6.** (a) "Between you and me" [1]: "between" is a preposition, so it takes the object pronoun "me" [1]. (b) "Any questions can be sent to me" [1]: "myself" is reflexive or emphatic and needs "I" earlier in the same clause, as in "I wrote it myself" [1].

**7.** Yes, it is Standard English, because its grammar is standard [1]; it is not formal, because "gutted" is informal vocabulary and the tone is conversational [1].

**8.** Four features, one mark each (4 × [1]) from: "me and my mate done" (pronoun order and form, non-standard past tense, informal "mate"); "ain't got nowhere" (non-standard "ain't" and double negative); "what used to be" (non-standard relative pronoun); "innit" (non-standard tag question); fillers and slang ("Basically", "like", "loads"). A rewrite in Standard English with all non-standard grammar corrected [2], fillers and slang removed [1], and formal register throughout [1]. For example: "My friend and I carried out some research, and we found that many people have nowhere to go in the evenings. The youth centre that used to be on Mill Street has closed."

**9.** Accent is pronunciation, and Standard English concerns grammar and vocabulary, so it can be spoken in any accent [1]. The student can keep their accent [1] but needs to use Standard English grammar and vocabulary, be intelligible, and use language appropriate to the formal setting [1].

**10.** The question session is part of the assessment, and the general criteria require spoken Standard English to be used generally [1]; repeated non-standard forms ("we never done", "it weren't") and informal "Nah" could undermine that, and the reply is also undeveloped [1]. A rewrite that corrects the grammar [1] and keeps a formal, developed answer [1]. For example: "No, we didn't cover that part, because we decided it was less relevant to the question than the survey results."

**11.** It implies that Standard English is right for some situations, such as formal presentations [1], but not that other varieties are wrong [1]; speakers are expected to judge the situation and move between varieties, which is code-switching [1].

**12.** Present the dialect words as examples, clearly signalled ("In parts of the North, a child is a 'bairn'") [1]; explain the meaning so that the audience understands [1]; keep the surrounding commentary in Standard English and formal register [1].

**13.** Any three of the following, one mark each (3 × [1]): rehearse aloud and record yourself to notice habitual forms; practise the Standard English versions of your own common non-standard forms; use pauses instead of fillers; slow down to stay intelligible and accurate; prepare formal phrases for the question session.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing accent with Standard English.
- Correcting one non-standard form but missing a second in the same sentence.
- Hypercorrecting with "I" or "myself".
- Treating the question session as informal.
- Removing fillers but leaving the grammar non-standard.
