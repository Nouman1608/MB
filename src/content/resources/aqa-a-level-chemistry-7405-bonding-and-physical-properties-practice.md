It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Bonding and physical properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bonding-and-physical-properties/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bonding-and-physical-properties-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Name the type of structure present in each substance.

**(a)** Diamond **[1]**

**(b)** Iodine **[1]**

**(c)** Magnesium **[1]**

**(d)** Sodium chloride **[1]**

**2.** The table gives some properties of four unlabelled solids.

| Solid | Melting point / °C | Conducts when solid? | Conducts when molten? |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | 3550 | No | No |
| X | 770 | No | Yes |
| Y | 1085 | Yes | Yes |
| Z | 44 | No | No |

Identify the type of structure in each solid, giving the evidence. **[4]**

**3.** Explain why diamond has a much higher melting point than iodine. **[3]**

**4.** Graphite and diamond are both forms of carbon.

**(a)** Explain why graphite conducts electricity. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why diamond does not conduct electricity. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why graphite is soft and can be used as a lubricant. **[2]**

**5.** Explain, with reference to forces, why sodium chloride has a higher melting point than ice. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the structure of ice and explain why it is less dense than liquid water. **[4]**

**7.** A student writes: "When iodine sublimes, the covalent bonds between the iodine atoms are broken." Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

**8.** Magnesium has a melting point of 650 °C. Explain why magnesium:

**(a)** has a high melting point **[2]**

**(b)** conducts electricity when solid **[1]**

**(c)** can be bent without breaking **[2]**

**9.** Draw a diagram showing the arrangement of the ions in sodium chloride using at least eight ions. Label the ions. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the energy changes that take place when solid iodine is heated until it forms iodine vapour, and state why the process needs less energy than melting an equal amount of sodium chloride. **[3]**

**11.** Silicon dioxide, SiO₂, melts at 1710 °C and does not conduct electricity when molten. Suggest the type of structure and explain both properties. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why liquid water does not conduct electricity well, even though ice and water contain polar molecules. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Macromolecular (giant covalent) [1]

**(b)** Molecular [1]

**(c)** Metallic [1]

**(d)** Ionic [1]. **[4]**

**2.** W is macromolecular: very high melting point and no conduction in either state [1]. X is ionic: high melting point, conducts only when molten (ions free to move) [1]. Y is metallic: conducts when solid (delocalised electrons) [1]. Z is molecular: low melting point and no conduction [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Diamond is a giant lattice of carbon atoms joined by strong covalent bonds [1]; these covalent bonds must be broken for diamond to melt, which needs a large amount of energy [1]. Iodine is molecular and only the weak London forces between I₂ molecules are overcome on melting, so far less energy is needed [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Each carbon atom in graphite forms three covalent bonds, so one electron per atom is delocalised [1]; these electrons are free to move through the layers and carry charge [1]

**(b)** All four outer electrons of each carbon atom are used in covalent bonds, so there are no mobile charged particles [1]

**(c)** The layers are held together only by weak London forces [1]; so the layers can slide over each other easily [1]. **[5]**

**5.** Sodium chloride is a giant ionic lattice with strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions [1]; ice is molecular and only hydrogen bonds between molecules are overcome on melting [1]; hydrogen bonds are much weaker than the electrostatic attraction in the ionic lattice, so less energy is needed to melt ice [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Water molecules are held in a lattice by hydrogen bonds [1]; each molecule is hydrogen bonded to four others [1] in an open structure with gaps [1]; the molecules are on average further apart than in liquid water, so ice is less dense [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The statement is incorrect [1]. Iodine is molecular, and only the weak London forces between I₂ molecules are overcome when it sublimes; the covalent bond within each I₂ molecule stays intact [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Strong electrostatic attraction between the Mg²⁺ ions and the delocalised electrons throughout the lattice [1]; a large amount of energy is needed to overcome it [1]

**(b)** Delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge [1]

**(c)** Layers of ions slide over each other [1]; the delocalised electrons keep the ions bonded in the new positions [1]. **[5]**

**9.** A cube (or part of a lattice) with eight ions at the corners, alternating positive and negative [1]; labelled Na⁺ and Cl⁻ so that each ion is clearly neighbouring ions of the opposite charge [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Energy is used to overcome the weak London forces between I₂ molecules [1], first to allow the molecules to move out of fixed positions (melting) and then to separate them completely (vapour) [1]. Sodium chloride needs more energy because its ions are held by much stronger electrostatic attraction [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Giant covalent (macromolecular) structure [1]. It has a high melting point because many strong covalent bonds must be broken [1]; it does not conduct, because all the electrons are used in bonds and there are no mobile ions or delocalised electrons [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Water is molecular, so it has no ions or delocalised electrons [1]; the molecules are polar but neutral, so they cannot carry a current (a very small amount of conduction comes from a tiny concentration of ions) [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that covalent bonds break on melting a molecular substance.
- Giving the structure type without evidence from the data.
- Describing graphite's conduction without stating that electrons are delocalised.
- Failing to compare the strength of the forces in two substances.
- Leaving out the labels or the charges on lattice diagrams.
