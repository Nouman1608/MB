## What goes in and out

| Substance | Enters or leaves | Where | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Enters | Leaves, through stomata | Diffusion |
| Oxygen (from photosynthesis) | Leaves | Through stomata | Diffusion |
| Water | Enters | Roots (mostly) | Absorbed through root hairs |
| Mineral ions | Enter | Roots (mostly) | Absorbed through root hairs |
| Water vapour | Leaves | Leaves, mostly through stomata | Evaporation and diffusion |

## Surface area features

- **Roots**: root hairs increase the **surface area** for absorbing water and mineral ions.
- **Leaves**: the **flattened shape** and **internal air spaces** increase the **surface area to volume ratio**,
  so gases reach every cell quickly.
- The CO₂ concentration gradient into the leaf is kept up because photosynthesis uses CO₂ inside the cells.

## Stomata and guard cells

```
plenty of water → guard cells swell → stoma opens → CO₂ in, O₂ and water vapour out
water short    → guard cells lose water → stoma closes → water loss reduced, wilting prevented
                                                       → but less CO₂ in, so photosynthesis slows
```

- **Guard cells** surround each stoma and control its size.
- The **stoma** is the pore, not the guard cells.

## Water loss

- Plants mainly lose water vapour from their **leaves**.
- **Most** of the loss is through the **stomata**.
- Evaporation is faster when conditions are **hot, dry and windy**.

| Condition | Why loss is faster |
|---|---|
| Hot | Faster evaporation from cells inside the leaf |
| Dry | Steeper water vapour concentration gradient |
| Windy | Vapour blown away, keeping the gradient steep |

**Evidence experiment**: cover the upper or lower surface of leaves with petroleum jelly and compare percentage
mass loss. Covering the surface with most stomata cuts loss the most.

Worked reminder: a leaf falls from 3.20 g to 2.40 g. Percentage loss = 0.80 ÷ 3.20 × 100 = **25%**.

## Transport

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water and mineral ions | Dissolved sugars |
| From → to | Roots → stem and leaves | Leaves → rest of plant, including growing regions and storage organs |
| Process | Transpiration stream | Translocation |
| Direction | Upwards only | Up or down |
| Cells | Dead, hollow, no end walls, lignin-strengthened walls | Living, with pores in end walls (sieve plates), companion cells |

**Transpiration stream**: water moves from the roots, through the xylem and out of the leaves; evaporation at the
leaves pulls more water up.

Worked reminder: dye rises 18 cm up a stem in 30 minutes. Speed = 18 ÷ 30 = **0.6 cm per minute**.

## Model answers to learn

- **Why does carbon dioxide keep diffusing into a leaf in daylight?** Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide inside
  the leaf, so its concentration there stays lower than in the air outside.
- **Why can a transplanted plant wilt in wet soil?** Its root hairs were damaged, so the surface area for
  absorbing water fell and water was lost faster than it was replaced.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stoma (plural stomata) | Pore in the leaf surface for gas exchange |
| Guard cells | Pair of cells around a stoma that control its size |
| Root hair | Extension of a root cell that increases surface area |
| Diffusion | Net movement of particles from a higher to a lower concentration |
| Wilting | Drooping of a plant that has lost more water than it has taken in |
| Xylem | Tissue transporting water and mineral ions from roots to leaves |
| Phloem | Tissue transporting dissolved sugars around the plant |
| Transpiration stream | Movement of water from roots through xylem and out of leaves |
| Translocation | Movement of dissolved sugars in the phloem |
| Lignin | Strengthening material in the walls of xylem |

## Exam traps

- Xylem = **water and mineral ions**; phloem = **sugars**. Do not swap.
- Translocation goes **both ways**; the transpiration stream goes **up**.
- Closing stomata saves water **but** reduces CO₂ uptake.
- Compare **percentage** mass loss when starting masses differ.
- Leaves get CO₂ by **diffusion**, not by "breathing".
- Root hairs increase **surface area**; they are not separate organisms or separate roots.

## Self-test

1. How does carbon dioxide enter a leaf?
2. Where are most of a plant's water and mineral ions absorbed?
3. How do root hairs help absorption?
4. Give two features of a leaf that increase its surface area to volume ratio.
5. Give two functions of stomata.
6. What controls the size of a stoma?
7. Through which structures is most water vapour lost?
8. Give three conditions that increase the rate of evaporation from leaves.
9. Explain how a plant prevents wilting when water is short.
10. What does xylem transport, and in which direction?
11. Name the process by which sugars are moved in phloem, and give two destinations.
12. Give one way the structure of xylem suits its function.

### Answers

1. By diffusion through the stomata.
2. In the roots.
3. They increase the surface area of the roots, so water and mineral ions are absorbed faster.
4. Flattened shape; internal air spaces.
5. Let carbon dioxide in (and oxygen out); help control water loss.
6. The guard cells surrounding it.
7. The stomata.
8. Hot, dry, windy.
9. The guard cells close the stomata, reducing water loss.
10. Water and mineral ions, from the roots to the stem and leaves.
11. Translocation; growing regions and storage organs.
12. Hollow tubes with no end walls let water flow freely (or lignin-strengthened walls stop collapse).
