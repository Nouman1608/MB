## The task in one box

| Feature | Section A (Component 2) |
|---|---|
| Text | One play from the modern drama list; prescribed edition, completely unmarked |
| Choice | One essay question from two on your play |
| Marks | 30: AO1 15, AO2 15 |
| Not assessed here | AO4 (context), which belongs to Section B |
| Time | About 45 minutes of the 90-minute paper |

**Set plays:** *A View from the Bridge*, *An Inspector Calls*, *The Curious Incident of the Dog in the
Night-time* (adapted by Simon Stephens), *Kindertransport*, *Death and the King's Horseman*. Set texts
change with the specification issue, so confirm yours.

## Dramatic methods checklist

| Method | Example of what to say |
|---|---|
| Stage directions | Lighting, sound, movement and pauses are the playwright's deliberate choices |
| Entrances and exits | Timing can interrupt, shock or shift power |
| Dramatic irony | The audience knows more than the characters, creating tension or judgement |
| Narrator or frame | Direct address shapes how the audience reads events |
| Structure | Act endings, cliffhangers, time shifts, circular shapes |
| Dialogue patterns | Interruptions, silences, long speeches show power |
| Setting and staging | A single set can feel trapping; overlapping times can show cause and effect |
| Character as device | A figure who tests others or voices the playwright's ideas |

**Always add the audience:** "On stage, this would..." or "An audience would feel...".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AO1 | An informed personal response: a clear argument with precise reference |
| AO2 | Analysis of language, form and structure, including dramatic methods, and their effects |
| Dramatic irony | When the audience understands something a character does not |
| Tragedy | A drama in which a central figure's downfall seems inevitable |
| Chorus figure | A character who comments on the action for the audience |
| Cliffhanger | An act or play ending that leaves tension unresolved |
| Prescribed edition | The edition of the play specified for the exam |

## Open-book habits

- Learn where key scenes sit by act and scene; annotate a separate study copy only.
- Plan your moments first, then look up short quotations to support them.
- Quote a few words, not whole speeches.
- Cap the time you spend searching.

## Traps

- Writing about characters as real people.
- Only quoting dialogue.
- Retelling the plot.
- Long context paragraphs (no AO4 here).
- Overrunning and squeezing the Section B essay, where AO4 is assessed.

## Self-test

1. How are the 30 marks split between assessment objectives?
2. Why should you avoid long context paragraphs in this essay?
3. Give three dramatic methods other than dialogue.
4. What condition applies to the copy of the play you take into the exam?
5. What is dramatic irony, and why does it create tension?
6. Roughly how long should you spend on Section A?

**Answers:** 1. AO1 15, AO2 15. 2. AO4 is not assessed in Section A; marks come from argument and
analysis of methods. 3. Any three: stage directions, entrances and exits, lighting and sound,
structure (act endings, time shifts), staging and set, a narrator or framing device. 4. It must be the
prescribed edition and completely unmarked. 5. The audience knows something a character does not, so
they watch anxiously for the moment the character discovers it, or judge the character for not
seeing it. 6. About 45 minutes, half of the 90-minute paper.
