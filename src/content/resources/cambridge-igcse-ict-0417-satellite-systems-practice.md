## Section A: short answers

**1.** State what information a GPS satellite broadcasts. **[2]**

**2.** Give **two** features of satellite navigation that are not provided by GPS alone. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by a *layer* in a Geographic Information System. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** places where a GPS receiver may not be able to calculate its position. **[2]**

**5.** A satellite television dish is fixed to the wall of a house. Explain why it does not need to move to follow the satellite. **[2]**

**6.** Give **two** disadvantages of using a satellite phone rather than a mobile phone. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Brightwell Parcels uses GPS trackers in its 60 delivery vans. Each tracker sends the van's position to head office every minute.

**(a)** Describe how the tracker works out the van's position. **[3]**

**(b)** The GPS part of the tracker does not use the mobile phone network. Explain why the tracker still needs a mobile network connection. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **two** benefits to Brightwell Parcels of knowing where every van is. **[2]**

**8.** Arun drives a large lorry and uses a standard car sat nav.

**(a)** Explain **one** risk of using a sat nav designed for cars to drive a lorry. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why spoken directions are safer than reading a map while driving. **[2]**

**9.** Hollinsford Council plans to build a new primary school and uses a GIS to choose the site.

**(a)** Name **three** layers the planners could use and say how each helps the decision. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how the GIS could identify sites that meet all their conditions. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **two** disadvantages for the council of using a GIS. **[2]**

**10.** A family moves to a farm in a mountain valley with no cable television network.

**(a)** Explain why satellite television is suitable for the family. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** problems the family may have with satellite television reception. **[2]**

**11.** Explain the difference between GPS and a GIS, giving one example use of each. **[4]**

**12.** Coastline Aid sends rescue teams to areas hit by earthquakes. Evaluate the use of satellite phones by the rescue teams. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The satellite's position [1] and the exact time the signal was sent [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: stored digital maps [1]; calculation of a route to a destination [1]; spoken or on-screen turn-by-turn directions [1]; automatic rerouting when a turn is missed [1]; estimated time of arrival [1]; live traffic information [1]; points of interest such as fuel stations [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**3.** One set of data about an area (such as roads, rivers or population) that can be shown, hidden or placed over other sets [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: in a tunnel [1]; in an underground car park [1]; between tall buildings in a city [1]; under dense tree cover [1]; inside a large building [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**5.** The satellite is geostationary: it orbits at the same rate as the Earth turns, above the equator [1], so it stays at the same point in the sky and the dish can stay pointed at it [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two of: handsets are more expensive [1]; calls are more expensive [1]; it usually needs a clear view of the sky so often does not work indoors [1]; there is a noticeable delay in conversation [1]; data speeds are slower [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**7. (a)** The receiver picks up signals from several satellites [1]. It uses the time each signal took to arrive to calculate its distance from each satellite [1]. With distances from at least three (normally four) satellites it calculates the van's position [1].

**(b)** GPS only receives signals; it cannot send anything [1], so the tracker uses the mobile network to send the calculated position to head office [1].

**(c)** Any two of: customers can be told accurately when their parcel will arrive [1]; the nearest van can be sent to a new collection [1]; stolen vans can be located [1]; routes and driver times can be checked to improve efficiency [1]. Maximum 2. **[7]**

**8. (a)** The car sat nav does not know the lorry's height, width or weight [1], so it may send Arun down a narrow road, under a low bridge or over a weight-limited bridge where the lorry could get stuck or cause damage [1].

**(b)** Arun can keep looking at the road instead of at a map [1], so he is less likely to miss a hazard and have an accident [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Any three, each with how it helps [1 each]: population of young children, to put the school where pupils live [1]; roads and public transport, so the site is easy to reach [1]; flood risk, to avoid land that may flood [1]; existing schools, to avoid building too close to one [1]; land use or ownership, to find land that is available [1]. Maximum 3.

**(b)** The layers are placed over one another [1] and a query is run so that only areas meeting every condition, for example low flood risk and within a set distance of a main road, are shown [1].

**(c)** Any two of: the software and data are expensive [1]; staff need training [1]; the results are only as good as the data, which may be out of date or inaccurate [1]; large data sets need powerful computers [1]. Maximum 2. **[7]**

**10. (a)** One satellite covers a very wide area including remote valleys [1], so the family can receive channels without any cable connection to the farm [1].

**(b)** Any two described: the mountains or trees may block the line of sight between the dish and the satellite [1]; heavy rain or snow can weaken the signal so the picture breaks up [1]; high winds may move the dish out of alignment so it loses the signal [1]. Maximum 2. **[4]**

**11.** GPS works out the position of a receiver using signals from satellites [1], for example finding where a hiker is on a mountain [1]. A GIS captures, stores and analyses data linked to locations in layers [1], for example mapping which homes are at risk of flooding [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Up to 6 marks. Advantages, for example: earthquakes often damage mobile masts and power, but satellite phones connect straight to a satellite and still work [1]; teams can call for equipment, medical help or extra staff quickly [1]; teams can report their position and stay safe [1]. Disadvantages, for example: handsets and calls are expensive for a charity [1]; the phones need a clear view of the sky, so teams must go outdoors to use them, away from collapsed buildings [1]; delay can make conversations harder in an emergency [1]. A reasoned conclusion, for example that the reliability when ground networks fail outweighs the cost for rescue work [1]. Maximum 6, and at least one advantage and one disadvantage are needed for full marks. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to remember how many satellites GPS uses?**
No figure is named in the syllabus. Say that the receiver uses signals from several satellites, at least three, and normally four.

**What gets the conclusion mark in an evaluate question?**
A judgement that weighs the points you have made for the named user, such as whether the benefit outweighs the cost.
