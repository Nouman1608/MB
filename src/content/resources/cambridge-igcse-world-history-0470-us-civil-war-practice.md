These questions cover key question 1.4 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470, the Paper 2 prescribed topic for November 2025
and June 2026.

## Section A

**1.** Name the **four** candidates in the presidential election of 1860. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** terms of the Missouri Compromise of 1820. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the events that followed Lincoln's election, from November 1860 to April 1861. **[4]**

**4.** Identify **three** weaknesses of the Confederacy during the war. **[3]**

**5.** Describe the Black Codes. **[4]**

**6.** Give **three** results of Radical Reconstruction for formerly enslaved people. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did the Democratic Party split in 1860, and why did this matter? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Lincoln issue the Emancipation Proclamation? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "The North won the Civil War because of its economic strength." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The election of Lincoln was the main reason for the outbreak of the Civil War." How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter by a Union soldier from Ohio, written in Georgia in December 1864. "We have left
nothing behind us but chimneys. The railroads are bent round trees, the barns are ash and the cattle are ours. I take
no joy in it, but the General says war is cruelty, and the quicker it is over the fewer of us will be buried here."

What can you learn from Source A about Northern strategy in the last year of the war? Use the source and your
knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from a speech by a Black member of a Southern state legislature, 1874. "Ten years ago I was
property. Today I make laws. My children read. But the night riders burned my neighbour's house for voting, and the
government in Washington looks the other way. Freedom given with one hand is being taken back with the other."

How useful is Source B as evidence about the success of Reconstruction? Use the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 1 mark each: Abraham Lincoln, Republican (1); Stephen Douglas, Northern Democrat (1); John Breckinridge,
Southern Democrat (1); John Bell, Constitutional Union (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: Missouri entered the Union as a slave state (1); Maine entered as a free state (1); the
balance of free and slave states in the Senate was kept (1); slavery was banned in the rest of the Louisiana Purchase
north of latitude 36°30′ (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: South Carolina seceded on 20 December 1860 (1); six more Deep South states seceded by
February 1861 (1); they formed the Confederate States of America, with Jefferson Davis as president (1); the Crittenden
Compromise failed (1); Lincoln was inaugurated on 4 March 1861 and called the Union perpetual (1); Confederate forces
fired on Fort Sumter on 12 April 1861 (1); Lincoln called for 75,000 volunteers (1); four more states, including
Virginia, seceded (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: few factories and weapons works (1); a smaller population (1); railways that wore out
and could not be replaced (1); inflation caused by printing money (1); state governors resisted Davis (1); the
blockade cut trade (1); cotton diplomacy failed to win foreign recognition (1); conscription exemptions caused class
resentment (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any four, 1 mark each: laws passed by Southern states in 1865-66 (1); under Johnson's lenient Reconstruction
(1); they restricted the rights of freedpeople (1); for example, requiring proof of employment or annual labour
contracts (1); vagrancy laws allowed unemployed Black people to be fined or forced to work (1); they limited the jobs
Black people could do or the property they could own (1); they were designed to keep Black people as cheap labour
(1); they angered Northerners and helped the Radicals take control of Reconstruction (1). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Black men gained the vote (1); Black men were elected to office, for example Hiram Revels
to the Senate (1); citizenship and equal protection under the 14th Amendment (1); Freedmen's Bureau schools and new
colleges (1); protection by federal troops in the military districts (1); the right to make contracts and marry
legally (1). **[3]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **why it split**: Southern Democrats demanded federal protection for slavery in all territories, which
followed from the Dred Scott decision; Northern Democrats backed Douglas's popular sovereignty; Douglas's Freeport
argument had made Southerners distrust him; the convention at Charleston broke up in April 1860. **Why it mattered**:
the Democrats were the last national party, so the split divided the anti-Republican vote, allowing Lincoln to win
with about 40% of the popular vote; it removed the party that had made compromises possible. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **military**: to weaken the Confederacy by undermining its
enslaved workforce, and to allow Black men to enlist; **diplomatic**: to make the war a war against slavery,
discouraging Britain and France from recognising the Confederacy; **political**: to respond to pressure from Radical
Republicans and abolitionists; **moral**: Lincoln believed slavery was wrong; **timing**: the victory at Antietam in
September 1862 let him announce it from strength. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content. **Agree:**
Northern industry produced weapons and supplies; railways moved troops; a larger population replaced losses; the
blockade strangled Southern trade. **Disagree:** the North had these advantages in 1861 and still did not win until
1865, so other factors were needed: Lincoln's leadership and his handling of the border states; emancipation and
Black soldiers; Grant and Sherman's strategy of total war; Southern weaknesses such as states' rights disputes,
inflation and the failure of cotton diplomacy. **Judgement:** for example, economic strength made victory possible but
leadership and strategy turned it into victory. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** the election triggered secession, beginning with
South Carolina in December 1860; Southerners believed a sectional Northern president would end slavery's expansion
and, in time, slavery itself; Lincoln refused the Crittenden Compromise; his response to Fort Sumter brought the
upper South out. **Disagree:** slavery and its expansion were the underlying cause; the Kansas-Nebraska Act, Bleeding
Kansas, Dred Scott and John Brown's raid had already divided the sections; economic differences and states' rights;
the collapse of the national parties. **Judgement:** for example, the election was the trigger, but it caused war only
because of the long conflict over slavery. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains what can be learned, supported by the source and by contextual knowledge.
Level 2 (3-4): explains using the source's details only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or gives knowledge
without using it. Indicative content: Northern strategy aimed at **destroying the South's resources**, not only its
armies ("nothing behind us but chimneys", railroads destroyed, cattle taken); it was **deliberate policy** ("the
General says war is cruelty"); it aimed to **end the war quickly** and save Union lives; it hit Southern civilians and
morale. Knowledge: this is Sherman's March to the Sea from Atlanta to Savannah (November-December 1864); Grant was
pressing Lee in Virginia at the same time; total war helped force surrender in April 1865. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): evaluates usefulness using content and provenance, supported by knowledge. Level 2
(3-4): evaluates using content or provenance only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or asserts usefulness.
Indicative content: **useful for successes**: freedom from slavery, Black officeholders ("Today I make laws") and
education ("My children read"); **useful for failures**: violence against Black voters ("night riders") and declining
federal support ("looks the other way"). **Provenance**: a Black legislator speaking from direct experience, but in
a speech meant to persuade, perhaps to demand federal action. Knowledge: hundreds of Black men held office during
Reconstruction; the Ku Klux Klan and other groups used terror; the Enforcement Acts of 1870-71 had limited lasting
effect; Northern support weakened after the Panic of 1873; troops were withdrawn in 1877. **Judgement:** useful
because it shows both sides from 1874, though one person's view of one state. **[6]**
