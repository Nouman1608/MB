## Questions

**1.** Outline why photosynthesis is described as a transformation of both energy and matter.
**[2]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why not all the energy in one trophic level reaches the next. **[2]**

**3.** Explain, with reference to the second law of thermodynamics, why food chains rarely have more
than four or five trophic levels. **[3]**

**4.** In a meadow food web: grasshoppers and rabbits eat grass; shrews and frogs eat grasshoppers;
herons eat frogs; foxes eat rabbits, shrews and blackberries.

**(a)** Identify the organism that feeds at more than one trophic level and name two of its levels.
**[2]**
**(b)** Predict the effect on grasshoppers of a disease that kills most of the frogs. **[2]**

**5.** Slugs in a garden ingest 1,500 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹, egest 650 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ and respire
610 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the energy assimilated. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net secondary productivity. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of ingested energy that becomes new slug biomass. **[1]**

**6.** In a lake (kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹): producers' NPP 15,000; primary consumers' NSP 1,200; secondary
consumers' NSP 90; tertiary consumers' NSP 6.

**(a)** Calculate the ecological efficiency of each transfer. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the overall efficiency from producers to tertiary consumers. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the lake supports very few tertiary consumers. **[2]**

**7.** Convert an NPP of 1,200 g m⁻² yr⁻¹ into t ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹. **[1]**

**8.** Sample plots of a crop had a mean dry mass of 260 g m⁻² in March and 560 g m⁻² in September.

**(a)** Estimate the NPP in g m⁻² yr⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** reason why this is likely to be an underestimate. **[1]**

**9.** A field produces 24,000 MJ of crop food energy per year. Fed to chickens, 15% of it becomes
chicken meat. A person needs 3,650 MJ per year. Calculate how many people the field can feed:

**(a)** eating the crop directly; **(b)** eating the chicken. **[3]**

**10.** A pesticide was measured in a river food chain (ppm): water 0.0002; algae 0.01; snails 0.08;
fish 0.6; otters 9.

**(a)** Calculate how many times more concentrated the pesticide is in otters than in fish. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the otters have the highest concentration. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a pyramid of biomass can be inverted but a pyramid of productivity cannot. **[3]**

**12.** To what extent does energy loss between trophic levels support the argument that reducing
meat consumption could feed more people? **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Light energy is converted into chemical energy [1], and inorganic carbon dioxide and water are
converted into organic glucose [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two: not all of the lower level is eaten (roots, bones) [1]; not all that is eaten is
digested and some is egested as faeces [1]; much of what is assimilated is respired and lost as
heat [1]; some energy is lost in excretion [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Each energy transformation degrades some energy to heat, so entropy increases [1]. At every
trophic level much of the energy is lost as heat from respiration [1], so after four or five levels
too little energy remains to support a viable population of a further level [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Fox [1]; primary consumer (eating blackberries) and secondary consumer (eating rabbits) or
tertiary consumer (eating shrews) [1].
**(b)** Grasshoppers are likely to increase [1] because predation by frogs falls, although shrews
may eat more of them and limit the rise [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** 1500 − 650 = **850 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** 850 − 610 = **240 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** 240 / 1500 × 100 = **16%** [1] **[3]**

**6. (a)** 1200 / 15000 × 100 = **8.0%**; 90 / 1200 × 100 = **7.5%**; 6 / 90 × 100 = **6.67%**
(3 s.f.) [1] [1] [1]
**(b)** 6 / 15000 × 100 = **0.04%** [1]
**(c)** Energy is lost as heat, in faeces and in uneaten material at each transfer [1], so only a
tiny fraction of producer energy reaches the top level, enough for only a small population [1].
**[6]**

**7.** 1200 × 10,000 = 12,000,000 g ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ = **12 t ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** Increase = 560 − 260 = 300 g m⁻² over 6 months [1]; NPP ≈ 300 / 0.5 = **600 g m⁻² yr⁻¹**
[1]
**(b)** Any one: biomass eaten by herbivores is not counted; leaves or roots that died and decayed
are missed; below-ground growth is hard to sample [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** 24000 / 3650 = **6.58 people** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** Chicken energy = 24000 × 0.15 = 3600 MJ [1]; 3600 / 3650 = **0.986 people** (3 s.f.) [1]
**[3]**

**10. (a)** 9 / 0.6 = **15 times** [1]
**(b)** The pesticide is persistent and fat-soluble, so it is not broken down or excreted [1]. Each
consumer eats many organisms from the level below, taking in all of their pesticide [1]. Otters are
at the top of the chain, so concentration has increased at every level (biomagnification) [1].
**[4]**

**11.** A pyramid of biomass shows standing stock at one moment [1], so fast-reproducing producers
such as phytoplankton can have a smaller mass than the zooplankton feeding on them [1]. A pyramid of
productivity shows energy flow over time, and each level receives only part of the energy of the
level below, so it always narrows upward [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Award up to 7: supporting argument, up to 3; counter-arguments, up to 3; a judgement [1].
- *Supporting:* only about 10% or less of energy passes from crops into animal biomass [1]; so the
  same land feeds several times more people if crops are eaten directly, as in the chicken example
  [1]; feed crops also use land, water and fertiliser that could grow human food [1].
- *Counter-arguments:* much grazing land, such as steep upland or dry rangeland, cannot grow crops
  [1]; animal foods provide protein and nutrients that are harder to obtain from some plant diets
  [1]; feeding more people also depends on distribution, waste and income, not only energy [1];
  some livestock eat by-products humans cannot eat [1].
- *Judgement:* for example, energy loss strongly supports reducing meat from crop-fed animals, but
  the argument is weaker for animals grazing land unsuitable for crops [1]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the upper level in efficiency calculations.
- Forgetting to convert 6 months into half a year in NPP estimates.
- Explaining biomagnification without saying the substance is persistent or not excreted.
- Giving only one side in "to what extent" answers.
