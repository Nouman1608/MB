## Colours to know cold

| Species | Oxidation state | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| VO₂⁺ | V +5 | yellow |
| VO²⁺ | V +4 | blue |
| V³⁺ | V +3 | green |
| V²⁺ | V +2 | violet |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ | Cr +6 | orange |
| CrO₄²⁻ | Cr +6 | yellow |
| Cr³⁺(aq) | Cr +3 | green |
| Cr²⁺(aq) | Cr +2 | blue |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | Cu +2 | yellow |
| [CoCl₄]²⁻ | Co +2 | blue |

Tip for vanadium formulae: in VO₂⁺, V = +1 + 4 = +5; in VO²⁺, V = +2 + 2 = +4.

## Redox with E⦵ values (17.19-17.20)

Values used on this page (use the ones a question gives you):

| Couple | E⦵ / V |
|---|---|
| VO₂⁺/VO²⁺ | +1.00 |
| VO²⁺/V³⁺ | +0.34 |
| V³⁺/V²⁺ | −0.26 |
| Zn²⁺/Zn | −0.76 |
| Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ | +0.77 |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ | +1.33 |
| Cr³⁺/Cr²⁺ | −0.41 |

**Method: how far will a reductant take a metal?**

1. List the metal's couples from most positive to least positive.
2. For each step, E⦵cell = E⦵(couple being reduced) − E⦵(reductant couple).
3. Keep going while E⦵cell is positive. The first negative step is where it stops.
4. Balance electrons, then add the half-equations.

**Worked reminder.** Fe²⁺ with VO₂⁺: step 1 gives 1.00 − 0.77 = +0.23 V (goes); step 2 gives 0.34 − 0.77 = −0.43 V (does not). Fe²⁺ stops at blue VO²⁺:

VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + Fe²⁺ → VO²⁺ + H₂O + Fe³⁺

**Zinc in acid** reduces both metals to +2: V to violet V²⁺ and Cr to blue Cr²⁺. Exclude air, or O₂ reoxidises them.

**Cr³⁺ to Cr(VI)**: excess NaOH, then warm with H₂O₂ (alkaline route), then boil off spare H₂O₂ and acidify.

green [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ → yellow CrO₄²⁻ → orange Cr₂O₇²⁻

2[Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ + 3H₂O₂ → 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2OH⁻ + 8H₂O

**Equilibrium (17.21):** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺. Alkali → yellow; acid → orange. Not redox (Cr stays +6).

## Hydroxide and ammonia tests (17.22)

| Ion | Precipitate | Excess NaOH | Excess NH₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cr³⁺ | grey-green | dark green solution | purple solution |
| Mn²⁺ | off-white, browns in air | stays | stays |
| Fe²⁺ | green, browns in air | stays | stays |
| Fe³⁺ | red-brown | stays | stays |
| Co²⁺ | blue | stays | straw solution, darkens in air |
| Ni²⁺ | green | stays | blue solution |
| Cu²⁺ | pale blue | stays | deep blue solution |
| Zn²⁺ | white | colourless solution | colourless solution |

Memory hook: **excess NaOH dissolves only Cr and Zn hydroxides** (amphoteric). **Excess NH₃ dissolves Cr, Co, Ni, Cu and Zn hydroxides** (ligand exchange).

## Equation patterns (17.23)

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Deprotonation | [M(H₂O)₆]ⁿ⁺ + nOH⁻ → [M(OH)ₙ(H₂O)₆₋ₙ] + nH₂O | [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → [Fe(OH)₃(H₂O)₃] + 3H₂O |
| Deprotonation by NH₃ | OH⁻ replaced by NH₃, product NH₄⁺ | [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 2NH₃ → [Co(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 2NH₄⁺ |
| Amphoteric (alkali) | hydroxide + OH⁻ → anion | [Zn(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 2OH⁻ → [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻ + 4H₂O |
| Amphoteric (acid) | hydroxide + H⁺ → aqua ion | [Cr(OH)₃(H₂O)₃] + 3H⁺ → [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ |
| Ligand exchange | hydroxide + NH₃ → ammine + OH⁻ + H₂O | [Zn(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 4NH₃ → [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ + 4H₂O |

## Ligand exchange and stability (17.24-17.25)

- [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (pale blue) → [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] (pale blue ppt) → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ (deep blue). Four NH₃ replace four H₂O; coordination number stays 6.
- [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O: blue → yellow; 6 → 4; octahedral → tetrahedral.
- [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O: pink → blue; same shape change. Water reverses both.
- Colour changes here come from a change of ligand and of coordination number.

**Chelate stability in steps**

1. Count particles on each side (EDTA⁴⁻ for six H₂O: 2 → 7; three en for six H₂O: 4 → 7).
2. More particles, so ΔSsystem is positive and large.
3. Similar bonds broken and made, so ΔH ≈ 0 and ΔSsurroundings = −ΔH/T is small.
4. ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings is positive; ΔStotal = R ln K, so K is large.

## Catalysis (17.26-17.32)

| Catalyst | Type | Reaction | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| V₂O₅ | heterogeneous | 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ | V +5 → +4 oxidising SO₂; O₂ restores +5 |
| Pt/Rh/Pd | heterogeneous | 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ | adsorb, bonds weaken, react, desorb |
| Fe²⁺ | homogeneous | S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂ | Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ (intermediate) → Fe²⁺ |
| Mn²⁺ | homogeneous, autocatalyst | MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ in acid | Mn²⁺ → Mn³⁺ → Mn²⁺; Mn²⁺ is a product |

- **Heterogeneous**: different phase; reaction at the surface.
- **Homogeneous**: same phase; goes via an intermediate species.
- V₂O₅ steps: SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄, then V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅.
- Fe²⁺ steps: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺, then 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂. Uncatalysed reaction is slow because two anions repel.
- Autocatalysis curve of [MnO₄⁻] against time: slow fall, then steep fall, then levels off.

**Worked reminder: checking a homogeneous catalyst with E⦵.** A catalyst couple must lie between the two reacting couples. Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (+0.77 V) sits between S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ (+2.01 V) and I₂/I⁻ (+0.54 V), so both steps have a positive E⦵cell: +1.24 V and +0.23 V.

## Core Practical 14 in brief (17.33)

The specification asks for "the preparation of a transition metal complex". Know a full method for one complex, for example chromium(II) ethanoate (zinc and acid reduce dichromate to blue Cr²⁺, which is added to sodium ethanoate to give a red precipitate), and how to work out percentage yield:

yield (%) = actual mass ÷ (moles of limiting reagent × ratio × Mr of product) × 100

Air-sensitive products need oxygen excluded during reaction, filtering and washing.

## Must-know distinctions

- **VO₂⁺ vs VO²⁺**: +5 yellow vs +4 blue.
- **Deprotonation vs ligand exchange**: a little NH₃ acts as a base; excess NH₃ acts as a ligand.
- **Amphoteric vs ligand exchange**: Zn(OH)₂ dissolving in NaOH is amphoteric; dissolving in NH₃ is ligand exchange.
- **Catalyst vs autocatalyst**: an autocatalyst forms during the reaction and then catalyses it (Mn²⁺).
- **Heterogeneous vs homogeneous**: phase relative to the reactants, not "solid vs liquid".

## Quick self-test

1. Which vanadium ion is blue, and what is vanadium's oxidation number in it?
2. Data as in the E⦵ table above. Can zinc reduce V³⁺ to V²⁺? Give E⦵cell.
3. How many moles of zinc reduce 0.0100 mol of VO₂⁺ to V²⁺?
4. What colour change shows Cr³⁺ being oxidised to chromate(VI)?
5. Why is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ not a redox reaction?
6. Which two ions in 17.22 give precipitates that dissolve in excess NaOH(aq)?
7. Name the cobalt complex and its colour in excess NH₃(aq).
8. Write the equation for [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with concentrated hydrochloric acid.
9. A ligand swap has ΔH = −2.5 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔSsystem = +62 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Find ΔStotal at 298 K.
10. Write the two steps for V₂O₅ in the Contact process.
11. Why does Fe²⁺ speed up the reaction of S₂O₈²⁻ with I⁻?
12. Moles of zinc needed to reduce 0.00200 mol Cr₂O₇²⁻ all the way to Cr²⁺?

### Answers

1. **VO²⁺, +4**.
2. Yes: −0.26 − (−0.76) = **+0.50 V**.
3. V goes +5 → +2: 3 e⁻ each, so 0.0300 mol e⁻; Zn gives 2 e⁻, so **0.0150 mol Zn**.
4. **Dark green to yellow**.
5. **Cr is +6 on both sides**; only H⁺ is transferred.
6. **Cr³⁺ and Zn²⁺**.
7. **[Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺, straw-coloured** (darkens in air).
8. **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O**.
9. ΔSsurroundings = +2500/298 = +8.4; ΔStotal = **+70.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**.
10. **SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄; V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅**.
11. Each catalysed step is between **oppositely charged ions**, so the activation energy is lower than for two repelling anions.
12. Each Cr goes +6 → +2 (4 e⁻), so 8 e⁻ per Cr₂O₇²⁻ = 0.0160 mol e⁻; **0.00800 mol Zn**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping VO₂⁺ and VO²⁺, or giving the right colour with the wrong formula.
- Subtracting E⦵ values the wrong way round, so a feasible step looks infeasible.
- Forgetting that Cr²⁺ and V²⁺ are reoxidised by air.
- Giving only the precipitate colour when the question also asks about excess reagent.
- Writing NH₃ as OH⁻ in a deprotonation equation without forming NH₄⁺.
- Charged formulae for precipitates, such as [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄]²⁺.
- Saying [CuCl₄]²⁻ is octahedral, or that its formation is redox.
- Explaining the chelate effect with bond strength instead of the increase in ΔSsystem.
- Leaving out desorption when describing a catalytic converter.
- Calling Mn²⁺ a reactant, or describing the autocatalysis curve as a steady fall.
