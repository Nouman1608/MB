These notes condense section **3.1.2.2 The mole and the Avogadro constant** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. For explanations and the multi-step worked examples, read the [Mole and Avogadro constant study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-mole-and-the-avogadro-constant/); to practise, use the [Mole and Avogadro constant practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-mole-and-the-avogadro-constant-practice/). ## What the specification asks

- The Avogadro constant is the number of particles in a mole. **You do not recall its value**; it is given.
- The mole applies to **electrons, atoms, molecules, ions, formulas and equations**.
- Concentration is measured in **mol dm⁻³**.
- Calculations with: the Avogadro constant; mass, Mᵣ and moles; concentration, volume and moles.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mole (mol) | The unit of amount of substance |
| Avogadro constant, L | Number of particles per mole, 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ (given in questions) |
| Amount of substance, n | Quantity measured in moles |
| Formula unit | The group of ions shown by an ionic formula, such as one NaCl |
| Concentration, c | Amount of solute per dm³ of solution, mol dm⁻³ |
| Standard form | A number written as a × 10ᵇ with 1 ≤ a < 10 |

## Core relationships

```
n = m ÷ Mᵣ        m = n × Mᵣ        Mᵣ = m ÷ n
n = c × V         c = n ÷ V         V = n ÷ c      (V in dm³)
N = n × L         n = N ÷ L
mass of one particle = Mᵣ ÷ L   (in g)
```

Unit conversions: cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³; g ÷ 1000 = kg.

## Counting particles inside a formula

| One mole of... | Contains |
|---|---|
| CO₂ molecules | 1 mol C atoms, 2 mol O atoms, 3 mol atoms, 22 mol electrons |
| NH₃ molecules | 1 mol N, 3 mol H, 4 mol atoms, 10 mol electrons |
| CaCl₂ formula units | 1 mol Ca²⁺, 2 mol Cl⁻, 3 mol ions |
| NH₄⁺ ions | 10 mol electrons (7 + 4 − 1) |
| SO₄²⁻ ions | 50 mol electrons (16 + 32 + 2) |

### Method in steps

1. Amount of the substance itself.
2. × number of the required particle in one formula.
3. × L only if a number (not an amount) is wanted.

**Electrons in an ion:** sum of proton numbers − charge (a negative charge adds electrons).

## Reading equations in moles

In N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: 1 mol N₂ reacts with 3 mol H₂ to give 2 mol NH₃. The same ratio holds for molecules. Using these ratios for reacting masses belongs to 3.1.2.5.

## Worked reminders

**Atoms in a mass.** 0.800 g of methane, CH₄ (Mᵣ 16.0):
n = 0.0500 mol; atoms = 5 × 0.0500 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.51 × 10²³**.

**Mass of one molecule.** Glucose (Mᵣ 180.0): 180.0 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = **2.99 × 10⁻²² g**.

**Mass from a count.** 2.50 × 10²² molecules of NH₃ (Mᵣ 17.0):
n = 0.04151 mol; m = **0.706 g**.

**Concentration in g dm⁻³.** 0.400 mol dm⁻³ NaOH (Mᵣ 40.0) = 0.400 × 40.0 = **16.0 g dm⁻³**.

**Ions in solution.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ Al₂(SO₄)₃:
n(salt) = 3.75 × 10⁻³ mol; n(SO₄²⁻) = 3 × 3.75 × 10⁻³ = **0.0113 mol**.

**Mixing.** Add amounts of the ion from each solution, then divide by the total volume. Never average concentrations.

## Precision

- Round to the significant figures of the **least precise** data value.
- Round once, at the end.
- In standard form the figures before × 10 are the significant figures.
- Volume 25.0 cm³ (3 s.f.) with concentration 0.15 mol dm⁻³ (2 s.f.) supports 2 s.f.

## Exam traps

- Answering in moles of molecules when atoms, ions or electrons are asked for.
- Using the proton number for electrons in an ion without the charge.
- c × V with V still in cm³.
- Multiplying by L when an amount is asked for, or forgetting it when a number is asked for.
- Averaging concentrations of mixed solutions.
- "L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol" (the unit is mol⁻¹).
- Rounding intermediate values.

## Self-test

1. What is the Avogadro constant?
2. How many moles of atoms are in 1 mol of C₂H₆?
3. How many electrons are in one hydroxide ion, OH⁻?
4. Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in 10.0 g of CaCO₃ (Mᵣ 100.1). L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
5. Calculate the mass of one atom of gold (Aᵣ 197.0) in grams.
6. A sample contains 3.011 × 10²³ molecules of O₂. What is its mass?
7. How many moles of ions are in 0.250 mol of Na₂SO₄?
8. Calculate the concentration, in mol dm⁻³, of 5.85 g of NaCl (Mᵣ 58.5) in 250 cm³ of solution.
9. What volume of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ HCl contains 0.0200 mol of HCl?
10. 50.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ CaCl₂ is mixed with 50.0 cm³ of water. What is the chloride ion concentration?
11. In 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O, how many moles of O₂ react with 1 mol of butane?
12. To how many significant figures should you report an answer from data of 0.0250 mol and 18 cm³?

### Answers

1. The number of particles in one mole, 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
2. 8 mol (2 C + 6 H).
3. 8 + 1 + 1 = **10**.
4. 0.0999 mol × 3 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.80 × 10²³**.
5. 197.0 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = **3.27 × 10⁻²² g**.
6. 0.500 mol × 32.0 = **16.0 g**.
7. 3 × 0.250 = **0.750 mol**.
8. 0.100 mol ÷ 0.250 dm³ = **0.400 mol dm⁻³**.
9. 0.0200 ÷ 0.500 = 0.0400 dm³ = **40.0 cm³**.
10. n(Cl⁻) = 2 × 0.100 × 0.0500 = 0.0100 mol; ÷ 0.100 dm³ = **0.100 mol dm⁻³**.
11. 13 ÷ 2 = **6.5 mol**.
12. **2**, because 18 cm³ has two significant figures.
