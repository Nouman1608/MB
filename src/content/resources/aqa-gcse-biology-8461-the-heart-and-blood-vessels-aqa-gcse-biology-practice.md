These questions cover section **4.2.2.2 The heart and blood vessels** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.2.2 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for questions 4, 7, 8 and 9. Data values are illustrative.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** Name the blood vessel that brings deoxygenated blood from the body to the heart. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart. **[1]**
**(c)** State which side of the heart contains deoxygenated blood. **[1]**

**2.** Describe what happens in the heart during one heartbeat, from blood entering the atria to blood leaving the ventricles. **[4]**

**3.** The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood. Explain why it is still classed as an artery. **[2]**

**4.** The table shows illustrative blood pressure values in four vessels (arbitrary units).

| Vessel | Aorta | Small artery | Capillary | Small vein |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure | 110 | 90 | 27 | 8 |

**(a)** Identify the vessel with the highest pressure. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in pressure from the small artery to the capillary. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the structure of the aorta wall suits the pressure inside it. **[2]**

**5.** Explain two advantages to a mammal of having a double circulatory system. **[4]**

**6.** In one lung disease the walls between neighbouring alveoli break down, leaving fewer, larger air sacs. Suggest and explain why a person with this disease becomes breathless during exercise. **[3]**

### Section B: calculations and longer answers

**7.** A student counts 23 pulse beats in 15 seconds just after exercise.

**(a)** Calculate the student's heart rate in beats per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** After five minutes' rest, the heart rate is 66 beats per minute. Calculate the percentage decrease from the rate in (a). Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** 2.7 dm³ of blood flows through a vessel in 45 seconds.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of flow in cm³ per second. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the rate in dm³ per minute. **[1]**

**9.** A red blood cell moves along a capillary at 0.5 mm per second. The capillary is 0.75 mm long.

**(a)** Calculate how long the cell takes to pass through the capillary. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the speed of the cell in µm per second. **[1]**

**10.** Compare the structure of arteries and capillaries, and relate each difference to the function of the vessel. **[4]**

**11.** A patient's natural pacemaker cells do not work properly, so the heart beats too slowly and irregularly.

**(a)** Suggest why the patient feels tired and breathless. **[2]**
**(b)** State how an artificial pacemaker corrects the problem. **[1]**

**12.** Explain how the structures of the heart and of the blood vessels help oxygen to reach respiring muscle cells quickly. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Vena cava [1]
**(b)** Pulmonary vein [1]
**(c)** The right side [1]
*Examiner insight:* Remember the left side of the heart appears on the right of a diagram, as if you are facing the person.

**2.** Blood enters the atria from the vena cava and the pulmonary vein [1]; the atria contract, pushing blood into the ventricles [1]; the ventricles contract, forcing blood into the pulmonary artery and the aorta [1]; valves close to stop blood flowing back into the chambers [1]
*Examiner insight:* The order matters: atria contract before ventricles.

**3.** Arteries are defined as vessels that carry blood away from the heart [1]; the pulmonary artery carries blood away from the right ventricle to the lungs [1]
*Examiner insight:* Oxygen content is not the test; direction is.

**4. (a)** The aorta [1]
**(b)** (90 − 27) ÷ 90 × 100 [1] = **70%** [1]
**(c)** The aorta has a thick wall with muscle and elastic fibres [1], so it can withstand the high pressure, stretching as blood surges in and recoiling afterwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) divide by the starting value, 90, not by 27.

**5.** Blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again [1], so it reaches the body at high pressure and substances are delivered quickly [1]. The two sides are separate, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix [1]; the blood sent to the body carries as much oxygen as possible, supporting a high rate of respiration [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each advantage needs a feature and its consequence for the body.

**6.** Fewer, larger air sacs have a smaller total surface area [1], so less oxygen diffuses into the blood per second [1]; during exercise muscles need more oxygen for respiration than can be supplied, so the person breathes faster and feels breathless [1]
*Examiner insight:* The answer applies the alveoli adaptations to a new situation; the link to surface area earns the first mark.

**7. (a)** 23 ÷ 15 × 60 [1] = **92 beats per minute** [1]
**(b)** (92 − 66) ÷ 92 × 100 [1] = **28.3%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Allow an answer to (b) carried forward from an incorrect (a).

**8. (a)** 2.7 dm³ = 2700 cm³ [1]; 2700 ÷ 45 = **60 cm³ per second** [1]
**(b)** 60 × 60 = 3600 cm³ = **3.6 dm³ per minute** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert dm³ to cm³ (× 1000) before dividing by time.

**9. (a)** Time = distance ÷ speed [1] = 0.75 ÷ 0.5 = **1.5 seconds** [1]
**(b)** 0.5 × 1000 = **500 µm per second** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange speed = distance ÷ time before substituting.

**10.** Any two differences, each with its link to function (2 marks each):
- arteries have thick walls with muscle and elastic fibres, whereas capillary walls are one cell thick [1]; arteries withstand high pressure, while capillaries give a short diffusion distance for exchange [1]
- arteries have a relatively wide lumen, whereas capillaries are very narrow [1]; arteries carry large volumes quickly away from the heart, while narrow capillaries reach between cells and slow the flow for exchange [1]
- (also accept: there are huge numbers of capillaries but fewer arteries, so every cell is close to a capillary)
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both vessels in each point; a separate paragraph on each vessel scores less.

**11. (a)** The heart pumps blood more slowly, so less oxygen reaches the muscles and other organs per minute [1]; cells respire less, so the patient feels tired, and the body breathes harder to try to get more oxygen [1]
**(b)** It is an electrical device that sends impulses to make the heart beat at a regular, normal rate [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" means apply your knowledge: link a slow heart rate to oxygen supply and respiration.

**12.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer links structure to speed of delivery):
- the heart is a muscular pump; the left ventricle has a thick muscular wall, so it pumps blood at high pressure [1]
- the double circulation returns blood from the lungs to be pumped again, so blood reaches the body at high pressure [1]
- valves stop backflow, so blood moves one way only [1]
- arteries have thick, elastic walls that withstand the pressure and keep blood moving [1]
- capillaries have walls one cell thick, so oxygen diffuses a short distance into muscle cells [1]
- capillaries are very numerous, so every muscle cell is close to a supply of oxygen [1]
*Examiner insight:* A list of structures without "so" links stays in the lower level; each point should end with how it speeds up oxygen delivery.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining arteries by oxygen content.
- Putting ventricular contraction before atrial contraction.
- Dividing by the wrong value in percentage change.
- Forgetting to convert dm³ to cm³, or mm to µm.
- Comparing vessels in separate paragraphs rather than matched points.
