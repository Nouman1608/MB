## What 3.6.2 asks you to know

| Statements | Content |
|---|---|
| a | Theories of evolution: the theory of evolution by natural selection, and other theories such as Lamarck's, based on the idea that changes during an organism's life can be inherited |
| b | Evolution occurs through natural selection; students should understand the time scales involved |
| c | New species arise as a result of isolation, genetic variation, natural selection and speciation |

The introduction to topic 3.6 adds the wider idea: all life today is descended from a universal common ancestor
that was a simple single-celled organism, and species that cannot respond to changes in their environment are at risk
of becoming **extinct**.

## Lamarck's idea and why it was replaced

Lamarck's theory is based mainly on the idea that **changes that occur in an organism during its lifetime can be
inherited**. A standard example is a giraffe that stretches its neck to reach leaves and has offspring with longer
necks.

The specification states that we now know that in the **vast majority of cases this type of inheritance cannot occur**.
Acquired characteristics (see 3.6.1) are caused by the environment and do not change the genes.

| | Lamarck's idea | Natural selection |
|---|---|---|
| Source of change | Changes in an individual's body during its life | Differences in genes between individuals |
| Inherited? | Acquired changes passed on | Only genes are passed on |
| Explains a long neck by | Stretching passes on a longer neck | Individuals with longer necks, because of their genes, survive and breed more |
| Accepted now? | Not in the vast majority of cases | Yes: the theory of evolution by natural selection |

## Natural selection

The specification gives three linked statements.

1. **Individual organisms within a species may show a wide range of variation because of differences in their genes.**
2. **Individuals with characteristics most suited to the environment are more likely to survive to breed
   successfully.**
3. **The genes that have enabled these individuals to survive are then passed on to the next generation.**

Add a fourth step in an extended answer: over many generations, the proportion of individuals with the helpful alleles
increases, so the population changes. This is evolution.

A full-mark answer uses the sequence: **variation → survival and breeding → genes passed on → proportion changes**.

### Worked example 1: a population changing over generations

Invented model. A beetle population on a field of dark soil has 1 000 beetles at the start: 500 brown and 500
green. Birds eat the beetles they can see. Each generation, 80 % of the brown beetles and 40 % of the green beetles
survive to breed. Brown parents produce brown offspring and green parents produce green offspring. The next
generation is again 1 000 beetles, in the same proportions as the survivors.

```
generation 1:  brown survivors = 500 × 0.8 = 400    green survivors = 500 × 0.4 = 200
               brown = 400 ÷ 600 × 100 = 66.7 %      (next generation: 667 brown, 333 green)
generation 2:  brown survivors = 667 × 0.8 = 533     green survivors = 333 × 0.4 = 133
               brown = 533 ÷ (533 + 133) × 100 = 80.0 %   (800 brown, 200 green)
generation 3:  brown survivors = 800 × 0.8 = 640     green survivors = 200 × 0.4 = 80
               brown = 640 ÷ 720 × 100 = 88.9 %      (889 brown, 111 green)
```

The proportion of brown beetles rises from 50 % to 66.7 %, 80.0 % and 88.9 %. Brown beetles are better camouflaged
against the dark soil, so they are more likely to survive to breed and pass on the genes for brown colour. Green beetles
are still present for several generations, because genes for green are still carried by some individuals. The model
is simplified: real survival rates vary and other factors are involved. It shows that natural selection acts
**gradually over generations**, and that **individuals do not change**; the **population** does.

### Worked example 2: change measured after a drought

Invented data. In a population of seed-eating birds, beak depth was measured before and after a drought that killed
most small, soft seeds, leaving large, hard seeds.

| | Before drought | After drought |
|---|---|---|
| Mean beak depth of adults (mm) | 9.2 | 9.9 |

```
change = 9.9 − 9.2 = 0.7 mm
percentage change = 0.7 ÷ 9.2 × 100 = 7.6 %
```

Interpretation: the birds with deeper beaks could crack the hard seeds, so they were more likely to survive and breed. The
characteristic is inherited, so the offspring of the survivors have, on average, deeper beaks. The mean beak depth of the
population increased by 7.6 %. A single bird's beak did not change during its lifetime.

## Time scales

Evolution by natural selection usually takes **many generations**. The time scale depends on how long a generation
lasts. A species with a short generation time, such as bacteria, can change over days or weeks. A species with a long
generation time may need thousands or millions of years for large changes.

```
number of generations × time per generation = total time
600 generations × 2 years per generation = 1 200 years
```

The specification asks for an **understanding** of time scales. You should be able to say that large changes such as new
species take a very long time, and that the time in years depends on the generation time.

## How new species arise

The specification lists four stages. They normally occur in this order.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Isolation** | Two populations of a species become separated, for example geographically |
| **Genetic variation** | Each population has a wide range of alleles controlling its characteristics |
| **Natural selection** | In each population the alleles controlling characteristics that help survival are selected |
| **Speciation** | The populations become so different that successful interbreeding, leading to fertile offspring, is no longer possible |

### Worked example 3: snails and a new river

Invented stimulus. "A population of land snails lives on a wooded hillside. A landslide creates a wide, fast river
across the hillside, separating the snails into a northern and a southern population. The north is shaded and damp. The
south is open and dry. After many thousands of generations, snails from the two sides will not produce fertile offspring
when they are brought together."

Applying the four stages:

1. Isolation: the river separates the two populations, so they cannot breed together.
2. Genetic variation: each population contains snails with different alleles for shell colour and thickness.
3. Natural selection: in the dry south, snails with thick, pale shells survive better, so those alleles become more
   common there; in the damp north, different alleles are selected.
4. Speciation: after many generations the populations are so different that they cannot interbreed successfully to
   produce fertile offspring, so they are now two species.

The key test of speciation is in the final stage: **fertile offspring** can no longer be produced.

## Extinction

Species that cannot respond sufficiently to changes in their environment are at risk of becoming extinct. If there is
no individual in the population with a characteristic suited to the new conditions, natural selection cannot help, and
the species may die out.

## Common mistakes

- Saying individuals "adapt" or "evolve" during their lives. Populations evolve over generations.
- Saying organisms "want" or "need" to change, or that the environment "makes" them change.
- Forgetting the genetic basis: survivors pass on **genes**, not features.
- Leaving out variation as the starting point.
- Writing "survival of the fittest" without saying that the fittest are best suited to the environment and that they
  must **breed**.
- Describing speciation without isolation, or defining it without fertile offspring.
- Presenting Lamarck's idea as correct in the vast majority of cases.

## Exam technique

- For **explain how natural selection occurs** questions, write the chain: variation (genes), better suited individuals
  survive and breed, genes passed on, the proportion rises.
- For data on a population, use values, calculate percentage changes and relate the change to survival and breeding.
- For **new species**, write the four stages in order, each with its reason.
- Use the word **allele** where relevant, and write "more likely to survive and breed", not "survive" only.
