Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science (0478) is a course about how computers work and how to make them do useful things. Half of it explains the machinery: how data is represented, stored and sent, what hardware and software do, and how the internet and newer technologies are used. The other half is about thinking like a programmer: designing algorithms, writing and testing programs, working with databases and reasoning with Boolean logic. The 2026-2028 syllabus is assessed by two compulsory written papers, Paper 1 Computer Systems and Paper 2 Algorithms, Programming and Logic, each lasting 105 minutes, each worth 75 marks and each carrying 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus sets out ten numbered topics. They fall naturally into two blocks, and those blocks match the two papers exactly.

**Computer systems (Topics 1 to 6)**

- **Topic 1, Data representation**: number systems; text, sound and images; data storage and compression. This is where binary and other number bases are introduced, along with how files of different kinds are encoded and made smaller.
- **Topic 2, Data transmission**: types and methods of data transmission, methods of error detection, and encryption. The focus is on how data travels and how it is checked and protected on the way.
- **Topic 3, Hardware**: computer architecture, input and output devices, data storage, and network hardware. This covers what is physically inside and around a computer system.
- **Topic 4, Software**: the programs that run on that hardware and how they relate to it.
- **Topic 5, The internet and its uses**: how the internet works and the services built on it.
- **Topic 6, Automated and emerging technologies**: systems that act with little human input, and developing technologies.

**Algorithms, programming and logic (Topics 7 to 10)**

- **Topic 7, Algorithm design and problem-solving**: planning solutions before they are coded.
- **Topic 8, Programming**: writing, reading and correcting program code.
- **Topic 9, Databases**: storing and retrieving structured data.
- **Topic 10, Boolean logic**: logic gates, expressions and the reasoning behind them.

The first block is largely about knowing and explaining; the second is largely about doing. Both need precise technical language.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Computer Systems | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% | Topics 1-6 |
| Paper 2 | Algorithms, Programming and Logic | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% | Topics 7-10 |

Some points about the assessment model:

- **Both papers are compulsory.** There are no optional routes and no coursework component: the two written papers make up the whole qualification.
- **Not tiered.** Every candidate sits the same two papers, and grades run from A* to G.
- **Linear.** The qualification is assessed through the two papers taken in the same examination series, rather than in stages.
- **Question styles differ.** Paper 1 uses short-answer and structured questions. Paper 2 uses short-answer, structured and scenario-based questions, so you will often be asked to apply your skills to an unfamiliar situation described in the question.
- **No calculators** are permitted in either paper, which matters for the number-base and file-size work in Topic 1.
- Both papers are externally assessed. First assessment under this syllabus is 2026, and it covers examinations in 2026 to 2028.

## How the parts fit together

Although the two papers test different topics, the course is not two unrelated halves. Binary from Topic 1 reappears in Topic 10, where the truth values of logic gates are simply ones and zeros, and it sits behind the storage and compression ideas in Topic 3. Error detection and encryption in Topic 2 make more sense once you are comfortable with how data is represented as bits.

On the practical side, Topics 7 and 8 work as a pair: an algorithm is a plan and a program is that plan written in code, so practising one sharpens the other. The conditions you write inside selection and loop statements are Boolean expressions, which is why Topic 10 is easier for students who programme regularly. Databases in Topic 9 ask you to describe and query data precisely, using the same careful logical thinking.

Three skills run across the whole course: using exact technical vocabulary, reading a scenario carefully before answering, and working through a process step by step, whether that is converting a number, tracing an algorithm or completing a truth table.

## Planning your study

A sensible route through the course runs two strands side by side rather than one after the other.

1. **Start the theory with Topic 1.** Number systems and data representation underpin a great deal that follows, so it pays to secure them first, then move through transmission and hardware.
2. **Begin programming early and keep it going.** Programming skill grows with frequent, short practice. Introduce Topics 7 and 8 near the start of the course and return to them every week or so, rather than saving them for the end.
3. **Add Software, The internet and its uses, and Automated and emerging technologies** once the hardware topic is in place, since they build on it.
4. **Bring in Databases and Boolean logic** once programming conditions and binary feel comfortable.

Over the year, a practical shape is: early on, Topics 1 to 3 alongside introductory algorithms and programming; in the middle, Topics 4 to 6 with more demanding programming and Topic 9; towards the end, Topic 10, a full review of both blocks, and timed practice. Begin full exam-style work once you have met every topic at least once, and use the course's exam-preparation page for paper-specific planning rather than improvising your own timings.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478 have?**
Two, both compulsory: Paper 1 Computer Systems and Paper 2 Algorithms, Programming and Logic. Each lasts 105 minutes, is worth 75 marks and carries 50% of the qualification.

**Which topics are tested on each paper?**
Paper 1 covers Topics 1 to 6, from Data representation to Automated and emerging technologies. Paper 2 covers Topics 7 to 10: Algorithm design and problem-solving, Programming, Databases and Boolean logic.

**Is IGCSE Computer Science 0478 tiered?**
No. Every candidate sits the same papers, and grades run from A* to G.

**Can I use a calculator in the 0478 exams?**
No. Calculators are not permitted in either paper, so practise number conversions and file-size working by hand.

**Which years does this syllabus cover?**
This page describes the 2026-2028 syllabus, with first assessment in 2026.
