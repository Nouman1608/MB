Take g = 9.81 m s⁻².

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the equation for the period of a mass-spring system and name two quantities that do not affect this period. **[2]**

**2.** A 0.60 kg mass oscillates on a spring with period 0.95 s. Calculate the spring constant. **[2]**

**3.** A simple pendulum has length 0.65 m.

**(a)** Calculate its period. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its frequency. **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** State the condition needed for the equation T = 2π√(l/g) to apply to a pendulum. **[1]**

**(b)** State the two points between which the length of a pendulum is measured. **[1]**

**5.**

**(a)** By what factor must the length of a pendulum be changed to double its period? **[1]**

**(b)** The mass on a spring is doubled. By what factor must the spring constant change to keep the same period? **[1]**

**6.** Describe what is meant by light damping and state its effect on the period of an oscillator. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A 0.50 kg trolley on a horizontal track is attached between springs with a combined spring constant of 72 N m⁻¹. It oscillates with amplitude 0.060 m. Ignore friction.

**(a)** Calculate the period. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total energy of the oscillation. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the speed of the trolley when its displacement is 0.030 m. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the displacement at which the kinetic and potential energies are equal. **[2]**

**8.** An undamped oscillator has period 1.6 s.

**(a)** Describe how its kinetic energy, potential energy and total energy vary with displacement. **[3]**

**(b)** State the period of the variation of its potential energy with time. **[1]**

**9.** In Required practical 4 a student times 20 oscillations of a simple pendulum for four lengths.

| l / m | 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.70 | 0.90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20T / s | 22.0 | 28.4 | 33.6 | 38.1 |

**(a)** Calculate T² for each length. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the first and last points to find the gradient of a graph of T² against l, and hence g. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the student timed 20 oscillations. **[1]**

**(d)** The student measured each length to the top of the bob instead of its centre. State and explain the effect on the value of g found from the gradient. **[1]**

**10.**

**(a)** Describe the motion of an oscillator that is lightly damped, critically damped and heavily damped, after it is displaced and released. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the suspension of a car is designed to be close to critically damped. **[1]**

**11.** A pendulum of length 0.90 m has a bob of mass 0.12 kg. It is released from rest with the bob 2.5 cm above its lowest point.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the bob. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum speed of the bob. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the period. **[1]**

**(d)** After 30 oscillations the bob rises only 1.5 cm above its lowest point. Calculate the energy transferred to the surroundings, and state what this shows about the oscillation. **[2]**

**12.** A graph of T² against m for a mass-spring system is a straight line of gradient 0.80 s² kg⁻¹. It crosses the m axis slightly to the left of the origin.

**(a)** Calculate the spring constant. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the line does not pass through the origin. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** T = 2π√(m/k) [1]; any two of: amplitude (while Hooke's law is obeyed), gravitational field strength g, whether the system oscillates horizontally or vertically [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* g does not appear in the mass-spring equation, so the period is the same on the Moon.

**2.** k = 4π²m/T² [1] = 4π² × 0.60 / 0.95² = 26 N m⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before substituting; squaring T is the usual slip.

**3. (a)** T = 2π√(0.65/9.81) = 1.6 s [1]

**(b)** f = 1/T = 0.62 Hz [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Give the frequency from the unrounded period (1.617 s).

**4. (a)** Small angles of swing (less than about 10°) [1]

**(b)** From the point of suspension to the centre of mass of the bob [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "To the bob" is too vague; say centre of mass.

**5. (a)** × 4 [1]

**(b)** × 2 [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* T depends on m/k, so the ratio must stay the same.

**6.** Resistive forces transfer energy to the surroundings so the amplitude decreases gradually over many oscillations [1]; the period is almost unchanged [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Light damping is defined by the system still oscillating many times.

**7. (a)** T = 2π√(0.50/72) = 0.52 s [1]

**(b)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 72 × 0.060² = 0.13 J [1]

**(c)** Ep = ½ × 72 × 0.030² = 0.0324 J, so Ek = 0.1296 − 0.0324 = 0.0972 J [1]; v = √(2 × 0.0972 / 0.50) = 0.62 m s⁻¹ [1]

**(d)** Ep = ½ × total, so ½kx² = ¼kA² [1]; x = A/√2 = 0.042 m [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* At half the amplitude the potential energy is only a quarter of the total, not half.

**8. (a)** Kinetic energy is maximum at equilibrium and zero at maximum displacement [1]; potential energy is zero at equilibrium and maximum at maximum displacement (both parabolic) [1]; total energy is constant at all displacements [1]

**(b)** 0.80 s [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Potential energy peaks at both extremes, so it repeats twice per oscillation.

**9. (a)** T = 1.100, 1.420, 1.680, 1.905 s, so T² = 1.21, 2.02, 2.82, 3.63 s² [1]

**(b)** Gradient = (3.63 − 1.21) / (0.90 − 0.30) [1] = 4.03 s² m⁻¹ [1]; g = 4π² / 4.03 = 9.8 m s⁻² [1]

**(c)** Reaction time is a smaller fraction of the total time, reducing the percentage uncertainty in T [1]

**(d)** No effect: every length is too short by the same amount, which shifts the line sideways without changing its gradient [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A constant error in one variable changes the intercept, not the gradient.

**10. (a)** Light: oscillates many times with gradually decreasing amplitude [1]; critical: returns to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillating [1]; heavy: returns to equilibrium without oscillating but more slowly [1]

**(b)** After a bump the car body returns to its normal position quickly without continuing to bounce [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Quickest return with no oscillation" is the key phrase for critical damping.

**11. (a)** Ek max = mgh = 0.12 × 9.81 × 0.025 = 0.029 J [1]

**(b)** v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.025) = 0.70 m s⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** T = 2π√(0.90/9.81) = 1.9 s [1]

**(d)** Energy lost = mgΔh = 0.12 × 9.81 × 0.010 = 0.012 J [1]; the oscillation is lightly damped, since the amplitude falls slowly over many swings [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The energy lost depends on the change in height, 1.0 cm, not on 1.5 cm.

**12. (a)** k = 4π² / gradient [1] = 39.5 / 0.80 = 49 N m⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** The spring's own mass also oscillates, adding to the effective mass, so T² is not zero when the added mass m is zero [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An intercept to the left of the origin means some extra mass is moving that was not included in m.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using T instead of T² on graphs.
- Not dividing the total time by the number of oscillations.
- Assuming half the amplitude means half the energy.
- Using the wrong length for a pendulum.
- Confusing critical and heavy damping.
- Claiming a constant length error changes the gradient.
