## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *cash flow forecast*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** examples of cash outflows for a restaurant. **[2]**

**3.** Wenmoor Florists has an opening balance of £2,400. In June, cash inflows are £11,300 and cash outflows are £12,900. Calculate the closing balance for June. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** solutions to a cash flow problem. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** consequence for a business of running out of cash. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why profit and cash are not the same. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Trulow Bike Repairs has the following cash flow forecast.

| £ | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash inflows | 8,000 | 7,000 | 12,500 |
| Cash outflows | 9,200 | 8,800 | 9,000 |
| Net cash flow | (i) | −1,800 | (iii) |
| Opening balance | −500 | −1,700 | −3,500 |
| Closing balance | −1,700 | (ii) | (iv) |

**(a)** Calculate the missing figures (i) to (iv). **[4]**
**(b)** Explain what the forecast shows about the business's cash position. **[3]**

**8.** Cuberly Catering supplied a wedding in April and sent an invoice for £9,500, to be paid in June. In April it paid £6,200 for food and staff.

**(a)** Calculate Cuberly Catering's profit on the wedding. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the effect of the wedding on Cuberly Catering's cash in April. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the business might need an overdraft even though the wedding was profitable. **[3]**

**9.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of a business offering customers a discount for paying early. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of positive cash flow. **[2]**

**11.** Tregowan Toys makes most of its sales in November and December, but buys most of its stock between August and October. Its cash flow forecast shows a negative closing balance of £14,000 in October. Analyse **two** ways Tregowan Toys could solve this cash flow problem. **[6]**

**12.** Ponsmoor Print's cash flow forecast shows negative closing balances in every month of the next year, getting worse each month. Its costs have risen sharply while sales have stayed the same. The owner plans to ask the bank for a larger overdraft. Evaluate whether an overdraft is the best solution for Ponsmoor Print. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A prediction of the cash inflows and outflows [1] of a business over future months, showing the expected balance [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: wages, food and drink supplies, rent, utility bills, loan repayments [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Net cash flow £11,300 − £12,900 = −£1,600 [1]; closing balance £2,400 − £1,600 = **£800** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two of: re-scheduling payments, an overdraft, reducing cash outflow, increasing cash inflow, finding new sources of finance [1] [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The business cannot pay its suppliers on time [1], so suppliers may stop delivering or refuse credit [1], which disrupts production or sales and could lead to insolvency [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Profit is recorded when a sale is made, but on a credit sale the cash arrives later [1], so a business can show a profit while having little cash in the bank [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** (i) 8,000 − 9,200 = **−1,200** [1]. (ii) −1,700 + (−1,800) = **−3,500** [1]. (iii) 12,500 − 9,000 = **3,500** [1]. (iv) −3,500 + 3,500 = **0** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The business starts October overdrawn and stays overdrawn at the end of October and November [1], with the shortfall growing to £3,500 by the end of November because outflows exceed inflows in October and November [1]. December's higher inflows bring the balance back to zero, so the problem is seasonal, but it still needs an overdraft to cover October and November [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** £9,500 − £6,200 = **£3,300** profit [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Cash falls by **£6,200** in April, with no inflow from the wedding [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The profit is only received in cash in June [1], but Cuberly has already paid £6,200 in April [1], so if its balance was low, it may not have enough cash to pay other bills in April and May, needing an overdraft until the customer pays [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Advantage: customers pay sooner [1], so cash comes into the business earlier and helps it pay its own bills [1]. Disadvantage: the business receives less for each sale [1], so revenue and profit fall [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The business can pay its bills on time [1], so it keeps the trust of suppliers and avoids the cost of an overdraft [1]. Also accept taking discounts, funding growth or having a safety margin, with development. **[2]**

**11.** First, an overdraft [1]: the shortage is seasonal and will be covered by November and December sales, so short-term borrowing fits the problem [1], although interest will be charged on the £14,000 [1]. Second, re-scheduling payments: Tregowan could ask suppliers for longer credit, paying for stock in November or December [1], when sales cash arrives, so it avoids interest [1], although suppliers may refuse or charge more for later payment [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: an overdraft lets a business spend more than it has, up to a limit, with interest on the amount used [1].
Application: the shortage is not seasonal but grows every month [1]; it is caused by rising costs while sales are flat [1].
Analysis: an overdraft would let the business keep paying bills in the short term [1], but interest adds to costs, making the shortage worse each month [1]; it treats the symptom, not the cause, which is costs higher than income [1].
Evaluation: the bank may refuse a larger overdraft for a business whose forecast never returns to a positive balance [1]; reducing cash outflows, such as finding cheaper suppliers, or increasing inflows through higher prices or promotion would tackle the cause [1]. Judgement: an overdraft is not the best solution on its own; it may buy a little time, but the owner must cut costs or raise sales, otherwise the business risks insolvency [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The key is noticing that the problem gets worse every month, so a short-term solution cannot fix it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding a negative net cash flow as if it were positive.
- Forgetting to carry the closing balance forward as the next opening balance.
- Treating profit and cash as the same thing.
- Recommending an overdraft for a long-term, worsening shortage.
- Describing what a forecast shows without saying when the problem occurs or why.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I check my forecast answers?**
For each month, check that inflows minus outflows equals net cash flow, that opening balance plus net cash flow equals closing balance, and that each closing balance matches the next opening balance.

**How do I choose a solution to a cash flow problem?**
Find the cause and length of the shortfall in the forecast, then pick the solution that fixes that cause; short-term gaps suit short-term solutions.
