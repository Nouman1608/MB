AQA A-Level Accounting 7127 is a linear, untiered qualification assessed by two written papers of
identical shape. Each lasts 3 hours, carries 120 marks and is worth 50% of the A-level, and each is
divided into three compulsory sections: Section A (10 multiple-choice questions plus short-answer, 30
marks), Section B (two structured questions, 40 marks) and Section C (two extended-answer questions,
50 marks). The papers differ in content: Paper 1 draws on specification sections 1-8 and 14-18, Paper
2 on sections 1-3, 8-13 and 17-18. These notes complement the site's guides to
[The Role of the Accountant in Business](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-role-of-the-accountant/)
and
[Types of Business Organisation](/resources/a-level-aqa-accounting-types-of-business-organisation/).

## The two papers overlap -- so some content is examinable twice

Sections 1-3, 8 and 17-18 appear in the content list for *both* papers. That overlap is a strategic
fact, not a curiosity: the foundational material on the accounting equation, double entry, and the
role and limitations of accounting is the highest-value revision on the whole course, because it can
be tested in either sitting. **Exam-preparation priority**: revise the shared sections first and to a
higher standard than the paper-specific ones, since every hour spent on them is an hour that
contributes to both 50% components.

## Section C is 50 of 120 marks -- almost as much as Sections A and B combined

The two extended-answer questions in Section C carry more marks than either other section, and they
are the part of the paper that most rewards written evaluation rather than computation. **Exam-
preparation priority**: do not treat extended answers as the part you get to if there is time. Work
backwards from a target: at roughly 1.5 minutes per mark, Section C needs about 75 minutes of a
180-minute paper, Section B about 60, and Section A about 45. Practising to that allocation is what
prevents the familiar pattern of immaculate ledger work followed by a two-paragraph evaluation.

## Section A mixes multiple choice with short answer -- and they need different speeds

Section A's 30 marks combine 10 multiple-choice questions with short-answer questions. Multiple-choice
accounting questions frequently hide a two-step calculation behind a one-line stem. **Exam-preparation
priority**: practise multiple-choice items specifically, timed. The risk is not getting them wrong
through ignorance but spending six minutes on one because the arithmetic did not resolve first time --
time that belongs to Section C.

## Presentation is worth marks in a way it is not in most subjects

Accounting rewards correctly formatted statements: headings, dates, correct ordering, workings shown
and labelled. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise producing statements in full, correct format from
memory rather than filling in pre-drawn pro-formas. A candidate who has only ever completed templates
often loses presentation marks in an exam where no template is provided.

## The own-figure rule makes finishing a question worthwhile

Where a later part of a question uses a figure calculated earlier, a wrong earlier figure carried
forward correctly is normally still credited for method. That only works if the working is visible and
the carry-forward is explicit. **Exam-preparation priority**: never abandon a multi-part question
because part (a) looked wrong -- state the figure you are carrying and continue.

## Worked routine: protecting marks in a multi-part computation

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - label the statement properly before any figures:
Name of business, name of the statement, and the period or date
it covers. These are creditable and take fifteen seconds.

Step 2 - show workings separately and reference them:
"Depreciation (W1)" with W1 set out below the statement, rather
than arithmetic squeezed into the statement itself.

Step 3 - if a figure is uncertain, state the assumption:
One line -- "assuming the machine was owned for the full year" --
lets a marker follow the reasoning rather than guess at it.

Step 4 - carry your own figure forward explicitly:
"Using profit from (a) of 41,200..." so the method marks in the
later part are available even if (a) was wrong.

Step 5 - in Section C, answer the evaluative question asked:
State a position, support it with two specific figures from the
scenario, acknowledge one limitation of the data, and conclude.
```

Step 5 is where Section C marks concentrate: an evaluation built on figures drawn from the scenario
reads quite differently from one built on general accounting principles.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise the shared content sections (1-3, 8, 17-18) first and hardest, since
  they are examinable on both papers; practise producing statements in full format from memory, not
  from pro-formas; time multiple-choice practice specifically.
- **During either paper**: hold to roughly 45 minutes for Section A, 60 for Section B and 75 for
  Section C.
- **In every computation**: label the statement, show and reference workings, state any assumption.
- **In every multi-part question**: carry your own figure forward explicitly rather than abandoning
  the question.
- **In Section C**: support the evaluation with specific figures from the scenario and finish with a
  conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Which specification sections appear on both papers, and why does that matter for revision order?
2. How should 180 minutes be allocated across the three sections?
3. Why is practising with pro-formas potentially misleading?
4. What makes carrying an own figure forward worthwhile even when it is probably wrong?

**Answers:** 1. Sections 1-3, 8 and 17-18 -- revising them well contributes to both 50% papers, so
they carry double the return of paper-specific content. 2. Roughly 45 minutes for Section A (30
marks), 60 for Section B (40 marks) and 75 for Section C (50 marks), at about 1.5 minutes per mark.
3. Because the exam supplies no template, so a candidate who has only completed pre-drawn statements
may lose presentation marks for headings, dates and ordering they never had to produce themselves.
4. Because method marks in the later part are normally still available for a correctly applied own
figure -- but only if the working is visible and the carry-forward is stated.
