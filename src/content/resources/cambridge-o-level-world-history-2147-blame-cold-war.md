**Key question 4 of Core Content Option B in Cambridge O Level History 2147 asks "Who was to blame for the Cold
War?"** The final focus point puts the choice directly: was the United States or the USSR more to blame for starting
it? To answer well you need the sequence of events from 1945 to 1949, an understanding of what each side feared, and
the ability to show how actions on one side produced reactions on the other. The question was the Paper 2 prescribed
topic for November 2024 and remains on Paper 1 throughout 2024–2026.

Short recall is in the [Cold War origins revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-blame-cold-war-revision-notes/),
and exam questions with mark schemes are in the
[Cold War origins practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-blame-cold-war-practice/). The
[O Level twentieth century study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-twentieth-century-1919-1989/) gives the
overview of Option B.

## What the specification sets

Focus points:

1. Why did the US–Soviet alliance begin to break down in 1945?
2. How had the USSR gained control of Eastern Europe by 1948?
3. How did the United States react to Soviet expansionism?
4. What were the consequences of the Berlin Blockade?
5. Who was more to blame for starting the Cold War: the United States or the USSR?

Specified content: the 1945 summit conferences and the breakdown of the US–Soviet alliance in 1945–46; Soviet
expansion into Eastern Europe to 1948 and American reactions to it; the occupation of Germany and the Berlin Blockade;
NATO and the Warsaw Pact.

## Why did the alliance break down in 1945?

The wartime alliance rested on a common enemy. Once Germany was beaten, older differences came back.

**Ideology.** The USA stood for capitalism, free elections and free trade; the USSR for a one-party communist state
and a planned economy. Each believed the other wanted to spread its system.

**Yalta, February 1945.** Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin agreed that Germany would be divided into zones, that
Eastern European countries would hold free elections, that the USSR would join the war against Japan, and that a
United Nations would be set up. They disagreed over Poland: Stalin wanted a friendly government and a frontier moved
westwards, and he already backed a communist-led Polish government based at Lublin.

**Potsdam, July–August 1945.** The cast had changed. Truman had replaced Roosevelt, who died in April, and Attlee
replaced Churchill during the conference. Truman was more suspicious of Stalin. They confirmed the division of Germany
and agreed on reparations, but argued over Poland's western frontier and over how much Stalin could take from Germany.
Truman told Stalin of a powerful new weapon; the atomic bomb was dropped on Hiroshima on 6 August, days after the
conference ended.

**1946: positions harden.** The American diplomat George Kennan's "Long Telegram" (February 1946) argued that Soviet
expansion had to be firmly contained. Churchill's "Iron Curtain" speech at Fulton, Missouri (March 1946) described a
divided Europe. The Soviet ambassador in Washington, Nikolai Novikov, sent a telegram in September 1946 describing US
policy as a bid for world domination. Each side's leaders were now being told the other was a threat.

## How had the USSR gained control of Eastern Europe by 1948?

| Country | How control was established |
|---|---|
| Poland | The Lublin Poles were installed as the government; non-communist leaders were pushed out; rigged elections in January 1947 |
| Hungary | Communists, led by Rákosi, divided and removed rival parties step by step ("salami tactics") and won control by 1947–49 |
| Romania and Bulgaria | Soviet troops present; communist-led coalitions became communist governments by 1947 |
| East Germany | Soviet zone run by Soviet officials and German communists |
| Czechoslovakia | Communist takeover in February 1948; the foreign minister Jan Masaryk died in March in suspicious circumstances |
| Yugoslavia | Communist under Tito, but independent of Moscow; expelled from the Cominform in 1948 |

The USSR also created the **Cominform** (1947) to co-ordinate communist parties and **Comecon** (1949) to bind the
economies of Eastern Europe to its own.

**Stalin's motives** are the core of the blame debate. He could argue that the USSR had been invaded through Eastern
Europe twice in thirty years, had lost more than 20 million people in the war, and needed friendly governments as a buffer.
Critics argue that he broke the Yalta promise of free elections and imposed communism by force.

## How did the USA react?

- **The Truman Doctrine (March 1947):** Truman asked Congress for aid to Greece and Turkey, where Britain could no
  longer afford to support governments against communist pressure, and declared that the USA would support free
  peoples resisting subjugation. This was the policy of **containment**.
- **The Marshall Plan (announced June 1947):** economic aid to rebuild Europe, about $13 billion over four years.
  It aimed to reduce poverty, which the USA believed helped communism to spread, and to create markets for US goods.
  Stalin refused it for the USSR and made the Eastern European states refuse it, calling it "dollar imperialism".

## The occupation of Germany and the Berlin Blockade

Germany and Berlin were divided into four zones. The Western powers wanted German economic recovery; the USSR wanted
reparations and a weak Germany. In 1947 the British and American zones were merged (Bizonia), later joined by the
French zone, and in June 1948 a new currency, the Deutschmark, was introduced in the Western zones.

On **24 June 1948** Stalin cut road, rail and canal links to West Berlin, which lay deep inside the Soviet zone.
The Western powers responded with the **Berlin Airlift**, flying in food, fuel and supplies for nearly eleven months.
Stalin lifted the blockade in **May 1949**.

**Consequences:**
- The division of Germany became permanent: the Federal Republic of Germany (West) was founded in May 1949 and the
  German Democratic Republic (East) in October 1949.
- **NATO** was formed in April 1949, committing the USA to the defence of Western Europe in peacetime for the first
  time.
- Berlin became a symbol of Western resolve and a flashpoint for the next forty years.
- The USSR tested its first atomic bomb in August 1949, beginning a nuclear arms race.

When West Germany joined NATO in 1955, the USSR and its satellites formed the **Warsaw Pact**. Europe was now divided
into two armed alliances.

## Who was more to blame?

Historians have argued about this in three broad schools:

- **Orthodox view (dominant in the West in the early Cold War):** the USSR was to blame; Stalin's expansion in Eastern
  Europe forced the USA to respond.
- **Revisionist view (from the 1960s):** the USA was to blame, because it used its economic power and the atomic bomb to
  push its interests, and Marshall Aid was designed to dominate Europe; the USSR acted defensively.
- **Post-revisionist view:** both sides contributed; each misread the other's defensive moves as aggression.

| USSR more to blame | USA more to blame |
|---|---|
| Broke the Yalta promise of free elections | Truman's hostile tone and use of the atomic bomb as a diplomatic weapon |
| Salami tactics and the Czech coup of 1948 | The Truman Doctrine divided the world into two camps |
| Blockade of Berlin was an aggressive act against civilians | Marshall Aid and the new currency divided Germany without Soviet agreement |
| Cominform and Comecon tightened control | NATO was formed before the Warsaw Pact |

## Annotated model answer: a (c) question

*"The USSR was more to blame than the USA for starting the Cold War." How far do you agree? Explain your answer.*

> The USSR was largely to blame because, despite promising free elections at Yalta, Stalin imposed communist
> governments across Eastern Europe, using rigged elections in Poland in 1947 and a coup in Czechoslovakia in 1948.
> **[Agree, with specific evidence.]** However, the USA also raised tension. The Truman Doctrine of 1947 presented the
> world as divided between free and communist peoples, and the Marshall Plan, which Stalin saw as a way to buy influence,
> led directly to the Cominform and to the currency reform that triggered the Berlin Blockade. **[Counter-argument
> explained, showing action and reaction.]** Overall the USSR was slightly more to blame, because its takeover of
> Eastern Europe came first and broke an agreement, but the Cold War developed as each side reacted to the other's
> fears. **[Judgement with a criterion: who acted first and who broke agreements.]**

## Common mistakes

- Dating the Warsaw Pact to 1949. It was formed in 1955.
- Saying the Marshall Plan was military aid; it was economic.
- Treating the Berlin Blockade and the Berlin Wall (1961) as the same event.
- Answering the blame question with a narrative and no weighing.

## Exam technique for 2147

- **Describe**: a summit or the airlift in specific steps.
- **Explain**: why one side acted as it did, linked to its fears.
- **How far**: compare the evidence against each side and decide using a clear criterion.
- **Sources**: Cold War cartoons and speeches are propaganda by design. Always ask which side produced them.
