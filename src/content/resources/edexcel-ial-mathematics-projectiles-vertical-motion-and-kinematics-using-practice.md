These questions cover Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level Mathematics (YMA01), Unit M2 section 1, topics 1.1 to 1.4, from the *Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Mathematics, Further Mathematics and Pure Mathematics* specification, Issue 3, April 2019. M2 is listed as an optional unit for IAS Further Mathematics and for IAL Mathematics and Further Mathematics. Each question that needs g says to use 9.8 m s⁻². Give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 12 m s⁻¹ from a balcony 8 m above horizontal ground. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Find the greatest height of the ball above the ground. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the time from projection until the ball hits the ground. **[3]**

**2.** A stone is thrown vertically upwards from the ground and returns to the ground after 5 s. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Find the speed of projection. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the greatest height reached. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the two times at which the stone is 20 m above the ground. **[3]**

**3.** A ball A is dropped from rest from the top of a tower 40 m high. At the same instant a ball B is projected vertically upwards from the foot of the tower at 20 m s⁻¹. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Show that the balls meet 2 s after release. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the height above the ground at which they meet. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the speed of each ball at the instant they meet. **[2]**

**4.** A ball is projected from level ground at 28 m s⁻¹ at 35° above the horizontal. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Find how long the ball is in the air. **[3]**
**(b)** Find how far it travels horizontally. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the greatest height above the ground. **[2]**

**5.** A ball is thrown horizontally at 14 m s⁻¹ from the top of a tower 44.1 m above level ground. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Find the time taken to reach the ground and the horizontal distance travelled. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the speed of the ball on landing, and the angle its direction makes with the horizontal. **[4]**

**6.** A particle is projected from a point O on level ground at 45° to the horizontal. It passes through a point that is 24 m horizontally and 6 m vertically above O. Find the speed of projection. Take g = 9.8. **[6]**

**7.** A particle moves on a straight line. Its displacement from O at time t seconds is x = 2t³ − 15t² + 36t metres, for t ≥ 0.

**(a)** Find expressions for the velocity and acceleration at time t. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the times when the particle is instantaneously at rest. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the total distance travelled in the first 4 s. **[4]**

**8.** A particle travels along a line with acceleration (12 − 6t) m s⁻² at time t seconds. At t = 0 the particle is at O with velocity −9 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity at time t and the displacement from O at time t. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the total distance travelled in the first 3 s. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the displacement from O and the speed when t = 5. **[2]**

**9.** A particle has position vector r = (t³ − 6t)i + (4t − t²)j metres at time t seconds.

**(a)** Find the velocity and acceleration at t = 3. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the speed when t = 3. **[1]**
**(c)** Find when the particle's velocity points along j. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the exact position vector at that time. **[2]**

**10.** A particle has acceleration a = (6t − 5)i + (8 − 12t)j m s⁻² at time t seconds. When t = 0, its velocity is (4i + 5j) m s⁻¹ and its position vector is (2i − j) m.

**(a)** Find the velocity at time t. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the position vector at time t. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the distance of the particle from the origin when t = 2. **[2]**

**11.** A ball is projected from the top of a tower, 30.4 m above level ground, with speed 15 m s⁻¹ at an angle α above the horizontal, where sin α = 0.8. Take g = 9.8.

**(a)** Show that the ball hits the ground 4 s after projection. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the horizontal distance of the landing point from the tower. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the speed of the ball on landing and the angle its path makes with the horizontal. **[4]**
**(d)** Find the greatest height of the ball above the ground. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Up positive: 0 = 12² − 2(9.8)s [1], s = 144/19.6 = 7.347 [1]. Greatest height = 8 + 7.35 = **15.3 m** [1]
**(b)** s = −8: −8 = 12t − 4.9t² [1], so 4.9t² − 12t − 8 = 0 and t = (12 + √(144 + 156.8))/9.8 [1], giving **t = 2.99 s** (negative root rejected) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the launch point as the origin, so the ground is s = −8. Using s = +8 gives a different quadratic.

**2. (a)** Time up is 2.5 s and v = 0 there, so 0 = u − 9.8(2.5) [1], **u = 24.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** H = 24.5² / 19.6 [1] = **30.6 m** (30.625) [1]
**(c)** 20 = 24.5t − 4.9t² [1], so 4.9t² − 24.5t + 20 = 0 and t = (24.5 ± √208.25)/9.8 [1], giving **t = 1.03 s and t = 3.97 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Two times" signals a quadratic with two valid roots.

**3. (a)** Height of A above the ground = 40 − 4.9t² [1]. Height of B = 20t − 4.9t² [1]. They meet when 40 − 4.9t² = 20t − 4.9t² [1], so 40 = 20t and **t = 2 s** [1]
**(b)** 20(2) − 4.9(4) = **20.4 m** [1]
**(c)** Speed of A = 9.8(2) = **19.6 m s⁻¹** [1]. Speed of B = |20 − 9.8(2)| = **0.4 m s⁻¹** (moving upwards) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs the equation set up and reduced in full. The 4.9t² terms cancelling is the key line to show.

**4. (a)** Vertical component = 28 sin 35° = 16.06 [1]. 0 = 16.06t − 4.9t² [1], so **T = 3.28 s** [1]
**(b)** Horizontal component = 28 cos 35° = 22.94 [1]. Range = 22.94 × 3.2776 = **75.2 m** [1]
**(c)** H = 16.06² / 19.6 [1] = **13.2 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values from part (a) into part (b). Early rounding can shift the third significant figure.

**5. (a)** 44.1 = 4.9t² [1], so **t = 3 s** [1]. Distance = 14 × 3 = **42 m** [1]
**(b)** Vertical speed = 9.8 × 3 = 29.4 [1]. Speed = √(14² + 29.4²) = √1060.36 [1] = **32.6 m s⁻¹** [1]. tan θ = 29.4/14, so **θ = 64.5° below the horizontal** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the angle with a reference, such as "below the horizontal". An angle with no reference can lose the last mark.

**6.** Let w be the horizontal and vertical component of the initial velocity (equal at 45°). Horizontal: 24 = wt [1]. Vertical: 6 = wt − 4.9t² [1]. Substitute: 6 = 24 − 4.9t² [1], so t² = 18/4.9 and t = 1.917 [1]. w = 24/1.917 = 12.52 [1]. Speed = w√2 = **17.7 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the two component equations and join them through t. A bare final answer leaves nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**7. (a)** **v = 6t² − 30t + 36** [1], **a = 12t − 30** [1]
**(b)** 6(t² − 5t + 6) = 0 [1], so **t = 2 and t = 3** [1]
**(c)** x(0) = 0, x(2) = 28, x(3) = 27, x(4) = 32 [2]. Distance = 28 + 1 + 5 [1] = **34 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Split the journey at the turning points. Using x(4) − x(0) = 32 gives displacement, not distance.

**8. (a)** v = 12t − 3t² + c, and v(0) = −9 gives c = −9 [1], so **v = 12t − 3t² − 9** [1]. x = 6t² − t³ − 9t + d with d = 0 [1], so **x = 6t² − t³ − 9t** [1]
**(b)** v = −3(t − 1)(t − 3), so the particle turns at t = 1, where x = −4. At t = 3, x = 0. Distance = 4 + 4 = **8 m** [2]
**(c)** x(5) = 150 − 125 − 45 = **−20 m** [1]. v(5) = 60 − 75 − 9 = −24, so the speed is **24 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the constant of integration being fixed from the given condition. Speed is a magnitude, so remove the minus sign from −24 in the final answer.

**9. (a)** v = (3t² − 6)i + (4 − 2t)j [1], a = 6t i − 2j [1]. At t = 3: **v = 21i − 2j** and **a = 18i − 2j** [1]
**(b)** Speed = √(21² + 2²) = √445 = **21.1 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** The i-component of v is zero: 3t² − 6 = 0 [1], so **t = √2 s** [1]
**(d)** r = (2√2 − 6√2)i + (4√2 − 2)j [1] = **−4√2 i + (4√2 − 2)j** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Moving parallel to j means the i-component is zero.

**10. (a)** v = (3t² − 5t + c₁)i + (8t − 6t² + c₂)j [2]. From v(0): c₁ = 4 and c₂ = 5 [1], so **v = (3t² − 5t + 4)i + (5 + 8t − 6t²)j** [1]
**(b)** r = (t³ − 5t²/2 + 4t + d₁)i + (5t + 4t² − 2t³ + d₂)j [2]. From r(0): d₁ = 2 and d₂ = −1 [1], so **r = (t³ − 5t²/2 + 4t + 2)i + (5t + 4t² − 2t³ − 1)j** [1]
**(c)** At t = 2: r = 8i + 9j [1]. Distance = √(8² + 9²) = √145 = **12.0 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each integration needs its own vector constant, fixed with its own condition.

**11. (a)** Vertical component = 15 × 0.8 = 12. Landing: s = −30.4, so −30.4 = 12t − 4.9t² [1]. 4.9t² − 12t − 30.4 = 0 [1]. Substituting t = 4 gives 78.4 − 48 − 30.4 = 0, and the other root is negative, so **t = 4 s** [1]
**(b)** Horizontal component = 15 × 0.6 = 9 [1]. Distance = 9 × 4 = **36 m** [1]
**(c)** Vertical velocity = 12 − 9.8(4) = −27.2 [1]. Speed = √(9² + 27.2²) = √820.84 = **28.7 m s⁻¹** [1]. tan θ = 27.2/9 [1], so **θ = 71.7° below the horizontal** [1]
**(d)** H above the tower = 12²/19.6 = 7.347 [1]. Greatest height above ground = 30.4 + 7.35 = **37.7 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" must demonstrate that the stated value satisfies the equation and that the other root is rejected. In part (d), add the tower height.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the sign of g the wrong way, so that up is positive but a = +9.8.
- Giving one root of a quadratic in t when both, or the later one, are needed.
- Rounding times or components early, then losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Giving a landing angle with no reference such as "below the horizontal".
- Reporting displacement when asked for distance, because the turning points were skipped.
- Forgetting the constant of integration, or fixing it from the wrong condition.
- Leaving a vector answer without i and j, or giving speed with a minus sign.
