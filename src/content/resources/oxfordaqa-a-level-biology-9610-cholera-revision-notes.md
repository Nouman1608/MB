## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cholera | A diarrhoeal disease caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae* |
| Toxin | A substance produced by a pathogen that disrupts the host's cells |
| Lumen | The space inside the intestine |
| Epithelium | The single layer of cells lining the intestine |
| Water potential | The tendency of water to move; water moves by osmosis from higher to lower water potential |
| Osmosis | Net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane from higher to lower water potential |
| Oral rehydration solution (ORS) | A solution of water, glucose and salts taken by mouth to replace water and ions |
| Reduced-osmolarity ORS | An ORS with a lower total solute concentration than older formulations |
| Informed consent | Agreement to take part given freely by someone who understands the trial, or by a parent or guardian |

## The mechanism in six steps (3.2.3.1)

1. Bacteria in the small intestine secrete a toxin.
2. The toxin increases secretion of chloride ions from epithelial cells into the lumen.
3. Sodium ions follow the chloride ions.
4. The water potential of the lumen falls below that of the epithelial cells and blood.
5. Water moves out of the cells and blood into the lumen by osmosis.
6. Severe watery diarrhoea follows, with loss of water and ions: dehydration and reduced blood volume.

**Remember:** the gradient's direction reverses. Normally water moves from lumen into the body; with the toxin it moves from body into lumen.

## Pathogen links (3.2.1)

- Interface: digestive system, via contaminated water or food.
- Mechanism: toxin production.

## ORS: what each part does (3.2.3.2)

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Water | Replaces lost water |
| Sodium ions | Replace lost sodium; absorbed by co-transport with glucose |
| Glucose | Drives sodium absorption through the co-transporter, which the toxin does not block |
| Potassium and chloride ions | Replace other ions lost |

**Why water follows:** absorbing sodium and glucose lowers the water potential inside the epithelial cells, so water moves in from the lumen by osmosis.

**Why water alone fails:** with no solutes absorbed, there is no gradient to draw water into the cells.

## Improving an ORS: the science

| Change | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| Lower total solute concentration | Keeps the lumen's water potential from falling too far, so less water is drawn into the gut |
| Enough sodium and glucose | Too little would not drive co-transport |
| Starch (for example rice) in place of glucose | A large molecule has little effect on water potential; it is hydrolysed gradually, releasing glucose at the epithelium |

## Applications and implications

| Applications (benefits) | Implications (issues) |
|---|---|
| Cheap; can be given at home | Needs clean water to make up |
| Little training needed | Must be mixed correctly |
| Reduces need for intravenous drips | Treats symptoms, not the infection |
| Works for other diarrhoeal diseases | New formulas need manufacture, distribution and retraining |
| | Prevention depends on safe water and sanitation |

## Ethics of human trials

- Informed consent: from the patient, or a parent or guardian for children; free to withdraw.
- Control group: given the standard ORS, never a placebo or no treatment.
- Monitoring: intravenous fluids for anyone who worsens; stop the trial early if one treatment is clearly better or worse.
- Independent ethics committee approval before starting.
- No exploitation of vulnerable groups; the community should benefit.
- Confidential medical data.
- Balance: without trials, a better treatment cannot be shown to work.

## Evaluating trial data

Check: random allocation, blinding of those measuring outcomes, similar groups (age, severity), sample size, and whether differences are statistically significant.

## Exam traps

- "The toxin stops water being absorbed." Say it increases chloride ion secretion.
- "Water moves to where there is less water." Use water potential.
- "ORS kills the bacteria." It replaces water and ions only.
- "Stronger ORS is better." Too high a solute concentration can worsen diarrhoea.
- "Use a placebo control." Unethical for dehydrated patients.
- Writing only advantages in a "discuss" answer.

## Quick self-test

1. Which ion's secretion is increased by the cholera toxin?
2. Explain why sodium ions also enter the lumen.
3. What happens to the water potential of the lumen during cholera?
4. State the direction of water movement across the epithelium during cholera, and the process.
5. Why is losing water and ions dangerous?
6. Why does an ORS contain glucose?
7. Why does drinking pure water rehydrate a patient poorly?
8. Explain why an ORS with a very high solute concentration could make diarrhoea worse.
9. Suggest one advantage of using rice starch in an ORS.
10. Why must the control group in an ORS trial receive the standard ORS?
11. Who gives consent for a young child in a trial?
12. Give one limitation of ORS as a way of tackling cholera.

### Answers

1. Chloride ions.
2. They are attracted by the negative charge of the chloride ions and follow them.
3. It falls (becomes more negative), below that of the epithelial cells.
4. From epithelial cells and blood into the lumen, by osmosis.
5. Blood volume falls and ion balance is disrupted; severe dehydration can be fatal.
6. Glucose is co-transported with sodium ions, so it carries sodium into the cells; water follows by osmosis.
7. With no solutes absorbed, there is no water potential gradient to draw water into the cells.
8. It lowers the water potential of the lumen, so water moves from the cells into the gut by osmosis.
9. Starch has little effect on water potential and releases glucose gradually as it is digested.
10. Giving no treatment or a placebo could kill a dehydrated patient.
11. A parent or guardian.
12. It treats the symptoms, not the infection or its source; it also needs clean water.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the cholera toxin block the sodium-glucose co-transporter?**
No. It increases chloride ion secretion. The co-transporter still works, which is why glucose and sodium in an ORS are absorbed and water follows.

**What does "discuss the ethical implications" need?**
Several issues, each with how it is handled, and a balanced judgement on whether the trial is justified.
