Condensed notes for the section 3.1 row **The legal system: legal personnel and the judiciary**: the roles and regulation of barristers, solicitors and legal executives; types of judge; the role of judges in civil and criminal courts; and judicial independence. For explanation and an annotated answer, use the [legal personnel and the judiciary study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-the-legal-system-legal-personnel-and-the-judiciary/).

## Roles in one line each

- **Solicitor:** client-facing lawyer who advises, drafts, negotiates and prepares cases; rights of audience in lower courts, higher courts with a higher rights qualification.
- **Barrister:** specialist advocate and adviser, usually instructed by a solicitor, rights of audience in all courts, mostly self-employed in chambers, bound by the cab-rank rule.
- **Chartered legal executive:** specialist lawyer qualified through CILEx and work experience, often employed in firms, can become a partner or apply for some judicial posts.

## Who regulates whom

| Body | Function |
|---|---|
| Legal Services Board | Oversees the front-line regulators (Legal Services Act 2007) |
| Solicitors Regulation Authority | Sets standards for solicitors and firms; investigates misconduct |
| Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal | Fines, suspends or strikes off solicitors |
| Bar Standards Board | Sets the BSB Handbook; brings disciplinary charges against barristers |
| CILEx Regulation | Regulates chartered legal executives |
| Legal Ombudsman | Resolves complaints about poor service; can order apology, refund or compensation |

**Negligence:** advocates' immunity (*Rondel v Worsley*, 1969) was removed in *Arthur JS Hall & Co v Simons* (2000).

## Role of judges

| Civil courts | Criminal courts |
|---|---|
| Case management under the Civil Procedure Rules | Rule on law and admissibility of evidence |
| Decide facts and law, usually without a jury | Sum up and direct the jury on the law |
| Give reasoned judgments | Direct an acquittal if no case to answer; never direct a conviction |
| Award damages or injunctions | Pass sentence |

Appeal judges correct errors, interpret statutes and create precedent.

## Judicial independence

**Reasons:** rule of law; separation of powers; Article 6 ECHR fair hearing by an independent and impartial tribunal; public confidence; protection of citizens against the state.

**Methods:**
1. **Security of tenure:** superior judges removable only by the monarch on an address of both Houses; inferior judges removable for incapacity or misbehaviour by the Lord Chancellor with the Lord Chief Justice's agreement.
2. **Immunity from suit:** *Sirros v Moore* (1975).
3. **Independence from the Executive:** s3 Constitutional Reform Act 2005; Judicial Appointments Commission; Lord Chancellor no longer a judge; Supreme Court separate from Parliament since 2009.
4. **Salaries** from the Consolidated Fund.
5. **Political neutrality:** judges barred from the House of Commons.

**Examples:** *R (Miller) v Secretary of State for Exiting the European Union* (2017) and *R (Miller) v The Prime Minister* (2019), both against the government. **Impartiality:** *Re Pinochet* (1999).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rights of audience | The right to appear as an advocate in a court |
| Cab-rank rule | A barrister must accept work in their field at a proper fee whoever the client |
| Pupillage | Supervised training a barrister completes before practising |
| Solicitor-advocate | Solicitor with a higher rights qualification |
| King's Counsel | Senior advocate appointed for excellence in advocacy |
| Security of tenure | Protection from removal from office except in limited ways |
| Immunity from suit | Judges cannot be sued for acts in their judicial capacity |
| Separation of powers | Legislature, Executive and judiciary kept apart |
| Impartiality | No bias, or appearance of bias, in a particular case |

## Exam traps

- Treating the Legal Ombudsman as a disciplinary tribunal.
- Saying legal executives must have a degree.
- Giving superior-judge tenure rules for circuit judges.
- Naming only the methods of independence without explaining why each protects it.
- Forgetting that the Supreme Court, not the House of Lords, has been the final court since 2009.
