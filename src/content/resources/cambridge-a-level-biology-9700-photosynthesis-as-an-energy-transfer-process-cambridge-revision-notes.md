These notes condense section **13.1 Photosynthesis as an energy transfer process** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, read the [13.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-photosynthesis-as-an-energy-transfer-process-cambridge/); to apply what you know, use the [13.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-photosynthesis-as-an-energy-transfer-process-cambridge-practice/).

## Core recall

### Where things happen

- **Thylakoids** (thylakoid membranes plus thylakoid spaces), stacked into **grana**: light-dependent stage.
- **Stroma**: light-independent stage (Calvin cycle).
- **Envelope**: double membrane round the chloroplast.

### Energy flow in seven words

Light → excited electrons → proton gradient → ATP (+ reduced NADP) → TP.

### Structure linked to function

- Stacked thylakoids: large membrane area for photosystems, carriers and ATP synthase.
- Small thylakoid space: protons pumped in quickly build a steep gradient.
- Stroma: contains rubisco and the other Calvin cycle enzymes, close to the thylakoids that supply ATP and reduced NADP.
- Starch grains: store carbohydrate made from TP.

### Pigments

| Pigment | Type | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorophyll a | Primary pigment | At the reaction centre; emits an energetic electron when photoactivated |
| Chlorophyll b | Accessory | Absorbs other wavelengths; passes energy to chlorophyll a |
| Carotene | Accessory (carotenoid) | Absorbs mainly blue light; passes energy on |
| Xanthophyll | Accessory (carotenoid) | Absorbs mainly blue light; passes energy on |

### Spectra

- **Absorption spectrum**: how much light a pigment absorbs at each wavelength.
- **Action spectrum**: rate of photosynthesis at each wavelength.
- Close match between the action spectrum and the combined absorption spectra shows the absorbed light drives photosynthesis.
- Green light is least absorbed, so it is reflected or transmitted.

### Rf values

```
Rf = distance moved by pigment ÷ distance moved by solvent front
```

- Measure both from the origin; measure to the centre of the spot.
- No units; always less than 1.
- Only compare with reference values for the **same solvent**.

### Photophosphorylation

| | Cyclic | Non-cyclic |
|---|---|---|
| Photosystems | PSI | PSII and PSI |
| Photolysis of water | No | Yes, catalysed by the oxygen-evolving complex |
| ATP | Yes | Yes |
| Reduced NADP | No | Yes |
| Oxygen released | No | Yes |

### ATP synthesis in three steps

1. Energetic electrons release energy along the electron transport chain.
2. The energy transfers protons from the stroma into the thylakoid space.
3. Protons return to the stroma by facilitated diffusion through ATP synthase, providing energy for ATP synthesis.

### Calvin cycle (outline only)

1. **Fixation**: rubisco combines CO₂ with RuBP (5C) to give two GP (3C).
2. **Reduction**: GP is reduced to TP (3C), using reduced NADP and ATP.
3. **Regeneration**: RuBP is regenerated from TP, using ATP.

**Uses of intermediates:** GP → some amino acids. TP → carbohydrates, lipids and amino acids.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photoactivation | Absorption of light energy raising an electron in chlorophyll to a higher energy level so it is emitted |
| Photosystem | Group of pigment molecules in the thylakoid membrane funnelling energy to a reaction centre |
| Photolysis | Splitting of water, catalysed by the oxygen-evolving complex, giving protons, electrons and oxygen |
| Photophosphorylation | Making ATP from ADP and phosphate using energy that came from light |
| Chemiosmosis | ATP synthesis driven by protons diffusing down their gradient through ATP synthase |
| Rubisco | Enzyme catalysing the fixation of CO₂ with RuBP |
| Reduced NADP | Carrier of electrons and hydrogen from the light-dependent stage to the reduction of GP |
| Grana | Stacks of thylakoids |

## Prediction rules

- **Light off or PSII blocked** → no reduced NADP → GP cannot be reduced → GP rises, RuBP falls.
- **CO₂ removed** → fixation slows, RuBP still regenerated → RuBP rises, GP falls.
- **Only PSI working** → cyclic only → ATP made, but no reduced NADP and no oxygen.

## Exam traps

- Protons are pumped **into** the thylakoid space, not out of it.
- One CO₂ fixed gives **two** GP, not one.
- Oxygen comes from **water**, not from CO₂.
- The direct product of the cycle is **TP**, not glucose.
- Do not describe carrier names or ATP synthase structure: they are not required, and time spent on them earns nothing.
- "Light splits water" is weaker than "the oxygen-evolving complex catalyses photolysis of water".
- In spectra answers, quote wavelengths from the graph.
- Rf from the bottom of the paper is wrong; use the origin line.

## Self-test

1. State the site of the light-dependent stage.
2. Name the two products of the light-dependent stage used in the Calvin cycle.
3. Which pigment is found at the reaction centre?
4. Give the role of an accessory pigment.
5. A pigment moves 39 mm and the solvent front 60 mm. Calculate Rf.
6. Why must the origin line be drawn in pencil?
7. Which photosystem is used in cyclic photophosphorylation?
8. Name the enzyme complex that catalyses photolysis.
9. In which direction are protons pumped across the thylakoid membrane?
10. How many carbon atoms are in RuBP, GP and TP?
11. What happens to RuBP concentration when CO₂ supply is cut?
12. Name two types of molecule made from TP.

### Answers

1. The thylakoids (thylakoid membranes), in the grana.
2. ATP and reduced NADP.
3. Chlorophyll a.
4. Absorbs wavelengths that chlorophyll a absorbs less well and passes the energy to chlorophyll a.
5. 39 ÷ 60 = **0.65**.
6. Ink contains pigments that would separate and confuse the result; pencil does not dissolve in the solvent.
7. Photosystem I only.
8. The oxygen-evolving complex.
9. From the stroma into the thylakoid space.
10. RuBP 5, GP 3, TP 3.
11. It rises: it is still regenerated from TP but is no longer used in fixation.
12. Any two of: carbohydrates, lipids, amino acids.
