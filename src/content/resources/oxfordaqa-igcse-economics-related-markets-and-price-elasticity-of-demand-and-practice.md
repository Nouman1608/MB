## Questions

**1.** Give the formula for price elasticity of demand. When a firm raises its price by 8%, quantity demanded drops by 12%. Work out PED and classify demand. **[3]**

**2.** **(a)** Say whether each pair is more likely to be complements or substitutes: pasta and pasta sauce; bus tickets and taxi rides; smartphones and phone cases. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what happens to demand for bus tickets if taxi fares rise. **[2]**

**3.** The price of Juice X rises from 4.00 to 4.40. Weekly demand for Juice Y rises from 1,200 to 1,296 cartons.
**(a)** Calculate cross elasticity of demand. **[3]**
**(b)** State the relationship between the two brands. **[1]**

**4.** A tailor raises the price of a school shirt from 10 to 12 and increases weekly output from 600 to 630.
**(a)** Calculate price elasticity of supply. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one factor that may explain this result. **[2]**

**5.** A ferry operator cuts its fare from 20 to 18 and passengers rise from 1,000 to 1,150 a week.
**(a)** Calculate PED. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out total revenue at each fare. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the result shows about the best pricing decision. **[2]**

**6.** Town C's taxi trips have PED of −0.4. Fares are 8 and there are 3,000 trips a week. The fare rises by 25%.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in trips. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the new number of trips. **[1]**
**(c)** Work out revenue at each fare and explain the change. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two reasons why demand for household tap water is likely to be more price inelastic than demand for one brand of bottled water. **[4]**

**8.** A disease cuts the supply of cattle in Country D. Explain the likely effects on the markets for beef, chicken (a substitute for beef) and bread rolls used for beef burgers. **[6]**

**9.** In market M, PES is 0.3. In market N, PES is 2.0. In each, price rises by 15%.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in quantity supplied in each market. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which market can respond better to a surge in demand, and what this means for buyers. **[3]**

**10.** A swimming pool cuts the price of a day ticket from 5.00 to 4.50. Sales rise from 400 to 428 a week. Ignoring fixed costs, each ticket costs 2.00 to serve.
**(a)** Calculate PED. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out revenue at each price. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the price cut was a good decision. **[4]**

**11.** The price of Brand R coffee machines rises 12%. Demand for Brand S coffee machines rises 9% and demand for capsules that only fit Brand R falls 6%.
**(a)** Calculate cross elasticity of demand between R and S. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate cross elasticity of demand between R machines and the capsules. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what each sign shows. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why a seller of Brand R capsules should watch the price of Brand R machines. **[2]**

**12.** In Country A, fishing crews land and sell 10,000 tonnes of fish in a typical season at 100 a tonne. A very good season raises the quantity sold by 20%. PED for fish is −0.5.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in price needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the crews' revenue at the old and new price. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the crews may be worse off despite selling more. **[2]**
**(d)** State one reason why this result may not hold in practice. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** PED = (% change in Qd) ÷ (% change in price) [1]. −12 ÷ 8 = **−1.5** [1]. Size is above 1, so demand is **elastic** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A number alone is weak. Answers that finish with the word elastic or inelastic and the reason (above or below 1) show the interpretation the question asks for.

**2. (a)** Pasta and sauce: **complements** [1]. Bus and taxi: **substitutes** [1]. Smartphones and cases: **complements** [1].
**(b)** Some travellers switch from taxis to buses [1], so demand for bus tickets shifts right [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the word shift for another good's price. Calling it a movement along the bus curve is a common slip.

**3. (a)** Price: (4.40 − 4.00) ÷ 4.00 × 100 = +10% [1]. Quantity of Y: (1,296 − 1,200) ÷ 1,200 × 100 = +8% [1]. XED = 8 ÷ 10 = **+0.8** [1].
**(b)** Positive, so the two juices are **substitutes** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The demand for good A is the numerator and the price of good B the denominator. Reversing them is a frequent source of lost credit.

**4. (a)** Price: +20% [1]. Quantity: (630 − 600) ÷ 600 = +5% [1]. PES = 5 ÷ 20 = **0.25**, which is inelastic [1].
**(b)** Spare capacity may be low, so the tailor cannot raise output quickly [1]. Without idle machines or stocks, output can barely rise within a few weeks [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A factor named but not linked to speed of response is a list item, not an explanation. Add the because.

**5. (a)** Price: (18 − 20) ÷ 20 = −10% [1]. Quantity: +15%. PED = 15 ÷ −10 = **−1.5** [1].
**(b)** Before: 20 × 1,000 = **20,000** [1]. After: 18 × 1,150 = **20,700** [1].
**(c)** Demand is elastic, so a price cut raises revenue, here by 700 [1]. Costs must also be checked, because more passengers may raise running costs [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Revenue and profit are different. Commenting on costs is a sign you understand what the data cannot tell you.

**6. (a)** Rearranging PED = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price [1], trips change by −0.4 × 25 = **−10%** [1].
**(b)** 3,000 × 0.90 = **2,700 trips** [1].
**(c)** New fare 8 × 1.25 = 10. Before: 8 × 3,000 = **24,000** [1]. After: 10 × 2,700 = **27,000** [1]. Revenue rises because demand is inelastic, so the fall in trips is proportionately smaller than the rise in fare [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the new price as well as the new quantity. Mixing the old price with the new quantity gives the wrong revenue.

**7.** Reason 1: tap water is a necessity for drinking, washing and cooking [1], so buyers keep using nearly the same amount if the price rises [1]. Reason 2: one brand of bottled water has many close substitutes, including other brands and tap water [1], so buyers switch when its price rises, which makes it more elastic [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparison questions reward a clear contrast. State why one good is inelastic and the other elastic, not just one of them.

**8.** Beef supply shifts left [1], so beef price rises and quantity falls [1]. Chicken is a substitute, so demand for chicken shifts right [1]. Its price and quantity rise [1]. Bread rolls are complements, so fewer burgers means demand for rolls shifts left [1], and their price and quantity fall [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain scores when each link names the curve and its direction. Writing only that the price goes up in each market leaves the reasoning out.

**9. (a)** M: 0.3 × 15 = **4.5%** [1]. N: 2.0 × 15 = **30%** [1].
**(b)** Market N responds better, because output rises by far more for the same price rise [1]. Buyers in M face larger and longer price increases, since supply cannot grow to meet demand [1]. Buyers in N get more goods at a smaller price rise [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the answer to the consumer, since the question asks what it means for buyers. Stopping at the calculation leaves that out.

**10. (a)** Price: −10% [1]. Quantity: +7%. PED = 7 ÷ −10 = **−0.7** [1].
**(b)** Before: 5.00 × 400 = **2,000**. After: 4.50 × 428 = **1,926** [1]. Revenue falls by 74 [1].
**(c)** Profit before: (5.00 − 2.00) × 400 = 1,200. After: (4.50 − 2.00) × 428 = 1,070 [1]. Demand is inelastic, so the cut lowered revenue and profit [1]. A cut could still help if it wins long-run loyalty or market share [1]. On these figures, the decision was poor, unless the pool has a longer-term aim [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation needs a judgement backed by the figures. A balanced list with no conclusion is incomplete.

**11. (a)** Quantity of S +9%, price of R +12% [1]. XED = 9 ÷ 12 = **+0.75** [1].
**(b)** Quantity of capsules −6%, price of R +12% [1]. XED = −6 ÷ 12 = **−0.5** [1].
**(c)** Positive XED means R and S are substitutes [1]. Negative means R machines and the capsules are complements [1].
**(d)** Capsule demand depends on how many Brand R machines are in use [1]. A rise in R's price cuts machine sales, so fewer capsules are needed [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the sign. Dropping the minus sign removes the very information that tells you complements from substitutes.

**12. (a)** % change in price = % change in quantity ÷ PED = 20 ÷ −0.5 = **−40%** [1]. The minus sign shows that price must fall [1].
**(b)** New price 100 × 0.60 = 60. Before: 100 × 10,000 = **1,000,000**. After: 60 × 12,000 = **720,000** [1]. Revenue falls by 280,000 [1].
**(c)** Demand is inelastic, so the large price fall is only partly offset by extra sales [1]. Revenue falls even though quantity sold rises [1].
**(d)** Some of the catch may be frozen or exported, which keeps the price from falling so far [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Real markets have stocks and trade. Mentioning a limit shows evaluation, but it must be tied to the reasoning above.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Working out each percentage change from the new figure rather than the original.
- Turning the elasticity fraction upside down.
- Leaving out the word elastic or inelastic after the number.
- Dropping the sign of cross elasticity.
- Describing a knock-on effect with no shift of a named curve.
- Confusing revenue with profit.
- Giving a factor with no explanation of why it matters.
- Mixing old prices with new quantities in revenue sums.
