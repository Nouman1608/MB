## Answers

**1.** The internuclear distance between two covalently bonded atoms [1].

**2.** Two σ bonds [1] and two π bonds [1].

**3.** C [1].

**4.** The energy required to break one mole of a particular covalent bond [1] in the gaseous state [1].

**5.** sp [1]; 180° [1].

**6.** The C=C bond has two shared pairs (a σ and a π bond) between the carbon atoms [1], so there is greater attraction between the nuclei and the shared electrons, pulling the nuclei closer together [1].

**7. (a)** NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s) [1].

**(b)** The nitrogen atom of NH₃ donates its lone pair [1] to an H⁺ ion (from HCl), which has an empty orbital, so both electrons in the new N–H bond come from nitrogen [1].

**(c)** Incorrect: once formed, all four N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ are identical in length and strength [1].

**8. (a)** Aluminium has only six electrons in its outer shell, so it has an empty orbital [1].

**(b)** A chlorine atom in each AlCl₃ unit donates a lone pair to the aluminium atom of the other unit [1], forming two coordinate (dative covalent) bonds, with two chlorine atoms bridging the aluminium atoms [1].

**(c)** Eight [1].

**9. (a)** Each carbon is sp² hybridised [1]. The σ bond forms by direct overlap of an sp² orbital from each carbon [1]. The π bond forms by sideways overlap of the unhybridised p orbital on each carbon, above and below the σ bond [1].

**(b)** The π bond is weaker than the σ bond because sideways overlap is less effective, so it breaks more easily [1].

**10. (a)** σ bonds: 8 C–H + 3 C–C + 1 C–O = 12 σ [1]; 1 π bond (in C=O) [1].

**(b)** Carbonyl carbon: sp² [1]; the other three carbons: sp³ [1].

**11. (a)** 1-iodobutane > 1-bromobutane > 1-chlorobutane [1].

**(b)** The rate depends mainly on how easily the C–X bond breaks [1]; C–I has the lowest bond energy, so it breaks most easily even though it is the least polar [1].

**12. (a)** Twelve [1].

**(b)** Sulfur is in Period 3, so it has available 3d orbitals and is large enough for six atoms around it [1]; oxygen is in Period 2, with no 2d orbitals, so it is limited to eight outer electrons [1].

**(c)** Two S=O double bonds (four bonding pairs) [1] and one lone pair on sulfur, giving ten electrons around sulfur [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the energy to break a bond" without "one mole" and "gaseous state".
- Counting the σ bonds in a double bond twice.
- Saying the coordinate bond in NH₄⁺ is different from the others after it has formed.
- Describing only one coordinate bond in Al₂Cl₆.
- Using polarity, rather than bond energy, to explain halogenoalkane reactivity.
- Forgetting that the π bond lies above and below the σ bond, not between the nuclei.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I count σ and π bonds quickly?**
Draw the displayed formula, count every bond line as one σ bond, then add one π bond for each double bond and two for each triple bond.
