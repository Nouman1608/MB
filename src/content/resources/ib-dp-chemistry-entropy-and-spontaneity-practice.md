The questions below test Reactivity 1.4, "Entropy and spontaneity", from the IB Diploma Programme Chemistry guide (first assessment 2025, published February 2023). Every understanding from Reactivity 1.4.1 to 1.4.4 appears at least once. The sub-topic is additional higher level, so every question is **HL only**, for the course examined from 2025 onwards.

Learn the unit first from the [entropy and spontaneity study guide](/resources/ib-dp-chemistry-entropy-and-spontaneity/) and the [condensed revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-chemistry-entropy-and-spontaneity-revision-notes/). The [course hub](/boards/ib/ib-dp/chemistry/) lists other units, the [checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-dp/chemistry/) tracks your progress, and a free [diagnostic](/diagnostics/) points to weak topics.

Use these data throughout: R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; T/K = t/°C + 273. Standard entropies, S⦵ / J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: N₂(g) 191.6, O₂(g) 205.2, NO(g) 210.8, NH₃(g) 192.8, H₂O(g) 188.8, Al(s) 28.3, Al₂O₃(s) 50.9, Fe(s) 27.3, Fe₂O₃(s) 87.4, PCl₅(g) 364.6, PCl₃(g) 311.8, Cl₂(g) 223.1. Give answers to three significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State what is meant by entropy. **[1]**
**(b)** Arrange solid, liquid and gaseous bromine in order of increasing entropy under the same conditions. **[1]**

**2.** Predict the sign of ΔS for each change, giving a reason.

**(a)** Br₂(l) → Br₂(g) **[1]**
**(b)** Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s) **[1]**
**(c)** 2C₈H₁₈(l) + 25O₂(g) → 16CO₂(g) + 18H₂O(l) **[1]**
**(d)** 2KClO₃(s) → 2KCl(s) + 3O₂(g) **[1]**

**3.** Calculate ΔS⦵ for the first step of nitric acid manufacture: 4NH₃(g) + 5O₂(g) → 4NO(g) + 6H₂O(g). **[3]**

**4.** For N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g) at 298 K, ΔH⦵ = +180.6 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔG⦵ = +173.2 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵ using these values. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that your answer agrees with the S⦵ data. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the temperature above which the reaction becomes spontaneous, and suggest why nitrogen monoxide forms in lightning strikes. **[2]**

**5.** A student calculated ΔG for a reaction with ΔH = −45.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = −120 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ at 350 K and obtained +41 955 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the error. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the correct ΔG and state whether the reaction is spontaneous at 350 K. **[2]**

**6.** State, for each pair of signs, whether the reaction is spontaneous at all temperatures, at no temperature, only at high temperature or only at low temperature.

**(a)** ΔH negative, ΔS positive **[1]**
**(b)** ΔH positive, ΔS positive **[1]**
**(c)** ΔH negative, ΔS negative **[1]**

**7.** The thermite reaction is 2Al(s) + Fe₂O₃(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(s), ΔH⦵ = −851.5 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵ for the system. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the entropy change of the surroundings at 298 K. **[1]**
**(c)** Use your answers to calculate ΔG⦵ at 298 K, and explain why the reaction is spontaneous even though the system's entropy falls. **[2]**

**8.** For CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g), use K = 4.0 at 800 K.

**(a)** Calculate ΔG⦵ at 800 K. **[2]**
**(b)** State the value of ΔG when the system is at equilibrium, and describe how ΔG changes as a mixture of only CO and H₂O approaches equilibrium. **[2]**

**9.** At 800 K, a mixture of the gases in question 8 contains [CO] = 0.100, [H₂O] = 0.200, [CO₂] = 0.300 and [H₂] = 0.400 mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate Q. **[1]**
**(b)** Use ΔG = ΔG⦵ + RT lnQ and your answer to question 8(a) to calculate ΔG. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce the direction of net reaction, and confirm it by comparing Q with K. **[2]**

**10.** Phosphorus(V) chloride dissociates: PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), ΔH⦵ = +87.9 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate ΔG⦵ at 298 K and interpret its sign. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the temperature at which the reaction becomes spontaneous. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate ΔG⦵ and K at 600 K, assuming ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵ do not change with temperature. **[3]**

**11.** A solid X decomposes into gaseous products. ΔG⦵ = +28.4 kJ mol⁻¹ at 400 K and −6.6 kJ mol⁻¹ at 600 K. Assume ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵ are constant.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵ and ΔH⦵. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the temperature at which ΔG⦵ = 0. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate K at 650 K and comment on the composition of the equilibrium mixture. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A measure of the dispersal of matter and/or energy in a system [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **Solid < liquid < gas** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Disorder" alone may not be credited; refer to the spread of energy or matter.

**2. (a)** Positive: a liquid becomes a gas [1].
**(b)** Negative: aqueous ions form a solid lattice [1].
**(c)** Negative: 25 mol of gas become 16 mol of gas [1].
**(d)** Positive: a solid produces 3 mol of gas [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the reason must use the gas moles; counting water as a gas would reverse the answer.

**3.** Products: (4 × 210.8) + (6 × 188.8) = 1976.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]. Reactants: (4 × 192.8) + (5 × 205.2) = 1797.2 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]. ΔS⦵ = **+178.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both sums; a bare final value with a coefficient slip leaves nothing for an examiner to credit.

**4. (a)** ΔS⦵ = (ΔH⦵ − ΔG⦵) ÷ T = (180.6 − 173.2) ÷ 298 [1] = 0.0248 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ = **+24.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (2 × 210.8) − (191.6 + 205.2) = +24.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, the same value [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** T = 180.6 ÷ 0.0248 = **7280 K** [1]. Lightning heats the air to extremely high temperatures, where TΔS becomes large enough to outweigh ΔH (and the reaction is fast) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 24.8 J to 0.0248 kJ before dividing; otherwise T comes out 1000 times too small.

**5. (a)** ΔS was used in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ with ΔH in kJ mol⁻¹ (not divided by 1000) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** ΔG = −45.0 − (350 × −0.120) = **−3.0 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; negative, so spontaneous at 350 K [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A huge ΔG value such as +41 955 kJ mol⁻¹ is a signal to recheck units before moving on.

**6. (a)** At all temperatures [1].
**(b)** Only at high temperature [1].
**(c)** Only at low temperature [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each answer follows from the sign of ΔH − TΔS; write that expression if you are unsure.

**7. (a)** ΔS⦵ = (50.9 + 2 × 27.3) − (2 × 28.3 + 87.4) = 105.5 − 144.0 [1] = **−38.5 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** ΔS(surroundings) = −ΔH ÷ T = +851 500 ÷ 298 = **+2860 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** ΔS(total) = 2857 − 38.5 = +2819 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, so ΔG⦵ = −TΔS(total) = −298 × 2.819 = **−840 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. The large release of heat raises the entropy of the surroundings by far more than the system's entropy falls, so total entropy increases [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded 2857 in (c); rounding first still gives −840, but in other questions early rounding can shift the third figure.

**8. (a)** ΔG⦵ = −RT lnK = −8.31 × 800 × ln 4.0 [1] = −9216 J mol⁻¹ = **−9.22 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **ΔG = 0** at equilibrium [1]. ΔG starts negative and becomes less negative as products form, reaching zero at equilibrium [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* R is in J, so the result is in J mol⁻¹; state the unit you convert to.

**9. (a)** Q = (0.300 × 0.400) ÷ (0.100 × 0.200) = **6.00** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** RT lnQ = 8.31 × 800 × ln 6.00 = +11 912 J mol⁻¹ [1]. ΔG = −9216 + 11 912 [1] = +2696 J mol⁻¹ = **+2.70 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** ΔG is positive, so the reverse reaction occurs (CO₂ and H₂ react to form CO and H₂O) [1]. Q = 6.00 is greater than K = 4.0, so the mixture must shift to the left, which agrees [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A positive ΔG here does not mean "no reaction"; name the direction that is spontaneous.

**10. (a)** ΔS⦵ = (311.8 + 223.1) − 364.6 [1] = **+170.3 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** ΔG⦵ = 87.9 − (298 × 0.1703) = **+37.2 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; positive, so dissociation is not spontaneous at 298 K and the equilibrium lies to the left [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** T = 87.9 ÷ 0.1703 [1] = **516 K**; spontaneous above this temperature [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** ΔG⦵ = 87.9 − (600 × 0.1703) = −14.3 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]. lnK = 14 280 ÷ (8.31 × 600) = 2.86 [1]; K = **17.5** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) a temperature without "above" is incomplete; the signs of ΔH and ΔS tell you which side.

**11. (a)** Subtract the two equations: ΔG⦵(400) − ΔG⦵(600) = 200 × ΔS⦵, so 28.4 − (−6.6) = 200ΔS⦵ [1]. ΔS⦵ = 0.175 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ = **+175 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. ΔH⦵ = ΔG⦵ + TΔS⦵ = 28.4 + (400 × 0.175) [1] = **+98.4 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** T = 98.4 ÷ 0.175 = **562 K** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** ΔG⦵ = 98.4 − (650 × 0.175) = −15.35 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]. K = e^(15 350 ÷ (8.31 × 650)) = **17.1** [1]. K > 1, so products are favoured in the equilibrium mixture [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that both given ΔG⦵ values are reproduced by your ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵; this catches sign slips at once.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Substituting ΔS in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ into an equation where ΔH is in kJ mol⁻¹.
- Using °C for T in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS or in RT lnK.
- Dropping coefficients when summing S⦵ values.
- Predicting ΔS from total moles instead of moles of gas.
- Giving a threshold temperature without "above" or "below".
- Leaving ΔG⦵ in kJ when calculating lnK.
- Reading positive ΔG as "nothing happens" instead of "the reverse reaction is spontaneous".
- Forgetting that a spontaneous reaction may still be slow.
