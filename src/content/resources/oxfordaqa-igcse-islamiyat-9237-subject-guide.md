OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiyat (9237), which the board itself
spells "Islamiat", is a study of Islamic beliefs, teachings and
practices, the life of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), and the sources of
authority on which Muslim belief and practice rest. It is a new
specification, first examined in May/June 2026, and is offered at
International GCSE only; OxfordAQA does not offer the subject at AS or
A-level. The qualification is linear, with no coursework, and is assessed
by two written papers, each 90 minutes long, worth 50 marks and 50% of
the grade.

## How the course is organised

The specification has two themes, and each theme has its own paper.

**Living the Muslim life.** This theme has two parts. Islam: Beliefs and
Teachings covers the core beliefs of the faith. Islam: Practices covers
how those beliefs are expressed in worship and daily life.

**The life of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) and sources of authority in
Islam.** This theme also has two parts. Life and Teachings of Hazrat
Muhammad (pbuh) studies the Prophet's (pbuh) life and what it teaches.
Sources of Authority in Islam looks at where Muslim teaching and
guidance come from and how they are used.

Both themes require study of the related Qur'an and Hadith passages set
out in the specification, so these passages run through the whole course
rather than belonging to a single topic.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Islam: Beliefs and Teachings; Islam: Practices | 90 minutes | 50 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Life and Teachings of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh); Sources of Authority in Islam | 90 minutes | 50 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed written exams, sat together at the
end of the course. There is a single, untiered entry. Because the two
papers carry equal weight, neither theme can be treated as the minor
one.

## How the parts fit together

Although the papers are separate, the two themes are closely connected.
Beliefs and practices in the first theme are grounded in the sources of
authority studied in the second: when you explain why Muslims believe or
act in a certain way, you will often refer to the Qur'an, the Hadith or
the example of the Prophet (pbuh). Equally, the life of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh)
(pbuh) gives concrete context for many of the beliefs and practices in
the first theme.

The set Qur'an and Hadith passages are the clearest link between the
papers. Knowing what each passage says, what it teaches and how it
relates to belief and practice is useful in both exams. Students who
learn the passages in isolation often find it harder to use them than
those who connect each passage to the topics it supports.

Two skills run across both papers: accurate knowledge, expressed in
clear and correct terms, and the ability to explain significance, that
is, why a belief, event or source matters to Muslims.

## Knowledge and explanation

Answers in Islamiyat generally need two things working together. The
first is secure, accurate knowledge: the correct names, events, terms and
teachings, set out clearly. The second is explanation: showing why that
knowledge matters, how it shapes Muslim belief or practice, and how it
connects to the sources of authority.

Students often find the first easier than the second. A useful habit is
to add a "why does this matter?" note to every point in your revision
material. For a practice, that might mean explaining its purpose and the
teaching behind it; for an event in the life of the Prophet (pbuh), it
might mean explaining what Muslims learn from it today. Where a set
Qur'an or Hadith passage supports a point, record that too, since it
gives your explanation a clear foundation. Working this way from the
start of the course means revision becomes a matter of practising
explanations you already have, rather than building them under pressure.

## Planning your study

A sensible approach is to begin with Islam: Beliefs and Teachings, since
it provides the foundation for everything else, then move to Islam:
Practices. The second theme can follow, starting with the life and
teachings of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) and then sources of authority.
Some schools teach the two themes side by side instead, which works well
because of the links between them.

Whatever the order, work on the set Qur'an and Hadith passages
throughout the course. Keep a single reference list with each passage,
its meaning and the topics it supports, and review it regularly rather
than learning the passages all at once near the exam.

Practise exam-style questions on each topic as soon as it has been
taught. Because this is a recent specification with few exam series behind
it, topic-by-topic practice questions and your teacher's own questions
matter more than usual. The course's exam-preparation page explains how to
work with the set passages in answers, so use it for exam technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat 9237 assessed?**
By two written papers, each 90 minutes long, worth 50 marks and 50% of the grade. Paper 1 covers beliefs, teachings and practices; Paper 2 covers the life and teachings of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) and sources of authority in Islam.

**Is it spelled Islamiyat or Islamiat?**
OxfordAQA's own spelling of the qualification is "Islamiat". Both spellings refer to the same subject, code 9237.

**Is there coursework in 9237?**
No. The qualification is linear and is assessed by the two exams only.

**Do I need to learn Qur'an and Hadith passages?**
Yes. Both papers require study of the related Qur'an and Hadith passages set out in the specification.

**Can I take OxfordAQA Islamiat at A-level?**
No. OxfordAQA does not offer Islamiat at AS or A-level.
