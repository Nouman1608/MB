These notes condense section 2.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Representation in pictorial or diagrammatic form", which names pictograms, pie charts, comparative pie charts, Venn diagrams, bar charts, sectional and percentage bar charts, dual bar charts, box-and-whisker diagrams and stem-and-leaf diagrams. Worked constructions are in the [diagram study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-representation-pictorial-diagrammatic/), and the [diagram practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-representation-pictorial-diagrammatic-practice/) let you test yourself.

## Key formulae

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Pie chart angle | (frequency ÷ total) × 360° |
| Frequency from a sector | (angle ÷ 360°) × total |
| Comparative pie radius | r₂ = r₁ × √(total₂ ÷ total₁) |
| Total from comparative pie radii | total₂ = total₁ × (r₂ ÷ r₁)² |
| Pictogram symbols | frequency ÷ key value |
| Bar height | frequency ÷ units per cm |
| Percentage bar section | (part ÷ group total) × bar height |
| Venn: n(A or B) | n(A) + n(B) − n(A and B) |

## Construction checklists

**Pictogram**
- Key that gives whole, half or quarter symbols.
- Symbols the same size, equally spaced, aligned in columns.
- Category labels and a title.

**Pie chart**
- Find 360° ÷ total once and keep it unrounded.
- Angles add to 360°; adjust the largest rounding if needed.
- Label sectors with category (and value or percentage if asked).

**Comparative pie charts**
- One chart per total, each with its own angle calculation.
- Radii in the ratio of the square roots of the totals.

**Bar, dual bar, sectional and percentage bar charts**
- Bars of equal width; gaps between categories (dual bars touch within a pair).
- Vertical scale from zero, equal steps, labelled with units.
- Key for paired or stacked sections.
- Sectional: total height = total; plot cumulative boundaries.
- Percentage: every bar the same height; sections as percentages of each bar's own total.

**Venn diagram**
- Rectangle for the whole group; one circle per category.
- Fill from the centre outwards; subtract inner regions from given totals.
- Put the "none" figure inside the rectangle but outside the circles.
- Check: all regions add to the total.

**Stem-and-leaf diagram**
- Order the data first; leaves in order and evenly spaced.
- Every stem from lowest to highest, including empty ones.
- Key with units, such as 14 | 2 means 142 g, or 4 | 7 means 4.7 s.

**Box-and-whisker diagram**
- Five values: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum.
- Linear scale under the diagram, labelled with units.
- Two or more diagrams drawn above one common scale.

## Worked reminders

**Pie chart with a non-integer angle.** 33 votes: A 14, B 11, C 8. One vote = 360 ÷ 33 = 10.909...°. Angles 152.7°, 120.0°, 87.3° (total 360°). To the nearest degree: 153°, 120°, 87°, total 360°.

**Comparative pie radius.** Totals 2000 and 3125. If the first radius is 4 cm, the second is 4 × √(3125/2000) = 4 × √1.5625 = 4 × 1.25 = 5 cm.

**Percentage bar chart.** A group of 80 has 28 in category P and 52 in Q. With a 10 cm bar: P = 28/80 × 10 = 3.5 cm and Q = 6.5 cm.

**Venn with an unknown.** 45 pupils: 25 in choir, 18 in band, x in both, 7 in neither. 25 + 18 − x + 7 = 45, so x = 5. Choir only 20, band only 13.

**Stem-and-leaf with decimals.** Times 6.2, 5.8, 6.9, 7.1, 6.4, 5.5 s:

```
5 | 5 8
6 | 2 4 9
7 | 1
Key: 6 | 2 means 6.2 seconds
```

## Exam traps

- Rounding the angle per item (360 ÷ 33 ≈ 11°) before multiplying: totals then drift from 360°.
- Making comparative pie radii proportional to the totals: area would then grow as the square of the total.
- Comparing the **sizes** of two sectors in comparative pie charts as if both charts had the same total.
- Sectional bar charts: plotting each section from zero instead of stacking.
- Percentage bar charts: using the grand total of all groups instead of each bar's own total.
- Dual bar charts with no key, or with the two groups in a different order for different categories.
- Venn diagrams: putting a pair total ("12 visit both") straight into the region that excludes the centre.
- Stem-and-leaf: a key with no units, or leaves not lined up.
- Box-and-whisker: median drawn in the middle of the box instead of at its value.

## Quick self-test

1. A pie chart shows 90 people. What angle represents one person?
2. A sector of 72° in a pie chart of 250 cars represents how many cars?
3. Totals of 900 and 1600 are shown by comparative pie charts. The first radius is 3 cm. Find the second.
4. Comparative pie charts have radii 2 cm and 3 cm. The first shows 400 items. How many does the second show?
5. In a pictogram one symbol represents 20 books. How many books do 3¼ symbols represent?
6. Which key, 10 or 4, suits frequencies 18, 26, 12 and 30 in a pictogram? Give a reason.
7. In a percentage bar chart drawn 10 cm high, a group of 60 has 21 members in category A. How high is section A?
8. 50 people: 30 like tea, 26 like coffee, 9 like neither. How many like both?
9. In a three-set Venn diagram, "8 study French and German" includes the 3 who study all three subjects. How many study French and German only?
10. Write a key for a stem-and-leaf diagram of lengths from 2.3 m to 4.8 m.
11. For the list 12, 15, 17, 20, 22, 25, 31, find Q1, the median and Q3 by the halves method.
12. What is the main rule when drawing two box-and-whisker diagrams for comparison?

### Answers

1. 360 ÷ 90 = 4°.
2. 72/360 × 250 = 50 cars.
3. 3 × √(1600/900) = 3 × 4/3 = 4 cm.
4. 400 × (3/2)² = 900 items.
5. 3.25 × 20 = 65 books.
6. Key 4: 4.5, 6.5, 3 and 7.5 symbols are whole or half symbols; key 10 gives 1.8, 2.6, 1.2 and 3.
7. 21/60 × 10 = 3.5 cm.
8. 30 + 26 − x + 9 = 50, so x = 15.
9. 8 − 3 = 5.
10. For example 3 | 4 means 3.4 m.
11. Median 20; lower half 12, 15, 17 gives Q1 = 15; upper half 22, 25, 31 gives Q3 = 25.
12. Draw them above one common, labelled scale.

## Frequently asked questions

**How accurate must a drawn angle or length be?**
Draw as accurately as your instruments allow and show the calculated value, so the method is clear even if the drawing is slightly off.

**Do the bars of a dual bar chart touch?**
The two bars in a pair usually touch, with a gap between pairs, so each category is clearly separated.
