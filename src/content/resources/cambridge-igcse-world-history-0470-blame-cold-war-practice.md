These questions cover key question 4 of Core Content Option B: "Who was to blame for the Cold War?"

## Section A

**1.** Give **two** decisions about Germany agreed at the Yalta Conference. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **three** changes between the Yalta and Potsdam conferences that made agreement harder. **[3]**

**3.** State **two** methods communists used to take control of Eastern European governments by 1948. **[2]**

**4.** Name **two** organisations Stalin set up in 1947-49 to tighten control over the Soviet bloc, and say what each
did. **[4]**

**5.** Give **three** reasons why Stalin wanted friendly governments in Eastern Europe. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** differences between the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan. **[2]**

**7.** Give **three** results of the Berlin Blockade for Germany. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the Berlin Airlift. **[4]**

**9.** Why did the United States introduce the Marshall Plan? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**10.** Why did Stalin begin the Berlin Blockade in 1948? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** "The Potsdam Conference was more important than the Yalta Conference in causing the breakdown of the wartime
alliance." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**12.** "The Cold War began because of American actions in 1947-48." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: Germany would be divided into four zones of occupation (1); Berlin would also be divided
into four zones (1); France would be given a zone (1); Germany would be demilitarised and Nazi war criminals tried (1);
reparations would be discussed, with Soviet claims as a basis (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: Roosevelt had died and Truman, who distrusted Stalin, replaced him (1); Churchill was
replaced by Attlee during the conference (1); Germany had surrendered, so the common enemy had gone (1); the Red Army
now occupied most of Eastern Europe (1); Stalin had installed the Lublin Poles despite the Yalta promise (1); the USA
had successfully tested the atomic bomb (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: joining coalition governments and taking the interior ministry and police (1); arresting
or putting opponents on trial (1); rigging elections (1); forcing socialist parties to merge with communists (1);
salami tactics, removing opponents one group at a time (1); relying on the presence of the Red Army (1). **[2]**

**4.** 1 mark for each organisation named and 1 for what it did, to a maximum of 4. Cominform (1), set up in 1947 to
co-ordinate communist parties and make sure they followed Moscow's line (1); Comecon (1), set up in 1949 to
co-ordinate the economies of the bloc and tie their trade to the USSR, as an alternative to Marshall Aid (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: Russia had been invaded through Eastern Europe in 1914 and 1941, so he wanted a buffer
zone (1); the USSR lost well over 20 million people in the war (1); communist ideology aimed to spread revolution (1);
the region could supply reparations and resources to rebuild the USSR (1); he distrusted the western powers (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Truman Doctrine was a political statement of policy, while the Marshall Plan was a
programme of economic aid (1); the Truman Doctrine aided Greece and Turkey specifically, while the Marshall Plan was
offered to all of Europe (1); the Truman Doctrine was announced in March 1947, the Marshall Plan in June 1947 (1); the
Marshall Plan's aid was far larger, about $13 billion against $400 million (1). **[2]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Federal Republic of Germany was set up in May 1949 (1); the German Democratic
Republic was set up in October 1949 (1); the division of Germany became permanent until 1990 (1); West Berlin stayed
under western control as an island inside East Germany (1); West Germans saw the western powers as protectors (1).
**[3]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: it began in June 1948 after the Soviet blockade cut road, rail and canal links (1);
American and British aircraft flew in food, coal and other supplies (1); aircraft used the three air corridors into
Berlin, which the USSR did not close (1); it lasted almost a year (1); flights landed day and night at West Berlin's
airfields (1); Stalin did not shoot down the aircraft, as that would have meant war (1); it succeeded, and Stalin
lifted the blockade on 12 May 1949 (1). **[4]**

**9.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **to contain communism**, because Truman believed poverty and unemployment made communist parties popular, as
in France and Italy, so rebuilding Europe would remove their appeal; **to back up the Truman Doctrine**, because the
promise to support free peoples needed money to work; **for American economic interests**, because a prosperous Europe
would buy American goods and avoid a return to depression; **humanitarian need**, because the winter of 1946-47 left
much of Europe short of food and fuel. **[6]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content: **to stop a West German state**, because the western powers were
planning one at the London talks in 1948, and Stalin feared a strong Germany allied to the USA; **the new
currency**, because the Deutschmark (June 1948) was introduced without Soviet agreement and threatened to undermine the
currency in the Soviet zone; **to force the West out of Berlin**, because West Berlin was a western outpost inside the
Soviet zone and a showcase for capitalism; **to test western resolve**, because success would show the USA would not
defend its position in Europe. **[6]**

**11.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** at Potsdam the mood was openly suspicious; Truman took a harder line than Roosevelt; reparations could only
be settled by each power taking them from its own zone, a first step towards dividing Germany; the dispute over the
Lublin government in Poland came into the open; Truman's mention of the atomic bomb and its use in August increased
Soviet fear. **Disagree:** Yalta's vague promises on Poland and free elections created the dispute, because each side
interpreted them differently; Yalta's zones of occupation laid the basis for a divided Germany; the breakdown was also
caused by events after both conferences, such as Soviet actions in Eastern Europe and the Long Telegram. **Judgement:**
for example, Yalta created the ambiguities, but Potsdam was where they turned into open distrust because the leaders
and circumstances had changed. **[10]**

**12.** Levels as for Question 11. Indicative content. **Agree:** the Truman Doctrine (March 1947) divided the world into
two ways of life and committed the USA to resisting communism everywhere; the Marshall Plan was seen in Moscow as dollar
imperialism designed to pull Eastern Europe out of the Soviet sphere; Bizonia and the Deutschmark broke four-power
control of Germany and provoked the blockade. **Disagree:** the alliance was already breaking down in 1945-46 over
Poland and the atomic bomb; Soviet takeovers in Poland, Romania, Bulgaria and Hungary came before or alongside American
moves; Kennan's telegram and Churchill's speech show western fear of Soviet actions already in 1946; the Czech coup and
the blockade were Soviet initiatives. **Judgement:** for example, American actions in 1947-48 turned rivalry into an
organised division of Europe, but they were a reaction to Soviet expansion, so the Cold War had begun before them.
**[10]**
