These questions cover section 12.1 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: formation of the probability distribution of a discrete variable. Recall the methods from the [probability distribution study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-probability-distribution-of-a-discrete-variable/) or the [probability distribution revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-probability-distribution-of-a-discrete-variable-revision-notes/). Give exact fractions or decimals unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** The discrete random variable X has the distribution shown.

| x | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(X = x) | 0.2 | 0.35 | k | 2k |

Find k, and hence find P(X ≥ 2). **[3]**

**2.** P(X = x) = k(5 − x) for x = 1, 2, 3, 4. Find k and P(X < 3). **[3]**

**3.** A fair spinner has four equal sections numbered 1, 1, 2 and 3. X is the score on one spin. Write down the probability distribution of X. **[2]**

**4.** Give a reason why each table is not a probability distribution.

**(a)** Probabilities 0.3, 0.4, 0.2, 0.2. **[1]**

**(b)** Probabilities 0.5, 0.6, −0.1. **[1]**

**5.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. X is the remainder when the score is divided by 3. Find the probability distribution of X. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Two fair four-sided dice, each numbered 1 to 4, are rolled. X is the product of the two scores.

**(a)** Draw a sample space diagram and find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**

**(b)** Find P(X > 6). **[2]**

**(c)** Find P(X is odd). **[1]**

**7.** A box holds 10 bulbs, of which 4 are faulty. Three bulbs are taken at random without replacement. X is the number of faulty bulbs taken.

**(a)** Show that P(X = 1) = 1/2. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the rest of the probability distribution of X. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the probability that at least one faulty bulb is taken. **[1]**

**8.** From the box in question 7, two bulbs are taken with replacement. Y is the number of faulty bulbs taken.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of Y. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why P(Y = 0) is different from the probability of taking no faulty bulbs in two draws without replacement. **[1]**

**9.** A basketball player takes shots until she scores or until she has taken 4 shots. The probability that she scores with any shot is 0.3, independently of other shots. X is the number of shots taken.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**

**(b)** Find P(X ≤ 2). **[1]**

**(c)** State the most likely value of X. **[1]**

**10.** X takes the values 1, 2, 3 and 4. P(X = 1) = P(X = 4), P(X = 2) = 2 × P(X = 1) and P(X = 3) = 0.3.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**

**(b)** Find P(1 < X ≤ 3). **[1]**

**11.** Six cards are numbered 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3. Two cards are taken at random without replacement. X is the sum of the two numbers.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the probability that X is even. **[1]**

**12.** The probability that it rains on Monday is 0.4. If it rains on Monday, the probability that it rains on Tuesday is 0.7; if Monday is dry, the probability that it rains on Tuesday is 0.2. X is the number of these two days on which it rains.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the two days cannot be treated as independent. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 0.2 + 0.35 + 3k = 1, so 3k = 0.45 [1] and **k = 0.15** [1]. P(X ≥ 2) = 0.15 + 0.30 = **0.45** [1]. **[3]**

**2.** k(4 + 3 + 2 + 1) = 1, so **k = 1/10** [1]. P(X < 3) = P(1) + P(2) = 0.4 + 0.3 [1] = **0.7** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** P(X = 1) = 1/2, P(X = 2) = 1/4, P(X = 3) = 1/4 [2] (1 mark for two correct). **[2]**

**4. (a)** The probabilities total 1.1, not 1 [1].

**(b)** A probability cannot be negative [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Remainder 0 from 3 and 6; 1 from 1 and 4; 2 from 2 and 5 [1]. P(X = 0) = P(X = 1) = P(X = 2) = **1/3** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 16 equally likely outcomes [1].

| x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(X = x) | 1/16 | 2/16 | 2/16 | 3/16 | 2/16 | 2/16 | 1/16 | 2/16 | 1/16 |

Values correct [1]; probabilities correct [2] (1 mark for at least six correct).

**(b)** P(8) + P(9) + P(12) + P(16) = (2 + 1 + 2 + 1) ÷ 16 [1] = **6/16 = 3/8** [1].

**(c)** Odd products 1, 3, 9: (1 + 2 + 1) ÷ 16 = **1/4** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The value 4 comes from three cells (1×4, 2×2, 4×1); missing one of them makes the total 15/16.

**7. (a)** Faulty-good-good: 4/10 × 6/9 × 5/8 = 120/720 [1]. There are 3 orders for the faulty bulb [1], so P(X = 1) = 3 × 120/720 = 360/720 = **1/2** [1].

**(b)** P(X = 0) = 6/10 × 5/9 × 4/8 = **1/6** [1]. P(X = 2) = 3 × 4/10 × 3/9 × 6/8 = 216/720 = **3/10** [1]. P(X = 3) = 4/10 × 3/9 × 2/8 = **1/30** [1].

**(c)** 1 − 1/6 = **5/6** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Check with a common denominator: 120 + 360 + 216 + 24 = 720.

**8. (a)** P(faulty) = 0.4 each draw. P(Y = 0) = 0.6² = **0.36** [1], P(Y = 1) = 2 × 0.4 × 0.6 = **0.48** [1], P(Y = 2) = 0.4² = **0.16** [1].

**(b)** Without replacement the second probability changes (to 5/9 for a good bulb), because the first bulb is not returned; with replacement it stays 6/10 [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** P(X = 1) = **0.3** [1]. P(X = 2) = 0.7 × 0.3 = **0.21** [1]. P(X = 3) = 0.7² × 0.3 = **0.147** [1]. P(X = 4) = 0.7³ = **0.343** [1].

**(b)** 0.3 + 0.21 = **0.51** [1].

**(c)** **4** (probability 0.343) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* X = 4 happens whenever the first three shots miss, whatever happens on the fourth, so it is 0.7³, not 0.7³ × 0.3.

**10. (a)** Let P(X = 1) = a. Then a + 2a + 0.3 + a = 1 [1], so 4a = 0.7 and a = 0.175 [1]. P(X = 1) = **0.175**, P(X = 2) = **0.35** [1], P(X = 3) = **0.3**, P(X = 4) = **0.175** [1].

**(b)** 0.35 + 0.3 = **0.65** [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** There are 15 equally likely pairs of cards [1]. Sum 3: 2 pairs; sum 4: 4 pairs (1 with a 3 three ways, plus the two 2s); sum 5: 6 pairs; sum 6: 3 pairs [1].

| x | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(X = x) | 2/15 | 4/15 | 6/15 | 3/15 |

Probabilities correct [2] (1 mark for two correct).

**(b)** (4 + 3) ÷ 15 = **7/15** [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** P(X = 0) = 0.6 × 0.8 = **0.48** [1]. P(X = 1) = 0.4 × 0.3 + 0.6 × 0.2 [1] = **0.24** [1]. P(X = 2) = 0.4 × 0.7 = **0.28** [1].

**(b)** The probability of rain on Tuesday depends on whether it rained on Monday (0.7 or 0.2) [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing outcomes that give the same value of X, such as the different orders in a selection.
- Using unchanged probabilities in a selection without replacement.
- Adding an extra factor for the final stage of a process that stops.
- Not checking that the probabilities total 1.
- Including impossible values or omitting X = 0.
