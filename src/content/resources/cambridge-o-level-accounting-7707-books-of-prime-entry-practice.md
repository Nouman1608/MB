## Short-answer questions

Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** In which book of prime entry is the purchase of a delivery van on credit recorded?

A: Cash book

B: General journal

C: Purchases journal

D: Sales journal

**[1]**

**2.** A customer pays for goods with a debit card. Where is the receipt recorded?

A: Bank column of the cash book

B: Cash column of the cash book

C: Petty cash book

D: Sales journal

**[1]**

**3.** Name the book of prime entry in which each item is first recorded.

**(a)** A credit note received from a supplier. **[1]**

**(b)** A copy of an invoice sent to a credit customer. **[1]**

**(c)** A petty cash voucher for postage stamps. **[1]**

**(d)** Goods taken by the owner for her own use. **[1]**

**4.** Explain the dual function of the cash book. **[2]**

**5.** A business operates a petty cash imprest of $250. At the end of the month $63 cash remains. State how much was spent and how much is restored. **[2]**

**6.** Goods with a list price of $1,200 are bought with a trade discount of 25% and a cash discount of 4% for payment within 7 days. The invoice is paid within 7 days. Calculate the amount paid. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Wyndham Sports made these credit sales in March.

- 2 March: Kemp, list price $450 less 10% trade discount
- 9 March: Ndlovu, $720
- 17 March: Ortiz, list price $1,000 less 15% trade discount

**(a)** Prepare the sales journal for March, showing its total. **[3]**

**(b)** State how the sales journal is posted to the ledger. **[2]**

**8.** On 1 April a business had cash of $150 and a bank balance of $2,600. In April:

- 3 April: cash sales of $380
- 6 April: paid P Rao the $1,200 owed, less 2.5% cash discount, by bank transfer
- 10 April: received a cheque from J Lee for the $800 owed, less 5% cash discount, and banked it
- 15 April: paid $400 cash into the bank
- 20 April: electricity of $310 paid by direct debit
- 26 April: card sales of $940
- 30 April: paid casual wages of $95 in cash

Prepare the three-column cash book for April, balanced at 30 April. **[8]**

**9.** A petty cashier has an imprest of $300. During May she paid: postage $24, cleaning $56, travel $38, postage $17, stationery $45, travel $22.

**(a)** Calculate the total of each analysis column (postage, cleaning, travel, stationery). **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the cash remaining before restoring the imprest, and the amount restored. **[2]**

**(c)** State the ledger entry for the postage column total. **[1]**

**10.** Fatima runs a market stall and keeps the day's takings in a cash box until she visits the bank once a week. Explain **two** benefits and **two** limitations of keeping cash at the business. **[4]**

**11.** Explain the difference between trade discount and cash discount, including how each is treated in the books. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether a small shop should use digital methods rather than manual books for the original entry of its transactions. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Credit purchases of non-current assets go in the general journal. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. Card receipts are paid into the bank account. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Purchases returns journal [1].

**(b)** Sales journal [1].

**(c)** Petty cash book [1].

**(d)** General journal [1]. **[4]**

**4.** It is a book of prime entry, because receipts and payments are recorded in it first [1]. It is also part of the ledger, because its cash and bank columns are the cash account and bank account, so only the other side of each entry is posted [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Spent 250 − 63 = **$187** [1]; restored **$187** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Invoice 1,200 × 75% = 900 [1]; paid 900 − (900 × 4%) = 900 − 36 = **$864** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

| Date | Customer | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2 | Kemp | 405 |
| Mar 9 | Ndlovu | 720 |
| Mar 17 | Ortiz | 850 |
| | Total | 1,975 |

Marks: Kemp 405 [1]; Ortiz 850 [1]; total 1,975 [1].

**(b)** Each customer's account in the sales ledger is debited with its invoice [1]; the total of 1,975 is credited to the sales account [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Postage 24 + 17 = **$41** [1]; cleaning **$56** and stationery **$45** [1]; travel 38 + 22 = **$60** [1].

**(b)** Total spent 202; cash remaining 300 − 202 = **$98** [1]; amount restored **$202** [1].

**(c)** Debit the postage account with $41 [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Benefits: Fatima has cash available to give change and make small payments at the stall [1]; she saves time by visiting the bank only once a week [1].

Limitations: a week's takings could be stolen or lost, and she would need to pay for security [1]; the cash earns no interest and counting errors or missing cash are harder to detect [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Trade discount is a reduction in list price given to trade customers or for bulk buying [1]; it is deducted on the invoice and never recorded in the books [1]. Cash discount is a reduction offered for paying within a stated time [1]; it is recorded only when earned, as discount allowed (an expense) by the seller and discount received (income) by the buyer [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Benefits of digital: entries are recorded quickly and totals are calculated automatically, so there are no adding-up errors [1]; entries can post directly to the ledger, saving time [1]; records can be backed up and searched easily [1].

Limitations of digital: the shop must pay for equipment and programs and train staff [1]; a keying error is carried through every record, and the data must be protected from unauthorised access [1].

Conclusion: for a shop with many transactions, digital entry saves time and improves accuracy, so it is worth the cost, provided records are backed up; a very small shop with few transactions might reasonably stay with manual books [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including trade discount in the books.
- Putting discount allowed on the payments side of the cash book.
- Recording card sales in the cash column.
- Forgetting the contra entry, or entering only one side of it.
- Restoring the imprest with the float instead of the amount spent.
