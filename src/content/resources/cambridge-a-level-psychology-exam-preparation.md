Cambridge A Level Psychology (9990, 2024-2026 syllabus) has four externally assessed written papers of identical length: each is 1 hour 30 minutes and 60 marks, and each is worth 25% of the full A Level. Paper 1 is Approaches, Issues and Debates, Paper 2 is Research Methods, Paper 3 is Specialist Options: Approaches, Issues and Debates and Paper 4 is Specialist Options: Application and Research Methods. Read the topic pages on [Approaches, Issues and Debates](/resources/a-level-psychology-approaches-issues-and-debates/), [Research Methods](/resources/a-level-cambridge-psychology-research-methods/) and the specialist options such as [Clinical Psychology](/resources/a-level-psychology-clinical-psychology/) with this plan.

## An edition with a final examination year

This is the 2024-2026 syllabus edition, sat in the June and November series (and March in India). Its final examination year is 2026. If you are reading this late in 2026, confirm which syllabus applies to your own examination series before you finalise your revision, and use the current specification for your series.

## Papers 1 and 2: the AS Level

Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level and are each 50% of the AS (25% of the A Level). Paper 1 Approaches, Issues and Debates draws on the core studies and on how psychologists compare perspectives; use the topic page on approaches, issues and debates to keep the comparisons clear. Paper 2 Research Methods tests how studies are designed, analysed and evaluated, so it rewards practising with data as much as learning terms. The research methods page lists the techniques that recur.

## Papers 3 and 4: the specialist options

Paper 3 Specialist Options: Approaches, Issues and Debates and Paper 4 Specialist Options: Application and Research Methods are A Level only, each 25% of the A Level. The syllabus names four specialist options: Clinical Psychology, Consumer Psychology, Health Psychology and Organisational Psychology. Revise the option or options you have been taught in depth. The study guides for [Consumer Psychology](/resources/a-level-psychology-consumer-psychology/) and [Health Psychology](/resources/a-level-psychology-health-psychology/) show how the option content connects to both the debates (Paper 3) and application and methods (Paper 4). Check the syllabus for how many options you must prepare.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Every paper: 90 minutes / 60 marks = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- All four papers: 4 x 90 = 360 minutes for 4 x 60 = 240 marks.
- AS Level only (Papers 1 and 2): 180 minutes for 120 marks.

A 10-mark essay therefore deserves about 15 minutes, and a 4-mark short answer about 6 minutes. Because all four papers share the same rate, you can practise one timing habit for the whole course.

## Command words and question styles

"Describe" wants what happened or what the theory claims, without evaluation. "Explain" wants a reason. "Outline" wants the main points briefly. "Evaluate" and "discuss" want strengths and limitations, with a reasoned conclusion. "Compare" wants similarities and differences within the answer. "Suggest" invites you to apply a concept to an unfamiliar situation. In psychology, evaluation points are strongest when tied to the evidence: say what the method was, then why that limits or supports the conclusion. Check the exact command-word definitions in the current syllabus.

## Worked practice scenario

Original context: an investigator compares recall of a word list in two conditions. Eight participants learn the list in a quiet room (Condition A) and a different eight learn it with background noise (Condition B). Scores out of 20:

- Condition A: 12, 15, 9, 14, 15, 11, 13, 16
- Condition B: 8, 10, 7, 12, 9, 11, 6, 9

Question: "Calculate the mean, median and range for each condition, and state what the investigator can conclude."

```
Condition A: total = 105; mean = 105 / 8 = 13.125 (13.1)
  ordered: 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 15, 16
  median = (13 + 14) / 2 = 13.5; range = 16 - 9 = 7

Condition B: total = 72; mean = 72 / 8 = 9.0
  ordered: 6, 7, 8, 9, 9, 10, 11, 12
  median = (9 + 9) / 2 = 9.0; range = 12 - 6 = 6
```

Model conclusion: "Participants in the quiet condition recalled more words on average (mean 13.1 against 9.0), and the medians point the same way (13.5 against 9.0), with similar spreads (ranges of 7 and 6). This suggests that background noise lowers recall in this sample. However, with only eight participants per condition and different people in each group, individual differences may partly explain the gap; a repeated-measures design or a larger sample would strengthen the conclusion." Note the structure: calculate, interpret in context, then qualify.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you sit AS Level (Papers 1 and 2) or the full A Level, and which series.
- Practise every paper at 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Prepare the core studies and be able to evaluate them, not just describe them.
- Practise data tasks and write out each calculation step.
- Revise the specialist option or options you have studied in depth.
- Read the command word and underline the context in each question.
- Apply answers to the context given, not to a generic example.
- Leave time at the end to check calculations and add a missing evaluation point.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Psychology 9990?**
There are four externally assessed written papers: Approaches, Issues and Debates; Research Methods; Specialist Options: Approaches, Issues and Debates; and Specialist Options: Application and Research Methods.

**How long is each paper?**
Each is 1 hour 30 minutes and 60 marks, and each is 25% of the full A Level.

**What is the AS Level?**
Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level, each at 50% of the AS, and can be a standalone qualification or the first half of a staged A Level.

**Can all four papers be taken together?**
Yes. All four papers can be taken in the same series for the full A Level, or the A Level can be staged over two years.
