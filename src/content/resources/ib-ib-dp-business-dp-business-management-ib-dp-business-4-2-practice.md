## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *market segmentation*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a niche market and a mass market. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the basis of segmentation used in each case: (i) a travel firm offers different holidays to families and to retired couples, (ii) a clothing brand sells heavier coats in northern regions, (iii) a phone network offers a plan for people who make very few calls. **[3]**

**4.** Explain one role of a marketing plan for a business launching a new product. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why "friendly staff" is unlikely to be an effective USP for a café. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one way a bank could differentiate itself from competitors. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

Questions 7 to 10 use the case below.

Glenvale Dairy is a medium-sized yoghurt producer. It has identified three segments in its national market:

| Segment | Buyers per year | Average spend per year |
|---|---|---|
| Families buying for children's lunchboxes | 900,000 | 40 |
| Health-conscious adults | 300,000 | 90 |
| People who need lactose-free products | 60,000 | 110 |

Two large national brands compete heavily in the children's segment with frequent price promotions. Glenvale has developed a lactose-free recipe that, in blind taste tests, scored as well as ordinary yoghurt. Few lactose-free products are sold in supermarkets, and most are seen as expensive and poor in taste.

**7.** Calculate:

**(a)** the value of each segment. **[3]**

**(b)** the lactose-free segment's value as a percentage of the total of the three segments, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**8.** Glenvale aims for a 15% share of the lactose-free segment. Calculate the number of buyers and the sales revenue this would represent. **[2]**

**9.** Using price and taste as axes, explain how a position map could help Glenvale position its lactose-free yoghurt. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate Glenvale's option to target the lactose-free segment rather than the children's lunchbox segment. **[10]**

**11.** Discuss the importance of a USP for Glenvale's lactose-free yoghurt. **[6]**

**12.** Explain two limitations of marketing planning. **[4]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Dividing a market into distinct groups of customers [1] who share similar needs, characteristics or buying behaviour, so that each can be targeted with a suitable offer [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A niche market is a small, specialised segment with distinct needs, often served at higher prices [1], whereas a mass market is a large market of customers with broadly similar needs, served with a standard offer at high volume [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (i) Demographic (family stage or age) [1]. (ii) Geographic (region or climate) [1]. (iii) Behavioural (usage rate) [1]. **[3]**

**4.** A plan sets clear marketing objectives, such as a sales target for the first year [1], so managers can allocate the launch budget and later compare actual results with the target [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Many cafés could claim friendly staff, so it is not unique [1], and customers cannot judge it before visiting and it is easy for rivals to copy, so it gives little reason to choose that café [1]. **[2]**

**6.** For example, offering fast, well-reviewed mobile app services or longer branch opening hours [1], which gives customers a practical reason to choose the bank over rivals with similar interest rates [1]. **[2]**

### Section B

**7. (a)** Children's: 900,000 × 40 = **36,000,000** [1]; health-conscious: 300,000 × 90 = **27,000,000** [1]; lactose-free: 60,000 × 110 = **6,600,000** [1]

**(b)** Total = 69,600,000; 6,600,000 ÷ 69,600,000 × 100 = **9.5%** [1] **[4]**

**8.** 15% × 60,000 = **9,000 buyers** [1]; 9,000 × 110 = **990,000** [1] **[2]**

**9.** Glenvale plots existing lactose-free products, which are seen as high price and poor taste, and ordinary yoghurts on the two axes [1]. The map would show a gap for good-tasting lactose-free yoghurt at a moderate price [1]. Glenvale's taste-test results support positioning in that gap [1], and the map helps it choose a price that is competitive while signalling quality, though research should confirm that buyers want that combination [1]. **[4]**

**10.** For lactose-free: each buyer spends 110, the highest of the three segments [1]; competition is weak, and existing products are seen as expensive and poor in taste, so there is a clear gap [1]; Glenvale's recipe matches ordinary yoghurt in taste tests, giving it a credible advantage [1]; buyers who cannot eat ordinary yoghurt may be loyal once they find one they like [1]. Against: the segment is the smallest, at 9.5% of the total value [1], so even 15% gives only 990,000 of sales [1]; large brands could launch their own lactose-free ranges if the segment grows [1]. Children's segment: much larger at 36,000,000, but two large brands compete with frequent price promotions, which a medium-sized firm would struggle to match [1]. Judgement: target the lactose-free niche, because Glenvale has a real advantage there and avoids a price war [1]; this depends on the taste advantage being hard to copy, and Glenvale could later extend the recipe to health-conscious adults [1]. **[10]**

**11.** A USP such as "lactose-free yoghurt that tastes as good as ordinary yoghurt" is unique in a segment where rivals are seen as poor in taste [1] and is valued by buyers who have had to accept inferior products [1]. Blind taste test results make it credible [1], and it gives a single clear message for promotion and packaging [1]. However, if large brands improve their recipes the USP may be copied, so it may not be defensible for long [1]. It is very important at launch, but Glenvale will need to build brand loyalty and keep improving the recipe to protect its position [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Limitation 1: plans rely on forecasts and assumptions [1], so a sudden change such as a new competitor or an economic downturn can make the plan out of date [1]. Limitation 2: planning takes time and money [1], which small firms may lack, and a rigid plan can discourage managers from responding quickly to opportunities [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

* Choosing the largest segment without considering competition or fit.
* Describing a position map without identifying a gap or a position.
* Offering a USP that is not unique or not valued by the target market.
* Not using the calculated segment values in an evaluation.
* Concluding without saying what the judgement depends on.
