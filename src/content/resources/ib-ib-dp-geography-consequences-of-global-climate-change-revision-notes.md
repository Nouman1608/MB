Condensed recall for **9.2 Consequences of global climate change** (Unit 2: Global climate --
vulnerability and resilience). For the full explanation, read the
[Global Climate study guide](/resources/ib-dp-geography-global-climate/).

## Commonly studied consequences

- Sea-level rise.
- Changing rainfall patterns.
- More frequent or intense extreme weather, such as heatwaves.
- Knock-on impacts on **ecosystems, farming, water supply and human health**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vulnerability | Degree to which a place, community or system is susceptible to harm from a climate hazard |
| Resilience | Capacity to absorb, recover from and adapt to climate-related disturbance |

## Analysing vulnerability

Marlbridge's study guide breaks vulnerability into three parts. This is **its own analytical
framework, not IB wording**.

- **Exposure:** the place faces the hazard.
- **Sensitivity:** how housing, infrastructure and people are affected.
- **Adaptive capacity:** resources and institutions available to respond.

The same hazard gives **different outcomes** in different places. For example, two places facing a
similar flood differ according to housing quality and building codes, drainage, economic resources
and institutions. So vulnerability depends on exposure, sensitivity and adaptive capacity, not on
hazard size alone.

## Factors behind differences

The IB guide names location, wealth, social differences such as age, gender and education, and risk
perception as reasons people and places differ in exposure and vulnerability.

## Exam traps

- Saying one place is "more vulnerable" without breaking down why.
- Describing consequences only for physical systems and ignoring people.
- Presenting the three-part framework as IB terminology.
- Using named consequences with invented figures; use general terms if you cannot recall data.

## Self-test

1. List three consequences of climate change.
2. Define vulnerability.
3. Define resilience.
4. Name the three parts of the vulnerability framework in the study guide.
5. Why can the same flood hazard produce different outcomes?

**Answers:** 1. For example sea-level rise, changing rainfall and extreme weather. 2. The degree to
which a place or system is susceptible to harm from a hazard. 3. The capacity to absorb, recover from
and adapt to disturbance. 4. Exposure, sensitivity and adaptive capacity. 5. Housing quality, drainage,
resources and institutions differ, so vulnerability differs.
