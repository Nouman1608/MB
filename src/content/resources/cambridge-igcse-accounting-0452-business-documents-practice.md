These questions cover sub-topic **2.2 Business documents**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** State who prepares a credit note and who receives it. **[2]**

**2.** Which document is used to write up the purchases journal?

A: a credit note received

B: an invoice received

C: a statement of account

D: a receipt **[1]**

**3.** Which document does a business keep as its own record of a cheque it has written?

A: paying-in slip

B: receipt

C: cheque counterfoil

D: bank statement **[1]**

**4.** Explain the difference between trade discount and cash discount. **[2]**

**5.** A business sells 120 units at a list price of 7.50 each, allowing 10% trade discount. Calculate the invoice total. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** items that appear on a statement of account. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** On 2 May 2027 Harbour Paints sold Tern Decorators 30 tins of paint at a list price of 42 each and 12 brush sets at 15 each. Tern Decorators receives 20% trade discount. The terms are 2% cash discount for payment within 10 days.

**(a)** Calculate the invoice total.

**(b)** On 5 May Tern Decorators returned five tins. Calculate the value of the credit note.

**(c)** On 9 May Tern Decorators paid the amount it owed for this invoice, after the return. Calculate the cash discount and the amount paid.

**(d)** State the amount Harbour Paints records in its sales journal for the invoice, and explain why. **[9]**

**8.** Name the document in each situation.

(i) Harbour Paints tells Tern Decorators how much it owes at the end of the month.

(ii) Tern Decorators tells Harbour Paints it was charged for 12 brush sets but received 10.

(iii) Harbour Paints deposits the day's cheques at the bank.

(iv) Tern Decorators pays a cash bill and is given proof of payment.

(v) The bank lists all of Harbour Paints' bank transactions for the month.

(vi) Harbour Paints reduces Tern Decorators' debt because of the brush sets that were not delivered. **[6]**

**9.** **(a)** For Tern Decorators, state the book of prime entry written up from each document: (i) an invoice from Harbour Paints; (ii) a credit note from Harbour Paints; (iii) a cheque counterfoil; (iv) a bank statement showing a bank charge not yet recorded.

**(b)** Explain why Tern Decorators does not record the statement of account it receives from Harbour Paints. **[6]**

**10.** Harbour Paints' statement of account to Tern Decorators for April 2027 shows: 1 April, balance 1,520; 6 April, invoice 2,340; 14 April, credit note 260; 20 April, cheque received 1,520. Tern Decorators' own account for Harbour Paints shows a balance of 2,340 at 30 April.

**(a)** Calculate the balance shown on the statement of account at 30 April.

**(b)** Suggest a reason for the difference between the two balances.

**(c)** State **one** action Tern Decorators should take. **[5]**

**11.** Lark Café recorded takings of 1,850 in the cash column of its cash book. On the same day it paid 1,600 of this cash into the bank, together with a cheque for 300 received from a customer, K Bell, keeping 250 as a till float.

**(a)** Name the document completed when the money is paid into the bank.

**(b)** State the cash book entries for the money paid into the bank.

**(c)** State the ledger entry needed for K Bell's cheque.

**(d)** Explain why no ledger posting is needed for the 1,600 transferred from cash to bank. **[6]**

**12.** Tern Decorators plans to stop using paper invoices and cheques, and instead to send emailed invoices, pay suppliers by online bank transfer and download its bank statements.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits of this change.

**(b)** Explain **two** risks of this change, suggesting how each could be reduced. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Prepared by the seller [1]; received by the buyer (customer) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Trade discount is a reduction in list price given to trade or bulk customers and is deducted on the invoice [1]. Cash discount is a reduction offered for paying within a stated period, recorded only when payment is made [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** 120 x 7.50 = 900; less 10% = 90 [1]; invoice total **810** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Any two [2]: opening balance; invoices sent; credit notes; payments received; closing balance owed. Total: 2 marks.

**7. (a)** 30 x 42 = 1,260 and 12 x 15 = 180, total 1,440 [1]; trade discount 1,440 x 20% = 288 [1]; invoice total **1,152** [1].

**(b)** 5 x 42 = 210; less 20% (42) [1] = **168** [1].

**(c)** Amount owed 1,152 - 168 = 984; cash discount 984 x 2% = **19.68** [1]; amount paid 984 - 19.68 = **964.32** [1].

**(d)** **1,152** [1]. Trade discount is a reduction in the price, not a transaction, so only the net amount owed is recorded [1]. Total: 9 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In (b), a credit note of 210 ignores the trade discount and is the most common error.

**8.** (i) Statement of account [1]; (ii) debit note [1]; (iii) paying-in slip [1]; (iv) receipt [1]; (v) bank statement [1]; (vi) credit note [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**9. (a)** (i) Purchases journal [1]; (ii) purchases returns journal [1]; (iii) cash book [1]; (iv) cash book [1].

**(b)** It summarises transactions that Tern Decorators has already recorded from the invoices, credit notes and payments [1]; recording it again would double count them, so it is used only to check the supplier's account [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**10. (a)** 1,520 + 2,340 - 260 - 1,520 [1] = **2,080** [1].

**(b)** The difference is 2,340 - 2,080 = 260, the value of the credit note [1], so Tern Decorators has probably not received or not recorded the credit note [1].

**(c)** Any one [1]: ask Harbour Paints for a copy of the credit note; record the credit note in the purchases returns journal once received; pay only 2,080. Total: 5 marks.

**11. (a)** Paying-in slip [1].

**(b)** Debit bank column 1,600 and credit cash column 1,600 (a contra entry) [1]; debit bank column 300 for K Bell's cheque [1].

**(c)** Credit K Bell's account 300 [1].

**(d)** Both halves of the transfer are in the cash book, which is itself the ledger account for cash and bank [1], so the double entry is already complete [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**12. (a)** Two benefits, each identified [1] and explained [1], for example: invoices reach customers immediately, so they can be paid sooner [2]; software can record invoices and downloaded statements without retyping, saving time and reducing errors [2]; less paper and postage are used, lowering costs and helping sustainability.

**(b)** Two risks, each stated [1] with a suitable measure [1], for example: files may be lost through computer failure, reduced by regular back-ups kept in another place [2]; hackers may access or change records or bank details, reduced by passwords and limiting who can access the system [2]; an emailed invoice may also arrive by post and be recorded twice, reduced by checking invoice numbers before recording. Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "It is not safe" is too vague. Name what could go wrong and how it would be prevented.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing who issues debit notes and credit notes.
- Ignoring trade discount on a credit note.
- Allowing cash discount when payment is outside the period.
- Recording the statement of account as if it were a new transaction.
- Forgetting that banking cash is a contra entry with no ledger posting.
