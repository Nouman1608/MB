## 1. Responding to change (2.80–2.82)

Organisms survive because they detect changes and respond to them. A change in the internal or external environment that an organism can detect is a **stimulus**. A **receptor** detects the stimulus. An **effector** carries out the response. In animals, effectors are muscles (which contract) and glands (which secrete). Something must link receptor to effector: nerves, hormones, or in plants, plant growth substances such as auxin.

**Homeostasis** is the maintenance of a constant internal environment. The specification names two examples you must know:

- **Body water content** -- water is gained from food and drink and lost in urine, sweat, faeces and breath. The kidneys adjust how much water leaves in urine so that the water content of the blood stays steady.
- **Body temperature** -- kept close to 37 °C, which keeps enzymes working near their best. The skin carries out much of this control (section 6).

Homeostasis matters because cells work properly only within narrow limits. Too little water makes blood plasma too concentrated, so cells lose water by osmosis. Too high a temperature denatures enzymes.

## 2. Plant responses (2.83–2.85)

Plants respond to stimuli by growing in a particular direction. A growth response to a directional stimulus is a **tropism**. It is positive if growth is towards the stimulus and negative if away from it.

| Response | Stimulus | Stem (shoot) | Root |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phototropism | Light from one direction | Positive -- grows towards light | Negative -- grows away from light |
| Geotropism | Gravity | Negative -- grows upwards | Positive -- grows downwards |

These responses help survival. A shoot that grows towards light absorbs more light for photosynthesis. A root that grows down anchors the plant and reaches water and mineral ions.

### How auxin causes phototropism in a stem

1. **Auxin** is made in the shoot tip and moves down the stem.
2. When light comes from one side, auxin moves to the **shaded side**.
3. Auxin stimulates **cell elongation**, so cells on the shaded side elongate more than cells on the lit side.
4. The shaded side grows longer, so the stem bends **towards** the light.

Note the word "elongation". Auxin makes existing cells longer in this response; it is not "the cells on the shaded side divide faster".

### Worked example 1

A seedling is lit from the left. After 8 hours the stem tip has bent 28° to the left. Calculate the mean rate of bending, then explain the result.

- Rate = angle ÷ time = 28 ÷ 8 = **3.5° per hour**.
- Explanation: auxin made in the tip moves to the shaded (right-hand) side [point 1]; it causes greater cell elongation on the right [point 2]; the right side grows longer, so the stem curves to the left, towards the light [point 3].

A seedling grown in the dark, or lit evenly from above, grows straight up because auxin is spread evenly.

## 3. Nervous and hormonal communication (2.86–2.89)

Humans co-ordinate responses in two ways.

| Feature | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Electrical impulses | Chemicals (hormones) |
| Carried by | Neurones (nerve cells) | Blood plasma |
| Speed | Very fast | Slower |
| Duration of effect | Short-lived | Usually longer-lasting |
| Area affected | Precise, one place | Can affect several organs |

The **central nervous system (CNS)** is the **brain and spinal cord**. It is linked to the sense organs and to effectors by **nerves**. When receptors in a sense organ are stimulated, they send electrical impulses along nerves into the CNS. The CNS sends impulses out along other nerves to effectors. This route gives **rapid** responses.

Three types of neurone carry these impulses: **sensory** neurones (receptor to CNS), **relay** neurones (inside the CNS) and **motor** neurones (CNS to effector).

### Synapses

Neurones do not touch. The small gap where one meets the next is a **synapse**.

1. An impulse reaches the end of the first neurone.
2. A chemical called a **neurotransmitter** is released into the gap.
3. The neurotransmitter **diffuses** across the gap.
4. It binds to receptors on the next neurone and starts a new impulse.

Because neurotransmitter is released from one side only, impulses cross a synapse in **one direction**.

## 4. The reflex arc (2.90)

A **reflex** is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus. It does not need conscious thought, so it protects the body quickly. The specification uses withdrawing a finger from a hot object.

| Stage | In the example |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | Heat from the hot object |
| Receptor | Temperature (and pain) receptors in the skin of the finger |
| Sensory neurone | Carries impulses to the spinal cord |
| Relay neurone | In the spinal cord; passes impulses on, across synapses |
| Motor neurone | Carries impulses out to the arm |
| Effector | Muscle in the arm (the biceps) contracts |
| Response | Hand pulled away from the hot object |

### Worked example 2

Explain why the hand is pulled away before the person feels pain.

- The pathway passes through the spinal cord, not through the conscious parts of the brain [1].
- Only a few neurones and synapses are involved, so the impulse travels a short route quickly [1].
- Impulses do also travel up the spinal cord to the brain, but this takes longer, so pain is felt after the hand has already moved [1].

## 5. The eye (2.91–2.92)

The eye is a sense organ containing light receptors.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Transparent front; refracts (bends) light into the eye |
| Iris | Muscles that control the size of the pupil |
| Pupil | Hole that lets light in |
| Lens | Changes shape to focus light on the retina |
| Ciliary muscle | Contracts or relaxes to change the shape of the lens |
| Suspensory ligaments | Attach the lens to the ciliary muscle |
| Retina | Contains light receptors: rods (dim light) and cones (colour) |
| Fovea | Part of the retina with most cones; gives the sharpest image |
| Optic nerve | Carries impulses from the retina to the brain |
| Blind spot | Where the optic nerve leaves; no receptors |
| Sclera | Tough white outer layer; protects the eye |

### Focusing (accommodation)

| | Near object | Distant object |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliary muscle | Contracts | Relaxes |
| Suspensory ligaments | Slacken | Pulled tight |
| Lens shape | Fatter (more convex) | Thinner (pulled flatter) |
| Refraction | More | Less |

### Responding to light intensity

The iris has **circular** and **radial** muscles.

- **Bright light:** circular muscles contract, radial muscles relax, pupil gets **smaller**. Less light enters, so the retina is not damaged.
- **Dim light:** radial muscles contract, circular muscles relax, pupil gets **wider**. More light reaches the retina.

### Worked example 3

A pupil narrows from 7.2 mm in dim light to 2.7 mm in bright light. Calculate the percentage change.

- Change = 2.7 − 7.2 = −4.5 mm.
- Percentage change = (−4.5 ÷ 7.2) × 100 = **−62.5%** (a 62.5% decrease).
- Always divide by the starting value.

## 6. Temperature control by the skin (2.93)

Temperature receptors and the brain detect a change in body temperature. The skin then acts as an effector.

**When the body is too hot:**

- **Sweating** increases. Sweat glands release sweat onto the skin. Evaporation of the water in sweat uses heat energy from the skin, cooling it.
- **Vasodilation**. Arterioles (small arteries) supplying the skin capillaries **widen**. More blood flows near the skin surface, so more heat is lost by radiation.

**When the body is too cold:**

- Sweating decreases, so less heat is lost by evaporation.
- **Vasoconstriction**. Arterioles supplying the skin capillaries **narrow**. Less blood flows near the surface, so less heat is lost.

The blood vessels do not move up or down in the skin. The arterioles change their width, which changes how much blood reaches the surface capillaries.

## 7. Hormones (2.94 and 2.95B)

A hormone is a chemical secreted by a gland into the blood, which carries it to target organs.

| Hormone | Source | Role and effects |
|---|---|---|
| Adrenaline | Adrenal glands | Prepares the body for action ("fight or flight"): raises heart rate and breathing rate, raises blood glucose by causing the liver to break down glycogen, widens pupils |
| Insulin | Pancreas | Lowers blood glucose: causes the liver and muscles to convert glucose to glycogen and cells to take up glucose |
| Testosterone | Testes | Male secondary sexual characteristics; sperm production |
| Oestrogen | Ovaries | Female secondary sexual characteristics; repairs and thickens the uterus lining in the menstrual cycle |
| Progesterone | Ovaries (and placenta in pregnancy) | Maintains the uterus lining |

**Paper 2 only (2.95B):**

| Hormone | Source | Role and effects |
|---|---|---|
| ADH | Pituitary gland | Makes the collecting ducts of the kidney more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood; urine becomes smaller in volume and more concentrated |
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Causes an egg to mature in the ovary; stimulates the ovaries to make oestrogen |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Triggers ovulation (release of the egg) |

ADH links back to homeostasis of body water content. If the blood becomes too concentrated (after sweating heavily, for example), more ADH is released and less water is lost in urine. If you drink a lot, less ADH is released and more dilute urine is produced. The full menstrual cycle sequence belongs to Topic 3; see the [reproduction and inheritance revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-reproduction-inheritance-revision-notes/).

## Common errors

- Saying auxin is "destroyed by light" or that it makes the shaded side cells "divide". Use "moves to the shaded side" and "cell elongation".
- Swapping signs: roots are **positively** geotropic, stems **negatively** geotropic.
- Writing "the nerve sends a message". Write "electrical impulses travel along neurones".
- Missing the relay neurone, or putting the reflex in the brain instead of the spinal cord.
- Saying the ciliary muscles "pull the lens fat". Contracting ciliary muscles let the ligaments slacken, so the lens becomes fatter by itself.
- "Capillaries move nearer the skin" -- they do not. Arterioles widen or narrow.
- "Sweat cools you." It is the **evaporation** of sweat that removes heat.
- Calling glycogen "glucagon" when describing insulin's effect.
