This set tests **3.2.2.3 Organisational design** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). Section A has short-answer and calculation questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Every business and figure is invented. Revise first with the [organisational design study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-organisational-design-9725/) and the [organisational design revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-organisational-design-9725-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term delayering. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between centralisation and decentralisation. **[2]**

**3.** Lindale Builders has a managing director, 4 site managers who report to her, 16 foremen (4 reporting to each site manager) and 192 building workers, divided equally between the foremen. Calculate:

**(a)** the total number of employees and the number of levels of hierarchy **[2]**

**(b)** the span of control of each foreman. **[1]**

**4.** Lindale's site managers each earn $65,000 a year. If the site manager level were removed, calculate the annual salary saving and state the managing director's new span of control. **[3]**

**5.** 117 of Lindale's 180 employees on site are members of a trade union. Calculate the employee representation rate. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit of delegation for the partners of a small accountancy firm. **[3]**

**7.** State **two** influences on the span of control a manager can have. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **one** disadvantage of a tall structure for a fast-growing technology business. **[3]**

## Section B

**9.** Solano Hotels owns 12 hotels in six countries. At present, head office decides prices, menus and staffing levels for every hotel. Analyse the impact of decentralising these decisions to hotel managers. **[6]**

**10.** Fernhill Bank plans to remove its regional manager level. There are 6 regional managers, each paid $80,000 a year. Removing each post would bring a one-off redundancy cost of $40,000.

**(a)** Calculate the annual salary saving and the net saving in the first year. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages for Fernhill of removing the regional manager level. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways a high employee representation rate could affect a manufacturer that plans to introduce new shift patterns. **[4]**

**12.** Ambrose Retail has 50 clothing stores. Each store has a store manager, an assistant manager and three supervisors, who supervise an average of 24 sales staff. Assistant managers earn $34,000 a year. The finance director proposes removing all 50 assistant manager posts, so supervisors report directly to the store manager. Store managers already work long hours, and Ambrose's most recent staff survey showed that sales staff value promotion opportunities. Customer satisfaction scores have fallen for two years. Evaluate whether Ambrose should remove the assistant manager level. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Removing one or more levels of hierarchy from an organisation's structure [1], usually removing middle management posts [1]. **[2]**

**2.** In a centralised organisation key decisions are made by senior managers or head office [1]; in a decentralised organisation authority to make decisions is passed to branches, regions or lower levels [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 1 + 4 + 16 + 192 = **213 employees** [1]; **4 levels** of hierarchy [1].

**(b)** 192 ÷ 16 = **12** workers per foreman [1]. **[3]**

**4.** 4 × 65,000 = **$260,000** a year [1] [1]. The managing director's span rises from 4 to **16** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** 117 ÷ 180 × 100 [1] = **65%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Partners can pass routine work, such as preparing standard tax returns, to trained junior staff [1]. This frees the partners' time to win new clients and handle complex cases [1], increasing the firm's revenue while juniors gain skills and responsibility that may motivate them [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Any two of: skills and experience of staff [1]; skills of the manager [1]; how routine or complex the work is; management style; technology; cost of managers. **[2]**

**8.** In a tall structure, ideas and decisions must pass through several levels of managers [1], so decisions are slow [1]. In a fast-moving technology market, slow decisions could let rivals launch products first, losing sales [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Hotel managers know local customers, competitors and laws [1], so they could set prices and menus that suit local tastes and events [1], which may increase occupancy and revenue [1]. Having real authority could also motivate managers and speed up responses to problems [1]. However, standards and prices could become inconsistent across the brand, confusing guests who stay at several Solano hotels [1], and head office would lose some bulk-buying power for food; Solano might decentralise menus and staffing while keeping brand standards central [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Annual saving = 6 × 80,000 = **$480,000** [1]. Redundancy cost = 6 × 40,000 = $240,000 [1]. First-year net saving = 480,000 − 240,000 = **$240,000** [1].

**(b)** Branch managers would report directly to head office, so senior managers' spans widen sharply [1], and they may not have time to support branches, so problems go unnoticed [1]. Regional managers' experience and local knowledge would be lost [1], and remaining staff may fear further cuts, lowering morale [1]. **[7]**

**11.** The union can negotiate on behalf of most workers, so managers have one body to consult [1], which can make it quicker to explain the plan and reach an agreement that all shifts follow [1]. However, if the union opposes the new shifts, it may insist on lengthy negotiation [1] or organise action such as an overtime ban, delaying the change [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Credit chains of reasoning and a judgement, for example:

- Removing 50 posts saves 50 × $34,000 = $1,700,000 a year in salaries [1].
- Communication between supervisors and store managers becomes more direct [1].
- Supervisors may gain responsibility, which can motivate them [1].
- But store managers already work long hours; adding three direct reports plus duties the assistant did may overload them [1], so problems in stores may go unsolved [1].
- Customer satisfaction has fallen for two years; weaker management cover on the shop floor could make service worse [1], reducing sales and offsetting the saving [1].
- Sales staff value promotion opportunities; removing a level removes a step, which may raise labour turnover [1].
- Redundancy costs will reduce the first-year saving [1].
- Ambrose could delayer only in smaller stores, or give supervisors training and some of the assistant's authority first [1].
- The saving is large and certain, while the costs depend on how the change is managed [1].
- Judgement: Ambrose should not remove the level in every store at once; it should trial delayering in a few stores and track customer satisfaction and turnover before extending it [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting the levels of hierarchy incorrectly by missing the top or bottom layer.
- Forgetting one-off redundancy costs when asked for a first-year saving.
- Confusing delegation with decentralisation.
- Analysing delayering only in terms of cost savings.
- Using information in the case, such as long working hours, without explaining its significance.
