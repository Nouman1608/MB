**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The features and use of the Qur'an as a source of authority.** The specification says: "The features of the Qur'an including the major themes of the Makki and Madni Surahs. The miraculous nature and infallibility of the Qur'an and the way in which it is used and treated." The prescribed passage is Qur'an 56:75-80.

The previous content area asked how the Qur'an reached us. This one asks what the Qur'an is like, why Muslims believe it is a miracle, and what they do with it.

## General features

- **114 surahs** (chapters), made up of verses (*ayat*). The surahs are arranged roughly from longest to shortest, beginning with al-Fatiha and ending with an-Nas, not in order of revelation.
- **Thirty equal parts** (*juz'*), which make it easy to read the whole book in a month, especially during Ramadhan.
- **Arabic.** Translations help understanding, but Muslims regard the Arabic text as the Qur'an itself.
- **Mixed content.** Most surahs combine belief, stories, law and moral advice, rather than dealing with one subject at a time.
- **Opening words.** Every surah except one begins with "In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful".

## Makki and Madni surahs

Scholars divide the surahs by the period in which they were revealed. **Makki** surahs came before the Hijrah, when the Muslims were a small and persecuted group. **Madni** surahs came after the Hijrah, when there was a community and a state to organise. The division is by time, not always place: a verse revealed on a journey after the Hijrah is still counted as Madni.

| | Makki surahs | Madni surahs |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | Persecuted minority | Community and state |
| Main themes | Tawhid, the Prophethood of Muhammad (pbuh), the Day of Judgement, the Hereafter | Law, community life, worship, relations with other groups |
| Style | Short verses, strong rhythm, powerful warnings and images | Longer verses, detailed and practical |
| Stories | Earlier prophets, as comfort and warning | Often the lessons of events in Madinah, such as Uhud |
| Typical content | Rejection of idol worship; moral character; reward and punishment | Prayer, Zakah, fasting and Hajj; marriage, inheritance and trade; the ban on interest; rules of war and treaties; the hypocrites |
| Examples | al-Alaq (96), al-Kafirun (109), al-Kahf (18) | al-Baqarah (2), Al Imran (3), an-Nisa (4), al-Ma'idah (5), an-Nur (24) |

**Why the difference matters.** In Makkah, the first task was to build firm belief. In Madinah, believers needed a way of life: how to pray, how to settle disputes, how to trade and how to defend the city. The Madni surahs are therefore the main source for the detailed rulings of Shariah, while the Makki surahs supply the basic beliefs on which those rulings rest.

## The miraculous nature of the Qur'an

The Arabic word is *i'jaz*, meaning the inability of others to match it. Muslims give several reasons for calling the Qur'an a miracle.

1. **Eloquence.** The Arabs prized poetry and speech. The Qur'an was recognised by them as unlike poetry or ordinary prose, and its rhythm and meaning moved listeners, including some of its opponents.
2. **The challenge.** The Qur'an invited its critics to produce a chapter like it: "then produce a surah the like thereof" (2:23; see also 17:88). The challenge has never been met in the view of Muslims.
3. **An unlettered Prophet.** The Prophet (pbuh) did not read or write before revelation (29:48). A book of this depth, from a man with no formal teacher, is taken as evidence that it came from Allah.
4. **Consistency.** "Then do they not reflect upon the Qur'an? If it had been from [any] other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction" (4:82). It was revealed over twenty-three years, in changing circumstances, yet is coherent.
5. **Truthful accounts and predictions.** It describes earlier prophets and nations, and gives news of events that later happened. For example, Surah ar-Rum (30:2-4) tells that the Romans, who had just been defeated, would soon be victorious.
6. **Effect on people.** Many accepted Islam after hearing it recited, and it continues to change lives and bring people together.

## The infallibility of the Qur'an

Infallibility means that the Qur'an is free from error and from change. Muslims believe this for two reasons.

- **It is the word of Allah.** "Falsehood cannot approach it from before it or from behind it" (41:42). An error would suggest a human author.
- **It is preserved.** Allah promised to protect it (15:9), and the record of its memorisation and compilation shows how the community kept that trust.

For this reason Muslims do not edit the text. Translations and commentaries (*tafsir*) can be corrected, but the Qur'an itself cannot.

## How the Qur'an is used

- **In worship.** Surah al-Fatiha is recited in every rak'ah of the daily prayers, and other verses are added. Ramadhan includes the recitation of the whole Qur'an, especially in Taraweeh.
- **As a source of law.** It is the first source of Shariah. A ruling that contradicts it cannot stand.
- **For guidance in life.** Muslims turn to it for decisions about family, money, character and justice.
- **For learning.** Children begin by learning to read it; many go on to memorise it. Scholars study its meanings, background and language.
- **For spiritual comfort.** Verses are recited in times of fear, grief and gratitude, and at births and funerals.
- **For sharing the message.** Verses are used to teach, to call people to Islam and to answer questions.

## How the Qur'an is treated

- **With cleanliness.** Many scholars hold that a person should be in a state of wudu to touch the printed copy, based on the passage under study.
- **With respect in handling.** It is kept in a clean and high place, not on the floor, and not taken to unclean places. It is often covered or placed on a stand.
- **With care in recitation.** The Qur'an commands "recite the Qur'an with measured recitation" (73:4). *Tajwid* is the set of rules for pronouncing each letter correctly.
- **With attention.** "So when the Qur'an is recited, then listen to it and pay attention" (7:204).
- **With obedience.** The best way to honour it is to act on it. Knowing the words without following them misses the point.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 56:75-80

The passage reads, in the specification's translation: "Then I swear by the setting of the stars, and indeed, it is an oath, if you could know, [most] great. Indeed, it is a noble Qur'an, in a Register well-protected; none touch it except the purified [i.e., the angels]. [It is] a revelation from the Lord of the worlds."

| Phrase | What it shows |
|---|---|
| "I swear by the setting of the stars" | A solemn oath that shows the importance of what follows |
| "A noble Qur'an" | Its honour and status |
| "In a Register well-protected" | Protection; its source in the safeguarded record with Allah |
| "None touch it except the purified" | Purity and respect; the source of rules on handling (the verse speaks of angels, and many scholars apply the principle to people) |
| "A revelation from the Lord of the worlds" | Its divine origin, for all of creation |

## Why the features make it a source of authority

A source of authority is a source that Muslims accept as the basis for belief and law. The features above explain why the Qur'an holds that place. It is believed to be Allah's word, so its commands bind every Muslim. It is believed to be free from error, so there is no higher text to correct it. It contains both beliefs (mostly Makki) and rulings (mostly Madni), so it gives a complete foundation. Its use in daily prayer keeps it present in the life of every Muslim, and its respectful treatment shows how it is valued. Other sources, such as Hadith, Ijma and Qiyas, are tested against it.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which Muslims show respect for the Qur'an. **[4]**

> Muslims keep the Qur'an clean and in a high place, and many make wudu before touching it [1], because Qur'an 56:79 says that only the purified touch it and it is a noble book [1]. They also recite it with tajwid and listen quietly when it is recited [1], following the command to recite it in measured tones and to listen and pay attention when it is read [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up Makki and Madni.** Makki: belief, the Hereafter, short and powerful. Madni: law, community, long and detailed.
- **Saying the Makki surahs came first in the book.** The order of the book is not the order of revelation.
- **Describing "miracle" without evidence.** Give a reason: the challenge, an unlettered Prophet, or consistency.
- **Writing that infallibility means nobody can misread it.** It means the text is free from error and change.
- **Leaving out how it is used.** The specification asks for both use and treatment.
