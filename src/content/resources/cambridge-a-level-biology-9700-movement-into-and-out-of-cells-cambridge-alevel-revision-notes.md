## 4.2.1 The six processes

| Process | Gradient | Protein? | ATP? | Limited by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | Down | No | No | Gradient, area, distance, temperature, size and polarity of molecule |
| Facilitated diffusion | Down | Channel or carrier | No | Gradient **and** number of proteins (rate levels off) |
| Osmosis | Water: higher to lower water potential | Not required | No | Water potential gradient, area, temperature |
| Active transport | Against | Carrier | Yes | Number of carriers and ATP supply from respiration |
| Endocytosis | Bulk in, in vesicles | No | Yes | ATP supply; membrane fluidity |
| Exocytosis | Bulk out, vesicles fuse | No | Yes | ATP supply; membrane fluidity |

### Reading uptake data

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Rate rises in proportion to concentration with no plateau | Simple diffusion |
| Rate levels off at high concentration | Proteins saturated: facilitated diffusion or active transport |
| Concentration inside exceeds outside | Active transport |
| Uptake falls without oxygen or with a respiratory inhibitor | Active transport (needs ATP) |

**Example (invented):** ions reach 60 mmol dm⁻³ inside cells in a 4 mmol dm⁻³ solution: 60 ÷ 4 = 15 times higher, so uptake is against the gradient.

## 4.2.3 SA:V formulas

```
Cuboid:   SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)   V = lwh
Cylinder: SA = 2πr² + 2πrl        V = πr²l
SA:V = SA / V, written as n : 1
```

**Reminder (cuboid):** 5 × 2 × 1 cm. SA = 2(10 + 5 + 2) = 34 cm²; V = 10 cm³; SA:V = **3.4 : 1**. A cube of the same volume (side about 2.15 cm) has SA:V of about 2.8 : 1, so the flatter shape has more surface per unit volume.

- SA:V falls as size increases.
- Long, thin or flat shapes keep a higher SA:V.

## 4.2.4 Agar blocks in steps

1. Cut cubes of indicator-containing agar of different sizes.
2. Place in the same volume and concentration of acid at the same temperature.
3. Time complete colour change, or measure the depth of change after a fixed time.
4. Rate = 1 000 ÷ time.
5. Larger cubes: lower SA:V **and** longer distance to the centre → much longer time.

## 4.2.2 Visking tubing

- Starch inside, iodine outside: contents turn blue-black; iodine diffuses in, starch stays inside.
- Sucrose solution inside, water outside: tubing gains mass by osmosis.
- Tubing is partially permeable: small molecules and water pass, large ones do not.

## 4.2.5 Estimating water potential

| Method | Measure | Equilibrium point |
|---|---|---|
| Mass change of tissue cylinders | % change in mass in each solution | 0% change |
| Plasmolysis of epidermis | % of cells plasmolysed | 50% (incipient plasmolysis) |

Interpolation: fraction of the way between two readings = (target − lower reading) ÷ (upper reading − lower reading).

## 4.2.6 Water potential rules

- Pure water = 0 kPa (the highest). Solutions are negative.
- Water moves from less negative to more negative water potential.
- Plant cell in higher water potential: turgid, wall stops bursting. In lower: plasmolysed, flaccid.
- Animal cell in higher water potential: swells, may burst. In lower: shrinks, crenated.
- Solute potential and pressure potential are not expected.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | Net movement of molecules or ions from higher to lower concentration by random motion |
| Facilitated diffusion | Diffusion through channel or carrier proteins |
| Osmosis | Net movement of water from higher to lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane |
| Active transport | Movement against a concentration gradient by carrier proteins, using ATP |
| Endocytosis | Bulk uptake by the membrane enclosing material in a vesicle |
| Exocytosis | Bulk release by vesicles fusing with the cell surface membrane |
| Water potential | The tendency of water to move out of a solution; pure water is 0 kPa |
| Incipient plasmolysis | The point at which half the cells have just begun to plasmolyse |

## Exam traps

- Osmosis must mention water potential and a partially permeable membrane.
- −300 kPa is **higher** than −700 kPa.
- A plateau alone does not distinguish facilitated diffusion from active transport.
- Use the radius in πr².
- Use percentage change in mass when starting masses differ.
- Plasmolysed plant cells do not "shrink" their walls; the protoplast pulls away.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two processes that need ATP.
2. Why does the rate of facilitated diffusion level off at high concentrations?
3. Give two pieces of evidence that a substance enters by active transport.
4. Calculate SA:V for a cylinder of radius 0.5 cm and length 4 cm.
5. Why do larger agar cubes take much longer to change colour?
6. Calculate the rate for an agar cube that changes colour in 400 s, using 1 000 ÷ t.
7. Starch suspension in Visking tubing is placed in iodine solution. What happens and why?
8. Which water potential is higher: −250 kPa or −650 kPa?
9. In which direction does water move between cell X (−500 kPa) and cell Y (−300 kPa)?
10. What percentage of plasmolysed cells is taken as incipient plasmolysis?
11. Why does a red blood cell burst in distilled water, but a plant cell does not?
12. 30% of cells are plasmolysed at 0.3 mol dm⁻³ and 70% at 0.4 mol dm⁻³. Estimate the concentration at incipient plasmolysis.

### Answers

1. Any two: active transport, endocytosis, exocytosis.
2. All the channel or carrier proteins are working at full capacity.
3. Concentration inside rises above that outside; uptake falls when respiration is inhibited or oxygen removed.
4. SA = 2π(0.25) + 2π(0.5)(4) = 1.57 + 12.57 = 14.14 cm²; V = π(0.25)(4) = 3.14 cm³; SA:V = 4.5 : 1.
5. Less surface area per unit volume and a longer distance from the surface to the centre.
6. 1 000 ÷ 400 = 2.5 s⁻¹.
7. Contents turn blue-black; iodine diffuses in through the partially permeable tubing but starch is too large to leave.
8. −250 kPa.
9. From Y to X, from higher to lower water potential.
10. 50%.
11. The red blood cell has no wall, so it swells until it bursts; the plant cell wall resists expansion and the cell becomes turgid.
12. Halfway: 0.35 mol dm⁻³.
