## Definitions

- **Electric field strength, E:** force per unit positive charge, E = F/q. Unit N C⁻¹ (= V m⁻¹). A vector; direction of the force on a positive charge. (6.2.1(d))
- **Field line:** a line whose tangent gives the direction of the force on a small positive charge. Lines start on positive, end on negative; closer lines mean a stronger field; lines never cross. (6.2.1(c))
- **Electric potential at a point:** work done bringing unit positive charge from infinity to that point. Zero at infinity. Scalar. Unit V = J C⁻¹. (6.2.4(a))
- **Permittivity, ε:** property of the material between plates, ε = εᵣε₀. Relative permittivity εᵣ is at least 1. (6.2.3(b))
- **Point-charge model:** a uniformly charged sphere acts, for points outside, as if its charge were at its centre. (6.2.1(b))

## Formulae

| Result | Equation | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Field strength | E = F/q | 6.2.1(d) |
| Coulomb's law | F = Qq/(4πε₀r²) | 6.2.2(a) |
| Field of a point charge | E = Q/(4πε₀r²) | 6.2.2(b) |
| Uniform field | E = V/d | 6.2.3(a) |
| Capacitor, vacuum or air | C = ε₀A/d | 6.2.3(b) |
| Capacitor with material | C = εA/d, ε = εᵣε₀ | 6.2.3(b) |
| Potential of a point charge | V = Q/(4πε₀r) | 6.2.4(b) |
| Isolated sphere | C = 4πε₀R | 6.2.4(c) |
| Potential energy | E_p = Vq = Qq/(4πε₀r) | 6.2.4(e) |

Data used in the examples: ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹; elementary charge 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; masses of electron (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg) and proton (1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg).

## Methods in steps

**Force between two charges**
1. Convert units (nC to 10⁻⁹ C, cm to m).
2. Use the sizes of Q and q in F = Qq/(4πε₀r²).
3. State direction in words: repel (like), attract (unlike).

**Field from two charges at a point**
1. Find the size and direction of each field separately.
2. Add as vectors: along a line, subtract if opposite directions; at right angles, use Pythagoras.
3. Zero-field point between like charges: equate Q₁/x² and Q₂/(d − x)².

**Charged particle in a uniform field**
1. E = V/d, F = qE, a = F/m.
2. Along the field from rest: qV = ½mv².
3. Entering across the field: horizontal speed constant, time t = length/speed, sideways displacement y = ½at².

**Work done and potential energy**
1. V = Q/(4πε₀r) at each radius, keeping the sign of Q.
2. W = qΔV (or the change in Qq/(4πε₀r)).
3. Positive work done on the charge when like charges are pushed together.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Point or spherical charge | Parallel plates |
|---|---|---|
| Field pattern | radial lines | parallel, evenly spaced lines |
| Field strength | E ∝ 1/r², varies with r | E = V/d, same at every point in the gap |
| Equation | E = Q/(4πε₀r²) | E = V/d |

| Compare | Gravitational | Electric |
|---|---|---|
| Force | F = Gm₁m₂/r², always attractive | F = Qq/(4πε₀r²), attractive or repulsive |
| Field strength | force per unit mass | force per unit charge |
| Common ground | inverse square of distance, radial field, potential zero at infinity | same |

**Field E and potential V:** E is a vector, V is a scalar. Add fields with direction; add potentials as numbers with signs.

**Fields as forms of field (6.2.2(d)):** gravitational, electric and magnetic fields each give rise to a force on the right kind of object. Link to section 5.4 and, for magnetic, section 6.3.

**Area under a force-distance graph (6.2.4(d)):** work done moving the charge between the two distances. Estimate by trapezia (over-estimates on this curve) or squares.

## Worked reminders

**Isolated sphere derivation:** V = Q/(4πε₀R) at the surface, C = Q/V, so C = 4πε₀R. For R = 0.10 m, C = 1.1 × 10⁻¹¹ F.

**Dielectric:** inserting material with εᵣ = 2.5 raises C by a factor 2.5. Plates of area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m² and gap 1.0 mm: C = 4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ F (air), 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ F with material.

**Potential energy sign:** two protons 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ m apart store e²/(4πε₀r) = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ J, positive because they repel.

**Fields from two opposite charges:** +4.0 nC and −4.0 nC, 0.10 m apart. At the midpoint each charge is 0.050 m away and each gives E = 1.44 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹. Both point towards the negative charge, so they add: **2.9 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹**. Between like charges at the midpoint they would cancel.

**Uniform field and an electron:** plates 1.8 cm apart with 450 V. E = 450/0.018 = 2.5 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹, F = eE = 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ N, and a = F/m = 4.4 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻², directed towards the positive plate (opposite to E).

**V against r:** for a +6.0 nC point charge V = 270 V at 0.20 m and 135 V at 0.40 m. Doubling r halves V (V ∝ 1/r) but quarters E (E ∝ 1/r²).

## Graph and diagram reminders

- **Radial field of a positive point charge:** straight lines directed outwards, equally spaced around the charge; lines get further apart as r increases.
- **Two opposite charges:** curved lines from the positive to the negative charge; none cross.
- **Two charged plates:** parallel lines at right angles to the plates in the middle; draw them bending outwards near the edges.
- **F against r (point charge):** a curve that falls steeply then flattens, never touching the axis. Doubling r quarters F.
- **V against r (positive charge):** a similar but less steep curve, V ∝ 1/r. For a negative charge V is negative and rises towards zero at infinity.

## Quick self-test

1. Define electric field strength and give its unit.
2. Find the force between two point charges of 1.0 µC, 0.30 m apart.
3. Find E at 0.20 m from a point charge of +2.0 nC.
4. By what factor does E change if r is tripled?
5. Plates 0.80 cm apart have a p.d. of 240 V. Find E.
6. Plates of area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m² are 1.0 mm apart in air. Find C.
7. Find the potential 0.30 m from a point charge of +5.0 nC.
8. Find C for an isolated sphere of radius 0.10 m.
9. Find the potential energy of two protons 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ m apart.
10. An electron is accelerated from rest through 500 V. Find its final speed.
11. State what the area under a force-distance graph represents.
12. Give one way in which gravitational and electric fields are alike and one way they differ.

### Answers

1. Force per unit positive charge, E = F/q; N C⁻¹ (or V m⁻¹).
2. **0.10 N**, repulsive.
3. **4.5 × 10² N C⁻¹**, directed away from the charge.
4. Divided by 9 (E ∝ 1/r²).
5. 240/0.0080 = **3.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹**.
6. 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 5.0 × 10⁻³ / 1.0 × 10⁻³ = **4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ F**.
7. **1.5 × 10² V**.
8. **1.1 × 10⁻¹¹ F**.
9. **2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ J**.
10. v = √(2eV/m) = **1.3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹**.
11. Work done moving the charge between the two distances.
12. Alike: both follow an inverse square law for a point source. Different: gravity only attracts, but electric forces can attract or repel.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring r from the surface of a sphere instead of from its centre.
- Leaving nC, µC, cm or mm unconverted.
- Quoting a force or field without a direction when the question asks for one.
- Adding fields from two charges as plain numbers when they point in different directions.
- Using the sign of a charge inconsistently in V = Q/(4πε₀r) so that the change in potential has the wrong sign.
- Using E = V/d for a radial field, or the inverse square law between plates.
- Writing C = ε₀A/d when a material with εᵣ is present.
- Deriving C = 4πε₀R without stating that V = Q/(4πε₀R) applies at the surface, or without starting from C = Q/V.
- Ignoring the sideways displacement limit: checking whether a charged particle actually leaves the plates.
