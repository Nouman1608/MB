These questions cover section **4.5.7 Momentum** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification. The whole
section is marked (HT only), so **every question here is Higher tier only**. It is examined on Paper 2. A
calculator is allowed throughout.

Learn the content first in the [momentum study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-momentum/) and
the [momentum revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-momentum-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (Higher tier only) State the unit of momentum. **[1]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the momentum of a 0.16 kg cricket ball moving at 25 m/s. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only) A car of mass 900 kg has a momentum of 18 000 kg m/s. Calculate its velocity. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) State what is meant by a closed system. **[1]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) State the principle of conservation of momentum. **[1]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) Name two safety features whose action is explained using the rate of change of momentum. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (Higher tier only) A railway truck of mass 12 000 kg moves at 3.0 m/s along a level track. It collides with a stationary
truck of mass 8000 kg, and the two trucks couple together.

**(a)** Calculate the total momentum before the collision. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the velocity of the coupled trucks just after the collision. **[2]**
**(c)** The measured velocity just after the collision is slightly less than your answer to (b). Suggest
why. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Trolley P (mass 1.5 kg) moves to the right at 2.0 m/s. Trolley Q (mass 1.0 kg) moves to the left at
3.0 m/s. They collide head-on and stick together.

**(a)** Calculate the total momentum before the collision. Take right as positive. **[2]**
**(b)** State the velocity of the trolleys after the collision. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain your answer to (b). **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A cue ball of mass 0.17 kg moving at 2.0 m/s hits a stationary ball of mass 0.16 kg head-on. After the
collision, the cue ball moves at 0.20 m/s in its original direction. Calculate the velocity of the other
ball. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) An astronaut of total mass 90 kg floats at rest in space. She throws a 3.0 kg tool away from her at
6.0 m/s.

**(a)** Explain why the astronaut starts to move. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate her velocity. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A car driver of mass 75 kg is travelling at 14 m/s when the car crashes. An air bag brings the driver
to rest in 0.12 s.

**(a)** Calculate the driver's change in momentum. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average force on the driver from the air bag. **[2]**
**(c)** Without an air bag, the driver would hit the steering wheel and stop in 0.015 s. Calculate the force
in this case. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how the air bag reduces the force on the driver. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A football of mass 0.45 kg hits a wall at 12 m/s and rebounds along the same line at 8.0 m/s. It is
in contact with the wall for 0.025 s. Calculate the average force of the wall on the ball. **[4]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) Explain how a thick crash mat in a gymnasium reduces the risk of injury when a gymnast lands on it.
**[4]**

**14.** (Higher tier only) Describe how light gates and two trolleys on a track can be used to test whether momentum is
conserved in a collision. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** kg m/s (kilogram metres per second) [1]

**2.** p = m v = 0.16 × 25 [1]; = **4.0 kg m/s** [1]

**3.** v = p / m = 18 000 / 900 [1]; = **20 m/s** [1]

**4.** A system on which no external resultant force acts [1]

**5.** In a closed system, the total momentum before an event is equal to the total momentum after the event
[1]

**6.** Any two from: air bags, seat belts, gymnasium crash mats, cycle helmets, cushioned playground
surfaces. First feature [1]; second feature [1]

**7. (a)** 12 000 × 3.0 + 8000 × 0 [1]; = **36 000 kg m/s** [1]
**(b)** 36 000 = (12 000 + 8000) v [1]; v = **1.8 m/s** [1]
**(c)** Friction (an external force) acts on the trucks, so the system is not perfectly closed [1]

**8. (a)** 1.5 × 2.0 + 1.0 × (−3.0) [1]; = 3.0 − 3.0 = **0** [1]
**(b)** **0 m/s**: the trolleys stop [1]
**(c)** Momentum is conserved, so the total after the collision must also be zero; the stuck-together
trolleys can only have zero momentum if their velocity is zero [1]
*Examiner insight:* Without the minus sign for Q, the total becomes 6.0 kg m/s and every later step is wrong.

**9.** Momentum before = 0.17 × 2.0 = 0.34 kg m/s [1]; 0.34 = 0.17 × 0.20 + 0.16 v, so 0.16 v = 0.306 [1];
v = **1.9 m/s** in the cue ball's original direction [1]

**10. (a)** The total momentum is zero before the throw and must stay zero (momentum is conserved) [1]; the
tool gains momentum in one direction, so the astronaut gains an equal momentum in the opposite direction [1]
**(b)** 90 v = 3.0 × 6.0 = 18 [1]; v = **0.20 m/s**, in the opposite direction to the tool [1]

**11. (a)** Δp = 75 × 14 [1]; = **1050 kg m/s** [1]
**(b)** F = 1050 / 0.12 [1]; = **8750 N** (about 8800 N) [1]
**(c)** 1050 / 0.015 = **70 000 N** [1]
**(d)** The air bag increases the time taken for the driver to stop [1]; so the rate of change of momentum
is smaller, and the force on the driver is smaller [1]

**12.** Taking the rebound direction as positive, Δv = 8.0 − (−12) = 20 m/s [1]; change in momentum =
0.45 × 20 = 9.0 kg m/s [1]; F = change in momentum / time = 9.0 / 0.025 [1]; = **360 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 12 − 8.0 = 4.0 m/s ignores the change of direction and gives 72 N.

**13.** When the gymnast lands, her momentum falls to zero [1]; the mat compresses, so she takes longer to
stop [1]; this reduces the rate of change of momentum [1]; so the force on her body is smaller, reducing the
risk of injury [1]

**14.** Measure the mass of each trolley with a balance [1]; use a light gate before the collision to
measure the velocity of the moving trolley (card length ÷ time the gate is blocked) [1]; use light gates
after the collision to measure the velocities of the trolleys afterwards [1]; calculate the total momentum
before and after and compare them, using a level, low-friction track so that the system is close to closed
[1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the minus sign for motion in the opposite direction.
- Using one trolley's mass after a collision in which they stick.
- Using the difference of the two speeds for a rebound.
- "The mat absorbs the momentum" instead of "increases the time".
- Answering in N when momentum is asked for.
