International GCSE Physics (4PH1). Questions 6, 11 and 12 use the
Physics-only units in statement 1.2P. Take g = 10 N/kg.

## Section A

**1.** State the unit of acceleration and the unit of force. **[2]**

**2.** Which quantity is measured in N/kg? **[1]**

**3.** Convert:

**(a)** 1.8 km to metres **[1]**
**(b)** 45 cm to metres **[1]**
**(c)** 3.0 minutes to seconds **[1]**

**4.** A student writes "the weight of the box is 12 kg". Explain what is wrong with this statement and correct it. **[2]**

**5.** Show that the unit N/kg is equivalent to m/s². **[2]**

**6.** State the units of momentum and of the moment of a force. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A cyclist travels at 18 km/h.

**(a)** Convert this speed to m/s. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how far the cyclist travels at this speed in 2.5 minutes. **[2]**

**8.** A toy car of mass 250 g accelerates uniformly from 0.40 m/s to 1.6 m/s in 0.80 s.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration of the car, giving the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resultant force on the car. **[3]**

**9.** A student calculates the distance a car travels using v² = u² + 2as and rearranges it to s = (v² − u²) × 2a.

**(a)** Use units to show that this rearrangement must be wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the correct rearrangement. **[1]**

**10.** A lift and its passengers have a total mass of 1.2 tonnes.

**(a)** Calculate their total weight. **[2]**
**(b)** The lift accelerates upwards at 0.50 m/s². Calculate the resultant force needed. **[2]**
**(c)** State the unit of the answer to (b) in terms of kg, m and s. **[1]**

**11.** A gardener pushes down with a force of 120 N at the end of a lever, 80 cm from the pivot, at right angles to the lever.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of the force, giving the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the answer should not be given as 9600. **[1]**

**12.** A lorry of mass 12 tonnes travels at 54 km/h. It brakes to rest in 6.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the momentum of the lorry before braking. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average braking force. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says the change in momentum could be written in N s. Show that N s is the same unit as kg m/s. **[2]**

**13.** A velocity–time graph for a runner has time on the horizontal axis in minutes and velocity on the vertical axis in km/h. Over the first 0.50 minutes the line rises steadily from 0 to 18 km/h.

**(a)** Convert both readings to SI units. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the runner's acceleration in m/s². **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the distance the runner covers in this time, in metres. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why using the values straight from the axes, without converting, would give a gradient that is not in m/s². **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Acceleration: m/s² [1]. Force: N (newton) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Gravitational field strength (g) [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** 1800 m [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 0.45 m [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 180 s [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons, not kilograms; 12 kg is the mass [1]. Weight = m × g = 12 × 10 = 120 N [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 1 N = 1 kg m/s², from F = m × a [1]. So N/kg = (kg m/s²) ÷ kg = m/s² [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Momentum: kg m/s [1]. Moment: Nm [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 18 × 1000 ÷ 3600 [1] = 5.0 m/s [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2.5 minutes = 150 s [1]; distance = speed × time = 5.0 × 150 = 750 m [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** a = (v − u) ÷ t = (1.6 − 0.40) ÷ 0.80 [1] = 1.5 m/s² [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** m = 250 g = 0.25 kg [1]; F = m × a = 0.25 × 1.5 [1] = 0.375 N, so 0.38 N (2 s.f.) [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Units of (v² − u²) × 2a are (m²/s²) × (m/s²) = m³/s⁴ [1], which is not metres, the unit of distance s, so the rearrangement cannot be correct [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** s = (v² − u²) ÷ 2a [1]. (Check: (m²/s²) ÷ (m/s²) = m.) **[1]**

**10. (a)** 1.2 tonnes = 1200 kg [1]; W = m × g = 1200 × 10 = 12 000 N [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** F = m × a = 1200 × 0.50 [1] = 600 N [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** kg m/s² [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** 80 cm = 0.80 m; moment = 120 × 0.80 [1] = 96 Nm [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 9600 is the moment in N cm, because the distance was not converted to metres; in Nm the value is 96 [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** 12 tonnes = 12 000 kg [1]; 54 km/h = 54 ÷ 3.6 = 15 m/s [1]; p = m × v = 12 000 × 15 = 180 000 kg m/s (1.8 × 10⁵ kg m/s) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** F = change in momentum ÷ time taken = (0 − 180 000) ÷ 6.0 [1] = −30 000 N, so the braking force is 30 000 N (3.0 × 10⁴ N) opposite to the motion [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 1 N = 1 kg m/s² [1], so 1 N s = 1 kg m/s² × s = 1 kg m/s [1]. **[2]**

**13. (a)** 0.50 minutes = 30 s [1]; 18 km/h = 18 ÷ 3.6 = 5.0 m/s [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Acceleration = gradient = (5.0 − 0) ÷ 30 [1] = 0.17 m/s² (0.167 m/s²) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Distance = area under the graph = ½ × 30 × 5.0 [1] = 75 m [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The gradient would be 18 ÷ 0.50 = 36 in km/h per minute, a unit that mixes kilometres, hours and minutes, so it is not metres per second per second [1]. **[1]**
