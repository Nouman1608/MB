## Questions

**1.** State where the oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from, and describe what happens to the hydrogen from that source. **[3]**

**2.** Distinguish between photophosphorylation and the hydrolysis of ATP. **[3]**

**3.** Compare cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation. **[4]**

**4.** A suspension of isolated chloroplasts fixes 7.2 µmol of carbon dioxide in a Calvin cycle.
**(a)** Calculate the amount of ATP and of reduced NADP needed. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount of glucose that could be made. **[2]**

**5.** A culture of unicellular algae in bright light is placed in darkness. Within one minute the GP level rises and the RuBP level falls. Explain these changes. **[4]**

**6.** Explain how three features of a chloroplast help it to carry out photosynthesis. **[5]**

**7.** The table shows the rate of oxygen release by a pondweed at different wavelengths, and the absorbance of a leaf pigment extract.

| Wavelength (nm) | 400 | 450 | 500 | 550 | 600 | 650 | 700 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (arbitrary units) | 30 | 78 | 42 | 20 | 35 | 70 | 12 |
| Absorbance | 0.62 | 0.82 | 0.30 | 0.12 | 0.25 | 0.70 | 0.08 |

**(a)** Define the terms action spectrum and absorption spectrum. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate at 550 nm as a percentage of the rate at 450 nm. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what the data show about the relationship between absorption and photosynthesis. **[3]**

**8.** A leaf extract was separated by chromatography in a propanone-based solvent. The solvent front moved 9.0 cm. Four pigments moved 8.1, 6.3, 4.5 and 3.6 cm.
**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of each pigment. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the pigments separate. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the pigments could be identified. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how you would investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of photosynthesis of Canadian pondweed. **[7]**

**10.** In an investigation of light intensity, a lamp was placed at four distances from a pondweed shoot. The number of bubbles in 2 minutes was counted.

| Distance (cm) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bubbles in 2 minutes | 52 | 15 | 7 | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate in bubbles per minute at each distance. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the light intensity is at 10 cm than at 20 cm. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe and explain the trend, and suggest why the rate at 10 cm is less than four times the rate at 20 cm. **[4]**

**11.** State how the sugars made in photosynthesis are used by plants, animals and other organisms. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The oxygen comes from water [1], split by photolysis in the light-dependent reactions [1]. The hydrogen (H⁺ and electrons) is used to reduce NADP and is then used to reduce GP and so is stored in glucose [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is naming carbon dioxide as the source of oxygen, which is wrong. Say where the hydrogen ends up, not only where it comes from.

**2.** Photophosphorylation joins ADP and Pi to make ATP [1], using energy from light (excited electrons) [1]. Hydrolysis of ATP splits it to ADP and Pi, releasing energy for processes such as active transport [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that only say ATP "gives energy" without naming ADP and Pi are too vague. Describe both reactions in terms of their reactants and products.

**3.** Similarity: both make ATP [1]. Cyclic uses only PSI whereas non-cyclic uses PSII and PSI [1]. Cyclic does not produce reduced NADP or oxygen, whereas non-cyclic does (photolysis of water) [1]. In cyclic, electrons return to PSI, whereas in non-cyclic they are replaced by electrons from water [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides stated for each point. Writing only "cyclic does not make oxygen" leaves half of the comparison missing.

**4. (a)** ATP per CO₂ = 3, so 7.2 × 3 = **21.6 µmol ATP** [1]. Reduced NADP per CO₂ = 2, so 7.2 × 2 = **14.4 µmol** [1]. Reasoning: 2 GP per CO₂ are reduced (2 ATP, 2 reduced NADP) and RuBP regeneration adds 1 ATP per CO₂ fixed [1].
**(b)** 6 CO₂ are needed for each glucose [1], so 7.2 ÷ 6 = **1.2 µmol glucose** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the per-CO₂ figures before multiplying, so that the reader can see your reasoning clearly. Give units.

**5.** In the dark, ATP and reduced NADP are no longer made by the light-dependent reactions [1]. GP cannot be reduced to GALP, so GP accumulates [1]. RuBP is still being used to fix CO₂ by RUBISCO [1] but it cannot be regenerated from GALP without ATP, so RuBP falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the cause (loss of ATP and reduced NADP) before the effect on each compound. Explaining GP and RuBP separately avoids vague statements.

**6.** Thylakoid membranes give a large surface area [1] for many photosystems and ATP synthase molecules [1]. Grana pack the photosystems closely for light capture, linked by lamellae [1]. Stroma contains the enzymes (such as RUBISCO) for the Calvin cycle, next to the thylakoids [1]. The small thylakoid space allows a steep H⁺ gradient to build up [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each feature needs a linked function. A list of structure names alone does not explain anything.

**7. (a)** An action spectrum is a graph of the rate of photosynthesis against wavelength [1]. An absorption spectrum is a graph of the absorbance of a pigment against wavelength [1].
**(b)** 20 ÷ 78 × 100 = **26%** [1].
**(c)** Both peak in blue (about 450 nm) and red (about 650 nm) [1], so the wavelengths best absorbed are those that give the highest rate [1]. The shapes are not identical, for example rate at 500 nm is higher than absorbance alone suggests, so accessory pigments or other effects may pass energy on to the photosystems [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table when describing the trend, and state the relationship rather than just describing each curve in turn.

**8. (a)** 8.1 ÷ 9.0 = **0.90** and 6.3 ÷ 9.0 = **0.70** [1]; 4.5 ÷ 9.0 = **0.50** and 3.6 ÷ 9.0 = **0.40** [1]
**(b)** The pigments differ in solubility in the solvent [1] and in how strongly they are attracted to the paper, so they move at different rates [1].
**(c)** Compare the Rf values with known reference values for the same solvent and paper [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Rf has no units. Showing the division makes your method clear. Marking the solvent front before it dries avoids an unreliable value.

**9.** Set up a lamp at a fixed distance, with a heat shield, and a shoot of pondweed in a tube [1]. Use a water bath at a set temperature, for example 10 °C, and measure it with a thermometer [1]. Add sodium hydrogencarbonate solution so that CO₂ is not limiting [1]. Leave the plant for a few minutes to adjust [1]. Count the bubbles for a fixed time, for example 3 minutes, or measure gas volume in a capillary [1]. Repeat at several temperatures such as 10, 20, 30, 40 °C, with the same shoot and the same light each time [1]. Repeat each temperature and calculate a mean rate [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a range with enough values, and say how each controlled variable is held constant rather than listing it. A plan that has two variables changing cannot support a conclusion.

**10. (a)** 26, 7.5, 3.5, 2.0 bubbles per minute [1].
**(b)** (20 ÷ 10)² = **4 times** [1].
**(c)** The rate falls as the lamp moves away [1], because light intensity falls and light is limiting the light-dependent reactions, so less ATP and reduced NADP are made [1]. The rate would be 4 × 7.5 = 30 bubbles per minute if proportional, but is 26 [1], so another factor such as CO₂ concentration or temperature is probably limiting at high intensity [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures from the table in the comparison. Writing the factor as "limiting" is more convincing if you say which stage it limits.

**11.** Used in respiration to release energy (in plants, animals and other organisms) [1]. Converted to polysaccharides such as starch and cellulose [1]. Used with nitrate to make amino acids and so proteins, and to make lipids [1]. Used to make the components of nucleic acids [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming one product from each class of molecule gains more than repeating respiration. Say "animals obtain the sugars by feeding" if the question mentions animals.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that the oxygen released comes from carbon dioxide.
- Describing ATP as "energy" instead of as a molecule that releases energy when hydrolysed.
- Missing the reduced NADP and oxygen difference in cyclic and non-cyclic comparisons.
- Saying the light-independent stage "needs no light" without linking it to ATP and reduced NADP.
- Explaining a change in GP or RuBP without naming ATP, reduced NADP or CO₂.
- Listing chloroplast structures with no function.
- Calculating Rf with units or measuring to the edge of the spot.
- Not saying how the other variables are controlled in a practical.
