These questions cover section 1.3, Use of technology in accounting, in Topic 1 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. All businesses and figures are invented. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign. Teaching is in the [use of technology study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-use-of-technology-in-accounting/) and the [use of technology revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-use-of-technology-in-accounting-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State **two** benefits of using accounting software. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** benefit of using a spreadsheet to prepare a budget. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each method of protecting data is hardware or software: (a) encryption; (b) an uninterruptible power supply; (c) a firewall; (d) a fingerprint scanner on the office door. **[4]**

**4.** A spreadsheet holds monthly sales in cells D2 to D5. Write the formula to total them in D6, and the formula in E2 to show D2 as a percentage of the total. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by the confidentiality of accounting data. **[2]**

**6.** Identify the security issue (data loss, access or confidentiality) in each incident: (a) a virus corrupts the sales ledger; (b) a cleaner uses an unattended computer to look at the wages file; (c) a printout of customer balances is left on a café table. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Westgate Bakery's spreadsheet shows monthly costs: wages 5,600, rent 2,000, electricity 900 and other costs 500.

**(a)** Calculate the total monthly costs, and the new total if wages rise by 5%. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a spreadsheet is useful for this kind of calculation. **[2]**

**8.** A bookkeeper using accounting software records the purchase of a delivery van for 9,000 by debiting motor expenses. The trial balance produced by the software agrees.

**(a)** State the effect of the error on profit for the year and on non-current assets. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the software did not detect the error. **[2]**

**9.** At Kinross Opticians, a laptop holding unencrypted customer records was stolen from a staff member's car, and the only copy of last month's records was on it. The three staff also share one password to the accounting system. Recommend **one** hardware method and **one** software method for each of two issues in this case, explaining how each method helps. **[8]**

**10.** Fernhill Joinery, a sole trader, keeps manual books. Evaluate whether it should change to accounting software. **[6]**

**11.** Explain why backups of accounting data should be made frequently and stored away from the business premises. **[4]**

**12.** Explain **two** limitations of using a spreadsheet, rather than accounting software, to keep a business's ledger accounts. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: transactions are posted to all accounts automatically; balances are always up to date; reports such as the trial balance are produced on demand; invoices are produced from the records; an audit trail shows who made each entry [1] [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Formulas recalculate automatically when a figure changes [1], so the owner can test "what if" changes, such as a rise in costs, quickly before deciding [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (a) Software [1]. (b) Hardware [1]. (c) Software [1]. (d) Hardware [1]. **[4]**

**4.** D6: =SUM(D2:D5) [1]. E2: =D2/D6*100 (or =D2/D$6*100) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Keeping information private, so it is seen only by authorised people [1] and used only for the purpose for which it was given, such as a customer's credit limit not being disclosed to others [1]. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Data loss [1]. (b) Access [1]. (c) Confidentiality [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Total 5,600 + 2,000 + 900 + 500 = **9,000** [1]. New wages 5,600 × 1.05 = 5,880 [1]. New total **9,280** [1].
**(b)** The wage rate can be held in one cell and every total updates when it changes [1], so different rises can be tested quickly without re-adding the figures [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Expenses are overstated by 9,000 [1], so profit for the year is understated by **9,000** [1]. The van is missing from non-current assets [1], which are understated by **9,000** [1].
**(b)** The software only checks that debits equal credits [1]; the debit was made to the wrong type of account, an error of principle, so the totals still agree [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Confidentiality, hardware: keep laptops locked away and never leave them in cars, or use a lockable case [1], so customer records are less likely to be stolen [1]. Confidentiality, software: encrypt the laptop's files [1], so a thief cannot read them even if it is stolen [1]. Data loss, hardware: keep a backup on an external drive stored away from the laptop [1], so the records survive if the laptop is lost [1]. Access, software: give each member of staff an individual password and access level [1], so entries can be traced to a person and staff see only what they need [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Any two issues from the case can be used, but each method must be linked to its issue.

**10.** For: entries are posted automatically, saving time and avoiding posting errors [1]; up-to-date balances and reports help the owner chase customers and plan [1]. Against: the software and training cost money, which matters to a small business [1]; errors of principle and wrong input are not detected, so a skilled bookkeeper is still needed [1]; a system failure without backups could lose records [1]. Judgement: change if the volume of transactions is large enough to justify the cost, with regular backups in place [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Frequent backups mean that, if the records are lost, only the work since the last backup must be re-entered [1], so less time is wasted and fewer records are missing [1]. Storing the copy away from the premises means a fire, flood or theft at the business does not destroy both the original and the backup [1], so the records can still be restored [1]. **[4]**

**12.** A spreadsheet has no built-in double-entry checks [1], so a one-sided entry or wrong formula can go unnoticed and the records may not balance [1]. A number can be typed over a formula by mistake [1], giving totals that look correct but are wrong, with no audit trail to show who changed them [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing single-word benefits with no consequence.
- Saying software prevents all errors.
- Classifying a protection method wrongly as hardware or software.
- Recommending methods without linking them to the case's issues.
- Ignoring costs and limits in an evaluation.
