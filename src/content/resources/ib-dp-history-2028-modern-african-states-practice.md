> **HL only. These are original questions written for Marlbridge** to practise Regional study 12 of Africa and the Middle East. They are not reproduced exam questions, and the real Paper 3 has essay questions only and requires one prescribed country. Use the country you have studied. Source extracts are constructed for practice. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-modern-african-states/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-modern-african-states-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short answers

**1.** Explain one reason why a single-party state was introduced in one prescribed country. **[3]**

**2.** Identify two environmental factors that contributed to instability in Niger, Somalia or Ethiopia. **[2]**

**3.** Describe one way in which mobile technology affected the economy of a prescribed country. **[2]**

## Section B: source and argument

*Constructed for practice, not a real source:* "The party said that opposition was a luxury that a poor country could not afford. By the end, the party was the only thing the country could not afford." (a historian's view, paraphrased)

**4.** Explain the argument made in this extract about single-party rule. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one way in which this view could be challenged. **[3]**

**6.** Examine the reasons why Somalia's state collapsed in 1991. **[6]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** To what extent was pressure from foreign governments and institutions the main reason for the move to multi-party democracy in one prescribed country? **[15]**

**8.** Evaluate the view that the African Union and the United Nations have been ineffective in dealing with conflict in one prescribed country. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark for a reason, up to two for development, with an example. For instance in Zambia: Kaunda's UNIP banned other parties in 1972 on the grounds that division would weaken national unity, and the state faced pressure from the Rhodesian war and economic problems. In Tunisia, Bourguiba presented the Neo-Destour as the embodiment of the nation after independence in 1956. Credit another country with accurate support. **[3]**

**2.** One mark each for two of: recurring droughts in the Sahel or the Horn of Africa; desertification and loss of grazing land; competition for water and pasture; famine linked to rainfall failure (for example Ethiopia 1983 to 1985, Niger in 2005 and 2010, Somalia in 1992 and 2011). **[2]**

**3.** One mark for the technology (mobile phones, mobile money); one for the effect, for example in Somalia, money transfers and payments continued without a working banking system and supported trade, while in Ethiopia and Zambia mobile services spread access to markets and banking. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that the single party justified itself by poverty and national unity; one for the claim that this was self-defeating; one for the implication that one-party rule worsened the economic crisis through corruption and the lack of accountability. **[3]**

**5.** One mark for a challenge, up to two for development. For example: some one-party states, such as Tunisia under Bourguiba, achieved gains in education and women's rights; the view overlooks external causes of economic problems such as falling copper prices and debt in Zambia; or it depends on hindsight, since many citizens supported the party at the time of independence. **[3]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Credit reasons: Barre's repressive rule and favouritism towards his own clan; the failure of the Ogaden War in 1977 to 1978 and the loss of Soviet and then US support; armed clan-based opposition movements; the economic crisis and the Cold War arms supply; Barre's flight in January 1991, which left no recognised government. Credit the effect of drought and the attempts to create Somaliland in 1991. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description, 3 to 4 for partial examination, 5 to 6 for balanced examination with a conclusion. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and the main factors (external pressure, economic crisis, domestic opposition);
- up to 4 for evidence of foreign pressure (the end of the Cold War, aid conditionality, IMF structural adjustment, Western diplomacy);
- up to 4 for domestic factors (union and church movements, student protests, split in the ruling elite, economic collapse; for Zambia, the Movement for Multiparty Democracy; for Niger, the 1991 national conference);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, with attention to how internal and external factors interacted. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and the criteria for "ineffective";
- up to 4 for evidence of weakness (the UN's withdrawal from Somalia after 1993, limited mandate and funding, slow response, the AU's reliance on external funding, lack of will to enforce suspensions);
- up to 4 for evidence of effectiveness (AMISOM's role against Al-Shabaab from 2007, humanitarian relief, UN sanctions and mediation, AU suspensions after coups);
- up to 4 for evaluation of perspectives and a judgement, for example that the effect depended on the willingness of member states. **[15]**

## Model paragraph and feedback

Here is a model paragraph for Question 7, using Zambia, followed by points on how to improve a draft.

*Model paragraph:* "Zambia's return to multi-party politics in 1991 followed years of economic decline, as the fall in copper prices left the one-party state with debt, shortages and unrest. Foreign donors and the IMF linked aid to reform, and the end of the Cold War reduced the value of Kaunda as an ally. However, the change was achieved by Zambians: trade unions led by Frederick Chiluba, church leaders and business people formed the Movement for Multiparty Democracy, and Kaunda agreed to hold an election. This essay argues that external pressure helped create the conditions, but organised domestic opposition was the main reason, because it turned discontent into a political movement."

*Feedback points:*

- The paragraph weighs two sets of causes and states which is stronger.
- Use one named country and give dates for key events.
- Mention how external and internal factors interacted.
- End with a judgement that responds to the exact words of the question.
