Condensed notes for sub-topic **4.2 Accounting for depreciation and disposal of non-current assets**. A full disposal at a profit, with journals and all three ledger accounts, is in the [4.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-accounting-for-depreciation-and-disposal-of-non-current/); test yourself with the [4.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-accounting-for-depreciation-and-disposal-of-non-current-practice/).

## What 4.2 asks for

- The meaning of depreciation and the need to account for it.
- Straight-line, reducing balance and revaluation methods.
- Appropriate methods for different types of non-current asset.
- Journal entries and ledger accounts for depreciation.
- Journal entries for the purchase and sale of non-current assets.
- The non-current asset, provision for depreciation and disposal accounts.
- Calculating the profit or loss on disposal.

## Meaning and need

**Depreciation:** the part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in a period, through wear and tear, passage of time or becoming out of date.

**Why account for it:** matching (cost spread over the years of use); profit not overstated; assets not overstated (prudence). It is a non-cash expense and does not create a replacement fund.

## Formulas

| Method | Formula | Charge pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Straight-line | (Cost - residual value) / useful life, or a % of cost | Same each full year |
| Reducing balance | % x net book value at the start of the year | Falls each year |
| Revaluation | Opening valuation + purchases - closing valuation | Varies |

**Net book value** = cost - accumulated depreciation (the provision balance for that asset).

**Part years:** follow the policy given. Monthly: multiply by months owned / 12. Or "full year in year of purchase, none in year of sale".

**Example:** machine 9,000 bought 1 May, 10% of cost per year, monthly basis, year end 31 December: 9,000 x 10% x 8/12 = 600.

## Choosing a method

| Asset | Method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Machinery, fixtures and fittings | Straight-line | Even use and benefit |
| Vehicles, computers | Reducing balance | Most value lost early; repairs rise later |
| Loose tools, small utensils | Revaluation | Many small items, hard to track individually |

## The entries

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Buy asset by bank | Asset account | Bank |
| Buy asset on credit | Asset account | Supplier |
| Annual depreciation | Statement of profit or loss | Provision for depreciation |
| Disposal: remove cost | Disposal | Asset account |
| Disposal: remove accumulated depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Disposal: proceeds by bank | Bank | Disposal |
| Profit on disposal | Disposal | Statement of profit or loss |
| Loss on disposal | Statement of profit or loss | Disposal |

## Disposal in four lines

1. Accumulated depreciation on the asset sold, to the date of sale.
2. Net book value = cost - accumulated depreciation.
3. Compare with proceeds.
4. Proceeds above net book value: **profit** (other income). Below: **loss** (expense).

**Example:** cost 20,000; accumulated depreciation 13,500; proceeds 5,800. Net book value 6,500; loss on disposal 700.

## In the financial statements

- **Statement of profit or loss:** depreciation for the year (expense); profit on disposal (income) or loss on disposal (expense).
- **Statement of financial position:** each class at cost, less accumulated depreciation, giving net book value.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | Part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in a period |
| Residual value | Expected amount received at the end of the asset's useful life |
| Useful life | Period over which the asset is expected to be used |
| Net book value | Cost less accumulated depreciation |
| Provision for depreciation | Account holding accumulated depreciation on a class of asset |
| Disposal account | Account used to calculate the profit or loss on sale of an asset |
| Obsolescence | Becoming out of date |

## Exam traps

- Reducing balance is on **net book value**, not cost.
- Depreciation is credited to the **provision** account, never the asset account.
- Profit or loss on disposal compares proceeds with **net book value**.
- With a monthly policy, include depreciation **up to the date of sale** in the amount transferred to disposal.
- The disposal account has **no balance** after the profit or loss is transferred.
- A balancing figure on the **debit** of the disposal account is a profit.

## Self-test

**1.** Define depreciation.

**2.** Give two reasons for charging depreciation.

**3.** A machine cost 15,000, residual value 3,000, life 4 years. Calculate annual straight-line depreciation.

**4.** A van cost 20,000. Depreciation is 25% reducing balance. Calculate depreciation for year 2.

**5.** Tools valued at 1,200 at the start, purchases 450, valued at 1,050 at the end. Calculate depreciation.

**6.** Which method suits computers, and why?

**7.** Give the journal entry for annual depreciation of 3,600.

**8.** Equipment cost 8,000; accumulated depreciation 5,200; sold for 3,100. Calculate the profit or loss.

**9.** On which side of the disposal account is the sale proceeds entered?

**10.** Equipment 6,000 bought 1 September, 15% of cost per year, monthly basis, year end 31 December. Calculate this year's depreciation.

**11.** Where is a loss on disposal shown?

**12.** Why does depreciation not provide cash for replacing an asset?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The part of the cost of a non-current asset that is used up in an accounting period.

**2.** Any two: to match the cost of the asset with the revenue it helps to earn; to avoid overstating profit; to avoid overstating assets (prudence).

**3.** (15,000 - 3,000) / 4 = 3,000.

**4.** Year 1: 5,000, net book value 15,000; year 2: 15,000 x 25% = 3,750.

**5.** 1,200 + 450 - 1,050 = 600.

**6.** Reducing balance: they lose value quickly in early years and become out of date.

**7.** Debit statement of profit or loss 3,600; credit provision for depreciation 3,600.

**8.** Net book value 2,800; profit on disposal 300.

**9.** The credit side.

**10.** 6,000 x 15% x 4/12 = 300.

**11.** As an expense in the statement of profit or loss.

**12.** It is a book entry with no cash payment; it reduces profit but sets no money aside.
