## 4.8 Water and ions: recall table

| Item | Role in plants | If short |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Solvent and transport medium; reactant in photosynthesis and hydrolysis; turgor for support and cell expansion; transpiration stream; cooling | Wilting, loss of turgor, slower growth and photosynthesis |
| Nitrate | Nitrogen for amino acids, proteins, enzymes, nucleotides, chlorophyll | Stunted growth; older leaves pale or yellow |
| Calcium ions | Calcium pectate in the middle lamella; membrane function; cell division | Weak, distorted new growth; dying growing tips |
| Magnesium ions | Centre of chlorophyll; enzyme activator | Yellowing between veins, older leaves first |

Mobile (nitrate, magnesium): old leaves show deficiency first. Poorly mobile (calcium): young tissue shows it first.

### Mineral deficiency practical in steps

1. Prepare a complete nutrient solution and solutions each missing one ion.
2. Use same-sized seedlings, several per solution, with the same light, temperature and aeration.
3. Record height, leaf colour, leaf area. Finish with dry mass.
4. Compare with the complete solution (control).

Percentage reduction = (control − treatment) / control × 100.

*Reminder:* control 3.2 g, magnesium-free 2.0 g. Reduction = 1.2 / 3.2 × 100 = **37.5%**.

Why dry mass? Fresh mass changes with water content. Dry mass measures the biological material made.

## 4.9 Core Practical 8: tensile strength

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Force at breaking | F = m × g (g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹) | N |
| Cross-sectional area | A = π (d / 2)² | m² |
| Tensile strength | F / A | Pa (N m⁻²) |

Method in steps:

1. Same length of each fibre; measure diameter in several places; take the mean.
2. Clamp one end and hang a mass holder on the other.
3. Add equal masses gradually until the fibre breaks. Record the total.
4. Repeat for several fibres. Ignore breaks at the clamp.

*Reminder:* diameter 0.20 mm, breaks at 1.9 kg. F = 1.9 × 9.81 = 18.6 N. A = π × (1.0 × 10⁻⁴)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁸ m². Strength = 18.6 / 3.14 × 10⁻⁸ = **5.9 × 10⁸ Pa**.

Link to structure: cellulose microfibrils run along the fibre and are held by hydrogen bonds. Secondary thickening adds layers of wall. Lignin adds stiffness.

Controls: length, moisture state, loading rate, measured (not assumed) diameter.

## 4.10 Bacterial growth

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| Carbon and nitrogen sources, minerals, vitamins if needed | Raw materials for respiration and biosynthesis |
| Water | Medium for reactions |
| Suitable temperature | Enzymes work best near an optimum; high heat denatures them |
| Suitable pH | Enzyme shape and activity |
| Oxygen (aerobes), absence of oxygen (obligate anaerobes) | Matches respiration type |
| No inhibitors | Antibiotics and disinfectants prevent growth |

Binary fission: N = N₀ × 2ⁿ.

*Reminder:* 1 cell, generation time 20 min, 3 hours. n = 180 / 20 = 9. N = 2⁹ = **512**.

## 4.12 Core Practical 9: antimicrobial plants

Aseptic technique, short list:

* Disinfect bench; work near a Bunsen flame.
* Flame the loop until red hot, then cool it.
* Open lids briefly; flame bottle necks.
* Sterile equipment only.
* Tape lid with short strips; incubate upside down at about 25 °C.
* Do not open afterwards; sterilise waste.

Method in steps:

1. Spread bacteria over nutrient agar to give a lawn.
2. Add sterile discs soaked in extracts. Include a solvent-only disc (negative control) and an antibiotic disc (positive control).
3. Incubate. Measure clear zones.
4. Compare areas: area = π (d / 2)².

*Reminder:* disc 6 mm, zone 16 mm across. Total area = π × 8² = 201 mm². Disc = 28.3 mm². Zone beyond the disc = **173 mm²**.

Variables to hold constant: disc size, extract volume and concentration, agar depth, bacterial density, incubation time and temperature.

## 4.13 Drug testing

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Withering | Studied foxglove from a mixed herbal remedy for dropsy; varied doses; recorded benefits and toxic effects; published in 1785 |
| Placebo | Looks identical, no active ingredient; controls for expectation |
| Blind | Patient does not know which treatment they have |
| Double blind | Neither patient nor staff who treat or assess know; reduces bias from both sides |
| Phase 1 | Few healthy volunteers: safety, side effects, dose range |
| Phase 2 | Small number of patients: effectiveness and dose |
| Phase 3 | Large number of patients: compare with placebo or existing treatment |

Random allocation spreads other differences between groups. Informed consent is an ethical requirement.

*Reminder:* 200 patients per group, improved: drug 84, placebo 60. Drug = 42.0%, placebo = 30.0%. Difference = **12.0 percentage points**. A statistical test is still needed.

## Evaluating the practicals

**Mineral deficiencies.** Seedlings differ genetically, so use several per treatment and take means. Leaf colour is a judgement, so compare against a colour chart or photograph under the same light. Deionised water and clean glassware matter, because tap water contains ions. Water culture shows that an ion is needed, but it does not copy soil, where ions bind to particles.

**Tensile strength.** Weak points in a fibre make it break below its true strength, and a few results can be well below the rest. Look at the spread of repeats before averaging. Adding masses too fast adds a shock load. Fibres kept in a damp store may behave differently from dry ones, so state the condition. Diameter is the largest source of error because the area depends on the diameter squared, so a small error in d gives roughly double that percentage error in area.

**Antimicrobial discs.** Different extracts may contain different concentrations, so equal volumes do not mean equal amounts of active substance. Compounds that dissolve in oil may not diffuse through agar, so a plant with no zone might still be antimicrobial. Measure the zone at two perpendicular diameters and take the mean. Do not treat a bigger zone as proof that a substance would work as a medicine in a person.

## Command words in this topic

* **Explain** needs a reason joined to the fact: "magnesium is part of chlorophyll, so less light is absorbed".
* **Describe** (a method) needs the order, a named control and a measurement.
* **Evaluate** needs a strength and a limitation, with a judgement about reliability.
* **Calculate** needs working and a unit, with an answer to a sensible number of significant figures.

## Distinctions to know

* Nitrate (the ion absorbed) versus nitrogen (the element in the molecules made).
* Magnesium (in chlorophyll) versus calcium (in the middle lamella).
* Breaking mass versus tensile strength (force per unit area).
* Placebo (the dummy treatment) versus double blind (who knows).
* Zone of inhibition shows growth did not occur. It does not show cells were killed.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two roles of water in plants.
2. Why does nitrate shortage cause yellowing of older leaves?
3. Name the compound in the middle lamella that needs calcium.
4. A fibre of diameter 0.10 mm breaks at 0.80 kg. Find its tensile strength to 2 significant figures.
5. A culture of 40 cells has a generation time of 30 minutes. How many cells after 3 hours?
6. Why is the Petri dish lid taped with short strips only?
7. Why compare areas rather than diameters of inhibition zones?
8. Why is a solvent-only disc used?
9. Give two ways a modern trial improved on Withering's method.
10. Which phase mainly tests safety in healthy volunteers?

### Answers

1. Any two of: solvent and transport; reactant in photosynthesis; gives turgor; transpiration; hydrolysis.
2. Nitrate is mobile and is moved to young leaves, so older leaves lose nitrogen needed for chlorophyll.
3. Calcium pectate.
4. F = 0.80 × 9.81 = 7.85 N. A = π × (5.0 × 10⁻⁵)² = 7.85 × 10⁻⁹ m². Strength = **1.0 × 10⁹ Pa**.
5. n = 180 / 30 = 6. 40 × 2⁶ = **2560**.
6. To allow oxygen in, so strictly anaerobic (and often more dangerous) bacteria are not favoured.
7. The zone is circular, so area grows with the square of the radius. Area reflects the extent of inhibition.
8. To show that the solvent alone does not stop growth.
9. Placebo control and double blinding (also random allocation or a larger sample).
10. Phase 1.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Giving "nitrogen" as the ion absorbed, when the answer is nitrate.
* Saying magnesium makes chlorophyll "green" without stating it sits at the centre of the molecule.
* Forgetting to convert mm to m before calculating pascals.
* Giving breaking mass as the answer to "determine tensile strength".
* Describing aseptic technique as "keeping things clean" without a named action.
* Not naming a control in the antimicrobial practical.
* Explaining a placebo only as a "fake drug".
* Treating all three phases as the same, instead of stating the size and purpose of each.
