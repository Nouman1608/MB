## Questions

**1.** (a) Write 84/126 in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 6 5/8 as an improper fraction. **[1]**

**2.** Work out 7/12 + 4/9. Give your answer as a mixed number. **[3]**

**3.** Work out 4 1/2 − 2 7/8. Give your answer as a mixed number. **[3]**

**4.** (a) Work out 5/14 × 7/10. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out −2 1/4 ÷ 1 1/2. **[3]**

**5.** (a) Find 5/9 of £8.10. **[2]**
**(b)** A bag of flour holds 1.2 kg. A baker uses 450 g. Write the amount used as a fraction of the bag, in its simplest form. **[3]**
**(c)** A farmer feeds the animals 9/4 of a 52 kg sack each week. How many kilograms is that? **[2]**

**6.** (a) Write 0.28 as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 13/16 as a decimal, showing your method. **[3]**

**7.** Use division to write 7/12 as a decimal. Show that the decimal recurs and write your answer using the word "recurring". **[3]**

**8.** (Higher tier only)
**(a)** Write 0.151515... (15 recurring) as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that 0.41111... (1 recurring) = 37/90. **[3]**
**(c)** Write 7/33 as a recurring decimal. **[2]**

**9.** (a) Work out 12.7 − 4.86. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out −3.2 × 0.15. **[3]**
**(c)** Work out −0.6 − (−1.35). **[2]**

**10.** (a) Work out 0.84 ÷ 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out 5.76 ÷ 0.24. **[3]**
**(c)** Work out −1.8 ÷ 0.4. **[2]**

**11.** A roll of ribbon is 7.5 m long. A florist uses 3/5 of the roll for a banner.
**(a)** Find the length of the banner. **[2]**
**(b)** The rest of the ribbon is cut into pieces that are each 0.45 m long. How many whole pieces can be cut, and how much ribbon is left over? **[5]**

**12.** A water tank is 3/8 full. After 2 1/4 litres are added, it is 5/8 full.
**(a)** Work out the capacity of the tank. **[4]**
**(b)** Work out the volume of water in the tank now. Give your answer as a mixed number of litres. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) The highest common factor of 84 and 126 is 42. Dividing both by 42 (or by smaller factors in steps) [1] gives **2/3** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 6 × 8 + 5 = 53, so **53/8** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing in several small steps is fine if you finish at a fraction with no common factor left; stopping at 14/21 would leave the answer unsimplified.

**2.** The lowest common denominator is 36 [1]. 7/12 = 21/36 and 4/9 = 16/36, so the sum is 37/36 [1]. As a mixed number this is **1 1/36** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both converted fractions. If you slip on the final conversion, the earlier lines still show your method.

**3.** 4 1/2 = 9/2 and 2 7/8 = 23/8 [1]. Over eighths: 36/8 − 23/8 [1] = 13/8 = **1 5/8** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Converting to improper fractions avoids borrowing. Subtracting the whole numbers and the fractions separately gives 1/2 − 7/8, which is negative, and this is where many slips begin.

**4.** (a) Cancel the 7 with the 14 and the 5 with the 10: (1 × 1)/(2 × 2) [1] = **1/4** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** −2 1/4 = −9/4 and 1 1/2 = 3/2, so the calculation is −9/4 ÷ 3/2 [1]. Multiply by the reciprocal: −9/4 × 2/3 [1] = −18/12 = **−3/2** or **−1 1/2** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the mixed numbers before you do anything else, and keep the minus sign with the whole of −2 1/4, not just the 2.

**5.** (a) £8.10 ÷ 9 = £0.90 [1], then × 5 = **£4.50** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Use the same units: 1.2 kg = 1200 g [1]. The fraction is 450/1200 [1]. Dividing by 150 gives **3/8** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** 52 ÷ 4 = 13 [1], and 13 × 9 = **117 kg** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs both quantities in the same unit before you write the fraction. A comment that the answer in (c) is more than 52 kg shows you have checked it makes sense for a fraction greater than 1.

**6.** (a) 0.28 = 28/100 [1] = **7/25** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Divide 13 by 16: 130 ÷ 16 = 8 remainder 2, 20 ÷ 16 = 1 remainder 4, 40 ÷ 16 = 2 remainder 8, 80 ÷ 16 = 5 exactly [1]. Digits 8, 1, 2, 5 [1]. So 13/16 = **0.8125** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* If the question says "showing your method", the division layout is part of the answer. A decimal on its own is a risk if it is wrong.

**7.** Divide 7 by 12: 70 ÷ 12 = 5 remainder 10 [1]. 100 ÷ 12 = 8 remainder 4, then 40 ÷ 12 = 3 remainder 4 [1]. The remainder 4 repeats, so the 3 repeats forever and 7/12 = 0.58333..., which is **0.58 with the 3 recurring** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why it recurs: a remainder has come back. Writing out 0.58333 and stopping gives no evidence of the repeating pattern.

**8.** (Higher tier only)
**(a)** Let x = 0.151515.... Then 100x = 15.151515... [1]. Subtracting gives 99x = 15, so x = 15/99 = **5/33** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Let x = 0.41111.... Then 10x = 4.1111... and 100x = 41.1111... [1]. Subtracting: 90x = 37 [1], so x = **37/90** as required [1] **[3]**
**(c)** 7/33 = 21/99 [1], so the decimal is **0.212121... (21 recurring)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, write the result you are proving as your last line, and make sure the multiples of x you subtract have the same repeating part.

**9.** (a) Write 12.70 − 4.86 with the points lined up [1]. The answer is **7.84** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 32 × 15 = 480 [1]. Together the factors have 3 decimal places, so the size is 0.480 [1]. The signs are different, so the answer is **−0.48** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Subtracting a negative is adding: −0.6 + 1.35 [1] = **0.75** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the sign decision as its own line. In part (c), a common slip is to treat −(−1.35) as −1.35.

**10.** (a) 84 ÷ 7 = 12 [1], and 0.84 has two decimal places, so **0.12** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Multiply both numbers by 100 [1] to get 576 ÷ 24 [1] = **24** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Multiply both numbers by 10 to get −18 ÷ 4 [1] = **−4.5** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Scale both numbers by the same power of 10. Scaling only the divisor changes the answer.

**11.** (a) 7.5 ÷ 5 = 1.5 [1], and 1.5 × 3 = **4.5 m** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The rest is 7.5 − 4.5 = 3.0 m [1]. Pieces: 3.0 ÷ 0.45 = 300 ÷ 45 [1] = 6.66... [1], so **6 whole pieces** [1]. These use 6 × 0.45 = 2.7 m, so **0.3 m** is left over [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "how many whole pieces" question, round down and say so. Rounding 6.66... up to 7 would need more ribbon than the florist has.

**12.** (a) The extra water fills 5/8 − 3/8 = 2/8 = 1/4 of the tank [1]. So 2 1/4 = 9/4 litres is 1/4 of the capacity [1]. Capacity = 9/4 ÷ 1/4 = 9/4 × 4 [1] = **9 litres** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** 5/8 of 9 = 45/8 [1] = **5 5/8 litres** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the fraction that the added water represents before you try to find the capacity. This question is easy to start wrongly by finding 5/8 of 2 1/4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Combining fractions with different denominators before a common denominator is found, or adding the denominators too.
- Leaving a fraction unsimplified, or not giving a mixed number when the question asks for one.
- Multiplying or dividing mixed numbers without writing them as improper fractions first.
- Losing or misplacing a minus sign in a negative fraction or negative decimal.
- Writing one quantity as a fraction of another without matching units first.
- Misplacing the decimal point in a product. Count the decimal places in both factors.
- Multiplying the divisor by a power of 10 but forgetting to multiply the other number.
- Writing a recurring decimal as a truncated decimal, or putting the wrong number of 9s in the denominator.
- Rounding up when a question asks for the number of whole pieces.
- Giving a bare answer when the question says "show", with no working to support it.
