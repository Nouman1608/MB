These notes cover section 3.1.4.1, Hot desert landscapes, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Students study either hot desert landscapes or river landscapes in Section D. Key ideas: "Hot deserts are shaped by a number of physical processes"; "Distinctive landforms result from different physical processes in hot deserts"; "Development of hot desert environments creates opportunities and challenges." For full explanations, read the [hot desert landscapes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-hot-desert-landscapes/). For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

## Specification checklist

- **Wind processes:** erosion (abrasion, deflation), transport (surface creep, saltation, suspension), deposition.
- **Water processes:** erosion (abrasion, hydraulic power), transport (traction, saltation, suspension), deposition.
- **Landforms from wind:** yardangs, zeugen, dunes.
- **Landforms from water:** badlands, wadis, alluvial fans, salt lakes.
- **Opportunities and challenges** of development, with examples.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Abrasion | Wearing away by particles carried in wind or water |
| Deflation | Wind removing loose, fine material |
| Surface creep | Large grains rolled along the ground |
| Saltation | Sand-sized grains bouncing |
| Suspension | Fine particles carried in the air or water |
| Traction | Large stones rolled along a river bed |
| Hydraulic power | Force of moving water |
| Flash flood | Sudden, short flood after intense rain |
| Wadi | Dry desert valley that floods after rain |

## Wind processes in one table

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Abrasion | Sand-blasting; strongest near the ground; undercuts rocks |
| Deflation | Fine material lifted away; leaves stony desert pavement or hollows |
| Surface creep | Large grains pushed or rolled along |
| Saltation | Sand bounces in short hops near the ground |
| Suspension | Dust carried high, far and fast |
| Deposition | Wind loses energy (speed falls, obstacle), so it drops sand |

## Water processes in one table

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Abrasion | Stones and sand carried by a flood wear the bed and banks |
| Hydraulic power | Force of the water breaks and removes rock |
| Traction | Boulders rolled along the bed |
| Saltation | Sand and gravel bounce |
| Suspension | Silt and clay carried in the flow |
| Deposition | Flood loses energy as it spreads, sinks into the ground and evaporates |

Rainfall is rare but intense. The ground is hard and dry, so most of it runs off as a flash flood.

## Landforms made by the wind

| Landform | Description | Formation |
|---|---|---|
| Yardang | Long, streamlined ridge aligned with the prevailing wind | Abrasion carves soft rock; deflation removes debris; harder rock remains as ridges |
| Zeugen | Table-shaped rock with a hard cap on softer rock, up to tens of metres high | Weathering widens joints in the cap; abrasion cuts the soft base; the cap is left on a pedestal |
| Dune | Mound of sand with a gentle windward slope and steep slip face | Sand deposited around an obstacle; sand moves up the windward slope and slides down the slip face, so the dune migrates downwind |

Types of dune: **barchan** (crescent, horns point downwind), **seif/linear** (ridge parallel to wind), **star** (arms; changing winds).

## Landforms made by water

| Landform | Description | Formation |
|---|---|---|
| Wadi | Steep-sided, flat-floored valley, mostly dry | Cut by flash floods over many years |
| Alluvial fan | Fan-shaped deposit at the mouth of a valley | Flood leaves a narrow valley, spreads, slows, sinks in; drops load, with boulders first |
| Badlands | Maze of gullies and ridges in soft rock, almost no plants | Rapid runoff from storms erodes bare soft rock |
| Salt lake (playa) | Flat basin with salt crust | Temporary lake forms in a basin with no outlet; water evaporates and leaves salts |

## Development: opportunities and challenges

| Opportunities | Detail (Arabian Peninsula; check your own example) |
|---|---|
| Oil and minerals | Large oil reserves bring export earnings; also phosphates and salt |
| Energy | Clear skies and open land suit solar power; Dubai solar park |
| Farming | Irrigation from groundwater with centre-pivot or drip systems |
| Tourism | Winter sun, dunes, desert safaris, resorts, Dubai |

| Challenges | Detail |
|---|---|
| Extreme temperatures | Over 45°C in summer; dangerous for workers; high cooling costs |
| Water supply | Little rain; reliance on energy-hungry desalination or non-renewable groundwater |
| Inaccessibility | Sand and distance make roads, pipelines and power lines expensive |

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a barchan dune is formed and moves. [4 marks]

**Model answer:** "Sand is carried by the wind by saltation. When the wind slows, for example around a small obstacle, the sand is deposited and a mound builds up. The sand is pushed up the gentle windward slope and then slides down the steeper slip face on the sheltered side. Because sand is constantly moved from the windward side to the slip face, the whole dune migrates downwind. Where the sand supply is limited and the wind is steady, the dune takes a crescent shape with its horns pointing downwind."

How it scores: one mark each for deposition when wind slows, windward slope, slip face, and downwind movement or crescent shape.

## Quick recall: five ideas to remember

- Wind erodes by abrasion and deflation; it carries by creep, saltation and suspension.
- Water erodes by abrasion and hydraulic power; it carries by traction, saltation and suspension.
- Wind landforms: yardangs, zeugen, dunes. Water landforms: wadis, alluvial fans, badlands, salt lakes.
- Deposition happens when energy is lost.
- Development brings oil, solar power, farming and tourism, but also heat, water and access problems.

## Exam traps

- Water as well as wind shapes deserts.
- Abrasion is erosion; saltation is transport. Do not mix categories.
- Say **how** the landform forms, step by step.
- Deposition happens because of **loss of energy**.
- Dunes migrate **downwind**.
- Development has costs as well as benefits (for example groundwater use).
- Use named places for examples.

## Self-test

1. Define deflation.
2. Name the three ways wind transports material.
3. Why does a flash flood deposit its load?
4. Describe a yardang.
5. How does a zeugen form?
6. Describe the features of a barchan dune.
7. Explain how an alluvial fan forms.
8. How does a salt lake form?
9. Give **two** opportunities and **two** challenges of developing a hot desert.
10. Why is water supply a challenge in hot deserts?

**Answers**

1. Removal of loose, fine material from a surface by the wind.
2. Surface creep, saltation and suspension.
3. It loses energy as it spreads out, sinks into the dry ground and evaporates.
4. A long streamlined ridge of rock aligned with the prevailing wind, separated by grooves.
5. A hard cap rock overlies softer rock; weathering opens joints; the wind-blown sand cuts away the soft base; the cap is left on a pedestal.
6. Crescent shaped; gentle windward slope; steep slip face; horns pointing downwind.
7. A flash flood carries sediment out of a narrow valley onto an open plain; it spreads out, slows and sinks in; the load is dropped, with larger rocks first; over time deposits build up into a fan.
8. A temporary lake forms in a basin with no outlet; water evaporates and leaves a crust of salt.
9. Opportunities: any two from oil and minerals; solar energy; farming with irrigation; tourism. Challenges: any two from extreme heat; water supply; inaccessibility.
10. There is very little rain and few rivers, so people rely on desalination (high energy cost) or groundwater that recharges very slowly.
