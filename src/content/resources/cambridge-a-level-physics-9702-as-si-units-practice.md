## Section A

**1.** Which of the following is an SI base unit?

A: newton

B: coulomb

C: kelvin

D: joule **[1]**

**2.** Which expression gives the volt in SI base units?

A: kg m² s⁻²

B: kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹

C: kg m s⁻² A⁻¹

D: kg m² s⁻² A **[1]**

**3.** Express the unit of the spring constant, N m⁻¹, in SI base units. **[1]**

**4.** Express the coulomb in SI base units, and use your answer to explain why "A s" and "C" describe the same unit. **[2]**

**5.** Convert:

**(a)** 3.3 MΩ into ohms, in standard form. **[1]**

**(b)** 750 cm³ into m³, in standard form. **[1]**

**(c)** 8.9 g cm⁻³ into kg m⁻³. **[1]**

**6.** A student writes the mass of a trolley as 450 g and substitutes "450" into F = ma. Explain the error and state the value that should be substituted. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The period T of a mass m on a spring of spring constant k is given by T = 2π√(m/k).

**(a)** Show that this equation is homogeneous. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the check in (a) does not prove that the factor 2π is correct. **[1]**

**8.** A student proposes that the speed v of a wave on a stretched string depends on the tension F in the string and the mass per unit length μ (unit kg m⁻¹) through v = F/μ.

**(a)** Determine the base units of F/μ. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether the proposed equation can be correct. Give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest a change to the equation that makes it homogeneous. **[1]**

**9.** In one model, the drag force F on a small sphere moving slowly through a liquid is F = bv, where v is the speed of the sphere and b is a constant.

**(a)** Determine the SI base units of b. **[2]**

**(b)** A sphere moving at 6.0 mm s⁻¹ experiences a drag force of 1.5 mN. Calculate b, with its unit. **[2]**

**10.** The resistance R of a wire of length L and cross-sectional area A is R = ρL/A, where ρ is the resistivity of the material.

**(a)** Show that the unit of resistivity is Ω m. **[1]**

**(b)** Express Ω m in SI base units. **[3]**

**11.** The power P delivered to a resistor of resistance R carrying current I is P = I²R.

**(a)** Using the base units of the ohm, show that the equation is homogeneous. **[2]**

**(b)** A current of 250 mA passes through a 4.7 kΩ resistor. Calculate the power, converting both prefixes first. **[2]**

**12.** The equation for the kinetic energy of an object is sometimes misremembered as E = mv. Use base units to show that this cannot be correct, and state the base units of each side. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B**: V = W/Q = kg m² s⁻² ÷ A s [1]. **[1]**

**3.** N m⁻¹ = kg m s⁻² ÷ m = **kg s⁻²** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Q = It, so the coulomb is **A s** [1]. The coulomb is the name given to one ampere flowing for one second, so the two are identical; "C" is a shorthand name for "A s" [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** **3.3 × 10⁶ Ω** [1].

**(b)** 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, so 750 cm³ = **7.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³** [1].

**(c)** 8.9 × 10⁻³ kg ÷ 10⁻⁶ m³ = **8900 kg m⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The gram is not the SI base unit of mass; F = ma requires mass in kilograms for the force to be in newtons [1]. Substitute **0.450 kg** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Left side: [T] = s [1]. Right side: √(kg ÷ kg s⁻²) = √(s²) [1] = s, and 2π has no unit, so both sides are in s and the equation is homogeneous [1].

**(b)** 2π is a pure number with no unit, so base units cannot reveal whether it is correct [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** F/μ = kg m s⁻² ÷ kg m⁻¹ [1] = **m² s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** No: v has units m s⁻¹, but F/μ has m² s⁻², so the equation is not homogeneous [1].

**(c)** v = √(F/μ), since √(m² s⁻²) = m s⁻¹ [1]. *(The check shows this is homogeneous; it cannot confirm any numerical factor.)* **[4]**

**9. (a)** b = F/v [1], so [b] = kg m s⁻² ÷ m s⁻¹ = **kg s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** b = 1.5 × 10⁻³ N ÷ 6.0 × 10⁻³ m s⁻¹ [1] = **0.25 kg s⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** ρ = RA/L, so its unit is Ω × m² ÷ m = **Ω m** [1].

**(b)** Ω = V/A = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ ÷ A [1] = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² [1]. So Ω m = **kg m³ s⁻³ A⁻²** [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** [I²R] = A² × kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² [1] = kg m² s⁻³, which is the base unit form of the watt, the unit of P [1].

**(b)** I = 0.250 A and R = 4.7 × 10³ Ω [1]; P = 0.250² × 4.7 × 10³ = **290 W** (2.9 × 10² W) [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Left side: [E] = kg m² s⁻² [1]. Right side: [mv] = kg m s⁻¹, which does not match, so the equation is not homogeneous and cannot be correct [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a derived unit such as V or C inside an answer that asked for base units.
- Showing the base units of one side only in a "show that" question.
- Claiming that a homogeneous equation is proved correct.
- Converting cm³ or mm² with the factor for a single power of the prefix.
- Using grams, or a prefixed value, directly in an equation.
