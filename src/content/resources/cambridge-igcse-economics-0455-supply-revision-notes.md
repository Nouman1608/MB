## 2.4.1 Definition and diagram

- **Supply:** the willingness and ability of producers to sell a product at a given price over a given period of time.
- **Supply schedule:** a table of quantities supplied at different prices.
- **Supply curve:** price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis; slopes **up** from left to right.
- **Why it slopes up:** higher price means more profit per unit; covers the higher cost of producing extra units; attracts new sellers.

## 2.4.2 Movements along the curve

| Price change | Effect on quantity supplied | Term | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price rises | Quantity supplied rises | Extension in supply | Up the curve |
| Price falls | Quantity supplied falls | Contraction in supply | Down the curve |

Example: the price of eggs rises from 2.50 to 3.00 a dozen; quantity supplied rises from 800 to 1,000 dozen a week.

```
Extension = 1,000 - 800 = 200 dozen
Percentage rise in quantity = 200 / 800 x 100 = 25%
Percentage rise in price = 0.50 / 2.50 x 100 = 20%
```

## 2.4.3 Individual and market supply

- **Market supply** = the sum of every firm's quantity supplied at each price.
- Add quantities across, never prices.
- A firm that enters or leaves the market shifts market supply.

| Price | Firm J | Firm K | Market |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 40 | 25 | 65 |
| 8 | 55 | 40 | 95 |
| 10 | 70 | 60 | 130 |

## 2.4.4 Conditions of supply (shifts)

| Increase in supply (shift right) | Decrease in supply (shift left) |
|---|---|
| Lower costs of production (wages, materials, energy, rent) | Higher costs of production |
| Improved technology | Breakdown or loss of equipment |
| Indirect tax cut or removed | New or higher indirect tax |
| New or larger subsidy | Subsidy cut or removed |
| Good weather, no disease | Drought, flood, crop disease |
| More firms enter | Firms leave |
| Other products the firm could make become less profitable | Other products become more profitable |

### Taxes and subsidies on the diagram

- **Indirect tax per unit:** the price needed for each quantity rises by the tax; the curve moves left (up by the tax).
- **Subsidy per unit:** the price needed for each quantity falls by the subsidy; the curve moves right (down by the subsidy).

Example: a tax of 1 per unit; before the tax the firm supplied 300 units at 5 and 220 units at 4.

```
After the tax the firm needs 5 + 1 = 6 to supply 300
At a price of 5 it now supplies 220 (the old amount at 4)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Supply | Willingness and ability to sell at a given price over a given period |
| Quantity supplied | Amount offered at one particular price |
| Extension in supply | Rise in quantity supplied caused by a rise in the product's price |
| Contraction in supply | Fall in quantity supplied caused by a fall in the product's price |
| Increase in supply | More supplied at every price; shift right |
| Decrease in supply | Less supplied at every price; shift left |
| Indirect tax | A tax on spending, paid to the government by the seller |
| Subsidy | A payment by the government to producers |

## The movement or shift test

1. Own price changed? Movement: extension (up) or contraction (down).
2. Costs, technology, tax, subsidy, weather or number of firms changed? Shift.
3. More supplied at the same price? Increase. Less? Decrease.

## Exam traps

- A supply curve sloping down.
- A tax shown as a rightward shift.
- "Supply increases" after a price rise instead of "quantity supplied extends".
- Shifting supply because demand changed.
- Treating a subsidy as money paid to consumers.
- No labels for S1, S2 and the arrow.

## Quick self-test

1. Define supply.
2. Why does a supply curve slope upward? Give one reason.
3. The price of a product falls. Name the change and the direction on the curve.
4. Quantity supplied rises from 400 to 520 when the price rises. Calculate the percentage rise in quantity supplied.
5. Using the table above, what is market supply at a price of 8?
6. Firm K leaves the market. What is market supply at a price of 10?
7. What happens to supply when wages rise? Why?
8. A government gives a subsidy of 2 per unit. Which way does the supply curve shift?
9. Name two conditions of supply other than costs of production.
10. New technology raises supply by 15% at every price. The old quantity at a price of 20 was 600. Find the new quantity.
11. Why does an indirect tax reduce supply?
12. Farmers can grow wheat or barley. The price of barley rises. What happens to the supply of wheat?

### Answers

1. The willingness and ability of producers to sell a product at a given price over a given period of time.
2. Any one: more profit per unit; higher price covers rising costs of extra output; new sellers enter.
3. Contraction in supply; movement down the curve.
4. 120 / 400 x 100 = 30%.
5. 55 + 40 = 95.
6. 70.
7. It decreases (shifts left), because each unit costs more to produce, so less is supplied at every price.
8. To the right (an increase in supply).
9. Any two: technology, indirect taxes, subsidies, weather, number of firms, prices of other products.
10. 600 x 1.15 = 690.
11. It adds to the cost of each unit sold, so producers need a higher price to supply each quantity.
12. It decreases, because farmers switch land to the more profitable barley.
