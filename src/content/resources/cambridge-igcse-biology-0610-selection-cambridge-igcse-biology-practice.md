> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **18.3 Selection** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (adaptation, antibiotic resistance and the differences between natural and artificial selection); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [selection study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-selection-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [selection revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-selection-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement is **not** part of the syllabus description of natural selection?

A: organisms produce many offspring

B: individuals change their alleles to suit the environment

C: there is a struggle for survival, including competition for resources

D: better adapted individuals pass on their alleles to the next generation

**[1]**

**2.** Suggest **one** desirable feature that a breeder might select in:

**(a)** a crop plant such as maize **[1]**

**(b)** a domesticated animal such as a goat. **[1]**

**3.** A student writes: "Giraffes stretched their necks to reach high leaves, so their offspring were born with longer necks." Explain why this is not how natural selection works. **[2]**

**4.** State why selective breeding has to be carried out over many generations. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Describe what is meant by adaptation. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why using antibiotics only when essential can limit the development of resistant bacteria. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Snails of one species live in two habitats nearby. Their shells are either pale yellow or dark brown. Thrushes find snails by sight and eat them. In a grassland with pale dry grass, 70% of the snails are pale yellow. In a woodland with dark leaf litter, 75% of the snails are dark brown.

Use the idea of natural selection to explain the difference between the two populations. **[5]**

**8.** A plant breeder wants a strawberry variety that is resistant to a fungal disease. In the first generation, 15% of her plants survived infection. After five generations of selective breeding, 72% survived.

**(a)** Outline how the breeder carried out selective breeding to produce this improvement. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the breeder grew the plants where the fungus was present. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in survival from the first generation to the fifth. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A culture contained 2.0 × 10⁶ bacteria. An antibiotic was added. After 6 hours, 500 bacteria were alive. After 48 hours, there were 1.6 × 10⁶ bacteria, all resistant to the antibiotic.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the original bacteria that were alive after 6 hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the increase in the number of bacteria between 6 hours and 48 hours. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why this is an example of natural selection. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* All breeds of domestic dog are descended from wolves. Wolves live in the wild; dog breeds such as the greyhound were produced by humans.

Outline **three** differences between the natural selection that acts on wolves and the artificial selection that produced the greyhound. **[3]**

**11.** An insecticide was sprayed every year in a region to kill mosquitoes. At first it killed almost all of them. After several years it killed very few.

Explain how the population of mosquitoes changed so that the insecticide no longer killed most of them. **[4]**

**12.** Cabbage, broccoli and kale were all produced by selective breeding from one species of wild plant. Cabbage has a large tight head of leaves, broccoli has large clusters of flower buds, and kale has many large, loose leaves.

**(a)** Suggest which feature breeders selected to produce broccoli, and describe how they would have done this. **[3]**

**(b)** *(Extended)* Suggest why broccoli plants might survive less well in the wild than the wild plant. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Individuals cannot change their alleles. Natural selection changes how common alleles are in a population.

**2. (a)** Any one: higher yield / larger cobs; disease or pest resistance; drought tolerance; short, strong stems [1].

**(b)** Any one: higher milk yield; more meat / faster growth; calm temperament; disease resistance [1].

**3.** Changes acquired during life (a stretched neck) are not inherited / do not change the alleles [1]. Giraffes with longer necks, caused by their alleles, were more likely to survive and reproduce, passing on those alleles [1].

**4.** Not all offspring inherit the desirable alleles / features, so selection must be repeated until the offspring show them reliably [1].

**5.** A process, resulting from natural selection [1], by which populations become more suited to their environment over many generations [1].

**6.** Each time an antibiotic is used, non-resistant bacteria are killed and resistant bacteria are selected / survive and reproduce [1]; using antibiotics less often gives fewer chances for resistant bacteria to be selected and to spread [1].

**7.** Five from:

- there is variation in shell colour, caused by different alleles [1]
- snails produce many offspring / more than can survive [1]
- there is a struggle for survival / thrushes eat snails they can see [1]
- in grassland, pale snails are camouflaged, so they are less likely to be eaten; in woodland, dark snails are camouflaged [1]
- camouflaged snails are more likely to survive and reproduce [1]
- they pass on their alleles for shell colour, so the allele becomes more common in each habitat over many generations [1]

*Examiner insight:* An answer must explain both habitats. The camouflage point is the application mark; generic answers lose it.

**8. (a)** Select the plants that survive infection / show the most resistance [1]; cross these plants to produce seeds for the next generation [1]; select the resistant offspring and breed from them, repeating each generation [1].

**(b)** So only resistant plants survived / so she could identify which plants were resistant [1].

**(c)** (72 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 [1] = 380% [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c), divide by the original value, 15. Writing "57%" gives the difference in percentage points, not the percentage increase.

**9. (a)** 500 ÷ (2.0 × 10⁶) × 100 [1] = 0.025% [1].

**(b)** The surviving bacteria were resistant, due to a mutation [1]. Non-resistant bacteria had been killed, so the resistant bacteria had less competition [1]. They reproduced rapidly / asexually and passed the resistance allele to their offspring [1].

**(c)** The antibiotic (the environment) selected the resistant bacteria, which survived and reproduced while others died [1].

**10.** Any three matched differences:

- in natural selection the environment selects (predators, prey, climate), whereas in artificial selection humans choose which dogs breed [1]
- natural selection favours features that help survival and reproduction in the wild, whereas artificial selection favoured features humans wanted, such as speed for racing [1]
- natural selection is usually slower, whereas artificial selection is usually faster [1]
- natural selection produces populations suited to their environment, whereas artificial selection produced a breed suited to human needs [1]

*Examiner insight:* Each difference needs both halves. "Humans select greyhounds" with no reference to wolves is a statement, not a difference.

**11.** Any four from:

- there was variation in the mosquito population; a few were resistant due to a mutation / allele for resistance [1]
- the insecticide killed non-resistant mosquitoes [1]
- resistant mosquitoes survived and had less competition [1]
- they reproduced and passed on the resistance allele [1]
- over several years / generations, the resistant allele became more common, so most mosquitoes were resistant [1]

*Examiner insight:* This is natural selection in a new context. "The mosquitoes got used to the insecticide" scores nothing, because individuals do not become resistant during their lives.

**12. (a)** Large clusters of flower buds [1]. Select wild plants with the largest flower bud clusters and cross them [1]; select the offspring with the largest clusters and breed from them over many generations [1].

**(b)** It has been bred for features useful to humans, not features that help survival in the wild / it may lack features needed to compete with other plants [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing natural selection in general terms without using the organism in the question.
- Writing that organisms "get used to" a chemical.
- Stopping after "survive" without reproduction and passing on alleles.
- Giving the difference in percentage points instead of the percentage increase.
- Stating only one side of a difference.
