These questions cover section 1.2, Diffusion, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Use relative atomic masses: H = 1, He = 4, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, Br = 80. Revise first with the [diffusion study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-diffusion/) and the [diffusion revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-diffusion-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term *diffusion*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** properties or conditions that make one gas diffuse through air faster than another. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the relative molecular mass of each gas and state which diffuses fastest at the same temperature: helium (He), oxygen (O₂), carbon monoxide (CO). **[3]**

**4.** A drop of perfume is placed in one corner of a still room. Explain why the smell is eventually detected in the opposite corner, even though no air is moving. **[2]**

**5.** Nitrogen (N₂) and carbon monoxide (CO) are released at the same temperature. A student predicts that carbon monoxide will diffuse faster because it contains an oxygen atom. Explain whether the student is correct. **[2]**

**6.** State why diffusion is faster in a gas than in a liquid. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is placed at the bottom of a beaker of water. The beaker is not stirred.

**(a)** Describe what is seen after one hour. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain this observation in terms of particles. **[2]**

**(c)** The experiment is repeated in water at 60 °C. Predict and explain how the result differs. **[2]**

**8.** A glass tube 120 cm long is set up with cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution at one end and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other. Both ends are closed with bungs.

**(a)** Write the equation, with state symbols, for the reaction that forms the white ring. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular masses of the two gases. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain whether the white ring forms nearer the ammonia end or nearer the hydrochloric acid end. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the ring takes several minutes to appear. **[1]**

**9.** The experiment in question 8 is repeated with methylamine, CH₃NH₂, in place of ammonia.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of methylamine. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict how the position of the white ring changes compared with the ammonia experiment. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** Hydrogen bromide (HBr) and ammonia are released at opposite ends of a sealed tube. A white solid forms where they meet. Predict where in the tube the solid forms, and explain your answer using relative molecular masses. **[3]**

**11.** An unglazed porous pot full of air is connected to a U-tube containing water. A large beaker of helium is placed over the pot. The water level on the side of the U-tube connected to the pot falls.

**(a)** Explain why the water level moves. **[3]**

**(b)** After a long time, the water levels return to equal heights. Explain why. **[1]**

**12.** A gas jar of brown bromine vapour is placed below an inverted gas jar of air, and the cover between them is removed. Describe and explain, in terms of kinetic particle theory, what is observed over the next 30 minutes and what is happening to the particles. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration [1], as a result of the random motion of the particles [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A **lower relative molecular mass** [1]; a **higher temperature** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** He = **4**; O₂ = 2 × 16 = **32**; CO = 12 + 16 = **28** [1] for any two correct, [1] for all three. Helium diffuses fastest [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Perfume molecules evaporate and **move randomly**, colliding with air molecules [1]; there is a **net movement** from the corner (high concentration) to the rest of the room (low concentration) until they reach the opposite corner [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The student is **not correct**: both gases have **Mr = 28** [1], so at the same temperature their molecules move at the same average speed and they diffuse at the **same rate** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Gas particles are far apart and move faster, so they collide with other particles less often (either point) [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** The purple colour has **spread** through part of the water, darkest near the crystal [1]

**(b)** The crystal dissolves and its particles **move randomly** among the water molecules [1], giving a **net movement** from high concentration near the crystal to lower concentration elsewhere [1]

**(c)** The colour spreads **faster** [1], because particles have **more kinetic energy** and move faster at the higher temperature [1]

**[5]**

**8. (a)** NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s): correct formulae [1], correct state symbols [1]

**(b)** NH₃ = 14 + 3 = **17** [1]; HCl = 1 + 35.5 = **36.5** [1]

**(c)** Nearer the **hydrochloric acid** end [1]: ammonia has the lower Mr so it diffuses faster and travels **further** in the same time [1]

**(d)** The gas molecules **collide with air molecules** and keep changing direction, so their overall progress along the tube is slow [1]

**[7]**

**9. (a)** CH₃NH₂ = 12 + 3 + 14 + 2 = **31** [1]

**(b)** The ring forms **closer to the middle of the tube** (further from the hydrochloric acid end than before) [1]. Methylamine (31) is heavier than ammonia (17), so it diffuses more slowly, but it is still lighter than HCl (36.5), so the ring is still slightly nearer the hydrochloric acid end [1]

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the comparison has two parts: methylamine against ammonia (slower) and methylamine against hydrogen chloride (still faster).

**10.** Mr of HBr = 1 + 80 = **81**; Mr of NH₃ = **17** [1]. Ammonia is much lighter, so it diffuses **much faster** than hydrogen bromide [1]. The solid forms **near the hydrogen bromide end** [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Helium (Mr 4) **diffuses into** the pot through the pores [1] **faster** than the heavier air molecules (N₂ 28, O₂ 32) diffuse out [1], so there are more gas particles in the pot and the **pressure inside increases**, pushing the water down on that side [1]

**(b)** Eventually the gases inside and outside the pot have the **same composition** (helium and air evenly mixed), so there is no net movement and the pressures are equal [1]

**[4]**

**12.** One mark per point, up to 4:

- observation: the brown colour **spreads upwards** into the top jar [1]
- after some time the colour is **evenly** brown throughout both jars (paler than the original bromine) [1]
- bromine and air particles **move randomly** and collide, with a **net movement** of bromine from high to low concentration [1]
- once evenly mixed, particles are still moving but there is **no net movement**, so the colour stops changing [1]

**[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "from high to low" without the word concentration.
- Using Ar instead of Mr for diatomic molecules such as O₂ or Cl₂.
- Saying the white ring forms near the ammonia end because ammonia is "faster".
- Forgetting that a lighter gas still only wins if its Mr is lower than the other gas, not just lower than ammonia.
- Not linking the rise in pressure in the porous pot to more particles entering than leaving.
