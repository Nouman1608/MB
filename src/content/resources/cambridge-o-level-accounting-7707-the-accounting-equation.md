This guide covers section **1.2 The accounting equation** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The syllabus asks you to understand "assets, liabilities and owner's equity", "the accounting equation" and "how to apply the accounting equation". The examples follow one invented business, Marigold Tailoring, owned by Sunita Rao. Amounts are in dollars.

## 1. Assets, liabilities and owner's equity

**Assets** are resources owned by the business that it expects to benefit from. They are grouped as:

- **non-current assets**, kept for use over more than a year, such as premises, machinery, vehicles, fixtures and fittings
- **current assets**, expected to be used or turned into cash within a year, such as inventory, trade receivables, cash and bank balances

**Liabilities** are amounts the business owes to people other than the owner:

- **non-current liabilities**, due after more than a year, such as a long-term bank loan
- **current liabilities**, due within a year, such as trade payables and a bank overdraft

**Owner's equity** (also called capital) is the owner's claim on the business. It is what would be left for the owner if all the assets were turned into cash at their recorded values and all the liabilities paid.

### Classifying tricky items

| Item | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bank overdraft | Current liability | The business owes the bank |
| Trade receivables | Current asset | Customers owe the business |
| Trade payables | Current liability | The business owes suppliers |
| Inventory of fabric | Current asset | Will be used or sold within a year |
| Loan repayable in five years | Non-current liability | Due after more than a year |
| Sewing machines | Non-current asset | Used for several years |
| Money invested by the owner | Owner's equity | The owner's claim |
| Drawings | Reduce owner's equity | Taken out by the owner |

Under the business entity concept, the owner's private possessions are not assets of the business unless she brings them into it.

## 2. The accounting equation

**Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity**

The two sides are always equal because every asset has been provided by someone: either by outsiders (liabilities) or by the owner (equity). The equation can be rearranged to find any missing figure:

- **Owner's equity = Assets − Liabilities**
- **Liabilities = Assets − Owner's equity**

### Finding a missing figure

| Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity | Working |
|---|---|---|---|
| 38,000 | 9,500 | ? | 38,000 − 9,500 = **28,500** |
| ? | 6,200 | 21,800 | 6,200 + 21,800 = **28,000** |
| 52,400 | ? | 40,900 | 52,400 − 40,900 = **11,500** |

When a question lists individual items, total the assets and the liabilities first, taking care with items such as an overdraft.

## 3. Applying the equation to transactions

Every transaction changes at least two items, and the equation still balances afterwards. Sunita starts Marigold Tailoring on 1 May by paying 15,000 of her own money into a business bank account.

| | Transaction | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Opening: capital paid into bank | 15,000 | 0 | 15,000 |
| 1 | Buys sewing machines on credit from Harlow Supplies, 4,200 | +4,200 → 19,200 | +4,200 → 4,200 | 15,000 |
| 2 | Buys fabric for 1,800, paying by bank transfer | +1,800 −1,800 → 19,200 | 4,200 | 15,000 |
| 3 | Receives a five-year bank loan of 5,000 into the bank | +5,000 → 24,200 | +5,000 → 9,200 | 15,000 |
| 4 | Pays Harlow Supplies 2,500 | −2,500 → 21,700 | −2,500 → 6,700 | 15,000 |
| 5 | Sells fabric that cost 600 as finished garments for 1,000 cash | −600 +1,000 → 22,100 | 6,700 | +400 → 15,400 |
| 6 | Takes 300 cash for personal use | −300 → 21,800 | 6,700 | −300 → 15,100 |
| 7 | Pays rent of 450 by bank transfer | −450 → 21,350 | 6,700 | −450 → 14,650 |
| 8 | Brings her own laptop, worth 700, into the business | +700 → 22,050 | 6,700 | +700 → 15,350 |

At the end: assets 22,050 = liabilities 6,700 + owner's equity 15,350.

Check the assets: bank 15,000 − 1,800 + 5,000 − 2,500 − 450 = 15,250; cash 1,000 − 300 = 700; equipment 4,200 + 700 = 4,900; inventory 1,800 − 600 = 1,200. Total 22,050. Liabilities: Harlow 4,200 − 2,500 = 1,700 plus loan 5,000 = 6,700.

### The patterns behind the table

| Pattern | Example | Total assets | Totals on the right |
|---|---|---|---|
| Both sides increase | 1, 3, 8 | Up | Liabilities or equity up |
| Both sides decrease | 4, 6, 7 | Down | Liabilities or equity down |
| One asset replaces another | 2 | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Profit or loss | 5 (profit 400), 7 (expense 450) | Up or down | Equity up or down by the same amount |

A further pattern is one liability replacing another, for example if Sunita's supplier agreed to turn an amount owed into a loan. Total liabilities are unchanged.

The key idea: **profits increase owner's equity and expenses decrease it**, because they belong to the owner. Drawings also reduce equity, but they are not expenses.

## 4. Owner's equity over a period

The equation explains how equity changes between two dates:

**Closing equity = Opening equity + Capital introduced + Profit − Drawings**

(A loss is subtracted instead of profit being added.)

### Worked example: profit from two statements of financial position

At the start of a year Marigold Tailoring had assets of 41,000 and liabilities of 13,000. At the end it had assets of 47,500 and liabilities of 12,300. During the year Sunita introduced 2,000 more capital and took drawings of 9,600.

| Step | Working | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Opening equity | 41,000 − 13,000 | 28,000 |
| Closing equity | 47,500 − 12,300 | 35,200 |
| Increase in equity | 35,200 − 28,000 | 7,200 |
| Add back drawings | (they reduced equity) | + 9,600 |
| Remove capital introduced | (not earned by trading) | − 2,000 |
| Profit for the year | | **14,800** |

Check: 28,000 + 2,000 + 14,800 − 9,600 = 35,200. This method is used when full records have not been kept, which you will meet again under incomplete records.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a bank overdraft as an asset because it is called "bank".
- Counting the owner's private assets as business assets.
- Treating drawings as an expense: they reduce equity but not profit.
- Forgetting that a sale at a profit changes equity, not just assets.
- Adding capital introduced to profit instead of removing it when working profit out from equity.
- Writing "assets = liabilities − capital".

## Exam technique

- **State** the equation exactly: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity.
- **Calculate**: set out the working, for example total assets then total liabilities.
- **Explain** the effect of a transaction: name the items that change, by how much, and show the equation still balances.
- **Identify** an item as an asset, liability or equity: one word each, but be precise about current and non-current where asked.
