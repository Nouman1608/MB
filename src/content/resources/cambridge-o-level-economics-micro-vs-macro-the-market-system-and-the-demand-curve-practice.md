## Questions

**1.** Define demand. **[2]**

**2.** State whether each of the following is a microeconomic or a macroeconomic matter.

**(a)** A street vendor decides how many mangoes to stock. **[1]**
**(b)** The rate of unemployment in Country C falls over a year. **[1]**
**(c)** A household cuts its spending on fuel after the price rises. **[1]**

**3.** Explain how the price mechanism helps to answer the question "what to produce?". **[4]**

**4.** The table shows the weekly demand of three customers for pencils at a stationery stall. All figures are illustrative.

| Price (cu) | Customer 1 | Customer 2 | Customer 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 30 | 20 | 10 |
| 20 | 24 | 15 | 8 |
| 30 | 18 | 10 | 6 |
| 40 | 12 | 5 | 4 |
| 50 | 6 | 0 | 2 |

**(a)** Calculate market demand at each price. **[2]**
**(b)** State the relationship between price and quantity demanded shown by the table. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate Customer 2's share of market demand at a price of 30 cu, as a percentage to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** Use the market demand you found in question 4. The price of a pencil falls from 40 cu to 30 cu.

**(a)** State the change in quantity demanded, using the correct term. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in quantity demanded, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows the monthly demand for woollen scarves in Town C. The figures are illustrative.

| Price (cu) | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity demanded | 50 | 40 | 30 | 20 |

A mild winter reduces demand by 20% at every price.

**(a)** Calculate the new quantity demanded at each price. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this is a decrease in demand and not a contraction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a rise in the price of scarves would be shown on the original demand curve. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the market for fish in Village D. The figures are illustrative.

| Price (cu) | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity demanded (kg) | 90 | 75 | 60 | 45 | 30 |
| Quantity supplied (kg) | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |

**(a)** Identify the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** State the situation at a price of 9 cu and its size. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the market moves from 9 cu towards equilibrium. **[2]**

**8.** A farm harvests 1,000 kg of vegetables. Method 1 uses 8 workers and equipment hire of 100 cu. Method 2 uses 3 workers and equipment hire of 400 cu. All figures are per day and are illustrative.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per kg of each method when the daily wage is 50 cu. **[2]**
**(b)** The wage rises to 80 cu a day. Calculate the cost per kg of each method and state which the farm should use. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this shows the price mechanism answering the question "how to produce?". **[2]**

**9.** Explain how a market system decides "for whom" goods are produced. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows weekly demand for bicycles in Town E from two groups. The figures are illustrative.

| Price (cu) | 100 | 150 | 200 | 250 | 300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commuters | 40 | 32 | 24 | 16 | 8 |
| Students | 30 | 25 | 20 | 15 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate market demand at each price. **[2]**
**(b)** The price falls from 250 cu to 200 cu. Calculate the percentage change in quantity demanded, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** A new cycle lane raises demand by 10 bicycles at every price. State the new market demand and name the change. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how a rise in bus fares would affect the demand for bicycles. **[2]**
**(e)** Evaluate whether the price mechanism alone ensures that those who most need bicycles obtain them. **[2]**

**11.** A new fashion makes sports shoes popular.

**(a)** Explain the effect on the demand curve for sports shoes. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the price mechanism moves resources into sports shoe production. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Demand is the quantity of a good or service buyers are willing and able to buy [1] at each price in a given period of time [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Willing and able" is the core of the definition. Saying only "what people want" describes a want, not demand.

**2.** **(a)** Microeconomic: one decision maker in one market [1].
**(b)** Macroeconomic: it concerns the whole economy [1].
**(c)** Microeconomic: one household's choice [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "state" question needs little, but a short reason shows you are using scale, not the identity of the decision maker, as the test.

**3.** Higher demand for a good raises its price [1]. The higher price brings higher profit for producers [1]. Producers move resources into making more of that good [1]. Output of that good rises, and falling demand does the reverse, so resources move to goods people want [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For a four-mark explain question, link the steps in order. A list of separate facts about prices is weaker than the same facts joined into a chain.

**4.** **(a)** Add across each row [1]: 60, 47, 34, 21, 8 [1]. **(b)** As price rises, quantity demanded falls: an inverse relationship [1]. **(c)** At 30 cu: 10 ÷ 34 = 0.2941 [1], so **29.4%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum for at least one row so that your method can be seen. In part (c), the denominator is the market total (34), not Customer 2's own figure.

**5.** **(a)** The quantity rises from 21 to 34 [1], which is an extension of 13 pencils [1]. **(b)** 13 ÷ 21 × 100 [1] = **61.9%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the starting quantity as the base. Using 34 as the base gives 38.2%, which is a different and incorrect figure.

**6.** **(a)** Multiply by 0.8: 0.8 × each quantity [1]: **40, 32, 24, 16** [1]. **(b)** The cause is the weather, not the price of scarves [1], so the whole curve shifts left and buyers want less at every price [1]. **(c)** A price rise causes a movement up and to the left along the original curve [1], a contraction of demand [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the cause first. "The curve shifts because the cause is not the good's own price" is the kind of reasoning that makes a shift-versus-movement answer convincing.

**7.** **(a)** Equilibrium price **7 cu** [1] and quantity **60 kg** [1]. **(b)** At 9 cu, supply is 100 and demand is 30, so there is excess supply (a surplus) [1] of **70 kg** [1]. **(c)** Sellers have unsold stock and cut the price [1]. As the price falls, quantity demanded rises and quantity supplied falls until they are equal at 7 cu [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both the direction and the size in part (b). "Surplus" alone is incomplete without 70 kg.

**8.** **(a)** Method 1: 8 × 50 + 100 = 500 cu, so 500 ÷ 1,000 = 0.50 cu per kg [1]. Method 2: 3 × 50 + 400 = 550 cu, so 0.55 cu per kg [1]. **(b)** Method 1: 8 × 80 + 100 = 740, so 0.74 cu per kg. Method 2: 3 × 80 + 400 = 640, so 0.64 cu per kg [1]. The farm should use Method 2 [1]. **(c)** A higher wage makes labour-intensive production dearer [1], so the farm switches to the cheaper method, using more equipment and fewer workers: the price of a resource has decided how to produce [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the working for both methods. Stating only the cheaper method gives no evidence of how you reached it.

**9.** Goods go to those who are willing and able to pay the market price [1]. Ability to pay depends on income [1]. People with low incomes get fewer goods, so income decides who receives output, not need alone [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers link price to income, not just to "people who want it".

**10.** **(a)** Add across each row [1]: 70, 57, 44, 31, 18 [1]. **(b)** Quantity rises from 31 to 44 [1], so 13 ÷ 31 × 100 = **41.9%** [1]. **(c)** New demand: **80, 67, 54, 41, 28** [1]. It is an increase in demand (a shift right) [1]. **(d)** Buses are a substitute for bicycles [1], so more people switch to bicycles and demand shifts right [1]. **(e)** The price mechanism gives bicycles to those who can pay [1], so someone with a real need but low income may be left out, although those with the highest willingness to pay are served [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh both sides and reach a judgement. Saying "it depends on income" once, without a counter-point, is only half an evaluation.

**11.** **(a)** Tastes have changed, a condition of demand [1], so the demand curve shifts right [1]. **(b)** Demand rises and the price of sports shoes rises [1]. Profit rises for makers [1]. Firms and workers move into making more sports shoes [1], and output rises as resources move away from less profitable goods [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Open with the signal (price and profit) and end with the response (resources move). The response is the step that completes the explanation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining demand without "willing and able" or without a price and a period.
- Saying "demand rises" when the price of the good falls.
- Using the wrong base when working out a percentage change.
- Adding a column instead of a row when aggregating individual demand.
- Naming equilibrium without giving the matching price and quantity.
- Stating a surplus or shortage without its size.
- Explaining the price mechanism with no chain of signal, response and result.
- Treating a government decision as micro or macro by who makes it, not by scale.
- Writing "evaluate" answers with only one side.
