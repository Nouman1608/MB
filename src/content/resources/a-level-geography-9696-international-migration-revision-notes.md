Condensed recall for **5.3 International migration**: voluntary and forced movements and their impacts. For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| International migration | Movement across national borders |
| Economic migrant | Moves mainly for work or better income |
| Refugee | Person who has fled their country through fear of persecution, conflict or violence |
| Asylum seeker | Person who has applied for refugee protection and awaits a decision |
| Forced (involuntary) migration | Movement with little or no choice |
| Brain drain | Emigration of skilled workers |
| Remittances | Money sent to family at home |

## Causes and patterns

- **Voluntary / economic:** wage and job differences between countries, family links and networks, recruitment of workers. Typical flows run from LICs and MICs to HICs, but also between neighbouring developing countries.
- **Forced:** war, persecution, disaster. Most refugees stay in neighbouring countries rather than travelling far.
- Patterns are shaped by distance, history (former colonial links), language and immigration policy.

## Impacts

| | Source country | Destination country |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Remittances, reduced unemployment; loss of skills (brain drain) | Fills labour gaps, taxes, skills; possible wage pressure in some sectors |
| Demographic | Fewer young adults, imbalanced sex ratio | Younger age structure, higher fertility, population growth |
| Social | Families split; return of ideas and skills | Cultural diversity; possible tension, segregation |
| Services | Less pressure on jobs | Pressure on housing, schools, health |

## Exam traps

- Refugees and economic migrants are different categories; do not merge them.
- Impacts differ by group: workers, employers and local residents may view them differently.
- Remittances are a benefit but dependence on them carries risk.
- Use named streams for the 5.4 case study, not here.

## Self-test

1. Difference between a refugee and an asylum seeker?
2. What is brain drain?
3. Give two benefits to destination countries.
4. Why do remittances help source countries?
5. Where do most refugees go?
6. Give a negative effect on source countries.

**Answers:** 1. Refugee status is granted; an asylum seeker is awaiting a decision. 2. Emigration of skilled and educated workers. 3. Fills labour shortages, adds tax revenue, brings skills and diversity. 4. They raise household incomes and bring in foreign currency. 5. To neighbouring countries. 6. Loss of skills or an ageing, depleted workforce.
