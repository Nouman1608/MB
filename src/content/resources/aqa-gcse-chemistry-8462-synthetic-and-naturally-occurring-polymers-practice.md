These questions cover section **4.7.3 Synthetic and naturally occurring polymers (chemistry only)** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 in
the separate chemistry qualification. Questions 7, 8 and 9 test condensation polymerisation and amino acids,
which the specification marks (HT only), and are labelled **Higher tier only**.

Learn the content first in the
[synthetic and naturally occurring polymers study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-synthetic-and-naturally-occurring-polymers/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-synthetic-and-naturally-occurring-polymers-revision-notes/).

Relative atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, F = 19.

## Questions

**1.** Propene, CH₃CH=CH₂, is used to make poly(propene).

**(a)** Name the type of polymerisation. **[1]**
**(b)** State what happens to the carbon-carbon double bond. **[1]**
**(c)** Draw the repeating unit of poly(propene). **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the repeating unit contains the same atoms as the monomer. **[1]**

**2.** Part of a polymer chain is shown: –CH₂–CHCl–CH₂–CHCl–CH₂–CHCl–

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**
**(b)** Name and draw the monomer. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how you know this is an addition polymer. **[1]**

**3.** Tetrafluoroethene, CF₂=CF₂, polymerises to make poly(tetrafluoroethene).

**(a)** Write the equation for the polymerisation using n. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative formula mass of one molecule of the polymer made from 500 monomer units. **[2]**

**4.** A student states: "Poly(ethene) is a hydrocarbon with a C=C bond in every repeating unit."

**(a)** Identify the error. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain what happens to the C=C bond during polymerisation. **[1]**

**5.** Name the monomers from which each natural polymer is made.

**(a)** Proteins **[1]**
**(b)** DNA **[1]**
**(c)** Starch **[1]**

**6.** DNA is a polymer essential for life.

**(a)** Describe the structure of most DNA molecules. **[3]**
**(b)** State the function of DNA. **[1]**
**(c)** State one way in which cellulose differs from DNA in its monomers. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) A polyester is made from ethanediol, HO–CH₂CH₂–OH, and hexanedioic acid, HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH.

**(a)** Explain why these are described as monomers with two functional groups each. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the small molecule given off when the monomers join. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why this is a condensation polymer and not an addition polymer. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Glycine has the formula H₂N–CH₂–COOH.

**(a)** Name the two functional groups in glycine. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative formula mass of glycine, C₂H₅NO₂. **[1]**
**(c)** Two glycine molecules react to form a dipeptide and one molecule of water. Calculate the relative formula mass of the dipeptide. **[2]**
**(d)** A polypeptide is made from 100 glycine molecules. State how many water molecules are given off. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A protein contains several different amino acids.

**(a)** Explain how different amino acids can be joined into one polypeptide chain. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the repeating units in a polyester made from a diol and a diacid with those in a polypeptide made from glycine only. **[2]**

**10.** A company wants to make a plastic bottle from poly(ethene) rather than from a polyester.

**(a)** State what the alkene monomer is made from before polymerisation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how cracking supplies the monomer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Addition** polymerisation [1]
**(b)** It **opens / becomes a single bond** [1]
**(c)** Two carbons in a chain with a CH₃ side group on one: **–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–** with the extending bonds drawn through the brackets [structure correct 1; extending bonds shown 1] **[2]**
**(d)** **No other molecule is formed**, so no atoms are lost or added [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** **–CH₂–CHCl–** [1]
**(b)** **Chloroethene** [1]; CH₂=CHCl drawn with the C=C double bond [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The chain is made of **single-bonded carbon atoms** and the monomer has a C=C bond (no other product) [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** n CF₂=CF₂ [1] → ( –CF₂–CF₂– )ₙ [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Relative formula mass of CF₂=CF₂ (C₂F₄) = 24 + 76 = 100 [1]; 500 × 100 = **50 000** [1] (using F = 19). **[2]**

**4. (a)** Poly(ethene) has **no C=C** bond [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** It **opens** to form a single bond that joins to the next unit [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** **Amino acids** [1]
**(b)** **Nucleotides** [1]
**(c)** **Sugars** (glucose) [1]

**6. (a)** Two [1] polymer chains [1] twisted in a **double helix** [1], each made from four different monomers (nucleotides). **[3]**
**(b)** It **encodes the genetic instructions** for the development and functioning of living organisms [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Cellulose is made from **sugars (glucose)**, but DNA is made from four different **nucleotides** [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Each monomer has **two reactive groups** [1]: ethanediol has two –OH groups and hexanedioic acid has two –COOH groups, so each can link to two others to build a chain [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Water** [1]
**(c)** A small molecule (water) is **lost** when monomers join [1]; the monomers contain **functional groups, not C=C bonds** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **Amino group (–NH₂)** [1] and **carboxyl group (–COOH)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (2 × 12) + 5 + 14 + (2 × 16) = **75** [1]
**(c)** 2 × 75 = 150 [1]; 150 − 18 = **132** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** **99** [1]. In a chain of 100 units there are 99 peptide links, one water lost for each. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Number of links = number of units minus one.

**9. (a)** Amino acids each have an **amino group** and a **carboxyl group** [1]; the –COOH of one reacts with the –NH₂ of the next, losing **water**, in a condensation reaction, so different amino acids can follow each other in any order [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Polyester: the repeating unit contains **both** monomers (a diol part and a diacid part) joined by an **ester link** [1]. Polypeptide from glycine: one type of repeating unit (–NH–CH₂–CO–) joined by **peptide links** [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** **Crude oil** (a fraction from crude oil, such as a hydrocarbon fraction) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Cracking breaks down **large hydrocarbons** [1] into smaller alkanes and **alkenes such as ethene**, which is the monomer [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the C=C in the polymer diagram.
- Omitting brackets, bonds extending through the brackets, or the n.
- Forgetting the loss of water in condensation polymerisation.
- Giving the wrong number of water molecules lost (links are one fewer than units).
- Confusing nucleotides, amino acids and sugars as monomers.
