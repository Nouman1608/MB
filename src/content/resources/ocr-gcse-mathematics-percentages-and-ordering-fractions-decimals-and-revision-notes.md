## Key facts

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Percent | "parts per hundred": 1% = 1/100 = 0.01 |
| Percentage to decimal | Divide by 100 |
| Decimal to percentage | Multiply by 100 |
| Fraction to decimal | Numerator ÷ denominator |
| Part as a percentage of whole | part/whole × 100, same units |
| Increase by r% | Multiply by 1 + r/100 |
| Decrease by r% | Multiply by 1 − r/100 |
| Percentage change | (new − original)/original × 100 |
| Original value | New value ÷ multiplier |
| Simple interest | Same amount each year, on the original sum |

## 2.03a Conversion table to learn

| Percentage | As a decimal | As a fraction |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 0.2 | 1/5 |
| 12.5% | 0.125 | 1/8 |
| 75% | 0.75 | 3/4 |
| 33.3...% | 0.333... | 1/3 |
| 50% | 0.5 | 1/2 |
| 150% | 1.5 | 1 1/2 |
| 25% | 0.25 | 1/4 |

Method for any fraction: divide, then multiply by 100.

```
17/40 = 0.425 → 42.5%
0.07 = 7/100 → 7%
```

## 2.03b Percentage of a quantity

Build the answer from 10%, 5% and 1%.

```
24% of 150:
10% = 15, so 20% = 30
 1% = 1.5, so 4% = 6
24% = 30 + 6 = 36
```

One quantity as a percentage of another:

```
30 of 120 → 30/120 × 100 = 25%
```

Steps in order:

1. Put both amounts in the same unit.
2. Write part over whole.
3. Multiply by 100.

## 2.03c Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| +5% | 1.05 |
| +30% | 1.3 |
| +150% | 2.5 |
| −6% | 0.94 |
| −12% | 0.88 |
| +1/4 | 5/4 |
| −2/5 | 3/5 |

Fractional multipliers work the same way as decimal ones. A multiplier of 110/100 is the same as 1.1.

```
Increase £200 by 6%:  200 × 1.06 = £212
Decrease 60 kg by 15%: 60 × 0.85 = 51 kg
```

### Percentage change in steps

1. Work out the change: new − original.
2. Divide by the **original**.
3. Multiply by 100.
4. Write "increase" or "decrease".

```
150 → 135: change −15; −15/150 × 100 = −10%, a 10% decrease
```

### Original value in steps

1. Write the multiplier for the change.
2. Divide the new value by it.
3. Check by multiplying forwards.

```
After a 12% decrease a price is £74.80.
multiplier 0.88; original = 74.80 ÷ 0.88 = £85
Check: 85 × 0.88 = £74.80
```

### Simple interest in steps

1. Interest per year = amount × rate ÷ 100.
2. Multiply by the number of years.
3. Add to the starting amount if the question asks for the total.

```
£600 at 4% for 3 years:
per year = £24; 3 years = £72; total = £672
```

To find a missing rate or time, work backwards: total interest ÷ years gives interest per year, and that divided by the amount, times 100, gives the rate.

## 2.04a Ordering

1. Convert every value to a decimal.
2. Pad to the same number of decimal places.
3. Put negatives first, the most negative being the smallest.
4. Write the answer in the original forms.

```
0.4, 3/8, 38%, −1/2
0.400, 0.375, 0.380, −0.500
Smallest first: −1/2, 3/8, 38%, 0.4
```

For fractions only, a common denominator is a quick alternative: 2/3 and 5/8 become 16/24 and 15/24, so 2/3 is larger.

## Worked reminders

**Fraction and decimal to percentage in both directions.** 0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20. In reverse, 5/8 = 0.625 = 62.5%.

**Fractional multiplier.** A £36 ticket is reduced by 1/4. The multiplier is 3/4, so 36 × 3/4 = £27.

**Original value with a fraction.** A length is increased by 1/4 and is now 45 cm. The multiplier is 5/4, so the original is 45 ÷ 5/4 = 45 × 4/5 = 36 cm.

**Percentages above 100.** 130% of 40 is 1.3 × 40 = 52. A multiplier above 1 always gives a larger result.

**Two changes in a row.** Increase by 25%, then decrease by 20%: 1.25 × 0.8 = 1. The end value equals the start value, so the overall change is 0%. Equal percentages up and down do not cancel, but these two do, because 5/4 × 4/5 = 1.

**Missing time in simple interest.** £500 earns £60 at 2% per year. Interest per year is 500 × 2/100 = £10, so the time is 60 ÷ 10 = 6 years.

**Percentage profit.** An item bought for £40 is sold for £56. The profit is £16, so the percentage profit is 16/40 × 100 = 40%. Equivalently, 56 ÷ 40 = 1.4, a 40% increase.

**Shortcut check.** To check an answer to a 12.5% question, divide by 8. To check 75%, find 25% and multiply by 3.

## Shortcut percentages

Some percentages are quick to find by dividing. Learn these before the exam, using 96 as the quantity.

| Percentage | Same as | Of 96 |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | ÷ 2 | 48 |
| 25% | ÷ 4 | 24 |
| 20% | ÷ 5 | 19.2 |
| 12.5% | ÷ 8 | 12 |
| 10% | ÷ 10 | 9.6 |
| 1% | ÷ 100 | 0.96 |

Thirds do not give exact decimals. 1/3 is 0.333... and 33.3...%, and 2/3 is 0.666... and 66.6...%. Write the recurring digits or round sensibly, and say which you have done.

## Choosing a method

| If the question says | Use |
|---|---|
| "Find x% of" | Multiply by x/100, or build from 10%, 5%, 1% |
| "What percentage is a of b" | a/b × 100 |
| "Increase" or "decrease by x%" | Multiply by 1 + x/100 or 1 − x/100 |
| "Percentage profit", "percentage rise" | Change ÷ original × 100 |
| "Before the sale", "original price" | Divide by the multiplier |
| "Simple interest" | Amount × rate ÷ 100, times years |
| "Put in order" | Convert to decimals and compare |

## Paper facts

The specification shows Paper 2 on Foundation tier and Paper 5 on Higher tier as papers where a calculator is not permitted. Practise the 10%, 5% and 1% route, simple fractions to percentages, and decimal comparisons by hand. On any paper, write the multiplier or fraction you use, so your method is visible.

## Must-know distinctions

- Percentage of a quantity gives an **amount**. Percentage change gives a **rate**.
- Increase by 20% (multiply by 1.2) is different from "20% of" (multiply by 0.2).
- Percentage change divides by the **original**. A reverse percentage divides by the **multiplier**.
- Simple interest adds a fixed sum each year. Compound interest changes, and is taught under section 5.03a.
- Fraction answers stay as fractions in the original forms when ordering.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the final figure as the divisor in a percentage change, when the starting figure is needed.
- Writing 0.12 as the multiplier for a 12% decrease, when it should be 0.88.
- Multiplying, not dividing, in an original-value question.
- Leaving money as £74.8 instead of £74.80.
- Writing "15%" with no word for increase or decrease when the question asks for the change.
- Mixing minutes and hours in a percentage of another quantity.
- Calling 0.4% equal to 0.4.
- Putting −0.5 above −0.3 in an ordering question.
- Reading ≤ as strictly less than and leaving out an end value.
- Giving rounded decimals in an ordering answer instead of the original forms.
- Dropping working in a multi-step question, so a slip in the arithmetic leaves nothing to credit.
- Forgetting the units of the answer, such as £, kg or years.
