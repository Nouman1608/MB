These questions cover **3.2.5.2 Substitution reactions** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: exchange of water by ammonia and by chloride ligands, incomplete substitution, bidentate and multidentate ligands, haem and carbon monoxide, and the chelate effect. This is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3. The enthalpy and entropy values in Question 9 are invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** Explain why ammonia ligands can replace water ligands around Co²⁺ without a change in co-ordination number. **[2]**

**2.** Write an equation for the reaction of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess ammonia. **[1]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by *incomplete substitution*, using the reaction of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess ammonia as your example. **[2]**

**4.** Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to a solution containing [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺. Give the formula of the iron complex formed and its co-ordination number. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by a *multidentate* ligand and give the example named in the specification. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why carbon monoxide is toxic. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the substitution reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the colour change. **[1]**

**(c)** State the change in co-ordination number and in shape of the copper complex. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why adding a large volume of water to the final solution partly reverses the colour change. **[1]**

**8.** Ethanedioate ions, C₂O₄²⁻, react with [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ to replace all six water ligands.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the charge on the complex formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify the donor atoms in the ethanedioate ligand. **[1]**

**9.** Ethane-1,2-diamine (en) replaces ammonia around nickel(II):

[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃

Take ΔH = −12 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +185 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** State the number of particles on each side of the equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔG at 298 K. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why ΔH for this reaction is small. **[1]**

**(d)** State which term, ΔH or TΔS, mainly drives the reaction. **[1]**

**10.** EDTA⁴⁻ reacts with [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction and give the charge on the iron complex formed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of enthalpy and entropy, why this substitution takes place. **[3]**

**11.** Describe how haemoglobin carries oxygen in the blood, referring to the type of bond formed. **[2]**

**12.** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ reacts with concentrated hydrochloric acid to form [CoCl₄]²⁻. For each of the following, state whether it changes and, if so, how: the co-ordination number, the shape, the overall charge of the complex, and the oxidation state of cobalt. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** NH₃ and H₂O are similar in size [1]; and both are uncharged, so six NH₃ fit around the metal as six H₂O do [1]

**2.** **[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O** [1]

**3.** Only some of the ligands are replaced, even with excess of the new ligand [1]; with copper, four of the six water ligands are replaced, giving [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ [1]

**4.** **[FeCl₄]⁻** [1]; co-ordination number **4** [1]

**5.** A ligand that forms several (more than two) co-ordinate bonds to one metal ion [1]; **EDTA⁴⁻** [1]

**6.** CO replaces the oxygen that is co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II) in haemoglobin [1]; so haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen to the tissues [1]

**7. (a)** **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]

**(b)** Blue to yellow (passing through green) [1]

**(c)** Co-ordination number falls from **6 to 4** [1]; shape changes from **octahedral to tetrahedral** [1]

**(d)** The reaction is an equilibrium; adding water shifts the position to the left (towards [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺), so the blue colour returns [1]

*Examiner insight:* Refer to the equilibrium and the direction of shift. "The water dilutes the colour" does not explain a change of colour from yellow back to blue.

**8. (a)** **[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3C₂O₄²⁻ → [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]

**(b)** +3 + 3(−2) = **−3** [1]

**(c)** **Two oxygen atoms** (one at each end of the ion) [1]

**9. (a)** **4** on the left and **7** on the right [1]

**(b)** TΔS = 298 × 0.185 = 55.1 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]; ΔG = −12 − 55.1 = **−67.1 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** Six Ni–N co-ordinate bonds are broken and six similar Ni–N bonds are formed [1]

**(d)** **TΔS** (entropy) [1]

*Examiner insight:* Convert ΔS to kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ before combining it with ΔH; mixing J and kJ is the most common error.

**10. (a)** **[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Fe(EDTA)]⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]; charge **1−** [1]

**(b)** ΔH is close to zero, because six co-ordinate bonds of similar strength are broken and made [1]; the number of particles increases from 2 to 7, so ΔS is positive [1]; so ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is negative and the reaction is feasible [1]

*Examiner insight:* All three steps are needed. An answer that mentions entropy but not the near-zero enthalpy change, or does not link both to ΔG, will not gain full credit.

**11.** Oxygen forms a co-ordinate bond to the Fe(II) ion in haem [1]; the bond is formed in the lungs and broken in the tissues, where the oxygen is released [1]

**12.** Co-ordination number: changes from **6 to 4** [1]; shape: **octahedral to tetrahedral** [1]; charge: **2+ to 2−** [1]; oxidation state: **unchanged at +2** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving [Cu(NH₃)₆]²⁺ as the product with excess ammonia.
- Forgetting that the charge on the complex changes when Cl⁻ replaces H₂O.
- Saying the chelate effect is caused by stronger bonds.
- Counting particles without the released ligands.
