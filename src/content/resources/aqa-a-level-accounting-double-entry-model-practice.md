## Questions

**1.** State the book of prime entry for each of these items at Gorsedd Print.

**(a)** A credit note received from a supplier for faulty paper.
**(b)** An invoice sent to a customer for a credit sale of printing.
**(c)** A cheque counterfoil for payment of the water bill.
**(d)** A credit note sent to a customer for goods returned.
**(e)** An invoice for a guillotine bought on credit. **[5]**

**2.** The bank column of Tolbury Joinery's cash book shows a debit balance of 4,200. The bank statement shows items not yet in the cash book: a standing order of 150 for rent, a direct debit of 85 for insurance, a credit transfer of 940 from Ludgrove Ltd and a cheque for 310 from Okeby Stores returned unpaid.

**(a)** Calculate the updated bank balance. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the double entry for the dishonoured cheque. **[2]**
**(c)** State the effect on Okeby Stores' receivables ledger account. **[1]**

**3.** Nethercott Hardware sold goods to Ilkeston Ltd at list price 750 less 20% trade discount. Ilkeston paid promptly and took 5% cash discount. Nethercott paid a supplier's 800 invoice with 780.

**(a)** Calculate the invoice amount and the cash received from Ilkeston. **[2]**
**(b)** Show how Nethercott records the receipt in its three column cash book. **[2]**
**(c)** Name and treat the discount on the supplier payment, and state the combined effect of both discounts on profit. **[2]**

**4.** Okeby Stores owes Tolbury Joinery 2,150 in the receivables ledger. Tolbury owes Okeby 1,380 in the payables ledger. The balances are set against each other. Show the entries and the balance remaining. **[4]**

**5.** Classify each item as revenue expenditure, capital expenditure, revenue income or capital income.

**(a)** Replacing a warehouse floor with a stronger one that allows heavier racking.
**(b)** Mending a puncture on a delivery van.
**(c)** Rent from a tenant using spare office space.
**(d)** Cash from selling an old forklift truck.
**(e)** The annual road tax on a van. **[5]**

**6.** Tolbury Joinery (year end 31 March) bought a lathe for 42,000 on 1 April 2022: residual value 6,000, life six years, straight line. Four full years of depreciation were charged and it was sold on 31 March 2026 for 15,000.

**(a)** Calculate the accumulated depreciation at disposal. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the disposal account. **[3]**
**(c)** State where the balancing figure is shown. **[1]**

**7.** Fenwick Moor Haulage bought a van for 25,000 on 1 April 2023. A full year's depreciation is charged in the year of purchase.

**(a)** Using 30% reducing balance, calculate the charge and net book value at the end of each of the first three years. **[3]**
**(b)** Using straight line with a residual value of 4,000 over five years, calculate the annual charge and net book value after three years. **[2]**
**(c)** Show the van in the statement of financial position after year 3 under reducing balance. **[1]**

**8.** Gorsedd Print's year ends on 31 December.

**(a)** The rates account shows 500 prepaid at the start, 3,000 paid in the year and 750 prepaid at the end. Calculate the charge to the income statement and state the treatment on the statement of financial position. **[3]**
**(b)** Commission receivable: 200 was due at the start, 1,800 was received and 260 is due at the end. Calculate the income for the year. **[2]**
**(c)** State the classification of the closing commission due. **[1]**

**9.** Cobbold Traders (year end 31 December 2025) has receivables of 48,000 before writing off 2,000 owed by Ludgrove Ltd. The provision for doubtful debts is 5% of remaining receivables; the opening provision is 1,500. A debt of 300 written off earlier is paid in full.

**(a)** Show the write-off entries in the receivables ledger and general ledger. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the provision required and the income statement charge for it. **[2]**
**(c)** Show the entries for the recovery. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the net cost to profit of all three items. **[1]**

**10.** Wetherley Pet Supplies (year ended 31 March 2026) has this trial balance.

| | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory at 1 April 2025 | 8,400 | |
| Purchases and purchases returns | 61,500 | 1,900 |
| Sales and sales returns | 1,300 | 104,800 |
| Wages | 14,200 | |
| Rent and rates | 6,000 | |
| Insurance | 2,760 | |
| Electricity | 3,150 | |
| Equipment at cost; accumulated depreciation | 20,000 | 6,000 |
| Receivables; payables | 9,600 | 6,300 |
| Provision for doubtful debts | | 300 |
| Bank | 4,150 | |
| Drawings; capital | 11,000 | 22,760 |

Adjustments: closing inventory 9,700; electricity owing 450; insurance prepaid 690; depreciation at 20% reducing balance on the net book value of the equipment; provision for doubtful debts at 5% of receivables.

**(a)** Prepare the income statement. **[7]**
**(b)** Prepare the statement of financial position, with subheadings. **[5]**

**11.** Vexley Studios, a design business, has fees 74,500, commission receivable 3,200, wages 38,000, rent 9,600 and software 2,880. Adjustments: fees include 1,500 received in advance; commission due of 400 is unrecorded; wages owing 1,200; computer equipment costing 12,500 is depreciated straight line over five years, no residual value. Prepare the income statement and state how the two income adjustments appear on the statement of financial position. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Purchases returns journal. [1] (b) Sales journal. [1] (c) Three column cash book. [1] (d) Sales returns journal. [1] (e) General journal, because the guillotine is not goods for resale. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the exact book. An asset is not goods for resale, so the purchases journal is unlikely to earn credit for part (e).

**2. (a)** 4,200 - 150 - 85 [1] + 940 [1] - 310 = **4,595** debit [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Debit Okeby Stores' receivables ledger account 310 [1]; credit bank 310 [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The balance owed by Okeby increases by 310 because the debt is owed again. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The direction of a dishonoured cheque is a common slip, so name the account debited and the account credited.

**3. (a)** Invoice 750 x 80% = **600** [1]; cash 600 x 95% = **570** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Debit bank 570 and debit discount allowed 30 [1]; credit Ilkeston Ltd's account 600 [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Discount received 20, credited as income [1]. Discount allowed 30 is an expense, so profit falls by **10** overall (30 - 20) [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate the trade discount from the cash discount. The trade discount never reaches the ledger.

**4.** Debit Okeby in the payables ledger 1,380 [1]; credit Okeby in the receivables ledger 1,380 [1]; narrated in the general journal [1]; Okeby still owes Tolbury 2,150 - 1,380 = **770** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both ledgers; an answer touching one account is incomplete.

**5.** (a) Capital expenditure. [1] (b) Revenue expenditure. [1] (c) Revenue income. [1] (d) Capital income. [1] (e) Revenue expenditure. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A short reason, such as "improves the asset", shows the test you are applying.

**6. (a)** Annual charge (42,000 - 6,000) / 6 = 6,000 [1]; four years = **24,000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)**
```
Disposal of lathe
Dr                        Cr
Lathe at cost   42,000    Accumulated depreciation  24,000
                          Bank                      15,000
                          Loss on disposal           3,000
                42,000                              42,000
```
Cost on the debit side [1]; accumulated depreciation and proceeds on the credit side [1]; loss **3,000** (net book value 18,000 - 15,000) [1] **[3]**
**(c)** In the income statement as an expense. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show net book value as a check line to catch slips.

**7. (a)** Year 1: 7,500, NBV 17,500 [1]; year 2: 5,250, NBV 12,250 [1]; year 3: 3,675, NBV 8,575 [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Charge (25,000 - 4,000) / 5 = **4,200** [1]; NBV after three years 25,000 - 12,600 = **12,400** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Van at cost 25,000, less accumulated depreciation 16,425, net book value **8,575**, under non-current assets. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the percentage to net book value each year; using original cost is the likeliest slip.

**8. (a)** Charge 500 + 3,000 - 750 = **2,750** [1] in the income statement [1]; the 750 is a current asset (prepayment) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** 1,800 + 260 - 200 = **1,860** [1] receivable credited to the income statement [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Current asset (income due). [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Start from the cash figure and adjust it; show one line per adjustment.

**9. (a)** Debit irrecoverable debts 2,000; credit Ludgrove Ltd's receivables ledger account 2,000. [1]. The receivables ledger account is then closed and the expense is in the general ledger. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Receivables 46,000; provision 5% = **2,300** [1]; increase 2,300 - 1,500 = **800** charged [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Reinstate: debit the customer 300, credit irrecoverable debts recovered 300 [1]; debit bank 300, credit the customer 300 [1] **[2]**
**(d)** 2,000 + 800 - 300 = **2,500** reduction in profit [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Only the change in provision is an expense, not the full 2,300.

**10. (a)** Wetherley Pet Supplies, income statement for the year ended 31 March 2026

**[7]**
**(b)** Statement of financial position at 31 March 2026

**11.** Fees 74,500 - 1,500 = 73,000 [1]; commission 3,200 + 400 = 3,600 [1]; wages 38,000 + 1,200 = 39,200 [1]; depreciation 12,500 / 5 = 2,500 [1]; expenses 39,200 + 9,600 + 2,880 + 2,500 = 54,180 [1]; profit 76,600 - 54,180 = **22,420** [1]; income received in advance is a current liability and commission due is a current asset [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A service business has no cost of sales, so start with income and label each adjustment.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a ledger account instead of the book of prime entry.
- Entering trade discount in the books.
- Swapping discount allowed and discount received.
- Charging the full provision, not the change.
- Using cost, not net book value, for reducing balance.
- Treating a prepayment as a liability.
- Leaving out headings, totals or the net assets check.
