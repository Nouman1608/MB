These notes condense content item 2.1.2, Changes in business aims and objectives, in Topic 2.1 (Growing the business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [changing aims study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-changes-in-business-aims/) follows one bakery over five years; test yourself with the [changing aims practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-changes-in-business-aims-practice/). The wider [business growth and changing aims revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-business-growth-and-changing-aims-revision-notes/) also cover 2.1.1.

## What the specification lists

**Why** aims and objectives change as businesses evolve, in response to: market conditions, technology, performance, legislation, internal reasons.

**How** they change: focus on survival or growth; entering or exiting markets; growing or reducing the workforce; increasing or decreasing product range.

Memory aid (the writer's own) for the five reasons: **M**ost **T**eams **P**lan **L**ong **I**nnings: Market conditions, Technology, Performance, Legislation, Internal reasons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aim | A long-term goal of the business |
| Objective | A specific, measurable target that helps achieve an aim |
| Market conditions | The state of the market: competition, demand and customer tastes |
| Performance | How well the business is doing, shown by sales, profit, market share |
| Survival | Staying in business by covering costs |
| Growth | Increasing the size of the business, such as sales, outlets or staff |
| Entering a market | Starting to sell to a new group of customers or in a new area |
| Exiting a market | Stopping selling to a group of customers or in an area |
| Product range | All the products a business offers |
| Workforce | The people employed by the business |

## The five reasons in brief

| Reason | Typical triggers |
|---|---|
| Market conditions | New rivals; rising or falling demand; changing tastes |
| Technology | Online selling; automation; products made out of date |
| Performance | Profit rising or falling; objectives met early or missed |
| Legislation | New costs (for example, wages); restrictions or bans |
| Internal reasons | New owner or manager; owner retiring; lack of finance or skills |

## The four changes in brief

- **Survival ↔ growth:** weak performance or hard conditions push towards survival; strong performance towards growth.
- **Enter ↔ exit markets:** new opportunities lead to entry; losses or decline lead to exit.
- **Grow ↔ reduce workforce:** expansion needs staff; cost cutting or automation reduces them.
- **Increase ↔ decrease product range:** new customers need new products; weak products are dropped.

## Reason → change chains

- Rival cuts prices → sales fall → survival → drop low-margin products.
- Website launched → distant customers → enter new market → more packing staff.
- Profit up → finance for expansion → growth → new products.
- Legal costs rise → cost-cutting objective → fewer staff hours or fewer products.
- Owner retiring → less appetite for risk → stop expanding or exit a market.

## Calculations that show performance

- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- Market share (%) = business's sales ÷ total market sales × 100.
- Net profit margin (%) = net profit ÷ sales revenue × 100.

Example: market share falls from £0.6m of a £4m market (15%) to £0.5m of a £4.5m market (11.1%).

## Exam traps

- Give both the reason **and** the change.
- Aims can move towards survival as well as growth.
- A business can grow in one area and shrink in another at the same time.
- Use the figures in the source as evidence of performance.
- Name a specific internal reason, not just "internal factors".

## Sentence starters

These are the writer's suggestions, not specification wording.

- "Because its profit has fallen for two years, the business is likely to switch its focus from growth to survival..."
- "The new technology makes a wider market reachable, so the business may set an objective to enter..."
- "This change would mean reducing the workforce, which could lower costs but may also lower staff motivation..."

## Self-test

1. List the five reasons why aims and objectives change.
2. List the four ways aims and objectives can change.
3. Define the term product range.
4. Give one way technology could lead a business to enter a new market.
5. Give one reason a business might change its focus from growth to survival.
6. Give one internal reason for a change in objectives.
7. Why might a business reduce its product range?
8. A firm's profit falls from £80,000 to £56,000. Calculate the percentage fall.
9. A firm's staff rise from 25 to 30. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. A business sells £300,000 in a market worth £2.5 million. Calculate its market share.
11. How could a new law lead to a reduction in the workforce?
12. Explain why a business might exit one market while entering another.

### Answers

1. Market conditions, technology, performance, legislation, internal reasons.
2. Focus on survival or growth; entering or exiting markets; growing or reducing the workforce; increasing or decreasing product range.
3. All the products a business offers for sale.
4. Selling online allows it to reach customers outside its local area or overseas.
5. Any one: falling sales or profit; a new competitor; a cash shortage; a fall in demand.
6. Any one: a new owner or manager; the owner retiring; lack of finance or skills.
7. To drop products that sell poorly or cost too much, saving costs and focusing on best sellers.
8. Fall £24,000; £24,000 ÷ £80,000 × 100 = 30%.
9. Increase 5; 5 ÷ 25 × 100 = 20%.
10. £300,000 ÷ £2,500,000 × 100 = 12%.
11. It might raise wage or compliance costs, so the business sets an objective to cut costs by employing fewer staff.
12. One market may be declining or loss-making while another is growing, so moving resources from one to the other improves overall performance.
