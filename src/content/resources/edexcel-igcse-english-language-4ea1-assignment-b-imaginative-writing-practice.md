Assignment B is a coursework piece of about 700 words, marked out of 30 (AO4 18, AO5 12), whose
purpose is to narrate, describe or entertain. Section A of this page practises the redrafting skills
that coursework allows; Section B gives full tasks of the kinds the specification describes.

## Section A: redrafting drills

**1.** State whether each proposed task would be suitable for Assignment B, giving a reason.
(a) *Write a letter to your headteacher arguing for a longer lunch break.*
(b) *Write a story that ends with the words "and the lights went out."* **[2]**

**2.** Cut this sentence to no more than twelve words without losing its meaning or mood:
*"The old and ancient tree stood there very tall and very silent in the middle of the big, empty field,
not moving at all."* **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** weaknesses in this opening and rewrite it:
*"Hi, my name is Ella and this is the story of the worst day of my life. It all started when I woke up."*
**[4]**

**4.** The sentences below are all the same length and structure. Rewrite them as a passage with
deliberate variety.
*"The door opened. The man walked in. He sat down. He looked at me."* **[3]**

**5.** A student is writing a continuation of "The Story of an Hour" from Josephine's viewpoint. Explain
**one** detail they must check against the original, and why. **[2]**

**6.** Rewrite this paragraph ending so that it uses a telling detail instead of stating the emotion:
*"When I got home, I felt lonely."* **[2]**

**7.** Correct the punctuation and layout of this dialogue:
*"I can't" she whispered "not tonight" "Then when" he asked.* **[3]**

**8.** A student's draft is 1100 words long. Their teacher comments that the middle section loses focus.
Explain **two** steps the student could take, within the rules, to improve the piece. **[2]**

## Section B: full tasks

Each task is marked out of 30 (AO4 18, AO5 12). Aim for about 700 words.

**9.** After reading "Night", write a story narrated by someone who cannot sleep. Your purpose is to
narrate. **[30]**

**10.** "Nobody had opened that door for years." Write an imaginative piece that begins with these
words. **[30]**

**11.** Write a description of a harbour, moving through one day from dawn to night. Your purpose is to
describe. **[30]**

**12.** Write a piece with the title "The Last Train", real or imagined, whose purpose is to entertain.
**[30]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** (a) Unsuitable: its purpose is to argue, which is transactional writing, not narrating, describing
or entertaining (1). (b) Suitable: it is an imaginative narrative task with a given closing line, a type
the specification describes (1).

**2.** One mark for twelve words or fewer (1); one for keeping the key image and mood (1). Example: *"The
ancient tree stood motionless in the middle of the empty field."* (11 words.)

**3.** Two weaknesses, 1 mark each: the narrator introduces herself directly ("Hi, my name is"), which is
a flat, unliterary opening (1); the phrase "worst day of my life" is a cliché that tells rather than
shows, and "woke up" is a predictable starting point (1). Rewrite, up to 2 marks: an opening that hooks
through action, detail or voice (1) and avoids telling the reader how to feel (1). Example: *"By the time
Ella noticed the smoke, the bus had already left without her."*

**4.** Up to 3 marks for deliberate variety: combining some sentences (1), keeping one short sentence for
emphasis (1), and varying openings or structures (1). Example: *"The door opened and a man walked in,
shaking rain from his coat. He sat down without a word. Then, slowly, he looked at me."*

**5.** For example: what Josephine knows and when (1). She is outside the door and does not know
Louise's private thoughts, so a continuation from her viewpoint must not reveal knowledge she could not
have (1). Accept other valid details, such as the setting or the sequence of events at the end.

**6.** One mark for removing the stated emotion (1); one for a specific detail that implies it (1).
Example: *"When I got home, I set the table for two out of habit, and then put one plate back."*

**7.** One mark for speech marks and commas placed correctly for the first speaker (1); one for correct
punctuation of the question (1); one for starting a new line for the new speaker (1):
*"I can't," she whispered, "not tonight."*
*"Then when?" he asked.*

**8.** Any two (1 each): the student cuts or merges the unfocused middle paragraphs themselves; they
reread the task and plan the middle around one turning point; they check each paragraph has a clear
job. Changes must be made by the student and before marking.

### Section B: how to mark questions 9 to 12

Use the specification's AO4 grid (five levels, 18 marks) and AO5 grid (five levels, 12 marks) on a
best-fit basis.

| Task | What the stronger response does |
|---|---|
| 9 ("Night") | Uses the Anthology text as a springboard, not a template; creates its own narrator and situation; uses night-time setting and interior thought; controls a retrospective or present-tense voice; ends with a moment that changes the narrator's understanding |
| 10 (given opening) | Uses the opening line to create immediate mystery; builds tension through what is behind the door; paces the revelation; avoids an "it was all a dream" ending |
| 11 (harbour) | Keeps plot minimal; structures by time of day with clear paragraph shifts; uses sensory detail beyond sight; varies sentence rhythm to match activity and calm; uses imagery strategically, not in lists |
| 12 ("The Last Train") | Entertains through voice, humour or suspense; one focused situation; a deliberate, satisfying ending; dialogue used sparingly and punctuated accurately |

| AO | Common weakness to avoid |
|---|---|
| AO4 | Over-plotting; a voice that slips; an ending that simply stops |
| AO5 | Repetitive vocabulary; comma splices; inconsistent tense; unpunctuated dialogue |

**Upper-level features.** Communication that is "perceptive and subtle", a task "sharply focused on
purpose", vocabulary used "strategically", and punctuation that aids "emphasis and precision", as the
specification's top-level descriptors put it.
