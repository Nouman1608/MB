## 1.5.1 Business stakeholders at a glance

A stakeholder is anyone, or any group, affected by or concerned with a business's actions (standard meaning). The specification lists eight groups.

| Group | Wants (typical examples) | Can affect the business by |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders (owners) | Profit share, rising share value | Pressing for profit, changing managers |
| Employees | Pay, security, good conditions, training | Effort, quality of work, leaving, taking industrial action |
| Customers | Quality, fair price, safety, service | Buying or not buying; reviews |
| Managers | Salary, growth, secure post, freedom to decide | Setting direction and daily decisions |
| Suppliers | Regular orders, prompt payment | Prices, delivery times, stopping supply |
| Local community | Jobs, little noise or pollution | Objecting to plans, using or avoiding the business |
| Pressure groups | A change in business behaviour | Campaigns that shape public opinion |
| The government | Tax, jobs, law kept, economic health | Taxes and rules |

The specification does not fix a list of objectives, so treat the entries above as examples that you can adapt.

## Affected by, and impact on

Two separate directions. Keep them apart in an answer.

- **Affected by** the business: the business acts and the group feels the result. A late-night delivery schedule affects neighbours; late payment affects a supplier.
- **Impact on** the business: the group acts and the business feels the result. Customers leaving bad reviews cut sales; a supplier raising prices lifts costs.

### Method in steps for a stakeholder question

1. Name the group or groups in the case.
2. State what that group wants.
3. Link the business decision to that want.
4. Say who gains and who loses.
5. Add a consequence for the business, such as sales, costs or reputation.

## Conflicts between stakeholder groups

| Pair | Source of conflict |
|---|---|
| Shareholders vs employees | Wages come out of the profit that could be shared |
| Shareholders vs customers | Low prices or high quality cost money |
| Managers vs shareholders | Pay and growth plans against returning profit to owners |
| Local community vs owners | Expansion and long hours against quiet |
| Pressure groups vs managers | Costly changes demanded by a campaign |
| Suppliers vs the business | High prices against low costs |

A conflict answer needs two groups, two different wants and a reason they cannot both be fully met. Add a way to ease it: talks, a trial period, a phased change or a bonus.

## 1.5.2 Types of technology

| Type | What it is (standard meaning) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| E-commerce | Buying and selling over the internet | A pottery sold through its own website |
| Social media | Platforms for posting, talking and advertising | Short videos showing how a product is made |
| Digital communication | Electronic communication inside and outside the business | Email, video calls, messaging apps |
| Payment systems | Electronic ways of taking or making payments | Card readers, contactless, online payment |

## Influence on sales, costs and the marketing mix

| Area | Possible gains | Possible drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Wider market, orders at any hour, more awareness | Website faults lose sales; more rivals visible online |
| Costs | Less postage, printing and travel; smaller premises | Set-up, training, delivery, packaging, fees per sale |
| Product | Quick feedback; digital products | Comments can expose faults |
| Price | Lower selling costs allow lower prices | Easy price comparison forces prices down |
| Promotion | Cheap, targeted posts and email | Needs time to manage; a bad post spreads fast |
| Place | Website works as a place to buy | Delivery must be reliable |

Marketing mix: product, price, promotion, place.

### Small calculations you may need

Formulae are listed in the specification and are not provided, so learn the ones you use.

Revenue and total cost are given in Appendix 3. Profit and percentage change are not listed there; they are standard forms.

- Revenue = price × quantity.
- Total cost = total fixed costs + total variable costs.
- Profit = sales revenue − total costs (the standard form, not an Appendix 3 formula).
- Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100 (the standard form).

Reminder: a business sells 40 online orders at £25 each, so revenue is 40 × £25 = £1,000. A payment fee of 3% costs £30.

## Worked reminders

### A supplier raises its prices

Ashby Veg Boxes buys £5,000 of seed and packaging a year. Its supplier raises prices by 8%.

1. Extra cost = £5,000 × 0.08 = £400.
2. New annual cost = £5,000 + £400 = £5,400.
3. Link to the groups: the supplier gains income, the owners lose profit unless Ashby raises its prices, and customers may pay more.

This is an impact of a stakeholder on the business. The business then chooses how to respond.

### A social media campaign

Ashby spends £180 on social media adverts. They bring 36 extra orders at £15 each. Stock for these orders costs 60% of the sales revenue.

1. Extra sales revenue = 36 × £15 = £540.
2. Stock cost = £540 × 0.60 = £324.
3. Profit from the campaign = £540 − £324 − £180 = £36.

The campaign pays for itself, but only just. A fuller answer would add that new customers may buy again, and that the result depends on the 60% stock cost staying the same.

## Writing a developed answer

When a question says explain, give a statement of fact and two expansion points. For one that asks you to analyse, use a chain of reasoning placed in the business described:

- Point: the business has taken a particular step.
- Because: say what that does to a named group or to sales, costs or the marketing mix.
- Which means: say what follows for the business, such as profit, reputation or loyalty.

For an evaluate question, set out a point for and a point against, then give a decision that follows from the case and say what would change it. For a justify question, choose one of the two options and give a reason that uses the information provided.

## Must-know distinctions

- Affected by versus impact on.
- Social media (a platform for posting and talking) versus digital communication (passing information electronically).
- E-commerce (the buying and selling) versus payment systems (the way the money moves).
- A benefit to sales versus a benefit to costs: do not mix them.
- Command words as the specification describes them: explain means a statement plus two expansion points with no context; outline means two linked points placed in context; analyse expands a point in the context given; justify picks one of two options to recommend; evaluate ends in a supported conclusion.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the eight stakeholder groups in the specification.
2. Give one objective of employees and one of the government.
3. A bakery has a wage bill of £64,000 and gives a 9% rise. Calculate the new wage bill.
4. State one possible conflict between shareholders and customers.
5. Explain one way a supplier can impact a business.
6. Define e-commerce.
7. A café lets customers pay by tapping a phone on a reader. Which type of technology is this?
8. Sales revenue rises from £25,000 to £30,000. Calculate the percentage change.
9. A card reader charges 2% on card sales of £1,850. Calculate the fee.
10. A shop sells 120 items at £45 each with total costs of £3,100. Calculate the profit.
11. Name the four elements of the marketing mix and give one way technology influences place.

### Answers

1. Shareholders (owners), employees, customers, managers, suppliers, local community, pressure groups, the government.
2. Employees: for example fair pay or job security. Government: for example tax revenue or more jobs.
3. 64,000 × 1.09 = **£69,760**.
4. For example, customers want lower prices while shareholders want higher profit.
5. A supplier may raise prices, which lifts the business's costs and may force it to raise its own prices or accept lower profit.
6. Buying and selling goods or services over the internet.
7. Payment systems.
8. (30,000 − 25,000) ÷ 25,000 × 100 = **20%**.
9. 2% of £1,850 = **£37**.
10. Revenue = 120 × £45 = £5,400; profit = £5,400 − £3,100 = **£2,300**.
11. Product, price, promotion, place. A website lets customers buy without visiting a shop, so the website becomes a place of sale.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a group name without saying what it wants.
- Mixing up which direction an influence runs, or giving only one.
- Giving a conflict between two groups without saying why both cannot be satisfied.
- Describing technology in general without linking it to sales, costs or a marketing mix element.
- Forgetting that technology also adds costs.
- Giving points that fit any business instead of using the facts in the case.
- Calculating without showing working, or leaving out the unit (£ or %).
- Writing a list for an evaluate question and no supported conclusion.
