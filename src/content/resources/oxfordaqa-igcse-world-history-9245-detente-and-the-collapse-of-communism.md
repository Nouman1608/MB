**This guide covers the whole of the Section B option Détente and the collapse of communism, c1969-1991 in OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245).** The specification divides it into two parts: "The problem of maintaining détente" and "The end of the Cold War in the USSR, Central and Eastern Europe". Section B is assessed through a 3-mark knowledge question, a 6-mark question explaining one cause or consequence, a 9-mark question explaining two, and a 12-mark question that ends in a judgement. The [Paper 1 Section B study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-b/) explains the question types for all four options. Follow up with the [détente and the collapse of communism revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-detente-and-the-collapse-of-communism-revision-notes/) and the [détente and the collapse of communism practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-detente-and-the-collapse-of-communism-practice/).

## What the specification says

The option "focuses on détente and the difficulties of maintaining progress towards a more peaceful world, leading to the end of the Cold War in the USSR and Eastern Europe". It also considers "the global impact of these events, and the roles of key individuals and groups in shaping change".

## The problem of maintaining détente

**Détente** means a relaxing of tension. It is used for the easing of relations between East and West in the 1970s.

### Reasons for détente

- **The USSR.** The arms race was a heavy burden on an economy that was growing slowly. Brezhnev wanted trade and technology from the West and recognition of the frontiers in Europe. The USSR also feared China, with which it had a bitter quarrel and fought border clashes in 1969.
- **The USA.** After the shock of Vietnam, Nixon wanted to reduce the cost of arms and to avoid direct clashes. He also used the Sino-Soviet split, improving relations with China (his visit in 1972) to put pressure on the USSR.
- **China.** Fear of the USSR drew China towards the USA.
- **West Germany.** Chancellor Willy Brandt's policy of **Ostpolitik** sought to improve relations with the East. Treaties with the USSR and Poland (1970) and an agreement with East Germany (1972) reduced tension in the centre of Europe.

### Progress

- **ABM Treaty and SALT I (1972).** Nixon and Brezhnev met in Moscow in May 1972. The Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty limited each side's defensive missile systems, and the first Strategic Arms Limitation Talks produced an agreement to freeze the number of strategic missile launchers for five years. They did not end the arms race, because both sides could still add warheads to missiles, but they were the first arms control agreements of the nuclear age. Further **summits** followed in 1973 and 1974.
- **Helsinki Accords (1975).** Thirty-five states, including the USA, USSR and most European states, signed the Accords. They recognised the post-war frontiers in Europe, which the USSR wanted, and they promised respect for human rights, which the West wanted. Over time the human rights clauses encouraged dissidents in the Soviet bloc.

### The collapse of détente

**Afghanistan.** In December 1979 Soviet troops entered Afghanistan to support a communist government against Muslim guerrillas, the Mujahideen. The USSR feared the loss of a communist ally and the spread of Islamic movements to its southern republics. President Carter condemned the invasion. He asked the Senate not to ratify **SALT II**, which had been signed earlier in 1979, imposed a grain embargo, and led a boycott of the **1980 Moscow Olympics** by the USA and about sixty other countries. The USSR and its allies boycotted the Los Angeles Olympics of 1984. The USA also gave aid to the Mujahideen.

**Reagan and the renewal of the Cold War.** Ronald Reagan, elected in 1980, called the USSR an "evil empire" and increased defence spending. In 1983 he announced the **Strategic Defence Initiative (SDI)**, nicknamed "Star Wars", a space-based system to defend against nuclear missiles. The USSR saw it as a threat that could upset the balance. The two sides deployed new missiles in Europe: Soviet SS-20 missiles and, from 1983, American Pershing II and cruise missiles. Protests against the missiles took place in Western Europe.

### The re-emergence of détente

In 1985 **Mikhail Gorbachev** became Soviet leader. He needed to cut military costs to reform the economy. Reagan, who had been strongly anti-communist, came to trust Gorbachev. They met in Geneva (1985) and at **Reykjavik** (October 1986), where they came close to agreeing deep cuts in nuclear weapons, although they failed to agree over SDI. In December 1987 they signed the **INF Treaty**, which removed all intermediate-range missiles from Europe. In July 1991 the **START** treaty, signed by Gorbachev and Reagan's successor George H. W. Bush, reduced the number of strategic nuclear warheads. This was a second period of détente.

## The end of the Cold War in the USSR, Central and Eastern Europe

### Soviet retreat from Afghanistan

The war was costly and unpopular. The Soviet army could not defeat the guerrillas, who were supplied by the USA and Pakistan. About fifteen thousand Soviet soldiers died. The war drained the economy and damaged morale and the reputation of the USSR. Gorbachev withdrew the troops in 1988-89.

### Solidarity in Poland, 1980-81

In August 1980 strikes over food prices broke out in the Gdansk shipyard. Led by the electrician **Lech Walesa**, the strikers won the right to form an independent trade union, **Solidarity**, the first in the eastern bloc. It soon had about ten million members, supported by the Catholic Church and the Polish Pope John Paul II. It demanded political freedoms and worried the USSR. In December 1981 General Jaruzelski imposed martial law, banned Solidarity and arrested its leaders. The movement went underground, but it survived and returned in 1989.

### Gorbachev's reforms

Gorbachev introduced **glasnost** (openness), allowing criticism and free discussion, and **perestroika** (restructuring) of the economy, with elements of the market. Glasnost made the problems of the USSR public and encouraged demands for further change. In foreign policy Gorbachev gave up the Brezhnev Doctrine, which meant that the USSR would not use force to keep communist governments in power in Eastern Europe.

### The end of Soviet control in Eastern Europe

- **Poland.** In 1989 talks led to semi-free elections in which Solidarity won almost every seat it was allowed to contest. A non-communist government took office.
- **Hungary.** The communist party agreed to multi-party elections; in the summer the border with Austria was opened, and thousands of East Germans crossed to the West.
- **East Germany.** Mass demonstrations and the exodus of citizens forced the resignation of Honecker in October. On 9 November 1989 the **Berlin Wall was opened**. Germany was reunified in October 1990.
- **Czechoslovakia.** The **Velvet Revolution** in November 1989, led by Václav Havel, ended communist rule peacefully.

### The collapse of the USSR

Nationalism grew in the Soviet republics. In August 1991 hardliners attempted a coup against Gorbachev, which failed, and strengthened Boris Yeltsin, leader of the Russian republic. The republics declared independence. Gorbachev resigned on 25 December 1991 and the USSR ceased to exist. The Cold War was over.

**The effect on the USA.** The USA became the only superpower, with greater influence in the world, though it also faced the problem of defining its role without an opponent.

## Writing the answers

**Worked example: a 9-mark answer.**

*Explain two reasons why détente collapsed in the late 1970s.* **[9]**

> **First, the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979 destroyed trust.** The USSR sent troops to support a communist government. The USA saw this as expansion near the oil supplies of the Gulf. Carter withdrew SALT II from the Senate, banned grain exports and led a boycott of the Moscow Olympics, so cooperation ended.
>
> **Second, the arms race continued despite SALT I.** SALT limited launchers but not warheads, and the USSR deployed SS-20 missiles in Europe. The USA and NATO planned to deploy Pershing and cruise missiles, and Reagan, elected in 1980, increased spending and later proposed SDI. Both sides believed that the other was trying to gain an advantage.

**The 12-mark judgement.** For "Gorbachev was the main reason for the collapse of Soviet control in Eastern Europe", cover Gorbachev (glasnost, perestroika, the end of the Brezhnev Doctrine), then other factors (Solidarity, the cost of Afghanistan and the arms race, nationalism, Reagan's pressure, the economic weakness of the bloc). Compare, and reach a judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Treating détente as the end of the Cold War. It was a period of reduced tension.
- Saying SALT I ended the arms race. It limited launchers only.
- Mixing glasnost (openness) with perestroika (restructuring).
- Dating the opening of the Berlin Wall in 1991 (it was 9 November 1989).
- Forgetting that Solidarity was suppressed in 1981 and returned in 1989.
- Giving Reagan or Gorbachev all the credit without weighing other factors.
