These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 6.04, Algebraic inequalities. Questions 1 to 9 are for both tiers. Questions 10 to 13 use content that is **Higher tier only** and are labelled. All of them can be answered without a calculator. Show your working on every question.

Learn the methods first in the [algebraic inequalities study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-algebraic-inequalities/) or the [algebraic inequalities revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-algebraic-inequalities-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** A number line has an open circle at −1 and a filled circle at 4, joined by a line. Write down the inequality it shows for x. **[1]**

**(b)** The temperature t °C in a room must be no more than 18 °C. Write this as an inequality. **[1]**

**2.** Solve 7x − 4 ≤ 31. **[2]**

**3.** Solve 9 − 4x > 1. **[2]**

**4.** Solve (2x + 1)/5 ≥ x − 4. **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** Solve −7 ≤ 3x − 1 < 11. **[2]**

**(b)** List the integers that satisfy the inequality in part (a). **[1]**

**6.** n is an integer. 3n + 2 > 20 and 2n − 1 < 19. Find all the possible values of n. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A rectangle has width x cm and length (x + 5) cm.

**(a)** Show that the perimeter of the rectangle is (4x + 10) cm. **[1]**

**(b)** The perimeter is at most 50 cm. Write and solve an inequality in x. **[2]**

**(c)** The length is more than 12 cm, and x is a whole number. Find all the possible values of x. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Solve 3(x + 4) − 2 < 5(x − 2). **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how you would show your answer to part (a) on a number line. **[1]**

**9.** Leah's working to solve 6 − 2x < 10 is:

−2x < 4, so x < −2

**(a)** Explain the mistake Leah has made. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the correct solution. **[1]**

**(c)** Test the value x = 0 to show that Leah's answer cannot be right. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** **(a)** Solve x² − 5x − 14 ≤ 0. **[3]**

**(b)** How many integers satisfy this inequality? **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Solve 2x² + x > 6. Give your answer in set notation. **[4]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** **(a)** Solve x² < 3x. **[3]**

**(b)** Tom divides both sides by x and writes x < 3. Explain why his answer is not fully correct. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** A region R on a coordinate grid satisfies all three inequalities y ≥ 1, y < 2x and x + y ≤ 5.

**(a)** For each boundary line, state whether it should be drawn solid or dashed. **[2]**

**(b)** List all the points with integer coordinates that lie in R. **[3]**

**(c)** Is the point (1, 2) in R? Give a reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **−1 < x ≤ 4** [1]

**(b)** **t ≤ 18** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "No more than" includes the value itself, so use ≤. An open circle on a number line always gives a strict symbol.

**2.** 7x ≤ 35 [1]; **x ≤ 5** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer of x = 5 or just 5 loses the final mark. The solution is a set of values, so keep the symbol.

**3.** −4x > −8 [1]; dividing by −4 reverses the sign, so **x < 2** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Alternatively add 4x to both sides: 9 > 1 + 4x, so 8 > 4x and 2 > x. This route never divides by a negative.

**4.** 2x + 1 ≥ 5(x − 4) [1]; 2x + 1 ≥ 5x − 20, so 21 ≥ 3x [1]; **x ≤ 7** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Multiplying both sides by 5 is safe because 5 is positive. The whole right-hand side, both x and −4, must be multiplied.

**5. (a)** −6 ≤ 3x < 12 [1]; **−2 ≤ x < 4** [1]

**(b)** **−2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* −2 is included because of ≤, and 4 is excluded because of <. Check both ends separately.

**6.** 3n > 18, so n > 6 [1]; 2n < 20, so n < 10 [1]; **n = 7, 8 or 9** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Combine the two results into 6 < n < 10 before listing. Including 6 or 10 is the usual slip.

**7. (a)** P = x + (x + 5) + x + (x + 5) = 4x + 10 [1]

**(b)** 4x + 10 ≤ 50 [1]; **x ≤ 10** [1]

**(c)** x + 5 > 12 gives x > 7 [1]; so 7 < x ≤ 10 and **x = 8, 9 or 10** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, write every side of the perimeter before simplifying. A bare 4x + 10 does not show where it came from.

**8. (a)** 3x + 12 − 2 < 5x − 10 [1]; 3x + 10 < 5x − 10, so 20 < 2x [1]; **x > 10** [1]

**(b)** An **open circle at 10** with an **arrow to the right** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Collecting x on the right keeps the coefficient positive (2x), so no sign reversal is needed.

**9. (a)** Her first step is correct, but she did not **reverse the sign** when dividing both sides by −2 [1]

**(b)** −2x < 4 gives **x > −2** [1]

**(c)** x = 0: 6 − 0 = 6 and 6 < 10 is true, so 0 is a solution; but 0 < −2 is false, so Leah's answer excludes a value that works [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Testing one value inside your answer and one outside is a quick check on any inequality, and it catches a missing sign reversal straight away.

**10. (a)** (x − 7)(x + 2) ≤ 0 [1]; critical values 7 and −2 [1]; **−2 ≤ x ≤ 7** [1]

**(b)** −2 to 7 inclusive is **10 integers** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "≤ 0" means on or below the x-axis, which for a U-shaped curve is between the roots, so the answer is one interval.

**11.** 2x² + x − 6 > 0 [1]; (2x − 3)(x + 2) > 0, critical values 1.5 and −2 [1]; x < −2 or x > 1.5 [1]; **{x | x < −2} ∪ {x | x > 1.5}** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "> 0" means above the axis, outside the roots, so there are two pieces. Join them with ∪ in set notation, never as one double inequality.

**12. (a)** x² − 3x < 0 [1]; x(x − 3) < 0, critical values 0 and 3 [1]; **0 < x < 3** [1]

**(b)** x could be negative or zero, so dividing by x might reverse the sign or be impossible; his answer wrongly includes negative values such as x = −1, where 1 < −3 is false [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Never divide an inequality by an unknown. Move every term to one side and factorise instead.

**13. (a)** y = 1 and x + y = 5 are **solid** (≥ and ≤) [1]; y = 2x is **dashed** (<) [1]

**(b)** (1, 1), (2, 1), (2, 2), (2, 3), (3, 1), (3, 2), (4, 1): at least five correct points [1]; all seven correct points [1]; no incorrect points listed [1]

**(c)** **No**: 2 < 2 × 1 is false, because (1, 2) lies on the dashed line y = 2x [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Check every listed point in all three inequalities. A point on a dashed boundary, such as (1, 2) or (2, 4), is not in the region.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to reverse the sign after dividing by a negative number.
- Multiplying only part of one side when clearing a fraction.
- Giving a value instead of an inequality as the final answer.
- Including an excluded end point when listing integers.
- Writing a two-piece quadratic solution as a single double inequality.
- Dividing an inequality by x.
- Drawing a strict boundary as a solid line, or listing points that lie on a dashed line.
