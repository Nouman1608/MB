OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) is a two-stage
course for students who want to study physics in depth after their
International GCSEs, often with a view to engineering, physical sciences
or medicine at university. It is a modular qualification: the full
A-level is made up of five written papers, each lasting 120 minutes,
each marked out of 80 and each worth 20% of the A-level. Two papers
belong to the AS stage and three to the A-level stage.

## How the course is organised

The specification has thirteen numbered topics, five at AS and eight at
A-level.

**AS stage (topics 1-5)**

- **1 Measurements and their errors**: SI units and prefixes, the
  limitations of measurement, and estimating physical quantities. This
  is a toolkit topic that the rest of the course depends on.
- **2 Mechanics and materials**: vectors, moments, straight-line and
  projectile motion, Newton's laws, momentum, work, energy and power,
  conservation of energy, the bulk properties of solids and the Young
  modulus.
- **3 Particles, radiation and radioactivity**: the constituents of the
  atom, elementary particles and radioactivity.
- **4 Electricity**: the basics of current and charge, current-voltage
  characteristics, resistivity, circuits, the potential divider, and
  electromotive force with internal resistance.
- **5 Oscillations and waves**: oscillating systems and resonance,
  progressive, longitudinal and transverse waves, superposition and
  stationary waves, interference, diffraction, refraction, electron
  collisions with atoms, the photoelectric effect and wave-particle
  duality.

**A-level stage (topics 6-13)**

- **6 Circular and periodic motion** and **7 Gravitational fields and
  satellites** extend AS mechanics to rotation, simple harmonic motion,
  Newton's gravitational law, field strength, potential and orbits.
- **8 Electric fields and capacitance**, **9 Exponential change** and
  **10 Magnetic fields** form a connected block on fields: Coulomb's
  law, electric potential, capacitors and their charge and discharge,
  magnetic flux density, forces on moving charges, flux linkage,
  electromagnetic induction, alternating currents and the transformer.
- **11 Thermal physics** covers energy transfer by heating and doing
  work, conduction, ideal gases and kinetic theory.
- **12 Nuclear energy** covers the radius of the nucleus, mass and
  energy, induced fission, reactor safety and nuclear fusion.
- **13 Energy sources** covers rotational motion, wind and solar energy,
  and hydroelectric power with pumped storage.

## How it's assessed

| Stage | Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (of A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS | AS Paper 1 | Mechanics, Materials and Atoms | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS | AS Paper 2 | Electricity, Waves and Particles | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Paper 1 | Fields and Their Consequences | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Paper 2 | Energy and Energy Resources | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Paper 3 | Physics in Practice | 120 minutes | 80 | 20% |

All five papers are externally assessed written exams, and the course is
not tiered. Because the qualification is modular, the AS papers can be
sat at the end of the first year and the A-level papers at the end of
the second, rather than everything in one final series. Check with your
school how and when you will be entered.

Practical work is assessed in writing. The specification sets ten
required practicals, and your understanding of them is tested through
the exam papers rather than in a hands-on practical exam. A-level Paper
3, Physics in Practice, can draw on any part of the specification, so it
tests AS content as well as A-level content.

The specification was first taught in September 2019, and the first AS
and A-level exams were in May/June 2020.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles give a good guide to how the topics cluster: the AS
papers divide mechanics, materials and atoms from electricity, waves and
particles, while the A-level papers separate fields from energy. Ask
your teacher for the exact topic-to-paper mapping, since a few topics
feed more than one paper. Beyond that, several threads run through the
whole course:

- **Measurement and uncertainty** from topic 1 appears in every practical
  and in Physics in Practice, so it cannot be treated as a one-off
  introduction.
- **Mechanics underpins the fields topics.** Circular motion, gravitational
  orbits and the motion of charged particles in fields all reuse Newton's
  laws, energy and momentum from AS.
- **Electricity leads to capacitance and induction.** Potential
  difference, resistance and circuits at AS are the foundation for
  capacitors, exponential discharge and the transformer at A-level.
- **Particles connect to nuclear energy.** The AS work on atoms and
  radioactivity is extended in exponential decay and in the nuclear
  energy topic.
- **Mathematical skills** such as rearranging equations, using graphs and
  handling exponential relationships run across all thirteen topics.

## Planning your study

A natural order follows the topic numbers, because the specification is
written so that each block builds on earlier ones:

- **First year (AS):** start with measurements and errors, then work
  through mechanics and materials, followed by particles, electricity and
  waves. Carry out and write up the relevant required practicals as you
  go, and keep a record of the methods, measurements and uncertainties
  for each.
- **End of the first year:** consolidate the AS topics and practise
  exam-style questions on both AS papers.
- **Second year (A-level):** move through circular and periodic motion
  and gravitation, then the fields block of electric fields, exponential
  change and magnetic fields, and finish with thermal physics, nuclear
  energy and energy sources.
- **Final term:** revisit AS content alongside A-level content, since
  Physics in Practice can test the whole specification, and review every
  required practical in writing.

Start past-paper style practice on each stage as soon as its content is
taught, and use the course's exam preparation page for how to approach
the five papers and the written practical questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA A-Level Physics 9630?**
Five. There are two AS papers and three A-level papers, each lasting 120 minutes, marked out of 80 and worth 20% of the A-level.

**Is there a practical exam in OxfordAQA A-Level Physics?**
No. There are ten required practicals, but they are assessed through the written exam papers rather than in a hands-on exam.

**What does A-level Paper 3, Physics in Practice, cover?**
It can draw on any part of the specification, including AS content, so the whole course needs to stay fresh for it.

**Is 9630 linear or modular?**
It is modular: the AS papers and the A-level papers can be taken in different series, and all five papers are equally weighted.

**Which topics are AS and which are A-level?**
Topics 1-5 (measurements, mechanics and materials, particles, electricity, and oscillations and waves) are AS. Topics 6-13 (from circular motion to energy sources) are A-level.
