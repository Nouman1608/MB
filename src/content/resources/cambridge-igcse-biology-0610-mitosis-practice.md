## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** *(Extended)* Which statement describes mitosis? **[1]**

A: nuclear division giving rise to genetically different cells

B: nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells

C: nuclear division that halves the chromosome number

D: the fusion of two nuclei to form a zygote

**2.** *(Extended)* State when the exact replication of chromosomes takes place. **[1]**

**3.** *(Extended)* State **two** roles of mitosis in the human body. **[2]**

**4.** *(Extended)* A cell from a dog has 78 chromosomes. It divides by mitosis. State the number of chromosomes in each daughter cell. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Which description is correct for a stem cell? **[1]**

A: a specialised cell that divides by meiosis

B: a specialised cell that cannot divide

C: an unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to produce cells that can become specialised

D: an unspecialised cell that divides by meiosis to produce gametes

**6.** *(Extended)* A single cell divides by mitosis, and every new cell divides again, six times in total. Calculate the number of cells produced. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** *(Extended)* The table shows the quantity of DNA in the nucleus of a cell over ten hours. The cell divides by mitosis at the end of the period.

| Time / hours | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DNA / arbitrary units | 10 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 20 | 10 |

**(a)** Describe and explain the change in DNA quantity between 2 hours and 6 hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the DNA quantity per nucleus is 10 units at 10 hours. **[2]**

**(c)** The original cell had 46 chromosomes. State the number of chromosomes in each daughter cell. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A person cuts their arm. Over the next few days the skin heals.

**(a)** Name the role of mitosis shown here. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the new skin cells contain the same genetic information as the cells around the cut. **[3]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A patient's bone marrow is damaged, so it no longer makes blood cells. The patient is given stem cells from the bone marrow of a donor.

**(a)** Explain how the donor's stem cells can produce new red blood cells and white blood cells in the patient. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the new blood cells carry the donor's genes, not the patient's. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Each statement below is incorrect. Rewrite each one so that it is correct.

**(a)** During mitosis, the chromosome number is halved. **[1]**

**(b)** Chromosomes are copied during mitosis. **[1]**

**(c)** Stem cells are specialised cells that divide by mitosis. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A gardener takes cuttings from a rose plant with yellow flowers. Each cutting grows into a new plant.

**(a)** Name the type of nuclear division that produces the cells of the new plants. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why all the new plants have yellow flowers when grown in the same conditions. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* In one example, a yeast cell divides every 90 minutes by budding.

**(a)** Calculate the number of yeast cells produced from one cell after 9 hours, if none die. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** State how the genetic information of these yeast cells compares. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** **Before mitosis** [1]

**3.** Any two of: **growth**; **repair of damaged tissues**; **replacement of cells** [2].

*Examiner insight:* Asexual reproduction is also a role of mitosis, but it does not apply to the human body, so it does not score here.

**4.** **78** [1]

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** 2⁶ = **64** [1]

**7. (a)** The DNA quantity **doubles, from 10 to 20 units** [1], because the **chromosomes are replicated / copied exactly** before mitosis [1].

**(b)** During mitosis the **copies of the chromosomes separate** [1], so **each daughter nucleus receives one copy** of every chromosome, half the DNA of the replicated cell [1].

**(c)** **46** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The DNA quantity halves at division, but the chromosome number does not; writing 23 in (c) confuses mitosis with meiosis.

**8. (a)** **Repair of damaged tissues** [1]

**(b)** The new cells are made by **mitosis** [1]. **Before mitosis the chromosomes are replicated exactly** [1], and during mitosis the **copies separate**, so each daughter cell gets the **same chromosomes** as the parent cell [1].

**9. (a)** Stem cells are **unspecialised** [1] and **divide by mitosis** [1]; their daughter cells **become specialised** as red blood cells or white blood cells [1].

**(b)** The new cells come from the **donor's cells by mitosis**, which produces **genetically identical** cells [1].

**10. (a)** During mitosis, the chromosome number is **maintained / stays the same** [1].

**(b)** Chromosomes are copied **before** mitosis [1].

**(c)** Stem cells are **unspecialised** cells that divide by mitosis [1].

**11. (a)** **Mitosis** [1]

**(b)** The new plants are **genetically identical** to the parent plant [1], so they have the **same alleles / genes for flower colour** [1].

**12. (a)** 9 hours = 540 minutes; 540 ÷ 90 = **6 divisions** [1]; 2⁶ = **64 cells** [1].

**(b)** They are **genetically identical** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Convert hours to minutes before dividing; 9 ÷ 90 gives a meaningless answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing chromosome replication during mitosis.
- Halving the chromosome number in mitosis.
- Describing stem cells as specialised.
- Giving asexual reproduction as a role in the human body.
- Describing a DNA change without explaining it in terms of replication or separation of copies.
