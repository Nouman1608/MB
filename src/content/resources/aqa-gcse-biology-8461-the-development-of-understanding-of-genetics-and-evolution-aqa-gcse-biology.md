## Darwin's theory as a chain of reasoning

Darwin's proposal came from observations on a round-the-world expedition, backed by years of experimentation and
discussion, and linked to growing knowledge of geology and fossils. The specification states the theory in three
steps:

1. Individual organisms within a species show a **wide range of variation** for a characteristic.
2. Individuals with characteristics **most suited to the environment** are more likely to **survive to breed
   successfully**.
3. The characteristics that enabled these individuals to survive are then **passed on to the next generation**.

Today we add the genetic explanation Darwin did not have: variation arises partly from **mutations** that create
new alleles, and the characteristics are passed on as **alleles in genes**. Over many generations the useful
allele becomes more common in the population.

**Annotated model answer**

*Question: A species of sea snail lives on pale sand. Most snails have pale shells, but a few have dark shells.
After a volcanic eruption the shore is covered in dark rock. Fifty years later most snails have dark shells.
Explain how this change happened by natural selection. [4]*

> There was variation in shell colour, caused by different alleles, some of which may have arisen by mutation [1].
> On the dark rock, birds could see pale snails more easily, so dark snails were more likely to survive [1].
> The surviving dark snails bred and passed on the allele for dark shells to their offspring [1].
> Over many generations the proportion of snails with the dark-shell allele increased [1].

The marks follow the three steps above plus "many generations". An answer that says the snails "turned dark to
hide" describes change during a lifetime, which is Lamarck's idea, and scores nothing for the mechanism.

## Why acceptance was gradual

Darwin published *On the Origin of Species* in **1859**. The specification gives three reasons it was slow to be
accepted. Learn each one with the reason behind it:

- **It challenged the idea that God made all the animals and plants that live on Earth**, a belief widely held
  at the time.
- **There was insufficient evidence** at the time to convince many scientists. The fossil record had large gaps
  and no one could watch a species change.
- **The mechanism of inheritance and variation was not known** until 50 years after the theory was published.
  Darwin could not explain how characteristics were passed on.

## Lamarck: the contrasting idea

Jean-Baptiste Lamarck's theory was based mainly on the idea that **changes that occur in an organism during its
lifetime can be inherited**. Under his idea, a weightlifter's large muscles would be passed to their children. We
now know that in the vast majority of cases this type of inheritance cannot occur: changes to body cells during
life do not change the alleles in the gametes.

Darwin's variation is already present at birth; Lamarck's is produced by use or disuse during life.

## Wallace and speciation

**Alfred Russel Wallace** independently proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection. He published
**joint writings with Darwin in 1858**, which prompted Darwin to publish *On the Origin of Species* the following
year. Wallace worked worldwide gathering evidence for evolutionary theory and is best known for his work on
**warning colouration** in animals (bright colours that signal to predators that an animal is harmful or
unpleasant to eat) and his **theory of speciation**.

A **species** is a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring. The steps that give rise
to a new species are:

1. A population becomes **isolated**, for example split by a new river, a mountain range or the sea.
2. Each group contains **genetic variation**, and new mutations arise separately in each.
3. The **conditions differ** in each area, so **natural selection** favours different alleles in each group.
4. Over a **long time**, the groups become so different that they **can no longer interbreed to produce
   fertile offspring**. They are now separate species.

Step 4 is the definition point. Writing "they look different" does not show speciation; the test is fertile
offspring.

## From Mendel's units to DNA

The specification describes a timeline. Each step depended on the one before:

| When | Development |
|---|---|
| Mid-19th century | **Gregor Mendel** carried out breeding experiments on plants. He observed that the inheritance of each characteristic is determined by "units" passed on to descendants **unchanged**. |
| Late 19th century | The behaviour of chromosomes during cell division was observed. |
| Early 20th century | Chromosomes and Mendel's units were seen to behave in similar ways, so the units, now called **genes**, were proposed to be located on chromosomes. |
| Mid-20th century | The **structure of DNA** was determined and the mechanism of gene function worked out. |

This work by many scientists led to the **gene theory**. Why was Mendel's importance not recognised until after
his death? When he published, chromosomes had not been observed and genes and DNA were unknown, so there was
no physical structure to explain his units. Few scientists read or understood his work, which used counting and
ratios in a way unusual for biology at the time.

## Evidence for evolution and fossils

The theory is now widely accepted because characteristics have been shown to pass to offspring in **genes**, and
because of the **fossil record** and the knowledge of how **antibiotic resistance** evolves in bacteria.

**Fossils** are the remains of organisms from millions of years ago, found in rocks. They may form:

- from parts of organisms that have **not decayed** because one or more conditions needed for decay are absent;
- when parts of the organism are **replaced by minerals** as they decay;
- as **preserved traces**, such as footprints, burrows and rootlet traces.

Many early forms of life were **soft-bodied**, so they left few traces, and what traces there were have mainly
been destroyed by **geological activity**. This is why scientists cannot be certain how life began on Earth.

**Worked example 1: reading fossil data** (invented figures)

| Age of rock layer (millions of years) | 500 | 400 | 300 | 250 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genera of a marine animal group found | 12 | 35 | 48 | 9 | 20 |

```
fall from 300 to 250 million years: 48 − 9 = 39 genera
percentage fall: 39 ÷ 48 × 100 = 81% (2 s.f.)
```

A good interpretation: the group diversified, then most genera became extinct over a short period, then the group
partly recovered. A good evaluation adds that the record is incomplete, so some genera may have existed but left
no fossils.

## Extinction

**Extinction** occurs when there are no remaining individuals of a species still alive. Factors that may
contribute include new diseases, new predators, new competitors, environmental change such as a changing
climate, habitat destruction, and catastrophic events such as a volcanic eruption. When describing a factor, link it to deaths or failure to breed.

## Resistant bacteria

Bacteria can evolve rapidly because they **reproduce at a fast rate**.

**Worked example 2.** One resistant bacterium divides every 20 minutes. How many resistant bacteria could there
be after 4 hours?

```
number of divisions = (4 × 60) ÷ 20 = 12
number of bacteria = 2^12 = 4096
```

The sequence the specification describes: **mutations** of bacterial pathogens produce new strains; some strains
might be **resistant** to antibiotics and so are not killed; they **survive and reproduce**, so the population of
the resistant strain rises; the strain then **spreads** because people are not immune to it and there is no
effective treatment. **MRSA** is resistant to antibiotics.

To reduce the rate of development of resistant strains:

- doctors should **not prescribe antibiotics inappropriately**, such as for non-serious or viral infections;
- patients should **complete their course** so all bacteria are killed and none survive to mutate and form
  resistant strains;
- the **agricultural use** of antibiotics should be restricted.

The development of new antibiotics is **costly and slow**, so it is unlikely to keep up with new resistant strains.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that the antibiotic **causes** the mutation. The mutation happens at random; the antibiotic selects.
- Saying organisms "adapt" or "decide" to change. Populations change because individuals with useful alleles
  survive and breed.
- Giving "they look different" as evidence of a new species instead of "cannot produce fertile offspring".
- Mixing up the dates: joint writings with Wallace in **1858**, *On the Origin of Species* in **1859**.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Describe** the steps of speciation: give the four steps in order, ending with fertile offspring.
- **Explain** natural selection: variation, selection pressure, survival and breeding, alleles passed on, many
  generations.
- **Suggest** why an idea was slowly accepted: link each reason to the evidence or beliefs of the time.
- **Evaluate** a claim from fossil data: describe the pattern, use a figure, then state a limitation.
