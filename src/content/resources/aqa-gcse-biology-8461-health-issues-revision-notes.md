These are condensed recall notes for **4.2.2.5 Health issues** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, Version 1.0, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. It is Paper 1 content at both tiers; nothing here is marked (HT only). Worked explanations are in the [health issues study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-health-issues/); questions are in the [health issues practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-health-issues-practice/). All figures below are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Health | "The state of physical and mental well-being" (specification wording) |
| Disease | A condition that harms the body or mind; a major cause of ill health |
| Communicable disease | Caused by a pathogen; can spread between people |
| Non-communicable disease | Not caused by a pathogen; cannot spread between people |
| Incidence | Number of new cases in a population over a stated time |
| Rate | Cases per set number of people (for example per 1000 or per 100 000) |
| Correlation | A link between two variables shown by a pattern in data |
| Epidemiology | The study of patterns of disease in populations |
| Sample | The group of people actually studied, chosen to represent a population |
| Bias | A systematic distortion, for example from a sample that is not representative |

## The relationship between health and disease

- Disease is a major cause of ill health, but not the only one.
- Other factors named in the specification: **diet**, **stress**, **life situations** (income, housing, access to healthcare). They affect physical **and** mental health.
- A person can be free of disease and still in poor health.

## Four interactions to learn as chains

1. **Immune defects → infections**: fewer working white blood cells → pathogens not destroyed → infection.
2. **Viruses → cancers**: virus living inside cells changes them → uncontrolled division → tumour.
3. **Pathogen-triggered immune reactions → allergies**: immune system later over-reacts → skin rashes, asthma.
4. **Severe physical ill health → mental illness**: pain, disability, loss of work or independence → depression.

Applying a chain to a new case: a patient taking drugs that suppress the immune system after a transplant keeps catching chest infections. This is interaction 1: the immune system destroys fewer pathogens.

## Data skills

**Rates**

```
rate per 1000 = cases / population x 1000
expected cases = (population / 1000) x rate per 1000
percentage = part / whole x 100
```

Mini example: 18 cases in a village of 1500. Rate = 18 ÷ 1500 × 1000 = 12 per 1000.

**Choosing a chart**

| Data | Chart |
|---|---|
| Separate categories (countries, types of disease) | Bar chart, gaps between bars |
| Continuous data in classes (age, mass) | Histogram, no gaps |
| Two measured variables per person or place | Scatter diagram |
| Raw values to be grouped | Frequency table first |

Frequency table checks: classes do not overlap ("0 to under 20", "20 to under 40"); frequencies add up to the total.

Mini example: ages 3, 8, 14, 21, 27 and 35 give a frequency of 3 for "0 to under 20" and 3 for "20 to under 40"; total 6, matching the six values.

**Describing a scatter diagram**

- Direction: positive, negative or none.
- Strength: strong (points close to a line) or weak (spread out).
- Meaning: a link, not proof of cause.

## Principles of sampling

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Large enough? | A few unusual people distort small samples |
| Representative? | Must match the population (age, sex, background) |
| Random? | Avoids researchers choosing people in a biased way |
| Repeated? | Agreement between independent studies raises confidence |

Classic biased samples: one school, one gym, one hospital ward, one day of the year, volunteers only.

## Exam traps

- Health includes **mental** well-being.
- Comparing raw numbers of cases between places of different size: use rates.
- Histograms have no gaps; bar charts do.
- Correlation is not causation.
- "Describe the relationship" needs both: disease causes ill health, and other factors do too.
- Name the interaction **and** explain the link.
- When evaluating a sample, refer to the details given (who, how many, where, when).

## Self-test

1. Give the specification's definition of health.
2. Name three factors other than disease that affect health.
3. Explain why a person with a defective immune system is more likely to catch infections.
4. Give an example of a disease interaction involving a virus.
5. What two types of condition can immune reactions first caused by a pathogen trigger?
6. A town of 40 000 people has 120 new cases in a year. Calculate the rate per 1000.
7. A chart shows 6 cases per 1000 per year. How many cases are expected in a city of 300 000?
8. Which chart would you use to show cases of a disease in five different countries?
9. Which chart would you use to show cases by age group?
10. Points on a scatter diagram fall from left to right and lie close to a line. Describe the correlation.
11. Give two features of a good sample in an epidemiological study.
12. A survey of 30 volunteers at one hospital is used to judge health in a whole country. Give two weaknesses.

### Answers

1. The state of physical and mental well-being.
2. Diet, stress, life situations.
3. Fewer pathogens are destroyed by white blood cells, so pathogens can reproduce and cause disease.
4. A virus living in cells triggering a cancer (for example HPV and cervical cancer).
5. Allergies, such as skin rashes and asthma.
6. 120 ÷ 40 000 × 1000 = 3 per 1000.
7. (300 000 ÷ 1000) × 6 = 1800 cases.
8. A bar chart.
9. A histogram (age is continuous).
10. A strong negative correlation.
11. Any two: large, representative, randomly chosen, repeated by other studies.
12. Any two: too small; not random (volunteers); not representative (hospital patients are already ill; one place only).
