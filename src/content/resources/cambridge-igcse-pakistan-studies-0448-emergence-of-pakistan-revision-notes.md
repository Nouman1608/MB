**These notes condense Section 2 of Paper 1, The emergence of Pakistan 1906–47, in Cambridge IGCSE
Pakistan Studies 0448.** The section is examined through Key Questions 6 to 10. Learn the timeline
first, then the reasons that link one event to the next, because the 7- and 14-mark parts reward
explanation rather than narrative. The full account is in the
[Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47 study guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-emergence-of-pakistan-1906-47/);
practise with the [Emergence of Pakistan practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-pakistan-studies-0448-emergence-of-pakistan-practice/).

**0448 Paper 1, Section 2 (2026 syllabus)**

## Timeline

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1905 | Partition of Bengal | Muslim-majority province welcomed by Muslims; Hindu agitation followed |
| 1906 | Simla Deputation (October); Muslim League founded at Dhaka (December) | Demand for separate electorates; a Muslim political party |
| 1909 | Morley–Minto Reforms | Separate electorates granted |
| 1911 | Partition of Bengal reversed | Muslims felt let down by the British |
| 1916 | Lucknow Pact | Congress accepted separate electorates; high point of co-operation |
| 1919 | Rowlatt Act; Amritsar Massacre; Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms | Repression and disappointing reform |
| 1919–24 | Khilafat Movement; Hijrat (1920); Non-Cooperation called off (1922); caliphate abolished (1924) | Brief unity, then lasting distrust |
| 1927 | Delhi Proposals; Simon Commission appointed | Muslim offer on electorates; an all-British commission |
| 1928 | Nehru Report | Rejected separate electorates; alienated Muslims |
| 1929 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points | Clear statement of Muslim demands |
| 1930 | Allama Iqbal's Allahabad address | A consolidated north-western Muslim state |
| 1930–32 | Round Table Conferences | No agreement on minorities |
| 1932 | Communal Award | Separate electorates kept |
| 1933 | Rahmat Ali's *Now or Never* | The name "Pakistan" |
| 1935 | Government of India Act | Provincial autonomy |
| 1937–39 | Elections; Congress rule | Muslims alienated; League revived |
| 1939 | Day of Deliverance (22 December) | Celebrating the end of Congress ministries |
| 1940 | Pakistan (Lahore) Resolution, 23 March | Demand for independent states |
| 1942 | Cripps Mission; Quit India Movement | Congress leaders jailed; League strengthened |
| 1944 | Gandhi–Jinnah talks | No agreement |
| 1945 | Simla Conference | Failed over who would nominate Muslim members |
| 1945–46 | Elections | League won the Muslim seats |
| 1946 | Cabinet Mission Plan; Direct Action Day (16 August) | Last plan for unity; violence |
| 1947 | 3 June Plan; Indian Independence Act; Radcliffe Award | Partition and independence |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Separate electorates | Muslims voting for Muslim candidates in reserved seats |
| Weightage | More seats than population share, to protect a minority |
| Dyarchy | Split of provincial subjects between elected ministers and British officials (1919) |
| Communalism | Loyalty to one's religious community above others, leading to tension |
| Khilafat | The caliphate of the Ottoman Sultan |
| Hijrat | Migration for religious reasons, here to Afghanistan in 1920 |
| Provincial autonomy | Elected provincial governments running most provincial subjects (1935) |
| Day of Deliverance | 22 December 1939, marking the resignation of Congress ministries |
| Grouping | The Cabinet Mission's plan to group provinces into three sections |
| Boundary award | The Radcliffe decision on the borders in Punjab and Bengal |

## Chains of cause and effect

- **Bengal reversal (1911) → Muslim distrust of British promises → greater reliance on the League.**
- **Nehru Report (1928) → Muslims convinced Congress would not protect them → Fourteen Points (1929).**
- **League's poor 1937 results → Congress rule without the League → Muslim grievances → League
  membership grows → Lahore Resolution (1940).**
- **Quit India (1942) → Congress leaders imprisoned → League free to organise → strong results in
  1945–46.**
- **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) → Nehru's remarks about grouping → League withdraws acceptance →
  Direct Action Day → violence → partition accepted as the only solution (1947).**

## Key Question 10 in brief

- **Jinnah:** Congress member and "ambassador of Hindu–Muslim unity" (Lucknow Pact, 1916); left
  Congress after Non-Cooperation; Fourteen Points; reorganised the League after the mid-1930s;
  negotiated with the British and Congress; led the League to victory in 1945–46.
- **Allama Iqbal:** philosophical case for Muslim nationhood; Allahabad address (1930); letters
  encouraging Jinnah to lead; died in 1938.
- **Rahmat Ali:** *Now or Never* (1933) and the name "Pakistan"; demanded a fully separate state
  rather than a Muslim state within a federation; had little influence on League policy.

## Exam traps

- The Simla Deputation (1906) and the Simla Conference (1945) are different events.
- The Nehru Report (1928) came before the Fourteen Points (1929), which answered it.
- Iqbal did not coin the name "Pakistan"; Rahmat Ali did.
- The Cripps Mission (1942) is not the Cabinet Mission (1946).
- Direct Action Day was called by the League after it withdrew from the Cabinet Mission Plan.
- In 14-mark answers on "how successful", balance success and failure for the period named only.

## Self-test

1. Who led the Simla Deputation in 1906?
2. Where was the Muslim League founded?
3. What did the Lucknow Pact give the Muslims?
4. What was the Hijrat Movement?
5. Why did Gandhi call off Non-Cooperation in 1922?
6. What did the Delhi Proposals offer?
7. What was the Communal Award?
8. Why did Jinnah call for a Day of Deliverance?
9. Who moved the Lahore Resolution?
10. Why did the Simla Conference of 1945 fail?
11. Name the three members of the Cabinet Mission.
12. What did Rahmat Ali want that differed from Iqbal's idea?

### Answers

1. The Aga Khan. 2. Dhaka, December 1906. 3. Congress accepted separate electorates and one-third
of the seats in the central legislature for Muslims. 4. The migration of Muslims to Afghanistan in
1920, on the view that India was no longer a place where they could freely practise Islam;
Afghanistan closed its border and many returned impoverished. 5. Violence at Chauri Chaura, where
police were killed. 6. Muslims would accept joint electorates in return for one-third of central
seats, the separation of Sindh from Bombay, reforms in the North-West Frontier Province and
Balochistan, and representation by population in Punjab and Bengal. 7. A British decision of 1932
keeping separate electorates for minorities. 8. To mark the resignation of the Congress ministries
in December 1939. 9. A. K. Fazlul Haq. 10. Congress rejected the League's claim to nominate all the
Muslim members of the proposed council. 11. Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V.
Alexander. 12. A completely separate homeland rather than a Muslim state within a framework of the
subcontinent.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which events are most often asked about in 4-mark questions?**
Any named item in the specified content can be asked; learn three or four facts for each.

**How do I balance a "how successful" question on 1927–39?**
Successes: the Fourteen Points, Iqbal's vision, the Communal Award, the League's revival after 1937.
Failures: the 1937 election results and the lack of agreement at the Round Table Conferences.
