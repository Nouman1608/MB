## Questions

**1.** Convert units.

**(a)** Change 54 km/h into m/s. **[1]**
**(b)** Change 8 m/s into km/h. **[1]**
**(c)** A drone flies 0.36 km in 90 s. Calculate its speed in m/s. **[2]**

**2.** A trolley rolls down a ramp. Describe how you would measure its average speed over the last 1.2 m of the ramp, and how you would make the result more reliable. **[4]**

**3.**

**(a)** A sprinter runs 100 m in 12.5 s. Calculate the average speed. **[2]**
**(b)** A hiker walks at a steady 1.4 m/s for 25 minutes. Calculate the distance walked in metres. **[2]**

**4.** A taxi drives 8 km north, then 3 km south. The journey takes 20 minutes.

**(a)** State the distance travelled. **[1]**
**(b)** State the displacement, including its direction. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the average speed in km/h. **[2]**
**(d)** State the average velocity in km/h. **[1]**

**5.** A displacement-time graph for a runner is made of straight lines joining these points: (0 s, 0 m), (8 s, 16 m), (14 s, 16 m), (20 s, 4 m).

**(a)** Calculate the speed in the first 8 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the motion between 8 s and 14 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the velocity between 14 s and 20 s and explain the sign of your answer. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the average speed over the whole 20 s. **[2]**

**6.** The velocity-time graph for a rider is made of straight lines joining: (0 s, 0 m/s), (5 s, 10 m/s), (15 s, 10 m/s), (18 s, 0 m/s).

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration during the first 5 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total distance travelled using the area under the graph. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the acceleration between 15 s and 18 s. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the average speed over the 18 s, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**7.**

**(a)** A scooter changes velocity from 3 m/s to 15 m/s in 4 s. Calculate its acceleration. **[2]**
**(b)** A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 1.6 m/s² for 9 s. Calculate its final velocity. **[2]**

**8.** A train travelling at 30 m/s brakes uniformly to rest with an acceleration of −1.5 m/s². Calculate the distance it travels while braking. **[4]**

**9.** A student runs 1200 m in 300 s, then 800 m in 100 s.

**(a)** Calculate the average speed for the whole run. **[3]**
**(b)** The student says the average speed is the mean of the two stage speeds. Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**10.** A skateboarder moving at 2 m/s accelerates uniformly at 1.5 m/s² over a distance of 39 m. Calculate the final speed. **[4]**

**11.** A car is travelling at 14 m/s. The driver reacts after 0.6 s (a value supplied for this question), during which the car keeps its speed. The car then brakes uniformly at 4.0 m/s² until it stops.

**(a)** Calculate the distance travelled during the 0.6 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance travelled while braking. **[3]**
**(c)** State the total distance from the moment the driver sees the hazard to stopping, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the time taken to brake to rest. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** From this list, state the two vector quantities: speed, displacement, distance, velocity. **[1]**
**(b)** A car drives round a roundabout at a constant 12 m/s. Explain why its velocity is not constant. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 54 ÷ 3.6 = **15 m/s** [1]
**(b)** 8 × 3.6 = **28.8 km/h** [1]
**(c)** 0.36 km = 360 m [1]; speed = 360 ÷ 90 = **4.0 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion step (km to m) separately, so that a slip in the final division does not hide that you knew what to do.

**2.** Mark two points 1.2 m apart on the ramp with a metre rule. [1] Time the trolley between the marks with light gates (or a video with a timer), since a hand stopwatch has a reaction time that is large compared with the short time. [1] Speed = distance ÷ time. [1] Repeat the run several times and take a mean, releasing from the same point each time. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A reason attached to your choice of equipment (why light gates beat a stopwatch here) is stronger than naming the equipment alone.

**3. (a)** speed = distance ÷ time = 100 ÷ 12.5 [1] = **8.0 m/s** [1]
**(b)** time = 25 × 60 = 1500 s [1]; distance = 1.4 × 1500 = **2100 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is lost most often by multiplying 1.4 by 25 and getting 35 m; check that the time is in seconds when the speed is in m/s.

**4. (a)** 8 + 3 = **11 km** [1]
**(b)** 8 − 3 = 5 km [1], **north** [1]
**(c)** time = 20 min = 1/3 h [1]; average speed = 11 ÷ (1/3) = **33 km/h** [1]
**(d)** 5 ÷ (1/3) = **15 km/h north** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Displacement and velocity answers need a direction; a number alone describes only the size.

**5. (a)** speed = 16 ÷ 8 [1] = **2.0 m/s** [1]
**(b)** The runner is **stationary** (horizontal line) for 6 s. [1]
**(c)** velocity = (4 − 16) ÷ 6 [1] = **−2.0 m/s** [1]; the negative sign shows motion back towards the start, opposite to the original direction [1]
**(d)** distance = 16 + 12 = 28 m [1]; average speed = 28 ÷ 20 = **1.4 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (d), using the final displacement of 4 m instead of the total distance of 28 m would give an average velocity, not an average speed.

**6. (a)** a = (10 − 0) ÷ 5 [1] = **2.0 m/s²** [1]
**(b)** triangle ½ × 5 × 10 = 25 m [1]; rectangle 10 × 10 = 100 m [1]; triangle ½ × 3 × 10 = 15 m, total **140 m** [1]
**(c)** a = (0 − 10) ÷ 3 [1] = **−3.3 m/s²** [1]
**(d)** 140 ÷ 18 = **7.8 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Splitting the shape into labelled rectangles and triangles makes your method clear and helps you spot a slip in the total; writing only 140 m hides your working.

**7. (a)** a = (15 − 3) ÷ 4 [1] = **3.0 m/s²** [1]
**(b)** v = u + at, rearranged from a = (v − u) ÷ t, so v = 0 + 1.6 × 9 [1] = **14.4 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the starting equation before substituting, and carry the unit m/s² through your working so the final unit is easy to check.

**8.** v² − u² = 2as [1]; 0² − 30² = 2 × (−1.5) × s [1]; −900 = −3.0s [1]; s = **300 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A negative acceleration with a negative left-hand side gives a positive distance; getting a negative distance usually signals a sign slip you should catch.

**9. (a)** total distance = 1200 + 800 = 2000 m [1]; total time = 300 + 100 = 400 s [1]; average speed = 2000 ÷ 400 = **5.0 m/s** [1]
**(b)** The stage speeds are 4.0 m/s and 8.0 m/s, whose mean is 6.0 m/s, but the student spent three times as long at the slower speed, so the mean of speeds is not the average speed. [1]
*Examiner insight:* For an explain question, give the reason (different times at each speed) as well as the correct value.

**10.** v² − u² = 2as [1]; v² = 2² + 2 × 1.5 × 39 [1]; v² = 4 + 117 = 121 [1]; v = **11 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the square root as the last step; stopping at 121 and quoting it as the speed is a common slip.

**11. (a)** distance = speed × time = 14 × 0.6 [1] = **8.4 m** [1]
**(b)** 0² − 14² = 2 × (−4.0) × s [1]; −196 = −8.0s [1]; s = **24.5 m** [1]
**(c)** 8.4 + 24.5 = 32.9, so **33 m** [1]
**(d)** t = (0 − 14) ÷ (−4.0) [1] = **3.5 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-part question, add the two distances only after both are found; showing each part separately keeps your working clear and makes a slip easy to trace.

**12. (a)** **displacement and velocity** [1]
**(b)** Velocity has both size and direction. [1] The speed is constant, but the direction of travel keeps changing round the roundabout, so the velocity changes. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name both parts of a vector (size and direction) and say which one is changing; stating only that the car is turning is not enough to answer an explain question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting minutes, hours or kilometres into seconds or metres before substituting.
- Giving a speed or distance when the question asks for a displacement or velocity with a direction.
- Reading the gradient on a velocity-time graph as a distance.
- Counting the total displacement instead of total distance when finding average speed.
- Missing out the stationary period when finding the total time.
- Squaring (v − u) instead of squaring v and u separately.
- Leaving off the negative sign on a deceleration, or giving a negative distance.
- Rounding too early and quoting an answer to the wrong number of significant figures.
