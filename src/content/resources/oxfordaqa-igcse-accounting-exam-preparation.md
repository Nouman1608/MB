OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) is a linear, untiered qualification assessed entirely
by examination -- there is no coursework component. Paper 1 (Introduction to Book-keeping and
Financial Accounting) and Paper 2 (Financial Statements) each run 1 hour 45 minutes, carry 75 marks
and are worth 50%. This is a recently launched specification: first teaching was September 2024 and it
was first examined in May/June 2026. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Sources and Recording of Data](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-accounting-sources-and-recording-of-data/)
and
[Verification of Accounting Records](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-accounting-verification-of-accounting-records/).

## The two papers are sequential in logic, so Paper 1's content underpins Paper 2

Paper 1 covers book-keeping and the foundations of financial accounting; Paper 2 covers the financial
statements those records produce. You cannot prepare a statement of profit or loss without the double
entry that feeds it. **Exam-preparation priority**: do not treat Paper 1 as finished once it is sat.
Trial balances, adjustments and the treatment of accruals and prepayments are Paper 1 material that
determines whether Paper 2's statements come out right.

## No coursework means every mark is earned under time pressure

Some accounting qualifications allow part of the grade to be built up over time. This one does not.
**Exam-preparation priority**: build exam speed deliberately. Accuracy achieved slowly is not the same
asset as accuracy achieved in 105 minutes, and the gap between the two is closed only by timed
practice.

## Seventy-five marks in 105 minutes is 1.4 minutes per mark

That is a workable but not generous budget for a subject in which answers involve laying out
statements. **Exam-preparation priority**: rehearse the layouts until producing them is mechanical.
Time spent recalling whether an item goes above or below a subtotal is time not spent on the
higher-tariff parts, and layout fluency is entirely a matter of practice.

## Presentation carries marks that content alone does not

Correct headings, correct dates, correct ordering and clearly referenced workings are creditable in
their own right. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise from a blank page rather than by completing
pre-drawn pro-formas, since the exam supplies no template.

## Command words separate a two-mark answer from a six-mark one

"State", "calculate", "explain" and "evaluate" ask for materially different things in accounting: a
figure, a figure with visible method, a figure with its business meaning, and a judgement between
alternatives. **Exam-preparation priority**: check every practice answer against its command word
before checking it against the content. A great many marks lost on written accounting papers are not
knowledge failures but answers that calculated when they were asked to explain, or explained when they
were asked to evaluate.

## The own-figure rule makes finishing a question worthwhile

Where a later part uses a figure calculated earlier, a wrong earlier figure carried forward correctly
normally still earns method marks. **Exam-preparation priority**: never abandon a multi-part question
because an earlier part looked wrong. State the figure you are carrying and continue.

## Adjustments are where most avoidable marks are lost

Accruals, prepayments, depreciation, irrecoverable debts and closing inventory each affect two places
-- the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position. **Exam-preparation
priority**: for each adjustment, rehearse both effects together rather than learning them separately.
Applying an adjustment in one statement and forgetting it in the other is the most common single error
in the subject.

## Worked routine: applying an adjustment in both statements

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - name the adjustment and its amount:
"Accrued electricity, 340" -- written down before any statement
is touched.

Step 2 - state the effect on the statement of profit or loss:
"Electricity expense increases by 340, so profit falls by 340."

Step 3 - state the effect on the statement of financial position:
"Accruals (current liabilities) increase by 340."

Step 4 - check the two effects are consistent:
Every adjustment has two sides. If you can only name one, the
treatment is incomplete.

Step 5 - reference the working:
"Electricity (W2)" with W2 set out separately, so a marker can
follow the reasoning even if a figure is wrong.
```

Step 4 is the check worth building into every practice question: it catches the single-sided
adjustment error before it propagates into the totals.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: keep Paper 1's book-keeping content revised through to Paper 2; practise to a
  1.4-minutes-per-mark budget; produce statements from a blank page rather than templates; rehearse
  each adjustment's two effects together.
- **During either paper**: label every statement with the business name, the statement name and the
  period or date.
- **In every calculation**: show and reference workings separately rather than embedding arithmetic.
- **In every multi-part question**: carry your own figure forward explicitly rather than stopping.

## Self-test

1. Why does Paper 1's content matter for Paper 2?
2. What does the absence of coursework imply for how you practise?
3. Why practise from a blank page rather than a pro-forma?
4. What is the most common single error when applying an adjustment?

**Answers:** 1. Because Paper 2's financial statements are produced from Paper 1's book-keeping --
trial balances, adjustments, accruals and prepayments determine whether the statements are correct.
2. That every mark is earned under time pressure, so speed must be built deliberately through timed
practice rather than relying on accuracy achieved at leisure. 3. Because the exam supplies no
template, and headings, dates, ordering and referenced workings are creditable in their own right.
4. Applying it in one statement and forgetting the corresponding effect in the other -- every
adjustment has two sides.
