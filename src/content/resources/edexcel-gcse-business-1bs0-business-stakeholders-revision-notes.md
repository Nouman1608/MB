These notes condense content item 1.5.1, Business stakeholders, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [stakeholders study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-stakeholders/) has a worked example tracing one decision through every group; test yourself with the [stakeholders practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-business-stakeholders-practice/). The wider [stakeholders and technology revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-stakeholders-and-technology-revision-notes/) cover 1.5.2 as well.

## What the specification lists

- Who business stakeholders are and their different objectives: shareholders (owners), employees, customers, managers, suppliers, local community, pressure groups, the government.
- Stakeholders and businesses: how stakeholders are affected by business activity; how stakeholders impact business activity; possible conflicts between stakeholder groups.

Memory aid for the eight groups (the writer's own): **S**hareholders, **E**mployees, **C**ustomers, **M**anagers, **S**uppliers, **L**ocal community, **P**ressure groups, **G**overnment.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder | Any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business |
| Shareholder | An owner of part of a company; one type of stakeholder |
| Objective | A target or goal a group wants to achieve |
| Pressure group | An organised group that campaigns to change how businesses or government behave on an issue |
| Local community | People living and working near the business |
| Stakeholder conflict | When meeting one group's objectives makes it harder to meet another's |
| Internal stakeholder | A group within the business: owners, managers, employees |
| External stakeholder | A group outside the business: customers, suppliers, local community, pressure groups, government |

## Objectives, and the power each group has

| Group | Main objectives | How it can impact the business |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders (owners) | Profit; rising value of the business; survival | Decide on investment; replace managers |
| Employees | Pay; job security; good conditions; training | Effort, quality of work, staying or leaving, industrial action |
| Customers | Low prices; quality; good service; choice | Buy or not; reviews; loyalty |
| Managers | Pay and bonuses; status; growth; job security | Make daily decisions; set direction |
| Suppliers | Regular orders; fair prices; prompt payment | Change prices, delivery times or credit terms; stop supplying |
| Local community | Jobs; little noise, traffic or pollution | Object to plans; support or avoid the business |
| Pressure groups | Change in behaviour on their issue | Campaigns, publicity, boycotts |
| Government | Tax revenue; jobs; legal behaviour; a healthy economy | Laws, taxes, planning permission, fines |

## Two directions

- **Affected by business activity:** decision → effect on group. Example: closing a branch → employees lose jobs; local community loses spending.
- **Impact on business activity:** group → effect on business. Example: customers post poor reviews → sales fall.

## Typical conflicts

| Conflict | The trade-off |
|---|---|
| Shareholders v employees | Higher wages mean less profit |
| Shareholders v customers | Lower prices or better quality mean less profit per sale |
| Managers v local community | Expansion brings growth but also noise and traffic |
| Business v suppliers | Lower prices paid mean lower supplier income |
| Business v pressure groups | Greener or fairer methods may cost more |

## Ways to handle conflict

- Prioritise the most influential group, or the one with most at stake.
- Compromise (for example, shorter opening hours than planned).
- Communicate early to reduce opposition.
- Share gains (for example, a bonus paid from extra profit).

## Exam traps

- Stakeholder ≠ shareholder.
- Name **how** a group is affected, not just which group.
- Match the direction asked: "affected by" or "impact on".
- Conflicts are normal; "keep everyone happy" is not an answer.
- In Analyse questions use the business and decision in the source.

## Sentence starter

The writer's suggestion: "This decision affects [group] because..., and [group] could respond by..., which would mean the business..."

## Self-test

1. Define the term stakeholder.
2. Name the eight stakeholder groups in the specification.
3. Give one objective of suppliers.
4. Give one objective of the government as a stakeholder.
5. State one way customers can impact business activity.
6. State one way a pressure group can impact a business.
7. A factory starts a night shift. Give one way the local community is affected.
8. Explain one conflict between shareholders and employees.
9. Why might managers favour growth more than shareholders do?
10. Give one way a business can reduce conflict with the local community.
11. A business's profit rises from £50,000 to £58,000 after a change. Calculate the percentage increase.
12. Why is a shareholder always a stakeholder, but a stakeholder not always a shareholder?

### Answers

1. Any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, the activities of a business.
2. Shareholders (owners), employees, customers, managers, suppliers, local community, pressure groups, the government.
3. Any one: regular orders; fair prices; prompt payment; a long-term relationship.
4. Any one: tax revenue; jobs; businesses obeying the law; a healthy economy.
5. Any one: choosing whether to buy; writing reviews; staying loyal or switching to rivals.
6. Any one: campaigns; publicity; organising a boycott.
7. Any one: more noise or traffic at night; more local jobs.
8. Employees want higher wages, but wages are a cost, so a pay rise reduces the profit available to shareholders.
9. A bigger business can bring managers higher pay, status and larger roles, whereas shareholders may prefer profit to be paid out.
10. Any one: consult residents early; limit noise or opening hours; support local projects.
11. Increase £8,000. £8,000 ÷ £50,000 × 100 = 16%.
12. Shareholders own the company, so they have an interest in it; but many groups with an interest, such as customers or suppliers, own no shares.
