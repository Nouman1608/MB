## 1.1 Formation of rocks

| Type | Formation | Named rocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Igneous** | Magma or lava cools and crystallises | Granite (slow cooling underground, **large crystals**); basalt (fast cooling at the surface, **small crystals**) |
| **Sedimentary** | Deposition and sedimentation of layers, then compaction and cementation | Limestone, sandstone, shale |
| **Metamorphic** | Existing rock changed by heat and/or pressure, without melting | Marble (from limestone), slate (from shale) |

**The eight named rock-cycle processes:** weathering, erosion,
transportation, sedimentation, compaction, cementation, crystallisation
and deposition.

```
rock at the surface -> weathering -> erosion -> transportation
  -> deposition -> sedimentation -> compaction -> cementation
  -> SEDIMENTARY ROCK
any rock -- heat and/or pressure, no melting --> METAMORPHIC ROCK
any rock -- melts --> magma -- crystallisation --> IGNEOUS ROCK
```

**Permeability** = "the ability of water to pass through the pore spaces
of rock and soil".

| Permeable | Impermeable |
|---|---|
| Most sedimentary rocks: limestone and sandstone | Igneous and metamorphic rocks, and some sedimentary rocks: shale |

## 1.2 Extraction of rocks, ores and minerals

**Ore** = "rock containing minerals and metals".

| Method | Named forms |
|---|---|
| Surface extraction | Opencast, open-pit, open-cut and strip mining |
| Subsurface extraction | Deep mining and shaft mining |
| Biological extraction | **Phytomining** (plants absorb metal compounds; harvested, burned, metal recovered from ash) and **bioleaching** (bacteria release metal compounds from low-grade ore into solution) |

**Eight factors in the decision to extract:** exploration; geology;
accessibility and terrain; quantity and quality of deposit (ore grade);
climate; environmental impact assessment; supply and demand; cost and
profit.

**Seven impacts:** loss of habitat and biodiversity; air, land, noise,
visual and water pollution; water usage; waste management; changes in
employment opportunities; local and national economies; facilities and
infrastructure.

**Managing damaged landscapes:**

- **Land restoration** — replacement of overburden, soil improvement,
  bioremediation, tree planting.
- **Repurposing land** — landfill, lakes, recreation, nature reserves.

For each, have one benefit and one limitation ready (1.2.6 says
"discuss the benefits and limitations").

## 1.3 Sustainable management of rocks, ores and minerals

- **Finite resource** = "a natural resource that is used up at a faster
  rate than it is replaced".
- **Sustainable management of resources** = "the use of strategies that
  ensure the needs of the present are met without compromising the
  ability of future generations to meet their own needs".

**Seven strategies:** reduce and reuse; recycling (accessibility, ease and
education); increased efficiency of extraction; avoiding resource
depletion; legislation; enforcement; use of alternative materials.

A quick benefit and limitation for each: recycling cuts mining but costs
money to collect and sort; legislation sets rules but only works with
enforcement; enforcement needs money and staff; alternative materials may
cost more or be finite too.

## Exam traps

- Saying metamorphic rock forms by melting.
- Calling shale permeable because it is sedimentary.
- Giving only two extraction methods: this edition has three.
- Mixing up the two lists in 1.2.5: restoration returns the land towards
  its earlier state; repurposing gives it a new use.
- Giving benefits without limitations in a "discuss" answer.

## Self-test

1. State the syllabus definition of permeability.
2. Name one permeable and one impermeable sedimentary rock.
3. Define an ore.
4. Name the two forms of biological extraction.
5. Give four of the eight factors that affect the decision to extract.
6. Name the two groups of strategies for managing damaged landscapes, with
   two examples of each.
7. Define a finite resource.
8. An ore has a grade of 2% metal. How many tonnes of metal are in 250
   tonnes of ore?

**Answers:** 1. The ability of water to pass through the pore spaces of
rock and soil. 2. Permeable: limestone or sandstone; impermeable: shale.
3. Rock containing minerals and metals. 4. Phytomining and bioleaching.
5. Any four of exploration; geology; accessibility and terrain; quantity
and quality of deposit (ore grade); climate; environmental impact
assessment; supply and demand; cost and profit. 6. Land restoration
(any two of replacement of overburden, soil improvement, bioremediation,
tree planting) and repurposing land (any two of landfill, lakes,
recreation, nature reserves). 7. A natural resource that is used up at a
faster rate than it is replaced. 8. 2 / 100 x 250 = 5 tonnes.
