## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain the purpose of bank reconciliation statements.
- (b) Understand the process of bank reconciliation.
- (c) Prepare a bank reconciliation statement.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bank reconciliation statement | Statement explaining the difference between the cash book and bank statement balances at a date |
| Unpresented cheque | Cheque recorded in the cash book but not yet on the bank statement |
| Uncredited lodgement | Money paid in and recorded, not yet on the statement |
| Credit transfer | Money paid directly into the account by a customer |
| Direct debit | Payment taken by a supplier from the account, by agreement; the amount can vary |
| Standing order | Fixed regular payment made by the bank on the business's instruction |
| Dishonoured cheque | A customer's cheque the bank refuses to pay; reverses the receipt |
| Overdraft | Negative bank balance; credit in the cash book, debit on the statement |

## Four purposes

1. Find unrecorded items so the cash book is complete.
2. Find errors by the business or the bank.
3. Detect fraud or unauthorised payments.
4. Confirm the correct bank figure for the statement of financial position.

## Which record does each item change?

| Item | Update cash book? | On reconciliation statement? |
|---|---|---|
| Bank charges, interest charged | Yes (credit side) | No |
| Interest received | Yes (debit side) | No |
| Direct debits, standing orders | Yes (credit side) | No |
| Credit transfers | Yes (debit side) | No |
| Dishonoured cheque | Yes (credit side) | No |
| Error in the cash book | Yes (correct it) | No |
| Unpresented cheques | No | Yes |
| Uncredited lodgements | No | Yes |
| Bank error | No | Yes (and tell the bank) |

## Statement layout (balance in funds)

```
Balance per updated cash book
Add unpresented cheques
Less uncredited lodgements
+/- bank errors
Balance per bank statement
```

Reverse direction: start from the bank statement, deduct unpresented cheques, add uncredited lodgements.

Mini-check: updated cash book 3,140; unpresented cheques 860; uncredited lodgement 1,200. Bank statement = 3,140 + 860 − 1,200 = 2,800.

## Overdrafts

- Treat the overdraft as a negative number: (1,500).
- Unrecorded payments make the cash book overdraft bigger; unrecorded receipts make it smaller.
- Unpresented cheques make the bank's overdraft smaller than the cash book's.
- Uncredited lodgements make the bank's overdraft bigger than the cash book's.

Mini-check: updated overdraft (900); unpresented cheques 400; uncredited lodgement 650. Bank: −900 + 400 − 650 = (1,150) overdrawn.

## Process in six steps

1. Tick matching items.
2. Enter unticked statement items in the cash book.
3. Correct cash book errors.
4. Balance the updated cash book.
5. List unpresented cheques, uncredited lodgements and bank errors.
6. Prepare the statement, titled and dated.

## Exam traps

- Unpresented cheques entered in the cash book.
- Reconciliation started from the unadjusted cash book balance.
- Dishonoured cheque added instead of deducted.
- Bank error entered in the cash book.
- Signs reversed with an overdraft.
- Thinking a credit balance on the bank statement means an overdraft. It means money in the account.
- Final line not labelled "balance per bank statement".

## Quick self-test

1. State two purposes of a bank reconciliation statement.
2. Why does the bank statement show a credit when the account has money in it?
3. Which of these update the cash book: bank charges, unpresented cheques, a credit transfer?
4. A customer's cheque for 240 is dishonoured. What is the cash book entry?
5. Cash book 1,950 (debit); bank charges 30 and a standing order 200 not recorded. Updated balance?
6. Updated cash book 2,410; unpresented cheques 535; uncredited lodgement 900. Bank statement balance?
7. The bank statement shows 4,100; unpresented cheques 720; uncredited lodgement 380. What is the updated cash book balance?
8. Updated cash book overdraft 1,250; unpresented cheques 300. No other items. Bank statement balance?
9. The bank charged another customer's cheque of 95 to the account. How is it treated?
10. A cheque payment of 418 was entered in the cash book as 481. How is it corrected?
11. Which balance is shown in the statement of financial position?
12. What is the difference between a direct debit and a standing order?

### Answers

1. Any two: find unrecorded items; find errors; detect fraud; confirm the correct bank balance.
2. The bank owes the money to the business, so it is a liability of the bank.
3. Bank charges and the credit transfer.
4. Credit the bank column (and debit the customer's account) with 240.
5. 1,950 − 30 − 200 = 1,720.
6. 2,410 + 535 − 900 = 2,045.
7. 4,100 − 720 + 380 = 3,760.
8. −1,250 + 300 = (950) overdrawn.
9. Shown on the reconciliation statement as a bank error and reported to the bank; not entered in the cash book.
10. The payment was overstated by 63, so 63 is added back on the debit side of the cash book.
11. The updated cash book balance.
12. A direct debit is taken by the payee and can vary; a standing order is a fixed amount paid by the bank on the business's instruction.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the updated cash book balance used in the statement of financial position?**
It includes every receipt and payment the business has made, including those the bank has not yet processed, so it is the business's true position.

**Do unpresented cheques from last month matter this month?**
Yes. Tick them against this month's statement; any still not presented stay on the new reconciliation.
