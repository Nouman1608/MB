These notes condense sub-topic 1(b) Elements, compounds and mixtures (points 1.8 to 1.13) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). None of the points has a C reference, so all of it can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2. The [elements, compounds and mixtures study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-elements-compounds-and-mixtures/) has the full explanations and worked examples; the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-elements-compounds-and-mixtures-practice/) test them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Element | A substance made of only one type of atom |
| Compound | Two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions |
| Mixture | Two or more substances not chemically combined; separable by physical methods |
| Pure substance | A single element or a single compound, with nothing else mixed in |
| Residue | The solid left in the filter paper |
| Filtrate | The liquid that passes through the filter paper |
| Distillate | The liquid collected from the condenser |
| Chromatogram | The developed paper showing the separated spots |
| Solvent front | The furthest point reached by the solvent |
| Rf value | Distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front |

## Classifying (1.8)

```
more than one kind of particle?             -> mixture
one kind of particle, one element in it?    -> element (atoms or molecules such as O2)
one kind of particle, two or more elements? -> compound
```

- A mixture of two elements (for example, H₂ and O₂ molecules together) is still a mixture.
- Compounds have different properties from their elements; separating them needs a chemical reaction.
- Mixtures can be in any proportion; compounds have fixed proportions (fixed formula).

## Purity (1.9)

| Substance | Melting and boiling |
|---|---|
| Pure | Sharp, fixed melting point and boiling point |
| Mixture | Melts or boils over a range of temperatures |

Impurities usually lower the melting point. A sharp melting point matching a data-book value suggests the substance is pure.

## Separation methods (1.10)

| Method | Use it for | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Filtration | Insoluble solid from a liquid | Filter paper in a funnel; residue and filtrate |
| Crystallisation | Soluble solid from its solution | Evaporate some water until a drop on a cold rod crystallises; cool; filter; wash with a little cold water; dry. Do not evaporate to dryness |
| Simple distillation | A solvent from a solution | Thermometer bulb level with side arm; condenser water in at the bottom; solute stays in the flask |
| Fractional distillation | Miscible liquids with different boiling points | Fractionating column; lowest boiling point liquid collected first; change receiver when the temperature rises |
| Paper chromatography | Dissolved coloured substances | Pencil baseline above solvent; lid on; mark solvent front |

### Method: choosing the technique

```
solid + liquid, solid insoluble    -> filtration
want the dissolved solid           -> crystallisation
want the liquid from a solution    -> simple distillation
two liquids that mix               -> fractional distillation
identify dyes in a mixture         -> paper chromatography
```

For a mixture of an insoluble and a soluble solid: dissolve, filter, then crystallise the filtrate.

## Chromatograms and Rf (1.11, 1.12, 1.13)

- Number of spots = **minimum** number of substances.
- Same Rf as a reference in the same solvent → probably the same substance.
- Spot on the baseline → insoluble in that solvent.
- Distances are measured **from the baseline**, to the centre of the spot.
- Rf has no units and is less than 1.

Reminder: front 12.0 cm, spot 4.8 cm → Rf = 4.8 ÷ 12.0 = 0.40.

Practical checklist (1.13):

- Baseline in pencil, about 1 cm or more above the bottom.
- Small, concentrated spots, each labelled in pencil below the line.
- Solvent depth below the baseline.
- Lid on the container.
- Remove before the solvent reaches the top; mark the front straight away; dry the paper.

## Exam traps

- Defining a compound as "a mixture of elements". It is chemically combined.
- Calling O₂ a compound because it has two atoms.
- Answering "evaporation" when crystals are wanted.
- Leaving out the reason for a pencil baseline: ink would dissolve and run.
- Saying the solvent should cover the spots.
- Calculating Rf with distances from the paper's edge.
- Claiming one spot "proves" a substance is pure: it is consistent with purity in that solvent.

## Self-test

1. State what is meant by a compound.
2. A sample contains Ne atoms and Ar atoms only. Classify it and give a reason.
3. A sample melts between 71 °C and 76 °C. What does this tell you?
4. Name the method used to obtain pure water from sea water.
5. Name the method used to separate ethanol (boiling point 78 °C) from water.
6. Explain why the thermometer bulb in distillation is placed level with the side arm.
7. Give one reason why a salt solution is not evaporated to dryness when making crystals.
8. Name the residue and the filtrate when muddy water is filtered.
9. Explain why the solvent level must be below the baseline in chromatography.
10. The solvent front moves 8.0 cm. A spot moves 5.2 cm. Calculate its Rf value.
11. A black ink gives three spots. What is the minimum number of dyes in the ink?
12. Two spots from different samples have the same Rf in water but different Rf in ethanol. Are they the same substance? Explain.

### Answers

1. Two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions.
2. A mixture: two different substances (two elements) not chemically combined.
3. It is not pure: it is a mixture / contains an impurity.
4. Simple distillation.
5. Fractional distillation.
6. To measure the temperature of the vapour that is entering the condenser.
7. Any one: crystals of some salts decompose or lose water of crystallisation; soluble impurities would be left in the solid.
8. Residue: mud (sand/soil) in the filter paper. Filtrate: the water that passes through.
9. Otherwise the spots dissolve into the solvent in the container instead of moving up the paper.
10. 5.2 ÷ 8.0 = 0.65.
11. Three.
12. No: a single substance has a fixed Rf in each solvent, so different Rf in ethanol shows they are different substances.
