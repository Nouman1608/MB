# Related concepts: edit and explain

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use. Marks in brackets are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/).

## Questions

**1.** Name the three related-concept examples in the IB brief for language acquisition. **[3]**

**2.** The sentence "Give me the report now" is written to a head teacher. Rewrite it and name the related concept you used. **[2]**

**3.** Read this original message to a school office: "hi i lost my bag. its blue. bye". Rewrite it with suitable conventions for a formal message. **[4]**

**4.** The phrase "it's raining cats and dogs" appears in a text. (a) Explain why translating it word for word fails. **[2]** (b) State its meaning. **[1]**

**5.** Choose the more precise word in each pair and say why: (a) walked / strolled for a relaxed evening; (b) said / whispered in a library. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one risk of using an idiom in a speaking task. **[2]**

## Model answers

**1.** Word choice, conventions, idiom [1 each].

**2.** "Could I please have the report by Friday?" [1]. Word choice (register) [1].

**3.** Greeting ("Dear Office team") [1]; clear purpose ("I lost a blue school bag in the sports hall on Monday") [1]; request ("Please let me know if it has been handed in") [1]; closing and name [1].

**4.** (a) The meaning is not the sum of the words; a literal reading is about animals [2]. (b) Very heavy rain [1].

**5.** (a) Strolled: shows a relaxed pace [1]. (b) Whispered: fits the quiet setting [1].

**6.** If you use it wrongly or in the wrong register, the listener may be confused [1] or you may seem unnatural [1].
