This page is a guide to AQA A-Level Biology (7402) past papers for the June 2024, June 2025 and June 2026 series. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published its report on the examination, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for the subject.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report. If a paper exposes a weak topic, go back to the matching study guide, for example [biological molecules](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-biological-molecules/), [cell structure](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-cell-structure-revision-notes/) or [the cell cycle, mitosis and Required Practical 2](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-cell-cycle-mitosis-and-required-practical-2/).

Check which specification a paper was set on. A-Level Biology 7402 is a linear qualification, not tiered, first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2017, so every series on this page was set on the same specification. There are three written papers, each 2 hours. Paper 1 (91 marks, 35 per cent) covers topics 1 to 4 and practical skills: 76 marks of short and long answers and 15 marks of extended response. Paper 2 (91 marks, 35 per cent) covers topics 5 to 8 and practical skills: 76 marks of short and long answers and 15 marks of comprehension. Paper 3 (78 marks, 30 per cent) can draw on topics 1 to 8: 38 marks of structured questions, 15 marks of critical analysis of experimental data and a 25-mark essay chosen from two titles. The total is 260 marks.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe a Paper 1 with a mark distribution almost identical to 2024, in which knowledge questions were a little harder and the maths slightly easier. Paper 2 was judged more accessible than the year before, with fewer omitted questions. Paper 3 showed a familiar pattern: many answers were rote-learnt phrases that did not use the information in the question, and some candidates wrote on six or more essay topics in little depth instead of four in enough depth. Across all three papers, answers lost marks for vague terminology and for ignoring instructions about what to include.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 7402/1: lipid digestion explained without saying how emulsification speeds up lipase action, and micelles confused with emulsification; membrane structure explained through permeability instead of the behaviour of the molecules in water; phosphodiester bonds muddled with hydrogen bonds.
- Paper 7402/1: the induced-fit model compared with an older model using vague wording; enzyme inhibition described without saying where the inhibitor binds; co-transport questions where candidates said energy is produced, not released, or gave fructose an active or facilitated route that the data did not show.
- Paper 7402/1: practical and maths skills, including calibration curves with the axes the wrong way round, misread values from graphs, DNA mass bar charts without full axis labels, and cell number calculations that miscounted meiotic divisions.
- Paper 7402/1: describing graph data instead of linking it to binary fission and plasmid replication; gas exchange answers that gave a large surface area without linking it to diffusion; circulation descriptions that skipped vessels or mixed up valves.
- Paper 7402/2: stages of respiration and photosynthesis mixed up, products of the Krebs cycle confused with those of the Calvin cycle, and a joules to kilojoules error in an energy calculation; probability questions where the chance of a son was left out.
- Paper 7402/2: genetics answers that ignored linkage, gave gametes' genotypes too rarely, or blamed mutation for variation in ratios; nerve and heart control answers that left out the sinoatrial node; DNA probes described by use instead of definition.
- Paper 7402/2: ecology answers that gave only "more food", confused nitrifying and nitrogen fixing bacteria, or named a biotic factor when an abiotic one was asked for; chi-squared answers without the critical value or with the wrong degrees of freedom.
- Paper 7402/3: hypothesis-testing answers that used "no statistical test" and "small sample" without saying why they mattered; log scales and standard form handled badly; and essays that described a lot of topic content with little on the reactions the title asked about.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports say many Paper 1 questions rewarded precise detail and application to the context: candidates knew basic definitions but often did not give enough to earn the marks, and several wrote at length about a process that was not relevant to the question. Paper 2 was a good discriminator, with strong recall but weaker application. On Paper 3, examiners again saw candidates not reading the question stem or the command, and an essay in which quantity of writing did not raise quality.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 7402/1: condensation shown without removing water from the right groups; facilitated diffusion and active transport defined without carrier proteins; microvilli confused with villi; ventilation described without explaining how rib movement changes thoracic volume and pressure.
- Paper 7402/1: uncertainty worked out for a single point on a ruler rather than a measurement between two points; mitotic index given as a percentage when the graph used a proportion; "results are due to chance" and comparisons of r with P in significance statements.
- Paper 7402/1: mRNA compared with tRNA by the wrong features, amino acid sequences read from the complementary instead of the correct strand, and an assumption that every mutation changes protein structure; enzyme practical answers that mistook slower browning for a faster reaction.
- Paper 7402/1: long answers on tissue fractionation, the production and release of enzymes, and the structure of ATP, where candidates wrote at length about processes the title did not need.
- Paper 7402/2: synapse diagrams and chloride or potassium movement, and photosynthesis answers that gave the products of the light-dependent reaction but the wrong roles in the next stage.
- Paper 7402/2: genetic crosses with wrong parental genotypes, Hardy-Weinberg calculations that used the right term with the wrong numbers, and electrophoresis answers that described the technique instead of comparing bands with the unknown sample.
- Paper 7402/2: plant hormone questions where negative indices on an axis were misread, dilutions given without volumes, and control variables stated as "amount" with no measurable quantity; answers on different activity patterns that jumped to different genes and different species.
- Paper 7402/3: answers that named a surface structure without the biological term asked for; viral structure errors; command words such as "apart from" ignored; rounding and significant figure errors; and essay answers that wrote about too few of the required topics.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A-Level Biology 7402 past papers?**
AQA publishes papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for 7402, and offers a past paper finder on its website.

**Which 7402 papers should I sit when practising a series?**
Sit all three papers for the series. Papers 1 and 2 are each 91 marks and Paper 3 is 78 marks, and each lasts 2 hours. Mark them with the official mark scheme.

**Are the paper-level thresholds the ones that decide my grade?**
No. The A-level is linear, so the subject-level boundaries out of 260 decide the grade. AQA gives the paper-level figures as notional boundaries, for illustration only.

**Why is there no examiner report summary for June 2026?**
When we checked, AQA's assessment resources page for 7402 listed reports only up to June 2025. We only summarise reports we have read, so June 2026 will be added once the board publishes them.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
