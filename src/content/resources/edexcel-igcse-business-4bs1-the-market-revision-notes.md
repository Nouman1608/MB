Quick recall for section 4.2 The market of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 4.2.1 to 4.2.3. Worked market analysis, a niche-versus-mass comparison and a segment profile are in the [study guide on the market](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-the-market/); questions are in the [practice questions on the market](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-the-market-practice/). Figures are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification list

- **4.2.1 Importance of marketing:** satisfying customer needs; building customer relationships; keeping customer loyalty; market orientation and product orientation; market share and analysis; niche and mass marketing.
- **4.2.2 Responding to changes in the market:** changing customer needs; changing customer/consumer spending patterns; increased competition.
- **4.2.3 Market segmentation to target customers:** location, demographics, lifestyle, income, age.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing | Identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs profitably |
| Customer relationship | The ongoing contact between a business and its customers after a sale |
| Customer loyalty | Customers choosing to buy from the same business repeatedly |
| Market orientation | Developing products based on what research shows customers want |
| Product orientation | Developing products based on what the business can make, then finding buyers |
| Market share | One business's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Market analysis | Studying a market's size, growth, competitors and trends |
| Niche market | A small, specialised part of a market |
| Mass market | The large, general part of a market buying standard products |
| Market segment | A group of customers with shared characteristics |
| Market segmentation | Dividing a market into segments to target each one |
| Spending pattern | How much customers spend and on what |

These are standard definitions; the specification lists the terms without defining them.

## 3. The customer chain

Satisfy needs → customers are pleased → build the relationship → customers return (loyalty) → lower cost of finding new customers, recommendations, steadier sales.

Ways to build relationships and loyalty: after-sales service, handling complaints well, loyalty schemes, personalised offers, members' clubs, social media contact.

## 4. Orientation comparison

| | Market-oriented | Product-oriented |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with | Customers | The product or technology |
| Risk | Low risk of a mismatch; research is costly | Product may not sell |
| Suits | Fast-changing, competitive markets | Specialist or highly innovative products |

## 5. Market share and analysis

- Market share (%) = business sales ÷ total market sales × 100 (standard form).
- Compare a firm's growth rate with the market's growth rate:
  - firm grows faster than market → share rises;
  - firm grows at the same rate → share unchanged;
  - firm grows slower → share falls, even though sales rose.

**Worked reminder.** A market grows from 450,000 to 540,000 (20%). A firm's sales grow from 36,000 to 45,000 (25%). Share: 36,000 ÷ 450,000 × 100 = 8.0%, then 45,000 ÷ 540,000 × 100 = **8.3%**. Share rose because the firm grew faster than the market.

## 6. Niche versus mass

| | Niche | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | Low | High |
| Price and profit per unit | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Competition | Less | Intense |
| Main risk | Small market that may shrink | Price wars; big rivals |
| Suits | Small, specialist firms | Large firms with economies of scale |

## 7. Responding to change

| Change | Typical response | Why it might not work |
|---|---|---|
| Customer needs change | Adapt or replace products | Development costs; may be too late |
| Spending falls | Cheaper ranges, smaller sizes, offers | Lower profit per sale |
| Spending shifts online | Online ordering, delivery | Investment needed; new competitors |
| More competition | Better service, loyalty schemes, target a niche, improve quality | Rivals may copy; price cuts can start a price war |

## 8. Segmentation bases with examples

- **Location:** a regional food brand sold only in shops in its home area.
- **Demographics:** baby products targeted at new parents.
- **Lifestyle:** fitness trackers for regular runners.
- **Income:** a hotel chain with budget and luxury brands.
- **Age:** a streaming service with a children's profile.

Benefits: less wasted promotion; better-fitting products; possible higher prices. Drawbacks: research cost; missed sales from untargeted groups.

## 9. Exam traps

- Assuming more sales means more market share.
- Calling a firm niche because it is small: niche describes the market targeted.
- Treating market orientation as "doing research" only: it means research drives product decisions.
- Suggesting a price war against a much larger rival without discussing the risk.
- Listing segmentation bases without saying how they shape product, price or promotion.
- Using "loyalty" and "relationship" as if they were the same: relationships are built; loyalty is the result.

## 10. Answer frames

- **Explain** one benefit of customer loyalty: repeat purchases → lower promotion cost per sale → higher profit.
- **Analyse** a response to increased competition: the threat → the response → why it suits this firm → effect on sales or share.
- **Justify** niche or mass: compare profit per unit and volume, the competition, and the firm's size and skills, then decide.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two of the reasons the specification lists for why marketing is important.
2. Define market orientation.
3. A firm sells 63,000 in a market worth 420,000. Calculate its market share.
4. A firm's sales rise by 5% while its market grows by 10%. What happens to its share?
5. Give one advantage of niche marketing.
6. Give one disadvantage of mass marketing.
7. Name the three changes in the market listed in 4.2.2.
8. Give the five bases of segmentation in the specification.
9. A sports shop targets people who exercise regularly. Which basis is this?
10. Give one way a café could build customer relationships.
11. Give one benefit of segmentation.
12. Why might cutting prices be a poor response to a large new competitor?

### Answers

1. Any two of: satisfying customer needs; building customer relationships; keeping customer loyalty (also market share and analysis, niche and mass marketing, orientation).
2. Developing products based on what market research shows customers want.
3. 63,000 ÷ 420,000 × 100 = 15%.
4. It falls, because the firm is growing more slowly than the market.
5. Any one of: less competition; loyal customers; higher prices possible.
6. Any one of: strong competition; lower profit per unit; heavy promotion costs.
7. Changing customer needs; changing customer/consumer spending patterns; increased competition.
8. Location, demographics, lifestyle, income, age.
9. Lifestyle.
10. Any one of: a loyalty card; remembering regular orders; answering reviews; offers for regular customers.
11. Promotion is aimed at people likely to buy, so less money is wasted.
12. A large rival can usually cut prices further, so the small business may lose profit without winning customers.
