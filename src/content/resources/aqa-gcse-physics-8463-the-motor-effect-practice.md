These questions cover section **4.7.2 The motor effect** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification, for first
teaching 2016, examined on Paper 2. Questions 1 to 4 and 7 test 4.7.2.1 (both tiers); questions marked
**Higher tier only** test 4.7.2.2 to 4.7.2.4. A calculator is allowed. All numerical data are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe the magnetic field around a long straight wire carrying a current. Include how the field changes
with distance from the wire. **[2]**

**2.** State two features of the magnetic field of a solenoid carrying a current. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why adding an iron core to a solenoid increases the strength of its magnetic field. **[1]**

**4.** Give one advantage of an electromagnet over a permanent magnet for lifting scrap steel in a scrapyard. **[1]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) State the factors that affect the size of the force on a current-carrying wire at right
angles to a magnetic field. **[3]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) A wire lies parallel to the field lines of a magnet and carries a current. State the force
on it. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A door lock uses an electromagnet. When a code is entered, a switch closes and a current flows through a coil
wound on an iron core. A spring-loaded iron bolt is held in the locked position next to the core.

**(a)** Explain how the lock opens when the switch is closed. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain what happens when the switch opens again. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) A horizontal wire carries a current from left to right between the poles of a magnet. The
field points into the page.

**(a)** Use Fleming's left-hand rule to state the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**
**(b)** State two ways to reverse the direction of the force. **[2]**
**(c)** State what happens to the force if both of the changes in (b) are made at the same time. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A wire carries a current of 6.0 A at right angles to a magnetic field of flux density
0.35 T. The length of wire in the field is 15 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the force on the wire. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** The current is doubled and the flux density is reduced to one third of its value. Calculate the new force. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) In a test, a force of 0.075 N acts on a wire carrying 1.5 A at right angles to a field of
0.25 T. Calculate the length of wire in the field, in centimetres. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A simple electric motor has a rectangular coil between the poles of a magnet. Explain why the
coil rotates when there is a current in it, and give two ways to make it rotate faster. **[5]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Explain how a moving-coil loudspeaker produces a sound wave from an alternating current, and
how it produces a louder sound of the same pitch. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Concentric circles centred on the wire [1]; the field gets weaker further from the wire (circles further apart) [1]

**2.** Any two from: inside it is strong [1]; inside it is uniform [1]; outside it has the same shape as the field of a bar magnet [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Like a magnet" is too vague; say it is shaped like a bar magnet's field.

**3.** The iron core becomes an induced magnet and its field adds to the field of the coil [1]

**4.** It can be switched off to release the steel (or its strength can be varied with the current) [1]

**5.** Magnetic flux density (strength of the field) [1]; size of the current [1]; length of wire in the field [1]

**6.** Zero (no force) [1]

**7. (a)** Current in the coil makes the iron core an electromagnet [1]; the electromagnet attracts the iron bolt [1]; the bolt moves towards the core, out of the locked position, compressing the spring [1]
**(b)** The current stops and the core loses its magnetism quickly [1]; the spring pushes the bolt back to the locked position [1] **[5]**

**8. (a)** Upwards (towards the top of the page) [1]
**(b)** Reverse the current [1]; reverse the magnetic field (swap the poles) [1]
**(c)** The force is unchanged (still upwards) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), check: first finger into the page, second finger left to right, thumb points up.

**9. (a)** l = 15 cm = 0.15 m [1]; F = B I l = 0.35 × 6.0 × 0.15 [1]; = 0.315 N = **0.32 N** (2 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** New F = 0.315 × 2 × ⅓ [1]; = **0.21 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 15 instead of 0.15 gives 31.5 N and loses the conversion mark.

**10.** l = F / (B I) [1]; = 0.075 / (0.25 × 1.5) = 0.075 / 0.375 = 0.20 m [1]; = **20 cm** [1]

**11.** The current flows in opposite directions in the two sides of the coil [1]; each side is at right angles to the field, so a force acts on it (motor effect) [1]; the forces are in opposite directions, one up and one down [1]; so the coil turns; faster with any two of: larger current [1]; stronger magnetic field (or more turns on the coil) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The opposite-currents point is the one most often missed; without it the opposite forces are unexplained.

**12.** Marked by levels; a top-level answer covers:
- An alternating current flows in a coil placed in the field of a permanent magnet [1]
- There is a force on the coil (motor effect) [1]
- When the current reverses, the force reverses, so the coil moves back and forth [1]
- The coil is attached to a cone, so the cone vibrates at the frequency of the current [1]
- The cone produces pressure variations (compressions and rarefactions) in the air: a sound wave [1]
- A larger current gives a larger force and a larger vibration, so a louder sound (larger amplitude); keeping the same frequency keeps the same pitch [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Field around a wire drawn as straight lines.
- "The iron core increases the current."
- Fleming's rule done with the right hand, or with field and current fingers swapped.
- Centimetres not converted to metres in F = BIl.
- Motor explanations that skip the opposite currents in the two sides.
- Loudspeaker answers that never link the frequency of the current to the frequency of the sound.
