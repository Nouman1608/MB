## Questions

**1.** Describe the mammalian nervous system in terms of the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system. **[2]**

**2.** A diagram shows three neurones in a reflex arc.

- Neurone **A** has its cell body inside the spinal cord and one long fibre that ends in a leg muscle.
- Neurone **B** is short and branched, and lies entirely inside the spinal cord.
- Neurone **C** has its cell body on a short side branch outside the spinal cord, with one end in the skin.

**(a)** Identify neurones A, B and C. **[3]**
**(b)** State what travels along a neurone. **[1]**
**(c)** An impulse travels the 0.90 m length of neurone A in 0.015 s. Calculate the speed of the impulse in m/s. **[2]**

**3.** This question is about synapses.

**(a)** Name the structures in which neurotransmitter molecules are stored. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the events at a synapse after an impulse arrives. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why an impulse can cross a synapse in one direction only. **[2]**

**4.** A boy steps on a drawing pin and lifts his foot before he feels pain.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a reflex action. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the receptor and the effector in this response. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one advantage of this response. **[1]**

**5.** A student measured her reaction time by catching a dropped ruler, five times with each hand. Catching the ruler is a voluntary action, not a reflex.

| Trial | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Right hand / s | 0.22 | 0.19 | 0.21 | 0.20 | 0.23 |
| Left hand / s | 0.25 | 0.27 | 0.24 | 0.26 | 0.28 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean reaction time for each hand. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the difference between the two means. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why she repeated each measurement five times. **[1]**
**(d)** State one variable she should keep the same. **[1]**

**6.** This question is about sense organs.

**(a)** Describe a sense organ. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the part of the eye that:
(i) contains the greatest density of light receptors **[1]**
(ii) refracts light at the front of the eye **[1]**
(iii) carries impulses to the brain. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why light falling on the blind spot is not detected. **[1]**

**7.** A girl in a dark cinema looks down from the screen to read a ticket held close to her face.

**(a)** Explain how her eye focuses on the ticket. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how her iris keeps her pupils wide in the dark. **[3]**

**8.** This question is about hormones.

**(a)** Describe a hormone. **[3]**
**(b)** State the hormones produced by:
(i) the pituitary gland **[1]**
(ii) the testes **[1]**
(iii) the ovaries. **[1]**

**9.** A man's heart rate rose from 68 to 102 beats per minute when a car swerved towards him.

**(a)** Name the hormone responsible and the gland that produces it. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in heart rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this hormone helps him to run out of the way. **[3]**
**(d)** Compare nervous and hormonal control. **[2]**

**10.** A cyclist's core body temperature was measured during exercise in a warm room.

| Time / min | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature / °C | 37.0 | 37.4 | 37.8 | 38.1 | 38.2 | 38.2 |

**(a)** Describe homeostasis. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total rise in core temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the roles of the hypothalamus and the skin temperature receptors in temperature control. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain two processes that stop the temperature rising further after 20 minutes. **[4]**

**11.** Person X and person Y each drank a glucose solution at 0 minutes. Person Y has Type 1 diabetes.

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X: blood glucose / mg per 100 cm³ | 90 | 140 | 120 | 95 | 90 |
| Y: blood glucose / mg per 100 cm³ | 160 | 260 | 290 | 280 | 270 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in blood glucose for X between 0 and 30 minutes. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in X's blood glucose between 30 and 120 minutes, using the idea of negative feedback. **[4]**
**(c)** State one sign of Type 1 diabetes shown in the table and one other sign. **[2]**
**(d)** State the treatment for Type 1 diabetes. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain the need to control blood glucose concentration. **[2]**

**12.** Arctic foxes live where air temperatures can be far below 0 °C.

**(a)** Name the structure in the skin that:
(i) secretes sweat **[1]**
(ii) makes a hair stand up **[1]**
(iii) forms an insulating layer beneath the skin. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest and explain two ways an arctic fox reduces heat loss through its skin in very cold air. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The CNS consists of the brain and the spinal cord [1]. The PNS consists of the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "The PNS is all the nerves" is not enough; the answer needs "outside the brain and spinal cord".

**2. (a)** A: **motor neurone** [1]; B: **relay neurone** [1]; C: **sensory neurone** [1].
**(b)** Electrical impulses [1].
**(c)** speed = 0.90 ÷ 0.015 [1] = **60 m/s** [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the division out and give the unit; a bare number without m/s is incomplete.

**3. (a)** Vesicles [1].
**(b)** The impulse causes neurotransmitter to be released from vesicles into the synaptic gap [1]. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap [1] and binds with receptor proteins, so an impulse is stimulated in the next neurone [1].
**(c)** Vesicles containing neurotransmitter are only in the first neurone [1]; receptor proteins are only on the next neurone [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the process as "diffuses"; words like "travels" or "flows" leave the movement unexplained.

**4. (a)** A response that is rapid [1] and automatic, without conscious thought [1].
**(b)** Receptor: pain or touch receptors in the skin of the foot [1]. Effector: a muscle in the leg [1].
**(c)** Reduces damage to the foot, because the foot is removed quickly [1].
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "The foot" is not an effector; name a muscle or gland, because effectors are the structures that respond.

**5. (a)** Right: 1.05 ÷ 5 = **0.21 s** [1]; left: 1.30 ÷ 5 = **0.26 s** [1].
**(b)** 0.26 − 0.21 = **0.05 s** [1].
**(c)** To identify anomalous results and calculate a reliable mean [1].
**(d)** Any one [1]: the same ruler; the same starting position of the hand; the same person dropping the ruler.
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give each mean with its unit and to the same decimal places as the data.

**6. (a)** A group of receptor cells responding to a specific stimulus [1].
**(b)** (i) Fovea [1] (ii) Cornea [1] (iii) Optic nerve [1].
**(c)** The blind spot has no light receptors [1].
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b)(ii), "lens" does not fit: the question asks about the front of the eye, which is the cornea.

**7. (a)** The ciliary muscles contract [1]. The suspensory ligaments slacken, so tension in them decreases [1]. The lens becomes fatter, more convex [1], so light is refracted more and focused on the retina [1].
**(b)** The radial muscles of the iris contract [1] and the circular muscles relax [1]; the muscles act antagonistically, so the pupil dilates and more light enters [1].
**[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), each step of the chain (muscle, ligament tension, lens shape, refraction) is a separate point, so skipping the ligaments costs marks.

**8. (a)** A chemical substance [1], produced by a gland and carried by the blood [1], which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs [1].
**(b)** (i) FSH and LH [1] (ii) Testosterone [1] (iii) Oestrogen and progesterone [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b)(i) and (iii), one hormone alone is incomplete; give both names, spelt clearly.

**9. (a)** Adrenaline [1], from the adrenal glands [1].
**(b)** (102 − 68) ÷ 68 × 100 [1] = **50 %** [1]
**(c)** Adrenaline increases the blood glucose concentration [1] and the heart rate, so glucose and oxygen reach the leg muscles faster [1]. More respiration releases more energy for muscle contraction [1].
**(d)** Nervous control acts faster than hormonal control [1]; the effect of hormonal control lasts longer [1].
**[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), use comparative words for both points; "nervous is fast" alone does not make a comparison.

**10. (a)** The maintenance of a constant internal environment [1].
**(b)** 38.2 − 37.0 = **1.2 °C** [1].
**(c)** Temperature receptors in the skin detect changes in temperature and send impulses to the brain [1]. The hypothalamus monitors blood temperature and coordinates the responses [1].
**(d)** Sweating: sweat glands release more sweat, and evaporation of the water transfers heat away from the body [1] [1]. Vasodilation: arterioles supplying the skin surface capillaries widen [1], so more blood flows near the surface and more heat is lost by radiation [1].
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For vasodilation, name the arterioles; writing that capillaries move nearer the skin gives a wrong mechanism.

**11. (a)** (140 − 90) ÷ 90 × 100 [1] = 55.6 % = **56 %** [1]
**(b)** Blood glucose rose above the set point [1]. The pancreas releases more insulin [1]. Insulin causes the liver to convert glucose to glycogen [1], so blood glucose falls back to the set point, reaching 90 again at 120 minutes [1].
**(c)** Shown: increased blood glucose concentration (Y is higher at every time) [1]. Other: glucose in the urine [1].
**(d)** Administration of insulin [1].
**(e)** Cells need a steady supply of glucose for respiration [1]; a high concentration lowers the water potential of the blood, so water leaves cells by osmosis [1].
**[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table in (b) and (c) when data are given.

**12. (a)** (i) Sweat gland [1] (ii) Hair erector muscle [1] (iii) Fatty tissue [1].
**(b)** Hair erector muscles contract, so the fur stands up and traps a thicker layer of air [1], which insulates and reduces heat loss [1]. Vasoconstriction: arterioles supplying the skin surface capillaries narrow [1], so less blood flows near the surface and less heat is lost [1].
**[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Shivering makes heat; it does not reduce heat loss, so it does not answer (b).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the relay neurone out of a reflex arc.
- Writing that neurotransmitter "travels" across the gap instead of diffusing.
- Describing the eye's response to dim light with the circular and radial muscles swapped.
- For near vision, saying the suspensory ligaments tighten.
- Naming "glucagon" when the answer needs "glycogen", or the other way round.
- Stating that skin capillaries move towards or away from the surface.
- Writing that the sweat itself cools the skin, without saying water evaporates.
- Comparing nervous and hormonal control without comparative words.
