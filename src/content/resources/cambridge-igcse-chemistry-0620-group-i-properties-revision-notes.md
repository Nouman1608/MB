Condensed notes for subtopic **8.2 Group I properties**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. This subtopic is **Core only**: every candidate needs all of it. For fuller explanations and a worked prediction, read the [Group I study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-i-properties/); for exam-style questions, use the [Group I practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-i-properties-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alkali metals | Group I elements: lithium, sodium, potassium (then rubidium, caesium) |
| Relatively soft | Can be cut with a knife |
| Tarnish | Shiny surface goes dull as it reacts with air |
| Alkaline solution | pH above 7; contains OH⁻ ions |
| Trend | Steady change in a property down the group |

## Physical properties

- Relatively soft; cut with a knife.
- Shiny when freshly cut; tarnish quickly in air.
- Stored under oil (keeps out air and water).
- Low density: Li, Na and K float on water.
- Low melting points for metals.
- Conduct electricity; one outer electron; form **1+** ions.

## The three syllabus trends (down the group)

| Property | Trend | Data / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | **Decreases** | Li 181 °C, Na 98 °C, K 63 °C |
| Density | **Increases** (general trend) | Describe the overall pattern in data given |
| Reactivity | **Increases** | Reaction with water gets more vigorous |

Memory link: Group I melting points go **down**; Group VII melting points go **up**. Group I reactivity goes **up**; Group VII reactivity goes **down**.

## Reaction with cold water

**metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen**

```
2Li + 2H2O → 2LiOH + H2
2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2
2K  + 2H2O → 2KOH  + H2
```

| Metal | What you see |
|---|---|
| Lithium | Floats, fizzes steadily, slowly disappears |
| Sodium | Floats, melts into a ball, darts about, fizzes rapidly, disappears |
| Potassium | Floats, melts, fizzes violently, hydrogen burns with a lilac flame |

| Observation | Shows |
|---|---|
| Floats | Less dense than water |
| Melts | Reaction is exothermic; low melting point |
| Fizzes | Hydrogen gas produced |
| Indicator turns blue/purple | Alkaline hydroxide formed |

Test for the gas: lighted splint, squeaky pop (hydrogen).

## Formula patterns

All Group I metals form M⁺ ions, so:

| Compound | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hydroxide | MOH | KOH |
| Chloride | MCl | LiCl |
| Oxide | M₂O | Na₂O |
| Sulfate | M₂SO₄ | K₂SO₄ |
| Nitrate | MNO₃ | NaNO₃ |

## Predicting rubidium and caesium

1. Find the trend in the data given.
2. Continue it in the same direction.
3. Apply the group pattern: one outer electron, M⁺, MOH, MCl.

| Property | Prediction for rubidium / caesium |
|---|---|
| Melting point | Lower than potassium (Rb 39 °C, Cs 28 °C) |
| Reactivity with water | More vigorous than potassium (explosive) |
| Products with water | RbOH / CsOH + hydrogen |
| Hardness | Softer than potassium |
| Ion | Rb⁺, Cs⁺ |

## Group I compared with transition elements

| Property | Group I metals | Transition elements (8.4) |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness | Soft; cut with a knife | Hard, strong |
| Melting point | Low (all three below 200 °C) | High (iron 1538 °C) |
| Density | Low; float on water | High |
| Reaction with cold water | Vigorous | Slow or none |
| Ion charges | Always 1+ | Variable, e.g. Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ *(Extended)* |

Useful in "compare" questions: give a point for each group, using comparative words.

## Model answer

*Describe the Group I alkali metals lithium, sodium and potassium.* (4 marks)

> They are relatively soft metals that can be cut with a knife. Down the group the melting point decreases, the density increases and the reactivity increases (for example, potassium reacts with water more vigorously than lithium).

## Background reason for the reactivity trend

Down the group the outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is less strongly attracted and lost more easily. The syllabus asks you to *describe* the trend; this reason helps when justifying a prediction.

## Exam traps

- Product with water is the **hydroxide**, not the oxide.
- **Hydrogen** must be named as a product.
- Group I melting point **decreases** down the group.
- NaOH, not NaOH₂: sodium ion is Na⁺.
- All three metals **float**.
- Lithium does **not** explode; potassium is the most vigorous of the three.
- Predictions need a comparison: "more reactive than potassium", not just "reactive".
- Balance with 2: 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂.
- Listing "shiny" as a special Group I property earns nothing; most metals are shiny. Softness, low density and low melting point are the unusual ones.

## Self-test

1. Name the first three alkali metals in order down the group.
2. Why are alkali metals stored under oil?
3. State the trend in melting point down Group I.
4. State the trend in reactivity down Group I.
5. Write the word equation for sodium and water.
6. Give the balanced symbol equation for potassium and water.
7. Why does sodium float on water?
8. Why does sodium melt into a ball when it reacts with water?
9. State the colour of universal indicator in the solution after lithium reacts with water.
10. Predict the formula of caesium hydroxide.
11. Predict how caesium reacts with water compared with potassium.
12. What charge do all Group I ions have, and why?

**Answers**

1. Lithium, sodium, potassium.
2. To stop them reacting with oxygen and water vapour in the air.
3. Decreases.
4. Increases.
5. Sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen.
6. 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂.
7. It is less dense than water.
8. The reaction releases heat and sodium has a low melting point.
9. Blue or purple (alkaline).
10. CsOH.
11. More vigorously / explosively.
12. 1+; each atom has one outer electron, which it loses.
