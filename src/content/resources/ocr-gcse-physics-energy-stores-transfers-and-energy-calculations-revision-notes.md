## Conservation of energy (P7.1a)

- Energy is never created or destroyed. It moves from store to store.
- In a closed system there is no net change to the total energy.
- Wasted energy is not lost. It spreads into thermal stores of the surroundings and is hard to use again.
- Energy is not a fuel that gets used up, and objects at rest still have energy.
- This statement is qualitative only: describe it in words.

## Changes in stores (P7.1b)

| Situation | Goes down | Goes up |
|---|---|---|
| Thrown upwards or up a slope | Kinetic | Gravitational potential (and thermal if friction) |
| Moving object hits an obstacle | Kinetic | Thermal, sound, some elastic or deformation |
| Constant force accelerating an object | Store supplying the force | Kinetic |
| Vehicle slowing down | Kinetic | Thermal (brakes, tyres, road) |
| Electric kettle heating water | Electrical (current doing work) | Thermal in water, then kettle and surroundings |
| Wind-up toy or compressed spring released | Elastic | Kinetic |
| Object falling | Gravitational potential | Kinetic |

**Method: describing a change**
1. Name the system or object.
2. Say which store decreases.
3. Say which store increases.
4. Say how it moved: heating, work done by a force, or work done by a current.
5. State that the total is unchanged.

## Three ways a system changes (P7.1c)

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Heating | Energy moves from hot to cold, or raises the temperature of a material |
| Work done by forces | A force moves an object through a distance |
| Work done by a current | Charge moves through a potential difference |

## Equations (P7.1d, P7.1e)

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic energy | E = ½mv² | J, kg, m/s |
| Gravitational potential energy | E = mgh | J, kg, N/kg, m |
| Elastic energy | E = ½kx² | J, N/m, m |
| Work done | W = Fs | J, N, m |
| Thermal energy change | ΔE = mcΔθ | J, kg, J/kg °C, °C |
| Charge | Q = It | C, A, s |
| Electrical energy | E = QV | J, C, V |
| Power | P = VI | W, V, A |
| Energy from power | E = Pt | J (W and s) or kW h (kW and h) |

**Conversion:** 1 kW h = 3 600 000 J. Cost = kW h × price per kW h.

**Method: any energy calculation**
1. Write the equation.
2. Convert units: g to kg, cm to m, minutes to seconds or hours.
3. Substitute and calculate.
4. Give the unit, and a sensible number of significant figures.

## Worked reminders

**Kinetic energy.** A cyclist and bike of mass 80 kg move at 9.0 m/s.

```
E = ½ × 80 × 9.0² = 3240 J
```

**Elastic energy.** A spring with k = 200 N/m is stretched by 4.0 cm.

```
x = 0.040 m
E = ½ × 200 × 0.040² = 0.16 J
```

**Kilowatt-hours.** A 60 W lamp is on for 2.0 hours.

```
E = 0.060 kW × 2.0 h = 0.12 kW h
In joules: 60 W × 7200 s = 432 000 J
```

**Heating.** A mug holds 0.25 kg of water, warmed from 20 °C to 90 °C (c = 4200 J/kg °C).

```
ΔE = 0.25 × 4200 × 70 = 73 500 J
```

**Model sentences for common situations.** Learn the pattern, then swap in the situation.

- A skier slides down a slope: "The gravitational potential store decreases. The kinetic store increases, and the thermal store of the skis and snow increases because of friction. The total energy is unchanged."
- A ball hits a wall and stops: "The kinetic store decreases. The thermal store of the ball and wall increases, and some energy is transferred to the surroundings by sound."
- A kettle is switched on: "Work is done by the current. The electrical energy is transferred to the thermal store of the water, and some also warms the kettle and the air."

More worked reminders follow.

**Rising object.** A 0.50 kg ball leaves the hand at 8.0 m/s and rises with no air resistance (g = 9.8 N/kg).

```
Kinetic energy = ½ × 0.50 × 8.0² = 16 J
Greatest height: h = 16 / (0.50 × 9.8) = 3.27 = 3.3 m
```

**Braking.** A van of mass 2000 kg slows from 10 m/s to rest with a constant braking force of 5000 N.

```
Energy transferred = ½ × 2000 × 10² = 100 000 J
Braking distance = 100 000 / 5000 = 20 m
```

**Electrical.** A 12 V supply drives 0.50 A through a motor for 30 s.

```
Q = It = 0.50 × 30 = 15 C
E = QV = 15 × 12 = 180 J
Check: P = VI = 6.0 W, E = Pt = 6.0 × 30 = 180 J
```

**Kettle time.** A 2.0 kW kettle heats 1.0 kg of water through 60 °C (c = 4200 J/kg °C).

```
ΔE = 1.0 × 4200 × 60 = 252 000 J
t = 252 000 / 2000 = 126 s (if no energy is wasted)
```

## Practical links

The specification suggests these investigations. Know what is measured and what controls matter.

| Investigation | What you measure | Energy idea |
|---|---|---|
| Joulemeter with different appliances | Energy used by each appliance | Work done by current, E = Pt |
| Light gates and trolleys | Speed of a trolley | Kinetic energy, E = ½mv² |
| Joulemeter and motor lifting a weight | Energy supplied and height gained | Gravitational potential energy, E = mgh |
| Bouncy balls | Drop and rebound heights | Energy changes and wasted energy |

Repeating measurements and calculating a mean improves reliability. Measure rebound height at eye level against a fixed rule, and drop each ball from the same height.

## Must-know distinctions

- Work done and energy transferred are one and the same amount, in joules.
- Kinetic energy depends on v². Doubling speed gives four times the energy.
- Gravitational potential energy depends on vertical height only, not the length of a slope.
- Power is a rate (J/s). Energy is an amount (J or kW h).
- Charge (C) is not energy. E = QV links them through potential difference.
- Dissipated is not destroyed.

## Quick self-test

1. State the law of conservation of energy.
2. Calculate the kinetic energy of a 2.0 kg trolley moving at 5.0 m/s.
3. Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained when a 4.0 kg box is lifted 2.5 m (g = 9.8 N/kg).
4. A spring has k = 80 N/m and is stretched 0.10 m. How much energy is stored?
5. Calculate the energy needed to heat 0.40 kg of copper by 25 °C (c = 400 J/kg °C).
6. How much energy is transferred when 20 C of charge passes through a 6.0 V potential difference?
7. A 3 kW fire runs for 20 minutes. How many kW h is that?
8. Convert 0.50 kW h into joules.
9. A 0.50 kg ball has 40 J of gravitational potential energy at the top of its path. How high is it (g = 9.8 N/kg)?
10. A car slows down. Name the store that decreases and the store that increases.
11. A ball's speed doubles. What happens to its kinetic energy?

### Answers

1. Energy is never created or destroyed; it only moves from one store to another. In a closed system the total stays the same.
2. ½ × 2.0 × 5.0² = **25 J**.
3. 4.0 × 9.8 × 2.5 = **98 J**.
4. ½ × 80 × 0.10² = **0.40 J**.
5. 0.40 × 400 × 25 = **4000 J**.
6. E = QV = 20 × 6.0 = **120 J**.
7. 3 kW × (20/60) h = **1.0 kW h**.
8. 0.50 × 3 600 000 = **1 800 000 J** (1.8 × 10⁶ J).
9. h = 40 / (0.50 × 9.8) = 8.16 = **8.2 m**.
10. Kinetic store decreases. Thermal store (brakes, tyres, road) increases.
11. It becomes **four times** as large.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying energy is "used up" or "lost" instead of naming the new store.
- Naming a store that goes down without naming the one that goes up.
- Leaving the speed unsquared, or squaring the product ½mv instead of just v.
- Using extension in cm, or mass in g, without converting.
- Putting the distance along a slope into mgh in place of the vertical rise.
- Giving kW h and joules mixed up, or forgetting to convert minutes into hours.
- Rounding too early, then giving an answer that is not to a sensible number of significant figures.
- Leaving out units, or writing J when the answer is in kW h.
- Writing "the kettle uses 2 kW" when asked for energy rather than power.
