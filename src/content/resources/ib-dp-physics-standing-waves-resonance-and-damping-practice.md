Where a question needs the speed of sound in air, it is given as 340 m s⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Define the terms node and antinode for a standing wave. **[2]**

**2.** A pipe of length 0.45 m is closed at one end and open at the other. The speed of sound in air is 340 m s⁻¹. Determine the frequency of the first harmonic and of the next two harmonics the pipe can sustain. **[3]**

**3.** A string of length 0.50 m is fixed at both ends and vibrates in its fourth harmonic at 640 Hz.

**(a)** Determine the wavelength of the wave. **[1]**
**(b)** Determine the speed of waves on the string. **[1]**
**(c)** Determine the frequency of the first harmonic. **[2]**

**4.** A standing wave on a string has antinode amplitude 5.0 mm and wavelength 0.48 m. Point P is 0.04 m from a node. Point Q is 0.28 m from the same node, on the same side, and the node adjacent to P on that side lies at 0.24 m.

**(a)** Calculate the amplitude of P. **[2]**
**(b)** State the amplitude of Q and the phase difference between P and Q. **[3]**

**5.** A string is fixed at both ends and a wave is sent along it. Explain how a standing wave forms on the string. **[4]**

**6.** A tuning fork of frequency 512 Hz is held over a tube whose air column length can be varied. Loud resonances are found at air column lengths of 0.165 m and 0.505 m.

**(a)** State the relationship between the difference in these two lengths and the wavelength. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength and the speed of sound. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict the next air column length that gives resonance. **[2]**

**7.** An open pipe and a pipe closed at one end both have length 0.85 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the first harmonic of each. **[2]**
**(b)** List every harmonic frequency below 650 Hz for each pipe. **[2]**
**(c)** For the third harmonic of the closed pipe, state where the displacement nodes lie, measured from the closed end. **[2]**

**8.** A mass on a spring is driven by a vibrating support. The table shows the amplitude of its oscillation for two different amounts of damping.

| Driving frequency / Hz | 2.0 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 4.5 | 5.0 | 6.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amplitude, light damping / mm | 4 | 9 | 18 | 31 | 17 | 8 | 3 |
| Amplitude, heavier damping / mm | 3 | 6 | 7.5 | 7 | 5.5 | 4 | 2.5 |

**(a)** Identify the driving frequency of greatest response with light damping. **[1]**
**(b)** State two differences between the response curves for the two amounts of damping. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the ratio of the peak amplitudes. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why damping limits the amplitude at resonance. **[2]**

**9.** A door closer is designed so that a door released from an open position swings shut and stops at the frame without bouncing.

**(a)** State the type of damping the designer aims for. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the displacement-time graph for light, critical and heavy damping after a single release. **[3]**

**10.** Pedestrians cross a footbridge whose natural frequency is close to the rate at which people step.

**(a)** Explain why large oscillations of the bridge might develop. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two changes that would reduce the risk. **[2]**

**11.** A guitar string of length 0.65 m, fixed at both ends, has a first harmonic of 220 Hz.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of waves on the string. **[2]**
**(b)** The string is held near a pipe closed at one end. Calculate the length of the pipe that has a first harmonic of 220 Hz. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that the third harmonic of the pipe matches the third harmonic of the string. **[2]**
**(d)** An open pipe of the same length as in (b) is used instead. Explain whether it can resonate with this string. **[2]**

**12.** A string of length 0.60 m is fixed at one end and free at the other. Waves on it travel at 90 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Determine the frequency of its first harmonic and state which harmonics can occur. **[3]**
**(b)** A pipe of length 0.50 m is closed at both ends. Determine its first harmonic frequency. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A node is a point on a standing wave where the amplitude is permanently zero [1]. An antinode is a point where the amplitude is a maximum [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say amplitude, not displacement, and say "permanently" or "at all times" for the node; a point that is merely momentarily at rest does not describe a node.

**2.** Closed pipe: λ = 4L = 1.80 m [1]. f₁ = v/λ = 340 ÷ 1.80 = **189 Hz** [1]. Only odd harmonics occur, so the next two are 3 × 189 = **567 Hz** and 5 × 189 = **944 Hz** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving 378 Hz as the next frequency, by treating the pipe as open, is a common slip; justify the odd-harmonic series from the node at the closed end.

**3. (a)** Four half-wavelengths fit: λ = 2L/4 = **0.25 m** [1].
**(b)** v = fλ = 640 × 0.25 = **160 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** f₁ = f₄ ÷ 4 [1] = **160 Hz** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the substitution for each part, and in part (c) divide the given 640 Hz by 4 rather than recalculating from your own earlier answers; clear working makes your reasoning easy to follow.

**4. (a)** Phase angle = 2π × 0.04 ÷ 0.48 = π/6 [1]. Amplitude = 5.0 × sin(π/6) = **2.5 mm** [1].
**(b)** Q is 0.04 m from the node at 0.24 m, so its amplitude is **2.5 mm** [1]. P and Q lie in adjacent loops, on either side of the node at 0.24 m [1], so the phase difference is **π (antiphase)** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Both amplitude and phase are asked for, so give the numerical phase difference as well as the word antiphase; reasoning from loops is clearer than quoting a sign from a formula.

**5.** The wave travels along the string and reflects at the fixed end [1]. The reflected wave travels in the opposite direction with the same frequency and amplitude [1]. The incident and reflected waves superpose [1]. Where they are always in antiphase there are nodes, and where always in phase there are antinodes, so the pattern stays in place; only wavelengths that fit the fixed ends give a stable pattern [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the two waves that superpose and say they are identical and opposite; saying "waves bounce back and forth" without superposition is incomplete.

**6. (a)** Successive resonances differ in length by half a wavelength [1].
**(b)** λ = 2 × (0.505 − 0.165) = **0.680 m** [1]. v = fλ = 512 × 0.680 = **348 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Next resonance: 0.505 + 0.340 [1] = **0.845 m** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Treating the first length alone as a quarter-wavelength is risky; stating that you use the difference between successive resonances shows a sound method.

**7. (a)** Open: f₁ = 340 ÷ (2 × 0.85) = **200 Hz** [1]. Closed: f₁ = 340 ÷ (4 × 0.85) = **100 Hz** [1].
**(b)** Open pipe: **200, 400, 600 Hz** [1]. Closed pipe: **100, 300, 500 Hz** (odd multiples only) [1].
**(c)** For the third harmonic, λ = 4L/3 = 1.13 m [1]. Displacement nodes are at the closed end and at λ/2 = **0.57 m** from it; the open end is an antinode [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Distance from which end must be stated; quote the position relative to the closed end and note the antinode at the open end to avoid ambiguity.

**8. (a)** **4.0 Hz** [1].
**(b)** Heavier damping gives a lower peak amplitude [1] and a broader response; its peak is also at a slightly lower frequency, about 3.5 Hz rather than 4.0 Hz [1].
**(c)** 31 ÷ 7.5 = **4.1** [1].
**(d)** Damping dissipates energy each cycle [1]. At the peak the energy supplied by the driver per cycle equals the energy dissipated per cycle, so the amplitude stops growing, and more damping means this balance is reached at a smaller amplitude [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Differences" questions want comparisons, not two separate descriptions, so use words such as lower, broader and shifted; the peak shift is only supported by the table to the resolution of the data.

**9. (a)** **Critical damping** [1].
**(b)** Light damping: the door would oscillate about the closed position with amplitude decreasing gradually [1]. Critical damping: displacement falls to zero in the shortest time with no oscillation [1]. Heavy damping: no oscillation, but the displacement falls to zero more slowly than for critical damping [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison of times is needed for critical and heavy damping; saying both "do not oscillate" will not distinguish them.

**10. (a)** The footsteps act as a periodic driving force at a frequency close to the natural frequency of the bridge [1]. Resonance occurs, so energy is transferred to the bridge efficiently and the amplitude grows large [1].
**(b)** Increase the damping (for example with energy-absorbing dampers) [1]. Change the stiffness or mass of the bridge so its natural frequency is moved away from the stepping frequency [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each suggestion to the physics of resonance; "make the bridge stronger" does not address the driving and natural frequencies.

**11. (a)** λ = 2L = 1.30 m [1]. v = fλ = 220 × 1.30 = **286 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** L = v/(4f) = 340 ÷ (4 × 220) [1] = **0.386 m** [1].
**(c)** Pipe third harmonic = 3 × 220 = 660 Hz [1]. String third harmonic = 3 × 220 = 660 Hz, so they match [1].
**(d)** The open pipe of the same length has first harmonic 340 ÷ (2 × 0.386) = 440 Hz [1]. This equals the second harmonic of the string, so the string can drive it at resonance at that frequency [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs both values calculated and compared; stating that the harmonics are equal without evidence does not demonstrate the match.

**12. (a)** Fixed end is a node and free end an antinode, so L = λ/4 and λ = 2.4 m [1]. f₁ = 90 ÷ 2.4 = **37.5 Hz** [1]. Only odd harmonics occur [1].
**(b)** Both ends are displacement nodes, so L = λ/2 and λ = 1.00 m [1]. f₁ = 340 ÷ 1.00 = **340 Hz** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Decide node or antinode at each end first; the free end of a string is an antinode, unlike a fixed end.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the open-pipe formula for a pipe closed at one end, or allowing even harmonics there.
- Numbering harmonics from a higher mode instead of the lowest frequency.
- Treating the length of a resonance tube as one wavelength, rather than using successive resonances half a wavelength apart.
- Stating damping effects without comparing: the peak is lower, broader and slightly lower in frequency.
- Failing to distinguish critical from heavy damping, which both avoid oscillation.
