## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the centre of mass of an object. **[1]**

**2.** A uniform rectangular tray is 50 cm long and 30 cm wide. State the distance of its centre of mass from each long edge and from each short edge. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why the centre of mass of a uniform circular plate is at its centre. **[2]**

**4.** A key ring is a uniform metal ring. State where its centre of mass is, and explain what is unusual about this position. **[2]**

**5.** A mirror hangs freely from one hook. State where its centre of mass is in relation to the hook. **[1]**

**6.** State **two** ways of making a desk lamp less likely to topple. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student wants to find the centre of mass of a piece of card cut into an irregular shape.

**(a)** List the apparatus needed, apart from the card. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the method. **[5]**

**8.** In the experiment in question 7, the student's three lines do not meet at one point but form a triangle about 1 cm across.

**(a)** State where the centre of mass is most likely to be. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** reasons why the lines did not meet at one point. **[2]**

**(c)** For each reason in (b), suggest an improvement. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why an object suspended from a pivot does not stay at rest with its centre of mass to one side of the pivot. **[3]**

**10.** A tall, narrow vase and a short, wide bowl are placed on a shelf in a train.

**(a)** Explain why the vase is more likely to topple when the train brakes or goes round a bend. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest how adding sand to the bottom of the vase would change its stability. **[2]**

**11.** A toy figure balances on a fingertip by its head. Long arms curve down from its shoulders and hold heavy weights below the level of the fingertip.

**(a)** State where the toy's centre of mass is, relative to the fingertip. **[1]**

**(b)** The toy is tipped slightly to one side. Explain why it returns to its original position. **[3]**

**12.** A uniform metre rule of weight 1.0 N has a 0.50 N mass hung at the 10 cm mark.

**(a)** Explain why the centre of mass of the rule plus mass is no longer at the 50 cm mark. **[1]**

**(b)** The rule balances on a pivot placed at its combined centre of mass. By taking moments, calculate the position of this pivot. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The point at which the mass of the object may be thought to be concentrated (the point at which its weight acts) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 15 cm from each long edge [1]; 25 cm from each short edge [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Every diameter of the plate is an axis of symmetry [1]; the centre of mass lies on every axis of symmetry, and all diameters cross only at the centre [1]. **[2]**

**4.** At the centre of the ring [1]; it is in the empty space, not in the metal itself [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Directly below the hook [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two [2]: make the base wider; make the base heavier (lowering the centre of mass); make the lamp shorter; keep heavy parts low.

**7. (a)** Any two [2]: stand and clamp; pin or nail to hang the card from; plumb line (thread and small weight); pencil and ruler.

**(b)** Make holes near the edge of the card [1]. Hang the card from the pin through one hole so it swings freely [1]. Hang the plumb line from the pin and wait until it is still [1]. Mark points along the plumb line and rule a line through them [1]. Repeat with another hole; the centre of mass is where the lines cross [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** Inside the triangle, near its middle [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: the card did not swing freely because of friction at the pin; the plumb line was still moving when marked; the pencil lines or thread were thick; the card bent; the marks were not exactly on the plumb line.

**(c)** Matching improvements [2], for example: use a smaller, smoother pin; wait until the plumb line is still; use thin thread and a sharp pencil; use stiffer card; mark with the eye directly in front of the thread. **[5]**

**9.** The weight acts at the centre of mass [1]. If the centre of mass is to one side, the weight's line of action does not pass through the pivot, so the weight has a moment about the pivot [1]. This turns the object until the centre of mass is directly below the pivot, when the moment is zero [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The vase has a higher centre of mass and a narrower base [1]. When it tilts, the line of action of its weight reaches the edge of its base at a small angle of tilt [1]. Once the line is outside the base, the weight's moment turns the vase over [1].

**(b)** The sand lowers the centre of mass [1], so the vase must tilt further before the line of action passes outside the base, making it more stable [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Below the fingertip [1].

**(b)** When the toy tilts, its centre of mass moves to one side of the fingertip but stays below it [1]. The weight then has a moment about the fingertip [1] that turns the toy back towards the position with its centre of mass directly below the fingertip [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** The added mass is not spread evenly along the rule, so the combined mass is no longer uniform (it is concentrated more towards the 10 cm end) [1].

**(b)** Let the pivot be at x cm. Moment of the 0.50 N mass = 0.50 × (x − 10) [1]. Moment of the rule's weight (at 50 cm) = 1.0 × (50 − x) [1]. Balanced: 0.50 (x − 10) = 1.0 (50 − x), so 0.50x − 5 = 50 − x [1], so 1.5x = 55 and x = **36.7 cm** (about 37 cm) [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the weight of the rule acts at the 50 cm mark only because the rule itself is uniform; distances must be measured from the pivot.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the lamina method without a plumb line or a repeat.
- Explaining toppling with "top-heavy" alone.
- Assuming the centre of mass must be inside the material.
- Placing a suspended object's centre of mass level with the pivot.
