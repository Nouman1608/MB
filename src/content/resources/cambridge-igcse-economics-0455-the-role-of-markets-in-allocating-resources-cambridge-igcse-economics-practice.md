These questions cover section 2.2 (the role of markets in allocating resources) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All places, firms and figures are invented. Revise first with the [role of markets study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-role-of-markets-in-allocating-resources-cambridge-igcse-economics/) and the [role of markets revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-role-of-markets-in-allocating-resources-cambridge-igcse-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** In a market system, which question is answered by "those who are willing and able to pay"? **[1]**

A: What to produce

B: How to produce

C: For whom to produce

D: Where to produce

**2.** At the current price of a good there is a shortage. What is likely to happen? **[1]**

A: The price falls and quantity supplied contracts

B: The price rises and quantity supplied extends

C: The price stays the same because the market is in equilibrium

D: The price falls and quantity demanded extends

**3.** Define the price mechanism. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** functions of prices in a market system. **[2]**

**5.** A furniture maker sells tables for 150. Each table costs 110 to make. Demand rises and the price increases to 170.

**(a)** Calculate the profit per table before and after the price rise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in profit per table. **[1]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a product market and a factor market. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** A clothing firm must sew 2,000 shirts a week. Method A uses 20 workers and no machinery. Method B uses 5 workers and machinery hired for 2,400 a week. Each worker is paid 200 a week.

**(a)** Calculate the total weekly cost and the cost per shirt of each method. **[4]**

**(b)** State which method the firm will choose and explain how a fall in wages could change this decision. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why the economic problem means every economy must answer the three key allocation questions. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how the price mechanism would reallocate resources if consumers switched from buying meat to buying plant-based foods. **[6]**

**10.** A football club has 4 tickets left for a final. Seven fans want one each. The most each is willing to pay is: 120, 95, 90, 80, 70, 65, 40.

**(a)** At a price of 65, calculate the shortage. **[2]**

**(b)** State the highest price at which all 4 tickets would still be sold, and explain why the club might choose it. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse how changes in factor markets help to move resources between industries. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether the price mechanism allocates resources in the best way for society. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. The price rations output to those who can pay, which answers "for whom". **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Buyers compete for scarce stock, so the price rises; producers respond by supplying more (an extension of supply). **[1]**

**3.** The system in which the interaction of demand and supply sets prices [1], and those prices guide the decisions of buyers and sellers about how resources are used [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: signalling [1]; incentive; rationing [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Before: 150 − 110 = **40** [1]; after: 170 − 110 = **60** [1].

**(b)** (60 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **50%** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** In a product market, households buy goods and services from firms [1]. In a factor market, firms buy or hire factors of production, such as labour, from households, paying wages, rent or interest [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Method A: 20 × 200 = **4,000** [1]; 4,000 ÷ 2,000 = **2.00 per shirt** [1]. Method B: 5 × 200 + 2,400 = **3,400** [1]; 3,400 ÷ 2,000 = **1.70 per shirt** [1].

**(b)** Method B, because its cost per shirt is lower [1]. If wages fell far enough (for example to 100, giving A = 2,000 and B = 2,900), the labour-intensive method would become cheaper and the firm would switch to it [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Resources are scarce while wants are unlimited [1], so not every want can be met and choices must be made [1]. Every economy therefore has to decide which goods to make, which methods to use and who receives the output [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Demand for plant-based foods rises, causing a shortage at the old price [1], so their price rises [1]. Producers earn higher profits, so they expand and new firms enter [1]. Demand for meat falls, causing a surplus, so its price and profits fall and producers cut output [1]. Firms making plant-based foods need more workers, land and capital and offer higher wages and rents [1], so factors move out of meat production and into plant-based food production [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Six fans would pay at least 65 [1]; shortage = 6 − 4 = **2 tickets** [1].

**(b)** **80**: at 80, four fans (120, 95, 90, 80) still want tickets, matching the 4 tickets; at any higher price only three would buy [1]. The club earns the highest price at which all tickets still sell, so no tickets are left unsold and no shortage remains [1]. **[4]**

**11.** When an industry expands, firms demand more labour, land and capital [1], so wages, rents and interest offered in that industry rise [1]. Higher rewards attract factors from industries where demand is falling and rewards are lower [1]. The movement of factors means output rises where consumers want more and falls where they want less [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For: prices respond quickly to changes in consumer wants, so resources move to the products people value most [1]; the profit motive gives firms an incentive to cut costs, choosing efficient methods [1]; no government needs to gather all the information on wants and costs [1]; consumers have wide choice [1]. Against: products go to those who can pay, so people on low incomes may lack essentials [1]; goods that people cannot be charged for, such as street lighting, may not be produced [1]; producers and consumers ignore costs imposed on others, such as pollution [1]. Conclusion: the price mechanism is efficient for most everyday goods, but it is not best for society where need differs from ability to pay or where others are affected, which is why most economies are mixed [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* The final mark rewards a judgement that says where the price mechanism works well and where it does not, rather than "it depends" alone.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the price mechanism allocates goods to those in greatest need.
- Explaining a price change without its effect on profit and on factor movement.
- Calculating total cost per method but not the cost per unit when asked.
- In rationing questions, counting the fans willing to pay less than the price.
- Listing advantages of markets without any limitation in a "Discuss" answer.
