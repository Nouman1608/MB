This guide covers subtopic **8.2 Group I properties** in Topic 8, The Periodic Table, of Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028 series. The [Periodic Table groups and trends study guide](/resources/periodic-table-groups-and-trends/) gives a short summary of the alkali metals. This page builds the detail you need to describe them confidently: what "relatively soft" looks like in the laboratory, the three trends with data, full equations and observations for the reactions with water, and a method for predicting the behaviour of rubidium and caesium. Afterwards, use the [Group I revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-i-properties-revision-notes/) and the [Group I practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-i-properties-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks for

This subtopic is **Core only**; there is no Supplement content, so every candidate needs all of it.

- Describe the Group I alkali metals, lithium, sodium and potassium, as relatively soft metals with general trends down the group, limited to (a) decreasing melting point, (b) increasing density, (c) increasing reactivity.
- Predict the properties of other elements in Group I, given information about the elements.

The words "limited to" matter: these are the three trends you can be asked to describe. Other properties may appear as *given information* in a prediction question.

## The alkali metals as metals

Lithium, sodium and potassium are metals, but unusual ones:

- **Relatively soft.** They can be cut with a knife; softness increases down the group.
- **Shiny when freshly cut**, but the surface tarnishes quickly as the metal reacts with oxygen and water vapour in the air.
- **Stored under oil** to keep air and water away.
- **Low density.** All three float on water.
- **Low melting points** for metals. Sodium melts below 100 °C.
- Like other metals, they **conduct electricity** and form **positive ions**: each atom has one outer electron and forms a **1+ ion** (Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺).

They are called alkali metals because they react with water to form alkaline solutions.

## The three trends

| Trend down the group (Li → Na → K) | Direction |
|---|---|
| Melting point | Decreases |
| Density | Increases (general trend) |
| Reactivity | Increases |

**Melting point data** (real values): lithium 181 °C, sodium 98 °C, potassium 63 °C. Each is lower than the one above.

**Density.** The syllabus describes a *general* trend of increasing density. When you are given a table of density values, describe the overall pattern from top to bottom; do not be thrown if one pair of neighbouring values does not fit perfectly.

**Reactivity** is shown most clearly by the reactions with cold water.

## Reactions with water

Each metal reacts with cold water to form a metal hydroxide and hydrogen:

```
2Li(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2LiOH(aq) + H2(g)
2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)
2K(s)  + 2H2O(l) → 2KOH(aq)  + H2(g)
```

General form: **metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen**.

| Metal | Observations in cold water |
|---|---|
| Lithium | Floats; fizzes steadily; slowly gets smaller |
| Sodium | Floats; melts into a silvery ball; moves around the surface; fizzes rapidly; gets smaller and disappears |
| Potassium | Floats; melts; moves rapidly; fizzes violently; the hydrogen ignites with a lilac flame |

What the observations tell you:

- **Floats**: density less than water.
- **Melts into a ball**: the reaction gives out heat, and the metal's melting point is low.
- **Fizzing**: hydrogen is produced.
- **Increasing vigour from lithium to potassium**: increasing reactivity.

The resulting solution is **alkaline**. Universal indicator added to the water turns blue or purple, and the solution contains OH⁻ ions.

### Why reactivity increases (background)

The syllabus asks you to *describe* the reactivity trend. A short reason is helpful when you are predicting: down the group the single outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is less strongly attracted and is lost more easily when the metal reacts. Losing that electron is what happens in every reaction of a Group I metal.

## Other reactions: same pattern, same formulae

Because all three have one outer electron and form 1+ ions, their compounds have the same formula pattern:

| Reacts with | Product | Formula pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Hydroxide + hydrogen | MOH |
| Chlorine | Chloride | MCl (e.g. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl) |
| Oxygen | Oxide | M₂O |

Their compounds are ionic, containing the M⁺ ion, and all sodium and potassium salts are soluble in water (one of the solubility rules in 7.3).

## Predicting other Group I elements

The prediction outcome is tested with **given information**, often a table that includes rubidium and caesium. Use three steps:

1. **Identify the trend** in the data given (direction and values).
2. **Continue it** for the missing element.
3. **Use the group pattern** for chemical properties: one outer electron, 1+ ion, MOH, MCl, M₂O.

### Worked example

| Element | Melting point / °C | Reaction with cold water |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 | Fizzes steadily |
| Sodium | 98 | Fizzes rapidly, melts into a ball |
| Potassium | 63 | Violent; hydrogen ignites |
| Rubidium | ? | ? |

*(a) Predict the melting point of rubidium.* The values decrease down the group, so rubidium's is below 63 °C. A prediction of about 40 °C is sensible (the actual value is 39 °C).

*(b) Predict how rubidium reacts with water.* More vigorously than potassium, because reactivity increases down the group; it would react explosively, forming rubidium hydroxide and hydrogen.

*(c) Write the equation.* 2Rb + 2H₂O → 2RbOH + H₂.

*(d) Predict the formula of rubidium chloride.* RbCl, because rubidium forms Rb⁺ like the other Group I metals.

## Annotated model answer

*Describe the trend in reactivity of the Group I metals, using their reactions with water as evidence.* **[3]**

> Reactivity increases down the group **[1]**. Lithium fizzes steadily on the surface of water, while sodium melts into a ball and fizzes rapidly **[1]**. Potassium reacts violently and the hydrogen produced catches fire with a lilac flame **[1]**.

The answer states the trend first, then gives comparative evidence for each metal. Simply listing observations without stating the trend would miss the first mark.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying melting point increases** down the group. It decreases for Group I (the opposite of Group VII).
- **Writing "oxide" instead of "hydroxide"** as the product with water.
- **Leaving out hydrogen** as a product.
- **Writing NaOH₂ or Na(OH)₂.** Sodium forms Na⁺, so the hydroxide is NaOH.
- **Saying the metals sink.** All three float.
- **Describing lithium as exploding.** Lithium reacts steadily; potassium is the most vigorous of the three.
- **Giving only "it is reactive" for a prediction.** Compare with the element above: "more reactive than potassium".

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe** the alkali metals: relatively soft metals, then the three trends with directions.
- **Describe** a reaction: give observations you would *see*, not just products.
- **Predict**: state the property and base it on the trend or the group pattern.
- **State** the products: name both, hydroxide and hydrogen.
- Equations: balance with 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂ and include state symbols if asked.
