## Section A -- Paper 1 style (document-based)

**1.** State two interlocking causes of the Civil War identified in this option. **[2]**

**2.** Identify one central unresolved question that Reconstruction had to address. **[1]**

**3.** A source from 1932 describes widespread bank failures and rising unemployment. Using this source and your own knowledge, explain one structural weakness of the 1920s economy that this source's evidence supports. **[4]**

## Section B -- Paper 2 style (outline study, essay format)

**4.** "Economic factors were the most important cause of the American Civil War." Assess this view. **[20]**

**5.** How far did the Progressive Era genuinely resolve the problems created by the Gilded Age? **[20]**

**6.** "The New Deal should be judged as a single, unified policy rather than as a set of distinct measures." How far do you agree? **[20]**

## Worked answers

**1.** Any two of: expansion of slavery into new territories, economic divergence between the industrialising North and the agrarian, slavery-dependent South, disputes over federal versus state power. **[2]**

**2.** What freedom and citizenship would actually mean for formerly enslaved people in the post-war South. **[1]**

**3.** A strong answer uses the source's specific evidence (bank failures, rising unemployment) as a starting point to explain a structural weakness such as the era's under-regulated banking system, which had insufficient safeguards against the kind of cascading failures the source describes once speculative excess and agricultural weakness began to unwind, connecting the source's specific detail to the broader structural argument rather than treating the source as a stand-alone illustration. **[4]** (Marks for accurate use of the source's evidence, and a correctly explained structural cause linked to it.)

**4.** A strong answer does not treat economic factors as a self-contained cause competing separately against slavery and states' rights -- it argues that economic divergence between North and South was itself substantially entangled with the expansion of slavery (since the South's agrarian economy was structurally dependent on slave labour, and disputes over extending slavery into new territories were as much about the future economic character of those territories as about the moral status of slavery itself). A strong response therefore reaches a supported judgement about which factor provides the most convincing overall explanation using specific evidence (a named crisis, law, or political event), rather than presenting a simple ranked list of independent causes with no interaction between them, or asserting a factor's importance without evidence. **[20]** (Marks distributed across AO1 knowledge, AO2 analysis of interlocking causation, and a reasoned, evidenced judgement.)

**5.** A strong answer first establishes the specific Gilded Age problems (inequality, corruption, poor urban and working conditions) and then evaluates named Progressive Era reforms against each one directly -- for example, assessing whether specific labour or consumer-protection legislation genuinely reduced the inequality or poor working conditions previously identified, rather than simply listing reforms as though their existence alone proves resolution. The strongest answers reach a genuinely qualified judgement (e.g. some problems were substantially addressed by specific measures while others persisted or were only partially resolved), avoiding both an uncritical "yes, fully resolved" and a dismissive "no, nothing changed" extreme, since the evidence for most Progressive reforms supports a mixed, evidence-based verdict rather than either absolute position. **[20]** (Marks for specific problem-to-reform matching, evidenced evaluation, and a genuinely qualified conclusion.)

**6.** A strong answer disagrees with the premise directly, arguing the New Deal should be evaluated as a set of distinct measures aimed at relief, recovery and reform respectively, each judged on its own terms and against its own specific aim -- relief measures judged on how quickly and widely they reduced immediate hardship, recovery measures judged against economic indicators over time, and reform measures judged on their longer-term structural impact. The strongest answers evaluate at least one specific measure from each category with named evidence, demonstrating that treating "the New Deal" as a single unified policy actively obscures meaningful differences in what different measures were designed to achieve and how successfully each did so, rather than clarifying the historical picture. **[20]** (Marks for correctly distinguishing the three aims, evidenced evaluation of specific measures within each, and a clear, well-supported rejection or qualification of the premise.)

## A note on Section B mark allocation

Each Section B question is worth 20 marks, split broadly across three assessment strands: accurate,
relevant knowledge of the specific topic; the ability to analyse causation, change or significance
rather than simply narrate events; and a sustained, evidenced judgement that directly answers the
question set, typically weighted most heavily. A response that narrates events accurately across an
entire 45-minute answer but never states and defends a clear position against the specific claim in
the question will not access the top mark band, regardless of factual accuracy -- this is why every
worked answer above explicitly states a judgement early and defends it with named evidence throughout,
rather than building toward a conclusion only in a final sentence.

## Why this set separates Paper 1 and Paper 2 styles

Section A mirrors Paper 1's document-based short-answer format, testing precise recall and source use
in smaller steps; Section B mirrors Paper 2's outline-study essay format, testing the sustained,
evidence-based judgement the revision notes identify as the skill that most reliably separates strong
from weak answers across this whole option. Practising both formats separately, rather than only the
essay style, reflects how both papers actually draw on the same four topics in genuinely different
question formats.
