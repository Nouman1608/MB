## Questions

**1.** A function machine adds 7 to the input x, then multiplies the result by 3.

**(a)** Find the output when x = −4. **[1]**
**(b)** The output is 42. Find the input. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the output as an expression in x. **[1]**

**2.** T(2, −3) and U(−4, −3) are two vertices of an isosceles triangle TUV, with TV = UV. V is in the 2nd quadrant and is 5 units above the line TU.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of V. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the equation of the line of symmetry of the triangle. **[1]**
**(c)** Work out the area of triangle TUV. **[2]**

**3.** A line has equation y = 2x − 3.

**(a)** Complete a table of values for x = −2, 0 and 3. **[2]**
**(b)** On squared paper, draw the graph for values of x from −2 to 3. **[2]**
**(c)** At what point does the line meet the x-axis? **[2]**

**4.** A line has equation 5x − 2y = 8.

**(a)** Rearrange the equation into the form y = mx + c. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the gradient and the y-intercept. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the x-intercept. **[1]**
**(d)** Which of these lines is parallel to 5x − 2y = 8? y = 2.5x + 1, y = −2.5x + 1, y = 0.4x + 1. **[1]**

**5.** Line L₁ has equation y = 3x + 2. Line L₂ passes through (0, −4) and (2, 2). Show that L₁ and L₂ are parallel. **[3]**

**6.** (Extension only) f(x) = 4x − 3 and g(x) = x² + 1.

**(a)** Find f(5) and g(−3). **[2]**
**(b)** Find fg(x), giving your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(c)** Solve fg(x) = 37. **[2]**

**7.** (Extension only) h(x) = 3 − x², for values of x from −2 to 3 inclusive.

**(a)** Find the range of h. **[3]**
**(b)** The function m(x) = 4/(x + 5) has no output for one particular input. Write down that input. **[1]**

**8.** (Extension only) f(x) = (5x + 2)/3.

**(a)** Find f⁻¹(x). **[3]**
**(b)** Find f⁻¹(4). **[1]**
**(c)** Solve f(x) = f⁻¹(x). **[3]**

**9.** (Extension only) A is the point (−5, −1) and B is the point (3, 5).

**(a)** Find the equation of line AB, in the form 4y = ax + b. **[3]**
**(b)** The point (k, 8) lies on line AB. Find k. **[2]**

**10.** (Extension only) Line L has equation 5x + 2y = 18.

**(a)** Find the gradient of L. **[2]**
**(b)** A second line is at right angles to L and goes through (5, −2). Write its equation in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the x-coordinate of the point where this perpendicular meets the x-axis. **[1]**

**11.** (Extension only) A(1, 2), B(7, 4) and C(6, 7) are three points.

**(a)** Show that AB is perpendicular to BC. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of line BC. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the line through A that is parallel to BC, and where it crosses the y-axis. **[2]**
**(d)** ABCD is a rectangle. Find the coordinates of D. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (−4 + 7) × 3 = 3 × 3 = **9** [1]
**(b)** 42 ÷ 3 = 14 [1], then 14 − 7 = **7** [1]
**(c)** **3(x + 7)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Working backwards by undoing each step in reverse order is quick and shows method. If you write 3x + 7 in part (c), the brackets are lost and the order of the machine is not recorded.

**2. (a)** V lies on the line of symmetry, halfway between x = −4 and x = 2, so its x-coordinate is −1 [1]. Its y-coordinate is −3 + 5 = 2, so **V = (−1, 2)** [1]
**(b)** The line of symmetry is vertical and passes through V, so it is **x = −1** [1]
**(c)** Base TU = 2 − (−4) = 6 [1], so the area is ½ × 6 × 5 = **15 square units** [1]
*Examiner insight:* When a coordinate is negative, subtract carefully. Writing 2 − (−4) rather than 2 − 4 shows the method, and a quick sketch of the triangle helps you catch a misplaced vertex.

**3. (a)** x = −2 gives y = −7 [1]. x = 0 gives y = −3 and x = 3 gives y = 3 [1]
**(b)** Plot (−2, −7), (0, −3) and (3, 3) accurately [1], then draw one straight ruled line through them from x = −2 to x = 3 [1]
**(c)** At the x-axis y = 0, so 2x − 3 = 0 [1], giving x = 1.5. The point is **(1.5, 0)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A line drawn freehand or joining points with short segments risks losing credit. Write the coordinate pair with the x-value first, and read it from your own graph as a check on the calculation.

**4. (a)** 5x − 8 = 2y [1], so **y = 2.5x − 4** [1]
**(b)** Gradient **2.5**, y-intercept **−4** [1]
**(c)** Set y = 0: 5x = 8, so **x = 1.6** [1]
**(d)** **y = 2.5x + 1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing every term by 2 is a frequent slip, so check that the −8 also becomes −4. Reading the gradient as 5 from the original equation is a common error.

**5.** Gradient of L₁ = 3 [1]. Gradient of L₂ = (2 − (−4))/(2 − 0) [1] = 6/2 = 3. The gradients are equal, so the lines are **parallel** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs the working and a closing statement. Compare the gradients explicitly, and say what equal gradients mean. A bare answer of 3 would not make the argument.

**6. (a)** f(5) = 4(5) − 3 = **17** [1]. g(−3) = (−3)² + 1 = **10** [1]
**(b)** fg(x) = 4(x² + 1) − 3 [1] = **4x² + 1** [1]
**(c)** 4x² + 1 = 37, so 4x² = 36 and x² = 9 [1], giving **x = 3 or x = −3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Put g inside f, not the other way round. Putting f(x) into g would give a different expression. Give both roots when you take a square root.

**7. (a)** h(−2) = 3 − 4 = −1, h(0) = 3 and h(3) = 3 − 9 = −6 [1]. The largest value is 3, at x = 0 [1]. The smallest value is −6, so the range is **−6 ≤ h(x) ≤ 3** [1]
**(b)** **x = −5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The largest output of h is not at either end of the domain. Checking the turning point is what separates a full answer from the endpoints-only response of −1 to −6.

**8. (a)** y = (5x + 2)/3, so 3y = 5x + 2 [1], then 5x = 3y − 2 [1], so **f⁻¹(x) = (3x − 2)/5** [1]
**(b)** (12 − 2)/5 = **2** [1]
**(c)** (5x + 2)/3 = (3x − 2)/5, so 5(5x + 2) = 3(3x − 2) [1], which gives 25x + 10 = 9x − 6 [1], so 16x = −16 and **x = −1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Test your inverse with part (b): f(2) should equal 4. In part (c), multiply both sides by 15 in one step to clear the two denominators and keep the signs right.

**9. (a)** Gradient = (5 − (−1))/(3 − (−5)) = 6/8 = 3/4 [1]. y − 5 = (3/4)(x − 3) [1], so 4y − 20 = 3x − 9 and **4y = 3x + 11** [1]
**(b)** 4(8) = 3k + 11 [1], so 3k = 21 and **k = 7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Substitute the other point to check the equation: for A, 4(−1) = −4 and 3(−5) + 11 = −4. When a form is specified, the final line must match it.

**10. (a)** 2y = −5x + 18 [1], so y = −2.5x + 9 and the gradient is **−5/2** [1]
**(b)** The perpendicular gradient is 2/5 [1]. y − (−2) = (2/5)(x − 5) [1], so **y = 0.4x − 4** [1]
**(c)** 0.4x − 4 = 0, so x = 4/0.4 = **10** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Turn the gradient upside down and change its sign. Quote the gradient of the original line as a fraction before flipping, so the reciprocal is exact.

**11. (a)** Gradient AB = (4 − 2)/(7 − 1) = 1/3 [1]. Gradient BC = (7 − 4)/(6 − 7) = −3 [1]. The product is (1/3) × (−3) = −1, so **AB is perpendicular to BC** and angle ABC is 90° [1]
**(b)** y − 4 = −3(x − 7) [1], so **y = −3x + 25** [1]
**(c)** Gradient −3 through A(1, 2): y − 2 = −3(x − 1) [1], so **y = −3x + 5**, which crosses the y-axis at **(0, 5)** [1]
**(d)** D = A + C − B = (1 + 6 − 7, 2 + 7 − 4) [1] = **(0, 5)**. Check: gradient CD = (7 − 5)/(6 − 0) = 1/3, the same as AB [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" about perpendicular lines needs both gradients and their product stated. In part (d), notice that the answer lies on your line from part (c); using earlier parts as a check is a good habit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing coordinates as (y, x).
- Taking the gradient straight from 5x − 2y = 8 without rearranging.
- Subtracting the coordinates in different orders in the numerator and denominator.
- Missing the turning point when finding the range of a quadratic on a restricted domain.
- Using fg when the question needs gf, or the reverse.
- Giving one root only after solving x² = 9.
- Forgetting to change the sign when finding a perpendicular gradient.
- Drawing a line through only two points, so that a slip is not caught.
- Leaving an equation in a different form from the one the question asks for.
