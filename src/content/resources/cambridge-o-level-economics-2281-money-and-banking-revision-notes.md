Quick recall for section 3.1 **Money and banking** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: "the forms, functions and characteristics of money" and "the role and importance of central banks and commercial banks for government, producers and consumers". Full explanations are in the [money and banking study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking/); test yourself with the [money and banking practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Money | Anything generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for settling debts |
| Barter | Swapping goods or services directly, without money |
| Double coincidence of wants | Under barter, each trader must want what the other offers, at the same time |
| Commodity money | An item valued for itself, used as money (grain, salt, gold) |
| Fiat money | Money with little value as a material, accepted because of trust and legal tender status |
| Legal tender | Money that must by law be accepted in payment of a debt |
| Commercial bank | A profit-making bank that takes deposits, lends and provides payment services |
| Central bank | The government's bank and the bankers' bank; issues currency and runs monetary policy |
| Lender of last resort | The central bank's role of lending to a sound bank that runs short of cash |
| Overdraft | Permission to spend more than is in an account, up to a limit, paying interest on the amount used |

## Forms of money

Commodity money → coins → banknotes → bank deposits → electronic and mobile money. The trend is from value in the item to value based on trust.

## Functions and the barter problem each solves

| Function | Barter problem solved |
|---|---|
| Medium of exchange | Double coincidence of wants |
| Unit of account | No common measure of value |
| Store of value | Goods perish or cost money to store |
| Standard of deferred payment | Debts in goods are hard to define and repay |

Rising prices weaken the store of value first. Very fast price rises can also stop money working as a medium of exchange.

## Characteristics (learn as a checklist)

- **Acceptable**: people trust it and will take it
- **Durable**: survives repeated use
- **Portable**: easy to carry or transfer
- **Divisible**: can make small payments and give change
- **Homogeneous**: every unit identical
- **Limited in supply**: cannot be created freely
- **Stable in value**: keeps its purchasing power

To judge an item, go through the list and give a reason for each point; then reach an overall verdict.

## Banks: roles

| Commercial banks | Central bank |
|---|---|
| Accept deposits | Issues notes and coins |
| Lend: loans, mortgages, overdrafts | Banker to the government |
| Payment services: cards, transfers, cheques | Banker to the commercial banks; settles payments between them |
| Currency exchange and advice | Carries out monetary policy |
| Profit from charging borrowers more than it pays savers | Supervises banks; lender of last resort |

## Importance to each group

- **Government**: the central bank manages its accounts and borrowing and helps control inflation; commercial banks buy government debt and move tax and benefit payments.
- **Producers**: loans finance investment; overdrafts bridge gaps between paying costs and receiving revenue; a trusted payment system and stable currency make planning easier.
- **Consumers**: safe deposits that earn interest; mortgages and loans for large purchases; everyday payments; the central bank protects deposits by supporting banks in a crisis.

## Worked reminders

- **Store of value**: $2,000 held in cash while prices rise 8% buys 2,000 ÷ 1.08 = $1,852 of goods at old prices.
- **Bank under pressure**: deposits $800 million, cash $60 million, 12% withdrawn = $96 million, shortfall $36 million. The central bank lends to cover it.
- **Overdraft cost**: $5,000 for 3 months at 12% a year = 5,000 × 0.12 × 3 ÷ 12 = $150.

## Exam traps

- Saying money must be valuable in itself.
- Listing functions without explaining them.
- Calling the central bank a bank for the public.
- Describing roles without saying why they matter to the named group.
- Mixing up characteristics (what good money is like) and functions (what money does).

## Quick self-test

1. Define money.
2. What is meant by a double coincidence of wants?
3. "A shirt costs $20 and a meal $10, so a shirt is worth two meals." Which function of money is shown?
4. Why are banknotes accepted even though the paper is almost worthless?
5. Give two reasons why fresh fish would make poor money.
6. Why do rising prices weaken money as a store of value?
7. State two roles of a central bank.
8. How does a commercial bank make a profit?
9. Explain what is meant by lender of last resort.
10. Give one way commercial banks are important to producers.
11. A firm uses an overdraft of $8,000 for 6 months at 10% a year. Calculate the interest.
12. Which characteristic of money is shown by "every $1 coin is identical"?

### Answers

1. Anything generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for settling debts.
2. Under barter, each trader must want what the other has to offer, at the same time.
3. Unit of account.
4. People trust the issuing central bank and the notes are legal tender, so everyone expects others to accept them.
5. Any two: not durable (it rots), not homogeneous (fish differ), not easily divisible, not portable in large amounts.
6. Each unit of money buys fewer goods as prices rise, so saved money loses purchasing power.
7. Any two: issues notes and coins; banker to the government; banker to the commercial banks; carries out monetary policy; supervises banks; lender of last resort.
8. It charges borrowers a higher rate of interest than it pays savers (and charges fees for services).
9. The central bank lends to a sound bank that runs short of cash, so that panic does not spread.
10. Loans for investment, overdrafts to cover gaps in cash flow, or accounts for paying wages and suppliers.
11. 8,000 × 0.10 × 6 ÷ 12 = $400.
12. Homogeneous (uniform).
