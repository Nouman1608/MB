These notes cover section 3.1.3 Estimation of physical quantities of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), first teaching 2015. The specification asks for orders of magnitude and for estimates of approximate values of physical quantities, including derived estimates that combine several estimates with physics. The order-of-magnitude rounding rule and a first table of reference values are in the [SI units and estimation revision notes](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-estimation-of-physical-quantities-revision-notes/); these notes add a layout for written estimates, further anchor values and worked estimates from across the course. Full teaching is in the [estimation of physical quantities study guide](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-estimation-of-physical-quantities/), and the [estimation practice questions](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-estimation-of-physical-quantities-practice/) test it.

## What 3.1.3 requires

- Use orders of magnitude.
- Estimate approximate values of physical quantities.
- Combine estimates, using physics knowledge, to give derived estimates, to the nearest order of magnitude.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Order of magnitude | The power of ten nearest to a value, judged by ratio |
| Estimate | A reasoned approximate value, not a measurement |
| Derived estimate | An estimate calculated from other estimates using a physics relationship |
| Assumption | A simplification stated so a reader can judge the estimate |
| Differ by n orders of magnitude | One value is about 10ⁿ times the other |
| Sense check | Comparing a calculated answer with an estimate to catch errors |

## A layout that earns credit

1. **Name the relationship** you will use (Ek = ½mv², P = E/t, N = (m/M) × N_A).
2. **List each estimated value** with a unit and a one-line reason.
3. **Substitute in SI units** and calculate.
4. **Give the answer to one or two significant figures**, then its order of magnitude if asked.
5. **Comment** on whether it is sensible or which estimate matters most.

An estimate without stated values cannot be followed. A reasonable range is usually accepted for each estimate, so the reasons matter more than the exact numbers.

## Worked estimates across the course

**Kinetic energy of a car on a motorway (mechanics).**

```
m ≈ 1200 kg, v ≈ 30 m s⁻¹
Ek = ½ × 1200 × 30² = 5.4 × 10⁵ J
5.4 is above 3.16, so the order of magnitude is 10⁶ J
```

**Power of a student running upstairs (energy and power).**

```
m ≈ 60 kg, one storey h ≈ 3 m, time ≈ 3 s
P = mgh/t = 60 × 9.81 × 3 / 3 ≈ 590 W ≈ 6 × 10² W
order of magnitude 10³ W
```

**Molecules in a glass of water (amount of substance).**

```
mass ≈ 0.25 kg, M = 0.018 kg mol⁻¹
moles = 0.25 / 0.018 ≈ 14 mol
N = 14 × 6.02 × 10²³ ≈ 8 × 10²⁴ → order of magnitude 10²⁵
```

**Size of an atom compared with its nucleus (particles).**

```
atom ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m, nucleus ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m
ratio = 10⁻¹⁰ / 10⁻¹⁵ = 10⁵
```

The atom is about five orders of magnitude larger than its nucleus.

**Pressure under a person standing on one foot (materials and forces).**

```
weight ≈ 70 × 9.81 ≈ 690 N, sole area ≈ 0.03 m²
p = F/A ≈ 690 / 0.03 ≈ 2 × 10⁴ Pa → order of magnitude 10⁴ Pa
```

That is about a fifth of atmospheric pressure, which is a useful sense check.

## Comparing quantities

"How many orders of magnitude larger" means take the ratio and find its power of ten. A visible-light photon (about 3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, from E = hc/λ with λ ≈ 6 × 10⁻⁷ m) compared with the kinetic energy of a car (about 5 × 10⁵ J) gives a ratio near 2 × 10²⁴: about 24 orders of magnitude.

## Using estimates to check calculations

Before accepting a calculated answer, estimate it with one-figure values:

- A wire's resistance comes out as 4 × 10⁶ Ω for a 1 m length of copper. Copper wires in circuits have resistances well below 1 Ω, so a power of ten in the area conversion has slipped.
- A falling ball's speed after 1 s is calculated as 98 m s⁻¹. With g ≈ 10 m s⁻², v ≈ 10 m s⁻¹, so a factor of 10 is wrong.

## Exam traps

- Giving an estimate with no stated values or reasons.
- Using a value far outside a sensible range, such as a 10 kg adult or a 300 m s⁻¹ car.
- Mixing units: grams with kilograms, minutes with seconds.
- Rounding 5.4 × 10⁵ to an order of magnitude of 10⁵ by looking only at the power.
- Quoting a derived estimate to four significant figures, which claims a precision the estimates do not have.
- Forgetting the molar mass is in kg mol⁻¹ when the mass is in kg.
- Confusing "the same order of magnitude" with "nearly equal": 2 × 10³ and 3 × 10³ share 10³, but 3 × 10³ and 4 × 10³ do not.

## Quick self-test

1. State the order of magnitude of 4.8 × 10⁻⁷ m.
2. State the order of magnitude of 2.6 × 10⁸ J.
3. Estimate the momentum of a 70 kg person walking at 1.4 m s⁻¹, to the nearest order of magnitude.
4. Estimate the gravitational potential energy gained by lifting an apple 1 m.
5. Estimate the number of moles of water in a 2 litre bottle (take the density of water as 1000 kg m⁻³).
6. Estimate the current drawn by a 2.5 kW kettle on 230 V mains.
7. By how many orders of magnitude does a nucleus of diameter 10⁻¹⁴ m differ from an atom of diameter 10⁻¹⁰ m?
8. Estimate the kinetic energy of a 0.06 kg tennis ball served at 50 m s⁻¹.
9. Estimate the time for sound to cross a 30 m sports hall.
10. A student calculates the energy of a red-light photon as 3 × 10⁻¹⁰ J. Use an estimate to judge this.

### Answers

1. 4.8 is above 3.16, so **10⁻⁶ m**.
2. 2.6 is below 3.16, so **10⁸ J**.
3. p = 70 × 1.4 ≈ 98 kg m s⁻¹, so **10² kg m s⁻¹**.
4. 0.1 × 9.81 × 1 ≈ **1 J** (order 10⁰ J).
5. 2 kg ÷ 0.018 kg mol⁻¹ ≈ **110 mol**, order 10² mol.
6. I = P/V = 2500 ÷ 230 ≈ **11 A**, order 10¹ A.
7. 10⁻¹⁰ ÷ 10⁻¹⁴ = 10⁴, so **four** orders of magnitude.
8. ½ × 0.06 × 50² = **75 J**, order 10² J.
9. 30 ÷ 340 ≈ **0.09 s**, order 10⁻¹ s.
10. hc/λ with λ ≈ 7 × 10⁻⁷ m gives about 3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, so the student's value is **10⁹ times too large**.

## Frequently asked questions

**How accurate do my estimated values need to be?**
They need to be sensible and justified. A range of values is normally acceptable for each estimate, so state each value with a short reason and work in SI units.
