## Reform movements before 1857

| Figure | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Shah Waliullah (1703–62) | Translated the Qur'an into Persian; sought unity around scripture |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | Led the Jihad movement in the north-west |
| Haji Shariatullah | Faraizi movement in Bengal; defended Muslim peasants |

These matter because they show Muslim political consciousness **preceded** British policy rather than resulting from it, a point worth stating explicitly in any answer that traces the roots of the Pakistan Movement back before 1857.

## 1857 and its aftermath

**Causes:** Doctrine of Lapse, annexations, greased cartridges, missionary activity, economic distress, loss of status.

**Consequences:** East India Company replaced by direct Crown rule; the Mughal emperor deposed; **Muslims blamed disproportionately** and excluded from government service and education.

## Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–98)

- Founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental school at Aligarh in 1875**, raised to college status in 1877.
- Wrote *The Causes of the Indian Revolt* and *Loyal Muhammadans of India*.
- Promoted modern scientific education alongside Islamic values.
- Advanced the **Two Nation Theory** — Hindus and Muslims as distinct nations in religion, culture, law and history.
- Opposed Muslim membership of Congress, arguing Muslims would be permanently outvoted.

His importance: Aligarh produced the educated professional class that later led the Muslim League.

## The road to Partition

```
1906  Simla Deputation; ALL INDIA MUSLIM LEAGUE founded
1909  Morley-Minto Reforms -- separate electorates granted
1916  Lucknow Pact -- brief Congress-League cooperation
1930  Iqbal's Allahabad Address -- a north-western Muslim state
1933  Rahmat Ali coins the name "PAKISTAN"
1937-39 Congress ministries -- convinced Muslims of the danger
1940  LAHORE RESOLUTION
1947  Independence; Radcliffe Award
```

**Jinnah** moved from "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity" to leader of the demand for a separate state — the shift driven by Congress rule in the provinces and the failure of constitutional guarantees.

## The Nehru Report and Jinnah's Fourteen Points

**Nehru Report (1928)** — replaced separate electorates with **joint electorates**, and reserved seats for Muslims **only at the centre and in provinces where they were a minority**, refusing reservation in the two Muslim-majority provinces (Bengal and the Punjab); it also gave the central government the residuary powers and refused the Muslim demand for one-third of the seats in the central legislature. Muslim League opinion at the time read it as rejecting nearly every Muslim demand, and it convinced many Muslims that Congress would not protect their interests within a united India.

**Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)** — Jinnah's **direct response** to the Nehru Report, restating the case for separate electorates, provincial autonomy and safeguards for Muslim political rights. The two documents are frequently confused: the Nehru Report is Congress's proposal that provoked the reaction, and the Fourteen Points are the Muslim League's reply to it — not the same document under two names.

## Problems facing Pakistan after independence, 1947

Four problems dominate this part of Section 1.3:

- **The refugee crisis** — several million people crossed the border amid appalling violence, needing food, shelter and employment. A Ministry of Refugees and Rehabilitation set up camps and allocated evacuee property, though resources were wholly inadequate to the scale of the crisis.
- **Division of assets** — Pakistan was allocated 17.5% of undivided India's financial assets but initially received only a fraction; India withheld Rs. 550 million, released following a Government of India decision in January 1948 — Gandhi's fast is the factor most commonly credited with prompting that decision, though historians debate how much weight it deserves.
- **Absence of administrative machinery** — Pakistan had no capital infrastructure, few trained civil servants and almost no industry, since most industrial areas remained in India. Karachi became the capital while a new administration was improvised from scratch (it served as capital until 1959; describing it as "temporary" from the outset reads the later move backwards onto 1947).
- **Princely states and Kashmir** — Junagadh, whose Muslim Nawab acceded to Pakistan, was occupied by Indian forces in November 1947 (a plebiscite followed in February 1948); Hyderabad, whose Nizam sought independence, was annexed after Indian military action (Operation Polo) in September 1948; Kashmir's disputed accession led to the first war between India and Pakistan in 1947–48. A UN-brokered ceasefire in January 1949 left the state divided and the dispute unresolved.

## Exam traps

- Muslim League founded **1906**, not 1905.
- The name "Pakistan" is **Rahmat Ali's**, not Iqbal's.
- Jinnah did not always want partition — say what changed his position.
- 1857 is the British "Mutiny"; note the terminology is contested.
- "Explain why" and "how far" questions need **causation and judgement**, not narrative.
- Confusing the Nehru Report (Congress's proposal) with Jinnah's Fourteen Points (the League's reply to it).
- Listing post-1947 problems without explaining how each was addressed — description alone does not answer an "explain" question.
- Forgetting that Kashmir's dispute began with a **disputed accession**, not a straightforward invasion.

## Self-test

1. When and where was the Muslim League founded?
2. State the Two Nation Theory in one sentence.
3. Why was Aligarh so important?
4. Who proposed a separate Muslim state in 1930, and who named it?
5. What convinced Jinnah that separate statehood was necessary?
6. What did the Nehru Report of 1928 propose, and how did the Muslim League respond?
7. Name two problems Pakistan faced immediately after independence.

**Answers:** 1. Dhaka, **1906**, following the Simla Deputation. 2. Hindus and Muslims are two distinct nations with separate religions, cultures, laws and histories, and therefore require separate political arrangements. 3. It reconciled Muslims to modern English education without abandoning Islamic values, producing the educated leadership that later drove the Pakistan Movement. 4. Allama Iqbal proposed it (Allahabad Address, 1930); Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coined "Pakistan" (1933). 5. The experience of Congress provincial ministries 1937–39 and the failure of constitutional safeguards to protect Muslim interests. 6. It proposed joint electorates in place of separate electorates, with seats reserved for Muslims only at the centre and where they were a minority (none in the Muslim-majority provinces); Jinnah responded with his Fourteen Points, restating the case for separate electorates and safeguards. 7. Any two: the refugee crisis, the delayed division of financial assets, the lack of administrative machinery, or the disputed accession of Kashmir.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Paper 1 structured?**
Paper 1 (1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, 50%) has Section A, one compulsory source-based question of four parts (25 marks), and Section B, two questions from a choice of four, each with parts worth 4, 7 and 14 marks.

**When was the Muslim League founded?**
In December 1906 at Dhaka, shortly after the Simla Deputation to the Viceroy.

**Were Junagadh and Hyderabad resolved peacefully?**
No. Indian forces occupied Junagadh in November 1947, and Hyderabad was annexed after Indian military action (Operation Polo) in September 1948; Kashmir led to war in 1947–48.
