## Reform movements before 1857

| Figure | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Shah Waliullah (1703–62) | Translated the Qur'an into Persian; sought unity around scripture |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | Led the Jihad movement in the north-west |
| Haji Shariatullah | Faraizi movement in Bengal; defended Muslim peasants |

These matter because they show Muslim political consciousness **preceded** British policy rather than resulting from it, a point worth stating explicitly in any answer that traces the roots of the Pakistan Movement back before 1857.

## 1857 and its aftermath

**Causes:** Doctrine of Lapse, annexations, greased cartridges, missionary activity, economic distress, loss of status.

**Consequences:** East India Company replaced by direct Crown rule; the Mughal emperor deposed; **Muslims blamed disproportionately** and excluded from government service and education.

## Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–98)

- Founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh, 1875**.
- Wrote *The Causes of the Indian Revolt* and *Loyal Muhammadans of India*.
- Promoted modern scientific education alongside Islamic values.
- Advanced the **Two Nation Theory** — Hindus and Muslims as distinct nations in religion, culture, law and history.
- Opposed Muslim membership of Congress, arguing Muslims would be permanently outvoted.

His importance: Aligarh produced the educated professional class that later led the Muslim League.

## The road to Partition

```
1906  Simla Deputation; ALL INDIA MUSLIM LEAGUE founded
1909  Morley-Minto Reforms -- separate electorates granted
1916  Lucknow Pact -- brief Congress-League cooperation
1930  Iqbal's Allahabad Address -- a north-western Muslim state
1933  Rahmat Ali coins the name "PAKISTAN"
1937-39 Congress ministries -- convinced Muslims of the danger
1940  LAHORE RESOLUTION
1947  Independence; Radcliffe Award
```

**Jinnah** moved from "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity" to leader of the demand for a separate state — the shift driven by Congress rule in the provinces and the failure of constitutional guarantees.

## The Nehru Report and Jinnah's Fourteen Points

**Nehru Report (1928)** — proposed **joint electorates, a unitary structure and no reserved seats for Muslims**, rejecting nearly every existing Muslim demand. It convinced Muslim opinion that Congress would not protect their interests within a united India.

**Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)** — Jinnah's **direct response** to the Nehru Report, restating the case for separate electorates, provincial autonomy and safeguards for Muslim political rights. The two documents are frequently confused: the Nehru Report is Congress's proposal that provoked the reaction, and the Fourteen Points are the Muslim League's reply to it — not the same document under two names.

## Problems facing Pakistan after independence, 1947

Four problems dominate this part of Section 1.3:

- **The refugee crisis** — several million people crossed the border amid appalling violence, needing food, shelter and employment. A Ministry of Refugees and Rehabilitation set up camps and allocated evacuee property, though resources were wholly inadequate to the scale of the crisis.
- **Division of assets** — Pakistan was allocated 17.5% of undivided India's financial assets but initially received only a fraction; India withheld Rs. 550 million, released only after Gandhi's intervention and fasting.
- **Absence of administrative machinery** — Pakistan had no capital infrastructure, few trained civil servants and almost no industry, since most industrial areas remained in India. Karachi became the temporary capital while a new administration was improvised from scratch.
- **Princely states and Kashmir** — the accession disputes over Junagadh, Hyderabad and above all Kashmir led to the first war with India in 1947–48; a UN-brokered ceasefire in January 1949 left the state divided and the dispute unresolved.

## Exam traps

- Muslim League founded **1906**, not 1905.
- The name "Pakistan" is **Rahmat Ali's**, not Iqbal's.
- Jinnah did not always want partition — say what changed his position.
- 1857 is the British "Mutiny"; note the terminology is contested.
- "Explain why" and "how far" questions need **causation and judgement**, not narrative.
- Confusing the Nehru Report (Congress's proposal) with Jinnah's Fourteen Points (the League's reply to it).
- Listing post-1947 problems without explaining how each was addressed — description alone does not answer an "explain" question.
- Forgetting that Kashmir's dispute began with a **disputed accession**, not a straightforward invasion.
