## Reform movements before 1857

| Figure | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Shah Waliullah (1703–62) | Translated the Qur'an into Persian; sought unity around scripture |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | Led the Jihad movement in the north-west |
| Haji Shariatullah | Faraizi movement in Bengal; defended Muslim peasants |

These matter because they show Muslim political consciousness **preceded** British policy rather than resulting from it.

## 1857 and its aftermath

**Causes:** Doctrine of Lapse, annexations, greased cartridges, missionary activity, economic distress, loss of status.

**Consequences:** East India Company replaced by direct Crown rule; the Mughal emperor deposed; **Muslims blamed disproportionately** and excluded from government service and education.

## Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–98)

- Founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh, 1875**.
- Wrote *The Causes of the Indian Revolt* and *Loyal Muhammadans of India*.
- Promoted modern scientific education alongside Islamic values.
- Advanced the **Two Nation Theory** — Hindus and Muslims as distinct nations in religion, culture, law and history.
- Opposed Muslim membership of Congress, arguing Muslims would be permanently outvoted.

His importance: Aligarh produced the educated professional class that later led the Muslim League.

## The road to Partition

```
1906  Simla Deputation; ALL INDIA MUSLIM LEAGUE founded
1909  Morley-Minto Reforms -- separate electorates granted
1916  Lucknow Pact -- brief Congress-League cooperation
1930  Iqbal's Allahabad Address -- a north-western Muslim state
1933  Rahmat Ali coins the name "PAKISTAN"
1937-39 Congress ministries -- convinced Muslims of the danger
1940  LAHORE RESOLUTION
1947  Independence; Radcliffe Award
```

**Jinnah** moved from "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity" to leader of the demand for a separate state — the shift driven by Congress rule in the provinces and the failure of constitutional guarantees.

## Exam traps

- Muslim League founded **1906**, not 1905.
- The name "Pakistan" is **Rahmat Ali's**, not Iqbal's.
- Jinnah did not always want partition — say what changed his position.
- 1857 is the British "Mutiny"; note the terminology is contested.
- "Explain why" and "how far" questions need **causation and judgement**, not narrative.

## Self-test

1. When and where was the Muslim League founded?
2. State the Two Nation Theory in one sentence.
3. Why was Aligarh so important?
4. Who proposed a separate Muslim state in 1930, and who named it?
5. What convinced Jinnah that separate statehood was necessary?

**Answers:** 1. Dhaka, **1906**, following the Simla Deputation. 2. Hindus and Muslims are two distinct nations with separate religions, cultures, laws and histories, and therefore require separate political arrangements. 3. It reconciled Muslims to modern English education without abandoning Islamic values, producing the educated leadership that later drove the Pakistan Movement. 4. Allama Iqbal proposed it (Allahabad Address, 1930); Chaudhry Rahmat Ali coined "Pakistan" (1933). 5. The experience of Congress provincial ministries 1937–39 and the failure of constitutional safeguards to protect Muslim interests.
