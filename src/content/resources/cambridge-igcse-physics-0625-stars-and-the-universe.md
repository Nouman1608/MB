## The Sun as a star

The Sun is a star of **medium size**, consisting mostly of **hydrogen and helium**. It radiates most of its energy in three regions of the electromagnetic spectrum: **infrared, visible light and ultraviolet**. All three are needed for the mark.

**Extended only.** Stars are powered by **nuclear reactions** that release energy. In stable stars these reactions are the **fusion of hydrogen into helium**. This links directly to fusion in Topic 5: light nuclei join, the products have slightly less mass, and the difference is released as energy.

## Scale: galaxies and light-years

- A **galaxy** is made up of many billions of stars.
- The Sun is one star in the galaxy called the **Milky Way**.
- The other stars of the Milky Way are **much further away** from the Earth than the Sun is.
- The Milky Way is one of **many billions of galaxies** making up the Universe; its diameter is about **100 000 light-years**.

A **light-year** is the distance travelled in (the vacuum of) space by light in **one year**. It is a **distance**, not a time. **Extended only:** 1 light-year = **9.5 × 10¹⁵ m**.

Useful conversions:

- Sirius, 8.6 light-years away: 8.6 × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = 8.2 × 10¹⁶ m.
- The Milky Way's diameter: 100 000 × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = 9.5 × 10²⁰ m. Light takes about 100 000 years to cross it.
- An object 4.0 × 10¹⁷ m away: 4.0 × 10¹⁷ ÷ 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = 42 light-years.

Light from a star 8.6 light-years away left it 8.6 years ago, so we see the star as it was then. This is why looking further away means looking further back in time.

## The life cycle of a star (Extended only)

The syllabus gives the stages in a fixed sequence. Learn the reasoning, not just the names:

1. A star forms from an **interstellar cloud of gas and dust** containing hydrogen.
2. A **protostar** is the cloud **collapsing** and **increasing in temperature** because of its own internal gravitational attraction.
3. It becomes a **stable star** when the **inward force of gravitational attraction is balanced by an outward force due to the high temperature in the centre**. Fusion of hydrogen into helium keeps the centre hot.
4. All stars eventually **run out of hydrogen** as fuel for the reaction.
5. When most of the hydrogen in the centre has been converted to helium, **most stars expand to form red giants**; **more massive stars expand to form red supergiants**.
6. **Less massive star:** the red giant forms a **planetary nebula** with a **white dwarf** star at its centre.
7. **More massive star:** the red supergiant **explodes as a supernova**, forming a **nebula containing hydrogen and new heavier elements**, leaving a **neutron star** or a **black hole** at its centre.
8. The nebula from a supernova **may form new stars with orbiting planets**.

The branch point is **mass**. The Sun, a medium-sized star, follows the red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf path. Step 8 explains why planets like the Earth contain heavy elements: they were made in earlier stars and scattered by supernovae.

**Annotated model answer.** *Explain why a stable star neither collapses nor expands. [2]*

> "Gravitational attraction pulls the star's matter inwards. The high temperature at the centre produces an outward force. In a stable star these two forces are balanced."

**Why this scores:** it names both forces with their directions and uses the word "balanced". Mentioning only gravity, or saying the forces "cancel out the fusion", loses marks.

## Redshift and the expanding Universe

**Redshift** is an **increase in the observed wavelength** of electromagnetic radiation emitted from **receding** stars and galaxies. Light from distant galaxies appears redshifted compared with light emitted on the Earth: the pattern of lines in their spectra is shifted towards longer wavelengths.

Redshift in the light from distant galaxies is evidence that the **Universe is expanding**, and it supports the **Big Bang Theory**: if galaxies are moving apart now, they were closer together in the past, and all the matter in the Universe was once at a single point.

**Extended only. Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR).** Microwave radiation of a specific frequency is observed at **all points in space around us**. It was produced shortly after the Universe was formed and has been **expanded into the microwave region** as the Universe expanded, stretching its wavelength. This is further evidence for the Big Bang.

## The Hubble constant (Extended only)

- The **speed v** at which a galaxy moves away is found from the **change in wavelength** of its starlight due to redshift.
- The **distance d** of a far galaxy is found from the **brightness of a supernova** in that galaxy.
- The **Hubble constant** is the ratio of the speed at which a galaxy moves away from the Earth to its distance from the Earth: **H₀ = v / d**. Current estimate: **H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second**.
- **d / v = 1 / H₀** is an estimate of the age of the Universe: it is the time taken for a galaxy to travel distance d at speed v from a single starting point.

### Worked example (Extended)

A galaxy is observed to recede at 4.4 × 10⁶ m/s. Calculate its distance in metres and in light-years.

```
d = v / H₀ = 4.4 × 10⁶ / 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 2.0 × 10²⁴ m
in light-years: 2.0 × 10²⁴ / 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = 2.1 × 10⁸ light-years
```

Every galaxy gives the same value of d/v, because v is proportional to d. That shared value, 1/H₀ = 4.5 × 10¹⁷ s, is about 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a light-year a unit of time.
- Naming only one or two of the Sun's three main radiation regions.
- Sending the Sun to a supernova, or a massive star to a white dwarf.
- Describing redshift as light "turning red" or galaxies "becoming red".
- Saying the CMBR "is from the Sun" or "is microwaves from galaxies".
- Using H₀ without converting light-years to metres, or confusing v/d with d/v.

## Exam technique

- **State:** short facts such as "hydrogen and helium" or "about 100 000 light-years".
- **Describe** the life cycle: give the stages in order and branch clearly on mass.
- **Explain** stability: two forces, their directions, balanced.
- **Explain** what redshift shows: expansion, then the Big Bang link.
- **Calculate:** write H₀ = v/d, rearrange, substitute in SI units, and convert to light-years or years only at the end.
