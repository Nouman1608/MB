## Core recall

- Solid waste streams: domestic, commercial, industrial, construction and demolition, agricultural, e-waste; some of each may be hazardous.
- Biodegradable waste is broken down by decomposers; non-biodegradable waste persists.
- Waste per person tends to rise with income; organics dominate in lower-income areas, paper, plastics and packaging grow in higher-income areas.
- Composition decides strategy: compost where organics dominate, reduce and recycle where packaging dominates.
- A linear economy goes take, make, use, dispose; a circular economy keeps materials in use and designs waste out.
- Sanitary landfill controls leachate, methane, pests and litter; an open dump controls nothing.
- Incineration cuts volume and can recover energy, but needs flue-gas cleaning and safe disposal of hazardous fly ash, and can lock in demand for waste.
- Anaerobic digestion makes biogas and digestate; composting makes a soil improver.
- Microplastics form as plastics fragment; floating plastic gathers in ocean gyres.
- The Basel Convention (1989) controls movement of hazardous waste between countries.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Solid domestic waste (SDW) | Household rubbish: food, garden waste, paper, plastics, glass, metals, textiles, e-waste |
| Hazardous waste | Waste harmful to health or the environment: batteries, solvents, medical waste |
| Leachate | Liquid that drains through waste and carries dissolved pollutants |
| Sanitary landfill | Engineered landfill with liner, leachate collection, gas capture, daily cover |
| Open dump | Uncontrolled tipping with no protective measures |
| Bottom ash / fly ash | Residue left in the furnace / fine ash caught by filters (hazardous) |
| Anaerobic digestion | Breakdown of organic waste without oxygen, producing biogas and digestate |
| Circular economy | Economic model that keeps products and materials in use and designs out waste |
| Cradle to cradle | Design approach in which every material becomes a future input |
| Contamination | Wrong or dirty items in recycling, which can cause loads to be rejected |
| Extended producer responsibility | Producers pay for end-of-life collection and treatment of their products |
| Pay-as-you-throw | Charging households by the amount of residual waste |
| Deposit-return scheme | Refundable deposit on containers to encourage their return |

## Strategies by level of the pollution management model

| Level | Solid waste strategies |
|---|---|
| Alter human activity | Buy less, choose durable and repairable goods, reduce packaging, refill and reuse, product bans |
| Control release | Recycling, composting, anaerobic digestion, incineration with energy recovery, sanitary landfill controls, deposit-return, landfill tax |
| Clean up and restore | Litter picks and beach clean-ups, restoring closed landfill sites, removing illegal dumps |

## Methods compared

| Method | Main benefit | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Sanitary landfill | Handles mixed waste cheaply with controls | Uses land; methane and leachate need decades of management |
| Incineration with energy recovery | Large volume reduction; electricity or heat | Hazardous fly ash; air emissions; high cost; may discourage recycling |
| Composting | Returns nutrients to soil; cuts landfill methane | Needs separated, uncontaminated organics |
| Anaerobic digestion | Biogas fuel and fertiliser | Expensive plant; needs steady organic supply |
| Recycling | Saves raw materials and often energy | Sorting cost; contamination; some materials degrade each cycle |
| Reuse and repair | Very little processing | Requires changes in design and habits |

## Calculation methods

```
Waste per person = total waste (kg) ÷ population
Mass of fraction = total × percentage ÷ 100
Diversion rate   = mass diverted ÷ total waste × 100
Real recycling   = collected × (1 − contamination fraction)
```

## Exam traps

- Putting recycling at the top of the hierarchy.
- Describing landfill without controls, or treating all landfills as open dumps.
- Forgetting that incineration produces hazardous fly ash.
- Claiming biodegradable plastics solve ocean litter.
- Quoting a recycling rate without allowing for contamination.
- Giving a strategy without its pollution management level when asked to classify.
- Missing units: t, kg per person per year, %.

## Quick self-test

1. Name three solid waste streams other than domestic waste.
2. Distinguish biodegradable from non-biodegradable waste.
3. A town of 30,000 people produces 13,500 t of SDW a year. Calculate waste per person in kg per year.
4. 45% of that waste is organic. Calculate the organic mass.
5. If 80% of the organic waste is composted, calculate the mass composted.
6. Kerbside schemes collect 2,400 t of recyclables, of which 15% is contaminated and rejected. Calculate the mass actually recycled.
7. A town diverts 5,400 t of its 13,500 t of waste. Calculate the diversion rate.
8. State two features of a sanitary landfill and what each controls.
9. Give one reason incineration may discourage recycling.
10. What are the two products of anaerobic digestion?
11. Outline why microplastics are a concern.
12. Give one advantage and one limitation of a landfill tax.

### Answers

1. Any three: commercial, industrial, construction and demolition, agricultural, e-waste.
2. Biodegradable waste can be broken down by decomposers (food, paper); non-biodegradable waste cannot and persists (most plastics, glass).
3. 13,500,000 ÷ 30,000 = **450 kg per person per year**.
4. 0.45 × 13,500 = **6,075 t**.
5. 0.80 × 6,075 = **4,860 t**.
6. 2,400 × 0.85 = **2,040 t**.
7. 5,400 ÷ 13,500 × 100 = **40%**.
8. Any two: liner, stops leachate reaching groundwater; leachate pipes, drain liquid for treatment; gas wells, capture methane; daily cover, reduces smells, pests and litter.
9. The plant needs a steady supply of waste to pay for itself, so there is pressure to keep waste volumes high.
10. Biogas (mainly methane) and digestate (fertiliser).
11. They are eaten by organisms, can carry toxins and move along food chains, and are almost impossible to remove from the environment.
12. Advantage: makes recycling and composting cheaper than disposal. Limitation: can encourage illegal dumping.
