These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 8.06, Three-dimensional shapes. All of 8.06 is for **both tiers**, so every question is for both tiers. No grids or diagrams are given: where a question asks you to describe a view, say its shape, its dimensions and any lines inside it. Squared and isometric paper are useful for checking your answers. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [three-dimensional shapes study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-three-dimensional-shapes/) or the [three-dimensional shapes revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-three-dimensional-shapes-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the name of the solid that has

**(a)** two triangular faces and three rectangular faces **[1]**

**(b)** one square face and four triangular faces **[1]**

**(c)** one curved surface and no edges. **[1]**

**2.** Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of a decagonal prism (a prism whose cross-section has 10 sides). **[2]**

**3.** A pyramid has 10 vertices.

**(a)** What shape is its base? **[1]**

**(b)** How many edges does it have? **[1]**

**4.** A cuboid is 7 cm long, 3 cm deep and 5 cm high. Write down the dimensions of its plan, its front elevation and its side elevation. **[3]**

**5.** Name the solid with

**(a)** plan a circle and front elevation a rectangle **[1]**

**(b)** plan a circle with a dot at its centre and front elevation a triangle. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Sam says that any solid with 6 faces must be a cuboid. Give a solid that shows Sam is wrong. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why a cone is not a pyramid. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A prism is made from centimetre cubes. Its front face is a T shape: a top row of 3 cubes, with 1 cube underneath the middle cube. The prism is 3 cubes deep.

**(a)** Work out the number of cubes in the prism. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the front elevation. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the plan, explaining whether it has any lines inside it. **[2]**

**(d)** Describe the side elevation from the right. **[2]**

**8.** A solid is made of cubes. Viewed from above, with the front at the bottom, the numbers of cubes in each stack are: back row 3, 1, 2; front row 1, 1, 2 (each row read from left to right).

**(a)** How many cubes are in the solid? **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the heights of the three columns in the front elevation, from left to right. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the heights of the two columns in the side elevation from the right, from left to right. **[2]**

**9.** A solid has these views.

Plan: a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm, with one line across the middle joining the midpoints of the 6 cm sides.

Front elevation: an isosceles triangle with base 6 cm and height 3 cm.

Side elevation: a rectangle 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

**(a)** Write down the name of the solid. **[1]**

**(b)** What does the line on the plan show? **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of the solid. **[1]**

**10.** A solid with flat faces has 7 faces and 10 vertices.

**(a)** Show that it could be a pentagonal prism. **[1]**

**(b)** Use faces + vertices − edges = 2 to find its number of edges. **[1]**

**(c)** A pyramid also has 7 faces. How many vertices does it have? **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Describe how to draw a cuboid 4 cm long, 2 cm deep and 3 cm high on isometric dot paper. **[3]**

**(b)** How many edges of the cuboid are visible in the drawing? **[1]**

**12.** A prism has a cross-section that is a regular octagon.

**(a)** How many of its faces are rectangles? **[1]**

**(b)** How many pairs of parallel faces does it have? **[1]**

**(c)** How many of its edges are parallel to one edge that joins the two octagonal ends? **[1]**

**13.** A solid is made from a cylinder with a cone fixed on top. The cone's base is the same size as the top of the cylinder. The solid stands upright.

**(a)** Describe the plan. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the front elevation. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the number of flat faces, curved surfaces and vertices of the solid. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Triangular prism** [1]

**(b)** **Square-based pyramid** [1]

**(c)** **Sphere** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Prism" or "pyramid" alone is not enough. Name the cross-section or base.

**2.** n = 10: 12 faces and 30 edges [1]; 20 vertices [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* For a prism the counts are n + 2, 3n and 2n. A quick check: 12 + 20 − 30 = 2.

**3. (a)** n + 1 = 10, so the base has 9 sides: a **nonagon** [1]

**(b)** 2n = **18 edges** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A pyramid's vertices are the base corners plus the apex, which is where n + 1 comes from.

**4.** Plan **7 cm by 3 cm** [1]; front elevation **7 cm by 5 cm** [1]; side elevation **3 cm by 5 cm** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Each view shows two of the three dimensions. The length 7 cm appears in two views, as does each other dimension.

**5. (a)** **Cylinder** [1]

**(b)** **Cone** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The dot in the plan of a cone is its apex, seen from above.

**6. (a)** For example a **pentagonal pyramid** (6 faces), or any 6-faced solid that is not a cuboid [1]

**(b)** A pyramid has a polygon base and triangular faces, but a cone has a circular base and a curved surface [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* One counter-example is enough to show a statement is false.

**7. (a)** Front face has 3 + 1 = 4 cubes [1]; 4 × 3 = **12 cubes** [1]

**(b)** A **T shape**, 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall [1]

**(c)** A **square 3 cm by 3 cm** [1]; with **no lines inside**, because from above you see only the flat top of the top row, which hides the cube below [1]

**(d)** A **rectangle 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall** [1]; with a **horizontal line at height 1 cm**, where the end of the top bar is nearer than the end of the stem below it [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* An inside line appears on a view only where there is an edge you can actually see. Always ask whether the edge is visible from that direction.

**8. (a)** 3 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 2 = **10 cubes** [1]

**(b)** Tallest stack in each column: max(3, 1), max(1, 1), max(2, 2) [1]; **3, 1, 2** [1]

**(c)** Tallest in each row: front row 2, back row 3 [1]; viewed from the right the front is on the left, so **2, 3** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* An elevation shows only the tallest stack in each line of sight. Shorter stacks behind are hidden.

**9. (a)** **Triangular prism** [1]

**(b)** The **top edge (ridge)** of the prism, where the two sloping faces meet [1]

**(c)** **5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Match the dimensions across views: the 6 cm and 3 cm of the triangle and the 4 cm depth all reappear in the plan and side elevation.

**10. (a)** A pentagonal prism has 5 + 2 = 7 faces and 2 × 5 = 10 vertices, which match [1]

**(b)** 7 + 10 − E = 2, so **E = 15** [1]

**(c)** n + 1 = 7 gives a hexagonal pyramid, with **7 vertices** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The pentagonal prism has 3 × 5 = 15 edges, which agrees with part (b).

**11. (a)** Use the paper with the dots in vertical columns; from the nearest bottom corner, draw a **vertical edge 3 units** long [1]; draw the **4-unit and 2-unit edges along the two sloping directions** (none horizontal) [1]; complete the parallel edges so the top, front and side faces are shown [1]

**(b)** **9 edges** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In an isometric drawing of a cuboid, 3 of the 12 edges are hidden at the back corner. Drawing them in makes the picture look like a wire frame, which is not the convention.

**12. (a)** **8** rectangular faces, one for each side of the octagon [1]

**(b)** 1 pair (the octagonal ends) + 4 pairs of rectangles = **5 pairs** [1]

**(c)** All 8 edges joining the ends are parallel, so **7 others** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A regular octagon has 4 pairs of parallel sides, so the rectangles on those sides are parallel in pairs.

**13. (a)** A **circle with a dot at its centre** (the apex) [1]

**(b)** A **rectangle with a triangle on top**, the triangle's base the same width as the rectangle [1]

**(c)** **1 flat face** (the base), **2 curved surfaces**, **1 vertex** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The circle where the cone meets the cylinder is a curved edge, so it appears as a line across the front elevation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming solids without the cross-section or base.
- Assuming the number of faces is enough to identify a solid.
- Missing lines inside a view, or drawing lines for edges that cannot be seen.
- Mixing up the left and right of a side elevation.
- Using the wrong pair of dimensions for a view.
- Drawing horizontal lines on isometric paper, or including hidden edges.
