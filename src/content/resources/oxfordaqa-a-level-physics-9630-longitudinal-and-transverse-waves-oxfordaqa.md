## The one test that classifies a wave

Compare two directions: the direction in which the particles (or fields) are displaced, and the direction in which the wave carries energy.

| Wave | What is displaced | Direction of displacement | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wave on a string | Pieces of string | At right angles to the string, so at 90° to the energy flow | Transverse |
| Sound in air | Air molecules | Back and forth along the line the energy travels | Longitudinal |
| Electromagnetic wave | Electric and magnetic fields | Both at 90° to the energy flow, and at 90° to each other | Transverse |

"Up and down" is not a definition of transverse. A wave on a horizontal string flicked sideways is still transverse, because the displacement is perpendicular to the direction of travel. Always phrase your answer as a comparison with the direction of energy propagation.

## Reading a longitudinal wave from a graph

A longitudinal wave is often drawn as a displacement-distance graph, which looks exactly like a transverse wave. The vertical axis is not height: it is the displacement of each particle **along** the direction of travel, with positive meaning displaced forwards.

To find compressions and rarefactions, look at the points of zero displacement:

- Where particles just behind are displaced forwards (positive) and particles just ahead are displaced backwards (negative), they crowd together. That point is the centre of a **compression**. On the graph, the curve crosses zero going downwards.
- Where particles behind are displaced backwards and those ahead forwards, they spread apart. That is a **rarefaction**, where the curve crosses zero going upwards.

Particles at the centres of compressions and rarefactions have zero displacement. Particles with the largest displacement sit midway between, where the air has its normal pressure.

### Worked example 1: compressions from a graph

A sound wave of speed 340 m s⁻¹ travels in the +x direction. Its displacement-distance graph has zero displacement at x = 0, 0.34 m and 0.68 m, with a positive maximum at x = 0.17 m and a negative maximum at x = 0.51 m.

**(a) Frequency.** λ = 0.68 m, so f = c/λ = 340/0.68 = **500 Hz**.

**(b) Compression or rarefaction at x = 0.34 m?** Particles just before 0.34 m are displaced forwards and particles just after are displaced backwards. They converge, so x = 0.34 m is the centre of a **compression**.

**(c) At x = 0.68 m?** Particles just before are displaced backwards and those just after forwards. They separate, so this is a **rarefaction**.

**(d) The particle at x = 0.17 m.** It has the maximum forward displacement, but the pressure there is normal, because its neighbours are displaced by almost the same amount and the spacing is unchanged.

The distance between the compression at 0.34 m and the next compression is one wavelength, 0.68 m. A compression and its neighbouring rarefaction are half a wavelength apart.

## Electromagnetic waves: one speed in a vacuum

The specification expects you to know that all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum, 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Radio waves and gamma rays differ only in frequency and wavelength.

### Worked example 2: two signals from a space probe

A probe 2.25 × 10¹¹ m from the Earth sends a radio message at the same moment as its lamp flashes. How long does each signal take, and which arrives first?

```
t = d / c = 2.25 × 10¹¹ / 3.00 × 10⁸ = 750 s = 12.5 minutes
```

Both take 750 s and arrive together, because radio waves and light have the same speed in a vacuum. This would not be true for sound, which is longitudinal, needs a medium and cannot cross empty space at all.

## Ultrasound in medicine

Ultrasound is longitudinal: a medical probe makes the tissue particles oscillate along the line of the beam. The pulse-echo principle is in the broader guide. Two further points carry marks.

**Choosing the frequency.** The finest detail that can be shown is roughly the size of the wavelength. A higher frequency gives a shorter wavelength and finer detail, but higher frequencies are absorbed more strongly, so they cannot image deep structures.

### Worked example 3: wavelength and detail

Take the speed of ultrasound in soft tissue as 1540 m s⁻¹, as a question would give it. Compare 2.0 MHz and 7.5 MHz.

```
2.0 MHz: λ = 1540 / 2.0 × 10⁶ = 7.7 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.77 mm
7.5 MHz: λ = 1540 / 7.5 × 10⁶ = 2.1 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.21 mm
```

The 7.5 MHz probe shows detail nearly four times finer, so it suits structures close to the skin. The 2.0 MHz probe is chosen for organs deep in the body, because it is absorbed less on the way in and out.

**Why ultrasound is used.** It does not ionise tissue, so it is used for imaging a fetus, where X-rays would be avoided. Its limitation is that it is almost completely reflected at a boundary with air, and strongly reflected and absorbed by bone, so it cannot image the lungs well or see clearly through bone.

## Polarisation as evidence

Polarisation restricts the oscillation to a single plane that contains the direction of travel. The argument for transverse waves runs in three steps, and full-mark answers include all three.

1. In a transverse wave the displacement has a direction at right angles to the travel, and that direction can be any of many.
2. A polariser transmits only the component of the oscillation in one direction, so it can select or block oscillations according to their direction.
3. A longitudinal wave oscillates along its line of travel, so there is no perpendicular direction to select, and a polariser would have no effect.

So if rotating a polariser changes the transmitted intensity, the wave must be transverse. Light, microwaves and radio waves behave like this. Sound does not.

### Polaroid material

Polaroid sheet transmits the component of the electric field along its transmission axis. Two applications follow directly.

- **Crossed Polaroids.** With the transmission axes at 90°, the second sheet blocks the plane-polarised light from the first, so almost nothing gets through.
- **Glare-reducing sunglasses.** Light reflected from water or a wet road is partly polarised, with its electric field mostly horizontal. Polaroid lenses with a vertical transmission axis absorb much of this reflected light while still transmitting light from other objects.

### Aligning aerials

A transmitting rod aerial produces waves whose electric field oscillates parallel to the rod. A receiving rod responds to the component of the field along its own length.

- Receiver parallel to the transmitter: strongest signal.
- Receiver at 90° to it: very weak signal.
- Receiver at an intermediate angle: reduced signal.

A laboratory version uses a microwave transmitter and a receiver. Rotating the receiver about the line joining them through a full turn gives two maxima and two near-zero minima, which is direct evidence that the microwaves are plane-polarised and therefore transverse.

## Common mistakes

- Defining transverse as "up and down" or longitudinal as "side to side" with no reference to the direction of energy travel.
- Saying sound can be polarised, or that polarisation shows light is "a wave". It shows the wave is transverse.
- Reading a longitudinal displacement graph as a picture of the wave's shape.
- Placing compressions at the points of maximum displacement. They are at zero displacement.
- Claiming that higher-frequency ultrasound penetrates further. It is absorbed more.
- Forgetting that electromagnetic waves all share one vacuum speed: a question about radio and light needs no separate speeds.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** the difference between longitudinal and transverse: one sentence for each type, each comparing displacement direction with energy direction, and one example of each.
- **Explain** polarisation as evidence: use the three-step argument above. A bare statement that "transverse waves can be polarised" earns at most one mark.
- **Suggest** a frequency for an ultrasound scan: state the trade-off between detail and depth and link it to the wavelength.
- **Calculate** questions on electromagnetic waves: use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ unless a different medium is stated.
