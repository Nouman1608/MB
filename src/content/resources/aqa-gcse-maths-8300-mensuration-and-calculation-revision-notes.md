## Formulae

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Triangle area | ½ × base × perpendicular height |
| Parallelogram area | base × perpendicular height |
| Trapezium area | ½(a + b)h |
| Prism volume | cross-section area × length |
| Circle | C = 2πr = πd; A = πr² |
| Cylinder | V = πr²h; curved surface = 2πrh |
| Cone | V = 1/3 πr²h; curved surface = πrl |
| Sphere | V = 4/3 πr³; surface = 4πr² |
| Pyramid | V = 1/3 × base area × height |
| Arc length | θ/360 × 2πr |
| Sector area | θ/360 × πr² |

## Bearings

Three figures; clockwise from north. Back bearing: ± 180°. Scale 1 : n means 1 cm on the drawing is n cm in real life.

## Trigonometry

- SOH CAH TOA. Pythagoras: a² + b² = c².
- Choose: two sides and need the third → Pythagoras; angle involved → trig.
- Find an angle: use the inverse function (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹). Calculator in degrees.

| θ | 0° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sin θ | 0 | 1/2 | √2/2 | √3/2 | 1 |
| cos θ | 1 | √3/2 | √2/2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| tan θ | 0 | √3/3 | 1 | √3 | undefined |

## Higher tier only: any triangle

| Rule | Formula | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Sine rule | a/sin A = b/sin B | A side and its opposite angle are known |
| Cosine rule | a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A | Two sides and the included angle; or three sides |
| Area | ½ab sin C | Two sides and the included angle |

**3D problems:** draw a right-angled triangle for each step; use Pythagoras for the base diagonal, then trig for the angle.

## Worked mini-examples

- Sector radius 6 cm, angle 60°: arc = 60/360 × 2π × 6 = **2π = 6.28 cm**; area = 60/360 × π × 36 = **6π = 18.8 cm²**.
- Cone radius 5, height 12: slant = 13; V = 1/3 × π × 25 × 12 = **100π = 314 cm³**.
- (Higher tier only) Sides 8 and 10, included angle 30°: area = ½ × 8 × 10 × sin 30° = **20 cm²**.

## Method cards

**Composite area**

1. Split the shape into rectangles, triangles, circles or parts of circles.
2. Write down the measurements each part needs, finding missing lengths by subtracting.
3. Work out each area, then add (or subtract a hole).
4. State units: cm², m².

**Volume of a prism or cylinder**

1. Find the area of the cross-section.
2. Multiply by the length (or height) of the prism.
3. Check units: cm³; convert 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.

**Finding a missing side or angle in a right-angled triangle**

1. Label hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent relative to the angle.
2. Choose sin, cos or tan from the two sides involved.
3. Write the equation, rearrange, then use the calculator in degree mode.
4. Check: the hypotenuse is the longest side; the answer should be sensible.

**Higher tier only: choosing a rule in a non-right-angled triangle**

1. Label the sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C.
2. If you know a matching pair (side and opposite angle) use the sine rule.
3. If you know two sides and the included angle, or all three sides, use the cosine rule.
4. For area, use ½ab sin C with the angle between the two sides.

## Units and conversions

| Conversion | Value |
|---|---|
| 1 m | 100 cm |
| 1 m² | 10 000 cm² |
| 1 m³ | 1 000 000 cm³ |

Convert lengths before working out areas and volumes, and square or cube the conversion factor when converting areas or volumes.

## Common errors

- Radius and diameter swapped.
- Slant height used in a volume.
- Bearing not three figures or measured anticlockwise.
- Calculator in radians.
- (Higher tier only) k² and k³ confused; sine rule used with a side that is not opposite the angle.

## Quick self-test

1. The bearing of Q from P is 130°. Give the bearing of P from Q.
2. Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height 4 cm.
3. A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 8 cm. Give its volume in terms of π.
4. A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 150°. Find the arc length in terms of π.
5. A right-angled triangle has legs 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
6. A ladder of length 6 m leans so that it makes 70° with the ground. How high does it reach? (3 s.f.)
7. Write down the exact value of sin 60°.
8. (Higher tier only) Two similar solids have lengths in ratio 2 : 5. Find the ratio of their volumes.
9. (Higher tier only) In a triangle, a = 9, b = 7 and C = 40°. Find the area (3 s.f.).
10. (Higher tier only) A cuboid is 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal.

### Answers

1. 130° + 180° = **310°**.
2. ½ × (6 + 10) × 4 = **32 cm²**.
3. π × 5² × 8 = **200π cm³**.
4. 150/360 × 2π × 12 = **10π cm**.
5. √(81 + 144) = √225 = **15 cm**.
6. 6 × sin 70° = **5.64 m**.
7. **√3/2**.
8. 2³ : 5³ = **8 : 125**.
9. ½ × 9 × 7 × sin 40° = 31.5 × 0.6428 = **20.2**.
10. √(9 + 16 + 144) = √169 = **13 cm**.
