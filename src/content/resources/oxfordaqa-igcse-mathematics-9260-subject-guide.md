OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) is a linear course for
students in the final years of secondary school who want a broad
mathematics qualification covering number, algebra, geometry and
statistics. It is examined at
one of two tiers. Core candidates sit two papers of 90 minutes and 80
marks each, and Extension candidates sit two papers of 120 minutes and
100 marks each; in both tiers each paper is worth 50% of the
qualification. There is no coursework, and a scientific calculator is
allowed in every paper.

## How the course is organised

The specification divides mathematics into four content areas. They are
not taught as four separate courses: almost every exam question draws on
more than one of them.

- **1 Number** covers structure and calculation; fractions, decimals and
  percentages; and ratio and proportion. This is the arithmetic and
  proportional reasoning that every other area relies on.
- **2 Algebra** covers notation and manipulation; functions, graphs and
  calculus; solving equations and inequalities; and sequences. Calculus
  appearing at International GCSE level is a notable feature of this
  specification, and it sits within the functions and graphs strand.
- **3 Geometry and measures** covers properties and constructions;
  mensuration and calculation; and transformations, matrices and vectors.
  Matrices are another strand that students coming from other
  specifications may not have met before.
- **4 Statistics and probability** covers presentation and analysis,
  interpretation, and probability.

Your teacher will tell you which content is assessed only at Extension
tier; the topic names above apply to the course as a whole, but the depth
expected in each strand differs between the tiers.

## How it's assessed

| Tier | Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Paper 1C: Paper 1 (Core) | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Core | Paper 2C: Paper 2 (Core) | 90 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Extension | Paper 1E: Paper 1 (Extension) | 120 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Extension | Paper 2E: Paper 2 (Extension) | 120 minutes | 100 | 50% |

Every paper is a written exam, externally assessed, and calculators are
permitted throughout. A candidate takes both papers of one tier, not a
mix. The Core tier gives access to grades 1-5 and the Extension tier to
grades 4-9, so the tier decision sets the range of grades available.

Two features shape the course more than the table suggests. First,
either paper of a tier may assess any part of the specification, so
there is no "Paper 1 topics" and "Paper 2 topics" split to revise
against. Second, the qualification is linear: both papers are sat at the
end of the course, in the same series. The specification was first
taught in September 2016 and first examined in May/June 2018, and the
version this page is written against (Version 5.1) applies to exams from
May/June 2018 onwards.

## How the parts fit together

Because both papers in a tier can test anything, the most useful way to
think about 9260 is as one connected body of skills rather than four
boxes. Some of the links that come up most often:

- **Number feeds everything.** Fractions, percentages and ratio reappear
  in probability, in similar shapes, in rates of change and in
  interpreting statistics. Weak arithmetic shows up as lost marks in
  every content area.
- **Algebra is the language of geometry and graphs.** Straight-line
  graphs, gradients and equations of lines connect algebra to
  coordinate geometry; quadratic and other graphs link to solving
  equations; and the calculus strand builds directly on graph work.
- **Geometry uses algebraic proof and vectors.** Circle theorems and
  geometrical proof ask for reasoning set out in steps, and vectors and
  transformations draw on algebraic notation.
- **Statistics and probability use number and algebra.** Averages from
  tables, conditional probability and tree diagrams all depend on
  confident fraction and decimal work.

Running across all four areas are the habits that earn marks: showing
method clearly, using the calculator efficiently without skipping
written working, checking answers for sense, and reading a problem
carefully before deciding which area of mathematics it belongs to.

## Planning your study

A sensible route through the course starts with Number, since the
arithmetic and proportional reasoning it builds are used everywhere
else. Algebra's notation and manipulation strand follows naturally, and
from there you can alternate between algebraic and geometric topics so
that each supports the other: straight-line graphs alongside coordinate
geometry, equations alongside angle and shape problems, vectors after
algebraic manipulation is secure. Statistics and probability can run in
parallel once fraction work is confident.

Across the two years, a general pattern works well:

- **First year:** build Number and core Algebra thoroughly, cover the
  main geometry properties and data handling, and keep a running list of
  methods you find difficult.
- **Early second year:** complete the remaining strands, including
  graphs of non-linear functions, calculus if you are on the Extension
  tier, matrices and transformations, and conditional probability.
- **Second half of the second year:** switch to mixed practice and full
  papers in your tier, because the open scope of each paper means mixed
  practice is the only realistic rehearsal.

Confirm your tier with your teacher as early as possible, and revisit
the decision if your results move clearly up or down. Start timed
practice in your tier once the content is broadly complete, and use the
course's exam preparation page for paper-specific technique rather than
inventing your own timing plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the Core and Extension tiers in OxfordAQA IGCSE Mathematics?**
Core candidates sit Paper 1C and Paper 2C, each 90 minutes and 80 marks, with grades 1-5 available. Extension candidates sit Paper 1E and Paper 2E, each 120 minutes and 100 marks, with grades 4-9 available.

**Can I use a calculator in OxfordAQA IGCSE Mathematics 9260?**
Yes. A scientific calculator is allowed in every paper at both tiers, although you should still show your method, since working earns marks.

**Does Paper 1 test different topics from Paper 2?**
No. Either paper of a tier may assess any part of the specification, so you need to be ready for every content area in both papers.

**Is there coursework in OxfordAQA IGCSE Mathematics?**
No. The qualification is linear with no coursework: the grade comes entirely from the two written papers in your tier, each worth 50%.

**What are the four content areas of 9260?**
Number; Algebra; Geometry and measures; and Statistics and probability. Algebra includes a functions, graphs and calculus strand, and Geometry and measures includes transformations, matrices and vectors.
