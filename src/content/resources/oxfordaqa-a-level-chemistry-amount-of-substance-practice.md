These questions cover section 3.1.2 (Amount of substance) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry 9620 specification, Version 5.3. Constants where needed: Avogadro constant L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; gas constant R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Ar values are given in the questions that need them. Work through the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-amount-of-substance-revision-notes/) or the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-amount-of-substance/) first if a method is unfamiliar. ## Questions

**1.** Define (a) relative atomic mass and (b) relative molecular mass, in terms of carbon-12. **[2]** **[2]**

**2.** Ammonium iron(II) sulfate crystals have the formula (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·6H₂O. (Ar: H 1.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, S 32.1, Fe 55.8)

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the term relative formula mass is used for this compound. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms in 3.40 g of ammonia, NH₃ (Mr 17.0). **[4]**

**4.** A technician needs 250 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution of concentration 0.150 mol dm⁻³ (Mr of NaOH 40.0).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of NaOH required. **[2]**
**(b)** 25.0 cm³ of this solution is diluted with water to 500 cm³. Calculate the new concentration. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how you would make up 250 cm³ of a standard solution from a weighed solid sample, naming the apparatus you use. **[4]**

**6.** A sample of gas of mass 0.350 g occupies 215 cm³ at 330 K and 101 kPa. Calculate the relative molecular mass of the gas and suggest one hydrocarbon it could be. **[5]**

**7.** A compound of phosphorus and oxygen contains 4.96 g of phosphorus and 6.40 g of oxygen (Ar: P 31.0, O 16.0). Its relative molecular mass is 284. Determine the empirical formula and the molecular formula. **[5]**

**8.**
**(a)** Balance the equation: Al + Fe₃O₄ → Al₂O₃ + Fe **[2]**
**(b)** Aqueous barium chloride reacts with aqueous sodium sulfate to form a precipitate. Write the ionic equation, including state symbols. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the spectator ions in part (b). **[1]**

**9.** Calcium carbonate dissolves in hydrochloric acid according to CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O. A 2.50 g sample of CaCO₃ (Ar: Ca 40.1, C 12.0, O 16.0) reacts completely. Calculate the volume of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid needed, and the volume of carbon dioxide released at 298 K and 101 kPa. **[6]**

**10.** Urea can be made by this reaction: 2NH₃ + CO₂ → (NH₂)₂CO + H₂O (Mr: NH₃ 17.0, CO₂ 44.0, (NH₂)₂CO 60.0).

**(a)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for making urea. **[3]**
**(b)** 51.0 g of ammonia reacts with excess carbon dioxide and 72.9 g of urea is isolated. Calculate the percentage yield. **[4]**
**(c)** Give two advantages of developing a process with a high atom economy. **[2]**

**11.** A 1.00 g sample of impure solid sodium hydroxide is dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. In a titration, 25.0 cm³ portions of this solution need a mean of 22.95 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Give the ionic equation for the neutralisation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount of HCl used. **[1]**
**(c)** State the amount of NaOH in each 25.0 cm³ portion. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the amount of NaOH in the 250 cm³ solution. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the mass of NaOH, then the percentage purity of the sample (Mr NaOH 40.0). **[2]**
**(f)** Suggest why a pipette is used for the 25.0 cm³ portions instead of a measuring cylinder. **[1]**

**12.** A hydrocarbon of mass 0.700 g burns completely to give 2.20 g of carbon dioxide and 0.900 g of water (Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0; Mr: CO₂ 44.0, H₂O 18.0). A separate 0.700 g sample, as vapour, occupies 364 cm³ at 350 K and 100 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the amounts of carbon and hydrogen atoms in the sample. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the empirical formula. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the relative molecular mass from the gas data. **[3]**
**(d)** Deduce the molecular formula. **[2]**
**(e)** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of this hydrocarbon. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Relative atomic mass is the weighted mean mass of an atom of the element, allowing for its isotopes, [1] divided by one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12. [1]
(b) Relative molecular mass is the mean mass of a molecule [1] divided by one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A definition usually needs both the averaging idea and the one-twelfth comparison; saying only "compared with carbon-12" is often too vague.

**2.** (a) (NH₄)₂ = 2 × (14.0 + 4.0) = 36.0 [1]; Fe + 2 SO₄ = 55.8 + 2 × (32.1 + 64.0) = 248.0 [1]; 6H₂O = 6 × 18.0 = 108.0, giving a total of **392.0** [1]
(b) The compound is ionic, so it exists as a lattice of ions and not as separate molecules. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Showing the bracket multipliers one group at a time makes slips easy to spot and usually keeps method credit available when the total is wrong.

**3.** n(NH₃) = 3.40 / 17.0 = 0.200 mol [1]; n(H atoms) = 3 × 0.200 = 0.600 mol [1]; number = 0.600 × 6.02 × 10²³ [1] = **3.61 × 10²³** atoms [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for atoms of hydrogen, not molecules. Answers usually lose credit by stopping at 1.20 × 10²³ molecules.

**4.** (a) n = 0.150 × 0.250 = 0.0375 mol [1]; m = 0.0375 × 40.0 = **1.50 g** [1]
(b) New concentration = 0.150 × 25.0 / 500 [1] = **0.00750 mol dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert cm³ to dm³ in the first line. Mixing cm³ and dm³ is the most common source of a factor-of-1000 error.

**5.** Weigh the solid accurately (for example by difference, using a weighing bottle). [1] Dissolve it in a beaker in less than 250 cm³ of distilled water, stirring. [1] Transfer to a 250 cm³ volumetric flask, rinsing the beaker and funnel into the flask. [1] Fill so the meniscus bottom sits on the mark, then stopper and invert to mix. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The rinsing step and the choice of a volumetric flask (not a beaker or measuring cylinder) are the details that usually separate full and partial credit.

**6.** Convert: V = 215 × 10⁻⁶ = 2.15 × 10⁻⁴ m³ and p = 101 000 Pa [1]; n = pV / RT = (101 000 × 2.15 × 10⁻⁴) / (8.31 × 330) [1] = 0.00792 mol [1]; Mr = 0.350 / 0.00792 = **44.2** [1]; propane, C₃H₈ (Mr 44.0) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A line showing the conversions before substitution is what examiners can credit; a bare 44.2 with unconverted units shows no evidence of method.

**7.** n(P) = 4.96 / 31.0 = 0.160 mol [1]; n(O) = 6.40 / 16.0 = 0.400 mol [1]; ratio 1 : 2.5, so multiply by 2 to give 2 : 5, so the empirical formula is **P₂O₅** [1]; empirical mass = 142 [1]; 284 / 142 = 2, so the molecular formula is **P₄O₁₀** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A ratio of 1 : 2.5 should be doubled. Rounding it to 1 : 2 or 1 : 3 is a typical way of losing the final marks.

**8.** (a) **8Al + 3Fe₃O₄ → 4Al₂O₃ + 9Fe** (correct formulae balanced; [1] for the Al₂O₃ and Fe coefficients, [1] for the rest)
(b) **Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)** [1] for species, [1] for state symbols
(c) Na⁺ and Cl⁻ [1]
*Examiner insight:* An ionic equation is expected to balance for charge as well as atoms, and spectator ions on either side usually cost credit.

**9.** n(CaCO₃) = 2.50 / 100.1 = 0.02498 mol [1]; n(HCl) = 2 × 0.02498 = 0.04995 mol [1]; V(HCl) = 0.04995 / 1.00 = 0.0500 dm³ = **50.0 cm³** [1]; n(CO₂) = 0.02498 mol [1]; V = nRT / p = (0.02498 × 8.31 × 298) / 101 000 [1] = 6.12 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = **612 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mole ratios come from the balanced equation. Using 1 : 1 for HCl is the usual slip, and a clear ratio line helps to show the method.

**10.** (a) Sum of reactant masses = 2 × 17.0 + 44.0 = 78.0 [1]; (60.0 / 78.0) × 100 [1] = **76.9%** [1]
(b) n(NH₃) = 51.0 / 17.0 = 3.00 mol [1]; n(urea) = 1.50 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 1.50 × 60.0 = 90.0 g [1]; yield = (72.9 / 90.0) × 100 = **81.0%** [1]
(c) Any two from: less raw material wasted, which lowers costs; less waste to dispose of or treat; conserves finite resources; less environmental harm. [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* Atom economy uses the equation alone while yield uses the experiment. Answers to (c) tend to earn credit when they name a consequence for industry or society, not just "less waste".

**11.** (a) **H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O** [1]
(b) n(HCl) = 0.02295 × 0.100 = **2.295 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]
(c) 1 : 1 ratio, so n(NaOH) = **2.295 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]
(d) × 10 for the whole 250 cm³: **0.02295 mol** [1]
(e) Mass = 0.02295 × 40.0 = 0.918 g [1]; purity = 0.918 / 1.00 × 100 = **91.8%** [1]
(f) A pipette measures a fixed volume with much smaller uncertainty. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Scaling up from the portion to the whole solution is a separate step to show; skipping it is a typical way to get a purity that is ten times too small.

**12.** (a) n(C) = n(CO₂) = 2.20 / 44.0 = **0.0500 mol** [1]; n(H) = 2 × 0.900 / 18.0 = **0.100 mol** [1]
(b) Ratio C : H = 0.0500 : 0.100 = 1 : 2 [1], so the empirical formula is **CH₂** [1]
(c) Convert to SI: V = 3.64 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; n = (100 000 × 3.64 × 10⁻⁴) / (8.31 × 350) = 0.01252 mol [1]; Mr = 0.700 / 0.01252 = **55.9** [1]
(d) Empirical mass = 14.0; 55.9 / 14.0 = 4 [1], so the molecular formula is **C₄H₈** [1]
(e) **C₄H₈ + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 4H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A multi-part question rewards a clear chain of results. Giving each intermediate value with its unit lets later parts be credited even if an earlier slip has occurred.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting cm³ or kPa directly into pV = nRT.
- Forgetting that each H₂O or NH₃ molecule contains more than one atom of the element being counted.
- Skipping the scaling step between a titration portion and the whole solution.
- Taking a 1 : 1 mole ratio where the equation gives 1 : 2.
- Rounding empirical-formula ratios such as 2.5 or 1.33 instead of multiplying to whole numbers.
- Mixing up percentage yield (compares masses made) and atom economy (compares masses in the equation).
- Describing high atom economy as "good for the environment" without saying why.
- Leaving out state symbols or including spectator ions when an ionic equation is requested.
