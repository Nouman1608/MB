AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. Every question suits both tiers. All data
are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 8 and 12.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Pollution can occur in water, in air and on land. For each, name **one** source of pollution given in the
specification. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why rapid growth in the human population is leading to more pollution. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why large areas of tropical forest have been cleared. **[2]**

**4.**

**(a)** Name the **two** gases whose increasing levels contribute to global warming. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe **one** biological consequence of global warming. **[1]**

**5.** Explain how recycling resources rather than dumping waste in landfill can help to maintain biodiversity.
**[2]**

**6.** A zoo runs a breeding programme for an endangered species of frog. Give **one** advantage and **one**
disadvantage of this programme. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** On island X, a flowering plant is pollinated by only one species of insect. On island Y, the same plant is
pollinated by five species of insect. A disease kills one species of pollinating insect on each island. Explain
on which island the plant population is more likely to survive. **[3]**

**8.** The table shows the forest area in a region.

| Year | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 | 2015 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forest area (thousand km²) | 520 | 488 | 455 | 430 | 412 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in forest area from 2000 to 2020. Give your answer to 2 significant
figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of forest loss between 2000 and 2010. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain **two** ways in which deforestation increases the carbon dioxide concentration of the atmosphere.
**[2]**

**9.** Over 30 years, the mean date on which a species of warbler arrives in Britain each spring has become
9 days earlier. Mean spring temperatures rose over the same period.

**(a)** Suggest how the rise in temperature could explain the earlier arrival. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** reason why scientists cannot be certain that temperature caused the change. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe **one** other biological consequence of global warming. **[1]**

**10.** A country plans to clear an area of tropical forest to grow crops for biofuels, which will replace some
of the petrol it imports. Evaluate the environmental implications of this plan. **[6]**

**11.** A factory releases toxic chemicals into a river. Students count the number of invertebrate species at
four sites.

| Site | Upstream of factory | At outlet | 500 m downstream | 2 km downstream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of invertebrate species | 14 | 3 | 6 | 11 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in the results. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the pattern. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what the students should also measure at each site to support their explanation. **[1]**

**12.** Researchers compare farms that grow one crop. On farms without field margins, a mean of 4 bee species
is recorded per field. On farms with field margins of wild flowers, a mean of 11 bee species is recorded.

**(a)** Calculate how many times more bee species are recorded with field margins. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why field margins increase the number of bee species. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** reason why a farmer might not want to leave field margins. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Water: any one of sewage, fertiliser or toxic chemicals [1]; air: smoke or acidic gases [1]; land:
landfill or toxic chemicals [1]

**2.** More people (and a higher standard of living) means more resources are used and more waste is produced [1];
unless waste and chemicals are properly handled, more pollution is caused [1]

**3.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: to provide land for cattle; to provide land for rice fields;
to grow crops for biofuels

**4. (a)** Carbon dioxide and methane (both needed) [1]
**(b)** Any one: species' distribution changes (they move towards the poles or uphill); loss of habitat;
changes in migration patterns; extinction; spread of pests or pathogens [1]

**5.** Less land is used for landfill, so more habitat remains for other organisms [1]; fewer new resources are
extracted (for example by quarrying), so fewer habitats are destroyed [1]

**6.** Advantage: increases the number of frogs, which may later be released into the wild [1];
disadvantage: any one of expensive; small captive population has little genetic variation; the frogs' wild
habitat may still be lost [1]

**7.** Island Y [1]; on Y the plant does not depend on one insect species, because four pollinators remain [1];
on X the plant loses its only pollinator, so it cannot make seeds and the population may die out [1]
*Examiner insight:* This is the "reduced dependence" idea from the specification; use the word "depend".

**8. (a)** 520 − 412 = 108; 108 ÷ 520 × 100 [1] = **21%** [1]
**(b)** (520 − 455) ÷ 10 = 65 ÷ 10 [1] = **6.5 thousand km² per year** [1]
**(c)** Fewer trees means less photosynthesis, so less carbon dioxide is removed from the air [1]; trees that are
burned, or left to decay by microorganisms, release carbon dioxide [1]

**9. (a)** Warmer springs may mean food such as insects is available earlier [1]; so warblers that arrive
earlier survive and breed more successfully (or the birds start migrating earlier in response to warmer
conditions) [1]
**(b)** It is a correlation; another factor, such as changes in the birds' wintering area, could cause it [1]
**(c)** Any one: species move to new ranges; loss of habitat; extinction; spread of pests or pathogens [1]

**10.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer gives both sides and a conclusion):
- clearing forest destroys habitats, so many species are lost and biodiversity falls [1]
- fewer trees means less carbon dioxide removed from the air by photosynthesis [1]
- burning or decay of cleared trees releases carbon dioxide, contributing to global warming [1]
- biofuel crops take in carbon dioxide as they grow, and burning them may replace some fossil fuel [1]
- a single crop gives few habitats compared with forest [1]
- a justified conclusion, for example that the loss of biodiversity and stored carbon outweighs the benefit of
  replacing some petrol [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers that discuss only the economy, with no biological effect, cannot reach the top level.

**11. (a)** The number of species is lowest at the outlet (3, compared with 14 upstream) [1]; it increases with
distance downstream but is still below the upstream value at 2 km (11) [1]
**(b)** The toxic chemicals kill invertebrates that cannot tolerate them, so fewer species survive near the outlet
[1]; further downstream the chemicals are diluted, so more species survive [1]
**(c)** The concentration of the toxic chemical in the water [1]

**12. (a)** 11 ÷ 4 = **2.75 times** [1]
**(b)** Wild flowers provide food (nectar and pollen) for more kinds of bee [1]; margins provide shelter and
nesting sites, giving more habitats than a single crop [1]
**(c)** Any one: less land for crops, so lower yield or income; cost or time to maintain the margins [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "pollution" without naming the pollutant or explaining the harm.
- Forgetting that dead trees release carbon dioxide when they are burned or decay.
- Describing a trend without quoting figures from the table.
- Evaluations with only one side, or with no conclusion.
- Defining biodiversity as the number of organisms rather than the variety of species.
- Treating a correlation between temperature and a change as proof.
