## Questions

**1.** Prepare the trial balance of Peverell Joinery at 30 June 2026. Capital is the balancing figure. Balances: machinery 28,500; inventory at 1 July 2025 4,300; purchases 35,800; wages 14,600; light and heat 2,150; trade receivables 6,900; bank (in hand) 3,420; drawings 9,500; revenue 71,200; trade payables 4,850; discount received 260; loan 10,000. **[6]**

**2.** Tregarth Garden Centre's trial balance agrees. Explain two reasons why this does not prove that its profit for the year is correct. **[4]**

**3.** For each error at Bowland Stationers, state whether the trial balance still agrees and name the type of error.

**(a)** A credit purchase of 2,300 was never recorded. **[1]**
**(b)** A cheque of 1,100 from Lomax was credited to Raikes's account (and debited to bank). **[1]**
**(c)** An electricity bill of 640 was recorded as 460 in both accounts. **[1]**
**(d)** Wages of 5,000 were debited to bank and credited to wages. **[1]**
**(e)** Discount allowed of 90 was debited to discount allowed, with no credit entry. **[1]**

**4.** Selkirk Fabrics' trial balance shows debits of 217,845 and credits of 217,575. Explain, with a calculation, why a transposition error might be the cause, and give one possible pair of figures. **[3]**

**5.** Prepare correcting journals, with narratives, for these errors at Fairbourne Bakery.

**(a)** An oven costing 4,800 was debited to repairs. **[2]**
**(b)** A credit purchase of 1,540 from Jessop Ltd was credited to Yeoman Ltd. **[2]**
**(c)** A receipt of 1,350 from the customer Zeller was debited to Zeller and credited to bank. **[2]**

**6.** Tindall Traders' credit total was 940 higher than its debit total, and a suspense account was opened. Later it found: (i) bank charges of 130 were credited to bank but not debited anywhere; (ii) a cheque for 1,070 was credited to a customer but the debit to bank was omitted; (iii) wages of 2,860 were debited to wages as 2,680 (bank was right); (iv) a credit purchase of 440 was debited to purchases but not credited to the supplier.

**(a)** State the amount and side of the opening suspense entry. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the correcting journals. **[4]**
**(c)** Prepare the suspense account and show that it closes. **[2]**

**7.** Define a compensating error and explain, with an example, why the trial balance still agrees. **[3]**

**8.** Sunnock Foods' profit for the year was first calculated as 33,720. Errors found: (i) shop fixtures of 3,600 were debited to purchases; (ii) bank charges of 215 were credited to bank but never debited; (iii) a cash sale of 750 was debited to revenue and credited to bank; (iv) a payment of 480 was debited to the wrong supplier's account; (v) the owner's electricity bill of 420 was debited to light and heat.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of revised profit. **[7]**
**(b)** State the change in gross profit and show how you found it. **[2]**

**9.** Dunmoor Stores' trade receivables control account shows a debit balance of 34,600, but its list of customer balances totals 31,055. Errors: (i) the sales returns day book was over-added by 350; (ii) the discount allowed total of 1,420 was debited, not credited, in the control account; (iii) a contra of 600 with a supplier was made in the individual accounts only; (iv) a customer balance of 1,085 was left out of the list; (v) an invoice for 2,070 was entered in a customer account as 2,700; (vi) an invoice for 800 was posted to the wrong customer.

**(a)** Prepare the corrected control account. **[6]**
**(b)** Prepare the corrected list total, stating the effect of each error. **[4]**
**(c)** State the entry needed in the trade payables control account. **[1]**

**10.** Zeller Print's trial balance disagreed and a suspense account was opened. Errors found: (a) bank charges of 95 were credited to bank but not debited; (b) a cheque of 740 was debited to bank but credited to the customer as 470; (c) repairs of 1,200 were debited to machinery. Profit was first calculated as 20,880.

**(i)** For each error, state whether the trial balance disagrees. **[3]**
**(ii)** Prepare correcting journals. **[3]**
**(iii)** Calculate the opening suspense entry and prepare the suspense account. **[2]**
**(iv)** Prepare the revised profit. **[2]**

Assets, expenses and drawings in the debit column [2]; revenue, liabilities and discount received in the credit column [1]; debit total 105,170 [1]; capital 105,170 − 86,310 = 18,860 [1]; credit total agrees [1].
*Examiner insight:* answers tend to gain credit for correct columns and for showing how the balancing figure was found; a bank balance in hand is a debit.

**2.** Some errors put equal amounts on both sides, such as omission or a purchase of equipment debited to purchases [1], which would still misstate profit [1]. Compensating errors cancel in the totals [1] but still give wrong expense or income figures [1].
*Examiner insight:* tie each reason to profit, not just to the trial balance, and give an example error.

**3.** (a) Agrees, omission [1]. (b) Agrees, commission [1]. (c) Agrees, original entry [1]. (d) Agrees, complete reversal [1]. (e) Disagrees, one-sided entry [1].
*Examiner insight:* the verdict and the name usually both need to be given; naming an error alone leaves the question unanswered.

**4.** The difference is 270 [1]. 270 ÷ 9 = 30, and a transposition changes a figure by a multiple of 9 [1]. For example, 6,250 posted as 6,520 on the debit side [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the division, and treat it as a clue not a proof.

**5.** (a) Dr Plant and equipment 4,800 [1]; Cr Repairs 4,800, correcting an error of principle [1].
(b) Dr Yeoman Ltd 1,540 [1]; Cr Jessop Ltd 1,540, correcting the supplier account used [1].
(c) Dr Bank 2,700 [1]; Cr Zeller 2,700, twice 1,350 to reverse and correct [1].
*Examiner insight:* a narrative helps, and the doubled amount for a reversal can cost a mark.

**6.** (a) Debit [1] 940 [1], since credits exceeded debits.
(b) (i) Dr Bank charges 130, Cr Suspense 130 [1]; (ii) Dr Bank 1,070, Cr Suspense 1,070 [1]; (iii) Dr Wages 180, Cr Suspense 180 [1]; (iv) Dr Suspense 440, Cr Trade payables 440 [1].
(c) Debit side: balance 940 and trade payables 440, total 1,380 [1]. Credit side: 130, 1,070 and 180, total 1,380, so the account closes [1].
*Examiner insight:* writing each journal with suspense on the clearing side makes the closing account easy to check.

**7.** A compensating error is two or more errors whose effects cancel [1]. For example, purchases and revenue are both over-added by 500 [1]; debits and credits each rise by 500, so the totals still agree [1].
*Examiner insight:* examples work best when the two errors are on opposite sides.

**8.** (a)
| Item | Effect | Profit |
|---|---:|---:|
| First calculated | | 33,720 |
| (i) Purchases overstated | 3,600 | 37,320 |
| (ii) Bank charges omitted | (215) | 37,105 |
| (iii) Revenue understated by 1,500 | 1,500 | 38,605 |
| (iv) Wrong supplier | nil | 38,605 |
| (v) Expense overstated | 420 | 39,025 |

Starting figure [1]; (i) [1]; (ii) [1]; (iii) [1]; (iv) no effect, since both accounts are payables [1]; (v) [1]; revised profit 39,025 [1].
(b) Gross profit increases by 3,600 + 1,500 = 5,100 [1] [1], because only (i) and (iii) involve purchases or revenue.
*Examiner insight:* give the direction of each effect, and say why an error with no effect on profit changes nothing.

**9.** (a)
| Trade receivables control | | | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance per books | 34,600 | Discount allowed (wrong side) | 2,840 |
| Returns day book over-added | 350 | Trade payables (contra) | 600 |
| | | **Balance carried down** | **31,510** |
| | **34,950** | | **34,950** |

Starting balance [1]; debit 350 [1]; credit 2,840, twice 1,420 [1]; credit 600 [1]; balance 31,510 [1]; totals agree [1].
(b) 31,055 + 1,085 omitted balance [1] − 630 for 2,700 entered instead of 2,070 [1] = 31,510 [1], which matches the control account; (vi) changes neither total [1].
(c) Debit trade payables control 600 [1].
*Examiner insight:* treat the control account and the list separately and say which total each error affects.

**10.** (i) (a) disagrees, one-sided [1]; (b) disagrees, different amounts on each side [1]; (c) agrees, principle [1].
(ii) Dr Bank charges 95, Cr Suspense 95 [1]; Dr Suspense 270, Cr Trade receivables 270 [1]; Dr Repairs 1,200, Cr Machinery 1,200 [1].
(iii) Debits exceeded credits by 175 (270 − 95), so suspense was opened as a credit of 175 [1]. The account has credits of 175 and 95 (total 270) and a debit of 270, so it closes [1].
(iv) Profit 20,880 − 95 − 1,200 [1] = 19,585 [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the opening difference as well as the closing check, and keep errors that agree out of suspense.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting suspense on the wrong side.
- Journalising a complete reversal for the original amount only.
- Sending errors that leave the trial balance agreeing through suspense.
- Adjusting profit in the wrong direction.
- Giving an error name with no statement of whether the trial balance agrees.
- Changing profit for a correction between two statement of financial position accounts.
- Correcting the control account for an error that is only in the list.
- Forgetting that a debit posted on the credit side is out by twice the amount.
