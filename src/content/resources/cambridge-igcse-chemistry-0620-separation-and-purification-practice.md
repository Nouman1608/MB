## Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the most suitable method to obtain each of the following.

**(a)** solid copper(II) oxide from a mixture with water **[1]**

**(b)** pure water from ink **[1]**

**(c)** crystals of copper(II) sulfate from copper(II) sulfate solution **[1]**

**2.** Which method separates two miscible liquids with boiling points of 64 °C and 97 °C?

A: filtration

B: crystallisation

C: simple distillation

D: fractional distillation

**[1]**

**3.** State where the bulb of the thermometer should be placed during distillation. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which the melting point of an impure substance differs from that of the pure substance. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Describe how you would obtain a pure, dry sample of sand **and** a pure sample of potassium chloride crystals from a mixture of the two solids. **[5]**

**7.** A student prepares crystals from a hot, concentrated solution of a salt.

**(a)** Describe how the student can tell when to stop heating. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why crystals form when the solution cools. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the crystals are washed with a small volume of cold distilled water. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why the solution is not simply heated until all the water has evaporated. **[1]**

**8.** Seawater is distilled using simple distillation.

**(a)** Explain why simple distillation separates water from seawater. **[1]**

**(b)** State the purpose of the condenser. **[1]**

**(c)** State where the cold water enters the condenser. **[1]**

**(d)** State what remains in the flask at the end. **[1]**

**9.** A mixture of ethanol (boiling point 78 °C) and water (boiling point 100 °C) is separated by fractional distillation.

**(a)** State which liquid is collected first and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the thermometer reading shows that the first liquid has finished distilling. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why simple distillation would not separate the two liquids well. **[1]**

**10.** Information about three substances is given.

| Substance | Solubility in water | Solubility in ethanol |
|---|---|---|
| F | soluble | insoluble |
| G | insoluble | soluble |
| H | insoluble | insoluble |

**(a)** Suggest how to separate H from a mixture of F and H. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a solvent to separate G from a mixture of G and H, and explain your choice. **[2]**

**11.** A student makes a sample of an organic solid, J. A data book gives the melting point of pure J as 135 °C.

**(a)** The student's sample melts between 128 °C and 132 °C. What can be concluded? Explain. **[2]**

**(b)** After purification, the sample melts at 135 °C. What can be concluded? **[1]**

**12.** A student makes copper(II) sulfate solution by adding excess copper(II) oxide to warm dilute sulfuric acid. Some black copper(II) oxide is left over. Describe how to obtain pure, dry crystals of copper(II) sulfate from this mixture. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Filtration [1].

**(b)** Simple distillation [1].

**(c)** Crystallisation [1].

**2.** **D** [1]. The liquids mix and have different boiling points.

**3.** Level with the side arm (where vapour leaves the flask) [1].

**4.** It is lower [1]; it is over a range of temperatures rather than sharp [1].

**5.** Water [1]; pure, because it boils sharply at the data value for water [1].

**6.** Any five, in a sensible order [5]:

- add water to the mixture and stir (potassium chloride dissolves, sand does not);
- filter: sand is left as the residue;
- wash the sand with distilled water and dry it (e.g. in a warm oven / between filter papers);
- heat the filtrate to evaporate some of the water until the crystallisation point (crystals form on a cooled glass rod);
- leave to cool so that crystals form;
- filter off the crystals, wash with a little cold distilled water and dry them.

**7.** **(a)** Dip a glass rod into the solution and remove it; when crystals form on the rod as it cools, stop heating [1].

**(b)** The solubility of the salt decreases as the temperature falls, so the solution cannot hold as much salt and the excess forms crystals [1].

**(c)** Washing removes the solution (containing soluble impurities) from the surface of the crystals [1]; cold water and a small volume are used so that as little of the crystals as possible dissolves [1].

**(d)** Soluble impurities would remain in the solid / the salt could decompose or lose water of crystallisation [1].

**8.** **(a)** Water has a much lower boiling point than the dissolved salts; the water boils and turns to vapour, but the salts do not [1].

**(b)** To cool the vapour so that it condenses to liquid water [1].

**(c)** At the bottom (lower end) [1].

**(d)** The salts (solid, or a more concentrated salt solution) [1].

**9.** **(a)** Ethanol [1]; it has the lower boiling point, so it reaches the top of the column as vapour first [1].

**(b)** The temperature rises above 78 °C (towards 100 °C) [1].

**(c)** The boiling points are close enough that both liquids would evaporate together, so the distillate would contain both [1].

**10.** **(a)** Add water and stir, so F dissolves [1]; filter, leaving H as the residue [1].

**(b)** Ethanol [1]; G dissolves in ethanol but H does not, so H can be filtered off [1].

**11.** **(a)** The sample is impure [1]; it melts below 135 °C and over a range, not sharply [1].

**(b)** The sample is (now) pure J [1].

**12.** Filter the mixture to remove the excess (insoluble) copper(II) oxide as the residue [1]; heat the filtrate to evaporate some water until crystals form on a cooled glass rod, then leave it to cool and crystallise [1]; filter off the crystals, wash with a little cold distilled water and dry them between filter papers [1].
*Filtration must come first: the copper(II) oxide is insoluble, while the copper(II) sulfate is in solution.*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "evaporate to dryness" when crystallisation is needed.
- Putting the thermometer bulb in the boiling liquid.
- Choosing filtration for a dissolved solid.
- Explaining a method by describing it again, without naming the property it depends on.
- Saying impurities raise the melting point.
- Missing a washing or drying step in multi-step separations.
