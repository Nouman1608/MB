Quick recall for section 4.1 Market research of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 4.1.1 to 4.1.3. Question design, worked data analysis and an annotated answer are in the [market research study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-market-research/); questions are in the [market research practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-market-research-practice/). Figures are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification list

- **4.1.1 Purpose:** identify and understand customer needs; identify gaps in the market; reduce risk; inform business decisions.
- **4.1.2 Methods:** primary (survey, questionnaire, focus group, observation, test marketing); secondary (internet, market reports, government reports).
- **4.1.3 Data:** qualitative and quantitative; the role of social media in collecting data; the importance of reliability.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market research | Collecting and analysing information about customers, competitors and the market |
| Primary research | New information collected first-hand for the business's own purpose |
| Secondary research | Information that already exists, collected by someone else for another purpose |
| Gap in the market | A customer need that no existing product meets well |
| Survey | Asking a group of people for information |
| Questionnaire | The written set of questions used to collect answers |
| Focus group | A small group discussing a product or idea with a leader |
| Observation | Watching and recording what people actually do |
| Test marketing | Selling a product in a limited area or period before a full launch |
| Quantitative data | Numerical data that can be counted or measured |
| Qualitative data | Descriptive data about opinions, feelings and reasons |
| Reliability | How far data can be trusted to give a true picture |
| Sample | The group of people actually asked, chosen to stand for the whole market |

Definitions are standard teaching; the specification lists the terms without defining them.

## 3. Methods at a glance

| Method | Type | Data mostly | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survey or questionnaire | Primary | Quantitative (closed questions) | Many responses; easy to count | Careless or untrue answers; design matters |
| Focus group | Primary | Qualitative | Explains reasons | Small; dominant voices |
| Observation | Primary | Quantitative counts | Real behaviour | No reasons |
| Test marketing | Primary | Quantitative (sales) | Real spending | Slow; reveals product to rivals |
| Internet | Secondary | Both | Quick, often free | Quality varies |
| Market reports | Secondary | Mostly quantitative | Detailed industry picture | Costly; may not fit a local market |
| Government reports | Secondary | Quantitative | Trusted, wide coverage | General; may be out of date |

## 4. Question design

- **Closed** question → fixed choices → quantitative.
- **Open** question → own words → qualitative.
- **Leading** question → pushes an answer → unreliable; avoid.
- Keep it short, clear and one idea per question.

## 5. Reliability checklist

1. Is the sample large enough?
2. Is it representative of the target market (age, location, income, lifestyle)?
3. Are the questions free of bias?
4. Is the data recent?
5. Is the source trustworthy and independent?

Unreliable data leads to poor decisions, such as over-ordering stock, which defeats the purpose of reducing risk.

## 6. Social media: role and limits

- **Role:** polls, comments, reviews, reactions to posts, competitor monitoring; cheap, fast, direct contact with customers.
- **Limits:** followers are not the whole market; some responses are from non-buyers; a few very active users can skew results.

## 7. Worked reminders

**Percentage.** 250 people asked; 90 would try a new service: 90 ÷ 250 × 100 = **36%**.

**Average.** 90 people who would try it say they would spend a total of 1,350 a month: 1,350 ÷ 90 = **15.00** each.

**Comparing two results.** A social media poll shows 54 of 150 interested (36%). A street survey of 150 people in the target area shows 33 interested (22%). The street survey is likely more reliable because it reaches the wider target market rather than existing followers.

## 8. Exam traps

- Government and market reports are **secondary**, even if very recent.
- Focus groups give mostly **qualitative** data.
- Research **reduces** risk; it does not remove it.
- Do not scale a small sample up to the whole market without commenting on reliability.
- "Cheap" and "quick" are only half an answer; say what the method finds out.
- Purposes must be linked to a decision in the case, not listed.
- For small businesses, budget and time often decide the method; for large ones, accuracy may justify cost.

## 9. Answer frames

- **Outline** a benefit of observation: shows real behaviour, so the business sees what customers actually do rather than what they say.
- **Explain** a limitation of a focus group: small group → may not represent customers → decisions based on it may be wrong.
- **Justify** a method: what must be found out → budget and time in the case → strength of the chosen method → weakness of the other → decision.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two purposes of market research from the specification.
2. Is a market report primary or secondary research?
3. Name the primary method that uses real sales in a limited area.
4. What type of data does an open question produce?
5. Give an example of a leading question.
6. Give one strength of observation.
7. 60 of 240 people say they would buy. Calculate the percentage.
8. Give one advantage of secondary research.
9. State one way social media can collect market research data.
10. Give one reason why social media data may be unreliable.
11. Why does a representative sample matter?
12. Why might a small business choose a questionnaire rather than test marketing?

### Answers

1. Any two of: identify and understand customer needs; identify gaps in the market; reduce risk; inform business decisions.
2. Secondary.
3. Test marketing.
4. Qualitative.
5. Any question that suggests the answer, for example "Wouldn't you agree our service is excellent?"
6. It records what people actually do rather than what they say.
7. 60 ÷ 240 × 100 = 25%.
8. It is usually cheaper and quicker to obtain than primary research.
9. Any one of: polls, reading comments or reviews, tracking reactions to posts, monitoring what customers say about competitors.
10. Followers may not represent the whole market, or some respondents may never buy.
11. If respondents match the target market, the results are more likely to reflect what real customers want.
12. It is cheaper and faster, and does not need the product to be made and distributed first.
