## Questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by internal economies of scale and external economies of scale, and give one example of each. **[4]**

**2.** A firm's long run total cost is £84,000 at 12,000 units and £120,000 at 20,000 units.

**(a)** Calculate long run average cost at each output. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the percentage change in average cost and say what it shows. **[2]**

**3.** A fictional quarry has the long run average costs below.

| Output (thousand tonnes) | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LRAC (£ per tonne) | 9.50 | 8.00 | 7.00 | 6.40 | 6.40 | 6.80 |

**(a)** State the minimum efficient scale. **[1]**
**(b)** The market buys 140 thousand tonnes. Calculate how many firms could operate at that scale. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how far above the minimum a firm producing 10 thousand tonnes has its average cost. **[2]**

**4.** Explain the relationship between short run average cost and long run average cost. **[4]**

**5.** A firm faces the demand curve P = 120 − 4Q, where P is price in pounds and Q is units sold. Calculate total revenue, average revenue and marginal revenue for the third unit, then explain why marginal revenue is below average revenue. **[4]**

**6.** For the firm in question 5:

**(a)** Calculate PED at a price of £80. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the effect on total revenue of cutting price from £80 to £70, and explain the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the output at which total revenue is highest and state PED there. **[2]**

**7.** A small print shop (illustrative) has revenue of £96,000 and explicit costs of £61,000. The owner could earn £28,000 elsewhere and has £50,000 of savings in the business that could earn 5%.

**(a)** Calculate accounting profit. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate economic profit. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain whether the shop earns supernormal profit. **[2]**

**8.** Three firms are described below.

| Firm | Output | AR (£) | AC (£) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 250 | 14.00 | 11.00 |
| R | 600 | 6.50 | 6.50 |
| S | 180 | 9.00 | 12.50 |

**(a)** For each firm, work out revenue, cost and the resulting profit or loss. **[3]**
**(b)** Identify which firm earns normal profit and which makes a loss, with reasons. **[2]**

**9.** A firm faces P = 100 − 2Q and has total cost TC = 80 + 10Q + Q². Show that profit is maximised at 15 units, find the price and the maximum profit, and check against the neighbouring outputs. **[7]**

**10.** Demand for the firm in question 9 falls to P = 40 − 2Q. Calculate its output and loss if it minimises its loss, and explain whether it should keep producing. **[5]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that economies of scale always benefit consumers. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Internal economies are falls in long run average cost that result from the firm itself growing larger [1], for example bulk-buying discounts or a larger machine [1]. External economies are falls in costs that result from the whole industry growing, so that every firm gains [1], for example a pool of skilled labour or specialist suppliers locating nearby [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns its credit by saying where the cost saving comes from, the firm or the industry. Naming an example without that distinction does not show the difference.

**2. (a)** 84,000/12,000 = **£7.00** [1]; 120,000/20,000 = **£6.00** [1]
**(b)** (6.00 − 7.00)/7.00 × 100 = **−14.3%** [1]. Output rose 66.7% but total cost only 42.9%, so average cost fell: economies of scale [1].
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value, not the new one, and state the sign. A fall described as a positive percentage is easy to misread.

**3. (a)** The smallest output at which LRAC reaches its minimum of £6.40 is **20 thousand tonnes** [1]
**(b)** 140/20 [1] = **7 firms** [1]
**(c)** (8.00 − 6.40)/6.40 × 100 [1] = **25%** above the minimum [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both 20 and 25 give £6.40, but MES is the first output reaching it. Choosing 25 is a common slip.

**4.** Each plant size has its own SRAC, because at least one factor is fixed [1]. The LRAC shows the lowest cost of each output when the firm can choose the best plant [1]. It is the envelope of the SRAC curves, touching each and never lying above any [1]. Its downward slope reflects economies of scale and its upward slope diseconomies, whereas the SRAC curves' shape comes from the fixed factor [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A diagram description naming both curves and the touching points adds precision, but the written explanation must still say why the LRAC lies below.

**5.** At 3 units, P = 120 − 12 = £108, so AR = **£108** [1]. TR = 3 × 108 = **£324** [1]. TR at 2 units = 2 × 112 = 224, so MR = 324 − 224 = **£100** [1]. MR is below AR because to sell the extra unit the firm must cut the price on all earlier units too, losing revenue on them [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate MR as a change in TR. Reading the price off the demand curve gives AR instead.

**6. (a)** At P = 80, Q = (120 − 80)/4 = 10 [1]. PED = −(1/4) × (80/10) = **−2** [1]
**(b)** At 70, Q = 12.5. TR rises from 800 to 875 [1] because demand is elastic, so the percentage rise in quantity exceeds the fall in price [1].
**(c)** MR = 120 − 8Q = 0 gives **Q = 15** [1]. P = 60, so PED = −(1/4) × (60/15) = **−1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the sign of the revenue change to the elasticity. A TR figure with no reference to PED answers only half the question.

**7. (a)** 96,000 − 61,000 = **£35,000** [1]
**(b)** Implicit costs = 28,000 + 0.05 × 50,000 = £30,500 [1]. Economic profit = 35,000 − 30,500 = **£4,500** [1]
**(c)** Yes [1]: economic profit is positive, so the shop earns £4,500 above the normal profit that covers the owner's forgone earnings [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that normal profit is already inside the implicit costs. A statement that the owner is simply well paid misses the idea.

**8. (a)** P: TR = 250 × 14 = £3,500, TC = 250 × 11 = £2,750, profit **+£750** [1]. R: TR = TC = **£3,900**, profit **£0** [1]. S: TR = 180 × 9 = £1,620, TC = 180 × 12.50 = £2,250, **loss £630** [1]
**(b)** R earns normal profit because AR equals AC, which includes normal profit [1]. S makes a loss because AR is below AC and it earns less than the owner could elsewhere [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the comparison, for example AR below AC, as part of the reasoning. A label with no link to the figures reads as a guess.

**9.** MR = 100 − 4Q [1]; MC = 10 + 2Q [1]. Setting equal: 100 − 4Q = 10 + 2Q gives 6Q = 90, so **Q = 15** [1]. Price = 100 − 30 = **£70** [1]. TR = 1,050 and TC = 80 + 150 + 225 = 455 [1], so profit = **£595** [1]. At 14 units profit is 1,008 − 416 = 592 and at 16 units it is 1,088 − 496 = 592, both lower [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Read price from the demand curve at the chosen output. A price taken from the MC curve is a frequent error.

**10.** MR = 40 − 4Q [1]. Setting 40 − 4Q = 10 + 2Q gives **Q = 5** and P = **£30** [1]. TR = 150 and TC = 80 + 50 + 25 = 155, so the **loss is £5** [1]. AVC = 10 + 5 = £15, below the price of £30 [1], so revenue covers all variable cost and part of fixed cost. Closing would lose the full fixed cost of £80, so the firm should keep producing [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A loss alone does not justify closure in the short run. Compare price with AVC, or the loss with the fixed cost.

**11.** Economies of scale are falls in LRAC as output rises, for example from technical or purchasing sources [1]. Lower costs allow lower prices [1], and in a competitive market rivals with similar costs force price cuts, so consumers gain [1]. External economies lower costs for all firms in an industry, which also spreads the benefit [1]. However, if the minimum efficient scale is large relative to the market, few firms survive [1], and a dominant firm with a cost advantage can keep the saving as supernormal profit [1]. Its cost advantage also acts as a barrier to entry, which can reduce choice and allow higher prices [1]. At very large outputs, diseconomies can reverse the gain [1]. The benefit may also come late, after large investment [1]. Overall, consumers gain only when competition passes the saving on, so the view is true only in some markets [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point works best as a chain from cause to effect on consumers. Finish with a judgement tied to market structure; a summary of earlier points adds little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating percentage change from the new value instead of the old.
- Giving MR as the price, or reading AR off the wrong curve.
- Describing economies of scale without saying whether they are internal or external.
- Stating MES with no link to market size.
- Calling accounting profit supernormal profit.
- Forgetting to subtract implicit costs.
- Taking the profit-maximising price from the MC curve.
- Calling every loss a reason to close without comparing price with AVC.
