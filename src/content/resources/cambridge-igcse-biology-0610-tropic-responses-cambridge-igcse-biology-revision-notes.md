These notes condense sub-topic **14.5 Tropic responses** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 are Core. Outcomes 4 and 5 (auxin) are Supplement content, labelled **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked experiments, read the [tropic responses study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-tropic-responses-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first. Then try the [tropic responses practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-tropic-responses-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## Core recall

**Gravitropism**: a response in which parts of a plant grow **towards or away from gravity**.

**Phototropism**: a response in which parts of a plant grow **towards or away from the direction of the light source**.

| | Light from one side | Gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot | grows towards it | grows away from it (up) |
| Root | grows away from it | grows towards it (down) |

**Why the responses matter**

- Shoot towards light: leaves receive more light for photosynthesis.
- Shoot away from gravity: a germinating seedling grows up through the soil towards light.
- Root towards gravity: anchorage, and access to water and mineral ions in the soil.

**Tropisms are growth responses.** One side of the organ grows faster than the other, so the organ curves. They take hours to days.

## Investigation checklist (Core)

| Step | Phototropism (shoots) | Gravitropism (roots and shoots) |
|---|---|---|
| Material | several seedlings, same species and age | several germinating seeds pinned to damp cotton wool |
| Stimulus | light through one slit in a box | gravity, with seeds placed in different orientations |
| Remove the other stimulus | keep temperature and water the same | keep in the **dark** |
| Control | light from all sides, or a slowly rotating clinostat | a clinostat rotating so gravity acts on all sides equally |
| Measure | angle of bending from vertical, mean of several | direction of root and shoot growth after a few days |

**Results to expect**

- One-sided light: shoots bend towards the slit; seedlings lit from all sides grow straight up.
- Fixed seeds in the dark: roots curve down and shoots curve up from any starting position.
- Clinostat: roots and shoots keep growing in their starting direction.

**Controlled variables** to name: species, age, number of seedlings, temperature, water supply, light intensity, time.

## Auxin -- Extended only

The four syllabus points, in order:

1. Auxin is **made in the shoot tip**.
2. Auxin **diffuses** through the plant from the shoot tip.
3. Auxin is **unequally distributed** in response to light and gravity.
4. Auxin **stimulates cell elongation**.

| Situation | Side with more auxin | Which side elongates more | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoot lit from one side | shaded side | shaded side | curves towards light |
| Shoot lying horizontally | lower side | lower side | curves upwards |

**Chemical control**: phototropism and gravitropism of a shoot are examples of the **chemical control of plant growth** because a chemical made in one place (the tip) changes the growth of cells in another place (the region below the tip).

**Classic experiment results**

- Tip removed: no bending.
- Opaque cap on the tip: no bending, so the tip detects the light.
- Opaque sleeve below the tip: still bends.
- Agar block that held a tip, placed on one side of a cut shoot in the dark: shoot bends away from that side. A chemical diffused into the agar.
- Plain agar block: no bending (control).

**Syllabus limit**: the auxin explanation is for **shoots only**. Describe root responses; do not explain them with auxin.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tropic response | growth of part of a plant in a direction set by a stimulus |
| Phototropism | growth towards or away from the direction of the light source |
| Gravitropism | growth towards or away from gravity |
| Stimulus | the change that causes the response: one-sided light or gravity |
| Auxin | the plant growth substance made in the shoot tip (Extended) |
| Cell elongation | cells getting longer; the effect of auxin (Extended) |
| Clinostat | a slowly rotating holder that removes a one-sided stimulus, used as a control |
| Chemical control | control of growth by a substance made in one part of the plant (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- "Turns" or "moves" towards light: write **grows**.
- Auxin causing cell **division**: it is cell **elongation**.
- More auxin on the **lit** side: wrong. Shaded side, and lower side for a horizontal shoot.
- Light left on during a gravitropism investigation.
- Missing "towards" or "away from" in a definition.
- No control, or a vague control such as "normal conditions".
- Root directions reversed: roots grow **down**, towards gravity, and away from light.
- Stopping an auxin explanation before the final step: the effect on cells and the direction of curvature.

## Self-test

**1.** Define phototropism.

**2.** Define gravitropism.

**3.** State the direction a root grows in response to gravity.

**4.** State the direction a shoot grows in response to light from one side.

**5.** Why are germinating seeds kept in the dark in a gravitropism investigation?

**6.** What is a clinostat used for in a tropism investigation?

**7.** Name two variables that should be kept the same when comparing seedlings in one-sided light and all-round light.

**8.** *(Extended)* Where is auxin made?

**9.** *(Extended)* What effect does auxin have on shoot cells?

**10.** *(Extended)* In a shoot lit from the right, which side has more auxin, and which way does the shoot bend?

**11.** *(Extended)* An opaque cap is placed over a shoot tip, and the shoot is lit from one side. Predict the result and explain it.

**12.** *(Extended)* Why are shoot tropisms described as chemical control of growth?

### Answers

**1.** A response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from the direction of the light source.

**2.** A response in which parts of a plant grow towards or away from gravity.

**3.** Towards gravity, downwards.

**4.** Towards the light.

**5.** So that light is not a second stimulus; any curvature is caused by gravity alone.

**6.** It rotates slowly so a stimulus such as gravity or one-sided light acts on all sides equally; it acts as the control.

**7.** Any two of: species, age, number of seedlings, temperature, water, light intensity, time.

**8.** In the shoot tip.

**9.** It stimulates cell elongation.

**10.** The left (shaded) side has more auxin; the shoot bends to the right, towards the light.

**11.** It grows straight up. The tip detects the light, so with the tip covered auxin is not unequally distributed.

**12.** A chemical (auxin) made in the shoot tip diffuses to the cells below, where it changes their growth.
