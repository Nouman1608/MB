## Key words

- **Terminating decimal:** stops after a finite number of digits (0.125).
- **Recurring decimal:** a digit or block repeats forever (0.1666...). (Higher tier only for conversions)
- **Dot notation:** a dot over the first and last digit of the repeating block. 0.1̇6̇ = 0.161616..., but 0.16̇ = 0.1666...
- **Operator:** something you do to a number. "2/3 of" means "× 2/3".
- **Multiplier:** the single number you multiply by to apply a percentage or fraction change in one step.

## Equivalents to know by heart (N10)

You save time on Paper 1 if these are instant.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |

| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |
| 1/20 | 0.05 | 5% |
| 1/25 | 0.04 | 4% |
| 1/40 | 0.025 | 2.5% |

## Converting: method in steps

**Decimal → fraction (terminating)**

1. Count the decimal places: 1 place → /10, 2 places → /100, 3 places → /1000.
2. Write the digits over that power of 10.
3. Cancel by common factors until none is left.

**Fraction → decimal**

1. If the denominator divides into 10, 100, 1000 or 10000, scale up to it.
2. Otherwise divide the numerator by the denominator (short division, add zeros after the point).
3. If a remainder repeats, the digits will repeat: you have a recurring decimal.

Check: in simplest form, a fraction terminates only when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.

**Percentage ↔ decimal ↔ fraction**

- Percentage → decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places left). 4.5% = 0.045.
- Decimal → percentage: multiply by 100. 1.3 = 130%.
- Percentage → fraction: put over 100 and simplify. 85% = 85/100 = 17/20.

## Ordering (N10)

1. Convert every value to a decimal.
2. Pad with zeros so all have the same number of decimal places.
3. Compare column by column from the left.
4. Write the answer using the **original** values.

Negative values reverse the order: −0.45 is smaller than −0.4.

## Recurring decimals to fractions (Higher tier only)

**Method in steps**

1. Let x = the decimal and write out at least two repeats.
2. Multiply by 10ⁿ, where n = the number of digits in the repeating block, so that one block crosses the point. If there are non-repeating digits after the point, multiply x by another power of 10 that moves only those across.
3. Line up the two equations so the recurring tails match. Subtract.
4. Solve for x and simplify.

| Decimal | Multiply to get | Subtract | Fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4̇ | 10x = 4.444... | 9x = 4 | 4/9 |
| 0.7̇2̇ | 100x = 72.7272... | 99x = 72 | 8/11 |
| 0.23̇ | 100x and 10x | 90x = 21 | 7/30 |

**Small worked reminder.** 0.23̇ = 0.2333...

```
10x  =  2.333...
100x = 23.333...
90x  = 21     ->   x = 21/90 = 7/30
```

**Fraction → recurring decimal:** divide until a remainder repeats. 5/9 = 0.5̇. 7/30 = 0.2333... = 0.23̇.

For a "show that" question, the full algebra must be written. Quoting "two digits recurring goes over 99" with no working does not show anything.

## Fractions in ratio (N11)

For a ratio **a : b**:

| You want | Fraction |
|---|---|
| First quantity as a fraction of the total | a/(a + b) |
| Second quantity as a fraction of the total | b/(a + b) |
| First as a fraction of the second | a/b |
| Second as a fraction of the first | b/a |

**Going the other way:** if X is 4/5 of Y, then X : Y = 4 : 5. If X is 4/9 of a total, then X : rest = 4 : 5.

**Small worked reminder.** Flour and sugar are mixed in the ratio 7 : 3. Flour is 7/10 of the mix. Sugar is 3/7 of the amount of flour.

**Method in steps for "share" problems that give a fraction**

1. Turn the fraction of the total into a ratio (2/9 of the total → 2 : 7).
2. Find what one part is worth from the information given (a difference, a total or one share).
3. Scale up to the quantity asked for.

## Fractions and percentages as operators (N12)

**"Of" means multiply.**

```
2/9 of 63  = 63 ÷ 9 × 2 = 14
160% of 35 = 1.6 × 35   = 56
```

**Multipliers**

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Increase by r% | 1 + r/100 |
| Decrease by r% | 1 − r/100 |
| Increase by a fraction p/q | 1 + p/q |
| Decrease by a fraction p/q | 1 − p/q |

Examples: decrease by 4.5% → × 0.955. Increase by 2/3 → × 5/3. Decrease by 3/5 → × 2/5.

**Successive changes:** multiply the multipliers, never add the percentages.

```
Increase by 2/3, then decrease by 2/5:   5/3 × 3/5 = 1   (no overall change)
```

**Reading a multiplier back:** a multiplier of 1.07 means a 7% increase. A multiplier of 0.92 means an 8% decrease. A multiplier of 2.5 means 250% of the original, which is a 150% increase.

**Undoing an operator:** divide by it. If 2/5 of a number is 18, the number is 18 ÷ 2/5 = 45.

**Fraction of a fraction:** when a fraction acts on part of a group, multiply along the chain, then add the separate branches with a common denominator.

## Must-know distinctions

- **"Percentage of" vs "increase by a percentage".** 30% of 50 is 15 (× 0.3). Increase 50 by 30% is 65 (× 1.3).
- **Fraction of the total vs fraction of the other part.** In 3 : 5, the first is 3/8 of the total but 3/5 of the second.
- **0.1̇6̇ vs 0.16̇.** Dots on both digits means both repeat. One dot means only that digit repeats.
- **Terminating vs recurring.** 3/16 = 0.1875 terminates. 3/14 recurs, because 14 has a factor of 7.
- **Rounded vs exact.** 2/3 written as 0.67 is a rounded value. On a "give your answer as a fraction" question, a rounded decimal loses the accuracy mark.

## Quick self-test

1. Write 0.85 as a fraction in its simplest form.
2. Write 3/16 as a decimal.
3. Write these in order, smallest first: 0.3, 7/25, 29%, 0.295
4. (Higher) Write 5/9 as a recurring decimal.
5. (Higher) Write 0.7̇2̇ as a fraction in its simplest form.
6. (Higher) Write 0.23̇ as a fraction in its simplest form.
7. Boys and girls in a club are in the ratio 7 : 3. What fraction of the club are boys? The girls are what fraction of the boys?
8. P is 4/5 of Q. Write P : Q.
9. Work out 2/9 of 63.
10. Write down the multiplier for a decrease of 4.5%.
11. Work out 160% of 35.
12. A price is increased by 2/3 and then the new price is decreased by 2/5. What is the overall effect?

### Answers

1. 85/100 = **17/20**
2. 3/16 = 1875/10000 = **0.1875**
3. 7/25 = 0.280, 29% = 0.290, 0.295, 0.300 → **7/25, 29%, 0.295, 0.3**
4. **0.5̇** (0.5555...)
5. 100x − x = 72, so x = 72/99 = **8/11**
6. 100x − 10x = 23.333... − 2.333... = 21, so x = 21/90 = **7/30**
7. Boys **7/10**; girls are **3/7** of the boys
8. **4 : 5**
9. 63 ÷ 9 × 2 = **14**
10. 1 − 0.045 = **0.955**
11. 1.6 × 35 = **56**
12. 5/3 × 3/5 = 1, so **no overall change**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Miscounting decimal places, so 0.045 becomes 45/100 instead of 45/1000.
- Not cancelling fully when the question says "simplest form".
- Ordering 0.3 below 0.295 because 295 is bigger than 3 as a whole number.
- Leaving the ordered answer as converted decimals rather than the values given.
- (Higher) Multiplying by 10 for a two-digit repeating block, so the tails do not cancel.
- (Higher) Misreading the dot notation and making every digit recur.
- (Higher) Quoting a shortcut on a "show that" question without the algebra.
- Turning "A gets 3/8" into the ratio 3 : 8 instead of 3 : 5.
- Using 0.3 as the multiplier for a 30% increase.
- Adding percentage changes in a two-step problem instead of multiplying the multipliers.
