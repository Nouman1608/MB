Condensed recall for section 3.5.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-identifying-and-understanding-customers/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

Understand the **importance of identifying and satisfying customer needs**, including in **local, national and international markets**, so that the business provides a product or service that customers will buy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing | Identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs profitably |
| Customer | A person or organisation that buys a business's goods or services |
| Consumer | An individual or household buying for personal use |
| Business customer | An organisation buying to use or resell |
| Target market | The group of customers the business aims to sell to |
| Customer needs | What customers want a product or service to do or provide |
| Local market | Customers in the town or region |
| National market | Customers across the country |
| International market | Customers in other countries |
| Customer loyalty | Customers choosing to buy from the same business again |

## Types of customer need

| Need | Example |
|---|---|
| Functional | Raincoat keeps out the rain; product is safe and lasts |
| Price | Value for money; affordable |
| Convenience | Easy to find, buy and use; quick delivery |
| Emotional or social | Status, belonging, enjoyment |
| Service | Advice, aftercare, easy returns |

Needs change over time, so businesses must keep checking.

## Why identify customer needs?

- Sell products customers **will buy**; lower risk of failure.
- **Increase sales and revenue**.
- Build **satisfaction and loyalty**.
- Stay ahead of **competitors**.
- Use **resources** wisely.
- Spot **changes and opportunities**.

## What if a business gets it wrong?

- Unsold stock and wasted costs.
- Falling sales and profit.
- Poor reputation.
- Wasted investment.

Example: 20,000 bottles cost $20,000; only 6,000 sell at $1.50 = $9,000, so the loss is $11,000.

## How to find out

- Sales records.
- Customer feedback: complaints, reviews, comment cards.
- Surveys and interviews.
- Observation of behaviour.
- Competitor analysis.

Percentage of respondents = number saying yes / number asked x 100. Example: 260 / 400 x 100 = 65%.

## Three kinds of market

| Market | Understand |
|---|---|
| Local | Local tastes, nearby rivals, what residents can afford |
| National | Regional differences, national distribution and advertising |
| International | Language, culture, religion, income, laws and standards, climate |

International selling often needs the product, price, packaging or advertising to be **adapted**.

## Adapting for another market

Cost of adapting = translation + product changes + other costs.
Extra profit = extra units x profit per unit.
Net gain = extra profit - cost of adapting.

Example: $12,000 cost; 3,200 units x $4 = $12,800; net gain $800.

## Satisfying needs

- Right features, quality and price.
- Good service.
- Easy to find and buy.
- Accurate communication.
- Listening to feedback and improving.

Sign of success: repeat purchases, good reviews, few complaints.

## Exam traps

- Marketing is not only advertising. It begins with identifying needs.
- Needs differ between markets and change over time.
- Do not assume overseas customers want the same product.
- Link every point to **sales, profit or loyalty**.
- Use details from the case: the type of customer, the market, the product.
- Consumers and business customers have different needs.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "analyse" question

Question style: "Analyse why a snack maker should research customers' needs before selling abroad."

1. **Different needs:** customers abroad may prefer different flavours or pack sizes, so the same product may not sell.
2. **Cost of getting it wrong:** translating labels and making a new flavour costs $12,000; if the product does not sell, this is a loss.
3. **Opportunity:** if research shows demand, the extra sales (for example 3,200 packs at $4 profit) can exceed the cost.
4. **Link:** identifying needs first reduces the risk, and satisfied customers will return.

## Revision checklist

- I can define marketing and target market.
- I can explain five types of customer need.
- I can give six reasons why identifying needs is important.
- I can describe the effects of getting customer needs wrong.
- I can list ways of finding out what customers want.
- I can explain how local, national and international markets differ.
- I can calculate the cost and benefit of adapting a product for a new market.

## Command words for this subtopic

- **Define:** use the exact meaning, such as "the group of customers a business aims to sell to" for target market.
- **Explain:** need, reason, consequence for sales or profit.
- **Calculate:** write the formula, show the numbers, give units and a percentage sign.
- **Evaluate:** compare extra profit with the cost of adapting, consider the risk that the forecast is wrong, and make a recommendation.

## Self-test

1. Define marketing.
2. Distinguish between a consumer and a business customer.
3. Give two types of customer need.
4. State two reasons why a business should identify customer needs.
5. State two ways of finding out what customers want.
6. Explain one risk of selling a product that does not meet customers' needs.
7. 150 of 600 customers surveyed want a new flavour. Calculate the percentage.
8. Give two ways that international customers may differ from local customers.
9. Sales are $60,000 local, $90,000 national and $50,000 international. Calculate national sales as a percentage of the total.
10. It costs $9,000 to adapt a product for a new country, and extra sales bring $2.50 profit on 4,000 units. Calculate the net gain.
11. Why do customer needs change over time?
12. Explain how listening to customer feedback could increase sales.

### Answers

1. Identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs profitably.
2. A consumer buys for personal use; a business customer buys to use in its business or to resell.
3. Any two: functional; price; convenience; emotional or social; service.
4. Any two: to sell products customers will buy; to increase sales; to build loyalty; to stay ahead of rivals.
5. Any two: sales records; feedback; surveys; observation; competitor analysis.
6. Unsold stock and wasted costs, so the business loses money.
7. 150 / 600 x 100 = **25%**.
8. Any two: language; culture or religion; income; laws and standards; climate.
9. Total = $200,000; 90,000 / 200,000 x 100 = **45%**.
10. 4,000 x $2.50 = $10,000; net gain = $10,000 - $9,000 = **$1,000**.
11. Tastes, incomes, technology and fashions change.
12. It shows what customers dislike or want, so the business can change its product or service to satisfy them and attract more buyers.
