## Which papers test this

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Core | not allowed | yes | **no** (excluded by the syllabus) |
| Paper 2 | Extended | not allowed | yes | **no** (excluded by the syllabus) |
| Paper 3 | Core | scientific calculator required | yes | yes |
| Paper 4 | Extended | scientific calculator required | yes | yes |

On Papers 3 and 4 the syllabus sets three accuracy rules:

- Give non-exact answers to **3 significant figures**, or **1 decimal place** for angles in degrees, unless the question says otherwise.
- Avoid rounding until the final answer. If a later part uses an earlier answer, use the **unrounded** value.
- Use the **π key** or **3.142**.

## 1.9.1 Rounding: the rules

| Accuracy asked for | Where you count from | Example: 0.006 382 | Example: 5097 |
|---|---|---|---|
| nearest 10 / 100 / 1000 | that column | -- | 5100 (nearest 100) |
| decimal places (d.p.) | the decimal point | 0.006 (3 d.p.) | -- |
| significant figures (s.f.) | the first **non-zero** digit | 0.0064 (2 s.f.) | 5100 (2 s.f.) |

**Method in steps**

```
1. Mark the last digit you keep.
2. Look ONLY at the next digit.
3. 5 or more -> round the kept digit up; 4 or less -> leave it.
4. Fill gaps before the decimal point with zeros. Keep trailing zeros after it if they are needed to show the accuracy.
```

**Small reminders**

```
7.4951  to 2 d.p.  -> 7.50        (the 0 must stay: 2 d.p. means two digits after the point)
299 600 to 3 s.f.  -> 300 000     (9 rounds up and carries twice)
8.0449  to 1 d.p.  -> 8.0         (look at the 4 only; do not round 8.0449 -> 8.045 -> 8.05 -> 8.1)
```

The last line is the "double rounding" trap. Round once, from the original number.

**Must-know distinctions**

- **Decimal places vs significant figures.** 0.006 382 to 2 d.p. is 0.01; to 2 s.f. it is 0.0064. Read the question twice.
- **Leading zeros vs middle zeros.** In 0.0064 the zeros before the 6 are not significant. In 5.07 the 0 is significant.
- **Placeholder zeros.** 5097 to 2 s.f. is 5100, not 51.

## 1.9.2 Estimating: the method

```
1. Round EVERY number to 1 s.f. (unless told otherwise).
2. WRITE the rounded calculation down -- this is the method mark.
3. Work it out mentally. Give that as the estimate.
```

**Worked reminder.** Estimate (38.6 × 5.12) ÷ 0.207.

```
38.6 -> 40     5.12 -> 5     0.207 -> 0.2
(40 x 5) / 0.2 = 200 / 0.2 = 1000
```

Estimate **1000**. (The exact value is 954.7…)

Useful mental facts for estimates:

| Calculation | Same as |
|---|---|
| ÷ 0.5 | × 2 |
| ÷ 0.2 | × 5 |
| ÷ 0.1 | × 10 |
| × 0.5 | ÷ 2 |

Dividing by a number between 0 and 1 makes the answer **bigger**. If your estimate went down, check it.

Estimates apply to measurements too: a 2.9 m by 3.8 m wall is about 3 × 4 = 12 m².

## 1.9.3 Reasonable accuracy in context

| Context | Rule |
|---|---|
| calculator paper, non-exact answer | 3 s.f. (angles 1 d.p.) unless told otherwise |
| exact answer | give it exactly, e.g. 2.5, not 2.50 or "2.5 to 3 s.f." |
| money | 2 d.p. (nearest cent): a display of 12.6 dollars is written **$12.60** |
| "how many are needed" | round **up** |
| "how many can be bought / fit / filled" | round **down** |

**Worked reminder.** 230 eggs are packed in boxes of 6. 230 ÷ 6 = 38.33…

- Boxes needed to hold all the eggs: **39**.
- Full boxes that can be filled: **38**.

Same division, two different answers. The wording decides.

## 1.14.1 Using the calculator efficiently

- Do **not** round inside a calculation. Carry full values with the **Ans** key or memory.
- Put **brackets** round a numerator or denominator with more than one term.
- Use the **×10ˣ** (EXP) key for standard form.
- Use the **π** key.
- Do a rough **estimate** first, so a mis-keyed decimal point stands out.

## 1.14.2 Entering values

**Time: minutes ÷ 60.**

| Time | In hours |
|---|---|
| 15 min | 0.25 h |
| 45 min | 0.75 h |
| 24 min | 0.4 h |
| 3 h 20 min | 3.333… h (enter 3 + 20 ÷ 60, do not type 3.33) |

Or use the degrees–minutes–seconds key: 3° 20′ 0″ is 3 h 20 min.

Never type 3 h 20 min as 3.20.

**Other entries:** convert units first (cm to m, g to kg) so every value is in the same unit; bracket negative numbers before squaring them.

## 1.14.3 Reading the display

**Decimal hours to hours and minutes: decimal part × 60.**

```
1.85 h  -> 0.85 x 60 = 51    -> 1 h 51 min
5.15 h  -> 0.15 x 60 = 9     -> 5 h 9 min      (not 5 h 15 min)
```

**Standard form display.** A display such as 2.3E-05 or 2.3×10⁻⁰⁵ means 2.3 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.000 023. Write it in proper notation, never "E-05".

**Money.** A display of 6.4 for dollars is written **$6.40**.
A display of 2.666 666 7 for dollars is written **$2.67**.

**"Write down all the figures on your calculator display."** Copy every digit, unrounded. Round in the next step only.

## Quick self-test

1. Round 3 849 162 to (a) the nearest thousand, (b) 3 significant figures.
2. Round 0.040 96 to (a) 2 significant figures, (b) 3 decimal places.
3. Round 19.997 to 2 decimal places.
4. (non-calculator) By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate (9.83 × 21.4) ÷ 0.496.
5. (non-calculator) By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate √(81.6 + 18.7).
6. (calculator) Write 2 hours 24 minutes in hours as a decimal.
7. (calculator) A calculation gives a time of 5.4 hours. Write this in hours and minutes.
8. 320 people need coach seats. Each coach holds 52 people. How many coaches are needed?
9. (calculator) Work out (5.2 + 3.75) ÷ (1.6 × 0.95). Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
10. Notebooks cost $2.70 each. How many can be bought with 40 dollars?
11. (calculator) The display shows 1.2E-05. Write the value as an ordinary number.
12. (calculator) A train travels 84 km in 1 hour 36 minutes. Find its average speed.

### Answers

1. (a) **3 849 000** (b) **3 850 000**
2. (a) **0.041** (b) **0.041** (here both give the same digits)
3. **20.00** (the 9s carry; keep both zeros)
4. (10 × 20) ÷ 0.5 = 200 ÷ 0.5 = **400** (exact 424.1…)
5. √(80 + 20) = √100 = **10**
6. 24 ÷ 60 = 0.4, so **2.4 hours**
7. 0.4 × 60 = 24, so **5 hours 24 minutes**
8. 320 ÷ 52 = 6.15…, round up: **7 coaches**
9. 8.95 ÷ 1.52 = 5.888… = **5.89** (brackets round top and bottom)
10. 40 ÷ 2.70 = 14.8…, round down: **14 notebooks**
11. 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ = **0.000 012**
12. 1 h 36 min = 1.6 h; 84 ÷ 1.6 = **52.5 km/h** (exact, so no rounding needed)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping a needed zero: 0.040 to 2 s.f., 7.50 to 2 d.p., 20.00 to 2 d.p.
- Writing 3849 instead of 3 849 000 when rounding to the nearest thousand.
- Counting leading zeros in a small decimal as significant figures.
- Double rounding: rounding a rounded value instead of rounding once from the original.
- Giving an estimate without writing the 1 s.f. values, or rounding only some of the numbers.
- Using normal rounding for whole objects instead of reading "needed" (up) or "can be bought" (down).
- Typing 1 h 36 min as 1.36, or reading 5.15 hours as 5 hours 15 minutes.
- Rounding an intermediate answer and carrying it forward, so the final 3 s.f. answer is out by one in the last digit.
- Leaving money as 6.4 dollars instead of $6.40, or copying "E-05" from the screen as the answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are estimation and calculator use different for Core and Extended 0580?**
No. The Core (C1.9, C1.14) and Extended (E1.9, E1.14) statements for estimation and using a calculator are identical in Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580; the tier only changes which papers you sit.

**How do you estimate a calculation for Cambridge IGCSE Maths?**
Round every number to 1 significant figure, write the rounded values down, then work out the simpler calculation. Giving an estimate without the 1 s.f. values, or rounding only some of the numbers, loses method credit.

**What is double rounding?**
Double rounding is rounding a value that has already been rounded instead of rounding once from the original. It can change the final digit, so always round from the full value.

**How do you enter 1 hour 36 minutes into a calculator?**
Convert the minutes to a fraction of an hour: 1 h 36 min = 1 + 36/60 = 1.6 hours. Typing 1.36 treats the minutes as hundredths of an hour.
