## What the specification asks for

- Relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass in terms of ¹²C.
- The term relative formula mass will be used for ionic compounds.
- You should be able to define relative atomic mass (Ar) and relative molecular mass (Mr).

The definitions are short, but examiners mark them word by word, and calculation questions in every later topic assume you can find Mr quickly and accurately.

## Why chemists need a relative scale

A single atom has a mass of the order of 10⁻²³ g or less, far too small to use directly. Instead, masses are compared with a standard. The standard chosen is the **carbon-12 atom**, which is given a mass of exactly 12 units. One-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom is therefore the unit against which everything else is measured.

Because the scale is a comparison of two masses, **Ar and Mr have no units**.

## The definitions

| Quantity | Definition |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | The average (weighted mean) mass of an atom of an element relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative isotopic mass | The mass of an atom of one isotope relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | The average (weighted mean) mass of a molecule relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative formula mass | The same idea applied to the formula unit of an ionic compound, which contains ions in a lattice, not molecules |

### Why "average"?

Most elements are mixtures of isotopes. Chlorine is about 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37, so the average mass of a chlorine atom is (35 × 75 + 37 × 25) ÷ 100 = 35.5. That is why most Ar values are not whole numbers. Relative isotopic mass, by contrast, refers to a single isotope and is very close to its mass number.

### Why "relative formula mass" for ionic compounds?

Sodium chloride is a giant lattice of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; there is no NaCl molecule. The formula NaCl gives only the ratio of ions, so the mass calculated from it is a relative formula mass. The symbol Mr is commonly used for both, but the words "relative molecular mass" should be kept for substances made of molecules.

## Calculating Mr

Add the Ar values of every atom in the formula, using the values given in the question or the Periodic Table supplied in the exam.

- A subscript multiplies the atom it follows: H₂O has two H atoms.
- A subscript after a bracket multiplies everything inside: Al₂(SO₄)₃ has 3 S atoms and 12 O atoms.
- A dot in a hydrate means "add": CuSO₄·5H₂O adds five whole water molecules to CuSO₄.

### Worked example 1: a hydrated salt

**Question.** Calculate the relative formula mass of hydrated calcium nitrate, Ca(NO₃)₂·4H₂O. (Ar: H 1.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Ca 40.1)

**Method.**

1. Count atoms: Ca 1; N 2; O 6 (from the nitrates) + 4 (from the water) = 10; H 8.
2. Mass = 40.1 + 2 × 14.0 + 10 × 16.0 + 8 × 1.0
3. = 40.1 + 28.0 + 160.0 + 8.0 = **236.1**

Writing out the atom count first is the step that prevents the commonest slip: forgetting that the oxygen in the water adds to the oxygen in the anion.

## Working backwards from Mr

Questions often give you Mr and ask for something unknown.

### Worked example 2: identifying an element

**Question.** A metal M forms a chloride MCl₂ with relative formula mass 95.3. Identify M. (Ar: Cl 35.5)

**Method.** Mass of two Cl = 2 × 35.5 = 71.0. So Ar(M) = 95.3 − 71.0 = 24.3. The element with Ar 24.3 is **magnesium**.

### Worked example 3: water of crystallisation

**Question.** Hydrated zinc sulfate, ZnSO₄·xH₂O, has Mr = 287.5. Find x. (Ar: H 1.0, O 16.0, S 32.1, Zn 65.4)

**Method.**

1. Mr(ZnSO₄) = 65.4 + 32.1 + 4 × 16.0 = 161.5
2. Mass of water = 287.5 − 161.5 = 126.0
3. Mr(H₂O) = 18.0, so x = 126.0 ÷ 18.0 = **7**

### Percentage by mass of an element

Percentage of element = (number of atoms × Ar ÷ Mr) × 100. For ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃ (Mr 80.0), the percentage of nitrogen is 2 × 14.0 ÷ 80.0 × 100 = 35.0%. This is the reverse of the empirical formula method in 3.1.2.4.

## Where the numbers come from

Ar values are found by mass spectrometry of elements, from the masses and abundances of their isotopes (subtopic 3.1.1.2). Mr of a molecular substance can be found directly from the molecular ion peak in its mass spectrum. The two subtopics therefore support each other: you may be asked to calculate Ar from a spectrum and then use it to find an Mr.

## Relative mass and molar mass

Mr has no units. **Molar mass**, the mass of one mole of a substance, has units of g mol⁻¹ and is numerically equal to Mr. Water has Mr = 18.0 and molar mass 18.0 g mol⁻¹. The mole is developed in subtopic 3.1.2.2.

## Common mistakes

- Giving units for Ar or Mr (such as "g") when the question asks for a relative mass.
- Leaving out "average" or "one-twelfth" or "carbon-12" from a definition.
- Defining Ar as "the mass of an atom compared with carbon": the comparison is with one-twelfth of a carbon-12 atom.
- Multiplying only the first atom inside a bracket: Ca(OH)₂ has two O and two H.
- Forgetting the water in a hydrate, or adding only one water molecule when the formula shows five.
- Using the atomic number instead of Ar.
- Calling the formula mass of an ionic compound a "relative molecular mass" in a definition question.
- Rounding Ar values to whole numbers when the question gives them to one decimal place.

## Exam technique for OxfordAQA command words

- **Define**: give every element of the definition: average mass, of an atom (or molecule), relative to one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
- **Calculate**: show the sum with the Ar values substituted; give the answer to the same precision as the data (usually one decimal place).
- **Deduce** or **Identify**: when working backwards, show the subtraction or division clearly, then name the element and quote its Ar as justification.
- **Explain**: for "why is Ar not a whole number", refer to isotopes and a weighted mean.
