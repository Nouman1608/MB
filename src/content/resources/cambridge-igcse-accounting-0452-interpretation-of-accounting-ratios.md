## What 6.2 covers

The syllabus lists:

1. how to prepare and comment on statements showing comparison of results for
   different years
2. how to interpret the ratios calculated in 6.1
3. make suggestions and recommendations for improving profitability, liquidity and
   working capital
4. the gross profit margin and the profit margin as indicators of a business's
   profitability
5. how gross profit for the year can be affected by the valuation of inventory,
   sales quantity and changes in selling and purchasing prices
6. how profit for the year can be affected by changes in gross profit, other income
   and expenses
7. the reasons for the difference between cash and profit

The ratios themselves are calculated in
[Calculation and understanding of accounting ratios](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios/).

## Worked example: Hartley Garden Stores Ltd

Hartley sells one main product, a garden storage box. In 2027 it sold 10,000 boxes
at $25, each costing $15. In 2028 its supplier raised the cost to $16; Hartley kept
its price at $25 and sold 11,000 boxes after giving trade customers longer to pay.
It also bought extra inventory for a new range due in 2029.

**A comparison statement**

| | 2027 | 2028 | Change | % change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 250,000 | 275,000 | +25,000 | +10.00% |
| Cost of sales | 150,000 | 176,000 | +26,000 | +17.33% |
| Gross profit | 100,000 | 99,000 | −1,000 | −1.00% |
| Other income | 4,000 | 2,000 | −2,000 | −50.00% |
| Expenses | 62,000 | 66,000 | +4,000 | +6.45% |
| Profit for the year | 42,000 | 35,000 | −7,000 | −16.67% |

The statement shows the story before any ratio is calculated: revenue grew by a
tenth, but cost of sales grew faster, so gross profit fell slightly; lower other
income and higher expenses then cut profit by a sixth.

**Ratios**

| Ratio | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | 40.00% | 36.00% |
| Profit margin | 16.80% | 12.73% |
| Current ratio | 2.10 : 1 | 1.45 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 1.05 : 1 | 0.62 : 1 |
| Inventory turnover (days) | 45 | 62 |
| Trade receivables turnover (days) | 30 | 41 |
| Trade payables turnover (days) | 38 | 32 |

## Tracing gross profit to its causes

Gross profit moves for four reasons named in the syllabus.

| Cause | Hartley in 2028 | Effect on gross profit | Effect on gross profit margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | cost up $1 on the 10,000 boxes it sold before | −10,000 | falls |
| Sales quantity | 1,000 extra boxes at the new gross profit of $9 each | +9,000 | none on its own |
| Selling price | unchanged at $25 | none | none |
| Inventory valuation | no error reported | none | none |

Net change: −10,000 + 9,000 = **−1,000**, which agrees with the comparison statement
(100,000 to 99,000). In an exam, naming each cause, its direction and, where the
figures allow, its amount earns the marks.

The key point: more volume can raise gross profit, but **only price and cost
changes move the gross profit margin**. A closing inventory overvaluation would raise
gross profit and margin without any real improvement, which is why an inventory
error is checked before drawing conclusions.

## From gross profit to profit for the year

Profit for the year = gross profit + other income − expenses. Hartley's profit fell
by 7,000: gross profit −1,000, other income −2,000 and expenses +4,000. Expenses rose
by 4,000 in total but fell as a share of revenue, from 24.80% (62,000 ÷ 250,000) to
24.00% (66,000 ÷ 275,000). The fall in profit margin from 16.80% to 12.73% therefore
comes from the lower gross profit margin and the halving of other income, partly
offset by better control of expenses relative to sales.

## Writing a full comment

A comment that earns full marks has three parts: **the movement with figures, the
cause from the scenario, and the consequence**.

> The gross profit margin fell from 40.00% to 36.00% because the supplier raised the
> cost of each box from $15 to $16 while Hartley kept its selling price at $25. Each
> $100 of sales now earns $4 less gross profit, so the 10% increase in sales did not
> increase gross profit.

> Liquidity has weakened: the acid test ratio fell from 1.05 : 1 to 0.62 : 1, so
> Hartley now has only 62 cents of liquid assets for each dollar of current
> liabilities. Customers take 41 days to pay instead of 30, while Hartley pays
> suppliers in 32 days instead of 38, so cash is leaving faster than it arrives.

## Recommendations

Each suggestion should name an action and the ratio it improves.

| Aim | Suggestions |
|---|---|
| Profitability | Find a cheaper supplier or negotiate a bulk discount; raise the selling price slightly if customers will accept it; control expenses such as advertising; seek other income such as commission |
| Liquidity | Reduce inventory before ordering the new range; offer cash discounts for prompt payment; chase overdue customers; raise long-term finance rather than relying on an overdraft |
| Working capital | Return trade customers' credit period towards 30 days; ask suppliers for longer credit; sell slow-moving inventory |

Recommendations may conflict: raising the price could reduce the sales volume that
drove revenue growth. Saying so shows evaluation.

## Why cash and profit differ

Hartley made a profit of 35,000 in 2028 but its bank balance fell. Profit is
calculated on the matching basis; the bank balance changes only when cash moves.
Common reasons:

- inventory increased (cash paid for goods not yet sold)
- trade receivables increased (sales recorded but cash not yet received)
- trade payables decreased (suppliers paid faster)
- non-current assets bought (cash paid, only depreciation charged)
- drawings or dividends paid, or loans repaid (cash out, not expenses)
- depreciation and other non-cash expenses reduce profit but not cash, pushing the
  other way

## Common mistakes

- Describing a change ("the ratio fell") without a cause or consequence.
- Saying higher sales volume raised the gross profit margin.
- Treating a fall in the current ratio as bad without checking whether it was
  unusually high before.
- Giving recommendations that do not match the problem identified.
- Saying profit equals the increase in cash.

## Exam technique

- **Comment** and **analyse**: figures, cause, consequence.
- **Suggest** and **advise**: actions linked to the ratio, with a possible drawback.
- **Evaluate**: weigh good and bad changes and reach an overall judgement.
