## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain **one** way in which an ageing population could affect the marketing of a travel company. **[3]**

**2.** Distinguish between a legal and an ethical influence on marketing. **[3]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by greenwashing. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**4.** Harbour Handbags sells a leather bag in the UK for £80 and exports to the eurozone. At an exchange rate of €1.15 to the pound, the bag sells for €92. The pound weakens to €1.05. The price elasticity of demand for the bag in the eurozone is −2.

**(a)** Calculate the new euro price. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in the euro price, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the expected percentage change in quantity demanded, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(d)** Evaluate the likely effect of the weaker pound on Harbour Handbags. **[6]**

**5.** Harbour's economists forecast that consumers' incomes will fall by 4%. Its luxury bags have a sales value of £1.5 million and a YED of +2.2. Its basic bags have a sales value of £0.8 million and a YED of −0.5.

**(a)** Calculate the expected change in demand for luxury bags and their new sales value. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the expected change in demand for basic bags and their new sales value. **[3]**

**(c)** Evaluate how Harbour Handbags should adjust its marketing in response to the forecast. **[9]**

**6.** A new law requires warning labels on bags of nuts. Mallow Nuts sells 300,000 bags a year. The labels cost £45,000 in total.

**(a)** Calculate the extra cost per bag. **[1]**

**(b)** Evaluate how Mallow Nuts should respond to the new law. **[6]**

**7.** Greenfield Books is a chain of 12 bookshops. Online retailers, e-books and social media influencers have changed how its customers find and buy books. Evaluate the importance of technological influences on the marketing of Greenfield Books. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An ageing population means more older customers with time and savings [1]. A travel company could develop slower-paced or cruise holidays [1], and adapt promotion, for example using print media and clear booking by telephone, as well as larger print on its website, to suit older customers [1]. **[3]**

**2.** A legal influence is a rule set by law that the business must follow, such as advertising rules or data protection, and breaking it can lead to penalties [1]. An ethical influence concerns what is considered morally right, such as fair treatment of suppliers or honest advertising [1]. A business may follow ethical standards even when not required, because customers expect them [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Making misleading or exaggerated claims [1] that a product or business is environmentally friendly in order to attract customers [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 80 × 1.05 = **€84** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** (84 − 92) ÷ 92 × 100 [1] = **−8.7%** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** −2 × −8.7% [1] = **+17.4%** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** A weaker pound makes the bag cheaper in the eurozone, raising sales by about 17.4% at the same sterling price [1]. Revenue per bag in sterling stays at £80, so total revenue rises by around 17.4%, and if production is in the UK the cost per bag does not change, so profit rises [1].
But extra demand needs more production, which may need more staff and materials [1]; imported materials such as dyes may cost more with a weaker pound [1]; the exchange rate can move back, so the gain may not last [1].
Judgement: Harbour benefits, and could promote more in the eurozone or keep the euro price lower to gain share, but should not rely on one exchange rate and could use hedging to protect against reversal [1]. **[6]**

**5. (a)** Change = 2.2 × −4 = **−8.8%** [1]. New sales = 1.5 × (1 − 0.088) [1] = **£1.368 million** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Change = −0.5 × −4 = **+2.0%** [1]. New sales = 0.8 × 1.02 [1] = **£0.816 million** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Impact: luxury sales fall by £132,000 (1.5m − 1.368m) and basic sales rise by £16,000, so total sales fall by £116,000 [2]. The forecast suggests the luxury range is more exposed.
Responses: introduce a mid-priced range or smaller items at a lower price, to keep customers who trade down [1]; promote durability and value for money rather than status [1]; offer instalment payment plans for luxury bags [1]; increase emphasis on basic bags, whose sales rise [1].
Limits: the forecast depends on the income change and the YED; if the recession is deeper, luxury sales will fall more [1]; heavy discounting of luxury bags could harm its image [1].
Judgement: Harbour should protect the luxury brand image and add a lower-priced range, while monitoring sales monthly [1]. **[9]**

**6. (a)** 45,000 ÷ 300,000 = **£0.15** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Options: absorb the cost, which keeps the price but lowers the contribution by £45,000 [1]; or raise the price, which keeps contribution per bag but may reduce sales [1]. Which is better depends on the price elasticity of demand: if demand is inelastic, raising the price by 15p will lose few sales [1]. Competitors face the same law, so they will also raise prices, which makes passing the cost on easier [1]. Mallow could also reduce the cost by redesigning packaging, or use the label as proof of safety [1]. Judgement: pass on at least part of the cost, if rivals do the same, and check the effect on sales [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Opportunities: an online shop and click and collect widen the market beyond the 12 towns [1]; social media and influencers can promote titles cheaply to targeted readers [1]; data on purchases can personalise offers and loyalty schemes [1].
Threats: online retailers sell at lower prices and with fast delivery, which reduces footfall [1]; e-books cut sales of printed books [1]; competitors with larger technology budgets can win customers more cheaply [1].
Evaluation: the shops' strengths, such as events, expert advice and the experience of browsing, can be marketed in ways that online sellers cannot copy [1]. Technology is a major influence because it affects all four Ps, especially place and promotion [1]. Judgement: technology is probably the most important influence for Greenfield, and it is both a threat and an opportunity, but the cost of investment must be balanced against the benefits [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the influence without linking it to the mix.
- Using the wrong sign for the change in income with an inferior good.
- Giving an exchange rate effect without saying whether it helps exporters or importers.
- Treating every influence as a threat.
