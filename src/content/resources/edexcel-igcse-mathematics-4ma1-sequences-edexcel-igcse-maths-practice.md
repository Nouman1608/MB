## Section A

**1.** The nth term of a sequence is n² − 2. Write down the first four terms. **[2]**

**2.** The first term of a sequence is 3. The term-to-term rule is "multiply by 2, then add 1".

**(a)** Write down the next three terms. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the 6th term. **[1]**

**3.** Here are the first four terms of a sequence: 9, 16, 23, 30, ...

**(a)** Write down the next two terms. **[1]**

**(b)** Find an expression for the nth term. **[2]**

**4.** Find an expression for the nth term of 45, 41, 37, 33, ... **[2]**

**5.** Here is a list of numbers: 36, 64, 100, 128, 144.

**(a)** Write down the numbers that are powers of 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the numbers that are square numbers. **[1]**

**6.** The nth term of a sequence is 4n + 7. Show that 151 is a term of the sequence and state its position. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The nth term of a sequence is 6n + 5.

**(a)** Explain why 2024 is not a term of the sequence. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the first term of the sequence that is greater than 300. **[2]**

**8.** Pattern 1 is a row of 1 square made from 4 matchsticks. Pattern 2 is a row of 2 squares made from 7 matchsticks. Pattern 3 is a row of 3 squares made from 10 matchsticks.

**(a)** Find an expression for the number of matchsticks in pattern n. **[2]**

**(b)** How many matchsticks are in pattern 20? **[1]**

**(c)** Which pattern uses exactly 100 matchsticks? **[2]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** The 2nd term of an arithmetic sequence is 11 and the 6th term is 31.

**(a)** Find the first term, a, and the common difference, d. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the 30th term. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Find the sum of the first 40 terms of the arithmetic series 3 + 7 + 11 + 15 + ... **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** An arithmetic series has first term 5 and common difference 4.

**(a)** Show that the sum of the first n terms is n(2n + 3). **[2]**

**(b)** Find the least number of terms needed for the sum to be greater than 600. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** Find the sum of all the multiples of 7 between 50 and 150. **[3]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** Prove that the sum of the first n even numbers is n(n + 1). **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** n = 1, 2, 3, 4 give **−1, 2, 7, 14** (2 marks for all four; 1 mark for two or three correct) [2].

**2. (a)** **7, 15, 31** [1].

**(b)** 5th term 63, so the 6th term is 2 × 63 + 1 = **127** [1].

**3. (a)** **37, 44** [1].

**(b)** Difference 7, so 7n [1]; 9 − 7 = 2, giving **7n + 2** [1].

**4.** Difference −4, so −4n [1]; 45 + 4 = 49, giving **49 − 4n** [1].

**5. (a)** **64 and 128** [1].

**(b)** **36, 64, 100 and 144** [1].

**6.** 4n + 7 = 151, so 4n = 144 [1]; n = 36, a whole number, so 151 is a term: the **36th term** [1].

**7. (a)** 6n + 5 = 2024 gives 6n = 2019 and n = 336.5 [1]; n is not a whole number, so 2024 is not a term [1]. (Alternatively: every term is 5 more than a multiple of 6, and 2024 − 5 = 2019 is not a multiple of 6.)

**(b)** 6n + 5 > 300 gives n > 49.16... [1]; n = 50, so the term is **305** [1].

**8. (a)** Difference 3 [1]; **3n + 1** [1].

**(b)** 3 × 20 + 1 = **61** [1].

**(c)** 3n + 1 = 100, so 3n = 99 [1]; **pattern 33** [1].

**9. (a)** a + d = 11 and a + 5d = 31 [1]; 4d = 20, so **d = 5** [1]; **a = 6** [1].

**(b)** 6 + 29 × 5 = **151** [1].

**10.** a = 3, d = 4, n = 40: S₄₀ = 20[6 + 39 × 4] [1] = 20 × 162 = **3240** [1].

**11. (a)** Sₙ = (n/2)[10 + 4(n − 1)] [1] = (n/2)(4n + 6) = n(2n + 3) [1].

**(b)** n(2n + 3) > 600 [1]; n = 16 gives 16 × 35 = 560 and n = 17 gives 17 × 37 = 629 [1]; **17 terms** [1]. (Solving 2n² + 3n − 600 = 0 gives n = 16.6..., so the next whole number, 17.)

**12.** First 56, last 147, d = 7; number of terms = (147 − 56)/7 + 1 = 14 [1]; S = (14/2)(56 + 147) [1] = 7 × 203 = **1421** [1].

**13.** The even numbers form an arithmetic series with a = 2 and d = 2 [1]; Sₙ = (n/2)[4 + 2(n − 1)] = (n/2)(2n + 2) [1] = n(n + 1), which holds for every positive integer n [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: working out 1² − 2 as 1 or −3. Square first, then subtract.
- Question 4: writing 4n + 49 or −4n + 41. The coefficient of n is the difference, including its sign, and the constant is the "zero term".
- Question 6: finding n = 36 but not stating that 151 is the 36th term.
- Question 7(b): giving n = 50 as the answer instead of the term 305.
- Question 8(a): using the pattern number as the number of matchsticks, or writing 4n because the first pattern has 4.
- Question 9(a): dividing 31 − 11 by 6 − 2 + 1 = 5 instead of 4. Between the 2nd and 6th terms there are four steps.
- Question 11(b): rounding n = 16.6 down to 16. S₁₆ = 560 is still below 600, so 17 terms are needed.
- Question 12: counting 13 terms by forgetting the +1, or starting at 49 (not a multiple of 7).
- Question 13: checking n = 1, 2 and 3 only, which is not a proof.

## Why the sum formula works

For question 10, write the series forwards and backwards: 3 + 7 + ... + 159 and 159 + 155 + ... + 3. Each column adds to 162, and there are 40 columns, so twice the sum is 40 × 162 and the sum is 20 × 162. The formula Sₙ = (n/2)[2a + (n − 1)d] packages exactly this pairing, because the first term plus the last term is a + a + (n − 1)d.
