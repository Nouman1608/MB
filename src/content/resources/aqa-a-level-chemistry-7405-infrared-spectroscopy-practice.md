These questions cover section **3.3.6.3 Infrared spectroscopy** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Infrared spectroscopy study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-infrared-spectroscopy/) and the [Infrared spectroscopy revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-infrared-spectroscopy-revision-notes/). Use these approximate absorption ranges, in cm⁻¹: O–H in alcohols 3230 to 3550; O–H in carboxylic acids 2500 to 3000; N–H 3300 to 3500; C–H 2850 to 3100; C≡N 2220 to 2260; C=O 1680 to 1750; C=C 1620 to 1680; C–O 1000 to 1300. In an exam you would be given the Data Booklet table.

## Questions

**1.** State what happens to a bond in a molecule when it absorbs infrared radiation. **[1]**

**2.** Convert an infrared wavelength of 5.88 µm into a wavenumber in cm⁻¹. Use wavenumber = 10 000 ÷ wavelength in µm. **[1]**

**3.** The infrared spectrum of compound A has a strong absorption at 1712 cm⁻¹ and a very broad absorption from 2500 to 3000 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the two bonds responsible. **[2]**

**(b)** State the functional group in A. **[1]**

**4.** The infrared spectrum of compound B shows a broad absorption at 3340 cm⁻¹ and an absorption at 1050 cm⁻¹. There is no absorption between 1650 and 1800 cm⁻¹. Identify the functional group in B and give the bonds responsible. **[3]**

**5.** Compound C has formula C₃H₆O₂. Its infrared spectrum has strong absorptions at 1743 cm⁻¹ and 1245 cm⁻¹ and no absorption above 3100 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the functional group in C. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why C cannot be a carboxylic acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest the name of one possible structure for C. **[1]**

**6.** A student oxidises propan-1-ol with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) and distils off the product as it forms. The infrared spectrum of the product shows a strong absorption at 1730 cm⁻¹ but no absorption between 2500 and 3600 cm⁻¹ except C–H absorptions.

**(a)** Name the product. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the spectrum supports your answer. **[2]**

**7.** A sample of propanone has been purified. Its infrared spectrum shows a strong absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ and a broad absorption at 3350 cm⁻¹. Suggest what impurity is present and give a possible reason. **[2]**

**8.** Explain what is meant by the fingerprint region of an infrared spectrum and how it is used. **[2]**

**9.** Carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour are greenhouse gases.

**(a)** State which bond in each molecule absorbs infrared radiation. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the absorption of infrared radiation by these gases leads to global warming. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why nitrogen and oxygen, which make up most of the atmosphere, are not greenhouse gases. **[1]**

**10.** A compound D has a molecular ion peak at m/z 73.0527 in its mass spectrum. Its infrared spectrum shows absorptions at 3350 cm⁻¹ (two peaks) and 1680 cm⁻¹, and no absorption at 2500 to 3000 cm⁻¹. Use precise masses H = 1.0078, C = 12.0000, N = 14.0031, O = 15.9949 to decide whether the molecular formula is C₃H₇NO or C₄H₁₁N, and state two functional groups that could account for the infrared data. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The bond vibrates more strongly (stretches or bends with a larger amplitude) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 10 000 ÷ 5.88 = **1700 cm⁻¹** (1701) [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** C=O [1]; O–H (of a carboxylic acid) [1]

**(b)** Carboxylic acid [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Alcohol [1]; broad absorption at 3340 cm⁻¹ is the O–H bond [1]; absorption at 1050 cm⁻¹ is the C–O bond (and the lack of absorption near 1700 shows no C=O) [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Ester [1]; C=O (1743) and C–O (1245) [1]

**(b)** There is no very broad O–H absorption between 2500 and 3000 cm⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** Methyl ethanoate (or ethyl methanoate) [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** Propanal [1]

**(b)** The C=O absorption at 1730 cm⁻¹ shows a carbonyl compound [1]; the lack of an O–H absorption shows it is not the alcohol or the carboxylic acid, so the product is an aldehyde [1]. **[3]**

**7.** A broad absorption at 3350 cm⁻¹ is an O–H bond, so an alcohol or water is present [1]; propan-2-ol, the starting material for the ketone, may not have been fully removed (or the sample was not dry) [1]. **[2]**

**8.** The region below about 1500 cm⁻¹ contains many absorptions that are unique to a particular molecule [1]; the spectrum of a sample is compared with a database, and a match in this region identifies the compound [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** C=O in carbon dioxide, C–H in methane, O–H in water [1]

**(b)** The Earth emits infrared radiation [1]; the bonds in these gases absorb it and vibrate more [1]; energy is passed to the atmosphere or re-emitted, including back towards the surface, so less energy escapes into space and the atmosphere warms [1]

**(c)** Their molecules are diatomic with identical atoms, so vibration produces no change in dipole and they do not absorb infrared radiation [1]. **[5]**

**10.** C₃H₇NO: 3(12.0000) + 7(1.0078) + 14.0031 + 15.9949 = 36.0000 + 7.0546 + 14.0031 + 15.9949 = **73.0526** [1]; C₄H₁₁N: 48.0000 + 11.0858 + 14.0031 = **73.0889** [1]; the measured value 73.0527 matches C₃H₇NO [1]. The two peaks near 3350 cm⁻¹ are N–H absorptions [1]; the absorption at 1680 cm⁻¹ is C=O, so an amide group could account for the data [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In the exam you are given the precise masses and the table of wavenumbers. Always quote the number, name the bond and then name the group.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not naming the bond for a quoted wavenumber.
- Placing carboxylic acid O–H and alcohol O–H in the same range.
- Concluding "ketone" from C=O alone.
- Forgetting to state what the missing absorption shows (for example, no O–H).
- Saying infrared radiation breaks bonds.
- Naming nitrogen or oxygen as greenhouse gases.
