## Questions

**1.** State whether each of the following is categorical data, discrete numerical data or continuous numerical data. (a) The shoe sizes of 30 pupils. **[1]** (b) The time taken by each pupil to run 100 m. **[1]** (c) The favourite colour of each pupil. **[1]**

**2.** Twenty households were asked how many pets they own. The results were:

```
1 0 2 1 3 1 0 2 2 1 4 1 0 1 2 3 1 2 1 0
```

**(a)** Complete a tally and frequency table for the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the mode. **[1]**
**(c)** Write down the fraction of households that own more than two pets. **[1]**

**3.** The table shows the clubs joined by 60 pupils in Years 10 and 11. Some entries are missing.

| | Chess | Drama | Art | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 10 | 12 | 14 | | 35 |
| Year 11 | | | | 25 |
| Total | 20 | 24 | 16 | 60 |

**(a)** Copy and complete the table. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the percentage of the Drama club who are in Year 11. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**4.** The number of text messages sent by a pupil on eight days was 12, 7, 15, 9, 7, 20, 11, 7.

**(a)** Find the mean. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the median. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the mode and the range. **[2]**

**5.** The mean of six numbers is 8.5. A seventh number is added and the mean of the seven numbers is 9.

**(a)** Find the seventh number. **[3]**
**(b)** The six original numbers are 4, 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13. Find the median of all seven numbers. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows the number of passengers in each of 30 cars.

| Passengers | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cars | 9 | 12 | 5 | 3 | 1 |

**(a)** Write down the mode. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the median. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean. **[3]**

**7.** The weekly savings, £s, of 40 pupils are summarised below.

| Savings s (£) | 0 < s ≤ 5 | 5 < s ≤ 10 | 10 < s ≤ 15 | 15 < s ≤ 20 | 20 < s ≤ 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 13 | 11 | 7 | 3 |

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate an estimate of the mean weekly savings. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why your answer to part (b) is only an estimate. **[1]**

**8.** A survey of 90 households recorded their main type of heating: gas 40, electricity 25, oil 15, wood 10.

**(a)** Calculate the angle of each sector of a pie chart for this data. **[3]**
**(b)** In a pie chart for a different survey, a sector of 135° represents 27 people. Find the total number of people in that survey. **[2]**

**9.** A pictogram shows the number of bicycles hired on four days. The key is: one full symbol represents 6 bicycles. Monday has 4 full symbols. Tuesday has 3 full symbols and a half symbol. Wednesday has 2 full symbols and a half symbol. Thursday has 5 full symbols.

**(a)** How many bicycles were hired on Wednesday? **[1]**
**(b)** Find the total number hired over the four days. **[2]**
**(c)** On Friday, 33 bicycles were hired. How many symbols are needed? **[1]**

**10.** The table shows the hot and cold drinks sold at a kiosk.

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot drinks | 30 | 25 | 20 | 15 |
| Cold drinks | 10 | 15 | 25 | 35 |

A composite bar chart is drawn with hot drinks at the bottom of each bar.

**(a)** Find the height of each of the four bars. **[2]**
**(b)** Between which two heights is the cold section of the March bar drawn? **[1]**
**(c)** Describe two changes shown by the data from January to April. **[2]**

**11.** Eight plants were grown with fertiliser A and eight with fertiliser B. Their heights in cm after four weeks were:

```
A: 12 15 16 16 18 19 20 24
B: 15 16 17 17 18 18 19 20
```

**(a)** Find the mean height for each fertiliser. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the median height for each fertiliser. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the range for each fertiliser. **[1]**
**(d)** Compare the two sets of plants, using your answers. **[2]**
**(e)** Give one reason why you should be careful about drawing a firm conclusion from this data. **[1]**

**12.** A survey asked 48 pupils how many hours t they spent online last week.

| Hours t | 0 < t ≤ 4 | 4 < t ≤ 8 | 8 < t ≤ 12 | 12 < t ≤ 16 | 16 < t ≤ 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | x | 15 | 9 | 4 |

In a pie chart of all 48 pupils, the sector for 4 < t ≤ 8 has an angle of 90°.

**(a)** Find the value of x. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate an estimate of the mean number of hours. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the angle of the sector for 12 < t ≤ 16. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Shoe sizes can only take separate values, so **discrete numerical** [1]. (b) Time can take any value in a range, so **continuous numerical** [1]. (c) A colour is described in words, so **categorical** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A short reason, such as "separate values only", shows you know the difference between discrete and continuous.

**2. (a)** Tally strokes grouped in fives, then counted [1]. Frequencies: 0 → 4, 1 → 8, 2 → 5, 3 → 2, 4 → 1, total 20 [1].
**(b)** Mode = **1** [1].
**(c)** Households with 3 or 4 pets: 2 + 1 = 3, so the fraction is **3/20** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that the frequencies add up to 20. Writing the mode as 8 (its frequency) gives the count, not the value.

**3. (a)** Year 10 Art = 35 − 12 − 14 = **9** [1]. Year 11 Chess = 20 − 12 = **8** and Year 11 Drama = 24 − 14 = **10** [1]. Year 11 Art = 25 − 8 − 10 = **7**, which agrees with 16 − 9 = 7 [1].
**(b)** Fraction = 10/24 [1]. Percentage = 10 ÷ 24 × 100 = **41.7%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the Drama column total (24) as the denominator, not 60. Checking a missing entry two ways guards against slips.

**4. (a)** Sum = 12 + 7 + 15 + 9 + 7 + 20 + 11 + 7 = 88 [1]. Mean = 88 ÷ 8 = **11** [1].
**(b)** Ordered: 7, 7, 7, 9, 11, 12, 15, 20 [1]. Median = (9 + 11) ÷ 2 = **10** [1].
**(c)** Mode = **7** [1]. Range = 20 − 7 = **13** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the addition. Taking the median from the unordered list is a common slip.

**5. (a)** Total of six numbers = 6 × 8.5 = 51 [1]. Total of seven numbers = 7 × 9 = 63 [1]. Seventh number = 63 − 51 = **12** [1].
**(b)** Ordered: 4, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13 [1]. Median = the fourth value = **9** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Work with totals, not means. Averaging the two means does not use the totals.

**6. (a)** The highest frequency is 12, for 2 passengers, so the mode is **2** [1].
**(b)** There are 30 cars, so the median lies between the 15th and 16th values [1]. Cumulative frequencies are 9, then 21, so both values are 2 and the median is **2** [1].
**(c)** Σf = 30 [1]. Σfx = 9 + 24 + 15 + 12 + 5 = 65 [1]. Mean = 65 ÷ 30 = **2.17** (3 s.f.) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write each product f × x down so your method is visible. Dividing by 5 columns instead of 30 cars is a frequent slip.

**7. (a)** The highest frequency is 13, so the modal class is **5 < s ≤ 10** [1].
**(b)** Midpoints: 2.5, 7.5, 12.5, 17.5, 22.5 [1]. Σ(f × midpoint) = 15 + 97.5 + 137.5 + 122.5 + 67.5 = 440 [1]. Estimated mean = 440 ÷ 40 = **£11** [1].
**(c)** The exact savings are not known, so each value is assumed to be at the midpoint of its class [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use midpoints, not class limits. Saying that exact values are unknown is stronger than just writing "approximate".

**8. (a)** One household is 360° ÷ 90 = 4° [1]. Gas = 40 × 4 = **160°** and electricity = 25 × 4 = **100°** [1]. Oil = 15 × 4 = **60°** and wood = 10 × 4 = **40°**; the total is 360° [1].
**(b)** 135° ÷ 27 = 5° per person [1]. Total = 360° ÷ 5° = **72 people** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Add your angles to check they make 360°, and label each sector.

**9. (a)** 2.5 × 6 = **15 bicycles** [1].
**(b)** Monday 24, Tuesday 21, Wednesday 15, Thursday 30 [1]. Total = **90 bicycles** [1].
**(c)** 33 ÷ 6 = **5.5 symbols** (five full and one half) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the key value in your working. Giving the number of symbols instead of bicycles is a common misreading.

**10. (a)** Totals: January 30 + 10 = **40**, February 25 + 15 = **40** [1]. March 20 + 25 = **45**, April 15 + 35 = **50** [1].
**(b)** The cold section runs from **20 to 45** [1].
**(c)** Hot drinks fell from 30 to 15 [1]. Cold drinks rose from 10 to 35, and the total rose from 40 to 50 [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table. "Hot drinks went down" with no numbers is a weaker answer.

**11. (a)** Each set has a total of 140, so each mean is 140 ÷ 8 = **17.5 cm** [1] [1].
**(b)** A: (16 + 18) ÷ 2 = **17 cm** [1]. B: the middle two values are 17 and 18, so the median is (17 + 18) ÷ 2 = **17.5 cm** [1].
**(c)** Range A = 24 − 12 = 12 and range B = 20 − 15 = 5 [1].
**(d)** The means are equal (17.5 cm), so the two fertilisers give the same average height [1]. The range for B is much smaller, so B gives more consistent heights, while A gives more variation [1].
**(e)** Only eight plants were used for each fertiliser, so the sample is small [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A good comparison has one statement on an average and one on spread, with figures and context. "B is better" alone gives no evidence.

**12. (a)** 90° ÷ 360° = 1/4 of the pupils, so x = 48 ÷ 4 = **12** [1]. Check: 8 + 12 + 15 + 9 + 4 = 48 [1].
**(b)** The highest frequency is 15, so the modal class is **8 < t ≤ 12** [1].
**(c)** Midpoints: 2, 6, 10, 14, 18 [1]. Σ(f × midpoint) = 16 + 72 + 150 + 126 + 72 = 436 [1]. Estimated mean = 436 ÷ 48 = **9.08 hours** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(d)** One pupil is 360° ÷ 48 = 7.5° [1]. Angle = 9 × 7.5° = **67.5°** [1].
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) feeds later parts, so check x against the total of 48 before moving on.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Taking the median from a list that has not been put in order.
* Giving the frequency of the mode instead of the mode.
* Dividing the sum of f × x by the number of rows instead of by the total frequency.
* Using class limits rather than midpoints when estimating a mean.
* Writing a grouped mean as an exact value, with no word such as "estimate".
* Pie chart angles that do not add to 360°.
* Comparing two data sets with an average only and giving no comment on spread.
