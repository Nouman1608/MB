All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Halo Speakers.

> **Case study: Halo Speakers**
>
> Halo Speakers is about to launch a new wireless speaker. Each speaker
> costs $60 to make. Halo is deciding between skimming, selling 1,500 a
> year at $140, and penetration pricing, selling 5,000 a year at $95.
> A cost-plus price with a 50% mark-up has also been suggested. An
> online campaign would cost $12,000, reach 200,000 people and is
> expected to persuade 1.2% of them to buy. Halo can sell direct on its
> website at $90 (with $7 postage per speaker, paid by Halo), or through
> retailers who buy at $72 each. Direct selling is expected to sell
> 3,000 speakers and retailers 9,000. Sales of Halo's older speaker in
> its life cycle were 5,000 in introduction, 24,000 in growth and 40,000
> in maturity.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following describes penetration pricing? **[1]**
- A. A high launch price that is lowered later
- B. A low launch price to win market share
- C. Adding a percentage to the cost
- D. Selling below cost to attract customers

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a stage of the product life cycle? **[1]**
- A. Segmentation
- B. Maturity
- C. Delegation
- D. Induction

**3.** Define the term "unique selling point". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** methods of promotion. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business may use a loss-leader. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the selling price of the speaker using cost-plus pricing with a 50% mark-up. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the yearly profit from skimming and from penetration pricing. **[4]**

**8.** Calculate the number of customers won by the online campaign, and the cost per customer. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the profit from direct selling and from selling through retailers. **[4]**

**10.** Calculate the percentage increase in sales of the older speaker from introduction to growth. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** extension strategies Halo could use when the new speaker reaches maturity. **[4]**

**12.** Analyse **two** factors that Halo should consider when choosing its promotional mix. **[4]**

**13.** Evaluate whether Halo should use skimming or penetration pricing. Use your answers to Questions 6 and 7. **[9]**

**14.** Evaluate whether Halo should sell the speaker direct or through retailers. Use your answers to Question 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A feature of a product [1] that competitors do not offer [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: advertising [1]; sales promotion [1]; personal selling [1]; public relations or sponsorship [1]; direct marketing [1]; social media [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A loss-leader is sold below cost to attract customers into a shop [1], who then buy other goods at normal prices, so total profit rises [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Mark-up = 50% x $60 = $30 [1]; price = $60 + $30 = **$90** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Skimming: profit per speaker = $140 - $60 = $80 [1]; 1,500 x $80 = **$120,000** [1]. Penetration: profit per speaker = $95 - $60 = $35 [1]; 5,000 x $35 = **$175,000** [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Customers = 1.2% x 200,000 = **2,400** [1]; cost per customer = $12,000 / 2,400 [1] = **$5** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Direct: profit per speaker = $90 - $60 - $7 = $23 [1]; 3,000 x $23 = **$69,000** [1]. Retailers: profit per speaker = $72 - $60 = $12 [1]; 9,000 x $12 = **$108,000** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** (24,000 - 5,000) / 5,000 [1] x 100 = **380%** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **New features or colours:** a new version of the speaker would appeal to existing customers again [1], so sales may rise when they have started to level off [1].
**New market:** selling in another country opens up a new group of customers [1], which prolongs the life of the product [1]. Accept price promotions or new packaging, with a reason [1] [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **Target market:** if the target market is young people, social media and online advertising reach them cheaply [1], whereas TV would waste money on people who will not buy [1].
**Budget:** Halo has $12,000 for an online campaign [1], so it cannot afford national TV advertising and should use methods with a low cost per customer, such as $5 [1]. Accept competitors' activities or life cycle stage, with a reason [1] [1]. **[4]**

**13.** **Skimming:** profit per speaker is $80, compared with $35, and the high price creates a premium image [1]; but only 1,500 are expected to sell, which gives $120,000 profit [1]; it may also leave market share to rivals [1].
**Penetration:** it sells 5,000, giving profit of $175,000, $55,000 more than skimming [1]; it quickly builds market share and a customer base [1]; but the low price may make the brand look cheap and is hard to raise later [1].
**Cost-plus:** the cost-plus price of $90 lies between the two, but ignores what customers will pay and what rivals charge [1].
**Judgement:** penetration looks better on profit, provided that Halo can make 5,000 speakers [1], but if the speaker has a unique feature and few rivals, skimming could be better in the long term, and Halo could lower the price later [1]. **[9]**

**14.** **Direct:** profit per speaker is $23, which is $11 more than selling through retailers [1]; Halo keeps control of its brand and has customer data [1]; but Halo must handle delivery and returns, and only 3,000 are expected to sell [1].
**Retailers:** retailers are expected to sell 9,000, giving $108,000, which is $39,000 more than direct selling [1]; they reach customers in shops, where people can try the product [1]; but each speaker earns less profit, because the retailer takes a margin [1], and Halo has less control over how the speaker is displayed and sold [1].
**Judgement:** retailers give the higher total profit ($108,000 against $69,000) and reach more customers, so they are probably the better main channel [1]. Halo could still sell direct through its website, to keep control of its brand and to learn about its customers [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing skimming and penetration.
- Forgetting to subtract the cost of making a speaker, or the postage on direct sales, when finding profit.
- Calculating 1.2% of the wrong figure for the campaign.
- Giving a pricing method without saying why it suits the product.
- Recommending one channel without comparing total profit and profit per item.
