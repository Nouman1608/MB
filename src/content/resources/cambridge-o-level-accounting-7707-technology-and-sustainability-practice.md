These questions cover section 7.3 Technology and sustainability of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029. Use them with the [Technology and sustainability study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-technology-and-sustainability/) and the [Technology and sustainability revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-technology-and-sustainability-revision-notes/). Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Which of the following is an example of a data storage device?

A: an online banking statement

B: an external hard drive

C: a bound cash book

D: a provider's remote server reached over the internet **[1]**

**2.** State the four types of storage system named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by a business using digital applications for **part** of its accounting records. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** qualities that accounting data should have if it is stored safely. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** ways in which storing accounting records can harm the environment. **[2]**

**6.** Hollin Garage keeps its only copy of its accounting records on a memory card in the office. State **two** risks of this arrangement. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why a back-up kept in the same room as the computer does not protect the records against a fire. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Juniper Florists keeps its sales ledger on a spreadsheet. On 1 May the selling price of a bouquet was entered as 3.50 instead of 35.00. During May, 64 bouquets were sold on credit at this price before the error was found.

**(a)** Calculate the amount by which revenue for May was understated. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the trial balance would not reveal this error. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of keeping the sales ledger digitally rather than by hand, using this case. **[4]**

**9.** Oriel Electrical backs up its records every Saturday evening. It trades Monday to Saturday and records about 28 credit sales each trading day. Its computer fails at the close of business on Thursday.

**(a)** Calculate the number of credit sales records that may need to be re-created. **[2]**

**(b)** The owner changes to a back-up at the close of business every day. Calculate the maximum number of credit sales records that could then be lost in a similar failure. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** other way the owner could reduce the risk of losing the records. **[1]**

**10.** Brackenfield Tutors sends 300 parents a paper invoice each month. Each invoice is printed in triplicate: one for the parent, one for the file and one for the owner's personal folder.

**(a)** Calculate the number of sheets of paper used for invoices in a year. **[1]**

**(b)** The owner decides to email the parent's copy, keep printing the file copy and stop the personal folder copy. Calculate the number of sheets saved in a year. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** risk the business takes on by moving its invoices to digital storage, and how it could reduce that risk. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** risks to a business's stakeholders of not storing accounting data safely. **[4]**

**12.** Saffron Kitchens is a restaurant with records on paper in an office above the kitchen. The owner is considering two options: (1) keeping the records on an external hard drive in the office, or (2) using a cloud service.

**(a)** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of **each** option for Saffron Kitchens. **[8]**

**(b)** Advise the owner which option to choose. Justify your answer. **[3]**

**13.** Evaluate the use of "other digital services", such as online banking statements and emailed invoices, as a store of a small business's accounting records. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A is an other digital service, C is manual storage and D describes a cloud service.

**2.** Manual; data storage devices; cloud services; other digital services. Two correct [1]; all four correct [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Some of the books or records are kept using a computer program [1], while others are still kept by hand, for example a digital sales ledger with a handwritten cash book [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: complete [1]; accurate [1]; available when needed; confidential or protected from unauthorised access. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: paper and ink used for printing [1]; electricity used by computers, devices or servers [1]; electronic waste from discarded devices; space for filing. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: the card could be lost or stolen because it is small [1]; it could fail or be corrupted so the data cannot be read [1]; it could be destroyed with the office in a fire or flood; there is no second copy. **[2]**

**7.** A fire would destroy both the computer and the back-up at the same time [1], so no copy would survive; a back-up protects against fire only if it is held off-site, for example in a cloud service [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Understatement per bouquet 35.00 - 3.50 = 31.50 [1]; 64 x 31.50 = **2,016** [1].

**(b)** The wrong figure was used for both the debit to the customers' accounts and the credit to sales [1], so total debits still equal total credits; it is an error of original entry [1].

**(c)** Advantage: one input posts the invoice to the customer's account and to sales and calculates the balances [1], so there are no arithmetic or casting errors in the ledger [1]. Disadvantage: one wrong input is repeated on every invoice that uses it [1], as here, where the price error affected all 64 sales before anyone noticed [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c), tie each point to the bouquets; a general list of advantages earns less.

**9. (a)** Monday to Thursday is 4 trading days since the back-up [1]; 4 x 28 = **112** records [1].

**(b)** At most one day's records: **28** [1].

**(c)** For example: keep a second copy off-site or in a cloud service; keep paper or emailed copies of invoices so records can be rebuilt [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 300 x 3 x 12 = **10,800** sheets [1].

**(b)** Sheets still printed: 300 x 1 x 12 = 3,600 [1]; sheets saved 10,800 - 3,600 = **7,200** [1].

**(c)** Risk: the emailed copies could be lost, or parents' details exposed, through device failure, deletion or hacking [1]. Reduced by regular back-ups held off-site, or by passwords and limited access [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Any two developed risks. For each, one mark for identifying the risk and one for explaining its effect on a named stakeholder:

Customers' private details could be seen by unauthorised people [1], so customers could suffer fraud and lose trust in the business [1].

Records could be lost so the owner cannot prepare accurate statements [1], and lenders could then make decisions on unreliable figures or refuse a loan [1].

*(Also acceptable: the tax authority cannot check income, leading to penalties for the owner; suppliers' queries about payments cannot be answered.)* **[4]**

**12. (a)** External hard drive, up to 4 marks:
Advantage: fast to use and does not need an internet connection, so the restaurant can record takings during busy service [1]; the owner controls the data and pays no monthly charge [1].
Disadvantage: the drive stays in the office above the kitchen, so a kitchen fire could destroy it with the paper records [1]; it could fail or be stolen, and back-ups depend on someone remembering to make them [1].

Cloud service, up to 4 marks:
Advantage: the data is held away from the restaurant, so a fire in the kitchen would not destroy it [1]; the owner can reach the records from home [1].
Disadvantage: there is a continuing charge, and it needs a reliable internet connection [1]; the business depends on the provider, and access must be controlled with passwords [1].

**(b)** A decision [1], supported by a reason linked to the restaurant [1] and a recognition of the drawback or a safeguard [1]. For example: choose the cloud service, because the main danger is fire in the kitchen below the office and only an off-site copy protects against it; keep an external drive as a back-up in case the internet connection fails. A reasoned choice of the hard drive can also earn full marks if it deals with the fire risk, for example by storing it away from the premises. **[11]**

*Examiner insight:* Both options must be discussed in (a); four points on one option and none on the other cannot earn full marks.

**13.** Up to 4 marks for developed points, up to 1 mark for a conclusion:
Records are created automatically, so less retyping and fewer input errors [1].
A bank statement is independent evidence prepared by a third party, useful for checking the cash book [1].
Less paper is used, which is more sustainable [1].
However, records are spread across many services, so it is slow to bring them together to prepare statements [1].
Conclusion: useful as a supporting source of evidence, but not suitable as the only store, so the business should keep its own copy [1].
*(Also acceptable as a point: each service may keep records only for a limited time, and the business does not control the format or availability.)* **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a brand of software instead of describing what a digital application does.
- Saying a back-up protects the records without asking where the back-up is kept.
- Giving advantages without disadvantages in a "discuss" or "evaluate" answer.
- Making generic points that never mention the business in the question.
- Ending an "advise" answer without a clear recommendation.
