## Section A: short answers

**1.** Give **three** examples of personal data. **[3]**

**2.** State what is meant by *sensitive data* and give **one** example. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** ways to minimise the danger of using the internet, as named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** eSafety rules for online gaming. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is a principle of a typical data protection act?

A: Users must change their passwords every month

B: Data must be accurate and kept up to date

C: Users must not open emails from unknown senders

D: Data must be backed up every day **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Explain why data protection legislation is needed. **[3]**

**7.** Westfield Medical Centre keeps records of patients' names, addresses, dates of birth and medical histories.

**(a)** Identify which of these items is sensitive data. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the medical centre must keep these records confidential. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how **two** principles of a typical data protection act apply to the medical centre. **[4]**

**8.** A school wants to put photographs of pupils in school uniform on its public website.

**(a)** Explain why this is a risk to the pupils. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** ways the school could reduce the risk. **[2]**

**9.** Tom receives an email from an address he does not recognise. It says he has won a prize and asks him to reply with his home address and date of birth.

Explain **two** reasons why Tom should not reply. **[4]**

**10.** Describe **four** ways a teenager can stay safe when using social media. **[4]**

**11.** Priya plays an online game in which players can chat. Another player offers her free game credits if she sends him her parents' card details.

**(a)** Explain why Priya should refuse. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe what else Priya should do. **[2]**

**12.** An online clothes shop collects customers' names, addresses, email addresses and religion when they register.

**(a)** Identify the data protection principle the shop may be breaking, and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** The shop sells its list of email addresses to a holiday company. Identify the principle broken. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three of: name [1]; address [1]; date of birth [1]; photograph (for example in school uniform) [1]; medical history [1]; phone number or email address [1]. Maximum 3. **[3]**

**2.** Personal data that could cause greater harm or discrimination to the person if it were disclosed [1]. Example, any one of: medical history; ethnic origin; religious beliefs; political views; criminal record [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Only use trusted websites recommended by teachers [1]; use a search engine that only allows access to age-appropriate websites [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Do not use your real name [1]; do not give out personal or financial data [1]. **[2]**

**5.** B [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any three of: organisations hold large amounts of personal data that could be misused [1]; without rules data could be collected unfairly or used for other purposes, such as being sold [1]; inaccurate data could lead to wrong decisions about people [1]; data could be lost or disclosed to the wrong people, causing identity theft or fraud [1]; the law gives people rights over their data and puts duties on organisations, with penalties [1]. Maximum 3. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Medical history [1].

**(b)** Disclosure could cause harm to patients, such as discrimination by an employer or distress [1]; names, addresses and dates of birth could be used for identity theft or fraud [1].

**(c)** Any two principles applied, 2 marks each: data must be kept secure [1], so records are password-protected, encrypted and only seen by authorised staff [1]; data must be accurate and up to date [1], so addresses and medical details are checked and corrected [1]; data must be used only for the purpose collected [1], so records are used for treatment and not passed to other companies [1]; data must not be kept longer than necessary [1], so records of former patients are deleted after a set period [1]. Maximum 4. **[7]**

**8. (a)** The uniform shows which school the pupils attend [1], so a stranger could identify pupils and know where to find them during the day [1].

**(b)** Any two of: get written permission from parents first [1]; do not include pupils' names with the photos [1]; use photos where pupils cannot be clearly identified, such as from behind or in groups [1]; put photos on a password-protected area for parents only [1]. Maximum 2. **[4]**

**9.** Any two reasons, each explained:
- The sender is unknown, so the email may be a scam / phishing attempt [1]; replying with his address and date of birth could allow identity theft or fraud [1].
- Replying confirms that his email address is active [1], so he may receive more spam and scam emails [1].
- He would be sending personal identifiable data by email [1], which can be forwarded or misused once sent [1].
Maximum 4. **[4]**

**10.** Any four described: know how to block and report unwanted users [1]; do not meet online contacts face to face, as they may not be who they claim [1]; do not share inappropriate images [1]; avoid inappropriate language [1]; respect other people's personal data by not posting it without permission [1]; set the profile to private / do not post personal details such as address or school [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Card details are financial data and must not be given out in online games [1]; the player is a stranger and is probably trying to steal money from her parents' account [1].

**(b)** Block the player so he cannot contact her again [1] and report him to the game's moderators, and tell a parent or trusted adult [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Data must be adequate, relevant and not excessive [1]; a clothes shop does not need to know customers' religion to sell clothes [1].

**(b)** Data must be used only for the purpose it was collected for [1]. **[3]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to name my country's data protection law?**
No. The syllabus asks for the principles of a typical act, so state the principles.

**Can I give general online safety tips in any eSafety answer?**
Only if they fit the context. A question about online gaming expects the gaming rules: not using your real name and not giving out personal or financial data.
