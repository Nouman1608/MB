## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by the standard electrode potential of a half-cell. Include the standard conditions. **[3]**

**2.** The standard hydrogen electrode is the reference for all E⦵ values.

**(a)** Describe how a standard hydrogen electrode is set up. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a reference electrode is needed. **[1]**

**3.** A student joins a cobalt half-cell to a silver half-cell. E⦵(Co²⁺/Co) = −0.28 V and E⦵(Ag⁺/Ag) = +0.80 V.

**(a)** Calculate E⦵cell. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the cell diagram. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the equation for the reaction when the cell supplies current. **[1]**
**(d)** The student made the salt bridge with potassium chloride solution. Explain why this is unsuitable and suggest a better salt. **[2]**
**(e)** State why a high-resistance voltmeter is used. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how you would set up a half-cell to measure E⦵ for MnO₄⁻(aq) + 8H⁺(aq) + 5e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺(aq) + 4H₂O(l), and how you would connect it to obtain the value. **[3]**

**5.** Use these values: Sn⁴⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Sn²⁺, +0.15 V; I₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2I⁻, +0.54 V; Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺, +0.77 V.

**(a)** Show by calculation that iodine can oxidise Sn²⁺ ions, and write the equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict whether Sn⁴⁺ ions will oxidise iodide ions. Give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the strongest reducing agent among Sn²⁺, I⁻ and Fe²⁺. **[1]**

**6.** In the cell from question 3, the cobalt(II) ion concentration is raised to 1.50 mol dm⁻³. Predict and explain the effect on the cobalt electrode potential and on the cell emf. **[3]**

**7.** Use these values: MnO₂ + 4H⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺ + 2H₂O, +1.23 V; Cl₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Cl⁻, +1.36 V.

**(a)** Calculate E⦵cell for manganese(IV) oxide oxidising chloride ions, and state what it predicts. **[2]**
**(b)** Chlorine is in fact made when manganese(IV) oxide is warmed with concentrated hydrochloric acid. Explain why the prediction fails. **[2]**

**8.** Use these values: Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺, +0.77 V; Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Fe, −0.44 V.

**(a)** Write an equation for the disproportionation of Fe²⁺ ions and use E⦵ values to show it is not feasible. **[2]**
**(b)** A technician keeps a clean iron nail in a stock solution of iron(II) sulfate. Explain, using E⦵cell, how this keeps Fe³⁺ out of the solution. **[2]**

**9.** Reaction X has E⦵cell = +0.62 V and reaction Y has E⦵cell = +0.31 V. Both transfer the same number of electrons.

**(a)** State which reaction has the larger total entropy change. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce how the equilibrium constant of X compares with that of Y. **[2]**

**10.** A supplier states that its iron wire is 99.5% iron by mass. 1.250 g of the wire was dissolved in excess dilute sulfuric acid, forming Fe²⁺ ions, and the solution was diluted to 250.0 cm³. Portions of 25.0 cm³ were titrated with 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). The mean titre was 29.20 cm³.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the titration reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** State the end-point observation. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in the wire. **[4]**
**(d)** Each burette reading has an uncertainty of ±0.05 cm³ and the pipette ±0.06 cm³. Calculate the total percentage uncertainty from these two items. **[2]**
**(e)** Use your answers to comment on the supplier's claim. **[1]**

**11.** A student prepared crystals of potassium iodate(VII), KIO₄ (Mr = 230.0). 0.250 g of the crystals was dissolved to make 100.0 cm³ of solution. Portions of 10.0 cm³ were acidified and added to excess potassium iodide:

IO₄⁻ + 7I⁻ + 8H⁺ → 4I₂ + 4H₂O

The iodine needed a mean titre of 16.85 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate. Name a suitable indicator and its colour change, and calculate the percentage purity of the crystals. **[7]**

**12.** Fuel cells can run on hydrogen or on ethanol.

**(a)** Explain the principle on which a fuel cell works. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the equations for the reactions at both electrodes of a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell with an acidic electrolyte. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the equation for the reaction at the negative electrode when the electrolyte is alkaline. **[1]**
**(d)** In an ethanol fuel cell with an acidic electrolyte, ethanol is oxidised to carbon dioxide at the negative electrode. Write the half-equation. **[2]**
**(e)** Write the overall equation for the ethanol fuel cell. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The voltage (emf) of a half-cell measured against a standard hydrogen electrode [1], at 298 K and 100 kPa pressure of any gases [1], with all ions at 1.00 mol dm⁻³ [1].
*Examiner insight:* "room temperature" in place of 298 K is unlikely to be accepted for that condition.

**2. (a)** A platinum electrode coated with platinum black [1], with H₂ gas at 100 kPa passed over it [1], dipping into H⁺(aq) of concentration 1.00 mol dm⁻³, all at 298 K [1].
**(b)** The potential of a single electrode cannot be measured; only a difference between two electrodes can [1].
*Examiner insight:* name platinum; "an inert electrode" alone is too vague for a description.

**3. (a)** E⦵cell = +0.80 − (−0.28) = **+1.08 V** [1]
**(b)** **Co(s) | Co²⁺(aq) || Ag⁺(aq) | Ag(s)** [1]
**(c)** **Co + 2Ag⁺ → Co²⁺ + 2Ag** [1]
**(d)** Chloride ions react with silver ions to form a silver chloride precipitate, lowering [Ag⁺] [1]; use potassium nitrate [1].
**(e)** It draws almost no current, so it measures the maximum emf [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c), balance the electrons; the E⦵ value stays +0.80 V even though the silver half-equation is doubled.

**4.** Use a platinum electrode [1] in a solution containing MnO₄⁻, Mn²⁺ and H⁺ ions, each at 1.00 mol dm⁻³ [1]. Connect it to a standard hydrogen electrode with a salt bridge and a high-resistance voltmeter, at 298 K [1].
*Examiner insight:* H⁺ is in the half-equation, so it must also be at 1.00 mol dm⁻³.

**5. (a)** E⦵cell = +0.54 − 0.15 = **+0.39 V**, positive so feasible [1]; **I₂ + Sn²⁺ → 2I⁻ + Sn⁴⁺** [1].
**(b)** No: for Sn⁴⁺ with I⁻, E⦵cell is 0.15 − 0.54 = −0.39 V, a negative value [1].
**(c)** **Sn²⁺**, the right-hand species of the least positive half-equation [1].
*Examiner insight:* on a "show" question, write the subtraction with signs; a bare "+0.39 V" shows nothing.

**6.** The equilibrium Co²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Co shifts to the right [1], so the cobalt electrode potential becomes less negative [1]. The gap between the two electrode potentials narrows, so the cell emf falls below 1.08 V [1].
*Examiner insight:* link the change to the equilibrium shift; "the emf goes down" alone explains nothing.

**7. (a)** E⦵cell = +1.23 − 1.36 = **−0.13 V** [1], so the reaction is predicted not to be feasible under standard conditions [1].
**(b)** Concentrated acid gives [H⁺] and [Cl⁻] far above 1.00 mol dm⁻³, making E for MnO₂ more positive and E for Cl₂/Cl⁻ less positive [1]. E⦵cell is only slightly negative, so these non-standard conditions make E cell positive [1].
*Examiner insight:* "warming speeds it up" misses the point; rate does not change feasibility.

**8. (a)** **3Fe²⁺ → Fe + 2Fe³⁺** [1]; E⦵cell = −0.44 − 0.77 = **−1.21 V**, negative, so not feasible [1].
**(b)** Iron reduces any Fe³⁺ formed: Fe + 2Fe³⁺ → 3Fe²⁺ [1]; E⦵cell = +0.77 − (−0.44) = +1.21 V, so this is feasible [1].
*Examiner insight:* for disproportionation, use the two half-equations that both contain the species in question.

**9. (a)** **X**, because ΔStotal is proportional to E⦵cell [1].
**(b)** ln K is proportional to E⦵cell, so ln K for X is 0.62/0.31 = 2 times ln K for Y [1]; **K for X equals (K for Y)²** [1].
*Examiner insight:* saying "K for X is twice K for Y" confuses K with ln K and is wrong.

**10. (a)** **5Fe²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O** [1]
**(b)** The first permanent pale pink colour [1].
**(c)** Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0150 × 29.20/1000 = 4.38 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]
Moles Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 4.38 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.19 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
Moles in 250.0 cm³ = 2.19 × 10⁻² mol [1]
Mass of iron = 2.19 × 10⁻² × 55.8 = 1.222 g; percentage = 1.222/1.250 × 100 = **97.8%** [1]
**(d)** Titre: ±0.10/29.20 × 100 = 0.34% [1]; total = 0.34 + 0.24 = **0.58%** [1]
**(e)** 0.58% of 97.8% is about 0.6 percentage points, much smaller than the 1.7 point gap from 99.5%, so measurement uncertainty cannot explain it: the claim is too high or a systematic error (such as air oxidising Fe²⁺) lowered the titre [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c), show the 1 : 5 ratio and the ×10 scale-up; a bare final answer leaves no route to partial credit.

**11.** Starch, added when the solution is pale yellow [1]; blue-black to colourless [1].
Moles S₂O₃²⁻ = 0.0500 × 16.85/1000 = 8.425 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]
1 IO₄⁻ gives 4I₂, which needs 8S₂O₃²⁻, so IO₄⁻ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 8 [1]
Moles IO₄⁻ in 10.0 cm³ = 1.053 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]
Moles in 100.0 cm³ = 1.053 × 10⁻³ mol; mass = 1.053 × 10⁻³ × 230.0 = 0.242 g [1]
Purity = 0.242/0.250 × 100 = **96.9%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in an unstructured calculation, write the overall ratio as a separate line; stopping at 1 : 4 forgets the thiosulfate step.

**12. (a)** It uses the energy released when a fuel reacts with oxygen to generate a voltage [1].
**(b)** Negative: **H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]; positive: **O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻** [1]
**(d)** C₂H₅OH + H₂O → CO₂ + H⁺ + e⁻ with correct species [1]; balanced: **C₂H₅OH + 3H₂O → 2CO₂ + 12H⁺ + 12e⁻** [1]
**(e)** **C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* check every half-equation for charge as well as atoms; an equation with 12H⁺ but 6e⁻ is not balanced.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting E⦵ values in the wrong order and reporting a negative emf for a working cell.
- Scaling E⦵ up along with its half-equation.
- Leaving out platinum where no solid is in the half-equation.
- Calling the salt bridge a path for electrons.
- Missing the 1 : 5 or 1 : 2 ratios, or the scale-up to the volumetric flask.
- Using a single burette reading for the titre uncertainty.
- Putting H⁺ in alkaline fuel cell equations, or OH⁻ in acid ones.
