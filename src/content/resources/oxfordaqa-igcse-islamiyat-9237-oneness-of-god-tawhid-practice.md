## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 5: four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |

### Section A

**1.** Shirk [1].

**2.** Surah al-Ikhlas, Surah 112 [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah (oneness of lordship); Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah (oneness of worship); Tawhid al-Asma wa as-Sifat (oneness of Allah's names and qualities).

**4.** Any two, [1] each: riya' (showing off in worship); relying on a lucky charm or omen as if it had power of its own; swearing by something other than Allah as though it were equal to Him; giving a helper the credit that belongs to Allah.

**5.** Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah is the belief that Allah alone is the Lord [1]: the Creator of everything [1], who owns the universe and sustains all creatures [1] and controls life, death and provision, so that no other being shares these powers [1]. Credit a source, such as "Is there a creator other than Allah who provides for you?" (35:3), as development.

**6.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and developed [1].

- **Meaning** [1]: major shirk gives worship or Allah's powers to another being, while minor shirk spoils sincerity without placing another being beside Allah [1].
- **Example** [1]: bowing to an idol is major shirk; praying at length so others will admire you is minor shirk [1].
- **Effect on faith** [1]: major shirk, if believed and not repented from, takes a person outside Islam; minor shirk is a serious sin but does not [1].
- **Effect on deeds** [1]: minor shirk reduces or cancels the reward of the particular deed, while major shirk makes all deeds worthless unless the person repents [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Prayer and worship go to Allah only** [1]: a Muslim does not pray to saints or objects but asks Allah directly, five times a day [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: a Muslim works hard but accepts that the result is in Allah's hands, so is calm when things go wrong [1].
- **Equality and respect** [1]: all people are servants of the same Lord, so Muslims should not look down on anyone [1].
- **Sincerity** [1]: a Muslim tries to give and pray quietly, to avoid riya' [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help" (Qur'an 1:5), recited in every rak'ah, keeps worship directed to Allah [2].
- "Say, indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds" (6:162) shows that every part of life is dedicated to Allah [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It denies Allah's rights** [1]: worship belongs to Allah alone, and shirk gives it to something else [1].
- **It is injustice** [1]: Luqman's advice calls shirk "great injustice" because creation is made to worship its Creator [1].
- **It is not forgiven if a person dies in it** [1]: Qur'an 4:48 says Allah does not forgive association with Him, though He forgives lesser sins [1].
- **It destroys the basis of other deeds** [1]: deeds without Tawhid have no foundation [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills" (4:48) [2].
- "Indeed, association [of others] with Allah is great injustice" (31:13) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The Shahadah, the first pillar, begins with Tawhid; a person enters Islam by affirming it.
- Every prophet is believed to have taught it, so it unites the history of revelation.
- It shapes equality, sincerity, courage and trust.
- The other key beliefs depend on it: angels, books and prophets are all creatures or messages of the One God.
- **Against the statement:** other beliefs are also essential, such as the Last Day, which motivates accountability, and prophethood, which shows how to worship. Some Muslims say belief must be shown in action, so practice is as important as belief.
- **Judgement:** most Muslims would say Tawhid is the first and foundational belief, though they would not separate it from the others, which grow out of it.

*Use of the passage*

- "He is Allah, [who is] One" states uniqueness.
- "the Eternal Refuge" shows complete dependence on Allah, supporting trust.
- "He neither begets nor is born" rejects the idea that Allah has a son or family, which links to major shirk.
- "nor is there to Him any equivalent" supports equality: no human or force can be placed beside Allah.
