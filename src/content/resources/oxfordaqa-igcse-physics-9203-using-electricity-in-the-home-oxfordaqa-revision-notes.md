These notes condense section **3.6.3 Using electricity in the home** (statements a to f) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. For worked trace examples and fault sequences read the [home electricity study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-using-electricity-in-the-home-oxfordaqa/), then use the [home electricity practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-using-electricity-in-the-home-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**dc (a).** Cells and batteries: current always in the same direction. Trace: flat horizontal line.

**ac (b).** Current repeatedly changes direction. Trace: wave crossing the zero line.

**Mains (c).** An ac supply with a set frequency and voltage (for example 230 V, 50 Hz in the UK). rms values are not required.

**Earthing (d).** If a fault makes the metal body of an appliance live, the earth wire conducts the current away harmlessly.

**Fuse or circuit breaker (e).** In the **live** wire. Too great a current → fuse wire overheats and melts, or circuit breaker trips → circuit disconnected. A circuit breaker is **much faster** and **can be reset**.

**Earthed appliances (f).** Fault: live touches the case → large current from live to earth → fuse melts → live disconnected.

**Double insulation (f).** Insulating case with no touchable metal parts → cannot become live → no earth wire needed.

## Oscilloscope method

| Quantity | Method |
|---|---|
| Period | Divisions for one complete cycle × time-base |
| Frequency | 1 ÷ period (period in seconds) |
| Peak ac pd | Divisions from centre line to peak × Y-gain |
| dc pd | Divisions from centre line to flat line × Y-gain |

**Unit reminders:** 1 ms = 0.001 s. A period of 20 ms gives 50 Hz; 10 ms gives 100 Hz; 40 ms gives 25 Hz.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct current (dc) | Current that always passes in the same direction |
| Alternating current (ac) | Current that repeatedly changes direction |
| Period | Time for one complete cycle |
| Frequency | Number of cycles per second, in hertz (Hz) |
| Peak potential difference | Maximum pd reached by an ac supply, measured from zero |
| Time-base | Time represented by one horizontal division of an oscilloscope |
| Y-gain | pd represented by one vertical division |
| Live wire | Wire carrying the alternating pd from the supply |
| Earth wire | Low-resistance safety wire connected to the metal case |
| Fuse | Thin wire in the live wire that melts if the current is too large |
| Circuit breaker | Switch in the live wire that trips if the current is too large; can be reset |
| Double insulated | Appliance with an insulating case and no earth connection |

## Why each safety feature is where it is

- **Fuse in the live wire**: once it breaks, the appliance is no longer connected to the high pd.
- **Earth wire on metal cases**: gives the fault current a very low-resistance path, so the current becomes large enough to blow the fuse.
- **Circuit breaker instead of a fuse**: cuts off faster, so less time for harm, and is simply reset after the fault is fixed.

## Fault sequence to learn

1. Fault: the live wire touches the metal case, so the case becomes live.
2. The earth wire gives a very low-resistance path, so a large current flows from live to earth.
3. The current exceeds the fuse rating, so the fuse melts (or the circuit breaker trips).
4. The live wire is disconnected and the case is safe to touch.

Without the earth wire, step 2 would happen through anyone who touched the case.

## Exam traps

- Peak pd is from the centre line, not peak-to-peak.
- Convert ms to s before 1 ÷ period.
- Fuses and circuit breakers go in the **live** wire.
- The earth wire does not blow; the fuse does.
- Circuit breakers trip; fuses melt.
- Double-insulated appliances have no earth wire.

## Quick self-test

1. What type of current does a battery supply?
2. How does an ac trace differ from a dc trace?
3. One cycle covers 8 divisions at 5 ms/div. Find the period and frequency.
4. A peak is 3 divisions above centre at 10 V/div. What is the peak pd?
5. What is the period of a 25 Hz supply?
6. Why is the fuse placed in the live wire?
7. Give two advantages of a circuit breaker over a fuse.
8. Describe what happens when the live wire touches the earthed metal case of a kettle.
9. Why does a double-insulated hairdryer not need an earth wire?
10. A trace's peaks are 4 divisions above and 4 below the centre at 2 V/div. A student says the peak is 16 V. Correct this.
11. Name the safety feature that conducts current away from a live metal case.
12. Mains electricity is described as having a "set frequency and voltage". What does this mean?

### Answers

1. Direct current (dc).
2. An ac trace is a wave crossing zero; a dc trace is a flat line.
3. 8 × 5 = 40 ms = 0.040 s; frequency = 1 ÷ 0.040 = **25 Hz**.
4. 3 × 10 = **30 V**.
5. 1 ÷ 25 = **0.040 s**.
6. So that when it melts the appliance is disconnected from the high pd.
7. It operates much faster and it can be reset.
8. A large current flows from live to earth through the earth wire; the fuse melts and disconnects the live wire.
9. Its case is an insulator with no touchable metal parts, so it cannot become live.
10. 16 V is peak-to-peak. The peak is 4 × 2 = **8 V**.
11. The earth wire (earthing).
12. The supply always alternates at the same number of cycles per second and has the same fixed voltage.
