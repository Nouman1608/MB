These questions cover subtopic 5.3, Circles, arcs and sectors, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use sector angles that are not factors of 360°, or major sectors, which only the Extended statement includes. Each question is also labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator); on calculator questions use the π key or 3.142.

For explanations, read the [circles, arcs and sectors study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a summary, use the [circles, arcs and sectors revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A circle has radius 6 cm. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find the circumference. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the area. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator) A circle has diameter 23 cm. Calculate its area. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 30°. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find the arc length. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the area of the sector. **[1]**

**4.** (calculator) A circular table top has area 1.5 m². Calculate its diameter. **[3]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A semicircle has diameter 20 cm. Find its perimeter in terms of π. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A sector is one eighth of a circle. Write down its sector angle. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) The inside edge of a running track is made of two straight sections, each 100 m long, joined by two semicircles of diameter 64 m.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the inside edge. **[3]**

**(b)** How many complete laps of the inside edge does an athlete run in 5 km? **[2]**

**8.** (Extended) (calculator) A circle has radius 7.5 cm. A minor sector has angle 48°. Calculate the perimeter of the major sector. **[4]**

**9.** (Extended) (calculator) A sector of a circle of radius 11 cm has arc length 20 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the sector angle. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the area of the sector. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A sector of a circle of radius 12 cm has area 24π cm².

**(a)** Find the sector angle. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the sector in terms of π. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator) A wheel has diameter 0.7 m. It turns 300 times in one minute without slipping.

**(a)** Calculate the distance travelled in that minute. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed in km/h. **[2]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A circle has radius 6 cm. A minor sector of this circle has area 15π cm².

**(a)** Find the angle of the minor sector. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the area of the major sector in terms of π. **[1]**

**13.** (non-calculator) Show that the perimeter of a quarter circle of radius r is r(π + 4)/2. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **12π cm** [1]

**(b)** **36π cm²** [1]

**2.** r = 11.5 cm [1]; area = π × 11.5² = 415.47… = **415 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* π × 23² = 1661.9 uses the diameter; it scores nothing because the formula needs the radius.

**3. (a)** 30/360 = 1/12 [1]; arc = 1/12 × 24π = **2π cm** [1]

**(b)** 1/12 × 144π = **12π cm²** [1]

**4.** πr² = 1.5 [1]; r = √(1.5 ÷ π) = 0.6909… m [1]; diameter = **1.38 m** (3 s.f.) [1]

**5.** Curved edge = ½ × 20π = 10π [1]; perimeter = **(10π + 20) cm** [1]

**6.** 360° ÷ 8 = **45°** [1]

**7. (a)** The two semicircles make one circle: 64π = 201.06… m [1]; straights = 200 m [1]; total = **401 m** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** 5000 ÷ 401.06… = 12.46… [1]; **12 complete laps** [1]

*Examiner insight:* use the unrounded 401.06… m; the question asks for complete laps, so round down.

**8.** Major angle = 360° − 48° = 312° [1]; arc = 312/360 × 2π × 7.5 = 13π = 40.84… cm [1]; perimeter = 40.84… + 7.5 + 7.5 [1] = **55.8 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**9. (a)** θ/360 × 2π × 11 = 20 [1]; θ = **104.2°** (1 d.p.) [1]

**(b)** 104.17…/360 × π × 11² [1] = **110 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* using the rounded angle 104.2° gives 110.03, which still rounds to 110; the exact value is ½ × 11 × 20 = 110.

**10. (a)** θ/360 × 144π = 24π [1]; θ/360 = 1/6, so **θ = 60°** [1]

**(b)** Arc = 1/6 × 24π = 4π [1]; perimeter = **(4π + 24) cm** [1]

**11. (a)** Circumference = 0.7π m [1]; distance = 300 × 0.7π = **660 m** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** 0.6597… km per minute × 60 [1] = **39.6 km/h** [1]

**12. (a)** Area of circle = 36π, so the fraction is 15π ÷ 36π = 5/12 [1]; angle = 5/12 × 360° = **150°** [1]

**(b)** 36π − 15π = **21π cm²** [1]

**13.** Arc = ¼ × 2πr = πr/2, and the two radii add 2r [1]; perimeter = πr/2 + 2r = πr/2 + 4r/2 = r(π + 4)/2 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter in πr².
- Leaving out the two radii from a sector perimeter or the diameter from a semicircle.
- Using the minor angle for a major sector.
- Rounding an angle or radius before using it again.
- Decimal answers when the question asks for π.
- Rounding a "complete laps" or "complete turns" answer up.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find the sector angle from the arc length?**
Set (θ/360) × 2πr equal to the arc length and solve for θ. On a calculator paper give the angle to 1 decimal place.

**How do I find the area of a major sector quickly?**
Find the area of the minor sector and subtract it from πr², or use (360 − θ)/360 × πr² directly.
