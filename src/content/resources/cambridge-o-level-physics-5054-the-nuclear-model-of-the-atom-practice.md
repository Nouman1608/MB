## Section A

**1.** State the charge of a proton, a neutron and an electron, and where in the atom each is found. **[3]**

**2.** Define *proton number* and *nucleon number*. **[2]**

**3.** A nucleus of potassium is written ⁴⁰₁₉K. State the number of protons and the number of neutrons in the nucleus. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by *isotopes* of an element. **[2]**

**5.** A magnesium atom has proton number 12. It loses two electrons. State the number of electrons in the ion formed and the charge on the ion. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by the term *nuclide*. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** In an alpha-particle scattering experiment, a beam of alpha particles is directed at a thin gold foil.

**(a)** Describe how the experiment is carried out and what is measured. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the apparatus is enclosed in a vacuum. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why the gold foil must be very thin. **[1]**

**8.** The results of an alpha-particle scattering experiment are summarised below.

| Observation | Fraction of alpha particles |
|---|---|
| A — passed straight through or deflected by a very small angle | Most |
| B — deflected through a large angle | A small number |
| C — deflected back towards the source | Very few |

**(a)** State what each of observations A, B and C shows about the atom or its nucleus. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why observation B shows that the nucleus is positively charged. **[2]**

**9.** Oxygen has three stable isotopes: ¹⁶₈O, ¹⁷₈O and ¹⁸₈O.

**(a)** State the number of protons and the number of neutrons in each. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why all three are atoms of the same element. **[1]**
**(c)** An atom of ¹⁸₈O gains two electrons. State the number of electrons in the ion. **[1]**

**10.** A nucleus X contains 29 protons and 34 neutrons.

**(a)** State the nucleon number of X. **[1]**
**(b)** Write X in nuclide notation, using the symbol X. **[1]**
**(c)** Nucleus Y contains 29 protons and 36 neutrons. State the relationship between X and Y. **[1]**
**(d)** Nucleus W contains 30 protons and 34 neutrons. State and explain whether W is the same element as X. **[2]**

**11.** An atom contains 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 13 electrons.

**(a)** State its proton number and nucleon number. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the atom has no overall charge. **[2]**
**(c)** The atom forms an ion containing 10 electrons. State the charge on the ion. **[1]**

**12.** Only a very small fraction of the alpha particles in the scattering experiment are deflected through large angles. Explain why this shows that the nucleus is very small compared with the atom. **[2]**

**13.** Explain why almost all of the mass of an atom is in its nucleus, even though the electrons occupy almost all of its volume. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Proton: **+1 (positive), in the nucleus** [1]. Neutron: **0 (no charge), in the nucleus** [1]. Electron: **−1 (negative), orbiting the nucleus** [1].

**2.** Proton number: the **number of protons in the nucleus** [1]. Nucleon number: the **total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus** [1].

**3.** **19 protons** [1]; 40 − 19 = **21 neutrons** [1].

**4.** Atoms of the **same element (same proton number)** [1] with **different numbers of neutrons** (different nucleon numbers) [1].

**5.** **10 electrons** [1]; charge **+2** [1].

**6.** A **particular type of nucleus**, with a stated number of protons and of neutrons (stated proton number and nucleon number) [1].

**7. (a)** A **narrow beam of alpha particles from a source** is aimed at the foil [1]. A **detector is moved round the foil to different angles** [1], and the **number of alpha particles scattered through each angle** is counted [1].
**(b)** Alpha particles are **absorbed (stopped) by a few centimetres of air**, so without a vacuum they would not reach the foil or the detector [1].
**(c)** So that each alpha particle **passes through only a few layers of atoms**, interacting with at most one nucleus, and is not absorbed by the foil [1].

**8. (a)** A: the atom is **mostly empty space** (the nucleus is very small) [1]. B: the nucleus is **positively charged** [1]. C: the nucleus contains **most of the mass of the atom** (concentrated in a small volume) [1].
**(b)** Alpha particles are **positively charged**, and the large deflections show a **strong repulsive force** acted on them [1]. Since **like charges repel**, the nucleus must be **positive** [1].

**9. (a)** All have **8 protons** [1]; neutrons: ¹⁶O has **8** [1]; ¹⁷O has **9** and ¹⁸O has **10** [1].
**(b)** They all have the **same proton number (8)**, which decides the element [1].
**(c)** 8 + 2 = **10 electrons** [1].

**10. (a)** 29 + 34 = **63** [1].
**(b)** **⁶³₂₉X** [1].
**(c)** They are **isotopes** of the same element (same proton number, different neutron number) [1].
**(d)** **No** [1]: W has **30 protons, a different proton number** from X, so it is a different element (the same nucleon number does not matter) [1].

**11. (a)** Proton number **13** [1]; nucleon number 13 + 14 = **27** [1].
**(b)** It has **equal numbers of protons and electrons** (13 each) [1], so the **positive and negative charges cancel** [1].
**(c)** **+3** [1].

**12.** An alpha particle is strongly deflected only if it **passes very close to the nucleus** [1]. Because so **few are deflected strongly**, the nucleus must occupy only a **tiny fraction of the atom's volume** [1].

**13.** Protons and neutrons, which are in the nucleus, each have a **relative mass of 1** [1], while an electron's mass is **very small (about 1/2000 of a proton's)**, so the electrons contribute almost nothing to the mass [1].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Linking "most passed straight through" to positive charge instead of to empty space.
- Saying alpha particles were deflected by electrons.
- Writing the nucleon number as the number of neutrons.
- Putting the proton number on top in nuclide notation.
- Describing isotopes as having different numbers of protons or electrons.
- Saying a positive ion has gained protons rather than lost electrons.
