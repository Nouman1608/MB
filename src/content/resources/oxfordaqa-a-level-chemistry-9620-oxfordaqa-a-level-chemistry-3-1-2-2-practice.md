These questions cover **3.1.2.2 The mole and the Avogadro constant** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, drawn on by Units 2 and 5). Use L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ and give answers to three significant figures unless told otherwise. If a method is unfamiliar, read the [mole and Avogadro constant study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-2/) or the [mole and Avogadro constant revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-2-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the Avogadro constant. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the amount, in moles, in 12.6 g of nitric acid, HNO₃ (Mr 63.0). **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the total number of ions in 0.0500 mol of aluminium sulfate, Al₂(SO₄)₃. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the mass of 0.0350 mol of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂ (Mr 74.1). **[1]**

**5.** Which sample contains the greatest number of atoms? (Ar: H 1.0, He 4.0, O 16.0)

A: 1.0 g of hydrogen, H₂

B: 4.0 g of helium, He

C: 16.0 g of oxygen, O₂

D: 9.0 g of water, H₂O

**[1]**

**6.** 2.81 g of potassium hydroxide, KOH (Mr 56.1), is dissolved in water and made up to 500 cm³. Calculate the concentration in mol dm⁻³. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the number of electrons in 2.00 g of methane, CH₄ (Mr 16.0). **[2]**

### Section B

**8.** This question is about the masses of individual particles.

**(a)** Calculate the mass, in grams, of one gold atom (Ar 197.0). **[1]**

**(b)** An element exists as molecules containing eight atoms. One molecule has a mass of 4.26 × 10⁻²² g. Calculate the relative molecular mass of the element, deduce its relative atomic mass and identify the element. **[3]**

**9.** 3.42 g of aluminium sulfate, Al₂(SO₄)₃ (Mr 342.3), is dissolved in water and made up to 200 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the solution in mol dm⁻³. **[2]**

**(b)** State the concentration of sulfate ions in the solution. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of Al³⁺ ions in 10.0 cm³ of the solution. **[2]**

**(d)** Give the concentration of aluminium sulfate in g dm⁻³. **[1]**

**10.** A student needs 250 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sodium carbonate solution. The only solid available is hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (Mr 286.0).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the hydrated solid the student must weigh out. **[2]**

**(b)** By mistake the student weighs out the mass needed for anhydrous sodium carbonate (Mr 106.0), 1.325 g, but uses the hydrated solid. Calculate the concentration of the solution actually made. **[2]**

**(c)** State the concentration of sodium ions in the solution the student intended to make. **[1]**

**11.** A stock solution of sulfuric acid has a concentration of 1.20 mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of stock solution needed to make 500 cm³ of 0.0600 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid. **[2]**

**(b)** Assuming each H₂SO₄ releases two H⁺ ions, give the concentration of H⁺ ions in the diluted acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of H⁺ ions in 1.00 cm³ of the diluted acid. **[2]**

**12.** Sodium reacts with water: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

**(a)** State the amount of hydrogen molecules formed when 0.400 mol of sodium reacts completely. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of hydrogen molecules in your answer to (a). **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of sodium (Ar 23.0) that must react to produce 1.00 × 10²² hydrogen molecules. **[3]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** The number of particles in one mole of a substance (6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) [1] **[1]**

**2.** 12.6 ÷ 63.0 = **0.200 mol** [1] **[1]**

**3.** Each formula unit has 2 Al³⁺ + 3 SO₄²⁻ = 5 ions, so 0.250 mol of ions [1]; 0.250 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.51 × 10²³** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A sulfate ion counts as one ion, not five atoms.

**4.** 0.0350 × 74.1 = **2.59 g** [1] **[1]**

**5.** **D** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A, B and C each contain 1.0 mol of atoms; D is 0.50 mol of molecules with three atoms each, which is 1.5 mol of atoms.

**6.** n = 2.81 ÷ 56.1 = 0.0501 mol [1]; c = 0.0501 ÷ 0.500 = **0.100 mol dm⁻³** [1] **[2]**

**7.** n(CH₄) = 2.00 ÷ 16.0 = 0.125 mol; each molecule has 6 + 4 = 10 electrons, so 1.25 mol of electrons [1]; 1.25 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **7.53 × 10²³** (accept 7.52 × 10²³) [1] **[2]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** 197.0 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = **3.27 × 10⁻²² g** [1]

**(b)** Mr = 4.26 × 10⁻²² × 6.02 × 10²³ = 256 [1]; Ar = 256 ÷ 8 = 32.1 [1]; the element is **sulfur** (S₈) [1] **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** n = 3.42 ÷ 342.3 = 9.99 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 9.99 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.200 = **0.0500 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** 3 × 0.0500 = **0.150 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** n(Al³⁺) = 0.0500 × 0.0100 × 2 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; number = **6.02 × 10²⁰** (accept 6.01 × 10²⁰) [1]

**(d)** 3.42 g in 0.200 dm³ = **17.1 g dm⁻³** [1] **[6]**

**10.**

**(a)** n = 0.0500 × 0.250 = 0.0125 mol [1]; m = 0.0125 × 286.0 = **3.58 g** [1]

**(b)** n = 1.325 ÷ 286.0 = 4.63 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 4.63 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.250 = **0.0185 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** 2 × 0.0500 = **0.100 mol dm⁻³** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The water of crystallisation adds mass but no extra sodium carbonate, so using the anhydrous mass gives a solution far weaker than intended.

**11.**

**(a)** V₁ = c₂V₂ ÷ c₁ = 0.0600 × 500 ÷ 1.20 [1] = **25.0 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 0.0600 = **0.120 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** n(H⁺) = 0.120 × (1.00 ÷ 1000) = 1.20 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; number = **7.22 × 10¹⁹** [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** 0.400 ÷ 2 = **0.200 mol** [1]

**(b)** 0.200 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.20 × 10²³** [1]

**(c)** n(H₂) = 1.00 × 10²² ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.0166 mol [1]; n(Na) = 2 × 0.0166 = 0.0332 mol [1]; mass = 0.0332 × 23.0 = **0.764 g** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the number of molecules to moles first, then use the equation's 2 : 1 ratio.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cm³ in n = c × V.
- Counting molecules when the question asks for atoms, ions or electrons.
- Using an anhydrous Mr for a hydrated solid.
- Rounding intermediate values too early.
