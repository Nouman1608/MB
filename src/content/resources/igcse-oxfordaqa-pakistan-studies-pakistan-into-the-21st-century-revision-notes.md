**These notes condense topic 3.1.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236, Pakistan
into the 21st Century: from Independence to 2022, a compulsory topic in Paper 1 Section B.** Use the
timeline and tables to fix the order of governments, what each constitutional change did, the main
features of each foreign relationship, and the key people and laws on women's empowerment.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Federalism | Power shared between the federal government and the provinces |
| Constitution | The basic law setting out how the state is governed |
| Amendment | A formal change to the constitution passed by parliament |
| Transparency | Openness of government decisions to public scrutiny |
| Rule of law | Everyone, including leaders, is subject to the law |
| Accountability | Officials can be questioned, held responsible and removed |
| No-confidence vote | A vote in the National Assembly to remove the Prime Minister |
| CPEC | China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, launched 2015 |
| IMF | International Monetary Fund, which lends to countries with balance-of-payments problems |

## Constitutional timeline

- **1949** Objectives Resolution: sovereignty belongs to Allah; democracy; minority rights.
- **1956** First constitution: Islamic Republic; parliamentary.
- **1962** Ayub Khan's presidential constitution.
- **1973** Current constitution: federal, parliamentary, two houses, Urdu as national language.
- **2010** 18th Amendment: President's dissolution power removed; provincial devolution; NWFP renamed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; right to education aged 5–16.
- **2010–11** 19th Amendment: revised process for judicial appointments.

## Governments 1999–2022

| Years | Leader | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| 1999–2008 | Pervez Musharraf | Coup 1999; President 2001; growth; local government; private TV; post-2001 US alliance; Lawyers' Movement 2007; resigned 2008 |
| 2008–13 | Asif Ali Zardari (President), PPP government | 18th and 19th Amendments; first full parliamentary term completed |
| 2013–18 | Nawaz Sharif, then Shahid Khaqan Abbasi (PML-N) | First democratic transfer of power; CPEC; energy projects; anti-militancy operations; Sharif disqualified 2017 |
| 2018–22 | Imran Khan (PTI) | Ehsaas; health cards; tree planting; IMF programme 2019; Covid-19 response; removed by no-confidence vote April 2022 |

## Covid-19 response

- National Command and Operation Centre coordinated policy.
- "Smart" lockdowns of high-infection areas rather than a long national lockdown.
- Ehsaas emergency cash for low-income families.
- Vaccination campaign from 2021.
- **Impact:** lost incomes for daily-wage workers, school closures, slower growth.

## Foreign relations

| Partner | Key points |
|---|---|
| India | Kashmir dispute; crises and dialogue; tensions in 2019; Kartarpur Corridor |
| China | Military co-operation (JF-17); CPEC roads, power and Gwadar; agricultural investment |
| Turkey | Close political and cultural ties |
| Saudi Arabia, UAE | Financial support; oil; jobs for Pakistani workers and remittances |
| USA | Post-2001 ally and aid donor; tension over drones and the 2011 Abbottabad raid; refugees and violence from the Afghan war |
| IMF | Support after the 2008 crisis and in later programmes, with conditions |
| UN | Pakistan a major contributor of peacekeeping troops |

## Women's empowerment

- **Benazir Bhutto:** Prime Minister 1988–90 and 1993–96; first woman elected to lead a Muslim-majority country.
- **Dr Sania Nishtar:** founder of Heartfile; led the Ehsaas programme, paying support directly to women.
- **Laws:** Protection of Women Act 2006; workplace harassment law 2010; anti-honour-killing law 2016.
- **Representation:** reserved seats for women in the national and provincial assemblies.
- **By 2022:** progress in schooling and politics; gaps remain in literacy, jobs and rural access.

## Culture

- **Urdu:** national language under the 1973 Constitution; a link language across provinces.
- **Regional languages:** Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Saraiki, Brahui; key to identity.
- **Sport:** cricket (1992 World Cup), hockey (Olympic golds), squash (Jahangir Khan, Jansher Khan).

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why the 18th Amendment was important. **[4]**

**Model answer:** It removed the President's power to dissolve the National Assembly, so elected
governments were harder to dismiss and parliament was strengthened [1–2]. It devolved many
subjects, such as health and education, to the provinces, strengthening federalism [1]. It also
renamed NWFP as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and made free education a constitutional right for children aged
5 to 16 [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing up the 1956 and 1973 constitutions.** 1973 is the current one.
- **Calling Zardari Prime Minister.** He was President; the Prime Ministers were from the PPP.
- **One-sided judgements.** For Musharraf, weigh growth and media freedom against political repression and violence.
- **Vague foreign policy answers.** Name a specific event, agreement or project.

## How is this topic examined?

This topic is examined in OxfordAQA 9236 Paper 1, Section B, worth 28 of the paper's 77 marks; each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes. The section mixes multiple-choice, short and extended response questions on this compulsory topic. Short items reward precise named facts, so learn the dates, places and people in the tables above; the extended responses reward a clear line of argument, two or three developed points with evidence, and a judgement that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who were the Prime Ministers after the 2013 election?**
Nawaz Sharif until 2017, then Shahid Khaqan
Abbasi until 2018.

**What was the Ehsaas programme?**
A social-protection and poverty-reduction programme from 2019,
including cash transfers and emergency Covid-19 payments.
