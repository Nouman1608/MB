These notes condense subtopic **5.3 Circles, arcs and sectors** from Topic 5 Mensuration of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. For explanations and worked examples, read the [circles, arcs and sectors study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors/); to practise, use the [circles, arcs and sectors practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors-practice/).

## Tier difference

| | Core (C5.3) | Extended (E5.3) |
|---|---|---|
| Circumference and area of a circle | yes | yes |
| Arc length and sector area | sector angle is a factor of 360° | any sector angle |
| Major and minor sectors | not named | named in the notes |
| Answers in terms of π | may be asked | may be asked |

## Formulas

```
GIVEN on the List of formulas:
  circumference   C = 2πr
  area            A = πr²
BUILD from them (not given):
  arc length      = (θ/360) × 2πr
  sector area     = (θ/360) × πr²
  sector perimeter = arc length + 2r
  semicircle perimeter = πr + 2r
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radius, r | Centre to the circle |
| Diameter, d | Across the circle through the centre; d = 2r |
| Circumference | The perimeter of a whole circle |
| Arc | Part of the circumference |
| Sector | Region bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Minor / major sector | Angle less than / more than 180°; the two angles add to 360° |
| In terms of π | Leave π as a symbol, e.g. 25π cm² |

## Fractions for factor angles (Core)

| θ | θ/360 | θ | θ/360 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 1/12 | 90° | 1/4 |
| 40° | 1/9 | 120° | 1/3 |
| 45° | 1/8 | 180° | 1/2 |
| 60° | 1/6 | 72° | 1/5 |

**Worked reminder.** Radius 6 cm, angle 120°. Arc = ⅓ × 12π = 4π cm. Area = ⅓ × 36π = 12π cm². Perimeter = (4π + 12) cm.

## Working backwards

| Given | Find | Method |
|---|---|---|
| circumference C | r | r = C ÷ 2π |
| area A | r | r = √(A ÷ π) |
| arc length L and r | θ | θ = L × 360 ÷ (2πr) |
| sector area S and r | θ | θ = S × 360 ÷ (πr²) |
| sector area S and θ | r | r² = S × 360 ÷ (θπ) |

Keep unrounded values in the calculator between steps.

**Worked reminder (Extended).** An arc of length 10π cm has radius 15 cm.

```
θ/360 × 30π = 10π  ->  θ/360 = 1/3  ->  θ = 120°
```

## Writing answers in terms of π

- Simplify the number in front: 72π/4 becomes 18π.
- Keep a sum as a sum: a semicircle perimeter is (5π + 10) cm, not 15π cm, because 10 has no π.
- Put brackets round the whole expression before the unit.
- Never replace π with a decimal partway through, then put π back.

## Circles in context

| Situation | What to use |
|---|---|
| Wheel turning once | distance moved = one circumference |
| Number of turns | total distance ÷ circumference (same units); round **down** for complete turns |
| Running track with semicircular ends | two semicircles make one full circle |
| Path round a circular pond | big circle area − small circle area |
| Clock hand sweeping | sector with angle = 6° per minute for the minute hand |

**Worked reminder.** The minute hand of a clock is 9 cm long. In 20 minutes it turns 120°, so its tip travels ⅓ × 18π = **6π cm**.

## Exam traps

- πd² or π × diameter² for area: use the **radius**.
- Sector perimeter without the two radii.
- Minor angle used for a major sector question (use 360° − θ).
- A decimal answer when "in terms of π" is asked, or an unsimplified one such as 72π/4.
- Using a rounded radius in a later part.
- Writing π ≈ 3.14 or 22/7: the syllabus says use the π key or 3.142.
- Square units on a length, or linear units on an area.

## Self-test

1. Find the circumference of a circle of radius 4.5 cm, in terms of π.
2. Find the area of a circle of diameter 10 cm, in terms of π.
3. A circle has area 81π cm². Find its circumference in terms of π.
4. Find the perimeter of a semicircle of radius 5 cm, in terms of π.
5. A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 72°. Find its arc length in terms of π.
6. Find the area of the sector in question 5, in terms of π.
7. Find the perimeter of a quarter circle of radius 8 cm, to 3 significant figures.
8. (Extended) Find the area of a major sector of radius 6 cm whose minor angle is 100°, to 3 significant figures.
9. (Extended) An arc of a circle of radius 9 cm has length 6π cm. Find the sector angle.
10. (Extended) A sector of angle 80° has area 20π cm². Find the radius.
11. A circle has circumference 31.4 cm. Find its radius to 3 significant figures.
12. A circular pond of radius 3 m has a path 1 m wide around it. Find the area of the path in terms of π.

### Answers

1. **9π cm**
2. r = 5, so **25π cm²**
3. r = 9, so **18π cm**
4. 5π + 10, so **(5π + 10) cm**
5. 1/5 × 20π = **4π cm**
6. 1/5 × 100π = **20π cm²**
7. ¼ × 16π + 16 = 4π + 16 = **28.6 cm**
8. 260/360 × 36π = 26π = **81.7 cm²**
9. θ/360 × 18π = 6π, so θ/360 = 1/3 and **θ = 120°**
10. 80/360 × πr² = 20π, so r² = 90 and **r = 9.49 cm** (exactly √90 = 3√10)
11. 31.4 ÷ 2π = **5.00 cm** (3 s.f.)
12. π × 4² − π × 3² = **7π m²**
