Revision notes for the focused study **Conflict and displacement** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Both examples are required: **post-war displacement in Europe (1945 to 1960)** and **the Indochina refugee crisis (1975 to 1990)**. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-conflict-and-displacement/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-conflict-and-displacement-practice/).

## Paper 1 in one box

| Question | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| First | Explain how the content of sources A and B can be used to answer the inquiry question | 6 |
| Second | Analyse how the context of source C influences how it can be used | 6 |
| Third | Examine how the perspectives in all the sources can be used | 12 |

Total 24 marks, 1 hour 15 minutes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Refugee | A person outside their country who has fled persecution or conflict |
| Displaced person (DP) | Someone forced from home; in 1945 the term meant people outside their home country because of the war |
| Stateless person | A person with no recognised nationality |
| Repatriation | Return to the country of origin |
| Resettlement | Movement to a new country |

## The three inquiry questions

1. What were the conditions that led to mass displacement?
2. What was the national and international response to displacement?
3. How was displacement experienced by different groups?

## Post-war displacement in Europe (1945 to 1960)

**1. Conditions**

- Combat operations and Allied victory: destroyed cities, millions outside their countries in 1945.
- Persecution and fear of reprisals: Jewish survivors who could not return, people who feared Soviet rule, ethnic Germans expelled from the east.
- Economic factors: shortages of food and housing, collapsed industry.

**2. Response**

- Camps, migration, emigration and repatriation: DP camps in Germany and Austria; many returned in 1945; others resettled.
- Allied governments and NGOs, including the International Red Cross.
- UN: UNRRA (from 1943) and the IRO (from 1947), then the UNHCR (early 1950s).

**3. Experiences**

- Refugees and stateless persons.
- Concentration camp survivors, including Jews, Roma and prisoners of war.
- Former forces under German command: screening, resettlement or repatriation.

## The Indochina refugee crisis (1975 to 1990)

**1. Conditions**

- Combat operations and Communist victory: the fall of Saigon (April 1975), Khmer Rouge rule in Cambodia (1975 to 1979), the Pathet Lao in Laos.
- Persecution and fear of reprisals: re-education camps, persecution of minorities.
- Economic factors: collectivisation, property loss and hardship.

**2. Response**

- Camps and resettlement in Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Hong Kong and the Philippines, then in the United States, Canada, Australia and France.
- The role of the USA (large-scale resettlement) and the Vietnamese government (policies causing flight, and cooperation on departures).
- The UNHCR and the Orderly Departure Program (1979), allowing legal emigration from Vietnam.

**3. Experiences**

- Vietnamese, including the Hoa (ethnic Chinese) and Montagnard.
- Cambodians fleeing the Khmer Rouge, including the Chams.
- Laotians and highland peoples, including the Hmong.

## Comparing the two examples

| Point | Europe 1945 to 1960 | Indochina 1975 to 1990 |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | End of a world war | End of a long war and communist takeover |
| Scale | Millions | Over a million resettled, many more displaced |
| Main agencies | UNRRA, IRO | UNHCR, with national governments |
| Route | Mainly by land | By land and by sea (boat people) |
| Solutions | Repatriation, resettlement | Camps, resettlement, Orderly Departure Program |
| Long-term effect | Shaped post-war refugee law | Tested international burden-sharing |

## Skills checklist

**Question 1.** Use both sources. Pick specific points from each. One source caps the mark at 3.

**Question 2.** Use the author, date, audience and purpose of source C and link them to how it can be used.

**Question 3.** State each source's perspective and compare them. Use all three sources for the top band; two sources cap the mark at 9.

## Worked example: a question 2 paragraph

Inquiry question: What was the national and international response to displacement?

Source C (constructed for practice) is a 1946 report by an aid worker for a relief agency describing overcrowded camps in the American zone of occupied Germany.

"The context shapes how the source can be used. The author was an aid worker, who saw conditions at first hand and wanted to secure resources, so the report is likely to emphasise need and the strain on the camps. This makes it useful for showing the scale of the international response and its limits. The date, 1946, is when UNRRA was still operating, so the report shows the early response and cannot show the later work of the IRO. As a report to a relief agency, its audience was donors and officials, which means it was written to persuade."

## Linking to concepts

- **Cause and consequence:** conflict causes displacement, and displacement has its own consequences.
- **Continuity and change:** new institutions (IRO, UNHCR), but continuing statelessness.
- **Perspectives:** refugees, host governments, aid agencies, the governments that people fled.
- **Significance:** the cases shaped the international refugee system.

## Common mistakes

- Quoting exact figures with no source or date.
- Treating displaced people as one group.
- Confusing UNRRA, IRO and UNHCR.
- Describing the war instead of the source.

## Key dates and organisations

| Date | Event or body |
|---|---|
| 1943 | UNRRA founded |
| 1945 | War ends in Europe; Potsdam Conference; millions displaced |
| 1947 | IRO replaces UNRRA |
| 1948 | Israel founded; Displaced Persons Act in the USA |
| Early 1950s | UNHCR begins work; 1951 Refugee Convention |
| April 1975 | Fall of Saigon; Khmer Rouge take Phnom Penh |
| 1978 to 1979 | Large departures of Hoa and boat people |
| 1979 | Geneva conference; Orderly Departure Program |
| 1989 | Comprehensive Plan of Action |

**Groups.** Europe: Jewish survivors, Roma, prisoners of war, stateless persons, ethnic Germans, former forces under German command. Indochina: Vietnamese, Hoa, Montagnard, Cambodians including the Chams, Laotians including the Hmong.

**Source types to expect.** Camp registers, agency reports, photographs, memoirs, oral testimony, government statements, newspaper reports and statistical tables. For each, ask who produced it and why.

## Markbands at a glance

| Question | Lower bands | Top band |
|---|---|---|
| First (6 marks) | Describes the sources, with little explanation of how the content answers the inquiry question | Explains how a range of specific content from both sources can be used, with specific and developed connections |
| Second (6 marks) | Describes the context with minimal analysis | Analyses how the context influences how the source can be used, supported by relevant references to the context |
| Third (12 marks) | Outlines perspectives briefly, with minimal links to the question | Insightful understanding of the perspectives of all the sources, effectively examining similarities and differences, with relevant references |

A useful routine is to spend about 10 minutes on the first question, 10 on the second and 20 to 25 on the third, leaving a little time to check that every answer refers back to the inquiry question.

## Self-test

1. What does UNRRA stand for?
2. When did the IRO replace UNRRA?
3. What is the Orderly Departure Program?
4. Who were the Hoa?
5. Name one group in Laos that fled after 1975.
6. Which organisation took over from the IRO?
7. What is a stateless person?

**Answers.**

1. United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration.
2. In 1947.
3. A 1979 arrangement that let Vietnamese people leave legally for resettlement, rather than by boat.
4. Ethnic Chinese living in Vietnam.
5. The Hmong (or other highland peoples).
6. The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees.
7. A person with no recognised nationality.
