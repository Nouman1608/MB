## Questions

**1.** (16.5.1) The list shows four changes that happen during puberty.

- the voice becomes deeper
- the breasts develop
- the hips widen
- hair grows on the face

**(a)** For each change, state whether it is caused mainly by testosterone or by oestrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the organ that produces testosterone and the organ that produces oestrogen. **[2]**

**2.** (16.6.1, 16.6.2, 16.6.3)

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a *sexually transmitted infection*. **[1]**
**(b)** HIV is a pathogen that causes a sexually transmitted infection. State what the letters HIV stand for. **[1]**
**(c)** State the condition that HIV infection may lead to. **[1]**

**3.** (16.5.2) Ultrasound scans measured the thickness of a woman's uterus lining during a regular 28-day cycle. Day 1 is the first day of menstruation.

| Day of cycle | 1 | 5 | 7 | 14 | 21 | 28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thickness of lining / mm | 11 | 3 | 3 | 10 | 13 | 12 |

**(a)** Describe the changes in the lining between day 1 and day 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate at which the lining thickened between day 7 and day 14. Give the unit. (calculator allowed) **[2]**
**(c)** State what happens in the ovary at about day 14. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the lining stays thick between day 14 and day 28. **[1]**

**4.** (16.5.2) A woman's menstrual cycles are regular and 32 days long. Her menstruation starts on 5 May, which is day 1 of the cycle. In this question, assume that ovulation happens 14 days before the next menstruation starts.

**(a)** Calculate the day of the cycle on which she ovulates, and give the date. **[2]**
**(b)** State the date on which her next menstruation is expected to start. **[1]**
**(c)** No egg is fertilised in this cycle. Describe the changes in her uterus lining between ovulation and the start of the next cycle. **[2]**

**5.** (16.6.4)

**(a)** For each situation, state whether HIV could be transmitted from an infected person to another person.

**(i)** sharing a hypodermic needle **[1]**
**(ii)** drinking from the same cup **[1]**
**(iii)** receiving a transfusion of unscreened blood from an infected donor **[1]**
**(iv)** a baby being breastfed by an infected mother **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how sharing a needle can pass HIV from one person to another. **[1]**

**6.** (16.6.5) Explain how each of these measures reduces the spread of HIV.

**(a)** testing all donated blood before it is used **[2]**
**(b)** giving people who inject drugs free sterile needles **[2]**
**(c)** having fewer sexual partners **[2]**

**(b)** Name the gland that secretes FSH and LH. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* (16.5.4) The table shows the concentration of LH in the blood of a woman during one 28-day cycle. The units are arbitrary.

| Day of cycle | 2 | 6 | 10 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 18 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**(a)** State the day on which ovulation is most likely to happen, and give a reason from the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the LH concentration is on day 14 than on day 2. (calculator allowed) **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* (16.5.3, 16.5.4) Explain how the hormones FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone control what happens:

- at the end of a menstrual cycle in which no pregnancy occurs, and
- during a pregnancy, so that the lining is kept and no further cycles happen. **[10]**

**11.** (16.6.2, 16.6.4, 16.6.5) The table shows new HIV diagnoses in one region (illustrative figures). At the start of year 3 the region began a programme of free condoms, free HIV testing and school education about STIs.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New HIV diagnoses | 2400 | 2300 | 2250 | 1900 | 1620 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in new HIV diagnoses from year 3 to year 5. (calculator allowed) **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest how each part of the programme could have reduced the number of new infections. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why these data alone do not prove that the programme caused the decrease. **[1]**
**(d)** Another STI, gonorrhoea, is caused by a bacterium and can be cured with antibiotics. Explain why antibiotics do not cure HIV infection. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Testosterone: **voice deepens** and **facial hair** -- both needed [1]. Oestrogen: **breasts develop** and **hips widen** -- both needed [1].
**(b)** Testosterone: the **testes** [1]. Oestrogen: the **ovaries** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Where one mark covers two answers, both must be right, so one slip in part (a) costs a whole mark.

**2. (a)** An infection that is **transmitted through sexual contact** [1].
**(b)** **Human immunodeficiency virus** [1].
**(c)** **AIDS** [1].

*Examiner insight:* "A disease of the reproductive organs" is not credited: the definition is about how the infection is passed on.

**3. (a)** Between day 1 and day 5 the lining **breaks down and is lost** from the body (menstruation), falling from 11 mm to 3 mm [1]; it then **stays thin at 3 mm** until day 7 [1].
**(b)** (10 − 3) ÷ (14 − 7) = 7 ÷ 7 [1] = **1.0 mm per day** [1].
**(c)** **Ovulation**: an egg is released from the ovary [1].
**(d)** The lining is kept thick so that an **embryo can implant** in it if fertilisation happens [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the accuracy mark needs the unit "mm per day"; a bare "1" or "1 mm" loses it.

**4. (a)** Ovulation is on day 32 − 14 = **day 18** [1], which is **22 May** [1].
**(b)** The next cycle's day 1 is day 33 of this one: **6 June** [1].
**(c)** The lining **stays thick** after ovulation [1]; as no embryo implants, it **breaks down and is lost** at the next menstruation [1].

*Examiner insight:* Day 1 is 5 May, so day 18 is 17 days later, not 18; write the day number first so the method mark is safe if the date slips.

**5. (a) (i)** **Yes** [1]. **(ii)** **No** [1]. **(iii)** **Yes** [1]. **(iv)** **Yes** [1].
**(b)** **Infected blood** left in or on the needle is **injected directly into the bloodstream** of the next user [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the answer must name infected blood entering the body; "germs on the needle" is too vague.

**6. (a)** Infected blood is **detected and not used** [1], so HIV is **not passed to the person who receives the transfusion** [1].
**(b)** People who inject drugs **do not need to share needles** [1], so **infected blood is not transferred** from one user to another [1].
**(c)** Body fluids are exchanged with **fewer people** [1], so there is a **lower chance of having sex with an infected person** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each part has a "what the measure does" mark and a "so the virus cannot pass" mark; restating the measure earns nothing.

**7.** *(Extended)* **(a)**

| Hormone | During the menstrual cycle | Later in pregnancy |
|---|---|---|
| oestrogen | **ovary** (developing follicle) [1] | **placenta** [1] |
| progesterone | **corpus luteum** in the ovary [1] | **placenta** [1] |

**(b)** The **pituitary gland** [1].

*Examiner insight:* For progesterone during the cycle, name the **corpus luteum**; "uterus" is where the hormones act, not where they are made.

**8.** *(Extended)* **(a)** **Day 14** (accept 14 to 15) [1]; **LH peaks** then, and the LH surge triggers ovulation [1].
**(b)** 60 ÷ 5 = **12 times** [1].
**(c)** The developing follicle releases **oestrogen**, which rises in the first half of the cycle [1]; **high oestrogen stimulates the pituitary gland** to release a surge of LH [1].
**(d)** It causes the empty follicle to develop into the **corpus luteum** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c) the second mark needs the link "high oestrogen → pituitary → LH"; "oestrogen turns into LH" scores zero.

**9.** *(Extended)* The progesterone-like hormone **inhibits the release of FSH** from the pituitary gland [1], so **no follicle develops** and no egg matures [1]. It also **inhibits LH**, so there is **no LH surge** to trigger ovulation [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Stops the hormones" is not enough; name FSH or LH and its effect in the ovary.

**10.** *(Extended)*

*No pregnancy:*
- No embryo implants, so the **corpus luteum breaks down** [1].
- **Progesterone** concentration **falls** [1].
- The lining is **no longer maintained**: **menstruation** [1].
- Low progesterone **no longer inhibits FSH**, so FSH rises [1].
- FSH makes a **new follicle** develop; its oestrogen **rebuilds the lining** [1].

*Pregnancy:*
- The **corpus luteum** keeps secreting progesterone in early pregnancy [1].
- Later the **placenta secretes progesterone and oestrogen** [1].
- Progesterone **maintains the lining**, so **no menstruation** [1].
- Progesterone **inhibits FSH and LH** release from the pituitary [1].
- So **no follicle develops and no ovulation** happens [1].

*Examiner insight:* An answer covering only one half can reach at most half the marks, so plan both; it is the **fall** in progesterone that leads to menstruation.

**11. (a)** Decrease = 2250 − 1620 = 630 [1]; (630 ÷ 2250) × 100 = **28%** [1].
**(b)** **Condoms**: a barrier, so body fluids are not exchanged during sex [1]. **Testing**: infected people find out and can avoid passing HIV on; their partners can be tested [1]. **Education**: people learn how HIV spreads, so use condoms and avoid sharing needles [1].
**(c)** Any one: other factors may have changed at the same time; more testing changes the number diagnosed, not only the number infected; data from one region over a short time [1].
**(d)** HIV is a **virus** [1]; antibiotics **kill bacteria but do not affect viruses** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a) showing 630 earns the method mark even if the division slips; in (b) each mark must be tied to a different part of the programme.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the uterus breaks down" instead of "the **lining** breaks down".
- Giving a rate without a unit.
- Saying FSH or LH is made in the ovary. Both come from the **pituitary gland**.
- Forgetting the **corpus luteum**, or that the **placenta** makes the hormones later in pregnancy.
- Saying progesterone "causes" menstruation, when it is the **fall** in progesterone that does.
- Writing "stops hormones" without naming FSH or LH.
- Naming an STI control measure without explaining how it stops body fluids passing between people.
- Treating HIV and AIDS as the same thing, or saying antibiotics treat HIV.
