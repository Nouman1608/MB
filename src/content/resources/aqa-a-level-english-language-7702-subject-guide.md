AQA A-Level English Language (7702) treats language as something to investigate: how it varies between people and
situations, how children acquire it, how it changes over time, and how it shapes and reflects identity and power.
It appeals to students who enjoy close analysis, are curious about the way people actually speak and write, and
want to develop their own writing as well. The qualification is linear and not tiered. It is assessed by two
written papers, each lasting 2 hours 30 minutes, worth 100 marks and carrying 40%, and a non-exam assessment
worth 100 marks and 20%.

## How the course is organised

The specification is organised into three areas, each linked to one component.

**Language, the individual and society.** Students study textual variations and representations, learning how
texts from different contexts create meaning, and children's language development. Methods of language analysis
are introduced here and used throughout the course.

**Language diversity and change.** Students explore how language varies according to factors such as region,
social group and identity, and how it has changed over time. This area also covers language discourses (the ways
people write and talk about language itself) and writing skills, alongside further work on methods of language
analysis.

**Language in action.** This is the area assessed by the non-exam assessment. It brings together a language
investigation, a piece of original writing and, once again, methods of language analysis.

The repeated appearance of methods of language analysis in all three areas is the clearest sign of how the course
works: the same analytical toolkit is applied to new material in each component.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7702/1 Paper 1: Language, the Individual and Society | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 | 40% |
| 7702/2 Paper 2: Language Diversity and Change | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 | 40% |
| 7702/NEA Non-exam assessment: Language in Action | No exam duration | 100 | 20% |

**Paper 1** covers textual variations and representations, and children's language development from 0 to 11.
Section A asks for analysis of two linked texts and a comparison, worth 70 marks in total across three questions.
Section B is a discursive essay on children's language development, worth 30 marks.

**Paper 2** covers language diversity and change, language discourses and writing skills. Section A is an
evaluative essay on diversity or change, worth 30 marks. Section B is an analysis of two texts, worth 40 marks,
followed by a directed writing task worth 30 marks.

**Language in Action** consists of a 2,000-word language investigation and a 1,500-word original writing piece
with commentary, 3,500 words in total. It is teacher-assessed and moderated by AQA.

The qualification is out of 300 marks. It was first taught in September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in
June 2017.

## How the parts fit together

The three components share a core skill: describing language precisely and explaining what it is doing in its
context. Methods of language analysis learned for Paper 1 are the same methods needed for the texts on Paper 2
and for the data in the investigation. A student who builds a reliable, well-practised analytical vocabulary in
the first term will use it in every component.

There are also links in content. Representation, studied through texts in Paper 1, connects closely to language
discourses in Paper 2, where students examine how people represent language and its users. Diversity and change
provide ideas and frameworks that can be drawn on when choosing a topic for the investigation. The
directed writing task on Paper 2 and the original writing in the NEA both develop the same craft of writing for
a specific audience and purpose.

Finally, the course rewards two different kinds of writing: analytical writing about texts and data, and
discursive or evaluative essays about language issues. Both appear on each exam paper, so neither can be left
until late in the course.

## Planning your study

A workable route through the two years is:

1. **Begin with methods of language analysis and textual variations and representations**, using a wide range of
   short texts to build confidence.
2. **Introduce children's language development** during the first year, alongside continued text analysis.
3. **Start language diversity and change, then language discourses**, linking each to the representation work
   already done.
4. **Develop writing skills throughout**, with regular short directed-writing exercises.
5. **Plan the NEA early.** Choose an investigation topic you can collect suitable data for, and start the
   original writing with enough time for redrafting. Your teacher will set the internal deadlines.

Keep a glossary of linguistic terms with examples and a file of language data collected from everyday life,
which feeds both the papers and the investigation. Short analysis tasks can start immediately; full papers are
most useful once each paper's content has been taught. How to divide time across the sections of each paper is
covered on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is AQA A-Level English Language assessed?**
By two written papers, each 2 hours 30 minutes, 100 marks and 40%, and the Language in Action non-exam assessment, worth 100 marks and 20%.

**What is on Paper 1?**
Section A is analysis of two linked texts and a comparison, worth 70 marks across three questions. Section B is a discursive essay on children's language development, worth 30 marks.

**What is on Paper 2?**
Section A is an evaluative essay on diversity or change (30 marks). Section B is an analysis of two texts (40 marks) followed by a directed writing task (30 marks).

**What does the NEA involve?**
A 2,000-word language investigation and a 1,500-word original writing piece with commentary, 3,500 words in total. It is teacher-assessed and moderated by AQA.

**How many marks is the A-level out of?**
The three components total 300 marks.
