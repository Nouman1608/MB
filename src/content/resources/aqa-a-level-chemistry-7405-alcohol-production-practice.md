These questions cover section **3.3.5.1 Alcohol production** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Alcohol production study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-alcohol-production/) and the [Alcohol production revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-alcohol-production-revision-notes/). Questions across the whole topic are in the [Alcohols practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alcohols-practice/). Use Mᵣ glucose = 180.0, ethanol = 46.0 and R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Ethanol is made industrially by the hydration of ethene. State the catalyst, the temperature and the pressure used. **[3]**

**2.** Pent-1-ene reacts with steam in the presence of an acid catalyst.

**(a)** Name the major product. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the minor product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why more of the major product forms. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of cyclohexene with steam in the presence of an acid catalyst, and name the product. **[4]**

**4.** 2-methylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, is hydrated using an acid catalyst.

**(a)** Draw or name the carbocation that leads to the major product. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the major product. **[1]**

**5.** Name two different alkenes that each give butan-2-ol as their only or major product on hydration. **[2]**

**6.** A student suggests carrying out fermentation of glucose at 70 °C so that the reaction is faster. Evaluate this suggestion. **[2]**

**7.** Ethanol can be made by two routes:

C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the percentage atom economy for making ethanol by fermentation. **[2]**

**(b)** State the atom economy of the hydration route and explain your answer. **[1]**

**8.** A producer wants 1.00 kg of ethanol from fermentation. The overall yield is 85.0%. Calculate the mass of glucose, in kg, that must be fermented. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the volume, in dm³, of carbon dioxide released when 45.0 g of glucose is fermented completely, measured at 298 K and 100 kPa. **[3]**

**10.** Give two advantages of making ethanol by hydration of ethene and two advantages of making it by fermentation. **[4]**

**11.** A company grows sugar cane for bioethanol on land cleared from rainforest. Explain why the ethanol may cause a net increase in carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, even though it is a biofuel. **[2]**

**12.** A government is deciding whether to require that petrol contains 10% bioethanol made by fermentation. Discuss the environmental and ethical issues it should consider. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Phosphoric acid (on a silica support) [1]; about 300 °C [1]; high pressure (about 60–70 atm) [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** **Pentan-2-ol** [1]

**(b)** **Pentan-1-ol** [1]

**(c)** Pentan-2-ol forms via a secondary carbocation and pentan-1-ol via a primary carbocation [1]; the secondary carbocation is more stable, so more of it forms [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Arrow from the C=C bond to H⁺ [1]; carbocation drawn on the ring with a + charge [1]; arrow from a lone pair on the O of H₂O to C⁺, and arrow from the O–H bond of the oxonium ion to O, releasing H⁺ [1]; product **cyclohexanol** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Both of the last two arrows are needed for the third mark; the H⁺ released shows the catalyst is regenerated.

**4. (a)** The tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃ (positive charge on the carbon bearing two methyl groups) [1]

**(b)** **2-methylbutan-2-ol** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** But-1-ene [1] and but-2-ene [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* But-2-ene is symmetrical and gives only butan-2-ol; but-1-ene gives it as the major product.

**6.** The suggestion is not sensible: although a higher temperature normally increases rate [1], at 70 °C the enzymes in yeast would be denatured and fermentation would stop (so a compromise of about 35 °C is used) [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Mass of desired product = 2 × 46.0 = 92.0; atom economy = 92.0 ÷ 180.0 × 100 [1] = **51.1%** [1]

**(b)** **100%**, because ethanol is the only product, so all reactant atoms end up in it [1]. **[3]**

**8.** n(ethanol) = 1000 ÷ 46.0 = 21.74 mol [1]; n(glucose) at 85.0% yield = 21.74 ÷ 2 ÷ 0.850 = 12.79 mol [1]; mass = 12.79 × 180.0 = 2302 g = **2.30 kg** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A yield below 100% means more starting material is needed, so divide by 0.850; multiplying gives 1.66 kg, which is too little.

**9.** n(glucose) = 45.0 ÷ 180.0 = 0.250 mol, so n(CO₂) = 0.500 mol [1]; V = nRT ÷ p = 0.500 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 1.00 × 10⁵ = 0.0124 m³ [1]; = **12.4 dm³** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Hydration, any two of: faster; continuous process; purer product; 100% atom economy [1] [1]. Fermentation, any two of: renewable raw material; lower temperature and pressure, so less energy for the reaction; simpler equipment [1] [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Clearing the forest releases the carbon stored in the trees and soil (by burning or decay) [1], and removes trees that would have kept absorbing CO₂, so the CO₂ released is more than the crop absorbs [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Indicative points; award one mark for each point made, up to a maximum of 6:

- Bioethanol is made from a renewable crop, so less finite crude oil is used
- By the photosynthesis, fermentation and combustion equations, CO₂ absorbed equals CO₂ released, so net emissions could fall
- Fossil fuels are still used for farm machinery, fertiliser manufacture, distillation and transport, so it is not truly carbon neutral
- Land may be cleared from forest, releasing stored carbon and destroying habitats
- Fertiliser run-off can pollute rivers, and the crops need large amounts of water
- Ethical: land used for fuel crops is not used for food, which may raise food prices for the poorest people
- A reasoned conclusion, for example that the policy helps most if crops are grown on land not needed for food and with low fossil-fuel inputs

Maximum **[6]**.

*Examiner insight:* A list of advantages only cannot reach the top of the range; include at least one environmental disadvantage and one ethical point.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Curly arrows starting from H⁺ or from a charge.
- Swapping the major and minor products.
- Treating a symmetrical alkene as giving two products.
- Ignoring enzyme denaturation when discussing temperature.
- Dividing and multiplying the wrong way when applying a yield.
- One-sided "discuss" answers.
