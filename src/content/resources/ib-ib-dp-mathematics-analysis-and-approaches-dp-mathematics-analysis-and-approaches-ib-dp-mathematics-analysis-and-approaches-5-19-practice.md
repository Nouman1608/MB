This set covers **syllabus section 5.19** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021): Maclaurin series for standard functions, new series by substitution, products, differentiation and integration, and series developed from differential equations. It is HL-only content. Questions are labelled "(calculator-free)" for Paper 1 style or "(calculator allowed)" for Paper 2 style.

Learn the methods first in the [section 5.19 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-19/) and the [section 5.19 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-5-19-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) Use the Maclaurin formula to show that cos x = 1 − x²/2 + x⁴/24 − … **[3]**

**2.** (calculator-free) Write down the first three terms of the Maclaurin series for e^(x/2). **[2]**

**3.** (calculator-free) Find the Maclaurin series for (1 + x)⁻² up to and including the term in x³, and state the values of x for which it is valid. **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Multiply the series for sin x and cos x to find the first three non-zero terms of the series for sin x cos x. **[3]**

**(b)** Use the identity sin x cos x = ½ sin 2x to check your answer. **[1]**

**5.** (calculator-free) Use the series for ln(1 + x) to find lim (x → 0) (ln(1 + x) − x)/x². **[2]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) Use the first three terms of the series for (1 + x)^(1/3) to estimate the cube root of 1.06, giving your answer to 4 decimal places. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator-free) Let f(x) = e^(sin x).

**(a)** Find f′(x) and f″(x), and show that f′(0) = f″(0) = 1. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence write down the Maclaurin series for f(x) up to and including the term in x². **[2]**

**(c)** By substituting the series for sin x into the series for eᵘ, show that the coefficient of x³ in the series for f(x) is zero. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator allowed)

**(a)** Write down the series for 1/(1 + x²) up to and including the term in x⁶. **[2]**

**(b)** By integrating your answer from 0 to x, find the series for arctan x up to and including the term in x⁷. **[3]**

**(c)** Use arctan(1/√3) = π/6 and your four terms to estimate π, giving your answer to 4 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator allowed)

**(a)** Find the first three non-zero terms of the series for (sin x)/x. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence estimate ∫₀^0.4 (sin x)/x dx, giving your answer to 5 significant figures. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why three terms give such an accurate estimate on this interval. **[1]**

**10.** (calculator-free) A function y = g(x) satisfies dy/dx = 1 + y², with y = 0 when x = 0.

**(a)** Find the values of y′(0), y″(0) and y‴(0). **[4]**

**(b)** Hence write down the Maclaurin series for y up to and including the term in x³. **[1]**

**(c)** Solve the differential equation exactly, and use your answer to (b) to write down the series for the function you obtain. **[3]**

**11.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** By writing cos x = 1 + u, where u = −x²/2 + x⁴/24 − …, show that ln(cos x) = −x²/2 − x⁴/12 − … **[4]**

**(b)** Hence, by differentiating, find the series for tan x up to and including the term in x³. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down lim (x → 0) ln(cos x)/x². **[1]**

**12.** (calculator allowed)

**(a)** By writing √(4 + x) = 2(1 + x/4)^(1/2), find the series for √(4 + x) up to and including the term in x³. **[3]**

**(b)** State the values of x for which the series is valid. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your series to estimate √4.2, giving your answer to 6 decimal places. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the series should not be used with x = 5 to estimate √9. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** f = cos x, f′ = −sin x, f″ = −cos x, f‴ = sin x, f⁗ = cos x [1]. Values at 0: 1, 0, −1, 0, 1 [1]. cos x = 1 + 0 − x²/2! + 0 + x⁴/4! − … = 1 − x²/2 + x⁴/24 − … [1]

**2.** 1 + x/2 + (x/2)²/2! [1] = **1 + x/2 + x²/8** [1]

**3.** p = −2: 1 + (−2)x + (−2)(−3)x²/2 + (−2)(−3)(−4)x³/6 [1] = **1 − 2x + 3x² − 4x³ + …** [1]. Valid for **|x| < 1** [1]

**4. (a)** (x − x³/6 + x⁵/120)(1 − x²/2 + x⁴/24) [1]. x³: −1/6 − 1/2 = −2/3 [1]. x⁵: 1/120 + 1/12 + 1/24 = 16/120 = 2/15, so **x − 2x³/3 + 2x⁵/15** [1]

**(b)** ½(2x − (2x)³/6 + (2x)⁵/120) = x − 2x³/3 + 2x⁵/15, which agrees [1]

**5.** (x − x²/2 + x³/3 − … − x)/x² = −1/2 + x/3 − … [1] → **−1/2** [1]

**6.** (1 + x)^(1/3) ≈ 1 + x/3 − x²/9 [1]. With x = 0.06: 1 + 0.02 − 0.0004 [1] = **1.0196** [1]

**7. (a)** f′(x) = cos x e^(sin x) [1]. f″(x) = (cos² x − sin x)e^(sin x) [1]. f′(0) = 1 × e⁰ = 1 and f″(0) = (1 − 0)e⁰ = 1 [1]

**(b)** f(0) = 1 [1], so f(x) = **1 + x + x²/2 + …** [1]

**(c)** eᵘ = 1 + u + u²/2 + u³/6 + … with u = x − x³/6 + … [1]. x³ terms: −x³/6 from u, none from u²/2 (its lowest power after x² is x⁴), and x³/6 from u³/6 [1]. Total −1/6 + 1/6 = 0 [1]

*Examiner insight:* Checking (b) against the composition in (c) is good practice: two independent routes that agree catch most slips.

**8. (a)** Substitute x² into (1 + x)⁻¹ = 1 − x + x² − x³ + … [1]: **1 − x² + x⁴ − x⁶ + …** [1]

**(b)** arctan x = ∫₀ˣ (1 − t² + t⁴ − t⁶) dt [1] = [t − t³/3 + t⁵/5 − t⁷/7]₀ˣ [1] = **x − x³/3 + x⁵/5 − x⁷/7 + …** [1]

**(c)** π ≈ 6(a − a³/3 + a⁵/5 − a⁷/7) with a = 1/√3 [1] = **3.138** (4 s.f.) [1]

**9. (a)** (x − x³/6 + x⁵/120)/x [1] = **1 − x²/6 + x⁴/120** [1]

**(b)** [x − x³/18 + x⁵/600]₀^0.4 [1] = 0.4 − 0.0035556 + 0.0000171 [1] = **0.39646** (5 s.f.) [1]

**(c)** x ≤ 0.4, so the omitted terms (from x⁶/5040 onwards) are extremely small [1]

**10. (a)** y′(0) = 1 + 0² = **1** [1]. y″ = 2y y′ [1], so y″(0) = **0**. y‴ = 2(y′)² + 2y y″ [1], so y‴(0) = **2** [1]

**(b)** y = 0 + x + 0 + 2x³/3! = **x + x³/3 + …** [1]

**(c)** ∫1/(1 + y²) dy = ∫dx, so arctan y = x + c [1]. y(0) = 0 gives c = 0, so y = tan x [1]. Hence **tan x = x + x³/3 + …** [1]

**11. (a)** ln(1 + u) = u − u²/2 + … [1]. u = −x²/2 + x⁴/24, and u² = x⁴/4 + (higher powers) [1]. ln(cos x) = −x²/2 + x⁴/24 − x⁴/8 + … [1] = −x²/2 − x⁴/12 − … [1]

**(b)** d/dx ln(cos x) = −tan x [1]. −tan x = −x − x³/3, so **tan x = x + x³/3 + …** [1]

**(c)** −1/2 + (terms in x²) → **−1/2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Questions 10 and 11 reach the same series for tan x by two different routes, a useful check that both are right.

**12. (a)** (1 + u)^(1/2) = 1 + u/2 − u²/8 + u³/16, with u = x/4 [1]. 2(1 + x/8 − x²/128 + x³/1024) [1] = **2 + x/4 − x²/64 + x³/512** [1]

**(b)** |x/4| < 1, so **|x| < 4** [1]

**(c)** x = 0.2: 2 + 0.05 − 0.000625 + 0.0000156 [1] = **2.049391** (6 d.p.) [1]

**(d)** x = 5 is outside |x| < 4, so the series does not converge to √9 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing (x/2)² as x²/2 in question 2, so the x² term becomes x²/4 instead of x²/8.
- Missing one of the three products that give the x⁵ term in question 4.
- Differentiating y² as 2y instead of 2y y′ in question 10.
- Dropping the u² term in question 11, which changes −x⁴/12 to +x⁴/24.
- Ignoring the interval of validity, as tested in question 12(d).
- Rounding intermediate terms in question 9 before adding them.
