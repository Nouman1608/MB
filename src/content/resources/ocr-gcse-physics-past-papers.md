This page is a guide to OCR GCSE (9-1) Physics A (Gateway Science, J249) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents. We could not retrieve the J249 examiner reports, so this page does not include examiner report notes; that is stated again in each series below. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's own site or its teacher platform.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR GCSE Physics A (Gateway Science) J249 qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/gateway-science-suite-physics-a-j249-from-2016/)
- [OCR past paper finder](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/past-paper-finder/)
- [OCR grade boundaries page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/)

OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder, and grade boundaries on the page above. Examiner reports for current series are held on Teach Cambridge, OCR's teacher site, so ask your teacher or exams officer if you cannot open them.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the examiner report for that paper: it tells you why answers that looked right still lost marks.

Check which specification a paper was set on. J249 is a linear qualification first assessed in 2018 and is still the current specification (Version 5.0, July 2026). There are two Foundation Tier papers (J249/01 and J249/02) and two Higher Tier papers (J249/03 and J249/04). Each is 90 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 50% of the qualification. Papers 1 and 3 assess topics P1 to P4 and practical skills (P9); Papers 2 and 4 assess topics P5 to P8, with earlier content assumed, and include synoptic questions. The papers below follow this structure; for any older paper, check the specification version before relying on a question.

## How to use this page

1. Pick a series and sit the two papers for your tier under timed conditions. Check the [exam calendar](/exam-calendar/) to plan how many papers you can fit in before your exams.
2. Mark them strictly with the official mark scheme, and look up any command word you misread on our [command words page](/command-words/).
3. Read the examiner report for each paper, if your school can give you access, and write down every mark you lost and why.
4. Fix weak topics with our revision notes, for example [current, charge and circuit conventions](/resources/ocr-gcse-physics-current-charge-and-circuit-conventions/), [deformation, springs and Hooke's law](/resources/ocr-gcse-physics-deformation-springs-and-hookes-law/), [atomic model, atomic size and density](/resources/ocr-gcse-physics-atomic-model-atomic-size-and-density/), [changes of state, specific heat capacity and latent heat](/resources/ocr-gcse-physics-changes-of-state-specific-heat-capacity-and-latent-heat/) and [dissipation, efficiency and reducing energy loss](/resources/ocr-gcse-physics-dissipation-efficiency-and-reducing-energy-loss/).
5. Compare your raw mark with the thresholds for that series only as a rough guide. Our [grade thresholds page](/grade-thresholds/) has more.
6. If you want a teacher to go through your papers with you, [book a free trial lesson](/trial/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR GCSE Physics A (J249) past papers?**
OCR publishes question papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder. Grade boundaries are on OCR's grade boundaries page, and examiner reports are on Teach Cambridge.

**Which J249 papers should I sit?**
Sit the two papers for your tier: Papers 1 and 2 for Foundation, or Papers 3 and 4 for Higher. Each is 90 marks and 1 hour 45 minutes.

**How are the grade thresholds used?**
The overall grade comes from adding the two paper marks of your tier, out of 180, and comparing the total with that series' boundaries. The paper rows show how each paper performed.

**Does this page include examiner report notes?**
No. We could not retrieve the J249 examiner reports, so we give none rather than guess. Ask your teacher for the reports on Teach Cambridge.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
