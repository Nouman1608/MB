## What the specification lists

- What is meant by a market, competition, market size and market growth.
- The importance of market size to a business.
- How a business could increase its market share (evaluate).
- Key features of monopoly, oligopoly and monopolistic competition.

## Core recall

### Market and competition

- Market: any arrangement where buyers and sellers exchange a good or service, physical or online.
- The definition (broad or narrow) changes the market's size and a firm's share; always state it.
- Competition: rivalry to win the same customers.
- Direct competitors: similar products, same customers. Indirect: different products, same need.
- Intensity depends on number and size of rivals, similarity of products, ease of switching, ease of entry.

### Formulas (standard forms)

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Market size by value | Total sales revenue in the market |
| Market size by volume | Total units sold in the market |
| Value | Volume × average price |
| Market growth (%) | (New size − old size) ÷ old size × 100 |
| Market share (%) | Firm's sales ÷ market sales × 100 |
| Sales needed for a target share | Target share × market size |
| Concentration ratio (not named in the specification) | Combined share of the largest few firms |

### Value versus volume

- Prices rising faster than units fall: value up, volume down.
- Volume matters for capacity planning; value for revenue potential.
- Inflation can make value growth overstate real expansion.

### Why market size matters

- Limits revenue potential.
- Determines whether large fixed costs can be spread.
- Large, growing markets attract entrants.
- Growing market: growth without taking customers from rivals. Static market: growth means taking share, inviting retaliation.

### Firm growth versus market growth

- Firm grows faster than the market: share rises.
- Firm grows slower than the market: share falls, even if sales rise.

### Raising market share

| Situation | Best approaches |
|---|---|
| Growing market | Distribution, capacity, promotion |
| Static market, similar products | Differentiation, service, loyalty |
| Fragmented market | Acquiring small rivals |
| Oligopoly | Branding, innovation; avoid price wars |

Evaluate on cost, speed, ease of copying and effect on profit.

### Market structures

| Feature | Monopoly | Oligopoly | Monopolistic competition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firms | One dominant | A few large | Many |
| Barriers | Very high | High | Low |
| Products | No close substitutes | Similar, branded | Differentiated |
| Interdependence | Little | High | Low |

### Decision making power

- Monopoly: most power over price and output, limited by willingness to pay and regulation.
- Oligopoly: power constrained by rivals' reactions; non-price competition common.
- Monopolistic competition: little power over price; compete on service, style, location.
- Power depends on how easily customers can switch and rivals can enter in the relevant local market.

## Quick worked reminder

Greystone Pet Foods (invented): sales £18m to £20.16m (+12%); market £120m to £138m (+15%); share 15.0% to 14.6%. Sales rose but share fell; keeping 15% needed £20.7m.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market | Buyers and sellers exchanging a good or service |
| Competition | Rivalry between businesses for the same customers |
| Market size | Total sales in a market over a period, by value or volume |
| Market growth | Percentage change in market size over time |
| Market share | A firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Barrier to entry | Anything that makes it hard for a new firm to enter a market |
| Interdependence | When each firm's decisions depend on rivals' likely reactions |
| Saturated market | A market where most potential buyers already buy |

## Exam traps

- State the market definition when quoting share.
- Say whether size or growth is by value or volume.
- Rising sales do not prove rising share.
- In an oligopoly, a price cut is likely to be matched.
- Link structure to a specific decision: price, output, product or promotion.

## Self-test

1. Define a market.
2. Distinguish direct and indirect competitors.
3. Market size rises from £80m to £86m. Calculate market growth.
4. A firm sells £5.2m in a £65m market. Calculate its market share.
5. Volume falls 4% while average price rises 8%. What happens to value, roughly?
6. Why might a business planning factory capacity prefer volume figures?
7. A firm's sales grow 6% while the market grows 9%. What happens to its share?
8. What sales are needed for a 20% share of a £45m market?
9. Give two reasons why market size matters to a business.
10. State two features of an oligopoly.
11. Why do oligopolists often avoid price competition?
12. The four largest firms sell £30m, £25m, £20m and £15m in a £120m market. Calculate the four-firm concentration ratio.

### Answers

1. Any arrangement, physical or online, where buyers and sellers exchange a good or service.
2. Direct competitors sell similar products to the same customers; indirect competitors sell different products that meet the same need.
3. (86 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = **7.5%**.
4. 5.2 ÷ 65 × 100 = **8%**.
5. It rises by about 3.7% (0.96 × 1.08 = 1.0368).
6. Because capacity depends on the number of units it must make, not their price.
7. It falls, because the firm is growing more slowly than the market.
8. 0.20 × 45 = **£9m**.
9. Any two: it limits revenue potential; it determines whether fixed costs can be spread; large markets attract entrants; growth affects how rivals react.
10. Any two: a few large firms dominate; high barriers to entry; branded, similar products; high interdependence.
11. Because rivals are likely to match a price cut, so each firm loses margin without gaining share.
12. (30 + 25 + 20 + 15) ÷ 120 × 100 = **75%**.
