Use the [recruitment and selection study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process/) and the [recruitment and selection revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business is invented, wage rates are invented, and amounts are in dollars. Show your workings.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define job description. **[1]**
**(b)** Define external recruitment. **[1]**

**2.** Give two types of employment. **[2]**

**3.** At Hollybank Surveyors, Mila and Jon each work two and a half days a week in the role of office manager. State the type of employment. **[1]**

**4.** A theme park hires five seasonal workers for 8 weeks. Each works 32 hours a week at 10.50 an hour. Calculate the total wage cost. **[2]**

**5.** A business spent 4,500 on job advertisements and 2,700 on staff time for interviews to fill six posts. Calculate the recruitment cost per post. **[2]**

**6.** Outline one benefit to a small business of using a person specification when shortlisting. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one effect of equal opportunities laws on a business's recruitment process. **[3]**

**8.** Harbour Sports is selecting a sales assistant. Weekend availability is essential. Applicants are scored from 0 to 5 on customer service and product knowledge.

| Applicant | Weekend availability | Customer service | Product knowledge |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| P | Yes | 4 | 3 |
| Q | No | 5 | 5 |
| R | Yes | 5 | 4 |
| S | Yes | 3 | 5 |

**(a)** Identify the applicant who should be rejected. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total score of each remaining applicant and identify the two to interview. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse one limitation of using interviews to select employees. **[4]**

**9.** Tidewell Café employs eight part-time staff, each working 18 hours a week at the legal minimum wage of 9.80 an hour. The minimum rises to 10.40.
**(a)** Calculate the extra cost per week. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra cost per year, assuming 52 weeks. **[1]**
**(c)** Analyse how Tidewell Café might respond to the higher cost. **[4]**

**10.** Norland Electronics plc needs a regional sales manager. Option A is to promote Dev, a member of the sales team for six years with the best sales record but no management experience. Option B is to recruit externally through an agency that charges 15% of the 48,000 salary. Justify which option Norland Electronics should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Treetop Toys is a toy shop that is very busy for six weeks before a major holiday and quiet for the rest of the year. Evaluate whether Treetop Toys should use seasonal staff, rather than more full-time staff, to cover the busy period. **[8]**

**12.** Explain one reason why a business might employ part-time workers. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A document describing a job: its title, duties, responsibilities, hours and who it reports to [1].
**(b)** Filling a vacancy with someone from outside the business [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: full-time [1], part-time [1], job share, casual, seasonal, temporary. **[2]**

**3.** Job share [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 5 × 8 × 32 = 1,280 hours [1]; 1,280 × 10.50 = **13,440** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 4,500 + 2,700 = 7,200 [1]; 7,200 ÷ 6 = **1,200** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Each application can be checked against the same essential criteria [1], so the owner quickly removes unsuitable applicants and spends interview time only on those who could do the job [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Businesses must not treat applicants less favourably because of, for example, age or gender [1]. Adverts and person specifications must state only real job requirements [1], and interviewers may need training and records of decisions, which adds time and cost but widens the pool of applicants [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Q, who is not available at weekends, an essential criterion [1].
**(b)** P = 7, R = 9, S = 8; interview R and S [1] [1].
**(c)** Interviews show how a candidate presents in a short conversation, not how they work [1]. A confident candidate may impress more than one who is better at the job [1], so Harbour Sports could appoint someone who sells poorly [1], leading to the cost of recruiting again; a practical task, such as serving a mock customer, would help [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** 8 × 18 = 144 hours; 144 × 0.60 [1] = **86.40** [1].
**(b)** 86.40 × 52 = **4,492.80** [1].
**(c)** Tidewell must pay the new minimum, so it cannot keep the old rate [1]. It could raise prices slightly; spread across many customers, a small rise covers the cost [1], but regulars may notice and some may go elsewhere [1]. Or it could accept lower profit, or cut staff hours in quiet periods, though that may slow service at busy times [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Option A is the better choice [1]. Dev knows Norland's products and customers, so he can start quickly [1], and promoting him shows other staff that good work leads to promotion, which motivates them [1]. It also avoids the agency fee of 15% × 48,000 = 7,200 [1]. His lack of management experience is a risk [1], so Norland should give him management training; Option B would be better only if no internal candidate meets the essential criteria for managing a team [1]. **[6]**

**11.** For seasonal staff: Treetop pays them only for the six busy weeks [1], whereas full-time staff would be paid through the quiet months with little to do [1], so wage costs are much lower [1]. Against: seasonal staff need training each year and may know little about the toys [1], so service may be weaker at the time when most sales are made [1]; they may also be less committed [1]. Judgement: for example, use seasonal staff, because the busy period is short and predictable, but rehire the same people each year where possible to cut training and keep standards up [2]. **[8]**

**12.** Part-time workers can be employed just for the hours when the business is busy [1], such as lunchtimes in a café [1], so it pays only for the cover it needs and wage costs are lower than employing full-time staff for the whole day [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the job description and the person specification.
- Forgetting that an essential criterion rules an applicant out whatever their other scores.
- Suggesting a business can pay below the minimum wage.
- One-sided answers on internal or external recruitment.
- Ending "evaluate" or "justify" answers without a decision.
