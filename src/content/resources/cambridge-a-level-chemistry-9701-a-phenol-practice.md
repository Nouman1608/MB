## Section A

**1.** State the temperature at which benzenediazonium chloride is made, and the temperature condition for converting it into phenol. **[2]**

**2.** Name the gas given off when phenol reacts with sodium. **[1]**

**3.** State what is observed when bromine water is added to aqueous phenol. **[2]**

**4.** Put water, phenol and ethanol in order of increasing acidity. **[1]**

**5.** State the positions on the ring to which the –OH group of phenol directs incoming electrophiles. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Phenol can be made from phenylamine in two steps.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of nitrous acid from sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for step 2, the conversion of benzenediazonium chloride into phenol. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of gas, at r.t.p., released when 0.0150 mol of benzenediazonium chloride is converted into phenol. **[1]**

**7.** Explain the relative acidities of water, phenol and ethanol. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why phenol reacts with bromine at room temperature without a halogen carrier, whereas benzene needs AlBr₃. **[3]**

**9.** 4-methylphenol, CH₃C₆H₄OH, is a phenolic compound.

**(a)** Predict the product of its reaction with excess bromine water, and write a balanced equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict the product(s) of its reaction with dilute nitric acid at room temperature. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation for its reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**10.** 2.35 g of phenol reacts with excess bromine water.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of phenol. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of 2,4,6-tribromophenol formed. **[2]**

**11.** You are given three unlabelled colourless liquids: phenol (as an aqueous solution), ethanol and ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Describe a test that identifies ethanoic acid, with the result. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe a second test that then distinguishes phenol from ethanol, with the results. **[2]**

**12.** 2-naphthol is a phenolic compound with its –OH on a naphthalene ring.

**(a)** State and explain whether 2-naphthol would react with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[2]**

**(b)** 2-naphthol is added to cold benzenediazonium chloride in alkaline solution. State the type of product formed and one property that makes it useful. **[2]**

**13.** Compound K, C₈H₁₀O, contains a benzene ring with an –OH group and two methyl groups. One mole of K reacts with exactly one mole of Br₂ in bromine water. Suggest a structure for K, explaining your reasoning. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Below 10 °C [1]; warm (with water) [1].

**2.** Hydrogen [1].

**3.** The orange bromine water is decolourised [1]; a white precipitate forms [1].

**4.** Ethanol < water < phenol [1]. Phenol is the strongest because its anion is stabilised by delocalisation; ethanol is the weakest because its ethyl group destabilises the ethoxide ion.

**5.** 2-, 4- and 6-positions [1].

**6. (a)** NaNO₂ + HCl → HNO₂ + NaCl [1].

**(b)** C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + HCl [1].

**(c)** n(N₂) = 0.0150 mol; volume = 0.0150 × 24.0 = 0.360 dm³ [1].

**7.** Acid strength depends on the stability of the anion formed when H⁺ is lost [1]. In the phenoxide ion, a lone pair on O overlaps with the ring's π system, so the negative charge is delocalised and the ion is stabilised: phenol is the most acidic [1]. In OH⁻ the charge is localised on O, so water is less acidic than phenol [1]. In ethoxide the electron-donating ethyl group increases the charge density on O, destabilising the ion, so ethanol is the least acidic [1].

**8.** A lone pair on the O of –OH is partly delocalised into the ring [1], increasing the electron density of the ring (especially at the 2-, 4- and 6-positions) [1], so the ring can polarise Br₂ and attract the electrophile without a halogen carrier; benzene's ring is less electron-rich, so AlBr₃ is needed to generate a stronger electrophile [1].

**9. (a)** 2,6-dibromo-4-methylphenol [1]; CH₃C₆H₄OH + 2Br₂ → CH₃C₆H₂Br₂OH + 2HBr [1].

**(b)** 4-methyl-2-nitrophenol (the 4-position is occupied, so nitration occurs at position 2, equivalent to 6) [1].

**(c)** CH₃C₆H₄OH + NaOH → CH₃C₆H₄ONa + H₂O [1].

**10. (a)** Mr(C₆H₅OH) = 94.0; n = 2.35 / 94.0 = 0.0250 mol [1].

**(b)** Mr(C₆H₂Br₃OH) = 330.7 [1]; mass = 0.0250 × 330.7 = 8.27 g [1].

**11. (a)** Add sodium carbonate (or sodium hydrogencarbonate) solution [1]; ethanoic acid gives effervescence of CO₂, the other two do not [1].

**(b)** Add bromine water [1]; phenol decolourises it and gives a white precipitate; ethanol does not react (the orange colour remains) [1].

**12. (a)** Yes [1]: like phenol, its –OH is bonded directly to an aromatic ring, so the anion formed is stabilised by delocalisation into the ring and it is acidic enough to react with NaOH [1].

**(b)** An azo compound (azo dye) [1]; it is intensely coloured, so it can be used as a dye [1].

**13.** One mole of Br₂ means only one of the 2-, 4- and 6-positions is free [1], so the two methyl groups must occupy two of these positions [1]. K could be 2,4-dimethylphenol (Br enters at 6) or 2,6-dimethylphenol (Br enters at 4) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving benzene's conditions (concentrated acids or AlBr₃) for phenol.
- Writing HBr instead of 3HBr, or forgetting occupied ring positions when balancing.
- Explaining acidity by O–H bond strength instead of anion stability.
- Saying the ethyl group stabilises ethoxide.
- Claiming phenol fizzes with carbonates.
- Copying phenol's locants for naphthol without looking at the ring structure.
