All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Lantern Coffee.

> **Case study: Lantern Coffee**
>
> Lantern Coffee began as one coffee stall and now has eight shops in
> the capital city. Its sales last year were $1.8 million in a coffee
> market worth $15 million. The owner, Zahra, has set an objective of
> 15% market share within two years. She also wants to sell only
> fair-trade coffee, even though it costs more, and 360 of the 450
> customers surveyed said they were satisfied with the service. A
> competitor has just opened four shops nearby, and some customers
> have been leaving. The government offers new businesses in the region
> a grant towards equipment, but Lantern is not a new business.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of a social objective? **[1]**
- A. Increasing the share price
- B. Buying only fair-trade supplies
- C. Cutting staff wages
- D. Raising prices

**2.** Which **one** of the following describes market share? **[1]**
- A. The profit the business makes in a year
- B. The total number of shops
- C. The percentage of total market sales held by the business
- D. The value of the owner's shares

**3.** Define the term "objective". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why a government might encourage new businesses. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a start-up business might set survival as its objective. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate Lantern Coffee's current market share. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the sales Lantern needs to reach its 15% objective if the market stays at $15 million. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the percentage of surveyed customers who said they were satisfied. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** reasons why Lantern's objectives are different from those of the stall it began as. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** way in which Zahra's fair-trade objective might benefit Lantern Coffee and **one** way in which it might not. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse how the new competitor might change Lantern's objectives. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether customer satisfaction or market share is the better measure of success for Lantern Coffee. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A specific target [1] that a business sets to help it achieve its aim [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: new businesses create jobs [1]; they bring in tax revenue [1]; they provide new products for consumers [1]; they strengthen the economy [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A new business has few customers and little cash [1], so its first priority is to cover its costs and stay open long enough to build up a customer base [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Market share = 1.8 / 15 x 100 [1] = **12%** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 15% x $15 million [1] = **$2.25 million** [1], an increase of $0.45 million on current sales. **[2]**

**8.** 360 / 450 x 100 [1] = **80%** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** **Size:** the stall was a single seller, so its objective was probably survival [1], whereas eight shops can aim for a share of the market [1].
**Stage of development:** Lantern now has the sales and staff to aim for growth and market share [1], and it can afford ethical objectives such as fair-trade supplies [1]. **[4]**

**10.** **Benefit:** customers who care about ethical coffee may choose Lantern over rivals [1], improving its image and loyalty [1].
**Drawback:** fair-trade coffee costs more [1], so either profit falls or prices rise, which may lose price-sensitive customers [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **More competition:** four new rival shops take customers, so market share may fall below 12% [1] and the 15% target becomes harder to reach [1].
**Possible changes:** Zahra may put more weight on customer satisfaction to keep customers [1], for example by improving service or loyalty offers [1]. She may delay opening new shops until she knows her share is secure, making survival or defending market share more important than growth [1]. She might also accept lower profit in the short term to match rival prices [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Customer satisfaction:** 80% of surveyed customers are satisfied [1], which suggests that most customers will return and recommend Lantern [1]. It also protects Lantern against the new competitor [1].
**Market share:** it shows how well Lantern is doing compared with rivals [1]; at 12%, there is room to grow to 15% [1]; and a higher share gives bargaining power with suppliers [1].
**Judgement:** the two are linked, because satisfied customers return and help the share to grow [1]. For Lantern, customer satisfaction is probably the better measure at the moment, because a competitor is attracting customers and satisfaction shows whether Lantern can keep them [1]. Market share remains useful for checking progress towards the 15% target [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "profit" as the answer to every question about objectives.
- Using the wrong formula for market share, such as dividing by profit.
- Forgetting to give the percentage sign or the units.
- Describing an objective without relating it to Lantern's size, competition or ethics.
- Judging customer satisfaction against market share without a clear conclusion.
