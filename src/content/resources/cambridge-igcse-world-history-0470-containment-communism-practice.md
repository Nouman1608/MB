These questions cover key question 5 of Core Content Option B: "How effectively did the United States contain the
spread of communism?"

## Section A

**1.** State **two** ways in which NSC-68 changed American containment policy. **[2]**

**2.** Give **three** reasons why the UN was able to act quickly over Korea in June 1950. **[3]**

**3.** Identify **three** effects of Chinese entry into the Korean War. **[3]**

**4.** Give **two** American actions against Cuba before the Bay of Pigs. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **three** options considered by Kennedy's advisers in October 1962, other than the blockade. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** reasons why the USA supported Ngo Dinh Diem. **[2]**

**7.** Give **three** features of Viet Cong tactics. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the Bay of Pigs invasion. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the events of the Tet Offensive and its effects in the USA. **[4]**

**10.** Why did the United States become involved in Korea in 1950? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Why did the USA withdraw from Vietnam? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** "American policy in Korea was more successful than American policy in Vietnam." How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

**13.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter written by an American student to a newspaper, November 1962. "Last month the
President stared down Khrushchev and the missiles went home. Yet Castro still makes his speeches in Havana, ninety
miles from Florida, and nobody in Washington talks of removing him now. We have won a battle and accepted a
neighbour."

What does Source A suggest about the results of the Cuban Missile Crisis for containment? Use the source and your
knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: containment was to be applied worldwide, not only in Europe (1); it called for a massive
increase in American military spending and forces (1); it assumed the USSR aimed at world domination, so military as
well as economic resistance was needed (1); it was put into practice after the Korean War began, so containment now
meant willingness to fight (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: the USSR was boycotting the Security Council, so it could not use its veto (1); the
USA pressed for action at once (1); the invasion was a clear breach of the 38th parallel border (1); UN observers
were already present in Korea (1); many member states saw a parallel with the aggression of the 1930s (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: UN forces were driven back from the Yalu River (1); Seoul fell to communist forces
again in January 1951 (1); hopes of uniting Korea were abandoned (1); the war became a stalemate near the 38th
parallel (1); MacArthur's demands to attack China led to his dismissal (1); China and the USA became long-term enemies
(1). **[3]**

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: cutting imports of Cuban sugar (1); a ban on most American exports to Cuba (1); breaking
off diplomatic relations in January 1961 (1); the CIA training Cuban exiles for an invasion (1). **[2]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: do nothing (1); diplomatic pressure alone, for example through the UN (1); air strikes
on the missile sites (1); a full-scale invasion of Cuba (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: he was strongly anti-communist (1); the USA feared Ho Chi Minh would win national
elections (1); the domino theory made the defence of South Vietnam seem essential (1); there seemed to be no other
leader able to hold the South together (1). **[2]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: guerrilla hit-and-run attacks (1); avoiding pitched battles with US forces (1);
living among and gaining support from villagers (1); tunnels for shelter and movement (1); booby traps and mines (1);
supply along the Ho Chi Minh Trail (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: it took place in April 1961 (1); about 1,400 Cuban exiles trained by the CIA landed at
the Bay of Pigs (1); the plan had been prepared under Eisenhower and approved by Kennedy (1); Kennedy refused to
provide open American air support (1); there was no popular rising against Castro (1); the invaders were defeated
and captured within days (1); it humiliated Kennedy and pushed Castro closer to the USSR (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: it began at the end of January 1968, during the Vietnamese new year holiday (1); the Viet
Cong and North Vietnamese attacked over 100 towns and cities across South Vietnam (1); they reached the US embassy
compound in Saigon (1); Hue was held for weeks (1); the attacks were driven back with heavy communist losses (1);
American viewers saw the fighting on television and doubted official claims of progress (1); Johnson announced in
March 1968 that he would not seek re-election (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **containment**, because Truman believed the invasion was a Soviet-backed test of the policy set out in the
Truman Doctrine, and that failure to act would invite aggression elsewhere; **the lesson of appeasement**, because
American leaders believed that failing to stop aggression in the 1930s had led to war; **the fall of China in 1949**,
because Truman had been accused of "losing" China and could not afford another communist victory in Asia; **NSC-68**,
because it had just argued for resisting communism everywhere; **the defence of Japan**, because a communist Korea
would threaten America's key ally in the region. **[6]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content: **military failure**, because attrition and bombing did not
defeat a guerrilla enemy supplied along the Ho Chi Minh Trail, and Tet showed the war was not being won; **opposition
at home**, because rising casualties, the draft, television coverage and protests such as those at Kent State made
the war politically impossible to continue; **cost**, because the war drained money from programmes at home;
**Nixon's policy**, because Vietnamisation and détente with China and the USSR reduced the importance of holding South
Vietnam; **weakness of the South Vietnamese government**, which never won the support of its own people. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** in Korea the South survived as a non-communist state, while all of Vietnam fell in 1975; Korea was fought
with UN backing and many allies, Vietnam with few; Truman kept the war limited by dismissing MacArthur; the Korean war
lasted three years, the American war in Vietnam far longer. **Disagree:** Korea also failed in the aim of uniting the
country after October 1950 and ended in stalemate; it brought China into the war and cost many lives; Vietnam's wider
dominoes such as Thailand and Malaysia did not fall; both wars showed the limits of American power. **Judgement:**
for example, Korea was more successful because the original containment aim was met, while in Vietnam it was not met
at all. **[10]**

**13.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains what the source suggests, supported by its details and by contextual
knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains what the source suggests using its details only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the
source's content or gives knowledge without using the source. Indicative content: the source suggests the crisis was
**a success in removing the missiles**, because Kennedy "stared down Khrushchev and the missiles went home"; but also
**a failure to remove communism from Cuba**, because Castro "still makes his speeches in Havana"; it suggests the USA
had **accepted** a communist Cuba ("accepted a neighbour"). Knowledge: Khrushchev agreed on 28 October to remove the
missiles; in return Kennedy promised publicly not to invade Cuba, which is why Washington no longer talked of removing
Castro; the secret agreement to remove missiles from Turkey shows the result was a compromise. **[6]**
