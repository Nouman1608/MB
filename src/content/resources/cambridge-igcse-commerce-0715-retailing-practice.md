## Section A - short questions

**1.** A trader sells fruit from a cart, moving from street to street each day. This is: **[1]**
A a street market   B an itinerant retailer   C a speciality shop   D a vending machine

**2.** A very large out-of-town store selling food, clothing and electrical goods, with a big free car park, is a: **[1]**
A department store   B hypermarket   C independent retailer   D multiple chain store

**3.** A retailer sells phone cases on its website but holds no stock; the manufacturer sends each order straight to the customer. This is: **[1]**
A click and collect   B subscription commerce   C dropshipping   D cash on delivery

**4.** Which selling technique is most often used by small independent shops to keep regular customers? **[1]**
A informal credit   B VR showrooms   C barcoding   D own brands

**5.** Define an own brand. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** features of a department store. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** Explain the difference between omnichannel retailing and multi-channel retailing. **[4]**

**8.** *Mama Joy's is an independent shop in a village. A supermarket chain has opened a branch 5 km away.*
Explain **three** reasons why Mama Joy's may survive. **[6]**

**9.** *TechZone sells laptops and televisions in its chain of stores.*
(a) Explain **two** selling techniques TechZone could use to increase sales. (4 marks)
(b) Explain why after-sales service is especially important to TechZone. (2 marks) **[6]**

**10.** *FreshDaily, a supermarket, is introducing EPoS tills, self-service checkouts and a smartphone app.*
Explain how each of these **three** digital trends could benefit FreshDaily. **[6]**

**11.** Compare a speciality shop with a supermarket, giving **two** differences. **[4]**

**12.** *Style Lane is a clothing retailer with five shops. It is considering selling online as well.*
Discuss the likely effects on Style Lane of starting to sell through ecommerce. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. No fixed premises; the trader goes to the customers. **[1]**

**2.** B. Out of town, food and non-food, large car park. **[1]**

**3.** C. The supplier ships directly; the retailer holds no stock. **[1]**

**4.** A. Trusted customers pay later without a formal agreement. **[1]**

**5.** A product sold under the retailer's own name [1], usually made for it by a manufacturer and sold more cheaply than national brands [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: many departments under one roof [1]; each department run like a separate shop [1]; usually in a city centre [1]; high level of customer service [1]; wide range of goods [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Omnichannel retailing joins all the channels (shop, website, app, social media) together [1], sharing stock, prices and customer accounts so the customer can move between them, such as ordering online and returning in store [1]. Multi-channel retailing means selling through several channels [1], but they may operate separately, so prices, stock or accounts may differ between the shop and the website [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each (reason plus explanation in context):
Personal service: the owner knows villagers by name and can advise or order special items [2].
Flexible opening hours: Mama Joy's can open early and late, when a trip of 5 km to the supermarket is inconvenient [2].
Additional services: delivery to elderly customers, selling mobile phone credit or bill payments [2].
Informal credit: trusted customers can pay at the end of the week [2].
Convenience: villagers without cars can walk to the shop for small purchases [2]. **[max 6]**

**9.** (a) Any two, 2 marks each: loyalty programme, where points on purchases encourage customers to return for accessories and upgrades [2]; direct mail or email offers sent to past customers when new models arrive [2]; branding, as a trusted name reassures customers buying expensive goods [2]; packaging and in-store displays that show product features clearly [2]. (max 4 marks)
(b) Laptops and televisions are expensive and technical [1], so customers buy with more confidence if TechZone offers delivery, installation, repairs or a guarantee [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**10.** 2 marks for each trend, 1 for the benefit and 1 for its development:
EPoS: every sale is recorded and stock updated automatically [1], so FreshDaily knows when to reorder and avoids empty shelves [1].
Self-service checkouts: customers scan and pay themselves [1], so queues are shorter and fewer cashiers are needed, cutting costs [1].
Smartphone app: customers browse, order and collect loyalty points [1], so FreshDaily can send targeted offers and encourage repeat visits [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Any two differences, 2 marks each: a speciality shop sells a deep range of one type of product, while a supermarket sells a wide range of mainly food and household goods [2]; a speciality shop offers expert advice from staff, while a supermarket relies on self service [2]; a speciality shop is usually smaller with higher prices, while a supermarket buys in bulk and charges lower prices [2]. **[max 4]**

**12.** Up to 2 for positive effects, up to 2 for negative effects, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Positive: Style Lane can sell 24/7 [1]; it reaches more potential customers beyond its five towns [1]; revenue may rise [1]; customer data allows targeted marketing [1]; in time it may need fewer or smaller shops, reducing store costs [1].
Negative: delivery costs and frequent clothing returns [1]; staff need training in online systems [1]; more warehousing space is needed for online stock [1]; the website must be maintained [1].
Conclusion: for example, worth doing because clothing sells well online, provided Style Lane uses its shops for click and collect and returns, which cuts delivery costs [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a hypermarket as "a large supermarket" with no further features.
- Writing that own brands are made by the retailer.
- Explaining omnichannel as simply "selling on many channels".
- Listing digital trends without saying how each benefits the named business.
- In "discuss" answers, ignoring the costs of ecommerce.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many types of retailer does the 0715 syllabus list?** Ten: itinerant retailers, vending machines, independent retailers, street markets, speciality shops, online retailers, multiple chain stores, supermarkets, hypermarkets and department stores.

**What is the difference between omnichannel and multi-channel retailing?** Omnichannel joins all channels into one experience with shared stock and accounts; multi-channel uses several channels that may run separately.
