## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Covalent bond | Two atoms held together by a common electron pair |
| Monomer / polymer | A small unit / a long chain of linked units |
| Condensation | Joining two molecules with the release of water |
| Hydrolysis | Breaking a bond by splitting water into -H and -OH |
| Monosaccharide | A single sugar unit, a pentose (5 C) or hexose (6 C) |
| Lipid | Dissolves in non-polar solvents, only sparingly in water |
| Amphipathic | Having both a hydrophilic and a hydrophobic region |
| Glycoprotein | A protein with attached carbohydrate chains |

## B1.1.1 Carbon

- Four covalent bonds per carbon atom: single bonds, or single plus double bonds.
- Partners: C, H, O, N, P, S.
- Skeletons: unbranched chain, branched chain, single ring, multiple rings.
- SI prefixes: kilo 10³, centi 10⁻², milli 10⁻³, micro 10⁻⁶, nano 10⁻⁹.

**Method: convert units**
1. Write the prefix as a power of ten.
2. Multiply or divide by the factor between the units.
3. Check the size makes sense (a bilayer of 7 nm is 0.007 µm, not 7000 µm).

## B1.1.2 and B1.1.3 Condensation and hydrolysis

| Polymer | Monomer | Bond |
|---|---|---|
| Polysaccharide | monosaccharide | glycosidic |
| Polypeptide | amino acid | peptide |
| Nucleic acid | nucleotide | phosphodiester |

- An unbranched chain with n monomers contains n − 1 bonds: that many waters are released on building and used on breaking.
- Worked reminder: three glucose units joined in a line release 2 waters: 3 × 180.16 − 2 × 18.02 = 504.44.
- Digestion is hydrolysis: large polymers become monomers small enough to absorb.

## B1.1.4 Monosaccharides

- Count all carbons, including the one outside the ring.
- Pentose examples: ribose, deoxyribose. Hexose examples: glucose, fructose, galactose.
- Glucose: soluble (many -OH groups), transportable, chemically stable, yields energy on oxidation.

## B1.1.5 and B1.1.6 Polysaccharides

| Feature | Starch | Glycogen | Cellulose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Found in | plants | animals | plant cell walls |
| Monomer | alpha-glucose | alpha-glucose | beta-glucose |
| Shape | coiled; amylopectin branched | coiled and highly branched | straight chains |
| Function | energy store | energy store, quickly mobilised | structural strength |

**Why storage polysaccharides suit storage**
- Coiling and branching make them compact.
- Large molecules are relatively insoluble, so no osmotic effect.
- Units added or removed easily by condensation and hydrolysis.
- Branching gives many ends, so many units are released at once.

**Why cellulose is strong**
- Beta-glucose units alternate in orientation, giving straight chains.
- Chains lie in bundles and are cross-linked by hydrogen bonds.
- Many weak bonds add up to high tensile strength.

## B1.1.7 Glycoproteins

- Carbohydrate chains face outwards from the plasma membrane and act as recognition markers.
- ABO: the antigen on red blood cells depends on the terminal sugar; group O cells lack A and B antigens.
- A person has antibodies against ABO antigens they do not carry.

## B1.1.8 to B1.1.11 Lipids and fats

| Compound | Built from | Water released |
|---|---|---|
| Triglyceride | glycerol + 3 fatty acids | 3 |
| Phospholipid | glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate | 3 |

| Fatty acid type | C=C bonds | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Saturated | none | straight, high melting point |
| Monounsaturated | one | bent, lower melting point |
| Polyunsaturated | two or more | more bends, lowest melting point |

- Plants: storage oils, rich in unsaturated fatty acids, liquid.
- Endotherms: storage fats richer in saturated fatty acids, firmer at body temperature.
- Energy density (data given in questions): lipid about 37 kJ per gram, carbohydrate about 17 kJ per gram, ratio 2.18.
- Fat = long-term store. Glycogen = short-term store.
- Insulation: poor heat conductor; matters most for endotherms in cold habitats, such as marine mammals in cold water.

**Method: compare two stores**
1. Divide the energy required by the energy per gram of each store.
2. Compare masses (or divide the larger by the smaller).
3. Add the biological reason: insolubility, no osmotic effect, compact.

## B1.1.12 and B1.1.13 Bilayers and steroids

- Heads hydrophilic (face water), tails hydrophobic (face inwards): the bilayer forms spontaneously.
- Core blocks ions and large polar molecules.
- Steroids have four fused rings (three of six carbons, one of five) and are non-polar.
- Oestradiol and testosterone cross the bilayer directly.

## Worked reminders

**Magnification.** Actual size = image size ÷ magnification. A bilayer measured as 21 mm on an image at ×3 000 000 is 21 mm ÷ 3 000 000 = 7 × 10⁻⁶ mm = 7 nm.

**Mass of a cellulose chain.** Each glucose unit in a chain contributes C6H10O5, 162.14, because one water is lost per bond. A chain of 5000 units has a mass of about 811 000 (five thousand times 162.14, plus one water).

**Fat reserve.** A 70 kg person with 18% body fat holds 70 × 0.18 = 12.6 kg of fat. At 37 kJ per gram this is 12 600 × 37 = 466 200 kJ. At a use of 10 000 kJ per day it would last 46.6 days if it were the only fuel. A glycogen store of 400 g holds 400 × 17 = 6800 kJ, under one day of that use. This is the quantitative reason fat is the long-term store.

**Melting point trend.** Stearic acid (0 C=C) melts at about 69 °C and oleic acid (1 C=C) at about 13 °C: a fall of 56 °C for one double bond. State the trend first, then explain it with bent chains and weaker packing.

## Command words in this topic

- **State / Outline:** a short answer or a brief account, with no explanation needed.
- **Distinguish:** give differences between two items, side by side, not separate descriptions.
- **Explain:** give a cause as well as a fact, for example "relatively insoluble because the molecule is large, so no osmotic effect".
- **Compare:** give similarities and differences, such as starch and glycogen being alpha-glucose polymers but glycogen being more highly branched.
- **Calculate:** show the working and the unit.

## Exam habits for B1.1

- Name the bond (glycosidic, peptide, phosphodiester, ester) when asked about a condensation reaction.
- When a question gives a table, quote paired values to support a trend.
- Link every structural feature to a function in the same sentence.
- Write the unit after each calculation and show the rearranged formula first.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Condensation vs hydrolysis:** water released vs water used.
- **Alpha vs beta glucose:** -OH on carbon 1 down vs up; coiled stores vs straight fibres.
- **Starch vs glycogen:** plants, less branched vs animals, more branched.
- **Saturated vs unsaturated:** fully single-bonded chain vs chain with a double bond or more.
- **Triglyceride vs phospholipid:** three fatty acids vs two plus phosphate.
- **Hydrophilic vs hydrophobic:** attracted to water vs repelled from it.

## Quick self-test

1. State how many covalent bonds a carbon atom can form.
2. Convert 2.5 mm to micrometres.
3. A polypeptide chain contains 120 amino acids in one chain. How many peptide bonds does it have?
4. State what is added to each end of a broken bond in hydrolysis.
5. Distinguish a pentose from a hexose.
6. State two reasons why glycogen is a good short-term energy store.
7. Explain why cellulose chains form strong fibres.
8. Name the carbohydrate-protein molecules that carry ABO antigens.
9. Calculate the mass of lipid that stores 5000 kJ, using 37 kJ per gram. Give the answer to 3 significant figures.
10. Explain why a fatty acid with two C=C bonds melts at a lower temperature than a saturated one.
11. Define amphipathic.
12. Explain why testosterone crosses a membrane without a channel.

### Answers

1. Four.
2. 2.5 mm = 2500 µm.
3. 119.
4. -H on one end and -OH on the other, from a split water molecule.
5. Pentose has five carbons; hexose has six.
6. Compact because of coiling and branching; relatively insoluble so no osmotic effect; many ends allow fast release (any two).
7. Straight chains of beta-glucose lie in bundles, cross-linked by many hydrogen bonds.
8. Glycoproteins.
9. 5000 ÷ 37 = 135 g.
10. The C=C bonds bend the chain, so molecules pack less closely and need less energy to separate.
11. It has both a hydrophilic and a hydrophobic region.
12. It is a non-polar steroid and dissolves in the hydrophobic core of the bilayer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying water is "added" in condensation, or not stating that hydrolysis splits it.
- Using "dissolves" for starch without saying why large size makes it relatively insoluble.
- Claiming cellulose is "a different shape" without naming alternating beta-glucose orientation and hydrogen bonds.
- Leaving out units, or a prefix error such as nm to µm by a factor of 100.
- Describing phospholipid heads as hydrophobic.
- Stating "unsaturated" without referring to C=C bonds.
- Giving the ratio of energy density without the biological consequence.
- Treating ABO antibodies as made against the person's own antigens.
