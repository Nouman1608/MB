These questions cover section 9.4, Statistical charts and diagrams, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. All data is invented. Where you would normally draw a chart, the question asks for the values or features you would use. The methods are in the [statistical charts and diagrams study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-statistical-charts-and-diagrams/), and the formulas are in the [statistical charts and diagrams revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-statistical-charts-and-diagrams-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** 90 people chose their favourite season: spring 27, summer 36, autumn 18, winter 9. Calculate the pie chart angle for each season. **[3]**

**2.** In a pie chart, a sector of 150° represents 45 people.

**(a)** How many people does the whole pie chart represent?

**(b)** How many people does a 40° sector represent? **[3]**

**3.** In a pictogram, one symbol represents 6 parcels.

**(a)** How many parcels are shown by 3½ symbols?

**(b)** How many parcels are shown by 1½ symbols?

**(c)** How many symbols would show 24 parcels? **[3]**

**4.** **(a)** What percentage of a pie chart is a 54° sector?

**(b)** What angle represents 35% of the total? **[2]**

**5.** In a composite bar chart, one bar has its section boundaries at 12, 27 and 35 on the vertical scale. Find the size of each of the three sections. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A class records favourite fruits.

| | Apple | Banana | Mango |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boys | 12 | 7 | 15 |
| Girls | 9 | 11 | 14 |

**(a)** Describe how to draw a dual bar chart of this data, including what the chart must show.

**(b)** For which fruit is the difference between boys and girls greatest?

**(c)** How many girls are in the class? **[4]**

**7.** A school's medals over two years are:

| | Gold | Silver | Bronze |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 4 | 7 | 9 |
| Year 2 | 6 | 5 | 10 |

**(a)** A composite bar chart stacks gold, then silver, then bronze. State the readings on the vertical scale at the top of each section for both years.

**(b)** Use the chart to state which year had more medals in total. **[4]**

**8.** A household's monthly spending is shown as percentages: rent 35%, food 25%, transport 15%, other 25%.

**(a)** Calculate the angle for each sector of a pie chart.

**(b)** The household spends 450 dollars a month on food. Find the total monthly spending and the amount spent on rent. **[5]**

**9.** Two pie charts of the same size show survey answers. In Country X, 2000 people were asked and the "yes" sector is 108°. In Country Y, 5000 people were asked and the "yes" sector is 72°.

**(a)** Find the number of people who answered "yes" in each country.

**(b)** Which country had the greater proportion answering "yes"? **[4]**

**10.** The numbers of pets in 40 households are shown.

| Pets | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Households | 12 | 15 | 8 | 4 | 1 |

**(a)** State what goes on each axis of a chart of this frequency distribution, and why the bars should be separate.

**(b)** Write down the mode.

**(c)** Find the percentage of households with at least 2 pets. **[4]**

**11.** Ice creams sold on four days were 16, 28, 10 and 22. A pictogram is to use one symbol for 4 ice creams. How many symbols are needed for each day? **[3]**

**12.** A bar chart of test results has bars of different widths, and its vertical axis starts at 50. Give two reasons why the chart is misleading. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Each person = 360 ÷ 90 = 4° [1]. Spring **108°**, summer **144°** [1], autumn **72°**, winter **36°** [1]. Check: 360°.

**2. (a)** 1° represents 45 ÷ 150 = 0.3 people [1], so the total is 360 × 0.3 = **108** [1].

**(b)** 40 × 0.3 = **12** people [1].

**3. (a)** 3.5 × 6 = **21** [1].

**(b)** 1.5 × 6 = **9** [1].

**(c)** 24 ÷ 6 = **4 symbols** [1].

**4. (a)** 54 ÷ 360 × 100 = **15%** [1].

**(b)** 0.35 × 360 = **126°** [1].

**5.** **12**, 27 − 12 = **15** [1] and 35 − 27 = **8** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Only the bottom section can be read directly from the scale.

**6. (a)** For each fruit, draw a boys' bar and a girls' bar side by side, of equal width, with gaps between the fruits [1]; the frequency axis starts at zero with an even scale, axes are labelled and there is a key for boys and girls [1].

**(b)** Banana: 11 − 7 = 4 (apple 3, mango 1), so **banana** [1].

**(c)** 9 + 11 + 14 = **34** [1].

**7. (a)** Year 1: **4, 11, 20** [1]. Year 2: **6, 11, 21** [2] (one mark for two correct).

**(b)** The tops of the bars are 20 and 21, so **Year 2** [1].

**8. (a)** Rent 0.35 × 360 = **126°** [1]; food **90°** [1]; transport **54°** and other **90°** [1].

**(b)** 450 is 25%, so total = 450 × 4 = **1800 dollars** [1]. Rent = 0.35 × 1800 = **630 dollars** [1].

**9. (a)** X: (108 ÷ 360) × 2000 = **600** [1]. Y: (72 ÷ 360) × 5000 = **1000** [1].

**(b)** X: 30%; Y: 20% [1]. **Country X** has the greater proportion, though Country Y has more people answering "yes" [1].

*Examiner insight:* A pie chart shows proportions, so the totals are needed before numbers can be compared.

**10. (a)** Number of pets on the horizontal axis and number of households (frequency) on the vertical axis [1]; the data is discrete, so there are no values between the bars [1].

**(b)** **1 pet** [1].

**(c)** (8 + 4 + 1) ÷ 40 = 13 ÷ 40 = **32.5%** [1].

**11.** 16 ÷ 4 = **4**; 28 ÷ 4 = **7** [1]; 10 ÷ 4 = **2½** [1]; 22 ÷ 4 = **5½** [1].

**12.** Bars of different widths make some categories look larger than their frequency [1]; the axis does not start at zero, so differences in height are exaggerated [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Pie chart angles that do not total 360°.
- Composite bar sections read without subtraction.
- Comparing pie chart sectors with different totals as if they were frequencies.
- Pictogram fractions read or drawn incorrectly.
- Charts with no key, labels or zero on the frequency axis.
- Giving the frequency as the mode.
