Section A has short-answer and calculation questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Every business and figure is invented. ## Section A

**1.** Define the term lean production. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between quality control and quality assurance. **[2]**

**3.** Ardley Plastics has fixed costs of $72,000 a month and a capacity of 12,000 units a month. Last month it produced 9,000 units. Calculate:

**(a)** its capacity utilisation **[2]**

**(b)** its fixed cost per unit. **[2]**

**4.** A warehouse team of 24 employees packed 4,320 orders a week. After new scanners were introduced, a team of 23 packed 4,968 orders a week. Calculate the percentage change in labour productivity. **[4]**

**5.** A firm needs 10,000 components a year. Making them would add fixed costs of $15,000 a year plus a variable cost of $2.40 per component. A supplier charges $3.60 per component. Calculate which option is cheaper and by how much. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit of Kaizen for a manufacturing business. **[3]**

**7.** State **two** ways a business can improve its customer service. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **one** cost of poor quality for a restaurant. **[2]**

## Section B

**9.** Calloway Garden Furniture can make 3,000 benches a quarter. Expected demand next year is 1,500, 4,000, 3,500 and 1,000 benches in quarters 1 to 4. Calloway plans to produce 3,000 benches in each of quarters 1 to 3 and 1,000 in quarter 4, holding inventory to cover the peak. It starts the year with no inventory.

**(a)** Calculate Calloway's inventory at the end of each of quarters 1, 2 and 3. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages for Calloway of building inventory to meet the peak. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse the benefits to a private health clinic of introducing total quality management (TQM). **[6]**

**11.** Explain **two** difficulties a car parts manufacturer that imports most of its components might face when introducing Just in Time. **[4]**

**12.** Ostley Bakeries supplies bread to supermarkets. Its bakery runs at 98% capacity utilisation, and orders are forecast to rise by 15% a year for the next three years. It could buy a new oven line for $400,000, which would take nine months to install. Alternatively, it could outsource the extra production to another bakery, at a cost of $0.12 more per loaf than baking it itself. Ostley's brand is known for consistent quality, and its supermarket contracts include penalties for late deliveries. Evaluate whether Ostley should outsource the extra production or invest in the new oven line. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** An approach to operations that aims to cut waste of all kinds [1], such as excess inventory, waiting time and defects, through methods like JIT and Kaizen [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Quality control inspects output, usually at the end of the process, to find and remove faulty items [1]; quality assurance builds checks into every stage to prevent faults happening [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 9,000 ÷ 12,000 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1].

**(b)** 72,000 ÷ 9,000 [1] = **$8** per unit [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Before: 4,320 ÷ 24 = 180 orders per employee [1]. After: 4,968 ÷ 23 = 216 orders per employee [1]. Change = (216 − 180) ÷ 180 × 100 [1] = **+20%** [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Make: 15,000 + (2.40 × 10,000) = $39,000 [1]. Buy: 3.60 × 10,000 = $36,000 [1]. Buying is cheaper by **$3,000** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Kaizen asks the employees who do each task to suggest small improvements [1]. Because they know the work best, they can find wasted movement or time, such as badly placed tools [1]. Many small gains add up to lower unit costs over time without large investment, improving competitiveness [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Any two of: understand customer needs [1]; improve the customer experience [1]; benchmark; train staff; reward loyalty; monitor and act on feedback and satisfaction levels. **[2]**

**8.** Food sent back must be remade, wasting ingredients and staff time [1]; customers who have a bad meal may post poor reviews, so future bookings fall [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Quarter 1: 3,000 − 1,500 = **1,500** [1]. Quarter 2: 1,500 + 3,000 − 4,000 = **500** [1]. Quarter 3: 500 + 3,000 − 3,500 = **0** [1].

**(b)** Storing up to 1,500 benches needs warehouse space, so Calloway pays storage costs for months [1], raising the cost of each bench [1]. The benches are paid for long before they are sold, so cash is tied up in inventory [1], which may force Calloway to use an overdraft in the first half of the year [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Under TQM every member of staff, from receptionists to doctors, takes responsibility for quality [1]. Errors such as wrong appointment details are prevented at source rather than found later [1], which reduces complaints and repeated treatment [1]. In a clinic, patients judge quality on the whole visit, so consistent service across every stage builds trust [1]. Satisfied patients return and recommend the clinic, supporting higher fees than rivals [1]. However, TQM needs training and time to change attitudes, so benefits may take months to appear [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Imported components travel long distances, so lead times are long and delays at ports are common [1]; with little inventory under JIT, one late shipment could stop the production line [1]. JIT needs suppliers to deliver small, frequent loads [1], which is costly over long distances and may mean losing discounts for large orders [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Credit chains of reasoning and a judgement, for example:

- At 98% utilisation Ostley cannot meet a 15% rise in orders from its current bakery [1].
- Outsourcing gives extra capacity immediately, with no $400,000 investment [1], so cash is kept for other uses [1].
- But each outsourced loaf costs $0.12 more, reducing Ostley's margin [1].
- Ostley loses direct control of quality, and its brand rests on consistency [1], so poor-quality bread from the other bakery could damage supermarket relationships [1].
- Late deliveries from the other bakery would trigger penalties under Ostley's contracts [1].
- The oven line takes nine months to install, so Ostley would need another solution in the meantime [1].
- With growth forecast for three years, the extra cost of outsourcing would build up, so investment is likely to be cheaper in the long run [1].
- But forecasts may be wrong; if orders do not grow, the new line would sit idle and raise fixed costs per loaf [1].
- A combination is possible: outsource for nine months while the line is installed [1].
- Judgement: Ostley should invest in the oven line and use outsourcing only as a short-term bridge, because sustained growth, quality control and contract penalties all favour capacity it controls, provided it can finance the $400,000 [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating productivity with the same headcount before and after when the case changes it.
- Leaving out the extra fixed costs when costing the "make" option.
- Describing TQM as an inspection department.
- Giving disadvantages of inventory without linking them to cash or costs in the case.
- Recommending outsourcing or investment without considering how long the extra demand will last.
