## P1 at a glance

| Ref | You must be able to |
|---|---|
| P1 | Understand and use the fundamental SI quantities and units: length, time, mass |
| P1 | Understand and use the derived quantities and units: velocity, acceleration, force, weight, moment |

## Fundamental quantities

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol | Common non-SI forms you must convert |
|---|---|---|---|
| length | metre | m | km, cm, mm |
| time | second | s | minutes, hours |
| mass | kilogram | kg | g, tonnes |

The SI unit of mass is the **kilogram**, not the gram.

## Derived quantities

| Quantity | Defined by | Unit | Base units |
|---|---|---|---|
| velocity (and speed) | displacement ÷ time | m s⁻¹ | m s⁻¹ |
| acceleration | change in velocity ÷ time | m s⁻² | m s⁻² |
| force | F = ma | N | kg m s⁻² |
| weight | W = mg | N | kg m s⁻² |
| moment | force × perpendicular distance | N m | kg m² s⁻² |

Appendix B of the specification lists weight = mass × g and F = ma among the formulae you must use **without being given them**.

**1 N** is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻². **1 kN = 1000 N.**

## Notation (Appendix A)

- m/s and m s⁻¹ both mean metres per second; m/s² and m s⁻² both mean metres per second per second.
- N m is the newton metre, the unit of the moment of a force.
- g is the acceleration due to gravity; F is a force or resultant force.
- The letters s and m are both units (second, metre) and variables (displacement, mass). Keep units next to numbers so they never get confused.

## Method: convert before you substitute

```
1. List every given quantity with its unit.
2. Convert each one to kg, m, s (or m s⁻¹, m s⁻², N).
3. Substitute into the formula.
4. Give the answer with its SI unit, rounded sensibly.
```

Key conversion factors:

| From | To | Do |
|---|---|---|
| km | m | × 1000 |
| cm | m | ÷ 100 |
| g | kg | ÷ 1000 |
| tonnes | kg | × 1000 |
| minutes | s | × 60 |
| hours | s | × 3600 |
| km h⁻¹ | m s⁻¹ | ÷ 3.6 |
| m s⁻¹ | km h⁻¹ | × 3.6 |
| cm s⁻² | m s⁻² | ÷ 100 |

Sense check: the same speed is always a smaller number in m s⁻¹ than in km h⁻¹.

Reminder: 126 km h⁻¹ ÷ 3.6 = 35 m s⁻¹. 0.8 tonnes = 800 kg.

## Mass versus weight

| Mass | Weight |
|---|---|
| amount of matter in a body | force of gravity on the body |
| kilograms, kg | newtons, N |
| the same everywhere | W = mg, so it changes if g changes |
| scalar | a force, acting vertically downwards |

The specification says g may be taken as constant and is used to varying degrees of accuracy, but g is **not a universal constant**: it depends on location. Use the value of g given in the question, exactly.

Reminder: a 5 kg box has weight 5 × 9.8 = 49 N with g = 9.8 m s⁻². At a place where g = 9.7 m s⁻² its weight is 48.5 N. Its mass is 5 kg in both places.

## Moment in one line

moment = F × d, where d is the **perpendicular** distance (in metres) from the point to the line of action of the force. Unit: **N m**. Calculations with moments in equilibrium belong to Section S.

Reminder: 25 N at right angles, 12 cm from a hinge: d = 0.12 m, moment = 3 N m.

## Three short worked reminders

**A. Force given in kN.** A resultant force of 2.4 kN acts on a car of mass 800 kg.

```
F = 2.4 kN = 2400 N
a = F ÷ m = 2400 ÷ 800 = 3 m s⁻²
```

Leaving F as 2.4 gives 0.003 m s⁻², which is 1000 times too small.

**B. Mixed time units.** A runner covers 400 m in 1 minute 20 seconds.

```
time = 60 + 20 = 80 s
average speed = 400 ÷ 80 = 5 m s⁻¹
in km/h: 5 × 3.6 = 18 km h⁻¹
```

**C. Mass in grams.** A trolley of mass 1500 g speeds up steadily from 0.5 m s⁻¹ to 1.7 m s⁻¹ in 3 s.

```
m = 1500 ÷ 1000 = 1.5 kg
a = (1.7 − 0.5) ÷ 3 = 0.4 m s⁻²
F = ma = 1.5 × 0.4 = 0.6 N
```

## Fast sense checks

- A speed in m s⁻¹ is always smaller than the same speed in km h⁻¹.
- A weight in newtons is roughly ten times the mass in kilograms when g is about 9.8.
- A moment worked out with centimetres is 100 times the moment in N m.
- If an answer is out by a factor of 1000, 100, 60 or 3.6, look for an unconverted unit.

## Method: check units in a formula

```
1. Write each term in base units (kg, m, s).
2. Every term added or subtracted, and both sides of =, must match.
3. For an unknown constant, rearrange and divide the units.
```

Reminder: in s = ut + ½at², the units are m, (m s⁻¹)(s) = m and (m s⁻²)(s²) = m. All three match.

Reminder: if a resistance is R = cv, then c = R/v has units N ÷ m s⁻¹ = kg m s⁻² × s m⁻¹ = kg s⁻¹.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Fundamental vs derived:** length, time and mass are fundamental; velocity, acceleration, force, weight and moment are derived from them.
- **Mass vs weight:** kg vs N; W = mg.
- **Velocity vs acceleration units:** m s⁻¹ vs m s⁻².
- **Moment vs force:** N m vs N. A moment needs a distance.
- **kN vs N:** multiply kN by 1000 before using F = ma.
- **Unit s vs variable s:** seconds vs displacement.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the SI units of length, mass and time.
2. Convert 5.4 km to metres.
3. Convert 2 hours 15 minutes to seconds.
4. Convert 45 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
5. Convert 3 m s⁻¹ to km h⁻¹.
6. Write the newton in base units.
7. Find the weight of a 60 kg person, taking g = 9.81 m s⁻².
8. Find the resultant force needed to give a 4 kg mass an acceleration of 2.5 m s⁻².
9. A 16 N force acts at right angles to a lever, 35 cm from the pivot. Find its moment.
10. A label says "weight 9.8 kg". Correct the physics.
11. Write the newton metre in base units.
12. Which of these is not a correct unit of acceleration: m s⁻², m/s², m s⁻¹ per s, N kg⁻¹, m s⁻¹?

### Answers

1. **metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s)**
2. 5.4 × 1000 = **5400 m**
3. 2 × 3600 + 15 × 60 = **8100 s**
4. 45 ÷ 3.6 = **12.5 m s⁻¹**
5. 3 × 3.6 = **10.8 km h⁻¹**
6. **1 N = 1 kg m s⁻²**
7. 60 × 9.81 = 588.6 = **589 N** (3 s.f.)
8. F = ma = 4 × 2.5 = **10 N**
9. 16 × 0.35 = **5.6 N m**
10. 9.8 kg is a **mass**. The body's weight is 9.8g N, which is 96.04 N or **96.0 N** (3 s.f.) with g = 9.8 m s⁻².
11. N m = kg m s⁻² × m = **kg m² s⁻²**
12. **m s⁻¹** is a velocity unit. (N kg⁻¹ = kg m s⁻² ÷ kg = m s⁻², so it is a correct acceleration unit.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a mass in grams, or a distance in centimetres, in F = ma, W = mg or a moment.
- Multiplying by 3.6 instead of dividing when converting km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
- Leaving a force in kN inside F = ma, which makes the acceleration 1000 times too small.
- Stating a weight in kg or a mass in N.
- Giving acceleration in m s⁻¹, or a moment in N.
- Using 9.81 when the question says g = 9.8, or the other way round.
- Rounding a converted speed early (for example 50 km h⁻¹ to 13.9 m s⁻¹) and carrying the rounded value on.
- Using the slant distance instead of the perpendicular distance in a moment.
- Missing units on the final answer.
