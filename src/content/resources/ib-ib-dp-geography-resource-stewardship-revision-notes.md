Condensed recall for **10.3 Resource stewardship** (Unit 3: Global resource consumption and
security). For the full explanation, read the
[Global resource consumption study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-global-resource-consumption-study-guide/).

## Definition

**Stewardship** is responsible management of resources so that they are available for the future. It
operates from individual to global scale and involves governments, businesses, international
organisations, non-governmental groups, communities and consumers.

## Approaches

| Approach | Examples |
|---|---|
| Efficiency and conservation | Insulation, efficient appliances, irrigation efficiency, reducing food waste |
| Substitution and technology | Renewable energy, recycled materials, improved crop varieties |
| Circular economy | Designs out waste by reusing, repairing, remanufacturing and recycling |
| Certification and labelling | Forestry and fisheries certification |
| Regulation, pricing and incentives | Taxes, quotas, standards, subsidies, carbon pricing |
| Agreements and cooperation | International agreements on shared resources; local community management |

The **linear** model is take-make-dispose; the circular economy contrasts with it.

## Evaluating stewardship

Ask whether the strategy is **effective, affordable, fair and enforceable**.

- **Rebound effect:** efficiency gains reduce cost and lead to more consumption, offsetting savings.
- **Equity:** lower-income countries argue that their right to develop must be respected, while
  higher-income countries are responsible for most past consumption.
- Balance competing views and use clear criteria.

## Exam traps

- Offering stewardship ideas with no evaluation or no named stakeholders.
- Assuming efficiency always reduces total use (the rebound effect).
- Ignoring the fairness dispute between countries.
- Listing places without explaining a pattern.

## Self-test

1. Define resource stewardship.
2. Contrast the circular and linear economy.
3. What is the rebound effect?
4. Give four criteria for evaluating a stewardship strategy.
5. Why is stewardship contested between countries?

**Answers:** 1. Responsible management so resources remain available for the future. 2. Circular designs
out waste through reuse, repair and recycling; linear is take-make-dispose. 3. Efficiency lowers costs
and encourages more consumption. 4. Effective, affordable, fair and enforceable. 5. States disagree
about historic responsibility, rights to develop and who bears the costs.
