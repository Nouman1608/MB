## Questions

**1.** Define the term "mission". **[2]**

**2.** State **three** reasons why a business sets objectives. **[3]**

**3.** Tarrant Tea Merchants is a sole trader. In one quarter it sells 3,000 boxes at £6.00. Variable costs are £2.40 a box and fixed costs are £7,200.

**(a)** Calculate revenue, total costs and profit for the quarter. **[4]**
**(b)** Fixed costs rise by 10% next quarter and sales stay the same. Calculate the new profit. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **two** reasons why profit is important to a business. **[4]**

**5.** Marl Street Bakery opened eight months ago. Monthly revenue is £9,500 and total costs are £10,200. The owner wants to open a second shop. Analyse why survival may be a more suitable objective than growth for the bakery now. **[4]**

**6.** Explain the difference between limited and unlimited liability. **[3]**

**7.** Marrick Glass plc has 2,400,000 ordinary shares in issue. The share price falls from £5.50 to £4.40. The company pays a dividend of 15p a share.

**(a)** Calculate market capitalisation before and after the fall. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in market capitalisation. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the total dividend paid. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain **one** possible reason for the fall in share price. **[2]**

**8.** Distinguish between a charity and a mutual. **[4]**

**9.** Bridie Hartnoll runs Corrin Joinery as a sole trader. Annual revenue is £210,000 and profit is £38,000. She wants to bid for a contract that needs £90,000 of machinery. A bank will lend £50,000, but only if her house, worth £300,000, is offered as security. A friend would invest £40,000 for 25% of the business if it became a private limited company. Recommend whether Bridie should convert to a private limited company. **[9]**

**10.** Rook Dairy Foods plc was founded by a family with the mission of providing honest dairy products from local farms. It now has 12,000,000 shares, 85% held by investment funds. The board has set targets of 12% annual profit growth and a dividend of 8p a share. It is considering buying cheaper imported milk, which would save £1,400,000 a year. Analyse how its status as a plc may affect this decision. **[8]**

**11.** Explain **one** reason why an investor might buy ordinary shares in a company and **one** reason why limited liability helps this decision. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The mission is the overall purpose of the business, why it exists [1]. It is broad and long-term rather than a numerical target [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition tends to gain full credit when it says both what a mission is and how it differs from an objective; "what the business wants to achieve" alone is vague.

**2.** Any three from: to give all departments a common direction; to motivate staff with something to aim at; to measure performance by comparing results with targets; to help choose between options; to make managers accountable to owners. One mark each [1] [1] [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A state question rewards three distinct reasons. Rewording one reason three times is likely to earn one point.

**3. (a)** Revenue = 3,000 × £6.00 = **£18,000** [1]. Variable costs = 3,000 × £2.40 = £7,200 [1]. Total costs = 7,200 + 7,200 = **£14,400** [1]. Profit = 18,000 − 14,400 = **£3,600** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Fixed costs = 7,200 × 1.10 = £7,920 [1]. Profit = 18,000 − 7,200 − 7,920 = **£2,880** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage. A common slip is to treat variable costs as the total costs, which overstates profit.

**4.** Any two developed reasons. Profit rewards owners for the risk they take [1], so they are willing to keep investing in the business [1]. Retained profit can fund expansion without borrowing [1], which avoids interest costs and keeps control with the owners [1]. Other valid reasons: attracts lenders and investors; builds a cushion for survival; pays dividends. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a consequence. Naming "reward" or "finance" without saying what follows is likely to earn only half of the available credit.

**5.** The bakery makes a loss of £9,500 − £10,200 = −£700 a month [1]. Losses drain cash, so the owner risks being unable to pay suppliers or wages [1]. A second shop would add fixed costs and need finance, which a loss-making firm may struggle to obtain [1]. Surviving first, by raising revenue or cutting costs, builds a base from which growth can later be funded [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figure in the case. Analysis that mentions the loss and follows it through a chain tends to score higher than a general claim that survival matters.

**6.** Limited liability means an owner can lose only the amount invested in the business [1]. Unlimited liability means the owner's personal assets, such as a house, can be used to pay business debts [1]. Sole traders have unlimited liability; shareholders in Ltd and plc companies have limited liability [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the wording about the owner's loss. Saying that "the company's debts are limited" is a typical way of losing the mark.

**7. (a)** Before: 2,400,000 × 5.50 = **£13,200,000** [1]. After: 2,400,000 × 4.40 = **£10,560,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Change = 10,560,000 − 13,200,000 = −£2,640,000 [1]. 2,640,000 ÷ 13,200,000 × 100 = **−20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 2,400,000 × £0.15 = **£360,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Any valid reason, such as lower profit forecasts or weak results [1], which reduce investors' demand for the shares so more sell than buy [1]. Other reasons: takeover news, scandal, higher interest rates, loss of confidence. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert pence to pounds before multiplying. A percentage fall should be shown as a negative or described as a fall, with the working visible.

**8.** A charity exists to pursue a charitable purpose [1], and puts any surplus back into that purpose [1]. A mutual is owned by its members, who are its customers or savers [1], and uses any surplus for their benefit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinguish question needs the difference stated, not two separate definitions. Saying that both "do not make profit" is inaccurate; the point is what happens to the surplus.

**9.** Staying as a sole trader: Bridie has unlimited liability, so the bank's condition puts a £300,000 house at risk [1], and £50,000 is about 1.3 years of her whole £38,000 profit [1]. She keeps all control and profit and avoids extra reporting [1]. Converting to a private limited company: limited liability would cap her loss at her investment [1]; the friend's £40,000 plus the £50,000 loan reaches the £90,000 needed [1]. Costs: she would give up 25% of the business and its profit [1], and a company has more reporting and less privacy [1]. Judgement: convert [1], because the contract's size relative to her income makes personal risk high, provided the contract is realistic and a lender will accept company security terms [1]. If the contract were small, staying as she is would be more attractive. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommend answer is stronger when it uses the figures, weighs both options and states a condition for the judgement. Listing features of each form does not reach a decision.

**10.** The dividend costs 12,000,000 × £0.08 = £960,000 [1]. The imported milk saving of £1,400,000 is larger than the whole dividend, by £440,000 [1], so it would help meet profit and dividend targets [1]. Institutions hold 85%, so the board faces pressure to meet the targets [1]. Ownership is separated from control; directors may favour short-term profit [1]. This conflicts with the mission of using local farms [1], so reputation and loyal customers could suffer [1]. A fall in profit could cut the share price and raise takeover risk, so the board may weigh this more than the founders' mission [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An analysis answer needs a chain from plc status to the pressure to the decision. A generic list of plc features, with no use of the case figures, tends to remain in the lower levels.

**11.** One reason: dividend income, or capital growth if the share price rises [1]. Limited liability means the investor can lose only the sum invested [1], so the risk is capped and investing in a business they do not run is more acceptable [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The two parts need separate reasons. Repeating the first reason in the second part does not earn the second mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a list when the command word is explain or analyse.
- Missing a step in a profit calculation, usually fixed costs.
- Forgetting to convert pence to pounds in dividend questions.
- Writing that market capitalisation is the money held by the company.
- Describing limited liability as limiting the company's debts.
- Treating charity and mutual as the same thing.
- Recommending a business form without using the figures in the case.
- Giving only advantages of a plc, or only disadvantages.
- Stating a judgement without a reason or a condition.
