## Questions

**1.** Define market failure. **[2]**

**2.** A bakery in Country A sells a good with a private cost of 24 per unit. Its fumes impose an external cost of 9 per unit on neighbours.

**(a)** Calculate the social cost per unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Express the external cost as a percentage share of the social cost, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**3.** For each activity, state whether it is a production or consumption externality, and whether it is positive or negative. **[4]**

**(a)** Smoke from a brick kiln drifts over a nearby village.
**(b)** A homeowner fits outdoor lights, which makes the street safer for everyone.
**(c)** A firm pays for staff courses, and several staff later join rival firms.
**(d)** Customers leaving a bar late at night wake the local residents.

**4.** A quarry in Region Z (illustrative) sells stone in blocks of 1,000 tonnes. The external cost of dust is 12 per block. Values are in money units per block.

| Block | Private benefit | Private cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 70 | 10 |
| 2 | 60 | 20 |
| 3 | 50 | 30 |
| 4 | 40 | 40 |
| 5 | 30 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the social cost of each block. **[1]**
**(b)** State the output the quarry chooses, with a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** State the socially best output, with a reason. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the welfare loss on the block that is over-produced. **[1]**
**(e)** A tax of 12 per block is introduced. Calculate the tax revenue. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a negative externality leads to over-production. **[4]**

**6.** A government in Country B taxes a sugary drink, which raises its price by 12%. Price elasticity of demand is -0.5.

**(a)** Work out by how much quantity demanded changes, as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the tax may do little to cut consumption of a good with inelastic demand. **[2]**

**7.** A town in Region Y (illustrative) offers adult education courses. Each unit is 100 places and costs 20 to supply. Each unit gives an external benefit of 14.

| Unit | Private benefit |
|---|---|
| 1 | 60 |
| 2 | 48 |
| 3 | 36 |
| 4 | 24 |
| 5 | 12 |

**(a)** Calculate the social benefit of each unit. **[1]**
**(b)** State the output chosen by the market, with a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** State the socially best output, with a reason. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the gain to society from the unit that is under-provided. **[1]**
**(e)** A subsidy of 14 per unit is introduced. Calculate the total subsidy bill at the new output. **[1]**

**8.** Explain two costs of the misallocation of resources. **[4]**

**9.** Explain two methods the government could use to reduce the over-production of a good with a negative externality. **[6]**

**10.** "A tax is the best way to deal with a negative externality." Evaluate this statement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Market failure is when the market system cannot [1] allocate resources efficiently, so resources are misallocated [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain more when the definition names both resources and efficiency, since "the market fails" alone repeats the term.

**2. (a)** Social cost = private cost + external cost [1] = 24 + 9 = **33** per unit [1].
**(b)** 9 / 33 × 100 [1] = **27.3%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the division step makes it easier to credit your method if the final rounding is slightly off.

**3. (a)** Production, negative [1].
**(b)** Consumption, positive [1].
**(c)** Production, positive [1].
**(d)** Consumption, negative [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Ask who creates the effect, the maker or the user, before you decide between production and consumption.

**4. (a)** Social cost = private cost + 12, giving 22, 32, 42, 52, 62 [1].
**(b)** The quarry continues while private benefit is at least private cost [1]. Block 4 gives 40 against 40 and block 5 gives 30 against 50, so output is **4 blocks** [1].
**(c)** Society continues while benefit is at least social cost [1]. Block 3 gives 50 against 42, but block 4 gives 40 against 52, so the best output is **3 blocks** [1].
**(d)** Block 4: social cost 52 minus benefit 40 = **12** [1].
**(e)** 3 blocks × 12 = **36** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* State which columns you are comparing in each part, because the market and social answers use different ones.

**5.** Producers consider only their private costs [1]. The external cost is paid by third parties and is ignored [1]. Costs to the producer are below the full social cost, so supply is higher than society would choose [1]. Price is too low and output too high, so the good is over-produced [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A full chain, from ignored cost to output compared with the social best, usually reads better than a statement that "it is over-produced".

**6. (a)** Percentage change in quantity = PED × percentage change in price [1] = -0.5 × 12 = **-6%** [1].
**(b)** Inelastic demand means consumers respond little to a price rise [1], so the quantity falls by a smaller percentage than the price rises, and consumption barely changes [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the sign and the unit with your answer shows you have read the elasticity correctly.

**7. (a)** Social benefit = private benefit + 14, giving 74, 62, 50, 38, 26 [1].
**(b)** The market provides units while private benefit is at least 20 [1]. Unit 4 gives 24 and unit 5 gives 12, so output is **4 units** [1].
**(c)** Society provides units while social benefit is at least 20 [1]. Unit 5 gives 26, so the best output is **5 units** [1].
**(d)** Unit 5: social benefit 26 minus cost 20 = **6** [1].
**(e)** The subsidy cuts the cost to 6, so unit 5 (benefit 12) is bought. Output is 5 units, so the bill is 5 × 14 = **70** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (e) depends on finding the new output first, so show that step before multiplying.

**8.** First cost: welfare is lost [1] because resources are used where they give less value than elsewhere, such as a good costing more to society than it is worth [1]. Second cost: third parties are harmed [1], for example by pollution, which they never agreed to [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each cost needs a short explanation. Listing two words earns less than two developed points.

**9.** Tax: it raises the producer's costs [1], so supply falls and output moves towards the social best [1]; a limit is that the right level is hard to find [1]. Regulation: the government sets a limit or bans an activity [1], which cuts output directly [1]; a limit is that enforcement costs money [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking each method to how it changes costs or behaviour is more convincing than naming it.

**10.** A tax raises the cost of production by the size of the external cost [1], so output falls towards the social best [1]. Strength: it also raises revenue that can fund clean-up or other services [1]. Strength: the firm can choose how to cut output, so the price mechanism still works [1]. Limit: the right tax is hard to set because external costs are hard to value [1]. Limit: with inelastic demand, consumption falls little [1]. Alternatives: regulation or education may be quicker, or may work together with a tax [1]. Judgement: a tax is often useful, but whether it is best depends on elasticity and how well the cost can be measured [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers tend to do better when the final judgement refers back to the conditions you have set out.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out resources or efficiency from a definition of market failure.
- Adding the external cost to the wrong side of a table.
- Choosing the output at the first block where benefit exceeds cost rather than the last.
- Using the private column when the question asks about the social optimum.
- Treating a subsidy bill as if it were revenue.
- Forgetting the negative sign or the unit in an elasticity answer.
- Naming a policy without saying how it changes costs or behaviour.
- Giving one-sided answers to an evaluate question.
