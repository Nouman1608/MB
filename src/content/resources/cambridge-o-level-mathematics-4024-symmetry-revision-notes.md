These notes summarise section 4.5, Symmetry, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [symmetry study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-symmetry/), and exam-style questions are in the [symmetry practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-symmetry-practice/).

## What is examined

- Line symmetry and order of rotational symmetry in two dimensions.
- Properties of triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons that come from their symmetries.
- Symmetry properties of prisms, cylinders, pyramids and cones, for example planes and axes of symmetry.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line of symmetry | a mirror line: each half is the reflection of the other |
| Rotational symmetry | the shape fits onto itself after a turn of less than 360° |
| Order | the number of positions in which the shape fits itself in one full turn |
| Centre of rotation | the fixed point about which the shape turns |
| Plane of symmetry | a flat cut that divides a solid into mirror-image halves |
| Axis of symmetry | a line through a solid about which it can be turned to fit itself |
| Regular polygon | all sides and all angles equal |

## The two key formulas

```
order of rotational symmetry = 360° ÷ smallest turn
smallest turn = 360° ÷ order
```

A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry and order n.

## Plane shapes at a glance

| Shape | Lines | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 | 1 |
| Square | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 | 2 |
| Parallelogram | 0 | 2 |
| Kite | 1 | 1 |
| Isosceles trapezium | 1 | 1 |
| Regular pentagon | 5 | 5 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 | 6 |
| Circle | infinite | infinite |

## What symmetry explains

- **Isosceles triangle**: the base angles are equal, and the line from the apex to the base is perpendicular to it and bisects it.
- **Rectangle**: opposite sides are equal, the diagonals are equal and bisect each other.
- **Rhombus**: the diagonals are lines of symmetry, so they bisect each other at 90° and bisect the angles at the vertices.
- **Kite**: one diagonal is the line of symmetry, so it bisects the angles at the ends of the diagonal and the other diagonal at 90°.
- **Parallelogram**: order 2 rotation about the centre gives opposite sides equal, opposite angles equal and diagonals that bisect each other.

## Method box: finding lines of symmetry

1. Test lines through opposite vertices.
2. Test lines through midpoints of opposite sides.
3. For a regular polygon, there is one line through each vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side or opposite vertex, so n lines in all.
4. Do not count a diagonal if reflecting in it moves a vertex onto a different-shaped part.

## Method box: reflecting and rotating points

```
reflect in x = a:      (x, y) → (2a − x, y)
reflect in y = b:      (x, y) → (x, 2b − y)
reflect in y = x:      (x, y) → (y, x)
rotate 90° anticlockwise about origin:   (x, y) → (−y, x)
rotate 180° about origin:                (x, y) → (−x, −y)
rotate 90° clockwise about origin:       (x, y) → (y, −x)
```

## Solids at a glance

| Solid | Planes | Axes |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid (edges all different) | 3 | 3, each of order 2 |
| Cube | 9 | 13 |
| Square-based pyramid | 4 | 1, of order 4 |
| Cone | infinitely many | 1 |
| Cylinder | infinitely many, plus one halfway along | 1 main axis |
| Triangular prism (equilateral ends) | 4 | 1 of order 3 and 3 of order 2 |
| Sphere | infinitely many | infinitely many |

Planes of a prism: count the lines of symmetry of the cross-section (each gives a plane along the length), then add one plane halfway along the length (provided the ends are identical).

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Contrast |
|---|---|
| Line symmetry | reflection |
| Rotational symmetry | turn |
| Plane of symmetry | a flat cut, used for solids |
| Axis of symmetry | a line, used for turning solids |
| Order 1 | no rotational symmetry |
| Order 0 | does not exist |

## Exam traps

- Counting a diagonal of a rectangle or parallelogram as a line of symmetry.
- Saying order 0 instead of order 1.
- Dividing 180° by n for a regular polygon.
- Forgetting the plane halfway along a prism, cylinder or cuboid.
- Writing "2" as a number of planes for a cuboid with three different dimensions. It is 3.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| State, Write down | give the number or answer without working |
| Draw | use a ruler to show the line or the shape |
| Complete | add the missing parts so the shape has the stated symmetry |
| Describe | give the key features, such as "an axis through the apex and the centre of the base" |

## Self-test

1. State the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of an isosceles trapezium.
2. A regular polygon has a smallest turn of 36°. Write down its number of sides.
3. How many lines of symmetry has a regular octagon?
4. Triangle PQR is isosceles with PQ = PR and angle QPR = 50°. Find angle PQR.
5. Reflect the point (4, 7) in the line x = 1.
6. Rotate the point (3, 1) through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.
7. How many planes of symmetry has a cuboid with a square cross-section?
8. State the order of rotational symmetry of a square-based pyramid about its vertical axis.
9. Name the quadrilateral with rotational symmetry of order 2 and no line symmetry.
10. How many planes of symmetry has a cone?

### Answers

1. **1 line**, **order 1**.
2. 360 ÷ 36 = **10 sides**.
3. **8**.
4. (180 − 50) ÷ 2 = **65°**.
5. (2 × 1 − 4, 7) = **(−2, 7)**.
6. (x, y) → (−y, x) gives **(−1, 3)**.
7. **5** (3 parallel to the faces, 2 along the diagonals of the square cross-section).
8. **4**.
9. A **parallelogram** (that is not a rectangle or rhombus).
10. **Infinitely many**.
