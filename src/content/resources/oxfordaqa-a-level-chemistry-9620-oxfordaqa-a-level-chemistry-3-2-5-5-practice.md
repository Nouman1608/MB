These questions cover **3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [variable oxidation states study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-5/) or the [variable oxidation states revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-5-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** What is the oxidation state of manganese in MnO₄²⁻?

A: +5

B: +6

C: +7

D: +8 **[1]**

**2.** Give the formula and the colour of the vanadium(IV) species formed when zinc reduces acidified vanadate(V). **[2]**

**3.** Use the data to deduce how far iron(II) ions can reduce acidified VO₂⁺, and state the final colour. Show your reasoning. **[3]**

**4.** Write an overall equation for the reduction of V³⁺ to V²⁺ by zinc. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why no indicator is added in a titration of Fe²⁺(aq) with acidified potassium manganate(VII). **[2]**

**6.** Use the data to explain why Tollens' reagent is a weaker oxidising agent than Ag⁺(aq). **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Five iron tablets, total mass 2.85 g, are dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and the solution is made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions are titrated with 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄; the mean titre is 11.60 cm³. Ar(Fe) = 55.8.

**(a)** Explain why dilute sulfuric acid is used rather than dilute hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of iron in one tablet. **[4]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in the tablets. **[1]**

**8.** 1.575 g of hydrated ethanedioic acid, H₂C₂O₄·xH₂O, is dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions, acidified and warmed, need 25.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. Mr(H₂C₂O₄) = 90.0.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction of MnO₄⁻ with C₂O₄²⁻ in acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the solution is warmed before titrating. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the value of x. **[4]**

**9.** Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) also oxidises Fe²⁺. The half-equation is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O.

**(a)** Write the overall equation for the reaction of dichromate(VI) with iron(II) ions. **[1]**

**(b)** 25.0 cm³ of an Fe²⁺ solution reacts with 24.50 cm³ of 0.0167 mol dm⁻³ K₂Cr₂O₇. Calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺. **[2]**

**(c)** State the colour change of the chromium species. **[1]**

**10.** This question is about the effect of pH on manganate(VII).

**(a)** Explain why acidified MnO₄⁻ is a stronger oxidising agent than MnO₄⁻ in neutral solution. **[2]**

**(b)** In a titration with too little acid, a brown precipitate forms. Identify it and write the half-equation for its formation from MnO₄⁻. **[2]**

**11.** Propanal is warmed with Tollens' reagent.

**(a)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of the silver complex. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a half-equation for the oxidation of propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO, to propanoic acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Combine your half-equations into an overall equation. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain what the E⦵ values for Ag⁺/Ag and [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺/Ag show about the effect of a ligand on a redox potential. **[2]**

**12.** 25.0 cm³ of a 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ solution of V²⁺ ions is titrated with acidified 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. The end point is reached after 12.0 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of V²⁺ used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of electrons accepted by the manganate(VII). **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the final oxidation state of vanadium and identify the vanadium species formed. **[2]**

**(d)** Use the data to explain why manganate(VII) oxidises vanadium to this oxidation state and does not stop at V³⁺. **[1]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** B [1]

**2.** VO²⁺ [1]; blue [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.** VO₂⁺ to VO²⁺: E⦵cell = 1.00 − 0.77 = +0.23 V, feasible [1]; VO²⁺ to V³⁺: E⦵cell = 0.34 − 0.77 = −0.43 V, not feasible [1]; so the reduction stops at VO²⁺ and the solution is blue [1]

**Total: 3**

**4.** 2V³⁺ + Zn → 2V²⁺ + Zn²⁺ [1]

**5.** MnO₄⁻ is purple and is decolourised (reduced to almost colourless Mn²⁺) as long as Fe²⁺ remains [1]; the first drop in excess gives a permanent pale pink colour, which marks the end point [1]

**Total: 2**

**6.** E⦵ for [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺/Ag (+0.37 V) is less positive than for Ag⁺/Ag (+0.80 V), so the silver(I) complex accepts electrons less readily [1]

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Cl⁻ ions would be oxidised by MnO₄⁻, so more titrant would be used and the titre would be too large [1]

**(b)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0100 × 11.60 ÷ 1000 = 1.16 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(Fe²⁺) in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 1.16 × 10⁻⁴ = 5.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; in 250 cm³ = 5.80 × 10⁻³ mol, mass = 5.80 × 10⁻³ × 55.8 = 0.324 g in five tablets [1]; per tablet = 0.0647 g (64.7 mg) [1]

**(c)** 0.3236 ÷ 2.85 × 100 = 11.4 % [1]

**Total: 6**

*Examiner insight:* Two scale factors appear here: × 10 from portion to flask, then ÷ 5 for one tablet. Label each step so a slip in one does not cost the method marks for the other.

**8.**

**(a)** 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O [1]

**(b)** The reaction is very slow at room temperature (until Mn²⁺, which catalyses it, has formed) [1]

**(c)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 25.00 ÷ 1000 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(C₂O₄²⁻) in 25.0 cm³ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol, so 1.25 × 10⁻² mol in 250 cm³ [1]; Mr = 1.575 ÷ 0.0125 = 126 [1]; x = (126 − 90.0) ÷ 18.0 = 2 [1]

**Total: 6**

**9.**

**(a)** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O [1]

**(b)** n(Cr₂O₇²⁻) = 0.0167 × 24.50 ÷ 1000 = 4.09 × 10⁻⁴ mol; n(Fe²⁺) = 6 × 4.09 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.45 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; [Fe²⁺] = 2.455 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0982 mol dm⁻³ [1]

**(c)** Orange to green [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* The ratio here is 1 : 6, not the 1 : 5 of manganate(VII). Always build the ratio from the half-equations you are given.

**10.**

**(a)** H⁺ is a reactant in the reduction half-equation MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O [1]; increasing [H⁺] shifts this equilibrium to the right, making the electrode potential more positive [1]

**(b)** Manganese(IV) oxide, MnO₂ [1]; MnO₄⁻ + 4H⁺ + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ + 2H₂O [1]

**Total: 4**

**11.**

**(a)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃ [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O → CH₃CH₂COOH + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]

**(c)** 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O → 2Ag + 4NH₃ + CH₃CH₂COOH + 2H⁺ [1]

**(d)** Complexing silver(I) with ammonia lowers the electrode potential from +0.80 V to +0.37 V [1]; so the ligand stabilises the higher oxidation state and makes it harder to reduce, giving a milder oxidising agent [1]

**Total: 5**

**12.**

**(a)** 0.0400 × 25.0 ÷ 1000 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]

**(b)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0500 × 12.0 ÷ 1000 = 6.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; electrons = 5 × 6.00 × 10⁻⁴ = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]

**(c)** Each V²⁺ loses 3.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 1.00 × 10⁻³ = 3 electrons [1]; final oxidation state +5, as VO₂⁺ [1]

**(d)** E⦵ for MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺ (+1.51 V) is more positive than every vanadium couple, including VO₂⁺/VO²⁺ (+1.00 V), so every oxidation step is feasible [1]

**Total: 5**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 1 : 5 for every manganate(VII) titration instead of building the ratio.
- Forgetting the scale-up from the portion titrated to the whole solution.
- Confusing VO²⁺ (+4) with VO₂⁺ (+5).
- Explaining acid choice as "HCl reacts with the iron" rather than "Cl⁻ is oxidised by MnO₄⁻".
- Leaving H⁺ on both sides of a combined equation.
