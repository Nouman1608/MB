These notes condense sub-topic **20.1 Food supply** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All three outcomes are **Core**; there is no Extended content in this sub-topic.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [food supply study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-supply/) first, then test yourself with the [food supply practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-supply-practice/). The [human influences on ecosystems revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-human-influences-on-ecosystems-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 20.

## Core recall: five methods (syllabus wording in bold)

| Method | Syllabus purpose | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Agricultural machinery | **use larger areas of land and improve efficiency** | fewer workers farm more land; sowing and harvesting are fast and well timed |
| Chemical fertilisers | **improve yields** | replace mineral ions removed by crops: nitrate for amino acids and proteins, magnesium for chlorophyll |
| Insecticides | **improve quality and yield** | kill insect pests that damage leaves, roots, fruit and grain |
| Herbicides | **reduce competition with weeds** | kill weeds that compete with the crop for light, water, mineral ions and space |
| Selective breeding | **improve production by crop plants and livestock** | breed from the best individuals over many generations |

**Memory hook:** "Machines, Minerals, Minus insects, Minus weeds, More from breeding."

## Core recall: monocultures

**Monoculture:** a large area growing a single crop variety.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| machinery used efficiently on one crop | biodiversity falls: habitats and hedgerows removed; one species grown |
| conditions tailored to one crop | pests and diseases spread easily through genetically similar plants |
| large yield at low cost per tonne | more pesticide needed |
| uniform crop, ripens together | same mineral ions removed each year, so more fertiliser needed |
| | fertiliser and pesticide may wash into water and pollute it |

## Core recall: intensive livestock production

**Intensive livestock production:** many animals kept in a small space, often indoors, with food and conditions controlled.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| less energy lost in movement and keeping warm, so more used for growth | disease spreads quickly, so antibiotics are often used |
| fast growth; high output of meat, eggs or milk | concentrated waste can pollute water |
| lower cost per unit of food | poor animal welfare; little space |
| less land per animal | large amounts of crop feed grown elsewhere |
| protection from predators and weather | energy needed for heating, lighting, ventilation |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Yield | Mass of crop or product obtained per unit area (or per animal) |
| Chemical fertiliser | Substance added to soil to supply mineral ions such as nitrate |
| Insecticide | Chemical that kills insect pests |
| Herbicide | Chemical that kills weeds |
| Weed | Plant growing where it is not wanted, competing with the crop |
| Selective breeding | Humans choose individuals with desirable features to breed over many generations |
| Monoculture | A large area of land growing one crop variety |
| Intensive livestock production | Keeping many animals in a small space with controlled conditions |
| Biodiversity | The number of different species that live in an area |

## Exam traps

- **Insecticide or herbicide?** Insects = insecticide; weeds = herbicide.
- **Vague fertiliser answers.** Name an ion and say "increases yield".
- **Herbicide reason.** It is **competition** for light, water and ions.
- **Machinery is an advantage** of monocultures, not a disadvantage.
- **Intensive animals and energy.** Say less energy is **lost** in movement and heat, not that the animals "use less energy to grow".
- **One-sided answers.** "Advantages and disadvantages" needs both.
- **Units.** Yields need "per hectare" or "per animal" and a time.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** a method: name it + its effect on yield, quality or efficiency.
- **Describe** advantages and disadvantages: each point with a reason.
- **Suggest**: match a method to the farm problem in the question.

## Self-test

1. How does agricultural machinery increase food production?
2. Name the mineral ion in fertilisers needed for making amino acids.
3. Why does adding magnesium ions increase crop yield?
4. State how insecticides increase food production.
5. Explain why removing weeds increases the yield of a crop.
6. Give one example of selective breeding improving production in livestock.
7. Describe what is meant by a monoculture.
8. Give two advantages of a large-scale monoculture.
9. Explain why pests spread easily in a monoculture.
10. Explain why intensively farmed pigs grow faster than free-range pigs.
11. Give two disadvantages of intensive livestock production.
12. A farmer's barley plants have pale leaves and pests have damaged the grain. Suggest two methods to improve the next harvest.

### Answers

1. It allows larger areas of land to be farmed and improves efficiency.
2. Nitrate.
3. Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll, so more photosynthesis and growth.
4. They kill insect pests, improving quality and yield.
5. Weeds compete with the crop for light, water and mineral ions; without them the crop gets more of each.
6. Any one: cows bred for higher milk yield; chickens bred to grow faster or lay more eggs; pigs bred for more meat.
7. A large area of land growing a single crop variety.
8. Any two: efficient use of machinery; conditions tailored to one crop; large yield at low cost; uniform crop.
9. The pest's food plant is everywhere and plants are genetically similar, so a pest or disease can move from plant to plant.
10. They move less and are kept warm, so less energy is lost in respiration for movement and heat, leaving more for growth.
11. Any two: disease spreads quickly; waste pollutes water; poor welfare; large amounts of feed needed; energy needed for heating and lighting.
12. Chemical fertiliser (nitrate, magnesium) for the pale leaves; insecticide for the pests.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping insecticides and herbicides.
- Writing "fertilisers help plants grow" with no ion or yield.
- Giving only advantages when disadvantages are asked for too.
- Not linking intensive livestock growth to reduced energy loss.
- Forgetting biodiversity as a disadvantage of monocultures.
