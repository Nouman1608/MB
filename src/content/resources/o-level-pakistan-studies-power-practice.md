**This page gives two full Paper 2 style questions on Section 3 Power, each worth 25 marks and
split into parts (a) to (d) like the real 2059 paper.** Allow about 30 minutes per question, which
is the time you have in the exam. Part (d) of each question asks you to analyse and judge, so
write a conclusion. Figures in the resources are illustrative, not official statistics.

## Question 1: Fuels and their transport

**Fig. 1.1** (described): a sketch map of Pakistan showing the Thar coalfield in south-east Sindh,
the Sui gas field in Balochistan, oil fields on the Potwar Plateau, a pipeline from Port Qasim to
Mahmood Kot, and the cities Karachi, Multan, Lahore, Quetta and Peshawar.

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 1.1, name the province where the Sui gas field is found. **[1]**

**(ii)** Name **one** oil field on the Potwar Plateau. **[1]**

**(iii)** Draw a labelled diagram to show how coal is obtained by open cast mining. **[3]**

**(b) (i)** Explain why most coal from Thar is used to generate electricity close to the mine.
**[3]**

**(ii)** Describe how imported oil reaches users in northern Punjab. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain how gas is supplied to villages that are far from the pipeline network, and the
problems of doing this. **[5]**

**(d)** Pakistan's domestic gas output is falling while demand rises. Evaluate **two** ways in
which Pakistan could meet the shortfall, and decide which is better. **[9]**

## Question 2: Electricity generation

**Fig. 2.1** (illustrative data): share of electricity generated by source in a recent year.

| Source | Share (%) |

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 2.1, state the percentage of electricity generated from renewable sources.
**[1]**

**(ii)** Name **one** nuclear power station site in Pakistan. **[1]**

**(iii)** State **two** differences between a renewable and a non-renewable source of
electricity. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a high share of imported fuel in Fig. 2.1 is a problem for Pakistan's economy.
**[4]**

**(c) (i)** Describe the physical conditions that favour the building of a multi-purpose hydel

**(ii)** Explain why hydel output falls in winter. **[2]**

**(d)** A village in the mountains of Chitral has no grid connection. Assess whether a micro-hydel
scheme or solar home systems would be the more feasible way to bring electricity to it. **[12]**

## Mark scheme: Question 1

**(a) (i)** Balochistan [1].

**(ii)** Any one of Khaur, Dhulian, Toot, Meyal or another correctly named Potwar field [1].

**(iii)** One mark each, up to 3: ground surface with overburden shown being removed; coal seam
near the surface exposed in a pit; benches or excavator/dumper shown; spoil heap of overburden.

**(b) (i)** Thar coal is lignite [1] with high moisture and ash and low heat value [1], so carrying
it long distances is uneconomic; burning it at mine-mouth power stations and sending electricity by
transmission line is cheaper [1]. Also credit: no rail line suited to bulk coal across the desert
[1].

**(ii)** Crude oil or products arrive by tanker at Karachi or Port Qasim [1]; refined fuel is
pumped through the White Oil Pipeline to Mahmood Kot near Multan [1], then carried onwards by rail
tank wagons or road tankers to depots in northern Punjab [1]. Credit refining at Karachi or at
Mahmood Kot in the chain.

**(c)** Up to 3 for method: gas is bottled as LPG [1]; cylinders are taken by truck to local
dealers [1]; households collect or have them delivered [1]. Up to 3 for problems: cost per unit
is higher than piped gas [1]; supply runs short in winter when demand peaks [1]; poor roads in
mountains and deserts delay delivery [1]; leaks and explosions are a safety risk [1]. Maximum 5.

**(d)** Levels marking.

- **Level 3 (7–9):** evaluates two developed options with Pakistani detail and reaches a
  supported judgement.
- **Level 2 (4–6):** explains two options with some evaluation, or one option well.
- **Level 1 (1–3):** simple statements.

Possible content. **Import more LNG:** quick to scale through the Port Qasim terminals and uses
the existing pipelines; but costs foreign exchange, price swings with world markets and worsens the
trade deficit. **Explore and drill new fields:** keeps money at home and creates jobs; but drilling
is costly and slow, success is uncertain and some prospects lie in insecure or remote areas.
**Switch demand to other energy:** solar water heaters, electric cooking, Thar coal for power
stations, freeing gas for homes and fertiliser. A good judgement might be that LNG fills the gap
now while exploration and renewables reduce dependence later.

## Mark scheme: Question 2

**(a) (i)** 33% (hydel 28 + wind and solar 5) [1].

**(ii)** Chashma or Karachi (K-2/K-3) [1].

**(iii)** Any two: renewables are naturally replaced / non-renewables run out [1]; renewables have
no fuel cost / non-renewables need fuel to be bought [1]; renewables produce little or no carbon
dioxide in use [1].

**(b)** Any four developed points: imports must be paid in foreign currency [1], widening the
trade deficit [1]; world oil and gas prices rise and fall, so generation costs are unpredictable
[1]; a falling rupee makes each unit dearer [1]; high electricity prices raise industrial costs and
reduce competitiveness of exports [1]; unpaid fuel bills feed circular debt [1].

**(c) (i)** Any three: a narrow, deep gorge so the dam can be short [1]; hard, impermeable rock
for foundations [1]; a large, reliable river flow from snowmelt and monsoon rain [1]; a wide valley
upstream to store water [1]; low earthquake and landslide risk [1].

**(ii)** Snow and ice in the mountains do not melt in cold months [1], and monsoon rain has ended,
so river flow and the head of water behind dams are reduced [1].

**(d)** Levels marking (Level 3: 9–12; Level 2: 5–8; Level 1: 1–4).

**Micro-hydel for:** fast, steep streams fed by snowmelt in Chitral; low running cost; can run day
and night; villagers can be trained to maintain it. **Against:** flow falls in winter, exactly
when lighting and heating needs are highest; floods and landslides can damage the intake; needs
initial capital and a suitable site.

**Solar for:** panels are cheap, light and easy to carry up mountain roads; each home can be
supplied separately; no water rights needed. **Against:** short winter days, snow and cloud cut
output; batteries are costly and need replacing; output is small, so it suits lights and phones
more than workshops.

A Level 3 answer weighs these for the specific place and decides, for example that micro-hydel is
more feasible where a reliable stream exists because it gives more power for small industry, with
solar as a back-up in winter.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should I spend on each Paper 2 question?**
About 30 minutes, since you answer three questions in 1 hour 30 minutes. Leave enough time for
part (d), the part that specifically tests analysis.

**What does "assess" mean in 2059 Paper 2?**
Weigh up the factors for and against a proposal and come to a judgement, according to the
syllabus glossary.

**Will the exam give me real statistics?**
Usually it gives a resource such as a table or graph. Use the figures provided, quoting them
accurately, rather than numbers from memory.
