## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student tests a solution for chloride ions.

**(a)** Describe the test, including the reagents in the order they are added. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why dilute hydrochloric acid must **not** be used to acidify the solution. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the solution is acidified before the second reagent is added. **[1]**

**8.** Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added gradually, until in excess, to separate solutions of three salts. Complete the table. **[3]**

| Cation | Observation with a few drops | Observation in excess |
|---|---|---|
| iron(II) | ...... | ...... |
| zinc | ...... | ...... |
| calcium | ...... | ...... |

**9.** Two colourless solutions each contain one of the cations Al³⁺ and Zn²⁺. Both give a white precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide that dissolves in excess.

Describe a test that distinguishes them, giving the result for each. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how to show that a solid contains nitrate ions, given that it contains no ammonium ions. **[3]**

**11.** A gas is bubbled through acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII).

**(a)** The solution turns from purple to colourless. Name the gas. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the anion that gives the same colour change when its solution is added to acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII). **[1]**

**12.** Solid Y is tested.

- Test 1: a flame test gives a lilac flame.
- Test 2: dilute hydrochloric acid is added; effervescence occurs, and the gas turns limewater milky.

**(a)** Identify the cation and anion in Y. **[2]**

**(b)** Name Y. **[1]**

**13.** Solution Z is tested.

- Aqueous sodium hydroxide: green precipitate, which dissolves in excess.
- Aqueous ammonia: green precipitate, which does not dissolve in excess.
- Dilute nitric acid, then aqueous barium nitrate: white precipitate.

**(a)** Identify the cation and anion present. **[2]**

**(b)** *(Extended)* Write an ionic equation, with state symbols, for the formation of the white precipitate. **[1]**

**(c)** A different solution gives a green precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide that does **not** dissolve in excess and turns brown at the surface on standing. Identify its cation. **[1]**

**14.** A student collects a colourless gas. It relights a glowing splint. Name the gas, and give the result of a lighted splint test on it if the student had wrongly assumed it was hydrogen. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]; it gives a squeaky "pop" [1].

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** Yellow [1].

**4.** **(a)** Red [1].

**(b)** Light green [1].

**(c)** Blue-green [1].

**5.** Ammonia [1].

**6.** **C** [1].

**7.** **(a)** Acidify with dilute nitric acid [1], then add aqueous silver nitrate; a white precipitate shows chloride [1].

**(b)** Hydrochloric acid contains chloride ions, so a white precipitate would form even if the solution had no chloride [1].

**(c)** To remove carbonate ions, which would otherwise also give a precipitate with silver nitrate [1].

**8.** One mark per correct row [3]:

- iron(II): green precipitate; insoluble in excess (turns brown near the surface on standing);
- zinc: white precipitate; dissolves in excess to give a colourless solution;
- calcium: white precipitate; insoluble in excess.

**9.** Add aqueous ammonia, a little at a time and then in excess [1].
*Excess sodium hydroxide cannot be the deciding test, because both precipitates dissolve in it.*
 Zn²⁺: white precipitate that dissolves in excess to give a colourless solution [1]. Al³⁺: white precipitate that does not dissolve in excess [1].

**10.** Dissolve the solid in water and add aqueous sodium hydroxide [1]; add aluminium foil and warm carefully [1]; ammonia is given off, which turns damp red litmus paper blue [1].

**11.** **(a)** Sulfur dioxide [1].

**(b)** Sulfite, SO₃²⁻ [1].

**12.** **(a)** Cation: potassium, K⁺ [1]. Anion: carbonate, CO₃²⁻ [1].
*Lilac is the potassium flame colour; effervescence with acid and a gas that turns limewater milky show carbon dioxide, so the anion is carbonate.*

**(b)** Potassium carbonate [1].

**13.** **(a)** Cation: chromium(III), Cr³⁺ [1]. Anion: sulfate, SO₄²⁻ [1].
*The green precipitate dissolving in excess sodium hydroxide rules out iron(II).*

**(b)** Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) [1].

**(c)** Iron(II), Fe²⁺ [1].

**14.** Oxygen [1]. A lighted splint would not pop; it would burn more brightly [1].
*Accept any answer that the splint does not give a pop.*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using hydrochloric or sulfuric acid to acidify in the halide or sulfate tests.
- Describing the halide precipitates as "white", "off-white" and "light yellow" instead of white, cream and yellow.
- Leaving out what happens in excess for cations, which is often the deciding observation.
- Forgetting to say "damp" for litmus, or "warm" for ammonium and nitrate tests.
- Swapping glowing and lighted splints.
- Writing "purple" instead of "lilac" for the potassium flame colour.
