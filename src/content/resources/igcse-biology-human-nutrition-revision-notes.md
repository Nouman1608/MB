Topic 7 spans five sub-topics (7.1-7.5) and is examined on every tier
— get the Core statements completely secure first, since Supplement
content builds directly on top of them rather than replacing them.

## 7.1 Diet — all Core, no Supplement content

A **balanced diet** contains the right foods in the right amounts.
Know the principal dietary source and importance of each nutrient:

| Nutrient | Importance |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Energy |
| Fats and oils | Energy store, insulation |
| Proteins | Growth and repair |
| Vitamin C | Deficiency causes **scurvy** |
| Vitamin D | Deficiency causes **rickets** |
| Calcium | Bones and teeth |
| Iron | Haemoglobin production |
| Fibre (roughage) | Aids movement of food through the gut |
| Water | Solvent, transport medium |

Only vitamins C and D and mineral ions calcium and iron are
syllabus-specified — do not spend revision time on other vitamins or
minerals for this topic; questions naming scurvy or rickets are asking
specifically for a vitamin deficiency link, not a general "eat
healthily" answer.

## 7.2 Digestive system — naming and function

**Alimentary canal** (Core): mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small
intestine (duodenum and ileum), large intestine (colon, rectum, anus).

**Associated organs** (Core): salivary glands, pancreas, liver, gall
bladder.

Five processes to define precisely, not just name:

- **Ingestion** — taking food/drink into the body.
- **Digestion** — breakdown of food.
- **Absorption** — movement of nutrients from intestines into blood.
- **Assimilation** — uptake and use of nutrients by cells.
- **Egestion** — removal of undigested food as faeces.

A common exam-trap confusion: **egestion is not excretion**. Egestion
removes food that was never absorbed into the body; excretion (a
different topic) removes waste made by the body's own metabolism.

## 7.3 Physical digestion — mechanical breakdown

**Physical digestion** (Core) breaks food into smaller pieces
**without chemical change** to the molecules — this increases surface
area for enzymes to act on in chemical digestion.

- Teeth types (Core): incisors, canines, premolars, molars — know
their structure (enamel, dentine, pulp, nerves, blood vessels,
cement) and function in physical digestion.
- Stomach's role in physical digestion (Core): churning food.
- **Supplement only**: bile's role in **emulsifying** fats and oils —
breaking large fat droplets into smaller ones to increase surface
area for lipase, without any chemical change to the fat itself (bile
is not an enzyme).

## 7.4 Chemical digestion — enzymes and their exact roles

**Chemical digestion** (Core) breaks large insoluble molecules into
small soluble molecules that can be absorbed.

| Enzyme | Substrate → Product |
|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch → simple reducing sugars |
| Proteases | Protein → amino acids |
| Lipase | Fats and oils → fatty acids and glycerol |

**Hydrochloric acid in gastric juice** (Core) has two distinct
functions — kills harmful microorganisms in food, AND provides
optimum acidic pH for enzyme activity. Both functions are
examinable separately; naming only one loses marks.

**Supplement adds precision**:

- Amylase breaks starch to **maltose** specifically (not glucose
directly); maltase then breaks maltose to glucose at the small
intestine's epithelium membranes.
- **Pepsin** breaks down protein in the stomach's acidic conditions;
**trypsin** breaks down protein in the small intestine's alkaline
conditions — same job (protease), opposite pH environments.
- Bile is **alkaline** and neutralises acidic stomach contents
entering the duodenum, creating a suitable pH for the small
intestine's own enzymes to work.

## 7.5 Absorption — where and how nutrients enter the blood

**Core**: the small intestine is where nutrients are absorbed; most
water is absorbed from the small intestine, with some also absorbed
from the colon.

**Supplement**: the small intestine's absorbing surface area is
increased by **villi and microvilli**. Know the structure of a
villus and the distinct roles of its two internal transport routes:

- **Capillaries** absorb sugars and amino acids.
- **Lacteals** (part of the lymphatic system) absorb fatty acids and
glycerol (fat digestion products) specifically — not sugars or
amino acids.

## Exam traps

- Confusing egestion (undigested food leaving the body) with
excretion (metabolic waste removal) — these are different processes
in different topics.
- Naming only one function of hydrochloric acid in the stomach when
the syllabus requires both (killing microorganisms and providing
optimum pH).
- Stating physical digestion involves a chemical change to food
molecules — it does not; only chemical digestion does.
- Mixing up pepsin and trypsin's pH environments (pepsin = acidic
stomach; trypsin = alkaline small intestine).
- Assuming capillaries and lacteals absorb the same nutrients — they
specialise in different digestion products.

## Self-test

1. Which two vitamins and which two mineral ions does this topic
specify, and what deficiency disease is linked to each vitamin?
2. Define egestion and explain how it differs from excretion.
3. What does bile do to fats, and why is this not chemical digestion?
4. Name the two functions of hydrochloric acid in gastric juice.
5. What is the key difference between what capillaries and lacteals
absorb in a villus?

**Answers:** 1. Vitamin C (scurvy) and Vitamin D (rickets); calcium
and iron. 2. Egestion is the removal of undigested food from the body
as faeces; excretion removes waste substances made by the body's own
metabolism, which is a different process entirely. 3. Bile emulsifies
fats and oils, breaking large droplets into smaller ones to increase
surface area for lipase — this is a physical change, not a chemical
one, since the fat molecules themselves are unchanged. 4. Killing
harmful microorganisms in food, and providing an acidic pH for
optimum enzyme activity. 5. Capillaries absorb sugars and amino
acids; lacteals absorb fatty acids and glycerol.
