## Answer pattern

The syllabus tells candidates to use the marks for each question as a guide to the length of answer. For each point, give: the term, what it means, how it shows up in this business, and the effect on the figures or the people affected.

## 7.1 Spot the concept

| Clue in the question | Concept | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| Owner's personal bill paid by the business | Business entity | Record as drawings |
| Same method as last year | Consistency | Do not change without good reason |
| Debit and credit of equal value | Duality | Both sides recorded |
| Business expected to carry on trading | Going concern | Spread asset cost over useful life |
| Price actually paid | Historic cost | Do not record a rise in market value |
| Cost belongs to this year though paid next year | Matching / accruals | Accrue or prepay |
| Tiny item, little effect on decisions | Materiality | Simple treatment, such as expense |
| Staff skill, reputation | Money measurement | Not recorded |
| Expected loss, possible gain | Prudence | Recognise loss now, gain when earned |
| Goods supplied, customer owes payment | Realisation | Revenue earned now |

## Concepts behind procedures

- Accruals, prepayments and depreciation come from matching / accruals.
- Allowances for irrecoverable debts and the inventory rule come from prudence.
- Drawings come from business entity; double entry comes from duality.
- Cost less depreciation comes from historic cost and going concern.

## Method boxes

**Accrual or prepayment, step by step**
1. Find the period the payment covers.
2. Work out the share that falls in this year (months used / months paid for).
3. Expense = share in this year. Any remainder is prepaid. Any unpaid cost for this year is accrued.
4. Show accruals and prepayments as the syllabus names them in the statement of financial position (other payables, other receivables).

Reminder: annual insurance of 2,400 paid on 1 October for 12 months, year end 31 December. Used = 2,400 x 3/12 = 600. Prepaid = 2,400 - 600 = 1,800.

Reminder: electricity paid in the year is 5,200, including 400 for last year's final bill, and 650 is owed at the year end. Expense = 5,200 - 400 + 650 = 5,450.

**Inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value**
1. Total cost for the item or line.
2. Total net realisable value (selling price less costs of selling).
3. Use the lower. The difference reduces profit.

Reminder: 18 units cost 25 each and could net 21 each. Cost 450; net realisable value 378; use 378; profit falls by 72.

**Allowance for irrecoverable debts**
1. Write off debts known to be irrecoverable first.
2. Apply the percentage to the remaining trade receivables.
3. Compare with the existing allowance. Charge the increase; credit back a decrease.

Reminder: trade receivables 8,000 less 300 written off = 7,700. Allowance at 4% = 308. Existing allowance 250, so charge 58.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Matching and realisation | Matching assigns costs and revenue to the right period; realisation says when revenue counts as earned |
| Prudence and materiality | Prudence stops profit being overstated; materiality only simplifies small items |
| Historic cost and going concern | Cost is the starting value; going concern is why cost can be spread by depreciation |
| Integrity and objectivity | Integrity is honesty; objectivity is freedom from bias and pressure |
| Confidentiality and professional behaviour | One protects information; the other covers laws and reputation |
| Safe and sustainable storage | Safe protects the data; sustainable limits waste and energy while keeping records usable |

## 7.2 Ethical considerations

**Need:** others rely on figures they cannot check; pressure to change profit; access to private information; a standard set in advance.

| Principle | Cue |
|---|---|
| Integrity | Honest, no misleading figures |
| Objectivity | No bias, conflicts or pressure |
| Professional competence and due care | Skills kept current, careful work |
| Confidentiality | No disclosure or misuse of information |
| Professional behaviour | Laws followed, profession's reputation protected |

**Significance (stakeholders):** owners and investors, lenders, suppliers, employees, tax authority. **Society:** public funds from tax, trust in financial information, jobs and lending decisions that rest on honest figures.

**Worked reminder:** draft profit is 18,400 after a 640 personal holiday was charged to travel expenses. Business entity makes it drawings, so profit is 18,400 + 640 = 19,040 and drawings rise by 640. Leaving it as an expense to cut tax would also challenge integrity and professional behaviour.

**Applying it to a dilemma:** state the pressure, name the concept being broken, name two principles with a reason each, quantify the overstatement or understatement, say who is harmed, then give a proper course of action.

## 7.3 Technology and sustainability

- **Digital applications:** may be used for all or part of the records. Faster posting and totals and fewer arithmetic slips, but cost, training and the same input error carried forward. No named software is needed.
- **Store safely:** complete, accurate, available and protected from loss and unauthorised access.
- **Store sustainably:** print less, avoid duplicate copies, keep formats readable, dispose of old devices responsibly once data is removed.
- **Risks of poor storage:** data loss, hacking or fraud, exposure of confidential information, penalties, cost of rebuilding records, loss of trust, waste.

| Storage system | Main advantage | Main disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Manual | No power or internet needed | Bulky and slow to search or copy |
| Data storage devices | Fast, large capacity, business holds the data | Small devices lost or fail; back-ups needed |
| Cloud services | Remote access; provider keeps equipment | Needs internet; continuing cost; third party |
| Other digital services | Records created automatically | Data scattered; depends on each service |

Most businesses combine methods. Working copies on a data storage device, a second copy in a cloud service and paper originals of key documents survive more events than a single method. Name a type, give an advantage, give a disadvantage, then link to the business described.

## Quick self-test

1. Which concept requires wages for the last week of December to be included in this year's expenses although they are paid in January?
2. A sale is agreed on 31 December and the goods are delivered on 2 January. In which year is the revenue recorded?
3. An accountant uses supplier prices seen in a client's records to negotiate a deal for herself. Which principle does she breach?
4. Annual insurance of 2,400 is paid on 1 October for 12 months. How much is prepaid at 31 December?
5. Eighteen units cost 25 each and have a net realisable value of 21 each. At what value are they included in inventory?
6. Shop fittings cost 9,000 and accumulated depreciation is 3,600. A replacement would now cost 11,000. At what amount are they shown?
7. Name the four types of storage system in 7.3.
8. Give one risk of keeping the only copy of the records on a small data storage device.
9. State one way using digital invoices can improve sustainability.
10. Which two principles are most directly challenged when an owner pressures an accountant to change profit?

### Answers

1. Matching / accruals.
2. The following year, because the goods have not passed to the customer (realisation).
3. Confidentiality.
4. 1,800.
5. 378, the lower of cost (450) and net realisable value (378).
6. 5,400 (9,000 - 3,600); the replacement cost is not used (historic cost).
7. Manual, data storage devices, cloud services, other digital services.
8. For example loss, theft, damage or device failure, so the records could not be recovered.
9. For example less paper and printing, or less physical storage space.
10. Integrity and objectivity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a definition of a concept but never applying it to the figures given.
- Mixing up matching and realisation.
- Saying prudence means reducing profit on purpose.
- Using market or replacement value where historic cost applies.
- Calculating a prepayment using the wrong number of months.
- Naming a principle without saying how it applies to the situation.
- Giving advantages of a storage system but no disadvantage when the question asks to discuss.
- Naming a software package, which the syllabus does not require.
