## Section A

**1.** Name **four** family structures identified in the syllabus. **[4]**

**2.** Define the "hidden curriculum" and give **one** example. **[3]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain **two** differences between the New Right and Postmodernist views of family diversity. **[6]**

**4.** Explain **three** ways an ageing population is changing family life. **[9]**

**5.** Distinguish between crime and deviance, giving **one** example of behaviour that is deviant but not criminal. **[6]**

**6.** Explain **three** material, cultural or in-school factors used to explain differences in educational achievement. **[9]**

## Section C

**7.** Explain the functionalist and Marxist views of the family's role in society, and identify **one** criticism of either view. **[8]**

**8.** Distinguish between formal and informal agencies of social control, and explain **two** ways an informal agency may prevent deviance. **[8]**

**9.** Explain how feminist sociologists have analysed gender and the hidden curriculum in schools. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why white-collar crime may be under-recorded compared with other crime types. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four: **nuclear**, **extended**, **reconstituted**, **lone parent**, **empty nest**, **childless** [1] [1] [1] [1].

**2.** The **hidden curriculum** is the set of **norms, values, attitudes and behaviours (such as punctuality and conformity) that schools teach without naming them as formal lessons** [1] [1]. Example: **school uniform policies**, which teach conformity and acceptance of authority without being taught as a subject [1].

**3.** The **New Right treats family diversity as a sign of social dysfunction**, arguing the traditional nuclear family is the ideal structure and that alternatives weaken social stability [1] [1]. **Postmodernists treat diversity positively**, viewing the variety of family forms as evidence of greater individual choice and freedom rather than decline [1] [1]. (Accept any two clearly contrasted points to this effect, 3 marks each.)

**4.** Any three, 3 marks each: **falling birth rates combined with rising life expectancy** produce an older population overall, changing the balance of family generations alive at once [1] [1] [1]. This creates the **"sandwich generation"** — adults caring simultaneously for ageing parents and their own children [1] [1] [1]. It also creates **"boomerang children"** who return to the family home as adults, and changes grandparent roles, with grandparents increasingly providing regular childcare [1] [1] [1].

**5. Crime** is behaviour that **breaks the law** [1]; **deviance** is behaviour that **breaks social norms** [1]. The two overlap but are not identical, and both are **relative across societies, cultures and time periods** [1] [1]. Example of deviant but not criminal behaviour: **any reasonable example**, such as **not queuing when expected to, or unconventional dress** — behaviour that breaks a social norm without breaking any law [1] [1].

**6.** Any three, 3 marks each, correctly categorised: **Material** — e.g. **unequal access to digital technology or study resources at home**, disadvantaging lower-income students [1] [1] [1]. **Cultural** — e.g. **cultural capital**, where some families possess knowledge, vocabulary and values that align more closely with what schools value and reward [1] [1] [1]; or **linguistic factors**, where the type of language used at home may or may not match the "elaborated code" schools expect. **In-school** — e.g. **teacher labelling**, where expectations placed on a student shape their subsequent performance, and **pro-school or anti-school subcultures**, where peer groups reinforce or resist academic engagement [1] [1] [1].

**7. Functionalism** views the family as performing essential functions for society and its members, including **emotional stabilisation of adult personalities** (the "warm bath" theory), alongside socialisation of children into shared norms and values [1] [1]. **Marxism** views the family as a **unit of consumption** that sustains capitalism, both by consuming goods and by reproducing a compliant workforce for the next generation [1] [1]. Criticism (either view, 4 marks): functionalism can be criticised for **assuming the nuclear family benefits all members equally**, ignoring conflict and inequality within families (e.g. an unequal domestic division of labour) [1] [1] [1] [1]; or Marxism can be criticised for **underestimating the genuine emotional and supportive functions** families provide that are not reducible to economic interests [1] [1] [1] [1].

**8. Formal agencies of social control** are official bodies with the power to punish, such as **police, courts, the armed forces, government and the penal system** [1] [1]. **Informal agencies** exert control through **socialisation and social pressure rather than legal punishment**, such as family, education, the workplace, peer group, media and religion [1] [1]. Any two ways an informal agency prevents deviance, 2 marks each: **the family socialises children into shared norms and values from an early age**, reducing the likelihood of later rule-breaking [1] [1]; **peer group disapproval discourages deviant behaviour** through fear of social exclusion or reputational damage [1] [1]; **religion provides a moral code reinforced by community expectation** rather than legal sanction [1] [1].

**9.** Feminist sociologists argue schools historically transmitted a **patriarchal curriculum**, channelling girls and boys toward different subjects and reinforcing traditional gender roles through both curriculum content and everyday classroom interaction [1] [1] [1]. This is linked to **gendered subject choice**, where girls remain under-represented in some STEM subjects and boys in some humanities/language subjects, a pattern feminists argue reflects socialisation rather than innate ability [1] [1] [1].

**10.** Any two, 3 marks each: white-collar crime is often committed by **individuals in positions of trust or authority**, making detection harder since there is less direct oversight of their actions [1] [1] [1]. Such crimes are frequently **complex or technical**, making them difficult for both the public and investigators to recognise as crime in the first place, and offences may be **dealt with internally by an organisation** rather than reported to police, further reducing official statistics [1] [1] [1].
