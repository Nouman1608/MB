## Section A

**1.** Name the highest mountain in the world. **[1]**

**2.** Name the two rivers that flow through Iraq and join before reaching the Persian Gulf. **[2]**

**3.** What is a plateau? **[1]**

**4.** Name the sea into which the Yellow River flows. **[1]**

**5.** Name **one** delta in South-East Asia. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the location of the Himalayas. **[3]**

**7.** Describe the course of the Yangtze river from its source to its mouth. **[4]**

**8.** The table gives approximate data for three major Asian rivers.

| River | Source | Mouth | Length (km) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yangtze | Tibetan Plateau | East China Sea | 6 300 |
| Yellow River | Tibetan Plateau | Bohai Sea | 5 460 |
| Mekong | Tibetan Plateau | South China Sea | 4 350 |

**(a)** Calculate how much longer the Yangtze is than the Mekong. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify **one** similarity in the location of these three rivers. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why many large rivers in Asia rise on the Tibetan Plateau. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the main upland and lowland areas of Iran or Turkey. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why the North China Plain is densely populated. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why the Tigris and Euphrates are important to the people of Iraq. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why the landscapes of the Middle and Far East are so varied. **[6]**

**13.** "Rivers are more important than mountains in determining where people live in the Middle and Far East." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Mount Everest [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The Tigris [1] and the Euphrates [1]. (They join as the Shatt al-Arab.) **[2]**

**3.** A large area of high land with a flat or gently sloping top [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The Bohai Sea (accept Yellow Sea, in the north-east of China) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any one from: Mekong Delta; Red River Delta; Irrawaddy Delta; Chao Phraya delta [1]. **[1]**

**6.** They lie along the northern edge of South Asia (India, Nepal and Bhutan) [1], to the south of the Tibetan Plateau [1]. They form part of a long arc of mountains stretching from the north-west to the south-east, with the highest peaks in the central section (accept: Nepal and Tibet border) [1]. **[3]**

**7.** The Yangtze rises on the Tibetan Plateau in the west of China [1]. It flows east through deep valleys in the mountains [1], then through the Sichuan Basin and the lowlands of central China [1]. It reaches the East China Sea near Shanghai, where it forms an estuary and delta [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** 6 300 - 4 350 = 1 950 km [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** They all rise on the Tibetan Plateau [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** The plateau is very high, so snow and glaciers store water and melt to feed rivers [1]. It is surrounded by lower land, so rivers flow downhill away from it in different directions [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Iran: the Zagros Mountains along the west, the Elburz Mountains in the north (with Mount Damavand), and the Iranian plateau in the centre, with large desert basins in the interior [up to 2 marks]. Lowlands around the Caspian coast and along the Persian Gulf [1]. Turkey: the Anatolian Plateau in the centre ringed by the Pontic mountains in the north and Taurus mountains in the south [up to 2 marks], with narrow coastal plains [1]. Marks are awarded for either country, up to a maximum of 4. **[4]**

**10.** The land is flat and fertile because it is built from silt carried by the Yellow River [1]. Flat land and water supply allow intensive farming, so many people can be fed [1]. Flat land makes it easy to build cities, roads and railways [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The rivers provide water for drinking and irrigation in a dry country [1]. Floods deposit fertile silt on the Mesopotamian plain, so farming is productive [1]. They provide transport routes and fish [1]. Their water supports towns and cities, but countries upstream such as Turkey can control the flow [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Plate movement has raised mountains and plateaus, for example the Himalayas where the Indian and Eurasian plates collided [1]. Plate margins around the Pacific have produced volcanic islands and mountain chains such as Japan [1]. Rivers have deposited sediment to create plains and deltas, for example the North China Plain and the Mekong Delta [1]. Climate varies from desert in Arabia and the Gobi to wet monsoon and tropical forests [1]. The region is very large and stretches across many latitudes, so relief and climate differ [1]. Examples and place names support each point [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: rivers supply water, silt and transport, so the largest populations live in basins, plains and deltas such as the Yangtze, Mekong and Tigris-Euphrates [1].
- Agree: in dry regions, such as Iraq, almost all settlement follows rivers [1].
- Disagree: mountains affect climate, rainfall and temperature, and some people live in uplands because of the cooler weather or resources [1].
- Disagree: mountains and deserts limit settlement and restrict movement; for example, the Himalayas and Rub' al Khali are lightly populated [1].
- Disagree: other factors, such as coasts, resources and trade, also matter [1].
- Judgement: rivers are generally the stronger attraction, since they bring water and fertile land, but uplands shape where rivers go and what the climate is; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a river without a source, direction or mouth.
- Confusing the Tigris with the Euphrates, or the Yangtze with the Yellow River.
- Giving locations without a compass direction or a neighbouring feature.
- Calling the Tibetan Plateau a mountain range.
- Mixing the physical reasons (plate movement, rivers) with human reasons without linking them.
- Judgements without a reasoned conclusion.
