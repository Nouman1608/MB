This study guide teaches subtopic 5.4, Surface area and volume, from topic 5 (Mensuration) of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The learning outcome is word-for-word the same in both columns: C5.4 (Core) and E5.4 (Extended) both ask you to carry out calculations and solve problems involving the surface area and volume of a cuboid, prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone. Core candidates take Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator); Extended candidates take Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). The examples below mix both kinds of paper. The one Extended-only step on this page is finding a length inside a solid with Pythagoras in three dimensions (E6.6), and it is labelled where it appears.

Use this page with the [Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics hub](/boards/cambridge/igcse/mathematics/) and the [printable 0580 checklist](/checklists/cambridge/igcse/mathematics/). For an overview of the whole of topic 5, see the [Mensuration study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-mensuration/).

## What this subtopic covers

| 0580 reference | What you must be able to do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| C5.4 / E5.4 | Surface area and volume of a cuboid and a prism (any solid with a uniform cross-section, such as a cylindrical sector) | Core and Extended |
| C5.4 / E5.4 | Surface area and volume of a cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone | Core and Extended |
| C5.4 / E5.4 | Give answers in terms of π when asked | Core and Extended |
| E6.6 (linked) | Find a slant height inside a pyramid from its vertical height | Extended only |

Compound solids and parts of solids (a hemisphere, a frustum) belong to the next subtopic, 5.5, and are not taught here.

## What the List of formulas gives you

Page 2 of every 0580 paper has a List of formulas. For this subtopic it gives:

| Given on the paper | Formula |
|---|---|
| Curved surface area of a cylinder | A = 2πrh |
| Curved surface area of a cone (slant edge l) | A = πrl |
| Surface area of a sphere | A = 4πr² |
| Volume of a prism (cross-section A, length l) | V = Al |
| Volume of a pyramid (base area A, height h) | V = (1/3)Ah |
| Volume of a cylinder | V = πr²h |
| Volume of a cone | V = (1/3)πr²h |
| Volume of a sphere | V = (4/3)πr³ |

What is **not** given, so you must know it or build it:

- the volume and surface area of a cuboid (a cuboid is a prism, so V = Al still works);
- the surface area of any prism or pyramid (add up the faces);
- the **total** surface area of a cylinder or cone (add the circular ends to the curved surface);
- the areas of rectangles, trapezia and parallelograms used as faces or cross-sections (C5.2).

On Papers 3 and 4 use the π key or 3.142, and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. When a question asks for an exact answer, leave it in terms of π.

## Cuboids

For a cuboid with length l, width w and height h:

```
V  = l × w × h
SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)      (three pairs of equal rectangles)
```

**Worked example 1 (non-calculator).** A closed box is a cuboid 8 cm by 5 cm by 3.5 cm. Find its volume and surface area. Then find the surface area if the box has no lid.

```
V  = 8 × 5 × 3.5 = 40 × 3.5 = 140 cm³

Faces: 8 × 5 = 40, 8 × 3.5 = 28, 5 × 3.5 = 17.5
SA = 2(40 + 28 + 17.5) = 2 × 85.5 = 171 cm²

No lid: remove one 8 × 5 face
SA = 171 − 40 = 131 cm²
```

Questions also run backwards. If a cuboid has volume 252 cm³ and a base 7 cm by 6 cm, then 252 = 42 × h, so h = 6 cm.

## Prisms

A prism has the same cross-section all the way through. The syllabus defines a prism as any solid with a uniform cross-section, so a cuboid, a cylinder and a cylindrical sector all count.

```
V  = (area of cross-section) × length
SA = 2 × (area of cross-section) + (perimeter of cross-section) × length
```

**Worked example 2 (non-calculator).** A triangular prism has a right-angled triangle as its cross-section, with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. The prism is 15 cm long. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
Cross-section area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²
V = 24 × 15 = 360 cm³

Two triangular ends   = 2 × 24 = 48 cm²
Rectangular faces     = (6 + 8 + 10) × 15 = 24 × 15 = 360 cm²
SA = 48 + 360 = 408 cm²
```

Use the two shorter sides (the ones at the right angle) for the triangle's base and height, never the 10 cm side.

**Worked example 3 (calculator).** A block of cheese is a prism whose cross-section is a sector of a circle with radius 9 cm and angle 40°. The block is 20 cm long. Find (a) its volume, in terms of π and to 3 significant figures, and (b) its total surface area.

```
(a) Sector area = (40/360) × π × 9² = (1/9) × 81π = 9π cm²
    V = 9π × 20 = 180π cm³ = 565.48... = 565 cm³ (3 s.f.)

(b) Two sector ends  = 2 × 9π = 18π
    Arc length       = (40/360) × 2 × π × 9 = 2π
    Curved face      = 2π × 20 = 40π
    Two flat faces   = 2 × (9 × 20) = 360
    SA = 18π + 40π + 360 = 58π + 360 = 542.21... = 542 cm² (3 s.f.)
```

A cylindrical sector has five faces: two sectors, one curved face and two rectangles. Missing the rectangles is the usual error.

## Cylinders

```
V             = πr²h
Curved SA     = 2πrh
Total SA      = 2πrh + 2πr²   (closed cylinder: two circular ends)
```

**Worked example 4 (non-calculator, answers in terms of π).** A closed cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 8 cm. Find its volume, its curved surface area and its total surface area.

```
V         = π × 5² × 8 = 200π cm³
Curved SA = 2 × π × 5 × 8 = 80π cm²
Ends      = 2 × π × 5² = 50π cm²
Total SA  = 80π + 50π = 130π cm²
```

Collect like terms (80π + 50π), but do not multiply out π.

**Worked example 5 (calculator, working backwards with units).** A cylindrical tank has internal radius 18 cm and holds 45 litres when full. Find the height of the tank.

```
45 litres = 45 000 cm³        (1 litre = 1000 cm³)

π × 18² × h = 45 000
h = 45 000 ÷ (π × 324)
h = 44.209... = 44.2 cm (3 s.f.)
```

Convert the capacity first, so every quantity is in centimetres.

## Spheres

```
V  = (4/3)πr³
SA = 4πr²
```

**Worked example 6 (non-calculator).** A ball has radius 3 cm. Find its volume and surface area in terms of π.

```
V  = (4/3) × π × 3³ = (4/3) × 27π = 36π cm³
SA = 4 × π × 3² = 36π cm²
```

The numbers match only because r = 3; the units (cm³, cm²) do not.

**Worked example 7 (calculator, finding the radius).** A sphere has volume 900 cm³. Find its radius and then its surface area.

```
(4/3)πr³ = 900
r³ = 900 × 3 ÷ (4π) = 214.86...
r  = ∛214.86... = 5.989... = 5.99 cm (3 s.f.)

SA = 4 × π × 5.989...² = 450.79... = 451 cm² (3 s.f.)
```

Use the unrounded radius in the second step. The syllabus says that when you use an earlier answer later in a question, you should use the value before it was rounded.

## Pyramids

```
V  = (1/3) × base area × perpendicular height
SA = base area + area of each triangular face
```

The height in the volume formula is the **vertical** height from the apex to the base, not the height of a sloping face.

**Worked example 8 (non-calculator).** A pyramid has a square base of side 16 cm. Its apex is 15 cm vertically above the centre of the base. Each triangular face has a height (slant height) of 17 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
Base area = 16 × 16 = 256 cm²
V = (1/3) × 256 × 15 = 256 × 5 = 1280 cm³

Each face = ½ × 16 × 17 = 136 cm²
SA = 256 + 4 × 136 = 256 + 544 = 800 cm²
```

The vertical height (15 cm) goes in the volume; the slant height (17 cm) goes in the face areas.

**Extended only.** You may be given only the vertical height and have to find the slant height yourself. The apex, the centre of the base and the midpoint of a base edge form a right-angled triangle inside the solid, which is Pythagoras in three dimensions (E6.6):

```
slant height² = 15² + 8²      (8 = half of 16)
              = 225 + 64 = 289
slant height  = 17 cm
```

## Cones

```
V         = (1/3)πr²h          (h = perpendicular height)
Curved SA = πrl                (l = slant height)
Total SA  = πrl + πr²          (solid cone: add the circular base)
l² = r² + h²
```

The radius, perpendicular height and slant height form a right-angled triangle with l as the hypotenuse. If you are given one height and need the other, use Pythagoras on that triangle.

**Worked example 9 (calculator).** A solid cone has radius 4.5 cm and perpendicular height 9.8 cm. Find its slant height, its volume and its total surface area.

```
l² = 4.5² + 9.8² = 20.25 + 96.04 = 116.29
l  = 10.783... = 10.8 cm (3 s.f.)

V  = (1/3) × π × 4.5² × 9.8 = 207.81... = 208 cm³ (3 s.f.)

Total SA = π × 4.5 × 10.783... + π × 4.5²
         = 152.45... + 63.61...
         = 216.06... = 216 cm² (3 s.f.)
```

Keep l = 10.783... in your calculator for the surface area step.

**Worked example 10 (non-calculator, working backwards).** A cone has radius 6 cm and volume 96π cm³. Find its perpendicular height and its curved surface area in terms of π.

```
(1/3) × π × 6² × h = 96π
12πh = 96π
h = 8 cm

l² = 6² + 8² = 100,  l = 10 cm
Curved SA = π × 6 × 10 = 60π cm²
```

Dividing both sides by π early keeps the arithmetic simple.

## Solving problems with volume

"Solve problems" in the outcome includes questions where one solid is melted and recast, or a liquid is poured from one container into another. The volume stays the same, so you set two volumes equal or divide one by the other.

**Worked example 11 (calculator).** A metal cube of side 10 cm is melted down and made into spheres of radius 1.5 cm. No metal is wasted. How many complete spheres can be made?

```
Cube volume   = 10³ = 1000 cm³
Sphere volume = (4/3) × π × 1.5³ = 4.5π = 14.137... cm³

1000 ÷ 14.137... = 70.73...
Number of complete spheres = 70
```

Round **down**: the 71st sphere would need more metal than is left.

## Common errors

- **Using the slant height in a volume formula.** Both the pyramid and the cone use the perpendicular height for volume.
- **Giving the curved surface area when the total is asked.** The List of formulas gives only the curved part of a cylinder and a cone; add the circular end or ends yourself.
- **Forgetting the 1/3.** Pyramids and cones are one third of the matching prism or cylinder.
- **Missing faces of a prism.** Count every face, including the rectangles of a cylindrical sector.
- **Mixing units.** Convert litres to cm³ (or m to cm) before you substitute.
- **Writing π as 3.14 or rounding mid-way.** Use the π key or 3.142, and round only the final answer.
