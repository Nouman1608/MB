**What do these questions practise?** The three key developments of optional topic 3.2.3 in
OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236: globalisation and TNCs, transport networks, and
expanding communication and financial networks. Short items come first, followed by explain and
evaluate items in the style of Paper 2 Section C.

## Short answer

**1.** Define *globalisation*. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** characteristics of a transnational corporation. **[2]**

**3.** Name **three** seaports of Pakistan. **[3]**

**4.** State **one** reason why air transport is important in the north of Pakistan. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** examples of how consumer culture in Pakistan's cities has changed. **[2]**

**6.** What does CPEC stand for? **[1]**

## Explain

**7.** Explain **two** positive impacts of TNCs on Pakistan's economy. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why the road and rail network is less dense in Balochistan than in Punjab. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how dry ports benefit exporters in inland cities. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** challenges of expanding internet access in rural Pakistan. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how mobile wallets support economic development. **[3]**

**12.** Describe **two** negative impacts of TNCs on the environment or people of Pakistan. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why Gwadar has been chosen for development as a major port. **[3]**

## Evaluate

**14.** "CPEC will bring more benefits than costs to Pakistan." Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The increasing interconnection of countries through trade, investment, migration, technology
and culture [1].

**2.** Any two: operates in more than one country [1]; headquarters usually in a richer country [1];
very large investment and turnover [1]; global brands [1]; organises production and sales across
borders [1].

**3.** Karachi Port (Keamari), Port Qasim, Gwadar [3].

**4.** Roads through the mountains are slow and often blocked by landslides or snow, so flights to
towns such as Gilgit and Skardu save time [1].

**5.** Any two: growth of online shopping [1]; mobile payments [1]; popularity of global brands [1];
large shopping malls [1]; influence of social media on fashion and food [1].

**6.** China–Pakistan Economic Corridor [1].

**7.** Any two developed: create jobs [1], which raise incomes and spending [1]; bring technology and
training [1], which improve local skills and productivity [1]; pay taxes [1], which fund public
services [1]; increase exports [1], which earn foreign exchange [1].

**8.** Balochistan is mountainous and dry, with plateaux and deserts, so building costs are high [1];
landslides, flash floods and shifting sand raise maintenance costs [1]. Its population is small and
scattered, so demand is low [1]. Punjab is flat and densely populated, with farming and industry,
so routes are cheaper to build and heavily used [1].

**9.** Goods are cleared through customs inland, close to factories, which saves time [1]. Sealed
containers travel by rail or road to the seaport without further checks [1]. This reduces delays and
congestion at Karachi's ports and lowers costs for exporters [1].

**10.** Any two developed: mountainous or desert terrain raises the cost of masts and fibre cables
[1], and low population density means few customers to pay for them [1]; unreliable electricity [1]
makes masts and devices hard to run [1]; low incomes and digital literacy [1] limit use even where
coverage exists [1]; a gender gap in phone ownership [1] excludes many women [1].

**11.** Mobile wallets let people without bank accounts make and receive payments [1], so workers can
receive wages and remittances safely and small traders can sell more easily [1]. Government cash
transfers can be paid directly, reaching women and rural households, which increases financial
inclusion [1].

**12.** Any two developed: factories producing for global brands may discharge waste water or
smoke [1], polluting rivers and air near industrial areas [1]; heavy water use by bottling or textile
plants [1] can lower local water supplies [1]; low wages or long hours in some supplier factories [1]
harm workers' well-being [1]; local small businesses may close because they cannot compete [1],
causing job losses [1].

**13.** It is a deep-water port that can take very large ships [1]. It lies near the Strait of Hormuz
and major Gulf shipping routes [1]. It is the southern end of CPEC, giving China and Central Asia a
shorter route to the sea, and it reduces pressure on Karachi's congested ports [1].

**14.** Level-marked. Benefits: investment in motorways and railways reduces transport costs [1–2];
new power stations helped reduce load-shedding [1–2]; Gwadar and special economic zones may attract
industry and trade [1]; construction and operation jobs [1]. Costs: debt repayments and payments to
power producers strain public finances [1–2]; many contracts and skilled jobs go to Chinese firms [1];
security risks and questions over benefits for local people in Balochistan [1–2]; environmental
impacts, including coal-fired power plants [1]. Top-level answers reach a supported judgement, for
example that long-term benefits depend on Pakistan using the infrastructure to raise exports. Maximum
9.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much detail should a 9-mark answer have?**
Several developed points on each side, specific
examples (named routes, ports or projects), and a conclusion that weighs them.

**Can I use CPEC in answers on transport and on globalisation?**
Yes. It is foreign direct investment
as well as a transport and energy programme.
