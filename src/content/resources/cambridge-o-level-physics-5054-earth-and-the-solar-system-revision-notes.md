Condensed recall for section **6.1 Earth and the Solar System**, Cambridge O Level Physics 5054, 2026–2028 series. For explanations and worked examples, read the [Earth and the Solar System study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-physics-5054-earth-and-the-solar-system/) first; for exam-style questions, use the [Earth and the Solar System practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-physics-5054-earth-and-the-solar-system-practice/).

## The five Earth facts (6.1.1)

| Fact | Value to quote | What kind of quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Earth orbits the Sun | about 365 days | orbital period |
| Shape of Earth's orbit | ellipse, approximately circular | why r is an *average* radius |
| Earth rotates on its axis | about 24 hours; the axis is tilted | rotation period, not an orbit |
| Moon orbits the Earth | about one month | orbital period of the Moon |
| Sunlight reaches the Earth | about 500 s | light travel time |

Speed of light in a vacuum: **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s** (from section 3.3). Sun–Earth distance from the light time: 3.0 × 10⁸ × 500 = **1.5 × 10¹¹ m**.

## Average orbital speed

```
v = 2πr / T

v = average orbital speed
r = average radius of the orbit
T = orbital period (time for one orbit)

rearranged:   T = 2πr / v        r = vT / 2π
```

You must **recall** this equation; it is not given on the paper.

**Method in four lines**

1. Write the equation.
2. Convert T to seconds (days × 24 × 3600; 1 day = 86 400 s).
3. Decide whether r is in m or km; the speed comes out in m/s or km/s to match.
4. Substitute, calculate, and give a unit with two or three significant figures.

**Quick example.** A moon orbits at r = 1.9 × 10⁶ km with T = 16.7 days. T = 16.7 × 86 400 = 1.44 × 10⁶ s. v = 2π × 1.9 × 10⁶ ÷ 1.44 × 10⁶ = **8.3 km/s**.

## Solar System contents (6.1.2)

- **One star**: the Sun, holding most of the Solar System's mass.
- **Eight planets**, in order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
- **Minor planets** orbiting the Sun: dwarf planets such as Pluto, and asteroids in the asteroid belt (between Mars and Jupiter).
- **Moons**, which orbit planets.
- **Smaller Solar System bodies**: comets and natural satellites.

Classification rule: what does the body orbit? The Sun → planet or minor planet; a planet → moon.

## Gravitational field strength

- Field strength **at a planet's surface depends on the planet's mass**: more mass, stronger surface field.
- Field strength **around a planet decreases as distance from it increases**.
- The Sun's surface field is **stronger than any planet's**, because the Sun has most of the mass.
- Weight on another body: W = mg with that body's g. Mass does not change.

## Orbits and the speed trend

- The force keeping an object in orbit around the Sun is the **gravitational attraction of the Sun**, acting towards the Sun.
- It acts at right angles to the motion, so it changes the direction of motion rather than speeding the planet up.
- As distance from the Sun increases: the **Sun's field strength decreases** and **orbital speed decreases**.
- Further planets have a longer path and a lower speed, so their periods are much longer.

## Analysing planetary data

The syllabus names five quantities you may be given: **orbital distance, orbital period, density, surface temperature, surface gravitational field strength**.

A full-mark data answer:

1. names the trend (increases / decreases / no clear pattern);
2. quotes at least two data pairs with units;
3. links the trend to a syllabus reason where one exists;
4. points out any value that does not fit.

Orbital period and orbital speed show clear trends with distance. Surface field strength does **not** follow orbital distance, because it depends on the planet's own mass.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Orbital period, T | Time for one complete orbit |
| Average orbital radius, r | Mean distance from the body being orbited; used because orbits are ellipses |
| Average orbital speed, v | Distance round one orbit (2πr) divided by the orbital period |
| Planet | One of the eight large bodies orbiting the Sun |
| Dwarf planet | A minor planet orbiting the Sun, such as Pluto |
| Asteroid | A minor planet; many lie in the asteroid belt |
| Moon | A body that orbits a planet |
| Comet | A smaller Solar System body orbiting the Sun |
| Gravitational field strength | Force per unit mass, g = W/m, in N/kg |

## Exam traps

- **24 hours is a rotation, not an orbit.** The Earth's orbital period is about 365 days.
- **One orbit is 2πr, not r.** Forgetting 2π gives an answer about six times too small.
- **Units must match.** km with seconds gives km/s, not m/s.
- **"Light takes 500 s"** is a time; multiply by 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s to get the distance.
- **Field strength and distance from the Sun.** A planet's surface field does not depend on its distance from the Sun.
- **Pluto is a dwarf planet**, not one of the eight planets.
- **Gravity pulls** towards the Sun; there is no outward "orbit force" in the syllabus.
- **The orbit is an ellipse** that is approximately circular, so avoid calling it a perfect circle.

## Self-test

1. State the approximate time for the Earth to orbit the Sun.
2. State the approximate time for the Earth to rotate once on its axis.
3. Light from the Sun takes about 500 s to reach the Earth. Calculate the Sun–Earth distance in metres.
4. Write the equation for average orbital speed and define each symbol.
5. A satellite moves at 7.6 × 10³ m/s and takes 5800 s per orbit. Calculate its orbital radius.
6. Name the force that keeps the planets in orbit and state its direction.
7. Which body is a moon: one orbiting the Sun or one orbiting a planet?
8. Where is the asteroid belt?
9. Planet A has twice the mass of planet B. Which has the stronger surface field?
10. How does the orbital speed of a planet change as its distance from the Sun increases?
11. Why is the average radius used in v = 2πr / T for the Earth?
12. Jupiter's average orbital radius is 7.8 × 10⁸ km and its average orbital speed is 13.1 km/s. Calculate its orbital period in Earth years.

**Answers**

1. About 365 days.
2. About 24 hours.
3. d = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 500 = 1.5 × 10¹¹ m.
4. v = 2πr / T: v is the average orbital speed, r the average radius of the orbit, T the orbital period.
5. r = vT / 2π = 7.6 × 10³ × 5800 ÷ 2π = 7.0 × 10⁶ m.
6. The gravitational attraction of the Sun, acting towards the Sun.
7. One orbiting a planet.
8. Between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter.
9. Planet A, because surface field strength depends on the planet's mass.
10. It decreases.
11. The orbit is an ellipse, so the distance from the Sun varies slightly; an average radius represents it.
12. T = 2πr / v = 2π × 7.8 × 10⁸ ÷ 13.1 = 3.74 × 10⁸ s; ÷ (365 × 86 400) = about 12 years.
