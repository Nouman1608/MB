## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by biodiversity. **[1]**

**2.** State how each of the following has helped humans to increase food production.

**(a)** chemical fertilisers **[1]**
**(b)** insecticides **[1]**
**(c)** herbicides **[1]**
**(d)** selective breeding **[1]**

**3.** A company grows a single variety of soya over 20 000 hectares. Describe two advantages and two disadvantages of large-scale monocultures of crop plants such as this. **[4]**

**4.** An intensive poultry unit keeps 24 000 chickens in a shed with a floor area of 3 000 m².

**(a)** Calculate the number of chickens per m². **[1]**
**(b)** Describe two advantages and two disadvantages of intensive livestock production. **[4]**

**5.** The area of a tropical forest fell from 4.8 million km² to 4.2 million km² over twenty years.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in forest area. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons for this habitat destruction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how deforestation can lead to loss of soil and to flooding. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain how deforestation increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[2]**

**6.** This question is about forests as a resource.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a sustainable resource. **[1]**
**(b)** (Extended) Explain how quotas and replanting help to conserve forests. **[2]**
**(c)** (Extended) Describe two reasons for conservation programmes. **[2]**

**7.** A student measured dissolved oxygen at sites along a river. Fertiliser runs off a field into the river at site B.

| Site | A (above drain) | B (at drain) | C (200 m below) | D (400 m below) | E (800 m below) | F (1 600 m below) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen / mg/dm³ | 9.2 | 8.8 | 3.1 | 2.4 | 6.5 | 8.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in dissolved oxygen from site A to site D. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** (Extended) Explain the fall in dissolved oxygen between site B and site D. **[5]**
**(c)** Suggest one effect on fish at site D. **[1]**

**8.** Describe the effects of non-biodegradable plastics in aquatic and in terrestrial ecosystems. **[4]**

**9.** Carbon dioxide and methane are air pollutants.

**(a)** State one source of carbon dioxide and two sources of methane. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how increasing concentrations of these gases lead to climate change. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) A fishery tested two net mesh sizes. With a 40 mm mesh, 62% of the 5 000 fish caught were juveniles that had not yet bred. With an 80 mm mesh, 14% of the 3 200 fish caught were juveniles.

**(a)** Calculate the number of juveniles caught with each mesh size. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how using the 80 mm mesh helps to conserve the fish stock. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how two other methods help to conserve fish stocks. **[4]**

**11.** The wild population of a rare antelope has fallen to 36 animals.

**(a)** State three reasons why organisms become endangered. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe two ways in which endangered species can be conserved. **[2]**
**(c)** (Extended) Describe how artificial insemination and in vitro fertilisation can be used in a captive breeding programme for the antelope. **[3]**
**(d)** (Extended) Explain the risk to the antelope species now that its population is so small. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of different species that live in an area [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "number of organisms" or "variety of life" is not credited; the definition must refer to different species in an area.

**2. (a)** Supply mineral ions such as nitrate, improving yields [1]
**(b)** Kill insect pests, improving quality and yield [1]
**(c)** Kill weeds, reducing competition with the crop [1]
**(d)** Breeding from the most productive plants or animals improves production [1]
*Examiner insight:* "kills pests" is too vague for insecticides and "kills plants" is wrong for herbicides; name insects and weeds.

**3.** Advantages, any two: machinery can be used efficiently for planting and harvesting [1]; high yield of one crop [1]; lower cost per unit of food produced
Disadvantages, any two: reduces biodiversity [1]; pests and diseases of the crop spread easily [1]; heavy use of pesticides or fertiliser; natural habitats removed
*Examiner insight:* when the question asks for two of each, extra advantages cannot make up for a missing disadvantage.

**4. (a)** 24 000 ÷ 3 000 = **8.0 chickens per m²** [1]
**(b)** Advantages, any two: less energy lost in movement and keeping warm, so faster growth [1]; large output at lower cost [1]; less land per animal
Disadvantages, any two: disease spreads quickly in crowded conditions [1]; waste can pollute water [1]; animal welfare concerns; large amounts of feed needed
*Examiner insight:* "cheaper" on its own is often too vague; link the advantage to energy or output.

**5. (a)** (4.8 − 4.2) ÷ 4.8 × 100 [1] = **12.5%** [1]
**(b)** Any two: more land for crop plants or livestock [1]; land for housing [1]; extraction of natural resources such as timber or minerals
**(c)** Tree roots no longer hold soil, so rain washes it away [1]; fewer trees take up water and transpire [1]; so more water runs off into rivers, causing flooding [1]
**(d)** Fewer trees carry out photosynthesis, so less carbon dioxide is removed [1]; burning or decomposition of felled trees releases carbon dioxide [1]
*Examiner insight:* "explain" needs the mechanism; "trees stop flooding" earns nothing without the link to water uptake or run-off.

**6. (a)** A resource that is produced as rapidly as it is removed from the environment, so it does not run out [1]
**(b)** Quotas limit the number of trees felled [1]; replanting replaces felled trees, so the forest is not lost [1]
**(c)** Any two: maintain or increase biodiversity [1]; reduce extinction [1]; protect vulnerable ecosystems; maintain ecosystem functions such as nutrient cycling or provision of food, drugs, fuel and genes
*Examiner insight:* the definition in (a) needs the idea of replacement at the same rate as removal; "does not run out" alone is not enough.

**7. (a)** (9.2 − 2.4) ÷ 9.2 × 100 [1] = **73.9%** [1]
**(b)** Fertiliser increases nitrate and other ions in the water [1]; producers such as algae grow rapidly [1]; when producers die, decomposition increases [1]; decomposers carry out more aerobic respiration [1]; this uses up dissolved oxygen [1]
**(c)** Fish die, or move away, because there is too little dissolved oxygen [1]
*Examiner insight:* the fifth mark in (b) depends on linking oxygen loss to decomposers' respiration; saying the algae use up the oxygen does not earn it.

**8.** Aquatic: animals become entangled and drown or cannot feed [1]; animals swallow plastic, blocking the gut [1]
Terrestrial: plastic is not broken down by decomposers, so it builds up in landfill [1]; animals eat or are trapped by plastic waste [1]
*Examiner insight:* the question names two ecosystems, so a full answer needs points for both; four aquatic points cap the credit.

**9. (a)** Carbon dioxide: combustion of fossil fuels, or burning forests [1]; methane, any two: cattle or other livestock [1]; rice fields [1]; landfill sites
**(b)** The gases absorb thermal energy that would otherwise leave the Earth [1]; higher concentrations cause an enhanced greenhouse effect [1]; mean global temperature rises, changing the climate, for example rainfall patterns or sea level [1]
*Examiner insight:* the syllabus term is the enhanced greenhouse effect; "the ozone layer" is a different issue and gains no credit.

**10. (a)** 40 mm: 0.62 × 5 000 = **3 100** [1]; 80 mm: 0.14 × 3 200 = **448** [1]
**(b)** Young fish can escape through the larger mesh [1]; they survive to reproduce, so the population is replaced [1]
**(c)** Any two methods, each with an explanation: quotas limit the mass of fish caught [1], so fewer are removed than are produced [1]; closed seasons stop fishing during breeding [1], so fish can reproduce [1]; protected areas and monitoring are also creditworthy
*Examiner insight:* each method earns a mark and its explanation a second, so a list of four methods with no explanation gets two marks at most.

**11. (a)** Any three: climate change [1]; habitat destruction [1]; hunting [1]; overharvesting; pollution; introduced species
**(b)** Any two: monitoring and protecting the species and its habitat [1]; education [1]; captive breeding programmes; seed banks, for plants
**(c)** AI: sperm is collected from a male and placed into a female, so animals need not be moved between zoos [1]; IVF: eggs are collected and fertilised by sperm outside the body [1]; the embryos are placed into a female to develop [1]
**(d)** A small population has reduced genetic variation [1]; fewer individuals may have alleles to survive environmental change or disease, so the risk of extinction rises [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (c), stating that IVF happens "inside the female" contradicts the process and loses the mark; "in vitro" means outside the body.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "pesticide" when the question asks about insecticides or herbicides specifically.
- Leaving out decomposers' aerobic respiration in eutrophication explanations.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original value in percentage changes.
- Naming conservation methods without explaining how each lets the population recover.
- Writing "the greenhouse effect" instead of the enhanced greenhouse effect.
- Answering only for aquatic ecosystems when terrestrial effects are asked for as well.
- Defining a sustainable resource without the rate of production matching the rate of removal.
