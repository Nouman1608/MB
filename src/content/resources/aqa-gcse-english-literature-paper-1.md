This guide covers **Paper 1 Shakespeare and the 19th-century Novel**,
one of two papers in AQA GCSE English Literature (8702), first
teaching September 2015. The qualification is untiered and closed
book -- candidates do not have access to the set texts during the
exam, so secure textual knowledge is essential.

## Where this fits in 8702

Paper 1 tests close, detailed knowledge of two whole texts studied in
full: a Shakespeare play and a 19th-century novel, both drawn from
prescribed lists. It pairs with Paper 2 (Modern Texts and Poetry),
which shifts to more recent prose or drama and poetry.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE ENGLISH LITERATURE (8702) — PAPER 1 SHAKESPEARE AND THE
19TH-CENTURY NOVEL**

- Section A: Shakespeare — a detailed study of one Shakespeare play,
including character, theme, context and dramatic method
- Section B: The 19th-century Novel — a detailed study of one novel
from the 19th century, including character, theme, context and the
writer's methods

## How to approach it

Because the exam is closed book, memorising precise, well-chosen
quotations for both the Shakespeare play and the novel is essential --
build a compact bank covering major characters and themes rather than
trying to memorise the whole text. Both sections reward integrating
context (historical, social) into analysis rather than treating it as
a separate add-on, since AQA's assessment objectives explicitly credit
this integration. Because Shakespeare is studied as drama, keep
performance and staging in view alongside language -- how a line is
spoken or staged is often as significant as what it says -- while the
novel section rewards tracking how a theme or character develops
across the whole text, not just in a single extract.
