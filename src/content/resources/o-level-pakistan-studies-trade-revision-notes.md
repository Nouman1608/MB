**These notes cover Section 6 Trade of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059 Paper 2 in a form
you can recall quickly.** The section is short, but it is heavy on definitions and on reading
graphs, so learn the terms exactly and practise describing trends with figures. The full
explanation is in the [Trade study guide](/resources/o-level-pakistan-studies-trade/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Export | A good or service sold to another country |
| Import | A good or service bought from another country |
| GDP | Value of all goods and services produced within a country in a year |
| GNP | GDP plus income earned abroad by residents, minus income paid to foreigners |
| Balance of trade | Value of goods exported minus value of goods imported |
| Trade deficit | When imports cost more than exports earn |
| Trading bloc | A group of countries that cut barriers to trade among themselves |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Quota | A limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported |
| Exchange rate | The price of one currency in terms of another (rupees per US dollar) |
| Remittances | Money sent home by people working abroad |
| Value-added | Extra value gained by processing a raw material into a finished product |

## Numbers to quote (rounded, fiscal year 2023–24)

- Goods exports: **about US$31 billion**; the textile group a little over half.
- Goods imports: **about US$55 billion**; fuel the largest single group.
- Trade deficit: **roughly US$24 billion**.
- Workers' remittances: **about US$30 billion**, nearly matching goods exports.
- Rice exports: a **record near US$4 billion**.
- Exchange rate: **about Rs 160 per US$ in mid-2021, about Rs 280 by 2024**.

Always write "approximately" and the year; they will change.

## Promote versus hinder

| Promotes trade | Hinders trade |
|---|---|
| GSP+ tariff-free access to the EU (since 2014) | Narrow export base (textiles, rice) |
| China–Pakistan FTA; SAFTA; ECO | Competition from Bangladesh, Vietnam, India |
| Ports on the Arabian Sea near Gulf markets | Tariffs, quotas and quality standards abroad |
| Land links to China and Afghanistan | Costly, unreliable power and gas |
| Cheaper rupee helps exporters | Dearer rupee-priced imports raise costs |
| Overseas Pakistani networks | Tension with India limits regional trade |

## How to describe a trade graph (worked example)

*Fig.* (illustrative): exports rise from US$25 billion to US$32 billion over five years, while
imports rise from US$45 billion to US$80 billion, then fall to US$55 billion.

> **Trend:** both exports and imports rose overall, but imports rose much faster, peaking at
> US$80 billion before falling to US$55 billion. **Figures:** exports grew by US$7 billion; the
> deficit widened from US$20 billion to a peak of about US$50 billion, then narrowed to about
> US$23 billion. **Anomaly:** the fall in imports after the peak.

Three moves: overall trend, figures with units, an anomaly or change in direction.

## Exam traps

- **Mixing up GDP and GNP.** GDP is "inside the country"; GNP is "the country's people and firms,
  wherever they are".
- **Writing that remittances are exports.** They are transfers, not sales of goods; they help the
  balance of payments, not the balance of trade.
- **Saying a weak rupee is simply good.** It helps exporters but raises import costs and inflation.
- **Two separate lists in a "compare" question.** Use "whereas" and match the points.
- **Describing a graph without numbers.** Quote at least two values with units.

## Self-test

1. Define the balance of trade.
2. Give two of Pakistan's main imports.
3. Which country is Pakistan's largest single export market?
4. What does GSP+ give Pakistan?
5. Give two reasons Pakistan struggles to raise its share of world trade.
6. Give one benefit and one cost of a falling rupee.

**Answers:** 1. Value of goods exported minus value of goods imported. 2. Any two of petroleum,
machinery, palm oil, chemicals, iron and steel, LNG. 3. The United States. 4. Tariff-free access
to the EU for most exports. 5. Any two of narrow export base, competition, trade barriers, high
production costs, small firms with little technology. 6. Exports become cheaper abroad; imports
and inflation rise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is GNP always bigger than GDP in Pakistan?**
Not necessarily. It depends on whether income earned abroad by Pakistanis is larger or smaller
than income paid out to foreign investors, so learn the definition rather than a rule.

**Why does the import bill rise when world oil prices rise?**
Pakistan imports most of the oil and much of the gas it uses, so the same volume costs more
foreign currency when prices go up.

**What is the difference between a tariff and a quota?**
A tariff is a tax on imports; a quota is a limit on the quantity that may be imported.
