## The equations you must know

```
amount (mol)          = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)
particles             = amount (mol) × 6.02 × 10²³
gas volume (dm³)      = amount (mol) × 24          (at r.t.p.)
concentration (mol/dm³) = amount (mol) ÷ volume (dm³)
concentration (g/dm³)   = concentration (mol/dm³) × molar mass
percentage yield      = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100
percentage purity     = mass of pure substance ÷ mass of sample × 100
percentage of element = (number of atoms × Ar) ÷ Mr × 100
```

Conversions: cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³. One mole of gas at r.t.p. = 24 dm³ = 24 000 cm³.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning for 5070 |
|---|---|
| Mole (mol) | The unit of amount of substance |
| Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³, the number of particles in one mole |
| Molar mass | Mass of one mole, in g/mol; numerically equal to Ar or Mr |
| Molar gas volume | 24 dm³ per mole of any gas at r.t.p. |
| Concentration | Amount or mass of solute per dm³ of solution |
| Limiting reactant | The reactant used up first; it fixes the amount of product |
| Excess reactant | The reactant left over when the reaction stops |
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms or ions |
| Molecular formula | Number and type of each atom in one molecule |
| Theoretical yield | Mass of product predicted from the equation |
| Concordant titres | Titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other |

## The three-step method for reacting quantities

1. **Convert** what you know into moles (from mass, gas volume, or concentration and volume).
2. **Ratio:** use the balanced equation's coefficients to find moles of the substance you want.
3. **Convert back** into the quantity asked for: mass, volume or concentration.

For a limiting reactant, do step 1 for both reactants, then ask how much of the second each would need. The one that runs out first decides step 2.

## Quick conversions worth memorising

- 1 mol of a diatomic gas such as O₂ contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules but 1.204 × 10²⁴ atoms.
- 1 mol of Na₂SO₄ contains 2 mol Na⁺ ions and 1 mol SO₄²⁻ ions.
- Gas volume ratios equal mole ratios: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O means 50 cm³ of H₂ needs 25 cm³ of O₂.
- A 1.00 mol/dm³ solution contains 0.0250 mol in 25.0 cm³.

## Titration data in four lines

1. Mean titre from **concordant** results only; leave out the rough titre.
2. Moles in the solution of known concentration = concentration × volume in dm³.
3. Apply the mole ratio from the balanced equation.
4. Unknown concentration = moles ÷ its volume in dm³; multiply by molar mass for g/dm³.

## Empirical formula routine

Mass (or %) → divide by Ar → divide by the smallest → whole-number ratio. If a value ends in .5 multiply all by 2; if it ends in .33 or .67 multiply by 3. Then molecular formula = empirical formula × (Mr ÷ empirical formula mass).

## Exam traps

- Particle counts must say **which** particles: atoms, ions or molecules.
- Use the limiting reactant, never the excess one, for product amounts.
- The 24 dm³ value applies to **gases only**, never to liquids or solutions.
- Molar mass has units of g/mol; Ar and Mr are ratios and have **no** units.
- Percentage yield uses the **theoretical** mass from the equation, not the mass of reactant.
- Purity questions: the impurity is assumed not to react; only the pure substance produces the product.
- Mean titre: exclude the rough titre and any result that is not concordant.
- Round only at the end; give three significant figures unless the data justify otherwise.

## Self-test

1. State the value of the Avogadro constant and what it represents.
2. Calculate the amount in 7.10 g of chlorine gas, Cl₂.
3. How many atoms are in 0.200 mol of methane, CH₄?
4. 0.0400 mol of an element has a mass of 2.60 g. Find its Ar and identify it.
5. What volume does 0.0150 mol of oxygen occupy at r.t.p., in cm³?
6. Convert 0.200 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide (Mr 56) into g/dm³.
7. How many moles of H₂SO₄ are in 20.0 cm³ of a 0.0500 mol/dm³ solution?
8. 2.4 g of carbon burns in 4.8 g of oxygen: C + O₂ → CO₂. Which is limiting?
9. A compound is 52.2% C, 13.0% H and 34.8% O. Find its empirical formula.
10. Calculate the percentage by mass of oxygen in calcium carbonate, CaCO₃.
11. Theoretical yield 5.00 g, actual yield 3.80 g. Calculate percentage yield.
12. A 4.00 g sample contains 3.40 g of pure iron(II) sulfate. Find its purity.

**Answers**

1. 6.02 × 10²³; the number of particles (atoms, ions or molecules) in one mole.
2. Mr of Cl₂ = 71; 7.10 ÷ 71 = 0.100 mol.
3. Molecules = 0.200 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²³; five atoms each, so 6.02 × 10²³ atoms.
4. Ar = 2.60 ÷ 0.0400 = 65, so the element is zinc.
5. 0.0150 × 24 000 = 360 cm³.
6. 0.200 × 56 = 11.2 g/dm³.
7. 0.0500 × (20.0 ÷ 1000) = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
8. C = 2.4 ÷ 12 = 0.20 mol; O₂ = 4.8 ÷ 32 = 0.15 mol; the ratio is 1 : 1, so oxygen runs out first and is limiting.
9. C 52.2 ÷ 12 = 4.35; H 13.0 ÷ 1 = 13.0; O 34.8 ÷ 16 = 2.175. Dividing by 2.175 gives 2 : 6 : 1, so C₂H₆O.
10. Mr = 100; (3 × 16) ÷ 100 × 100 = 48.0%.
11. 3.80 ÷ 5.00 × 100 = 76.0%.
12. 3.40 ÷ 4.00 × 100 = 85.0%.
