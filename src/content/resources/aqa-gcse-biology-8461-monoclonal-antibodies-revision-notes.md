## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antigen | A molecule (protein) on a cell surface that an antibody binds to |
| Antibody | A protein made by lymphocytes that binds to a specific antigen |
| Monoclonal antibodies | Identical antibodies produced from a single clone of cells |
| Lymphocyte | A type of white blood cell that makes antibodies |
| Tumour cell | A cell that divides repeatedly |
| Hybridoma cell | A lymphocyte combined with a tumour cell; it can both divide and make the antibody |
| Clone | A group of genetically identical cells produced from one cell |
| Fluorescent dye | A dye that glows, used to show where an antibody has bound |

## Why they are useful

Monoclonal antibodies are **specific to one binding site on one protein antigen**, so they target a **specific
chemical or specific cells** in the body.

## Production in five steps

1. Stimulate **mouse lymphocytes** to make a particular antibody.
2. Combine the lymphocytes with a particular kind of **tumour cell**.
3. This makes a **hybridoma cell**, which can both **divide** and **make the antibody**.
4. **Clone a single hybridoma cell** to make many identical cells, all making the same antibody.
5. **Collect and purify** a large amount of the antibody.

Memory aid: *Lymphocyte gives the antibody; tumour gives the dividing; hybridoma gives both.*

## The uses

| Use | Target | Attached to the antibody |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis, such as pregnancy tests | Hormone in urine | Coloured beads |
| Measuring hormones and other chemicals in blood | The chemical in the sample | A marker |
| Detecting pathogens | Antigen on the pathogen | A marker |
| Research: locating molecules | Specific molecule in a cell or tissue | Fluorescent dye |
| Treating cancer | Antigen on cancer cells | Radioactive substance, toxic drug, or chemical that stops cells growing and dividing |

You do **not** need to recall specific tests or treatments; you must explain how one works from given
information.

## Pregnancy test strip: what each part does

| Part | Antibodies | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Sample end | Mobile antibodies on coloured beads | Bind the hormone if present; move along the strip with urine |
| Test line | Fixed antibodies | Bind the hormone; hormone with beads attached is held here, so a coloured line appears |
| Control line | Fixed antibodies | Bind the mobile antibodies; line appears whether or not the hormone is present |

**Result:** two lines = pregnant; control line only = not pregnant; no control line = test not working.

## Cancer treatment in a chain

Antibody bound to a drug → injected → binds only to antigens on cancer cells → drug delivered to cancer cells →
other body cells not harmed.

## Evaluation points

**For:**

- Very specific, so tests are precise and treatments targeted.
- Healthy cells are mostly not harmed by targeted cancer treatment.
- Many uses: diagnosis, measuring, detecting pathogens, research, treatment.

**Against:**

- They create **more side effects than expected**.
- They are **not yet as widely used** as everyone hoped when they were first developed.
- Using mice raises ethical concerns for some people.

## Links to other parts of the course

- **4.3.1 Communicable diseases:** ordinary antibodies are made by white blood cells during infection or after
  vaccination; monoclonal antibodies use the same antigen-binding idea, made in a culture.
- **4.3.3 Plant disease (Higher tier only):** testing kits containing monoclonal antibodies are one way to
  identify a plant pathogen.
- **4.1.2 Cell division:** cloning a hybridoma cell relies on mitosis, so every cell in the clone is
  genetically identical.

## Exam traps

- Saying the tumour cell is injected into the mouse. The lymphocytes are combined with tumour cells to make the
  hybridoma.
- Leaving out "single" before "hybridoma cell is cloned".
- Saying the antibody itself kills cancer cells. It delivers the substance that does.
- Forgetting the control line, or saying the control line shows pregnancy.
- Writing "specific to a disease" rather than specific to an antigen.
- Answering a Foundation-style question with this content: it is Higher tier only.

## Self-test

1. What does "monoclonal" mean?
2. Why can a monoclonal antibody target one type of cell only?
3. Which animal's lymphocytes are used in production?
4. What is a hybridoma cell?
5. Why is a lymphocyte alone not used to produce antibodies in large amounts?
6. What two properties does a hybridoma cell have?
7. What happens to the antibody after it is collected?
8. Name three substances that can be attached to a monoclonal antibody to treat cancer.
9. Why does a monoclonal antibody cancer treatment harm fewer healthy cells than the drug alone?
10. In research, what is attached to the antibody, and why?
11. What does a line at the control window of a pregnancy test show?
12. Give two disadvantages of monoclonal antibodies named in the specification.

### Answers

1. Produced from a single clone of cells.
2. It is specific to one binding site on one protein antigen, found only on that cell type.
3. Mouse.
4. A cell made by combining a lymphocyte with a tumour cell.
5. It does not keep dividing, so it cannot produce large numbers of cells.
6. It can divide and it can make the antibody.
7. It is purified.
8. A radioactive substance; a toxic drug; a chemical that stops cells growing and dividing.
9. The antibody binds only to antigens on cancer cells, so the drug is delivered to them and not to other cells.
10. A fluorescent dye, so the position of the specific molecule can be seen.
11. That the test is working.
12. They create more side effects than expected; they are not yet as widely used as everyone hoped.
