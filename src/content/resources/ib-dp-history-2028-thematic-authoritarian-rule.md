The thematic study **Authoritarian rule (from 1750 CE onwards)** is one of four options in IB DP History (first assessment 2028). A class studies one thematic study, assessed in paper 2. The guide sets four inquiry questions, each with four lines of inquiry, and **prescribes no examples**. You and your teacher choose examples, and each line of inquiry must be explored using at least two examples from more than one region. This guide explains the framework, suggests a bank of examples, and shows how to build paper 2 answers. It is the same for SL and HL. The material includes persecution and mass death, so use careful, precise language.

## What the guide means by authoritarian rule

The guide defines authoritarian rule as the concentration of political power within a small group or an individual. It lies at one end of a spectrum, with democratic processes at the other, and societies can contain both authoritarian and democratic elements. Types listed include monarchies, oligarchies, colonial governments, dictatorships or totalitarian states, and military regimes. Examples may be hybrid, with non-authoritarian elements.

## How the study is assessed

Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 25 marks. Section A (6 marks) asks you to analyse a specified concept using an example from your thematic study. In section B you answer one question with part (a), a short response (4 marks), and part (b), an essay (15 marks) using examples from at least two regions: Africa and the Middle East, the Americas, Asia and Oceania, or Europe. The top essay band needs consistent analysis, a reasoned judgement and well-developed connections between examples.

## Inquiry question 1: Why did authoritarian rule emerge?

**Lines of inquiry:** role of ideas, social factors, role of conflict, economic factors.

- **Ideas.** Fascist ideas of national unity and race, and communist ideas of class struggle and a vanguard party, justified concentrated power. Colonial rule drew on ideas of racial hierarchy and a "civilising mission".
- **Social.** Fear of disorder and class conflict made strong rulers attractive to some groups. In Chile, middle-class opposition to Salvador Allende's government and fear of social revolution contributed to support for the military coup of 11 September 1973.
- **Conflict.** The First World War and the Russian Civil War shaped the Bolshevik state. Germany's defeat in 1918 and the humiliation many felt contributed to the appeal of extreme nationalism. Colonial regimes were established through conquest.
- **Economic.** The Great Depression, with mass unemployment in Germany, helped the Nazis win votes up to 1932. Economic crisis in Chile under Allende (high inflation and shortages) was part of the context of the coup.

## Inquiry question 2: How was authoritarian rule maintained?

**Lines of inquiry:** legal methods, use of force, propaganda, popular support.

- **Legal methods.** The Enabling Act of March 1933 allowed Hitler's government to make laws without the Reichstag. The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 stripped Jews of citizenship. Pinochet's 1980 constitution gave the regime a legal framework. Colonial governments used laws on land, labour and movement.
- **Use of force.** The Gestapo and the SS in Germany, the NKVD in the Soviet Union (the Great Terror of 1937 to 1938), and Pinochet's secret police, the DINA, used arrest, torture and killings. In the Congo Free State, the Force Publique enforced rubber quotas by violence.
- **Propaganda.** Joseph Goebbels' ministry controlled the press, film and radio in Germany. In China, Mao's image was spread through posters and the "Little Red Book" during the Cultural Revolution. Propaganda aimed to build loyalty as well as to hide the truth.
- **Popular support.** Regimes can rely on real support as well as fear: many Germans supported Hitler's early economic recovery and foreign policy successes, and many Soviet citizens supported industrialisation and national pride. Historians disagree on the balance between consent and coercion.

## Inquiry question 3: How did authoritarian rule affect people's lives?

**Lines of inquiry:** economic effects, social effects, experiences of women, experiences of marginalised groups.

- **Economic.** Stalin's Five-Year Plans industrialised the USSR rapidly and forced collectivisation, which was followed by the famine of 1932 to 1933 that caused millions of deaths, including in Ukraine. Mao's Great Leap Forward (1958 to 1962) led to a famine whose death toll is estimated in the tens of millions, with estimates varying widely.
- **Social.** Regimes tried to control education, youth organisations and religion. The Hitler Youth and the Soviet Komsomol are examples. Informers and fear affected family and community trust.
- **Women.** The Nazi regime promoted the ideal of women as mothers and housekeepers, whereas the Soviet state encouraged women to work in industry while also expecting them to carry the burden of domestic labour. Under Pinochet, women's groups, such as relatives of the disappeared, became important voices of protest.
- **Marginalised groups.** The Nazi regime persecuted Jews, Roma, disabled people and political opponents. In the Congo Free State, African workers faced forced labour and mutilation. Indigenous peoples and minorities in many regimes faced discrimination.

## Inquiry question 4: How was authoritarian rule challenged?

**Lines of inquiry:** internal opposition, popular resistance, impact of policies, external threats.

- **Internal opposition.** Conspirators in the German army tried to kill Hitler in July 1944. Within the Chinese Communist Party, disagreements over policy emerged after the Great Leap Forward.
- **Popular resistance.** Gandhi's Salt March in 1930 and the wider non-cooperation campaigns challenged British rule in India. In Chile, the protests of the 1980s and the campaign for a "No" vote in the 1988 plebiscite mobilised many people. Resistance could be small-scale, such as the White Rose group in Germany.
- **Impact of policies.** Rulers' policies may create opposition. Famines undermined faith in leaders. The Congo Free State's abuses led to a reform campaign, with figures such as E. D. Morel and Roger Casement, and Belgium took over the territory in 1908.
- **External threats.** Defeat in war ended Nazi rule in 1945. International criticism, economic pressure and the end of the Cold War affected military regimes in Latin America.

## Making connections between examples

- **Similarity:** most regimes combined force, propaganda and some popular support.
- **Difference:** totalitarian states aimed to control all of life, while some military and colonial regimes were more limited.
- **Pattern:** economic crisis and conflict often preceded the emergence of authoritarian rule.
- **Exception:** Pinochet's regime ended by a plebiscite that it had called.

## Worked example: a paragraph for part (b)

Question: "To what extent did authoritarian regimes depend on force to remain in power?"

"Force was central to all the regimes studied, but few relied on it alone. In Stalin's USSR the NKVD arrested and executed hundreds of thousands during the Great Terror of 1937 to 1938, and the camp system disciplined the population. Yet the regime also built support through industrialisation, education and appeals to national pride. In Nazi Germany, the Gestapo and concentration camps removed opponents, but the regime also used propaganda and early economic recovery to win consent from many. By contrast, in the Congo Free State, force was the main instrument, because the system aimed at extracting rubber with little interest in winning consent. The comparison suggests that force was most dominant where a regime had least need or chance of building support."

The paragraph draws on Europe and Africa, so it meets the two-region requirement. Remember that the Soviet Union and Germany are both in Europe, so they count as one region between them.

## Writing part (a)

Part (a) is a 4-mark short response on one example. Choose the example you know best, identify it, give two or three accurate details and explain how they answer the question. For instance, to explain how a regime used legal methods: the Enabling Act of 23 March 1933 let Hitler's cabinet pass laws without the Reichstag, so he could ban parties and unions with a legal appearance, and the Nuremberg Laws of 1935 then defined citizenship by race.

## Common mistakes

- Treating all authoritarian regimes as identical.
- Ignoring popular support.
- Using two European examples in the essay.
- Quoting casualty figures without saying they are estimates.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is colonial rule authoritarian?** The guide lists colonial governments as a type, and they are a good source of examples from Africa and Asia.

**Can I use a regime that still exists?** Contemporary examples must be treated historically, with causes rather than consequences.
