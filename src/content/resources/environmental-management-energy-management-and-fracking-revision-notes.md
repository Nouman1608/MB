These notes condense sub-topics 1.5 (conservation and management of energy resources) and 1.6 (fracking) of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680) for exams in 2027 to 2029. Examiners ask you to describe named strategies and then discuss their benefits and limitations, so every item below has both. For the full explanation, use the [Energy Management and Fracking study guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-environmental-management-energy-management-and-fracking/); the rest of Topic 1 is in the [Natural Resources revision notes](/resources/environmental-management-natural-resources-revision-notes/).

## 1.5.1 The eight named strategies

| Strategy | Example | + | – |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reducing consumption | Switch off devices, lower thermostat | Free, immediate | Depends on behaviour |
| Insulation | Loft, cavity wall, double glazing | Less heat lost, lower bills | Up-front cost |
| Energy-efficient devices | LED bulbs, efficient appliances, electric and fuel-efficient vehicles | Same service, less energy | Higher purchase price |
| Exploiting existing energy resources | Fuller, more efficient use of reserves and plant already in place | Uses existing infrastructure | Prolongs fossil fuel use |
| Education on energy conservation | Campaigns, energy labels, school programmes | Cheap, long-term change | Slow, hard to measure |
| Transport policies | Public transport, cycle lanes, congestion charges | Less fuel, cleaner air | Costly, can be unpopular |
| Battery storage | Store surplus solar and wind electricity | Smooths out intermittency | Expensive; needs mined materials |
| Development of new energy resources | Hydrogen, heat pumps | Less fossil fuel dependence | Research and infrastructure costs |

**Memory hook:** three are about **using less** (reducing consumption, insulation, efficient devices), two about **behaviour and policy** (education, transport policies), and three about **supply** (existing resources, storage, new resources).

## 1.5.2 New energy resources

| Resource | How it is produced or works | + | – |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blue hydrogen** | Produced from **natural gas**; carbon dioxide captured and stored | Uses existing gas supply and network | Still fossil-fuel based; carbon capture costly |
| **Green hydrogen** | Produced using **renewable resources** (renewable electricity splits water) | Very low emissions | Expensive; needs lots of renewable power |
| **Ground source heat pump** | Transfers heat from the ground via buried pipes to heat buildings | Steady output; no on-site emissions | Installation cost; needs land to dig |
| **Air source heat pump** | Transfers heat from the outside air to heat buildings | Easier to fit than ground source | Less efficient in very cold weather; needs good insulation |

Hydrogen's only product when used is water, but it is difficult to store and transport. Heat pumps are not geothermal power stations: they work anywhere.

## 1.6 Fracking

**Definition (learn word for word):** fracking is the extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing.

**Process:** drill down then horizontally into shale → pump in water, sand and chemicals at high pressure → rock cracks → sand props cracks open → gas or oil flows up the well. Shale is impermeable, so fracturing is needed to release it.

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Energy security, fewer imports | Still a fossil fuel; carbon dioxide emissions |
| Jobs and tax revenue | Small earthquakes (induced seismicity) |
| Gas replacing coal cuts emissions | Groundwater contamination risk |
| Can lower energy prices | Very high water use; wastewater disposal |
| Uses existing gas pipelines | Methane leaks; noise, traffic and visual impact |

England paused fracking with a moratorium in 2019 after tremors at a Lancashire site.

## How 1.5 and 1.6 connect to the rest of Topic 1

- **Demand (1.4.5) drives management (1.5):** transport, wealth, climate, population size and industry raise demand; reducing consumption, efficiency and transport policies tackle the same factors from the other side.
- **Intermittency (1.4.4) drives storage:** solar and wind vary with the weather, which is why battery storage and hydrogen appear as management strategies.
- **Shale permeability (1.1.4) explains fracking (1.6):** gas held in impermeable shale only flows once the rock is fractured.
- **Finite resources (1.3.1):** fracking extends finite fossil fuel supplies; 1.5 strategies make them last longer or replace them.

Use these links in longer answers: a source showing rising energy demand can be answered with 1.5 strategies, and a question on energy security can bring in fracking on one side and new energy resources on the other.

## Worked example: a "discuss" answer

"Discuss the benefits and limitations of using battery storage to manage energy resources." **[4]**

- Benefit: stores surplus electricity from solar and wind for use when output is low [1], reducing the need for fossil fuel back-up [1].
- Limitation: large batteries are expensive [1], and making them needs mined materials such as lithium, with environmental impacts [1].

## Exam traps

- Writing "fracking is drilling for oil" — no shale, no hydraulic fracturing, no mark.
- Swapping blue (natural gas) and green (renewable) hydrogen.
- Calling a heat pump geothermal power.
- Giving only benefits when the question says "discuss the benefits and limitations".
- Claiming electric vehicles cause no pollution — the electricity may come from fossil fuels.

## Self-test

1. Give the syllabus definition of fracking.
2. Name three strategies from 1.5.1 that reduce demand.
3. What is blue hydrogen produced from?
4. State one limitation of a ground source heat pump.
5. Give two environmental limitations of fracking.

**Answers:** 1. The extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing. 2. Any three of reducing consumption, insulation, energy-efficient devices, education, transport policies. 3. Natural gas. 4. High installation cost, or it needs land for buried pipes. 5. Any two of earthquakes, groundwater contamination, high water use, methane leaks, carbon dioxide emissions.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the syllabus definition of fracking in Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management 0680?**
Fracking is the extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing. Answers that leave out shale rock or the hydraulic fracturing process miss the definition.

**What is the difference between blue and green hydrogen?**
Blue hydrogen is produced from natural gas, while green hydrogen comes from renewable sources. Swapping the two is a common exam trap. When hydrogen is used its only product is water, but it is difficult to store and transport.

**Is a heat pump the same as geothermal power?**
No. Calling a heat pump geothermal power is a common exam trap.

**Do electric vehicles cause no pollution?**
No. Claiming electric vehicles cause no pollution is an exam trap, because the electricity they use may come from fossil fuels.
