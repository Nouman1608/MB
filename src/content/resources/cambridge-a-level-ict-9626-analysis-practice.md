## Questions

**1.** State **four** methods that an analyst can use to research a situation. **[4]**

**2.** A garden centre wants a new stock and sales system. It has 6 staff and about 4,000 regular customers.

**(a)** Explain why a questionnaire is suitable for finding out what customers think of the current system. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** disadvantage of using a questionnaire for this purpose. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why an interview with the owner is suitable. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe what the analyst would learn by observing staff at the tills on a Saturday. **[2]**
**(e)** Describe **one** problem with the observation in (d). **[1]**

**3.** The garden centre uses a paper delivery note, a hand-written order book and a weekly sales sheet.

**(a)** Describe what the analyst is doing when carrying out document analysis. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** things the analyst could learn from these documents. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** limitation of document analysis. **[1]**

**4.** Describe the purpose of each of the following.

**(a)** the user requirements specification **[2]**
**(b)** the system specification **[2]**
**(c)** the design specification **[2]**

**5.** Each of the following statements from the garden centre project belongs in one of the three specifications. State which one in each case.

**(a)** "Staff must be able to find the price of any plant by scanning its barcode." **[1]**
**(b)** "Two barcode scanners and a receipt printer will be needed." **[1]**
**(c)** "The Plant table will have fields PlantID, Name, Price and QuantityInStock." **[1]**
**(d)** "The operating system will be one that supports the chosen database software." **[1]**

**6.** An analyst has produced a user requirements specification for a clinic. A manager says: "I do not need to read it, because the design specification will say everything I need."

Explain why the manager is wrong. **[3]**

**7.** A town council wants to replace the system used to report faulty street lights. Residents report faults by phone, and the call handlers write them in a notebook.

Evaluate the use of interviews, questionnaires and observation for researching this situation, and recommend a combination of methods. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Questionnaires [1]; interviews [1]; observation [1]; document analysis. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Surveys" and "talking to people" are not in the syllabus wording; use the four names.

**2. (a)** There are many customers (about 4,000) [1], so a questionnaire can reach many of them at low cost, and the answers can be collected and compared. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Any one: many may not return it, so the sample may be small or unrepresentative; the analyst cannot ask follow-up questions; badly worded questions give poor data. [1] **[1]**
**(c)** The owner is one person with detailed knowledge of the business [1]; an interview allows the analyst to ask follow-up questions and explore complex needs such as supplier orders and seasonal stock. [1] **[2]**
**(d)** The analyst would see how the staff really serve customers [1], and the problems they meet, such as slow searches for prices or queues. [1] **[2]**
**(e)** Staff may behave differently when watched, so the observation may not show normal working. (Accept: it is time-consuming; one Saturday may not be typical.) [1] **[1]**

**3. (a)** The analyst studies the existing documents used by the system [1], such as delivery notes, the order book and the sales sheet, to find out what data is recorded and how. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Any two from: the data items recorded; the layout and format of forms; the volume of orders or sales; the calculations used; the rules followed. [1 each] **[2]**
**(c)** The documents may be out of date, or may not show what staff really do or why. [1] **[1]**

**4. (a)** To state what the users need the system to do [1]; it is agreed with the users and is used to check later whether the finished system meets their needs. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** To list the hardware and software needed to run the new system [1], so that the organisation knows what to buy or install. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** To describe how the system will be built, such as data flow, file structures, forms and reports [1], so that developers can build it and testers can check it. [1] **[2]**

**5. (a)** User requirements specification. [1]
**(b)** System specification. [1]
**(c)** Design specification. [1]
**(d)** System specification. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A statement about tools or platforms is system specification; a statement about tables, forms or layouts is design; a statement about what staff can do is requirements.

**6.** The design specification describes how the system will be built, which is a technical document for developers [1]. The URS records what the users need, in language the manager can check [1]. The manager should read it to confirm that the needs are correct and complete, since the finished system will later be judged against it. [1] **[3]**

**7.** Interviews (up to 2 marks): suitable for the call handlers and the manager, who can describe the process in detail and be asked follow-up questions [1]; time-consuming, and answers differ between people, so hard to compare. [1]
Questionnaires (up to 2 marks): suitable for many residents who report faults, to find what they want, such as online reporting [1]; low return rate, and no follow-up of answers. [1]
Observation (up to 2 marks): shows how call handlers record the faults and where information is lost [1]; staff may behave differently when watched, and it takes time. [1]
Conclusion (1 mark, only if justified): for example interview the manager and handlers, observe a busy shift and send a short questionnaire to residents, so the weakness of each method is covered by another. [1]
Maximum 6. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement. A list of pros and cons with no recommendation is capped below full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a method without saying why it suits the people in the scenario.
- Treating observation as "asking staff" or interview as "watching".
- Confusing the URS with the system specification.
- Writing "it is cheap" or "it is quick" for every method.
- Giving advantages of a combination of methods without naming the methods.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which research methods are in the 9626 syllabus?**
Questionnaires, interviews, observation and document analysis. Advantages and disadvantages of each are in section 16.2.

**What is the difference between the three specifications?**
The user requirements specification states what the users need, the system specification lists the hardware and software, and the design specification describes how the system will be built.
