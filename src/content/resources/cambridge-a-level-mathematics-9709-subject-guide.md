Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics (9709) combines pure mathematics with applied mathematics in mechanics and in probability and statistics. Unlike many courses, it is built from six separate papers, and the qualification you receive depends on which combination you take. A full A Level is always Paper 1, Pure Mathematics 1 (1 hour 50 minutes, 75 marks, 30%), Paper 3, Pure Mathematics 3 (1 hour 50 minutes, 75 marks, 30%), Paper 5, Probability & Statistics 1 (1 hour 15 minutes, 50 marks, 20%), and either Paper 4, Mechanics, or Paper 6, Probability & Statistics 2 (each 1 hour 15 minutes, 50 marks, 20%). This page explains the six areas of content, the routes through the qualification and how to plan your study.

## How the course is organised

Each of the six content areas matches one paper.

**Pure mathematics**

- **Pure Mathematics 1:** quadratics, functions, coordinate geometry, circular measure, trigonometry, series, differentiation and integration. This is the foundation for everything else.
- **Pure Mathematics 2:** algebra, logarithmic and exponential functions, trigonometry, differentiation, integration and numerical solution of equations. It exists only for the AS Level, Pure Mathematics only route.
- **Pure Mathematics 3:** the same headings as Pure Mathematics 2 taken further, plus vectors, differential equations and complex numbers.

**Applied mathematics**

- **Mechanics:** forces and equilibrium, kinematics of motion in a straight line, momentum, Newton's laws of motion, and energy, work and power.
- **Probability & Statistics 1:** representation of data, permutations and combinations, probability, discrete random variables and the normal distribution.
- **Probability & Statistics 2:** the Poisson distribution, linear combinations of random variables, continuous random variables, sampling and estimation, and hypothesis tests. It builds on Probability & Statistics 1.

## How it's assessed

All six papers are externally assessed written examinations.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of AS Level | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Pure Mathematics 1 | 1 hour 50 minutes | 75 | 60% | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Pure Mathematics 2 | 1 hour 15 minutes | 50 | 40% | 0% (AS only) |
| Paper 3 | Pure Mathematics 3 | 1 hour 50 minutes | 75 | n/a | 30% |
| Paper 4 | Mechanics | 1 hour 15 minutes | 50 | 40% | 20% |
| Paper 5 | Probability & Statistics 1 | 1 hour 15 minutes | 50 | 40% | 20% |
| Paper 6 | Probability & Statistics 2 | 1 hour 15 minutes | 50 | n/a | 20% |

**AS Level routes.** The AS Level is Paper 1 (60%) plus one of Paper 2, Paper 4 or Paper 5 (40%). That gives three AS options: Pure Mathematics only (Papers 1 and 2), Pure Mathematics and Mechanics (Papers 1 and 4), or Pure Mathematics and Probability & Statistics (Papers 1 and 5).

**A Level routes.** The A Level is Papers 1, 3 and 5, plus either Paper 4 or Paper 6. Paper 6 cannot be combined with Paper 4, because it depends on Paper 5 content.

**Carrying an AS forward.** Only the Papers 1 and 4 or Papers 1 and 5 combinations can be carried forward as the first stage of a staged A Level, which is completed by adding Paper 3 and the complementary applied paper. The Pure Mathematics only AS (Papers 1 and 2) is certificated as a standalone AS Level and cannot be carried forward. Paper 2 counts for nothing at A Level, and Papers 3 and 6 are not offered at AS.

The syllabus covers the 2026-2027 examination window, with June, November and (in India) March series, and was first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

Pure Mathematics 1 is the hub. Its algebra, functions, trigonometry and calculus are used in every other paper, so weaknesses there tend to show up everywhere. Pure Mathematics 3 extends the same threads, and adds vectors, differential equations and complex numbers, so the two papers should be seen as one continuous strand rather than separate courses.

The applied papers draw on the pure content in different ways. Mechanics relies on resolving forces, solving equations and, in places, calculus for motion. Probability & Statistics 1 uses algebra and counting methods, and its work on discrete random variables and the normal distribution leads directly into the continuous random variables, sampling and hypothesis tests of Probability & Statistics 2. That dependency is why Paper 6 can only be taken alongside Paper 5.

Across the course, the same habits matter: clear, logical working, accurate algebra, and the ability to model a situation mathematically before solving it.

## Planning your study

Your route decides your plan, so confirm early with your teacher which papers you will sit.

For a staged two-year A Level, a typical shape in general terms is:

- **First stage:** Pure Mathematics 1 alongside either Mechanics or Probability & Statistics 1, according to your AS route. Start with quadratics, functions and coordinate geometry before moving to trigonometry, series and calculus.
- **Second stage:** Pure Mathematics 3, building on Pure Mathematics 1, with the remaining applied paper: Probability & Statistics 1 if you began with Mechanics, or Mechanics or Probability & Statistics 2 if you began with Probability & Statistics 1.

If all papers are taken in a single series, it still makes sense to secure Pure Mathematics 1 before Pure Mathematics 3, and Probability & Statistics 1 before Probability & Statistics 2.

Practise questions on each topic as soon as it is taught, keep a list of methods you find difficult, and begin full past papers for each paper in the final months. Advice on each paper is on the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which papers do you take for Cambridge A Level Mathematics 9709?**
Paper 1 Pure Mathematics 1, Paper 3 Pure Mathematics 3 and Paper 5 Probability & Statistics 1, plus either Paper 4 Mechanics or Paper 6 Probability & Statistics 2.

**Does Paper 2 count towards the A Level?**
No. Paper 2 Pure Mathematics 2 is offered only as part of the AS Level, Pure Mathematics only route, and carries a weighting of 0% at A Level.

**Can I take Paper 4 and Paper 6 together?**
No. The A Level requires one of them, and Paper 6 cannot be combined with Paper 4 because it depends on Paper 5 content.

**Can every AS Level route be carried forward to the A Level?**
No. Papers 1 and 4, or Papers 1 and 5, can be carried forward by adding Paper 3 and the complementary applied paper. The Pure Mathematics only AS (Papers 1 and 2) cannot be carried forward.

**When is 9709 examined?**
The 2026-2027 syllabus is examined in the June and November series, and in a March series in India.
