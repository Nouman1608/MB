## Section A — Multiple choice

**1.** A change in a good's own price causes: **[1]**
A. a shift of the demand curve  B. a movement along the demand curve  C. a shift of the supply curve  D. no change to the market

**2.** A market with only one seller and high barriers to entry is best described as: **[1]**
A. a competitive market  B. an oligopoly  C. a monopoly  D. a perfectly elastic market

**3.** A market dominated by a small number of large firms is best described as: **[1]**
A. a monopoly  B. an oligopoly  C. a competitive market  D. a planned market

**4.** A negative externality means that: **[1]**
A. third parties gain a benefit not reflected in the price  B. third parties bear a cost not reflected in the price  C. the good is under-produced  D. the market always corrects itself

**5.** If demand is price inelastic, a rise in price will: **[1]**
A. reduce total revenue  B. increase total revenue  C. leave total revenue unchanged  D. make the good a luxury

**6.** Market failure caused by resources being misallocated occurs when: **[1]**
A. the market produces exactly what consumers want  B. resources are used to produce the wrong combination of goods relative to society's wants  C. the government sets a price  D. a firm earns high profits

**7.** Capital, as the term is used in economics, means: **[1]**
A. money held by a business  B. man-made aids to production such as machinery and tools  C. profit retained by a firm  D. the price of a good

**8.** A maximum price set below the market equilibrium price will cause: **[1]**
A. a surplus  B. a shortage  C. no change to quantity  D. supply to increase

**9.** Cross elasticity of demand measures: **[1]**
A. the responsiveness of demand for one good to a change in the price of another good  B. the responsiveness of demand to a change in income  C. the responsiveness of supply to a change in price  D. the responsiveness of demand to a change in its own price

**10.** Equilibrium price is the price at which: **[1]**
A. supply exceeds demand  B. demand exceeds supply  C. quantity demanded equals quantity supplied  D. the government sets the market price

## Answers

**Section A (multiple choice): 1. B 2. C 3. B 4. B 5. B 6. B 7. B 8. B 9. A 10. C**

**11.** Effective demand is the **desire for a good backed by the willingness and the ability to pay for it** [1] [1]. Someone who wants a car but has no money contributes nothing to market demand, because **producers only respond to demand that can actually be paid for** [1].

**12. Opportunity cost** is the value of the **next best alternative forgone** when a choice is made, not the total of everything given up [1] [1]. In economics, **capital means machinery, tools and equipment** used in production — not money, which is merely used to *buy* capital [1].

**13.** A **negative externality** imposes costs on third parties who are not part of the transaction, e.g. air pollution from a factory harming nearby residents [1] [1]. A **positive externality** creates benefits for third parties, e.g. a vaccinated individual reducing the spread of disease to others [1] [1].

**14. (a)** Supply shifts **left** [1]; there is a shortage at the old price, so the **price rises and the equilibrium quantity falls** [1] [1].
**(b)** Demand shifts **right** [1]; there is a shortage at the old price, so the **price rises and the equilibrium quantity rises** [1] [1].
**(c)** Both effects **push the price up, so the price rises sharply** [1]. The effect on quantity is **indeterminate** [1], because supply reduces it while demand raises it — the outcome depends on which shift is larger [1].

**15.** If demand is **elastic**, a price cut causes a **proportionally larger rise in quantity**, so total revenue rises [1]. Example: price falls from $10 to $8 (−20%) and quantity rises from 100 to 150 (+50%); revenue rises from $1000 to **$1200** [1] [1].
If demand is **inelastic**, a price rise causes a **proportionally smaller fall in quantity**, so total revenue rises [1]. Example: price rises from $2 to $2.40 (+20%) and quantity falls from 500 to 475 (−5%); revenue rises from $1000 to **$1140** [1] [1].
The rule is that revenue rises when price moves **towards** the inelastic direction — cut price where demand is elastic, raise it where demand is inelastic.

**16. Advantages**, 2 marks each (any three): **lower prices**, since firms must match rivals or lose customers [1] [1]. **Better quality and more choice**, as firms differentiate their products to attract buyers [1] [1]. **Innovation** — firms invest in new products and processes to gain an advantage, so consumers benefit from improved goods over time [1] [1]. **Greater efficiency**, since firms must cut costs to survive, which keeps prices down [1] [1].
**Disadvantages**, 2 marks each (any two): **duplication and wasted resources** — competing firms each build their own networks or advertise heavily, and advertising costs are passed to consumers without improving the product [1] [1]. **Loss of economies of scale** — several small firms may each have higher unit costs than one large one, so prices could actually be higher; competition may also produce **excessive choice and confusing pricing** that makes comparison difficult [1] [1].

**17.** A monopoly can **restrict output and charge a higher price than would prevail under competition**, so consumers pay more and consume less [1] [1]. With no competitive pressure it may also allow **quality to fall, be slow to innovate and become inefficient**, since it will retain customers regardless [1].
Regulation: the government can **set a maximum price or a price cap** linked to inflation, so the monopoly cannot exploit its position [1]; it can **use competition law to break the firm up or prevent mergers that create monopoly** [1]; or it can **open the market to competition by removing barriers to entry, or take the firm into public ownership** [1].

**18. (a)** A maximum price set below equilibrium causes **quantity demanded to rise and quantity supplied to fall**, since the price is now more attractive to buyers but less attractive to producers [1] [1]; on a demand-and-supply diagram this creates a gap between the two curves at the new lower price, representing **excess demand** [1]. **(b)** Any two, 1 mark each: **shortages and queues**, since quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied [1]; the emergence of a **black market**, where the good is resold above the legal price [1]; producers may **reduce quality** to cut costs at the lower legal price [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying quantity definitely rises when demand and supply both shift.
- Confusing effective demand with wants.
- Saying elastic demand always means cutting prices raises profit — revenue is not profit.
- Listing disadvantages of monopoly without any regulatory remedy.
- Confusing a monopoly (one dominant seller) with an oligopoly (a few large firms).
