AQA A-Level Accounting (7127) is a two-year course for students who want to understand how businesses record
transactions, prepare financial statements and use financial information to make decisions. It suits students
heading towards accountancy, finance, business or economics, but it also stands on its own as a rigorous,
numerate subject with a clear ethical dimension. The qualification is linear and not tiered: everything is
assessed at the end of the course in two written papers, each lasting 3 hours, worth 120 marks and carrying 50%
of the A-level.

## How the course is organised

The specification is set out in 18 numbered content sections. They fall naturally into four groups, although
AQA presents them as one continuous list.

**Foundations of accounting (sections 1 to 5).** The course opens with the role of the accountant in business
and the types of business organisation, then moves into the double entry model, the verification of accounting
records and the accounting concepts that sit behind every record. This is the grammar of the subject: almost
everything later on assumes fluent double entry and a secure grasp of the concepts.

**Preparing and analysing financial statements (sections 6 to 8).** Students learn to prepare the financial
statements of sole traders, are introduced to limited company accounts, and then analyse and evaluate the
financial information those statements contain.

**Management accounting and decision making (sections 9 to 13).** Budgeting, marginal costing, standard costing
and variance analysis, absorption and activity based costing, and capital investment appraisal shift the focus
from recording the past to planning and controlling the future.

**Extending financial accounting and judgement (sections 14 to 18).** Accounting for organisations with
incomplete records, partnership accounts and accounting for limited companies take the financial accounting
strand further. Interpretation, analysis and communication of accounting information, and the impact of ethical
considerations, ask students to explain and judge rather than only calculate.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content drawn on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7127/1 Paper 1 | 3 hours | 120 | 50% | Sections 1-8 and 14-18 |
| 7127/2 Paper 2 | 3 hours | 120 | 50% | Sections 1-3, 8-13 and 17-18 |

Both papers are written exams with the same internal shape. Each is split into three compulsory sections:

- **Section A** (30 marks): 10 multiple-choice questions plus short-answer questions.
- **Section B** (40 marks): two structured questions.
- **Section C** (50 marks): two extended-answer questions.

There are no optional questions and no tiers, so every candidate sits the same two papers. The specification
was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed at A-level in June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The content split between the papers is the most useful thing to understand early. Paper 1 leans towards
financial accounting: sole trader statements, incomplete records, partnerships and limited companies. Paper 2
leans towards management accounting: budgeting, the costing methods and investment appraisal. The overlap is
deliberate. Sections 1 to 3 (the role of the accountant, types of organisation and double entry) appear on both
papers, as do section 8 (analysis and evaluation of financial information), section 17 (interpretation, analysis
and communication) and section 18 (ethical considerations).

That overlap tells you which skills the course treats as permanent rather than topic-specific. Ratio analysis,
written evaluation and ethical reasoning can be tested against a set of limited company accounts on Paper 1 or
against a budget or variance report on Paper 2. A student who treats ethics as a short section to learn once is
likely to be caught out, because it can be attached to almost any scenario.

The section structure of the papers also shapes the skills you need. Section A rewards accuracy and quick recall
across the whole of the paper's content. Section B rewards organised, methodical working through a structured
task. Section C, worth the most marks, rewards the ability to combine a substantial calculation with a reasoned
written judgement.

## Planning your study

A sensible route through the course follows the dependencies between sections:

1. **Start with sections 1 to 5.** Double entry, verification of records and the accounting concepts underpin
   everything that follows. Time spent making these automatic is repaid in every later topic.
2. **Move to financial statements (sections 6 and 7), then analysis (section 8).** Preparing statements before
   analysing them means ratios are calculated on figures you understand.
3. **Take management accounting as a block (sections 9 to 13).** Budgeting leads naturally into marginal,
   standard and absorption costing, and capital investment appraisal brings forward-looking decision making
   together.
4. **Return to financial accounting in more depth (sections 14 to 16).** Incomplete records, partnerships and
   accounting for limited companies all build on the double entry and statement preparation learned earlier.
5. **Thread sections 17 and 18 throughout.** Practise short written interpretations and ethical judgements
   alongside each topic instead of leaving them to the end.

In the first year, aim to secure the foundations, sole trader and limited company statements, and the first
management accounting topics. In the second year, complete the remaining sections and begin regular mixed
practice that combines content from both papers. Short timed questions from Section A and Section B styles can
start as soon as a topic is taught; full papers make most sense once the content for that paper has been covered.
Detailed paper-by-paper exam strategy belongs on the exam preparation page linked below.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in AQA A-Level Accounting?**
There are two written papers, 7127/1 and 7127/2. Each lasts 3 hours, is worth 120 marks and counts for 50% of the A-level.

**Is there any coursework in AQA A-Level Accounting?**
No. The qualification is assessed entirely by the two written papers, and it is linear, so both are taken at the end of the course.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers sections 1-8 and 14-18, with a financial accounting emphasis. Paper 2 covers sections 1-3, 8-13 and 17-18, with a management accounting emphasis. Sections 1-3, 8, 17 and 18 can appear on either paper.

**How is each paper structured?**
Each paper has three compulsory sections: Section A has 10 multiple-choice questions plus short-answer questions (30 marks), Section B has two structured questions (40 marks), and Section C has two extended-answer questions (50 marks).

**When was this specification first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed at A-level in June 2019.
