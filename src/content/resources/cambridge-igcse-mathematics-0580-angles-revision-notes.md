These notes condense subtopic **4.6 Angles** from Topic 4 Geometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Everything here is for both tiers except **irregular polygons**, which are Extended only. For explanations and worked examples, read the [angles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles/); to test yourself, use the [angles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-angles-practice/).

## The facts and their reasons

Every unknown angle comes from one of these. Learn the right-hand column word for word: the syllabus expects correct geometrical terminology in reasons.

| Situation | Fact | Reason to write |
|---|---|---|
| Angles round a point | sum to 360° | angles at a point sum to 360° |
| Angles on one side of a straight line | sum to 180° | angles on a straight line sum to 180° |
| Two straight lines crossing | opposite angles equal | vertically opposite angles are equal |
| Triangle | angles sum to 180° | angle sum of a triangle is 180° |
| Quadrilateral | angles sum to 360° | angle sum of a quadrilateral is 360° |
| Parallel lines, same position at each crossing | equal | corresponding angles are equal |
| Parallel lines, between them, opposite sides of the transversal | equal | alternate angles are equal |
| Parallel lines, between them, same side of the transversal | sum to 180° | co-interior angles sum to 180° |
| Isosceles triangle | base angles equal | base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal |

**Three-letter notation:** angle PQR is the angle **at Q**, between QP and QR.

## Polygon angles

```
exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°
interior + exterior = 180° at each vertex
interior angle sum = (n − 2) × 180°
regular polygon:  exterior = 360° ÷ n,   n = 360° ÷ exterior
```

| Polygon | n | Interior angle sum | Regular: each interior | Regular: each exterior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° | 60° | 120° |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° | 90° | 90° |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 108° | 72° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 120° | 60° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° | 135° | 45° |
| Nonagon | 9 | 1260° | 140° | 40° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° | 144° | 36° |
| Dodecagon | 12 | 1800° | 150° | 30° |

**Is it a regular polygon?** n = 360 ÷ exterior angle must be a whole number. An interior angle of 130° gives exterior 50° and 360 ÷ 50 = 7.2, so no regular polygon has interior angles of 130°.

**Irregular polygons (Extended only).** Only the sums work. Write an equation: (sum of the given angles) = (n − 2) × 180°, or (sum of the exterior angles) = 360°.

## Method for any angle chain

```
1. Mark every given angle and every equal-side or parallel marker on the diagram.
2. Find the angle you CAN find next, not necessarily the one asked for.
3. Write the value and its reason on the same line.
4. Repeat until you reach the target angle.
5. Check: angles on a line, in a triangle, at a point still add correctly.
```

**Worked reminder.** Lines AB and CD are parallel. A transversal meets AB at P and CD at Q, and B and D are on the same side of PQ. Angle BPQ = 64°. Find angle PQD.

```
angle BPQ and angle PQD are on the same side of PQ, between the lines
angle PQD = 180° − 64° = 116°   (co-interior angles sum to 180°)
```

## Algebra in angle questions

Form an equation from one fact, solve it, then **substitute back** to give the angle asked for.

- Angles on a line: (5x − 10) + (2x + 22) = 180 gives 7x = 168, x = 24, angles 110° and 70°.
- Exterior angles: a pentagon's exterior angles are x, x, 2x, 2x, 3x; then 9x = 360, x = 40, and the largest exterior angle is 120°, so the smallest interior angle is 60°.

## Exam traps

- Naming an angle with the wrong middle letter.
- Using parallel-line facts when the lines are not marked or stated as parallel.
- Writing "co-interior angles are equal". They are supplementary.
- Dividing the interior angle sum by n for an irregular polygon.
- Giving 360° ÷ n as the interior angle; it is the exterior angle.
- Short reasons such as "alternate" or "triangle". Write the full property.
- Measuring a diagram marked "not to scale".
- Forgetting to find the actual angle after solving for x.

## Self-test

1. Angles at a point are 85°, 120°, 72° and x. Find x.
2. Two angles on a straight line are 3y and y + 40°. Find y.
3. A triangle has angles 2a, 3a and 4a. Find the smallest angle.
4. An isosceles triangle has one angle of 70°. Give both possible sets of the other two angles.
5. Write down the reason that links two angles between parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
6. A transversal crosses two parallel lines. One of the angles it makes is 47°. What sizes can the other seven angles be?
7. Find the interior angle sum of a 14-sided polygon.
8. Find each interior angle of a regular 20-sided polygon.
9. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 18°. How many sides does it have?
10. Explain why no regular polygon has an interior angle of 145°.
11. (Extended) A quadrilateral has angles x, 2x, 100° and 116°. Find x.
12. (Extended) Four interior angles of a pentagon are 100°, 110°, 120° and 95°. Find the fifth.

### Answers

1. x = 360 − 85 − 120 − 72 = **83°**
2. 4y + 40 = 180, so **y = 35°**
3. 9a = 180, a = 20, smallest angle = **40°**
4. **70° and 40°** (70° is a base angle), or **55° and 55°** (70° is the apex angle)
5. **Alternate angles are equal.**
6. Each is either **47°** or **133°**.
7. (14 − 2) × 180 = **2160°**
8. Exterior 360 ÷ 20 = 18°, interior = **162°**
9. 360 ÷ 18 = **20 sides**
10. Exterior = 35°, and 360 ÷ 35 = 10.28…, which is **not a whole number**.
11. 3x + 216 = 360, so **x = 48°**
12. Sum = 540°; 540 − 425 = **115°**
