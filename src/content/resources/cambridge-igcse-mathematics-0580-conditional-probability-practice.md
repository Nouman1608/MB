These questions cover subtopic E8.4, Conditional probability, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is Extended only. No notation or formula is needed: every answer comes from reading the right numbers in a table, Venn diagram or tree diagram. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Learn the methods first with the [conditional probability study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-conditional-probability/) and the [conditional probability revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-conditional-probability-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (non-calculator) The table shows the 90 members of a sports club.

| | Swimming | Gym | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult | 22 | 28 | 50 |
| Child | 25 | 15 | 40 |

**(a)** A child member is chosen at random. Find the probability that the child swims. **[1]**

**(b)** A member who uses the gym is chosen at random. Find the probability that the member is an adult. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) In a class of 30 students, 8 study only Art, 5 study Art and Biology, 10 study only Biology and 7 study neither. A Biology student is chosen at random. Find the probability that this student also studies Art. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A bag contains 5 green and 3 yellow counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. Given that the first counter is green, write down the probability that the second counter is green. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A fair six-sided dice is rolled.

**(a)** Given that the score is odd, find the probability that it is 5 or more. **[1]**

**(b)** Given that the score is greater than 2, find the probability that it is even. **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) In a Venn diagram for events A and B, the probabilities are: A only 0.15, A and B 0.25, B only 0.35, neither 0.25. Given that A happens, find the probability that B also happens. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Two fair coins are tossed. Given that at least one coin shows heads, find the probability that both show heads. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (calculator) The probability that Sara passes her driving test at the first attempt is 0.6. If she fails, the probability that she passes at the second attempt is 0.7.

**(a)** Find the probability that she passes within two attempts. **[2]**

**(b)** Given that she passes within two attempts, find the probability that she passed at the first attempt. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator) A shop buys light bulbs from two suppliers. Supplier X provides 70% of the bulbs and 2% of these are faulty. Supplier Y provides 30% of the bulbs and 6% of these are faulty.

**(a)** Find the probability that a bulb chosen at random is faulty. **[2]**

**(b)** A bulb is chosen at random and is faulty. Find the probability that it came from supplier Y. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A bag contains 3 red, 2 blue and 1 green ball. Two balls are taken at random without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that both balls are red. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that neither ball is red. **[1]**

**(c)** Given that at least one ball is red, find the probability that both are red. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Two fair four-sided spinners, each numbered 1 to 4, are spun and the scores are added.

**(a)** Given that the total is 6, find the probability that both spinners show 3. **[2]**

**(b)** Given that at least one spinner shows 4, find the probability that the total is 7 or more. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) 120 tourists were asked whether they visited a museum (M) or a gallery (G). 70 visited M, 55 visited G, x visited both and 15 visited neither.

**(a)** Find x. **[2]**

**(b)** A tourist who visited the gallery is chosen at random. Find the probability that they also visited the museum. **[1]**

**(c)** A tourist who visited exactly one of the two places is chosen at random. Find the probability that it was the museum. **[2]**

**12.** (non-calculator) The table gives the probabilities for a student chosen at random from Years 10 and 11.

| | Hot meal | Packed lunch |
|---|---|---|
| Year 10 | 0.18 | 0.27 |
| Year 11 | 0.32 | 0.23 |

**(a)** Given that the student has a hot meal, find the probability that the student is in Year 11. **[2]**

**(b)** Given that the student is in Year 10, find the probability that the student has a packed lunch. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 25/40 = **5/8** [1]

**(b)** Gym total = 28 + 15 = 43; **28/43** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the gym column total is not in the table, so add it first. Dividing by 90 or by 50 answers a different question.

**2.** Biology students: 5 + 10 = 15 [1]; 5/15 = **1/3** [1]

**3.** 4 green out of 7 left: **4/7** [1]

**4. (a)** Odd: 1, 3, 5; only 5 is 5 or more: **1/3** [1]

**(b)** Greater than 2: 3, 4, 5, 6; even: 4, 6: **2/4 = 1/2** [1]

**5.** P(A) = 0.15 + 0.25 = 0.4 [1]; 0.25 ÷ 0.4 = **5/8** (0.625) [1]

**6.** At least one head: HH, HT, TH [1]; **1/3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 1/2 and 1/4 are the common wrong answers. Listing the outcomes that satisfy the condition settles it.

**7. (a)** 0.6 + 0.4 × 0.7 [1] = 0.6 + 0.28 = **0.88** [1]

**(b)** 0.6 ÷ 0.88 [1] = **15/22** (0.682) [1]

**8. (a)** 0.7 × 0.02 + 0.3 × 0.06 [1] = 0.014 + 0.018 = **0.032** [1]

**(b)** 0.018 ÷ 0.032 [1] = **9/16** (0.5625) [1]

*Examiner insight:* supplier Y provides fewer bulbs but more than half of the faulty ones. Giving 0.06 or 0.3 for part (b) uses a single branch rather than dividing the paths.

**9. (a)** 3/6 × 2/5 = **1/5** [1]

**(b)** 3/6 × 2/5 = **1/5** [1]

**(c)** P(at least one red) = 1 − 1/5 = 4/5 [1]; (1/5) ÷ (4/5) = **1/4** [1]

**10. (a)** Total 6: (2, 4), (3, 3), (4, 2) [1]; **1/3** [1]

**(b)** At least one 4: (4, 1), (4, 2), (4, 3), (4, 4), (1, 4), (2, 4), (3, 4), so 7 outcomes [1]; totals of 7 or more: (4, 3), (4, 4), (3, 4), so **3/7** [1]

**11. (a)** 70 + 55 − x + 15 = 120 [1]; **x = 20** [1]

**(b)** 20/55 = **4/11** [1]

**(c)** Museum only = 50 and gallery only = 35, so exactly one = 85 [1]; 50/85 = **10/17** [1]

**12. (a)** P(hot meal) = 0.18 + 0.32 = 0.5 [1]; 0.32 ÷ 0.5 = **0.64** [1]

**(b)** P(Year 10) = 0.18 + 0.27 = 0.45 [1]; 0.27 ÷ 0.45 = **0.6** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the grand total instead of the total for the condition.
- Swapping the event and the condition.
- Quoting a single tree branch in a reverse question instead of dividing paths.
- Missing outcomes that satisfy an "at least one" condition.
- Forgetting to add a missing row or column total before dividing.
