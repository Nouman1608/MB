## What the specification lists

- Risk and reward: meaning, relationship, evaluating the relationship.
- Quantifiable and unquantifiable risk: distinguish and evaluate.
- Ways to reduce risk; consequences of poor risk management for the business and stakeholders.
- Specific risks faced by an entrepreneur.
- Uncertainty: meaning; internal and external causes and their impact.
- Effects of uncertainty on aims and objectives, planning, decision making, business analysis and forecasting.

## Core recall

### Risk and reward

- Risk: chance that a decision turns out worse than hoped.
- Reward: the gain if it succeeds (profit, growth, share, return).
- Usually, higher potential reward means higher risk.
- Expected value (EV) = sum of probability × outcome. It is an average, not a forecast of what will happen.
- Spread (best minus worst case) and the chance of a loss show risk better than EV alone.

### Evaluating risk against reward: four questions

1. Can the business survive the worst case?
2. Does the option fit the objectives (growth or stability)?
3. What is the owners' attitude to risk?
4. How reliable are the probabilities?

### Quantifiable and unquantifiable

| | Quantifiable | Unquantifiable |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Data or records | No reliable basis for a number |
| Examples | Bad debts, machine breakdowns, theft | Rival's new technology, fashion change |
| Management | Insurance, provisions, pricing in | Flexibility, reserves, scenarios |

A risk can become quantifiable as records build up.

### Risk matrix (a common tool, not named in the specification)

- Score likelihood (1-5) × impact (1-5); deal with the highest scores first.
- Limitations: scores are judgements; a rare disaster and a frequent small loss can score the same.

### Four responses

| Response | Example |
|---|---|
| Reduce | Deposits and credit checks; staff training |
| Transfer | Insurance; fixed-price contracts |
| Avoid | Refuse the risky activity |
| Accept | Budget for it when action costs more than the likely loss |

### Poor risk management: who suffers

Owners (lost profit or capital), employees (job losses), lenders (missed repayments), suppliers (unpaid bills), customers (lost service), community (lost jobs). Severity depends on the loss relative to resources.

### The entrepreneur's specific risks

- Income: may earn less than in a job, or nothing.
- Capital: savings invested may be lost.
- Liability: unlimited for a sole trader or ordinary partnership; limited for a company, though lenders may ask for personal guarantees.
- Personal: stress, long hours, loss of employment benefits.
- Set against rewards: independence, all profits, an asset to sell.

### Uncertainty

- Cannot know what will happen, or cannot give reliable probabilities.
- Internal causes: staff performance, new equipment, reorganisation, cost estimates.
- External causes: exchange rates, interest rates, competitors, regulation, technology, tastes, weather.

### Effects of uncertainty on the five named areas

| Area | Typical effect |
|---|---|
| Aims and objectives | Ranges instead of single targets; survival may replace growth |
| Planning | Shorter plans, flexible budgets, scenarios |
| Decision making | Delay, lower-risk choices, fixing costs in advance |
| Business analysis | SWOT and PESTLE findings date quickly |
| Forecasting | Less accurate; several scenarios replace one figure |

## Quick worked reminder

Ashcombe Print (invented): option A EV £60,000, worst case +£50,000; option B EV £72,000, 60% chance of a loss, worst case −£20,000. B has the higher reward and the higher risk; the choice depends on reserves, objectives and the reliability of B's probabilities.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Risk | The possibility that an outcome is worse than expected |
| Reward | The benefit gained if a decision succeeds |
| Expected value | The probability-weighted average of the possible outcomes |
| Quantifiable risk | A risk whose likelihood or cost can be measured from data |
| Unquantifiable risk | A risk with no reliable basis for a number |
| Uncertainty | Not knowing what will happen, or being unable to give reliable probabilities |
| Entrepreneur | A person who takes the risk of starting or running a business for a reward |
| Unlimited liability | The owner is personally responsible for all business debts |

## Exam traps

- Do not choose on EV alone; check the worst case.
- Risk and uncertainty overlap, but uncertainty means probabilities are not reliable.
- Insurance does not cover strategic risks such as a rival's move.
- State whether the entrepreneur has limited or unlimited liability before discussing personal assets.
- Link each effect of uncertainty to the case.

## Self-test

1. Define risk and reward.
2. Option X: £80,000 (0.6), −£30,000 (0.4). Calculate its expected value.
3. What is the chance that Option X makes a loss?
4. Give one example each of a quantifiable and an unquantifiable risk.
5. A risk has likelihood 4 and impact 2. Another has likelihood 2 and impact 4. What does the matrix say, and what is its limitation here?
6. Name the four responses to a risk.
7. Why might insurance not protect a business from losing customers to a new rival?
8. State three specific risks an entrepreneur faces.
9. Distinguish between an internal and an external cause of uncertainty.
10. How might uncertainty affect a business's objectives?
11. How might uncertainty affect forecasting?
12. Give one consequence of poor risk management for suppliers.

### Answers

1. Risk: the chance that a decision turns out worse than hoped. Reward: the gain if it succeeds.
2. (0.6 × 80,000) + (0.4 × −30,000) = 48,000 − 12,000 = **£36,000**.
3. **40%** (0.4).
4. Quantifiable: bad debts based on past records. Unquantifiable: a rival launching a new technology.
5. Both score 8, so the matrix ranks them equally; but the second has a larger impact, which a business with low reserves may find harder to survive.
6. Reduce, transfer, avoid, accept.
7. Insurance covers insurable events such as fire or injury, not ordinary commercial losses from competition.
8. Any three: loss of income; loss of capital invested; personal liability for debts if unlimited; stress and loss of employment benefits.
9. Internal causes arise within the business, such as a new machine's reliability; external causes arise outside it, such as a change in interest rates.
10. Targets may be set as ranges, or survival may replace growth as the main objective.
11. Forecasts become less accurate, so several scenarios replace a single figure.
12. Unpaid invoices if the business fails, or the loss of a regular customer.
