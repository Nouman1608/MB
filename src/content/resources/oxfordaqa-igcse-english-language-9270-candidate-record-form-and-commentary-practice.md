## The scenario (original)

Sana's centre-set task is to write the opening speech for a radio debate on the proposition "Young
people should spend a year volunteering before university", either supporting or opposing it. Her
speech must persuade listeners, with factual accuracy, logical consistency and some emotional appeal.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** State the four things the specification requires students to do on the Candidate record form.
**[4]**

**2.** Sana lists a source as "a website about volunteering". Explain why this is not sufficient and
rewrite it with the details a moderator would need (you may invent plausible details). **[3]**

**3.** Name two processes the specification gives as examples for gathering information, and explain
why one might suit a long printed article. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why the note-making example should not be copied text. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the form matters to the reading marks as well as the writing marks. **[2]**

## Section B — Longer questions

**6.** Sana chose these five sources. For **three** of them, write a one-sentence reason for her choice
that links to her task. **[6]**

- (a) A charity's annual report on young volunteers.
- (b) A documentary following three gap-year volunteers.
- (c) An autobiography chapter by a writer who volunteered abroad.
- (d) A newspaper opinion column arguing that gap years favour wealthy families.
- (e) An interview Sana conducted with a teacher who runs the school's volunteering club.

**7.** Explain why including source (d) could strengthen Sana's speech even if she supports the
proposition. **[3]**

**8.** Improve this commentary sentence so that it explains rather than describes: "My speech has an
introduction, three points and a conclusion." **[3]**

**9.** Write a commentary paragraph of about 100 words explaining one piece of information Sana
included and one she rejected, with reasons. **[5]**

**10.** Write a commentary paragraph of about 80 words explaining two presentational or stylistic
features suitable for a radio speech. **[4]**

**11.** Read this commentary extract and identify **three** weaknesses. **[3]**

> I did my speech about volunteering. I found lots of sources on the internet. My speech is good
> because it is persuasive. I used lots of techniques. I am happy with it.

**12.** Create a checklist of six points Sana should check before submitting her Candidate record
form. **[6]**

## Answers

**How these are marked.** Short questions are point-marked as shown. For the longer questions, the
bracketed credits show what a strong answer includes; other valid points of the same quality earn the
same credit.

**1.** [1] each: give details of all sources, sufficient for a moderator to access them, at least
five; explain how and why the sources were chosen and the processes used; include one example of
note-making; write a commentary on the final assignment.

**2.** It is too vague for a moderator to find the exact page [1]. A rewritten entry with the
organisation or author and page title [1] and the full web address and date accessed [1].

**3.** Two of note-making, annotation, highlighting [2]. Explanation [1]: for example, annotation or
colour-coded highlighting suits a long printed article because key points can be marked in place and
found again quickly.

**4.** The example must show the student's own selection and transformation of information [1];
copied text shows neither and may raise concerns about authenticity [1].

**5.** Every reading band includes "five sources cited on Candidate record form" [1], and the upper
reading bands reward "explanation, example and commentary" on the reading and research processes,
which the form provides [1].

**6.** Three reasons, [2] each, with [1] for a reason and [1] for a link to the task's audience or
purpose: for example, (a) gives factual information on young volunteers, supporting the factual
accuracy the task requires; (b) gives real young people's experiences, adding emotional appeal for
listeners; (c) offers a personal account Sana can rework as an anecdote; (d) presents the opposing
view, so she can answer it; (e) gives a local, expert voice and a perspective she gathered herself.

**7.** A persuasive speech is stronger when it anticipates objections [1]; the column gives Sana a
counter-argument about fairness to answer [1], which supports the "logical consistency" the task
asks for and shows breadth of reading [1].

**8.** An improved sentence that keeps the structure [1], gives a reason [1], and links it to the
audience or purpose [1], for example: "I organised the speech into three points, each opening with a
signpost such as 'First', so that radio listeners, who cannot reread, could follow the argument."

**9.** Credit up to 5: a named piece of included information [1]; a reason linked to purpose or
audience [1]; a named piece of rejected information [1]; a reason for the rejection, such as being
out of date, too technical or unreliable [1]; clear, explaining sentences using "because" or "so
that" [1].

**10.** Credit up to 4: two features suited to speech, such as signposting, repetition, rhetorical
questions, direct address or a memorable final line [2]; an explanation of each feature's effect on
listeners [2].

**11.** Any three, [1] each: it retells the topic rather than explaining choices; sources are not
named or explained; "good because it is persuasive" gives no evidence; "lots of techniques" names no
feature; no reasons for including or rejecting information; no comment on organisation; the final
sentence evaluates without explanation.

**12.** Any six, [1] each: at least five sources listed; each source detailed enough for a moderator
to find; a reason given for each source; the processes named and explained; one note-making example
included; the commentary covers inclusion and rejection; the commentary covers organisation; the
commentary covers presentational and stylistic features; sentences explain with "because" or "so
that"; the centre's deadline and rules are met.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Vague source details that a moderator could not follow.
- Reasons for sources that ignore the task's audience and purpose.
- Commentaries that describe the assignment instead of explaining choices.
- Missing one of the three commentary focuses.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a word limit for the commentary?**
The specification does not set one for the commentary. It says a word limit may be set for the task
where appropriate, so follow your teacher's guidance.
