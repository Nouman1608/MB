## Section A

**1.** Define the term *laissez-faire management*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify the **four** traditional functions of a manager. **[4]**

**3.** Explain **one** difference between Fayol's and Mintzberg's views of what managers do. **[2]**

**4.** A factory manager (i) chairs a meeting with a union to agree a pay deal, (ii) tells all supervisors about a new safety rule, and (iii) attends the opening of a local school as the company's representative. Identify the Mintzberg role shown by each activity. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why a Theory X manager is likely to use an autocratic style. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** advantage of a paternalistic management style. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Using an example from a building firm, explain how the controlling function links back to planning. **[4]**

**8.** After a new manager took over at Hendry Bakeries, waste fell from 1 200 loaves a week to 900 loaves a week. Each wasted loaf costs $0.80 to make.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in waste.

**(b)** Calculate the annual saving, assuming 52 weeks.

**(c)** Explain **one** reason why the fall in waste may not be due to the new manager. **[6]**

**9.** Analyse how a manager's role as resource allocator could affect the performance of a school. **[4]**

**10.** Ellerby Design employs twelve experienced graphic designers. Its new manager uses an autocratic style. Analyse the likely effects on the business. **[6]**

**11.** Explain how McGregor's Theory Y could influence the way a manager motivates staff. **[4]**

**12.** Pardoe Engineering is facing a fall in orders and must cut costs quickly. Its managing director must decide whether to use an autocratic or a democratic style to manage the changes. Evaluate which style would be more appropriate. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** A style in which the manager gives staff broad objectives [1] and leaves them to make most decisions about how to do their work [1].

**2.** Planning [1]; organising [1]; directing [1]; controlling [1].

**3.** Fayol described management as an orderly set of elements such as planning and controlling, from his experience [1], whereas Mintzberg observed that managers' work is fragmented and reactive, filling many interpersonal, informational and decisional roles [1].

**4.** (i) Negotiator [1]. (ii) Disseminator [1]. (iii) Figurehead [1].

**5.** A Theory X manager assumes staff dislike work and avoid responsibility [1], so believes close control and direct instructions are needed to get work done [1].

**6.** Staff feel the manager cares about their welfare, which builds loyalty [1], so labour turnover may be lower and staff accept decisions more readily [1].

**7.** Planning sets targets, such as completing a house in 20 weeks within a $250 000 budget [1]. Controlling compares actual progress and costs with these targets each week [1]. If the project is behind schedule or over budget, the manager takes corrective action, such as adding workers [1], and uses what is learned to set more realistic targets in the next plan [1].

**8.** **(a)** (1 200 − 900) ÷ 1 200 × 100 [1] = 25% [1].

**(b)** Weekly saving = 300 × 0.80 = $240 [1]; annual saving = 240 × 52 = $12 480 [1].

**(c)** Any one, explained. Demand may have fallen [1], so fewer loaves were baked and less was wasted, whatever the manager did [1]. Allow new ovens or better forecasting software installed at the same time.

**9.** As resource allocator, the head teacher decides how the budget is divided, for example between teachers, equipment and buildings [1]. Spending more on extra teachers could reduce class sizes [1], which may raise exam results and the school's reputation [1]. However, this leaves less for other areas, such as repairs, which could affect learning conditions [1].

**10.** The designers are experienced and creative, so they expect to use their own judgement [1]. An autocratic manager makes decisions without consulting them [1], so their ideas are lost and the quality of designs may fall [1]. Designers may feel undervalued, lowering motivation [1], and some may leave for firms that give them more freedom [1], raising recruitment costs and losing clients who valued particular designers [1].

**11.** A Theory Y manager assumes staff can enjoy work and seek responsibility [1]. The manager is therefore likely to delegate tasks and empower staff to make decisions [1], and to use non-financial motivators such as job enrichment and recognition [1], rather than relying on close supervision and pay linked to output [1].

**12.** For autocratic: cuts must be made quickly, and an autocratic style allows fast decisions without lengthy consultation [1]; staff may disagree about which costs to cut, so a clear decision from the top avoids deadlock [1]; the managing director may have financial information that staff lack [1].
For democratic: staff know where waste occurs and may suggest savings managers would miss [1]; involving them may reduce resistance and keep morale higher during difficult changes [1]; if job losses are needed, consultation is more likely to keep trust with the remaining workforce [1].
Judgement: a mixed approach may be most appropriate [1]; the managing director could set the size of the saving needed autocratically but consult staff on how to achieve it [1]. The choice depends on how urgent the cuts are and how skilled and trusted the workforce is [1]. If the business would fail without immediate action, the autocratic element should dominate [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a function or style without an example from the business.
- Mislabelling Mintzberg roles, such as calling a deal-making activity "leader".
- Describing Theory X and Theory Y as kinds of workers rather than managers' assumptions.
- Calculating a weekly figure when an annual one is asked for.
- Choosing a style in an "evaluate" question without saying what the choice depends on.
