## Triangle properties

- The interior angles add to 180°.
- An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior angles opposite it.
- An isosceles triangle has two equal sides and the angles opposite them are equal. An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three 60° angles.
- The longest side is opposite the largest angle.
- Triangle inequality: any two sides add to more than the third side.

**Example 1.** The angles of a triangle are (x + 20)°, 2x° and (3x − 20)°. Find them.
(x + 20) + 2x + (3x − 20) = 180, so 6x = 180 and x = 30. The angles are **50°, 60°, 70°**. Check: they add to 180°.

**Example 2.** An exterior angle is 125° and one opposite interior angle is 48°. The other opposite angle is 125 − 48 = **77°**.

**Example 3.** Can 5 cm, 7 cm and 13 cm form a triangle? 5 + 7 = 12, which is less than 13, so **no**. Can 6, 7 and 12? 6 + 7 = 13 > 12, and the other pairs are larger, so **yes**.

## Bearings

A bearing is an angle measured **clockwise from north**, written with three figures (so 045°, not 45°). To find a back bearing, add or subtract 180°.

**Example 4.** A boat leaves harbour H and sails 12 km on bearing 060° to point K. It then sails 9 km on bearing 150° to point P.
- At K, the bearing 150° is 90° clockwise from 060°, so the path turns through a right angle. HKP is a right-angled triangle.
- HP² = 12² + 9² = 225, so HP = **15 km**.
- The angle at H between HK and HP is tan⁻¹(9/12) = 36.9°. The bearing of P from H is 060° + 36.9° = **096.9°**.
- The bearing of H from P is 096.9° + 180° = **276.9°**.

Always sketch a north line at each point. Clockwise angles are measured from that line.

## Pythagoras' theorem

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c (the side opposite the right angle): **a² + b² = c²**.

**Example 5.** A support wire of length 8.5 m is fixed to a pole at a point, and its foot is 4.0 m from the base of the pole. How high is the fixing point?
Height² = 8.5² − 4.0² = 72.25 − 16 = 56.25, so height = **7.5 m**. When finding a shorter side, you subtract.

**Example 6.** A rectangular screen is 40 cm wide and 25 cm high. Diagonal² = 40² + 25² = 2225, so the diagonal is **47.2 cm** (3 s.f.).

## Trigonometric ratios in right-angled triangles

Label the sides from the angle θ you are using: hypotenuse, opposite (across from θ) and adjacent (next to θ).

- sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
- cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
- tan θ = opposite / adjacent

To find an angle, use the inverse: θ = sin⁻¹(…), cos⁻¹(…) or tan⁻¹(…). Check that your calculator is in degree mode.

**Example 7.** A ramp rises 1.2 m and makes 15° with the ground. Its length L is the hypotenuse and 1.2 is opposite, so sin 15° = 1.2/L, giving L = 1.2 ÷ sin 15° = **4.64 m**.

**Example 8.** You stand 30 m from a tower. The angle of elevation of its top is 38° and your eye is 1.6 m above ground. Height above eye level = 30 × tan 38° = 23.44 m. Tower height = 23.44 + 1.6 = **25.0 m** (3 s.f.).

**Example 9.** A slope rises 7 m over a horizontal distance of 24 m. tan θ = 7/24, so θ = tan⁻¹(7/24) = **16.3°**.

An angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal. It equals the angle of elevation from the lower point to the upper, because the two are alternate angles.

## Converse of Pythagoras (extended)

If the three sides satisfy a² + b² = c² for the longest side c, the triangle is right-angled. If they do not, it is not.

**Example 10.** Is a triangle with sides 9, 40 and 41 right-angled? 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681 and 41² = 1681. **Yes**, and the right angle is opposite the side of 41.
For 7, 8 and 11: 49 + 64 = 113, but 11² = 121. **Not right-angled.**

## Sine rule and cosine rule (extended)

For a triangle with sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C:

- **Sine rule:** a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C
- **Cosine rule:** a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A, and cos A = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc

**Example 11 (sine rule, side).** A = 48°, B = 71°, a = 14. Then b = 14 × sin 71° ÷ sin 48° = **17.8**.

**Example 12 (sine rule, ambiguous case).** a = 9, b = 12, A = 40°.
sin B = 12 × sin 40° ÷ 9 = 0.857. So B = 59.0° or B = 180° − 59.0° = 121.0°.
Both are possible because 40° + 121.0° is still less than 180°. Then C = 81.0° or 19.0°, and c = 13.8 or 4.56. Only a given angle opposite the shorter given side can produce two triangles.

**Example 13 (cosine rule, side).** Two sides 7.2 and 9.5 enclose 58°. Third side² = 7.2² + 9.5² − 2(7.2)(9.5)cos 58° = 69.6, so the side is **8.34**.

**Example 14 (cosine rule, angle).** Sides 6, 8, 11. cos C = (36 + 64 − 121)/(2 × 6 × 8) = −0.21875, so C = **102.6°**. A negative cosine means an obtuse angle.

**Link to trigonometric functions.** For an obtuse angle, sin θ = sin(180° − θ), which is why the sine rule can give two answers. The cosine of an obtuse angle is negative.

**Example 15 (application).** Surveyors measure from A: B is 420 m away on bearing 072°, and C is 310 m away on bearing 135°. Angle BAC = 135° − 72° = 63°. BC² = 420² + 310² − 2(420)(310)cos 63° gives BC = **393 m**.

## Enrichment ideas

These appear in the framework's enrichment list only.

- **3D coordinate geometry:** the distance between (x₁, y₁, z₁) and (x₂, y₂, z₂) is √[(Δx)² + (Δy)² + (Δz)²]. From (1, 2, 3) to (5, −2, 6): √(16 + 16 + 9) = √41 = 6.40.
- **Area of a triangle:** area = ½ab sin C. With sides 8 and 11 enclosing 35°: ½ × 8 × 11 × sin 35° = **25.2**.
- **Unit circle:** a point on a circle of radius 1 at angle θ from the positive x-axis is (cos θ, sin θ). For 150°, sin = 0.5 and cos = −0.866.
- **Radians:** 180° = π radians. So 150° = 5π/6, and 2.4 rad = 137.5°.
- **Circle with centre at the origin:** x² + y² = r². The point (3, −4) lies on x² + y² = 25.
- **Identities:** sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, cos θ = 4/5 and tan θ = 3/4. If θ is obtuse, cos θ = −4/5.

1. Sketch the situation and label what you know. Add north lines for bearings.
2. Is there a right angle? If yes, use Pythagoras for two sides and one unknown side, or sin, cos, tan when an angle is involved.
3. No right angle? Check what you know. A matching side and opposite angle pair, plus one more piece: use the sine rule. Two sides and the angle between them, or three sides: use the cosine rule.
4. Substitute, solve, then check the answer is sensible (longest side opposite largest angle).
5. Round at the end and give units or degrees.

## Linking to the criteria

Criterion A rewards choosing and applying the right method. Criterion C rewards clear diagrams and reasoning. Criterion D asks you to justify the accuracy of a solution and whether it makes sense in context, so state your rounding and compare answers with the situation. A criterion B investigation might explore Pythagorean triples and generalise a rule.

## Common errors

- Using a² + b² = c² when the unknown is a shorter side, instead of subtracting.
- Calculator in radian mode.
- Measuring bearings from the wrong north line or anticlockwise.
- Using the cosine rule's minus sign wrongly, for instance computing (b² + c² − 2bc) first.
- Forgetting the second possible angle in the ambiguous case.
- Rounding early.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you write a bearing?**
A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from north and written with three figures, so 045° rather than 45°. Sketch a north line at each point before measuring.

**How do you find the back bearing?**
Add 180° to the bearing, or subtract 180° if the result would be 360° or more. If P is on a bearing of 096.9° from H, then H is on a bearing of 276.9° from P.

**What is an angle of depression?**
An angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal. It equals the angle of elevation from the lower point back up to the higher one.

**Which trigonometry topics in IB MYP Mathematics are extended?**
Triangle properties, bearings, Pythagoras' theorem and right-angled trigonometry are standard. The converse of Pythagoras and the sine and cosine rules are extended content in the spatial reasoning branch.
