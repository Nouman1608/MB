These questions cover section 3.3.4 Good customer services of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Good customer services study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-good-customer-services-aqa-gcse-business/) and the [Good customer services revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-good-customer-services-aqa-gcse-business-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** methods of good customer service. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** examples of post-sales services. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** dangers to a business of poor customer service. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** way good product knowledge among staff can benefit a business. **[3]**

**5.** Explain how a business's website can improve its customer service. **[3]**

**6.** A hair salon has 800 regular clients who each spend £45 a month. Poor service causes 6% of them to leave.

**(a)** Calculate the number of clients lost. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the revenue lost per year. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Kingfisher Kitchens sells and fits kitchen appliances such as ovens and dishwashers. Analyse how post-sales services could benefit Kingfisher Kitchens. **[6]**

**8.** Explain how customer engagement can lead to increased spend by customers. **[4]**

**9.** Sandpiper Travel receives many complaints on social media about slow replies to customer emails.

**(a)** Explain **one** danger to Sandpiper Travel of these public complaints. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** way Sandpiper Travel could use social media to improve its customer service. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how e-commerce has allowed customer service to develop. **[3]**

**11.** Westbrook Books is an independent bookshop competing with large online retailers that offer lower prices. Analyse how Westbrook Books could use good customer service to compete. **[6]**

**12.** Fernhill Motors, a car dealership, is considering spending £30,000 a year on staff training in product knowledge and customer engagement. Last year it sold 400 cars, and online reviews often mention pushy, poorly informed sales staff. Evaluate whether Fernhill Motors should make this investment. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: product knowledge [1]; customer engagement (creating a positive experience for the customer) [1]; post-sales services. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: user training [1]; help lines [1]; servicing. **[2]**

**3.** Any two of: dissatisfied customers [1]; poor reputation via word of mouth [1]; reduction in revenue. **[2]**

**4.** Staff who know the products can match each product to the customer's needs [1], so customers buy the right product first time and trust the advice they are given [1], which means fewer returns and more repeat visits, increasing revenue (or reducing costs) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A website can give product information, prices, opening times and answers to common questions [1] at any time of day, without customers needing to phone or visit [1], so customers get answers quickly and conveniently, raising their satisfaction (or the business can let customers track orders online) [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** 6% of 800 = 48 clients [1].
**(b)** 48 × £45 = £2,160 a month [1]; £2,160 × 12 = £25,920 a year [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Kitchen appliances are expensive and can be complicated to use [1], so offering user training, such as showing customers how to use a new oven's settings, helps them get the most from their purchase [1]. A help line reassures customers that problems will be solved quickly [1], making them more confident about buying from Kingfisher rather than a cheaper online seller [1]. Servicing plans can also earn extra revenue after the sale [1], and satisfied customers are more likely to return for future appliances and recommend Kingfisher, increasing spend and profitability [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Customer engagement means creating a positive experience, such as a friendly welcome and staff who take time to help [1]. Customers who enjoy visiting are more likely to stay longer and return more often [1]. Staff who build a relationship can also suggest products that suit the customer, such as accessories [1], so each customer spends more over time, increasing the business's revenue [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Complaints on social media can be seen by many potential customers [1], so Sandpiper's reputation suffers far more widely than through one customer telling friends [1], and people planning holidays may book with rivals instead, reducing revenue [1].
**(b)** Sandpiper could reply quickly to messages on social media, apologising and solving problems in public [1]. This shows other customers that it cares and fixes problems [1], which could repair its reputation and turn a complaint into a sign of good service [1]. **[6]**

**10.** E-commerce lets customers buy at any time and from anywhere, without visiting a shop [1]. Online stores can remember past orders and preferences, making repeat purchases quicker [1], and customers can track deliveries and arrange returns online, which makes buying more convenient [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Westbrook cannot match the online retailers' prices [1], so it needs to offer something they cannot [1]. Staff with strong product knowledge can recommend books based on what customers have enjoyed before [1], which online retailers find harder to do in a personal way. Customer engagement, such as author events and a welcoming shop, creates a positive experience [1] that makes visiting enjoyable rather than just a transaction. These customers are likely to become loyal and spend more over time, even if prices are higher [1], allowing Westbrook to keep a profitable group of regular customers despite the competition [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For the investment:** online reviews show customers find staff pushy and poorly informed [1], which is likely to be losing sales to rival dealers [1]. Training in product knowledge would help staff explain cars accurately and recommend the right model [1], and training in engagement would create a more positive experience [1]. Cars are expensive, so even a small rise in sales could cover the cost: if each car earns £1,000 profit, Fernhill needs only 30 extra sales a year, 7.5% of last year's 400 [1]. Better reviews would also improve its reputation via word of mouth [1]. **Against:** £30,000 a year is a significant ongoing cost [1], and training may not change staff behaviour if, for example, their commission encourages pushy selling. **Judgement:** Fernhill should invest [1], because the reviews show a clear problem that is likely to be costing sales, and the number of extra sales needed to cover the cost is small; it should monitor reviews and sales afterwards to check the training is working [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* An assumption such as the profit per car is acceptable if you state it clearly and use it to support your judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing good service as politeness without naming specific methods.
- Listing benefits without showing how satisfaction leads to loyalty, spend and profit.
- Forgetting that social media is both an opportunity and a risk.
- Choosing methods that do not suit the product in the case.
- Ignoring the cost of providing better service in an evaluation.
- Forgetting to multiply monthly figures by 12 for a year.
