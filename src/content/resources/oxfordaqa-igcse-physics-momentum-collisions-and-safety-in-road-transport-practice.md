## Questions

**1.** A golf ball of mass 0.046 kg leaves the club at 55 m/s.
**(a)** Calculate the momentum of the ball. **[2]**
**(b)** State the unit of momentum. **[1]**

**2.** A trolley of mass 1.5 kg moving at 2.0 m/s collides with a stationary trolley of mass 0.50 kg. The trolleys stick together. Calculate their velocity after the collision. **[4]**

**3.** Two trolleys of mass 0.80 kg and 0.30 kg are at rest, touching, with a compressed spring between them. The spring is released and the 0.30 kg trolley moves off at 1.6 m/s.
**(a)** State the total momentum of the trolleys before release. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the velocity of the 0.80 kg trolley. **[3]**

**4.** At a fairground, a dodgem car and driver of total mass 220 kg moves right at 3.0 m/s. A second car and driver of total mass 180 kg moves left at 2.0 m/s. They collide head on and stick together. Calculate their velocity immediately after the collision, giving its direction. **[5]**

**5.** A ball of mass 0.050 kg hits a wall at 24 m/s and rebounds along the same line at 16 m/s. The contact lasts 0.010 s. Calculate the average force on the ball. **[4]**

**6.** Explain how an air bag and a seat belt together reduce the risk of serious injury to a driver in a head-on collision. **[4]**

**7.** A gymnast of mass 50 kg lands at 5.0 m/s and stops completely.
**(a)** Calculate the force on the gymnast if she stops in 0.050 s on a hard floor. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the force if she stops in 0.40 s on a crash mat. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a cycle helmet protects a cyclist's head in a similar way. **[2]**

**8.** A lorry of mass 8000 kg moves along a straight, level road at a steady speed. The driving force is 6200 N.
**(a)** State the size of the resistive forces. **[1]**
**(b)** The driver increases the driving force to 7400 N. The resistive forces stay the same. Calculate the initial acceleration. **[3]**

**9.** A van of mass 2400 kg travels at 15 m/s. The driver's reaction time is 0.80 s. The braking force is 7200 N.
**(a)** Calculate the thinking distance. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the van. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the braking distance. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the stopping distance, and the deceleration while braking. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** State two factors that can increase a driver's reaction time. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which part of the stopping distance is affected by a longer reaction time. **[2]**
**(c)** A car brakes with the same force at 30 m/s and at 15 m/s. Compare the kinetic energies and the braking distances. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why a very large deceleration is dangerous. **[2]**

**11.** A car of mass 1250 kg travels at 25 m/s on a dry road. The driver's reaction time is 0.60 s and the braking force is 6250 N.
**(a)** Calculate the stopping distance. **[5]**
**(b)** The driver has been drinking alcohol and the reaction time becomes 1.4 s. Calculate the new stopping distance. **[2]**
**(c)** On a wet road the braking force is halved. Calculate the braking distance and explain why it changes. **[3]**
**(d)** Describe the energy transfers in the brakes as the car stops. **[2]**

**12.** A truck of mass 2400 kg moves at 6.0 m/s and runs into the back of a stationary car of mass 1200 kg. The two vehicles stay joined after impact.
**(a)** Find their common velocity. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in momentum of the car. **[2]**
**(c)** The contact lasts 0.30 s. Calculate the average force on the car. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how a crumple zone on the car would change this force. **[2]**
**(e)** Show that the kinetic energy has decreased in the collision. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** p = mv = 0.046 × 55 [1] = 2.53, so **2.5 kg m/s** (2 significant figures) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **kg m/s** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution before the answer. Give the unit with the number as well as stating it separately.

**2.** Take the original direction as positive. Momentum before = 1.5 × 2.0 + 0.50 × 0 [1] = 3.0 kg m/s [1]. Momentum after = (1.5 + 0.50) × v = 2.0v [1]. Conservation of momentum: v = 3.0 / 2.0 = **1.5 m/s** in the original direction [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the combined mass after the collision is the key step; writing a line with total momentum before and after makes your method clear to the reader.

**3. (a)** **0** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Take the direction of the 0.30 kg trolley as positive. Total after must be zero: 0.30 × 1.6 + 0.80 × v = 0 [1]. 0.48 + 0.80v = 0, so 0.80v = −0.48 [1]. v = **−0.60 m/s**, so the 0.80 kg trolley moves at 0.60 m/s, opposite to the other trolley [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the direction as well as the speed. A negative sign with no explanation of what it means leaves the answer incomplete.

**4.** Take right as positive. Before: 220 × 3.0 + 180 × (−2.0) [1] = 660 − 360 [1] = 300 kg m/s [1]. After: (220 + 180) × v = 400v [1]. v = 300 / 400 = **0.75 m/s to the right** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign of the second car's momentum is the common slip. Mark the positive direction on a sketch first.

**5.** Take the direction towards the wall as positive. Initial momentum = 0.050 × 24 = 1.2 kg m/s [1]. Final momentum = 0.050 × (−16) = −0.80 kg m/s [1]. Δp = −0.80 − 1.2 = −2.0 kg m/s, size 2.0 kg m/s [1]. F = Δp / t = 2.0 / 0.010 = **200 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* When an object rebounds, the velocity changes sign, so the change is the sum of the two speeds, not their difference.

**6.** The driver's momentum must change to zero in the collision [1]. The seat belt stretches slightly and the air bag inflates and cushions the head and chest, so the driver stops over a longer time [1]. F = Δp / t, so a longer time means a smaller force [1]. The air bag also spreads the force over a larger area [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the chain: longer time, momentum changes more slowly, smaller force. Saying only that the features "protect" or "absorb the impact" does not explain.

**7. (a)** Δp = 50 × 5.0 = 250 kg m/s [1]. F = 250 / 0.050 [1] = **5000 N** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** F = 250 / 0.40 [1] = **625 N** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The foam inside the helmet crushes, so the head takes longer to stop [1]. The change in momentum happens over a longer time, so the force on the head is smaller [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) asks for the same idea in a new context; reusing the time-and-force reasoning, with the helmet foam named, is the point of the question.

**8. (a)** **6200 N** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Resultant force = 7400 − 6200 = 1200 N [1]. a = F / m = 1200 / 8000 [1] = **0.15 m/s²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The resultant force is a difference, not the driving force itself. Subtracting the resistive force before using F = ma is the step to show.

**9. (a)** Thinking distance = speed × reaction time = 15 × 0.80 [1] = **12 m** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2400 × 15² [1] = **270 000 J** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Work done by the brakes = kinetic energy lost, so 7200 × d = 270 000 [1]. d = **37.5 m** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Stopping distance = 12 + 37.5 = **49.5 m** [1]. a = F / m = 7200 / 2400 [1] = **3.0 m/s²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the braking distance alone as the stopping distance is a common loss. Naming the two parts and adding them shows the method clearly.

**10. (a)** Any two of: tiredness, distractions, drugs, alcohol [1] [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The thinking distance [1], because the vehicle keeps moving at its original speed during the reaction time [1] **[2]**
**(c)** KE is proportional to speed squared, so the KE at 30 m/s is four times the KE at 15 m/s [1]. The same braking force does the same amount of work per metre, so the braking distance is four times as long [1] [1] **[3]**
**(d)** Brakes can overheat and become less effective [1]; the wheels may skid so the driver loses control [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is about a comparison. Give the ratio (four times), not just "greater", and state why using kinetic energy.

**11. (a)** Thinking distance = 25 × 0.60 = 15 m [1]. KE = ½ × 1250 × 25² [1] = 390 625 J [1]. Braking distance = 390 625 / 6250 = 62.5 m [1]. Stopping distance = 15 + 62.5 = **77.5 m** [1] **[5]**
**(b)** Thinking distance = 25 × 1.4 = 35 m [1]. Stopping distance = 35 + 62.5 = **97.5 m** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Braking distance = 390 625 / 3125 = **125 m** [1]. On a wet road there is less friction between the tyres and the road, so the largest braking force available is smaller [1], and the car needs a longer distance to lose its kinetic energy [1] **[3]**
**(d)** Friction between the brake pads and the wheel does work [1]; kinetic energy is transferred to thermal energy and the temperature of the brakes rises [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), halving the force doubles the braking distance because the energy to remove is the same. Say that the thinking distance is unchanged.

**12. (a)** Take the truck's direction as positive. Before: 2400 × 6.0 = 14 400 kg m/s [1]. After: (2400 + 1200) × v = 3600v [1]. v = **4.0 m/s** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Δp = 1200 × 4.0 [1] = **4800 kg m/s** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** F = Δp / t = 4800 / 0.30 [1] = **16 000 N** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** The crumple zone would collapse, increasing the contact time [1]; the same change in momentum over a longer time gives a smaller force on the car and its passengers [1] **[2]**
**(e)** KE before = ½ × 2400 × 6.0² = 43 200 J [1]. KE after = ½ × 3600 × 4.0² = 28 800 J [1]. 28 800 < 43 200, so kinetic energy has decreased [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out both kinetic energy totals and compare them. Momentum is conserved here but kinetic energy is not, and saying so shows understanding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to define a positive direction, then adding momenta that point in opposite directions.
- Using only one object's mass after two objects stick together, or doubling braking distance when speed doubles (it is four times).
- Saying a safety feature "reduces the momentum" or "absorbs the force" rather than increasing the time.
