**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 2, Renaissance and Reformation (circa 1350 to 1700), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. The model cases are Italy, England (Renaissance) and Germany (Reformation). For fuller coverage see the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-renaissance-reformation/).

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from two different regional studies in two hours. Top-band essays understand the argument in the question, evaluate a range of perspectives in a balanced way and reach a judgement that follows from the evaluation.

## Italy: why did the Renaissance emerge there?

- **Politics and society:** competing city-states, rich patrons, civic pride.
- **Economy:** trade, banking, cloth; wealth to spend on art.
- **Ideas:** humanism, classical texts, Greek scholars after 1453; the Church as a major patron.
- **Individuals:** Medici (Cosimo, Lorenzo), Sforza (Francesco, Ludovico), popes (Nicholas V, Julius II, Leo X).
- Caution: "rebirth" can overstate the break with the Middle Ages.

| Arts | Holbein at court; Inigo Jones and classical building |

## Reformation: emergence

- **State of the Church:** rich and powerful; pluralism, poorly trained priests, indulgences; humanist criticism.
- **Ideas:** justification by faith; scripture alone; challenge to papal authority.
- **Response:** excommunication (1521); Diet of Worms; Edict of Worms; Charles V distracted by France and the Ottomans.
- **Princes:** Frederick the Wise protected Luther; princes also gained wealth and power.
- **Print:** pamphlets, woodcuts and German Bible spread ideas faster than before.

## Germany: impact

- **Religious:** Lutheran services in German, clergy marriage; Peace of Augsburg (1555).
- **Social and political:** Peasants' War; princes gained authority over churches; Empire divided by faith.
- **Economic:** church lands taken over; funds redirected to schools and poor relief.
- **Thirty Years' War:** Bohemian revolt, foreign intervention; religious and dynastic motives; heavy damage; Westphalia (1648).

## Counter-Reformation

| Element | Points |
|---|---|
| Erasmus | Reformer who stayed Catholic; split with Luther over free will |
| Trent | Reaffirmed doctrine; seminaries; bishops to reside in dioceses |
| Paul III | Jesuits (1540); called Trent; Roman Inquisition (1542) |
| Paul IV | Severe; Index (1559); Rome ghetto |
| Pius IV | Concluded the Council (1563) |
| Jesuits | Education, missions, discipline |

## Historians' views

- Humanism and the "Renaissance" idea: older view of sharp break; newer view stresses continuity and local variety.
- Reformation causes: Church weakness against political and social explanations; "confessionalization" stresses church and state building together.
- Thirty Years' War: religious war or struggle for power?

## Common mistakes

- Using Italy as the only example when the question asks for another country.
- Treating Luther as the single cause; ignoring princes, print and politics.
- Saying the Church was "corrupt" without evidence.
- Confusing Counter-Reformation and Catholic Reformation.
- Writing a narrative of events instead of answering the question.

## Self-test

1. Name one reason the Renaissance began in Italy rather than elsewhere.
2. What year was the Diet of Worms?
3. Why did Charles V fail to enforce the Edict of Worms?
4. What did the Peace of Augsburg allow?
5. Name two things the Council of Trent did.
6. Which pope approved the Jesuits?

**Answers:** 1. Competing rich city-states and patrons, or trade and banking wealth. 2. 1521. 3. He was occupied with wars against France and the Ottomans and relied on princes who protected Luther. 4. Each ruler could choose Catholicism or Lutheranism for his territory. 5. Reaffirmed doctrine; reformed clergy training and discipline. 6. Paul III.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which country should I choose for the Renaissance beyond Italy?**
Any non-Italian country you know in depth, such as England, France or the Low Countries.

**Do I need dates?**
Yes, for the key events, but explanation counts more than a long list of dates.
