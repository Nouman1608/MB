## Core definitions

- **Effective demand:** the quantity consumers are **willing and able** to buy at each price over a period. Wants or needs without purchasing power do not count.
- **Supply:** the quantity producers are **willing and able** to sell at each price over a period.
- **Individual demand or supply:** one consumer's or one firm's schedule.
- **Market demand or supply:** the **horizontal sum** of all individual schedules: add quantities at each price.

## Shapes

- Demand slopes **down**: a lower price makes the good more affordable and cheaper relative to alternatives, so quantity demanded extends.
- Supply slopes **up**: a higher price raises profitability and covers the higher cost of extra output, so quantity supplied extends.
- Both curves hold **other things equal**. Any change except the good's own price moves the curve.

## Building market curves: checks

- Add quantities, not prices.
- A buyer whose quantity would be negative at a price contributes **zero**. This can create a **kink** in market demand where a group drops out.
- Market supply from n identical firms = n x each firm's quantity at each price. Entry of firms shifts market supply right.

## Determinants

| Demand shifts right when... | Supply shifts right when... |
|---|---|
| income rises (normal good) or falls (inferior good) | costs of production fall |
| a substitute becomes dearer | technology improves |
| a complement becomes cheaper | an indirect tax is cut or a subsidy is given |
| tastes move in favour | new firms enter |
| population in the buying group grows | the price of an alternative product the firm could make falls |
| prices are expected to rise | weather improves harvests |
| credit becomes cheaper or easier to get | expectations encourage selling more now |

Reverse each change for a leftward shift.

## Shift or movement: the one test

Ask: **did the good's own price change first?**

- **Yes:** movement along the curve. Use **extension** (more) or **contraction** (less) of quantity demanded or supplied.
- **No:** shift of the curve. Use **increase** (right) or **decrease** (left) in demand or supply.

A rightward shift means **more is demanded (or supplied) at every price**, or, read vertically, buyers will pay more (or sellers need less) for each quantity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ceteris paribus | Other things being equal: all determinants except the one studied are held constant |
| Normal good | Demand rises when income rises |
| Inferior good | Demand falls when income rises, as buyers switch to better alternatives |
| Substitute | A good that can be used in place of another |
| Complement | A good used together with another |
| Extension | A rise in quantity demanded or supplied caused by a change in the good's own price |
| Contraction | A fall in quantity demanded or supplied caused by a change in the good's own price |
| Increase in demand | A rightward shift of the demand curve |
| Decrease in supply | A leftward shift of the supply curve |

## Exam traps

- "Price fell, so demand rose" is wrong: quantity demanded extended along the same curve.
- A rise in the cost of an input moves **supply**, not demand.
- An indirect tax on a good shifts **supply** left (upwards by the tax); it does not shift demand.
- A leftward supply shift looks like a move "up" the page. Describe it as a decrease in supply.
- A change in the price of a complement shifts the demand for the other good; it does not move the complement's own curve.
- Leaving out "able to pay" from effective demand.
- Giving a quantity with no time period.

## Self-test

1. Define effective demand.
2. Why does a person's need for a good not necessarily appear in market demand?
3. Two buyers have demand Qd = 20 - 2P and Qd = 30 - 2P. What is market demand at a price of 6?
4. At what price does the first buyer in Question 3 leave the market?
5. State the effect on the demand for tea of a fall in the price of coffee.
6. State the effect on the supply of wheat of an improvement in seed technology.
7. A good's price rises. Is this a shift or a movement along its demand curve?
8. Name the change in the curve when incomes fall and the good is inferior.
9. Fifty identical firms each supply Qs = 2P - 4. Write the market supply equation.
10. Distinguish an increase in supply from an extension in supply.
11. Why does an indirect tax shift supply, not demand?
12. Give two determinants of supply other than costs.

**Answers**

1. The quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at each price over a given period.
2. Without the ability to pay, the need is not backed by purchasing power, so it is not effective demand and gives firms no price signal.
3. First buyer: 20 - 12 = 8; second: 30 - 12 = 18; market demand = 26.
4. At a price of 10 (20 - 2P = 0), and above.
5. Demand for tea shifts left, because coffee is a substitute that has become cheaper.
6. Supply of wheat shifts right, because more can be produced at each price.
7. A movement: a contraction in quantity demanded along the existing curve.
8. Demand for an inferior good increases (shifts right) when income falls.
9. Qs = 50(2P - 4) = 100P - 200.
10. An increase is a rightward shift of the whole curve caused by a non-price determinant; an extension is a movement along the curve caused by a rise in the good's own price.
11. The tax is paid by sellers and raises their cost of supplying each unit, so less is supplied at each price; consumers' willingness and ability to buy at each price is unchanged.
12. Any two of: technology, subsidies, number of firms, prices of other goods the firm could make, weather, producers' expectations.
