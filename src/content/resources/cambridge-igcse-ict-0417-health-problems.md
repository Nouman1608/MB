Subtopic 5.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers potential health problems related to the prolonged use of IT equipment. The syllabus names four: "repetitive strain injury (RSI), back problems, eye problems, headaches", and asks for "the causes of these health issues and strategies for preventing them". The [Effects of Using IT study guide](/resources/igcse-ict-effects-of-using-it/) gives a one-line summary of each. This guide explains the mechanism behind each problem, so that you can match a precise cause to a precise prevention and explain why the prevention works.

## Why these problems happen

All four problems come from the same root: the body stays in one position, or repeats one small movement, for a long time while the user concentrates on a screen. What differs is *which* part of the body takes the strain:

- the **hands, wrists and arms** (RSI)
- the **spine, neck and shoulders** (back problems)
- the **eyes** (eye problems)
- the **head**, often as a knock-on effect of strain elsewhere (headaches)

Health questions in 0417 are about the user, not the equipment. Electrical hazards such as trailing cables and overloaded sockets belong to physical safety in Topic 8, so keep them out of a 5.2 answer.

## Repetitive strain injury (RSI)

**What it is:** pain, stiffness, swelling or tingling in the fingers, wrists, arms or shoulders caused by repeating the same action many times.

**Causes:**

- typing for long periods without a break
- repeatedly clicking a mouse or scrolling
- holding the wrists bent upwards or sideways while typing
- heavy use of a phone or tablet touch screen, particularly texting with the thumbs

**Prevention:**

- take regular breaks and do simple hand and wrist exercises
- use an ergonomic keyboard and mouse, shaped so the wrists stay in a natural position
- use a wrist rest so the wrists are supported and straight
- keep correct posture, with forearms roughly level with the keyboard
- use voice recognition software to reduce the amount of typing

**Why prevention works:** RSI builds up when the same tendons and muscles are used over and over in a strained position. Breaks give them time to recover; ergonomic equipment and wrist rests remove the strained position.

## Back problems

**What it is:** pain in the lower back, upper back, neck or shoulders.

**Causes:**

- sitting in the same position for a long time
- slouching or leaning forward towards the screen
- a chair without proper back support, or at the wrong height for the desk
- a screen that is too low or too high, so the neck is bent or tilted back
- using a laptop on the lap, which makes the user look down

**Prevention:**

- use an adjustable chair with good lower-back support, set so the feet are flat on the floor
- use a footrest if the feet do not reach the floor
- position the screen at eye level, using a monitor that can be raised and tilted, or a laptop stand
- sit up straight with good posture
- take breaks to stand, walk and stretch

**Why prevention works:** a well-adjusted chair and screen keep the spine and neck in a neutral position, and breaks stop the muscles stiffening from being held still.

## Eye problems

**What it is:** eye strain, tired or sore eyes, dry eyes and blurred vision. Users blink less when concentrating on a screen, which dries the eyes.

**Causes:**

- looking at a screen for long periods without a break
- glare or reflections on the screen from windows or lights
- poor room lighting, either too dim or too bright compared with the screen
- screen brightness or contrast set badly, or text that is too small
- a flickering screen

**Prevention:**

- take regular breaks and look at distant objects so the eyes can refocus
- use an anti-glare screen or filter
- fit blinds to windows and position the screen away from direct light
- adjust the brightness, contrast and text size
- use a screen that does not flicker
- have regular eye tests, as some users need glasses for screen distance

**Why prevention works:** the eyes tire when they focus at one distance for a long time and when they keep adjusting to glare. Breaks rest the focusing muscles; removing glare stops constant readjustment.

## Headaches

**What it is:** pain in the head, often building up during a long session.

**Causes:**

- eye strain from the causes above
- glare and poor lighting
- poor posture causing tension in the neck and shoulders
- working for long periods without breaks

**Prevention:**

- use an anti-glare screen and improve the lighting
- adjust the screen settings
- take regular breaks away from the screen
- keep good posture
- have regular eye tests

**Why prevention works:** headaches from IT use usually follow eye strain or muscle tension, so removing those causes prevents the headache.

## Linking cause to prevention

| If the cause is... | The matching prevention is... |
|---|---|
| Bent wrists while typing | Wrist rest or ergonomic keyboard |
| Constant clicking | Ergonomic mouse, breaks, voice recognition |
| Screen too low | Raise or tilt the screen to eye level |
| Chair at wrong height | Adjustable chair; footrest |
| Glare from a window | Blinds, anti-glare screen, move the screen |
| Small text | Increase text size or zoom |
| No breaks | Scheduled breaks to move and look away |

## Worked example: an annotated answer

**Question:** A student uses a laptop on her bed for several hours each evening and has started to get neck pain and headaches. Explain **two** causes of her health problems and describe a way to prevent each. **[4]**

**Model answer:**

The laptop is on the bed, so she has to look down at the screen and bend her neck for long periods *(cause linked to the scenario)*. She should put the laptop on a desk with a stand, or use a separate monitor, so the screen is at eye level *(prevention that removes that cause)*.

She works for several hours without stopping, which strains her eyes and leads to headaches *(second, different cause)*. She should take regular breaks and look at objects in the distance so her eyes can rest *(matching prevention)*.

Each cause comes from the scenario and each prevention fixes that cause. "Take breaks" earns credit only once.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "take breaks" as the prevention for every problem. It is valid, but a second prevention must be different.
- Giving a prevention that does not match the cause, such as an anti-glare screen for back pain.
- Saying RSI is caused by "using a computer". Name the repeated action: typing, clicking or texting.
- Including electrocution or trip hazards, which are safety issues in Topic 8.
- Calling a problem "eye cancer" or "blindness". Keep to the effects the syllabus names: strain, tiredness and blurred vision.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** or **state** a health problem: name it exactly, such as "RSI".
- **Describe** a cause: say what the user does, for example "keeps the wrists bent while typing".
- **Explain** a prevention: say what to do and why it helps, using "so that".
- In a scenario question, take each cause from the details given (laptop on bed, window behind the screen, long shifts).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is RSI caused only by keyboards?**
No. Any repeated small movement can cause it, including clicking a mouse and texting on a phone.

**Do I need to know medical terms for these conditions?**
No. Name the four problems the syllabus lists and describe their causes and prevention in plain language.
