OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics (J560) is the full GCSE mathematics course for students in the final
years of secondary school. It covers number, algebra, ratio and proportion, geometry and measures,
probability and statistics, and it is examined at the end of the course in one of two tiers.
Foundation candidates sit Papers 1, 2 and 3; Higher candidates sit Papers 4, 5 and 6. Every paper
lasts 90 minutes and carries 100 marks, and OCR gives each one a weighting of 33⅓% of the
qualification.

## How the course is organised

OCR sets out the content in twelve areas, which group naturally into five broad themes.

**Number** (areas 1 to 4): number operations and integers, including calculations with integers,
whole number theory, combining arithmetic operations and inverse operations; fractions, decimals and
percentages and how to order them; indices and surds, covering powers and roots, standard form and
exact calculations; and approximation and estimation.

**Ratio, proportion and rates of change** (area 5): calculations with ratio, direct and inverse
proportion, and discrete growth and decay.

**Algebra** (areas 6 and 7): algebraic expressions, formulae, equations and inequalities, the
language of functions and sequences, followed by graphs of equations and functions, straight line
graphs, transformations of curves and interpreting graphs.

**Geometry and measures** (areas 8 to 10): basic geometry (conventions and notation, ruler and
compass constructions, angles, polygons, circles and three-dimensional shapes); congruence and
similarity, including plane isometric transformations and plane vector geometry; and mensuration,
from units and measurement to perimeter, area, volume, surface area and triangle mensuration.

**Probability and statistics** (areas 11 and 12): basic probability and experiments, combined events
and probability diagrams, sampling, interpreting and representing data, and analysing data.

## How it's assessed

| Tier | Paper | Calculator | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | J560/01 | Yes | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| Foundation | J560/02 | No | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| Foundation | J560/03 | Yes | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.34% |
| Higher | J560/04 | Yes | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| Higher | J560/05 | No | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.33% |
| Higher | J560/06 | Yes | 90 minutes | 100 | 33.34% |

OCR prints every paper as 33⅓%; the figures above are recorded to two decimal places so each tier
totals exactly 100%. The course is linear: a candidate is entered for one tier and sits all three of
that tier's papers in the same series. In each tier, two papers allow a calculator and one does not
(Paper 2 at Foundation, Paper 5 at Higher). OCR states that the topics apply across both tiers and
may be assessed on any paper, so there is no "algebra paper" or "geometry paper". The specification
has been examined since 2017.

## How the parts fit together

Because any topic can appear on any paper, the twelve content areas are best thought of as one body
of mathematics rather than separate units. In practice they lean on each other heavily. Fractions
and percentages feed directly into ratio, proportion and growth and decay. Algebraic manipulation is
needed for straight line graphs, for formulae in mensuration and for many geometry proofs. Ratio and
similarity are closely linked, and probability calculations use fraction and decimal skills
constantly.

The calculator split shapes how skills should be practised. The non-calculator paper rewards secure
mental and written methods, exact answers with fractions, pi and surds, and good estimation. The
calculator papers still expect clear working, and a correct calculator answer with no method shown
can lose marks if it turns out to be wrong.

Tier entry is something to discuss with your teacher. Whichever tier you sit, the same twelve
content areas apply, so the choice is about the level of demand you are ready for rather than a
different list of topics.

## Planning your study

Most schools teach J560 over the final years of secondary school. A sensible order is to secure
number first (areas 1 to 4), because every later topic depends on it, then build algebra and ratio
in parallel. Geometry and measures can be introduced early at a basic level and then extended once
algebraic skills are stronger, for example when moving from angle facts to circle theorems and
congruence proofs. Probability and statistics sit comfortably later, once fractions and data
handling are secure.

A useful pattern for each term is to learn new content, mix it with review of earlier areas, and
regularly practise without a calculator so the non-calculator paper is not a shock. In the final
year, move to full timed papers in your tier, check where marks are being lost, and use the
exam-preparation page for the detailed paper strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers do you sit for OCR GCSE Mathematics?**
Three, all in the same tier. Foundation candidates sit Papers 1, 2 and 3; Higher candidates sit Papers 4, 5 and 6. Each paper is 90 minutes and 100 marks.

**Which J560 paper is non-calculator?**
Paper 2 at Foundation tier and Paper 5 at Higher tier. The other two papers in each tier allow a calculator.

**Are the J560 papers equally weighted?**
Yes. OCR gives each paper a weighting of 33⅓%. Marlbridge records them as 33.33%, 33.33% and 33.34% so each tier adds up to exactly 100%.

**Can any topic come up on any paper?**
Yes. OCR states that topics apply across both tiers and may be assessed on any paper, so revision should cover the whole specification for every paper.

**Is there coursework in OCR GCSE Mathematics?**
No. Assessment is entirely by the three externally assessed written papers in your tier, taken at the end of the course.
