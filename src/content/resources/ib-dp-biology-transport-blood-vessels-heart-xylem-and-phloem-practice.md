## Questions

**1.** A micrograph of a vessel has a scale bar of 25 mm that represents 50 µm. The lumen is 42 mm across and the wall is 9 mm thick.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the actual width of the lumen in µm. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate wall thickness ÷ lumen diameter and state whether the vessel is an artery or a vein. **[2]**

**2.** Explain how the structure of capillaries is adapted for exchange of materials, including fenestrated capillaries. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** Explain how elastic fibres in artery walls help to maintain blood flow between heartbeats. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why veins have valves but arteries do not. **[2]**

**4.** A student counts the radial pulse of a rested person for 15 s, three times: 17, 18 and 16 beats. After exercise the count in 15 s is 31.

**(a)** Calculate the mean resting heart rate in beats per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase after exercise. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two ways to improve the reliability of the measurement. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Describe how atherosclerosis develops in a coronary artery. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how complete blockage of a coronary artery leads to a heart attack. **[3]**

**6.** Region M has 450 new cases of coronary heart disease in a population of 360 000. Region N has 150 in a population of 200 000.

**(a)** Calculate the rate per 100 000 for each region. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the rate in M is than in N, to 3 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** A study of 30 countries found a correlation coefficient of +0.78 linking dietary saturated fat with deaths from coronary heart disease. The same study found r = +0.05 between mean annual rainfall and these deaths. Evaluate what these results show. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** Explain how water is drawn from the roots to the leaves during transpiration. **[5]**
**(b)** (HL only) A cut stem exudes 4.2 cm³ of sap in 6 hours on a humid night. Calculate the rate in cm³ per hour. **[1]**
**(c)** (HL only) Explain how root pressure is generated, and state one condition when it is most useful. **[3]**

**8.** A dye moves 14.4 mm along a stem in 2 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the rate in mm per hour, and in µm per second. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain three adaptations of xylem vessels for transporting water. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Describe the positions of xylem and phloem in a transverse section of a dicotyledonous stem and of a root. **[3]**
**(b)** State what a plan diagram shows, and give one function of the cortex. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) In a capillary, hydrostatic pressure is 4.0 kPa at the arteriole end and 1.4 kPa at the venule end. The pull of plasma proteins keeps fluid in at 3.4 kPa throughout.

**(a)** Calculate the net outward force at each end. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the results show about fluid movement. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how excess tissue fluid is returned to the circulation. **[2]**
**(d)** State one difference between plasma and tissue fluid. **[1]**

**11.** (HL only) The table shows pressures on the left side of a heart in kPa. One cycle lasts 0.75 s.

| Time (s) | Atrium | Ventricle | Aorta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 1.3 | 1.0 | 10.5 |
| 0.15 | 0.8 | 3.0 | 10.4 |
| 0.25 | 0.9 | 15.2 | 14.8 |
| 0.45 | 1.0 | 4.0 | 11.5 |
| 0.60 | 1.3 | 0.6 | 10.8 |

**(a)** Explain why both valves are closed at 0.15 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why blood leaves the ventricle at 0.25 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the semilunar valve is closed at 0.45 s. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain what happens to the atrioventricular valve between 0.45 s and 0.60 s. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the heart rate. **[1]**
**(f)** State the role of the pacemaker. **[1]**

**12.** (HL only) **(a)** Outline the path of blood in the single circulation of a bony fish. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage of double circulation in mammals. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the structure of sieve tube elements and companion cells helps translocation. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 25 mm = 25 000 µm; 25 000 ÷ 50 = **×500** [1]
**(b)** 42 ÷ 500 = 0.084 mm = **84 µm** [1]
**(c)** 9 ÷ 42 = **0.21** [1]; the wall is thick relative to the lumen, so it is an **artery** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion and the division. Cite the ratio rather than "it looks thick".

**2.** Branching networks give a large surface area [1]; a wall one cell thick and a narrow lumen give a short diffusion distance and slow flow that allows time for exchange [1]; fenestrations (pores) in sites such as kidney glomeruli or villi allow rapid passage of substances [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each feature to its effect on exchange. A bare list of "thin, narrow, branched" is weaker.

**3. (a)** The wall stretches when the ventricle contracts [1]; it recoils afterwards, keeping pressure up and pushing blood on between beats [1]
**(b)** Venous pressure is low, so blood could drift backwards, especially when muscles squeeze veins [1]; valves close to give one-way flow, while arterial pressure already drives blood forwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the reason arteries differ, not only what valves do. Compare the two vessels directly.

**4. (a)** Mean = (17 + 18 + 16) ÷ 3 = 17; 17 × 4 = **68 beats per minute** [1]
**(b)** After exercise 31 × 4 = 124 [1]; (124 − 68) ÷ 68 × 100 = **82.4%** [1]
**(c)** Rest before counting [1]; use fingertips, not the thumb, and count for longer or repeat and average [1]
*Examiner insight:* For percentage change divide by the original value. Reliability points should be specific actions, not "be careful".

**5. (a)** Fatty material (plaque) builds up beneath the lining of the artery [1]; this narrows the lumen and reduces blood flow [1]
**(b)** A plaque can rupture and a clot form that blocks the artery [1]; muscle beyond the blockage gets no oxygen and cannot respire aerobically [1]; the cells die, which is a heart attack [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the chain of events in order. Naming the heart attack without the loss of oxygen supply leaves out the explanation.

**6. (a)** M: 450 ÷ 360 000 × 100 000 = **125** [1]; N: 150 ÷ 200 000 × 100 000 = **75** per 100 000 [1]
**(b)** 125 ÷ 75 = **1.67** times [1]
**(c)** r = +0.78 shows a fairly strong positive correlation, supporting a link [1]; it does not prove that saturated fat causes the disease, because other factors such as smoking or inactivity may differ between countries [1]; r = +0.05 shows no relationship with rainfall, which is consistent with no link [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers gain credit for stating both what the data support and what they cannot show. Avoid "proves".

**7. (a)** Water evaporates from the walls of mesophyll cells into the air spaces [1]; this lowers water potential in the walls, so water is drawn out of the xylem through the walls by capillary action [1]; tension (negative pressure potential) develops in the xylem [1]; cohesion between water molecules, by hydrogen bonds, keeps the column continuous [1]; the tension pulls the column up from the roots [1]
**(b)** 4.2 ÷ 6 = **0.7 cm³ per hour** [1]
**(c)** Root cells actively transport mineral ions into the xylem [1]; this lowers water potential there, so water enters by osmosis and positive pressure builds [1]; useful in humid air, or in spring before leaves open [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "pulled" for transpiration and "pushed" for root pressure. Mixing the two directions is a common loss of credit.

**8. (a)** 14.4 ÷ 2 = **7.2 mm per hour** [1]; 7200 µm ÷ 3600 s = **2.0 µm per second** [1]
**(b)** No cell contents and incomplete or missing end walls, so water flows without obstruction [1]; lignified walls resist collapse under tension [1]; pits allow water to enter or leave sideways [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For "explain", each adaptation needs its reason. State both parts.

**9. (a)** Stem: vascular bundles in a ring, xylem towards the inside and phloem towards the outside [1]; root: vascular tissue in a central core [1]; xylem often star-shaped with phloem between its arms [1]
**(b)** It shows the arrangement and proportions of tissues, not individual cells [1]; the cortex stores food or provides a route for water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Position words matter. Write "inside", "outside" and "centre" rather than only naming tissues.

**10. (a)** Arteriole end: 4.0 − 3.4 = **+0.6 kPa** [1]; venule end: 1.4 − 3.4 = **−2.0 kPa** [1]
**(b)** Net force is outward at the arteriole end, so fluid leaves and forms tissue fluid [1]; it is inward at the venule end, so much fluid returns [1]
**(c)** Lymph capillaries have thin walls with gaps, so excess fluid enters [1]; valves prevent backflow and lymph returns to the blood near the heart [1]
**(d)** Plasma contains more protein than tissue fluid (or: tissue fluid has no red blood cells) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the sign of each net value and say what it means.

**11. (a)** Ventricular pressure (3.0) exceeds atrial pressure (0.8), closing the atrioventricular valve [1]; it is below aortic pressure (10.4), so the semilunar valve stays shut [1]
**(b)** Ventricular pressure (15.2) exceeds aortic pressure (14.8), opening the semilunar valve [1]
**(c)** Aortic pressure (11.5) exceeds ventricular pressure (4.0) [1]
**(d)** By 0.60 s ventricular pressure (0.6) has fallen below atrial pressure (1.3) [1]; the atrioventricular valve opens and the ventricle fills [1]
**(e)** 60 ÷ 0.75 = **80 beats per minute** [1]
**(f)** The sinoatrial node starts each heartbeat, triggering atrial contraction [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the pressures from the table when explaining valve positions.

**12. (a)** Heart to gill capillaries, then to the body capillaries [1]; then back to the heart, so blood passes through the heart once per circuit [1]
**(b)** The heart pumps blood again after the lungs, restoring pressure [1]; so blood reaches body tissues faster, supporting high metabolic rates [1]
**(c)** Sieve plates have pores, so sap passes between cells [1]; little cytoplasm, few organelles and no nucleus reduce obstruction to flow [1]; companion cells have many mitochondria that supply ATP for active loading [1]; plasmodesmata connect companion cells to sieve tube elements [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each cell feature with its effect on flow or loading. A list of features without effects is incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing artery and vein wall thickness without the lumen.
- Forgetting units after micrograph calculations.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new value, not the original.
- Comparing case numbers instead of rates per head.
- Stating that a strong correlation proves causation.
- Describing transpiration as pushing.
- Not interpreting the sign of a net pressure.
- Explaining valve positions without citing which pressure is greater.
- Leaving out the reason behind each phloem adaptation.
