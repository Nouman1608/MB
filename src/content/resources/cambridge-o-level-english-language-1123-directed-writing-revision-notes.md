These notes cover **Section A, Directed Writing**, of 1123 Paper 2: candidates "use, develop and
evaluate the information in the text(s) to create a discursive/argumentative/persuasive speech, email,
report, letter or article". The paper overview is in the
[Paper 2 Writing study guide](/resources/o-level-english-language-paper-2-writing/); the full method and
a model paragraph are in the
[Directed writing study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-english-language-1123-directed-writing/).

## Task facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Marks | 25: writing 15 (W1–W5) + reading 10 (R3, R5) |
| Sources | one or two texts, about 400–450 words in total, printed on the question paper |
| Length | about 250–350 words |
| Forms | speech, email, report, letter, article |
| Purposes | discursive, argumentative, persuasive |
| Choice | none: one compulsory question |

## Reading objectives

- **R3**: analyse, evaluate and develop facts, ideas and opinions, using appropriate support from the
  text.
- **R5**: select and use information for specific purposes.

## Use → develop → evaluate

| Verb | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Use | a source point restated in your own words |
| Develop | a consequence, example, connection or application to the audience |
| Evaluate | a judgement on evidence, bias, strength or importance |

Evaluate (syllabus): "judge or calculate the quality, importance, amount, or value of something".
Consider (syllabus): "review and respond to given information".

## Six questions for every source

1. Fact or opinion?
2. Whose perspective, and what is their interest?
3. Any bias or loaded language?
4. What evidence supports the claim?
5. Where do the sources disagree, and who is more convincing?
6. What is missing?

## Evaluation phrases

- "Although X claims..., it offers no evidence that..."
- "This view is understandable, but..."
- "The more convincing argument is..., because..."
- "Neither text considers..."
- "As the company behind the scheme, the writer can hardly be neutral."

## Form conventions

| Form | Must have |
|---|---|
| Speech | direct address, signposts, rhetorical devices, a strong close |
| Email | greeting, early purpose, short paragraphs, clear request, sign-off |
| Report | title, purpose, sections or headings, findings, recommendation, impersonal tone |
| Letter | salutation, purpose, argument, call to action, appropriate close |
| Article | headline, hook, viewpoint, developed paragraphs, memorable ending |

W4 (register appropriate to context) is assessed in Section A, not Section B.
Check the question for bullet points it asks you to cover: each needs its own clear place in the answer.

## Purposes

- **Discursive**: both sides weighed, then a judgement.
- **Argumentative**: a position, defended; counter-arguments answered.
- **Persuasive**: reasons plus appeals to the audience's interests and feelings; a call to action.

## Paragraph recipe

**Source point** (used) → **your development** → **your evaluation** → **link to the purpose or
audience**.

## Ten-minute plan

1. Question: form, audience, purpose, bullet points.
2. Read sources twice; annotate U / D / E.
3. Decide position or judgement.
4. Four or five paragraphs: source point + development/evaluation for each.
5. Opening and closing in the form's style.

## Mini example: annotating a source

Source sentence (original): *"The new sports centre will be used by thousands of young people every
week, according to the developer."*

- **U** (use): a new sports centre is planned for young people.
- **D** (develop): for students at our school, it could replace the overcrowded gym and allow more
  teams to train.
- **E** (evaluate): the figure comes from the developer, who profits from the project, and is a
  prediction, not evidence.

## Register ladder

| Audience | Register |
|---|---|
| classmates (speech) | lively, direct, inclusive "we" |
| parents (letter, article) | clear, respectful, reasoned |
| head teacher (report, email) | formal, concise, objective |
| council or newspaper editor (letter) | formal, measured, persuasive |

## Exam traps

- Summarising the sources in order.
- Using one source when two are given.
- Lifting long phrases from the texts.
- An essay instead of the form asked for.
- Register drift.
- A missing bullet point.
- Opinions with no link to the sources.
- Far beyond 350 words, leaving no time for Section B.

## Self-test

1. How are Section A's 25 marks divided?
2. How long are the sources in total, and how long should your answer be?
3. Name the five forms.
4. What is the difference between "develop" and "evaluate"?
5. Define "evaluate" as the syllabus does.
6. Give two questions you can ask to evaluate a source.
7. Which writing objective is assessed in Section A but not in Section B?
8. What must a report contain that a speech does not?
9. How does a persuasive piece differ from a discursive one?
10. Write an evaluation sentence about a source that says "Everyone agrees the park should close."
11. What does "consider" mean in the syllabus?
12. Why is retelling the sources in order a weak approach?

**Answers**

1. Writing 15 (W1–W5) and reading 10 (R3, R5).
2. About 400–450 words in total; about 250–350 words.
3. Speech, email, report, letter, article.
4. Developing takes an idea further (a consequence, example or connection); evaluating judges its
   quality, evidence or importance.
5. "Judge or calculate the quality, importance, amount, or value of something."
6. Any two: fact or opinion? whose perspective? any bias? what evidence? where do sources disagree?
   what is missing?
7. W4, register appropriate to context.
8. For example a title, a stated purpose, headings or sections, findings and a recommendation, in an
   impersonal tone.
9. A persuasive piece aims to move the audience to agree or act; a discursive piece weighs both sides
   before judging.
10. For example: "The claim that 'everyone' agrees is an exaggeration: the text names no one and
    ignores the families who use the park daily."
11. "Review and respond to given information."
12. It uses the material but does not develop or evaluate it, so it limits the reading marks for R3.
