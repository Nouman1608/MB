## Questions

**1.** State the two conditions that must be satisfied for an object's oscillation to be simple harmonic. **[2]**

**2.** The defining equation of simple harmonic motion is a = –ω²x. Explain the significance of the minus sign. **[3]**

**3.** An oscillating block completes 45 oscillations in 27.0 s. Determine its time period, its frequency and its angular frequency. **[3]**

**4.** A block of mass 0.30 kg oscillates on a horizontal spring of spring constant 48 N m⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the time period. **[2]**
**(b)** The block is replaced by one of a different mass so that the period becomes 1.5 s. Calculate the new mass. **[3]**

**5.** A simple pendulum has length 1.20 m.

**(a)** Calculate its period on Earth. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its period on the Moon, where g = 1.62 m s⁻². **[2]**
**(c)** State why the formula only gives a good result for small swings. **[1]**

**6.** In an experiment, the period T of a simple pendulum is measured for different lengths l. A graph of T² against l is a straight line through the origin. It passes through the point (0.80 m, 3.22 s²).

**(a)** Show that T² is proportional to l, and state the gradient in terms of g. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine g from the graph. **[3]**

**7.** A block on a horizontal frictionless surface is attached to a spring. It is pulled to its maximum extension and released. Describe the energy changes during one complete cycle. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only) A particle moves with x = x₀ sin(ωt + φ), with x₀ = 0.050 m and period 1.2 s. At t = 0 its displacement is +0.030 m and it is moving towards the equilibrium position.

**(a)** Calculate ω. **[1]**
**(b)** Determine φ in radians. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the displacement at t = 0.50 s. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the velocity at t = 0. **[1]**

**9.** (HL only) A mass on a spring oscillates with amplitude 0.080 m that completes 3.2 oscillations every second.

**(a)** Determine the angular frequency. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed when the displacement is 0.050 m. **[2]**
**(c)** State the maximum speed. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum acceleration. **[1]**

**10.** (HL only) A mass of 0.25 kg oscillates with ω = 14 rad s⁻¹ and amplitude 0.060 m.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the potential energy at displacement 0.040 m. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the kinetic energy and the speed at that displacement. **[2]**

**11.** A mass of 0.40 kg hangs at rest from a vertical spring and extends it by 0.056 m. It is then displaced a further 0.035 m below that position, held still, and let go.

**(a)** Determine the spring constant. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time period of the oscillation. **[2]**
**(c)** (HL only) Calculate the maximum speed. **[2]**
**(d)** (HL only) Calculate the maximum kinetic energy, and show that it equals ½kx₀². **[3]**

**12.** (HL only) A pendulum bob swings on a string of length 0.90 m with amplitude 0.050 m measured along the arc.

**(a)** Treating the motion as SHM, calculate ω and the maximum speed. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the maximum height the bob rises above its lowest point, and use energy conservation to find the speed at the lowest point. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The net (restoring) force acts towards the equilibrium position [1]. Its magnitude is proportional to the displacement from equilibrium [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say only "force is proportional to displacement" leave out the direction, which is the other half of the condition.

**2.** The acceleration is directed towards the equilibrium position [1]. The displacement is measured away from equilibrium, so a and x have opposite signs [1]. This keeps the object returning to equilibrium, so the motion is oscillatory [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer links the sign to direction. Saying "the acceleration is negative" is incomplete, because it is only negative when the displacement is positive.

**3.** T = 27.0/45 = **0.600 s** [1]. f = 1/T = **1.67 Hz** [1]. ω = 2πf = **10.5 rad s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep units on all three. Hz and rad s⁻¹ are different and mixing them up is an easy way to lose credit.

**4. (a)** T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.30/48) [1] = **0.497 s** [1]
**(b)** Rearrange: m = kT²/4π² [1] = 48 × 1.5²/(4π²) [1] = **2.74 kg** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the rearranged formula before substituting. Squaring both sides is where most slips happen in a rearrangement.

**5. (a)** T = 2π√(l/g) = 2π√(1.20/9.81) [1] = **2.20 s** [1]
**(b)** T = 2π√(1.20/1.62) [1] = **5.41 s** [1]
**(c)** The restoring force is only proportional to displacement for small angles [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For (c), simply writing "the angle must be small" is a statement of the condition. Linking it to the restoring force gives the physics.

**6. (a)** T = 2π√(l/g), so T² = 4π²l/g [1]. This is proportional to l, with gradient 4π²/g [1]
**(b)** Gradient = 3.22/0.80 = 4.025 s² m⁻¹ [1]. g = 4π²/gradient [1] = **9.81 m s⁻²** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the gradient from the line, using the point given. Reading the gradient from a large triangle, or from a point far from the origin as here, reduces the effect of reading errors.

**7.** At maximum extension the block is momentarily at rest, so the kinetic energy is zero and the elastic potential energy is maximum [1]. As it moves towards equilibrium, elastic potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases [1]. At equilibrium the kinetic energy is maximum and the elastic potential energy is minimum [1]. The reverse happens on the other side, and the total energy remains constant throughout, with the cycle repeating [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of potential energy and say what happens to the total. "Energy changes" alone does not describe the cycle.

**8. (a)** ω = 2π/1.2 = **5.24 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** 0.030 = 0.050 sin φ gives sin φ = 0.6 [1]. The two solutions are 0.644 rad and π – 0.644 = 2.50 rad [1]. Moving towards equilibrium means negative velocity, so cos φ < 0 and φ = **2.50 rad** [1]
**(c)** Argument = 5.236 × 0.50 + 2.498 = 5.116 rad [1]. x = 0.050 sin(5.116) = **–0.046 m** [1]
**(d)** v = ωx₀ cos φ = 5.236 × 0.050 × cos(2.498) = **–0.209 m s⁻¹** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Choosing the correct phase angle depends on the direction of motion, so state your reason for rejecting the other root.

**9. (a)** ω = 2π × 3.2 = **20.1 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** v = ω√(x₀² – x²) = 20.1 × √(0.080² – 0.050²) [1] = **1.26 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** v_max = ωx₀ = **1.61 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** a_max = ω²x₀ = **32.3 m s⁻²** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded ω in later parts, because small rounding in 20.1 changes the third significant figure of ω².

**10. (a)** E_T = ½mω²x₀² [1] = ½ × 0.25 × 14² × 0.060² = **0.0882 J** [1]
**(b)** E_p = ½mω²x² = ½ × 0.25 × 14² × 0.040² [1] = **0.0392 J** [1]
**(c)** E_K = 0.0882 – 0.0392 = **0.0490 J** [1]; v = √(2E_K/m) = **0.626 m s⁻¹** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Checking with v = ω√(x₀² – x²) gives the same speed, a useful way to confirm the energy working.

**11. (a)** At equilibrium kx = mg [1], so k = 0.40 × 9.81/0.056 = **70.1 N m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.40/70.07) [1] = **0.475 s** [1]
**(c)** The amplitude is 0.035 m, not 0.091 m. ω = 13.2 rad s⁻¹, so v_max = ωx₀ [1] = **0.463 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** E_K,max = ½mv² = ½ × 0.40 × 0.463² [1] = **0.0429 J** [1]. ½kx₀² = ½ × 70.07 × 0.035² = 0.0429 J, equal to the kinetic energy [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The amplitude is measured from the equilibrium position, not from the natural length of the spring, and this is the usual mistake on a vertical spring.

**12. (a)** ω = √(g/l) = √(9.81/0.90) = **3.30 rad s⁻¹** [1]. v_max = ωx₀ [1] = **0.165 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** θ = 0.050/0.90 = 0.0556 rad, h = l(1 – cos θ) [1] = **1.39 × 10⁻³ m** [1]. v = √(2gh) = **0.165 m s⁻¹**, which agrees with part (a) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison of the two methods is worth stating in words, because it shows why the small-angle SHM approximation is acceptable here.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating the conditions for SHM without mentioning that the force opposes the displacement.
- Reading ω as a speed or mixing up hertz and radians per second.
- Squaring or square-rooting the wrong side when rearranging T = 2π√(m/k) or T = 2π√(l/g).
- Using the spring extension at equilibrium as the amplitude.
- Working in degrees for the phase angle.
- Taking the first solution of sin φ without using the direction of motion.
- Using v = ωx₀ at a general position instead of v = ω√(x₀² – x²).
- Giving energy answers without saying which kind of potential energy it is, or forgetting the total is constant.
- Rounding ω early and then giving a final answer that differs in the third significant figure.
