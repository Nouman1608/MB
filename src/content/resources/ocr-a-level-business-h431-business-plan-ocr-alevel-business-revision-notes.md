## The seven specification points

1. Explain the purpose of a business plan.
2. Describe the main contents of a business plan and explain why these are included.
3. Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages to a business of having a business plan.
4. Evaluate the impact and importance of a business plan to the stakeholders of a business.
5. Explain what is meant by the "Plan-Do-Review" cycle.
6. Analyse how the Plan-Do-Review cycle can improve a business's performance.
7. Evaluate the impact and importance of a strategic review to a business and its stakeholders.

## Core recall

### Purpose by stage

| Stage | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Start-up | Test viability; persuade lenders or investors |
| Growth | Justify and finance an expansion |
| Established | Set targets, coordinate departments, provide a benchmark for review |

### Contents and who reads them

| Content | Why included | Main reader |
|---|---|---|
| Executive summary | Quick grasp of the proposal | Lenders, investors |
| Aims and objectives | Shows direction and targets | Owners, staff |
| Market research | Evidence that customers exist | Investors, lenders |
| Marketing plan | How customers will be reached | Investors |
| Operations and staffing | How the product is delivered and by whom | Owners, staff |
| Cash-flow and profit forecasts | Ability to survive and repay | Lenders, suppliers |
| Finance required and sources | How much is needed, owner's stake | Lenders |
| Risks and contingencies | Shows the owner has planned for problems | Lenders, investors |

- Bank: repayment, security, cash flow, owner's own stake.
- Equity investor: growth, return on shares, management team.
- Supplier: ability to pay on credit.
- Employees: direction and job security.

### Testing a plan

- Forecasts are assumptions. Test them: what if sales are 10% or 20% lower?
- Find the lowest closing cash balance; a thin balance needs an overdraft facility in place.
- Check whether the forecast is supported by market research.

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Forces research and clear thinking | Time and skill to write |
| Needed to win finance | Forecasts may be wrong or optimistic |
| Sets targets and a benchmark | Goes out of date in fast markets |
| Coordinates staff | Can be rigid; may be written to win a loan and then ignored |
| Reveals dangers early | Gives false confidence if believed uncritically |

A plan matters most when: outside finance is needed; sums are large and hard to recover; the owner lacks experience; several people must coordinate.

### Plan-Do-Review

- Plan: objectives, targets, budgets.
- Do: carry out the plan.
- Review: compare actual with plan, find causes, feed lessons into the next plan.
- Improves performance because targets are measurable, causes are found, and the next plan acts on them.
- Fails if: reviews skipped, data poor, blame culture.

### Strategic review

- Asks whether the strategy itself still fits, not just whether targets were met.
- Examines: objectives, market and competition, strengths and weaknesses, resources, future options.
- Triggers: persistent under-performance, new competitor, change of owner, new technology.
- Impact: may save the business from a failing strategy; may bring retraining, redundancy, lost supplier orders or new investment.
- Most important when the environment has changed sharply.

## Quick worked reminder

Juniper & Moss (invented) opens with £15,000. Planned closing balances: £3,500, £3,500, £5,000, £8,500. With receipts 20% lower and payments unchanged: £2,300, £500, −£200, £500. A one-fifth sales shortfall needs extra finance in month 3, so arrange an overdraft before opening.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business plan | A written document setting out what a business intends to do, how, and the results expected |
| Cash-flow forecast | A prediction of cash receipts and payments over future periods |
| Closing balance | Opening balance plus net cash flow for the period |
| Variance | The difference between a planned and an actual figure |
| Plan-Do-Review | A repeating cycle of planning, carrying out and reviewing, with lessons fed back |
| Strategic review | A wider examination of whether the strategy still suits the business and its environment |
| Overdraft facility | An agreed limit up to which a bank account can go below zero |

## Exam traps

- Give the reader and the decision for each content, not just its name.
- Forecasts are not facts: test at least one assumption.
- Plan-Do-Review is a loop; a single comparison of plan and actual is not the whole cycle.
- A strategic review is broader than a budget review.
- The plan's importance varies by stakeholder: decisive for a lender, indirect for employees.

## Self-test

1. State two purposes of a business plan for a start-up.
2. Which content of a plan does a bank examine most closely, and why?
3. Why does an equity investor look closely at the management team?
4. Opening balance £4,000, receipts £7,500, payments £9,200. Find the closing balance.
5. Planned sales revenue £30,000; actual £27,600. State the variance and whether it is adverse or favourable.
6. Give two disadvantages of having a business plan.
7. When is a business plan least useful?
8. Explain the review stage of Plan-Do-Review.
9. How does Plan-Do-Review improve performance?
10. Give two events that might trigger a strategic review.
11. Give one effect of a strategic review on employees.
12. Why might a plan written mainly to win a loan be dangerous?

### Answers

1. Any two: test whether the idea is viable; persuade a lender or investor; set targets.
2. The cash-flow forecast, because it shows whether there will be enough cash to repay the loan and interest on time.
3. Because growth and returns depend on the people running the business, and the investor shares in the risk.
4. 4,000 + 7,500 − 9,200 = **£2,300**.
5. **£2,400 adverse**.
6. Any two: time and skill to write; forecasts may be wrong; becomes out of date; rigid; false confidence.
7. When the market changes so quickly that forecasts are soon out of date, or when the plan is written and then ignored.
8. Comparing actual results with the plan, finding the causes of differences and using them to improve the next plan.
9. It sets measurable targets, finds why results differ and acts on the causes in the next plan, so the gap narrows.
10. Any two: persistent under-performance; a new competitor; a change of owner; new technology.
11. Retraining for new roles, or redundancy if activities are stopped; uncertainty may lower motivation.
12. Its forecasts may be over-optimistic, so the owner may believe them and take on more risk than the business can bear.
