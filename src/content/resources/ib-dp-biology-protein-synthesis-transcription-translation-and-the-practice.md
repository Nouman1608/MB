## Questions

**1.** Describe the role of hydrogen bonding in transcription. **[3]**

**2.** A segment of a gene has the template strand 3'-TACAAGCCTGGTACCTTTATC-5'.

**(a)** Deduce the base sequence of the mRNA transcribed from it. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the amino acid sequence it codes for. **[2]**

**3.** A mature neuron does not divide, and it uses only some of its genes.

**(a)** Explain why the DNA template sequences must stay unchanged throughout the life of the neuron. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why transcription is a key stage for controlling which genes are expressed. **[2]**

**4.** The genetic code uses codons of three bases.

**(a)** Show by calculation that a two-base codon cannot specify every amino acid. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is meant by degeneracy of the code and give one consequence of it. **[2]**

**5.** A human gene is inserted into yeast cells, and the yeast makes the human polypeptide. Explain what this shows about the genetic code. **[3]**

**6.** Translation occurs at ribosomes.

**(a)** State where mRNA binds on the ribosome and how many tRNAs can bind to the large subunit at one time. **[2]**
**(b)** An mRNA codon reads 5'-ACC-3'. Write the anticodon of the tRNA that pairs with it, from 3' to 5'. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how tRNA ensures that the correct amino acid is added. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the elongation stage of translation. **[5]**

**8.** The mRNA 5'-AUGUUCGGACCAUGGAAAUAG-3' codes for a short polypeptide. For each change, deduce the effect on the polypeptide.

**(a)** The sixth base, C, is replaced by U. **[2]**
**(b)** The eighth base, G, is replaced by A. **[2]**
**(c)** The fifteenth base, G, is replaced by A. **[2]**
**(d)** The seventh base is deleted. **[2]**

**9.** Sickle cell disease results from a point mutation in the gene for the β chain of haemoglobin. Explain how this mutation changes the structure of the protein. **[4]**

**10.** *(HL only)* Answer all parts.

**(a)** State the direction in which RNA polymerase adds nucleotides to the growing RNA, and which end of the template it moves towards. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the role of the promoter and of transcription factors. **[3]**
**(c)** A gene for tRNA is transcribed but is described as non-coding. Explain. **[2]**

**11.** *(HL only)* A gene has five exons, of 96, 150, 72, 138 and 108 bases, separated by four introns of 520, 330, 880 and 410 bases.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the pre-mRNA, ignoring caps, tails and untranslated regions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the pre-mRNA that is intron, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of codons in the mature mRNA when all five exons are retained, and when the 72 base exon is omitted. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how this gives two polypeptides from one gene. **[1]**
**(e)** State one function of the 5' cap. **[1]**

**12.** *(HL only)* Answer all parts.

**(a)** Describe the initiation of translation. **[4]**
**(b)** Outline how pre-proinsulin is converted into insulin in two steps. **[2]**
**(c)** In an experiment, the amount of protein Z in cultured cells was measured with a proteasome inhibitor. The amount was 100, 118, 141 and 166 arbitrary units at 0, 2, 4 and 6 hours. Calculate the percentage increase at 6 hours, and explain why protein Z accumulated. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Hydrogen bonds form between template bases and complementary free RNA nucleotides [1]. Template A takes U, T takes A, G takes C and C takes G, so the RNA sequence follows the template [1]. They are weak, so the RNA can separate from the template and the DNA strands can pair up again [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the pairs and the weakness of the bonds are separate points; "bonds hold them together" alone does not describe a role.

**2. (a)** Complement each template base, using U for A: 5'-AUGUUCGGACCAUGGAAAUAG-3' [2]. **[2]**
**(b)** Codons AUG UUC GGA CCA UGG AAA UAG [1]. Met-Phe-Gly-Pro-Trp-Lys, then stop [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the template complement with T instead of U, or including the stop codon as an amino acid, loses the mark for that stage.

**3. (a)** The neuron does not divide, so its DNA is never replaced by copying [1]. Any lasting damage to a sequence would be kept, so the template must be conserved for the cell's life [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Transcription is the first stage of gene expression [1]. Switching it on or off decides whether a gene is expressed, because only genes that are transcribed can have their mRNA translated [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers should link the cell's non-dividing state to the template, rather than just saying DNA is stable.

**4. (a)** Two bases give 4 × 4 = 16 different codons [1]. This is fewer than the 20 amino acids [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Several different codons specify one amino acid [1]. A change to a codon's last base can keep the same amino acid, so some mutations have no effect [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The calculation must be shown; stating that three is "enough" gains nothing without the figures.

**5.** Yeast ribosomes read the human mRNA codons and add the amino acids the codons specify [1]. A given codon has the same meaning in humans and yeast [1]. This shows that the genetic code is universal, shared by nearly all organisms [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the term "universal", and explain it with the codon meaning being the same; a bare term may not be enough.

**6. (a)** mRNA binds to the small subunit [1]. Two tRNAs can bind at once to the large subunit [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 3'-UGG-5' [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Each tRNA carries one specific amino acid [1]. Its anticodon pairs by complementary bases with the codon, so only the tRNA with the matching anticodon delivers its amino acid [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For part (b), check the direction label: writing the same letters in the wrong order is a common slip.

**7.** A tRNA with an anticodon complementary to the next codon binds to the ribosome [1]. Two tRNAs are bound side by side on the mRNA at this point [1]. A peptide bond forms between the amino acid on the new tRNA and the end of the growing chain [1]. The ribosome moves along the mRNA by one codon [1]. The empty tRNA is released and the next codon is exposed, so the cycle repeats [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The order of events matters; listing "peptide bond, tRNA, movement" without sequence is less likely to gain full credit.

**8. (a)** The codon changes from UUC to UUU, which still codes for Phe [1]. The polypeptide is unchanged, a silent mutation [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** GGA becomes GAA, so Gly is replaced by Glu [1]. The polypeptide is Met-Phe-Glu-Pro-Trp-Lys, one amino acid different [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** UGG becomes UGA, a stop codon [1]. Translation ends early, giving Met-Phe-Gly-Pro [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** All codons after the deletion shift: AUG UUC GAC CAU GGA AAU [1]. The polypeptide begins Met-Phe-Asp-His-Gly-Asn and no longer matches the original after the second amino acid [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of change and quote the new amino acids; "the protein changes" without the sequence is too vague.

**9.** In the mRNA, a codon for glutamic acid, GAG, becomes GUG [1]. Valine replaces glutamic acid in the β chain [1]. Valine has a side chain without charge, unlike glutamic acid [1]. The altered haemoglobin molecules stick together in low oxygen and form fibres, changing the protein's structure and function [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the single base change to the amino acid, and the amino acid to structure; skipping the middle step loses credit.

**10. (a)** RNA polymerase adds nucleotides to the 3' end of the RNA, so the RNA is made 5' to 3' [1]. It moves along the template towards the template's 5' end [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The promoter is a DNA sequence near the start of a gene where transcription begins [1]. Transcription factors bind to the promoter [1]. They help RNA polymerase to attach and begin transcription [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The gene is transcribed into tRNA [1]. The product is RNA that is not translated, so the gene does not code for a polypeptide [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) rewards the distinction between coding for RNA and coding for a polypeptide.

**11. (a)** Exons: 96 + 150 + 72 + 138 + 108 = 564 [1]. Pre-mRNA: 564 + 2140 = **2704 bases** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2140 / 2704 × 100 = **79.1 per cent** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 564 / 3 = **188 codons** with all exons [1]; omitting 72 gives 492 / 3 = **164 codons** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Different combinations of exons are spliced together, giving different mRNAs and so different polypeptides [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** The cap helps stabilise the mRNA [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep units (bases or codons) with each figure, and show the division by three.

**12. (a)** The small ribosomal subunit attaches to the 5' end of the mRNA [1]. It moves along the mRNA to the start codon [1]. The initiator tRNA binds to the start codon, and a second tRNA binds [1]. The large subunit then attaches [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** First stage: a leading sequence is removed from pre-proinsulin to give proinsulin [1]. Second stage: a middle section is cut out, leaving two chains that form active insulin [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (166 − 100) / 100 × 100 = **66 per cent** increase [1]. The inhibitor blocked proteasomes, so protein Z was not broken down while synthesis continued [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage change uses the starting value as the divisor; and explanations should refer to breakdown versus synthesis.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using T in mRNA or forgetting that translation halts at the stop codon.
- Giving template bases instead of mRNA bases.
- Giving an anticodon in the same direction as the codon without checking the 3' and 5' labels.
- Not stating the new amino acid in a mutation question.
- Using "mutation" without saying whether the DNA, mRNA or protein changes.
- Treating degeneracy as "one codon for many amino acids", which reverses it.
- Describing elongation without the order of tRNA binding, bond formation and movement.
- HL: stating that introns "code for nothing" without saying they are removed from the transcript.
- HL: describing proteasomes without relating them to protein synthesis and breakdown.
