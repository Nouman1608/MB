These questions cover **3.2.5.4 Formation of coloured ions** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [formation of coloured ions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-4/) or the [formation of coloured ions revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-4-revision-notes/) first.

Use h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ throughout.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which ion is colourless in aqueous solution?

A: Cu²⁺

B: Fe³⁺

C: Zn²⁺

D: V³⁺ **[1]**

**2.** Explain, in terms of electrons and energy levels, what happens when a solution of [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ ions absorbs visible light. **[2]**

**3.** A complex ion absorbs light of wavelength 430 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the energy absorbed by one ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy absorbed per mole of ions, in kJ mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**4.** A d-electron transition in a complex has ΔE = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of light absorbed, in nm. **[1]**

**(b)** Light of this wavelength is orange-red. Suggest the colour of the solution. **[1]**

**5.** A pink solution of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ turns blue when concentrated hydrochloric acid is added. Explain this colour change. **[3]**

**6.** In a colorimetry experiment, a student adds excess aqueous ammonia to each copper(II) standard but forgets to add it to the unknown. State and explain the effect on the concentration obtained for the unknown. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** A complex absorbs light of frequency 6.00 × 10¹⁴ Hz most strongly.

**(a)** Calculate ΔE for one ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength absorbed, in nm. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔE in kJ mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**(d)** Light of this wavelength is green. Suggest the colour of the complex. **[1]**

**8.** A student finds the concentration of a copper(II) sulfate solution by colorimetry. Excess aqueous ammonia is added to each standard and to the sample before measurement.

| [Cu²⁺] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.0050 | 0.0100 | 0.0150 | 0.0200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.080 | 0.160 | 0.240 | 0.320 |

20.0 cm³ of the copper(II) sulfate solution is diluted to 100.0 cm³ (with excess ammonia added), and this diluted solution has an absorbance of 0.200.

**(a)** Explain why excess ammonia is added. **[1]**

**(b)** The solution is deep blue. Suggest the colour of filter used, and give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the concentration of Cu²⁺ in the diluted solution. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the concentration of Cu²⁺ in the original copper(II) sulfate solution. **[1]**

**(e)** Calculate the mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O (Mr = 249.6) dissolved in each dm³ of the original solution. **[1]**

**9.** Zinc and dilute acid reduce a green solution of V³⁺(aq) to violet V²⁺(aq).

**(a)** Both ions are aqua complexes. Explain why they have different colours. **[2]**

**(b)** When the violet solution is poured into an open beaker and left, it slowly turns green. Suggest why. **[1]**

**10.** A student says that any pale green solution must contain iron(II) ions.

**(a)** Iron(II) and iron(III) aqua ions have the same ligand but different colours. Explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Give one reason why identifying an ion from its colour alone can be unreliable. **[1]**

**11.** The visible absorption spectrum of a complex shows strong absorption with a maximum at 610 nm and very little absorption between 400 nm and 500 nm.

**(a)** Light of 610 nm is orange. Suggest the colour of the complex. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔE, in kJ mol⁻¹, for the transition at 610 nm. **[2]**

**(c)** When a different ligand replaces the water ligands, the absorption maximum moves to 540 nm. State whether ΔE has increased or decreased, and explain how you know. **[2]**

**12.** Scandium forms only Sc³⁺ ions, and its compounds are white. Titanium forms Ti³⁺ ions, whose aqua complex is coloured.

**(a)** Give the electron configurations of Sc³⁺ and Ti³⁺ in terms of s, p and d sub-levels. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to explain why Ti³⁺ complexes are coloured but Sc³⁺ compounds are not. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** C [1]

**2.** A d electron absorbs energy (a photon of visible light) [1]; it moves from the ground state to an excited state, and the energy of the light matches ΔE between them [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.**

**(a)** ΔE = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 4.30 × 10⁻⁷ = 4.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]

**(b)** 4.626 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 = 278 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]

**Total: 2**

**4.**

**(a)** λ = hc/ΔE = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 6.22 × 10⁻⁷ m = 622 nm [1]

**(b)** Blue or blue-green (the complementary colour of orange-red) [1]

**Total: 2**

**5.** Water ligands are replaced by chloride ligands, giving [CoCl₄]²⁻ [1]; the ligand and the co-ordination number (6 to 4) change, so ΔE changes [1]; a different wavelength of light is absorbed, so a different colour is transmitted [1]

**Total: 3**

*Examiner insight:* "The ligand changes" scores only the first mark. Every colour-change answer needs ΔE and the wavelength absorbed.

**6.** The concentration found would be too low [1]; without ammonia the copper is present as pale [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, which absorbs less at this wavelength than the ammonia complex the calibration was made with [1]

**Total: 2**

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** ΔE = hν = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 6.00 × 10¹⁴ = 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]

**(b)** λ = c/ν = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 6.00 × 10¹⁴ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ m = 500 nm [1]

**(c)** 3.978 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 = 239 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]

**(d)** Purple (red-violet) [1]

**Total: 4**

**8.**

**(a)** It forms the deep blue [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ complex, which absorbs more strongly, so absorbance changes more with concentration (more accurate readings) [1]

**(b)** Orange or red, because a blue solution absorbs these colours most [1]

**(c)** Gradient = 0.080 ÷ 0.0050 = 16 dm³ mol⁻¹; [Cu²⁺] = 0.200 ÷ 16 = 0.0125 mol dm⁻³ [1]

**(d)** Dilution factor 100.0 ÷ 20.0 = 5; 0.0125 × 5 = 0.0625 mol dm⁻³ [1]

**(e)** 0.0625 × 249.6 = 15.6 g [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* Forgetting the dilution factor in (d) is the commonest error; it then carries into (e), where only an error-carried-forward mark is possible.

**9.**

**(a)** The vanadium has a different oxidation state (+3 and +2) [1]; this changes ΔE, so a different wavelength of light is absorbed [1]

**(b)** V²⁺ is oxidised back to V³⁺ (green) by oxygen in the air [1]

**Total: 3**

**10.**

**(a)** The oxidation state differs (+2 and +3), which changes ΔE between the ground and excited states [1]; so they absorb different wavelengths and transmit different colours [1]

**(b)** Different ions can have similar colours (for example V³⁺(aq) is also green), or colour depends on concentration and on the ligands present [1]

**Total: 3**

**11.**

**(a)** Blue [1]

**(b)** ΔE per ion = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 6.10 × 10⁻⁷ = 3.26 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]; × 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 = 196 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** Increased [1]; the wavelength absorbed is shorter, and ΔE = hc/λ, so a shorter wavelength means a larger energy gap [1]

**Total: 5**

**12.**

**(a)** Sc³⁺: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ (3d⁰) [1]; Ti³⁺: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹ [1]

**(b)** Ti³⁺ has a d electron that can be promoted from the ground state to an excited state by absorbing visible light [1]; Sc³⁺ has no d electrons, so no such transition can happen and no visible light is absorbed [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* Remove the 4s electrons before the 3d electrons when writing ion configurations. Ti³⁺ is 3d¹, not 4s¹.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that a blue solution absorbs blue light.
- Leaving out the ΔE and wavelength links in colour-change explanations.
- Using wavelengths in nm in ΔE = hc/λ.
- Forgetting ÷ 1000 for kJ mol⁻¹ or × L for per mole.
- Not applying the dilution factor after reading from a calibration graph.
