Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies (0448) is assessed by two externally assessed papers and is not tiered. Paper 1, The History and Culture of Pakistan, and Paper 2, The Environment of Pakistan, are each 1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks and 50% of the qualification. The syllabus is examined in the June series only. Content pages that support the two papers include [The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906-47](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-emergence-of-pakistan-1906-47/), [Nationhood, 1947-99](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-nationhood-1947-99/) and [The Land of Pakistan](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-the-land-of-pakistan/).

## Paper 1: The History and Culture of Pakistan (75 marks, 50%)

Paper 1 has two sections. Section A is a compulsory source-based question worth 25 marks. Section B is worth 50 marks. That makes Section A one third of the paper and Section B two thirds, so the longer section deserves the larger share of your time. The source question tests whether you can read, compare and evaluate material you have not studied in advance, using your historical knowledge to support what the sources say. Section B tests knowledge and explanation across the history and culture content. The record available to us does not describe how Section B is organised beyond its mark total, so check the specification for the exact question format.

## Paper 2: The Environment of Pakistan (75 marks, 50%)

Paper 2 asks you to answer three of five questions, and each is worth 25 marks (3 x 25 = 75). Because you choose, the key skill is selection: read all five questions, pick the three where you can write the most accurate and developed answers, and commit. Revision should cover the whole environment content, including land, natural resources, agriculture, industry, power, trade and population topics, so that on the day you actually have a choice. Pages for this paper include [Agricultural Development](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-agricultural-development/), [Power](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-power/) and [Population and Employment](/resources/igcse-cambridge-pakistan-studies-population-and-employment/).

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Each paper: 90 minutes / 75 marks = 1.2 minutes per mark.
- Paper 1 Section A: 25 marks x 1.2 = 30 minutes.
- Paper 1 Section B: 50 marks x 1.2 = 60 minutes.
- Paper 2: three questions of 25 marks each, 25 x 1.2 = 30 minutes per question, which uses all 90 minutes.

Since the arithmetic leaves no spare time, aim for about 28 minutes per Paper 2 question so you have around 6 minutes in hand to read the paper and make your choice. Choosing should take a minute or two, not ten. Each paper is worth 50% over 75 marks, so every mark is worth 50 / 75 = 0.67% of the qualification (to two decimal places).

## Command words and question styles

- **Describe**: say what something is or what happened, with relevant detail.
- **Explain**: give reasons and link them to an outcome.
- **How far** or **to what extent**: weigh factors and reach a supported judgement.
- **Source questions**: refer to the source itself, quoting or paraphrasing, and then add your own knowledge. Do not copy out whole sentences.
- **Use** or **compare** sources: show how the sources agree or differ, and use their origin or purpose where it helps.

## Worked practice scenario

An original Paper 2 style question: "Explain why the Indus river system matters for agriculture in Pakistan. [8 marks, an illustrative mark allocation for practice]"

Plan: (1) Water supply in a country with limited rainfall in many areas. (2) Irrigation canals linked to the rivers. (3) Fertile alluvial soil from river deposits. (4) Consequence: crops depend on the supply, so water management is crucial.

Model answer: "The Indus river system matters because large areas of Pakistan receive too little rainfall to farm without a reliable source of water, and the rivers supply it through a network of irrigation canals. This allows farmers to grow crops where they otherwise could not and to water them across the year rather than only in wet periods. In addition, the rivers deposit fertile silt on the plains, which gives productive land for crops. As a result, farming in these areas depends heavily on how well the river water is shared and managed, which is why irrigation and water storage are so important to agricultural output."

Each paragraph sentence makes a point and then develops it with a reason or result, which is how explain questions earn marks. The marks shown here are illustrative; mark allocations on the real paper vary.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm the June series date and the time of each 1 hour 30 minute paper.
- Learn key dates, people and events for the history content, and the facts and figures for the environment content.
- Practise source questions: reading, inferring, comparing and evaluating.
- On Paper 2, read all five questions before choosing three.
- Allocate time by marks: about 1.2 minutes per mark.
- Give a reason for each point, not a list of facts.
- Use examples and place names where they fit.
- Check that you have answered exactly three questions on Paper 2.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448?**
There are two externally assessed papers, each 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks. They are each worth 50% of the qualification, and there are no tiers.

**What is the structure of Paper 1?**
Section A is a compulsory source-based question worth 25 marks, and Section B is worth 50 marks. Paper 1 covers the history and culture of Pakistan.

**What is the structure of Paper 2?**
You answer three of five questions, and each question is worth 25 marks. Paper 2 covers the environment of Pakistan.

**Which exam series is available?**
This syllabus is examined in the June series only, with no November series for this IGCSE variant. The examination-series window is 2026.

**Is it the same as O Level Pakistan Studies 2059?**
The paper structure is the same, according to the verified record. They are separate syllabuses with different codes, so use the 0448 document for your IGCSE.
