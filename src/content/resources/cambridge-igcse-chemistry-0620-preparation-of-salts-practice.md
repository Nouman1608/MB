## Section B

**6.** A student makes crystals of magnesium sulfate from magnesium oxide and dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why magnesium oxide is added until some remains undissolved. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how the student obtains pure, dry crystals from the mixture. **[4]**

**7.** Sodium nitrate is made by titration.

**(a)** Name the acid and the alkali used. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why titration is used rather than adding one reactant in excess. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how the student makes sure the final crystals do not contain indicator. **[1]**

**8.** Copper(II) carbonate is added to dilute hydrochloric acid until it is in excess.

**(a)** State **two** observations during the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** State how the student knows that all the acid has reacted. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the salt formed and write the balanced equation. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Lead(II) iodide is insoluble in water.

**(a)** Name **two** solutions that could be mixed to make lead(II) iodide. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation for its formation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how a pure, dry sample of the precipitate is obtained. **[3]**

**10.** For each salt, name the method of preparation and give a reason.

**(a)** ammonium sulfate **[2]**

**(b)** iron(II) chloride **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* calcium carbonate **[2]**

**11.** Blue copper(II) sulfate crystals were dried in a very hot oven and turned white.

**(a)** Explain the colour change. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest how the crystals should have been dried. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student tries to make barium sulfate by adding solid barium carbonate to dilute sulfuric acid. The reaction soon stops even though both reactants remain. Suggest why. **[2]**

**13.** A student wants to make zinc nitrate crystals.

**(a)** Name the acid needed. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest an insoluble zinc compound to react with it, and state the sign that the acid has all reacted. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the student should not use aqueous zinc sulfate as the starting material. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Insoluble [1].

**(b)** Soluble [1].

**(c)** Insoluble [1].

**(d)** Soluble [1].

**2.** **A** [1]. Zinc carbonate is insoluble, so an excess can be added and filtered off.

**3.** A substance containing no water [1].

**4.** Copper does not react with dilute acids / is too unreactive [1].

**5.** CoCl₂·6H₂O [1].

**6. (a)** MgO + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂O [1].

**(b)** To make sure all the sulfuric acid has reacted [1].

**(c)** Filter to remove excess magnesium oxide [1]; heat the filtrate to evaporate water until saturated / crystals form on a glass rod [1]; leave to cool so crystals form [1]; filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers [1].

**7. (a)** Nitric acid [1]; sodium hydroxide [1].

**(b)** Both reactants are soluble, so an excess could not be removed by filtering [1].

**(c)** Repeat the titration with the same volumes but without indicator [1].

**8. (a)** Effervescence / bubbles [1]; green solid dissolves and a blue-green solution forms [1].

**(b)** Effervescence stops and some solid remains [1].

**(c)** Copper(II) chloride [1]; CuCO₃ + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [1].

**9. (a)** Lead(II) nitrate [1]; potassium iodide or sodium iodide [1].

**(b)** Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s): correct balanced ionic equation [1]; state symbols [1].

**(c)** Filter [1]; wash the residue with distilled water [1]; dry in a warm oven or between filter papers [1].

**10. (a)** Titration [1]; ammonium salts are soluble and the alkali (aqueous ammonia) is soluble, so excess could not be filtered off [1].

**(b)** Excess iron (or excess iron(II) carbonate) with hydrochloric acid, then filter and crystallise [1]; the salt is soluble and the solid can be filtered off [1].

**(c)** Precipitation [1]; calcium carbonate is insoluble, so it is made by mixing solutions of calcium nitrate and sodium carbonate [1].

**11. (a)** Heating drove off the water of crystallisation [1]; leaving white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate [1].

**(b)** Between filter papers / in a warm (not hot) place [1].

**12.** Barium sulfate is insoluble [1]; it forms a layer on the barium carbonate that stops the acid reaching it [1].

**13. (a)** Nitric acid [1].

**(b)** Zinc carbonate (or zinc oxide) [1]; effervescence stops / solid remains undissolved [1] (for zinc oxide: solid stops dissolving).

**(c)** Zinc sulfate and nitric acid do not react to give zinc nitrate on its own; the sulfate ions would stay in solution and contaminate any crystals [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating "excess" or not explaining its purpose.
- Writing "evaporate to dryness" instead of heating to saturation and cooling.
- Forgetting to filter before crystallising, or to wash a precipitate.
- Choosing titration for a reaction where one reactant is an insoluble solid.
- Suggesting an insoluble starting material in a precipitation.
- Misremembering the exceptions to the chloride and sulfate rules.
