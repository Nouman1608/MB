## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between shortwave and longwave radiation in the Earth's energy balance. **[2]**

**2.** State two natural factors that can change the global energy balance. **[2]**

**3.** Outline why a large volcanic eruption causes global cooling that lasts only a year or two. **[2]**

**4.** Distinguish between a positive feedback and a negative feedback in the climate system. **[2]**

**5.** Outline one reason why methane has a larger warming effect per molecule than carbon dioxide, but a shorter-lived one. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A planet receives 340 W per square metre of solar radiation averaged over its surface.

**(a)** Calculate the energy absorbed if its albedo is 0.30. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy absorbed if melting ice lowers its albedo to 0.28. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the change in (b) is described as part of a positive feedback. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows invented emissions data for two countries.

| | Country S | Country T |
|---|---|---|
| Population (millions) | 200 | 40 |
| Territorial emissions (Mt) | 1,200 | 480 |
| Emissions embodied in exports (Mt) | 300 | 60 |
| Emissions embodied in imports (Mt) | 50 | 140 |

**(a)** Calculate the territorial emissions per person for each country. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the consumption-based emissions per person for each country. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why the two measures give different pictures of responsibility. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how a weakening of the thermohaline circulation could change the regional energy balance of north-west Europe. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why thawing permafrost is described as a carbon store becoming a carbon source. **[3]**

## Section C

**10.** Examine the relative importance of natural and human factors in causing changes to the global energy balance. **[10]**

**11.** "The countries that emit the most greenhouse gases are not always the countries most responsible for climate change." Discuss this statement. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Shortwave radiation is the high-energy, short-wavelength radiation emitted by the very hot Sun, arriving as incoming solar energy [1]; longwave radiation is the infrared energy emitted by the much cooler Earth's surface and atmosphere, leaving towards space [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: variations in solar output, for example over the sunspot cycle [1]; volcanic aerosols causing global dimming [1]; changes in the Earth's orbit and tilt [1]; natural changes in albedo, such as snow cover [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The eruption injects sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere, where it forms sulphate aerosols that reflect incoming sunlight [1]. The aerosols gradually fall out of the atmosphere, so the reflection, and the cooling, fades within one to two years [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A positive feedback amplifies the original change, pushing the system further from its starting state [1]; a negative feedback counteracts the change, moving the system back towards balance [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Methane absorbs strongly at infrared wavelengths that are not already saturated by other gases, so each molecule traps more energy [1], but it is broken down chemically in the atmosphere within about a decade, whereas much carbon dioxide remains for centuries [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 340 x (1 - 0.30) = 340 x 0.70 = **238 W per square metre** [1].

**(b)** 340 x (1 - 0.28) = 340 x 0.72 = **244.8 W per square metre** [1].

**(c)** The lower albedo means 6.8 W per square metre more energy is absorbed, so the surface warms further [1]; further warming melts more ice and lowers albedo again, so the change amplifies itself [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** S: 1,200 / 200 = **6.0 t per person** [1]. T: 480 / 40 = **12.0 t per person** [1].

**(b)** S: 1,200 - 300 + 50 = 950 Mt, so 950 / 200 = **4.75 t per person** [1]. T: 480 - 60 + 140 = 560 Mt, so 560 / 40 = **14.0 t per person** [1].

**(c)** Country S manufactures goods for export, so its territorial figure includes emissions caused by other countries' consumption [1]; Country T imports emission-intensive goods, so its territorial figure hides emissions its residents caused abroad, and the gap between the countries widens from 2 times to almost 3 times on a consumption basis [1]. **[6]**

**8.** The thermohaline circulation carries warm surface water north into the North Atlantic, where heat is released to the atmosphere [1]. Meltwater and extra rainfall make surface water less salty and less dense, so less water sinks and the circulation weakens [1]. Less heat would then be transferred northward [1], so north-west Europe could receive less energy than at present relative to the warming elsewhere, with possible changes to its temperatures and rainfall patterns [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Permafrost holds large amounts of organic matter that froze before it decomposed, so it has stored carbon for long periods [1]. Warming thaws the ground and microbes decompose the material, releasing carbon dioxide, or methane in waterlogged soils [1]. The released gases enhance the greenhouse effect, causing more thaw, so the store becomes a source and drives a positive feedback [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Indicative content; award one mark per valid, developed point up to 10:

- Defines the energy balance as incoming shortwave against outgoing longwave radiation, with change occurring when either side alters [1].
- Orbital variations change the distribution of solar energy over tens of thousands of years and pace glacial cycles, so they are important over geological time but too slow to explain recent change [1].
- Solar output varies slightly over the sunspot cycle; it has not risen since the mid-20th century while temperatures have, so it cannot explain recent warming [1].
- Volcanic eruptions cause global dimming, but the effect lasts only one to two years [1].
- Human emissions of carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide have enhanced the greenhouse effect, with concentrations rising since industrialisation [1].
- Human land use changes albedo, for example through deforestation and urbanisation [1].
- Feedbacks blur the distinction: ice-albedo, water vapour and permafrost feedbacks are natural processes triggered by human-caused warming [1].
- Uses a located example accurately, such as the cooling after the eruption of Mount Pinatubo in 1991 [1].
- Weighs factors by timescale: natural factors dominate over very long periods, human factors over the last century [1].
- Conclusion: a supported judgement that recent change is driven mainly by human factors amplified by natural feedbacks [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Indicative content; award one mark per valid, developed point up to 10:

- Distinguishes total, per-capita, cumulative and consumption-based measures of emissions [1].
- The largest current annual emitter, China, has high totals partly because of its population and its role as a manufacturing exporter [1].
- On a per-capita basis, many high-income and oil-producing countries emit far more per person than large emerging economies such as India [1].
- Cumulative emissions place more responsibility on countries that industrialised early, because carbon dioxide persists for centuries; the United States has the largest cumulative total [1].
- Consumption-based accounting moves emissions from exporters to importers, raising the footprint of many high-income consumers [1].
- Counter-argument: current totals determine how fast concentrations rise now, so large emitters matter most for the future regardless of history [1].
- Counter-argument: emerging economies have chosen coal-based growth and benefit from the exports they produce [1].
- Links to development: richer countries gained wealth from past emissions and have more capacity to cut [1].
- Considers that responsibility within countries also varies by income [1].
- Conclusion: a supported judgement, for example that responsibility depends on the measure, and that a fair assessment combines cumulative, per-capita and consumption-based views [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" rewards a balanced weighing of measures, with each point tied to a named country or a clearly defined measure, and a conclusion that answers the statement directly.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that greenhouse gases trap sunlight rather than outgoing longwave radiation.
- Forgetting to subtract export emissions and add import emissions in consumption-based calculations.
- Describing a feedback without closing the loop back to the starting change.
- Treating natural factors as irrelevant instead of explaining why their timing does not fit recent warming.
