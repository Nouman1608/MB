## The four equations and relationships

```
law of reflection        i = r
refractive index         n = sin i / sin r      (i in air)
critical angle           n = 1 / sin c
linear magnification     = image length / object length   (no unit)
```

Leaving a material into air: sin (angle in air) = n × sin (angle inside).

## Reflection (3.2.1)

- Normal: line at 90° to the surface where the ray strikes. Every angle is
  measured from it.
- Plane-mirror image: virtual, upright, same size, same distance behind the
  mirror as the object is in front.
- Law of reflection experiment: ray box, mirror on paper, normal drawn,
  mark the reflected ray with two crosses, measure i and r for several
  angles.
- Image experiment: two pins sighted in line with the image of an object
  pin from two positions; extend the sight lines behind the mirror — they
  cross at the image. Measure both distances from the mirror line.

## Refraction (3.2.2)

- Into a denser medium: slower, bends towards the normal.
- Out into air: faster, bends away from the normal.
- Along the normal: speed changes, no change of direction.
- Rectangular block: emergent ray parallel to incident ray, shifted
  sideways. Triangular prism: ray deviated towards the base.
- Semicircular block: aim at the centre of the flat face so the ray crosses
  the curved face along a normal — used to show internal reflection.

## Total internal reflection

Conditions, both needed:

1. Light travels from the denser medium towards the less dense one
   (glass → air, water → air).
2. Angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.

At exactly c, the refracted ray runs along the boundary. Below c, a
refracted ray plus a weak reflected ray. Above c, reflection only.

Higher n → smaller c. Typical values: n = 1.50 gives c = 41.8°; n = 1.33
gives c = 48.8°.

**Optical fibres in telecommunications:** light is totally internally
reflected along the fibre. Advantages: much more information per second
than copper cable, no electrical interference, thin and light.

## Thin lenses (3.2.3)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Principal axis | Line through the lens centre at 90° to the lens |
| Principal focus (F) | Point where a parallel beam converges (converging lens) or appears to diverge from (diverging lens) |
| Focal length | Distance from lens centre to F |
| Real image | Formed where rays actually converge; can be caught on a screen |
| Virtual image | Formed where diverging rays appear to come from; cannot be caught on a screen |
| Linear magnification | Image length ÷ object length |

**Converging lens images**

| Object position | Image |
|---|---|
| Beyond 2F | Real, inverted, diminished, between F and 2F |
| At 2F | Real, inverted, same size, at 2F |
| Between F and 2F | Real, inverted, magnified, beyond 2F |
| Inside F | Virtual, upright, magnified, same side as object |

Diverging lens: always virtual, upright, diminished.

Magnifying glass: converging lens with the object inside F.

**Ray-diagram rules:** parallel ray → through F; ray through the centre →
straight on; ray through near F → parallel. Ruler, arrows, dashed lines for
virtual extensions.

**The eye**

| Eye | Where distant/near light focuses | Correcting lens |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | On the retina | None |
| Short-sighted | Distant objects: in front of the retina | Diverging |
| Long-sighted | Near objects: behind the retina | Converging |

## Dispersion (3.2.4)

- A prism refracts each colour by a different amount; violet most, red
  least.
- Increasing frequency: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
- Increasing wavelength: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Angle of incidence | Angle between the incident ray and the normal |
| Angle of reflection | Angle between the reflected ray and the normal |
| Angle of refraction | Angle between the refracted ray and the normal |
| Refractive index | sin i ÷ sin r, for light passing from air into the material |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90° |
| Total internal reflection | All the light reflected back inside the denser medium, when i is greater than c |
| Dispersion | Separation of white light into its colours by refraction |

## Exam traps

- An angle given "to the surface" must be subtracted from 90° first.
- n is always greater than 1 for glass and water. If you get n < 1, you
  inverted the sines.
- Total internal reflection never happens when light enters glass from air.
- In the mirror-distance question, object and image both move, so the gap
  changes by twice the distance moved.
- A real image formed by a single lens is always inverted; a virtual image
  from a single lens is always upright.
- Magnification is a ratio: no unit, and use the same unit for both lengths.
- Frequency of light does not change on refraction; speed and wavelength do.
- Red has the longest wavelength and lowest frequency of the visible
  colours, so it is refracted least.

## Self-test

1. A ray meets a mirror at 20° to its surface. State the angle of
   reflection.
2. A ray enters glass at i = 40°; r = 25°. Calculate n.
3. State the two conditions for total internal reflection.
4. Calculate the critical angle for a material of refractive index 1.60.
5. A material has a critical angle of 42°. Find its refractive index.
6. State the nature of the image formed by a plane mirror.
7. An object is placed between F and 2F of a converging lens. Describe the
   image.
8. A 2.0 cm object gives a 5.0 cm image. Find the magnification.
9. Which lens corrects short sight, and why?
10. Where must the object be for a converging lens to act as a magnifying
    glass?
11. Which colour of visible light has the highest frequency?
12. Give two advantages of optical fibres over copper cables for
    telecommunications.

**Answers**

1. Angle of incidence = 90° − 20° = 70°, so the angle of reflection is
   **70°**.
2. n = sin 40° / sin 25° = 0.643 / 0.423 = **1.52**.
3. Light travelling from the denser to the less dense medium; angle of
   incidence greater than the critical angle.
4. sin c = 1 / 1.60 = 0.625, so c = **38.7°**.
5. n = 1 / sin 42° = 1 / 0.669 = **1.49**.
6. Virtual, upright, same size, as far behind the mirror as the object is in
   front.
7. Real, inverted, magnified, beyond 2F on the other side.
8. 5.0 / 2.0 = **2.5** (no unit).
9. A diverging lens: it spreads the rays from a distant object slightly so
   that the eye focuses them on the retina rather than in front of it.
10. Closer to the lens than the principal focus.
11. **Violet**.
12. More information carried per second; not affected by electrical
    interference (also accept: thinner and lighter).
