## League and UN compared

| | League of Nations | United Nations |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | 1920 (Covenant 1919) | 1945 (Charter, San Francisco) |
| Great powers | USA never joined; Britain and France led | USA and USSR both permanent members |
| Decisions | Council unanimity | Security Council majority, but P5 veto |
| Force | No army; relied on sanctions | Peacekeeping forces from members |
| Rights | No general human rights statement | Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) |
| Agencies | ILO, Refugees, Health, Drugs, Slavery, Mandates | ILO, WHO, FAO, UNESCO, UNICEF, UNHCR, Trusteeship |

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Collective security | Members acting together against an aggressor |
| Covenant / Charter | Founding documents of the League / UN |
| Sanctions | Economic penalties |
| Mandate | Former colony run by a League member under supervision |
| Veto | Right of a permanent member to block a decision |
| Peacekeeping | UN forces supervising ceasefires and protecting civilians |
| Identity war | Conflict based on ethnic or religious identity |

## Worked example: explain two ways different

*Explain two ways in which the UN in 1950 was different from the League in 1931.* (6 marks)

1. **Membership:** in 1931 the USA was not a member and the USSR had not joined; in 1950 both were
   permanent members of the Security Council.
2. **Force:** in 1931 the League could only condemn Japan over Manchuria; in 1950 the UN authorised an armed
   force to defend South Korea.

## Essay plan in outline

*"The UN was more successful than the League in keeping the peace in the years 1919–2011." How far do
you agree?*

- **For the UN:** wider membership including both superpowers; peacekeeping forces; successes in Korea,
  Namibia and Mozambique; no third world war.
- **For the League (or against the UN):** the League's 1920s settlements; UN failures in the Middle East,
  Lebanon, Somalia and Srebrenica; the veto paralysed the Council in the Cold War; Kosovo and Iraq bypassed
  it.
- **Judgement:** the UN lasted and achieved more, but both depended on great-power agreement.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Saying the League had a veto by name.** Its Council worked by unanimity, which had the same effect.
- **Treating the agencies as minor.** They were often the most successful part of both organisations.
- **Forgetting the USSR's boycott in 1950.** It explains how the Korea resolution passed.
- **Narrating cases without judging success.** Always say how far each succeeded.
- **Stopping at 1989.** Topic 5 runs to 2011.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did the USA not join the League?**
The Senate refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles, fearing
foreign commitments.

**What made the veto a structural problem?**
Any permanent member could block action, especially against
itself or its allies.

**Why was Mozambique a success?**
Both sides wanted peace, the mandate was clear and elections were held.
