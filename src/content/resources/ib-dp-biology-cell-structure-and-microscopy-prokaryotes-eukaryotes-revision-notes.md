## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cell theory | Organisms are made of cells; the cell is the smallest unit of life; cells arise from existing cells |
| Deduction | Using a general theory to predict a specific case |
| Resolution | How well a microscope separates two close points |
| Magnification | Image size ÷ actual size |
| Graticule | Scale in the eyepiece, calibrated with a stage micrometer |
| Scale bar | A line on an image that stands for a stated real length |
| Compartmentalization | Division of the cytoplasm by membranes into regions with different conditions |
| Nucleoid | Region of a prokaryote cell holding its naked DNA |
| Atypical cell | One whose number of nuclei differs from the usual one |
| Differentiation (HL only) | Cells becoming specialized through different gene expression |

## Calculations

| To find | Use | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Magnification | image size ÷ actual size | none (write ×) |
| Actual size | image size ÷ magnification | same as image size |
| Image size | actual size × magnification | same as actual size |
| Scale bar length | real length × magnification | convert to mm |
| One graticule division | stage micrometer length ÷ graticule divisions | µm |

Conversions: 1 mm = 1000 µm. 1 µm = 1000 nm.

### Method in steps

1. Measure image length with a ruler, in mm.
2. Convert actual size to mm (or image size to µm).
3. Apply the triangle once.
4. Check that the answer is a sensible size for a cell or organelle, and write the unit.

### Worked reminder

A micrograph has a 15 mm scale bar labelled 0.5 µm. Magnification = 15 mm ÷ 0.5 µm = 15 000 µm ÷ 0.5 µm = ×30 000. A mitochondrion 24 mm wide on the print is 24 ÷ 30 000 mm = 0.0008 mm = 0.8 µm.

### Two more reminders

**Graticule.** Sixty graticule divisions line up with 33 stage micrometer divisions of 10 µm. One division = 330 ÷ 60 = 5.5 µm. A cell 9 divisions long is 9 × 5.5 = 49.5 µm.

**Scale bar.** A 0.2 µm bar on an image at ×45 000 is 0.2 × 45 000 = 9000 µm = 9 mm long.

Under a different objective lens the graticule value changes, so recalibrate. A scale bar stays correct when an image is enlarged or reduced, but a stated magnification does not.

## Developments in microscopy

| Technique | Key idea | Benefit or cost |
|---|---|---|
| Electron microscopy | Electron beam, much shorter wavelength than light | High resolution, shows ultrastructure; dead, stained specimens, possible artefacts |
| Freeze fracture | Frozen specimen cracked along the middle of membranes | Shows proteins within the membrane |
| Cryo-EM | Very rapid freezing; no stain or fixing | Molecules close to natural state |
| Fluorescent stain | Dye emits light after absorbing light | Stained structure glows |
| Immunofluorescence | Fluorescent antibody binds a chosen protein | Shows where a protein is located |

## Structures in all cells

- DNA: stores genetic information.
- Cytoplasm, mostly water: medium for metabolic reactions.
- Plasma membrane of lipids: barrier that separates the cell from its surroundings.

## Prokaryote versus eukaryote

| Feature | Prokaryote (Gram-positive bacterium) | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| DNA | Naked loop in the nucleoid region | Chromosomes of DNA bound to histones, in a nucleus |
| Nuclear envelope | None | Double membrane with pores |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S in cytoplasm |
| Organelles | No membrane-bound organelles | Mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes and others |
| Cell wall | Present | Plants and fungi only |
| Cytoskeleton | Not listed for prokaryotes in the guide | Microtubules and microfilaments |

## Prokaryote components and their jobs

| Component | Job |
|---|---|
| Cell wall | Supports the cell and resists bursting |
| Plasma membrane | Controls what enters and leaves |
| Cytoplasm | Medium for metabolism, holds ribosomes |
| Naked DNA in a loop | Carries genetic information, lies in the nucleoid region |
| 70S ribosomes | Make proteins |

Prokaryote structure varies, but the guide asks only for the Gram-positive model (*Bacillus*, *Staphylococcus*).

## Eukaryote organelles

| Organelle | Function |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Holds DNA, controls gene expression |
| Mitochondrion | ATP production by aerobic respiration |
| Rough ER | Protein synthesis for secretion |
| Smooth ER | Lipid synthesis |
| Golgi apparatus | Modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles |
| Lysosome | Digests unwanted material |
| Sap vacuole | Storage and turgor in plants |
| Cytoskeleton | Support and movement |

## Processes of life

Remember the eight: reproduction, growth, metabolism, nutrition, excretion, movement, homeostasis and response to stimuli. For a named unicellular organism, give a structure for each. Example: *Paramecium* uses cilia to move and a contractile vacuole for water balance.

For each eukaryote, the cytoplasm is compartmentalized, so reactions with different conditions run side by side in separate membrane-bound spaces.

## Animals, fungi and plants

| Feature | Animal | Fungus | Plant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | No | Chitin | Cellulose |
| Vacuole | Small | Varies | Large central |
| Plastids | No | No | Yes |
| Centrioles | Yes | Generally no | No (flowering plants) |
| Cilia and flagella | Often | Rare | Absent (flowering plants) |

## Atypical cells

| Cell | Nuclei | Link to function |
|---|---|---|
| Aseptate hypha | Many, in one cytoplasm | Continuous flow of material |
| Skeletal muscle fibre | Many | Formed by fusion; long cell |
| Mature red blood cell (mammal) | None | More room for haemoglobin |
| Phloem sieve tube element | None when mature | Open channel for sap |

## Micrograph clues and annotation

- Prokaryote: tiny, no nucleus, nucleoid.
- Plant: wall, large vacuole, chloroplasts.
- Animal: nucleus, no wall; microvilli suggest absorption.
- Drawing: pencil, clean lines, no shading, ruled labels, scale bar.
- Annotating means naming the structure and stating its function.
- Secretory vesicles carry products to the plasma membrane; microvilli increase surface area; the plasma membrane is a thin double line.

## HL only: origins and specialization

**Endosymbiosis (A2.2.12).** Mitochondria arose from an engulfed aerobic prokaryote. Chloroplasts arose later in some eukaryotes in a similar way. Evidence: 70S ribosomes, naked circular DNA, ability to replicate.

**Differentiation (A2.2.13).** Same genes, different expression, often triggered by the environment.

**Multicellularity (A2.2.14).** Evolved repeatedly. Advantages: larger body size, cell specialization.

## Must-know distinctions

- Prokaryote DNA is naked; eukaryote DNA is bound to histones.
- 70S ribosomes (prokaryotes, mitochondria, chloroplasts) versus 80S (eukaryotic cytoplasm).
- Resolution is not magnification. More magnification does not give more detail if resolution is low.
- Plant cell walls contain cellulose; fungal walls contain chitin.
- Rough ER has ribosomes and makes proteins; smooth ER lacks them and makes lipids.

## Quick self-test

1. A cell is 7 µm across. How long is its image at ×4000? Give the answer in mm.
2. An image is 35 mm long at ×5000. What is the actual length in µm?
3. How long is a 2.5 µm scale bar drawn on an image at ×8000?
4. Convert 0.2 mm to µm and 750 nm to µm.
5. Name the three structures found in all cells.
6. State two differences between prokaryote and eukaryote ribosomes or DNA.
7. Give one function of the Golgi apparatus.
8. State two ways a plant cell differs from an animal cell.
9. Explain why a mature mammalian red blood cell can contain more haemoglobin than a nucleated cell.
10. (HL only) State two pieces of evidence for the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria.
11. A scale bar on a micrograph is 12 mm long and is labelled 0.25 µm. Calculate the magnification.
12. A plant cell measures 90 mm on an image at ×3000. Calculate its actual length in µm.

### Answers

1. 7 µm × 4000 = 28 000 µm = **28 mm**.
2. 35 mm ÷ 5000 = 0.007 mm = **7 µm**.
3. 2.5 µm × 8000 = 20 000 µm = **20 mm**.
4. 0.2 mm = **200 µm**. 750 nm = **0.75 µm**.
5. DNA, cytoplasm, plasma membrane.
6. Prokaryote ribosomes are 70S, eukaryote 80S. Prokaryote DNA is naked and in a loop; eukaryote DNA is bound to histones in a nucleus.
7. It modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles.
8. Plant cells have a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large sap vacuole (any two).
9. It has no nucleus, so there is more volume available for haemoglobin.
10. Any two of: 70S ribosomes, naked circular DNA, ability to replicate.
11. 12 mm = 12 000 µm. 12 000 ÷ 0.25 = **×48 000**.
12. 90 mm ÷ 3000 = 0.03 mm = **30 µm**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting units before applying the magnification triangle.
- Giving a magnification without the multiplication sign, or an actual size without a unit.
- Writing that prokaryotes have no DNA or no ribosomes.
- Listing an organelle without a function when asked to annotate.
- Saying a structure is "in plants" without saying which cell is in the micrograph.
- Confusing resolution with magnification.
- Saying differentiating cells lose genes, rather than switching genes on and off.
- Stating evidence for endosymbiosis without linking it to prokaryotes (HL only).
