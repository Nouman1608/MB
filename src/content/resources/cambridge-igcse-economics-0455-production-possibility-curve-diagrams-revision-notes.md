These notes condense section 1.4 (production possibility curve diagrams) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All countries and figures are invented. The worked explanations are in the [PPC diagrams study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-production-possibility-curve-diagrams/); test yourself with the [PPC diagrams practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-production-possibility-curve-diagrams-practice/). The idea behind the slope is revised in the [opportunity cost revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-opportunity-cost-revision-notes/).

## 1.4.1 Definition and drawing

- **PPC:** the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce when all resources are fully and efficiently used, with given technology.
- **Assumptions:** two goods; fixed quantity and quality of resources; fixed technology.
- **What it illustrates:** scarcity (limits to output), choice (which point) and opportunity cost (the slope).

Drawing rules:

1. Name each axis with the good and the unit.
2. Plot the schedule and join the points with a smooth curve meeting both axes.
3. Label the curve PPC (or PPC1 and PPC2 for a shift) and letter every point you mention.
4. Show any shift with an arrow from the old curve to the new one.

| Curve shape | Opportunity cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bowed outward (concave to the origin) | Increasing | Resources are specialised |
| Straight line | Constant | Resources equally suited to both goods |

## 1.4.2 Location of points

| Point | Meaning | Is more of one good possible without less of the other? |
|---|---|---|
| On | Full, efficient use of resources | No |
| Under | Unemployed or inefficiently used resources | Yes |
| Beyond | Unattainable now | Not until capacity grows |

Method for a data question:

```
Find the curve's output of good B at the given output of good A
Compare: actual below curve value = under; equal = on; above = beyond
Lost output = curve value - actual value
```

Example (Country Rendo, invented): the curve allows 70 units of steel at 15 units of wheat. Rendo makes 56 units of steel at 15 units of wheat.

```
Lost steel = 70 - 56 = 14 units
14 / 70 x 100 = 20% of possible steel output
```

## 1.4.3 Movements along the curve

- A movement along the curve reallocates fully used resources between the two goods.
- More of one good always means less of the other.
- **Opportunity cost = good lost / good gained** (state both units).

Example schedule (Rendo):

| Point | Wheat | Steel |
|---|---|---|
| P | 0 | 100 |
| Q | 15 | 70 |
| R | 25 | 40 |
| S | 30 | 0 |

```
P to Q: 30 steel lost / 15 wheat gained = 2 steel per wheat
Q to R: 30 steel lost / 10 wheat gained = 3 steel per wheat
R to S: 40 steel lost / 5 wheat gained = 8 steel per wheat
```

The cost per unit of wheat rises at each step: increasing opportunity cost.

## 1.4.4 Shifts of the curve

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| More or better factors that help both goods (education, net investment, larger workforce) | Whole curve outward |
| Improvement for one good only (a new crop variety) | Pivot outward on that good's axis |
| Disaster, war, emigration, disease, depletion | Inward (whole or on one axis) |
| Fall in unemployment | No shift; movement from under the curve towards it |

**Consequences of an outward shift:** more of both goods possible, higher potential living standards, higher incomes and tax revenue; possible costs include pollution and depletion that may later reduce capacity.

**Consequences of an inward shift:** lower potential output, lower incomes, fewer goods and services.

Example of a pivot (Rendo): a new seed raises maximum wheat from 30 to 36.

```
Growth in wheat capacity = (36 - 30) / 30 x 100 = 20%
Maximum steel unchanged at 100
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Production possibility curve | Maximum combinations of two goods with full, efficient use of resources |
| Productive efficiency | Producing on the curve, with no waste of resources |
| Unattainable | Beyond the curve with present resources and technology |
| Increasing opportunity cost | Each extra unit of one good costs more of the other |
| Movement along the PPC | Reallocating resources between the two goods |
| Outward shift | A rise in productive capacity: economic growth |
| Inward shift | A fall in productive capacity |

## Exam traps

- Unlabelled axes or curves lose marks even when the shape is right.
- A fall in unemployment is a movement towards the curve, not a shift.
- A point beyond the curve is unattainable, not inefficient.
- Divide the good lost by the good gained, not the reverse.
- A cause affecting one good only gives a pivot, not a full shift.
- A straight-line PPC means constant, not zero, opportunity cost.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a production possibility curve.
2. What does a point under the curve show?
3. Why is a typical PPC bowed outward?
4. Using Rendo's schedule, calculate the opportunity cost of one unit of wheat between Q and R.
5. Moving from S to R, what is the opportunity cost of one unit of steel?
6. Name two causes of an outward shift of the whole curve.
7. A flood destroys farmland. Which way does the curve move, and on which axis?
8. Is a fall in unemployment a shift of the PPC? Explain briefly.
9. Rendo's curve allows 40 steel at 25 wheat. It produces 32 steel at 25 wheat. Calculate the steel lost.
10. What does a straight-line PPC show?
11. State one consequence of an outward shift for living standards.
12. Why can a point beyond the curve not be produced now?

### Answers

1. The maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce when all resources are fully and efficiently used with given technology.
2. Unemployed or inefficiently used resources.
3. Resources are specialised, so moving more of them to one good draws in factors less suited to it, raising opportunity cost.
4. 30 steel lost / 10 wheat gained = 3 units of steel per unit of wheat.
5. 5 wheat lost / 40 steel gained = 0.125 units of wheat per unit of steel.
6. Any two: discovery of resources, a larger labour force, net investment, education and training, better technology.
7. Inward, on the axis of the farm good; the other intercept may be unchanged.
8. No. Capacity is unchanged; the economy moves from under the curve towards it.
9. 40 - 32 = 8 units of steel.
10. Constant opportunity cost, because resources are equally suited to both goods.
11. More goods and services can be produced, so potential living standards rise.
12. The economy lacks the resources or technology to reach it; it needs growth (an outward shift).
