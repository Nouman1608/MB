## Section A

**1.** Define the term ecosystem. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each of the following as biotic or abiotic: rainfall, earthworms, sunlight, oak trees. **[2]**

**3.** State the role of a producer in an ecosystem. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** types of decomposer. **[2]**

**5.** State what the arrows in a food chain show. **[1]**

## Section B

A small woodland ecosystem contains: oak trees, grass, caterpillars, rabbits, blue tits, foxes, owls, fungi and earthworms. Blue tits eat caterpillars; rabbits eat grass; foxes eat rabbits; owls eat blue tits and rabbits.

**6.** Use this information to write **two** food chains, each with at least three organisms. **[2]**

**7.** Explain what would happen to the woodland ecosystem if disease killed most of the rabbits. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how nutrients are cycled in this woodland. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how adding fertiliser to fields next to a pond could affect the pond ecosystem. **[6]**

**10.** Describe the global distribution of tropical rainforests and hot deserts. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why the climate of the tundra limits plant growth. **[3]**

**12.** Explain how the general atmospheric circulation helps to explain the location of tropical rainforests and hot deserts. **[4]**

**13.** "Decomposers are the most important component of an ecosystem." Do you agree? Justify your answer. **[6]**

**14.** Compare the characteristics of the temperate deciduous forest and the boreal forest biomes. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A community of living things and the non-living environment with which they interact [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Biotic: earthworms, oak trees [1]. Abiotic: rainfall, sunlight [1]. **[2]**

**3.** It makes its own food by photosynthesis, using sunlight, so it is the source of energy for the rest of the ecosystem [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Bacteria [1]; fungi [1]. (Accept earthworms as a detritivore that helps decomposition.) **[2]**

**5.** The direction in which energy flows, from the organism eaten to the organism that eats it [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: oak tree → caterpillar → blue tit → owl [1]; grass → rabbit → fox [1]; grass → rabbit → owl [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Foxes and owls lose a food source, so their numbers may fall [1]. Owls may eat more blue tits instead, reducing blue tit numbers [1], which could let caterpillars increase and damage more oak leaves [1]. With fewer rabbits grazing, grass may grow taller and spread [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Oak trees and grass take up nutrients from the soil [1]. Caterpillars and rabbits gain nutrients by eating plants, and predators gain them by eating these animals [1]. Dead leaves, dead animals and droppings fall to the ground [1], where fungi and bacteria, helped by earthworms, break them down and return nutrients to the soil for plants to reuse [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Fertiliser washes into the pond, adding nitrates and phosphates [1]. Algae grow rapidly, forming a bloom on the surface [1]. The bloom blocks light, so plants below die [1]. When algae and plants die, decomposers multiply and use up dissolved oxygen [1]. Fish and other animals die from lack of oxygen [1], and predators such as herons lose their food and leave, so the whole food web is disrupted [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Tropical rainforests lie in a belt close to the equator [1], for example in the Amazon Basin, the Congo Basin and South-East Asia [1]. Hot deserts lie further from the equator, mostly around 15-30° north and south [1], for example the Sahara, the Arabian Desert and central Australia [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Temperatures are very low for most of the year [1], so the growing season is short [1]. The ground below the surface stays frozen, so roots cannot grow deep and drainage is poor; plants are low-growing mosses, lichens and shrubs [1]. **[3]**

**12.** At the equator, intense heating makes air rise [1]; as it rises it cools and condenses, giving heavy rainfall all year that supports rainforest [1]. This air spreads away from the equator and sinks around 30° north and south [1]; sinking air warms and creates high pressure with few clouds and little rain, producing hot deserts [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: decomposers release nutrients from dead material, without which producers would run short [1].
- Agree: without nutrient cycling, the whole food web would eventually decline [1].
- Disagree: producers are the only route by which energy enters the ecosystem, so nothing could survive without them [1].
- Disagree: abiotic components such as sunlight and water are essential for all life in the system [1].
- Components are interdependent, so removing any one disrupts the rest [1].
- Judgement: decomposers are essential, but no single component is "most important" because each depends on the others; a justified choice of producers is also credited [1]. **[6]**

**14.** Both are forest biomes outside the tropics [1]. Temperate deciduous forest has mild conditions with rainfall all year, while boreal forest has long, very cold winters and short summers [1]. Deciduous trees lose their leaves in winter, whereas boreal conifers such as pine and spruce are mostly evergreen [1]. Temperate deciduous forest lies in places such as western Europe and the eastern USA; boreal forest lies further north, across northern Canada, Scandinavia and Russia [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Food chains with arrows pointing the wrong way.
- Comparisons that describe one biome and then the other without linking them.
- Describing only one effect of a change rather than a chain through the web.
- Calling soil biotic.
- Naming biomes without locations or latitudes.
- Explaining desert locations without sinking air and high pressure.
- Extended answers that agree or disagree without a justified judgement.
