**Key question 2.5 in Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O Level 2147 is answered
through three case studies only: Korea 1950–53, Cuba 1959–62 and
Vietnam 1955–75.** For each, learn the American reaction, the key
events and a verdict on how well containment worked. The full account
is in the
[containment study guide](/resources/cambridge-world-history-us-containment-korea-cuba-vietnam/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Containment** | Stopping communism spreading to new countries (Truman Doctrine, 1947) |
| **Domino theory** | If one state fell to communism, its neighbours would follow (Eisenhower, 1954) |
| **38th parallel** | Line dividing North and South Korea |
| **17th parallel** | Line dividing North and South Vietnam under the Geneva Accords (1954) |
| **Quarantine** | Kennedy's naval blockade of Cuba, October 1962 |
| **Vietnamisation** | Nixon's policy of handing the fighting to South Vietnam's army |

## Korea, 1950–53

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 25 June 1950 | North Korea invades the South |
| June 1950 | UN Security Council (USSR absent) backs military help for South Korea |
| September 1950 | Inchon landings; UN forces push north past the 38th parallel |
| Oct–Nov 1950 | China enters the war; UN forces driven back |
| April 1951 | Truman dismisses MacArthur |
| 27 July 1953 | Armistice at Panmunjom; border near the 38th parallel |

**Verdict:** containment achieved — South Korea saved — but not
"rollback", since Korea stayed divided.

## Cuba, 1959–62

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| January 1959 | Castro overthrows Batista |
| 1960 | Nationalisation of US property; US trade embargo begins |
| January 1961 | USA breaks off diplomatic relations |
| April 1961 | Bay of Pigs invasion fails |
| 14 October 1962 | U-2 photographs missile sites |
| 22 October 1962 | Kennedy announces quarantine |
| 27 October 1962 | U-2 shot down over Cuba |
| 28 October 1962 | Khrushchev agrees to remove the missiles |
| 1963 | Hotline; Partial Test Ban Treaty |

**Deal:** missiles out; US pledge not to invade Cuba; secret removal
of US Jupiter missiles from Turkey.

**Verdict:** mixed — Cuba stayed communist, but Soviet missiles were
removed without war.

## Vietnam, 1955–75

- **Why involved:** domino theory; support for Diem after the Geneva
  Accords; elections of 1956 not held.
- **Escalation:** advisers (about 16,000 by 1963) → Gulf of Tonkin
  (August 1964) → Rolling Thunder bombing and combat troops (March
  1965) → over 500,000 troops by 1968.
- **US tactics:** bombing, search and destroy, napalm, Agent Orange,
  strategic hamlets.
- **Viet Cong tactics:** guerrilla war, tunnels, Ho Chi Minh Trail,
  support from the North, China and the USSR.
- **Why withdrew:** Tet Offensive (January 1968); My Lai (1968);
  anti-war movement and casualties; Nixon's Vietnamisation; Paris
  Peace Accords (January 1973).
- **End:** Saigon falls, 30 April 1975.

**Verdict:** failure — Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia all communist by the
end of 1975; about 58,000 US dead.

## Comparing the three

The cases get harder for the USA: in Korea it fought a conventional
war with UN backing; in Cuba it faced a popular revolution 90 miles
from Florida, which it could not reverse; in Vietnam it fought a
guerrilla war for an unpopular ally. Use this pattern to build a
conclusion.

## Key dates to learn

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| March 1947 | Truman Doctrine: US support for "free peoples" resisting communism |
| 25 June 1950 | North Korea invades South Korea |
| September 1950 | Inchon landings under MacArthur |
| October–November 1950 | Chinese forces enter the war |
| April 1951 | Truman dismisses MacArthur |
| 27 July 1953 | Armistice at Panmunjom; border near the 38th parallel |
| January 1959 | Castro takes power in Cuba |
| April 1961 | Bay of Pigs invasion fails |
| October 1962 | Cuban Missile Crisis (13 days) |
| August 1964 | Gulf of Tonkin incident and resolution |
| January 1968 | Tet Offensive |
| January 1973 | Paris Peace Accords; US forces withdraw |
| 30 April 1975 | Fall of Saigon |

Learning these dates lets you frame each case study precisely, which is what moves a (c) answer from general narrative to a supported judgement.

## How to judge "effectively"

Use the same three tests for each case study, then compare:

1. **Was communism stopped from spreading there?** Korea: yes, South Korea survived; Cuba: no, Castro stayed in power; Vietnam: no, the whole country was united under communist rule in 1975.
2. **At what cost?** Lives, money and US prestige, for example the long war in Vietnam and the anti-war movement at home.
3. **Did it achieve wider aims?** For example, the removal of Soviet missiles from Cuba in 1962, even though Castro's regime survived.

## Exam traps

- Bringing in the Berlin Blockade or Marshall Plan as case studies —
  they belong to key question 2.4.
- Saying the USA "won" the Korean War: it ended in an armistice, not a
  peace treaty.
- Forgetting the secret Turkey deal when judging the Missile Crisis.
- Describing Vietnam tactics without linking them to why the USA
  failed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was the USSR not able to veto UN action in Korea?**
It was
boycotting the Security Council in 1950 in protest at Nationalist
China, not Communist China, holding China's seat.

**What was the Bay of Pigs?**
A failed invasion of Cuba in April 1961
by about 1,400 CIA-trained Cuban exiles, approved by Kennedy.

**When did the Vietnam War end?**
US combat forces left after the
Paris Peace Accords of January 1973; the war ended when Saigon fell to
North Vietnam on 30 April 1975.

## Self-test

1. What triggered the Korean War?
2. Why did China join the war in late 1950?
3. What three things were agreed to end the Missile Crisis?
4. Give two reasons why the USA withdrew from Vietnam.
5. Which case study shows containment working best? Why?

**Answers:** 1. North Korea's invasion of the South on 25 June 1950.
2. UN forces crossed the 38th parallel and advanced towards the Yalu
River on China's border. 3. Soviet missiles removed; US promise not to
invade Cuba; US missiles secretly removed from Turkey. 4. Any two: the
Tet Offensive; rising casualties and the anti-war movement; My Lai;
Nixon's Vietnamisation; the cost of the war. 5. Usually Korea, because
South Korea stayed non-communist.
