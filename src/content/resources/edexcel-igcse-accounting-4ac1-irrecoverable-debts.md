## What section 2.6 asks you to do

| Point | Specification wording |
|---|---|
| 2.6 (a) | Explain why it is necessary to provide a provision for irrecoverable debts |
| 2.6 (b) | Distinguish between an irrecoverable debt and a provision for an irrecoverable debt |
| 2.6 (c) | Calculate and record irrecoverable debts and provision for irrecoverable debts in the books of account |

The specification glossary gives "allowance for doubtful debt" as the newer name, but the content tables say "provision for irrecoverable debts", so this page uses that term.

## Two different things

An **irrecoverable debt** is an amount owed by a named customer that the business has decided it will not collect: the customer is bankrupt, has disappeared or refuses to pay and the cost of chasing exceeds the debt. The amount is removed from trade receivables and becomes an expense.

A **provision for irrecoverable debts** is an estimate, made at the year end, of how much of the *remaining* trade receivables may not be collected. No individual customer is named and no customer account is changed. The estimate is usually a percentage based on past experience.

| Feature | Irrecoverable debt | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty | Known loss | Estimated possible loss |
| Customer | Named | Not identified |
| Customer's account | Closed by a credit entry | Unchanged |
| Ledger account used | Irrecoverable debts (expense) | Provision for irrecoverable debts (credit balance) |
| Income statement | Whole amount as an expense | Only the increase (expense) or decrease (income) |
| Statement of financial position | Trade receivables shown after the write-off | Deducted from trade receivables |

## Why a provision is necessary

Build an "explain" answer from these points, each with its reason:

- **Prudence.** Assets and profit should not be overstated. Without a provision, trade receivables include amounts the business does not expect to receive.
- **Accruals (matching).** The sales that will produce these bad debts were made this year, so the expected loss belongs in this year's income statement.
- **A realistic figure for users.** The owner, lenders and suppliers judge liquidity from trade receivables; showing the amount expected to be collected helps them make sound decisions.
- **Profit available to the owner.** An overstated profit could lead the owner to withdraw cash the business will never receive.

## Calculating the provision

Always follow this order:

1. Write off the known irrecoverable debts.
2. Apply the percentage to trade receivables **after** the write-offs.
3. Compare the required provision with the balance already in the provision account.
4. Record only the change: an increase is an expense; a decrease is income.

Some questions give different percentages for debts of different ages, because older debts are less likely to be paid. Apply each rate to its own group and add the results.

## Worked example: Westbury Tools over three years

Westbury Tools has a 31 December year end and keeps a provision of 5% of trade receivables. It has no provision before 2025.

| Year | Receivables before write-offs | Written off | Receivables after write-offs | Provision required (5%) | Opening provision | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2025 | 30,400 | 400 (K. Lomax) | 30,000 | 1,500 | 0 | Increase 1,500 |
| 2026 | 37,250 | 1,250 (P. Dunne 650, S. Ray 600) | 36,000 | 1,800 | 1,500 | Increase 300 |
| 2027 | 28,900 | 900 (T. Vale) | 28,000 | 1,400 | 1,800 | Decrease 400 |

### Recording the write-offs

Journal for each write-off: debit irrecoverable debts, credit the customer's account. In 2026:

```
Irrecoverable debts account (2026)
Debit                                Credit
P. Dunne              650            Income statement      1,250
S. Ray                600
                    1,250                                  1,250
```

The customers' accounts in the receivables ledger are credited, so they close.

### The provision account over three years

```
Provision for irrecoverable debts account
Debit                                Credit
2025                                 2025
31 Dec Balance c/d     1,500         31 Dec Income statement   1,500
2026                                 2026
31 Dec Balance c/d     1,800         1 Jan  Balance b/d        1,500
                                     31 Dec Income statement     300
                       1,800                                   1,800
2027                                 2027
31 Dec Income statement  400         1 Jan  Balance b/d        1,800
31 Dec Balance c/d     1,400
                       1,800                                   1,800
2028                                 1 Jan  Balance b/d        1,400
```

Notice that the balance carried down is always the required provision, and the income statement entry is always the difference.

### Effect on the financial statements

| Year | Income statement | Statement of financial position (current assets) |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Expenses: irrecoverable debts 400; increase in provision 1,500 (total 1,900) | Trade receivables 30,000 less provision 1,500 = 28,500 |
| 2026 | Expenses: irrecoverable debts 1,250; increase in provision 300 (total 1,550) | 36,000 less 1,800 = 34,200 |
| 2027 | Expense: irrecoverable debts 900. Income: decrease in provision 400 | 28,000 less 1,400 = 26,600 |

In 2027 the net effect on profit is a reduction of 900 − 400 = 500. A decrease in the provision is not "money coming in"; it is an adjustment because the business now expects fewer bad debts than it had allowed for.

## Exam technique

- **Explain why**: give the concept and its consequence, such as "prudence, so that trade receivables are not overstated in the statement of financial position".
- **Distinguish**: compare the two on the same feature in one sentence: "an irrecoverable debt is a known loss on a named customer, whereas a provision is an estimate of possible losses on the remaining customers".
- **Calculate**: show receivables after write-offs, the required provision, the opening provision and the change.
- **Record**: balance the provision account and bring down the new balance; label income statement entries "increase in provision" or "decrease in provision".

## Common errors

- Calculating the provision on receivables before deducting the write-offs.
- Charging the whole new provision as an expense when only the increase should be charged.
- Treating a decrease in the provision as an expense.
- Crediting the customer's account when making a provision.
- Deducting the provision from trade payables or showing it as a liability.
- Forgetting that the irrecoverable debts account is closed to the income statement each year.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a provision for irrecoverable debts a liability?**
No. It is deducted from trade receivables in current assets, reducing them to the amount the business expects to collect.

**Why is only the change in the provision charged each year?**
The opening balance was charged in earlier years. Charging the whole balance again would count the same expected loss twice.

**What is the double entry for writing off an irrecoverable debt?**
Debit the irrecoverable debts account and credit the customer's account. At the year end the irrecoverable debts account is transferred to the income statement as an expense.
