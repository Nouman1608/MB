These notes condense AQA A-level Sociology (7192) Theory and Methods, the identical content listed
as 3.1.3 (Paper 1, 10 marks) and 3.3.2 (Paper 3, 30 marks). The essentials: know the strengths and
limitations of each method and source; explain positivism and interpretivism; compare structural and
social action theories; contrast modernity with post-modernity; and evaluate debates about science,
value freedom and social policy.

Full explanations: [AQA Theory and Methods study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-sociology-7192-theory-and-methods/).

## Specification coverage

Eleven points (3.1.3 = 3.3.2): methods and research design; sources of data; primary/secondary and
quantitative/qualitative data; positivism, interpretivism and social facts; theoretical, practical
and ethical considerations; consensus, conflict, structural and social action theories; modernity and
post-modernity; sociology as a science; theory and methods; subjectivity, objectivity and value
freedom; sociology and social policy.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Social facts** | Ways of acting, thinking and feeling external to the individual that constrain them (Durkheim) |
| **Positivism** | Sociology should use scientific, quantitative methods to find causal laws |
| **Interpretivism** | Sociology should understand meanings using qualitative methods |
| **Verstehen** | Empathetic understanding (Weber) |
| **Reliability / validity** | Replicability / truthfulness of data |
| **Operationalisation** | Turning concepts into measurable indicators |
| **Triangulation** | Using several methods to cross-check findings |
| **Methodological pluralism** | Combining methods to build a fuller picture |
| **Meta-narrative** | A grand theory claiming to explain all of society (Lyotard) |
| **Reflexivity** | Constant self-monitoring of individuals and institutions in late modernity (Giddens) |
| **Falsification** | Testing theories by trying to prove them false (Popper) |
| **Paradigm** | Shared framework of assumptions in a science (Kuhn) |
| **Open / closed systems** | Systems where variables cannot / can be controlled (realists) |
| **Value freedom** | Keeping personal values out of research |
| **Committed sociology** | Openly taking sides in research (Becker, Gouldner) |

## Methods and sources — quick evaluation

- **Questionnaires:** reliable, representative, quick to analyse — but low validity and response.
- **Structured interviews:** standardised — interviewer bias, shallow.
- **Unstructured interviews:** valid, rapport — costly, unrepresentative, hard to replicate.
- **Participant observation (overt/covert):** natural behaviour, verstehen — access, ethics, going
  native.
- **Experiments (lab/field):** cause and effect — artificial, ethical problems.
- **Documents:** Scott's four criteria (1990): authenticity, credibility, representativeness, meaning.
- **Official statistics:** cheap, large-scale trends — socially constructed (Atkinson, 1978).

## Positivism versus interpretivism

| | Positivism | Interpretivism |
|---|---|---|
| Society | Objective social facts | Constructed through meanings |
| Aim | Causal laws | Understanding (verstehen) |
| Methods | Quantitative, reliable | Qualitative, valid |
| Classic study | Durkheim, *Suicide* (1897) | Douglas (1967); Atkinson (1978) on suicide |

## Theories at a glance

| Theory | Structure or action? | Consensus or conflict? | Key names |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functionalism | Structure | Consensus | Durkheim, Parsons, Merton |
| Marxism | Structure | Conflict | Marx, Althusser, Gramsci |
| Feminism | Mostly structure | Conflict | Oakley, Walby (1990) |
| Interactionism | Action | — | Mead, Blumer (1969) |
| Ethnomethodology | Action | — | Garfinkel (1967) |
| Structuration | Both | — | Giddens (1984) |
| Postmodernism | Neither — rejects grand theory | — | Lyotard (1979), Baudrillard |

## Modernity, post-modernity and late modernity

- **Modernity:** nation state, industrial capitalism, rationality, science, progress.
- **Post-modernity:** fragmentation, media saturation, hyperreality, decline of meta-narratives
  (Lyotard, Baudrillard).
- **Late modernity:** Giddens — reflexivity; Beck — risk society (1986).

## Is sociology a science?

| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| Positivist | Yes — use the logic of natural science |
| Popper | Science = falsification; some theories (e.g. Marxism) unfalsifiable |
| Kuhn (1962) | Sociology is pre-paradigmatic |
| Realists (Keat and Urry, 1975; Sayer) | Science studies underlying causes in open systems too |
| Interpretivist | Human meaning makes natural-science methods inappropriate |

## Values and policy

- **Value freedom:** Comte and Durkheim (objective science); Weber (values in choice of topic, not
  in data collection); Becker (1967) and Gouldner (1962) (committed sociology); feminists (research
  for women).
- **Social policy:** positivist/functionalist "piecemeal social engineering"; social democratic —
  Townsend (1979), Black Report (1980); Marxist — policy serves capitalism; feminist — equal pay and
  domestic violence policy; New Right — Murray on dependency; postmodernist scepticism. Constraints:
  funding, ideology, electoral politics.

## Worked example: 10-mark "outline and explain two" plan

*Outline and explain two reasons why interpretivists reject the use of official statistics.*
(practice)

1. **Socially constructed** — statistics are the outcome of decisions by victims, police, coroners
   and officials; Atkinson (1978) showed coroners using common-sense theories about suicide, so the
   statistics tell us about the decision-makers, not the reality. Develop: this undermines validity.
2. **No access to meaning** — statistics count acts but cannot reveal the meanings people attach to
   them; verstehen requires qualitative methods. Develop: Douglas (1967) argued suicide has different
   meanings in different contexts, which numbers hide.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Confusing **structural/social action** (the level of analysis) with **consensus/conflict** (the view
  of society).
- Describing methods without linking them to **theory** (point 9).
- Treating postmodernism as just "anything goes" — explain the rejection of meta-narratives.
- Writing about Popper and Kuhn without applying them to sociology.
- Forgetting **social policy** — it is a full specification point.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a social fact.
2. Give Scott's four criteria for documents.
3. What does Kuhn mean by "pre-paradigmatic"?
4. Distinguish Weber's view of values from Becker's.
5. Name one study intended to influence social policy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there any difference between 3.1.3 and 3.3.2?** No — the content lists are identical; only the
marks available differ between Paper 1 and Paper 3.

**Which theories must I know?** The specification names consensus, conflict, structural and social
action theories; functionalism, Marxism, feminism and interactionism cover these, plus postmodern and
late-modern theory for point 7.

**How do I evaluate a theory?** Use other theories' criticisms, empirical evidence and the
structure–action debate.
