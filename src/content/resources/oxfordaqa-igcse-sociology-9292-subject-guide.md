OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology (9292) is a new course, taught
for the first time from September 2026, that introduces students to the
sociological study of everyday life: families, schools, inequality, and
the processes by which people learn to fit into, or resist, the society
around them. It is a linear qualification with two written papers, each
lasting 90 minutes, each marked out of 60 and each worth 50%, and no
coursework. The first exams are in May/June 2028, so students starting
now are the first cohort on this specification.

## How the course is organised

The specification divides the content between two papers, and research
methods is shared by both.

**Paper 1, Introducing Sociology**

- **Families**: how families are organised and how family life varies
  and changes.
- **Education**: what schools do for individuals and for society, and
  how experiences of education differ.
- **Research methods**: how sociologists collect and analyse evidence
  about the social world.

**Paper 2, Exploring Sociology**

- **Differences and inequalities**: how social groups differ in their
  life chances and why.
- **Socialisation and social control**: how people learn the norms and
  values of their society, and how behaviour is regulated.
- **Research methods**: the same methods content, also assessed in Paper
  1, now applied to the Paper 2 topics.

A simple way to picture the course: Paper 1 introduces the discipline
through two key social institutions, and Paper 2 explores how society
shapes and sorts the individuals within it.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Content | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Families; Education; Research Methods | 90 minutes | 60 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Differences and Inequalities; Socialisation and Social Control; Research Methods | 90 minutes | 60 | 50% |

The papers are written exams set and marked by the board, with no
tiers: all candidates answer the same questions. The qualification is
linear, so both are taken in the same series at the end of the course.

Because the specification is new, no exam has yet been sat on it, and
there are no past papers from earlier series. This page follows Version
1.0 of the specification.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers are closely connected, and seeing the links early makes
both easier:

- **Research methods is the common thread.** It is assessed in both
  papers, so the methods you learn for studying families or education
  are the same ones you use to discuss research on inequality or
  socialisation.
- **Families and education are agents of socialisation.** The Paper 1
  institutions are among the main places where the socialisation and
  social control covered in Paper 2 actually happen.
- **Inequality runs through Paper 1 topics.** Differences in family life
  and in educational experience are examples of the differences and
  inequalities studied in Paper 2.
- **Social control appears in schools and homes.** Rules, rewards and
  sanctions in families and education are concrete cases of the wider
  idea of social control.

The shared skills are using sociological terms precisely, applying them
to examples and evidence, and weighing up different explanations of the
same social pattern.

## Being the first cohort

Starting a specification in its opening year has practical
consequences. Teachers and students are working from the specification
itself rather than from a bank of earlier papers and examiners'
comments, so it is worth reading the content lists for both papers
closely and keeping them beside your notes. Ask your teacher to show you
how each topic heading is worded, since the wording signals what you may
be asked to explain. Treat early practice answers as a way of finding
out what a good response looks like, and keep a folder of marked work so
you can see how your writing improves before the first series in
May/June 2028.

## Planning your study

A sensible order starts with research methods basics, so that every
topic afterwards can be discussed in terms of evidence:

- **First year:** research methods, then families and education. With
  each topic, ask how a sociologist would find out about it, and note the
  strengths and weaknesses of the method you choose.
- **Second year, first part:** differences and inequalities, then
  socialisation and social control, linking back to examples from
  families and education.
- **Second year, final months:** revise both papers together, ahead of
  the first exam series in May/June 2028, and give research methods a
  full review since it appears in both.

As there are no past papers yet, build practice around teacher-set
questions and any sample material your school provides, starting as
soon as the first topic is taught. The course's exam preparation page
covers how to approach the two papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is OxfordAQA IGCSE Sociology 9292 first examined?**
First teaching began in September 2026, and the first exams are in May/June 2028.

**How many papers are there?**
Two. Paper 1 covers families, education and research methods; Paper 2 covers differences and inequalities, socialisation and social control, and research methods. Each lasts 90 minutes, is marked out of 60 and is worth 50%.

**Is research methods examined in both papers?**
Yes. Research methods is part of Paper 1 and is also assessed in Paper 2, so it needs to be secure for both.

**Is there coursework in OxfordAQA IGCSE Sociology?**
No. The qualification is linear with no coursework, and the grade comes from the two written papers.

**Are there past papers for 9292?**
Not yet. No exam has been sat on this specification, so use teacher-set questions and any sample material your school provides.
