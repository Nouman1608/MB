This set covers one content point from key development 3.1.1.2: "the Khilafat movement and its
importance for Muslim-Hindu relations", with "non-cooperation and the growth of communalism
1920-27". The tariffs (1, 2, 4, 6 and 9 marks) follow the pattern used in the
[Pakistan movement practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-pakistan-movement-practice/),
which you should also attempt; none of the questions here repeats those. Learn the content first from
the [Khilafat movement study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-khilafat-movement/).

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** Which treaty of 1920 divided much of the Ottoman Empire's remaining territory? **[1]**

A: Treaty of Versailles &nbsp; B: Treaty of Sèvres &nbsp; C: Treaty of Lausanne &nbsp; D: Treaty of Paris

**2.** To which country did Muslims migrate during the Hijrat of 1920? **[1]**

A: Persia &nbsp; B: Turkey &nbsp; C: Afghanistan &nbsp; D: Arabia

**3.** In which year was the caliphate abolished? **[1]**

A: 1920 &nbsp; B: 1922 &nbsp; C: 1924 &nbsp; D: 1927

**4.** Which leader called off the Non-Cooperation movement in February 1922? **[1]**

A: Muhammad Ali Jauhar &nbsp; B: Gandhi &nbsp; C: Jinnah &nbsp; D: Nehru

**5.** State **two** reasons why Indian Muslims were angered by the post-war treatment of the Ottoman
Empire. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** consequences of the Hijrat movement for those who took part. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** methods used in the Non-Cooperation movement. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why Gandhi supported the Khilafat movement. **[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why the Moplah uprising (1921) was important for
Muslim-Hindu relations. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** ways in which the Khilafat movement increased the political awareness of
Muslims in the subcontinent. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** reasons why relations between Muslims and Hindus worsened between 1922 and
1927. **[6]**

**12.** "The abolition of the caliphate in 1924 was the main reason for the failure of the Khilafat
movement."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer using the statement above and your
contextual knowledge. **[9]**

**13.** Which was more important in damaging Muslim-Hindu unity in the 1920s?

- the calling-off of Non-Cooperation after Chauri Chaura (1922)
- the growth of communal movements such as *shuddhi* and *sangathan*

Explain your answer with reference to both bullet points. **[9]**

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 mark for each point identified and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and
9-mark questions are marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other
accurate points.

**1.** **B: Treaty of Sèvres** [1]. The Treaty of Lausanne (1923) later replaced it. **[1]**

**2.** **C: Afghanistan** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** **C: 1924** [1], by Mustafa Kemal's Turkish government. The sultanate had been abolished in
1922. **[1]**

**4.** **B: Gandhi** [1], after the violence at Chauri Chaura. **[1]**

**5.** Two reasons, 1 mark each: Indian Muslims had fought for Britain and expected fair treatment of
the Ottoman Empire [1]; the holy places passed out of Ottoman control [1]. Also credit: Allied forces
occupied Istanbul; the empire was divided, threatening the Khalifa's position. **[2]**

**6.** Two consequences, 1 mark each: many migrants had sold their land and property and lost
everything [1]; Afghanistan closed the border, so many were turned back or died on the journey [1].
Also credit: the failure discredited some leaders and weakened the wider movement. **[2]**

**7.** Two methods, 1 mark each: returning British titles and honours [1]; boycotting British goods
in favour of home-spun cloth [1]. Also credit: boycotts of government schools, courts or council
elections. **[2]**

**8.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- he saw it as a way to unite Hindus and Muslims [1], since a joint campaign would make opposition to
  British rule far stronger [1];
- he believed the British had broken promises and treated Muslims unjustly [1], so supporting their
  cause fitted his campaign against injustice under British rule [1].

Also credit: Muslim support would turn Non-Cooperation into a mass, all-India movement. **[4]**

**9.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Moplah peasants attacked Hindus as well as British authority [1], so reports of the violence spread
  fear and distrust among Hindus across India [1];
- it raised doubts about whether a joint Hindu-Muslim campaign could remain non-violent [1], which
  weakened support for the alliance shortly before Chauri Chaura [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Mass participation:* boycotts, meetings and protests involved ordinary Muslims, including in
  villages, in national politics for the first time.
- *The ulema in politics:* religious scholars became political leaders and organisers, linking
  religion to political action.
- *Organisation:* the Khilafat Committee built a network of branches, fund-raising and publicity that
  gave Muslims experience of running a campaign.
- *Sacrifice and arrest:* the imprisonment of the Ali brothers made them heroes and showed that
  political action carried risks worth taking. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Sense of betrayal:* Gandhi called off Non-Cooperation in 1922 without consulting Khilafat leaders,
  and many Muslims concluded that Congress would not stand by them.
- *Rival religious movements:* *shuddhi* sought to reconvert Muslims to Hinduism and *sangathan*
  organised Hindus for self-defence; Muslim *tabligh* and *tanzim* responded, and each side saw the
  other as a threat.
- *Communal riots:* violence increased in several towns during the mid-1920s, deepening fear.
- *Loss of a shared cause:* once the caliphate was abolished in 1924, the issue that had united the
  communities disappeared. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the movement's central aim was to preserve the Khalifa; when the Turkish government
  abolished the office in March 1924, there was nothing left to campaign for, and the committee lost
  its purpose.
- *Disagree:* the movement had already lost momentum before 1924: the Hijrat ended in disaster in
  1920, the Moplah violence divided supporters in 1921, Gandhi withdrew Non-Cooperation in 1922, and
  leaders were imprisoned.
- *Judgement:* for example, the abolition was the final and decisive blow, but the movement was
  already weakened, so it explains the timing of the end rather than the whole failure. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Answers must address both bullet points. Indicative
content:
- *Calling-off of Non-Cooperation:* it ended the joint campaign abruptly; Khilafat leaders had not
  been consulted, so Muslims felt used and distrusted Congress leadership.
- *Shuddhi and sangathan:* they turned rivalry into open competition between religious communities,
  provoked Muslim counter-movements and contributed to riots through the mid-1920s.
- *Judgement:* for example, the calling-off was the turning point that broke trust, but the communal
  movements made the damage lasting by organising each community against the other. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying Britain abolished the caliphate.
- Treating the Hijrat as unconnected to the Khilafat movement.
- Describing the alliance without explaining why it broke down.
- In 9-mark answers, writing only about the factor in the statement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the Treaty of Sèvres in detail?**
No. Know that the post-war settlement divided Ottoman lands and removed the holy places from Ottoman
control, because that explains Muslim anger.

**Is the Khilafat movement part of Section A or Section C?**
Section A: it belongs to the compulsory Pakistan movement topic.
