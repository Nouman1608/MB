## Questions

**1.** Define returns to scale and state whether it is a short-run or a long-run concept. **[2]**

**2.** A ceramics workshop adds identical bundles of all inputs. Output is 60, 150, 225 and 270 units with 1, 2, 3 and 4 bundles. Each bundle costs 900 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in inputs and output at each step and state the returns to scale. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate long-run average cost at each scale and comment on the pattern. **[3]**

**3.** A mill's long-run average cost per thousand metres is 8.0, 6.4, 5.6, 5.2, 5.2 and 5.6 cu at outputs of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 thousand metres.

**(a)** State the minimum efficient scale. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in LRAC from 5 to 20. **[2]**
**(c)** Market demand is 130 thousand metres. Calculate the greatest number of firms able to reach minimum efficient scale. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain what happens to average cost between 25 and 30. **[2]**

**4.** A juice maker spends 90,000 cu a year on promotion.

**(a)** Calculate promotion cost per unit at 30,000 units and at 90,000 units. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this is an internal economy of scale and how it appears on an LRAC diagram. **[3]**

**5.** Many electronics assembly firms locate in one district.

**(a)** Explain two external economies of scale they might gain. **[4]**
**(b)** A firm's LRAC at 8,000 units is 4.40 cu. Input costs fall 5% because of the district. Calculate the new LRAC and describe the effect on the curve. **[3]**

**6.** A firm's LRAC rises from 14.00 cu at 50,000 units to 15.40 cu at 70,000 units.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise in LRAC. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate long-run total cost at both outputs and the extra cost. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain two internal causes of the rise. **[4]**

**7.** A firm faces these prices: 50, 45, 40, 35, 30, 25 and 20 cu when it sells 1 to 7 units.

**(a)** Calculate TR and MR at each output. **[4]**
**(b)** State the output at which TR is greatest. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the link between MR and price elasticity of demand. **[3]**

**8.** A farm is a price taker at 14 cu per unit and raises sales from 300 to 420 units.

**(a)** Calculate TR at each output and the MR of the extra units. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why AR equals MR and describe the farm's demand curve. **[2]**

**9.** A joinery sells 4,000 items for 76,000 cu. Explicit costs are 52,000 cu. The owner's best alternative earns 18,000 cu a year (normal profit).

**(a)** Calculate accounting profit. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate supernormal profit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate AR and ATC (including normal profit) and check (b) per unit. **[2]**
**(d)** Next year TR is 66,000 cu on the same costs. State the type and size of profit. **[2]**

**10.** Demand for a firm's product is P = 64 − 4Q.

**(a)** Derive TR and MR as expressions in Q. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the output where MR is zero, and TR at that output. **[3]**
**(c)** At Q = 6, ATC is 30 cu. Calculate supernormal profit. **[2]**

**11.** A firm doubles output from 20,000 to 40,000 units. LRAC falls from 18.00 to 14.40 cu. The price stays at 20 cu.

**(a)** Calculate supernormal profit before and after. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether consumers will benefit from the lower cost. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Returns to scale are the change in output [1] when all inputs rise in the same proportion; a long-run concept [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say "all inputs" and "long run" separately tend to be clearer than those that only mention bigger output.

**2. (a)** 1 to 2 bundles: inputs +100%, output (150 − 60)/60 = +150%, increasing [1]. 2 to 3: inputs +50%, output (225 − 150)/150 = +50%, constant [1]. 3 to 4: inputs +33.3%, output (270 − 225)/225 = +20%, decreasing [1].
**(b)** LRAC = 900, 1,800, 2,700 and 3,600 divided by output: 15.00, 12.00, 12.00 and 13.33 [2]. Average cost falls, stays flat, then rises, matching increasing, constant and decreasing returns [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each division. A pattern comment that links returns to cost direction is what shows understanding of the link.

**3. (a)** 20 thousand metres, the first output with the minimum LRAC [1].
**(b)** (5.2 − 8.0)/8.0 × 100 [1] = **−35.0%** [1].
**(c)** 130/20 = 6.5 [1], so **6 firms** [1].
**(d)** LRAC rises from 5.2 to 5.6 cu [1], diseconomies of scale such as coordination problems [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 6.5 up to 7 is an easy slip, because a seventh firm could not reach minimum efficient scale.

**4. (a)** 90,000/30,000 = **3.00 cu** [1]; 90,000/90,000 = **1.00 cu** [1].
**(b)** It arises from the firm's own growth [1], spreading a fixed promotion cost over more units [1]. The firm moves down along its LRAC curve rather than the curve shifting [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State both the cause and the movement along the curve.

**5. (a)** Any two, each with a reason: a pool of skilled workers [1], so recruitment and training costs fall [1]; specialist component suppliers nearby [1], so inputs are cheaper [1].
**(b)** 4.40 × 0.95 = **4.18 cu** [1]. External economies lower costs for every firm [1], so the whole LRAC curve shifts down [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Each economy needs a cause and a cost effect. A list of names earns less.

**6. (a)** (15.40 − 14.00)/14.00 × 100 [1] = **10.0%** [1].
**(b)** 50,000 × 14.00 = 700,000 cu and 70,000 × 15.40 = 1,078,000 cu [2]; extra cost **378,000 cu** [1].
**(c)** Any two with reasons: long chains of command slow communication [1], so decisions cost more [1]; workers in a big firm feel less motivated [1], so output per worker falls [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is easier if you keep average cost and total cost separate, and give units with each answer.

**7. (a)** TR = 50, 90, 120, 140, 150, 150, 140 [2]. MR = 50, 40, 30, 20, 10, 0, −10 [2].
**(b)** 5 or 6 units, where TR is 150 [1].
**(c)** While MR is positive, a price cut raises TR, so demand is elastic [1]. At MR = 0, elasticity is one [1]. When MR is negative, a price cut lowers TR, so demand is inelastic [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate MR as the change in TR, never TR divided by quantity, which gives price.

**8. (a)** TR = 14 × 300 = **4,200 cu** [1]; 14 × 420 = **5,880 cu** [1]; MR = 1,680/120 = **14 cu** [1].
**(b)** The farm sells every extra unit at the same price, so MR equals AR [1]. Its demand curve is a horizontal line at 14 cu [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A price taker's MR needs the working: the change in TR over the change in output.

**9. (a)** 76,000 − 52,000 = **24,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** 24,000 − 18,000 [1] = **6,000 cu** supernormal [1].
**(c)** AR = 76,000/4,000 = 19.00; ATC = (52,000 + 18,000)/4,000 = 17.50 [1]; (19.00 − 17.50) × 4,000 = **6,000 cu** [1].
**(d)** Accounting profit is 66,000 − 52,000 = 14,000 cu [1], 4,000 cu below normal profit, so **subnormal profit of −4,000 cu** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Positive accounting profit can still be subnormal. Say "below normal profit" explicitly.

**10. (a)** TR = P × Q = 64Q − 4Q² [1]. MR has the same intercept and twice the slope [1]: MR = 64 − 8Q [1].
**(b)** 64 − 8Q = 0 gives **Q = 8** [1]; P = 64 − 32 = 32 [1]; TR = 32 × 8 = **256 cu** [1].
**(c)** P = 64 − 24 = 40 [1]; (40 − 30) × 6 = **60 cu** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that you substituted Q into the demand equation for price before using ATC.

**11. (a)** Before: (20 − 18) × 20,000 = **40,000 cu** [2]. After: (20 − 14.40) × 40,000 = **224,000 cu** [2].
**(b)** The LRAC fell by 20% [1]. Supernormal profit rose by 184,000 cu, so the firm gains if the price stays at 20 cu [1]. Consumers benefit only if competition pushes the price down [2], whereas a firm with market power may keep the saving [1]. A judgement: benefit depends on whether rivals or new entrants exist and the size of the market relative to minimum efficient scale [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation earns credit when it reaches a conditional judgement and not a list of points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding 6.5 firms up to 7 in a minimum efficient scale question.
- Describing returns to scale without comparing percentage changes in inputs and output.
- Treating a fall in average cost as a fall in total cost.
- Showing an internal economy as a shift of the LRAC, or an external economy as a movement along it.
- Dividing TR by quantity and calling it MR.
- Calling accounting profit supernormal without deducting normal profit.
- Not stating "below normal profit" when the answer is subnormal.
- Giving a list of economies with no mechanism for lower cost per unit.
