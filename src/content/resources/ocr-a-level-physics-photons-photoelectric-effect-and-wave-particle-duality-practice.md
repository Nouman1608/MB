These questions cover section 4.5 (Quantum physics: photons, the photoelectric effect and wave-particle duality) of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg and h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-photons-photoelectric-effect-and-wave-particle-duality/) or [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-photons-photoelectric-effect-and-wave-particle-duality-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** (a) Define the electronvolt. **[1]**
**(b)** Convert a photon energy of 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J to eV. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the kinetic energy, in J, gained by an electron accelerated from rest through 450 V. **[1]**

**2.** A photon has energy 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Calculate its frequency and its wavelength. **[3]**

**3.** Calculate the energy, in eV, of a photon of ultraviolet radiation of wavelength 280 nm. **[3]**

**4.** A laser pointer emits 5.0 mW of light of wavelength 650 nm. Calculate the number of photons emitted per second. **[3]**

**5.** A red LED just begins to emit light when the potential difference across it is 1.9 V. Its wavelength is 655 nm. Use eV = hc/λ to estimate the Planck constant. **[3]**

**6.** Describe how you would use a set of LEDs of different colours to determine the Planck constant, including how you would analyse the results. **[5]**

**7.** Sodium has a work function of 2.36 eV.

**(a)** Calculate its threshold frequency. **[2]**
**(b)** Light of wavelength 450 nm falls on a clean sodium surface. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons, in eV. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why light of wavelength 650 nm releases no electrons, however bright it is. **[1]**

**8.** Explain three observations about the photoelectric effect that the wave model of light cannot explain, and how the photon model accounts for each. **[6]**

**9.** The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons from a metal was measured at four frequencies.

| f / 10¹⁴ Hz | 7.5 | 8.5 | 9.5 | 10.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KEmax / eV | 0.51 | 0.92 | 1.34 | 1.75 |

**(a)** Using hf = φ + KEmax, explain why a graph of KEmax against f is a straight line, and state what its gradient and its intercept on the KEmax axis represent. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the data to determine the Planck constant in J s. **[3]**
**(c)** Determine the work function of the metal in eV. **[2]**

**10.** Describe a demonstration of the photoelectric effect using a gold-leaf electroscope and a zinc plate. Include the preparation of the plate, what you would see, and what each observation shows. **[5]**

**11.** Light of wavelength 520 nm falls on a caesium surface of work function 2.10 eV.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons, in J. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate their maximum speed. **[2]**
**(c)** The intensity of the light is tripled. State and explain the effect on the maximum kinetic energy and on the photoelectric current. **[2]**

**12.** In an electron diffraction tube, electrons are accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 150 V and pass through a thin slice of polycrystalline graphite onto a fluorescent screen.

**(a)** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the pattern seen on the screen and explain how it provides evidence for the wave nature of electrons. **[3]**
**(c)** The potential difference is increased to 600 V. State the new wavelength and the effect on the pattern. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The energy an electron gains when accelerated across a potential difference of 1 V [1] **[1]**
**(b)** 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **2.25 eV** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** E = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 450 = **7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ J** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the unit with each answer; a bare number in a mixed eV and J question is easy to misread.

**2.** f = E/h = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ [1] = **4.8 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]. λ = c/f = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 4.83 × 10¹⁴ = **6.2 × 10⁻⁷ m** (620 nm) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values into the second part; rounding f to 4.8 first changes the third significant figure of λ.

**3.** E = hc/λ [1] = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 280 × 10⁻⁹ = 7.10 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. In eV: 7.10 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4.44 eV** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert nanometres to metres at the start; a 10⁹ slip is the most common cause of a wrong answer here.

**4.** Photon energy = hc/λ = 3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. N = P/E = 5.0 × 10⁻³ / 3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = **1.6 × 10¹⁶ s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the energy of one photon as its own step. Power divided by a wavelength is a common wrong route.

**5.** h = eVλ/c [1] = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.9 × 655 × 10⁻⁹ / 3.00 × 10⁸ [1] = **6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ J s** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before you substitute, and give the unit J s with the answer.

**6.** Connect an LED (correct polarity) in series with a protective resistor and a variable supply, with a voltmeter across the LED [1]. Shield from stray light and raise the potential difference slowly until the LED just glows; record V [1]. Repeat for LEDs of different colours, taking λ from the supplier's data, and repeat each reading to average [1]. Plot V against 1/λ and draw a best-fit line [1]. The gradient is hc/e, so h = gradient × e/c [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State what is plotted and what the gradient equals; "plot a graph" alone leaves out the link to the equation.

**7. (a)** f₀ = φ/h = 2.36 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ [1] = **5.7 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Photon energy = hc/λ = 4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1] = 2.76 eV [1]. KEmax = 2.76 − 2.36 = **0.40 eV** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** A 650 nm photon has energy 1.91 eV, which is less than the work function, and one photon interacts with one electron, so no electron can escape [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), talk about the energy of a single photon. Referring to "the light" or its brightness does not explain the absence of emission.

**8.** (i) No emission below the threshold frequency, however intense the light [1]. A wave model would predict that enough intensity delivers enough energy; the photon model says each photon must carry at least φ [1]. (ii) Emission is immediate even at low intensity [1]. The wave model predicts a delay while energy builds up; in the photon model one photon gives its energy to one electron at once [1]. (iii) KEmax is independent of intensity but increases with frequency [1]. The wave model predicts more intense light gives more energetic electrons; photon energy is hf and intensity only changes the number of photons [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each observation needs both halves: what is seen, and why the wave model fails or the photon model works.

**9. (a)** KEmax = hf − φ has the form y = mx + c [1]. The gradient is h; the intercept on the KEmax axis is −φ [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Gradient = (1.75 − 0.51) / (3.0 × 10¹⁴) [1] = 4.13 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV s [1]. h = 4.13 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ J s** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** φ = hf − KEmax = 4.13 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 7.5 × 10¹⁴ − 0.51 [1] = **2.6 eV** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the gradient from points far apart on the line, and convert eV to J before quoting h.

**10.** Clean the zinc plate with emery paper and fix it to the electroscope; charge it negatively so the leaf rises [1]. Shine ultraviolet light on it and the leaf falls [1]: electrons are emitted from the zinc, so the charge is lost [1]. A bright visible lamp does not make the leaf fall, so there is a threshold frequency [1]. If the plate is charged positively the leaf does not fall, because emitted electrons are attracted back [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the plate is negative and what is leaving it; "the leaf falls" without a cause earns little.

**11. (a)** Photon energy = hc/λ = 3.83 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. φ = 2.10 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.36 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. KEmax = **4.7 × 10⁻²⁰ J** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** ½mv² = 4.65 × 10⁻²⁰ [1]. v = √(2 × 4.65 × 10⁻²⁰ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **3.2 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** KEmax is unchanged because photon energy depends only on frequency [1]. The photoelectric current triples because three times as many photons per second release three times as many electrons per second [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Put both quantities in the answer, and give the reason in terms of photons.

**12. (a)** eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 150 = 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁷ J, and p = √(2m × eV) [1] = 6.6 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹ [1]. λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / 6.61 × 10⁻²⁴ = **1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** A set of concentric rings appears [1]. The atoms in the graphite, and the spacing between them, diffract the electrons, and diffraction and interference are wave properties [1]. The pattern is only possible if the electrons behave as waves [1] **[3]**
**(c)** p ∝ √V, so four times V doubles p and halves λ to **5.0 × 10⁻¹¹ m** [1]. The rings become smaller in radius, closer to the centre [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the observation (rings) to the property (diffraction) and then to the conclusion (wave behaviour).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using wavelength in nanometres without converting to metres.
- Mixing eV and J in one equation.
- Answering "why no emission" with the brightness of the beam rather than the energy of one photon.
- Saying more intense light gives faster electrons.
- Describing the electroscope without cleaning the plate or stating the charge on it.
- Quoting h in eV s or without a unit.
- Using p = mv for electrons that were accelerated, when the voltage is given and p = √(2meV) is the route.
- Stating that rings "prove" waves without naming diffraction by the graphite.
