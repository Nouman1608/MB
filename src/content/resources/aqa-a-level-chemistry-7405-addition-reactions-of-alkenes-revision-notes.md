These notes condense section **3.3.4.2 Addition reactions of alkenes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Addition reactions of alkenes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-reactions-of-alkenes/), and exam-style questions are in the [Addition reactions of alkenes practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-reactions-of-alkenes-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [Structure, bonding and reactivity revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-structure-bonding-and-reactivity-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Outline the **mechanisms** of electrophilic addition of **HBr, H₂SO₄ and Br₂** to alkenes.
- Describe the **bromine water test** for unsaturation.
- Explain **major and minor products** from unsymmetrical alkenes using the stability of **primary, secondary and tertiary carbocations**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrophile | Electron-pair acceptor |
| Electrophilic addition | An electrophile adds across a C=C bond; the pi bond is lost |
| Carbocation | Ion with a positively charged carbon atom |
| Inductive effect | Alkyl groups donate electron density towards an adjacent positive carbon |
| Unsymmetrical alkene | Different groups on the two carbons of the double bond |
| Major product | The product formed in larger amount |
| Induced dipole | A dipole created in a non-polar molecule by a nearby charge |

## Reagents, conditions and products

| Reagent | Conditions | Product from ethene | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBr | Room temperature | CH₃CH₂Br | Bromoethane |
| H₂SO₄ | Concentrated, cold | CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH | Ethyl hydrogensulfate |
| Br₂ | Room temperature | CH₂BrCH₂Br | 1,2-dibromoethane |

Hydrolysis of the hydrogensulfate: CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH + H₂SO₄ (warm with water).

## Mechanism for HBr

1. Draw the polar H–Br with Hδ⁺ and Brδ⁻.
2. Arrow 1: from the **C=C bond** to the **H** of H–Br.
3. Arrow 2: from the **H–Br bond** to **Br**.
4. Show the **carbocation** (positive charge on the carbon without the new H) and **Br⁻**.
5. Arrow 3: from a **lone pair on Br⁻** to the carbocation C⁺.
6. Draw the final bromoalkane.

## Mechanism for Br₂

- Bromine is non-polar but a dipole is **induced** by the approaching double bond.
- Arrow 1: C=C to the nearer Br (δ+).
- Arrow 2: the Br–Br bond to the farther Br.
- Arrow 3: lone pair on Br⁻ to C⁺.

## Mechanism for H₂SO₄

- The electrophile is the H of an O–H bond.
- Arrow 1: C=C to H; arrow 2: O–H bond to O, giving HSO₄⁻.
- Arrow 3: lone pair on an O of HSO₄⁻ to C⁺.

## Test for unsaturation

| | Alkene | Alkane |
|---|---|---|
| Add bromine water | Orange to **colourless** | Stays orange (in the dark) |

## Major and minor products

Propene + HBr:

| Route | Carbocation | Product | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| H adds to CH₂ | Secondary, CH₃CH⁺CH₃ | 2-bromopropane | Major |
| H adds to CH | Primary, CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺ | 1-bromopropane | Minor |

**Stability: tertiary > secondary > primary.**

Why: alkyl groups donate electron density to the positive carbon (inductive effect), spreading the charge and stabilising the ion. The more stable ion forms more quickly, so its product is the major one.

Examples:

| Alkene + HBr | Major | Minor |
|---|---|---|
| Propene | 2-bromopropane | 1-bromopropane |
| But-1-ene | 2-bromobutane | 1-bromobutane |
| 2-methylpropene | 2-bromo-2-methylpropane | 1-bromo-2-methylpropane |
| 2-methylbut-2-ene | 2-bromo-2-methylbutane | 2-bromo-3-methylbutane |
| But-2-ene | 2-bromobutane only (symmetrical) | None |

## Symmetrical or not?

Compare the groups on the two ends of the double bond. If they match, only one product forms. If they differ, expect a major and a minor product.

## Calculations

- Addition is 1 : 1 with HBr and Br₂, so moles of product equal moles of alkene.
- Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100.

Example: 0.0500 mol of propene gives at most 0.0500 mol of 2-bromopropane (Mᵣ 122.9), which is 0.0500 × 122.9 = 6.145 g, or 6.15 g to three significant figures.

## Writing it properly

- Show full charges on ions (Br⁻, C⁺).
- Place the positive charge on the correct carbon when naming the major product.
- Start arrows at a bond or lone pair, never at an atom or charge alone.
- Name the intermediate type (primary, secondary or tertiary) in explanations.

## Exam traps

- The first arrow drawn from the reagent to the alkene.
- Placing the positive charge on the wrong carbon.
- Forgetting the induced dipole on Br₂.
- Explaining the major product by "Markovnikov's rule" alone, with no reference to carbocation stability.
- "Clear" instead of "colourless" for the bromine water result.
- No hydrolysis step when converting an alkyl hydrogensulfate into an alcohol.
- Giving two products for a symmetrical alkene.
- Not stating which product is major.

## Self-test

1. What is an electrophile?
2. Why does an alkene react with an electrophile?
3. Write an equation for the reaction of ethene with HBr.
4. Why is bromine able to react with an alkene although it is non-polar?
5. What is seen when an alkene is shaken with bromine water?
6. Name the product of ethene with concentrated sulfuric acid.
7. How is this product converted into ethanol?
8. Why is the secondary carbocation more stable than the primary one?
9. Name the major product of propene with HBr.
10. Name the major product of 2-methylpropene with HBr.
11. Why does but-2-ene give only one product with HBr?
12. 0.250 mol of ethene reacts with excess bromine. What mass of 1,2-dibromoethane (Mᵣ = 187.8) is the maximum?

### Answers

1. A species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a covalent bond.
2. The C=C double bond is a region of high electron density.
3. CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CH₂Br.
4. The electrons in the C=C bond repel electrons in the Br–Br bond, inducing a dipole.
5. The orange colour disappears and the solution becomes colourless.
6. Ethyl hydrogensulfate, CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH.
7. Warm with water.
8. Two alkyl groups donate electron density to the positive carbon, spreading the charge.
9. 2-bromopropane.
10. 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
11. It is symmetrical, so H adding to either carbon gives the same carbocation.
12. 0.250 × 187.8 = 46.95 g, so about 47.0 g.
