**Excretion** is the removal of waste products of metabolism from the body. Metabolism means the chemical reactions in cells. Do not confuse excretion with **egestion**: faeces are mostly undigested food that never entered the body's cells, so passing faeces is egestion.

## 2.70 Excretion in flowering plants

Plants have no excretory organs. Their two main waste gases are made by the two processes you already know.

- **Carbon dioxide** is a waste product of **respiration**. Every living plant cell respires all the time, day and night.
- **Oxygen** is a waste product of **photosynthesis**. Only cells with chloroplasts make it, and only in the light.

Both gases leave the leaf by **diffusion** through the **stomata**, when their concentration in the leaf air spaces is higher than in the air outside.

What leaves the leaf depends on the balance of the two processes:

- **In the dark:** only respiration happens. Carbon dioxide builds up in the air spaces and diffuses out. Oxygen diffuses in.
- **In bright light:** if photosynthesis is faster than respiration, net carbon-dioxide uptake and net oxygen release occur. Some carbon dioxide produced by respiration may be used in photosynthesis, but the net model does not prove that no respiratory carbon-dioxide molecule diffuses out.

In bright light, respiration still produces carbon dioxide. The rates of both processes determine net exchange; a net uptake does not mean every respiratory carbon-dioxide molecule is reused inside the leaf.

### Worked example 1

*A plant is kept in a sealed, transparent container in bright light for 6 hours and then in darkness for 6 hours. Explain how the oxygen concentration in the container changes.*

1. In bright light, the rate of photosynthesis is greater than the rate of respiration.
2. So the leaf makes more oxygen than it uses. Oxygen is a waste product here and diffuses out through the stomata. The oxygen concentration rises.
3. In darkness, photosynthesis stops but respiration continues.
4. The leaf takes in oxygen through the stomata and gives out carbon dioxide from respiration. In this simplified sealed-container model, oxygen falls while respiration exceeds photosynthesis.

**Model assumption:** a continued oxygen rise during illumination assumes enough carbon dioxide, suitable light and temperature, and no leak or other limiting change.

## 2.71 Organs of excretion in humans

| Organ | Excretory products | Where the waste comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Lungs | Carbon dioxide (and water vapour) | Respiration in all cells |
| Kidneys | Urea, excess water, excess ions (salts), in urine | Urea is made in the liver from excess amino acids |
| Skin | Water, ions (salts) and a small amount of urea, in sweat | As above |

Urea is carried in the plasma (point 2.60) from the liver to the kidneys. Sweating mainly cools the body, but sweat still removes some waste, so the skin counts as an organ of excretion.

Follow a molecule of urea from the blood to the outside:

- The **renal artery** brings blood, including urea, to each **kidney**.
- Each kidney filters the blood and makes **urine**. Cleaned blood leaves in the **renal vein**.
- A **ureter** carries urine from each kidney to the **bladder**.
- The **bladder** stores urine. A ring of muscle (a sphincter) keeps it closed.
- The **urethra** carries urine from the bladder out of the body.

Ureter (two, kidney to bladder) and urethra (one, bladder to outside) are easy to swap; the wrong word is not credited.

Inside the kidney are an outer **cortex**, an inner **medulla** and the **pelvis**, where urine collects.

Each kidney contains a very large number of tiny tubules called **nephrons**. In order, filtrate passes through:

1. **Bowman's capsule** -- a cup-shaped end of the nephron in the cortex. It surrounds the glomerulus and collects the filtrate.
2. **Proximal convoluted tubule** (first coiled tubule) -- reabsorbs all the glucose, plus some water and ions, into the blood.
3. **Loop of Henle** -- dips down into the medulla. It builds up a high concentration of ions (salts) in the tissue of the medulla.
4. **Distal convoluted tubule** (second coiled tubule) -- adjusts the ions reabsorbed.
5. **Collecting duct** -- runs down through the medulla. Water is reabsorbed here, under the control of ADH. Collecting ducts empty urine into the pelvis.

The **glomerulus** is not part of the tubule itself. It is a knot of blood capillaries inside Bowman's capsule, supplied by a branch of the renal artery.

## 2.75B Ultrafiltration (Paper 2 only)

Ultrafiltration is filtration under **high pressure** in the glomerulus.

- The afferent arteriole taking blood **into** the glomerulus is usually wider than the efferent arteriole taking blood **out**. This contributes to high hydrostatic pressure in glomerular capillaries relative to the capsule; it is a simplified account of the pressure balance.
- Small molecules are forced out of the blood, through the capillary wall and the wall of Bowman's capsule, into the capsule.
- The liquid in the capsule is the **glomerular filtrate**.

**Composition of glomerular filtrate:** water, glucose, urea, ions (salts) and amino acids -- at about the same concentrations as in blood plasma.

**Normally mostly excluded from the filtrate:** blood cells and large plasma proteins are retained by the filtration barrier. Healthy glomerular filtrate has very little protein, rather than an absolute zero in every circumstance.

## 2.77B Selective reabsorption of glucose (Paper 2 only)

The filtrate contains useful substances as well as waste. Glucose is the key example.

- **Why** it is reabsorbed: glucose is needed by body cells for respiration. Losing it in urine would waste an energy source.
- **Where:** the **proximal convoluted tubule**. All of the glucose is normally reabsorbed here, so a healthy person's urine contains no glucose.
- **How:** partly by **active transport**, which moves glucose against its concentration gradient and uses energy from respiration. The tubule cells have many mitochondria to supply this energy, and microvilli that give a large surface area.

It is called **selective** because the tubule reabsorbs substances to different extents. In healthy kidneys at normal blood glucose levels, glucose is almost completely reabsorbed. Some urea is also reabsorbed and recycled, while the remainder is excreted in urine.

If the blood glucose concentration is very high (for example in untreated diabetes), there is more glucose in the filtrate than the tubule can reabsorb, and some glucose appears in the urine.

### Worked example 2

*The table gives example concentrations, in g per dm³, in one person.*

| Substance | Blood plasma | Glomerular filtrate | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | 80 | 0 | 0 |
| Glucose | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0 |
| Urea | 0.30 | 0.30 | 20 |

The zero protein value in this example is an idealised school-level value; normal filtrate contains very little protein.

*(a) Explain the figures for protein. (b) Explain the figures for glucose. (c) Calculate how many times more concentrated urea is in urine than in the filtrate, and explain the difference.*

(a) Protein molecules are too large to be forced out of the glomerulus, so there is none in the filtrate or the urine.

(b) Glucose is a small molecule, so it passes into the filtrate at the same concentration as in plasma. All of it is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule, so there is none in urine.

(c) 20 ÷ 0.30 = **66.7 times**. Most filtered water is reabsorbed, so the urea remaining for excretion can become much more concentrated. Some urea is also reabsorbed and recycled. The concentration ratio alone does not show that the mass of urea stayed constant.

### Water reabsorption from the collecting duct

- The loop of Henle gives the tissue of the medulla a high concentration of ions.
- The collecting duct carries filtrate down through this concentrated medulla.
- Water moves out of the collecting duct by **osmosis**, from the dilute filtrate into the concentrated tissue, and then into the blood capillaries.
- How much water leaves depends on how **permeable** the walls of the collecting duct are. ADH controls this.

### The role of ADH

**ADH** (antidiuretic hormone) is released by the **pituitary gland** in the brain into the blood. Its target is the collecting duct.

| Situation | Blood water content | ADH released | Collecting duct | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweating heavily, little to drink, salty meal | Too low | More | More permeable to water | Small volume, concentrated |
| Drinking a lot of water | Too high | Less | Less permeable to water | Large volume, dilute |

This is **negative feedback**: the response reverses the change. ADH also appears under co-ordination (points 2.81 and 2.95B).

### Worked example 3

*In one person, filtrate is formed at 125 cm³ per minute and urine at 1.0 cm³ per minute. Calculate (a) the volume of filtrate formed per day in dm³ and (b) the percentage of the filtrate volume that is reabsorbed.*

(a) 125 × 60 × 24 = 180 000 cm³. Divide by 1000: **180 dm³** per day.

(b) Volume reabsorbed = 125 − 1.0 = 124 cm³ per minute.
Percentage = 124 ÷ 125 × 100 = **99.2%**.

Almost all the filtrate goes back into the blood.

## 2.72B and 2.79B Putting it together (Paper 2 only)

The kidney has two roles.

- **Excretion:** it removes **urea** (from the breakdown of excess amino acids in the liver), so urea does not build up in the blood. Urea is filtered; some is reabsorbed and recycled, and the remainder is excreted in urine.
- **Osmoregulation:** it controls the **water content** (and ion content) of the blood by changing how much water is reabsorbed in the collecting duct, under the control of ADH.

**Urine** therefore contains **water, urea and ions** (2.79B). A healthy person's urine contains no glucose and no protein.

To answer a "how does the kidney..." question, use this order: ultrafiltration in the glomerulus → selective reabsorption of glucose in the proximal convoluted tubule → water reabsorption from the collecting duct, controlled by ADH → urine to the ureter, bladder and urethra.

## Common errors

- Writing that oxygen is a waste product of respiration, or carbon dioxide a waste product of photosynthesis. Match each gas to the process that makes it.
- Calling faeces an excretory product. That is egestion.
- Confusing **urea** (the waste chemical) with **urine** (the liquid), and **ureter** with **urethra**.
- Saying the glomerulus "filters out urea". Ultrafiltration is not selective: every small molecule passes into the filtrate.
- Saying "glucose is filtered out so it is not in the blood". Glucose is filtered and then **reabsorbed**.
- Writing "ADH makes the kidney reabsorb more water" without naming the collecting duct and its permeability.
- Saying more ADH makes more urine. More ADH means **less**, more concentrated urine.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the waste gases of a plant?**
Carbon dioxide is a waste product of respiration and oxygen is a waste product of photosynthesis. Both leave the leaf by diffusion through the stomata.

**What are the parts of a nephron?**
Bowman's capsule, the proximal convoluted tubule, the loop of Henle, the distal convoluted tubule and the collecting duct.

**What is in glomerular filtrate?**
Water, glucose, urea and ions. Blood cells and large proteins are normally too large to pass into the filtrate.

**What are the two roles of the kidney?**
Excretion of urea and osmoregulation, which controls the water content of the blood.

**Why is selective reabsorption called selective?**
The tubule reabsorbs particular useful substances, such as all the glucose and some water and ions, while urea stays in the filtrate.
