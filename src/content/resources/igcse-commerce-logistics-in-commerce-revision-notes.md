Logistics in commerce is about moving goods to the right place, at the right time and in the right
quantity, and storing them until they are needed. Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715, first examined 2028)
splits Topic 4 into 4.1 Transport and 4.2 Warehousing. Expect to compare transport methods, choose the
best one for a given situation, and explain why warehouses - and warehouse automation - matter in the
supply chain.

For full explanations see the
[logistics study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-logistics-in-commerce-study-guide/), then test
yourself with the [logistics practice questions](/resources/igcse-commerce-logistics-in-commerce-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage (0715, 2028)

| Section | What the syllabus lists |
|---|---|
| 4.1 Transport | Three functions (time, place, quantity); road, rail, air, sea and pipeline methods; advantages and disadvantages; choosing a method; containerisation and transshipment |
| 4.2 Warehousing | Functions; bonded, cold storage, cash and carry and RDC warehouses; automation (robotics, automated sorting, voice picking) and its benefits |

## What are the three functions of transport?

| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Time | Goods arrive when customers need them |
| Place | Goods are moved from where they are made to where they are bought |
| Quantity | The right amount arrives, avoiding shortages or excess stock |

## Which transport method should a business use?

| Method | Examples | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Road | Delivery vans, trucks, couriers | Door-to-door; flexible routes and times; good for short distances | Congestion; limited load; pollution; accidents |
| Rail | Freight trains | Bulky goods over long distances; fewer emissions per tonne | Fixed routes and times; goods need transferring to road at each end |
| Air | Freight planes | Fastest over long distances; secure; good for valuable, perishable or urgent goods | Most expensive; limited capacity; airports needed |
| Sea | Ferry, tanker, container ship, cargo liner | Cheapest for heavy, bulky goods internationally; huge capacity | Slow; port delays; weather; risk of piracy or damage |
| Pipeline | Oil, gas, water | Continuous flow; low running cost; little labour | Very high build cost; only liquids and gases; leaks hard to detect |

**Factors affecting the choice** (learn all six):
1. **Nature of goods** - perishable, fragile, bulky, liquid or valuable.
2. **Urgency** - how fast the goods are needed.
3. **Cost** - compared with the value of the goods.
4. **Security** - risk of theft or damage.
5. **Distance** - local, national or international.
6. **Reliability of transporter** - record of delivering on time.

**Worked example.** A florist in Nairobi exports fresh roses to Amsterdam for Valentine's Day.
- Nature: perishable. Urgency: high. Distance: long and international.
- Best choice: **air freight**, refrigerated. The high cost is justified by the high value of fresh flowers
  and the short shelf life. Sea would be cheaper but far too slow.

## What is containerisation?

- Goods are packed in standard-sized metal containers that move between ship, train and truck without
  being unpacked.
- **Transshipment**: containers are transferred from one ship or vehicle to another at a hub port on the
  way to their destination.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Faster loading with cranes | High cost of containers, cranes and special ports |
| Less theft and damage - containers are sealed | Smaller ports cannot handle large container ships |
| Lower insurance and labour costs | Empty containers must be returned |
| Easy transfer between transport methods | Not suitable for all goods, e.g. some bulk liquids |

## What does a warehouse do in the supply chain?

| Function | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Links with seasonal production | Stores crops harvested once a year for sale all year |
| Links with demand | Builds up stock before seasonal peaks (e.g. festivals) |
| Protecting supply | Keeps buffer stock if deliveries are delayed |
| Price stability | Releasing stored goods steadily avoids sharp price rises and falls |
| Logistics postponement | Final assembly, labelling or packing happens near the customer once orders are known |

## Which types of warehouse are there?

| Type | Key feature | Typical user |
|---|---|---|
| Bonded | Imported goods stored before customs duty is paid | Importers of spirits or tobacco |
| Cold storage | Refrigerated or frozen | Food, medicines |
| Cash and carry | Wholesale warehouse where retailers pay and collect | Small shopkeepers |
| Regional distribution centre (RDC) | Large hub serving a chain's shops in a region | Supermarket chains |

## How does automation change warehousing?

- **Uses**: robotics (moving and picking), automated sorting (conveyors and scanners), voice picking
  (workers follow spoken instructions through a headset).
- **Benefits**: lower labour costs; less human error; improved picking accuracy; improved inventory
  control; improved customer service through faster, correct orders.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Saying air is "best" without linking it to the nature of goods or urgency.
- Forgetting the extra handling cost of rail, which usually needs road at each end.
- Confusing a bonded warehouse with a cash and carry.
- Describing containerisation without mentioning standard sizes or transfer between modes.
- Listing automation tools without a benefit.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the three functions of transport in 0715?** Time, place and quantity. Transport delivers the
right quantity of goods to the right place at the right time.

**Why do importers use bonded warehouses?** Customs duty is paid only when goods leave the warehouse, so
the importer can delay payment, or avoid it if the goods are re-exported.

**What is logistics postponement?** Delaying final steps such as labelling or packing until orders are
known, often at a warehouse near the customer, so stock matches demand.
