These questions cover sub-topic **2.1 The double entry system of book-keeping**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars. Folio columns are not needed.

## Section A

**1.** State the account debited and the account credited when a business pays wages in cash. **[2]**

**2.** Which entry records goods returned by a credit customer?

A: debit sales, credit the customer

B: debit sales returns, credit the customer

C: debit the customer, credit sales returns

D: debit purchases returns, credit the customer **[1]**

**3.** In which ledger is the account of a credit customer kept?

A: nominal ledger

B: purchases ledger

C: sales ledger

D: general journal **[1]**

**4.** A customer's account has debit entries totalling 5,240 and credit entries totalling 4,790. State the amount of the balance and the side on which it is brought down. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** features of a three-column running balance account. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the balance of the drawings account is not transferred to the statement of profit or loss. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Tilly Hughes owns Tilly's Garden Shop. State the account debited and the account credited for each transaction.

(i) Bought goods on credit from Ormond Seeds, 1,800.

(ii) Sold goods on credit to Parkside Hotel, 920.

(iii) Bought a new till, paying by bank transfer, 650.

(iv) Parkside Hotel returned goods, 70.

(v) Paid insurance by bank transfer, 380.

(vi) Tilly took 200 cash for her own use.

(vii) Received a bank loan, 5,000.

(viii) Returned goods to Ormond Seeds, 140. **[8]**

**8.** In June 2027 Tilly's transactions with Ormond Seeds were:

| Date | Transaction |
|---|---|
| 1 June | Balance owed to Ormond Seeds, 760 |
| 4 June | Goods bought on credit, 1,800 |
| 9 June | Goods returned, 140 |
| 15 June | Paid the 760 owed at 1 June by bank transfer |
| 26 June | Goods bought on credit, 640 |

**(a)** Prepare the account of Ormond Seeds in Tilly's purchases ledger, balanced at 30 June 2027.

**(b)** State what the balance on 1 July 2027 represents. **[6]**

**9.** Parkside Hotel's transactions with Tilly in June 2027 were: 1 June, balance owed by the hotel 480; 3 June, credit sales 920; 8 June, returns 70; 12 June, the hotel paid 480 by bank transfer; 20 June, the hotel paid 900 by bank transfer; 28 June, credit sales 310.

**(a)** Prepare the account of Parkside Hotel in Tilly's sales ledger using a three-column running balance format.

**(b)** Explain the balance on the account after the entry on 20 June. **[7]**

**10.** The following account appears in the ledger of Ellery Stationers.

```

                            =====                              =====
```

**(a)** State whether this account is in the sales ledger or the purchases ledger, giving a reason.

**(b)** Describe the transactions on 11 July and 15 July.

**(c)** State what the balance on 1 August represents and the financial statement in which it would be included. **[6]**

**11.** Tilly's financial year ends on 31 December 2027. Commission received by bank transfer during the year was: 14 March, 620; 22 July, 845; 30 November, 735.

**(a)** Prepare the commission received account for the year, showing the transfer at 31 December 2027.

**(b)** State where the other half of the transfer entry is recorded.

**(c)** At 31 December 2027 the drawings account has a debit balance of 9,600. State the double entry to close it. **[7]**

**12.** **(a)** State the ledger in which each account is kept: (i) Sales; (ii) Ormond Seeds; (iii) Parkside Hotel; (iv) Loan.

**(b)** Tilly is thinking of keeping her ledger accounts digitally. Explain **one** advantage and **one** limitation of doing so. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Debit Wages [1]; credit Cash [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** 450 [1], brought down on the debit side [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Any two [2]: debit, credit and balance columns; the balance is recalculated after every entry; each balance is labelled Dr or Cr; there is no need to balance the account off. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Drawings are amounts taken by the owner for private use, not expenses of running the business [1], so they reduce the owner's capital and are transferred to the capital account [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7.** One mark for each transaction with both accounts correct [8]:

(i) debit Purchases, credit Ormond Seeds.

(ii) debit Parkside Hotel, credit Sales.

(iii) debit Equipment (or Shop equipment), credit Bank.

(iv) debit Sales returns, credit Parkside Hotel.

(v) debit Insurance, credit Bank.

(vi) debit Drawings, credit Cash.

(vii) debit Bank, credit Loan.

(viii) debit Ormond Seeds, credit Purchases returns.

Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The till in (iii) is a non-current asset, so it is not debited to purchases, which is only for goods bought for resale.

**8. (a)**

```
Ormond Seeds account
Date     Details                $  Date     Details                $
2027                               2027
9 Jun    Purchases returns    140  1 Jun    Balance b/d          760
15 Jun   Bank                 760  4 Jun    Purchases          1,800
30 Jun   Balance c/d        2,300  26 Jun   Purchases            640
                            -----                              -----
                            3,200                              3,200
                            =====                              =====
                                   1 Jul    Balance b/d        2,300
```

Opening balance on the credit side [1]; purchases on the credit side [1]; returns and bank on the debit side [1]; balance c/d 2,300 with totals of 3,200 [1]; balance b/d 2,300 on the credit side [1].

**(b)** Tilly owes Ormond Seeds 2,300 [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**9. (a)**

```
Parkside Hotel account
Date     Details           Debit    Credit    Balance
2027
1 Jun    Balance                                480 Dr
3 Jun    Sales               920              1,400 Dr
8 Jun    Sales returns                  70    1,330 Dr
12 Jun   Bank                          480      850 Dr
20 Jun   Bank                          900       50 Cr
28 Jun   Sales               310                260 Dr
```

Opening balance 480 Dr [1]; sales in the debit column [1]; returns and receipts in the credit column [1]; correct balances to 12 June [1]; balances of 50 Cr and 260 Dr correctly labelled [1].

**(b)** The hotel paid 50 more than it owed [1], so Tilly owed the hotel 50 at that date, shown by the credit balance [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**10. (a)** Sales ledger [1]: the account records sales and sales returns, and Rahman Supplies has a debit balance, so it is a customer [1].

**(b)** 11 July: Rahman Supplies returned goods worth 300 [1]. 15 July: Rahman Supplies paid 1,340 by bank, settling the opening balance [1].

**(c)** Rahman Supplies owes Ellery Stationers 2,660, a trade receivable [1], included in current assets in the statement of financial position [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The name "Supplies" is a distraction. Decide from the entries, not from the business name.

**11. (a)**

```
Commission received account
Date     Details                     $  Date     Details           $
2027                                    2027
31 Dec   Statement of profit        2,200  14 Mar   Bank            620
           or loss                         22 Jul   Bank            845
                                           30 Nov   Bank            735
                                 -----                            -----
                                 2,200                            2,200
                                 =====                            =====
```

Three receipts on the credit side [1]; detail "Bank" [1]; transfer on the debit side [1]; amount 2,200 with totals [1].

**(b)** In the statement of profit or loss, as other income [1].

**(c)** Debit Capital 9,600 [1]; credit Drawings 9,600 [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**12. (a)** (i) Nominal (general) ledger [1]; (ii) purchases ledger [1]; (iii) sales ledger [1]; (iv) nominal (general) ledger [1].

**(b)** Advantage, identified [1] and explained [1], for example: one entry posts both sides automatically, so balances are always up to date and arithmetic errors are fewer. Limitation, identified [1] and explained [1], for example: if Tilly chooses the wrong account the software posts the error automatically, so she still needs to understand double entry; or the system needs equipment, training and protection from data loss. Total: 8 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Posting returns to the sales or purchases account instead of a returns account.
- Debiting purchases for a non-current asset.
- Putting "Balance b/d" on the same side as "Balance c/d".
- Not labelling running balances Dr or Cr, especially after an overpayment.
- Deciding whether an account is a customer's or a supplier's from the name instead of from the entries.
