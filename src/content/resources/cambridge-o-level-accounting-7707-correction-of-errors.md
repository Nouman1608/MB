## The three-step routine for any error

For every error, write down three things before you touch the journal:

1. **What was done** (the entries actually made).
2. **What should have been done** (the correct double entry).
3. **What is needed to get from 1 to 2.** That difference is the correcting
journal entry.

This routine works for every type of error, including ones you have not
met before. A correcting entry is always made in the **general journal**,
with a date, the account debited first, the account credited indented
below, the amounts and a short **narrative** explaining the correction.

## Correcting errors that do not affect the trial balance

These six errors have equal debits and credits, so their corrections have
equal debits and credits too, and no suspense account is involved.

| Error | What happened | Correcting entry |
|---|---|---|
| Omission | A credit sale of $720 to B Osei was never recorded | Dr B Osei $720, Cr Revenue $720 |
| Commission | A $380 receipt from K Nair was credited to K Nayar | Dr K Nayar $380, Cr K Nair $380 |
| Principle | A $1,500 printer for office use was debited to purchases | Dr Office equipment $1,500, Cr Purchases $1,500 |
| Original entry | A purchase invoice of $940 from Rift Ltd was entered as $490 | Dr Purchases $450, Cr Rift Ltd $450 |
| Complete reversal | Rent paid of $600 was debited to bank and credited to rent | Dr Rent $1,200, Cr Bank $1,200 |
| Compensating | Revenue and wages accounts were both overcast by $100 | Dr Revenue $100, Cr Wages $100 |

Two of these deserve care.

- **Original entry.** Only the **difference** is corrected: $940 − $490 =
$450 more purchases and $450 more owed to Rift Ltd.
- **Complete reversal.** The correction is **double** the amount. The first
$600 cancels the wrong entry; the second $600 makes the right one. Writing
$600 only brings both accounts to zero.

## Using a suspense account

When the trial balance totals disagree and the error cannot be found at
once, the difference is entered in a **suspense account** so that the trial
balance balances temporarily. If the debit total is the larger, the
difference is entered on the **credit** side of the suspense account, and
the other way round.

As each error that caused the difference is found, it is corrected with a
journal entry in which **one side is the suspense account**. When all such
errors are corrected, the suspense account closes with no balance. If a
balance remains, an error is still undiscovered.

A suspense account is temporary. It is not an asset, a liability, an
expense or an income, so it should not appear in final statements once the
errors are found.

## Worked example: Kamal Traders

Kamal's trial balance at 31 December 2027 showed debit totals of $96,840 and
credit totals of $96,410. The difference of $430 was entered on the credit
side of a suspense account. His draft profit for the year was $23,480. These
errors were then found:

1. The sales journal was undercast by $400.
2. Rent paid of $1,250 was entered correctly in the cash book but posted to
the rent account as $1,520.
3. Discount allowed of $90 was entered in the customer's account but not in
the discount allowed account.
4. Office equipment costing $2,000 was debited to the purchases account.
5. Cash drawings of $150 were entered in the cash book but not posted to the
drawings account.
6. Goods of $300 returned by a customer, A Bello, were entered in the sales
returns journal as $30.

Errors 4 and 6 have equal debits and credits; the other four caused the
difference.

**Journal entries** (narratives in brackets):

```
                                                      Debit $   Credit $
1  Suspense                                              400
     Revenue                                                        400
   (Sales journal undercast)
2  Suspense                                              270
     Rent                                                           270
   (Rent of $1,250 posted as $1,520)
3  Discount allowed                                       90
     Suspense                                                        90
   (Discount allowed not posted to discount allowed account)
4  Office equipment                                    2,000
     Purchases                                                    2,000
   (Purchase of office equipment debited to purchases)
5  Drawings                                              150
     Suspense                                                       150
   (Cash drawings not posted to drawings account)
6  Sales returns                                         270
     A Bello                                                        270
   (Sales returns of $300 entered as $30)
```

**Suspense account:**

```
Suspense account
Date     Details                      $   Date     Details                      $
2027                                      2027
31 Dec   Revenue                    400   31 Dec   Difference in trial        430
31 Dec   Rent                       270              balance
                                          31 Dec   Discount allowed            90
                                          31 Dec   Drawings                   150
                                   ----                                      ----
                                    670                                       670
                                   ====                                      ====
```

The account closes with no balance, so all the errors behind the difference
have been found.

## Adjusting the profit for the year

Each correcting entry that touches an income or expense account changes
profit. A correction that **credits** an expense or an income account
increases profit; one that **debits** them reduces profit. Entries between
statement of financial position accounts (or drawings) have no effect.

```
Kamal Traders
Statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2027
                                                        $          $
Draft profit for the year                                     23,480
Add  Revenue undercast                                400
     Rent overstated                                  270
     Office equipment removed from purchases        2,000
                                                    -----
                                                                2,670
                                                               ------
                                                               26,150
Less Discount allowed omitted                          90
     Sales returns understated                        270
                                                    -----
                                                                  360
                                                               ------
Corrected profit for the year                                  25,790
                                                               ======
```

Error 5 is not in the statement: drawings are not an expense. Error 4 would
also bring a depreciation charge on the office equipment if the question
gives a policy; here there is none.

## Effect on the statement of financial position

| Item | Effect of the corrections |
|---|---|
| Office equipment (non-current assets) | Increases by $2,000 |
| Trade receivables (current assets) | Decrease by $270 (A Bello) |
| Suspense account | Removed: the $430 credit balance disappears |
| Capital: profit for the year | Increases by $2,310 |
| Capital: drawings | Increase by $150 |
| Closing capital | Increases by $2,310 − $150 = $2,160 |

Check: assets rise by $2,000 − $270 = $1,730. On the other side, capital
rises by $2,160 and the $430 suspense credit is removed: $2,160 − $430 =
$1,730.

## Common mistakes

- Using the suspense account to correct an error of omission, commission,
principle, original entry, complete reversal or compensation.
- Correcting a complete reversal with the single amount instead of double.
- Correcting an error of original entry with the full figure instead of the
difference.
- Placing the opening difference on the wrong side of the suspense account.
- Leaving out narratives when a question asks for journal entries.
- Adding the change in drawings to the statement of corrected profit.
- Forgetting that a correction to purchases, sales returns or carriage
inwards also changes **gross** profit.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare journal entries**: date or number, account debited, account
credited, amounts in the right columns, and a narrative if asked for.
- **Prepare the suspense account**: open it with the trial balance
difference, post only the corrections that involve it, and total both
sides.
- **Calculate the corrected profit**: start from the draft figure and show
each adjustment with a plus or minus, so method marks remain available if
one line slips.
- **Explain** questions on the suspense account need a reason, for example
"it allows the trial balance to balance temporarily so that the statements
can be drafted while the errors are investigated".
