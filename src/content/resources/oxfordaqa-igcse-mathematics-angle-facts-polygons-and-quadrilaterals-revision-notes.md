## G1: Terms and notation

| Term or symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vertex (vertices) | a corner where edges meet |
| Edge | a side of a polygon, or where two faces meet |
| Plane | a flat surface |
| AB ∥ CD | AB parallel to CD (matching arrowheads) |
| AB ⊥ CD | AB perpendicular to CD (small square marks 90°) |
| ∠ABC | the angle at B between BA and BC |
| Side a | the side opposite angle A in triangle ABC |
| Regular polygon | all sides equal and all angles equal |

Tick marks show equal sides. Arrowheads show parallel lines. Diagrams may not be drawn to scale.

## G2: Angles at a point and on a line

| Fact | Rule |
|---|---|
| Angles at a point | total 360° |
| Angles on a straight line | total 180° |
| Vertically opposite angles | equal |
| Perpendicular lines | four angles of 90° |

**Method.** Mark every known angle on the diagram. Write the sum as an equation. Solve it. Write the reason beside each line.

**Reminder.** Angles of 130°, 85° and 70° meet at a point with a fourth angle. The fourth is 360 − 285 = 75°.

## G3: Parallel lines

| Name | Property | Memory aid |
|---|---|---|
| Corresponding | equal | same position at each crossing |
| Alternate | equal | opposite sides of the transversal, between the lines |
| Interior (co-interior) | add up to 180° | same side of the transversal, between the lines |

Use the full names in your reasons. The specification asks for alternate, corresponding and interior, not colloquial letter shapes.

**Method in steps.**

1. Mark the parallel lines with arrowheads.
2. Pick an angle you know and find the angle you need in as few steps as possible.
3. Name the property each time: "alternate angles", "angles on a straight line" and so on.

**Reminder.** Parallel lines PQ and RS are crossed by a transversal. One interior angle is 107°, so the other is 180 − 107 = 73°.

## G3: Triangles

| Fact | Rule |
|---|---|
| Angles in a triangle | total 180° |
| Exterior angle | equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles |
| Equilateral | 3 equal sides, 3 angles of 60° |
| Isosceles | 2 equal sides; the angles opposite them are equal |
| Scalene | no equal sides, no equal angles |
| Right-angled | one angle of 90° |
| Acute-angled | all angles below 90° |
| Obtuse-angled | one angle above 90° |

**Reminder.** An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 100°. Each base angle is (180 − 100) ÷ 2 = 40°.

**Reminder.** Two angles of a triangle are 52° and 67°. The third is 180 − 119 = 61°, so the triangle is scalene and acute-angled.

## G3 and G5: Quadrilaterals

Angles in any quadrilateral add up to 360°.

| Shape | Key properties |
|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal sides, 4 right angles; diagonals equal, bisect each other at 90° |
| Rectangle | opposite sides equal and parallel, 4 right angles; diagonals equal and bisect each other |
| Parallelogram | opposite sides equal and parallel; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other |
| Rhombus | 4 equal sides; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other at 90° and bisect the angles |
| Kite | two pairs of adjacent equal sides; one pair of equal opposite angles; diagonals cross at 90° |
| Trapezium | one pair of parallel sides |

**Reminder.** A kite has angles 84°, 112°, x and 112°. Then x = 360 − 308 = 52°. The two 112° angles are the equal pair, so x is the odd one out.

## G4: Polygons

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sum of interior angles | (n − 2) × 180° |
| Sum of exterior angles | 360° |
| Regular polygon, exterior angle | 360° ÷ n |
| Regular polygon, interior angle | 180° − exterior angle |
| Regular polygon, angle at the centre | 360° ÷ n |
| Interior plus exterior at one vertex | 180° |

| Sides | Name | Interior sum | Regular: exterior | Regular: interior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | pentagon | 540° | 72° | 108° |
| 6 | hexagon | 720° | 60° | 120° |
| 8 | octagon | 1080° | 45° | 135° |
| 9 | nonagon | 1260° | 40° | 140° |
| 10 | decagon | 1440° | 36° | 144° |
| 12 | dodecagon | 1800° | 30° | 150° |

**Method for finding n.** If you are given an interior angle of a regular polygon, subtract it from 180° to get the exterior angle, then divide 360° by that.

**Reminder.** A regular polygon has interior angle 165°. Exterior angle = 15°, so n = 360 ÷ 15 = 24.

## Worked reminders

**Exterior angle of a triangle.** Triangle PQR has angle P = 48° and angle Q = 77°. Side QR is extended beyond R. The exterior angle at R is 48 + 77 = 125°. Check: angle R = 180 − 125 = 55°, and 48 + 77 + 55 = 180.

**Missing angle in a pentagon.** Four angles of a pentagon are 95°, 110°, 120° and 105°.

```
Sum for 5 sides = 3 × 180 = 540°
Four angles total 430°
Fifth angle = 540 − 430 = 110°
```

**Trapezium.** ABCD has AB ∥ DC and angle DAB = 73°. Angles DAB and ADC are interior angles between the same parallel lines, so angle ADC = 180 − 73 = 107°.

**Isosceles triangle in a regular polygon.** In a regular 12-sided polygon, the centre O joins two neighbouring vertices to make an isosceles triangle with apex angle 30°. Each base angle is (180 − 30) ÷ 2 = 75°. Two base angles together make the interior angle: 2 × 75 = 150°.

**Square and its diagonal.** The diagonal of a square bisects the 90° corner, so it makes 45° with each side.

## Setting out a reason

A clear line of working has three parts: the angle, its value and the fact used. For example:

```
Angle BCD = 180 − 112 = 68°    (angles on a straight line)
Angle CDE = 68°                (alternate angles, AB ∥ DE)
```

Work in the order you find the angles, so each line depends only on lines above it. If a question says "give a reason", write the fact in words. A bare calculation shows the arithmetic but not why it is allowed.

## Must-know distinctions

- Alternate angles are equal. Interior angles add up to 180°.
- Interior angle sum depends on n. Exterior angle sum is always 360°.
- Dividing 360° by the exterior angle gives n. Dividing 360° by the interior angle does not.
- A rhombus has equal sides. A kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides. A rhombus is a special kite, but a kite is not usually a rhombus.
- A rectangle has equal diagonals. A rhombus has diagonals that cross at 90°. A square has both.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal. Adjacent angles on a line are not, unless both are 90°.

## Quick self-test

1. Angles of 130°, 85° and 70° meet at a point with a fourth angle. Find the fourth angle.
2. Two straight lines cross. One angle is 68°. Write down the other three angles.
3. Two alternate angles lie between parallel lines. One is 54°. What is the other?
4. Two interior angles lie between parallel lines. One is 107°. What is the other?
5. Two angles of a triangle are 52° and 67°. Find the third angle.
6. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 100°. Find each base angle.
7. Find the sum of the interior angles of a 12-sided polygon.
8. Find the exterior angle of a regular 24-sided polygon.
9. A regular polygon has an interior angle of 165°. How many sides has it?
10. A quadrilateral has diagonals that cross at 90°, one diagonal bisecting the other but not vice versa. Name it.
11. Parallelogram ABCD has angle A = (5k + 10)° and angle B = (3k + 30)°. Find k.

### Answers

1. 360 − (130 + 85 + 70) = **75°**.
2. **68°, 112°, 112°** (vertically opposite, and angles on a straight line).
3. **54°**, because alternate angles are equal.
4. 180 − 107 = **73°**, because interior angles add up to 180°.
5. 180 − (52 + 67) = **61°**.
6. (180 − 100) ÷ 2 = **40°**.
7. (12 − 2) × 180 = **1800°**.
8. 360 ÷ 24 = **15°**.
9. Exterior angle = 180 − 165 = 15°, so n = 360 ÷ 15 = **24**.
10. **Kite**.
11. Adjacent angles add up to 180°: 8k + 40 = 180, so 8k = 140 and k = **17.5**. Angle A = 97.5°, angle B = 82.5°.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving an angle with no reason. Name the fact each time, such as "angles on a straight line".
- Mixing up alternate and interior angles, so the wrong sum or the wrong equality is used.
- Finding n from the interior angle by dividing 360° by it.
- Forgetting that the interior angle sum uses (n − 2), not n.
- Measuring an angle from the diagram instead of calculating it.
- Describing a shape as "a rhombus" when the working only shows opposite sides parallel.
- Writing the equation correctly but dropping a bracket, such as 3a + a + 35 + 2a − 15 with the wrong sign.
- Leaving the answer as x when the question asks for the size of an angle.
