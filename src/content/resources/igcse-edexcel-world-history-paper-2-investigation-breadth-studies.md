This guide covers **Paper 2 – Investigation and Breadth Studies**, for
Pearson Edexcel International GCSE History (4HI1), specification
Issue 5. Paper 2 pairs one Historical Investigation, chosen from five
options, with one Breadth Study in change, chosen from eight options --
a different structure from Paper 1's two Depth Studies, and testing a
different historical skill.

## Where this fits in 4HI1

Where Paper 1's Depth Studies examine a narrow period in close focus,
Paper 2 asks for two different skills. The Historical Investigation
tests source evaluation and enquiry within a defined topic; the
Breadth Study tests understanding of change and continuity across a
long timespan, sometimes a century or more. As with Paper 1, the
specific Investigation and Breadth Study a candidate needs to know
depend entirely on which options their centre has chosen to teach.

## How to approach it

Confirm which Investigation and which Breadth Study a centre has
chosen before revising anything else -- as with Paper 1, this is the
single most consequential piece of information a candidate needs, and
getting it wrong wastes revision time on the wrong content entirely.
The Historical Investigation is assessed partly through unseen sources
in the exam itself, so preparation should focus on the background
narrative and context needed to interpret unfamiliar sources
confidently, rather than trying to memorise every possible source in
advance. The Breadth Study, by contrast, rewards being able to trace
change over the whole period -- building a timeline that identifies
turning points and periods of continuity is usually more useful than a
list of isolated events, since exam questions frequently ask
candidates to assess how much, or how quickly, something changed.

## Common mistakes

Treating the Historical Investigation like a second Depth Study and
over-memorising narrative detail while under-preparing the skill of
reading and evaluating unfamiliar sources, which the paper specifically
tests. Writing about a Breadth Study as a sequence of disconnected
events rather than explicitly addressing change, continuity, and the
pace of change, which is what the assessment objectives for this
component reward. Confusing which centre-chosen options apply --
because Paper 2 offers thirteen total options across two independent
choices, candidates sometimes revise material relevant to a different
centre's selection found in shared revision materials.

## Worked example

A Historical Investigation question presents two unfamiliar sources
about the same event and asks candidates to assess how far one source
is more useful than the other for a specific enquiry. What separates a
strong answer from a weak one?

```
WEAK: describes what each source says, then states "Source A is more
      useful because it gives more detail."

STRONG: evaluates each source against the specific enquiry named in
        the question -- considering the source's origin, purpose and
        content -- and uses contextual knowledge of the period to
        judge how reliable or typical each source's perspective is,
        before reaching a supported judgement about which is more
        useful and why.
```

The background narrative knowledge built while studying the
Investigation exists to support exactly this kind of source
evaluation -- it is not tested for its own sake the way Depth Study
content is on Paper 1.

## Quick revision checklist

- Confirm the specific Historical Investigation and Breadth Study your
centre has taught before revising further.
- For the Investigation, build contextual background knowledge that
helps interpret unfamiliar sources, rather than memorising a fixed
narrative.
- Practise evaluating a source's origin, purpose and content, not just
summarising what it says.
- For the Breadth Study, construct a timeline highlighting turning
points and periods of continuity across the full period studied.
- Practise answering "how far" and "how much" change questions, which
this component's assessment objectives specifically reward.
- Do not rely on shared revision notes without checking they match
your centre's chosen options -- Paper 2 offers thirteen total choices
across two independent selections.

## Assessment objectives

Across the qualification, Pearson Edexcel weights AO1 (knowledge and
understanding of key features and characteristics of the periods
studied) at 34% and AO2 (explaining and analysing historical events
using second-order concepts such as cause, consequence, change and
significance) at 36%, with the remainder assessing source-based
skills -- the strand the Historical Investigation is specifically
designed to test. Because AO1 and AO2 together make up the majority of
the marks across both papers, secure factual knowledge remains the
foundation even on a source-based component like the Investigation:
strong source evaluation still depends on being able to bring accurate
contextual knowledge to bear on what a source says and omits.
