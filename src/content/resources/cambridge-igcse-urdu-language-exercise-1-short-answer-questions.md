Exercise 1 is the opening task of **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second
Language (0539). It is worth **8 of the paper's 60 marks**. You read one short text printed in the
question paper and answer a series of questions in Urdu, each needing only **a single word or a short
phrase**. This guide goes deeper on that one exercise than the site's
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/), which
covers all six exercises together.

## What the syllabus says

The 2025-2027 syllabus describes Exercise 1 as short answer questions that test **skimming and
gist-reading** skills. The text will be one of these types:

- advertisement (اشتہار)
- brochure or leaflet (کتابچہ، پرچہ)
- guide (رہنما کتابچہ)
- report (رپورٹ)
- manual or instructions (ہدایات)
- newspaper or magazine article (اخبار یا رسالے کا مضمون)

All four reading objectives are tested: **R1** identify and select relevant information, **R2**
understand ideas, opinions and attitudes, **R3** understand the connections between them, and **R4**
understand what is implied but not directly stated, such as gist or the writer's purpose. In practice
most of the eight questions are R1 detail questions, with one or two that ask *why*, *how* or *for
what purpose*.

Dictionaries are not allowed and every question and answer is in Urdu.

## How each text type helps you

Short practical texts are built to be scanned. Their layout tells you where to look before you read a
single sentence closely.

| Text type | Layout clues | Typical questions |
|---|---|---|
| Advertisement | bold headline, prices, offers (رعایت، مفت) | کتنے پیسے؟ کیا فائدہ؟ |
| Leaflet / brochure | headings, bullet points, opening times | کب؟ کہاں؟ کس کے لیے؟ |
| Guide | places, directions, advice | کہاں؟ کیسے پہنچیں؟ |
| Instructions / manual | numbered steps, polite imperatives (کیجیے، نہ کریں) | پہلے کیا کرنا ہے؟ کس بات سے بچنا ہے؟ |
| Report / article | headline, then who, what, where, when in the first lines | کیا ہوا؟ کس نے کہا؟ کیوں؟ |

Instructions use the polite imperative ending **ـیے** (رکھیے، بھریے، کیجیے) and the negative **نہ**
(فلیش استعمال نہ کریں). Recognising these forms quickly tells you that a sentence is a rule or a step,
which is exactly what "what must you do" questions target.

## Reading the question word first

The Urdu question word tells you the **form** your answer must take. Predicting the form before you
look at the text stops you writing a correct-looking detail that answers a different question.

| Question word | Meaning | Answer form |
|---|---|---|
| کب | when | a day, date or time |
| کتنے دن / کتنے عرصے | for how long | a duration, sometimes worked out |
| کہاں / کس جگہ | where | a place |
| کتنا / کتنے / کتنی | how much, how many | a number or amount |
| کون / کس نے | who | a person or group |
| کیا | what | a thing or an action |
| کیسے / کس طرح | how | a method or means |
| کیوں | why | a reason, often near کیونکہ or اس لیے |
| کس مقصد کے لیے | for what purpose | the gist of the whole text |

Two of these need extra care. **کب** and **کتنے دن** are easy to confuse: "when did they work" wants a
time (چھٹی کے بعد), not a length of time (چھ مہینے). And **کتنے دن** may require a small calculation:
a fair "from the 15th to the 19th" lasts five days, which the text never states in words.

## Worked example

The leaflet below is an original text written for this guide.

> **شہر کا عجائب گھر — گرمیوں کی خاص نمائش**
>
> عجائب گھر میں یکم جولائی سے 31 اگست تک "پرانے سکّے" کے عنوان سے ایک خاص نمائش لگائی جا رہی ہے۔
> عجائب گھر منگل سے اتوار تک صبح 9 بجے سے شام 5 بجے تک کھلا رہتا ہے؛ پیر کو چھٹی ہوتی ہے۔
>
> - **ٹکٹ:** بڑوں کے لیے 200 روپے، بچوں کے لیے 50 روپے۔ پانچ سال سے کم عمر بچوں کا داخلہ مفت ہے۔
> - **گائیڈ:** ہر جمعے کو دوپہر 2 بجے ایک گائیڈ نمائش کی سیر کروائے گا۔
> - **تصویریں:** تصویریں کھینچنے کی اجازت ہے، مگر فلیش استعمال نہ کریں۔

**Question A.** عجائب گھر کس دن بند رہتا ہے؟

The text never uses the word بند. It says the museum is open Tuesday to Sunday and that Monday is a
holiday (چھٹی). The answer is **پیر** (or پیر کو). This is a small R3 step: you connect "open Tuesday to
Sunday" with "closed" yourself.

**Question B.** ایک چار سال کے بچے کے ٹکٹ کی قیمت کیا ہے؟

The obvious answer, 50 روپے, is wrong. The condition in the next sentence, پانچ سال سے کم عمر بچوں کا
داخلہ مفت ہے, applies to a four-year-old. The answer is **مفت** (or کچھ نہیں). Words that limit or
change a rule (صرف، کم از کم، سے کم، کے علاوہ، مگر، البتہ، سوائے) are the most common source of
distractors in this exercise.

**Question C.** تصویریں کھینچتے وقت کس بات کا خیال رکھنا ہے؟

The answer is **فلیش استعمال نہ کریں** (or فلیش کے بغیر). Copying "تصویریں کھینچنے کی اجازت ہے" would
repeat the permission, not the condition the question asks about.

**Question D.** یہ پرچہ کس مقصد کے لیے لکھا گیا ہے؟

This is a gist question (R4). The answer is the purpose of the whole text: **لوگوں کو نمائش دیکھنے کی
دعوت دینا** or **نمائش کے بارے میں معلومات دینا**. A single detail such as "ٹکٹ کی قیمت بتانا" is too
narrow, because it describes one line rather than the text.

## Writing the answer

- **Keep it short.** A word or phrase is enough. A full sentence is not wrong, but it costs time and
  every extra word is a chance to add an error or an extra, contradictory detail.
- **Be precise.** "صبح" does not answer "what time"; "صبح 9 بجے" does. Include the unit: 200 روپے,
  not 200.
- **Give one answer.** If you list two possibilities and one is wrong, the answer becomes ambiguous and
  may not be credited. Decide, then write one.
- **Lifting is usually fine here.** Unlike the summary in Exercise 4, short-answer questions do not ask
  for your own words. Copying the exact phrase from the text is often the safest answer, provided you
  copy only the part that answers the question.
- **Spell carefully.** A misplaced dot can turn one word into another, so check names, numbers and
  key words letter by letter.
- **Match the question's grammar where it helps.** "کس جگہ" can be answered with a bare place name
  or with میں (ایکسپو سینٹر میں); both communicate the answer.

## Common mistakes

1. **Answering the nearby detail.** The right paragraph, the wrong line: a price for a child instead of
   the price for the age group named in the question.
2. **Ignoring a qualifier.** Missing مگر, صرف or سے کم, which reverses or narrows the rule.
3. **Confusing when and how long.** Writing a duration for a کب question, or a date for a کتنے دن one.
4. **Giving a detail for a gist question.** A purpose question wants the point of the whole text.
5. **Writing an essay.** Two lines for a one-mark question rarely add marks and often add errors.
6. **Leaving blanks.** A sensible guess can score; a blank cannot.

## Exam technique and timing

Exercise 1 carries 8 marks and the text is short, so it should take less time than its share of the
paper. Around **10-12 minutes** is a reasonable target, leaving more time for the longer reading in
Exercise 3 and the writing tasks. A reliable routine:

1. **Look at the layout** (headline, headings, bullet points) for 20 seconds to see what kind of text
   it is and where each type of information sits.
2. **Read all the questions** and underline each question word. Predict the answer form: time, place,
   number, reason or purpose.
3. **Scan** for the key word of each question, then read the whole sentence it sits in, and the next
   one, for qualifiers.
4. **Answer in order.** Questions in this exercise usually follow the order of the text, so if you
   cannot find one answer, the next question's answer tells you roughly where it should be.
5. **Leave the gist question until last** if it comes earlier; you will understand the purpose better
   once you have answered the detail questions.
6. **Check** that each answer has the form the question word asked for.

The Urdu rubric you will meet is short: **ایک لفظ یا مختصر فقرے میں جواب دیجیے** (answer in one word
or a short phrase). Treat it literally.
