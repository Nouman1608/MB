> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** for revision and practice on Criterion B. They are **not** reproduced IB material and they do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of any on-screen examination. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (achievement levels 1-8), so the `[n]` marks below are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Study first: the [Criterion B study guide](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-criterion-b-inquiring-and-designing/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-criterion-b-inquiring-and-designing-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** (Criterion B) Define the term *independent variable*. **[1]**

**2.** (Criterion B) A student investigates how the concentration of a plant fertiliser solution affects the height of bean seedlings after 14 days.

**(a)** State the independent variable. **[1]**
**(b)** State the dependent variable. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify two variables that must be controlled and, for each one, state how the student should control it. **[4]**

**3.** (Criterion B) A student wants to investigate how the mass of a trolley affects the distance it rolls along a flat floor after it leaves the bottom of a ramp. Formulate a testable hypothesis and explain it using scientific reasoning. **[3]**

**4.** (Criterion B) A student writes the hypothesis: "Light affects plants."

**(a)** Explain two reasons why this is not a good testable hypothesis. **[2]**
**(b)** Rewrite it as a testable hypothesis with scientific reasoning, for the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed. **[2]**

**5.** (Criterion B) Read this method.

1. Take some water.
2. Heat it up.
3. Add salt.
4. See what happens.
5. Do it again with a different amount.

Identify four ways in which this method is not suitable for a scientific investigation. **[4]**

**6.** (Criterion B) Explain why a student should repeat each measurement three times and calculate a mean. **[2]**

**7.** (Criterion B) A student will react 25 cm³ of acid with a metal and needs to measure the temperature rise, which is expected to be about 4 °C.

Equipment available: 250 cm³ beaker, 10 cm³ measuring cylinder, 25 cm³ measuring cylinder, thermometer marked every 1 °C, digital thermometer reading to 0.1 °C, ruler.

**(a)** Select the two most suitable items for this task. **[2]**
**(b)** Justify your selection. **[2]**

**8.** (Criterion B) Design an investigation to find out how the surface area of marble chips affects the rate of reaction with dilute hydrochloric acid. Your design should include a hypothesis with reasoning, the variables and how each is managed, the equipment, a numbered method, how enough data will be collected and a safety step. **[8]**

**9.** (Criterion B) Identify two hazards in the investigation in Question 8 and state a precaution for each. **[3]**

**10.** (Criterion B) A teacher gives this open-ended problem: "Investigate what affects how long a paper helicopter takes to fall to the floor."

**(a)** List three factors that could be changed. **[1]**
**(b)** Choose one as the independent variable. Explain why it is a good choice and how the student will control the other factors. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The variable that the investigator selects and changes (manipulates) in an experiment [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Concentration of the fertiliser solution [1].
**(b)** Height of the seedlings (after 14 days) [1].
**(c)** Any two controlled variables, each with a method, 2 marks each (variable [1], control method [1]):
- the volume of solution given to each plant: measure the same volume in a measuring cylinder each time;
- the type and age of the bean seeds: use seeds from one packet and plant them on the same day;
- the amount of light and the temperature: keep all pots in the same place;
- the type and volume of soil and pot size: use the same soil from one bag, measured in the same pots;
- the frequency of watering: water at the same times each day.
**[4]**
*Marking note:* A control method such as "keep it the same" earns no mark. It must say what is measured or done.

**3.** Hypothesis: if the mass of the trolley is increased, then the distance it rolls along the floor will stay about the same, or increase slightly [1]. Reasoning: a greater mass gains more kinetic energy from the ramp [1], but it also has a greater weight and so a greater friction force on the floor, so the two effects roughly cancel [1]. **[3]**
*Marking note:* Either direction of change can earn the marks if the reasoning is scientific and consistent. A hypothesis with no reasoning earns one mark at most.

**4. (a)** It does not state what aspect of light changes (intensity, colour, duration) [1]. It does not say what is measured or what the effect is, so it cannot be tested and gives no direction of change [1].
**(b)** If the light intensity is increased, then the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed (for example bubbles of oxygen per minute) will increase [1], because more light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll, so more glucose and oxygen are made until another factor becomes limiting [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Any four of: the amount of water is not measured [1]; no temperature is measured or controlled [1]; the amount of salt is not stated [1]; "see what happens" says nothing about what is measured, so there is no dependent variable [1]; step 5 changes the amount without saying which quantity or how much [1]; no repeats [1]; no equipment, units or safety [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Repeats show whether results are consistent and let the student spot a result that does not fit the pattern [1]. A mean reduces the effect of random error, so the result is more reliable [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** The 25 cm³ measuring cylinder [1] and the digital thermometer [1].
**(b)** The 25 cm³ cylinder matches the volume needed and so measures it more precisely than the 10 cm³ cylinder (which would have to be used several times) [1]. A temperature rise of about 4 °C needs a thermometer that reads to 0.1 °C, because a thermometer marked every 1 °C would give a coarse result with a large percentage uncertainty [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Award marks as follows, up to 8.
- Hypothesis with reasoning: if the surface area of the chips is increased, then the rate of reaction will increase, because more acid particles can collide with the marble at the same time [1].
- Independent variable and values: surface area, using at least three forms (large lumps, small chips, powder) of the same mass [1].
- Dependent variable and measurement: volume of carbon dioxide in a gas syringe every 10 s, or loss of mass on a balance [1].
- Controlled variables with methods: same mass of marble (balance), same volume and concentration of acid (measuring cylinder), same temperature (room temperature, checked with a thermometer) [1].
- Equipment: named items with sizes (for example 50 cm³ cylinder, 100 cm³ gas syringe, conical flask, balance, stopwatch) [1].
- Numbered, logical method: measure acid, add marble, start the timer at once, record at set times [1].
- Sufficient data: at least three trials for each form, with a results table with units, so that a mean can be found [1].
- Safety linked to the investigation: eye protection and careful handling of the acid [1].
**[8]**

**9.** Hazard 1: dilute hydrochloric acid can irritate the eyes and skin [1]. Precaution: wear eye protection and rinse splashes with water [1]. Hazard 2 with a matching precaution, for example broken glassware from a conical flask or syringe, so keep apparatus away from the edge of the bench and sweep up glass with a brush [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Any three of: wing length, wing width, mass (paper clips), height of release, type of paper [1].
**(b)** The independent variable is chosen and is measurable, for example the wing length, which can be measured with a ruler to the nearest millimetre [1]. It is a good choice because the effect on fall time can be timed with a stopwatch and the result is easy to measure [1]. Other factors are controlled by using the same paper type, the same mass (clip) and the same release height measured with a metre rule [1]. The same person releases and times each drop and several drops are done for each length [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a control method as "keep the same" with no detail.
- Writing a hypothesis with no direction of change or no scientific reason.
- Using equipment with the wrong precision for the quantity.
- Leaving out repeats or a range of values.
- Giving a safety step that has nothing to do with the investigation.
- Describing steps out of order.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I mark my own design question?**
Compare your answer with each marking point and award only the marks you can find written down. If you did something in your head but did not write it, it does not count.

**What if my hypothesis is the opposite of the one given?**
That is fine if your reasoning is scientific and consistent. The criterion rewards a testable, explained hypothesis, not a particular result.

**Why are marks given for equipment sizes?**
The descriptors ask for appropriate materials and equipment. Sizes and ranges show that the equipment fits the measurement.
