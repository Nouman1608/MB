AQA GCSE Physics (8463) is the separate-science GCSE in physics: a full
qualification in its own right, rather than one part of a combined
science award. It covers eight topics, from energy and electricity to
waves, magnetism and space. Students are entered at Foundation or
Higher tier, and each tier sits two written papers of 1 hour 45 minutes
and 100 marks, each worth 50% of the GCSE.

## How the course is organised

The eight topics divide neatly into two halves, one for each paper.

**Topics 1 to 4 (Paper 1)**

- *Energy:* energy changes in a system and the ways energy is stored
  before and after them; conservation and dissipation of energy; and
  national and global energy resources.
- *Electricity:* current, potential difference and resistance; series
  and parallel circuits; domestic uses and safety; energy transfers;
  and static electricity.
- *Particle model of matter:* changes of state; internal energy and
  energy transfers; and the particle model and pressure.
- *Atomic structure:* atoms and isotopes; atoms and nuclear radiation;
  hazards and uses of radioactive emissions and background radiation;
  and nuclear fission and fusion.

**Topics 5 to 8 (Paper 2)**

- *Forces:* forces and their interactions; work done and energy
  transfer; forces and elasticity; moments, levers and gears; pressure
  in fluids; forces and motion; and momentum.
- *Waves:* waves in air, fluids and solids; electromagnetic waves; and
  black body radiation.
- *Magnetism and electromagnetism:* permanent and induced magnetism and
  magnetic fields; the motor effect; and induced potential,
  transformers and the National Grid.
- *Space physics:* the solar system, the stability of orbital motions
  and satellites; and red-shift.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear, so both papers are sat at the end of the
course, and it is tiered. Each tier has its own version of each paper.

| Paper | Tier codes | Topics | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 8463/1F, 8463/1H | Energy; Electricity; Particle model of matter; Atomic structure | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | 8463/2F, 8463/2H | Forces; Waves; Magnetism and electromagnetism; Space physics | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |

You sit both papers at the same tier. The specification was first
taught in September 2016 and first examined in May and June 2018. It
is one of AQA's three separate science GCSEs, and it shares this
two-paper structure with GCSE Biology (8461) and GCSE Chemistry
(8462). As a separate science, it includes physics-only content that
is not part of AQA's Combined Science: Trilogy (8464) route, so check
which course you are entered for before using any resource.

## How the parts fit together

Energy is the idea that links almost every topic. It is introduced in
Topic 1 and then returns as energy transfers in circuits, internal
energy in the particle model, the energy released in fission and
fusion, work done by forces, and the energy carried by waves. If you
understand energy stores and transfers well, much of the rest of the
course becomes easier to organise.

Other links cross the paper boundary. Electricity on Paper 1 leads
directly into the motor effect and transformers on Paper 2, because
both depend on current and potential difference. Atomic structure and
radiation connect to electromagnetic waves and black body radiation,
and the particle model's treatment of pressure in gases prepares you
for pressure in fluids in the forces topic. Space physics brings
together forces, orbits and waves when it explains satellites and
red-shift.

Two kinds of skill run through the whole course. The first is
quantitative: using equations, units and rearrangement accurately. The
second is practical and explanatory: describing how an investigation
works, interpreting data and explaining results in terms of a physical
model.

## Planning your study

1. **Start with energy.** It gives the vocabulary and the equations that
   later topics rely on.
2. **Work through the Paper 1 topics.** Electricity, the particle model
   and atomic structure can follow in roughly that order.
3. **Move to the Paper 2 topics.** Forces is the largest topic and is
   often split across several weeks; waves, magnetism and
   electromagnetism, and space physics then complete the content.
4. **Revisit Paper 1 topics as you go.** Short mixed quizzes stop the
   earlier material fading while you concentrate on Paper 2.
5. **Practise equations and units regularly.** A few calculations each
   week build accuracy and speed.

Topic-by-topic questions should start straight away. Full papers and
the paper-by-paper exam preparation page are most useful once all
eight topics have been taught, typically in the final months before
the exams.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE Physics?**
There are two written papers at each tier. Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks and carries 50% of the GCSE.

**Which topics are on Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers Energy, Electricity, Particle model of matter and Atomic structure. Paper 2 covers Forces, Waves, Magnetism and electromagnetism, and Space physics.

**Is AQA GCSE Physics tiered?**
Yes. It is offered at Foundation and Higher tier, and each tier has its own Paper 1 and Paper 2.

**How is 8463 different from Combined Science: Trilogy?**
GCSE Physics 8463 is a separate science GCSE and a full qualification on its own. It includes physics-only content that is not part of the Combined Science: Trilogy (8464) route.

**When was AQA GCSE Physics 8463 first examined?**
It was first taught from September 2016 and first examined in May and June 2018.
