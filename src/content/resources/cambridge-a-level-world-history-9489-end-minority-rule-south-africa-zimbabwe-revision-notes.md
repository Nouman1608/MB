**These notes condense End of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe, 1948-94, the second topic of the Paper 4 International option in Cambridge 9489.** For explanation and an annotated conclusion see the [South Africa and Zimbabwe study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-end-minority-rule-south-africa-zimbabwe/); for essays and short questions see the [South Africa and Zimbabwe practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-end-minority-rule-south-africa-zimbabwe-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 8.1 How was the apartheid state established in South Africa and what was its impact from 1948 to 1959?
- 8.2 How did resistance grow in South Africa and how did the authorities respond in the period 1960-77?
- 8.3 Why did apartheid and minority rule in South Africa come to an end, 1978-94?
- 8.4 How was the ending of white minority rule in Zimbabwe achieved in the period 1965-80?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apartheid | "Separateness": the National Party's system of racial separation from 1948 |
| Swart gevaar | "Black danger": the fear the National Party exploited in 1948 |
| Pass laws | Laws requiring Africans to carry documents controlling movement and residence |
| Bantustans / homelands | Ethnic reserves given "self-government" or "independence" |
| Separate development | Verwoerd's presentation of apartheid as separate nationhood |
| Freedom Charter | 1955 statement of the Congress Alliance's aims |
| Umkhonto we Sizwe | "Spear of the Nation": the ANC's armed wing (1961) |
| Black Consciousness | Biko's movement for Black psychological and political self-reliance |
| Total Strategy | Botha's combination of reform and repression against the "total onslaught" |
| UDF | United Democratic Front (1983), an alliance of anti-apartheid groups |
| Third Force | Covert security elements accused of stoking violence, 1990-94 |
| UDI | Rhodesia's Unilateral Declaration of Independence, 1965 |
| Internal Settlement | 1978 agreement between Smith and moderate African leaders |
| Lancaster House | 1979 London agreement ending white minority rule in Zimbabwe |

## 8.1 Why 1948? -- four reasons

War and urbanisation; Afrikaner nationalism and *swart gevaar*; a weak United Party campaign; an electoral system weighted to rural seats.

## 8.2 Resistance and response -- three phases

1. **Non-violence to armed struggle (1960-64):** Sharpeville, bannings, MK, Rivonia.
2. **Quiet decade (mid-1960s to early 1970s):** leaders jailed or exiled; Bantustans expanded.
3. **New generation (1969-77):** SASO, Black Consciousness, Soweto, Biko's death.

## 8.3 Why apartheid ended -- weigh these

- **Internal:** UDF, unions, township revolt, school boycotts.
- **Economic:** recession, sanctions, the 1985 debt crisis, skills shortages.
- **International:** AAM boycotts, US sanctions law (1986), independence of neighbours.
- **End of the Cold War:** the communist threat argument collapsed.
- **Leadership:** de Klerk's decision of 1990; Mandela's commitment to negotiation.

## 8.4 Why white rule ended in Zimbabwe

Guerrilla war intensified after 1972; Mozambique's independence opened a long front; South African and US pressure on Smith; sanctions and the war's cost; white emigration; the failure of the Internal Settlement to win recognition or end the war; front-line states' pressure on the guerrillas; Carrington's diplomacy.

## Exam traps

- **Sharpeville** was a PAC campaign, not an ANC one.
- **Tricameral Parliament** excluded Africans.
- **Muzorewa's government** was not majority rule in practice.
- **1994 results:** ANC about 62 per cent, not two-thirds.
- **International pressure:** mandatory economic sanctions never passed the UN Security Council.

## Self-test

1. Which party won the 1948 election? *The National Party, led by D.F. Malan, with the Afrikaner Party.*
2. What did the Group Areas Act (1950) do? *It assigned racial groups to separate residential and business areas.*
3. Who designed Bantu Education? *Hendrik Verwoerd.*
4. Where was the Freedom Charter adopted? *At the Congress of the People, Kliptown, 1955.*
5. Why did the PAC break from the ANC? *Africanists rejected co-operation with white and Indian allies.*
6. How many were killed at Sharpeville? *69.*
7. What triggered the Soweto uprising? *Compulsory use of Afrikaans as a medium of instruction.*
8. What was Botha's Total Strategy? *Combining limited reform with repression against a supposed "total onslaught".*
9. What did de Klerk announce on 2 February 1990? *The unbanning of the ANC, PAC and Communist Party and Mandela's coming release.*
10. When did Smith declare UDI? *11 November 1965.*
11. Which outside powers backed ZIPRA and ZANLA? *The USSR backed ZIPRA; China backed ZANLA.*
12. How many seats did Lancaster House reserve for whites? *20 of 100, for seven years.*
