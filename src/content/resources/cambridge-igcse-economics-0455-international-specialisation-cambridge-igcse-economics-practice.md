These questions cover section 6.1 (international specialisation) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All countries, firms and figures are invented. Revise first with the [international specialisation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-international-specialisation-cambridge-igcse-economics/) and the [international specialisation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-international-specialisation-cambridge-igcse-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which is an example of specialisation based on superior resource allocation? **[1]**

A: A country with large oil reserves exporting crude oil

B: A country cutting income tax for all workers

C: A country raising tariffs on imported cars

D: A country printing more money

**2.** Which is a disadvantage of national specialisation for an economy? **[1]**

A: Lower prices for consumers

B: Larger markets for exporters

C: Greater exposure to a fall in world demand for its main export

D: Economies of scale in export industries

**3.** Define specialisation at a national level. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how economies of scale can give a country cheaper production methods. **[2]**

**5.** Clothing costs 24 per item to make in Country J. Country K makes it for 15, and transport and trade costs are 7 per item. Calculate the saving per item if Country J imports instead of producing at home. **[2]**

**6.** State two advantages of national specialisation for consumers. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Country L (invented) has a highly educated workforce but little farmland.

**(a)** Suggest one type of product in which Country L may specialise, and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain one disadvantage for Country L of importing most of its food. **[2]**

**8.** A firm in Country M produces ceramic tiles with fixed costs of 300,000 a year and variable costs of 5 per tile. It currently sells 60,000 tiles at home and, after the country specialises in tiles, sells 150,000 including exports.

**(a)** Calculate average cost per tile before and after. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain one benefit to the firm of this fall in average cost. **[2]**

**9.** Explain two disadvantages of national specialisation for firms. **[4]**

**10.** Country N earns 80% of its export revenue from cocoa. Explain why this makes the economy vulnerable. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse how national specialisation could lead to structural unemployment. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether consumers always benefit from their country specialising. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. Oil reserves are a natural resource; B, C and D are policies, not bases for specialisation. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. A, B and D are advantages. **[1]**

**3.** A country concentrating its resources on producing a limited range of goods and services [1], exporting some of them and importing other goods it needs [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Industries producing large quantities for world markets spread fixed costs over more units and can buy inputs in bulk [1], so average cost falls and the country can produce more cheaply than smaller producers [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Import cost = 15 + 7 = 22 [1]; saving = 24 − 22 = **2 per item** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: lower prices [1]; wider choice [1]; better quality. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Software, financial services or medicines [1], because these need skilled, educated labour, which Country L has, and little land [1].

**(b)** Food supplies depend on other countries [1], so a poor harvest abroad, transport problems or a fall in the exchange rate could make food scarce or expensive [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Before: (300,000 + 5 × 60,000) ÷ 60,000 = 600,000 ÷ 60,000 = **10** [1]. After: (300,000 + 5 × 150,000) ÷ 150,000 = 1,050,000 ÷ 150,000 = **7** [1]. Fall of 3 per tile, or 30% [1].

**(b)** The firm can cut its price to win more sales at home and abroad [1], or keep its price and earn a higher profit per tile to fund investment [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Disadvantage 1: firms in industries the country does not specialise in face cheaper imports [1], so they lose sales and may close [1]. Disadvantage 2: export firms depend on demand from abroad [1]; a recession in buying countries cuts their orders and profits [1]. Other creditable disadvantage: imported components may be delayed or become dearer. **[4]**

**10.** A fall in the world price or demand for cocoa [1] would cut most of the country's export earnings at once [1]. Farmers' incomes, jobs in processing and tax revenue would fall together [1], and the country would have less foreign currency to pay for imports [1]. **[4]**

**11.** As the country specialises, industries that cannot compete with imports decline [1]. Their workers lose their jobs [1]. These workers may lack the skills needed in the growing export industries, or live in different regions [1], so they stay unemployed until they retrain or move, which is structural unemployment [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Benefits: goods come from the lowest-cost producers, so prices are lower [1]; consumers can buy goods their country cannot produce, widening choice [1]; competition among specialist producers can raise quality [1]. Limits: if the currency falls, imported goods become dearer [1]; supplies can be interrupted by problems abroad [1]; consumers who work in declining industries may lose income, outweighing lower prices [1]; transport costs can remove the price advantage for some goods [1]. Judgement: most consumers benefit most of the time through lower prices and wider choice, but not always, especially those whose jobs depend on industries that cannot compete [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* Remember that consumers are also workers. Linking lower prices to possible job losses is a strong evaluation point.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to add transport costs to the foreign price.
- Calculating average cost from variable cost only.
- Giving advantages for "the economy" when the question asks about consumers or firms.
- Naming "unemployment" without explaining why it is structural.
- Reaching no judgement in a "Discuss" answer.
