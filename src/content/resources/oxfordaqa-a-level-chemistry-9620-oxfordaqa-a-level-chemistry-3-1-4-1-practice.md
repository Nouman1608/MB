These questions cover **3.1.4.1 Enthalpy change** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Use Ar values H 1.0, C 12.0 and O 16.0 where needed. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [enthalpy change study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-4-1/) or the [enthalpy change revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-4-1-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an exothermic reaction and give the sign of its enthalpy change. **[2]**

**2.** Give the conditions that standard enthalpy changes refer to in this specification. **[2]**

**3.** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the standard enthalpy of formation of each compound.

**(a)** potassium bromide, KBr(s) **[1]**

**(b)** ethane, C₂H₆(g) **[1]**

**(c)** sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄(l) **[1]**

**4.** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the standard enthalpy of combustion of pentane, C₅H₁₂(l). **[2]**

**5.** State the value of ΔfH° for magnesium, Mg(s), and explain your answer. **[2]**

**6.** For N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g), ΔH = +181 kJ mol⁻¹. Give ΔfH° for NO(g), and ΔH for 2NO(g) → N₂(g) + O₂(g). **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Calcium carbonate decomposes on strong heating: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), ΔH = +178 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Draw a labelled enthalpy level diagram for this reaction. **[3]**

**(b)** State what happens to the temperature of the surroundings as the reaction takes place. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your diagram to explain why ΔH is positive. **[1]**

**8.** For each equation, state whether its enthalpy change is a standard enthalpy of combustion, a standard enthalpy of formation, both, or neither. **[4]**

**(a)** H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l)

**(b)** CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(g)

**(c)** 2Na(s) + Cl₂(g) → 2NaCl(s)

**(d)** C(s) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g)

**9.** This question is about the definition of enthalpy of formation.

**(a)** Define the term *standard enthalpy of formation*. **[3]**

**(b)** ΔfH° for carbon as graphite is zero, but for carbon as diamond it is +1.9 kJ mol⁻¹. Explain why. **[2]**

**10.** Butane is used in camping stoves. ΔcH° for butane, C₄H₁₀, is −2877 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the heat released when 1.00 kg of butane burns completely. **[2]**

**(b)** A pan of water needs 250 kJ to bring it to the boil. Calculate the minimum mass of butane needed, assuming all the heat goes into the water. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why more butane than this is needed in practice. **[1]**

**11.** Ethanol, C₂H₅OH, is used as a fuel. ΔcH° for ethanol is −1367 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Write the equation for the standard enthalpy of combustion of ethanol. **[2]**

**(b)** Burning one mole of ethanol vapour releases more heat than burning one mole of liquid ethanol. Explain why, and explain why the liquid is used for ΔcH°. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the heat released per gram of ethanol. **[1]**

**12.** For H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(g), ΔH = −242 kJ mol⁻¹. ΔfH° for H₂O(l) is −286 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Explain why −242 kJ mol⁻¹ is not the standard enthalpy of formation of water. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔH for H₂O(l) → H₂O(g). **[2]**

**(c)** State whether this change is exothermic or endothermic and explain why in terms of forces between molecules. **[1]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Heat energy is transferred from the reacting system to the surroundings [1]; ΔH is negative [1] **[2]**

**2.** A pressure of 100 kPa [1]; and a stated temperature, usually 298 K [1] **[2]**

**3. (a)** K(s) + ½Br₂(l) → KBr(s) [1]

**(b)** 2C(s) + 3H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g) [1]

**(c)** H₂(g) + S(s) + 2O₂(g) → H₂SO₄(l) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Bromine is a liquid in its standard state; Br₂(g) loses the mark in (a).

**4.** C₅H₁₂(l) + 8O₂(g) → 5CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(l): correct formulae and balancing [1]; correct state symbols, with H₂O(l) [1] **[2]**

**5.** Zero [1]; magnesium is an element in its standard state, so forming it from its elements involves no change [1] **[2]**

**6.** ΔfH°(NO) = +181 ÷ 2 = **+90.5 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; ΔH for the reverse = **−181 kJ mol⁻¹** [1] **[2]**

### Section B

**7. (a)** Vertical axis labelled enthalpy [1]; CaCO₃(s) on the lower level and CaO(s) + CO₂(g) on the higher level [1]; upward arrow from reactants to products labelled ΔH = +178 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** It falls (the reaction takes in heat from the surroundings) [1]

**(c)** The products have more enthalpy than the reactant [1] **[5]**

**8. (a)** Both: combustion of hydrogen and formation of water [1]

**(b)** Neither: water is a gas, not in its standard state [1]

**(c)** Neither: two moles of NaCl are formed [1]

**(d)** Formation of methane only [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** The enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed [1]; from its elements [1]; under standard conditions, with all reactants and products in their standard states [1]

**(b)** Graphite is the standard state of carbon, so forming it from the element involves no change [1]; diamond is not the standard state, so converting graphite to diamond is a real change with an enthalpy change [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** n = 1000 ÷ 58.0 = 17.2 mol [1]; heat = 17.24 × 2877 = **4.96 × 10⁴ kJ** [1]

**(b)** n = 250 ÷ 2877 = 0.0869 mol [1]; mass = 0.0869 × 58.0 = **5.04 g** [1]

**(c)** Heat is lost to the surroundings and the pan, or combustion is incomplete [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In (a), the mass must be in grams before dividing by Mr; using 1.00 gives an answer a thousand times too small.

**11. (a)** C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l): formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1]

**(b)** Liquid ethanol needs energy to overcome the forces between molecules before it becomes vapour, so less heat is released overall [1]; standard enthalpies use the standard state, and ethanol is a liquid at 298 K and 100 kPa [1]

**(c)** 1367 ÷ 46.0 = **29.7 kJ g⁻¹** [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** The product is water vapour, H₂O(g) [1]; the standard state of water at 298 K is liquid, so the standard value must form H₂O(l) [1]

**(b)** H₂O(l) → H₂O(g) equals the gas-forming reaction minus the liquid-forming one [1]: ΔH = −242 − (−286) = **+44 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** Endothermic: energy is needed to overcome the hydrogen bonds between water molecules [1] **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions without "one mole", "standard conditions" or "standard states".
- Standard pressure given as 101 kPa or 1 atm.
- Wrong state symbols, especially for bromine, carbon and water.
- Not halving ΔH when an equation forms two moles of product.
- Giving heat released as a negative number.
