**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The oneness of God (Tawhid): "the different types of Tawhid and Shirk, and their meaning and importance".** The prescribed passage for this area is Qur'an 112:1-4, Surah al-Ikhlas. The broader [Islam: Beliefs and Teachings study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-islamiyat-beliefs-and-teachings/) lists Tawhid in one short section. This page stays on that one idea and takes it further: what each type of Tawhid means, what counts as Shirk, why the difference matters, and how to turn all of it into marks.

## What Tawhid means

*Tawhid* comes from an Arabic root meaning "to make one". In Islam it is the belief that Allah is one in His being, in His lordship, in His right to be worshipped and in His names and qualities. Nothing is equal to Him, and nothing shares His power. It is the first thing a Muslim says in the Shahadah ("there is no deity except Allah") and the idea that every prophet is said to have taught.

The word does not appear as a noun in the Qur'an, but the idea runs through it. The clearest short statement is the prescribed passage:

> "Say, 'He is Allah, [who is] One, Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent.'" (Qur'an 112:1-4)

Read it as four claims.

1. **"One"** (*Ahad*): Allah is a single, unique being, not one among several.
2. **"The Eternal Refuge"** (*As-Samad*): every creature depends on Allah, and He depends on nothing.
3. **"He neither begets nor is born"**: Allah has no children and no parents, so He is not part of any family of gods and did not come into being.
4. **"Nor is there to Him any equivalent"**: no being, force or idea can be placed beside Him.

Examiners often reward a candidate who can match a phrase of this passage to a point of belief, so practise doing that.

## The types of Tawhid

Muslim scholars, especially in the Sunni tradition, usually describe Tawhid in three parts. The specification asks for "the different types", so learn all three with a meaning and an example of each.

### 1. Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah: oneness of lordship

*Rabb* means Lord: the one who creates, owns, sustains and controls. This type of Tawhid is the belief that Allah alone does these things. He brought the universe into being, He gives life and death, and He sends rain and provision. The Qur'an asks, "Is there a creator other than Allah who provides for you from the heaven and earth?" (35:3). Believing in Rububiyyah means that no star, ruler, spirit or "luck" runs the world.

**Example in daily life:** a farmer plants seed and waters it, but a Muslim farmer understands that growth is Allah's gift, so thanks go to Allah.

### 2. Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah: oneness of worship

*Ilah* means a deity, something that is worshipped. This type is the belief that Allah alone deserves worship, prayer, trust and total devotion. The opening chapter of the Qur'an states it in every prayer: "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help" (1:5). It means that acts of worship such as prayer, fasting, sacrifice and asking for help in matters beyond human power are directed to Allah only.

**Example in daily life:** a Muslim who is ill goes to a doctor, which is encouraged, but the final plea for healing is made to Allah in prayer.

Many scholars treat this as the most practical type, because it is the one that changes behaviour. The Qur'an says that in Makkah many people already agreed that Allah created the world, yet still worshipped idols alongside Him. They accepted lordship but not exclusive worship. The Prophet (pbuh) was sent to call them to the second.

### 3. Tawhid al-Asma wa as-Sifat: oneness of names and qualities

This is the belief that Allah's names and attributes are unique to Him. Muslims affirm what Allah says about Himself, such as that He hears, sees, knows and is merciful, but they do not compare Him to creation. "There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing" (42:11). A person can be "merciful" in a human, limited way; Allah's mercy is perfect and without limit. Muslims also do not give Allah's special names to anyone else in the way they are used for Him.

| Type | Core belief | One-line proof | Effect on a Muslim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rububiyyah | Allah alone is Creator and Lord | 35:3 | Gratitude and trust |
| Uluhiyyah | Allah alone is worshipped | 1:5 | Prayer and sincerity |
| Asma wa Sifat | Allah's names and qualities are unique | 42:11 | Reverence, no comparisons |

Some books add a fourth category, *Tawhid al-Hakimiyyah*, the oneness of Allah's authority to make law. Use it only if the question allows it. The three-part model is safest for the specification.

## Shirk: associating partners with Allah

*Shirk* means "association": giving a share of Allah's lordship, worship or qualities to someone or something else. Islam teaches that it is the opposite of Tawhid and the gravest of wrongs. The Qur'an says, "Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills" (4:48). In the advice of Luqman to his son, shirk is called "great injustice" (31:13).

Shirk is usually divided into two main kinds.

### Major shirk (*shirk akbar*)

Major shirk takes a person outside the faith if it is believed and practised and not repented from. Examples:

- worshipping idols, as in pre-Islamic Makkah, or any created thing such as the sun, fire or a human being;
- praying to saints or the dead as if they could answer prayer independently of Allah;
- believing that someone else shares Allah's power to create, to give life or to know the unseen;
- claiming that Allah has children, since this places another being alongside Him (this is why 112:3 matters).

### Minor shirk (*shirk asghar*)

Minor shirk does not remove a person from Islam, but it damages the worship it touches and is a serious sin. The usual example is **riya'**, doing good deeds to be seen and praised by people instead of for Allah. A hadith in the Musnad of Ahmad reports that the Prophet (pbuh) feared this for his community more than any other thing. Other examples given by scholars include relying on lucky charms, or saying "if it were not for this person I would have been ruined" as if the helper rather than Allah had the final power.

Some teachers speak of a hidden shirk (*shirk khafi*), which is the subtle form that lives in the intention. For the examination, the main contrast is **major** against **minor**.

| | Major shirk | Minor shirk |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Giving worship or Allah's powers to another | Spoiling sincerity in worship |
| Example | Bowing to an idol | Praying long only to impress |
| Effect | Leaves Islam if not repented | Reduces reward; a sin to repent of |
| Forgiveness | Repentance before death is needed | Repentance and sincerity restore the deed |

## Why Tawhid is important

Plan your "importance" answers around these points, each with its effect on how a Muslim lives.

- **It is the foundation of faith.** The Shahadah, the first pillar, begins with it, and the Hadith of Jibril places belief in Allah first.
- **It frees people from fear of other people.** If only Allah controls benefit and harm, a Muslim need not flatter the powerful or be afraid of superstition.
- **It gives equality.** Every human being is a servant of the same Lord, so no race, class or nation is closer to Allah by birth. The Prophet (pbuh) taught that one's closeness depends on taqwa.
- **It gives purpose and unity.** Muslims face one Qiblah and worship one God, and the whole Ummah shares a single centre.
- **It encourages sincerity.** The Qur'an says, "Say, indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds" (6:162).
- **It builds trust (tawakkul).** A Muslim works hard and then relies on Allah for the result.

The Prophet (pbuh) told Mu'adh ibn Jabal that the right of Allah over His servants is that they worship Him and associate nothing with Him, and that the right of the servants is that He will not punish those who associate nothing with Him (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim). Use this when a question asks about "importance" and invites a Hadith.

## Tawhid in Makkah and in modern life

The first message of the Prophet (pbuh) in Makkah was a call to Tawhid, and the Quraysh leaders opposed it partly because the Ka'aba and its idols brought them prestige and trade. This shows that Tawhid was a social and economic challenge as well as a religious one. When the Prophet (pbuh) entered Makkah in victory, he cleared the Ka'aba of idols, and it was restored as the house of the One God, the house that Ibraheem (AS) and Isma'il (AS) had built.

Today the same idea shows up in everyday choices. A student who studies hard and prays for success shows Tawhid. A person who thinks lucky numbers decide exam results is at risk of slipping towards shirk. A Muslim who gives charity quietly is protecting sincerity from riya'.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** differences between major shirk and minor shirk. **[4]**

> Major shirk means giving to someone else what belongs to Allah alone, such as worshipping an idol [1]. It takes a person outside Islam unless they repent, because it breaks the central belief in the oneness of Allah [1]. Minor shirk is spoiling the sincerity of worship, for example praying at length only so that others will praise you [1]. It is a serious sin that damages the reward of the deed but does not by itself end a person's Islam [1].

Notice the pattern: each point is named, then developed with a reason or effect. The two differences chosen (what it is, and what it does to a person's faith) are separate, which protects the marks.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing "Tawhid means believing in one God" and stopping.** The specification asks for types, meaning and importance. Name the three types.
- **Thinking shirk is only idol worship.** Idols are the clearest example of major shirk; riya' is the standard example of minor shirk.
- **Mixing up Rububiyyah and Uluhiyyah.** Lordship is about what Allah does (creates, sustains). Worship is about what people do (pray, trust, sacrifice).
- **Saying minor shirk takes someone out of Islam.** It is a grave sin, but it is not treated as leaving the faith.
- **Forgetting the passage.** In a 12-mark question, tie the claims you make to the four statements in Surah al-Ikhlas.

## Using the passage in a 12-mark answer

A strong answer to a statement such as "Tawhid is the most important belief in Islam" can follow this plan.

1. **Support the statement:** the Shahadah, the first pillar; every prophet taught it; the passage says Allah is "One".
2. **Explain the effects:** unity, equality, sincerity, freedom from fear.
3. **Examine the other side:** other beliefs, such as the Last Day or prophethood, shape behaviour too, and the six key beliefs depend on each other.
4. **Conclude:** most Muslims would say Tawhid comes first, because the other beliefs are about Allah's messengers, books, angels and decree, and so presuppose Him.

Quote or paraphrase at least two parts of the passage ("One", "Eternal Refuge", "neither begets nor is born", "no equivalent") and say what each shows.
