Subtopic 11.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the α-particle scattering experiment, the nuclear atom, nucleon and proton numbers, isotopes, the three radiations including β⁺, antiparticles, neutrinos, energy spectra, decay equations and the unified atomic mass unit. These notes are condensed for recall. For the full explanations and annotated examples, read the [Atoms, nuclei and radiation study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-atoms-nuclei-and-radiation/); then try the [Atoms, nuclei and radiation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-atoms-nuclei-and-radiation-practice/).

## α-particle scattering: observation → inference

| Observation | Inference |
|---|---|
| Most pass straight through or deflect slightly | Atom is mostly empty space |
| Very few deflect through large angles (some over 90°) | Small, concentrated positive charge repels α-particles |
| Deflecting region not knocked aside | It holds most of the atom's mass |
| Large deflections very rare | Nucleus is tiny compared with the atom |

- Thin gold foil (each α meets at most one nucleus) in a vacuum (no absorption by air).
- Sizes: nucleus about 10⁻¹⁵ to 10⁻¹⁴ m; atom about 10⁻¹⁰ m.

## Nuclear model and notation

- Nucleus: protons (+e) and neutrons (0). Orbital electrons (−e).
- Neutral atom: electrons = protons = Z.
- **Nucleon number A** = protons + neutrons. **Proton number Z** = protons.
- Neutrons N = A − Z.
- Nucleus charge = Ze.
- **Isotopes**: same Z, different N (so different A); same chemical behaviour.

## The radiations

| | α | β⁻ | β⁺ | γ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Helium nucleus (2p + 2n) | Electron | Positron | Electromagnetic radiation |
| Mass | ≈ 4 u | ≈ 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ u | ≈ 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ u | 0 |
| Charge | +2e | −e | +e | 0 |
| Also emitted | — | Electron antineutrino ν̄ | Electron neutrino ν | — |
| Effect on A, Z | A − 4, Z − 2 | A same, Z + 1 | A same, Z − 1 | No change |

- **Antiparticle**: same mass, opposite charge. Positron = antiparticle of the electron.
- β-particles come from the **nucleus**, not from the orbital electrons.

## Decay equations: rules

- Conserve **nucleon number** (top numbers) and **charge** (bottom numbers).
- Include ν̄ with β⁻ and ν with β⁺.
- γ often follows α or β decay as the daughter nucleus loses excess energy.

**Examples to practise reading:**

```
²⁴¹₉₅Am → ²³⁷₉₃Np + ⁴₂α
⁶⁰₂₇Co → ⁶⁰₂₈Ni + ⁰₋₁β + ν̄
```

## Counting decays in a chain

1. Number of α = (change in A) ÷ 4.
2. Z after the α-decays = initial Z − 2 × (number of α).
3. Number of β⁻ = final Z − that value.

Example: ²³⁵₉₂U → ²⁰⁷₈₂Pb: ΔA = 28, so 7 α; Z → 92 − 14 = 78; 82 − 78 = 4 β⁻.

## Energy spectra

| | α | β |
|---|---|---|
| Bodies sharing the energy | 2 (α + daughter) | 3 (β + (anti)neutrino + daughter) |
| Spectrum | Discrete (sharp values) | Continuous, zero up to a maximum |
| Reason | Sharing fixed by energy and momentum conservation | (Anti)neutrino takes a variable share |

## Unified atomic mass unit

```
1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
nucleus mass ≈ A u
electron mass ≈ 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ u
```

Example: a carbon-14 nucleus (A = 14, Z = 6) has mass ≈ 14 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ = 2.32 × 10⁻²⁶ kg and charge 6 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Its 8 neutrons add mass but no charge.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleon | A proton or a neutron |
| Nucleon number A | Total number of protons and neutrons |
| Proton number Z | Number of protons |
| Isotope | Same element, different number of neutrons |
| Nuclide | A particular nucleus, defined by A and Z |
| Antiparticle | Same mass, opposite charge |
| Positron | Antiparticle of the electron |
| Unified atomic mass unit | 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg |

## Exam traps

- Giving observations without inferences, or the other way round.
- "Most α-particles bounced back."
- Confusing A with the number of neutrons.
- Writing the β⁻ particle with a top number of 1.
- Pairing the neutrino with β⁻.
- Forgetting the (anti)neutrino in a β decay equation.
- Saying γ-emission changes the element.
- Using 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (proton mass) when the question asks for u.

## Self-test

1. What does the rare large-angle scattering of α-particles show?
2. Why must the foil be very thin?
3. How many neutrons are in ²³⁵₉₂U?
4. Define isotopes.
5. State the mass in u and charge of a β⁺ particle.
6. Which particle accompanies β⁻ emission?
7. What is an antiparticle?
8. Write the products of the β⁻ decay of ⁶⁰₂₇Co.
9. A nucleus emits a γ-ray. What happens to A and Z?
10. How many α and β⁻ decays take ²³⁵₉₂U to ²⁰⁷₈₂Pb?
11. Why do β-particles have a continuous range of energies?
12. Convert 12 u to kilograms.

**Answers:** 1. A tiny region of concentrated positive charge containing most of the atom's mass. 2. So that each α-particle is deflected by at most one nucleus. 3. 235 − 92 = 143. 4. Forms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. 5. About 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ u; +e. 6. An electron antineutrino. 7. A particle with the same mass but opposite charge to the corresponding particle. 8. ⁶⁰₂₈Ni, a β⁻ particle (⁰₋₁β) and an antineutrino. 9. Neither changes. 10. 7 α and 4 β⁻. 11. The energy released is shared between three bodies, and the (anti)neutrino takes a variable share. 12. 12 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ = 1.99 × 10⁻²⁶ kg.
