## Questions

**1.** Describe the graph of each equation: say what shape it is and one feature that identifies it.

**(a)** y = 7 − x² **[1]**
**(b)** y = −x³ **[1]**
**(c)** y = 5/x **[1]**

**2.** The curve C has equation y = x² + 6x − 27.

**(a)** Solve x² + 6x − 27 = 0 to find where C crosses the x-axis. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the equation of the axis of symmetry. Then find the turning point, stating whether it is a maximum or a minimum. **[3]**
**(c)** Write down the y-intercept. **[1]**

**3.** Here is part of a table for y = x² − 12x + 20.

| x | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 20 | 0 | | −16 | | 0 | |

**(a)** Find the three missing values. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the roots of x² − 12x + 20 = 0 from the table. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the table to solve x² − 12x + 20 = −12. **[1]**
**(d)** One solution of x² − 12x + 20 = −7 is x = 3. Use symmetry to find the other solution. **[2]**

**4.** A parabola crosses the x-axis at x = −2 and x = 6. It passes through the point (0, 12).

**(a)** Find the equation of the parabola as an expanded expression in x. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out where its turning point is. **[2]**

**5.** Consider the curve y = x³ − 36x.

**(a)** Factorise x³ − 36x completely and hence find where the curve crosses the x-axis. **[3]**
**(b)** When x = 3, y = −81. Use the symmetry of the curve to write down y when x = −3, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**6.** The curve y = k/x passes through the point (5, 2).

**(a)** Find k. **[1]**
**(b)** Find y when x = −8. **[1]**
**(c)** State which two quadrants the curve lies in, and explain why it never meets the y-axis. **[2]**

**7.** The curve y = x² − 10x + k has its turning point on the x-axis. Find the value of k. **[3]**

**8.** (Extension only) The curve y = x² + 8x − 5 has a turning point.

**(a)** Express x² + 8x − 5 as (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the turning point as a coordinate pair. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact x-coordinates of the points where the curve crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**9.** (Extension only) A curve has equation y = 5 + 6x − x².

**(a)** Express the equation as y = q − (x − p)², finding p and q. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the turning point is a maximum or a minimum, and give its coordinates. **[1]**
**(c)** State the range of values that y can take. **[1]**

**10.** (Extension only) The curve y = kˣ, where k is positive, passes through the point (2, 6.25).

**(a)** Find k. **[2]**
**(b)** Find y when x = −1. **[1]**
**(c)** Find y when x = 3. **[1]**
**(d)** Write down the coordinates of the point where the curve crosses the y-axis. **[1]**

**11.** (Extension only)

**(a)** Write down the greatest value of sin x and the period of tan x. **[2]**
**(b)** Find every angle x from 0° to 360° for which sin x = −0.5. **[2]**
**(c)** Given that cos 40° = 0.766 (to 3 significant figures), write down cos 140° and another angle between 0° and 360° that has a cosine of 0.766. **[2]**

**12.** (Extension only) The curve D has equation y = x² + 2x − 48.

**(a)** Find the x-coordinates of the points where D crosses the x-axis. **[2]**
**(b)** By completing the square, find the coordinates of the turning point of D. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe a sketch of D, giving every point where it meets the axes and the turning point. **[2]**
**(d)** The line y = −45 meets D at two points. Find their x-coordinates. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** An upside-down (∩-shaped) parabola with its maximum at (0, 7) [1]
**(b)** An S-shaped cubic through the origin that falls from left to right [1]
**(c)** Two separate branches in the top-right and bottom-left quadrants, not touching either axis [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the shape alone is thin; add the feature that separates it from similar graphs, such as the maximum point, the direction or the two branches.

**2. (a)** (x + 9)(x − 3) = 0 [1] so x = **−9 and x = 3** [1]
**(b)** The axis is halfway between the roots: x = (−9 + 3)/2 = **−3** [1]. At x = −3, y = 9 − 18 − 27 = −36 [1]. The turning point is **(−3, −36)**, a minimum, because the x² coefficient is positive [1]
**(c)** **−27** [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A turning point needs both coordinates and a statement of type. Writing only "x = −3" answers half the question.

**3. (a)** x = 4: 16 − 48 + 20 = **−12** [1]; x = 8: 64 − 96 + 20 = **−12** and x = 12: 144 − 144 + 20 = **20** [1]
**(b)** **x = 2 and x = 10** [1]
**(c)** **x = 4 and x = 8** [1]
**(d)** The axis of symmetry is x = 6, because the table has equal values at x = 2 and x = 10 [1]. The other solution is 12 − 3 = **9** [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "solve using the table" part, show which y-value you used. Giving both x-values matters because a quadratic equation often has two solutions.

**4. (a)** y = a(x + 2)(x − 6) [1]. Substituting (0, 12): a × 2 × (−6) = 12, so a = −1 [1]. y = −(x² − 4x − 12) = **−x² + 4x + 12** [1]
**(b)** The axis is x = (−2 + 6)/2 = 2 [1]. y = −4 + 8 + 12 = 16, so the turning point is **(2, 16)** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Starting from the factorised form keeps the working short. Check your final equation by testing the point (0, 12) and one root.

**5. (a)** x(x² − 36) [1] = x(x − 6)(x + 6) [1]. The curve crosses the x-axis at **x = 0, x = 6 and x = −6** [1]
**(b)** The curve is rotationally symmetrical about the origin, so (3, −81) maps to (−3, 81) [1]. **y = 81** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at x(x² − 36) is partial factorisation, so take it to linear factors before reading off roots. Give a reason for the symmetry, not only the value.

**6. (a)** k = xy = 5 × 2 = **10** [1]
**(b)** y = 10/(−8) = **−1.25** [1]
**(c)** k is positive, so the curve is in the **first and third quadrants** [1]. The equation is undefined at x = 0 (you cannot divide by zero), so no point has x = 0 [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation for the y-axis should mention that x = 0 is not allowed; "it just goes up forever" does not explain it.

**7.** A turning point on the x-axis means the curve touches the axis, so the quadratic is a perfect square: x² − 10x + k = (x − a)² [1]. Comparing the x terms, −2a = −10, so a = 5 [1]. Then k = a² = **25** [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the answer by substituting back: x² − 10x + 25 = (x − 5)² has its minimum at (5, 0), on the axis.

**8. (a)** (x + 4)² − 16 − 5 [1] = **(x + 4)² − 21** [1]
**(b)** **(−4, −21)** [1]
**(c)** (x + 4)² = 21 [1], so x = **−4 + √21 and x = −4 − √21** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The turning point is (−p, q), so the sign of the number in the bracket flips. Leaving roots as exact surds is the neater answer; a decimal alone loses the exact form.

**9. (a)** 5 + 6x − x² = −(x² − 6x) + 5 = −[(x − 3)² − 9] + 5 [1] = **14 − (x − 3)²** [1]
**(b)** A **maximum**, at **(3, 14)** [1]
**(c)** **y ≤ 14** [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Take care with the minus sign when factoring out the negative: the 9 changes sign inside the bracket. Expand your answer to check it returns the original expression.

**10. (a)** k² = 6.25 [1], so k = **2.5** (k is positive) [1]
**(b)** 2.5⁻¹ = 1/2.5 = **0.4** [1]
**(c)** 2.5³ = **15.625** [1]
**(d)** **(0, 1)** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute the point into y = kˣ to form an equation first. Remember that kˣ passes through (0, 1) for every positive k, which gives a quick check.

**11. (a)** Greatest value of sin x is **1** [1]; the period of tan x is **180°** [1]
**(b)** The first angle is 210° [1], and the second is 330°. Solutions: **x = 210° and x = 330°** [1]
**(c)** cos 140° = **−0.766** [1], because cos 140° = −cos 40°. Another angle is 360° − 40° = **320°** [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Within 0° to 360°, sin x = −0.5 has two solutions, so give both. Sketching the wave and drawing the line y = −0.5 shows where the second one comes from.

**12. (a)** (x − 6)(x + 8) = 0 [1] so **x = 6 and x = −8** [1]
**(b)** x² + 2x − 48 = (x + 1)² − 1 − 48 [1] = (x + 1)² − 49 [1]. The turning point is **(−1, −49)** [1]
**(c)** U-shaped curve crossing the x-axis at **(−8, 0) and (6, 0)** and the y-axis at **(0, −48)** [1], with a minimum at **(−1, −49)**, symmetrical about x = −1 [1]
**(d)** x² + 2x − 48 = −45 gives x² + 2x − 3 = 0 [1], so (x + 3)(x − 1) = 0 and **x = −3 and x = 1** [1]
**[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch earns credit for the labelled features, so write the coordinates on it. In part (d) the answers must be symmetrical about x = −1, which gives a quick check: −3 and 1 average to −1.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the turning point as one number instead of a coordinate pair.
- Getting the sign wrong in a completed square, so (x + 4)² − 21 is read as a turning point at x = 4.
- Factorising only part of an expression, such as stopping at x(x² − 36).
- Stating one solution when the equation or the interval needs two.
- Joining the two branches of y = k/x or letting them reach an axis.
- Drawing y = kˣ below the x-axis or leaving out (0, 1).
- Forgetting that the cosine and sine curves have two solutions in a full turn.
- Reading values off a table without saying which y-value was used.
- Leaving a sketch unlabelled when intercepts or the turning point were asked for.
