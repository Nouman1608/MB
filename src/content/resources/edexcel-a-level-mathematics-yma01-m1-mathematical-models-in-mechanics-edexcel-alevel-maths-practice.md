These questions cover section 1 of Unit M1: Mechanics 1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): the basic ideas of mathematical modelling, the specified terms (particle, lamina, rigid body, rod, inextensible string, smooth and rough surface, light smooth pulley, bead, wire, peg) and the assumptions behind them. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers that use g to 3 significant figures.

Related: [M1 mathematical models study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-mathematical-models-in-mechanics-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [M1 mathematical models revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-mathematical-models-in-mechanics-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A skier sliding down a slope is modelled as a particle. State what is meant by this. **[1]**

**2.** A plank is modelled as a uniform rod. State what is meant by the word *uniform*, and how it is used when taking moments. **[2]**

**3.** Two particles are joined by a light inextensible string. Explain how you would use **(a)** the fact that the string is light and **(b)** the fact that it is inextensible, when writing equations of motion for the particles. **[2]**

**4.** A string passes over a pulley that is modelled as smooth. State how this assumption is used. **[1]**

**5.** A small ring is modelled as a bead threaded on a smooth wire.

**(a)** State what is meant by a bead in this model. **[1]**

**(b)** State the direction of the force exerted by the wire on the bead. **[1]**

**6.** A road sign is to be held up by two vertical cables. Explain why the sign should be modelled as a lamina rather than as a particle when working out where to attach the cables. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards with speed 12.6 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Using a suitable model, find the greatest height of the ball above the point of projection. **[2]**

**(b)** State two modelling assumptions you have used. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on how the real greatest height would compare with your answer, giving a reason. **[2]**

**8.** A particle A of mass 4 kg lies on a rough horizontal table, with coefficient of friction 0.25. It is attached to a light inextensible string that passes over a light smooth pulley at the edge of the table. The other end of the string is attached to a particle B of mass 3 kg, which hangs freely. The system is released from rest.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain how you used the fact that the pulley is light and smooth. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how you used the fact that the string is inextensible. **[1]**

**9.** A plank AB of length 4 m and mass 20 kg rests horizontally on two supports, one at A and one at C, where AC = 3 m. A child of mass 30 kg stands on the plank 2.5 m from A. The plank is modelled as a uniform rod and the child as a particle.

**(a)** Find the reaction at each support. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain how you used each of the two modelling assumptions. **[2]**

**(c)** The plank is later found to be non-uniform. State what extra information you would need to find the reactions. **[1]**

**10.** A car of mass 1200 kg moves along a straight horizontal road. The engine provides a constant driving force of 3000 N, and the car is modelled as a particle with a constant resistance of 600 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the car. **[2]**

**(b)** The car starts from rest. Use the model to find its speed after 10 s. **[2]**

**(c)** In reality the resistance increases as the speed increases. State, with a reason, whether the real speed after 10 s would be greater or less than your answer. **[2]**

**11.** A bead of mass 0.2 kg is threaded on a smooth straight horizontal wire. A force of 3 N acts on the bead at 30° above the wire, in the vertical plane containing the wire.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the bead along the wire. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the force exerted on the bead by the wire. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how you used the fact that the wire is smooth. **[1]**

**12.** A child slides from rest down a straight slope inclined at 10° to the horizontal. A student models the child as a particle sliding down a smooth plane.

**(a)** Use this model to find the speed of the child after sliding 20 m. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the real speed is likely to be less than your answer, and suggest one refinement to the model. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The skier's size is ignored, so the skier's mass is treated as concentrated at a single point [1].

**2.** The mass of the plank is spread evenly along its length [1], so its weight acts at the midpoint of the plank [1].

**3. (a)** Light: the tension is the same throughout the string, so the same tension T appears in each particle's equation [1].

**(b)** Inextensible: the particles have the same magnitude of acceleration, so the same a appears in each equation [1].

**4.** There is no friction at the pulley, so the tension is the same on both sides of the pulley [1].

**5. (a)** A particle with a hole through it, so it can be threaded on the wire and slide along it [1].

**(b)** Perpendicular to the wire [1].

**6.** A lamina keeps its shape and area, so its weight acts at a definite point of the sign (its centre of mass) and the cables are at definite distances from it [1]. A particle has no size, so the positions of the cables could not be found [1].

**7. (a)** v² = u² + 2as with v = 0 and a = −9.8: 0 = 12.6² − 19.6s [1], so **s = 8.1 m** [1].

**(b)** Any two of: the ball is a particle; air resistance is ignored; g is constant at 9.8 m s⁻² [2].

**(c)** The real height would be **less than 8.1 m** [1], because air resistance would act downwards while the ball rises, increasing its deceleration [1].

**8. (a)** Friction on A = 0.25 × 4 × 9.8 = 9.8 N [1].

B: 3g − T = 3a [1]. A: T − 9.8 = 4a [1].

Adding: 29.4 − 9.8 = 7a, so **a = 2.8 m s⁻²** [1].

T = 9.8 + 4(2.8) = **21 N** [1].

**(b)** The tension is the same on both sides of the pulley, so the same T is used in the equations for A and for B [1].

**(c)** A and B have the same magnitude of acceleration, so the same a is used in both equations [1].

**9. (a)** Weights: plank 20g = 196 N at 2 m from A (uniform); child 30g = 294 N at 2.5 m from A.

Moments about A: 3R_C = 196 × 2 + 294 × 2.5 [1] = 392 + 735 = 1127, so **R_C = 376 N** [1].

Resolving vertically: R_A + R_C = 490 [1], so **R_A = 114 N** [1].

**(b)** Uniform rod: the plank's weight acts at its midpoint, 2 m from A [1]. Child as a particle: the child's weight acts at a single point, 2.5 m from A [1].

**(c)** The position of the plank's centre of mass (where its weight acts) [1].

**10. (a)** 3000 − 600 = 1200a [1], so **a = 2 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 10 [1] = **20 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** **Less** [1]. As the speed increases the resistance increases, so the resultant force and the acceleration decrease [1].

**11. (a)** Along the wire: 3 cos 30° = 0.2a [1], so **a = 13.0 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** Perpendicular to the wire: R + 3 sin 30° = 0.2 × 9.8 [1], so R = 1.96 − 1.5 = **0.46 N** [1].

**(c)** There is no friction, so there is no force along the wire opposing the motion [1].

**12. (a)** Acceleration down the slope = 9.8 sin 10° [1].

v² = 0 + 2(9.8 sin 10°)(20) = 68.07 [1], so **v = 8.25 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Friction from the slope and air resistance would oppose the motion, so the acceleration would be smaller [1]. Refinement: model the slope as rough, with a coefficient of friction μ, or include air resistance [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Restating the word ("it doesn't stretch") instead of giving its effect ("the accelerations are equal").
- Swapping the effects of light and inextensible.
- Saying a particle has no mass.
- Listing an assumption that the question has already stated.
- Saying the real answer is "different" without saying larger or smaller, and why.
- Giving a refinement that is too vague to be tested, such as "consider all forces".
