These questions cover section 13.2 (Investigation of limiting factors) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. They practise Paper 4 explanations and the planning, analysis and evaluation skills of Paper 5. All data are invented for the exercise. Revise first with the [13.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-investigation-of-limiting-factors-cambridge-alevel/) and the [13.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-investigation-of-limiting-factors-cambridge-alevel-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** DCPIP is added to an illuminated chloroplast suspension. Which statement is correct?

A: DCPIP turns blue because it is oxidised by oxygen from photolysis.

B: DCPIP turns colourless because it accepts electrons from the light-dependent stage.

C: DCPIP turns colourless because it reacts with triose phosphate.

D: DCPIP stays blue because it is reduced. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why increasing carbon dioxide concentration can increase the rate of photosynthesis. **[2]**

**3.** Relative light intensity is calculated as 10 000 ÷ d², where d is the lamp distance in cm. Calculate the relative light intensity at 8 cm and at 16 cm, and state how many times greater the intensity is at 8 cm. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why leaves are ground in an ice-cold buffer solution when preparing a chloroplast suspension. **[2]**

**5.** On a graph of rate of photosynthesis against light intensity, the curve rises and then levels off. Which statement about the level part of the curve is correct?

A: Light intensity is the limiting factor.

B: A factor other than light intensity is limiting.

C: Photosynthesis has stopped.

D: All the chlorophyll has been denatured. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why counting bubbles from a pondweed shoot is less reliable than measuring the volume of gas collected. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** The rate of photosynthesis of a plant was measured at different light intensities at two carbon dioxide concentrations, at 25 °C.

| Light intensity / arbitrary units | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate at 0.04 % CO₂ / arbitrary units | 0 | 8 | 14 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| Rate at 0.10 % CO₂ / arbitrary units | 0 | 8 | 15 | 23 | 28 | 29 |

**(a)** State the limiting factor at a light intensity of 10 arbitrary units. Give a reason from the data. **[2]**

**(b)** State the limiting factor at a light intensity of 40 arbitrary units and 0.04 % CO₂. Explain your answer using the data. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the rate rises as light intensity increases from 0 to 20 arbitrary units. **[2]**

**8.** Tubes of chloroplast suspension with DCPIP were lit through coloured filters of equal light intensity. The time for the DCPIP to become colourless was recorded.

| Filter colour | Blue | Green | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time / s | 180 | 900 | 150 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate for each colour as 1000 ÷ time, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the differences in rate between the green filter and the other two. **[3]**

**(c)** State two variables, other than light intensity, that should be kept constant. **[2]**

**9.** Pondweed in hydrogencarbonate solution was kept at different temperatures with a bright light. Gas was collected for 4 minutes in a capillary tube of internal radius 0.5 mm.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Length of gas bubble / mm | 6 | 13 | 22 | 15 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of gas production at 30 °C in mm³ min⁻¹. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the results between 10 °C and 30 °C and between 30 °C and 40 °C. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why changing temperature would have little effect on the rate if the experiment were repeated in very dim light. **[1]**

**10.** Plan an investigation, using an aquatic plant, into the effect of carbon dioxide concentration on the rate of photosynthesis. **[6]**

**11.** A student measured oxygen production by pondweed with a lamp at distances from 50 cm down to 5 cm, in a laboratory lit by sunlight from windows. No other precautions were taken.

**(a)** Identify two sources of error in this procedure and suggest an improvement for each. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the volume of oxygen collected is described as a net rate. **[1]**

**12.**

**(a)** Explain why the rate at which methylene blue decolourises measures the light-dependent stage only. **[2]**

**(b)** State the purpose of a tube of chloroplast suspension and methylene blue kept in darkness. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Carbon dioxide is the substrate fixed by rubisco with RuBP [1]. More CO₂ means more fixation per second, so more GP and TP are made, provided light and temperature are not limiting [1].

**3.** At 8 cm: 10 000 ÷ 64 = 156; at 16 cm: 10 000 ÷ 256 = 39.1 [1]. The intensity at 8 cm is **4 times** greater [1].

**4.** Cold slows enzymes released from broken cells that could damage the chloroplasts [1]. The buffer keeps the pH constant so chloroplast proteins are not denatured [1].

**5.** B [1]

**6.** Bubbles vary in size, so equal counts do not mean equal volumes of gas [1].

**7.**

**(a)** Light intensity [1]. At 10 units the rate is the same (8) at both CO₂ concentrations, so CO₂ is not limiting there [1].

**(b)** Carbon dioxide concentration [1]. At 0.04 % CO₂ the rate stays at 16 from 30 to 50 units, so more light has no effect, but at 0.10 % CO₂ the rate at 40 units is higher (28) [1].

**(c)** More light means more photoactivation of chlorophyll in the light-dependent stage [1]. So more ATP and reduced NADP are made, allowing more GP to be reduced to TP in the Calvin cycle [1].

**8.**

**(a)** Blue 5.6 and red 6.7 [1]; green 1.1 [1].

**(b)** Chlorophylls absorb blue and red light strongly but absorb little green light [1]. With green light, fewer chlorophyll molecules are photoactivated, so fewer electrons are emitted [1]. Fewer electrons are available to reduce DCPIP, so it takes longer to decolourise [1].

**(c)** Temperature [1]; volume and concentration of DCPIP and of chloroplast suspension [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Volume = π × 0.5² × 22 = 17.3 mm³ [1]; rate = 17.3 ÷ 4 = **4.32 mm³ min⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** From 10 °C to 30 °C, molecules have more kinetic energy, so more enzyme–substrate collisions occur in the Calvin cycle and the rate rises [1]. 30 °C is near the optimum [1]. Above it, enzymes such as rubisco begin to denature, active sites change shape and the rate falls [1].

**(c)** In dim light, light intensity is the limiting factor, so the Calvin cycle is held back by the supply of ATP and reduced NADP, not by enzyme activity [1].

**10.** Independent variable: use a range of at least five sodium hydrogencarbonate concentrations, for example 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 %, as the CO₂ source [1]. Dependent variable: collect oxygen in a capillary tube or gas syringe over a fixed time, and calculate volume per minute [1]. Keep light intensity constant with a fixed lamp distance or light meter, in a darkened room [1]. Keep temperature constant with a water bath or heat shield, checked with a thermometer [1]. Use the same plant, or pieces of the same mass and length, and leave it to acclimatise for a few minutes in each solution [1]. Repeat at least three times for each concentration, calculate means and plot rate against concentration [1].

**11.**

**(a)** Heat from the lamp at short distances raises the temperature [1]; place a glass tank of water as a heat shield, or use an LED, and monitor temperature [1]. Sunlight adds to the lamp light and changes during the day [1]; carry out the experiment in a darkened room [1].

**(b)** Some oxygen made in photosynthesis is used in the plant's respiration, or dissolves, so only the excess is collected [1].

**12.**

**(a)** Methylene blue accepts electrons emitted by chlorophyll in the light-dependent stage [1]. Because it takes these electrons, NADP is not reduced and the Calvin cycle is not involved in the colour change [1].

**(b)** To show that light is needed for the colour change [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the limiting factor without giving evidence from the data.
- Explaining a light effect without linking it to ATP and reduced NADP.
- Writing that DCPIP detects oxygen.
- Giving controlled variables without saying how they are controlled in a plan.
- Suggesting vague improvements such as "be more accurate".
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in πr².
