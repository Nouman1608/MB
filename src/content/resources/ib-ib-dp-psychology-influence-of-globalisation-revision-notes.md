These notes cover the HL extension to the sociocultural approach: **"the influence of globalization on individual attitudes, identities and behaviour"** (IB DP Psychology, first assessment 2019). The core material it builds on is in the [sociocultural approach revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-psychology-sociocultural-approach-revision-notes/); full explanations and an essay plan are in the [globalisation study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-psychology-influence-of-globalisation/).

## Recall in one minute

- Globalisation: growing interconnection of societies through trade, migration, media and digital communication.
- The psychological question: how contact with a global culture changes individual attitudes, identities and behaviour.
- People can now experience culture contact **without migrating**.
- Identity outcomes range from integrated bicultural identities to identity confusion.
- Effects are not all negative: global identity can increase cooperation beyond national borders.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | the process by which societies become more interconnected economically, culturally and technologically |
| Global culture | shared practices, media, brands and values spread across countries, often in English |
| Bicultural identity | holding a local identity and a global identity at the same time (Arnett, 2002) |
| Identity confusion | uncertainty about identity when local and global cultures conflict |
| Self-selected culture | a group a person chooses to join because it shares their values |
| Globalisation-based acculturation | cultural change through contact with global culture at home, without migration (Chen, Benet-Martínez and Bond, 2008) |
| Bicultural identity integration | the sense that one's two cultural identities fit together rather than conflict |
| Natural experiment | a study of a change the researcher did not cause, such as the arrival of television |
| Etic measure | a tool developed in one culture and applied to another |

## Research table

| Study or theory | Key point | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arnett (2002) | bicultural identity, identity confusion, self-selected cultures, longer emerging adulthood | organises a large field; explains varied outcomes | a review, not a test; hard to falsify |
| Chen, Benet-Martínez and Bond (2008) | bicultural identity integration associated with better adjustment, including for people exposed to global culture at home in Hong Kong | extends acculturation research beyond migrants | correlational; one setting |
| Becker et al. (2002) | after television arrived in a Fijian province, more adolescent girls showed disordered eating attitudes, and girls linked this to admiring television characters | natural experiment with interviews adding credibility | different girls in each sample; other social changes; Western questionnaire |
| Buchan et al. (2009) | people more exposed to globalisation contributed more to a global group in a public goods game across six countries | measures behaviour with real money; many cultures | artificial game; exposure linked to education and income |

## Linking to the core

- **Acculturation (Berry):** integration is again linked to well-being; globalisation adds acculturation without migration.
- **Social identity theory:** a global in-group can form; Buchan's findings fit identification with a larger group.
- **Social cognitive theory:** television characters as models whose behaviour and appearance are observed and imitated, as the Fiji interviews suggest.
- **Cultural dimensions:** globalisation may shift societies on dimensions such as individualism, but national averages hide individual variation.

## Evaluation checklist

- Which aspect of globalisation does the study measure: media, migration, trade or technology?
- Can other social changes explain the result?
- Were the same people followed over time, or different people compared?
- Was the measure developed in the culture being studied?
- Does the interpretation assume that change is "Westernisation" or that it is harmful?

## Model paragraph (evaluation)

> Becker et al. (2002) suggest that exposure to global television influenced eating attitudes among
> Fijian adolescent girls, since disordered eating scores rose after television arrived and girls
> themselves said they wanted to look like television characters. The interviews strengthen the case,
> because the link was made by the participants rather than only by the researchers. However, the two
> samples were different girls and Fiji was changing in other ways at the same time, so television
> cannot be isolated as the cause. The study therefore shows a strong association within a real cultural
> change rather than proof that global media causes disordered eating.

## Exam traps

- **Answering with core content only.** Berry's model alone does not address globalisation.
- **Economics instead of psychology.** Keep the focus on individuals.
- **Treating Fiji as proof of cause.** It is strong evidence of association within a natural experiment.
- **One-sided essays.** Include a positive or neutral effect, such as cooperation or integrated identities.
- **SL students.** The extension is HL only; SL questions do not ask about it.

## Self-test

1. Define globalisation.
2. Why can globalisation produce acculturation without migration?
3. Name two consequences of globalisation for identity proposed by Arnett (2002).
4. What is bicultural identity integration?
5. What did Chen, Benet-Martínez and Bond (2008) find about it?
6. Why did Becker et al. (2002) count as a natural experiment?
7. State two findings of Becker et al. (2002).
8. Give two limitations of Becker et al. (2002).
9. What did participants choose between in Buchan et al. (2009)?
10. What was the main finding of Buchan et al. (2009)?
11. Which core theory helps explain why Fijian girls copied television characters?
12. At which level is this extension examined?

**Answers**

1. The process by which societies become more interconnected through trade, migration, travel, media and digital communication.
2. Global media, brands and online networks bring another culture into people's own homes and communities.
3. Any two: bicultural identity; identity confusion; self-selected cultures; a longer emerging adulthood.
4. The sense that one's two cultural identities fit together rather than conflict.
5. It was associated with better psychological adjustment, including for people experiencing global culture at home.
6. The researchers studied a change they did not cause: television arriving in a province in 1995.
7. A rise in the proportion of girls with scores indicating disordered eating attitudes; reports of self-induced vomiting to control weight in the later sample; girls describing a wish to look like television characters.
8. Any two: different girls in each sample; other social changes at the same time; self-report; a questionnaire developed in Western countries.
9. Keeping money, contributing to a local group or contributing to a global group.
10. People more exposed to globalisation, and people in more globalised countries, contributed more to the global group.
11. Social cognitive theory: observational learning from models.
12. HL only.
