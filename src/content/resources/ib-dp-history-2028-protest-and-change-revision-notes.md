Revision notes for the focused study **Protest and change** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Both examples are required: **feminism in the USA (1960 to 1979)** and **revolution in Tunisia (1989 to 2015)**. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-protest-and-change/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-protest-and-change-practice/).

## Paper 1 in one box

| Question | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| First | Explain how the content of sources A and B can be used to answer the inquiry question | 6 |
| Second | Analyse how the context of source C influences how it can be used | 6 |
| Third | Examine how the perspectives in all the sources can be used | 12 |

Total 24 marks, 1 hour 15 minutes.

## The three inquiry questions

1. What led to the emergence of protest movements?
2. How did the protest movement challenge authority?
3. What changes did the protest movement achieve, and with what limitations?

## Feminism in the USA (1960 to 1979)

**1. Emergence**

- Reactions to domesticity: women's wartime and postwar work, but pay gaps and the image of the contented housewife.
- Contraception: the pill approved in 1960; access varied by state and marital status.
- Early feminist literature: de Beauvoir's *The Second Sex*; Friedan's *The Feminine Mystique* (1963).

**2. Challenge to authority**

- Protest and activism: marches, the Women's Strike for Equality (August 1970), the Miss America protest (1968).
- Groups: NOW (1966), the National Women's Political Caucus (1971), and the many small groups of the Women's Liberation Movement.
- Mass media: coverage spread the message, but often mocked it; *Ms.* magazine (1971).

**3. Changes and limits**

- Political and legal: Title IX (1972), *Roe v. Wade* (1973), the Equal Rights Amendment passed by Congress (1972) but not ratified in time.
- Economic: the Equal Pay Act (1963), Title VII of the Civil Rights Act (1964); pay and employment gaps continued.
- Marginalised women: African American and working-class women criticised a focus on white, middle-class concerns (for example the Combahee River Collective, 1974).

## Revolution in Tunisia (1989 to 2015)

**1. Emergence**

- Repression and censorship under the Ben Ali regime (in power from 1987 to 2011).
- Failed economic reform and unemployment, especially among young graduates and in inland regions.
- Influence and spread of ideas: satellite television, the internet and union networks.

**2. Challenge to authority**

- The December Revolution: Mohamed Bouazizi's self-immolation (17 December 2010), protests, and Ben Ali's flight (14 January 2011).
- Parties: Ennahda (Islamist; largest share in October 2011) and Nidaa Tounes (founded 2012; won 2014 elections).
- Social media: spread images and organised protests.

**3. Changes and limits**

- Political and legal: Ben Ali ousted; the 2014 Constitution.
- Social: youth radicalisation and terrorism (the Bardo and Sousse attacks in 2015).
- Women: rights from the 1956 Personal Status Code, defended and written into the 2014 Constitution.

## Comparing the two examples

| Point | USA | Tunisia |
|---|---|---|
| Authority challenged | Laws, institutions and social norms | An authoritarian regime |
| Form | Organisations, lobbying, protest | Mass street protest, rapid collapse of regime |
| Role of media | Print, television, magazines | Satellite TV, social media |
| Main achievements | Legal protections (Title IX, Roe) | Regime change and constitution |
| Limits | Backlash, unratified ERA, exclusion of some women | Economic hardship, terrorism |

## Skills checklist

**Question 1.** Use both sources and pick specific content. A single source caps the mark at 3.

**Question 2.** Use the context of source C: author, date, audience, purpose. Link each to how the source can be used.

**Question 3.** State each source's perspective, compare, and combine. All three sources are needed for the top band; two sources cap the mark at 9.

## Worked example: a question 3 opening

Inquiry question: What changes did the protest movement achieve, and with what limitations?

Source A (constructed for practice): a NOW press statement welcoming a law banning sex discrimination in education. Source B (constructed for practice): a pamphlet by an opponent of the Equal Rights Amendment warning that it will harm homemakers.

"Source A is written by an advocacy group that celebrates legal change as proof of progress, so its perspective is that the movement is achieving important reforms. Source B comes from an opponent who fears that further change will harm women who work at home, so its perspective is that the proposed changes are unwelcome and a threat. They disagree about whether change is beneficial, but both show that the movement achieved real legal gains and that it faced organised opposition. Together they suggest that change was real and also limited by a conservative backlash."

## Linking to concepts

- **Cause and consequence:** long-term conditions and short-term triggers (Bouazizi's act).
- **Continuity and change:** laws changed faster than attitudes and economies.
- **Perspectives:** supporters, opponents and the excluded.
- **Significance:** the lasting effect on politics and society.

## Common mistakes

- Saying social media alone caused the Tunisian revolution.
- Treating the US movement as one organisation.
- Forgetting that the ERA was not ratified in time.
- Describing the events and not the sources.

## Markbands at a glance

| Question | Lower bands | Top band |
|---|---|---|
| First (6 marks) | Describes the sources, with little explanation of how the content answers the inquiry question | Explains how a range of specific content from both sources can be used, with specific and developed connections |
| Second (6 marks) | Describes the context with minimal analysis | Analyses how the context influences how the source can be used, supported by relevant references to the context |
| Third (12 marks) | Outlines perspectives briefly, with minimal links to the question | Insightful understanding of the perspectives of all the sources, effectively examining similarities and differences, with relevant references |

A useful routine is to spend about 10 minutes on the first question, 10 on the second and 20 to 25 on the third, leaving a little time to check that every answer refers back to the inquiry question.

## Self-test

1. Who wrote *The Feminine Mystique*?
2. What did Title IX ban?
3. When was *Roe v. Wade* decided?
4. What triggered the protests in Tunisia in December 2010?
5. When did Ben Ali leave Tunisia?
6. Name the two parties named in the syllabus.
7. Name one limit on change in Tunisia after 2011.

**Answers.**

1. Betty Friedan (1963).
2. Sex discrimination in education programmes that receive federal funding.
3. 1973.
4. The self-immolation of Mohamed Bouazizi in Sidi Bouzid on 17 December 2010.
5. 14 January 2011.
6. Ennahda and Nidaa Tounes.
7. Any one: continuing unemployment; terrorist attacks; political tensions and assassinations.
