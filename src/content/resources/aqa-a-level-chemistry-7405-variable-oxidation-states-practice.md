These questions cover section **3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Variable oxidation states study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-variable-oxidation-states/) and the [Variable oxidation states revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-variable-oxidation-states-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values Fe = 55.8, and for hydrates H₂O = 18.0.

## Questions

**1.** Zinc is added to an acidified solution of vanadate(V) ions. Give the formula and colour of the vanadium species with oxidation states +5, +4, +3 and +2. **[4]**

**2.** Write the half-equation for the reduction of VO²⁺ to V³⁺ in acidic solution, and the overall equation for its reduction by zinc. **[3]**

**3.** Electrode potentials: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn, E = –0.76 V; V³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ V²⁺, E = –0.26 V; Sn⁴⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Sn²⁺, E = +0.15 V.

**(a)** Show that zinc can reduce V³⁺ to V²⁺. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that Sn²⁺ cannot reduce V³⁺ to V²⁺. **[2]**

**4.** Iron(II) hydroxide, a pale green solid, turns brown when exposed to air. Use the data Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ (+0.77 V) and Fe(OH)₃ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe(OH)₂ + OH⁻ (–0.56 V) to explain why iron(II) is more easily oxidised in alkaline conditions than in acidic conditions. **[3]**

**5.** The electrode potential for [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ + e⁻ ⇌ [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is +0.36 V, compared with +0.77 V for [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺. State and explain what this shows about the effect of the ligand. **[2]**

**6.** Tollens' reagent is used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones.

**(a)** Give the formula of the silver-containing ion in Tollens' reagent. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the half-equation for its reduction. **[1]**

**(c)** State what is observed with propanal and with propanone. **[2]**

**7.** In a titration, acidified potassium manganate(VII) is added to a solution containing iron(II) ions.

**(a)** State why dilute sulfuric acid is used instead of hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the colour change at the end point and explain why no indicator is added. **[2]**

**8.** 25.0 cm³ of an iron(II) sulfate solution needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII) for complete reaction. Calculate the concentration of the iron(II) sulfate solution. **[3]**

**9.** An iron tablet is dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and made up to 250 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion needs 14.20 cm³ of 0.00400 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). Calculate the mass of iron in the tablet. **[4]**

**10.** 25.0 cm³ of a sodium ethanedioate solution requires 20.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). Calculate the concentration of the ethanedioate solution. The equation is 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂. **[3]**

**11.** 3.92 g of hydrated ammonium iron(II) sulfate, (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·xH₂O, is dissolved in water and made up to 100 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion needs 25.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). Calculate the Mᵣ and the value of x. Mᵣ of (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂ = 284. **[4]**

**12.** Hydrogen peroxide in hair bleach reacts with manganate(VII): 2MnO₄⁻ + 5H₂O₂ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 5O₂. 10.0 cm³ of bleach is diluted to 250 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion needs 21.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). Calculate the concentration of hydrogen peroxide in the bleach in mol dm⁻³. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why a manganate(VII) titration with ethanedioate is started with the ethanedioate solution warmed. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** +5: VO₂⁺, yellow [1]; +4: VO²⁺, blue [1]; +3: V³⁺, green [1]; +2: V²⁺, violet [1]. **[4]**

**2.** VO²⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → V³⁺ + H₂O [1]; 2VO²⁺ + 4H⁺ + Zn → 2V³⁺ + 2H₂O + Zn²⁺ [2: 1 for combining the half-equations with 2 electrons, 1 for the balanced overall equation]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** E(cell) = E(V³⁺/V²⁺) – E(Zn²⁺/Zn) = –0.26 – (–0.76) [1] = +0.50 V, which is positive, so the reaction is feasible [1]

**(b)** E(cell) = –0.26 – (+0.15) = –0.41 V [1]; negative, so Sn²⁺ cannot reduce V³⁺ [1]. **[4]**

**4.** In acid, E for Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ is +0.77 V, which is positive, so Fe²⁺ is not easily oxidised by oxygen [1]; in alkaline conditions E for Fe(OH)₃/Fe(OH)₂ is –0.56 V, much more negative [1]; so Fe(OH)₂ is a stronger reducing agent and is more easily oxidised by oxygen from the air to brown Fe(OH)₃ [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The potential is less positive with the cyanide ligand [1]; cyanide stabilises the iron(III) state more than the iron(II) state, so it is harder to reduce iron(III), showing that the ligand affects the redox potential [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ [1]

**(b)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃ [1]

**(c)** Propanal gives a silver mirror [1]; propanone gives no change [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Hydrochloric acid would be oxidised by manganate(VII), forming chlorine, and would use up some of the titrant [1]

**(b)** The solution changes from colourless (or pale yellow) to a permanent pale pink [1]; manganate(VII) is purple and Mn²⁺ is almost colourless, so it acts as its own indicator [1]. **[3]**

**8.** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 0.02240 = 4.48 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(Fe²⁺) = 5 × 4.48 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.24 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 2.24 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0896 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.00400 × 0.01420 = 5.68 × 10⁻⁵ mol [1]; n(Fe²⁺) in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 5.68 × 10⁻⁵ = 2.84 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n in 250 cm³ = 2.84 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; mass = 2.84 × 10⁻³ × 55.8 = **0.158 g** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 0.02000 = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(C₂O₄²⁻) = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 1.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0400 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 0.02500 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol, so n(Fe²⁺) in 25.0 cm³ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; in 100 cm³ = 1.00 × 10⁻² mol [1]; Mᵣ = 3.92 ÷ 1.00 × 10⁻² = **392** [1]; x = (392 – 284) ÷ 18.0 = **6** [1]. **[4]**

**12.** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 0.02100 = 4.20 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(H₂O₂) = 4.20 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.05 × 10⁻³ mol in 25.0 cm³ [1]; in 250 cm³ this is 1.05 × 10⁻² mol, all from the 10.0 cm³ of bleach [1]; c = 1.05 × 10⁻² ÷ 0.0100 = **1.05 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In dilution problems, track which volume each amount of substance came from. The 10.0 cm³ of bleach is the volume that contained all the hydrogen peroxide.

**13.** The reaction is slow at room temperature at the start [1]; warming to about 60 °C speeds it up until Mn²⁺ forms, which then catalyses the reaction [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 1 : 1 for manganate(VII) and iron(II); the ratio is 1 : 5.
- Using 1 : 5 for ethanedioate; the ratio is 2 : 5.
- Not scaling the aliquot up to the full volume.
- Using hydrochloric acid in the titration.
- Not giving the E(cell) working in a feasibility question.
