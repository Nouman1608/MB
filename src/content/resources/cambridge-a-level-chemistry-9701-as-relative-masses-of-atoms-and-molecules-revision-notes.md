## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unified atomic mass unit, u | One twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom (about 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g) |
| Relative isotopic mass | Mass of one atom of an isotope relative to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | Weighted mean mass of an atom of an element relative to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | Weighted mean mass of a molecule relative to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Relative formula mass | Weighted mean mass of a formula unit relative to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Weighted mean | Average that allows for the abundance of each isotope |
| Water of crystallisation | Water molecules held in a hydrated crystal, shown after a dot in the formula |

## What to remember about the definitions

- Every definition compares a mass with **1/12 of a carbon-12 atom** (= 1 u).
- **Weighted mean** in Ar, Mr and relative formula mass; **not** in relative isotopic mass.
- No units: they are ratios of masses.
- Only carbon-12 has a relative isotopic mass of exactly 12; others are close to, but not exactly, their mass numbers.
- Mr: molecular substances. Relative formula mass: ionic and giant structures (NaCl, MgO, SiO₂).

## Converting between real mass and u

    relative mass = mass of one particle (g) ÷ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g
    mass of one particle (g) = relative mass × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g

Example: a ⁵⁶Fe atom of mass 9.288 × 10⁻²³ g has relative isotopic mass 55.93.

## Relative atomic mass from abundances

    Ar = Σ (relative isotopic mass × % abundance) ÷ 100

| Element | Isotopes and abundances | Ar |
|---|---|---|
| Mg | ²⁴Mg 79.0 %, ²⁵Mg 10.0 %, ²⁶Mg 11.0 % | 24.3 |
| Li | ⁶Li 7.5 %, ⁷Li 92.5 % | 6.9 |
| Cl | ³⁵Cl 75.8 % (34.97), ³⁷Cl 24.2 % (36.97) | 35.45 |

**Method: abundance from Ar (two isotopes)**
1. Let fraction of the lighter isotope = x; heavier = 1 − x.
2. Write mass₁x + mass₂(1 − x) = Ar.
3. Solve for x; convert to a percentage.

Example: boron, ¹⁰B and ¹¹B, Ar 10.8: x = 0.20, so 20 % ¹⁰B.

**Sense check:** Ar lies between the lightest and heaviest isotopic masses, nearest the most abundant.

## Relative molecular and formula masses

| Formula | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| H₂SO₄ | 2(1.0) + 32.1 + 4(16.0) | 98.1 |
| CaCO₃ | 40.1 + 12.0 + 3(16.0) | 100.1 |
| Mg(NO₃)₂ | 24.3 + 2(14.0 + 48.0) | 148.3 |
| (NH₄)₂SO₄ | 2(14.0 + 4.0) + 32.1 + 64.0 | 132.1 |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 159.6 + 5(18.0) | 249.6 |
| Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | 106.0 + 10(18.0) | 286.0 |

**Method: formula mass**
1. List each element and count its atoms, multiplying through any bracket.
2. Add the hydrate water as n × 18.0.
3. Multiply counts by Ar and add.

**Percentage water in a hydrate** = mass of water in formula ÷ formula mass × 100. CuSO₄·5H₂O: 36.1 %.

## Exam traps

- Missing "weighted mean" or "one twelfth" in a definition.
- Writing "weighted mean" for relative isotopic mass.
- Giving relative masses units.
- Calling NaCl's value a relative molecular mass.
- Multiplying only the first atom in a bracket.
- Leaving out the water in a hydrate.
- Rounding too early: keep at least one extra figure until the final answer.

## Self-test

**1.** Define the unified atomic mass unit.

**2.** Define relative atomic mass.

**3.** How does the definition of relative isotopic mass differ from that of relative atomic mass?

**4.** Why is the relative formula mass, not the relative molecular mass, used for calcium oxide?

**5.** Neon contains ²⁰Ne (90.5 %), ²¹Ne (0.3 %) and ²²Ne (9.2 %). Calculate Ar to 3 significant figures.

**6.** An element has isotopes of mass 85 and 87, and Ar = 85.5. What percentage is the isotope of mass 85?

**7.** Calculate the relative formula mass of Al₂(SO₄)₃ (Al 27.0, S 32.1, O 16.0).

**8.** Calculate the relative formula mass of Ca₃(PO₄)₂ (Ca 40.1, P 31.0, O 16.0).

**9.** Calculate the percentage by mass of water in Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.

**10.** One atom has a mass of 3.82 × 10⁻²³ g. Calculate its relative isotopic mass.

**11.** Why does relative atomic mass have no units?

**12.** Why is the Ar of chlorine 35.5 and not 36?

## Self-test answers

**1.** One twelfth of the mass of one atom of carbon-12.

**2.** The weighted mean mass of an atom of an element relative to one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

**3.** Relative isotopic mass is for one atom of a single isotope, so it is not a weighted mean; Ar is the weighted mean over all the isotopes of the element.

**4.** Calcium oxide is ionic and contains no molecules, so its formula unit is used.

**5.** (20 × 90.5 + 21 × 0.3 + 22 × 9.2) ÷ 100 = 20.2.

**6.** 85x + 87(1 − x) = 85.5, so x = 0.75: 75 %.

**7.** 2(27.0) + 3(32.1 + 64.0) = 342.3.

**8.** 3(40.1) + 2(31.0 + 64.0) = 310.3.

**9.** Formula mass 286.0; water 180.0; 180.0 ÷ 286.0 × 100 = 62.9 %.

**10.** 3.82 × 10⁻²³ ÷ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ = 23.0 (sodium-23).

**11.** It is a ratio of two masses, so the units cancel.

**12.** Chlorine is a mixture of ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl, and the weighted mean of their masses, allowing for abundance, is 35.5.
