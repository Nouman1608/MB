## Section A

**1.** Describe how a colorimeter should be prepared before the absorbance of a series of coloured solutions is measured. **[3]**

**2.** Standard solutions of a coloured substance give these absorbances.

| Concentration / mg cm⁻³ | 0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.00 | 0.12 | 0.24 | 0.36 | 0.48 |

An unknown solution has an absorbance of 0.30. Calculate its concentration. **[3]**

**3.** A student makes a two-fold serial dilution from a 0.80 mol dm⁻³ solution, using 5 cm³ of solution and 5 cm³ of distilled water at each step. State the concentration of the first four dilutions and name one precaution that prevents errors building up through the series. **[3]**

**4.** An eyepiece graticule is calibrated using a stage micrometer. 100 graticule divisions line up with 60 stage divisions, and each stage division is 10 μm. Calculate the length represented by one graticule division, and the length of a cell that measures 12 graticule divisions. **[3]**

**5.** A student draws a cell and the drawing is 84 mm long. The real cell is 0.070 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student's drawing of a plant tissue is made in coloured pencil with shading. The labels are written with lines that cross each other and have arrowheads, and there is no title. Give **three** ways in which the drawing should be improved. **[3]**

**7.** A chromatography run separates a mixture of amino acids. The solvent front is 9.0 cm from the origin. Spot A is 2.7 cm and spot B is 5.4 cm from the origin. Standards run on the same paper have Rf values of 0.31 for glycine and 0.58 for leucine.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf values of spots A and B. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the amino acids, giving your reasoning. **[2]**

**8.** A sample is tested with Benedict's reagent after heating and stays blue. A second portion of the sample is boiled with dilute hydrochloric acid, neutralised with sodium hydrogencarbonate and tested again with Benedict's reagent, giving a brick-red precipitate. What does this show about the sample? Explain what happened in the second test. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why each of the following is done when using aseptic technique.

**(a)** The inoculating loop is heated until red hot before use. **[1]**

**(b)** The lid of the Petri dish is lifted only slightly. **[1]**

**(c)** The dish is sealed with short strips of tape, not all the way round. **[1]**

**10.** In an antibiotic test, the clear zones around two discs have diameters of 16 mm and 10 mm. Calculate the area of each clear zone and the ratio of the areas, and state what the ratio suggests. **[3]**

**11.** A survey uses 20 quadrats, each of area 0.25 m², placed at random in a field of 600 m². A total of 80 daisies are counted. Estimate the population of daisies in the field. **[3]**

**12.** A student wants to study how the number of plant species changes from the edge of a woodland to 100 m inside it. State which sampling method is best and explain why. **[3]**

**13.** Describe how a potometer should be set up to make sure that the measurements are valid. **[3]**

## Answers

**How these answers work.** Marking points are shown in brackets. The mark total for each question is the sum of the points. Alternative wording that carries the same meaning is credited.

**1.** Select a filter of the colour complementary to the colour of the solutions [1]. Set the instrument to zero (zero absorbance) using a blank containing the solvent only, in the same type of cuvette [1]. Use the same cuvette (or matched cuvettes), wipe the outside, and keep the same volume for every reading [1]. **[3]**

**2.** The gradient is 0.48 ÷ 2.0 = 0.24 absorbance units per mg cm⁻³ [1]. Concentration = absorbance ÷ gradient = 0.30 ÷ 0.24 [1] = **1.25 mg cm⁻³** [1]. (Reading across from 0.30 on a calibration curve gives the same value.) **[3]**

**3.** 0.40, 0.20, 0.10 and 0.050 mol dm⁻³ [2]. A precaution, any one [1]: use a clean pipette or syringe, or rinse it, between tubes; mix thoroughly before taking each sample; measure the volumes accurately. **[3]**

**4.** 60 stage divisions = 60 × 10 = 600 μm [1]. One graticule division = 600 ÷ 100 = **6.0 μm** [1]. The cell = 12 × 6.0 = **72 μm** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object [1] = 84 ÷ 0.070 = **× 1200** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* If the image is in mm and the object is in μm, convert one so both are in the same unit.

**6.** Any three [1 each]: use a sharp pencil with clear, continuous lines, and no shading or colour; use ruled label lines that do not cross; remove arrowheads; draw the labels horizontally; add a title; add the magnification or a scale bar; make the drawing larger or with accurate proportions. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Rf of A = 2.7 ÷ 9.0 = **0.30** [1]. Rf of B = 5.4 ÷ 9.0 = **0.60** [1].

**(b)** A is glycine, as 0.30 is close to 0.31 [1]. B is leucine, as 0.60 is close to 0.58 [1]. **[4]**

**8.** The sample contains a non-reducing sugar, such as sucrose [1]. In the first test no reducing sugar was present, so the Benedict's reagent stayed blue [1]. Boiling with acid hydrolysed the non-reducing sugar into reducing sugars (monosaccharides), and after neutralisation these gave a precipitate [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** To sterilise the loop and kill microorganisms that would contaminate the culture [1]. **(b)** To reduce the entry of microorganisms from the air into the dish (and of the culture into the room) [1]. **(c)** So that oxygen can enter and the growth of anaerobic pathogens is not encouraged [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Area = π r². For 16 mm: π × 8² = **201 mm²** [1]. For 10 mm: π × 5² = **78.5 mm²** [1]. The first area is 2.6 times the second (201 ÷ 78.5 = 2.56), which suggests that the first antibiotic is more effective at inhibiting bacterial growth [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Mean per quadrat = 80 ÷ 20 = 4 [1]. Density = 4 ÷ 0.25 = 16 per m² [1]. Estimated population = 16 × 600 = **9600** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** A belt transect (a line transect with quadrats at regular intervals) [1]. The conditions change gradually from the edge to the interior, such as light and soil moisture, so systematic sampling along a line shows the change [1]. Random sampling would not show the pattern along the gradient [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Cut the shoot under water, at a slant, and assemble the apparatus under water so that no air enters the xylem [1]. Make sure the joints are watertight, using petroleum jelly, and dry the leaves of the shoot [1]. Introduce one air bubble into the capillary, and measure how far it moves in a fixed time with a ruler, resetting it between readings [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: forgetting to set the colorimeter to zero with a blank.
- Question 2: treating the absorbance as the concentration.
- Question 4: using the wrong number of divisions in the ratio, or forgetting the unit.
- Question 5: dividing by 70 without converting μm and mm.
- Question 7: calculating Rf from the solvent distance divided by the solute distance.
- Question 9: saying "to keep it sterile" without a reason.
- Question 10: using the diameter in πr².
- Question 11: forgetting to divide by the area of a quadrat.
- Question 12: choosing random sampling for a gradient.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to recall the Rf formula?**
Yes. Rf is one of the biological formulae that you must recall: distance moved by the solute divided by distance moved by the solvent.

**Why does a graticule need calibrating?**
Its divisions have no fixed size. They represent different lengths with each objective lens, so each must be calibrated against a stage micrometer.
