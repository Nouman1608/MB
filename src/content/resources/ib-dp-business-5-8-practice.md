## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *innovation*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between research and development. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the difference between market pull and technology push as sources of new products. **[2]**

**4.** Quillon Audio has developed: (i) a new method of moulding speaker cones; (ii) the name "Quillon Pulse" for its range; (iii) the illustrated booklet sold with each speaker. State the form of intellectual property protection that would normally apply to each. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one reason why a patent may not fully protect Quillon's moulding method. **[2]**

**6.** Saffrey Toys ran its R&D pipeline as follows last year: 60 ideas screened at 1,000 each; 12 concept tested at 15,000 each; 3 prototypes at 90,000 each; 1 product launched.

**(a)** Calculate the total R&D spending. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of screened ideas that were launched, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**7.** Outline two factors that may lead a business to reduce its R&D spending. **[4]**

**8.** Distinguish between incremental and disruptive innovation, using one example of each. **[4]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** Merrow Health makes walking aids for older people. Staff noticed that users often leave their walking frames out of reach when they sit down. Merrow developed a frame that folds and clips to a chair.

**(a)** Explain whether this is an example of a known or a latent unmet need. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain two benefits to Merrow of developing products that address unmet needs. **[4]**

**10.** Dunmore Pharma spends 18% of its revenue on R&D. A new chief executive wants to cut this to 12% to raise dividends. Revenue is 250 million.

**(a)** Calculate the reduction in R&D spending. **[2]**

**(b)** Discuss the proposal to cut R&D spending. **[8]**

**11.** Tarrock Cameras makes high-quality cameras and has improved each model a little every year. A rival has launched a low-cost device that does many of the same jobs and is selling quickly among younger customers. Tarrock's board must choose between continuing incremental improvements and setting up a separate team to develop a new, low-cost product.

**(a)** Explain why the rival's product could be a disruptive innovation. **[2]**

**(b)** Recommend which option Tarrock should choose. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The successful commercial use [1] of a new idea, product, service or process [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* An invention that never reaches customers is not an innovation. Including "commercial use" separates the two.

**2.** Research is work to discover new knowledge [1], whereas development turns that knowledge into a working product, service or process that can be produced and sold [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Market pull starts from evidence of what customers need and develops a product to meet it [1], whereas technology push starts from a new capability created by R&D and then looks for a market use [1]. **[2]**

**4.** (i) Patent [1]. (ii) Trademark [1]. (iii) Copyright [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A patent publishes details of the invention [1], so rivals can study it and develop a different method that achieves the same result without breaking the patent. Also accept: enforcement is costly or difficult in other countries; protection ends after a limited period [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 60 × 1,000 + 12 × 15,000 + 3 × 90,000 = 60,000 + 180,000 + 270,000 [1] = **510,000** [1].

**(b)** 1 ÷ 60 × 100 = **1.7%** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The single launched product has to cover the cost of all 59 failed ideas, which is the point an analysis question would build on.

**7.** Any two, each outlined. Shortage of finance: when profit or cash falls, R&D is easy to cut because the loss of future products is not felt at once [1][1]. Shareholder pressure: owners wanting higher dividends now may push managers to cut long-term spending [1][1]. Also accept: a stable market with little change; products early in their life cycle; loss of government incentives. **[4]**

**8.** Incremental innovation makes small improvements to an existing product or process [1], for example a longer-lasting battery in an existing phone model [1]. Disruptive innovation creates a product or business model that changes how a market works [1], for example streaming services replacing the rental of films on disc [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** A latent need [1]: users did not ask for a frame that clips to a chair; staff identified the problem by observing behaviour, and users may not have realized a solution was possible [1]. Also accept "known" if justified by users complaining about the problem. **[2]**

**(b)** Less direct competition: as no current product meets the need, Merrow can charge a premium price before rivals respond [1][1]. Customer loyalty and reputation: a product that solves a real problem for older users strengthens Merrow's brand with care homes and families, helping sales of its other products [1][1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** 18% − 12% = 6% of 250 million [1] = **15 million** [1].

**(b)** For the cut: 15 million a year becomes available for dividends or debt reduction [1]; some R&D projects may have low chances of success, so cutting the weakest improves efficiency [1]. Against: pharmaceutical firms depend on new products because patents on existing drugs expire, after which rivals can copy them [1]; fewer projects means a thinner pipeline and possibly no replacements in later years [1]; skilled researchers may leave and be hard to rehire [1]; rivals who keep investing may gain an advantage [1]. Conditions: depends on how full the current pipeline is and how soon existing patents end [1]. Judgement: a cut of this size is risky in an R&D-intensive industry; a smaller, targeted cut of weak projects would protect the pipeline while freeing some cash [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers use the industry context. Linking patent expiry to the need for a pipeline is more convincing than a general list of R&D benefits.

**11. (a)** The rival's product is cheaper and simpler and appeals to customers Tarrock does not serve, younger buyers [1]; if it improves, it could take mainstream customers and change how the market works [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Incremental option: low risk and builds on Tarrock's skills and loyal customers [1]; but it protects only the high-end segment, which may shrink if the low-cost device improves [1]. Separate team: allows Tarrock to compete for younger customers and reduces the risk of being overtaken [1]; a separate team avoids the new product being judged by the standards of the existing range [1]; but it is costly, uncertain and may take sales from Tarrock's own cameras [1]; Tarrock lacks experience of low-cost markets [1]. Recommendation: set up the separate team while continuing incremental improvement on the main range [1], because the rival's fast sales suggest the market is shifting and doing nothing new carries the greater long-term risk, provided Tarrock can fund both [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* "Recommend" needs a clear choice. Combining options is acceptable only if the answer explains why both are affordable and necessary.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "invention" where the question asks about innovation.
- Matching the wrong form of IP to an asset.
- Claiming patents guarantee profit or last for ever.
- Giving R&D benefits without the uncertainty of returns.
- Ignoring the industry when judging R&D spending.
- Ending an AO3 answer without a judgement and the conditions it rests on.

## Frequently asked questions

**What do these questions cover?**
Section 5.8 of the IB DP Business Management guide (first assessment 2024): the importance of R&D, unmet needs, intellectual property protection and incremental and disruptive innovation. The section is HL only.

**How should you answer a question on cutting R&D spending?**
Use the industry context, weigh short-term financial gains against the loss of future products, and give a judgement that says what it depends on.

**Are these IB past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions written for practice and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of IB papers.
