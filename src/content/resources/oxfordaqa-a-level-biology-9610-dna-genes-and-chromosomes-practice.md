## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the three components of a nucleotide. **[3]**

**2.** A sample of double-stranded DNA is 32 % thymine. Calculate the percentage of guanine. **[2]**

**3.** Which statement about tRNA is correct?

A: It is a double helix.

B: It contains thymine.

C: It is a single strand folded into a clover-leaf shape that carries an anticodon.

D: It is a long, straight polynucleotide. **[1]**

**4.** What is meant by a locus? **[1]**

**5.** State the enzyme that breaks hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs during DNA replication. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** Compare the DNA in the nucleus of a human cell with the DNA in a bacterial cell. **[3]**

**7.** A eukaryotic gene is 5,400 base pairs long. Its introns total 2,640 base pairs. The exons together code for a polypeptide, and the last codon is a stop codon.

**(a)** Calculate the number of base pairs in the exons. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the polypeptide is shorter than the length of the gene suggests. **[2]**

**8.** A fragment of DNA contains 2,400 base pairs. 35 % of its bases are guanine or cytosine. An adenine-thymine pair is held by two hydrogen bonds and a guanine-cytosine pair by three hydrogen bonds.

**(a)** Calculate the number of guanine-cytosine pairs and the number of adenine-thymine pairs. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total number of hydrogen bonds holding the two strands together. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how DNA replicates. **[5]**

**10.** In an experiment, the strands of a DNA molecule were labelled so that every original strand could be recognised. The DNA was allowed to replicate four times in an unlabelled medium.

**(a)** Calculate how many DNA molecules there were after four rounds of replication. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the molecules that contained an original strand. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the result shows that replication is semi-conservative. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how the structure of DNA makes it suitable for storing genetic information and for being copied accurately. **[4]**

**12.** Compare the structure of a molecule of mRNA with a molecule of DNA. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A pentose (five-carbon sugar) [1], a phosphate group [1], a nitrogen-containing organic base [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Adenine = thymine = 32 %, so A + T = 64 % [1]. C + G = 36 %, so guanine = 36 / 2 = 18 % [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Halve the remainder at the end. A frequent slip is to stop at 36 %.

**3.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The fixed position of a gene on a particular DNA molecule (chromosome) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** DNA helicase [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Human nuclear DNA is linear, bacterial DNA is circular [1]. Human DNA is associated with histone proteins, bacterial DNA is not associated with proteins [1]. Human DNA is longer and contains introns and non-coding repeats, bacterial DNA is shorter and has few or no introns [1]. Human DNA is inside a nucleus, bacterial DNA is free in the cytoplasm [1; any three points]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 5,400 - 2,640 = 2,760 base pairs [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 2,760 / 3 = 920 codons [1]. One is the stop codon, which does not code for an amino acid, so 919 amino acids [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Introns do not code for amino acids [1] and are removed from the pre-mRNA before translation, so only the exons are translated [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** G-C pairs = 0.35 x 2,400 = 840 [1]. A-T pairs = 2,400 - 840 = 1,560 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** (840 x 3) + (1,560 x 2) = 2,520 + 3,120 [1] = 5,640 hydrogen bonds [1]. **[2]**

**9.** The double helix unwinds, and DNA helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds between the base pairs [1], so the two strands separate and each acts as a template [1]. Free DNA nucleotides are attracted to the exposed bases on each strand [1] and pair with them by complementary base pairing (A with T, C with G) [1]. DNA polymerase joins the adjacent nucleotides, forming a new strand, so that two molecules are formed, each with one original and one new strand (semi-conservative) [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** 2^4 = 16 molecules [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The two original strands are in two different molecules [1]. 2 / 16 x 100 = 12.5 % [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Each new molecule always contains one original strand and one new strand after the first round [1], and the original strands are never lost, but remain in just two molecules out of 16 as the new strands multiply [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The base sequence along the molecule stores information in a code [1]. The sugar-phosphate backbone is strong and protects the bases inside the helix [1]. Hydrogen bonds are weak individually, so the strands can be separated during replication, but many hydrogen bonds make the molecule stable [1]. Complementary base pairing means that each strand acts as a template, so the sequence can be copied accurately [1]. **[4]**

**12.** mRNA is single-stranded, whereas DNA is a double helix [1]. mRNA contains ribose and uracil, whereas DNA contains deoxyribose and thymine [1]. mRNA is shorter than a DNA molecule, and there are no hydrogen bonds between its strands, since it has only one [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping at C + G in a base percentage question, without halving.
- Confusing the jobs of helicase and DNA polymerase.
- Saying that introns are "junk" without saying that they are removed before translation.
- Giving hydrogen bonds as the bonds along one strand.
- Describing the DNA of mitochondria as linear or attached to histones.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.1.7 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**Do I need to remember that A-T has two hydrogen bonds and G-C has three?**
The specification asks for hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs. The numbers are standard knowledge, and a question that depends on them gives them to you, as in question 8.
