Quick-recall notes for the three named studies in **3.2.2 Social influence** of OxfordAQA International
GCSE Psychology (9218): "Piliavin's subway study, Latane and Darley's smoke study and Levine's football
supporter study." For full descriptions and a model answer, use the
[Studies of Bystander Behaviour study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-bystander-studies/);
for the factors they test, see the
[factors affecting bystander intervention revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-bystander-intervention-factors-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Field experiment** | Experiment in a natural setting with the independent variable manipulated by the researcher |
| **Laboratory experiment** | Experiment in a controlled, artificial setting |
| **Confederate** | Someone working for the researcher who pretends to be a participant or victim |
| **Naive participant** | A real participant who does not know the true aim |
| **Pluralistic ignorance** | Using others' calm to conclude there is no emergency |
| **Diffusion of responsibility** | Responsibility shared among bystanders, so each feels less obliged |
| **Arousal: cost-reward model** | Bystanders reduce unpleasant arousal by choosing the lowest-cost response |
| **Social identity** | The part of a person's sense of self that comes from group membership |
| **Ecological validity** | How far findings reflect real-life behaviour |

## Latané and Darley: smoke study (1968)

- **Method:** laboratory experiment; male Columbia students completing a questionnaire; smoke through
  a vent.
- **Conditions:** alone; with two passive confederates; three naive participants.
- **Results:** alone 75% reported; with passive confederates 10%; in 38% of three-person groups
  someone reported.
- **Conclusion:** others' calm leads people to misinterpret an emergency (pluralistic ignorance).
- **For:** high control; standardised procedure.
- **Against:** male students only; artificial; deception and possible distress.

## Piliavin: subway study (1969)

- **Method:** field experiment; New York subway; seven-and-a-half-minute non-stop stretch; teams of
  victim, model and two observers.
- **IV:** cane (sober) or drunk victim; black or white victim.
- **Participants:** about 4,450 passengers; 103 trials.
- **Results:** cane victim helped spontaneously in 62 of 65 trials, drunk victim in 19 of 38; cane
  victim helped faster; most helpers men; slight same-race helping in the drunk condition; **no**
  diffusion of responsibility in larger groups.
- **Conclusion:** helping depends on costs and rewards: arousal: cost-reward model.
- **For:** high ecological validity; large, varied sample.
- **Against:** less control; no consent, no right to withdraw, no debrief.

## Levine: football supporter study (2005)

- **Method:** field experiment after questionnaires; Manchester United supporters; a jogger
  (confederate) falls and holds his ankle.
- **IV:** jogger's shirt: Manchester United, Liverpool, or plain.
- **Study 1 (team identity prominent):** own-team shirt helped by over nine in ten; rival or plain
  shirt by about a third or fewer.
- **Study 2 (football-fan identity prominent):** either team's shirt helped much more than plain.
- **Conclusion:** people help those who share their group identity, and the group can be widened.
- **For:** realistic incident; hidden observers; practical message about shared identity.
- **Against:** small, male, single-club samples; deception.

## Which study for which factor?

| Factor | Study | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Presence of others | Latané and Darley | Passive others greatly reduced reporting |
| Cost of helping | Piliavin | Higher-cost (drunk) victim helped less |
| Similarity to victim | Levine | Shared identity increased helping |
| Challenge to the bystander effect | Piliavin | No reduction in helping with larger groups in a clear emergency |

## Comparing methods

| | Control | Ecological validity | Consent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laboratory (smoke) | High | Lower | Consented to a study, but deceived about its aim |
| Field (subway) | Lower | High | None |
| Field (football) | Moderate | Fairly high | Consented to the questionnaire study, deceived about the fall |

## Ten-second summaries

- **Smoke:** passive others made people ignore smoke, so others' calm changes how we read a situation.
- **Subway:** drunk victims were helped less, so people count the cost of helping.
- **Football:** own-group victims were helped more, so who counts as "us" shapes helping.

## Exam traps

- **75% / 10%** = smoke study. **62 of 65 / 19 of 38** = subway study. Keep them separate.
- Piliavin found **no** diffusion of responsibility.
- The subway and football studies are **field** experiments; the smoke study is a **laboratory**
  experiment.
- Levine's key IV is the **shirt**; the key extra finding is that a **broader identity** widened help.
- Ethical evaluation must name the issue and explain it for that study.

## Self-test

1. What type of study was Latané and Darley's smoke study?
2. What were participants doing when the smoke appeared?
3. What percentage reported the smoke when alone?
4. How did passive confederates affect reporting?
5. What were the two victim types in Piliavin's study?
6. How many times was the cane victim helped spontaneously, out of how many trials?
7. What did Piliavin find about group size?
8. Name the model Piliavin proposed.
9. What was the independent variable in Levine's Study 1?
10. What changed in Levine's Study 2, and what was the effect?
11. Give one ethical issue in Piliavin's study.
12. Give one strength of the smoke study and one weakness.

**Answers:**
1. A laboratory experiment.
2. Filling in a questionnaire about the problems of urban life.
3. 75%.
4. Reporting fell to 10% of participants.
5. A sober victim with a cane, and a victim who appeared drunk.
6. 62 out of 65.
7. Larger groups did not help less; there was no diffusion of responsibility.
8. The arousal: cost-reward model.
9. The shirt the fallen jogger wore: Manchester United, Liverpool, or plain.
10. Participants were made to think of themselves as football fans in general; the jogger was then
    helped much more in either team's shirt than in the plain shirt.
11. Any one: no informed consent; no right to withdraw; no debrief; possible distress.
12. Strength, any one: high control; standardised procedure. Weakness, any one: male students only;
    artificial setting; deception and possible distress.
