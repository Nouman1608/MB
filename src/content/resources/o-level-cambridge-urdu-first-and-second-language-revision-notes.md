## Which specification are you sitting?

Confirm this with your centre before revising — the two Paper 1s are
built differently, and revising the wrong one wastes time.

| | 3247 First Language | 3248 Second Language |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 length | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Paper 1 marks | 50 (25 + 25) | 50 (across 5 exercises) |
| Paper 1 shape | Part 1 Reading, Part 2 Writing | 5 sequential exercises |
| Paired with | Paper 2 Texts (Unseen Passage, Poetry, Prose) | Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation |
| Pitched at | Native/near-native fluency | Learned additional language |

## 3247 Paper 1 — the two parts

**Part 1 Reading (25 marks).** A non-literary text of roughly 400
words, followed by comprehension questions of differing lengths.
Marked against four reading objectives:

- **R1** understand and convey information
- **R2** understand, order and present facts, ideas and opinions
- **R3** evaluate information and select what is relevant
- **R4** recognise implicit meanings and attitudes

**Part 2 Writing (25 marks).** ONE composition of 300–400 words,
chosen from argumentative, discursive, descriptive and narrative
titles — each title drawn from a *different* topic area, so the
choice of title also fixes the topic:

1. Health and fitness (food and diet, sport)
2. The world of youth (music, culture, technology, fashion, family)
3. Education and training (school and college, work, professions)
4. The world we live in (current affairs, environment, travel, media)

Marked on three strands, each with its own levels ladder:
**task achievement and content** (10 marks), **structure** (5 marks),
**language** (10 marks). Top band language needs a confident range of
complex sentence structures and wide, accurate vocabulary; top band
content needs insight and full relevance, not just correctness.

## 3248 Paper 1 — the five exercises

| Exercise | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short-answer questions — skim/gist reading of a short functional text | 8 |
| 2 | Multiple matching — match sentences to paragraphs | 9 |
| 3 | Note-making — brief notes under supplied headings on a longer text | 9 |
| 4 | Summary — ≤100 words on the Exercise 3 text, own words | 10 |
| 5 | Writing exercise — ~150-word functional piece from a prompt (purpose, format and audience specified) | 14 |

Reading carries roughly 60% of Paper 1's marks and Writing roughly
40% — the opposite emphasis from Paper 2, which is almost entirely
Writing. Source texts are drawn from contemporary topics: conservation
and environment, culture and history, science and technology, music
and the arts, human achievement.

## Register — heavily credited in both

| Task | Register | Address |
|---|---|---|
| Formal letter, report, article | Formal / neutral | **aap** throughout |
| Functional email/message (3248 Ex. 5) | Matches the specified audience | Check the brief |
| Message to a friend | Informal | **tum** acceptable |
| Discursive/argumentative composition | Formal but engaged | Consistent stance |

Drifting register mid-answer costs marks even when every sentence is
individually correct — in 3248 this sits inside **W5**, "use
appropriate register and style/format for the given purpose and
audience", one of the five writing objectives actually named in the
syllabus.

## The accuracy points that decide the grade

- **Gender agreement** — noun, adjective **and** verb must all agree.
- **Verb forms** — tense, person, and a consistently held respect level.
- **Postpositions** — ne, ko, se, mein, par. The **ergative *ne*** is
  required with transitive perfective verbs.
- **Izafat** and compound constructions in formal writing.
- **Script accuracy** — dots and diacritics; a misplaced dot changes
  the word entirely.
- **Punctuation** to Urdu convention.

3248 marks this explicitly under **W4** (control of punctuation and
spelling); 3247 folds it into the Language strand.

## Reading method — both specifications

1. Read the **questions or task headings first**.
2. Read the passage once for overall sense.
3. Locate the relevant section and read **around** it — inference
   answers (3247's R4; 3248's Exercise 1 gist questions) are rarely
   contained in one sentence.
4. Answer **in your own words** wherever a summary or note-making
   task requires it — 3248's Exercise 4 explicitly instructs this.
5. Match answer length to the **marks available** for that part.

## Exam traps

- **3247**: choosing a Part 2 title without checking which topic area
  it belongs to, then running out of things to say. Writing under 300
  words or drifting well past 400.
- **3248**: exceeding the ~100-word limit on the Exercise 4 summary,
  which wastes time for no extra credit. Copying phrases from the
  Exercise 3 text instead of using your own words.
- **Both**: mixing respect levels within one piece; losing gender
  agreement under time pressure; omitting the ergative *ne*; leaving
  no time to check the script at the end.

## Self-test

1. How many marks does 3247 Part 2 Writing carry, and across which
   three strands?
2. Name the four 3247 Part 2 topic areas.
3. How many exercises does 3248 Paper 1 contain, and which one is
   worth the most marks?
4. Which 3248 assessment objective explicitly names register and
   audience?
5. Why does a misplaced dot matter in Urdu script?

**Answers:** 1. 25 marks, across task achievement and content (10),
structure (5) and language (10). 2. Health and fitness; the world of
youth; education and training; the world we live in. 3. Five
exercises; Exercise 5, the writing exercise, carries the most at 14
marks. 4. W5 — "use appropriate register and style/format for the
given purpose and audience." 5. Urdu letters are distinguished by the
number and position of dots; changing them produces a different
letter and therefore a different word.
