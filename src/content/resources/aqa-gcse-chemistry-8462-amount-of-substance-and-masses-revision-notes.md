These notes condense **4.3.2 Use of amount of substance in relation to masses of pure substances** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content.
Sections 4.3.2.1 to 4.3.2.4 are **Higher Tier only** (HT only), as is the second skill in 4.3.2.5. Calculating the
mass of solute in a given volume of solution, in g/dm³, is for both tiers. For full explanations, see the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-amount-of-substance-and-masses/); then practise with the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-amount-of-substance-and-masses-practice/).

## Core facts

| Fact | Tier |
|---|---|
| Amounts are measured in moles (mol) | HT only |
| Mass of one mole in grams = Mr | HT only |
| Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ per mole | HT only |
| Equation coefficients give mole ratios | HT only |
| The limiting reactant is completely used up and limits the product | HT only |
| Concentration in g/dm³ = mass ÷ volume (dm³) | Both |
| Concentration is related to mass of solute and volume of solution | HT only for the explanation |

## Key equations

```
moles = mass / Mr                    mass = moles x Mr
particles = moles x 6.02 x 10^23
concentration (g/dm3) = mass of solute (g) / volume (dm3)
volume (dm3) = volume (cm3) / 1000
```

## Method for reacting mass questions (HT only)

1. Write and check the balanced equation.
2. Moles of the known substance = mass ÷ Mr.
3. Use the equation ratio to get moles of the wanted substance.
4. Mass of the wanted substance = moles × Mr.

| Example | Working |
|---|---|
| Moles in 14.6 g HCl | 14.6 ÷ 36.5 = 0.40 mol |
| Mass of 0.25 mol CaCO₃ | 0.25 × 100 = 25 g |
| Mass of MgCl₂ from 6.0 g Mg (Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂) | 6.0 ÷ 24 = 0.25 mol; × 95 = 23.8 g |
| Molecules in 0.40 mol | 0.40 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.4 × 10²³ |

## Balancing from masses (HT only)

Convert each mass to moles, divide all by the smallest, and write the ratio as whole numbers.

Example: 5.4 g Al, 4.8 g O₂, 10.2 g Al₂O₃ gives 0.20, 0.15 and 0.10 mol, which is 4 : 3 : 2, so
4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃.

## Limiting reactants (HT only)

1. Find moles of each reactant.
2. Use the ratio to see which would run out first.
3. The one that runs out is limiting.
4. Base the mass of product on the limiting reactant.

Example: 0.25 mol Mg needs 0.50 mol HCl but only 0.20 mol HCl is present, so HCl is limiting.

## Concentration calculations

| Question | Working |
|---|---|
| 12 g in 500 cm³ | 500 ÷ 1000 = 0.5 dm³; 12 ÷ 0.5 = 24 g/dm³ |
| Mass in 250 cm³ at 40 g/dm³ | 0.25 × 40 = 10 g |
| Volume holding 9 g at 36 g/dm³ | 9 ÷ 36 = 0.25 dm³ = 250 cm³ |

HT only: concentration is directly proportional to the mass of solute (double the mass, double the concentration)
and inversely proportional to the volume (double the volume, half the concentration).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mole | A unit for amount of substance; 6.02 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³ per mole |
| Limiting reactant | The reactant that is completely used up |
| Excess | More reactant than needed |
| Solute | The substance dissolved |
| Concentration | Amount of solute per unit volume |
| dm³ | Cubic decimetre, equal to 1000 cm³ |

## Worked examples in brief (HT only)

**Atoms in a compound.** 0.50 mol of CO₂ has 0.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ molecules and twice as many oxygen atoms:
6.02 × 10²³.

**Hydrogen made.** 4.8 g Mg (0.20 mol) with excess acid makes 0.20 mol H₂, which is 0.40 g.

**Doubling quantities.** Doubling the mass of a reactant doubles the moles and doubles the mass of product.

## Exam traps

- **Not converting cm³ to dm³.**
- **Using the mass ratio instead of the mole ratio.**
- **Wrong Mr** from missing a bracket multiplier.
- **Choosing the limiting reactant by smaller mass.** Compare moles against the ratio.
- **Writing the Avogadro constant without the power of ten.**
- **Rounding mid-way.** Round the final answer only.

## Quick self-test

1. (HT only) How many moles are in 44 g of CO₂?
2. (HT only) What is the mass of 2.0 mol of NaOH?
3. (HT only) How many atoms are in 0.50 mol of carbon?
4. (HT only) 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. How many moles of water form from 3 mol of hydrogen with excess oxygen?
5. (HT only) What does "limiting reactant" mean?
6. Calculate the concentration of 15 g of salt in 300 cm³ of solution.
7. Calculate the mass of solute in 200 cm³ of a 30 g/dm³ solution.
8. (HT only) The mass of solute in a solution is doubled with the volume unchanged. What happens to the
   concentration?
9. (HT only) 4.8 g of Mg burns to make MgO (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO). Calculate the mass of MgO.
10. Convert 750 cm³ to dm³.

### Answers

1. 44 ÷ 44 = 1.0 mol.
2. 2.0 × 40 = 80 g.
3. 0.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³.
4. 3 mol (ratio 1 : 1).
5. The reactant that is completely used up and limits the amount of product.
6. 0.3 dm³; 15 ÷ 0.3 = 50 g/dm³.
7. 0.2 dm³; 30 × 0.2 = 6.0 g.
8. It doubles.
9. 0.20 mol Mg gives 0.20 mol MgO; 0.20 × 40 = 8.0 g.
10. 0.75 dm³.
