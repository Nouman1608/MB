**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.6 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Ijma and Qiyas as a source of Shariah: "The meaning, role and importance of Ijma (consensus) and Qiyas (analogy) in forming religious rulings."** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 4:115.

The Qur'an and Sunnah are the first two sources of Shariah. They were complete when the Prophet (pbuh) died, but the Muslim community went on growing, and new situations arose that no text mentions by name. Ijma and Qiyas are the tools that scholars use to reach rulings in those cases, always by reference to the first two sources and never against them.

## The order of the sources

1. **The Qur'an.** The word of Allah.
2. **The Sunnah.** The example of the Prophet (pbuh), recorded in the Hadith.
3. **Ijma.** The agreement of qualified scholars.
4. **Qiyas.** Reasoning by analogy from a ruling already found in the text.

This is the order stated by Imam al-Shafi'i and followed by the Sunni schools. Each source is tested by those before it: Ijma and Qiyas can never contradict the Qur'an or an authentic Hadith. The Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) supports the principle: asked how he would judge, he said by the Book of Allah, then by the Sunnah, then by striving to form his own opinion, and the Prophet (pbuh) approved (Abu Dawud).

## Ijma (consensus)

**Meaning.** Ijma is the agreement of the qualified scholars (*mujtahidun*) of the Muslim community, in a given period after the Prophet's (pbuh) death, on a ruling about a matter of religion.

**Who counts?** The scholars must be trained to give independent rulings. An agreement of ordinary people or of non-experts is not Ijma in this sense.

**What does it cover?** Matters that are not settled in a clear text, or that the texts leave open. Where a clear text exists, the text decides.

**Types.**

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Explicit | Every scholar states his agreement |
| Silent | Some scholars state the ruling and the rest, aware of it, do not object |
| Agreement of the companions | The most widely accepted kind of Ijma |

Some schools stress particular forms of agreement. The Maliki school gives special weight to the agreement of the people of Madinah. Ja'fari scholars accept consensus where it includes or reveals the view of the Imam.

**Examples.** Scholars often cite the following.

- **The first caliph.** After the Prophet's (pbuh) death the companions agreed to choose Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) to lead the community.
- **The Qur'an in one book.** The companions agreed to the collection of the Qur'an under Abu Bakr (RA) and the standard copies under Uthman (RA).
- **Taraweeh.** Hazrat Umar (RA) gathered people to pray the Ramadhan night prayer behind one imam, and the companions accepted it.
- **The grandmother's inheritance share.** The companions agreed that a grandmother receives one-sixth when there is no mother, a ruling based on a report from the Prophet (pbuh) that they confirmed together.

**Evidence for Ijma.** Muslims point to several sources.

- **Qur'an 4:115**, the prescribed passage, warns against following "other than the way of the believers". Imam al-Shafi'i is traditionally said to have used this verse as evidence that the agreement of the believers is binding.
- **Qur'an 4:59** speaks of "those in authority among you", whom many scholars take to include the learned.
- **A well-known Hadith** reports that Allah will not let the Prophet's (pbuh) community agree on error. Scholars discuss the chains of the different versions, but they also point to the meaning found in many reports.

**Why it matters.** Ijma gives the community a way to settle important questions with certainty. It protects the community from isolated mistakes, and it holds the Ummah together on matters that would otherwise divide it.

## Qiyas (analogy)

**Meaning.** Qiyas is extending the ruling of a case found in the Qur'an or Sunnah to a new case, because the two share the same reason.

**The four parts of Qiyas.**

| Part | Arabic term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Original case | Asl | The case with a ruling in the text |
| New case | Far' | The case with no ruling in the text |
| The ruling | Hukm | What the text says about the original case |
| The reason | 'Illa | The shared quality that is the cause of the ruling |

**Worked example: intoxicants.**

1. The original case is wine. The Qur'an forbids it (5:90).
2. The new case is a modern drug that was unknown in the Prophet's (pbuh) time.
3. The reason is that wine clouds the mind and harms the person. This is the *'illa*.
4. The new drug has the same effect, so it takes the same ruling: it is forbidden.

**Worked example: business at the Friday call.** The Qur'an commands believers to leave trade when the call to Friday prayer is made (62:9). Scholars reasoned that the cause is distraction from prayer, so other deals that would distract are treated in the same way.

**Worked example: a fortiori.** The Qur'an tells children not to say "uff" to their parents (17:23). The reason is to avoid hurting them, so shouting at them or striking them is forbidden even more strongly.

**Conditions.**

- There must be no clear text on the new case.
- The original case must have a ruling in the Qur'an or Sunnah.
- The reason must be clear and must really be shared by both cases.
- The result must not contradict a text.
- A qualified scholar must do it.

**Views of the schools.** The Hanafi school makes wide use of reasoning, including qiyas and istihsan. The Maliki school uses it along with the practice of Madinah and public interest. Al-Shafi'i set strict rules for it. The Hanbali school uses it only where necessary. The Ja'fari school does not use qiyas in the Sunni sense and relies on reason ('aql).

**Why it matters.** Qiyas lets Islamic law deal with new situations, such as modern drugs or new kinds of trade, without waiting for new revelation. It also keeps reasoning disciplined, because a scholar cannot simply give a personal opinion; he must link it to a text.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 4:115

The specification gives the verse as: "And whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him and follows other than the way of the believers - We will give him what he has taken and drive him into Hell, and evil it is as a destination."

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| "opposes the Messenger after guidance has become clear" | Opposing clear guidance is a serious wrong; Ijma and Qiyas cannot be used against the Sunnah |
| "follows other than the way of the believers" | Used by many scholars as evidence for Ijma: the community's way is a guide |
| "We will give him what he has taken" | Each person is responsible for the path he chooses |
| "evil it is as a destination" | A strong warning about the result of splitting from the community's path |

Be careful in the exam. The verse is a warning, and scholars have drawn the case for Ijma from it. Write "many scholars use this verse as evidence for Ijma" and not "the verse says that Ijma is a source".

## Ijma and Qiyas compared

| | Ijma | Qiyas |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Agreement of qualified scholars | Reasoning from a text |
| Works by | Community decision | Matching cases by a shared reason |
| Strength | Very strong when universal | Depends on the clarity of the reason |
| Example | Collecting the Qur'an in one book | Treating modern drugs like wine |
| Limit | Cannot contradict the text | Cannot contradict the text |

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Qiyas is important in Islamic law. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> Qiyas allows Muslims to deal with new situations that the Qur'an and Sunnah do not name [1]. For example, a modern drug can be forbidden by comparison with wine, because both harm the mind [1]. It also keeps reasoning disciplined [1], because a scholar must link his ruling to a text and a shared reason, not give a personal opinion [1]. The Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) shows the Prophet (pbuh) approving of striving to form an opinion when the Qur'an and Sunnah do not give a direct answer [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing Ijma and Qiyas.** Ijma is agreement of scholars; Qiyas is reasoning by analogy.
- **Saying that Ijma means the opinion of everyone.** It means the agreement of qualified scholars.
- **Forgetting the shared reason ('illa).** Without it Qiyas is just a comparison.
- **Placing them above the Qur'an and Sunnah.** They are tested by those sources.
- **Overclaiming from 4:115.** Say that many scholars use it as evidence for Ijma.
