## Short-answer questions

Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** An accountant tells her neighbour which local shops owe her client money. Which fundamental principle has she breached?

A: Confidentiality

B: Integrity

C: Objectivity

D: Professional competence and due care

**[1]**

**2.** An accountant is asked to review the accounts of a company in which her husband is a director. Which fundamental principle is most threatened?

A: Confidentiality

B: Objectivity

C: Professional behaviour

D: Professional competence and due care

**[1]**

**3.** Name the fundamental principle most clearly breached in each situation.

**(a)** A book-keeper records a purchase invoice from a supplier that does not exist. **[1]**

**(b)** An accountant prepares a bank reconciliation in a hurry and does not check it. **[1]**

**(c)** An accountant claims on his website to hold a qualification he does not have. **[1]**

**(d)** A clerk uses information about a planned takeover to buy shares for herself. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why accounting needs an ethical framework. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the difference between professional competence and due care. **[2]**

**6.** A business has a draft profit of $64,000. The owner asks the book-keeper not to record wages of $2,400 owing at the year end.

**(a)** Calculate the correct profit. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate, as a percentage of the correct profit, how much profit would be overstated if the owner's request were followed. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Hannah is the accountant of Brackley Furnishings Limited. Sales for the year are below target. A director asks her to record $9,000 of goods delivered on 4 January as December sales, and mentions that he will support her promotion.

**(a)** Identify **two** fundamental principles that Hannah would breach if she agreed, explaining why each applies. **[4]**

**(b)** Advise Hannah what she should do. **[2]**

**8.** Clifton Electrical's closing inventory cost $15,000. It includes obsolete items that cost $4,000 and can be sold for only $500. The owner wants the inventory valued at cost because he is applying for a bank loan.

**(a)** Calculate the value at which closing inventory should be shown. **[2]**

**(b)** State the amount by which profit would be overstated if the owner's request were followed. **[1]**

**(c)** Name **two** fundamental principles the book-keeper would breach by agreeing. **[2]**

**9.** Explain the significance of accountants applying an ethical framework to:

**(a)** lenders **[2]**

**(b)** tax authorities. **[2]**

**10.** Discuss the significance to society of accountants applying an ethical framework. **[6]**

**11.** Olu prepares the accounts of a small hotel. The tax authority sends a formal request for the hotel's records. Later, Olu's friend, who is thinking of opening a rival hotel, asks how much profit the hotel makes. Explain whether Olu should disclose the information in each case. **[4]**

**12.** "As long as an accountant obeys the law, ethics do not matter." Evaluate this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. The information was gained through work and disclosed without authority. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. A family link creates a conflict of interest. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Integrity [1].

**(b)** Professional competence and due care [1].

**(c)** Professional behaviour [1].

**(d)** Confidentiality [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Any one reason, developed. Users such as banks and investors rely on the figures but cannot check them [1], so they need confidence that the figures are honest [1]. Also accept: accountants face pressure from owners or managers, and principles set in advance give them a standard to apply. **[2]**

**5.** Professional competence means having the knowledge and skill needed for the work and keeping it up to date [1]. Due care means applying that skill carefully, thoroughly and on time, for example checking the work [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 64,000 − 2,400 = **$61,600** [1].

**(b)** 2,400 ÷ 61,600 × 100 [1] = **3.90%** [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Integrity [1]: recording January deliveries as December sales would knowingly make revenue and profit misleading [1]. Objectivity [1]: the promise of promotion is a personal interest and pressure that could influence her judgement [1]. Also creditable: professional behaviour, because misleading users could discredit the profession.

**(b)** Hannah should refuse and record the sales in January, when the goods were delivered, as the realisation concept requires [1]. She should explain the reason to the director and, if the pressure continues, report it to a more senior person or other directors [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Write-down 4,000 − 500 = 3,500 [1]; inventory **$11,500** [1].

**(b)** **$3,500** [1].

**(c)** Any two: integrity [1]; professional behaviour [1]; objectivity (giving way to pressure). **[5]**

**9. (a)** Lenders decide whether to lend using the accounts [1]; honest figures mean loans go to businesses that can repay, so lenders avoid losses [1].

**(b)** Tax is calculated on reported profit [1]; ethical accounting means the correct tax is paid, so the authority does not lose revenue needed for public services [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Honest accounting keeps trust in financial information, so people are willing to lend and invest [1]. Money flows to businesses that genuinely perform well, which supports growth [1]. The correct tax is collected, which pays for public services such as schools and hospitals [1]. Hidden problems are less likely, so sudden business failures that cost jobs and leave suppliers unpaid are reduced [1]. However, a framework cannot prevent every deliberate fraud, and it depends on individuals choosing to follow it [1]. Conclusion: applying the framework is highly significant because harm from unethical accounting reaches people who never see the accounts, such as employees and taxpayers [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Tax authority: Olu may disclose, because confidentiality allows disclosure where there is a legal requirement or proper authority [1], and a formal request from the tax authority is such a case [1]. Friend: he should refuse [1], because the information was obtained through his work and disclosing it to a potential rival without the hotel's permission would breach confidentiality [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Agree in part: professional behaviour does require accountants to comply with the law [1], and many unethical acts, such as hiding sales from the tax authority, are also illegal [1].

Disagree: some actions are legal yet unethical, for example choosing an estimate to raise a personal bonus breaches objectivity [1]; working carelessly breaches due care even if no law is broken [1]; misleading figures that are technically allowed still breach integrity [1].

Judgement: the statement is wrong; the law sets a minimum, while the ethical framework guides the many judgements in accounting that the law does not cover [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a principle without linking it to a fact in the scenario.
- Using one reason for two principles.
- Treating confidentiality as an absolute ban.
- Answering a question on society with effects on the owner only.
- Leaving out the action when asked to advise.
