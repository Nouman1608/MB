These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 1.01, Calculations with integers (1.01a, Four rules). All of it is content for both tiers. Answer every question **without a calculator** and show your written method. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer problems in context.

Revise first with the [1.01 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-calculations-with-integers/) or the [1.01 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-calculations-with-integers-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Work out

**(a)** −17 + 5 **[1]**

**(b)** 6 − (−13) **[1]**

**(c)** −4 − 9 − (−20) **[1]**

**2.** Work out

**(a)** (−9) × 7 **[1]**

**(b)** (−84) ÷ (−12) **[1]**

**(c)** (−2) × (−5) × (−3) × (−1) **[1]**

**3.** Write down

**(a)** the sum of −11 and 4, **[1]**

**(b)** the difference between −6 and 15, **[1]**

**(c)** the quotient when −96 is divided by 8. **[1]**

**4.** Work out 3 007 − 1 459. **[2]**

**6.** Work out 6 104 ÷ 14. **[3]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows the temperature at midnight in five towns.

| Town | Arnhill | Bexford | Calder | Dunmore | Elm Bay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | −8 | 3 | −15 | 11 | −1 |

**(a)** Find the difference between the highest and the lowest temperature. **[1]**

**(b)** By 4 am the temperature in every town has fallen by 6 degrees. Which towns are then colder than −10 °C? **[2]**

**(c)** Find the mean of the five midnight temperatures. **[2]**

**8.** 347 students and 28 teachers go on a trip. Each coach seats 53 people.

**(a)** How many coaches are needed? **[3]**

**(b)** Each coach costs £385 to hire. Find the total cost of the coaches. **[2]**

**(c)** Each student pays £9. The teachers pay nothing. Does the money collected cover the cost? Show how you decide. **[2]**

**9.** A bank account starts the month at −£86. During the month there are four transactions: £250 paid in, £174 paid out, £63 paid out and £40 paid in.

**(a)** Find the balance at the end of the month. **[2]**

**(b)** How much must then be paid in to bring the balance up to £100? **[1]**

**10.**

**(a)** Use a written method to show that 125 × 48 = 6 000. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence write down the value of (−6 000) ÷ (−48). **[1]**

**(c)** Hence work out 125 × 49. **[1]**

**11.** A factory packs 7 500 bolts into bags of 36.

**(a)** How many bags can be filled completely? **[3]**

**(b)** How many bolts are left over? **[1]**

**12.** Kai says, "(−5) × (−4) × (−3) is positive, because two negatives make a positive."

**(a)** Explain why Kai is wrong, and give the correct value. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down four integers, at least two of them negative, whose product is −24. **[2]**

**13.** A rectangular floor has area 2 976 cm² and width 24 cm.

**(a)** Find its length. **[3]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **−12** [1]

**(b)** 6 + 13 = **19** [1]

**(c)** −13 + 20 = **7** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c) rewrite − (−20) as + 20 before you start, then work left to right.

**2. (a)** **−63** [1]

**(b)** Same signs, so positive: **7** [1]

**(c)** Four negatives (even), so positive: **30** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Count all the negative factors in part (c). Pairing only the first two gives the wrong sign.

**3. (a)** **−7** [1]

**(b)** 15 − (−6) = **21** [1]

**(c)** **−12** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Difference" means the gap between the numbers, so the answer to part (b) is positive.

**4.** Column subtraction with exchange across the zeros [1]; **1 548** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check by adding: 1 548 + 1 459 = 3 007.

**5.** 257 × 4 = 1 028 [1]; 257 × 60 = 15 420 [1]; total **16 448** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A grid with 200, 50, 7 against 60, 4 earns the same marks. An estimate of 300 × 60 = 18 000 shows the answer is the right size.

**6.** A correct start, such as 14 × 400 = 5 600 leaving 504, or 61 ÷ 14 = 4 remainder 5 [1]; continuing correctly, for example 14 × 36 = 504 [1]; **436** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Writing the 14 times table first makes each step quicker. Check: 436 × 14 = 4 360 + 1 744 = 6 104.

**7. (a)** 11 − (−15) = **26 degrees** [1]

**(b)** New temperatures: −14, −3, −21, 5, −7 [1]; **Arnhill and Calder** [1]

**(c)** Total −8 + 3 − 15 + 11 − 1 = −10 [1]; mean −10 ÷ 5 = **−2 °C** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "Colder than −10" means further below zero, so −14 and −21 qualify but −7 does not.

**8. (a)** 347 + 28 = 375 people [1]; 375 ÷ 53 = 7 remainder 4 [1]; **8 coaches** [1]

**(b)** 8 × 385 [1]; **£3 080** [1]

**(c)** 347 × 9 = £3 123 [1]; £3 123 > £3 080, so **yes**, with £43 to spare [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (a) the teachers need seats too, and 4 people left over still need an eighth coach. Seven coaches seat only 371.

**9. (a)** −86 + 250 = 164, then 164 − 174 = −10 [1]; −10 − 63 + 40 = **−£33** [1]

**(b)** 100 − (−33) = **£133** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep a running balance line by line. A slip with one transaction then costs only the accuracy mark.

**10. (a)** 125 × 40 = 5 000 and 125 × 8 = 1 000 [1]; 5 000 + 1 000 = 6 000 [1]

**(b)** **125** [1]

**(c)** 6 000 + 125 = **6 125** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Hence" means use the result you have just shown. In part (c), 49 lots of 125 is one more 125 than 48 lots.

**11. (a)** Division method, such as 36 × 200 = 7 200 leaving 300 [1]; 36 × 8 = 288, leaving 12 [1]; **208 bags** [1]

**(b)** 7 500 − 36 × 208 = 7 500 − 7 488 = **12 bolts** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Filled completely" means round down. A 209th bag would hold only 12 bolts.

**12. (a)** There are three negative factors, an odd number, so the product is negative [1]; **−60** [1]

**(b)** Exactly three of the four integers negative, so the product is negative [1]; sizes that multiply to 24, for example (−1) × (−2) × (−3) × 4 [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Two negatives make a positive" applies to one pair. With three negatives, the third makes the product negative again.

**13. (a)** Length = 2 976 ÷ 24 [1]; division method shown, such as 24 × 100 = 2 400, 24 × 24 = 576 [1]; **124 cm** [1]

**(b)** 2 × (124 + 24) [1]; **296 cm** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Area is length × width, so the inverse, division, gives the missing length. Units for perimeter are cm, not cm².

## Where marks are usually lost

- Turning − (−a) into − a instead of + a.
- Using "two negatives make a positive" when adding two negative numbers.
- Taking the sign of a long product from one pair of factors only.
- Leaving out the place-value zero in the tens row of a multiplication.
- Rounding a remainder down when everyone needs a seat, or up when only full bags count.
- Forgetting a group of people (such as teachers) when finding the total to be divided.
- Giving the difference between two temperatures as a negative number.
- Writing the answer with no written method on a non-calculator paper.
