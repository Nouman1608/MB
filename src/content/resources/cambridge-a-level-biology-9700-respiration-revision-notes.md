These notes condense sub-topic **12.2 Respiration** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content for Paper 4, with investigations relevant to Paper 5. For explanations and worked examples, use the [respiration study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-respiration/); for exam-style questions, use the [respiration practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-respiration-practice/).

## Where each stage happens

| Stage | Site |
|---|---|
| Glycolysis | Cytoplasm |
| Link reaction | Mitochondrial matrix |
| Krebs cycle | Mitochondrial matrix |
| Oxidative phosphorylation | Inner mitochondrial membrane |

## Glycolysis

1. Glucose + 2 ATP → **fructose 1,6-bisphosphate (6C)**.
2. Splits → **2 triose phosphate (3C)**.
3. Each oxidised (NAD reduced) → **pyruvate (3C)**, making 2 ATP.

Net per glucose: **2 ATP, 2 reduced NAD, 2 pyruvate**.

## Link reaction (oxygen available)

- Pyruvate enters the matrix.
- **Decarboxylated** (CO₂ out) and **dehydrogenated** (NAD reduced).
- Acetyl (2C) + **coenzyme A** → acetyl coenzyme A.

## Krebs cycle

1. Acetyl (2C) + **oxaloacetate (4C)** → **citrate (6C)**; CoA released.
2. 6C → 5C: CO₂ out, NAD reduced.
3. 5C → 4C: CO₂ out, NAD reduced, ATP made.
4. 4C → oxaloacetate: FAD reduced, NAD reduced.

Per turn: 2 CO₂, 3 reduced NAD, 1 reduced FAD, 1 ATP.

## Totals per glucose

| | CO₂ | Reduced NAD | Reduced FAD | ATP (direct) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | 0 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| Link × 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| Krebs × 2 | 4 | 6 | 2 | 2 |
| Total | 6 | 10 | 2 | 4 |

## Oxidative phosphorylation

1. NAD and FAD carry hydrogen to carriers in the inner membrane.
2. H → **H⁺ + energetic electron**.
3. Electrons pass along the **electron transport chain**, releasing energy.
4. Energy moves **H⁺ from matrix to intermembrane space**.
5. H⁺ return by **facilitated diffusion through ATP synthase** → ATP (**chemiosmosis**).
6. **Oxygen = final electron acceptor** → forms **water**.

No carrier names or ATP synthase details needed.

## Mitochondrion

- **Cristae**: large area for ETC and ATP synthase.
- **Inner membrane**: impermeable to H⁺ except through ATP synthase.
- **Intermembrane space**: small, so H⁺ concentration rises fast.
- **Matrix**: link reaction and Krebs enzymes; DNA; ribosomes.

## Anaerobic respiration

- **No O₂** → ETC stops → reduced NAD not oxidised → Krebs and link reaction stop.
- Glycolysis continues only if NAD is regenerated.
- **Mammals**: pyruvate + reduced NAD → **lactate** + NAD.
- **Yeast**: pyruvate → **ethanal** + CO₂; ethanal + reduced NAD → **ethanol** + NAD.
- Yield: net **2 ATP** per glucose (glycolysis only).

**Aerobic yield much greater**: glucose fully oxidised; 10 reduced NAD and 2 reduced FAD drive oxidative phosphorylation; anaerobic products still contain much energy.

## Rice

- **Aerenchyma**: air spaces carry O₂ to submerged roots.
- **Ethanol fermentation** in roots; tolerates ethanol.
- **Fast stem growth** keeps leaves above water.

## Investigations

**Redox indicators (DCPIP, methylene blue)**
- Blue (oxidised) → colourless (reduced) as dehydrogenases pass hydrogen to the dye.
- Rate = 1 ÷ time to decolourise.
- Vary temperature or glucose concentration; keep yeast and dye volumes constant.
- Control: boiled yeast. Do not shake (O₂ re-oxidises methylene blue).

**Respirometer (temperature)**
- KOH absorbs CO₂; liquid moves as O₂ used.
- Water baths; equilibrate; repeats; control tube at each temperature.
- Rate = πr² × distance ÷ time.

### Worked reminder: rate from decolourisation time

```
time 250 s → rate = 1000 ÷ 250 = 4.0 (arbitrary units)
time 400 s → rate = 1000 ÷ 400 = 2.5
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Decarboxylation | Removal of carbon dioxide from a molecule |
| Dehydrogenation | Removal of hydrogen from a molecule |
| Coenzyme A | Carries acetyl groups into the Krebs cycle |
| NAD, FAD | Coenzymes that accept hydrogen and carry it to the ETC |
| Electron transport chain | Carriers in the inner membrane along which electrons pass |
| ATP synthase | Enzyme through which protons flow to make ATP |
| Chemiosmosis | ATP synthesis driven by a proton gradient |
| Fermentation | Anaerobic respiration regenerating NAD |
| Aerenchyma | Plant tissue with large air spaces |
| Redox indicator | Dye that changes colour when reduced |

## Exam traps

- Krebs on cristae, or ETC in the matrix.
- Link reaction and Krebs counted once per glucose.
- "ATP synthase pumps protons".
- "Fermentation makes ATP".
- Oxygen's role given vaguely.
- Rate given as time rather than 1/time.

## Self-test

1. Where does the link reaction occur?
2. What is the 6C phosphorylated sugar formed in glycolysis?
3. State the net ATP yield of glycolysis.
4. What is the role of coenzyme A?
5. Name the 4C acceptor in the Krebs cycle.
6. How many CO₂ are released per glucose in the Krebs cycle?
7. What happens to hydrogen atoms at the inner membrane?
8. How do protons re-enter the matrix?
9. What is the role of oxygen?
10. Why does glycolysis stop without fermentation in anaerobic conditions?
11. Give the three syllabus adaptations of rice.
12. Why is boiled yeast used in a methylene blue investigation?

### Answers

1. Mitochondrial matrix.
2. Fructose 1,6-bisphosphate.
3. 2 ATP.
4. Carries the acetyl (2C) group into the Krebs cycle.
5. Oxaloacetate.
6. Four.
7. They split into protons and energetic electrons.
8. By facilitated diffusion through ATP synthase.
9. Final electron acceptor; combines with electrons and protons to form water.
10. NAD is not regenerated, so there is none to accept hydrogen from triose phosphate.
11. Aerenchyma; ethanol fermentation in roots; faster stem growth.
12. As a control, showing decolourisation needs living (enzyme-active) cells.
