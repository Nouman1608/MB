## Section A

**1.** Define *outsourcing*. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** areas of outsourcing growth named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**3.** Define *contract manufacturing*. **[2]**

**4.** Identify **three** services that a contract logistics provider may carry out for an online retailer. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** reasons why contract manufacturing has grown. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** risks to a business of outsourcing its production. **[2]**

**7.** A logistics provider charges $1.80 for each parcel it packs and delivers. Calculate the charge for 3,500 parcels. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain how the growth of ecommerce has led to the growth of contract logistics. **[4]**

**9.** FreshPress is a new company that has created a cold-pressed juice recipe. It has little capital. Explain **two** reasons why contract manufacturing would be suitable for FreshPress. **[4]**

**10.** Explain the difference between contract manufacturing and a franchise. **[4]**

**11.** SpiceRoute packs 10,000 packets of spices each month in-house. Its monthly costs are: machinery lease $1,200; two packing staff at $800 each; packing materials $0.15 per packet. A contractor offers to pack the spices for $0.38 per packet, materials included.
(a) Calculate SpiceRoute's in-house cost per packet.
(b) Calculate the monthly saving if SpiceRoute uses the contractor.
(c) In a quiet month SpiceRoute packs only 4,000 packets. Calculate the in-house cost per packet that month and comment on the result. **[8]**

**12.** A medicine company is considering outsourcing the manufacture of its tablets to a contract manufacturer in another country. Discuss whether it should do so. **[8]**

**13.** A sports clothing brand uses contract manufacturers and a contract logistics provider. Explain **two** ways the brand could reduce the risks of outsourcing. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Paying another business to carry out an activity that the business could do itself [1].

**2.** Contract manufacturing [1] and logistics [1].

**3.** Paying another firm to make products, or parts of them [1], to the client's own design and specification, usually to be sold under the client's brand [1].

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: storing goods in a warehouse; picking and packing orders; transport and delivery to customers; handling returned goods; preparing export documents and arranging customs clearance [1] [1] [1].

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: new brands avoid the cost of building a factory; access to specialist machinery and skilled staff; lower unit costs from the contractor's economies of scale; flexibility to change output; the client can focus on design and selling; digital communication makes contractors easy to manage at a distance [1] [1].

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: less control over quality; less control over delivery times; dependence on the contractor; designs or information may be copied; the contractor's poor working conditions or pollution may harm the client's reputation [1] [1].

**7.** 3,500 x $1.80 [1] = **$6,300** [1].

**8.** Ecommerce means many more orders are sent as individual parcels to customers' homes [1], and customers expect fast delivery, tracking and easy returns [1]. Building warehouses, vehicle fleets and tracking systems in-house is expensive for an online seller [1], so many sellers pay specialist providers that already have them, which has increased demand for contract logistics [1].

**9.** **Low capital needed**: FreshPress would not have to buy pressing and bottling machinery or a factory [1], so it can launch the juice with the little capital it has [1]. **Expertise**: a contract manufacturer already has hygienic equipment and staff trained in food safety [1], so the juice is produced to a consistent, safe standard from the start [1].

**10.** In **contract manufacturing**, a contractor makes products to the client's design [1], and the client sells them under its own brand; the contractor has no outlet of its own [1]. In a **franchise**, the franchisee runs its own outlet using the franchisor's brand, products and methods [1], paying a fee and royalties for the right to do so [1].

**11.** (a) Staff = 2 x $800 = $1,600; materials = 10,000 x $0.15 = $1,500 [1]. Total = $1,200 + $1,600 + $1,500 = $4,300 [1]. Cost per packet = $4,300 / 10,000 = **$0.43** [1]. (b) Contractor = 10,000 x $0.38 = $3,800 [1]; saving = $4,300 − $3,800 = **$500** per month [1]. (c) Materials = 4,000 x $0.15 = $600; total = $1,200 + $1,600 + $600 = $3,400 [1]; cost per packet = $3,400 / 4,000 = **$0.85** [1]. Comment: the in-house cost per packet doubles because the lease and wages stay the same when output falls, so outsourcing becomes even more attractive in quiet months [1].

**12.** **For:** the contractor may have specialist equipment and expertise [1]; production costs may be lower, raising the company's profit per tablet [1]; the company would not need to invest in a new factory [1]. **Against:** quality failures in medicines could harm patients [1] and lead to product recalls and serious damage to the company's reputation [1]; the company would have less control over its formulas, which might be copied [1]; distance makes inspections slower and more costly [1]. **Judgement:** for example, it should outsource only if the contractor meets strict, independently checked quality standards and the contract protects its formulas; otherwise in-house production is safer for a product where quality is critical [1].

**13.** Any two, 2 marks each (the method, then how it reduces risk): **inspect and test** goods regularly, so faults are found before products reach customers; **use more than one contractor**, so production continues if one fails or raises prices; write **clear contracts** with quality, delivery and confidentiality terms, giving the brand a remedy if standards are not met; **check working conditions** at contractors' sites to avoid reputation damage [1] [1] [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 3: forgetting that the products are made to the client's design.
- Question 8: describing ecommerce without explaining why it makes outsourcing logistics attractive.
- Question 11: dividing by 10,000 in part (c) instead of 4,000, or giving no comment.
- Question 12: treating medicines like any other product and missing the safety argument.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Question 12 have one right answer?** No. Either decision is credited if it follows from the points made and refers to the medicine company.

**Why is the comment in Question 11 worth a mark?** Interpreting a calculation shows analysis: here, fixed costs explain why outsourcing becomes cheaper per unit when output falls.
