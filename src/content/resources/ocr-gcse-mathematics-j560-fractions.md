This guide covers section 2.01, Fractions, of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Every statement in 2.01 sits in the first two columns of the specification, so all of it is content for **both tiers**. Algebraic fractions are a separate section (6.01g) and are not covered here.

The [fractions and decimal fractions guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-fractions-and-decimal-fractions/) teaches the four operations with one example each. This page goes further on 2.01 alone: equivalence checks, multi-step calculations that mix operations, negative mixed numbers, successive fractions of a quantity, dividing to find a whole, and quantities in different units. Condense it with the [2.01 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-fractions-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [2.01 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-fractions-practice/).

| Ref | Statement | Column |
|---|---|---|
| 2.01a | Recognise and use equivalence between simple fractions and mixed numbers | First (both tiers) |
| 2.01b | Add, subtract, multiply and divide simple fractions (proper and improper), including mixed numbers and negative fractions | First (both tiers) |

| 2.01c | Calculate with fractions greater than 1 | Second (both tiers) |

Fractions can appear on any paper, but they matter most on the non-calculator papers, where every step must be written.

## Equivalence: checking quickly

Two fractions are equivalent if one can be turned into the other by multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number. A fast test is to compare the cross products: a/b and c/d are equivalent when a × d = b × c.

**Worked example 1.** Which of 15/20, 21/28, 18/27 and 30/40 are equivalent to 3/4?

```
15/20: 15 × 4 = 60, 20 × 3 = 60   equivalent
21/28: 21 × 4 = 84, 28 × 3 = 84   equivalent
18/27: 18 × 4 = 72, 27 × 3 = 81   not equivalent (18/27 = 2/3)
30/40: 30 × 4 = 120, 40 × 3 = 120 equivalent
```

Mixed numbers have equivalent forms too: 2 4/6 = 2 2/3 = 8/3. When an answer is asked for "in its simplest form", divide top and bottom by their highest common factor, so the fraction cannot be simplified further.

## More complex calculations

The second-column statement asks for "more complex calculations, including the use of improper fractions". In practice that means several operations in one question, with mixed numbers converted to improper fractions first.

**Worked example 2.** Work out 2/3 + 1/2 × 3/5.

```
order of operations: multiply first
1/2 × 3/5 = 3/10
2/3 + 3/10 = 20/30 + 9/30 = 29/30
```

The answer is **29/30**. Adding first, (2/3 + 1/2) × 3/5 = 7/6 × 3/5 = 7/10, would answer a different question.

**Worked example 3.** Work out 2 1/3 − 4 1/2.

```
improper:   7/3 − 9/2
common denominator 6:   14/6 − 27/6 = −13/6
mixed:      −2 1/6
```

The answer is **−2 1/6**. A negative answer is expected, because 4 1/2 is larger than 2 1/3. Converting to improper fractions avoids splitting into whole-number and fraction parts with different signs.

**Worked example 4.** Work out (−3/4) × 2 2/3, then 3 3/4 ÷ 1 1/2.

```
(−3/4) × 8/3 = −(3 × 8)/(4 × 3) = −24/12 = −2
cancel first to see it faster: the 3s cancel, and 8/4 = 2

15/4 ÷ 3/2 = 15/4 × 2/3 = 30/12 = 5/2 = 2 1/2
```

In context, 3 3/4 ÷ 1 1/2 = 2 1/2 tells you that **2** full pieces of length 1 1/2 m can be cut from 3 3/4 m of wood, with half a piece's length (3/4 m) left.

**Worked example 5 (a "show that").** Show that 1 2/3 + 2 3/4 = 4 5/12.

```
1 2/3 = 5/3 = 20/12
2 3/4 = 11/4 = 33/12
20/12 + 33/12 = 53/12
53 ÷ 12 = 4 remainder 5, so 53/12 = 4 5/12
```

Each line must be visible, because the answer is already given. The conversion of 53/12 back to a mixed number is the step most often left out.

## Fractions of a quantity

"Of" means multiply. For a fraction a/b of an amount, divide by b and multiply by a, in either order, choosing whichever keeps the numbers whole.

### Successive fractions

**Worked example 6.** Sam has £360. He spends 1/3 of it on a coat and 2/5 of what is left on boots. What fraction of the £360 does he have left?

```
coat:   1/3 of 360 = 120,   left 360 − 120 = 240
boots:  2/5 of 240 = 96,    left 240 − 96  = 144
fraction left: 144/360 = 2/5
```

The trap is the phrase "of what is left". The 2/5 applies to £240, not to £360. A single-step check: he keeps 2/3, then keeps 3/5 of that, and 2/3 × 3/5 = 2/5.

### Fractions greater than 1

The method does not change. 5/3 of 4.2 kg is 4.2 ÷ 3 × 5 = 1.4 × 5 = **7 kg**. A recipe for 240 g scaled up by 1 3/4 needs 240 × 7/4 = 60 × 7 = **420 g**. An answer larger than the starting amount is a useful check that you used a fraction above 1.

### Finding the whole by dividing

If 3/8 of a number is 27, then the number × 3/8 = 27, so the number is 27 ÷ 3/8 = 27 × 8/3 = **72**. In words: 1/8 is 27 ÷ 3 = 9, so the whole is 9 × 8 = 72.

## One quantity as a fraction of another

Write "part over whole", in the **same units**, then simplify.

**Worked example 7.**

```
450 g as a fraction of 2 kg:        450/2 000 = 9/40
40 minutes as a fraction of 1 1/2 hours:  40/90 = 4/9
```

Convert first: 2 kg = 2 000 g and 1 1/2 hours = 90 minutes. The quantity after "of" goes on the bottom.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the denominators: 2/3 + 3/10 is not 5/13.
- Ignoring the order of operations in a mixed calculation.
- Subtracting mixed numbers part by part when the fraction parts give a negative.
- Inverting the first fraction, or both, when dividing. Only the divisor is inverted.
- Applying a second fraction to the original amount instead of "what is left".
- Mixing units in "as a fraction of" questions.
- Leaving an answer as an improper fraction when the question says "give your answer as a mixed number".

## Exam technique

- **"Give your answer in its simplest form"**: cancel fully; an unsimplified answer usually loses the final mark.
- **"Show that"**: write every step, including the final conversion that matches the given answer.
- **Non-calculator papers**: cancel before multiplying to keep the numbers small.
- **Context questions**: say what the fraction means, for example "2 full pieces".
