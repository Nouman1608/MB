## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a microblog. **[1]**

**2.** Describe the difference between a chat room and a forum. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** ways in which a business may use social networking. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by an echo chamber. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** risks to a government of using social media to share information. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** advantages of email compared with a chat room. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The Tern Hill Cycling Club has 2,500 followers. One post receives 175 likes and comments. The club pays £120 to promote the post, which brings 400 visits to its website, and 20 people buy a membership.

**(a)** Calculate the engagement rate of the post as a percentage. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost per visit. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of visits that led to a membership purchase. **[2]**

**(d)** Each membership brings the club £12 of income. State, with a calculation, whether the promotion paid for itself. **[2]**

**8.** A council uses a microblog to warn people about floods. Explain **two** reasons why this is useful. **[4]**

**9.** A teacher sets up a forum for her class to discuss a project. Explain **two** advantages of a forum for this task compared with instant messaging. **[4]**

**10.** Discuss the effects of social networking on the mental health of different age groups. **[6]**

**11.** A shop receives a review on a social media site that wrongly accuses it of selling unsafe goods. Describe the possible effect on the shop and how it could respond using social networking. **[4]**

**12.** Explain how social networking can lead to ideological polarisation. **[3]**

**13.** A government wants to use social networking to consult citizens on a new law. Evaluate this approach. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** A service where users share very short public posts that others can read, reply to and share [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A chat room is a live conversation among many users, usually on a topic, and messages may not be kept [1]. A forum organises posts into threads that stay visible so users can read and reply later [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: advertising products [1]; giving product information [1]; giving safety information about products [1]; collecting customer feedback [1]; answering customer questions [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A situation in which a person mainly sees opinions and information that match their own views [1], because of the people they follow and because the platform shows more of what they already like, so they rarely meet other views [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: false or distorted information spreads and is hard to correct [1]; people without internet access miss the information [1]; messages can be misread or attacked in replies [1]; accounts could be hacked and used to send false messages [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two from: email provides a formal record kept for later [1]; attachments can be sent [1]; messages are private to the recipient [1]; long messages can be written and sent to people anywhere [1]; works between different providers [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 175 ÷ 2,500 [1] = 7% [1].

**(b)** £120 ÷ 400 = £0.30 [1].

**(c)** 20 ÷ 400 [1] = 5% [1].

**(d)** 20 × £12 = £240 income [1], which is more than the £120 cost, so it paid for itself [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Messages can be posted instantly, so people get the warning as events happen [1], which allows them to move belongings or avoid affected roads [1]. Followers can share the message [1], which spreads it far beyond the council's own followers within minutes [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Posts are grouped into threads by topic, so pupils can find and follow each discussion [1], unlike an instant message stream where earlier comments are lost [1]. The forum is a lasting record that remains available for revision or for pupils who were absent [1]. The teacher can moderate posts and see all contributions [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Young people: comparing themselves with others can reduce self-esteem [1]; bullying can cause anxiety and depression [1]; late-night use reduces sleep [1]. Working adults: constant contact can cause stress [1]; online communities can give support [1]. Older people: staying in touch with family reduces loneliness [1]; they may be more at risk of scams, causing worry [1]. Conclusion: effects depend on how the platform is used, and on the person's age [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**11.** The review may be seen by many people and spread quickly [1], so customers may stay away and sales fall [1]. The shop could reply publicly and politely, giving the facts [1], ask the site to remove the review, and post evidence such as safety certificates on its own account [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Users choose to follow people with similar opinions [1], and the platform shows more content like it [1], so groups reinforce each other's views and move to more extreme positions [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Advantages: reaches many people quickly and cheaply [1]; citizens can reply easily, so more people take part [1]; feedback can be collected and analysed [1]. Disadvantages: people without internet access or confidence are left out [1]; responses may come from a loud minority and so not represent everyone [1]; false information or abuse may distort the discussion [1]. Conclusion, for example that it should be used alongside other methods such as postal surveys and meetings [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a list of platforms without describing how one differs from another.
- Writing about "people" in general rather than the group named in the question.
- Giving only positive effects in discuss and evaluate questions.
- Forgetting to link a feature (such as shared posts) to its effect (fast spread) and its consequence (people warned).
- Working out percentages against the wrong total.
