Cambridge O Level Chemistry (5070) is a single-tier chemistry course
covering the structure of matter, chemical reactions and their
quantities, the elements and their compounds, environmental chemistry,
organic chemistry and practical analysis. This page describes the
syllabus for the 2026-2028 examination series, which runs in June and
November. Every candidate takes a multiple-choice paper (30%), a theory
paper (50%) and one practical component (20%), all externally assessed.

## How the course is organised

5070 has 12 topics. A helpful way to see them is as a progression from
the particle level up to applied and practical chemistry.

- **Topic 1, States of matter:** solids, liquids and gases, and
  diffusion.
- **Topic 2, Atoms, elements and compounds:** elements, compounds and
  mixtures, atomic structure, isotopes, and ionic, covalent, giant
  covalent and metallic structures.
- **Topic 3, Stoichiometry:** formulae, relative masses, and the mole and
  the Avogadro constant.
- **Topics 4 to 6, Electrochemistry, Chemical energetics and Chemical
  reactions:** electrolysis and fuel cells, exothermic and endothermic
  reactions, physical and chemical change, rates, reversible reactions and
  equilibrium, and redox.
- **Topic 7, Acids, bases and salts:** properties of acids and bases,
  oxides, and the preparation of salts.
- **Topics 8 and 9, The Periodic Table and Metals:** the arrangement of
  elements, Group I and Group VII, transition elements and noble gases,
  then the properties, uses, alloys, reactivity, corrosion and extraction
  of metals.
- **Topic 10, Chemistry of the environment:** water, fertilisers, and air
  quality and climate.
- **Topic 11, Organic chemistry:** formulae and naming, fuels, alkanes,
  alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids and polymers.
- **Topic 12, Experimental techniques and chemical analysis:**
  experimental design, acid–base titrations, chromatography, separation
  and purification, and identification of ions and gases.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Theory | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 3 | Practical Test | 90 minutes | 40 | 20% |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Practical | 60 minutes | 40 | 20% |

All candidates take Papers 1 and 2. For the practical component, you
take either Paper 3, a Practical Test, or Paper 4, an Alternative to
Practical. The course is not tiered and the qualification is
component-based. Your school decides which practical option you sit, so
it is worth knowing early whether your practical marks will come from
bench work or a written paper.

## How the parts fit together

The theory paper, worth half the qualification, is where the links
between topics matter most. Calculations based on Stoichiometry turn up
in electrolysis, energetics, titrations and salt preparation. Bonding
and structure from Topic 2 explain the physical properties of metals, the
trends in Group I and Group VII, and the behaviour of organic molecules.
Redox ideas from Chemical reactions are needed again for electrolysis,
corrosion and metal extraction, and the reactivity series connects all
three.

Topic 12 underpins the practical component. Separation techniques,
titrations, chromatography and the identification of ions and gases each
apply chemistry learned elsewhere, so doing experiments throughout the
course is better preparation for Paper 3 or Paper 4 than studying
practical methods only at the end. The multiple-choice paper, meanwhile,
rewards broad and precise knowledge across all 12 topics.

Chemistry of the environment and Organic chemistry are good examples of
topics that look self-contained but are not. Fertilisers depend on acids,
bases and salt preparation; air quality and climate depend on combustion
and the products of burning fuels, which are also part of the organic
topic; and water treatment draws on separation and purification from
Topic 12. Making these connections explicit in your notes, for example
with a short "links to other topics" list at the end of each one, turns
twelve separate chapters into a single subject that is easier to revise.

## Planning your study

One reasonable approach for a two-year course:

- **First year, first half:** Topics 1 to 3, building fluency with
  formulae, equations and the mole.
- **First year, second half:** Topics 4 to 7, with practical work on
  rates, electrolysis and making salts.
- **Second year, first half:** Topics 8 to 10, connecting the Periodic
  Table and metals to environmental chemistry.
- **Second year, second half:** Topic 11, then whole-course revision.
- **Throughout:** Topic 12 skills, developed through regular experiments
  and analysis of results.

Attempt past-paper questions on each block once it has been taught. Full
timed papers and detailed paper technique belong in the revision period,
supported by the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same Paper 1 and Paper 2, plus one practical component.

**What is the difference between Paper 3 and Paper 4 in 5070?**
Paper 3 is a Practical Test lasting 90 minutes, and Paper 4 is an Alternative to Practical lasting 60 minutes. Both carry 40 marks and 20%, and you take one of them.

**How long is the O Level Chemistry theory paper?**
Paper 2, Theory, lasts 105 minutes and carries 80 marks, worth 50% of the qualification.

**How many topics are in O Level Chemistry 5070?**
There are 12 topics, from States of matter to Experimental techniques and chemical analysis.

**When are 5070 exams held?**
The 2026-2028 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.
