**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 19.2:
making nitriles from halogenoalkanes; making hydroxynitriles from aldehydes and ketones; hydrolysis of
nitriles to carboxylic acids.

## The three reactions

| Reaction | Reagents and conditions | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halogenoalkane → nitrile | KCN in **ethanol**, heat | nucleophilic substitution | CH₃CH₂Br + KCN → CH₃CH₂CN + KBr |
| Aldehyde or ketone → hydroxynitrile | HCN, **KCN catalyst**, heat | nucleophilic addition | CH₃CHO + HCN → CH₃CH(OH)CN |
| Nitrile → carboxylic acid | dilute acid, heat; or dilute alkali, heat, then acidify | hydrolysis | CH₃CH₂CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH₂COOH + NH₄Cl |

All three keep or add carbons: the first two **add one carbon**; hydrolysis keeps the count.

## Naming

- Count the C≡N carbon as **C1**: CH₃CH₂CH₂CN is **butanenitrile**.
- Hydroxynitriles: the OH is on **C2**. From ethanal: **2-hydroxypropanenitrile**. From propanone:
  **2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile**.
- Nitrile names are required for straight chains only.

## Nucleophilic addition of HCN, step by step

1. C=O is polar, Cᵟ⁺=Oᵟ⁻.
2. Curly arrow: lone pair on **C** of CN⁻ → δ+ carbonyl carbon. Curly arrow: C=O π bond → O.
3. Intermediate: alkoxide ion, with O⁻.
4. O⁻ takes H⁺ from HCN → hydroxynitrile + CN⁻ (regenerated).

KCN is a catalyst because HCN is a weak acid that provides few CN⁻ ions; CN⁻ is regenerated in step 4.

## Cyanide: same nucleophile, two mechanisms

| | With a halogenoalkane | With an aldehyde or ketone |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon attacked | sp³ carbon bonded to X | sp² carbonyl carbon |
| Leaving group | halide ion leaves | none: the π bond breaks |
| Mechanism | nucleophilic substitution | nucleophilic addition |
| Reagent | KCN in ethanol | HCN with KCN catalyst |

## Hydrolysis products

| Nitrile | Acid hydrolysis gives | Alkaline hydrolysis gives (before acidifying) |
|---|---|---|
| ethanenitrile, CH₃CN | ethanoic acid + NH₄⁺ | ethanoate ion + NH₃ |
| propanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CN | propanoic acid + NH₄⁺ | propanoate ion + NH₃ |
| 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, CH₃CH(OH)CN | 2-hydroxypropanoic acid + NH₄⁺ | 2-hydroxypropanoate ion + NH₃ |

Alkaline equation: RCN + NaOH + H₂O → RCOONa + NH₃. Then RCOONa + HCl → RCOOH + NaCl.

## Chirality in hydroxynitriles

- From an aldehyde other than methanal (e.g. ethanal, propanal): the new C(OH)(CN) carbon carries H, OH, CN
  and an alkyl group: **chiral**.
- From propanone: two CH₃ groups: **not chiral**.
- From butanone: CH₃, C₂H₅, OH and CN: **chiral**.

## Carbon-count routes

| Start (n carbons) | Route | Product (carbons) |
|---|---|---|
| halogenoalkane | KCN/ethanol, then hydrolysis | carboxylic acid (n + 1) |
| aldehyde | HCN/KCN, then hydrolysis | 2-hydroxycarboxylic acid (n + 1) |
| ketone | HCN/KCN | hydroxynitrile with C(OH)(CN) carbon carrying two alkyl groups (n + 1) |
| primary alcohol | oxidise (distil) to aldehyde, then HCN/KCN | hydroxynitrile (n + 1) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nitrile | compound containing the –C≡N group |
| Hydroxynitrile | compound containing both –OH and –C≡N, here on the same carbon |
| Nucleophilic substitution | a nucleophile replaces a leaving group |
| Nucleophilic addition | a nucleophile adds to a C=O carbon and the π bond breaks; nothing leaves |
| Catalyst | increases the rate and is regenerated, so is not used up |
| Alkoxide ion | intermediate with a negatively charged oxygen, R–O⁻ |

## Exam traps

- KCN must be **in ethanol** for halogenoalkanes; aqueous conditions give alcohol by-products.
- For hydroxynitriles, **HCN** is the reagent and **KCN** the catalyst.
- The cyanide curly arrow starts at the lone pair **on carbon**.
- Do not stop at the alkoxide: show O⁻ gaining H⁺ (from HCN, which regenerates CN⁻).
- Nitrile hydrolysis with alkali needs **acidification** to give the acid.
- CH₃CN is **ethanenitrile** (two carbons), not methanenitrile.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagent and conditions to make propanenitrile from bromoethane.
2. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN.
3. Give the reagents and conditions to make a hydroxynitrile from propanal.
4. Name the product of question 3.
5. Why is KCN described as a catalyst in hydroxynitrile formation?
6. Name the intermediate formed when CN⁻ attacks a carbonyl carbon.
7. Name the mechanism for the reaction of CN⁻ with (a) 1-chloropropane, (b) propanone.
8. Write the equation for acid hydrolysis of butanenitrile with HCl.
9. Which gas is released when a nitrile is heated with dilute NaOH?
10. Does the hydroxynitrile from propanone have a chiral centre? Explain.
11. Name the product when 2-hydroxypropanenitrile is hydrolysed by dilute acid.
12. Give a two-step route from chloromethane to ethanoic acid.

**Answers:**

1. KCN in ethanol, heat.
2. Pentanenitrile.
3. HCN with KCN catalyst, heat.
4. 2-hydroxybutanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN.
5. It provides CN⁻ ions, which HCN (a weak acid) supplies only in low concentration, and CN⁻ is regenerated
   in the final step, so KCN is not used up.
6. An alkoxide ion (negatively charged oxygen).
7. (a) nucleophilic substitution; (b) nucleophilic addition.
8. CH₃CH₂CH₂CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NH₄Cl.
9. Ammonia.
10. No: the carbon carries two identical CH₃ groups as well as OH and CN.
11. 2-hydroxypropanoic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH.
12. KCN in ethanol, heat → ethanenitrile; then dilute acid, heat → ethanoic acid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the hydroxynitrile mechanism part of 19.2?**
The mechanism is a 17.1 outcome (carbonyl compounds), with ethanal and propanone as the named examples, and
19.2 asks you to recall the reaction. Learn the mechanism with both topics.

**How can I recognise a nitrile in an infrared spectrum?**
The C≡N bond absorbs at 2200–2250 cm⁻¹, a region where few other common bonds absorb.
