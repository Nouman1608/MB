Condensed recall for section 3.1.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-stakeholders/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- What a **stakeholder** is and the main stakeholders: **owners, employees, customers, local community, government, suppliers**.
- Their **objectives**: maximising pay for workers, minimising environmental impact on the local community, high dividend payments for owners.
- The **impact** of business activity on stakeholders.
- The **influence** stakeholders have on a business.
- **Conflict** between stakeholders' interests.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder | Any person or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business |
| Shareholder | An owner of shares in a company; one type of stakeholder |
| Dividend | A payment to shareholders from profit |
| Trade union | A group of workers that negotiates pay and conditions |
| Conflict of interests | When stakeholders want things that cannot all be given at once |

## The six stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Wants | Can influence by |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | High profit and dividends; growing value | Voting; choosing managers; deciding profit use |
| Employees | High pay; job security; good conditions | Working harder or less; leaving; strikes; unions |
| Customers | Quality, fair prices, good service | Buying elsewhere; reviews; recommendations |
| Local community | Low pollution, noise and traffic; jobs | Protests; complaints; influencing planning permission |
| Government | Tax; legal compliance; jobs | Laws; taxes; licences; grants |
| Suppliers | Regular orders; prompt payment; fair prices | Raising prices; refusing supply; late delivery |

## Impact of business activity

For any decision, run through the stakeholders and say whether each gains or loses.

- **Opening a bigger factory:** owners may gain profit later; employees gain jobs; community gains jobs but loses peace; government gains tax; suppliers gain orders.
- **Cutting staff:** owners save costs; employees lose jobs; customers may get slower service; government may pay benefits.
- **Raising prices:** owners may gain profit; customers lose unless quality rises.

## What gives a stakeholder influence?

- Power: can they cause the business serious harm or benefit?
- Numbers: many customers acting together matter more than one.
- Importance: a sole supplier of a key part has high influence.
- Legal power: the government makes laws and sets taxes.

## Typical conflicts

| Conflict | Reason |
|---|---|
| Employees and owners | Higher pay reduces profit available for dividends |
| Customers and owners | Lower prices please customers but cut profit |
| Community and owners | Expansion brings jobs but also noise and pollution |
| Suppliers and owners | Late payment or lower prices help the business's cash but hurt suppliers |
| Government and owners | Higher taxes or tighter laws raise the business's costs |

Worked example: a 4% pay rise on a $1,200,000 wage bill costs 4% x $1,200,000 = $48,000. If profit was $200,000, then $152,000 is left for dividends, a 24% fall.

## Resolving conflicts

- **Consult** stakeholders and explain decisions.
- **Compromise** so each group gets some of what it wants.
- **Prioritise** the stakeholders who matter most to long-term success.
- **Find joint benefits**, such as training that raises skills and quality.

## Exam traps

- A stakeholder is not the same as a shareholder.
- Always say what the stakeholder **wants** and how they are **affected**.
- A conflict question needs **two** named groups and a reason.
- Do not assume owners always matter most; employees, customers and the community can change a business's actions.
- Use case details such as figures and place names in analysis.
- Judge by **power** and **long-term effect**.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "analyse conflict" question

Question style: "Analyse the conflict between employees and owners when a business has a profit of $200,000."

1. **State the interests:** employees want higher pay and job security; owners want high dividends.
2. **Show the clash with figures:** a 4% rise on a $1,200,000 wage bill costs $48,000, which reduces the profit available for dividends from $200,000 to $152,000.
3. **Explain the effect on each:** employees gain security and motivation; owners lose income now, and may complain or sell their shares.
4. **Link to the business:** a compromise, such as a smaller rise plus a bonus, can keep both groups reasonably satisfied.

A conflict answer must name **two groups**, say what **each wants** and show **why one gain is the other's loss**.

## Revision checklist

- I can define a stakeholder and name the six in the specification.
- I can state the main objective of each stakeholder.
- I can explain how a decision affects at least three stakeholders.
- I can describe how each stakeholder can influence a business.
- I can give five examples of conflict between stakeholders.
- I can name four ways of resolving conflicts.
- I can calculate a dividend per share and the cost of a pay rise.

## Self-test

1. Define "stakeholder".
2. Name the six main stakeholders in the specification.
3. State one objective of owners and one objective of employees.
4. State one objective of the local community.
5. Explain one way a business decision could benefit the local community.
6. Explain one way customers can influence a business.
7. Give one reason why suppliers want a business to pay promptly.
8. Describe one conflict between owners and employees.
9. A company's wage bill is $500,000 and staff get a 6% rise. Calculate the extra cost.
10. A business makes a profit of $90,000 and pays out 40% as dividends to 3,000 shares. Calculate the dividend per share.
11. Give one way a business could resolve a conflict between stakeholders.
12. Explain why the government is a stakeholder of a factory.

### Answers

1. A person or group with an interest in, or affected by, what a business does.
2. Owners, employees, customers, local community, government, suppliers.
3. Owners: high dividends or profit. Employees: higher pay (or job security or good conditions).
4. Any one: minimise pollution, noise or traffic; create local jobs.
5. For example, a new factory creates local jobs and more spending in local shops.
6. For example, customers can stop buying and go to a rival, which cuts the business's sales.
7. Late payment harms the supplier's cash flow, so it may struggle to pay its own bills.
8. Employees want higher pay, but higher pay reduces the profit available to pay dividends to owners.
9. 6% x $500,000 = **$30,000**.
10. 40% x $90,000 = $36,000; $36,000 / 3,000 = **$12** per share.
11. Any one: consult, compromise, prioritise, or find a decision that benefits both groups.
12. The government receives tax from the factory's profit and wages, and sets laws it must follow, such as safety and pollution rules.
