## What you must be able to do

- Write equations for chlorine with **water** (to chloride and chlorate(I); and to chloride and oxygen).
- Write the equation for chlorine with **cold, dilute NaOH**, and state the **uses** of the solution.
- Explain these reactions as **disproportionation** where appropriate.
- Appreciate the **benefits and risks** of water treatment with chlorine.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Disproportionation | The same element is oxidised and reduced in one reaction |
| Chlorate(I) | The ClO⁻ ion; chlorine is +1 |
| Bleach | Solution of sodium chlorate(I) (with sodium chloride) |
| Oxidising agent | Species that takes electrons |
| Water treatment | Adding chemicals to make water safe to drink |

## Oxidation states of chlorine

| Species | Cl₂ | Cl⁻ | ClO⁻ | HClO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxidation state | 0 | –1 | +1 | +1 |

## Chlorine and water

```
Cl₂(g) + H₂O(l) ⇌ HClO(aq) + HCl(aq)
Cl₂(g) + H₂O(l) ⇌ 2H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) + ClO⁻(aq)
```

- Disproportionation: Cl 0 → –1 (reduced) and 0 → +1 (oxidised).
- The solution is acidic and the ClO⁻ / HClO is a strong oxidising agent.
- ClO⁻ kills bacteria.

In sunlight:

```
2Cl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 4H⁺(aq) + 4Cl⁻(aq) + O₂(g)
```

Chlorine is reduced (0 to –1) and oxygen is oxidised (–2 to 0). It is not disproportionation.

## Chlorine and cold, dilute sodium hydroxide

```
Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O
Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O
```

- Disproportionation again: Cl 0 → –1 and 0 → +1.
- Must be **cold and dilute**; hot concentrated alkali gives a different product.
- The solution contains sodium chlorate(I), the active bleaching agent.

**Uses of the solution:** household bleach, disinfectant for kitchens and bathrooms, and treatment of swimming pool water.

## Water treatment with chlorine

| Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|
| Kills bacteria and viruses | Chlorine gas is toxic and corrosive |
| Prevents diseases such as cholera and typhoid | Forms chlorinated hydrocarbons with organic matter, some suspected to be carcinogenic |
| A residual level keeps the water safe in pipes | Taste, smell and eye or skin irritation |

Conclusion for exams: the benefits to health outweigh the toxic effects, because only very small amounts are used and the diseases prevented are serious.

## Society and water supplies

The specification asks you to appreciate that society weighs advantages and disadvantages when deciding whether chemicals should be added to water supplies. Fluoride is an example.

- Advantage: protects teeth from decay.
- Disadvantage: excess can harm teeth; some people object because everyone receives it without choice.
- Give both sides in a discussion.

## Worked examples

**Mass of bleach.** 7.10 g of Cl₂ (Mᵣ 71.0) gives 0.100 mol Cl₂, so 0.100 mol NaClO (Mᵣ 74.5) = 7.45 g. If 6.20 g is obtained, yield = 6.20 ÷ 7.45 × 100 = 83.2%.

**Volume of NaOH.** 0.0100 mol Cl₂ needs 0.0200 mol NaOH (ratio 1 : 2). At 0.200 mol dm⁻³, volume = 0.100 dm³.

**Dosing.** 0.50 mg dm⁻³ in 5.00 × 10⁴ dm³ = 25.0 g of chlorine.

## Showing disproportionation

1. Write the equation.
2. Give the oxidation state of the element in the reactant.
3. Give the oxidation state in each product.
4. State that one product is higher (oxidised) and one lower (reduced).

## Mark-scheme style answers

**Why is chlorine added to water?** It kills bacteria; the chlorate(I) ions it forms are oxidising agents.

**Why is the reaction with water disproportionation?** Chlorine is oxidised from 0 to +1 (in chlorate(I)) and reduced from 0 to –1 (in chloride) in the same reaction.

**Why must the alkali be cold and dilute?** Cold, dilute alkali gives chlorate(I); hot, concentrated alkali gives a different product with chlorine in a higher oxidation state.

**What does bleach do?** The chlorate(I) ion is an oxidising agent. It oxidises coloured compounds to colourless ones and kills microorganisms.

## Safety and handling

- Chlorine is a toxic, corrosive gas; reactions should be done in a fume cupboard.
- Bleach should not be mixed with acids, because chlorine gas is released: ClO⁻ + Cl⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl₂ + H₂O.
- Store bleach away from light and heat, since chlorate(I) decomposes.

## Exam traps

- Missing the chlorate(I) ion in the reaction with water.
- Single arrow for the equilibrium with water.
- Hot or concentrated NaOH given for making bleach.
- No oxidation states in a disproportionation answer.
- Sunlight reaction written with chlorate(I) instead of oxygen.
- Calling the sunlight reaction disproportionation.
- One-sided answers in "discuss" questions.
- Confusing chlorate(I) with chlorate(V).

## Self-test

1. What is the oxidation state of chlorine in ClO⁻?
2. Define disproportionation.
3. Write an equation for chlorine with water giving chloride and chlorate(I) ions.
4. What gas is formed when chlorine water is left in sunlight?
5. Write the equation for chlorine with cold dilute sodium hydroxide.
6. Name one use of sodium chlorate(I) solution.
7. Why is chlorine added to drinking water?
8. Give one risk of chlorine in water treatment.
9. Why do the benefits outweigh the risks?
10. How many moles of NaOH react with 0.050 mol of Cl₂?
11. Is the reaction of chlorine with water in sunlight disproportionation?
12. What is the colour of chlorine water?

### Answers

1. +1.
2. A reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced.
3. Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ 2H⁺ + Cl⁻ + ClO⁻.
4. Oxygen.
5. Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O.
6. Household bleach (or disinfectant, or swimming pool water treatment).
7. To kill bacteria and other microorganisms.
8. Chlorine is toxic, or it forms chlorinated hydrocarbons.
9. Only small amounts are needed and the disease prevented is serious.
10. 0.10 mol.
11. No. Chlorine is only reduced.
12. Pale green.
