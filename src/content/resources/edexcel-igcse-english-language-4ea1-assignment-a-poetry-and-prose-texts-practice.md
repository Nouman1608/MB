Assignment A is a coursework essay of about 1200 words on three Anthology Part 2 texts, marked out of
30 (AO1 12, AO2 18). Section A of this page practises the decisions and paragraph skills; Section B
gives full assignment tasks with indicative content. Have your anthology open.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A student chooses "The Necklace", "Night" and "The Story of an Hour". Explain whether this trio
meets the requirements, and why. **[2]**

**2.** A student proposes the focus "nature". Explain one problem with this focus for a three-text
essay and suggest an improved focus. **[2]**

**3.** Write a task in the specification's suggested format for a focus on "fear" using one poem and two
prose texts from Part 2. **[2]**

**4.** Improve this thesis statement so that it makes an arguable claim about all three texts:
*"In this essay I will write about loss in three texts."* **[3]**

**5.** For each sentence, state whether it mainly targets AO1 or AO2, and why.
(a) *"Maupassant suggests that Mathilde's desire to be admired is the real cause of her suffering."*
(b) *"The twist in the final line forces the reader to reread the whole story as irony."* **[2]**

**6.** Rewrite this retelling sentence as an analytical sentence:
*"In 'The Necklace', Mathilde loses the necklace and has to work for ten years to pay for a new one."* **[3]**

**7.** A student's teacher writes on a draft: *"Your third paragraph retells the poem; focus on method."*
Explain whether this feedback is allowed and what the student must do next. **[2]**

**8.** Explain, with an example, the difference between using a quotation and a reference to a specific
moment as a textual reference. **[2]**

## Section B: full assignment tasks

Each task is marked out of 30 (AO1 12, AO2 18). Aim for about 1200 words.

**9.** Discuss how the writers use language and structure to present relationships within families in
"Out, Out–", "Night" and "The Story of an Hour".
In your response you should:
- discuss the ideas and perspectives of the writers about family relationships
- discuss how the writers use language and structure to achieve their effects
- include textual references to illustrate the points you make. **[30]**

**10.** Discuss how the writers use language and structure to present identity and how others see us in
"Still I Rise", "An Unknown Girl" and "The Necklace". **[30]**

**11.** For a trio of your choice (meeting the requirements), write a thesis statement and three
paragraph headings for an integrated essay, each heading naming the methods you will analyse. **[6]**

**12.** Read this student paragraph, decide which AO2 level it best fits, and give **two** reasons. Then
write one sentence that would move it up a level.

> *In "Disabled" Owen uses lots of imagery. He talks about the past and the present. This shows that
> the man is sad. In "The Necklace" the ending is a twist which is shocking for the reader.* **[4]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** It does not meet the requirements (1): all three are prose texts, and at least one poetry text is
needed alongside at least one prose text (1).

**2.** One mark for a valid problem: for example, few Part 2 texts present nature centrally, or the
focus is a topic rather than an idea to argue (1). One mark for a narrower, arguable focus that fits
three texts, such as "settings that reflect a character's feelings" (1).

**3.** One mark for the correct format; one for a valid trio (one poem plus two prose texts) (2).
Example: *"Discuss how the writers use language and structure to present fear in 'Out, Out–',
'Whistle and I'll Come to You' and 'Night'."*

**4.** One mark for naming the three texts or the writers (1); one for a claim about how loss is
presented (1); one for a distinction between the texts (1). Example: *"Owen, Frost and Maupassant all
present loss as irreversible, but where Frost's calm narration makes it seem brutally ordinary, Owen
and Maupassant use structure to make the reader feel the gap between what was and what is."*

**5.** (a) AO1: it interprets the writer's idea or perspective about the character (1). (b) AO2: it
analyses a structural device and its effect on the reader (1).

**6.** One mark for naming a method (for example time compression, contrast or structure) (1); one for
the effect on the reader (1); one for linking to an idea (1). Example: *"Maupassant compresses ten years
of labour into a brief summary, so the reader feels the sudden, crushing cost of a single proud decision."*

**7.** Yes, it is allowed: teachers may suggest general improvements at draft stage (1). The student
must make the corrections and update the work themselves (1).

**8.** A quotation uses the writer's exact words, such as Owen's closing question "Why don't they
come?" (1); a reference to a moment cites a specific event without quoting, such as Madame Loisel
choosing the diamond necklace over her friend's other jewels (1).

### Section B: how to mark questions 9 and 10

Use the specification's AO1 grid (four levels) and AO2 grid (five levels) on a best-fit basis. Higher
responses show "perceptive interpretation" (AO1 Level 4) and "perceptive analysis" with "subtle and
discriminating selection" (AO2 Level 5).

**9. Indicative content.**

| AO | Points a strong essay might make |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Frost's family respond to the boy's death with practical acceptance; Munro's narrator recalls her father's response to a frightening confession; Chopin presents marriage as a bond that limits the self, revealed when Louise imagines life alone |
| AO2 | Frost: the sister's call to supper placed just before the accident; the narrator's voice wishing the work had stopped; the abrupt final lines. Munro: retrospective adult narration looking back on youth; night as a setting for private fear; the father's restrained dialogue. Chopin: the open window; the sister at the closed door; the ironic final sentence |
| Connections | Family as protection or limitation; what is said and unsaid between family members; endings that return characters to ordinary life |

**10. Indicative content.**

| AO | Points a strong essay might make |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Angelou's speaker refuses the identity others impose; Alvi's speaker feels both connected to and separate from the culture of the bazaar; Mathilde builds her sense of herself around how wealthy she appears |
| AO2 | Angelou: direct address, rhetorical questions, similes, the refrain. Alvi: short lines, present tense, imagery of the henna pattern. Maupassant: contrast between Mathilde's dreams and her home; the ball as her moment of being seen; the twist ending |
| Connections | Identity as defiance (Angelou), as longing (Alvi), as performance with a cost (Maupassant) |

**11.** One mark for a valid trio (1); two marks for a thesis making an arguable claim about all three
(2); one mark for each paragraph heading that names an aspect of the focus and specific methods (3).
Total 6.

**12.** Level 2 (some developing comment) (1). Reasons, any two (2): devices are identified but not
analysed ("lots of imagery"); no textual references; the comment on effect is simple ("sad", "shocking");
the texts are not connected. One sentence that moves it up, with a specific reference and explained
effect (1). Example: *"Owen sets the bright, crowded memory of the town against the dark present, so
each return to the wheelchair feels like the loss happening again."* Total 4.
