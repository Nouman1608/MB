## Questions

**1.** For a randomly chosen pupil, A is the event "walks to school" and B is the event "has a packed lunch". Write each of the following using probability notation.

**(a)** The pupil does not walk to school. **[1]**
**(b)** The pupil walks to school and has a packed lunch. **[1]**
**(c)** The pupil walks to school or has a packed lunch (or both). **[1]**

**2.** The ten letters of the word PERCENTILE are written on identical tiles and placed in a bag. One tile is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that it is an E. Give your answer as a fraction. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability that it is a vowel. Give your answer as a decimal. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that it is not an E. Give your answer as a percentage. **[1]**

**3.** Find the probability of the complement in each case.

**(a)** The probability that it rains on a given day is 0.35. Find the probability that it does not rain. **[1]**
**(b)** The probability that a match has no goals is 5/12. Find the probability that it has at least one goal. **[1]**
**(c)** P(A) = 64%. Find P(A′) as a percentage. **[1]**

**4.** Two fair dice are rolled and the scores are added.

**(a)** Find the probability that the total is 8. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the total is more than 3. **[2]**

**5.** A bag holds 7 yellow and 3 green counters. Two counters are taken one after the other.

**(a)** The first counter is replaced before the second is taken. Find the probability that both are green. **[2]**
**(b)** The first counter is not replaced. Find the probability that both are green. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the answer to (b) differs from the answer to (a). **[1]**

**6.** A hat holds the names of 5 girls and 4 boys. Two names are drawn without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that one name is a girl's and one is a boy's. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that both names are of the same gender. **[2]**

**7.** A shelf holds 9 mugs and 4 of them are chipped. Three mugs are taken without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that none of the three is chipped. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that at least one is chipped. **[2]**

**8.** For two events, P(A) = 0.48, P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.12.

**(a)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that neither A nor B happens. **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(A ∩ B′). **[1]**

**9.** For two events, P(A) = 0.64, P(B) = 0.38 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.85.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that B happens but A does not. **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(A′ ∩ B′). **[1]**

**10.** A library has 120 registered readers. Of these, 70 have borrowed fiction (F), 55 have borrowed non-fiction (N) and 25 have borrowed both. A reader is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find P(F). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(F ∩ N). **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(F ∪ N). **[2]**
**(d)** Find the probability that the reader has borrowed neither. **[1]**
**(e)** Two different readers are chosen at random. Find the probability that neither has borrowed either. **[2]**

**11.** A bag holds 6 red, 4 blue and 2 green counters. Two counters are taken without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that both are red. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the two counters are different colours. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the probability that at least one counter is green. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) P(A′) [1]
(b) P(A ∩ B) [1]
(c) P(A ∪ B) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Learn the symbols as pairs: ∩ goes with "and" and ∪ goes with "or", and writing the word beside the symbol in your working helps you avoid swapping them.

**2.** (a) E appears 3 times in 10 letters, so P(E) = **3/10** [1]
(b) The vowels are E, E, I and E, which is 4 tiles, so P(vowel) = 4/10 = **0.4** [1]
(c) There are 3 E tiles, so P(not E) = 1 – 3/10 = 7/10 = **70%** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Count repeated letters one tile at a time. A common slip is to count the letter types (8 different letters) instead of the 10 tiles.

**3.** (a) 1 – 0.35 = **0.65** [1]
(b) 1 – 5/12 = **7/12** [1]
(c) 100% – 64% = **36%** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the form the question uses. Switching between decimal, fraction and percentage in the middle of an answer invites arithmetic slips and can leave the final form unclear.

**4.** (a) There are 36 equally likely pairs. The pairs with total 8 are (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3) and (6,2), so 5 pairs. [1] P = **5/36** [1]
(b) A total of 3 or less comes from (1,1), (1,2) and (2,1), which is 3 pairs. [1] P(more than 3) = 1 – 3/36 = 33/36 = **11/12** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A written list or table of the outcomes shows your counting. A bare fraction with a wrong count gives the reader nothing to credit.

**5.** (a) Each pick is from 10 counters, so P(both green) = 3/10 × 3/10 [1] = 9/100 = **0.09** [1]
(b) P(both green) = 3/10 × 2/9 [1] = 6/90 = **1/15** [1]
(c) After a green counter is kept out, only 2 green counters remain among 9 counters, so the second pick has a smaller chance of green. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "state why" part, link the change in the numbers to the context. "The numbers change" alone is vague, while "2 green left out of 9" is specific.

**6.** (a) Girl then boy: 5/9 × 4/8 = 20/72. [1] Boy then girl: 4/9 × 5/8 = 20/72. [1] P(one of each) = 40/72 = **5/9** [1]
(b) "Same gender" is the complement of "one of each". [1] P = 1 – 5/9 = **4/9** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* If the question gives no order, include both orders. Check part (b) by adding girl-girl 20/72 and boy-boy 12/72, which also gives 32/72 = 4/9.

**7.** (a) 5 mugs are not chipped. P(none chipped) = 5/9 × 4/8 × 3/7 [1] = 60/504 = **5/42** [1]
(b) "At least one" is the complement of "none". [1] P = 1 – 5/42 = **37/42** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Reduce the numerator and denominator by one at each pick. Keeping 9 as the total on every pick is a frequent error without replacement.

**8.** (a) P(A ∪ B) = 0.48 + 0.35 – 0.12 [1] = **0.71** [1]
(b) P(neither) = 1 – 0.71 = **0.29** [1]
(c) P(A ∩ B′) = 0.48 – 0.12 = **0.36** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the rule first and then substitute. Showing the subtracted overlap makes it clear you did not simply add the two probabilities.

**9.** (a) Rearranging the rule, P(A ∩ B) = P(A) + P(B) – P(A ∪ B) [1] = 0.64 + 0.38 – 0.85 = **0.17** [1]
(b) P(B ∩ A′) = 0.38 – 0.17 = **0.21** [1]
(c) P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 – 0.85 = **0.15** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* When a union is given, rearrange the rule before you substitute. Check that your overlap is no larger than either single probability.

**10.** (a) P(F) = 70/120 = **7/12** [1]
(b) P(F ∩ N) = 25/120 = **5/24** [1]
(c) P(F ∪ N) = 70/120 + 55/120 – 25/120 [1] = 100/120 = **5/6** [1]
(d) P(neither) = 1 – 5/6 = **1/6** [1]
(e) 20 readers have borrowed neither, so P(first has borrowed neither) = 20/120 and then P(second has borrowed neither) = 19/119. [1] P = 20/120 × 19/119 = **19/714** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the earlier parts. Part (d) gives 20 readers from 120, and for (e) the numerator and denominator both drop by one, because the first reader is not put back.

**11.** (a) P(both red) = 6/12 × 5/11 [1] = 30/132 = **5/22** [1]
(b) Same colour: blue 4/12 × 3/11 = 12/132 and green 2/12 × 1/11 = 2/132. [1] With red 30/132, P(same) = 44/132 = 1/3. [1] Different colours is the complement. [1] P = 1 – 1/3 = **2/3** [1]
(c) P(no green) = 10/12 × 9/11 = 90/132. [1] P(at least one green) = 1 – 90/132 = 42/132 = **7/22** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For "different colours" with three colours, finding "same colour" and subtracting from 1 needs three short products instead of six. Write each product clearly so the sum is easy to follow.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating "A or B" as a sum of the two separate probabilities, with no allowance for the overlap.
- Mixing up ∩ and ∪ in notation questions.
- Using the full total on every pick when the question says "without replacement".
- Counting only one order for "one of each" or "different colours".
- Counting letter types instead of tiles when letters repeat, as in question 2.
- Using a long list for "at least one" when the complement is quicker.
- Not simplifying a fraction, or giving a decimal or percentage when the question asked for another form.
- Writing only a final answer. The syllabus says essential working must be shown for full marks to be awarded.
