Condensed for the final weeks. For the full explanation, use the
[Post-2000 Poetry and Prose study guide](/resources/a-level-edexcel-english-literature-post-2000-poetry-and-prose/).
Unit 1 (WET01/01) is the first of four units in Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level
English Literature (YET01). The others are Unit 2 (drama: one pre-1900 and one post-1900 play),
Unit 3 (an unseen post-1900 poem and two prose texts from a chosen theme) and Unit 4 (Shakespeare
and pre-1900 poetry).

## Unit 1 at a glance

| Section | Focus | Marks | AOs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Comparative essay: named poem + own choice from the prescribed *Poems of the Decade* list | 25 | AO1, AO2, AO4 |
| B | Essay on chosen post-2000 prose fiction text | 25 | AO1, AO2, AO3 |

**Set texts (revised by specification issue, not by series):** 21 prescribed
poems from the *Poems of the Decade* anthology (ISBN 978-0571325405) for Section A, and one of
five prose titles for Section B — *Brooklyn*, *The Kite Runner*, *Life of
Pi*, *Purple Hibiscus* and *The White Tiger* (see the [study guide](/resources/a-level-edexcel-english-literature-post-2000-poetry-and-prose/) for authors, page numbers and full detail).

**The 21 prescribed poems (specification Appendix 5):** *Eat Me* (Patience Agbabi); *Chainsaw Versus the Pampas Grass* (Simon Armitage); *Material* (Ros Barber); *History* (John Burnside); *An Easy Passage* (Julia Copus); *The Deliverer* (Tishani Doshi); *The Map Woman* (Carol Ann Duffy); *The Lammas Hireling* (Ian Duhig); *To My Nine-Year-Old Self* (Helen Dunmore); *A Minor Role* (U A Fanthorpe); *The Gun* (Vicki Feaver); *The Furthest Distances I’ve Travelled* (Leontia Flynn); *Giuseppe* (Roderick Ford); *Out of the Bag* (Seamus Heaney); *Effects* (Alan Jenkins); *Genetics* (Sinéad Morrissey); *From the Journal of a Disappointed Man* (Andrew Motion); *Look We Have Coming to Dover* (Daljit Nagra); *Please Hold* (Ciaran O’Driscoll); *On Her Blindness* (Adam Thorpe); *Ode on a Grayson Perry Urn* (Tim Turnbull).

2 hours, **open book**, 50 marks total = 50% of the IAS, 25% of the full IAL. Both sections offer a
choice of two questions, so four exam-day decisions in total (two per section, though you only
answer one per section) shape how the paper actually plays out for you.

## Section A — comparative poetry

- One poem is named by the exam board; you choose the second from the 21 prescribed *Poems of the Decade* poems listed above.
- Marked against AO1 (coherent, accurate argument), AO2 (analysing the ways in which meanings are shaped — through form, structure and language among other methods, not a fixed checklist), AO4 (connections across texts) — note AO3 (context) is **not** assessed in this section, unlike Section B.
- Since it's open book, the value isn't in memorised quotations but in **fast, precise textual navigation** — know your anthology well enough to locate strong evidence quickly.
- There is a choice of two questions in Section A, each requiring one comparative essay — read both
  before choosing, since one may suit the poems you feel strongest on far better than the other.
- Structure comparative answers **by point of comparison**, not poem-by-poem — this is what makes
  AO4 (connections across texts) visible throughout the response rather than only in a conclusion.

## Section B — post-2000 prose

- AO1, AO2, AO3 (context) — context here is contemporary, not historical.
- Both texts postdate 2000, so contextual analysis should focus on **recent social/cultural concerns** the writer engages with, not period history.
- There is also a choice of two questions in Section B — one essay from that choice, on your chosen post-2000 prose fiction text.
- The prescribed prose text is revised by specification issue, not rotated by examination series, so confirm which specification issue governs your own series before beginning detailed revision — do not expect a different title each series.

## Where marks tend to be lost

Weaker Section A responses discuss the two poems in separate halves rather than integrating them
point-by-point, which caps how much AO4 credit is available even if each individual poem is
analysed well. Weaker Section B responses drift into general "issue" commentary — describing what
a novel is "about" — instead of analysing the specific techniques (structure, narrative voice, form)
through which the writer explores that concern, which is what AO2 actually rewards.

## Where Unit 1 sits in the wider qualification

Units 1-2 together make up the International Advanced Subsidiary (IAS): Unit 1 (this paper,
Post-2000 Poetry and Prose) and Unit 2 (Drama, also 2 hours, 50 marks, open book, with one pre-1900
and one post-1900 play). At the full A-Level stage, Unit 3 sets a compulsory unseen post-1900 poem
and two prose texts from a chosen theme (the theme lists include pre-1900 as well as later novels),
and Unit 4 sets a Shakespeare play and pre-1900 poetry. Studying contemporary poetry
and prose first is a common and sensible teaching order rather than a sequence the specification
itself mandates: it allows modular teaching at appropriate stages or a linear course assessed at
the end, with no prescribed unit order. Where centres do teach in this order, it lets candidates
build analytical skill on recent, more accessible texts before tackling the older, more
linguistically demanding material later in the course. If you're only sitting the IAS (not continuing to the
full IAL), Unit 1 is worth double its full-IAL weighting: 50% of the AS rather than 25% of the full
A-Level.

## Exam traps

- Using Section A's AO4 (connections) as an excuse to skip close analysis of either individual poem.
- Bringing in 19th/20th-century-style historical context for post-2000 texts, when the relevant context is contemporary.
- Forgetting the exam is open book — wasting revision time memorising quotations that could instead go toward analytical technique.
- Confusing which AOs apply to which section — Section A has no AO3, Section B does.
- Assuming the set prose text changes every examination series — it is revised by specification issue, not rotated by series, so confirm the specification issue rather than expecting a new title each series.
- Choosing a Section A or Section B question in the exam hall on gut instinct rather than briefly reading both options first.

## Self-test

1. What proportion of the full IAL does Unit 1 represent?
2. Which assessment objective is tested in Section B but not Section A?
3. Why does being open-book change what's worth prioritising in revision?
4. What kind of context is relevant for post-2000 texts?
5. How many question choices does each section offer, and why does this matter for exam-day strategy?
6. Which two units make up the International Advanced Subsidiary, and how do they differ in focus?
7. In a common teaching order, why might Unit 1 come before Unit 4?

**Answers:** 1. 25% of the full International Advanced Level (50% of the AS). 2. AO3 (context). 3. Since the text is available in the exam, memorising exact quotations matters less than being able to navigate the text quickly and analyse it well under time pressure. 4. Contemporary social and cultural context, since both set texts were published after 2000. 5. Two questions per section — reading both before committing lets you choose the option that best fits the poems and prose text you know most confidently, rather than answering the first question you see. 6. Units 1 and 2 — Unit 1 covers post-2000 poetry and prose, Unit 2 covers drama, both open book and worth 50 marks / 25% of the full IAL each. 7. Post-2000 texts are generally more linguistically accessible than the Shakespeare and pre-1900 poetry studied in Unit 4, so studying them first can let candidates build core analytical skills before tackling older, more demanding language — though the specification itself prescribes no unit order.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Edexcel International A Level English Literature Unit 1 open book?**
Yes. Unit 1, Post-2000 Poetry and Prose, is a 2-hour open-book exam worth 50 marks, which is 50% of the International AS and 25% of the full International A Level.

**What are the set texts for Edexcel YET01 Unit 1?**
Section A uses the 21 prescribed poems from the Poems of the Decade anthology, and Section B uses one of five prose titles: Brooklyn, The Kite Runner, Life of Pi, Purple Hibiscus or The White Tiger. The texts are revised by specification issue, not rotated by series.

**Do I choose both poems in Section A?**
No. One poem is named by the exam board and you choose the second from the prescribed list to compare with it.

**What are the other units of Edexcel International A Level English Literature?**
Unit 2 is drama, with one pre-1900 and one post-1900 play; Unit 3 sets an unseen post-1900 poem and two prose texts from a chosen theme; and Unit 4 covers Shakespeare and pre-1900 poetry. Units 1 and 2 make up the International Advanced Subsidiary.
