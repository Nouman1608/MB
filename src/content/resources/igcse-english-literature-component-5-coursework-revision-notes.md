Component 5 is the optional coursework component of Cambridge IGCSE Literature in English (0475): you
submit a portfolio of **two assignments of 600–1200 words each, on two different complete texts**,
marked by your teacher out of 25 and moderated by Cambridge. It is taken only alongside Paper 1 and
Paper 3 Drama (Open Text), and it is assessed on the same four assessment objectives as the exams.
For the full explanation, use the
[Component 5 Coursework study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-english-literature-component-5-coursework-study-guide/).

## Which route includes Component 5?

The 0475 syllabus for 2026 allows three routes. Every candidate takes Paper 1 Poetry and Prose (50%).
They then take **one** of:

| Route | Components after Paper 1 | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paper 2 Drama | 50% |
| 2 | Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) + Paper 4 Unseen | 25% + 25% |
| 3 | Paper 3 Drama (Open Text) + **Component 5 Coursework** | 25% + **25%** |

So Component 5 replaces Paper 4 Unseen, not Paper 2, and it is worth a quarter of the qualification.
Confirm your route with your teacher before you plan your revision.

## Format at a glance

| Rule (2026 syllabus) | Detail |
|---|---|
| Portfolio | Two assignments, each on a different text |
| Length | 600–1200 words each, **including quotations**, excluding references and bibliography |
| Text basis | Study of one complete text per assignment |
| Text standard | Equivalent in scope and demand to the Paper 1, 2 and 3 set texts; originally written in English |
| Set texts | One assignment (not both) may be on a text set for Papers 1, 2 and 3 |
| Poetry or short stories | Cover at least **two** poems or stories; comparison is not required |
| Empathic option | One assignment may be an empathic response to a prose or drama text |
| Titles | Must allow all four AOs to be assessed |
| Marking | Each assignment marked out of 25; component worth 25 marks |
| Assessment | Internally assessed, externally moderated by Cambridge |

The syllabus warns that candidates "may disadvantage themselves" if assignments are significantly over
or under the word guidance. Aim for the middle of the range, not the edge.

## Key terms

| Term | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Portfolio | The two assignments you finally submit, chosen from more pieces written during the course |
| Critical assignment | An analytical essay answering a title on a text |
| Empathic assignment | A piece written in the voice of a character from a prose or drama text, at a chosen moment |
| Internal assessment | Your teacher marks the work against the level descriptors |
| External moderation | Cambridge checks a sample of the centre's marking and can adjust it |
| Individual Candidate Record Card | The form that must accompany each portfolio |

## The four assessment objectives

All four AOs carry equal weight (25% each) across the qualification.

| AO | Wording (summarised from the syllabus) | In coursework this looks like |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Detailed knowledge of the text, supported by reference | Well-chosen quotations from across the whole text |
| AO2 | Understanding of meanings and contexts, beyond the surface | A sustained argument about ideas and attitudes |
| AO3 | How writers use language, structure and form | Close analysis of specific choices and their effects |
| AO4 | A sensitive and informed personal response | Your own evaluative judgement, kept up to the conclusion |

In an empathic task the descriptors shift: AO1 becomes using textual detail seamlessly, and AO3 becomes
sustaining a convincing voice for the character.

## How the levels work

The Component 5 criteria have eight levels. Teachers choose the best-fit level, then place the work high,
middle or low in the band depending on whether it "convincingly", "adequately" or "just" meets it.

| Level | Marks | Key phrases from the descriptors |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 23–25 | reference used "skilfully and with flair"; "individuality and insight"; "sustains personal and evaluative engagement" |
| 7 | 20–22 | "much well-selected reference"; "clear critical understanding"; "perceptive, convincing" response |
| 6 | 17–19 | "careful and relevant reference"; "some of its deeper implications"; "developed response" to effects |
| 5 | 14–16 | "some thoroughness" in evidence; "some response to the way the writer uses language" |
| 4 | 11–13 | "some supporting textual detail"; "some understanding of meaning" |
| 3 | 8–10 | "a little supporting reference"; "basic understanding of surface meaning" |
| 2 | 5–7 | "a few straightforward comments" |
| 1 | 1–4 | "limited textual reference" |

The jump from Level 6 to Level 7 is mainly about **critical** understanding and a response that is
**sustained** across the whole essay, not only in the best paragraph.

## Writing a title that works

A good title is a question, focused on the writer's methods, and narrow enough for 1200 words.

| Weak title | Why it limits marks | Stronger version |
|---|---|---|
| "Jealousy in *Othello*" | A topic, not a question; invites narration | "How does Shakespeare use Iago's soliloquies to make the audience complicit in his plan?" |
| "Write about two poems" | No focus for AO2 or AO4 | "How do two of the poems you have studied present memory as unreliable?" |
| "Tell the story from Pip's view" | Empathic task with no moment or purpose | "You are Pip on the night after Estella's departure. Write your thoughts." |

Note that a poetry or short-story assignment must cover **at least two** poems or stories, but you do
not have to compare them.

## Worked example: improving a paragraph

**Draft:** "In *Great Expectations* Miss Havisham stopped all her clocks, which shows she is sad about
being jilted. This is very effective."

**Revised:** "Dickens freezes Satis House at the moment of Miss Havisham's humiliation: the stopped
clocks make her grief physical, so the reader sees a woman who has turned a single event into a whole
way of living. Because Pip describes this world with a child's literal eye, the strangeness is shown
before it is explained, and the reader feels his unease before understanding its cause."

The revision names a method (symbolic setting, first-person retrospective narration), explains its
effect (AO3), develops a reading (AO2) and shows personal engagement (AO4), using only a brief
reference (AO1).

## Rules you must follow

- Submit your own work. Teachers may not correct or edit drafts; they can give only brief summative
  comments on progress while you draft and redraft.
- Do not use online tools that act as writing assistants, meaning tools that point to sections and
  propose specific replacement words.
- Reference any source you use in full (author, publication, date, page; for a website, the address and
  date accessed).
- Proofread carefully, and keep to the 600–1200 word guidance.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Retelling the plot.** Narrative earns little above Level 3; every paragraph needs analysis of a method.
- **Writing both assignments on set texts.** Only one of the two may be on a Paper 1, 2 or 3 set text.
- **Writing both as empathic pieces.** Only one may be empathic, and only on prose or drama.
- **An empathic poem response.** The empathic option is limited to prose or drama texts.
- **Ignoring the whole text.** Assignments must show you have studied the whole text.
- **One poem only.** A poetry or short-story assignment must cover at least two poems or stories.
- **Counting the bibliography.** Quotations count towards the word total; references and bibliography do not.
- **Long block quotations.** They eat the word count and add little; embed short phrases instead.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can both coursework assignments be on the same text?**
No. The 0475 syllabus requires the two assignments to be on different texts, and each must be based on
the study of one complete text.

**Do quotations count towards the 1200 words?**
Yes. The word guidance includes quotations but excludes references and the bibliography, so heavy
quotation leaves less room for analysis.

**Is Component 5 an alternative to Paper 2?**
No. It is taken with Paper 3 Drama (Open Text), as an alternative to Paper 4 Unseen. Both Paper 3 and
Component 5 are worth 25%.

**What makes an empathic response score highly?**
The top level asks you to use textual detail seamlessly and to sustain an "entirely convincing voice"
for the character, so the piece must sound like the character at that moment, not like a summary.
