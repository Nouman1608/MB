These questions cover section **4.2.2.4 Coronary heart disease: a non-communicable disease** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.2.4 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for questions 7 and 11. All data are invented.

Learn the content first in the [coronary heart disease study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-coronary-heart-disease/) and the [coronary heart disease revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-coronary-heart-disease-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** State why coronary heart disease is described as a non-communicable disease. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the blood vessels in which fatty material builds up in coronary heart disease. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why a person with coronary heart disease may feel chest pain during exercise but not at rest. **[3]**

**3.** State whether each treatment is a drug, a mechanical device or a transplant: statins, a stent, an artificial heart, a donor heart. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why statins would not quickly help a patient whose coronary artery is already badly narrowed. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the consequences for the body of a heart valve that leaks. **[3]**

**6.** A doctor recommends a mechanical replacement valve for a 30-year-old patient and a biological valve for an 80-year-old patient. Suggest reasons for each choice. **[3]**

### Section B: data and longer questions

**7.** In a trial (invented data), 1500 adults took a new cholesterol-lowering drug for four years and 1500 similar adults took a placebo.

| Group | People | Heart attacks in four years |
|---|---|---|
| New drug | 1500 | 45 |
| Placebo | 1500 | 75 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group that had a heart attack. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in risk in the drug group compared with the placebo group. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why one group was given a placebo. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why using large groups makes the result more reliable. **[1]**

**8.**

**(a)** Suggest one reason why artificial hearts are used only occasionally. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one advantage of an artificial heart compared with a donor heart. **[1]**

**9.** A patient receives a donor heart.

**(a)** Explain why the patient must take drugs for the rest of their life after the transplant. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why these drugs increase the patient's risk of infections. **[1]**

**10.** A patient has heart failure. Doctors are considering an artificial heart now and a heart transplant when a donor heart becomes available. Evaluate these two treatments for this patient. **[6]**

**11.** A patient's blood cholesterol was measured every three months after starting statins (invented data).

| Months after starting statins | 0 | 3 | 6 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood cholesterol (mmol/dm³) | 7.8 | 6.4 | 5.5 | 5.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in blood cholesterol over the nine months. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the pattern in the data. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this change reduces the patient's risk of a heart attack. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** It is not caused by a pathogen, so it cannot be passed from person to person [1]
**(b)** The coronary arteries [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The arteries" or "the aorta" does not earn (b).

**2.** The narrowed coronary arteries let less blood reach the heart muscle [1]; at rest this supplies just enough oxygen, but during exercise the heart beats faster and its muscle needs more oxygen for respiration [1]; the narrowed arteries cannot supply it, so the muscle lacks oxygen, causing pain [1]
*Examiner insight:* The contrast between demand at rest and demand during exercise is the key idea.

**3.** Statins: drug [1]; stent: mechanical device [1]; artificial heart: mechanical device [1]; donor heart: transplant [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark per correct match.

**4.** Statins lower blood cholesterol, which only slows the rate at which new fatty material is deposited [1]; they do not remove the material already narrowing the artery, so blood flow to the heart muscle does not improve quickly [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Statins take a long time" earns one mark at most without the reason.

**5.** When the valve should be closed, some blood flows backwards [1]; so less blood is pumped forwards with each beat and the heart has to work harder [1]; less oxygen reaches body cells, so the person may feel tired or breathless [1]
*Examiner insight:* Follow the effect from the valve to the heart to the rest of the body.

**6.** A mechanical valve lasts a long time [1], so a young patient is less likely to need another operation later [1]; a biological valve usually avoids the need for lifelong anti-clotting drugs, which suits an older patient for whom a valve that wears out sooner matters less [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions reward linking a property of each valve to the patient's age.

**7. (a)** Drug: 45 ÷ 1500 × 100 = **3.0%** [1]; placebo: 75 ÷ 1500 × 100 = **5.0%** [1]
**(b)** (5.0 − 3.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
**(c)** To show that any difference is caused by the drug itself and not by taking a tablet or being in a trial [1]
**(d)** Unusual results from a few individuals have less effect on the overall result [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide by the placebo value, the starting risk.

**8. (a)** Any one: they are usually a short-term measure; there is a risk of blood clots or infection; the patient depends on a power supply [1]
**(b)** It is less likely to be rejected, because it is not living tissue from another person (or: it does not depend on a donor becoming available) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), make the comparison clear: say what the donor heart has that the artificial heart avoids.

**9. (a)** The patient's immune system may recognise the donor heart as foreign and reject it [1]; drugs that suppress the immune system are needed to prevent rejection [1]
**(b)** The drugs reduce the activity of the immune system, so it is less able to destroy pathogens [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link part (b) to the white blood cells' role in defence.

**10.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer covers both treatments and concludes for this patient):
- the artificial heart can be used now, keeping the patient alive while waiting for a donor [1]
- it is not living tissue, so it is less likely to be rejected [1]
- but it is usually a short-term measure, with risks of blood clots and infection [1]
- a transplant can give a long-term solution [1]
- but there is a shortage of donor hearts, it is major surgery, and anti-rejection drugs are needed for life [1]
- judgement: use the artificial heart now to keep the patient alive, then a transplant for the long term when a heart is available [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two treatments are not alternatives here: the best answers see that one can lead to the other.

**11. (a)** (7.8 − 5.2) ÷ 7.8 × 100 [1] = **33%** [1]
**(b)** Cholesterol falls throughout the nine months [1]; the fall is fastest in the first three months and then slows [1]
**(c)** Lower blood cholesterol means fatty material is deposited in the coronary arteries more slowly [1]; so the arteries are less likely to narrow or block, keeping the oxygen supply to the heart muscle [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), use the figures: the fall is 1.4 in the first three months but only 0.3 in the last three.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the wrong vessels: coronary arteries, not the aorta.
- Saying statins remove fatty deposits.
- Missing the link between a leaking valve and less blood pumped forward.
- Dividing by the wrong group in percentage reduction.
- Describing a pattern without quoting figures.
- Evaluating without a final judgement for the patient.
