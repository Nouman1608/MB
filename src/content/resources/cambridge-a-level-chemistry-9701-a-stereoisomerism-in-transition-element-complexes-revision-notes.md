subtopic 28.4 Stereoisomerism in transition element complexes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Stereoisomers** | same formula and same bonds, different arrangement of ligands in space |
| **Geometrical (cis/trans) isomers** | differ in whether two identical ligands are adjacent (cis, 90°) or opposite (trans, 180°) |
| **Optical isomers (enantiomers)** | non-superimposable mirror images; rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions |
| **Chiral** | not superimposable on its mirror image; at this level, has no plane of symmetry |
| **Bidentate ligand** | forms two dative bonds to the metal, e.g. en (H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂) or C₂O₄²⁻ |
| **en** | 1,2-diaminoethane, bonds through both N atoms |

## Decision routine for any complex

1. Find the **coordination number** and **shape** (count donor atoms: bidentate counts 2).
2. **Tetrahedral?** No cis/trans isomers (all positions equivalent).
3. **Square planar or octahedral with two identical ligands among different ones?** Look for cis (90°) and
   trans (180°) arrangements.
4. For each geometrical isomer, look for a **plane of symmetry**. None means a pair of optical isomers.
5. Add them up.
6. For **polarity**, check whether the bond dipoles cancel by symmetry.

## Syllabus examples summarised

| Complex | Shape | cis/trans? | Optical? | Total stereoisomers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | square planar | yes | no | 2 |
| [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | octahedral | yes | no | 2 |
| [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | octahedral | yes | cis form only | 3 |
| [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ | octahedral | no | yes | 2 (one optical pair) |

## Why bidentate ligands matter

- A bidentate ligand can only span **two adjacent (cis) positions**.
- Two or three bidentate ligands around an octahedron create a **twisted, propeller-like** arrangement.
- That twist removes planes of symmetry, so **optical isomerism appears**.
- Monodentate-only complexes such as [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ keep a plane of symmetry and are **not** chiral.

Transfer the same reasoning to any bidentate ligand. [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ behaves exactly like [Ni(en)₃]²⁺: one
pair of optical isomers and no cis/trans isomers.

## Polarity (bond-dipole model)

| Isomer type | Bond dipoles | Overall |
|---|---|---|
| trans-[MA₂B₂] (square planar) | opposite pairs cancel | non-polar |
| cis-[MA₂B₂] (square planar) | do not cancel | polar |
| trans-[MA₄B₂] (octahedral) | cancel | non-polar |
| cis-[MA₄B₂] (octahedral) | do not cancel | polar |
| [M(en)₃] type | symmetrical, cancel | non-polar |
| cis-[M(en)₂B₂] | do not cancel | polar |
| trans-[M(en)₂B₂] | cancel | non-polar |

A polar isomer can be expected to be more soluble in a polar solvent such as water than its non-polar
isomer.

## Drawing checklist

- Octahedral: two vertical bonds plus four "equatorial" bonds drawn with wedges and dashes.
- en: a curved line joining **adjacent** positions, N at both ends.
- Optical pair: draw the complex, a mirror line, then its true reflection.
- Label isomers *cis* and *trans* explicitly.

## Exam traps

- **No cis/trans in tetrahedral complexes**: a common wrong answer for [MA₂B₂] when the shape is not stated.
- **trans-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is not optically active**: it has a plane of symmetry through the Ni and both en
  ligands.
- **[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has three stereoisomers**, not two.
- **[Ni(en)₃]²⁺ has no cis/trans isomers**: all three ligands are identical.
- **Show the reason** for chirality: "no plane of symmetry" or "mirror image not superimposable".
- **Polarity**: state that the dipoles cancel (or do not) and why; "trans is non-polar" alone may not score.
- **Cisplatin is the cis isomer** of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂].

## Self-test

1. Define *stereoisomers*.
2. Why does a tetrahedral complex [MA₂B₂] have no geometrical isomers?
3. What is the bond angle between two cis ligands in an octahedral complex? Between two trans ligands?
4. Why can an en ligand not occupy two trans positions?
5. How many stereoisomers does [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ have? Name them.
6. Explain why cis-[Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is not optically active.
7. State the type of stereoisomerism shown by [Ni(en)₃]²⁺.
8. How many stereoisomers does [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ have? Identify which are chiral.
9. Which isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is polar? Explain.
10. Predict the stereoisomers of [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻.
11. Is trans-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ polar or non-polar? Explain.
12. Suggest which isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is more soluble in water, and why.

**Answers:**

1. Compounds with the same molecular formula and the same bonds but a different arrangement of atoms
   (ligands) in space.
2. All four positions are adjacent to each other (about 109.5° apart), so every arrangement of two A and two B
   ligands is the same.
3. 90°; 180°.
4. The two N donor atoms are too close together; the ligand can only bridge two adjacent positions.
5. Two: cis (H₂O ligands at 90°) and trans (H₂O ligands at 180°).
6. It has a plane of symmetry, so it is superimposable on its mirror image.
7. Optical isomerism (one pair of enantiomers).
8. Three: trans (not chiral) and the two optical isomers of the cis form (both chiral).
9. cis: its Pt–Cl and Pt–N bond dipoles do not cancel; in trans each bond dipole is cancelled by the
   identical opposite bond.
10. One pair of optical isomers; no geometrical isomers, because all three bidentate ligands are identical.
11. Non-polar: the two Ni–O bonds are opposite each other and the en ligands are arranged symmetrically, so the
    bond dipoles cancel.
12. cis: it is polar, so it interacts more strongly with polar water molecules.
