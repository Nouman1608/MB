This guide covers subtopic **3.3 The mole and the Avogadro constant** in Topic 3,
Stoichiometry, for Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028 series). The overview in
[Formulae, Equations and the Mole](/resources/formulae-equations-and-the-mole/) lists the
formulae; this page works through one example of every calculation type the syllabus
names, so you can recognise each one in an exam.

## What the syllabus asks for

**Core** — one outcome only: state that **concentration can be measured in g/dm³ or
mol/dm³**.

**Supplement** (Extended candidates) — everything else:

- The **mole (mol)** is the unit of amount of substance; one mole contains
  **6.02 × 10²³ particles** (atoms, ions or molecules). This number is the **Avogadro
  constant**.
- Use **amount (mol) = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)** to find amount, mass, molar mass,
  Ar or Mr, and number of particles.
- Use the **molar gas volume, 24 dm³ at r.t.p.**
- Calculate **reacting masses, limiting reactants, gas volumes, solution volumes and
  concentrations** (g/dm³ and mol/dm³), converting between cm³ and dm³.
- Use **titration data** to find moles of solute, concentration or volume.
- Calculate **empirical and molecular formulae**.
- Calculate **percentage yield, percentage composition by mass and percentage purity**.

Core candidates: the proportion method in
[Relative Masses of Atoms and Molecules](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules/)
is your route to reacting masses. Everything below the next section is Supplement.

## Concentration units (Core)

Concentration tells you how much solute is dissolved in each dm³ (1000 cm³) of solution.

- **g/dm³** — mass of solute per dm³.
- **mol/dm³** — amount of solute in moles per dm³.

*(Supplement)* To convert: g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × Mr. A 0.200 mol/dm³ sodium chloride solution
(Mr 58.5) contains 0.200 × 58.5 = **11.7 g/dm³**.

## The toolkit (Supplement)

```
n = m ÷ M                 n in mol, m in g, M (molar mass) in g/mol = Mr
particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³
gas volume (dm³) = n × 24          at r.t.p. only
n = c × V                 c in mol/dm³, V in dm³ (cm³ ÷ 1000)
```

The molar mass in g/mol has the same number as Mr: CO₂ has Mr 44 and molar mass
44 g/mol.

## Worked examples

### 1. Number of particles

*How many molecules, and how many atoms, are in 9.0 g of water?*

```
n = 9.0 ÷ 18 = 0.50 mol
molecules = 0.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³
each H2O has 3 atoms: atoms = 3 × 3.01 × 10²³ = 9.03 × 10²³
```

Read the question for *which* particle: molecules, atoms or ions give different answers.

### 2. Molar mass from data

*0.25 mol of a gas has a mass of 11 g. Find its molar mass.*
M = m ÷ n = 11 ÷ 0.25 = **44 g/mol**.

### 3. Gas volume

*What volume does 3.2 g of oxygen gas occupy at r.t.p.?*
n = 3.2 ÷ 32 = 0.10 mol (O₂, so M = 32); volume = 0.10 × 24 = **2.4 dm³** (2400 cm³).

### 4. Limiting reactant

*2.4 g of magnesium is added to 100 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. Which reactant is
limiting, and what volume of hydrogen forms at r.t.p.?*

```
Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2
n(Mg) = 2.4 ÷ 24 = 0.10 mol
n(HCl) = 1.0 × (100 ÷ 1000) = 0.10 mol
0.10 mol Mg would need 2 × 0.10 = 0.20 mol HCl — only 0.10 mol is present
HCl is limiting (magnesium is in excess)
n(H2) = 0.10 ÷ 2 = 0.050 mol
volume = 0.050 × 24 = 1.2 dm³
```

The limiting reactant decides the yield; always base the product calculation on it.

### 5. Titration

*25.0 cm³ of calcium hydroxide solution is neutralised by 12.50 cm³ of 0.0400 mol/dm³
hydrochloric acid. Find the concentration of the calcium hydroxide in mol/dm³ and g/dm³.*

```
Ca(OH)2 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + 2H2O
n(HCl) = 0.0400 × (12.50 ÷ 1000) = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol
ratio 1 : 2, so n(Ca(OH)2) = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol
c = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (25.0 ÷ 1000) = 0.0100 mol/dm³
in g/dm³: 0.0100 × 74 = 0.740 g/dm³
```

### 6. Empirical and molecular formula

*A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. Its Mr is 60.*

| | C | H | O |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass in 100 g | 40.0 | 6.7 | 53.3 |
| ÷ Ar | 3.33 | 6.7 | 3.33 |
| ÷ smallest | 1 | 2 | 1 |

Empirical formula **CH₂O** (mass 30). 60 ÷ 30 = 2, so the molecular formula is
**C₂H₄O₂**.

### 7. The three percentages

```
percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100
percentage composition of element = (Ar × number of atoms) ÷ Mr × 100
percentage purity = mass of pure substance ÷ mass of impure sample × 100
```

- **Composition:** nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃ (Mr 80): 28 ÷ 80 × 100 =
  **35.0%**.
- **Yield:** 12.4 g of CuCO₃ (0.100 mol) should give 0.100 × 80 = 8.0 g of CuO. If 7.2 g
  is collected, yield = 7.2 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = **90.0%**.
- **Purity:** a 5.00 g sample of impure calcium carbonate reacts with excess acid to give
  1.98 g of CO₂. n(CO₂) = 1.98 ÷ 44 = 0.0450 mol = n(CaCO₃); mass of CaCO₃ = 0.0450 × 100 =
  4.50 g; purity = 4.50 ÷ 5.00 × 100 = **90.0%**.

## Choosing the right route

| You are given | Convert to moles with | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | n = m ÷ M | Use the equation ratio |
| Gas volume at r.t.p. | n = V ÷ 24 | Use the equation ratio |
| Solution volume and concentration | n = c × V | Use the equation ratio |
| Number of particles | n = particles ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ | Use the equation ratio |

Every calculation is the same shape: **to moles → ratio → out of moles**.

## Common mistakes

- **cm³ not divided by 1000** before n = c × V.
- **Skipping the mole ratio** from the balanced equation.
- **Using 24 dm³ for a solid, liquid or solution** — gases at r.t.p. only.
- **Using Ar for a diatomic gas**: oxygen gas is 32 g/mol, not 16.
- **Rounding too early** in empirical formula work; keep 2–3 decimal places until the last
  division.
- **Percentage yield above 100%** — a sign the product was wet or impure, or the
  calculation is upside down.
- **Basing a calculation on the reactant in excess** instead of the limiting one.

## Exam technique: command words

- **State** (Core): "g/dm³ or mol/dm³" — both units.
- **Calculate**: set out each line with units, as in the examples; give the final answer to
  the significant figures the data justify (usually three).
- **Deduce** a formula from data: show the table — the method marks are in the division
  steps.
- **Show that**: every step, ending on the given value.
