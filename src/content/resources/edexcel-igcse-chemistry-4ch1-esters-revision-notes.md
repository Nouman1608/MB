These notes condense sub-topic 4(g) Esters (points 4.38C to 4.43C) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). Every point has a C reference, so the whole sub-topic is **Paper 2 only**. Worked examples and model answers are in the [esters study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-esters/); then try the [esters practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-esters-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ester | Compound containing the -COO- ester link, made from an acid and an alcohol |
| Esterification | Reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol to form an ester and water |
| Volatile | Evaporates easily at room temperature |
| Catalyst | Speeds up a reaction without being used up |
| Reversible reaction | Products can react to re-form the reactants |

## The ester link and equation

```
carboxylic acid + alcohol  ⇌  ester + water        (concentrated H2SO4 catalyst)
CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH  ⇌  CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O
```

- Ethyl ethanoate: C₄H₈O₂, Mr = 88, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃.
- Displayed formula: a C=O carbon bonded to a CH₃ group and to an oxygen; that oxygen bonded to a CH₂CH₃ group.
- Every carbon has four bonds; each oxygen has two.

## Naming and working backwards

| Part of ester | Comes from | Name rule |
|---|---|---|
| First word (alkyl) | Alcohol | Methyl, ethyl, propyl, butyl |
| Second word (-oate) | Carboxylic acid | Methanoate, ethanoate, propanoate |

| Alcohol + acid | Ester |
|---|---|
| Methanol + ethanoic acid | Methyl ethanoate (CH₃COOCH₃) |
| Ethanol + ethanoic acid | Ethyl ethanoate (CH₃COOCH₂CH₃) |
| Propan-1-ol + ethanoic acid | Propyl ethanoate |
| Ethanol + propanoic acid | Ethyl propanoate (CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃) |

### Method in steps: from an ester to its acid and alcohol

1. Find the ester link, C(=O)-O.
2. Carbons on the C=O side, including the carbonyl carbon, give the acid (count them for the stem, add -oic acid).
3. Carbons on the single-bonded oxygen side give the alcohol (count them, add -anol).
4. Write the name: alcohol part first (alkyl), acid part second (-oate).

Example: CH₃CH₂COOCH₃. C=O side has 3 carbons (propanoic acid); the other side has 1 carbon (methanol). The ester is methyl propanoate.

## Properties and uses

- Small esters are **volatile** (weak intermolecular forces).
- Distinctive, **fruity** smells: pear drops (ethyl ethanoate), banana, pineapple.
- Uses: food flavourings, perfumes and cosmetics, solvents.
- Esters are insoluble in water and float on it as an oily layer.

## Practical: making ethyl ethanoate

| Step | Reason |
|---|---|
| Mix ethanol and ethanoic acid in a test tube | Reactants |
| Add a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid | Acid catalyst |
| Warm in a hot water bath (about 60 to 70 °C) | Speeds up the reaction; no naked flame because the liquids are flammable |
| Pour into sodium carbonate solution | Neutralises unreacted acid (fizzing) |
| Waft the smell | Sweet, fruity smell and an oily layer show an ester formed |

## Calculation pattern

1. Moles = mass / Mr (ethanol 46, ethanoic acid 60, ethyl ethanoate 88, methanol 32).
2. The ratio acid : alcohol : ester is 1 : 1 : 1.
3. Maximum mass = moles x Mr of ester.
4. Percentage yield = actual / maximum x 100.
5. The yield is below 100% because the reaction is reversible and some product is lost on separation.

## Answer frames

- **Name an ester:** "alcohol part first, ending -yl; acid part second, ending -oate".
- **Why an acid catalyst:** "it speeds up the reaction".
- **Why sodium carbonate:** "to remove the unreacted acid".
- **Describe the product:** "fruity-smelling oily layer".

## Exam traps

- The first word comes from the alcohol, not the acid.
- Water is also formed.
- CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ are different esters (isomers).
- The catalyst is an acid, not a base.
- Do not heat with a naked flame.
- All of this sub-topic is Paper 2 only.

## Isomers and comparisons

**Esters with the formula C₃H₆O₂ (Mr = 74).** Two exist: methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃ (ethanoic acid + methanol), and ethyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₃ (methanoic acid + ethanol). They are isomers: the same molecular formula, different structural formulae. Split the three carbons between the acid part (including the carbonyl carbon) and the alcohol part: 2 + 1 or 1 + 2.

| | Alcohol | Carboxylic acid | Ester |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional group | -OH | -COOH | -COO- |
| Smell | Sharp, spirit-like | Sharp, vinegar | Sweet, fruity |
| Fizzes with carbonate? | No | Yes | No |
| Mixes with water? | Yes (short chains) | Yes (short chains) | Little or not at all |

## Self-test

1. Name the functional group in esters.
2. Name the ester made from ethanol and ethanoic acid.
3. Write the equation for the reaction in question 2.
4. Name the ester made from methanol and ethanoic acid.
5. Name the acid and alcohol that form CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃.
6. State the catalyst used in esterification.
7. State why the mixture is poured into sodium carbonate solution.
8. State two uses of esters.
9. 3.2 g of methanol (Mr = 32) reacts with excess ethanoic acid. Calculate the maximum mass of methyl ethanoate (Mr = 74).
10. The actual mass obtained in question 9 is 70% of the maximum. Calculate it.

### Answers

1. The ester link, -COO-.
2. Ethyl ethanoate.
3. CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O.
4. Methyl ethanoate.
5. Propanoic acid and ethanol (ethyl propanoate).
6. Concentrated sulfuric acid.
7. To neutralise (remove) unreacted acid so that the smell of the ester can be detected.
8. Any two: food flavourings; perfumes; cosmetics; solvents.
9. Moles of methanol = 3.2 / 32 = 0.10 mol; maximum ester = 0.10 x 74 = **7.4 g**.
10. 0.70 x 7.4 = **5.18 g** (about 5.2 g).
