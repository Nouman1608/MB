These notes condense **3.2.1.2 Operations competitiveness** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [operations competitiveness study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-operations-competitiveness-9725/) has the full worked examples; the [operations competitiveness practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-operations-competitiveness-9725-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

1. **Capacity**: high or low utilisation; make or buy; matching supply to demand; problems of mismatch.
2. **Efficiency and productivity**: importance, ways to increase, difficulties.
3. **Lean production**: JIT, Kaizen, standardised processes, teamwork; benefits and difficulties.
4. **Customer service**: value; understand needs, improve experience, benchmark, train, reward loyalty, monitor and act on feedback.
5. **Quality**: meaning, measurement, QC, QA, TQM, quality circles, benchmarking; benefits, difficulties, costs of poor quality.
6. **Wider importance**: technology, ethics, environment, competitiveness, links to other functions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacity utilisation | Actual output as a % of maximum potential output |
| Make or buy | Choosing whether to produce an input in-house or purchase it from a supplier |
| Outsourcing | Paying another business to carry out an activity or produce part of the output |
| Flexible labour | Temporary, part-time, agency or zero hours staff whose hours can follow demand |
| Efficiency | Producing output with the fewest resources |
| Labour productivity | Output per employee in a period |
| Lean production | An approach that cuts waste in all forms |
| Just in Time | Inputs arrive only as they are needed |
| Kaizen | Continuous improvement through small, frequent changes |
| Quality control | Inspecting output to find and remove faults |
| Quality assurance | Building checks into each stage to prevent faults |
| Total quality management | A culture in which every employee is responsible for quality |
| Quality circle | A small group of employees meeting regularly to solve quality problems |
| Benchmarking | Comparing performance with the best and adopting their methods |

## Capacity in numbers

- **Fixed cost per unit = fixed costs ÷ output**: it falls as utilisation rises.
- **Make or buy break point**: extra fixed costs of making ÷ (buying price − variable cost of making). Above that quantity, making is cheaper.

| High utilisation | Low utilisation |
|---|---|
| Lower unit costs | Spare room for new orders |
| Staff fully used | Time for maintenance and training |
| No room for rush orders; maintenance squeezed | Higher unit costs; may signal weak demand |

## Matching supply to demand: choose by time scale

| Short term | Medium term | Long term |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime or cut shifts; use inventory | Flexible labour; outsourcing | Increase or decrease capacity |

Mismatch costs: under-supply → lost sales and reputation; over-supply → unsold stock, idle resources, higher unit costs.

## Lean production: how the parts connect

Standardised processes make problems visible → teams and Kaizen fix them → reliable processes allow JIT with little inventory.
**Benefits:** less waste, lower inventory costs, faster response, involved staff.
**Difficulties:** reliance on suppliers, little buffer against disruption, training time, culture change.

## Quality methods compared

- **QC** detects faults at the end; **QA** prevents them at each stage.
- **TQM** spreads responsibility to everyone; **quality circles** use small staff groups.
- **Benchmarking** sets an external standard.

**Costs of poor quality:** scrap and rework, refunds, lost customers, reputation damage, possible legal claims.
**Difficulties of improving quality:** training cost, slower output at first, time to change culture.

## Customer service: six named ways

Understand customer needs · improve the customer experience · benchmark · train · reward loyalty · monitor and act on feedback and satisfaction levels.

## Links to other functions

| Function | How it connects to operations competitiveness |
|---|---|
| Marketing | Demand forecasts decide capacity; quality and service shape the brand and the price that can be charged |
| Finance | Funds new capacity, quality systems and training; benefits from lower unit costs and less inventory |
| Human resources | Recruits flexible labour, trains staff for Kaizen and TQM, and manages resistance to change |

## Exam traps

- Full utilisation is not automatically good.
- In make or buy, use **total** costs of making, including the extra fixed costs.
- Lean production is wider than JIT.
- Productivity is not the same as production: more output from more staff may mean no change in productivity.
- Give the **consequence for competitiveness**, not just the operational effect.

## Self-test

1. Fixed costs are $48,000 a month. Calculate the fixed cost per unit at outputs of 8,000 and 12,000 units.
2. Making a part adds fixed costs of $20,000 and a variable cost of $4 per unit; buying costs $6.50 per unit. At what quantity are the costs equal?
3. Labour productivity rises from 400 to 440 units per employee. Calculate the percentage increase.
4. A factory makes 30,000 units with a 2% defect rate. Rework costs $5 a unit. Calculate the monthly rework cost.
5. State two ways of matching supply to a seasonal rise in demand.
6. Distinguish quality control from quality assurance.
7. Name the four elements of lean production in the specification.
8. Give one difficulty of increasing labour productivity.
9. Give one benefit and one drawback of using zero hours contracts to match supply to demand.
10. What is benchmarking?
11. State one problem of supply exceeding demand.
12. Give one way technology can improve operations competitiveness.

### Answers

1. 48,000 ÷ 8,000 = **$6**; 48,000 ÷ 12,000 = **$4**.
2. 20,000 ÷ (6.50 − 4) = 20,000 ÷ 2.50 = **8,000 units**.
3. (440 − 400) ÷ 400 × 100 = **10%**.
4. 30,000 × 2% = 600 units; 600 × 5 = **$3,000**.
5. Any two: build inventory beforehand, overtime, flexible labour, outsourcing.
6. QC inspects finished output to find faults; QA builds checks into each stage to prevent faults.
7. Just in Time, Kaizen, standardised processes, teamwork.
8. Investment cost, staff resistance, hard-to-measure output, risk to quality (any one).
9. Benefit: labour cost follows demand. Drawback: no guaranteed income, so lower commitment and possible ethical criticism.
10. Comparing processes or performance with the best performers and adopting what works.
11. Unsold inventory, idle resources, higher unit costs or price cuts (any one).
12. Better links with suppliers and customers, such as online ordering or computerised inventory control (any valid point).
