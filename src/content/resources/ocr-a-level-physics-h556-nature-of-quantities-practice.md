Use g = 9.81 m s⁻². Angles in Question 11 are measured anticlockwise from the +x direction.

## Section A

**1.** From this list, identify the vector quantities: kinetic energy, acceleration, density, weight, temperature, electric field strength. **[2]**

**2.** Using an example, explain the difference between distance and displacement. **[2]**

**3.** A swimmer swims at 1.2 m s⁻¹ relative to the water, pointing straight across a river that flows at 0.50 m s⁻¹. Calculate the swimmer's resultant velocity. **[3]**

**4.** A child pulls a sledge with a force of 75 N along a rope at 25° above the horizontal. Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the force. **[2]**

**5.** Two forces, each of 6.0 N, act at a point. Determine the angle between them that gives a resultant of 6.0 N. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Two tugs pull a ship. One rope has tension 5.0 kN and the other 4.0 kN, and the angle between the ropes is 40°.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the angle between the resultant and the 5.0 kN rope. **[2]**

**7.** Forces of 4.5 N and 3.0 N act at a point with 50° between them.

**(a)** Describe how to find the resultant by scale drawing. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the resultant to check the drawing. **[2]**

**8.** A boat can move at 3.0 m s⁻¹ relative to the water. It must cross a river 60 m wide, travelling straight across, while the river flows at 1.8 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the angle upstream at which the boat must head. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its resultant speed across the river. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the time taken to cross. **[1]**

**9.** A ball strikes a smooth wall at 8.0 m s⁻¹, travelling at 30° to the normal, and rebounds at 8.0 m s⁻¹ at 30° to the normal on the other side. Calculate the magnitude of the change in velocity and state its direction. **[3]**

**10.** A trolley of mass 1.5 kg rests on a ramp inclined at 12° to the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the component of its weight acting down the ramp. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the component of its weight perpendicular to the ramp. **[1]**

**11.** Three coplanar forces act at a point: 20 N at 0°, 15 N at 120° and 10 N at 250°. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant. **[4]**

**12.** An aircraft flies 300 km on a bearing of 045° and then 200 km on a bearing of 135°. Calculate its resultant displacement. **[3]**

**13.** A student says that the resultant of a 3.0 N force and a 4.0 N force is 7.0 N. State when this is true and give the full range of possible resultants. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **Acceleration, weight and electric field strength** [2]. One mark for two correct with no scalars included. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Kinetic energy is a scalar even though it depends on velocity, because v² has no direction.

**2.** Distance is the total length of path travelled, a scalar; displacement is the straight-line distance from start to finish in a stated direction, a vector [1]. Example: one lap of a 400 m track is 400 m of distance but zero displacement [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Magnitude = √(1.2² + 0.50²) [1] = **1.3 m s⁻¹** [1]. Direction: tan⁻¹(0.50 ÷ 1.2) = **22.6° downstream** of the straight-across direction [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Horizontal: 75 cos 25° = **68.0 N** [1]. Vertical: 75 sin 25° = **31.7 N** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The triangle of sides 6.0, 6.0 and 6.0 N is equilateral, so the interior angle is 60° [1]. The angle between the forces drawn from the point is 180° − 60° = **120°** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Interior angle = 180° − 40° = 140° [1]. R² = 5.0² + 4.0² − 2(5.0)(4.0) cos 140° = 71.6, so **R = 8.5 kN** [1].

**(b)** sin α ÷ 4.0 = sin 140° ÷ 8.46 [1], so **α = 17.7°** to the 5.0 kN rope [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Using cos 40° instead of cos 140° gives 3.2 kN, smaller than either force alone, which should signal an error.

**7. (a)** Choose and state a scale, such as 1 cm to 0.5 N [1]. Draw the 4.5 N force, then draw the 3.0 N force from its tip at 50° to the direction of the first, using a protractor [1]. Draw the resultant from the start of the first to the tip of the second; measure its length and angle and convert using the scale [1].

**(b)** R² = 4.5² + 3.0² + 2(4.5)(3.0) cos 50° = 46.6, so **R = 6.8 N** [1], at sin⁻¹(3.0 sin 130° ÷ 6.83) = **19.7°** to the 4.5 N force [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** The upstream component of the boat's velocity must cancel the current: 3.0 sin θ = 1.8 [1], so **θ = 36.9°** upstream of straight across [1].

**(b)** √(3.0² − 1.8²) = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** t = 60 ÷ 2.4 = **25 s** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Here the boat's velocity is the hypotenuse; the resultant is a shorter side of the right-angled triangle.

**9.** The component parallel to the wall, 8.0 sin 30° = 4.0 m s⁻¹, is unchanged [1]. The normal component, 8.0 cos 30° = 6.93 m s⁻¹, reverses [1]. Δv = 6.93 − (−6.93) = **13.9 m s⁻¹, along the normal, away from the wall** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Resolving is quicker than a vector triangle here, because only one component changes.

**10. (a)** W = 1.5 × 9.81 = 14.7 N [1]. Down the ramp: 14.7 sin 12° = **3.06 N** [1].

**(b)** Perpendicular: 14.7 cos 12° = **14.4 N** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** x-components: 20 + 15 cos 120° + 10 cos 250° = 20 − 7.50 − 3.42 = 9.08 N [1]. y-components: 0 + 15 sin 120° + 10 sin 250° = 12.99 − 9.40 = 3.59 N [1]. R = √(9.08² + 3.59²) = **9.8 N** [1]. Direction: tan⁻¹(3.59 ÷ 9.08) = **21.6°** anticlockwise from +x [1]. **[4]**

**12.** The two legs are at 90° to each other (bearings differ by 90°) [1]. R = √(300² + 200²) = **361 km** [1]. Angle between the first leg and R = tan⁻¹(200 ÷ 300) = 33.7°, so the bearing is 045° + 33.7° = **079°** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Only when the two forces act in the same direction [1]. The resultant can be anywhere from **1.0 N to 7.0 N**, depending on the angle between them [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not giving a direction for a resultant, or giving an angle without saying what it is measured from.
- Putting the angle between the vectors, instead of the interior angle, into the cosine rule.
- Using sin where cos is needed when the angle is taken from the normal or the vertical.
- Treating a change in direction at constant speed as no change in velocity.
- Losing a sign when adding components in the second or third quadrant.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I resolve instead of drawing a vector triangle?**
Resolve when there are three or more vectors, or when only one component changes, as in a bounce off a wall. Use a triangle for two vectors, especially when the question asks for a scale drawing.

**How do I give the direction of a resultant?**
State an angle and what it is measured from, such as "21.6° anticlockwise from the +x direction" or "22.6° downstream of straight across". A compass bearing is also clear.

**Does the order of adding vectors matter?**
No. a + b = b + a, so drawing the second vector first gives the same resultant. Subtraction does depend on order: a − b is the reverse of b − a.
