Questions or parts marked **(Extended)** use terms only in the Extended list: plane, perpendicular bisector, hemisphere, frustum, and major and minor arc. All questions are non-calculator.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write down the type of each angle.

**(a)** 95° **[1]**

**(b)** 270° **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Angle ABC = 47°.

**(a)** Find the reflex angle ABC. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the letter of the vertex of angle ABC. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Write down the mathematical name of a triangle with sides

**(a)** 6 cm, 6 cm and 6 cm, **[1]**

**(b)** 5 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Write down the mathematical name of each quadrilateral.

**(a)** It has exactly one pair of parallel sides. **[1]**

**(b)** It has two pairs of equal adjacent sides and no parallel sides. **[1]**

**(c)** It has four right angles and sides of 4 cm and 9 cm. **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the name of a polygon with 8 sides. **[1]**

**(b)** A hexagon has all six sides 5 cm long. Explain why it is not necessarily a regular hexagon. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Write down the circle term for each description.

**(a)** A line from the centre to the circumference. **[1]**

**(b)** The region bounded by two radii and an arc. **[1]**

**(c)** A straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point. **[1]**

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the name of the solid that is half of a sphere. **[1]**

**(b)** Points A and B divide a circle into two arcs of different lengths. Explain what is meant by the major arc AB. **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of a triangular prism. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of a square-based pyramid. **[1]**

**(c)** A prism has 21 edges. Find the number of faces it has. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) Write "always", "sometimes" or "never" for each statement.

**(a)** A rhombus is a square. **[1]**

**(b)** An isosceles triangle has a right angle. **[1]**

**(c)** A triangle has two obtuse angles. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Four rectangles have these dimensions.

R1: 4 cm by 6 cm

R2: 6 cm by 9 cm

R3: 6 cm by 4 cm

R4: 8 cm by 10 cm

**(a)** Write down the rectangle that is congruent to R1. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the rectangle that is similar to R1 but not congruent to it, and give the scale factor from R1. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why R4 is not similar to R1. **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) At one vertex of a polygon, the interior angle is 3 times the exterior angle.

**(a)** Find the exterior angle and the interior angle at this vertex. **[2]**

**(b)** State the type of angle the interior angle is. **[1]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A circle has centre O. A and B are points on the circle, and angle AOB = 100°.

**(a)** Write down the name of the straight line AB. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the name of the region bounded by OA, OB and the shorter arc AB. **[1]**

**(c)** (Extended) Write down the name of the longer arc AB. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why triangle OAB is isosceles. **[1]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write down the geometrical term for each description.

**(a)** Two lines that never meet and stay the same distance apart. **[1]**

**(b)** A line that passes through the midpoint of segment AB at right angles to AB. **[1]**

**(c)** A flat surface that extends in all directions. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Obtuse** [1]

**(b)** **Reflex** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 270° is not "obtuse"; any angle between 180° and 360° is reflex.

**2. (a)** 360° – 47° = **313°** [1]

**(b)** **B** [1]

**3. (a)** **Equilateral** [1]

**(b)** **Scalene** [1]

**4. (a)** **Trapezium** [1]

**(b)** **Kite** [1]

**(c)** **Rectangle** [1]

**5. (a)** **Octagon** [1]

**(b)** A regular polygon needs equal angles as well as equal sides; the angles of this hexagon need not be equal [1]

**6. (a)** **Radius** [1]

**(b)** **Sector** [1]

**(c)** **Tangent** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "segment" for (b) is the most common error. A segment is cut off by a chord, not by two radii.

**7. (a)** **Hemisphere** [1]

**(b)** The **longer** of the two arcs between A and B [1]

**8. (a)** 5 faces and 9 edges [1]; 6 vertices [1]

**(b)** 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices [1]

**(c)** A prism with an n-sided cross-section has 3n edges, so 3n = 21 and n = 7 [1]; faces = 7 + 2 = **9** [1]

**9. (a)** **Sometimes** (only when its angles are right angles) [1]

**(b)** **Sometimes** (for example 45°, 45°, 90°) [1]

**(c)** **Never** [1]; two obtuse angles add up to more than 180°, but the three angles of a triangle add up to 180° [1]

**10. (a)** **R3** [1]

**(b)** **R2** [1]; scale factor 6/4 = 9/6 = **1.5** [1]

**(c)** 8/4 = 2 but 10/6 ≠ 2, so the sides are not all multiplied by the same scale factor [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (c) compare corresponding sides (short with short, long with long); a single ratio is not enough to show similarity.

**11. (a)** Exterior + interior = 180°, so exterior + 3 × exterior = 180° [1]; exterior = **45°**, interior = **135°** [1]

**(b)** **Obtuse** [1]

**12. (a)** **Chord** [1]

**(b)** **Sector** (the minor sector) [1]

**(c)** **Major arc** [1]

**(d)** OA and OB are both radii, so they are equal [1]

**13. (a)** **Parallel** lines [1]

**(b)** **Perpendicular bisector** [1]

**(c)** **Plane** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling angles over 180° obtuse.
- Swapping sector and segment.
- Calling a shape with equal sides but unequal angles regular.
- Giving a general name (rectangle, parallelogram) when a more precise one fits.
- Comparing only one pair of sides when checking similarity.
- Writing "never" or "sometimes" without a reason when one is asked for.
