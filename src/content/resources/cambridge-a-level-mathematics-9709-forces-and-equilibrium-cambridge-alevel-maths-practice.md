These questions cover section **4.1 Forces and equilibrium** of Mechanics (Paper 4) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. Take g = 10 m s⁻². Give answers to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [4.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-forces-and-equilibrium-cambridge-alevel-maths/) and the [4.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-forces-and-equilibrium-cambridge-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Two horizontal forces of 7 N and 4 N act on a particle at right angles to each other. Find the magnitude of their resultant and the angle it makes with the 7 N force. **[3]**

**2.** Three horizontal forces act on a particle: 12 N in the positive x-direction, 9 N in the positive y-direction, and 10 N at 210° anticlockwise from the positive x-direction. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant. **[4]**

**3.** A box of mass 8 kg rests on rough horizontal ground, with coefficient of friction 0.5. A horizontal force of 30 N acts on the box, which does not move. Find the friction force and state, with a reason, whether the box is in limiting equilibrium. **[2]**

**4.** A particle of mass 0.5 kg rests on a rough plane inclined at 35° to the horizontal, and is on the point of slipping down the plane. Find the coefficient of friction. **[3]**

**5.** A block of mass 3 kg on rough horizontal ground is pulled by a horizontal force of 12 N and is about to slip. Find the coefficient of friction and the magnitude of the total contact force between the block and the ground. **[3]**

**6.** A crate A of mass 15 kg rests on top of a crate B of mass 25 kg, which stands on horizontal ground.

**(a)** Find the force exerted on A by B.

**(b)** State the force exerted on B by A, naming the law you use.

**(c)** Find the force exerted on B by the ground. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A particle of mass 5 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string makes 40° with the horizontal and the other makes 55° with the horizontal, on opposite sides of the particle. Find the tension in each string. **[5]**

**8.** A particle of mass 2 kg hangs from a light string. A horizontal force of P N holds the particle in equilibrium with the string at 25° to the vertical.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string and the value of P.

**(b)** P is increased to 15 N and the particle rests in a new equilibrium position. Find the new angle between the string and the vertical, and the new tension. **[6]**

**9.** A particle of mass 6 kg rests on a rough plane inclined at 15° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.4.

**(a)** Show that the particle remains at rest when no other force acts.

**(b)** A force of P N is applied at 20° above a line of greatest slope, in the vertical plane containing that line, pulling up the plane. Find the greatest value of P for which the particle remains in equilibrium. **[7]**

**10.** A particle of mass m kg is on a rough plane inclined at α to the horizontal, where tan α = 3/4. The coefficient of friction is 0.2. A horizontal force of 30 N, acting in the vertical plane containing a line of greatest slope and directed towards the plane, holds the particle on the point of slipping down the plane. Find m. **[6]**

**11.** Three horizontal forces act on a particle in equilibrium: 20 N in the positive x-direction, 15 N in the positive y-direction and a third force P N.

**(a)** Find P and its direction.

**(b)** The 15 N force is removed. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant of the remaining forces. **[5]**

**12.** A small ring of mass 0.2 kg is threaded on a fixed rough horizontal rod. The coefficient of friction is 0.5. A light string attached to the ring pulls it with tension T N at 30° below the horizontal, and the ring is about to slip.

**(a)** Find T.

**(b)** State what would happen if the rod were smooth. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Resultant = √(7² + 4²) [1] = **8.06 N** [1]. Angle = tan⁻¹(4/7) = **29.7°** [1]

**2.** x: 12 + 10 cos 210° = 12 − 8.660 = 3.340 [1]. y: 9 + 10 sin 210° = 9 − 5 = 4 [1]. Magnitude √(3.340² + 4²) = **5.21 N** [1]. Direction tan⁻¹(4/3.340) = **50.1°** anticlockwise from the positive x-direction [1]

**3.** Equilibrium gives **F = 30 N** [1]. μR = 0.5 × 80 = 40 N, and 30 < 40, so the box is **not** in limiting equilibrium [1]

**4.** R = 5 cos 35° [1]. F = 5 sin 35° [1]. μ = F/R = tan 35° = **0.700** [1]

**5.** R = 30 N and F = 12 N, so μ = 12/30 = **0.4** [1]. Contact force = √(30² + 12²) [1] = **32.3 N** [1]

**6.** **(a)** **150 N vertically upwards** [1]

**(b)** **150 N vertically downwards**, by Newton's third law [1]

**(c)** 250 + 150 = **400 N upwards** [1]

**7.** Horizontal: T₁ cos 40° = T₂ cos 55° [1]. Vertical: T₁ sin 40° + T₂ sin 55° = 50 [1]. Substituting T₁ = T₂ cos 55°/cos 40° = 0.7487T₂ and solving [1]: **T₁ = 28.8 N** [1], **T₂ = 38.4 N** [1]

**8.** **(a)** Vertical: T cos 25° = 20 [1], so **T = 22.1 N** [1]. Horizontal: P = T sin 25° [1] = **9.33** [1]

**(b)** tan θ = 15/20, so **θ = 36.9°** [1]. T = √(15² + 20²) = **25 N** [1]

**9.** **(a)** Weight component down the plane 60 sin 15° = 15.5 N; maximum friction 0.4 × 60 cos 15° = 23.2 N [1]. 15.5 < 23.2, so friction can hold the particle and it remains at rest [1]

**(b)** Perpendicular: R + P sin 20° = 60 cos 15°, so R = 57.96 − 0.3420P [1]. At the greatest P the particle is about to move up, so friction acts down the plane with F = 0.4R [1]. Along: P cos 20° = 60 sin 15° + 0.4(57.96 − 0.3420P) [1]. P(0.9397 + 0.1368) = 15.53 + 23.18 [1]. **P = 36.0** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the upward component of P reduces R, and so reduces the friction available; ignoring it gives P = 41.2, which is wrong.

**10.** cos α = 0.8, sin α = 0.6. Perpendicular: R = 10m(0.8) + 30(0.6) [1] = 8m + 18 [1]. About to slip down, so friction acts up the plane [1]. Along: 30(0.8) + 0.2(8m + 18) = 10m(0.6) [1]. 24 + 1.6m + 3.6 = 6m, so 4.4m = 27.6 [1]. **m = 6.27** [1]

**11.** **(a)** P balances the resultant of the other two: P = √(20² + 15²) [1] = **25 N** [1]. Direction: opposite to the resultant, at tan⁻¹(15/20) = **36.9° below the negative x-direction** [1]

**(b)** The remaining forces must have the resultant that balanced the 15 N force, so the resultant is **15 N** [1] in the **negative y-direction** [1]

**12.** **(a)** Vertical: R = 2 + T sin 30° = 2 + 0.5T [1]. Horizontal, limiting: T cos 30° = 0.5R [1]. 0.8660T = 1 + 0.25T [1]. **T = 1.62** [1]

**(b)** With no friction, any horizontal component of tension is unbalanced, so the ring would **move along the rod for any T > 0** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking R = mg when a force has a component perpendicular to the surface.
- Friction drawn the wrong way for the motion that would occur.
- Using F = μR when the body is not in limiting equilibrium.
- Mixing up sin and cos for a string angle measured from the vertical.
- Treating a third-law pair as acting on the same body.
- Answers given to 2 significant figures, or found from a scale drawing.
