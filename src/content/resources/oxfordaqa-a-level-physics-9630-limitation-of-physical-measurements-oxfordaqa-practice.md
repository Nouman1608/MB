These questions cover **3.1.2 Limitation of physical measurements** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 and used throughout Unit 5. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. All experimental data are invented for practice. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [limitation of physical measurements study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-limitation-of-physical-measurements-oxfordaqa/) or the [limitation of physical measurements revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-limitation-of-physical-measurements-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Explain the difference between an accurate set of measurements and a precise set of measurements. **[2]**

**2.** A student repeats a measurement several times and calculates the mean. Which type of error does this reduce?

A: a zero error

B: random error

C: systematic error from a miscalibrated meter

D: parallax error made the same way every time

**[1]**

**3.** A digital stopwatch displays times to 0.01 s. State its resolution, and explain why the uncertainty in a time measured by hand with this stopwatch is larger than its resolution. **[2]**

**4.** Five measurements of a wire's diameter are 0.52 mm, 0.50 mm, 0.53 mm, 0.51 mm and 0.49 mm. Calculate the mean diameter, its absolute uncertainty and its percentage uncertainty. **[3]**

**5.** A quantity Z = X/Y². The percentage uncertainty in X is 2% and in Y is 3%. What is the percentage uncertainty in Z?

A: 1%

B: 5%

C: 8%

D: 11%

**[1]**

**6.** A calculated speed is 3.4167 m s⁻¹ with an absolute uncertainty of 0.0836 m s⁻¹. Write the result to an appropriate number of significant figures. **[1]**

**7.** Theory predicts that a straight-line graph should pass through the origin. A student's best-fit line has an intercept that is well outside its uncertainty range from zero. Explain what this suggests. **[2]**

### Section B

**8.** A student finds the thickness of one sheet of paper by measuring a stack of 200 sheets with vernier calipers. The stack is 19.6 ± 0.1 mm thick.

**(a)** Calculate the thickness of one sheet and its absolute uncertainty. **[2]**

**(b)** Measuring a single sheet with a micrometer gives 0.10 ± 0.01 mm. Use percentage uncertainties to explain why the stack method is better. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest one systematic error that could affect the stack measurement and state its effect on the result. **[2]**

**9.** A trolley of mass 0.850 ± 0.005 kg passes through a light gate. A card of length 50.0 ± 0.5 mm on the trolley blocks the beam for 0.0625 ± 0.0005 s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the trolley and its absolute uncertainty. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the trolley and its absolute uncertainty. **[3]**

**(c)** State which measurement contributes most to the uncertainty in the kinetic energy. **[1]**

**10.** A spring has an unstretched length of 52 ± 1 mm. With a load of 4.00 ± 0.02 N it is 87 ± 1 mm long.

**(a)** Calculate the extension and its percentage uncertainty. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the spring constant and its absolute uncertainty. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest one change to the method that would reduce the percentage uncertainty in the extension. **[1]**

**11.** A trolley is released from rest on a ramp. A student plots a graph of v² against distance travelled s; theory gives v² = 2as. From the graph (data invented for this question):

- best-fit line: gradient 1.36 m s⁻², intercept 0.004 m² s⁻²
- steepest acceptable line: gradient 1.45 m s⁻², intercept −0.010 m² s⁻²
- shallowest acceptable line: gradient 1.28 m s⁻², intercept 0.018 m² s⁻²

**(a)** Calculate the uncertainty in the gradient. **[1]**

**(b)** Determine the acceleration of the trolley with its absolute uncertainty. **[2]**

**(c)** Determine the uncertainty in the intercept and state what it shows about systematic error. **[2]**

**12.** A student measures the density of a small stone. The balance reads 45.62 g (resolution 0.01 g). The water level in a measuring cylinder, which has 1 cm³ divisions, rises from 50 cm³ to 67 cm³ when the stone is lowered in.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the stone in g cm⁻³ with its absolute uncertainty. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate the method and suggest two specific improvements. **[3]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Accurate: the measurements (or their mean) are close to the true value [1]; precise: the measurements have very little spread about their mean [1] **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A, C and D are all systematic: they shift every reading the same way, so the mean is shifted too.

**3.** Resolution **0.01 s** [1]; the experimenter's reaction time in starting and stopping the watch varies and is much larger than 0.01 s [1] **[2]**

**4.** Mean = 2.55 ÷ 5 = **0.51 mm** [1]; uncertainty = (0.53 − 0.49) ÷ 2 = **±0.02 mm** [1]; percentage = 0.02 ÷ 0.51 × 100 = **3.9%** [1] **[3]**

**5.** **C** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* 2% + 2 × 3% = 8%. Option B forgets the square; dividing does not mean subtracting uncertainties.

**6.** **3.42 ± 0.08 m s⁻¹** [1] **[1]**

**7.** Every reading has been shifted by the same amount [1], which suggests a systematic error such as a zero error in one of the measured quantities [1] **[2]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** 19.6 ÷ 200 = **0.0980 mm** [1]; 0.1 ÷ 200 = **±0.0005 mm** [1]

**(b)** Stack: 0.1 ÷ 19.6 = 0.51% [1]; single sheet: 0.01 ÷ 0.10 = 10%, so the stack has a much smaller percentage uncertainty [1]

**(c)** Squeezing the stack with the calipers (or a zero error on the calipers) [1]; every measured thickness would be too small (or shifted by the zero error), so the result is inaccurate however often it is repeated [1] **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** v = 0.0500 ÷ 0.0625 = **0.800 m s⁻¹** [1]; % uncertainty = 1.0% + 0.8% = 1.8% [1]; absolute = **±0.01 m s⁻¹** (0.014) [1]

**(b)** Ek = ½ × 0.850 × 0.800² = **0.272 J** [1]; % uncertainty = 0.59% + 2 × 1.8% = 4.2% [1]; absolute = 0.011, so **0.27 ± 0.01 J** [1]

**(c)** The card length (its 1.0% is doubled because v is squared) [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The mass uncertainty is the smallest term. Improving the balance would barely change the answer; a longer card would.

**10.**

**(a)** Extension = 87 − 52 = 35 mm, uncertainty 1 + 1 = ±2 mm [1]; percentage = 2 ÷ 35 × 100 = **5.7%** [1]

**(b)** k = 4.00 ÷ 0.035 = 114 N m⁻¹ [1]; % uncertainty = 5.7% + 0.5% = 6.2% [1]; absolute = 7.1, so **k = 114 ± 7 N m⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** Use a larger load to produce a larger extension (or use a pointer fixed to the spring and read the scale at eye level) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Absolute uncertainties add for the difference even though the lengths are subtracted.

**11.**

**(a)** (1.45 − 1.28) ÷ 2 = **0.09 m s⁻²** (0.085) [1]

**(b)** Gradient = 2a, so a = 1.36 ÷ 2 = 0.68 m s⁻² [1]; % uncertainty = 0.085 ÷ 1.36 = 6.3%, giving **a = 0.68 ± 0.04 m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** (0.018 − (−0.010)) ÷ 2 = **±0.014 m² s⁻²** [1]; the range 0.004 ± 0.014 includes zero, so there is no evidence of a systematic error [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** Volume = 67 − 50 = 17 cm³ with uncertainty ±2 cm³ (two readings) [1]; ρ = 45.62 ÷ 17 = 2.68 g cm⁻³ [1]; % uncertainty = 0.02% + 11.8% ≈ 12%, so **ρ = 2.7 ± 0.3 g cm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** The volume dominates the uncertainty; the mass uncertainty is negligible [1]. Improvement 1: use a narrower measuring cylinder with finer divisions, or a larger stone, so the volume change is a larger number of divisions [1]. Improvement 2: read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level to avoid parallax, and check for trapped air bubbles, which would make the volume too large [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Use a more accurate balance" scores nothing here. An improvement must target the measurement that controls the uncertainty.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating zero errors and consistent parallax as random errors.
- Adding percentage uncertainties when lengths are subtracted.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 for a squared quantity.
- Quoting results with more significant figures than the uncertainty supports.
- Suggesting improvements to a measurement that is already precise.
