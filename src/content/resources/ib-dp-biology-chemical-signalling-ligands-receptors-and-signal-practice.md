## Questions

**1.** (HL only) State the term for a signalling chemical that binds to a receptor, and explain why only some cells respond to a hormone carried in the blood. **[2]**

**2.** (HL only) A culture of the marine bacterium *Vibrio fischeri* was sampled at different cell densities.

| Density (million cells per cm³) | 0.05 | 0.5 | 5 | 50 | 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light output (arbitrary units) | 0.1 | 1 | 10 | 4000 | 90 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the light output per million cells at 500 million cells per cm³. **[1]**
**(b)** State the density range over which light output per cell begins to rise. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain these results in terms of quorum sensing. **[3]**

**3.** (HL only) Distinguish between hormones and neurotransmitters in the way they reach their target cells, and state one role of calcium ions as a signal inside cells. **[4]**

**4.** (HL only)
**(a)** State the chemical group of each of: testosterone, insulin, epinephrine. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest one reason why signalling chemicals belong to many chemical groups. **[1]**
**(c)** State the function of cytokines. **[1]**

**5.** (HL only) Explain why the insulin receptor is a transmembrane protein while the oestradiol receptor is found inside cells, including the distribution of amino acids in each. **[5]**

**6.** (HL only) A neuron has a resting potential of −70 mV. When acetylcholine binds to its receptors, the potential changes to −58 mV. The threshold for an action potential is −50 mV.
**(a)** Calculate the change in membrane potential. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how acetylcholine causes this change. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce whether an action potential occurs, and suggest what would be needed to trigger one. **[2]**

**7.** (HL only) Outline how binding of epinephrine to its receptor leads to a response inside a liver cell, describing the roles of the G protein and the second messenger. **[6]**

**8.** (HL only) Liver cell preparations were treated and glucose released was measured (µmol per g in 10 minutes).

| Treatment | Glucose released |
|---|---|
| Control | 1.8 |
| Epinephrine | 14.4 |
| Epinephrine plus an adenylyl cyclase inhibitor | 2.1 |
| A cell-permeable cAMP analogue, no epinephrine | 13.2 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater glucose release is with epinephrine than in the control. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall caused by the inhibitor compared with epinephrine alone, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Deduce what the data suggest about cAMP. **[2]**

**9.** (HL only) Muscle cells have 4.0 glucose transporter molecules per µm² of plasma membrane without insulin and 31.0 with insulin.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how insulin causes this change. **[3]**
**(c)** Predict the effect of a tyrosine kinase inhibitor on glucose uptake. **[1]**

**10.** (HL only) A gene responds to a steroid hormone. Relative mRNA concentration was measured.

| Time after adding hormone (min) | 0 | 30 | 90 | 240 | 720 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative mRNA | 1 | 1 | 6 | 22 | 35 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of increase between 90 and 240 minutes, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why mRNA does not increase in the first 30 minutes. **[3]**
**(c)** State the target cells of progesterone that are covered in C2.1. **[1]**

**11.** (HL only) After a meal, blood glucose rose from 5.0 to 8.4 mmol per dm³ and then returned to near 5.4 mmol per dm³ as insulin secretion rose.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise in glucose. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain this as negative feedback. **[4]**
**(c)** Outline one example of positive feedback and state how it differs from your answer to (b). **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Ligand [1]. Only target cells have the specific receptor with a binding site complementary to the hormone [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Specific receptor" earns credit only when linked to cells with or without it; "the hormone goes only to those cells" does not answer why.

**2. (a)** 90 000/500 = **180** (units per million cells) [1].
**(b)** Between 5 and 50 million cells per cm³, where the value goes from 2 to 80 [1].
**(c)** Each cell releases autoinducer [1]. At low density it diffuses away and stays below a threshold [1]. At high density the concentration is high enough to bind receptors and switch on bioluminescence genes in the population together [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to light per cell in (c); total light would rise with numbers even without quorum sensing.

**3.** Hormones travel in the blood to distant cells [1]; neurotransmitters diffuse across a synaptic gap to a nearby cell [1]. Hormones are slower and longer-lasting [1]; neurotransmitters fast and brief. Calcium ions trigger exocytosis or muscle contraction [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides of each point; giving only the hormone route does not distinguish.

**4. (a)** Testosterone is a steroid [1]; insulin is a protein [1]; epinephrine is an amine [1].
**(b)** Different chemicals vary in solubility, stability or speed, suiting different jobs [1].
**(c)** They regulate immune responses [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason must connect a chemical property to a function, not just "more variety".

**5.** Insulin is a protein and hydrophilic, so it cannot cross the bilayer [1]. Its receptor spans the membrane, binding it outside [1], with hydrophobic amino acids in the part within the bilayer [1] and hydrophilic ones outside and inside [1]. Oestradiol is lipid-soluble and enters, so its receptor is a soluble protein inside the cell, mostly hydrophilic on the surface, with a pocket for the ligand [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Place the hydrophobic residues in the membrane-spanning region by name; "mixed amino acids" gains nothing.

**6. (a)** −58 − (−70) = **+12 mV** [1].
**(b)** Acetylcholine binds the receptor [1]. The channel in the receptor opens [1] and positive ions, mainly sodium, diffuse in [1].
**(c)** No, −58 mV is 8 mV more negative than the −50 mV threshold [1]. More receptors opening, from more acetylcholine, would be needed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the final potential with the threshold using the values given; state the sign of the change.

**7.** Epinephrine binds to a receptor in the plasma membrane [1]. The receptor changes shape [1]. The G protein swaps GDP for GTP and is activated [1]. It activates adenylyl cyclase [1], which converts ATP to cAMP, the second messenger [1]. cAMP starts a cascade that breaks down glycogen and releases glucose [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the steps in order and name the enzyme; "a cascade occurs" alone describes nothing.

**8. (a)** 14.4/1.8 = **8 times** [1].
**(b)** (14.4 − 2.1)/14.4 × 100 [1] = **85.4%** [1].
**(c)** The analogue raises glucose release without epinephrine [1], and blocking cAMP production prevents the epinephrine effect, so cAMP carries the signal [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Support a conclusion with both the analogue and the inhibitor results; one alone leaves the link incomplete.

**9. (a)** (31.0 − 4.0)/4.0 × 100 [1] = **675%** [1].
**(b)** Insulin binds the receptor [1], activating tyrosine kinase, which phosphorylates tyrosine inside the cell [1]. A sequence of reactions brings vesicles carrying glucose transporter proteins to the plasma membrane [1].
**(c)** Uptake would fall [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the order binding, phosphorylation, vesicle movement; state that vesicles fuse with the membrane.

**10. (a)** (22 − 6)/(240 − 90) [1] = **0.11 per min** [1].
**(b)** The steroid must cross the membrane and bind its receptor [1]. The receptor binds DNA sequences [1], and transcription must occur before mRNA appears [1].
**(c)** Cells of the endometrium [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote units for a rate and link the delay to the steps from the hormone to transcription.

**11. (a)** (8.4 − 5.0)/5.0 × 100 [1] = **68%** [1].
**(b)** Glucose rises, which is a change from the set point [1]. Insulin is released and binds to receptors on target cells [1], causing glucose uptake [1]. Glucose falls and insulin secretion decreases, reversing the change [1].
**(c)** Stretching of the cervix triggers oxytocin release [1], which strengthens contractions, causing more stretching [1]. Positive feedback amplifies a change instead of reversing it [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that the response opposes the change; for positive feedback, state the loop and how it escalates.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying "specific" without linking the binding site shape to the ligand.
- Treating the response as caused by the hormone entering a transmembrane receptor.
- Forgetting that the G protein needs GTP.
- Giving cAMP as the first messenger.
- Mixing fold change and percentage change.
- Omitting units from rates.
- Missing the DNA-sequence and transcription steps for steroid receptors.
- Naming a feedback example without the change that is reversed or amplified.
