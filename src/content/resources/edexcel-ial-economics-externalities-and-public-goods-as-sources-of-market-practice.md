## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between private cost and social cost. **[3]**

**2.** For each of the following, state whether it is a public good or a private good, and give a reason using rivalry and excludability.

**(a)** A seat in a cinema. **[1]**
**(b)** A flood-warning siren heard across a whole town. **[1]**
**(c)** A loaf of bread. **[1]**
**(d)** A free-to-air radio signal that anyone with a receiver can pick up. **[1]**

**3.** A paper mill in Country A sells paper to printers. All figures are in currency units per tonne. Demand (MPB, equal to MSB) is 120 - 4Q. The mill's private cost (MPC) is 30 + 2Q. Smoke adds an external cost (MEC) of 18 per tonne.

**(a)** Find the market output and price. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the socially optimal output and the price consumers would pay at that output. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare loss. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the welfare loss differs from the total external cost at the market output. **[2]**

**4.** A dental scheme in Country G offers check-ups. Per check-up: MPB = 96 - 4Q and MC = 16 + Q. Healthier mouths reduce the spread of some infections to others, giving MEB = 10 per check-up.

**(a)** Find the market output and price. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the socially optimal output. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare gain from reaching the social optimum. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one reason why the welfare gain might be uncertain. **[2]**

**5.** Six harbour-side households in Country H share a fog signal that costs 90 to install. The households' maximum willingness to pay is 30, 25, 20, 15, 12 and 8.

**(a)** Show that installing the fog signal would be worthwhile for the households as a whole. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a private firm is unlikely to provide it. **[3]**

**6.** A bridge in Country J carries n trips per hour. Demand (MPB) is 45 - 0.05n. The average private cost per trip is 15 + 0.05n, so each extra driver slows everyone else and marginal social cost is MSC = 15 + 0.1n.

**(a)** Find the number of trips in a free market and the external cost per trip at that level. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the socially optimal number of trips. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare loss at the market level. **[2]**

**7.** State whether each of the following is an external cost of production, an external cost of consumption, an external benefit of production or an external benefit of consumption, and give a reason.

**(a)** Train passengers talk loudly on their phones. **[1]**
**(b)** A research hospital develops a technique that other hospitals copy free. **[1]**
**(c)** Night-time freight lorries from a depot wake people living nearby. **[1]**
**(d)** A household keeps its front garden attractive and the whole street gains. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how the failure of one lender could impose external costs on households and firms with no dealings with it. **[6]**

**9.** A firm in Country K produces a good. The table shows the marginal private benefit (MPB) and marginal private cost (MPC) of each unit. Each unit also imposes an external cost of 8.

| Unit | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPB | 50 | 44 | 38 | 32 | 26 | 20 |
| MPC | 14 | 20 | 26 | 32 | 38 | 44 |

**(a)** State the quantity produced in a free market. **[2]**
**(b)** State the socially optimal quantity. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the total external cost at each of these two quantities. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the welfare loss from the market outcome and explain why it arises. **[2]**

**10.** A beach in Country L has free open access and is busy in summer. Explain whether it meets the specification's definition of a public good. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that the market provides too little education because of externalities. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Private cost is the cost paid by the consumer or producer directly involved [1]. Social cost is private cost plus external cost [1]. External cost falls on third parties who are not compensated, so social cost exceeds private cost whenever one exists [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the relationship (social = private + external) and name who bears the external part.

**2. (a)** Private good: rival (one viewer takes the seat) and excludable (no ticket, no entry) [1].
**(b)** Public good: non-rival (one household hearing it does not reduce another's warning) and non-excludable [1].
**(c)** Private good: rival and excludable [1].
**(d)** Public good: non-rival and non-excludable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare label with no reason is hard to reward. Name both properties.

**3. (a)** Set MPB = MPC: 120 - 4Q = 30 + 2Q, so 6Q = 90 and **Q = 15 tonnes** [1]. Price = 120 - 60 = **60** [1].
**(b)** MSC = 30 + 2Q + 18 = 48 + 2Q. Set MSB = MSC: 120 - 4Q = 48 + 2Q, so 6Q = 72 and **Q = 12 tonnes** [1]. Price = 120 - 48 = **72** [1].
**(c)** At Q = 15, MSC = 48 + 30 = 78 and MSB = 60, a gap of 18 [1]. Area = 0.5 x (15 - 12) x 18 = **27** [1].
**(d)** Total external cost = 18 x 15 = 270 [1]. The welfare loss counts only the net loss on tonnes 13 to 15, so it is far smaller [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the equation you set equal and the line MSC = MPC + MEC.

**4. (a)** Set MPB = MC: 96 - 4Q = 16 + Q, so 5Q = 80 and **Q = 16** [1]. Price = 16 + 16 = **32** [1].
**(b)** MSB = 96 + 10 - 4Q = 106 - 4Q. Set equal to MC: 106 - 4Q = 16 + Q, so 5Q = 90 and **Q = 18** [1]. MC at 18 is 34 [1].
**(c)** At Q = 16, MSB = 106 - 64 = 42 and MC = 32, a gap of 10 [1]. Area = 0.5 x (18 - 16) x 10 = **10** [1].
**(d)** The external benefit of 10 is an estimate, because lower infection spread is hard to value in money [1]. A smaller value would shrink the triangle [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (d), link the uncertainty to the diagram, the gap between MSB and MPB.

**5. (a)** Total willingness to pay = 30 + 25 + 20 + 15 + 12 + 8 = 110 [1], which exceeds 90, so net benefit = **20** [1].
**(b)** The highest single willingness to pay is 30, below 90, so no household buys alone [1]. The signal is non-excludable, so households can free ride [1]. With an equal share of 15, the households valuing it at 12 and 8 would refuse, so only 60 is raised, short of 90 [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie free riding to its consequence, non-provision of a good with positive net benefit.

**6. (a)** Set MPB = private cost: 45 - 0.05n = 15 + 0.05n, so **n = 300** [1]. External cost per trip = 0.1 x 300 - 0.05 x 300 = **15** [1].
**(b)** Set MSB = MSC: 45 - 0.05n = 15 + 0.1n [1], so **n = 200** [1]. At n = 200, MSB = MSC = **35** [1].
**(c)** At n = 300, MSC = 15 + 30 = 45 and MSB = 45 - 15 = 30, a gap of 15 [1]. Area = 0.5 x (300 - 200) x 15 = **750** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not mix average private cost and marginal social cost.

**7. (a)** External cost of consumption: the noise arises in use and affects other passengers [1].
**(b)** External benefit of production: others copy the hospital's technique free [1].
**(c)** External cost of production: the harm arises as the depot operates [1].
**(d)** External benefit of consumption: the gain arises from the household's use of its garden [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Decide making or using, then cost or benefit, and give the reason.

**8.** Depositors and creditors of the failed lender lose money [1]. Other banks holding its debts suffer losses [1]. Customers of sound banks lose confidence and withdraw savings [1]. Banks lend less, so firms cannot borrow [1]. Output and jobs fall, hurting households with no link to the lender [1]. The lender ignored these costs when taking risks, so social cost exceeds private cost and too much risk is taken [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Follow the chain from lender to named third party to consequence.

**9. (a)** The market produces while MPB is at least MPC. At unit 4, MPB = MPC = 32 [1], and unit 5 has MPB 26 below MPC 38, so the market quantity is **4 units** [1].
**(b)** MSC = MPC + 8: 22, 28, 34, 40, 46, 52 [1]. Unit 3 has MPB 38 above MSC 34, but unit 4 has MPB 32 below MSC 40, so the optimum is **3 units** [1].
**(c)** Total external cost = 8 x 4 = **32** at the market quantity and 8 x 3 = **24** at the social quantity [1], a difference of 8 [1].
**(d)** The fourth unit costs society 40 but is worth only 32, so the welfare loss is **8** [1]. The producer ignores the external cost of 8 per unit and produces until private benefit equals private cost [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* With discrete data, compare unit by unit and say why you stop where you do.

**10.** A public good must be non-rival and non-excludable. It is non-excludable if anyone can walk on [1], and non-rival when quiet [1]. In summer, extra visitors reduce others' space, so it becomes rival [1]. So it is a mixed case rather than a pure public good, and crowding is itself a negative externality [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Test each property separately; a good can pass one test and fail the other.

**11.** *Analysis.* An external benefit is a gain to a third party who does not pay [1]. A learner values education for personal gain such as higher pay [1]. Third parties also gain: employers, colleagues and communities with less crime [1]. So MSB lies above MPB [1]. The market quantity is below the social optimum [1]. The welfare gain is the triangle between MSB and MC from the market to the social quantity [1].
*Evaluation.* The external benefit varies by level and quality of course, so the gap between MSB and MPB may be small [1]. External benefits are hard to measure, so the welfare area is an estimate [1]. Private benefits are often large already, so many people may buy close to the optimum [1]. Some courses create few external benefits [1]. More education has an opportunity cost [1]. A final judgement that education is probably underprovided, by an amount that depends on the evidence, completes the answer [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Build a clear chain first, then weigh it against measurement, size and cost, and end with a judgement that follows.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not showing the equation that is set equal, so an arithmetic slip cannot be traced.
- Using the whole area under the curves instead of the triangle between Qs and Qm.
- Treating total external cost as the welfare loss.
- Calling a good public because the state provides it, or testing only one of rivalry and excludability.
- Describing free riding with no link to non-provision, or ending an evaluation without a judgement.
