## What the guide states

- Students develop factual and conceptual knowledge about individuals and societies.
- Year 5 strands: (i) use a wide range of terminology in context; (ii) demonstrate knowledge and understanding of subject-specific content and concepts through developed descriptions, explanations and examples.
- Maximum 8, in bands 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 and 7-8.
- In the on-screen examination, Criterion A is assessed in all three tasks: 6 marks in Investigating, 6 in Communicating and 8 in Thinking critically, which is 20 of the 80 marks.

## Band wording (Year 5)

| Band | Terminology | Knowledge and understanding |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | limited relevant terminology | basic; minimal descriptions and/or examples |
| 3-4 | some terminology, accurate and appropriate | adequate; satisfactory descriptions, explanations and examples |
| 5-6 | a range, accurate and appropriate | substantial; accurate descriptions, explanations and examples |
| 7-8 | consistently a wide range, effectively | excellent; thorough, accurate descriptions, explanations and examples |

What moves you up: more relevant terms, used accurately, with more detail and better examples.

## Command terms

| Term | Guide meaning | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | provide an answer from a number of possibilities; recognise and state briefly a distinguishing fact or feature | one short point |
| List | a sequence of brief answers with no explanation | words only |
| Describe | detailed account or picture of a situation, event, pattern or process | detail |
| Explain | detailed account including reasons or causes | detail plus why |
| Demonstrate | make clear by reasoning or evidence, illustrating with examples | idea plus example |
| Use | apply knowledge or rules to put theory into practice | apply to the case |
| Summarize | abstract a general theme or major point(s) | main idea |
| Exemplify | represent with an example | a specific case |

## Method: point, detail, reason, example

1. **Point**: the main idea in one sentence.
2. **Detail**: who, what, where, when, how many.
3. **Reason**: because, so, which led to.
4. **Example**: a named case and how it shows the idea.

Use the order that fits the question. For "describe", focus on detail. For "explain", focus on reasons.

## Key terms to know

### Geography

- **Urbanisation**: the increase in the proportion of people living in towns and cities.
- **Migration**: movement of people from one place to another. **Push factors** make people leave. **Pull factors** attract them.
- **Natural increase**: births minus deaths. Rate = birth rate − death rate.
- **Sustainability**: using resources at a rate that allows natural regeneration and minimises damage to the environment (the guide's geography concept).
- **Scale**: the local, regional, national or global level at which something is studied.
- **Process**: a measured movement or change that leads to particular results.

### History

- **Causality**: the relationship between cause and effect. A cause gives rise to an event; a consequence is its result.
- **Conflict**: disagreement or struggle from inequalities of power, interests or opinions.
- **Cooperation**: working together for the same end.
- **Ideology**: a system of ideas and ideals that forms the basis of political or economic theories and policies.
- **Innovation**: generating new ideas, products or solutions by altering existing ones.
- **Perspective**: how someone looks at something, in light of their position.
- **Significance**: the value of an event or idea, taking account of its historical context.

### Economics

- **Scarcity**: limited resources against unlimited wants.
- **Demand**: the quantity of a good that consumers want and are able to buy at a given price.
- **Supply**: the quantity that producers are willing and able to sell at a given price.
- **Opportunity cost**: the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.

Check your own course for the exact definitions your teacher uses.

## Worked calculation

Natural increase rate = birth rate − death rate.

- Birth rate 24 per 1000 and death rate 7 per 1000: natural increase = 24 − 7 = 17 per 1000 = 1.7% per year.
- Always add a comment: "The population is growing by 1.7% a year through natural increase alone."

## Mini worked answer

*Explain one reason why cities in Europe grew quickly in the nineteenth century.*

Industrialisation created many jobs in factories in towns. Rural workers moved to the towns to find work, which is rural to urban migration. This increased the urban population quickly, for example in Manchester, where cotton mills drew in thousands of workers. The pull of jobs, together with a push from low incomes on farms, caused rapid urbanisation.

Why it works: it names the cause, uses four terms accurately, and gives a named example.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Describe vs explain**: describe says what. Explain adds why.
- **Identify vs describe**: identify names a feature. Describe adds detail.
- **Example vs illustration**: an example is a specific case, named and located.
- **Push vs pull**: push factors are at the place of origin. Pull factors are at the destination.
- **Cause vs consequence**: cause comes before. Consequence comes after.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Terms used wrongly or not at all.
- Describing when the question says explain.
- "In some countries..." with no named case.
- A fact that is correct but off the point.
- A number with no meaning or comment.
- Long answers with a single idea repeated.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate natural increase if the birth rate is 15 per 1000 and the death rate is 9 per 1000.
2. Give one push factor and one pull factor for migration to a city.
3. What is the difference between "describe" and "explain"?
4. Use the terms *scarcity* and *opportunity cost* in one sentence.
5. Name one cause and one consequence of the growth of factory towns.

### Answers

1. 15 − 9 = 6 per 1000, which is 0.6% per year.
2. Push: unemployment, low rural incomes, shortage of farmland or poor services. Pull: jobs, schools, hospitals, higher wages (any one of each).
3. Describe gives a detailed account of what happens. Explain gives a detailed account including reasons or causes.
4. For example: "Because of scarcity, a council with a limited budget must choose between a new school and a new road, and the opportunity cost of the school is the road that is not built."
5. Cause: factories needed many workers (or people moved for jobs). Consequence: overcrowding and disease in towns (or public health laws).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Criterion A only about memorising facts?**
No. The descriptors reward accurate use of terminology and developed explanations with examples, not lists of facts.

**How many terms should I use?**
Enough to answer the question accurately. Quality and accuracy matter more than a long list.

**What if the question uses a source?**
The guide says you may demonstrate knowledge either from your course or from information in the source material. Use both, and name the source detail you are using.
