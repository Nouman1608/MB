## Key terms

- **Sectionalism**: loyalty to a region over the nation.
- **States' rights**: claim that states can limit federal power.
- **Popular sovereignty**: settlers in a territory decide on slavery for themselves.
- **Nullification**: South Carolina's claim (1832) that a state could void a federal law.
- **Emancipation Proclamation**: Lincoln's order freeing enslaved people in rebel areas, effective 1 January 1863.
- **Black Codes**: southern laws (1865 to 1866) restricting Black freedom.
- **Radical Republicans**: congressional group seeking Black rights and a firm Reconstruction.
- **Redeemers**: southern Democrats who regained control in the 1870s.
- **Sharecropping**: system where tenants paid landlords a share of the crop.

## Slavery in the USA

- Cotton: over half of US exports by the 1850s; nearly four million enslaved people by 1860.
- Conditions: gang labour, family separation, harsh punishment, sexual abuse.
- Adaptation: families, churches, culture. Resistance: slowing work, escape, Underground Railroad, rare revolts (Nat Turner, 1831).
- Politics: Garrison, Douglass, gag rules. Many northerners opposed slavery's expansion without seeking abolition.

## Long-term causes

- Nullification Crisis (1832 to 1833), which set out the states' rights argument.
- Sectionalism: industrial North, cotton South.
- Westward expansion and the status of new territories.
- Important distinction: economic differences mattered most where they linked to slavery.

## Short-term causes

| Event | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Compromise of 1850 | Fugitive Slave Act angered the North |
| Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854) | Repealed the Missouri line; violence in Kansas; Republican Party formed |
| *Dred Scott* (1857) | Denied Black citizenship; opened all territories to slavery |
| Harpers Ferry (1859) | Raised southern fear of slave revolt |
| Election of 1860 | Lincoln won without southern support; secession began |

## Reasons the Union won

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Resources | About 22 million people against about 9 million; most industry and railways |
| Blockade | Cut Confederate trade |
| Leaders | Lincoln, Grant, Sherman; Davis faced state-level resistance; Lee skilled but could not offset resources |
| Emancipation | Changed war aims; stopped foreign help for the Confederacy |
| Black soldiers | About 180,000 served |
| Foreign relations | Britain and France stayed out; Trent Affair settled |

## Impact of the war

- Political: stronger federal power, Republican dominance, end of secession as an option.
- Economic: Homestead Act, Pacific Railway Act and banking acts in the North; devastation in the South.
- Social: at least 620,000 dead; nearly four million freed; "Lost Cause" memory.
- Women: farms, factories, nursing, relief work; no new political rights, but wider activism.

## Reconstruction

| Stage | Key points |
|---|---|
| Presidential | Lincoln's lenient plan; Johnson's pardons |
| Congressional | Civil Rights Act (1866), Reconstruction Acts (1867), Johnson impeached (1868) |
| Amendments | 13th (end slavery), 14th (citizenship, equal protection), 15th (voting) |
| Resistance | Black Codes, Klan, terror; Enforcement Acts |
| Freed people's actions | Schools, churches, voting, office-holding, land demands |
| End | Compromise of 1877; troops withdrawn |

Success: legal equality on paper, Black political participation, schools. Failure: little land reform, violence, return of white control, sharecropping.

## Debates

- Cause: slavery central (McPherson, Foner) versus blundering politicians versus economics.
- Outcome: inevitable because of resources, or contingent on leaders and battles?
- Reconstruction: Dunning School (failure from Black rule, now rejected), Du Bois (a lost democratic chance), Foner (unfinished revolution).

## Essay skeleton

1. State the argument and your line.
2. Rank two or three factors, with dates and specific events.
3. Bring in a perspective and test it.
4. Consider the counter-argument.
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was Lincoln the main reason for the Union victory?"

1. Line: Lincoln was important, but resources and military and social factors were as vital.
2. For Lincoln: held the border states, kept the coalition together, chose Grant, issued the Emancipation Proclamation, kept foreign powers out.
3. Other factors: the Union's population, industry and railways; the blockade; Black soldiers; Confederate weaknesses (states' rights, shortage of resources).
4. Perspective: those who stress leadership against those who stress resources; Union victory as inevitable or contingent.
5. Judgement: resources made victory possible, and leadership, especially emancipation, turned potential into victory.

## Common mistakes

- Equating states' rights with a cause separate from slavery.
- Narrating battles.
- Ignoring Reconstruction.
- Treating the North as united in support of abolition.

## Self-test

1. What did the Kansas-Nebraska Act do?
2. Name one decision made in *Dred Scott*.
3. Give two reasons why the Union won.
4. What did the 14th Amendment do?
5. What was the Compromise of 1877?
6. Give one success and one failure of Reconstruction.

**Answers:** 1. Allowed popular sovereignty and repealed the Missouri Compromise line. 2. Black Americans could not be citizens, or Congress could not ban slavery in the territories. 3. Any two: resources, blockade, leadership, emancipation, Black soldiers. 4. Defined citizenship and guaranteed equal protection. 5. The settlement of the disputed 1876 election: Hayes became president and troops left the South. 6. For example, success: 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments; failure: loss of Black voting rights and economic dependence through sharecropping.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many causes should I use?**
Two or three, ranked.

**Do I need dates for every event?**
Know the main ones and the order of events.
