These questions cover subtopic 3.1 Coordinates of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The statement is the same for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Sketch rough axes whenever no diagram is given.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the point on the y-axis that is 3 units below the origin. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the point 5 units to the right of (–2, 4). **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Find the distance between

**(a)** (–5, 2) and (7, 2), **[1]**

**(b)** (–1, –8) and (–1, 3). **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A(3, –4), B(3, 5) and C(–2, 5) are three points.

**(a)** State which of the lines AB and BC is vertical. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why angle ABC is a right angle. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Write down every point in this list that lies on the line y = –2.

(4, –2)   (–2, 4)   (0, –2)   (–2, –2)   (–2, 0) **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Three vertices of a rectangle are (–3, 1), (4, 1) and (4, –5).

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the fourth vertex. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the area of the rectangle. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Point P is in the top-left quadrant. It is 6 units from the x-axis and 2 units from the y-axis. Write down the coordinates of P. **[1]**

**(b)** Point Q is in the bottom-right quadrant and is the same distances from the axes as P. Write down the coordinates of Q. **[1]**

**7.** (calculator) A(–1.5, 2.4) and B(3.7, 2.4) are two points.

**(a)** Find the length of AB. **[1]**

**(b)** C is directly below B and BC = 4.1 units. Write down the coordinates of C. **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator) ABCD is a parallelogram with the letters in order. A is (–2, 1), B is (3, 2) and C is (5, 6).

**(a)** Write down the step from B to C as a change in x and a change in y. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of D. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that the step from D to C is the same as the step from A to B. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) E(1, 2) and F(4, –1) are adjacent vertices of square EFGH. The letters go anticlockwise round the square.

**(a)** Write down the step from E to F. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of G and of H. **[2]**

**(c)** By drawing the smallest rectangle with sides along grid lines that encloses the square, find the area of EFGH. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) P(–3, –2), Q(–3, 6) and R(5, 1) are the vertices of a triangle.

**(a)** Find the length of PQ. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the area of triangle PQR. **[2]**

**(c)** T is the point (k, 0). The area of triangle PQT is 20 square units. Find the two possible values of k. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A triangle has vertices A(–2, –1), B(4, 1) and C(1, 5). None of its sides is horizontal or vertical.

Find the area of triangle ABC. **[4]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A map uses coordinates in kilometres, with x measured east and y measured north from a depot at (0, 0). A drone flies from the depot to A(–3, 4). It then flies due east for 9 km to B, then due south to C on the x-axis, and then along the x-axis back to the depot.

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of B. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of C. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the total distance flown from A to B to C and back to the depot. **[1]**

**13.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** A point lies on the line x = –3 and its y-coordinate is twice its x-coordinate. Write down its coordinates. **[1]**

**(b)** List every point with positive whole-number coordinates for which x + y = 4. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **(0, –3)** [1]

**(b)** **(3, 4)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a point on the y-axis has x = 0; writing (–3, 0) puts it on the x-axis instead.

**2. (a)** 7 – (–5) = **12 units** [1]

**(b)** 3 – (–8) = **11 units** [1]

**3. (a)** **AB** is vertical [1]

**(b)** A and B have the same x-coordinate, so AB is vertical; B and C have the same y-coordinate, so BC is horizontal; a vertical and a horizontal line meet at 90° [1]

*Examiner insight:* both facts are needed for the mark; "they look perpendicular" is not a reason.

**4.** (4, –2) and (0, –2) [1]; (–2, –2) [1]

*Examiner insight:* the test is y = –2 only; the x-coordinate can be anything. Including (–2, 4) or (–2, 0) loses a mark.

**5. (a)** **(–3, –5)** [1]

**(b)** Length 4 – (–3) = 7 and width 1 – (–5) = 6 [1]; area = 7 × 6 = **42 square units** [1]

**6. (a)** **(–2, 6)** [1]

**(b)** **(2, –6)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the distance from the x-axis is the size of the y-coordinate, not the x-coordinate; (–6, 2) is a common wrong answer.

**7. (a)** 3.7 – (–1.5) = **5.2 units** [1]

**(b)** 2.4 – 4.1 = –1.7, so **C(3.7, –1.7)** [1]

**8. (a)** Across 5 – 3 = **2**, up 6 – 2 = **4** [1]

**(b)** D = (–2 + 2, 1 + 4) = **(0, 5)** [1]

**(c)** A to B: across 3 – (–2) = 5, up 2 – 1 = 1; D to C: across 5 – 0 = 5, up 6 – 5 = 1, so the steps are equal [1]

*Examiner insight:* applying the step B → C to A, not to B, is what keeps D at a corner rather than on a diagonal.

**9. (a)** Across **3**, up **–3** (3 across, 3 down) [1]

**(b)** Turning the step 90° anticlockwise gives across 3, up 3, so **G = (7, 2)** [1] and **H = (4, 5)** [1]

**(c)** Enclosing rectangle from x = 1 to 7 and y = –1 to 5 has area 6 × 6 = 36, and the four corner triangles have area 4 × ½ × 3 × 3 = 18 [1]; area of EFGH = 36 – 18 = **18 square units** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check the turn with a sketch. Turning the wrong way gives G(1, –4) and H(–2, –1), a square on the other side of EF.

**10. (a)** 6 – (–2) = **8 units** [1]

**(b)** Height = horizontal distance from R to x = –3 = 5 – (–3) = 8 [1]; area = ½ × 8 × 8 = **32 square units** [1]

**(c)** ½ × 8 × h = 20 gives h = 5, the horizontal distance from T to x = –3 [1]; **k = 2 or k = –8** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the point can be on either side of PQ, so there are two answers. Giving only k = 2 scores one mark out of two.

**11.** Enclosing rectangle: x from –2 to 4 and y from –1 to 5, area 6 × 6 = 36 [1]

Corner triangles: ½ × 6 × 2 = 6 and ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 [1]; ½ × 3 × 6 = 9 [1]

Area of ABC = 36 – (6 + 6 + 9) = **15 square units** [1]

*Examiner insight:* label each corner triangle with its horizontal and vertical sides on a sketch, so none is counted twice or missed.

**12. (a)** 9 km east of A: (–3 + 9, 4) = **(6, 4)** [1]

**(b)** Due south of B on the x-axis: **(6, 0)** [1]

**(c)** AB = 9, BC = 4, C to depot = 6; total **19 km** [1]

**13. (a)** x = –3, y = 2 × (–3) = –6: **(–3, –6)** [1]

**(b)** Any two of (1, 3), (2, 2), (3, 1) [1]; all three and no others [1]

*Examiner insight:* "positive" excludes zero, so (0, 4) and (4, 0) are not included.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting a negative coordinate as if it were positive.
- Giving the distance from an axis using the wrong coordinate.
- Applying a step from the wrong vertex of a named shape.
- Turning a step clockwise when the letters go anticlockwise.
- Giving one answer when the position of a point allows two.
- Writing coordinates in the wrong order or without brackets.
