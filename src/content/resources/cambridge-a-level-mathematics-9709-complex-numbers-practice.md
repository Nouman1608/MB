These questions cover section **3.9 Complex numbers** of Pure Mathematics 3 (Paper 3) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. Show full working for multiplication, division and square roots. Give arguments in the range −π < θ ≤ π unless a question says otherwise. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [3.9 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-complex-numbers/) and the [3.9 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-complex-numbers-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** The complex numbers z and w are 3 − 4i and 1 + 2i. Showing your working, find zw and z/w in the form x + iy. **[4]**

**2.** Find the modulus and argument of −2 + 2√3 i, giving the argument exactly. **[2]**

**3.** Solve the equation z² + 4z + 13 = 0. **[2]**

**4.** Find the complex number z satisfying 2z + iz\* = 1 + 5i. **[4]**

**5.** The complex numbers u and v are 2e^(iπ/3) and 3e^(−iπ/4). Find the modulus and argument of uv and of u/v. **[3]**

**6.** It is given that 1 + i is a root of z² + az + b = 0, where a and b are real. Find a and b. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** The polynomial p(z) is 2z³ − 3z² + 8z + 5.

**(a)** Showing your working, verify that 1 + 2i is a root of p(z) = 0.

**(b)** Find the other two roots of p(z) = 0. **[6]**

**8.** Given that 2i is a root of z⁴ + 2z³ + 6z² + 8z + 8 = 0, find all the roots. **[5]**

**9.** **(a)** Find the two square roots of 21 − 20i, giving each in the form x + iy where x and y are real and exact.

**(b)** Hence solve the equation z² − 5z + (1 + 5i) = 0. **[8]**

**10.** The region R of an Argand diagram consists of the points representing z with |z − 2| ≤ 2 and |z − 2| ≤ |z − 2i|.

**(a)** Sketch R.

**(b)** Find the greatest value of |z| for points in R.

**(c)** Find the greatest value of arg z for points in R with z ≠ 0. **[7]**

**11.** The complex number z is 1 + 2i. On an Argand diagram, the points P, Q, S and T represent z, iz, z\* and z + 3 − i. Find the complex numbers represented by Q, S and T, and describe the single transformation that maps P to each of them. **[4]**

**12.** The complex numbers u and v are 1 + i√3 and 1 − i.

**(a)** Find the modulus and argument of u.

**(b)** Using your answer to (a), show that u³ = −8.

**(c)** Find the exact modulus and argument of u/v. **[7]**

**13.** Find the two complex numbers z that satisfy zz\* + 2z = 13 + 6i, giving each in exact form. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** zw = 3 + 6i − 4i − 8i² [1] = **11 + 2i** [1]. z/w = (3 − 4i)(1 − 2i)/((1 + 2i)(1 − 2i)) = (3 − 6i − 4i + 8i²)/5 [1] = (−5 − 10i)/5 = **−1 − 2i** [1]

**2.** |z| = √(4 + 12) = **4** [1]. Second quadrant, with tan⁻¹(2√3/2) = π/3, so **arg z = 2π/3** [1]

**3.** z = (−4 ± √(16 − 52))/2 = (−4 ± 6i)/2 [1] = **−2 ± 3i** [1]

**4.** z = x + iy: 2x + 2iy + i(x − iy) [1] = (2x + y) + i(x + 2y) [1]. So 2x + y = 1 and x + 2y = 5 [1]. **z = −1 + 3i** [1]

**5.** |uv| = 6 and arg(uv) = π/3 − π/4 = **π/12** [1]. |u/v| = **2/3** [1] and arg(u/v) = π/3 + π/4 = **7π/12** [1]

**6.** The coefficients are real, so 1 − i is the other root [1]. Sum of roots = 2 = −a, so **a = −2** [1]. Product = (1 + i)(1 − i) = 2, so **b = 2** [1]

**7.** **(a)** (1 + 2i)² = −3 + 4i and (1 + 2i)³ = (−3 + 4i)(1 + 2i) = −11 − 2i [1]. p(1 + 2i) = 2(−11 − 2i) − 3(−3 + 4i) + 8(1 + 2i) + 5 [1] = (−22 + 9 + 8 + 5) + i(−4 − 12 + 16) = 0 [1]

**(b)** The coefficients are real, so **1 − 2i** is a root [1]. (z − 1 − 2i)(z − 1 + 2i) = z² − 2z + 5 [1]. p(z) = (z² − 2z + 5)(2z + 1), so the third root is **z = −½** [1]

**8.** The coefficients are real, so −2i is also a root [1], and z² + 4 is a factor [1]. Dividing: z⁴ + 2z³ + 6z² + 8z + 8 = (z² + 4)(z² + 2z + 2) [1]. z² + 2z + 2 = 0 gives z = −1 ± i [1]. Roots: **2i, −2i, −1 + i, −1 − i** [1]

**9.** **(a)** (x + iy)² = 21 − 20i gives x² − y² = 21 and xy = −10 [1]. x² − 100/x² = 21, so x⁴ − 21x² − 100 = 0 [1]. (x² − 25)(x² + 4) = 0 [1]. x is real, so x² = −4 is rejected and x = ±5 [1]. Square roots **5 − 2i and −5 + 2i** [1]

**(b)** Discriminant = 25 − 4(1 + 5i) = 21 − 20i [1]. z = (5 ± (5 − 2i))/2 [1]. **z = 5 − i or z = i** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check z = i: i² − 5i + 1 + 5i = −1 + 1 = 0.

**10.** **(a)** Circle centre 2, radius 2, passing through O and 4 [1]. Perpendicular bisector of 2 and 2i, which is the line y = x [1]. R shaded: the part of the disc on or below y = x [1]

**(b)** The point of the disc farthest from O is 4, which lies below y = x, so it is in R [1]. Greatest |z| = **4** [1]

**(c)** The line y = x meets the circle at O and at 2 + 2i [1]. The disc lies in Re z ≥ 0, and every point of R is on or below y = x, so arg z ≤ π/4 throughout R. The greatest value is **π/4**, at 2 + 2i [1]

**11.** Q: iz = i − 2 = **−2 + i**, a rotation of π/2 anticlockwise about O [1]. S: z\* = **1 − 2i**, a reflection in the real axis [1]. T: **4 + i**, a translation by 3 units in the real direction and −1 in the imaginary direction [1]. In each of Q and S the distance from O is unchanged: |iz| = |z\*| = √5 [1]

**12.** **(a)** |u| = √(1 + 3) = **2** [1]. arg u = tan⁻¹(√3) = **π/3** [1]

**(b)** |u³| = 2³ = 8 and arg(u³) = 3 × π/3 = π [1]. So u³ = 8(cos π + i sin π) = −8 [1]

**(c)** |v| = √2 and arg v = −π/4 [1]. |u/v| = 2/√2 = **√2** [1]. arg(u/v) = π/3 − (−π/4) = **7π/12** [1]

**13.** z = x + iy: x² + y² + 2x + 2iy = 13 + 6i [1]. Imaginary parts: 2y = 6, so y = 3 [1]. Real parts: x² + 9 + 2x = 13, so x² + 2x − 4 = 0 [1]. x = −1 ± √5: **z = (−1 + √5) + 3i or z = (−1 − √5) + 3i** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- An argument in the wrong quadrant from tan⁻¹(y/x) alone.
- Missing working when dividing or finding square roots.
- Not stating "the coefficients are real" before using a conjugate root.
- Keeping x² = −4 when x must be real.
- Shading the wrong side of a perpendicular bisector.
- Giving an argument outside the required range after adding arguments.
