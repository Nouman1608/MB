## 1. The rule

Debit: increase in an asset, expense or drawings. Credit: increase in a liability, income or capital. Every transaction has at least one debit and one credit of equal total.

## 2. Source document to book of prime entry

| Source document | Book of prime entry |
|---|---|
| Sales invoice | Sales journal |
| Purchase invoice | Purchases journal |
| Credit note sent | Sales returns journal |
| Credit note received | Purchases returns journal |
| Journal voucher | General journal |
| Cheque counterfoil, till roll, cash receipt, paying-in slip counterfoil | Three column cash book |
| Bank statement items: standing order, direct debit, credit transfer, dishonoured cheque, debit card transaction, direct transfer | Three column cash book |

**Ledgers:** receivables ledger (credit customers), payables ledger (credit suppliers), general ledger (all other accounts).

## 3. Core double entries

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Credit sale | Customer (receivables ledger) | Sales |
| Credit purchase | Purchases | Supplier (payables ledger) |
| Goods returned by customer | Sales returns | Customer |
| Goods returned to supplier | Supplier | Purchases returns |
| Customer pays, less cash discount | Bank; Discounts allowed | Customer |
| Pay supplier, less cash discount | Supplier | Bank; Discounts received |
| Customer's cheque dishonoured | Customer | Bank |
| Direct debit for an expense | Expense | Bank |
| Credit transfer received from customer | Bank | Customer |
| Contra (set-off) | Supplier (payables ledger) | Customer (receivables ledger) |
| Write off irrecoverable debt | Irrecoverable debts | Customer |
| Recovery: reinstate, then receive | Customer, then Bank | Irrecoverable debts recovered, then Customer |
| Increase provision for doubtful debts | Income statement | Provision for doubtful debts |
| Decrease provision | Provision for doubtful debts | Income statement |
| Depreciation | Depreciation (expense) | Accumulated depreciation |

Trade discount: **no entry**. Record the net invoice value only.

## 4. Disposal account in four steps

1. Debit Disposal, credit Non-current asset: **cost**.
2. Debit Accumulated depreciation, credit Disposal: **depreciation to date**.
3. Debit Bank (or the buyer), credit Disposal: **proceeds**.
4. Balance the account. A balancing figure on the credit side is a **loss** (expense). On the debit side it is a **profit** (income).

Quick check: proceeds − carrying amount = profit (positive) or loss (negative).

## 5. Formulas

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Straight line depreciation | (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life |
| Reducing balance depreciation | rate × carrying amount at start of year |
| Carrying amount | cost − accumulated depreciation |
| Cost of sales | opening inventory + purchases − purchases returns − closing inventory |
| Revenue (net) | sales − sales returns |
| Provision charge | new provision − old provision (increase = expense; decrease = income) |
| Expense charged | amount paid + amount owing − amount prepaid |
| Income credited | amount received + income due − income received in advance |

Calculate the provision on receivables **after** writing off irrecoverable debts.

## 6. Adjustments: where each one goes

| Adjustment | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Other payables (expense owing) | Increase expense | Current liability |
| Other receivables (expense prepaid) | Reduce expense | Current asset |
| Income due | Increase income | Current asset |
| Income received in advance | Reduce income | Current liability |
| Irrecoverable debt written off | Expense | Reduces trade receivables |
| Irrecoverable debt recovered | Income | Bank increases |
| Provision for doubtful debts | Change only | Deducted from trade receivables |
| Depreciation | Charge for the year | Deducted from cost |
| Loss / profit on disposal | Expense / income | Asset and its depreciation removed |
| Closing inventory | Reduces cost of sales | Current asset |

## 7. Method: statements from a trial balance

1. Mark each trial balance line as income statement or statement of financial position.
2. Tick off each additional item twice: once in the income statement, once in the statement of financial position.
3. Revenue less sales returns.
4. Cost of sales using opening and closing inventory and purchases less purchases returns. Service businesses skip this step.
5. Gross profit, then add other income (discounts received, rent received, profit on disposal, decrease in provision, irrecoverable debts recovered).
6. Deduct adjusted expenses to get profit for the year.
7. Statement of financial position under the five subheadings: **non-current assets, current assets, current liabilities, non-current liabilities, equity**.
8. Equity = opening capital + profit − drawings. It must equal net assets.

## 8. Capital or revenue?

| | Expenditure | Income |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital** | Buys or improves a non-current asset (including delivery and installation) | Capital introduced, loans received, sale proceeds of non-current assets |
| **Revenue** | Running costs and maintenance | Sales, fees, rent received, commission received |

Test: does the spending give a benefit over more than one period by adding to an asset? If yes, it is capital expenditure.

## 9. Must-know distinctions

- **Trade vs cash discount.** Trade discount reduces list price and is never recorded. Cash discount rewards prompt payment and is recorded through the cash book discount columns.
- **Irrecoverable debt vs provision for doubtful debts.** An irrecoverable debt is a specific debt that will not be paid and is removed. A provision is an estimate against debts that might not be paid; the debts stay in receivables.
- **Other payable vs income received in advance.** Both are current liabilities. One is an expense not yet paid; the other is income received before it is earned.
- **Straight line vs reducing balance.** Straight line gives an equal charge each year. Reducing balance gives larger charges in early years, which suits assets such as vehicles that lose more value early on.
- **Contra in the cash book vs contra between ledgers.** Moving cash into the bank is a cash book contra. A set-off between a customer's and supplier's accounts for the same business goes through the general journal.

## 10. Service business layout

A service business has no cost of sales or gross profit. Pell Street Tutors received fees of 58,400, of which 900 is for next year's lessons.

```
Income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026
Fees (58,400 - 900)                              57,500
Expenses: rent 9,600; wages 21,300;
insurance 720; depreciation 1,450               (33,070)
Profit for the year                              24,430
```

The 900 is a current liability. The statement of financial position uses the same five subheadings as for a trading business.

## Quick self-test

1. Goods with a list price of 5,000 are sold on credit with 20% trade discount and 3% cash discount for payment within 7 days. The customer pays in time. What is the invoice value, the discount allowed and the amount received?
2. A machine costs 45,000, has a residual value of 5,000 and a useful life of eight years. Calculate the straight line charge.
3. Equipment costs 20,000 and is depreciated at 30% reducing balance. Calculate the year 2 charge and the carrying amount at the end of year 2.
4. Trade receivables are 24,000. The provision is to be 4%; last year's provision was 1,100. What goes to the income statement?
5. A van cost 12,000, accumulated depreciation is 7,500 and it is sold for 5,100. Profit or loss on disposal?
6. Opening inventory 6,200; purchases 51,000; purchases returns 800; closing inventory 7,400. Cost of sales?
7. Insurance paid in the year is 2,600, of which 400 covers next year. State the income statement charge and the statement of financial position entry.
8. Which book of prime entry records a credit note received from a supplier?
9. In a contra entry, which ledger account is debited?
10. Is repainting the shop capital or revenue expenditure?
11. State the two double entries when a written-off debt is recovered.
12. Under which subheading does income received in advance appear?

### Answers

1. Invoice 5,000 × 80% = **4,000**; discount allowed 4,000 × 3% = **120**; received **3,880**.
2. (45,000 − 5,000) ÷ 8 = **5,000** a year.
3. Year 1 charge 6,000, carrying amount 14,000. Year 2 charge 30% × 14,000 = **4,200**; carrying amount **9,800**.
4. New provision 4% × 24,000 = 960. Decrease of 1,100 − 960 = **140**, shown as income (added to gross profit).
5. Carrying amount 12,000 − 7,500 = 4,500; proceeds 5,100, so **profit of 600**.
6. 6,200 + 51,000 − 800 − 7,400 = **49,000**.
7. Expense **2,200**; other receivable of **400** under current assets.
8. **Purchases returns journal.**
9. The **supplier's account in the payables ledger** (the customer's account in the receivables ledger is credited).
10. **Revenue expenditure**: it maintains the asset rather than improving it.
11. Debit customer, credit Irrecoverable debts recovered; then debit Bank, credit customer.
12. **Current liabilities.**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Entering trade discount in the ledger, or calculating cash discount on the list price instead of the invoice value.
- Posting the discount column totals to the wrong side, or forgetting them entirely.
- Charging the full provision for doubtful debts instead of the increase or decrease.
- Calculating the provision before writing off the irrecoverable debt.
- Applying the reducing balance rate to cost every year.
- Recording a recovered debt only as cash received, without reinstating the customer's account.
- Writing "accruals" or "prepayments" in the statement of financial position without the subheading the specification uses (current liabilities or current assets).
- Omitting or misnaming subheadings in the statement of financial position.
- Adding drawings to expenses rather than deducting them from equity.
- Leaving a disposal account without the cost or the accumulated depreciation transfer.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between trade discount and cash discount?**
Trade discount reduces the list price and is never recorded; only the net invoice value is entered. Cash discount is given for prompt payment and is recorded in the discount columns of the cash book.

**What are the four steps in a disposal account?**
Debit disposal and credit the asset with its cost; debit accumulated depreciation and credit disposal; debit bank and credit disposal with the proceeds; then transfer the balance as the profit or loss on disposal.

**How do you decide if spending is capital or revenue?**
Ask whether the spending gives a benefit over more than one period by adding to an asset. If yes, it is capital expenditure; if not, it is revenue expenditure.

**How should accruals and prepayments be shown in the statement of financial position?**
Show them under the subheadings the specification uses: accrued expenses under current liabilities and prepayments under current assets, rather than as separate unlabelled headings.

**Does a service business have a gross profit?**
No. A service business has no cost of sales or gross profit; fees are listed and expenses deducted to reach profit for the year.
