## Section A

**1.** Strontium is in Group 2. Predict the formulas of its oxide and its chloride. **[2]**

**2.** The successive ionisation energies of an element show the largest jump between the fifth and sixth values. State the group of the element. **[1]**

**3.** Caesium is in Group 1. Predict the likely pH of the solution formed when caesium oxide is added to water. **[1]**

**4.** Predict the type of bonding in germanium(IV) chloride, GeCl₄, and give a reason. **[2]**

**5.** Selenium is in Group 16. Predict the formula of the oxide in which selenium has its highest oxidation number. **[1]**

**6.** State what the period number of an element tells you about its atoms. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Rubidium is in Group 1, below potassium.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of rubidium with water. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict, with a reason, how vigorous this reaction is compared with that of sodium. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict the pH of a solution of rubidium chloride, and explain your answer in terms of bonding. **[2]**

**8.** Germanium is in Group 14, below silicon.

**(a)** Write an equation for the hydrolysis of GeCl₄ by water. **[1]**

**(b)** State one observation you would expect when GeCl₄ is added to water. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict, with a reason, whether GeO₂ is more or less acidic than SiO₂. **[2]**

**9.** Element A has these properties:

- its oxide has the formula A₂O and dissolves in water to give a strongly alkaline solution;
- its chloride, ACl, is a white solid with a high melting point that dissolves to give a neutral solution;
- A reacts with cold water more vigorously than sodium.

Deduce the group of A, its likely position relative to sodium, and a possible identity. **[3]**

**10.** Element B has these properties:

- B has a very high melting point and conducts electricity only slightly, more so when heated;
- its chloride, BCl₄, is a colourless liquid that fumes in moist air;
- its oxide, BO₂, is a white solid with a very high melting point that does not dissolve in water.

**(a)** Deduce the group of B and the structure of the element. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest the identity of B and write an equation for the reaction of BCl₄ with water. **[2]**

**11.** Element C has the first four ionisation energies 578, 1817, 2745 and 11 577 kJ mol⁻¹. Its oxide is insoluble in water but reacts with both dilute hydrochloric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide. Its chloride gives a solution of pH about 3.

**(a)** Use the ionisation energies to deduce the group of C. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe what the behaviour of the oxide shows about the nature of C. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify C and explain why its chloride solution is acidic. **[2]**

**12.** Element D is in Period 4. It forms two oxides, DO₂ and DO₃, both of which dissolve in water to give acidic solutions. The element is a solid that does not conduct electricity.

**(a)** Deduce the group of D and whether it is a metal or a non-metal. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify D and write an equation for the reaction of DO₃ with water. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** SrO [1]; SrCl₂ [1].

**2.** Group 15 [1].

**3.** About 13–14 (strongly alkaline) [1].

**4.** Covalent [1]. Germanium is in Group 14 like silicon, so by analogy with SiCl₄ the electronegativity difference between germanium and chlorine is small and the Ge–Cl bonds are covalent [1].

**5.** SeO₃ [1].

**6.** The number of occupied electron shells (principal quantum shells) [1].

**7. (a)** 2Rb + 2H₂O → 2RbOH + H₂ [1].

**(b)** More vigorous [1]: rubidium is lower in Group 1, so its atoms are larger and its first ionisation energy is lower; the outer electron is lost more easily [1].

**(c)** pH 7 [1]. RbCl is ionic, because of the large electronegativity difference, so it dissolves as hydrated ions without reaction with water [1].

**8. (a)** GeCl₄ + 2H₂O → GeO₂ + 4HCl [1].

**(b)** Misty fumes (of HCl), or a white solid forms, or an acidic solution forms [1].

**(c)** Less acidic [1]: germanium is lower in Group 14 than silicon, so it has more metallic character [1].

**9.** Group 1, from the formulas A₂O and ACl (oxidation number +1) and the basic oxide and ionic chloride [1]. Below sodium in Group 1, because it reacts more vigorously with water [1]. For example potassium (accept any Group 1 metal below sodium) [1].

**10. (a)** Group 14, from the formulas BCl₄ and BO₂ (oxidation number +4) [1]. The element has a giant covalent structure and is a semiconductor [1].

**(b)** Silicon (accept germanium) [1]. SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl [1].

**11. (a)** The large jump is between the third and fourth ionisation energies [1], so C has three outer electrons and is in Group 13 [1].

**(b)** The oxide is amphoteric, so C lies at the boundary between metals and non-metals [1].

**(c)** Aluminium [1]. Al³⁺ is small and highly charged, so in [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ it polarises the water molecules and H⁺ ions are released [1].

**12. (a)** Group 16: the oxides DO₂ (+4) and DO₃ (+6) match sulfur's SO₂ and SO₃ [1]. Non-metal: its oxides are acidic and the element does not conduct [1].

**(b)** Selenium [1]. SeO₃ + H₂O → H₂SeO₄ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a prediction with no reason or analogy.
- Using the period number to decide the number of outer electrons.
- Misreading which pair of ionisation energies contains the jump.
- Naming one element without showing the group-level deduction.
- Forgetting to adjust a prediction for an element lower in a group.
