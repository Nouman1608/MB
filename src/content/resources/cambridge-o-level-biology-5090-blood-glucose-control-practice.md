## Section A

**1.** Which hormone causes the liver to convert glycogen into glucose?

A: adrenaline only

B: glucagon

C: insulin

D: testosterone

**[1]**

**2.** Which organ secretes insulin?

A: the liver

B: the kidney

C: the pancreas

D: the stomach

**[1]**

**3.** State the name of the storage carbohydrate made in the liver from glucose. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** signs of Type 1 diabetes. **[2]**

**5.** State how Type 1 diabetes is treated. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain why the blood glucose concentration must be kept within narrow limits. **[3]**

**7.** A healthy person eats a large plate of rice.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the blood glucose concentration soon after eating, and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the body brings the concentration back to normal. **[4]**

**(c)** State the name given to this type of control. **[1]**

**8.** The table shows the blood glucose concentration of two people after each drank a glucose solution at time 0.

| Time (minutes) | Person X (mg per 100 cm3) | Person Y (mg per 100 cm3) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 90 | 160 |
| 60 | 135 | 300 |
| 120 | 100 | 270 |
| 180 | 90 | 250 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in the blood glucose concentration of person X in the first 60 minutes. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the concentration of person Y between 60 and 180 minutes. **[2]**

**(c)** One of the people has Type 1 diabetes. Identify the person and give **two** reasons from the data. **[3]**

**(d)** Predict one other test result you would expect for this person and explain it. **[2]**

**9.** During a long run, an athlete's muscles use large amounts of glucose.

**(a)** Name the hormone released by the pancreas as the blood glucose concentration falls. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the effect of this hormone on the liver. **[2]**

**(c)** State the name of the hormone that a runner may release if she is frightened by a dog, and state its effect on the blood glucose concentration. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why insulin is given to patients by injection and not as a tablet. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a person with untreated Type 1 diabetes has glucose in the urine. **[3]**

**12.** State the difference between glucose, glycogen and glucagon. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Glycogen [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Increased blood glucose concentration [1]. Glucose in the urine [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Injections of insulin [1]. **[1]**

**6.** If the concentration is too low, cells cannot respire fast enough to release the energy they need [1]. If it is too high, the water balance of cells is upset by osmosis [1], and glucose is lost in the urine [1]. **[3]**

**7.**

**(a)** The concentration rises [1] because glucose from the digested rice is absorbed into the blood [1].

**(b)** The pancreas detects the rise [1] and secretes insulin [1]. Insulin is carried in the blood to the liver [1], which converts glucose into glycogen, so the concentration falls [1].

**(c)** Negative feedback [1]. **[7]**

**8.**

**(a)** (135 - 90) divided by 90 x 100 [1] = 50 per cent [1].

**(b)** (300 - 250) divided by 300 x 100 [1] = 16.7 per cent (to one decimal place) [1].

**(c)** Person Y [1]. The starting concentration is much higher than normal [1], and it stays very high after 180 minutes, so it does not return to the start [1].

**(d)** Glucose in the urine [1], because the concentration is too high for the kidney tubules to reabsorb all of the glucose [1]. **[9]**

**9.**

**(a)** Glucagon [1].

**(b)** It causes the liver to convert glycogen into glucose [1] and release it into the blood [1].

**(c)** Adrenaline [1], which increases the blood glucose concentration [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Insulin is a protein [1], so if swallowed it would be digested in the gut, and it would not enter the blood as insulin [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The blood glucose concentration is very high because there is not enough insulin [1]. All the glucose is filtered at the glomerulus [1], but the kidney tubules cannot reabsorb all of it, so some is lost in the urine [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Glucose is the sugar carried in the blood and used in respiration [1]. Glycogen is the insoluble storage carbohydrate made from glucose in the liver [1]. Glucagon is the hormone that causes the liver to change glycogen to glucose [1]. **[3]**

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the effects of insulin and glucagon.
- Writing "glucagon stores glucose".
- Confusing glucose, glycogen and glucagon.
- Not stating that the liver is the organ that responds to the hormones.
- Saying that insulin cannot be swallowed "because it is too strong" instead of "because it is a protein that is digested".
