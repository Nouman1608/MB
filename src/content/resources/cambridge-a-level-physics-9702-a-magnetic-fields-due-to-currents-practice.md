## Section A

**1.** A long straight vertical wire carries a current upwards. State the shape of the magnetic field lines around it and their direction when viewed from above. **[2]**

**2.** State how the magnetic field around a long straight wire changes with distance from the wire, and how this is shown on a field-line sketch. **[2]**

**3.** A long solenoid carries a steady current.

**(a)** Describe the magnetic field inside the solenoid, away from its ends. **[2]**

**(b)** Looking at end X of the solenoid, the current in the turns flows anticlockwise. State the polarity of end X. **[1]**

**4.** State the effect of placing an iron core inside a current-carrying solenoid, and explain why it has this effect. **[2]**

**5.** Two long parallel wires carry currents in opposite directions. State whether the wires attract or repel. **[1]**

**6.** Which material would **not** increase the magnetic field of a solenoid if used as its core?

A: soft iron

B: steel

C: copper

D: an iron alloy

**[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A flat circular coil lies in the plane at right angles to this page. It cuts the page at two points, L (left) and R (right). At L the current comes out of the page; at R it goes into the page.

**(a)** Sketch (or describe) the magnetic field pattern of the coil in the plane of the page. **[3]**

**(b)** State the direction of the field at the centre of the coil. **[1]**

**(c)** State how the field pattern of the coil resembles that of a bar magnet, and which side of the coil acts as the north pole. **[2]**

**8.** Two long parallel vertical wires, P and Q, pass through a horizontal card. P is to the left of Q. Both carry current downwards through the card (into the page when viewed from above).

**(a)** Explain the origin of the force on Q. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine the direction of the magnetic field due to P at the position of Q, viewed from above. **[1]**

**(c)** Hence determine the direction of the force on Q. **[1]**

**(d)** State the direction of the force on P, and name the law you used. **[2]**

**9.** Two long parallel wires, A and B, are 5.0 cm apart. A carries 8.0 A and B carries 2.0 A in the same direction. The flux density due to A at the position of B is 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ T.

**(a)** Calculate the force on a 0.50 m length of B. **[2]**

**(b)** State the size of the force on a 0.50 m length of A. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the flux density due to B at the position of A. **[2]**

**(d)** A student says: "A carries four times the current, so A feels four times the force." Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**10.** An electromagnet consists of a coil wound on a soft-iron core. It is used in a relay to pull an iron armature and close a switch.

**(a)** Explain why the iron core is used. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the core is made of soft iron rather than a material that stays magnetised. **[2]**

**11.** A loosely wound, flexible coil of wire hangs vertically. A large current is passed through it.

**(a)** State what happens to the length of the coil. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer. **[3]**

**12.** Two long parallel wires carry equal currents in the same direction.

**(a)** Describe the resultant magnetic field at the point midway between them. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the combined field pattern to explain why the wires attract. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Concentric circles centred on the wire, in horizontal planes [1]; anticlockwise viewed from above [1].

**2.** The field becomes weaker with increasing distance from the wire [1]; the circles are drawn further apart as distance increases [1].

**3. (a)** Strong and uniform [1]: straight, parallel, equally spaced lines along the axis [1].

**(b)** North pole [1].

**4.** The flux density is greatly increased for the same current [1], because the iron is magnetised by the solenoid's field and adds its own field in the same direction [1].

**5.** Repel [1].

**6.** C [1].

**7. (a)** Small loops round L and R, anticlockwise round L and clockwise round R [1]; lines near the centre nearly straight and parallel, along the axis through the coil [1]; symmetrical pattern with arrows, lines not crossing [1].

**(b)** Up the page (from the bottom side of the coil through to the top side) [1].

**(c)** Lines emerge from one face and loop round to enter the other, as for a short bar magnet [1]; the face the lines emerge from (the upper side as drawn) is the north pole [1].

**8. (a)** P carries a current, so it produces a magnetic field [1]. Q carries a current in this field, so it experiences a force [1].

**(b)** Current into the page in P gives a clockwise field; at Q, to the right of P, the field points down the page (towards the bottom of the diagram) [1].

**(c)** Left-hand rule: current into the page, field down the page → force to the left, towards P [1].

**(d)** To the right, towards Q [1], by Newton's third law [1].

**9. (a)** F = B_A I_B L = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ × 2.0 × 0.50 [1] = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ N [1].

**(b)** 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ N (equal and opposite) [1].

**(c)** B_B = F/(I_A L) = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (8.0 × 0.50) [1] = 8.0 × 10⁻⁶ T [1].

**(d)** The forces form a Newton's third law pair, so they are equal in size: A has the larger current but sits in the weaker field produced by B [1].

**10. (a)** The iron core becomes magnetised by the coil's field [1], greatly increasing the flux density, so a smaller current produces enough force to pull the armature [1].

**(b)** Soft iron loses its magnetism when the current is switched off [1], so the armature is released and the switch can open again; a material that stayed magnetised would keep holding the armature [1].

**11. (a)** The coil shortens [1].

**(b)** Adjacent turns carry current in the same direction [1]. Each turn is in the magnetic field produced by the neighbouring turns, so it experiences a force [1]. Currents in the same direction attract, so the turns pull towards each other [1].

**12. (a)** The fields of the two wires at the midpoint are equal in size [1] and opposite in direction, so the resultant field is zero [1].

**(b)** The fields partly cancel in the region between the wires and reinforce outside them [1], so the field is weaker between the wires and each wire is pushed towards the weaker-field region, towards the other wire [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the left hand to find the field around a wire.
- Drawing equally spaced circles round a straight wire.
- Missing the straight, axial lines at the centre of a coil.
- Saying any metal core increases a solenoid's field.
- Not stating that each wire is in the field produced by the other when explaining the force.
- Giving unequal forces to wires that carry unequal currents.
