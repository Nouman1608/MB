## Questions

**1.** Other than leadership, state **two** factors that affect the successful implementation of a strategy. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by total float in network analysis. **[2]**

**3.** Halsetown Theatre is staging a new play. Times are in days.

| Activity | Duration | Depends on | Nodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Cast the play | 2 | -- | 1 → 2 |
| B Read-through and blocking | 5 | A | 2 → 3 |
| C Design the set | 3 | A | 2 → 4 |
| D Build the set | 4 | C | 4 → 5 |
| E Rehearse with lighting and music | 6 | B | 3 → 6 |
| F Paint and fit the set | 2 | D | 5 → 6 |
| G Preview performances | 3 | E, F | 6 → 7 |

Calculate the Earliest Start Time and Latest Finish Time at each node, and identify the critical path and minimum project duration. **[4]**

**4.** Using your answer to question 3, calculate the total float on activity D and explain what it means for Halsetown's production manager. **[3]**

**5.** Kittermoor Ciders is launching a new cider. Times are in days. Activities: A Order apples (3, nodes 1 → 2); B Clean tanks (4, nodes 1 → 3); C Press apples (2, nodes 2 → 4); D Prepare bottling line (5, nodes 3 → 5); E Blend and filter (3, nodes 4 → 5); F Bottle first batch (2, nodes 5 → 6).

| Node | EST | LFT |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 3 | ? |
| 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 4 | ? | 6 |
| 5 | 9 | 9 |
| 6 | ? | ? |

**(a)** Complete the four missing values. **[4]**
**(b)** The apple press breaks, so activity C takes 4 days instead of 2. State the effect on the project duration and the new critical path. **[2]**

**6.** Brisworth Dental is opening a second clinic as part of its growth strategy. Explain **one** benefit to Brisworth of using project management for the opening. **[3]**

**7.** Vannick Software's objective is to double subscription revenue from schools in three years. Its sales team is paid commission only on one-off licence sales. Explain why the links between objectives, strategy and functional decisions are important for Vannick. **[4]**

**8.** Sallowfield Cameras spent fifteen years improving its compact digital cameras while most buyers began taking photos on their phones. Explain **two** possible reasons for this strategic drift. **[4]**

**9.** Trevanna Coffee, a chain of cafés, plans to expand overseas through franchising. Analyse how leadership and communication could affect the success of this strategy. **[6]**

**10.** Morrowfield Hotels operates in a market where booking habits and technology change quickly. Analyse the value of strategic planning to Morrowfield. **[6]**

**11.** Carnbray Foods has won a trial contract to supply a supermarket with a new ready-meal range. The supermarket wants the first delivery within **12 weeks** and may offer a three-year contract if the trial succeeds. Times are in weeks.

| Activity | Duration | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| A Agree recipes | 2 | -- |
| B Order packaging | 3 | A |
| C Test kitchen trials | 4 | A |
| D Install new production line | 5 | A |
| E Train line staff | 2 | D |
| F Pilot production | 3 | C, E |
| G Deliver first order | 1 | B, F |

Further information: a contractor can install the line in 4 weeks for an extra £18,000. Carnbray's last two launches both overran. The production director has just resigned; the operations manager will run the launch alongside her current job.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum project duration and identify the critical path. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether network analysis will allow Carnbray to implement this strategy successfully. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: resources [1]; quality of planning and monitoring; communication; external factors [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" needs only the factor named; leadership is excluded by the question, so naming it earns nothing.

**2.** Total float is the length of time an activity can be delayed [1] without delaying the completion of the whole project [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Include "the whole project"; without it the definition is incomplete.

**3.** Forward pass: node 2 = 2, node 3 = 7, node 4 = 5, node 5 = 9 [1]; node 6 = highest of 7 + 6 = 13 and 9 + 2 = 11, so 13; node 7 = 16 [1]. Backward pass: node 7 = 16, node 6 = 13, node 5 = 11, node 4 = 7, node 3 = 7, node 2 = lowest of 7 − 5 = 2 and 7 − 3 = 4, so 2, node 1 = 0 [1]. Critical path **A → B → E → G**, minimum duration **16 days** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that 2 + 5 + 6 + 3 = 16; if the critical path durations do not add to the project length, an earlier node value is wrong.

**4.** Total float = LFT at node 5 − duration − EST at node 4 [1] = 11 − 4 − 5 = **2 days** [1]. Building the set can start up to 2 days late or overrun by 2 days without delaying the previews, so the manager could move set builders to help elsewhere for up to 2 days [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the LFT of D's end node, not its start node; using node 4's LFT gives a wrong float.

**5. (a)** Node 2 LFT = 6 − 2 = **4** [1]. Node 4 EST = 3 + 2 = **5** [1]. Node 6 EST = 9 + 2 = **11** [1]. Node 6 LFT = **11** [1].
**(b)** C has 1 day of float, so 2 extra days delay the project by 1 day, to **12 days** [1]. The new critical path is **A → C → E → F** (3 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 12) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State the float before the effect; it shows why the project slips by 1 day, not 2.

**6.** Project management gives the opening a named manager with a timetable and budget [1]. Tasks such as fitting out treatment rooms then have clear owners and deadlines [1], so Brisworth can spot delays early and open on the date promised to patients, protecting revenue and reputation [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* One benefit developed into its effect on Brisworth's opening date scores better than two benefits stated briefly.

**7.** The strategy only works if functional decisions support it [1]. Vannick's commission scheme rewards one-off licences, so sales staff are paid to push what the strategy is moving away from [1]. Linking HR decisions to the objective, for example by paying commission on new subscriptions, would make the sales effort pull towards doubling subscription revenue [1]. Linked targets also let managers measure whether each function is contributing, so problems are spotted early [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the commission scheme in the case; a general list of reasons for alignment stays at knowledge level.

**8.** Past success: compact cameras had been profitable for years [1], so managers assumed improving them would keep working and saw no need to change [1]. Poor environmental scanning: the firm may not have tracked how quickly phone cameras improved [1], so it reacted only after sales had fallen too far to recover [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a linked consequence for Sallowfield; two bare reasons with no development earn limited credit.

**9.** Leadership: Trevanna's directors must choose franchisees carefully and keep standards consistent abroad [1]. Clear brand rules and regular visits would protect quality, so the brand's reputation grows in each new country [1]. Rushing to sign franchisees to hit expansion targets could create poor outlets that damage the brand at home too [1]. Communication: franchisees need clear manuals, training and regular contact [1]. In a country with a different language, misunderstandings about recipes or service could cut quality and sales [1]. Two-way communication also tells Trevanna what local customers want, so it can adapt the menu [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie every point to franchising in another country; definitions of leadership or communication add little.

**10.** Strategic planning makes Morrowfield analyse its market and strengths before committing large sums, such as hotel refurbishments [1], reducing the chance of investing in the wrong thing [1]. It gives every hotel a shared direction and targets, such as occupancy, to monitor progress against [1]. However, booking habits change quickly, so a detailed five-year plan may date within a year [1]. If managers stick rigidly to it, Morrowfield risks strategic drift [1]. Planning is most valuable if Morrowfield sets a clear direction but reviews the plan often [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Weighing the value against the fast-changing market is what lifts this answer beyond description.

**11. (a)** Paths: A–D–E–F–G = 2 + 5 + 2 + 3 + 1 = 13; A–C–F–G = 10; A–B–G = 6. Minimum duration **13 weeks**; critical path **A → D → E → F → G** [1]. This is 1 week longer than the 12-week deadline [1].
**(b)** Network analysis shows Carnbray, before it commits, that the current plan misses the deadline by 1 week [1]. It also shows where to act: installing the line (D) is critical, so paying the contractor £18,000 to cut D to 4 weeks reduces the project to 12 weeks [1]. B has 7 weeks of float and C has 3 weeks, so staff from those activities could help with training or pilot runs [1]. Once the launch starts, the network becomes a monitoring tool, showing each week whether critical activities are on time [1].

However, even with the contractor, A–D–E–F–G has zero float, so any overrun on it misses the deadline [1]. Carnbray's last two launches overran, suggesting unreliable time estimates [1], and the network is only as good as its estimates [1]. It also ignores quality: rushed pilot production could fail the supermarket's standards and lose the three-year contract [1].

Other implementation factors are weak. The operations manager is running the launch alongside her existing job, so leadership and monitoring may suffer [1], and communication with the supermarket and line staff depends on her limited time [1].

Judgement: network analysis is necessary but not sufficient. It shows that 12 weeks is possible only with the contractor and with no margin [1]. Given the overruns and the leadership gap, Carnbray should negotiate a 14-week deadline or appoint a dedicated launch manager first; the £18,000 is worth spending only if the long-term contract is likely [1]. **[14]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the calculated 13 weeks and the zero-float result as evidence in the evaluation, then reach a judgement that depends on the case facts.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the lower value when two arrows enter a node on the forward pass.
- Taking the higher value when two arrows leave a node on the backward pass.
- Calculating float as LFT − EST and leaving out the duration.
- Naming a critical path without stating the project duration.
- Assuming any delay extends the project, instead of comparing it with the float.
- Defining strategic drift without explaining why the business failed to adapt.
- Ending an evaluation with no clear judgement about what the business should do.
