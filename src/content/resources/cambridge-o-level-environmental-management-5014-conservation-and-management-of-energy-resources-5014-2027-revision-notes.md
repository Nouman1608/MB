## Syllabus coverage (5014, 2027-2029)

| Ref | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| 1.5.1 | Describe eight strategies for managing energy resources |
| 1.5.2 | Describe blue and green hydrogen, ground source and air source heat pumps |
| 1.5.3 | Discuss benefits and limitations of the strategies |

## Eight strategies

| Strategy | Examples | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reducing consumption | Switching off devices; lower heating; fewer journeys | Free and immediate | Depends on people choosing to change |
| Insulation | Roof insulation, cavity walls, double glazing, draught-proofing | Saves heating or cooling energy for years | Upfront cost; harder to fit in old buildings |
| Energy-efficient devices and vehicles | LED lamps; efficient appliances; electrically propelled vehicles | Same job with less energy | Higher purchase price; EVs only as clean as the electricity |
| Exploiting existing energy resources | Improving existing power stations; recovering more from current fields | Avoids opening new sites | Often still relies on finite fossil fuels |
| Education on energy conservation | Campaigns, lessons, energy labels, smart meters | Supports all other strategies | Slow; awareness does not always change behaviour |
| Transport policies | Public transport, cycle lanes, car sharing, driving charges | Cuts fuel use and air pollution | Needs convenient alternatives; charges can be unpopular |
| Battery storage | Home and grid batteries storing solar and wind power | Matches renewable supply to demand | Expensive; batteries use finite materials |
| Development of new energy resources | Hydrogen fuels, heat pumps | Cleaner or more secure supply | New infrastructure and high costs |

**Use less**: reducing consumption, insulation, efficient devices, education, transport policies.
**Supply or store differently**: exploiting existing resources, battery storage, new resources.

## New energy resources

| Resource | Syllabus wording | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue hydrogen | Produced from natural gas | Uses existing gas; cheaper to make at present | Fossil fuel; CO₂ must be captured or it is released |
| Green hydrogen | Produced using renewable resources | Very low emissions | Needs much renewable electricity; costly |
| Ground source heat pump | Transfer of heat from the ground for heating | Steady ground temperature; more heat out than electricity in | Needs land or boreholes for pipes; high installation cost |
| Air source heat pump | Transfer of heat from the air for heating | Easier to fit than ground source | Less effective in very cold weather; needs electricity |

Hydrogen: burned or used in a fuel cell, the product is **water**. The colour describes **how it is made**.

## Calculation patterns

- **Percentage saving** = saving / original use x 100.
- **Payback time** = cost of the measure / saving per year.
- *Invented example:* double glazing costs 2400 units and saves 200 units a year → 12 years.
- *Invented example:* bill falls from 800 to 600 units → saving 200 → 25%.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Energy conservation | Reducing the amount of energy used |
| Insulation | Material that slows heat transfer into or out of a building |
| Electrically propelled vehicle | Vehicle driven by electric motors, such as an electric car or hybrid |
| Transport policy | Government measure to change how people travel |
| Battery storage | Storing surplus electricity for later use |
| Blue hydrogen | Hydrogen fuel produced from natural gas |
| Green hydrogen | Hydrogen fuel produced using renewable resources |
| Heat pump | Device using electricity to move heat from the ground or air into a building |
| Payback time | Time for savings to equal the cost of a measure |

## Exam traps

- Battery storage stores energy; it does not generate it.
- Blue = natural gas; green = renewable resources. Do not swap them.
- Hydrogen produces water when used; any carbon dioxide comes from making blue hydrogen.
- Heat pumps are not geothermal power stations and do not need volcanic areas.
- "Electric cars are zero-carbon" is incomplete: the electricity may come from fossil fuels.
- In "discuss", give both sides and a judgement for the situation given.
- Percentages use the original value as the base.

## Self-test

1. Name **three** forms of insulation for a house.
2. How does an LED lamp help manage energy resources?
3. What is an electrically propelled vehicle?
4. Why might an electric car still be responsible for carbon dioxide emissions?
5. Give **two** examples of transport policies.
6. Why is battery storage important for solar power?
7. From what is blue hydrogen produced?
8. What is produced when hydrogen is used as a fuel?
9. Where does a ground source heat pump get its heat?
10. Give **one** limitation of an air source heat pump.
11. Insulation costs 1500 units and saves 250 units a year. Calculate the payback time.
12. Give **one** reason why education is needed alongside other strategies.

**Answers:** 1. Any three: roof (loft) insulation, cavity wall insulation, double glazing,
draught-proofing. 2. It gives the same light using less electricity. 3. A vehicle driven by electric
motors, such as an electric car, bus or hybrid. 4. The electricity used to charge it may be generated by
burning fossil fuels. 5. Any two: better public transport, cycle lanes, car-sharing lanes, park-and-ride,
charges for driving into city centres. 6. Solar panels generate most at midday and none at night, so
batteries store surplus electricity for use later. 7. Natural gas. 8. Water. 9. From the ground, through
fluid in buried pipes. 10. Less effective in very cold weather, or high installation cost, or needs
electricity. 11. 1500 / 250 = 6 years. 12. People need to know how and why to save energy before they use
the other strategies, such as choosing efficient devices or public transport.
