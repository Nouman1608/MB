## Key facts about the UK framework

- The **Bank of England** has had operational independence over interest rates since **1997**.
- Rates are set by the nine-member **Monetary Policy Committee**.
- The target is **2% CPI inflation**, set by the government; it is symmetric.
- If inflation is more than **1 percentage point** from target, the Governor writes an **open letter** to the Chancellor.
- **QE** was first used by the Bank of England in **March 2009**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monetary policy | Use of interest rates, the money supply and related tools to influence AD |
| Bank Rate | The Bank of England's policy rate, which steers other interest rates |
| Transmission mechanism | The channels through which a rate change affects spending and inflation |
| Money supply | The total stock of money, mostly bank deposits |
| Demand for money | The desire to hold wealth as money; falls as interest rates rise |
| Inflation target | A numerical goal for inflation that guides policy |
| Quantitative easing | Creating central bank money to buy financial assets |
| Quantitative tightening | Reducing the stock of assets bought through QE |
| Bond yield | The return on a bond; falls when its price rises |
| Appreciation | A rise in the value of a currency |
| Hot money | Short-term financial flows chasing higher interest rates |

## The transmission mechanism: a rate rise

| Channel | Effect | AD component |
|---|---|---|
| Borrowing costs up | Mortgages and loans dearer | C falls, I falls |
| Saving rewarded | Households save more | C falls |
| Asset prices down | Lower wealth and confidence | C falls |
| Expected returns versus cost | Fewer profitable projects | I falls |
| Currency appreciates | Exports dearer, imports cheaper | X - M falls |

Result: **AD shifts left**, lowering inflation and output relative to what they would have been. A cut reverses every arrow.

### Worked reminder: mortgage

Interest-only mortgage of 150,000. Rate falls from 6% to 4.5%: annual interest falls from 9,000 to 6,750, saving **2,250** a year (187.50 a month), raising disposable income.

### Worked reminder: investment

A project returns 8%. Borrowing at 6.5%: worth doing. Borrowing at 8.5%: not worth doing. A 2-point rise in rates switches the decision.

### Worked reminder: exchange rate

1 euro = 0.85 pounds becomes 1 euro = 0.80 pounds (the pound appreciates). A 400-euro European import falls from 340 to **320** pounds; a 170-pound UK export rises from 200 to **212.50** euros.

## Money market diagram

- Vertical axis: interest rate. Horizontal axis: quantity of money.
- Money demand slopes down; money supply is vertical.
- Money supply shifts right: interest rate falls; AD rises.

## QE in four steps

1. Central bank creates reserves and buys government bonds.
2. Bond prices rise, yields fall: **long-term interest rates fall**.
3. Sellers buy other assets: **asset prices and wealth rise**.
4. Cheaper borrowing and higher wealth raise **C and I**: AD shifts right.

## Evaluation points

| For effectiveness | Against effectiveness |
|---|---|
| Quick to change, no legislation needed | Long and variable time lags |
| Independent, credible, anchors expectations | Weak when confidence is very low |
| Well suited to demand-pull inflation | Poor fit for cost-push inflation |
| QE adds a tool at the lower limit | Rates near zero cannot fall much further |
| Exchange rate channel lowers import prices | One rate for the whole country |
| | Side effects: asset prices, savers, exporters |

## Exam traps

- The government sets the **target**; the Bank sets the **rate**.
- QE creates central bank reserves; it is not printing banknotes.
- A rise in rates reduces inflation with a **lag**.
- Rate rises against cost-push inflation reduce output and jobs without removing the cause.
- An appreciation reduces AD **and** lowers import prices: two routes to lower inflation.
- Fixed-rate mortgages delay the effect of rate changes.

## Self-test

1. Who sets Bank Rate in the UK, and who sets the inflation target?
2. State the UK inflation target.
3. What happens if inflation is more than one percentage point from target?
4. Explain how a cut in Bank Rate affects investment.
5. Give two channels through which a rate rise reduces consumption.
6. On a money market diagram, what happens to the interest rate when the money supply increases?
7. Explain how QE lowers long-term interest rates.
8. Why did the Bank of England introduce QE in 2009?
9. The pound appreciates. State two effects on the UK economy.
10. Why is monetary policy less effective against cost-push inflation?
11. A 250,000 interest-only mortgage rate rises from 3% to 5%. Calculate the rise in annual interest.
12. Give one side effect of a long period of very low interest rates.

### Answers

1. The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee sets Bank Rate; the government sets the target.
2. **2% CPI** inflation.
3. The Governor writes an open letter to the Chancellor explaining why and what will be done.
4. Borrowing is cheaper, so more projects have expected returns above their cost; investment rises.
5. Any two: dearer borrowing; higher reward for saving; lower asset prices and wealth; higher mortgage payments.
6. The interest rate falls.
7. Buying bonds raises their prices, which lowers their yields.
8. Bank Rate was already close to zero and could not usefully be cut further.
9. Any two: exports dearer; imports cheaper; net exports fall; inflation falls; current account may worsen.
10. Raising rates cuts AD but does not lower the costs causing inflation, so output and jobs fall.
11. 5% x 250,000 - 3% x 250,000 = 12,500 - 7,500 = **5,000** a year.
12. Any one: higher asset prices that widen wealth gaps; low returns for savers; encouragement of excessive borrowing.
