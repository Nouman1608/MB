These notes condense section 11.4, Alkanes, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All four outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [alkanes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alkanes/); then try the [alkanes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alkanes-practice/).

## The four outcomes in one line each

1. Bonding in alkanes is single covalent; alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons.
2. Alkanes are generally unreactive, except for combustion and substitution by chlorine.
3. Substitution: one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms.
4. Chlorine substitution is photochemical: ultraviolet light provides the activation energy, Ea. Draw monosubstituted products.

## The first four alkanes

| Name | Molecular formula | Structural formula | Physical state at room temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methane | CH₄ | CH₄ | gas |
| Ethane | C₂H₆ | CH₃CH₃ | gas |
| Propane | C₃H₈ | CH₃CH₂CH₃ | gas |
| Butane | C₄H₁₀ | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ | gas |

General formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Each carbon forms four single covalent bonds.

## Reactivity summary

| Reagent | Does an alkane react? |
|---|---|
| Oxygen (burning) | yes: combustion |
| Chlorine in UV light | yes: substitution |
| Chlorine in the dark | no |
| Aqueous bromine (normal conditions) | no: stays orange |
| Acids and alkalis | no |

## Combustion equations

| Alkane | Complete combustion |
|---|---|
| CH₄ | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| C₂H₆ | 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O |
| C₃H₈ | C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| C₄H₁₀ | 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O |

Balancing order: **C, then H, then O**; double everything if O₂ ends up as a half.

Incomplete combustion (too little oxygen) gives carbon monoxide and/or carbon particulates.

### Balancing check: ethane

1. 2 C atoms → 2CO₂.
2. 6 H atoms → 3H₂O.
3. O atoms on the right: 4 + 3 = 7, so 3½O₂.
4. Double: 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O.
5. Count: 4 C, 12 H and 14 O on each side.

### Gas volumes from the equation

At the same temperature and pressure, gas volumes react in the same ratio as the moles in the equation. For methane, 1 volume reacts with 2 volumes of oxygen to give 1 volume of carbon dioxide; 10 cm³ of methane therefore needs 20 cm³ of oxygen and gives 10 cm³ of carbon dioxide. Water is a liquid at room temperature and is left out of the volume count.

## Substitution with chlorine

```
alkane + chlorine --UV light--> chloroalkane + hydrogen chloride
CH4 + Cl2 --UV--> CH3Cl + HCl
```

- One H replaced by one Cl (monosubstitution).
- The other product is always HCl.
- UV light provides the activation energy; no reaction in the dark.

One-sentence explanation for the condition: "Ultraviolet light provides the activation energy, the minimum energy the colliding particles need to react, so without it no reaction occurs."

## Monosubstituted products

| Alkane | Product(s) | Structural formula(e) |
|---|---|---|
| Methane | chloromethane | CH₃Cl |
| Ethane | chloroethane | CH₃CH₂Cl |
| Propane | 1-chloropropane; 2-chloropropane | CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl; CH₃CHClCH₃ |
| Butane | 1-chlorobutane; 2-chlorobutane | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl; CH₃CH₂CHClCH₃ |

Propane and butane give two products because H atoms on end carbons and inner carbons are in different positions. The two products are structural isomers.

## Substitution or addition?

| Clue in the equation | Reaction type |
|---|---|
| two products, one of them HCl | substitution |
| one product only | addition |
| reactant is an alkane | substitution (or combustion) |
| reactant has C=C | addition |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alkane | saturated hydrocarbon with general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ |
| Saturated | all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds |
| Combustion | reaction of a fuel with oxygen, releasing energy |
| Substitution reaction | one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms |
| Photochemical reaction | reaction that needs light |
| Activation energy, Ea | minimum energy colliding particles must have to react |
| Monosubstitution | replacement of only one hydrogen atom |

## Exam traps

- The second product of substitution is **HCl**, not H₂.
- Condition: **ultraviolet light**, which provides the activation energy. "Heat" or "a catalyst" does not score.
- Monosubstitution: draw only **one** Cl.
- Alkanes do **not** decolourise aqueous bromine.
- Check the final combustion equation has whole numbers.
- 1-chloropropane and "3-chloropropane" are the same compound; use the lower number.
- Combustion and substitution are the only alkane reactions in this section; cracking (section 11.5) is a separate process that breaks alkanes down using heat and a catalyst.
- In a substitution product, the carbon chain is unchanged: same number of carbons, one fewer H, one Cl.

## Self-test

1. State the type of bonding in alkanes.
2. Why are alkanes described as saturated?
3. Name the two types of reaction that alkanes undergo.
4. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane.
5. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethane.
6. Define a substitution reaction.
7. State the condition needed for methane to react with chlorine.
8. Explain the role of this condition.
9. Write the equation for the reaction of ethane with chlorine (monosubstitution).
10. Name the two monosubstituted products of propane with chlorine.
11. State the observation when aqueous bromine is shaken with hexane in normal laboratory conditions.
12. Name the products of incomplete combustion of methane.

## Answers

1. Single covalent bonds.
2. All their carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds.
3. Combustion and substitution (by chlorine).
4. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.
5. 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O.
6. A reaction in which one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms.
7. Ultraviolet light.
8. It provides the activation energy for the reaction.
9. C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl.
10. 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane.
11. No change: the solution stays orange.
12. Carbon monoxide and/or carbon (particulates), with water.
