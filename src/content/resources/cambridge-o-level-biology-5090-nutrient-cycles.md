The syllabus asks you to describe the carbon cycle, limited to photosynthesis, respiration, feeding, decomposition, the formation of fossil fuels and combustion; to outline the nitrogen cycle in making nitrogen available for plant and animal protein, limited to decomposition of protein to ammonium ions, nitrification, nitrogen fixation by lightning and bacteria, absorption of nitrate ions by plants, production of amino acids and protein, feeding and digestion of protein, and denitrification; and to outline the role of fungi and bacteria in decomposition. ## Why nutrients are cycled

Energy flows through an ecosystem in one direction, but the chemical elements that organisms are built from, such as carbon and nitrogen, are **recycled**. There is a fixed supply of each on Earth. Without recycling, the supply would be used up. Living things take the elements from the environment, use them to build their bodies, and return them to the environment when they die or excrete waste.

## The carbon cycle

Carbon is the key element in all organic compounds: carbohydrates, fats, proteins and DNA. The cycle moves carbon between the atmosphere (as carbon dioxide), living organisms and fossil fuels.

| Process | What happens to carbon |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Plants take carbon dioxide from the air and use it to make carbohydrates; carbon is moved from the air into producers |
| Feeding | Carbon in organic compounds passes along food chains from plants to herbivores to carnivores |
| Respiration | All living things (plants, animals, decomposers) release carbon dioxide into the air as they respire |
| Decomposition | Decomposers feed on dead organisms and waste, respire and release carbon dioxide |
| Formation of fossil fuels | Over millions of years, dead organisms buried under certain conditions form coal, oil and gas, which store carbon |
| Combustion | Burning wood or fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide into the air |

### The cycle in words

Carbon dioxide in the air is taken in by producers in photosynthesis and becomes part of carbohydrates. When animals eat the plants, the carbon passes to them. Plants, animals and decomposers release carbon dioxide in respiration. When organisms die, decomposers break them down and release more carbon dioxide. Some dead material forms fossil fuels, and when these are burned, carbon dioxide returns to the air.

### Worked example 1: tracing a carbon atom

*Trace the path of a carbon atom from a molecule of carbon dioxide in the air, through a rabbit, and back to the air.*

1. The carbon dioxide enters a leaf and is used in photosynthesis to make glucose in a grass plant.
2. A rabbit eats the grass, and the carbon becomes part of the rabbit's body (for example in proteins and fats).
3. The rabbit respires and releases carbon dioxide into the air. Alternatively, when the rabbit dies, decomposers feed on it and respire, releasing carbon dioxide.

A different route: if a fox eats the rabbit, the carbon passes to the fox and returns to the air as the fox respires.

### Balance and carbon dioxide levels

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the air and respiration, decomposition and combustion add it. When these are in balance, the concentration stays steady. Burning large amounts of fossil fuels adds carbon dioxide faster than it is removed, and cutting down forests removes plants that would take it in. These effects are covered in the effects of humans subtopic.

## The nitrogen cycle

Organisms need nitrogen to make proteins (amino acids) and DNA. Nitrogen gas makes up about 78 per cent of the air, but most organisms **cannot use nitrogen gas directly**. Plants take up nitrogen as **nitrate ions** from the soil. Animals get nitrogen by eating plants or other animals.

### The stages

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Decomposition | Decomposers break down proteins in dead plants and animals, and urea in waste, producing **ammonium ions** |
| Nitrification | Bacteria in the soil change ammonium ions into **nitrate ions** (in the presence of oxygen) |
| Nitrogen fixation | Nitrogen gas from the air is changed into compounds that plants can use, by **lightning** and by **nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (in the soil and in the roots of some plants such as legumes) |
| Absorption | Plants absorb nitrate ions through their roots |
| Protein production | Plants use nitrate ions to make amino acids and then proteins |
| Feeding and digestion | Animals eat plants, digest the protein to amino acids and build their own proteins |
| Denitrification | Bacteria in waterlogged soil change nitrate ions back into nitrogen gas, which returns to the air |

### The cycle in order

Nitrogen gas → (fixation by lightning and bacteria) → nitrate and ammonium compounds in soil → (absorption) → plant protein → (feeding) → animal protein → (death and waste) → decomposition → ammonium ions → (nitrification) → nitrate ions → plants again. Denitrification returns nitrogen to the air.

### Worked example 2: why legumes improve soil

*A farmer grows clover (a legume) in a field one year and wheat the next. The wheat grows better after the clover. Explain.*

- The roots of clover contain nitrogen-fixing bacteria, which change nitrogen gas from the air into compounds containing nitrogen.
- When the clover dies and decomposes, the nitrogen compounds are released into the soil as ammonium ions.
- Nitrifying bacteria change these into nitrate ions.
- The wheat absorbs the nitrate ions and uses them to make amino acids and proteins, so it grows better.

### Worked example 3: effect of waterlogging

*A field is flooded and the soil has no oxygen. The crop becomes yellow. Suggest why.*

Denitrifying bacteria thrive in waterlogged soil. They change nitrate ions into nitrogen gas, which escapes into the air. Fewer nitrate ions are available for the plants to absorb, so the plants cannot make enough protein, and they grow poorly, with yellow leaves. Nitrifying bacteria need oxygen, so nitrification is also reduced.

## The role of fungi and bacteria in decomposition

**Decomposition** is the breakdown of dead organisms and waste by microorganisms. The decomposers are mainly **fungi and bacteria**. They:

- release enzymes onto the dead matter, which digest it into soluble molecules (extracellular digestion);
- absorb the soluble molecules, using them for energy and growth;
- respire, so releasing **carbon dioxide** into the air;
- release the mineral ions, such as **ammonium ions**, which return to the soil.

Decomposers therefore recycle nutrients and make them available again for plants. Without them, dead material would pile up and the nutrients would stay locked in it. Decomposition is fastest when it is warm, moist and there is oxygen, because the decomposers are living organisms with enzymes that have optimum conditions.

### Worked example 4: a decay experiment

*Equal masses of leaf litter were placed in four bags, kept at different temperatures. After 4 weeks the mass lost was recorded.*

| Temperature (degrees Celsius) | Mass lost (g) |
|---|---|
| 5 | 2 |
| 15 | 7 |
| 25 | 15 |
| 60 | 1 |

Decomposition increases with temperature up to 25 degrees Celsius because the enzymes in the decomposers work faster. At 60 degrees Celsius the decomposers are killed or their enzymes are denatured. The percentage increase in mass lost from 5 to 25 degrees Celsius is (15 - 2) divided by 2 x 100 = 650 per cent.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that plants take up nitrogen gas. They take up nitrate ions.
- Mixing up nitrification (ammonium to nitrate) and denitrification (nitrate to nitrogen gas).
- Saying that decomposers "eat" dead things. They digest them with enzymes outside their bodies and absorb the products.
- Forgetting that respiration by decomposers releases carbon dioxide.
- Listing the stages of the nitrogen cycle without saying which compound is formed.
- Saying that burning fossil fuels removes carbon dioxide.
- Writing that fossil fuels form quickly. It takes millions of years.

## Command words

- **Describe**: give the stages of the carbon cycle in order.
- **Outline**: give the main stages of the nitrogen cycle.
- **Explain**: give the role of bacteria and fungi in recycling.
