## Section A

**1.** Which pair of bases is found in a DNA molecule?

A: A with C

B: A with T

C: C with T

D: G with T

**[1]**

**2.** What is a gene?

A: a chromosome in the nucleus

B: a length of DNA that codes for a protein

C: a chain of amino acids

D: a base in a nucleotide

**[1]**

**3.** State the shape of a DNA molecule. **[1]**

**4.** State what holds the two strands of DNA together. **[1]**

**5.** Name the substance that each strand of DNA is a chain of. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** One strand of a section of DNA has the base sequence G G A T C C T A.

**(a)** Write the sequence of bases on the complementary strand. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how you worked out your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** State the total number of bases in this section of DNA (both strands). **[1]**

**7.** In a sample of DNA, 34 per cent of the bases are G.

**(a)** State the percentage of C. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of A and the percentage of T. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how the DNA in a nucleus controls the activity of the cell. **[4]**

**9.** The enzyme amylase is made of a chain of 496 amino acids.

**(a)** State what determines the order of amino acids in the amylase. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why a change in the order of amino acids may stop the enzyme working. **[3]**

**10.** A student states that "a chromosome and a gene are the same thing".

**(a)** Explain why this is not correct. **[2]**

**(b)** State where chromosomes are found in a cell. **[1]**

**11.** The base sequence of part of a gene is changed so that one amino acid in a protein is different. The protein no longer works.

**(a)** Suggest why the protein no longer works. **[2]**

**(b)** State the term for a change in the base sequence of DNA. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why a muscle cell and a nerve cell from the same person contain the same DNA but have different features. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A double helix [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Bonds between pairs of bases [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Nucleotides [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** C C T A G G A T [2 marks if all eight are correct; 1 mark if at least six are correct].

**(b)** A pairs with T [1] and C pairs with G [1].

**(c)** 8 x 2 = 16 bases [1]. **[5]**

**7.**

**(a)** G pairs with C, so C is 34 per cent [1].

**(b)** G + C = 34 + 34 = 68 per cent [1]. A + T = 100 - 68 = 32 per cent [1]. A and T are equal, so each is 16 per cent [1]. **[4]**

**8.** DNA contains genes, which code for proteins [1]. The base sequence of a gene determines the sequence of amino acids in a protein [1]. Proteins include enzymes [1], which control the chemical reactions of the cell, so DNA controls the activity of the cell [1]. **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** The sequence of bases in the gene [1].

**(b)** The order of amino acids decides the shape of the enzyme [1]. A different order may give a different shape, including a different active site [1], so the substrate may no longer fit and the enzyme cannot work [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** A chromosome is a long DNA molecule that contains many genes [1], whereas a gene is a short length of DNA that codes for one protein [1].

**(b)** In the nucleus [1]. **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** A different amino acid may change the shape of the protein [1], so it cannot carry out its function (for example, an enzyme's active site no longer fits the substrate) [1].

**(b)** A mutation [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Both cells came from the same zygote by mitosis, so they have the same DNA [1]. They use different genes [1], so they make different proteins and have different features [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Pairing A with C or T with G.
- Writing that DNA "makes" proteins instead of "controls their production".
- Saying a gene codes for a trait without mentioning protein.
- Mixing up bases and amino acids.
- Forgetting to show the working in a base percentage question.
