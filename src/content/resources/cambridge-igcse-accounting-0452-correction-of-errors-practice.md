These questions cover sub-topic **3.2 Corrections of errors**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Narratives are not required unless asked for. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

Revise first with the [3.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-correction-of-errors/) and the [3.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-correction-of-errors-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the purpose of a suspense account. **[2]**

**2.** Which error requires an entry in a suspense account when it is corrected?

A: a purchase invoice was not recorded

B: discount received was debited to the supplier but not entered in the discount received account

C: a sale to P Ali was posted to the account of P Alley

D: repairs to a machine were debited to the machinery account **[1]**

**3.** A trial balance shows debit total 51,240 and credit total 50,790. What is entered in the suspense account?

A: 450 debit

B: 450 credit

C: 900 debit

D: 900 credit **[1]**

**4.** Wages of 530 were debited to the rent account. Prepare the journal entry to correct the error. **[2]**

**5.** The total of the sales returns journal, 840, was not posted to the sales returns account. Customers' accounts were correct. Prepare the journal entry to correct the error. **[2]**

**6.** State the effect on the profit for the year of correcting each error.

(i) Carriage inwards of 120 was debited to carriage outwards.

(ii) Commission received of 300 was credited to the sales account. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Prepare journal entries to correct these errors in the books of Glenmore Joinery. None affects the trial balance.

(i) A credit purchase of timber for 1,160 from Ash Timber was not recorded.

(ii) A payment of 480 to Birch Supplies was debited to Beech Supplies.

(iii) A new saw costing 2,300 was debited to the purchases account.

(iv) Cash sales of 260 were debited to the sales account and credited to the cash account.

(v) A credit sale of 1,540 to Kerr Homes was entered in the sales journal as 1,450.

(vi) Drawings of 350 by bank transfer were debited to the wages account. **[6]**

**8.** Glenmore Joinery's trial balance at 31 December 2027 showed debit total 63,420 and credit total 64,105. The difference was entered in a suspense account. These errors were found:

(i) Discount received of 145 was correctly debited to the supplier's account but was not entered in the discount received account.

(ii) The sales journal was undercast by 400.

(iii) Electricity of 380 paid by bank was correctly entered in the cash book but was credited to the electricity account.

(iv) Carriage outwards of 235 paid in cash was correctly entered in the cash book but was credited to the carriage outwards account.

**(a)** Prepare journal entries to correct the errors.

**(b)** Prepare the suspense account, showing the opening difference and the corrections. **[8]**

**9.** Glenmore Joinery's draft profit for the year ended 31 December 2027 was 18,950. Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit for the year after correcting the errors in Question 8. **[5]**

**10.** For each error in Question 7, state the effect of its correction on the statement of financial position. Name the item or items affected and the amount. **[6]**

**11.** Pinecrest Stores had a draft profit for the year of 31,400. These errors were then found:

(i) A bank loan of 5,000 received was credited to the sales account.

(ii) Rent received of 750 was credited to the commission received account.

(iii) Shop fittings bought for 1,800 were debited to the repairs account. Fittings are depreciated at 10% of cost per year, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase.

(iv) Discount allowed of 90 was debited to the discount received account.

**(a)** Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit for the year.

**(b)** Explain why error (ii) has no effect on profit. **[6]**

**12.** The book-keeper at Pinecrest Stores cannot find the cause of a 270 suspense balance and suggests treating it as an expense so that the financial statements can be finished.

**(a)** Evaluate the book-keeper's suggestion.

**(b)** Explain why the suspense account should have no balance once all errors have been corrected. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** To hold the difference in the trial balance temporarily [1], so that the totals agree and the financial statements can be drafted until the errors are found and corrected [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **B** [1]. The credit total is smaller, so 450 is entered on the credit side. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Dr Wages 530 [1]; Cr Rent 530 [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Dr Sales returns 840 [1]; Cr Suspense 840 [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** (i) No effect on profit for the year (both are expenses) [1]. (ii) No effect on profit for the year (both are income) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Error (i) does change gross profit, because carriage inwards is part of cost of sales. Read whether the question asks about gross profit or profit for the year.

**7.** One mark for each complete entry [6]:

(i) Dr Purchases 1,160; Cr Ash Timber 1,160.

(ii) Dr Birch Supplies 480; Cr Beech Supplies 480.

(iii) Dr Tools and equipment (or Machinery) 2,300; Cr Purchases 2,300.

(iv) Dr Cash 520; Cr Sales 520.

(v) Dr Kerr Homes 90; Cr Sales 90.

(vi) Dr Drawings 350; Cr Wages 350.

Total: 6 marks.

**8. (a)** One mark for each entry [4]:

(i) Dr Suspense 145; Cr Discount received 145.

(ii) Dr Suspense 400; Cr Sales 400.

(iii) Dr Electricity 760; Cr Suspense 760.

(iv) Dr Carriage outwards 470; Cr Suspense 470.

**(b)**

```
Suspense account
Date      Details                      $   Date      Details                    $
2027                                       2027
31 Dec    Difference in trial        685   31 Dec    Electricity              760
            balance                        31 Dec    Carriage outwards        470
31 Dec    Discount received          145
31 Dec    Sales                      400
                                   -----                                    -----
                                   1,230                                    1,230
                                   =====                                    =====
```

Difference of 685 on the debit side [1]; discount received and sales on the debit side [1]; electricity and carriage outwards on the credit side [1]; totals of 1,230 with no balance [1]. Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Errors (iii) and (iv) put a credit where a debit belonged, so each correction is twice the original amount.

**9.**

| | $ |
|---|---|
| Draft profit for the year | 18,950 |
| Add discount received | 145 |
| Add sales | 400 |
| Less electricity | (760) |
| Less carriage outwards | (470) |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | **18,265** |

One mark for each adjustment, correctly added or subtracted [4]; corrected profit 18,265 [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**10.** One mark for each error [6]:

(i) Trade payables increase by 1,160; capital decreases by 1,160 through lower profit.

(ii) No effect: both accounts are suppliers, so total trade payables are unchanged.

(iii) Non-current assets increase by 2,300; capital increases by 2,300 through higher profit (ignoring depreciation).

(iv) Cash increases by 520; capital increases by 520.

(v) Trade receivables increase by 90; capital increases by 90.

(vi) No overall effect on capital: profit rises by 350 but drawings rise by 350.

Total: 6 marks.

**11. (a)**

| | $ |
|---|---|
| Draft profit for the year | 31,400 |
| Less loan wrongly treated as sales | (5,000) |
| Rent received and commission received | no effect |
| Add shop fittings wrongly charged to repairs | 1,800 |
| Less depreciation on shop fittings (10% x 1,800) | (180) |
| Discount allowed and discount received | no effect |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | **28,020** |

Loan 5,000 deducted [1]; fittings 1,800 added [1]; depreciation 180 deducted [1]; error (iv) shown as no effect [1]; corrected profit 28,020 [1].

**(b)** Both rent received and commission received are income accounts in the statement of profit or loss, so total income, and therefore profit, is unchanged [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In error (iv), discount received was reduced by 90 instead of discount allowed being increased by 90. Either way profit is 90 lower, so correcting it changes two accounts but not the profit.

**12. (a)** Writing off the balance would let the statements be finished and the amount is small [1]. However, the balance shows that errors remain, which might be much larger errors that partly cancel [1]; profit and the statement of financial position could be misstated [1]. The suggestion should be rejected: the errors should be found and corrected, which is also what professional competence and due care require [1].

**(b)** The suspense balance equals the net effect of the errors that made the trial balance disagree [1]; when each has been corrected through suspense, the entries cancel the opening difference, leaving no balance [1]. Total: 6 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using suspense for errors that do not affect the trial balance.
- Correcting a wrong-side entry with the single amount.
- Placing the trial balance difference on the larger side.
- Adjusting profit for corrections between two income accounts or two expense accounts.
- Forgetting the depreciation on a non-current asset that was wrongly expensed, when a policy is given.
