## Questions

**1.** Define the term "stakeholder". **[2]**

**2.** Explain one way in which the needs of two stakeholder groups might overlap when a business makes a decision. **[3]**

**3.** Stowbridge Haulage Ltd has annual revenue of £6,400,000 and an operating profit of £512,000. Its drivers' pay bill is £1,800,000 a year. The managing director agrees a 3% pay rise for drivers. Assuming nothing else changes, calculate the new operating profit margin. **[4]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why a business should consider stakeholder needs when making decisions. **[4]**

**5.** Stowbridge Haulage plans to extend its depot on the edge of a village. Each stakeholder has been scored from 1 (low) to 5 (high), where 4 or 5 counts as high.

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders | 4 | 5 |
| Drivers | 3 | 5 |
| County council planning committee | 5 | 4 |
| Village residents' group | 2 | 5 |
| Fuel supplier | 2 | 2 |

Analyse how Stowbridge should manage its relationship with (i) the planning committee and (ii) the residents' group. **[6]**

**6.** Explain the difference between communication and consultation as ways of managing stakeholder relationships. Give one example of each. **[4]**

**7.** Thornleby Care Homes Ltd runs homes for 140 residents, who each pay £1,050 a week. Its care staff pay bill is £2,700,000 a year. Staff representatives ask for a 5% rise. The owners want their return unchanged, and residents' families have said they oppose fee increases.

**(a)** Assuming 52 weeks a year, calculate the weekly increase per resident needed to pay for the rise entirely from fees. **[3]**
**(b)** Analyse why this decision creates a conflict between stakeholder needs. **[3]**

**8.** Ormsdale Garden Centres buys £150,000 of plants a month from Cleavehill Nurseries, which sells 60% of its output to Ormsdale. Two other nurseries could supply Ormsdale. Ormsdale wants to extend its payment time from 30 days to 60 days.

**(a)** Calculate the extra amount Cleavehill would have to fund as a result. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse two influences on the relationship between Ormsdale and Cleavehill. **[4]**

**9.** Vellmoor Cycles plc plans to close its UK assembly factory and move production overseas. This would save £3.1m a year at a one-off cost of £4.4m. The factory employs 210 people and is the largest employer in its town. Its "made in Britain" label is used in advertising. Institutional shareholders have pressed for higher margins. A bank has lent Vellmoor money on a five-year loan.

Evaluate the extent to which Vellmoor should give priority to the needs of its shareholders when deciding whether to close the factory. **[12]**

**10.** Dovecrest Printers Ltd has 38 employees. The directors want to move from five shorter shifts to four ten-hour shifts, which they expect will cut the £22,000 annual overtime bill by 60%. Some employees have childcare commitments. Justify whether Dovecrest should simply communicate the change or consult employees first. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An individual or group [1] with an interest in a business, or who is affected by its activities and decisions [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs both the idea of a group or person and the idea of interest or effect; a list of examples alone does not define the term.

**2.** Identify two groups and a shared need, such as shareholders and employees both wanting the business to grow [1]. Link it to a decision, such as launching a new product [1]. Explain that success raises profit for dividends and creates jobs, so both gain from one decision [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the two groups and the decision; "needs can overlap" with no example shows the idea but not its application.

**3.** Extra cost = 3% x £1,800,000 = £54,000 [1]. New operating profit = £512,000 − £54,000 = £458,000 [1]. Margin = £458,000 / £6,400,000 x 100 [1] = **7.2%** (7.16%, down from 8.0%) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula and each stage so that a slip in one step does not hide the working that followed; state the unit as a percentage.

**4.** Reason 1: stakeholders can support or block a decision [1], for example staff resistance could stall a restructuring and so affect results [1]. Reason 2: considering them protects reputation and ethical aims [1], because ignoring a community or customers can damage the brand and make future recruitment or sales harder [1]. Other valid reasons include better information from those affected. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a developed consequence; two bare labels such as "reputation" and "support" show knowledge but little reasoning.

**5.** (i) The planning committee has high power (5) and high interest (4), so it should be managed closely [1]. Stowbridge should engage early, for example by meeting committee members and offering a traffic and noise plan [1], because a refusal would block the extension [1]. (ii) The residents have low power (2) but high interest (5), so they should be kept informed and consulted [1], for example through an open evening and a clear route plan for lorries [1]. Their power could rise if they petition, use local media or lobby the committee, so their position should be reassessed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the scores to justify each position, then say what Stowbridge would actually do; naming a box without an action leaves the analysis unfinished.

**6.** Communication passes information to stakeholders [1], usually one-way, such as a newsletter or email update [1]. Consultation asks for stakeholders' views before a decision is made [1], so it is two-way, such as a staff meeting or customer survey [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show a difference in direction or purpose; two separate descriptions that sound alike will not show the contrast.

**7. (a)** Cost of the rise = 5% x £2,700,000 = £135,000 [1]. Resident-weeks = 140 x 52 = 7,280 [1]. Weekly increase = £135,000 / 7,280 = **£18.54 per resident** (about 1.8% of the £1,050 fee) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Staff want higher pay, which is a cost to the business [1]. Paying from fees raises costs for families, while paying from profit cuts the owners' return [1]. Neither can be met fully without reducing another group's outcome, though a compromise, such as a smaller rise, might partly meet each need [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), tie each group to the direction of its interest; saying "they want different things" with no link to money or care quality stays generic.

**8. (a)** Extending by 30 days means one extra month of purchases is unpaid [1], so Cleavehill funds an extra **£150,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Dependence: Cleavehill sells 60% of its output to Ormsdale, so it is in a weak position to refuse [1] and may accept longer terms despite the cash-flow pressure [1]. Alternatives: Ormsdale can switch to two other nurseries, which strengthens its position [1], but pushing too far could damage a supplier it relies on or hurt trust if Cleavehill's finances fail [1]. Other valid influences include history and trust. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the influence, then apply a figure or fact from the case to show who gains power; do not simply list influences from memory.

**9.** Shareholders' case: the saving of £3.1m a year gives a payback of 4.4 / 3.1 = 1.4 years, about 17 months [1], which is attractive for investors seeking higher margins [1]. Shareholders have high power as owners and could press for change in the board [1]. Against: the 210 employees and the town lose their largest employer [1], and the closure may reduce morale and result in a loss of skills and local goodwill [1]. The "made in Britain" label is part of the brand, so customers may react and the saving could be offset by lost sales [1]. The bank has lent for five years, so it needs confidence that the business will remain stable; a damaged brand or lost sales would put repayments at risk [1]. Consultation or phased closure could reduce the harm [1]. Judgement [up to 4]: Shareholders' needs deserve substantial weight because the decision cannot succeed without their support and the payback is short [1]. However, priority should not be absolute: if the brand relies on British production, the real cost may exceed the saving [1]. Weighing power against long-run effects, Vellmoor should give shareholders' needs strong but not exclusive priority [1], because a managed approach, such as consulting staff and testing customer reaction, protects the long-term value that shareholders also want [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation weighs groups against each other using case evidence and reaches a conclusion that depends on stated conditions, not one that repeats earlier points.

**10.** Recommendation: consult before deciding [1]. The change directly affects working life, so employees have high interest [1], and the directors need their cooperation to make it work [1]. Consultation could reveal childcare problems the directors have not considered [1], so the plan can be adjusted, for example with alternative shift options [1]. It also builds trust and reduces the chance of resignations, which would be costly in a team of only 38 [1]. Against: consultation takes time, and the expected saving is 60% x £22,000 = £13,200 a year [1], so delay costs money but the sum is small compared with the cost of losing skilled staff [1]. Communication alone would be quick but could cause resentment [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A justified choice names the option, gives at least two reasons tied to the case, and shows why the rejected option is weaker.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining "stakeholder" by listing examples only.
- Giving a number of stakeholders without saying what each wants from this particular decision.
- Calculating the cost but not interpreting it, such as stating the new margin with no comment on what it means.
- Mapping a group without stating the action that follows.
- Treating a group's power as fixed when the case hints that it could change.
- Writing about overlap or conflict in general terms with no case evidence.
- Evaluating by repeating earlier points rather than weighing groups against each other.
- Ending with a conclusion that does not say what it depends on, such as time horizon or mission.
