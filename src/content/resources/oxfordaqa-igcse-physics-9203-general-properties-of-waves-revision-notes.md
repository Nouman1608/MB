Condensed recall for **3.3.1 General properties of waves** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Explanations and worked examples are in the [General properties of waves study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-general-properties-of-waves/); exam-style questions are in the [General properties of waves practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-general-properties-of-waves-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wave | A disturbance from an oscillating source that transfers energy and information without transferring matter |
| Transverse wave | Oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer |
| Longitudinal wave | Oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Compression | Region of a longitudinal wave where particles are close together |
| Rarefaction | Region of a longitudinal wave where particles are spread apart |
| Mechanical wave | A wave that travels through a material; can be transverse or longitudinal |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from the rest position |
| Wavelength, λ | Distance between the same points on adjacent waves |
| Frequency, f | Number of waves per second, in hertz (Hz) |
| Period, T | Time for one complete wave; T = 1 ÷ f |
| Wavefront | A line joining points at the same stage of oscillation, such as crests |
| Refraction | Change in direction caused by a change in wave velocity |
| Diffraction | Spreading of a wave through a gap or around an edge |

## Equations

```
v = f x λ     wave speed (m/s) = frequency (Hz) x wavelength (m)
T = 1 / f     period (s) = 1 / frequency (Hz)
```

## Wave types

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Transverse | All electromagnetic waves (light, radio, X-rays…), water waves, waves on a rope |
| Longitudinal | Sound waves |
| Either (mechanical) | Waves on a slinky, depending on how it is moved |

## At a boundary (e)

- Reflected: bounces back.
- Transmitted: passes through (may refract).
- Absorbed: energy transferred to the material.
- Usually a combination.

## Refraction (f)

- Cause: change in wave velocity at a boundary (or change of water depth).
- Frequency unchanged; wavelength changes in the same proportion as speed.
- Slower → shorter wavelength.

## Diffraction (f)

- Appreciable when wavelength is comparable to the gap or obstacle size.
- Gap much wider than wavelength → little spreading.

| Application | Wavelength vs size | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Long-wave radio and hills | Wavelength comparable to hills | Diffracts around hills; reception in valleys |
| FM and TV signals | Wavelength much smaller than hills | Little diffraction; need a direct path |
| Telescope or camera aperture | Aperture much larger than light's wavelength | Less diffraction, sharper image |
| Medical ultrasound | Very short wavelength | Narrow beam, fine detail |

## Evidence that waves carry energy, not matter

- A floating object on water waves bobs up and down but does not travel with the waves.
- A ribbon on a slinky oscillates about one position while the wave passes along the spring.
- Pattern for an answer: describe what the marker does, then conclude that the medium oscillates about a fixed position while energy moves through it.

## Reading a displacement-distance graph

1. Count complete waves along the distance axis; wavelength = distance ÷ number of complete waves.
2. Amplitude = height of a crest above the rest line.
3. If the frequency is given, wave speed = frequency × wavelength.

## Reading a displacement-time graph

1. Time for one complete wave = period, T.
2. Frequency = 1 ÷ T.

## Wave speed check values

| Wave | Typical figure used in questions |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic waves in a vacuum | 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s |
| Sound in air | about 340 m/s |

Use the value the question gives; these are only for checking that an answer is sensible.

## Exam traps

- **Amplitude** is rest position to crest, not crest to trough.
- **Count wavelengths**, not crests, on a graph.
- **Frequency does not change** when a wave refracts.
- **Diffraction needs a comparison:** wavelength versus gap size.
- **Waves carry energy, not matter.** Name the evidence (a floating object, a ribbon on a slinky).
- **Convert prefixes** before calculating.
- **Sound is longitudinal**; light is transverse.

## Self-test

**1.** What does a wave transfer, and what does it not transfer?

**2.** Define a longitudinal wave.

**3.** Name one transverse and one longitudinal wave.

**4.** A wave has frequency 40 Hz and wavelength 0.25 m. Find its speed.

**5.** Find the period of a 40 Hz wave.

**6.** A radio wave has frequency 1.5 MHz and speed 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Find its wavelength.

**7.** A crest-to-trough height on a water wave is 12 cm. What is the amplitude?

**8.** Seven complete waves fit into 3.5 m. Find the wavelength.

**9.** Water waves move into shallower water and slow down. What happens to their frequency and wavelength?

**10.** When is diffraction through a gap most noticeable?

**11.** Why can long-wave radio be received behind hills when FM often cannot?

**12.** Describe an observation that shows water waves do not carry the water along.

## Self-test answers

**1.** Energy and information; not matter.

**2.** A wave in which the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

**3.** Transverse: light (or any electromagnetic wave, or water waves). Longitudinal: sound.

**4.** v = 40 × 0.25 = 10 m/s.

**5.** T = 1 ÷ 40 = 0.025 s.

**6.** λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.5 × 10⁶ = 200 m.

**7.** 6 cm.

**8.** 3.5 ÷ 7 = 0.50 m.

**9.** Frequency stays the same; wavelength decreases.

**10.** When the gap is about the same size as the wavelength.

**11.** Long-wave radio has a wavelength comparable to the size of hills, so it diffracts around them; FM wavelengths are much shorter and diffract very little.

**12.** A floating cork bobs up and down as waves pass but stays in roughly the same place.
