## Questions

**1.** (Higher Tier only) A coil is connected to a sensitive voltmeter. State **two** changes, to the magnet or the coil, that would increase the induced p.d. when the magnet is pushed in. **[2]**

**2.** (Higher Tier only) A bar magnet is held still inside a coil that is part of a complete circuit.

**(a)** State the reading on a sensitive ammeter in the circuit. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer to (a). **[2]**

**3.** (Higher Tier only) The south pole of a bar magnet is pushed towards end X of a coil that is part of a complete circuit. State the polarity that end X takes while the magnet approaches, and explain, in terms of energy, why it takes that polarity. **[3]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) The same magnet is pushed into a coil that is not connected to anything else. Describe what is induced, and what is not. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) A simple alternator has a coil that rotates between the poles of a magnet.

**(a)** Name the parts that connect the rotating coil to the external circuit. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the output is alternating current. **[3]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) An oscilloscope shows the output of an alternator. The time-base is 2.0 ms per division. The trace covers 10 divisions and shows exactly 4 complete cycles. The vertical setting is 4.0 V per division and the peak is 1.5 divisions above the centre line.

**(a)** Calculate the period of the output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the peak p.d. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher Tier only) A different alternator turns at 1800 revolutions per minute. One revolution gives one cycle.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the period, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of complete cycles in 2.0 s. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A dynamo produces direct current.

**(a)** Explain how the split-ring commutator makes the external current flow in one direction only. **[3]**
**(b)** The dynamo turns at 9 revolutions per second. Calculate the number of pulses of p.d. each second. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the output p.d. over one rotation. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) An alternator turns at 1200 revolutions per minute and gives a peak p.d. of 8.0 V. In this simple model the peak p.d. is proportional to the rotation speed.

**(a)** Calculate its frequency. **[2]**
**(b)** The speed is reduced to 900 revolutions per minute. Calculate the new frequency and the new peak p.d. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) A wind-up torch contains a dynamo.

**(a)** State **one** similarity between the construction of a dynamo and an alternator. **[1]**
**(b)** State **two** differences between the output of a dynamo and the output of an alternator. **[2]**
**(c)** The handle is harder to turn when the bulb is lit than when the bulb is disconnected. Explain why. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) A student drops a small magnet through a coil at different speeds and records the largest p.d. induced. The coil is connected to a sensitive voltmeter.

| Speed of magnet through coil (m/s) | Peak p.d. (mV) |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.8 |
| 1.0 | 1.6 |
| 1.5 | 2.4 |
| 2.0 | 3.2 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in the results. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the peak p.d. at 2.5 m/s. Show your working. **[2]**
**(c)** The magnet leaves the bottom of the coil. Explain why the p.d. is reversed compared with when it entered. **[2]**
**(d)** The coil is now joined into a complete circuit and the magnet falls through it more slowly than before. Explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Move the magnet faster [1]. Use a stronger magnet, or more turns on the coil [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name changes that make the field change faster or act on more wire. Vague answers such as "make it stronger" do not say what is made stronger.

**2. (a)** Zero [1].
**(b)** The magnetic field through the coil is not changing [1]. A p.d. is induced only when the field changes, so no current is driven [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the field is "not strong enough" misses the point. State that the field is steady, not changing.

**3.** End X becomes a south pole [1]. The field through the coil is increasing, and the induced current gives a field that opposes this, repelling the approaching south pole [1]. Work has to be done to push the magnet against this force, and that work is the energy transferred to the circuit; if the force helped, energy would appear from nothing [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A pole alone gains little. Link it to opposing the change and to the work done on the magnet.

**4.** A p.d. is induced across the ends of the coil [1]. There is no current, because the circuit is not complete [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep p.d. and current separate. Writing "a current is induced" for an open circuit is a typical slip.

**5. (a)** Slip rings (and brushes) [1].
**(b)** The coil's sides cut through the magnetic field lines as it turns [1]. In each half turn a given side moves through the field in the opposite direction, so the induced p.d. reverses [1]. Slip rings keep each brush connected to the same end of the coil, so the external current also reverses every half turn [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the direction of the sides' movement through the field. "It spins" on its own does not explain the reversal.

**6. (a)** Total time = 10 × 2.0 = 20 ms [1]. Period = 20 ÷ 4 = **5.0 ms** (0.0050 s) [1].
**(b)** Frequency = 1 ÷ 0.0050 = **200 Hz** [1].
**(c)** Peak = 1.5 × 4.0 = **6.0 V** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert milliseconds to seconds before using frequency = 1 ÷ period, and show the unit with every answer.

**7. (a)** 1800 ÷ 60 = 30 revolutions per second [1], so the frequency is **30 Hz** [1].
**(b)** Period = 1 ÷ 30 = **0.033 s** [1].
**(c)** 30 × 2.0 = **60 cycles** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to give 1800 Hz because the revolutions per minute value is copied across. Convert to per second first.

**8. (a)** The p.d. induced in the coil reverses every half turn [1]. The commutator halves swap which brush they touch at that moment [1]. This cancels the reversal, so the external current always flows the same way [1].
**(b)** 2 × 9 = **18 pulses per second** [1].
**(c)** The p.d. rises from zero to a peak and falls back to zero, twice per rotation, always in the same direction [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the connections are swapped. "The commutator changes the current" is too vague to explain the mechanism.

**9. (a)** 1200 ÷ 60 = 20 revolutions per second [1], so the frequency is **20 Hz** [1].
**(b)** 900 ÷ 60 = **15 Hz** [1]. Peak p.d. = 8.0 × (900 ÷ 1200) = **6.0 V** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* When the question says to assume proportionality, show the ratio you used. A bare answer gives the marker nothing to follow.

**10. (a)** Both have a coil rotating in a magnetic field (or both transfer kinetic energy to electrical energy) [1].
**(b)** The dynamo gives d.c. and the alternator gives a.c. [1]. The dynamo output pulses in one direction; the alternator output reverses direction each half turn [1].
**(c)** When the bulb is lit a current flows in the coil [1]. The current's magnetic field opposes the change that induces it, so there is a force opposing the rotation [1]. More work must be done on the handle, and that work is transferred to light and heat [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the extra effort to the opposing effect of the induced current. "Because the bulb needs energy" does not explain why the handle itself resists.

**11. (a)** The peak p.d. increases as the speed increases [1]. It is directly proportional, with 1.6 mV for each m/s [1].
**(b)** 3.2 ÷ 2.0 = 1.6 mV per m/s [1]; 2.5 × 1.6 = **4.0 mV** [1].
**(c)** The magnet is now leaving, so the field through the coil is decreasing rather than increasing [1]. The induced p.d. reverses direction [1].
**(d)** A current now flows [1]. Its magnetic field opposes the magnet's motion, giving an upward force that slows the fall [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use numbers from the table when you describe a pattern. Saying "it goes up" is weaker than showing a constant ratio.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "current" where "potential difference" is needed, especially for open circuits.
- Saying a strong magnet at rest induces a p.d., instead of stating that the field must change.
- Naming the induced pole without saying that it opposes the change.
- Explaining the alternator output with "the coil spins" and no mention of field lines being cut in opposite directions.
- Writing that the commutator "converts" a.c. to d.c. without saying it swaps connections every half turn.
- Forgetting to divide by 60 for revolutions per minute.
- Leaving milliseconds in the calculation when finding frequency.
- Missing the units, or giving Hz for a period.
