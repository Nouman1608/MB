Condensed notes on sub-topic **3(b) Properties of waves** (statements 3.2
to 3.9) of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1), Issue 4. For
full explanations and worked examples, use the
[Properties of waves study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-properties-of-waves/).

## Equations

```
v = f × λ        (know and use)   v in m/s, f in Hz, λ in m
f = 1 / T        (use)            T in s
```

Rearranged: λ = v ÷ f; f = v ÷ λ; T = 1 ÷ f.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| transverse wave | oscillations perpendicular to the direction of wave travel |
| longitudinal wave | oscillations parallel to the direction of wave travel |
| amplitude | maximum displacement from the rest position |
| wavelength | distance between the same points on two adjacent waves |
| frequency | number of waves passing a point per second (Hz) |
| period | time for one complete wave (s) |
| wavefront | line joining points at the same stage of the cycle (e.g. a crest) |
| compression | region where particles in a longitudinal wave are close together |
| rarefaction | region where particles are spread apart |
| reflection | wave bouncing back from a boundary |
| refraction | change in speed (and often direction) on entering a new medium |
| Doppler effect | change in observed frequency and wavelength when the source moves relative to the observer |

## Wave types at a glance

- **Transverse:** light, all EM waves, water waves, rope waves; crests and
  troughs.
- **Longitudinal:** sound, push-pull slinky waves; compressions and
  rarefactions.
- Both transfer **energy and information**, not matter: particles
  oscillate about fixed positions.

## Typical speeds for context

| Wave | Speed |
|---|---|
| sound in air | about 340 m/s |
| EM waves in free space | 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s |
| ripple tank water waves | a few cm/s |

## Refraction checklist

- Speed changes at the boundary.
- Wavelength changes in the same proportion as speed.
- Frequency stays **the same**.
- Direction changes if the wave meets the boundary at an angle (not along
  the normal).
- Water waves: slower in shallow water, so wavelength shorter.

## Doppler effect sequence

Source moves towards observer → each wave emitted closer → wavefronts
bunched → shorter observed λ → higher observed f (higher pitch for sound).
Source moves away → wavefronts spread → longer λ → lower f. The source's
own frequency does not change.

## Measuring a wave

- Wavelength: measure across several waves and divide, for accuracy.
- Frequency: count waves passing a point in a measured time, f = number ÷
  time.
- Then v = f × λ.

## Two wave graphs

- **Displacement−distance graph:** a snapshot; crest-to-crest distance
  along the axis is the wavelength.
- **Displacement−time graph:** one point's motion; crest-to-crest time is
  the period, so f = 1 ÷ T.
- On both, amplitude = rest line to crest.

## Model sentences

- Transverse: "The particles oscillate at right angles to the direction
  in which the wave transfers energy."
- Longitudinal: "The particles oscillate along the direction in which the
  wave transfers energy, forming compressions and rarefactions."
- Refraction: "The wave changes speed as it enters the new medium; its
  frequency stays the same, so its wavelength changes, and if it arrives
  at an angle its direction changes."
- Energy, not matter: "Each particle oscillates about a fixed position;
  only energy and information travel with the wave."

## Exam traps

- Amplitude is rest position to crest, not trough to crest.
- Wavelength is crest to crest along the wave, not up and down.
- Sound is longitudinal, light is transverse.
- In refraction, frequency is constant.
- Convert cm, kHz and ms before substituting.
- "The siren's frequency changes": no, the **observed** frequency changes.
- "Particles move along with the wave": no, they oscillate in place.

## Self-test

1. Explain the difference between a transverse and a longitudinal wave.
2. Give one example of each type of wave.
3. Define the wavelength of a wave.
4. A wave's crest is 3.0 cm above the rest position and its trough 3.0 cm
   below. State the amplitude.
5. What evidence shows that a water wave does not transfer water across a
   pond?
6. A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 6.8 m. Calculate its speed.
7. A wave has a period of 0.040 s. Calculate its frequency.
8. Sound of frequency 680 Hz travels at 340 m/s. Calculate its
   wavelength.
9. Water waves pass from deep to shallow water. State what happens to
   their speed, wavelength and frequency.
10. Explain why a passing ambulance siren sounds lower in pitch as it
    moves away.
11. Name the parts of a longitudinal wave where particles are close
    together and far apart.
12. A dipper makes 20 waves in 5.0 s; the waves have a wavelength of
    2.0 cm. Calculate their speed in m/s.

## Answers

1. In a transverse wave the oscillations are perpendicular to the
   direction of travel; in a longitudinal wave they are parallel to it.
2. Transverse: light (or any EM wave, water wave). Longitudinal: sound.
3. The distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next
   wave, for example crest to crest.
4. 3.0 cm.
5. A floating object bobs up and down but stays in the same place as the
   waves pass.
6. v = 50 × 6.8 = 340 m/s.
7. f = 1 ÷ 0.040 = 25 Hz.
8. λ = 340 ÷ 680 = 0.50 m.
9. Speed decreases; wavelength decreases; frequency stays the same.
10. As it moves away, each wave is emitted further from you, so the
    wavefronts are spread out; the observed wavelength is longer and the
    observed frequency (pitch) is lower.
11. Compressions (close together) and rarefactions (far apart).
12. f = 20 ÷ 5.0 = 4.0 Hz; v = 4.0 × 0.020 = 0.080 m/s.
