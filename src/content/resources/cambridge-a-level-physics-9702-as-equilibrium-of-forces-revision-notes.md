Condensed for the final weeks. For the full explanation and worked examples, use the [Equilibrium of forces study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-equilibrium-of-forces/); for exam-style questions, the [Equilibrium of forces practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-equilibrium-of-forces-practice/). These notes cover subtopic **4.2 Equilibrium of forces** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## Learning outcomes

1. State and apply the **principle of moments**.
2. Equilibrium = **no resultant force and no resultant torque**.
3. Use a **vector triangle** for coplanar forces in equilibrium.

## Statements to learn

> **Principle of moments:** for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about that same point.

> **Equilibrium:** there is no resultant force and no resultant torque acting on the system.

| Condition fails | Result |
|---|---|
| Resultant force ≠ 0 | Linear acceleration |
| Resultant torque ≠ 0 | Rotation starts or changes |

## Three equations from one diagram

1. Resolve horizontally: forces right = forces left.
2. Resolve vertically: forces up = forces down.
3. Take moments about a well-chosen point.

**Choosing the point:** take moments where an unknown force acts (a hinge, a support, the foot of a ladder) so that force has zero moment and drops out.

## Standard set-ups

| Set-up | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Beam on two supports | Moments about one support give the other support's force; then up = down |
| Beam about to tip over one support | Reaction at the other support is zero |
| Uniform beam | Weight acts at the midpoint |
| Hinged rod held by a cable | Moments about the hinge remove the hinge force |
| Ladder against a smooth wall | Wall force is horizontal; ground gives normal force and friction |
| Hanging object on two strings | Vector triangle or resolve both tensions |

**Recall examples:**

- A see-saw pivoted at its centre: a 300 N child sits 1.5 m from the pivot. A 400 N child balances at 300 × 1.5 ÷ 400 = 1.125 m on the other side.
- A uniform 5.0 m beam of weight 200 N on end supports carries 500 N at 1.5 m from the left. Moments about the left: R_right × 5.0 = 200 × 2.5 + 500 × 1.5, so R_right = 250 N and R_left = 450 N.

## Vector triangle rules

- Three forces in equilibrium, drawn tip to tail, form a **closed triangle**.
- Arrows follow each other **round** the triangle in one direction.
- Solve with Pythagoras and trigonometry (right-angled), the sine or cosine rule, or a scale drawing.
- Three non-parallel forces in equilibrium have lines of action that meet at **one point**.

**Hanging picture:** a 25 N picture hangs from a string over a nail, each half at 35° to the horizontal. Vertical components: 2T sin 35° = 25, so T = 21.8 N, almost the full weight in each half. Shallower angles give larger tensions.

## Ladder checklist (smooth wall, rough ground)

- Wall: one horizontal force R.
- Ground: normal force N (up) and friction f (towards the wall).
- N = weight; f = R.
- Moments about the foot: weight × (horizontal distance to its line of action) = R × (height of the top).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium | No resultant force and no resultant torque |
| Coplanar forces | Forces acting in the same plane |
| Smooth surface | Exerts only a force perpendicular to itself (no friction) |
| Reaction / normal force | Force from a support, perpendicular to the contact surface |
| On the point of tipping | One support's reaction has just become zero |

## Exam traps

- Giving only one condition for equilibrium.
- Leaving out "about the same point" in the principle of moments.
- Ignoring the beam's own weight.
- Using the length along a slanted ladder instead of the perpendicular distance.
- Drawing a "triangle" whose arrows meet head to head.
- Assuming the tension in each string equals half the weight when the strings are not vertical.
- Forgetting the friction force at the foot of a ladder.

## Self-test

1. State the principle of moments.
2. State the two conditions for equilibrium.
3. Why is a single condition not enough?
4. A 400 N person and a 250 N person sit on a see-saw pivoted at its centre. The 400 N person is 1.0 m from the pivot. Where must the other sit?
5. Which reaction is zero when a plank is about to tip about support B?
6. What forces does a smooth wall exert on a ladder?
7. In a vector triangle for three forces in equilibrium, how are the arrows arranged?
8. A lamp of weight 10 N is held by a horizontal force so its cord is at 30° to the vertical. Find the horizontal force.
9. In question 8, find the tension in the cord.
10. Why is it useful to take moments about a hinge?
11. A 15 N ball hangs on a string against a smooth vertical wall, with the string at 25° to the wall. Find the force the wall exerts.
12. State a property of the lines of action of three non-parallel forces in equilibrium.

### Answers

1. For a body in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about the same point.
2. No resultant force and no resultant torque.
3. A couple has zero resultant force but turns the body; a force through the centre of gravity has zero moment but accelerates it.
4. 400 × 1.0 ÷ 250 = 1.6 m on the other side.
5. The reaction at the other support, A.
6. Only a horizontal force, perpendicular to the wall (no friction).
7. Tip to tail, following each other round a closed triangle.
8. F = 10 tan 30° = 5.8 N.
9. T = 10 ÷ cos 30° = 11.5 N.
10. The unknown hinge force has zero moment about the hinge, so it does not appear in the equation.
11. R = 15 tan 25° = 7.0 N.
12. They pass through a single point.
