## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free vibration | Vibration after a system is displaced and released, with no periodic force; at the natural frequency |
| Natural frequency f₀ | Frequency at which a system vibrates freely |
| Forced vibration | Vibration caused by a periodic driving force; at the driving frequency |
| Driving frequency f | Frequency of the periodic force from the driver |
| Resonance | Large increase in amplitude when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency |
| Damping | Energy transfer to the surroundings that reduces amplitude |
| Resonance curve | Graph of amplitude against driving frequency |

## Natural frequencies to calculate

| System | f₀ |
|---|---|
| Mass-spring | (1/2π)√(k/m) |
| Simple pendulum | (1/2π)√(g/l) |
| String fixed at both ends | f₁ = (1/2l)√(T/μ), then 2f₁, 3f₁, … |

A string or air column has many natural frequencies; a single mass-spring or pendulum has one.

## How a forced system responds

| Driving frequency | Amplitude |
|---|---|
| f much less than f₀ | Small |
| f = f₀ (light damping) | Maximum: resonance |
| f much greater than f₀ | Very small |

Steady amplitude: energy supplied per cycle = energy lost to damping per cycle.

## Effect of more damping on the resonance curve

1. Lower peak amplitude.
2. Broader, less sharp peak.
3. Peak at a slightly lower frequency.

All the curves come together at very low and very high driving frequencies.

## Examples

| Type | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wanted | Pushing a swing | Pushes at the swing's natural frequency |
| Wanted | String or air column in an instrument | Resonates at frequencies set by its stationary waves |
| Unwanted | Car mirror or panel at one engine speed | Engine vibration frequency matches its natural frequency |
| Unwanted | Footbridge swaying under crowds | London's Millennium Footbridge (2000) was fitted with dampers |
| Controlled | Car suspension | Shock absorbers damp oscillations after bumps |

## Reducing unwanted resonance

- Change the natural frequency, by changing mass or stiffness, so it is far from any driving frequency.
- Increase damping, so that the peak amplitude is lower.

## Barton's pendulums in three lines

- All pendulums vibrate at the driver's frequency.
- The one with the same length as the driver has the largest amplitude: resonance.
- Adding damping reduces this amplitude and makes the pendulums' responses more alike.

## Worked reminder

A string has first harmonic 120 Hz. A vibration generator is swept from 50 Hz to 400 Hz. Large-amplitude stationary waves appear at 120 Hz, 240 Hz and 360 Hz. Between these, the string vibrates at the driving frequency with only a small amplitude.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Natural frequency and driving frequency.** The natural frequency belongs to the driven system; the driving frequency belongs to the driver. Resonance is when they match.
- **Free and forced.** A free vibration is at the natural frequency and dies away with damping; a forced vibration is at the driving frequency and is kept going by the driver.
- **Damping and resonance.** Damping does not prevent resonance; it lowers and broadens the peak.

## Sketching resonance curves

- Axes: amplitude (vertical) against driving frequency (horizontal); mark f₀ on the frequency axis.
- Light damping: tall, narrow peak at f₀.
- Heavier damping: lower, wider peak just below f₀.
- Every curve starts at the same small amplitude at low frequency and falls towards zero at high frequency.

## Exam traps

- Forced system said to vibrate at its natural frequency.
- Resonance defined without stating that the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.
- Heavier damping drawn with a taller or sharper peak.
- Heavily damped peak drawn at a higher frequency.
- "Fundamental" used instead of first harmonic.
- No energy argument for why the amplitude stops growing.

## Quick self-test

1. What is a free vibration?
2. What is a forced vibration?
3. At what frequency does a system undergoing a forced vibration vibrate?
4. Define resonance.
5. Find the natural frequency of a 0.50 kg mass on a spring of constant 80 N m⁻¹.
6. A pendulum is 1.0 m long. At what driving frequency will it resonate?
7. State three effects of increasing damping on a resonance curve.
8. Why does the amplitude at resonance stop increasing?
9. Give two ways of reducing an unwanted resonance.
10. A string's first harmonic is 150 Hz. State the next two resonant frequencies.
11. In Barton's pendulums, which light pendulum swings with the largest amplitude?
12. An engine turning at 2400 revolutions per minute vibrates once per revolution. Find the driving frequency it applies to the car body.

### Answers

1. Vibration at the natural frequency after the system is displaced and released, with no periodic force acting.
2. Vibration caused by a periodic driving force.
3. The driving frequency.
4. A large increase in amplitude that occurs when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.
5. f₀ = (1/2π)√(80/0.50) = 2.0 Hz.
6. f₀ = (1/2π)√(9.81/1.0) = 0.50 Hz.
7. Lower peak, broader peak, peak at a slightly lower frequency.
8. It grows until the energy lost to damping per cycle equals the energy supplied by the driver per cycle.
9. Change the natural frequency (mass or stiffness); add damping.
10. 300 Hz and 450 Hz.
11. The one whose length equals the driver's length, because its natural frequency equals the driving frequency.
12. 2400 / 60 = 40 Hz.
