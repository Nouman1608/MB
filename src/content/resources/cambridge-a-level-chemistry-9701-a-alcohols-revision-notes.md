**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** content:
subtopic 32.1 Alcohols: describe the reaction with acyl chlorides to form esters, using ethyl ethanoate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Acyl chloride** | compound containing –COCl, e.g. ethanoyl chloride CH₃COCl |
| **Ester** | compound containing –COO– between two carbon groups, e.g. ethyl ethanoate |
| **Nucleophile** | electron-pair donor; here the O atom of the alcohol |
| **Addition–elimination** | nucleophile adds to C=O, then Cl⁻ (and H⁺) is eliminated and C=O re-forms |
| **Steamy fumes** | white fumes of HCl formed in moist air |

## The reaction to learn

    CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl

- **Room temperature**, **no catalyst**, no heating.
- **Fast**, **exothermic**, **not reversible**.
- By-product **HCl**: steamy fumes; use a fume cupboard.
- Keep **dry**: water hydrolyses the acyl chloride to CH₃COOH + HCl.

General form: R'COCl + ROH → R'COOR + HCl

The R' group (with C=O) comes from the acyl chloride; the R group (on the single-bonded O) comes from the alcohol.
Phenol reacts in the same way to give phenyl esters.

## Mechanism outline (addition–elimination)

    1. lone pair on alcohol O → δ+ carbonyl C;  C=O π electrons → O
    2. O⁻ re-forms C=O;  C–Cl electrons → Cl⁻ leaves
    3. H⁺ lost from the alcohol O;  H⁺ + Cl⁻ → HCl

Carbonyl C is strongly δ+ because it is bonded to both O and Cl.

## Two routes compared

| | + Carboxylic acid | + Acyl chloride |
|---|---|---|
| Catalyst | conc. H₂SO₄ | none |
| Temperature | heat under reflux | room temperature |
| Reversible | yes (⇌) | no (→) |
| By-product | H₂O | HCl |
| Yield | limited by equilibrium | high |

## Naming esters

**Alcohol part first** (as alkyl), **acid part second** (as -oate).

| Alcohol + acyl chloride | Ester |
|---|---|
| methanol + ethanoyl chloride | methyl ethanoate |
| ethanol + propanoyl chloride | ethyl propanoate |
| propan-1-ol + ethanoyl chloride | propyl ethanoate |
| pentan-1-ol + ethanoyl chloride | pentyl ethanoate |

**Reverse it:** split the ester at the single-bonded O. C=O side ← acyl chloride; other side ← alcohol.

## Practical points

- Add the acyl chloride **slowly** to the alcohol: the reaction is vigorous and exothermic.
- Work in a **fume cupboard**: HCl and the acyl chloride vapour are corrosive and irritating.
- Use **dry** glassware and reagents; moisture produces the carboxylic acid instead.
- The ester can be separated from the mixture by **distillation**.

## Why the acyl chloride is so reactive

| Feature | Effect |
|---|---|
| C bonded to O and Cl | large δ+ on the carbonyl carbon attracts the alcohol O |
| C=O π bond | accepts the electron pair, so the alcohol can add |
| Cl⁻ a good leaving group | eliminated readily as C=O re-forms |
| HCl formed, not H₂O | no back reaction, so no equilibrium |

## Multi-step thinking

- A **diol** reacts at **both** –OH groups: HOCH₂CH₂OH + 2CH₃COCl → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂OOCCH₃ + 2HCl.
- Moles of acyl chloride used = number of –OH groups × moles of alcohol.
- A diol with a **dioyl chloride** links repeatedly to give a polyester (Topic 35).

## Typical calculation

    n(alcohol) = mass / Mr
    n(ester) = n(alcohol)              (1 : 1)
    mass(ester) = n × Mr(ester)
    V(HCl) at r.t.p. = n × 24.0 dm³

Useful Mr: ethanol 46.0, ethyl ethanoate 88.0, ethanoyl chloride 78.5.

## Observations used in structure questions

| Observation | Tells you |
|---|---|
| steamy fumes when ethanoyl chloride is added | –OH (or –NH₂) group present |
| product has a sweet, fruity smell | an ester formed |
| no fumes, no reaction | no –OH or N–H group |

## Exam traps

- **No catalyst and no heat** with an acyl chloride.
- **HCl**, not water, is the by-product.
- Use **→**, not ⇌.
- **Alkyl part first** in the ester name: ethyl ethanoate, not ethanoyl ethyl.
- Arrows start from the **lone pair on O**, not from the H of –OH.
- Anhydrous conditions: water destroys the acyl chloride.
- For diols, **double** the acyl chloride and the HCl.

## Self-test

1. Write the equation for ethanol reacting with ethanoyl chloride.
2. State the conditions needed.
3. What is observed when the reaction is carried out in moist air?
4. Name the ester formed from methanol and butanoyl chloride, and give its structural formula.
5. Give two advantages of this method over esterification with ethanoic acid.
6. Why must the reagents be dry?
7. Which atom of the alcohol attacks the acyl chloride, and why is it able to?
8. Name the alcohol and acyl chloride needed to make propyl propanoate.
9. Calculate the mass of ethyl ethanoate formed from 0.920 g of ethanol with excess ethanoyl chloride.
10. Write the equation for ethane-1,2-diol reacting with excess ethanoyl chloride.
11. What volume of HCl, at r.t.p., forms when 0.0250 mol of ethanol reacts completely?

**Answers:**

1. CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl.
2. Room temperature; no catalyst.
3. Steamy (white) fumes of HCl.
4. Methyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃.
5. Any two: faster; no catalyst or heating; not reversible; higher yield.
6. Water reacts with the acyl chloride (hydrolysis) to give a carboxylic acid and HCl, wasting reagent and
   lowering the ester yield.
7. The oxygen atom: it has lone pairs, so it acts as a nucleophile towards the δ+ carbonyl carbon.
8. Propan-1-ol and propanoyl chloride.
9. n = 0.920 / 46.0 = 0.0200 mol; mass = 0.0200 × 88.0 = 1.76 g.
10. HOCH₂CH₂OH + 2CH₃COCl → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂OOCCH₃ + 2HCl.
11. 0.0250 × 24.0 = 0.600 dm³.
