## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an exothermic reaction, in terms of the surroundings. **[2]**

**2.** State the sign of ΔH for an endothermic reaction. **[1]**

**3.** Define activation energy. **[2]**

**4.** Classify each reaction as exothermic or endothermic: burning ethanol; thermal decomposition of copper(II) carbonate; sodium hydroxide reacting with hydrochloric acid; photosynthesis. **[4]**

**5.** A student mixes citric acid solution with sodium hydrogencarbonate. The temperature changes from 21.5 °C to 16.0 °C. Calculate the temperature change and deduce whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**6.** State the effect of adding a catalyst on the activation energy and on the enthalpy change of a reaction. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Methane burns in oxygen in an exothermic reaction. Describe the reaction pathway diagram you would draw for this reaction, including all labels. **[4]**

**8.** A reaction pathway diagram shows the reactants at 30 kJ/mol, the top of the curve at 190 kJ/mol and the products at 120 kJ/mol.

**(a)** State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic, giving a reason.

**(b)** Calculate ΔH.

**(c)** Calculate the activation energy.

**(d)** With a catalyst, the top of the curve is at 130 kJ/mol. Calculate the new activation energy. **[5]**

**9.** Use these bond energies to calculate the enthalpy change for the combustion of methane.

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

C–H = 412 kJ/mol; O=O = 498 kJ/mol; C=O = 805 kJ/mol; O–H = 464 kJ/mol **[4]**

**10.** Use these bond energies to calculate the enthalpy change for the Haber process reaction.

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃

N≡N = 945 kJ/mol; H–H = 436 kJ/mol; N–H = 391 kJ/mol **[3]**

**11.** Explain, in terms of bond breaking and bond making, why the reaction in Question 10 is exothermic. **[2]**

**12.** Burning magnesium is strongly exothermic, but magnesium ribbon must be held in a flame before it starts to burn. Explain why. **[2]**

**13.** A student writes: "The thermometer reading went up, so the reaction took in heat and ΔH is positive." Correct the student's statement. **[2]**

**14.** 25 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid is mixed with 25 cm³ of dilute sodium hydroxide in a polystyrene cup. The temperature rises from 19.0 °C to 26.5 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature change.

**(b)** State the sign of ΔH for this reaction.

**(c)** Suggest why a polystyrene cup is used rather than a glass beaker. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Thermal energy is transferred from the reaction to the surroundings [1], so the temperature of the surroundings increases [1].

**2.** Positive [1].

**3.** The minimum energy [1] that colliding particles must have to react [1].

**4.** Burning ethanol: exothermic [1]. Thermal decomposition of copper(II) carbonate: endothermic [1]. Sodium hydroxide with hydrochloric acid: exothermic [1]. Photosynthesis: endothermic [1].

**5.** Change = 16.0 − 21.5 = −5.5 °C, a fall of 5.5 °C [1]. The temperature of the surroundings falls, so the reaction is endothermic [1].

**6.** Activation energy decreases [1]; enthalpy change is unchanged [1].

**7.** Axes labelled energy (vertical) and progress of reaction (horizontal) [1]. Reactants (CH₄ + 2O₂) line higher than products (CO₂ + 2H₂O) line [1]. Activation energy arrow from the reactant line up to the top of the curve [1]. ΔH arrow from the reactant line down to the product line, labelled as negative [1].

**8. (a)** Endothermic [1], because the products are at a higher energy than the reactants [1].

**(b)** ΔH = 120 − 30 = **+90 kJ/mol** [1].

**(c)** Ea = 190 − 30 = **160 kJ/mol** [1].

**(d)** Ea = 130 − 30 = **100 kJ/mol** [1].

**9.** Bonds broken: (4 × 412) + (2 × 498) = 1648 + 996 = 2644 kJ [1]. Bonds made: (2 × 805) + (4 × 464) = 1610 + 1856 = 3466 kJ [1]. ΔH = 2644 − 3466 [1] = **−822 kJ/mol** [1].

**10.** Bonds broken: 945 + (3 × 436) = 2253 kJ [1]. Bonds made: 6 × 391 = 2346 kJ [1]. ΔH = 2253 − 2346 = **−93 kJ/mol** [1].

**11.** Breaking bonds in N₂ and H₂ takes in 2253 kJ, but making the N–H bonds releases 2346 kJ [1]. More energy is released by bond making than is taken in by bond breaking, so overall energy is given out [1].

**12.** Magnesium and oxygen particles must collide with at least the activation energy to react; at room temperature too few do, so the flame supplies this energy [1]. Once some magnesium reacts, the energy released supplies the activation energy for the rest, so the reaction continues [1].

**13.** A rising thermometer reading means thermal energy was given out to the surroundings, so the reaction is exothermic [1], and ΔH is negative [1].

**14. (a)** 26.5 − 19.0 = **+7.5 °C** (a rise of 7.5 °C) [1].

**(b)** Negative [1].

**(c)** Polystyrene is a poor conductor of heat (an insulator), so less thermal energy is lost to the surroundings and the temperature rise measured is more accurate [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: writing "it gives out heat" without mentioning the temperature of the surroundings.
- Question 3: missing "minimum" or "colliding". Both words carry the meaning.
- Question 7: drawing the activation energy arrow from the products, or drawing the ΔH arrow from the top of the curve.
- Question 8(b): giving 90 kJ/mol without the + sign.
- Question 9: counting only one C=O bond in CO₂, or two O–H bonds in total instead of four.
- Question 10: forgetting that two NH₃ molecules contain six N–H bonds.
- Question 12: claiming the reaction is endothermic because it needs heating to start.
- Question 13: correcting only half of the statement. Both "took in heat" and "positive" are wrong.
