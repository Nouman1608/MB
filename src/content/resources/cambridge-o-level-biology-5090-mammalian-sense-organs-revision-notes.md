These notes condense subtopic **14.2 Mammalian sense organs** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: sense organs and their stimuli, the parts of the eye and what each does, the pupil reflex and accommodation. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [14.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-sense-organs/). Then try the [14.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-mammalian-sense-organs-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Sense organ | A group of receptor cells responding to a specific stimulus |
| Stimuli | Light, sound, touch, temperature, chemicals |
| Receptor | Detects a stimulus and starts an impulse in a sensory neurone |
| Refraction | Bending of light as it passes into a transparent material |

## Parts of the eye

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Refracts light |
| Iris | Controls how much light enters the pupil |
| Pupil | Hole through which light enters |
| Lens | Focuses light onto the retina |
| Ciliary muscles | Control the shape of the lens |
| Suspensory ligaments | Control the shape of the lens (with the ciliary muscles) |
| Retina | Contains light receptors, some sensitive to light of different colours |
| Fovea | Greatest density of light receptors |
| Optic nerve | Carries impulses to the brain |
| Blind spot | Where the optic nerve leaves; no receptors |

**Path of light:** cornea, pupil, lens, retina. Impulses go along the optic nerve to the brain.

## Pupil reflex

| Light | Circular muscles | Radial muscles | Pupil | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bright | Contract | Relax | Smaller | Less light enters; retina protected |
| Dim | Relax | Contract | Larger | More light enters |

- The muscles act **antagonistically**: when one set contracts, the other relaxes.
- The response is a reflex: rapid and automatic.

## Accommodation

| | Near object | Distant object |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliary muscles | Contract | Relax |
| Suspensory ligaments | Slack (less tension) | Taut (more tension) |
| Lens shape | Thicker, more curved | Thinner, flatter |
| Refraction by lens | More | Less |

## Worked calculation

*The pupil diameter falls from 6 mm to 3 mm when a light is switched on. Find the percentage decrease.*

1. Decrease = 6 - 3 = 3 mm.
2. Percentage = 3 divided by 6 x 100 = 50 per cent.

Divide by the **starting** value. In this case the starting value is 6 mm.

## Memory aids

- **Near: muscles contract, ligaments loose, lens fat.**
- **Far: muscles relax, ligaments tight, lens thin.**
- **Bright light: circular muscles contract.** Circular makes the pupil Closed down; radial makes it Radiate outwards.
- The iris controls the **pupil**; the ciliary muscles control the **lens**.

## Stimuli and sense organs

| Stimulus | Sense organ | Receptor cells respond to |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Eye | Light of different intensities and colours |
| Sound | Ear | Sound vibrations |
| Touch | Skin | Pressure on the skin |
| Temperature | Skin | Changes in temperature |
| Chemicals | Nose and tongue | Chemicals in air and in food |

Receptors are **specific**: each detects only one type of stimulus. They send impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS.

## Diagram tips for the eye

- Cornea: front, clear, curved.
- Iris: coloured ring behind the cornea, with the pupil as the gap in its centre.
- Lens: behind the pupil, held by suspensory ligaments.
- Ciliary muscles: ring of tissue to which the ligaments attach.
- Retina: the lining at the back; the fovea is the small central spot on it.
- Optic nerve: leaves from the back; the blind spot is where it leaves.

## Short model answers

**Explain the pupil reflex in bright light.** Receptors in the retina detect the high light intensity. The circular muscles of the iris contract and the radial muscles relax, so the pupil gets smaller and less light enters the eye.

**Explain how the eye focuses on a near object.** The ciliary muscles contract, so tension in the suspensory ligaments falls. The lens becomes thicker and more curved, so it refracts light more strongly and focuses the image on the retina.

**State the function of the fovea.** It has the greatest density of light receptors.

**State why there is no image detected at the blind spot.** There are no light receptors there, because the optic nerve leaves the eye at that point.

**State the function of the cornea.** It refracts light.

## Exam traps

- The **lens** focuses light on the retina. The iris does not.
- In bright light the **circular** muscles contract, not the radial.
- The ciliary muscles **relax** for distant objects.
- The lens changes **shape**; it does not move.
- The cornea refracts light but does not change shape to focus.
- The optic nerve, not the retina, carries impulses to the brain.
- Do not call the pupil a muscle.
- Answers on accommodation should mention **tension in the suspensory ligaments**.

### Answers

1. A group of receptor cells that respond to a specific stimulus.
2. Light, sound, touch, temperature and chemicals.
3. It refracts light.
4. How much light enters the pupil.
5. The fovea.
6. The optic nerve leaves the eye there, so there are no receptors.
7. The circular muscles.
8. They contract.
9. They contract.
10. They are pulled tight (tension increases).
11. Thicker and more curved.
12. The optic nerve.
