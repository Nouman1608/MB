All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Marlow Mowers.

> **Case study: Marlow Mowers**
>
> Marlow Mowers makes electric lawn mowers and uses 2,000 motors a
> month, or about 100 a day. At present it buys all 2,000 motors in one
> delivery a month at $6.00 each, with a delivery charge of $120. It
> holds an average of 1,000 motors in stock, and each motor costs $0.40
> a month to hold. The managing director is considering JIT with four
> deliveries a month. Smaller orders would cost $6.30 per motor, with
> a $120 delivery charge for each delivery, and the average stock would
> be 250 motors. Marlow is also choosing between two suppliers for the
> motors. Supplier X charges $6.00 per motor, is expected to deliver
> 15% of its 8 monthly deliveries late and 4% of motors are faulty.
> Supplier Y charges $6.60, is expected to deliver 2% of its 8 monthly
> deliveries late and 1% of motors are faulty. Each late delivery stops
> production and costs Marlow $1,500.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a benefit of just in time stock control? **[1]**
- A. Lower storage costs
- B. Larger discounts for bulk orders
- C. Protection from late deliveries
- D. Fewer deliveries

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a cost of holding stock? **[1]**
- A. Faster delivery to customers
- B. Money tied up in stock
- C. A bulk discount
- D. Better customer service

**3.** Define the term "buffer stock". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** factors a business should consider when choosing a supplier. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** drawback of just in time stock control. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate Marlow's total cost of buying motors each month at present, including the delivery charge. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate Marlow's total monthly cost of buying motors using JIT, including deliveries. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the monthly holding cost at present and under JIT, and the net change in Marlow's monthly costs if it adopts JIT. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the size of the buffer stock Marlow would need to avoid a stoppage if a delivery is up to 3 days late. **[2]**

**10.** Calculate the expected cost of late deliveries each month for Supplier X and Supplier Y. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse which supplier is better for Marlow Mowers. Use your answer to Question 10. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Marlow Mowers should adopt just in time stock control. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A minimum amount of stock kept as a safety margin [1] in case of late deliveries or a sudden rise in demand [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: price [1]; quality [1]; reliability [1]; delivery location or speed [1]; credit terms [1]. **[2]**

**5.** More frequent deliveries mean higher delivery costs [1], and smaller orders lose bulk-buying discounts [1]. Accept: a late delivery can stop production because there is little stock [1], so output and sales are lost [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Motors = 2,000 x $6.00 = $12,000 [1]; plus $120 delivery = **$12,120** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Motors = 2,000 x $6.30 = $12,600 [1]; deliveries = 4 x $120 = $480; total = **$13,080** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Holding cost now = 1,000 x $0.40 = $400 [1]; under JIT = 250 x $0.40 = $100 [1]; purchase and delivery cost rises by $13,080 - $12,120 = $960 [1]; net change = $960 - $300 saving = **$660 a month higher** under JIT [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Usage 100 motors a day x 3 days = **300 motors** [2]. Award 1 mark for the method shown with an arithmetic error. **[2]**

**10.** Supplier X: 15% x 8 = 1.2 late deliveries; 1.2 x $1,500 = **$1,800** [1]. Supplier Y: 2% x 8 = 0.16 late deliveries [1]; 0.16 x $1,500 = **$240** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** **Total cost:** X: motors 2,000 x $6.00 = $12,000, plus $1,800 for late deliveries = $13,800 [1]. Y: 2,000 x $6.60 = $13,200, plus $240 = $13,440 [1].
**Quality:** X gives 2,000 x 96% = 1,920 usable motors and Y gives 1,980 [1]. Cost per usable motor: X = $13,800 / 1,920 = $7.19; Y = $13,440 / 1,980 = $6.79 [1].
**Conclusion:** Y has a higher price per motor but is cheaper overall because it is more reliable and supplies fewer faulty motors [1]. Late deliveries stop production and faulty motors lead to waste or returns, so Y is likely to protect Marlow's reputation [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For JIT:** holding costs fall from $400 to $100 a month [1]; storage space is released [1]; less risk of stock becoming damaged or out of date [1].
**Against:** the purchase and delivery cost rises by $960 [1], so Marlow is $660 a month worse off on the figures [1]; with so little stock a late delivery would stop production [1], and with Supplier X expected to be late 15% of the time, that is a real risk [1].
**Judgement:** on the figures alone, Marlow should not adopt JIT, because it costs $660 a month more [1]. JIT might become worthwhile with a reliable supplier such as Y, a smaller price gap for small orders or a use for the released space; a small buffer stock of 300 motors could protect against stoppages [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the delivery charge, or counting it once when there are four deliveries under JIT.
- Calculating only the holding-cost saving and not the extra purchase cost.
- Choosing a supplier on price alone.
- Writing that JIT means "no stock" and "no costs".
- Not using figures from earlier parts when asked to evaluate.
