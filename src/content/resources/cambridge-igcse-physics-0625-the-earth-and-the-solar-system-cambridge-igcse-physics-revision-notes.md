| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Earth's rotation | about 24 hours |
| Earth's orbit of the Sun | about 365 days |
| Moon's orbit of the Earth | about one month |
| 1 day | 86 400 s |
| Speed of light (use when given) | 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s |

## 6.1.1 The Earth

- Earth **rotates** on a **tilted** axis once in about 24 hours.
- Rotation → **day and night**, and the Sun's apparent daily motion: rises in the east, highest around midday, sets in the west.
- Earth **orbits** the Sun in about 365 days; with the tilted axis, this gives the **seasons**.
- Hemisphere tilted towards the Sun: Sun higher, longer days, more energy per square metre → summer.
- The two hemispheres have opposite seasons at the same time.
- Moon **orbits** the Earth in about a month; it reflects sunlight; half is always lit.
- We see different amounts of the lit half → **phases** (new, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full).

**Extended only**

- Average orbital speed: **v = 2πr / T** (r = average orbital radius, T = period).
- Convert T to seconds: days × 86 400.

## 6.1.2 The Solar System

**Members**

- One star: the **Sun**.
- Eight planets in order: **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune**.
- **Minor planets:** dwarf planets (such as Pluto) and asteroids (asteroid belt, between Mars and Jupiter).
- **Moons** orbit planets.
- Smaller bodies: **comets** and natural satellites.

**Method in steps: the accretion model**

1. An interstellar cloud of gas and dust, containing **many elements**, collapses under **gravity**.
2. The cloud is **rotating**, so it flattens into an **accretion disc** around the forming Sun.
3. Near the Sun: hot; only rocky and metallic material can collect → **small, rocky** inner planets.
4. Far from the Sun: cold; gases are available; large cores attract much gas → **large, gaseous** outer planets.

**Gravity**

- Surface gravitational field strength depends on the planet's **mass**.
- Field strength around a planet **decreases with distance**.
- The Sun has **most of the mass** of the Solar System, so planets orbit it.
- The **gravitational attraction of the Sun** keeps objects in orbit.
- Light travel time: **t = distance ÷ speed of light**.

**Extended only**

- Planets, minor planets and comets have **elliptical** orbits; the Sun is **not at the centre** unless the orbit is nearly circular.
- The Sun's field **weakens with distance**; orbital speeds **decrease** with distance.
- In an ellipse the object is **fastest closest to the Sun**: GPE → KE moving in, KE → GPE moving out; total energy conserved.
- Data analysis: high density → rocky; larger distance → longer period, lower temperature.

## Exam traps

- Seasons are caused by the **tilt**, not distance from the Sun.
- Day and night come from **rotation**; seasons from the **orbit**.
- Phases are not caused by the Earth's shadow.
- Pluto is a **dwarf planet**.
- Convert days to seconds and million km to m before using v = 2πr / T.
- "Gravity is stronger near the Sun" alone does not explain the faster speed; name the energy transfer.

## Self-test

1. How long does the Earth take to rotate once?
2. Explain why the Sun appears to rise in the east.
3. Explain why the southern hemisphere has summer when the northern has winter.
4. Why does the Moon show phases?
5. List the planets in order from the Sun.
6. Where is the asteroid belt?
7. Name the three features of the accretion model in the syllabus.
8. Planet P has more mass than planet Q. Which has the greater surface gravitational field strength?
9. A radio signal travels 2.25 × 10¹¹ m from a probe near Mars. How long does it take, in minutes?
10. *(Extended)* A moon orbits at 6.0 × 10⁸ m with a period of 20 days. Find its average orbital speed.
11. *(Extended)* Where in its orbit is a comet slowest? Explain.
12. *(Extended)* How do the orbital speeds of planets change with distance from the Sun?

### Answers

1. About **24 hours**.
2. The Earth rotates from west to east, so the Sun **appears** to move from east to west.
3. Because of the **tilted axis**, when the north leans towards the Sun the south leans **away** from it.
4. It reflects sunlight; half is always lit, and as it orbits we see **different amounts of the lit half**.
5. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
6. Between **Mars and Jupiter**.
7. Dependence on **gravity**; **many elements** in the cloud; **rotation** forming an **accretion disc**.
8. **P** (greater mass).
9. t = 2.25 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 750 s = **12.5 minutes**.
10. T = 20 × 86 400 = 1.728 × 10⁶ s; v = 2π × 6.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.728 × 10⁶ = **2.2 × 10³ m/s**.
11. **Furthest from the Sun**: it has converted the most kinetic energy into gravitational potential energy.
12. They **decrease** as distance increases.
