## 2.1.3(a) Nucleotides: DNA against RNA

Each nucleotide is built from a five-carbon sugar, a phosphate and a base containing nitrogen. It is the monomer of nucleic acids.

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Pyrimidines | Cytosine, thymine | Cytosine, uracil |
| Purines | Adenine, guanine | Adenine, guanine |
| Strands | Two, antiparallel, twisted | Usually one |

- **Purines** (A, G) have two rings. **Pyrimidines** (C, T, U) have one ring.
- Memory aid: "CUT the pyrimidines" (cytosine, uracil, thymine); "PURe As Gold" (adenine and guanine are purines).
- Deoxyribose has a hydrogen atom, not a hydroxyl group, on carbon 2′.
- Link to practical: OCR lists a modelling activity, investigating DNA structure using RasMol. Be able to read a molecular model.

## 2.1.3(b) Phosphodiester bonds

- **Synthesis:** condensation. 5′ phosphate of one nucleotide joins the 3′ hydroxyl of the next. One water is released per bond.
- **Breakdown:** hydrolysis. One water is used per bond.
- A chain of n nucleotides has n − 1 phosphodiester bonds.
- A double-stranded fragment of n base pairs has 2(n − 1) of them.

| Step | Method |
|---|---|
| 1 | Count nucleotides in one strand: n |
| 2 | Bonds in one strand = n − 1 |
| 3 | Multiply by 2 for a double helix |
| 4 | Water released or used = number of bonds |

Reminder: a 30-nucleotide chain has 29 bonds; a 30 base-pair fragment has 58.

## 2.1.3(c) ADP and ATP

| | ADP | ATP |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | Ribose | Ribose |
| Base | Adenine | Adenine |
| Inorganic phosphates | 2 | 3 |

- Both are **phosphorylated nucleotides**.
- ATP + water → ADP + Pi releases energy (hydrolysis). ADP + Pi → ATP is phosphorylation.
- Describe ATP as a molecule that releases energy when hydrolysed, not as an energy "store" or "source of energy" without the reaction.
- Reminder calculation: a process needing 183 kJ per mole, when each mole of ATP supplies 30.5 kJ, needs at least 183 ÷ 30.5 = 6.0 mol of ATP.

## 2.1.3(d)(i) DNA structure

- Two polynucleotides; **sugar-phosphate backbone** on the outside, bases inside.
- **Complementary base pairing:** A to T with two hydrogen bonds; G to C with three.
- Each pair = one purine + one pyrimidine, so the width is constant.
- **Antiparallel:** one strand runs 5′ to 3′, the other 3′ to 5′.
- The twisting of the paired strands gives the **double helix**.
- Stability: many hydrogen bonds together hold the strands; the backbone is covalent.
- Weak individual hydrogen bonds allow the strands to separate for copying.

Method for hydrogen-bond questions: let a = A–T pairs and g = G–C pairs. Write a + g = total pairs and 2a + 3g = total hydrogen bonds. Solve together.

## 2.1.3(d)(ii) Practical: DNA purification by precipitation

| Reagent or step | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Mashing | Breaks up tissue and cell walls |
| Detergent | Dissolves phospholipids of membranes, releasing DNA |
| Salt | Masks the negative charge on phosphates so DNA clumps |
| Gentle warming | Helps detergent work; inactivates enzymes that digest DNA |
| Protease (optional) | Digests proteins bound to DNA |
| Filtering | Removes debris |
| Ice-cold ethanol layer | DNA is insoluble in ethanol and precipitates at the boundary |
| Glass rod | Spools the white strands |

Controlling variables when comparing tissues: same starting mass, same volumes, same temperature and times, same ethanol layering. Dependent variable: dried mass or length of DNA. The product is crude, with RNA and protein present.

## 2.1.3(e) Semi-conservative replication

**Semi-conservative:** each new molecule = one original strand + one new strand.

Steps:

1. Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between bases and unwinds the helix.
2. Each strand is a template.
3. Free nucleotides pair by complementary base pairing.
4. DNA polymerase forms phosphodiester bonds, adding nucleotides at the 3′ end of the new strand.
5. Two molecules, each hybrid of old and new.

- Accuracy: templates plus base pairing copy the sequence, conserving genetic information for daughter cells.
- **Spontaneous mutation:** random changes in base sequence that occur without a mutagen, for example a wrong nucleotide incorporated. They are random in place and time.
- At AS Level the specification does not require learners to distinguish between types of mutation.

Labelling reminder (¹⁵N heavy, ¹⁴N light): start with all-heavy; after one generation in light medium, all hybrid; after a second generation, half hybrid and half light. A conservative model predicts no hybrid band at generation one.

After n rounds from one molecule: 2ⁿ molecules, always only two containing an original strand.

## Worked reminders

### Yield from a purification

A sample of 15.0 g of tissue gives 4.5 mg of dried DNA.

- Yield = 4.5 ÷ 15.0 = 0.30 mg per g.
- Compare tissues by yield per gram, not by total mass, because the starting masses may differ.
- Percentage difference = (new − baseline) ÷ baseline × 100. State your baseline.

### Hybrid DNA after switching medium

All-heavy bacteria (HH) are grown for one generation in light medium, then returned to heavy medium for one generation.

- After light medium: all HL.
- In heavy medium each HL splits into one HH and one HL.
- Result: half HH, half HL, no LL.

### Expected mutations

Data supplied: 2.0 × 10⁶ base pairs and 1 × 10⁻⁹ errors per base pair per replication.

- Errors per replication = 2.0 × 10⁶ × 1 × 10⁻⁹ = 2.0 × 10⁻³.
- Across 10⁶ replications = 2.0 × 10³ errors.
- A rate that is tiny per replication still gives many errors in a large population.

## Exam wording to use

| If the question says | Write |
|---|---|
| Describe the structure of DNA | Two antiparallel polynucleotides, sugar-phosphate backbone, complementary base pairs joined by hydrogen bonds, twisted into a double helix |
| Explain why replication is semi-conservative | Each new molecule contains one original template strand and one new strand |
| Explain the role of DNA polymerase | Joins free nucleotides by forming phosphodiester bonds on the new strand, using the template and complementary base pairing |
| Explain the role of helicase | Breaks hydrogen bonds between complementary bases so the strands separate and act as templates |
| Describe a spontaneous mutation | A random change in the base sequence that arises during replication without a mutagen |

## Linking ideas across the unit

- ATP, ADP and the RNA adenine nucleotide share the same sugar and base, so you can compare them in one sentence.
- Phosphodiester bonds appear twice: in DNA synthesis by DNA polymerase and in the backbone of every polynucleotide.
- Hydrogen bonds appear twice: between complementary bases in the helix and, in replication, as the bonds helicase breaks.
- Precipitation in the practical works because of the same phosphate groups that make the backbone negatively charged.
- If a question gives numbers, write the equation you are using before you substitute, and give units.

## Must-know distinctions

| Often confused | Difference |
|---|---|
| Phosphodiester bond / hydrogen bond | Covalent link between nucleotides in a strand / weak link between complementary bases across strands |
| Helicase / DNA polymerase | Breaks hydrogen bonds between bases / forms phosphodiester bonds between nucleotides |
| ADP / ATP | Two phosphates / three phosphates |
| Purine / pyrimidine | Two rings / one ring |
| Condensation / hydrolysis | Water released / water used |

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three components of a nucleotide.
2. State one structural difference between ribose and deoxyribose.
3. How many phosphodiester bonds join the nucleotides in a single strand of 48 nucleotides?
4. How many phosphodiester bonds are there in a double-stranded fragment of 48 base pairs?
5. How many hydrogen bonds join a G–C pair?
6. State what is meant by antiparallel.
7. A 12 base-pair fragment has 30 hydrogen bonds. How many are G–C pairs?
8. Why is ice-cold ethanol added in DNA purification?
9. Name the enzyme that breaks hydrogen bonds during replication.
10. After four rounds of semi-conservative replication, how many molecules contain an original strand if you began with one molecule?

### Answers

1. A pentose sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogenous base.
2. Deoxyribose has hydrogen on carbon 2′ where ribose has a hydroxyl group (one fewer oxygen atom).
3. 47.
4. 2 × 47 = 94.
5. Three.
6. The two strands run in opposite directions, 5′ to 3′ and 3′ to 5′.
7. a + g = 12 and 2a + 3g = 30, so g = 30 − 24 = 6 G–C pairs (and 6 A–T pairs).
8. DNA is soluble in water but not in ethanol, so it precipitates as strands at the boundary.
9. Helicase.
10. Two (out of 16 molecules).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "hydrogen bonds" when the bond between nucleotides in one strand is a phosphodiester bond.
- Counting nucleotides instead of bonds between them.
- Saying "thymine in RNA" or "ribose in DNA".
- Describing helicase as "unzipping the DNA" without saying it breaks hydrogen bonds between bases.
- Leaving out complementary base pairing when explaining accurate copying.
- Calling mutations "caused by" a mutagen when the question says spontaneous.
- Explaining the purification steps without saying what each reagent does to the cell or the DNA.
- Quoting percentage change without stating which value is the baseline.
