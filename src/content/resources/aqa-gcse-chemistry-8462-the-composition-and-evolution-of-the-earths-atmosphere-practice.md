These questions cover section **4.9.1 The composition and evolution of the Earth's atmosphere** of the AQA GCSE
Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 at both
Foundation and Higher Tier. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is
labelled Higher tier only.

## Questions

**1.** For about 200 million years, the proportions of gases in the atmosphere have been much the same as they are today.

**(a)** Name the two gases that make up most of the atmosphere and give their approximate percentages. **[3]**
**(b)** Name **two** gases present in small proportions. **[2]**

**2.** A room contains 450 dm³ of air. Calculate the approximate volume of oxygen in the room. **[2]**

**3.** A student passes 120 cm³ of air repeatedly over heated copper in a closed syringe system until the volume is constant at 95 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the air that was oxygen. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the word equation for the reaction in the syringe. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why the student should let the apparatus cool before taking the final reading. **[1]**

**4.** The early atmosphere of the Earth was different from the atmosphere today.

**(a)** Name the main gas in the early atmosphere. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** other substances that were in the early atmosphere. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the source of these gases. **[1]**
**(d)** Compare the early atmosphere with the atmospheres of Mars and Venus today. **[1]**

**5.** The oceans formed about 4 billion years ago.

**(a)** Explain how the oceans formed. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe **two** ways in which the formation of the oceans reduced the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[2]**

**6.** Algae first produced oxygen about 2.7 billion years ago.

**(a)** Write the word equation for photosynthesis. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why plants and algae changed the percentages of carbon dioxide and oxygen in the atmosphere. **[2]**
**(c)** State why animals could only evolve after the percentage of oxygen had risen. **[1]**

**7.** Scientists use evidence to build theories about the early atmosphere.

**(a)** Suggest **two** reasons why there is limited evidence about the Earth's early atmosphere. **[2]**
**(b)** A student says, "The theory about volcanoes must be correct because it is in the textbook." Explain why this is not good scientific reasoning. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how each of these deposits formed.

**(a)** Limestone **[2]**
**(b)** Coal **[2]**
**(c)** Crude oil and natural gas **[2]**

**9.** The table shows the approximate percentage of oxygen in the atmosphere at different times.

| Time / billion years ago | Percentage of oxygen |
|---|---|
| 3.0 | 0 |
| 2.0 | 3 |
| 1.0 | 10 |
| 0.5 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the average increase in percentage of oxygen per billion years between 2.0 and 1.0 billion years ago. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total increase between 3.0 and 0.5 billion years ago. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest what caused the increase. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the main changes in the Earth's atmosphere over time and the likely cause of each. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Nitrogen** [1], about **80%** (four-fifths) [1]; **oxygen** about **20%** (one-fifth) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: carbon dioxide; water vapour; noble gases (argon). **[2]**

**2.** One-fifth (20%) of 450 [1] = **90 dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Oxygen used = 120 − 95 = 25 cm³ [1]; 25 ÷ 120 × 100 = **20.8%** (about 21%) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** copper + oxygen → copper oxide [1]
**(c)** A hot gas **occupies a larger volume**, so reading it hot would give an inaccurate (too high) final volume [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** **Carbon dioxide** [1]
**(b)** Any two [1] each: water vapour; nitrogen; methane; ammonia. **[2]**
**(c)** **Volcanic activity** (volcanoes) [1]
**(d)** It was like the atmospheres of Mars and Venus: **mainly carbon dioxide** with little or no oxygen [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** The Earth **cooled** [1], so the **water vapour condensed** to form the oceans [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Carbon dioxide **dissolved** in the oceans [1]; **carbonates were precipitated**, forming sediments [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** carbon dioxide + water [1] → glucose + oxygen [1] (in light) **[2]**
**(b)** Photosynthesis **removes carbon dioxide** from the air [1] and **releases oxygen**, so the percentage of carbon dioxide fell and oxygen rose [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Animals need **oxygen** to survive / respire [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Any two [1] each: the Earth is about **4.6 billion years old**, so the events were a very long time ago; rocks and other evidence have been changed over time; there are no direct records. **[2]**
**(b)** A theory is supported by **evidence**, and being in a book does not prove it [1]; theories can **change** if new evidence is found (and should be tested, for example by peer review) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **Shells and skeletons** of marine organisms (calcium carbonate) settled on the sea bed [1]; they were compressed by layers above over **millions of years** into rock [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Plant material** was buried [1]; **heat and pressure** over millions of years changed it into coal [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Remains of **plankton** were buried in mud [1]; **heat and pressure** over millions of years (without oxygen) changed them into oil and gas [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** (10 − 3) ÷ 1 = **7 percentage points per billion years** [1 for working, 1 for answer] **[2]**
**(b)** 20 − 0 = **20 percentage points** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** **Photosynthesis** by algae and plants [1], which produced oxygen as a product [1]. **[2]**

**10.** One mark per point up to six: early atmosphere mainly carbon dioxide from volcanoes [1]; water vapour condensed as the Earth cooled to form oceans [1]; carbon dioxide dissolved in the oceans and carbonates formed sediments [1]; algae then plants photosynthesised, using carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen [1]; carbon was locked up in sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels [1]; oxygen reached about 20% and nitrogen about 80%, allowing animals to evolve [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the early atmosphere as mainly nitrogen or oxygen.
- Saying photosynthesis uses oxygen.
- Forgetting "millions of years" and "heat and pressure" when describing fossil fuels.
- Not saying what the evidence cannot show.
- Giving a percentage without working in practical calculations.
