Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680) explores how people use the Earth's resources, the damage that use can cause, and how environments can be managed more sustainably. It draws on both the natural world and human decision-making, and applies them to real issues such as energy supply, food production, pollution, climate change, natural hazards and population growth. This page covers the 2027-2029 syllabus, first examined in 2027. It is assessed by two written papers, Paper 1 Principles of Environmental Management and Paper 2 Environmental Management in Context, each lasting 105 minutes, each marked out of 80 and each worth 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has seven topics. They can be grouped by the part of the environment each one deals with.

**Resources from the Earth**

- **Topic 1, Natural resources**: how rocks form; extracting rocks, ores and minerals; managing them sustainably; energy resources; conserving and managing energy resources; and fracking.
- **Topic 2, Land**: soils and crop growth, food production and crop yield, and soil erosion.

**Water and air**

- **Topic 3, Water**: water sources and supply, water pollution, water-related diseases, marine aquaculture, oil pollution and plastic pollution.
- **Topic 4, The atmosphere and human activities**: the atmosphere, climate change, acid rain and ozone depletion.

**Living systems and investigation**

- **Topic 5, Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork**: ecosystems, forest ecosystems, managing biodiversity, and fieldwork investigations.

**Hazards and people**

- **Topic 6, Natural hazards**: earthquakes and volcanoes, tropical cyclones, flooding and drought.
- **Topic 7, Human population**: population density, distribution and structure; population size; and managing population size.

Each topic follows a similar pattern: understand the natural system, see how human activity affects it, then consider how it can be managed.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Question types |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Principles of Environmental Management | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% | Short-answer and structured questions, with extended-response questions based on source material |
| Paper 2 | Environmental Management in Context | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% | Short-answer, data processing and analysis, and extended-response questions based on source material |

- Both papers are compulsory and **externally assessed**.
- The qualification is **not tiered**: everyone takes the same papers.
- Assessment is **linear**, with both papers in the same examination series.
- **Source material** features in both papers. Paper 2 places particular weight on handling data, so numerical and graphical skills matter.

**Which edition applies to you.** The 2027-2029 syllabus is first examined in 2027. Cambridge also publishes a 2025-2026 edition of 0680 for examinations in 2025 and 2026. If your examinations are in 2027 or later, the 2027-2029 edition described here is the one to follow.

## How the parts fit together

Environmental Management is unusually interconnected. Very few issues belong to one topic alone.

- **Energy links resources and the atmosphere.** Energy resources and fracking in Topic 1 connect directly to climate change and acid rain in Topic 4.
- **Land and water are linked through farming.** Food production and soil erosion in Topic 2 affect water quality and supply in Topic 3.
- **Ecosystems sit at the centre.** Forest ecosystems and biodiversity in Topic 5 are affected by land use, pollution, climate change and natural hazards from the other topics.
- **Population drives demand.** Human population in Topic 7 is the background to pressure on every resource in the course.

The papers reflect this. Paper 1 tests the principles from across the topics, while Paper 2 asks you to apply them in context, often to unfamiliar case material or data. Fieldwork in Topic 5 supports both, because planning an investigation, collecting data and interpreting results are skills that data-based questions draw on.

Skills to develop throughout the course include:

- reading and interpreting graphs, tables, maps and diagrams;
- carrying out simple calculations from data;
- explaining causes and effects in clear sequences;
- evaluating management strategies, weighing benefits against costs.

## Planning your study

A logical route is to follow the syllabus order, which moves from resources through the physical environment to living systems, hazards and people.

1. **Begin with Topics 1 and 2.** They introduce the idea of sustainable management, which runs through the whole course.
2. **Continue with Topics 3 and 4**, linking each new issue back to resource use.
3. **Study Topic 5 with practical work.** If your school runs fieldwork, use it to understand sampling and data collection rather than treating it as a separate activity.
4. **Complete Topics 6 and 7**, which pull together many earlier ideas.

Across the year, aim to cover roughly the first four topics in the earlier part of the course, Topics 5 to 7 in the later part, and keep the final stage for review and practice. Keep a bank of short case examples for each topic as you go; they make extended answers more convincing. Once all seven topics have been taught, start full-length practice papers, and use the course's exam-preparation page for guidance on each paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management 0680 assessed?**
By two compulsory written papers, Paper 1 Principles of Environmental Management and Paper 2 Environmental Management in Context. Each lasts 105 minutes, is marked out of 80 and is worth 50%.

**What are the seven topics?**
Natural resources; Land; Water; The atmosphere and human activities; Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork; Natural hazards; and Human population.

**Is IGCSE Environmental Management tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same two papers.

**Which syllabus should I follow?**
This page covers the 2027-2029 syllabus, first examined in 2027. A separate 2025-2026 edition applies to examinations in 2025 and 2026.

**Is fieldwork part of the course?**
Yes. Fieldwork investigations are part of Topic 5, Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork.
