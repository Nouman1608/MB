These questions cover subtopic 1.7 Indices I in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 8 and 14 use Core content (C1.7: positive, zero and negative integer indices and the rules of indices) and suit every candidate. Questions 9 to 13 are labelled **(Extended)** because they use fractional indices, which are in E1.7 only. All questions are non-calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [Indices I study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-indices-i/) and the [Indices I revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-indices-i-revision-notes/).

**7.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 81 and 27 as powers of 3. **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the value of 25^(3/2) × 5⁻⁴. **[2]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Show that 2^(1/2) × 8^(1/2) is an integer, and find its value. **[2]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the value of 0.04^(−0.5). **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1** [1]

**(b)** 1/2⁴ = **1/16** [1]

**(c)** (5/3)² = **25/9** (or 2 7/9) [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (c), turn the fraction upside down to make the index positive; 9/25 is the value of (3/5)², not (3/5)⁻².

**2. (a)** 4² = **16** [1]

**(b)** 6^(−2 − (−4)) = 6² = **36** [1]

*Examiner insight:* subtracting −4 gives +2; writing −2 − 4 = −6 is the usual slip and gives 1/46 656, which should look suspicious.

**3.** (3⁻¹)³ = 3⁻³ [1]; 3⁻³ × 3⁵ = 3² = **9** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a power of a power multiplies the indices (−1 × 3 = −3); adding them would give 3² and then 3⁷, which is far too large.

**4. (a)** **10⁻³** [1]

**(b)** (1/2)⁻³ = 2³ = **8** [1]

**5.** 16 = 2⁴ [1]; 2⁴ × 2⁻⁷ = **2⁻³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the question asks for a power of 2, so 2⁻³ is the answer; 1/8 alone may not earn the final mark.

**6.** Values 0.25, 0.125, 1, 10 [1]; **2⁻³, 4⁻¹, 3⁰, 0.1⁻¹** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 0.1⁻¹ is the reciprocal of one tenth, which is 10; a negative index on a number less than 1 gives a value greater than 1.

**7. (a)** 81 = 3⁴ and 27 = 3³ [1]

**(b)** 3⁸ ÷ 3⁹ [1] = 3⁻¹ = **1/3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "hence" means use part (a): 81² = (3⁴)² = 3⁸ and 27³ = (3³)³ = 3⁹. Working out 6561 ÷ 19 683 by hand is slow and error-prone.

**8.** A negative index means the reciprocal of the positive power, not a negative number [1]; 5⁻² = 1/5² = **1/25** [1]

**9. (a)** √121 = **11** [1]

**(b)** fourth root of 16 = 2, 2³ = **8** [1]

**(c)** ∛8 = 2, so **1/2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the negative sign gives a reciprocal, so −2 is wrong; the denominator 3 gives a cube root, so 8 ÷ 3 is wrong.

**10.** Negative index: (64/27)^(2/3) [1]; cube root: 4/3 [1]; squared: **16/9** (or 1 7/9) [1]

*Examiner insight:* doing the cube root before the square keeps the numbers small; squaring 64/27 first gives 4096/729, which is hard to root without a calculator.

**11.** 25^(3/2) = (√25)³ = 125 [1]; 125 × 1/625 = **1/5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* as powers of 5: 5³ × 5⁻⁴ = 5⁻¹ = 1/5, which is a useful check.

**12.** 8 = 2³, so 8^(1/2) = 2^(3/2) [1]; 2^(1/2) × 2^(3/2) = 2² = **4**, an integer [1]

**13.** 0.04 = 1/25 and −0.5 = −1/2, so (1/25)^(−1/2) = 25^(1/2) [1] = **5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* changing both the base and the index to fractions first turns an unfamiliar question into a familiar one.

**14.** −2 + 5 − (−1) = 4 [1]; 10⁴ = **10 000** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the question asks for an ordinary number, so 10⁴ alone does not earn the final mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating a negative index as a negative number, or a zero index as 0.
- Index arithmetic slips with negatives, particularly when dividing.
- Combining powers with different bases before rewriting them.
- Giving a value when a power was asked for, or the reverse.
- (Extended) Dividing by the denominator of a fractional index instead of taking a root.

## Approaching index questions

First make every base the same, then write the index arithmetic as a separate line, and only then evaluate. For fractional indices, deal with the minus sign last ("one over"), the denominator first (root) and the numerator second (power). Convert decimal indices and decimal bases to fractions before starting.
