## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by **consumer protection**. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why consumers need protection. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** actions a buyer can take to resolve a complaint. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** remedies a seller can offer a customer with a faulty product. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by a **guarantee**. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A customer buys a coat online for $85 with $6 postage. The coat is the wrong size and is returned at the customer's expense of $5. The seller agrees to refund the price, the original postage and the return postage.

**(a)** Calculate the total refund. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the seller might offer a credit note instead of a refund. **[2]**

**7.** A shop sold 2,500 phone chargers in a year. 60 were returned as faulty. Each return cost the shop $12 to replace and handle.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of chargers returned as faulty. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total cost of the returns. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** action the shop could take to reduce the number of returns. **[1]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why consumer protection is needed when buying goods online. **[4]**

**9.** A customer bought a washing machine that leaks after four weeks. Describe the steps the customer could take to resolve the problem. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why keeping a receipt helps a customer who wants to make a complaint. **[2]**

**11.** A company discovers that a children's toy may break in a dangerous way. It has sold 4,000 toys at $28 each. Calculate the maximum cost of offering refunds, and explain why the company should recall the toy anyway. **[4]**

**12.** Explain **one** way in which consumer protection benefits an honest business. **[2]**

**13.** Evaluate whether it is better for a seller to replace a faulty product immediately or to investigate the complaint first. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Laws, rules and actions that protect buyers from unsafe, unfair or misleading treatment [1].

**2.** Any two from: safety; quality; accurate information; fair prices and contracts; unequal knowledge and power; vulnerable groups; honest advertising; building trust [1 each].

**3.** Any two from: keep proof of purchase; contact the seller; make a formal complaint in writing; use the guarantee; contact a consumer organisation; ask the card provider for a refund; take legal action [1 each].

**4.** Any two from: refund; repair; replacement; credit note; compensation [1 each].

**5.** A promise by the maker or seller to repair or replace a product within a set period of time [1].

**6.** **(a)** 85 + 6 [1] + 5 = $96 [1].

**(b)** The customer can use the credit note to buy another coat or other goods [1], so the seller keeps the money in the business instead of paying it out [1].

**7.** **(a)** 60 ÷ 2,500 × 100 [1] = 2.4% [1].

**(b)** 60 × 12 = $720 [1].

**(c)** Any one: ask the supplier to improve quality; check chargers before sale; change to a more reliable supplier [1].

**8.** Any two developed: the buyer cannot see or test the product before buying [1], so may receive something of poor quality or different from the description [1]; the seller may be in another country [1], so it is hard to complain or get a refund [1]; the buyer pays before receiving the goods [1], so may lose money if they do not arrive [1]; online prices and contract terms may be unclear [1], so hidden charges can appear [1]. Maximum 4.

**9.** The customer should keep the receipt as proof of purchase [1]. They should contact the seller, explain the fault and ask for a repair, replacement or refund [1]. If the seller does not respond, they should send a formal written complaint or use the guarantee [1]. As a last step they could contact a consumer organisation or take legal action [1].

**10.** It is proof of what was bought, when and from whom [1], so the seller cannot deny the sale and the customer can show the product is within the guarantee period [1].

**11.** Maximum refund = 4,000 × 28 [1] = $112,000 [1]. The toy may injure children [1], and ignoring the danger could lead to legal action, fines and a loss of reputation that would cost the company more [1].

**12.** Dishonest rivals cannot win customers by cheating [1], so honest businesses compete fairly and customers trust the market and buy more [1].

**13.** Any five from: replacing at once pleases the customer and keeps loyalty [1]; it is quick and simple for low-value items [1]; but replacing without checking may mean accepting false claims [1]; investigating finds the cause of the fault, so the problem can be fixed [1]; it may annoy a customer if it takes too long [1]; for expensive or unsafe goods, investigation is more important [1]; a justified decision, for example replace low-cost goods at once but investigate repeated or costly faults [1]. Maximum 5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 6(a): leaving out the return postage or the original postage.
- Question 7(a): dividing by 60 instead of 2,500.
- Question 8: giving general reasons that are not linked to online buying.
- Question 9: writing about the legal action first.
- Question 11: working out the cost with the wrong number of toys.
- Question 13: not reaching a decision.

## Quick reference for checking your answers

| Item | Key point |
