## Questions

**1.** A student heats tubes of sugar solution with Benedict's solution in a water bath at 80 °C. Complete a risk assessment line by stating one hazard, the risk it creates and a control measure. **[3]**

**2.** A colorimeter was used with protein standards of 0, 20, 40, 60 and 80 g dm⁻³. The absorbances were 0.00, 0.18, 0.36, 0.54 and 0.72.

**(a)** A sample gave an absorbance of 0.45. Calculate its concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** State why the meter is zeroed with a blank before the readings. **[1]**
**(c)** A second sample reads 0.95. Explain what should be done. **[2]**

**3.** A student has a 100 g dm⁻³ stock and makes a doubling dilution series.

**(a)** State the volumes of previous solution and water used to make 10 cm³ at each step. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the concentration of the fourth dilution made. **[1]**
**(c)** State one action that makes the series accurate. **[1]**

**4.** A bacterial culture is diluted to 10⁻⁵. A sample of 0.2 cm³ is spread on agar and 40 colonies grow.

**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria per cm³ in the original culture. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why this may differ from the true number of cells. **[1]**

**5.** A potometer capillary has a radius of 0.40 mm. A bubble moves 36 mm in 3.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water taken up. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate in mm³ per minute. **[1]**
**(c)** The shoot has 25 cm² of leaf. Calculate the rate per cm². **[1]**
**(d)** State two actions in setting up that make the results valid. **[2]**

**6.** At ×40, 0.10 mm on a stage micrometer spans 40 eyepiece graticule divisions.

**(a)** Calculate the length of one division in µm. **[1]**
**(b)** A cell is 18 divisions wide. Calculate its width in µm. **[1]**
**(c)** A drawing of the cell is 90 mm wide. Calculate its magnification. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the graticule needs recalibrating for another objective. **[1]**

**7.** Two unknown samples gave these results.

| Test | P | Q |
|---|---|---|
| Benedict's | stays blue | orange |
| Benedict's after boiling with acid and neutralising | brick red | brick red |
| Iodine in potassium iodide | yellow-brown | blue-black |
| Biuret | blue | blue |

**(a)** State what each sample contains. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the acid-treated sample is neutralised before Benedict's. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how the Benedict's test could give more than a yes or no result. **[1]**

**8.** A chromatogram has a solvent front at 7.2 cm. The mixture gives spots at 3.6 cm and 5.4 cm. Known amino acids J, K and L have Rf values of 0.50, 0.62 and 0.75.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf values. **[2]**
**(b)** State which amino acids are in the mixture. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the baseline is drawn in pencil. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why ninhydrin is used. **[1]**

**9.** Two antibiotic discs gave clear zones of radius 11 mm and 7 mm on a bacterial lawn.

**(a)** Calculate the area of each zone. **[2]**
**(b)** State why a disc without antibiotic is included. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe two aseptic steps when spreading the bacteria. **[2]**

**10.** Eight 1 m² quadrats, placed randomly, gave counts of 12, 9, 15, 7, 11, 10, 14 and 6 plants. The area is 450 m².

**(a)** Calculate the mean number per quadrat. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the population. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how to place the quadrats at random. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain when a belt transect would be better. **[2]**
**(e)** In a beetle study, 28 were marked, a second catch of 24 had 6 marked. Estimate the population. **[2]**

**11.** An oxygen sensor on a data logger shows a rise from 4.5 to 9.0 mg dm⁻³ over 15 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two advantages of a data logger over a student with a stopwatch. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain a limitation of using a computer model of a molecule. **[1]**

**12.** A class measures pulse before and after exercise. The mean was 76 beats per minute before and 120 after.

**(a)** Describe three measures that make the use of volunteers ethical. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change. **[2]**
**(c)** State what to include when citing a website used for background. **[2]**
**(d)** State one hazard of the exercise and a control. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Hazard: hot water or steam [1]. Risk: scalds or burns if spilt or knocked [1]. Control: stand the bath on a tray away from the edge and use tongs or a holder [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit when hazard, risk and control are separate statements; "be careful" is too vague.

**2. (a)** Gradient = 0.72 / 80 = 0.009 per g dm⁻³ [1]. Concentration = 0.45 / 0.009 = **50 g dm⁻³** [1].
**(b)** To remove the absorbance of the solvent and cuvette so readings are from the sample only [1].
**(c)** The reading is above the highest standard [1]. Dilute the sample with a known factor, re-read, then multiply back [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show how the value was read from the line; extrapolating beyond the standards is usually treated as unreliable.

**3. (a)** 5 cm³ previous solution [1] plus 5 cm³ water [1].
**(b)** 100 → 50 → 25 → 12.5 → **6.25 g dm⁻³** [1].
**(c)** Mix each tube thoroughly before transferring, using a clean pipette or measuring syringe [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give both volumes; stating only "dilute it" does not show the dilution factor.

**4. (a)** 40 / 0.2 = 200 per cm³ of dilution [1]. Divide by 10⁻⁵ [1]. **2.0 × 10⁷ per cm³** [1].
**(b)** A colony may come from a clump of cells, and only living cells form colonies [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the dilution step and the division by the volume spread so that each stage of the method can be seen.

**5. (a)** Volume = π × 0.40² × 36 [1] = **18 mm³** [1].
**(b)** 18.1 / 3.0 = **6.0 mm³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** 6.0 / 25 = **0.24 mm³ min⁻¹ cm⁻²** [1].
**(d)** Any two: cut the shoot under water at an angle, or make airtight joints assembled under water [1]; dry the leaves, or let the bubble settle before timing [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded volume in the next step; units such as mm³ per minute should appear on each answer.

**6. (a)** 100 µm / 40 = **2.5 µm** [1].
**(b)** 18 × 2.5 = **45 µm** [1].
**(c)** 45 µm = 0.045 mm [1]. 90 / 0.045 = **×2000** [1].
**(d)** Changing the objective changes the apparent size of each division [1].
*Examiner insight:* Convert units before dividing; mixing mm and µm is a common reason for answers out by a factor of 1000.

**7. (a)** P: non-reducing sugar present [1]; no reducing sugar, starch or protein [1]. Q: reducing sugar present [1]; starch present and no protein [1].
**(b)** Benedict's requires alkaline conditions, which the acid would prevent [1].
**(c)** Compare colour with standards, or filter the precipitate and measure with a colorimeter [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link each result to its conclusion; listing colours without naming the molecule does not answer "state what it contains".

**8. (a)** 3.6 / 7.2 = **0.50** [1] and 5.4 / 7.2 = **0.75** [1].
**(b)** J and L [1].
**(c)** Pencil is insoluble in the solvent, so it does not run [1].
**(d)** Amino acids are colourless; ninhydrin reacts to make them visible [1].
*Examiner insight:* Rf has no unit because it is a ratio; compare with standards run on the same sheet.

**9. (a)** π × 11² = **380 mm²** [1]; π × 7² = **154 mm²** [1].
**(b)** To show that the zone is caused by the antibiotic and not the disc or the medium [1].
**(c)** Any two: flame the loop or spreader and let it cool [1]; open the lid only slightly, or flame the bottle neck [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name what is done, such as "flame the loop"; "keep everything sterile" repeats the question.

**10. (a)** 84 / 8 = **10.5** [1].
**(b)** 10.5 × 450 [1] = **4725 (about 4700)** [1].
**(c)** Use a grid with random number tables or a generator for coordinates [1]; place a quadrat at each pair [1].
**(d)** When conditions change gradually, such as with distance from a shore [1]; quadrats at set intervals show the trend [1].
**(e)** N = 28 × 24 / 6 [1] = **112** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say how randomness is achieved; "throw the quadrat" is not usually given credit as random.

**11. (a)** (9.0 − 4.5) / 15 = **0.30 mg dm⁻³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Frequent readings [1]; no human timing error or continuous record [1].
**(c)** The model simplifies, so it only reflects its assumptions [1].
*Examiner insight:* An advantage gains credit when it is compared with the manual method, not just named.

**12. (a)** Informed consent [1]; right to stop [1]; excluding anyone for whom exercise is unsafe, with anonymous data [1].
**(b)** (120 − 76) / 76 × 100 [1] = **+57.9 %** [1].
**(c)** Author, year, page title, website name and date accessed [1] [1].
**(d)** Hazard: tripping or overexertion; control: clear space and stop if unwell [1].
*Examiner insight:* A percentage change needs the sign or the word increase, and divides by the starting value.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating only a hazard in a risk assessment.
- Not converting micrometres to millimetres in magnification.
- Giving Rf with units, or measuring to the spot edge.
- Omitting the dilution factor from plate counts.
- Using vague words such as "careful", "clean" or "fair" in method answers.
- Extrapolating a calibration line.
- Describing random sampling without saying how coordinates are chosen.
- Dividing a percentage change by the final value.
