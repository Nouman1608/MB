These questions cover section **3.21.1 Large data set** of AQA A-level Mathematics (7357). All the data below are invented extracts, written in the style of regional household purchase data. They are not taken from AQA's data set, which you should explore on the AQA website. A calculator is needed. Revise first with the [large data set study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-large-data-set/) and the [use of data revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-use-of-data-in-statistics-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** In an extract, the value 0 is used to mean "not recorded". The quantities of a food item (in grams per person per week) for five regions are 120, 135, 0, 128 and 140.

**(a)** Calculate the mean including the 0, and the mean excluding it. **[2]**

**(b)** State which mean should be used, and why. **[1]**

**2.** The quantities of fruit juice (ml per person per week) in eight regions are 410, 385, 450, 395, 520, 405, 430 and 415.

**(a)** Calculate the mean and the median. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[2]**

**(c)** A value is a possible outlier if it is more than 1.5 × IQR above the upper quartile. Show whether 520 is a possible outlier. **[2]**

**(d)** State which measure of location is more suitable for these data, and why. **[1]**

**3.** For the data in Question 2, Σx = 3410 and Σx² = 1 466 400. Show that the standard deviation (dividing by n) is 40.1 ml, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**4.** The value 520 is removed from the data in Question 2, leaving seven values with mean 412.9 and standard deviation 20.2. Describe the effect of removing 520 on the mean and the standard deviation, and state whether it should be removed. **[2]**

**5.** For one region, the quantity of fresh fruit rose from 1020 g to 1050 g per person per week between two years. Calculate the percentage increase, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Summary data for fresh fruit purchased (g per person per week) in 12 regions are given for two years.

| | n | Σx | Σx² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year A | 12 | 15 240 | 19 452 000 |
| Year B | 12 | 15 960 | 21 330 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean and standard deviation for Year A. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean and standard deviation for Year B, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**(c)** Compare the two years, with reference to your answers and to a limitation of the data. **[3]**

**7.** Describe how to use the random integer function on a calculator to select a simple random sample of 5 regions from the 12 regions in the data. **[3]**

**8.** State two reasons why conclusions about individual households should not be drawn from regional average data. **[2]**

**9.** The quantity of fresh fruit (g per person per week) for one region in the years 2010 to 2014 is 1000, 1030, 1010, 1060 and 1090. A line of best fit is y = 996 + 21t, where t is the number of years after 2010.

**(a)** Interpret the gradient 21 in context. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the line to predict the quantity in 2025, and comment on the reliability of the prediction. **[3]**

**10.** For 12 regions, the product moment correlation coefficient between the quantity of fruit bought and the quantity of vegetables bought is 0.86. A student writes: "This shows that buying more fruit makes people buy more vegetables." Criticise the statement. **[2]**

**11.** A student wants to investigate whether regional differences in fruit purchase were greater in the later of two years than in the earlier. Describe two statistics she should compare and give two cautions about her conclusion. **[4]**

**12.** Describe three checks you would make on an extract of a large data set before calculating summary statistics. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Including 0: 523 ÷ 5 = **104.6 g** [1]. Excluding 0: 523 ÷ 4 = **130.75 g** [1].

**(b)** The mean excluding 0, because 0 means "not recorded" and is not a measurement; including it pulls the mean down wrongly [1].

*Examiner insight:* a missing-value code is not a data value. Exclude it and reduce n.

**2. (a)** Mean = 3410 ÷ 8 = **426.25 ml** [1]. Ordered: 385, 395, 405, 410, 415, 430, 450, 520; median = (410 + 415) ÷ 2 = **412.5 ml** [1].

**(b)** Lower quartile = (395 + 405) ÷ 2 = **400** [1]. Upper quartile = (430 + 450) ÷ 2 = **440**; IQR = 440 − 400 = **40** [1].

**(c)** Upper fence = 440 + 1.5 × 40 = 500 [1]. 520 > 500, so 520 is a possible outlier [1].

**(d)** The median (or median with IQR), because the outlier pulls the mean upward [1].

**3.** σ² = Σx²/n − x̄² = 1 466 400 ÷ 8 − 426.25² [1] = 183 300 − 181 689.06 = 1610.94 [1]. σ = √1610.94 = **40.1** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* show the substitution into the formula even if the calculator gives σ directly; the first mark is for the method.

**4.** Removing 520 lowers the mean from 426.25 to 412.9 and roughly halves the standard deviation from 40.1 to 20.2, so the outlier had a large effect on both [1]. It should be removed only if it is an error or does not belong to the population; otherwise keep it and use the median and IQR [1].

**5.** (1050 − 1020) ÷ 1020 × 100 [1] = **2.94%** [1].

**6. (a)** Mean = 15 240 ÷ 12 = **1270 g** [1]. σ² = 19 452 000 ÷ 12 − 1270² = 1 621 000 − 1 612 900 = 8100, so σ = **90 g** [1].

**(b)** Mean = 15 960 ÷ 12 = **1330 g** [1]. σ² = 21 330 000 ÷ 12 − 1330² = 1 777 500 − 1 768 900 = 8600 [1]; σ = **92.7 g** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(c)** Location: Year B has a higher mean (1330 g against 1270 g) [1]. Spread: the standard deviations are similar (92.7 g against 90 g), with Year B slightly more variable [1]. Caution: there are only 12 regions and two years, and regional averages say nothing about individual households [1].

**7.** Number the regions 1 to 12 [1]. Generate random integers from 1 to 12 with the calculator [1]. Ignore any repeats and carry on until five different regions have been chosen [1].

**8.** Any two: regional averages hide variation between households within a region [1]; regions differ in size and in the age or income mix of their populations [1]; quantities bought are not the same as quantities eaten [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** On average, the quantity rises by 21 g per person per week each year [1].

**(b)** t = 15, so y = 996 + 21 × 15 = **1311 g** [1]. The prediction is an extrapolation far beyond the data (2010 to 2014) [1], and trends may not continue for 11 more years, so it is unreliable [1].

**10.** Correlation does not show causation [1]. Another factor, such as income or region size, could affect both quantities, or the regions with higher purchases of one item may simply buy more of everything [1].

**11.** Compare the standard deviations (or ranges) for the two years [1], and the interquartile ranges [1]. Cautions: the conclusion is based on a small number of regions and two years only [1]; a larger standard deviation could be due to one unusual region (an outlier), so check this before concluding [1].

**12.** Any three: look for missing values or codes that stand for "not recorded" [1]; check that units and scales are the same [1]; look for impossible values or values outside the expected range [1]; check for duplicate rows [1]; check that categories are labelled consistently [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* every comment on data needs a figure, the units and the context. A limitation is worth a mark only if it is specific to the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating a missing-value code as a real value.
- Dividing by n − 1 when the question asks for the standard deviation of the data given, or the reverse.
- Quoting the answer without units or a statement in context.
- Describing a change without giving the size.
- Drawing a causal conclusion from a correlation.
