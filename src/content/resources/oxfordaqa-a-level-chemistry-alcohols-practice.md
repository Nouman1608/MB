## Questions

**1.** Each alcohol below is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). For each one, state whether it
is primary, secondary or tertiary and give the final colour of the mixture.

**(a)** cyclohexylmethanol, C₆H₁₁CH₂OH **[1]**
**(b)** 3-methylpentan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(c)** 1-methylcyclopentanol (the –OH and a methyl group are on the same ring carbon) **[1]**

**2.** Write equations, using [O] to represent the oxidising agent, for:

**(a)** the oxidation of 2-methylbutan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH, to 2-methylbutanal **[1]**
**(b)** the oxidation of 2-methylbutan-1-ol directly to 2-methylbutanoic acid **[1]**
**(c)** the oxidation of 3-methylpentan-2-ol to a ketone. Name the ketone. **[1]**

**3.** A student wants to make 2-methylbutanal. She heats 2-methylbutan-1-ol with an excess of acidified
potassium dichromate(VI) under reflux for 40 minutes, then distils the mixture.

**(a)** Name the main organic product she obtains and explain why it forms. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two changes to her method that would give 2-methylbutanal instead. **[2]**

**4.** Two unlabelled bottles contain 2,2-dimethylpropanal, (CH₃)₃CCHO, and cyclohexanone. Describe a test
using Fehling's solution that distinguishes them. Give the condition, what is seen with each compound, and an
equation, using [O], for the compound that reacts. **[4]**

**5.** When 3-methylbutan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH(CH₃)₂, is warmed with a suitable acid, it loses water.

**(a)** Give the name of an acid that catalyses this reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline how 3-methylbut-1-ene forms, showing the curly arrows and intermediates in words. **[3]**
**(c)** A second alkene also forms. Name it and explain why it forms. **[2]**

**6.** Hexan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, is heated with concentrated sulfuric acid. Four alkenes are formed.
Name all four and explain why there are four. **[3]**

**7.** Alcohol E has the molecular formula C₆H₁₄O.

- When E is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI), the mixture turns green. The organic product does
  not change the colour of Fehling's solution.
- When E is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid, only one alkene forms.

Deduce the structure and name of E. Explain your reasoning. **[4]**

**8.** The overall equation for the oxidation of ethanol to ethanoic acid by acidified dichromate(VI) is:

```
2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 16H⁺ + 3CH₃CH₂OH → 4Cr³⁺ + 3CH₃COOH + 11H₂O
```

A 10.0 cm³ sample of an aqueous ethanol solution reacts exactly with 20.0 cm³ of 0.0450 mol dm⁻³ potassium
dichromate(VI) in excess acid. Calculate the concentration of ethanol in the sample in g dm⁻³. **[3]**

**9.** In Required practical 4, a student makes 4-methylcyclohexene, C₇H₁₂, by heating 4-methylcyclohexanol,
C₇H₁₄O, with concentrated phosphoric acid and distilling the alkene off as it forms.

**(a)** Explain why the alkene distils from the flask while most of the alcohol stays behind. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the thermometer bulb is placed, and why. **[1]**
**(c)** State why anti-bumping granules are added. **[1]**
**(d)** 6.84 g of 4-methylcyclohexanol gives 4.32 g of 4-methylcyclohexene. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**10.** A company plans to make poly(propene) from propan-2-ol obtained from plant material.

**(a)** State the reagent and condition used to convert propan-2-ol to propene, and write an equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the repeating unit of poly(propene). **[1]**
**(c)** Give one advantage of this route over making propene from crude oil. **[1]**

**11.** 2-Methylpentan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH, is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI) in two experiments.

- Experiment A: 2-methylpentan-1-ol in excess, gentle warming, product distilled off as it forms.
- Experiment B: excess oxidising agent, heated under reflux, then distilled.

**(a)** Name the organic product of each experiment and write an equation, using [O], for each. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the product of Experiment A has a lower boiling point than 2-methylpentan-1-ol. **[2]**
**(c)** Each product is warmed with Tollens' reagent. State what is seen in each case. **[2]**
**(d)** In Experiment A, 5.61 g of 2-methylpentan-1-ol gives 3.50 g of product. Calculate the percentage yield. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Primary**; turns **green** [1]
**(b)** **Secondary**; turns **green** [1]
**(c)** **Tertiary**; stays **orange** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs both the class and the colour; a correct class with the wrong colour does not score.

**2. (a)** **CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO + H₂O** [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH + H₂O** [1]
**(c)** CH₃CH(OH)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ + H₂O; **3-methylpentan-2-one** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Unbalanced [O] equations are not credited; count the oxygens, and remember that 2[O] is needed for the one-step route to the acid.

**3. (a)** **2-methylbutanoic acid** [1]; under reflux the aldehyde formed first condenses and returns to the flask, where the excess oxidising agent oxidises it further [1]
**(b)** Use the alcohol in excess (limited oxidising agent) [1]; set the apparatus for distillation and remove the aldehyde as soon as it forms, instead of refluxing [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Use distillation" alone gives only one mark in (b); the second needs the change in amounts of reactants.

**4.** Warm each with Fehling's solution in a water bath [1]; 2,2-dimethylpropanal: blue solution gives a red precipitate [1]; cyclohexanone: stays blue [1]; (CH₃)₃CCHO + [O] → (CH₃)₃CCOOH [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A test needs an observation for both compounds, so leaving out "stays blue" for the ketone costs a mark even if everything else is right.

**5. (a)** Concentrated H₃PO₄ (or concentrated H₂SO₄) [1]
**(b)** Arrow from an oxygen lone pair (on –OH) to H⁺, giving the protonated alcohol R–OH₂⁺ [1]; arrow from the C–O bond to O, water leaves, giving the secondary carbocation CH₃C⁺HCH(CH₃)₂ [1]; arrow from a C–H bond on C1 to the C1–C2 bond, forming C=C and releasing H⁺ [1]
**(c)** **2-methylbut-2-ene**, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃ [1]; H⁺ can also be lost from C3, the other carbon next to the positive carbon, and C3 carries a hydrogen [1]
*Examiner insight:* Arrows must begin at a lone pair or a bond; an arrow drawn from H⁺ or from a positive charge is not credited.

**6.** **(E)-hex-2-ene and (Z)-hex-2-ene** [1]; **(E)-hex-3-ene and (Z)-hex-3-ene** [1]; H⁺ can be lost from C2 or C4 (which are not equivalent), giving two positions of C=C, and each alkene has two different groups on each double-bond carbon, so each shows E/Z isomerism [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hexene" without a position number and an E/Z label is not a full name, so it cannot gain a naming mark.

**7.** Green colour: E is oxidised, so it is primary or secondary [1]; the product does not react with Fehling's, so it is a ketone and E is secondary [1]; only one alkene, so only one neighbouring carbon of the C–OH carbon carries H (the other neighbour has no H) [1]; E is **3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol**, CH₃CH(OH)C(CH₃)₃ [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare name earns one mark at most; the reasoning marks need each observation linked to a deduction.

**8.** n(Cr₂O₇²⁻) = 20.0/1000 × 0.0450 = 9.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(ethanol) = 9.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 3/2 = 1.35 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; concentration = 1.35 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0100 = 0.135 mol dm⁻³, × 46.0 = **6.21 g dm⁻³** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the ratio the wrong way round (× 2/3) gives 2.76 g dm⁻³; show the ratio as a labelled step so your method is visible.

**9. (a)** The alkene has a lower boiling point than the alcohol [1]; the alcohol forms hydrogen bonds between its molecules but the alkene has only weaker van der Waals forces [1]
**(b)** Level with the side arm to the condenser, so it measures the temperature of the vapour that distils [1]
**(c)** To give smooth boiling (prevent violent bumping) [1]
**(d)** n(alcohol) = 6.84 ÷ 114.0 = 0.0600 mol [1]; n(alkene) = 4.32 ÷ 96.0 = 0.0450 mol [1]; yield = 0.0450 ÷ 0.0600 × 100 = **75.0 %** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing the masses (4.32 ÷ 6.84) gives 63.2 % and earns no credit for the yield; both quantities must be in moles first.

**10. (a)** Heat with concentrated phosphoric (or sulfuric) acid [1]; **CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O** [1]
**(b)** –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]– with extension bonds at both ends [1]
**(c)** It does not use monomers derived from crude oil, a finite resource [1]
*Examiner insight:* A repeating unit without the two extension bonds, or with a C=C left in it, is not credited.

**11. (a)** A: **2-methylpentanal** [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO + H₂O [1]; B: **2-methylpentanoic acid** [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH + H₂O [1]
**(b)** 2-Methylpentan-1-ol molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other (O–H group) [1]; 2-methylpentanal has no O–H, so no hydrogen bonding between its molecules and less energy is needed to separate them [1]
**(c)** 2-Methylpentanal (A): a silver mirror forms [1]; 2-methylpentanoic acid (B): no change [1]
**(d)** n(2-methylpentan-1-ol) = 5.61 ÷ 102.0 = 0.0550 mol; n(2-methylpentanal) = 3.50 ÷ 100.0 = 0.0350 mol [1]; yield = 0.0350 ÷ 0.0550 × 100 = **63.6 %** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "the aldehyde has weaker bonds" scores nothing; the answer must name hydrogen bonding between molecules of the alcohol and its absence in the aldehyde.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a colour change for a tertiary alcohol; it stays orange.
- Leaving H₂O out of alcohol → aldehyde or ketone equations, or using one [O] for the one-step route to the acid.
- Describing distillation for an aldehyde without "as it forms" and the reason.
- No observation for the compound that does not react in a Fehling's or Tollens' test.
- Curly arrows starting at H⁺ or a positive charge, or no H⁺ released in the last step.
