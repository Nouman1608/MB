Quick recall for section 1.1, Business objectives, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [business objectives study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-business-objectives/); exam-style questions are in the [business objectives practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-business-objectives-practice/). Paper 1 uses a small business (up to 49 employees) and Paper 2 a large one (more than 250), and both can test this section. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists

- 1.1.1 Financial aims and objectives: survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security.
- 1.1.1 Non-financial aims and objectives: social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control.
- 1.1.2 Why aims and objectives change as businesses evolve: market conditions, technology, performance, legislation, internal reasons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aim | The general direction a business wants to go in (standard meaning; not defined in the specification) |
| Objective | A specific target that helps achieve the aim, ideally measurable and dated |
| Survival | Continuing to trade, covering costs and having enough cash |
| Profit | Revenue minus total costs |
| Sales | The quantity sold or the revenue earned |
| Market share | The business's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Financial security | A steady income and reserves to cope with a bad period |
| Social objective | A target that benefits the community or environment |

## Standard measures

- Profit = revenue − total costs.
- Market share (%) = business sales ÷ total market sales × 100.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Reminder: revenue 150,000 in a market of 2,000,000 gives a share of 7.5%. A 10% target needs 200,000.

## Matching objectives to the business

| Situation in the case | Objective likely to lead |
|---|---|
| Just started, or losing money | Survival |
| Owner depends on the business for income | Financial security |
| Owner left a job "to be my own boss" | Independence and control |
| Owner loves the craft | Personal satisfaction |
| Launching something untried | Challenge |
| Large company with shareholders | Profit, market share |
| Strong rivals, mass market | Market share, sales |
| Customers care about ethics or the environment | Social objectives |

## Conflicts to recognise

- Sales against profit: a price cut can raise sales but lower profit.
- Social objectives against profit: ethical sourcing or greener packaging raises costs.
- Growth against independence: outside investors want a say.
- Market share against financial security: heavy spending leaves little in reserve.

Reminder: 10,000 units at 8.00 with variable cost 5.00 and fixed costs 20,000 gives profit 10,000. At 7.00 and 13,000 units, profit is 13,000 × 2.00 − 20,000 = 6,000. Sales up 30%, profit down 40%.

## Why objectives change: the chain

Change → effect on the business → new objective.

| Reason | Example chain |
|---|---|
| Market conditions | Recession cuts demand → sales fall → survival replaces growth |
| Technology | Cheap online ordering → wider reach → new online sales target |
| Performance | Profit beats target → owners more ambitious → higher target or share objective |
| Legislation | New safety rule → higher costs → lower profit target, or deadline to comply |
| Internal reasons | New owner or manager → different priorities → new objectives |

## Command words for this section

From the specification's command-word table:

- Define: give the meaning of a term.
- State: a short answer picked from the information given.
- Outline: two linked points, in context.
- Explain: a statement of fact with two expansion points; no context.
- Analyse: an extended chain placed in the context of the question.
- Justify: recommend one of two options using the information provided.
- Evaluate: weigh both sides and reach a supported conclusion.

## Exam traps

- Naming "profit" when the case points to survival or independence.
- Treating market share and sales as the same thing.
- Giving a market share answer in dollars instead of a percentage.
- A reason for change with no new objective.
- Forgetting non-financial objectives in a question about objectives in general.
- An "evaluate" answer with no judgement, or a judgement that ignores the case.

## Self-test

1. List the five financial objectives in the specification.
2. List the four non-financial objectives.
3. Revenue 64,000; total costs 59,500. Calculate profit.
4. A business has sales of 45,000 in a market of 600,000. Calculate its market share.
5. Sales rose from 80,000 to 92,000. Calculate the percentage change.
6. A baker says: "I don't want a boss ever again." Which objective is this?
7. Which objective is most likely for a business in its first months of trading?
8. Give one way social objectives can conflict with profit.
9. A new law raises the minimum wage. Which reason for changing objectives is this?
10. Explain how good performance can change objectives.
11. What does "outline" require, according to the specification?
12. A market grows from 500,000 to 600,000 and a firm's sales stay at 50,000. What happens to its market share?

### Answers

1. Survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security.
2. Social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control.
3. 64,000 − 59,500 = 4,500.
4. 45,000 ÷ 600,000 × 100 = 7.5%.
5. (92,000 − 80,000) ÷ 80,000 × 100 = 15%.
6. Independence and control.
7. Survival.
8. Paying more for ethically sourced materials raises costs, so profit falls (any valid example).
9. Legislation.
10. Results that beat a target show the business can do more, so owners raise the target or set a new one, such as a higher market share.
11. Two linked points about a business concept or issue, placed in context.
12. It falls from 10% to 8.3%.
