## Key ideas at a glance

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Gradient | (change in y) ÷ (change in x), with units of y per unit of x |
| Gradient and ratio | Gradient 3/4 means vertical : horizontal = 3 : 4 |
| Direct proportion | y = kx, straight line through the origin, gradient k |
| Inverse proportion | y = k/x, so xy = k, a falling curve that never meets the axes |
| Conversion graph | Straight line; through the origin for currency, not for Celsius to Fahrenheit |
| Distance-time gradient | Speed (velocity if direction matters) |
| Velocity-time gradient | Acceleration (Higher tier only) |
| Velocity-time area | Distance (Higher tier only) |
| Chord gradient | Average rate of change (Higher tier only) |
| Tangent gradient | Instantaneous rate of change (Higher tier only) |

## 7.04a Real-world graphs

**Method: reading a graph**

1. Check the axis labels and units.
2. Note the starting value.
3. Read across and up with a ruler, and show the lines if you can.
4. Give the answer with units.

**Conversion graphs**

- Currency: line through the origin. The gradient is the exchange rate.
- Temperature: F = 1.8C + 32. Gradient 1.8, intercept 32.
- Not a proportion graph if the line misses the origin.

**Distance-time features**

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Up-sloping straight line | Constant speed away |
| Horizontal | Stationary |
| Down-sloping straight line | Constant speed back |
| Steeper | Faster |

Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time. Returning to the start makes the distance from the start 0, but the distance travelled is not 0.

**Proportion graphs**

| | Direct | Inverse |
|---|---|---|
| Equation | y = kx | y = k/x |
| Shape | Straight line through (0, 0) | Curve falling to the right |
| Constant | y ÷ x = k | x × y = k |
| Doubling x | y doubles | y halves |

Reminder: 6 workers take 10 days gives k = 60, so 15 workers take 60 ÷ 15 = 4 days.

## 7.04b Gradients and rates of change

**Method: finding a gradient from a straight line**

1. Pick two points that are easy to read.
2. Find the change in y and the change in x.
3. Divide, keeping the sign.
4. Add units: y-units per x-unit.

Reminder: a line from (2, 5) to (8, 23) has gradient 18 ÷ 6 = 3.

**Higher tier only: velocity-time graphs**

- Gradient is acceleration in m/s².
- Negative gradient is deceleration.
- A horizontal line is constant velocity.

**Higher tier only: chords and tangents**

| | Chord | Tangent |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Line joining two points on the curve | Line touching the curve at one point |
| Gives | Average rate of change | Instantaneous rate of change |
| How | Gradient between the two points | Draw with a ruler, then read two widely spaced points |

Reminder: for s = 3t², the chord from t = 2 to t = 2 + h has gradient 12 + 3h. As h gets smaller, the gradient approaches 12 m/s, the instantaneous rate at t = 2.

## 7.04c Areas under graphs (Higher tier only)

**Method: area under a graph**

1. Split the region into triangles, rectangles and trapezia.
2. Trapezium area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × width.
3. For a curve, use strips and trapezia to estimate.
4. State the units (axis unit × axis unit) and what the area means.

| Graph | Area means |
|---|---|
| Velocity against time | Distance travelled |
| Rate of earning (£ per hour) against time | Total money earned |

Reminder: velocity rising steadily from 4 m/s to 10 m/s over 6 s gives distance ½ × (4 + 10) × 6 = 42 m.

Over or under: on a concave curve the trapezia fall short of it, so the estimate is too small. On a convex curve the estimate is too big.

## Worked reminders

**Distance-time.** A graph joins (0, 0), (2, 30), (3, 30), (6, 0), with hours across and km up.

```
0 to 2 h:   30 ÷ 2 = 15 km/h away
2 to 3 h:   horizontal, so stationary for 1 h
3 to 6 h:   30 ÷ 3 = 10 km/h back
Distance travelled = 60 km, so average speed = 60 ÷ 6 = 10 km/h
```

**Inverse proportion.** Five machines take 12 hours.

```
k = 5 × 12 = 60
Eight machines: 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 hours
```

**Higher tier only: velocity-time.** A graph joins (0, 0), (4, 8), (10, 8), (14, 0), with seconds across and m/s up.

```
Acceleration at the start = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 m/s²
Distance = ½ × (6 + 14) × 8 = 80 m
Check: 16 + 48 + 16 = 80 m
```

**Higher tier only: drawing a tangent.** Rest the ruler against the curve at the point so the curve sits evenly on both sides. Draw the line long. Pick two widely spaced points on the line, then divide the rise by the run. Give the answer as an estimate, because a different but sensible tangent will give a slightly different value.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Speed vs distance from start.** Gradient of a distance-time graph: speed. Height of that graph: distance from the start.
- **Gradient vs area.** Gradient is a rate. Area is an accumulated total.
- **Chord vs tangent.** Chord means average. Tangent means at one instant.
- **Linear vs proportional.** Every direct proportion graph is a straight line, but not every straight line is a direct proportion graph.
- **Direct vs inverse.** Direct: ratio y ÷ x fixed. Inverse: product x × y fixed.

## Quick self-test

1. A conversion line goes through (0, 0) and (200, 262) with pounds across and dollars up. State the gradient and use it to convert £45 to dollars.
2. Convert 68 °F to °C using F = 1.8C + 32.
3. y is directly proportional to x, and y = 7 when x = 1. Find y when x = 9.
4. y is inversely proportional to x, and x = 6 when y = 6. Find y when x = 4.
5. A runner covers 15 km in 20 minutes at constant speed. Find the speed in km/h.
6. Find the gradient of the line joining (2, 5) and (8, 23).
7. (Higher tier only) A velocity rises steadily from 4 m/s to 10 m/s over 6 s. Find the acceleration and the distance travelled.
8. (Higher tier only) For s = 3t², find the average rate of change of s between t = 1 and t = 2.
9. (Higher tier only) For s = 3t², show that the chord from t = 2 to t = 2 + h has gradient 12 + 3h, and state what the gradient approaches as h becomes very small.
10. (Higher tier only) A graph shows earnings rate in £ per hour. It rises in a straight line from 0 to 30 over 4 hours. Find the total earned.
11. A ramp has gradient 3/4. How far does it rise over a horizontal distance of 20 m?

### Answers

1. Gradient 262 ÷ 200 = **1.31 dollars per pound**. £45 gives 45 × 1.31 = **$58.95**.
2. 68 = 1.8C + 32, so 1.8C = 36 and C = **20 °C**.
3. y = 7x, so y = 7 × 9 = **63**.
4. xy = 36, so y = 36 ÷ 4 = **9**.
5. 20 minutes = 1/3 hour, so speed = 15 ÷ (1/3) = **45 km/h**.
6. (23 - 5) ÷ (8 - 2) = 18 ÷ 6 = **3**.
7. Acceleration = (10 - 4) ÷ 6 = **1 m/s²**. Distance = ½ × (4 + 10) × 6 = **42 m**.
8. s(2) = 12 and s(1) = 3, so (12 - 3) ÷ (2 - 1) = **9**.
9. (3(2 + h)² - 12) ÷ h = (12h + 3h²) ÷ h = **12 + 3h**. As h gets very small, the gradient approaches **12**.
10. Triangle area = ½ × 4 × 30 = **£60**.
11. 3/4 × 20 = **15 m**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving units off gradients, areas and conversions.
- Reading a scale wrongly when each small square is not 1 unit.
- Writing "constant speed" for a horizontal distance-time line.
- Using 20 minutes as 20 in a km/h calculation instead of 1/3 hour.
- Choosing two points too close together when finding the gradient of a tangent, which magnifies reading errors.
- Counting the base of a trapezium as a parallel side, or forgetting the ½.
- Not showing the two points used to find a gradient, so a small misread cannot be followed.
- Writing "the area is the distance" without checking that the vertical axis is velocity or speed.
- Stating an estimate without saying whether it is an over- or under-estimate when the question asks.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does the gradient of a distance-time graph show?**
The gradient of a distance-time graph is the speed, or velocity if direction matters. A horizontal line means the object is stationary, not moving at constant speed.

**What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent?**
The area under a velocity-time graph is the distance travelled. Finding it, along with using the gradient as acceleration, is Higher tier only in OCR GCSE Mathematics J560.

**What is the difference between a chord and a tangent on a curve?**
A chord joins two points on the curve, and its gradient gives the average rate of change between them. A tangent touches the curve at one point, and its gradient gives the instantaneous rate of change there.

**Is every straight-line graph a direct proportion graph?**
No. A direct proportion graph is a straight line through the origin, so a straight line that misses the origin, such as F = 1.8C + 32, is linear but not proportional.
