This set covers **section 3.2** (SL and HL) of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: right-angled triangle trigonometry, the sine rule, the cosine rule and the area of a triangle. Each question says whether it is calculator-free or calculator allowed. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures; angles are in degrees. Revise first with the [triangle trigonometry study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-2/) and the [triangle trigonometry revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-2-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator allowed) Triangle ABC has a right angle at B, AB = 9 cm and angle BAC = 34°.

**(a)** Find BC. **[1]**
**(b)** Find AC. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator-free) A triangle has two sides of 5 cm and 8 cm with an included angle of 120°.

**(a)** Find the exact length of the third side. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact area of the triangle. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator-free) A triangle has sides 7 cm, 8 cm and 13 cm. Show that its largest angle is 120°. **[3]**

**4.** (calculator allowed) In triangle ABC, angle A = 48°, angle B = 71° and AB = 14 cm. Find BC. **[3]**

**5.** (calculator allowed) A triangle has sides 6 cm and 9 cm and area 18 cm². Find the two possible sizes of the angle between these sides. **[3]**

**6.** (calculator-free) A triangle has sides 6 cm and x cm with an included angle of 60°, and its area is 18√3 cm². Find x. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) In quadrilateral ABCD, AB = 7 cm, BC = 10 cm, angle ABC = 80°, CD = 6 cm and angle ACD = 50°.

**(a)** Find AC. **[2]**
**(b)** Find AD. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of ABCD. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) In triangle PQR, PQ = 11 cm, PR = 15 cm and QR = 8 cm.

**(a)** Find the largest angle of the triangle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the perpendicular distance from Q to the line PR. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator-free) In triangle ABC, AB = x cm, AC = (x + 2) cm, angle BAC = 60° and BC = 2√7 cm.

**(a)** Show that x² + 2x − 24 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Find x. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact area of the triangle. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator-free) The sides of a triangle are in the ratio 4 : 5 : 6.

**(a)** Show that the cosine of the largest angle is 1/8. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the cosine of the smallest angle is 3/4. **[2]**
**(c)** Use cos 2θ = 2cos²θ − 1 to show that the largest angle is twice the smallest. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) Two sides of a triangle are 12 cm long and the angle between them is θ. The area of the triangle is 50 cm².

**(a)** Find the two possible values of θ. **[3]**
**(b)** For the obtuse value of θ, find the length of the third side. **[2]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) In triangle XYZ, angle XYZ = 32°, XZ = 15 cm and YZ = 9 cm.

**(a)** Find angle YXZ. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why there is only one possible value for angle YXZ. **[1]**
**(c)** Find XY. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** BC = 9 tan 34° = 6.07 cm [1]
**(b)** AC = 9/cos 34° = 10.9 cm [1]

**2. (a)** c² = 25 + 64 − 80 cos 120° = 89 + 40 = 129 [1], c = √129 cm [1]
**(b)** ½ × 5 × 8 × sin 120° [1] = 20 × √3/2 = 10√3 cm² [1]

**3.** The largest angle is opposite the 13 cm side [1]. cos θ = (49 + 64 − 169)/(2 × 7 × 8) = −56/112 [1] = −½, so θ = 120° [1]

**4.** Angle C = 180° − 48° − 71° = 61° [1]. BC/sin 48° = 14/sin 61° [1], so BC = 11.9 cm [1]

**5.** ½ × 6 × 9 × sin C = 18, so sin C = 2/3 [1]. C = 41.8° [1] or C = 138° [1]

**6.** ½ × 6 × x × √3/2 = 18√3 [1], so (3√3/2)x = 18√3 and x = 12 [1]

**7. (a)** AC² = 49 + 100 − 140 cos 80° [1], AC = 11.2 cm [1]
**(b)** AD² = AC² + 36 − 12 × AC × cos 50° [1], AD = 8.63 cm [1]
**(c)** Area ABC = ½ × 7 × 10 × sin 80° = 34.5 [1]; area ACD = ½ × AC × 6 × sin 50° = 25.7 [1]. Total 60.1 cm² [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the stored value of AC (11.166…) in (b) and (c); using 11.2 changes the final answers in the third figure.

**8. (a)** The largest angle is Q, opposite PR = 15 [1]. cos Q = (121 + 64 − 225)/(2 × 11 × 8) = −40/176, so Q = 103° (103.1°) [1]
**(b)** ½ × 11 × 8 × sin Q [1] = 42.8 cm² [1]
**(c)** ½ × 15 × h = 42.85 [1], so h = 5.71 cm [1]

**9. (a)** (2√7)² = x² + (x + 2)² − 2x(x + 2) cos 60° [1]. 28 = x² + x² + 4x + 4 − x² − 2x [1] = x² + 2x + 4, so x² + 2x − 24 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (x + 6)(x − 4) = 0 [1]; x > 0, so x = 4 [1]
**(c)** ½ × 4 × 6 × √3/2 [1] = 6√3 cm² [1]

**10. (a)** Take sides 4k, 5k, 6k; the largest angle is opposite 6k [1]. cos θ = (16k² + 25k² − 36k²)/(2 × 4k × 5k) [1] = 5k²/40k² = 1/8 [1]
**(b)** cos α = (25k² + 36k² − 16k²)/(2 × 5k × 6k) [1] = 45/60 = 3/4 [1]
**(c)** cos 2α = 2(3/4)² − 1 = 18/16 − 1 = 1/8 [1]. Both 2α and θ are between 0° and 180° with the same cosine, so θ = 2α [1]

**11. (a)** ½ × 12 × 12 × sin θ = 50 [1], so sin θ = 100/144 [1]. θ = 44.0° or 136° [1]
**(b)** c² = 144 + 144 − 288 cos 136.0° [1], c = 22.3 cm [1]

**12. (a)** sin X / 9 = sin 32° / 15 [1], so sin X = 0.3180 and X = 18.5° [1]
**(b)** X is opposite YZ = 9, which is shorter than XZ = 15, so X < 32° and must be acute [1]
**(c)** Angle XZY = 180° − 32° − 18.54° = 129.46° [1]. XY/sin 129.46° = 15/sin 32° [1], XY = 21.9 cm [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Pairing sides and angles wrongly in the sine rule.
- Sign errors with cos 120° and other obtuse angles.
- Using the sine rule for the largest angle.
- Missing the second angle in an area question.
- Rounding intermediate values and carrying them forward.
- Decimals on calculator-free questions.
