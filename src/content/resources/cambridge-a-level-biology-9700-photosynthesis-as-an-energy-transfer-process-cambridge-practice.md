These questions cover section 13.1 (Photosynthesis as an energy transfer process) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. This is A Level content, examined in Paper 4; Question 11 also practises Paper 5 planning skills. All data are invented for the exercise. ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is made in non-cyclic photophosphorylation but **not** in cyclic photophosphorylation?

A: ATP

B: Reduced NADP and oxygen

C: A proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane

D: Photoactivated chlorophyll **[1]**

**2.** State the site, within a chloroplast, of the light-dependent stage and of the light-independent stage. **[2]**

**3.** In a chromatogram the solvent front is 92 mm from the origin. A yellow spot has moved 46 mm. Reference values for this solvent are: carotene 0.94, xanthophyll 0.50, chlorophyll a 0.42, chlorophyll b 0.31. Calculate the Rf value and identify the pigment. **[2]**

**4.** At a wavelength of 480 nm the rate of photosynthesis is high, although chlorophyll a absorbs little light at this wavelength. Explain this observation. **[2]**

**5.** Name the enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide in the Calvin cycle and state the product of the reaction it catalyses. **[2]**

**6.** During photophosphorylation, where is the concentration of protons highest?

A: The stroma

B: The thylakoid space

C: The space between the two membranes of the envelope

D: The cytoplasm of the palisade cell **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** An electron micrograph of a chloroplast shows many grana and several large starch grains.

**(a)** Describe how the structure of the thylakoids is related to their function. **[3]**

**(b)** The same leaf was kept in darkness for 48 hours. Suggest why its chloroplasts then showed far fewer starch grains. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how light energy absorbed in the thylakoids is used to synthesise ATP. **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** Compare cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation. **[3]**

**(b)** A herbicide stops photosystem II from working. Predict and explain its effect on oxygen release and on ATP production by isolated chloroplasts in light. **[2]**

**10.** Algal cells were kept in bright light with plenty of carbon dioxide. At 60 seconds the light was switched off. The concentrations of GP and RuBP were measured.

| Time / s | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GP / arbitrary units | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 3.4 | 4.1 |
| RuBP / arbitrary units | 1.5 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 0.6 | 0.2 |

**(a)** Describe the changes in GP and RuBP after the light was switched off. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain these changes. **[3]**

**11.** A student wants to identify the pigments in a leaf by paper chromatography.

**(a)** Describe how the student should prepare and run the chromatogram. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the student must compare Rf values with reference values obtained using the same solvent. **[1]**

**12.**

**(a)** State how many molecules of carbon dioxide must be fixed for the Calvin cycle to produce one triose phosphate molecule that can leave the cycle. **[1]**

**(b)** A hexose sugar has six carbon atoms and is made from two TP molecules. State how many turns of the Calvin cycle (one CO₂ fixed per turn) are needed for one hexose. **[1]**

**(c)** The Calvin cycle does not use light directly. Explain why it stops within a few minutes of a plant being placed in darkness. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Light-dependent stage: thylakoids (thylakoid membranes of the grana) [1]. Light-independent stage: stroma [1].

**3.** Rf = 46 ÷ 92 = **0.50** [1]. The pigment is **xanthophyll** [1].

**4.** Accessory pigments, such as carotene, xanthophyll or chlorophyll b, absorb light at 480 nm [1]. They pass the absorbed energy to chlorophyll a at the reaction centre, so it can still be used for photosynthesis [1].

**5.** Rubisco [1]. Two molecules of glycerate 3-phosphate (GP), each with three carbon atoms [1].

**6.** B [1]

**7.**

**(a)** Thylakoid membranes are stacked into grana, giving a large surface area that holds many photosystems, so more light is absorbed [1]. The membranes contain the electron transport chain and ATP synthase needed for the light-dependent stage [1]. The small, enclosed thylakoid space lets a proton gradient build up quickly for ATP synthesis [1].

**(b)** In darkness no ATP or reduced NADP is made, so no TP is made and no new starch is formed [1]. The stored starch is hydrolysed to sugars, used in respiration or exported from the leaf [1].

**8.** Light is absorbed by pigments and chlorophyll is photoactivated, emitting high-energy electrons [1]. The electrons release energy as they pass along the electron transport chain [1]. This energy transfers protons from the stroma into the thylakoid space, creating a proton gradient [1]. Protons return to the stroma by facilitated diffusion through ATP synthase, which provides energy to make ATP from ADP and phosphate [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Similarity: both involve photoactivation of chlorophyll and make ATP using an electron transport chain [1]. Cyclic uses PSI only, while non-cyclic uses PSII and PSI [1]. Only non-cyclic involves photolysis of water, releasing oxygen, and makes reduced NADP [1].

**(b)** Oxygen release stops, because photolysis of water by the oxygen-evolving complex is linked to PSII [1]. Some ATP is still made, because cyclic photophosphorylation through PSI still works [1].

**10.**

**(a)** GP rises, from 2.0 to 4.1 arbitrary units by 120 s [1]. RuBP falls, from 1.5 to 0.2 arbitrary units [1].

**(b)** No ATP or reduced NADP is made in the dark [1]. So GP cannot be reduced to TP, but RuBP is still combined with CO₂ by rubisco, so GP accumulates [1]. RuBP is used up in fixation but cannot be regenerated, because little TP is made and no ATP is available [1].

**11.**

**(a)** Grind leaves with sand and a small volume of solvent, such as propanone, to extract the pigments [1]. Draw an origin line in pencil near the bottom of the paper [1]. Apply the extract to one point repeatedly, drying between applications, to give a small, concentrated spot [1]. Stand the paper in solvent in a covered container with the origin above the solvent surface; remove it before the solvent reaches the top and mark the solvent front at once [1].

**(b)** A pigment's Rf value depends on the solvent used, so values from another solvent would not match [1].

**12.**

**(a)** Three [1].

**(b)** Six [1].

**(c)** The Calvin cycle needs ATP and reduced NADP, which are made only in the light-dependent stage [1]. Once the existing supply is used up, GP can no longer be reduced and RuBP can no longer be regenerated, so the cycle stops [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming "GP" without saying that **two** molecules form from each CO₂ fixed.
- Describing proton movement the wrong way across the thylakoid membrane.
- In "compare" questions, giving only differences with no similarity.
- Explaining the light-off experiment for GP but not for RuBP, or the reverse.
- Forgetting that oxygen in photosynthesis comes from water.
- Giving an Rf value with units, or greater than 1.
