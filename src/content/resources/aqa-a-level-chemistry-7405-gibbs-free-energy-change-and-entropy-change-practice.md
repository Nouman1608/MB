These questions cover section **3.1.8.2 Gibbs free-energy change, ΔG, and entropy change, ΔS** of the AQA A-level Chemistry specification (7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Gibbs free-energy and entropy study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-gibbs-free-energy-change-and-entropy-change/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-gibbs-free-energy-change-and-entropy-change-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by entropy. **[2]**

**2.** Predict whether the entropy change is positive or negative for each process, giving a reason.

**(a)** Melting of a solid wax **[1]**

**(b)** 2NO₂(g) → N₂O₄(g) **[1]**

**(c)** NH₄Cl(s) → NH₃(g) + HCl(g) **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the entropy change for C₂H₄(g) + H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g), given S in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: C₂H₄ 219.6, H₂ 130.6, C₂H₆ 229.2. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the entropy change for 2H₂O₂(l) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g), given S in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: H₂O₂(l) 109.6, H₂O(l) 69.9, O₂(g) 205.0. Comment on the sign. **[3]**

**5.** For the hydrogenation in question 3, ΔH = −137 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔG at 298 K and state whether the reaction is feasible. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the temperature at which the reaction ceases to be feasible. **[2]**

**6.** For CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), ΔH = +178 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +160.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔG at 298 K and state whether the reaction is feasible. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the minimum temperature at which the decomposition is feasible. **[2]**

**7.** A reaction has ΔH = −200 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +50 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. State, with a reason, whether it is feasible at all temperatures. **[2]**

**8.** A student calculates ΔG for question 6(a) as 178 − 298 × 160.4 = −47 621 kJ mol⁻¹. Identify the error and give the correct value. **[2]**

**9.** The table gives ΔG for a reaction at different temperatures.

| T / K | 400 | 600 | 800 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ΔG / kJ mol⁻¹ | +128 | +92 | +56 | +20 |

**(a)** Calculate the gradient of a plot of ΔG against T. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence find ΔS, in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔH. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the temperature at which the reaction becomes feasible. **[1]**

**10.** Ammonium nitrate dissolves spontaneously in water although the process is endothermic. Explain. **[3]**

**11.** A reaction has ΔG = −80 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K but does not seem to happen when the reactants are mixed. Suggest two reasons. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why, for a reaction with negative ΔH and negative ΔS, there is a temperature above which the reaction is no longer feasible. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Entropy is a measure of the disorder of a system [1]; the number of ways in which the particles and their energy can be arranged [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Positive: the particles become more disordered as the solid becomes a liquid [1]

**(b)** Negative: two moles of gas become one mole [1]

**(c)** Positive: a solid forms two moles of gas [1]. **[3]**

**3.** ΔS = 229.2 − (219.6 + 130.6) [1] = **−121.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** ΔS = [2(69.9) + 205.0] − 2(109.6) [1] = 344.8 − 219.2 = **+125.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]; the sign is positive because a gas is formed from liquids [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** ΔS = −0.1210 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]; ΔG = −137 − 298 × (−0.1210) = −137 + 36.1 = **−100.9 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; negative, so feasible [1]

**(b)** T = ΔH ÷ ΔS = −137 ÷ −0.1210 [1] = **1130 K** (1132 K) [1]. **[5]**

**6. (a)** ΔS = 0.1604 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]; ΔG = 178 − 298 × 0.1604 = 178 − 47.8 = **+130 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; positive, so not feasible at 298 K [1]

**(b)** T = 178 ÷ 0.1604 [1] = **1110 K** [1]. **[5]**

**7.** Yes [1]; ΔH is negative and ΔS is positive, so −TΔS is also negative and ΔG is negative at every temperature [1]. **[2]**

**8.** The entropy change was not converted from J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ to kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]; the correct value is 178 − 298 × 0.1604 = **+130 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Gradient = (20 − 128) ÷ (1000 − 400) [1] = **−0.18 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** ΔS = −gradient = **+180 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** At 400 K: 128 = ΔH − 400 × 0.18 [1]; ΔH = **+200 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(d)** T = 200 ÷ 0.18 = **1111 K** [1]. **[6]**

**10.** The dissolving is endothermic, so ΔH is positive [1]; the entropy increases because the ordered solid becomes ions free to move in solution, so ΔS is positive [1]; at room temperature TΔS is greater than ΔH, so ΔG is negative [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The reaction has a high activation energy, so the rate is very slow at 298 K [1]; the reactants may need a catalyst, or a source of energy to start (feasible does not mean fast) [1]. **[2]**

**12.** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; with ΔS negative, −TΔS is positive and grows as T increases [1]; at low T, ΔH (negative) dominates, so ΔG is negative [1]; above T = ΔH ÷ ΔS, the −TΔS term is bigger than ΔH and ΔG becomes positive [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using ΔS in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ with ΔH in kJ mol⁻¹.
- Using a temperature in °C.
- Not multiplying entropy values by the coefficients.
- Reactants minus products for ΔS.
- Treating feasibility as a prediction of rate.
