## Section A

**1.** A student investigates how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis in a piece of pondweed by counting the bubbles of gas released in one minute at different distances from a lamp. State the independent variable, the dependent variable and **one** controlled variable. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how the student could control **two** variables, other than light intensity, that would affect the number of bubbles. **[4]**

**3.** The student needs 20 cm³ of each of three solutions, of 25 %, 50 % and 75 % concentration, made from a 100 % stock solution and distilled water. Give the volume of stock solution and the volume of distilled water for each. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why a glass tank of water is placed between the lamp and the pondweed. **[2]**

**5.** A cylinder of potato has a mass of 2.50 g before and 2.15 g after being left in a salt solution. Calculate the percentage change in mass. **[2]**

**6.** Give **two** reasons why a gas syringe is a better way of measuring the rate of a reaction that releases oxygen than counting bubbles. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Plan an investigation into the effect of temperature on the rate at which amylase breaks down starch. You may use starch solution, amylase solution, iodine solution in potassium iodide, a white tile, a water bath and any standard laboratory equipment. **[6]**

**8.** A student investigates the effect of pH on the activity of catalase. Pieces of liver of different sizes are added to 10 cm³ of buffer solution at each of pH 4, 7 and 10, followed by hydrogen peroxide. The student measures the height of the foam after 5 minutes, using a ruler, and does the experiment once at each pH.

Evaluate whether this method is appropriate to meet the aim. Suggest an improvement for each weakness you identify. **[5]**

**9.** In an enzyme investigation, a student sets up a tube that is the same as the others except that the enzyme has been boiled before use. Explain the purpose of this tube. **[2]**

**10.** State **two** reasons for carrying out a pilot study before an investigation. **[2]**

**11.** A student investigates the effect of fertiliser concentration on the growth of seedlings. The seedlings vary in their starting height and are all grown on one windowsill. Describe how the student could reduce the effect of these two factors on the results. **[3]**

**12.** An investigation uses 3 % hydrogen peroxide, a cork borer and a sharp knife to cut cylinders of potato. Identify **two** hazards and, for each, suggest a way of reducing the risk. **[4]**

## Answers

**How these answers work.** Marking points are shown in brackets. The mark total for each question is the sum of the points. Alternative wording that carries the same meaning is credited.

**1.** Independent variable: light intensity (or distance of the lamp from the pondweed) [1]. Dependent variable: number of bubbles released per minute [1]. Controlled variable, any one: temperature; carbon dioxide concentration; same piece of pondweed or same length; same time period [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Temperature [1]: use a water tank as a heat shield, or a water bath, and check the temperature with a thermometer [1]. Carbon dioxide concentration [1]: add the same mass of sodium hydrogencarbonate, or use the same volume of hydrogencarbonate solution, in each test [1]. Other acceptable: same length of pondweed from the same plant; same time to settle before counting. **[4]**

**3.** 25 %: 5 cm³ stock and 15 cm³ water [1]. 50 %: 10 cm³ stock and 10 cm³ water [1]. 75 %: 15 cm³ stock and 5 cm³ water [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check that the two volumes add to 20 cm³ in each row.

**4.** The lamp gives out heat as well as light [1]. The water absorbs the heat, so the temperature of the pondweed does not rise as the lamp is moved closer (temperature, which also affects the rate, is controlled) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Change = 2.15 − 2.50 = −0.35 g [1]. Percentage change = −0.35 ÷ 2.50 × 100 = **−14 %** (a decrease of 14.0 %) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two [1 each]: it measures the volume in cm³ directly; bubbles vary in size, so counting them is not a true measure of volume; it can be read to a known resolution; it can record a volume at fixed times, giving a rate. **[2]**

**7.** Any six [1 each]: independent variable stated (temperature) and a range of at least five temperatures, for example 20 to 60 °C; dependent variable stated and how it is measured (time taken for starch to be broken down, tested with iodine on a white tile at fixed intervals); enzyme and starch solutions each warmed to the test temperature in the water bath before mixing; the same volume and concentration of enzyme and starch each time; pH controlled with a buffer solution; temperature monitored with a thermometer or thermostat; repeat at least three times at each temperature and calculate a mean; results presented as rate (1 ÷ time) against temperature; a hazard identified (hot water, or iodine staining) with a precaution. **[6]**

**8.** Any five [1 each]. Appropriate features: the independent variable, pH, is changed using buffers [1]. Weaknesses with improvements: the liver pieces differ in size, so surface area and the amount of enzyme differ [1]; use pieces of the same mass and surface area, cut with a cork borer to the same length [1]. Foam height is not a good measure of the amount of oxygen released, as bubbles may collapse or overflow [1]; collect the gas in a gas syringe and measure the volume per minute [1]. Only one test at each pH and only three pH values [1]; repeat three times, use a mean, and use at least five pH values such as 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 [1]. **[5]**

**9.** It is a control [1]. Boiling denatures the enzyme, so if no reaction happens in this tube, the changes in the other tubes were caused by active enzyme and not by the substrate or the conditions alone [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any two [1 each]: to check that the range of the independent variable is suitable; to check that the measuring method works and the apparatus is suitable; to find a sensible time or volume to use; to find problems before the main experiment. **[2]**

**11.** Starting height: measure the height of every seedling before the investigation and use seedlings of the same height, or measure the growth (increase in height) rather than the final height [1]. Position: place the seedlings in a growth cabinet or rotate their positions regularly, so that light and temperature are the same for all [1]. Use several seedlings per concentration and calculate a mean, to reduce the effect of natural variation [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Cork borer or knife is sharp [1]: cut onto a cutting tile, cutting away from the hand [1]. Hydrogen peroxide can irritate the skin and eyes [1]: wear eye protection and gloves, and wash any splashes off the skin with water [1]. Other acceptable: wet floor from spills, which should be wiped up. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: giving "the rate" without saying what is counted or measured.
- Question 3: giving volumes that do not add up to the total volume needed.
- Question 5: forgetting the sign, or dividing by the final mass instead of the initial mass.
- Question 7: describing the method without values, or without a control of pH.
- Question 8: criticising the result and not the method. Name the weakness, say its effect and give an improvement.
- Question 12: writing "be careful" instead of a specific precaution.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much detail does a six-mark planning question need?**
Enough to be repeated by another person: the variable you change and measure, the values and the apparatus, how you control the main variables, repeats, and a safety point.

**Do I need to know a particular enzyme practical?**
No. The question gives a context that is unfamiliar. Use the biology you know to decide the variables.
