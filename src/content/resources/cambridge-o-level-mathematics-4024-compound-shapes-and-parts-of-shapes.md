This guide covers section 5.5, Compound shapes and parts of shapes, in the Mensuration topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to carry out calculations and solve problems involving the perimeters and areas of compound shapes and parts of shapes, and the surface areas and volumes of compound solids and parts of solids. Answers may be asked for in terms of π. The syllabus example is to find the surface area and volume of a frustum. After this guide, use the [compound shapes and parts of shapes revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-revision-notes/) and the [compound shapes and parts of shapes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-practice/).

## What section 5.5 asks for

1. Break a compound plane shape into simple pieces, or subtract a piece from a larger shape, to find the area and perimeter.
2. Find the area and perimeter of parts of circles, such as semicircles, quarter circles and segments.
3. Do the same in three dimensions with compound solids, such as a cylinder with a hemisphere on top.
4. Work with parts of solids, such as hemispheres, half cylinders and frustums.

The pieces use the formulas from the earlier sections on area, circles and solids. This section is about assembling them correctly.

## Compound plane shapes

Choose between two methods.

- **Addition**: split the shape into rectangles, triangles and so on, then add the areas.
- **Subtraction**: take a larger simple shape and subtract the part that is missing.

For the **perimeter**, add the lengths of the outside edges only. Do not include any lines used to split the shape up. Be careful to work out missing lengths, using the fact that opposite sides of a rectilinear shape must balance.

### Worked example 1

An L-shape is formed by removing a 4 cm by 3 cm rectangle from the corner of a 10 cm by 8 cm rectangle. Find its area and perimeter.

```
area = 10 × 8 − 4 × 3 = 80 − 12 = 68 cm²
perimeter = 10 + 8 + 10 + 8 = 36 cm
```

The perimeter is the same as that of the full rectangle. The removed corner replaces a 4 cm and a 3 cm edge on the outside with a 4 cm and a 3 cm edge on the inside of the notch, which has the same total length.

### Worked example 2

A shape is a rectangle 12 cm by 6 cm with a semicircle on one of the 6 cm sides. Find its area and perimeter.

```
semicircle radius = 3 cm
area = 12 × 6 + 1/2 × π × 3² = 72 + 4.5π = 86.1 cm²
perimeter = 12 + 12 + 6 + (1/2 × 2π × 3) = 30 + 3π = 39.4 cm
```

The side of the rectangle where the semicircle is attached is not part of the outside, so it is not included in the perimeter. Instead, the curve is included.

### Worked example 3

A circle of radius 5 cm is drawn inside a square of side 10 cm so that it touches all four sides. Find the shaded area outside the circle but inside the square.

```
square = 100 cm²
circle = π × 25 = 78.54 cm²
shaded area = 100 − 25π = 21.5 cm²
```

## Parts of circles: segments

A **segment** is the region between a chord and an arc. To find its area, subtract the triangle formed by the chord and two radii from the sector.

### Worked example 4

A chord subtends a right angle at the centre of a circle of radius 8 cm. Find the area of the minor segment.

```
sector = 90/360 × π × 8² = 16π = 50.27 cm²
triangle = 1/2 × 8 × 8 = 32 cm²
segment = 16π − 32 = 18.3 cm²
```

For other angles, the triangle's area is 1/2 r² sin θ, which uses the sine formula for area of a triangle.

## Compound solids

A compound solid is made by joining simple solids. Volumes add. For surface area, include only the surfaces that are on the outside: when two solids are joined, the joined faces disappear from the total.

### Worked example 5

A solid is a cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 4 cm on top. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
volume = cylinder + hemisphere
       = π × 4² × 10 + 2/3 × π × 4³
       = 160π + 128π/3 = 202.67π = 637 cm³
surface area = base + curved cylinder + curved hemisphere
             = π × 16 + 2π × 4 × 10 + 2π × 16
             = 16π + 80π + 32π = 128π = 402 cm²
```

The top circle of the cylinder and the flat face of the hemisphere are joined together inside the solid, so neither is counted.

### Worked example 6

A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 5 cm with a cone of height 4 cm on top, with the same radius. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
slant height of cone = √(3² + 4²) = 5 cm
volume = π × 9 × 5 + 1/3 × π × 9 × 4 = 45π + 12π = 57π = 179 cm³
surface area = base π × 9 + cylinder curved 2π × 3 × 5 + cone curved π × 3 × 5
             = 9π + 30π + 15π = 54π = 170 cm²
```

## Parts of solids

### Half a cylinder

### Worked example 7

A solid is half of a cylinder of radius 6 cm and length 10 cm, cut along its length through the axis. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
volume = 1/2 × π × 6² × 10 = 180π = 565 cm³
surface area = curved part + flat rectangle + two half-circles
             = 1/2 × 2π × 6 × 10 + 12 × 10 + π × 36
             = 60π + 120 + 36π = 421.6 cm²
```

### Hollow shapes

### Worked example 8

A pipe has outer radius 5 cm, inner radius 4 cm and length 20 cm. Find the volume of the material.

```
cross-section = π × 5² − π × 4² = 9π
volume = 9π × 20 = 180π = 565 cm³
```

## Frustums

A **frustum** is what is left of a cone (or pyramid) when a smaller, similar cone is cut off the top by a plane parallel to the base. You find its volume by subtracting the removed cone from the original cone.

### Worked example 9

A cone has base radius 6 cm and height 12 cm. A smaller cone of base radius 3 cm and height 6 cm is cut off the top, leaving a frustum. Find its volume and total surface area.

```
big cone volume = 1/3 × π × 36 × 12 = 144π
small cone volume = 1/3 × π × 9 × 6 = 18π
frustum volume = 144π − 18π = 126π = 396 cm³
```

For the surface area, find the slant heights.

```
big cone slant height = √(6² + 12²) = √180 = 13.42 cm
small cone slant height = √(3² + 6²) = √45 = 6.708 cm
curved surface of frustum = π × 6 × 13.42 − π × 3 × 6.708 = 252.9 − 63.2 = 189.7 cm²
top circle = π × 9 = 28.27 cm², bottom circle = π × 36 = 113.1 cm²
total surface area = 189.7 + 28.3 + 113.1 = 331 cm²
```

Because the small cone is an enlargement of the big cone by scale factor 1/2, its volume is (1/2)³ = 1/8 of the big cone's, and the frustum is 7/8 of the whole: 7/8 × 144π = 126π. This is a good check.

## Common mistakes

- Including internal lines in a perimeter, or leaving out the curved part.
- Forgetting to subtract hidden faces in the surface area of a compound solid.
- Counting the flat face of a hemisphere when it is joined to another solid.
- Finding a frustum's curved surface area without subtracting the small cone.
- Using the vertical height instead of the slant height in a cone's curved area.
- Using the diameter for the radius.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the area: split the shape and show each part.
- Write the volume of each part, then add or subtract.
- Check the list of surfaces for the total area, so none is missed.
- Keep π in your working when asked for the answer in terms of π.
