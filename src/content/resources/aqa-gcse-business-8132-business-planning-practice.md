These questions cover section 3.1.6 Business planning of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Business planning study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-business-planning/) and the [Business planning revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-business-planning-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Identify **two** reasons, other than raising finance, why a business creates a business plan. **[2]**

**2.** A sandwich shop has these costs: bread, rent of the shop, sandwich packaging, insurance. Identify the **two** fixed costs. **[2]**

**3.** A theatre sells 1,250 tickets at £8 each. Calculate its revenue. **[1]**

**4.** A florist has fixed costs of £2,300 a month. The variable cost of each bouquet is £9. Calculate total costs in a month when it makes 400 bouquets. **[2]**

**5.** A business plan forecasts revenue of £18,000 and total costs of £19,350. Calculate the result and state whether it is a profit or a loss. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** way a business plan can help an entrepreneur when setting up a new business. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Lowther Lettering makes hand-painted shop signs. The owner wants a £20,000 bank loan and has prepared a business plan. The plan includes these forecasts:

| Item | Forecast |
|---|---|
| Price per sign | £150 |
| Variable cost per sign | £45 |
| Fixed costs per month | £6,300 |
| Signs sold per month | 80 |

**(a)** Calculate the forecast monthly profit. **[4]**
**(b)** The bank asks what would happen if sales were 25% lower than forecast. Calculate the profit or loss at this level of sales. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the bank should lend to Lowther Lettering on the basis of this plan. **[6]**

**8.** Explain why a business plan should be reviewed after a business starts trading. **[4]**

**9.** Hollin Café's plan shows revenue of £32,000, profit of £4,400 and fixed costs of £12,000 for a period in which it expects 4,000 customers.

**(a)** Calculate total costs. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the variable cost per customer. **[2]**

**10.** Analyse **one** drawback of business planning for an entrepreneur who wants to launch an ice-cream van before the start of summer. **[4]**

**11.** Ellery Events is a new wedding-planning business set up by three partners. Analyse how detailing in the plan how the functions of the business will be organised could help Ellery Events. **[6]**

**12.** The owner of Pawprint Grooming is opening a dog-grooming salon using her own savings and a small bank loan. Evaluate whether her business plan is more useful to her or to the bank. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: importance in setting up a new business [1]; setting objectives [1]; detailing how the functions of the business will be organised. **[2]**

**2.** Rent of the shop [1] and insurance [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Bread and packaging rise with every sandwich made, so they are variable.

**3.** 1,250 × £8 = £10,000 [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Total variable costs = 400 × £9 = £3,600 [1]. Total costs = £3,600 + £2,300 = £5,900 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** £18,000 − £19,350 = −£1,350 [1], so it is a **loss** of £1,350 [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Writing the plan makes the entrepreneur research the market and work out costs before spending money [1]. This may show that prices are too low to cover costs, or that demand is weaker than expected [1], so the problem can be fixed, or the idea dropped, before savings or borrowed money are lost [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Revenue = 80 × £150 = £12,000 [1]. Total variable costs = 80 × £45 = £3,600 [1]. Total costs = £3,600 + £6,300 = £9,900 [1]. Profit = £12,000 − £9,900 = £2,100 [1].
**(b)** 25% of 80 = 20, so sales are 60 signs [1]. Revenue = 60 × £150 = £9,000; total costs = (60 × £45) + £6,300 = £2,700 + £6,300 = £9,000 [1]. Profit = £9,000 − £9,000 = £0, so the business makes neither a profit nor a loss [1].
**(c)** The forecast profit of £2,100 a month is £25,200 a year, which suggests the owner could make repayments on a £20,000 loan [1]. Even if sales fall by a quarter, the business still covers all its costs [1], which gives the bank some reassurance [1]. However, at 60 signs there is no profit left to make loan repayments, unless they are already counted in fixed costs [1], and the figures are only forecasts for a business with no trading record [1]. The bank should lend if the plan shows evidence behind the 80-sign forecast, such as orders already agreed with shops, but might lend a smaller amount or ask for security if it does not [1]. **[13]**

**8.** Forecasts in the plan were estimates made before trading began [1], so actual sales and costs are likely to differ [1]. Comparing actual results with the plan shows where the business is behind its objectives [1], so the owner can act early, for example by cutting costs or changing promotion, and the plan stays useful rather than misleading [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Total costs = £32,000 − £4,400 = £27,600 [1].
**(b)** Total variable costs = £27,600 − £12,000 = £15,600 [1]. Variable cost per customer = £15,600 ÷ 4,000 = £3.90 [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Researching and writing a plan takes time [1]. The ice-cream van makes most of its sales in summer [1], so if planning delays the launch past the start of the season, the business misses its busiest weeks [1] and loses revenue that it cannot recover until the next year, which may leave it short of cash [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" rewards a developed chain applied to the business. One well-developed drawback is better than three listed ones.

**11.** With three partners, there is a risk that two people do the same job while another task is missed [1]. The plan can give each partner a clear function, for example one handles marketing to couples, one manages suppliers such as venues and caterers (operations), and one controls the budget (finance) [1]. Each partner then knows what they are responsible for [1], so decisions are made faster and disagreements are reduced [1]. Clear roles also show what the partners cannot cover, such as accounts, so they can plan to buy in help [1]. As a result, weddings are more likely to run smoothly, which protects Ellery Events' reputation in a business that depends on recommendations [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Useful to the owner:** writing the plan makes her research local demand for dog grooming and the prices rivals charge [1], so she can set a price that covers her costs [1]. Because she is risking her own savings, testing the idea on paper first protects money she cannot easily replace [1]. Objectives in the plan, such as a number of bookings a week, give her targets to check progress against once she opens [1]. **Useful to the bank:** the bank has no trading history to judge, so the plan's forecasts are its main evidence that the loan can be repaid [1]. Because the loan is small, the bank's risk is limited, and it may rely partly on her own investment as a sign of commitment [1]. **Judgement:** the plan is more useful to the owner [1], because most of the money at risk is hers and she will use the plan every week, while the bank uses it once to make a decision [1]. This would change if the loan were large, because the bank would then study the forecasts much more closely [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming raising finance as the only reason for a plan.
- Multiplying fixed costs by the number of units.
- Using the variable cost per unit as if it were total variable costs.
- Writing a negative profit without saying it is a loss.
- Forgetting to work out the new quantity first when sales change by a percentage.
- Treating forecasts as certain in an evaluation.
- Giving benefits for "the business" without saying which reader gains.
