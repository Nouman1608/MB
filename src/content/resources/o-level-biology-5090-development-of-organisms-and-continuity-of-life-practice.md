## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Describe a haploid nucleus and a diploid nucleus. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many pairs of chromosomes are in a human diploid cell. **[1]**

**2.** A plant species has a diploid number of 26.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a male gamete nucleus of this plant. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in the zygote formed at fertilisation. **[1]**
**(c)** The zygote divides to form an embryo. Name the type of division and state the chromosome number in each embryo cell. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** Outline two roles of mitosis in a human. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what is meant by a stem cell. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a cancer forms. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe meiosis. **[1]**

**4.** A farmer grows potatoes from tubers taken from one high-yielding plant.

**(a)** Describe asexual reproduction. **[2]**
**(b)** Discuss one advantage and one disadvantage to the farmer of growing the crop in this way. **[4]**

**5.** **(a)** State the function of the sepals, the anther and the stigma. **[3]**
**(b)** State two ways in which the pollen of an insect-pollinated flower differs from the pollen of a wind-pollinated flower. **[2]**

**6.** A plant species can self-pollinate or cross-pollinate.

**(a)** Distinguish between self-pollination and cross-pollination. **[2]**
**(b)** In one area the numbers of insect pollinators fall sharply and summers become hotter. Discuss how relying on self-pollination would affect a population of this plant. **[3]**

**7.** Describe what happens between a pollen grain landing on a stigma and the formation of a seed and a fruit. **[5]**

**8.** A student placed 50 seeds on damp cotton wool in air at each of five temperatures and counted the seeds that had germinated after six days.

| Temperature / °C | 5 | 15 | 25 | 35 | 45 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seeds germinated | 4 | 21 | 46 | 38 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage germination at 15 °C and at 25 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the effect of temperature on germination shown by these results. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why no seeds germinated at 45 °C. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe the role of water and enzymes in germination. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** State the function of the testa, the plumule and the cotyledons. **[3]**
**(b)** A fruit has two thin wings. Explain how this feature helps dispersal. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why seed dispersal benefits a plant species. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** State the function of the prostate gland, the oviduct and the cervix. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the flagellum, the mitochondria and the acrosome help a sperm cell to carry out its function. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how two features of an egg cell are related to its function. **[2]**
**(d)** Compare male and female gametes in terms of numbers and motility. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** State one secondary sexual characteristic controlled by testosterone and one controlled by oestrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the roles of FSH and LH in the menstrual cycle. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the roles of oestrogen and progesterone in controlling the lining of the uterus. **[4]**

**12.** **(a)** Describe the early development of a zygote up to implantation. **[2]**
**(b)** State the function of the amniotic fluid. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the functions of the placenta and the umbilical cord. **[4]**
**(d)** A pregnant woman is infected with a virus. State why this may be a risk to the fetus. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Haploid: a single set of chromosomes [1]. Diploid: two sets of chromosomes (a pair of each type) [1].
**(b)** **23 pairs** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Half the chromosomes" is weaker than "a single set"; use the syllabus wording so the description cannot be misread.

**2. (a)** 26 ÷ 2 = **13** [1].
**(b)** 13 + 13 = **26** [1].
**(c)** **Mitosis** [1]; **26** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the type of division and the number are separate answers, so a correct number with "meiosis" still loses the first mark.

**3. (a)** Any two: growth; repair of damaged tissues; replacement of dying cells; asexual reproduction [2].
**(b)** An unspecialised cell [1] that divides by mitosis to give daughter cells that can become specialised [1].
**(c)** Uncontrolled cell division [1].
**(d)** A reduction division that halves the chromosome number (diploid to haploid), giving genetically different cells / gametes [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "unspecialised" alone describes the cell but not what it does; add that its daughter cells specialise.

**4. (a)** Offspring produced from one parent [1] that are genetically identical [1].
**(b)** Advantage: all plants are genetically identical to the parent [1], so they all keep its high yield [1]. Disadvantage: there is no variation [1], so a new disease or change in conditions could damage the whole crop [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs a reason with each point; "no variation" without saying why that matters gives only half the credit available.

**5. (a)** Sepals protect the flower in bud [1]; anther makes (and releases) pollen [1]; stigma receives pollen [1].
**(b)** Any two, stated as comparisons: insect pollen is larger; sticky or spiky rather than smooth; made in smaller amounts [2].
*Examiner insight:* Make each difference a comparison ("larger than", "spiky, not smooth"); a feature of one type only is often not credited.

**6. (a)** Self: pollen goes to a stigma of the same flower or the same plant [1]. Cross: pollen goes to a stigma on a different plant of the same species [1].
**(b)** Seeds can still form without insect pollinators [1]. But there is less variation [1], so the population is less able to respond to the hotter summers [1].
*Examiner insight:* Cover both sides of a "discuss": the pollinator point is an advantage, the variation point a disadvantage.

**7.** A pollen tube grows from the pollen grain [1] down through the style to the ovary [1]. It enters the ovule (through the micropyle) [1]. The male nucleus fuses with the female nucleus (fertilisation) [1]. The ovule becomes the seed and the ovary becomes the fruit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the term "nucleus" for what fuses; "the pollen fuses with the ovule" describes the wrong structures.

**8. (a)** 21 ÷ 50 × 100 = **42%** [1]; 46 ÷ 50 × 100 = **92%** [1].
**(b)** Germination increases as temperature rises from 5 °C to 25 °C [1], then falls at 35 °C and is zero at 45 °C [1].
**(c)** Enzymes in the seed are denatured [1], so stored food cannot be broken down for respiration and growth [1].
**(d)** Water is absorbed and activates enzymes [1]. Enzymes such as amylase break down stored starch to maltose (or proteases break proteins to amino acids) [1]. Soluble products move to the embryo and are used in respiration and growth [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), quote the temperatures from the table; a trend described with no data is less likely to get both marks.

**9. (a)** Testa protects the embryo [1]; plumule becomes the shoot [1]; cotyledons store food [1].
**(b)** Wings increase surface area / slow the fall [1], so the wind carries the fruit further from the parent [1].
**(c)** Plants colonise new areas [1] and there is less competition with the parent (and each other) for light, water and mineral ions [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (c), name what the plants compete for; "less competition" with no resource named is vague.

**10. (a)** Prostate gland adds fluid to sperm to form semen [1]; oviduct carries the egg to the uterus / is where fertilisation happens [1]; cervix is a ring of muscle at the base of the uterus that widens at birth [1].
**(b)** Flagellum lashes so the sperm swims to the egg [1]; mitochondria release energy (by respiration) for swimming [1]; acrosome enzymes digest a way through the jelly coat [1].
**(c)** Energy stores supply the early embryo [1]; the jelly coat changes at fertilisation so no other sperm can enter [1].
**(d)** Sperm are made in far greater numbers than eggs [1]; sperm swim but eggs cannot [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), say mitochondria "release energy"; writing that they "make energy" is a common reason for losing the mark.

**11. (a)** Testosterone: any one of facial or body hair, deeper voice, muscle growth [1]. Oestrogen: any one of breast development, wider hips, start of the menstrual cycle [1].
**(b)** FSH (from the pituitary) causes an egg to develop in the ovary [1] and stimulates oestrogen production [1]. LH causes ovulation [1].
**(c)** Oestrogen makes the uterus lining thicken (rebuild) [1]. Progesterone keeps the lining thick [1]. If there is no pregnancy, progesterone level falls [1] and the lining breaks down (menstruation) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep each hormone with its own effect; swapping FSH and LH, or giving ovulation to oestrogen, loses the linked marks.

**12. (a)** The zygote divides by mitosis to form a ball of cells (embryo) [1], which implants in the lining of the uterus [1].
**(b)** Protects the fetus from knocks / supports it [1].
**(c)** Placenta: oxygen and dissolved nutrients pass from mother's blood to fetus's blood [1]; carbon dioxide and urea pass from fetus to mother [1]; the two bloods do not mix [1]. Umbilical cord carries the fetus's blood to and from the placenta [1].
**(d)** Some viruses can cross the placenta and affect the fetus [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give direction for each substance in (c); "exchanges gases and food" without saying which way scores less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that mitosis halves the chromosome number, or that meiosis gives identical cells.
- Giving "46 pairs" instead of 23 pairs for a human diploid cell.
- Saying pollen lands on "the flower" or "the ovary" instead of the stigma.
- Describing self-pollination as pollen from "the same species" (cross-pollination is also within one species).
- Describing a trend without quoting data from the table.
- Saying water "gives energy" to a germinating seed.
- Saying the acrosome digests "the egg" rather than the jelly coat.
- Mixing up which hormone causes ovulation (LH) and which keeps the lining thick (progesterone).
- Saying maternal and fetal blood mix at the placenta.
