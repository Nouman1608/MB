## What you must be able to do

- (a) Identify and explain errors that do and do not affect the balancing of the trial balance.
- (b) Prepare journal entries to correct errors.
- (c) Prepare a suspense account.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Error of omission | Transaction not recorded at all |
| Error of commission | Correct amount, correct type of account, wrong account |
| Error of principle | Correct amount, wrong class of account (capital/revenue) |
| Error of original entry | Wrong amount used on both sides |
| Complete reversal | Correct accounts and amount, each on the wrong side |
| Compensating errors | Errors of equal size that cancel |
| Casting error | Wrong addition of an account or a book |
| Transposition | Digits swapped, such as 1,250 posted as 1,520 |
| Suspense account | Temporary account holding the trial balance difference |

## The deciding question

Were equal debits and credits posted? Yes: the trial balance agrees, no suspense. No: it disagrees, and the correction involves suspense.

| Agrees (no suspense) | Disagrees (suspense) |
|---|---|
| Omission | One side posted only |
| Commission | Different amounts on each side |
| Principle | Both entries on the same side |
| Original entry | Casting error in an account or day book total |
| Complete reversal | Balance missed out of, or misplaced in, the trial balance |
| Compensating | One side posted to the wrong side of an account |

## Three-step correction

1. What was done.
2. What should have been done.
3. The difference = the journal entry. If one side was correct, the other side of the correction is suspense.

## Correction patterns

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Omission of credit sale 300 | Dr customer 300, Cr sales 300 |
| Commission: receipt from A. Ray 150 credited to A. Rae | Dr A. Rae 150, Cr A. Ray 150 |
| Principle: van 9,000 debited to motor expenses | Dr motor vehicles 9,000, Cr motor expenses 9,000 |
| Original entry: purchase 640 entered as 460 both sides | Dr purchases 180, Cr supplier 180 |
| Complete reversal: cash sale 75 Dr sales, Cr cash | Dr cash 150, Cr sales 150 |
| Sales day book overcast 200 | Dr sales 200, Cr suspense 200 |
| Wages 980 posted as 890 (bank correct) | Dr wages 90, Cr suspense 90 |
| Receipt 410 debited to customer (should be credit) | Dr suspense 820, Cr customer 820 |
| Discount received 60 not posted to its account | Dr suspense 60, Cr discount received 60 |

## Suspense account rules

- Opening entry: the trial balance difference, on the side of the smaller total.
- Debits higher than credits: suspense starts with a credit balance.
- Post the suspense side of every correction.
- The account should clear to nil. If not, an error remains.

Mini-check: credits exceed debits by 340. Suspense opens with a debit of 340.

## Effect on profit

| Correction changes | Profit effect |
|---|---|
| Income account up | Increase |
| Income account down | Decrease |
| Expense account up | Decrease |
| Expense account down | Increase |
| Asset, liability or capital account only | None |

Example: draft profit 22,000. Corrections: sales up 300; wages up 90; equipment moved out of repairs 1,500 (no depreciation this year). Corrected profit = 22,000 + 300 − 90 + 1,500 = 23,710.

## Exam traps

- Suspense used for omission, commission or principle.
- Single amount used to correct a wrong-side posting or complete reversal.
- Day book casting error corrected in individual accounts.
- Difference placed on the wrong side of suspense.
- Narratives missing.
- Profit changed for a correction to trade receivables or another asset.

## Quick self-test

1. Name four errors that do not affect the trial balance.
2. A purchase of 260 was debited to purchases but not credited to the supplier. Does the trial balance agree? Correction?
3. Rent received of 500 was debited to rent received and credited to bank. Name the error and correct it.
4. The purchases day book was undercast by 150. Correct it.
5. Debits 54,900, credits 55,320. What is the suspense opening entry?
6. Machinery repairs of 700 were debited to the machinery account. Name the error and correct it.
7. A sale of 1,200 to B. Hart was posted to B. Harte's account. Correct it.
8. Discount allowed of 45 was credited to the discount allowed account. Correct it.
9. What does a suspense account that does not clear tell you?
10. Draft profit 18,400. Correction: Dr suspense 400, Cr sales 400. Corrected profit?
11. Why does an error of original entry not affect the trial balance?
12. What effect on profit does correcting a commission error between two customers have?

### Answers

1. Any four: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating.
2. No. Dr suspense 260, Cr supplier 260.
3. Complete reversal. Dr bank 1,000, Cr rent received 1,000.
4. Dr purchases 150, Cr suspense 150.
5. Debit suspense 420 (credits are higher).
6. Error of principle. Dr repairs 700, Cr machinery 700.
7. Dr B. Hart 1,200, Cr B. Harte 1,200.
8. It needed a debit of 45 but has a credit of 45: Dr discount allowed 90, Cr suspense 90.
9. At least one error has not been found.
10. 18,400 + 400 = 18,800.
11. The same wrong amount is used for both the debit and the credit.
12. None. Both accounts are trade receivables.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a suspense account appear in the financial statements?**
Only if errors remain unfound at the time the statements are prepared; the aim is always to clear it to nil.

**How do I correct a complete reversal?**
Use double the amount on each side: one amount cancels the wrong entries, the other makes the correct ones.
