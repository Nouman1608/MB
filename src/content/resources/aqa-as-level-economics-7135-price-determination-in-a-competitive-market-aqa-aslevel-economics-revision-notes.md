These notes condense **3.1.2 Price determination in a competitive market** of AQA AS Economics (7135), specification version 1.3, parts 3.1.2.1 to 3.1.2.6. For the full explanation and a worked market, read the [3.1.2 study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-price-determination-in-a-competitive-market-aqa-aslevel-economics/); to test yourself, try the [3.1.2 practice questions](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-price-determination-in-a-competitive-market-aqa-aslevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Precise meaning |
|---|---|
| Demand | Quantity buyers are willing and able to buy at each price in a given period |
| Supply | Quantity sellers are willing and able to offer at each price in a given period |
| Extension / contraction | Movement along a curve caused by a change in the good's own price |
| Shift (increase / decrease) | Whole curve moves because a non-price factor changes |
| PED | % change in quantity demanded / % change in price |
| YED | % change in quantity demanded / % change in income |
| XED | % change in quantity demanded of good A / % change in price of good B |
| PES | % change in quantity supplied / % change in price |
| Equilibrium | Price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, with no tendency to change |
| Disequilibrium | Any price at which the market does not clear, giving excess demand or excess supply |
| Normal good | YED positive |
| Inferior good | YED negative |
| Composite demand | One good demanded for several different uses |
| Derived demand | Demand for an input that comes from demand for the final product |
| Joint supply | One production process yields two products |

## 3.1.2.1 Demand

- The demand curve slopes downwards: a lower price raises quantity demanded.
- Shift factors named in the specification: income, wealth, prices of substitutes and complements, and individual preferences. Spending is also influenced by social and emotional factors, such as fashion, peer influence and habit.
- Right shift = increase in demand; left shift = decrease. Never call a movement along the curve a "rise in demand".

## 3.1.2.2 Elasticities of demand

| Value | PED reading | YED reading | XED reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below -1 | Price elastic | Strongly inferior | Strong complements |
| -1 to 0 | Price inelastic | Inferior | Weak complements |
| 0 | Perfectly inelastic | Income has no effect | Unrelated goods |
| 0 to +1 | -- | Normal, income inelastic (necessity) | Weak substitutes |
| Above +1 | -- | Normal, income elastic (luxury) | Close substitutes |

**PED and total revenue (total expenditure):**

| Demand | Price rise | Price fall |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic | Revenue falls | Revenue rises |
| Inelastic | Revenue rises | Revenue falls |
| Unit elastic | Unchanged | Unchanged |

**Influences.** PED: number and closeness of substitutes, share of income spent, necessity or habit, time period, breadth of definition (a brand is more elastic than the product). YED: whether the good is a necessity or a luxury. XED: how close the substitute or complement is.

**Quick calculation.** Price rises from 40 to 44 (+10%) and quantity demanded falls from 500 to 470 (-6%). PED = -6/10 = -0.6, inelastic; revenue rises from 20,000 to 20,680.

## 3.1.2.3 Supply

- The supply curve slopes upwards because a higher price means higher profits, which gives the incentive to expand production.
- Shift factors: costs of production (wages, raw materials, energy), productivity and technology, indirect taxes and subsidies, number of firms, weather for farm goods, and producers' expectations.

## 3.1.2.4 Price elasticity of supply

- PES is positive because the supply curve slopes upwards. Above 1 = elastic; below 1 = inelastic.
- Higher PES when: spare capacity exists, stocks are held, inputs can be switched easily, production is quick, and the time period is longer.
- Example: price rises from 5.00 to 5.50 (+10%) and quantity supplied rises from 800 to 840 (+5%). PES = 0.5, inelastic.

## 3.1.2.5 Equilibrium

- **Excess demand** (price below equilibrium): shortage, sellers raise prices, supply extends, demand contracts.
- **Excess supply** (price above equilibrium): surplus, sellers cut prices, demand extends, supply contracts.
- Algebra: set Qd = Qs. For Qd = 200 - 5P and Qs = 20 + 10P, 180 = 15P, so P = 12 and Q = 140. At P = 10, Qd = 150 and Qs = 120, an excess demand of 30.
- **Elasticity sets the split.** For a given rise in demand, inelastic supply gives a large price rise and a small quantity rise; elastic supply gives the reverse.

## 3.1.2.6 Interrelationships

| Link | Direction of the knock-on |
|---|---|
| Joint demand | Price of A falls, demand for complement B rises |
| Substitutes | Price of A rises, demand for substitute B rises |
| Composite demand | More of a good used in market A, supply to market B falls, B's price rises |
| Derived demand | Demand for final good rises, demand for its inputs (labour, materials) rises |
| Joint supply | Output of A rises, supply of by-product B rises, price of B falls |

## Exam traps

- A PED figure written without a minus sign, or a PES written with one.
- Using the new value as the base of a percentage change instead of the original.
- Applying the PED and revenue rule after a demand shift.
- Mixing up joint demand (consumers buy together) and joint supply (producers make together).
- "Price falls so demand rises": the price change causes an extension, not a shift.
- Ending an explanation at the new price without stating the excess that caused the change.
- In two-shift questions, saying both price and quantity rise without checking the sizes of the shifts.

## Self-test

1. State three determinants of demand other than the good's own price.
2. Quantity demanded falls from 250 to 220 when price rises from 8 to 10. Calculate PED and state what happens to total revenue.
3. Income rises 5% and quantity demanded of a good falls 2%. Calculate YED and classify the good.
4. The price of good B rises 20% and demand for good A rises 6%. Calculate XED and state the relationship.
5. Why does the supply curve slope upwards?
6. Give two reasons why PES might be low for fresh fish on a given morning.
7. Qd = 180 - 6P and Qs = 30 + 4P. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.
8. In question 7, what is the excess at a price of 12?
9. Give an example of joint supply.
10. Explain why the demand for steel is a derived demand.
11. Demand rises and supply falls. Which of price or quantity is certain to change, and in which direction?
12. Why is a positive XED of 0.1 weak evidence that two goods are substitutes?

### Answers

1. Any three: income, wealth, prices of substitutes, prices of complements, preferences, social or emotional factors such as fashion.
2. Quantity -12%, price +25%, PED = -12/25 = -0.48 (inelastic). Revenue rises from 8 x 250 = 2,000 to 10 x 220 = 2,200.
3. YED = -2/5 = -0.4, an inferior good.
4. XED = 6/20 = +0.3, substitutes, though not close ones.
5. A higher price raises the profit from extra output, which gives firms the incentive to expand production.
6. The catch has already been landed and fish cannot be stored for long, so quantity cannot be increased quickly.
7. 150 = 10P, so P = 15 and Q = 90.
8. Qd = 108 and Qs = 78, an excess demand of 30.
9. Beef and leather, or lamb and wool.
10. Steel is wanted because of demand for products made with it, such as cars and buildings.
11. Price rises; quantity is uncertain.
12. Its value is close to zero, so the price of B has little effect on demand for A.
