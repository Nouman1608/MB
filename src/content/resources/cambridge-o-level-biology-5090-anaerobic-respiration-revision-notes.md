These notes condense subtopic **10.3 Anaerobic respiration** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definition, the word equations for humans and yeast, why lactic acid builds up during vigorous exercise, and how the oxygen debt (EPOC) is removed. The full explanations and worked data are in the [10.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-anaerobic-respiration/). Then try the [10.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-anaerobic-respiration-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Anaerobic respiration | The release of a relatively small amount of energy by the breakdown of glucose without using oxygen |
| Lactic acid | The product of anaerobic respiration in human muscle |
| EPOC (oxygen debt) | Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption: the extra oxygen taken in after exercise to break down lactic acid |
| Fermentation | Anaerobic respiration in yeast |

## Word equations

- **Humans:** glucose → lactic acid (energy released)
- **Yeast:** glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide (energy released)

No oxygen on the left and no water in the products of either.

## Aerobic and anaerobic compared

| Feature | Aerobic | Anaerobic |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Needed | Not needed |
| Energy released | Relatively large amount | Relatively small amount |
| Glucose breakdown | Complete | Partial |
| Products (human) | Carbon dioxide and water | Lactic acid |
| Products (yeast) | Carbon dioxide and water | Ethanol and carbon dioxide |

## Why lactic acid builds up

1. Vigorous exercise means a very high demand for energy.
2. Oxygen cannot be supplied fast enough to meet the demand.
3. Muscles respire anaerobically as well, making lactic acid.
4. Lactic acid is made faster than it is removed, so it builds up in the **muscles and blood**.

## Removing the oxygen debt

1. The **fast heart rate continues**, carrying lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the **liver**.
2. **Deeper and faster breathing continues**, supplying the oxygen needed to **break down lactic acid** in the liver.

When the lactic acid is gone, the heart rate and breathing rate return to normal.

## Yeast uses

| Use | Role of anaerobic respiration |
|---|---|
| Bread-making | Carbon dioxide makes the dough rise; ethanol evaporates in baking |
| Brewing | Ethanol is the useful product |

Demonstration: yeast in boiled, cooled glucose solution under a layer of oil, with the gas passed into limewater, which turns cloudy.

## Worked data

Blood lactic acid: rest 1.0, end of sprint 12.0, 30 minutes later 3.0 (mmol per dm cubed).

- Percentage fall in 30 minutes: (12.0 - 3.0) divided by 12.0 x 100 = 75 per cent.
- Mean rate of fall: 9.0 divided by 30 = 0.3 mmol per dm cubed per minute.

## Sprint and marathon

| Event | Main respiration | Lactic acid |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint | Anaerobic plus aerobic | Builds up; large oxygen debt |
| Marathon | Mostly aerobic | Little |
| Walking | Aerobic | None |

Training improves oxygen delivery (stronger heart, more capillaries), so less lactic acid is made at the same effort.

## Order of events in recovery

1. Exercise stops but heart rate and breathing stay high.
2. Blood carries lactic acid to the liver.
3. Oxygen from fast, deep breathing is used to break down the lactic acid.
4. Lactic acid falls to its resting level; heart and breathing rates return to normal.

The time this takes depends on how much lactic acid was made and on the person's fitness.

## Aerobic and anaerobic: key numbers

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Glucose breakdown | Aerobic: complete. Anaerobic: partial |
| Oxygen debt | Extra oxygen needed after exercise to break down lactic acid |
| Where lactic acid is broken down | The liver |
| Evidence of anaerobic respiration in yeast | Limewater turns cloudy without oxygen |
| Energy comparison | Aerobic releases a relatively large amount; anaerobic a relatively small amount |

## Short model answers

**Explain why lactic acid builds up in a sprinter's muscles.** The muscles need energy faster than oxygen can be supplied, so they respire anaerobically. This makes lactic acid faster than it is removed.

**Explain why breathing stays fast after exercise.** Extra oxygen is needed to break down the lactic acid in the liver (the oxygen debt).

**State why the heart rate stays high after exercise.** To carry lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver.

**Explain why bread rises.** Yeast respires anaerobically and releases carbon dioxide, which forms bubbles in the dough.

## Exam traps

- Human: **lactic acid**. Yeast: **ethanol and carbon dioxide**.
- Anaerobic respiration **does** release energy, but a small amount.
- No water in the products of anaerobic respiration.
- Lactic acid is broken down in the **liver**.
- The oxygen debt is the oxygen needed to **remove lactic acid**, not a lack of oxygen.
- Do not say that lactic acid causes the oxygen debt directly; its breakdown needs the oxygen.

### Answers

1. The release of a relatively small amount of energy by the breakdown of glucose without using oxygen.
2. Glucose → lactic acid.
3. Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
4. Oxygen cannot be supplied fast enough, so the muscle respires anaerobically.
5. In the muscles and the blood.
6. Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption.
7. To carry lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver.
8. To supply oxygen for breaking down lactic acid in the liver.
9. The liver.
10. It releases carbon dioxide, which forms bubbles in the dough.
11. Boiling removes dissolved oxygen; cooling stops the yeast being killed.
12. (10.0 - 4.0) divided by 10.0 x 100 = 60 per cent.
