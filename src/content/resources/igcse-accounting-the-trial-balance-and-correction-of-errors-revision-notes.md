## 3.1 The trial balance

**Definition.** A listing of every ledger balance on a given date, split between a debit and a credit column.

| Purposes | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Tests that total debits equal total credits | Equal totals do not prove the records are correct |
| Signals that an error exists when the totals differ | Six error types leave the totals equal |
| Gives the balances needed for the financial statements | Does not say which account is wrong |

**Where each balance goes**

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Assets, expenses, purchases, carriage inwards | Liabilities, revenue and other income |
| Sales returns, drawings, opening inventory | Purchases returns, capital |
| Bank in hand | Bank overdraft |

**Method for a trial balance from a list of balances**
1. Put each balance in its normal column.
2. Add each column.
3. If a figure is missing, find it by balancing (capital is often the missing item).
4. If the totals still differ, the difference goes to a suspense account.

**Amending a trial balance.** State each error, its effect on each column, then the amended totals. A balance put in the wrong column changes the gap by twice its value. A mis-copied figure changes the gap by the difference between right and wrong.

## Errors that do not affect the trial balance

| Type | Meaning | Short example (Nibley Joinery) |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Wrong account of the right class | Gas of 410 debited to electricity |
| Compensating | Two errors cancel out | Wages overcast by 300, rent received overcast by 300 |
| Complete reversal | Right accounts, sides swapped | Cheque of 650 from a customer debited to the customer and credited to bank |
| Omission | Transaction not recorded at all | A purchase invoice of 1,100 never entered |
| Original entry | Wrong amount on both sides | Purchase of 1,860 entered as 1,680 in both accounts |
| Principle | Wrong class of account | Purchase of a lathe debited to repairs |

**Memory aid.** Alphabetical order is the syllabus order: commission, compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry, principle. Say "C C C O O P".

## 3.2 Correcting errors

**Journal layout.** Date, account debited, account credited, amount, narrative.

**Method in steps**
1. Write down what was done.
2. Write down what should have been done.
3. Find the difference in each account and make that the journal.
4. Add a narrative.

| Error | Correcting journal |
|---|---|
| Omission | Make the whole missing entry |
| Commission | Debit or credit the right account, and take it from the wrong account |
| Principle | Move the amount from the wrong class to the right class |
| Original entry | Journal for the difference only: 1,860 − 1,680 = 180, debit purchases and credit the supplier |
| Complete reversal | Journal for double the amount: 650 + 650 = 1,300, debit bank and credit the customer |
| Compensating | Reverse each error separately, in one journal |

## Suspense account

**Use.** A temporary balancing figure when the trial balance does not agree and the cause is not yet known. It is not part of the final statements.

- Debits larger than credits: put the difference on the credit side of suspense.
- Credits larger than debits: put the difference on the debit side of suspense.
- Errors that need it: one-sided entries, different amounts on each side, two entries on the same side, addition mistakes.
- Errors that do not need it: the six types above.
- Finish: the account must clear to nil. If it does not, look for another error.

**Worked reminder (Nibley Joinery).** The trial balance showed debits greater than credits, and a credit of 2,330 was placed in suspense. Errors found:

| Item | What happened | Effect on trial balance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A credit sale of 1,470 to B Holt was debited to B Holt as 1,740 and credited to sales as 1,470 | Debits too high by 270 |
| 2 | Wages of 2,060 paid by bank were debited to wages, with no credit to bank | Debits too high by 2,060 |

| Correcting journal | Debit | Credit | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Suspense | B Holt | 270 |
| 2 | Suspense | Bank | 2,060 |

The two debits to suspense total 2,330, which equals the credit that opened it, so the account clears.

## Adjusting profit

**Rule.** Only a correction that involves a revenue or expense account changes profit.

| Correction | Profit |
|---|---|
| Revenue increased or expense decreased | Rises |
| Revenue decreased or expense increased | Falls |
| Both accounts are assets, liabilities or capital | No change |
| Both accounts are expenses, or both revenue | No change |

**Worked reminder.** Nibley Joinery's draft profit was 18,640.

| Item | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| A drill costing 2,450 was debited to repairs | +2,450 |
| A credit sale of 800 was entered as 600 in both accounts | +200 |
| Electricity of 330 was debited to the gas account | 0 |
| Stationery of 160 paid by cheque was omitted | −160 |
| **Corrected profit** | **21,130** |

## Effect on the statement of financial position

- Name each asset, liability or capital figure that changes, and say whether it rises or falls.
- A correction that raises profit raises capital by the same amount, because profit is added to capital.
- A complete reversal affecting bank changes the bank balance by double the amount.
- Total assets and total equity plus liabilities must stay equal after the changes.

In the drill example, non-current assets rise by 2,450, trade receivables rise by 200 (the sale was on credit) and the stationery omission lowers bank by 160. Assets rise by 2,490 and profit, and therefore capital, rises by 2,490, which matches 2,450 + 200 − 160.

## Exam technique

The syllabus says candidates may be asked to prepare suspense accounts and journal entries, so practise both in full.

- Read each described error twice: first to classify it, then to decide whether it affects the trial balance totals.
- Give the account names, the Dr and Cr labels and the amounts in every journal. Answers tend to gain credit when each line is clear and complete.
- Work in two parts when a question gives several errors: first the journals, then the effect on profit and on the statement of financial position.
- Show a running total when adjusting profit, so that one slip does not hide the method.
- Check that the suspense account clears to nil before moving on.

## Quick self-test

1. State two purposes of a trial balance.
2. A trial balance agrees. Give one reason the accounts may still contain an error.
3. Name the error type: office furniture was debited to office expenses.
4. Name the error type: rent of 380 was debited to rates.
5. A receipt of 650 from a customer was debited to the customer and credited to bank. Give the correcting journal.
6. A trial balance has debits 430 greater than credits. State the suspense entry.
7. A purchase of 1,860 was entered as 1,680 in both accounts. Give the correcting journal.
8. Does a compensating error need a suspense account?
9. A correction increases profit by 600. Draft profit is 14,500. State the corrected profit.
10. An expense paid by bank was omitted. State the effect on profit and on bank.

### Answers

1. It checks that debits equal credits; it shows when an error exists; it provides balances for financial statements (any two).
2. An error of one of the six types does not change the totals.
3. Principle.
4. Commission.
5. Debit bank 1,300, credit customer 1,300 (double the amount).
6. A credit balance of 430 in suspense.
7. Debit purchases 180, credit the supplier 180.
8. No, the totals still agree.
9. 15,100.
10. Profit falls and bank falls by the same amount.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the trial balance balances so there are no errors" without qualification.
- Putting the suspense balance on the wrong side.
- Opening a suspense account for an error that leaves the totals equal.
- Journals for a reversal or original entry that use the wrong amount.
- Giving a journal without a narrative.
- Changing profit when both accounts in the correction are expenses.
- Forgetting to carry the profit change into capital on the statement of financial position.
- Naming an error type without giving a reason from the facts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which six errors do not affect the trial balance?**
Errors of commission, compensating errors, complete reversal, omission, original entry and principle. In each one the debits and credits stay equal, so the trial balance still agrees even though the books are wrong.

**Which side of the suspense account does the difference go on?**
If debits are larger than credits, put the difference on the credit side of the suspense account; if credits are larger, put it on the debit side. The suspense account is only a temporary balancing figure and is not part of the final statements.

**Which error corrections change profit?**
Only a correction that involves a revenue or expense account changes profit. A correction that raises profit also raises capital by the same amount in the statement of financial position.

**How do you write a correcting journal entry in Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452?**
Write down what was done, then what should have been done, and make the difference in each account the journal entry. The journal layout is date, account debited, account credited, amount and narrative.

**Does a trial balance that agrees prove the accounts are correct?**
No. A trial balance can agree while the accounts still contain one of the six errors that do not affect it, so writing that a balancing trial balance means there are no errors loses marks.
