> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** for revision and practice on Criterion A. They are **not** reproduced IB material and they do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of any on-screen examination. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (achievement levels 1-8), so the `[n]` marks below are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Study first: the [Criterion A study guide](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-criterion-a-knowing-and-understanding/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-criterion-a-knowing-and-understanding-revision-notes/). The questions use the MYP command terms. For the longer answers, use the examples from the units you have studied.

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion A) Identify one push factor that may cause people to leave a rural area. **[1]**

**2.** (Criterion A) Describe what is meant by *urbanisation*. **[2]**

**3.** (Criterion A) A country has a birth rate of 24 per 1000 and a death rate of 7 per 1000.

**(a)** Calculate the natural increase per 1000 people. **[1]**
**(b)** State the natural increase as a percentage per year. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what the figure shows about the population. **[1]**

**4.** (Criterion A) Another country has a birth rate of 12 per 1000 and a death rate of 11 per 1000, but its total population is growing quickly. Explain how this is possible. **[3]**

**5.** (Criterion A) Write one sentence that uses both the term *scarcity* and the term *opportunity cost* correctly. **[2]**

**6.** (Criterion A) Explain how the building of railways changed trade in the nineteenth century. Use at least one example. **[4]**

**7.** (Criterion A) A frost destroys much of the apple harvest in a region. Explain what is likely to happen to the price of apples, using the terms *supply*, *demand* and *scarcity*. **[4]**

**8.** (Criterion A) Explain two ways in which the sustainable management of a city can reduce damage to the environment. **[4]**

**9.** (Criterion A) Match each term to the correct meaning. Write the number and the letter.

Terms: 1 migration; 2 ideology; 3 cooperation; 4 significance

Meanings:
A. a system of ideas and ideals that forms the basis of political or economic theories
B. the movement of people from one place to another
C. the value of an event or idea in its historical context
D. individuals or societies working together towards the same end **[4]**

**10.** (Criterion A) Describe and explain the causes and the effects of rural to urban migration in a country or region you have studied. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any one of: unemployment, low incomes, shortage of farmland, poor services such as schools and hospitals, drought, conflict [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Urbanisation is an increase [1] in the proportion of a country's population living in towns and cities [1]. **[2]**
*Marking note:* "Cities getting bigger" earns at most one point. The idea of a proportion or share is the second point.

**3. (a)** 24 − 7 = **17 per 1000** [1].
**(b)** 17 ÷ 1000 × 100 = **1.7%** per year [1].
**(c)** The population is growing through natural increase, because there are more births than deaths, before migration is counted [1]. **[3]**

**4.** The natural increase is very small (12 − 11 = 1 per 1000, or 0.1%), so births and deaths cannot explain a fast rise [1]. The growth must come from net migration, with more people moving into the country than leaving it [1]. For example, it could be caused by pull factors such as jobs and higher wages, or by people seeking safety [1]. **[3]**

**5.** For example: "Because of scarcity, a family with a limited budget must choose between a holiday and a new computer, and the opportunity cost of the computer is the holiday." [1 for each term used correctly in context] **[2]**
*Marking note:* A sentence that only defines the terms, with no context, earns one mark at most.

**6.** Railways moved goods and people faster, in larger amounts and at lower cost than roads and canals [1]. Producers could therefore reach more distant markets, so trade expanded [1]. Perishable goods such as milk, fish and fresh vegetables could be sent from the countryside or coast to growing industrial towns [1]. A specific example, such as coal and manufactured goods carried between industrial regions and ports in Britain, or the linking of east and west coasts by a railway across North America [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The frost reduces the quantity of apples, so supply falls [1]. Apples become scarcer [1]. If demand stays the same, there is more demand than supply at the old price, so the price rises until the quantity demanded falls to match the reduced supply [1]. Some consumers may buy fewer apples or switch to other fruit, which shows demand responding to the higher price [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Any two developed ways, each with a measure and an effect (2 marks each).
- Public transport, cycling and walking routes: fewer cars means lower emissions and better air quality [1] [1].
- Recycling and waste management: less waste goes to landfill, which saves land and reduces pollution [1] [1].
- Green spaces and urban trees: they absorb carbon dioxide, reduce heat and protect wildlife [1] [1].
- Energy-efficient buildings or renewable energy: lower fossil fuel use and lower carbon emissions [1] [1].
- Building on brownfield land: protects countryside and farmland on the edge of the city [1] [1].
**[4]**

**9.** **1-B** [1]; **2-A** [1]; **3-D** [1]; **4-C** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Award up to eight marks. Indicative content, with credit for any relevant region with accurate detail.
- Terminology used accurately, for example rural to urban migration, push factors, pull factors, urbanisation, natural increase, informal settlement (up to 2 marks for a wide range used appropriately).
- Causes (push): low rural incomes, shortage of land, poor services, drought or conflict [1]. Causes (pull): jobs, schools, healthcare, perceived higher living standards [1]. At least one cause is explained with a reason and linked to the region [1].
- Effects on the city: rapid growth of population, pressure on housing and services, informal settlements, congestion [1]. Positive effects, such as a larger workforce and economic growth [1].
- Effects on the countryside: loss of young workers, an ageing population, but money sent home by migrants (remittances) [1].
- A named, specific example with place names and some detail or figures [1].
- Balanced, accurate and well-organised explanation that links causes to effects [1].
**[8]**
*Marking note:* A response that only lists causes can reach about half of the marks. The upper marks need explanation (why) and a specific example.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a term wrongly, for example calling migration "urbanisation".
- Describing when the command term asks for an explanation.
- Writing "in some countries" with no named place.
- Giving a calculation without units or meaning.
- Leaving out the example that the question asks for.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a different example in question 10?**
Yes. Use any region you have studied. Marks go to accurate terms, explanation and a specific example, not to a particular country.

**Why are some marks for using terms in a sentence?**
Strand i rewards terminology used accurately in context, so a definition on its own is not enough.

**How do I improve an answer that only lists points?**
Add a reason to each point (because, so, which led to) and then an example.
