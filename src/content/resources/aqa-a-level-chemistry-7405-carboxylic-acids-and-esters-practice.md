These questions cover section **3.3.9.1 Carboxylic acids and esters** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Carboxylic acids and esters study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-carboxylic-acids-and-esters/) and the [Carboxylic acids and derivatives revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-derivatives-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0.

## Questions

**1.** Name the ester formed in each reaction.

**(a)** ethanoic acid with propan-1-ol **[1]**

**(b)** propanoic acid with methanol **[1]**

**2.** Draw or write the structural formula of ethyl butanoate and state its molecular formula. **[2]**

**3.** Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate solution.

**(a)** State what is observed. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why ethanoic acid is described as a weak acid although it reacts with carbonates. **[2]**

**4.** Ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol to form ethyl ethanoate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction, using a suitable arrow. **[2]**

**(b)** State the catalyst and the method of heating. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two ways to increase the yield of ester. **[2]**

**5.** A crude ester is purified in a separating funnel by shaking with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution.

**(a)** Explain why this step is carried out. **[2]**

**(b)** State which layer is the ester and give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** State the purpose of adding anhydrous calcium chloride to the separated ester. **[1]**

**6.** State two uses of esters. **[2]**

**7.** Methyl propanoate is heated under reflux with dilute sulfuric acid, and in a separate experiment with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Name the organic products in each experiment. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the reaction with sodium hydroxide gives a better yield of products than the reaction with acid. **[2]**

**8.** 4.60 g of ethanol (Mᵣ = 46.0) is heated under reflux with excess ethanoic acid and a catalyst. 6.16 g of ethyl ethanoate (Mᵣ = 88.0) is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**9.** 10.2 g of ethyl propanoate (Mᵣ = 102.0) is heated under reflux with excess aqueous sodium hydroxide. 8.64 g of sodium propanoate (Mᵣ = 96.0) is isolated.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**10.** Vegetable oils are esters of propane-1,2,3-triol.

**(a)** Draw the structure of propane-1,2,3-triol. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how soap is made from a vegetable oil. **[3]**

**(c)** State what the soap is chemically. **[1]**

**11.** Biodiesel is a mixture of methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids.

**(a)** State the reagents and a condition used to make biodiesel from vegetable oil. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the by-product. **[1]**

**(c)** 8.90 g of glyceryl tristearate (Mᵣ = 890.0) reacts completely with excess methanol to form methyl stearate (Mᵣ = 298.0) and glycerol. Calculate the mass of methyl stearate formed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Propyl ethanoate [1]

**(b)** Methyl propanoate [1]. **[2]**

**2.** CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ [1]; C₆H₁₂O₂ [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Effervescence (bubbles of carbon dioxide) [1]

**(b)** 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ [1]

**(c)** It only partially dissociates in water, CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ [1]; so the equilibrium lies to the left, but the H⁺ it releases is enough to react with carbonate [1]. **[4]**

**4. (a)** CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O [1]; reversible arrow used [1]

**(b)** Concentrated sulfuric acid (a few drops) [1]; heat under reflux [1]

**(c)** Use an excess of one reactant (for example ethanol) [1]; remove the ester (or water) as it forms, for example by distillation [1]. **[6]**

**5. (a)** To remove unreacted carboxylic acid and the acid catalyst [1]; the acid reacts with hydrogencarbonate, giving carbon dioxide that is released as bubbles [1]

**(b)** The upper layer [1]; the ester is less dense than water (and immiscible with it) [1]

**(c)** To remove traces of water (drying agent) [1]. **[5]**

**6.** Any two from: solvents; plasticisers; perfumes; food flavourings [2]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Acid: propanoic acid [1] and methanol [1]; alkali: sodium propanoate [1] and methanol [1]

**(b)** Alkaline hydrolysis is irreversible and goes to completion [1]; acid hydrolysis is reversible, so an equilibrium mixture of ester, acid, alcohol and water forms [1]. **[6]**

**8.** n(ethanol) = 4.60 ÷ 46.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 88.0 = 8.80 g [1]; percentage yield = 6.16 ÷ 8.80 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COONa + CH₃CH₂OH [1]

**(b)** n(ester) = 10.2 ÷ 102.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 96.0 = 9.60 g [1]; percentage yield = 8.64 ÷ 9.60 × 100 = **90.0 %** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** HOCH₂–CH(OH)–CH₂OH [1]

**(b)** Heat the oil under reflux with aqueous sodium hydroxide [1]; all three ester links are hydrolysed [1]; the products are glycerol and the sodium salts of the long-chain carboxylic acids (soap) [1]

**(c)** The sodium (or potassium) salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Vegetable oil and methanol [1]; a catalyst [1]

**(b)** Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) [1]

**(c)** n(triglyceride) = 8.90 ÷ 890.0 = 0.0100 mol [1]; n(methyl stearate) = 3 × 0.0100 = 0.0300 mol [1]; mass = 0.0300 × 298.0 = **8.94 g** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A triglyceride gives three moles of ester (or soap) for every mole of fat, because it has three ester links.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming esters the wrong way round.
- Giving the carboxylic acid, not its salt, from alkaline hydrolysis.
- Drawing a single-headed arrow for a reversible esterification.
- Forgetting water as a product.
- Using one mole of sodium hydroxide per mole of fat instead of three.
- Describing biodiesel as a hydrocarbon fuel.
