## Questions

**1.** State **one** excretory product of each organ.

**(a)** Lungs **[1]**
**(b)** Kidneys **[1]**
**(c)** Skin **[1]**

**2.** Plants make waste gases but have no excretory organs.

**(a)** Name the process that makes oxygen as a waste product in a leaf. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a leaf in bright light gives out oxygen but takes in carbon dioxide. **[3]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) Name the structure of the urinary system that:

**(a)** carries urine from a kidney to the bladder **[1]**
**(b)** stores urine **[1]**
**(c)** carries urine out of the body **[1]**
**(d)** carries blood into a kidney. **[1]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) These are parts of a nephron: collecting duct, Bowman's capsule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, proximal convoluted tubule.

**(a)** Write the parts in the order that filtrate passes through them. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the knot of blood capillaries inside Bowman's capsule. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the part of the nephron that makes the tissue of the medulla concentrated. **[1]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) Describe how glomerular filtrate is formed, and explain why it contains no red blood cells. **[4]**

**6.** (Paper 2 only) The table shows the concentrations of three substances in fluid from different places in a person's kidney, in g per dm³.

| Substance | Blood plasma | Glomerular filtrate | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose | 0.9 | 0.9 | 0 |
| Amino acids | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0 |
| Urea | 0.30 | 0.30 | 18.0 |

**(a)** Name the process that produces glomerular filtrate. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the part of the nephron where the glucose concentration falls to zero, and give **one** reason why the body reabsorbs glucose. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times more concentrated urea is in urine than in the filtrate. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the concentration of urea is higher in urine than in the filtrate. **[2]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) In one person, glomerular filtrate is formed at 120 cm³ per minute and urine is produced at 1.5 cm³ per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of filtrate formed in one day. Give your answer in dm³. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the filtrate volume that is reabsorbed into the blood. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how water is reabsorbed into the blood from the collecting duct. **[3]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) A runner sweats heavily on a hot day and does not drink. Describe how ADH changes the volume and concentration of the runner's urine. **[5]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) The cells lining the proximal convoluted tubule have many mitochondria and many microvilli.

**(a)** Explain how each feature helps these cells reabsorb glucose. **[4]**
**(b)** A person's blood glucose concentration is very high. Suggest why glucose is found in their urine. **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) A small mammal from a desert has much longer loops of Henle than a similar mammal from a wet habitat. Suggest how its longer loops help it survive in the desert. **[3]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) Describe the path of a urea molecule from the renal artery to outside the body, and explain why the concentration of urea in the fluid rises along the way. **[8]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) A urine test shows that a person's urine contains protein. Suggest which part of the nephron is damaged, and explain your answer. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Carbon dioxide (accept water vapour) [1]
**(b)** Urea (accept water or ions/salts; do not accept urine) [1]
**(c)** Water, ions (salts) or urea, in sweat [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each organ is a separate mark, and "urine" for the kidneys is a product made of several substances, so a mark scheme usually asks for a named substance such as urea.

**2. (a)** Photosynthesis [1]
**(b)** In bright light the rate of photosynthesis is greater than the rate of respiration [1]; so more oxygen is made than is used, and the extra oxygen diffuses out through the stomata [1]; carbon dioxide is used faster than respiration makes it, so more diffuses in through the stomata [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Plants photosynthesise in the day and respire at night" loses the first mark -- respiration happens all the time, and the mark is for comparing the two rates.

**3. (a)** Ureter [1]
**(b)** Bladder [1]
**(c)** Urethra [1]
**(d)** Renal artery [1]
*Examiner insight:* Spelling that could be either ureter or urethra (for example "urether") is not credited, because the two words name different structures.

**4. (a)** Bowman's capsule → proximal convoluted tubule [1]; → loop of Henle → distal convoluted tubule → collecting duct [1]
**(b)** Glomerulus [1]
**(c)** Loop of Henle [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "first" and "second" convoluted tubule are accepted in place of proximal and distal, but the two must not be swapped.

**5.** The blood vessel entering the glomerulus is wider than the vessel leaving it, so blood pressure in the glomerulus is high [1]; small molecules (water, glucose, urea, ions) are forced out of the capillaries into Bowman's capsule [1]; this is ultrafiltration [1]; red blood cells are too large to pass through the capillary wall and capsule wall, so they stay in the blood [1]
*Examiner insight:* "High pressure" alone is not enough for the first mark -- you need to say where the pressure is (the glomerulus) or why it is high.

**6. (a)** Ultrafiltration [1]
**(b)** Proximal convoluted tubule [1]; glucose is needed for respiration (as a source of energy) [1]
**(c)** 18.0 ÷ 0.30 [1] = **60 times** [1]
**(d)** Water is reabsorbed from the filtrate (in the collecting duct) [1]; urea is not reabsorbed, so the same mass of urea is in a smaller volume of water [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), "urea is added to the urine" is a common wrong idea and earns no credit; the marks are for water leaving and urea staying.

**7. (a)** 120 × 60 × 24 = 172 800 cm³ [1]; ÷ 1000 = **172.8 dm³** [1]
**(b)** (120 − 1.5) ÷ 120 × 100 [1] = **98.8%** [1]
**(c)** The loop of Henle makes the tissue of the medulla concentrated (high ion concentration) [1]; water moves out of the collecting duct by osmosis into the medulla [1]; and then into the blood capillaries [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), an answer of 172 800 with no unit change keeps the method mark only; the final answer must be in the unit the question asks for.

**8.** Water lost in sweat lowers the water content of the blood [1]; this is detected by the brain (hypothalamus) [1]; the pituitary gland releases more ADH [1]; ADH makes the walls of the collecting duct more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood [1]; so a small volume of concentrated urine is produced [1]
*Examiner insight:* The last mark needs both volume and concentration, because the question asks about both.

**9. (a)** Mitochondria carry out respiration, releasing energy [1]; this energy is needed for active transport of glucose into the cells [1]; microvilli give a large surface area [1]; so glucose is reabsorbed faster [1]
**(b)** There is more glucose in the filtrate than the tubule can reabsorb [1]; so the glucose that is not reabsorbed stays in the filtrate and passes into the urine [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Mitochondria make energy" is not credited -- energy is released (or transferred) by respiration, not made.

**10.** Longer loops make the tissue of the medulla more concentrated [1]; so more water is reabsorbed by osmosis from the collecting ducts [1]; so the mammal makes a small volume of very concentrated urine and conserves water [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "suggest" question, credit goes to a linked chain of reasoning; "it saves water" on its own, without the mechanism, scores at most one mark.

**11.** Urea is carried in the plasma to the glomerulus [1]; high pressure forces urea into Bowman's capsule by ultrafiltration [1]; urea is a small molecule, so it passes through the filter [1]; it moves along the proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle and distal convoluted tubule [1]; urea is not reabsorbed into the blood [1]; water is reabsorbed by osmosis (especially from the collecting duct), so urea concentration rises [1]; urine passes from the collecting duct to the ureter and into the bladder [1]; it is stored in the bladder and leaves the body through the urethra [1]
*Examiner insight:* Extended answers are marked on separate creditworthy points, so a path that skips the ureter or names it in the wrong place loses that point even if the rest is correct.

**12.** The glomerulus (or Bowman's capsule) is damaged [1]; proteins are normally too large to pass out of the glomerulus into the filtrate [1]; damage lets protein pass into the filtrate, and it then passes into the urine [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming a tubule (for example the collecting duct) scores no marks here, because the explanation marks depend on linking the protein to filtration.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "plants respire only at night" instead of comparing the rates of photosynthesis and respiration.
- Answering "urine" when asked for an excretory product of the kidneys.
- Mixing up ureter and urethra, or renal artery and renal vein.
- Swapping the proximal and distal convoluted tubules.
- Explaining ultrafiltration without saying why the pressure in the glomerulus is high.
- Writing "urea is added" or "urea is made in the kidney" to explain the high urea concentration in urine.
- Stating "ADH reabsorbs water" rather than "ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable to water".
- Leaving a volume calculation in cm³ when dm³ is asked for, or dividing by the urine volume in a percentage reabsorbed calculation.
- Writing "mitochondria make energy".
