**These notes condense Cambridge 9489 AS topic 8, International relations 1919-29: conflict and cooperation, for recall.** The key questions are dissatisfaction with the peace settlements (8.1), the creation and challenges of the League (8.2), continuing tension after Versailles (8.3), and the improvement of 1924-29 (8.4). The explanations are in the [International relations 1919-29 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-international-relations-1919-29/); exam questions are in the [International relations 1919-29 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-international-relations-1919-29-practice/).

**Rotation (2027-2029 syllabus):** Paper 2 (Outline Study) in 2027 and 2029; Paper 1 (Historical Sources) in 2028.

## The Big Three -- aims and what they got

| Leader | Main aims | Satisfied? |
|---|---|---|
| Clemenceau (France) | Security; reparations; weak Germany; Rhineland buffer | Partly: Alsace-Lorraine, demilitarised Rhineland, reparations -- but no separate Rhineland state and no lasting guarantee |
| Wilson (USA) | Fourteen Points; self-determination; League | Partly: League created -- but the Senate rejected the treaty |
| Lloyd George (Britain) | Punish Germany but keep it as a trading partner; protect navy and empire | Largely: German fleet and colonies gone -- but soon thought the treaty too harsh |

## Reactions to the settlement

| Power | Reaction |
|---|---|
| France | Too lenient; promised Anglo-American guarantee lapsed |
| Britain | Growing sympathy for revision (Keynes, 1919) |
| USA | Senate refused ratification; separate peace with Germany (1921) |
| Italy | "Mutilated victory": no Fiume or Dalmatia |
| Japan | Gained Shandong and Pacific mandates; racial equality clause rejected |
| Germany | *Diktat*; anger at Article 231, reparations and territorial loss |
| Russia | Excluded; borders settled by war with Poland (Treaty of Riga, 1921) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Self-determination | Right of a nation to choose its own government |
| Mandate | Former colony governed by a League member under supervision |
| Article 231 | Clause making Germany accept responsibility for loss and damage |
| Reparations | Compensation payments imposed on the defeated |
| Collective security | Members acting together against an aggressor |
| Covenant | The League's founding rules |
| Unanimity | Decisions requiring every member's agreement |
| Sanctions | Economic or military measures against an aggressor |
| Conference of Ambassadors | Allied body outside the League that enforced the treaties |
| Successor states | New or enlarged states formed from the old empires |
| Passive resistance | German refusal to work for the occupiers in the Ruhr |
| Fulfilment | Stresemann's policy of meeting obligations to win revision |
| Locarno spirit | Optimism about co-operation after 1925 |

## League successes and failures, 1920s

| Success | Partial | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Aaland Islands; Greco-Bulgarian border | Upper Silesia; Memel | Vilna; Corfu (decided outside the League) |
| Nansen's refugee work; ILO | Slavery Commission; Mandates Commission | Disarmament Commission |

**Pattern:** the League succeeded when the parties were small powers willing to accept its decisions; it failed when a great power or its ally was involved.

## The treaties of conciliation

| Agreement | Purpose | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Dawes Plan (1924) | Phased reparations; US loan | Depended on American money |
| Geneva Protocol (1924) | Compulsory arbitration | Rejected by Britain |
| Locarno (1925) | Western borders guaranteed | Eastern borders left open |
| Kellogg-Briand (1928) | Renounce war | No enforcement |
| Young Plan (1929) | Lower total, longer schedule | Overtaken by the Depression |
| Hague Conference (1929) | Young Plan accepted; Rhineland evacuation by 1930 | Creditor disputes |

## Exam traps

- **Article 231** does not use the words "war guilt"; that was the German description.
- **Corfu** was resolved by the Conference of Ambassadors, not the League Council.
- **Rapallo (1922)** is the German-Soviet pact; do not confuse it with the 1920 Italian-Yugoslav treaty of the same name.
- **Lausanne** replaced Sèvres -- the first major treaty revision, won by force.
- **"Reality or illusion?"** needs evidence on both sides; mention the eastern frontiers, American loans and the effect of the Crash.

## Self-test

1. What did Clemenceau most want from the peace? *Security against Germany.*
2. Which treaty applied to Hungary? *Trianon.*
3. Give one example of self-determination being denied. *Any one: Germans in the Sudetenland; ban on Austrian union with Germany; Arabs under mandates.*
4. Name the permanent members of the League Council in 1920. *Britain, France, Italy and Japan.*
5. How was the Aaland Islands dispute settled? *Finland kept the islands, which were demilitarised with autonomy for the Swedish-speaking population.*
6. Why did the League fail over Vilna? *Poland, an ally of France, kept the city it had seized, and Britain and France would not act.*
7. What did the Commission for Refugees issue? *Nansen passports.*
8. Why did France occupy the Ruhr? *Germany had defaulted on reparations deliveries.*
9. What did the Washington Conference achieve? *Battleship limits, the Four-Power Treaty in place of the Anglo-Japanese alliance, and a guarantee of China's integrity.*
10. Why did the Rapallo Pact alarm the Western powers? *It linked the two outcasts, Germany and Soviet Russia, outside the Versailles system.*
11. What did the Locarno Treaties not guarantee? *Germany's eastern borders.*
12. Why was the Wall Street Crash so damaging to the improvement of the 1920s? *American loans were recalled, wrecking German recovery and the reparations settlement.*
