Condensed recall for section 4.3.4 of Unit 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Global industries and companies study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-industries-and-companies/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-industries-and-companies-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Investment per job   = total investment / jobs created
Wage premium         = (MNC wage / local average wage - 1) x 100
Tax forgone          = profit x tax rate x years of tax holiday
Weekly pay           = hourly rate x hours per day x days per week
Shortfall            = living wage - actual pay
Lost sales           = percentage lost x rival's sales
```

## Impact on the local economy

| Area | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Labour, wages and conditions | Jobs; higher pay; training; safer workplaces | Low pay relative to profits; job losses if the MNC leaves |
| Local businesses | New customers and suppliers; technology spreads; higher standards | Local rivals lose sales; suppliers over-dependent; imported parts |
| Community and environment | Infrastructure; community projects; local spending | Pollution; congestion; strain on housing and services; cultural change |

## Impact on the national economy

| Area | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | Extra output and incomes | Profits leave; dependence on a few firms |
| FDI flows | Capital and technology arrive | Inflows can reverse; foreign ownership of key sectors |
| Balance of payments | Exports and import substitution | Profit outflows; imported components |
| Technology and skills | Transfer of methods and management skills | Research and key skills kept at home |
| Consumers | Choice, quality, lower prices | Local brands squeezed |
| Business culture | Better practices, wider outlook | Local ways undermined |
| Tax revenues | Corporate, income and indirect taxes | Tax holidays, subsidies, profit shifting |

- Always ask: compared with what? What would have happened without the MNC?
- Profits sent abroad are an outflow, not a benefit to the host country.

## International business ethics

| Area | Examples | Possible consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder conflicts | Shareholders want profit; workers, communities and governments want other things | Disputes, strikes, bad publicity |
| Environment | Emissions and waste disposal; sustainability of resources | Fines, damage to local people and supply |
| Supply chain: pay and conditions | Below-living wages, long hours, unsafe factories | Boycotts, loss of reputation |
| Supply chain: exploitation | Child labour, forced labour | Legal action; serious reputational harm |
| Marketing: labelling | Misleading claims, unclear ingredients | Fines; loss of trust |
| Marketing: promotion | Marketing unsuitable products to vulnerable groups | Public anger and regulation |

- Ethical standards often cost more in the short term but protect reputation and long-term profit.
- MNCs are responsible for suppliers, not only for their own factories.

## Controlling MNCs

| Factor | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Power of the MNC | MNC bargaining power can weaken control | Governments may be too weak to resist |
| Political influence | Lobbying shapes rules | Rules may be relaxed |
| Legal control | Laws and fines set minimum standards | Weak enforcement; differences between countries |
| Consumer pressure | Customers choose ethical brands | Needs information and alternatives |
| Pressure groups | Campaigns and lawsuits expose abuses | Lack of funds |
| Social media | Fast, wide exposure | Misinformation; attention fades |
| Self-regulation | Codes and audits | Depends on honesty; greenwashing risk |

The best control combines laws with public pressure and the MNC's own standards.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MNC | A business with operations in more than one country |
| Balance of payments | Record of a country's trade and money flows with the rest of the world |
| Technology transfer | Spread of new methods and equipment to the host country |
| Sustainability | Using resources so that they remain available for the future |
| Self-regulation | Setting and policing one's own standards |
| Pressure group | Organisation campaigning to influence business or government |

## Exam traps

- Local economy and national economy are different. Make clear which one a point relates to.
- Do not count profits sent abroad as a gain for the host country.
- A tax holiday reduces the benefit to the government, but may still be worthwhile if the alternative is no investment.
- Ethical issues must be linked to a stakeholder and a consequence.
- Legal control is only as strong as enforcement.
- For **assess**, weigh benefits against costs and judge, saying what the judgement depends on.

## Answer frames

- **Impact of an MNC:** name the area, say whether the effect is good or bad, support it with a figure and note who gains or loses.
- **Ethical issue:** state the issue, the stakeholder affected, the possible consequence for the MNC and an action it could take.
- **Control:** choose two factors, show how they work together, and note one limitation.
- **Judgement:** say what the balance depends on, such as the tax deal, local competition or enforcement.

## Self-test

1. An MNC invests $60m and creates 400 jobs. Calculate the investment per job.
2. It pays $8,000 against a local average of $5,000. Calculate the wage premium.
3. Annual profit is $9m and tax is 30%. Calculate the annual tax, and the tax forgone over a four-year holiday.
4. A local rival with sales of $25m loses 10%. Calculate the lost sales.
5. A supplier pays $2 an hour for 9 hours a day, 6 days a week. Calculate weekly pay.
6. The living wage is $140 a week. Calculate the shortfall and express it as a percentage of the living wage.
7. Name two possible benefits of an MNC for a host country's economy.
8. Name two possible costs of an MNC for a host country.
9. Give one way in which consumers can control an MNC.
10. State one limit of self-regulation.

### Answers

1. 60m / 400 = **$150,000**.
2. 8,000 / 5,000 - 1 = **60%** above.
3. 30% x 9m = **$2.7m** a year; 4 x 2.7m = **$10.8m**.
4. 10% x 25m = **$2.5m**.
5. 2 x 9 x 6 = **$108**.
6. 140 - 108 = **$32**; 32 / 140 x 100 = **22.9%**.
7. Any two: jobs and wages; FDI; technology and skills transfer; tax revenue; more choice for consumers; export earnings.
8. Any two: pollution; profits sent abroad; local firms lose sales; tax holidays; cultural change; dependence on one firm.
9. Any one: boycotting a brand; choosing ethical alternatives; posting complaints on social media.
10. Any one: depends on the MNC's honesty; may be used for public relations; no independent enforcement.
