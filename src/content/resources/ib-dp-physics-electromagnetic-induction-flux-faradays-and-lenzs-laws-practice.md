Use g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** A circular coil of radius 4.0 cm lies in a uniform magnetic field of 0.25 T. The plane of the coil makes an angle of 40° with the field direction. Calculate the magnetic flux through one turn. **[3]**

**2.** A coil has 250 turns. The flux through each turn falls uniformly from 5.0 × 10⁻³ Wb to 1.0 × 10⁻³ Wb in 20 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the induced emf. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the minus sign in Faraday's law tells you about the induced current. **[2]**

**3.** A straight rod of length 0.28 m is dragged at a steady 2.2 m s⁻¹ along horizontal conducting rails in a vertical uniform field of 0.38 T. The rod and rails have a combined resistance of 0.35 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the emf induced across the rod. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the horizontal pull needed to hold the speed steady. **[3]**

**4.** The south pole of a bar magnet is moved towards the end of a coil that is connected to a resistor. State and explain the polarity induced in the end of the coil nearest the magnet, and explain how energy conservation requires this. **[4]**

**5.** A magnet hanging from a spring oscillates vertically in and out of a coil connected to a resistor.

**(a)** Describe how the induced emf varies during one oscillation. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the amplitude of the oscillation decreases with time. **[2]**

**6.** A rectangular coil of 150 turns measures 6.0 cm by 9.0 cm. It rotates at 25 Hz about an axis perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.12 T. Timing starts when the coil's plane is perpendicular to the field.

**(a)** Calculate the peak emf. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the emf at t = 3.0 ms. **[2]**
**(c)** The frequency rises to 40 Hz. State the new peak emf and period. **[2]**

**7.** A generator's emf has a peak of 8.4 V and a period of 16 ms. Its rotation speed is reduced to 75% of the original. Determine the original frequency, the new peak emf and the new period. **[4]**

**8.** A coil of 80 turns has a magnetic flux through it that changes as follows. From 0 to 0.20 s it rises uniformly from 0 to 6.0 × 10⁻³ Wb. From 0.20 s to 0.50 s it stays constant. From 0.50 s to 0.80 s it falls uniformly to zero. Using ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt, find the emf in each of the three intervals and describe the shape of the emf–time graph. **[6]**

**9.** A coil with an iron core is in series with a lamp, a battery and a switch. Explain why the lamp brightens gradually when the switch is closed, and why a spark can appear when it is opened. **[4]**

**10.** A wind-driven generator has a coil of 500 turns and area 0.020 m² rotating in a uniform field of 0.080 T at 6.0 revolutions per second.

**(a)** Calculate the peak emf. **[3]**
**(b)** The wind speed rises so that the coil turns at twice the rate. State the new peak emf. **[1]**
**(c)** Without changing the rotation rate, suggest two changes that would give the same new peak emf as in (b). **[2]**
**(d)** State the orientation of the coil at an instant when the emf is zero, and explain why. **[3]**

**11.** A horizontal rod of mass 0.050 kg and length 0.50 m rests across two horizontal rails in a vertical uniform field of 0.40 T. The total resistance of the circuit is 0.80 Ω. A light string runs over a pulley to a hanging mass of 0.050 kg, and the rod is released. Friction is negligible.

**(a)** Show that the magnetic force on the rod is B²L²v / R when its speed is v. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the terminal speed of the rod. Assume the rails and string are long enough. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that, at terminal speed, the rate at which the hanging mass loses gravitational potential energy equals the rate of energy dissipation in the circuit. **[3]**
**(d)** State and explain how the terminal speed changes if the resistance is doubled. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The angle between the field and the normal is 90° − 40° = 50° [1]. A = π × 0.040² = 5.03 × 10⁻³ m² [1]. Φ = BA cos θ = 0.25 × 5.03 × 10⁻³ × cos 50° = **8.1 × 10⁻⁴ Wb** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* using 40° directly is the standard error; write the angle to the normal explicitly so the step can be credited.

**2. (a)** ΔΦ = 4.0 × 10⁻³ Wb [1]. |ε| = 250 × 4.0 × 10⁻³ / 0.020 = **50 V** [1].
**(b)** The induced current's effect opposes the change that causes it [1]. Here the flux is decreasing, so the current produces a field in the same direction as the existing flux, to oppose the fall [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* saying the induced field opposes "the field" loses the point; refer to the change in flux and say whether it is rising or falling.

**3. (a)** ε = BvL [1] = 0.38 × 2.2 × 0.28 = **0.23 V** (0.234 V) [1].
**(b)** I = ε/R = 0.2341 / 0.35 = 0.669 A [1]. F = BIL = 0.38 × 0.669 × 0.28 [1] = **0.071 N** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* keep unrounded values between parts; rounding the emf to 0.23 V first shifts the final force.

**4.** The flux through the coil is increasing [1]. The induced current opposes this increase, so the nearest end becomes a south pole [1] and repels the magnet [1]. The opposite polarity would pull the magnet in and give electrical energy plus kinetic energy for free, breaking energy conservation; instead, work done against the repulsion supplies the electrical energy [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* link the polarity to a force on the magnet and then to work done; naming the polarity alone gains little.

**5. (a)** The emf alternates in sign as the magnet moves up then down [1]. The emf is largest in size as the magnet passes equilibrium, where its speed is greatest [1], and zero at the top and bottom, where it is momentarily at rest [1].
**(b)** The induced current heats the resistor [1]. That energy comes from the oscillating system, so the amplitude falls [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* tie the size of the emf to the rate of change of flux, which follows the speed, not to the size of the flux.

**6. (a)** A = 0.060 × 0.090 = 5.4 × 10⁻³ m² [1]. ω = 2π × 25 = 157 rad s⁻¹ [1]. ε_max = NBAω = 150 × 0.12 × 5.4 × 10⁻³ × 157 = **15 V** (15.3 V) [1].
**(b)** ε = ε_max sin ωt, with ωt = 157 × 0.0030 = 0.471 rad [1]. ε = 15.27 × sin 0.471 = **6.9 V** [1].
**(c)** Peak emf = 15.27 × 40/25 = **24 V** (24.4 V) [1]; period = 1/40 = **0.025 s** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* work in radians for part (b), and show the substitution so that your method can be seen.

**7.** Original f = 1/0.016 = **62.5 Hz** [1]. Peak emf is proportional to f, so the new peak = 0.75 × 8.4 = **6.3 V** [1]. New f = 46.9 Hz [1], so new period = 0.016 / 0.75 = **21 ms** (21.3 ms) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the period goes up when the frequency goes down; say which quantity is multiplied by 0.75 and which is divided by it.

**8.** 0 to 0.20 s: ε = −80 × (6.0 × 10⁻³ / 0.20) = **−2.4 V** [1][1]. 0.20 to 0.50 s: ΔΦ = 0, so ε = **0** [1]. 0.50 to 0.80 s: ε = −80 × (−6.0 × 10⁻³ / 0.30) = **+1.6 V** [1][1]. The graph has three flat sections: negative, zero, positive [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the two non-zero emfs have opposite signs and different sizes because the ramps differ in duration; show each gradient separately.

**9.** When the switch is closed, the current grows and so does the flux through the coil [1]. A changing flux induces an emf in the same coil, which by Lenz's law opposes the rise in current, so the current and the lamp brightness grow gradually [1]. When the switch is opened, the current falls rapidly and the flux collapses [1]. The induced emf now opposes the fall and can become large across the gap, causing a spark [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* describe the self-induced emf as opposing the change in current, not the current itself.

**10. (a)** ω = 2π × 6.0 = 37.7 rad s⁻¹ [1]. ε_max = NBAω = 500 × 0.080 × 0.020 × 37.7 [1] = **30 V** (30.2 V) [1].
**(b)** Peak emf is proportional to f, so it doubles to **60 V** (60.3 V) [1].
**(c)** Any two from: double the number of turns, double the coil area, double the field strength [1][1].
**(d)** The coil's plane is perpendicular to the field, with the normal along B [1]. The flux is then at its maximum [1], but momentarily not changing, so the rate of change of flux, and hence the emf, is zero [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* "maximum flux therefore maximum emf" is a common wrong link; the emf follows the gradient of the flux.

**11. (a)** ε = BvL and I = ε/R = BvL/R [1]. F = BIL = B²L²v/R [1].
**(b)** At terminal speed the magnetic force equals the pull of the hanging mass, mg = 0.050 × 9.81 = 0.4905 N [1]. v = mgR / (B²L²) = 0.4905 × 0.80 / (0.16 × 0.25) = **9.8 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Rate of loss of potential energy = mgv = 0.4905 × 9.81 = 4.81 W [1]. ε = 0.40 × 9.81 × 0.50 = 1.962 V [1], so power in the circuit = ε²/R = 1.962² / 0.80 = 4.81 W, which is equal [1].
**(d)** The terminal speed doubles [1], because the current for a given speed is halved, so twice the speed is needed to give the same magnetic force [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* in "show that" parts every step must be visible; the terminal speed also needs the statement that the net force is zero.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring θ from the surface of the loop instead of from its normal.
- Forgetting N in the emf of a coil.
- Describing Lenz's law as opposing the field instead of opposing the change in flux.
- Substituting f where ω = 2πf is needed.
- Writing that emf is maximum when flux is maximum for a rotating coil.
- Not linking induced current to a force and to work done.
