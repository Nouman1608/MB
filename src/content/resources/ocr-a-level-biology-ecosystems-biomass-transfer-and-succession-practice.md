These questions cover section 6.3.1(a) to (e) of the OCR A Level Biology A (H420) specification, Version 4.1 (April 2026), for first assessment in 2025: ecosystems, biomass transfer, the nitrogen and carbon cycles, primary succession, and sampling with statistics. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-biology-ecosystems-biomass-transfer-and-succession/) and [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-biology-ecosystems-biomass-transfer-and-succession-revision-notes/). Use 4.18 J per gram per °C for water.

## Questions

**1.** A rock pool is an ecosystem.

**(a)** State what is meant by an ecosystem. **[1]**
**(b)** Name one biotic and one abiotic factor that could change the number of periwinkles in the pool, and explain each effect. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how to find the dry biomass of grass per square metre in a meadow. **[4]**

**3.** A 0.85 g sample of dried grass seed is burned under 40 g of water. The water temperature rises from 19.0 °C to 38.5 °C.

**(a)** Find the energy transferred to the water, in joules. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy per gram of seed. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the true energy content is higher than your value. **[1]**

**4.** In a lake, the energy stored in the biomass of producers is 31 500 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹. Primary consumers ingest 4 800 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ and incorporate 624 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ into their biomass. Secondary consumers ingest 540 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹ and incorporate 54 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of biomass transfer in the primary consumers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency in the secondary consumers. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy ingested but not incorporated by the primary consumers, and give one way it is lost. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how farmers can increase the biomass available to humans by manipulating biomass transfer, and give one disadvantage. **[6]**

**6.** A farmer finds that a waterlogged field has low nitrate content and poor crop growth.

**(a)** Identify the overall process that produces nitrate from ammonium, and name the bacteria at each step. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why waterlogging lowers the nitrate content. **[3]**
**(c)** The farmer sows clover. Explain how this can increase the nitrogen available to later crops. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how photosynthesis, respiration and decomposition cycle carbon in a woodland, and describe how the oceans can store carbon for a very long time. **[5]**

**8.** Data were collected from sand dunes of different ages.

| Dune age (years) | Soil organic matter (%) | Plant species per m² |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.3 | 2 |
| 25 | 1.2 | 5 |
| 60 | 3.8 | 9 |
| 120 | 6.5 | 12 |

**(a)** Describe the trends in the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how pioneer species lead to an increase in the number of plant species. **[3]**
**(c)** The 120-year dune has not become woodland because it is grazed by rabbits. Name this type of succession and explain why it happens. **[2]**
**(d)** A sample from the 120-year dune has 7 species, with 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4 and 3 individuals. Calculate Simpson's Index of Diversity, D = 1 − Σ(n/N)², to 2 decimal places. **[2]**

**9.** A student uses mark-release-recapture to estimate the number of ground beetles in a heathland. She marks 36 beetles and releases them. A second sample contains 42 beetles, of which 9 are marked.

**(a)** Estimate the size of the beetle population. **[2]**
**(b)** Give two assumptions needed for this estimate to be reliable. **[2]**
**(c)** The marked beetles are easier to trap than unmarked ones. State and explain the effect on the estimate. **[2]**

**10.** Plant species per quadrat were recorded at nine points along a transect inland from a beach. Distance from the sea (m): 2, 6, 11, 17, 25, 33, 40, 52, 61. Number of species: 3, 5, 4, 7, 9, 8, 12, 11, 15.

**(a)** State a null hypothesis. **[1]**
**(b)** Rank both sets (1 = highest) and find Σd². **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. **[2]**
**(d)** The critical value for n = 9 at P = 0.05 is 0.700. State the conclusion. **[2]**

**11.** Seedling heights (mm) were measured after ten days in full light (10 seedlings: mean 28.5, standard deviation 3.03) and in shade (10 seedlings: mean 22.0, standard deviation 2.58).

**(a)** State a null hypothesis. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate t, using t = (mean 1 − mean 2) ÷ √(s1²/n1 + s2²/n2). **[3]**
**(c)** State the degrees of freedom. At P = 0.05 the critical t value is 2.10. Give your conclusion. **[3]**

**12.** Sixty slugs were found under four types of cover. The numbers under a stone, log, plank and plastic sheet were 27, 15, 9 and 9. The null hypothesis is that slugs are spread equally.

Calculate χ², state the degrees of freedom and, given a critical value of 7.81 at P = 0.05, draw a conclusion. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** All the organisms in an area, together with the non-living features and the interactions between them [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Biotic, for example predation by crabs [1], which reduces periwinkle numbers [1]. Abiotic, for example salinity rising on hot days as water evaporates, which stresses periwinkles [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A factor named with no effect on the organism, such as "temperature", is not enough.

**2.** Cut the grass at ground level in a known area, for example random quadrats [1]. Weigh the sample, then dry it in an oven at about 80 °C [1]. Re-weigh at intervals until the mass is constant [1]. Divide the dry mass by the area sampled, or scale up to 1 m² [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Dry it" without "to constant mass" is an easy mark to lose.

**3. (a)** Temperature rise = 38.5 − 19.0 = 19.5 °C [1]. Energy = 40 × 4.18 × 19.5 = 3 260 J (3 260.4 J) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 3 260.4 ÷ 0.85 = **3 840 J g⁻¹** (3.84 kJ g⁻¹) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Heat is lost to the surroundings and apparatus, so the water gains less energy than was released [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the unit. A bare number is weaker than a number with its unit.

**4. (a)** Efficiency = incorporated ÷ ingested × 100 = 624 ÷ 4 800 [1] = **13.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 54 ÷ 540 × 100 = **10.0%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 4 800 − 624 = **4 176 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹** [1]. It is lost, for example, as faeces, urine, or heat from respiration [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the intake of that trophic level, not by the producers' energy.

**5.** Fertilisers or irrigation increase crop growth [1]. Herbicides remove competing weeds [1]. Pesticides or biological control reduce biomass lost to pests [1]. Keeping livestock indoors, warm and with limited movement reduces respiration, so less biomass is lost as heat and movement [1]. More of the intake is built into the animal's biomass, which increases the transfer to humans [1]. Disadvantage, for example eutrophication, harm to non-target species or welfare concerns [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each method to the specific loss it reduces.

**6. (a)** Nitrification [1]. Nitrosomonas converts ammonium to nitrite [1]. Nitrobacter converts nitrite to nitrate [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Waterlogged soil is short of oxygen, which nitrifying bacteria need, so less nitrate is made [1]. Denitrifying bacteria are favoured in anaerobic conditions [1]. They convert nitrate to nitrogen gas, which leaves the soil [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Clover is a legume with Rhizobium bacteria in its root nodules [1]. The bacteria fix nitrogen gas into ammonium, and when the clover dies and decomposes, the nitrogen compounds are released and converted to nitrate for the next crop [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the organism and its step together. "Bacteria make nitrate" does not separate the two steps.

**7.** Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the air to build organic compounds [1]. Respiration by plants, animals and decomposers returns carbon dioxide [1]. Decomposers break down dead material, releasing carbon dioxide [1]. Oceans: carbon dioxide dissolves and forms hydrogencarbonate and carbonate ions [1]. Marine organisms build calcium carbonate shells, which form limestone sediment, a long-term store [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction of each process (into or out of the atmosphere).

**8. (a)** Soil organic matter rises from 0.3% to 6.5% as the dunes age [1]. The number of plant species also rises, from 2 to 12 per m² [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Pioneers such as marram grass die and decompose, adding organic matter [1]. This forms soil that holds more water and nutrients, so conditions are less extreme [1]. New species can then colonise [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Deflected succession [1]. Grazing by rabbits stops shrubs and trees establishing, so the community is held at an earlier stage [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** N = 42 [1]. Σ(n/N)² = 0.159, so D = 1 − 0.159 = **0.84** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* When describing a trend, quote values from the table, not only "it increases".

**9. (a)** N = 36 × 42 ÷ 9 [1] = **168** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Two from: marked animals mix evenly with the population; marking does not affect survival or capture; no births, deaths or movement in or out; the mark is not lost [1][1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Marked animals make up too large a share of the second catch [1]. The calculation divides by the number of marked animals, so the population is underestimated [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which quantity in the formula is too high or low, and how the estimate changes.

**10. (a)** There is no significant correlation between distance from the sea and the number of plant species [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Distance ranks run from 9 down to 1. Species ranks: 9, 7, 8, 6, 4, 5, 2, 3, 1 [1]. d = 0, 1, −1, 0, 1, −1, 1, −1, 0, so d² sums to 6 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** rs = 1 − (6 × 6) ÷ (9 × 80) [1] = 1 − 36/720 = **0.95** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 0.95 is greater than 0.700 [1], so the null hypothesis is rejected: more species are found further inland, and the positive correlation is significant [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion should reject or keep the null hypothesis, in context, without claiming cause.

**11. (a)** There is no significant difference between the mean heights in full light and in shade [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** s1²/n1 = 9.18/10 = 0.918 and s2²/n2 = 6.66/10 = 0.666 [1]. √(0.918 + 0.666) = 1.258 [1]. t = 6.5 ÷ 1.258 = **5.17** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Degrees of freedom = 10 + 10 − 2 = 18 [1]. 5.17 is greater than 2.10, so reject the null hypothesis [1]. The mean height is significantly greater in full light [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values through the calculation, and state the degrees of freedom before reading the critical value.

**12.** Expected = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 in each [1]. χ² = (12²/15) + 0 + (6²/15) + (6²/15) = 9.6 + 0 + 2.4 + 2.4 [1] = **14.4** [1]. Degrees of freedom = 4 − 1 = 3 [1]. 14.4 is greater than 7.81, so reject the null hypothesis: the slugs are not spread equally [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each (O − E)² ÷ E term, and state the expected values first.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring fresh mass and calling it biomass.
- Dividing by the wrong total in efficiency calculations.
- Saying "energy is lost" without naming the route.
- Swapping Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter.
- Using the wrong degrees of freedom or critical value.
- Stating that a correlation shows cause.
