This guide explains how **Paper 2 Drama** works in Cambridge IGCSE
Literature in English 0475 for the 2026 series: its format and marks,
the choice it forces between passage-based and essay questions, and
what the assessment objectives actually reward when you write about a
play rather than a poem or novel.

## Where this fits in 0475

The 0475 syllabus is organised around papers rather than a topic list.
Every candidate takes Paper 1 Poetry and Prose, and then completes the
qualification through one of three routes: Paper 2 Drama, OR Paper 3
Drama (Open Text) together with Paper 4 Unseen, OR Paper 3 together with
Component 5 Coursework. All routes lead to grades A* to G. Paper 2 is
therefore the single-exam drama route — one written paper covering two
plays, worth 50% of the whole qualification, mirroring Paper 1's other
50%. Schools choose the route; if yours sits Paper 2, the drama texts
carry as much weight as poetry and prose combined, which is worth
letting sink in when dividing revision time.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE LITERATURE IN ENGLISH 0475 — PAPER 2 DRAMA (2026)**

- Format — a written paper of 1 hour 30 minutes carrying 50 marks,
externally set and marked by Cambridge International, worth 50% of the
qualification for candidates on this route.
- Task structure — candidates answer two questions on two set texts,
each carrying equal marks (25 each). For each text there is a choice of
two questions: either (a) a passage-based question or (b) an essay
question.
- The one-of-each rule — candidates must answer one passage-based
question and one essay question across the paper. You cannot answer two
essays or two passage questions, so both skills must be exam-ready for
both plays.
- Closed book — set texts may not be taken into the exam room. The
relevant passages for the (a) questions are printed on the question
paper itself.
- Set texts — the plays for this component are listed in Section 3 of
the syllabus and are specific to the examination year, so always
confirm the list in the current syllabus rather than relying on a
previous year's.
- Assessment objectives — every question tests all four AOs, weighted
equally at 25% each: AO1, detailed knowledge of the text supported by
reference and quotation; AO2, understanding of meanings and contexts,
going beyond surface meaning to deeper awareness of ideas and
attitudes; AO3, recognising and appreciating how writers use language,
structure and form to create and shape meanings and effects; and AO4,
communicating a sensitive and informed personal response.
- What answers must show — the syllabus itemises this as knowledge of
content through detail and quotation (AO1); understanding of
characters, relationships, situations and themes (AO2); understanding
of the writer's intentions and methods, responding to the use of
language (AO3); and a personal response, sometimes asked for directly
through questions like "What do you think?" or "What are your feelings
about…?" (AO4).

## Paper 2 or the Paper 3 route?

It helps to understand what taking Paper 2 spares you and what it
demands. The alternative routes both run through Paper 3, which the
syllabus names Drama (Open Text) — so drama is examined with the text
available — paired with either Paper 4 Unseen, an exam on material you
have never studied, or Component 5 Coursework, which has its own
assessment criteria set out in the syllabus. Choosing Paper 2 instead
concentrates everything into one closed-book sitting: no unseen paper
to prepare for and no coursework folder to assemble, but also no text
open on the desk and two plays to hold securely in memory at once. That
trade shapes revision priorities for the whole course. Candidates on
this route should rehearse retrieving quotations under time pressure —
something open-text candidates never need to do — and should practise
both question types on both plays, since the one-of-each rule removes
the option of leaning on a favourite format.

## How to approach it

Because the marks split evenly across the four objectives, a
technically observant answer with no personal engagement caps itself
just as surely as an enthusiastic one with no quotation — build
paragraphs that do all four jobs: a claim about the play, evidence from
the text, a comment on the dramatic method behind the evidence, and
your own evaluation of its effect. Plan the essay question for five
minutes before writing: three or four points arranged so the argument
develops, rather than a character description that happens to mention
the question's key word in each paragraph. Since Paper 2 is closed book,
memorising a bank of short, flexible quotations for each play matters
more here than on an open-text route; ten to fifteen lines per play,
chosen to cover several characters and themes, will serve almost any
essay title. Treat the passage-based option as a close-reading
exercise — the extract is printed in front of you, so the examiner
expects detailed work with its specific language and stagecraft, not a
general character survey. And remember these are plays: writing about
audience, staging, entrances, dramatic irony and what an audience sees
and hears signals the genre awareness AO3 is looking for, in a way that
treating the text like a novel never will. In timed practice, split the
90 minutes evenly and hold the discipline of finishing the first
question at 45 minutes — two balanced 25-mark answers nearly always
outscore one long and one rushed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Paper 2 Drama structured?**
It lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 50 marks: two questions on two set plays, 25 marks each. You
must answer one passage-based (a) question and one essay (b) question.

**Is Paper 2 open book?**
No. Set texts may not be taken into the exam room; passages for the passage-based questions are
printed on the paper.

**Who takes Paper 2?**
Candidates on the route Paper 1 plus Paper 2. Paper 2 is worth 50% of the qualification, the same as
Paper 1.


## A note on syllabus currency

This guide is written against the Cambridge IGCSE Literature in
English (0475) syllabus for the 2026 examination series. Cambridge has
since published a 2027 syllabus, and a further restructure applies
from the 2028-2030 cycle: the current four-paper-plus-coursework
structure is replaced by a two-component route (Paper 1 Poetry and
Prose, now open text, plus either Paper 2 Drama and Unseen or a
50-mark coursework component). Confirm which syllabus cycle your
candidates are sitting before revising from this guide.
