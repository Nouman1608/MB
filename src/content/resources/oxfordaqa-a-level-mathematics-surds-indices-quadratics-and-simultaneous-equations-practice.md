## Questions

**1.** Simplify √98 − √32 + √18, giving your answer in the form k√2. **[3]**

**2.** Rationalise the denominator of 20/(6 − √11), simplifying your answer fully. **[3]**

**3.** Show that (2 + √7)/(4 − √7) = (5 + 2√7)/3. **[4]**

**4.**

**(a)** Evaluate (125/27)^(−2/3). **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 4^(x + 1) = 8^(x − 2). **[2]**

**5.** Solve 6x² + 11x − 35 = 0. **[3]**

**6.** f(x) = 2x² − 8x + 11.

**(a)** Write f(x) in the form a(x + p)² + q. **[3]**
**(b)** State the vertex of the graph of y = f(x) and the equation of its line of symmetry. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the graph of y = f(x) does not cross the x-axis. **[1]**

**7.** Solve 3x² − 8x + 2 = 0, giving your answers in the form (a ± √b)/c. **[3]**

**8.** The equation x² + kx + (k + 3) = 0 has real coefficients and k is a constant.

**(a)** Find the values of k for which the equation has equal roots. **[3]**
**(b)** Determine the range of k that gives the equation two distinct real roots. **[3]**

**9.** Solve the simultaneous equations 2x + y = 3 and x² + y² = 5. **[6]**

**10.**

**(a)** Solve 3(2 − x) ≥ 4x − 15. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 4x² + x − 18 > 0. **[3]**
**(c)** Hence find all values of x that satisfy both 3(2 − x) ≥ 4x − 15 and 4x² + x − 18 > 0. **[2]**

**11.** The line y = 2x + k and the curve y = x² − 4x + 5 are drawn on the same axes.

**(a)** Show that any intersection point has an x-coordinate satisfying x² − 6x + 5 − k = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** The line is a tangent to the curve. Find the value of k. **[3]**
**(c)** For k = −1, find the exact coordinates of the points where the line meets the curve. **[3]**

**12.** A farmer uses a straight wall as one side of a rectangular enclosure. The other three sides use 40 m of fencing. The sides perpendicular to the wall are each x metres long and the enclosed area is A m².

**(a)** Show that A = 40x − 2x². **[1]**
**(b)** Write A in the form p − 2(x − q)². Hence state the largest area available and the width that produces it. **[3]**
**(c)** The farmer needs an area of at least 150 m². Find the possible values of x. **[3]**
**(d)** Find, in the form m ± n√10, the values of x that give an area of exactly 120 m². **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** √98 = 7√2 [1]; √32 = 4√2 and √18 = 3√2 [1]; 7√2 − 4√2 + 3√2 = **6√2** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each simplification on its own line. Working that is laid out clearly makes a slip easy to spot and fix, whereas a bare total gives you nothing to check.

**2.** Multiply top and bottom by (6 + √11) [1]; denominator 36 − 11 = 25 [1]; 20(6 + √11)/25 = **(24 + 4√11)/5** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Simplifying fully" means cancelling the common factor of 5 from 20 and 25. Stopping at 20(6 + √11)/25 leaves the answer unfinished.

**3.** Multiply top and bottom by (4 + √7) [1]; numerator (2 + √7)(4 + √7) = 8 + 2√7 + 4√7 + 7 = 15 + 6√7 [1]; denominator 16 − 7 = 9 [1]; (15 + 6√7)/9 = **(5 + 2√7)/3**, as required [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, every stage must be visible and you must reach the stated result. Working backwards from the printed result is not a safe route to full credit.

**4. (a)** (125/27)^(−2/3) = (27/125)^(2/3) [1]; (3/5)² = **9/25** [1]
**(b)** 2^(2x + 2) = 2^(3x − 6) [1]; 2x + 2 = 3x − 6, so **x = 8** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert to a common base before comparing exponents. Taking the reciprocal first, then the root, then the power keeps part (a) free of large numbers.

**5.** Need two numbers with product −210 and sum 11: 21 and −10 [1]; (3x − 5)(2x + 7) = 0 [1]; **x = 5/3 or x = −7/2** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both roots. A correct factorisation with only one root given leaves the question unfinished. The formula is a fine alternative if you are careful with the signs.

**6. (a)** 2(x² − 4x) + 11 [1]; 2[(x − 2)² − 4] + 11 [1]; **2(x − 2)² + 3** [1]
**(b)** Vertex **(2, 3)** [1]; line of symmetry **x = 2** [1]
**(c)** The minimum value of y is 3, which is above the x-axis (and a > 0 so the parabola opens upward) [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the 2 out of both terms before completing the square. For part (c), refer to the minimum value; the discriminant, 64 − 88 = −24, is another valid way to justify it.

**7.** b² − 4ac = 64 − 24 = 40 [1]; x = (8 ± √40)/(2 × 3) [1]; √40 = 2√10, so **x = (4 ± √10)/3** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the formula with values substituted, in brackets where a number is negative. This makes slips easier to find. Simplify √40 before cancelling, and cancel the common factor of 2 from every term.

**8. (a)** b² − 4ac = k² − 4(k + 3) = k² − 4k − 12 [1]; equal roots means (k − 6)(k + 2) = 0 [1]; **k = 6 or k = −2** [1]
**(b)** Distinct real roots needs k² − 4k − 12 > 0 [1]; (k − 6)(k + 2) > 0 [1]; **k < −2 or k > 6** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A set of values is an inequality, not an equation. Do not write a single chain such as −2 > k > 6, and keep the two regions joined by "or".

**9.** y = 3 − 2x [1]; x² + (3 − 2x)² = 5 [1]; 5x² − 12x + 4 = 0 [1]; (5x − 2)(x − 2) = 0 [1]; x = 2/5 or x = 2 [1]; y = 3 − 2x gives y = 11/5 and y = −1, so **(2/5, 11/5) and (2, −1)** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute back into the linear equation, which gives one y for each x. Using the quadratic to find y can produce wrong pairs, and the answer should be presented as pairs.

**10. (a)** 6 − 3x ≥ 4x − 15 [1]; 21 ≥ 7x, so **x ≤ 3** [1]
**(b)** (x − 2)(4x + 9) > 0 [1]; critical values x = 2 and x = −9/4 [1]; upward parabola, so **x < −9/4 or x > 2** [1]
**(c)** x < −9/4 [1]; 2 < x ≤ 3 [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is helped by a quick sketch or sign test, to decide between "outside" and "between". For part (c), draw both solution sets on one number line, then check whether each end point is included.

**11. (a)** x² − 4x + 5 = 2x + k [1]; x² − 6x + 5 − k = 0, as required [1]
**(b)** b² − 4ac = 36 − 4(5 − k) = 16 + 4k [1]; tangent means one repeated root, so 16 + 4k = 0 [1]; **k = −4** [1]
**(c)** x² − 6x + 6 = 0 [1]; x = (6 ± √12)/2 = 3 ± √3 [1]; y = 2x − 1 gives **(3 + √3, 5 + 2√3) and (3 − √3, 5 − 2√3)** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (a) the final equation must appear explicitly. In part (c), "exact" rules out decimals, so keep the surds, and find y from the line.

**12. (a)** Length parallel to the wall is 40 − 2x, so A = x(40 − 2x) = **40x − 2x²** [1]
**(b)** −2(x² − 20x) [1]; = 200 − 2(x − 10)², so p = 200 and q = 10 [1]; greatest area **200 m²** when **x = 10** [1]
**(c)** 40x − 2x² ≥ 150, so x² − 20x + 75 ≤ 0 [1]; (x − 5)(x − 15) ≤ 0 [1]; **5 ≤ x ≤ 15** [1]
**(d)** 2x² − 40x + 120 = 0, so x² − 20x + 60 = 0 [1]; x = (20 ± √160)/2 with √160 = 4√10 [1]; **x = 10 ± 2√10**, and both lie between 0 and 20 so both are valid [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* In context questions, state the limits on x (here 0 < x < 20) and check each answer against them. The completed-square form in part (b) also tells you the maximum without any further working.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a common factor uncancelled, such as 20(6 + √11)/25, after rationalising.
- Sign errors in the conjugate, or squaring a bracket with surds and forgetting the middle term.
- Working out the power before the root, then dealing with huge numbers.
- Taking x² and x terms into a bracket without removing the coefficient a first.
- Giving only one root of a quadratic, or answering a "set of values" question with an equation.
- Mixing up "outside the roots" and "between the roots" for quadratic inequalities.
- Finding the second variable from the quadratic instead of the linear equation, producing false pairs.
- Giving decimals when the question says "exact", "in surd form" or "in the form m ± n√10".
- Ignoring the context's limits on a variable, and then accepting an impossible value.
