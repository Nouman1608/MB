## Questions

**1.** A library recorded 300 loans in one week. A pie chart of the loans has these angles: fiction 144°, non-fiction 90°, comics 66°, magazines 60°.

**(a)** Work out the number of fiction loans. **[2]**
**(b)** How many more non-fiction loans than magazine loans were there? **[2]**

**2.** A dual bar chart shows the number of members of a sports club in two years, by age group.

| Age group | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Under 12 | 35 | 42 |
| 12 to 17 | 48 | 45 |
| 18 to 29 | 30 | 36 |
| 30 and over | 22 | 27 |

**(a)** Find the total membership in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the percentage increase in total membership, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**3.** A bar chart compares the annual profit of two companies: A made £52 million and B made £56 million. The vertical axis of the chart starts at £50 million.

**(a)** Explain why the chart is misleading. **[1]**
**(b)** How many times taller than A's bar does B's bar appear? **[1]**
**(c)** Work out B's actual profit as a percentage of A's, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**

**4.** A line graph shows the number of cars in a car park at the start of each hour. The points are joined with straight lines.

| Time | 08:00 | 09:00 | 10:00 | 11:00 | 12:00 | 13:00 | 14:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cars | 40 | 85 | 120 | 135 | 150 | 110 | 70 |

**(a)** In which hour was the increase greatest, and what was it? **[1]**
**(b)** The car park holds 140 cars. Estimate the time at which it first became full. **[2]**
**(c)** The manager says there will be 30 cars at 15:00 because the fall continues at the same rate. Comment. **[1]**

**5.** The battery life, in hours, of eight tests of each of two phone models is recorded.

```
Model P: 14, 15, 15, 16, 17, 18, 18, 19
Model Q: 12, 13, 16, 17, 17, 19, 20, 22
```

**(a)** Find the mean for each model. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the range for each model. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare the two models, using your answers. **[2]**

**6.** Waiting times at two clinics were recorded for 50 patients each.

| Waiting time, t (minutes) | Clinic A | Clinic B |
|---|---|---|
| 0 < t ≤ 10 | 5 | 12 |
| 10 < t ≤ 20 | 14 | 20 |
| 20 < t ≤ 30 | 18 | 10 |
| 30 < t ≤ 40 | 9 | 6 |
| 40 < t ≤ 50 | 4 | 2 |

**(a)** Estimate the mean waiting time at each clinic. **[4]**
**(b)** Compare the waiting times at the two clinics, using the modal classes and your answer to part (a). **[2]**

**7.** The heights of 9 seedlings grown without fertiliser and 9 grown with fertiliser were measured in cm.

```
    Without                | Stem |     With
           8   7   5   2   |  1   |  8
       9   6   4   3   1   |  2   |  1  2  4  5  7  8
                           |  3   |  1  3
```

Key: for the "without" group 4 | 2 means 24 cm. For the "with" group 2 | 4 means 24 cm.

**(a)** Find the median height for each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the range for each group. **[2]**
**(c)** Does the fertiliser help growth? Give a conclusion and one reason for caution. **[2]**

**8.** State the correlation you would expect (positive, negative or none, and weak or strong where it applies).

**(a)** The age of a child (3 to 12 years) and the child's height. **[1]**
**(b)** The height above sea level of a mountain town and its average temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** A pupil's house number and their test mark. **[1]**

**9.** Monthly sales of sun cream and of ice lollies in a seaside shop show strong positive correlation. A shop assistant says that buying sun cream makes customers want lollies.

**(a)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the assistant's claim is not justified by the data. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest another factor that could explain the correlation. **[1]**

**10.** A kiosk records the midday temperature in °C (x) and the number of hot drinks sold that day, in hundreds (y), on ten days.

| x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 14.8 | 13.1 | 12.4 | 11.9 | 10.6 | 9.2 | 8.7 | 7.1 | 6.4 | 5.0 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the mean point (x̄, ȳ). **[2]**
**(c)** Dev draws a line of best fit by eye through (1, 14.4) and (9, 5.2). Show that the line passes close to the mean point. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the equation of Dev's line in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(e)** Use the line to estimate the drinks sold on a day when the temperature is 6.5 °C. **[1]**
**(f)** Explain what the gradient means in this context. **[1]**
**(g)** Say why the line is unsuitable for a day at 20 °C. **[1]**

**11.** A teacher records hours of sleep (x) and reaction time in milliseconds (y) for nine pupils.

| x | 5 | 5.5 | 6 | 6.5 | 7 | 7.5 | 8 | 8.5 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 342 | 331 | 322 | 305 | 296 | 284 | 330 | 262 | 251 |

**(a)** One pupil does not follow the trend. Identify the pupil and give a reason for your choice. **[2]**
**(b)** Ignoring that pupil, find the mean point for the other eight. **[2]**
**(c)** A line of best fit passes through (5, 342) and (9, 250). Find its equation. **[3]**
**(d)** Estimate the reaction time after 7.2 hours of sleep. **[1]**
**(e)** Estimate the reaction time after 10 hours of sleep and comment on its reliability. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Fraction = 144/360 [1]. Loans = 144/360 × 300 = **120** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Non-fiction = 90/360 × 300 = 75 and magazines = 60/360 × 300 = 50 [1]. Difference = 75 - 50 = **25** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert angles to numbers of loans before subtracting. A difference of angles (30°) answers a different question.

**2. (a)** 2024: 35 + 48 + 30 + 22 = **135** [1]. 2025: 42 + 45 + 36 + 27 = **150** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Increase = 150 - 135 = 15 [1]. 15/135 × 100 = **11.1%** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide a percentage change by the starting value. Dividing 15 by 150 gives 10.0%, a common slip.

**3. (a)** The vertical axis starts at £50 million, not 0, so the bars exaggerate the difference [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Visible heights are 6 and 2, so B looks **3 times** as tall [1] **[1]**
**(c)** 56/52 × 100 = **107.7%** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what is wrong with the scale, not only "it is misleading". Linking the 3 times effect to the true ratio makes it specific.

**4. (a)** Between 08:00 and 09:00, an increase of **45** cars [1] **[1]**
**(b)** From 135 at 11:00 the line rises 15 in the hour, so 5 more cars takes 5/15 hour = 20 minutes [1]. Full at about **11:20** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The fall of 40 an hour would give 30, but the graph ends at 14:00, so this extrapolation is unreliable [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* State the steady-change assumption behind an estimate between readings. Predictions beyond the last reading need a comment on reliability.

**5. (a)** P: 132 ÷ 8 = **16.5** hours [1]. Q: 136 ÷ 8 = **17** hours [1] **[2]**
**(b)** P: 19 - 14 = **5** [1]. Q: 22 - 12 = **10** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Q has a slightly higher mean (17 against 16.5) [1], but P is more consistent because its range is smaller (5 against 10) [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong comparison quotes an average and a spread for both sets and concludes in context.

**6. (a)** Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35, 45. A: 5(5) + 14(15) + 18(25) + 9(35) + 4(45) = 25 + 210 + 450 + 315 + 180 = 1180 [1]. 1180 ÷ 50 = **23.6** minutes [1]. B: 60 + 300 + 250 + 210 + 90 = 910 [1]. 910 ÷ 50 = **18.2** minutes [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Modal class: A is 20 < t ≤ 30, B is 10 < t ≤ 20 [1]. Clinic B had shorter waits on average (18.2 against 23.6 minutes) [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the mean is an estimate because midpoints are assumed. Quote both modal classes.

**7. (a)** There are 9 values, so the median is the 5th. Without: **21 cm** [1]. With: **25 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Without: 29 - 12 = **17 cm** [1]. With: 33 - 18 = **15 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The median height was greater with fertiliser (25 cm against 21 cm), so fertiliser may help growth [1]. Caution: only 9 seedlings in each group, a small sample [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Left-hand leaves are written outward from the stem, so read the key carefully. Attach a limit to any inference.

**8. (a)** **Strong positive** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** **Negative** (strong or moderate) [1] **[1]**
**(c)** **No correlation** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give direction and strength together.

**9. (a)** **Strong positive** correlation [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Correlation shows a link, not that one variable causes the other [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Hot weather (or beach visitors) increases both sales [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the extra factor to both quantities; a vague "other things" is weak.

**10. (a)** **Strong negative** correlation [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Mean x = 48 ÷ 10 = **4.8** [1]. Mean y = 99.2 ÷ 10 = **9.92** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** At x = 4.8 the line has y = 14.4 - 1.15(4.8 - 1) = 10.03, close to 9.92 [1] **[1]**
**(d)** m = (5.2 - 14.4) ÷ (9 - 1) = -9.2 ÷ 8 = **-1.15** [1]. 14.4 = -1.15(1) + c [1]. c = 15.55, so **y = -1.15x + 15.55** [1] **[3]**
**(e)** y = -1.15(6.5) + 15.55 = **8.075** hundred, about 810 drinks [1] **[1]**
**(f)** About **115 fewer drinks are sold for each 1 °C rise** in temperature [1] **[1]**
**(g)** 20 °C is far outside the data (1 to 9). The line gives -7.45, an impossible number of drinks [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For a gradient's meaning, give units and direction (a fall). Check your equation against both given points.

**11. (a)** The pupil with 8 hours and 330 ms [1], because it is far above the downward trend (about 273 expected) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Mean x = 55 ÷ 8 = **6.875** [1]. Mean y = 2393 ÷ 8 = **299.1** (to 1 decimal place) [1] **[2]**
**(c)** m = (250 - 342) ÷ (9 - 5) = -92 ÷ 4 = -23 [1]. 342 = -23(5) + c [1]. c = 457, so **y = -23x + 457** [1] **[3]**
**(d)** y = -23(7.2) + 457 = **291.4** ms [1] **[1]**
**(e)** y = -23(10) + 457 = **227** ms [1]. Ten hours is outside the data range (5 to 9), so this extrapolation is unreliable [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Identify the outlier by its values and leave it out when placing the line. Estimates inside the data range are safer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a pie chart angle as the frequency.
- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change.
- Quoting an average but no spread, or numbers for only one set.
- Not saying which direction is better (longer time is worse, longer battery life is better).
- Misreading the key on a back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram.
- Writing "positive" or "weak" alone.
- Claiming correlation proves cause.
- Trusting an extrapolated estimate or missing an impossible value.
