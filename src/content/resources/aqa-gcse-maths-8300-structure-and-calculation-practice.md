These questions cover section 3.1.1, **Structure and calculation** (N1 to N9), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification, for teaching from September 2015 with exams from May/June 2017 (version 1.0). Parts on the product rule (N5), estimating roots (N6), fractional indices (N7) and surds (N8) are Higher tier only and are labelled "(Higher)"; everything else suits both tiers. Each question is labelled "(non-calculator)" in the style of Paper 1 or "(calculator)" in the style of Papers 2 and 3. These questions are different from the ones in the [Number practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-mathematics-number-practice/), which cover the whole of Topic 1.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-structure-and-calculation/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-structure-and-calculation-revision-notes/). The course hub is [AQA GCSE Mathematics](/boards/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) lists every statement.

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write these numbers in order, smallest first: 0.7, 2/3, −0.75, −4/5, 0.67 **[2]**
**(b)** Write < or > between −3/8 and −0.4 **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) You are told that 63 × 48 = 3024. Use this fact to write down the value of:

**(a)** 6.3 × 0.48 **[1]**
**(b)** 302.4 ÷ 4.8 **[1]**
**(c)** 0.3024 ÷ 0.063 **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Work out:

**(a)** 3 2/5 − 1 3/4, giving your answer as a mixed number **[2]**
**(b)** −2 1/4 ÷ 1 1/2 **[2]**
**(c)** (−0.3) × (−0.4) − 0.5 **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Work out 20 − 4 × 3² ÷ 6 **[2]**
**(b)** Insert one pair of brackets to make this correct: 8 + 12 ÷ 4 − 2 = 14 **[1]**
**(c)** Work out 2/5 of the reciprocal of 0.25 **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 252 as a product of its prime factors in index form. **[2]**
**(b)** A = 2³ × 3 × 5² and B = 2 × 3² × 5 × 7. Find the HCF and the LCM of A and B, leaving your answers in index form or as ordinary numbers. **[2]**
**(c)** Two warning lights are switched on together. One flashes every 40 seconds and the other every 75 seconds. After how many minutes do they next flash together? **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Ravi chooses two different fruits from apple (A), banana (B), cherry (C) and date (D). Order does not matter. List all the possible pairs. **[2]**
**(b)** (Higher) A password is three letters from A, B, C, D, E (repeats allowed) followed by two odd digits (repeats allowed). How many passwords are possible? **[2]**
**(c)** (Higher) How many four-digit numbers greater than 5000 have every digit even? **[2]**

**7.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write down the value of 13² and of ∛125 **[2]**
**(b)** (Higher) Show that √90 lies between 9.4 and 9.5 **[2]**
**(c)** Write 7⁵ × 7⁻² ÷ 7⁶ as a single power of 7, then find its value as a fraction. **[2]**
**(d)** 2ⁿ = 1/32. Find n. **[1]**
**(e)** (Higher) Work out 125^(−2/3) **[2]**
**(f)** (Higher) Work out (16/81)^(3/4) **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A shape is made from a square of side 8 cm with a semicircle drawn outwards on one side, so the diameter of the semicircle is that side.

**(a)** Find the area of the shape. Give your answer in terms of π. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the perimeter of the shape. Give your answer in terms of π. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) (Higher) A rectangle has length √75 cm and width √12 cm.

**(a)** Show that its area is 30 cm². **[2]**
**(b)** Find its perimeter in the form k√3. **[2]**
**(c)** Write 30/√12 in the form a√3, and explain what your answer represents. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) Give each answer in standard form.

**(a)** (6 × 10⁴) × (4 × 10⁻⁹) **[2]**
**(b)** (9 × 10⁷) ÷ (1.5 × 10³) **[1]**
**(c)** 5.2 × 10⁶ − 8 × 10⁵ **[2]**

**11.** (calculator)

**(a)** A calculator shows 4.56E−03. Write this as an ordinary number. **[1]**
**(b)** A country spends £9.38 × 10¹¹ in a year and has 6.7 × 10⁷ people. Work out the spending per person. Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**
**(c)** A virus particle has a diameter of 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ m. How many particles placed end to end would make a line 3 cm long? Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator)

**(a)** Leah's bank account has a balance of £245.60. She then has debits of £82.35 and £129.99, a credit of £310.00 and a debit of £412.50. Work out her new balance and say what it means. **[3]**
**(b)** A trader buys 24 cushions at a cost price of £3.85 each. He sells 15 of them at a selling price of £5.20 each. Has he made a profit or a loss so far, and how much? **[2]**
**(c)** What is the least number of extra cushions he must sell at £5.20 to make a profit on all 24? **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Convert: −4/5 = −0.8, 2/3 = 0.666… [1]; **−4/5, −0.75, 2/3, 0.67, 0.7** [1]
**(b)** −3/8 = −0.375 so **−3/8 > −0.4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final order must use the original values; a list of decimals only loses the accuracy mark, and 2/3 must sit below 0.67 because 0.666… is less than 0.670.

**2. (a)** **3.024** [1]
**(b)** **63** [1]
**(c)** **4.8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each part is a single accuracy mark with no method credit, so check every answer with an estimate (300 ÷ 5 ≈ 60 for part (b)).

**3. (a)** 17/5 − 7/4 = 68/20 − 35/20 [1] = 33/20 = **1 13/20** [1]
**(b)** −9/4 × 2/3 [1] = −18/12 = **−1 1/2** [1]
**(c)** 0.12 − 0.5 = **−0.38** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Method marks are for converting to improper fractions with a common denominator or a reciprocal; an unsimplified 33/20 when a mixed number is asked for loses the final mark.

**4. (a)** 3² = 9, then 4 × 9 ÷ 6 = 6 [1]; 20 − 6 = **14** [1]
**(b)** **8 + 12 ÷ (4 − 2) = 14** [1]
**(c)** Reciprocal of 0.25 is 4 [1]; 2/5 × 4 = **8/5 = 1.6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), working left to right gives (20 − 4) × 9 ÷ 6 = 24 and scores nothing, because the method mark is for applying priority correctly.

**5. (a)** 252 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 7 [1] = **2² × 3² × 7** [1]
**(b)** HCF = 2 × 3 × 5 = **30** [1]; LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5² × 7 = **12 600** [1]
**(c)** 40 = 2³ × 5 and 75 = 3 × 5², LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5² = 600 seconds [1]; **10 minutes** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A correct product without index form earns only the method mark in (a), and in (c) an answer of 600 without converting to minutes loses the final mark.

**6. (a)** AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD: at least four correct with no repeats [1]; all **6** pairs and no extras [1]
**(b)** 5³ × 5² (five letters, five odd digits) [1] = **3125** [1]
**(c)** First digit 6 or 8, then 5 choices for each other digit: 2 × 5 × 5 × 5 [1] = **250** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Listing BA as well as AB counts as a repeat and loses the second mark in (a); in (b) and (c) adding the choices instead of multiplying earns no method mark.

**7. (a)** **169** [1]; **5** [1]
**(b)** 9.4² = 88.36 and 9.5² = 90.25 [1]; 88.36 < 90 < 90.25, so 9.4 < √90 < 9.5 [1]
**(c)** 7^(5 − 2 − 6) = **7⁻³** [1]; **1/343** [1]
**(d)** 32 = 2⁵, so **n = −5** [1]
**(e)** Cube root of 125 = 5, 5² = 25 [1]; reciprocal gives **1/25** [1]
**(f)** Fourth root of 16/81 = 2/3 [1]; (2/3)³ = **8/27** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that" in (b), both squares must be worked out and compared with 90; stating "√90 ≈ 9.49" from memory earns no marks on a non-calculator paper.

**8. (a)** Square 64; semicircle radius 4, area ½ × π × 4² = 8π [1]; total **64 + 8π cm²** [1]
**(b)** Three sides 3 × 8 = 24; curved edge ½ × 2 × π × 4 = 4π [1]; total **24 + 4π cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The side covered by the semicircle is not part of the perimeter, so 32 + 4π loses the accuracy mark; a decimal answer loses it too when "in terms of π" is asked.

**9. (a)** √75 × √12 = √900 [1] = 30, so the area is **30 cm²** [1]
**(b)** √75 = 5√3 and √12 = 2√3 [1]; 2(5√3 + 2√3) = **14√3 cm** [1]
**(c)** 30/(2√3) = 15/√3 = 15√3/3 = **5√3** [1]; it is the **length** of the rectangle (area ÷ width) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a) "show that" needs the √900 step written down; in (c) leaving 15√3/3 uncancelled loses the accuracy mark.

**10. (a)** 24 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **2.4 × 10⁻⁴** [1]
**(b)** **6 × 10⁴** [1]
**(c)** 5.2 × 10⁶ − 0.8 × 10⁶ [1] = **4.4 × 10⁶** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 24 × 10⁻⁵ is correct in value but not in standard form, so it earns the method mark only.

**11. (a)** **0.00456** [1]
**(b)** 9.38 × 10¹¹ ÷ 6.7 × 10⁷ = 14 000 [1] = **£1.4 × 10⁴** [1]
**(c)** 3 cm = 0.03 m (or 3 × 10⁻² m) [1]; 0.03 ÷ (1.2 × 10⁻⁷) [1] = **2.5 × 10⁵** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the units must match before dividing; using 3 instead of 0.03 gives 2.5 × 10⁷ and keeps only the division method mark.

**12. (a)** 245.60 − 82.35 − 129.99 + 310.00 − 412.50 [1] = −69.24 [1]; **−£69.24, so she is overdrawn by £69.24** [1]
**(b)** Cost 24 × 3.85 = £92.40 and takings 15 × 5.20 = £78.00 [1]; **a loss of £14.40** [1]
**(c)** 92.40 ÷ 5.20 = 17.769… [1]; so 18 must be sold in total (18 × 5.20 = £93.60 > £92.40) [1]; **3 more cushions** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) rounding 17.769… down to 17 gives £88.40, still a loss; the final mark needs the answer as extra cushions (3), not the total (18).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Ordering negative decimals by the size of their digits, so −0.75 is placed before −0.8.
- Moving the decimal point the wrong number of places when using a given product.
- Working left to right instead of using priority of operations.
- Using the HCF when the context needs the LCM, or giving seconds when minutes were asked for.
- Counting AB and BA as different when order does not matter.
- (Higher) Adding instead of multiplying with the product rule.
- (Higher) Ignoring the minus sign in a negative fractional index, so 125^(−2/3) is given as 25 instead of 1/25.
- Including the hidden side in the perimeter of a compound shape.
- Leaving standard form with A outside 1 ≤ A < 10.
- Rounding down in a "how many must be sold" problem.
