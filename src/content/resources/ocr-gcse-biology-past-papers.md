This page is a guide to OCR GCSE (9-1) Biology A (Gateway Science, J247) past papers from June 2023 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and, where OCR's examiners' report for that paper was available, a short account of what examiners saw, written by us. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR GCSE Biology A (Gateway Science, J247) qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/gateway-science-suite-biology-a-j247-from-2016/)
- [OCR GCSE Biology A (J247) assessment page, with question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/gateway-science-suite-biology-a-j247-from-2016/assessment/)
- [OCR grade boundaries](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/grade-boundaries-archive/grade-boundaries-archive.aspx)

Use paper, mark scheme and examiners' report together. Sit the paper under timed conditions, mark it strictly with the mark scheme, then read the report for that paper to see why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series are a quick way into the report, not a replacement for it.

Check which specification a paper belongs to. J247 is Gateway Science Biology A; OCR's Biology B (Twenty First Century Science) is J257 and has different boundaries and papers. J247 has four 90-mark papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes: Foundation Papers 1 and 2 (J247/01 and J247/02) and Higher Papers 3 and 4 (J247/03 and J247/04). Papers 1 and 3 test topics B1 to B3 and B7 practical skills; Papers 2 and 4 test topics B4 to B6 and B7, with assumed knowledge of B1 to B3. Every series below uses this structure. For topic-by-topic study, start with our [cell level systems notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-biology-cell-level-systems/).

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe papers on which candidates attempted more of the paper than before and picked up marks on calculations, especially where working was shown. Examiners kept returning to command words and to using the information given in a question.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J247/01, Foundation): the parts of DNA; cell structures in different types of cell and what sub-cellular structures do; enzyme action; blood vessels and eye structure and function; drawing conclusions in the Level of Response question. Answers that gave a description when an explanation was asked for also lost marks.
- Paper 2 (J247/02, Foundation): matching a disease to the organ it affects; drawing conclusions from data; deciding which numbers to use in a calculation; identifying trophic levels from a diagram; describing how to use a quadrat. Candidates were more confident choosing from a list than writing freely.
- Paper 3 (J247/03, Higher): describing how to set up a light microscope; finishing a two-step calculation; transcription and translation mixed up; 'amount' written where volume or concentration was needed for a control variable; cerebrum and cerebellum confused. Practical planning and unit work had improved on earlier series, but accuracy, validity and reliability were still muddled by many.
- Paper 4 (J247/04, Higher): naming the type of organism that causes disease; extending an experiment; judging evidence on food security; explaining inheritance from a family tree; explaining why stem cells can make many white blood cells; the effects of an invasive species; the genetic engineering Level of Response question, where many stopped at Level 2. Examiners also asked for more practice with enquiry skills (AO3).

## June 2024

OCR's assessment page lists examiners' reports for all four papers in the 2024 slot, but the files for Paper 2 and Paper 4 are the June 2023 reports. We have therefore summarised only Papers 1 and 3 for this series; the 2023 section below covers the Paper 2 and Paper 4 reports.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J247/01, Foundation): the advantages of the electron microscope; how insulin responds after a meal; the steps of a reflex action; how different contraceptive methods work; osmosis in plants; drawing conclusions from a graph in the Level of Response question. Scientific drawing, aerobic respiration, the protein test and control variables were handled better.
- Paper 3 (J247/03, Higher): explaining a mass change in terms of water moving by osmosis rather than a solution moving; unit conversions between millimetres and micrometres and between litres and millilitres; Benedict's test colours mixed up with those of other reagents; control variables confused with independent and dependent variables; DNA's function described as making different amino acids. Practical-based answers were better than in the previous series.

## June 2023

June 2023 was the first series without advance information on topics since the pandemic, and examiners noted that the cohort had met remote teaching. The notes below cover all four papers.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J247/01, Foundation): DNA, cell structure, microscopes, the endocrine and nervous systems, identifying ovulation, calculations, and the Level of Response question on measuring the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed. Cell division, digestive enzymes and blood vessels were handled better by many.
- Paper 2 (J247/02, Foundation): using the wrong method to find a ratio; describing changes to an experiment; naming benefits and consequences in a habitat without explaining them; mixing up ways of estimating plant numbers in an area. Unit conversions and conclusions from graphs were generally sound.
- Paper 3 (J247/03, Higher): percentage change and rounding to three significant figures; conclusions that the data could not support; protein synthesis described without the effect of a mutation; mixing up methods for measuring photosynthesis and transpiration.
- Paper 4 (J247/04, Higher): conditions needed for decomposition; the functions of the iris and cerebellum; Punnett square crosses; natural selection; calculations. Stronger answers covered the immune response, capture-recapture sampling, asexual reproduction and the enzymes used in genetic engineering.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR J247 past papers and mark schemes?**
On the assessment page for OCR GCSE Biology A, linked above, which lists question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports by series.

**Which papers should a Foundation or Higher candidate use?**
Foundation candidates use Papers 1 and 2 (J247/01 and 02) and Higher candidates use Papers 3 and 4 (J247/03 and 04). Check the paper code on the front cover.

**Why are some grade columns missing in the tables?**
OCR's documents show n/a where a tier cannot award a grade. Foundation papers award grades 5 to 1 and Higher papers 9 to 3.

**What mistakes do examiners report most often?**
Answers that describe when the command word asks for an explanation, unit conversions, calculations left unfinished, confusion between control, independent and dependent variables, and trouble drawing conclusions from data.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and are only a guide for the series they belong to.

## About these notes

The grade thresholds are copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and the examiner notes are our own summaries of OCR's examiners' reports for the series listed above, written in our words. Thresholds change every series and must never be used to predict future ones. If you spot a mistake, please [report a correction](/report-a-correction/).
