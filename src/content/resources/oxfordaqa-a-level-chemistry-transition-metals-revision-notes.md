## 3.2.5.1 Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transition metal properties (Ti to Cu) | Complexes, coloured ions, variable oxidation state, catalytic activity |
| Their cause | An incomplete d sub-level in the atom or ion |
| Ligand | Molecule or ion giving an electron pair to a transition metal ion to form a co-ordinate bond |
| Complex | Central metal atom or ion with ligands around it |
| Co-ordination number | Number of co-ordinate bonds formed by the central metal atom or ion |

Ions lose 4s electrons before 3d. Cu²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁹; V³⁺ is [Ar] 3d².

## 3.2.5.2 Ligand substitution

| Swap | Co-ordination number | Why |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O ⇄ NH₃ (Co²⁺, Cu²⁺) | stays 6 | same size, both uncharged |
| H₂O → Cl⁻ (Co²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe³⁺) | 6 → 4 | Cl⁻ is larger |

- Complete swap: [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺. Incomplete swap: [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ (blue to deep blue).
- With Cl⁻: [CoCl₄]²⁻ (pink to blue), [CuCl₄]²⁻, [FeCl₄]⁻.
- Bidentate: H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ (two N donors), C₂O₄²⁻ (two O donors). Multidentate: EDTA⁴⁻ (six donors).
- Haem: iron(II) with a multidentate ligand. O₂ bonds co-ordinately to Fe(II) for transport in blood. CO replaces the O₂, so it is toxic.

### Method in steps: explain the chelate effect

1. Write the equation and count particles on each side.
2. More particles on the right, so **ΔS is positive**.
3. Similar bonds broken and formed (same donor atom type, same number), so **ΔH is small**.
4. ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is therefore **negative**; the chelating ligand replaces the monodentate ones.
5. If asked to calculate, change ΔS from J to kJ first.

## 3.2.5.3 Shapes and isomers

| Shape | Bond angle | Forms with | Stereoisomerism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Octahedral | 90° | small ligands such as H₂O and NH₃ | cis–trans with monodentate ligands; optical with bidentate ones |
| Tetrahedral | 109.5° | larger ligands such as Cl⁻ | none listed |
| Square planar | 90° | four ligands in a plane, as in [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | cis–trans; the cis form is cisplatin |
| Linear | 180° | Ag⁺ with two NH₃ (Tollens') | none |

Cis–trans isomerism in complexes is a special case of E–Z isomerism.

**Worked reminder.** [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺: two bidentate ligands give four bonds and two waters give two more, so the co-ordination number is 6 and the ion is octahedral. Both diamines are neutral, so nickel is +2. The two water ligands can sit at 90° or at 180°, so cis and trans isomers are possible.

## 3.2.5.4 Colour

- The ion takes in certain visible wavelengths (**absorbed**); you see the ones it **transmits or reflects**.
- That energy lifts a d electron out of its ground state into an excited state.
- **ΔE = hν = hc/λ**. Changing the **oxidation state, co-ordination number or ligand** changes ΔE, so the colour changes.
- Spectroscopy relies on absorption of visible light; a simple colorimeter measures concentration.

### Method in steps: ΔE calculations

1. Convert λ from nm to m (× 10⁻⁹).
2. ΔE (J, one ion) = hc/λ, or hν if you have frequency.
3. For kJ mol⁻¹: multiply by L, then divide by 1000.
4. Going the other way: divide kJ mol⁻¹ by L (after × 1000) before using λ = hc/ΔE.

### Method in steps: colorimetry

Filter of the colour absorbed most → zero with solvent → standards → calibration graph → read unknown → multiply by any dilution factor.

**Worked reminder.** A calibration line has gradient 4.0 dm³ mol⁻¹. A solution diluted 1 in 20 reads 0.36. Diluted concentration = 0.36 ÷ 4.0 = 0.090 mol dm⁻³, so the original was 0.090 × 20 = **1.80 mol dm⁻³**.

## 3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states

| Colour | V species | Oxidation state |
|---|---|---|
| yellow | VO₂⁺ | +5 |
| blue | VO²⁺ | +4 |
| green | V³⁺ | +3 |
| violet | V²⁺ | +2 |

Zinc in acid reduces vanadate(V) through all four. Green also appears briefly as a yellow–blue mixture.

- **pH effect**: half-equations with H⁺ (such as MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺) shift with acidity; more H⁺ makes the higher oxidation state easier to reduce.
- **Ligand effect**: E⦵(Ag⁺/Ag) = +0.80 V but E⦵([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺/Ag) = +0.37 V.
- **Tollens' reagent**: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃. Silver mirror with an aldehyde; no change with a ketone.

### Titration ratios

| Reaction | MnO₄⁻ : other |
|---|---|
| MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O | 1 : 5 |
| 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O | 2 : 5 |

### Method in steps: MnO₄⁻ titration

1. Moles of MnO₄⁻ = concentration × titre (in dm³).
2. Use the ratio to get moles of analyte in the portion titrated.
3. Scale up to the whole solution (for example × 10 for 25.0 cm³ from 250 cm³).
4. Convert to mass, percentage or Mr as asked.
5. For an unfamiliar reductant, build the equation from half-equations first, then repeat steps 1 to 4.

No indicator is needed: stop at the first pink that stays. Acidify with dilute H₂SO₄, never HCl.

## 3.2.5.6 Catalysts

| Catalyst | Type | Reaction | How it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| V₂O₅ | heterogeneous | Contact process | V +5 → +4 oxidising SO₂, then back to +5 with O₂ |
| Fe | heterogeneous | Haber process | active sites on the surface |
| Fe²⁺ | homogeneous | S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻ | Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺; each step between oppositely charged ions |
| Mn²⁺ | homogeneous, autocatalyst | MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ | Mn²⁺ → Mn³⁺ → Mn²⁺; a product speeds up the reaction |

- Heterogeneous: different phase; **support medium** for a large surface area at low cost; **poisoning** blocks active sites and costs money.
- Homogeneous: same phase; goes through an **intermediate species**.
- Variable oxidation states let the catalyst pass electrons on and be regenerated.

Key equations:

```
V₂O₅ + SO₂ → V₂O₄ + SO₃        V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅
2Fe²⁺ + S₂O₈²⁻ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2SO₄²⁻        2I⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ → I₂ + 2Fe²⁺
MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 4Mn²⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O        C₂O₄²⁻ + 2Mn³⁺ → 2CO₂ + 2Mn²⁺
```

## Must-know distinctions

- **Mono-, bi- and multidentate**: ligands with one, two or many donor atoms.
- **Number of ligands vs co-ordination number**: [Co(C₂O₄)₃]⁴⁻ has three ligands but six bonds.
- **Cis vs trans**: identical ligands at 90° vs 180°.
- **Absorbed vs seen**: the colour you see is what is not absorbed.
- **Heterogeneous vs homogeneous**: different phase vs same phase.
- **Catalyst vs autocatalyst**: an autocatalyst is formed in the very reaction it catalyses.

## Quick self-test

1. What are the oxidation state and co-ordination number of iron in [Fe(EDTA)]⁻?
2. Why does replacing H₂O by Cl⁻ around Cu²⁺ change the co-ordination number?
3. Count the particles on each side: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3C₂O₄²⁻ → [Co(C₂O₄)₃]⁴⁻ + 6H₂O.
4. A ligand exchange has ΔH = −2.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +96 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Find ΔG at 310 K.
5. A d-electron transition needs 3.70 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Find λ in nm (c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s).
6. Give the formula and colour of vanadium(III) in solution.
7. What do you see when Tollens' reagent is warmed with a ketone?
8. How many moles of Fe²⁺ react with 13.20 cm³ of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻?
9. How many moles of C₂O₄²⁻ react with 21.60 cm³ of 0.0120 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻?
10. Why does the MnO₄⁻ and C₂O₄²⁻ reaction start slowly?
11. What shape is the silver complex in Tollens' reagent, and what is its bond angle?

### Answers

1. Fe − 4 = −1, so **+3**; co-ordination number **6** (EDTA⁴⁻ has six donor atoms).
2. Cl⁻ is **larger** than H₂O, so only four fit round the ion: 6 → 4.
3. **4 on the left, 7 on the right**; entropy increases.
4. ΔG = −2.0 − (310 × 0.096) = **−31.8 kJ mol⁻¹**.
5. λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 3.70 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 5.38 × 10⁻⁷ m = **538 nm**.
6. **V³⁺, green**.
7. **No change**; ketones are not oxidised by it.
8. 0.0150 × 0.01320 = 1.98 × 10⁻⁴ mol MnO₄⁻, × 5 = **9.90 × 10⁻⁴ mol**.
9. 0.0120 × 0.02160 = 2.592 × 10⁻⁴ mol MnO₄⁻, × 5/2 = **6.48 × 10⁻⁴ mol**.
10. Two **negative ions repel**, and the catalyst, **Mn²⁺**, has not formed yet.
11. **Linear, 180°**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing [Ar] 4s² 3d⁷ for Co²⁺: the 4s electrons are removed first, so it is [Ar] 3d⁷.
- Calling [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ four-co-ordinate; the two waters still count.
- Leaving out the entropy argument, or the particle count, in a chelate explanation.
- Forgetting to convert ΔS into kJ before using ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
- Leaving λ in nm inside hc/λ.
- Stating that the colour seen is the colour absorbed.
- Writing the V₂O₅ steps without showing that the catalyst is regenerated.
- Using the Fe²⁺ ratio (1 : 5) for ethanedioate (2 : 5).
- Forgetting to scale up from the titrated portion to the whole solution.
