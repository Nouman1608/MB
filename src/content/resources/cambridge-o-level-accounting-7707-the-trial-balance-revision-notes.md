## What the syllabus asks

1. The purposes and limitations of a trial balance.
2. Preparing a trial balance from a list of balances, and amending one that
contains errors.
3. The six errors that do not affect the trial balance: commission,
compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry, principle.

## Core idea in one line

A trial balance lists every ledger balance **at a date** in debit and credit
columns; equal totals show the double entry is arithmetically sound, but not
that every entry is right.

## Purposes

- Checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry.
- Signals that errors exist before statements are prepared.
- Summarises balances for the statement of profit or loss and statement of
financial position.

## Limitations

- Six types of error leave the totals in agreement.
- It does not show whether an item has been recorded in the right account
class, the right period or at the right value.
- Agreement can be a coincidence, as with compensating errors.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trial balance | List of ledger balances at a stated date in debit and credit columns |
| Debit balance | Debit side of the account exceeds the credit side (assets, expenses, drawings) |
| Credit balance | Credit side exceeds the debit side (capital, liabilities, incomes) |
| Casting | Adding up a column or account; an overcast total is too high, an undercast one too low |
| Error of commission | Right amount, right side, wrong account of the same class |
| Error of principle | Right amount, right side, wrong class of account |
| Error of original entry | Wrong amount in the book of prime entry, copied to both accounts |
| Complete reversal | Right accounts, right amount, debit and credit swapped |
| Error of omission | Transaction not recorded at all |
| Compensating error | Two errors of equal value that cancel |

## Which column?

**Debit:** assets; expenses (including carriage inwards, carriage outwards
and discount allowed); purchases; sales returns; drawings; opening
inventory; cash; bank when in funds.

**Credit:** capital; liabilities (trade payables, loans, bank overdraft);
revenue; purchases returns; incomes (rent received, commission received,
discount received); provision for depreciation; allowance for irrecoverable
debts.

Memory aid: anything that **reduces** an asset (a provision or an allowance)
sits opposite the asset, on the credit side.

## Amending a trial balance: method

1. Check each item against the column rules above.
2. List the items in the wrong column and add them up.
3. Redraft the whole trial balance with a heading ("at" a date), the
corrected columns and new totals.
4. Check: one balance in the wrong column makes the difference **twice** its
amount.

Example: the totals differ by $860. A balance of $430, such as discount
received, may be on the wrong side.

## The six hidden errors: recognition test

Ask two questions about any error.

1. **Was the same amount debited and credited?** If not, the trial balance
will not agree, so it is not one of the six.
2. If yes, **what went wrong?**
   - Nothing recorded: **omission**.
   - Wrong figure in the book of prime entry: **original entry**.
   - Debit and credit swapped: **complete reversal**.
   - Wrong account, same class (two customers, two expenses): **commission**.
   - Wrong class (expense versus non-current asset): **principle**.
   - Two separate mistakes of equal size: **compensating**.

## Errors that do show up

- One side of an entry missed out.
- Both entries on the same side.
- Different amounts on the two sides (for example $540 debited, $450
credited).
- An account or a journal total added up wrongly and posted.
- A balance left out of the trial balance or put in the wrong column.

These lead to a suspense account, covered in 3.2.

## Exam traps

- "At 31 December", never "for the year ended".
- Only **opening** inventory goes in the list; closing inventory is a note.
- A bank overdraft is a credit balance, even though "bank" is usually a
debit.
- Discount allowed (expense) is a debit; discount received (income) is a
credit.
- A transposition (writing $1,620 as $1,260) is an error of original entry
only if it is in the book of prime entry and so both postings use the wrong
figure. Made in one account only, it does affect the trial balance.
- Commission versus principle turns on the **class** of account.
- Totals that agree do not prove that the books are correct.

## Quick self-test

1. State two purposes of a trial balance.
2. Give one limitation of a trial balance.
3. In which column does the allowance for irrecoverable debts appear?
4. In which column does carriage outwards appear?
5. A trial balance is out by $1,500. One balance is in the wrong column.
What is its amount?
6. Rent paid to the landlord, $900, was debited to the rates account. Name
the error.
7. Office furniture bought for $1,200 was debited to purchases. Name the
error.
8. A sales invoice for $2,450 was entered in the sales journal as $2,540.
Name the error.
9. Does posting a $310 cheque received from a customer to the debit of the
customer's account and the debit of the bank account affect the trial
balance?
10. Cash drawings of $200 were debited to cash and credited to drawings.
Name the error.
11. Why is closing inventory usually not in the trial balance?
12. A trial balance agrees, yet the purchases account is overcast by $50
and the revenue account is overcast by $50. Name the error.

### Answers

1. Any two: checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry; shows
that errors exist; provides a summary for preparing the financial
statements.
2. Any one: some errors do not affect it; it does not prove the entries are
in the right accounts; it does not check periods or values.
3. Credit.
4. Debit (it is an expense).
5. $750 ($1,500 ÷ 2).
6. Commission (both are expense accounts).
7. Principle (a non-current asset recorded as an expense).
8. Original entry.
9. Yes. Two debits and no credit make the debit total $620 higher than the
credit total.
10. Complete reversal.
11. It is counted at the year end and has not yet been entered in the
ledger, so it is given as a note and used in the statements.
12. Compensating errors.
