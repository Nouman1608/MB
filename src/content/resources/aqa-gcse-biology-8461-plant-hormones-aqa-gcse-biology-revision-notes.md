These notes condense section **4.5.4 Plant hormones** (4.5.4.1 and 4.5.4.2, with required practical activity 8)
of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. The section is **Biology only**.
Gibberellins, ethene and all uses of plant hormones are **Higher tier only**. It is assessed on Paper 2.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [Plant hormones study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-hormones-aqa-gcse-biology/).
Then try the [Plant hormones practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-hormones-aqa-gcse-biology-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Plant hormone | A chemical that coordinates and controls plant growth and responses |
| Phototropism | A growth response to light |
| Gravitropism (geotropism) | A growth response to gravity |
| Auxin | A plant hormone; unequal distribution causes unequal growth in roots and shoots |
| Cell elongation | Cells growing longer; how auxin makes shoots grow |
| Gibberellins | Hormones important in initiating seed germination (Higher tier only) |
| Ethene | A hormone controlling cell division and fruit ripening (Higher tier only) |
| Seed dormancy | A period when a seed does not germinate |

## Auxin: the two rules

- In **shoots**, auxin **stimulates** growth.
- In **roots**, auxin **inhibits** growth.

## The three responses

| Situation | Where auxin collects | Effect | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoot lit from one side | Shaded side | Shaded side elongates faster | Bends towards light |
| Shoot on its side | Lower side | Lower side grows faster | Bends upwards |
| Root on its side | Lower side | Lower side grows more slowly | Bends downwards |

Why it matters: shoots reach light for photosynthesis; roots reach water and mineral ions and anchor the plant.

## Required practical 8

- Investigate the effect of **light or gravity** on **newly germinated seedlings**.
- Record results as **length measurements** and **labelled biological drawings**.
- Light: compare one-sided light, all-round light and darkness.
- Gravity: seeds on damp cotton wool in a dish stood on its edge, in the dark; turn some dishes.
- Controls: type and age of seedlings, temperature, water, number of seeds.
- Drawings: single clear lines, no shading, ruled label lines, accurate angles.

## Gibberellins and ethene (Higher tier only)

- **Gibberellins:** initiate seed germination.
- **Ethene:** controls cell division and fruit ripening.
- Mechanisms are not required.

## Uses (Higher tier only)

| Hormone | Uses |
|---|---|
| Auxins | Weed killers; rooting powders; promoting growth in tissue culture |
| Ethene | Controlling ripening of fruit during storage and transport |
| Gibberellins | Ending seed dormancy; promoting flowering; increasing fruit size |

**Weedkillers and biodiversity:** removing weeds removes wild plant species and the food and shelter they give
animals, so biodiversity falls.

## Explanation chains

- **Bending towards light:** light from one side → auxin to shaded side → shaded cells elongate more → shoot
  bends towards the light.
- **Root growing down:** root sideways → auxin to lower side → lower side growth inhibited → upper side grows
  faster → root bends down.
- **Rooting powder (Higher tier only):** auxin on a cutting → roots grow → new plant established quickly.
- **Ethene (Higher tier only):** fruit picked unripe → firm, travels without damage → ethene added at the
  destination → fruit ripens when needed.

## Analysing practical 8 results

- Calculate a **mean** for each condition, leaving out anomalies. Example: 18, 20, 19, 31 and 21 mm → 31 is
  anomalous → (18 + 20 + 19 + 21) ÷ 4 = **19.5 mm**.
- Compare the **direction** of growth as well as the length: bending shows a tropism; length alone does not.
- Shoots grown in darkness are often longer, pale and thin; light from one side gives bent shoots.
- Use the drawings to show the angle of bending accurately.

## Comparing the responses

| | Shoot | Root |
|---|---|---|
| Response to light from one side | Grows towards light | Not required at GCSE |
| Response to gravity | Grows upwards (against gravity) | Grows downwards (with gravity) |
| Auxin's effect | Stimulates growth | Inhibits growth |
| Benefit | More light for photosynthesis | Water, mineral ions, anchorage |

## Exam traps

- Auxin **inhibits** root growth; it does not speed it up.
- Auxin collects on the **lower** side of both roots and shoots placed sideways; the responses differ because
  the effects differ.
- At GCSE, auxin **moves** to the shaded side; it is not destroyed by light.
- Shoots bend **away** from the side with more auxin (that side grows faster).
- Ethene controls **ripening**, not fruit size; gibberellins increase fruit size.
- Rooting powders contain **auxins**, not gibberellins.
- Results in practical 8 need both **measurements** and **drawings**.

## Self-test

1. What is phototropism?
2. What is another name for gravitropism?
3. What does auxin do in shoots?
4. What does auxin do in roots?
5. A shoot is lit from the left. Which side does auxin move to?
6. Why does a root placed on its side bend downwards?
7. Why is it useful for a shoot to grow towards light?
8. In practical 8, why is a dish of seedlings kept in all-round light?
9. Give two ways of recording results in practical 8.
10. (Higher tier only) What do gibberellins initiate?
11. (Higher tier only) Give two uses of auxins.
12. (Higher tier only) Why is fruit often picked unripe and ripened with ethene later?

### Answers

1. A growth response to light.
2. Geotropism.
3. Stimulates growth by cell elongation.
4. Inhibits growth.
5. The right (shaded) side.
6. Auxin collects on the lower side and inhibits growth there, so the upper side grows faster.
7. It receives more light for photosynthesis.
8. As a control, for comparison with one-sided light.
9. Length measurements; labelled biological drawings.
10. Seed germination.
11. Any two: weed killers; rooting powders; promoting growth in tissue culture.
12. Unripe fruit is firmer, so it is less damaged during storage and transport; it is ripened when needed.
