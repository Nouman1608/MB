The thematic study **Conflict (from 750 CE onwards)** is one of four options in IB DP History (first assessment 2028). Teachers choose one of the four thematic studies, and it is assessed in paper 2. The guide sets four inquiry questions, each with four lines of inquiry, and **prescribes no examples**. You choose examples with your teacher, and each line of inquiry must be explored using at least two examples from more than one region. This guide explains the framework, offers a bank of illustrative examples (you may use others), and shows how to build paper 2 answers. It is the same for SL and HL.

## What the guide means by conflict

The guide defines conflict as a situation where two or more groups use violence to resolve disputes. It sits at one end of a spectrum, with peaceful cooperation at the other, and relations between groups can contain both. Types of conflict listed include civil wars, interstate wars, wars of independence, revolutions, terrorist movements, total wars, world wars, proxy wars and the Cold War. You may focus on one type or range widely. Sensitive topics must be handled with care.

## How the study is assessed

Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 25 marks.

- **Section A (6 marks):** choose one of two questions on a specified concept, illustrated by an example from your thematic study.
- **Section B:** choose one of two questions on your thematic study. Part (a) is a short response (4 marks) on one example. Part (b) is an essay (15 marks) that must use examples from **at least two regions**.

The four regions are Africa and the Middle East, the Americas, Asia and Oceania, and Europe. An example may represent only one region in a response. For the essay, the top markband (13 to 15) needs consistently analytical writing, a reasoned judgement, relevant examples, and well-developed connections between examples that are integrated into the argument.

## Inquiry question 1: Why did conflict emerge?

**Lines of inquiry:** economic factors, political factors, social factors, environmental factors.

- **Economic.** In the American Civil War, a Southern economy based on slave-grown cotton and an industrialising North developed different interests, including over the expansion of slavery into new territories. Japan's drive for resources, in the context of the global depression of the 1930s, was an economic factor in its expansion in Asia before 1941.
- **Political.** Abraham Lincoln's election in November 1860 led South Carolina and then other states to secede. In Algeria, the exclusion of the Muslim majority from political power under French rule, and the repression of the Sétif demonstrations in 1945, led to the creation of the National Liberation Front (FLN), which launched the revolt in November 1954.
- **Social.** Racist ideology in Nazi Germany and nationalism in the interwar period fed expansion and persecution. Religious identity and the call to defend or reclaim holy places were important in the Crusades, though political and economic motives also mattered.
- **Environmental.** Competition for land, water and resources can raise tension. Take care to show how the environment worked together with political choices, and avoid claiming that the environment alone caused a war.

## Inquiry question 2: What determined the outcome of the conflict?

**Lines of inquiry:** role of leadership, strategies and tactics, mobilisation of resources, role of technology.

- **Leadership.** Lincoln's determination and his choice of Ulysses S. Grant as commander shaped the Union victory. In China, Mao Zedong and the army commanders built a disciplined army and rural support. Consider also the political leaders, such as Churchill, Roosevelt and Stalin, in the Second World War.
- **Strategies and tactics.** The Union blockaded Southern ports and cut the South in two (the "Anaconda Plan" and later Sherman's march). The FLN used guerrilla warfare and urban attacks, including in Algiers in 1957, while the French army used counter-insurgency methods, including torture, which damaged France's standing.
- **Mobilisation of resources.** The Union's larger population, industry and railways gave it an advantage. In the Second World War, the United States' industrial output, through programmes such as Lend-Lease, supplied its allies, and the Soviet Union moved factories east.
- **Technology.** Railways and the telegraph shaped the Civil War, and rifled muskets made defence strong. In the Second World War, radar, aircraft carriers, tanks and the atomic bombs (August 1945) affected strategy. Show that technology matters only through how it is used and by whom.

## Inquiry question 3: How did the conflict affect people's lives?

**Lines of inquiry:** economic impact, social impact, experiences of women, experiences of marginalised groups.

- **Economic.** Southern cities and railways were destroyed in the Civil War, and the emancipation of about four million enslaved people changed the Southern economy. Much of Europe's industry was destroyed in 1945, and rationing and shortages persisted.
- **Social.** Families were separated, populations were displaced, and the dead numbered in the hundreds of thousands in the Civil War and tens of millions in the Second World War. Societies were also changed by wartime experiences, such as the memory of loss.
- **Women.** Women worked in factories and offices, ran farms and served as nurses (Clara Barton in the Civil War). In the Second World War women filled industrial jobs in Britain and the United States and served in combat roles in the Soviet Union. In Algeria, women acted as couriers and fighters for the FLN. Consider whether wartime changes lasted.
- **Marginalised groups.** About 180,000 Black men served in the Union army. The Holocaust murdered about six million Jews and many others, including Roma. Algerians who fought for France (the harkis) were abandoned and attacked after 1962.

## Inquiry question 4: How was peace established?

**Lines of inquiry:** military outcome, political decision-making, social factors, post-conflict peace-building.

- **Military outcome.** Robert E. Lee surrendered at Appomattox in April 1865. Germany and Japan surrendered unconditionally in 1945.
- **Political decision-making.** The Evian Accords of March 1962 ended the Algerian war, followed by independence after a referendum. The Allies' decisions at Yalta and Potsdam shaped Europe after 1945.
- **Social factors.** War weariness in France contributed to the decision to leave Algeria. Public opinion in the United States affected the end of the Vietnam War.
- **Post-conflict peace-building.** Reconstruction after the Civil War included the Thirteenth, Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments, but ended in 1877 without securing equal rights. After 1945 the Marshall Plan helped to rebuild Western Europe, and the occupation of Japan brought a new constitution in 1947.

## Making connections between examples

For the 15-mark essay you need a judgement and connected examples. Ways to connect include:

- **Similarity and difference:** both the Civil War and the Second World War were won by the side with greater resources, but the Algerian war was won politically by the weaker side.
- **Patterns and exceptions:** industrial strength usually mattered, but not in Algeria or Vietnam.
- **Manifestation in different places:** how leadership worked in a democracy (Lincoln) compared with a revolutionary movement (Mao).

## Worked example: a paragraph for part (b)

Question: "To what extent was technology the most important factor in determining the outcome of conflicts?"

"Technology mattered in the American Civil War, but mainly because of who could use it. The Union's railways and telegraph, and its industry, allowed it to move armies and supplies on a scale the Confederacy could not match, so technology and resources worked together. In the Second World War, radar and the production of aircraft and ships gave the Allies advantages, but the Red Army's victories at Stalingrad and Kursk also depended on mass mobilisation and leadership. By contrast, in Algeria the French army had greater firepower than the FLN, yet it did not win, because the war was decided by political and moral pressure. These examples suggest that technology was rarely decisive on its own; its effect depended on the resources, strategy and politics around it."

The paragraph makes a claim, uses examples from the Americas, Europe and Africa, and connects them through a pattern and an exception. Each example is brief but accurate.

## Writing part (a)

Part (a) is a 4-mark short response on one example. Pick the example you know best for the line of inquiry named. Identify it, give two or three accurate details, then explain how they answer the question. For example, to explain how a leader affected the outcome of the American Civil War: Lincoln chose Grant to command all Union armies in 1864, Grant pressed the Confederacy on several fronts and accepted heavy losses, and this wore down Lee's army until the surrender at Appomattox in April 1865.

## Common mistakes

- Telling the story of a war, instead of answering the question.
- Using two examples from the same region.
- Using an example only once, in a list of unconnected points.
- Claiming one factor decided everything.
- Giving figures with no care (say "about").

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn every example above?** No. Choose at least two per line of inquiry, from more than one region, and know them well.

**Can I use a contemporary conflict?** Yes, if it falls within roughly the past two decades, but it must be treated historically (for example, causes but not consequences).
