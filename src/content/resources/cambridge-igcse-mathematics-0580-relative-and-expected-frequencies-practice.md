These questions cover subtopic 8.2, Relative and expected frequencies, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is the same for Core (C8.2) and Extended (E8.2), so every question suits both tiers. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Learn the methods first with the [relative and expected frequencies study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-relative-and-expected-frequencies/) and the [relative and expected frequencies revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-relative-and-expected-frequencies-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (non-calculator) A coin is tossed 80 times and lands heads 46 times. Find the relative frequency of heads. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator) The probability that an item from a machine is faulty is 0.04. Find the expected number of faulty items in 650 items. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A fair six-sided dice is rolled 150 times. Find the expected number of

**(a)** fives, **[1]**

**(b)** even numbers. **[1]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by

**(a)** a fair spinner, **[1]**

**(b)** choosing a ticket at random. **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A spinner has P(blue) = 0.35. It is spun 60 times.

**(a)** Find the expected number of blues. **[1]**

**(b)** Leila says she will get exactly this number of blues. Is she right? Give a reason. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) After 400 spins of a spinner, the relative frequency of landing on 3 is 0.31. How many times did it land on 3? **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (calculator) A paper cup is thrown and lands either upright or on its side. The running totals are shown.

| Number of throws | 20 | 50 | 100 | 200 | 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number upright | 6 | 13 | 31 | 58 | 147 |

**(a)** Work out the relative frequency of landing upright for 20 throws and for 500 throws. **[2]**

**(b)** Which relative frequency is the best estimate of the probability of landing upright? Give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** The cup is thrown 1200 times. Estimate the number of times it lands upright. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A dice is rolled 240 times.

| Score | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 38 | 41 | 36 | 39 | 42 | 44 |

**(a)** Write down the expected frequency of each score if the dice is fair. **[1]**

**(b)** Is there evidence that this dice is biased? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** A different dice is rolled 240 times and shows a six 81 times. Estimate the probability of a six with this dice, and explain whether it appears biased. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A spinner can land on red, green or white. P(red) = 0.2 and P(green) = 0.45.

**(a)** Find P(white). **[1]**

**(b)** The spinner is spun 140 times. Find the expected number of whites. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the expected number of spins that do not land on green. **[1]**

**10.** (calculator) In a random sample of 300 students at a school, 84 walk to school. The school has 1250 students.

**(a)** Estimate the number of students at the school who walk to school. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the estimate might be unreliable if all 300 students lived in the same street. **[1]**

**11.** (non-calculator) In a game, a player rolls a fair dice and wins a prize of $2 if it shows a six. Each game costs 50 cents. The game is played 120 times. Find the expected profit for the person running the game. **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A biased spinner has P(red) = 0.15. In a number of spins, the expected number of reds is 36. Find the number of spins. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 46/80 = **0.575** [1]

**2.** 0.04 × 650 = **26** [1]

**3. (a)** 150 × 1/6 = **25** [1]

**(b)** 150 × 1/2 = **75** [1]

**4. (a)** Each outcome is **equally likely** [1]

**(b)** Every ticket has the **same chance** of being chosen [1]

**5. (a)** 0.35 × 60 = **21** [1]

**(b)** **No**: 21 is what you expect on average; because the results are random, the actual number can differ [1]

**6.** 0.31 × 400 = **124** [1]

**7. (a)** 6/20 = **0.3** [1]; 147/500 = **0.294** [1]

**(b)** **0.294** [1], because it is based on the greatest number of throws [1]

**(c)** 0.294 × 1200 [1] = 352.8, so about **353** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the 500-throw figure already includes the earlier throws, because these are running totals. Do not add the columns together.

**8. (a)** 240 ÷ 6 = **40** [1]

**(b)** **No**: every frequency (36 to 44) is close to 40 [1]

**(c)** 81/240 = **0.3375** [1]; this is about twice 1/6 (0.166…) and 81 is far more than the expected 40, so the dice **appears biased towards six** [1]

**9. (a)** 1 − 0.2 − 0.45 = **0.35** [1]

**(b)** 0.35 × 140 = **49** [1]

**(c)** 0.55 × 140 = **77** [1]

**10. (a)** 84/300 = 0.28 [1]; 0.28 × 1250 = **350** [1]

**(b)** The sample would not be representative of the whole school (students in one street may all travel the same way), so it is not random [1]

**11.** Expected number of wins = 120 × 1/6 = 20 [1]; expected prizes = 20 × $2 = $40, and takings = 120 × $0.50 = $60 [1]; expected profit = **$20** [1]

*Examiner insight:* keep the units consistent. Mixing 50 cents with dollars, for instance writing 120 × 50 = 6000 and then subtracting 40, is a frequent slip.

**12.** 0.15 × n = 36 [1]; n = 36 ÷ 0.15 = **240** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Averaging relative frequencies instead of using the largest number of trials or combining counts.
- Adding running totals as if they were separate experiments.
- Saying an expected frequency is guaranteed.
- Giving "it is unfair" as a reason without comparing numbers.
- Dividing by the probability when you should multiply.
