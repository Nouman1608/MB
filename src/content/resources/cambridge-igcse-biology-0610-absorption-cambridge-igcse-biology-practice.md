These questions cover sub-topic **7.5 Absorption** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 and 2 are Core. Questions on villi, microvilli, capillaries and lacteals (statements 3 to 5) are labelled **(Extended)**. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [absorption study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-absorption-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [absorption revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-absorption-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define absorption. **[1]**

**2.** In which region are most digested nutrients absorbed?

A: colon

B: oesophagus

C: small intestine

D: stomach **[1]**

**3.** State where most water is absorbed **and** one other region where some water is absorbed. **[2]**

**4.** *(Extended)* Name the structures on the surface of epithelial cells that increase the surface area of the small intestine. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Which row correctly shows what is absorbed into the capillaries and the lacteal of a villus?

A: capillaries: fatty acids; lacteal: glucose

B: capillaries: glucose and amino acids; lacteal: fatty acids and glycerol

C: capillaries: glycerol; lacteal: amino acids

D: capillaries: fatty acids and glycerol; lacteal: glucose and amino acids **[1]**

**6.** Explain why starch must be digested before it can be absorbed. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A person drinks a glass of water and eats a meal.

**(a)** Name **three** digestive juices that add water to the contents of the gut.

**(b)** Describe what happens to the water in the small intestine and in the colon.

**(c)** Suggest why a person whose food passes through the colon very quickly may produce watery faeces. **[7]**

**8.** *(Extended)* Describe the structure of a villus. **[4]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Explain the significance of villi and microvilli in the small intestine. **[3]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A meal contains bread, chicken and butter.

**(a)** Name the product of the digestion of the starch in bread that is absorbed into the blood.

**(b)** Name the vessel in the villus that absorbs the products of the digestion of butter fat, and name those products.

**(c)** Name the blood vessel that carries absorbed nutrients from the small intestine to the liver.

**(d)** Explain how the flow of blood in the capillaries helps to absorb amino acids from the chicken. **[7]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A scientist estimates the internal surface area of a short section of small intestine in three ways. The values are for practice only.

| Model of the section | Internal surface area / cm² |
|---|---|
| a smooth tube | 30 |
| a tube with villi | 300 |
| a tube with villi and microvilli | 6000 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the surface area is when villi are present than for a smooth tube.

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the surface area becomes when microvilli are added to the villi.

**(c)** Explain why this increase in surface area is important for a person. **[5]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Some people have a disease in which the villi of the small intestine become damaged and flattened.

**(a)** Suggest and explain **two** effects this would have on absorption.

**(b)** Suggest **one** effect on the person's health. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** (small intestine) [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Most: small intestine [1]. Some: colon [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** *(Extended)* Microvilli [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**5.** *(Extended)* **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**6.** Starch molecules are large and insoluble [1], so they cannot pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood until they are broken down to small soluble sugars [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7. (a)** Any three from: saliva; gastric juice; pancreatic juice; bile [3].

**(b)** Most of the water is absorbed in the small intestine [1]; some of the remaining water is absorbed in the colon [1], so the faeces become semi-solid [1].

**(c)** The remains spend less time in the colon, so less water is absorbed from them [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**8.** *(Extended)* A finger-like projection of the small intestine wall [1]; covered by a single layer of epithelial cells with microvilli [1]; containing a network of blood capillaries [1]; with a central lacteal [1]. Total: 4 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Thin walls" alone does not score the epithelium mark; say "one cell thick" or "single layer of cells".

**9.** *(Extended)* Villi and microvilli increase the internal surface area [1], so more digested molecules are in contact with the absorbing surface at once [1], and absorption is faster and more complete before the food moves on [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**10.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Glucose [1].

**(b)** The lacteal [1]; fatty acids and glycerol [1].

**(c)** The hepatic portal vein [1].

**(d)** Blood carries absorbed amino acids away [1], so their concentration in the blood stays lower than in the small intestine [1], keeping a concentration gradient so more amino acids move into the blood [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**11.** *(Extended)* **(a)** 300 ÷ 30 = **10 times** [1].

**(b)** 6000 ÷ 300 = **20 times** [1].

**(c)** A larger surface area allows more nutrients to be absorbed at the same time [1], so absorption is faster [1], and most of the digested food is absorbed before it passes out of the small intestine [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**12.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Any two effects, each with its explanation [4]: a smaller internal surface area, so fewer nutrients are absorbed at once; slower absorption, so some digested food passes on to the colon unabsorbed; fewer microvilli and less contact with the capillaries and lacteals, so less glucose, amino acids or fat products reach the blood and lymph.

**(b)** Weight loss / tiredness / lack of energy / deficiency of a named nutrient [1]. Total: 5 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "intestine" or "large intestine" instead of small intestine for nutrient absorption.
- Saying most water is absorbed in the colon.
- (Extended) Writing "thin walls" without "one cell thick".
- (Extended) Mixing up villi and microvilli.
- (Extended) Swapping what capillaries and lacteals absorb.
- (Extended) Saying "more surface area" without linking it to faster absorption.
- Dividing the wrong way round in surface-area ratio questions; the larger area goes on top.
