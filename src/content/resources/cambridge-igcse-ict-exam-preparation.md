Cambridge IGCSE ICT (0417) is not tiered, and every candidate sits all three components: Paper 1 Theory (1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 40%), Paper 2 Document Production, Databases and Presentations (2 hours 15 minutes, 70 marks, 30%) and Component 3 Spreadsheets and Website Authoring (2 hours 15 minutes, 70 marks, 30%). All three are externally assessed. This page turns that structure into a plan, and works best alongside the topic pages on [Document Production](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-document-production-study-guide/), [Databases](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-databases-study-guide/) and [File Management](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-file-management-study-guide/).

## Paper 1 Theory: one compulsory written paper

Paper 1 is a compulsory written paper of 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, worth 40% of the qualification. According to the verified assessment record, it covers sections 1 to 21 of the syllabus, so in principle any topic from computer systems, input and output devices, networks, the effects of using IT, the systems life cycle and safety and security, through to the practical-skills topics, can be examined in a written form.

Because the paper is compulsory and spans the whole syllabus, build your revision around coverage first and depth second. A sensible sequence is to list the 21 sections, mark each one red, amber or green, and spend the first revision block turning every red into amber. Only then should you polish areas you already know. Written answers should be precise: name the device or process, then state what it does and why it suits the situation described.

## Paper 2: a practical test, not a written one

Paper 2 is a practical test of 2 hours 15 minutes and 70 marks, worth 30%. It is based on sections 17 to 19 (document production, databases and presentations) together with sections 11 to 16 (file management, images, layout, styles, proofing, and graphs and charts). That second group matters: the general skills are assessed through the three main tasks rather than in isolation.

Preparation therefore means rehearsing complete tasks at a computer, not rereading notes. Practise importing data, editing images, applying consistent styles, setting up a database table with the right field types, building queries and reports, and assembling a short presentation with sensible layout. Save files with the exact names and in the folders the paper asks for, because [file management](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-file-management-revision-notes/) skills are part of the assessed content.

## Component 3: spreadsheets and website authoring

Component 3 is the second practical test: 2 hours 15 minutes, 70 marks, 30%. It is based on sections 20 and 21 (spreadsheets and website authoring) plus the same sections 11 to 16. Treat it as two mini-projects. Give each its own planned block of time, and decide in advance what you will do if one of them stalls, for example moving on and returning once the rest is complete.

For spreadsheets, rehearse formulas, functions, absolute and relative references, formatting and chart production until they are automatic. For website authoring, practise structuring a page, applying a style sheet, adding and resizing images, creating links and checking the result in a browser. In both cases, proofread output as if you were the audience: the [audience](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-audience-study-guide/) and [communication](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-communication-study-guide/) topics apply to everything you produce.

## Timing and marks per minute

Work out your budget from the record rather than guessing.

- Paper 1: 90 minutes for 80 marks is 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark, so a 4-mark question deserves about 4.5 minutes. Keep 5 to 10 minutes at the end for checking.
- Paper 2: 135 minutes for 70 marks is 135 / 70 = about 1.93 minutes per mark.
- Component 3: also 135 / 70 = about 1.93 minutes per mark.

Across the whole qualification, weightings are 40% + 30% + 30% = 100%. The two practical tests together carry 60%, which is more than the theory paper. Do not let the written paper absorb all of your revision time. In a practical test, an early error such as saving in the wrong format or mislabelling a field can cost marks across several later steps, so slow down for the first few minutes and read the whole instruction sheet.

## Command words and question styles

Written questions in IT subjects commonly use verbs such as state, describe, explain, give and compare. Treat them as different levels of demand. State needs a short fact. Describe needs features or steps in order. Explain needs a reason, usually with a because or so that. Compare needs both items discussed and a point of difference.

In the practical tests, the equivalent skill is following instructions exactly: every bullet on the task sheet is usually a separate piece of evidence. Tick them off as you complete them, and read each one twice before you act.

## Worked practice scenario

A school shop keeps a stock list in a spreadsheet. Row 2 to row 5 hold the following items: pens (unit price 4.50, quantity 12), notebooks (3.25, 8), rulers (12.80, 5) and erasers (0.95, 40). The shop owner wants a column for line totals, a grand total, and a 5% discount applied only when the grand total is above 150.

**Model working.**

1. In cell D2 enter the formula that multiplies unit price by quantity, written with cell references (B2 times C2), then fill it down to D5. Using references means the totals update if a price changes.
2. The line totals are 54.00, 26.00, 64.00 and 38.00.
3. In D6 use a sum function over D2 to D5. The grand total is 54.00 + 26.00 + 64.00 + 38.00 = 182.00.
4. In D7 use a conditional (IF) function: if D6 is greater than 150, multiply D6 by 0.95, otherwise show D6. Since 182.00 is greater than 150, the result is 182.00 x 0.95 = 172.90.
5. Format D2 to D7 as currency with two decimal places and check that the discounted figure is labelled clearly.

The marks in a task like this come from the correct references, the correct function, a sensible label and a verified result, so state or display each of them.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Before: list all 21 syllabus sections and rate your confidence in each.
- Before: complete at least one full timed practical task for each of Paper 2 and Component 3.
- Before: check that you know the software you will be using and its shortcuts.
- During Paper 1: aim for roughly 1.1 minutes per mark and move on from stuck questions.
- During practical tests: read the whole task sheet before you touch the software.
- During practical tests: save early, use the exact file names requested, and save again after each major step.
- During practical tests: tick off each instruction as you complete it.
- At the end: proofread text, check formulas display the right values, and confirm every required file exists.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many components are there in Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417?**
There are three, and all candidates take all three: Paper 1 Theory (1h30, 80 marks, 40%), Paper 2 Document Production, Databases and Presentations (2h15, 70 marks, 30%) and Component 3 Spreadsheets and Website Authoring (2h15, 70 marks, 30%).

**Is IGCSE ICT tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered, so every candidate takes the same three components.

**Which component is theory and which are practical?**
Paper 1 is the written theory paper. Paper 2 and Component 3 are practical tests, and together they are worth 60% of the qualification.

**Are the components externally assessed?**
Yes. According to the verified record, all three components are externally assessed.
