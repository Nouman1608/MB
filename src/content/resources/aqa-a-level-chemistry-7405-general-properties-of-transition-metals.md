## d-block elements and transition elements

The d-block is the region of the Periodic Table where the d sub-level is being filled. In period 4 it runs from scandium to zinc, ten elements.

The specification focuses on the elements **Ti to Cu**, whose characteristics come from an **incomplete d sub-level in atoms or ions**. A transition element is therefore one that forms at least one stable ion with a partially filled d sub-level.

| Element | Atom | Common ion | Ion configuration | Transition metal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scandium | [Ar] 3d¹ 4s² | Sc³⁺ | [Ar] (no d electrons) | No |
| Titanium | [Ar] 3d² 4s² | Ti³⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹ | Yes |
| Vanadium | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² | V³⁺ | [Ar] 3d² | Yes |
| Chromium | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ | Cr³⁺ | [Ar] 3d³ | Yes |
| Manganese | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² | Mn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁵ | Yes |
| Iron | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² | Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁶, [Ar] 3d⁵ | Yes |
| Cobalt | [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² | Co²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁷ | Yes |
| Nickel | [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² | Ni²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁸ | Yes |
| Copper | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ | Cu²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁹ | Yes |
| Zinc | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² | Zn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ (full) | No |

Scandium and zinc are d-block elements but they are **not** transition metals in this definition. Scandium forms only Sc³⁺, which has no d electrons. Zinc forms only Zn²⁺, which has a full 3d sub-level. Neither has an incomplete d sub-level in any of its ions. Their compounds are colourless and they do not show variable oxidation state. Copper is a transition metal because Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, even though Cu⁺ (3d¹⁰) is not.

### Writing configurations of ions

1. Write the configuration of the atom.
2. Remove the **4s electrons first**, then 3d electrons if more must be removed.
3. Check the electron count equals the proton number minus the charge.

### Worked example 1: Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺

Iron (Z = 26) is [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s². To form Fe²⁺, remove the two 4s electrons: [Ar] 3d⁶. To form Fe³⁺, remove one 3d electron as well: [Ar] 3d⁵. Both have an incomplete d sub-level, so iron is a transition metal.

### Worked example 2: Cu⁺ and Cu²⁺

Copper (Z = 29) is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹, not 3d⁹ 4s². Remove the 4s electron to form Cu⁺: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ (full, colourless). Remove one 3d electron to form Cu²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁹ (incomplete, coloured blue in water). Copper counts as a transition metal because of Cu²⁺.

### Worked example 3: is zinc a transition metal?

Zinc is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s². The only common ion is Zn²⁺, [Ar] 3d¹⁰. The d sub-level is full in the atom and in the ion. So zinc is not a transition metal.

## The four characteristic properties

The incomplete d sub-level gives these properties, which main-group metals such as sodium or calcium do not show to the same degree.

### 1. Complex formation

Transition metal ions have empty orbitals available to accept lone pairs from ligands, forming co-ordinate bonds. They form a very wide range of complexes, such as [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻.

### 2. Formation of coloured ions

Most transition metal ions in solution are coloured because d electrons can move between slightly different energy levels by absorbing visible light. Examples: Cu²⁺(aq) is blue, Fe³⁺(aq) is yellow-brown and Mn²⁺(aq) is very pale pink. The origin of the colour is on the [Formation of coloured ions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-formation-of-coloured-ions/).

### 3. Variable oxidation state

The 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so different numbers can be lost. Iron forms Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺, and vanadium forms +2, +3, +4 and +5. Calcium, in contrast, forms only Ca²⁺, since after two electrons are lost the next ionisation energy is far too high.

### 4. Catalytic activity

Transition metals and their compounds are good catalysts, for example iron in the Haber process and vanadium(V) oxide in the Contact process. Variable oxidation states and the ability to adsorb molecules on the surface both contribute.

## Ligands, complexes and co-ordination number

A **ligand** is a molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal by donating a pair of electrons. Ligands therefore have at least one lone pair. Common examples are H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻ and OH⁻.

A **co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond** is a covalent bond in which both electrons in the shared pair come from the same atom, here the ligand.

A **complex** is a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands.

The **co-ordination number** is the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion. It counts bonds, not ligands. A monodentate ligand makes one bond; a bidentate ligand makes two (covered on the substitution page).

| Complex | Central ion | Ligands | Co-ordination number |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | Cu²⁺ | 6 H₂O | 6 |
| [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ | Co³⁺ | 6 NH₃ | 6 |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | Cu²⁺ | 4 Cl⁻ | 4 |
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | Ag⁺ | 2 NH₃ | 2 |

### Finding the oxidation state of the metal

The charge on the complex is the sum of the oxidation state of the metal and the charges on the ligands. Uncharged ligands (H₂O, NH₃) contribute zero.

- [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺: 2+ overall, H₂O is neutral, so Cu is +2.
- [CuCl₄]²⁻: 4 × (–1) = –4. So Cu + (–4) = –2, giving Cu = +2.
- [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻: 6 × (–1) = –6. So Fe + (–6) = –3, giving Fe = +3.

### Worked example 4: how a ligand bonds

In [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, each water molecule uses a lone pair on its oxygen atom to form a co-ordinate bond to the Cu²⁺ ion. The oxygen is the donor atom. In ammonia, the lone pair on nitrogen is used. The ion has 6 co-ordinate bonds, so the co-ordination number is 6.

## Common mistakes

- Defining a transition metal as "an element in the d-block". Scandium and zinc are d-block but not transition metals.
- Saying a transition element has a partially filled d sub-level "in its ions only". The definition says atoms or ions.
- Removing 3d electrons before 4s when writing ion configurations.
- Writing copper as [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s² instead of [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
- Counting ligands rather than co-ordinate bonds for the co-ordination number.
- Writing the ligand as accepting a pair of electrons. A ligand donates.
- Forgetting the charge of the ligand when finding the oxidation state of the metal.
- Saying all transition metal compounds are coloured. Cu⁺ and Zn²⁺ compounds are not.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Define / State what is meant by**: use the specification words. A ligand is a molecule or ion that donates a pair of electrons to a metal ion to form a co-ordinate bond.
- **Explain why zinc is not a transition metal**: write the Zn²⁺ configuration and say it has a full d sub-level.
- **Deduce the oxidation state**: show the charge balance.
- **Give the co-ordination number**: count bonds.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do the 4s electrons leave first even though 4s fills first?**
Once the 3d sub-level is occupied it is lower in energy than 4s, so 4s electrons are removed first from the atom.

**Is scandium a transition metal?**
Not under this definition. It forms Sc³⁺, which has no d electrons.

**Why is copper a transition metal when Cu⁺ has a full d sub-level?**
Copper forms the Cu²⁺ ion, which has an incomplete d sub-level (3d⁹). The definition only needs one such ion.
