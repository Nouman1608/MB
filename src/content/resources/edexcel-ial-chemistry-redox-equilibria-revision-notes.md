## Definitions (16.1–16.4, 16.13)

- **Oxidation:** electron loss; oxidation number goes up. **Reduction:** electron gain; oxidation number goes down. True for s-, p- and d-block elements alike.
- **Standard electrode potential, E⦵:** the voltage of a half-cell measured against a standard hydrogen electrode, both under standard conditions.
- **Standard conditions:** 298 K; 100 kPa for any gas; 1.00 mol dm⁻³ for each ion.
- **Standard hydrogen electrode:** platinum coated with platinum black; H₂ at 100 kPa; H⁺(aq) at 1.00 mol dm⁻³; 298 K. E⦵ = 0.00 V by definition.
- **Why a reference?** Only a potential difference between two electrodes can be measured.
- **Standard reduction potential:** another name for E⦵, since each half-equation is written as a reduction.
- **Electrochemical series:** half-equations listed in order of E⦵. At the positive end, left-hand species are the strongest oxidising agents. At the negative end, right-hand species are the strongest reducing agents.

## Building half-cells (16.5, Core Practical 12)

| System | Electrode | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Metal / metal ion | the metal itself, cleaned | its ion at 1.00 mol dm⁻³ |
| Non-metal / ion | platinum, with gas at 100 kPa | the ion at 1.00 mol dm⁻³ |
| Two ions, one element | platinum | both ions, each 1.00 mol dm⁻³ |

Connect to the reference with a potassium nitrate salt bridge and a high-resistance voltmeter. The salt bridge completes the circuit by ion movement without mixing the solutions; nitrate forms no precipitates. The high-resistance meter draws almost no current, so it reads the true emf.

## Cell emf and diagrams (16.7, 16.8)

**Method in steps**

1. Find both E⦵ values.
2. More positive E⦵ = positive electrode, written on the right, as a reduction.
3. E⦵cell = E⦵(right) − E⦵(left). It must be positive for the cell as written.
4. Balance electrons to get the overall equation. Never multiply E⦵ values.

**Notation:** | phase boundary; , same phase; || salt bridge; electrodes at the ends.

**Worked reminder.** Use these values: Al³⁺/Al −1.66 V; Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34 V.
Al(s) | Al³⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s); E⦵cell = +0.34 − (−1.66) = **+2.00 V**; 2Al + 3Cu²⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3Cu.

## Conditions, feasibility and their limits (16.9–16.12)

- A half-cell is an equilibrium. Raising the concentration of the oxidised form moves it right and makes E more positive. Temperature and pressure also change E.
- **Feasible** if E⦵cell > 0.
- E⦵cell ∝ ΔStotal ∝ ln K (same number of electrons):

| E⦵cell | ΔStotal | K | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large, positive | Large, positive | Very large | Goes almost to completion |
| Small, positive | Small, positive | Just over 1 | Equilibrium mixture |
| Negative | Negative | Less than 1 | Not feasible as written |

- Doubling E⦵cell doubles ln K, so K is squared.
- **Limits:** E⦵ predicts thermodynamics, not rate. A feasible reaction can still be imperceptibly slow when its activation energy is large (kinetic stability). Non-standard conditions, such as concentrated reagents or heat, can change E and reverse a prediction, most often when E⦵cell is close to zero.

## Disproportionation (16.14)

Take E⦵ for the half-equation where the species is reduced, minus E⦵ for the half-equation where it is the product.

**Worked reminder.** Use these values: Cu⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Cu, +0.52 V; Cu²⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Cu⁺, +0.15 V.
2Cu⁺ → Cu + Cu²⁺: E⦵cell = +0.52 − 0.15 = **+0.37 V**, so copper(I) in aqueous solution is expected to disproportionate.

## Redox titrations (16.15–16.17, Core Practicals 13a and 13b)

| | Iron(II) / manganate(VII) | Iodine / thiosulfate |
|---|---|---|
| Ionic equation | 5Fe²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O | 2S₂O₃²⁻ + I₂ → S₄O₆²⁻ + 2I⁻ |
| Ratio | Fe²⁺ : MnO₄⁻ = 5 : 1 | S₂O₃²⁻ : I₂ = 2 : 1 |
| Indicator | none (self-indicating) | starch, added near the end |
| End point | first permanent pale pink | blue-black to colourless |
| Conditions | excess dilute sulfuric acid | excess KI to release I₂ from the oxidising agent |

**Why sulfuric acid?** Hydrochloric acid would be oxidised to chlorine by manganate(VII). Nitric acid is itself an oxidising agent. A weak acid cannot supply enough H⁺.

**Method in steps (any redox titration)**

1. Moles of titrant = concentration × titre in dm³.
2. Use the equation ratio (chain the ratios if there are two steps, such as oxidant → I₂ → S₂O₃²⁻).
3. Scale from the pipetted portion to the whole solution.
4. Convert to mass, then to a percentage or concentration.

**Useful chain ratios:** 2Cu²⁺ gives one I₂, so Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 1. IO₃⁻ gives 3I₂, so IO₃⁻ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 6.

## Uncertainty and validity (16.16)

- Percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty ÷ measured value) × 100.
- A titre is the difference of two burette readings, so its uncertainty is twice that of one reading.
- Total percentage uncertainty = the sum of the percentage uncertainties of all measurements.
- If the gap between your result and an accepted value is bigger than the total uncertainty, apparatus alone cannot explain it. Suggest a systematic error, such as air oxidising Fe²⁺.
- A bigger titre or bigger mass reduces percentage uncertainty.

## Fuel cells (16.18, 16.19)

A fuel cell turns the energy released when a fuel reacts with oxygen into a voltage. Fuel and oxygen are supplied continuously. Hydrogen, methanol and other hydrogen-rich fuels are used.

| Electrolyte | Negative electrode (oxidation) | Positive electrode (reduction) |
|---|---|---|
| Acid | H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ | O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O |
| Alkali | H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻ | O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ |

Overall in both: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

## Must-know distinctions

- **E vs E⦵:** E is the measured value under any conditions; E⦵ only under standard conditions.
- **Thermodynamic vs kinetic:** feasible (E⦵cell > 0) does not mean fast.
- **Positive vs negative electrode:** in a cell, reduction happens at the positive electrode.
- **Metal vs platinum electrode:** platinum only when no solid in the half-equation can act as the electrode.

## Quick self-test

1. State the three standard conditions for E⦵.
2. Why is platinum black used in the hydrogen electrode?
3. Use these values: Mg²⁺/Mg −2.37 V; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77 V. Calculate E⦵cell and write the cell diagram.
4. Use these values: Br₂/Br⁻ +1.07 V; Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ +0.15 V. Can bromine oxidise Sn²⁺? Give E⦵cell.
5. A reaction has E⦵cell = −0.40 V. State the sign of ΔStotal and whether K is above or below 1.
6. Give two reasons a reaction with positive E⦵cell might not be seen.
7. How many moles of Fe²⁺ react with 22.40 cm³ of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄?
8. How many moles of thiosulfate react with 0.0100 mol of I₂?
9. A titre of 12.50 cm³ comes from two burette readings, each ±0.05 cm³. What is its percentage uncertainty?
10. Write the positive electrode reaction in an alkaline hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell.
11. Why is starch not added at the start of an iodine titration?

### Answers

1. 298 K; 100 kPa for gases; 1.00 mol dm⁻³ for ions.
2. It gives a large surface area where the H₂/H⁺ equilibrium is set up quickly.
3. E⦵cell = +0.77 − (−2.37) = **+3.14 V**; Mg(s) | Mg²⁺(aq) || Fe³⁺(aq), Fe²⁺(aq) | Pt(s).
4. E⦵cell = +1.07 − 0.15 = **+0.92 V**, so yes: Br₂ + Sn²⁺ → 2Br⁻ + Sn⁴⁺ is feasible.
5. ΔStotal is negative; K is less than 1.
6. High activation energy (slow rate); non-standard conditions changing E.
7. 5 × 0.0150 × 0.02240 = **1.68 × 10⁻³ mol**.
8. **0.0200 mol**.
9. ±0.10 ÷ 12.50 × 100 = **0.80%**.
10. O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.
11. A lot of iodine binds to starch and is released slowly, so the colour change is less sharp.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing E⦵cell = E⦵(left) − E⦵(right) and getting a negative value for a working cell.
- Multiplying E⦵ by the factor used to balance electrons.
- Giving only two of the three standard conditions, or "room temperature" instead of 298 K.
- Omitting platinum from an ion/ion half-cell, or from the hydrogen electrode.
- Describing a salt bridge as carrying electrons. It carries ions.
- Stating a reaction "will happen" from E⦵ alone when the question asks about limitations.
- Using a 1 : 1 ratio for Fe²⁺ with manganate(VII), or 1 : 1 for iodine with thiosulfate.
- Forgetting to scale up from the pipetted portion to the volumetric flask.
- Counting a titre as one burette reading when working out uncertainty.
- Writing H⁺ in the alkaline fuel cell equations, or OH⁻ in the acid ones.
