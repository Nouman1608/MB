All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## The strategic idea

HR approach should follow competitive strategy:

- Competing on **low price** → control labour cost per unit; flexible numbers.
- Competing on **quality, service or innovation** → develop and keep skilled staff.

## Hard and soft HRM

| | Hard | Soft |
|---|---|---|
| Staff seen as | A cost | An asset |
| Time horizon | Short | Long |
| Tools | Flexible contracts, targets, monitoring | Training, consultation, career paths |
| Suits | Low-skill, replaceable work; cost focus | Scarce skills; quality focus |
| Theory link | Theory X; Taylor | Theory Y; Mayo, Herzberg |

**Core and periphery:** permanent core staff managed "soft"; temporary, zero hours or gig workers managed "hard". Gains: keeps expertise and flexes cost. Risks: two-tier workforce, low loyalty at the edge, uneven service.

## Choosing a flexible contract: four tests

1. Demand pattern: steady, seasonal or unpredictable?
2. Skill: quick to learn or months of training?
3. Customer contact: will turnover of staff be noticed?
4. Employee needs: will it attract or repel the people wanted?

| Situation | Suitable contracts |
|---|---|
| Predictable daily peaks | Part-time, shift working |
| Seasonal peak | Temporary, annualised hours |
| Demand changing hour by hour | Zero hours, gig economy |
| Scarce skilled staff | Full-time with flexi-time, home working, job sharing |
| Long operating hours | Shift working, compressed hours |

**Worked reminder: Lindow Garden Centre.** 4 extra staff, 8 weeks, 30 hours a week = 4 × 8 × 30 = 960 hours. Agency at $18: 960 × 18 = $17,280. Own temporary contracts at $13 plus $350 recruitment each: 960 × 13 + 4 × 350 = 12,480 + 1,400 = $13,880. Saving $3,400, but the agency is quicker and covers absence.

## Poor performance

**Measures to match the work:** output per worker or per hour; sales per employee; reject or wastage rate; absenteeism rate; labour turnover; complaints per 1,000 transactions; appraisal results.

Standard forms:

- Labour turnover (%) = leavers ÷ average employees × 100
- Absenteeism (%) = days lost ÷ days available × 100
- Sales per employee = revenue ÷ average employees

**Causes in four groups:**

- Person: skills, health, personal circumstances.
- Job: boring, unclear, badly designed.
- Management: weak leadership, no feedback, poor selection.
- Resources: equipment, systems, conditions.

**Consequence chain:** lower output or quality → higher unit cost, lost customers → lower profit; inside, colleagues lose morale → more absence and turnover.

## Improving performance

| Cause | Strategy | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Skills gap | Training, mentoring | Weeks to months |
| Low motivation | Incentives, job enrichment, recognition | Incentives are a recurring cost |
| Unclear goals | Appraisal, MBO, feedback | Mainly management time |
| Weak leadership | Leadership development; new manager | Slow or disruptive |
| Poor resources | New equipment or systems | High capital cost |
| Persistent failure | Discipline, redeployment, dismissal | Legal risk; replacement cost |

## MBO

- Associated with Peter Drucker.
- Corporate → department → individual objectives, **agreed** with each employee.
- SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
- Pitfalls: soft targets; measurable crowds out unmeasurable; objectives go out of date; staff judged on results they cannot control.
- Most useful: skilled staff, measurable results, stable conditions.

## IT and AI in HRM

| Area | Change | Risk to weigh |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment | AI screening and ranking | Copies past bias |
| Workforce planning | Forecasting from HR data | Poor data, poor forecasts |
| Training | Online modules | Completion is not competence |
| Scheduling | Shifts matched to forecast demand | Unpredictable hours for staff |
| Queries | Portals, chatbots | Impersonal for complex cases |
| Monitoring | Activity tracking | Damaged trust |

Trend: routine HR work automated; HR staff shift to judgement-based work.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HRM strategy | Long-term plan for managing people to support business objectives |
| Hard HRM | Treating employees as a cost to be minimised and controlled |
| Soft HRM | Treating employees as an asset to be developed |
| Annualised hours | Working hours set for a year and spread to match demand |
| Compressed hours | Full-time hours worked in fewer, longer days |
| Gig economy | Work paid per task, without an ongoing employment contract |
| MBO | Agreeing objectives with each employee that link to business objectives |

## Exam traps

- Calling hard HRM "bad" rather than a choice with costs.
- Recommending a contract without referring to demand or skills.
- Calculating a measure and not interpreting it.
- Recommending a strategy that does not fit the cause in the case.
- Treating AI only as a saving.

## Self-test

1. Give two features of soft HRM.
2. What is meant by a core and peripheral workforce?
3. Which contract suits demand that changes hour by hour?
4. A firm has 40 average employees and 10 leavers. Calculate labour turnover.
5. Revenue is $2,100,000 and average employees are 30. Calculate sales per employee.
6. Name the four groups of causes of poor performance.
7. Which strategy fits poor performance caused by unclear goals?
8. What does the A in SMART stand for?
9. Give one pitfall of MBO.
10. Give one risk of AI screening of job applications.
11. Why might a business competing on quality prefer soft HRM?
12. Give one reason why a business might pay more for agency staff than for its own temporary staff.

### Answers

1. Training and development; consultation; long-term commitment (any two).
2. A permanent, well-trained core plus flexible peripheral workers on temporary, zero hours or gig arrangements.
3. Zero hours contracts or gig economy work.
4. 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = **25%**.
5. 2,100,000 ÷ 30 = **$70,000**.
6. Person, job, management, resources.
7. Appraisal, MBO or regular feedback.
8. Achievable.
9. Measurable targets may crowd out important but unmeasured work (or objectives go out of date).
10. It may copy bias from past hiring data and reject suitable candidates.
11. Quality depends on skilled, loyal staff, which soft HRM develops and keeps.
12. Agencies supply staff quickly and replace absent workers at once.
