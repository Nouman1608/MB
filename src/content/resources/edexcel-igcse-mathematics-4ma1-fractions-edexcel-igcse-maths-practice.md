This set covers **section 1.2, Fractions**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. It is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes, so every question is for both tiers. Where a question says "show your working", write the common denominator or reciprocal step; an answer from the calculator's fraction key alone does not show the method.

## Section A

**1.** Write 84/126 in its simplest form. **[2]**

**2.**

**(a)** Write 4 2/7 as an improper fraction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 47/6 as a mixed number. **[1]**

**3.** Write these fractions in order of size, smallest first: 3/5, 4/7, 5/9, 7/12. Show how you decide. **[2]**

**4.** Find 7/15 of 345 kg. **[2]**

**5.** Write 35 minutes as a fraction of 2 hours. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Write 4/25 as a decimal and as a percentage. **[2]**

**(b)** Write 7/9 as a recurring decimal. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Show your working. Give each answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.

**(a)** Work out 2 5/6 + 3 4/9. **[3]**

**(b)** Work out 5 1/4 − 1 2/3. **[3]**

**8.** Show your working.

**(a)** Work out 1 3/5 × 2 1/12, giving your answer as a mixed number. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that 3 3/4 ÷ 1 7/8 = 2. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** Complete the statement: dividing by 6 is the same as multiplying by ___ **[1]**

**(b)** Work out 5/8 ÷ 10, giving your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**10.** 3/8 of the members of a club are juniors. There are 42 juniors.

**(a)** Work out the total number of members. **[2]**

**(b)** 2/7 of the members who are not juniors are over 60. How many members are over 60? **[2]**

**11.** A garden is divided into a patio, a lawn and flower beds. The patio covers 1/4 of the garden. The lawn covers 2/3 of the rest. The flower beds cover 18 m².

**(a)** What fraction of the garden is covered by flower beds? **[3]**

**(b)** Work out the total area of the garden. **[2]**

**12.** A jug holds 2 1/4 litres of juice. Each glass holds 3/8 of a litre.

**(a)** How many glasses can be filled from a full jug? **[2]**

**(b)** Four glasses are filled from a full jug. What fraction of the juice is left in the jug? **[3]**

**13.** Which of the fractions 5/11 and 6/13 is closer to 1/2? Show how you decide. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** HCF of 84 and 126 is 42 [1]; **2/3** [1]

**2. (a)** 4 × 7 + 2 = 30, so **30/7** [1]

**(b)** 47 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 5, so **7 5/6** [1]

**3.** Decimals: 0.6, 0.571…, 0.555…, 0.583… [1]; **5/9, 4/7, 7/12, 3/5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* A common denominator of 1260 also works but is slow; decimals to 3 places are enough to separate these four fractions.

**4.** 345 ÷ 15 = 23 [1]; 23 × 7 = **161 kg** [1]

**5.** 2 hours = 120 minutes, so 35/120 [1]; = **7/24** [1]

**6. (a)** **0.16** [1] and **16%** [1]

**(b)** **0.777…**, written 0.7̇ [1]

**7. (a)** Common denominator 18: 2 15/18 + 3 8/18 [1]; = 5 23/18 [1]; = **6 5/18** [1]

**(b)** 21/4 − 5/3 [1]; = 63/12 − 20/12 [1]; = 43/12 = **3 7/12** [1]

**8. (a)** 8/5 × 25/12 [1]; cancel to 2/1 × 5/3 [1]; = 10/3 = **3 1/3** [1]

**(b)** 15/4 ÷ 15/8 [1]; = 15/4 × 8/15 [1]; = 120/60 = 2 [1]

**9. (a)** **1/6** [1]

**(b)** 5/8 × 1/10 [1]; = 5/80 = **1/16** [1]

**10. (a)** 1/8 of members = 42 ÷ 3 = 14 [1]; total = 14 × 8 = **112** [1]

**(b)** Not juniors: 112 − 42 = 70 [1]; 2/7 of 70 = **20** [1]

**11. (a)** Rest after patio = 3/4 [1]; lawn = 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2 [1]; flower beds = 1 − 1/4 − 1/2 = **1/4** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "2/3 of the rest" means 2/3 of the 3/4 left after the patio, so multiply; taking 2/3 of the whole garden leaves a negative fraction for the flower beds, a sign that the method is wrong.

**(b)** 1/4 of the garden = 18 m² [1]; total = **72 m²** [1]

**12. (a)** 9/4 ÷ 3/8 = 9/4 × 8/3 [1]; = **6 glasses** [1]

**(b)** Used: 4 × 3/8 = 3/2 litres [1]; left: 9/4 − 6/4 = 3/4 litre [1]; fraction of the jug: (3/4) ÷ (9/4) = **1/3** [1]

**13.** 1/2 − 5/11 = 11/22 − 10/22 = 1/22 [1]; 6/13 is less than 1/2 by 13/26 − 12/26 = 1/26 [1]; 1/26 < 1/22, so **6/13 is closer** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Converting to decimals (0.4545… and 0.4615…) is also valid, but the fraction differences give an exact comparison with no rounding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping part-way when simplifying, for example leaving 14/21 in question 1.
- Writing 35/2 in question 5 by not converting hours to minutes.
- Trying to subtract 2/3 from 1/4 in question 7(b) without borrowing or converting.
- Multiplying 1 × 2 and 3/5 × 1/12 separately in question 8(a).
- Taking 2/3 of the whole garden for the lawn in question 11.
- Giving 3/4 (litres) instead of the fraction of the jug in question 12(b).
