## Section B

**7.** (19.1.4) The restriction enzyme BamHI recognises this sequence and cuts each strand between the two G nucleotides at the start of the sequence (reading 5′ to 3′):

```
5′ – G G A T C C – 3′
3′ – C C T A G G – 5′
```

**(a)** Give the base sequence of the single-stranded overhang (sticky end) left on each fragment. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the plasmid and the donor DNA must be cut with the same restriction enzyme. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the role of DNA ligase after the sticky ends have paired. **[1]**

**8.** (19.1.3, 19.1.4, 19.1.6) Describe how the human insulin gene could be obtained from pancreatic cells, inserted into a plasmid, and how bacteria that express it could be identified. **[6]**

**9.** (19.1.8) In PCR, the reaction mixture is heated to 95 °C, cooled to 60 °C, then heated to 72 °C in each cycle.

**(a)** Explain why the mixture is heated to 95 °C. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what happens at 60 °C and why two different primers are needed. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why Taq polymerase is used rather than DNA polymerase from human cells. **[2]**

**(d)** The sample contains 4 copies of the target sequence. Calculate the maximum number of copies after 18 cycles. **[1]**

**(e)** Suggest why fewer copies than this are produced in practice. **[1]**

**10.** (19.1.9) A linear DNA molecule 8000 base pairs (bp) long has two sites for enzyme P, at 1500 and 5200, and one site for enzyme Q, at 3000.

**(a)** Give the lengths of the fragments produced by enzyme P alone. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the lengths of the fragments produced when enzymes P and Q are used together, and state how many bands are seen on the gel. **[2]**

**(c)** State which fragment from (b) travels furthest from the well. **[1]**

**11.** (19.1.10) cDNA made from the mRNA of liver cells was labelled green, and cDNA from kidney cells was labelled red. Both were applied to a microarray. The spot colours for five genes are shown.

| Gene | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spot colour | Red | Yellow | None | Green | Yellow |

**(a)** Use the results to describe which genes are expressed in each tissue. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why mRNA is first converted to cDNA. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the probes on the microarray must be single-stranded. **[1]**

**12.** (19.1.6) A gene for pest resistance is inserted into plant cells with a gene for a fluorescent protein. Explain how the fluorescent protein shows that the pest-resistance gene is being expressed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** DNA made by joining DNA from two or more different sources (often different species) [1].

**2.** Reverse transcriptase makes single-stranded cDNA using mRNA as a template [1]. DNA ligase joins DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds in the sugar–phosphate backbone [1].

**3.** Bacterial RNA polymerase cannot bind to / recognise the human promoter (or no promoter is present) [1], so the gene is not transcribed, no mRNA is made and the protein is not translated [1].

**4.** A form of genetic engineering [1] in which DNA is inserted, deleted or replaced at a specific (targeted) site in the genome [1].

**5.** DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate groups, so it is attracted to the positive electrode [1]. The gel acts as a mesh that slows longer fragments more [1].

**6.** Any two [2]: free and rapid access worldwide; no need to repeat sequencing; new sequences can be compared to identify genes, species or pathogens; primers and probes can be designed from them; evolutionary relationships can be studied.

**7. (a)** 5′-GATC-3′ [1].

**(b)** Gives the same, complementary sticky ends on both [1], so the gene and plasmid ends can base-pair by hydrogen bonds [1].

**(c)** Forms phosphodiester bonds to seal the sugar–phosphate backbones [1].

**8.** One mark each, to a maximum of 6:
- Extract insulin mRNA from pancreatic β cells, which contain many copies.
- Reverse transcriptase makes single-stranded cDNA from the mRNA.
- DNA polymerase makes it double-stranded; the gene has no introns.
- Cut plasmid (and add matching ends to the gene) with the same restriction enzyme to give complementary sticky ends.
- Sticky ends pair; DNA ligase seals with phosphodiester bonds.
- Include a bacterial promoter so the gene is transcribed.
- Include a marker gene coding for a fluorescent protein, e.g. GFP, beside the insulin gene.
- Bacteria that fluoresce contain and express the recombinant plasmid; select and culture them.

**9. (a)** To break the hydrogen bonds between strands, separating them into single-stranded templates [1].

**(b)** Primers anneal / bind by complementary base pairing to the ends of the target sequence [1]. One primer for each strand, marking each end, because DNA polymerase can only extend an existing strand [1].

**(c)** Taq polymerase is thermostable and not denatured at 95 °C [1]; human DNA polymerase would be denatured and need replacing every cycle [1].

**(d)** 4 × 2¹⁸ = 4 × 262 144 = **1 048 576** [1].

**(e)** Primers or nucleotides run out / enzyme activity falls in later cycles [1].

**10. (a)** 1500 bp [1]; 5200 − 1500 = 3700 bp and 8000 − 5200 = 2800 bp [1].

**(b)** 1500, 1500, 2200 and 2800 bp [1]; three bands, because the two 1500 bp fragments run together [1].

**(c)** The 1500 bp fragments (the band containing both) [1].

**11. (a)** Gene 1 is expressed in kidney only [1]. Gene 4 in liver only [1]. Genes 2 and 5 in both; gene 3 in neither [1].

**(b)** mRNA is unstable and easily broken down; cDNA is stable DNA that can be labelled and hybridise with the DNA probes [1].

**(c)** So that complementary cDNA can hybridise / base-pair with them [1].

**12.** The fluorescent-protein gene is placed beside the pest-resistance gene under the same promoter / inserted on the same DNA [1]. Cells that glow under blue or ultraviolet light must be transcribing and translating the inserted DNA [1], so the pest-resistance gene is also expressed in them; these cells are selected without being killed [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving ligase the role of forming hydrogen bonds.
- Saying reverse transcriptase makes mRNA.
- Forgetting that a linear molecule cut n times gives n + 1 fragments, or not noticing equal fragments form one band.
- Describing a yellow spot as "no expression".
- Giving PCR temperatures without saying what happens and why.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has short recall questions; Section B has sequences, calculations and data. The learning outcome is in brackets at the start of each question.

**Why can two fragments of the same length give one band?**
Bands separate only by length, so fragments of identical length travel the same distance and appear as a single, brighter band.
