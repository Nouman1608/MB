This set covers **section 1.3, Decimals**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. **Tiers:** questions marked **(Higher tier only)** convert recurring decimals into fractions, which the specification lists for the Higher Tier only. Everything else is for both tiers.

## Section A

**1.** Write down the value of the digit 5 in the number 12.0358. **[1]**

**2.** Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: 0.6, 0.06, 0.606, 0.66, 0.066 **[2]**

**3.** Work out

**(a)** 0.3 × 0.07 **[1]**

**(b)** 4.8 ÷ 0.06 **[1]**

**4.** Write 0.84 as a fraction in its simplest form and as a percentage. **[2]**

**5.** Write 3.125 as a mixed number in its simplest form. **[2]**

**6.** Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: −0.4, −0.35, −0.405, −0.04 **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** You are given that 37 × 64 = 2368. Use this fact to write down the value of

**(a)** 3.7 × 6.4 **[1]**

**(b)** 0.37 × 640 **[1]**

**(c)** 2368 ÷ 0.64 **[1]**

**8.** Four runners' times, in seconds, for a 100 m race are 12.38, 12.4, 12.308 and 12.83.

**(a)** Write the times in order, fastest first. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the difference between the fastest and slowest times. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the decimal part of the fastest time, 0.308, as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Write 0.8̇ as a fraction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 0.6̇3̇ as a fraction in its simplest form. Show your working. **[3]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Prove algebraically that the recurring decimal 0.83̇ can be written as 5/6. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Write 0.2̇34̇ as a fraction in its simplest form. Show your working. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Use algebra to show that 0.9̇ = 1. **[2]**

**(b)** x = 0.1̇2̇ and y = 0.4̇. Work out x + y, giving your answer as a fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**13.** Sami buys 3.75 m of fabric at £4.80 per metre.

**(a)** You are given that 375 × 48 = 18 000. Use this to work out the cost of the fabric. **[2]**

**(b)** He pays with a £20 note. Work out his change. **[1]**

**14.** **(Higher tier only)** Show that 0.4̇5̇ + 0.5̇4̇ = 1. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **5 thousandths** (0.005) [1]

**2.** Padded: 0.600, 0.060, 0.606, 0.660, 0.066, or three in the correct order [1]; **0.06, 0.066, 0.6, 0.606, 0.66** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Padding with zeros so every number has three decimal places turns the comparison into ordering 600, 60, 606, 660 and 66, which is hard to get wrong.

**3. (a)** 3 × 7 = 21 with three decimal places: **0.021** [1]

**(b)** 480 ÷ 6 = **80** [1]

**4.** 84/100 = **21/25** [1]; **84%** [1]

**5.** 3 + 125/1000 [1]; = **3 1/8** [1]

**6.** Recognises the largest size is the smallest number [1]; **−0.405, −0.4, −0.35, −0.04** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Picture a number line: −0.405 is furthest left of zero, so it is the smallest, and −0.04 is closest to zero, so it is the largest.

**7. (a)** **23.68** [1]

**(b)** 0.37 × 640 = 37 × 6.4 = **236.8** [1]

**(c)** 236 800 ÷ 64 = **3700** [1]

**8. (a)** **12.308, 12.38, 12.4, 12.83** [1]

**(b)** 12.83 − 12.308 = **0.522 seconds** [1]

**(c)** 308/1000 [1]; = **77/250** [1]

**9. (a)** **8/9** [1]

**(b)** x = 0.6363…, 100x = 63.6363… [1]; 99x = 63 [1]; x = 63/99 = **7/11** [1]

**10.** x = 0.8333…; 10x = 8.333… and 100x = 83.333… [1]; 100x − 10x: 90x = 75 [1]; x = 75/90 = 5/6 [1]

*Examiner insight:* Subtracting x from 10x gives 9x = 7.5, which also works, but leaves a decimal to clear; choosing two multiples whose tails both start right after the point keeps the arithmetic whole.

**11.** x = 0.234234…, 1000x = 234.234… [1]; 999x = 234 [1]; x = 234/999 = **26/111** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The dots over 2 and 4 mean the whole block 234 repeats, so the multiplier is 1000. Cancel by 9 (since 2 + 3 + 4 = 9), giving 26/111.

**12. (a)** x = 0.999…, 10x = 9.999… [1]; 9x = 9, so x = 1 [1]

**(b)** x = 12/99 = 4/33 and y = 4/9 [1]; 12/99 + 44/99 [1]; = **56/99** [1]

**13. (a)** 3.75 × 4.80 has 2 + 2 = 4 decimal places in total, so 18 000 becomes 1.8000 [1]; cost **£18.00** [1]

**(b)** 20 − 18 = **£2.00** [1]

**14.** 0.4̇5̇ = 45/99 [1]; 0.5̇4̇ = 54/99 [1]; 45/99 + 54/99 = 99/99 = 1 [1]

*Examiner insight:* Adding the decimals digit by digit gives 0.999…, which the method in question 12(a) shows is equal to 1; the fraction route is the clearer proof.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing 0.606 as the largest in question 2 because it has the most digits.
- Giving 0.21 in question 3(a) by counting decimal places wrongly.
- Ordering −0.04 first in question 6 as if the numbers were positive.
- Writing 308/100 in question 8(c), or not cancelling 308/1000 fully.
- Using 10x in question 9(b), so the repeating tails do not match.
- Not reaching exactly 5/6 in question 10, which is needed for a "prove" question.
