**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level**, subtopic 3.7.

## What the syllabus requires

Use dot-and-cross diagrams to show **ionic, covalent and coordinate bonding**, including **every compound named in 3.4 and 3.5**. Diagrams may include **expanded octets** and species with an **odd number of electrons**.

## Conventions

| Bond type | How to draw it |
|---|---|
| Covalent | shared pair in the overlap: **one dot + one cross** |
| Coordinate | shared pair from one atom: **two dots** (or two crosses) |
| Double / triple bond | **two / three** shared pairs between the same two atoms |
| Ionic | separate ions in **square brackets**, **charge** outside, top right; transferred electrons shown with the symbol of the atom they came from |

- Outer-shell electrons only, unless asked otherwise.
- Group electrons clearly in pairs.

## Five-step check

1. Total outer electrons = sum for all atoms, **+1 per negative charge, −1 per positive charge**.
2. Place bonds (H 1, halogen 1, O 2, N 3, C 4; P and S can form more).
3. Fill lone pairs on outer atoms.
4. Check each atom: H **2**; Period 2 atoms **8** (except B in BF₃: **6**); Period 3 central atoms can have **10 or 12**.
5. Recount. An **odd** total means one **unpaired** electron.

## Ionic diagrams

| Compound | Diagram |
|---|---|
| NaCl | [Na]⁺ and [Cl]⁻ (Cl⁻: 7 x + 1 •) |
| MgO | [Mg]²⁺ and [O]²⁻ (O²⁻: 6 x + 2 •) |
| CaF₂ | [Ca]²⁺ and **two** [F]⁻ (each 7 x + 1 •) |

The cation's outer shell is empty: show it with no electrons, or show the complete shell underneath.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dot-and-cross diagram | Diagram showing outer electrons of different atoms with different symbols |
| Lone pair | Pair of outer electrons not involved in bonding |
| Expanded octet | More than eight electrons around a central atom (Period 3 and beyond) |
| Odd-electron species | Species with an odd total of electrons, so one is unpaired |
| Coordinate bond | Shared pair with both electrons from one atom |

## Exam traps

- Ionic diagrams must **not** show shared pairs.
- Each ion needs **brackets and a charge**.
- CaF₂ contains **two** F⁻ ions, not F₂²⁻ or F²⁻.
- A coordinate bond shows **both electrons from the donor**.
- BF₃: boron has **six** electrons. Do not add a fictitious lone pair.
- NO: nitrogen has **seven** electrons; the unpaired electron must be visible.
- Lone pairs on outer atoms are part of the diagram: PCl₅ has 15 lone pairs on its chlorine atoms.
- Adjust the electron total for **charge**.

## Self-test

1. How many electrons in total are shown in a dot-and-cross diagram of NH₃ (outer shells only)?
2. How many shared pairs appear between the two atoms in N₂?
3. How is a coordinate bond shown?
4. How many electrons surround boron in BF₃?
5. Describe the diagram for the oxide ion in MgO.
6. How many lone pairs does each oxygen atom have in CO₂?
7. What is the total number of outer electrons in SO₂, and how many surround sulfur?
8. Explain why a dot-and-cross diagram for NO must contain an unpaired electron.
9. How many coordinate bonds are shown in Al₂Cl₆?
10. How many lone pairs in total are on the fluorine atoms in SF₆?
11. What total number of electrons does the diagram of H₃O⁺ show?
12. Give two features that distinguish an ionic dot-and-cross diagram from a covalent one.

**Answers:**

1. 8 (5 from N + 3 from H).
2. Three.
3. A shared pair made up of two electrons with the same symbol (both from the donor atom), often labelled with an arrow.
4. Six.
5. [O]²⁻ in brackets with a 2− charge: six crosses (oxygen's own) and two dots (from magnesium), eight electrons in four pairs.
6. Two.
7. 18 electrons; sulfur is surrounded by 10.
8. The total number of outer electrons is 11, an odd number, so they cannot all be paired.
9. Two.
10. 18 (three on each of six F atoms).
11. 8 (6 + 3 − 1).
12. Ions drawn separately in square brackets; charges shown; no shared pairs between the particles.
