**B.3 Object oriented programming (OOP)** is the third sub-topic of Theme B, "Computational thinking and problem-solving", in the IB Diploma Programme Computer Science course first assessed in 2027. It is studied at both levels but is much larger at HL (23 hours) than at SL (7 hours), and the IB subject brief says Paper 2 includes additional HL questions focused on OOP. Programming questions can be answered in Java or Python.

> **Scope of this page.** This overview is written from the IB's public *Diploma Programme Subject Brief, Sciences: Computer science, first assessment 2027* (© International Baccalaureate Organization 2024). The brief names B.3 and gives its teaching hours and assessment, but it does **not** list the B.3 learning outcomes. Those are in the full DP Computer Science guide, available to IB schools through the Programme Resource Centre. Follow your school's copy of the full guide for exact content, terminology and depth.

## What does the IB subject brief say about B.3?

| Item | Subject brief |
|---|---|
| Name | B.3 Object oriented programming (OOP) |
| Theme | B, "Computational thinking and problem-solving" (Theme B "focuses on how we can use computing systems to solve real-world problems") |
| Recommended teaching hours | 7 SL / 23 HL |
| Paper 2 | Questions for SL and HL on the three topics in Theme B; additional questions for HL focused on OOP and abstract data types |
| Languages | The course can be studied in Python or Java; Paper 2 programming questions have equivalent Java and Python versions |

Because Paper 2 at SL covers "the three topics in Theme B", B.3 is examined at SL as well as HL. The large difference in hours (7 against 23), together with the extra HL questions, means HL students should expect considerably more depth.

## How does B.3 fit with the rest of Theme B?

The brief describes the course as developing "skills in algorithmic thinking and computer programming", underpinned by the computational thinking process. In Theme B, B.1 sets out that process, B.2 Programming builds general programming skill (40 SL / 42 HL hours), and B.3 applies programming in an object oriented style. At HL, B.4 Abstract data types (23 hours, HL only) follows.

## General background: what is object oriented programming?

*This short section is general computer-science background, not the IB's list of B.3 outcomes.* In object oriented programming, a program is organised around **classes**, which define the data (attributes) and behaviour (methods) of a kind of thing, and **objects**, which are individual instances of a class. Both Java and Python, the two languages the brief names, support this style. The exact OOP terms and features you must know, such as how far the course goes into relationships between classes, are defined in the full guide.

## How should you prepare for B.3?

- Get your teacher's list of B.3 learning outcomes from the full guide and turn it into a checklist.
- Write small object oriented programs in your course language (Java or Python) regularly; Paper 2 includes programming questions.
- At HL, plan for B.3 to take more than three times the SL teaching time and practise the longer HL Paper 2 questions.
- Use the computational thinking process from B.1 when designing classes: specify the problem and success criteria, decompose it, abstract the data that matters, then test and evaluate.
- Consider using OOP in your computational solution (internal assessment) if it suits your problem.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is OOP examined at SL?**
Yes. The brief says Paper 2 has questions for SL and HL on the three topics in Theme B, and gives B.3 7 hours at SL. HL has 23 hours and additional Paper 2 questions on OOP.

**Do I have to use Java for OOP?**
No. The brief says the course can be studied in Python or Java, and Paper 2 programming questions have equivalent versions in each.

**Where is the full list of B.3 content?**
In the full DP Computer Science guide, available through the IB Programme Resource Centre. The public subject brief does not list it.

**How heavily is Paper 2 weighted?**
35% of the final grade at SL (1 hour 15 minutes) and 40% at HL (2 hours).
