These questions cover key question 1.6 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "What caused the First World War?"

## Section A

**1.** Name the three members of the Triple Alliance and the three members of the Triple Entente. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the Anglo-Japanese Alliance of 1902. **[4]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why HMS Dreadnought increased tension between Britain and Germany. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the First Moroccan Crisis of 1905-06. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **three** results of the Balkan Wars of 1912-13. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the events in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did Britain end its policy of isolation between 1902 and 1907? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why were problems in the Balkans so difficult for the Great Powers to solve? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Colonial rivalry was more important than the arms race in causing tension between the Great Powers before
1914." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "Germany was mainly responsible for turning the assassination at Sarajevo into a general war." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter written by a German naval officer to his brother, 1908. "The English talk as if
our fleet were a knife held at their throat. But we are a great trading nation with colonies across the world. Must
we ask London's permission to protect our own ships? Every new dreadnought they launch tells us they intend to keep
us small."

What does Source A tell us about German attitudes to the naval race? Use the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from a newspaper published in Belgrade, Serbia, in October 1908. "Vienna has stolen Bosnia
as a thief steals at night. Our brothers there are Serbs, and they are now prisoners of the Habsburgs. Russia
watches and does nothing. Serbia will not forget, and one day Serbia will not be alone."

Why was Source B published in October 1908? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 1 mark each: Triple Alliance: Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy (1); Triple Entente: Britain, France and
Russia (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any four, 1 mark each: signed by Britain and Japan in 1902 (1); if either fought one power, the other would stay
neutral (1); if either fought two powers, the other would help (1); it was aimed mainly at Russia (1); it protected
British interests in the Far East (1); it let Britain concentrate more of its navy nearer home (1); it ended Britain's
isolation (1); it allowed Japan to fight Russia in 1904-05 without another power joining (1). **[4]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: it made earlier battleships obsolete, so Germany could compete from a more equal start
(1); Germany began building its own dreadnoughts (1); Britain saw German building as a threat to its naval supremacy
(1); public pressure in Britain led to demands for more ships, such as "We want eight" (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: Wilhelm II visited Tangier in 1905 (1); he declared support for Moroccan independence (1);
he aimed to test or break the Entente Cordiale (1); France had been promised influence in Morocco in 1904 (1); an
international conference met at Algeciras in 1906 (1); only Austria-Hungary supported Germany (1); France's position
in Morocco was confirmed (1); Britain and France grew closer (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Ottoman Empire lost almost all its European territory (1); Serbia nearly doubled in
size (1); Serbia became more confident and ambitious (1); Austria-Hungary became more alarmed by Serbia (1); Albania was
created, blocking Serbia's access to the sea (1); Bulgaria was defeated in the second war and resentful (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, visited Sarajevo, the
capital of Bosnia (1); with his wife Sophie (1); a bomb was thrown at their car earlier in the day but missed them (1);
later the car took a wrong turning (1); Gavrilo Princip shot both of them (1); Princip was a Bosnian Serb (1); he was
one of a group linked to the Black Hand (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **fear of Germany**, because German Navy Laws threatened British naval supremacy, so Britain needed friends;
**overstretch**, because Britain could not defend a worldwide empire alone, as the difficulties of the Boer War
showed, so an alliance with Japan protected the Far East; **settling colonial disputes**, because agreements with
France (1904) and Russia (1907) removed old rivalries in Africa and Asia; **German diplomacy backfired**, because the
First Moroccan Crisis pushed Britain and France closer. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **Ottoman decline** left territory that rivals wanted; **opposed
interests**: Austria-Hungary feared Slav nationalism among its own peoples, while Russia wanted to protect Slavs and
gain access to the Straits; **Serbian nationalism** aimed at a greater Serbia including Bosnia; **each crisis left
resentment**: Russia was humiliated in 1908-09 and Serbia gained from the Balkan Wars, making Austria-Hungary more
determined to act; **alliances** meant Germany backed Austria-Hungary, so Russia could not easily compromise. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Colonial rivalry:** the Moroccan crises of 1905 and 1911 brought Germany close to war with France and drove Britain
and France together; Fashoda and the Kruger Telegram; Weltpolitik alarmed Britain. **Arms race:** the naval race was
the main cause of Anglo-German hostility; the land arms race and the Schlieffen Plan created fear of a sudden attack
and made mobilisation dangerous; British public opinion turned against Germany. **Judgement:** for example, colonial
crises created dangerous moments, but the arms race created lasting fear and the plans that made 1914 escalate.
**[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** the blank cheque encouraged Austria-Hungary to take a
hard line; Germany declared war on Russia and France; the Schlieffen Plan meant attacking France through Belgium,
which brought Britain in. **Disagree:** Austria-Hungary chose to send a harsh ultimatum and declare war on Serbia;
Russia's general mobilisation on 30 July set off the German plans; Serbian nationalists carried out the assassination;
France supported Russia; the alliance system tied the powers together. **Judgement:** for example, Germany's
decisions made a general war more likely, but several powers took the steps that made it happen. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains what the source shows, supported by its details and by contextual knowledge.
Level 2 (3-4): explains using the source's details only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or gives knowledge
without using it. Indicative content: Germans saw their fleet as **legitimate and defensive**, protecting trade and
colonies; they **resented British claims** to naval supremacy ("ask London's permission"); they saw British building
as an attempt to **keep Germany down**, so the race fed itself. Knowledge: Tirpitz's Navy Laws of 1898 and 1900;
Weltpolitik; HMS Dreadnought (1906) and German building in response; the 1909 British demand for eight new ships.
**[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the purpose in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains
the purpose using the source only, or the context without the purpose. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative
content: **context**: Austria-Hungary annexed Bosnia-Herzegovina in October 1908; many Bosnians were Serbs; Russia did
not act, and backed down in 1909 when Germany supported Austria-Hungary. **Purpose**: to express and stir Serbian
anger ("stolen", "prisoners"); to shame Russia into supporting Serbia ("watches and does nothing"); to warn
Austria-Hungary that Serbia would seek allies and revenge ("will not be alone"). **[6]**
