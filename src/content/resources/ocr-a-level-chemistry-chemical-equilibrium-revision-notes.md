## Key terms (3.2.3(a))

- **Reversible reaction:** a reaction that can go in both directions, shown by ⇌.
- **Closed system:** nothing can enter or leave. Equilibrium can only be reached in one.
- **Dynamic equilibrium:** forward and reverse reactions run at the same rate, so no concentration in the mixture changes.
- **Position of equilibrium:** the relative amounts of reactants and products in the equilibrium mixture.
- **Homogeneous equilibrium:** all reactants and products share one phase, such as a gas mixture or one solution.

Picture it on a graph of rate against time. The forward rate starts high and falls. The reverse rate starts at zero and rises. Where the two lines meet and level off together, equilibrium has been reached.

## Shifting the position (3.2.3(b))

The specification does not require a definition of le Chatelier's principle. Use the working idea: **the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change you make**.

| You do this | The system responds by | So the position moves |
|---|---|---|
| Add more of a reactant | using some of it up | right |
| Take away some product | making more of it | right |
| Add more of a product | using some of it up | left |
| Raise the total pressure | forming fewer gas molecules | to the side with fewer gas moles |
| Lower the total pressure | forming more gas molecules | to the side with more gas moles |
| Heat the mixture | taking in heat | in the endothermic direction |
| Cool the mixture | giving out heat | in the exothermic direction |
| Add a catalyst | nothing changes in the balance | nowhere |

### Method: predicting a shift

1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols and the sign of ΔH for the forward reaction.
2. For pressure, count gas moles on each side. Ignore liquids and solids.
3. For temperature, identify which direction is endothermic.
4. State the direction of shift **and** the reason.
5. Say what happens to the amount (or yield) of the product you were asked about.

### Small worked reminder

2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g), forward reaction exothermic.

- Increase pressure: 3 gas moles on the left, 2 on the right. Shifts right. More CO₂.
- Increase temperature: shifts left, the endothermic direction. Less CO₂.

## Catalysts (3.2.3(c))

- A catalyst speeds up both directions equally, by the same factor.
- The position of equilibrium is unchanged, so the equilibrium yield is unchanged.
- Equilibrium is reached sooner, so more product is made in a given time.

## Practical work on equilibrium (3.2.3(d))

The specification asks for qualitative effects only. Choose a system with a colour change so you can see the shift.

| System | Colours | Change made | What you see | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O | yellow ⇌ orange | Add dilute acid | Turns orange | Added H⁺ shifts the position right |
| (same) | (same) | Then add dilute alkali | Turns yellow | OH⁻ removes H⁺, shift left |
| [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O | pink ⇌ blue | Add concentrated HCl | Turns blue | Added Cl⁻ shifts the position right |
| (same) | (same) | Warm in hot water | Turns blue | Forward reaction is endothermic |
| (same) | (same) | Cool in iced water | Turns pink | Exothermic direction (reverse) favoured |

Good practice in a temperature investigation:

- Use equal volumes of the same solution in each tube.
- Keep one tube at room temperature as a control for colour comparison.
- Use a hot water bath and an ice bath, and leave the tubes long enough for the colour to stop changing.
- View the tubes against a white background.

Reading the result: if heating gives more of the product colour, the forward reaction is endothermic. If cooling gives more product colour, the forward reaction is exothermic.

## Industrial compromise (3.2.3(e))

Industry balances four things: **equilibrium yield, rate, safety and cost**.

Haber process: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹.

| Condition | Equilibrium says | Rate, safety, cost say | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Low (exothermic) | Too slow if low | Compromise, about 450 °C |
| Pressure | High (4 → 2 gas moles) | Expensive, energy-hungry plant; safety risk | High but limited, about 2 × 10⁴ kPa |
| Catalyst | No effect | Faster at a lower temperature | Iron catalyst |

Unreacted gases are recycled, so the overall conversion is high even though a single pass through the reactor is not.

## The equilibrium constant Kc (3.2.3(f))

For the homogeneous reaction wW + xX ⇌ yY + zZ:

```
Kc = [Y]ʸ [Z]ᶻ / ([W]ʷ [X]ˣ)
```

- Each square bracket is an equilibrium concentration in mol dm⁻³.
- Products on top; every concentration carries its balancing number as a power.
- You do not need Kc units in 3.2.3.
- At a fixed temperature Kc has one value.

### Method: calculating Kc

1. Write the Kc expression from the balanced equation.
2. Check every value you have is an **equilibrium** concentration.
3. Substitute, raising each concentration to its power.
4. Work out the top and bottom separately, then divide.
5. Round to the significant figures of the data.

### Small worked reminder

2NOCl(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + Cl₂(g). At equilibrium, [NOCl] = 0.250, [NO] = 0.0500 and [Cl₂] = 0.0250 mol dm⁻³.

```
Kc = [NO]²[Cl₂] / [NOCl]²
   = 0.0500² × 0.0250 / 0.250²
   = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ / 0.0625
   = 1.00 × 10⁻³
```

To find an unknown concentration, rearrange the expression first, then substitute. When the unknown appears squared, finish with a square root.

## Size of Kc (3.2.3(g))

| Kc | Position |
|---|---|
| ≫ 1 | Far right, mostly products |
| ≈ 1 | Similar amounts of both |
| ≪ 1 | Far left, mostly reactants |

Only a qualitative estimate is required.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Constant** concentrations at equilibrium, not **equal** concentrations.
- **Position** of equilibrium (moved by concentration, pressure and temperature) versus the **value** of Kc (changed only by temperature).
- **Rate** (how fast equilibrium is reached) versus **yield** (how much product at equilibrium). A catalyst changes the first, not the second.
- **Total** moles versus **gas** moles when you judge a pressure change.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two features of a mixture at dynamic equilibrium.
2. Why can a reaction in an open beaker that gives off a gas not reach equilibrium?
3. For H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2HCl(g), what is the effect of raising the pressure on the position?
4. A forward reaction is exothermic. What happens to the yield of product when the temperature is raised?
5. What does a catalyst do to the time taken to reach equilibrium, and to the position?
6. What colour change do you see when dilute acid is added to yellow chromate(VI) solution?
7. A blue solution turns pink when cooled in ice: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O. Is the forward reaction exothermic or endothermic?
8. Write the Kc expression for 2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g).
9. For CO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ COCl₂(g), equilibrium concentrations are [CO] = 0.0200, [Cl₂] = 0.0150 and [COCl₂] = 0.120 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kc.
10. For 2H₂S(g) ⇌ 2H₂(g) + S₂(g), Kc = 2.00 × 10⁻⁴. At equilibrium [H₂S] = 0.100 and [H₂] = 0.0100 mol dm⁻³. Calculate [S₂].
11. A reaction has Kc = 3 × 10¹⁸. Estimate the position of equilibrium.
12. Why is the Haber process not run at a much lower temperature, even though that would raise the yield?

### Answers

1. Forward and reverse rates are equal, and no concentration changes.
2. It is not a closed system. The gas escapes, so the reverse reaction cannot happen at an equal rate.
3. No effect: 2 gas moles on each side.
4. The yield falls. The position shifts left, the endothermic direction.
5. It shortens the time taken; the position is unchanged.
6. Yellow to orange.
7. Cooling shifts it left, so the reverse is exothermic and the forward reaction is **endothermic**.
8. Kc = [CO₂]² / ([CO]²[O₂]).
9. Kc = 0.120 / (0.0200 × 0.0150) = 0.120 / 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ = **400**.
10. [S₂] = Kc × [H₂S]² / [H₂]² = 2.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.0100 / 1.00 × 10⁻⁴ = **0.0200 mol dm⁻³**.
11. Far to the right: almost all products.
12. The rate would be too slow, so too little ammonia would be made per hour.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the concentrations are equal" instead of "do not change" in a definition of dynamic equilibrium.
- Stating a shift with no reason. "Shifts right" alone rarely earns full credit; add "fewer gas moles" or "endothermic direction".
- Counting water as a gas mole when it is a liquid in the equation, or counting solids.
- Saying a catalyst increases yield, or that it speeds up only the forward reaction.
- Describing a practical with no control tube, or with tubes that differ in volume or starting colour.
- Concluding the wrong sign of ΔH from a colour change. Decide which way the position moved first, then link it to heating or cooling.
- Inverting the Kc expression, or leaving out a power such as the 2 in [NO]².
- Substituting starting concentrations instead of equilibrium concentrations.
- In a compromise question, writing only about yield and forgetting rate, cost and safety.
