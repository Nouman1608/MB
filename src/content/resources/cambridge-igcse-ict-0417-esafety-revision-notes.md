Section 8.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers data protection, personal and sensitive data, and eSafety on the internet, by email, on social media and in online games. These notes are for recall. The [eSafety study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-esafety/) explains the reasons behind each point with a worked scenario, and the [eSafety practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-esafety-practice/) test you on applying them.

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus wording | Recall |
|---|---|
| Data protection | Principles of a typical data protection act; why legislation is required |
| Personal data | Name, address, date of birth, photo in school uniform, medical history; need for confidentiality |
| eSafety | Need for it on the internet, email, social media, online gaming; how to minimise danger in each |

## Why data protection law is needed

Without it, organisations could collect too much data, use it for other purposes, keep it inaccurate or for ever, or lose it. The law gives **data subjects** rights and puts **duties on organisations**, with penalties for breaking it.

## Principles: data must be...

1. Processed **fairly and lawfully**.
2. Used **only for the stated purpose**.
3. **Adequate, relevant, not excessive**.
4. **Accurate and up to date**.
5. **Not kept longer than necessary**.
6. Processed in line with the **data subject's rights**.
7. Kept **secure**.
8. **Not transferred** to countries without adequate protection.

Memory aid: *fair, purpose, amount, accurate, time, rights, secure, abroad*.

## Personal and sensitive data

| Item (syllabus example) | Why it is personal |
|---|---|
| Personal name | Identifies the person |
| Address | Shows where they live |
| Date of birth | Confirms identity; used in security checks |
| Photograph in school uniform | Shows appearance and which school they attend |
| Medical history | Private health information; **sensitive** |

**Sensitive data**: personal data that could cause greater harm or discrimination if disclosed: medical history; also ethnic origin, religion, political views, criminal record.

### Harm from disclosure

- Identity theft and fraud.
- Being located by strangers.
- Discrimination.
- Distress and embarrassment.
- Targeted scams.

## eSafety by context

| Context | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Internet | Use trusted websites recommended by teachers | Avoids unsuitable content, malware and scams |
| Internet | Use a search engine that only allows age-appropriate sites | Filters out unsuitable results |
| Email | Do not open or reply to emails from unknown people | May contain malware or phishing links; replying confirms the address |
| Email | Avoid sending personal identifiable data or images | Can be forwarded, misdirected or intercepted |
| Social media | Block and report unwanted users | Stops contact and alerts the platform |
| Social media | Do not meet online contacts face to face | They may not be who they claim |
| Social media | Do not distribute inappropriate images | Cannot be recalled; may be illegal |
| Social media | Do not use inappropriate language | Hurts others; permanent; may break rules or law |
| Social media | Respect others' personal data | Do not post others' details or photos without permission |
| Online gaming | Do not use your real name | Other players cannot identify or find you |
| Online gaming | Do not give personal or financial data | Strangers may try to steal accounts or money |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data protection act | Law controlling how personal data is collected, stored and used |
| Data subject | The living person the data is about |
| Personal data | Data about a living person who can be identified from it |
| Sensitive data | Personal data that could cause greater harm if disclosed |
| Confidential | Kept private and shown only to those with a right to see it |
| Inappropriate disclosure | Personal data being seen by, or given to, people who should not have it |
| eSafety | Using technology in ways that protect yourself and others from harm |
| Block | Stop a user from contacting you |
| Report | Tell the platform about a user or content that breaks its rules |

## Exam traps

- **Principles are duties on organisations**, not personal advice.
- **Name the context**: a gaming question needs gaming rules, not email ones.
- **Always add the reason**: "do not use your real name, so you cannot be identified".
- **School uniform** matters because it reveals the school.
- **Medical history** is personal *and* sensitive.
- **Data protection is not the same as data security**: security (passwords, encryption) is one way of meeting the "kept secure" principle.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why data protection legislation is required.
2. State four principles of a typical data protection act.
3. Is "do not share your password" a data protection principle? Explain.
4. Define personal data.
5. Why is a photograph of a pupil in school uniform personal data?
6. Give one example of sensitive data.
7. Give two possible consequences of personal data being disclosed inappropriately.
8. Give two ways to minimise danger when using the internet.
9. Why should you not reply to an email from an unknown sender?
10. Give two eSafety actions for social media.
11. Give two eSafety rules for online gaming.
12. A shop asks customers for their date of birth to send a birthday voucher, then uses the data to sell to a marketing firm. Which principle is broken?

## Self-test answers

1. Any two of: stop data being collected unfairly; stop misuse for other purposes; ensure accuracy; stop data being kept too long; ensure it is kept secure; give people rights over their data.
2. Any four of the eight principles above.
3. No. It is good eSafety advice for an individual; principles are duties on organisations holding data.
4. Data about a living person who can be identified from it.
5. It shows what the pupil looks like and which school they attend, so they could be identified and found.
6. Medical history (or ethnic origin, religion, political views, criminal record).
7. Any two of: identity theft; fraud; being located; discrimination; distress; targeted scams.
8. Use trusted websites recommended by teachers; use a search engine that only shows age-appropriate sites.
9. It may contain malware or phishing links, and replying confirms the address is active.
10. Any two of: block and report unwanted users; do not meet online contacts; do not share inappropriate images; avoid inappropriate language; respect others' personal data.
11. Do not use your real name; do not give out personal or financial data.
12. Data must be used only for the purpose it was collected for.
