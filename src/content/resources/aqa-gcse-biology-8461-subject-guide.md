AQA GCSE Biology (8461) is the separate science Biology GCSE, taken by students who study biology as a full
qualification in its own right rather than as part of a combined science award. It covers cells, body systems,
disease, energy, control systems, genetics and ecology. The course is linear and tiered: students are entered
for Foundation or Higher, and on either tier they sit two written papers, each lasting 1 hour 45 minutes, worth
100 marks and carrying 50% of the GCSE.

## How the course is organised

The specification is set out in seven content topics and a short section of key ideas.

**Topics 1 to 4: cells, organisation, disease and energy.** Cell biology covers cell structure, cell division and
transport in cells. Organisation is the largest topic, moving from the principles of organisation through the
digestive system, the heart, blood vessels and blood, to coronary heart disease, health issues, lifestyle and
non-communicable diseases, cancer, plant tissues and the plant organ system. Infection and response covers
communicable diseases, monoclonal antibodies and plant disease. Bioenergetics covers photosynthesis and
respiration.

**Topics 5 to 7: control, inheritance and ecosystems.** Homeostasis and response covers homeostasis, the human
nervous system, hormonal coordination and plant hormones. Inheritance, variation and evolution covers
reproduction, variation and evolution, how understanding of genetics and evolution developed, and the
classification of living organisms. Ecology covers adaptations, interdependence and competition, how ecosystems
are organised, biodiversity and human impact, trophic levels and food production.

**Topic 8: key ideas.** This short section collects the big ideas that run across the whole course rather than
adding new content of its own.

Some subtopics are labelled "biology only", which marks content that appears in this separate Biology GCSE but
not in combined science. One subtopic, monoclonal antibodies, is also labelled "HT only", meaning it is assessed
on the Higher tier only.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Tier | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Topics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8461/1F Paper 1 | Foundation | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 1-4 |
| 8461/1H Paper 1 | Higher | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 1-4 |
| 8461/2F Paper 2 | Foundation | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 5-7 |
| 8461/2H Paper 2 | Higher | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 5-7 |

Each tier has its own question papers; Foundation and Higher candidates do not share a paper. Questions are a mix
of multiple choice, structured, closed short answer and open response. Across the qualification, the assessment
objectives are weighted AO1 40%, AO2 40% and AO3 20%, and the same split applies to each paper.

There is no separate practical mark. Instead, 10 required practicals are examined within the written papers, so
the practical work done in lessons is assessed through exam questions. The specification was first taught in
September 2016 and first examined in May/June 2018.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers divide the content cleanly, with topics 1 to 4 on Paper 1 and topics 5 to 7 on Paper 2, but the
biology itself does not divide so neatly. Cell structure and transport in cells underpin almost every later
topic. Respiration in bioenergetics explains why organisms need the exchange and transport systems met in
organisation. Hormonal coordination in topic 5 connects to reproduction in topic 6, and photosynthesis in topic 4
reappears when ecology looks at how energy moves through an ecosystem.

The required practicals and the AO3 weighting are the skills thread. Because a fifth of the marks sit with AO3,
which covers analysing information and evaluating methods, students need to be comfortable reading data, spotting patterns, judging methods and suggesting
improvements, not only recalling facts. Practising that way throughout the course is more effective than leaving
it to the final months.

Tier choice also shapes study. Higher tier students need the HT-only content and should expect more demanding
application questions; Foundation students should concentrate on secure recall and clear, structured answers.
Your teacher will confirm which tier you are entered for; until then, working to the Higher standard where
possible keeps both options open.

## Planning your study

A logical two-year route follows the paper split:

1. **Start with cell biology.** It is the foundation for the rest of the course.
2. **Work through organisation, infection and response, and bioenergetics** to complete the Paper 1 content.
3. **Move to homeostasis and response, then inheritance, variation and evolution.**
4. **Finish with ecology**, which draws together ideas from bioenergetics, inheritance and organisation.

Review the key ideas section at the end of each topic, and keep a running log of each required practical: what
was measured, how variables were controlled and what the results showed. Practice questions can start as soon as
a topic is taught. Full past papers fit best once all of a paper's content is covered, which usually means Paper 1
practice comes earlier than Paper 2. Exam timing and technique are covered on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE Biology?**
Two. On either tier, Paper 1 and Paper 2 each last 1 hour 45 minutes, are worth 100 marks and count for 50% of the GCSE.

**What topics are on Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers cell biology, organisation, infection and response, and bioenergetics (topics 1-4). Paper 2 covers homeostasis and response, inheritance, variation and evolution, and ecology (topics 5-7).

**Do Foundation and Higher students sit the same paper?**
No. Each tier has its own Paper 1 and Paper 2, coded F for Foundation and H for Higher.

**How are the required practicals assessed?**
There are 10 required practicals, examined through questions in the written papers. There is no separate practical mark.

**What does "biology only" mean on the specification?**
It marks content that is part of the separate Biology GCSE but not combined science. "HT only" marks content assessed on the Higher tier only.
