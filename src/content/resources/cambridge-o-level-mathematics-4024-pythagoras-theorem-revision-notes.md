These notes summarise section 6.1, Pythagoras' theorem, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [Pythagoras' theorem study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem/), and exam-style questions are in the [Pythagoras' theorem practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-practice/).

## What is examined

Know and use Pythagoras' theorem. The theorem is not in the List of formulas, so you must recall it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypotenuse | the longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle |
| Pythagorean triple | three whole numbers a, b, c with a² + b² = c² |
| Converse | if a² + b² = c² then the triangle is right-angled |
| Surd | an exact root that is not a whole number, such as √40 |
| Diagonal | a line joining opposite corners |
| Perpendicular height | height at 90° to the base |

## The theorem

```
a² + b² = c²
```

where c is the hypotenuse.

| To find | Use |
|---|---|
| hypotenuse | c = √(a² + b²) |
| shorter side | a = √(c² − b²) |

Rule of thumb: the hypotenuse is always the longest side. If your answer for a shorter side is bigger than the hypotenuse, you added by mistake.

## Method box

1. Sketch the triangle and mark the right angle.
2. Label the hypotenuse c, which is opposite the right angle.
3. Write the equation a² + b² = c² with the numbers.
4. Rearrange, then take the square root.
5. Check that the hypotenuse is the longest side.
6. Round to 3 significant figures, or give a surd if asked.

## Testing for a right angle

```
Find the largest square (the longest side squared).
Add the other two squares.
Equal  →  right-angled.   Not equal  →  not right-angled.
```

Example: 7, 9, 12 gives 49 + 81 = 130, but 12² = 144. Not right-angled.

## Where the theorem is used

| Situation | Triangle to draw |
|---|---|
| Ladder against a wall | wall, ground and ladder |
| Diagonal of a rectangle | two sides and the diagonal |
| Distance between two points | horizontal and vertical differences |
| Height of an isosceles triangle | half the base, equal side, height |
| Height of an equilateral triangle | half the base (side ÷ 2), side, height |
| Side of a rhombus | half of each diagonal |
| Bearings (east and north) | the two legs at right angles |

## Special results

```
equilateral triangle of side s:   height = (√3 / 2) × s,   for s = 8: 6.93
square of side s:                 diagonal = s√2,          for s = 7: 9.90
isosceles 13, 13, 10:             height 12, area 60
rhombus with diagonals 16, 30:    side 17
distance from (−2, 3) to (4, −5): 10
```

## Exact answers

Simplify surds by taking out square factors: √40 = √(4 × 10) = 2√10 and √80 = 4√5. Give a surd only if the question asks. Otherwise, give 3 significant figures.

## Algebraic problems

If the sides are expressions, form an equation and solve it.

```
x² + (x + 7)² = 13²   →   x² + 7x − 60 = 0   →   x = 5
```

Remember to expand (x + 7)² fully: x² + 14x + 49.

## Why it works

Draw a square on each side of a right-angled triangle. The area of the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the areas of the other two squares. For the 3, 4, 5 triangle: 9 + 16 = 25. Four copies of the triangle can be arranged inside a square of side a + b in two different ways, and comparing the leftover areas gives a² + b² = c². You do not need to prove it in the examination, but the picture helps you remember what the equation says.

## Useful checks

1. The hypotenuse is less than the sum of the two shorter sides: 25 < 7 + 24.
2. The hypotenuse is greater than either shorter side.
3. A scale drawing should agree within measurement error.
4. A multiple of a triple needs no calculation: 15, 20, 25 is 5 times 3, 4, 5.

## Must-know distinctions

| Find hypotenuse | Find shorter side |
|---|---|
| add the squares | subtract the squares |
| answer larger than both sides | answer smaller than the hypotenuse |

## Exam traps

- Adding when you should subtract.
- Forgetting the final square root.
- Using the theorem for triangles without a right angle.
- Mistaking a shorter side for the hypotenuse.
- Expanding (x + 7)² incorrectly.
- Rounding before the end.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Calculate | work out, showing the squares and the root |
| Show that | prove by exact working |
| Find | calculate the missing length |
| Work out | calculate with or without a calculator |
| Give your answer as a surd | simplify the root and leave it exact |

## Self-test

1. Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 7 cm and 24 cm.
2. The hypotenuse is 13 cm and one side is 12 cm. Find the other side.
3. Find the hypotenuse for sides 4 cm and 6 cm, as a simplified surd.
4. Is a triangle with sides 10 cm, 24 cm and 26 cm right-angled?
5. Find the distance from (0, 0) to (5, 12).
6. A 10 m ladder has its foot 6 m from a wall. How high does it reach?
7. Find the diagonal of a square of side 7 cm to 3 significant figures.
8. Find the height of an equilateral triangle of side 10 cm to 3 significant figures.
9. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 9 cm and one side 4 cm. Find the other side to 3 significant figures.
10. A rhombus has diagonals of 10 cm and 24 cm. Find the side.

### Answers

1. √(49 + 576) = √625 = **25 cm**.
2. √(169 − 144) = **5 cm**.
3. √(16 + 36) = √52 = **2√13 cm**.
4. 10² + 24² = 676 = 26², so **yes**.
5. √(25 + 144) = **13**.
6. √(100 − 36) = **8 m**.
7. √98 = **9.90 cm**.
8. √(100 − 25) = √75 = **8.66 cm**.
9. √(81 − 16) = √65 = **8.06 cm**.
10. Half-diagonals 5 and 12, so side = **13 cm**.
