These notes condense **3.1.1.3 Electron configuration** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For worked examples and full explanations see the [electron configuration study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-3/); for exam-style questions use the [electron configuration practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-3-practice/).

## Filling rules

- Order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p.
- Capacity: s 2 (1 orbital), p 6 (3 orbitals), d 10 (5 orbitals).
- Two electrons per orbital, opposite spins.
- Fill orbitals in a sub-shell singly before pairing.
- Exceptions: Cr [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹; Cu [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.

| Br⁻ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶ |
| S²⁻ | 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ |

**Ions of d-block elements lose 4s electrons before 3d electrons.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shell | Main energy level, numbered n = 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| Sub-shell | Division of a shell: s, p or d |
| Orbital | Region holding up to two electrons of opposite spin |
| First ionisation energy | Energy needed to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions |
| Successive ionisation energies | Energies to remove the second, third and later electrons, one at a time, from gaseous ions |
| Shielding | Reduction of nuclear attraction on outer electrons by electrons in inner shells |
| Nuclear charge | Total positive charge of the nucleus, equal to the number of protons |

## Ionisation energy equations

- First: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻
- Second: X⁺(g) → X²⁺(g) + e⁻
- nth: X⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾⁺(g) → Xⁿ⁺(g) + e⁻

## Three factors

Nuclear charge (up), distance from nucleus (down), shielding (down). Use all three where they apply.

## Trends and the evidence they give

**Down Group 2** (approx. kJ mol⁻¹: Be 900, Mg 738, Ca 590, Sr 550, Ba 503): first ionisation energy falls. Extra shell each time, so outer electron further away and more shielded; this outweighs the larger nuclear charge. Evidence for **shells**.

**Across Period 3** (Na 496, Mg 738, Al 578, Si 787, P 1012, S 1000, Cl 1251, Ar 1521): general rise because nuclear charge increases with similar shielding in the same shell.

- Dip at Al: 3p electron is higher in energy than 3s and partly shielded by it. Evidence for **sub-shells**.
- Dip at S: paired electrons in one 3p orbital repel. Evidence for **orbitals holding two electrons**.

**Successive ionisation energies**: always increase; a big jump means the next electron comes from a shell nearer the nucleus. Electrons removed before the first big jump = outer-shell electrons = group number (Groups 1 and 2) or group number minus 10 (Groups 13 to 18).

**Second ionisation energy across Period 3**: highest for Na, because Na⁺ loses a 2p electron from a full inner shell.

## Reading successive ionisation energy data

1. Look for the largest proportional jump, not the largest difference in kJ mol⁻¹; values grow so quickly that a ratio is the fairer test.
2. Count the electrons removed before the jump: that is the number of outer-shell electrons.
3. Name the group and, if the period is given, the element.
4. Check the next jump: it should come after a further eight electrons for Period 3 elements, marking the move from n = 2 to n = 1.

Example (approximate values): aluminium 578, 1817, 2745, 11577. The jump comes after three electrons, so the element is in Group 13, with configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p¹. The fourth electron comes from 2p.

## Exam traps

- Remove 4s before 3d when forming d-block ions.
- Every ionisation equation removes one electron and shows gaseous species.
- "Stable full sub-shell" and "stable half-filled sub-shell" do not earn credit; give energies, shielding or repulsion.
- In successive ionisation energy questions, explain using the configuration of the ion losing the electron.
- Chromium and copper are the only exceptions you need to Z = 36.
- Shielding is by inner shells; electrons in the same shell shield each other very little.

## Self-test

1. Write the full configuration of arsenic (Z = 33).
2. Write the configuration of a Cr³⁺ ion (Cr has Z = 24).
3. Which atom up to Z = 36 has the configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹?
4. Name two species that are isoelectronic with Ar and carry opposite charges.
5. Define first ionisation energy.
6. Write the equation for the third ionisation energy of aluminium.
7. Why does first ionisation energy decrease from Mg to Ca?
8. Why is the first ionisation energy of Al lower than that of Mg?
9. Why is the first ionisation energy of S lower than that of P?
10. Which Period 3 element has the highest second ionisation energy, and why?
11. An element's successive ionisation energies show the first large jump between the third and fourth values. Which group is it in?
12. In which block is selenium (Z = 34), and what is its outer sub-shell configuration?

### Answers

1. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³
2. Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹; remove 4s¹ then two 3d: [Ar] 3d³.
3. Copper.
4. For example K⁺ and Cl⁻ (also Ca²⁺, S²⁻).
5. The energy needed to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.
6. Al²⁺(g) → Al³⁺(g) + e⁻
7. Calcium's outer electron is in a shell further from the nucleus and more shielded; this outweighs the greater nuclear charge.
8. Al's outer electron is in 3p, higher in energy than 3s and partly shielded by the 3s electrons.
9. In S two electrons share a 3p orbital and repel, so one is easier to remove.
10. Sodium: Na⁺ loses an electron from the 2p sub-shell, closer to the nucleus and less shielded.
11. Group 13.
12. p block; 4s² 4p⁴.
