## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A worker is paid wages and uses them to buy food at a supermarket. Which function of money does this show? **[1]**

A: Unit of account

B: Medium of exchange

C: Store of value

D: Means of deferred payment

**2.** Define money. **[1]**

**3.** State the function of money shown in each case.

**(a)** A firm records its costs and revenue in pounds. **[1]**

**(b)** A household buys a sofa and pays for it in 12 monthly instalments. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** qualities that allow an item to work well as money. **[2]**

**5.** Aisha keeps £3,000 of savings in a current account that pays no interest. Prices rise by 4% over the year.

**(a)** Calculate the value of her savings at the end of the year in terms of last year's prices, to the nearest pound. **[2]**

**(b)** State the interest rate her account would need to pay for her savings to keep their purchasing power. **[1]**

**6.** An island economy trades 12 different goods. Calculate how many exchange rates are needed if every pair of goods is bartered, and how many prices are needed if money is used. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**7.** Explain why barter makes it difficult for workers to specialise. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why a credit card is not money, even though it can be used to pay in shops. **[3]**

**9.** A café stops accepting cash and takes only card and phone payments. Explain why it is still being paid in money. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why money can act as a means of deferred payment only if it is also a good store of value. **[3]**

**11.** In Country Z, prices are doubling every month.

**(a)** Calculate how many times higher prices will be after three months. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** functions of money that are weakened in Country Z. **[4]**

**12.** "The most important function of money is as a medium of exchange." Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Money is being accepted in exchange for goods. **[1]**

**2.** Anything that is generally accepted in payment for goods and services (and in settling debts) [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Unit of account [1].

**(b)** Means of deferred payment [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the sofa is paid for later, so the debt is fixed in money and settled over time. "Medium of exchange" is too general for the credit.

**4.** Any two: generally acceptable [1]; durable [1]; divisible; portable; limited in supply; hard to forge. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 3,000 ÷ 1.04 [1] = **£2,885** [1].

**(b)** **4%** (an interest rate equal to the rate of inflation) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Barter: 12 × 11 ÷ 2 = **66** exchange rates [1]. Money: **12** prices, one for each good [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Under barter a worker must swap what they make for what they need directly [1], which requires a double coincidence of wants, so a specialist such as a teacher would have to find a farmer who wants teaching [1]. This takes so long that people make many things for themselves instead of specialising, so productivity stays low [1]. **[3]**

**8.** A credit card gives the holder access to borrowed money [1]: the card company pays the shop and the holder repays later [1]. The money is the deposit or loan that is spent; the card is a way of borrowing it, linked to the means of deferred payment, not money itself [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Card and phone payments transfer bank deposits from the customer's account to the café's account [1]. Deposits are generally accepted in payment, so they act as a medium of exchange [1]. Money is therefore more than notes and coins: deposits are money too [1]. **[3]**

**10.** A means of deferred payment lets debts be fixed now and repaid in the future [1]. Lenders will only accept repayment later if money keeps its value until then [1]; if money lost value quickly, they would be repaid in money that buys much less, so they would refuse to lend or demand very high interest [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** 2 × 2 × 2 = **8 times** higher [1].

**(b)** Any two developed:

Store of value: money loses purchasing power very quickly [1], so people spend it at once rather than save it [1].

Means of deferred payment: lenders would be repaid in money worth far less [1], so they stop lending or demand very high interest rates [1].

Unit of account (an alternative for the same marks): prices change so fast that they cannot be compared or relied on, so firms struggle to set prices and plan. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* For each function, name it and give a reason linked to rapidly rising prices. Two named functions with no reasoning score only two of the four marks.

**12.** *For:*

- Without a medium of exchange, trade needs a double coincidence of wants [1], so trade is slow and people cannot specialise [1].
- Specialisation raises productivity and output, so this function supports economic growth [1].
- People accept money only because they can spend it, so the other functions rest on this one [1].

*Against:*

- A unit of account is essential for firms to calculate costs and profit and for comparing prices [1].
- The store of value lets households save, and those savings fund investment [1].
- The means of deferred payment allows mortgages and business loans, which support spending and investment [1].
- The functions depend on each other, for example deferred payment needs a store of value [1].

*Judgement:* For example, the medium of exchange is the most important because the other functions only matter if money is accepted in the first place, but in practice money is useful because it performs all four together, so ranking them depends on which problem of barter you see as most serious [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing store of value and means of deferred payment.
- Calling a credit or debit card money.
- Dividing by the wrong figure in a real-value calculation, such as 0.04 instead of 1.04 for 4% inflation.
- Naming a function without linking it to the situation in the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I spot which function a question is about?**
Look for the clue: buying and selling means medium of exchange; comparing or recording values means unit of account; saving means store of value; paying later means deferred payment.

**Can one example show more than one function?**
Yes. A savings account holds value and is also priced in pounds; choose the function the question is focused on and justify it.
