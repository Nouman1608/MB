These questions cover sub-topic C.5, Doppler effect, in the IB *Physics guide* (first assessment 2025). Questions 1 to 8 apply to SL and HL. Questions 9 to 12 are HL only. Learn the content in the [C.5 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-physics-dp-physics-ib-dp-physics-c-5/) and the [C.5 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-physics-dp-physics-ib-dp-physics-c-5-revision-notes/).

Data where needed: c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; speed of sound in air v = 340 m s⁻¹. All stars, galaxies and vehicles are invented.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the Doppler effect. **[1]**

**2.** A source of sound moves to the right at constant speed through still air. Describe the wavefront pattern and state how the wavelength and frequency detected by an observer to the right of the source compare with those emitted. **[2]**

**3.** A hydrogen line with rest wavelength 434.0 nm is observed at 436.6 nm in the spectrum of a galaxy. Determine the speed of the galaxy along the line of sight and state its direction. **[2]**

**4.** An observer moves towards a stationary source of sound. Which statement is correct?

A: The wavelength in the air decreases and the observed frequency increases.

B: The wavelength in the air is unchanged and the observed frequency increases.

C: The wavelength in the air is unchanged and the observed frequency decreases.

D: The wavelength in the air increases and the observed frequency is unchanged. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why Δλ/λ ≈ v/c should not be used for a galaxy receding at 0.6c. **[1]**

**6.** A satellite transmits at 1.2 GHz while moving directly towards a ground station at 7.5 km s⁻¹. Calculate the change in frequency detected and state whether it is an increase or a decrease. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A line of rest wavelength 656.3 nm in the spectrum of a star moves regularly between 656.1 nm and 656.5 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum speed of the star along the line of sight. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the line alternates between shorter and longer wavelengths. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why, at certain times, the line is observed at exactly 656.3 nm even though the star is still moving. **[1]**

**8.** Two lines in the spectrum of a galaxy are observed at 491.0 nm and 662.9 nm. Their laboratory wavelengths are 486.1 nm and 656.3 nm.

**(a)** Show that both lines are shifted by the same fraction of their wavelength. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the galaxy and state its direction relative to Earth. **[2]**

**9.** (HL) An ambulance siren emits 960 Hz. The ambulance travels at 30 m s⁻¹ past a stationary pedestrian.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency heard as the ambulance approaches. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency heard as it moves away. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound in the air ahead of the ambulance. **[1]**

**10.** (HL) A stationary listener hears a motorcycle horn at 720 Hz as it approaches and at 600 Hz after it has passed. Determine the speed of the motorcycle and the frequency emitted by the horn. **[4]**

**11.** (HL) A stationary alarm emits 500 Hz.

**(a)** A student on a bus moves directly towards the alarm at 12 m s⁻¹. Calculate the frequency the student hears. **[2]**

**(b)** In a different situation, the alarm moves at 12 m s⁻¹ towards a stationary student. Calculate the frequency heard. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the answers to (a) and (b) are not the same. **[1]**

**12.** (HL) A source of frequency f, moving with speed us through a medium in which waves travel at speed v, emits one wavefront every period T. Use this to show that the wavelength ahead of the source is (v − us)/f and explain why the observed frequency there is higher than f. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The change in the observed frequency (and wavelength) of a wave when the source and observer move relative to each other [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Wavefronts are circles that are closer together on the right (ahead of the source) and further apart on the left [1]. The observer to the right detects a shorter wavelength and a higher frequency than emitted [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Δλ = 436.6 − 434.0 = 2.6 nm; v = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 2.6/434.0 [1] = **1.8 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, moving away** (redshift) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The approximation is only valid when v is much less than c; 0.6c is not [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Δf = f v/c = 1.2 × 10⁹ × 7.5 × 10³ / 3.00 × 10⁸ [1] = **3.0 × 10⁴ Hz (30 kHz), an increase** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Δλ = 0.2 nm [1]; v = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.2/656.3 = **9.1 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** The star is orbiting another body [1], so it moves alternately towards us (blueshift, shorter λ) and away from us (redshift, longer λ) [1].

**(c)** At those points its velocity is perpendicular to the line of sight, so the line-of-sight component is zero [1]. **[5]**

**8.** **(a)** (491.0 − 486.1)/486.1 = 0.0101 [1]; (662.9 − 656.3)/656.3 = 0.0101 [1].

**(b)** v = 0.0101 × 3.00 × 10⁸ [1] = **3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, receding** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the same fractional shift for every line is the evidence that the shift is caused by motion.

**9.** **(a)** f′ = 960 × 340/(340 − 30) [1] = **1050 Hz** (1053 Hz) [1].

**(b)** f′ = 960 × 340/(340 + 30) [1] = **882 Hz** [1].

**(c)** λ = (340 − 30)/960 = **0.323 m** [1]. **[5]**

**10.** 720 = f × 340/(340 − u) and 600 = f × 340/(340 + u) [1]. Dividing: 1.2 = (340 + u)/(340 − u), so 408 − 1.2u = 340 + u [1], giving u = 68/2.2 = **31 m s⁻¹** [1]. f = 720 × (340 − 30.9)/340 = **655 Hz** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* dividing the two equations eliminates f; check by substituting back into the second equation (655 × 340/370.9 ≈ 600 Hz).

**11.** **(a)** f′ = 500 × (340 + 12)/340 [1] = **518 Hz** (517.6 Hz) [1].

**(b)** f′ = 500 × 340/(340 − 12) [1] = **518 Hz** (518.3 Hz) [1].

**(c)** For sound the motion is measured relative to the medium; a moving source changes the wavelength in the air, while a moving observer only meets wavefronts more often, so the formulas differ (the values differ slightly, by about 0.7 Hz) [1]. **[5]**

**12.** In one period the wavefront moves vT and the source moves usT in the same direction [1], so the distance between successive wavefronts ahead is λ′ = vT − usT = (v − us)/f [1]. The wave speed is still v, so f′ = v/λ′ = f v/(v − us), which is greater than f because λ′ < λ [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a speed from a spectrum without stating towards or away.
- Sign errors: an approaching source or observer must give a higher frequency.
- Rounding intermediate values in two-reading problems, so the check fails.
- Saying the wave speed changes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which parts of C.5 are HL only?**
The formulas for sound and other mechanical waves with a moving source or moving observer are additional higher level. Wavefront diagrams, the light relationship and spectral-line shifts are for SL and HL.

**How do I solve a Doppler problem with an approach and a recession reading?**
Write both equations, divide one by the other to cancel the emitted frequency, solve for the speed, then substitute back to find the frequency.
