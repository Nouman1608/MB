These questions cover section **3.3.6.2 Mass spectrometry** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Mass spectrometry study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-mass-spectrometry/) and the [Mass spectrometry revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-mass-spectrometry-revision-notes/). Use these precise atomic masses throughout: ¹H = 1.0078, ¹²C = 12.0000, ¹⁴N = 14.0031, ¹⁶O = 15.9949.

## Questions

**1.** In a mass spectrometer a molecule M is ionised by electron impact.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of the molecular ion. **[1]**

**(b)** State what the m/z value of the molecular ion peak shows. **[1]**

**2.** C₂H₆O and CH₂O₂ both have a molecular ion peak at m/z 46 in a low-resolution mass spectrum.

**(a)** Calculate the precise mass of each molecule. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how a high-resolution mass spectrum can tell the two apart. **[1]**

**3.** A compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen has a molecular ion at m/z 60.0210. The possible formulae are C₃H₈O (60.0573) and C₂H₄O₂ (60.0210).

**(a)** State the molecular formula. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your choice. **[1]**

**4.** A compound containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen only has a precise molecular mass of 56.0261. Show that its molecular formula is C₃H₄O and not C₄H₈. **[3]**

**5.** A compound has a molecular ion with m/z 88.0522 and contains C, H and O only. Possible formulae are C₄H₈O₂, C₅H₁₂O and C₃H₄O₃.

**(a)** Calculate the precise mass of each possible formula. **[3]**

**(b)** State the molecular formula of the compound. **[1]**

**(c)** The compound fizzes when added to sodium carbonate solution. Suggest a structure for the compound. **[1]**

**6.** Calculate the precise mass of a molecule of C₆H₁₂O₂. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why the precise mass of C₃H₆O (58.0417) is slightly greater than 58 whereas the precise mass of C₂H₂O₂ (58.0054) is much closer to 58. **[2]**

**8.** A compound has a molecular ion at m/z 72.0210. It contains carbon, hydrogen and oxygen only, and the possible formulae are C₄H₈O (72.0573), C₃H₄O₂ (72.0210) and C₅H₁₂ (72.0936). It decolourises bromine water and fizzes with sodium carbonate solution.

**(a)** State the molecular formula. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the compound and explain how each test result supports your answer. **[4]**

**9.** The mass spectrum of propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH (Mᵣ = 74), has a peak at m/z 45.

**(a)** Suggest the formula of the ion responsible. **[1]**

**(b)** State the mass of the species that was lost to form this ion. **[1]**

**10.** Explain why only some of the particles formed when a molecular ion breaks up appear as peaks in a mass spectrum. **[2]**

**11.** The mass spectrum of a compound with a molecular ion at m/z 58 has a strong peak at m/z 43. Suggest what was lost and give the formula of one fragment ion that has m/z 43. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** M(g) + e⁻ → M⁺•(g) + 2e⁻ [1]

**(b)** The relative molecular mass of the compound [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** C₂H₆O: 2(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 15.9949 = **46.0417** [1]; CH₂O₂: 12.0000 + 2(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = **46.0054** [1]

**(b)** The two have different precise masses, so the peak positions can be told apart at four decimal places [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** C₂H₄O₂ [1]

**(b)** Its calculated precise mass, 60.0210, matches the measured value, whereas C₃H₈O gives 60.0573 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** C₃H₄O: 3(12.0000) + 4(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 36.0000 + 4.0312 + 15.9949 = 56.0261 [1]; C₄H₈: 4(12.0000) + 8(1.0078) = 56.0624 [1]; only C₃H₄O matches the measured 56.0261 (and C₄H₈ contains no oxygen, which the compound does) [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** C₄H₈O₂ = 48.0000 + 8.0624 + 31.9898 = **88.0522** [1]; C₅H₁₂O = 60.0000 + 12.0936 + 15.9949 = **88.0885** [1]; C₃H₄O₃ = 36.0000 + 4.0312 + 47.9847 = **88.0159** [1]

**(b)** C₄H₈O₂ [1]

**(c)** A carboxylic acid, for example butanoic acid CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (or 2-methylpropanoic acid) [1]. **[5]**

**6.** 6(12.0000) = 72.0000 and 12(1.0078) = 12.0936 and 2(15.9949) = 31.9898 [1]; total = **116.0834** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Hydrogen atoms (1.0078) are heavier than 1, so the more hydrogen atoms there are, the higher the mass above the whole number [1]; oxygen atoms (15.9949) are lighter than 16, so the extra oxygen in C₂H₂O₂ lowers the total and its mass is closer to a whole number [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** C₃H₄O₂ [1]

**(b)** The compound is propenoic acid, CH₂=CHCOOH [1]; decolourising bromine water shows a C=C bond [1]; fizzing with sodium carbonate shows a carboxylic acid group [1]; C₃H₄O₂ has one C=C and one COOH, which fits the formula [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The formula tells you which atoms are present, and the test results tell you which groups. Always say what each result shows.

**9. (a)** COOH⁺ (the ion CHO₂⁺) [1]

**(b)** 29, the loss of an ethyl radical, C₂H₅• [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Only positively charged fragments are accelerated and detected [1]; neutral fragments such as radicals are not detected [1]. **[2]**

**11.** A mass of 15 was lost, which is a methyl radical, CH₃• [1]; the ion with m/z 43 could be CH₃CO⁺ or C₃H₇⁺ [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using relative atomic masses such as H = 1.0 instead of the precise masses given.
- Stopping at the nominal mass and not comparing the decimal places.
- Not saying why the other candidates are wrong.
- Giving a formula when the question asks for a structure, or the reverse.
- Treating neutral fragments as peaks.
