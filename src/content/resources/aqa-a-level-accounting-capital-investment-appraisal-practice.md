This set covers section 3.13, Capital investment appraisal, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127)
specification, version 1.1 (October 2026), for A-level exams from June 2019 onwards. The course is
untiered; this section is assessed on Paper 2. All businesses and figures are invented. Round present
values to the nearest £ and payback periods to the nearest month.

## Questions

**1.** Hollins Ltd is appraising a new machine. Which **one** of the following is a relevant cash flow
for the appraisal? **[1]**

A. Annual depreciation on the machine
B. A feasibility study paid for last month
C. The cash expected from selling the machine at the end of its life
D. A share of the existing factory rent charged to the machine's department

**2.** What is the discount factor for a cash flow at the end of year 3 when the cost of capital is 9%? **[1]**

A. 0.270
B. 0.772
C. 0.842
D. 0.917

**3.** State the meaning of:

**(a)** discount factor **[1]**
**(b)** cost of capital **[1]**

**4.** Marsh Couriers plans to buy a van for £84 000. It will generate net cash inflows of £24 000 a year.
Calculate the payback period in years and months. **[2]**

**5.** Linden Roasters is considering a coffee roaster costing £75 000. It will be used for 3 years and
then sold for £15 000. Depreciation is straight-line. Forecast profits from the roaster, after charging
depreciation, are: year 1 £8 000; year 2 £14 000; year 3 £11 000.

Calculate the net cash flow for each of years 1 to 3. **[3]**

**6.** Pelham Tyres is considering a project with an initial outlay of £150 000. Net cash inflows are
forecast as: year 1 £35 000; year 2 £45 000; year 3 £50 000; year 4 £48 000; year 5 £30 000.

Calculate the payback period. **[4]**

**7.** Brook Dental Practice plans to buy imaging equipment for £200 000. Net cash inflows will be
£60 000 a year for 4 years, and the equipment will then be sold for £25 000. The cost of capital is 11%.
Discount factors: year 1 0.901; year 2 0.812; year 3 0.731; year 4 0.659.

**(a)** Calculate the net present value of the project. **[5]**
**(b)** State, with a reason, whether the project should go ahead on financial grounds. **[1]**

**8.** Explain **one** benefit and **one** limitation of the payback method of capital investment
appraisal. **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** Explain **one** reason why net present value is often preferred to payback. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** limitation of the net present value method. **[2]**

**10.** Ravensworth Bakery can afford one of two ovens, X or Y. Each costs £140 000. The cost of capital
is 9%. Discount factors: year 1 0.917; year 2 0.842; year 3 0.772; year 4 0.708; year 5 0.650.

| Year | Oven X net cash inflow (£) | Oven Y net cash inflow (£) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 000 | 20 000 |
| 2 | 50 000 | 30 000 |
| 3 | 36 000 | 40 000 |
| 4 | 20 000 | 60 000 |
| 5 | 10 000 | 70 000 |

Oven Y uses less energy. Its maker is a new company with no service network in the bakery's area.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period for each oven. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the net present value of each oven. **[4]**
**(c)** Evaluate which oven the bakery should buy. Use your answers to (a) and (b) and other relevant
factors. **[4]**

**11.** Holloway Engineering is considering an automated cutter. The finance director has provided:

- cost of the cutter, paid now: £96 000;
- a consultant's report on the cutter, already paid: £4 000;
- useful life 3 years, residual value at the end of year 3: £12 000;
- annual savings in labour costs: £38 000; extra annual maintenance: £6 000;
- annual depreciation of £28 000, charged in the profit forecast;
- £3 000 a year of existing factory rent, apportioned to the cutter in the profit forecast.

The cost of capital is 10%. Discount factors: year 1 0.909; year 2 0.826; year 3 0.751.

**(a)** Calculate the annual net cash flow, explaining how you have treated the consultant's report,
the depreciation and the rent. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the net present value of the cutter. **[4]**
**(c)** Recommend whether the business should buy the cutter, including one non-financial factor. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C**: the residual value is a future cash inflow caused by the project [1].
*Examiner insight:* A and D are classic distractors; depreciation is non-cash and apportioned rent is paid anyway.

**2.** 1 ÷ 1.09³ = 0.772, so **B** [1].
*Examiner insight:* 0.917 and 0.842 are the year-1 and year-2 factors; check you used the right power of n.

**3. (a)** The number a future cash flow is multiplied by to give its present value, based on the cost
of capital and the year [1].
**(b)** The rate of return a business must pay to the providers of its finance; the minimum return a
project must earn [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Interest rate" alone is too vague for (b); link it to the return required on finance.

**4.** 84 000 ÷ 24 000 = 3.5 years [1]; 0.5 × 12 = 6 months, so **3 years 6 months** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "3.5 years" alone does not answer "in years and months", so the final mark is at risk.

**5.** Depreciation = (75 000 − 15 000) ÷ 3 = £20 000 a year [1]. Add back: year 1 **£28 000**; year 2
**£34 000** [1]; year 3 31 000 + 15 000 residual value = **£46 000** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Depreciation is added back because it is non-cash; leaving the residual value out of year 3 loses a mark.

**6.** Cumulative: year 1 (115 000); year 2 (70 000) [1]; year 3 (20 000), turning positive in year 4
[1]. (20 000 ÷ 48 000) × 12 = 5 months [1]. Payback = **3 years 5 months** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the cumulative column; with a wrong final answer, visible working is the only route to method credit.

**7. (a)** Year 4 cash flow = 60 000 + 25 000 = £85 000 [1]. Present values: 54 060; 48 720; 43 860 [1];
85 000 × 0.659 = 56 015 [1]. Total = £202 655 [1]. NPV = 202 655 − 200 000 = **£2 655** [1].
**(b)** Go ahead: the NPV is positive, so the project earns more than the 11% cost of capital, though
the margin is small [1].
*Examiner insight:* The decision must follow from your own NPV figure; a positive NPV with "reject" and no reason gains nothing.

**8.** Benefit: payback is simple and quick to calculate and understand [1], so managers who are not
accountants can use it to compare projects [1]. Limitation: it ignores cash flows after the payback
point [1], so a project with large later inflows may be rejected even though it adds more value [1].
*Examiner insight:* In this answer each pair of marks is a point plus its development, so a bare list of points earns only the first mark of each pair.

**9. (a)** NPV allows for the time value of money [1]: later cash flows are discounted because cash
received sooner can be reinvested, so the result reflects what the project is worth today [1].
**(b)** The answer depends on the cost of capital [1], which is hard to estimate; a small change in the
rate can turn a positive NPV negative [1].
*Examiner insight:* "It is more accurate" earns nothing on its own; say why, such as discounting or using all the cash flows.

**10. (a)** X cumulative: (80 000), (30 000), turning positive in year 3 [1]; 2 years + (30 000 ÷ 36 000)
× 12 = **2 years 10 months** [1]. Y cumulative: (120 000), (90 000), (50 000), turning positive in year 4
[1]; 3 years + (50 000 ÷ 60 000) × 12 = **3 years 10 months** [1].
**(b)** X present values: 55 020 + 42 100 + 27 792 + 14 160 + 6 500 = 145 572 [1]; NPV = **£5 572** [1].
Y present values: 18 340 + 25 260 + 30 880 + 42 480 + 45 500 = 162 460 [1]; NPV = **£22 460** [1].
**(c)** Both NPVs are positive, but Y adds £16 888 more value [1]. X pays back a year sooner; Y depends
on large inflows in years 4 and 5, which are the least certain forecasts [1]. Y's lower energy use
helps costs and reputation, but having no local service network risks long breakdowns [1].
Recommendation: buy Y if the bakery can secure a service contract, as its NPV is far higher; otherwise
X is the safer choice [1].
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs a decision justified by the figures; "it depends" with no choice does not earn it.

**11. (a)** Net cash flow = 38 000 − 6 000 = **£32 000 a year** [1]. The consultant's report is
excluded as a sunk cost [1]. Depreciation is excluded because it is non-cash [1]. The rent is excluded
because it is paid whether or not the cutter is bought [1].
**(b)** Years 1 and 2: 32 000 × 0.909 = 29 088 and 32 000 × 0.826 = 26 432 [1]. Year 3 cash flow = 32 000
+ 12 000 = 44 000 [1]; 44 000 × 0.751 = 33 044, total present value 88 564 [1]. NPV = 88 564 − 96 000
= **−£7 436** [1].
**(c)** Non-financial factor: the cutter may improve accuracy and safety by removing manual cutting [1].
Recommendation: do not buy on the current figures, as the NPV is negative, unless the gains in quality
or safety are judged to outweigh a £7 436 shortfall [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each exclusion in (a) needs its reason; naming the item without saying why it is left out scores nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding depreciation as a cash outflow instead of excluding it.
- Including sunk costs such as reports or surveys already paid for.
- Including apportioned rent or overheads that do not change.
- Leaving the residual value out of NPV or putting it in the wrong year.
- Converting a decimal of a year to months wrongly, or using the wrong year's inflow as divisor.
- Discounting the year-0 outlay or forgetting to deduct it.
- Giving an NPV without a sign or a decision.
- Evaluations with no non-financial factor or no final recommendation.
