## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by respiration. **[2]**

**2.** This question is about aerobic respiration.

**(a)** State the word equation for aerobic respiration. **[2]**
**(b)** State the balanced chemical equation for aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**3.** Describe aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**4.** State the word equation for anaerobic respiration in:

**(a)** human muscle cells **[1]**
**(b)** yeast. **[2]**

**5.** Compare aerobic respiration with anaerobic respiration in human muscle cells. **[4]**

**6.** Use the balanced equation for aerobic respiration to answer these.

**(a)** A liver cell respires 7 molecules of glucose aerobically. Calculate the number of oxygen molecules used. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of carbon dioxide molecules released by the same cell. **[1]**
**(c)** A root cell releases 54 molecules of water by aerobic respiration. Calculate the number of glucose molecules it respired. **[1]**

**7.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on respiration in yeast. She mixed yeast with glucose solution and placed equal volumes in five boiling tubes, each in a water-bath at a different temperature. She left the tubes for 10 minutes, then collected the gas released over water in an upturned measuring cylinder for 10 minutes.

| Temperature / °C | 15 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of gas in 10 min / cm³ | 3.0 | 8.0 | 15.0 | 11.0 | 2.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of gas release at 35 °C, in cm³ per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the results. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the results. **[3]**
**(d)** State two variables, other than the volume of the mixture, that the student should keep the same. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest why the tubes were left in the water-baths for 10 minutes before the gas was collected. **[1]**
**(f)** Suggest a control that would show the gas is released by living yeast. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why lactic acid builds up in the muscles and blood during vigorous exercise. **[4]**

**9.** An athlete's resting oxygen uptake is 0.35 dm³ per minute. Her oxygen uptake was measured each minute after a 400 m race.

| Minute after race | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen uptake / dm³ per min | 2.35 | 1.75 | 1.15 | 0.75 | 0.50 | 0.35 | 0.35 |

**(a)** Calculate the total extra oxygen she used after the race, above her resting uptake. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the letters EPOC stand for. **[1]**
**(c)** Outline how the oxygen debt is removed after exercise. **[4]**
**(d)** Explain why her oxygen uptake had returned to 0.35 dm³ per minute by minute 6. **[2]**

**10.** A student boiled some glucose solution, cooled it to 30 °C and added yeast. She covered the mixture with a layer of oil and connected the flask by a delivery tube to a tube of limewater. After 20 minutes the limewater turned milky and the temperature in the flask had risen slightly.

**(a)** Suggest why the glucose solution was boiled and then covered with oil. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the gas that turned the limewater milky. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the other product of respiration that would be found in the flask. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the temperature in the flask rose. **[1]**

**11.** State the use of energy from respiration in each example.

**(a)** A root hair cell takes in nitrate ions from soil water where nitrate ions are less concentrated than in the cell. **[1]**
**(b)** A ribosome joins amino acids together to make an enzyme. **[1]**
**(c)** A message travels along a neurone from the skin to the spinal cord. **[1]**
**(d)** A penguin keeps its body at about the same temperature in freezing weather. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The chemical reactions in all living cells [1] that release energy from glucose [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Respiration is breathing" or "respiration produces energy" contradicts the definition; use the words "chemical reactions", "cells" and "release".

**2. (a)** glucose + oxygen [1] → carbon dioxide + water [1]
**(b)** Correct formulae C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂, CO₂ and H₂O [1]; balanced with 6O₂, 6CO₂ and 6H₂O [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the balance by counting oxygen atoms (18 on each side) before moving on; a missing 6 makes the equation unbalanced.

**3.** The release of a relatively large amount of energy [1] by the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Uses oxygen" alone does not describe aerobic respiration; the amount of energy is a separate idea and needs "relatively large".

**4. (a)** glucose → lactic acid [1]
**(b)** glucose → ethanol [1] + carbon dioxide [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Alcohol is another name for ethanol, but adding oxygen or water as a product makes the yeast equation wrong.

**5.** Any four [4]:
- aerobic uses oxygen; anaerobic does not use oxygen
- aerobic releases a relatively large amount of energy; anaerobic releases a relatively small amount
- aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water; anaerobic produces lactic acid
- anaerobic produces no carbon dioxide
- anaerobic leads to an oxygen debt after exercise; aerobic does not
- both break down glucose / both release energy **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides of each point; "anaerobic releases less energy" is clearer than a list of anaerobic facts with no reference to aerobic.

**6. (a)** 7 × 6 = **42 molecules** [1]
**(b)** 7 × 6 = **42 molecules** [1]
**(c)** 54 ÷ 6 = **9 molecules** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the ratio (1 glucose : 6 oxygen : 6 carbon dioxide : 6 water) before calculating, so any error is easy to trace.

**7. (a)** 15.0 ÷ 10 = **1.5 cm³ per minute** [1]
**(b)** The volume of gas increases from 3.0 cm³ at 15 °C to a maximum of 15.0 cm³ at 35 °C [1]; it then decreases to 2.0 cm³ at 55 °C [1].
**(c)** Respiration is controlled by enzymes [1]. As temperature rises to 35 °C, molecules have more kinetic energy, so enzymes and substrates collide more often [1]. Above the optimum, the enzymes are denatured: the active site changes shape so the substrate no longer fits [1].
**(d)** Any two [2]: mass of yeast; concentration of glucose solution; collection time; size of tube; type of yeast.
**(e)** So that the mixture reached the temperature of the water-bath [1].
**(f)** Repeat with boiled (killed) yeast, which should release no gas [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) quote values with units, including the highest point; in (c) "the yeast died" is weaker than naming denatured enzymes and the active site.

**8.** Muscles contract hard and need energy very quickly [1]. Oxygen cannot be supplied to the muscles fast enough for aerobic respiration to release all of it [1]. So the muscles also respire anaerobically [1], producing lactic acid, which builds up in the muscles and passes into the blood [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Not enough oxygen" needs to be linked to the rate of supply to the muscles; an answer that says the body has "run out of oxygen" is inaccurate.

**9. (a)** Subtract 0.35 from each value: 2.00, 1.40, 0.80, 0.40, 0.15, 0, 0 [1]; total = **4.75 dm³** [1]
**(b)** Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption [1]
**(c)** Heart rate stays fast [1] to transport lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver [1]. Breathing stays deeper and faster [1] to supply oxygen for the breakdown of lactic acid in the liver [1].
**(d)** All the lactic acid had been broken down, so the oxygen debt was repaid [1]; oxygen was now needed only for normal aerobic respiration at rest [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) give a reason with each change; "breathing stays fast to get more energy" misses the lactic acid and liver link that the outcome asks for.

**10. (a)** Boiling removes dissolved oxygen [1]; the oil stops oxygen from the air dissolving back in, so the yeast respires anaerobically [1].
**(b)** Carbon dioxide [1]
**(c)** Ethanol (alcohol) [1]
**(d)** Respiration releases energy, and some of it is released as heat [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the oil has a different job from boiling; one reason stated twice in different words does not cover both.

**11. (a)** Active transport [1]
**(b)** Protein synthesis [1]
**(c)** Passage of electrical impulses along neurones [1]
**(d)** Maintenance of a constant body temperature [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the syllabus wording for each use; "moving ions" or "keeping warm" is too vague to name the process.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that respiration produces or makes energy.
- Treating respiration and breathing as the same thing.
- Putting carbon dioxide in the human anaerobic equation, or lactic acid in the yeast equation.
- Leaving the 6s out of the balanced equation, or writing oxygen as O.
- Describing yeast results without quoting the temperature and value of the highest rate.
- Explaining the fall above the optimum without the words "enzymes" and "denatured".
- Vague controlled variables such as "same amount" with no quantity named.
- Saying lactic acid is broken down in the muscles, or that the heart beats fast after exercise "to get more energy".
- Forgetting to subtract the resting value when calculating an oxygen debt.
