These questions cover **3.1.1.2 Mass number and isotopes** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, and available to Unit 5). Use Ar values from the Periodic Table where you need to identify an element. Some isotope data are simplified and are labelled as such. Revise first with the [mass number and isotopes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-2/) or the [mass number and isotopes revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-2-revision-notes/) if you need to.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in each species.

**(a)** ⁵²Cr³⁺ (atomic number 24) **[1]**

**(b)** ⁷⁹Se²⁻ (atomic number 34) **[1]**

**2.** An ion Y³⁻ has 36 electrons and 42 neutrons. Identify Y and give the full symbol of the ion, including its mass number. **[3]**

**3.** Which pair of species are isotopes of one element?

A: ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca

B: ¹²C and ¹⁴C

C: O₂ and O₃

D: ²³Na and ²³Na⁺

**[1]**

**4.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in the oxonium ion ¹H₃¹⁶O⁺. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why sulfur-32 and sulfur-34 have the same chemical properties but a sample of pure sulfur-34 would have a slightly greater density than a sample of pure sulfur-32. **[2]**

**6.** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the ionisation of a krypton atom by electron impact in a TOF mass spectrometer. **[1]**

**7.** The mass spectrum of gallium shows peaks at m/z 69 (height 6.0) and m/z 71 (height 4.0). Calculate the relative atomic mass of gallium. **[2]**

### Section B

**8.** A sample of gallium is analysed in a TOF mass spectrometer.

**(a)** Explain why the gallium atoms must be converted into positive ions before they can be analysed. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the detector measures the relative abundance of each isotope. **[2]**

**(c)** State which of the two ions, ⁶⁹Ga⁺ or ⁷¹Ga⁺, has the greater speed in the drift region, and explain why. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows simplified data for the isotopes in a sample of zinc.

| m/z | 64 | 66 | 67 | 68 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abundance / % | 48.6 | 27.9 | 4.1 | 18.8 | 0.6 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of zinc from these data. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the relative atomic mass of zinc is not a whole number. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the number of neutrons in the isotope responsible for the tallest peak (zinc has atomic number 30). **[1]**

**10.** An element G has two isotopes, of mass numbers 121 and 123. Its relative atomic mass is 121.8.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. **[3]**

**(b)** Use the Periodic Table to identify G. **[1]**

**11.** The mass spectrum of a metal has peaks at m/z 50 (4.3%), 52 (83.8%), 53 (9.5%) and 54 (2.4%).

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass to one decimal place and identify the metal. **[3]**

**(b)** The metal has atomic number 24. Give the number of neutrons in an atom of the most abundant isotope. **[1]**

**(c)** State which ion takes the longest time to reach the detector. **[1]**

**12.** A colourless liquid is known to be butane, C₄H₁₀, propan-1-ol, C₃H₈O, or ethanoic acid, C₂H₄O₂. Its mass spectrum has a molecular ion peak at m/z 58, with a very small peak at m/z 59.

**(a)** Identify the compound. Show your reasoning using relative molecular masses (Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0). **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the formation of the molecular ion from a molecule of the compound. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why there is a very small peak at m/z 59. **[1]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Protons 24, neutrons 28, electrons 21 [1] (all three needed)

**(b)** Protons 34, neutrons 45, electrons 36 [1] (all three needed) **[2]**

**2.** Protons = 36 − 3 = 33, so Y is **arsenic** [1]; mass number = 33 + 42 = 75 [1]; symbol **⁷⁵As³⁻** (with 33 as the atomic number, bottom left) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a negative ion, the atom has gained electrons, so subtract the charge from the electron count to find the protons.

**3.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A has the same mass number but different elements; C is two forms of an element, not isotopes; D is an atom and its ion.

**4.** Protons: 3 × 1 + 8 = **11** [1]; neutrons: 0 + 8 = **8** [1]; electrons: 11 − 1 = **10** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Both isotopes have 16 protons and the same electron configuration, and chemical properties depend on electrons [1]; sulfur-34 atoms have two more neutrons, so each atom has a greater mass and the same volume of sample has a greater mass [1] **[2]**

**6.** Kr(g) + e⁻ → Kr⁺(g) + 2e⁻ [1] **[1]**

**7.** Ar = (69 × 6.0 + 71 × 4.0) ÷ (6.0 + 4.0) [1] = 698 ÷ 10.0 = **69.8** [1] **[2]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** Only charged particles are accelerated by the electric field [1]; and only ions can be detected, because they produce a current at the detector [1]

**(b)** Each positive ion gains an electron at the detector, producing a current [1]; the size of the current is proportional to the number of ions with that m/z, so it measures abundance [1]

**(c)** **⁶⁹Ga⁺** [1]; both ions have the same kinetic energy, and since KE = ½mv² the lighter ion must have the higher speed [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "lighter ions are faster" needs the reason: equal kinetic energy.

**9.**

**(a)** Ar = (64 × 48.6 + 66 × 27.9 + 67 × 4.1 + 68 × 18.8 + 70 × 0.6) ÷ 100 [1] = **65.5** [1]

**(b)** It is a weighted mean of the masses of several isotopes [1]

**(c)** 64 − 30 = **34** [1] **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** Let x = fraction of ¹²³G: 121(1 − x) + 123x = 121.8 [1]; 2x = 0.8, so x = 0.40 [1]; ¹²³G = **40%**, ¹²¹G = **60%** [1]

**(b)** **Antimony**, Sb (Ar 121.8) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* 121.8 is nearer 121 than 123, so the lighter isotope must be more abundant: a quick check that catches swapped answers.

**11.**

**(a)** Ar = (50 × 4.3 + 52 × 83.8 + 53 × 9.5 + 54 × 2.4) ÷ 100 [1] = **52.1** [1]; the metal is **chromium** (Ar 52.0) [1]

**(b)** 52 − 24 = **28** [1]

**(c)** ⁵⁴Cr⁺, the heaviest ion [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** Mr: butane 58.0, propan-1-ol 60.0, ethanoic acid 60.0 [1]; the molecular ion peak at 58 shows the compound is **butane** [1]

**(b)** C₄H₁₀(g) + e⁻ → C₄H₁₀⁺(g) + 2e⁻ [1]

**(c)** A small proportion of molecules contain a carbon-13 atom (a heavier isotope), so their molecular ion has m/z one unit higher [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mr comes from the molecular ion, the peak at the largest m/z apart from tiny isotope peaks, not from the tallest peak.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding instead of subtracting the charge when counting electrons in ions.
- Stating that acceleration gives ions the same speed.
- Dividing by 100 when abundances are peak heights with a different total.
- Swapping the two abundances in reverse calculations.
