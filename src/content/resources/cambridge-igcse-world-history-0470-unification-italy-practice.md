These questions cover key question 1.2 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470, "How was Italy unified?", the Paper 2
prescribed topic for November 2026.

## Section A

**1.** Name **three** Italian states that were ruled directly by Austria or by rulers linked to Austria before
1859. **[3]**

**2.** Describe Mazzini's ideas about Italian unity. **[4]**

**3.** State **two** ways in which Cavour strengthened Piedmont in the 1850s. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what was agreed at Plombières in 1858. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** results of the war of 1859 for Piedmont. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how Venetia and Rome became part of Italy. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why was Italy not unified in 1848-49? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Cavour try to control Garibaldi in 1860? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Cavour hindered more than he helped the unification of Italy." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**10.** "Foreign countries did more than Italians to unify Italy." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a letter by a Neapolitan landowner, written in November 1860. "The Redshirts came as
liberators, and the peasants cheered because they believed the land would be theirs. Now come the officials from
Turin, with their laws, their taxes and their conscription. They speak to us as if we were Africans. I fear the
peasants will soon be cheering for brigands."

What can you learn from Source A about the results of Garibaldi's campaign in the south? Use the source and your
knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary cartoon set in 1860, written for this page. Garibaldi, in a
red shirt and holding a sword, kneels to offer a crown on a cushion to Victor Emmanuel. Behind a curtain, Cavour
watches with a smile, holding a pen labelled "Diplomacy".

What is the message of the cartoon described in Source B? Use details of the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: Lombardy (1); Venetia (1); Tuscany (1); Modena (1); Parma (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any four, 1 mark each: Italy should be united as one nation (1); it should be a republic, not ruled by kings
(1); it should be democratic (1); it should be created by the Italian people rising together (1); not by foreign help
or by princes (1); he founded Young Italy in 1831 to spread these ideas (1); he believed unity was a moral and almost
religious duty (1). **[4]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: built railways (1); signed trade agreements and encouraged commerce (1); developed banks
and credit (1); reduced the power and property of the Church (1); strengthened the army (1); joined the Crimean War to
gain allies (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: a secret meeting between Cavour and Napoleon III in July 1858 (1); France would help
Piedmont fight Austria (1); only if Austria appeared to be the aggressor (1); Piedmont would gain Lombardy and
Venetia to form a kingdom of northern Italy (1); France would gain Nice and Savoy (1); a marriage alliance between the
two royal families was arranged (1); Italy might become a federation under the Pope's presidency (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Piedmont gained Lombardy (1); Austria kept Venetia (1); Cavour resigned in anger (1); the
central Italian states revolted, leading to their annexation in 1860 (1); Piedmont had to give Nice and Savoy to
France in 1860 (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: Italy allied with Prussia against Austria in 1866 (1); Italy was defeated at Custoza and
Lissa (1); Prussia defeated Austria (1); Venetia was handed to Italy through France (1); Rome was protected by a French
garrison (1); Garibaldi's attempts failed in 1862 and 1867 (1); France withdrew its troops in 1870 to fight Prussia
(1); Italian troops entered Rome in September 1870 and it became the capital (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **Austria's military strength**, because Radetzky's army recovered from the Five Days and beat Piedmont at
Custoza in 1848 and Novara in 1849; **divisions among Italians**, because Charles Albert wanted an enlarged Piedmont
while Mazzini wanted a republic, so they did not co-operate; **the Pope's refusal** to fight Austria in April 1848
removed a potential national leader and split Catholic opinion; **foreign intervention**, because French troops
destroyed the Roman Republic in 1849; **little peasant support** for a movement of the towns and the educated. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **fear of war with France**, because Garibaldi wanted to march on
Rome, defended by a French garrison, which could bring French troops against Piedmont; **fear of a republic**, because
Garibaldi had been a Mazzinian and might set up a radical state in the south; **to keep the leadership for Piedmont**
and the monarchy, so that the new Italy would be built on Piedmont's laws; **fear of social revolution**, because
peasants who joined Garibaldi expected land. Cavour therefore sent the Piedmontese army through the Papal States to
reach Naples first. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Hindered:** his aim was a northern kingdom, not all of Italy; he gave away Nice and Savoy; he did not openly support
the Thousand and tried to restrain Garibaldi; he imposed Piedmont's laws and officials on the south. **Helped:** he
modernised Piedmont; the Crimean War and Congress of Paris won friends; Plombières brought the French alliance that
won Lombardy; he turned the revolts in central Italy into annexation; his army's march south secured Naples and
avoided war with France. **Judgement:** for example, his caution limited his aims, but without his diplomacy Austria
would not have been defeated. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** French armies won Magenta and Solferino in 1859;
Venetia came through Prussia's victory in 1866, not Italian success; Rome fell only when France withdrew in 1870;
British goodwill discouraged intervention. **Disagree:** Cavour created the alliance with France; Garibaldi conquered
the south with Italian volunteers; Italians in the central duchies voted for union; Mazzini's ideas created the
national cause. **Judgement:** for example, Italians provided the leadership and the south, but foreign armies
decided the battles against Austria. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains what can be learned, supported by the source and by contextual knowledge.
Level 2 (3-4): explains using the source's details only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or gives knowledge
without using it. Indicative content: **Garibaldi was welcomed** at first ("came as liberators"); **peasants expected
land reform** ("believed the land would be theirs"), which did not happen; **Piedmontese rule was resented**: "laws",
"taxes" and "conscription" from Turin; **northerners looked down on southerners** ("as if we were Africans");
**unrest and brigandage were coming**. Knowledge: plebiscites in October 1860 approved union; Piedmont's laws and
officials were extended to the south; a long brigand war followed in the 1860s. **[6]**

**12.** Levels as for Question 11. Indicative content: the message is that **Garibaldi's conquests made Victor
Emmanuel king of all Italy**: the crown is offered by the soldier, not won by the king; Garibaldi **kneels**, showing
that he accepted the king's authority rather than ruling the south himself; **Cavour behind the curtain** suggests
that his diplomacy was quietly directing events and that he gained most from them. Knowledge: at Teano in October
1860 Garibaldi handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel; Cavour had sent the Piedmontese army south to take control;
the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in March 1861. **[6]**
